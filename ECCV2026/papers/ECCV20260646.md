## Problem

Existing canopy height change datasets reduce the problem to binary change detection, ignoring the continuous nature of vegetation height change and the inherent uncertainty in ALS-derived labels. No public benchmark provides continuous, high-resolution canopy height change with spatially resolved uncertainty over a large region, leaving a gap in evaluating models for realistic, noisy regression of tree growth and loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260646/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-year PlanetScope optical time series (RGB + NIR, ~3 m GSD, 1–9 cloud-free images per year between 2018 and 2023) and produces a dense 3 m-resolution map of continuous canopy height change in meters, paired with a per-pixel change variance layer (m²). The test split is restricted to high-confidence pixels (|z-score| > 1.65) to exclude low-signal-to-noise samples, and a vegetation mask (height > 3 m, eroded by one pixel) restricts evaluation to woody canopy.

## Method

The CHC dataset derives continuous height change by vertically differencing 95th-percentile DSMs from two national ALS campaigns (2018, 2023) at 3 m grid resolution, with a simulated uncertainty layer combining systematic error (from residuals on static road surfaces) and heteroscedastic statistical error (from an MLP trained via Gaussian NLL loss to predict offset and variance as a function of pulse density and surface geometry). For benchmarking, frozen GFM encoders (ViT, Swin-Transformer, and CNN backbones) feed a trainable UPerNet decoder, with temporal fusion handled by a Lightweight Temporal Attention Encoder for non-temporal models. The key training innovation is uncertainty-aware loss weighting: a weighted MSE that scales per-pixel error by inverse target variance, and a thresholded MSE that excludes low-confidence pixels, both of which improve GFM performance by 4–9% RMSE over standard MSE on this fundamentally noisy regression task.

## Results

On the CHC test split, no Geospatial Foundation Model (GFM) outperforms a fully supervised UNet trained with thresholded MSE (tMSE), which achieves an RMSE of 5.65 m and R² of 0.48. The task-specific ECHOSAT model attains the best overall RMSE of 5.25 m (R² = 0.55), while among GFMs, Prithvi-EO 2.0 (RMSE 5.90 m) and DOFA (RMSE 5.99 m) lead. Scale-MAE performs worse than a randomly initialized, non-fine-tuned ResNet-50 encoder (RMSE 7.83 m vs. 6.98 m) across all loss weighting schemes, indicating that frozen GFM features do not reliably capture subpixel canopy height change signals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The supervised UNet with tMSE (RMSE 5.65 m) outperforms all GFMs, and the task-specific ECHOSAT model (RMSE 5.25 m) further beats both by 0.40 m and 0.45 m respectively, despite operating at coarser 10 m resolution. Among GFMs, Prithvi-EO 2.0 (5.90 m) and DOFA (5.99 m) are the strongest, with DINOv3 close behind (5.95 m); all three benefit from uncertainty-aware loss weighting (tMSE or wMSE), reducing RMSE by 4–9% over standard MSE. CROMA achieves the best F1 for detecting height increases (0.85) but lags on decreases (F1 0.47), while the supervised UNet leads on decreases (F1 0.64). Scale-MAE, SpectralGPT+, TerraMind, and RemoteCLIP all fall below or near the random ResNet-50 baseline (RMSE 6.98 m), indicating their frozen encoders fail to encode the fine-grained structural cues needed for continuous height change regression. The overall pattern confirms that current GFMs remain outperformed by supervised baselines and specialized models on pixel-level regression tasks, a gap the CHC benchmark is designed to expose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three threads: (1) 3D change detection from repeat ALS, which prior datasets (3DCD, SMARS, Open-Canopy-Δ) frame as binary or ternary classification rather than continuous regression; (2) LiDAR uncertainty propagation literature, which identifies pulse density and scan geometry as key drivers of height bias but has not been operationalized as a pixel-level uncertainty layer for training; and (3) the GFM benchmarking paradigm (PANGAEA), which to date has included only two regression tasks. The paper repurposes heteroscedastic regression techniques (weighted MSE, Gaussian NLL) from the noisy-label literature into a spatially resolved canopy-height-change setting, and positions the CHC dataset as the first public benchmark combining continuous height change, per-pixel uncertainty, and multi-year optical time series at 3 m resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CHC dataset fills a concrete gap: no prior public benchmark provides continuous canopy height change with associated uncertainty over a large area, which is essential for evaluating models on the small-magnitude growth signals that dominate real forest dynamics. The empirical finding that no GFM outperforms a supervised UNet (and that a coarser 10 m task-specific model, ECHOSAT, beats all GFMs) reinforces the open question of why self-supervised pretraining has not yet transferred to fine-grained regression, and suggests that temporal reasoning and sub-pixel structural sensitivity remain unsolved for frozen encoders. The uncertainty-weighted training strategy (wMSE/tMSE) offers a practical, low-cost lever that yields 4–9% RMSE reductions for the best models, but its benefit is inconsistent across architectures, leaving open how to best integrate label uncertainty into GFM fine-tuning. Limitations include a single geographic region (Spain), a single optical sensor (PlanetScope), and a 5-year observation gap that conflates growth with disturbance.
