## Problem

Accurate foreground segmentation without any human annotations remains a core challenge in computer vision. Existing unsupervised methods either depend on weak human cues (points, scribbles), rely on pretrained segmentation models like SAM for pseudo-labels, or produce coarse bipartitions that require heavy post-processing (CRFs, bilateral solvers) to reach usable quality. This paper asks whether a model can learn accurate segmentation purely from self-supervised features with no annotations, no external segmentation priors, and no post-processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260730/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single raw RGB image as input with no labels, prompts, or auxiliary signals. During training, images are resized to 768×768 and processed by a frozen DINOv3-7B backbone to extract dense patch features; at inference, the same pipeline generalizes to much higher resolutions (up to 2560×2560) without retraining. The output is a binary per-patch foreground/background mask (upscaled to full image resolution) that serves directly as a saliency map, with no morphological refinement, CRF, or other post-processing applied.

## Method

Selfment operates in three stages. First, a frozen DINOv3-7B backbone produces dense patch embeddings, from which a patch-level affinity graph is built and partitioned via Normalized Cut (using the Fiedler vector) to yield a coarse foreground-background bipartition. Second, Iterative Patch Optimization (IPO) refines this bipartition by iteratively reassigning each patch to the cluster (foreground or background) whose centroid it is most similar to in normalized feature space, updating centroids each step for 20 iterations with an orientation-consistency constraint to prevent label flipping. Third, the refined masks serve as noisy pseudo-labels to train a lightweight two-layer projection head and binary classifier using a combined loss of InfoNCE-style contrastive alignment, soft Dice, and BCE, enabling the model to learn stable, transferable object representations. The entire pipeline requires only 1,000 unlabeled images and 0.54M trainable parameters, and the IPO module is backbone-agnostic, transferring to DINO, Perception Encoder, and TIPSv2 features.

## Results

On unsupervised saliency detection, Selfment (1280×1280) achieves Fmax of 95.9 on ECSSD, 86.4 on DUTS, 94.4 on HKUIS, and 91.7 on PASCAL-S, improving over the previous best unsupervised method (SelfMask) by +4.0%, +7.0%, +4.6%, and +5.7% respectively. On camouflaged object detection (zero-shot, no fine-tuning), Selfment reaches Sm of .910 on CHAMELEON, .869 on CAMO, .873 on COD10K, and .902 on NC4K, surpassing all prior unsupervised methods and even exceeding several fully supervised approaches such as FSPNet on CAMO (.869 vs .856). The method requires only 0.54M trainable parameters in the segmentation head and trains in 27.6 minutes on 8×A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Selfment decisively outperforms all recent self-supervised segmentation baselines. Against TokenCut [48] (the closest prior method using NCut on DINO features), Selfment gains +7.5 Fmax on ECSSD (95.9 vs 87.8 at 768) and +15.5 Fmax on PASCAL-S (91.7 vs 76.2), with the gap widening at higher resolutions where TokenCut degrades. Against SelfMask [40] and FOUND [42], the two strongest prior SSL-based methods, Selfment leads by +4.0 and +4.5 Fmax on ECSSD respectively, and by +7.0 and +10.0 Fmax on PASCAL-S. Notably, Selfment is the only method that consistently improves with higher input resolution (768→1280), while TokenCut, SelfMask, and FOUND all degrade. On camouflaged object detection, Selfment zero-shot surpasses the best prior unsupervised method UCOD-DPL [50] by +.046 Sm on CHAMELEON (.910 vs .864) and +.076 on CAMO (.869 vs .793), and even exceeds the fully supervised FSPNet [17] on CAMO (.869 vs .856 Sm), though it remains below the strongest supervised method BiRefNet [54] (.932 Sm on CAMO).

## Relation to Prior Work

Selfment directly extends the TokenCut [48] pipeline (self-supervised ViT features + Normalized Cut bipartition) by addressing its two main weaknesses: unstable spectral relaxation and the need for heavy post-processing (CRFs, bilateral solvers). The key departures are the Iterative Patch Optimization module, which refines NCut's coarse bipartition through iterative centroid-based reassignment in feature space, and a self-supervised training stage that distills the refined pseudo-labels into a lightweight segmentation head via contrastive and Dice objectives. Unlike prior unsupervised methods that lean on SAM or other off-the-shelf segmentation models for pseudo-labels, Selfment relies solely on DINOv3 dense features and graph partitioning, and unlike TokenCut it does not degrade at higher inference resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot transfer to camouflaged object detection—rivaling fully supervised methods like FSPNet on CAMO without any task-specific fine-tuning—suggests that self-supervised dense features encode objectness cues general enough to handle extreme visual camouflage, a finding with broader implications for annotation-free perception. The framework's backbone-agnosticism (validated on DINO, DINOv3, PE, and TIPSv2) makes it a practical template for converting any SSL feature extractor into a segmentation tool. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object or dense semantic segmentation, whether the DINOv3-7B dependency is truly necessary or whether smaller backbones with IPO can close the gap, and how the method behaves on scenes with multiple salient objects or no clear foreground-background dichotomy.
