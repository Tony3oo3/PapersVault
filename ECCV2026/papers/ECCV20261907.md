## Problem

MLLM-based universal multimodal embedders process dense visual token sequences (e.g., up to 7,290 tokens in LLaVA-OneVision) whose quadratic attention cost is prohibitive for large-scale retrieval, yet their contribution to the final single-vector embedding is marginal. Existing MLLM embedding methods have focused on data scale, hard negative mining, and multi-stage training but have not addressed this visual token redundancy bottleneck, leaving a significant efficiency gap for latency-critical deployment.

## Input / Output

The embedder accepts text queries, natural images (resized to 448×448, 1 tile), visual document images (resized to 896×896, up to 4 tiles), or interleaved image-text inputs, and produces a single L2-normalized embedding vector extracted from the last token's hidden state. Each image tile is compressed to 64 visual tokens (vs. 256 in vanilla InternVL3) before entering the LLM. The companion reranker takes a query–candidate pair (or a short candidate list) and outputs a pointwise Yes/No relevance token or a listwise position index identifying the most relevant candidate.

## Method

The architecture inserts a parameter-free compression module (bilinear spatial interpolation followed by pixel unshuffle with factor α=2) between the visual encoder and the LLM, reducing per-tile visual tokens from 256 to 64 without introducing learnable parameters. Training follows a three-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline: (1) generative continued training on 32M instruction-following samples to re-align compressed visual features with the LLM's semantic space; (2) contrastive pretraining on 16M retrieval samples, progressing from in-batch negatives to global hard negative mining (sampling from ranks 50–100 of a retrieval list); (3) task-aware finetuning on 1.5M curated samples where Qwen3-VL-8B acts as a judge to identify true positives and high-quality hard negatives from top-20 retrieval results. A synergistic reranker, initialized from the stage-1 checkpoint, is trained on the same judge-curated data with a joint pointwise (cross-entropy on Yes/No) and listwise (cross-entropy on position index) objective, and at inference the embedder retrieves candidates that the reranker then re-ranks.

## Results

On the MMEB benchmark (36 sub-datasets, 4 meta-tasks), Magic-MM-Embedding achieves an overall Precision@1 of 70.2 (E+R) at 1.9B scale and 72.9 (E+R) at 8.1B scale, using only 64 visual tokens per image tile (75% fewer than standard MLLMs). On VisDoc, the E+R pipeline reaches 73.3 (2B) and 75.5 (8B) NDCG@5, surpassing GME (75.2 at 8B) despite using only public data. On cross-modal retrieval, the 2B model scores 94.2 average on SugarCrepe (vs. 70.2 for UniME-V2), and the 8B model reaches 98.5 on ShareGPT4V text-to-image. Inference latency drops to 29.9 ms (2B) and 50.9 ms (8B) for MMEB queries, roughly 5–30× faster than competing MLLM embedders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest MLLM-based baseline UniME-V2, Magic-MM-Embedding wins at both scales: at 2B, the embedder-only score of 68.0 exceeds UniME-V2's full E+R pipeline (67.4) by 0.6 points, and the E+R configuration reaches 70.2 vs. 67.4. At 8B, Magic-MM-Embedding (E) scores 71.9 vs. UniME-V2 (E) at 71.2, and E+R reaches 72.9 vs. 69.0 for UniME-V2 (E+R, Qwen2-VL 8.3B). On VisDoc, the 8B E+R pipeline (75.5) surpasses GME (75.2), the strongest document-retrieval baseline, despite GME using proprietary task-aware data; the 2B E+R (73.3) also beats GME-2B (72.7). On cross-modal retrieval, the 2B model dominates UniME-V2 on SugarCrepe by a massive margin (94.2 vs. 70.2 average across Replace/Swap/Add), and the 8B model improves ShareGPT4V T→I from 95.1 to 98.5. Dual-tower baselines (CLIP, SigLIP, EVA-CLIP, MagicLens) are far behind, scoring 27.8–43.7 on MMEB overall, confirming the architectural advantage of MLLM-based embedders. Inference efficiency comparisons show Magic-MM-Embedding uses only 64 visual tokens per tile vs. 7,371 for UniME-V2 (LLaVA-OV), reducing MMEB query latency from 906.9 ms to 50.9 ms at 8B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on InternVL3 as its backbone MLLM and positions itself within the rapidly evolving MLLM-based universal multimodal embedding literature (UniME, UniME-V2, VLM2Vec, GME, LLaVE). Its key departure is identifying and addressing a bottleneck that prior MLLM embedders have overlooked: the quadratic computational cost of processing dense visual token sequences (up to 7,290 tokens in LLaVA-OneVision). The paper introduces a parameter-free compression module (bilinear interpolation + pixel unshuffle) that reduces visual tokens by 75%, paired with a three-stage progressive training pipeline that includes an MLLM-as-a-Judge for data curation — a combination not previously explored for embedding tasks, where most visual token compression work targets general generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that aggressive visual token compression is not a performance trade-off but a strategic advantage when supported by a dedicated training pipeline, which challenges the prevailing assumption that dense visual tokens are necessary for fine-grained multimodal retrieval. The practical efficiency gains are substantial (e.g., 162.8 ms → 29.9 ms query latency, 53h → 25h training time), making MLLM-based embedders more deployable in latency-critical systems. Limitations include reliance on the InternVL3 architecture, a complex multi-stage training pipeline that increases engineering overhead, and the fact that the strongest VisDoc baseline (GME) uses proprietary data, so the comparison is not fully apples-to-apples. Open questions include whether the parameter-free compression generalizes to video inputs or other MLLM backbones, and whether the 64-token sweet spot holds across all retrieval granularities.
