## Problem

State-Space Models flatten 2D feature maps into 1D sequences via isotropic serialization (e.g., raster scanning), which forces the recurrent state to traverse orthogonally to the dominant axis of anisotropic thin structures such as cracks, vessels, and roads. This serialization-induced geometry mismatch fragments what should be a single continuous trace into disjoint segments, breaking long-range connectivity and aggravating texture leakage. The paper identifies this mismatch as a key bottleneck that existing SSM-based segmentation frameworks have not addressed at the modeling level.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 256×256 and produces a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask. Internally, Haar DWT is applied to multi-channel convolutional feature maps (not raw pixels) at each encoder stage, and the IDWT restores full spatial resolution before the next stage. Training uses a combined BCE + Dice loss with AdamW (lr=1e-4, cosine decay) for 100 epochs on a single RTX 3090, with data augmentation including random flip, ±15° rotation, and photometric distortion.

## Method

FGOS-Net decouples topology modeling from noisy boundary details via a frequency-geometric disentanglement pipeline. Each FGOS stage first applies an invertible Haar DWT to split features into a low-frequency topology carrier and directional high-frequency bands; the HF bands then predict a lightweight deformation field that spatially aligns the topology carrier via GridSample to correct downsampling-induced misregistration. The aligned carrier undergoes Frequency-Aligned Scanning (FA-Scan), which assigns deterministic sub-band-matched trajectories (horizontal for LH, vertical for HL, Hilbert for LL/HH) to a shared VSS Block, turning serialization into a geometry-conditioned decision that adds zero learnable parameters. In parallel, Active Spectral-Geometric Probing (ASGP) evolves N=64 sparse probes on the topology carrier over T=3 gradient-guided steps with a diversity repulsion constraint, generating a spatial gate that selectively injects only structure-consistent high-frequency details. A parallel Gated Feature Aggregation decoder and a dual-branch Boundary Refinement Module fuse multi-scale features and sharpen boundaries, yielding 6.26M parameters and 7.87 GFLOPs at 80 FPS.

## Results

FGOS-Net achieves the best mIoU and clDice across all four crack benchmarks: 91.29% mIoU / 97.12% clDice on DeepCrack, 79.15% / 83.85% on Crack500, 80.75% / 92.48% on CrackMap, and 85.73% / 91.98% on TUT. In terms of efficiency, it uses only 6.26M parameters and 7.87 GFLOPs while running at 80.2 FPS on a single RTX 3090. On cross-domain thin-structure tasks, it reaches 80.45% mIoU / 83.35% clDice on CHASEDB1 (retinal vessels) and 79.83% / 80.86% on Massachusetts Roads (aerial roads), outperforming the strongest SSM baseline SCSegamba by +2.28/+6.05 and +4.42/+7.46 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGOS-Net outperforms all ten baselines on mIoU and clDice across every crack benchmark. On DeepCrack, it surpasses the strongest SSM baseline SCSegamba by +0.58% mIoU and +2.64% clDice, and beats the topology-specialized GLCP by +1.46% mIoU and +0.76% clDice. On CrackMap (texture-heavy), FGOS-Net's advantage is most pronounced: it leads CT-CrackSeg by +0.82% mIoU and +1.68% clDice, while SCSegamba collapses to 59.87% clDice due to severe texture leakage. On TUT (eight material domains), FGOS-Net exceeds FFM by +1.11% mIoU and +0.89% clDice, and SCSegamba by +2.13% mIoU and +6.99% clDice. In cross-domain transfer (Table 4), FGOS-Net improves over SCSegamba by +2.28% mIoU / +6.05% clDice on CHASEDB1 and +4.42% / +7.46% on Massachusetts Roads, confirming that the geometry-aligned serialization generalizes beyond cracks. Notably, FFM achieves the highest Recall on DeepCrack (95.93%) but at the cost of 81.22% Precision and 86.61% clDice, illustrating the texture-leakage trade-off that FGOS-Net's ASGP module explicitly addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VMamba VSS Block as its core SSM processing unit but repositions the serialization step from a fixed raster operation to a geometry-conditioned decision. It explicitly departs from prior SSM segmentation work (VMamba, SCSegamba, Swin-UMamba) by identifying 'serialization-induced geometry mismatch' as a distinct failure mode for anisotropic thin structures, where isotropic scanning forces state propagation orthogonal to the structure's dominant axis. The frequency decomposition (Haar DWT) and Hilbert indexing are treated as known tools repurposed as a geometry-revealing interface rather than standalone novelties; the conceptual contribution is the task-specific coupling between directional sub-bands and scan trajectory assignment (FA-Scan) plus topology-conditioned detail gating (ASGP).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why SSMs underperform on topology-critical thin structures, and the ablation chain cleanly isolates geometry-aligned serialization as the primary driver over branch diversity or parameter scaling. The efficiency profile (7.87 GFLOPs, 80 FPS, 6.26M params) makes it practically deployable, and cross-domain transfer to retinal vessels and aerial roads with identical hyperparameters suggests the alignment principle is not crack-specific. Open questions include whether the deterministic sub-band-to-trajectory assignment generalizes to structures with highly variable or multi-directional orientations, and whether adaptive (learned) scan routing can further improve performance without sacrificing the zero-parameter overhead of the current design.
