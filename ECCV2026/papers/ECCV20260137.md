## Problem

Existing proactive streaming video models reduce the 'when-to-speak' decision to point-wise binary classification at each frame, which produces flickering activations and poorly resolved transition boundaries. The paper argues that temporal transitions in streaming video naturally form span-structured activation patterns (onset, persistence, offset), and that a reliable activation module must model how activation states change over a temporal neighborhood rather than making isolated 0/1 decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260137/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The activation model (Qwen3-VL-2B, ~2B params) consumes streaming video frames sampled at 1 FPS, encoded into visual tokens and accumulated in a running visual cache, conditioned on a user query and prior interaction context. It outputs a binary activation sequence over a sliding window of size W, where each position is 0 (inactive), 1 (active), or [M] (masked). A trigger fires only when an active span is sustained for at least γ consecutive positions; upon triggering, the accumulated visual context since the last query is forwarded to a frozen downstream Video-LLM (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3-8B, Gemma3-4B) to generate a text response.

## Method

STRIDE replaces the per-frame binary head with a lightweight masked diffusion module that jointly predicts and iteratively refines activation signals over a sliding temporal window. During training, three structured masking strategies—boundary-anchored span masking, span unmasking, and full masking—corrupt contiguous regions of the activation sequence to force the model to reason about onset/offset from broader temporal context, while sequence duplication appends a copy of the activation region to enable bidirectional conditioning on the causal backbone without architectural modification. At inference, the window slides forward with each new frame; confident past decisions are retained via a confidence threshold τ, while uncertain positions are re-masked and resolved over K=8 progressive denoising steps that unmask high-confidence positions first to establish temporal anchors. This plug-and-play design decouples when-to-speak modeling from response generation, requiring only ~113 ms and 5.2 GB VRAM for the activation stage.

## Results

On ET-Bench, STRIDE (2B params) achieves 32.6 average F1, outperforming the autoregressive Baseline-AR (24.3) by 8.3 points and the 5B ETChat model (28.5) by 4.1 points. The gain is most dramatic on Temporal Video Grounding (TVG), where STRIDE scores 62.8 F1 versus 35.7 for Baseline-AR (+27.1). On OVO-Bench end-to-end streaming, adding STRIDE to InternVL3-8B improves overall score from 51.64 to 56.98 (+5.34), and to Gemma3-4B from 42.13 to 50.51 (+8.38), surpassing all streaming baselines (best: StreamAgent-7B at 49.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary ablation counterpart Baseline-AR (same architecture, autoregressive binary head), STRIDE wins decisively on TVG (62.8 vs 35.7, +27.1 F1) and overall average (32.6 vs 24.3, +8.3), confirming that structured span-level denoising substantially improves boundary resolution over point-wise prediction. STRIDE also outperforms the 5B ETChat model (28.5 avg) and the 13B LITA model (21.1 avg) despite using only 2B parameters, and beats the 8B Dispider (26.3) and StreamBridge baselines on average. On OVO-Bench end-to-end, STRIDE+InternVL3-8B (56.98) surpasses all streaming baselines including StreamAgent-7B (49.40) and QueryStream-7B (47.51), while STRIDE+Gemma3-4B (50.51) also exceeds them. The one case where STRIDE does not improve is Qwen3-VL-8B on OVO-Bench (51.77 both with and without STRIDE), suggesting the backbone's inherent temporal sensitivity may already be sufficient for that model. On StreamingBench, STRIDE consistently improves the Proactive Output (PO) subtask, which directly measures when-to-speak timing without explicit cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

STRIDE sits at the intersection of two research threads: masked diffusion language models (MDMs) such as LLaDA, which iteratively denoise partially masked discrete sequences, and the modular streaming-video architecture popularized by StreamBridge, which decouples a lightweight per-frame activation front-end from a frozen downstream Video-LLM. The key departure is reframing the activation sub-problem: prior modular approaches (Dispider, StreamBridge, StreamAgent) treat 'when to speak' as independent per-frame binary classification, whereas STRIDE models activation as a span-structured sequence over a sliding temporal window and applies structured span-aware masking strategies during training. A practical enabler is the sequence-duplication trick, which recovers bidirectional conditioning within a causal-attention backbone without architectural modification, allowing a compact AR-pretrained VLM to serve as the diffusion-based activation predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'when-to-speak' decision is a prerequisite for any real-time streaming assistant (egocentric helpers, autonomous driving, embodied agents), yet prior solutions produce flickering, poorly bounded triggers that degrade downstream response quality. STRIDE's plug-and-play design—frozen downstream Video-LLM plus a 2B activation module adding ~100 ms latency—makes it immediately deployable across existing Video-LLM backbones, and the 27-point TVG F1 gain over the autoregressive baseline demonstrates that span-level structure is not a minor refinement but a critical factor in boundary resolution. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher frame rates (>1 FPS), longer context windows, and multi-query or multi-agent streaming settings, and whether the span-structured denoising paradigm transfers to other sequential decision problems in streaming perception.
