## Problem

Existing multimodal model editing benchmarks rely on simplified perturbations (e.g., noise addition) that fail to capture the variability of real clinical deployment, where edits must remain reliable under image/text variation, modality and protocol shifts, compositional multi-finding cases, and longitudinal temporal progression. No systematic study has examined how gradient-based versus memory-based editing families behave under these clinically grounded stress tests, nor provided mechanistic explanations for their divergent failure modes in medical VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262768/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an edit request (a medical image from CT, X-ray, or MRI; a clinical question; and a target corrected answer) with probe questions that stress specific clinical axes—unrelated images for image-locality, semantically matched images for image-generality, modality-shifted counterparts, multi-finding compositions, and prior-to-follow-up temporal pairs. The benchmark spans 1,398 unique images and 16,276 questions across 9 anatomical sites and 4 modalities, with all scoring performed via free autoregressive generation rather than teacher-forced evaluation, and metrics macro-averaged over 200 sequential edit instances per task.

## Method

M³Bench redefines the standard reliability/locality/generality axes through five clinically motivated dimensions (image, text, modality, composition, temporality), yielding 10 tasks constructed via a two-stage pipeline: LLM-based clinical attribute distillation of raw QA pairs into structured schemas (condition, anatomical site, modality, progression status), followed by programmatic assembly of controlled evaluation sets that isolate one clinical variable while holding others fixed. The paper evaluates four representative editors—MEND and LoRA (gradient-based) and GRACE and BalancEdit (memory-based)—across six VLM backbones under both single and 200-edit sequential settings. A key mechanistic finding is the 'cone effect': pre-trained medical VLM representations concentrate in a narrow anisotropic cone (mean cosine similarity ~0.8, mean resultant length ~0.9), making localized edits intrinsically difficult; gradient-based editors induce global concept drift across all topic centroids (explaining catastrophic locality violations), while memory-based editors apply binary-gated edits within tight spherical boundaries that preserve locality but fail on compositional transfer where single- and multi-finding embeddings interleave. The authors further introduce BELoRA, a hybrid combining BalancEdit's key-based routing with LoRA adapters, and show that late+mid-layer interventions in the LM plus projector yield the best overall balance, while expanding to the vision encoder provides no consistent gains.

## Results

Across 4 medical VLMs (LLaVA-Med, BioMed-Qwen, Huatuo-7B, Huatuo-34B) and 10 clinically grounded tasks, no single editing method dominates all criteria. LoRA achieves the highest reliability (0.94–0.98) and image generality (0.95–0.98) but collapses on text locality (0.03–0.11), while BalancEdit (BE) attains the best overall harmonic mean on 3 of 4 backbones (0.46 LLaVA-Med, 0.39 BioQwen, 0.39 Huatuo-7B, 0.45 Huatuo-34B). Compositional generality and temporality remain the hardest axes for all editors, with best scores of only 0.68 (C-gen, LoRA/LLaVA-Med) and 0.72 (Temporality, LoRA/BioQwen) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four editing methods—two gradient-based (MEND, LoRA) and two memory-based (GRACE, BalancEdit)—across 6 VLM backbones. LoRA dominates on reliability (0.94–0.98) and image/text generality (up to 0.98 I-gen, 0.77 T-gen) but suffers catastrophic text-locality collapse (0.03–0.11), meaning edits corrupt unrelated questions on the same image. BalancEdit is the most well-rounded method, achieving the best overall harmonic mean on 3 of 4 medical backbones (0.46, 0.39, 0.39, 0.45) and the strongest text-locality scores (0.55–0.77), yet it lags LoRA by 0.25–0.35 on image generality and shows high backbone-dependent hyperparameter sensitivity (optimal α ranges from 0.05 to 0.2). MEND is consistently the weakest performer (overall 0.07–0.23), while GRACE occupies a middle position (0.07–0.27). Compositional generality and temporality remain unsolved for all methods, with no editor exceeding 0.68 on C-gen or 0.72 on temporality, indicating that current editing paradigms cannot yet handle multi-finding clinical cases or longitudinal progression coherently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the general knowledge-editing evaluation framework (Reliability, Locality, Generality from MEND [23] and subsequent work) to the medical multimodal setting, arguing that existing benchmarks (MMKE-Bench [9], MedMKEB [30]) rely on simplified perturbations that underestimate clinical variability. It repurposes four established editing methods—two gradient-based (MEND, LoRA) and two memory-based (GRACE, BalancEdit)—as evaluation subjects rather than proposing a new editor, while introducing a hybrid BELoRA design for ablation purposes. The mechanistic analysis draws on the anisotropy/cone-effect literature [19] to explain why editing behaves differently across backbones, connecting representation geometry to empirical editing failures in a way prior editing benchmarks have not attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M³Bench is the first clinically grounded stress test for multimodal model editing, and its 10-task suite (including modality shift, clinical composition, and temporal consistency) exposes failure modes invisible to general-domain benchmarks. The finding that medical fine-tuning *worsens* the latent-space cone effect—making representations more anisotropic and editing harder than on general VLMs—is a counterintuitive result with direct implications for how medical VLMs are trained and subsequently adapted. Open questions remain: compositional transfer and temporal consistency are unsolved by all current editors, and the backbone-dependent hyperparameter sensitivity of memory-based methods (especially the α radius) poses a practical barrier to deployment. The benchmark and its geometric analysis provide a concrete starting point for designing editors that respect clinical composition and longitudinal reasoning.
