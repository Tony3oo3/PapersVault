## Problem

Mechanistic circuit discovery in vision models has remained at the neuron or feature level, identifying which components matter but not how information is routed between them. Edge-based circuits, which explicitly model residual-stream connections and enable causal interventions at the connection level, have been developed and validated only for large language models. Whether faithful, sparse edge-based circuits can be meaningfully recovered in large-scale vision transformers remained an open question.

## Input / Output

For circuit discovery, the method consumes paired clean and corrupted images, where corruption is produced by segmenting the foreground object and inpainting the masked region (via the ForAug pipeline), removing class-specific evidence while preserving background statistics. The output is a sparse subset of residual-stream edges (the circuit) in a simplified computation graph where attention-head receiver nodes are collapsed into a single per-layer attention-input node, reducing the edge count by a factor of H. For steering, the method additionally produces per-component corruption-aligned direction vectors (v_j ∈ R^{P×d}) that are applied as directional ablations at sender hook locations during inference.

## Method

Vi-CD adapts sequential activation patching to vision transformers by iteratively pruning edges from a candidate circuit using target logit difference as the selection criterion: for each edge, the residual-stream contribution is swapped from the clean run if the edge is retained in the circuit, or from the corrupted (inpainted) run if removed, and the edge is pruned if the resulting logit-difference loss is below threshold. A key scalability innovation is the graph simplification that collapses multiple attention-head receiver nodes into a single shared attention-input node per layer, reducing O(L²H²) candidate edges by a factor of H without altering model functionality. For downstream steering, Vi-CD computes corruption-aligned direction vectors per circuit component as the mean normalized activation difference between clean and corrupted runs, then applies ReLU-gated directional ablation (projection removal) at sender outputs along circuit edges only, suppressing the attack-aligned signal while leaving unrelated computations intact. The approach is validated on ViT-B (supervised, ImageNet) and OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 (contrastive), recovering circuits up to 10× sparser than gradient-based baselines (EAP, EAP-IG) while preserving classification accuracy.

## Results

Vi-CD recovers class-specific circuits that are approximately 10× sparser than the strongest baseline (EAP-IG) while maintaining near-perfect classification accuracy on both ViT-B and OpenCLIP, retaining fewer than 10% of total edges. For typographic attack defense on OpenCLIP, circuit steering reduces Top-1 ASR from 39.1% to 2.8% (large text) and from 39.4% to 1.6% (small text), with only a modest clean-accuracy drop (57.0% → 55.8%). On the RoCOCO danger benchmark, steering with weapon-related circuits more than halves the Recall Score of Manipulated Samples (RSMS) from 11.68% to ~5%, while maintaining or slightly improving retrieval recall (R_mean up to 64.37%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vi-CD is compared against two gradient-based circuit discovery methods adapted from the language domain: EAP (Syed et al., 2024) and EAP-IG (Hanna et al., 2024, with step counts 3, 5, and 10), plus random edge pruning as a lower bound. Vi-CD dramatically outperforms both baselines: on ViT-B, it achieves near-perfect classification accuracy while retaining fewer than 10% of edges, whereas EAP-IG requires substantially more edges to reach comparable performance and EAP remains close to random pruning throughout. The same pattern holds on OpenCLIP, where Vi-CD achieves high faithfulness at extreme sparsity levels while baselines plateau at low accuracy until a much larger fraction of edges is included. Among the baselines, EAP-IG consistently outperforms EAP, with only marginal differences across its step-count variants. The paper also confirms that the steering effect is specific to discovered circuits: random circuits of the same size do not produce comparable ASR reductions (Appendix G), ruling out a general ablation artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the sequential activation-patching circuit discovery framework of Conmy et al. (ACD, NeurIPS 2023) from language models to vision transformers, introducing the first edge-based circuit discovery method for ViTs. Prior vision circuit work (Cammarata et al., Rajaram et al., Dreyer et al.) operates at the neuron or feature level, identifying which components matter but not how information flows between them; the edge-centric formulation has so far been confined to LLMs (EAP, EAP-IG, RelP, CD-T). Vi-CD bridges this gap by formalizing the residual-stream computation graph of a ViT, reducing its edge count via an attention-input node abstraction, and constructing clean/corrupted input pairs through segmentation-and-inpainting (ForAug) to make activation patching tractable for spatially distributed patch embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical steering results—reducing typographic attack success rates by over 90% and halving RoCOCO safety violations while preserving clean accuracy—demonstrate that edge-based circuits in vision are not merely descriptive but actionable, offering a concrete path toward targeted model correction without retraining. Key limitations include the computational cost of sequential per-edge patching (scaling to the largest ViTs remains open), the dependence on paired clean/corrupted inputs that may not generalize trivially to open-ended tasks, and the observation that circuits for the same class vary across runs, suggesting a distribution over plausible subgraphs rather than a single ground-truth circuit. Extending the framework to SAE-derived feature directions, grounding/retrieval tasks, and training-dynamics analysis are natural next steps.
