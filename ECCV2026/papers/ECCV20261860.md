## Problem

Self-supervised image restoration under signal-dependent Poisson noise remains difficult because standard self-supervised losses assume additive Gaussian noise, and pixel-space learned priors are brittle under distribution shifts and non-Gaussian degradation. Additionally, foundation vision encoders like CLIP have not been effectively integrated into operator-aware inverse problem solvers that can handle diverse forward operators (CFA mosaicing, convolutional blur) without expensive fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single Poisson-corrupted measurement vector y (either a single-channel CFA mosaiced image or a 3-channel blurred RGB image at 256×256) together with the known linear forward operator A (a binary CFA masking matrix or a 9×9 Gaussian blur kernel). It outputs a full 3-channel RGB reconstruction at the same spatial resolution. The forward operator is not learned; it is provided as part of the problem specification, and the solver's data-consistency step is tailored to its algebraic structure (diagonal for CFA, block-circulant for convolution).

## Method

The solver unrolls T iterations (T=2 for demosaicing, T=3 for deblurring) of a scaled ADMM scheme, alternating a closed-form data-consistency update (element-wise division for CFA, FFT-based division for deblurring) with a plug-and-play prior step. The prior is a frozen CLIP RN50 encoder producing dense multi-scale features (H/2 down to H/16) fed into a lightweight U-Net-like decoder (~11M trainable parameters, shared across all iterations) that maps features back to an RGB estimate. For self-supervised training without clean ground truth, the loss couples GR2R measurement-domain re-corruption (using binomial splitting to respect Poisson statistics) with an Equivariant Imaging regularizer that generates virtual acquisitions via operator-appropriate transformations (random rotations for CFA, random scaling for deblurring) to prevent degenerate solutions and enforce learning beyond the range space of A.

## Results

On Poisson CFA demosaicing (BSDS500, γ=0.01), the self-supervised variant achieves 30.53 dB PSNR / 0.8609 SSIM, and the supervised variant reaches 30.75 dB / 0.8703 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline RAM (30.46 dB) and GSPnP (29.25 dB). Under severe noise (γ=0.05), Ours (Self) attains 26.98 dB PSNR, exceeding GSPnP by +0.48 dB and RAM by +0.81 dB. On Poisson deblurring (BSDS500, γ=0.05), Ours (Sup) achieves 27.58 dB / 0.7613 SSIM, improving over GSPnP by +0.55 dB and +0.039 SSIM. The method also runs at 0.09 TFLOPs and 0.024 s per image, roughly 100× faster than Transfer CLIP and 20–40× faster than DPIR/GSPnP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPIR [23], the proposed method wins by large margins across all settings (e.g., +4.57 dB on demosaicing at γ=0.01, +5.27 dB at γ=0.05), confirming that a frozen-CLIP prior with operator-aware ADMM unrolling substantially outperforms a generic CNN denoiser in a PnP loop. Transfer CLIP [5] is the closest architectural relative (same frozen CLIP RN50 + lightweight decoder) but lacks explicit data-consistency and Poisson-specific self-supervision; the proposed method beats it by +2.65 dB (γ=0.01) and +5.07 dB (γ=0.05) on demosaicing, with the gap widening sharply under stronger noise. GSPnP [10] is the strongest iterative PnP baseline: the proposed self-supervised variant edges it out by +0.48 dB on demosaicing (γ=0.05, BSDS500) and by +0.42 to +0.49 dB on deblurring (γ=0.05), while the supervised variant extends these margins to +0.55–0.67 dB. RAM [20] is the only baseline that slightly outperforms Ours (Self) on DIV2K demosaicing at mild noise (30.48 vs. 30.30 dB, a 0.18 dB gap), but the proposed method surpasses RAM on BSDS500 at both noise levels and on all deblurring settings, with the advantage growing to +1.68 dB at γ=0.05 deblurring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active threads: (i) unrolled plug-and-play solvers that embed physics-driven data-consistency steps (ADMM, HQS) into trainable networks, (ii) the Transfer CLIP line of work showing that frozen CLIP RN50 dense multi-scale features serve as distortion-invariant, content-related representations for a lightweight decoder, and (iii) Poisson-specific self-supervised objectives (GR2R for signal-dependent noise, Equivariant Imaging for learning beyond the range space). The key departure from Transfer CLIP is the explicit operator-aware ADMM data-consistency step and the Poisson-tailored self-supervision, while the departure from classical PnP (DPIR, GSPnP) is the replacement of a pixel-space denoiser with a frozen foundation-model encoder plus compact decoder, reducing trainable parameters and improving cross-dataset robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is strong for photon-limited imaging (microscopy, low-light photography, medical imaging) where clean ground truth is unavailable and Poisson noise invalidates Gaussian-assumption methods. The ~100× inference speedup over Transfer CLIP and ~20–40× over DPIR/GSPnP, combined with self-supervised performance within ~0.2 dB of supervised, makes this a viable lightweight deployment option. Limitations include evaluation on only two forward operators (CFA, Gaussian blur), two datasets, and two noise levels; whether the frozen-CLIP prior generalizes to more ill-conditioned or nonlinear Poisson problems (e.g., compressed sensing, spectral imaging) remains open. The ablation showing DnCNN/DRUNet backbones within ~0.6 dB of CLIP under self-supervised training also raises the question of whether the foundation-model inductive bias is truly necessary or whether a well-regularized small CNN suffices.
