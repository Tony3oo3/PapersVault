## Problem

Conventional style transfer methods operate in a One-to-One manner, transferring style from a single or few reference artworks and thus failing to capture an artist's broader stylistic distribution. Text-to-image diffusion models conditioned on artist names (e.g., 'in Van Gogh style') offer more flexibility but suffer from text-induced bias, collapsing the artist's diverse style into a narrow set of iconic compositions. Neither paradigm reliably reproduces the coherent, global visual identity that defines an artist across their full body of work.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single content image (photographic scenes from VanGogh2Photo) and a set of K style exemplar artworks from a target artist (drawn from WikiArt's 80,000+ artworks across 1,000+ artists) as input. During training, the style exemplars are paired with a fixed generic prompt ('A painting') to eliminate text variance; at inference, only the content image and the pre-trained Style Extraction Function are needed. The output is a single artistically stylized image that reflects the artist's global style distribution while preserving the content's semantic structure, with the Artist Style Book (top-6 CLIP-similar real artworks) provided as a qualitative reference.

## Method

The core contribution is a Many-to-One Global Style Transfer paradigm realized through two components. Global Style Guidance (GSG) trains a lightweight MLP (Style Extraction Function) in the U-Net bottleneck h-space to predict a residual global style offset Δh_t, using noise reconstruction loss under a fixed prompt so that style semantics are learned purely from visual statistics across hundreds of artworks; zero-initialization ensures training starts from an unbiased state. Content Alignment Guidance (CAG) is a training-free mechanism that first maps the content image into a noisy latent via DDIM inversion, then at each diffusion timestep applies CLIP-based perceptual guidance (using Tweedie's formula to approximate the clean image) to preserve semantic structure while permitting artist-specific geometric deformation. The asymmetric reverse process formulation from Asyrp is used so that only the predicted-clean component is modulated by Δh_t while the denoising direction is preserved, maintaining reconstruction fidelity. Time-step robustness is validated via t-SNE showing well-separated artist clusters in h-space across all diffusion timesteps.

## Results

On WikiArt across three artists (Van Gogh, Chagall, Renoir), GST achieves the highest CLIP-Diversity (0.297, 0.311, 0.318) and 1-Precision (0.988, 0.977, 0.999) among all compared methods, indicating superior stylistic diversity and memorization avoidance. FID and ArtFID are competitive but not always best: Textual Inversion achieves lower ArtFID for Van Gogh (13.68 vs. 19.25) and Chagall (21.15 vs. 26.39), while GST matches or beats baselines on FID for Renoir (12.27). CFSD (content preservation) is higher (worse) for GST on Van Gogh (0.2896) and Chagall (0.2626) compared to Custom Diffusion (0.1208, 0.1108), reflecting a trade-off between global style expressiveness and strict content fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GST consistently outperforms all four personalization baselines (Stable Diffusion, Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion, LoRA fine-tuning) on CLIP-Diversity and 1-Precision across all three artists, with CLIP-Div values closely matching those of the real artwork corpus (e.g., 0.297 vs. 0.335 for Van Gogh). However, Textual Inversion achieves substantially lower ArtFID for Van Gogh (13.68 vs. 19.25) and Chagall (21.15 vs. 26.39), and Custom Diffusion achieves the best CFSD for Van Gogh (0.1208) and Chagall (0.1108), indicating stronger content preservation at the cost of stylistic diversity. Against the style transfer baselines (StyleInjection, StyTR², CAST, S2WAT, StyleSSP, StyleAligned), comparison is qualitative only (Fig. 5): these One-to-One methods produce inconsistent, blurred results when averaged across multiple artworks, whereas GST yields coherent artist-level stylization. The ablation (Table 3) confirms CAG's role: removing it increases FID from 9.46 to 11.05 and ArtFID from 19.25 to 22.45 on Van Gogh. Overall, GST trades some content fidelity (higher CFSD) for significantly broader stylistic coverage, positioning it as a diversity-first approach rather than a fidelity-first one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the implicit function architecture (a lightweight MLP operating on U-Net bottleneck features) introduced by Asyrp [13] for instance-level semantic editing, but fundamentally reorients its training objective: instead of learning a text-driven attribute offset between two prompts, the Style Extraction Function is trained on hundreds of artworks under a single fixed prompt ('A painting'), learning purely visual, distribution-level style semantics. It also builds on DDIM inversion and CLIP-based perceptual guidance (as in prior inversion-based style transfer methods like InST and Diff-NST) for content preservation, but applies them in a training-free manner alongside the learned global style offset. The paper positions itself as a departure from both the One-to-One style transfer paradigm (NST, StyleInjection, StyleAligned) and text-conditioned T2I personalization (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion), framing its Many-to-One Global Style Transfer as a distinct formulation that avoids both instance-level overfitting and text-induced artistic bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: neither single-reference style transfer nor artist-name-prompted T2I models reliably capture an artist's full stylistic distribution, and the Many-to-One formulation with text-independent visual supervision offers a principled path forward. The finding that h-space clusters remain well-separated across diffusion timesteps provides useful evidence for where style guidance can be most effectively applied. However, the method requires per-artist SEF training (not zero-shot), is evaluated on only three artists, and the quantitative results show a trade-off—improved diversity and memorization avoidance come at the cost of somewhat higher ArtFID and CFSD compared to fine-tuning baselines like Textual Inversion. Open questions include scalability to artists with highly heterogeneous styles, whether the approach generalizes to living artists with evolving visual vocabularies, and whether the per-artist training cost can be reduced.
