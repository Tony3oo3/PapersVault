## Problem

Existing EEG-to-3D reconstruction methods, such as Neuro-3D, employ a uniform diffusion decoder throughout the entire denoising process, ignoring that both EEG visual representations and diffusion generation operate on a coarse-to-fine semantic hierarchy. Low-frequency EEG components encode global object structure while high-frequency components carry local geometric detail, yet a fixed architecture cannot adapt its spatial granularity to match this evolving hierarchy across diffusion timesteps.

## Input / Output

The method takes 64-channel EEG recordings at 1000 Hz, split into a 0.5-second static image-evoked segment and a 6-second rotating-video-evoked segment per trial, preprocessed with the MNE toolbox. The output is a 3D point cloud of 2048 points (downsampled from 8192 via Farthest Point Sampling), evaluated for geometric fidelity via Chamfer Distance and Earth Mover's Distance, and for semantic alignment via N-way Top-k classification with a retrained PointNet++ classifier.

## Method

Mind2Cloud introduces a two-granularity diffusion decoder that integrates a global Transformer branch (with a learned spatial interaction matrix for position-aware attention) and a local PVCNN branch, fused via a timestep-conditioned learnable mask M_t ∈ (0,1)^D generated from sinusoidal timestep embeddings. During early denoising steps (high noise), the mask emphasizes the Transformer to recover coarse global structure; in later steps, it shifts weight toward PVCNN for local geometric refinement. A two-stream EEG encoder separately encodes static and dynamic signals with Transformer encoders, fuses them via cross-attention, and aligns the resulting embedding with CLIP image features through symmetric InfoNCE and MSE losses. A lightweight adversarial discriminator (stacked residual Conv1D blocks + global pooling) provides additional geometric realism supervision. The full model is trained end-to-end with a weighted combination of diffusion noise-prediction loss, EEG-CLIP alignment loss, and adversarial generator loss (α=0.95, λ_adv=0.05).

## Results

On the EEG-3D dataset (5 subjects, S01–S05), Mind2Cloud achieves a Chamfer Distance of 2.53×10² and EMD of 14.02×10², substantially outperforming the Neuro-3D baseline (CD 4.32×10², EMD 16.31×10²). For semantic fidelity, Mind2Cloud reaches 52.92% average 2-way/top-1 accuracy and 32.47% 10-way/top-3 accuracy, compared to Neuro-3D's 50.80% and 31.72%. Extended to all 12 subjects (S01–S12), the method maintains CD 2.65×10² and EMD 14.43×10², confirming cross-subject robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Neuro-3D (CVPR 2025), the first EEG-to-3D reconstruction framework, which also provides the EEG-3D dataset and evaluation protocol. Mind2Cloud outperforms Neuro-3D on every reported metric: CD improves from 4.32 to 2.53 (−41%), EMD from 16.31 to 14.02 (−14%), 2-way/top-1 average accuracy from 50.80% to 52.92%, and 10-way/top-3 average accuracy from 31.72% to 32.47%. The Top-1-of-5 semantic scores also improve (68.33%→70.83% for 2-way, 53.89%→56.67% for 10-way). Notably, Mind2Cloud operates at 2048 points (via FPS downsampling) while Neuro-3D was originally trained at 8192 points, yet still achieves superior geometric and semantic fidelity. Ablation variants (removing the discriminator, PVCNN branch, or replacing timestep-aware fusion with fixed weights) all degrade relative to the full model, confirming that the two-granularity design and adversarial refinement are both necessary for the observed gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EEG-to-3D reconstruction paradigm established by Neuro-3D (CVPR 2025), which first demonstrated feasibility of generating 3D shapes from non-invasive EEG signals using a diffusion-based point cloud generator. Architecturally, it draws on TIGER (2024), which combined convolutional and Transformer modules for fMRI-based 3D decoding, but departs by introducing a timestep-adaptive fusion mechanism that dynamically shifts between a global Transformer branch (early, high-noise denoising) and a local PVCNN branch (late, detail-refinement stages). The two-stream EEG encoder (static + dynamic) follows the Neuro-3D design, while the key conceptual departure is aligning the decoder's spatial granularity with both the hierarchical frequency structure of EEG signals and the coarse-to-fine trajectory of diffusion denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EEG-to-3D reconstruction is a nascent direction with only one prior method (Neuro-3D), so establishing a stronger baseline with clear ablations is valuable for the small but growing community. The timestep-aware global/local fusion strategy is a transferable idea that could inform other diffusion-based 3D generation tasks where early steps benefit from long-range modeling and later steps from local refinement. However, the paper acknowledges significant open questions: no photorealistic appearance (color/texture) is reconstructed, cross-subject generalization remains imperfect, and the evaluation relies on a single external baseline with a retrained classifier, leaving the true magnitude of improvement somewhat uncertain. Future work on vision-language priors for color and domain adaptation for cross-subject robustness are natural next steps.
