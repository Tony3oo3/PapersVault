## Problem

Vision-language models trained with standard next-token prediction lack explicit control over visual representation learning, causing visual embeddings to be passively optimized and inject redundant or spurious signals into cross-modal attention. This manifests as object hallucination, attention sinks, and catastrophic forgetting after full-parameter instruction tuning, because task-irrelevant visual noise propagates through intermediate transformer layers and dilutes query-relevant evidence.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard VLM pipelines (InternVL2, InternVL2.5, LLaVA-OneVision) where RGB images are encoded into visual token sequences and concatenated with text tokens as LLM input. The stochastic regularization is applied specifically to the visual components of value states within the attention module, targeting mid-to-late transformer layers (60%–80% depth). At inference time, the noise is removed and only the learned mean-shift residual Δ_φ(v) is applied, so the output remains a deterministic text answer.

## Method

IRA formulates information regularization as a layer-wise variational inference problem: a lightweight posterior sampler (a linear layer predicting mean shift and variance) is attached to each target transformer layer, producing stochastic visual value states z = v + Δ_φ(v) + σ_q·ε via the reparameterization trick before attention computation. The prior is a data-dependent Gaussian centered on the gradient-detached value state, preventing trivial posterior-prior collapse while anchoring regularization to pretrained representations. An uncertainty-conditioned weighting mechanism adaptively scales the KL penalty per visual token using a product of attention entropy and inverse importance score, so that low-attention, high-entropy tokens receive stronger regularization while semantically critical visual details are preserved. The final objective combines the standard language reconstruction loss with a scheduled β-weighted sum of per-layer KL divergences, and the mechanism is applied only to mid-to-late layers where representations are sufficiently abstract to benefit from compression while still retaining grounding signals.

## Results

On InternVL2.5-8B, IRA improves MME from 1981 (SFT) to 2038 (+57), MMMU from 46.4 to 47.6, and OK-VQA from 39.8 to 43.6 over standard SFT. On LLaVA-OneVision-8B, IRA yields the largest single-benchmark gain on OK-VQA (41.7→47.4, +5.7) and MME (2030→2109, +79). Across all three model families, IRA consistently outperforms SFT on reasoning-intensive benchmarks (MMMU, MME, MME-RW, MMBench) while maintaining competitive or slightly better scores on text-intensive tasks (TextVQA, DocVQA, ChartQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; instead, it benchmarks IRA against two internal baselines: the pretrained checkpoint (Pt) and standard full-parameter supervised fine-tuning (SFT). IRA consistently outperforms SFT on reasoning-heavy benchmarks: MME improves by +57 to +167 points across the three model families, MMMU by +0.6 to +1.2, and MME-RW by +0.4 to +2.9. The largest single gain is on OK-VQA for LLaVA-OneVision (+5.7 over SFT), suggesting IRA particularly benefits knowledge-intensive visual reasoning. On text-intensive tasks (TextVQA, DocVQA, ChartQA), IRA matches or slightly exceeds SFT, confirming that the uncertainty-conditioned weighting prevents over-regularization of fine-grained visual details. IRA slightly underperforms SFT on MMStar for InternVL2.5 (58.8 vs. 61.1) and MME-RW for LLaVA-OV (40.0 vs. 40.9), indicating minor trade-offs on certain perception-focused subtasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information bottleneck framework (Tishby et al., Alemi et al.) from a global compression objective to a layer-wise, data-dependent variational regularization applied directly to attention value states in VLMs. It departs from prior attention-sink mitigation work (gated attention, attention distribution optimization) by regularizing the input representations rather than the attention weights themselves, and from VIB-Probe by using the information bottleneck as an internal training regularizer rather than an external diagnostic. The data-dependent prior design draws on Dziugaite et al. and Li et al., while the layer-wise stochastic structure is inspired by Ladder-VAE, though without explicit bottom-up/top-down pathways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes attention in VLMs from a passive weighting mechanism to an active representation-learning channel, offering a principled way to control how much visual information enters cross-modal reasoning. The curvature-based diagnostic linking representational geometry to attention sink provides a new interpretability tool for multimodal architectures. However, experiments are limited to 8B-parameter models and single-image training data, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or benefits pre-training. The modest 1–2 point gains on most benchmarks suggest the technique is a useful but not transformative component, and its practical adoption will depend on whether the added stochasticity and training complexity justify the improvements in production settings.
