## Problem

Test-time adaptation methods are evaluated exclusively by task accuracy, leaving unexamined whether adaptation preserves the model's reliance on task-relevant features or silently increases dependence on spurious correlations. This paper shows that across five TTA methods and five benchmarks, adaptation consistently shifts concept sensitivity away from causal/pathology-related concepts and toward nuisance/artifact concepts—a phenomenon termed Concept Sensitivity Drift—even when accuracy improves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260563/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained model (ResNet-50, WRN-28-10, DenseNet-121, or ViT-B/16), a stream of unlabeled target images, and a set of frozen Concept Activation Vectors learned offline via logistic regression on source-domain activations at a chosen penultimate layer (200 positive + 200 random examples per concept). During adaptation, only batch-normalization affine parameters (γ, β) are updated; no target labels, CLIP inference, or architectural modifications are required at test time. The output is the adapted model's class predictions (accuracy, worst-group accuracy) or AUC scores for multi-label medical findings, with CSD reported as a diagnostic side-product.

## Method

CS-TTA is a source-concept-supervised, target-label-free plug-in regularizer added to any existing TTA objective as λ·L_preserve. It first computes a batch-level mean sensitivity S̄_C for each monitored concept via the TCAV directional derivative, then selectively penalizes concepts whose drift from the source sensitivity exceeds a threshold τ, using a stop-gradient on the source value as a fixed target. To avoid second-order Hessian terms, the default implementation replaces the exact sensitivity with a differentiable CAV-projection surrogate P_C = a^(l)(x;θ)ᵀ v_C, which preserves the concept-direction component of the activation at O(|C|·B·d) overhead (1.1–2.8% wall-clock). The threshold τ serves a dual role: it avoids constraining concepts unaffected by adaptation and limits the number of active regularization terms, while λ controls overall strength. Because the regularizer operates on internal activations and frozen CAV directions, it is agnostic to the form of L_TTA and which parameters are updated, making it composable with Tent, EATA, CoTTA, SAR, and DeYO without modification.

## Results

Across five TTA baselines and five benchmarks, CS-TTA consistently improves worst-group accuracy on spurious-correlation settings: Tent+CS-TTA raises Waterbirds WG from 56.2% to 65.8% (+9.6 pts) and DeYO+CS-TTA reaches 68.5% (+4.7 pts over DeYO alone). On cross-hospital chest X-ray adaptation (CheXpert→MIMIC-CXR), CS-TTA improves AUC by 1.4–1.8 points over all baselines (e.g., 0.762 source → 0.788 with DeYO+CS-TTA). On corruption benchmarks, CS-TTA adds 0.6–1.4 points overall accuracy (e.g., CIFAR-10-C: 74.5% source → 81.8% with DeYO+CS-TTA; ImageNet-C: 39.2% → 46.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CS-TTA is evaluated as a plug-in on top of five TTA methods (Tent, EATA, CoTTA, SAR, DeYO) and consistently outperforms each baseline without CS-TTA on every benchmark. The largest gains appear on spurious-correlation benchmarks: Tent+CS-TTA recovers Waterbirds worst-group accuracy from 56.2% (Tent alone, which degrades below the 58.1% source) to 65.8%, and CoTTA+CS-TTA lifts WG from 55.1% (CoTTA alone) toward the source level. DeYO, the strongest baseline, still benefits: DeYO+CS-TTA reaches 68.5% WG versus 63.8% for DeYO alone (+4.7 pts). On the medical domain, all five baselines improve AUC over the 0.762 source, but CS-TTA adds a further 1.4–1.8 AUC points (e.g., 0.778 for Tent+CS-TTA vs. ~0.764 for Tent alone), indicating that the AUC gains of standard TTA partly come from exploiting hospital-specific artifacts. On corruption benchmarks (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C), the gains are smaller (0.6–1.4 pts) but consistent across all five baselines, with DeYO+CS-TTA achieving the best overall accuracy (81.8% / 46.5%). No baseline is beaten by CS-TTA on any metric; the regularizer is strictly additive and never degrades performance relative to the corresponding TTA method alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes TCAV (Kim et al., ICML 2018), originally designed as a static interpretability tool for fixed models, as a dynamic diagnostic that tracks concept sensitivity throughout the TTA adaptation process. It positions itself against the five mainstream TTA methods (Tent, EATA, CoTTA, SAR, DeYO), all of which evaluate success solely through task accuracy, by introducing Concept Sensitivity Drift as an orthogonal evaluation axis. Unlike CONDA, which requires a concept bottleneck architecture, and PatchSAE, which analyzes sparse-autoencoder feature remapping, CS-TTA operates as a frozen, external plug-in regularizer on standard models without altering the prediction pipeline or trainable parameter set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that accuracy gains during TTA can coincide with increased reliance on spurious or institutional artifacts is practically significant for deployment trustworthiness, particularly in medical imaging where models are already known to exploit hospital-specific markers. CS-TTA offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic mitigation, but its limitations are notable: it requires source-domain concept examples to train CAVs, CSD is correlational rather than causal, and preserving a sub-optimal source profile can constrain improvement. Open questions include distinguishing beneficial from harmful drift, extending to continual TTA settings, and whether concept discovery methods (e.g., ACE) can reduce the manual annotation burden.
