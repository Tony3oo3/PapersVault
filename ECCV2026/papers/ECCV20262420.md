## Problem

Existing diffusion-based relighting methods lack an explicit mechanism to learn illumination-specific feature transformations between source and target images: control-based approaches depend on accurate auxiliary signals, decomposition-based methods propagate intrinsic estimation errors, and image-to-image translation treats relighting as generic instance-level mapping without isolating lighting features. Additionally, publicly available portrait relighting datasets remain scarce in complex, multi-source illumination configurations, limiting both training and evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB portrait image and a free-form text prompt describing the desired lighting change (specifying direction, configuration such as Rembrandt or Tyndall lighting, and photometric properties like color temperature and shadow softness) as conditioning. It outputs a relit RGB image in the same spatial layout as the source. The source image is injected as a conditioning signal into the velocity network at every denoising step, while the text prompt is encoded and concatenated to guide the generative trajectory.

## Method

CFT builds on rectified flow and jointly trains three objectives: noise-to-target generation (L1), noise-to-source reconstruction under a neutral condition (L2), and a source-to-target transport loss (L3) computed via the parallelogram law to approximate the direct path velocity. The key insight is that L3 is supervised not with the original (x_src, x_tgt) pair but with an alternative pair (x_src', x_tgt') that shares the same illumination transformation yet differs in identity and scene content, forcing the model to learn illumination-consistent feature transport rather than fitting instance-specific pixel differences. The final loss is L1 + L2 + α·L3 with α=0.1, balancing generative fidelity against transport consistency. The framework is backbone-agnostic and has been validated on both Flux-Kontext and Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, and generalizes to style transfer by swapping the illumination-consistent pairs for style-consistent triplets.

## Results

On the constructed Portrait Relighting Dataset, Flux-Kontext with CFT achieves the best performance across all five metrics: SSIM 0.9202, PSNR 23.51, LPIPS 0.0946, FID 18.73, and IE 7.42. Qwen-Image-Edit with CFT also leads among its backbone variants (SSIM 0.8894, FID 25.55, IE 10.87). In the transfer-learning experiment on the Multi-Illumination Dataset, CFT-trained Flux-Kontext improves SSIM from 0.5431 (original) to 0.7325 and reduces IE from 29.35 to 27.35 without any additional fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFT (Flux-Kontext) outperforms all six baselines on every metric. Against the control-based IC-light, CFT reduces FID from 47.32 to 18.73 and IE from 15.36 to 7.42, while also improving SSIM from 0.7436 to 0.9202, demonstrating that explicit illumination feature transport is far more effective than environment-map conditioning. Against the decomposition-based Latent Intrinsic (the strongest non-CFT baseline), CFT gains +0.078 SSIM, +5.33 PSNR, and reduces FID from 35.06 to 18.73, confirming that intrinsic decomposition errors propagate and limit relighting quality. Against the image-to-image translation baselines LBM and FlowEdit, CFT achieves substantially lower FID (18.73 vs. 40.87 and 78.12) and IE (7.42 vs. 16.93 and 23.22), showing that instance-level transport without explicit illumination modeling is insufficient for complex lighting. Compared to the original pretrained Flux-Kontext (no fine-tuning), CFT-trained Flux-Kontext improves SSIM from 0.6386 to 0.9202 and FID from 53.38 to 18.73, underscoring the necessity of relighting-specific training data. The ablation further shows that CFT's advantage over the w/o-CFT variant (FID 20.38→18.73, IE 7.82→7.42) is consistent but moderate, indicating that the base model already captures much of the task while CFT refines illumination consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the rectified flow framework and the parallelogram-law approximation for inversion-free editing (as in FlowEdit and FireFlow), but departs from prior relighting methods by introducing a cross-instance supervision strategy for the source-to-target transport loss. Where control-based methods (IC-light, LightIt) rely on explicit illumination conditions and decomposition-based methods (Intrinsic Edit, Latent Intrinsic) operate in intrinsic spaces, CFT instead reformulates relighting as illumination feature transport and supervises the direct transport pathway with an alternative image pair sharing the same lighting transformation but different content. The approach is demonstrated as a plug-in training principle on top of existing flow-matching backbones (Flux-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-instance transport supervision offers a clean, generalizable principle for isolating attribute-specific features during image editing, and its successful transfer to style transfer suggests applicability beyond relighting. The 34K-pair portrait relighting dataset with complex, multi-source lighting fills a notable gap in publicly available resources. Open questions include whether the method scales to video relighting or 3D-consistent editing, how it behaves when matched-illumination pairs are scarce, and whether the consistency–diversity trade-off observed in style transfer (slightly worse FID) becomes problematic for other one-to-many editing tasks.
