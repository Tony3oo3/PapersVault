## Problem

Existing human image animation methods struggle to simultaneously control arbitrary human motion trajectories and camera movements within a reconstructed 3D scene. Specifically, they lack user-friendly trajectory control decoupled from source actions, fail to adapt human motion to uneven scene geometry (causing floating or ground penetration), and cannot jointly handle camera dynamics and human motion without inter-frame flicker or physical inconsistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261068/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is entirely training-free and takes four inputs: a reference subject-scene RGB image (from which a metric 3D point cloud is reconstructed via Depth-Pro), a user-drawn 2D motion trajectory on the X-Z plane, a SMPL-X action sequence (from a source video or text-to-motion model), and a camera trajectory parameterized by intrinsics K and N frames of extrinsics [R_n | T_n]. It outputs a photorealistic video of the reference subject performing the target actions along the specified 3D trajectory, adapting altitude and orientation to scene ground elevation, while the camera follows the prescribed path. The frame count N for camera viewpoints can differ from the action sequence length, though the paper keeps them equal for simplicity.

## Method

The pipeline operates in a unified world-coordinate space where the SMPL-X action mesh and scene point cloud are aligned via least-squares rigid transformation. The first key component is ground-adaptive 3D motion retargeting: a 2D trajectory is mapped to X-Z displacements, local ground height is estimated by averaging the Y-coordinates of the lowest 5% of points within a 0.15 m cylindrical neighborhood, and the pelvis Y-coordinate is shifted by the altitude gap relative to the start frame; orientation is then derived from temporally smoothed (Gaussian kernel, σ=3) yaw angles of trajectory tangents. The second key component is a viewpoint-adaptive scene-visibility guided latent fusion: for each frame, a binary mask is computed by comparing projected human and scene depths, and during DDIM denoising the predicted latents are fused with forward-diffused point-cloud latents via this mask weighted by an exponentially decaying factor λ(t) = λ_max·exp(β(t−T)) (λ_max ∈ [0.7, 0.95], β ≈ 0.1–0.3 for T=50 steps), so early steps strongly anchor global scene geometry while later steps free the model to synthesize fine-grained human appearance. The entire approach is built on the pre-trained RealisDance DiT backbone (Wan-2.1 with Flow Matching) and requires no additional training or fine-tuning.

## Results

On RealisDance-Val, 3STC-HIA achieves the best Background Consistency (96.08), Motion Smoothness (98.83), and Aesthetic Quality (59.47) among all compared methods, while remaining competitive on Subject Consistency (94.13 vs. 94.58 for RealisMotion) and Temporal Flicker (97.51 vs. 97.82 for Uni3C). On Trajectory100, it achieves a Translation Error of 0.478 m, substantially outperforming RealisMotion (1.305 m) and RealisDance-DiT (1.815 m), with a Yaw Error of 0.162° comparable to RealisDance-DiT (0.158°). FID (27.92) and FVD (573.41) are slightly worse than the best baselines (Uni3C: 23.86/516.36; RealisDance: 25.13/305.84), which the authors attribute to prioritizing trajectory and scene-layout fidelity over statistical distribution matching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3STC-HIA is the only method in the comparison that simultaneously supports action control, motion trajectory control, and camera trajectory control. On RealisDance-Val, it leads in Background Consistency (+0.21 over RealisMotion), Motion Smoothness (+1.22 over RealisMotion), and Aesthetic Quality (+2.55 over RealisMotion), while trailing slightly on Subject Consistency (94.13 vs. 94.58 for RealisMotion) and FID/FVD (27.92/573.41 vs. 24.10/519.66 for RealisMotion). On Trajectory100, it dramatically reduces Translation Error to 0.478 m versus 1.305 m for RealisMotion and 1.815 m for RealisDance-DiT, but its Yaw Error (0.162°) is slightly higher than RealisMotion (0.095°) and comparable to RealisDance-DiT (0.158°). Among camera-control-capable baselines, Uni3C achieves the best FID (23.86) and Dynamic Degree (73.24), while HumanVid lags significantly on most perceptual metrics. Overall, 3STC-HIA trades small FID/FVD and yaw-accuracy costs for substantially better trajectory fidelity and scene-geometry coherence, which the authors argue is the more practically relevant objective for controllable animation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D world-space human image animation line initiated by Uni3C and RealisMotion, which separately addressed camera control and motion trajectory control but not both simultaneously. It builds directly on the RealisDance-DiT backbone (itself based on Wan-2.1 with Flow Matching) and adopts Uni3C's least-squares rigid transformation for SMPL-to-point-cloud alignment, but departs by introducing a training-free ground-adaptive retargeting module (using a bottom-5%-points ground heuristic) and a scene-visibility-masked latent fusion mechanism that injects point-cloud geometric priors during early denoising steps. Unlike prior methods that treat trajectory and camera as independent or mutually exclusive controls, 3STC-HIA unifies them in a single aligned 3D coordinate space with decoupled, composable control signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling a single training-free pipeline to simultaneously control human action, motion trajectory, and camera movement while maintaining physical scene consistency (no floating/penetration), which is critical for film preview, virtual production, and interactive content generation. The acknowledged trade-off—slightly worse FID/FVD compared to baselines that prioritize distribution matching over control fidelity—highlights an open tension between controllability and perceptual quality in video generation. The authors identify human-scene interaction modeling and multi-person coordinated motion as key future directions, and the reliance on monocular depth estimation (Depth-Pro) and SfM for 3D reconstruction remains a potential accuracy bottleneck for complex or occluded scenes.
