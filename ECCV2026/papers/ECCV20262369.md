## Problem

Vision-centric ARC solvers such as VARC learn most of their representations from scarce ARC supervision, while dominant LLM-based pipelines benefit from massive language pretraining, creating a pretraining asymmetry. This paper asks whether large-scale visual self-supervised pretraining on natural images can close that gap and provide transferable representations for few-shot abstract grid reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262369/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each ARC task provides 2–5 input-output grid demonstrations (grids up to 30×30 with at most 10 discrete colors) plus one or more inference inputs. Grids are placed on a fixed 64×64 canvas, partitioned into 2×2 patches, and mapped to learnable color embeddings; a task token identifies the current transformation. The model outputs a full predicted grid via per-patch color logits, scored by exact grid match.

## Method

NAT-ARC extends the VARC pipeline with a three-stage procedure: (i) initializing the encoder from a publicly available ImageNet MAE checkpoint (discarding patch/positional embeddings to handle canvas-size mismatch), (ii) offline training on ARC-1 and RE-ARC with the pretrained encoder and a randomly initialized lightweight decoder, and (iii) per-task test-time adaptation using LoRA adapters on attention and MLP layers while freezing the backbone. The architecture is an MAE-compatible encoder-decoder Transformer with 2D RoPE for spatial awareness, scaled across base (114M), large (334M), and huge (664M) encoder sizes. A key finding is that ImageNet pretraining yields faster offline convergence and more reliable scaling than random initialization, and that prediction-level ensembling across no-pretraining, ImageNet MAE, and ARC-style grid MAE regimes reaches 70.2% pass@2 on ARC-1.

## Results

Nat-ARC (0.6B params, ImageNet MAE pretrained) achieves 63.4 ± 0.7% pass@2 on ARC-1 and 8.6 ± 0.8% on ARC-2 as a single model. Prediction-level ensembling across no-pretraining, ImageNet MAE, and ARC-style grid pretraining checkpoints (2B total params) reaches 70.2 ± 0.6% pass@2 on ARC-1 and 13.1 ± 0.7% on ARC-2. ImageNet MAE pretraining consistently improves over matched randomly initialized baselines across base (114M), large (334M), and huge (664M) encoder scales, with the best single-model gain of approximately 9 points at the huge scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nat-ARC's single model (63.4% ARC-1) outperforms the vision-centric VARC baseline (54.0%) by 9.4 points and the TTT+BARC LLM system (62.8%) by 0.6 points, while remaining below LoopViT (65.8%) and Loop-OWM (68.5%) by 2.4 and 5.1 points respectively. The 70.2% ensemble surpasses LoopViT and TTT+BARC but still trails Loop-OWM (68.5% → actually the ensemble beats it by 1.7 points) and The ARChitects (71.6%) by 1.4 points. Against general-purpose LLMs, Nat-ARC substantially outperforms GPT-5 (44.0%) and DeepSeek-R1 (15.8%), though Gemini 3 Pro (98.0%) remains far ahead. On ARC-2, Nat-ARC's single model (8.6%) is comparable to VARC (8.3%) and LoopViT (14.2%) but well below Loop-OWM (22.5%) and Gemini 3 Pro (77.1%). The paper notes that these comparisons span different model types, training data, and inference-time computation, so the table should be read as contextual rather than a direct ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VARC pipeline [27], which formulated ARC as conditional image-to-image translation, by inserting an explicit ImageNet MAE pretraining stage before offline ARC training. It departs from the dominant LLM-centric ARC solvers (e.g., TTT+BARC, The ARChitects) that rely on language-model pretraining and symbolic/textual grid representations, instead arguing that the pretraining asymmetry between language- and vision-centric solvers can be closed by transferring natural-image visual representations. The architecture follows the encoder-decoder Transformer design of VARC and LoopViT [35], with 2D RoPE and LoRA-based test-time adaptation, but the key conceptual contribution is treating large-scale visual self-supervised pretraining as a drop-in initialization for the ARC encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a purely vision-based pipeline with ImageNet MAE pretraining reaches 70.2% pass@2 on ARC-1 via prediction-level ensembling is competitive with LLM-heavy systems and suggests that abstract grid reasoning may not fundamentally require language-model priors. However, the mechanistic explanation remains incomplete: the task-level analysis (match-and-copy, connected-component) is qualitative and limited to 15 tasks, and the paper does not isolate whether gains come from better representations, better optimization landscapes, or both. Open questions include whether the scaling advantage of pretraining persists beyond the huge (664M) encoder, whether the approach transfers to ARC-2's harder tasks (where Nat-ARC scores only 13.1%), and whether the toy natural-image transformation experiment generalizes beyond simple geometric operations.
