## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action models for autonomous driving rely on text-centric VQA and chain-of-thought pretraining, which injects semantic knowledge but fails to ground the model's attention in spatially relevant driving regions. As a result, learned representations emphasize linguistic semantics over the motion and spatial dependencies essential for reliable trajectory prediction, producing scattered attention maps with no clear focus on traffic-critical objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262674/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a front-camera RGB image, ego-vehicle state, and a language instruction as input. The TGP-Prompter first outputs a 2D-TGP trajectory—a sequence of 8 projected 2D image-plane coordinates (plus retained yaw angles) derived from the expert BEV trajectory via a pinhole camera model—which is then fed as a textual coordinate prompt to the TGP-Planner. The TGP-Planner produces step-by-step CoT reasoning (critical object detection, natural-language explanation, meta-behavior selection) followed by a 4-second future BEV trajectory in text-waypoint format at 0.5 s intervals.

## Method

DriveTeach-VLA uses a dual-model architecture (TGP-Prompter and TGP-Planner, both Qwen2.5-VL-3B) to decouple spatial guidance from trajectory planning. For 'what to see,' Driving-aware Vision Distillation (DVD) overlays Grounding DINO-detected traffic-critical bounding boxes onto images and self-distills the ViT encoder: a teacher ViT processes bbox-augmented images while a student ViT processes raw images, with block-wise (2×4 grid) Smooth-L1 alignment in latent space and EMA teacher updates. For 'where to look,' 2D-TGP projects expert BEV trajectories onto the image plane and conditions the TGP-Planner's decoding as a textual coordinate sequence, exploiting the MLLM's native grounding ability. Training proceeds in three stages—DVD pretraining, TGP-guided CoT-SFT (with Qwen2.5-VL-72B pseudo-labeled reasoning), and TGP-guided GRPO reinforcement learning—using teacher forcing with ground-truth 2D-TGP during training and the TGP-Prompter's predicted 2D-TGP at inference, yielding only a ~0.4 PDMS train-test gap.

## Results

On NAVSIM navtest, DriveTeach-VLA achieves 90.4 PDMS with a single inference pass, surpassing the best VLA-based method RecogDrive (89.6) and the most similar autoregressive baseline AutoVLA (89.1). On nuScenes open-loop evaluation, DriveTeach-VLA achieves the best L2 trajectory error of 0.30 m (ST-P3 protocol) and 0.60 m (UniAD protocol) with a collision rate of 0.12%, outperforming all prior methods including Impromptu VLA (0.33 m / 0.13%) and AutoVLA (0.48 m / 0.13%). The model narrows the gap to human driving (94.8 PDMS) to within 4.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveTeach-VLA outperforms all VLA-based baselines on NAVSIM navtest PDMS: it beats RecogDrive (89.6) by 0.8, AutoVLA (89.1) by 1.3, and CuriousVLA (88.9) by 1.5, while using the same Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone as AutoVLA and CuriousVLA. Among end-to-end methods, it trails only ASSCG (91.4) by 1.0 PDMS but surpasses DiffusionDrive (88.1) and WoTE (88.3) by 2.3 and 2.1 respectively. On nuScenes, DriveTeach-VLA achieves the best L2 error (0.30 m ST-P3, 0.60 m UniAD) and lowest collision rate (0.12%), beating Impromptu VLA (0.33 m / 0.13%) and AutoVLA (0.48 m / 0.13%) under the ST-P3 protocol, and UniAD (1.03 m / 0.31%) and AutoVLA (0.86 m / 0.35%) under the UniAD protocol. Notably, DriveTeach-VLA's advantage over AutoVLA is particularly meaningful since both share the same autoregressive text-waypoint paradigm and backbone, isolating the contribution of DVD pretraining and 2D-TGP spatial guidance. The efficiency analysis further shows DriveTeach-VLA (3.18 s/sample on L20) is faster than AutoVLA's text-waypoint variant (7.65 s) despite the dual-model overhead, due to fewer CoT reasoning tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveTeach-VLA sits within the AD-VLA paradigm that formulates trajectory prediction as autoregressive decoding over a Qwen2.5-VL backbone, following the text-waypoint scheme of ImpromptuVLA, Poutine, and AutoVLA. It departs from the now-standard three-stage pipeline (VQA pretraining → CoT-SFT → GRPO) by replacing the text-centric VQA stage with a vision-centric Driving-aware Vision Distillation (DVD) that self-distills the ViT encoder using Grounding DINO bounding-box-augmented images, and by injecting 2D image-plane trajectory projections (2D-TGP) as spatial conditioning into both the SFT and GRPO stages. The self-distillation mechanism with EMA is inherited from DINO, the CoT task structure from Poutine, and the GRPO rollout procedure from AutoVLA/CuriousVLA, but the dual-model Prompter–Planner split and the pinhole-camera projection of BEV trajectories into 2D text coordinates are novel architectural choices not present in prior AD-VLAs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete failure mode of text-centric VLA training—spatially ungrounded attention during trajectory decoding—and shows that vision-grounded pretraining plus explicit 2D spatial guidance closes this gap, yielding SoTA PDMS on NAVSIM (90.4) and best L2/collision on nuScenes. The small train-test gap (~0.4 PDMS) between teacher-forced and predicted 2D-TGP validates the dual-model design, while the efficiency analysis reveals that vision-grounded spatial conditioning reduces reliance on long CoT reasoning tokens, making DriveTeach-VLA faster than AutoVLA despite the extra model call. Open questions include robustness under domain shift (the DVD stage depends on Grounding DINO detection quality, degrading at 40% bbox noise), whether the dual-model overhead is acceptable for real-time deployment, and whether the 2D-TGP concept generalizes to multi-camera or 3D-grounded settings.
