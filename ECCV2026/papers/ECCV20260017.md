## Problem

Mainstream token mixers in vision backbones (convolution, attention, MLP) are opaque black boxes that lack interpretability, while existing clustering-based alternatives (CoC, FEC) suffer from suboptimal performance due to quadratic computational complexity and reliance solely on Euclidean geometry, which distorts hierarchical semantic relationships. The paper asks whether clustering can be made both efficient and expressive enough to serve as a competitive, interpretable token mixer for general-purpose visual representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260017/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224 for classification; variable resolution with reflect padding for detection/segmentation) that are split into non-overlapping patches equipped with spatial coordinates and projected into embedding tokens. A four-stage hierarchical architecture progressively reduces token count by a factor of four at each stage via spatial concatenation, producing multi-scale feature maps. Depending on the downstream head, outputs include class labels (ImageNet-1K), per-pixel semantic masks (ADE20K via Semantic FPN), or bounding boxes and instance masks (COCO via Mask R-CNN).

## Method

ClusterMixer performs token mixing through a three-step soft-clustering procedure: (i) cluster centers are estimated via parameter-free average pooling over neighboring patches, (ii) tokens are softly assigned to centers using a similarity metric plus a learnable relative positional bias, and (iii) features are aggregated from assigned tokens and propagated back. Two strategies make this practical: hierarchical clustering restricts patch-level mixing to local windows (reducing quadratic to linear complexity) and adds a lightweight window-level global mixing step; and dual-geometry similarity applies Euclidean cosine distance at the fine-grained patch level while using Lorentz-model hyperbolic distance at the abstract window level, exploiting hyperbolic space's natural fit for tree-like hierarchical semantics. The full HCFormer backbone follows the MetaFormer paradigm with four stages, each containing residual blocks of ClusterMixer, and is trained end-to-end with standard supervised objectives (AdamW, cosine LR schedule, Mixup/CutMix augmentation for classification).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, HCFormer-Medium (33.7M params) achieves 82.4% top-1 accuracy, outperforming Swin-Tiny (81.3%) and MLP-Mixer-B/16 (76.4%) with fewer parameters. At the 16M scale, HCFormer-Small reaches 79.8% top-1, exceeding ResMLP-12 by 3.2% and PVT-Tiny by 4.7%. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, HCFormer-Medium achieves 43.3% mIoU with Semantic FPN, surpassing CoC-Medium/4 (40.2%) and FEC-Large (40.5%). On COCO object detection with Mask R-CNN, HCFormer-Medium attains 40.9% AP^box and 37.6% AP^mask, outperforming all clustering-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCFormer consistently outperforms all 14 compared baselines across all three tasks. Against prior clustering-based backbones, HCFormer gains 1.2–3.3% top-1 over CoC and 0.3–2.4% over FEC at comparable parameter counts (e.g., HCFormer-Small 79.8% vs. CoC-Small 77.5% vs. FEC-Base 78.1% at ~14–16M). Against attention-based methods, HCFormer-Medium (33.7M) exceeds Swin-Tiny (29M) by 1.1% and PVT-Small (24.5M) by 2.6%, while HCFormer-Small (16.1M) matches DeiT-Small (22.1M) at 79.8% with 6M fewer parameters. Against MLP-based methods, HCFormer-Small surpasses ResMLP-12 (15M) by 3.2% and MLP-Mixer-B/16 (59M) by 3.4%. Against convolutional baselines, HCFormer-Small exceeds ConvMixer-1024/12 (14.6M) by 2.0% and ResNet-50 (26M) by 0% (both at 79.8%). The hyperbolic vision-language models (MERU, HyCoCLIP, HCL) operate in a zero-shot setting and are not directly comparable to HCFormer's supervised results. On ADE20K, HCFormer-Small (18.9M) achieves 40.4% mIoU, beating CoC-Small/4 (17.6M, 36.6%) by 3.8% and FEC-Base (18M, 37.7%) by 2.7%; on COCO, HCFormer-Small (34.1M) reaches 38.7% AP^box vs. CoC-Small/49 (33.6M, 37.2%) and FEC-Base (33.1M, 37.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

HCFormer extends the clustering-based vision backbone lineage initiated by CoC (hard clustering token mixer) and FEC (clustering-based downsampling) by introducing soft clustering and a dual-geometry similarity mechanism. It inherits the MetaFormer hierarchical architecture and pooling-based center estimation, but departs from prior clustering methods by performing patch-level clustering in Euclidean space and window-level clustering in hyperbolic (Lorentz) space, replacing the shifted-window strategy of Swin with a hierarchical patch/window mixing scheme. The hyperbolic geometry component draws on the broader literature of hyperbolic embeddings for hierarchical data, but applies it specifically to the cluster-assignment step of a token mixer rather than to contrastive or generative objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully interpretable, clustering-based backbone can match or exceed attention- and convolution-based architectures across classification, segmentation, and detection, offering a concrete path toward transparent vision backbones. However, the performance gains over the closest clustering baselines (CoC, FEC) are modest (1–3%), and the hyperbolic component contributes only ~1.6% at the window level while incurring additional computational cost and numerical fragility. Open questions include whether the dual-geometry design scales to much larger models or higher-resolution inputs, whether the interpretability advantage translates into practical trust or debugging benefits in deployed systems, and whether the hyperbolic window-level clustering remains beneficial when the number of windows grows substantially.
