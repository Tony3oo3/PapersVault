## Problem

Large DiT-based image-to-3D shape generation models exceed 2.5 GB in parameter size, making them impractical for resource-constrained and real-time applications. Existing diffusion compression strategies designed for image or video generation fail to transfer to 3D shape synthesis, causing severe geometric degradation such as structural collapse and topology distortion, while prior 3D efficiency work focuses on inference acceleration rather than fundamental backbone compression. This paper presents the first systematic approach to physically compressing image-to-shape DiTs while preserving geometric fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The compression pipeline operates on the DiT backbone (U-Net or Transformer) of image-to-3D generation models, taking a single conditional RGB image as input and producing a 3D mesh or point cloud as output. The vitality analysis generates point clouds from both the full and layer-ablated models, computing Earth Mover's Distance between them over a dataset of 210 DALL-E 3–generated images. During fine-tuning, the compressed model is trained on rendered images from the Objaverse 10K subset, and evaluation uses 200 image-shape pairs from Objaverse with Uni3D-I and OpenShape-I embedding metrics.

## Method

The framework introduces a three-stage vitality-guided compression pipeline. First, each DiT layer's contribution to 3D synthesis is quantified via per-layer ablation, measuring the EMD between full-model and layer-removed point clouds to produce a vitality score; this reveals non-uniform importance with distinct sensitivity patterns between double-block and single-block layers. Second, structured pruning removes low-vitality layers using separate thresholds for double-block (τ_d) and single-block (τ_s) modules, followed by adaptive mixed-precision quantization that assigns 8-bit precision to highly vital layers and 4-bit to less vital ones. Third, a selective distillation fine-tuning step updates only the least-vital retained layer in each block, using a flow-matching loss that matches the compressed student's ODE path to the full teacher's output under both conditional and unconditional inputs, achieving up to 66% model-size reduction with near-identical synthesis quality.

## Results

On Objaverse (200 image-shape pairs), the compressed models maintain or slightly exceed full-model quality: Step1X-3D+Ours achieves Uni3D-I 0.3580 / OpenShape-I 0.1489 at 0.843 GB (−65.63% size), Hy3D 2.0+Ours reaches Uni3D-I 0.3601 / OpenShape-I 0.1491 at 0.909 GB (−66.37%), and Hy3D 2mini+Ours scores Uni3D-I 0.3608 / OpenShape-I 0.1484 at 0.578 GB (−44.50%). Inference TFLOPs, latency, and peak VRAM are also consistently reduced (e.g., Step1X-3D drops from 290.68 TFLOPs / 6.23 s / 2.718 GB to 113.24 TFLOPs / 2.78 s / 1.206 GB). Applied to TRELLIS's SSF stage, the method achieves a 70.3% size reduction with only a 1.6% Uni3D-I drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The compressed models outperform all external baselines on both Uni3D-I and OpenShape-I while being substantially smaller: Hy3D 2mini+Ours (0.578 GB) surpasses Craftsman3D (2.322 GB, Uni3D-I 0.3519) and TRELLIS (2.175 GB, Uni3D-I 0.3442) in quality at roughly one-quarter the size. Against the full-model counterparts, compression introduces negligible quality loss—Step1X-3D+Ours actually edges out the original on OpenShape-I (0.1489 vs. 0.1480), and Hy3D 2.0+Ours improves Uni3D-I from 0.3582 to 0.3601. Earlier feedforward and diffusion baselines (Splatter Image, TripoSR, LGM) are clearly inferior in both semantic alignment and geometric detail, with Uni3D-I scores 0.18–0.30 versus 0.36 for the compressed DiTs. The paper also applies TinyFusion and Diff-Pruning (image-domain compression methods) to the same 3D DiT backbone as ablation baselines; these cause severe geometric degradation (structural collapse, topology distortion), confirming that domain-specific vitality analysis is necessary for 3D shape synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the layer-vitality concept from Stable Flow [1], which measured per-layer importance in text-to-image DiTs via DINO perceptual distance, to the 3D shape generation domain by replacing DINO with Earth Mover's Distance on point clouds. It deliberately departs from existing diffusion compression methods (TinyFusion, Diff-Pruning) designed for image/video generation, demonstrating that their strategies cause severe geometric degradation when naively transferred to 3D. The compression pipeline itself—structured pruning, mixed-precision quantization, and distillation fine-tuning—draws on well-established Transformer compression techniques, but the key contribution is the geometry-aware vitality metric and block-type-specific thresholds that make these techniques effective for 3D DiTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic physical compression framework for image-to-shape DiTs, achieving up to 66% model-size reduction with near-identical synthesis quality across three state-of-the-art architectures, making large 3D generative models viable in memory-constrained and interactive settings. The plug-and-play generality (validated on Step1X-3D, Hunyuan3D 2.0/2mini, and TRELLIS) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include extending compression to appearance-oriented flows (e.g., TRELLIS's SLAT Flow), whether the vitality analysis holds for other 3D representations (SDF, Gaussian splatting), and the fine-tuning cost relative to the compression gains in practice.
