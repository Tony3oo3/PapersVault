## Problem

Sparse attention for video DiTs reduces quadratic cost by computing only a subset of attention blocks, but existing methods either drop low-score blocks entirely (causing information loss) or rely on learned predictors (introducing training overhead). More fundamentally, once a compensation branch is available, conventional score-based block selection is misaligned with the actual objective: high-score blocks may be well-approximated by centroids, while low-score blocks with diverse key-value interactions suffer the largest compensation error.

## Input / Output

SVG-EAR is a drop-in replacement for the self-attention layer within existing video DiT architectures (Wan2.2-14B and HunyuanVideo-13B at 720p). It consumes the same text prompts (T2V) or image-plus-text prompts (I2V) as the base model and produces generated video (21 frames × 3,600 tokens/frame for Wan2.2; 33 frames × 3,600 tokens/frame for HunyuanVideo). The method is entirely training-free and parameter-free, requiring only a user-specified density budget ρ that controls the fraction of blocks computed exactly versus compensated.

## Method

SVG-EAR combines two components: (1) a parameter-free linear compensation branch that approximates skipped attention blocks by replacing keys and values with their cluster centroids, reducing per-block cost to a single logit and scalar-vector product; and (2) an error-aware routing strategy that, instead of selecting blocks by attention score, estimates the per-block compensation error via a lightweight probe (using query centroids as proxies, reducing cost from O(N_q·N_k·d) to O(C_q·N_k·d)) and greedily assigns exact computation to blocks with the highest error-to-size ratio under the density budget. A theoretical upper bound (Proposition 4.1) relates the true attention-map MSE to the estimated error plus a residual term that scales with clustering quality δ_q² and decreases with sequence length. A custom fused streaming Triton kernel avoids materializing intermediate logits, keeping routing overhead at ~6.5% of end-to-end latency while achieving up to 13.74× kernel speedup over a naive PyTorch implementation.

## Results

SVG-EAR achieves PSNR of 29.759 (Wan2.2 I2V), 24.995 (Wan2.2 T2V), and 31.043 (HunyuanVideo T2V), consistently outperforming all baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. In terms of efficiency, SVG-EAR delivers 1.59× speedup on Wan2.2 T2V and 1.93× on HunyuanVideo, while SVG-EAR-Turbo reaches 1.75× on Wan2.2 T2V. The method also matches or exceeds full-attention VBench quality scores (e.g., ImgQual 0.704 vs. 0.704 on Wan2.2 I2V; SubCons 0.959 vs. 0.960), confirming that the sparse approximation preserves perceptual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVG-EAR outperforms all three baselines—SpargeAttention, SVG (static), and SVG2 (dynamic top-p)—on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across every model/task configuration. Against the strongest baseline SVG2, SVG-EAR gains +2.09 dB PSNR on Wan2.2 I2V (29.759 vs. 27.668), +1.44 dB on Wan2.2 T2V (24.995 vs. 23.556), and +1.60 dB on HunyuanVideo (31.043 vs. 29.445), while also reducing LPIPS by 20–21%. The gap over the static SVG baseline is even larger (e.g., +5.54 dB on Wan2.2 T2V), confirming that dynamic error-aware routing is critical. SpargeAttention, which uses a different sparsification strategy, trails SVG-EAR by 2.6–6.5 dB PSNR depending on the setting. Notably, SVG-EAR also matches or slightly exceeds full-attention VBench reference-free scores (ImgQual, SubCons), whereas all baselines show small degradations, indicating that the centroid compensation branch effectively recovers the information lost by skipping blocks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVG-EAR builds directly on the cluster-permute-route pipeline established by SVG2 [54], inheriting its semantic-aware k-means clustering, block-sparse structure, and adaptive budget allocation. It departs from the score-based (top-p/top-k) block selection used by SVG, SVG2, and SpargeAttention by introducing error-aware routing that prioritizes blocks where centroid-based compensation fails, rather than blocks with the highest attention scores. Conceptually, it also contrasts with SLA/SLA2 [63,64], which pair sparse attention with a learned linear branch requiring fine-tuning; SVG-EAR achieves a similar compensation effect with a parameter-free centroid approximation, preserving plug-and-play applicability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, training-free recipe for improving sparse attention in video DiTs that can be dropped into existing SVG2-based pipelines with minimal overhead (6.5% of end-to-end latency), making it immediately useful for practitioners accelerating 720p video generation on a single GPU. The core insight—that once a compensation branch exists, routing should target high-error blocks rather than high-score blocks—reframes the block-selection problem and could inform future sparse-attention designs beyond video. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is whether the approach generalizes to attention mechanisms outside DiTs (e.g., linear attention, state-space models), and whether the clustering-quality bound tightens further at longer sequence lengths typical of minute-scale video.
