## Problem

Real-world robotic reinforcement learning is hampered by dangerous exploration and sparse rewards, and existing remedies either require many demonstrations or continuous human teleoperation during training. HIL-SERL showed that human intervention dramatically accelerates learning, but the constant human presence creates a scalability bottleneck due to fatigue, latency, and cost. The paper asks whether a single demonstration can be structured into an automated intervention system that matches human-in-the-loop performance without any operator.

## Input / Output

The policy observes RGB images from two wrist-mounted or scene-mounted cameras plus proprioceptive states (end-effector pose, velocity, forces/torques, and gripper state on Franka; end-effector pose and forces/torques on UR5) and outputs a 6D delta end-effector pose action. Beyond the standard RL observation, the framework consumes a single pre-collected demonstration trajectory and two manually identified recovery points on it, which drive all intervention logic. Episodes terminate on binary task success or after 300 time steps, and evaluation disables all intervention mechanisms.

## Method

AutoSERL extends SERL by replacing human teleoperation with three rule-based mechanisms derived entirely from one demonstration trajectory. A sliding window of fixed length (spanning the two recovery points) advances along the demo at each step; when the end-effector deviates beyond threshold th₂ from the nearest forward trajectory point, a directional angle check (≤90°) prevents backward pulls and motion planning guides the robot to that point. A safety recovery mechanism monitors stagnation over l_stag steps near the interaction region and, upon detection, replays the demo segment between the two recovery points to restore productive contact. An intervention termination criterion disables all guidance once an episode succeeds with fewer than l_term interventions, preserving the policy's ability to explore beyond the demonstrated trajectory. The key insight is that geometric heuristics over a single trajectory—sliding-window proximity, forward-direction filtering, and replay-based recovery—suffice to replicate the corrective function of a human operator across contact-intensive manipulation tasks.

## Results

AutoSERL achieves a 100% success rate (50/50) on all six contact-intensive manipulation tasks (plug insertion, USB insertion, hanger suspension, correction tape suspension, spoon suspension, and drawer opening) using only a single demonstration. Under the same training time budget, SERL (initialized with 20 demonstrations) achieves 0/50 on four of six tasks, while AutoSERL reaches 50/50 on all. In terms of training efficiency, AutoSERL requires less or equal time to reach 100% success compared to HIL-SERL on five out of six tasks (e.g., 25 min vs. 60 min for correction tape suspension, 35 min vs. 70 min for spoon suspension).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoSERL (1 demonstration) decisively outperforms SERL (20 demonstrations) under identical training time: SERL achieves 0/50 on plug insertion, hanger suspension, spoon suspension, and drawer opening, while AutoSERL reaches 50/50 on all six tasks. Against HIL-SERL (20 demonstrations + continuous human intervention), AutoSERL matches or beats training time on 5 of 6 tasks, with the largest gap on correction tape suspension (25 min vs. 60 min) and spoon suspension (35 min vs. 70 min); HIL-SERL is slightly faster only on USB insertion (6 min vs. 8 min). Against behavior cloning (1–20 demonstrations depending on task), AutoSERL wins on every task, with the most dramatic margins on spoon suspension (50/50 vs. 0/50) and USB insertion (50/50 vs. 5/50). Against MILES (a dedicated one-shot imitation learning method with ±4 cm / ±4° data augmentation), AutoSERL also wins on all six tasks, most notably on USB insertion (50/50 vs. 0/50), drawer opening (50/50 vs. 0/50), and correction tape suspension (50/50 vs. 1/50), though MILES is competitive on hanger suspension (42/50) and plug insertion (33/50).

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoSERL builds directly on SERL [14], which mixes demonstration data with online RL experience for sample-efficient training on physical robots, and on HIL-SERL [15], which adds continuous human teleoperation during training to correct deadlocks and unsafe states. The key departure is replacing the human operator with three rule-based automated mechanisms (sliding window intervention, safety recovery via trajectory replay, and intervention termination) derived entirely from a single demonstration trajectory. Conceptually, the paper also positions itself against safety-shield approaches (CMDPs, reachability-based methods) that require additional hardware or complex coordination, arguing that simple geometric heuristics over a single trajectory suffice for the considered task class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for labs and deployment settings where continuous human supervision during RL training is a cost and scalability bottleneck: one demonstration, structured into an automated intervention system, matches or beats 20-demonstration RL plus human-in-the-loop training across six contact-intensive tasks. However, the framework is currently limited to 6D delta end-effector pose action spaces and tasks involving a handheld object interacting with a single target, and the single-trajectory recovery mechanism may struggle with highly diverse failure modes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher-dimensional action spaces (e.g., dexterous hands with more DOF), multi-object interactions, and whether the geometric heuristics can be replaced by learned policies for broader applicability.
