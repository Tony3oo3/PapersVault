## Problem

Forecasting slow-evolving neurodegenerative pathologies from longitudinal 3D brain MRI is extremely challenging because disease-related morphological change over a typical one-year interval is under 1% of total volumetric variance, making the temporal signal far weaker than in natural video. The paper identifies two structural failure modes that cause standard generative sequence models to fail in this low-signal regime: Identity Collapse, where dominant stationary anatomy overwhelms the optimization and the model degenerates to an identity mapping, and the Continuous Interpolation Trap, where dense sample-specific nuisance noise prevents Lipschitz-continuous networks from isolating sparse biological drift, causing spurious variance to diffuse across the volume.

## Input / Output

The method takes a baseline 3D brain MRI volume (downsampled to 93×112×96), patient clinical metadata, and a target time horizon Δt, and produces a patient-specific forecasted 3D brain MRI at the future time point. Rather than generating the full future volume in pixel space, the autoregressive model predicts a sequence of discrete FSQ tokens encoding only the temporal drift in a compressed latent space; these tokens are added to the baseline latent representation and decoded back to the image domain, so the output is structurally anchored to the input scan.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. First, a Latent Drift Tokenizer (autoencoder) compresses each MRI into a continuous latent code, then computes the temporal residual Δz = z_fut − z_cur and passes it through a Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) bottleneck with a calibrated step size h > 2ε, which acts as a non-Lipschitz topological dead-zone: any noise fluctuation below h/2 is mapped exactly to zero while consistent structural drift above the threshold is preserved as a discrete token. Second, a Decoder-Transformer autoregressively predicts the sequence of quantized drift tokens conditioned on the continuous baseline latent z_cur and an MLP embedding of clinical metadata and time horizon, optimizing cross-entropy over the FSQ vocabulary. This design simultaneously escapes Identity Collapse by removing the dominant baseline anatomy from the prediction target and breaks the Continuous Interpolation Trap by converting the regression problem into discrete event modeling with a built-in noise-suppression threshold, as formalized in Theorem 2.

## Results

On the combined ADNI/AIBL longitudinal 3D brain MRI benchmark (3,981 training pairs, 1,140 test pairs), Latent Drift achieves the best structural similarity (Diff-SSIM 0.8204, NCC 0.9880) and the best downstream clinical utility (F1 87.51, Accuracy 88.33, Recall 86.78) across all seven baselines. In generative fidelity, it attains FID(i3d) 13.92 and FID(cls.) 12.77, trailing only CycleGAN (12.13 / 7.03) on FID but substantially outperforming all diffusion and autoregressive baselines. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm that the gains in Accuracy, F1, and Diff-SSIM over every baseline are statistically significant at p < 0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latent Drift outperforms all seven baselines on the clinically most relevant metrics: it leads NeuroAR (the strongest autoregressive baseline) by 5.65 points in F1 (87.51 vs 81.86), 4.38 points in Accuracy (88.33 vs 83.95), and 9.87 points in Recall (86.78 vs 76.91), and it surpasses the best diffusion baseline BrLP by 7.15 F1 points. On structural similarity, it achieves the highest Diff-SSIM (0.8204) and NCC (0.9880), exceeding NeuroAR's 0.8128 and BrLP's 0.9819 respectively. The sole exception is CycleGAN, which attains lower FID scores (12.13 vs 13.92 on i3d; 7.03 vs 12.77 on classifier) and slightly higher Pix-SSIM (0.9612 vs 0.9483), but its substantially lower Diff-SSIM (0.7713) and F1 (75.44) indicate it reproduces static anatomy rather than capturing the sparse pathological drift. Among diffusion baselines, Palette and I2I-DiT show the weakest progression fidelity (Diff-SSIM 0.8030 and 0.7799; F1 80.36 and 62.37), consistent with the paper's analysis that continuous diffusion models fall into the Continuous Interpolation Trap. The autoregressive baselines VQGAN and RQ-Transformer lag further behind (F1 70.55 and 60.78), confirming that standard tokenization without a drift-specific objective cannot isolate the subtle atrophy signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the autoregressive + quantized-token paradigm from VQGAN and RQ-Transformer, and the latent-space disease progression idea from Brain Latent Progression and NeuroAR, but departs fundamentally in what is predicted: rather than synthesizing the absolute future anatomy, it predicts a compressed temporal residual (latent drift) and applies Finite Scalar Quantization as a noise-suppression filter rather than a mere compression step. The theoretical analysis identifying two specific optimization pathologies—Identity Collapse from source dominance and the Continuous Interpolation Trap from dense nuisance noise—provides a principled justification for this architectural shift that prior brain-forecasting work (NeuroAR, BrLP, Palette, I2I-DiT) lacked. The spatial-temporal decoupling strategy draws an analogy to motion modeling in natural video, but the paper argues the neurodegeneration setting is qualitatively harder because the signal is far sparser and the noise is sample-specific.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-mode failure analysis gives the field a diagnostic framework for understanding why generative models struggle with any low-signal, slow-evolving longitudinal task, potentially generalizing beyond Alzheimer's to Parkinson's, MS, or other pathologies. However, validation is limited to well-registered, downsampled ADNI/AIBL cohorts, and the shared FSQ grid may under-represent fast-changing regions (e.g., ventricles); the authors acknowledge that raw multi-site data with gradient nonlinearity and region-aware quantization remain open. The practical impact—earlier, patient-specific trajectory forecasting to inform clinical trial enrollment and intervention timing—is meaningful but contingent on the method transferring to noisier, more heterogeneous real-world data.
