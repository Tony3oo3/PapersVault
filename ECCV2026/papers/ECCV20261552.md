## Problem

State-of-the-art large-scale dataset distillation methods rely on full-sized pre-trained teacher models to generate soft labels during downstream training, incurring storage costs that often exceed the synthetic images themselves, slowing training by querying large models at every iteration, and—contrary to intuition—yielding suboptimal downstream performance. No prior work has systematically matched teacher capacity to the limited knowledge that a small synthetic dataset can actually support.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a synthetic dataset produced by any existing DD method (e.g., SRe2L, G_VBSM, RDED) and a full-sized pre-trained teacher (ResNet-18, ~11.4M params). It outputs a pruned teacher model (as small as 0.03% of original parameters, ~0.01 MB) that generates soft labels online during downstream training. The method is plug-and-play: it wraps around any DD pipeline without modifying the synthetic images or the downstream student architecture, and the pruned teacher replaces the full teacher in the label-generation step of Eq. 1.

## Method

Flash-DD proposes a two-stage DD-oriented parameter reduction: first, iterative structured pruning (l1-norm) on the full teacher removes redundant parameters while preserving accuracy within a tolerance δ; second, further pruning is guided by a loss-degradation metric Dis(θ_T) that measures the student's early-training loss reduction, identifying the capacity at which the teacher's knowledge aligns with the synthetic dataset's learnable space. The optimal teacher is selected by maximizing Dis(θ_T) + γ·Acc(θ_T) over the surviving capacity range, with a theoretical lower bound (Proposition 1) showing that when the teacher's residual capacity lies in the low-variance subspace of the data covariance, the student's generalization error is unchanged. For larger storage budgets, the method extends to ensemble label generation with guidelines (Eq. 8) constraining sub-teacher capacity gaps, minimum accuracy, and proximity to the optimal single-teacher size, enabling complementary perspectives without conflicting supervision.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K IPC 50, Flash-DD applied to G_VBSM achieves 60.3% downstream accuracy with only 12.1 MB extra storage (vs. 84.1 MB for the original), while RDED+Flash-DD reaches 58.5% with 10.7 MB. On Places365-Standard IPC 1, the method requires just 0.03% of the original extra storage (0.01 MB) and accelerates soft-label generation by 843.81× while matching prior SOTA accuracy. With a 1.8% storage budget (ImageNet-1K IPC 1), Flash-DD boosts accuracy by up to 13.4% over the previous leading method RDED (20.0% vs. 6.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flash-DD is applied as a plug-and-play module on three SOTA large-scale DD baselines: SRe²L [33], G_VBSM [24], and RDED [29]. Against SRe²L, Flash-DD yields the largest gains at low IPC (e.g., +33.9% on ImageNet-100 IPC 10, +23.2% on ImageNet-1K IPC 10) while reducing extra storage from ~43 MB to under 5 MB. Against G_VBSM, improvements are more moderate at high IPC (+7.3% on ImageNet-100 IPC 50) but still substantial at low IPC (+14.2% on ImageNet-1K IPC 1), with memory cut from 84.1 MB to 12.1 MB. Against RDED (the strongest baseline), Flash-DD still wins across all settings, with the headline +13.4% on ImageNet-1K IPC 1 (20.0% vs. 6.6%) and +9.4% on ImageNet-100 IPC 50 (71.0% vs. 61.6%), while using 75–99% less extra storage. In every configuration tested, Flash-DD simultaneously improves accuracy and reduces the teacher-model storage overhead, confirming that the parameter-reduction strategy identifies a capacity sweet spot that the full-size teacher overshoots.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of large-scale dataset distillation (SRe2L, G_VBSM, RDED) and model compression, repurposing structured pruning and ensemble strategies to address a specific bottleneck in the DD pipeline: the storage and latency cost of full-sized teacher models used for online soft-label generation. Rather than proposing a new distillation objective or synthetic-data construction method, it treats the teacher model itself as the optimization target, introducing a DD-oriented capacity-matching criterion that aligns teacher size with the learnable capacity of the synthetic dataset. The ensemble guidelines (Eq. 8) extend standard model-ensemble ideas with DD-specific constraints on capacity uniformity and minimum accuracy thresholds, positioning the work as a plug-and-play layer atop existing DD methods rather than a replacement for them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing teacher storage from ~45 MB to 0.01–7 MB (0.03%–16%) while accelerating label generation by up to 843× removes a major hidden cost that undermines the storage-efficiency promise of dataset distillation. The conceptual finding that teacher capacity should match synthetic-dataset learnable space—rather than being as large as possible—challenges the implicit assumption in prior work that a stronger teacher always yields better soft labels. Open questions include whether the capacity-matching criterion generalizes to offline label settings, non-ResNet teacher architectures, and whether the ensemble strategy scales beyond 3–4 teachers under very tight budgets. The method is validated on three datasets and six student architectures, but all experiments use ResNet-18 as the teacher backbone, leaving open whether the pruning-based capacity reduction transfers cleanly to transformer-based teachers.
