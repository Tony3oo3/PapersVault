## Problem

Existing image editing methods rely on single-step appearance manipulation and fail on edits requiring multi-step spatial, temporal, causal, or commonsense reasoning. While video generation models encode strong temporal coherence and physical priors, directly applying them to image editing is hindered by a fundamental mismatch: editing instructions specify only a target state, whereas video models expect a temporal evolution description, leading to high-variance and unreliable edits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260136/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a free-form text editing instruction as input. It produces a 31-frame video trajectory where the first frame is the source image, intermediate frames form an implicit reasoning trajectory showing the physical transformation, and the final frame is the edited result. The editing instruction is first converted by an MLLM into a video-oriented temporal description (specifying initial state, intermediate transitions, and final state) before conditioning the video diffusion model, and the reward signal is computed from a collage of uniformly sampled frames rather than a single frame.

## Method

VGEdit is a two-stage framework that repurposes a latent video diffusion model (HunyuanVideo-1.5) for reasoning-informed image editing. Stage 1 (Instruction Transfer) uses a frozen MLLM (Qwen3-VL-30B) with in-context exemplars to rewrite the editing instruction into a physically plausible temporal transformation description compatible with video generation. Stage 2 (RL Adaptation) fine-tunes the video model via DiffusionNFT, an off-policy RL method that optimizes the forward diffusion process through a flow-matching objective by contrasting reward-partitioned positive and negative denoisers. The reward is computed by a frozen MLLM scoring a collage of uniformly sampled frames using a logit-based expectation over predefined score tokens, providing a smoother signal than discrete sampling. The model is trained with LoRA (rank 32) and the Muon optimizer, requiring no ground-truth editing videos—only reward feedback over intermediate and final frames.

## Results

On RISE-Bench, VGEdit achieves 22.3% overall accuracy, nearly doubling the best prior method UniCoT (12.5%) and substantially outperforming all image-editing and unified multimodal baselines. On KRIS-Bench, VGEdit scores 72.48 overall, surpassing the strongest baseline Qwen-Image (62.77) by nearly 10 points, with particularly large gains in Logical Reasoning (62.29 vs. 48.57) and Instruction Decomposition (86.56 vs. 78.44). On the general ImgEdit benchmark, VGEdit reaches 4.33 overall, slightly exceeding Qwen-Image (4.27) and GPT-Image (4.20), confirming that the video-generation-based approach generalizes beyond reasoning-centric edits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGEdit outperforms all 14 baselines across all three benchmarks. On RISE-Bench, the largest gap is against UniCoT (12.5% vs. 22.3%), the strongest prior method that also uses chain-of-thought reasoning; image-only editors like Step1X-Edit (1.9%) and FLUX.1-Kontext-Dev (5.8%) struggle severely with multi-step reasoning. On KRIS-Bench, VGEdit (72.48) beats Qwen-Image (62.77) by 9.7 points and FLUX.1-Kontext-Dev (57.35) by 15.1 points, with the biggest relative gains in Logical Reasoning (+13.7 over Qwen-Image) and Instruction Decomposition (+8.1). On ImgEdit, VGEdit (4.33) marginally edges out Qwen-Image (4.27) and GPT-Image (4.20), while the zero-shot HunyuanVideo-1.5 baseline (2.87) confirms that instruction transfer and RL adaptation are both necessary. The zero-shot video model baseline (HunyuanVideo-1.5) underperforms all image-editing methods on RISE (6.2%), highlighting that simply prompting a video model with an editing instruction is insufficient without the proposed two-stage adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two converging threads: (1) the emerging use of video generation models as world simulators with physical and temporal priors, and (2) RL-based alignment of diffusion models via MLLM rewards (as in UniWorld-V2 and DiffusionNFT). It departs from prior video-for-editing work (Rotstein et al., ChronoEdit) that relied on zero-shot prompting or direct fine-tuning on source-target pairs, by introducing an explicit two-stage pipeline: MLLM-based instruction transfer to reframe edits as temporal transformations, followed by trajectory-level RL with DiffusionNFT. The key conceptual departure is treating intermediate video frames as an implicit reasoning chain rather than mere interpolation, and optimizing the full trajectory (via frame collages) rather than only the final frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video generation priors—temporal coherence, physical consistency, causal dynamics—can be effectively transferred to image editing without requiring ground-truth editing videos, offering a scalable path toward physically grounded edits. However, absolute performance on reasoning-centric benchmarks remains low (22.3% on RISE), indicating that multi-step physical/causal reasoning in visual editing is far from solved. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across video model architectures, whether longer or more complex edit trajectories benefit further, and whether the RL signal from a single MLLM judge is sufficient for truly novel physical scenarios not well-represented in the video model's training distribution.
