## Problem

Arbitrary medical slice super-resolution synthesizes intermediate slices at any scale from anisotropic clinical acquisitions, but existing implicit-neural-representation methods treat this ill-posed inverse problem as unconstrained regression, risking hallucinated anatomy and subtle alteration of originally observed slices. The paper addresses this gap by reformulating the task as a dual-prior constrained recovery process that simultaneously enforces exact measurement fidelity and anatomically plausible detail synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260624/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an anisotropic 3D volume V_LR ∈ R^{S×H×W} (CT or MRI) with coarse through-plane spacing and produces a high-resolution volume V_SR ∈ R^{Ŝ×H×W} at an arbitrary upsampling factor R along the slice axis, while preserving in-plane resolution. A single model handles all scales jointly (trained on R ∈ {2,3,4}, tested at unseen R ∈ {5,6,7}), and the output is mathematically guaranteed to reproduce every originally acquired slice with zero error via the Measurement-Consistent Projection constraint.

## Method

DP-NSL decomposes the reconstruction via null-range-space decomposition into a deterministic range-space anchor V_R = U(V_LR) (trilinear upsampling of observed slices) and a learnable null-space component V_N = Π_N(V̂_N), where Π_N = I − UD confines all synthesized details to the unobservable subspace, guaranteeing D(V_SR) = V_LR identically. Within the null space, a Mixture-of-Splines (MoS) module replaces point-wise MLP decoding with a bank of K B-spline experts (orders p ∈ {2,3,4}) whose separable tensor-product bases provide C^{p−1} analytic continuity; a lightweight routing network computes per-coordinate mixing weights from local features, adaptively allocating smoothness order to homogeneous tissue versus sharp anatomical boundaries. A Local Spatial Consistency Decoder (LSCD) then applies Inception-style multi-scale depthwise 3D convolutions (3×3×3, 5×5×5, 7×7×7) to inject local inductive bias before the final projection. The 3D EDSR encoder extracts spatial features, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with an L1 reconstruction loss under supervised learning.

## Results

DP-NSL achieves the highest PSNR/SSIM across all four datasets at in-scales (×2, ×3, ×4), with headline gains of up to 1.07 dB over the second-best method (e.g., 42.55 dB on Colon ×2 vs. 41.83 dB for HIIF; 42.49 dB on Liver ×2 vs. 41.40 dB for LTE). At out-of-scales (×5, ×6, ×7), DP-NSL maintains a consistent lead, reaching 34.05 dB on Colon ×5 and 40.17 dB on IXI ×5. On the real-data RPLHR-CT benchmark at ×5, DP-NSL scores 34.91 dB PSNR / 0.8925 SSIM, surpassing the next-best LTE (31.20 dB) by 3.71 dB. Downstream kidney segmentation on KiTS19 shows the highest Dice (0.8581 at ×3, 0.8067 at ×6), confirming that the reconstruction quality translates to clinical utility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-NSL outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and scale. Among fixed-scale methods, EDSR3D is the strongest single-scale competitor (e.g., 43.25 dB on Hepatic Vessels ×2) but cannot generalize across scales, while DP-NSL surpasses it by 0.15 dB there and by 0.71–1.07 dB on other datasets. Among generic ASSR methods, HIIF is the closest in-scales rival (41.83 dB on Colon ×2 vs. DP-NSL's 42.55 dB, a 0.72 dB gap), but HIIF degrades severely at out-of-scales (31.59 dB at Colon ×5 vs. DP-NSL's 34.05 dB). LTE and MetaSR trail by 0.5–1.1 dB in-scales. Among medical ASSR baselines, SAINR and DC²SR are the strongest (within 0.2–0.5 dB of DP-NSL on some settings), but both lack explicit data-fidelity constraints and thus risk altering observed slices. ArSSR and CycleINR are consistently 0.5–1.5 dB behind DP-NSL. The largest absolute margin appears on the real-data RPLHR-CT benchmark, where DP-NSL leads LTE by 3.71 dB at ×5, underscoring the benefit of the null-space constraint under realistic acquisition conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate research threads: the Null-Range-Space Decomposition (NRSD) framework from diffusion-based restoration (DDNM) and B-spline coefficient estimation from screen-content super-resolution (BTC). It departs from the dominant INR-based medical SR paradigm (ArSSR, SAINR, CycleINR, DC2SR) by replacing unconstrained coordinate-wise MLP regression with a hard orthogonal projection that mathematically guarantees zero error at observed slices, and by substituting black-box decoders with a Mixture-of-Splines module that routes among B-spline experts of different analytic orders. The LSCD decoder further distinguishes the work from point-wise LIIF-style decoders by injecting multi-scale local inductive bias through depthwise convolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The guaranteed data-fidelity property (zero MAE at all originally acquired slice positions) addresses a clinically critical concern: existing INR-based methods can subtly alter observed pixel values, which is unacceptable in diagnostic workflows. The null-space formulation provides a clean theoretical separation between what is known and what must be synthesized, and the B-spline mixture offers interpretable, content-aware geometric priors that generalize to unseen upsampling factors without retraining. Open questions include whether the MCP formulation extends to more realistic degradation models (motion blur, non-uniform noise, partial-volume effects) beyond the idealized downsampling assumption, and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or multi-modal reconstruction settings.
