## Problem

Counting individual trees from satellite imagery remains largely unexplored due to two compounding challenges: at satellite resolution, crown boundaries become inherently ambiguous in dense forests, and large-scale manual annotations are prohibitively expensive while automated LiDAR-derived supervision is noisy. No dedicated large-scale benchmark or method exists that simultaneously handles heterogeneous counting regimes (isolated trees vs. dense canopies) and heterogeneous annotation quality.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 64×64 RGB+NIR patches at nominal satellite resolution (0.8–4.2 m GSD across Gaofen-2, PlanetScope, and SPOT-6 sensors) and produces an unnormalized per-pixel tree density map whose sum yields the total tree count. Training supervision comes in two forms: strong labels as sparse binary point annotations (manual photointerpretation) and weak labels as dense tree-cover masks or continuous density maps derived from airborne LiDAR, with the two sampled in a 1:1 stratified ratio during training.

## Method

TREEMATCH uses a UNet with ResNet-50 encoder to predict a continuous density map, trained with an Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) loss that aligns the prediction to annotations via a squared-Euclidean cost matrix solved with entropy-regularized Sinkhorn iterations. Unlike balanced OT, UOT permits local mass creation or destruction at a controlled KL penalty, decoupling geometric alignment from exact count equality and thereby tolerating annotation noise. A self-correction mechanism computes marginal residuals (γᵀ1 − y) from the transport plan to progressively refine weak annotations during training via a scheduled coefficient λ(t) that ramps from 0 to 0.8 over 400 steps; the correction is computed without backpropagation, and a second UOT loss between the prediction and the corrected target (with stop-gradient) provides the weak-label training signal. Strong labels additionally supervise a global count loss, ensuring accurate total-count calibration alongside spatial alignment.

## Results

On the TINYTREES benchmark spanning three continents and three satellite sensors, TREEMATCH achieves the best performance across all metrics and regions: RMSE of 60.6 (China/Gaofen-2), 72.4 (Rwanda/PlanetScope), and 147.2 (France/SPOT-6) trees per hectare, with R² values of 0.60, 0.47, and 0.35 respectively. The task remains challenging overall, with R² below 0.6 for all methods and regions. TREEMATCH improves upon the strongest baseline (DM-Count) by 4.4 RMSE on China, 4.4 on Rwanda, and 10.8 on France, while also achieving the best nMAE (36.6, 51.1, 37.4) across all splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TREEMATCH outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three regions. The closest competitor is DM-Count (balanced OT), which TREEMATCH beats by 4.4 RMSE on China (60.6 vs. 65.0), 4.4 on Rwanda (72.4 vs. 76.8), and 10.8 on France (147.2 vs. 158.0), with the gap widening on the noisier France split where unbalanced mass tolerance matters most. Detection-based methods (YOLOv8, CenterNet, P2P, TreeFormer) perform substantially worse, with CenterNet achieving R² of only 0.26/0.01/0.01 and YOLOv8 failing entirely on Rwanda (R² = −0.50) and France (n/a), confirming that instance-level detection is ill-suited to dense canopy. Regression baselines (density regression, count regression, uncertainty-aware regression) are competitive on China but degrade sharply on Rwanda and France, and incorporating weak supervision actually hurts density regression. The Bayesian crowd-counting baseline is particularly unstable, producing R² = −2.06 on China, while VRSNet and TreeFormer remain moderate but consistently below TREEMATCH by 5–15 RMSE points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the crowd-counting distribution-matching literature (DM-Count, Ma et al.'s UOT for counting) to the satellite tree-counting domain, replacing balanced OT with unbalanced OT to accommodate mass discrepancies inherent in noisy LiDAR-derived annotations. It departs from prior tree-mapping work, which has treated counting as a by-product of detection on small-scale aerial imagery, by formulating it as a spatial density-matching problem at satellite resolution where individual crown boundaries are inherently ambiguous. The self-correction mechanism via marginal residuals is a novel addition not present in prior UOT-based counting methods, enabling progressive refinement of weak supervision during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TINYTREES benchmark (216M annotations, 3 continents, 3 sensors, 25,890 km²) fills a critical gap: no prior dataset supported systematic evaluation of satellite-scale tree counting, and existing aerial-imagery datasets are geographically narrow and inaccessible. The UOT formulation with residual-based correction offers a principled path to exploiting large-scale noisy LiDAR supervision alongside sparse manual labels, which is the dominant annotation regime in operational forestry. Key open questions include the O(N²) memory bottleneck limiting spatial context, poor cross-region generalization (a Rwanda-trained model achieves 1066 nMAE on China), and the potential for joint modeling with canopy height estimation to leverage allometric relationships.
