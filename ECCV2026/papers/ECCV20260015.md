## Problem

Existing neuron suppression methods for concept erasure in text-to-image diffusion models cause collateral damage to benign semantics because sensitive and benign features are non-orthogonally superposed in the activation space. Suppressing identified sensitive neurons inevitably perturbs the generative manifold of unrelated features, degrading image quality and text alignment. The paper addresses this by reframing erasure as a geometric projection problem that explicitly accounts for feature entanglement rather than treating sensitive concepts as spatially isolated.

## Input / Output

The method intercepts intermediate activations at a single layer of a pre-trained diffusion model (identified via a sensitive score metric), with a Top-K SAE (expansion factor 4, hidden dimension 3072) trained on those activations to provide the sparse feature decomposition. At inference, the latent activation vector at the target layer is modified by subtracting a scaled orthogonalized direction before subsequent denoising steps proceed, producing a safe image. The approach is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on Stable Diffusion 1.4, FLUX.1 Dev, AltDiffusion, and Show-o2.

## Method

OrthoEraser is a three-stage geometric framework. First, a Top-K SAE decomposes dense polysemantic activations into a sparse basis, and sensitive neurons are identified via differential weighted frequency scores (ΔWFS) computed between sensitive and non-sensitive prompt sets. Second, coupled neuron detection uses zero-ablation: the activation shift of each benign neuron after removing sensitive neuron contributions (re-encoded through the SAE) reveals which benign features are geometrically entangled with the sensitive subspace. Third, the core novelty is an analytical gradient orthogonalization step: the aggregated sensitive direction is projected onto the null space of the coupled neurons' decoder weights (obtained via QR decomposition), yielding an erasure vector with provably zero component along the protected benign subspace. The final safe activation is the original latent minus a scaled orthogonalized direction (λ=3), making sensitive concept elimination and benign semantic preservation mutually independent by construction rather than by empirical tuning.

## Results

On the I2P nudity detection benchmark, OrthoEraser reduces total detected instances from 646 (SD1.4 baseline) to just 5, outperforming the closest competitor SNCE (17) by a factor of 3.4×. On MS COCO fidelity, OrthoEraser achieves an FID of 1.15 — an order-of-magnitude improvement over the next-best method (16.64, shared by SPM and SNCE) — while maintaining a CLIP Score of 31.33, virtually identical to the unmodified baseline (31.34). On adversarial robustness (Ring-A-Bell), OrthoEraser reduces the attack success rate from 98.7% to 2.7%, surpassing SNCE (6.3%) and RECE (13.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrthoEraser outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest safety baseline SNCE [16], OrthoEraser reduces I2P detections from 17 to 5 and improves FID from 16.64 to 1.15 (a 14.5× gap), while matching the baseline CLIP Score (31.33 vs 31.34). Against fine-tuning-based methods ESD [10] and UCE [12], OrthoEraser achieves 24× and 37× fewer detections respectively, with FID improvements of 14.7× and 19.9×. Against adapter-based SPM [32] and modulation-based DuMo [15], OrthoEraser reduces detections from 56 and 34 to 5, again with a dramatic FID advantage (1.15 vs 16.64 and unavailable). On adversarial robustness (Table 5), OrthoEraser achieves the lowest Ring-A-Bell ASR (2.7%) and P4D ASR (34.6%), beating SNCE (6.3% and 42.6%) and RECE (13.4% and 64.7%), confirming that the orthogonal projection generalizes beyond standard safety to jailbreak resistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuron-suppression line of concept erasure (SNCE, DuMo, SPM) by reframing the core problem geometrically: rather than treating sensitive concepts as spatially isolated units that can be simply zeroed out, it models collateral damage as a consequence of non-orthogonal feature superposition and solves it via analytical projection onto the null space of coupled benign neurons. It builds on sparse autoencoders (Cunningham et al.) for high-resolution feature disentanglement and on SNCE's weighted-frequency scoring for sensitive neuron identification, but departs from all prior methods by introducing a coupled-neuron detection step (zero-ablation shift analysis) and a QR-decomposition-based orthogonal projection that mathematically severs the intervention signal from the protected benign subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude FID improvement (1.15 vs. 16.64) over the next-best method provides strong empirical evidence that the geometric entanglement hypothesis is correct and that orthogonal projection is the right tool for decoupling erasure from preservation. The architecture-agnostic validation across SD1.4, FLUX.1 Dev, Show-o2, and AltDiffusion suggests broad practical applicability for safety alignment of deployed T2I systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-concept simultaneous erasure with heavily overlapping subspaces, whether SAE training cost is prohibitive for very large models, and whether the single-layer intervention assumption holds when sensitive semantics are distributed across many layers.
