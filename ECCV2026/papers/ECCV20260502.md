## Problem

Fine-grained open-vocabulary semantic segmentation in remote sensing is hampered by visual similarity between subtle categories (e.g., deciduous vs. coniferous trees) and semantic ambiguity in text queries. Existing SAM 3-based methods lack explicit fine-grained visual-linguistic alignment, suffer from text-encoder feature collapse on closely related classes, and rely on a single forward pass without iterative spatial refinement. Additionally, no benchmark exists that systematically evaluates OVSS under fine-grained semantic distinctions across diverse remote sensing scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 1008×1008) and one or more text prompts (class names, with synonym-expanded vocabulary to test prompt robustness) as input, and produces a pixel-level segmentation mask along with a dense semantic probability map and instance-level predictions. The framework is entirely training-free: no task-specific fine-tuning or additional supervision is applied, and all guidance is derived at inference time from the image itself and the text query. Evaluation uses mIoU against ground-truth annotations across eight datasets spanning satellite, aerial, and UAV imagery at resolutions from 128×128 to 4000×3000.

## Method

SANO3 is a training-free framework that enhances SAM 3 through collaborative refinement of textual and spatial guidance. First, it extracts discriminative visual primitives by over-clustering DINOv2 features via K-Means (K_init = Q×C) followed by hierarchical merging of similar clusters, yielding visual prototypes aligned to SAM 3's feature space. Second, it performs visual-conditioned relation-aware alignment (VARA): cross-attention refines token-level text features conditioned on each visual prototype, and a Plackett-Luce ranking model measures distributional consistency between visual-anchored and text-anchored orderings to assign prototypes to queries, avoiding the feature-collapse problem of naive cosine matching. Third, hybrid prototype injection concatenates selected dynamic (image-specific) prototypes and a static global memory-bank prototype (updated with momentum η=0.9) into the text token stream fed to SAM 3's fusion encoder. Finally, an iterative information-gain prompting loop greedily selects positive and negative point prompts that maximize entropy reduction in the segmentation probability map, progressively refining boundaries until the information gain drops below a threshold.

## Results

SANO3 achieves a mean mIoU of 34.55% across the eight FG-OVSSRS Bench datasets, outperforming the strongest SAM 3-based baseline (SAM 3 at 32.39%) and the best CLIP-based method (SegEarth-OV at 25.73%). The largest individual gains over SAM 3 are on YRCC (+5.12 mIoU, 57.78 vs. 52.66) and Barley (+8.23 mIoU, 18.78 vs. 10.55), where fine-grained semantic distinctions are most challenging. SANO3 attains the best score on 6 of 8 datasets, with near-parity on SkyScapes (26.83 vs. 26.84) and a slight deficit on RSMI (28.50 vs. 29.17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SANO3 outperforms all nine baselines in average mIoU, with the most direct competitors being SAM 3 (32.39%) and SegEarth-OV3 (32.23%), both of which it surpasses by roughly 2.1–2.3 points on average. Against SAM 3, SANO3 wins on 6 of 8 datasets (Forest, Cloud, Rescue, GIS, YRCC, Barley), ties on SkyScapes (26.83 vs. 26.84), and trails slightly on RSMI (28.50 vs. 29.17). The most dramatic improvement is on Barley, where SANO3 nearly doubles SAM 3's score (18.78 vs. 10.55), indicating that the visual-primitive alignment and iterative prompting are particularly effective for agricultural fine-grained categories. Among CLIP-based methods, SegEarth-OV (25.73%) is the strongest, yet SANO3 still leads by 8.82 points on average; notably, CorrCLIP drops to 19.24% on Cloud and SegEarth-OV to 9.06% on Barley, whereas SANO3 maintains consistent performance across all domains. SegEarth-OV3, the closest SAM 3-based competitor, is essentially matched by SAM 3 itself (32.23 vs. 32.39), suggesting its post-processing gains are marginal, while SANO3's architectural enhancements yield a clear and consistent advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SANO3 builds directly on SAM 3's promptable segmentation architecture, extending it with VFM-guided visual priors (DINOv2 self-similarity clustering) to address fine-grained semantic confusion that SAM 3's native text encoder cannot resolve. It departs from the dominant CLIP-based OVSS lineage (GEM, ProxyCLIP, CorrCLIP, etc.) that modifies attention pooling for dense prediction, and from the closest SAM 3-based competitor SegEarth-OV3, which relies on simple post-processing of the segmentation head. The key conceptual departure is replacing absolute cosine-similarity matching with a Plackett-Luce ranking alignment over visual-conditioned text features, motivated by observed feature collapse in SAM 3's text encoder for semantically subtle categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of FG-OVSSRS Bench is the most broadly useful contribution, providing the first standardized fine-grained remote sensing OVSS evaluation across eight heterogeneous datasets (satellite, aerial, UAV) with expanded synonym vocabularies, filling a gap where prior benchmarks used only coarse land-cover labels. The training-free design makes the approach immediately deployable without task-specific retraining, which is practically important for remote sensing workflows. However, the average mIoU of ~34.55% remains modest, the iterative prompting component (IGVP) contributes only marginal gains in ablation (≤1.3%), and the method's sensitivity to multiple hyperparameters (C, τ, τ_e) limits straightforward adoption. Open questions include whether the ranking-based alignment generalizes beyond the N=4 candidate pool and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-spectral remote sensing inputs.
