## Problem

JEPA-based video self-supervised models like V-JEPA 2 excel at global semantic understanding and prediction but produce noisy, fragmented dense features that fail to capture fine-grained spatial structure needed for localization, geometry, and tracking. The paper identifies that restricting the prediction loss to masked tokens alone causes visible context tokens to degenerate into global aggregators, preventing the emergence of spatially coherent representations. The challenge is to simultaneously preserve dense spatio-temporal structure and capture temporal dynamics within a single unified model.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (processed via a 2D 16×16 convolutional patch embedding) and video clips (processed via a 3D 16×16×2 convolutional patch embedding), with a learnable modality token distinguishing the two pathways. During training, inputs are masked by randomly dropping patch tokens; the encoder processes visible tokens and the predictor reconstructs representations for both masked and context positions. A 3D RoPE positional encoding enables flexible resolution handling (images up to 512×512, videos up to 64 frames at 384×384 during cool-down), and the frozen encoder outputs per-patch dense embeddings usable for depth, segmentation, VOS, as well as global features for action recognition and anticipation.

## Method

V-JEPA 2.1 introduces a Dense Predictive Loss that extends the standard JEPA masked-token L1 objective to all tokens via a context loss weighted by the inverse square-root of each context patch's minimum spatio-temporal distance to any masked token, enforcing local continuity between masked and visible regions. Deep Self-Supervision concatenates outputs from three intermediate encoder blocks with the final layer, fuses them through a lightweight MLP, and trains a multi-level predictor that produces four outputs supervised at each level, allowing local information to flow into final layers. A Multi-Modal Tokenizer applies modality-specific 2D/3D convolutions and a learnable modality embedding to process images and videos in their native form within a shared ViT encoder. The full recipe scales from ViT-L (300M) to ViT-G (2B) parameters on the VisionMix-163M dataset (142M curated images + 19M video samples), with a high-resolution cool-down phase, and supports distillation into ViT-L and ViT-B variants using a frozen teacher and reduced 12-block predictor.

## Results

V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G (2B) achieves state-of-the-art results across a broad range of benchmarks: 7.71 mAP on Ego4D short-term object-interaction anticipation, 40.8 Recall@5 on EPIC-Kitchens-100 action anticipation, 0.307 RMSE on NYUv2 monocular depth estimation (linear probe), 77.7% top-1 accuracy on Something-Something-V2 action recognition, and 72.7 J&F-Mean on YouTube-VOS. On robotic manipulation, the model yields a +20% grasp success rate over V-JEPA 2 AC on a real Franka arm, and on navigation planning it reaches 5.687 ATE on Tartan Drive with 10× faster planning speed than prior work. The 1B ViT-g variant also substantially outperforms V-JEPA 2 (1B) on dense tasks (e.g., 0.350 vs. 0.642 RMSE on NYUv2, 47.8 vs. 24.4 mIoU on ADE20K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest image-based competitor DINOv3 (7B), V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G (2B) wins on NYUv2 depth (0.307 vs. 0.309 RMSE) and KITTI (2.461 vs. 2.346), but trails on ADE20K segmentation (47.9 vs. 55.9 mIoU), Cityscapes (73.5 vs. 81.1), VOC (85.0 vs. 86.6), DAVIS (69.0 vs. 71.1), and YouTube-VOS (72.7 vs. 74.1), reflecting the paper's note that VisionMix lacks highly cluttered scenes. On action recognition (SSv2), V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G achieves 77.7%, surpassing DINOv3 7B (70.1%), InternVideo2-1B (69.7%), VideoPrism (68.5%), and DINOv2 (50.7%), establishing a clear lead in motion understanding. On Ego4D STA, V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G (7.71 mAP) outperforms the previous best method STAformer (5.67) by ~35% and DINOv3 7B (5.68) by ~36%, while on EPIC-Kitchens-100 it reaches 40.8 Recall@5 versus 39.7 for V-JEPA 2. Compared to the direct predecessor V-JEPA 2 (1B), V-JEPA 2.1 delivers dramatic gains on all dense tasks (e.g., 0.642→0.307 RMSE on NYUv2, 24.4→47.9 mIoU on ADE20K) while maintaining or improving global recognition (77.3→77.7% SSv2). On navigation planning, V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G achieves 5.687 ATE on Tartan Drive versus 5.831 for NWM, with 10× faster planning (10.6 s vs. 103.2 s). The 1B ViT-g variant remains competitive with 1B image encoders like DINOv2 and AM-RADIO on depth and segmentation, while clearly outperforming video-only encoders (InternVideo2, SigLIP2) on all dense metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

V-JEPA 2.1 extends the V-JEPA 2 latent mask-denoising framework (itself grounded in LeCun's JEPA formulation) by addressing a specific failure mode: when the prediction loss is applied only to masked tokens, visible context tokens degenerate into global aggregators rather than preserving local spatial structure. The paper departs from V-JEPA 2 by introducing a distance-weighted context loss that supervises all tokens, a multi-level predictor for deep self-supervision across intermediate encoder layers, and modality-specific 2D/3D tokenizers for unified image-video training. It also draws on the data curation strategies of DINOv2 (LVD-142M) and V-JEPA 2 (YT1B-derived video) to construct the VisionMix-163M training set, and positions itself against DINOv2/DINOv3 for dense features and V-JEPA 2 for video dynamics, aiming to unify both capabilities in a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single self-supervised video model can simultaneously produce high-quality dense spatio-temporal features (depth, segmentation, VOS) and strong global semantic understanding (action recognition, anticipation), a combination that prior methods achieved only by choosing one or the other. The 20% zero-shot improvement in real-robot grasping and 10× faster navigation planning suggest practical value for embodied AI beyond benchmark scores. Open questions include whether the dense features transfer to more complex multi-step manipulation, whether the context-loss weighting scheme generalizes to other masking strategies, and whether the approach can be integrated into pixel-level world models for closed-loop planning. The authors note that ADE20K and Cityscapes segmentation remain slightly below the best image-only encoders, likely due to limited exposure to highly cluttered scenes in the training distribution.
