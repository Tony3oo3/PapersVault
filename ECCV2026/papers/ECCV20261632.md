## Problem

Existing event-based multi-small-object tracking methods follow a 'convert-then-detect-and-track' pipeline that projects asynchronous event streams into frame-like representations, causing temporal downsampling that fragments fast-moving trajectories, background redundancy that inflates computation, and loss of the discriminative cues needed to associate textureless small objects. Additionally, no large-scale benchmark with per-event instance-level annotations existed for evaluating event-based MSOT.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw asynchronous event streams from a DAVIS346 camera (346×260 spatial, up to 1 MHz temporal), partitioned into fixed 800 ms packets and voxelized at an extremely fine 1×1 pixel × 1 ms resolution to preserve temporal detail. It produces per-event binary target probabilities, discretized 3D motion direction vectors (azimuth and elevation classes), and instance-level trajectory labels that group events into complete object tracks, enabling both offline evaluation and online streaming inference with a 20 ms sliding-window stride.

## Method

Ev-ISNet reformulates MSOT as 3D spatiotemporal instance segmentation over sparse event point clouds. A lightweight 3D submanifold sparse U-Net extracts per-event features, feeding two parallel MLP heads: one for binary target classification and one for discretized motion direction (derived from the principal eigenvector of local event covariance). Intra-packet association builds a k-NN graph over target events, predicts edge-level instance affinity via a Geometric Consistency network (2-layer MLP on feature differences), prunes edges below a threshold, and extracts connected components with Union-Find to form trajectory segments. Inter-packet association reuses the same edge-prediction weights on cross-packet k-NN candidates to merge segments into continuous tracks, enabling streaming inference. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a joint loss combining segmentation cross-entropy, direction classification, and edge affinity terms, requiring only 7.73 MB of parameters.

## Results

On EV-UAV-Track, Ev-ISNet achieves MOTA 64.48, MOTP 1.29 px, IDF1 48.73, and mIoU 36.49, outperforming the best frame-based method (RVT+SORT, MOTA 37.08) by over 30% and the best point-cloud instance segmentation method (OneFormer3D, MOTA 55.09) by 9.4 points. On the Bee Swarm dataset, Ev-ISNet reaches MOTA 31.5 and IDF1 28.9, surpassing the next-best method HAIS (MOTA 23.1) by 8.4 points. The model is also highly efficient, using only 7.73 MB of parameters and 1.48 s runtime per 8-second sequence, compared to 353.2 MB and 1.57 s for HAIS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ev-ISNet decisively outperforms all 13 baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against frame-based detect-then-track pipelines (CenterTrack, FairMOT, ByteTrack, MOTIP, RVT+SORT/DeepSort/TrackTrack), the MOTA gap ranges from 27.7 to 37.7 points, with the closest competitor RVT+SORT achieving only 37.08 MOTA versus 64.48. SNN-based methods (SpikeYOLO variants, SNNTracker) trail by 29–31 MOTA points, confirming that spiking architectures with short-term context cannot match the long-range temporal modeling of sparse 3D convolutions. Among the closest competitors—3D point-cloud instance segmentation methods adapted to events—OneFormer3D (MOTA 55.09) and HAIS (MOTA 52.71) are surpassed by 9.4 and 11.8 MOTA points respectively, while Ev-ISNet uses 28–46× fewer parameters (7.73 MB vs. 220.4 and 353.2 MB). On the dense Bee Swarm dataset, Ev-ISNet extends its lead to 8.4 MOTA over HAIS and 12.2 over SNNTracker, demonstrating robustness in high-density, highly dynamic scenarios. The method also achieves the lowest runtime (1.48 s) and smallest model size, making it the most efficient option among all compared approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'convert-then-detect-and-track' pipeline in event-based tracking (RVT, SpikeYOLO combined with SORT/DeepSort/TrackTrack) by eliminating the event-to-frame conversion step entirely. It repurposes 3D instance segmentation machinery—submanifold sparse convolutions, k-NN graph construction, and union-find clustering—from static point cloud segmentation (PointGroup, HAIS, OneFormer3D) into a streaming tracking formulation, replacing the centroid-centered instance assumption with geometric-consistency-based edge affinity for slender, elongated trajectories. The direction prediction branch draws on local covariance analysis to handle occlusions, a mechanism not present in prior event trackers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >30% MOTA gap over the best frame-based baselines, combined with a 7.73 MB parameter count (vs. 247–465 MB for competitors), suggests that preserving native event sparsity and temporal resolution is far more important than the field's current assumption that framing is a necessary preprocessing step. The EV-UAV-Track dataset (147 sequences, 2.3M annotated events) fills a critical gap for event-based MSOT research. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales to larger scenes with more objects, whether the 800 ms window and K=15 neighbor settings transfer across different event camera resolutions, and how the method handles camera ego-motion in uncontrolled deployment.
