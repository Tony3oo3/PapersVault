## Problem

RGB-only MLLMs augmented with implicit geometric priors from feed-forward reconstruction consistently underperform explicit 3D-input methods on spatial perception tasks. The authors argue this gap stems not from insufficient geometric information but from a training-paradigm misalignment: text-dominated fine-tuning suppresses geometric representations, and naive last-layer feature concatenation fails to activate the hierarchical structural cues already present in reconstruction encoders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260319/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a short RGB image sequence (4 consecutive frames sampled at 1 FPS) together with a natural language query; during pre-training a red cross marks a single prompted pixel whose 3D coordinate and semantic category must be predicted. All spatial outputs are expressed in a unified first-frame metric coordinate system rounded to two decimal places, ensuring cross-task consistency. Downstream outputs include a 9-DoF 3D bounding box (center, size, rotation) plus a target frame index for grounding, a free-text caption conditioned on object-center coordinates for dense captioning, and a set of (box, category) pairs for video object detection.

## Method

GAP-MLLM introduces a two-stage training paradigm: (1) sparse geometry-semantics joint pre-training, where the MLLM simultaneously predicts the metric 3D coordinate and semantic label of a prompted pixel, forcing structural reasoning to be a primary objective rather than an auxiliary signal; and (2) object-level fine-tuning on grounding, captioning, and detection tasks. Architecturally, a multi-level progressive fusion module replaces naive last-layer concatenation: both the visual branch (Qwen3-VL) and geometric branch (VGGT) produce L=24 layers of spatially aligned tokens, and each layer j receives an independent token-level gating coefficient g = σ(MLP([T^V, T^G])) that adaptively blends the two modalities. The final-layer fused tokens serve as the primary decoder input, while three selected intermediate layers (j = 5, 11, 17) are injected into early LLM decoder blocks via direct addition (a DeepStack-style strategy), preserving hierarchical geometric signals throughout decoding. Ablations confirm that intermediate layers naturally emphasize geometry while later layers favor semantics, validating the layer-dependent gating design.

## Results

On ScanRefer (3D visual grounding), GAP-MLLM-3B achieves Acc@0.25 of 53.1 and Acc@0.5 of 26.0 using only RGB input, surpassing the strongest RGB-only baseline VG-LLM-4B (36.4 / 11.8) by over 11 points on both metrics while using fewer parameters. On Scan2Cap (3D dense captioning), GAP-MLLM-3B attains CIDEr 84.7 and BLEU-4 42.1 at IoU ≥ 0.5, outperforming all RGB-only methods and several explicit 3D-input baselines. On 3D video object detection (EmbodiedScan), GAP-MLLM-3B reaches F1@0.25 of 50.6 (4-frame) and 48.5 (6-frame), exceeding VG-LLM-4B (38.2 / 36.4) and SpatialLM (29.1 / 31.1) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAP-MLLM-3B (RGB-only) outperforms the strongest prior RGB-only method VG-LLM-4B by +16.7 Acc@0.25 and +14.2 Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer, and by +6.1 CIDEr on Scan2Cap. It also surpasses the explicit 3D-input baselines ScanRefer (37.3), 3D-LLM (30.3), and Chat-3D v2 (35.9) on grounding, approaching the top 3D-input methods Video-3D LLM (58.1) and ChatScene (55.5) within ~5 points. On 3D video object detection, GAP-MLLM-3B achieves F1@0.25 of 50.6, beating VG-LLM-4B (38.2) by 12.4 points and SpatialLM (29.1) by 21.5 points, while also exceeding the larger VG-LLM-8B (41.2). On metric 3D reconstruction (ScanNet), GAP-MLLM achieves the best metric-evaluation Overall score (0.0616 m) among all compared methods, outperforming CUT3R (0.1122 m) and MapAnything (0.2953 m), though CUT3R is slightly better under Sim(3)-aligned evaluation. Applying the GAP pre-training to VG-LLM's architecture (VG-LLM-4B w/ GAP) yields consistent gains across all tasks, confirming the paradigm generalizes beyond the Qwen3-VL backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in RGB-only 3D perception with MLLMs (VG-LLM, VLM-3R), which typically fuses last-layer geometric encoder features via simple addition and fine-tunes directly on text-dominated downstream tasks. It repurposes feed-forward reconstruction models (VGGT) not merely as feature providers but as multi-level geometric token sources, and introduces a sparse geometry-semantics joint pre-training objective inspired by DepthLM to explicitly activate structural perception before task adaptation. The multi-level gated fusion with hierarchical DeepStack-style injection draws on Qwen3-VL's token-merging design and DeepStack's progressive token injection, but applies them specifically to balance geometric and semantic contributions across encoder layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that the RGB-only vs. explicit-3D performance gap is a training-paradigm problem (text-dominated fine-tuning suppressing geometric representations) rather than a data-sufficiency problem—reframes how the community should approach 3D perception in MLLMs. The sparse pre-training recipe (≈500K samples, pixel-level supervision equivalent to ~2 images) is computationally cheap and generalizes across backbones (Qwen3-VL, Qwen2.5-VL), making it a practical drop-in improvement. Open questions include whether the approach extends to outdoor/large-scale scenes, whether it benefits from denser geometric supervision, and whether the specific layer-selection heuristic (L1=5, L2=11, L3=17) transfers to other encoder depths.
