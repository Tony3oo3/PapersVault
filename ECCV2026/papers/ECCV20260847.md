## Problem

Prior autoregressive (AR) generative classifiers rely on a fixed token order (e.g., raster-scan), which imposes a restrictive inductive bias that causes the model to depend on partial discriminative cues rather than holistic image understanding. While diffusion-based generative classifiers have been shown to outperform AR counterparts, recent advances in any-order AR models have made a re-examination timely. The paper asks whether marginalizing over multiple token orders can unlock the full classification potential of AR models and close or reverse the gap with diffusion classifiers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260847/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB images, tokenizes them into 256 discrete tokens via a VQ-VAE (LlamaGen tokenizer with 16384 codebook size), and produces a single class label from a set of M classes (e.g., 1000 for ImageNet). Classification is performed by estimating class-conditional log-likelihoods p(x|c_i) for each class and selecting argmax, with the log-prior term ignored under a uniform class assumption. For 1000-class evaluation, results are reported on 2K subsets of the validation set to manage compute, while 200-class benchmarks (IN-R, IN-A) are evaluated in full.

## Method

The core contribution is an order-marginalized generative classifier built on RandAR, an any-order AR model that explicitly conditions on position-instruction tokens. Instead of evaluating a single fixed-order likelihood, the method samples K random token permutations, appends a class token to each permuted sequence, and computes the order-conditional log-likelihood log p(x|π_k, c_i) in a single forward pass per order. These are aggregated via Jensen's inequality lower bound (1/K) Σ log p(x|π_k, c_i), which the authors show significantly outperforms the direct Monte Carlo estimate of log E_π[p(x|π,c)]. The same K sampled orders are shared across all M classes to ensure consistency, and the LlamaGen tokenizer is augmented with latent noise corruption during training to improve robustness to minor token-level perturbations. This yields up to 25× faster inference than diffusion classifiers (which require 100–250 forward passes) while achieving superior in-domain and OOD accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-Val, RandAR-XL/16 achieves 81.3% top-1 accuracy, outperforming DiT-XL (77.2%), SiT-XL (69.7%), and SiT+REPA-XL (73.3%) by 4.1, 11.6, and 8.0 points respectively. On out-of-distribution benchmarks, the gains are more pronounced: RandAR-XL reaches 53.0% on ImageNet-R vs. 40.2% for DiT-XL, and 65.2% on ImageNet-C (Gaussian) vs. 50.5% for DiT-XL. Compared to the state-of-the-art SSL model DINOv2-XL (82.7% IN-Val), RandAR-XL trails by only 1.4% in-domain but surpasses it on 3 of 5 OOD sets (IN-R, IN-S, IN-C Gauss). The method is up to 25× faster than diffusion-based classifiers at equivalent or higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion classifiers, RandAR-XL dominates across all benchmarks: it beats DiT-XL by 4.1 pts on IN-Val (81.3 vs 77.2), 12.8 pts on IN-R (53.0 vs 40.2), and 14.7 pts on IN-C Gauss (65.2 vs 50.5), while being up to 25× faster. Against prior AR-based GCs, the gains are even larger: RandAR-XL outperforms LlamaGen-XL by 25.4 pts on IN-Val (81.3 vs 55.9), VAR-XL by 18.3 pts, and A-VARC+-XL by 9.4 pts, confirming that order marginalization is the key differentiator. Against the joint energy-based model DAT (L/16), RandAR-L wins on IN-Val (78.0 vs 76.4) and all OOD sets except IN-C JPEG. Against DINOv2-XL, RandAR-XL is competitive: it loses by 1.4 pts on IN-Val (81.3 vs 82.7) and 11.2 pts on IN-A (23.3 vs 34.5), but wins on IN-R (+4.4), IN-S (+10.5), and IN-C Gauss (+15.2), and essentially ties on IN-C JPEG (75.1 vs 75.4). On WILDS real-world shift benchmarks (Camelyon17, CelebA, FMoW), RandAR matches or exceeds DiT on in-domain accuracy and substantially outperforms it on OOD worst-group accuracy (e.g., 78.3 vs 58.6 on Camelyon17 OOD at B/16 scale), while also surpassing discriminative baselines (ERM, RWY, σ-stitching) on OOD metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RandAR (Pang et al., CVPR 2025), a decoder-only any-order autoregressive model that explicitly conditions on token positions, repurposing its multi-order generation capability for classification rather than synthesis. It departs from prior AR-based generative classifiers (LlamaGen, VAR, A-VARC+) that relied on fixed raster-scan or scale-wise token orders, identifying this fixed order as a restrictive inductive bias that causes reliance on partial discriminative cues. The work also positions itself against diffusion classifiers (DiT, SiT), which have dominated the generative classification literature, by reframing DMs as implicit AR models following a low-to-high frequency order and showing that explicit order marginalization in AR models captures a more comprehensive signal. A theoretical connection to masked diffusion is established, showing the order-marginalized AR objective equals the MD evidence lower bound in expectation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that order-marginalized AR classifiers outperform diffusion classifiers across all ImageNet benchmarks while being up to 25× faster in inference is a significant practical and conceptual shift, reversing the assumed hierarchy where DMs dominated visual generative classification. Competing with DINOv2 (SOTA self-supervised learning) on OOD benchmarks is a first for generative classifiers, suggesting that the generative paradigm may be more viable for robust classification than previously thought. Open questions include whether any-order AR models can be further scaled, whether image-adaptive token-order predictors (analogous to noise optimization in diffusion classifiers) can close the remaining gap on IN-A, and whether the strong generative classification performance can be distilled into efficient discriminative models.
