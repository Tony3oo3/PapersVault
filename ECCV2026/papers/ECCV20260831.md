## Problem

Existing CZSL methods suffer from two interrelated limitations: (1) ambiguous vision-language alignment when attributes or objects are visually/semantically proximate (e.g., tan vs. brown vs. beige), causing attention to collapse onto identical high-saliency patches, and (2) the absence of explicit conditional interaction between attribute and object pathways in multi-branch architectures, which prevents the model from capturing compositional semantics needed to generalize to unseen attribute-object pairs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder to yield patch tokens and a global [CLS] token. On the text side, soft learnable prompts are prepended to attribute, object, and composition tokens and passed through a frozen CLIP text encoder. The model outputs a predicted attribute-object composition label, evaluated under both closed-world (candidate set restricted to the benchmark split) and open-world (all |A|×|O| pairings considered) protocols, with final predictions integrating scores from the composition branch, the imaginative composition branch, and the product of independent attribute and object probabilities.

## Method

PCI is a two-stage framework built on CLIP. Stage I (BAD) performs bidirectional cross-modal attention to inject text-guided priors into visual patch tokens (Visual Enrichment), then applies entropic optimal transport via differentiable Sinkhorn iterations with text- and patch-side marginal constraints to enforce globally consistent, competitive evidence allocation across patches, regularized by a Jensen-Shannon divergence loss that aligns the model's native attention with the OT plan. Stage II (PCM) freezes all BAD parameters and introduces a learnable bank of M attribute prototypes, dynamically refined by a Query-Transformer over attribute-salient patch tokens; a Gumbel-Softmax mechanism differentiably selects the most relevant prototype, which then conditionally modulates the object feature via FiLM-style affine transformation (γ(p*)⊙f_o + β(p*)), explicitly modeling how attribute semantics reshape object representations to synthesize 'imagined' compositional features. Training uses a combined objective of classification cross-entropy, OT distributional regularization, prototype alignment (bidirectional JS divergence), and orthogonality constraints on the prototype bank.

## Results

PCI achieves state-of-the-art results across all four benchmarks under both Closed-world and Open-world protocols. In the Closed-world setting, PCI attains HM of 60.4 and AUC of 49.0 on UT-Zappos, HM of 40.8 and AUC of 24.1 on MIT-States, HM of 36.4 and AUC of 17.6 on C-GQA, and HM of 98.6 and AUC of 99.2 on Clothing16K. In the more challenging Open-world setting, PCI leads with AUC of 42.1 (UT-Zappos), 9.6 (MIT-States), 4.8 (C-GQA), and 96.4 (Clothing16K), consistently outperforming the strongest prior methods by 0.7–4.6% in AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCI outperforms all 17 compared baselines on every benchmark and metric under both evaluation protocols. The closest competitors are VP-CMJL [38] and CLUSPRO [28]: on UT-Zappos (Closed-world), PCI improves HM by 1.9 points over VP-CMJL (60.4 vs. 58.5) and AUC by 1.1 points (49.0 vs. 47.9); on C-GQA, PCI gains 1.5 HM and 1.3 AUC over VP-CMJL (36.4 vs. 34.9; 17.6 vs. 16.3). On MIT-States, where attribute ambiguity is highest, PCI edges out CLUSPRO by 0.1 HM and 0.3 AUC (40.8 vs. 40.7; 24.1 vs. 23.8). On Clothing16K, PCI achieves a near-perfect Closed-world AUC of 99.2, surpassing AIF [22] by 1.0 points and ATIF [22] by 2.6 points. In the Open-world setting, the margins widen: PCI leads VP-CMJL by 1.6 AUC on UT-Zappos (42.1 vs. 41.4) and by 4.6 AUC on Clothing16K (96.4 vs. 91.8), confirming that the prototype-conditioned imagination mechanism provides the largest gains when the candidate space is most challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCI builds directly on Troika's three-branch (attribute/object/composition) paradigm for CZSL, inheriting its CLIP-based backbone and soft-prompting scheme. It departs from prior multi-path methods (Troika, MSCI, Logic, CANet) in two conceptual ways: (1) replacing vanilla cross-modal attention with entropic optimal transport to enforce globally consistent, competitive text-patch alignment, and (2) introducing a prototype-conditioned FiLM-style modulation that explicitly models how attribute semantics reshape object features, rather than treating the two primitives as independent classifiers. The optimal-transport regularizer draws from the OT literature (Cuturi, Kantorovich) but is a novel application to primitive disentanglement in CZSL, while the compositional modulation is inspired by human concept-formation cognition and FiLM conditioning layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses two concrete failure modes in CZSL—ambiguous vision-language matching for semantically proximate attributes and the absence of explicit cross-primitive interaction—that prior multi-branch architectures leave unaddressed. The two-stage design (disentangle first, then compose) is a clean architectural principle that could transfer to other compositional recognition settings. Open questions include whether the frozen-BAD-then-train-PCM pipeline is optimal versus joint training, whether Gumbel-Softmax prototype selection scales to very large attribute vocabularies (C-GQA already has 453), and whether the approach generalizes beyond attribute-object pairs to richer compositional structures (e.g., multi-attribute or spatial compositions).
