## Problem

Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) models encode scene geometry implicitly in network parameters rather than storing explicit 3D maps, leading to widespread claims in the literature that they are inherently privacy-preserving. This paper demonstrates that this assumption is incorrect: the 3D structure and even approximate appearance of the training scene can be extracted from a trained SCR model through a query-based attack, even when the attacker has no access to training images or explicit scene geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262072/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack consumes batches of proxy images drawn from unrelated natural-image datasets (PASCAL VOC, SUN RGB-D, or synthetic geometric patterns) and a trained SCR model (ACE, GLACE, DSAC*, or ACE-G) under black-box, gray-box, or white-box access. The SCR model's public backbone extracts dense feature maps, which are forwarded to the scene-specific head to obtain per-pixel 3D coordinates in a fixed world frame. The final outputs are a voxelized occupancy grid (2 cm resolution for indoor, 10 cm for outdoor) representing the recovered scene geometry, and synthesized RGB images from arbitrary virtual camera poses produced by a Transformer-encoder + CNN-decoder inversion network.

## Method

The attack exploits the fact that SCR models are locally stable near the training feature manifold but produce erratic predictions for out-of-distribution features induced by unrelated proxy images. A six-stage pipeline first queries the model with proxy batches to obtain dense 3D predictions, then computes a stability score for each feature by measuring the variance of predicted coordinates under small isotropic Gaussian perturbations (n=4 samples, σ=0.01). In the white-box setting, features are further refined via gradient descent to minimize their stability score, pulling them toward the stable regions corresponding to learned geometry. Only the top-p% (20%) most stable predictions per batch are retained, and these are accumulated across many independent batches into a global voxel occupancy grid, where voxels consistently populated across batches correspond to true scene surfaces. Finally, recovered feature vectors are projected onto virtual camera poses and passed through a feature inversion network (five-block Transformer encoder + convolutional upsampling decoder) trained on ScanNet scenes using a proxy-query protocol that mimics the attack's noisy, sparse input conditions, yielding approximate novel-view renderings of the scene.

## Results

On 7-Scenes with ACE, the white-box attack achieves an average F1 of 77.3 at 2 cm and 94.2 at 5 cm, with a half Chamfer Distance of 1.91 cm; the black-box variant already reaches F1 of 62.3/82.8 and CD of 3.12 cm without any model internals. Across four SCR architectures on 7-Scenes (white-box), ACE and GLACE yield the strongest reconstructions (F1² ≈ 77), while DSAC* and ACE-G are more resistant (F1² ≈ 64 and 53 respectively). On Cambridge Landmarks, ACE achieves F1¹⁰ of 59.0 and CD of 0.18 m, confirming the attack transfers to outdoor scenes. For image reconstruction, the white-box attack attains an average PSNR of 15.32 on 7-Scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole explicit numerical baseline is Chelani et al. [9] (3DV 2025), which inverts obfuscated SfM representations using neighborhood information and reports an average PSNR of 16.01 across 7-Scenes. The present attack achieves 15.32 PSNR under a substantially more constrained setting: it has no access to target-scene images, no explicit 3D map, and relies solely on proxy-image queries, so the ~0.7 dB gap is expected. Beyond this single comparison, the paper's main empirical contribution is demonstrating that the attack generalizes across four SCR architectures (ACE, GLACE, DSAC*, ACE-G) and two datasets (indoor 7-Scenes, outdoor Cambridge Landmarks), with ACE being most vulnerable (F1²=77.3 on 7-Scenes) and ACE-G most resistant (F1²=52.5). The black-box variant, requiring only input-output queries, already recovers F1²=62.3 on average, showing that even the weakest access model leaks substantial geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the model-inversion attack paradigm—well established in ML security for image classifiers and SfM-based localization—toward a target that the visual localization community has repeatedly declared immune: Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR). Prior privacy work in localization focused on explicit 3D maps (SfM point clouds, line clouds, coordinate permutations) and their defenses, while SCR was assumed safe precisely because it stores no explicit geometry. The attack itself combines standard ingredients (proxy queries, stability-based filtering via perturbation variance, voxel accumulation, and a feature-inversion network) in a new pipeline, but the conceptual move is showing that the 'implicit encoding' argument does not actually prevent extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding directly invalidates privacy claims made in multiple widely-cited SCR papers (ACE, DSAC++, and others), with practical consequences for any deployment in homes, offices, or medical facilities where scene layout is sensitive. Open questions include: effective defenses for SCR (output perturbation, training-time regularization, access-control policies), whether the attack extends to pose-only APIs that never expose intermediate 3D coordinates, and how query-efficient reconstruction can be made. The moderate image-reconstruction quality (PSNR ≈ 15) and the reliance on pseudo-GT for evaluation also leave room for both stronger attacks and stronger defenses to be developed.
