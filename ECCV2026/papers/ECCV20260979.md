## Problem

Existing event-based feature tracking methods rely on fixed, hand-tuned temporal windows for event accumulation, which cannot adapt to the highly variable motion dynamics encountered in real-world scenes. When motion abruptly changes or ceases, a fixed window either provides insufficient event cues or blurs non-linear motion, causing tracking to degrade or fail entirely. This paper asks whether the accumulation decision can be learned adaptively rather than prescribed by heuristic rules.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an asynchronous event stream (e.g., DAVIS346C at 346×260) and the current tracked patch center. At each sub-timestep (Δt = 0.005 s), the RL agent receives the buffered event representation (P×P×C tensor) plus temporal window metadata and outputs a binary go/stop decision. Upon a stop action, the frozen tracker (BlinkTrack or Deep-EV-Tracker) processes the accumulated events and outputs a 2D displacement (Δx, Δy) that updates the patch center for the next accumulation cycle, making the process fully causal and online.

## Method

The paper formulates event accumulation as a sequential decision-making problem and trains a lightweight RL agent (two CNN layers + three MLP layers) with PPO to choose between 'go' (accumulate the next event frame) and 'stop' (trigger tracker inference). The reward at stop is the negative tracking error scaled by the event-stack length minus a penalty λ for overusing stop, encouraging the agent to gather sufficient motion information without excessive accumulation. A key design choice is using the raw event representation as the agent's state rather than tracker-encoder features, which are misaligned with the decision objective and add latency. The critic network receives privileged information (ground-truth motion speed, occlusion flags, tracking error, and remaining timesteps) available only during training to stabilize value estimation when failures are caused by scene difficulty rather than the agent's decision. The entire framework is plug-and-play: the underlying tracker is frozen, and the RL agent is trained on MultiTrack for ~12 hours on a single GPU, adding only ~0.4 ms per decision at inference.

## Results

On the novel DEFT dataset, BT+Ours achieves the best average FA of 0.642 and EFA of 0.640, improving over the base BlinkTrack (FA 0.378, EFA 0.372) by +0.264/+0.268. DT+Ours improves over Deep-EV-Tracker by an even larger margin of +0.379/+0.375 in FA/EFA. On the standard EC and EDS benchmarks, DT+Ours attains the highest average FA of 0.743 and EFA of 0.686, while BT+Ours reaches 0.734/0.672, both surpassing their respective base trackers and the Kalman-filter-augmented variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The RL-based adaptive temporal agent consistently outperforms all baselines across both the novel DEFT dataset and the standard EC/EDS benchmarks. On DEFT, BT+Ours (FA 0.642) beats BT (FA 0.378) by +0.264 and BT+K.F (FA 0.378) by a similar margin, while DT+Ours (FA 0.556 avg) surpasses DT (FA 0.086 avg) by +0.379. The Kalman filter augmentation provides only a negligible +0.001 improvement over BT on EC/EDS, whereas the RL agent yields +0.033/+0.025 (FA/EFA) for BT and +0.071/+0.067 for DT. Rule-based HASTE [4] and classical ICP/EM-ICP [48,105] are substantially outperformed, with HASTE achieving at most FA 0.442 on EC versus 0.864 for DT+Ours. The largest relative gains appear on DEFT, where base trackers frequently fail under abrupt motion changes (FA dropping to 0.016–0.052), confirming that adaptive temporal windowing is most critical under highly dynamic conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of event-based feature tracking and reinforcement learning, introducing RL (PPO with GAE) to the tracking pipeline for the first time. It builds directly on BlinkTrack and Deep-EV-Tracker as frozen base trackers, departing from their fixed-temporal-window accumulation by replacing hand-tuned heuristics with a learned binary go/stop policy. The privileged-information critic design borrows from asymmetric actor-critic literature in robotics, while the adaptive temporal windowing concept is novel specifically to tracking (as opposed to detection or recognition, where window boundaries are less constrained by sequential prediction). The DEFT dataset extends the EC/EDS benchmark line by targeting the motion-diversity gap the authors identify in existing evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play RL temporal controller offers a general mechanism that could transfer to other event-based sequential tasks where accumulation timing matters, and the finding that naive temporal-interval augmentation actively degrades performance (0.191 vs. 0.378 FA) challenges the intuitive assumption that more training diversity always helps. However, the evidence base is limited: DEFT contains only ~57 seconds across 8 scenarios, the method is validated on just two base trackers, and the large DEFT gains (e.g., 0.378→0.642 FA for BT) have not yet been stress-tested on larger or more varied real-world deployments. Open questions include whether the binary go/stop formulation scales to multi-scale or multi-object tracking, and whether the privileged-information critic can be replaced with self-supervised alternatives for fully online deployment.
