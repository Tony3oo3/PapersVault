## Problem

Multimodal reasoning agents that rely on external tools such as web search suffer from three coupled failures: poor calibration on when to invoke search, over-trust of noisy retrieved evidence without explicit verification, and brittle credit assignment under sparse outcome-level rewards that collapse GRPO's within-group variance. Existing RL finetuning methods provide no fine-grained signal for query quality or evidence filtering, and adding external verifiers at inference time negates the cost advantage of compact open-source models.

## Input / Output

The agent receives an RGB image paired with a natural-language question and produces a structured multi-step reasoning trace in ReAct format, where each step contains <reason>...</reason> natural-language text and an action (tool call or final answer) with parameters. When text search is invoked, the model proposes k candidate queries in a single step (one is randomly executed), and after retrieval it emits a binary usefulness vector in <verify>...</verify> tags (e.g., <verify> 0,1,0,1 </verify>) with a short justification for each retrieved snippet. The final output is a text answer; the verification scores and candidate queries are intermediate structured outputs that are optimized during training but require no external verifier at inference.

## Method

SVRL augments the GRPO objective with a multiplicative trajectory reward r(τ) = (1−α)·r_acc·r_svrl + α·r_fmt, where r_svrl = r_aware · r_count · r_qalign · r_salign. The search-aware factor r_aware uses the pretrained model's tool-free accuracy as a competence prior to label instances as search-free, then downweights trajectories that invoke search on those instances (λ_sa = 0.1), targeting avoidable tool calls without suppressing necessary ones. The query-count factor r_count rewards the number of unique, well-formed candidate queries proposed per search step, encouraging diverse and specific search intent. For self-verification, a train-time oracle (GPT-5 with ground-truth access) labels which proposed queries and retrieved snippets are useful; the agent's self-generated verification scores are rewarded for alignment with these labels via r_qalign and r_salign, but only when the final answer is correct (to prevent reward hacking). Training uses Dr. GRPO (which removes standard-deviation and length normalization to stabilize gradients under trajectory-dependent rewards) with group size G=4 on 5,000 FVQA examples, finetuning Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct; the external verifier is discarded at inference, leaving the agent to perform verification autonomously within its reasoning trace.

## Results

SVRL-full-7B achieves 65.3% LLM-judge accuracy on FVQA-test and 64.7% on InfoSeek, improving over the MMSearch-R1++ baseline by 8.9 and 9.6 points respectively, while simultaneously reducing the search ratio from 80.3% to 61.4% on FVQA-test. On out-of-distribution benchmarks, SVRL gains 6.5 points on MMSearch (60.3 vs. 53.8) and 9.4 points on LiveVQA (57.8 vs. 48.4). The 7B SVRL model approaches GPT-4o with RAG on FVQA-test (65.3 vs. 66.0) and surpasses it on InfoSeek (64.7 vs. 59.1), all while using substantially fewer tool calls.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary adaptive-search baseline MMSearch-R1++, SVRL-full-7B wins on all five benchmarks in accuracy (gains of +0.9 to +9.6 points) while also reducing the search ratio on four of five datasets, indicating a better accuracy-cost tradeoff. Compared to GPT-4o with always-on RAG, SVRL narrows the gap to within 0.7 points on FVQA-test (65.3 vs. 66.0) and actually surpasses it on InfoSeek (64.7 vs. 59.1), but trails on MMSearch (60.3 vs. 62.5), LiveVQA (57.8 vs. 59.6), and SimpleVQA (58.3 vs. 63.4), suggesting residual sensitivity to query language and time-sensitive content. Against the Qwen-2.5-VL-7B direct-answer baseline (no tools), SVRL improves accuracy by 38.6 points on FVQA-test and 44.7 points on InfoSeek, confirming that the learned search-and-verify behavior is the dominant source of gain. The paper also notes that SVRL's test-time scaling curve continues to improve with additional parallel searches (up to 15), whereas MMSearch-R1++ saturates after roughly 5 searches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVRL extends the GRPO-based RL finetuning paradigm (originally developed for text-only math and code reasoning in DeepSeekMath) to multimodal tool-augmented VQA, building directly on the MMSearch-R1 agent setup for text and image search. It departs from MMSearch-R1 by replacing the coarse outcome-level reward with fine-grained trajectory-level signals: a search-aware calibration factor, a query-diversity count, and verifier-aligned self-verification scores for filtering retrieved evidence. The stabilization via Dr. GRPO addresses a known instability in vanilla GRPO under trajectory-dependent rewards, and the self-verification mechanism contrasts with prior web-agent pipelines that rely on external re-ranking or verification stages at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVRL demonstrates that a 7B open-source multimodal model can approach GPT-4o-level accuracy on multi-hop VQA (65.3 vs. 66.0 on FVQA-test) while reducing search ratio and eliminating the need for an external verifier at inference, making tool-augmented reasoning more deployable in resource-constrained settings. Key limitations include dependence on web tool availability and failure modes, potential bias from the training-time GPT-5 verifier labels, and sensitivity to prompt and tool formatting. Open questions include whether the self-verification signal generalizes to richer tool suites and modalities, whether judge dependence can be reduced via self-supervised verification, and how robust the learned behavior is under retrieval distribution shift and evolving web content.
