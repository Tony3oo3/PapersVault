## Problem

Existing compositional text-to-3D generation methods, particularly those based on 3D Gaussian Splatting, parameterize scenes as volumetric density without explicit object boundaries, causing severe inter-object penetrations where entangled representations produce physically implausible scenes. No prior approach resolves collisions at the object-entity level with explicit surface-level geometry, leaving a gap between semantic completeness and spatial plausibility in multi-object scene synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262453/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single scene-level text prompt describing 2–5 objects and produces a penetration-free 3D Gaussian Splatting scene normalized to the [−1,1] cube, with a total Gaussian budget capped at 800k primitives. Internally, the prompt is first parsed by a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-3B LLM into 2D bounding boxes (output in CSS format), which condition a box-conditioned image generator to synthesize a reference image; SAM3D then lifts this image into per-object 3D Gaussians, and a SuperGlue + PnP depth-calibration step aligns the coarse 3D layout to the reference depth before optimization begins.

## Method

CompSAG's core mechanism is a Gaussian Surface Offset (GSO) that mimics physical repulsion: each object maintains a lightweight-MLP SDF proxy surface (Eikonal-regularized, initialized from SAM3D mesh), and colliding Gaussians are identified by negative SDF values, projected onto the zero-level set, and pushed out via an aggregated repulsive force whose magnitude equals the absolute SDF penetration depth and whose direction is filtered by the inter-object separation axis. A momentum-based update with an ease-in-out schedule applies the offset stably until the collision fraction drops below 3%. For appearance, a relation-aware SDS loss distills interacting subject-relation-object pairs from a pre-trained diffusion model, while a Gaussian Surface Rectification module simultaneously aligns Gaussian positions to the SDF boundary and constrains the thinnest-axis rotation to match surface normals, suppressing the spiky artifacts that SDS typically introduces. The entire pipeline is initialized by a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen2.5-3B layout LLM trained on ~5k synthetic scene instructions from the SVG dataset, providing precise 2D spatial grounding that reduces initial collisions before GSO refinement.

## Results

On the main compositional text-to-3D benchmark (Table 1), CompSAG with the fine-tuned layout LLM and user-adjusted layout (Q*+U) achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CLIP 33.87, B-VQA 58.11, GPT-CoT 56.64, and CVR 0.038. The CVR (Colliding Volume Ratio) metric is particularly striking: CompSAG reduces inter-object penetration to 0.034–0.042, compared to 0.103–0.278 for all baselines, confirming near-complete elimination of interpenetration. On T³ Bench Multiple Objects (Table 2), CompSAG scores 55.5 Quality, 41.9 Alignment, and 4.42/5 in user study, outperforming the next-best CompGS (54.2, 37.9, 4.15). Generation time is 30 minutes per scene, matching the fastest compositional baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompSAG outperforms all 10 baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest compositional 3DGS baselines, CompSAG (Q*+U) leads CompGS by +4.49 on B-VQA (58.11 vs 53.62) and +5.41 on GPT-CoT (56.64 vs 51.23), while reducing CVR from 0.186 to 0.038 — a 5× reduction in inter-object penetration. Against Layout-your-3D, the closest competitor on CVR, CompSAG achieves 0.034 vs 0.103, a 3× improvement, while also gaining +5.54 on B-VQA. Non-compositional methods (ProlificDreamer, MVDream, GaussianDreamer) are far behind on B-VQA and GPT-CoT, confirming that compositional decomposition is essential for multi-object fidelity. On T³ Bench, CompSAG edges out CompGS by +1.3 Quality and +4.0 Alignment, with a user-study preference of 4.42 vs 4.15. The ablation (Table 4) shows GSO contributes the largest single gain (+6.95 B-VQA when removed), followed by GSR (+5.09) and relation-aware SDS (+2.81).

## Relation to Prior Work

CompSAG sits at the intersection of three active research threads: compositional text-to-3D generation via 3DGS (CompGS, Layout-Your-3D, GALA3D, CG3D), surface-aligned Gaussian regularization (SuGaR, GS-Pull), and SDS-based appearance distillation (DreamFusion). Its key departure from prior compositional 3DGS methods is the introduction of per-object SDF proxy surfaces that enable explicit, surface-level collision detection and resolution via a physics-inspired repulsive force (GSO), whereas existing approaches either ignore surface geometry entirely or rely on coarse bounding-sphere penalties (TCL). The Gaussian Surface Rectification module repurposes SuGaR/GS-Pull alignment losses in a compositional setting to suppress SDS-induced spiky artifacts, and the layout-guided initialization leverages a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-3B LLM on Synthetic Visual Genome for spatial grounding, building on the LLM-for-layout trend in GALA3D and Layout-Your-3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Inter-object penetration has been a persistent but under-addressed failure mode in compositional 3D generation; CompSAG demonstrates that maintaining lightweight per-object SDFs and applying momentum-based repulsive offsets is both effective (CVR drops from ~0.28 to ~0.03) and cheap (~8 min of a 30-min pipeline), offering a practical module that other compositional 3DGS pipelines could adopt. Open questions remain around scalability to dense scenes (hard Gaussian caps limit per-object fidelity), degenerate symmetric collision configurations where normal-based forces cancel, and the rigid-body assumption that prevents modeling of soft or deformable objects. The authors suggest coupling the penetration-free output with differentiable physics solvers (MPM/FEM) as a path toward dynamic 4D generation, positioning CompSAG as a strong geometric initialization for downstream simulation.
