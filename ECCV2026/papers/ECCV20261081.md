## Problem

Existing video diffusion world models are restricted to single-agent control and fail to associate specific actions with their correct subjects when multiple agents share a scene. This action-binding failure stems from a fundamental limitation of attribute binding in diffusion models, which tend to ignore or merge multiple conditioning signals, making multi-agent simulation in a single video infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261081/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes initial video frames at 512×512, 2D position coordinates for each subject, a text description of the game environment, and a sequence of discrete per-subject actions (from a unified 25-action space shared across 46 games) as input. It jointly produces the next video frame and updated subject position coordinates in an autoregressive rollout. Notably, actions are relative to each subject's current orientation (e.g., 'forward' means one tile in the direction it faces), so the model must infer orientation from pixels rather than receiving absolute directional commands.

## Method

ActionParty introduces subject state tokens (2D coordinates) that are concatenated with video tokens and jointly denoised within a DiT, following an update-and-render paradigm analogous to game engines. Cross-attention with a strict binding mask (M_CA) ensures each subject's state token only attends to its own action embeddings, while self-attention with a spatial mask (M_SA) blocks subject-to-subject communication to prevent state mixing. A 3D RoPE biasing mechanism anchors each subject token to the video tokens at its previous-frame spatial position, reducing the model's task from global disambiguation to local refinement. The model is fine-tuned from Wan2.1-1.3B using flow matching loss over a 5-frame autoregressive context window, with minimal overhead (only N×T extra tokens per subject) compared to multi-view approaches.

## Results

On the Melting Pot benchmark (46 2D games, up to 7 players), ActionParty achieves 0.779 Movement Accuracy (MA), 0.903 Subject Preservation (SP), and 0.886 Detection Rate (DR), substantially outperforming the best baseline (Text-Action) which scores only 0.158 MA, 0.668 SP, and 0.433 DR. In terms of visual fidelity, ActionParty attains the best LPIPS (0.0102), PSNR (36.35 dB), and FVD (17.16), indicating generated videos closely match ground-truth. For context-dependent 'Interact' actions, ActionParty reaches 0.774 Effect Accuracy, more than doubling the best baseline (0.326 for Text-Action).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActionParty dominates all four baselines on every action-binding metric. Against the strongest baseline, Text-Action (same autoregressive backbone but using text descriptions of actions instead of explicit subject state tokens), ActionParty improves MA from 0.158 to 0.779 (5×), SP from 0.668 to 0.903, and DR from 0.433 to 0.886. The Zero-shot I2V baseline (Wan2.1-14B prompted with text instructions) performs worst on MA (0.027) and FVD (281.55), confirming that even a 10× larger model cannot resolve action-subject binding from text alone. The View-based baseline (generating per-subject 3×3 grid views) achieves the lowest MA (0.013) and SP (0.237), showing that separate views without explicit spatial anchoring fail to bind actions correctly. The Pretrained AR baseline (no action control) serves as a random-action lower bound with MA of 0.065. Notably, ActionParty also achieves the best visual fidelity metrics (LPIPS 0.0102, PSNR 36.35, FVD 17.16), indicating that correct action binding directly translates to more accurate video generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ActionParty builds on the autoregressive video DiT paradigm (Wan2.1, Diffusion Forcing, Self-Forcing) and the world-model framing of Genie/GameNGen, but departs fundamentally from the single-agent assumption that has defined this line of work. Conceptually, it draws on attribute-binding literature (GLIGEN, LLM-grounded video diffusion) for the idea of explicit spatio-temporal grounding, and on game-engine architecture (state update vs. rendering) to structure information flow via attention masks. Unlike concurrent multi-agent approaches (Multiverse, Solaris) that generate separate per-subject views—making binding trivial but scaling linearly in actors—ActionParty generates one shared video and solves binding through subject state tokens with RoPE spatial biasing, a mechanism not previously applied to action conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies and resolves what appears to be a fundamental bottleneck in action-conditioned video generation: even the most powerful video models (Veo 3) cannot reliably associate actions with the correct subject in a multi-agent scene. The subject-state-token + RoPE-biasing recipe is lightweight (6% overhead for 7 players) and could be grafted onto other video DiTs, making it a potentially reusable component for future world models. However, the evaluation is confined to 2D tile-based games with discrete actions and identical-looking sprites, leaving open whether the approach transfers to 3D, continuous-action, or partially-observed settings. The requirement for explicit initial subject positions also limits its applicability to open-world scenarios where subjects must be discovered from pixels alone.
