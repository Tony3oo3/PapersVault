## Problem

Extending T2V/I2V foundation models to subject-to-video (S2V) generation creates an inherent trade-off: in-pair training data (reference extracted from the target video) causes copy-and-paste artifacts that degrade motion, aesthetics, and prompt alignment, while cross-pair data (reference from a different video) eliminates copy-paste but weakens subject consistency. Existing S2V methods optimize one dimension at the expense of the other, and most rely on large-scale noisy datasets rather than high-quality human-aligned data.

## Input / Output

The method takes one to four reference images (which may be uncropped, with subjects occupying only a small fraction of the frame) and a free-form text prompt as input. A multi-stage prompt rephraser (using Qwen 3 and a VLM) rewrites the user prompt to match training caption style before generation. The base MM-DiT produces 480P video, which is optionally refined to 720P via a super-resolution stage that also incorporates reference subjects for identity preservation.

## Method

LibraGen builds on a Seedance-style MM-DiT and injects reference subjects by concatenating VAE-encoded reference frames after video frames along the temporal dimension, with binary flags marking reference tokens—requiring no extra vision encoders or segmentation. The Tune-to-Balance training paradigm first fine-tunes separate LoRAs on in-pair and cross-pair data, then linearly interpolates their weights to balance subject consistency against foundation-model capabilities. Two tailored DPO pipelines are then applied and merged: Consis-DPO constructs preference pairs by manipulating temporal RoPE offsets on reference embeddings to degrade identity in negatives, while Real-Fake DPO uses in-pair model outputs (which exhibit copy-paste artifacts) as negatives against real training videos to restore motion and visual quality. At inference, a time-dependent dynamic CFG schedule progressively shifts guidance weight from text (early denoising, structural/motion) to reference (late denoising, fine-grained identity details), enabling fine-grained control over the prompt-fidelity vs. reference-fidelity trade-off.

## Results

On an in-house benchmark of 200 diverse single- and multi-subject test cases, LibraGen achieves the best Motion Smoothness (0.5380), Motion Quality (0.9930), Aesthetic Score (0.6496), and Text Alignment (3.594) among all compared methods. It matches Vidu Q1 on Visual Quality (2.795) and trails Kling O1 slightly on IQA (71.60 vs. 72.84) and VQ (2.795 vs. 2.797). In subject-consistency GSB ratios, LibraGen achieves positive win rates against every baseline, peaking at 0.700 against MAGREF in the 4-reference setting. All results are obtained using only ~9,000 thousand-scale training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two commercial baselines, LibraGen wins on motion (MS, MQ) and text alignment (TA) over both Vidu Q1 and Kling O1, while matching Vidu Q1 on VQ and trailing Kling O1 by a narrow margin on IQA (71.60 vs. 72.84) and VQ (2.795 vs. 2.797). Against the two open-source baselines, the gap is substantial: LibraGen surpasses MAGREF by 0.155 in MS, 0.008 in MQ, and 1.810 in TA, and outperforms Phantom by 0.154 in MS, 0.006 in MQ, and 1.596 in TA. In subject-consistency GSB ratios (Table 2), LibraGen achieves positive win rates across all reference-count settings (1–4 images) against every baseline, with the largest margins against MAGREF (0.423–0.700) and Phantom (0.286–0.500), and smaller but consistent advantages over Vidu Q1 (0.100–0.154) and Kling O1 (0.071–0.100). The authors note that MAGREF's condition-injection mechanism (concatenating all references into one frame) causes it to degrade on small-scale subjects, explaining its particularly large gap to LibraGen.

## Relation to Prior Work

LibraGen builds directly on the Seedance 1.0 MM-DiT architecture and positions itself within the established S2V generation literature, which has largely split into two camps: in-pair methods (extracting references from the target video, e.g., Phantom, MAGREF) that suffer from copy-paste artifacts, and cross-pair methods (sourcing references from separate clips) that sacrifice subject consistency. The paper's conceptual departure is framing S2V extension as a task-incremental learning balance game rather than a straightforward fine-tuning problem, and its technical departure is a 'quality-over-quantity' data curation pipeline that distills million-scale raw data into a thousand-scale human-aligned subset, combined with a Tune-to-Balance post-training paradigm using LoRA model merging and dual DPO pipelines (Consis-DPO and Real-Fake DPO). Unlike prior S2V methods that require additional vision encoders or explicit subject segmentation, LibraGen injects reference information through simple temporal concatenation in the VAE latent space with binary flags, keeping the architectural modification minimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim that thousand-scale high-quality training data can match or exceed million-scale noisy datasets challenges the prevailing scale-driven assumption in video generation and offers a practical recipe for labs with limited compute or data curation budgets. The balance-game framing and the Tune-to-Balance paradigm (SFT merging + dual DPO + dynamic CFG) provide a reusable template for extending any T2V/I2V foundation model to new conditional tasks without catastrophic forgetting. Open questions include whether the quality-over-quantity principle generalizes beyond the Seedance backbone and to longer or higher-resolution videos, whether the in-house 200-case benchmark is sufficiently diverse to support the strong GSB-ratio claims, and how the approach scales when the base model itself is updated or when the number of reference subjects grows beyond four.
