## Problem

Image restoration is fundamentally limited by the distortion-perception tradeoff: minimizing pixel-wise error collapses to over-smoothed conditional means, while optimizing perceptual realism introduces structural deviations. Existing remedies—posterior sampling via diffusion or multi-stage MMSE-then-refine pipelines—remain computationally expensive and architecturally complex, requiring dozens to hundreds of sampling steps. The paper asks whether a single, unified transport can directly balance both objectives in just a few inference steps without decomposing the problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (blur, noise, low resolution, missing patches, or grayscale) and produces a restored full-color image. Internally, the degraded image is encoded by a pretrained Tiny AutoEncoder into a 16-channel latent z₀, and the flow model transports it to a clean latent z₁ in 3–5 ODE integration steps (K=3 for most tasks, K=5 for blind face restoration). Training uses FFHQ at 512×512 (BFR) or 256×256 (other tasks); the convolution-only U-Net backbone contains only 21–32M parameters, roughly 5–10× smaller than diffusion-based baselines.

## Method

PCFlow parameterizes a direct velocity field v_θ(z_t, t) in latent space via a Latent Consistency Flow Matching (LCFM) objective that enforces geometric consistency across neighboring timesteps, straightening the transport path for few-step inference. To counter the L₂ regression-to-mean tendency, a Latent Consistency Perceptual Loss (LCPL) is applied to the predicted trajectory endpoints, using both an external E-LatentLPIPS network and internal decoder-feature alignment to steer the flow toward perceptually sharp data manifolds. Because the structural (LCFM) and perceptual (LCPL) gradients conflict in low-SNR regimes, the paper introduces an asymmetric conflict-free gradient projection: when the two gradients have negative inner product, the conflicting component of the structural gradient is orthogonally removed while the perceptual gradient is preserved intact, combined with an SNR-adaptive λ schedule that ramps perceptual influence from zero at early timesteps to full strength near the clean target. Training proceeds in two stages—250 epochs of pure reconstruction followed by 250 epochs with perceptual supervision and gradient alignment—using a lightweight convolution-only U-Net (no attention) over the Tiny AutoEncoder latent space.

## Results

On the primary Blind Face Restoration task (CelebA-Test), PCFlow achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 35.89 and NIQE of 3.95 using only 32M parameters and 5 inference steps (K=5), outperforming ELIR (FID 44.64) and PMRF (FID 37.22) while running at 42.62 FPS versus 33.11 and 0.57 FPS respectively. On CelebAdult, PCFlow attains the best FID of 98.85. Across the four additional restoration tasks (super-resolution, denoising, inpainting, colorization), PCFlow consistently improves FID over ELIR by 1.5–6.5 points while using fewer parameters (21M vs. 27M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ELIR (the closest architectural baseline, also using latent consistency flow matching), PCFlow wins decisively on perceptual quality: FID drops from 44.64 to 35.89 on CelebA-Test and NIQE from 5.26 to 3.95, while PCFlow is both smaller (32M vs. 37.5M params) and faster (42.62 vs. 33.11 FPS). The trade-off is a modest loss in distortion metrics (PSNR 24.44 vs. 25.56, SSIM 0.6680 vs. 0.7030), consistent with the distortion-perception tradeoff. Against PMRF (183M params, 25 steps), PCFlow achieves a better FID (35.89 vs. 37.22) and NIQE (3.95 vs. 4.12) at roughly 75× higher throughput (42.62 vs. 0.57 FPS), though PMRF retains an edge in PSNR (25.85 vs. 24.44). Against DifFace (182M params, 100 steps), PCFlow improves FID from 37.43 to 35.89 and NIQE from 4.37 to 3.95 while being 5.7× smaller and 55× faster. Against CodeFormer (94M params), PCFlow is 3× smaller and substantially better on FID (35.89 vs. 52.23) and NIQE (3.95 vs. 4.65). On the four secondary tasks (SR, denoising, inpainting, colorization), PCFlow consistently beats ELIR in FID (e.g., 45.50 vs. 49.25 on SR; 45.21 vs. 51.72 on colorization) while PMRF retains a slight PSNR/SSIM advantage, confirming that the unified transport formulation shifts the operating point toward better perceptual quality without the two-stage MMSE-then-refine pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCFlow builds directly on ELIR's latent consistency flow matching framework and architecture, but departs from the prevailing two-stage paradigm (MMSE estimation followed by generative refinement) by learning a single direct transport from degraded to clean latents. The perceptual supervision is integrated via a consistency-style loss (LCPL) that enforces perceptual alignment between adjacent trajectory predictions, drawing on both external (E-LatentLPIPS) and internal (decoder-feature) perceptual objectives. The conflict-free gradient projection adapts the asymmetric projection idea from multi-task gradient surgery [28] to the specific setting of structural vs. perceptual objectives in flow matching, with an SNR-adaptive schedule that modulates perceptual influence over the transport trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the dramatic efficiency gain: a 32M-parameter, 5-step model matches or exceeds 183M-parameter, 25-step diffusion baselines on perceptual quality, making high-quality restoration feasible in latency-sensitive settings. The conflict-free gradient alignment technique is a reusable recipe for any multi-objective generative training where structural and perceptual losses compete. However, all experiments are confined to face restoration (FFHQ/CelebA) at 256×256 or 512×512, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to broader restoration domains (e.g., natural images, medical imaging) or higher resolutions where the distortion-perception tradeoff is more severe. The paper also does not fully resolve the tradeoff—PCFlow trades modest PSNR/SSIM for FID gains—so the fundamental tension remains.
