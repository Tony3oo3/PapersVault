## Problem

Label-deficient concealed object segmentation (LDCOS) suffers from two complementary limitations: mean-teacher consistency frameworks accumulate errors on ambiguous concealed boundaries, while SAM-based pseudo-labeling is one-way and domain-mismatched. Standard homogeneous co-training cannot resolve this because architecturally identical networks share correlated failure modes on concealed targets, and one-way distillation prevents the domain-adapted segmenter from reciprocally improving the foundation model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260706/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images resized to 352×352 with sparse supervision (point or scribble annotations for weak supervision, or a subset of fully labeled images for semi-supervision). During training, K=12 stochastic augmentations (flips, rotations, scales) are applied to each image for SAM ensemble pseudo-label generation. At inference, only the lightweight student segmenter (ResNet-50 or PVT-V2 backbone) is deployed, producing a binary segmentation mask at the same spatial resolution as the input, incurring no additional cost over the base segmenter.

## Method

SCALER alternates two optimization phases within each training batch. In Phase I, the segmenter is trained under a mean-teacher framework with dual pseudo-label supervision from its own EMA teacher and a frozen SAM ensemble, using entropy-based image-level weighting to down-weight uncertain samples and uncertainty-based pixel-level weighting to attenuate ambiguous boundary pixels, with piecewise refinement that applies binary masking for difficult samples and strong-augmentation mining for simple ones. In Phase II, the roles reverse: the fixed segmenter generates consensus pseudo-labels to update SAM via two losses tailored to SAM's inductive bias—an augmentation invariance loss that reinforces SAM's pretrained geometric robustness, and a noise resistance loss that applies only pixel-level filtering (deliberately omitting image-level entropy gating) so SAM learns from all samples including difficult ones. This asymmetric design, where each model's loss is matched to its own properties rather than applying a shared co-training rule, is the core distinction from both homogeneous co-training and one-way distillation, and convergence is established via block coordinate descent analysis.

## Results

On the strongest WSCOS setting (scribble supervision, PVT-V2 backbone, SAM3), SCALER achieves Fβ of 0.830/0.828/0.782/0.843 and Eφ of 0.941/0.875/0.917/0.927 on CHAMELEON/CAMO/COD10K/NC4K, outperforming the best prior method SEE by 4.27% (scribble) and 5.72% (point) in average Fβ. Under semi-supervised 1/8 labeled data with PVT-V2 and SAM3, SCALER reaches Fβ of 0.862/0.789/0.753/0.823 across the four COD benchmarks. SCALER also improves the foundation model itself: the SAM3 fine-tuned via SCALER achieves Fβ of 0.853 on CHAMELEON (scribble), surpassing the SAM3+SAM3 homogeneous co-training baseline (0.782) by 7.1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCALER consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across eight LDCOS tasks. Against the two most direct SAM-based competitors, SCALER surpasses WS-SAM [16] and SEE [17] on every COD, PIS, and TOD benchmark under both weakly- and semi-supervised settings; the gap is largest with stronger backbones (PVT-V2 + SAM3), where SCALER gains 4.27% (scribble) and 5.72% (point) in average Fβ over SEE. Against raw SAM and SAM-Adapter [7], SCALER improves Fβ by 15–25 points, confirming that the asymmetric mutual learning loop far exceeds one-way pseudo-label distillation. In the co-training comparison (Table 9), replacing homogeneous segmenter pairs with the heterogeneous Seg+SAM architecture improves all three co-training methods (Co-teaching, CPS, UCMT), but applying SCALER's asymmetric Phase I/II losses on top of the heterogeneous architecture yields further gains of 1–2 points in Fβ, isolating the contribution of the model-specific optimization. Under semi-supervised 1/16 labeled data, SCALER outperforms PGCL [1], EPS [29], and CoSOD [5] by 1–3 points in Fβ on COD10K. Cross-task experiments (Table 10) show SCALER's Phase II also improves UniMatch, S³D, SAPNet, and EBSA on semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, infrared target detection, and shadow detection, confirming generalizability beyond COS.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCALER sits at the intersection of three research threads: the mean-teacher consistency paradigm for semi-supervised segmentation (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017), SAM-based pseudo-labeling for concealed object segmentation (WS-SAM, SEE), and co-training with multiple models (Co-teaching, CPS, UCMT). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) replacing one-way SAM-to-segmenter distillation with a bidirectional mutual learning loop, and (2) replacing homogeneous co-training (identical architectures) with a heterogeneous specialist-generalist pairing where each model receives a distinct, bias-aware optimization strategy rather than a shared symmetric loss. The SAM-Adapter mechanism provides the concrete interface for making SAM learnable within this loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core principle—that a lightweight task-specific model can productively guide a large foundation model under label scarcity, provided optimization is asymmetric and tailored to each model's inductive bias—generalizes beyond COS, as demonstrated by cross-task experiments on semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, infrared detection, and shadow detection. The paper identifies three failure modes (cold-start, systematic co-failure, capacity mismatch) that delineate when the framework degrades gracefully. Open questions include whether the asymmetric principle extends to other foundation model families (e.g., diffusion-based segmenters), whether the convergence guarantees hold under more aggressive non-stationarity, and whether the 60-epoch convergence advantage persists at larger scale or with more complex backbones.
