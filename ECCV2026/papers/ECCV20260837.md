## Problem

Diffusion models reconstruct low-frequency structure before high-frequency detail in the reverse process, yet the standard forward process injects white noise that corrupts all frequencies uniformly, creating a forward-reverse mismatch that hinders fine-texture synthesis. Existing blue-to-white noise transitions (e.g., BNDM) interpolate Cholesky factors in Euclidean space, producing off-geodesic covariance trajectories with larger power fluctuations and anisotropic spectral evolution, and rely on a single fixed schedule that is sensitive to dataset shifts and cannot adapt to per-image frequency characteristics.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the method takes a sample from a standard Gaussian prior and iteratively denoises it through a U-Net over T=250 sampling steps to produce a synthetic RGB image (64×64, 128×128, or 256×256 depending on the dataset). For image super-resolution, the low-resolution input is concatenated along the channel dimension with the noisy image as the U-Net input, and the adaptive temperature τ_z is predicted from the low-resolution image rather than a learned latent. The denoising network outputs two heads: one predicting the clean-minus-colored-noise target and one predicting the covariance-difference correction term.

## Method

The paper decomposes time-varying noise covariance evolution into a transition path on the SPD manifold and a transition schedule along it. For the path, the covariance evolves along the Bures-Wasserstein geodesic from a blue-noise endpoint Σ_b to the white-noise endpoint I, yielding a closed-form coloring operator C_γ = (1−γ)QΛ^{1/2}Q^T + γI with position-independent sensitivity to the schedule, smaller noise power fluctuations, and isotropic power spectrum evolution compared to Cholesky interpolation or LE/AIRM geodesics. For the schedule, a VAE-style encoder q_φ maps the clean image to a latent z, and a small MLP p_θ predicts an adaptive temperature τ_z that modulates the sigmoid transition speed per image, avoiding per-dataset retuning. The U-Net denoiser is trained with a two-head loss (predicting h_1 = x_0 − C_γ ε and h_2 = α_{t−1}(QΛ^{1/2}Q^T − I)ε) jointly with the encoder and temperature predictor under a KL regularizer, while the forward process blends colored noise with the clean image via x_t = α_t C_γ ε + (1−α_t) x_0.

## Results

The method achieves the best FID across all four datasets: 6.17 on AFHQ-Cat (64²), 8.47 on AFHQ-Cat (128²), 6.19 on CelebA (64²), and 8.26 on LSUN-Church (64²), outperforming the closest baseline BNDM by 1.78, 0.99, 0.98, and 3.09 FID points respectively. On the extended evaluation (Table 1b), the method also attains the lowest KID on every dataset (e.g., 0.0034 vs. 0.0052 for BNDM on AFHQ-Cat 64²) and the highest Precision in most cases (e.g., 0.79 vs. 0.73 for BNDM), while maintaining Recall comparable to baselines. The method also extends to DDIM (FID 6.33 vs. 7.11 for BNDM-DDIM on AFHQ-Cat) and image super-resolution (PSNR 30.01 vs. 29.30 for BNDM on AFHQ-Cat 64²→128²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against five baselines: DDPM (stochastic), DDIM, IHDM (inverse heat dissipation), IADB (iterative alpha-deblending), and BNDM (blue noise for diffusion models, the direct predecessor). It wins on FID against all five baselines on every dataset, with the largest margin over IHDM (up to 9.5 FID on LSUN-Church) and the most relevant margin over BNDM (1.78 FID on AFHQ-Cat 64², 3.09 on LSUN-Church 64²). On KID, the method also outperforms BNDM and IADB on all reported datasets (e.g., 0.0034 vs. 0.0052 on AFHQ-Cat 64²). Precision is highest for the proposed method in most settings (0.79 vs. 0.73 for BNDM on AFHQ-Cat 64²), while Recall is slightly lower than BNDM on AFHQ-Cat 64² (0.45 vs. 0.49) but comparable elsewhere. Notably, on the diverse AFHQ dataset (cat/dog/wildlife), BNDM underperforms IADB (FID 12.79 vs. 11.06), whereas the proposed method achieves 9.51, demonstrating the benefit of the adaptive schedule over a fixed one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Huang et al. (BNDM, SIGGRAPH 2024), which introduced a blue-to-white noise transition in the forward diffusion process to better align corruption with the coarse-to-fine reverse denoising. The key departure is geometric: BNDM interpolates Cholesky factors in Euclidean space, while this paper recognizes that covariance matrices reside on the SPD manifold and should evolve along a Riemannian geodesic, specifically the Bures-Wasserstein geodesic. The broader lineage includes Rissanen et al.'s inverse heat dissipation formulation and the deterministic diffusion family (IADB, DDIM), but this work is the first to explicitly frame the noise covariance transition as a path-selection problem on the SPD manifold and to decouple path geometry from transition schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Riemannian-geometry framing provides a principled, closed-form alternative to ad-hoc Euclidean interpolation for time-varying noise covariances, and the adaptive per-image schedule removes the need for per-dataset retuning that plagues fixed-schedule methods. However, all experiments are in pixel space with U-Net backbones on relatively small datasets (≤256×256), leaving open whether the BW-geodesic advantage persists in latent diffusion models, with transformer denoisers, or at higher resolutions where frequency structure is more complex. The modest but consistent FID/KID gains (roughly 15–30% relative over BNDM) suggest the forward-noise geometry is a real but secondary lever compared to architecture and training scale.
