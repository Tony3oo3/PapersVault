key_contribution: A modular multi-stream architecture (SignBind-LLM) that decomposes sign language translation into independently pre-trained experts for continuous signing, fingerspelling, and lipreading, fused via learned cross-attention temporal alignment and decoded by a pre-trained LLM, achieving state-of-the-art on How2Sign (23.1 B-4), BOBSL (7.0 B-4), and ChicagoFSWild+ (73.6% letter accuracy) with 124× less training compute than prior methods.
limitations: The two-stage design is vulnerable to LLM hallucination when fused pseudo-glosses are ambiguous (e.g., 'LOWER HOUSE' translated as 'basement' instead of 'lower body'), and the lipreading module captures only speech-derived mouthings rather than the full range of sign-language mouth gestures integral to grammatical marking. The approach also depends on the quality of GPT-4o-generated pseudo-glosses as an upper bound on expert supervision.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that at matched decoder scale (250M Flan-T5-Base), the multi-stream architecture already surpasses all prior methods (16.5 vs. 15.5 B-4), confirming the gains are architectural rather than a scaling artifact—a clean result that is somewhat counterintuitive given the field's recent emphasis on larger LLMs. The ablation showing lipreading as the single most impactful input (−8.5 B-4 when removed) is also a useful empirical anchor, though not deeply surprising given the paper's motivation. Overall, the results are solid and well-supported by ablations across three benchmarks, but the core idea (decompose, specialise, fuse) follows established patterns in multi-task learning.

## Problem

Current gloss-free sign language translation systems force a single network to simultaneously learn continuous signing, high-speed fingerspelling, and asynchronous non-manual cues, resulting in systematic mistranslation of fingerspelled proper nouns and technical terms and underuse of lip movements that carry up to 40% of linguistic information. The temporal misalignment between mouthings and manual signs further prevents naive frame-level fusion from capturing their correspondence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261853/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sign language video as input, which is split via MediaPipe into full-frame streams (224×224, 16-frame segments) for manual recognition and tightly-cropped face regions for lipreading. The visual pipeline produces an intermediate pseudo-gloss sequence in sign-language word order (lacking function words), which a pre-trained encoder-decoder LLM (Flan-T5) then translates into a fluent English sentence. The LLM is trained independently on (pseudo-gloss, English) text pairs and never sees visual features, enabling flexible swapping of decoder sizes without retraining the visual pipeline.

## Method

SignBind-LLM decomposes translation into three dedicated expert streams—continuous signing, fingerspelling, and lipreading—each pre-trained independently via CTC loss on approximately two million GPT-4o-generated pseudo-gloss sequences, eliminating manual gloss annotation. A lightweight MLP segment router with straight-through Gumbel-Softmax assigns each temporal segment to the appropriate manual expert (Sign, Fingerspelling, or Rest), gating their outputs at fusion time. The lipreading stream (masked ViT with 50% masking) is integrated via multi-head cross-attention where manual-stream positions query lipreading features, encoding the inductive bias that manual signs are the primary lexical channel and mouthings provide disambiguation; no fixed temporal offset is imposed, allowing the model to learn variable asynchrony. A shallow transformer encoder models long-range dependencies in the fused representation, and the entire fusion module is trained via CTC to emit the correct pseudo-gloss sequence. Training follows a strictly staged curriculum: experts are pre-trained and frozen before the fusion module is trained, and the LLM is fine-tuned separately on text pairs, requiring only 259 GPU-hours total.

## Results

On How2Sign, SignBind-LLM with Flan-T5-XL (3.0B) achieves 23.1 BLEU-4, 50.2 BLEU-1, and 42.7 ROUGE-L, setting a new state-of-the-art. At the matched 250M decoder scale (Flan-T5-Base), it already surpasses the previous best (SSVP-SLT, 15.5 B-4) with 16.5 B-4, confirming the gains are architectural. On BOBSL, the method reaches 7.0 B-4 and 25.8 R-L, a +4.4 B-4 improvement over the prior best. On ChicagoFSWild+, it achieves 73.6% letter accuracy, a 2.1 pp gain over FS PoseNet (71.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On How2Sign, SignBind-LLM outperforms all 10 prior methods in Table 1. At the same 250M decoder scale, it beats SSVP-SLT (15.5→16.5 B-4) and Geo-Sign (15.1 B-4, 582M decoder) using only RGB input and 124× less training compute (259 vs. 32,000 GPU-hours). Scaling to Flan-T5-XL yields a further +7.6 B-4 over SSVP-SLT. On BOBSL, the method achieves 7.0 B-4 versus 2.6 for the previous best (Lost in Trans. [22]), a 2.7× improvement, and 25.8 R-L versus 15.6. On ChicagoFSWild+, it reaches 73.6% letter accuracy, surpassing FS PoseNet (71.1%) by 2.1 pp and FSS-Net (64.4%) by 9.2 pp, despite not being specifically designed as a fingerspelling system. Notably, SignBind-LLM uses only RGB video (no pose input), whereas several strong baselines (MSLU, Uni-Sign, SignMusketeers, SSVP-SLT) rely on pose, yet it still outperforms them on B-4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant gloss-free SLT paradigm (Sign2GPT, Geo-Sign, Uni-Sign, SSVP-SLT) in which a single end-to-end network must simultaneously learn continuous signing, fingerspelling, and non-manual cues. Instead, it draws on three separate research threads—CTC-based continuous sign recognition, visual speech recognition (building on the masked-ViT approach of VALLR [49]), and fingerspelling detection—and recombines them as independently pre-trained expert streams fused via learned cross-attention. The use of GPT-4o-generated pseudo-glosses as weak CTC supervision echoes the pseudo-labeling strategies in semi-supervised learning, while the straight-through Gumbel-Softmax router borrows from discrete variational inference. The LLM decoder (Flan-T5) is decoupled from the visual pipeline, a design choice shared with Sign2GPT but taken further here by training the LLM purely on text pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 124× reduction in training compute (259 vs. ~32,000 GPU-hours) and the demonstration that architectural decomposition—not LLM scale—drives the gains make this a practically important result for groups with limited compute budgets. The first integration of lipreading into SLT opens a previously untapped disambiguation channel, and the learned temporal alignment mechanism is directly applicable to any multi-modal setting with asynchronous signals. Key limitations include the two-stage design's vulnerability to LLM hallucination on ambiguous pseudo-glosses (Example 3), the dependence on GPT-4o pseudo-gloss quality as an upper bound, and the fact that the lipreading module captures only speech-derived mouthings rather than full sign-language mouth gestures. Whether this decomposition generalizes to sign languages with different non-manual cue structures, and whether end-to-end joint training could eventually match the modular approach at sufficient scale, remain open.
