## Problem

Existing MLLMs lack 3D geometric priors and are confined to single images or short clips (16–32 frames), making them unable to handle long-horizon streaming video where spatial cues are scattered across thousands of frames. Naively extending context incurs quadratic attention costs, while aggressive temporal subsampling discards fine-grained spatial details critical for 3D reasoning. The core unsolved challenge is how to selectively maintain, progressively update, and reason over spatial memory from unbounded video streams with constant memory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262270/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming egocentric video processed in large chunks (~2648 tokens, spanning multiple frames) alongside a text question, and produces either a spatial QA answer (multiple-choice or numerical) or a dense 3D scene description covering global context, object counts, and pairwise spatial relations. At inference, a dual KV cache mechanism (fixed-length sliding window cache + TTT pending cache) ensures constant memory regardless of video length, enabling processing of 120+ minute videos without OOM. Training progresses from 32 uniformly sampled frames to 64–128 frames.

## Method

Spatial-TTT employs a hybrid architecture that interleaves TTT layers with self-attention anchor layers at a 3:1 ratio, preserving pretrained cross-modal alignment while compressing long-range temporal dependencies into adaptive fast weights (SwiGLU-MLP). Within each TTT layer, large-chunk updates (2648 tokens) run in parallel with sliding-window attention sharing Q/K/V projections, and a spatial-predictive mechanism applies depthwise 3D spatiotemporal convolutions (3×3×3, Dirac-initialized) to Q/K/V to inject local geometric neighborhood structure into the memory update dynamics. Fast weights are optimized with the Muon optimizer (orthogonalized gradients with momentum + L2 norm). Training follows a two-stage progressive strategy: first, dense scene-description supervision (16K samples from ScanNet/ARKitScenes via SceneVerse) with sliding-window annealing teaches fast weights to encode global 3D structure; second, 2M spatial VQA samples fine-tune streaming spatial reasoning with fixed window=chunk size. The model is initialized from Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct with shared QKV projections and zero-initialized gating for stable convergence.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, Spatial-TTT-2B achieves a macro-average of 64.4, surpassing the strongest proprietary model (Gemini-3-pro, 56.0) by 8.4 points and the strongest open-source spatial model (VST-7B-SFT, 60.6) by 3.8 points, despite being only 2B parameters. On MindCube-Tiny, it reaches 76.2 average accuracy, outperforming Gemini-3-pro (63.9) by 12.3 points and MindCube-3B (51.7) by 24.5 points. On VSI-SUPER-Count (10–120 min videos), Spatial-TTT-2B scores 31.8–45.6 MRA while all other baselines score below 1.0, demonstrating its unique ability to maintain spatial memory over arbitrarily long streams without OOM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spatial-TTT-2B outperforms all proprietary models (GPT-5: 55.0, Gemini-3-pro: 56.0, Kimi-K2.5: 53.6) by 8–9 points on VSI-Bench average, with the largest margin on Relative Direction (73.0 vs. 57.5 for Gemini-3-pro) and Route Plan (47.4 vs. 61.9 for Gemini-3-pro, though Cambrian-S-3B leads at 78.8 on Appr. Order). Among open-source spatial models, it beats VST-7B-SFT (60.6) by 3.8 points and Cambrian-S-3B (57.3) by 7.1 points, while also surpassing the larger Qwen3-VL-8B general model (57.9) by 6.5 points. On MindCube-Tiny, the 24.5-point gap over MindCube-3B (51.7) and 12.3-point gap over Gemini-3-pro (63.9) highlight superior cross-view spatial reasoning. On VSI-SUPER-Count, Spatial-TTT-2B is the only model that maintains non-trivial scores (31.8–45.6) across 10–120 minute videos, whereas Qwen3-VL-2B and Cambrian-S-7B hit OOM beyond 120 minutes and all long-video models (MovieChat, Flash-VStream) score 0.0 on counting. The efficiency analysis shows Spatial-TTT-2B uses 43% fewer TFLOPs and 44% less peak memory than Qwen3-VL-2B at 1024 frames, while Spatial-MLLM-4B OOMs entirely beyond 256 frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Test-Time Training paradigm (Sun et al., ICML 2024) and the large-chunk update strategy from LaCT, repurposing fast-weight memory mechanisms—previously applied to language modeling and novel view synthesis—for streaming visual spatial understanding. It departs from prior spatial MLLM work (Spatial-MLLM, VST, Cambrian-S, MindCube) that operates on single images or short clips (16–32 frames) by formulating spatial intelligence as an online, unbounded-stream problem. The hybrid TTT-plus-self-attention architecture and the spatial-predictive 3D depthwise convolution on Q/K/V projections are novel architectural contributions not present in either the TTT or spatial-MLLM literatures, and the dense scene-description dataset bridges a supervision gap that prior spatial QA datasets leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spatial-TTT demonstrates that a compact 2B model with linear-complexity streaming updates can match or exceed 72B general MLLMs and proprietary models (GPT-5, Gemini-3-Pro) on spatial benchmarks, while maintaining stable performance on 120-minute egocentric videos where all baselines either OOM or collapse. This opens a practical path toward always-on spatial agents (robotics, AR, autonomous driving) that must accumulate 3D evidence over arbitrarily long observation streams without quadratic memory growth. Open questions include whether the TTT fast-weight memory degrades over truly unbounded horizons (hours to days), whether the approach generalizes beyond indoor scenes to outdoor or dynamic environments, and whether the dense scene-description supervision strategy transfers to other streaming perception tasks beyond spatial reasoning.
