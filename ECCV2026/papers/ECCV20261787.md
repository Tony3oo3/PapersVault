## Problem

Training-free guidance (TFG) steers pretrained diffusion models toward desired attributes at inference, but strong guidance can push samples catastrophically off the data manifold, producing collapsed or distorted images. Standard evaluation metrics (Validity, FID) cannot detect this manifold departure and may even reward off-manifold adversarial-like artifacts. The paper asks whether the prediction target (ε, v, or x) — a design decision predating any guidance algorithm — determines whether guided samples remain on-manifold, and provides both theoretical and empirical evidence that it does.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained 256×256 diffusion model (DiT-XL/2, SiT-XL/2, JiT-H/16, or PixelFlow), a target class label (one of 143 bird species or an ImageNet class), and a guidance strength ρ swept across 5–10 values. Latent-space models (DiT, SiT) require a VAE decoder pass at every denoising step to compute pixel-space guidance gradients, while pixel-space models (JiT, PixelFlow) apply guidance directly. Output is a set of guided images evaluated via Child FID (FID against the target species domain), Parent FID, Validity (top-1 accuracy of a separate evaluation classifier), and DINOv2 Precision/Recall, with separate classifiers used for guidance and evaluation to avoid circularity.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is an error amplification hierarchy (Proposition 1): recovering the clean image x̂ from the noisy state z_t requires dividing by t under ε-prediction (amplifying prediction error by (1−t)/t, which diverges as t→0), multiplying by (1−t) under v-prediction (bounded attenuation), and no correction under x-prediction (direct output). Proposition 2 shows these per-step errors compound across Euler sampling, causing ε-prediction's cumulative trajectory perturbation to diverge (containing a −ln t₀ term) while x-prediction's remains O(1−t₀). Empirically, the paper validates this hierarchy through crossed-lines ablations (identical MLPs, D=2 to 512, where x-prediction holds >93% on-manifold rate while ε-prediction collapses to 0.5%), a new 143-species fine-grained bird benchmark with Child FID and guidance-strength Pareto sweeps (revealing a 5.2-point C-FID gap between x- and ε-prediction at matched Validity), and style transfer via CLIP Gram matrix matching. The key practical insight is that x-prediction bypasses the unstable recovery formula entirely, making it the most robust foundation for inference-time guidance across all tested tasks.

## Results

On the fine-grained bird classification benchmark (143 species, ImageNet 256×256), at matched Validity (~26.6%), JiT-H (x-prediction) achieves C-FID 32.9 versus DiT (ε-prediction) 38.1 and SiT (v-prediction) 34.7, a 5.2-point gap between x- and ε-prediction invisible to standard FID or accuracy. In the controlled crossed-lines ablation at D=512, x-prediction maintains 93.3% on-manifold rate while ε-prediction collapses to 0.5% and v-prediction degrades to 21.5%. In style transfer, JiT-H at ~80% Content Accuracy achieves Gram Distance 5.48, outperforming DiT (5.62) and PixelFlow (5.56) at comparable content preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JiT-H (x-prediction, pixel) dominates all baselines on C-FID: at matched Validity it achieves 32.9 versus DiT's 38.1 (ε-prediction, latent), SiT's 34.7 (v-prediction, latent), and PixelFlow's 44.1 (v-prediction, pixel, at ρ=0). DiT (ε-prediction) shows the most severe failure mode—mode collapse with DINOv2 Precision 0.24 but Recall only 0.49, and content accuracy dropping to 1.5% under strong style guidance—while JiT-H retains Recall up to 0.59 and 80% content accuracy at ρ=10. SiT (v-prediction, latent) consistently outperforms DiT, confirming the bounded error attenuation of v-prediction, but still trails JiT-H by 1.8 C-FID points at matched Validity. PixelFlow (v-prediction, pixel) serves as the critical control: despite sharing pixel space with JiT, its C-FID reverses under strong guidance (improving to 36.2 at ρ=2 then worsening to 47.7), isolating prediction target rather than operating space as the decisive factor for guidance robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on prediction-target differences in diffusion models (ε, v, x-prediction) from a training-time concern to an inference-time one. It builds directly on the TFG framework (Ye et al., NeurIPS 2024) and DPS (Chung et al., ICLR 2023) as the guidance mechanism, and on the Flow Matching formulation (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) as the mathematical substrate. The key departure is from the standard evaluation paradigm: prior TFG work (15 of 17 surveyed papers) relies on classifier Validity and global FID, which the authors show cannot detect—and actively incentivize—off-manifold departure. The theoretical analysis connects to prior observations of ε-prediction's training-time error amplification (Hang et al. 2023; Jin & Wang 2026; Karras et al. 2022) but formalizes how those per-step errors compound into divergent trajectory perturbation under iterative guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that prediction target is a first-order design decision for inference-time control—rather than a training-time detail invisible at matched FID—gives practitioners a concrete, actionable criterion for building guidance pipelines. The C-FID metric and Pareto-sweep protocol address a genuine blind spot in how the field evaluates guidance quality, and the fine-grained bird benchmark provides a reusable diagnostic. Limitations temper the conclusions: no single experiment perfectly isolates prediction target from architecture and operating space (the DiT-vs-SiT and PixelFlow-vs-JiT pairs are the most controlled), all experiments are at 256×256, and only gradient-based TFG methods are tested. Open questions include whether the hierarchy holds at higher resolutions, in video generation where errors compound across frames, and under inference-time scaling methods that compound guidance across many candidates.
