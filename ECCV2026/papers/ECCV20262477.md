## Problem

Federated learning suffers from unstable optimization and slow convergence when client data is both statistically heterogeneous (non-IID distributions) and scale-heterogeneous (uneven dataset sizes), causing gradient drift and oscillations. Existing RL-based FL frameworks treat client participation as a binary select/reject decision and rely on sample-inefficient on-policy algorithms, leaving the continuous dimension of *how much* data each client should contribute unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The RL agent consumes a fixed-size state vector built from a compact global-model summary (not raw weights) and per-client drift features o_k = ‖w_k − w_global‖, normalized within each round for cross-client comparability. It outputs a continuous action vector a_t = ⟨d_t^1, …, d_t^K⟩ where each d_t^k ∈ [0,1] specifies the fraction of client k's local dataset to sample for that round's training. Clients with d_t^k > 0 participate; the sampled subsets are used for local SGD updates before weighted aggregation into the next global model.

## Method

FedDO formulates per-round client coordination as a continuous-control problem solved by a DSAC-T (Distributional Soft Actor-Critic with Three Refinements) off-policy agent. The agent's twin critics model full return distributions (Gaussian mean and variance) rather than point Q-values, with a variance-aware clipping boundary and variance-scaled KL loss to suppress over-optimistic estimation and decouple learning from absolute reward scale. The actor maximizes an entropy-regularized soft-value surrogate, producing stochastic data-usage ratios that jointly encode participation intensity and workload allocation. A reward shaped by the exponential of round-over-round accuracy change (base Ξ=64) drives the policy toward steady convergence. For scalability to thousands of clients, low-rank projection compresses the high-dimensional state input and low-rank factorization shrinks the action-output layer, reducing parameter count by up to ~52% with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

FedDO achieves top-1 accuracy of 32.45% on TinyImageNet (CNN, Dir(0.1)), surpassing the best baseline (FedNTD at 14.44%) by over 18 percentage points. On CIFAR-10 with CNN under Dir(0.5), FedDO reaches 89.70% versus 63.28% for the strongest baseline (FedCross). In terms of convergence, FedDO reaches the target accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, Dir(0.1)) in only 344 communication rounds, while most baselines fail to converge within 1000 rounds. Across all 36 model–dataset–heterogeneity combinations in Table 1, FedDO is the best or tied-best method in every case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDO outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset, model architecture, and heterogeneity setting tested. The largest margins appear under high heterogeneity: on TinyImageNet (CNN, Dir(0.1)), FedDO achieves 32.45% versus 14.44% for FedNTD (the best baseline), a gap of nearly 18 points; on CIFAR-10 (CNN, Dir(0.5)), FedDO reaches 89.70% while FedCross, the strongest competitor, manages only 63.28%. FedCross is generally the closest baseline, particularly on CIFAR-100 where it benefits from cross-model aggregation, yet FedDO still leads by 0.3–2.5 points. FedAA, the other RL-based method, consistently underperforms (e.g., 9.47% on TinyImageNet Dir(0.1) vs. FedDO's 32.45%), suggesting that binary client selection is far less effective than FedDO's continuous data-usage control. Under IID conditions the gaps narrow substantially (e.g., 90.99% vs. 91.06% on FashionMNIST), but FedDO remains competitive or best, indicating its advantage stems from a more stable optimization mechanism rather than solely compensating for heterogeneity. In convergence speed (Table 2), FedDO reaches the target accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, Dir(0.1)) in 344 rounds, whereas six of seven baselines fail to converge within 1000 rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedDO builds directly on two pillars: the DSAC-T distributional off-policy RL algorithm as its policy backbone, and the FedAvg weighted-averaging protocol as the base federated training loop. It departs from prior RL-for-FL work (FedAA, Oort, multi-agent RL client selection) by replacing binary client selection with a continuous per-client data-usage ratio in [0,1], framing client coordination as a continuous control problem rather than a discrete scheduling one. It also contrasts with optimization-based heterogeneity mitigations (FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedDyn) that impose fixed regularization, instead learning an adaptive, round-level policy through interaction with the federated environment. The low-rank parameterization for scaling to thousands of clients is a practical addition not present in prior RL-FL frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous data-usage ratio offers a more granular lever than binary selection for simultaneously addressing statistical and scale heterogeneity, and the reported convergence speedups (e.g., 344 rounds vs. >1000 under Dir(0.1) on CIFAR-10) are practically significant for communication-limited deployments. However, the DRL agent must be trained offline per dataset, which limits true online adaptability and raises questions about transfer across tasks or dynamic client populations. The paper acknowledges but does not address privacy (no differential privacy or secure aggregation), and scalability beyond 2000 clients remains untested. Open questions include whether the learned policy generalizes to asynchronous settings, whether the offline training cost is acceptable in practice, and whether the large accuracy gaps over some baselines persist under more carefully tuned comparisons.
