## Problem

Current vision-language models, including RL-trained variants, fail at multi-view spatial reasoning because they rely on single-view shortcuts and never explicitly establish cross-view spatial alignment (viewpoint relations, object correspondences, occlusion changes). Even when intermediate description steps are introduced, they remain view-local and do not reason about how entities transform across viewpoints, making answers brittle on tasks requiring genuine multi-view integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261729/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives 2–8 RGB images of the same scene captured from different viewpoints along with a single multiple-choice text question. The output is a structured, tag-delimited reasoning trace consisting of three ordered sections: <spatial_thinking> (explicit cross-view alignment and viewpoint transformation inference), <thinking> (question-driven reasoning conditioned on the spatial workspace), and <answer> (a single option letter). The spatial_thinking section is the key structural novelty—it is not a per-image description but a deliberate reconciliation of shared visual cues across views before any task-specific inference begins.

## Method

ViewFusion enforces a two-stage 'think twice' protocol that separates cross-view spatial pre-alignment from question answering, making alignment a deliberate first step rather than an implicit byproduct. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) SFT on 18K instances with synthesized structured reasoning traces (generated by Qwen3-32B-VL-Instruct) that explicitly contain the three-section format, and (2) GRPO-based RL on 16K instances using a composite reward combining binary answer correctness, a strict format-validity indicator (all three tags present in correct order), and a length-shaping term active only when the answer is correct and token count falls within [320, 512]. The format reward (weight λ=0.02) stabilizes the two-stage generation during RL, while the length reward (ω=0.2) prevents both premature truncation and over-verbose outputs. This design prevents the shortcut behaviors observed in vanilla GRPO training, where models commit to answers before integrating the full multi-view context.

## Results

ViewFusion (SFT+RL, 4B) achieves 35.4% accuracy on MMSI-Bench, 77.0% on MindCube, and 45.4% on ViewSpatial-Bench, yielding an average of 52.6% across the three multi-view spatial reasoning benchmarks. This represents a +5.3% absolute gain over Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct on MMSI-Bench (35.4% vs. 30.1%) and a dramatic +40.0% improvement on MindCube (77.0% vs. 37.0%). Among open-source models, ViewFusion is the best-performing 4B-scale system, surpassing even the larger VST-7B-RL (7B) on both MMSI-Bench (35.4% vs. 34.8%) and MindCube (77.0% vs. 39.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViewFusion (SFT+RL) outperforms all open-source baselines on MindCube by a wide margin (77.0% vs. best open-source 43.4% for SpatialLadder-3B), and also leads on MMSI-Bench among open-source models (35.4% vs. 34.8% for VST-7B-RL, a 7B model). Against the Qwen3-VL-4B family, ViewFusion gains +5.3% on MMSI-Bench over Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and +6.4% over Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking, showing that explicit two-stage cross-view pre-thinking surpasses simply encouraging longer deliberation. On ViewSpatial-Bench, ViewFusion (45.4%) edges out Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct (42.5%) and VST-7B-RL (42.4%) but trails VST-3B-RL (45.0%) by a narrow margin. Against proprietary models, ViewFusion still lags GPT-5 (41.8% MMSI, 45.5% ViewSpatial) and Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (45.2% MMSI, 50.3% ViewSpatial), though it surpasses Gemini-3-Pro-Preview on MindCube (77.0% vs. 70.8%). Among spatial-intelligence-specific models (SpatialLadder, Spatial-MLLM, SpaceR, ViLaSR, Cambrian-S), ViewFusion dominates on MindCube and is competitive or superior on MMSI-Bench, confirming that the two-stage protocol addresses a gap not covered by prior spatial tuning approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViewFusion sits at the intersection of two active threads: RL-based reasoning for MLLMs (GRPO from DeepSeek-R1, R1-VL, Video-R1) and spatial reasoning with vision-language models (SpatialVLM, Visual Spatial Tuning, Cambrian-S). It departs from prior 'observe-first' RL methods (Observe-R1, Visionary-R1, HumanOmniV2) that introduce observation stages as view-local descriptive summaries, by instead enforcing an explicit cross-view spatial pre-alignment stage that reasons about viewpoint transformations and object correspondences before any question-driven inference. The training recipe repurposes GRPO with a composite reward (correctness + format + length) to stabilize the two-stage protocol, building on the data infrastructure of VST-500K and MindCube-Trainset rather than introducing new data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly structuring multi-view reasoning into a pre-alignment phase followed by question solving yields gains that longer deliberation alone (Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking) does not achieve, suggesting that the *structure* of reasoning matters as much as its length for spatial tasks. The very large MindCube improvement (77% vs. 37%) hints that the two-stage protocol is particularly effective for mental-model construction, though the overlap between MindCube-Trainset (used for SFT) and the MindCube evaluation set warrants scrutiny. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 4B models and multiple-choice formats, whether the synthetic reasoning traces from Qwen3-32B-VL are necessary or whether the model could learn the protocol end-to-end, and whether the format reward's modest ablation impact (35.4→35.0) reflects a general property of SFT-initialized RL or is specific to this task.
