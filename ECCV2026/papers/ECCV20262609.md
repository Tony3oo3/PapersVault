## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based planar reconstruction methods treat recovered planes as isolated, independent entities, completely devoid of topological connectivity. This lack of structural awareness produces fragmented models with misaligned boundaries that fail to meet the precision required for downstream applications like robotics simulation, 3D printing, and AR/VR. The paper addresses this by explicitly integrating topological adjacency constraints into the 3DGS optimization loop rather than relegating them to post-processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262609/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses as input, leveraging SAM for semantic segmentation and a pre-trained Metric3D model for monocular depth and normal prediction to initialize plane primitives. The output is a globally consistent structured planar model comprising a set of bounded planes with parametric equations, their intersection edges (finite 3D line segments), and corner vertices, assembled into a connected mesh-like representation. A key detail is that the 2D adjacency graph extracted from segmentations is refined through optimization into verified 3D adjacencies, filtering out spurious view-dependent connections caused by occlusions or depth discontinuities.

## Method

TopoGS operates in three stages: (1) hierarchical segmentation (SAM masks subdivided by mean-shift on predicted normals) followed by RANSAC plane fitting and 2D adjacency construction; (2) joint 3DGS optimization where Gaussian primitives are anchored to segmented regions and refined under a tri-consistency objective combining photometric loss, a geometry loss (rendered depth/normal vs. parametric plane depth/normal), and a novel topology loss that constrains projected 3D plane-intersection lines to align with observed 2D shared boundaries using an uncertainty-aware exponential weighting scheme that self-filters spurious adjacencies; (3) structural assembly that verifies 3D adjacencies, extracts finite intersection edges via corner detection, and fuses multi-view boundaries into a connected model. The optimization is staged—geometry loss for the first 4k iterations, then topology loss is introduced to snap adjacent planes together—with adaptive plane merging and pruning every 2000 iterations to maintain a compact representation.

## Results

On ScanNet++ (25 scenes), TopoGS achieves the best Chamfer Distance (6.01 cm), Planar Fidelity (12.34), Planar Accuracy (9.96), and Planar CD (11.15) among all compared methods. It also attains the highest Rand Index (0.946) for 3D plane segmentation. The full pipeline with structural assembly (Ours +Assembly) further improves VOI to 2.867 and Segmentation Covering to 0.472, the best in their respective categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoGS outperforms the two-stage baselines (PlanarGS+AirPlanes and DA3+AirPlanes) on all planar-quality metrics: it reduces Planar Fidelity from 24.74/20.35 to 12.34 and Planar CD from 22.00/16.60 to 11.15, while also achieving the lowest overall CD (6.01 vs. 14.02/8.49). Against the closest direct competitor, NeuralPlane, TopoGS wins on CD (6.01 vs. 7.56), F-score (70.18 vs. 53.83), Fidelity (12.34 vs. 14.50), Accuracy (9.96 vs. 11.83), and RI (0.946 vs. 0.944), though NeuralPlane edges it slightly on VOI (2.872 vs. 2.933). Compared to PlanarSplatting, TopoGS improves CD by 33% (6.01 vs. 9.06), Fidelity by 18% (12.34 vs. 15.07), and RI (0.946 vs. 0.933), while PlanarSplatting retains a marginal VOI advantage (2.869 vs. 2.933). The DA3+AirPlanes pipeline reports a higher F-score (74.40 vs. 70.18) due to its strong pre-trained depth priors, but this comes at the cost of substantially worse planar structure (Fidelity 20.35 vs. 12.34), confirming that decoupled two-stage pipelines propagate front-end depth errors into inaccurate plane fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopoGS builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering framework (Kerbl et al.) and borrows the photometric/scale loss formulation from PGSR, while adopting PlanarSplatting's initialization pipeline (Metric3D depth/normals + SAM segmentation). Its key conceptual departure is from prior end-to-end planar reconstruction methods (NeuralPlane, PlanarSplatting, PGS, GSPlane, GSFlats), which all treat recovered planes as isolated geometric entities without explicit adjacency constraints. By embedding 2D segmentation-derived topological relationships as differentiable losses within the 3DGS optimization loop, TopoGS bridges the gap between per-plane geometric fitting and global structural coherence that two-stage MVS pipelines attempt through post-hoc spatial partitioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit integration of topological adjacency as a first-class optimization constraint (rather than optional post-processing) offers a principled mechanism to resolve scale-deviant local optima in textureless indoor regions, which is a persistent failure mode for photometric-only 3DGS methods. However, the approach remains limited to planar scenes and cannot produce strictly watertight models due to inherent occlusions in multi-view captures; recovering missing corners and extending to non-planar surfaces are acknowledged open problems. For practitioners in architectural scanning, AR/VR, and robotics simulation, the topology-aware formulation provides a practical path toward editable, structurally consistent scene models without the error accumulation of decoupled two-stage pipelines.
