## Problem

Existing 3D dog pose benchmarks suffer from two intertwined limitations: distributional bias toward canonical viewpoints, static poses, and narrow morphologies (inherited from Analysis-by-Synthesis fitting), and systematic alignment degradation between images and 3D annotations under non-canonical conditions. This biased coverage masks a deeper challenge—multimodal 3D pose ambiguities under self-occlusion—that deterministic regression cannot resolve once the distribution is broadened.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image of a dog and produces 3D keypoint coordinates (J×3), weak-perspective camera parameters (scale s, translation tx, ty), and 2D projected keypoints (J×2). At inference, the model draws N=20 independent samples from the learned flow; a per-keypoint Mode-of-N aggregation over the 2D predictions forms a reference pose, and the 3D hypothesis whose projection best matches it is selected as the final output, avoiding oracle access to ground truth.

## Method

The paper contributes a 4.2M-image synthetic benchmark built by decoupling dog geometry into three independently sourced libraries—70K retargeted poses (from Sketchfab and mocap via Auto-Rig Pro), 500 breed-specific DSMAL shapes (generated with Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image and lifted to 3D via Step1X-3D), and 1,000 texture maps—then combinatorially rendering with stratified 60-viewpoint sampling to guarantee pixel-perfect 3D alignment. For estimation, a Rectified Flow model parameterized by an MMDiT transformer (with separate image, 2D-keypoint, and 3D-keypoint modality modules) learns the conditional distribution p(x|c) by predicting the velocity field along straight-line ODE trajectories from Gaussian noise to the target, conditioned on frozen DINOv3-base patch tokens. The 2D and 3D tasks are trained jointly with summed flow-matching losses, and the probabilistic formulation is shown to be complementary to the balanced data: it yields gains under ambiguity only when the training distribution is sufficiently diverse to make multiple hypotheses meaningful rather than noisy.

## Results

Trained exclusively on synthetic data, the proposed Rectified Flow-based probabilistic estimator (Mode-of-N) achieves 85.57 PCK on StanfordExtra, 87.70 PCK on Animal3D, and 64.97 PCK on the balanced synthetic benchmark, outperforming all baselines in every setting. With oracle Best-of-N selection, the method reaches 88.30 PCK (StanfordExtra), 93.00 PCK (Animal3D), and 79.88 PCK (Synthetic). The performance gap is most dramatic on the balanced synthetic test set, where the strongest baselines (GenZoo, AniMer+) reach only ~33 PCK while the proposed method achieves 64.97 (Mode-of-N) or 79.88 (Best-of-N), a more than 2× improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three deterministic baselines: BITE (AbS optimization), GenZoo (direct regression on diffusion-rendered synthetic data), and AniMer+ (hybrid 2D/3D supervised regression). On StanfordExtra, Ours (Mode) beats BITE by 2.69 PCK (85.57 vs. 82.88) despite BITE being trained directly on the StanfordExtra training set, and outperforms GenZoo and AniMer+ by 8+ PCK. On Animal3D, Ours (Mode) surpasses AniMer+ (the strongest baseline there) by 3.15 PCK (87.70 vs. 84.55) even though AniMer+ leverages both StanfordExtra and Animal3D training data. The most striking comparison is on the balanced synthetic benchmark: AniMer+ and GenZoo plateau at ~33 PCK while Ours (Mode) reaches 64.97 and Ours (Best) reaches 79.88, a gap of ~47 PCK. Notably, even the deterministic variant (Ours Det.) already achieves 55.06 PCK on the synthetic set, indicating that the balanced training data distribution itself accounts for a large portion of the improvement, with probabilistic modeling contributing an additional ~10 PCK on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Analysis-by-Synthesis (AbS) paradigm for 3D dog pose (BITE, WLDO, BARC) by eliminating 2D-supervised fitting entirely, instead decoupling pose, shape, and texture into independently sourced libraries (70K retargeted motions, 500 DSMAL shapes, 1,000 textures) and rendering with pixel-perfect alignment. It adapts the Rectified Flow + MMDiT architecture from high-resolution image synthesis (Stable Diffusion 3) and the 2D-anchored multi-hypothesis aggregation principle from D3DP (human pose) to the quadruped setting, where self-occlusion and morphological diversity make ambiguity more severe. Prior synthetic benchmarks (SyDog, DigiDogs, RGBT-Dog, GenZoo) are limited in per-factor diversity and lack a standard parametric model; this work builds on DSMAL to enable combinatorial composition with broad per-factor coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.2M-image benchmark with exact 3D ground truth and balanced distributions over viewpoint, articulation, and morphology addresses a data-quality bottleneck that scaling real-image annotations cannot fix, and the sim-to-real transfer (trained solely on synthetic, outperforming methods trained on real data) validates the approach. The ablation revealing that probabilistic regression and balanced data are complementary—probabilistic sampling hurts on biased data but helps on balanced data—reframes how the community should think about the interaction between data distribution and model formulation. Open questions include handling environmental occlusion (not just self-occlusion), exploiting temporal cues from video, and closing the remaining sim-to-real gap via human-in-the-loop curation; the DSMAL model's lack of pose-dependent corrective shapes also limits extreme articulations.
