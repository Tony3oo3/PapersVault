## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods update only per-channel affine parameters (scale and bias) in normalization layers, which perform axis-aligned transformations and are geometrically incapable of correcting the cross-channel correlation structure changes induced by distribution shift. The paper shows via correlation distance analysis that this disruption is most severe in earlier network layers, yet no prior method addresses it at intermediate layers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB test images (e.g., 224×224 for ViT) during inference and produces class labels, requiring no source data, target labels, or batch statistics beyond the current mini-batch. MixTTA operates as a plug-in within the normalization layers of a pre-trained ViT backbone, so the input/output interface is identical to the base model; adaptation is performed online by minimizing prediction entropy on the incoming test stream.

## Method

MixTTA extends each normalization layer's diagonal affine transform with a residual low-rank cross-channel term (AB)ᵀx, where A∈ℝ^{C×r} and B∈ℝ^{r×C} with r≪C, enabling inter-channel mixing at minimal parameter cost. Two projection mechanisms ensure stability: Decoupling Projection enforces zero diagonal on the low-rank factor (via orthogonal projection of B's columns against A's rows with stop-gradient) so the low-rank branch captures purely off-diagonal corrections, and Spectral Projection suppresses the dominant rank-1 direction of the subspace feature covariance to prevent collapse under non-stationary streams. The module is applied to the second LayerNorm in the first five ViT encoder blocks, with B initialized to zero so the network exactly recovers Tent at the start of adaptation. The entire module is a drop-in replacement that can be integrated into any existing normalization-based TTA method (Tent, EATA, SAR, DeYO, ReCAP) without modifying their optimization objectives.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5, mild scenario), MixTTA as a plug-in improves the strongest baselines: DeYO from 63.8% to 65.6% (+1.8 pp) and ReCAP from 64.0% to 64.9% (+0.9 pp) in average top-1 accuracy. Under wild scenarios, gains are larger: in the mixed-shift setting, DeYO+MixTTA reaches 62.5% (+2.8 pp over DeYO) and ReCAP+MixTTA reaches 62.4% (+2.3 pp over ReCAP), while under batch-size-1 adaptation, ReCAP+MixTTA achieves 66.8%. On ImageNet-Sketch, ReCAP+MixTTA attains 43.7% (mild) and 44.5% (label shift), outperforming LinearTCA+ (42.2% and 44.6%) in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixTTA consistently improves four of five normalization-based TTA baselines on ImageNet-C (mild, severity 5): EATA +1.8 pp (61.7→63.5), DeYO +1.8 pp (63.8→65.6), ReCAP +0.9 pp (64.0→64.9), and SAR +0.5 pp (56.7→57.2). Tent is the exception in overall average (61.2→55.2), but MixTTA recovers Tent's catastrophic failures on Weather corruptions (Snow: 18.1→57.2, Frost: 21.2→41.6), suggesting the plug-in stabilizes fragile entropy minimization at the cost of some Noise-corruption accuracy. Against the correlation-alignment baseline LinearTCA+ (64.5%), ReCAP+MixTTA (64.9%) and DeYO+MixTTA (65.6%) both outperform it, and the gap widens under mixed shifts where LinearTCA+ gains only +0.2 pp while MixTTA gains +2.3–2.8 pp. On ImageNet-Sketch, ReCAP+MixTTA (43.7% mild, 44.0% batch-1) edges out LinearTCA+ (42.2%, 43.8%), confirming that per-layer cross-channel mixing complements final-layer correlation alignment. Under the most constrained setting (batch size 1), MixTTA yields the largest relative gains, e.g., EATA improves from 47.2% to 61.2% (+14 pp), indicating the low-rank branch is most valuable when batch statistics are unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tent paradigm (Wang et al., ICLR 2021) of updating only normalization-layer affine parameters during test-time adaptation by identifying a geometric limitation: per-channel axis-aligned scaling and shifting cannot correct cross-channel correlation shifts induced by distribution shift. It departs from TCA (You et al., ICML 2025), which performs correlation alignment only at the final representation, by placing low-rank cross-channel correction at each normalization layer throughout the network. The low-rank factorization draws conceptual inspiration from LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022), but applies it to inter-channel dependencies within normalization layers rather than to network weight updates, targeting correlation structure rather than weight adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric argument for why the dominant TTA paradigm is structurally limited, supported by the empirical finding that correlation disruption is most severe in early layers—a gap that final-layer-only methods like TCA leave unaddressed. As a plug-in module with ~4% overhead and a single hyperparameter (r=4) that works across all baselines, MixTTA is practically deployable. Open questions include whether adaptive rank selection could further improve performance under varying shift severities, whether the approach generalizes to architectures beyond ViT and ResNet, and whether the low-rank structure is truly necessary or whether other constrained parameterizations could achieve similar effects.
