## Problem

Adapter-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning for temporal action detection operates on dense token grids, so inference cost scales linearly with video length regardless of token informativeness. Token selection can prune redundant tokens to reduce attention cost, but it destroys the regular spatial grid that convolutional adapters require, forcing an expensive dense reconstruction that nullifies the speedup. No existing mechanism allows convolutional adapters to operate directly on dynamically pruned token sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260499/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed video as a sequence of frames (768 frames at 224×224 for THUMOS-14 and ATTACH; 192 frames at 160×160 for ActivityNet-1.3) processed by a frozen video ViT backbone (VideoMAEv2 or InternVideoNext), with optional learnable auxiliary task tokens (e.g., landmark embeddings) injected for multitask supervision. Output is per-frame action class scores with temporal boundaries, produced by an ActionFormer detection head, evaluated as mAP at multiple IoU thresholds. The entire pipeline is compatible with FlashAttention, requiring only K auxiliary-token attention rows rather than the full N×N matrix.

## Method

The core primitive is SparseConv2D, a native sparse 2D convolution that processes dynamically pruned token sequences without reconstructing the dense spatial grid. It exploits the locality of convolution (a 3×3 kernel needs only 9 neighbors per output) by precomputing a neighbor index table in O(N_kept) that maps each kept token to its spatial neighbors' positions in the sparse list, leveraging the known regular 2D patch grid. Custom CUDA kernels then gather neighbor features directly via this table, with an implicit GEMM path for large channels and fused tiled kernels for small ones. In the SV-TAD framework, sparse bottleneck adapters (down-proj → GELU → SparseConv2D → cross-attention → up-proj → residual) are inserted after each Transformer block's attention layer, while attention-based token selection at blocks 4, 8, 12, 16 progressively prunes the sequence (keep rate 0.6 per stage, compounding to ~0.13 after four stages). A lightweight cross-attention module lets auxiliary tokens query the sparse visual bottleneck features, providing temporal context relay and enabling auxiliary task supervision at negligible compute cost.

## Results

On THUMOS-14, SV-TAD with InternVideoNext-L achieves 74.13% average mAP at 87.05 TFLOPs, surpassing the previous state of the art (AdaTAD with VMAEv2-G, 73.87% at 176.87 TFLOPs) by +0.26% at roughly half the computational cost. With VideoMAEv2-L, SV-TAD matches AdaTAD's accuracy (73.47% vs. 73.50%) while using 64% fewer TFLOPs (21.25 vs. 59.40). On ActivityNet-1.3, SV-TAD with InternVideoNext-L reaches 40.06% average mAP at 34.00 TFLOPs, outperforming AdaTAD VMAEv2-G (39.20% at 83.59 TFLOPs) by +0.86%. On ATTACH, adding auxiliary landmark supervision to SV-TAD improves average mAP from 16.28% to 18.69% (+2.41%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline AdaTAD [16], SV-TAD wins at every backbone scale: with VMAEv2-B it gains +1.77% average mAP on THUMOS-14 (72.44% vs. 70.67%) at 43% fewer TFLOPs, and with VMAEv2-L it matches accuracy (73.47% vs. 73.50%) at 64% fewer TFLOPs. Scaling to InternVideoNext-L, SV-TAD surpasses AdaTAD's billion-parameter VMAEv2-G by +0.26% on THUMOS-14 and +0.86% on ActivityNet-1.3 while using roughly half the compute (87.05 vs. 176.87 TFLOPs; 34.00 vs. 83.59 TFLOPs). Against LoSA [9] (VMAEv2-G, 132 TFLOPs), SV-TAD with VMAEv2-L achieves comparable THUMOS-14 accuracy (73.47% vs. 71.0%) at 84% fewer TFLOPs. ViT-TAD [29] (ViT-B, 69.5%) and Re2TAD [33] (Re2VSwin-T, 59.4%) are substantially outperformed, though ViT-TAD uses a reduced input regime (256 frames at 160×160 vs. 768 at 224×224). Older CNN-based baselines (AFSD, E2E-TAD, BasicTAD, TALLFormer) trail by 10–20+ mAP points, reflecting the generational gap between convolutional and transformer-based TAD. On ATTACH, SV-TAD+Kp (18.69%) sets a new state of the art, with no prior TAD formulation reported for this benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: attention-based token selection in Vision Transformers (EViT, ToMe, DynamicViT) and parameter-efficient convolutional adapters for video understanding (AdaTAD, LoSA, ST-Adapter). The key departure is that prior token selection methods reduce only the quadratic attention cost but leave convolutional adapters operating on dense grids, forcing expensive scatter-gather reconstruction that negates most of the speedup. The authors introduce a native sparse 2D convolution primitive (neighbor index table + custom CUDA kernels) that lets adapters operate directly on pruned token sets, explicitly arguing that existing 3D sparse engines (MinkowskiEngine, submanifold convolutions) are architecturally incompatible with ViT's dynamic, layer-varying sparsity patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 64% TFLOPs reduction with maintained or improved accuracy across three backbone scales and three benchmarks makes this a practically significant efficiency win for deploying large video transformers on long sequences. The sparse convolution primitive is general and applicable wherever convolutions follow token selection (feature pyramids, spatial adapters, task heads), potentially enabling a broader class of efficient ViT architectures. Limitations include NVIDIA-only CUDA kernels, the sparse kernel only outperforming dense below ~55% keep rate, and the requirement for progressive multi-stage pruning to compound savings. Open directions include learned content-adaptive keep-rate schedules and extending the primitive to 3D spatio-temporal neighborhoods.
