## Problem

Unsupervised semantic segmentation has achieved strong results on single RGB modality, but naively extending these methods to multi-modal inputs (depth, thermal, etc.) causes performance degradation rather than the expected gains. Without explicit label supervision to arbitrate inter-modal inconsistencies, heterogeneous sensor signals introduce conflicting structural patterns that corrupt the latent space and prevent effective exploitation of complementary information. This paper defines and tackles the novel task of Unsupervised Multi-modal Semantic Segmentation (UMSS) for the first time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260568/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a paired multi-modal group per sample — an RGB image plus one or more spatially aligned auxiliary modalities (depth, thermal, NIR, DoLP, AoLP) — and produces pixel-level discrete semantic cluster assignments. A frozen DINOv3 backbone extracts dense features from each modality, and a 5-crop pre-processing strategy is used to enhance spatial resolution and correspondence quality. The framework scales from bi-modal (RGB+Depth, RGB+Thermal) to quad-modal (RGB+NIR+DoLP+AoLP) settings, with each auxiliary modality processed through an independent branch.

## Method

UniM2 extends the STEGO correspondence-distillation paradigm to the multi-modal domain via two key mechanisms. First, Cross-modal Correspondence Synergy (CMCS) replaces the single-modality correspondence target with a weighted sum of modality-specific correspondence tensors, enforcing that the unified embedding preserves only structural relationships consistently supported across all constituent modalities. Second, the Cross-modal Harmonizer (CMH) designates RGB as a stable semantic anchor and routes auxiliary supervision through a lightweight learnable two-layer convolutional buffer, adaptively suppressing unreliable pixel-wise alignment while retaining complementary structural cues. Each modality is refined by a Modality-Specific Network before a learnable Conv Fusion module integrates them, and the entire pipeline is trained on Self, KNN, and Random multi-modal group pairs without any human annotations.

## Results

On NYU-Depth-v2 with DINOv3-Base/16, UniM2 achieves 38.1 mIoU and 58.8% pixel accuracy, improving over the RGB-only STEGO baseline (31.7 mIoU) by 6.4 mIoU. On MFNet with the same backbone, UniM2 reaches 45.7 mIoU and 76.1% accuracy, a 9.8 mIoU gain over the RGB-only STEGO baseline (35.9 mIoU). Notably, naively adding depth or thermal modalities to existing USS frameworks (STEGO, EAGLE) *degrades* performance by 0.6–7.3 mIoU, whereas UniM2 is the only method that consistently converts auxiliary modalities into positive gains. On the quad-modal MCubeS dataset, UniM2 achieves 21.8 mIoU (IND setting) vs. 19.1 for STEGO RGB-only, confirming scalability beyond bi-modal fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniM2 decisively outperforms all compared baselines across both primary datasets. Against the closest USS extensions, UniM2 beats STEGO by 6.4 mIoU on NYU-Depth-v2 and 9.8 mIoU on MFNet (ViT-B/16), and beats EAGLE by 7.2 and 7.9 mIoU respectively. Critically, the multi-modal variants of STEGO and EAGLE (naive depth/thermal fusion) actually *underperform* their own RGB-only counterparts by 0.6–7.3 mIoU, while UniM2 is the only method that turns auxiliary modalities into consistent gains. Against image-level fusion baselines (SwinFusion: 36.2 mIoU, Mask-DiFuser: 39.1 mIoU on MFNet), UniM2 leads by 9.5 and 6.6 mIoU. Against RGB-to-X distillation methods (CORAL: 21.3, MMD: 20.1 mIoU on NYU-Depth-v2), UniM2 leads by 16.8 and 18.0 mIoU. The SOTA supervised fusion modules CBAM and StitchFusion, when naively embedded into the USS pipeline (Fig. 1b), also degrade performance relative to RGB-only, further confirming that UniM2's CMCS+CMH design is necessary to exploit multi-modal complementarity without labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the correspondence distillation paradigm of STEGO (Hamilton et al., ICLR 2022) from single-modality unsupervised semantic segmentation to a multi-modal setting, introducing Cross-modal Correspondence Synergy (CMCS) as a label-free supervisory signal that enforces structural agreement across heterogeneous sensors. It explicitly departs from supervised multi-modal fusion methods (CBAM, StitchFusion) by demonstrating that their reliance on pixel-level annotations for modality arbitration makes them counterproductive in label-free settings. The Cross-modal Harmonizer (CMH) mechanism distinguishes UniM2 from both cross-modal knowledge distillation (one-way teacher-student imitation) and unsupervised domain adaptation (source-to-target alignment), instead treating all modalities as joint contributors to a shared semantic manifold with RGB as a stable anchor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes UMSS as a formal task and provides the first systematic evidence that naively adding auxiliary modalities to unsupervised segmentation frameworks degrades performance rather than improving it—a finding that challenges the common assumption that more sensor data is always beneficial. The CMH mechanism offers a generalizable, modular solution for modality conflicts that scales to N auxiliary modalities, though its asymmetric design (RGB as fixed reference) limits applicability to settings where RGB is unavailable or unreliable. Open questions include whether the framework can operate without a dominant reference modality, how it handles truly noisy or missing modalities at test time, and whether the correspondence synergy principle transfers to other unsupervised tasks beyond segmentation.
