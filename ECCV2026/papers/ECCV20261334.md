## Problem

Existing listening-speaking avatar methods rely on dual-stream audio conditioning, which introduces an interlocutor look-ahead dependency incompatible with causal user-LLM interaction, and most 3D talking-head pipelines train and evaluate in coefficient space rather than the rendered visual quality users actually perceive. No prior framework simultaneously achieves real-time generation, unified listening-speaking behavior without future information, and high-fidelity rendered output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single merged audio stream (microphone + LLM output via a Streaming Audio Scheduler that emits fixed 100-frame windows), a reference FLAME identity shape and motion frame, 75 history FLAME frames compressed to 20 tokens via History Context Packing, a per-frame binary listening-speaking state (+1 speaking / −1 listening), and a 2-D motion-magnitude vector in [0,1]². It outputs a 100-frame FLAME motion sequence (100-D expression + pose per frame) that is differentiably rendered to 2D RGB images by GAGAvatar, achieving 59 FPS end-to-end on a single RTX 3090 with 4-step Euler sampling.

## Method

EmbodiedHead is the first Rectified-Flow Diffusion Transformer for speech-driven talking-head generation, injecting conditions through three complementary pathways: History Context Packing compresses long-range motion context into fixed tokens, a state-conditioned FiLM modulates mHuBERT audio features in cross-attention to encode the listening/speaking mode, and AdaLN carries global conditioning (timestep, identity, pooled audio, motion magnitude). A Streaming Audio Scheduler causally merges microphone and LLM audio into unified windows with aligned LS-states, eliminating the dual-stream look-ahead problem. Training proceeds in two stages: coefficient-space flow matching with per-parameter-group reweighting for stable dynamics, followed by joint DiT-and-renderer fine-tuning with L1 and LPIPS image losses, where Rectified Flow's straight-path property enables a single Euler step to produce the exact inference endpoint that image supervision targets.

## Results

On the EmbodiedHead speaking test set, the full two-stage model (Stage 2) achieves LVE 5.71, FDD 1.58, MOD 3.04, BA 0.46, SID 2.64, PSNR 18.28, SSIM 0.617, and LPIPS 0.184, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the DualTalk listening-speaking test set, EmbodiedHead achieves LVE 7.18, FDD 1.74, MOD 2.57, and SID 2.67, surpassing dual-stream baselines on lip-sync and diversity metrics while trailing slightly on BA (0.39 vs. 0.42 for DualTalk). The full pipeline runs at 59 FPS on a single RTX 3090 with 4-step Euler sampling, and the motion module alone exceeds 900 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the standard speaking test set, EmbodiedHead (Stage 2) dominates all four speech-driven baselines: it reduces LVE from 6.86 (ARTalk, the closest competitor) to 5.71, FDD from 2.42 to 1.58, and LPIPS from 0.221 to 0.184, while also achieving the highest PSNR (18.28 vs. 17.68 for CodeTalker) and SID (2.64 vs. 2.39 for ARTalk). FaceFormer and CodeTalker, which do not predict head motion, are excluded from BA but trail substantially on lip-sync (LVE ≈ 11) and rendered quality. On the interactive DualTalk test set, EmbodiedHead beats all three dual-stream baselines on LVE (7.18 vs. 7.94/12.73/8.02), FDD (1.74 vs. 1.95/3.04/1.94), MOD (2.57 vs. 2.84/4.83/2.98), and SID (2.67 vs. 2.47/1.57/2.57), but trails DualTalk (0.42) and TIMAR (0.41) by a small margin on BA (0.39), which the authors attribute to those methods' access to interlocutor visual cues. In the user study, EmbodiedHead is preferred over DualTalk, TIMAR, and UniLS on all four perceptual criteria, with preference rates ranging from 68.6% to 91.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three lines of work: (1) speech-driven 3D talking-head generation (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, DiffPoseTalk, ARTalk), which typically separates motion prediction from rendering and evaluates in coefficient space; (2) listening-speaking integrated avatar generation (DualTalk, UniLS, TIMAR), which relies on dual-stream audio conditioning and is incompatible with causal user-LLM interaction due to interlocutor look-ahead; and (3) Rectified-Flow Diffusion Transformers (PixArt-α, DiT), which enable few-step sampling. EmbodiedHead departs from prior work by being the first to couple a Rectified-Flow DiT with a differentiable renderer for talking-head generation, replacing dual-stream audio with a single-stream interface plus explicit per-frame listening-speaking state conditioning, and introducing a two-stage training scheme that jointly optimizes the motion model and renderer in image space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying conversational AI avatars: the dual-stream architecture used by prior listening-speaking methods is fundamentally incompatible with causal user-LLM interaction because the interlocutor's reactive audio is unavailable at inference time. The single-stream LS-state solution, combined with 59 FPS real-time rendering on a single GPU, makes head-embodied LLM avatars feasible for live deployment. Open questions include whether the FLAME-parameterized approach can capture the full range of expressive non-verbal behavior (e.g., subtle gaze shifts, body language), whether the GAGAvatar renderer's lower fidelity compared to photorealistic pipelines limits perceived social presence, and how the framework scales to multi-person or longer-horizon conversations. The reliance on monocular video tracking for training data also means the method inherits tracker biases, partially mitigated but not eliminated by the image-domain refinement stage.
