## Problem

Existing driving scene editors treat language grounding, object-level editing, and multi-agent simulation as loosely coupled stages, relying on heuristic localization (instance IDs, bounding boxes, or attribute matching), manual intervention, and single-agent validation. No prior framework provides a unified representation that is simultaneously expressive enough to capture diverse road dynamics and accessible enough to be controlled through free-form natural language, limiting semantic expressiveness and hindering scalable, reactive scenario generation.

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-view driving images (front, front-left, front-right cameras; 100 frames at 10 Hz per sequence) and free-form natural language prompts requiring no predefined identifiers, bounding boxes, or spatial annotations. Outputs include an edited 4D Gaussian scene graph, rendered 2D RGB images and BEV maps for validation, and refined trajectories for all scene agents. The pipeline supports both human-typed prompts and VLM-generated prompts, with the VLM also evaluating rendered outputs for semantic plausibility in an iterative loop.

## Method

SIMSplat builds a scene-graph-based 4D Gaussian Splatting representation with rigid nodes (vehicles), non-rigid nodes (pedestrians with joint-level deformation), and static background, then embeds three types of language-aligned features directly into the Gaussian nodes: appearance features (CLIP embeddings compressed via a lightweight autoencoder into per-Gaussian latent codes), motion features (trajectories encoded by a trajectory encoder and quantized against a motion codebook with separate prototypes for vehicles and pedestrians), and location features (quantized against a shared location codebook). At inference, a text prompt is encoded with E5 Sentence Transformer, appearance similarity narrows candidate regions at the pixel level, and motion/location cosine similarity selects the target object at the node level. After editing (insertion, removal, replacement, trajectory modification), a multi-agent path refinement module using SMART-1B predicts future trajectories for all affected agents conditioned on the edited target trajectory, producing reactive full-scene simulations. The entire pipeline integrates with VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B) for automated scenario mining, where the VLM generates prompts, evaluates rendered outputs for plausibility, and iteratively refines parameters until physical and semantic constraints are satisfied.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, SIMSplat achieves 0.73 accuracy and 0.80 video-level IoU for open-vocabulary object querying, more than doubling the strongest baseline 4DLangSplat (0.35 acc / 0.49 vIoU). For end-to-end scene editing, the human-guided version reaches 87.2% task completion and the VLM-driven version 71.1%, compared to 26.7% (ChatSim) and 27.8% (OmniRe). In multi-agent motion generation, SIMSplat with path refinement achieves the lowest total failure rate of 20.0%, versus 53.3% (ChatSim), 70.0% (OmniRe), and 80.0% (GPT-5 as motion generator).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For object grounding, SIMSplat's motion-aware language alignment more than doubles the accuracy of both LangSplat (0.28 → 0.73) and 4DLangSplat (0.35 → 0.73), with the largest gains on vehicles (0.33 → 0.83 acc) where motion and location cues disambiguate among multiple similar objects. For scene editing, SIMSplat (human-guided) achieves 87.2% task completion, far exceeding ChatSim (26.7%) and OmniRe† (27.8%); even the fully automated VLM-driven version (71.1%) outperforms both baselines by over 40 points, largely because ChatSim cannot edit existing objects or pedestrians while OmniRe lacks language-based grounding. In multi-agent motion generation, SIMSplat with path refinement reduces total failure to 20.0% versus 53.3% for ChatSim, 70.0% for OmniRe†, and 80.0% for GPT-5-as-motion-generator, with the biggest improvement in vehicle-vehicle collisions (13.3% vs. 53.3–63.3%). The ablation (Table 3) confirms that motion features primarily benefit vehicle grounding while location features primarily benefit pedestrian grounding, and their combination is necessary for the full 0.73/0.80 performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIMSplat builds on the scene-graph-based 4D Gaussian Splatting lineage (DrivingGaussian, HUGS, OmniRe) for structured per-object scene representation, and on SMART-1B for multi-agent motion prediction. It departs from prior language-Gaussian methods (LangSplat, 4D LangSplat) by introducing motion-aware alignment with trajectory codebooks specifically designed for driving dynamics, rather than the appearance-only or simple indoor-motion features those methods capture. It also unifies capabilities that prior driving editors (ChatSim, SceneCrafter, HUGSIM) treat as loosely connected stages—grounding, editing, and simulation—into a single language-accessible scene-graph framework, and extends the scope from rigid vehicles to include non-rigid pedestrian manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making 4D Gaussian scenes directly queryable through free-form language and coupling edits with multi-agent path refinement, SIMSplat addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving validation: generating diverse, safety-critical scenarios (jaywalking pedestrians, aggressive cut-ins) without manual intervention at each step. The VLM-driven closed-loop pipeline for automated scenario mining is a step toward scalable long-tail scenario generation, though it currently relies on a single dataset (Waymo) and a fixed motion prediction model (SMART-1B), leaving open questions about generalization to other driving distributions, real-time performance, and whether the codebook-based motion representation scales to more complex multi-interaction scenarios. The finding that motion and location features contribute asymmetrically to vehicle versus pedestrian grounding suggests that domain-specific feature design matters more than generic language alignment for driving scenes.
