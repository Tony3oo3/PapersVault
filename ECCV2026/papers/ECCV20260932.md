## Problem

Existing video retrieval models, whether dual-encoder (CLIP) or MLLM-based, struggle with nuanced queries that require distinguishing temporally opposite actions (e.g., opening vs. closing a door), understanding negation modifiers, or composing a video with a text edit instruction. Prior MLLM adaptation methods rely on large-scale multimodal contrastive training that still underperforms on these fine-grained aspects, leaving a gap between coarse-grained matching and the specific, nuanced understanding real search queries demand.

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input types: a plain text query, a video clip (16 uniformly spaced frames), or a composed query consisting of a source video plus a text edit instruction. Embeddings are extracted by prompting the MLLM with an Explicit One-word Limitation (EOL) prompt and reading the final-layer hidden state of the next-token position; for composed inputs, the EOL prompt is modified to instruct the model to imagine the edit applied before summarizing. The output is a single d-dimensional embedding vector per input, and retrieval is performed via cosine similarity ranking over a candidate gallery.

## Method

TARA (Text Adapted Retrieval Alignment) repurposes a generative MLLM as a unified video-text embedding encoder by fine-tuning it with a contrastive loss on a curated 20K text-triplet dataset (NLI-Nuance) that requires no video data. The key mechanism is the careful design of hard negatives to instill three specific nuances: temporal (chiral verb-object pairs from Ego4D where the negative uses the temporally opposite verb), negation (NLI triplets filtered for negation operators), and multimodal (Composed Video Retrieval translated into composed text retrieval by replacing videos with captions). Despite training exclusively on text, the method achieves state-of-the-art on all nuanced retrieval benchmarks. The paper provides both empirical and theoretical evidence that this works because text-only contrastive training induces uniformity pressure that spreads embeddings on the hypersphere, reducing the modality gap between video and text subspaces; a formal proposition shows that under the Modality Gap Assumption, decreasing the text-only contrastive loss necessarily decreases the text-to-video contrastive loss up to an irreducible alignment noise term.

## Results

On the CiA temporal retrieval benchmark, Tarsier2+TARA achieves state-of-the-art mAP of 88.9 (Chiral), 66.7 (Static), and 58.6 (All) on SSv2, substantially outperforming the base Tarsier2 (77.7/26.9/24.0) and all prior methods. On RTime, Tarsier2+TARA reaches 67.2% R@1 (T2V) and 77.9% R@1 (V2T), surpassing all fine-tuned baselines. On NegBench, Tarsier2+TARA achieves 76.7/73.6 R@5 (standard/negated) on COCO and 65.1/65.0 on MSRVTT, beating NegCLIP variants by 6–12 points. On WebVid-CoVR composed retrieval, TARA attains 66.3% R@1 zero-shot, exceeding methods fine-tuned on the full CoVR training set (best prior: 59.8% R@1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TARA consistently outperforms all baselines across every nuanced retrieval benchmark. On CiA (temporal), Tarsier2+TARA (58.6 mAP All/SSv2) beats the strongest prior MLLM method CaRe [62] (23.7) by 35 points and the best dual-encoder InternVideo2 [55] (20.6) by 38 points, while using only 20K text triplets versus 275K–13K multimodal samples. On RTime, TARA (67.2 T2V) surpasses ArrowRL-Qwen2.5 [63] (55.6) and UMT-Neg (54.5) which are fine-tuned directly on RTime data. On NegBench, Tarsier2+TARA (76.7 R@5 COCO) exceeds NegCLIP+CC-NegCap [1] (68.6) by 8 points and CLIP+CC-NegCap (58.5) by 18 points, despite being zero-shot on negation. On WebVid-CoVR, TARA (66.3 R@1) beats CoVR-2 [50] (59.8) and CoVR [51] (53.1) which are fine-tuned on the full CoVR training set. On standard MMEB-V2 benchmarks, Tarsier2+TARA (66.1 avg classification, 45.9 avg retrieval) improves over its base Tarsier2 (27.4/16.4) by 39/30 points and is competitive with Qwen3VL-Embedding [33] (77.5/58.9) trained on massive multimodal data; a simple concatenation ensemble of TARA and Qwen3VL-Embedding (78.5/59.5) even surpasses the latter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the E5-V line of work (Jiang et al., 2024) on extracting embeddings from MLLMs via EOL prompts, but departs fundamentally from the standard two-stage post-training recipe (NLI text → large-scale multimodal contrastive data) used by VLM2Vec, LAMRA, GVE, and Qwen3VL-Embedding. Instead of scaling training data to 1.4M–13M multimodal samples, TARA fine-tunes on only 20K text triplets whose hard negatives are specifically curated to encode temporal, negation, and composed-retrieval nuances. The theoretical grounding draws on the modality gap literature (Liang et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2023/2024), which previously showed that closing the gap enables text-only training for dual-encoder models, but this is the first work to demonstrate the same principle for unified MLLM architectures and to provide a formal proof linking text-only contrastive loss reduction to cross-modal retrieval improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1-hour fine-tuning recipe on 8 GPUs that consistently improves any base MLLM and achieves SOTA across four distinct nuanced-retrieval benchmarks, outperforming methods trained on orders of magnitude more multimodal data. This suggests that the bottleneck for fine-grained cross-modal retrieval in MLLMs is not data volume but the quality of the training signal (hard-negative design) and the modality gap. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other fine-grained aspects (adverbs, spatial relations), whether incorporating even small amounts of video data during training would further improve performance, and whether the modality-gap-closing effect persists as base models scale or change architecture.
