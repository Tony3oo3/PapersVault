## Problem

Existing training-free material transfer methods suffer from two compounding issues: attention-based injection entangles heterogeneous material attributes (color, texture, structural patterns) into a single representation, causing structural distortion and imprecise transfer, and inversion-based editing in flow models accumulates reconstruction errors along the deterministic ODE trajectory, degrading object details. No prior method simultaneously avoids inversion-induced drift and provides fine-grained, attribute-level control over the transfer process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source object image and a reference material exemplar image as input, both encoded into the latent space of FLUX.1-dev via its VAE encoder. The reference material is first decomposed into three attribute-specific exemplars (color, texture, structural patterns) before latent encoding. Editing is performed over 10 effective steps within a 28-step diffusion schedule, producing a single material-transferred image that preserves the source object's geometry while adopting the exemplar's material appearance.

## Method

MaterialFlow extends FlowEdit's inversion-free velocity-difference framework to exemplar-conditioned editing by introducing a trajectory-aware normalization factor λ_t that scales the exemplar-conditioned velocity to match the source trajectory's displacement magnitude, preventing the exemplar from either dominating or being negligible relative to the source flow. The reference material is explicitly disentangled into three complementary attributes: color (extracted via 2D Fourier low-pass filtering, summarized as a CIELab chromatic vector, and recombined with the object's low-frequency luminance via histogram alignment), texture (isolated via 2D Fourier high-pass filtering), and structural patterns (captured by spatially coherent patch extraction from the exemplar). At each timestep, attribute-specific velocity differences are computed for each disentangled exemplar, normalized by the trajectory-aware factor, aggregated, and combined with the text-guided velocity difference to update the source latent via first-order integration, all without any parameter updates or explicit latent inversion.

## Results

On the MTB benchmark, MaterialFlow achieves the highest overall user-study score of 59.29%, surpassing the next-best method ZeST (48.57%) by 10.7 points, while requiring no auxiliary modules. In VQA-based transfer quality, MaterialFlow leads on Color alignment (79.89% vs. MaTe's 67.00%) and Object preservation (83.81% vs. MaTe's 68.81%), though ZeST edges it out on Texture (57.00% vs. 42.67%) and Pattern (49.56% vs. 34.78%) alignment. The method operates with only 10 effective editing steps on FLUX.1-dev, avoiding both fine-tuning and inversion-based reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaterialFlow outperforms all five baselines on overall user-study score (59.29%) and object preservation (83.81%), with the largest margins over IP-Adapter+SDXL (+53.1 pts overall) and ProSpect (+57.6 pts). Against the strongest competitor ZeST, MaterialFlow wins on Color alignment (+21.8 pts) and Object preservation (+29.1 pts) but loses on Texture (−14.3 pts) and Pattern (−14.8 pts), reflecting ZeST's stronger micro-texture and structural-pattern transfer at the cost of structural fidelity. MaTe, which relies on fine-tuning, degrades substantially under the unified training-free protocol (10.00% overall vs. its reported trained performance), while MaterialFusion and IP-Adapter+SDXL, both requiring auxiliary modules, score below 30% overall. Notably, MaterialFlow achieves the best results without any auxiliary encoder, depth map, or trainable adapter, operating solely through velocity-field modulation in the FLUX.1-dev latent space.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaterialFlow directly extends FlowEdit's inversion-free velocity-difference framework from text-guided to exemplar-guided editing, introducing a trajectory-aware normalization mechanism to rebalance the exemplar-conditioned velocity field against the source trajectory. It departs from prior material transfer methods (ZeST, MaterialFusion, ProSpect, MaTe) that rely on attention-based cross-image injection, auxiliary adapters (ControlNet, IP-Adapter), or per-task fine-tuning, instead operating purely through latent-space velocity modulation in a pre-trained Rectified Flow model (FLUX.1-dev). The attribute-aware disentanglement via Fourier filtering, CIELab color extraction, and patch-based structural pattern isolation is a hand-crafted decomposition that has no direct precedent in the material transfer literature, though frequency-domain attribute separation is well studied in stylization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both inversion and attention-based injection, MaterialFlow offers a lightweight, training-free path to fine-grained material transfer that avoids the structural distortion and texture leakage plaguing prior methods, which is practically valuable for design, AR preview, and 3D asset pipelines. However, evaluation is limited to the 30×30 MTB benchmark, and the VQA-based metric (Qwen3-VL) introduces a potential circularity since the same model family underpins both generation and evaluation. Open questions include whether the hand-crafted Fourier/CIELab disentanglement generalizes to materials with complex multi-scale structure, whether the trajectory-aware normalization extends to multi-turn or 3D-consistent editing, and whether the approach transfers to non-Rectified-Flow backbones.
