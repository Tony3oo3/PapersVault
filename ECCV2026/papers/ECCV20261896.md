## Problem

Motion tokenization faces a fundamental trade-off: increasing the number of discrete tokens improves reconstruction fidelity but makes it harder for generative models to learn the longer sequences, degrading generation quality. Additionally, existing CNN-based tokenizers rely on local receptive fields that cannot effectively support global language-motion alignment. The paper asks whether language guidance during tokenization can yield compact, semantically informed representations that maintain reconstruction quality while simplifying generative learning.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language description (up to 77 tokens, encoded by a frozen LLaMA-3.2-1B) and produces a 196-frame 3D human motion sequence in the 64–67 dimensional 'meng' representation. Internally, the tokenizer compresses the 196-frame motion into a small set of learnable latent tokens (25, 36, or 49) that are quantized across 10 residual scales into 104, 160, or 236 total discrete tokens. Unlike image tokenizers, no patchify is applied since 1D motion sequences are computationally cheap, and the detokenizer reconstructs motion via learnable mask tokens interacting with dequantized embeddings through cross-attention.

## Method

LG-Tok replaces the standard CNN encoder with a 9-layer Transformer tokenizer that concatenates learnable latent tokens, linearly projected motion, and frozen LLaMA text embeddings, using self-attention to produce globally context-aware latent representations that are then multi-scale vector-quantized. A language-drop scheme randomly removes text input with probability 0.1 during training, preventing the tokenizer from shortcutting through text embeddings and forcing tokens to remain self-sufficient motion representations. At inference, the detokenizer performs classifier-free guidance directly in motion space—extrapolating from unconditional to conditional reconstructions—providing a complementary guidance mechanism to logit-space methods. The architecture incorporates RMSNorm, SwiGLU activations, RoPE (base=100), and UNet-like long skip connections, and is paired with MoSa's Scale-wise Autoregressive generative model (or MoMask for generalizability), where the compact semantic tokens reduce perplexity from 146.5 to 103.1 on Motion-X.

## Results

On HumanML3D, LG-Tok (236 tokens) achieves the best FID of 0.057 and Top-1 R-Precision of 0.542, outperforming the previous best MoSa (FID 0.064, Top-1 0.518) and MARDM (FID 0.114, Top-1 0.500). On the larger Motion-X dataset, LG-Tok-mid (160 tokens) attains the highest Top-1 R-Precision of 0.591 and FID of 0.076, while LG-Tok-mini (104 tokens, less than half the tokens of full LG-Tok) still achieves FID 0.071 and Top-1 0.588, surpassing all baselines. The language-drop scheme with classifier-free guidance (g=2.0 on HumanML3D) further improves generation FID from 0.061 (g=0) to 0.057.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LG-Tok outperforms all eight baselines on both HumanML3D and Motion-X across FID, Top-1 R-Precision, MM-Dist, and CLIP-score. Against the closest competitor MoSa (which shares the same SAR generative backbone), LG-Tok improves FID from 0.064 to 0.057 and Top-1 from 0.518 to 0.542 on HumanML3D, and from 0.210 to 0.088 on Motion-X. Against MARDM (the strongest non-VQ baseline), LG-Tok nearly halves the FID (0.057 vs. 0.114 on HumanML3D; 0.088 vs. 0.147 on Motion-X). The compact LG-Tok-mini variant (104 tokens) already surpasses MoMask (294 tokens) and MARDM on both datasets, and on Motion-X it beats MoSa by a wide margin (FID 0.071 vs. 0.210). On the larger Motion-X dataset, LG-Tok-mid (160 tokens) achieves the best Top-1 (0.591) and FID (0.076), while the full 236-token LG-Tok shows a slight generation degradation (FID 0.088), confirming the reconstruction–generation trade-off discussed in the paper. LG-Tok also generalizes to the MoMask framework, where MoMask+LG-Tok improves reconstruction FID from 0.029 to 0.019 and generation FID from 0.116 to 0.111 on HumanML3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-based motion tokenization line (T2M-GPT, MoMask, MMM, MoSa) by replacing CNN-based encoders with a fully Transformer-based tokenizer-detokenizer and, more importantly, by injecting frozen LLaMA text embeddings into the tokenization stage itself—a role previously reserved for the generation phase. It builds directly on MoSa's Scale-wise Autoregressive (SAR) multi-scale token structure as its generative backbone and adapts the Classifier-Free Guidance principle from Ho & Salimans from logit/noise space to the motion space. The closest conceptual relative is TxtTok (image domain), which also uses language during tokenization, but LG-Tok departs by introducing a language-drop scheme to prevent shortcut learning and by using decoupled learnable queries over 196-frame sequences rather than frame-aligned latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a fundamental reconstruction-generation trade-off in discrete motion tokenization: more tokens improve reconstruction but burden the autoregressive generator, and language-guided semantic compression is a principled way to break this trade-off. The finding that LG-Tok-mini (104 tokens) matches or exceeds full-token baselines suggests that much of the information in prior motion tokens was redundant high-level semantics that language already encodes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer sequences (>200 frames), whether the language-drop probability (p=0.1) is robust across domains, and whether the motion-space guidance extrapolation (Eq. 4) can be combined with logit-space guidance for further gains. The trade-off analysis also hints that dataset scale shifts the optimal token count, a consideration for future large-scale motion generation.
