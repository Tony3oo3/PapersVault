## Problem

Traditional diffusion distillation (DMD) caps student performance at the teacher level because the optimization target is derived exclusively from the pretrained teacher. Recent attempts to break this ceiling by adding RL objectives rely on naive linear combinations of distillation and RL losses, which introduces optimization conflicts and lacks a principled mechanism for balancing the two. The paper asks whether distribution matching itself can be reframed as a reward, thereby unifying distillation and RL under a single policy-optimization objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262507/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a single RGB image via a 4-step flow-matching generator distilled from SD3-Medium (1024×1024) or SD3.5-Medium (512×512). Training is entirely data-free, using only prompts from LAION-AeS-6.5+ without any external image datasets. The distilled student supports stochastic (SDE-based) sampling at inference, enabling multi-step trajectory reuse during training via importance sampling.

## Method

The core insight is a mathematical identity showing that the DMD gradient (Eq. 4) is exactly equivalent to a policy-gradient update where the score difference (s_real − s_fake) acts as a reward R_dm on the denoising MDP, bridging distillation and RL under one objective. To handle the high variance of R_dm at larger diffused timesteps, the paper introduces Group Normalized Distribution Matching (GNDM), which applies GRPO-style group-mean subtraction and standardization across G samples sharing the same generation and diffusion timesteps, yielding a stabilized advantage estimate. The multi-reward variant GNDMR combines R_dm with external rewards (HPS, CLIP) using an adaptive weight β_dm,t that dynamically rescales auxiliary reward gradients to match the evolving amplitude of the distribution-matching signal, preventing collapse. Finally, because the denoising transition is Gaussian, the importance-sampling ratio is available in closed form, allowing multiple generator updates per sampled trajectory and halving training cost (GNDMR-IS).

## Results

On SD3-Medium (1024×1024, 4-step), GNDMR achieves HPS 30.37, PS 22.88, MPS 12.53, CS 38.20, FID 28.02, and FID-SD 12.21, outperforming the closest RL-distillation baseline DMDR (HPS 29.50, FID-SD 14.11) and the base 50-step model (HPS 29.00, FID 24.48). On SD3.5-Medium (512×512, 4-step), GNDMR reaches HPS 31.25, FID 24.44, and FID-SD 13.93, again surpassing DMDR (HPS 30.83, FID-SD 16.73). The importance-sampling variant GNDMR-IS halves training cost (128×4k vs 128×8k) while retaining HPS 30.00 and FID-SD 12.47 on SD3, approaching the full GNDMR performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GNDMR decisively outperforms DMDR (the closest RL+distillation baseline) on every aesthetic and fidelity metric: HPS 30.37 vs 29.50, MPS 12.53 vs 11.98, FID 28.02 vs 29.10, and FID-SD 12.21 vs 14.11 on SD3, with similar margins on SD3.5. Against pure distillation methods (DMD2, LCM, Flash-SD3, Hyper-SD), GNDMR wins on all perceptual scores (HPS, PS, MPS) by 2–4 points, but trades a modest FID increase (~1–2 points) relative to Flash-SD3 (26.01) and Hyper-SD (26.94), reflecting the aesthetic–fidelity trade-off. Compared to the 50-step base model, GNDMR improves HPS by 1.37 and MPS by 0.43 while using only 4 NFE, though the base model retains a lower FID (24.48 vs 28.02) and higher CLIP Score (38.86 vs 38.20). Against Flow-GRPO (50-step RL ceiling), GNDMR matches or slightly exceeds HPS (30.37 vs 30.35) and FID-SD (12.21 vs 12.39) with 1/12th the inference cost, though Flow-GRPO retains a small edge on PS (22.90 vs 22.88) and FID (26.63 vs 28.02). The IS variant GNDMR-IS achieves 98% of GNDMR's HPS at half the training cost, confirming the sampling-efficiency gain from the reward-centric formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DMD/DMD2 line of work, which established the score-difference gradient for distilling multi-step diffusion models into few-step generators, and on DMDR, which first jointly optimized DMD with RL objectives. The key conceptual departure is re-conceptualizing the distribution matching loss as a reward (R_dm) within a GRPO-style policy gradient framework, rather than treating it as a separate loss term to be linearly combined with RL objectives. This reframing, motivated by the structural similarity between the DMD gradient (Eq. 4) and the DDPO policy gradient (Eq. 5), allows group normalization, importance sampling, and adaptive multi-reward weighting to be applied uniformly, drawing on established RL machinery (GRPO, IS estimators) that prior distillation-RL hybrids handled ad hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified reward framework provides a principled way to balance distribution fidelity against aesthetic/semantic rewards without the optimization conflicts that arise from naive loss summation, and the importance sampling variant halves training cost while maintaining performance—practically significant for teams deploying few-step generators. The observation that R_dm naturally converges toward zero (analogous to PREF-GRPO's win-rate converging to 0.5) offers an inherent safeguard against reward hacking that unbounded reward maximization lacks. Open questions include whether the framework extends cleanly to video generation, whether the group-size and timestep-sharing choices generalize beyond SD3/SD3.5, and whether the modest FID/HPS gains over DMDR persist at higher resolutions or with more aggressive distillation (1-step).
