## Problem

Generating high-resolution images (2K–4K) with latent diffusion models is costly because native high-res sampling requires expensive denoising, while post-hoc pixel-space super-resolution adds latency and introduces ringing or semantic drift. Existing latent-space approaches (DemoFusion, LSRNA) still require a second diffusion stage, making them multi-stage and tightly coupled to specific VAEs. The paper asks whether a single deterministic feed-forward pass in latent space can upscale a generator's output to 2× or 4× resolution while preserving manifold geometry and high-frequency detail, without any additional denoising.

## Input / Output

LUA takes a latent tensor z ∈ R^{h×w×C} produced by a frozen diffusion generator (e.g., 64×64×16 from FLUX at 512 px, or 128×128×16 at 1024 px) and outputs an upscaled latent ẑ ∈ R^{αh×αw×C} for α ∈ {2,4}. The upscaled latent is decoded once by the frozen VAE (stride 8) to yield the final RGB image at 2048² or 4096². Because the channel width C differs across models (16 for FLUX/SD3, 4 for SDXL), only the first 1×1 convolution is swapped for cross-VAE transfer; the backbone and scale heads remain unchanged.

## Method

LUA is a SwinIR-style windowed-attention backbone with a 1×1 input convolution adapting to the VAE channel width and lightweight scale-specific pixel-shuffle heads for ×2 and ×4, jointly trained with balanced sampling so the backbone learns scale-agnostic features. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: Stage I aligns latent structure via L1 and channel-wise FFT-magnitude losses; Stage II adds pixel-domain downsampled L1 and Gaussian high-frequency residual terms to couple latent fidelity to decoded appearance; Stage III refines edges in pixel space using L1, FFT, and an EAGLE edge-aware gradient loss. This progressive latent-to-pixel schedule is essential—latent-only training leaves grid/blur artifacts, while pixel-only training fails to converge through the frozen VAE. At inference the module is a single deterministic forward pass, touching roughly 1/64 as many spatial positions as pixel-space SR, and transfers across FLUX, SD3, and SDXL by replacing only the first convolution and fine-tuning on ~50k latent pairs.

## Results

On OpenImages at 2048², SDXL+LUA achieves the best FID (180.80), pFID (97.90), KID (0.0018), and CLIP (0.764) among all compared methods while running in 3.52 s—roughly 6× faster than LSRNA-DemoFusion (20.77 s) and 8× faster than DemoFusion (28.99 s). At 4096², LUA again leads on FID (176.90), pFID (61.80), KID (0.0015), and CLIP (0.759) with the lowest runtime (6.87 s), compared to 91.64 s for LSRNA-DemoFusion and 225.77 s for DemoFusion. At 1024², LUA has the lowest latency (1.42 s) and best patch-level fidelity (pFID 191.75, CLIP 0.738) but trails SDXL (Direct) and LSRNA-DemoFusion on global FID (209.80 vs. 194.55), which the authors attribute to the 64×64 input latent constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 2048² and 4096², SDXL+LUA outperforms all five baselines on FID, pFID, KID, and CLIP while also being the fastest method. Against LSRNA-DemoFusion (the strongest multi-stage baseline), LUA improves FID from 181.24→180.80 at 2048² and 177.95→176.90 at 4096², while reducing runtime by ~6× (3.52 s vs. 20.77 s) and ~13× (6.87 s vs. 91.64 s) respectively. Against DemoFusion, the speed advantage is even more dramatic: 3.52 s vs. 28.99 s at 2048² and 6.87 s vs. 225.77 s at 4096², with LUA also achieving lower FID (180.80 vs. 184.79; 176.90 vs. 185.36). Against SDXL+SwinIR (pixel-space SR), LUA wins on FID, pFID, KID, CLIP, and speed at both 2048² and 4096², confirming that latent-space upscaling avoids the ringing and noise artifacts of post-decode SR. Against SDXL (Direct) high-resolution sampling, LUA avoids the quality collapse at 4096² (FID 176.90 vs. 280.42) and is 21× faster (6.87 s vs. 148.71 s). The one setting where LUA trails is 1024² global FID (209.80 vs. 194.55 for SDXL Direct and LSRNA-DemoFusion), which the authors attribute to the limited information in the 64×64 input latent; however, LUA still leads on patch-level pFID and CLIP at that resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUA repurposes the SwinIR backbone—originally designed for pixel-space image restoration—for latent-space upscaling, and departs from multi-stage latent SR pipelines (DemoFusion, LSRNA) by eliminating the second diffusion pass entirely in favor of a single feed-forward adapter. The multi-stage latent-pixel training curriculum (latent L1+FFT → joint latent-pixel consistency → pixel-space edge refinement) is the paper's principal methodological contribution, addressing the instability of training through a frozen VAE decoder. Architecturally, the shared-backbone-with-scale-specific-pixel-shuffle-heads design follows the MDSR/SwinIR multi-scale recipe, while the cross-VAE transfer strategy (replacing only the first convolution) is a practical extension not seen in prior latent SR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LUA offers a drop-in, single-decode path to 2K/4K synthesis that is roughly 6× faster than multi-stage re-diffusion baselines and transfers across FLUX, SD3, and SDXL with only a first-layer channel change and ~50k fine-tuning pairs, making it a practical tool for production pipelines where latency and model coupling matter. Key limitations remain: at 1024² the 64×64 input latent constrains recoverable detail, the adapter is deterministic and cannot correct base-latent hallucinations, and it is trained only on clean VAE-encoded latents rather than real-world degradations. Open directions include temporal consistency for video, joint latent refinement-and-upscaling, and extending to smaller-stride VAEs that would increase latent information density.
