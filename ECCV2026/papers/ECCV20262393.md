## Problem

Existing 3D relighting methods—whether inverse rendering, diffusion-based generation, or hybrid approaches—treat each lighting change as a full recomputation problem, requiring re-evaluation of rendering equations, re-running diffusion processes, or regenerating appearance branches. None maintain lighting edits as persistent, incremental updates to a reusable scene representation, making progressive lighting design (adding one source at a time) impractical. The paper asks whether latent scene tokens, which encode geometry and appearance jointly without explicit 3D structure, can support relighting as a direct, composable transformation in token space.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 4–32 posed RGB images at 512×512 resolution along with a lighting specification (environment map, point light, or area light) that is discretized into Plücker ray tokens encoding ray geometry, spectral color, and radiant power. It produces relit images at arbitrary target camera poses and, critically, edited scene tokens (3072 tokens of dimension 768) that remain in the same latent space as the encoder output, enabling them to be fed back into the editor for further lighting edits without re-encoding pixels.

## Method

LumiTokens inserts a Scene Token Editor between the LVSM encoder and decoder: a 12-layer transformer that concatenates scene tokens with light-ray tokens and processes them through self-attention (rather than cross-attention), so light tokens can attend to scene geometry to determine where shadows and highlights fall while scene tokens update their appearance accordingly. All lighting types share a unified Plücker ray parameterization—environment maps use zero moment vectors, point lights encode 3D position via both direction and moment, and area lights sample origins across the emitting surface—enabling native 3D user interaction without explicit spatial structure. Training jointly fine-tunes the encoder and editor (decoder frozen) with a rendering loss (L2 + LPIPS) applied at each step of a random multi-edit chain, plus a lighting invariance loss that penalizes token differences across different source illuminations of the same scene, encouraging the encoder to factor out source lighting. The decoder uses a DPT head instead of LVSM's per-token MLP to recover sharper high-frequency detail. Because the editor's output stays in the latent manifold, multiple lighting edits compose directly in token space, avoiding the lossy decode-and-re-encode round trip that degrades pixel-space editing.

## Results

On multi-view relighting (4 input views), LumiTokens achieves 30.48 PSNR and 0.954 SSIM under image-level rescaling, exceeding the strongest baseline Neural Gaffer by 2.1 dB in PSNR. On novel-view relighting (8 input views), it reaches 27.76 PSNR and 0.069 LPIPS on synthetic data while requiring only 8 source views compared to 30–50 views needed by inverse-rendering baselines. The method's LPIPS remains stable at 0.045 under both image-level and scene-level rescaling protocols, indicating consistent perceptual quality across views, whereas DiffusionRenderer degrades from 0.038 to 0.086 under scene-level rescaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Neural Gaffer (single-image diffusion), LumiTokens wins on PSNR (30.48 vs. 28.40) and SSIM (0.954 vs. 0.947) in multi-view relighting, but Neural Gaffer achieves a lower LPIPS (0.030 vs. 0.045), likely benefiting from its generative prior producing perceptually sharper textures. Against DiffusionRenderer (video-diffusion with cross-attention), LumiTokens leads by 4.1 dB in PSNR and maintains a stable LPIPS of 0.045 under both rescaling protocols, while DiffusionRenderer's LPIPS nearly doubles under scene-level rescaling (0.038→0.086), revealing cross-view inconsistency. Against LightSwitch (multi-view diffusion), LumiTokens dominates across all metrics (30.48 vs. 21.22 PSNR). In novel-view relighting, LumiTokens achieves the best PSNR (27.76 dB) and LPIPS (0.069) on synthetic data with only 8 input views, whereas TensoIR (inverse rendering) edges it out on SSIM (0.928 vs. 0.917) but requires 30–50 views and per-scene optimization. The key practical advantage is input efficiency: LumiTokens needs 8 views versus 30–50 for all inverse-rendering baselines, eliminating costly per-scene optimization entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LVSM's latent scene token architecture (encoder-decoder over unstructured 1D tokens) and inserts a Scene Token Editor between encoder and decoder to perform relighting as a direct transformation in that latent space. It departs from all three established relighting paradigms—inverse rendering with explicit BRDF, diffusion-based view-space generation, and hybrid feed-forward-plus-generative methods—by treating lighting as a composable edit on persistent scene tokens rather than a decomposition, regeneration, or re-rendering problem. The unification of environment maps, point lights, and area lights into Plücker ray tokens is a novel interface design that enables 3D user interaction with a spatially unstructured representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive relighting capability—chaining multiple lighting edits in token space without decode-and-re-encode—opens a practical workflow for interactive 3D content creation that existing paradigms cannot replicate without full recomputation at each step. The sparse-view requirement (4–8 views vs. 30–50 for inverse rendering baselines) and elimination of per-scene optimization make the approach significantly more accessible. However, all experiments are on synthetic Objaverse objects rendered in Blender Cycles; generalization to real-world captures with complex materials, imperfect poses, and uncontrolled lighting remains untested. The paper also acknowledges that longer editing chains may accumulate drift, and comparisons with the most recent hybrid methods (RelitLRM, NeAR) were not possible due to availability constraints.
