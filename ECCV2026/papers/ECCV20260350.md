## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction from uncalibrated images remains challenging because existing methods either require known camera parameters, entangle geometry and appearance in a unified backbone (limiting texture fidelity), or introduce information leakage when jointly estimating camera poses from source and target views. No prior approach simultaneously addresses semantic-aware modeling, decoupled appearance reconstruction, and leakage-free training in a fully camera-free setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260350/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N uncalibrated RGB source images (no intrinsics or extrinsics) and produces per-pixel 3D Gaussians (center, opacity, color, scale, rotation) in a camera-centric frame, which are transformed to a world frame and rendered via differentiable splatting into novel-view images. Training uses 2 source views at 256×256 resolution on DL3DV, with cross-dataset evaluation on ACID and RealEstate10K; high-resolution evaluation extends to 448×448 and 504×504. The model contains 1.535B parameters and runs in ~165 ms per inference pass on a single H100 GPU.

## Method

StructSplat adopts a structured representation with three specialized encoders: a VGGT-based geometry encoder for 3D structure, a frozen DINOv3 semantic encoder for view-invariant high-level priors, and a lightweight CNN texture encoder for pixel-aligned high-frequency appearance. The Gaussian decoder modifies standard DPT heads with a redesigned Reassembling block that spatially aligns and concatenates geometric and semantic features, then injects high-resolution texture features just before the final prediction layer to preserve fine details. A parallel-stream camera alignment strategy processes both source+target and source-only views through the geometry extractor, then aligns the two coordinate systems via closed-form quaternion rotation alignment and scale+offset translation alignment, ensuring the Gaussian decoder never accesses target-view features and preventing information leakage during training.

## Results

On DL3DV (2 source views, no camera parameters), StructSplat achieves 28.045 PSNR, 0.888 SSIM, and 0.091 LPIPS, surpassing the best camera-free baseline AnySplat (22.377 PSNR) by +5.67 dB. In cross-dataset evaluation, StructSplat achieves 24.372 PSNR on ACID (+1.94 dB over AnySplat) and 22.240 PSNR on RealEstate10K (+1.72 dB over AnySplat). Notably, StructSplat also outperforms intrinsics-dependent methods such as NoPoSplat (25.592 PSNR on DL3DV) despite requiring no camera parameters at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructSplat decisively outperforms all camera-free baselines across all three datasets. On DL3DV, it leads AnySplat by +5.67 dB PSNR (28.045 vs. 22.377), Depth Anything 3 by +7.33 dB, and Splatt3R by +12.11 dB. In cross-dataset evaluation, the margins narrow but remain consistent: +1.94 dB over AnySplat on ACID and +1.72 dB on RealEstate10K. Against intrinsics-dependent methods, StructSplat surpasses NoPoSplat (25.592 PSNR) and FLARE (23.441 PSNR) on DL3DV, and is competitive with PF3plat (22.766 PSNR) on RealEstate10K despite using no camera parameters at all. The largest gap appears on DL3DV where the structured representation and camera alignment strategy provide the most benefit; on the more challenging ACID and RealEstate10K datasets, the advantage over AnySplat shrinks to roughly 1.7–1.9 dB but the method still maintains the best SSIM and LPIPS among camera-free approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

StructSplat builds on the VGGT geometry backbone and DPT decoder architecture, but departs from prior feed-forward 3DGS methods (Splatt3R, Depth Anything 3, AnySplat) that entangle geometry and appearance in a single unified backbone. The key conceptual departure is a structured representation that explicitly separates geometry (VGGT), semantics (frozen DINOv3), and texture (lightweight CNN) into distinct encoders with dedicated roles, motivated by the observation that geometry-oriented features inherently fail to capture high-frequency appearance. The camera alignment strategy for leakage-free training is also novel relative to prior pose-free methods that either ignore the issue or use simpler scale-only alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured representation insight—that compact 3D geometry features are fundamentally biased toward spatial smoothness and cannot recover fine textures—provides a clear architectural principle for future feed-forward 3D reconstruction work. The camera alignment strategy addresses a practical training concern (information leakage via cross-attention) that likely affects other feed-forward multi-view models but has not been systematically studied. Open questions remain for extremely sparse views, severe occlusions, complex view-dependent lighting, and dynamic scenes, all acknowledged by the authors as limitations.
