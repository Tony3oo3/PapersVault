## Problem

Existing 3D editing methods for Gaussian Splatting assets are either prohibitively slow (per-scene optimization or iterative diffusion sampling requiring hundreds of seconds), lack precision for fine-grained 2D-to-3D control, or are fundamentally destructive to the original asset's identity through multiview regeneration or spatial edit leakage. There is no framework that enables interactive, identity-preserving appearance refinement of 3DGS assets from arbitrary 2D user edits at real-time speeds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261843/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an existing 3DGS asset (rendered from canonical viewpoints to initialize a Gaussian LRM) plus one or more user-provided 2D edited views at 512×512 (or 2K crops for super-resolution refinement) with corresponding camera poses. The 2D edits can originate from diverse sources—manual paint-overs, super-resolution outputs, or generative editors like Nano Banana. The output is an updated 3DGS asset with modified Gaussian attributes (position, scale, opacity, rotation, and 4-order spherical harmonics), where local edits are spatially confined via ray-traced voxel identification and global edits propagate coherently across all Gaussians, all at ~0.3 s per edit after a one-time preprocessing step.

## Method

SplatPainter is a two-stage feedforward pipeline. Stage I (one-time preprocessing) renders the input 3DGS from canonical views, feeds them through a Gaussian LRM (GS-LRM backbone) to produce a dense, feature-enriched Gaussian cloud, then compresses it via a voxel-based local transformer (6-layer encoder → cross-attention with top-25%-opacity queries → 6-layer decoder) into compact voxel latents. Stage II (interactive refinement) is a deep transformer with 8 TTT layers, where each layer updates a small set of fast weights (a SwiGLU-MLP mapping) via 5 Muon-optimizer gradient steps on a self-supervised key-value reconstruction loss computed from the new 2D edit tokens; the adapted mapping is then applied in parallel to both image tokens and voxel latents to produce updated representations. For local edits, ray tracing restricts updates to visible voxels and an additional TTT layer merges refined tokens with original GS tokens (doubling Gaussian count for fine detail); for global edits, the same mechanism updates all tokens. The entire system is trained end-to-end with an L2 + LPIPS image reconstruction loss on Objaverse1.0 and TexVerse, with Stage I frozen during Stage II training.

## Results

On the local refinement task (Table 1), SplatPainter (Stage II) achieves PSNR 21.07, SSIM 0.5780, and LPIPS 0.3673 in ~2.2 s, outperforming the best feedforward baseline GS-LRM (PSNR 20.28) and matching or exceeding optimization-based methods (3DGS: 20.52, SRGS: 21.01) that require 323–495 s. On global relighting (Table 2), the method reaches PSNR 25.03 with 4 input views in ~1.7 s, surpassing ReLitLRM (PSNR 21.68, 22.1 s). On creative editing (Table 3), SplatPainter achieves PSNR 25.61 on edited regions and 33.02 on non-edited regions, dramatically outperforming VoxHammer (11.25/13.86) and VineDresser3D (11.99/15.79), confirming strong identity preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against feedforward baselines, SplatPainter outperforms GS-LRM [98] by +0.79 dB PSNR (21.07 vs 20.28) at a comparable inference cost (~2.2 s vs 0.92 s), and decisively beats GenDen [52] by +3.75 dB PSNR (21.07 vs 17.32). Against optimization-based methods, SplatPainter matches or slightly exceeds SRGS [28] in PSNR (21.07 vs 21.01) and 3DGS [41] (21.07 vs 20.52) while being 150–225× faster (2.2 s vs 323–495 s). For global relighting, SplatPainter (4 views) surpasses ReLitLRM [101] by +3.35 dB PSNR (25.03 vs 21.68) and is ~13× faster (1.74 s vs 22.1 s). On creative editing, the advantage is most dramatic: SplatPainter achieves PSNR 25.61 on edited regions and 33.02 on non-edited regions, exceeding VoxHammer [2] by +14.36/+19.16 dB and VineDresser3D [16] by +13.62/+17.23 dB respectively, demonstrating that the voxel-localized TTT update prevents the spatial leakage and identity drift that plague structured-latent diffusion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatPainter builds on GS-LRM as its feature-extraction backbone and repurposes Test-Time Training (originally designed for distribution-shift generalization) as a state-aware iterative editing mechanism, which is a novel architectural repurposing. It departs from the dominant 'edit-generate-lift' paradigm (multiview diffusion + reconstruction, e.g., DGE, CMD) and from direct 3D latent diffusion (VoxHammer, VineDresser3D) by operating directly on a compact voxelized Gaussian representation with local TTT updates, avoiding both destructive regeneration and spatial leakage. The voxel-based local transformer for compression draws on continuous reconstruction and memory-augmented architectures, but applies them specifically to create an editable, structured GS latent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D content pipelines: existing editing methods are either too slow (hundreds of seconds) or destructive to the original asset's identity, making iterative creative workflows infeasible. The demonstrated identity preservation (PSNR 33.02 on unedited regions vs. ~14–16 for baselines) and interactive speed (~0.3 s per edit) make this a potentially adoptable tool for production 3DGS authoring. Key limitations include appearance-only editing (no geometry changes), reliance on a pre-trained LRM backbone, and the creative-editing evaluation being limited to 10 generated examples. Open questions include whether the TTT state-aware mechanism can extend to geometric editing and how it scales to larger, unbounded scenes.
