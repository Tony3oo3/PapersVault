## Problem

Current monocular 3D geometry estimation methods are fragmented by fixed camera model assumptions (pinhole, fisheye, or panoramic) and rigid input schemes that cannot dynamically incorporate optional geometric cues like camera intrinsics or sparse depth. Deploying a unified 3D perception system in practical settings with heterogeneous sensors thus requires maintaining multiple isolated models, incurring prohibitive memory overhead and complicating sensor fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262402/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single monocular image from any projection (pinhole, fisheye, or equirectangular) along with optional camera intrinsics K and sparse depth S (e.g., from LiDAR or SfM). It outputs a per-pixel metric 3D point cloud factorized into a unit ray direction r, a scalar radial distance d, and a global metric scale s, plus a binary sky-region mask. The ray-distance factorization is the key structural choice: unlike planar depth (which diverges at 180° FoV) or direct XYZ regression (which entangles projection with geometry), the representation remains valid for full 360° scenes and points behind the camera.

## Method

OmniPoint uses a DINOv2-ViT-Large backbone with DPT heads, but its core novelty is a decoupled ray-distance representation (P = d·r) paired with a decoupled training objective: L_ray supervises the unprojection mapping independently, while L_point projects the predicted distance along the ground-truth ray to isolate structural errors and prevent gradient cross-contamination. To overcome the severe scarcity of non-pinhole training data, a bidirectional augmentation pipeline synthesizes fisheye/panoramic views from labeled pinhole datasets (Perspective-to-Any) and generates pseudo-labels by sampling virtual pinhole patches from unlabeled omnidirectional images (Any-to-Perspective). For flexible prior integration, learnable input-state embeddings signal the active configuration to the ViT to prevent feature distribution shifts, and sparse depth is densified via a fully vectorized Gaussian splatting procedure before being fused with image features through a lightweight convolutional encoder.

## Results

On standard pinhole images (S.FoV), OmniPoint achieves 4.04 Rel / 96.8 δ₁ for depth and 5.55 Rel / 95.6 δ₁ for point clouds, matching pinhole-specialized models like MoGe V2 (3.98 / 96.8). On fisheye images (L.FoV), OmniPoint reduces the relative error from 11.5 (UniK3D) to 6.66 Rel for point clouds, a 42% improvement. On 360° panoramas, OmniPoint achieves 5.79 Rel / 95.1 δ₁ for depth, outperforming the panorama-specific DA² (6.65 Rel) and UniK3D (10.4 Rel). For zero-shot metric depth, OmniPoint scores 83.5 mean δ₁ across six benchmarks, surpassing UniK3D (82.6) and MoGe V2 (77.5), and reaches 98.0 mean δ₁ when conditioned on sparse depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On pinhole (S.FoV) images, OmniPoint (4.04 Rel) is competitive with the best pinhole-specialized models MoGe V2 (3.98 Rel) and MoGe V1 (4.05 Rel), confirming that the universal ray-distance representation does not sacrifice accuracy in the standard setting. On fisheye (L.FoV) images, OmniPoint dramatically outperforms all baselines: it reduces point-cloud Rel from 11.5 (UniK3D, the only other method handling fisheye) to 6.66, and from 23.4 (MoGe V2) to 6.66, a >3× improvement. On 360° panoramas, OmniPoint (5.79 Rel depth, 5.90 Rel point) beats the panorama-specific DA² (6.65 / 7.30) and the universal UniK3D (10.4 / 11.4) by a wide margin, while pinhole-only methods like MoGe V2 (25.1 / 102.8) and DA3 (25.5 / 101.5) fail catastrophically. For zero-shot metric depth (Table 3), OmniPoint (83.5 mean δ₁) edges out UniK3D (82.6) and substantially outperforms MoGe V2 (77.5) and UniDepth V2 (74.4); with sparse depth conditioning, OmniPoint jumps to 98.0 mean δ₁, far exceeding all baselines. The only comparison where OmniPoint is slightly behind is S.FoV depth Rel vs. MoGe V2 (4.04 vs. 3.98), a negligible 0.06 gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniPoint builds directly on the MoGe/MoGe V2 lineage, adopting their ROE alignment for scale recovery, metric alignment loss, and normal/local consistency losses, while replacing the pinhole-specific planar-depth output with a camera-agnostic ray-direction plus radial-distance representation. It departs from UniK3D, the closest prior universal-camera work, by rejecting Spherical Harmonics ray parameterization in favor of explicit per-pixel ray vectors, and by introducing a bidirectional 3D-space data augmentation pipeline (Perspective-to-Any synthesis and Any-to-Perspective self-training) to overcome the severe scarcity of non-pinhole ground truth. The flexible prior-injection mechanism (input-state embeddings plus vectorized Gaussian smoothing for sparse depth) extends the conditional depth estimation line (PromptDA, PriorDA) to a camera-agnostic setting, unifying three previously disjoint research directions—pinhole depth, wide-FoV/panoramic depth, and depth completion—into a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For robotics and autonomous driving systems that must fuse heterogeneous sensor suites (forward pinhole + surround fisheye + LiDAR), OmniPoint eliminates the need to maintain and coordinate multiple isolated depth models, reducing memory overhead and simplifying sensor fusion. The dramatic sparse-depth conditioning result (83.5 → 98.0 mean δ₁) positions the model as a drop-in depth densification system, while the bidirectional augmentation strategy offers a reusable recipe for any future camera model lacking labeled 3D data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even more exotic projections (e.g., catadioptric or multi-lens arrays), whether the DINOv2 backbone is truly necessary versus lighter alternatives, and how the method behaves under significant domain shift (e.g., underwater or thermal imaging) where the ray-distance factorization may not capture all relevant geometry.
