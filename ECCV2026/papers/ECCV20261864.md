## Problem

Existing human trajectory prediction methods operate in fully observed third-person or bird's-eye-view scenes and rely on motion history and context, neglecting the cognitive and experiential factors—particularly perceived path uncertainty—that drive human navigation behaviors such as scanning, hesitation, and backtracking. Moreover, no publicly available multimodal egocentric navigation dataset with cognitive annotations exists to study these effects at scale.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3-second past window (30 steps at 10 Hz) comprising egocentric RGB frames (resized to 224×224, encoded by a frozen DINOv2 into 384-dim CLS tokens), body-frame motion deltas (Δx, Δy, Δψ), 6D continuous head rotations, normalized gaze coordinates (u, v), and a body-frame navigation goal [d, sin β, cos β]. It jointly produces a 1-second future horizon (10 steps) of 3-DOF body-frame trajectory, a 6D head-pose sequence, and a single scalar perceived uncertainty score in [0, 1] at the current timestep—making the uncertainty a moment-to-moment state signal rather than a temporal sequence.

## Method

EgoCogNav uses a three-module architecture with late fusion: a perception stream (frozen DINOv2 features encoded by a 2-layer transformer) and an action stream (motion, head, gaze, and goal encoded by a 4-layer transformer) are independently self-attended, temporally mean-pooled, and concatenated into a shared representation. The cognition module then performs three operations: (1) a 2-layer MLP with sigmoid predicts perceived uncertainty from the shared features, providing gradient signal that shapes the encoder toward decision-difficulty-sensitive representations; (2) 16 learnable navigation pattern vectors are queried via cross-attention to retrieve situation-relevant context beyond the immediate input window; and (3) uncertainty-conditioned decoding (UCD) applies adaptive layer normalization parameterized by the predicted uncertainty to modulate how prediction heads process the memory-augmented features. Training uses a multi-task objective combining discounted L1 trajectory loss with variance regularization, rotation-matrix L1 for head poses, and MSE for uncertainty, all with equal weighting.

## Results

On the CEN test set, EgoCogNav achieves ADE 0.1051, FDE 0.2074, and head-rotation L1 0.0698 across all scenarios, reducing ADE/FDE by 3.8%/5.0% over the strongest baseline (EgoCast-adapted). For perceived uncertainty prediction, EgoCogNav reaches Spearman ρ = 0.788 and MAE 0.0986, substantially outperforming the EMU entropy proxy (ρ = 0.081) and the PATH_U heuristic (ρ = 0.195). The behavioral sensitivity metric ΔU (mean uncertainty elevation during annotated navigation behaviors vs. neutral segments) is 0.0829 for EgoCogNav versus 0.0122 (EMU) and 0.0212 (PATH_U), confirming the model captures decision-difficulty coupling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoCogNav outperforms all four trajectory/head baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor, the adapted EgoCast forecasting module [16], EgoCogNav reduces ADE from 0.1092 to 0.1051 (−3.8%) and FDE from 0.2184 to 0.2074 (−5.0%) on all scenarios, with similar margins on the high-uncertainty subset. The early-fusion M_Transformer baseline [41,46] lags by a wider margin (ADE 0.1536 vs. 0.1051), indicating that late fusion with modality-specific temporal encoding preserves sensory patterns better than concatenating all streams into a single transformer. For uncertainty prediction specifically, EgoCogNav (ρ = 0.788, MAE 0.0986) dramatically surpasses both the EMU entropy proxy [22] (ρ = 0.081, MAE 0.1887) and the adapted PATH_U model [67] (ρ = 0.195, MAE 0.1857), showing that hand-crafted scene-ambiguity or decision-complexity features cannot capture the subjective, person-specific nature of perceived path uncertainty. The ΔU behavioral-sensitivity gap is equally pronounced: EgoCogNav elevates predicted uncertainty by 0.0829 during annotated hesitation, wrong-turn, and backtracking events, versus only 0.0122 and 0.0212 for the two rule-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of egocentric trajectory prediction (EgoNav, Lookout, EgoCast) and cognitive wayfinding models (EMU theory, PATH-U), which have previously been studied in isolation and typically from third-person or BEV perspectives. It repurposes the adaptive layer normalization mechanism from diffusion transformers (DiT) as an uncertainty-conditioned decoding module, and borrows the learnable query idea from MTR++ for a memory-augmented navigation pattern retrieval. The key departure is treating perceived uncertainty not as an external input or rule-based heuristic but as a jointly predicted internal state that both shapes encoder representations through shared gradients and modulates decoding, all within a single egocentric perception-decision-action loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CEN dataset is the first public resource combining egocentric video, gaze, motion, and self-reported cognitive uncertainty for navigation research, opening a previously inaccessible empirical space for studying how internal states drive observable behaviors like hesitation and backtracking. The ablation finding that a simple uncertainty prediction head outperforms the more elaborate UCD and memory modules suggests that the gradient signal from cognitive supervision may be the primary driver of improved forecasting, raising open questions about whether explicit conditioning mechanisms are necessary at scale. Limitations include the short 1-second prediction horizon, single-trajectory output, small participant pool (17), and the fact that all evaluation is on the authors' own dataset, making generalizability claims tentative. Future work on generative multi-hypothesis forecasting, longer horizons, and richer cognitive signals (affect, spatial memory) would be needed to assess whether this framework scales to practical assistive navigation.
