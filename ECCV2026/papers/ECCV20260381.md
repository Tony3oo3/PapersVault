## Problem

Existing event-guided deblurring methods either treat event streams as generic feature modalities in end-to-end networks or rely on oversimplified blur models (uniform kernels, linear motion), failing to exploit the physical relationship between event-triggered brightness changes and the actual formation of spatially varying motion blur. This leaves a gap for a unified framework that explicitly reconstructs per-pixel blur kernels from events while handling complex, non-uniform real-world motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a blurred RGB image and a corresponding event stream as input; the event stream is discretized into M=8 fixed temporal bins (event frames) following standard preprocessing. The output is a single deblurred sharp image at the same spatial resolution as the input. During training, 256×256 patches are randomly cropped from the blurred image and its event representation, and the predicted per-pixel blur kernels are constrained to a 5×5 spatial support.

## Method

The core contribution is a physically grounded pipeline that first estimates dense pixel-wise motion trajectories from the event stream via a differentiable event-alignment objective (maximizing gradient sharpness of motion-compensated events with an L1 smoothness regularizer), then constructs per-pixel point spread functions by splatting temporal displacements onto a discrete kernel grid. These kernels define a spatially varying blur operator that is embedded into an ADMM-inspired unrolled network: each of N=6 stages alternates between a data-consistency update (USM, one gradient step on blur fidelity + event structural consistency + dual term), a learned proximal update (UZM, a lightweight encoder-decoder replacing hand-crafted image priors), and a dual-variable update (UUM). The final output is a learnable weighted aggregation of all intermediate reconstructions, supervised end-to-end with a Charbonnier loss against ground-truth sharp images.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, the proposed method achieves 37.15 PSNR and 0.978 SSIM, outperforming the best frame-based method (EVSSM, 34.51 PSNR) by 2.64 dB and the best prior event-guided method (CMTA-7, 36.78 PSNR) by 0.37 dB. On HS-ERGB, it reaches 29.43 PSNR / 0.818 SSIM, surpassing MAT (28.97 / 0.816) by 0.46 dB. On REBlur, it attains 37.24 PSNR / 0.971 SSIM, exceeding MAT (36.97 / 0.969) by 0.27 dB. The method also reports lower model parameter count and faster execution time than most competitors (Fig. 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 16 baselines across all three benchmarks. On GoPro, it leads the strongest event-guided competitor CMTA-7 [19] by 0.37 dB PSNR (37.15 vs. 36.78) and MAT [56] by 0.48 dB, while surpassing the best frame-only method EVSSM [21] by a substantial 2.64 dB. On HS-ERGB, the margin over MAT [56] narrows to 0.46 dB (29.43 vs. 28.97), and on REBlur it is 0.27 dB (37.24 vs. 36.97), indicating consistent but smaller gains on real-event datasets. Among frame-based methods, the gap is largest: the proposed method exceeds Concertormer [23] (34.42) and EVSSM [21] (34.51) by over 2.6 dB on GoPro, confirming that explicit event-derived blur modeling provides a clear advantage over learned image priors alone. SSIM gains are more modest (0.001–0.007 over the best baselines), suggesting the primary improvement is in pixel-level fidelity rather than structural similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in event-guided deblurring: end-to-end methods that treat event streams as generic feature modalities (EFNet, MAENet, MAT) and physics-informed methods that rely on simplified blur assumptions such as uniform kernels or linear motion (Nakabayashi et al., Zhang et al.). Instead, it explicitly models the physical formation of spatially varying blur by estimating dense pixel-wise motion trajectories from events via a differentiable alignment objective and constructing per-pixel PSFs, then embeds this operator into an ADMM-inspired deep unfolding framework. The deep unfolding architecture connects to the broader literature on learned optimization for inverse problems, while the event-alignment formulation for motion estimation is a novel contribution that bridges event camera physics with the deblurring pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding the blur operator in the physical event-to-trajectory-to-PSF pipeline, the paper offers an interpretable alternative to purely data-driven deblurring that may generalize better to unseen motion patterns. However, the approach requires synchronized event data, which limits applicability to systems equipped with event sensors, and the evaluation is conducted at relatively modest resolutions (480×480). Open questions include whether the trajectory-based kernel construction remains accurate under severe occlusions, rapid illumination changes, or very short exposures where event density is low, and whether the framework can be made lightweight enough for real-time deployment on embedded platforms.
