## Problem

Diffusion and flow-based generators produce high-quality images but treat intermediate denoising states as opaque computational by-products, offering users no way to inspect, evaluate, or redirect the generation process. While recent work exploits generation order to improve sample quality, intermediate states remain disposable scratchpads rather than semantically meaningful objects. The paper asks whether the generative trajectory itself can be made interpretable, structurally organized, and editable at every stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262625/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and guidance scale as conditioning, operating in DINOv2 feature space at 256×256 resolution (16×16 token grid). Rather than producing a single final image, it outputs a sequence of L=4 hierarchical latent canvases progressing from coarse object/background segmentation to full-resolution detail, each decodable via a shared ViT-XL RAE decoder into a visually interpretable RGB image. This trajectory structure enables mid-generation inspection and localized editing at any semantic level before subsequent stages are generated.

## Method

TF constructs a teacher hierarchy by unsupervised clustering over DINOv2 tokens: K-means (K=2) with a center prior separates object from background, and agglomerative clustering with a combined cosine-spatial distance recovers part and subpart regions. A single shared DiT backbone is trained across all levels using hierarchy-conditioned one-step flow matching (iMF with x-prediction and v-loss), where each level's denoising is conditioned on the previous level's canvas via channel-wise concatenation and the level index via learned embeddings. A structural loss penalizes predicted tokens from deviating from their assigned region's mean feature, enforcing hierarchical consistency. At inference, generation proceeds sequentially from coarse to fine with exactly one NFE per level (4 total), and every intermediate canvas is decodable and editable—supporting feature swaps and shape modifications that propagate forward through the Markov chain while leaving coarser levels untouched.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, TF achieves FID 1.98 (TF-H/16+FD-loss, 1.1B params) and FID 2.02 (TF-L/16+FD-loss, 515M params) at only 80 training epochs with just 4 NFEs total. Without the FD-loss post-training, the base models reach FID 5.97–7.93 at 80 epochs, still competitive with multi-step methods trained for 200+ epochs. The paper emphasizes that FID is not the primary contribution; the key result is that TF exposes L=4 decodable, editable intermediate states at the same inference cost (4 NFEs) as a single-step generator, a capability no baseline provides.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most closely related hierarchical method NVG [41], TF-L/16+FD-loss achieves FID 2.02 with 4 NFEs and 80 epochs, outperforming NVG-d16 (FID 3.03, 184 NFEs, 200 epochs) and NVG-d20 (FID 2.44, 184 NFEs, 250 epochs) while requiring 46× fewer network evaluations. Among one-step generators, TF-H/16+FD-loss (FID 1.98, 4 NFEs) is competitive with iMF-L/2 (FID 1.86, 1 NFE, 640 epochs) and matches pMF-L/16 (FID 2.52, 1 NFE, 320 epochs) at the B-size variant. TF trails the strongest multi-step baseline RAE+DiT-XL/2 (FID 1.13) by ~0.85 FID, but that method requires 100 NFEs and 800 epochs versus TF's 4 NFEs and 80 epochs. The paper's central claim is not FID superiority but that TF is the only method in the comparison that produces semantically structured, decodable, and editable intermediate states at every generation stage—capability that no baseline (including NVG, which uses discrete VQ-VAE tokens without region structure) provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

TF inherits its generative backbone from the one-step MeanFlow/iMF line of work (Geng et al.) and the x-prediction parameterization from Pixel MeanFlow (Lu et al.), while operating in the DINOv2 feature space made visually decodable by Representation Autoencoders (Zheng et al.). Conceptually, it departs from prior trajectory-structuring methods (Diffusion Forcing, DeCo, Latent Forcing) that treat intermediate states as disposable computational scratchpads, and from NVG's hierarchical generation that relies on resolution-tied binary VQ-VAE splits. The key positioning is that TF makes the generation path itself a user-facing, semantically structured, and editable interface rather than an opaque denoising schedule, combining one-step flow matching per level (L-NFE total) with unsupervised clustering over pretrained features to define the hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing the generative trajectory as a first-class interactive object, TF opens a route toward image synthesis where users can inspect, evaluate, and locally edit at any semantic granularity (object → part → subpart) without re-running the full pipeline, all at only 4 NFEs. The trajectory-aware metrics (LIS, PMR) provide a principled way to measure structural controllability that FID cannot capture, which could become a useful evaluation standard for any hierarchical generator. Key limitations include the fixed clustering depth and center prior (limiting generalization to non-centered compositions), the Markov conditioning that prevents direct coarse-to-fine information flow, and the fact that results are reported at only 80 training epochs on class-conditional ImageNet. Open questions include whether adaptive per-image hierarchy depth, richer segmentation-based hierarchy sources, and text-conditional extension can preserve the trajectory-level controllability at scale.
