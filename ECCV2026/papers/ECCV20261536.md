## Problem

Current spatial intelligence research relies on closed, under-specified data pipelines that release only fixed, preprocessed datasets while keeping generative engines proprietary, making controlled ablations, consistent scaling, and systematic study of design choices infeasible. This black-box ecosystem produces 'spatial myopia'—models with high benchmark scores but limited real-world versatility—and fragments progress into disconnected silos. The paper argues that advancing spatial intelligence requires open, reusable data infrastructure rather than static dataset releases.

## Input / Output

The engine consumes posed video sequences or multi-view images (from ScanNet, Matterport3D, ARKitScenes, ScanNet++, Hypersim, and in-the-wild web data) and produces a canonical scene representation: a 3D mesh with object-aligned oriented bounding boxes parameterized as (x, y, z, x_l, y_l, z_l, r, p, y) in a Z-up world coordinate system. From this, it extracts per-frame attributes (2D/3D boxes, SAM-refined masks, partial point clouds, semantic tags, and metric-scale flags) and synthesizes 3 million spatial QA pairs organized into five task categories with 19 sub-tasks. A metric flag on each object gates whether measurement-related QA is generated, preventing noisy supervision from non-real-scale boxes.

## Method

OpenSpatial is a 3D box-centric data engine built on three key designs: (1) OBBs serve as the fundamental primitive, providing viewpoint-invariant, metric-reasoning-capable anchors that are more compact than dense 3D reconstructions yet richer than 2D projections; (2) an automated 3D lifting pipeline (Gemini for per-view object recognition, SAM for instance masks, 3D instance association, and convex-hull fitting) extends data generation beyond curated datasets to in-the-wild web sources, achieving 80.2% precision after depth-based occupancy filtering; (3) scene-graph-driven QA synthesis programmatically enumerates objects, attributes, and inter-object relations to generate balanced supervision across Spatial Measurement, Spatial Relationship, Camera Perception, Multi-view Consistency, and Scene-Aware Reasoning, with single-view QA using visually marked anchors and multi-view QA sampling overlapping view pairs linked by shared 3D boxes. The pipeline applies frustum culling and volumetric occupancy validation to filter occluded or truncated objects before SAM-based mask refinement. Models are trained via supervised fine-tuning (single epoch, 32 GPUs, batch 128, AdamW at 5e-5) with a 1:1 mixture of OpenSpatial-3M spatial data and LLaVA-OneVision general data to preserve multimodal capabilities.

## Results

The best OpenSpatial model (OpenSpatial-Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves a 3D-Avg of 62.1, surpassing all open-source baselines and approaching the proprietary Gemini-2.5-Pro (62.4). On individual spatial benchmarks, it reaches 68.2 on BLINK, 59.8 on AllAngles, 41.9 on MMSI, and 94.0 on CVBench-3D. Relative to its Qwen3-VL-8B baseline, the model gains +10.3 on AllAngles, +13.8 on MMSI, and +6.0 on VSI, with an average relative improvement of ~19% across spatial tasks. The paper also reports that a 500k subset of OpenSpatial data outperforms VST (500k) and SenseNova-SI (800k) on most benchmarks while maintaining the lowest mean deviation (−2.5) across all evaluation axes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenSpatial-Qwen3-VL-8B (3D-Avg 62.1) outperforms all open-source spatial-intelligence baselines: VST-TB-SFT (57.9, +4.2), SenseNova-SI-1.1-Qwen3-VL-8B (55.5, +6.6), and SpaceR-7B (50.8, +11.3). The largest margins appear on MMSI (+8.6 over VST, +7.3 over SenseNova-SI) and AllAngles (+10.3 over VST, +12.5 over SenseNova-SI), indicating the data engine's particular strength in multi-image spatial consistency and multi-view perspective-taking. Against the proprietary Gemini-2.5-Pro, OpenSpatial-Qwen3-VL-8B trails slightly on 3D-Avg (62.1 vs 62.4) and BLINK (68.2 vs 70.6) but surpasses it on MMSI (41.9 vs 36.9) and CVBench-3D (94.0 vs 91.3). In the data-quality comparison (Table 2), a 500k OpenSpatial subset achieves the lowest mean deviation (−2.5) and beats VST (500k) on BLINK (64.2 vs 61.4) and AllAngles (53.9 vs 50.7), while VST retains a narrow edge on VSI (44.6 vs 43.0). The paper also shows that adding SenseNova-SI-800k as a complement to OpenSpatial-3M further boosts MMSI and VSI scores, confirming data-source complementarity rather than redundancy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-driven scaling paradigm for spatial VLMs pioneered by SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, VST, and SenseNova-SI, which demonstrated that large-scale synthetic spatial QA improves model performance. However, it departs fundamentally from the closed-pipeline, static-dataset-release model by open-sourcing the entire generation engine, enabling controlled ablations and reproducible scaling studies that prior works (e.g., VST, SenseNova-SI) did not permit. The 3D oriented bounding box as a viewpoint-invariant primitive, combined with scene-graph-driven programmatic QA enumeration, distinguishes OpenSpatial from prior approaches that relied on 2D projections or less structured synthesis. The 3D lifting pipeline for in-the-wild data extends the data source beyond curated indoor datasets (EmbodiedScan, ScanNet, Matterport3D) that underpin most prior spatial data efforts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By open-sourcing both the engine and the 3M-sample dataset, OpenSpatial addresses a critical reproducibility gap in spatial intelligence research where prior works released only fixed, preprocessed outputs from proprietary pipelines. The consistent 5–20% gains across four different VLM architectures (InternVL2.5/3, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL) and eight spatial benchmarks validate the data quality, while the modular ablations (box-centric vs. point-cloud, filtering on/off, task diversity) provide actionable guidance for future data design. Key limitations include an indoor-heavy data distribution that causes performance bottlenecks in outdoor and desktop scenarios, and observed diminishing returns in data scaling that suggest further gains require exponentially larger or more diverse corpora. Open questions include whether the 3D lifting pipeline (80.2% precision) can be improved to unlock truly large-scale in-the-wild spatial data, and whether the five-task taxonomy captures the full spectrum of spatial cognition needed for embodied AI.
