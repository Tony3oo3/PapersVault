## Problem

Existing bokeh editing tools consistently fail on complex geometries (reflections, transparent surfaces, fine structures) because their training data pipelines face a fundamental trilemma: physically captured data is geometrically accurate but non-scalable, depth-based synthesis is scalable but propagates geometric errors, and compositional synthesis avoids depth estimation but limits scene diversity. No prior approach simultaneously achieves geometric fidelity and large-scale data generation, leaving a gap in training supervision for robust focus-DoF editing.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB source image and two scalar offsets—Δκ (focal-plane shift) and Δa (aperture-radius change)—encoded via Fourier features and a lightweight MLP into a cross-attention conditioning vector. The output is a bokeh-edited image at the requested focus-DoF setting, supporting bidirectional editing (both sharpening and blurring, and shifting focus in either depth direction) from either all-in-focus or already-blurred inputs. Training supervision pairs are rendered on-the-fly from 25×25 light fields reconstructed from 960p DL3DV-10K video clips, with scene-aware baseline calibration (via RAFT optical flow) ensuring consistent parallax across diverse depth ranges.

## Method

The Vid2Bokeh pipeline reconstructs dense 25×25 light fields directly from casual videos using the LaCT feed-forward 3D model, bypassing depth estimation entirely and instead leveraging light field representation to naturally resolve reflections and intricate occlusions. A scene-aware calibration step adjusts sub-aperture baseline spacing so that mean optical flow (computed via RAFT) falls within 1–3 pixels, yielding physically plausible parallax across scenes with vastly different depth ranges. Bokeh images are then rendered on-the-fly via the standard light-field refocusing integral (Eq. 1) at randomly sampled focal-plane and aperture configurations, producing supervision pairs that densely cover the joint focus-DoF space. The editing model is an SDXL-based diffusion model fine-tuned with a spatial-concatenation design (inspired by InstructMove): the source image latent and a noise-initialized target latent are concatenated, and the U-Net denoises the target branch conditioned on the source latent and the (Δκ, Δa) conditioning vector. Training runs for 100K iterations on 8 A100 GPUs (~4 days), and the resulting 100K-scene dataset (10 TB) is the first to simultaneously provide scene diversity, optical density (25×25 angular resolution), and geometric fidelity for bokeh editing.

## Results

On the Vid2Bokeh test set for refocusing (Table 3), the proposed method achieves PSNR 29.62 (near→far) and 29.51 (far→near), SSIM 0.907/0.893, LPIPS 0.198/0.183, and DISTS 0.108/0.121, outperforming all baselines by a clear margin. For bokeh rendering on Vid2Bokeh (Table 4), the method reaches PSNR 28.05, SSIM 0.897, LPIPS 0.129, and DISTS 0.092 (averaged over 5 aperture settings), surpassing the strongest baseline Bokehlicious (PSNR 27.42, SSIM 0.842) by ~0.6 dB in PSNR and ~0.055 in SSIM. On the EBB! validation set, the method also leads with PSNR 24.02 and DISTS 0.085, confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated metric. Against DiffCamera [80] (the strongest direct refocusing baseline), Vid2Bokeh gains +1.3 dB PSNR and +0.043 SSIM in the near-to-far direction, with the gap widening to +0.67 dB in the far-to-near direction. Against the deblur-then-refocus pipelines (DRBNet+Dr.Bokeh, DRBNet+BokehMe, DRBNet+BokehDiff), the advantage is even larger: +1.6 dB PSNR over DRBNet+Dr.Bokeh and +0.8 dB over DRBNet+BokehDiff, reflecting that the two-stage approach accumulates errors. For bokeh rendering (Table 4), Vid2Bokeh beats Bokehlicious [60] by +0.63 dB PSNR and +0.055 SSIM, and BokehDiff [94] by +0.69 dB PSNR and +0.051 SSIM. On defocus blur removal (Table 5), the method matches DRBNet [56] on PSNR (26.70 vs 26.67) but substantially outperforms it on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.105 vs 0.137, DISTS 0.078 vs 0.091 on DPDD), and leads on Vid2Bokeh by +2.1 dB PSNR over DRBNet. The consistent wins across all tasks and both directions confirm that geometrically faithful light-field training data, rather than architectural novelty, drives the performance gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant depth-based synthesis paradigm for bokeh training data by replacing depth estimation with feed-forward 3D reconstruction (LaCT) to synthesize dense 25×25 light fields directly from casual videos. It builds on the SDXL diffusion architecture for the editing model and on the DL3DV-10K video dataset as its scene source, but the core novelty is the data acquisition pipeline itself: the observation that light field representation is inherently more robust than 2.5D depth maps for handling reflections, transparent surfaces, and fine occlusions. The paper positions itself against a trilemma in prior work—physical capture (faithful but non-scalable), compositional synthesis (depth-free but limited diversity), and depth-based synthesis (scalable but geometrically inaccurate)—and claims to be the first to simultaneously achieve depth-estimation-free generation and large-scale scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Vid2Bokeh Dataset (100K scenes, 25×25 angular resolution) is the first bokeh training resource to jointly provide scene diversity, optical density, and geometric fidelity, and the pipeline's low barrier (casual handheld video, <30s per scene on one A100) makes it practically accessible for scaling custom data. The central empirical claim—that geometric fidelity in training data, rather than model architecture, is the key to geometrically consistent editing on complex geometries—redirects attention from model design to data quality in this subfield. Limitations include the circular-aperture-only model, 960p resolution, lack of RAW-space highlight handling, and reliance on DL3DV-10K's scene distribution; open directions include per-pixel smooth monotonic transitions, 4K extension, and leveraging the angular information for occlusion separation or depth estimation.
