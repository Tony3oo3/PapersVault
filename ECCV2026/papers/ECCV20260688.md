## Problem

When VLMs are deployed in domains with substantial visual distribution shifts (e.g., exocentric→egocentric, RGB→depth, human→robot), existing adaptation strategies face a fundamental trade-off: freezing the vision encoder limits domain-specific visual learning, while fine-tuning it causes catastrophic forgetting of pretrained knowledge. The paper asks how a VLM can leverage domain-specific visual signals across the depth of a frozen vision encoder without disrupting its pretrained representations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video as a sequence of T per-timestep frames (each processed independently by the SigLIP encoder into N spatial patch tokens) paired with a text instruction or question. For video QA tasks, the output is an autoregressive text response; for robotic control, the output is an action trajectory comprising two 2D coordinates (pick/place positions) and two quaternions (rotation). In the depth-modality setting, input frames are single-channel depth maps generated by DepthAnythingV2 rather than RGB images, while language instructions remain unchanged.

## Method

VisCoP augments a frozen vision encoder with M=16 learnable visual probes that perform layer-wise cross-attention against intermediate encoder representations at every transformer layer, creating a parallel adaptation pathway that extracts domain-specific spatio-temporal features without modifying pretrained encoder weights. Each interaction module is a multi-head cross-attention (initialized from the corresponding layer's self-attention weights) where probes attend to all frames jointly, enabling spatio-temporal modeling that the intra-frame-only vision encoder cannot perform. After the final encoder layer, the updated probes are projected to the LLM embedding space via a dedicated connector and prepended to the standard visual embeddings, while the LLM is updated via LoRA (r=16) for video domains or fully for robotic control. This design confines parameter changes to a low-rank, probe-defined visual subspace, directing gradient flow away from the frozen encoder and toward the probe pathway, thereby learning new domain-specific cues while preserving source-domain representations.

## Results

On the egocentric video adaptation task, VisCoP achieves the highest target-domain average accuracy of 73.96% (Δ_target = +3.53 over the base VLM) while simultaneously improving source-domain retention to 76.19% (Δ_source = +1.77). For the more challenging RGB→depth modality shift, VisCoP yields a Δ_target of +19.27 with Δ_source of +1.84, whereas full vision-encoder fine-tuning causes severe forgetting (Δ_source = −3.87). In the cross-task robot control setting, VisCoP reaches 67.82% average success rate on VIMA-Bench (Δ_target = +67.82 from a 0% base) while retaining the most source-domain knowledge (Δ_source = −4.58 vs. −8.87 for the strongest fine-tuning baseline). VisCoP adds only 2% parameters and 0.013 s of visual-feature-extraction latency over the base VLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisCoP outperforms Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) [19] by +2.60 points on target-domain accuracy (73.96 vs. 71.36) and by +1.22 points on source-domain retention (76.19 vs. 74.97), with a substantially larger Δ_target (+3.53 vs. +0.93). Against Model Tailor [58], which fuses fine-tuned LLM parameters back into the base model, VisCoP leads by +3.69 on target and +0.90 on source, while Model Tailor actually degrades target performance below the base VLM (Δ_target = −0.16). Both baselines rely on recombining or post-hoc merging of pretrained representations without learning new visual cues from intermediate encoder layers, which the paper identifies as the key limitation. VisCoP's advantage is most pronounced under larger distribution shifts: on the depth-modality task it gains +19.27 on target while preserving source (Δ_source = +1.84), whereas the strongest fine-tuning baseline (VE + connector) suffers Δ_source = −3.87. On robot control, VisCoP achieves 67.82% average success versus 65.13% for the best fine-tuning strategy, while retaining 4.58 points more source-domain accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisCoP builds on the VideoLLaMA3 VLM architecture (SigLIP encoder + Qwen 2.5 LLM + MLP connector) and departs from the dominant paradigm of domain adaptation in VLMs, which either freezes the vision encoder and trains only lightweight connectors (limiting visual specialization) or fine-tunes the encoder (causing catastrophic forgetting). It also distinguishes itself from Q-Former and Perceiver Resampler, which compress final-layer visual tokens into a fixed set of queries, and from visual prompt tuning, which conditions on fixed encoder representations without learning new visual features. The key conceptual departure is introducing learnable probes that perform layer-wise cross-attention with intermediate encoder representations, creating a parallel adaptation pathway that extracts domain-specific signals without modifying pretrained encoder parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VisCoP addresses a fundamental and practically important trade-off in deploying VLMs to novel domains: the tension between learning domain-specific visual representations and retaining broad pretrained knowledge. The consistent gains across three qualitatively different domain shifts (viewpoint, modality, task) and the minimal overhead (2% more parameters, lower inference latency) make this a practical technique likely to be adopted in applied VLM pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other base VLM architectures, how it behaves under even more extreme distribution shifts, and whether the probe mechanism can be combined with other parameter-efficient methods for multi-domain or continual adaptation scenarios.
