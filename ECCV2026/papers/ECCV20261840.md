## Problem

Existing VLM distillation methods require teacher and student to share identical token types—vocabulary size, token splits, and token-index ordering—because they rely on token-level metrics such as KL divergence. When VLMs employ different tokenizers or image-embedding strategies, output token counts mismatch and indices no longer map to equivalent meanings, rendering standard distillation infeasible. This constraint severely limits viable teacher-student pairs, even within the same model family, and blocks the use of the most powerful large VLMs as teachers for smaller students.

## Input / Output

During training, the same image and text prompt are fed into both the large (teacher) and small (student) VLMs, each producing hidden feature sequences from its VLM-body. The Recalibrator consumes the small VLM's question features concatenated with the large VLM's answer features, and its output is scored by the large VLM's language head. At inference, both the Recalibrator and the large VLM are entirely removed, so the small VLM generates its text answer using its original architecture with zero additional computational overhead.

## Method

GenRecal introduces a Recalibrator—a lightweight module of two transformer decoder blocks (configured to match the student's decoder) sandwiched between two linear projection layers (Proj-pre and Proj-post) that bridge hidden-dimension mismatches between teacher and student. The Recalibrator is trained with an autoregressive loss that predicts the large VLM's answer token index given the small VLM's question features, combined with a KL divergence loss and a regularization term that prevents the Recalibrator's output from drifting away from the large VLM's own features. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) align features by training only the Recalibrator with both VLMs frozen, (2) distill knowledge by additionally training the small VLM's body under the same losses plus the student's own autoregressive loss, and (3) fine-tune the student via SFT after discarding the Recalibrator. The key insight is that projecting student features into the teacher's high-capacity latent space and scoring them with the teacher's head avoids the token-correspondence problem entirely, enabling distillation across arbitrary tokenizer and image-embedding combinations without any logit-space matching, padding, or transport cost.

## Results

The best GenRecal configuration (InternVL2.5-8B student distilled from InternVL2.5-78B teacher) achieves 89.5 on MMB, 73.2 on MM-Vet, 68.1 on MMMU, 74.9 on MathVista, and 81.4 on RealWorldQA, surpassing GPT-4o (83.4 MMB, 69.1 MM-Vet, 69.1 MMMU, 63.8 MathVista, 75.4 RWQA) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (82.6 MMB, 70.1 MM-Vet, 68.3 MMMU) on most benchmarks. The 7B student (Qwen2-VL-7B-GenRecal) reaches 93.9 on AI2D and 95.3 on ChartQA, exceeding the 72B teacher (Qwen2-VL-72B: 88.1 AI2D, 88.3 ChartQA). Across all student sizes (1B–8B), GenRecal consistently improves over the base model by 5–12 points on MMMU and 8–15 points on MathVista, with the 8B model matching or exceeding the 78B teacher on MMB (89.5 vs 88.3) and MathVista (74.9 vs 72.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against closed-source VLMs, the 8B GenRecal model (InternVL2.5-8B distilled from InternVL2.5-78B) outperforms GPT-4o on MMB (89.5 vs 83.4), MM-Vet (73.2 vs 69.1), MathVista (74.9 vs 63.8), and RWQA (81.4 vs 75.4), but trails on MMMU-Pro (48.8 vs 51.9) and BLINK (65.3 vs 68.0). It also surpasses Claude-3.5-Sonnet on MMB (89.5 vs 82.6), MMMU (68.1 vs 68.3, near-tie), MathVista (74.9 vs 67.7), and RWQA (81.4 vs 60.1), and beats Gemini-1.5-Pro by 15.6 points on MMB and 11.2 on MMMU. Among open-source 72B+ teachers, GenRecal-8B matches or exceeds InternVL2.5-78B on MMB (89.5 vs 88.3) and MathVista (74.9 vs 72.3), and surpasses Qwen2-VL-72B on MMB (89.5 vs 86.5) and AI2D (93.0 vs 88.1). Against traditional distillation baselines with the same-token-type teacher (Qwen2-VL-72B→7B), GenRecal achieves 87.8/69.5/67.8/64.2 on MMB/MathVista/MM-Vet/MMMU, beating LLaVA-KD (85.0/61.8/65.9/58.2), DistiLLM (84.8/61.5/65.3/57.9), and MiniLLM (84.4/61.3/65.1/57.4) by 2.8–7.7 points. Against cross-tokenizer methods ULD and MOT, GenRecal achieves 76.4 average vs 69.4 and 70.8 respectively (Table 7), while using comparable FLOPs (298.3×10¹² vs 296.1×10¹²) and shorter total training time (18.8h vs 22.2h/25.0h). Across all 20 compared baselines, GenRecal wins on the majority of benchmarks; the only consistent losses are on MMMU-Pro and BLINK versus GPT-4o, and on MM-Vet-v2 versus Claude-3.5-Sonnet (67.2 vs 71.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM knowledge distillation line (LLaVA-KD, LLaVA-MoD, Align-KD, MoVE-KD) by removing the assumption that teacher and student must share identical token types (vocabulary size, token splits, index ordering). Conceptually, it repurposes the linear word-embedding alignment idea from multilingual NLP (Conneau et al., Mikolov et al., Smith et al.) but applies it to post-body hidden states via a shallow transformer decoder (the Recalibrator) rather than input embeddings. Unlike recent cross-tokenizer LLM distillation methods (ULD, MOT) that match logit-space probability mass via Wasserstein distance or optimal transport, GenRecal aligns features before the language head so the teacher head reads student features directly, avoiding token correspondence, padding, and transport cost entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making VLM distillation token-type-agnostic, GenRecal unlocks a much larger space of viable teacher-student pairs, which is critical for on-device deployment where the best available teacher may use a different tokenizer than the target student. The Recalibrator is discarded at inference, so the distilled model incurs zero additional cost. Open questions include whether the approach extends to intermediate-layer distillation for fine-grained sequential knowledge, multi-teacher settings, and whether the open-weight teacher requirement can be relaxed. A key limitation is that the method requires access to the teacher's hidden features during training, restricting it to open-weight teachers.
