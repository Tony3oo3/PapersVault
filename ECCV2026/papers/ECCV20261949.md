## Problem

Existing uncertainty estimation methods for vision-language models fail to capture visual ambiguity: entropy-based estimators like Semantic Entropy underestimate uncertainty when overconfident visual embeddings suppress output diversity under stochastic decoding, distance-based methods like SNNE overestimate uncertainty due to wording variation among semantically equivalent answers, and text-perturbation approaches reflect prompt sensitivity rather than genuine visual ambiguity. The paper asks whether VLMs can recognize visual ambiguity and proposes a principled way to estimate uncertainty that isolates the visual contribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261949/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a fixed text question as input. The image is perturbed with Gaussian noise (σ=20) to produce M=10 local visual variants, while the question remains unchanged across all variants. The output is a single scalar uncertainty score computed as the mass-weighted expected pairwise distance among semantic prototype embeddings, where higher values indicate greater likelihood that the model's answer is incorrect.

## Method

Visual Semantic Entropy (VSE) combines two components: (1) visual perturbation, which adds fixed-scale Gaussian noise to the input image to generate M local visual variants while keeping the text query fixed, thereby probing prediction instability that stochastic decoding alone cannot reveal under confident visual embeddings; and (2) Prototype Semantic Aggregation (ProtoSem), which clusters the M generated answers using DeBERTa-v2-xlarge-mnli embeddings with hierarchical clustering, selects a representative prototype per cluster (the answer minimizing average intra-cluster distance), and computes uncertainty as the expected pairwise distance between two independently drawn prototypes weighted by their empirical cluster masses. This design avoids modality confounding from textual perturbations, eliminates intra-cluster wording inflation, and ensures uncertainty reflects only genuine semantic disagreement across visually distinct interpretations.

## Results

On AOKVQA, VSE achieves the best AUC across all five VLMs: 0.783 (Qwen2.5-VL), 0.778 (Gemma3), 0.724 (LLaVA-NeXT), 0.798 (Qwen3-VL), and 0.792 (Intern3.5-VL), outperforming the strongest baseline (KLE or SE) by 0.9–7.2% AUC depending on the model. On the visually adversarial VILP dataset, VSE reaches 0.650 AUC with Qwen2.5-VL versus 0.535 for the best baseline (SE), a 11.5-point gap. On VLM-are-biased, VSE attains 0.826 AUC (Qwen2.5-VL) and 0.776 (Gemma3), surpassing VL-Uncertainty by 4.3 and 1.8 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSE outperforms all eight baseline categories on AOKVQA across every model. Among entropy-based methods, VSE beats KLE (the strongest prior entropy method) by 0.9% on Qwen2.5-VL (0.783 vs. 0.774) and by 2.6% on Qwen3-VL (0.798 vs. 0.772), and surpasses VL-Uncertainty by up to 9.2% AUC (0.724 vs. 0.616 on LLaVA-NeXT). Consistency-based methods (C&U, SelfCheckGPT) trail VSE by 5–12% AUC, with C&U reaching at most 0.734 on Intern3.5-VL versus VSE's 0.792. Logit-based and verbalized methods are consistently weakest, with AUC scores generally below 0.70. On the adversarial VILP dataset, the gap widens dramatically: VSE achieves 0.650 AUC (Qwen2.5-VL) while SE, C&U, and VL-U all fall below 0.54, confirming that methods relying on text perturbation or decoding stochasticity fail under visual ambiguity. On MMVet (a reasoning-focused benchmark with less visual ambiguity), VSE still leads (0.781 vs. 0.758 for SNNE on Qwen2.5-VL), though the margin narrows, suggesting that sampling-based methods are more competitive when visual ambiguity is less dominant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the entropy-based uncertainty estimation lineage from Semantic Entropy (SE) and its semantic-distance variants (SNNE, KLE) by identifying three specific failure modes in the VLM setting: confident visual embeddings suppress decoding diversity, wording variation inflates pairwise distances, and textual perturbations dominate output variability under joint image-text perturbation. It departs from VL-Uncertainty and C&U, which perturb both image and text, by restricting perturbation to the visual modality only, and from SNNE's raw pairwise aggregation by introducing prototype-based semantic clustering that absorbs intra-cluster wording variation. The method thus sits at the intersection of perturbation-based probing and semantic-level aggregation, combining elements of both families into a unified estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that text perturbations dominate output variability (text purity consistently exceeds image purity across four models) is practically important: it means prior joint-perturbation methods were largely measuring prompt sensitivity rather than visual ambiguity, a confound that practitioners deploying VLMs in safety-critical settings would want to avoid. VSE's image-only perturbation with prototype aggregation offers a cleaner signal, though it inherits the computational cost of multi-sample generation and depends on the quality of the semantic distance model for clustering. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D inputs, whether the fixed perturbation scale (σ=20 Gaussian noise) is optimal across architectures, and how VSE behaves under distribution shift beyond the evaluated VQA benchmarks.
