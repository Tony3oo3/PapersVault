## Problem

Existing DPO-based preference optimization for diffusion models enforces a linear supervision direction from the noisy latent directly to the clean endpoint, ignoring the intrinsically curved generative trajectory in latent space. This 'Linear Shortcut Fallacy' forces intermediate states off the pretrained data manifold, accumulating off-trajectory drift that degrades structural integrity and semantic fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261997/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (SDXL) using paired preference data (winner/loser image pairs with shared text prompts) from Pick-a-Pic v2. During training, both images in each pair undergo DDIM inversion to reconstruct their full generation trajectories, from which curvature-adaptive anchors are extracted. The fine-tuned model takes a text prompt and generates a 1024×1024 RGB image, with the key difference being that the denoising objective targets trajectory-consistent intermediate states rather than the raw clean endpoint.

## Method

TCPO introduces two mechanisms to rectify the linear shortcut. First, Curvature-Adaptive Sampling (CAS) reconstructs the generation trajectory via DDIM inversion, computes a scale-invariant curvature proxy κᵢ as the cosine distance between consecutive normalized velocity fields, and dynamically selects the furthest intermediate latent whose accumulated curvature stays within a budget η = (1/α)·Σκⱼ, yielding a geometric anchor on the manifold. Second, Trajectory-Rectified Fusion (TRF) fuses the implicit noise vector pointing to this geometric anchor with the noise vector pointing to the clean preference target (semantic anchor) via Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) with coefficient γ, producing a rectified target noise that pulls the aggressive semantic update back onto the valid data manifold. The final loss follows the standard DPO-Diff formulation but replaces the endpoint-directed target noise with the TRF-rectified noise, reweighting the denoising objective by the preference margin while preserving trajectory consistency.

## Results

On the Pick-a-Pic v2 test set, TCPO-finetuned SDXL achieves HPS 28.87, PickScore 22.80, and MPS 12.22, outperforming DPO (28.59, 22.64, 11.99) and InPO (28.52, 22.58, 11.92). On HPD v2, TCPO reaches the highest scores across all preference metrics: HPS 29.50, PickScore 23.40, and MPS 14.90. On PartiPrompts, TCPO attains the best mean score on all four evaluators (Aesthetic 5.90, PickScore 23.16, HPS 29.18, MPS 11.85). Training efficiency is 4.31× faster than DPO (~180 vs ~776 GPU hours) and 1.23× faster than InPO (~180 vs ~221 GPU hours) on A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCPO consistently outperforms DPO and InPO on all three preference metrics (PickScore, HPS, MPS) across all four evaluation datasets; for example, on Pick-a-Pic v2 it gains +0.28 HPS over DPO and +0.22 PickScore over InPO. Against MaPO, TCPO wins decisively on HPS, PickScore, and MPS on every dataset (e.g., +0.34 HPS on HPD v2, +0.33 MPS on Pick-a-Pic v2), but MaPO edges out TCPO on the Aesthetic score on DrawBench (5.74 vs 5.73), HPD v2 (6.24 vs 6.19), and Pick-a-Pic v2 (6.20 vs 6.07), suggesting MaPO optimizes raw visual appeal at the expense of text-image alignment. On PartiPrompts, TCPO achieves the highest mean on all four metrics simultaneously, including a 0.28 HPS margin over the next-best DPO. In terms of training efficiency, TCPO requires ~180 A100 GPU hours versus ~776 for DPO (4.31× speedup) and ~221 for InPO (1.23× speedup), while simultaneously delivering the best preference scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCPO builds directly on the Diffusion-DPO framework [40] and the DDIM inversion machinery popularized by InPO [20], but departs from both by identifying a geometric mismatch they share: endpoint-directed supervision implicitly assumes a linear (chord) path through a curved generative trajectory. The Curvature-Adaptive Sampling component repurposes DDIM inversion not merely for reparameterization (as InPO does) but to extract a local curvature signal that dynamically selects an on-manifold anchor. The Trajectory-Rectified Fusion step borrows SLERP from the quaternion-rotation literature [33] and applies it to the noise-prediction space, grounding the interpolation in the concentration-of-measure geometry of high-dimensional Gaussians rather than treating noise vectors as ordinary Euclidean points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Linear Shortcut Fallacy' framing gives practitioners a concrete geometric diagnosis for why naive DPO fine-tuning can degrade structural fidelity, and the 4.31× training speedup over DPO makes preference alignment substantially more accessible. However, the quality gains over the strongest baseline (InPO) are modest across most metrics, and the method's reliance on a 5-step DDIM inversion per training pair introduces a fixed overhead that may not scale favorably to longer inversion schedules or flow-matching backbones. Open questions include whether the curvature-budget formulation transfers cleanly to flow-matching models (the authors report SD3.5 results only in supplementary material) and whether the SLERP fusion remains beneficial when the geometric and semantic anchors are already well-aligned in low-curvature regimes.
