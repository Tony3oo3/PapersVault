## Problem

Surface normal estimation for LiDAR point clouds remains challenging due to their sparse, non-uniform distribution, which makes local-patch-based methods unreliable. Existing single-step methods for LiDAR (e.g., LiSu) rely on deep stacks of convolutional and attention layers to achieve wide receptive fields, resulting in high parameter counts and inference costs, while their group-level attention without explicit neighbor search limits local structural modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262498/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw LiDAR point cloud of ~100k 3D coordinates per frame (64-beam sensor, 10 Hz, with 0.02 m Gaussian noise) as input and produces a per-point unit surface normal vector as output. No local patches are extracted as a pre-processing step; instead, the full point cloud is serialized into a 1-D sequence via Hilbert curve space-filling curve ordering before being fed into the network. The model is trained on the LiSu dataset (CARLA-simulated urban/rural scenes) and evaluated on both LiSu and real-world Waymo Open Dataset point clouds.

## Method

ESNE is a U-Net architecture whose encoder-decoder blocks each combine a bidirectional State Space Model (SSM) module for long-range global context and a Sequential Local Attention (SLA) module for local structure. The key efficiency insight is replacing spatial k-NN search with sequential k-NN, which identifies neighbors by index offsets in the space-filling-curve-ordered sequence, reducing neighbor lookup to O(k) per point. A Variability-guided Feature Refinement (VFR) module, placed at the stage preceding the bottleneck, computes the standard deviation of SLA attention weights as a scalar structural-variability signal and uses it to conditionally refine feature vectors via an MLP with residual connection. The network is trained end-to-end with a composite loss combining weighted MAE, spatial and temporal graph total variation regularization, and an Eikonal unit-norm constraint, achieving 4.9M parameters and 41 ms inference time versus 46.2M parameters and 80 ms for LiSu.

## Results

On the LiSu dataset, ESNE achieves a mean angular error of 4.17° and median of 0.20°, improving mean accuracy by over 33.4% compared to the previous state-of-the-art LiSu (6.26°). Inference time is 0.04 s per frame, more than 2× faster than LiSu (0.08 s) and roughly 188× faster than MSECNet (7.53 s). The model also reduces parameters by 89.4% (4.9M vs. 46.2M) and peak memory by 41% (0.59 GB vs. 1.01 GB) relative to LiSu. On the downstream surface reconstruction task (NKSR), ESNE's estimated normals yield CD-L1 of 0.0357 and F-Score of 0.934 on CARLA (Original), closest to the GT-normal upper bound (0.0340 / 0.941).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESNE outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor LiSu [47] (the only other method designed for full-frame LiDAR without local patches), ESNE reduces mean angular error from 6.26° to 4.17° (−33.4%), halves the median from 0.40° to 0.20°, and doubles inference speed (0.04 s vs. 0.08 s) while using 89.4% fewer parameters. Among patch-based learning methods, MSECNet [72] is the strongest on RMSE (15.01°) but ESNE still wins on mean (4.17° vs. 6.65°), median (0.20° vs. 1.55°), and is ~188× faster (0.04 s vs. 7.53 s). SHS-Net [34] achieves a competitive median (0.83°) but lags ESNE on mean (7.88° vs. 4.17°) and is 570× slower. Traditional geometric methods (PCA, Jet) are far inferior in accuracy (mean errors of 72.75° and 38.41° respectively), confirming the necessity of learned approaches for sparse LiDAR. On the surface reconstruction downstream task, ESNE's normals produce reconstruction quality (CD-L1 0.0357, FS 0.934) that is closest to the GT-normal upper bound, marginally ahead of LiSu (0.0362 / 0.932) and clearly ahead of MSECNet (0.0374 / 0.927).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mamba state space model (originally for 1D sequences) for 3D point cloud processing by serializing points along space-filling curves (Hilbert curves), building on the serialization strategy from Point Transformer V3. It departs from the prior LiDAR-specific method LiSu, which relies on stacked convolutional and attention layers within local point groups, by replacing global attention with linear-complexity SSM and introducing an explicit sequential k-NN-based local attention that exploits index proximity in the serialized order. The variability-guided feature refinement module, which uses the standard deviation of attention weights as a structural heterogeneity signal, is a novel addition not present in prior normal estimation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 33.4% mean accuracy improvement combined with 2× inference speedup and 89.4% parameter reduction over the only prior LiDAR-specific method (LiSu) makes real-time surface normal estimation on large-scale LiDAR scans practically viable for autonomous driving and robotics applications. The sequential k-NN approach demonstrates that approximate spatial neighbors from 1D index proximity can substitute for expensive exact spatial searches with only modest accuracy loss (12.4% degradation on high-curvature regions), opening a path to scalable local attention for large point clouds. Open questions include whether the space-filling curve serialization generalizes to point clouds with very non-uniform density or multi-scale structures, and whether the SSM's fixed-order sequence assumption captures all relevant global context in 3D scenes.
