## Problem

Existing medical generative models are fragmented across modalities, relying on modality-specific components or disconnected external models that prevent strict semantic alignment between imaging and clinical text. No unified medical discrete diffusion model existed that could simultaneously support image generation, report generation, and joint image-report pair synthesis within a single framework without task-specific architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260144/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates on a shared discrete token space where medical images (512×512) are quantized via a VQGAN encoder into VQ-VAE codebook tokens and clinical reports are tokenized with the LLaMA tokenizer (max 256 tokens), both diffused over a unified vocabulary. For conditional generation, one modality is retained while the other is initialized as [MASK] tokens; for unconditional or joint generation, the full sequence starts as [MASK]. Inference follows the MaskGIT strategy, progressively refining masked tokens in parallel batches to produce the output modality or paired image-report output.

## Method

MeDiM uses a pretrained MLLM (Liquid) as the denoising backbone within an absorbing-state discrete diffusion process, leveraging the MLLM's cross-modal distribution-alignment priors from large-scale vision-language pretraining to guide unified medical generation. Three key adaptations bridge the gap between autoregressive MLLM training and bidirectional diffusion denoising: (1) removing the causal attention mask to enable fully bidirectional information flow between image and text tokens, (2) injecting continuous timestep embeddings so the backbone is aware of the current diffusion stage, and (3) applying AdaLN/AdaLN-Zero to modulate intermediate layers with timestep-conditioned normalization parameters. The forward process replaces tokens with a [MASK] absorbing state via a categorical transition matrix, and the reverse process is trained with a weighted negative log-likelihood objective over the transition schedule. Trained for 1M steps on 8 A100 GPUs (160 GPU hours) with a warmup cosine annealing schedule starting at 1e-5, the model requires no modality-specific components and generalizes across chest X-ray and pathology domains.

## Results

On medical image generation, MeDiM achieves state-of-the-art FID of 16.60 on MIMIC-CXR (chest X-ray) and 24.19 on PathGen (pathology), substantially outperforming the best expert baselines (Diff-CXR at 19.50 FID and PixCell at 98.54 FID, respectively). For medical report generation, MeDiM attains 0.2650 METEOR on MIMIC-CXR and 0.2580 METEOR on PathGen, leading all baselines on both benchmarks. Jointly generated image-report pairs further improve downstream VLM report generation by +6.43% BLEU-1, +18.57% BLEU-2, +31.58% BLEU-3, and +4.80% METEOR on PathGen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On chest X-ray image generation (Table 1), MeDiM (16.60 FID) outperforms the best expert model Diff-CXR (19.50 FID) by 3.9 FID points, and dominates all MLLM baselines (Liquid 156.09, MMaDA 134.01) and DDM (UniDisc 82.54) by wide margins. On pathology image generation (Table 2), the gap is even more dramatic: MeDiM (24.19 FID) beats the best expert PixCell (98.54 FID) by over 74 FID points and the best MLLM MMaDA (155.76 FID) by 131.57 FID points. For report generation on MIMIC-CXR (Table 3), MeDiM leads on BLEU-1/2/3 and METEOR (0.328/0.185/0.109/0.265) over R2Gen (0.305/0.179/0.104/0.233), though R2Gen retains an advantage on ROUGE-L (0.395 vs. 0.297). On PathGen, MeDiM achieves the best BLEU-1 (0.185) and METEOR (0.258), but MMaDA edges ahead on BLEU-2 (0.108 vs. 0.084) and BLEU-3 (0.052 vs. 0.037), while R2Gen leads on ROUGE-L (0.278 vs. 0.226). Overall, MeDiM is the only method that simultaneously supports image generation, report generation, and joint image-report pair generation without modality-specific components, and it achieves the strongest or near-strongest results across all three tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeDiM extends the discrete diffusion framework (Austin et al. 2021; Sahoo et al. 2024) and MaskGIT-style inference (Chang et al. 2022) into the medical domain, where prior unified generation models (Liquid, MMaDA, UniDisc) have only been applied to natural images. It departs from medical-specific generators (PairAug, MedM2G, Diff-CXR, PathLDM) that rely on modality-specific architectures by using a single MLLM backbone for all modalities. The key conceptual departure from MMaDA is that MeDiM adapts general-purpose autoregressive MLLMs (e.g., Liquid) to discrete diffusion via causal-mask removal and timestep embedding injection, rather than being restricted to diffusion-native MLLM backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeDiM demonstrates that a single unified model can handle medical image generation, report generation, and joint image-report synthesis without modality-specific components, which is a practical step toward generalist medical AI agents. The finding that generated image-report pairs improve downstream VLM performance (+31.58% BLEU-3 on PathGen) suggests synthetic data from unified generators can partially address the chronic data scarcity in medical imaging. Open questions include whether this approach scales to 3D volumetric imaging (CT, MRI), integrates structured EHR data, and generalizes beyond the two 2D modalities (chest X-ray, pathology) evaluated here; clinical validation and multi-center evaluation remain absent.
