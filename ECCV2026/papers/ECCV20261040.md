## Problem

Generating high-quality 3D characters from single images remains difficult due to complex articulated body poses, self-occlusion, and the need for consistent novel view synthesis across a full viewing orbit. Existing multi-view diffusion methods suffer from the Janus problem and slow optimization, while recent video diffusion approaches for NVS have not been specifically tailored to handle pose canonicalization, controllable viewpoints, and multi-view conditioning in the demanding character generation setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261040/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 1–4 character reference images in arbitrary poses with random backgrounds, plus a user-specified target camera pose encoded as Plücker embeddings (F×H×W×6). Reference images are encoded individually by a frozen 3D causal VAE (each treated as a single-frame video), zero-padded leftwards along the temporal axis to prevent the backbone from treating them as initial frames for image-to-video continuation, then channel-concatenated with the noisy latent. The output is a 1024×1024 orbital rotation video depicting the character in a canonical A/T pose, with the observation starting from the user-defined camera position.

## Method

RCM is built on the Wan 2.2 video diffusion model (DiT backbone + 3D causal VAE) formulated under flow matching, with two key architectural additions: (1) a lightweight CNN-based Camera Encoder that processes Plücker embeddings into spatially-aligned camera latents, element-wise summed with the input latent to provide geometry-aware viewpoint conditioning, and (2) a channel-wise concatenation scheme for reference image latents that suppresses background distractions while preserving character identity. Training follows a progressive three-stage strategy: Stage I learns pose canonicalization (arbitrary pose → A/T pose, no camera conditioning), Stage II introduces static camera pose conditioning for controllable viewpoints, and Stage III learns dynamic orbital rotation with per-frame varying camera poses. The model is fine-tuned end-to-end at 1024×1024 resolution on ~120K rendered videos from ~46K proprietary character models, with random background compositing to decouple character from environment.

## Results

On the RCM-Hard benchmark, RCM achieves PSNR 19.41, SSIM 0.89, Chamfer Distance 0.0909, and FID 25.31, outperforming the strongest baseline Wan 2.2 (PSNR 16.78, SSIM 0.84, CD 0.1616, FID 80.87) by a wide margin on all four metrics. In the user study on RCM-Wild, RCM scores 4.25/5 overall, surpassing the next-best method Wan 2.2 (3.03/5) and CharacterGen (2.64/5) across all five subjective criteria including pose canonicalization (4.36 vs. 2.26 for Wan 2.2). The FID gap is particularly striking: RCM's 25.31 is roughly 3× lower than Wan 2.2's 80.87 and nearly 18× lower than CharacterGen's 458.06.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCM outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric. Against multi-view diffusion methods (SyncDreamer, EpiDiff, SV3D, Hi3D, AR-1-to-3), RCM achieves substantially lower Chamfer Distance (0.0909 vs. 0.0979–0.1318) and dramatically lower FID (25.31 vs. 177–190), indicating far superior geometric and perceptual fidelity. CharacterGen, the only prior method explicitly designed for pose canonicalization, is the weakest baseline on RCM-Hard (PSNR 9.31, FID 458.06), struggling with identity preservation on complex poses. Among video diffusion baselines, Wan 2.2 is the closest competitor (PSNR 16.78, FID 80.87) but still trails RCM by 2.63 dB in PSNR and 3.2× in FID; Wan 2.1 performs poorly (PSNR 7.33, SSIM 0.64), failing to produce coherent orbital rotations. The three camera-control models (InfCam, ReCamMaster, TrajectoryCrafter) are compared qualitatively and all fail to execute a full 360° orbital rotation, suffering identity drift and geometric collapse at side/back views, whereas RCM maintains consistent character identity throughout the complete orbit.

## Relation to Prior Work

RCM builds directly on the Wan 2.2 video diffusion backbone and the flow matching formulation, repurposing a general-purpose video generator for the specific task of character novel view synthesis. Architecturally, it departs from prior multi-view diffusion methods (SyncDreamer, CharacterGen, Zero123++) by leveraging temporal consistency from video diffusion rather than spatial cross-view attention, and introduces a lightweight CNN-based Camera Encoder that processes Plücker embeddings via element-wise summation—a design choice validated against cross-attention and IP-Adapter alternatives. The progressive three-stage training strategy (pose canonicalization → viewpoint initialization → character rotation) is a key departure from prior work that typically trains all objectives jointly, and the multi-view conditioning (up to 4 reference images) extends the single-image paradigm common in earlier character generation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The open-sourcing of RCM-Wild and RCM-Hard benchmarks addresses a genuine gap in character generation evaluation, providing both perceptual (human study) and objective (PSNR/SSIM/CD/FID) protocols that the community can adopt. The demonstrated 3× FID improvement over the unmodified Wan 2.2 base model (25.31 vs. 80.87) and the clean ablation isolating each training stage's contribution make the progressive strategy a reusable recipe for other video-diffusion-based 3D tasks. Key limitations remain: RCM produces only static A/T-pose rotations (no dynamic animation), relies on ~46K proprietary character models for training, and the 3D reconstruction quality is bounded by the downstream Hunyuan3D 2.0 pipeline. Extending to articulated motion and reducing data requirements are natural next steps.
