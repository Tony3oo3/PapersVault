## Problem

Existing continual deepfake detection frameworks are designed under image-level assumptions and treat spatial and temporal forensic cues as a single entangled representation, which is inadequate for video deepfakes where spatial artifacts (e.g., blending boundaries) and temporal inconsistencies (e.g., flickering) carry complementary but distinct evidence. This leads to unbalanced adaptation and severe catastrophic forgetting when detectors are sequentially updated on new forgery types, as different deepfake video paradigms rely on different combinations of spatial and temporal cues.

## Input / Output

The model processes 32-frame video clips at 224×224 spatial resolution through a 3D ResNet-18 backbone, with up to 270 frames extracted per video during training and up to 110 frames during evaluation. During continual updates, the model also consumes a replay memory of 200 clips from prior tasks. Video-level real/fake scores are computed by averaging clip-level logits, and performance is reported as video-level AUC across sequential tasks.

## Method

MSFD decomposes intermediate feature maps from multiple backbone layers into three complementary frequency spectra—1D temporal, 2D spatial, and 3D spatiotemporal—via FFT, enabling independent preservation of each modality during sequential model updates. A Modality-Specific Adaptor (MSA) applies learnable modality-wise reweighting (residual spectral modulation) and Gumbel-Softmax-based radial masking to select discriminative frequency bands per modality, computing a frequency-domain distillation loss that aligns the current model's spectra with the frozen previous model's. A Cross-Modality Decorrelation Loss (CDL) further penalizes Pearson correlation between the spatiotemporal spectrum and its spatial/temporal projections, encouraging the ST branch to encode joint space-time dynamics orthogonal to single-modality cues. The total objective combines binary cross-entropy, logit-level distillation on replay samples, the frequency-domain distillation loss, and the CDL, all optimized jointly per task.

## Results

Under Protocol 1 (Dataset-Incremental across FF++, DFD, CDF, DFDCp, FFIW, KoDF), MSFD achieves the highest overall AUC of 93.02% with only 2.29% average forgetting, outperforming the strongest baseline IDER (91.10% AUC, 2.97% forgetting) by ~1.9 points in AUC. Under Protocol 2 (Fake-Incremental on AIGVDBench), MSFD reaches 96.67% AUC_all, surpassing iCaRL-FC (94.93%) by 1.7 points, with the largest margin on I2V (91.47% vs. 87.99%). Under Protocol 3 (Few-shot Incremental), MSFD attains 83.65% Average Accuracy with negative forgetting (AF = −1.25), indicating positive backward transfer, while the best baseline IDER achieves 83.47% AA with AF = −2.10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSFD outperforms all 14 baselines on AUC_all across all three protocols. In Protocol 1, the closest competitor is IDER (91.10% AUC_all, 2.97% F_avg), which MSFD surpasses by 1.92 points in AUC while also reducing forgetting by 0.68 points; DCE achieves near-zero forgetting (0.07%) but sacrifices 4.04 points in AUC_all (88.98%), illustrating a stability–plasticity trade-off that MSFD avoids. Among continual deepfake detection methods, SURLID (87.61%) and DFIL (87.88%) lag MSFD by 5.4 and 5.1 points respectively, confirming that image-level single-representation distillation is insufficient for video. In Protocol 2, MSFD leads iCaRL-FC by 1.74 points (96.67 vs. 94.93) and SURLID by 3.21 points, with the largest gap on I2V (91.47 vs. 87.99 for iCaRL-FC). In Protocol 3, MSFD achieves the highest AA (83.65%) and the only method combining top AA with negative AF (−1.25), whereas IDER's lower AF (−2.10) comes at the cost of 0.18 points lower AA. Video continual learning methods (STSP, HCE, Drift, vCLIMB, ESSENTIAL) consistently underperform, with ESSENTIAL dropping to 78.05% AUC_all, highlighting that semantics-focused preservation targets are inadequate for low-level forensic cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: continual deepfake detection (CoReD, DFIL, SURLID), which operates under image-level assumptions and preserves a single entangled representation; video deepfake detection (FTCN, AltFreezing), which models spatiotemporal cues but assumes a fixed closed-world distribution; and continual video learning (vCLIMB, STSP, ESSENTIAL), which preserves high-level semantics rather than low-level forensic signals. Building on iCaRL's replay-based framework and frequency-domain knowledge distillation, the paper departs from prior work by explicitly decomposing video features into spatial, temporal, and spatiotemporal frequency spectra via 1D/2D/3D FFTs and preserving each modality independently, rather than treating the representation as a monolithic whole.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled mechanism for retaining both spatial and temporal forensic cues during sequential model updates in video deepfake detection, addressing a gap that image-centric continual methods and semantics-focused video continual methods both leave open. The modality-sensitivity analysis (different forgery types relying on different cues) offers a useful diagnostic for understanding why single-representation approaches fail. However, the authors acknowledge that race-specific distribution shifts (e.g., KoDF) still trigger forgetting, and spatial/temporal branches degrade at uneven rates, leaving open the question of how to handle demographic and stylistic shifts without modality-specific retraining. Future work on modality-aware memory selection and adaptive per-task modality weighting could further close these gaps.
