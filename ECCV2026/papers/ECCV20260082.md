## Problem

Video generation models used for single-image-to-3D scene generation suffer from geometric distortions and blurry content because they are conditioned only on limited geometric information extracted from a single input frame, which is insufficient to guide the generation of an entire multi-frame video. Providing corresponding geometric guidance for every frame remains an open challenge, as previous methods rely on monocular depth maps or camera embeddings that offer only partial spatial priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (192×336 during training) and a predefined camera trajectory as input, and produces a 49-frame novel view video (576×1024) that is subsequently reconstructed into a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene. During inference, the point cloud of the input image is estimated via MASt3R under the given trajectory to construct rendered partial views, which serve as the conditioning signal for the first-stage video model. The final 3DGS scene can be rendered from arbitrary camera poses for evaluation.

## Method

GeoWorld is a two-stage pipeline that provides full-frame geometry features to the video generation process. In the first stage, a fine-tuned Wan2.1-1.3B video model generates a coarse but content-complete video from rendered partial views; this video is then processed by VGGT to extract full-frame geometry features. In the second stage, a geometry-constrained diffusion model refines the video using these features, guided by a geometric alignment loss (MSE between VGGT aggregator features of predicted and ground-truth videos) that imposes real-world geometric constraints. A geometry adaptation module aligns the geometry features with the video latent space via an MLP adapter and a Squeeze-and-Excitation-style global weighting that discards the bottom 50% of low-information tokens, after which the features are fused through single-frame cross-attention with relative position embeddings in each transformer layer.

## Results

On RealEstate10K novel view synthesis, GeoWorld achieves PSNR 17.28, SSIM 0.6193, and LPIPS 0.3297, substantially outperforming all baselines in fidelity metrics. On Tanks and Temples, GeoWorld reaches PSNR 14.99 and SSIM 0.4625, again leading all compared methods. For 3D scene generation (Table 2), GeoWorld achieves PSNR 16.64 / SSIM 0.5970 / LPIPS 0.4284 on RealEstate10K and PSNR 15.00 / SSIM 0.4642 / LPIPS 0.5058 on Tanks and Temples, surpassing all baselines across every metric. GeoWorld also achieves 7.5× faster inference than Hunyuan-Voyager (148s vs. 1110s) while using only 3.9B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoWorld decisively outperforms all four baselines on fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM) and perceptual quality (LPIPS) across both RealEstate10K and Tanks and Temples. On RealEstate10K, GeoWorld's PSNR of 17.28 exceeds Hunyuan-Voyager (14.85) by 2.43 dB, See3D (14.60) by 2.68 dB, ViewCrafter (14.37) by 2.91 dB, and FlexWorld (14.28) by 3.00 dB. The only metrics where GeoWorld does not lead are FID and FVD on novel view synthesis, where FlexWorld achieves slightly lower values (FID 30.56 vs. 31.00; FVD 270.4 vs. 311.7 on RealEstate10K), indicating marginally better distributional realism for FlexWorld's generated videos. However, in the downstream 3D scene generation task (Table 2), GeoWorld wins on every metric against every baseline, with PSNR gains of 2.1–3.3 dB over the strongest competitor. GeoWorld also achieves 7.5× faster inference than Hunyuan-Voyager (148s vs. 1110s) and uses only 3.9B parameters versus 12.8B for Hunyuan-Voyager.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of conditioning video diffusion models on single-frame geometric cues (monocular depth maps, camera embeddings) used by methods like ViewCrafter, FlexWorld, and Hunyuan-Voyager. Instead, it introduces a two-stage pipeline that first generates a coarse content-complete video, then leverages VGGT to extract full-frame geometry features from that video, which guide a second, geometry-constrained diffusion model. The geometric alignment loss and the Squeeze-and-Excitation-inspired global weighting in the geometry adaptation module are novel design choices that distinguish this work from prior attempts to inject geometric priors into video generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that two smaller video models (3.9B total parameters) outperform a 12.8B single-model baseline (Hunyuan-Voyager) by ~2.4 dB PSNR while being 7.5× faster suggests that explicit geometric conditioning from a dedicated geometry model is a more efficient path to 3D consistency than scaling up the video generator alone. This two-stage 'draft-then-refine-with-geometry' paradigm could be adopted by other controllable video generation pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer sequences beyond 49 frames, whether alternative geometry models (e.g., Fast3R, DUSt3R) yield comparable or better features, and how sensitive the pipeline is to the quality of the first-stage coarse video.
