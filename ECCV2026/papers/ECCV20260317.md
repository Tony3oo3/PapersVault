## Problem

Personalizing Image-to-Video diffusion models with specific visual effects currently requires training a separate LoRA module per effect, incurring 56 GPU hours of iterative optimization that blocks interactive creative workflows. Existing hypernetwork approaches that predict adapter weights from semantics alone struggle to converge when scaled to the high-dimensional, temporally structured parameter space of modern I2V Diffusion Transformers, and directly regressing raw LoRA factors suffers from inherent factorization non-identifiability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260317/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text effect prompt (e.g., 'polar bear spa') and the frozen base model weights W₀ of an I2V diffusion backbone as input, and produces a complete set of layer-wise LoRA weight updates (rank r=128) in a single 3.3-second forward pass on an A100. The predicted LoRA is then applied to the frozen backbone to generate 512×288 videos with the target effect. Weight tokens are constructed by slicing each W₀ layer into row and column vectors, projecting them into a shared d=2048 space, and compressing via cross-attention to n=256 tokens per layer before feeding the hypernetwork.

## Method

Prompt2Effect is a 1.3B-parameter Transformer hypernetwork with two key design principles. First, it is weight-driven: the frozen base weights W₀ are tokenized (rows as B-tokens, columns as A-tokens) and fed as structural input alongside the effect prompt, grounding prediction in each layer's native input-output geometry rather than relying solely on semantic embeddings. Second, it predicts SVD-canonicalized LoRA factors (B* = US^{1/2}, A* = S^{1/2}V^T) instead of raw (A, B), resolving the (BR, R⁻¹A) factorization ambiguity and imposing an orthogonal, energy-ordered parameterization that stabilizes large-scale training. The architecture uses learnable cross-attention queries to compress weight tokens, injects the text prompt via cross-attention (not concatenation), adds layer-index and module-type embeddings for structural awareness, and decodes via a cross-attention uncompression head that projects back to full layer dimensionality. Training uses a normalized MSE loss against SVD-canonicalized ground-truth LoRAs across 75 effect categories, and the predicted weights can serve as zero-shot adapters or as initialization that accelerates subsequent LoRA fine-tuning by ~10×.

## Results

On in-distribution (IID) effects, Prompt2Effect achieves a CLIP Score of 25.10, Aesthetic Quality of 56.30, and VLM Overall score of 56.93, closely matching the fully optimized per-effect LoRA upper bound (25.06 / 57.60 / 57.57) while requiring only 3.3 seconds of hypernetwork inference instead of 56 GPU training hours. On out-of-distribution (OOD) effects, zero-shot Prompt2Effect scores 48.87 VLM Overall (vs. 42.46 for the base model), and using its prediction as initialization for just 100 LoRA steps (Init-100) reaches 56.33 VLM Overall, approaching the 56.70 of a fully converged 1000-step LoRA. On the public Wan2.1-I2V-14B-480P backbone, Prompt2Effect achieves 64.49 VLM Overall versus 65.16 for per-effect LoRA, confirming model-agnostic generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HyperDreamBooth [25], Prompt2Effect wins decisively on every metric: CLIP Score 25.10 vs. 22.34, Aesthetic Quality 56.30 vs. 35.30, and VLM Overall 56.93 vs. 0.00, indicating that the prior hypernetwork fails to produce usable LoRA weights when scaled to video diffusion. Against the fully optimized per-effect LoRA upper bound, Prompt2Effect is essentially on-par: it slightly exceeds LoRA on CLIP Score (25.10 vs. 25.06) and Motion Smoothness (98.54 vs. 98.44), while LoRA retains a small edge on Aesthetic Quality (57.60 vs. 56.30) and VLM Overall (57.57 vs. 56.93). The gap narrows further on the public Wan2.1-I2V-14B backbone, where Prompt2Effect reaches 64.49 VLM Overall versus 65.16 for LoRA. In the OOD fast-adaptation setting, Prompt2Effect's zero-shot prediction used as initialization (Init-100) achieves 56.33 VLM Overall after only 100 steps, matching the 56.70 of a 1000-step LoRA trained from scratch, demonstrating a 10× training speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

Prompt2Effect extends the hypernetwork-for-weight-prediction paradigm (Ha et al. 2016; HyperDreamBooth for image personalization; LoFA for compact-condition LoRA prediction) to the substantially harder setting of global dynamic video effects in Diffusion Transformer backbones. Two key departures from prior work define its contribution: (1) conditioning the predictor on the frozen base weights W₀ rather than relying solely on semantic embeddings, and (2) predicting SVD-canonicalized LoRA factors (B* = US^{1/2}, A* = S^{1/2}Vᵀ) to eliminate the non-identifiability of the raw BA factorization. The paper positions itself as the first to scale prompt-conditioned LoRA synthesis to large-scale I2V models, where prior methods either target static UNet architectures or compact reference-image conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 56-GPU-hour-to-3.3-second reduction makes per-effect video personalization practically interactive, removing the iterative data-curation-and-training bottleneck that has limited creative workflows. The SVD-canonicalization insight—that the non-identifiability of ΔW = BA causes unstable optimization in weight regression, and that enforcing an orthogonal energy-ordered parameterization resolves it—generalizes beyond this specific application to any hypernetwork predicting factorized low-rank updates. However, OOD performance remains constrained by the 75-effect training set, and the primary experiments use a proprietary backbone; broader validation across diverse effect taxonomies and model scales is needed before the approach can be considered a general solution to video personalization.
