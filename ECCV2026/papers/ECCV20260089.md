## Problem

Existing RGB-only VLMs that incorporate 3D geometry encoders rely solely on implicit 3D representations, which capture coarse global spatial layouts but lack the fine-grained quantitative geometric details needed for precise 3D localization and reasoning. This mirrors a cognitive gap: humans use both abstract '3D cognitive maps' for scene awareness and structured '3D reconstruction maps' for precise spatial tasks, yet current VLMs only approximate the former.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB video sequence (rescaled to 392×518, up to 32 frames, patch size 14) and a natural language query, requiring no external 3D sensor data. Internally, the 3D geometry encoder (AnySplat) reconstructs depth maps, camera poses, and 3D Gaussian splats from the video; depth maps (c'=1) are used as the default explicit 3D attribute for EGTs. Outputs include 3D bounding boxes (in camera or reference-frame coordinates), dense object captions conditioned on object centers, and free-form spatial reasoning answers.

## Method

VLM-IE3D introduces a three-stream architecture: a 2D visual encoder, a 3D geometry encoder (AnySplat) whose fusion-decoder outputs serve as Implicit Geometry Tokens (IGTs) encoding global spatial priors, and a lightweight 3D explicit embedding module (one-layer patch embedding + two-layer MLP) that converts reconstructed depth maps into Explicit Geometry Tokens (EGTs) preserving fine-grained quantitative geometry. A 3D-aware adapter fuses the three token streams: after 2×2 spatial compression, an Implicit-Explicit Attention (IEA) module performs multi-head cross-attention with IGTs as queries and EGTs as keys/values to align and merge the two 3D representations, followed by element-wise addition with 2D visual tokens. The resulting unified 3D-aware visual tokens are fed into a frozen-encoder Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone, with only the VLM backbone and the 3D explicit embedding module trained (0.10B additional parameters, ~3.2% overhead). The key design insight is that IGTs and EGTs are inherently complementary—IGTs provide abstract global structure while EGTs supply interpretable spatial measurements—and the lightweight MLP embedding deliberately avoids deep encoders to prevent over-abstraction that would overlap with IGTs' role.

## Results

On VSI-Bench (spatial reasoning), VLM-IE3D-4B achieves the best average score of 47.6%, outperforming proprietary Gemini-1.5-Pro (45.4%) and the much larger LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B (40.9%). On 3D scene understanding tasks, VLM-IE3D achieves 80.4 C@0.5 on Scan2Cap (best overall), 43.2 Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer (best among 2D-input methods), and 42.8 F1_25 on 3D video detection. The method adds only 0.10B parameters (3.2%) over the implicit-only baseline VG LLM while gaining 1 FPS inference speed cost (7→6 FPS on H100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary 2D-input competitor VG LLM [50], VLM-IE3D consistently wins: +1.8 C@0.5 on Scan2Cap (80.4 vs 78.6), +6.8 Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer (43.2 vs 36.4), +2.5 P25 on 3D video detection (44.2 vs 41.7), and +0.3 average on VSI-Bench (47.6 vs 47.3). Against the no-3D baseline Qwen2.5-VL-3B [2], gains are substantially larger: +22.4 C@0.5 on Scan2Cap, +9.2 Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer, and +12.1 P25 on 3D video detection. On Scan2Cap, VLM-IE3D (80.4 C@0.5) slightly edges out the 3D-input method Video-3D LLM [49] (80.0) and LLaVA-3D [52] (79.2), demonstrating that RGB-only implicit+explicit geometry can match explicit 3D scene inputs. On VSI-Bench, VLM-IE3D-4B surpasses all proprietary models (GPT-4o: 34.0, Gemini-1.5-Pro: 45.4) and open-source models up to 72B parameters (LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B: 40.9, LLaVA-OneVision-72B: 40.2), with the largest margin on Relative Distance (+3.1 over VG LLM) where explicit geometric structure is most critical. The only task where VLM-IE3D trails VG LLM is Approximation Order on VSI-Bench (31.9 vs 36.4), suggesting implicit representations retain a slight edge for ordinal spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RGB-only 3D VLM paradigm established by works like VG LLM, VLM-3R, and Spatial-MLLM, which inject implicit 3D representations from pre-trained geometry encoders (e.g., AnySplat, VGGT, DUSt3R) into VLM backbones. The key departure is the argument that these implicit latent features, while capturing global scene layout, are too compressed for quantitative spatial reasoning; the paper repurposes the same geometry encoder's prediction heads (depth, camera poses, 3D Gaussians) to produce explicit geometry tokens that complement the implicit ones. Architecturally, it builds on Qwen2.5-VL's spatial merging and introduces a lightweight implicit-explicit attention (IEA) module for fusion, rather than relying on a single representation stream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is enabling fine-grained 3D spatial understanding (localization, detection, counting) from accessible RGB video without expensive depth sensors or point cloud infrastructure, which is critical for embodied AI and real-world deployment. The ablation showing that a heavy deep encoder (DepthAnything V2) for explicit geometry actually underperforms a two-layer MLP is a useful negative result, suggesting that explicit spatial coordinates should be preserved rather than re-abstracted. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to large-scale outdoor scenes, whether the 3D geometry encoder's reconstruction quality becomes a hard bottleneck, and whether the implicit/explicit decomposition is the optimal way to structure 3D inductive biases or whether other factorizations (e.g., semantic vs. geometric) might be more effective.
