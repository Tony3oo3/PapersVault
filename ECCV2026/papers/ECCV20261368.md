## Problem

Reinforcement learning for end-to-end autonomous driving in closed-loop simulation suffers from a significant sim2real gap: 3DGS-based scene reconstruction simulators produce artifacts (blurred lane markings, degraded backgrounds) when rendering novel trajectories outside the recorded camera distribution, and they inherently lack rare corner cases such as sudden cut-ins or braking that are absent from the reconstruction data. This limits both the photorealism of sensor inputs and the scenario diversity needed for robust policy training.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes multi-view camera images, 3D bounding boxes, and HDMaps from real-world driving sequences (nuScenes) to reconstruct 3DGS scenes, with the DriveRestorer additionally taking degraded rendered video as a control condition alongside structural cues. The trained policy outputs a probabilistic distribution over driving actions from multi-view camera inputs, while the simulator produces rendered novel-view sensor data at 125 FPS. DAA and CTG generate additional trajectory-level outputs—corner-case surrounding-vehicle paths and extended ego-vehicle maneuvers—that are rendered into training data rather than consumed directly by the policy.

## Method

ReconDreamer-RL integrates three components into a two-stage training pipeline. ReconSimulator combines 3DGS scene reconstruction with an iterative video-diffusion restoration loop (DriveRestorer) for appearance modeling and a kinematic bicycle model for physical feasibility, modeling static background and moving vehicles as separate Gaussian sets with explicit pose transforms. The Dynamic Adversary Agent (DAA) selects target vehicles from BEV by distance, generates novel interactive trajectories (e.g., cut-ins) via text-to-trajectory or custom functions, validates them against drivable-region, collision, and kinematic constraints, and renders them in the simulator for both imitation and RL stages. The Cousin Trajectory Generator (CTG) extends and interpolates expert trajectories to produce Cousin-nuScenes with 4× more non-straight-line maneuvers, correcting the data bias toward simple driving. Training alternates PPO-based closed-loop RL in parallel ReconSimulator workers with imitation-learning updates from the three data sources (nuScenes, DAA corner cases, CTG data) to stabilize the policy and prevent drift from humanlike behavior.

## Results

On the closed-loop 3DGS evaluation using nuScenes, ReconDreamer-RL achieves a Collision Ratio (CR) of 0.077, a 5× reduction over the best imitation learning baseline (VAD, CR=0.386) and a 3× reduction over the prior RL method RAD (CR=0.238). In cut-in corner cases specifically, ReconDreamer-RL drops CR to 0.089 versus 0.317 for RAD and 0.449 for VAD. The method also achieves the lowest Deviation Ratio (DR=0.040) and matches RAD on comfort metrics (Long. Jerk 4.264, Lat. Jerk 0.189) while rendering at 125 FPS, comparable to RAD-3DGS (135 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReconDreamer-RL outperforms all three imitation learning baselines (VAD, GenAD, VADv2) by a wide margin on every metric, with the headline 5× Collision Ratio reduction over VAD (0.077 vs. 0.386) and 4.3× over GenAD (0.077 vs. 0.333). Against the closest RL baseline RAD, which also trains in a 3DGS closed-loop environment, ReconDreamer-RL achieves a 3× lower CR (0.077 vs. 0.238) and a 2× lower DCR (0.048 vs. 0.143), with the gap widening dramatically in cut-in corner cases (CR 0.089 vs. 0.317). On comfort, ReconDreamer-RL matches RAD in unedited scenes (Long. Jerk 4.264 vs. 4.237) but surpasses it in cut-in scenarios (3.832 vs. 4.912). In rendering speed, ReconSimulator (125 FPS) is comparable to RAD-3DGS (135 FPS) and roughly 600× faster than EmerNeRF (0.21 FPS), confirming that the diffusion-prior integration does not introduce a prohibitive computational bottleneck for RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 3DGS-based driving scene reconstruction and closed-loop reinforcement learning for end-to-end autonomous driving. It directly extends RAD [15], which first demonstrated RL training in 3DGS environments, by replacing its raw 3DGS rendering with a diffusion-prior-enhanced pipeline (DriveRestorer from ReconDreamer [35]) for appearance modeling and adding a kinematic bicycle model for physical plausibility. The video diffusion prior itself traces back to DriveDreamer [48] and DriveDreamer-2 [63], which established that generative video models can correct reconstruction artifacts along novel trajectories. The two data-augmentation components—DAA for adversarial corner-case generation and CTG for trajectory diversity—draw on traffic-flow modeling and text-to-trajectory generation but are specifically designed to feed a two-stage imitation-then-RL training loop rather than to serve as standalone scenario generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a concrete and widely acknowledged bottleneck: reconstruction-based simulators produce high-fidelity sensor data only near recorded camera paths and lack the rare corner-case interactions that determine real-world safety. By demonstrating that a diffusion-prior-enhanced 3DGS simulator can run at 125 FPS while supporting free ego-vehicle navigation and dynamic scene editing, the paper makes photorealistic closed-loop RL training practically feasible at scale. Key open questions remain: evaluation is limited to nuScenes scenes, the approach inherits 3DGS's dependence on recorded geometry (it cannot reconstruct entirely unseen environments), and the sim-to-real transfer of policies trained in these reconstructed scenes has not been validated on real vehicles. The cross-dataset generalization of DriveRestorer (Waymo-trained model applied to nuScenes) is a promising but underexplored direction for reducing per-dataset reconstruction cost.
