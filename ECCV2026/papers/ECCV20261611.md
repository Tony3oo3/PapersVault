## Problem

Document layout analysis has remained outside the foundation-model paradigm, relying on fixed-ontology bounding-box detection with flat, non-hierarchical labels. This abstraction fails on irregular or curved regions, discards the nested structural hierarchy of documents, and produces static outputs that cannot adapt to user-specified layout specifications or domain-specific ontologies without task-specific retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261611/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a document image and a natural language prompt as input, where the prompt specifies the desired segmentation granularity, semantic vocabulary (generic vs. domain-specific), and any spatial or task constraints (e.g., PII redaction, spatial reasoning queries). It outputs a serialized hierarchical layout tree where each node carries an open-set label, a pixel-accurate binary mask grounding that node in the image, and an optional natural language explanation. The same document can be re-parsed under entirely different ontologies by changing only the prompt, without any architectural or weight modification.

## Method

DocLAYOUT-VL couples a VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL or InternVL3.5) with a novel Hierarchical Grounding Contextualizer (HGC) and a SAM-based segmentation decoder. The VLM autoregressively generates a serialized layout tree interleaved with special [LAY] tokens that establish one-to-one anchors between semantic nodes and spatial regions. The HGC is a lightweight multi-layer transformer that refines these [LAY] token embeddings via Hierarchical Contextual Attention, a dynamic tree-aware attention mask that restricts each node's attention to its parent, children, and siblings, thereby enforcing structural locality and preventing unrelated regions from influencing one another. The refined embeddings feed a SAM prompt encoder and mask decoder that produce pixel-level masks conditioned on dense visual features. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (flat grounding → hierarchical nesting → instruction tuning) with a composite loss combining autoregressive cross-entropy, Dice+BCE segmentation, a background-exclusion penalty, and structural containment/non-overlap penalties that enforce geometric consistency between predicted masks and the generated tree hierarchy.

## Results

On the open-set HOPLAY-DOCS-BENCH (four tracks: flat, hierarchical, curved, promptable), DocLAYOUT-VL-8B-IT achieves an Overall Score of 79.4 (80.1 English), nearly doubling the best closed-source VLM (Gemini 3 Pro: 37.2) and the best fine-tuned VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-ft: 45.8). On closed-set benchmarks (Table 3), DocLAYOUT-VL-8B-it reaches 96.5 mAP on PubLayNet, 97.3 on TableBank, and 77.5 on IndicDLP, all zero-shot, matching or exceeding dedicated detectors like DocLayout-YOLO (96.9 PLN, 97.5 TB) while generalizing far better on label-rich sets (M6Docs: 76.9 vs. 70.9 for DocLayout-YOLO; IndicDLP: 77.5 vs. 55.0 for IndicDLP-YOLO). The 4B variant (OS 71.8) already surpasses all 235B+ parameter VLM baselines, demonstrating that architectural design (HGC + segmentation) matters more than raw model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocLAYOUT-VL-8B-IT (OS 79.4) outperforms all 27 baselines by a wide margin. Against pipeline hierarchical methods, it leads by 72.8 points over Dolphin (OS 6.6) and 75.1 over DocParser (OS 4.3), which lack pixel-level grounding. Among open-source VLMs, the 8B DocLAYOUT-VL surpasses the much larger Qwen3-VL-235B-it (OS 31.5) and InternVL3.5-241B (OS 28.0) by ~48 points, showing that the HGC + segmentation architecture compensates for 30x fewer parameters. Among closed-source models, it beats Gemini 3 Pro (OS 37.2) by 42.2 points, GPT-5.2 (OS 28.6) by 50.8, and Claude Sonnet 4.6 (OS 30.5) by 48.9; notably, Gemini 3 Pro achieves the best flat localization (Om 72.2) but collapses on hierarchy (HB Om 6.9) and curved segmentation (CB Om 40.3), while DocLAYOUT-VL is strong across all four tracks. Fine-tuned VLMs (Qwen3-VL-8B-ft: OS 45.8; InternVL3.5-8B-ft: OS 42.8) close the gap but still trail by 33.6 and 36.6 points respectively, confirming that task-specific box supervision alone cannot replicate hierarchical mask grounding. On closed-set benchmarks (Table 3), DocLAYOUT-VL-8B-it matches or exceeds the strongest dedicated detectors zero-shot: 96.5 vs. 96.9 mAP on PubLayNet (DocLayout-YOLO), 97.3 vs. 97.5 on TableBank, while substantially outperforming them on harder sets (M6Docs: 76.9 vs. 70.9; IndicDLP: 77.5 vs. 55.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in document layout analysis, which has treated the problem as closed-set bounding-box detection over fixed ontologies (PubLayNet, DocLayNet, DocBank, etc.), by reframing it as promptable, open-set, hierarchical pixel-level segmentation. It builds directly on SAM's promptable segmentation architecture for the mask decoder and on VLM backbones (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5) for autoregressive layout-tree generation, but introduces the Hierarchical Grounding Contextualizer (HGC) as a novel bridge that injects tree-structural context into the segmentation prompts via a dynamic attention mask over parent-child-sibling relations. Prior hierarchical layout work (HRDoc, DocHieNet, GraphDoc) reconstructs trees post-hoc from flat detections; here the hierarchy is generated natively and jointly with masks, and prior VLM-SAM grounding approaches for natural scenes are shown to fail on text-dense documents due to distribution shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a concrete, well-evidenced path toward treating document layout as a controllable, instruction-driven interface rather than a fixed preprocessing step, which could reshape how downstream document AI pipelines (extraction, QA, verification) consume layout signals. The 80M-mask dataset and four-track benchmark are substantial community resources, and the finding that architectural components (HGC, structural losses) yield larger gains than doubling data volume offers a practical training recipe. Key open questions include cross-page dependency modeling, inference latency for documents with many layout elements, and whether the large gaps over VLM baselines persist as those models improve their native spatial grounding capabilities.
