## Problem

Existing SE(3)-equivariant encoders for 3D shape assembly produce a single entangled feature that jointly encodes rotation and translation, making it difficult for downstream pose predictors to generalize across diverse spatial configurations. While dual-branch designs have begun to separate these factors, they rely on loss-based separation without explicit structural mechanisms to suppress the non-target motion component in each branch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261237/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The SOT encoder consumes 3D point clouds of individual parts (with arbitrary SE(3) transformations applied) and outputs two disentangled vector-list feature maps: an SO(3)-equivariant rotation feature and a T(3)-equivariant translation feature. These features are passed to a plug-and-play downstream assembly architecture (e.g., NSM, SE(3)-Assembly, 2BY2NET, or CMNet) that regresses a rotation matrix and translation vector per part. The encoder operates on vector-neuron representations (N×C×3 tensors) rather than scalar features, and the translation branch is trained under a wide-range random translation setting (t_i ~ U([-2|C_{P_i}|, 2|C_{P_i}|])) to improve robustness beyond the centroid-fixed translations used in prior work.

## Method

The SOT encoder uses a dual-branch design where each branch explicitly suppresses the opposing motion factor. The rotation branch stacks RETI-VN layers that apply a QR-decomposition-based projection (I − Q_t Q_t^T) to remove components in a learned translation subspace, combined with a row-sum weight regularizer L_w that drives W·1_n^T·t ≈ 0, yielding an approximately SO(3)-equivariant, translation-invariant feature. The translation branch first computes an SE(3)-equivariant feature via standard VNT layers, then directly subtracts the rotation-branch output to isolate translational variation, with a translation supervision loss that provably suppresses the rotation-dependent residual g(P)R. This replaces the strong weight constraint (Σ_l W_kl = 1) of VNT with a weaker structural mechanism, preserving representational capacity while still achieving factor-wise equivariance and invariance.

## Results

On the 2BY2 dataset with SE(3)-Assembly, SOT achieves RMSE-R of 32.36° and CD of 0.620, outperforming DGCNN (43.20°, 1.148), VN-DGCNN (39.18°, 2.258), and FINet-EB (36.41°, 1.403). With 2BY2NET, SOT reaches the lowest rotation error of 22.47° and CD of 1.527, compared to 29.78°/4.933 for VN-DGCNN. On Breaking Bad (everyday), SOT with SE(3)-Assembly attains RMSE-R 49.24° and CD 0.084, the best across all encoders. Part Accuracy (PA) also improves consistently, e.g., 0.5911 vs. 0.5536 (VN-DGCNN) and 0.5243 (FINet-EB) for SE(3)-Assembly on 2BY2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOT consistently outperforms all three encoder baselines (DGCNN, VN-DGCNN, FINet-EB) across every assembly method and dataset. Against DGCNN, SOT reduces RMSE-R by 8–11° on 2BY2 (e.g., 32.36° vs. 43.20° with SE(3)-Assembly) and cuts CD by roughly 45%. Against VN-DGCNN, the gains are most dramatic on Chamfer Distance: SOT achieves CD 0.620 vs. 2.258 for VN-DGCNN under SE(3)-Assembly on 2BY2, a 3.6× reduction. FINet-EB is the closest competitor but still trails SOT on all metrics; for instance, on Breaking Bad with SE(3)-Assembly, SOT reaches RMSE-R 49.24° vs. 55.38° for FINet-EB. In the dedicated rotation-branch comparison (Table 4), RETI-VN (SOT's core layer) substantially outperforms VNT [16], which enforces a strict row-sum-to-one weight constraint: RMSE-R drops from 84.42° (VNT) to 32.36° (RETI-VN) under SE(3)-Assembly, confirming that the weaker structural constraint in RETI-VN preserves more representational capacity while still suppressing translation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SE(3)-equivariant encoder line (VN-DGCNN, SE(3)-transformers, SE(3)-Assembly) by decomposing the single equivariant feature into explicit SO(3)- and T(3)-equivariant branches. It departs from VNT (Katzir et al., ECCV 2022), which enforces a strong row-sum constraint (ΣW_kl=1) to eliminate translation, by replacing it with a weaker QR-decomposition-based projection (RETI-VN) that approximately suppresses translation while preserving representational capacity. It also builds on dual-branch designs (FINet, DBDNet) that separate rotation and translation via independent modules, but adds structural suppression mechanisms (projection onto the translation null space and direct subtraction between SE(3)- and SO(3)-equivariant outputs) rather than relying solely on loss-based separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SOT encoder is a plug-and-play module that consistently improves pose accuracy across multiple assembly architectures (NSM, SE(3)-Assembly, 2BY2NET, CMNet) and datasets, suggesting that explicit factor disentanglement is a broadly useful inductive bias for 3D pose estimation beyond shape assembly. The theoretical observation (Proposition 2) that an SE(3)-equivariant composite map cannot be identically zero provides a clean motivation for the direct-subtraction mechanism, and the equivariance/invariance analyses offer direct evidence that the learned features satisfy the desired group-theoretic properties. Open questions include whether the QR-based subspace dimension r can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach extends to higher-dimensional symmetry groups, and whether the disentanglement principle transfers to tasks like 6D object pose estimation or robotic manipulation at scale.
