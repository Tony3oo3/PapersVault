## Problem

Existing multi-view open-vocabulary 3D detection pipelines decouple geometry-based instance construction from semantic labeling, generating class-agnostic fragments and assigning categories only after merging. When geometric evidence is view-dependent and incomplete—as is typical with RGB-only multi-view observations—this geometry-only merging produces irreversible association errors such as over-merging distinct objects or fragmenting a single instance. The paper asks whether semantic constraints can be injected directly into the fragment-merging step to prevent these errors without sacrificing open-vocabulary flexibility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128 uniformly sampled RGB frames per scene (resized to 378×504) and optionally ground-truth camera poses; in the pose-free setting it relies on a reconstruction backbone (Depth Anything 3 or VGGT) to estimate poses and depth. An MLLM (GPT-5.1) is queried per view for K=5 category hypotheses, and SAM 3 produces category-aware 2D masks that are back-projected into 3D. The output is a set of axis-aligned 3D bounding boxes, each annotated with an open-vocabulary category label and a confidence score derived from confidence-weighted cross-view support statistics.

## Method

Group3D constructs two scene-level memories: a Scene Vocabulary Memory aggregating MLLM-predicted categories across views, and a 3D Fragment Memory storing point-cloud fragments lifted from SAM 3 masks via depth-and-pose back-projection. The core contribution is an MLLM-driven semantic compatibility grouping step that partitions the scene vocabulary into groups of categories that could plausibly refer to the same physical object under taxonomy noise (e.g., chair–sofa) while excluding structurally incompatible pairs (e.g., wall–window, table–cup). During instance construction, two 3D fragments are merge-eligible only if their categories fall in the same compatibility group AND their voxelized point clouds satisfy a geometric overlap predicate (IoU ≥ τ_iou or containment ratio ≥ τ_cont), preventing geometry-driven over-merging of semantically distinct objects. Final category assignment uses a saturation-weighted aggregation s(ℓ) = s̄(ℓ)·(1−exp(−N(ℓ)/τ)) that rewards repeated cross-view support while preventing dominance by categories with many fragments. The entire pipeline is training-free and zero-shot with respect to 3D supervision, operating solely on multi-view RGB inputs.

## Results

On ScanNet20 (pose-known, zero-shot), Group3D achieves 51.1 mAP25 and 27.4 mAP50, substantially outperforming the strongest multi-view baseline Zoo3D₁ (32.8 mAP25) and even surpassing point-cloud-based methods like OV-Uni3DETR (25.3 mAP25). In the more challenging pose-free setting, Group3D retains 41.2 mAP25 on ScanNet20 and 22.3 mAP25 on ScanNet60. On ARKitScenes (pose-known, zero-shot), Group3D reaches 20.5 mAP25, outperforming Zoo3D₁ (16.1) and Zoo3D₀ (13.0). On the long-tail ScanNet200 benchmark, Group3D achieves 17.9 mAP25 (pose-known) and 12.6 mAP25 (pose-free), again leading all multi-view baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Group3D achieves a decisive margin over all multi-view image-based baselines: on ScanNet20 (pose-known, zero-shot), it scores 51.1 mAP25 versus Zoo3D₁'s 32.8 and Zoo3D₀'s 30.5, a gap of roughly 18–20 points. It also surpasses the strongest point-cloud-based open-vocabulary methods (OV-Uni3DETR at 25.3, ImOV3D at 21.5, GLIS at 20.8 mAP25) despite using only RGB images without explicit 3D geometry. In the pose-free regime, where reconstruction noise degrades geometric cues, Group3D still leads with 41.2 mAP25 on ScanNet20 versus Zoo3D₁'s 27.9. On ScanNet60, the advantage narrows but persists (29.1 vs. 23.9 mAP25 for Zoo3D₁), and on the long-tail ScanNet200 setting Group3D maintains a 1.4-point lead over Zoo3D₁ (17.9 vs. 16.5). Cross-dataset generalization to ARKitScenes confirms the trend: Group3D reaches 20.5 mAP25 (pose-known) and 18.4 (pose-free), outperforming Zoo3D₁ by 4.4 and 2.3 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

Group3D extends the multi-view open-vocabulary 3D detection pipeline established by Zoo3D and OpenM3D, which lift 2D masks into 3D and aggregate across views, but departs from their geometry-first merging by injecting MLLM-driven semantic compatibility groups as a hard constraint during fragment association. Rather than treating the MLLM as a post-hoc labeler (as in CLIP-style alignment or Zoo3D's vision-language similarity scoring), Group3D repurposes it to generate a structured partition of the scene vocabulary into merge-eligible groups, turning open-vocabulary category variability into a gating signal. The pipeline also builds on recent foundation models (SAM 3 for segmentation, Depth Anything 3 / VGGT for reconstruction) but the core methodological contribution is the group-gated merging criterion that combines semantic compatibility with voxel-level geometric overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that enforcing semantic constraints at merge time—rather than only after instances are formed—can substantially reduce irreversible over-merging errors in multi-view RGB settings where geometric evidence is incomplete, yielding a large margin over the closest multi-view baseline (Zoo3D) in a fully zero-shot, training-free regime. This suggests a broader principle for multi-view 3D perception: language-driven semantic priors can complement geometric reasoning during instance construction, potentially reducing dependence on dense 3D supervision. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether axis-aligned boxes limit practical utility, and how sensitive performance is to MLLM quality and the granularity of the induced compatibility groups.
