## Problem

Object-level late fusion in collaborative perception transmits only 3D boxes and scalar confidence scores, which are insufficient for robust association and fusion under heterogeneous detector backbones, localization noise, and asynchronous communication delays. Feature-level fusion methods address these issues but require collaborative training, architectural compatibility, and MB/frame bandwidth, making them impractical for open V2X deployments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262414/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each remote agent transmits a sparse set of Top-K evidential proposals (~2 KB/frame total), where each proposal carries a 3D box, Beta objectness parameters (α⁺, α⁻), and per-dimension Normal-Inverse-Gamma regression parameters (γ, ν, αᵣ, βᵣ). The ego agent also consumes its own detections, relative agent poses, and per-message timestamps. The output is a set of fused 3D bounding boxes with a single fused confidence score per object, where the box geometry comes from one selected source and the confidence is aggregated across all matched proposals in the cluster.

## Method

HERO equips each detector with an evidential head (Beta-EDL for objectness, NIG for box regression) trained independently with no cross-agent collaboration. On the ego side, proposals are associated via a dual gate combining absolute BEV distance and a Mahalanobis distance that adapts to each proposal's planar NIG variance, then clustered greedily with at most one proposal per agent. The key design is a decoupled fusion rule: semantic evidence is aggregated by reliability- and freshness-weighted Beta-sum across the cluster, while geometry is selected (not averaged) from the single proposal maximizing evidence strength × reliability × freshness, avoiding drift from correlated source-level pose bias. Detection-Level Pose Refinement (DPR) estimates per-agent translation bias from matched ego-agent clusters and corrects proposals before re-running association, and a two-timescale freshness decay (slower for semantics, faster for geometry) discounts stale messages without retraining.

## Results

On OPV2V-H with four heterogeneous agents (L_P+C_E+L_S+C_R), HERO achieves AP50=0.922 and AP70=0.841, matching the strongest feature-level baseline HEAL (AP50=0.923, AP70=0.855) while transmitting only ~2 KB/frame of sparse proposals. On the real-world DAIR-V2X dataset, HERO attains the best AP50 across all three V2I heterogeneous combinations: 0.823 (L_P+L_S), 0.744 (L_P+C_E), and 0.753 (L_P+C_R). Under stress conditions, HERO retains AP70=0.763 at 0.6 m/deg pose noise and AP70=0.774 at 500 ms delay, degrading far more gracefully than feature-level baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERO is most competitive with HEAL [19], the strongest heterogeneous baseline: on OPV2V-H (4-agent) HERO trails by only 0.001 AP50 and 0.014 AP70, while on DAIR-V2X HERO leads HEAL by 0.053–0.085 AP50 across all three V2I pairs. Against GenComm [48], HERO wins AP70 by a large margin (0.841 vs 0.775 on OPV2V-H) but is marginally behind on AP50 (0.922 vs 0.925); on DAIR-V2X HERO dominates GenComm by 0.08–0.11 AP50. HERO clearly outperforms CodeFilling [12] on both metrics (AP50 +0.006, AP70 +0.054 on OPV2V-H; AP50 +0.04–0.11 on DAIR-V2X) and all feature-level baselines (CoBEVT, DiscoNet, AttFusion, F-Cooper) by 0.02–0.09 AP50 and 0.09–0.16 AP70. STAMP [6], a task- and model-agnostic method, is the weakest baseline, with HERO exceeding it by 0.09 AP50 and 0.12 AP70 on OPV2V-H. The key differentiator is robustness: under pose noise and delay, HERO's AP70 drop is substantially smaller than all baselines, whereas max-score-based methods like GenComm and CodeFilling collapse more sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

HERO builds on Evidential Deep Learning (Sensoy et al., 2018) for semantic uncertainty via Beta distributions and Deep Evidential Regression (Amini et al., 2020) for geometric uncertainty via Normal-Inverse-Gamma distributions, repurposing these as lightweight reliability signals for object-level fusion rather than for calibration or safety decisions. It departs from the dominant feature-level fusion paradigm (AttFusion, CoBEVT, DiscoNet) by operating entirely on sparse 3D proposals, and from prior object-level methods (OptiMatch, VIPS) by introducing a decoupled fusion rule: semantic evidence is aggregated across matched proposals via Beta-sum, while geometry is selected from a single most-reliable source rather than averaged. The detection-level pose refinement (DPR) and Mahalanobis-gated association extend ideas from pose-correction literature (CoAlign, OptiMatch) into an inference-time, training-free setting for heterogeneous agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HERO demonstrates that object-level fusion with only ~2 KB/frame can match feature-level SOTA accuracy while being substantially more robust under pose noise and asynchronous delay, making it a practical candidate for open, heterogeneous V2X deployments where collaborative training and dense feature exchange are infeasible. The decoupling principle—aggregating semantics but selecting geometry—addresses a real failure mode (correlated source-level pose bias corrupting averaged boxes) that prior NMS-based or averaging-based late fusion cannot handle. Open questions include whether the evidential signals remain well-calibrated under truly open heterogeneity (unseen detector architectures), how the method scales to streaming/temporal settings where per-frame false positives accumulate, and whether the Beta-sum fusion rule can be made more principled when evidence scales are genuinely incomparable across modalities.
