## Problem

Existing optimization-based 3D generation methods struggle with two fundamental limitations for multi-object scenes: (1) they cannot synthesize physically plausible interaction regions between objects because Gaussian primitives lack geometric constraints governing where and how objects should collide, and (2) Score Distillation Sampling operates independently per view, inevitably producing cross-view hallucinations (the Janus problem) that break multi-view consistency in compositional scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260786/cropped_page2_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a complex text prompt describing multiple objects with spatial relationships (e.g., 'a tie on a beggle') or a single RGB image of a multi-object scene. It outputs a compositional 3D scene parameterized as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with per-object bounding boxes, where Gaussian primitives in interaction regions are geometrically constrained to form physically coherent contact. The pipeline is coarse-to-fine: an LLM plans 3D bounding-box layout, GLIGEN generates a 2D reference image, SAM segments instances, and LGM reconstructs individual objects before joint refinement.

## Method

I²C-3D introduces two key mechanisms within a coarse-to-fine optimization framework. First, the Inclusive Interactive Collisions (I²C) strategy clusters Gaussian primitives via K-means (initialized at bounding-box centers), then applies an In-Box loss that penalizes primitives outside their assigned bounding box and an Interaction Collision loss that, using the triangle inequality, guides the remaining inter-object primitives toward the midpoint region between neighboring boxes (with a margin τ set to 5% of the maximum box diagonal). Second, Multi-View Adaptive Score Distillation Sampling (MV-ASDS) modulates cross-attention maps by scaling spatial-token attention by a factor k=1.25 and applying instance-token segmentation masks to the attention, then distills cross-view consistency priors from Stable Zero123 applied both to the full scene and to individual entities (via bounding-box normalization), thereby enforcing multi-view coherence while preserving fine-grained spatial layout.

## Results

On the ComboVerse benchmark (50 text prompts), I²C-3D achieves the highest CLIP-Score of 0.314 and leads all GPT-4V evaluation dimensions: Prompt Alignment (92.7), Spatial Arrangement (84.5), Geometric Fidelity (82.6), and Scene Quality (85.3). It also dramatically reduces the Janus Rate to 17.3% (vs. 49–70% for baselines) and raises multi-view consistency accuracy (ACC) to 88%, while completing optimization in 28 minutes. In single image-to-3D experiments, I²C-3D performs well on CLIP-Sim and PSNR with competitive SSIM and LPIPS scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I²C-3D outperforms all 14 baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest NeRF-based methods (Latent-NeRF, SJC, Magic3D), it gains 12–42 points on GPT-4V Prompt Alignment and reduces JR by 30–44 points, while also being faster than DreamFusion (28 min vs. 6 h) and Magic3D (28 min vs. 5.3 h). Among 3DGS-based competitors, it surpasses gsgen (CLIP-Score 0.314 vs. 0.304; JR 17.3 vs. 64.2) and GraphDreamer (ACC 88 vs. 48) by wide margins, and beats GaussianDreamer on all GPT-4V sub-scores despite a slightly longer runtime (28 min vs. 15 min). For single image-to-3D, I²C-3D produces more complete geometry and fewer cross-view artifacts than InstantMesh, Hunyuan3D, ReconViaGen, and TRELLIS, which all exhibit object incompleteness or severe multi-view inconsistency. In compositional 3D generation comparisons against Layout-your-3D and ComboVerse, I²C-3D yields more natural interaction regions (e.g., tie-and-collar wearing) and better spatial coherence. A user study across 20 prompts with 50+ objects confirms human preference for I²C-3D over all baselines on five criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Score Distillation Sampling lineage initiated by DreamFusion by introducing two targeted modifications: a geometric collision constraint (I²C) that guides 3D Gaussian primitives into physically plausible interaction regions, and a multi-view attention-modulated SDS variant (MV-ASDS) that distills cross-view consistency from Stable Zero123. It departs from prior compositional 3D generation methods (ComboVerse, Layout-your-3D, GraphDreamer) that focus on spatial layout but produce ambiguous or absent interaction regions and suffer from the Janus problem. The pipeline composes existing building blocks—LLM-based 3D layout estimation, GLIGEN for reference image generation, SAM for segmentation, and LGM for single-object reconstruction—into a coarse-to-fine optimization framework built on ThreeStudio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in 3D content creation: generating multi-object scenes where objects interact naturally (e.g., a tie on a collar) while remaining consistent across viewpoints, a capability that prior SDS-based and layout-based methods lack. The geometric observation that Gaussian primitives concentrate near the midpoint of connecting lines between interacting objects provides an interpretable, lightweight constraint that could inform future interaction-aware 3D generation. However, the method remains per-scene optimization (~28 min), limiting scalability to large scenes, and evaluation is confined to the 50-prompt ComboVerse benchmark with GPT-4V scoring, leaving open questions about robustness to diverse object counts, novel interaction types, and transfer to newer diffusion backbones.
