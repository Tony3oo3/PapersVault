## Problem

Existing model-free 6D pose trackers are limited to single-object scenarios and rely on short-horizon inter-frame feature matching, causing them to fail entirely after complete occlusion. No prior method can simultaneously track multiple unknown rigid objects without CAD models while recovering poses when objects reappear after prolonged disappearance, a critical gap for open-world robotic manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262765/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a monocular RGB-D video stream and a small set of user-provided 2D image points on each target object as initialization; SAM2 then generates per-object segmentation masks from these points. It produces per-frame SE(3) poses for each tracked object (object frame initialized to the camera frame at t=0) and an online object-centric TSDF volume from which a 3D mesh is extracted via marching cubes. The pipeline is fully causal and operates at 2–10 Hz, with runtime scaling roughly linearly in the number of tracked points.

## Method

Point2Pose uses a long-range 2D point tracker (TAPIR or CoTracker) as a persistent data-association module: object-specific query points are tracked jointly in a single pass, providing temporally consistent correspondences across arbitrary temporal gaps without explicit feature matching. Tracked 2D points are lifted to 3D via depth and accumulated into an object-centric keypoint map, with new SuperPoint-detected keypoints added at keyframes using a greedy spatial-diversity-and-trackability criterion. Pose estimation solves a frame-to-map registration via a multi-hypothesis sequential RANSAC+SVD scheme that handles geometric degeneracies (e.g., symmetric objects), followed by TSDF-based hypothesis selection and iterative SDF refinement using dense masked depth. An online factor graph (GTSAM) jointly optimizes inverse keyframe poses and keypoint coordinates for global consistency, while segmented depth observations are fused into a projective TSDF for both reconstruction and pose refinement. The same point-level tracking mechanism naturally extends to multiple objects by aggregating their query sets, and because point identity persists across occlusion, recovery upon re-appearance is immediate without a separate relocalization stage.

## Results

On the paper's own YCBMultiTrack-Real benchmark (multi-object, severe occlusion), Point2Pose achieves 89.43% ADD-S AUC and 74.17% ADD AUC, substantially outperforming BundleSDF (62.08% / 42.34%) and FoundationPose (42.49% / 35.23%). On YCBMultiTrack-Synthetic, Point2Pose reaches 88.67% ADD-S vs. BundleSDF's 76.74%, while FoundationPose with CAD models leads at 98.39%. On the public HO3D benchmark, Point2Pose achieves 94.63% ADD-S (vs. BundleSDF 93.34%) but trails on ADD (80.79% vs. 87.36%) and Chamfer Distance (1.02 cm vs. 0.58 cm), reflecting a trade-off between pose accuracy and model-free generality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BundleSDF (the closest CAD-free baseline), Point2Pose wins decisively on the YCBMultiTrack-Real benchmark by 27.35 points in ADD-S (89.43% vs. 62.08%) and 31.83 points in ADD (74.17% vs. 42.34%), primarily because BundleSDF loses track after complete occlusion and fails to relocalize. On YCBMultiTrack-Synthetic, Point2Pose also leads BundleSDF by 11.93 points in ADD-S (88.67% vs. 76.74%). On the public HO3D benchmark, Point2Pose slightly edges out BundleSDF in ADD-S (94.63% vs. 93.34%) but lags in ADD (80.79% vs. 87.36%) and reconstruction quality (1.02 cm vs. 0.58 cm Chamfer Distance), with the gap concentrated on textureless objects like AP12. On YCBInEOAT, BundleSDF holds a small advantage in both ADD-S (94.51% vs. 92.67%) and ADD (89.07% vs. 85.11%). Against FoundationPose (which uses object CAD meshes), Point2Pose is outperformed on HO3D (94.63% vs. 97.16% ADD-S) and YCBInEOAT (92.67% vs. 96.00%), as expected given the additional geometric prior. However, on YCBMultiTrack-Real—where objects undergo complete occlusion and re-entry—Point2Pose dramatically surpasses FoundationPose by 46.94 points in ADD-S (89.43% vs. 42.49%), demonstrating that even a CAD-based method cannot recover from prolonged occlusion in multi-object dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Point2Pose repurposes long-range 2D point trackers (TAPIR, CoTracker) as a persistent data-association module for 3D pose tracking, departing from the frame-to-frame feature matching paradigm used by BundleTrack and BundleSDF. While BundleSDF introduced joint pose-shape optimization with neural implicit fields, and FoundationPose leverages render-and-compare with CAD models, neither addresses multi-object tracking with complete occlusion recovery in a model-free setting. The method combines this tracking insight with classical TSDF fusion and GTSAM-based factor graph optimization, positioning itself at the intersection of video point tracking, point-cloud registration, and online volumetric reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to track multiple unknown objects through complete occlusion without CAD models or category priors is practically important for open-world robotics and AR, where pre-built geometric models are unavailable. The YCBMultiTrack dataset fills a clear gap in multi-object RGB-D tracking benchmarks with motion-capture ground truth. Key limitations remain: performance degrades on textureless surfaces, the pipeline depends on accurate segmentation, and the classical TSDF representation may underperform modern neural reconstruction. Open questions include whether texture-robust point trackers or learned implicit representations can close the remaining accuracy gap with CAD-based methods while preserving the model-free and multi-object advantages.
