## Problem

Existing AG-ReID methods directly align aerial and ground features in Euclidean space, which enforces view-invariant representations but suppresses view-specific discriminative cues that are essential for reliable retrieval. Additionally, most approaches rely solely on global representations, attenuating mid-level structural and fine-grained local patterns. The paper asks whether a geometric framework can simultaneously maintain cross-view identity consistency and preserve view-conditioned discriminative detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260659/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes person images resized to 256×128×3 from aerial or ground cameras, processed by a frozen CLIP-ViT-B/16 encoder, along with two CoOp-based text prompts (a view-agnostic prompt emphasizing shared identity and a view-aware prompt encoding viewpoint-specific information). At inference, the output is a concatenated retrieval vector formed by mapping the parent (coarse) and child (fine) hyperbolic embeddings back to Euclidean space via the logarithmic map, scaling each by its respective factor, and concatenating them for similarity-based cross-view ranking.

## Method

HiHR comprises three modules: (1) Multi-granularity Feature Extraction pulls class and patch tokens from CLIP ViT layers {5,8,12} and encodes dual-level text prompts; (2) Text-guided Multi-granularity Fusion (TMF) uses cross-attention with text embeddings as queries and multi-layer class tokens as keys/values, aggregates patch tokens with the same attention weights, and fuses everything through a Transformer layer to produce complementary view-agnostic and view-aware feature vectors; (3) Hierarchical Hyperbolic Learning (HHL) maps these two vectors into a Lorentz hyperbolic manifold with learnable scale separation (s₁, s₂) so the view-agnostic embedding sits at a coarser radial level and the view-aware embedding at a finer one. Four entailment-cone constraints (prototype→child, parent-prompt→child-prompt, parent-prompt→parent-visual, child-prompt→child-visual) enforce hierarchical consistency and vision-language alignment. Training combines label-smoothing cross-entropy and triplet loss at the parent level (cross-view positives) with intra-view triplet loss at the child level, plus the entailment regularizations, all optimized end-to-end.

## Results

On AG-ReID and AG-ReID v2, HiHR achieves the best mAP across all six evaluation protocols, reaching 79.21 mAP / 86.06 R1 (A→G) and 87.57 mAP / 91.40 R1 (A→W). On CARGO, the most challenging cross-view A→G setting, HiHR attains 70.53 mAP and 75.53 R1, outperforming the strongest baseline (GSAlign) by +8.98 mAP and +8.66 R1. On LAGPeR, HiHR leads all three protocols, e.g., 33.80 mAP / 46.95 R1 on A→G. The consistent mAP advantage across all four benchmarks indicates improved full-ranking retrieval quality rather than only top-1 gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiHR outperforms all nine baselines on mAP across every evaluation protocol on all four benchmarks. The closest competitor is LATex [32], which is the only method that occasionally matches or exceeds HiHR on Rank-1 (e.g., 89.40 vs. 87.94 R1 on AG-ReID G→A, and 90.18 vs. 87.50 R1 on CARGO G→G), yet HiHR still leads on mAP in every setting. On CARGO A→G, the most difficult cross-view protocol, HiHR achieves 70.53 mAP, surpassing GSAlign [13] (61.55) by +8.98 mAP and SAS-VPReID [31] (57.36) by +13.17 mAP, demonstrating the largest margin of improvement. On LAGPeR, HiHR beats the strongest baseline SAS-VPReID [31] by +3.04 mAP and +3.12 R1 on A→G, and by +3.34 mAP and +3.88 R1 on G→A. Earlier baselines—Explain [16], VDT [33], SD-ReID [26], SeCap [22], DTST [25], and VIF [7]—are consistently outperformed by 2–15 mAP depending on the protocol, with the largest gaps appearing under the most viewpoint-challenging A→G settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-ReID paradigm of using pre-trained vision-language encoders for person retrieval by introducing dual-level CoOp-based prompts (view-agnostic and view-aware) to extract complementary multi-granularity features. It departs from prior AG-ReID methods (VDT, GSAlign, SeCap, LATex) that directly align cross-view samples in a single Euclidean embedding, arguing that such alignment suppresses view-specific discriminative cues. The key conceptual departure is the first use of Lorentz hyperbolic geometry with entailment-cone regularization to impose a coarse-to-fine hierarchical structure, separating identity-level separability (parent) from view-conditioned refinement (child), building on the broader hyperbolic representation learning literature for hierarchical data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode in AG-ReID—excessive cross-view feature collapse that erases view-specific yet discriminative appearance cues—and proposes a geometrically motivated solution that preserves both identity consistency and view-specific detail. The +8.98 mAP gap on CARGO A→G over the strongest baseline is substantial and suggests that hierarchical structure, not just stronger alignment, is the missing ingredient for the hardest cross-view setting. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic hierarchy is truly necessary versus a well-designed Euclidean two-level loss, whether the approach generalizes to other cross-domain retrieval tasks (e.g., RGB-T, multi-sensor), and whether the single-author, single-institution scope limits the robustness of the reported gains.
