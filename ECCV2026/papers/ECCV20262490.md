## Problem

Existing zero-shot semantic navigation methods rely on local observations and greedy frontier search, lacking a structured global semantic representation and explicit long-horizon planning. This leads to myopic exploration, redundant backtracking, and accumulated errors when LLM priors misalign with the actual scene, particularly in long-distance episodes. The paper addresses how to incrementally build a multi-granular semantic topology online from partial observations and use it as a planning state for globally consistent decisions.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes egocentric RGB-D frames with known pose and a textual target description (a category label for ObjectNav or a rich instance description with attributes and spatial relations for TextInstanceNav). It outputs a sequence of discrete low-level actions (move_forward, turn_left, turn_right, stop) while maintaining an online hierarchical 3D scene graph (HSG) with Object-Zone-Region nodes and a hierarchical belief state over that graph. The HSG is updated every N steps via incremental re-clustering, and VLM/LLM inference for semantic descriptions and priors introduces latency that prevents real-time execution.

## Method

The framework couples two modules: (1) an online HSG builder that projects open-vocabulary object instances (YOLOE) onto an occupancy grid, establishes object adjacency via Generalized Voronoi Diagram, and applies spectral clustering on a weighted affinity matrix combining spatial distance and semantic embedding similarity to induce Zone and Region nodes bottom-up; (2) a hierarchical belief-based planner that fuses LLM semantic priors with frontier information-gain evidence into a belief state, then runs POUCT on an HSG-based simulator to perform finite-horizon rollouts estimating long-term expected returns of candidate macro-actions (visit a region or zone). The selected macro-action is executed by a local planner that orders high-gain frontiers via an asymmetric TSP and generates segment-by-segment paths with the Fast Marching Method. The key insight is treating the HSG as a compact state abstraction for explicit lookahead rather than merely a structured prompt for LLM scoring, enabling the agent to evaluate multi-step consequences and avoid the myopia of greedy strategies.

## Results

On ObjectNav, the method achieves SR/SPL of 45.9/19.6 (MP3D), 61.6/33.9 (HM3Dv1), 80.1/39.5 (HM3Dv2), and 69.9/36.4 (HSSD), improving average SR and SPL by 5.4% and 2.3% over the strongest training-free baseline ApexNav. On TextInstanceNav, it reaches 38.1% SR and 16.0% SPL, outperforming the previous SOTA (UniGoal) by 17.9% in SR and 4.6% in SPL. In long-distance episodes (initial shortest-path > 10 m), gains widen to an average of 9.4% SR and 5.0% SPL over ApexNav/UniGoal, confirming the benefit of explicit long-horizon planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary training-free baseline ApexNav [37], the proposed method wins on all four ObjectNav datasets: +6.7 SR on MP3D (45.9 vs 39.2), +2.0 SR on HM3Dv1 (61.6 vs 59.6), +3.9 SR on HM3Dv2 (80.1 vs 76.2), and +8.8 SR on HSSD (69.9 vs 61.1). On TextInstanceNav, the gap is even larger: 38.1% SR vs UniGoal's 20.2% (+17.9 SR) and 16.0% SPL vs 11.4% (+4.6 SPL), attributed to the richer spatial-relational queries that exploit the multi-granularity HSG. Among other training-free methods, the approach also surpasses VLFM [34] (by up to 16.5 SR on HM3Dv2), SG-Nav [32] (by up to 30.5 SR on HM3Dv2), and BeliefMapNav [42] (by 4.7 SR on HSSD). In the long-distance subset (>10 m initial distance), advantages over ApexNav and UniGoal widen to +9.4% SR and +5.0% SPL on average, indicating that the HSG-based rollouts most effectively reduce redundant backtracking when the target is far. Training-based methods (e.g., VLingNav, Uni-NaVid) achieve higher absolute SR on some datasets but require task-specific training data, whereas the proposed method is fully zero-shot and still competitive or superior on most benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot semantic navigation line (ESC, VLFM, SG-Nav, UniGoal, ApexNav) by replacing local greedy frontier search with an online hierarchical 3D scene graph (HSG) as a structured planning state. It builds on POUCT for POMDP planning and FUEL's ATSP-based frontier ordering, but departs from prior scene-graph navigation methods (SG-Nav, UniGoal) that use graphs merely as LLM prompts: here the HSG serves as a belief-state substrate with explicit simulator rollouts for long-horizon return estimation. The incremental GVD + spectral clustering construction extends online scene-graph systems like Kimera and Hydra from flat object-level maps to a multi-granular Object-Zone-Region hierarchy tailored for navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode—myopic exploration and redundant backtracking in long-distance zero-shot navigation—by coupling hierarchical spatial memory with explicit long-horizon lookahead, yielding a 9.4% average SR gain on long-range episodes. The level-horizon ablation (L=2 prefers shorter H than L=1) offers a practical design principle for balancing simulator fidelity against accumulated model bias. Key limitations remain: evaluation is simulation-only with posed RGB-D, VLM/LLM inference prevents real-time execution, and the approach has not been validated on physical robots, leaving open questions about robustness to localization drift and computational feasibility in deployment.
