## Problem

Reconstructing physically plausible 4D multi-object interactions from monocular video is fundamentally ill-posed due to severe depth ambiguities, heavy mutual occlusions, and evolving topological states. Existing approaches either depend on prohibitively expensive multi-camera rigs confined to studio settings, or rely on generative models trained on synthetic data that degrade sharply on real-world interactions, producing implausible deformations, floating contacts, and temporal jittering. No prior method systematically addresses the joint challenge of geometry recovery, interaction-aware dynamics, and long-term memory preservation across complex multi-object scenes from a single viewpoint.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild monocular video as input and produces a temporally coherent 4D Gaussian Splat reconstruction organized into interaction event segments, along with a structured Interaction Knowledge Graph (IKG) encoding object entities, event boundaries, and spatio-temporal relations. The 3D scene is initialized at keyframes flagged by the IKG and propagated forward using 4 orthogonal rendered planes as spatial conditioning for the 4D operator, with a memory bank of size 8 anchoring long-term consistency. The IKG itself is a dynamic directed graph G=(O,E,R) where each relation tuple specifies interacting object pairs, semantic predicates with confidence and distance hints, depth ordering, and categorical relative positions across horizontal, vertical, and depth axes.

## Method

HAT-4D is a multi-agent pipeline where Qwen3-VL first parses the video into an Interaction Knowledge Graph that serves as a formal constraint engine for all downstream generation. A 3D Object Generation Skill uses SAM3D to reconstruct individual entities as 3D Gaussian Splats at keyframes, followed by a Composition Skill that refines 6DoF poses via lightweight optimization and computes exact contact points for physical plausibility. A segment-wise 4D Propagation Operator (L4GM) then extends the composed scene across time, conditioned on both immediately preceding frames and keyframes cached in a memory bank to maintain identity consistency through occlusions. A dedicated Evaluation Skill renders multi-view outputs and scores them against IKG constraints on dynamic plausibility and static fidelity, triggering selective rollback—dynamic errors re-run 4D propagation while static artifacts roll back to 3D generation. Multi-level human-in-the-loop refinement (Gaussian-level attribute editing, region-level local re-generation, object-level re-synthesis) and online prompt-based fine-tuning inject human corrections at sparse keyframes to resolve ambiguities that VLMs alone cannot disambiguate.

## Results

On the MVOIK-4D benchmark, HAT-4D achieves the best LPIPS (0.1433), FVD (830.4), Deform (3.45), Relation (2.57), Intra (0.0005), and Long-term memory (0.0901) scores among all compared methods. The closest competitor, FB4D, trails by 9.1% on LPIPS (0.1576), 7.1% on FVD (889.8), and 32.4% on Deform (2.33). HAT-4D is marginally behind FB4D on CLIP (0.8275 vs. 0.8307), a gap the authors attribute to the longer structured IKG context slightly weakening VLM-based semantic discrimination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAT-4D outperforms all five baselines on six of seven metrics, losing only a narrow margin on CLIP to FB4D (0.8275 vs. 0.8307). Against the strongest baseline FB4D, HAT-4D improves LPIPS by 9.1% (0.1433 vs. 0.1576), FVD by 7.1% (830.4 vs. 889.8), Deform by 48.0% (3.45 vs. 2.33), Relation by 35.7% (2.57 vs. 1.89), Intra by 54.5% (0.0005 vs. 0.0011), and Long-term memory by 92.3% (0.0901 vs. 0.0468). Against L4GM, the 4D propagation backbone, HAT-4D gains 27.2% on LPIPS, 13.8% on FVD, 36.5% on Deform, and 50.6% on Long-term memory, confirming that the IKG-guided agent pipeline and memory bank substantially augment the raw L4GM operator. SV4D and GVFDiffusion, both trained on large-scale synthetic data, show the weakest interaction and memory scores, consistent with the domain gap the paper identifies. STAG4D achieves the best Long score among baselines (0.0650) but still trails HAT-4D by 38.4%, and its Deform score (2.09) is the lowest of all methods, indicating poor handling of physical deformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAT-4D builds on two core generative operators—SAM3D for single-object 3D Gaussian Splat reconstruction and L4GM for segment-wise 4D propagation—but repositions them within a VLM-driven multi-agent orchestration that no prior monocular 4D pipeline provides. It departs from the two dominant paradigms in the field: hardware-intensive multi-camera rigs (DIVA-360, 4DSLoMo) that are unscalable, and end-to-end generative models (SV4D, GVF-Diffusion, STAG4D, FB4D) that target isolated, stylized assets and degrade on real-world multi-object interactions. The Interaction Knowledge Graph as an explicit causality engine, and the multi-level human-in-the-loop refinement extending the Segment-Anything-style data engine from 2D/3D to dynamic 4D, are the paper's principal conceptual departures from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine data bottleneck for Embodied AI and VLA training: high-fidelity 4D multi-object interaction sequences are currently obtainable only via expensive multi-camera studios or unreliable generative models. MVOIK-4D (112 scenes, 77 tasks, 39 interaction categories) and its multi-dimensional evaluation protocol (deformation realism, interaction consistency, long-term memory) fill a concrete gap in benchmarking. However, the framework's reliance on human intervention at keyframes and on a specific VLM (Qwen3-VL) raises open questions about full automation and scalability; the authors themselves note persistent failures on complex flexible deformation and fast non-rigid motion, and the benchmark is self-constructed, limiting external comparability.
