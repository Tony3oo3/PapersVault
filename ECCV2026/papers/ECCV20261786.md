## Problem

Event-based feature matching has not achieved generalizable wide-baseline correspondence because event appearance is motion-dependent (handcrafted encodings change with speed), existing datasets provide only narrow-baseline supervision, and dense spatiotemporal attention scales as O((THW)²), making large-scale training infeasible. No prior event matching model can be deployed zero-shot on unseen datasets with arbitrary viewpoint and motion changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261786/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts two asynchronous event streams (event-to-event) or one event stream paired with one RGB image (event-to-image), resized to a longer side of ~640 px with a 128 ms temporal window. Events are binned into logarithmically-windowed multi-scale voxels (B temporal bins) before tokenization. The output is a semi-dense correspondence map with sub-pixel refinement and a per-pixel matching score map; downstream, these matches feed COLMAP to recover camera poses and 3D point clouds.

## Method

The architecture combines three key components: (1) a Temporal Aggregation Transformer (TAg) that uses separable spatial-temporal attention to reduce cost from O((THW)²) to O(T(HW)²+HWT²), then queries the finest temporal bin against coarser bins to produce a motion-invariant feature map; (2) a Sparsity-aware Event Token Selection (SETS) module that learns per-token halting scores via a ponder loss, adaptively pruning uninformative spatiotemporal tokens and reducing spatial-attention FLOPs by ~21%; and (3) a LoFTR-style coarse-to-fine matching head with cross/self attention, dual-softmax, mutual-nearest-neighbor selection, and sub-pixel refinement. The ViT backbone is initialized from DINO weights, and the full model is trained end-to-end on ~3 million synthetic wide-baseline pairs from E-MegaDepth (generated by warping MegaDepth images through random 6-DoF transforms and converting to events via Vid2E) combined with real M3ED data, enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen datasets.

## Results

On the ECM dataset, Match-Any-Events achieves AUC@5° of 54.61 and 68.90% precision for event-to-event matching, and AUC@5° of 48.58 and 60.73% precision for event-to-image matching. On M3ED, it reaches AUC@5° of 52.99 (event-to-event) and 54.97 (event-to-image), with precisions of 67.40% and 62.33% respectively. On the EDS pose estimation benchmark, the method achieves AUC@5° of 40.4, a 59% improvement over the previous best event-based method (SuperEvent at 25.4). Overall, the paper reports a 37.7% improvement over the previous best event feature matching methods in zero-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest event-specific baseline SuperEvent, Match-Any-Events achieves 2.93× the AUC@5° on ECM (54.61 vs. 11.40) and a 59% improvement on EDS pose estimation (40.4 vs. 25.4 AUC@5°), with the gap widening on ECM due to its larger viewpoint changes. Against MatchAnything (M-A), the best cross-modal foundation model, the method surpasses M-A(E2V) by ~13 points in AUC@5° on ECM event-to-event (54.61 vs. 41.68) and by ~21 points on M3ED event-to-image (54.97 vs. 31.92). VGGT, a 1.2B-parameter 3D foundation model, performs poorly on raw event inputs (AUC@5° of 1.86 on ECM) and only reaches 31.44 with E2V reconstruction, still trailing Match-Any-Events by 23 points. Notably, VGGT(Events) is competitive on the indoor EDS dataset, suggesting structured indoor geometry benefits 3D-reasoning models, but it fails on outdoor wide-baseline scenes. All comparisons are zero-shot: Match-Any-Events is trained once on E-MegaDepth + M3ED and evaluated without fine-tuning on ECM, EDS, or M3ED test splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based matching line (SuperEvent, EventPoint, SD2Event) by replacing detector-based or narrow-baseline architectures with a semi-dense, motion-robust transformer that explicitly separates spatial and temporal aggregation, drawing on video-transformer separable attention (ViViT, ST-Transformer) and adaptive token pruning (ACT, DynamicViT). It departs from prior event matching in two key ways: (1) the Temporal Aggregation Transformer learns multi-timescale features end-to-end rather than relying on handcrafted voxel binning or Concentrate Net-style averaging, and (2) the Sparsity-aware Event Token Selection module exploits the inherent sparsity of events to reduce spatial attention cost. On the supervision side, it moves beyond the narrow-baseline, driving-scene data (M3ED, EDS) that constrained prior methods by introducing E-MegaDepth (synthetic wide-baseline pairs from MegaDepth) and ECM (real hetero-stereo with dense correspondences), enabling zero-shot generalization that no prior event matcher achieved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot wide-baseline capability is practically important for robotics and AR/VR where event cameras must establish correspondences across arbitrary viewpoint changes without per-scene retraining. The released E-MegaDepth (~3M pairs) and ECM datasets fill a critical gap in wide-baseline event supervision that has limited the field. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger baselines or longer temporal windows, how it performs on different event camera hardware with varying noise characteristics, and whether the separable-attention-plus-token-selection recipe transfers to other spatiotemporal modalities (e.g., LiDAR, IMU streams). The paper does not explore real-time deployment constraints beyond reporting 49 ms inference, and the cross-modal event-to-image results, while strong, are evaluated on a single hardware setup.
