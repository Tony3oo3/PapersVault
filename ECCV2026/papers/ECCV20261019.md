## Problem

Existing articulated object reconstruction methods rely primarily on photometric supervision, which cannot disentangle geometry, appearance, and motion parameters during optimization. This entanglement causes the optimizer to incorrectly adjust geometry to explain motion-induced appearance changes, resulting in blurred part boundaries, geometric artifacts, and physically implausible articulation. The paper addresses this gap by introducing explicit structural constraints—spatial coherence and structural connectivity—into the 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (800×800) of an articulated object captured in exactly two configurations (initial and articulated states) along with camera parameters; the sparse-view setting uses only 10 views per state, and real-world experiments use 10 smartphone images with COLMAP poses and SAM masks. It produces per-part 3D Gaussian splats, an oriented bounding box (scale, rotation, translation) for each part, and a rigid articulation transform (rotation + translation) mapping each part from the initial to the articulated state. The OBB parameters are not merely descriptive—they are jointly optimized differentiable variables that drive the structural losses.

## Method

Each part is represented by canonical Gaussians in the unit cube [−1,1]³ coupled with an affine OBB transform T₀ᵏ (encoding scale, rotation, translation) and a rigid articulation transform T₁ᵏ. Two OBB-based structure-aware losses are introduced: a part fitting loss (coverage + margin terms computed on rendered binary structure images) that forces each OBB to tightly enclose its part geometry, and a part contact loss that uses the Separating Axis Theorem to compute differentiable inter-OBB distances and penalize separation between adjacent parts. An extent regularization with learnable softmax per-axis weights encourages anisotropic OBB expansion along under-covered directions. Optimization runs for 10,000 Adam iterations with a progressive schedule: structure-aware losses are active from the start to establish part decomposition, photometric loss is introduced at iteration 400, and contact/extent losses are disabled at iteration 1500 once structures are established. Initialization uses VGGT point clouds with DBSCAN clustering to provide coarse OBBs, making the method tolerant to imperfect initial part segmentation.

## Results

On the PARIS benchmark (100 views), StructureGS achieves 0.088° mean angular error, 0.919×10⁻³ whole-object Chamfer distance, and 43.39 dB PSNR, outperforming all baselines across motion, geometry, and photometric metrics. Under the challenging sparse-view setting (10 views), the advantage is most pronounced: StructureGS maintains 0.701° angular error and 1.320×10⁻³ CD-w, while the nearest competitor (ArticulatedGS) degrades to 8.553° and 19.847×10⁻³ respectively. On the multi-part DTA-Multi dataset, StructureGS reduces angular error from 89.86° (ScrewSplat, Storage scene) to 0.542° and whole-object CD from 38.15 to 3.238 (×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructureGS outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric in both full- and sparse-view settings. Against PARIS [18] (NeRF-based part-wise reconstruction), the gains are largest in geometry: whole-object CD drops from 5.895 to 0.919 (×10⁻³) and movable-part CD from 67.935 to 1.400, while PSNR improves from 29.3 to 43.4 dB. Against ScrewSplat [12] (screw-motion parameterization), StructureGS reduces angular error from 7.79° to 0.088° and CD-w from 13.94 to 0.919; on the multi-part DTA Storage scene, ScrewSplat fails to separate two movable parts (89.86° angle error on one joint) whereas StructureGS achieves 0.542°. The closest competitor is ArticulatedGS [7], which achieves a nearly identical full-view angular error (0.091° vs. 0.088°), but StructureGS clearly wins on geometry (CD-w 0.919 vs. 1.908) and photometry (PSNR 43.39 vs. 37.34). The gap widens dramatically under sparse views: ArticulatedGS's angular error jumps to 8.553° and CD-w to 19.847, while StructureGS remains stable at 0.701° and 1.320, confirming that OBB-based structural constraints effectively regularize the solution when photometric evidence is limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the geometric representation for articulated objects, following the part-wise paradigm established by PARIS, ArticulatedGS, ArtGS, and ScrewSplat. It departs from these methods by introducing explicit structural guidance via oriented bounding boxes (OBBs) per part, replacing the reliance on photometric-only supervision or clustering-based part discovery. The key conceptual departure is formulating two differentiable OBB-based losses—part fitting (spatial coherence) and part contact (structural connectivity via the Separating Axis Theorem)—that directly constrain the geometry-motion entanglement problem identified as a limitation of prior photometric approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental ambiguity in articulated object reconstruction: photometric supervision cannot distinguish whether appearance changes arise from geometry or motion, leading to blurred part boundaries and implausible kinematics. The OBB-based structural prior provides a practical, differentiable solution that yields robust reconstructions even under sparse views (10 images), which is critical for real-world capture with consumer devices. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than two articulation states, to objects with unknown part counts, and to cases where articulation produces minimal visual change (as in the oven failure case). The method's dependence on a known number of parts and coarse initialization via VGGT/DBSCAN also limits fully autonomous deployment.
