## Problem

Existing single-image 3D human reconstruction methods either rely on 2D diffusion-based generation that inherently struggles with 3D consistency, or use 2D-condition token inference that fails to establish explicit correspondence between 2D image features and 3D tokens, causing identity drift in 3D space. The core gap is the lack of a framework that directly infers invisible 3D regions from visible 3D identity tokens while explicitly preserving identity features throughout the entire reconstruction pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB image (segmented via SAM and centralized) along with estimated SMPL-X shape/pose parameters and camera pose (following the SiTH pipeline) as input. It produces either a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Gaussian parameters: position offsets, colors, scales, opacities, rotations) or a mesh (extracted via isosurface from signed distance values at voxel vertices). Inference completes in approximately 0.6 seconds with ~9 GB GPU memory, split into ~0.2 s for the token inference stage and ~0.4 s for the encoder-decoder stage.

## Method

IPRM operates in two stages within an SMPL-based sparse voxel space (n=128) that replaces inefficient SMPL surface point sampling. In stage 1, DINOv2 image features are projected into active voxels and classified as visible (identity) or invisible tokens via back-face culling and ray traversal; an identity-aware 3D token inference module then uses visibility-based cross-attention blocks to infer invisible tokens conditioned solely on visible identity tokens (preserving them unchanged), followed by an adaptive 3D Human Feature that concatenates a visible human feature from the input image with an invisible human feature predicted from the inferred tokens to globally refine all 3D tokens. In stage 2, a Trellis-based encoder-decoder decodes the complete 3D voxel features into 3DGS or mesh, augmented by a 3D ID Adapter: a parallel identity branch (trained to reconstruct only the input view) provides 3D identity condition tokens whose keys and values replace those in the target branch's self-attention layers (inspired by ReferenceNet), applied exclusively to visible identity tokens to prevent identity drift at the token level. Training is two-stage: the inference module is trained from scratch with 3D MSE loss against multi-view-projected DINOv2 ground-truth voxel features, then the encoder-decoder and identity branch are fine-tuned with L1/D-SSIM/LPIPS (3DGS) or depth/normal L1 (mesh) losses plus a single-view identity consistency loss.

## Results

On the Synthetic Data benchmark, IPRM achieves 27.04 PSNR, 0.954 SSIM, and 0.03 LPIPS for 3DGS reconstruction, with an input-view PSNR of 28.90 and Face Consistency of 0.033. On THuman2.1, it reaches 26.74 PSNR and 0.948 SSIM. Inference completes in 0.63 seconds with ~9 GB GPU memory, substantially faster than PSHuman (1 min, ~40 GB) and LHM (2.01 s, ~18 GB). For mesh reconstruction on THuman2.1, IPRM achieves 0.4458 CD and 0.4331 P2S, comparable to the optimization-based PSHuman (0.4399 CD, 0.4077 P2S) while operating in a feed-forward manner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPRM outperforms all baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest feed-forward competitor LHM-0.5B, IPRM gains +1.86 PSNR on Synthetic Data (27.04 vs 25.18) and +2.26 PSNR on input-view (28.90 vs 26.64), while running 3.2× faster (0.63 s vs 2.01 s) with half the memory (~9 GB vs ~18 GB). Compared to the fine-tuned general 3D generator Trellis*, IPRM improves PSNR by +5.37 on Synthetic Data and +5.41 on THuman2.1, with substantially better Face Consistency (0.033 vs 0.058), demonstrating the benefit of SMPL priors and the 3D ID Adapter over stochastic sampling. Against diffusion-based methods (PSHuman, SiTH, DreamGaussian), IPRM achieves 5–9 dB higher PSNR while being 100–300× faster, eliminating the iterative denoising bottleneck. On mesh reconstruction (Table 2), IPRM (CD 0.4458, P2S 0.4331) is within 1.3% of the optimization-based PSHuman (CD 0.4399, P2S 0.4077) but operates feed-forward, and clearly surpasses SIFu (CD 0.5754) and GTA (CD 0.5791). On CustomHumans (Table 3), IPRM leads LHM by +1.02 PSNR and +0.018 SSIM, confirming gains on in-the-wild data beyond synthetic benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPRM directly extends the Large Human Reconstruction Model (LHM) paradigm by replacing its 2D-condition token inference (which updates all SMPL-sampled point tokens via self-attention conditioned on 2D image features) with a 3D token inference that propagates visible identity tokens to invisible tokens via visibility-based cross-attention. The sparse voxel representation is adapted from Trellis's structured 3D latents, and the 3D ID Adapter generalizes the 2D ID-Adapter concept by using a parallel identity branch to provide token-wise 3D guidance, with the key-value replacement mechanism borrowed from Animate Anyone's ReferenceNet design. The key conceptual departure is anchoring the entire reconstruction pipeline in a human-based 3D SMPL feature space rather than relying on 2D generative priors or 2D image conditioning, which the authors argue is the root cause of identity drift and 3D inconsistency in prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IPRM's 0.6-second feed-forward inference with ~9GB memory makes high-quality, identity-preserving 3D human reconstruction practical for interactive applications (gaming, AR/VR, fashion), a significant step beyond the multi-second to multi-minute runtimes of prior SOTA. The 3D ID Adapter and visibility-based cross-attention are modular techniques that could transfer to other parametric-model-conditioned reconstruction tasks. However, the paper acknowledges that out-of-body regions (loose clothing, complex props) remain a fundamental limitation of the SMPL-anchored approach, and the authors suggest combining SMPL priors with 3D generative priors as a future direction. An open question is whether the identity preservation gains generalize to more diverse populations and clothing styles beyond the training distribution.
