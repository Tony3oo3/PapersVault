## Problem

Existing vision-language dataset distillation methods enforce uniform full-dimensional alignment in Euclidean space, which is overly restrictive because image-text correlation is effectively low-rank with shared semantics concentrated in a small subspace and modality-private variation spread across weakly correlated residual directions. These methods also lack explicit control over which representation directions should be aligned versus preserved as modality-private, and Euclidean geometry provides limited inductive bias for the hierarchical structure inherent in multimodal semantics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes real image-text paired datasets (Flickr8k, Flickr30k, MS COCO) and pretrained NFNet + BERT encoder weights, producing a compact synthetic set of N ∈ {100, 200, 500} pairs where each image is a learnable 3×224×224 pixel tensor and each caption is parameterized as continuous 768-dimensional token embeddings with a fixed attention mask (following CovMatch's differentiable text parameterization). The synthetic set is trained so that a retrieval model initialized from scratch on it achieves comparable bidirectional Recall@K to one trained on the full real dataset.

## Method

RAHA lifts projected image-text embeddings onto the Lorentz hyperboloid via an exponential map and computes a hyperbolic InfoNCE contrastive loss (hITC) using geodesic distances to enforce pairwise discriminability on synthetic pairs. For cross-modal structure transfer, it computes the tangent-space cross-covariance of real batches, performs an SVD to extract an adaptive rank-k range subspace (smallest k capturing fraction ρ of squared singular-value energy), and decomposes both real and synthetic features into range and residual components. Row-wise relevance distributions in each subspace are matched from real to synthetic via entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations), with a one-sided energy regularizer on the range (preventing collapse) and a compression regularizer on the residual (penalizing residual energy that exceeds range energy). The total objective combines hITC, range matching + regularizer, and residual matching + compression regularizer, optimized via an online distillation protocol that alternates synthetic-parameter updates with single-step real-data model updates to expose the synthetic set to a slowly evolving encoder geometry.

## Results

On bidirectional image-text retrieval (IR@K, TR@K for K∈{1,5,10}) across Flickr8k, Flickr30k, and MS COCO at budgets N∈{100,200,500}, RAHA attains the best overall mean recall at N=500 on Flickr8k and remains competitive with trajectory-based baselines at all budgets. On cross-architecture transfer (Flickr8k, averaged over IR/TR@{1,5,10}), RAHA achieves mean scores of 6.9 / 8.7 / 12.7 at N=100/200/500, surpassing CovMatch (7.3 / 7.2 / 8.7) at N≥200. On robustness (degradation δ under JPEG, quantization, AWGN, and PGD perturbations), RAHA reduces average δ at N=100 (Image-δ: 4.1 vs. 4.6; Text-δ: 3.7 vs. 4.6 for CovMatch), though CovMatch retains a slight edge at N=200/500.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against coreset-selection baselines (Random, Herding, K-Center, Forgetting), RAHA consistently outperforms at all budgets, confirming the benefit of optimizing synthetic pairs over selecting real ones. Among trajectory-matching methods, LoRS [66] and RepBlend [70] achieve higher cross-architecture transfer at N=100/200 (10.2/10.8 and 8.1/8.9 respectively vs. RAHA's 6.9/8.7), but RAHA surpasses both at N=500 (12.7 vs. 11.0 and 10.7), indicating that rank-aware relevance distillation benefits more from additional synthetic capacity. Against the strongest distribution-matching baseline CovMatch [27], RAHA trades a small retrieval gap at N=100 for substantially better transfer at N=200 (8.7 vs. 7.2) and N=500 (12.7 vs. 8.7), while also improving robustness at N=100 (lower δ on both modalities). The generative baseline EDGE [75] is compared in the appendix. Overall, RAHA does not uniformly dominate every cell but offers a distinct inductive bias—selective range alignment with residual regularization—that pays off most in transfer and moderate-budget retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAHA extends the vision-language dataset distillation (VLDD) line initiated by MTT-VL and refined by LoRS (low-rank similarity) and CovMatch (Euclidean cross-covariance matching) by moving the alignment objective into hyperbolic space and introducing an explicit rank-adaptive range/residual decomposition of batch-level cross-covariance. It draws on hyperbolic vision-language representations (MERU, HDD) for the Lorentz lifting and tangent-space operations, and on entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp) to match row-wise relevance distributions between real and synthetic batches in each subspace. The key departure from prior VLDD methods is the selective treatment of coupled vs. weakly-coupled directions: the dominant range is matched with an energy floor, while the residual is actively compressed, a control mechanism absent from both trajectory-matching and uniform Euclidean statistics-matching baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAHA offers a principled way to allocate limited synthetic capacity across cross-modal directions, which is practically important as VL datasets grow to billions of pairs under increasing privacy, licensing, and provenance constraints. The cross-architecture transfer and perturbation-robustness gains (e.g., mean transfer improving from 7.2 to 8.7 at N=200) suggest the distilled relevance structure is less encoder-specific than uniform Euclidean alignment produces. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic inductive bias yields measurable benefits on datasets with weak semantic hierarchy, whether the approach extends beyond retrieval to generation or grounding tasks, and how the batch-scaling cost of the SVD-plus-Sinkhorn pipeline behaves at much larger budgets or higher-dimensional encoders.
