## Problem

Pretrained video generation models produce high-quality individual frames but suffer from motion artifacts—high-frequency flicker, unnatural trajectories, and semantic drift—because their training objectives optimize per-frame reconstruction rather than temporal coherence. Existing inference-time scaling methods (evolutionary search, noise-trajectory search) can rank candidate samples but cannot induce explicit motion corrections, and retraining frontier backbones is prohibitively expensive. The paper asks whether language-free VFM feedback can be converted from a discrete ranking oracle into a differentiable energy whose gradients locally reshape the sampling trajectory at inference time, without any weight updates.

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-in, training-free module that wraps any pretrained video generator (DiT with ε-prediction or flow matching, or 3D U-Net). For T2V it consumes a text prompt; for I2V it additionally takes a reference image. Internally, at selected denoising timesteps (activated from t=0.5 onward), it decodes low-resolution proxy frames from a clean-latent estimate and feeds them to off-the-shelf VFMs (CLIP, DINOv2, RAFT optical flow) for gradient computation. The output is a single generated video whose sampling trajectory has been steered toward higher motion fidelity, with a user-controllable budget knob N_VFM governing the number of VFM calibration steps.

## Method

The framework has three complementary components operating entirely at inference time. (1) Dynamic Latent Control (DLC) formulates a Boltzmann-reweighted target distribution p*(z₀|c) ∝ p_θ(z₀|c)·exp(−λ_E·L(z₀)) and, at selected steps, estimates a clean latent via local Euler extrapolation, decodes proxy frames, computes a composite loss (optical-flow incoherence + DINO structural deviation + CLIP semantic drift), and adds the backpropagated gradient to the velocity field to steer the trajectory toward lower energy. (2) Dynamic RoPE Control (DRC) uses the same VFM-derived motion signals to dynamically modulate temporal Rotary Position Embeddings—shrinking the temporal receptive field (via dilation ρ_t) to suppress flicker during fast/jittery motion and expanding it for long-range coherence during slow motion—without modifying any weights. (3) Amortization Trajectory Rectification (ATR) exploits the local smoothness of the VFM-induced rectification field R(z,t) to cache the correction at sparse slow steps and reuse it across intervening fast base steps, with a Lipschitz-based error bound guaranteeing bounded approximation error; this reduces VFM forward/backward passes from 50 to 8 per trajectory (8.2× speedup) while preserving nearly identical quality.

## Results

On VBench 2.0 T2V metrics, the proposed method (DLC+DRC+ATR) improves CogVideoX-1.5 Motion Rationality from 33.91% to 36.00% (N=8) and Motion Order from 26.60% to 29.00%, while raising HunyuanVideo Human Anatomy from 88.58% to 90.30% and Mechanics from 76.09% to 79.10% (N=8), all without weight updates. On VBench-I2V, Wan 2.1 + Ours (N=8) reaches 98.10% i2v_subject and 98.50% Motion Smoothness, and Hunyuan I2V Camera Motion improves from 23.00% to 26.50%. Against the dense-gradient baseline Universal Guidance on HunyuanVideo, the ATR-amortized method achieves FVD 328.5 vs. 385.4 at only 1.3× latency overhead (6.8 s) versus 9.3× (48.6 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms both base backbones and search-based baselines (EvoSearch, Noise Trajectory Search) across all advanced motion fidelity metrics. On CogVideoX-1.5, the method gains +2.1–2.4 pp in Motion Rationality and +2.2–2.4 pp in Motion Order over the base model, while search baselines yield only ≤0.3 pp improvement, confirming that ranking-only feedback cannot induce explicit motion corrections. On HunyuanVideo, gains are larger: Human Anatomy improves by +1.72 pp (88.58%→90.30%) and Mechanics by +2.71 pp (76.09%→78.80%) at N=8, far exceeding the ≤0.3 pp from search. Against the dense-gradient baseline Universal Guidance on HunyuanVideo, the ATR-amortized method achieves superior FVD (328.5 vs. 385.4) and Motion (0.70 vs. 0.63) at 1.3× latency overhead versus 9.3×. On I2V (VBench-I2V), Wan 2.1 + Ours (N=8) surpasses the base on all six metrics (e.g., i2v_subject 98.10% vs. 96.30%, Camera Motion 36.80% vs. 33.50%), while EvoSearch and Noise Trajectory Search provide <0.2 pp gains. The method trades a small amount of superficial temporal smoothness (e.g., Motion Smoothness drops from 98.70% to 96.90% on HunyuanVideo) for substantially better motion rationality and dynamic degree, a trade-off the authors attribute to DRC sharpening short-range dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant inference-time scaling paradigm for diffusion/flow models, which relies on search-based verifiers (EvoSearch, Noise Trajectory Search) that rank candidate trajectories but cannot induce explicit corrections. Instead, it repurposes off-the-shelf Vision Foundation Models (CLIP, DINOv2, RAFT) as differentiable energy functions whose gradients directly reshape the sampling velocity field, converting a ranking oracle into a continuous trajectory-steering signal. On the motion-modeling side, it is complementary to both implicit approaches (spatiotemporal attention) and explicit methods (optical-flow conditioning, MoCoGAN-style disentanglement), which bake motion priors into architecture or training; here, motion signals are measured online at inference and fed back without any weight updates. The ATR amortization mechanism draws on the smoothness of the VFM-induced rectification field along the ODE trajectory, a property not previously exploited in guidance literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-in design means the framework can be applied to any pretrained video generator and automatically benefits from stronger VFMs as they become available, without retraining—addressing a practical barrier for deploying motion improvements on frontier models. The ATR amortization (1.3× overhead vs. 10.7× for dense gradients) makes gradient-based calibration practically deployable, which is a meaningful efficiency contribution. Limitations include a small trade-off on superficial temporal smoothness for some backbones, reliance on the proxy-target gradient correlation assumption (Assumption 1) that is not formally verified, and the fact that absolute metric gains, while consistent, remain modest (e.g., ~2% on Motion Rationality). Open questions include whether the approach extends to video editing or long-form generation, whether the Lipschitz smoothness of the rectification field holds across all architectures at scale, and whether DRC's attention modulation generalizes beyond RoPE-based DiTs.
