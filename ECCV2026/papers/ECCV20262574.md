## Problem

Pretrained visual geometry transformers (e.g., VGGT) generalize well to thermal-only reconstruction but produce two disjoint, misaligned 3D reconstructions when RGB and thermal images are processed jointly, because they lack explicit cross-modal geometric consistency. Existing solutions either require synchronous paired captures with expensive sensor rigs or rely on fragile cross-modal feature matching, leaving unpaired RGB-thermal pose estimation and reconstruction from realistic asynchronous data largely unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262574/cropped_page11_idx13.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary mixture of RGB and thermal image frames (thermal-to-RGB ratio τ ∈ [0,1]) that need not share camera poses or capture times, and produces aligned camera intrinsics/extrinsics, per-pixel depth maps with uncertainty, and 3D point maps for all input frames in a single feed-forward pass. Input sequences are partitioned into sub-sequences of length N ∈ {1,2,3,4,6,12} to handle arbitrary frame counts, and the model operates at ~10 FPS on a single A100, matching the speed of the unmodified VGGT backbone.

## Method

SEAR adapts the RGB-pretrained VGGT via two mechanisms: (1) LoRA adapters (rank 64, α=128, ~50M trainable parameters, <5% of the original model) inserted into every linear and multihead-attention layer of the 24-layer alternating-attention module, with all original weights frozen; and (2) two learnable thermal camera tokens (initialized from the RGB camera token weights) appended to DINOv2-extracted thermal tokens, giving the model modality-specific positional signals in both frame-wise and global attention. A critical batching strategy samples RGB and thermal images from the same scene but different viewpoints (no shared poses) with a randomly sampled thermal ratio per batch, preventing the model from exploiting trivial pixel correspondences and forcing it to learn genuine inter-modal geometric alignment. Training uses VGGT's original multitask loss (Huber camera loss + uncertainty-aware depth loss, λ_camera=5.0) on ~15,000 RGB-thermal pairs aggregated from five public datasets, requiring only ~2 days on a single A100.

## Results

SEAR achieves an AUC@30 of 70.0 on the Public Datasets and 62.8 on the SEAR Dataset, substantially outperforming the next-best method MINIMA_ROMA (41.0 and 48.2 respectively). On point cloud quality (LiDAR ground truth), SEAR attains a Chamfer distance of 0.27, PCC of 0.06, and PCA of 0.47, compared to 0.46/0.27/0.66 for the next-best MASt3R. Inference speed is nearly identical to the base VGGT model (9.94 vs. 10.46 FPS on Public Datasets), while hybrid methods like MINIMA_ROMA run at 0.05 FPS—making SEAR roughly 200× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEAR outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric across both evaluation splits. The closest competitor is MINIMA_ROMA (AUC 41.0/48.2), which SEAR surpasses by 29.0 and 14.6 AUC points respectively, while also being ~200× faster (9.94 vs. 0.05 FPS). COLMAP+SPSG shows a deceptively high AUC on the SEAR Dataset (74.4) but registers only 27.9% of frames, meaning its metrics are computed on a small, biased subset; on the Public Datasets its registration drops to 44.7%. Among feed-forward methods, VGGT (the base model) achieves only AUC 22.9/23.3 and produces two disjoint point clouds for RGB and thermal, while MASt3R (30.8/39.1) and DUSt3R (18.9/18.7) similarly fail to align modalities. Hybrid matching-based methods (MA_ELOFTR, MINIMA_ROMA, MP-SfM) suffer from sparse or uncertain cross-modal correspondences, yielding Chamfer distances of 2.82, 1.03, and 1.08 versus SEAR's 0.27. SEAR matches VGGT's inference speed (9.94 vs. 10.46 FPS) while adding only ~50M trainable parameters (<5% of the base model), confirming that the LoRA-based adaptation introduces negligible overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEAR builds directly on VGGT's alternating-attention transformer architecture and DINOv2 tokenizer, applying LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning—a technique well-established in NLP and visual segmentation but previously unexplored for 3D geometric foundation models. It departs from prior RGB-T reconstruction work (ThermoNeRF, ThermalGaussian, ThermalNeRF) that either assumes synchronous paired captures with known poses or relies on post-hoc cross-modal feature matching (COLMAP, MINIMA, ELoFTR), by instead learning cross-modal alignment end-to-end with minimal parameter updates. The batching strategy that prevents the model from exploiting trivial RGB-thermal correspondences is a novel training design choice not present in prior multimodal 3D work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fewer than 5% of VGGT's parameters need updating to bridge the RGB-thermal modality gap makes multimodal 3D reconstruction accessible without large-scale multimodal retraining or expensive synchronous sensor setups, which is practically significant for infrastructure inspection and low-light mapping. However, ground-truth poses for most training/evaluation datasets are derived from VGGT itself, introducing a circularity that limits the strength of the quantitative claims. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other modalities (multi-spectral, LiDAR), whether the small SEAR dataset (9 scenes) is sufficient to validate generalization, and whether the learnable thermal camera token's marginal contribution (p=0.24 vs. no token) suggests the architecture could be further simplified.
