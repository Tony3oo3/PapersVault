## Problem

Existing medical image modality translation methods (GANs, diffusion bridges, transformers) operate at the pixel or feature level, leaving anatomical geometry entangled with modality-specific textures. This implicit coupling causes structural distortions and anatomically inconsistent results that limit clinical diagnostic utility, as no prior approach explicitly separates invariant geometric structure from appearance during cross-modal synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262128/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 source MRI slice (T1, T2, or PD-weighted) and a discrete target modality label as input, producing a 256×256 target-modality MRI slice. The source image is first encoded into a sparse set of N=1024 2D Gaussian tokens (each with 5 geometric parameters plus texture features) via parameter compression from the full H×W grid, and the target modality label is converted to a continuous embedding via sinusoidal positional encoding. The final output is rendered from these sparse tokens through a differentiable Gaussian splatting operation followed by a lightweight decoder.

## Method

Proto-Gaussian uses a two-stage pipeline built on explicit 2D Gaussian representations. In Stage 1 (Geometric Prototype Learning), a shared Gaussian Bank of K prototypes is learned via self-supervised alignment: position parameters are fixed as absolute grid coordinates while shape parameters (σx, σy, ρ) are synthesized by attention-weighted combination of prototypes, trained with intermodality self-consistency loss, cross-modal alignment loss, and Diz-Zenzo structural tensor regularization. In Stage 2 (Texture-Aware Synthesis), a Label-Adaptive Token Refinement (LATR) module takes the source image, extracted geometric prototypes, and target modality embedding to produce a small geometry adjustment ΔG and target texture features, which are then rendered into a dense image by a fully differentiable 2D Gaussian splatting renderer that computes weighted Gaussian kernel contributions at every pixel. The key distinction from prior work is the explicit parametric decoupling: geometry lives in the Gaussian covariance parameters (modality-invariant, shared across modalities) while appearance lives in separate texture feature vectors, enabling structure-preserving texture transfer without architectural entanglement.

## Results

On the IXI dataset (T1↔T2), Proto-Gaussian achieves PSNR of 30.57 dB (T1→T2) and 29.69 dB (T2→T1), SSIM of 0.9512 and 0.9546, LPIPS of 0.0493 and 0.059, and FID of 17.35 and 25.38, respectively. On BraTS (T1↔T2), it reaches PSNR 28.52/27.94 dB, SSIM 0.9428/0.9317, LPIPS 0.0532/0.0784, and FID 20.83/43.68. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (SelfRDB) by 1.33–1.43 dB in PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 26.3–36.4% on IXI, while on BraTS it gains 1.03–1.48 dB in PSNR with 32.3–42.0% LPIPS reduction. Proto-Gaussian also uses only 6.38 M parameters, roughly 88% fewer than Pix2pix (54.40 M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proto-Gaussian outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three evaluation settings (IXI T1↔T2, IXI T1↔PD, BraTS T1↔T2). The closest competitor is SelfRDB [2], a diffusion-bridge method, which Proto-Gaussian surpasses by 1.33 dB (T1→T2) and 1.43 dB (T2→T1) in PSNR on IXI, while cutting LPIPS from 0.0766 to 0.0493 (35.6% reduction). MaskGAN [36] is competitive on FID for T1→T2 on IXI (20.27 vs. 17.35) but trails significantly on PSNR (28.81 vs. 30.57) and SSIM. Among GAN-based baselines, SAGAN [57] and Pix2pix [22] show the weakest structural fidelity (SSIM 0.9291 and 0.9102, respectively), confirming the paper's claim that adversarial training struggles with fine anatomical detail. Transformer-based methods TransUNet [8] and ResViT [11] achieve mid-range LPIPS but lag on PSNR by 2–3 dB, reflecting their implicit representation's inability to explicitly preserve geometry. On BraTS, where pathology introduces greater structural complexity, Proto-Gaussian's advantage narrows slightly (1.03 dB over MaskGAN on T1→T2 PSNR) but remains consistent across all four metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the 2D Gaussian splatting paradigm from image super-resolution (GaussianSR, AAAI 2025) and the parametric Gaussian primitive formulation from 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., ACM TOG 2023) to the medical image modality translation domain. It departs fundamentally from the dominant GAN-based (Pix2Pix, SAGAN, CycleGAN), diffusion-based (SelfRDB, Contourdiff), and Transformer-based (TransUNet, ResViT) approaches, all of which operate at the pixel or feature level with implicit representations. The key conceptual shift is treating anatomical geometry as an explicit, parametric 2D Gaussian field that can be learned modality-invariantly and then re-rendered with modality-specific texture, rather than learning an end-to-end pixel-to-pixel mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit structure-texture disentanglement via Gaussian prototypes addresses a persistent failure mode in medical image translation—structural distortion—that implicit methods have struggled to resolve, and the 6.38M parameter count (88% fewer than Pix2Pix) makes the approach attractive for resource-constrained clinical deployment. However, the method is limited to 2D axial slices without inter-slice continuity, and the evaluation is confined to two relatively small datasets (100 and 150 subjects) with only pairwise translation tasks. Open questions include whether the Gaussian representation generalizes to 3D volumetric translation, whether it handles many-to-one modality synthesis, and whether the structural fidelity gains translate into measurable clinical diagnostic utility.
