## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs struggle with long-form audio: they cannot reliably understand inputs beyond a few minutes, and the large token-rate gap between text (~2-3 words/s) and speech (25 tokens/s) causes misaligned prosody, error accumulation, and degraded timbre consistency in long-form speech generation. No prior open-source system simultaneously supports hour-scale audio understanding, 10+ minute speech synthesis, and zero-shot voice cloning with stable speaker identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261690/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts text, images, video, and audio inputs (up to 60+ minutes of audio) alongside a short reference audio clip for voice cloning, and produces both text responses and streaming speech audio (10+ minutes). Speech tokens are generated at 25 Hz via an FSQ tokenizer, and the reference audio conditions the SpeechLM for zero-shot timbre transfer. The MLLM and SpeechLM operate in a streaming pipeline where text tokens flow into the SpeechLM buffer in real time, enabling low-latency interactive use.

## Method

MGM-Omni uses a dual-track 'brain-mouth' architecture: a Qwen2.5-VL-based MLLM handles omnimodal understanding (with a dual audio encoder fusing Qwen2-Audio and Belle-Whisper features via cross-attention information mining), while a Qwen3-based SpeechLM with a 6-block TTS-Adapter generates speech tokens autoregressively. The key generation innovation is Chunk-Based Parallel Decoding: text is segmented into sentence-aligned chunks (50-150 tokens), and within each chunk the model predicts k=4 speech tokens in parallel per step by extending the vocabulary to |V_text| + k·|V_speech|, with a 4-token delay to maintain alignment. This narrows the text-speech token-rate gap, reduces error accumulation, and accelerates inference by ~3×. Speech tokens are converted to audio via CosyVoice2's flow-matching model and a HiFi-GAN vocoder. Training uses ~400k hours of audio with LoRA for the MLLM and a two-stage (frozen-LLM adapter pre-training, then joint fine-tuning) procedure for the SpeechLM.

## Results

On the proposed Long-TTS-Eval benchmark, MGM-Omni-TTS-2B achieves the lowest error rates (EN WER 4.98, ZH CER 5.58) and fastest inference (RTF 0.19) among all compared long-form TTS systems, outperforming CosyVoice2 (EN WER 14.80, RTF 0.34), MOSS-TTSD (EN WER 8.69, RTF 0.23), Higgs-Audio-v2 (EN WER 27.09, RTF 0.33), and VibeVoice (EN WER 19.23, RTF 0.45). For long-form timbre consistency, MGM-Omni-TTS-2B achieves the highest segment-level speaker similarity (EN 84.66%, ZH 94.32%) with the lowest variance (EN std 1.63, ZH std 0.97), indicating the most stable voice preservation over 10+ minute generations. On short-form Seed-TTS-Eval, MGM-Omni-TTS-4B attains EN WER 2.22 and ZH CER 1.18 with speaker similarity 0.686/0.758, matching or exceeding CosyVoice2 and Qwen2.5-Omni. For audio understanding, MGM-Omni-32B achieves LibriSpeech clean WER 1.5 and AIR-Bench average 6.5, competitive with Qwen2.5-Omni while adding long-audio and speech-generation capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On long-form TTS (the paper's primary differentiator), MGM-Omni-TTS-2B decisively outperforms all four baselines: it reduces EN WER by 66% vs. CosyVoice2 (4.98 vs. 14.80), by 43% vs. MOSS-TTSD (4.98 vs. 8.69), by 82% vs. Higgs-Audio-v2 (4.98 vs. 27.09), and by 74% vs. VibeVoice (4.98 vs. 19.23), while also achieving the lowest RTF (0.19 vs. 0.23–0.45). On timbre consistency, MGM-Omni leads all baselines in both mean similarity and stability (e.g., ZH SIM 94.32% ± 0.97 vs. CosyVoice2's 92.23% ± 1.82 and Higgs-Audio-v2's 89.00% ± 4.88). For short-form TTS on Seed-TTS-Eval, MGM-Omni-TTS-4B matches or slightly beats CosyVoice2 (EN WER 2.22 vs. 2.57; ZH CER 1.18 vs. 1.45) and Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (EN WER 2.22 vs. 2.33; ZH CER 1.18 vs. 1.42). On ASR, MGM-Omni-32B beats Whisper-large-v3 (LibriSpeech clean 1.5 vs. 1.8), Lyra (1.5 vs. 2.0), VITA-1.5 (1.5 vs. 3.4), and Ola (1.5 vs. 1.9), but is slightly behind Qwen2-Audio on LibriSpeech clean (1.5 vs. 1.3) and Qwen2.5-Omni on AISHELL CER (1.8 vs. 1.5). On AIR-Bench audio QA, MGM-Omni-7B (avg 6.5) outperforms Qwen2.5-Omni (5.7), Ola (6.4), and all other omni-LLMs. On visual benchmarks, MGM-Omni-7B largely preserves Qwen2.5-VL's capability (ChartQA 87.8 vs. 87.3, VideoMME 66.3 vs. 65.1) with minor drops on AI2D (83.2 vs. 85.8) and MME (2285 vs. 2347), confirming the dual-track design does not significantly degrade vision.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGM-Omni builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the multimodal reasoning backbone and Qwen3 as the speech generation backbone, adopting CosyVoice2's FSQ tokenizer and flow-matching vocoder, and following Lyra's two-stage audio-to-text pre-training pipeline. Its key architectural departure is the 'brain-mouth' dual-track design that cleanly decouples omni-modal understanding (MLLM) from real-time speech synthesis (SpeechLM), in contrast to prior omni-LLMs like Qwen2.5-Omni and Lyra that use a single unified model or tightly coupled thinker-talker pipelines. The Chunk-Based Parallel Decoding mechanism is the paper's central technical contribution, extending the parallel decoding idea (previously applied mainly to RVQ tokenizers) to FSQ tokenizers and combining it with sentence-level chunking to bridge the text-speech token-rate gap for long-form generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is data efficiency: MGM-Omni achieves competitive long-form TTS quality with ~400k hours of audio, far less than the 1M–10M hours used by concurrent long-form TTS systems, with ablations attributing this primarily to chunk-based decoding. The introduced Long-TTS-Eval benchmark fills a gap in systematic evaluation of long-horizon and text-normalization-robust TTS, which prior benchmarks (Seed-TTS-Eval) do not stress-test. However, the paper acknowledges limitations in visual understanding relative to specialized VLMs, lack of fully duplex speech interaction, and no support for agentic tool use, leaving open questions about whether the dual-track design can be extended to real-time bidirectional voice communication and fine-grained prosodic control.
