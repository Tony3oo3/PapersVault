## Problem

Existing Referring 3D Gaussian Splatting (R3DGS) methods are strictly confined to single-target queries, failing to handle the ambiguity of real-world instructions that may reference zero, one, or multiple objects. Moreover, current R3DGS paradigms suffer from two fundamental bottlenecks: they operate on 2D rendered pixels rather than discrete 3D Gaussian points (lacking intrinsic 3D geometric understanding), and they require per-scene optimization to embed heavy semantic features into millions of Gaussians, incurring prohibitive computational and memory costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260237/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-reconstructed 3DGS scene (a set of explicit 3D Gaussians with position, covariance, color, and opacity), a free-form natural language instruction, and the multi-view images used for reconstruction. It produces a binary foreground/background label for every individual 3D Gaussian in the scene, which can be rendered into 2D pixel-level segmentation masks from any viewpoint. Notably, the output is dynamic: the method may return an empty mask (no target present), a single-target mask, or a multi-target mask, all without any per-scene training or additional feature storage beyond the original 3DGS parameters.

## Method

ZeroSplat is a training-free, zero-feature three-stage pipeline that lifts 2D VLM priors into 3D space via geometric constraints rather than embedding semantic features into Gaussians. Stage 1 selects curvature-salient keyframes from the scene and uses a VLM (qwen3-vl-30b) in two passes: first extracting concise semantic labels from the verbose instruction, then generating 2D bounding boxes as spatial anchors. Stage 2 uses SAM3 to produce multi-view 2D masks (with adaptive confidence-based view selection and sub-mask merging), then back-projects them into 3D by weighting each Gaussian's contribution via its volume-rendering transmittance and opacity across all valid views, followed by cross-view background cropping to remove geometric artifacts. Stage 3 applies cross-view bounding-box verification to prune Gaussians that fall outside VLM-predicted boxes in most keyframes, and fills internal cavities using a KD-Tree-based 3D KNN spatial label diffusion that adapts to local point density. The entire pipeline requires no gradient-based optimization, no per-Gaussian semantic feature storage, and runs on a single GPU with ~10 GB peak VRAM.

## Results

On the newly introduced GR3DGS task, ZeroSplat achieves 50.8 mIoU on GR-LERF (pixel-level) and 41.2 mIoU on GR-ScanNet (point-level), substantially outperforming the best pixel-based baseline (GS-Grouping, 28.3 mIoU on GR-LERF) and the best point-based baseline (InstanceGaussian, 24.5 mIoU on GR-ScanNet). On the standard single-target R3DGS benchmark (Ref-LERF), ZeroSplat reaches 32.7 mIoU, surpassing the fully supervised ReferSplat (29.2 mIoU) by +3.5 mIoU despite being entirely training-free. On open-vocabulary ScanNet, ZeroSplat attains 44.5/43.7/49.7 mIoU (19/15/10-class), leading all point-based competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroSplat decisively outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary GR3DGS task: on GR-LERF it leads the best pixel-based method (GS-Grouping, 28.3) by +22.5 mIoU and the best point-based method (LUDVIG, 23.8) by +27.0 mIoU; on GR-ScanNet it surpasses InstanceGaussian (24.5) by +16.7 mIoU. On the single-target R3DGS benchmark (Ref-LERF), ZeroSplat (32.7) beats the dedicated referring method ReferSplat (29.2) by +3.5 mIoU without any per-scene training. On open-vocabulary ScanNet, ZeroSplat leads InstanceGaussian (40.7/42.5/47.9) by 3.8/1.2/1.8 mIoU across 19/15/10-class settings. However, on open-vocabulary LERF (pixel-level), ZeroSplat (52.4) trails the best pixel-based methods 3DVLGS (62.0) and Occam's LGS (61.3) by roughly 9–10 mIoU, though it still leads all point-based baselines (best: LUDVIG at 50.4). The paper also highlights an efficiency advantage: ZeroSplat requires zero scene optimization, zero extra feature storage, and ~10 GB peak VRAM, compared to 1–2 hours of training and ~3 GB additional storage for most baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scene representation by introducing a training-free, zero-feature pipeline that lifts 2D VLM and SAM3 priors into 3D space via multi-view geometric constraints (alpha-compositing back-projection, cross-view verification, and KNN spatial diffusion). It departs fundamentally from the two dominant paradigms in 3DGS semantic understanding: pixel-based methods (LangSplat, ReferSplat) that operate on rendered 2D images and lack intrinsic 3D point-level reasoning, and point-based methods (OpenGaussian, InstanceGaussian, Dr.Splat, LUDVIG) that require per-scene optimization to embed high-capacity semantic features into millions of Gaussians. Conceptually, ZeroSplat repurposes the volume rendering equation not as a rendering tool but as a semantic assignment mechanism, eliminating the need for any learned 3D feature storage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GR3DGS task and its two benchmarks (GR-LERF, GR-ScanNet) fill a clear gap: prior R3DGS work assumed exactly one target per query, which is unrealistic for embodied AI and interactive scene understanding where instructions may reference zero, one, or many objects. The training-free, zero-feature design (no per-scene optimization, no extra storage, ~10 GB peak VRAM vs. ~20–28 GB for baselines) offers a practical pathway for deploying language-grounded 3D understanding on resource-constrained devices. Open questions include scalability to large outdoor scenes, sensitivity to VLM hallucinations in the label-extraction stage, and whether the multi-view geometric constraints remain reliable under sparse or highly occluded viewpoints. The benchmarks, while well-constructed, cover only a handful of indoor scenes, limiting generalizability claims.
