## Problem

Hybrid SfM systems that combine learned priors with traditional bundle adjustment achieve the best reconstruction accuracy, but their reliance on dense pairwise correspondences makes them prohibitively slow and costly for online applications like VSLAM. Moreover, existing methods are typically dedicated to either offline unordered collections or online streaming, with no unified framework that handles both scenarios with shared hyperparameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260284/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a set of unposed RGB images (assumed same width W and height H) in either streaming or unordered fashion, optionally accepting known intrinsics or depth maps. It outputs per-image camera extrinsics (SE(3) poses), a single focal length per image (or globally for video), dense adjustable depth maps parameterized as exp[αF + β] over m depth channels, and sparse multi-view point tracks. The depth maps are not fixed predictions but are refined during optimization, with α and β jointly tuned alongside cameras and 3D points.

## Method

BLASt3R couples three components: (1) a coarse-to-fine multi-view matcher that uses a ViT encoder-decoder with a streaming memory bank to perform patch-level matching (cosine similarity with learnable threshold) followed by pixel-level matching restricted to matched patches, achieving linear complexity in sequence length; (2) a monocular ViT-Large network (initialized from DINOv3) that predicts a raymap and m depth channels, forming an 'adjustable pointmap' Y = R̃·exp[αF + β] where the linear combination coefficients α and offset β are optimized during BA; (3) a unified depth-regularized bundle adjustment that minimizes a hybrid reprojection error combining 2D pixel residuals with a log-space depth residual weighted by ω, solved via damped Levenberg-Marquardt with Schur complement elimination of 3D points on GPU. The depth regularization interpolates between classical sparse BA (ω=0) and dense SLAM (ω>0), and the same hyperparameters serve both online VSLAM and offline SfM.

## Results

On TUM-RGBD (fr1 subset), BLASt3R achieves an average ATE RMSE of 3.0 cm in uncalibrated mode, outperforming the best calibrated system (GIORIE-SLAM, 3.6 cm) and the best uncalibrated system (MUSt3R-512, 4.5 cm). In calibrated mode, BLASt3R reaches 2.5 cm average ATE, beating all prior calibrated methods. On ETH3D-MVS, BLASt3R achieves 98.5% mAA@30, substantially exceeding VGGT-BA (88.4%) and MASt3R-SfM (81.5%), while running orders of magnitude faster than matching-based baselines (e.g., 0.5 min vs. 1.5 min for 25 views on Tanks&Temples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLASt3R's uncalibrated VSLAM (3.0 cm avg ATE on TUM-RGBD) surpasses all calibrated systems including GIORIE-SLAM (3.6 cm), DROID-SLAM (3.8 cm), and DPV-SLAM++ (5.4 cm), and beats the best uncalibrated feed-forward baselines MUSt3R-512 (4.5 cm) and ViPE (4.8 cm) by 1.5–1.8 cm. On ETH3D-SLAM, BLASt3R achieves 1.2 cm avg ATE, outperforming ViPE (1.8 cm) and MUSt3R-512 (4.2 cm). For offline SfM on ETH3D-MVS, BLASt3R's 98.5% mAA@30 exceeds VGGT-BA (88.4%) by ~10 points and MASt3R-SfM (81.5%) by ~17 points, while running in 0.5–2.1 minutes versus 1.5–140 minutes for VGGT-BA on Tanks&Temples. On dense reconstruction (7-Scenes/ETH3D), BLASt3R matches or beats π³ and VGGT on accuracy and completeness, with notably better Normal Consistency on ETH3D (0.881 mean vs. 0.815 for π³). The monocular depth ablation shows that the adjustable multi-channel depth (nz=16) improves Rel error from 8.65 to 4.87 on average, approaching MoGe-2 (5.62) and surpassing it on several datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLASt3R extends the latent memory mechanism of MUSt3R [16] to the dense pixel-matching paradigm of MASt3R [53], introducing a coarse-to-fine multi-view matcher that streams over arbitrary image sequences while maintaining constant per-frame cost via retrieval. It adopts DUSt3R's [108] pointmap prediction and maximum spanning tree initialization, but departs from the feed-forward family by coupling these learned priors to a regularized bundle adjustment that jointly optimizes cameras, tracks, and adjustable depth parameters. The key conceptual departure from prior hybrid systems (MASt3R-SfM, VGGT-BA, MASt3R-SLAM) is the unified BA formulation with depth regularization that interpolates between sparse SfM and dense SLAM, enabling a single pipeline and shared hyperparameters for both online VSLAM and offline unordered SfM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single uncalibrated pipeline that outperforms all previously calibrated VSLAM systems and scales to thousands of views with near-feed-forward speed while matching or exceeding the accuracy of much slower matching-based methods. The adjustable pointmap concept (multi-channel depth with corrective coefficients optimized during BA) is a reusable idea that could benefit other dense reconstruction systems. An interesting negative result is that on MVS-heavy datasets with reliable triangulation baselines (ETH3D, Tanks&Temples), standard COLMAP BA actually outperforms the depth-regularized variant, suggesting the depth term is most valuable in degenerate cases (pure translation, static viewpoint) common in online settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-camera rigs, whether the depth regularization can be made adaptive per-scene rather than relying on a fixed ω schedule, and how the method behaves under severe photometric changes or dynamic content beyond what current benchmarks capture.
