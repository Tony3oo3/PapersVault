## Problem

Detector-based and query-based scene graph generation methods exhibit complementary predictive behaviors—detector-based models excel on triplets within their detector's reachability but fail on unreachable instances, while query-based models capture unreachable triplets at the cost of precision on reachable ones—yet this complementarity has never been systematically quantified or exploited within a unified framework. The paper identifies this gap through a controlled experiment measuring micro-Recall on detector-reachable (Det-T) versus unreachable (UDet-T) triplet subsets, revealing that neither mechanism alone achieves strong performance on both.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a ranked set of Top-K triplets, each consisting of a subject bounding box and category, a predicate label, and an object bounding box and category, with a composite score computed as the product of subject, object, and predicate probabilities. At inference, predictions from both query groups are concatenated and deduplicated via NMS before Top-K selection. The object detector uses 200 entity queries, the Entity Pair Selector retains 300 top pairs for TD-Qs, and 800 BU-Qs are allocated for global exploration, all processed through a 4-layer transformer decoder.

## Method

Dual-SGG integrates detector-based and query-based reasoning into a single triplet decoder via a dual-query design: top-down queries (TD-Qs) are initialized from entity pairs selected by an Entity Pair Selector (EPS) that scores pairs by spatial and content compatibility, while bottom-up queries (BU-Qs) use learnable content embeddings and center-biased frozen anchors to explore triplets globally without detector conditioning. Both query groups are jointly updated through stacked transformer blocks, with self-attention masks blocking information flow from TD-Qs to BU-Qs to preserve their distinct predictive behaviors. Training assigns TD-Qs labels directly from the object detector's Hungarian-matched entity pairs (ensuring detector-conditioned supervision only when both instances are reachable), while BU-Qs use standard one-to-one Hungarian matching over triplets. The EPS is trained with focal losses on spatial and content logits, and the overall objective combines object detection, EPS, TD, and BU losses with unit weights to avoid hyperparameter sensitivity.

## Results

On the Visual Genome test set, Dual-SGG (ResNet-50 backbone, 84.7M params) achieves R@50/100 of 33.5/38.5, mR@50/100 of 10.3/12.3, and F@50/100 of 15.8/18.6, outperforming the second-best ResNet-based method EGTR (R@50=30.2, F@50=12.5) across all standard SGG metrics. With logit adjustment (τ=0.3) for unbiased evaluation, Dual-SGG+LA attains state-of-the-art F@50/100 of 23.6/27.4 and mR@50/100 of 22.2/25.2, surpassing Salience-SGG (F@50=22.1, F@100=26.2). On OIv6, Dual-SGG achieves the best score of 52.9 (vs. 51.8 for Salience-SGG), and on GQA-200, Dual-SGG+LA (τ=0.3) sets new SOTA with mR@50/100 of 19.0/21.1 and F@50/100 of 20.4/23.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among ResNet-backbone SGG models on VG, Dual-SGG outperforms EGTR by +3.3 R@50 and +3.3 F@50, and surpasses RelTR by +6.0 R@50 while matching its mR@100 (12.3). Compared to the heavier X101-FPN-based DRM w/o DKT, Dual-SGG is within 0.5 R@50 and 0.4 R@100 while using far fewer parameters (84.7M vs. >300M) and running at 14.0 FPS. In the unbiased setting, Dual-SGG+LA (τ=0.3) achieves the best F@100 (27.4) and mR@100 (25.2) on VG, beating Salience-SGG by +1.2 F@100 and +3.6 mR@100, and Hydra-SGG by +2.7 F@100. On GQA-200, Dual-SGG+LA (τ=0.3) leads all unbiased methods with F@50/100 of 20.4/23.2, exceeding Salience-SGG (19.2/21.7) and DRM (18.7/21.3). On OIv6, Dual-SGG achieves the highest score (52.9) and wmAPrel (46.0), edging out Salience-SGG (51.8, 45.6) and Hydra-SGG (50.1, 42.8) across all reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two dominant SGG paradigms—detector-based models (following EGTR [11]) and query-based models (following Iterative-SGG [15])—and the anchor-based query representation from DAB-DETR [28]. Its key departure is the systematic, controlled analysis of these two mechanisms from the perspective of 'detector-conditioned reachability,' a diagnostic lens not previously formalized in the SGG literature. The dual-query design (TD-Qs + BU-Qs) is conceptually inspired by DN-DETR/DINO-DETR's auxiliary queries but repurposed for a fundamentally different goal: explicitly modeling complementary prediction behaviors within a single decoder rather than stabilizing training. Unlike prior fusion approaches that merge outputs of separate models via probabilistic ensembling, Dual-SGG integrates both reasoning paths end-to-end, avoiding score-calibration issues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative evidence that detector-based and query-based SGG methods are genuinely complementary in a measurable, exploitable way, and that joint training in a unified decoder outperforms all tested post-hoc fusion strategies (max, average, normalization). This suggests that future SGG architectures should consider multi-mechanism designs rather than choosing one reasoning paradigm. A noted limitation is that the complementarity does not recover every ground-truth triplet from both specialized models (Fig. 4c), and the benefit, while consistent, is moderate in absolute terms (e.g., +1.0 R@100 over EGTR). The approach's reliance on a pre-trained object detector as a conditioning signal also means its advantage is partially contingent on detector quality, though the Swin-B experiment shows the gain persists under stronger detection.
