## Problem

Existing document layout analysis methods either rely on fragmented multi-stage pipelines that propagate detection errors into reading-order prediction, or use computationally heavy generative Transformers (1–3B parameters) that are impractical for real-time deployment. Critically, all prior approaches are confined to axis-aligned bounding boxes, which fail to provide pixel-level isolation of layout elements under real-world geometric distortions such as page warping, perspective skew, and non-uniform illumination, and none jointly models detection, segmentation, and reading order within a single efficient architecture.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB document image as input and, in one non-autoregressive forward pass, outputs for each detected layout element: a category label (from 25 classes), an axis-aligned bounding box, a pixel-level segmentation mask, and a globally consistent reading-order sequence. The pixel-level masks are the key differentiator from prior box-only methods, enabling precise isolation of text, formulas, and tables in warped or skewed regions before they are passed to a downstream OCR engine. The 33M-parameter model runs at 132.1 FPS on an A100 GPU at batch size 32, positioning it as a real-time front-end module rather than a standalone parser.

## Method

RT-DocLayout extends RT-DETR by adding a mask prediction head and integrating reading-order prediction directly into the query-based Transformer decoder, so that each of N object queries simultaneously classifies, regresses a bounding box, generates a high-fidelity mask, and contributes to a pairwise precedence matrix. Reading order is computed via an antisymmetric scorer S_{i,j} = (q_i^T W_q^T W_k q_j − q_j^T W_q^T W_k q_i)/√d_h over refined query embeddings, then converted to a global sequence by a voting-based ranking that sums sigmoid-transformed incoming edges per element. Training uses Hungarian bipartite matching and a weighted multi-task loss combining focal loss, L1+GIoU, BCE+Dice (with MaskDINO-style point sampling), and a locality-aware GCE loss on pairwise order targets, with the order loss weighted 50× to counteract gradient dilution across O(N²) pairs. A two-stage Physical Spatial Aware Data Augmentation pipeline—first applying mesh-based non-rigid surface warping (sinusoidal displacement fields) and then a composite homography for perspective/rotation/shear—synthesizes realistic distortions while preserving pixel-accurate mask ground truth, which is essential for training the segmentation head under deformation.

## Results

On OmniDocBench v1.5 with PaddleOCR-VL-1.5-0.9B as the downstream recognizer, RT-DocLayout achieves an Overall score of 94.50, with TextEdit 0.035, Formula CDM 94.21, Table TEDS 92.76, and Reading Order Edit 0.042. On Real5-OmniDocBench (real-world distortions), it reaches 92.05 Overall, with particularly strong scores on Warping (91.25) and Skew (91.66) where box-based methods degrade substantially. The model operates at 132.1 FPS with only 33M parameters, making it the fastest and most compact method among all evaluated layout analysis modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RT-DocLayout outperforms all four baselines on Overall accuracy across both benchmarks. Against DocLayout-YOLO (20M, 107.4 FPS), it gains +7.21 Overall on OmniDocBench v1.5 (94.50 vs. 87.29) and +17.98 on Real5-OmniDocBench (92.05 vs. 74.07), with the largest gap on Skew (91.66 vs. 48.74). Against MinerU2.5 (1.2B, 2.4 FPS), RT-DocLayout achieves +3.19 Overall while running 55× faster (132.1 vs. 2.4 FPS). Against Dolphin-v2 (3B, 0.9 FPS), the gap is +4.14 Overall with a 147× speed advantage. Against the closest competitor PP-DocLayoutV2 (53M, 110.9 FPS), RT-DocLayout gains +0.94 Overall on OmniDocBench v1.5 and +6.22 on Real5-OmniDocBench, while using 38% fewer parameters and running 19% faster; the controlled same-data comparison (Table 3) confirms the gap persists when both models are trained on the identical 38K dataset, isolating the benefit of mask-centric localization and coupled reading-order optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

RT-DocLayout extends the RT-DETR real-time detection framework by incorporating MaskDINO-style pixel-level segmentation and, more notably, integrating reading order prediction directly into the query-based decoder via an antisymmetric pairwise scorer. This departs from prior document layout systems (PP-DocLayoutV2, DocLayout-YOLO) that rely solely on axis-aligned bounding boxes and treat reading order as a separate post-hoc module (XYCut or decoupled pointer networks), and from large VLMs (MinerU2.5, Dolphin-v2) that achieve comparable accuracy at 37–90× the parameter count and 100× lower throughput. The physical spatial aware augmentation pipeline, which decouples intrinsic surface warping from extrinsic projective transformation, is a novel contribution not present in prior document layout training recipes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 33M-parameter model can match or exceed 1.2B–3B VLMs on document layout analysis at 132 FPS, the paper provides a practical, deployable front-end for industrial document parsing pipelines where latency and cost are critical. The ablation revealing that decoupled reading-order learning performs worse than the simple XYCut heuristic (RO 0.189 vs. 0.074) challenges the assumption that learned modules inherently outperform rule-based baselines without joint optimization, and motivates tighter coupling of structural and sequential reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 25 annotated categories and 38K in-house training set, and whether the physical augmentation pipeline transfers to other geometrically challenging vision tasks.
