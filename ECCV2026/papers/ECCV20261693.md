## Problem

MLLM-based visual document retrieval methods (e.g., ColPali, ColQwen, VisRAG) achieve strong accuracy but require full MLLM encoding for every page in the corpus, incurring prohibitive latency and cost at scale. The paper identifies that user queries are typically keyword-anchored, meaning most pages lack the distinctive content cues needed for relevance and can be pruned with far cheaper visual-text signals before expensive multi-modal reasoning is invoked.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261693/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text query and a collection of document page images (raw RGB, no OCR) as input, and produces a ranked list of pages by relevance to the query. The query undergoes POS-based filtering to retain only content-bearing tokens before embedding, while document images are split into non-overlapping ViT patches (size p×p) for patch-level visual encoding. The two-stage pipeline first selects a small candidate set (|D_c| ≪ N) via the lightweight visual encoder, then refines only those candidates with the MLLM, yielding an overall complexity of O(N·C_VE + |D_c|·C_MLLM) rather than O(N·C_MLLM).

## Method

LightSTAR decomposes retrieval into two synergistic stages. Stage 1 (LLM-free Visual Selection) uses a 542M ViT-based encoder (InternViT-300M) pretrained with token-level visual-text alignment, combined with content-grounded query embedding (POS-tagging to discard function words) and a scale-adaptive late interaction that averages top-k patch similarities instead of max-pooling, trained via in-batch contrastive loss with LoRA. Stage 2 (Vision-adaptive Semantic Refinement) applies InternVL3 MLLM only to the top candidates, introducing a dual-branch final ViT layer (text-aware + layout-aware) whose outputs are spatially fused via a binary mask derived from whitespace-token similarity, so text-dominant patches retain text-aligned features while background patches receive layout-aware features. The refinement stage is trained with a hardness-aware InfoNCE objective that stop-gradient-weights negatives by their similarity to the query, forcing the model to focus on confusable candidates. Both stages share lower ViT layers via weight sharing, and all training uses LoRA for parameter efficiency.

## Results

On the ViDoRe benchmark, LightSTAR achieves an average NDCG@5 of 89.1 with only 2B parameters, outperforming the previous best MLLM-based method ColQwen2.5 (88.8) and Nomic-Embed-Multimodal (86.3). In terms of efficiency, LightSTAR reduces end-to-end latency to 123.9 s on a 5,000-page corpus, compared to 466.6 s for ColQwen2.5, 257.3 s for ColPali, and 1219.9 s for VisRAG-Ret. At 7,000 pages, LightSTAR is 10.4× faster than VisRAG-Ret, 4.1× faster than ColQwen2.5, and 2.3× faster than ColPali, while maintaining the highest retrieval accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among MLLM-based methods, LightSTAR (89.1 avg NDCG@5) outperforms ColQwen2.5 (88.8) by 0.3 points on average, winning on 5 of 8 sub-datasets (DocQ, InfoQ, TATQ, AI, Health.) while trailing slightly on ArxivQ (86.2 vs 87.2), Energy (95.4 vs 96.3), and Gov. (95.8 vs 96.3). LightSTAR also surpasses Nomic-Embed-Multimodal (86.3) by 2.8 points, ColPali (81.9) by 7.2 points, and VisRAG-Ret (78.0) by 11.1 points. Against non-MLLM baselines, the gap is even larger: SigLIP (59.8), BGE-M3 (66.0), and BM25 (69.3) all trail by 19–60 NDCG@5 points. Critically, LightSTAR achieves these accuracy gains while being 3.8× faster than ColQwen2.5 and 9.8× faster than VisRAG-Ret at 5,000 pages, and the latency advantage grows with corpus size (10.4× vs VisRAG-Ret at 7,000 pages). The LLM-free Visual Selection module alone achieves 97.8% Recall@100, exceeding SigLIP (91.2%) and BGE-M3 (91.5%), confirming that the lightweight first stage preserves nearly all relevant candidates before MLLM refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightSTAR extends the ColPali/ColQwen2.5/VisRAG paradigm of MLLM-based visual document retrieval with late interaction, but departs fundamentally in its computational strategy: rather than applying a full MLLM to every page, it introduces a two-stage cascade where a lightweight LLM-free ViT encoder (initialized from InternVL3 and pretrained with token-level visual-text alignment from Guan et al., ICCV 2025) performs corpus-wide filtering, and the MLLM is invoked only on a small candidate set. The scale-adaptive late interaction modifies ColBERT-style max-pooling with top-k averaging, and the hardness-aware contrastive objective is a stop-gradient-weighted variant of InfoNCE. The adaptive region-wise feature fusion—spatially multiplexing text-aware and layout-aware ViT features via a whitespace-similarity mask—is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior single-encoder retrieval methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.4× end-to-end speedup over VisRAG-Ret while matching or exceeding SOTA NDCG@5 (89.1 vs. 88.8 for ColQwen2.5) makes MLLM-quality visual document retrieval practical for large-scale RAG pipelines and enterprise search where latency budgets are tight. The keyword-anchored query assumption is well-motivated for typical document retrieval but may degrade for open-ended or compositional queries where no single content word anchors relevance. Open questions include how the candidate budget (top-100) interacts with corpus size at extreme scales, whether the POS-based filtering generalizes to non-English or low-resource languages, and whether the two-stage design can be further compressed (e.g., quantized ViT for the selection stage) for edge deployment.
