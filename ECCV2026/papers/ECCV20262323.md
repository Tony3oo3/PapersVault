## Problem

Existing 3D semantic occupancy datasets target autonomous driving and model dynamic objects as rigid bounding boxes, leaving non-rigid humans poorly represented. For mobile robots navigating pedestrian-dense environments, accurate occupancy must capture deformable human geometry at limb level, yet no dataset or benchmark addresses this need.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262323/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The annotation pipeline consumes synchronized front-view stereo RGB images and dense LiDAR sweeps along a robot trajectory (from CODa), producing a 3D semantic occupancy voxel grid at up to 0.02 m resolution (benchmarked at 0.2 m over x∈[0.4,10.0] m, y∈[−4.8,4.8] m, z∈[−1.0,3.8] m at 5 Hz). Static voxels carry Cityscapes semantic labels; pedestrian voxels carry per-instance IDs derived from optimized SMPL meshes rather than rigid boxes. The dataset is released in NuScenes format with 10 merged semantic classes, and the benchmark additionally outputs pedestrian velocity vectors (v_x, v_y) and panoptic occupancy labels.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a training-free human mesh optimization that fuses monocular image and LiDAR cues in three stages: (1) CLIFF provides an initial SMPL estimate from the person crop, (2) visibility filtering via 2D keypoint confidence selects LiDAR-visible mesh vertices, which are coarsely registered to the point cloud via ICP, and (3) all SMPL parameters (β, θ, t_cam) are jointly refined by minimizing a composite objective combining 2D joint reprojection (Geman-McClure robust loss), a symmetric size-normalized Chamfer distance to LiDAR, a MoG pose prior, anti-hyperextension and shape priors, and a novel occlusion-aware pose-consistency term that penalizes quaternion drift on invisible joints. For static geometry, multi-frame LiDAR is fused in an OctoMap with pedestrian points explicitly masked out. The resulting dataset is benchmarked with monocular (FlashOcc, Panoptic-FlashOcc), stereo (VoxFormer-T), and detection (BEVDet4D) baselines on both semantic occupancy prediction and pedestrian velocity prediction tasks.

## Results

On SLOPER4D, the RGB+LiDAR fusion method achieves PVE 64.4 mm, MPJPE 55.8 mm, and PA-MPJPE 43.5 mm, substantially outperforming the RGB-only CLIFF initialization (PVE 93.7, MPJPE 84.7, PA-MPJPE 69.3) without any LiDAR-specific training. On HumanM3, the method reaches MPJPE 83.9 mm and PA-MPJPE 58.1 mm, improving over CLIFF (106.3 / 66.5) and approaching LiDAR-trained baselines like LiDAR-HMR (77.6 MPJPE). On the MobileOcc occupancy benchmark at 0.2 m resolution, FlashOcc (8f) achieves the highest mIoU of 27.18, with Panoptic-FlashOcc close behind at 26.64 and VoxFormer-T at 24.89. Pedestrian velocity prediction via Panoptic-FlashOcc-vel yields AVE of 0.97 m/s (all GT), 0.39 m/s (true positives), and 0.67 m/s (occupied voxels).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against image-only HMR baselines (CLIFF, PLIKS, SPIN, ROMP, HybrIK, Cha), the RGB+LiDAR method consistently wins on PVE and MPJPE: on 3DPW with Ouster-128 simulated LiDAR it reaches PVE 50.5 mm vs. 73.3–96.9 mm for image methods, and on SLOPER4D it cuts CLIFF's PVE from 93.7 to 64.4 mm and PA-MPJPE from 69.3 to 43.5 mm. Against video-based methods (VIBE, DynaBOA, WHAM-B, TRACE, MotionBERT, SMPLify), the method achieves the best PVE (50.5 vs. 71.0–106.8 mm) and MPJPE (39.1 vs. 59.4–82.9 mm) on 3DPW without requiring root alignment. Against LiDAR-trained baselines on SLOPER4D (LiDAR-HMR, VoteHMR, SAHSR), the method is competitive on MPJPE (55.8 vs. 51.0–54.6) and clearly superior on PA-MPJPE (43.5 mm, the only method reporting this metric alongside LiDAR baselines), while on HumanM3 it trails LiDAR-HMR slightly on MPJPE (83.9 vs. 77.6) but remains the only method reporting PA-MPJPE. A key distinction is that all LiDAR baselines are trained with dataset-specific supervision, whereas the proposed method is entirely training-free at inference. For occupancy prediction on MobileOcc, FlashOcc (8f) leads with mIoU 27.18, Panoptic-FlashOcc follows at 26.64, and VoxFormer-T (stereo) trails at 24.89 but shows relative strength on the pedestrian class (28.47 IoU vs. 31.00 for FlashOcc), suggesting stereo depth helps human geometry despite overall lower performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D semantic occupancy prediction task—well established in autonomous driving datasets (SemanticKITTI, Occ3D, OpenOccupancy) where humans are modeled as rigid bounding boxes—into the mobile robot domain, where non-rigid, deformable human geometry is the dominant dynamic object. The annotation pipeline builds directly on SMPLify's optimization framework, replacing its interpenetration term with a LiDAR-mesh Chamfer distance and an occlusion-aware pose consistency prior, while retaining the 2D joint reprojection, MoG pose prior, anti-hyperextension, and shape terms. For the benchmark, the paper repurposes existing occupancy prediction architectures (FlashOcc, VoxFormer, Panoptic-FlashOcc, BEVDet4D) originally designed for multi-camera surround-view driving into a monocular/stereo mobile-robot setting, adapting them without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MobileOcc is the first dataset to provide per-instance, non-rigid (SMPL mesh) human occupancy with tracking IDs for mobile robot navigation, filling a clear gap left by driving-centric occupancy datasets that treat pedestrians as rigid boxes. The training-free mesh optimization—requiring no LiDAR-specific training—matching or exceeding dedicated LiDAR HMR methods on three external datasets suggests a broadly applicable technique for any RGB+LiDAR human reconstruction pipeline. Key limitations include outdoor-only coverage, SMPL's inability to model carried objects, and a notable nighttime detection recall drop (76.9%), leaving open questions about indoor generalization, human-object interaction occupancy, and robustness under adverse weather.
