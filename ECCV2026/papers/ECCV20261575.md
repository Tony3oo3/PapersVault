## Problem

Discrete-token autoregressive image generation models cannot be fine-tuned end-to-end because non-differentiable discrete sampling breaks the computational graph, and full-sequence gradient recording over hundreds of tokens incurs prohibitive memory costs. While softmax relaxations restore differentiability, they distribute gradients across all logits—including those nearly impossible to sample—introducing spurious, non-causal updates that destabilize optimization over long autoregressive trajectories. No prior method under the next-token prediction paradigm enables fully differentiable optimization across the entire generation process.

## Input / Output

The framework fine-tunes a pre-trained autoregressive model (Janus-Pro-1B) that takes a text prompt and sequentially generates a sequence of discrete visual tokens (hundreds per image), which are then decoded back into RGB images via a learned tokenizer. Fine-tuning is driven by differentiable reward signals—either HPSv2 human-preference scores or LAION aesthetic predictor scores—computed on the decoded image, with gradients propagated back through the token generation trajectory. The method operates at the token-sequence level rather than pixel or latent-diffusion level, and the periodic subsampling of gradient positions (e.g., 33–50% of tokens) is the key constraint that makes long-sequence optimization tractable on a single GPU.

## Method

G³AFT introduces two complementary modules for reward-guided fine-tuning of discrete-token autoregressive models. Global Scope Gradient Recording (GSGR) replaces the conventional end-segment gradient recording (borrowed from diffusion fine-tuning) with periodic strided sampling across the entire token trajectory, randomly offset per sample to capture coarse global structure while reducing peak memory from 67 GB to 54 GB at the same gradient-record ratio. Noise Suppressed Gradient Projection (NSGP) first applies a pure softmax relaxation to make discrete sampling differentiable, then projects the resulting surrogate gradient onto the causal subspace spanned by the top-τ salient logits, explicitly zeroing out gradient components from logits that are irrelevant to the sampled outcome. This filtering removes relaxation-induced noise and aligns updates with the actual sampling mechanism, yielding cleaner credit assignment over long autoregressive chains. The two modules are applied jointly: GSGR determines which token positions contribute gradients, and NSGP refines the gradient direction at each retained position before aggregation and parameter update.

## Results

On the HPSv2 benchmark, G³AFT achieves an average reward of 28.03, surpassing the GRPO baseline (27.70) and the un-fine-tuned Janus-Pro-1B (26.30). The method leads in three of four categories—Anime (28.52), Concept-Art (28.37), and Paintings (28.59)—while trailing GRPO on Photo (26.64 vs. 27.42). Under aesthetic-reward fine-tuning on the Animals benchmark, the average LAION aesthetic score improves from 5.37 to 7.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G³AFT is compared against two baselines: the un-fine-tuned Janus-Pro-1B model and GRPO (a reinforcement-learning-based fine-tuning method). Against Janus-Pro-1B, G³AFT achieves substantial gains of +1.73 to +2.88 points across all four HPSv2 categories, confirming that reward-guided fine-tuning is effective. Against GRPO, G³AFT wins on Anime (+0.47), Concept-Art (+0.78), and Paintings (+0.86), but loses on Photo (−0.78), yielding a +0.33 advantage in overall average. The authors attribute the Photo gap to GRPO's exploration-exploitation dynamics, which can better capture photorealistic detail, whereas G³AFT's direct gradient-based optimization more faithfully tracks the human-preference reward signal in stylized domains. On the aesthetic-reward track, no direct RL baseline is reported; G³AFT simply improves the base model's Animals benchmark score from 5.37 to 7.05.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the reward-backpropagation fine-tuning paradigm established for continuous diffusion models (Clark et al., ICLR 2024; Prabhudesai et al., 2023) to the fundamentally different setting of discrete-token autoregressive image generation. The key conceptual departure is that the diffusion assumption—later timesteps encode progressively richer semantics—does not transfer to autoregressive token sequences, where adjacent tokens are highly redundant; GSGR replaces end-segment gradient recording with periodic subsampling to capture global structure. NSGP further departs from standard Gumbel-softmax relaxations by projecting gradients onto the effective support of the sampling distribution, filtering out spurious updates that the relaxation introduces across irrelevant logits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

G³AFT provides the first fully differentiable fine-tuning recipe for discrete-token autoregressive image generators, removing the need for RL-based workarounds (e.g., GRPO) that suffer from high-variance credit assignment over long token sequences. The practical memory reduction (67 GB → 54 GB) while simultaneously improving reward alignment makes end-to-end fine-tuning of models like Janus-Pro-1B more accessible. Open questions include whether the periodic sampling and NSGP filtering generalize to larger autoregressive models or to tasks beyond reward-guided aesthetic alignment, and whether the approach can be combined with the entropy-driven selection strategy to further reduce the gradient record ratio without quality loss.
