## Problem

The standard 2D-to-3D lifting paradigm for multi-view pose estimation introduces two geometric inconsistencies: projection inconsistency (2D detections deviate from true 3D projections) and cross-view inconsistency (independent per-camera detectors produce mutually incompatible 2D poses). Existing fusion methods apply confidence weighting or outlier dropping but never correct the 2D inputs themselves, so detection errors propagate unchecked into the 3D estimate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262392/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-view 2D keypoint detections (17 joints) from off-the-shelf detectors (CPN or ViTPose) over a 27-frame temporal window from V=4 cameras, requiring no camera intrinsics or extrinsics. It outputs a 3D pose per view in camera coordinates, refined 2D projections, and per-joint uncertainty scores in [0,1]. At inference, the 2D component is initialized directly from detector outputs while only the 3D component starts from pure Gaussian noise, enabling convergence in a single DDIM step.

## Method

CoDePose extends the diffusion state to a coupled 5D vector (3D + 2D coordinates per joint) and trains a cross-view transformer denoiser to jointly denoise both components under a shared noise schedule. The denoiser uses per-view PoseFormer-style spatio-temporal blocks, a cross-view encoder-decoder transformer for global fusion, and three output heads (3D pose, refined 2D, per-joint uncertainty). A key insight is that 2D detection errors are approximately Gaussian, so detector outputs lie near the forward diffusion trajectory, justifying asymmetric inference initialization that collapses the reverse process to one DDIM step. Two stabilizing losses enforce the geometric constraints the coupling implies: a body-coordinate consistency loss aligns per-view 3D predictions in a canonical, view-invariant frame (requiring no camera parameters), and a self-calibrated uncertainty loss downweights unreliable joints to prevent outlier 2D detections from corrupting the 3D estimate.

## Results

On Human3.6M (S9 & S11, 4 cameras, T=27), CoDePose achieves 12.1 mm MPJPE with ViTPose 2D input, a 19.9% improvement over the previous state-of-the-art FusionFormer (15.1 mm) and a 10.4% improvement over the 3D-only diffusion baseline (13.5 mm). With CPN input, CoDePose reaches 24.8 mm, outperforming all lifting-based methods. Cross-dataset generalization yields 14.4 mm on HumanEva and 4.2 mm on MPI-INF-3DHP, beating FusionFormer by 6.5% and 22.2% respectively. Thanks to asymmetric initialization, a single DDIM step (S=1) already achieves 12.15 mm, nearly matching the 5-step result (12.07 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

With ViTPose input, CoDePose (12.1 mm) outperforms FusionFormer (15.1 mm) by 3.0 mm (19.9%) and the 3D-only diffusion baseline (13.5 mm) by 1.4 mm (10.4%), confirming that coupled 2D-3D denoising adds value beyond diffusion alone. With CPN input, CoDePose (24.8 mm) beats PoseIRM (25.1 mm) and FusionFormer (25.4 mm), the strongest prior lifting-based methods. CoDePose also surpasses all image-based multi-view methods (best: MvP at 18.6 mm) despite operating on 2D keypoints rather than raw pixels. On cross-dataset transfer (10-epoch finetune), CoDePose achieves 14.4 mm on HumanEva and 4.2 mm on MPI-INF-3DHP, improving over FusionFormer by 6.5% and 22.2% respectively. Against monocular diffusion baselines (D3DP 39.5 mm, DiffPose 36.9 mm, DDHPose 39.0 mm), the multi-view coupled formulation yields a 3× reduction in error.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDePose extends the diffusion-based 3D pose estimation line (D3DP, DiffPose, DDHPose) by a key architectural departure: rather than treating 2D detections as fixed external conditions throughout reverse sampling, it incorporates them into the diffusion state itself, creating a coupled 2D-3D denoising process. The per-view backbone follows the PoseFormer-style spatio-temporal transformer, and the x-predict diffusion formulation follows Li & He (2025). The asymmetric initialization strategy—starting 2D from detector outputs and 3D from pure noise—is motivated by the empirical observation that 2D detection errors approximate a Gaussian, positioning detector outputs as intermediate samples on the forward diffusion trajectory. This distinguishes CoDePose from all prior diffusion-based lifting methods, which propagate detection errors uncorrected through every denoising step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 19.9% MPJPE improvement over the previous SOTA (FusionFormer) and the ability to achieve near-optimal accuracy in a single DDIM step (12.15 mm vs. 12.06 mm at S=20) make this practically significant for real-time multi-view systems where inference latency matters. The finding that multi-hypothesis aggregation—critical in monocular diffusion pose estimation—is nearly unnecessary in the multi-view setting (0.05 mm gain from N=1 to N=5) clarifies the role of geometric constraints in resolving depth ambiguity. Open questions include whether an end-to-end formulation could allow gradient flow from 3D supervision back to the 2D branch, whether a data-driven 2D noise schedule could better capture joint- and view-specific detector errors, and how the framework extends to dynamic camera configurations and in-the-wild settings with heavy-tailed detection failures.
