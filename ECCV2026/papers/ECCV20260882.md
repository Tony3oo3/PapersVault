## Problem

Existing world models fall into two disconnected categories: static scene generators that produce explorable 3D environments without active agents, and controllable-entity models that let a single agent perform a small set of predefined actions in an otherwise uncontrollable environment. No prior framework unifies realistic 3D scene grounding with open-ended, user-specified character behaviors (locomotion, gestures, object interactions) directed by natural language while maintaining long-horizon temporal coherence.

## Input / Output

The system takes a 3DGS scene (pre-generated via Marble or real-world reconstruction), a 3D or four-view character (front, left, right, back renders), a natural-language text instruction, and a single-frame bounding-box anchor that is automatically propagated to all subsequent frames. At inference, the text instruction is parsed into locomotion, gesture, object-centric, or camera-behavior categories, each determining a camera path that is rendered against the 3DGS scene to produce a projection video used as explicit visual conditioning. The model outputs 96-frame video clips at 360P or 720P, with the last 33 frames of the previous clip serving as temporal context for autoregressive continuation.

## Method

CustomX is initialized from the 13B-parameter HunyuanCustom video generator and fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64) on 2,084 GTA-V gameplay clips using Flow Matching as the training objective. Conditioning is multi-modal: scene and mask tokens are channel-concatenated and projected into the noisy target tokens, while text tokens (encoded by LLaVA jointly with character tokens) and four-view character tokens are sequence-concatenated and processed by a full-attention Transformer backbone with 3D-RoPE positional embeddings (shifted per view to encode viewpoint identity). Camera control is achieved geometrically by rendering a 3DGS projection video along the user-specified path, rather than injecting Plücker embeddings. For autoregressive long-horizon generation, the target video is split into a first-quarter preceding segment and a three-quarter new segment, with Gaussian noise augmentation on the preceding tokens to bridge the train-inference distribution gap. DMD2 distillation compresses the 30-step denoising schedule to 4 steps, reducing 360P inference from 121 s to 21 s on a single H100.

## Results

On the WorldScore benchmark, CustomX achieves a Static score of 84.64 and Dynamic score of 77.22, substantially outperforming the strongest dedicated world model (Hunyuan-GameCraft: 69.92 / 57.77) and the strongest video generation base model (HunyuanCustom: 62.64 / 61.11). For action control, CustomX reaches a 100% success rate on the four seen locomotion actions and 80.7% on 142 novel richer actions, compared to 56.7% and 51.4% for HunyuanCustom. Character consistency is also best, with DINOv2 0.698 and CLIP 0.721, exceeding the next-best HunyuanVideo (0.645 / 0.709).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CustomX outperforms all eight baselines across nearly every WorldScore metric. Against the closest dedicated world model, Hunyuan-GameCraft, CustomX gains +14.7 on Static (84.64 vs 69.92) and +19.5 on Dynamic (77.22 vs 57.77), with particularly large margins on Camera Control (88.91 vs 77.45) and Motion Accuracy (61.08 vs 16.65). Versus the base model HunyuanCustom, CustomX improves Static by +22.0 and Dynamic by +16.1, while also boosting Motion Smoothness from 89.56 to 94.47. On action control (Table 2), CustomX achieves 100% locomotion success and 80.7% on 142 novel actions, far exceeding Wan2.2-I2V (53.3% / 74.6%) and HunyuanCustom (56.7% / 51.4%); the three dedicated world models (DeepVerse, Hunyuan-GameCraft, Matrix-Game-2.0) support only locomotion and score 6.7%, 16.7%, and 3.3% respectively. For character consistency, CustomX leads with DINOv2 0.698 and CLIP 0.721, beating HunyuanVideo (0.645 / 0.709) and HunyuanCustom (0.558 / 0.665), while the dedicated world models trail significantly (DINOv2 ≤ 0.339). The only metric where a baseline is competitive is Motion Magnitude, where Matrix-Game-2.0 (47.36) and Wan2.2 (38.93) exceed CustomX (24.12), though this reflects a trade-off favoring smoothness over amplitude.

## Relation to Prior Work

CustomX sits at the intersection of two established paradigms: static 3DGS world generation (which produces explorable environments but no active agents) and controllable-entity world models (which allow a single agent to perform a limited action set in an otherwise fixed environment). It builds directly on HunyuanCustom as a 13B-parameter multimodal diffusion transformer backbone, adopting its Flow Matching training objective and multi-view subject conditioning, while departing from prior camera-control methods that inject Plücker embeddings by instead conditioning on 3DGS-rendered projection videos along a specified trajectory. The autoregressive long-horizon generation extends the single-clip setting of prior controllable-entity models (DeepVerse, Hunyuan-GameCraft, Matrix-Game) by prepending preceding video tokens as an additional conditioning signal, with a noise-augmentation trick to bridge the train/inference gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly integrate user-specified 3D/multi-view characters, open-ended text-controlled actions (up to ~150 distinct behaviors), 3DGS-grounded scenes, and iterative long-horizon generation in a single system, offering a practical pipeline for interactive character-in-scene video synthesis. The finding that minimal post-training on basic locomotion data (2,084 clips, 5 characters) enhances both motion dynamics and generalization to 142 unseen actions—mirroring the LLM pre-training/post-training analogy—suggests a broader principle for video model adaptation. Key limitations include single-subject restriction, non-real-time inference (21s per clip even after DMD2 distillation), and dependence on game-rendered training data that imparts a stylized look without real-world augmentation. Open questions include multi-character interaction, real-time operation, and whether the post-training transfer principle holds across architectures and action taxonomies.
