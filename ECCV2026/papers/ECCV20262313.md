## Problem

Talking head generation and text-to-speech synthesis have been treated as separate tasks, despite facial motion and speech being deeply interwoven in natural communication. No prior work has jointly modeled, generated, and conditioned on both audio and facial motion within a single generative framework, leaving a gap in holistic audio-visual synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262313/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any combination of text, reference audio, reference motion (17 non-mouth LivePortrait keypoints per frame), a source portrait image, or a driving video, with the inpainting-style training allowing any subset to be absent at inference. The motion stream outputs only 4 mouth-related expression deformation vectors (T×4×3) from LivePortrait's 21-dimensional embedding, which are combined with fixed identity/pose features and rendered to video via LivePortrait's warping module. The audio stream outputs mel-spectrograms, and the two are temporally aligned through RoPE-scaled positional embeddings.

## Method

JAM-Flow is a dual-stream Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer with a Motion-DiT (generating low-dimensional lip keypoints) and an Audio-DiT (denoising mel-spectrograms), coupled via selective joint attention in only half the transformer layers to balance cross-modal interaction with modality-specific representation. Two key architectural innovations enable stable joint training: (1) RoPE alignment that scales positional angles so audio and motion tokens at the same timestep share comparable angular positions despite different sequence lengths, and (2) asymmetric modality-specific attention masks where motion tokens use local temporal windows while audio tokens retain global self-attention but restrict cross-modal attention to same-timestamp motion tokens. Training is two-stage: each modality is pretrained independently (F5-TTS for audio, LivePortrait-based keypoint model for motion), then jointly fine-tuned with an inpainting-style flow matching objective where audio and motion segments are randomly masked in all combinations, forcing the model to learn cross-modal dependencies from partial context. This inpainting supervision is the core mechanism enabling flexible inference under arbitrary input configurations without task-specific architectures.

## Results

On HDTF talking-head generation, JAM-Flow (V2V) achieves the best FID (11.63), FVD (25.07), LSE-C (8.09), and LSE-D (7.18) among all compared methods, while generating a 20-second video in 45 seconds on a single RTX A6000 (vs. 2.5 min–30 min for baselines). On LibriSpeech-PC TTS, the model reports WER 4.91% and SIM-o 0.64, slightly behind dedicated TTS systems (e.g., F5-TTS: 2.42% WER) due to noisy Whisper pseudo-transcripts in training data. On CelebV-Dub automated dubbing, JAM-Flow achieves the highest speaker similarity (spkSIM 0.410) and competitive WER (6.39%), with a user study showing 62.6% preference over VoiceCraft-Dub.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For talking-head generation on HDTF, JAM-Flow (V2V) substantially outperforms all baselines: FID 11.63 vs. 20.36 (Hallo3, best prior), FVD 25.07 vs. 160.84 (Hallo3), and LSE-C 8.09 vs. 7.89 (SadTalker), while being 3–40× faster in inference (45 s vs. 2.5 min–30 min). The I2V variant is also competitive (FID 17.57, FVD 192.30) and faster than all diffusion-based baselines. For TTS on LibriSpeech-PC, JAM-Flow trails dedicated systems on WER (4.91% vs. 2.31% for MegaTTS 3, 2.42% for F5-TTS), which the authors attribute to Whisper pseudo-transcript noise in CelebV-Dub rather than model capacity; SIM-o (0.64) is competitive with F5-TTS (0.66) and E2 TTS (0.69). For automated video dubbing on CelebV-Dub, JAM-Flow achieves the highest spkSIM (0.410 vs. 0.333 for VoiceCraft-Dub) and the second-best WER (6.39% vs. 7.01% for VoiceCraft-Dub), though LSE-C/LSE-D scores are lower than VoiceCraft-Dub (3.43 vs. 6.05; 10.56 vs. 8.33), a gap the authors note is unreliable for TTS-generated audio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—audio-driven talking head generation (LivePortrait, SadTalker, Hallo) and flow-matching TTS (F5-TTS, VoiceBox)—into a single dual-stream MM-DiT architecture with selective joint attention. The architectural template follows the MM-DiT paradigm (separate DiT branches fused via joint self-attention), while the training objective inherits the inpainting-style conditional flow matching from F5-TTS and the compact keypoint representation from LivePortrait. The key departure from prior work is the joint inpainting-style supervision that randomly masks either or both modalities, enabling mutual conditioning rather than the unidirectional audio-to-visual flow assumed by all prior talking head methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to treat speech and facial motion as a jointly generated, mutually conditioning pair within one model, opening the door to applications like expressive dubbing, silent video revoicing, and text-only avatar generation without separate pipelines. The near real-time inference (45 s for 20 s video on a single A6000, approaching real-time on H100) is a significant practical advantage over diffusion-based talking head methods that take 23–30 minutes. Key limitations include reliance on Whisper pseudo-transcripts (bounding WER at ~4.9%), LivePortrait's restriction to facial regions, and the absence of diffusion distillation; whether the inpainting-style joint supervision paradigm generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., depth+video, full-body audio+video) remains an open question.
