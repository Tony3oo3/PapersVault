## Problem

Existing collaborative perception methods rely on confidence maps co-trained with detection heads to weight agent contributions during fusion, but these maps are inherently coupled to detection scores and fail to provide unbiased, physically grounded evidence—leading to self-reinforcing false positives and poor suppression of collaborative noise. Additionally, how to deeply integrate such guidance signals into the multi-scale fusion process remains under-explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262444/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes ego and neighbor LiDAR point clouds voxelized onto a 0.4 m × 0.4 m BEV grid, encoded by a shared PointPillar backbone into H×W×C feature maps. A dedicated uncertainty head produces a dense H×W map in [0,1] (higher = less reliable) supervised by raw LiDAR point density rather than detection scores. The final output is 3D object bounding boxes for the ego agent; the uncertainty map is transmitted alongside BEV features to neighbors, adding only ~0.39% bandwidth overhead (one extra channel vs. C=256 feature channels).

## Method

UECP introduces a physically grounded uncertainty map supervised by LiDAR point density (via a focal-style regression loss plus Sobel-gradient consistency term), decoupling fusion guidance from detection-head training. The core UAPF module performs coarse-to-fine multi-scale fusion (scales 4, 2, 1) using two submodules: Uncertainty-Weighted Downsampling (UWD), which computes downsampled features as the ratio of two average-pooling operations weighted by (1−U) to preserve high-reliability regions, and Uncertainty-Guided Residual Fusion (UGRF), which derives per-agent spatial fusion weights from uncertainty maps (with a learnable positive scalar γ and Gaussian smoothing) and applies a residual update—ego feature plus a gated, convolutionally refined weighted-sum residual—so that collaborators act as a controlled refinement signal rather than overwriting ego features. This residual architecture stabilizes training and suppresses feature contamination from unreliable agents.

## Results

UECP achieves state-of-the-art 3D detection AP on both DAIR-V2X (81.78 / 77.59 / 61.12 at IoU 0.3/0.5/0.7) and V2V4REAL (65.69 / 63.38 / 38.90), outperforming the second-best method by +1.93/+2.41/+3.15 and +2.56/+3.28/+3.66 respectively. The gains are largest at the strictest IoU threshold (AP@70), indicating the uncertainty-guided fusion more effectively suppresses false positives. Under pose noise (σ up to 1 m) and communication latency (up to 500 ms), UECP retains the highest accuracy among all compared methods, while communication-heavy baselines degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UECP outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across both datasets. On DAIR-V2X, the closest competitor is CodeFilling (79.85/75.18/57.26), which UECP surpasses by +1.93/+2.41/+3.15; HEAL (79.11/73.95/55.56) and V2X-ViT (77.28/72.47/57.97) trail by larger margins. On V2V4REAL, CoBEVT (63.13/60.10/33.94) is the nearest rival, beaten by +2.56/+3.28/+3.66, while CodeFilling (60.15/59.78/35.07) and HEAL (51.57/48.70/35.24) lag further behind. The advantage is most pronounced at AP@70, where UECP's uncertainty-guided residual fusion suppresses false positives that confidence-map-based methods (Where2comm, HEAL) tend to amplify. Replacing the confidence map with the proposed uncertainty map in Where2comm and HEAL alone yields +3.71 and +2.03 average AP gains, confirming the benefit is attributable to the uncertainty signal rather than the fusion architecture. Under pose noise and latency stress tests, UECP degrades less than any baseline, with communication-heavy methods (V2X-ViT, CoBEVT) showing the steepest accuracy drops.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the intermediate-fusion collaborative perception lineage (V2X-ViT, AttnFuse, HEAL, Where2comm) that exchanges BEV feature maps between agents, but departs from the dominant practice of using detector-co-learned confidence maps to weight fusion. It repurposes the uncertainty-estimation literature (evidential learning, aleatoric/epistemic decomposition) from single-agent 3D detection into the multi-agent fusion setting, replacing model-posterior confidence with a physically grounded signal (LiDAR point density) that is decoupled from the detection head. The UAPF module (UWD + UGRF) is a new architectural contribution that embeds this uncertainty signal into a multi-scale pyramid with residual gating, rather than using it merely as a scalar gate as in Where2comm or HEAL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The confidence self-reinforcement problem identified here—where spurious high-confidence activations amplify false positives across agents—is a practical failure mode that has limited the robustness of confidence-weighted fusion in deployment. By showing that a single extra channel (~0.4% bandwidth) supervised by raw sensor density can consistently improve AP by 2–4 points across two real-world benchmarks and degrade gracefully under pose noise and latency, the paper offers a low-cost, drop-in improvement that other collaborative perception pipelines can adopt. Open questions include whether the density-based uncertainty generalizes to camera-only or heterogeneous sensor agents (the paper only evaluates LiDAR/PointPillar), and whether the near-equivalence between 'Density-direct' and learned uncertainty (Table 4b) suggests the learned head is largely redundant, potentially simplifying the pipeline further.
