## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation methods either rely on multi-stage training pipelines with pretrained audio-visual alignment modules (incurring high computational cost and long runtimes) or convert visual input into text to leverage pretrained text-to-audio models (sacrificing fine-grained temporal cues). Additionally, standard V2A datasets provide only minimal captions (under 6–10 words), limiting the semantic richness available to guide audio synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260991/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a silent video clip (sampled at 8 FPS, first 8 seconds) and a text prompt (max 77 tokens, typically a sound-oriented caption) as input. The video is encoded frame-wise via CLIP, the text via FLAN-T5, and the target audio is downsampled to 16 kHz, converted to a mel-spectrogram, and encoded into a VAE latent. At inference, the predicted latent is decoded back to a mel-spectrogram and vocoded into a waveform using BigVGAN; the text prompt can be produced on-the-fly by the SoundCap VLM without access to audio.

## Method

Flowley is a single-stage flow-matching transformer with a multi-to-single stream design: 5 MM-DiT-style multi-stream blocks jointly attend over visual, textual, and audio latents, followed by 10 DiT-style single-stream blocks that refine the audio stream via cross-attention to both text and video. The key architectural contribution is Progressive Soft-masked Cross-Attention (PSCA), which injects audio-visual temporal alignment directly into the attention mechanism through a differentiable soft mask that allows full attention within a hard window, applies a cosine decay over adjacent frames, and scales the fade zone progressively across layers (early layers explore wider context, later layers converge to a hard sliding window) — all at zero additional computational cost over standard attention. A companion plug-and-play pipeline, SoundCap, uses an AV-LLM (video-SALMONN) to generate detailed sound-oriented captions as ground truth, then fine-tunes a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) to produce these captions from video alone at inference, enriching the textual conditioning without requiring audio access. Training combines a flow-matching MSE loss with a cosine-similarity velocity-direction term, a tangent-based noise schedule, data-noise alignment for acceleration, and 10% random modality masking to support classifier-free guidance at inference.

## Results

On VGGSound, Flowley (169M params) achieves the best KAD (0.42), KL divergence (1.57), Inception Score (18.25), IB-Score (29.32), and LB-Score (24.87) among all compared methods, outperforming models up to 8× larger (e.g., VinTAGe at 1.32B params). Its Align Acc of 89.37 trails only Frieren (97.13) and MDSGen (91.70), both of which use the same pretrained audio-visual encoder as the evaluation metric's backbone. With SoundCap, Flowley further improves IS to 19.68 and KAD to 0.39. In zero-shot evaluation on Movie Gen Audio Bench, Flowley + SoundCap (169M, ~400 h data) surpasses Movie Gen Audio (13B, ~1M h data) on IS (8.18 vs. 8.01) while remaining competitive on Align Acc (61.07 vs. 64.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flowley decisively outperforms all baselines on distribution matching (KAD 0.42 vs. next-best MMAudio 0.57, a 26.3% margin) and generation quality (IS 18.25 vs. VinTAGe 17.34 despite VinTAGe having ~8× more parameters). On semantic alignment, Flowley leads with IB-Score 29.32 and LB-Score 24.87, surpassing MMAudio (28.09 / 21.98) and FoleyCrafter (25.75 / 24.66). The sole metric where Flowley trails is Align Acc: Frieren (97.13) and MDSGen (91.70) score higher, though the authors note both use the same pretrained audio-visual encoder as the metric's backbone, introducing potential evaluation bias; in subjective human evaluation, Flowley is preferred over all three for temporal synchronization (76% win rate vs. MDSGen). Against MMAudio (the closest competitor at 157M params), Flowley wins on every metric except Align Acc (89.37 vs. 89.73, a 0.36-point gap). In the zero-shot Movie Gen Audio Bench comparison, Flowley + SoundCap (169M) beats Movie Gen Audio (13B) on IS (8.18 vs. 8.01) but falls behind on IB-Score (25.78 vs. 35.86) and Align Acc (61.07 vs. 64.33), which the authors attribute to the narrower training data distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flowley builds on the flow-matching generative framework (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) and the multi-to-single-stream DiT/MM-DiT architecture, but departs from prior V2A methods in two key ways. First, it eliminates the multi-stage training pipelines (Frieren, FoleyCrafter, Mel-QCD) and external pretrained audio-visual alignment modules (MMAudio, VinTAGe) by embedding temporal synchronization directly into a novel Progressive Soft-masked Cross-Attention (PSCA) mechanism at zero additional computational cost. Second, it introduces SoundCap, a plug-and-play AV-LLM-based captioning pipeline that addresses the long-standing gap of sound-oriented text annotations in V2A datasets, complementing purely visual conditioning with rich semantic text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 169M-parameter single-stage model can match or exceed 1.3B+ multi-stage competitors on VGGSound, and that adding SoundCap lets it outperform a 13B model (Movie Gen Audio) on zero-shot audio quality, suggests that architectural alignment mechanisms and data quality (captions) may matter more than raw scale for V2A. However, semantic alignment on out-of-distribution content (Movie Gen Audio Bench) still lags significantly, and Align Acc trails methods that share the evaluation encoder, leaving open whether PSCA generalizes beyond VGGSound's domain. SoundCap's plug-and-play nature and its 31.6% KAD improvement for MMAudio indicate broad applicability, but its dependence on AV-LLM quality and the need for noise-aware prompting limit straightforward deployment on noisier datasets.
