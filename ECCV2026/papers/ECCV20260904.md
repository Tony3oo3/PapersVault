## Problem

Existing text-guided medical image segmentation methods are restricted to single-round queries and cannot handle multi-round conversations where later queries reference mask results from earlier rounds. This limitation is particularly problematic in medical education, where learners iteratively build understanding through relation-driven, cross-round queries (e.g., 'segment the structure adjacent to the one you just outlined'). No prior method supports this entity-level reasoning across dialogue rounds in the medical domain.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a medical image, the current round's text query, the full conversation history (all prior Q&A pairs), and a reference mask from a previously selected round. The reference mask is used to crop the corresponding region from the original image and extract its bounding box coordinates, which are encoded as separate visual and spatial features. The output is a binary segmentation mask for the current round's target entity, accompanied by a free-form text response that may include the [SEG] token triggering mask generation.

## Method

MediRound builds on LLaVA-Med (Mistral-7B) as the multimodal language model and MedSAM as the segmentation backbone, introducing a [SEG] token whose last-layer hidden state drives the MedSAM mask decoder. The key architectural design concatenates the current query, full conversation history, the reference round's cropped image (encoded by LLaVA-Med's visual encoder), and its bounding box (encoded by a linear projection) into a single input sequence, enabling the model to reason over cross-round entity relationships. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning on the LLM with a combined autoregressive cross-entropy loss for text and a weighted BCE+DICE loss for masks, under a teacher-forcing strategy. To address error propagation in the chain-like multi-round pipeline, a lightweight Judgment & Correction Mechanism (JCM) is applied at inference: two small MLPs evaluate and optionally refine the [SEG] embedding before decoding, preventing low-quality masks from contaminating subsequent rounds.

## Results

On the MR-MedSeg test set, MediRound+JCM achieves 63.3 Dice, 54.5 gIoU, and 60.2 cIoU across all multi-round conversations, outperforming the strongest baseline (Qwen3-VL+MediSee at 45.2 Dice) by 18.1 points. The JCM mechanism adds a further +2.3 Dice over MediRound alone (61.0→63.3), with gains growing in later rounds (e.g., Round 8 cIoU improves from 46.1 to 54.8). On the single-round SA-Med2D-20M benchmark, MediRound reaches 62.1 Dice and 66.3 cIoU, remaining competitive with the dedicated referring-segmentation model MediSee (61.2 Dice, 70.8 cIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MediRound+JCM decisively outperforms all 11 baselines on the multi-round MEMR-Seg task, achieving 63.3 test Dice versus 45.2–45.6 for the strongest MLLM+segmentation hybrids (Qwen3-VL+MediSee, GPT-4o+MediSee) and 45.3 for SegLLM-13B, a gap of roughly 18 Dice points. The JCM mechanism contributes an additional +2.3 Dice over vanilla MediRound, with the advantage widening in later rounds (Round 8 cIoU: 54.8 vs. 46.1), confirming its role in mitigating error propagation. In the single-round referring-segmentation setting (Table 3, SA-Med2D-20M), MediRound (62.1 Dice) marginally surpasses MediSee (61.2 Dice) but trails it on cIoU (66.3 vs. 70.8), indicating that the multi-round architecture does not sacrifice single-round quality. Human-guided baselines (e.g., Human-Thinking+MediSee at 45.6 test Dice) remain well below MediRound, showing that even expert-written prompts cannot substitute for the model's native cross-round entity reasoning. The paper also notes that GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro paired with IMIS-Net perform poorly (17.4–21.9 test Dice), highlighting the difficulty of multi-round medical reasoning for general-purpose MLLMs without domain-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

MediRound directly extends SegLLM's multi-round reasoning segmentation paradigm from natural images to the medical domain, adapting the [SEG] token mechanism and iterative dialogue structure while incorporating medical-specific backbones (MedSAM, LLaVA-Med). It also builds on the LISA-style architecture where an MLLM's hidden state drives a segmentation decoder, but departs from single-round medical referring segmentation methods (MediSee, IMIS-Net, BiomedParse) by introducing cross-round entity-level reasoning and a reference-mask conditioning mechanism. The Judgment & Correction Mechanism is a novel addition not present in prior multi-round segmentation work, motivated by the error-propagation problem unique to chain-like iterative prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in medical AI: existing segmentation tools assume one-shot expert queries, whereas real medical education involves progressive, relation-driven questioning across multiple rounds. The MR-MedSeg dataset (177K dialogues, 168 entities, 9 modalities) provides a much-needed benchmark for this setting, and the JCM offers a lightweight, plug-and-play solution to error accumulation that could generalize to other iterative prediction pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric data, whether the JCM threshold is robust across imaging modalities, and how the method performs when the reference round is severely wrong rather than merely suboptimal.
