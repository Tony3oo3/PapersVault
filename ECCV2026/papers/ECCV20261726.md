## Problem

Existing multi-view anomaly detection methods rely on Normalizing Flows (typically RealNVP) whose coupling-block architecture limits expressivity and struggles with the high dimensionality of multi-view feature maps. Flow Matching, a more flexible generative framework capable of handling high-dimensional distributions, had not yet been applied to multi-view anomaly detection. Additionally, the MANTA-Tiny benchmark lacked comprehensive evaluation across popular AD methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261726/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 5-view RGB images per object (resized to 256×256) and processes them through a frozen WideResNet50 to extract feature maps at three spatial resolutions (64×64, 32×32, 16×16). At inference, a 5-step Euler ODE integration maps each view's features to Gaussian space, producing three per-view anomaly score maps. These are combined by summation for pixel-level segmentation (all three maps) and for detection (only the two highest-level maps, then max-pooled), with sample-wise scores obtained by max-aggregation across all five views.

## Method

MATCH learns the normal data distribution via optimal transport conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM) on frozen WideResNet50 latent features, using a simple L2 regression loss against the conditional vector field (x₁ − x₀). The architecture follows an RD4AD-inspired design: a bottleneck with spatial alignment (strided convolutions to match resolutions), three ResNet-style blocks, and progressive decoder blocks that upsample back to original spatial sizes, with sinusoidal time embeddings and learned view embeddings injected at each stage. The key simplification is omitting the divergence term in the likelihood formula, reducing the anomaly score to the squared Mahalanobis distance ‖x₀‖²/2 in Gaussian space, which eliminates costly backpropagation per ODE step and triples throughput (18.77 FPS on an RTX 3090) without measurable detection loss. This design choice is validated by ablations showing the divergence contributes only 0.57% of the score magnitude while consuming 3–12× more compute and memory.

## Results

On Real-IAD, MATCH achieves an I-AUROC of 91.17±0.08 for image-wise detection, a P-AUPRO of 94.76±0.02 for segmentation, and a P-AP of 32.68±0.02, outperforming all nine baselines on most metrics. On MANTA-Tiny, MATCH reaches a P-AUPRO of 89.66±0.02 and P-AUROC of 95.65±0.01, setting new state-of-the-art segmentation results across all five object categories. The method runs at 18.77 FPS on a consumer RTX 3090 GPU, enabled by omitting the costly divergence term in likelihood estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most direct flow-based competitor Multi-Flow (RealNVP-based), MATCH wins I-AUROC by ~0.9 points (91.17 vs 90.27) and P-AUPRO by ~6.9 points (94.76 vs 87.91), but narrowly loses S-AUROC by 0.22 points (95.63 vs 95.85). Compared to RD4AD, MATCH improves I-AUROC by ~1.9 points and P-AUPRO by ~2.1 points, though RD4AD retains a large lead on P-AUROC (98.81 vs 90.24), suggesting RD4AD's reconstruction-based approach localizes pixel boundaries more sharply. On MANTA-Tiny, MATCH dominates segmentation with P-AUPRO of 89.66, beating the runner-up RD4AD (85.59) by over 4 points across all categories, and P-AUROC of 95.65 vs RD4AD's 94.60. The sole metric where MATCH trails is MANTA-Tiny I-AUROC (90.66 vs SimpleNet's 93.19), which the authors attribute to the higher variance of tiny defects in that dataset. All other baselines (CFA, DSR, EfficientAD, FastFlow, PaDiM, STFPM) are outperformed by MATCH on every reported metric on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATCH positions itself as the first Flow Matching-based approach to multi-view anomaly detection, departing from the dominant Normalizing Flow (RealNVP) paradigm used by methods like Multi-Flow, CFlowAD, and FastFlow. Architecturally, it adapts the Reverse Distillation (RD4AD) decoder structure but replaces the invertible coupling blocks with a flexible ODE-based vector field trained via the OT-CFM loss, enabling multi-scale feature processing without the expressivity constraints of chained bijections. The key methodological departure is the omission of the divergence term in likelihood estimation, which prior NF-based AD methods require for principled density estimation but which MATCH shows to be computationally expensive and empirically negligible for detection performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the divergence term contributes only 0.57% of the anomaly score magnitude while tripling inference cost is a practical insight that could simplify likelihood-based AD more broadly, not just in multi-view settings. MATCH's 18.77 FPS on consumer hardware makes FM-based AD viable for real-time industrial deployment, a barrier that RealNVP-based methods face due to their architectural constraints. Open questions remain around handling high-variance defect categories (where MATCH trails SimpleNet on MANTA-Tiny I-AUROC), incorporating explicit 3D or multi-view correspondence priors into the FM pipeline, and whether the divergence-free scoring generalizes to other flow-based generative tasks beyond anomaly detection.
