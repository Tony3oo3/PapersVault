## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction foundation models (VGGT, π³, MapAnything) are trained on large-scale perspective image datasets and degrade substantially on fisheye imagery due to curvilinear distortions that shift pixel spatial arrangements outside the learned feature manifold. Conventional rectification sacrifices peripheral coverage or introduces extreme resampling artifacts, while fine-tuning or retraining from scratch is infeasible given the severe scarcity of fisheye images with 3D ground truth. The paper asks whether a lightweight adaptation can extend existing perspective-trained models to fisheye inputs without performance regression on perspective images.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a sequence of S uncalibrated RGB images that may be perspective, fisheye, or a mixture of both within a single sequence. During training, fisheye inputs are synthesized from perspective images via the Kannala-Brandt distortion model; dense geometric outputs (depth, point maps, ray directions) are undistorted back to perspective coordinates before loss computation, while camera extrinsics remain unchanged. At inference, a per-frame camera-type classifier (a linear probe on the encoder's class token) routes each frame through masked attention, enabling a single forward pass over heterogeneous camera sequences.

## Method

Fisheye3R inserts K learnable calibration tokens (default K=8) into every transformer layer of a frozen backbone—image encoder, frame-wise attention, and global attention—starting after L₀ initial layers (default L₀=12). These tokens modulate high-level features so that fisheye patch embeddings are realigned onto the perspective feature manifold, effectively performing latent-space recalibration rather than image-space rectification. A masked attention mechanism ensures backwards compatibility: a binary per-frame mask (derived from a lightweight camera-type classifier) nullifies the calibration tokens' influence on perspective frames while activating them for fisheye frames, enabling mixed-camera inference without parameter drift. Three learning schemes accommodate varying data availability: self-supervised distillation from the model's own perspective predictions (AugUndo-style), supervised learning with perspective ground truth, and supervised learning with fisheye ground truth. Only the calibration tokens and the classifier are trained; the entire backbone remains frozen, keeping computational overhead negligible relative to full fine-tuning.

## Results

Fisheye3R achieves consistent improvements across all 135 tests (3 datasets × 3 models × 15 metrics) on fisheye sequences. On ScanNet++, MapAnything+SL+ reduces ATE from 0.163 to 0.066 (60% reduction) and Chamfer Distance from 0.083 to 0.051 (39% reduction), while π³+SL+ drops ATE from 0.164 to 0.072 and CD from 0.084 to 0.054. Even self-supervised learning with only unlabeled perspective RGB images yields 50%+ improvement in 26/135 metric-dataset-model combinations, and supervised learning with fisheye ground truth (SL+) achieves 50%+ improvement in 77/135 cases. Gains are most dramatic on the outdoor KITTI360 dataset, where VGGT's ATE drops from 0.768 to 0.117 (85% reduction) under SL+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines—VGGT, π³, and MapAnything—are outperformed by Fisheye3R across every metric, dataset, and learning scheme. The unmodified baselines suffer severe degradation on fisheye inputs: for example, VGGT on KITTI360 achieves only 0.829 RRA and 0.768 ATE, while the adapted version reaches 1.000 RRA and 0.117 ATE under SL+. MapAnything shows the largest absolute gains on ScanNet++ (ATE: 0.163→0.066) and KITTI360 (ATE: 1.215→0.152), suggesting its multi-modal encoder benefits most from latent-space recalibration. Notably, even the weakest scheme (SSL, no ground truth at all) surpasses the unmodified baselines on nearly all metrics, with 50%+ relative improvement in 26/135 cases. The masked attention mechanism ensures that perspective-image performance is fully preserved (backwards compatibility), a property none of the baselines offer when handling mixed-camera sequences. On the outdoor KITTI360 dataset, where radial distortion is most extreme, the SL+ scheme achieves 50%+ improvement in the majority of metrics, confirming that the calibration tokens generalize beyond indoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of unified feed-forward 3D reconstruction foundation models (VGGT, π³, MapAnything) by introducing a lightweight token-based adaptation mechanism in latent space, rather than the conventional image-space rectification or full finetuning. It draws on the adapter/LoRA paradigm of inserting learnable tokens into frozen transformer layers, but departs from prior fisheye-aware 3D perception work (which typically relies on canonical projections, explicit calibration solvers, or isolated subtasks like monocular depth) by operating directly on the native distorted input. The masked attention mechanism for camera-type-conditional activation is a novel contribution that addresses the backwards-compatibility problem not previously tackled in the multi-camera 3D reconstruction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: mixed-camera systems in AR/VR, robotics, and autonomous driving require a single model to handle both perspective and fisheye inputs without deploying separate networks, and this paper provides a general, drop-in solution for any transformer-based 3R foundation model. The finding that self-supervised adaptation using only unlabeled perspective RGB images (no fisheye data, no ground truth) yields large improvements challenges the assumption that domain-specific annotated data is necessary for camera-model adaptation. Limitations include restriction to transformer architectures and dependence on the base model's quality in the SSL regime; open questions include whether the approach extends to non-central or catadioptric cameras and whether the calibration tokens can be made fully projection-agnostic rather than generalizing only partially across distortion models.
