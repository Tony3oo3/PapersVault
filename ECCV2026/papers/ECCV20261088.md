## Problem

Self-supervised RGB-T tracking relies on forward-backward loops for supervision, but pseudo-labels are unreliable due to dynamic modality discrepancies, causing error accumulation and modality drift throughout the tracking loop. The regulation of cross-modal interaction—both during progressive feature extraction and at the critical forward-to-backward transition—remains insufficiently addressed in existing methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261088/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes synchronized RGB and thermal infrared video streams along with an initial target bounding box; template crops are resized to 128×128 and search regions to 256×256. At inference, it outputs a single target bounding box per frame. During training, no per-frame annotations are used—supervision comes solely from requiring the backward trajectory to recover the initial target location, forming a closed-loop verification signal.

## Method

LGCTrack introduces a local-to-global coordination strategy within a dual-stream ViT-Base encoder. At the local level, a Hierarchical Local Coordination (HLC) module is inserted as a bypass branch at every encoder layer: each modality's queries attend to the other modality's keys and values via cross-attention, and a per-token adaptive gate (sigmoid of a two-layer network) controls how much cross-modal information is injected as a residual, preventing unreliable cues from propagating. At the global level, a Turning-phase Global Coordination (TGC) module operates at the forward-to-backward transition: each modality's global token is updated by attending to the other modality's spatial tokens, and the refined token is written to the opposite stream (cross-write initialization), coupling the two branches under the closed-loop objective. Training proceeds by tracking forward from the initial frame to the end of a clip, applying TGC at the turning phase, then tracking backward to the initial frame, with focal, GIoU, and L1 losses computed only at the recovered initial position.

## Results

On LasHeR, LGCTrack achieves 59.5% PR, 55.3% NPR, and 45.7% SR, the best among all self-supervised methods and within ~11 points of the top fully supervised tracker (GMMT: 70.7% PR). On RGBT234, it reaches 84.7% PR and 62.0% SR, and on GTOT it attains 91.9% PR and 77.6% SR, slightly surpassing the fully supervised CAFormer (91.8% PR, 76.9% SR). LGCTrack also runs at 52.0 FPS, the fastest among all compared methods, with 167.1M parameters and 178.4G FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among self-supervised baselines, LGCTrack outperforms SSTrack [54] by +4.7% PR / +7.3% SR on LasHeR, +6.2% PR / +6.2% SR on RGBT234, and +12.5% PR / +12.3% SR on GTOT. Against GDSTrack [20], the margins are even larger: +13.6% PR / +10.3% SR on LasHeR, +13.8% PR / +13.5% SR on RGBT234, and +18.0% PR / +17.8% SR on GTOT. Against the strongest fully supervised trackers, LGCTrack narrows the gap substantially: it trails GMMT [37] by only 3.2% PR on RGBT234 and 1.7% PR on GTOT, and actually surpasses CAFormer [45] on GTOT (91.9 vs 91.8 PR; 77.6 vs 76.9 SR). On the challenging large-scale LasHeR benchmark, a ~11-point gap to the best supervised methods (GMMT, TBSI) remains, but LGCTrack still leads all self-supervised approaches by a clear margin. In terms of efficiency, LGCTrack runs at 52.0 FPS versus 15.0 FPS for GDSTrack and 23.3 FPS for Un-Track [42], making it the fastest method in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

LGCTrack builds directly on SSTrack's forward-backward self-supervised tracking loop and ODTrack's global-token mechanism, extending both to the RGB-T setting. It departs from prior self-supervised RGB-T trackers (GDSTrack, S2OTFormer) that focus on stronger cross-modal fusion by instead emphasizing *controllable* cross-modal interaction: a reliability-aware hierarchical gate at every encoder layer (HLC) and a turning-phase global-token refinement (TGC) that cross-writes refined tokens to the opposite modality stream. Conceptually, the paper reframes the core difficulty of loop-based self-supervised RGB-T tracking as a coordination problem across two granularities (local feature exchange vs. global target-state stabilization) rather than a fusion-strength problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial and consistent gains over prior self-supervised baselines (e.g., +13.6% PR over GDSTrack on LasHeR, +12.5% PR over SSTrack on GTOT) and the near-parity with fully supervised trackers like CAFormer on GTOT demonstrate that dense per-frame annotations are not strictly necessary for competitive RGB-T tracking, which has direct practical value given the cost of thermal-video annotation. The local-to-global coordination design is modular and could inform other multi-modal tracking or detection settings where modality reliability is dynamic. Open questions include whether the all-layer HLC and cross-write TGC choices generalize beyond ViT-Base backbones, whether the approach extends to longer sequences or more than two modalities, and whether the closed-loop verification signal can be further strengthened (e.g., with multi-loop or bidirectional consistency).
