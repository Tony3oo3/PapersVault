## Problem

The impact of real-world visual noise on Open Vocabulary Object Detectors (OV-ODs) remains poorly understood due to their architectural complexity (multiple transformer backbones, fusion networks, box predictors) and the near-impossibility of finding well-annotated noise-paired real-world datasets. This paper asks: where in the OV-OD architecture does robustness degrade, why does it degrade there, and what is the most effective direction to improve it?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The OV-ODs under analysis take an RGB image and a text prompt/caption as input, producing bounding boxes, class labels, and confidence scores. For robustness evaluation, synthetic noise (pixelation via downsample-upsample, turbulence via GAN, motion blur) is applied only to the input image while textual captions remain unchanged. The key metric is relative robustness = 1 − (Acc_clean − Acc_noisy)/Acc_clean, enabling cross-model and cross-dataset comparison independent of absolute performance. The proposed NN&TK0 module operates on a fully frozen OV-OD, inserting 32×32 trainable spatial tokens at each vision backbone layer and a single-head self-attention non-local block across layers.

## Method

Robust Onion is an interpretability-driven empirical framework that categorizes noise-induced feature collapse into observable (distinct noisy-clean clusters) and minimal (little separation) types, then uses controlled synthetic noises as practical proxies for real-world degradations. By peeling OV-ODs layer-by-layer with UMAP visualizations of backbone, enhancer, and fusion features across noise severities, the analysis reveals that the vision backbone—particularly shallow layers—dominates robustness, while pretraining strategy, architectural modules, and language/caption expressiveness contribute minimally. To validate these insights, the paper proposes NN&TK0, a lightweight plug-and-play approach: TK0 extends LR-TK0's trainable tokens to hierarchical transformers (Swin) by inserting a fixed 32×32 set of spatial tokens at each backbone layer (interpolated to match resolution), and NN adds a single-head self-attention non-local block that cross-exchanges spatial token information across all layers. This combination achieves robustness comparable to end-to-end fine-tuning using only 2.41M trainable parameters (96× fewer), validated on real-world driving datasets (BDD-100K, DAWN, Foggy Cityscapes, Virtual KITTI 2), WiderFace, and VisDRONE.

## Results

The proposed NN & TK0 approach (2.41M trainable parameters) achieves robustness comparable to end-to-end training (231.76M params) on real-world noisy driving datasets while using 96× fewer parameters. On DAWN, NN & TK0 reaches 36.1 mAP (slightly exceeding E2E's 36.0 and Fuse's 35.6), and on Foggy Cityscapes and Virtual KITTI 2 it matches Fuse (29.9 vs 29.8) while remaining well below E2E (31.6). Critically, NN & TK0 preserves zero-shot COCO performance at 33.4 mAP versus only 3.0 (E2E) and 3.8 (Fuse), demonstrating that the frozen OV-OD backbone retains its generalization ability. On the training domain (BDD-100K), NN & TK0 achieves 55.2 mAP versus 66.4 (E2E) and 65.1 (Fuse), a gap the authors attribute to the limited capacity of a single-head self-attention block versus a full multi-layer fusion decoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four adaptation strategies for GLIP-T: end-to-end training (E2E, 231.76M params), fusion-only training (Fuse, 91.85M params), and the proposed TK0 (1.57M) and NN (0.84M) individually and combined as NN & TK0 (2.41M). On the most challenging real-world transfer benchmarks (DAWN, Foggy Cityscapes, Virtual KITTI 2), NN & TK0 matches or slightly exceeds Fuse and is within ~1.7 mAP of E2E, while using 96× fewer trainable parameters. The decisive advantage is zero-shot COCO preservation: NN & TK0 retains 33.4 mAP versus 3.0 (E2E) and 3.8 (Fuse), confirming that modifying only the vision backbone's shallow layers and adding cross-layer token exchange avoids the catastrophic forgetting that full-model or fusion-only fine-tuning causes. On the in-domain BDD-100K evaluation, E2E and Fuse still lead (66.4/65.1 vs. 55.2), indicating that the lightweight approach trades some in-domain accuracy for cross-domain robustness and zero-shot retention. The paper also validates on WiderFace (NN & TK0: 13.23 AP vs. E2E 10.86, Fuse 10.55, 0-shot 11.98) and VisDRONE (NN & TK0: 30.97 AP-50 vs. E2E 30.56, Fuse 29.89, 0-shot 27.09), where it achieves the best results with only 2.41M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robustness analysis framework from LR0.FM [41], which studied feature collapse in VLM-based image classification, to the substantially more complex setting of Open Vocabulary Object Detectors with their multi-component architectures (vision backbone, FPN, fusion, text encoder, box predictor). It departs from prior robustness benchmarks for closed-vocabulary detectors (COCO-O [35], Chhipa et al. [13], Liu et al. [32]) by introducing a controlled synthetic-noise protocol (turbulence, motion blur, pixelation) that mimics two distinct feature-collapse regimes, enabling layer-by-layer 'peeling' of OV-OD components. The paper also unifies and explains several previously disconnected observations from the robustness literature (Table 3), positioning itself as an interpretability-driven study rather than a new detection method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic, component-level analysis of how visual noise degrades OV-ODs, providing practitioners with a clear design roadmap: invest in the vision backbone (depth and cross-layer exchange) rather than auxiliary modules or caption engineering. The 96× parameter efficiency of the NN & TK0 plug-and-play approach over end-to-end fine-tuning is practically significant for resource-constrained deployments in autonomous driving, surveillance, and medical imaging. Open questions remain: whether noise-aware caption training (requiring new datasets) could unlock additional robustness gains, whether the synthetic-to-real transfer holds for degradation types not covered (e.g., sensor-specific artifacts), and whether the backbone-dominance finding persists as OV-OD architectures evolve toward unified end-to-end designs.
