## Problem

Existing training-free vision token pruning methods for LVLMs rely on text-to-vision attention-score magnitudes to rank token importance, but these scores are confounded by positional bias and visual attention sinks, causing instruction-irrelevant tokens to be retained while truly instruction-relevant ones are pruned. Additionally, layer-wise pruning that reuses bypassed tokens in subsequent blocks introduces a representation gap between tokens that pass through self-attention and those that do not, destabilizing importance estimation in deeper layers.

## Input / Output

DIVA operates on standard LVLM inputs: an image (or sequence of video frames) encoded into vision tokens, plus a text instruction, and produces the same autoregressive text response as the unpruned model. Pruning is applied at every LLM block starting from block 2, with a configurable per-block token budget (e.g., 8–640 tokens for LLaVA-1.5-7B, 40–640 for LLaVA-NeXT-7B). The method is fully training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no modification to the pretrained model weights.

## Method

DIVA-Pruner computes a Context Shift (CS) score for each vision token by measuring the forward Kullback-Leibler divergence between two text-to-vision attention distributions: one induced by a contextual probe set (suffix tokens plus instruction tokens selected via cosine-similarity comparison against suffix and prefix references) and one induced by a non-contextual probe set (a single prefix token that cannot attend to the instruction under causal masking). Only the top-K vision tokens by CS score are fed through self-attention at each block, reducing KV-cache memory and attention FLOPs. A lightweight VF-Calibrator then emulates the self-attention value aggregation for bypassed tokens by taking a convex combination (γ=0.1–0.3) of each token's own value feature and the <BOS> token's value feature, projecting through the output layer, and applying a residual update, thereby closing the representation gap and enabling stable token re-scoring in subsequent blocks.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, DIVA achieves 100.9% relative performance at 128 tokens (78% reduction), 99.8% at 64 tokens (89% reduction), and 96.1% at 32 tokens (94% reduction), outperforming all 10+ baselines at every budget. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B (high-resolution, 2880 baseline tokens), DIVA retains 98.1% at 640 tokens and 92.6% at 80 tokens (97.2% reduction), the latter with a 10.8× KV-cache reduction. Under extreme 99% compression (8 tokens) on LLaVA-1.5-7B, DIVA still retains 89.0% of baseline performance, and on LLaVA-1.5-13B it maintains 90.4% at 99% reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVA outperforms all 11 baselines across every token budget tested. FastV [8] is consistently the weakest baseline, dropping to 84.8% at 128 tokens and 63.9% at 32 tokens, far below DIVA. Among the stronger competitors, VisionTrim [50] and D²Pruner [52] are the closest at 128 tokens (both 100.1% vs. DIVA's 100.9%), but DIVA pulls ahead at tighter budgets: at 64 tokens DIVA reaches 99.8% vs. VisionTrim's 99.5% and D²Pruner's 98.2%, and at 32 tokens DIVA achieves 96.1% vs. CDPruner's 95.2% (the next best). On LLaVA-NeXT-7B, DIVA leads at every budget, with the gap widening at aggressive compression (92.6% at 80 tokens vs. no baseline reported at that level; VisionZip at 320 tokens reaches only 92.7%). On fine-grained OCR/document tasks (Table 3), DIVA's advantage is most pronounced: at 10% token retention on Qwen3-VL-8B, DIVA reaches 82.6% relative performance vs. PDrop's 68.1% and FastV's 59.5%. On video understanding (Table 4), DIVA with 16 tokens/frame achieves 94.5% relative performance, surpassing DivPrune [2] (94.2%) and CDPruner [54] (90.2%) which use 32 tokens/frame.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIVA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: attention-based vision token pruning (FastV, SparseVLM, PDrop) and layer-wise adaptive pruning with token reuse (ZipVL, SparseVLM's deleted pools). It departs from prior attention-based methods by replacing raw text-to-vision attention magnitudes—which are confounded by positional bias and visual attention sinks—with a dual-probe Kullback-Leibler divergence score that isolates instruction-conditioned signal. The VF-Calibrator addresses a gap left by prior layer-wise reuse strategies (ZipVL, SparseVLM), which carry bypassed tokens forward without compensating for the asymmetric self-attention update, by emulating the value-aggregation step with a lightweight BOS-anchored mixing operation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.8× KV-cache reduction at 92.6% retained performance on LLaVA-NeXT-7B makes DIVA practically relevant for deploying LVLMs under tight memory budgets, and the dual-probe discrepancy principle is general enough to potentially transfer to other token-pruning or token-selection settings beyond vision. The diagnostic analysis showing that conventional attention-based ranking captures sink tokens with near-1.0 recall across layers is a useful warning for the field: prior pruning methods may have been systematically retaining irrelevant tokens. Open questions include whether the fixed γ in VF-Calibrator generalizes across architectures without per-model tuning, whether the approach extends to audio or other non-visual token streams, and whether the KLD-based scoring remains effective when the instruction is very short or ambiguous.
