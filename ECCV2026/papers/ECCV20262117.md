## Problem

MLLMs struggle to bridge the representational gap between 2D semantic understanding and 3D spatial geometry. Existing 3D-aware approaches either depend on costly 3D-specific data (point clouds, depth maps) or, when using RGB-only inputs, rely on topology-agnostic frame sampling that disrupts spatiotemporal connectivity and static monolithic fusion that induces cross-modal contention across diverse spatial reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262117/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes long RGB video sequences (initially downsampled to 128 candidate frames, then adaptively sparsified to as few as 8–32 keyframes) together with a natural language instruction. Camera poses and implicit 3D geometric features are extracted from the sparse frames via VGGT, while 2D visual and text tokens are encoded by Qwen3-VL. The output is an autoregressive textual response addressing spatial reasoning queries such as route planning, relative direction, object counting, and metric distance estimation.

## Method

SpaR3D-MoE introduces two key mechanisms. First, Adaptive Spatiotemporal Manifold Sampling (ASMS) constructs a spatiotemporal graph with a composite distance metric combining camera translation, geometric feature cosine similarity, and temporal interval, then performs quality-gated farthest point sampling (modulated by a motion-aware quality gate that penalizes low-information or motion-blurred frames) to extract topologically crucial keyframes. Second, the Heterogeneous Geometry-Inductive Mixture-of-Experts (HGI-MoE) routes multimodal tokens to four architecturally distinct experts—holistic additive fusion, geometric-semantic cross-attention, a pose-conditioned dynamic adapter using a HyperNet, and a gravity-aligned structural expert with learnable probes—via an Instruction-Pose Aware Router that concatenates instruction, visual, and geometric tokens through an MLP and adds camera pose as a global spatial bias in the routing logit space. A Top-K gating mechanism with a load-balancing loss ensures stable expert utilization, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy plus the auxiliary routing penalty.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, SpaR3D-MoE achieves a state-of-the-art average score of 63.5 using only 32 non-uniformly sampled sparse RGB frames, surpassing the strongest open-source baseline Qwen3VL-8B (55.7) by 7.8 points and the best commercial model Gemini-1.5-Pro (45.4) by 18.1 points. The largest relative gains appear in Route Plan (44.0 vs. 32.5 for Qwen3VL-8B, +35.4%) and Relative Direction (70.1 vs. 46.3 for Gemini-1.5-Pro, +51.4%). On ScanQA, the method reaches EM@1 of 30.4 and CIDEr of 101.5, and on SQA3D it achieves an average EM@1 of 58.3, establishing SOTA among video-based RGB-only methods across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaR3D-MoE decisively outperforms all spatial-aware MLLM baselines on VSI-Bench: it exceeds Spatial-MLLM (48.4) by 15.1 points, VGLLM-4B (46.1) by 17.4 points, Spacer (45.5) by 18.0 points, and ViLaSR (45.4) by 18.1 points, with the largest margins in Route Plan (+10.5 over Spatial-MLLM) and Relative Direction (+23.9 over Spatial-MLLM). Against the strongest general-purpose MLLM baselines, it surpasses Qwen3VL-8B (55.7) by 7.8 points and Gemini-1.5-Pro (45.4) by 18.1 points, while using only 32 sparse frames versus ~85 dense frames for Gemini. On ScanQA, SpaR3D-MoE (EM@1 30.4, CIDEr 101.5) edges out Video-3D LLM (EM@1 30.1, CIDEr 102.1) on exact match but trails by 0.6 on CIDEr, while clearly beating 3D-LLaVA (CIDEr 92.6) and all other 3D/2.5D-input models. On SQA3D, it achieves 58.3 average EM@1, narrowly trailing Video-3D LLM (58.6) but surpassing the best video-based RGB-only method Spatial-MLLM-4B (55.9) by 2.4 points and all Qwen2.5-VL variants by 11+ points. Overall, the method wins or ties on every benchmark against all RGB-only video baselines, and its advantage is most pronounced on complex navigation and fine-grained metric estimation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: RGB-based 3D-aware MLLMs (Spatial-MLLM, VGLLM, Spacer) and Mixture-of-Experts architectures (Mixtral, MoE-LLaVA, UniMoE). It departs from prior 3D-spatial MLLMs by replacing their static, monolithic fusion of 2D visual and 3D geometric features with a heterogeneous MoE whose experts perform varying levels of cross-modal interaction, and by replacing topology-agnostic uniform frame sampling with a manifold-aware adaptive strategy. The MoE paradigm itself is repurposed from language and general multimodal scaling into the specific problem of 3D spatial reasoning, where the routing signal is augmented with camera pose and instruction semantics rather than purely token-level content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA 3D spatial reasoning from only 32 sparse RGB frames (versus ~85 dense frames for Gemini-1.5-Pro), making the approach far more deployable in embodied and resource-constrained settings. The finding that a pose-conditioned dynamic adapter expert (E2) is the single most critical component (4.2-point drop when removed) highlights that explicit ego-motion conditioning is essential for sparse-view spatial alignment, a lesson transferable to other multi-view 3D tasks. Open questions include whether the expert specialization generalizes to unseen spatial task types, whether the framework scales to online/streaming video (acknowledged as future work), and whether the load-balancing dynamics remain stable at larger expert counts or longer sequences.
