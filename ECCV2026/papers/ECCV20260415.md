## Problem

Existing scene text detectors fail on small, blurred, distant text, yet manually annotating such instances at scale is prohibitively expensive, and no existing video dataset captures the natural large-to-small scale progression needed to study early detection. The paper addresses this by asking whether reliable detections of large, clear text in later video frames can be automatically back-tracked to generate supervision for their earlier, degraded counterparts without any human annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260415/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes ego-centric video sequences (5–15 s clips) in which scene text naturally transitions from small and blurry to large and legible; a single-point prompt at the center of a detected text quadrilateral initiates SAM-2 carrier-object tracking, and CoTracker3 tracks bounding points across preceding frames. At inference the trained student detector operates independently on each RGB frame with no tracking module or future-frame access, outputting quadrilateral text boxes and (in spotting mode) recognized text strings. The introduced SceneText50 benchmark provides 841 clips, 67,371 annotated frames, and 14,263 text trajectories with an average scale ratio of 7.94× between the smallest and largest appearance of each instance.

## Method

The core mechanism is a hybrid large-to-small back-tracker that avoids tracking the text pixels directly (unreliable when small and textureless) and instead tracks the carrier object (signboard, vehicle panel) via SAM-2 segmentation. To recover the text quadrilateral in each back-tracked frame, the method fits a fixed-vertex, equiangular core polygon to each segmentation mask and estimates a RANSAC homography between consecutive core polygons, projecting the reference-frame text box into the target frame while preserving orientation and character order. A complementary point-based tracker (CoTracker3, confidence > 0.8) handles instances that do not reach the final frame, and a strict overlap-priority protocol merges both sources into a dense pseudo-label set. Training freezes the image encoder and fine-tunes only the decoder on these pseudo-labels with EMA (decay 0.999), AdamW, and batch size 8 on a single A6000, yielding a student detector that detects and recognizes distant text several frames earlier than the teacher while maintaining comparable precision on large, clear instances.

## Results

On the proposed SceneText50 dataset, the method achieves AUC 44.71, Success@0.5IoU 49.23, and Precision@20px 74.62, outperforming the strongest baseline CoTracker3 (38.65 / 44.01 / 57.02) by 6.06, 5.22, and 17.60 points respectively. On DSText, the gap widens further: AUC 85.95 vs 80.59 and Precision@20px 73.62 vs 57.54. For the self-learning detection task, pseudo-label training more than doubles Grounding DINO's precision (14.21 → 34.03) and improves DeepSolo's recall from 37.75 to 40.27, while maintaining comparable precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins on all three metrics across all three datasets against every baseline. The closest competitor is CoTracker3 (point-based tracking), which the method surpasses by 6.06 AUC and 17.60 P@20 on SceneText50, and by 5.36 AUC and 16.08 P@20 on DSText; on ArTVideo the margin is minimal (0.20 AUC, 0.09 P@20), suggesting the task is already near saturation for large, clear text. Among single-object trackers, MixFormer is the strongest (AUC 37.77 on SceneText50) but still trails by 6.94 AUC and 8.14 P@20. SAM-2, used as the carrier-object segmentation backbone, performs worst when applied directly as a tracker (AUC 26.43 on SceneText50), confirming that raw segmentation masks without the core-polygon homography correspondence are insufficient for geometric text localization. SeqTrack and ARTrackv2 show moderate performance on ArTVideo but degrade substantially on SceneText50 and DSText, where small-text degradation is more severe. SiamRPN++ and STARK, representing older Siamese and transformer architectures, are consistently outperformed by 5–15 points across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-improvement paradigm in visual perception—where high-confidence predictions serve as supervision for harder cases (as in scene-adaptive detection [42,44] and early event detection [12,13])—to the specific problem of small, distant scene text. For the tracking component, it departs from both detection-based trackers (MixFormer, STARK) and point-based trackers (CoTracker3) by introducing a carrier-object strategy: rather than tracking the text pixels directly, it tracks the larger surface the text is printed on using SAM-2, then recovers text geometry via a novel core-polygon homography. The core polygon formulation, with its fixed-vertex equiangular constraint and a convergence guarantee (Theorem 1), is a new geometric tool not previously applied to scene text correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving, robotics, and assistive technology where early recognition of distant signage is safety-critical but current detectors fail. SceneText50 fills a notable benchmark void as the first video dataset explicitly capturing small-to-large text transitions across 17 countries, enabling evaluation of temporal consistency and early-detection capability. Limitations include the focus on Latin-script text, ego-centric outdoor videos only, and reliance on offline multi-module tracking for label generation; open questions include generalization to non-Latin scripts, indoor scenes, and whether the carrier-object strategy extends to text on highly deformable or transparent surfaces.
