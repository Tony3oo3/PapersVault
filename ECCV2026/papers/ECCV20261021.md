## Problem

Photorealistic, animatable sign avatars are essential for natural communication with the Deaf community, but no existing dataset simultaneously provides multi-view sign language video, dedicated head-portrait views, and precise SMPL-X annotations needed for avatar modeling. Prior avatar methods target general body motion and fail to capture the fine-grained hand articulations and nuanced facial expressions that carry meaning in sign language.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261021/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view RGB video (16 cameras at 2048×2448, 25 fps) or monocular RGB video (1920×1080) along with SMPL-X driving pose parameters to produce a photorealistic 3D sign avatar rendered to arbitrary novel views. The avatar is internally decomposed into three Gaussian attribute maps at different resolutions—body (1024×512), hands (256×256), and head (256×128)—each predicted by a dedicated StyleUNet and deformed via linear blend skinning before splatting-based rasterization.

## Method

The core contribution is a decoupled sign avatar representation that splits the whole-body 3D Gaussians into anatomically distinct body, head, and hand components, each processed by a specialized StyleUNet. A key mechanism is partial kinematic decoupling for hands: body joints are fixed in canonical pose while only hand joints are active, so the hand pose maps depend solely on hand gestures and generalize independently of body motion. A motion-aware sampling strategy filters motion-blurred frames using hand confidence scores, Laplacian gradients, and SMPL-X velocity, then rebalances gesture categories to prevent overfitting to static poses. For annotation, a hybrid SMPL-X fitting pipeline triangulates 2D keypoints from DWPose and HaMeR across views, fits body parameters with a robust-kernel objective, and replaces facial parameters with INFERNO outputs, followed by temporal smoothing via SmoothNet.

## Results

On the MVSign multi-view benchmark, PHOSA achieves PSNR 27.03 / SSIM 0.9689 / LPIPS 0.0393 (full body), outperforming the strongest baseline Mmlphuman (25.91 / 0.9652 / 0.0460) by 8.1% relative LPIPS reduction. On the hand region, PHOSA reaches LPIPS 0.2568 vs. 0.2675 for Mmlphuman (4.0% gain), and on the face region LPIPS 0.1757 vs. 0.1869 (5.9% gain). On in-the-wild monocular sign videos, the advantages widen to 15.8%, 11.6%, and 12.6% relative LPIPS improvements on full, hand, and face regions respectively. For SMPL-X fitting accuracy on the SGNify mocap dataset, the hybrid pipeline achieves the lowest per-vertex error (40.38 mm body, 14.11 mm left hand, 13.79 mm right hand), beating the next-best NSA by ~2 mm on each hand.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHOSA outperforms all five avatar-modeling baselines (SplattingAvatar, GaussianAvatar, AnimatableGS, EVA, Mmlphuman) across every metric on both MVSign and in-the-wild settings. The closest competitor is Mmlphuman [57], which trails by 1.12 dB PSNR and 0.0067 LPIPS on full-body MVSign rendering; the gap widens substantially on the hand region (0.2568 vs. 0.2675 LPIPS) and on in-the-wild monocular video (0.0773 vs. 0.1023 LPIPS full-body). EVA [19], which explicitly targets expressive avatars, is second-best on full-body PSNR (25.56) but lags PHOSA by 1.47 dB and 0.0043 LPIPS. For SMPL-X fitting accuracy (Table 3), the hybrid pipeline beats all six prior estimators—FrankMocap, PIXIE, PyMAF-X, SMPLify-X, SGNify, and NSA—achieving the lowest per-vertex error on body (40.38 mm) and both hands (14.11 / 13.79 mm), with the largest margin over NSA (16.17 / 15.23 mm on hands). In the user study with 20 Deaf participants, PHOSA is preferred over all three representative baselines on every criterion, with 56.8% choosing it for comprehensibility and 62.4% for hand/facial clarity, versus at most 25.2% for the next-best method (Mmlphuman) on temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Animatable Gaussians framework [Li et al., CVPR 2024], which uses StyleUNet to predict pose-dependent Gaussian maps via template-guided 2D parameterization and LBS deformation, by introducing a decoupled representation that separates body, head, and hands into independent Gaussian maps. The key architectural departure is partial kinematic decoupling for hands—fixing body joints while preserving hand mobility—so that hand pose maps become independent of body pose, improving gesture generalization. On the data side, the paper fills a clear gap: existing multi-view human datasets (DNA-Rendering, ZJU-MoCap, HumanRF) lack sign-specific hand/facial detail, while sign language datasets (PHOENIX-14T, How2Sign, SignAvatars) lack multi-view imagery and precise SMPL-X annotations needed for avatar modeling. The hybrid SMPL-X fitting pipeline (DWPose + HaMeR + INFERNO + triangulation) is a novel annotation contribution that also serves as a plug-in for monocular settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MVSign is the first multi-view Chinese sign language benchmark with dedicated head views and SMPL-X annotations, enabling systematic evaluation of photorealistic sign avatars—a task previously lacking any standardized benchmark. The decoupled representation with partial kinematic decoupling is a practical technique that other fine-grained avatar tasks (e.g., interacting hands, expressive faces) could adopt. However, the dataset is small-scale (5 signers, ~115K frames), limiting conclusions about generalization across diverse signers and sign languages. The three-StyleUNet architecture limits real-time inference, and the paper acknowledges this as a key limitation; replacing it with lighter backbones while preserving fidelity remains an open challenge. The user study with Deaf community members (82.5% preferring photorealistic avatars over SMPL-X meshes for comprehensibility) adds important human-centered validation that purely signal-based metrics miss.
