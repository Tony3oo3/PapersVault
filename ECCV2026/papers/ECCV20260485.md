## Problem

Existing DiT-based motion transfer methods manipulate motion representations (noise warping, RoPE manipulation, cross-frame attention) without understanding how motion and structure are internally encoded within the model. This lack of mechanistic understanding leads to generated videos with plausible but inaccurate trajectories and misaligned object structures relative to the reference. The paper asks: how are motion and structural cues internally encoded in video DiTs, and can this knowledge be leveraged for controllable motion transfer?

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (providing motion dynamics and spatial layout) and a target text prompt (defining the desired content) as input, and produces a generated video that follows the reference motion while rendering the prompted subject. It operates on the latent representations of CogVideoX (or Wan) with 50 denoising steps and 12 optimization steps; the reference video is consumed twice—once to extract inter-frame displacement maps from motion-specific heads and once to extract value features from structure-specialized heads for injection during generation.

## Method

HALO is a training-free framework built on a key empirical finding: video DiTs contain two functionally distinct attention head subsets—motion-specific heads (identified by strong cross-frame patch correspondences in displacement maps) and structure-specialized heads (identified by low attention-map entropy with sharply diagonal patterns). For motion fidelity, HALO constructs inter-frame displacement maps using only motion-specific heads, refines them via semantic correspondence computed from diffusion features (selecting the nearest top-k attention candidate to the semantic best match), and optimizes the target latent to minimize L2 distance between reference and generated displacement maps with a semantic reweighting bias. For structural alignment, HALO selectively injects value features from the reference video's low-entropy heads (entropy < 7) into the corresponding heads of the target generation, preserving spatial layout while suppressing noise and identity leakage. This head-level decomposition enables interpretable, parameter-free control over both motion and structure simultaneously.

## Results

On the standard motion transfer benchmark, HALO achieves the best CLIP score (31.7) and Motion Fidelity (66.2), along with the second-best FTD (19.4), outperforming the strongest DiT-based baseline GWTF (CLIP 31.6, MF 62.5, FTD 21.6). On the Movie Scene Dataset, HALO leads in CLIP (30.5) and MF (52.6) over all compared methods. In a user study with 20 participants, HALO attains the highest preference scores across all three criteria, most notably Motion Accuracy at 93.3 versus 72.8 for GWTF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HALO outperforms the strongest DiT-based baseline GWTF on the two most critical metrics for motion transfer—CLIP (31.7 vs. 31.6) and Motion Fidelity (66.2 vs. 62.5)—while also achieving a lower FTD (19.4 vs. 21.6), though it trails slightly on Temporal Consistency (87.5 vs. 88.2), which the authors note tends to favor more static outputs. Against DiTFlow, HALO gains a substantial 6.6-point MF advantage (66.2 vs. 59.6) and a 0.7-point CLIP edge, at the cost of 2.0 points in TC (87.5 vs. 89.5). HALO dominates RoPECraft across all four metrics (CLIP 31.7 vs. 30.3, TC 87.5 vs. 85.9, MF 66.2 vs. 58.2, FTD 19.4 vs. 19.6). All three U-Net-based baselines (MoFT, ConMO, MotionClone) are clearly outperformed, with MF gaps of 10.6–31.4 points. On the Movie Scene Dataset, HALO leads in both CLIP (30.5) and MF (52.6), confirming robustness under cinematic conditions, and the user study shows a decisive preference for HALO in Motion Accuracy (93.3 vs. 72.8 for GWTF and 69.0 for DiTFlow).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior DiT-based motion transfer methods (DiTFlow, GWTF, RoPECraft) that manipulate motion representations—noise warping, RoPE embeddings, or raw cross-frame attention—without understanding how motion and structure are internally encoded. It introduces the first head-level functional analysis of video DiTs, building on SparseGen's temporal/spatial head classification and displacement-map formulations from DiTFlow, to identify distinct motion-specific and structure-specialized attention heads. The semantic correspondence refinement draws on diffusion-feature correspondence work (Tang et al., Wang et al.), while the entropy-based structural head selection extends attention-entropy analysis from VLM grounding to video DiT structure preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By revealing that unified attention in video DiTs still contains functionally specialized heads for motion and structure, this work provides an interpretable, training-free mechanism for controllable video generation that could generalize beyond motion transfer to other editing tasks (demonstrated with video editing). The Movie Scene Dataset addresses a practical gap by providing production-oriented cinematic evaluation. Open questions include whether the head specialization pattern is stable across architectures and scales, whether explicit control over motion direction and intensity is achievable through these heads, and whether the entropy threshold for structural heads transfers across model sizes.
