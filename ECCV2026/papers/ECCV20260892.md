## Problem

Existing zero-shot video temporal grounding methods rely on per-frame feature matching, which handles simple single-stage events but fails on complex multi-stage queries requiring temporal ordering, causal dependencies, and conditional logic. The paper quantifies this 'reasoning gap' as an 11.6 mIoU drop between simple and complex queries, and asks how to selectively activate structured reasoning in large vision-language models only when a query demands it, without task-specific training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260892/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (downsampled to 3 FPS) and a natural language query as input, and outputs a single start–end timestamp pair (t_s, t_e). Rather than feeding all frames to the LVLM, a query-conditioned DPP greedily selects an adaptive number of keyframes (typically ~12 vs. 86 for baselines) whose count varies per (video, query) pair based on a relative-gain stopping criterion. The LVLM backbone is LLaVA-1.6-7B, and inference uses greedy decoding with a 512-token cap.

## Method

DART builds a query-conditioned DPP kernel that jointly encodes frame–query relevance (via sigmoid-weighted similarity scores) and inter-frame visual-temporal diversity (via a product of visual and temporal Gaussian kernels), then greedily selects keyframes with an adaptive stopping threshold. The eigenspectrum of this kernel yields a spectral entropy that serves as a difficulty indicator: low entropy routes the query to a Fast path where the LVLM directly predicts timestamps, while high entropy triggers a Slow path using Temporal Markup Prompting (TMP). TMP is a structured three-step CoT procedure—global event decomposition, per-frame temporal role annotation from an ordered label set {BEFORE, START, DURING, END, AFTER} with monotonicity constraints, and boundary extraction from the first START and last END labels—executed sequentially in a single generation. The entire pipeline is training-free, leveraging the pre-trained LVLM's reasoning capabilities without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On the IID setting, DART achieves 48.93 mIoU on Charades-STA and 39.89 mIoU on ActivityNet Captions, surpassing the strongest prior zero-shot method TAG by +3.24 and +3.34 mIoU respectively. Under OOD moment-shifted splits, DART maintains 48.1/47.8 mIoU (Charades-STA OOD-1/OOD-2) and 39.7/39.5 mIoU (ActivityNet OOD-1/OOD-2), outperforming TAG by 3.4–3.5 mIoU. On the compositional Charades-CG split, DART reaches 45.30 mIoU (novel-composition) and 51.09 mIoU (novel-word), a +3.35 and +3.23 mIoU gain over TAG. Notably, DART achieves these gains while processing only 12 keyframes per query versus 86 for TFVTG and TAG, with an inference time of 3.9 s per query on a single A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DART outperforms all zero-shot baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest prior zero-shot method TAG [26], DART gains +3.24 mIoU on Charades-STA (48.93 vs. 45.69) and +3.34 mIoU on ActivityNet Captions (39.89 vs. 36.55), with consistent 2–4 point improvements on R@0.5 and R@0.7. Against TFVTG [68], the gain is +4.42 mIoU on Charades-STA and +5.79 mIoU on ActivityNet, and DART uses 7× fewer frames (12 vs. 86). On OOD moment-shifted splits (Table 2), DART extends its lead to +3.4 mIoU over TAG on Charades-STA OOD-1 (48.1 vs. 44.7) and +3.5 mIoU on ActivityNet OOD-1 (39.7 vs. 36.2). On the compositional Charades-CG benchmark (Table 3), DART surpasses TAG by +3.35 mIoU on novel-composition queries and +3.23 mIoU on novel-word queries, and also outperforms the fully supervised VISA [29] by +3.27 mIoU on novel-composition. In the cross-dataset transfer setting (Table 4), DART beats TFVTG by +3.20 mIoU (R@1 R@0.5: 53.17 vs. 49.97) and +4.47 mIoU (R@5 R@0.5: 87.43 vs. 83.50), while also exceeding the supervised 2D-TAN [64] and SCDM [60] baselines. DART also surpasses the weakly supervised CPL [71] and Huang et al. [18] on Charades-STA mIoU, narrowing the gap to the best fully supervised method EMB [16] to 4.16 mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot video temporal grounding line initiated by Luo et al. (WACV 2024) and advanced by TFVTG (ECCV 2024) and TAG (2025), which all treat LVLMs as frozen feature extractors scoring frame-query similarity independently. It departs from this paradigm by introducing difficulty-adaptive routing: a query-conditioned Determinantal Point Process (building on Kulesza & Taskar, 2012) serves the dual role of selecting diverse keyframes and providing a spectral-entropy difficulty signal, while a structured Temporal Markup Prompting procedure (inspired by task-specific CoT work like SpatialVLM) handles complex multi-stage queries. The key conceptual departure is that not all queries require reasoning, and the decision of *when* to reason is derived from the same kernel used for evidence selection, unifying two previously separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a 'reasoning gap' (11.6 mIoU drop on complex vs. simple queries) that prior zero-shot VTG work had not explicitly measured, and shows that structured, task-specific reasoning closes this gap while using 7× fewer frames than baselines. The finding that keyframe quality is a prerequisite for effective reasoning (removing DPP collapses routing and TMP to near-useless) highlights an underappreciated dependency in LVLM-based pipelines. Open questions include whether the spectral-entropy routing generalizes to much longer videos or multi-event queries, whether the routing threshold θ transfers across domains without re-tuning, and whether the approach extends to other temporal tasks (e.g., video QA, highlight detection) where difficulty-adaptive reasoning could similarly help.
