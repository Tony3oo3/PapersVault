## Problem

In segment-level weakly supervised temporal action localization (WTAL), proposal confidence scores are computed via handcrafted post-processing from noisy segment-level predictions, causing a systematic mismatch between score and true proposal quality. This score-quality misalignment degrades both direct baseline localization and the construction of pseudo labels that supervise a downstream localization head, capping achievable accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes I3D-extracted RGB and optical-flow features (D=1024, projected to d=512) from uniformly partitioned video segments, along with video-level action labels as the sole training supervision. It operates at the proposal level using a surrounding-contrast feature extractor, and outputs re-aligned proposal scores defined as the element-wise product of the background-suppressed class score and a learned semantic-completeness score. The re-aligned proposals then feed either directly into Soft-NMS for localization or into a pseudo-label construction pipeline (Gaussian fusion) that trains an ActionFormer/TriDet-style localization head.

## Method

SCLR introduces Semantic Completeness Learning (SCL) to realign proposal scores by assessing quality through proposal-to-class-center similarity in semantic space rather than temporal IoU. For each GT class, SCL constructs a local core semantic center C^SE (anchor selected via rank-fusion of class evidence and actionness, fused with its IoU-neighborhood using Bhattacharyya-coefficient center-scale affinity) and a global teacher center C^TE (aggregated over all proposals with rank-normalized scores and a non-local temporal distance gate). A self-supervised decomposition then separates C^SE into a class-stable generic component (aligned across both centers via cosine loss, stabilized by class-wise EMA) and a video-conditioned specific component (aligned to a background descriptor with the generic subspace projected out), enforced by a decorrelation penalty. Finally, proposal-to-reconstructed-center cosine similarity provides soft labels that supervise a per-proposal completeness branch, whose output multiplies the base score to produce the re-aligned confidence used consistently in both training and inference.

## Results

On THUMOS14, SCLR alone (Ours) improves the DDG-Net baseline from 47.3% to 50.4% average mAP@[0.1:0.7], and with a localization head trained on re-aligned pseudo labels (Ours*), achieves 53.2% average mAP@[0.1:0.7], surpassing the previous SOTA PseudoFormer (51.9%) by 1.3%. On ActivityNet1.3, Ours* reaches 30.5% average mAP@[0.5:0.95], outperforming PseudoFormer (29.0%) by 1.5%. Across four diverse baselines (DELU, DDG-Net, AFPS, LAC), SCLR consistently reduces ECE@0.7 and raises Kendall-τ by ~7.2 points, confirming its plug-and-play score-alignment effect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On THUMOS14, Ours* (53.2% avg mAP) outperforms all weakly supervised baselines, beating PseudoFormer (51.9%) by 1.3%, NoCo (50.9%) by 2.3%, and PivoTAL (49.6%) by 3.6%. SCLR alone (50.4%) already surpasses PivoTAL, AFPS, DDG-Net, and DELU, confirming that score realignment alone recovers 3.1% over the DDG-Net baseline. On ActivityNet1.3, Ours* (30.5%) beats PseudoFormer (29.0%) by 1.5% and PivoTAL (28.1%) by 2.4%, while SCLR alone (27.5%) improves over DDG-Net (25.8%) by 1.7%. In the generalization study (Table 8), SCLR plugged into DELU, DDG-Net, AFPS, and LAC yields consistent mAP gains (e.g., LAC: 52.8→56.4) and sharp ECE@0.7 reductions (e.g., DELU: 63.9→28.8), demonstrating that the framework is backbone-agnostic. The method does not surpass fully supervised TriDet (74.2% on THUMOS14, 36.8% on ActivityNet1.3), but narrows the weak-to-full supervision gap substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCLR builds directly on the S-MIL proposal-level framework (Zhou et al., CVPR 2023) and borrows the surrounding-contrast feature extractor from P-MIL (Ren et al., CVPR 2023), while using ActionFormer/TriDet-style transformers for the downstream localization head. Its key conceptual departure from prior pseudo-label WTAL work (PseudoFormer, NoCo, MLLM4WTAL) is shifting the intervention point upstream: rather than refining pseudo-label construction or selection, SCLR targets the root cause—score-quality misalignment in the baseline's proposal scoring—via a new Semantic Completeness Learning module. It also differentiates from P-MIL and SEAL, which assess completeness through attention-driven overlap with pseudo-labels, by instead constructing class-semantic centers (dual local-global) and learning proposal-center similarity as a class-conditional quality signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying score-quality misalignment as a practical bottleneck and providing a plug-and-play realignment module, SCLR offers a general recipe that can be grafted onto diverse WTAL backbones without architectural changes, which is valuable for the community as baselines evolve. The ablation revealing that the generic (class-stable) component drives most of the gain while the specific (video-conditioned) component contributes little on its own suggests the field may over-invest in video-specific contextual modeling at the proposal level. Open questions include whether the dual-center decomposition and EMA-based class memory remain effective at larger scale (e.g., 200-class ActivityNet with more diverse contexts) and whether the semantic-completeness criterion can be extended to multi-modal or language-guided settings.
