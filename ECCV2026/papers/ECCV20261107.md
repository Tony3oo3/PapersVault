## Problem

Existing Zero-Shot Skeleton Action Recognition methods suffer from two coupled failures: recursive graph convolutions cause spectral smoothing that dilutes high-frequency motion details critical for discrimination, and rigid point-to-point semantic projections ignore that kinetically active limbs carry far richer semantic signal than static ones, leaving cross-modal alignment vulnerable to background noise and 'attention sinks'.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261107/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-person 3D skeleton sequences (B×T×M×N×C, temporally interpolated to 120 frames and projected to 256 dimensions, with joints partitioned into 5 groups padded with virtual nodes) alongside fine-grained text descriptions generated by GPT-4o and encoded via CLIP-ViT-B/32. Output is a single action class label, evaluated under both ZSL (unseen-only search) and GZSL (seen+unseen search with Calibrated Stacking penalty γ=1.5 to counter seen-category bias).

## Method

Saber comprises two coupled modules. The Scale-Aware Bipartite Encoder (SAB-Encoder) first decouples multi-scale temporal dynamics via local and strided temporal folding with Multi-Head Self-Attention, then applies a Multi-Head Instance-Adaptive (MI) paradigm that constructs a bipartite affinity graph between an individual's body-part features and all other participants' features; the operator (I−A) acts as an instance-adaptive structural high-pass filter that preserves high-frequency motion while suppressing low-frequency redundancy. The Kinetically Driven Probabilistic Alignment maps each part-level representation into a variance-adaptive Gaussian whose covariance is exponentially scaled by (1−w), where w is a content-aware kinetic gate derived from LayerNorm'd structural deviation; cross-modal matching then uses reverse KL divergence between these adaptive distributions and unit-variance text prototypes, so kinetically active parts yield tight, precise matching while noisy parts are softly down-weighted. Training optimizes an InfoNCE contrastive loss (τ=0.07) plus an L1 sparsity penalty (λ=0.1) on the energy gates to prevent degenerate uniform gating.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D (X-Sub, 55/5 split), Saber achieves 87.5% ZSL accuracy and 76.1% GZSL harmonic mean, surpassing the previous best (Neuron and FS-VAE at 86.9% ZSL, FS-VAE at 75.7% H). On NTU RGB+D 120 (X-Sub, 110/10 split), it reaches 76.8% ZSL and 65.9% H, outperforming InfoCPL (74.8% ZSL) and SCoPLe (74.5% ZSL, 62.3% H). On PKU-MMD (X-Sub, 46/5 split), Saber attains 79.3% ZSL and 68.6% H, exceeding STAR (76.3% ZSL, 65.0% H). The model uses 10.36M parameters and 2.14G FLOPs, delivering 752 FPS on NTU RGB+D—substantially faster than transport-based methods (TDSM: 341 FPS, Flora: 345 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Saber achieves the best ZSL accuracy across all four standard splits on NTU RGB+D and NTU RGB+D 120, with margins of 0.6–3.6% over the nearest competitors (Neuron, FS-VAE, TDSM, InfoCPL). On the GZSL harmonic mean, Saber leads by 0.4% over FS-VAE on NTU-60 55/5 (76.1 vs 75.7) and by 2.6% over FS-VAE/Neuron on NTU-120 110/10 (65.9 vs 63.3). On PKU-MMD X-Sub, Saber outperforms STAR by 3.0% ZSL (79.3 vs 76.3) and 3.6% H (68.6 vs 65.0) on the 46/5 split. The one notable exception is PKU-MMD X-View 46/5 ZSL, where STAR edges out Saber (75.4 vs 73.5), though Saber still wins on the corresponding GZSL harmonic mean (73.5 vs 72.8). Compared to transport-based methods (TDSM, Flora), Saber matches or exceeds their ZSL accuracy while running 2–5× faster (752 FPS vs 341–345 FPS) with roughly 1/5 the parameters (10.36M vs 53.7–74.25M). Embedding-based baselines (SCoPLe, Neuron, PURLS) and generative baselines (CADA-VAE, SynSE, SA-DVAE) are consistently outperformed by 2–10+ points on ZSL across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

Saber carves new ground at the intersection of graph signal processing and probabilistic cross-modal alignment, departing from all three established ZSAR paradigms (embedding, generative, transport) rather than extending any single one. It repurposes the graph Laplacian operator (I−A) as an instance-adaptive high-pass filter to counteract spectral smoothing in recursive GCNs like ST-GCN and Shift-GCN, and introduces a variance-adaptive Gaussian KL-divergence formulation for part-level semantic anchoring that replaces the rigid point-to-point projections common in embedding methods. The multi-head bipartite attention for multi-person interaction modeling draws on transformer self-attention but applies it to a structurally constrained bipartite graph between an individual's body parts and peer context, a formulation not previously explored in skeleton ZSAR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The kinetic-energy-driven probabilistic alignment offers a physically interpretable mechanism for resolving cross-modal uncertainty: active limbs get low-variance anchors while static regions are naturally suppressed via inflated covariance, providing a principled alternative to ad-hoc attention or generative augmentation. The consistent SOTA across three benchmarks with a compact 10.36M parameter model (vs. 74M for TDSM) suggests the approach is both effective and deployable. Open questions include whether the kinetic gating generalizes beyond skeleton modalities (e.g., video or depth), whether the GPT-4o-generated descriptions introduce a hidden dependency on LLM quality, and whether the probabilistic framework can be extended to few-shot or open-world settings where the category space is not fixed.
