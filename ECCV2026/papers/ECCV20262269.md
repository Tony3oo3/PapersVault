## Problem

Existing explainability methods for vision and language models are designed for fixed-output classification or static contrastive alignment tasks, and cannot capture the dynamic, token-by-token generation process of autoregressive VLMs. In particular, they fail to track how visual information flows through transformer layers at each generation step, and they do not distinguish between tokens that are visually grounded versus those driven purely by linguistic context, leading to noisy and misleading attribution maps.

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image and a text prompt, DEX-AR runs a forward pass through an autoregressive VLM (e.g., LLaVA, PaliGemma, Florence-2) and produces two types of outputs: a per-token 2D heatmap for each generated word highlighting the image regions most influential for that specific token, and an aggregated sequence-level 2D heatmap that weights each token's map by its visual relevance score. The 2D heatmaps are obtained by reshaping the N visual-token gradients into the ViT patch grid (H×W) and applying min-max normalization, so their spatial resolution matches the vision encoder's patch layout rather than the original pixel resolution.

## Method

DEX-AR computes, at each generation step t and layer l, the gradient of the intermediate logit (obtained by projecting the layer-l hidden state through the LM head, following the Logit Lens approach) with respect to the attention map, then extracts only the gradient entries corresponding to the last token's row and the visual-token columns. Two dynamic filtering mechanisms are applied: (1) head-level filtering, where each attention head is weighted by the ReLU of the difference between its maximum gradient magnitude on visual tokens versus text tokens, suppressing heads that attend primarily to linguistic context; and (2) token-level filtering, where each generated token is weighted by the ReLU of the difference between the global maximum visual and text gradient across all layers and heads, suppressing tokens predicted from linguistic priors. The per-token heatmap is a weighted sum over layers and heads, and the sequence-level map is a weighted sum over tokens. A batched computation exploits the disjoint-row structure of causal attention gradients to replace T_a sequential forward/backward passes with a single forward pass and L backward passes, yielding mathematically identical maps at up to 18× speedup.

## Results

On the primary perturbation-based evaluation (AUC of normalized perplexity), DEX-AR achieves the best positive-perturbation scores across all four VLM architectures: 2.31 on LLaVA-1.5, 18.10 on BakLLaVA-v1, 0.85 on Florence-2, and 2.71 on PaliGemma (ImageNet). On PascalVOC segmentation, DEX-AR achieves a soft-IoU of 17.70% and IoU of 36.34% on LLaVA-1.5, representing 73.5% and 25.7% relative improvements over the next-best method. The dual-filtering ablation shows SNR improving from 9.16 (no filtering) to 96.12 (both head and filler-word filtering) on PascalVOC-QA. DEX-AR runs at 0.71 sec/image, making it 8–21× faster than gradient-based baselines like Integrated Gradients (8.20 s) and IIA (15.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEX-AR consistently outperforms all seven baselines on positive-perturbation AUC across all four architectures, with the largest margin on BakLLaVA-v1 (18.10 vs. 13.50 for Integrated Gradients, a 5.5-point gap). On negative perturbation, DEX-AR wins decisively on BakLLaVA-v1 (2.48 vs. 3.74 for Attn×Grad) and ties RISE on Florence-2 (0.78), but is slightly behind IIA on LLaVA-1.5 (0.96 vs. 0.90) and GradCAM on PaliGemma (0.90 vs. 0.87). On PascalVOC segmentation, DEX-AR dominates soft-IoU by 2–3× over all baselines (e.g., 17.70 vs. 10.20 for GradCAM on LLaVA-1.5) and leads on IoU, though GradCAM surpasses it on EPG for PaliGemma (26.95 vs. 20.43) due to its peaky, narrow maps. In terms of runtime, DEX-AR (0.71 s/img) is 8–21× faster than Integrated Gradients (8.20 s), RISE (11.8 s), IIA (15.3 s), and CheferCAM (6.20 s), while remaining comparable to the lighter attention-based methods (GradCAM 0.66 s, Attn×Grad 0.65 s). Attention Rollout and raw Attention serve as weak baselines that DEX-AR surpasses on both perturbation and segmentation metrics across all architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEX-AR extends the gradient-based attribution lineage (GradCAM, Integrated Gradients, Attn×Grad, CheferCAM) and attention-rollout methods into the autoregressive VLM setting, where prior approaches were designed for static classification or contrastive alignment tasks (e.g., CLIP explainability). It builds on the Logit Lens idea of projecting intermediate hidden states into vocabulary space to compute per-layer intermediate logits, and departs from the most recent autoregressive-specific method TAM by using layer-wise gradients on attention maps at each generation step rather than post-hoc causal estimation on static visual features. The dual-filtering mechanism (head-level visual-focus weighting and token-level visual-necessity scoring) is a novel contribution not present in any prior VLM explainability method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are deployed in high-stakes domains (autonomous systems, accessibility tools), the inability to trace which image regions drive specific generated tokens is a critical interpretability gap that this paper addresses with a model-agnostic, architecture-agnostic method. The introduction of a perplexity-based perturbation metric and the PascalVOC-QA dataset with explicit filler-word annotations provides the field with missing evaluation infrastructure for autoregressive VLM explainability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-attention-based or mixture-of-experts architectures, whether the synthetic PascalVOC-QA construction overestimates the practical benefit of token filtering, and how the method scales to very long generated sequences or multi-turn dialogues.
