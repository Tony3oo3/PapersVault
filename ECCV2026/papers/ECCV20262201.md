## Problem

Self-supervised stereo matching relies on photometric consistency, which fundamentally breaks down in occluded regions, producing persistent erroneous feedback on one fixed side of every occluder. Existing remedies—occlusion masking, smoothness regularization, flip augmentation, or local-neighborhood losses—either discard information or depend on unreliable local assumptions, leaving the occlusion bottleneck unresolved regardless of network architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262201/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the method takes a standard RGB stereo pair and outputs a dense disparity map aligned with the left view, identical to the baseline network. During training, the right input is replaced by a pseudo-image generated by the network's own current disparity estimates (pixels relocated backward, max-disparity conflict resolution, void padding), while photometric feedback is always computed against the original real images. Training crops are 512×256 with batch size 8 on two RTX 4090 GPUs; the pseudo-image generation adds ~25% training time but no extra GPU memory and is absent at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a pseudo-stereo inputs strategy that decouples the network's input pair from its feedback pair: the network receives a real image plus a pseudo-generated image, but the photometric loss is computed by warping the original opposite view, so occlusions are probabilistically relocated to either side of an occluder rather than persistently on one side. To handle the resulting 50/50 correct/incorrect feedback conflict, the method identifies occluded pixels directly from the rendering process (out-of-bounds or occluded-by-another-pixel) and detaches their gradients from the photometric loss. A fully pseudo-stereo variant (using I^L/I~^L and I^R/I~^R as inputs) prevents the network from learning to distinguish real from pseudo images, eliminating overfitting observed in long-term training. Refined pseudo-image generation (adjacent-pixel void filling and wider-then-crop edge handling) reduces artifacts that would otherwise degrade accuracy under the fully pseudo-stereo regime. The framework is architecture-agnostic and has been validated on PSMNet, GMStereo, and MonSter backbones across KITTI, SceneFlow, and Spring datasets.

## Results

On the KITTI 2015 test set, Pseudo-Stereo-MonSter achieves 4.39% D1-all and 0.8 px EPE-all, outperforming the best prior method (CRD-Fusion at 6.11% D1-all and 1.1 px EPE-all) by 1.72 percentage points and 0.3 px respectively. On KITTI 2012, Pseudo-Stereo-PSMNet reaches 4.08% 3-px-all and 0.9 px EPE-all, compared to CRD-Fusion's 5.40% and 1.1 px. The method also generalizes across backbones and datasets: on SceneFlow, MonSter with Pseudo-Stereo achieves 2.32 px EPE (vs. 5.28 px without), and on Spring, 1.77 px EPE (vs. 2.37 px without).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pseudo-Stereo outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric on both KITTI 2012 and KITTI 2015. The closest competitor, CRD-Fusion [3], is surpassed by 1.72 pp on D1-all (4.68% vs. 6.11%) and 0.3 px on EPE-all (0.9 vs. 1.1) with PSMNet, and by 1.72 pp / 0.3 px with MonSter. OASM-DDS [17], the second-closest method, trails by 2.43 pp on D1-all (4.68% vs. 6.51%). The weakest baseline, OASM [16], is outperformed by 4.30 pp on D1-all (4.68% vs. 8.98%). PASMnet [31] (KITTI 2015 only) is beaten by 2.55 pp on D1-all. UHP [35] and Flow2Stereo [19] are both surpassed by roughly 1.7–2.0 pp on D1-all. The consistent margin across all baselines and both backbones (PSMNet and MonSter) indicates the gains stem from the occlusion-handling strategy rather than architectural improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the photometric-consistency framework for self-supervised stereo matching (Godard et al., ICCV 2019) but fundamentally departs from the dominant occlusion-handling paradigm. Prior methods either mask occluded regions and compensate with smoothness or local-regularity losses (OASM, PASMnet, OASM-DDS, UHP, CRD-Fusion), or rely on external multi-view hardware or NeRF-based view synthesis (multiscopic vision, NeRF-Supervised). The key conceptual departure is decoupling the network's input images from its feedback images via pseudo-stereo inputs, which transforms fixed one-sided occlusion into probabilistic two-sided occlusion without any additional constraints or hardware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves what the authors demonstrate is a core, architecture-independent bottleneck in self-supervised stereo matching, with substantial gains (D1-all from 5.67% to 4.06% on KITTI 2015; occlusion D1 from 20.82% to 9.05%) validated across three distinct backbones and four datasets. The technique requires no extra hardware, no external data, and only ~25% additional training time, making it broadly adoptable. Open questions remain for textureless and reflective regions, which the authors note are inherently intractable with binocular cues alone; they suggest multi-frame self-supervised paradigms as a complementary direction where the improved single-frame accuracy would provide a stronger foundation.
