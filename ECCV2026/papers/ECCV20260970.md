## Problem

State-of-the-art latent normalizing flows suffer from two coupled limitations: they require explicit noise augmentation on VAE latents (necessitating separate denoising steps at inference) and rely on a frozen VAE encoder that is not jointly optimized with the flow, yielding suboptimal reconstruction and generation quality. The paper asks whether both issues can be resolved with a single, minimal architectural change rather than a complex multi-stage pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260970/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation on ImageNet (256×256 or 512×512), the model takes a class label and samples z ~ N(0, I) in the 64-dimensional VAE latent space; the NF inverts z to a latent x, which the VAE decoder maps to a pixel-space image. For text-to-image (COCO 256×256), a FLAN-T5-XL text encoder replaces the class label. The NF uses a deep-shallow TARFlow architecture (6 blocks, 1152 hidden dim, last block 46 layers) operating on the continuous latent tokens, and a revised score-based CFG step is applied post-generation to sharpen outputs.

## Method

SimFlow fixes the VAE encoder's predicted variance to a constant σ̄² (default 0.5²) so that each latent token is sampled from N(μ, σ̄²I) rather than a near-deterministic point. This single change simultaneously (a) provides built-in noise augmentation that smooths the latent space for NF generalization without external noising/denoising modules, and (b) renders the entropy term in the VAE ELBO a constant, reducing the joint training objective to a simple sum of reconstruction and NF log-likelihood with equal weights. The NF (TARFlow) and VAE (MAR architecture) are trained end-to-end from scratch with no stop-gradient, and the framework is further enhanced by REPA-E, which aligns NF hidden states with frozen DINOv2-B features via a projector, allowing representation gradients to flow back into the VAE encoder. A theoretical analysis shows that fixed variance prevents latent collapse because shrinking inter-image latent distances no longer reduces the generative loss, and a revised CFG formulation applies a score-based correction across all NF blocks rather than only the final one.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, SimFlow achieves a gFID of 2.15 with classifier-free guidance in 160 epochs (1.4B params), outperforming the prior NF method STARFlow (gFID 2.40, 320 epochs) and the diffusion model DiT (gFID 2.27, 1400 epochs). With REPA-E representation alignment, SimFlow further reduces gFID to 1.91, establishing a new state-of-the-art among NF-based models. On ImageNet 512×512, SimFlow+REPA-E achieves gFID 2.74, surpassing STARFlow (3.00) and DiT (3.04). The jointly-trained VAE also improves reconstruction quality (rFID 1.21 vs. STARFlow's 2.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimFlow (gFID 2.15) outperforms the closest NF baseline STARFlow (gFID 2.40) by 0.25 gFID while using half the training epochs (160 vs. 320). Against the representative diffusion model DiT (gFID 2.27, 1400 epochs), SimFlow achieves better gFID with 8× faster convergence. SimFlow+REPA-E (gFID 1.91) sets a new SOTA among all NF-based methods, though it remains behind the best diffusion-based approaches like REPA-E (1.12) and RAE (1.13) on the same benchmark. On ImageNet 512×512, SimFlow+REPA-E (gFID 2.74) surpasses STARFlow (3.00) and DiT (3.04) but trails top diffusion methods like RAE (1.13) and DDT (1.28). The jointly-trained VAE also improves reconstruction (rFID 1.21 vs. STARFlow's 2.73), and the revised CFG method further improves generation quality over STARFlow's CFG with only mild inference overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimFlow builds directly on STARFlow's latent autoregressive flow architecture and the MAR VAE, but departs from the standard two-stage paradigm (train VAE, freeze it, add noise, train NF, denoise) by training the VAE and NF jointly from scratch. The key conceptual departure is that fixing the encoder-predicted variance to a constant (e.g., 0.5) simultaneously replaces the explicit noise-augmentation-and-denoising pipeline of STARFlow/TARFlow and eliminates the need for the stop-gradient mechanism that REPA-E requires to prevent latent collapse during end-to-end training. From the ELBO perspective, this single change makes the entropy term constant, reducing the training objective to just reconstruction plus generation loss, which the authors show is far easier to balance than the three-term objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that NFs can outperform DiT (a representative diffusion model) for the first time on ImageNet 256, with 8× faster convergence, shifts the competitive landscape for high-quality image generation and suggests latent NFs are a more efficient generative paradigm than previously thought. The fixed-variance trick is remarkably simple and generalizable—validated across MAR, SiT, and REPA-E backbones—making it a low-cost addition to any latent generative pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale text-to-image (only preliminary COCO results are shown), whether a second-stage fine-tuning with a frozen VAE after joint training can close the remaining gap to the best diffusion methods (REPA-E at gFID 1.12), and whether the optimal variance is dataset- and architecture-dependent.
