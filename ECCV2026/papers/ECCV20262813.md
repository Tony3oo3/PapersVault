## Problem

Flow- and diffusion-based generative policies for robot manipulation require tens to hundreds of iterative ODE solver steps per action, creating inference latency that is prohibitive for time-sensitive manipulation. Existing few-step acceleration methods either depend on pre-trained teachers, suffer from mode averaging (consistency distillation) or mode collapse (score distillation), or introduce expensive Jacobian-vector products (MeanFlow), leaving a gap for a teacher-free, high-precision one-step policy.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes multi-modal observations (multi-view RGB, 3D point clouds, proprioception, and language instructions) together with a warm-start prior constructed from the unexecuted suffix of the previously generated action chunk, shifted and padded to full horizon H. It outputs a continuous action trajectory chunk of horizon H (end-effector SE(3) poses or joint angles) via a single interval-averaged velocity query from a noised warm-start initialization rather than pure Gaussian noise. The same model supports flexible NFE from 1 to K by chaining sub-interval velocity queries, trading latency for precision.

## Method

OFP is a from-scratch self-distillation framework that learns an interval-averaged velocity field u_θ(z_t, t, r|o) through three complementary losses. First, self-consistency training uses an EMA teacher to predict trajectory endpoints over nested sub-intervals, with a time-contracting schedule that transitions from global to local consistency, avoiding the JVP computations required by MeanFlow. Second, self-guided regularization extracts a Classifier-Free Guidance augmentation signal from the model's own conditional and unconditional predictions (via the analytical score-velocity relationship for OT paths), sharpening one-step outputs toward high-density expert modes without an external teacher. Third, a training-free warm-start mechanism initializes generation from a noised projection of the unexecuted action suffix, reducing the transport distance the single-step flow must bridge. The unified objective combines standard flow-matching anchoring, self-consistency, and self-guidance losses, enabling both one-step and multi-step inference from the same network.

## Results

On 56 simulated manipulation tasks (Adroit, DexArt, MetaWorld), OFP at NFE=1 achieves 71.6% average success rate, outperforming DP3 at NFE=100 (66.4%) by 8% and 3D FM Policy at NFE=100 (59.8%) by 19.7%. In wall-clock time, OFP requires 17.58 ms per action chunk versus 3225.67 ms for DP3 (≈183× speedup) and 1865.72 ms for FM Policy (≈106× speedup). On RoboTwin 2.0 integrated into the π_0.5 VLA model, OFP at NFE=1 reaches 94.7% average success rate, surpassing the original π_0.5 policy at NFE=10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFP at NFE=1 outperforms all single-step baselines across both 2D and 3D settings: it beats CP (54.7%) by 16.9%, OneDP (62.4%) by 9.2%, and MP1 (57.4%) by 14.2% on the 3D 56-task benchmark. Against multi-step baselines, OFP at NFE=1 exceeds DP3 at NFE=100 (66.4%) by 8% and FM Policy at NFE=100 (59.8%) by 19.7%, while requiring only 1/100th the inference time. In the 2D image-based setting, OFP (68.3%) also surpasses DP at NFE=100 (64.2%) and FM Policy at NFE=100 (67.2%). Unlike OneDP, which is locked to single-step inference, OFP supports flexible NFE: at NFE=4 it further improves to 66.2% average on the 4 DexArt tasks (vs. 64.5% at NFE=1), while CP improves from 60.7% to 64.2% over the same range. On RoboTwin 2.0 with the π_0.5 backbone, OFP at NFE=1 (94.7%) surpasses the original 10-step π_0.5 policy and outperforms CFM, Shortcut Models, and iMF at NFE=1.

## Relation to Prior Work

OFP unifies two previously separate acceleration paradigms for generative policies—consistency distillation (as in Consistency Policy) and score distillation (as in One-Step Diffusion Policy)—into a single from-scratch self-distillation framework that requires no pre-trained teacher. It builds directly on the flow matching ODE formulation and optimal-transport straight-line paths, and adopts the interval-averaged velocity parameterization from MeanFlow while explicitly avoiding its costly Jacobian-vector products. The self-guided regularization repurposes Classifier-Free Guidance as a training-time signal estimated from an EMA copy of the model itself, and the warm-start mechanism repurposes a standard receding-horizon action-reuse trick as a training-free inference prior that reduces the generative transport distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 100×+ inference speedup that simultaneously improves action quality over 100-step baselines addresses the central latency bottleneck preventing flow-based policies from achieving high-frequency control in real-time manipulation. The from-scratch design (no teacher model) and the demonstrated transfer to the large π_0.5 VLA on RoboTwin 2.0 suggest the approach is broadly applicable rather than tied to a narrow policy class. Key limitations remain: all evaluations are in simulation with no physical-robot validation, and the method's interaction with further system-level accelerations (quantization, pruning) is left as future work. An open question is whether the self-guidance signal remains stable and effective as action dimensionality and task horizon grow substantially beyond the settings tested here.
