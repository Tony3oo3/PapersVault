## Problem

VFM-based deepfake detectors (e.g., CLIP, DINOv3) trained on large datasets become heavily dependent on semantic content, performing well on images matching training categories but degrading sharply on unseen semantic classes. This semantic bias limits real-world generalization, where AI-generated images appear across diverse and unpredictable categories. The paper asks whether the decision basis of these detectors can be explicitly corrected to focus on intrinsic synthesis traces rather than category-specific semantics.

## Input / Output

At inference, BCNet takes a single RGB image and outputs a binary real/fake probability (threshold 0.5). The backbone is a frozen DINOv3 ViT-H+/16 with LoRA adapters (rank 16, α=32) in all 32 Transformer layers. During training, fake samples are additionally consumed in two augmented forms: a semantically-erased view (ASE) and a gradient-perturbed view (NPE), but real samples are never modified, preserving their natural statistical manifold as an unaltered reference.

## Method

BCNet corrects the semantic decision basis of a frozen VFM via two complementary modules applied exclusively to fake samples. ASE extracts self-attention maps from the final Transformer block, thresholds them at τ=0.75 to build a binary mask that zeros out high-attention (semantic) regions, and multiplies the fake image by this mask to force the LoRA layers to learn from background and boundary textures. NPE computes the sign of the classification-loss gradient with respect to input pixels, scales it by ε=0.0005, and adds it to the original fake image, exposing diverse forgery patterns and disrupting residual semantic shortcuts. The total loss is an asymmetric weighted sum (λ_CE=0.1, λ_ASE=0.45, λ_NPE=0.45) where correction penalties apply only when y=1, ensuring the optimization prioritizes the harder erased and perturbed cases while keeping real-image features undistorted.

## Results

On AIGI-Bench (25 generative subsets), BCNet achieves 88.6% average accuracy and 94.9% average A.P., surpassing the runner-up FerretNet (79.9% Acc) by 8.7%. On WildRF (real social media images), BCNet reaches 96.7% accuracy, while on Chameleon (high-fidelity) it scores 82.8% and on AIGCDetectBenchmark it achieves 96.6% average accuracy. The method is trained on only 144k images from ProGAN and SDv1.4 (Setting-II protocol) yet generalizes across 51 AI-generated datasets spanning GANs, diffusion models, and face-swap methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BCNet outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On AIGI-Bench, it leads the runner-up FerretNet (79.9% Acc) by 8.7 points and Effort (88.6% Acc) by a narrow margin, with the largest gaps on semantic-heavy subsets like DALLE-3 (94.5% vs. SAFE's ~45%) and E4S (83.8% vs. SAFE's 48.4%). On WildRF, BCNet achieves 96.7% average accuracy versus VIB-Net's 61.4%, a 35-point margin; on the Twitter subset specifically, CNNSpot drops to near-random (49.9%) while BCNet sustains 97.4%. On Chameleon, BCNet (82.8%) exceeds VIB-Net (60.8%) by 22 points, confirming that semantic erasure is critical for high-fidelity detection. On AIGCDetectBenchmark, BCNet (96.6% avg Acc) beats Effort (93.3%) by 3.3%, with the largest advantage on diffusion models like WFIR (95.4% vs. CNNSpot's 50.2%) and ADM (91.5% vs. Effort's 78.2%). No baseline matches BCNet on any individual subset across the five benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VFM-based detection paradigm (frozen CLIP/DINOv2/DINOv3 backbones with LoRA adaptation) established by UnivFD, Effort, AIDE, and FerretNet, and specifically addresses the semantic-bias limitation identified by C2P-CLIP [67] but not previously resolved. Its two correction modules repurpose existing ideas in a new combination: ASE draws on attention-based dropout [10] and random erasing [89] but targets semantically salient regions identified via the model's own attention maps, while NPE adapts FGSM-style sign-normalized gradients [19] and gradient-regularization insights [23,41] into a training-only perturbation applied exclusively to synthetic samples. The asymmetric design—correcting only fake inputs while preserving real-image statistics—departs from prior augmentation strategies that treat both classes symmetrically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that semantic bias is the dominant generalization bottleneck (not low-level artifact suppression), the paper redirects attention toward decision-basis correction as a practical lever for deploying detectors on truly wild, multi-category content. The 96.7% WildRF accuracy and 8.7% AIGI-Bench margin are practically significant for social-media moderation, yet the notably weaker Flux.1 result (63%) reveals that even semantic-agnostic methods struggle with the newest, highest-fidelity architectures. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video deepfakes, whether inference-time semantic erasure is feasible without the attention-map overhead, and how performance scales as generative models continue to suppress the very forgery traces the method relies on.
