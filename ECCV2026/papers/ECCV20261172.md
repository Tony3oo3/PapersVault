## Problem

Monocular 3D lane detection is inherently ill-posed, and while SD maps offer a lightweight road-topology prior, their road-level centerlines misalign with actual lane boundaries by several meters, making direct integration challenging. Additionally, the prevalent sparse-point lane representation suffers from endpoint truncation in training annotations, causing fragmented detections at long range or under occlusion that existing endpoint-refinement heads address only partially.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261172/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a front-view RGB image (resized to 720×960) and a prior SD map represented as N road-level centerlines, each with M sampled 2D ground-plane points within x∈[−15 m, 15 m] and y∈[3 m, 103 m]. The SD map is synthesized from Waymo HD maps via a four-step degradation pipeline (mask rendering, skeleton extraction, junction separation, spline resampling) to mimic real-world positional inaccuracies. The output is a set of 3D lanes represented as sparse points at fixed Y-intervals, each with a category label and completed endpoints recovered by clustering per-anchor offset predictions.

## Method

SGC-Lane builds on the LATR transformer decoder and introduces three key components. First, an FV-Guided Map Encoder predicts per-centerline lane widths from front-view features, expands each road-level centerline into β parallel lane boundaries via normal-direction offsets, and produces both a 2D lane probability heatmap (rasterized with Gaussian smoothing) that gates FV features and lane-aware map features for downstream fusion. Second, a Local Spatial Cross-Attention module localizes each per-point query to a ground (x, y) position, retrieves the K nearest map tokens by Euclidean distance, and performs cross-attention only over those local tokens to inject geometric priors without global mismatch. Third, a Step Consistency Completion Head regresses start and end Y-offsets from every anchor point, enforces a second-order-difference consistency loss on successive Y-offsets to exploit the fixed-interval structure, and clusters all per-anchor endpoint candidates to yield robust completed lane extents. A perspective-aware non-uniform sampling plane allocates denser Y-samples near the ego vehicle and sparser ones at range, enriching 3D positional cues for deformable cross-attention without modifying the decoder.

## Results

On the OpenLane validation set, SGC-Lane achieves a state-of-the-art F1-score of 65.6% with a ResNet-50 backbone, outperforming the previous best (Chang et al., 64.7%) by 0.9% and the LATR baseline (61.9%) by 3.7%. With a stronger Swin-B backbone, the F1-score further rises to 68.0%, surpassing GLane3D (66.0%) by 2.0%. Localization accuracy is also competitive, with near-range X error of 0.204 m and far-range X error of 0.254 m (ResNet-50). Scenario-wise, the method leads in Curve (72.4%), Intersection (57.1%), Up&Down (56.9%), and Night (58.6%) F1-scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGC-Lane outperforms all eight compared methods on overall F1-score with ResNet-50, leading Chang et al. [3] (64.7%) by 0.9%, SC-Lane [21] (64.3%) by 1.3%, GLane3D [20] (63.9%) by 1.7%, and the LATR [19] baseline (61.9%) by 3.7%. In the Swin-B setting, SGC-Lane (68.0%) surpasses GLane3D (66.0%) by 2.0% F1. In scenario-wise evaluation, SGC-Lane achieves the best F1 in Curve (72.4%), Intersection (57.1%), Up&Down (56.9%), and Night (58.6%), and ties Chang et al. on Merge&Split (65.5%). The sole scenario where SGC-Lane trails is ExtremeWeather (58.7% vs. GLane3D's 62.0%), which the authors attribute to GLane3D's bottom-up keypoint-grouping strategy being better suited for fragmented lane segments under heavy weather. On localization, SGC-Lane matches or slightly trails GLane3D on X-error (0.204 vs. 0.193 m near) but is competitive overall, confirming that the F1 gains come without sacrificing geometric precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGC-Lane builds directly on the LATR transformer decoder (deformable cross-attention over front-view features with sparse-point lane queries) and extends the EP-Head endpoint-prediction paradigm into a sequence-consistent completion strategy. It departs from prior SD-map-augmented perception work (SMERF, SEPT), which fused road-level centerlines into BEV features for topology reasoning, by explicitly addressing the representation gap between road-level centerlines and physical lane boundaries through an FV-guided width prediction and a localized cross-attention mechanism. The perspective-aware non-uniform sampling plane also extends the positional-encoding line of work (PETRv2, LATR's learnable plane) with a density profile matched to perspective projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practical deployment gap: SD maps are far more widely available and cheaper than HD maps, yet their road-level centerline representation has been difficult to exploit for lane-level 3D detection. The demonstrated ability to gain meaningful F1 improvements (especially in curves, intersections, and merge/split scenarios) from only coarse, meter-inaccurate priors suggests a viable path toward map-assisted perception without HD-map infrastructure. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly noisy real-world SD maps (the current evaluation uses synthesized priors from Waymo HD maps), whether temporal fusion can further reduce the completeness-precision trade-off observed with SCC-Head, and how the method performs when the SD map is entirely absent or severely degraded.
