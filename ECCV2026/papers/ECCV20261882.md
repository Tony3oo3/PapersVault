## Problem

Synthesizing high-fidelity mmWave radar signals for dynamic human motion remains difficult: physics-based ray tracing scales exponentially with multipath order, while neural RF representations entangle primary surface scattering with environmental multipath, degrading the time-varying micro-Doppler signatures essential for action recognition. No existing simulator efficiently produces dynamic human radar signals that preserve fine-grained kinematic details while faithfully reproducing site-specific multipath interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261882/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes SMPL-parameterized dynamic human mesh sequences (≈3,000 frames per action, 8 action categories) and a parametrically defined static room layout (walls, floor, ceiling specified by scalar distances relative to the radar, not scanned geometry) as input. Static interior objects require no explicit modeling—their scattering is absorbed by learnable parameters on proxy boundaries. The output is a coherent IF mmWave signal (matching a TI AWR1843BOOST 3-TX/4-RX configuration) that is subsequently transformed into Range-Doppler amplitude heatmaps via 2D FFT for downstream sensing tasks.

## Method

HybridSim explicitly decouples electromagnetic propagation into a direct (1-bounce) path and an indirect (≥2-bounce) path. The direct path uses a tri-plane representation to extract pose-invariant human features, a GCN with stochastic masking as a training-time regularizer for spatial coherence, and a microfacet BRDF decoder to predict per-vertex material parameters (roughness, metallic factor, complex refractive index); the phase is computed analytically from propagation delay. The indirect path replaces intractable multi-bounce ray tracing with a unified multi-scatterer surrogate: geometrically constrained 3D Gaussian patches on both the human mesh and room boundaries, combined with proxy virtual receivers on each wall, reducing multipath to a differentiable three-segment propagation model (tx → scatterer → proxy receiver → physical receiver). Training minimizes MSE on log-scale (dB) Range-Doppler heatmaps using an amplitude-only strategy, with a learnable noise floor injected to bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

On the mmMesh dataset, HybridSim achieves 22.30 dB PSNR, 0.262 SSIM, and 0.089 LPIPS on intra-subject RD heatmap synthesis, and 22.29 dB PSNR, 0.262 SSIM, and 0.084 LPIPS on cross-subject evaluation, outperforming both baselines by ~2.2 dB PSNR and reducing LPIPS by roughly 65%. On the downstream human activity recognition task (sim-to-real), a classifier trained exclusively on HybridSim-generated data reaches 92.07% accuracy and 0.9216 F1-score on real measurements, compared to 54.22% for mmGPE and 30.68% for RF-Genesis. Inference runs under 1 second per frame on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HybridSim decisively outperforms both baselines across all reported metrics. Against mmGPE [21] (the strongest baseline), HybridSim gains +2.2 dB PSNR, +0.042 SSIM, and reduces LPIPS from 0.253 to 0.084 in the cross-subject setting; on the downstream HAR task, accuracy jumps from 54.22% to 92.07% (+37.85 points). Against RF-Genesis [6], the gap is even wider: +2.27 dB PSNR, LPIPS drops from 0.271 to 0.084, and HAR accuracy improves from 30.68% to 92.07%. The paper notes that the modest PSNR gain (~11%) belies the much larger downstream accuracy improvement, because PSNR is dominated by the sparse radar signal's background noise floor while HAR accuracy directly reflects preservation of micro-Doppler kinematic semantics. Both baselines are constrained to the same single-radar TI AWR1843BOOST configuration to ensure fair comparison; other RF synthesis methods were excluded due to incompatible transceiver geometries or closed-source implementations.

## Relation to Prior Work

HybridSim sits at the intersection of physics-based RF ray tracing (Yun & Iskander, RF-Genesis) and neural RF representations (NeRF², RF-3DGS, GSRF), but departs from both by explicitly decoupling the electromagnetic propagation into a microfacet-BRDF direct path (inheriting the inverse-rendering formulation of Hofmann et al.) and a 3DGS-based indirect path with proxy virtual receivers. Unlike monolithic neural radiance-field approaches that entangle primary surface scattering with environmental multipath, or pure ray tracers whose cost scales exponentially with bounce order, the paper's core architectural claim is that separating these two physical phenomena into distinct, interpretable modules preserves time-varying micro-Doppler signatures essential for articulated-motion sensing. The human parameterization via tri-plane features and GCN regularization draws from the HUGS/SMPL-X lineage, while the proxy-receiver formulation for multipath is a novel surrogate that replaces intractable multi-bounce integrals with a differentiable three-segment propagation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37.85-point absolute HAR accuracy gain over the strongest baseline (92.07% vs 54.22%) demonstrates that decoupled physics-learning synthesis can close the sim-to-real gap far more effectively than either pure-physics or pure-neural approaches, making large-scale synthetic radar data generation practical for training robust activity-recognition models. A key open question is generalization beyond the single fixed room: the current scene representation requires retraining for novel layouts, and the authors identify integrating generalized neural scene priors for zero-shot or few-shot room adaptation as the critical next step. The finding that a modest ~2 dB PSNR improvement translates into a >37-point accuracy jump also raises broader questions about whether standard reconstruction metrics are adequate for evaluating radar signal synthesis, suggesting the field needs task-aware evaluation protocols.
