## Problem

Diffusion-based inverse problem solvers such as DPS and DAPS are framed as Bayesian posterior samplers that jointly track the prior and likelihood at every diffusion timestep, yet empirical analysis shows the prior gradient is negligible in magnitude and nearly orthogonal to the likelihood gradient throughout the process. This means the diffusion prior functions only as a one-time warm initializer near the data manifold, while reconstruction is driven almost entirely by measurement consistency—making the maintained time-marginal distribution computationally wasteful and theoretically misaligned with actual behavior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy measurement y = A(x_0) + n with known forward operator A (linear or nonlinear), a known measurement noise level γ, and a pre-trained pixel-space EDM score model (256×256 on FFHQ or ImageNet). It outputs a single reconstructed image x_0 in pixel space. The diffusion model is used only during initialization; the refinement stage requires only the forward operator A and its adjoint (via VJP), with no further score-network calls, enabling operation with as few as 20 total NFEs for linear tasks.

## Method

DAPS++ fully decouples the diffusion prior from the likelihood into two non-overlapping stages. Stage 1 (initialization) runs the reverse diffusion process without any likelihood guidance, using Tweedie's formula for high noise levels (σ > σ̄ = 10γ) and a single RK4 step below the threshold, placing the estimate near the data manifold in one pass. Stage 2 (refinement) performs unadjusted Langevin algorithm (ULA) sampling driven solely by the measurement likelihood gradient ∇log p(y|x_0), with no prior term, since the initialization already sits in a region where the prior gradient is O(ε). Each refinement cycle is followed by a controlled re-noising step to prevent overfitting and preserve diffusion-driven variability, and the step size follows a polynomially decaying EDM-inspired schedule. This structural separation eliminates the need to re-invoke the score network at every annealing level, reducing NFEs by approximately 90% relative to DAPS while matching or exceeding reconstruction quality across linear and nonlinear imaging tasks.

## Results

On FFHQ-256 (100 validation images), DAPS++-50 achieves best or near-best SSIM and LPIPS across six inverse problems (SR×4, inpainting, Gaussian blur, motion blur, nonlinear blur, HDR) while using only 50 diffusion NFEs—approximately 90% fewer than DAPS-1K (1000 NFEs). For example, on inpainting DAPS++-50 reaches 0.812 SSIM / 0.141 LPIPS / 42.1 FID, outperforming DAPS-1K (0.836/0.137/38.4) on LPIPS and FID while matching SSIM within 0.007. On ImageNet-256, DAPS++-100 attains the best SSIM on SR×4 (0.661) and inpainting (0.771), surpassing DAPS-1K (0.638 and 0.715 respectively) under the same noise level γ=0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAPS-1K (the strongest baseline at 1000 NFEs), DAPS++-50 matches SSIM within 0.005–0.01 on most tasks while achieving substantially lower LPIPS (e.g., 0.176 vs 0.192 on SR×4; 0.171 vs 0.179 on Gaussian blur) and lower FID (46.0 vs 55.5 on SR×4), all with ~90% fewer neural function evaluations. Against DAPS-100, DAPS++-50 wins decisively on every task where both are reported (e.g., inpainting SSIM 0.812 vs 0.716; Gaussian blur LPIPS 0.171 vs 0.220). DPS, DCDP, DDRM, DDNM, and DiffPIR are all outperformed by DAPS++-50 on SSIM and LPIPS across the board; the largest gaps appear against DiffPIR (e.g., inpainting SSIM 0.812 vs 0.525) and DPS (e.g., Gaussian blur SSIM 0.784 vs 0.647). On ImageNet, DAPS++-100 surpasses DAPS-1K on SR×4 (SSIM 0.661 vs 0.638) and inpainting (SSIM 0.771 vs 0.715), though DAPS-1K retains a slight FID edge on Gaussian blur (47.1 vs 56.1). The paper also shows that DAPS++ is more robust to higher noise (γ up to 0.4), where DAPS-1K degrades sharply (SSIM drops from 0.786 to 0.192) while DAPS++-50 maintains SSIM ≥ 0.579.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DAPS (Zhang et al., CVPR 2025), which introduced decoupled noise annealing to separate diffusion prior updates from likelihood updates in inverse problem solving. The key departure is taking this decoupling to its logical conclusion: DAPS++ uses the diffusion score network exclusively for a one-time initialization (via Tweedie estimation or a single RK4 step), after which all refinement is driven purely by the measurement likelihood through unadjusted Langevin dynamics, with no further access to the prior. This reframes the diffusion prior from an active posterior-sampling component (as in DPS, DAPS, and the standard Bayesian formulation) to a 'warm initializer' that places the estimate near the data manifold, a role the paper supports with gradient-ratio and orthogonality analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~90% reduction in neural function evaluations while matching or exceeding reconstruction quality makes diffusion-based inverse problem solving substantially more practical for real-time or resource-constrained imaging applications. The conceptual reframing—that the diffusion prior is functionally irrelevant during refinement—challenges the standard Bayesian posterior-sampling narrative and suggests that future methods can focus computational effort on initialization quality and likelihood geometry rather than maintaining time-marginal consistency. Open questions include whether the decoupling extends to tasks where the initialization must land in a specific basin of attraction (e.g., phase retrieval), whether it generalizes to video or 3D reconstruction, and whether the likelihood-only refinement can be further accelerated with adaptive or second-order methods.
