## Problem

Existing annotation-free event-based depth estimation methods (e.g., EventDAM, DepthAnyEvent) distill knowledge from Vision Foundation Models but process event streams as independent frames, ignoring the inherent temporal continuity of asynchronous event data. This frame-wise treatment fails to leverage the rich spatio-temporal and multi-view geometric priors encoded in sequence-aware foundation models, yielding temporally inconsistent and less accurate depth predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262554/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes only asynchronous event streams, which are partitioned into 50 ms windows and accumulated into 3-channel frame-like representations (positive/negative polarity histograms across 5 temporal bins) at 252×504 resolution. Sequences of 24 temporally synchronized event frames are processed jointly to exploit multi-view geometric context. The primary output is a dense per-frame depth map; the framework also extends to produce camera poses and 3D point clouds. RGB images are used exclusively during training as the teacher's input for distillation and are not required at test time.

## Method

EventVGGT distills multi-view geometric priors from the VGGT teacher into an event-based student via a tri-level strategy. At the output level, Cross-Modal Feature Mixture (CMFM) stochastically replaces 25% of RGB features with temporally aligned event features, decodes the mixed sequence through a shared Alternating-Attention Transformer, and supervises the auxiliary depth with the teacher's RGB depth using an aleatoric-uncertainty-weighted loss plus a gradient penalty. At the feature level, Spatio-Temporal Feature Distillation (STFD) aligns both intra-frame spatial features and inter-frame feature deltas (frame-to-frame differences) via channel-wise cosine similarity, preserving event dynamics rather than forcing static mimicry. At the temporal level, Temporal Consistency Distillation (TCD) penalizes L1 discrepancies in the per-pixel inter-frame depth rate of change between student and teacher outputs, enforcing geometrically coherent temporal flow. The student is adapted with LoRA (r=16, α=32, ~1.6M trainable parameters) and trained on 24-frame sequences, reducing the 30 m absolute depth error on EventScape by over 53% relative to the prior event-only state of the art.

## Results

On EventScape, EventVGGT (event-only input) achieves absolute mean depth errors of 0.54 m / 0.79 m / 1.06 m at 10 m / 20 m / 30 m, reducing the 30 m error by 53.9% over the best event-only baseline EventDAM (2.30 m) and outperforming all multi-modal (E+I) methods (best: SRFNet at 2.76 m). On the real-world MVSEC Night2 sequence, EventVGGT lowers the 30 m error from 3.22 m (EventDAM) to 2.48 m. In zero-shot transfer to the unseen DENSE dataset, EventVGGT achieves 0.54 / 0.89 / 1.33 m at 10/20/30 m, compared to 1.20 / 2.60 / 5.18 m for EventDAM and 1.50 / 3.57 / 6.12 m for SRFNet (E+I).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventVGGT (event-only) decisively outperforms the strongest event-only baseline EventDAM [50] on EventScape, cutting the 30 m absolute error from 2.30 m to 1.06 m (−53.9%) and the 20 m error from 1.52 m to 0.79 m (−48%). It also surpasses all multi-modal (E+I) fusion methods—SRFNet [30] (2.76 m), ER-F2D [8] (2.81 m), HMNet [14] (3.27 m), and RAMNet [12] (3.58 m) at 30 m—despite using only event data at inference. On MVSEC night sequences, EventVGGT reduces the 30 m error from 3.22 m (EventDAM) to 2.48 m (Night2) and 2.64 m (Night3), while matching or exceeding SRFNet (3.52 m at 30 m on Night3) that relies on paired RGB. In zero-shot evaluation on DENSE, EventVGGT (1.33 m at 30 m) outperforms EventDAM (5.18 m) by nearly 4× and SRFNet (6.12 m) by 4.6×. Against the raw VGGT teacher applied directly to event data (3.44 m at 30 m on MVSEC Night1), EventVGGT achieves 2.61 m, and even surpasses VGGT on its native RGB input (3.33 m) in δ-accuracy metrics, confirming that the distillation pipeline transfers geometric priors rather than merely mimicking the teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VGGT's Alternating-Attention Transformer architecture as a frozen teacher, repurposing its multi-view geometric reasoning for the event domain via LoRA-adapted student encoders. It departs from prior annotation-free event depth methods (EventDAM, DepthAnyEvent) that process event streams as independent frames, instead treating the asynchronous event sequence as a coherent video to exploit VGGT's spatio-temporal priors. The tri-level distillation strategy (CMFM at output, STFD at feature, TCD at temporal level) is a novel decomposition of the cross-modal transfer problem, with the stochastic feature-mixture 'stepping stone' being a distinctive design choice for bridging the RGB–event modality gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single event-only student can outperform multi-modal (E+I) fusion methods at 30m range and generalize zero-shot to unseen datasets (DENSE, MVSEC), the paper validates that rich multi-view geometric priors can be fully internalized through structured distillation, reducing reliance on paired RGB at inference. The inherited far-field depth compression bias from VGGT remains an open limitation, and the authors note that integrating dense ground-truth depth could calibrate this artifact. The framework's natural extension to camera pose and point cloud estimation suggests a broader template for distilling sequence-aware foundation models into sparse, asynchronous sensor modalities beyond events.
