## Problem

Neural video codecs have surpassed classical codecs in compression efficiency but remain impractical for production deployment due to two barriers: catastrophic decoding failures when encoder and decoder run on different hardware platforms (caused by floating-point divergence in entropy model parameters), and insufficient speed on commodity NPUs. Existing mitigations—integer quantization, calibration side-streams, and fixed codebooks—either fail on heterogeneous NPU toolchains, provide only probabilistic guarantees, or sacrifice compression performance.

## Input / Output

The codec consumes YUV video frames (targeting 360p–720p for video conferencing, up to 1080p with the smaller MLVC-S variant) along with recurrent temporal context features from the previous frame. I-frames are not a separate model but are encoded as P-frames conditioned on a uniform gray reference (0.5 in YUV), unifying the architecture. The output is an arithmetic-coded bitstream and a reconstructed frame; the decoder also maintains a gated memory state and temporal context that are updated per frame for subsequent decoding.

## Method

The key mechanism is scale-sending: scale parameters σ are derived deterministically from the quantized hyperlatent via absolute value, spatial expansion (s×s blocks), channel replication (factor r), and a fixed codebook lookup, so they are identical on encoder and decoder without requiring bit-exact arithmetic. This is combined with a gated memory module in the decoder (forget/output gates over a persistent state) for long-term temporal modeling, hardware-compatible ReGLU activations (channel-split gating with ReLU1 capping) replacing WSiLU, and long-term reference (LTR) recovery frames that periodically reset the prediction chain to bound cross-platform divergence. Training uses I-frame dropout (p=0.5) to handle the unified I/P-frame model, and perceptual fine-tuning with LPIPS plus face-segmentation ROI weighting tailored to video conferencing. The full pipeline runs in FP16 on Apple, Intel, and Qualcomm NPUs at 100+ FPS average at 360p.

## Results

On the VCD-TH video conferencing benchmark (subjective MOS evaluation), MLVC achieves −75.5% BD-rate at 360p and −78.8% at 540p relative to the HEVC-QSV hardware anchor, with a combined bitrate-ladder (MLVC-multi) yielding −71.8% overall. The lightweight MLVC-S variant reaches −65.4% BD-rate at 540p while running at 300 FPS on Apple M3 Pro NPU. On the HEVC B-E objective benchmark (PSNR), the full MLVC model achieves −52.0% BD-rate same-platform and −58.7% cross-platform. Both encoder and decoder sustain 100+ FPS average at 360p across Apple, Intel, and Qualcomm NPUs, with MLVC-S extending real-time operation to 720p on all three platforms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the hardware HEVC-QSV anchor, MLVC achieves a dramatic −75.5% BD-rate (MOS) at 360p, meaning it delivers the same perceived quality at roughly one-quarter the bitrate. Compared to DCVC-RT (the strongest neural codec baseline), MLVC's −75.5% is within 6 percentage points of the perceptually fine-tuned DCVC-RT (−81.9%), yet MLVC is 30% faster (130 vs. 102 FPS at 360p) and, critically, decodes reliably across heterogeneous NPU platforms where DCVC-RT suffers catastrophic failures (BD-rate = ∞). Against the fixed-prior cross-platform codec of Tian et al. [49], MLVC achieves −45.2% BD-rate on HEVC B-E versus +15.0% for that method, a 60-point margin in favor of MLVC. The cross-platform BD-rate matrix (Fig. 6b) confirms that all encoder–decoder platform pairs (Apple, Intel, Qualcomm, NVIDIA) stay within 2 BD-rate points of same-platform performance with zero catastrophic decoding failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DCVC-RT [24], adopting its floating-point architecture as a starting point and modifying it with a scale-sending mechanism, gated memory, ReGLU activations, and long-term reference (LTR) recovery. It departs from the dominant quantization-based approach to cross-platform consistency (used by DCVC-RT, MobileNVC, and others) by arguing that bit-exact integer arithmetic is infeasible on commodity NPUs, and instead transmits scale indices through the hyperprior to guarantee entropy-coding consistency without requiring identical floating-point behavior. The LTR recovery mechanism is borrowed conceptually from traditional codecs (HEVC/VVC), while the memory module and ReGLU gating are novel architectural additions tailored to hardware constraints. The paper positions itself against three prior strategies for cross-platform consistency—network quantization, calibration side-streams (Tian et al. [50]), and fixed-prior codebooks (Tian et al. [49])—arguing each has fundamental limitations for NPU deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neural video codec demonstrated to decode reliably across heterogeneous consumer NPUs (Apple, Intel, Qualcomm) at real-time speeds, closing the gap between neural codec research and actual production deployment in systems like video conferencing. The ~18 percentage point BD-rate cost of cross-platform constraints (scale sending, simple activations, periodic I-frames, unified I/P model) is a meaningful and likely fundamental trade-off that the field must accept for practical deployment, and the paper's recovery of most of this gap through targeted architectural changes provides a template for future work. Open questions include power consumption optimization, simultaneous encode/decode at 1080p+, whether the scale-sending mechanism generalizes to more complex entropy models, and whether the perceptual-quality gap versus unconstrained DCVC-RT (~6 points in MOS BD-rate) can be further closed without sacrificing cross-platform robustness.
