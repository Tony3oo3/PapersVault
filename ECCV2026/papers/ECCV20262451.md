## Problem

Existing cross-domain few-shot segmentation (CD-FSS) methods all rely on source-domain training or target-domain fine-tuning, incurring high computational costs and overfitting risk. The authors observe that incorporating powerful vision foundation models (e.g., DINOv3) into these paradigms yields only marginal gains or even degrades performance, because strong pretrained representations accelerate overfitting of the trainable parameters. This motivates a fully training-free framework that exploits foundation-model features without any learnable parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262451/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K support images (each paired with a binary foreground mask) and a single query image, all from an unseen target domain, resized to 480×480. No source-domain data or pre-trained task-specific weights are used; the only model is the frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16 encoder. The output is a binary query segmentation mask (foreground vs. background) produced by comparing fused foreground and background similarity maps, evaluated via mIoU over 1200–2400 episodes per dataset.

## Method

The framework is entirely training-free and built on the frozen DINOv3 encoder, with three modules operating on multi-layer patch-token features reshaped to 2D maps. (1) SAFR identifies position-aware layers (where attention outputs carry high variance in position-sensitive channels) and re-fuses the raw attention outputs from the most semantically discriminative layers (lowest position-variance, highest Fisher discriminant ratio) with normalized weights, bypassing the standard final output that accumulates positional noise via residual connections. (2) ASE computes support prototypes via masked average pooling, derives a robust threshold from support-support similarity to localize reliable query foreground/background regions, then aggregates query information into support features through masked cross-attention that restricts each support region to attend only to the corresponding query region. (3) HPM extracts global (MAP), regional (k-means++ with N_r=9 clusters), and pixel-level prototypes from the enhanced support features, computes per-prototype cosine similarity maps, and fuses them with softmax-normalized weights proportional to each prototype type's foreground-background similarity gap, yielding the final binary prediction.

## Results

The proposed training-free method (DINOv3-B backbone) achieves an average mIoU of 68.39% (1-shot) and 73.08% (5-shot) across four target domains (Deepglobe, ISIC, Chest X-ray, FSS-1000), without any training or fine-tuning. It surpasses the best training-based CD-FSS method LoEC (ViT-B) by 3.38% and 2.65% mIoU under 1-shot and 5-shot settings, respectively, and outperforms the best training-free method Matcher (DINOv3-B+SAM-B) by 7.19% and 5.81%. Per-dataset, the method scores 49.71/58.88 on Deepglobe, 55.73/62.55 on ISIC, 85.44/87.01 on Chest X-ray, and 82.67/83.89 on FSS-1000 (1-shot/5-shot mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 17 baselines on average mIoU across both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. Among training-based CD-FSS methods, the closest competitor is LoEC (ViT-B) at 65.01/70.43, which the method surpasses by 3.38/2.65 points; notably, replacing LoEC's backbone with DINOv3-B actually degrades its performance to 63.37/68.84, illustrating the overfitting issue the paper identifies. DFN (63.99/69.77) and SDRC (63.22/67.30) are the next closest training-based baselines, trailed by 4.40/3.31 and 5.17/5.78 points respectively. Among training-free methods, Matcher with DINOv3-B+SAM-B (61.20/67.27) is the strongest baseline, outperformed by 7.19/5.81 points; GF-SAM with DINOv3-B+SAM-B (56.94/61.24) and MAUP (55.66/58.25) lag further behind. A key finding is that swapping ImageNet-pretrained backbones for DINOv3 in training-based methods (PATNet, ABCDFSS, LoEC) yields marginal gains or outright degradation, confirming that trainable parameters overfit even with stronger initialization, whereas the training-free approach fully exploits VFM representations without this risk.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of cross-domain few-shot segmentation (CD-FSS) and training-free adaptation. While prior CD-FSS work spans three paradigms (prototype-based, affinity-based, source-free fine-tuning) and prior training-free FSS work (PerSAM, Matcher, GF-SAM) builds on SAM's class-agnostic framework, no prior method eliminates all trainable parameters while operating on a single VFM encoder for cross-domain settings. The paper repurposes DINOv3's multi-layer attention outputs through a variance/FDR-based layer selection criterion (SAFR), adapts masked cross-attention for support-query alignment without projection layers (ASE), and fuses multi-granularity prototype matching (HPM), synthesizing ideas from prototype learning, attention mechanisms, and self-supervised representation analysis into a unified training-free pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical elimination of all training for CD-FSS removes a significant computational and overfitting barrier, making the approach immediately deployable across new domains without source-domain data. The finding that powerful VFMs actively degrade training-based CD-FSS methods challenges the field's default assumption that stronger backbones monotonically improve downstream performance. Open questions include whether the multi-module pipeline (SAFR + ASE + HPM) can be simplified, whether the approach generalizes beyond the four tested domains to more extreme distribution shifts, and whether the DINOv3-specific layer-selection heuristic transfers to other VFM architectures. The method's reliance on a single frozen encoder also raises questions about robustness to encoder updates or architectural changes.
