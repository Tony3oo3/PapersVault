## Problem

Micro-action recognition requires detecting transient, low-amplitude bodily movements that are easily overwhelmed by dominant appearance features in standard video encoders. Existing MAR methods either depend on costly pose annotations and coarse joint graphs that miss sub-joint cues (e.g., finger tremors), or rely on auxiliary modalities that limit practical applicability. A key unresolved gap is how to capture both holistic motion context and fine-grained subtle dynamics from raw RGB alone without additional supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261079/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16-frame RGB video clips resized to 224×224, from which it also derives per-frame temporal gradient maps (channel-averaged ℓ₁ differences between consecutive frames) as a modality-free motion cue. It produces two-level predictions on MA-52 (52 action-level and 7 body-level classes) and single-level gesture labels on iMiGUE (32 classes), using a tri-head architecture where the final prediction comes from an aggregated head that fuses holistic and subtle branch logits via learned instance-wise weights.

## Method

A³-MAE builds on a VideoMAE ViT-B/16 backbone fine-tuned on Kinetics-400 and introduces three modules in an amplify-aggregate-adjust pipeline. (1) TGLA computes temporal gradient maps, thresholds at the 90th percentile, extracts the highest-scoring connected component per frame, and aggregates across frames via a temporal-agreement ratio (intersection/union > 0.2) to yield a stable ROI; ROIAlign then crops and resizes backbone features within this ROI to form a subtle-motion branch aligned with the holistic branch. (2) HSMA applies dual cross-attention (each branch queries the other) with gated residual updates using learnable γ coefficients, then concatenates temporally averaged features from both branches. (3) CADA estimates per-branch confidence via softmax entropy, maps it through a small MLP to instance-wise weights, and trains with three complementary losses: selective confidence distillation (KL from a confidence-weighted teacher to the aggregated head), weight entropy regularization (preventing collapse to one branch), and confidence-weight alignment (KL between loss-derived targets and learned weights). The overall objective combines tri-head cross-entropy with these three CADA losses, adding only ~14M parameters and 0.12 GFLOPs over the VideoMAE backbone.

## Results

On MA-52, A³-MAE achieves 67.92% Action Top-1, 82.62% Body Top-1, and 70.64% F1mean, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics using RGB video only. On iMiGUE, it reaches 67.08% Action Top-1, surpassing the previous best (VSwimT at 60.11%) by nearly 7 points. The VideoMAE baseline alone already reaches 66.47% Action Top-1 on MA-52, outperforming all prior RGB-only and pose-based methods, and A³-MAE adds a further +1.45 points over this baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A³-MAE (RGB only) outperforms every compared method on both benchmarks. On MA-52 Action Top-1, it leads the closest RGB-only competitor, the VideoMAE baseline, by +1.45 points (67.92 vs. 66.47) and the strongest pose-based MAR method MMN by +5.21 points (67.92 vs. 62.71). Against the multi-modal PCAN* (V+P), which was the prior state-of-the-art, A³-MAE gains +3.33 points in Action Top-1 and +1.88 in Body Top-1 while using only RGB input. On iMiGUE, the margin is even larger: A³-MAE (67.08) beats the next-best VSwimT (60.11) by 6.97 points and MMN (60.21) by 6.87 points. Notably, the VideoMAE baseline alone already surpasses all prior pose-based methods (e.g., FR-Head 61.17, SkateFormer 59.67) on MA-52 Action Top-1, demonstrating that the architectural design of A³-MAE adds meaningful gains on top of a strong pretrained backbone rather than merely benefiting from the backbone itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VideoMAE's masked spatiotemporal reconstruction pretraining as a strong RGB-only backbone and repurposes the temporal gradient (TG) concept from LTG—not as an auxiliary training signal but as a spatial localization mechanism that carves out a subtle-motion feature branch via ROIAlign. It departs from the dominant MAR paradigm (PCAN, MMN, MANet) that injects skeletal priors or pose graphs, arguing instead that raw RGB with a well-designed VideoMAE fine-tuning recipe already captures most discriminative cues, and that the remaining gap is addressed by explicit holistic-subtle collaboration rather than by adding modalities. The dual cross-attention aggregation and confidence-aware dynamic weighting are novel architectural contributions layered on top of the VideoMAE encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for MAR practitioners: it demonstrates that costly pose annotations and auxiliary modalities can be eliminated without sacrificing accuracy, lowering the barrier to deploying micro-action recognition in real-world settings. The amplify-aggregate-adjust paradigm (TG-based ROI → dual cross-attention → confidence-weighted fusion) is a reusable recipe for any task where fine-grained local cues coexist with holistic context. Open questions include robustness to camera motion (TG is sensitive to global displacement), generalization beyond the two interview-style benchmarks, and whether the heuristic temporal-agreement thresholding scales to longer or more dynamic videos.
