## Problem

Diffusion-based synthetic data pipelines that enforce 3D structural alignment via ControlNet visual prompts (e.g., edge maps from CAD renders) suffer from over-conditioning: backgrounds become blurred, textures oversimplified, and scenes incoherent. No existing mechanism adaptively balances the strength of visual conditioning against the diffusion model's generative prior on a per-sample basis, and text prompts generated independently of the visual prompt often conflict with the geometric structure, producing hallucinated or inconsistent outputs.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a 3D CAD model, randomly sampled camera extrinsics (distance, azimuth, elevation) with fixed 35 mm focal-length intrinsics, and a real ImageNet reference image. The render is processed by a Canny edge detector to produce the visual prompt; the render and reference image are jointly consumed by a 7-agent VLM system to compose text prompts. Outputs are photorealistic synthetic images (Stable Diffusion v1.5, 20 denoising timesteps) paired with 2D classification labels, 3D pose annotations, and metadata, with the full dataset spanning 1 million images across all 1,000 ImageNet classes.

## Method

The core mechanism is a self-supervised adaptive visual prompt modulator: a lightweight 3-layer MLP that predicts a per-sample conditioning scale λ from the L2 norms of the diffusion model's intermediate U-Net features and the ControlNet output at the first denoising timestep, modifying the injection to f̂ = f + λ·F(I_prompt; θ). Pseudo-labels for training are obtained by generating a sequence of images across λ ∈ {0.3, …, 1.0}, applying k-means (k=2) clustering on pixel features to detect the 'just noticeable difference' threshold where pose alignment suddenly appears, and selecting the smallest λ in the aligned cluster—avoiding the degenerate λ=0 solution that a denoising-loss objective would trivially minimize. A complementary 7-agent VLM framework (using Qwen3-VL-23B-Instruct, 4-bit quantized, orchestrated via LangGraph StateGraph) decomposes prompt construction into specialized roles—subject identification, genre selection, pose extraction, scene composition, positive/negative prompt synthesis, and protected-term identification—ensuring textual guidance remains consistent with the visual prompt's geometric structure. Generated samples are filtered using DreamSim perceptual similarity (grayscale-invariant) between each synthetic image and its source render to retain the highest-quality outputs.

## Results

On ImageNet classification (50 classes), pre-training with AC3S synthetic data before fine-tuning yields the best overall top-1 accuracy across all three backbones: 77.33% (ResNet-18), 82.75% (ConvNeXt-S), and 84.19% (Swin-S), outperforming the 3D-DST baseline by up to 1.02 points. For 3D pose estimation on PASCAL3D+, AC3S pre-training delivers an average gain of 1.23% at the π/6 threshold and 2.17% at π/18 over 3D-DST, with the largest single-class improvement being +7.39% on sofa at π/18. In the synthetic-only setting (no real data fine-tuning), AC3S dramatically outperforms 3D-DST, e.g., 34.48% vs 10.84% on sofa at π/6, indicating AC3S images better approximate the real data distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AC3S consistently outperforms both baselines across all evaluated tasks and architectures. Against 3D-DST (the primary baseline sharing the same CAD-rendering + ControlNet pipeline), AC3S gains 0.37–1.02 points in overall ImageNet classification and 1.23–2.17 points in PASCAL3D+ pose estimation, with the gap widening to 20+ points in the synthetic-only pose estimation setting (Table 5). Against Text2Img [14], which uses a simpler text-only prompting strategy without 3D geometric control, AC3S achieves larger margins: +3.54 points on ConvNeXt-S and +2.83 points on Swin-S for overall classification, reflecting the benefit of combining adaptive visual conditioning with 3D-aware text prompts. The FID ablation (Table 2) further confirms that the visual prompt modulator and multi-agent VLM prompt generator each contribute independently (−9.66 and −8.26 FID points respectively), and their combination yields the largest improvement of −15.95 FID points over the 3D-DST baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D-DST pipeline (Ma et al., ICLR 2023), which established the paradigm of rendering CAD models, extracting edge maps, and conditioning ControlNet to generate synthetic images with 3D annotations. The key departure is addressing the over-conditioning problem inherent in 3D-DST: rather than injecting ControlNet features at full strength (Eq. 1 in Zhang et al., ICCV 2023), the authors introduce a learned per-sample attenuation scale that balances geometric fidelity against the diffusion model's generative prior. On the text side, the paper moves beyond the template-based and single-captioner approaches used in prior synthetic data work toward a multi-agent VLM framework (built on Qwen3-VL and LangGraph) that composes 3D-aware prompts consistent with the visual conditioning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the released 1M-image dataset spanning all 1,000 ImageNet classes with 2D and 3D annotations, which provides a ready resource for training classification and pose estimation models without requiring real data collection. The finding that unmodulated ControlNet outputs actively harm the denoising objective (unlike text conditioning, which helps) is a useful diagnostic for anyone building ControlNet-based pipelines. Open questions include whether the modulator generalizes to newer diffusion backbones (SDXL, DiT-based models), whether the 50-class evaluation protocol captures the full benefit at scale, and whether the multi-agent prompt system's gains persist when the underlying VLM is swapped or when class categories are more semantically ambiguous.
