## Problem

Instruction-guided video editing models struggle to simultaneously apply precise semantic modifications and preserve faithful temporal motion, and existing approaches over-rely on external priors (VLM features, depth/skeleton maps) that bottleneck robustness and generalization. The paper attributes this difficulty to the lack of factorization between semantic structure planning and motion modeling within the diffusion backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAMA takes a source video and a free-form text editing instruction, producing an edited target video at 480p resolution across multiple aspect ratios (1/2, 2/3, 3/4, 1/1 and reciprocals). The source and noisy target are encoded as VAE latent token sequences and concatenated into a single in-context sequence with learned type embeddings (id 0 = source, id 1 = semantic tokens, id 2 = target) to disambiguate roles. Semantic anchoring operates on N sparsely sampled anchor frames (N=1 in all experiments), extracting M=64 local SigLIP patch tokens plus one global token per anchor frame, projected into the latent space via a two-layer MLP.

## Method

SAMA is built on Wan2.1-T2V-14B with flow matching and factorizes editing into two complementary capabilities learned within the same DiT backbone. Semantic Anchoring prepends projected SigLIP semantic tokens to the target latent sequence, applies the same forward noising to both, and supervises token prediction with an L1 loss, providing instruction-aware structural planning at sparse anchor frames. Motion Alignment pre-trains the backbone on three motion-centric pretext tasks applied only to the source stream—cube inpainting (temporal block masking), speed perturbation (temporal acceleration), and tube shuffle (2×2×2 spatio-temporal permutation)—forcing the model to internalize temporal dynamics from raw videos. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: Stage 0 (factorized pre-training on image editing pairs plus text-to-video data with SA on all samples and MA on video samples, no paired editing data required) already yields strong zero-shot editing, and Stage 1 (SFT on Ditto-1M, OpenVE-3M, ReCo-Data with SA retained) resolves residual semantic-motion conflicts and improves edit fidelity.

## Results

On VIE-Bench, SAMA achieves the best overall scores among all compared methods on Swap/Change (9.340) and Remove (9.144), and is competitive on Add (8.711) and Style/Tone Change (9.259). On OpenVE-Bench, SAMA attains the top overall score, leading in Global Style (4.05), Local Change (3.93), Local Remove (3.32), Subtitle Edit (3.63), and Creative Edit (3.11). On ReCo-Bench, SAMA achieves the highest overall score across all four task categories: Add (8.92), Replace (9.10), Remove (8.09), and Style (9.25). The factorized pre-training stage alone (without any paired editing data) already yields strong zero-shot editing capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMA outperforms all open-source baselines (InstructX, UniVideo, VACE, Ditto, ReCo, ICVE, etc.) across all four VIE-Bench task categories, with the largest margins on Remove (9.144 vs. InstructX 8.322, +0.822) and Swap/Change (9.340 vs. InstructX 9.072, +0.268). Against the strongest closed-source system Kling-Omni, SAMA wins on Swap/Change (9.340 vs. 9.194, +0.146) and Remove (9.144 vs. 9.133, +0.011), but trails on Add (8.711 vs. 9.181, −0.470) and Style/Tone Change (9.259 vs. 9.341, −0.082). SAMA beats Runway on all four VIE-Bench categories, with the largest gap on Remove (9.144 vs. 8.504, +0.640). On OpenVE-Bench, SAMA leads in 5 of 7 sub-categories, trailing only Runway on Background Change (2.59 vs. 2.62) and Local Add (2.54 vs. 2.78). On ReCo-Bench, SAMA achieves the best overall score in every task category, with the most notable advantage on Replace (9.10 vs. UniVideo 8.90) and Style (9.25 vs. ReCo 9.17).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMA builds on the Wan2.1-T2V-14B video diffusion transformer and the flow-matching training paradigm, adopting the in-context V2V token-concatenation formulation popularized by UNIC, VACE, and EditVerse. Its key conceptual departure is from prior instruction-guided video editing methods that inject explicit external priors (VLM-extracted semantic conditions, depth maps, skeletons) to resolve the semantic-motion conflict; instead, SAMA argues this conflict stems from the lack of factorization between semantic structure planning and motion modeling. The Semantic Anchoring component draws on the semantic-alignment lineage (REPA, VideoREPA, SemanticGen) but repurposes SigLIP patch features as sparse anchor-frame tokens within the denoising sequence, while Motion Alignment repurposes self-supervised video pretext tasks (speed prediction, cubic puzzles, masked video modeling) as motion-centric restoration objectives applied to the source stream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factorized pre-training stage yielding strong zero-shot editing without any paired video-editing data is a notable conceptual result, suggesting that disentangled semantic and motion representations can induce editing behavior emergently. This reduces reliance on brittle external priors and offers a cleaner training recipe for open-source video editing. However, zero-shot results still exhibit temporal inconsistency in attribute edits, blurriness in added objects, and residual ghosting in removals, and the quantitative gains over strong open-source baselines (UniVideo, InstructX) are modest. Open questions include whether the factorization generalizes to long videos, extreme fast motion, and whether stronger semantic tokenization (beyond SigLIP patches) can close the remaining gap to commercial systems like Kling-Omni.
