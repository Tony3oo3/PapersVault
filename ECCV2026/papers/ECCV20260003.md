## Problem

Classical knowledge distillation forces the student to simultaneously match ground-truth labels and teacher predictions, but these two targets can be significantly misaligned due to the capacity gap between teacher and student. This 'transfer gap'—where teacher predictions are naturally imbalanced even on balanced data—makes the dual alignment objective ill-posed and undermines distillation efficacy. The paper asks whether teacher predictions can be repurposed not as a target to imitate but as a structural component of the student's own classification objective.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image fed through a lightweight student network (e.g., WRN-16-2, MobileNetV2, ShuffleNetV1) and a frozen pre-trained teacher network of any architecture. The teacher's logits and intermediate features are consumed as auxiliary supervision: teacher logits define the gating weights and soft-aggregation coefficients, while teacher features are pooled into expert prototypes. The student outputs a single class label over K classes, with no additional auxiliary outputs at inference time.

## Method

MoE-KD introduces the teacher's predicted class Y^T as a latent variable that decomposes the student's classifier into K conditional experts weighted by an input-dependent gating function. The gating function reuses the pre-trained teacher classifier applied to a projected version of the student's features (via a lightweight projector G), so no retraining of the teacher is needed. Each expert is a shared-weight softmax classifier with an expert-specific bias b_k = W^T e_k, where e_k is a soft aggregation of teacher features weighted by the teacher's own class-k predictions, injecting both logit-level and feature-level teacher knowledge. Training is formulated as an EM algorithm: the E-step computes the Bayes-optimal posterior over the latent variable given current parameters, and the M-step maximizes the resulting ELBO over a mini-batch, with a proven monotonic convergence guarantee. This eliminates the need for separate KL-divergence or cross-entropy loss terms—the entire distillation signal is embedded in the ELBO.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with homogeneous architectures (Table 1), MoE-KD achieves the best Top-1 accuracy in 5 out of 6 teacher-student pairs, with headline results of 76.98% (WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2), 75.21% (WRN-40-2→WRN-40-1), and 74.58% (ResNet110→ResNet32). On heterogeneous architectures (Table 2), MoE-KD wins all 5 pairs, reaching 78.69% (ResNet32x4→ShuffleNetV2) and 77.52% (WRN-40-2→ShuffleNetV1). On ImageNet-1K, MoE-KD achieves 73.15% Top-1 (ResNet34→ResNet18), surpassing the previous best TeKAP by 0.28%, though it trails DiffKD by 0.21% on the ResNet50→MobileNetV1 pair.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoE-KD outperforms all 25 baselines on 5 of 6 CIFAR-100 homogeneous pairs and all 5 heterogeneous pairs. The largest margins are over classical KD (e.g., +2.06% on WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2) and over recent methods like WTTM (+0.61% on WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2, +1.04% on ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4) and DiffKD (+0.38% on ResNet56→ResNet20). The sole loss is to EKD on ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4 (77.10% vs. 77.72%), a 0.62% gap. On ImageNet-1K, MoE-KD beats TeKAP by 0.28% and WKD-F by 0.65% on ResNet34→ResNet18, but trails DiffKD by 0.21% on ResNet50→MobileNetV1. The paper also shows that SRRL, which reuses the teacher classifier similarly, is mathematically subsumed as a special case of the MoE-KD ELBO, and empirically MoE-KD improves over SRRL by 1.02% on WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions knowledge distillation by treating the teacher's class predictions as latent variables within a Mixture-of-Experts framework, rather than as soft labels to be matched via KL divergence as in classical KD (Hinton et al., 2015). The gating function reuses the pre-trained teacher classifier (building on the reused-teacher-classifier idea from [6] and SRRL [83]), while experts are parameterized via multi-level teacher outputs (logit-level and feature-level) through soft aggregation inspired by deep sets. The authors prove that their ELBO formulation mathematically subsumes SRRL as a special case, establishing a clean theoretical lineage. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant 'teacher-centered' KD paradigm (where students passively imitate) toward a 'student-centered' partition-and-classify paradigm, drawing an explicit analogy to noise transition matrices in label-noise learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MoE reframing resolves a long-standing tension in classical KD—simultaneously aligning student predictions with both ground-truth labels and teacher predictions—by decomposing the classification task into subtasks weighted by the teacher's own confidence, which is a principled way to handle the transfer gap. The EM formulation with a convergence proof provides theoretical grounding that most prior KD methods lack. However, the paper acknowledges it explores only one parameterization scheme for the MoE framework and is limited to classification; extending the approach to other tasks (detection, segmentation) or exploring alternative expert/gating parameterizations remains open. The practical overhead is minimal (a small projector and two-layer MLP), making the method easy to adopt.
