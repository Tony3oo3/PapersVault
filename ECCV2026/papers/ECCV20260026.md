## Problem

Existing gait analysis methods and foundation models are designed for healthy adults and fail to capture the high-entropy, developmentally variable motor patterns of children aged 3–17. Clinical assessment of pediatric gait disorders (e.g., cerebral palsy, hemiplegia) currently relies on expensive 3D motion capture or intrusive IMU sensors, while standard RGB video-based approaches lack the fine-grained, phase-specific scoring granularity required for the Edinburgh Visual Gait Score (EVGS). This paper defines fine-grained pediatric gait decoding from video as a new CV problem and provides the first dedicated multi-view dataset and benchmark for it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3–5 second RGB video clips (1920×1080, originally 60 FPS, downsampled to 30 FPS) from sagittal and coronal camera views, from which T=16 frames are uniformly sampled per temporal window. Skeletal keypoints (from Sapiens-2B) are rendered directly onto each frame as visual prompts, and instance segmentation masks (from SAM 3) guide deterministic patch pruning. The output is a binary 'typical vs. atypical' prediction for each of 17 EVGS items per limb (34 bilateral items total), where the original 3-point ordinal scale is binarized by merging moderate and marked deviation scores.

## Method

ChildGait-Video builds on the VideoMAE v2 ViT encoder with an MLP classification head, trained via a two-stage paradigm: pre-training on Kinetics-710 for general human motion understanding, followed by supervised fine-tuning on the CGV dataset. Two key mechanisms differentiate it from standard video fine-tuning: (1) Token-Level Kinematic Prompting, which renders 2D skeletal keypoints and their connectivity graph onto the RGB frame so that ViT patches inherently encode local anatomical topology alongside appearance; and (2) Mask-Guided Patch Pruning, which deterministically drops all patches whose region falls outside the subject's instance segmentation mask, forcing self-attention to allocate capacity exclusively to the child's gait while reducing computational cost. The model is optimized end-to-end with a Soft Target Cross-Entropy loss that accounts for the positive/negative sample ratio per item. The paper further demonstrates that zero-shot MLLMs (Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.2, etc.) and fine-tuned VLMs (Qwen3-VL-4B) achieve only ~50–60% average accuracy, confirming that generic multimodal reasoning cannot substitute for domain-specific spatiotemporal modeling of pediatric gait.

## Results

On the CGV test set, ChildGait-Video achieves an average accuracy of 84% for both left and right limbs across all 17 EVGS scoring items (34 bilateral items total), with a per-item accuracy range of 70%–93% and an average F1-score of 0.83. This represents a 15% and 12% improvement over the fine-tuned VideoMAE v2 baseline (69% L-AVG, 72% R-AVG, F1 0.70/0.71), and a 30+ point margin over the best gait-recognition baselines (BiggerGait at 54%/53%, F1 0.53/0.51). Zero-shot MLLMs (Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.2, Qwen3-VL-235B, etc.) achieve only 50–60% average accuracy, barely above random for binary scoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChildGait-Video outperforms all 13 baselines across every scoring item. Against the strongest gait-recognition model, BiggerGait (pre-trained on CCPG), it gains +30/+31 points in average accuracy (84% vs. 54%/53%) and +0.30/+0.32 in F1. The fine-tuned VideoMAE v2 baseline is the closest competitor at 69%/72% average accuracy (F1 0.70/0.71); ChildGait-Video's two-stage adaptation (Kinetics-710 pre-training + CGV fine-tuning with kinematic prompting and mask-guided pruning) adds +15/+12 points over it, a difference confirmed statistically significant by McNemar's test (p = 2.3×10⁻⁴). Among gait-specific architectures, SwinGait (Gait3D pre-training) and GaitBase (GREW pre-training) are the best at 54%/56% and 53%/56% respectively, still trailing ChildGait-Video by ~28–30 points. Zero-shot MLLMs (Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.2, Qwen3-VL-235B, GLM4.6V, Qwen3.5-9B, InternVL3-8B) all cluster around 50–60% average accuracy, and fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-4B on CGV yields only a marginal +2%/+1% gain, confirming that VLMs are fundamentally unsuited to this fine-grained kinematic scoring task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the adult-centric gait recognition literature (GaitSet, GaitBase, SwinGait, BiggerGait) by identifying a fundamental domain shift: pediatric skeletal proportions and high-entropy motor patterns invalidate representations learned on healthy adult subjects. It repurposes VideoMAE v2, a general video foundation model pre-trained on Kinetics-710, as the backbone rather than a gait-specific architecture, and augments it with two domain-specific adaptations—token-level kinematic prompting (rendering skeletal keypoints onto RGB frames) and mask-guided patch pruning (SAM 3 segmentation to suppress background). The paper also positions itself against the rapidly growing MLLM/VLM literature by systematically demonstrating that both zero-shot and fine-tuned vision-language models (Qwen3-VL, Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.2) are inadequate for fine-grained clinical gait scoring, thereby carving out a distinct problem space from general-purpose video understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CGV dataset is the first open-sourced multi-view pediatric gait video resource with clinically grounded EVGS annotations, filling a critical gap that limits scalable, non-intrusive screening for conditions like cerebral palsy in resource-constrained settings. A key open question is whether the 70–93% per-item accuracy (binary typical/atypical) is sufficient for clinical deployment, especially given the simplification of merging ordinal scores 1 and 2, and whether the framework generalizes beyond the single-hospital, 110-subject cohort to in-the-wild, longitudinal, and multi-pathology settings. The finding that a general video foundation model substantially outperforms all purpose-built gait recognition architectures suggests the field may need to rethink whether gait-specific inductive biases are actually beneficial for clinical scoring versus identity discrimination.
