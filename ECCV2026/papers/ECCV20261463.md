## Problem

Tone style transfer for photo retouching lacks high-quality large-scale triplet datasets with stylized ground truth, forcing existing methods to rely on self-supervised or proxy objectives that inherently constrain model capability. Additionally, prevailing architectures extract content and reference features independently before fusing them in a decoder, causing semantic loss that leads to inappropriate color transfer (e.g., mapping object colors onto human faces) and degraded visual aesthetics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261463/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image and a reference image as input, which are concatenated along the spatial dimension to form a joint visual context. A binary mask is applied to the region corresponding to the stylized output while the content and reference regions remain visible; the masked region is initialized as noise and progressively denoised. The output is a tone-stylized image that preserves the semantic structure of the content image while adopting the color distribution, luminance, contrast, and saturation characteristics of the reference. The TST100K dataset provides 100,000 content-reference-stylized triplets for training, and TST2K (2,000 manually reviewed triplets spanning portrait, food, landscape, night, and lifestyle scenes) serves as the evaluation benchmark.

## Method

ICTone formulates tone style transfer as an in-context generation task using a diffusion transformer (FLUX.1 Fill backbone, fine-tuned via LoRA), where the content and reference images are jointly conditioned as visible context and the model learns to denoise the masked output region, implicitly performing semantic-aware tone transfer without explicit content-style disentanglement. Training uses a flow-matching objective as the primary loss, followed by a tone reward feedback loss that backpropagates through a pretrained CLIP-based tone style scorer (ViT-B/16) to enforce stylistic fidelity. The tone style scorer itself is trained in two stages: weakly-supervised contrastive learning on preset-generated pairs, then preference learning with 20K human-ranked annotations using a triplet loss. The TST100K dataset is constructed by applying ~3,000 curated presets to normalized images, then filtering with both an aesthetic non-degradation constraint and a tone consistency threshold (cosine similarity ≥ 0.8 in the learned tone embedding space) to ensure reliable supervision.

## Results

On TST2K, ICTone achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CP 0.8644, ΔE 5.776, CD 2.634, and Aes 0.7904 (vs. GT Aes 0.7978). On the cross-dataset generalization benchmark PST50, ICTone attains the highest CP (0.7902) and lowest CD (4.448), though SA-LUT achieves a slightly lower ΔE (11.25 vs. 12.81). In the user study, ICTone achieves a perfect average ranking of 1.00 across 20 participants, outperforming the next-best method Neural Preset (2.70). Retraining CAP-VSTNet and Neural Preset on TST100K (denoted with *) yields substantial gains (e.g., CAP-VSTNet ΔE drops from 11.87 to 7.359), confirming the value of the triplet dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICTone outperforms all 15 baselines on TST2K across every metric, with the largest margins over traditional color-transfer methods (WCT2, PhotoWCT, MKL) and a clear lead over the strongest learned baselines CAP-VSTNet* (ΔE 7.359, CD 3.663) and Neural Preset* (ΔE 7.886, CD 3.860). On PST50, ICTone wins on CP (0.7902 vs. 0.7697 for SA-LUT), CD (4.448 vs. 4.483), and Aes (0.2820 vs. 0.2048), but SA-LUT achieves a lower ΔE (11.25 vs. 12.81), indicating a slight trade-off in raw colorimetric distance under cross-dataset conditions. The closed-source Nano Banana2 suffers from reference foreground leakage, yielding a low CP of 0.5968 on TST2K despite a competitive Aes of 0.7885. CSGO's anomalously high PST50 Aes (0.3607, exceeding GT) is attributed to excessive saturation enhancement rather than genuine retouching fidelity. In the user study, ICTone achieves a perfect average rank of 1.00, while the next-best methods are Neural Preset (2.70) and MKL (3.60), confirming that quantitative gains translate to perceptual preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-decoder paradigm in photorealistic tone style transfer (Neural Preset, CAP-VSTNet, SA-LUT, PhotoWCT), where content and reference features are extracted independently and fused in a decoder, by instead formulating tone transfer as an in-context generation task within a diffusion transformer (DiT) framework. It extends the ReFL reward feedback learning paradigm from text-to-image synthesis to the content-reference in-context setting, and adopts the flow-matching objective as its primary training loss. The two-stage tone style scorer (weakly supervised contrastive learning followed by preference learning) is a novel measurement tool that has no direct predecessor in the tone transfer literature, though it draws on CLIP-style embedding architectures and supervised contrastive learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TST100K dataset addresses a fundamental bottleneck in the field: prior methods were forced into self-supervised or proxy-objective training because no large-scale ground-truth triplet data existed, and the retraining experiments confirm that data quality was the primary limiter rather than architecture. The in-context formulation offers a conceptually cleaner alternative to explicit content-style disentanglement, leveraging generative model priors for semantic-aware transfer. Open questions include the computational cost of spatial concatenation (acknowledged as inefficient by the authors), the stylistic bias inherited from the aesthetic assessment model used in filtering, and whether more efficient conditioning mechanisms (e.g., cross-attention or token-level injection) can match the in-context approach without the overhead.
