## Problem

Existing diffusion-based data augmentation methods improve synthetic data by increasing realism, diversity, or domain alignment, but overlook a more fundamental question: how should a sample's usefulness for the downstream classification task be measured and optimized? In few-shot medical imaging, where every added example matters, generated samples that appear visually plausible can contribute little to classifier performance, and no principled criterion existed to distinguish task-useful samples from merely realistic ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262755/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label as conditioning, a small set of labeled images (16 or 32 per class) to fine-tune both the Stable Diffusion 2.1 generator and the ViT-B/16 classifier via LoRA, and a validation set used exclusively for gradient-based reward computation during RL (not for classifier training). It produces 500 synthetic 224×224 RGB images per class for augmenting the training set. The validation set is class-balanced for RL reward computation, while the classifier is trained on the full unbalanced validation split for checkpoint selection.

## Method

The core contribution is Class-Contrastive Influence (C2I), a gradient-based criterion that scores a synthetic sample by the gap between its cosine-similarity influence on same-class validation gradients versus other-class validation gradients, penalized by within-class variance. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that in logistic regression, maximizing C2I drives the sample toward the global mean of the validation set, which lies near the decision boundary, explaining why high-C2I samples are hard, boundary-proximal examples. The diffusion model is then fine-tuned via DDPO (denoising diffusion policy optimization) using C2I as the RL reward: projected LoRA gradients of generated samples are compared against precomputed validation gradients to compute the reward on-the-fly, steering generation toward class-informative regions without any architectural changes to the generator or classifier. For multi-class settings, C2I is extended as a softmax over class-conditional mean influence scores, reducing to a sigmoid of the influence gap in the binary case.

## Results

On the ViT-B/16 backbone, C2I-guided augmentation achieves an average AUC of 0.894 across BreastMNIST (0.885), DermaMNIST-binary (0.853), and PneumoniaMNIST (0.945), outperforming all baselines. With the ResNet-18 backbone (not used during RL fine-tuning), the method still leads with 0.882 average AUC, confirming cross-architecture transfer. Under noisy test conditions (Table 2), C2I yields the best average robustness AUC on BreastMNIST (0.819) and DermaMNIST-binary (0.834), though DataDream slightly edges it on PneumoniaMNIST (0.910 vs. 0.893). On the 7-class DermaMNIST-all task, C2I reaches 68.3% accuracy versus 66.9% for the best baseline (RandAugment).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C2I consistently outperforms all six baselines on average AUC for both ViT and ResNet-18 backbones. Against the strongest standard augmentation (RandomErasing, 0.885 avg AUC on ViT), C2I gains +0.9 points; against the strongest diffusion-based method (Dataset Expansion, 0.880), the gap is +1.4 points. DistDiff is the weakest baseline, underperforming even the original-only setting on BreastMNIST (0.764 vs. 0.828), while C2I improves it to 0.885. On robustness under noise, C2I leads on BreastMNIST (0.819 avg vs. 0.799 for DataDream) and DermaMNIST-binary (0.834 vs. 0.820 for both DataDream and Dataset Expansion), but DataDream retains a slight edge on PneumoniaMNIST (0.910 vs. 0.893). The ResNet-18 results (0.882 avg) confirm that C2I-generated samples transfer to an unseen architecture, outperforming DataDream (0.865) and Dataset Expansion (0.864) by 1.7–1.8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: diffusion-model-based data augmentation (DataDream, Dataset Expansion, DistDiff), gradient-based influence estimation (Pruthi et al., LESS), and RL fine-tuning of diffusion models (DDPO, DPOK). The key departure is repurposing influence estimation not as a data-selection criterion (as in LESS for LLMs) but as a generative reward signal, and introducing the class-contrastive framing (C2I) that captures the gap between same-class and opposite-class gradient alignment rather than raw influence magnitude. The theoretical connection to hard, boundary-proximal examples via Theorem 1 (logistic regression) provides a principled justification that prior augmentation methods, which optimize for realism or diversity, lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the augmentation question from 'how to generate realistic images' to 'how to generate task-useful images,' offering a plug-and-play RL scheme that requires no architectural changes. However, the empirical gains are modest (e.g., ~1 AUC point over the best baseline on average), the theoretical result is restricted to logistic regression, and the method depends on a validation split for gradient computation, limiting applicability in truly label-scarce settings. Open questions include whether C2I-guided generation scales to larger datasets or more classes, whether the validation-set dependency can be removed, and whether the approach composes with other augmentation strategies beyond simple replacement.
