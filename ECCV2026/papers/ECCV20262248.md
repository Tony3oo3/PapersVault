## Problem

Scanpath prediction has relied on specialized architectures with hand-crafted geometric priors (inhibition of return, saccadic biases, separate spatial/temporal branches), which restrict flexible conditioning on task instructions, viewer identity, or fixation attributes without bespoke architectural modifications. The paper asks whether pretrained Large Vision-Language Models, with their jointly learned semantic and sequential priors, can model human scanpaths natively as autoregressive sequence generation, absorbing diverse conditioning factors through simple prompt changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a natural-language prompt that can specify the viewing task (free-viewing, visual search for a named target), an anonymized subject identifier, and optionally per-fixation durations. Output is an ordered sequence of fixation coordinates encoded as zero-padded two-digit integers (00–99) representing percentage positions along each image dimension, with each fixation contributing exactly four coordinate tokens (two for x, two for y) to guarantee uniform tokenization. The same autoregressive outputs can be decomposed via a prefix-tree traversal into a full 100×100 spatial probability map for AUC computation, or scored in just four forward passes per fixation for exact Information Gain.

## Method

The core contribution is reframing scanpath prediction as next-token prediction over a discretized coordinate vocabulary, fine-tuning InternVL3.5-8B with LoRA (rank 8–32) on standard cross-entropy loss over ground-truth scanpath token sequences. Coordinates are mapped to zero-padded two-digit integers so every fixation occupies exactly four tokens, eliminating tokenization-length confounds and making the VLM's autoregressive log-likelihood directly equivalent to the Information Gain metric. For full spatial probability maps, the joint P(x,y) is decomposed via the chain rule into a four-phase prefix tree (1+10+100+1000 = 1,111 forward passes with KV caching), while IG-only evaluation requires just four passes per fixation by conditioning on the ground-truth history. Conditioning on subject identity, search targets, or fixation durations requires only prompt modifications—no architectural changes—demonstrating that the VLM's pretrained representations, not the vision encoder alone, drive the 46% IG improvement over matched-backbone DeepGaze III.

## Results

DeepGaze3.5-VL (Combined) achieves state-of-the-art Information Gain across all five eye-tracking datasets: 2.18 bits/fixation on MIT1003, 2.19 on COCO-FreeView, 2.07 on CAT2000, 2.75 on DAEMONS, and 1.93 on FiGrIm. The headline result is a 46% IG improvement over the matched-backbone DeepGaze III-6B baseline (2.18 vs. 1.49 bits on MIT1003), with parallel gains on every other dataset. AUC scores are consistently competitive (0.93–0.95), confirming that the IG gains reflect genuine probabilistic calibration rather than mere ranking improvements. On COCO-Search18, the search-conditioned model reaches 3.27 bits/fixation aggregate IG, outperforming HAT (3.03 target-present / 1.68 target-absent) in both conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest dedicated scanpath baseline, DeepGaze III-6B (retrained with the same InternViT-6B vision encoder for a matched-backbone comparison), DeepGaze3.5-VL wins on all five datasets by 0.44–0.69 bits IG (e.g., 2.18 vs. 1.49 on MIT1003, a 46% relative gain), demonstrating that the improvement comes from the VLM's sequential and semantic priors rather than from a stronger vision encoder. Against HAT [44], the method outperforms by 0.69–0.94 bits IG across all five datasets, and against TPPGaze [41] by 0.67–1.23 bits where both are reported. On COCO-Search18 alignment metrics (Table 2), DeepGaze3.5-VL beats both GazeFormer [31] and GazeXplain [32] on ScanMatch (0.60 vs. 0.48/0.43), MultiMatch (0.89 vs. 0.81/0.80), Fixation Edit Distance (1.67 vs. 2.06/2.10), Sequence Score (0.65 vs. 0.54/0.47), and Semantic Sequence Score (0.47 vs. 0.44/0.39) for target-present trials, though it is marginally worse on String Edit Distance (2.21 vs. 1.98 for GazeXplain). The LODO (out-of-distribution) setting shows the method still outperforms in-distribution DeepGaze III-6B on four of five held-out datasets, confirming transferable attention modeling rather than dataset overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the entire lineage of specialized scanpath architectures—mechanistic models (inhibition of return, Lévy walks, object-based targeting), hand-crafted sequential networks (DeepGaze III), and recent generative frameworks (diffusion models, neural point processes)—all of which share the pattern of pretrained spatial features paired with from-scratch temporal machinery. Instead, it repurposes the autoregressive token-prediction paradigm already proven in VLMs for geometric tasks (Pix2Seq, Shikra, Kosmos2) by discretizing fixation coordinates into a uniform digit vocabulary, thereby inheriting both semantic and sequential priors from billions of tokens of visually grounded reasoning. The key conceptual departure is that no bespoke geometric head, saccadic bias module, or point-process intensity function is needed; conditioning on task, subject, or duration is handled entirely through natural-language prompt modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 46% Information Gain improvement over a matched-backbone DeepGaze III (which uses a *larger* vision encoder) convincingly isolates the contribution of the VLM's language/sequential modeling rather than raw visual features, supporting the paper's 'Bitter Lesson' claim that general autoregressive methods overtake hand-designed oculomotor priors. The counterfactual intervention capability—sweeping fixation duration in-silico and recovering ambient/focal processing transitions—opens a new experimental tool for cognitive science that is impractical in vivo. Open questions include whether the 100×100 coordinate grid can be refined without prohibitive prefix-tree cost, whether the approach extends to dynamic/video scenes, and whether the strong MMMU–IG correlation (ρ=0.93) implies that future VLM improvements will automatically transfer to gaze modeling or whether task-specific fine-tuning remains necessary.
