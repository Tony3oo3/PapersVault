## Problem

Existing 4D human reconstruction methods either rely on slow iterative optimization over dense calibrated views or are limited to static per-timestamp 3D reconstruction, making them unable to produce photorealistic novel-time renderings from uncalibrated sparse-view videos. No prior feed-forward model achieves metric-scale 4D human-object reconstruction with both novel-view and novel-time synthesis in an efficient streaming manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260762/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes n (typically 4) uncalibrated sparse-view RGB frames at ~45° camera angles with a consistent temporal length k, processed at 518×518 resolution. It outputs a pixel-aligned 4D dynamic 3D Gaussian representation (position, opacity, color, rotation, scale maps per view per frame), a dense dual 3D motion field between consecutive frames, and a predicted metric scale factor. Novel-view and novel-time images are rendered on demand from the interpolated 3DGS, with the interpolation step adding only ~1.5 ms per 10 intermediate frames.

## Method

Forge4D decomposes 4D reconstruction into streaming 3D Gaussian prediction and dense motion estimation, trained in three progressive stages on a VGGT backbone with a DPT head. Stage 1 introduces a metric gauge regularization that computes a consistent translation-scale ratio between predicted and GT camera extrinsics, resolving the fundamental scale ambiguity between VGGT's normalized output and real-world metric scale. Stage 2 adds learnable state tokens that iteratively encode prior-frame information via cross-attention (serving as Key/Value to the current frame and Query to update themselves), enforcing temporal scale consistency in a memory-friendly streaming fashion without global attention over all frames. Stage 3 formulates dense motion prediction as a 3D Gaussian point-matching problem, supervised self-supervisedly by a retargeting loss that warps current 3DGS to the adjacent frame and compares rendered images, plus an occlusion-aware optical flow loss using SEA-RAFT pseudo-ground-truth with cyclic consistency masking. An occlusion-aware fusion MLP then merges deformed 3DGS from two neighboring frames at novel timestamps, using a dual consistency factor to preserve occluded regions and eliminate flickering artifacts.

## Results

On the DNA-Rendering test set, Forge4D achieves 29.04 PSNR / 0.9566 SSIM / 0.0535 LPIPS for novel-time and novel-view synthesis, surpassing the best optimization-based baseline D-3DGS (20.92 PSNR) by +8.12 dB and the best feed-forward 4D baseline L4GM (18.03 PSNR) by +11.01 dB. On the out-of-domain Genebody dataset, the gap widens to +11.84 dB over D-3DGS and +12.57 dB over L4GM (27.42 vs. 14.86 PSNR). For in-the-wild evaluation on SelfCap and VVC, Forge4D reaches 25.47 PSNR on novel-time synthesis, exceeding L4GM (14.88 PSNR) by +10.59 dB. Additionally, Forge4D achieves a metric-scale point distance of 0.0264 m (vs. 0.3309 m for MoGe-2) and a dense motion error of 0.00953 (vs. 0.01274 for POMATO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Forge4D outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluation setting. Against optimization-based 4D methods (D-3DGS, DualGS, SpaceTimeGS), Forge4D gains 8–13 dB in PSNR on both DNA-Rendering and Genebody, demonstrating that iterative per-scene optimization fails under sparse-view conditions where the feed-forward model generalizes. Against the closest feed-forward 4D competitor L4GM, Forge4D leads by +11.01 dB (DNA-Rendering) and +12.57 dB (Genebody) on novel-time synthesis, and by +10.59 dB on in-the-wild data, attributed to the retargeting loss and occlusion-aware fusion. Among pose-free feed-forward 3D baselines (NoPosplat, AnySplat), Forge4D exceeds AnySplat by +2.90 dB PSNR on DNA-Rendering static novel-view synthesis, while NoPosplat trails by a much larger margin (11.76 vs. 29.82 PSNR). For metric-scale recovery, Forge4D's 0.0264 m point distance is 12.5× better than MoGe-2 (0.3309 m). For dense motion prediction, Forge4D reduces motion error by 25% and retargeted point distance by 35× compared to POMATO (0.0215 m vs. 0.7555 m). The template-based LHM baseline, which cannot perform novel-time synthesis, is outperformed on novel-view PSNR by +12.9 dB on in-the-wild data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Forge4D builds directly on VGGT as a pre-trained visual geometry backbone, extending the feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction line (AnySplat, NoPosplat, GPS-Gaussian) from static, per-timestamp scenes to dynamic 4D human-object sequences. It departs from iterative optimization methods (D-3DGS, SpaceTimeGS, DualGS) by achieving single-pass reconstruction, and from the concurrent L4GM by addressing scale alignment, temporal consistency via state tokens, and explicit dense motion prediction as a 3D point-matching problem. The formulation of motion learning through a self-supervised retargeting loss and occlusion-aware optical flow supervision represents a conceptual departure from prior 4D methods that rely solely on middle-frame photometric supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first feed-forward model to jointly handle uncalibrated sparse-view 4D human reconstruction with metric-scale recovery and novel-time interpolation at ~44 effective FPS, opening the door to real-time holographic communication, sports broadcasting, and AR/VR applications that previously required per-scene optimization. Key limitations remain: performance degrades under large inter-frame motions or long temporal gaps due to the constant-velocity assumption, and the method is currently validated primarily on human-object interaction scenarios. Open questions include whether the point-matching formulation generalizes to non-rigid deformations beyond human motion, and whether the streaming state-token mechanism scales to very long video sequences without drift.
