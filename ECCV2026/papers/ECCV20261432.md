## Problem

Most image deblurring methods operate directly in the spatial domain, overlooking the critical role of Fourier phase in encoding structural and geometric information needed to recover sharp details. Existing unrolled networks apply algorithm unrolling to spatial-domain optimization without explicitly modeling the amplitude-phase decomposition, and fully deep architectures suffer from poor generalization under high noise and limited training data. This paper asks whether principled LMMSE estimation of both amplitude and phase, combined with algorithm unrolling, can yield a more robust and data-efficient deblurring framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurred, noisy RGB image as input and produces a deblurred sharp image as output. It operates in a blind setting (no prior knowledge of the blur kernel), working internally in the Fourier domain where the observation is decomposed into amplitude and phase matrices. Training is performed on 256×256 patches with standard augmentations, while evaluation is conducted on full-resolution images from GoPro, RealBlur-R, RealBlur-J, and a synthetic COCO benchmark with controlled motion blur and Gaussian noise.

## Method

UPADNet derives novel LMMSE estimators for the amplitude and phase of a blurred, noisy observation, showing that the naive multiplicative (amplitude) and additive (phase) Fourier relations are suboptimal under noise and kernel uncertainty. A regularized objective combining amplitude and phase data-fidelity terms with mixed ℓ₁/ℓ₂ penalties is solved via alternating optimization with Half-Quadratic Splitting, yielding closed-form updates and soft-thresholding proximal operators. The iterative algorithm is then unrolled into a deep network where each iteration becomes a UPADBlock with learnable parameters, and a Weight Generator module (using SimpleGate and SCA from NAFNet) dynamically produces iteration-specific weights and frequency masks. A multi-scale strategy with intermediate downsampling and upsampling is applied across 72 stacked UPADBlocks, and GELU activations replace the ideal soft-thresholding to improve gradient flow. The entire network is trained end-to-end with AdamW on paired clean/degraded data, requiring only 27M parameters.

## Results

On GoPro, UPADNet achieves 34.63 dB PSNR and 0.975 SSIM, setting a new state-of-the-art among all compared methods. On the real-world RealBlur-R and RealBlur-J benchmarks, UPADNet reaches 41.29/0.979 and 34.35/0.946 (PSNR/SSIM) respectively, outperforming the closest competitor EVSSM by 0.02 and 0.01 dB. The model uses only 27M parameters, substantially fewer than AdaRevD (220M) and NAFNet (67.8M), while also demonstrating superior noise robustness (30.86 dB at σ=0.05 on COCO vs. 30.11 dB for AdaRevD) and data efficiency (only 0.46 dB drop from 100% to 60% training data vs. 2.59 dB for AdaRevD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPADNet outperforms all 11 baselines across GoPro, RealBlur-R, and RealBlur-J. The closest competitor is EVSSM (17.1M params), which UPADNet edges out by 0.12 dB on GoPro and 0.02/0.01 dB on RealBlur-R/J; the margin widens substantially against larger models like AdaRevD (220M params), where UPADNet leads by 4.76 dB on RealBlur-R and 4.23 dB on RealBlur-J despite using 8× fewer parameters. Against frequency-domain methods (FFTformer, UFPNet), UPADNet gains 0.42–1.73 dB, confirming the benefit of explicit phase-amplitude decomposition over implicit spectral modeling. On the COCO noise-robustness benchmark, UPADNet maintains a consistent 0.48–0.75 dB advantage over AdaRevD as Gaussian noise increases from σ=0.01 to σ=0.05, while unrolled baselines DUBLID and Net-A degrade more sharply. Under reduced training data (60%), UPADNet retains 32.12 dB PSNR versus 29.51 dB for AdaRevD and 27.21 dB for Stripformer, highlighting its data efficiency as an unrolled architecture with physics-inspired inductive bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

UPADNet extends the algorithm-unrolling paradigm (Monga et al., 2021) by unrolling an iterative phase-amplitude recovery algorithm rather than a spatial-domain optimization, and it draws on Half-Quadratic Splitting (Geman & Reynolds, 1995) to handle the non-smooth ℓ₁ penalties in the Fourier-domain objective. The key conceptual departure from prior unrolled deblurring networks (DUBLID, DeepSN-Net) is the explicit Fourier-domain decomposition into amplitude and phase, for which the authors derive novel LMMSE estimators (Theorems 1–2) that provably dominate the naive multiplicative/additive relations under noise. The Weight Generator module borrows the SimpleGate and SCA mechanisms from NAFNet (Chen et al., 2022) to produce iteration-specific parameters, but the overall architecture is fundamentally different from spatial-domain CNN or transformer deblurring pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, statistically grounded justification for why explicit phase modeling improves deblurring: phase carries structural information that naive Fourier inversion corrupts under noise, and LMMSE estimation corrects this. The consistent outperformance in high-noise and reduced-training-data regimes suggests that the structured inductive bias from the LMMSE formulation acts as a regularizer that pure data-driven networks lack, which is practically important for resource-constrained or real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the shift-invariant convolution assumption (Eq. 1) limits applicability to non-uniform or spatially-varying blur, whether the LMMSE framework extends naturally to other inverse problems (denoising, super-resolution), and whether the 72-block depth is necessary or whether fewer blocks with stronger per-block capacity could match performance at lower cost.
