## Problem

Arbitrary-scale super-resolution (ASSR) models trained on synthetic bicubic degradations suffer severe performance collapse on real-world images, where complex physical distortions are highly entangled with continuous zooming scales. Existing remedies—constructing real-world paired datasets or iteratively generating paired data for retraining—are fundamentally limited by discrete scale coverage, hardware biases, and prohibitive computational costs across infinite fractional scales. This paper asks whether a frozen, synthetic-trained ASSR model can be adapted to real-world continuous-scale inference without any domain-specific retraining.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a real-world LR image, a continuous target scale factor s, and an arbitrary unpaired HR reference image (any random image bicubically downsampled by s to serve as a scale-matched proxy). The CDR module produces a canonical bicubic-style LR variant that is fed into a frozen ASSR backbone (LIIF, CiaoSR, or HIIF), which then queries pixel values at arbitrary continuous coordinates to yield the final super-resolved HR image. The unpaired reference is never semantically related to the input; it only supplies degradation and scale statistics, making the system truly zero-shot with no paired real-world data required.

## Method

The Continuous Degradation Rectifier (CDR) is a plug-and-play conditional diffusion proxy that projects scale-entangled real-world LR inputs into a canonical bicubic latent space via a progressive orthogonal decoupling mechanism. First, a VQ-VAE discrete codebook bottleneck purifies scale-invariant content by structurally filtering high-frequency artifacts, supervised with clean bicubic targets across random continuous scales to enforce scale-normalization. Second, with the content encoder frozen, a degradation encoder and a scale modulation encoder are sequentially trained via scale-equivariant contrastive learning—anchoring contrastive triplets on continuous scaling factors rather than content—to strictly orthogonalize scale-independent degradation from continuous scale modulation. Third, these orthogonal representations are fused through a scale-adaptive bandwidth modulation that applies channel-wise affine parameters (predicted from the scale feature) to a base degradation kernel, dynamically adjusting its frequency bandwidth to match the target scale. The resulting scale-adaptive kernel conditions a DDPM reverse diffusion process to synthesize the bicubic-style LR image, which is then passed to the frozen ASSR model for arbitrary-scale reconstruction.

## Results

On RealSR (×4), CiaoSR+ours achieves 26.92 dB PSNR and 0.1910 LPIPS, surpassing the strongest fixed-scale diffusion baseline RealDGen (26.16 dB / 0.2226 LPIPS) by +0.75 dB PSNR and −0.03 LPIPS. On DRealSR (×4), the method sets a new PSNR record of 28.81 dB (vs. 28.66 dB for RealDGen). On the continuous-scale COZ benchmark (×2–×6), CDR yields consistent PSNR gains of +3.46 to +4.0 dB over unrectified ASSR backbones, with SSIM improvements up to +0.098 and LPIPS reductions up to −0.200, even at extreme out-of-distribution scales (×5, ×6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDR-equipped ASSR models outperform all seven GAN- and diffusion-based fixed-scale baselines (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN, SynDiff, ICF, PiSA, AdaDiffSR, OSEDiff) on PSNR and LPIPS across both RealSR and DRealSR, with margins of +0.8 to +3.7 dB PSNR over the weakest baselines. Against the strongest diffusion baseline RealDGen [28], CiaoSR+ours wins on PSNR by +0.75 dB (RealSR) and +0.14 dB (DRealSR) and on LPIPS by −0.03 (RealSR), but trails on SSIM (−0.01 on RealSR, −0.06 on DRealSR) and LPIPS (+0.08 on DRealSR), reflecting a slight structural-fidelity trade-off on DRealSR. On the continuous-scale COZ benchmark, CDR delivers uniform +3.5–4.0 dB PSNR gains over unrectified ASSR baselines at every tested scale (×2 through ×6), confirming that the scale-adaptive bandwidth modulation generalizes well beyond the training range. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that fixed-scale training variants (×2, ×3, ×4) yield only marginal gains (+0.1–0.4 dB) over the unrectified baseline, whereas the proposed continuous-scale formulation achieves +3.5–4.0 dB, validating the necessity of explicit scale modulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RealDGen [28] paradigm of kernel-based content-degradation modulation from fixed scales to arbitrary continuous scales, and builds on the dual diffusion implicit bridge [33] as its generative backbone. It departs fundamentally from the dominant 'generate-then-retrain' pipeline (SynDiff, RealDGen) that requires synthesizing paired data and retraining ASSR models for each scale, instead proposing a zero-shot inference-time proxy that rectifies inputs on the fly. The progressive orthogonal decoupling mechanism—combining a VQ-VAE codebook bottleneck for content purification with scale-equivariant contrastive learning for degradation-scale separation—is a novel architectural synthesis not present in prior degradation-modeling or INR-based ASSR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any real-world paired training data and operating as a plug-and-play module for any frozen ASSR backbone, CDR dramatically lowers the barrier to deploying arbitrary-scale super-resolution in real-world settings where collecting scale-matched physical data is prohibitively expensive. The reliance on an arbitrary unpaired HR reference image (bicubically downsampled at the target scale) is a practical constraint that may limit deployment in fully autonomous pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video super-resolution, whether the unpaired reference can be replaced by a learned prior, and how performance holds under even more extreme or adversarial degradations beyond the tested range.
