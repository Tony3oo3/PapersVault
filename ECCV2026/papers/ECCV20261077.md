## Problem

2D-centric visuomotor policies rely on perspective-dependent visual representations, so even minor SE(3) camera perturbations disrupt action-relevant spatial grounding, and re-collecting demonstrations for unseen viewpoints is prohibitively expensive. Existing novel-view-synthesis approaches either produce geometrically inconsistent 2D renderings (diffusion-based) or require dense calibrated multi-view capture with costly per-scene optimization (classical 3DGS), neither of which suits the sparse, uncalibrated conditions of real robotic datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes V sparse, uncalibrated RGB images from multiple cameras (two external + one wrist) at a single timestep, with no camera pose annotations required, and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation along with predicted per-view SE(3) camera poses and dense per-pixel 3D point maps. Novel views are rendered by rotating the camera about a world axis by θ ∈ [−30°, 30°] and translating along the camera +Z axis by μ ∈ [0.5, 6] cm, with each augmented frame paired to the original action label to form the expanded training set. The policy additionally consumes robot proprioceptive state alongside the visual observation.

## Method

GenSplat is a feed-forward 3DGS framework built on a permutation-equivariant transformer (initialized from VGGT) that processes multi-view DINOv2 patch tokens through alternating intra-frame and global self-attention, then routes to decoders predicting camera poses (SE(3)), dense 3D point maps with confidence, and Gaussian rendering attributes (opacity, quaternion, anisotropic scale, SH coefficients) via a DPT head. The key innovation is a 3D-prior distillation objective that extracts geometric supervision from a pretrained visual geometry foundation model: a point-map MSE loss on per-pixel 3D coordinates and a camera-pose loss combining geodesic distance on SO(3) with Huber loss on relative translations, both affine-invariant to avoid requiring absolute tracking. This distillation regularizes the reconstruction against the geometric collapse (floaters, boundary tearing, flat-surface distortion) that plagues pure photometric optimization, and is combined with L1+LPIPS photometric loss and a depth-alignment loss during end-to-end training. The model is pretrained on 271k images from DROID, then used to render one augmented novel view per original frame, which is concatenated with the source data to train π0 or Diffusion Policy via standard behavioral cloning.

## Results

Under large camera perturbations (30° rotation, 6 cm translation), GenSplat achieves the highest average policy success rate of 62.2% across six real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming the unaugmented baseline (48.9%) by +13.3% and the closest feed-forward baseline AnySplat (56.1%) by +6.1%. Synthesis speed is 0.14 s/frame, orders of magnitude faster than diffusion-based SEVA (50 s) and per-scene InstantSplat (85.2 s). For Diffusion Policy under large perturbations, GenSplat yields a 56.0% relative gain (27.78% → 43.33%), while for π₀ the absolute improvements range from +6.7% (small) to +13.3% (large) perturbation levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenSplat (62.2%) outperforms all five compared NVS methods on average policy success under large perturbations. Against the closest feed-forward baseline AnySplat (56.1%), GenSplat gains +6.1% absolute, attributed to 3D-prior distillation enforcing geometric smoothness. Diffusion-based SEVA (57.7%) and per-scene InstantSplat (55.0%) also trail GenSplat by 4.5% and 7.2% respectively, while being 350× and 600× slower in synthesis. Notably, VISTA (37.8%) and NoPoSplat (36.7%) perform *worse* than the unaugmented baseline (48.9%), indicating that geometrically inconsistent augmentations actively harm policy learning. In terms of speed, GenSplat (0.14 s) is comparable to AnySplat (0.16 s) and NoPoSplat (0.05 s) but vastly faster than SEVA (50 s) and InstantSplat (85.2 s), making it the only method that combines feed-forward efficiency with top-tier policy performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenSplat sits at the intersection of feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (AnySplat, NoPoSplat, InstantSplat) and robotic policy augmentation via novel view synthesis (VISTA, SEVA, RoboGSim). Architecturally, it inherits the permutation-equivariant transformer design from VGGT and initializes from the pi^3 foundation model, but departs from prior feed-forward 3DGS by introducing a 3D-prior distillation loss that regularizes reconstruction with dense point-map supervision from a pretrained geometry model. Conceptually, it positions itself against two failure modes: diffusion-based NVS that lacks metric 3D consistency, and standard feed-forward 3DGS that suffers geometric collapse under the extreme sparsity and missing poses characteristic of robotic datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete deployment bottleneck—camera pose generalization without re-collecting teleoperated demonstrations—and provides an architecture-agnostic, single-pass solution that integrates with both pi_0 and Diffusion Policy. A practically important finding is that low-fidelity NVS augmentation (VISTA, NoPoSplat) actively degrades policy success below the unaugmented baseline, cautioning against indiscriminate synthetic data expansion. The saturation at N=3 rendered views offers a clear data-efficiency guideline. Limitations include evaluation on a single 7-DoF arm across six tasks with 100 demos each, and the authors note that dynamic wrist-camera observations during active manipulation remain unexplored.
