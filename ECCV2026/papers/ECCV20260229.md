## Problem

Video-LLM-based Vision-Language Navigation agents suffer from State Drift in long-horizon scenarios: their internal representations progressively decouple from the true task state, manifesting as Progress Drift (mis-tracking which instruction sub-goal is active) and Memory Drift (degradation of history representations causing loss of visited landmarks). Existing approaches rely on next-action prediction, a result-oriented objective that enforces correct local moves but provides no explicit constraint on the agent's internal cognitive process, leaving it vulnerable to aimless wandering and failure to execute essential maneuvers.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a natural language instruction and a stream of monocular egocentric RGB frames, processed through a streaming Video-LLM backbone (StreamVLN with LLaVA-Video) that maintains a compressed history context. At each step it outputs a discrete low-level action (move_forward, turn_left, turn_right, stop). During training, two auxiliary outputs are additionally generated: a structured text token sequence describing completed versus remaining sub-goals (a verbatim prefix of the instruction), and a dense 2D spatial feature map reconstructing the SAM embedding of the most recently passed landmark. Both auxiliary heads are discarded at inference, incurring no deployment overhead.

## Method

The Dual-Anchoring Framework regularizes the Video-LLM's internal state through two complementary branches. Instruction Progress Anchoring is a co-training task: a pipeline using Qwen3-VL with visual kinematics prompting and dual-step reasoning synthesizes 3.6M progress descriptions, and the model is trained to generate a structured 'mental checklist' (completed sub-goals in verbatim instruction wording) before each action, maximizing P(y_prog, a_t | I, H_t). Memory Landmark Anchoring introduces a Landmark-Centric World Model that operates retrospectively: a Learnable Spatial Query Decoder projects the Video-LLM's output tokens into a compact latent space, then uses cross-attention with pixel-wise spatial queries to reconstruct the frozen SAM feature map of the most recently passed landmark (937K grounded samples), minimizing MSE as a 'rear-view mirror' constraint. Training follows a two-stage schedule: Stage 1 jointly optimizes navigation, progress, and world-model losses on 335K navigation trajectories plus anchoring data; Stage 2 adds 240K DAgger rollouts and 630K general vision-language samples to mitigate exposure bias and prevent catastrophic forgetting, while keeping both anchoring objectives active.

## Results

On R2R-CE (val unseen), the Dual-Anchoring Framework achieves SR 65.6%, SPL 62.1%, and NE 4.15 m using only a single RGB camera (S-RGB), outperforming the StreamVLN backbone (SR 56.9%, SPL 51.9%) by +8.7% SR and +10.2% SPL. On the more challenging RxR-CE benchmark with longer trajectories, it reaches SR 61.7% and SPL 53.3%, an 8.8% absolute SR gain over StreamVLN. The paper reports a 15.2% relative SR improvement overall and a 24.7% SPL gain specifically on long-horizon episodes (>9.81 m), confirming that the anchoring mechanism becomes increasingly critical as trajectory length grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method outperforms all 21 baselines on both R2R-CE and RxR-CE. Against the closest S-RGB competitor DualVLN [46], it gains +1.3% SR and +3.6% SPL on R2R-CE, and +0.3% SR and +1.5% SPL on RxR-CE. Over its direct backbone StreamVLN [47], the gains are substantially larger: +8.7% SR / +10.2% SPL on R2R-CE and +8.8% SR / +7.3% SPL on RxR-CE, demonstrating that the Dual-Anchoring objectives (not just the backbone) drive the improvement. Compared to the strongest multi-sensor baselines (HNR* [44] at SR 61.0 / SPL 51.0 on R2R-CE; ETPNav* [1] at SR 57.0 / SPL 49.0), the method surpasses them using only a single RGB camera, highlighting the efficiency of the anchoring approach. On RxR-CE, where trajectories are longer and state drift is most severe, the method leads all baselines by a clear margin (SR 61.7 vs. next-best 57.4 from NavFoM [52]), with the gap widening further on long-horizon episodes where SPL improves by +33.2% relative to the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on StreamVLN's streaming Video-LLM backbone for VLN-CE, but departs from the dominant next-action prediction paradigm by arguing that result-oriented supervision alone causes the agent's internal state to decouple from physical reality over long trajectories. It positions itself against two prior lines: (1) auxiliary-task approaches (CoT, hierarchical planning) that remain action-centric and instantaneous, and (2) future-centric world models (NWM, NavForesee, NavMorph) that prioritize foresight over historical grounding. The key conceptual departure is the retrospective Landmark-Centric World Model, which reconstructs past SAM features rather than predicting future states, and the structured progress annotation that forces explicit 'done vs. remaining' articulation before each action.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic framework (State Drift decomposed into Progress Drift and Memory Drift) that explains why Video-LLM navigators fail on long instructions, and offers a principled, modular fix that adds no inference-time overhead. The 24.7% SPL gain on long-horizon episodes and successful zero-fine-tuning deployment to a Unitree Go2 quadruped suggest the approach has practical relevance for real-world service robots. Open questions include whether the retrospective landmark mechanism scales to multi-landmark or multi-room tracking, whether the SAM-based supervision can be replaced by lighter-weight features for edge deployment, and whether the 3.6M-sample progress annotation pipeline generalizes to non-English or highly compositional instructions.
