## Problem

Visual Place Recognition has been predominantly formulated as a single-image retrieval task, and while multi-view inputs offer broader scene coverage, existing multi-view aggregation methods remain underexplored and lack geometric awareness. No prior VPR architecture effectively integrates 3D geometry-grounded tokens from multiple views to produce descriptors that generalize across diverse environments with varying illumination, weather, and viewpoint conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260995/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or more RGB images (no camera intrinsics or extrinsics required) and produces a single unified global descriptor of dimension 17152, formed by concatenating five sub-descriptors: 2D CLS (256-d), 2D register (256-d), 2D patch (8192-d, 64 clusters), 3D register (256-d), and 3D patch (8192-d, 64 clusters). For sequence-level matching, the model processes a variable-length input sequence with an anchor frame and multiple support frames, aggregating tokens across frames to yield the same descriptor format, enabling retrieval via nearest-neighbor search at arbitrary sequence lengths.

## Method

UniPR-3D builds on a VGGT backbone (24 alternating frame/global attention layers) initialized with pre-trained weights and fine-tuned via LoRA, preceded by a DINOv2 encoder that extracts 2D CLS, register, and patch tokens. The VGGT alternating attention blocks then produce 3D camera, register, and patch tokens; the camera token is discarded to ensure viewpoint invariance. Register and CLS tokens (both 2D and 3D) are aggregated via GeM pooling sandwiched between lightweight MLPs, while patch tokens are aggregated using an Optimal Transport scheme with a Sinkhorn algorithm and a learnable dustbin entry to discard uninformative features. For sequence-level matching, a multi-frame projector applies GeM pooling across frames for register/CLS tokens and extends the OT assignment to jointly cluster patch tokens from all frames, enabling variable-length sequence processing without architectural bias. Training uses multi-similarity loss with AdamW on GSV-Cities (single-frame) and MSLS (sequence), in two stages: first the descriptor head, then joint fine-tuning of VGGT attention blocks and the DINOv2 encoder.

## Results

On single-frame VPR, UniPR-3D* achieves R@1 of 92.9 on MSLS val, 96.6 on Pittsburgh250k, 78.9 on Nordland, 96.3 on SF-XL v1, and 97.9 on Tokyo 24/7, setting new state-of-the-art across all benchmarks. On sequence matching, UniPR-3D reaches R@1 of 95.4 on Oxford1 (2 m threshold) and 80.6 on Oxford2 (2 m), surpassing the previous best (CaseVPR) by over 10 percentage points on Oxford1. The method also generalizes to variable-length sequences at test time, improving from R@1=70.3 (3 frames) to R@1=92.4 (15 frames) on Oxford2 despite being trained only on 5-frame sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPR-3D* outperforms the strongest single-frame baseline MegaLoc [7] on every dataset, with the largest margins on Nordland (+2.2 R@1) and SF-XL v1 (+1.0 R@1), while maintaining consistent gains of 1–2 points on MSLS val and Pittsburgh250k. Against SALAD [39], the gap widens substantially on challenging datasets: +5.4 R@1 on SF-XL v1 and +4.5 R@1 on SF-XL night, confirming that 3D geometric tokens provide robustness beyond texture-only descriptors. BoQ [3] is competitive on Tokyo 24/7 (97.5 vs. 97.9 R@1) but trails by 5+ points on SF-XL v1. For sequence matching, UniPR-3D surpasses CaseVPR [43] by 4.9 R@1 on Oxford1 (2 m) and 7.8 R@1 on Oxford2 (2 m), and by 2.5 R@1 on MSLS val, while also outperforming SeqVLAD [60] and SeqMatchNet [34] by 10–30+ points across all sequence benchmarks. The method's advantage is most pronounced under extreme conditions (seasonal change on Nordland, day-night on SF-XL), where geometry-aware 3D tokens provide stable structural cues that 2D-only baselines cannot capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPR-3D extends the VPR lineage from single-view 2D feature extraction (NetVLAD, MixVPR, AnyLoc, SALAD, MegaLoc) to a multi-view, geometry-grounded formulation by repurposing the VGGT backbone's 3D tokens as the primary descriptor signal. Its aggregation design directly inherits from SALAD's optimal-transport/Sinkhorn scheme and SuperGlue's dustbin mechanism, but applies them separately to 2D and 3D token streams with tailored strategies (GeM for register/CLS tokens, OT for patch tokens). Unlike prior sequence methods (SeqVLAD, CaseVPR, SeqMatchNet) that aggregate single-view features along the temporal axis with fixed-length inductive biases, UniPR-3D leverages VGGT's spatially-registered multi-view tokens to support variable-length sequences without architectural constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that 3D geometry-grounded tokens from a foundation model carry meaningful discriminative signal for place recognition beyond what 2D texture features provide, opening a new axis of improvement as 3D foundation models (VGGT, Dens3R, MORE) mature. The variable-length sequence generalization and the finding that explicit pose injection is redundant (3D tokens already encode spatial relationships) are practically useful insights. Limitations include higher inference latency (~140 ms vs. 75 ms for CaseVPR), dependence on the VGGT backbone's quality, and evaluation restricted to outdoor/urban driving scenes; whether the 3D-token advantage persists in indoor, highly repetitive, or texture-poor environments remains open.
