## Problem

Circuit schematics pose a unique challenge for MLLMs due to their dense component layouts and intricate topological dependencies that demand fine-grained structural parsing. Existing approaches—whether tool-augmented pipelines, text-conversion methods, or visual chain-of-thought with bounding boxes—either propagate errors through intermediate stages or fragment thin, long-range wire connections. Latent-space reasoning offers a promising alternative but currently suffers from coarse-grained feature extraction and a default raster-scan ordering that misaligns with the circuit's intrinsic topological logic.

## Input / Output

The model takes a circuit schematic image and a natural-language query as input, and produces a textual answer (e.g., connection judgment, component count, topology identification) interleaved with k=4 autoregressively generated latent tokens that encode topological reasoning steps. The latent tokens are positioned between a preceding text segment (o_pre) and a succeeding text segment (o_post), so the model generates text → latent vectors → text in a single forward pass. The latent vectors are constrained to align with expert-enhanced circuit features ordered by BFS topological distance from a root component, rather than spatial raster order.

## Method

Circuit-MLLM builds on Qwen2.5-VL 7B and introduces two core mechanisms within the latent space. First, a Latent-Space Circuit Knowledge Mining module deploys three heterogeneous vision experts (HAWP for wireframe/junction detection, DeepLSD for line-segment extraction, DINOv2 for patch-level semantics), aligns their feature maps to a common grid, and fuses them with the MLLM's original visual tokens via a learnable projection and fusion layer to produce knowledge-enriched target vectors. Second, a Topology-Guided Sequencing Strategy constructs a pixel-level Topological Logic Mask using BFS distance from a root component, sorts valid feature patches by this mask value to enforce a root→wire→target ordering, and compresses the ordered sequence into k fixed-length latent targets via 1D adaptive average pooling. During training, the model's hidden states at latent-token positions are supervised by a cosine-distance alignment loss (L_topo) against these ordered targets, while surrounding text tokens are optimized with standard cross-entropy (L_text), yielding a joint objective L_total = λ·L_topo + L_text that anchors latent reasoning in topologically structured expert visual space.

## Results

On Circuit-MLLM-Bench, Circuit-MLLM (7B) achieves an average score of 76.20, surpassing GPT-5.1 (61.35) by 24.3% and its backbone Qwen2.5-VL-7B (48.28) by 57.8%. On the most challenging Connection Identification task, Circuit-MLLM reaches 57.30 EMR, roughly three times the backbone's 15.20 and 19.1 points above GPT-5.1 (30.20). On topological analysis sub-tasks (Table 2), Circuit-MLLM attains 72.26 average, outperforming the strongest latent-space baseline Mirage (69.82) by 2.44 points and ILVR (68.37) by 3.89 points, with a 25% EMR gain over ILVR on Hard-difficulty Connection Identification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Masala-CHAI (tool-augmented circuit MLLM), Circuit-MLLM wins decisively by 25.3 points on average (76.20 vs. 50.88) and by 27.1 points on topological tasks (72.26 vs. 45.18), indicating that external image-modification tools provide only marginal benefit for topology analysis. Against Mirage, the strongest latent-space baseline, Circuit-MLLM achieves a 2.44-point average gain on topological tasks (72.26 vs. 69.82), with the largest margin on Medium-difficulty Connection Judgment (70.31 vs. 66.15 Acc.) and Hard Connection Identification EMR (28.52 vs. 26.80). Against ILVR, Circuit-MLLM gains 3.89 points on average (72.26 vs. 68.37), with a particularly large 25% EMR improvement on Hard Connection Identification (28.52 vs. 22.68). Notably, even the SFT+GRPO baseline (67.74) without any latent mechanism is outperformed by Circuit-MLLM by 4.5 points, confirming that the topology-guided latent sequencing and expert knowledge mining jointly contribute beyond what reinforcement learning alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent-space visual reasoning paradigm (LVR, Mirage, ILVR) by introducing two circuit-specific mechanisms: a multi-expert knowledge mining module (DINOv2, HAWP, DeepLSD) that injects fine-grained structural priors into latent tokens during training, and a BFS-based topological sequencing strategy that replaces the default raster-scan order with a root-to-target wire-following order. It departs from tool-based circuit MLLMs (Masala-CHAI, MAPs) by eliminating external detectors and OCR pipelines entirely, and from Visual Chain-of-Thought methods by avoiding bounding-box representations that fragment thin wire connections. The visual attribution analysis (Section 2.3) provides empirical motivation for the topology-guided sequencing, showing that MLLMs already exhibit a root→wire→target attention pattern that prior latent-space methods fail to exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a 7B model can substantially outperform frontier closed-source models on circuit topology analysis, suggesting that domain-specific latent-space structuring can compensate for scale in specialized EDA tasks. However, the evaluation relies entirely on a self-constructed benchmark (Circuit-MLLM-Bench) with no external validation, and the method is tested only at 7B scale, leaving open whether the topology-guided sequencing generalizes to larger models or to other structured-visual domains (e.g., PCB layouts, flow diagrams). The finding that tool-augmented approaches (Masala-CHAI) yield negligible gains over the raw backbone on topological tasks is a useful negative result for the EDA-MLLM community, but the narrow domain scope limits broader methodological impact.
