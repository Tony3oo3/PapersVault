## Problem

Existing 3D neuron segmentation methods for fluorescence microscopy rely on sliding-window inference with limited receptive fields, producing fragmented results that violate the elongated, sparse topology of neurons. Single-pass models operate as black boxes with no mechanism to detect or correct topological errors, and prior agent-based segmentation tools (e.g., MedSAM2) process 3D volumes slice-by-slice with 2D encoders, which is inadequate for the structural sparsity and ambiguous boundaries of neurons.

## Input / Output

The system takes a 3D fluorescence microscopy volume and an initial segmentation mask (from any off-the-shelf model) as input. The volume is projected via Maximum Intensity Projection along the z-axis to produce 2D xy and yz views, then partitioned into 128×128-pixel sub-blocks for agent inspection. The output is a refined 3D segmentation mask, produced iteratively over up to 5 rounds, where each round applies voxel-level edits only to sub-regions flagged as topologically anomalous.

## Method

NeuroRefiner formalizes the neuroscientist's global-observation-then-local-editing workflow as a three-agent pipeline: a Global Inspector computes 0th-order Betti numbers per sub-block to flag topological violations, a Refinement Advisor generates natural-language editing instructions (spatial location + operation type) from orthogonal xy/yz projections, and a Change Validator accepts or rejects each edit based on instruction adherence and topological improvement. The execution tool, TopoRefineNet, is a 3D U-Net that concatenates the image and noisy mask, encodes them hierarchically, and fuses with a frozen text embedding via cross-attention at the bottleneck to produce instruction-guided voxel edits. TopoRefineNet is trained with a two-stage curriculum: first on synthetic morphological defects (erosion for false negatives, copy-paste/noise for false positives) to learn cross-modal alignment, then on 5,310 real segmentation errors from five architectures to generalize to diverse instruction patterns. All agents use Qwen3-VL-8B as the foundation model, and the entire loop is training-free at inference time, requiring no fine-tuning of the VLM.

## Results

On the ZBFWB dataset (the most challenging benchmark with low-contrast zebrafish larvae), NeuroRefiner achieves an F1 score of 86.13%, surpassing the best single-pass method GBP-Net (83.11%) by 3.02% and improving the initial 3D U-Net segmentation (72.94%) by 13.19%. On BigNeuron, it reaches 90.05% F1 and 72.02% MES, outperforming GBP-Net (89.03% F1, 70.77% MES) and reducing SSD from 8.41 to 6.94. On CWMBS, the method improves F1 by over 10% on the weak-signal subset (90.07% on 3D U-Net init, 91.85% on nnUNet init) and by ~2.6% on the strong-noise subset (86.37%) relative to the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroRefiner outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and metric. On ZBFWB, it beats the strongest single-pass method GBP-Net by 3.02% F1 (86.13 vs. 83.11) and 4.78 in SSD (23.66 vs. 28.44), while improving the 3D U-Net initialization by 13.19% F1. Among refinement baselines, SegRefiner is the closest competitor (74.99% F1 on ZBFWB, 88.19% on BigNeuron), but NeuroRefiner still leads by 11.14% and 1.86% F1 respectively, indicating that topology-aware multi-agent reasoning provides substantially larger gains than generic diffusion-based refinement. On CWMBS (Table 1), the method achieves 90.07% F1 on the weak-signal subset (vs. 88.91% for GBP-Net) and 86.37% on the strong-noise subset (vs. 83.77% for GBP-Net), with the largest absolute gains over 3D U-Net-based refinement baselines (Transfiner: 74.95%, SegFix: 76.32%). The advantage is most pronounced on ZBFWB, where low contrast and long-range axonal projections cause CNN-based methods to fragment severely (F1 as low as 64–68%), while the iterative topology-guided correction recovers connectivity that no single-pass or single-refinement approach can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—sliding-window neuron segmentation (GBP-Net, SGSNet, ADTL-Net), LLM-driven agent-based biomedical segmentation (Ophiuchus, IBISAgent, GenCellAgent), and iterative segmentation refinement (SegRefiner, Mask Transfiner)—into a unified topology-aware pipeline. It departs from existing agent-based segmentation, which relies on general-purpose 2D tools like SAM2/MedSAM2, by introducing TopoRefineNet, a dedicated 3D U-Net-based editor conditioned on natural-language instructions via cross-attention. It also departs from prior refinement methods that target boundary regularization by incorporating Betti-number-based topological diagnostics and a multi-agent validation loop, formalizing the neuroscientist's global-observation/local-editing workflow as an explicit algorithmic protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that iterative, topology-guided refinement can substantially close the gap between single-pass segmentation and expert-level reconstruction, particularly on challenging low-contrast datasets like ZBFWB where fragmentation is severe. The finding that the framework's gains are largely independent of the specific VLM (weaker models still yield >4% F1 improvement) suggests the multi-agent structure and domain-specific tool design matter more than raw model capability, a useful principle for deploying LLM agents in resource-constrained biomedical settings. A key limitation is the reliance on 2D MIP projections, which lose depth information in dense neuronal regions; the authors identify depth-aware encoding as a natural next step. Broader open questions include whether this agent-tool paradigm generalizes to EM neuron reconstruction or other sparse tubular structures, and whether the iterative loop's compute cost is acceptable for large-scale whole-brain datasets.
