## Problem

Situated reasoning typically depends on active physical exploration, which is infeasible for robots with mechanical constraints (e.g., inability to climb stairs) or visually impaired users who hesitate to explore in unsafe conditions. While instrumental simulation (task-oriented prediction) has been explored with world models, emulative simulation—mentally walking through a scene to answer spatial 'what-if' questions—remains underexplored, with no existing dataset providing temporally consistent panoramic video toward target situations paired with reasoning annotations along the imagined path.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single current egocentric equirectangular panorama (1024×2048), a textual target-situation description (e.g., 'stand by the lit round mirror one meter behind'), and a what-if question as input, and produces a 21-frame imagined panoramic video trajectory (4N+1 convention) plus a text answer. For reasoning, the generated video is uniformly sampled every 5 frames (s_Δ5) to feed the MLLM, reducing pipeline-specific frame-sampling biases. The dataset additionally releases depth maps, semantic maps, and camera poses alongside RGB panoramas, and the QA set spans 10 question types distributed across start state (3 questions), path (4 questions), and end state (3 questions) per trajectory.

## Method

The paper introduces WanderDream, a 15.8K-video / 158K-QA dataset built from HM3D (robotic object navigation via Habitat-Sim shortest-path planner) and ScanNet++ (human situated paths via 3D Probabilistic Roadmap + Dijkstra with capsule-validated edges and micro-PRM repair), and evaluates it through a sequential framework that chains a diffusion-based world model (HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX, or Wan) with an MLLM (Qwen3-VL-32B or LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B). The world model is conditioned either via MLLM-driven prompt extension that decomposes the target trajectory into discrete camera actions (MOVE_FORWARD, TURN_LEFT, TURN_RIGHT) or via LoRA/SFT fine-tuning on WanderDream-Gen; the MLLM then answers situated questions over the sampled generated frames. QA pairs are generated by GPT-5 using Set-of-Mark instance-ID overlays on cubemaps plus trajectory metadata JSON, then human-verified. The key empirical finding is that higher video-generation quality (measured by FVD, End-FID, S-SSIM, LPIPS) correlates with stronger downstream reasoning accuracy, and that intermediate imagined frames (s_Δ5) outperform or match direct start+end frame input for end-state questions, confirming that the imagined path itself carries reasoning-relevant information.

## Results

On WanderDream-Gen, Wan2.1 fine-tuned with LoRA achieves the best video generation quality, with FVD of 7.90 (ScanNet++) and 5.96 (HM3D), and End-FID of 70.56 and 40.20 respectively. On WanderDream-QA (ScanNet++), CogVideoX1.5 with SFT yields the highest end-state reasoning score (47.5) and path reasoning score (37.6), surpassing the no-imagination baseline (45.5 and 36.4). On the real-world sim-to-real test set, the sequential framework with Wan2.1+LoRA achieves 43.0 QA accuracy, outperforming both the no-imagination MLLM (38.5) and MindJourney (31.9), while also producing the best video generation (FVD 27.49 vs. 41.65 for prompt extension).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only external baseline is MindJourney [108], a closed-loop step-wise simulation framework that generates novel views per question using the SVC novel-view synthesis model. On the real-world test set, the paper's sequential framework with Wan2.1+LoRA achieves 43.0 QA accuracy, outperforming MindJourney (31.9) by 11.1 points and the no-imagination MLLM baseline (38.5) by 4.5 points. MindJourney actually performs worse than simply feeding the start panorama to the MLLM, likely because its SVC-based novel-view synthesis fails in occluded or corner regions common in real panoramic captures. Among the world-model configurations evaluated internally, CogVideoX1.5+SFT leads on ScanNet++ end-state reasoning (47.5 vs. 45.5 baseline), while Wan2.1+LoRA leads on HM3D path reasoning (53.4 vs. 53.2 baseline), confirming that higher video generation quality (lower FVD/End-FID) correlates with stronger downstream reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task—emulative simulation—by drawing on the cognitive-science distinction between instrumental simulation (task-oriented, well-served by navigation world models like Navigation World Models and PathDreamer) and emulative simulation (experience-oriented, placing oneself in the agent's shoes to explore and reason along an imagined path). Prior situated-reasoning datasets (SQA3D, MSQA, OpenEQA, VSI-Bench) either require pre-explored scenes or active exploration, and the closest prior work, MindJourney, performs step-wise view synthesis per question without generating a coherent trajectory toward a target situation. WanderDream departs from all of these by providing temporally consistent panoramic video trajectories from a current viewpoint to a described target situation, paired with 10 types of QA distributed along the path, enabling joint evaluation of world-model imagination and MLLM reasoning in a single pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WanderDream is the first large-scale benchmark (15.8K panoramic videos, 158K QA pairs) for emulative simulation, filling a clear gap between instrumental world models and situated reasoning that previously required physical exploration. The demonstrated correlation between world-model video quality and downstream QA accuracy provides a concrete evaluation axis for future world models, and the sim-to-real transferability (despite occlusions and non-shortest-path real trajectories) suggests the dataset could serve as a training signal for real-world assistive agents. Key open questions include whether unified end-to-end video-and-text models can replace the current sequential pipeline, whether the modest QA gains (≈2–4 points over start-frame-only baselines) will grow with better world models, and whether the small 182-QA real-world test set can be scaled to validate broader generality.
