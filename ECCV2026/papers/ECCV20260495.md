## Problem

Normalizing Flows are constrained by strict invertibility, forcing them to expend model capacity on low-level pixel details at the expense of high-level semantic structure. While Masked Image Modeling has proven effective for representation learning, its integration into generative pipelines has remained modular—typically pre-training a tokenizer separately before training a generative model—leaving the generative flow unexposed to the semantic benefits of masking. This paper asks whether MIM can be integrated directly into end-to-end NF training to resolve the NF capacity bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260495/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a class label as conditioning and produces a 256×256 RGB image via a single ODE trajectory through the Normalizing Flow, requiring no iterative denoising. During training, the encoder consumes a randomly masked version of the input image (40–60% of 256 patches masked) rather than the full image, and the latent space is compressed to 128 tokens—half the 256-token grid used by standard latent models. The NF additionally provides exact log-likelihood computation for any latent sample, enabling principled density estimation alongside sampling.

## Method

MIMFlow comprises three jointly optimized modules: a masked Transformer encoder (MAE-Tok backbone) that extracts K=128 learnable query tokens from masked images, a STARFlow-based Normalizing Flow that models the latent distribution as a VAE prior, and a pixel decoder that reconstructs images from the latent. The key mechanism is the learnable token bottleneck: because the encoder only sees a partially observed image and must compress it into fewer tokens than patches, the latent space is forced to encode global structural semantics rather than local pixel noise, giving the NF a simpler, low-frequency manifold to model. Training proceeds in two stages—90 epochs of end-to-end joint optimization (reconstruction + NF NLL + auxiliary DINO/CLIP feature prediction) followed by 2 epochs of adversarial decoder fine-tuning with a patch-based discriminator—while the encoder continues to receive masked inputs throughout to preserve distributional consistency between training and sampling.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditioned generation, MIMFlow-L achieves a gFID of 2.50 (with CFG=2.0) and 3.64 (without guidance), with IS 233.5, Precision 0.82, and Recall 0.57. The model uses only 128 latent tokens (50% fewer than the standard 256-token grid) and 482M parameters. Linear probing on the learned latent space reaches 71.3% top-1 accuracy, substantially above the 56.6% of the unmasked baseline. Hardware efficiency improves over SimFlow-L: training memory drops from 52.3 GB to 37.6 GB, throughput rises from 2.83 to 3.11 it/s, and per-image sampling time halves from 0.020 s to 0.011 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Within the Normalizing Flow paradigm, MIMFlow-L is the clear winner: it reduces gFID from 3.72 (SimFlow-L, 475M params) to 2.50, a 32.8% improvement, and outperforms the much larger FAE-NF-XXL (1.4B params, gFID 2.67) and FlowBack-XL (831M params, gFID 4.18) while using roughly 3× fewer parameters than the former. It also narrows the gap to STARFlow-XXL (1.4B params, gFID 2.40) to just 0.10 FID. However, MIMFlow-L trails the strongest latent diffusion and autoregressive models: REPA-E (gFID 1.12), RAE (1.13), DDT (1.26), MAR (1.55), and VAR (1.73) all achieve lower FIDs, though several of these use 675M–2.0B parameters and 256–680 tokens. Notably, MIMFlow-L's no-guidance gFID of 3.64 is far better than SimFlow-XXL's 10.13, indicating the MIM-trained latent space is inherently more structured and requires less external guidance. The paper positions MIMFlow as the best NF-based generator at the ~500M parameter scale, demonstrating that masked semantic decoupling lets a flow model extract more generative value per parameter than prior NF designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIMFlow extends the SimFlow line of end-to-end latent normalizing flow training by inserting a masked-image-modeling bottleneck between the encoder and the flow, replacing the standard full-image VAE encoder. Architecturally, it adopts the STARFlow NF backbone and the MAE-Tok Transformer tokenizer, but departs from prior MIM-tokenizer work (MAETok, DeTok, VTP) that treats representation learning as a frozen, modular pre-training stage. The key conceptual departure is making the mask an active, end-to-end training signal that shapes the latent manifold the NF must model, rather than a one-off tokenizer objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a principled decoupling of semantic modeling (NF) from texture synthesis (decoder) can resolve the long-standing capacity bottleneck of normalizing flows, yielding a 32.8% FID improvement over a same-scale NF baseline while using 50% fewer latent tokens and roughly half the sampling time. This suggests a general recipe—use information-removing pre-processing to simplify the distribution a flow must model—that could transfer to other flow-based or likelihood-based generative settings. Open questions include whether the masked-bottleneck benefit extends to text-to-image generation, to diffusion or autoregressive backbones, and to higher resolutions; the authors explicitly scope their claims to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256.
