## Problem

Existing proactive defenses against diffusion-based facial image manipulation rely on white-box access to specific internal modules (e.g., VAE encoder or denoising U-Net) of a known model, making them brittle under cross-architecture transfer and across diverse manipulation paradigms such as inpainting, editing, mimicry, and identity-preserving generation. No prior method achieves universal, zero-knowledge protection that generalizes to unseen generative architectures and manipulation types simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single clean RGB facial image and produces an imperceptibly perturbed version x' = x + δ constrained to an L∞ ball of radius 8/255. The perturbation is crafted offline via 100 PGD iterations using an ensemble of surrogate extractors (VAE encoders from SD v1.5 and SDXL, a ReferenceNet, CLIP, and ArcFace), so no access to the target manipulation model is required. The output is a single protected image that simultaneously resists all four manipulation paradigms without task-specific re-optimization.

## Method

FaceArmor decomposes the defense into two architecture-invariant representation tiers: a structural tier that maximizes latent-space distance across an ensemble of VAE encoders and perturbs fine-grained ReferenceNet features, and a semantic tier that decouples CLIP text-image alignment (both untargeted and targeted) and obscures ArcFace biometric identity. To resolve the spatial gradient conflicts inherent in jointly optimizing these heterogeneous objectives (e.g., identity gradients localize to the face while texture gradients target background), the method extracts intrinsic attention heatmaps from the surrogate models as adaptive soft spatial weights, applies a Hadamard product to each gradient, and then integrates PCGrad projection to eliminate residual conflicts in overlapping transition regions. Expectation over Transformation (EOT) with Gaussian blur, JPEG compression, noise, and resizing is incorporated during optimization to ensure robustness to real-world image processing, and the final perturbation is updated via sign-based PGD with L∞ projection.

## Results

On SD 1.5, FaceArmor achieves the best score on all five metrics (FID, LPIPS, SSIM, CLIP, FR) across all four manipulation scenarios. For example, on Image Editing it reaches FID=163.43, LPIPS=0.5727, SSIM=0.3121, CLIP=0.6247, FR=0.3425, outperforming the next-best method (FaceLock) by 30.6 FID points and 0.072 LPIPS. On SD XL, FaceArmor similarly leads every metric in every scenario, with a particularly large margin on Image Inpainting (FID=129.30 vs. 127.30 for AdvDM, the closest competitor). In zero-knowledge transfer to DiT-based models (SD 3, Flux), FaceArmor again achieves the best on all five metrics, e.g., FID=156.44 and CLIP=0.7826 on SD 3, where the best baseline (AdvDM) only reaches FID=143.88 and CLIP=0.8480.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaceArmor is the only method that achieves the best score on all five metrics (FID, LPIPS, SSIM, CLIP, FR) across all four manipulation scenarios on both SD 1.5 and SD XL. PhotoGuard and EditShield are narrowly scoped: PhotoGuard only protects against Image Inpainting, while EditShield only covers Image Editing, both failing completely on the other two paradigms. AdvDM and SDST show moderate breadth (effective on Inpainting and Editing) but collapse on Face Mimicry and Identity-Preserving Generation. FaceLock performs well on Inpainting and Editing (e.g., FR=0.3002 on Editing, second only to FaceArmor's 0.3425) but is ineffective on Mimicry and ID-Preserving. IDProtector is the strongest baseline on Identity-Preserving Generation (FR=0.6006 vs. FaceArmor's 0.5528) yet fails on Editing and Mimicry. In zero-knowledge transfer to DiT-based models (SD 3, Flux), all baselines degrade substantially (e.g., EditShield's CLIP rises from 0.8879 to 0.9309 on Flux), while FaceArmor maintains the best CLIP score (0.7332 on Flux), confirming its architecture-agnostic advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in adversarial image protection, where prior methods (PhotoGuard, EditShield, AdvDM, SDST, FaceLock, IDProtector) each target a specific internal module of a known diffusion model (VAE encoder, denoising U-Net, attention maps, or identity embeddings) under white-box assumptions. FaceArmor instead repurposes off-the-shelf feature extractors (VAE ensembles, ReferenceNet, CLIP, ArcFace) as surrogate objectives, shifting the attack surface from model-internal components to an architecture-invariant representation space. The multi-objective gradient conflict resolution via attention-based soft weighting combined with PCGrad borrows from multi-task learning literature but applies it in a novel spatially-structured adversarial setting. The EOT robustness training and PGD optimization follow standard adversarial attack practice, but the two-tier (structural/semantic) decomposition of the feature space is the paper's core conceptual contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the zero-knowledge, multi-paradigm coverage: a single perturbation crafted against surrogate extractors transfers to four distinct manipulation tasks and four architectures (including DiT-based SD3 and Flux) where all component-specific baselines degrade substantially. This addresses a real deployment concern—users cannot know which tool an attacker will use. Limitations include the ~90-second per-image optimization cost (vs. 28–74 s for baselines), restriction to facial images, and the fact that the 'universal' guarantee is bounded to diffusion-based pipelines; adaptive attacks that jointly optimize the perturbation and the manipulation prompt remain untested. Open questions include whether the two-tier feature decomposition generalizes to non-diffusion generative models (e.g., GANs, autoregressive) and whether the attention-weighting scheme can be distilled into a faster single-pass inference.
