## Problem

Existing representation alignment (REPA) methods for video generation are confined to single-modality RGB alignment with one foundation model, leaving multi-modal world models (e.g., joint RGB-depth-mask generation) unexplored. Naively extending REPA to multiple foundation models simultaneously triggers feature conflicts that actually degrade generation quality, revealing that shared diffusion representations cannot be forced to match heterogeneous modality-specific priors without explicit disentanglement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261938/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an initial tri-modal frame (RGB image, depth map, segmentation mask) plus a sequence of structured control signals (camera poses for RealEstate10K or actions for Minecraft) and autoregressively generates temporally coherent tri-modal video sequences. For RealEstate10K, generation operates in pixel space at 256×256 resolution with 8-frame training clips; for Minecraft, it uses a latent-diffusion paradigm with 50-frame VAE-encoded sequences. Outputs are pixel-aligned across all three modalities, evaluated over both short-range (8-frame) and long-range (200-frame) horizons.

## Method

M²-REPA introduces modality-specific decoupling layers (lightweight MLPs) that project shared diffusion intermediate features into K distinct modality-specific subspaces, each aligned to its corresponding frozen foundation model expert (DINOv2 for RGB, DepthAnythingV2 for depth, SAM2 for mask) via token-wise cosine similarity. A second regularization term minimizes pairwise Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) similarity among the decoupled features, explicitly penalizing redundant information sharing and encouraging each subspace to capture complementary semantic structures. The total training objective combines the standard flow-matching loss with the multi-modal alignment loss and the CKA-based decoupling loss, with only the diffusion backbone and MLP projectors updated while all foundation models remain frozen. This divide-and-conquer strategy resolves the feature conflicts observed in naive multi-expert alignment, enabling joint optimization that exploits complementary priors across modalities without inference-time overhead.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (8-frame), M²-REPA achieves FVD 81.13, LPIPS 0.2123, SSIM 0.7439, PSNR 20.48, AbsRel 0.8224, δ₁ 0.4449, and mIoU 0.9045, outperforming all baselines across all three modalities. For long-term 200-frame generation, M²-REPA reaches FVD 262.50, δ₁ 0.1408, and mIoU 0.6591, again leading every competitor. On Minecraft (150-frame, DiT backbone), M²-REPA achieves FVD 81.81 vs. 129.50–144.75 for single/naive-multi REPA variants, confirming backbone-agnostic gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M²-REPA decisively outperforms DFoT [38] (FVD 110.88→81.13 at 8 frames; 401.50→262.50 at 200 frames) and Geometry Forcing [46] (FVD 106.00→81.13; 371.50→262.50), which are RGB-only methods and thus lack depth/mask metrics. Against the tri-modal RGB-DM baseline, M²-REPA improves FVD by ~21% (102.44→81.13) at 8 frames and ~36% (407.50→262.50) at 200 frames, while also boosting depth δ₁ from 0.1724 to 0.4449 and mask mIoU from 0.8839 to 0.9045. Single-modality REPA variants (DINOv2, SAM2, DepthAnythingV2) each excel in their own modality but degrade others; M²-REPA simultaneously improves all three modalities, e.g., achieving the best FVD (81.13), best δ₁ (0.4449), and best mIoU (0.9045) at 8 frames. The naive multi-expert combination (REPA D+S+DA) actually underperforms individual REPA variants on long-video FVD (328.75 vs. 301.25 for SAM2 alone), confirming the feature-conflict problem that M²-REPA's decoupling resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the REPA (Representation Alignment) line of work, which aligns early-stage diffusion features with frozen foundation-model features to improve generation quality, from single-modality RGB generation with one expert (e.g., DINOv2) to multi-modal (RGB-Depth-Mask) generation with multiple modality-specific experts. It builds on autoregressive video diffusion frameworks (Diffusion Forcing, DFoT) and multi-modal world models (TesserAct, Aether, WorldWeaver), but departs from prior REPA methods by introducing modality-specific decoupling layers and a CKA-based complementarity regularizer to resolve the feature conflicts that arise when naively aligning shared diffusion features to heterogeneous foundation models simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that naively combining multiple foundation-model experts in REPA degrades performance due to feature entanglement, and offers a clean, plug-and-play fix (decoupling MLPs + CKA regularization) that is backbone-agnostic (validated on both U-Net/UViT and DiT). This opens a path for multi-expert knowledge distillation in multi-modal world models without inference-time overhead. Limitations include dependence on the quality and coverage of the chosen foundation experts, additional training compute, and evaluation on only two datasets; open questions include scalability to more than three modalities, integration with latent-space VAEs at scale, and whether the decoupling strategy generalizes to non-visual modalities.
