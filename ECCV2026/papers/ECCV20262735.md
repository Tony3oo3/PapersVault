## Problem

Existing KB-VQA benchmarks (InfoSeek, E-VQA) systematically violate three assumptions that answer accuracy implicitly relies on: annotated answers must be derivable from the provided knowledge base, questions must be well-posed with sufficient constraints, and visual scenes must require grounded disambiguation. The paper shows these violations are widespread (e.g., 22% of InfoSeek answers are unsupported by the KB, ~50% of questions are underspecified, and images are dominated by single entities), causing distorted model rankings and overestimated reasoning capabilities.

## Input / Output

The evaluated KB-VQA systems consume an RGB image, a natural-language question, and a controlled external knowledge base (2M Wikipedia articles for E-VQA; 100K articles for InfoSeek) to produce a free-form text answer. Initial retrieval is performed via frozen EVA-CLIP-8B image-to-image cosine similarity over the KB, followed by method-specific evidence selection or aggregation. The augmentation protocol modifies the input image by spatially concatenating a single distractor entity (same or different semantic category) alongside the anchor, while the question and annotated answer remain unchanged by construction.

## Method

The paper contributes two protocols applied to existing benchmarks. First, a four-stage audit-and-repair pipeline: (1) dual-LLM evidence verification (Qwen3-30B-A3B and DeepSeek-v3.2) to flag unsupported or contradicted answers, (2) evidence-grounded answer correction or removal, (3) minimal question edits to restore missing attribute, temporal, or spatial constraints, and (4) a leakage and consistency validation pass. Second, a controlled multi-entity augmentation that inserts one distractor entity (intra-category or inter-category) into the image while preserving the anchor answer, thereby forcing models to perform grounded entity disambiguation rather than relying on coarse global image similarity. Re-evaluation of five representative baselines (IBA, EchoSight, ReflectiVA, CoMeM, Wiki-PRF) on original, fixed, and augmented splits reveals that fixing alone reverses method rankings (e.g., IBA overtakes ReflectiVA on InfoSeek) and that augmentation collapses initial retrieval recall (InfoSeek R@1: 43.5→14.7/20.4) and end-to-end accuracy, exposing grounded disambiguation as the critical bottleneck.

## Results

After applying the audit-and-repair protocol, most KB-VQA methods improve on InfoSeek (e.g., IBA 34.5→42.4, EchoSight 30.9→37.2, Wiki-PRF 44.9→43.6), with the largest gains on Time and Numerical subsets. Critically, method rankings reverse: on unfixed InfoSeek, Wiki-PRF leads IBA by 10.4 points, but after fixing the gap shrinks to 1.2, and IBA overtakes ReflectiVA (42.4 vs. 38.1). Under multi-entity augmentation, all methods suffer severe drops in both retrieval recall (InfoSeek R@1: 43.5→14.7/20.4 for Intra/Inter) and end-to-end QA accuracy (e.g., IBA 40.1→21.4/21.6, EchoSight 38.7→15.9/17.8 on InfoSeek), confirming that single-entity shortcuts inflate scores in current benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new KB-VQA method but audits and repairs existing benchmarks, so the 'comparison' is how each baseline's measured performance shifts when dataset validity is restored. On unfixed InfoSeek, Wiki-PRF (44.9) and ReflectiVA (37.3) appear to dominate IBA (34.5) and EchoSight (30.9), but after fixing, IBA becomes the top method (42.4) and the Wiki-PRF–IBA gap collapses from 10.4 to 1.2 points. ReflectiVA's apparent advantage over IBA reverses entirely (37.3→38.1 vs. 34.5→42.4). On E-VQA, shifts are modest (IBA 41.9→42.7, EchoSight 40.4→40.9), consistent with E-VQA having fewer answer-evidence conflicts. Under augmentation, all retrieval-based methods degrade sharply—IBA drops from 40.1 to 21.4 (Intra) / 21.6 (Inter) on InfoSeek, EchoSight from 38.7 to 15.9/17.8, and Wiki-PRF from 43.9 to 23.6/25.9—while non-retrieval VLMs (LLaVA-v1.5, Qwen2.5-VL) are not evaluated under augmentation. The key takeaway is that unfixed benchmarks systematically favor aggregation/filtering pipelines over explicit re-ranking, a conclusion that reverses once annotation validity is enforced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new model or method for KB-VQA but instead treats the evaluation infrastructure itself as the object of study. It builds on the two dominant KB-VQA benchmarks (InfoSeek and E-VQA) and the MM-RAG solution landscape (re-ranking methods like IBA and EchoSight; aggregation methods like ReflectiVA, CoMeM, and Wiki-PRF) by auditing their validity assumptions and showing that answer accuracy as a metric is unreliable under current benchmark conditions. Conceptually, it repurposes data-auditing and repair protocols familiar from NLP benchmark construction into the multi-modal KB-VQA setting, and introduces a controlled visual augmentation protocol (single-distractor injection) that has no direct precedent in the KB-VQA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 22% of InfoSeek instances have answers unsupported by their own knowledge base, and that method rankings reverse after fixing (e.g., IBA overtakes ReflectiVA), directly challenges the community's current comparative conclusions and may redirect optimization effort toward re-ranking over aggregation. The augmentation results—showing retrieval recall collapsing by roughly 3× under a single distractor—reveal that current KB-VQA methods exploit single-entity shortcuts rather than performing genuine grounded disambiguation, a gap that will matter as real-world queries become more complex. Open questions include whether the fixing protocol generalizes to other KB-VQA or RAG benchmarks, how to scale multi-entity augmentation beyond two entities, and whether any existing architecture can recover robust grounding under visual clutter without task-specific training.
