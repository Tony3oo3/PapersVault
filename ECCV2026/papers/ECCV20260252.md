## Problem

Frozen VLMs suffer from an evidence utilization gap: they can articulate correct reasoning plans yet misread small, localized visual evidence that determines the final answer. Prior inference-time visual interventions (e.g., FGVP, Set-of-Mark) are open-loop, applying fixed heuristics without verifying whether the emphasized region is actually the decisive evidence the model relies on. The paper asks how to design a test-time visual intervention that is both instance-adaptive and self-verifying, without retraining the backbone.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text instruction as input and produces a structured text answer (enforced via a 'Final answer: ...' format) along with a soft pixel spotlight mask of shape H×W in [0,1]. The spotlight mask is applied to the image before feeding it to the frozen VLM, and the method requires access to token-level log probabilities from the VLM to compute answer-span entropy and anchor entropies. The spotlight module is a lightweight CLIP-based network with LoRA adapters, and its parameters are reset after each instance to avoid cross-sample drift.

## Method

SPOT-E introduces a CLIP-based question-conditioned visual spotlight module that computes patch-text cosine similarities on a global view and multiple local crops, fuses the resulting relevance maps via max pooling, and produces a soft mask that highlights evidence while dimming the background. The key insight is that answer-span entropy is a label-free proxy for evidence usability, but naive entropy minimization is ambiguous because both evidence-grounded confidence and shortcut collapse yield low entropy. To disambiguate, the method defines low-entropy anchors (token positions where the frozen VLM is already near-deterministic at baseline) and constructs an entropy-shaping reward that rewards answer-entropy reduction (with dynamic scaling) while penalizing entropy increases on those anchors. Per-instance, only the spotlight LoRA parameters are optimized via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) over a short episode of candidate masks scored by the composite reward, and the final prediction is selected by Best-of-N. The VLM backbone remains fully frozen throughout.

## Results

SPOT-E yields consistent gains across all 9 evaluated backbones (3 closed-source APIs and 6 open-source VLMs) on 8 benchmarks. Gains are largest on evidence-intensive tasks: LLaVA-NeXT-7B improves by +6.2 on TextVQA (78.5→84.7) and +3.5 on MMMU (38.0→41.0), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B gains +3.0 on MathVista (67.8→70.8) and +3.5 on MMMU (55.0→58.5). Even strong closed-source models benefit, with GPT-4o improving by +2.5 on TextVQA and +2.0 on MathVista. Under visual corruptions (Gaussian noise, downsampling, occlusion), SPOT-E consistently reduces the accuracy drop relative to the frozen baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPOT-E outperforms all five inference-time visual intervention baselines on every benchmark in Table 2 (frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B). The margin is most dramatic on MMMU, where SPOT-E reaches 58.5 versus 50.4 for the strongest baseline AttWarp (+8.1) and 45.1 for SoM (+13.4), indicating that question-conditioned spotlighting with entropy feedback captures compositional reasoning cues that fixed heuristics miss. On TextVQA, SPOT-E leads AttWarp by +2.2 (86.9 vs 84.7) and ViCrop by +3.1. On POPE, SPOT-E ties AttWarp at 87.4 but surpasses ViCrop (+0.7) and API (+1.6), suggesting comparable hallucination mitigation. On DocVQA, SPOT-E achieves 86.5, beating AttWarp (+2.4) and ViCrop (+4.0), while FGVP-RBM collapses to 38.6, highlighting the fragility of fixed mask-based interventions on document layouts. SoM is consistently the weakest baseline across all five benchmarks, confirming that discrete mark-based prompting is insufficient for fine-grained evidence localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPOT-E sits at the intersection of two established threads: inference-time visual interventions (FGVP, Set-of-Mark, ViCrop, AttWarp) that manipulate the visual input to a frozen VLM, and entropy/uncertainty-based signals used for calibration and selective prediction. The key departure is that prior visual interventions are open-loop—they highlight regions without verifying the model actually relied on them—whereas SPOT-E introduces a closed-loop feedback signal (answer-span entropy) combined with a low-entropy anchor preservation term to disambiguate evidence-grounded confidence from shortcut collapse. The per-instance optimization leverages GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) applied to a lightweight CLIP-based spotlight module with LoRA adapters, repurposing a reinforcement-learning algorithm originally designed for mathematical reasoning as a test-time adaptation tool for visual grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SPOT-E—freezing the backbone and optimizing only a small spotlight module per instance—makes it directly deployable on already-shipped VLMs without retraining, addressing a practical bottleneck (the evidence utilization gap) that affects chart reading, document parsing, and fine-grained OCR in production. The entropy-shaping principle (reduce answer entropy while preserving baseline high-confidence tokens) is a general, label-free objective that could transfer to other test-time adaptation settings beyond visual spotlighting. Remaining limitations include per-instance optimization latency (8 GRPO steps), continued failure on extremely small or inherently ambiguous evidence, and the reliance on structured answer-span extraction that may not generalize to free-form generation. An open question is whether the anchor-preservation idea can be extended to multi-turn or agentic settings where the 'baseline' trajectory is itself uncertain.
