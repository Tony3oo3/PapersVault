## Problem

Scaling 3D scene generation to unbounded outdoor environments like city blocks creates a fundamental tension: diffusion-based chunk generation relies on fixed local context and cannot maintain long-range structural coherence, while 2D-to-3D lifting pipelines produce plausible appearances but distort underlying geometry. No existing method simultaneously achieves high-fidelity local geometry, global spatial consistency across large grids, and text-controllable layout without requiring hard-to-acquire structural priors like scene graphs or HD maps.

## Input / Output

The method takes an optional natural language prompt describing spatial relationships (e.g., directional placement, height-rank ordering) and a target grid dimension (H×W chunks, from 2×2 up to 32×32), and produces a complete 3D outdoor scene as a grid of mesh chunks. Each chunk is decoded from a set of 16 latent tokens via the VAE into point clouds, surface normals, and high-resolution occupancy grids (up to 256 voxels per axis). Text conditioning is optional; the model also supports purely unconditional generation, and the text is encoded via a frozen CLIP encoder with classifier-free guidance (drop probability 0.1).

## Method

Scale3D decouples scene generation into a two-stage hierarchical autoregressive pipeline. A causal transformer first predicts global chunk embeddings in Z-order spatial traversal using 2D RoPE positional encodings, modeling long-range dependencies across the entire grid; a masked autoregressive model with bidirectional attention then synthesizes fine-grained tokens within each chunk, conditioned on the global embedding and tokens from top/left neighboring chunks to enforce boundary continuity. The local generator uses a diffusion head (12-layer DDPM) rather than categorical prediction, following MAR-Diffusion. An enhanced 3D chunk VAE improves reconstruction via Flexible Resolution Control (direct mesh partitioning bypassing the 32-bit SDF limit, raising occupancy resolution from 50 to 1000 voxels) and Salient Geometry Guidance (incorporating sharp-edge sampled points and normals as additional encoder input). Training combines a diffusion objective, a coherency-aware cosine regularization between neighboring chunks to prevent mode collapse, and DPO on ~400 human-ranked preference pairs to suppress repetitive or empty outputs.

## Results

On unconditional generation, Scale3D achieves the best FPD and KPD* at all scales ≥8×8. At 32×32 on the 13-scene dataset, Scale3D reaches FPD=0.75 and KPD*=1.97, outperforming the strongest NuiScene variant (FPD=0.82, KPD*=2.36) by ~9% and ~16% respectively. At 8×8 (13 scenes), Scale3D scores FPD=0.27 vs. NuiScene(16×16) at 0.34. For text-conditioned generation, Scale3D attains CLIP scores of 0.94–0.98 and Spatial Relation Accuracy of 0.78 (direction) / 0.71 (height-rank) at 8×8, far exceeding random baselines of 0.25/0.17.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline NuiScene, Scale3D is competitive at 2×2 but pulls ahead decisively at larger scales: at 32×32 on 13 scenes, Scale3D reduces FPD from 0.82 to 0.75 and KPD* from 2.36 to 1.97, with the gap widening monotonically from 4×4 onward. The adapted XCube baseline (FPD/KPD* = 0.45/0.72 at 8×8) is outperformed by Scale3D (FPD/KPD* = 0.27/0.59 on 13 scenes), and both XCube and LT3SD fail to maintain structural integrity even at their maximum viable 8×8 scale, exhibiting discontinuous ground planes and severe voxel-blurring artifacts respectively. The VAE component also shows a clear advantage: Scale3D VAE achieves IoU of 0.6686 vs. 0.4690 for NuiScene VAE on the single-scene set (and 0.6114 vs. 0.4066 on 13 scenes), with point Chamfer distance reduced by ~85%. A controlled experiment using the frozen NuiScene VAE confirms that the hierarchical AR architecture itself (not just the VAE) drives the large-scale gains, with Scale3D+NuiScene VAE still beating original NuiScene at 32×32 (FPD 0.93 vs. 1.26). For text-conditioned generation, no direct quantitative baseline exists since NuiScene does not support text conditioning; Scale3D's spatial relation accuracy of 0.78/0.71 substantially exceeds the 0.25/0.17 random baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

Scale3D builds directly on NuiScene's chunk-based VAE and unbounded outdoor scene generation paradigm, but replaces its diffusion-based chunk synthesis with a hierarchical autoregressive framework. The local generation stage adopts the masked autoregressive + diffusion head formulation from MAR-Diffusion [14], while the VAE incorporates sharp-edge sampling from Dora [2] as salient geometry guidance. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant diffusion-based chunk generation line (BlockFusion, LT3SD, XCube) by arguing that iterative denoising with fixed local context is fundamentally incompatible with long-range spatial coherence, and instead leverages autoregression's natural sequential dependency modeling to enforce global structure before local detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical AR decomposition offers a principled alternative to the inpainting/outpainting patchwork that plagues current diffusion-based scene extension, potentially enabling truly seamless large-scale generation. The text-conditioned spatial control via an automated annotation pipeline (height maps → watershed segmentation → LLM captions) removes the need for hard-to-acquire structural priors like scene graphs or HD maps, lowering the barrier for controllable 3D scene synthesis. Key limitations include evaluation on only 13 scenes, restriction to outdoor geometry, and the acknowledged need for inference acceleration; open questions include whether the hierarchical AR paradigm generalizes to indoor scenes, dynamic content, or even larger city-scale environments, and whether the DPO-based preference alignment can be replaced by more scalable reward signals.
