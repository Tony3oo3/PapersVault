## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation methods compress rich MLLM reasoning into sparse target cues (points, boxes, or latent embeddings) that are often ambiguous or noisy, and they feed all cues jointly into a single segmentation process, so any erroneous cue contaminates the entire prediction. No efficient mechanism exists to isolate and suppress the impact of unreliable cues while preserving the original reasoning intent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261940/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language query (e.g., 'find a tool that can tighten screws') and produces a binary pixel-wise segmentation mask. The MLLM first emits a chain-of-thought trace, then a concise textual description (semantic cue) and a bounding box (spatial cue); these are consumed by SAM3's text-prompt and box-prompt pathways respectively. The dynamic gating module operates at the pixel-decoder feature resolution (h×w) and is bilinearly upscaled to full image resolution before combining branch logits.

## Method

DGSeg processes the MLLM's semantic and spatial cues through two independent SAM3 branches to isolate potential noise, then fuses their predictions via a lightweight dynamic gating module (two convolutional layers on concatenated pixel-decoder features producing a sigmoid pixel-wise weight map). Training is two-stage: Stage 1 fine-tunes the MLLM with GRPO reinforcement learning using a composite reward (format validity, bounding-box IoU, and segmentation-mask IoU for the semantic cue); Stage 2 freezes the MLLM and SAM3 backbone and trains only the gating module with a relative branch-quality supervision signal—a soft target derived from the softmax of each branch's IoU with ground truth—combined with a segmentation loss and an entropy penalty to discourage overly averaged weights. This design lets the gate learn which branch is more reliable at each pixel, adaptively suppressing noisy regions without requiring iterative MLLM refinement.

## Results

On the ReasonSeg benchmark (zero-shot), DGSeg achieves 69.6% and 67.3% gIoU on the validation and test splits with the 7B-scale Qwen2.5-VL backbone, and 66.0% and 60.0% gIoU with the 3B-scale model. On referring segmentation benchmarks (RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg), DGSeg reaches a leading average cIoU of 76.9% (7B) and 75.2% (3B). The dynamic gating module adds only 0.3% FLOPs and a 2.0% FPS drop over the no-fusion baseline while delivering consistent gIoU gains across all splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGSeg consistently outperforms all baselines on ReasonSeg. With the 3B backbone, it surpasses Seg-Zero by 3.4% (Val) and 3.9% (Test) gIoU, and CoPRS by 4.7% and 2.2% gIoU respectively. With the 7B backbone, DGSeg leads SAM-Veteran (the strongest 7B baseline) by 1.4% on Val and 4.7% on Test gIoU, and beats PIXELTHINK by 5.8% and 7.1% gIoU on Val and Test. On referring segmentation (RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg), DGSeg 7B ties RISE and SAM-Veteran at 76.9% average cIoU while matching or exceeding them on individual splits (e.g., 73.9 vs. 73.4 on RefCOCOg). Notably, SAM3 Agent [5]—which uses the same segmentation backbone as DGSeg—achieves only 65.4% Val gIoU on ReasonSeg, confirming that the dual-branch dynamic gating design, rather than the backbone alone, drives the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-guided reasoning segmentation paradigm (LISA, Seg-Zero, CoReS) by departing from the standard practice of encoding all target cues into a single unified prompt representation. Rather than relying on iterative MLLM refinement (SAM-Veteran, RS-Agent) or hand-crafted attention extraction (LENS), DGSeg isolates complementary semantic and spatial cues into separate segmentation branches and introduces a lightweight dynamic gating module trained with relative branch-quality supervision. The framework repurposes SAM3's pixel decoder features as input to the gating network and uses GRPO-based RL to align the MLLM's structured outputs with the segmentation model's prompt space, bridging the reasoning and execution stages more tightly than prior SFT-only approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-branch-plus-learnable-fusion pipeline offers a generalizable recipe for mitigating cue noise in any MLLM-to-segmentation pipeline, and the ablation showing it can accommodate alternative fusion strategies (e.g., MoE) positions it as a flexible scaffold rather than a fixed architecture. A notable limitation is the dependence on SAM3 as the segmentation backbone and the zero-shot evaluation protocol on ReasonSeg, leaving open whether the approach transfers to other promptable segmenters or benefits from task-specific fine-tuning. The paper also highlights that the 0.3% FLOPs overhead makes the gating module practically deployable, but the modest gap over the strongest baseline (SAM-Veteran) on the validation split suggests diminishing returns from fusion alone without further improvements in cue generation quality.
