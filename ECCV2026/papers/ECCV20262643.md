## Problem

Existing forward-correction methods for noisy-label learning treat the class-dependent transition matrix T as full-rank and unconstrained, estimating all C² entries independently. However, real-world label confusion is structured—annotators systematically confuse semantically similar classes—causing T to exhibit reduced effective rank. This full-rank modeling assumption wastes capacity on unstructured tail modes, leading to statistical inefficiency, numerical instability during optimization, and degraded generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100/CIFAR-100N; 224×224 random crops from 256×256 resizes for ImageNet-1K and Food-101N) paired with noisy one-hot labels, and outputs a clean class prediction via a standard softmax classifier. Unusually, the transition matrix T ∈ ℝ^{C×C} is jointly learned with the network parameters and projected to be row-stochastic after each update, so the effective input to the loss is the forward-corrected posterior T^⊤ p_θ(x) rather than the raw network output.

## Method

LENL (Low-Effective-Rank Noisy-Label Learning) optimizes a joint objective combining forward-corrected cross-entropy loss with a nuclear-norm regularizer λ‖T‖_* on the transition matrix, explicitly encouraging the singular spectrum of T to concentrate on a few dominant modes that capture systematic semantic confusion while suppressing spurious tail modes. To compute the nuclear norm without the numerical instability of SVD (which produces NaN gradients when T has near-zero singular values), the paper introduces a Newton-Schulz iteration that approximates (TT^⊤ + εI)^{1/2} via five matrix-multiplication-only steps, yielding a decomposition-free and scalable regularizer. A generalization bound (Theorem 1) shows that the noisy-risk upper bound scales with ‖T‖_* times the Rademacher complexity, providing theoretical justification for the rank constraint. The framework is instantiated as LENL-SVD (exact SVD, for small C) and LENL-NS (Newton-Schulz, for large C such as ImageNet-1K with C=1000), both trained end-to-end with SGD and row-wise softmax projection to maintain stochasticity.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, LENL-SVD achieves 70.42% (Sym-20%), 61.35% (Sym-50%), 73.21% (Pair-20%), and 62.88% (Pair-45%) top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline PLM by 0.88–0.98 points across all settings. On ImageNet-1K (C=1000), LENL-NS achieves 73.35% (Sym-20%) and 65.59% (Pair-45%), surpassing the best baseline Co-Teaching by 6.66 and 1.51 points respectively, while VolMinNet fails entirely due to numerical instability. On real-world noisy datasets, LENL-NS reaches 61.51% on CIFAR-100N, 97.20% accuracy / 79.75% F1 on Noisy Ostracods, and 83.30% on Food-101N, consistently leading all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LENL outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and noise setting. Against the strongest transition-matrix baseline VolMinNet [17], LENL gains 5.48 points on CIFAR-100 Sym-20% (70.42 vs. 64.94) and 3.98 points on Pair-45% (62.88 vs. 58.90); VolMinNet also fails to converge on ImageNet-1K and Noisy Ostracods due to SVD numerical instability. Against the sample-selection baseline Co-Teaching [7], LENL leads by 25.53 points on CIFAR-100 Sym-20% (70.42 vs. 44.89) and 6.66 points on ImageNet-1K Sym-20% (73.35 vs. 66.69). Against the part-level-label method PLM [45], LENL achieves consistent 0.88–0.98 point gains on CIFAR-100 and a 9.48 point margin on ImageNet-1K Sym-20% (73.35 vs. 63.87). Against the instance-dependent method IDLE [18], LENL leads by 0.34 points on CIFAR-100 Sym-20% and 10.49 points on ImageNet-1K Sym-20%, with the gap widening under higher noise. On real-world noisy data, LENL-NS beats PLM by 0.99 points on CIFAR-100N (61.51 vs. 60.52) and 2.41 points on Food-101N (83.30 vs. 80.89), while also achieving the highest F1 (79.75) on Noisy Ostracods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the forward-correction framework of Patrini et al. (CVPR 2017), which models label noise via a class-conditional transition matrix T, by introducing a structural inductive bias that prior transition-matrix methods (VolMinNet, Dual-T, Class2Simi, PLM) all lack: an explicit reduced-effective-rank constraint on T via nuclear-norm regularization. The optimization side draws on the classical Newton-Schulz iteration (Schulz, 1933) from numerical linear algebra, repurposing it as a decomposition-free surrogate for the nuclear norm to sidestep the SVD instability that plagues existing methods at scale. Conceptually, the paper bridges matrix-completion-style low-rank regularization with the noisy-label learning literature, arguing that semantic clustering in real datasets makes the full-rank assumption both unnecessary and harmful.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that real-world transition matrices concentrate 70–90% of spectral energy in a small number of dominant modes provides a concrete, actionable structural prior that practitioners can exploit without additional clean data or anchor points. The Newton-Schulz optimizer addresses a genuine practical bottleneck—SVD-based nuclear-norm gradients produce NaNs for near-singular T—making low-rank transition modeling viable at ImageNet scale where VolMinNet simply fails. However, the method assumes all samples are in-distribution; the authors themselves flag that out-of-distribution outliers produce full-rank mislabeling patterns that violate the low-rank assumption, leaving the integration of outlier detection with rank constraints as an open problem. The generalization bound (Theorem 1) is a useful theoretical anchor but relies on a uniform lower bound c on forward-corrected predictions that may be hard to verify in practice.
