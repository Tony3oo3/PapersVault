## Problem

Existing 3D instance segmentation methods assume offline-collected RGB-D or LiDAR data with ground-truth poses, making them unsuitable for real-time embodied agents that must perceive incrementally from unposed monocular video. Moreover, bottom-up segmentation paradigms (segment-then-recognize) fix granularity to a predefined taxonomy and cannot adapt to task-specific requirements—e.g., merging a towel into a bathtub when the task is 'find the towel on the bathtub.' A further challenge is that modern dense SLAM systems continuously re-optimize poses and depth, causing prior mask associations to become stale and degrading existing segmentation pipelines when fed SLAM reconstructions instead of sensor data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a live stream of unposed RGB images and a task-relevant open-category set (e.g., {towel, bathtub}), which can be automatically inferred from a natural-language instruction via an LLM. It produces per-instance 3D masks back-projected onto the MASt3R-SLAM-reconstructed point cloud, along with semantic category labels and a unified 3D instance-and-semantic map. The system operates online at approximately 7 FPS, with the main computational cost dominated by the SLAM front-end rather than the segmentation pipeline itself.

## Method

The framework builds on MASt3R-SLAM for real-time dense reconstruction, exploiting two key properties: (1) its pointmap matching produces dense pixel correspondences that are independent of the continuously re-optimized pose estimates, and (2) its incremental pose graph identifies only spatio-temporally relevant keyframe pairs for association. For each new keyframe, YOLO-World detects task-relevant objects and SAM2 refines them into non-overlapping instance masks, making segmentation granularity top-down and task-driven rather than bottom-up. Cross-view mask association uses three metrics computed only along pose-graph edges: a Geometric Association Metric (bidirectional overlap ratio through SLAM correspondences), a Semantic Similarity Metric (cosine similarity of pre-computed CLIP text embeddings per category), and a Mutual Exclusivity Metric (IoU-based constraint preventing merging of distinct intra-frame masks). A priority-ordered online clustering algorithm processes candidate mask pairs in descending geometric confidence, enforcing mutual exclusivity to prevent error accumulation, followed by a second-stage 3D bounding-box IoU merge to handle temporally distant but spatially close keyframes.

## Results

On ScanNet200 with MASt3R-SLAM inputs (no GT poses/depth), the method achieves AP50=7.4 and AP25=19.3 in the open-vocabulary setting, dramatically outperforming Open-YOLO 3D (AP50=0.8) and OnlineAnySeg (AP50=0.1) under the same SLAM-reconstruction conditions. In the class-agnostic setting, it reaches AP50=16.4 and AP25=44.7, competitive with GT-based methods. On the Replica dataset, the method achieves AP50=23.4 and AP25=37.3 using only unposed monocular video, surpassing even GT-based baselines such as Open-YOLO 3D (AP25=34.8) and the offline feed-forward PanSt3R (AP25=34.3). The system runs at approximately 7 FPS, with the SLAM backend being the dominant computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the fair MASt3R-SLAM input setting on ScanNet200, the method overwhelmingly outperforms both Open-YOLO 3D (AP50: 7.4 vs. 0.8) and OnlineAnySeg (AP50: 7.4 vs. 0.1) in the open-vocabulary setting, demonstrating that existing 3D segmentation networks and naive CLIP-averaging strategies collapse under noisy SLAM reconstructions. In the class-agnostic setting, the method (AP25=44.7) also substantially surpasses OnlineAnySeg with SLAM inputs (AP25=19.5) and approaches GT-based SAM3D (AP25=49.6). On the Replica dataset, the method achieves the best AP25 (37.3) among all compared methods, beating GT-based Open-YOLO 3D (34.8), Open3DIS (28.2), OVIR-3D (27.5), and the offline feed-forward PanSt3R (34.3), while also exceeding PanSt3R on AP50 (23.4 vs. 20.9). The method does not match the absolute AP50 of GT-based Open-YOLO 3D on ScanNet200 (7.4 vs. 31.7), which the authors attribute to residual geometric errors in MASt3R-SLAM reconstructions affecting small and long-tail objects. Overall, the key finding is that the method is the only one that remains functional and competitive when fed with online SLAM-reconstructed geometry rather than privileged sensor data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: feed-forward dense SLAM (MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM), open-vocabulary 2D segmentation (SAM2, YOLO-World, CLIP), and online 3D instance segmentation (OnlineAnySeg, EmbodiedSAM). It departs from prior online segmentation methods by replacing their reliance on ground-truth poses and sensor depth with SLAM-derived point correspondences that are invariant to backend pose optimization, and from bottom-up 3D segmentation paradigms (SAM3D, OpenMask3D) by adopting a task-oriented, top-down granularity determined by an open-vocabulary detector rather than a fixed 3D segmentation model. The priority-ordered mask clustering with mutual exclusivity constraints is a novel algorithmic contribution not present in prior online mask-merging approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that existing 3D instance segmentation methods (OpenYOLO 3D, OnlineAnySeg) collapse catastrophically when fed SLAM-reconstructed geometry instead of ground-truth point clouds, revealing a critical robustness gap that the field has not adequately addressed. For embodied AI, this work shows that task-adaptive 3D perception from a single unposed camera is feasible in real time, opening a path toward lightweight robotic perception without depth sensors. Key limitations include restriction to static scenes, sensitivity to 2D detector and SLAM errors, and a substantial absolute performance gap to ground-truth-based methods on ScanNet200 (7.4 vs. 31.7 AP50). Future directions include extending to dynamic scenes and creating a bidirectional feedback loop where semantic/instance information improves SLAM reconstruction.
