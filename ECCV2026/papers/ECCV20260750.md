## Problem

Dense crowd simulation faces a fundamental scale gap: macroscopic (Eulerian) methods capture global collective coherence but cannot produce individual trajectories, while microscopic (Lagrangian) methods model individual behaviors yet struggle to preserve systemic consistency in dense scenarios due to ground-truth scarcity. Existing approaches treat these two scales as isolated and decoupled, preventing any single framework from jointly achieving robustness and fidelity.

## Input / Output

The framework takes three inputs: an initial 2D Eulerian velocity field (a grid of 2D vectors), initial positions of N individuals (N 2D points), and a static walkable-area mask (binary spatial constraint). It iteratively produces two coupled outputs over T time-steps: a sequence of evolved velocity fields (one 2D vector field per step) and a sequence of N individual 2D positions per step. The velocity field is downsampled before governing-equation feature extraction and upsampled afterward for efficiency, and the walkable area serves as both a boundary condition for velocity evolution and a spatial constraint for trajectory validity.

## Method

ELHINN couples two sequential modules that exploit Eulerian–Lagrangian duality in reverse: the evolved Eulerian velocity field acts as a physical prior guiding Lagrangian trajectories. The global velocity evolution module (HINN++) embeds a learnable Navier-Stokes governing equation with five weighted operators (convection, viscosity, alignment, navigation, cohesion), augments it with a lightweight KAN residual correction to capture heterogeneous nonlinearities beyond the predefined operators, and enforces no-slip boundary conditions from the walkable area; a ConvResNet then predicts the next-step velocity field autoregressively. The local trajectory refinement module (PINN) queries local velocities from the evolved field at each individual's position, passes them through an MLP to obtain motion vectors, updates positions via a discrete kinematic equation, and applies an entrance-aware resampling strategy (EARS) that removes individuals exiting the walkable area and reinitializes them at detected inward-flow boundary points. Training uses scheduled sampling with Smooth L1 loss for HINN++, and a composite velocity-consistency plus collision-avoidance loss for PINN, with HINN++ parameters frozen before PINN training.

## Results

On the DCFD dataset for macroscopic velocity evolution, HINN++ achieves IS 1.7469, FID 0.0278, and SSIM 0.6030, outperforming the closest competitor PPNN (FID 0.0275, SSIM 0.4893) by a large margin on structural similarity. On the MOT20 dataset for microscopic trajectory refinement, ELHINN achieves the best JADE of 8.274 and JFDE of 12.836, surpassing the next-best method PCS (JADE 9.231, JFDE 14.897) by approximately 10% on both accuracy metrics. ELHINN also attains the lowest energy deviation (+0.979 vs. GT 184.208) and travel distance deviation (−0.125), indicating physically plausible motion intensity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELHINN achieves the best JADE (8.274) and JFDE (12.836) among all 12 baselines, outperforming the strongest physics-informed method PCS (JADE 9.231, JFDE 14.897) by ~10% and the diffusion-based SPDiff (JADE 9.850, JFDE 14.715) by ~16% on JADE. Among data-physics networks, Social-LSTM and STGAT produce near-static trajectories with large negative deviations in speed change and angle change, while PCS and SPDiff enforce basic continuity but yield almost linear paths lacking nonlinear turning. Physics-based Boids and SFM perform worst on accuracy (JADE 63.013 and 13.247 respectively), with Boids exhibiting extreme energy inflation (+1848 vs. GT 184). Generative platforms (Sora, Kling, Runway) produce unrealistically intense motions with energy deviations exceeding +250 and JADE values between 19–29, far behind ELHINN. On the macroscopic side, HINN++ surpasses PPNN (the closest competitor) by 23% on SSIM (0.6030 vs. 0.4893) while matching its FID (0.0278 vs. 0.0275), and outperforms HINN by 26% on SSIM and 56% on FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the authors' own HINN [42] (ACM MM 2024) into HINN++ by adding KAN-based residual correction and explicit environmental boundary conditions, while introducing a PINN-based trajectory refinement module guided by the evolved Eulerian velocity fields. Conceptually, it reverses the Eulerian-Lagrangian duality exploited in EL-PINN [35] (which reconstructs Eulerian fields from Lagrangian data) by instead using learned Eulerian velocity fields as physical priors to constrain Lagrangian trajectories. The framework also draws on standard physics-informed learning (PINN [31]), scheduled sampling [3], and the ConvResNet architecture from PPNN [26], but the key departure is treating macroscopic and microscopic crowd simulation as a single coupled problem rather than two isolated tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly coupling Eulerian velocity evolution with Lagrangian trajectory refinement, ELHINN addresses a long-standing gap where macroscopic methods lack individual trajectories and microscopic methods lose collective coherence in dense crowds. The framework is data-efficient for the macroscopic component (no dense trajectory supervision needed) and provides a physically grounded velocity prior that stabilizes trajectory generation. However, the evaluation is limited to 2D top-down scenarios with static walkable areas and fixed population sizes; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to variable-density crowds, emergency/panic scenarios, 3D or cross-view settings, and whether the KAN residual correction scales to more complex heterogeneous behaviors. The timely comparison with generative video platforms (Sora, Kling, Runway) highlights their physical inconsistency in dense crowd settings, positioning physics-informed methods as a complementary or superior alternative for simulation fidelity.
