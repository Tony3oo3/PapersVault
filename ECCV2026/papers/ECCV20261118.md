## Problem

Existing LLM-based approaches to ComfyUI workflow generation treat the task as direct text-to-JSON prediction, which fails due to structural brittleness and the absence of multi-level expert knowledge (strategic planning, topological reasoning, parameter binding). The paper argues that successful workflow synthesis requires explicitly modeling hierarchical knowledge—its structure, reasoning dynamics, and experiential heuristics—rather than relying on surface-level pattern completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261118/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a natural-language task instruction (e.g., 'transform the input photo into a stylized version preserving layout') along with optional input images, and produces a complete, executable ComfyUI workflow JSON encoding a DAG of nodes, parameters, and typed connections. The workflow is then executed in ComfyUI to yield a final visual output. The JSON must satisfy strict type-safe data-flow constraints across potentially dozens of nodes, making even small link or parameter errors fatal to execution.

## Method

The framework operates in three phases. First, Knowledge Inversion hierarchically distills raw workflows into five abstraction levels: full pseudo-code (rule-based regularization), skeleton pseudo-code (topology without parameters), strategy reasoning traces (LLM-inferred decision logic), high-level strategy descriptions, and reconstructed task instructions. Second, Knowledge Injection fine-tunes Qwen3-14B via LoRA on paired (task→strategy) and (strategy→pseudo-code) examples, with an auxiliary objective for intermediate reasoning extraction; rule-based expansion handles the lower-level skeleton→full pseudo-code transition without additional training. Third, Knowledge Inference reverses the inversion pipeline at test time: an SFT-trained strategy planner produces the high-level plan, a second SFT model generates the skeleton pseudo-code, a pretrained LLM fills in parameters, and a rule-based reconstructor emits the final JSON, with self-refinement loops after both planning and generation stages to correct structural incoherence.

## Results

On the internal test set (30 workflows), the proposed method achieves VND=152, NodeComp=96.3%, LinkComp=98.3%, and TaskCons=86.9%, substantially outperforming the strongest baselines (GPT-5+RAG: VND=42, NodeComp=56.7%, LinkComp=56.7%, TaskCons=53.6%). In execution, the method reaches 86.9% Pass and 61.1% Resolve on average, compared to 48.1%/25.6% for RAG and 36.4%/29.4% for ComfyAgent. On the external FlowBench (30 tasks), the method scores VND=145, NodeComp=94.4%, LinkComp=98.0%, TaskCons=93.4%, Pass=66.7%, Solve=36.7%; on ComfyBench (20 tasks), it achieves VND=138, NodeComp=79.8%, LinkComp=82.7%, TaskCons=85.0%, Pass=50.0%, Solve=25.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six baselines (Zero-Shot, Few-Shot, CoT, CoT-SC, RAG, ComfyAgent) use GPT-5 as the underlying LLM, ensuring a fair comparison. The proposed method wins decisively on every metric across all three evaluation settings (internal test set, FlowBench, ComfyBench). The closest competitors are GPT-5+RAG and GPT-5+ComfyAgent, which reach VND≈40–45 and NodeComp/LinkComp≈56–65%; the proposed method exceeds these by roughly 3.5× in node diversity and by 30–40 percentage points in structural completeness. In execution, the gap is equally large: Pass improves from 48.1% (RAG) to 86.9%, and Resolve from 25.6% (RAG) to 61.1% on the internal set. Zero-Shot GPT-5 produces no valid workflows at all (all metrics at 0), highlighting that direct text-to-JSON generation without injected knowledge is infeasible for this task. Even a larger end-to-end model (Qwen3-32B trained directly from instruction to pseudo-code) underperforms the proposed 14B hierarchical approach on Pass (56.7% vs. 86.9%) and Solve (33.3% vs. 61.1%), confirming the benefit of multi-level knowledge decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant lines of ComfyUI workflow generation: template-based systems (ComfyAgent, ComfyMind) that constrain the solution space to handcrafted meta-node schemas, and end-to-end learning approaches (ComfyGPT, ComfyUI-R1) that train LLMs to predict workflow JSON directly, leaving domain knowledge implicitly entangled in model weights. Rather than simplifying the problem or burying expertise in opaque parameters, the authors introduce a knowledge-centric paradigm that explicitly inverts real workflows into a hierarchy of representations (task instruction → strategy → skeleton pseudo-code → full pseudo-code), injects this structure via supervised fine-tuning, and performs reversible reasoning at inference. The conceptual lineage draws on LLM-agent planning and self-refinement, but the core novelty is treating hierarchical knowledge itself as the representational currency for workflow composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc text-to-JSON generation for visual creation pipelines, and the finding that a 14B model with structured hierarchical supervision outperforms a 32B model trained end-to-end suggests that explicit knowledge decomposition is more effective than raw scale for compositional reasoning tasks. Limitations include sensitivity to rare node types (the model substitutes common nodes, breaking functional dependencies) and evaluation on relatively small test sets (30 in-domain workflows, 20 ComfyBench tasks, 30 FlowBench tasks). Open questions include whether the knowledge-inversion pipeline generalizes beyond ComfyUI to other DAG-based visual programming systems, whether reinforcement-style optimization (e.g., GRPO) can complement the SFT-based injection for robustness, and how the approach scales as the node ecosystem grows without requiring retraining.
