## Problem

Neural Parametric Head Models (NPHMs) offer substantially higher geometric fidelity than classical 3DMMs like FLAME, but their expressive latent space makes single-image fitting notoriously difficult—existing photometric optimization (e.g., MonoNPHM) is slow and brittle in practice. No feed-forward regressor existed to directly predict NPHM parameters from an image, leaving a gap between the model's theoretical capacity and practical usability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262697/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at arbitrary resolution and produces two sets of NPHM latent codes: 66 identity tokens (65 local SDF experts + 1 global) and a single expression code, which are decoded through MonoNPHM's SDF and extracted as a 3D mesh via marching cubes. The feed-forward pass runs at 8 fps on an RTX 3080; an optional 85-second inference-time optimization refines the latents against per-pixel surface normals and canonical point maps for additional fidelity.

## Method

Pix2NPHM encodes the input with two geometrically pre-trained ViT backbones (one for surface normals, one for canonical point maps, following the Pixel3DMM encoder-decoder architecture), concatenates their token sequences with learnable classifier tokens, and decodes through 8 transformer layers plus MLP heads to regress NPHM identity and expression latents. Training combines a 3D SDF-space L1 loss on 102K curated NPHM registrations with a novel 2D self-supervised loss that renders SDF-gradient normals and matches them via cosine similarity to estimated pseudo-ground-truth normals from large-scale video data. A key finding is that face-specific geometric pre-training of the ViT encoders significantly outperforms generic DINOv2 features, and the normal-based 2D supervision yields more stable gradients than the photometric losses standard in FLAME regressors. An optional test-time optimization step further refines the feed-forward prediction against dense geometric cues, improving posed reconstruction by an additional margin.

## Results

On the NeRSemble SVFR benchmark, Pix2NPHM (full, with inference-time optimization) achieves L1 Chamfer distance of 1.37 (Posed) and 1.54 (Neutral), outperforming the best optimization-based baseline Pixel3DMM (1.66/1.66) by ~17% and ~7% respectively. The feed-forward-only variant already reaches 1.55 (Posed) and 1.57 (Neutral), surpassing all feed-forward FLAME regressors (best: SHeaP at 2.08/1.86). On the NoW benchmark, the full method achieves a median error of 0.81 and mean of 1.01, beating all public baselines (best public: SHeaP at 0.95/1.18), though TokenFace (0.76/0.95, not publicly available) remains slightly ahead. On AffectNet emotion recognition, Pix2NPHM achieves 71.1% 8-way emotion accuracy, outperforming SHeaP (69.5%), EMOCA (67.6%), and SMIRK (65.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pix2NPHM decisively outperforms all FLAME-based feed-forward regressors (DECA, MICA, EMOCAv2, SHeaP) on both NeRSemble tasks, with the largest gap over MonoNPHM's photometric fitting (L1 2.50→1.37 Posed, a 45% reduction). Against the strongest optimization-based FLAME baselines (Pixel3DMM, FlowFace), the full method improves Posed L1 by 17% (1.66→1.37) and Neutral L1 by 7% (1.66→1.54), while the feed-forward-only variant already matches or exceeds them. On NoW, Pix2NPHM beats all publicly available methods (SHeaP, MICA, DECA, Pixel3DMM, FlowFace) but is slightly outperformed by the non-public TokenFace (median 0.76 vs. 0.81; mean 0.95 vs. 1.01), which the authors note has not been independently replicated. For AffectNet emotion recognition, Pix2NPHM leads all baselines (SMIRK, EMOCA, SHeaP) on every reported metric, with the largest margin over SMIRK (71.1% vs. 65.3% accuracy). The ablation further confirms that replacing FLAME with NPHM as the underlying 3DMM yields consistent gains (e.g., ffwd FLAME L1=1.81 vs. ffwd NPHM L1=1.55 on Posed), validating the core motivation of the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feed-forward 3DMM regression paradigm (DECA, MICA, SHeaP, TokenFace) by replacing the underlying FLAME model with the more expressive NPHM [16], which was previously only fit via slow photometric optimization. It builds directly on Pixel3DMM [18] for the geometric pre-training strategy of the ViT backbones (surface normal and canonical point map regression) and for the video tracker used in 2D data registration. The key conceptual departure is replacing photometric/spherical-harmonic losses with surface-normal supervision for 2D self-supervision, and showing that face-specific geometrically pre-trained ViTs outperform the generic DINOv2 backbone commonly used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making NPHM fitting fast and robust via a feed-forward network, this work unlocks the higher geometric fidelity of neural parametric head models for large-scale, in-the-wild applications (avatars, animation, tracking) that were previously limited to slow per-image optimization. The 21% improvement on NeRSemble SVFR and consistent gains across benchmarks demonstrate that the NPHM latent space, once properly initialized, is a meaningful upgrade over FLAME. Open questions include: whether the approach can handle 3D hair (currently a MonoNPHM limitation), whether joint fine-tuning of NPHM and the regressor yields further gains, and how to resolve the inherent single-image ambiguity via uncertainty estimation or multi-view extensions.
