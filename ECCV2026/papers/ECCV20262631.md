## Problem

Multi-oriented text in real-world scenes remains a major challenge for scene text recognition, yet existing rotation-aware methods (e.g., ASTER, ESIR, SVTRv2) rely on explicit orientation estimation or rectification modules that lack theoretical guarantees, introduce extra inference cost, and require rotation-based data augmentation. This paper asks whether rotation invariance can be embedded directly into the STR architecture with provable guarantees, eliminating the need for orientation correction at inference time.

## Input / Output

The model takes a square 128×128 scene text image as input (deliberately square rather than the typical 32×128 to preserve feature resolution for vertically oriented text) and autoregressively outputs a variable-length character sequence (max length 25, 96-character vocabulary). A key property is that rotating the input by any canonical angle (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) produces exactly identical logits and predictions, while non-canonical rotations yield approximately invariant outputs.

## Method

RISTER combines a rotation-equivariant encoder (RELG) with a rotation-invariant decoder (RITD) to achieve end-to-end rotation invariance. RELG uses three stages of Fourier-based equivariant convolutions (F-Conv under the C4 rotation group) with progressive downsampling to 1/8 resolution, followed by self-attention global blocks to model inter-character dependencies while preserving fine-grained geometric details. The core theoretical contribution is a proof that cross-attention is rotation-invariant: when query tokens are fixed and source features undergo spatial rotation, the attended output remains identical. RITD exploits this by feeding rotation-invariant query tokens (derived from previously decoded characters via self-attention and MLP) into cross-attention over the equivariant visual features, guaranteeing orientation-independent variable-length decoding. The entire system is trained with a standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss on Union14M-Filter without any rotation augmentation, and F-Conv incurs no additional inference cost over standard convolutions since its parameters are fixed by cyclic shifting after training.

## Results

RISTER-L achieves a state-of-the-art average accuracy of 92.46% across 13 benchmarks (Union14M-Benchmarks + Common Benchmarks), surpassing the previous best SVTRv2 (90.30%) by 2.16%. On the Multi-Oriented subset of Union14M, RISTER-S/B/L reach 96.0–96.6%, outperforming SVTRv2 (89.5%) by over 6.5%. On the dedicated oriented-text comparison (Table 6), RISTER-S achieves 94.65% average on Mul-Ori + ASOT, beating the second-best method (SLOAN, 90.65%) by 4.00%. RISTER-L attains the highest accuracy on 8 out of 13 individual benchmarks while maintaining under 40M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RISTER-L (92.46% avg) outperforms the previous state-of-the-art SVTRv2 (90.30%) by 2.16% overall, and RISTER-S (90.37%) edges out SVTRv2 by 0.07% with fewer parameters (21.8M vs. 21.0M). The most dramatic gains appear on multi-oriented text: RISTER-S achieves 96.0% on U14M-Multi-Oriented versus SVTRv2's 89.5% (+6.5%), and on the dedicated oriented comparison (Table 6), RISTER-S scores 94.65% average on Mul-Ori + ASOT, beating SLOAN (90.65%) by 4.00% and ASTER (79.80%) by 14.85%. On standard horizontal benchmarks (IC13, SVT, CUTE80), RISTER-L matches or slightly exceeds SVTRv2 (98.9 vs. 98.7 on IC13; 98.1 vs. 98.0 on SVT; 97.6 vs. 99.0 on CUTE80), showing that rotation invariance does not sacrifice horizontal-text accuracy. RISTER-T, the smallest variant (15.9M params), achieves 88.13% average, competitive with much larger models like MGP-STR (86.54%, 148M params) and CPPD (88.14%, 26.9M params). Among encoder comparisons (Table 3), RELG outperforms SVTR by 2.0% on U14M-Multi-Oriented (96.6% vs. 94.6%) while maintaining comparable 0° accuracy, and surpasses equivariant ViT variants (Stand-Alone, LieTransformer) by 1.5–2.6% on multi-oriented samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the rotation-equivariant convolution literature (G-CNN, F-Conv, PDO-eConv) and equivariant self-attention (LieTransformer, Stand-Alone) for scene text recognition, a task where these tools had not been applied. It departs from prior multi-oriented STR methods (AON, SLOAN, ASTER, SVTRv2) that explicitly estimate or correct orientation, instead embedding invariance directly into the architecture. The key conceptual contribution is identifying and proving the rotation-invariance property of cross-attention on 2D feature inputs, which had been overlooked in the attention-based STR decoder literature, and using it to derive design principles for a rotation-invariant decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RISTER provides the first theoretically grounded rotation-invariant STR framework, eliminating the error accumulation and data-augmentation dependence of prior orientation-correction methods while incurring zero additional inference cost (F-Conv inference is identical to standard convolution). The 4% accuracy gain on multi-oriented benchmarks and competitive performance on standard benchmarks demonstrate that architectural invariance can outperform data-driven orientation handling. Limitations include exact invariance only at C4 canonical angles (approximate at arbitrary angles), a requirement for square 128×128 inputs, and the autoregressive decoding bottleneck; the authors note extending to non-square inputs for long-text or formula recognition as future work.
