## Problem

Margin-penalty softmax losses (ArcFace, CosFace) dominate biometric verification, but their geometric logit modifications are heuristic extensions decoupled from the underlying probabilistic formulation. The recently introduced α-divergence loss framework offers tunable posterior sparsity for α>1, yet standard geometric margins do not naturally extend to this generalized setting, and no prior work has exploited the reference measure to encode margin penalties probabilistically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262324/cropped_page11_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For face verification, aligned 112×112 RGB images are encoded into identity embeddings via a ResNet-100 backbone; for speaker verification, audio utterances are processed by a ResNet-34. The final fully-connected layer computes cosine similarities between the embedding and class prototypes, which are scaled and fed into the loss. A distinctive property of the output is that the α-softargmax posterior assigns exactly zero probability to the vast majority of classes (typically <0.4% are non-zero), producing a sparse distribution rather than the dense softmax output.

## Method

Q-Margin introduces a probabilistic margin by encoding the penalty directly into the non-uniform reference measure q of the α-divergence loss, setting q_j = exp(−s·m·δ_{y,j}) to down-weight the target class prior rather than geometrically modifying the target logit. Minimizing the resulting α-divergence loss compels the model to produce a substantially higher target logit to compensate, implicitly creating a decision margin while preserving the sparsity benefits of α>1. In the limit α→1, Q-Margin reduces exactly to CosFace, providing a theoretical bridge between geometric and probabilistic margins. The extreme posterior sparsity (active support ~0.4% of 2M+ classes) enables an exact top-K truncation of the iterative bisection algorithm for computing the normalizer τ*, reducing training overhead from ~27% to ~5% relative to ArcFace on WebFace42M.

## Results

On IJB-B and IJB-C (TAR at FAR=10⁻⁴/10⁻⁵), Q-Margin (α=1.25, s=35, m=0.2) achieves 96.44/93.67% and 97.78/96.35% respectively, outperforming identically-trained ArcFace (96.09/92.26, 97.54/95.70) and CosFace (96.05/93.15, 97.49/95.82) baselines. On VoxCeleb1-H speaker verification, Q-Margin (α=1.5, s=10, m=0.1) reaches 87.91% TAR at FAR=10⁻³ and Q-Margin (α=1.75, s=5, m=0.1) reaches 74.60% at FAR=10⁻⁴, surpassing ArcFace (86.62/72.08) and CosFace (86.68/70.61). The best Q-Margin configuration activates only ~0.4% of the 2M+ identity classes per example, yet incurs only ~5% throughput overhead versus ArcFace when the top-5% truncation is applied.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the controlled re-implementation block (identical ResNet-100 + WebFace42M recipe), Q-Margin wins on every IJB-B and IJB-C metric against ArcFace, CosFace, EntMax, and SparseMax, with the largest gains at FAR=10⁻⁵ (e.g., +1.41 pp over ArcFace on IJB-B, +0.65 pp on IJB-C). Against the reported PartialFC numbers, Q-Margin essentially ties on IJB-C (97.78 vs. 97.82 at 10⁻⁴; 96.35 vs. 96.46 at 10⁻⁵) while activating only ~0.4% of classes per example. Q-Margin trails UniFace and TopoFR on IJB-C (96.35 vs. 96.68/97.10 at 10⁻⁵), but those methods use a deeper R-200 backbone and additional architectural components, so the gap is not attributable to the loss alone. In speaker verification on VoxCeleb1-H, Q-Margin (α=1.5, s=10, m=0.1) improves TAR at FAR=10⁻³ to 87.91% over ArcFace (86.62%) and CosFace (86.68%), and Q-Margin (α=1.75, s=5, m=0.1) achieves the best FAR=10⁻⁴ score of 74.60% versus 72.08% (ArcFace) and 70.61% (CosFace). The paper notes that higher α values (1.5–1.75) are more beneficial in the smaller speaker setting (5,994 identities) than in face recognition (2M identities), where α=1.25 is optimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: margin-penalty softmax losses (ArcFace, CosFace) that modify target logits geometrically, and the Fenchel-Young / f-divergence loss framework of Blondel et al. and Roulet et al., which generalizes Cross-Entropy to a family of convex losses with tunable posterior sparsity. The key departure is that prior margin methods (including SparseMax+ArcFace hybrids) always apply the penalty to the logit itself, whereas Q-Margin encodes the margin into the non-uniform reference measure q, leaving the logit untouched. This yields a clean theoretical result: CosFace emerges as the α→1 special case, unifying the geometric and probabilistic margin perspectives within a single divergence-based formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic-margin formulation offers a principled alternative to heuristic logit manipulation and, through the inherent sparsity of α-softargmax posteriors, enables exact top-K truncation of the iterative bisection solver—reducing the ~27% throughput penalty of the naive implementation to ~5% on 2M-identity datasets. This makes α-divergence losses practically viable at the scale where face recognition is actually trained. Limitations include single-run WebFace42M experiments (no confidence intervals), the extra α hyperparameter that couples with scale s and requires joint tuning, and the modest (0.4–0.5%) gains at low FARs over identically-trained ArcFace/CosFace. Open questions include whether Q-Margin composes with adaptive-margin strategies (AdaFace, KappaFace) and whether dynamic, sample-dependent reference measures could further improve robustness.
