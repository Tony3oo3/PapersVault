## Problem

Class-conditional diffusion models trained on long-tailed data suffer from mode collapse and reduced diversity for tail classes due to limited training samples. Existing contrastive or regularization approaches either directly penalize conditional latents (admitting a trivial shortcut that maximizes inter-class variance at the expense of intra-class diversity) or fail to exploit the observed similarity between head and tail class images during early denoising steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261438/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label and Gaussian noise as input and produces synthetic RGB images at resolutions up to 256×256 (ImageNet-LT) or 64×64 (TinyImageNet-LT, Places-LT). During training, the noise estimation network is split into an encoder e(·) and decoder d(·); the encoder produces intermediate latents used for the contrastive loss, while the decoder produces noise estimates used for the alignment loss. Training requires two forward passes per sample (one conditional, one unconditional), increasing training time to ~1.3× the baseline, but inference latency is unchanged.

## Method

CCUA introduces two synergistic losses added to the standard DDPM/SiT training objective. First, an Unsupervised Contrastive Loss (UCL) is applied to unconditional latents using an InfoNCE formulation with negative samples only—each sample serves as its own positive and all other batch members as negatives—pushing unconditional representations apart to increase diversity regardless of class. Second, an Alignment Loss (AL) minimizes the squared distance between conditional and unconditional noise estimates, weighted linearly by t/T so that large timesteps (initial denoising steps) dominate, enabling knowledge transfer from data-rich head classes to tail classes. The key design choice is to diversify unconditional latents and then align conditional to unconditional, rather than directly applying contrastive pressure to conditional latents; this avoids the shortcut solution where inter-class variance is maximized at the cost of intra-class diversity. The framework is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both U-Net (DDPM) and Diffusion Transformer (SiT) backbones.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT 256×256 with SiT (900k steps), CCUA achieves FID 15.1 (vs. SiT baseline 19.9, a 4.8-point improvement), IS 153.1 (vs. 111.7), and FID_tail 22.5 (vs. 33.9, an 11.4-point improvement), demonstrating substantial gains in both overall quality and tail-class diversity. On TinyImageNet-LT 64×64 with DDPM, CCUA reaches FID 15.2 and FID_tail 30.4, outperforming the DDPM baseline (18.7 / 40.1) and approaching the balanced-data reference (15.7 / 25.6). On Places-LT 64×64, CCUA achieves FID 12.0 and FID_tail 20.8, again surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCUA consistently outperforms all six baselines on FID and FID_tail across every training step on ImageNet-LT 256×256. Against the SiT baseline, CCUA improves FID by 4.8 points (15.1 vs. 19.9) and FID_tail by 11.4 points (22.5 vs. 33.9) at 900k steps, while also achieving the highest IS (153.1 vs. 111.7) and Precision (75.7% vs. 70.3%). Compared to CBDM, CCUA wins on FID (15.1 vs. 19.4) and FID_tail (22.5 vs. 32.7), though CBDM achieves slightly higher Precision (72.5% vs. 75.7% — actually CCUA is higher). Against REPA, CCUA achieves lower FID (15.1 vs. 18.1) and substantially lower FID_tail (22.5 vs. 33.8), despite REPA having a higher IS score; REPA's advantage in IS does not translate to better tail-class quality. Versus the concurrent Dispersive Loss, CCUA improves FID by 4.6 points (15.1 vs. 19.7) and FID_tail by 11.6 points (22.5 vs. 34.1), confirming that implicit diversification via conditional-unconditional alignment is more effective than directly applying contrastive loss to conditional latents. On TinyImageNet-LT with DDPM, CCUA (FID 15.2, FID_tail 30.4) beats both DDPM (18.7 / 40.1) and OCLT (17.7 / 39.7), and on Places-LT it achieves the best FID (12.0) and FID_tail (20.8) over DDPM (13.9 / 23.7) and OCLT (13.0 / 22.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the conditional-unconditional alignment principle from long-tailed GANs (UTLO [20], Transitional-GAN [35]) to diffusion models, shifting the alignment from lower-resolution GAN features to large-timestep denoising steps where head and tail class images share low-frequency structure. It departs from concurrent Dispersive Loss [41], which applies InfoNCE contrastive regularization directly to conditional latents, by instead diversifying unconditional latents and implicitly transferring that diversity to conditional generation through an alignment loss. The unsupervised contrastive loss with negative samples only draws on the InfoNCE framework [27] but removes positive-pair augmentation, a design choice motivated by the need to maximize intra-class diversity rather than inter-class separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe (works on both U-Net DDPM and SiT/DiT) for improving tail-class generation quality with modest implementation overhead, making it accessible to practitioners training on real-world imbalanced datasets. The 1.3× training cost from dual forward passes (conditional + unconditional) is a non-trivial limitation for large-scale training, and the method's applicability to video or 3D diffusion remains unexplored. An open question is whether the linear timestep weighting in the alignment loss is optimal, and whether the synergy between UCL and AL generalizes to other forms of data imbalance beyond long-tailed class distributions.
