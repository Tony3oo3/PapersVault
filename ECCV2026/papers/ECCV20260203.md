## Problem

Low-resolution face recognition is hampered by a domain gap between high-resolution gallery images and severely degraded low-resolution probe images, where a single feed-forward network in standard transformers cannot simultaneously extract fine-grained identity cues from HR inputs and coarse structural cues from LR inputs. Fine-tuning pretrained encoders on LR data further triggers catastrophic forgetting, eroding the very HR features needed for gallery-side matching. No prior architecture explicitly addresses all three challenges—resolution-aware feature extraction, cross-domain generalization, and stable adaptation—within a unified design.

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-resolution controlled gallery face images alongside low-resolution probe inputs that may be single images or multi-frame surveillance video clips (e.g., BRIAR probe clips of 5,435 sequences, IJB-S surveillance footage). LR probes are typically 32×32 or smaller with extreme degradations (motion blur, occlusion, overexposure), while gallery images are full-resolution. The output is a fixed-dimensional face embedding used for 1:1 verification (TAR@FAR, TPIR@FPIR) and 1:N closed-set identification (Rank-1/5/10/20 retrieval), with video probes requiring per-frame embedding extraction followed by aggregation.

## Method

FaceMoE replaces the single FFN layer in each Swin-B transformer block with N=3 parallel two-layer MLP experts (GELU-activated, hidden dimension h) and a top-k router that, for each token, computes routing logits via a learned linear projection and selects the top k=2 experts, producing a convex combination of their outputs. This sparse routing causes experts to emergently specialize in distinct semantic facial regions—high-frequency edges/contours, low-frequency smooth areas, and landmark regions (eyes, nose)—enabling resolution-aware feature extraction where degraded LR tokens are routed to experts tuned for the regions that still retain identity cues. Training optimizes a composite loss of CosFace margin loss, a router z-loss (quadratic penalty on logit magnitude to prevent over-confident routing), and a load-balancing loss (jointly penalizing expert importance and token count to prevent collapse). The modular, sparsely-activated design yields 2.17× more parameters than standard Swin-B with only 1.66× more FLOPs, and during LR fine-tuning induces selective drift: some experts adapt to low-resolution patterns while others retain pretrained HR knowledge, mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On BRIAR Protocol 3.1 (the primary low-resolution benchmark), FaceMoE achieves 42.36%, 61.47%, and 81.27% TAR@FAR at 0.01%, 0.1%, and 1% FAR, respectively, outperforming the previous best method ProxyFusion (40.10/53.90/68.90) by 2.26/7.57/12.37 points. On IJB-S (Surveillance-to-Surveillance), FaceMoE reaches 14.85% TPIR@FPIR=1% and 44.81% Rank-1, surpassing PETALface (12.25% / 38.32%). On TinyFace, FaceMoE attains 76.18% Rank-1 retrieval while maintaining minimal performance drop on HR and mixed-quality datasets (LFW, CFP-FP, IJB-B, IJB-C, etc.), unlike standard finetuned models which suffer catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaceMoE outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest prior method ProxyFusion [18], FaceMoE gains +2.26 / +7.57 / +12.37 points in TAR@FAR on BRIAR and +2.60 / +6.49 / +4.53 in TPIR@FPIR / Rank-1 / Rank-5 on IJB-S. PETALface [39], the closest architectural competitor, is surpassed by +7.24 / +6.12 / +5.84 on BRIAR and by +2.60 / +6.49 / +4.62 on IJB-S. Traditional feature-aggregation methods (GAP [28], NAN [65], MCN [63], CAFace [23], CoNAN [17]) all trail FaceMoE by 5–10+ points on BRIAR, confirming that improving the feature encoder itself is more impactful than post-hoc frame selection. Notably, naively finetuning CosFace [59] on BRIAR causes catastrophic forgetting (TAR@FAR drops from 34.29 to 11.62 at 0.01% FAR), whereas FaceMoE's sparse MoE design avoids this collapse. On TinyFace, FaceMoE (76.18% Rank-1) edges out PETALface (75.45%) and all pretrained baselines (≤74.40%), while preserving HR performance—something ArcFace [4], CurricularFace [14], and AdaFace [22] fail to do after finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaceMoE adapts the sparsely-gated top-k Mixture of Experts paradigm (Shazeer et al., 2017; Riquelme et al., 2021) from language and general vision models into the transformer-based face recognition encoder, replacing the single FFN in each block with multiple specialized FFN experts. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant LR-FR line of work (CAFace, CoNAN, ProxyFusion) that improves feature *aggregation* over a fixed encoder, instead targeting the encoder itself to achieve resolution-aware feature extraction. It also contrasts with PETALface's quality-adaptive LoRA modules by using a modular, sparsely-activated expert structure that naturally induces selective drift during fine-tuning, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting without explicit regularization on the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By addressing the three core LR-FR challenges (degraded feature extraction, HR/LR domain gap, and catastrophic forgetting) within a single architectural change, FaceMoE offers a practical path to deploying transformer face recognizers in surveillance settings where probes are severely degraded. The 2.17× capacity gain at only 1.66× FLOPs makes the approach attractive for resource-constrained deployments. Open questions include whether the observed expert-to-semantic-region specialization is robust across architectures and scales, whether more than three experts or deeper MoE stacks yield further gains, and how the method behaves under even more extreme degradations (e.g., sub-16×16 faces) where identity cues are nearly absent.
