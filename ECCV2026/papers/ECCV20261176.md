## Problem

Vision-language models can describe urban scenes in rich detail but consistently fail to produce reliable human preference labels in domain-specific tasks such as safety assessment and aesthetic evaluation. The standard remedy—fine-tuning, LoRA, or RLHF—requires modifying model weights and large-scale domain-specific annotations. The paper asks whether a completely frozen VLM can be aligned with human preferences post-hoc, exploiting the observation that VLMs are strong concept extractors but poor decision calibrators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261176/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of street-view images and a perception category (e.g., safety, wealthy, boring) as input. CLIP ViT-L/14 extracts 768-d visual embeddings, while the frozen VLM (GPT-4o or Qwen2.5-VL-72B) generates continuous 1–10 scores on 5–10 auto-mined semantic dimensions per image. The output is a three-way pairwise preference label (left/right/equal) accompanied by per-dimension concept scores and a calibrated score difference, with per-pair LWRR weights providing interpretable attribution of which dimensions drove the decision.

## Method

UrbanAlign is a three-stage post-hoc pipeline that keeps the VLM entirely frozen. Stage 1 (concept mining) uses the VLM to discover 5–10 interpretable evaluation dimensions from high- and low-consensus exemplars (selected via TrueSkill ratings), then refines the dimension set through a temperature-scheduled search over 15 trials. Stage 2 (multi-agent structured scoring) chains an Observer (describes visual details without judgement), a Debater (argues both HIGH and LOW for each dimension), and a Judge (synthesises final 1–10 scores), reducing dimension-score variance by up to 3× compared to single-shot prompting. Stage 3 (local manifold calibration) applies locally-weighted ridge regression on a hybrid differential manifold that fuses L2-normalised CLIP differences with normalised semantic score differences (weighted by α=0.3); for each query pair, the K=20 nearest reference pairs are retrieved by cosine similarity, kernel-weighted, and a local ridge solution yields per-dimension weights that map the semantic differential to a calibrated TrueSkill score difference, with a statistical re-inference step producing the final label.

## Results

On Place Pulse 2.0, UrbanAlign achieves 70.8% average pairwise accuracy (κ = 0.41) across six urban perception categories, outperforming the strongest same-feature control (Global Ridge on Δ_sem: 61.2%) by +9.6 pp and zero-shot GPT-4o (56.7%) by +14.1 pp. The largest per-category gain is on safety (81.6%, κ = 0.63), where UrbanAlign matches the human split-half agreement ceiling (~81.8%). LWRR calibration alone contributes +9.9 pp over raw multi-agent scores (60.9% → 70.8%), with the biggest effects on safety (+18.1 pp) and depressing (+12.4 pp). Cross-dataset generalization is confirmed on SPECS (67.5% avg, beating zero-shot on 8/10 categories) and AVA (+4.1 pp over zero-shot at every difficulty tier).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UrbanAlign wins on all six categories against every baseline. Against the strongest same-feature control (Global Ridge trained on UrbanAlign's own Δ_sem), it leads by +9.6 pp on average, confirming that local calibration—not feature quality—is the key differentiator. The zero-shot GPT-4o baseline (56.7%) is outperformed by +14.1 pp, and the supervised CLIP Siamese (57.1%) by +13.7 pp. The SegFormer + CLIP regression baseline (58.2%) and Bradley-Terry logistic (59.7%) are both surpassed by 11–13 pp. Notably, on the 'boring' category UrbanAlign (65.3%) still beats the best baseline (Seg. Regression at 62.2%) by only +3.1 pp, reflecting the inherent difficulty of perceiving absence-of-stimulation signals. The ResNet-50 Siamese baseline (51.4%) is weakest overall, with near-chance performance on lively (45.7%) and wealthy (47.0%), underscoring the limitations of purely visual supervised approaches for subjective urban perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesises three established lines of work into a unified post-hoc pipeline: Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al. 2020; post-hoc CBMs by Yuksekgonul et al. 2023; label-free concept discovery by Oikarinen et al. 2023) provide the interpretable-dimension bottleneck; multi-agent debate and self-consistency (Du et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2023) supply the structured deliberation mechanism; and locally-weighted regression (Cleveland 1979; Ruppert & Wand 1994) is repurposed as a per-query calibration layer on a hybrid visual-semantic manifold. The key departure from prior VLM alignment work (RLHF, LoRA, vision-guided RL) is the explicit constraint of zero weight modification, and the departure from single-shot VLM scoring is the decomposition into Observer-Debater-Judge roles that separate observation, argumentation, and judgement. Unlike retrieval-augmented generation, the local fitting here operates on a differential manifold of pairwise comparisons rather than a document corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that domain-specific preference alignment becomes a lightweight, interpretable post-processing step rather than a retraining exercise, lowering the barrier for deploying VLMs in new urban or aesthetic domains. The finding that local calibration (LWRR) contributes +9.9 pp over the same semantic features used by a global ridge model suggests that the heterogeneity of human preference across the perceptual manifold is a first-order factor, not a minor correction. Open questions include whether the framework handles cross-cultural perception shifts where dimension relevance changes systematically, whether the interpretable weights can be used for bias auditing in planning contexts, and whether the approach extends to multi-image or video settings where pairwise comparison structure breaks down.
