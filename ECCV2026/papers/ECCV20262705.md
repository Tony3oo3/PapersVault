## Problem

Conventional one-hot output encodings produce overconfident predictions under adversarial attack, causing entropy- and probability-based detection methods to fail. While Hadamard-coded outputs improved robustness in image classification, they had never been properly applied to semantic segmentation (prior work fell far behind SOTA mIoU) or object detection, and no prior work exploited the intrinsic redundancy of Hadamard codewords as a perturbation-detection signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and produces, for each pixel (segmentation) or detected object (detection), a valid class probability vector obtained by projecting the network's L-dimensional soft codeword onto the probability simplex. Alongside the decoded probabilities, the decoder emits a per-position error vector quantifying how far the predicted soft codeword deviates from the valid Hadamard codeword space. These per-position quantities are aggregated into a single scalar anomaly score per image, which is compared against a learned threshold to flag adversarial or disturbed inputs.

## Method

HadamardNet replaces the standard one-hot output layer with S columns of a Sylvester Hadamard matrix (length L ≥ S), so the network predicts a soft codeword h_i ∈ [0,1]^L per pixel or object via sigmoid activations. The key decoding step formulates a linear equation system H·P_i = h_i + e_i and solves it by projecting the bipolar-transformed prediction onto the probability simplex (Michelot's algorithm), yielding both optimal class posteriors P_i* and a minimal error vector e_i* that measures codeword inconsistency. Three anomaly-score variants are built on e_i*: a simple L1-norm average, a regression model predicting expected error magnitude from posterior confidence and entropy, and a quantile model predicting the 85th-percentile error threshold. The regression and quantile detectors are trained exclusively on clean data in a second stage, requiring no adversarial examples. The entire pipeline is architecture- and task-agnostic, demonstrated on SegFormer, DeepLabv3+, Faster R-CNN, and DETR across five datasets.

## Results

For semantic segmentation on Cityscapes (SegFormer MiT-B0), the proposed quantile detector (19) achieves 80.2% global AuROC across all perturbations and strengths, statistically matching the three-pass Feature Squeezing baseline (80.7%) while requiring only 124 GFLOPs vs. 366 GFLOPs. For object detection on Pascal VOC (Faster R-CNN), the regression detector (18) achieves 74.4% global AuROC, significantly outperforming the multi-pass Feature Squeezing baseline (69.8%) and all single-pass baselines. Clean-data performance is preserved: mIoU is statistically equivalent (76.5% vs. 76.4% for SegFormer on Cityscapes) and AP is close (40.4% vs. 41.0% for Faster R-CNN on VOC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the multi-pass Feature Squeezing baseline (Xu et al. [43]), the proposed quantile detector matches its 80.7% global AuROC with 80.2% in a single pass (124 vs. 366 GFLOPs) for semantic segmentation, and the regression detector surpasses it by 4.6 points (74.4% vs. 69.8%) for object detection. The entropy baseline (Smith et al. [36]) and max-posterior baseline (Hendrycks et al. [13]) are consistently outperformed by 8–13 points in global AuROC across both tasks (e.g., 80.2% vs. 71.6%/71.4% on Cityscapes segmentation; 74.4% vs. 59.6%/61.2% on VOC detection). The FGSM-optimized detector of Maag et al. [27] is competitive only on FGSM (98.2% vs. 95.9%) but degrades severely on structurally different attacks like Metzen (48.0% vs. 75.9%), whereas the proposed method maintains balanced performance across all perturbation types. On clean data, Hadamard-coded outputs achieve statistically equivalent mIoU (76.5% vs. 76.4%) and near-equivalent AP (40.4% vs. 41.0%), confirming no meaningful task-performance trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hadamard-coded output representation line initiated by Hsu et al. for image classification and partially explored by Hoyos et al. for semantic segmentation, but departs fundamentally in its decoding: rather than the prior matrix-multiplication-plus-softmax approach, it derives a rigorous simplex-projection-based decoder that simultaneously yields valid class probabilities and an inconsistency (error) vector. No prior work exploited the intrinsic redundancy of Hadamard codewords as a detection signal, and no prior work applied Hadamard-coded outputs to object detection. The perturbation detection component builds on the broader uncertainty-based detection literature (entropy, max-posterior, energy score, feature squeezing) but replaces model-specific or multi-pass mechanisms with a single-pass, architecture-agnostic signal derived purely from codeword geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, single-pass perturbation detector that is model- and task-agnostic, requiring no adversarial training data and adding negligible FLOPs, which is directly relevant to safety-critical deployment in autonomous driving. The simplex-projection decoding is a clean mathematical contribution that resolves the previously inconsistent equation system and gives a principled inconsistency measure. Limitations include the quantile detector's failure on object detection (due to few objects per image), a small mIoU gap on BDD100K segmentation, and the fact that the regression detector's advantage over the simpler L1-norm score is dataset-dependent. Open questions include whether the redundancy signal generalizes to video, 3D perception, or more subtle natural corruptions beyond the tested perturbations.
