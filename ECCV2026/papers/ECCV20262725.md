## Problem

Human-pet interaction estimation and generation remain underexplored due to the absence of large-scale, high-quality datasets capturing synchronized multimodal signals from both agents. Existing animal datasets focus on single-animal settings without human interaction or audio, while human interaction datasets do not cover cross-species scenarios. Close-range human-animal interactions introduce severe cross-occlusion and require structured coordination that makes data collection particularly challenging.

## Input / Output

The model takes human body motion (SMPL/SMPL-X joint rotations and positions), human hand motion (MANO parameters), and MERT audio features as input, all tokenized into discrete representations via modality-specific VQ-VAE encoders. It produces a 3D dog motion sequence of J_p joint positions over 300 frames (~10 s at 30 fps), decoded from PetVAE tokens and combined with separately generated global root translations. The mapping is explicitly modeled as a one-to-many conditional distribution p(P|H, A) rather than a deterministic function, reflecting that the same human gesture can elicit multiple plausible pet responses.

## Method

InterPetMoGen (IPMG) is a 28M-parameter MotionGPT-style autoregressive transformer that generates discrete pet motion tokens conditioned on human motion and audio. A PetVAE (VQ-VAE with a bone-length regressor for skeletal consistency) learns a compact discrete latent space for dog motion, while human body and hand are tokenized as two separate streams to capture their distinct kinematic patterns. The key architectural contribution is Modality-Aware Attention (MAA): human tokens attend bidirectionally as global conditioning, while translation and pet tokens attend causally to preceding tokens only, enforcing a coarse-to-fine generation hierarchy (global context → trajectory → fine pose). Audio features from pretrained MERT are injected via cross-attention to provide temporal cues for interaction timing. Training proceeds in two stages: four VQ-VAEs are first trained (500 epochs) to tokenize each modality with a shared codebook of 512, then the autoregressive GPT is trained (300 epochs) to predict the next pet/translation token given the multi-modal context.

## Results

On the InterPet4D validation set, IPMG with Modality-Aware Attention (MAA) achieves the best FID_k of 11.21 and FID_s of 12.96, reducing kinetic FID by 47.2% over the Seq2Seq-Transformer baseline (21.22) and by 82.6% over the Diffusion-Transformer baseline (64.37). It also attains the highest retrieval precision, with R-Precision@3 of 0.63 for hand conditioning and 0.59 for body conditioning, and a diversity score of 5.93 (kinetic) / 6.01 (static). In the user study, IPMG scores 6.58/7 in naturality and 6.63/7 in overall quality, far exceeding the strongest baseline (Seq2Seq at 4.04 and 3.67 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Since no prior methods exist for human-to-pet gesture-to-motion generation, the paper compares against two generic motion-generation architectures: a Seq2Seq-Transformer with plain multi-head attention and a Diffusion-Transformer (DiT). IPMG with MAA decisively outperforms both: it reduces FID_k from 21.22 (Seq2Seq) to 11.21 (a 47.2% improvement) and from 64.37 (DiT) to 11.21 (an 82.6% improvement), while also achieving the highest hand and body R-Precision scores (0.63 and 0.59 at Top-3, versus 0.41/0.38 for Seq2Seq and 0.26/0.23 for DiT). The DiT baseline performs worst overall, suggesting that generic diffusion backbones struggle to model structured cross-species interactions without appropriate motion priors. Among IPMG variants, the full MAA mechanism provides the largest gain over the causal-MHA variant (FID_k 13.83→11.21), confirming that bidirectional conditioning on human tokens is critical for capturing the global interaction context that drives pet responses. The user study corroborates these quantitative findings, with IPMG scoring 6.58/7 in naturality versus 4.04 for Seq2Seq and 2.48 for DiT, with statistically significant differences (p<0.05 for all three criteria).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MotionGPT paradigm of autoregressive, token-based motion generation from the human-to-human setting to the cross-species (human-to-pet) domain, introducing a PetVAE tokenizer and a Modality-Aware Attention (MAA) mechanism to handle the morphological asymmetry between human and dog kinematics. It draws on the human interaction dataset lineage (InterHuman, BUDDI, GRAB) and animal pose estimation work (SMAL, DogMo, AnimalAvatar), but departs from both by being the first to jointly capture and model synchronized 3D motion of both agents with audio in a naturalistic interaction setting. The MAA module, which enforces a coarse-to-fine hierarchical attention pattern (bidirectional for human conditioning, causal for translation and pet tokens), is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior motion generation frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InterPet4D fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale, multimodal, 4D benchmark for human-pet interaction research, enabling structured study of cross-species behavioral dynamics that was previously impossible due to data scarcity. The practical impact extends to socially aware robotics, animation, and behavioral analysis, where understanding how pets respond to human gestures and voice is essential. Key open questions remain: whether the framework generalizes to other species (cats, birds), how to incorporate physical contact forces for petting interactions, and whether the 28M-parameter model can scale to longer, variable-length interactions. The fixed 10-second clip length and dog-only coverage limit immediate applicability but establish a clear foundation for future expansion.
