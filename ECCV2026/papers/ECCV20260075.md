## Problem

Event-based BOS tomography for general 3D flows remains unexplored: existing event-BOS work is limited to 2D displacement estimation, and frame-based BOS tomography requires high-speed cameras with bright illumination that are impractical for high-speed, multi-view acquisition. Two specific challenges make this difficult—event streams in BOS scenes are sparse and noisy (preventing reliable optical-flow-based displacement estimation), and deploying dense multi-view event-camera arrays is often infeasible, necessitating sparse-view reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams (per-pixel brightness-change events with microsecond timestamps) grouped into accumulated event images over arbitrary time windows, along with a single reference background image (captured by a co-located frame camera or recovered via brightness modulation). The output is a continuous 4D neural field mapping (x, t) → (ρ, u, p), from which the refractive-index field is derived via the Gladstone-Dale relation; the field is queried on an 80³ spatial grid at up to 1000 Hz for evaluation. Unlike frame-based BOS that pairs a reference and deflected image, the event input provides only temporal log-brightness differences, so the forward model must predict brightness *changes* rather than absolute intensities.

## Method

The method represents the dynamic fluid as a coordinate-based MLP (8 layers × 256 units, sinusoidal positional encodings with coarse-to-fine annealing) that outputs density, velocity, and pressure at any (x, t). Instead of estimating 2D displacements, it directly matches rendered log-brightness differences—computed via differentiable quasi-linear refractive ray tracing through the neural refractive-index field—to accumulated event images, using an adjoint-gradient formulation for memory-bounded backpropagation. A physics-informed loss penalizes residuals of mass conservation, momentum conservation (with buoyancy), and heat transfer under low-Mach thermal-convection assumptions, regularizing the ill-posed sparse-view inverse problem. The total objective combines this render loss with boundary conditions (ambient density at the volume boundary, optional inlet supervision), ℓ₁ total-variation on density gradients, and the physics loss, optimized with Adam for 80K iterations.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, the proposed event-based method achieves the best density reconstruction across all four thermal-convection scenarios in both single-view and dual-view settings. In the dual-view setup, it attains RMSE of 0.0149–0.0141 and PCC of 0.967–0.972, outperforming the 1000 fps frame-based baseline (RMSE 0.0143–0.0172, PCC 0.955–0.971) and the NeRIF* neural tomography baseline (RMSE 0.0133–0.0341, PCC 0.826–0.929). For optical flow, the reprojection-based flow from the reconstructed density achieves AEE of 0.044–0.102 with 0% outliers, substantially outperforming EBOS (AEE 0.467–0.739, %Out up to 25.5%) and matching the classical Farneback method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 1000 fps frame-based baseline, the event-based method wins on every scenario in both 1-view and 2-view settings, with the largest gains on the Heated air puff sequence (PCC 0.852 vs. 0.825 in 1-view; 0.917 vs. 0.899 in 2-view), confirming that event streams provide denser temporal supervision for high-speed dynamics. Against NeRIF* (the 4D-extended neural BOS tomography baseline), the proposed method achieves lower RMSE and higher PCC across all four dual-view scenarios, with the most pronounced advantage on the asymmetric Cold airflow sequence (RMSE 0.0141 vs. 0.0341; PCC 0.972 vs. 0.826), underscoring the benefit of physics-informed regularization for sparse-view reconstruction. For optical flow, the reprojection-based flow from the reconstructed density substantially outperforms EBOS on all three metrics (e.g., AEE 0.075 vs. 0.660 and %Out 0% vs. 20.4% on Hot airflow) while remaining comparable to the classical Farneback method (AEE 0.075 vs. 0.062), with the key advantage that it requires only a reference background image rather than synchronized intensity frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) frame-based BOS tomography using neural implicit fields and physics-informed regularization (NeRIF [23], Teh et al. [55], Li et al. [30]), (2) event-based BOS limited to 2D displacement estimation (Shiba et al. [51], Lyu et al. [33,34]), and (3) differentiable refractive ray tracing with adjoint gradients (Teh et al. [56]). The key departure is formulating the first event-based BOS tomography pipeline: rather than estimating 2D displacements from events and then running a separate tomographic solver, the authors jointly optimize a 4D neural density field by matching rendered log-brightness differences directly to accumulated event signals, with physics-informed Navier-Stokes/heat-transfer residuals as auxiliary supervision. The quasi-linear ray-tracing approximation and the event generation model (Eq. 6–8) are the critical bridges that make the event signal a valid supervision signal for an inverse-rendering objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enabling high-speed, time-resolved 3D airflow reconstruction under ambient lighting without the bandwidth and illumination demands of synchronized multi-view high-speed cameras, which is relevant to aerodynamic testing and HVAC diagnostics. However, the method is currently tailored to low-Mach thermal convection in air; generalizing to combustion, gas leaks, or higher-speed flows would require adapting the governing equations and scene priors. A fundamental open question is how to handle near-steady flows that produce extremely sparse event streams, and whether the absolute-density calibration via ambient boundary conditions remains reliable when the boundary is partially disturbed. The modest quantitative gap over 1000 fps frames (e.g., 0.0149 vs. 0.0172 RMSE in dual-view) suggests the advantage is more about practical feasibility (ambient light, low bandwidth) than raw reconstruction quality.
