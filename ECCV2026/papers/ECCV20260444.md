## Problem

Existing latent-action pretraining objectives for VLA models remain anchored to pixel-level variation rather than action-relevant state transitions, making them vulnerable to appearance bias, nuisance camera motion, and information leakage when future frames enter the predictor. This misalignment causes learned latent actions to degenerate into compressed frame-difference encoders that are temporally predictive yet weakly tied to controllable dynamics, resulting in brittle policies and inefficient fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB frames at the initial timestep (current observation only) and a language instruction, processed through a Qwen3-VL backbone augmented with learnable latent-action tokens. Future video frames are encoded by a frozen V-JEPA2 target encoder solely to construct latent supervision targets and are never fed into the VLM, enforcing a strict information barrier. The output is a continuous end-effector action trajectory over a horizon H, generated by a conditional flow-matching head that integrates a learned vector field from noise to data space, conditioned on the latent action representation.

## Method

VLA-JEPA introduces a leakage-free JEPA-style pretraining framework where a V-JEPA2 world-state encoder produces latent representations from video frames, and an autoregressive transformer world model with time-causal attention (bidirectional within each timestep, strictly causal across timesteps) predicts future latent states conditioned on current states and VLM-derived latent action tokens. The critical design choice is that future frames serve exclusively as prediction targets via the target encoder, while the student pathway (VLM + latent action tokens) sees only the current observation, eliminating the shortcut that causes latent-action collapse in prior methods. Training follows a streamlined two-stage recipe: first, JEPA alignment pretraining on action-free human videos (Something-Something-V2) and action-labeled robot data (DROID) with a combined world-modeling loss; second, action-head fine-tuning with a conditional flow-matching objective jointly optimized with the world-modeling loss (L = L_FM + β·L_WM). This replaces the multi-stage pipelines of prior latent-action methods with a single unified pretraining stage that learns action-relevant transition semantics in latent space without pixel reconstruction.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, VLA-JEPA achieves the highest average success rate of 97.2% across four task suites (Spatial 96.2%, Object 99.6%, Goal 97.2%, LIBERO-10 95.8%), surpassing OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and π0.5 (96.9%). On the LIBERO-Plus robustness benchmark, VLA-JEPA attains a total score of 78.1%, outperforming the next-best method (+ViT variant at 72.6%) and OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%) by a substantial margin, with particular strength under Language (85.4%), Background (93.6%), and Layout (85.1%) perturbations. On SimplerEnv, VLA-JEPA achieves 65.2% average on the Google Robot and 57.3% on the WidowX Robot, the best among methods trained on OXE subsets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLA-JEPA outperforms all 14 baselines on the LIBERO average (97.2%), edging out OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and π0.5 (96.9%) by narrow margins, while substantially surpassing latent-action methods like UniVLA (95.2%), villa-X (90.1%), and LAPA (65.7%). The advantage is most pronounced on LIBERO-Plus, where VLA-JEPA scores 78.1% total versus 69.6% for OpenVLA-OFT and 72.6% for the +ViT ablation, with particularly large gaps under Camera (+12.8 over +ViT), Noise (+18.3 over +ViT), and Layout (+1.3 over +ViT) perturbations. On SimplerEnv, VLA-JEPA leads all OXE-trained methods on the Google Robot (65.2% avg vs. 51.7% for RoboVLMs) and ties LAPA* (57.3%) on the WidowX Robot, though LAPA* benefits from in-distribution expert rollouts. In real-world Franka experiments, VLA-JEPA achieves the best success rate under both in-distribution and object-layout OOD settings, while ranking second on task OOD behind π0.5, though with notably more stable execution trajectories and fewer safety-boundary violations.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLA-JEPA builds directly on the JEPA family of latent-predictive architectures (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA2) by repurposing their core principle—predicting future representations in latent space rather than reconstructing pixels—for the VLA pretraining setting. It departs from the dominant latent-action pretraining line (LAPA, UniVLA, villa-X, Moto, CoMo, StaMo), which typically compresses frame-to-frame pixel differences into discrete or continuous motion tokens, by replacing that pixel-tethered objective with a leakage-free world-model alignment loss where future frames serve only as prediction targets. The use of a VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL) with learnable latent-action tokens and a flow-matching action head positions the work at the intersection of JEPA-style self-supervised video modeling and modern VLA architectures like π0, while simplifying the multi-stage training pipelines that prior latent-action methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode—information leakage and pixel-level shortcut learning—that has plagued latent-action pretraining, offering a clean architectural fix (future frames as targets only, never inputs) that practitioners can adopt without redesigning their entire pipeline. The finding that human-video pretraining primarily strengthens robustness and stability of existing skills rather than introducing new action capabilities reframes how the community should think about the role of action-free video in VLA training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more diverse embodiments and task families beyond table-top manipulation, whether the V-JEPA2 encoder can be replaced by lighter alternatives, and how the method behaves under truly novel physical dynamics absent from both human and robot training data.
