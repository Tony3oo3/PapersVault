## Problem

Caching-based diffusion acceleration requires deciding when to perform full recomputation (refresh), but existing schedules either use fixed intervals that ignore temporal sensitivity, rely on online local heuristics that miss accumulated segment-level degradation, or perform global optimization over all (timestep, length) pairs at O(T·L) offline cost. No prior method provides a lightweight continuous surrogate that captures the temporal structure of caching risk without exhaustive interval enumeration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (FLUX.1-dev, Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, or Qwen-Image), a text prompt, and a user-specified refresh budget B (number of full-computation steps). Offline, it requires only a small set of prompts (as few as 10) and a fixed probe length l' (default 5) to construct the risk profile. The output is a refresh schedule (set of timesteps for full forward passes) that, combined with a chosen reuse strategy (direct residual reuse or DCTA), produces the final generated image (1024×1024 or 1664×928) or video (480×832, 81 frames). The method is training-free and orthogonal to the specific feature-reuse formulation used between refreshes.

## Method

ERC exploits the empirical observation that ground-truth cache risk E(t,l) — the LPIPS between a cached output and full inference — correlates strongly with a cumulative surrogate H(t,l) = Σ r(ŝ) where r(t) = E(t, l') is measured at a single fixed probe length l'. This reduces schedule construction from O(T·L) to O(T): one evaluates r(t) once per timestep, computes the prefix-sum cumulative risk R(t), and partitions the cumulative risk axis into B−1 equal-mass segments. Refresh boundaries are placed at the timesteps where R(t) crosses each equal-mass threshold, automatically concentrating recomputation in high-risk regions and extending cache reuse in low-risk regions. The resulting schedule is model-agnostic and can be paired with any reuse strategy (direct residual copy, first-order extrapolation, or DCTA), and the entire offline profiling cost is a single O(T) forward pass per prompt rather than a full 2D risk map.

## Results

On FLUX.1 [dev] at B=26 (1.90× speedup), ERC achieves the best LPIPS (0.0816), SSIM (0.9442), and PSNR (31.53) among all methods, outperforming LeMiCa (0.0886/0.9354/30.10) by a clear margin. On Qwen-Image at B=25 (1.96×), ERC attains LPIPS 0.0653, SSIM 0.9711, PSNR 33.59, surpassing LeMiCa (0.1192/0.9490/31.58) by a wide gap. On Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B at B=25 (1.92×), ERC edges out LeMiCa on all four metrics (LPIPS 0.1133 vs 0.1146, SSIM 0.9357 vs 0.9345, PSNR 33.57 vs 33.54, VBench 74.64 vs 74.53). The key practical advantage is that ERC constructs its schedule in O(T) time from a single probing curve, versus O(T·L) for LeMiCa's full interval risk map.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LeMiCa (the strongest global-optimization baseline), ERC wins on all three metrics at B=26 on FLUX.1 (LPIPS 0.0816 vs 0.0886, SSIM 0.9442 vs 0.9354, PSNR 31.53 vs 30.10) and at B=25 on Qwen-Image (0.0653 vs 0.1192 LPIPS), while roughly tying at B=15 on FLUX.1 (ERC wins PSNR by 0.44 dB but loses LPIPS by 0.01) and at B=17 on Wan2.1 (LeMiCa slightly ahead on LPIPS/SSIM/PSNR, ERC slightly ahead on VBench). Against the online-heuristic baselines, ERC clearly dominates: at comparable speedup on FLUX.1, ERC (B=15) beats DiCache (LPIPS 0.1954 vs 0.2235), TeaCache (0.1954 vs 0.3548), and TaylorSeer (0.1954 vs 0.2088) on LPIPS. On Qwen-Image at B=17, ERC (LPIPS 0.1268) outperforms TaylorSeer (0.2055) by a wide margin and is competitive with DiCache (0.0978) at a lower latency. In the few-step (T=30) setting on Qwen-Image, ERC (B=15) achieves LPIPS 0.0694 and PSNR 34.48, beating both MagCache (0.0708/32.65) and DiCache (0.0794/32.48) at the same 1.95× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the training-free feature-caching line for diffusion model acceleration, which has evolved through three paradigms: fixed-interval reuse (DeepCache, δ-DiT), online heuristic triggering (TeaCache, DiCache, MagCache), and offline global optimization (LeMiCa). ERC departs from all three by reframing the scheduling problem as a 1D risk-allocation task: it exploits an empirically observed correlation between ground-truth segment-level cache risk and a cumulative sum of per-timestep probe errors, then places refresh boundaries via equal-mass partitioning on that cumulative axis. Conceptually, it repurposes the idea of cumulative error accumulation (implicit in LeMiCa's path formulation) into a lightweight surrogate that avoids constructing the full O(T·L) interval risk map, while remaining orthogonal to the specific feature-reuse rule (direct copy, Taylor extrapolation, DCTA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is the reduction of schedule-construction cost from O(T·L) to O(T), making adaptive caching schedules feasible for very long diffusion trajectories without expensive offline profiling; only ~10 prompts are needed to build a stable proxy. However, the method's gains over the strongest baseline (LeMiCa) are modest and sometimes reversed (e.g., LeMiCa wins on LPIPS at B=15 on FLUX.1), and the core correlation between surrogate and ground-truth risk is validated empirically on a handful of architectures without a theoretical guarantee. Open questions include whether the 1D cumulative surrogate remains valid for substantially different architectures (e.g., autoregressive video models, multi-scale cascades), whether the probe length l' can be made architecture-agnostic, and how the method interacts with emerging few-step distillation regimes where the temporal risk landscape may shift qualitatively.
