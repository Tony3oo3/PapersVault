## Problem

Video generation models frequently violate multiview geometry in nominally static scenes, producing non-rigid surface deformations and occlusion inconsistencies (hallucinated content in disoccluded regions), yet no existing metric jointly detects both artifact types with dense, interpretable localization. Sparse correspondence methods fail in occluded regions where occlusion inconsistency manifests, while semantic-feature-based metrics are insensitive to subtle surface deformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260929/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GeCo consumes a video clip processed in sliding windows of N frames, with off-the-shelf frozen estimators (UFM for optical flow, VGGT for depth and camera pose) providing per-frame geometry. It outputs three dense per-pixel inconsistency maps (motion, structure, fused) plus scalar frame-level and video-level scores; for the guidance application, it takes a text prompt and a frozen diffusion model (CogVideoX-5B) and produces geometrically consistent generated videos. Evaluation windows are resampled to a maximum of 8 FPS over ~3-second segments to enable fair cross-model comparison.

## Method

GeCo computes two complementary, scale-invariant cues per frame pair: a motion cue measuring the residual between dense optical flow and camera-induced rigid flow (sensitive to geometric deformation in co-visible regions), and a structure cue measuring depth reprojection error via 3D warping with z-buffering (sensitive to occlusion inconsistency where flow is unreliable). Both cues are normalized to dimensionless units via the pinhole camera model and fused with a co-visibility mask so that structure consistency dominates in occluded regions while both contribute in co-visible areas. Temporal aggregation over a sliding window yields per-frame error maps that localize artifacts spatially. Because all components are differentiable, GeCo also serves as a training-free inference-time guidance loss: during diffusion sampling, the latent is updated by backpropagating through the frozen decoder and GeCo pipeline, with latent slicing, recursive denoising, and time-travel re-noising for efficiency and stability.

## Results

On WarpBench (deformation), GeCo's motion cue achieves 64.90% AP and 44.48% IoU on CO3D-Warp and 87.12% AP / 52.36% IoU on ScanNet-Warp, substantially outperforming MEt3R (16.26% AP, negative SRCC) and WorldScore (48.70% AP, 29.56% IoU). On OccluBench (occlusion inconsistency), the fused GeCo metric reaches 83.48% AP, 69.83% IoU, and 81.74% F1, far exceeding MEt3R (46.51% AP, 48.57% F1) and WorldScore (5.36% AP, 10.61% F1). In the GeCo-Eval benchmark across 8 video models and 4 scenarios, LTX-Video achieves the best open-source fused score (0.026 on object-centric) while Veo 3.1 leads among commercial models on outdoor scenes (0.210 fused). GeCo-guided inference on CogVideoX-5B reduces the fused consistency score from 0.147 to 0.135 while slightly increasing motion (TM: 74.35% → 83.16%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeCo decisively outperforms all three baselines, but the pattern of wins reveals the complementary design. Against MEt3R (DUSt3R warping + DINO features), GeCo-Motion dominates on deformation (64.90% vs 16.26% AP on CO3D-Warp; MEt3R even shows negative SRCC, indicating anti-correlation with true deformation magnitude), while GeCo-Fused dominates on occlusion (83.48% vs 46.51% AP, 81.74% vs 48.57% F1). Against WorldScore (DROID-SLAM flow + reprojection error), GeCo wins on both tasks: on ScanNet-Warp deformation, GeCo-Motion reaches 87.12% AP vs WorldScore's 77.51%, and on OccluBench the gap is dramatic (83.48% vs 5.36% AP), because WorldScore's flow-based correspondences break down in occluded regions. TSED (SIFT + symmetric epipolar distance) is the weakest baseline, scoring only 36.78% and 38.46% anomaly-detection accuracy on CO3D-Warp and ScanNet-Warp respectively, and is excluded from localization tasks due to its sparse, non-dense output. The key finding is that no single baseline captures both artifact types: MEt3R handles occlusion but misses deformation, WorldScore handles scene deformation but fails on occlusion, and only GeCo's fused metric achieves strong performance on both simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeCo builds on the Frame Guidance [25] framework for training-free latent-space optimization (adopting its latent slicing, recursive denoising, and time-travel strategies) and repurposes off-the-shelf geometry estimators (VGGT for depth/pose, UFM for optical flow) in a novel dual-cue combination. Conceptually, it departs from prior geometric consistency metrics—MEt3R (semantic features via 3D warping), WorldScore (learned-flow reprojection), TSED (sparse epipolar constraints)—by fusing a motion residual cue and a depth reprojection cue into a single scale-invariant per-pixel map that simultaneously detects surface deformation and occlusion inconsistency, a gap the authors explicitly identify in Table 1. The paper also extends the evaluation scope beyond single-artifact detection to a unified benchmark (GeCo-Eval) spanning four scene categories and eight state-of-the-art video models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeCo provides the first dense, interpretable diagnostic that jointly localizes both geometric deformation and occlusion inconsistency in generated videos, filling a gap that limits downstream 3D asset creation and world-model development. The training-free guidance application is immediately practical for any video diffusion model, though the 10× generation-time overhead (2→19 min on H200) and the rigid-scene assumption (dynamic content triggers false positives) are significant practical constraints. Open questions include extending the metric to handle dynamic objects via motion masking, reducing guidance cost through efficient samplers or lightweight estimators, and whether the metric's signal quality improves as underlying geometry foundation models advance.
