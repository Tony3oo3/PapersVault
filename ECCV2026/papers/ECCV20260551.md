## Problem

Modern smartphones use heterogeneous multi-camera systems (wide + ultra-wide) where hardware differences in aperture, stabilization, and exposure cause systematic blur asymmetry between views, yet existing stereo restoration methods assume homogeneous cameras and symmetric degradation. No dedicated benchmark or method directly addresses hardware-induced asymmetric blur under the rectified stereo geometry provided by real mobile imaging pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260551/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of device-rectified RGB images at 720×1280: a motion-blurred ultra-wide frame and its synchronized sharp wide-view counterpart. The output is a single restored sharp ultra-wide image. Blur in the HSD benchmark is generated via temporal integration of consecutive ultra-wide frames (mimicking longer exposure), while the wide view at the same timestamp serves as the sharp reference; pairs with PSNR > 33 dB are filtered out to ensure non-trivial degradation.

## Method

PECA is a lightweight cross-view attention module that restricts correspondence search to a directional 1D epipolar window whose extent is bounded by an optics-derived disparity ceiling D_phys = ⌈f·B/d_eff / S⌉, where f·B/d_eff is the maximum physically feasible disparity from the stereo baseline and minimum focus distance. Within this window, cosine-similarity attention (with temperature τ) retrieves sharp reference features, and residual fusion injects them into the ultra-wide feature stream, acting as a soft confidence gate that emphasizes cross-view guidance where matches are reliable and falls back to self-deblurring in occluded or ambiguous regions. The module is architecture-agnostic and plugs into CNN (XYDeblur), Transformer (Restormer), and NAFNet backbones, reducing attention complexity from O((HW)²) to O(HW·D_max) with D_max ≪ W. Trained end-to-end with standard L1 loss under supervised learning, PECA consistently improves PSNR by 0.26–0.76 dB over naive stereo concatenation across all three backbones on the HSD benchmark.

## Results

On the HSD dataset, PECA-enhanced models achieve the best PSNR across all three backbones: 32.22 dB (XYDeblur), 32.47 dB (Restormer), and 32.92 dB (NAFNet), with corresponding SSIM values of 0.9620, 0.9636, and 0.9669. PECA adds only ~3G MACs to the attention module (2.9G for XYDeblur) while delivering +1.30 to +1.53 dB over single-image baselines and +0.26 to +0.68 dB over naive stereo concatenation. Parameter counts remain within ~10% of the stereo concatenation variant, confirming that gains come from structured cross-view retrieval rather than capacity scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PECA is evaluated as a plug-in module on three representative backbones (XYDeblur, Restormer, NAFNet), each compared against four variants: single-image baseline, channel-expansion (same backbone with more parameters), naive stereo concatenation, and PECA. PECA wins on every comparison: it outperforms the single-image baseline by +1.46 dB (XYDeblur), +1.53 dB (Restormer), and +0.76 dB (NAFNet) in PSNR. Against channel-expansion, which adds 56–124% more parameters, PECA still leads by +1.30 dB (XYDeblur), +1.30 dB (Restormer), and +0.64 dB (NAFNet), demonstrating that structured cross-view retrieval beats brute-force capacity scaling. Versus naive stereo concatenation, PECA gains +0.26 dB (XYDeblur), +0.64 dB (Restormer), and +0.68 dB (NAFNet) at nearly identical parameter and MAC budgets, confirming that explicit epipolar-constrained attention is critical over simple channel-wise fusion. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that PECA's constrained 1D window (2.9G MACs) outperforms full-row attention (7.6G MACs, +0.03 dB over global) by +1.47 dB, validating the physical disparity bound as the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem formulation at the intersection of stereo deblurring and heterogeneous multi-camera imaging. Prior stereo restoration work (stereo deblurring, stereo SR) assumes homogeneous cameras and symmetric degradation, while recent heterogeneous multi-camera studies rely on unrectified data and global dense correspondence. PECA repurposes the epipolar-restricted attention mechanism from stereo SR (PASSRnet, iPASSR, NAFSSR) but departs by adding an optics-derived disparity upper bound and a directional constraint, and by formulating the task asymmetrically (sharp reference → blurry target) rather than symmetrically. The HSD benchmark is the first dedicated dataset for rectified heterogeneous stereo deblurring under controlled hardware-induced blur asymmetry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HSD benchmark and PECA module provide the field with a practical, reproducible setup for studying asymmetric blur in the exact configuration found in commodity smartphones, a setting that prior benchmarks have not addressed. The physics-derived disparity bound is a clean, interpretable constraint that reduces attention complexity from O(HW²) to O(HW·D_max) without requiring learned matching or occlusion masks. Key limitations include the reliance on temporal integration to approximate motion blur (rather than real exposure-induced blur), the absence of rolling-shutter modeling, and evaluation on a single device family at 720×1280. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-rectified or video settings, other device ecosystems, and whether the disparity bound remains tight under aggressive autofocus or OIS-induced geometry changes.
