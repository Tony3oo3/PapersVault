## Problem

VLA models rely on frame-based RGB perception that degrades fundamentally at the sensing stage under extreme low light, motion blur, and black clipping—conditions where image enhancement cannot recover information already lost during capture. Event cameras naturally mitigate these degradations through asynchronous, high-dynamic-range sensing, but integrating their sparse, non-stationary streams into pretrained VLA architectures without disrupting the visual encoder's token distribution remains an open problem.

## Input / Output

The system consumes wrist-mounted RGB frames (346×260, 30 FPS, 10 ms exposure) and asynchronous event streams from a DAVIS346 camera with microsecond timestamps, alongside a language instruction and robot proprioceptive state. Events are windowed via a recent-count scheme (retaining the most recent 2000 events), accumulated into a polarity-agnostic dense grayscale frame, min-max normalized, and demosaiced to a 3-channel image-like representation matching SigLIP's input distribution. The model outputs normalized robot action chunks of size 40 at 30 Hz (≈1.33 s of actions per forward pass), executed in a streaming buffer.

## Method

E-VLA builds on a frozen SmolVLA backbone (SigLIP ViT encoder + LLM + action expert) and introduces two pretrained-compatible event fusion strategies that preserve the original visual token length. The first is a parameter-free overlay that replaces pixel values in the RGB frame with polarity-colored event markers before ViT encoding; the second is a hierarchical event adapter—a compact ViT with a 16×16 weight-sharing patch embedding and four transformer blocks—whose intermediate outputs are progressively fused (via MLP(Concat)) into SigLIP layers 3, 6, 9, and 12, adding only 13 M parameters (<3% of the model). A key design choice is recent-count event windowing (2000 events) rather than fixed-duration windows, which stabilizes perception under the highly non-stationary event rates induced by robot arm motion. Training uses a two-stage recipe (action expert first, then joint fine-tuning of adapter + action expert) with random image dropout to prevent shortcut learning, and the VLM backbone remains frozen throughout to preserve pretrained visual-language priors.

## Results

On the Pick-Place task under progressively reduced illumination, the E-VLA event adapter achieves 94.2% average success across 75–20 lux, compared to 70.0% for the best image-enhancement baseline (EvLight) and 47.5% for image-only. At the critical 20 lux (near black-clipping) condition, the event adapter maintains 90% success while image-only drops to 0% and all enhancement baselines fall to 5–10%. The parameter-free overlay fusion also substantially outperforms baselines, reaching 60% at 20 lux versus 10% for the best enhancement method. Under severe motion blur (1000 ms exposure), the event adapter improves Pick-Place from 0% (image-only) to 25% and Sorting from 5% to 32.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-VLA (event adapter) dominates all four baselines at every illumination level below 75 lux. At 20 lux, the event adapter achieves 90% success versus 10% for RetinexNet, Retinexformer, and EvLight, and 5% for E2VID — a gap of 80–85 percentage points. Even the parameter-free overlay fusion (80.8% average) outperforms the strongest enhancement baseline EvLight (70.0% average) by over 10 points, and surpasses E2VID (35.8%) by more than 45 points. At 30 lux, the event adapter reaches 90% while RetinexNet (80%), Retinexformer (75%), and EvLight (75%) trail by 5–15 points, and E2VID collapses to 10%. The key distinction is that enhancement methods (RetinexNet, Retinexformer, EvLight) operate on already-degraded frames and cannot recover information lost at capture time, whereas E2VID's event-to-image reconstruction introduces its own artifacts that degrade VLA performance (35.8% average, worst among all methods). E-VLA's advantage is most pronounced in the 25–20 lux regime where frames are nearly black-clipped, confirming that event streams provide actionable structural cues that frame-based imaging fundamentally cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SmolVLA lightweight VLA architecture by integrating asynchronous event-camera streams (DAVIS346) into the visual pipeline, departing from the frame-based RGB perception that underpins all prior VLA systems (RT-1, OpenVLA, π₀, etc.). Prior event-based robotics work (visual servoing, grasping, navigation) relied on modular, task-specific pipelines, whereas E-VLA embeds event cues directly into a pretrained, token-compatible VLA backbone via a parameter-free overlay or a 13M-parameter hierarchical adapter. The paper also contributes the first real-world synchronized RGB-event-action manipulation dataset, filling a gap left by existing event datasets that target perception tasks (deblurring, optical flow) rather than language-conditioned control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E-VLA provides the first systematic evidence that event-driven perception can be plugged into scalable VLA models without large-scale event pretraining, opening a practical path to robust manipulation under conditions (black clipping, severe motion blur) where frame-based imaging fundamentally fails. Key open questions remain: color-based discrimination still degrades because events encode intensity changes rather than absolute appearance, strong viewpoint occlusion (e.g., Stacking) is not mitigated by event sensing, and results are validated on a single 6-DoF platform with three tasks. Broader implications include whether event pretraining or multi-view event integration could close the remaining gaps, and whether the recent-count windowing insight generalizes to higher-DoF or faster manipulation regimes.
