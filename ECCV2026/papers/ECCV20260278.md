## Problem

Domain-incremental object detection requires a model to sequentially adapt to new visual domains while retaining performance on all previously seen ones, but existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (prompt-based, bias-based) operate in a shared parameter space where joint optimization corrupts previously learned feature statistics, causing catastrophic forgetting. The paper identifies that shallow modulation mechanisms (input prompts, additive biases) provide insufficient control over internal representations under significant domain shifts, and that even backbone fine-tuning, while offering adequate plasticity, inevitably overwrites domain-specific knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces bounding boxes with class labels via a ViTDet detector (ViT-Base backbone, ImageNet-1K pretrained). A critical constraint is the exemplar-free setting: at each incremental session, the model trains exclusively on the current domain's image-annotation pairs without any access to historical data, while the label space (e.g., 6 object categories) remains fixed across all domains. Inference requires no domain routing or branch selection—fused LoRA weights yield a single unified forward pass.

## Method

OKR incrementally injects a dedicated low-rank LoRA branch (rank 16) into each transformer block of the ViT feature extractor for every new domain, freezing all prior branches so that only the new LoRA parameters are updated; all branches are then linearly fused into a single weight matrix for unified inference without redundant forward passes. To prevent the new branch's gradients from distorting the historical subspace, a gradient-based orthogonal refreshing strategy approximates the composite prior subspace via SVD of the feature matrix (obtained by passing current-domain data through the model with previous LoRAs) and projects each gradient step onto the orthogonal complement, redirecting learning toward complementary directions. A topology-aware consistency loss further aligns confidence-weighted class prototypes of each new domain with those of the base domain using a softmax-weighted cosine similarity objective, preventing semantic fragmentation across independently optimized subspaces. The total trainable parameters increase by only 1.8%, and the SVD-based projection is computed once per session, adding negligible per-step overhead.

## Results

On the Pascal VOC series (4 style domains), OKR achieves 65.2% mAP at the final session with zero forgetting (ΔmAP = −0.0), outperforming the best exemplar-free method LDB+SOYO (59.6%) by +5.6% and the exemplar-based TP-DIOD-B (57.5%) by +7.7%. On the BDD100K series (3 driving scenes), OKR reaches 58.2% mAP at Session 3 (ΔmAP = −0.0), surpassing LDB+SOYO (51.7%) by +6.5%. On the challenging 16-domain VOC-Corruption series, OKR attains 61.0% average mAP at Session 5, exceeding LDB (50.7%) by +10.3%. Notably, OKR delivers a +17.6% mAP gain on the Comic domain specifically, where prior methods struggle most.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OKR outperforms all 11 baselines across every session and dataset. Against the strongest exemplar-free competitor LDB+SOYO [41], OKR gains +5.6% mAP on Pascal VOC (65.2 vs. 59.6) and +6.5% on BDD100K (58.2 vs. 51.7), while achieving zero forgetting (ΔmAP = −0.0) versus −5.6% and −6.5% respectively. Over LDB [33], the gains are +8.4% (VOC) and +7.1% (BDD100K). Prompt-based methods L2P [46] and S-Prompt [43] trail by +19.7% and +20.2% on VOC Session 4, respectively, due to their reliance on domain routing and shallow input-level modulation. Source-free adaptation baselines IRG [38] and MCC [19] suffer severe forgetting (−32.0% and −41.5% ΔmAP on VOC), while distillation-based LwF [22] and PASS [61] lose 12–15% mAP. Even the exemplar-based TP-DIOD-B [5] (150 samples/domain) is surpassed by +7.7% at VOC Session 4, confirming OKR's advantage without any stored data. On the 16-domain VOC-Corruption series, OKR leads LDB by +10.3% average mAP and achieves a markedly better stability–plasticity balance (Old: 59.3% vs. 53.9%; New: 66.5% vs. 41.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

OKR extends the PEFT-based DIOD paradigm established by LDB (frozen backbone + learnable domain biases) by replacing shallow bias/prompt modulation with LoRA-based low-rank subspace expansion, enabling intrinsic weight-level adaptation. The gradient-based orthogonal refreshing strategy is directly inspired by gradient projection memory for continual learning (Saha et al.), repurposing the insight that gradient updates lie in the span of inputs to project new-domain gradients onto the orthogonal complement of historical subspaces. Unlike prior prompt-based methods (L2P, S-Prompt) that require domain routing at inference, OKR exploits LoRA's linear additivity to fuse all domain branches into a single unified model, eliminating redundant forward passes and the need for explicit domain identification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-zero forgetting (ΔmAP = −0.0) while maintaining strong plasticity on new domains is a practically significant result for deployment scenarios like autonomous driving where models must adapt to new environments without degrading on previously seen ones. The approach is parameter-efficient (1.8% additional parameters) and inference-efficient (single forward pass, no domain routing), making it attractive for real-world continual adaptation. Open questions include how the SVD-based subspace approximation degrades as the number of accumulated domains grows large, whether the orthogonal constraint becomes overly restrictive in high-dimensional settings, and whether the framework generalizes beyond ViTDet to CNN-based detectors or other PEFT mechanisms.
