## Problem

CLIP-based ophthalmic models output only probability scores, leaving clinicians without visual evidence for why a prediction was made. Existing post-hoc explainability methods (Grad-CAM, attention rollout, gradient-based attributions) produce sparse, noisy, or semantically misaligned heatmaps in ophthalmic settings, failing to coherently highlight disease-relevant anatomical structures such as the optic disc/cup complex or retinal lesions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 ophthalmic image (fundus or OCT) and a target class text prompt as input, operating entirely post-hoc on a pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone without any model modification. The image is partitioned into 49 SLIC superpixels, and the output is a set of K selected superpixel regions with associated transport weights, rendered as a binary explanation heatmap. All region features are extracted in a single batched forward pass and cached, keeping the per-image runtime at approximately 5.28 seconds on a single GPU.

## Method

OTSMDL is a three-stage post-hoc region selection pipeline. First, SLIC superpixels partition the image into N=49 candidate regions. Second, a submodular-inspired scoring function evaluates each region along three axes—importance (region-text similarity plus a deletion-based contribution gap), contrast (margin over the most similar competing class), and uniqueness (squared L2 distance from the global feature mean)—with a diminishing-marginal-returns penalty to suppress visual redundancy, yielding an initial subset S₀. Third, and most critically, an Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) module performs global re-ranking: the submodular scores serve a dual role as both the initialization pool and the source mass distribution (a=score-guided, b=uniform over all N candidates), while a fused cost matrix combining visual feature discrepancy and text-semantic mismatch drives Sinkhorn iterations. The column-wise transported mass produces a global importance weight over all candidates, from which the top-K regions are extracted; this OT loop iterates until the selected set stabilizes. The use of unbalanced (rather than balanced) OT is motivated by the scarcity of diagnostic evidence in medical images, preventing mass from being diluted across irrelevant background regions.

## Results

On the three diabetic retinopathy datasets (IDRiD, APTOS 2019, EyePACS), OTSMDL achieves the best Insertion AUC (0.624, 0.484, 0.253) and second-best Deletion AUC (0.226, 0.232, 0.122) among 13 baselines. On OCT and glaucoma datasets, OTSMDL attains the best Insertion AUC on OCTDL (0.530) and PAPILA (0.725), and the best Deletion AUC on OCTDL (0.164) and OCTID (0.334). The paper reports statistically significant improvements (p<0.05) over all baselines in Insertion AUC on all ophthalmic datasets except OCTID. On mask-based localization (IDRiD, OIMHS), OTSMDL achieves the best mDice/mIoU at higher insertion ratios (e.g., 0.323 mDice / 0.200 mIoU at ratio 1.0 on IDRiD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTSMDL achieves the best Insertion AUC on all three DR datasets, surpassing the next-best methods (RISE at 0.583 on IDRiD, LibraGrad at 0.411 on APTOS, LeGrad at 0.245 on EyePACS) by margins of 0.041, 0.073, and 0.008 respectively. On Deletion AUC, OTSMDL is competitive with Less-is-More [7] (0.224 vs. 0.226 on IDRiD; 0.209 vs. 0.232 on APTOS; 0.113 vs. 0.122 on EyePACS), with Less-is-More slightly ahead on APTOS and EyePACS. On OCTDL and PAPILA, OTSMDL dominates both metrics (Insertion 0.530 vs. next-best 0.500 from RISE; 0.725 vs. 0.721 from LeGrad), while on OCTID it ranks third in Insertion (0.482 vs. Grad-ECLIP's 0.511 and Less-is-More's 0.506) but first in Deletion (0.334). Gradient-based methods (Grad-CAM, LeGrad, LibraGrad, Grad-ECLIP) and attention-propagation methods (Rollout, GAME) consistently underperform OTSMDL on Insertion AUC across all seven ophthalmic datasets, with gaps often exceeding 0.1. The closest overall competitor is Less-is-More [7], which shares the submodular scoring philosophy but relies on purely greedy selection without the OT-based global re-ranking that OTSMDL introduces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the submodular subset selection framework for explainable regions introduced by Chen et al. (Less-is-More, ICLR 2024) by replacing its greedy sequential search with a global re-ranking step based on Unbalanced Optimal Transport (Chizat et al., 2018). The key conceptual departure is the dual role assigned to submodular-inspired scores: rather than serving as the final selection criterion, they initialize both the candidate pool and the source mass distribution for OT, allowing all N superpixels to compete under a unified visual-textual cost matrix. The work also positions itself against the broader CLIP explainability literature (Grad-ECLIP, LeGrad, LibraGrad, MaskCLIP, CLIPSurgery, RISE, etc.), arguing that gradient-based and attention-propagation methods produce fragmented or semantically misaligned heatmaps when transferred from natural images to pathology-centric ophthalmic imaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Interpretable region-level evidence is critical for clinical trust in ophthalmic AI, where probability-only outputs are insufficient for patient and clinician confidence. The OT-based global re-ranking offers a principled mechanism to suppress background noise and enforce structural coherence that purely local scoring cannot achieve, and the unbalanced formulation is well-motivated by the sparsity of diagnostic cues in retinal images. However, the method remains post-hoc and region-level (superpixel granularity), and the paper acknowledges the need for finer-grained localization and adaptive region budgeting. Whether the submodular+OT combination generalizes beyond ophthalmic imaging to other fine-grained medical domains (e.g., histopathology, dermatology) remains an open question.
