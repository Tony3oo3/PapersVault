## Problem

Retrieval-based image geolocalization models can pinpoint a user's location from a single photo, raising serious privacy and safety concerns, yet robustness studies targeting these models are virtually non-existent. The paper addresses this gap by asking whether adversarial perturbations can be crafted to reliably mislead geolocalization models, and whether exploiting the inherent geographic structure of the task yields stronger attacks than generic gradient-based methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261284/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes an RGB query image and an attacker-chosen target GPS coordinate (sampled from a gallery with Haversine distance >2500 km from the ground-truth location) and produces an L∞-bounded perturbation δ (budget ε ∈ {2/255, 4/255}) that, when added to the image, redirects the geolocalization model's prediction toward the target. The perturbation is optimized against a surrogate retrieval model's gallery embeddings via cross-entropy loss, and the final output is the single best perturbation selected after N beam-search iterations. No access to the victim model's architecture or gallery is required in the black-box setting.

## Method

RoadTrip Attack (RTA) reframes adversarial geolocalization as finding an optimal sequence of intermediate geographic 'stops' between the ground-truth location and the adversarial target, rather than optimizing a single direct path. At each of N iterations, every candidate in the beam of size K samples J intermediate targets uniformly within a disk centered on the final target, whose radius is adaptively scaled by η·Haversine(current, target) (η=0.5). For each sampled intermediate target, a short PGD optimization (5 steps, step size ε/2) refines the perturbation, and the top-K candidates closest to the target by Haversine distance are retained for the next iteration. This beam-search-over-geography strategy acts as a form of 'geographic annealing,' navigating the non-convex feature space of contrastively-trained geolocalization models more effectively than direct PGD, multi-start, random-restart, or linearly-interpolated variants, while requiring only ~6 GB VRAM and parallelizable computation.

## Results

On Im2GPS3k attacking GeoCLIP with ε=2/255, RTA reduces ACC_GT@2500km to 1.03% (vs. 8.34% for PGD) while achieving 93.43% ACC_Target@1km (vs. 71.81% for PGD). At ε=4/255, RTA pushes ACC_Target@1km to 98.93% and ACC_GT@2500km to 0.73%. In black-box transfer to Img2Loc and G3, RTA at ε=2/255 achieves ACC_GT@2500km of 27.86% and 19.12% respectively, compared to 63.43% and 78.14% for GeoShield, while also producing lower LPIPS (0.027 vs. 0.047), indicating less visible perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTA dominates all three standard adversarial baselines (FGSM, PGD, Carlini-Wagner) on both Im2GPS3k and YFCC4k. Against PGD (the strongest baseline) at ε=2/255 on Im2GPS3k, RTA reduces ACC_GT@2500km from 8.34% to 1.03% (an 8× improvement in fooling) and raises ACC_Target@1km from 71.81% to 93.43% (+21.6 pp). FGSM is far weaker, achieving only 0.33% ACC_Target@1km and leaving 55.39% of images still correctly localized at 2500 km. Carlini-Wagner (c=1.0) is intermediate, reaching 81.95% ACC_Target@1km but still trailing RTA by over 11 pp. In the black-box setting, RTA substantially outperforms GeoShield (the only prior geolocalization-specific attack): on G3 at ε=2/255, RTA achieves 19.12% ACC_GT@2500km versus GeoShield's 78.14%, and on Img2Loc it reaches 27.86% versus 63.43%. RTA also produces visually less disruptive perturbations, with LPIPS of 0.027 versus GeoShield's 0.047 at ε=2/255, and the gap widens at higher budgets (0.174 vs. 0.305 at ε=16/255).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) as its inner optimization loop, but departs from standard adversarial attack formulations by introducing a beam search over geographically sampled intermediate targets. It positions itself against generic adversarial methods (FGSM, PGD, Carlini-Wagner) and the sole prior geolocalization-specific attack, GeoShield, which relies on feature disentanglement and scale-adaptive perturbations rather than exploiting the spatial structure of the problem. The key conceptual departure is reframing the adversarial search as a 'road trip' through intermediate locations, leveraging Haversine distance to adaptively scale the exploration radius, rather than treating geolocalization as a generic classification or retrieval task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As retrieval-based geolocalization models become widely available, the privacy and safety risks they pose to users are increasingly urgent, yet systematic adversarial robustness studies for these models were virtually absent before this work. The demonstrated black-box transferability (attacking via a surrogate model without access to the victim's architecture or gallery) makes the threat model realistic and the defense practically relevant. Open questions remain regarding robustness against purification defenses (JPEG compression, input transformations, generative reconstruction), which the authors explicitly flag as future work. The finding that geographic structure in the target space yields substantially stronger attacks than generic multi-path exploration suggests that domain-aware adversarial methods may be broadly applicable to other spatially-structured prediction tasks.
