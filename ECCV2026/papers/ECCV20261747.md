## Problem

In Ego-Exo proficiency estimation, naively adding more exocentric views degrades performance because redundant or noisy perspectives dilute discriminative motion cues, and the enlarged feature space causes the model to memorise view-specific patterns rather than learn generalisable representations. Existing multiview fusion methods rely on static weighting strategies that cannot suppress uninformative views, and general multimodal fusion approaches fail to handle the heterogeneous feature divergence and cross-view overfitting inherent to ego-exo data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes one synchronised egocentric video and up to four exocentric videos, each uniformly sampled to 384 frames and split into 8 consecutive-frame clips (48 queries per video), encoded by a frozen pretrained backbone (TimeSformer or Video Swin Transformer with Kinetics-400/600 or HowTo100M weights). The architecture is view-number agnostic via a permutation-invariant Transformer with shared learnable queries, so it generalises across different numbers of exocentric cameras. The output is a discrete proficiency label from an ordered set of K skill levels (4 on EgoExo4D, 5 on EgoExo-Fitness), predicted through an ordinal CORN classifier that decomposes the task into rank-consistent binary subtasks.

## Method

The framework combines two complementary modules. At the data level, AdaMVS (Adaptive Multiview Selector) uses a transformer-based score predictor to compute per-view importance weights, selects the Top-K most informative exocentric views (K=2 optimal), and fuses them via weighted token-level combination; training uses Gumbel-Softmax differentiable weighting while inference switches to soft fusion to retain subtle complementary cues, all under weak supervision from the task loss alone. At the feature level, the VIB-GB module couples two regularisers: Gradient Blending dynamically reweights ego and exo branch gradients using the Overfitting-to-Generalisation Ratio (OGR) computed from per-branch train-validation loss gaps, down-scaling branches that overfit faster; and a Variational Information Bottleneck constrains each branch's latent code to a unit Gaussian prior, compressing view-specific noise while preserving task-relevant information. The total loss combines CORN ordinal classification, the OGR-weighted gradient blending term, and the VIB KL term, trained end-to-end with Adam (lr 1e-4, batch 64, 100 epochs, dropout 0.5).

## Results

On Ego-Exo4D, AdaMVS with Kinetics-400 pretraining achieves 53.0% proficiency estimation accuracy in the Ego+Exos setting, a 5.5% absolute improvement over the previous best (SkillFormer, 47.5%). On EgoExo-Fitness, the method reaches 48.8% (K600) and 46.4% (K400) in Ego+Exos, outperforming the strongest baseline (TimeSformer + EgoVLPv2, 41.7%) by 7.1%. The method also leads in ego-only (52.1%) and exo-only (49.2%) settings on Ego-Exo4D, confirming that adaptive view selection benefits each modality independently. A lightweight AdaMVS-Small variant achieves 50.1% Ego+Exos accuracy with only 0.26 GFLOPs and 2.19M parameters, an over 11× reduction in computation versus the full model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaMVS outperforms all six compared baselines on both datasets in the Ego+Exos setting. Against SkillFormer (K600, 47.5% on Ego-Exo4D), the method gains 5.5% with K400 (53.0%) and 3.1% with K600 (50.6%); on EgoExo-Fitness the margin widens to 10.1% (48.8% vs. 38.7%). TimeSformer baselines, evaluated under five different pretraining configurations, peak at 43.7% (HowTo100M) on Ego-Exo4D and 41.7% (EgoVLPv2) on EgoExo-Fitness, trailing AdaMVS by 9.3% and 7.1% respectively. In the fusion-method comparison (Table 2), AdaMVS (53.0%) surpasses TokenFusion (49.0%), Concat-late (49.0%), CrossFusion (46.8%), MVCNN (46.6%), MVAD (45.0%), and MTV (43.5%) on Ego-Exo4D Ego+Exos, with the largest gap over MTV at 9.5%. Notably, several baselines (MTV, CrossFusion, MVAD) actually degrade when combining ego and exo views relative to their ego-only scores, whereas AdaMVS consistently improves with fusion, validating its redundancy-aware selection mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EgoExo proficiency estimation line initiated by EgoExo-4D and EgoExo-Fitness, which reframed Action Quality Assessment to incorporate egocentric motion cues alongside exocentric spatial context. It departs from prior multiview fusion approaches (static summation, concatenation, cross-attention) by introducing adaptive, token-level view selection (AdaMVS) that learns which exocentric views are informative without explicit view labels, and from prior overfitting mitigations by repurposing the Overfitting-to-Generalisation Ratio (OGR) from multimodal classification into a dynamic gradient-balancing mechanism coupled with a Variational Information Bottleneck. The method builds on TimeSformer/Video Swin Transformer backbones and CORN ordinal regression, but its core novelty is the joint data-level (view pruning) and feature-level (information compression) regularisation framework, which prior EgoExo work such as SkillFormer does not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnosis of why naively adding exocentric views degrades ego-exo proficiency estimation—redundancy and branch-imbalance overfitting co-occur—and offers a modular, interpretable solution that practitioners can adopt for other heterogeneous multiview tasks. The AdaMVS-Small variant's 11× reduction in FLOPs and parameters while retaining most accuracy makes the approach practical for deployment. Open questions remain around scalability to more than four exocentric views, the subjectivity of proficiency annotations limiting dataset size, and whether the OGR-based gradient balancing generalises to settings with more than two modalities or highly asynchronous views.
