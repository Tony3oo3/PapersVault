## Problem

Existing 3D-aware image editing methods either lack fine-grained multi-object control (Object 3DIT handles only single-object rigid transforms), entangle object and camera motion (Neural Assets), or require per-scene optimization (Image Sculpting). No prior approach achieves full-scene visual compositing with disentangled, 3D-grounded control over objects, camera, and background simultaneously while preserving photorealistic quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261924/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts one or more RGB images containing objects (at 384×512 to 512×512 resolution), 3D bounding boxes, camera parameters, and an optional background image. During layering, SAM2 masks, Grounding DINO boxes, and Depth Pro metric depth are fused to back-project 3D point clouds into Blender-editable meshes. The output is a single photorealistic composited image where the diffusion compositor fuses the noisy Blender target render with source context to correct reconstruction artifacts and fill in view-changed regions.

## Method

BlenderFusion decouples 3D control from image generation by using Blender as a symbolic editing engine and a diffusion model purely as a compositor. The compositor is a dual-stream extension of Stable Diffusion v2.1 where a single weight-sharing network processes source and target scenes in parallel with cross-stream self-attention; input channels are expanded from 4 to 15 to accommodate VAE-encoded Blender renders, instance masks, and Plücker camera embeddings, while per-object CLIP labels and 3D-box positional encodings serve as text tokens. Two training strategies enable disentangled control: source masking randomly drops objects from the source stream (p=0.5) so the model learns to ignore altered context, and simulated object jittering replaces the source image/camera with the target's while jittering object poses, forcing the model to rely on object pose differences rather than camera motion for object-level edits.

## Results

On the standard video-frame setup, BlenderFusion consistently outperforms both baselines across all three datasets. On MOVi-E, it achieves object-level PSNR of 18.90 (vs. 14.06 for Object 3DIT and 13.74 for Neural Assets) and frame-level FID of 9.11 (vs. 15.71 and 23.08). On Objectron, BlenderFusion reaches object DINO similarity of 0.959 and frame FID of 3.25, while on Waymo Open Dataset it attains object PSNR of 20.93 and frame PSNR of 24.11. In the user study, BlenderFusion is preferred over Neural Assets in 87.04% of overall comparisons, with the gap widening to 93.75% on fine-grained compositional tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlenderFusion outperforms both baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against Object 3DIT, the gains are most pronounced on MOVi-E (object PSNR +4.84, frame FID −6.60) and Objectron (object PSNR +2.18, frame FID −2.89), where 3DIT's single-object, rigid-transformation design struggles with multi-object scenes and disentangled control. Against Neural Assets, BlenderFusion leads by a wider margin on object-level metrics (e.g., object PSNR +2.22 on MOVi-E, +4.06 on WOD) and frame-level FID (9.11 vs. 23.08 on MOVi-E; 3.25 vs. 6.18 on Objectron), reflecting NA's lossy DINO encoding and foreground-background interference. On object fidelity (Table 3), BlenderFusion preserves identity better than Neural Assets (DINO 0.866 vs. 0.748 on Objectron; 0.891 vs. 0.840 on WOD). In the user study, BlenderFusion is preferred 87.04% of the time overall, with the advantage growing to 93.75% on fine-grained compositional editing where baselines fail most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

BlenderFusion builds on Stable Diffusion v2.1 as its generative backbone but departs fundamentally from prior 3D-aware editing methods (Object 3DIT, Neural Assets, Image Sculpting) by decoupling 3D geometric control from image synthesis: Blender handles all spatial manipulation while the diffusion model is reduced to a compositor that fuses renders with a background. Conceptually, it extends the procedural-generation-with-graphics-software line (BlenderAlchemy, SceneCraft) by replacing VLM-interpreted user intent with direct interactive manipulation, and it extends visual compositing from 2D layout-guided or token-based approaches to explicit 3D-grounded multi-object editing. The dual-stream architecture with source masking and simulated object jittering are novel training strategies designed to address the entanglement between object and camera motion inherent in video-based supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, end-to-end framework that makes 3D-aware visual compositing accessible without per-scene optimization, and the clean architectural principle—let a graphics engine handle geometry, let a diffusion model handle appearance—offers a reusable design pattern for future controllable generation systems. Key limitations remain: dependence on upstream 2.5D reconstruction quality, imperfect relighting and shadow generation, and limited pose diversity in real-world training data that necessitates synthetic pre-training for rotation control. Open questions include whether stronger 3D reconstruction models (e.g., feed-forward mesh generators) can eliminate the layering bottleneck, and whether explicit lighting-aware supervision can close the gap on physically accurate compositing.
