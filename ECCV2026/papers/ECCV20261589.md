## Problem

Conventional 3D human pose estimation methods optimize whole-body reconstruction in root-centric coordinates, which does not satisfy the practical demands of close-range human-robot interaction where robots need precise metric-scale localization of task-relevant body parts (e.g., wrists for handshaking, underarm for wheelchair transfers) directly in the camera coordinate frame. In close-range egocentric settings, severe truncation, occlusion, and limited field of view introduce depth ambiguities that disproportionately degrade accuracy on these task-critical regions, and no existing method combines task-aware attention with metric 3D keypoint localization in a unified VLM framework.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an egocentric RGB image (typically 720p from a robot-mounted camera), a natural-language interaction prompt (e.g., 'assist the person to stand from left'), and camera intrinsics. The focal length is unified to a fixed value of 1000 by resizing the image, and the principal point offset is simulated via random crop augmentation during training. Output is generated autoregressively as a sequence of tokens: first the names of task-relevant keypoints, then their 2D pixel coordinates, and finally their 3D positions encoded as voxel indices (0–999 per axis) within a predefined interaction cuboid, which are decoded back to metric-scale camera-space coordinates.

## Method

TAIHRI builds on Qwen3-VL (4B/2B) and discretizes the continuous 3D interaction volume into a 1000×1000×1000 voxel grid so that 3D localization reduces to next-token prediction. The key architectural insight is a two-step chain-of-thought reasoning process: the model first predicts 2D keypoint locations in the image plane, then reasons about depth to produce 3D voxel coordinates, making the inherently ambiguous monocular depth problem more tractable. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with a mixed prompt strategy (50% interaction-centric prompts from a 6,000-entry bank, 50% generic prompts) followed by reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) using GRPO with a pose-aware reward combining Huber loss and a PCK-style success indicator over visible joints. The model is trained on the newly constructed CloseHRI dataset (1M+ synthetic close-range egocentric images generated via SMPL-X rendering, SDXL synthesis, and SAM3/VitPose quality filtering) augmented with BEDLAM and PDhuman, and is evaluated using G-MPJPE in the global camera coordinate frame rather than root-relative coordinates.

## Results

On Harmony4D-Egocentric, TAIHRI achieves G-MPJPE of 93.83 mm (Upper), 114.98 mm (Lower), 107.81 mm (L-Upper), and 98.23 mm (R-Upper), outperforming the best baseline 3DB-DINOv3 by 24–35 mm across configurations. On EgoBody, TAIHRI reaches 75.77 mm (Upper), 101.58 mm (Lower), 81.42 mm (L-Upper), and 81.27 mm (R-Upper), again leading all competitors. In the VLM/foundation-model comparison (Table 2), TAIHRI achieves 97.2 mm G-MPJPE on 4 Upper-body keypoints, substantially beating the best depth-based pipeline (VP+DepthLM at 282.3 mm) and general VLMs (Gemini-2.5-Pro at 436.9 mm, Qwen3-VL-235B at 1298.3 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against global 3D pose estimation methods, TAIHRI wins on every configuration of both datasets: on Harmony4D it beats 3DB-DINOv3 (the strongest baseline) by 24–35 mm, and on EgoBody it beats PromptHMR (best on Upper) by 8.7 mm and 3DB-DINOv3 (best on Lower/L-Upper/R-Upper) by 6–12 mm. The gap is largest for distal keypoints (L-Upper, R-Upper), confirming the paper's claim that root-relative methods struggle with far-from-root joints in close-range views. Against general VLMs (Table 2), TAIHRI achieves 97.2 mm versus Gemini-2.5-Pro's 436.9 mm and Qwen3-VL-235B's 1298.3 mm; GPT-5.2 and Rex-Omni cannot produce 3D coordinates at all. Against depth-based pipelines, TAIHRI (97.2 mm) outperforms VP+DepthLM (282.3 mm) and VP+DepthAnything3 (352.2 mm) by roughly 3×, attributed to TAIHRI's joint modeling of body structure versus per-pixel depth estimation. The 2B-parameter variant (TAIHRI-2B) already surpasses all non-VLM baselines, indicating that the architecture and training strategy—not model scale—drive the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAIHRI sits at the intersection of two research threads: camera-coordinate 3D human pose estimation (CameraHMR, PromptHMR, SAM 3D Body), which recovers global human pose but optimizes for whole-body root-centric reconstruction, and VLM-based spatial grounding (Rex-Omni, DepthLM, DepthAnything3), which performs next-token prediction for 2D/3D localization but lacks articulated human structure. The paper departs from both by reframing 3D keypoint localization as a task-aware, selective token-prediction problem within a VLM, where interaction-centric language prompts direct attention to specific body parts in a discretized egocentric interaction volume rather than recovering the full skeleton. The two-stage 2D-then-3D reasoning pipeline draws on chain-of-thought prompting, while the GRPO-based reinforcement finetuning with a pose-aware reward is a novel application of RL to keypoint regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a compact VLM (4B parameters) can outperform specialized 3D pose estimation methods by a substantial margin on close-range egocentric benchmarks, suggesting that language-conditioned, task-aware perception is a viable paradigm for embodied HRI. The CloseHRI dataset (1M+ synthetic egocentric images) and the 6,000+ prompt bank provide reusable resources for the community. Open questions include whether the discretized interaction space (1000³ voxels) limits precision for sub-centimeter tasks, how the approach scales to multi-person or dynamic interaction sequences, and whether the reliance on known camera intrinsics and a fixed interaction volume constrains real-world deployment. The real-robot demonstration is encouraging but limited to two simple tasks, leaving open the question of robustness in unstructured, long-horizon interaction.
