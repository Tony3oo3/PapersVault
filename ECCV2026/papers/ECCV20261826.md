## Problem

Existing source-free object detection (SFOD) methods rely on computationally heavy backbones (Faster R-CNN, DETR) that prioritize accuracy at the expense of real-time throughput and model size, making them unsuitable for latency-constrained applications like autonomous driving and robotics. NMS-free dual-head detectors such as YOLOv10 are natural candidates for efficient SFOD, but vanilla mean-teacher self-training on them is suboptimal: the O2O and O2M heads produce complementary yet mismatched pseudo-labels, and domain shift collapses multi-scale feature discriminability in a way that standard self-training only partially recovers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB driving-scene images from the target domain (no source data or labels required) and produces a set of duplicate-free bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores via the O2O head alone at inference, preserving YOLOv10's end-to-end NMS-free pipeline with zero additional post-processing overhead. Both DHF and MARD operate exclusively during training-time adaptation; the deployed student is a standard YOLOv10 detector. Experiments use 640×640 input resolution on Cityscapes, Foggy Cityscapes, KITTI, Sim10k, and BDD100k benchmarks.

## Method

RT-SFOD builds a mean-teacher adaptation loop on YOLOv10 with two training-time modules. DHF (Dual-Head Pseudo-Label Fusion) treats high-confidence O2O predictions as precision anchors and selectively supplements them with non-redundant O2M candidates whose maximum IoU with any O2O anchor falls below a threshold, after class-wise NMS on the O2M extras, achieving near-O2O precision (0.919) while boosting recall to 0.525. MARD (Multi-scale Adaptive Representation Diversification) enforces per-channel variance lower-bounds and off-diagonal covariance penalties on PAN feature maps, with foreground vectors sampled inside pseudo-boxes and background vectors from non-box regions, each box assigned to a specific pyramid level by size to maintain scale consistency. An adaptive weight modulated by a linear warmup and batch-average pseudo-label confidence prevents over-regularization when labels are noisy. The total loss combines standard YOLOv10 detection loss (box, classification, distribution focal) over both student heads with the MARD term, and the teacher is updated via epoch-level EMA after AdaBN warm-start.

## Results

On the Cityscapes→Foggy Cityscapes (C2F) benchmark, RT-SFOD-L achieves 53.8 mAP, surpassing the previous best SFOD method SF-YOLO-L (51.6) by +2.2 mAP while running 1.3× faster (67 vs. 52 FPS) with 1.9× fewer parameters (24.4M vs. 46.5M). The mid-scale RT-SFOD-M reaches 47.4 mAP at 105 FPS with 15.4M parameters, outperforming all Faster R-CNN and Deformable DETR-based SFOD methods by 0.5–3.8 mAP at 3.6× higher throughput. On Sim10k→Cityscapes, RT-SFOD-L achieves 71.2 AP (vs. 69.8 for SF-YOLO-L), and on Cityscapes→BDD100k it reaches 46.5 mAP (vs. 43.0 for VFM-SFOD), demonstrating consistent 1.4–3.5 mAP gains across all four domain-shift scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RT-SFOD-L (53.8 mAP) outperforms all SFOD baselines on C2F: +2.2 over SF-YOLO-L (51.6), +6.7 over VFM-SFOD (47.1), +6.9 over FALCON-SFOD (46.9), and +8.9 over IRG (37.1). At matched scale, RT-SFOD-S (44.3 mAP, 7.2M params, 233 FPS) beats SF-YOLO-S (42.9 mAP, 7.2M, 172 FPS) by +1.4 mAP while being 1.4× faster, and matches FRANCK (44.9) within 0.6 mAP at 8× higher throughput. RT-SFOD-M (47.4 mAP) surpasses all four Faster R-CNN-based SFOD methods (IRG 37.1, PETS 35.9, Simple-SFOD 45.0, FALCON 46.9) and both Deformable DETR methods (DRU 43.6, FRANCK 44.9) by 0.5–10.3 mAP, while using 2.6× fewer parameters and 3.6× higher FPS than the Def-DETR group. On S2C, RT-SFOD-L (71.2) beats SF-YOLO-L (69.8) by +1.4 and VFM-SFOD (67.4) by +3.8; on K2C, RT-SFOD-L (60.9) trails SF-YOLO-L (63.7) by 2.8 but exceeds VFM-SFOD (54.7) by +6.2, with the gap attributed to SF-YOLO-L's 1.9× larger model. On C2B, RT-SFOD-L (46.5) leads all SFOD methods by +3.5 over VFM-SFOD (43.0) and +9.6 over FALCON-SFOD (36.9), and even RT-SFOD-S (41.1) exceeds FRANCK (40.7) at 8× higher speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the source-free object detection (SFOD) line of work, which has been dominated by Faster R-CNN-based methods (IRG, PETS, Simple-SFOD, FALCON-SFOD) and more recently DETR-based methods (DRU, FRANCK, VFM-SFOD), by shifting the backbone to YOLOv10, an NMS-free dual-head detector. It builds directly on the Mean-Teacher self-training paradigm and the AdaBN warm-start from Simple-SFOD, but identifies two challenges specific to dual-head architectures: the precision-recall mismatch between O2O and O2M pseudo-labels, and multi-scale feature rank collapse under domain-shift. The closest prior work, SF-YOLO, applied SFOD to a single-head NMS-based YOLOv5; this paper is the first to systematically address the pseudo-label fusion and representation degradation problems that arise in NMS-free dual-head settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time deployment scenarios (autonomous driving, surveillance, robotics) where latency and memory constraints are binding: RT-SFOD achieves competitive or superior mAP at 1.3× higher throughput and ~2× fewer parameters than prior SFOD methods. The observation that domain-shift collapses multi-scale feature effective rank, and that standard MT self-training recovers less than half of the lost diversity, is a previously unreported phenomenon that may inform adaptation strategies beyond SFOD. Open questions include whether the MARD regularization generalizes to non-PAN architectures, whether the efficiency advantage persists under FP16/TensorRT deployment (reported only in supplementary), and whether the approach extends to more extreme domain gaps or multi-target adaptation.
