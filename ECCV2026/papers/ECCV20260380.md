## Problem

Existing continual anomaly detection (CAD) methods are evaluated under unrealistic discrete task shifts, never compared head-to-head, and never profiled for edge deployment feasibility. The paper shows that under realistic continuous drift and unsupervised conditions, dedicated CAD methods (DNE, IUF, UCAD) do not consistently outperform traditional anomaly detection baselines augmented with simple experience replay, raising the question of whether their added architectural complexity is justified.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (processed at 224×224 to yield a 14×14 patch grid) and produces both an image-level anomaly score (the maximum over all patch scores) and a spatially-resolved pixel-level anomaly map where each of the 196 patch locations carries its own score. At inference, the image's CLS token is matched against stored task prototypes to route to the correct memory bank without any task label, and the per-patch scores are computed via nearest-neighbor distance within a local spatial neighborhood rather than a global search.

## Method

DINOSaur is a training-free method built on a frozen DINOv3 ViT-S/16 backbone (21M parameters), so no weights are ever updated and catastrophic forgetting is eliminated by construction. For each task, it constructs a spatially-indexed memory bank: at every (i,j) position in the 14×14 patch grid, a greedy coreset of M representative feature vectors is stored independently, preserving the spatial layout of the product. At inference, each test patch is scored only against memory features within a fixed spatial neighborhood of radius r (default r=3), enforcing spatial consistency and reducing per-patch comparisons by 4×. Continual learning is handled by maintaining a separate memory bank per task and routing new images to the correct bank via nearest-neighbor matching of the CLS token against per-task mean prototypes, requiring no replay, no gradient updates, and no task labels at test time.

## Results

On the discrete-task protocol, DINOSaur achieves 0.968 image-level AUROC on MVTec-AD and 0.768 on MVTec-LOCO, with zero forgetting (FM = 0.000) and 0.991 recall on MVTec-AD. On the continuous drift protocol, DINOSaur reaches 0.928 AUROC on color drift and 0.891 on blur drift, while all methods collapse to chance (~0.52) under geometric drift. On the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, DINOSaur runs at 91.6 ms per image (10.9 FPS) with 21.6 M parameters and 7.1 MB per-task storage, enabling on-device adaptation to a new task in under 30 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINOSaur outperforms all five baselines on every protocol with meaningful signal. Against the strongest baseline, PatchCore with experience replay, DINOSaur gains +4.3 AUROC points on MVTec-AD (0.968 vs. 0.925) and +2.5 on MVTec-LOCO (0.768 vs. 0.743), while also being 23% faster on the Jetson (91.6 ms vs. 119 ms) and requiring no replay buffer. Among the three dedicated CAD methods, UCAD is the only one with meaningful detection (0.798 AUROC on MVTec-AD), yet DINOSaur still leads by 17 points; DNE and IUF collapse to near-chance (0.500 and 0.508 AUROC) with zero or near-zero recall, revealing fundamental architectural limitations in the unsupervised setting. EfficientAD with replay also fails under continual learning (0.529 AUROC on MVTec-AD). On continuous drift, DINOSaur extends its lead over PatchCore by +9.2 points on color drift (0.928 vs. 0.836) and +5.9 on blur drift (0.891 vs. 0.832), while all methods—including DINOSaur—degrade to chance under geometric drift, indicating a shared representational limitation rather than a method-specific failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying the evaluation of three previously independent CAD methods (DNE, IUF, UCAD) under a single protocol, while also extending the task formulation from discrete category shifts to continuous within-product drift and adding logical anomaly evaluation via MVTec-LOCO. Methodologically, DINOSaur repurposes the frozen-backbone-plus-memory paradigm from PatchCore but introduces spatially-indexed coreset memory and neighborhood-restricted scoring, deliberately departing from the complex architectures (SAM, diffusion models, VLMs) that prior CAD work has adopted. The paper also introduces the first computational efficiency profiling of CAD methods on actual edge hardware (Jetson Orin Nano, Raspberry Pi 5), a dimension entirely absent from prior CAD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no dedicated CAD method consistently outperforms PatchCore with simple experience replay challenges the field's investment in specialized continual-learning mechanisms for anomaly detection, suggesting that strong frozen features plus nonparametric memory may be sufficient for most industrial scenarios. The continuous drift protocol reveals that geometric distortion is an unsolved problem where all methods collapse to chance, pointing toward the need for geometrically equivariant representations. Open questions include whether DINOSaur's local neighborhood scoring can be augmented with global reasoning for logical anomalies, and how the benchmark extends to truly online streaming without explicit task boundaries—both critical for real deployment.
