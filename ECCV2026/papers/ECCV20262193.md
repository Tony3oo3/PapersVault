## Problem

Uncalibrated volumetric video streaming for human reconstruction requires temporal consistency and computational efficiency from sparse-view inputs, yet existing methods either rely on per-scene iterative optimization, demand calibrated camera rigs, or are limited to 0.5K single-frame synthesis. No prior feed-forward approach simultaneously achieves high-resolution (2K) 360° human video reconstruction from uncalibrated sparse views in a streaming fashion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes four uncalibrated RGB video streams captured at 90° angular separation, with the AA Transformer backbone processing 518×518 frames while a supplementary EdgeNeXt pathway ingests the full 2072×2072 (2K) resolution. It outputs a temporally consistent 3D Gaussian Splatting volumetric video renderable at 2K from any 360° viewpoint at 3.01 FPS on a single RTX 4090, along with per-view camera parameters, per-pixel depth maps, and a foreground mask. The Gaussian count equals 4×H×W (one per pixel across all four views), and positions are obtained by back-projecting predicted depth through predicted camera intrinsics/extrinsics.

## Method

HiReFF builds on VGGT's Alternating-Attention Transformer backbone and AnySplat's DPT-based Gaussian head, introducing three key mechanisms. First, Scale-synchronized Camera Calibration resolves the metric-scale ambiguity of VGGT's predictions by computing a per-frame scale factor from the ratio of ground-truth to predicted translations, then rendering with rescaled ground-truth cameras while freezing the camera head and supervising it indirectly through the AA Transformer features. Second, Gaussian-wise Foreground Masking applies a learned mask head to modulate Gaussian parameters (opacity, scale, SH coefficients) rather than cropping input images, preserving camera estimation accuracy under the wide 90° baseline. Third, High-resolution Side-tuning keeps the backbone at 518×518 while an EdgeNeXt-based MLP extracts features from 2K images and injects them additively into the mid-level of the DPT Gaussian head, enabling 2K rendering with only 34% additional training VRAM and 8.6% inference speed loss over 0.5K input.

## Results

On the DNA-Rendering test set (20 sequences, 2072×2072 resolution, 4 uncalibrated 90°-spaced views), HiReFF achieves PSNR 26.51, SSIM 0.9164, and LPIPS 0.1277, outperforming all uncalibrated baselines across every metric. Compared to the closest uncalibrated competitor AnySplat (4 views, unaligned), HiReFF improves PSNR by +2.73 and reduces LPIPS by 0.046. Even against the calibrated 8-view GPS-Gaussian, HiReFF achieves higher PSNR (+0.41) and lower LPIPS (−0.011) while requiring only 4 uncalibrated views. At inference, the method renders 2K novel views at 3.01 FPS on a single RTX 4090 with 14 GB VRAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiReFF outperforms all four baselines on PSNR and LPIPS. Against AnySplat (the most directly comparable uncalibrated 4-view method), HiReFF gains +2.73 PSNR and −0.046 LPIPS without any post-hoc camera optimization, and still leads the camera-aligned AnySplat variant by +0.93 PSNR. Against NoPoSplat (2 views), the improvement is +3.08 PSNR over the aligned version, reflecting both the additional input views and the scale-synchronized calibration. Against 4DGT (monocular, 1 view), HiReFF leads by a large margin of +9.34 PSNR, confirming that multi-view geometry is critical. Against GPS-Gaussian (8 calibrated views, pre-segmented foreground), HiReFF achieves slightly higher PSNR (+0.41) and lower LPIPS (−0.011) despite using half as many views and no camera calibration; the authors note GPS-Gaussian's SSIM edge (0.9172 vs 0.9164) partly stems from its masked-input evaluation protocol rather than true reconstruction superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiReFF extends the feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting paradigm established by AnySplat (which itself builds on VGGT's Alternating-Attention Transformer) from single-frame, 0.5K-resolution synthesis to streaming 2K-resolution human video reconstruction. The key architectural departure is the High-resolution Side-tuning strategy, which borrows the additive side-network concept from Zhang et al. (ECCV 2020) to inject high-resolution image features into the Gaussian head while keeping the expensive backbone at 518×518. Two additional contributions—Scale-synchronized Camera Calibration and Gaussian-wise Foreground Masking—address the specific challenges of 90°-spaced uncalibrated views and foreground-only human reconstruction, respectively, which prior feed-forward methods (AnySplat, NoPoSplat) did not handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a practical gap for holographic communication, AR/VR, and live sports broadcasting by demonstrating that 2K-resolution 360° human video can be reconstructed in a streaming fashion (3 FPS on a single RTX 4090) from just four uncalibrated views, without per-scene optimization or calibrated rigs. The side-tuning approach for high-resolution rendering is a clean, generalizable technique that could transfer to other feed-forward 3DGS pipelines. The main limitation is isolated floating points in regions occluded from all but one input view, which the authors plan to address with human-specific priors (hands, faces). An open question is whether the scale-synchronized calibration strategy generalizes to even sparser or more irregularly spaced view configurations.
