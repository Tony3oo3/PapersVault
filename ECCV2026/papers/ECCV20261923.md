## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods model motion with piecewise linear velocities and short temporal opacity windows, causing Gaussian primitives to drift from true curved trajectories and forcing repeated elimination and regeneration. This temporal fragmentation leads to unbounded memory growth and prevents scaling to extended video sequences beyond a few hundred frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261923/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view video (22–24 cameras at 60 FPS on SelfCap at 3840×2160, or 30 FPS on N3V at 2704×2028) along with a RoMa-derived point cloud for initialization. It produces a fixed-budget (0.5M primitives, ~160 MB) time-varying 3D Gaussian representation that supports real-time novel-view rendering at over 110 FPS, maintaining constant memory regardless of sequence length (600–1200 frames).

## Method

TRiGS replaces fragmented linear motion with three coupled components: (1) a rigid-body parameterization in the Lie algebra se(3) mapped to SE(3) via the exponential map, so rotation and translation are jointly determined rather than independently optimized; (2) a hierarchical decomposition where a learnable base motion is refined by a quadratic Bézier residual localized to a visibility-driven temporal window, capturing nonlinear dynamics without primitive proliferation; and (3) a per-primitive local anchor with gauge fixing (projecting the anchor onto the plane orthogonal to the rotation axis) that resolves the unidentifiability of the rotation center and enables independent local rigid motions. A motion-guided relocation strategy periodically recycles low-opacity Gaussians by cloning high-difficulty active primitives, keeping the total count fixed while reallocating capacity to complex dynamic regions.

## Results

On the SelfCap dataset extended to 1200 frames, TRiGS achieves 26.05 PSNR, 0.904 SSIM, and 0.099 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline FTGS (25.41 PSNR, 0.871 SSIM, 0.146 LPIPS) while maintaining a constant 0.5M Gaussian budget and 160 MB memory. On the standard N3V benchmark (~300 frames), TRiGS reaches 33.36 PSNR and 0.031 LPIPS, surpassing FTGS (32.97 PSNR, 0.043 LPIPS) and all other baselines. Critically, TRiGS's PSNR degrades only 0.62 dB from 600 to 1200 frames, whereas FTGS drops 0.82 dB and its memory footprint doubles from 490 MB to 977 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRiGS outperforms all 16 compared baselines on both SelfCap and N3V. On SelfCap 1200 frames, TRiGS leads FTGS by 0.64 dB PSNR and 0.047 LPIPS, and leads GIFStream by 1.78 dB PSNR; the gap widens at longer sequences as FTGS's PSNR degrades from 26.23 to 25.41 while TRiGS stays above 26.0. On N3V, TRiGS achieves 33.36 PSNR vs. FTGS's 32.97 and LocalDyGS's 32.28, with the lowest LPIPS (0.031 vs. 0.043 for FTGS and LocalDyGS). In efficiency, TRiGS uses only 0.5M Gaussians and 160 MB at 1200 frames, compared to FTGS's 4M Gaussians and 977 MB, and GIFStream's 11.53M Gaussians and 2825 MB, while maintaining the highest FPS (120). The closest competitor is FTGS, which is competitive at 600 frames (26.23 vs. 26.67 PSNR) but suffers progressive quality loss and memory bloat as sequences extend, confirming TRiGS's advantage in temporal stability and scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRiGS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting's primitive representation and FreeTimeGS's temporal opacity and regularization framework, but departs fundamentally from the piecewise linear velocity modeling that dominates explicit 4DGS methods. Rather than treating rotation and translation as independent per-primitive parameters (or approximating motion with short linear segments), it couples them through a Lie algebra exponential map and introduces per-primitive local anchors to resolve the ambiguity of a shared global rotation center. The hierarchical Bezier residual and motion-guided relocation strategy further distinguish it from both implicit deformation-field approaches (which accumulate drift) and prior explicit methods (which proliferate primitives to patch trajectory errors).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical scalability bottleneck in 4DGS: existing explicit methods see their Gaussian count and memory roughly double when extending from 600 to 1200 frames, while TRiGS holds a constant 0.5M primitives and 160 MB across all budgets. This makes long-sequence dynamic reconstruction (e.g., multi-minute video) feasible on a single GPU, which prior methods could not achieve without streaming or quality degradation. Open questions include whether the rigid-body assumption generalizes to highly non-rigid deformations (cloth, fluids), how the method behaves with many independently articulated objects, and whether the fixed-budget relocation strategy can be made adaptive to scene complexity.
