## Problem

Ultra-high-resolution remote sensing imagery generates extremely long visual token sequences in MLLMs, creating prohibitive inference costs. Existing token compression methods apply static, uniform strategies that ignore a key property of remote sensing tasks: a semantic-geometric duality where object-semantic tasks benefit from aggressive background pruning while scene-geometric tasks require preserving spatial topology and connectivity. No prior method adapts what to compress versus preserve based on task intent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260091/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a UHR remote sensing image (processed via AnyRes-style multi-cropping into a dense patch grid of up to ~14k visual tokens) and a free-form text instruction. It produces a compressed visual token sequence (reduced to ~6.4k tokens at 42.4× compression) that is directly concatenated into the host MLLM's input without any additional projection or normalization layers, after which the MLLM generates a standard text response. The entire compression pipeline is plug-and-play: the host MLLM weights remain untouched, and the only learned component is a ~1M-parameter router frozen at inference.

## Method

DualComp decomposes visual token compression into two task-adaptive streams controlled by a lightweight duality-aware router (a parasitic MLP on the host MLLM's text embeddings) that predicts a duality factor λ and an overall compression ratio ρ from the instruction. The semantic stream (SCSA) performs size-adaptive local clustering using frozen CLIP features with a λ-dependent threshold, scores clusters via [CLS]-to-patch ViT attention, and applies a size-aware representation strategy that retains individual tokens for small clusters (protecting small objects) while using attention-weighted averages for large background clusters. The geometric stream (IGSR) extracts structural anchors from local feature differences, modulates them with a text-aware structural modulation signal (TASM) computed via CLIP text-vision similarity, and reconstructs spatial connectivity through a fully parallel greedy path-tracing algorithm constrained by decreasing Chebyshev distance. The two streams are fused via λ-weighted concatenation and a topological sequence unrolling step that orders geometric tokens by spatial connectivity so the host LLM's native positional encoding (e.g., RoPE) can capture 2D continuity. All compression modules are parameter-free and training-free; only the router is pretrained offline using a dual-perspective annotation scheme (LLM Likert-scale probing plus expert linguistic rules) and then frozen.

## Results

On XLRS-Bench, DualComp achieves 53.1% average accuracy at 42.4× compression, outperforming the uncompressed GeoLLaVA-8K baseline (51.5% at 24×) by +1.6 points while halving the visual token volume (6.4k vs. 13.8k) and LLM FLOPs (99.8 vs. 198.1 TFLOPs). End-to-end inference drops from 8.15 s/image to 3.87 s/image. On geometry-sensitive subtasks, Route Planning improves from 66% to 72% and Anomaly Detection from 67% to 69%, while on semantic-dominant subtasks, Regional Counting rises from 38% to 45% and Object Color from 31.6% to 34.0%. When transferred to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, DualComp at 10.24× compression reaches 47.9% vs. 46.9% for VisionZip at 10×, confirming generality beyond the remote-sensing-specific backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualComp (53.1%) outperforms all three generic token-compression baselines attached to GeoLLaVA-8K: VisionZip (42.8%, −10.3 pts), FastV (47.1%, −6.0 pts), and SparseVLM (41.2%, −11.9 pts), all of which operate at a fixed 24× ratio. Against the uncompressed GeoLLaVA-8K host (51.5%), DualComp gains +1.6 points while compressing 42.4× instead of 24×, indicating that task-adaptive scheduling preserves more useful evidence than no compression at all. DualComp also surpasses the strongest general-purpose MLLMs in the table—GPT-5.2 (47.5%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (50.2%)—by 5.6 and 2.9 points respectively, despite using a much smaller remote-sensing-specific backbone. The largest per-subtask gains appear on geometry-dominant tasks (Route Planning +6 pts, Anomaly Detection +2 pts over GeoLLaVA-8K), consistent with the paper's semantic-geometric duality hypothesis. In the transferability experiment on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, DualComp at 10.24× (47.9%) beats VisionZip at the same 10× ratio (46.9%) and even VisionZip at a looser 1.43× (47.5%), confirming that the advantage stems from semantic-geometric scheduling rather than a milder compression setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in visual token compression—treating token importance as a single-dimensional estimation problem (via attention scores, CLIP similarity, or cross-modal relevance)—by introducing a task-adaptive dual-stream decomposition grounded in the observed semantic-geometric duality of UHR remote sensing tasks. Conceptually, it builds on training-free compression techniques (ToMe-style clustering, [CLS]-attention scoring, CLIP-based similarity) and instruction-aware selection (SparseVLM, VisionTrim), but reorganizes them into two specialized pathways (SCSA for semantic aggregation, IGSR for geometric topology recovery) coordinated by a lightweight router. The greedy path-tracing topology completion mechanism is a novel component not present in prior compression literature, and the parasitic router design (attached to text embeddings, ~1M params, frozen at inference) distinguishes it from heavier instruction-conditioned compression approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic-geometric duality observation provides a useful organizing principle for the remote sensing MLLM community, suggesting that a single compression policy is fundamentally mismatched to heterogeneous task demands in UHR settings. The plug-and-play, training-free nature of the compression modules (with only a small offline-trained router) makes the approach practically deployable without retraining host models. However, the evaluation is concentrated on a single benchmark (XLRS-Bench) and primarily one backbone (GeoLLaVA-8K), with the Qwen2.5-VL transfer showing only a modest +0.5% gain, leaving open whether the duality framing generalizes to other domains (e.g., medical imaging, autonomous driving) where similar foreground-background tension exists. The router's reliance on a dual-perspective annotation pipeline (LLM Likert probing + linguistic rules) for supervision also raises questions about robustness to out-of-distribution instructions.
