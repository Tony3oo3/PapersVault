## Problem

Existing autoregressive point cloud generators rely on heuristic tokenization strategies—spatial coordinate sorting, space-filling curves, or stochastic downsampling—that disrupt the intrinsic manifold topology of unstructured point sets, producing fragmented and structurally incoherent shapes. The paper asks whether point cloud tokenization can be reformulated as a principled structural decomposition that explicitly preserves graph connectivity and topological boundaries throughout the generation process.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2,048-point 3D point cloud (globally normalized to [-1, 1]) as input, optionally conditioned on a class label for multi-category generation, or a sparse 1,024-point cloud for the upsampling task. It produces a 2,048-point 3D point cloud as output. Internally, the input is encoded via a sparse convolutional encoder into latent features of dimension D, which are then decomposed across T scale levels into a non-balanced binary tree of structural labels paired with quantized codebook entries; the final shape is decoded by an MLP from the accumulated quantized features across all scales.

## Method

PointRSP introduces a Topology-Aware Partitioning Autoencoder that decomposes point clouds into a non-balanced binary tree via a hybrid recursive spectral partitioning strategy: a bottom-up greedy merge stage captures fine-grained local details by iteratively merging the most similar token pairs, followed by a top-down spectral bisection stage that recursively splits the merged tokens using Fiedler vectors of a nearest-neighbor graph Laplacian, preserving global manifold topology without forcing uniform splits. A Dual-Stream Autoregressive Generator then synthesizes shapes in this latent space through a cascaded architecture where a structural predictor models the binary branching decisions of the tessellation tree and a feature predictor generates residual coordinates conditioned on the evolving structure, both built on self-attention with RoPE. To resolve cold-start instability in early cascaded steps where reconstructed coordinates are unreliable, a Geometry-Calibrated Positional Encoding anchors latent embeddings by blending raw coordinates with multi-scale structural group centers (weighted exponentially toward finer scales), transitioning to pure coordinate-based encoding once generation passes the spectral split horizon.

## Results

On ShapeNetV2 single-category generation (Table 1), PointRSP reports mean 1-NNA of 58.67% (CD) and 56.86% (EMD) across airplane, chair, and car. The paper claims state-of-the-art performance, yet the reported numbers are the lowest among all 11 compared methods; the strongest baseline 1-GAN achieves 74.12% (CD) and 88.86% (EMD) mean, while the closest autoregressive competitor PointNSP reaches 60.88% (CD) and 57.67% (EMD). In multi-class generation (Table 2), PointGPT leads with 94.94% (CD) on airplane, whereas PointRSP reports 76.52%. For 2× upsampling, TIGER attains the best airplane CD score (71.65%) versus PointRSP's 67.29%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Despite the paper's repeated claims of state-of-the-art performance, the numbers in Table 1 show PointRSP with the lowest mean 1-NNA (58.67% CD / 56.86% EMD) among all 11 compared methods, trailing the closest autoregressive baseline PointNSP by ~2.2% (CD) and ~0.8% (EMD) on the mean. The strongest overall method is 1-GAN (74.12% CD / 88.86% EMD), followed by PointGrow (70.96% / 68.77%) and CanonicalVAE (68.72% / 66.29%) among autoregressive models. In the multi-class setting (Table 2), PointGPT dominates with 94.94% CD on airplane and 89.35% on car, while PointRSP reports 76.52% and 58.39% respectively. For 2× upsampling, TIGER leads on airplane (71.65% CD) and chair (52.80% CD), with PointRSP trailing by 4.4 and 0.75 points respectively. The ablation (Table 3) shows progressive improvement from the PointNSP-based baseline (53.66% CD) to the full model (51.47% CD), but the absolute gap to the strongest baselines remains substantial, and the paper does not provide a direct explanation for the discrepancy between its SOTA claims and the reported figures.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointRSP extends the next-scale prediction autoregressive paradigm introduced by PointNSP and VAR, but replaces their heuristic or stochastic subset sampling with a deterministic, topology-preserving tessellation via hybrid recursive spectral partitioning. It directly modifies NVG's balanced binary sequence matrix quantizer, relaxing the forced uniform bisection into a non-balanced binary tree guided by graph-Laplacian Fiedler vectors. The dual-stream cascaded generator (structural predictor + feature predictor) departs from the sequential token-by-token AR used in prior point cloud models, instead adopting a hierarchical coarse-to-fine strategy analogous to cascaded image generation, with a novel geometry-calibrated positional encoding to handle the absence of regular grid indices in unstructured point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and persistent problem in autoregressive 3D generation: how to impose a meaningful ordering on inherently permutation-invariant, unstructured point sets without destroying manifold continuity. The reframing of tokenization as a topology-preserving tessellation problem, combined with the non-balanced spectral partitioning, offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc spatial sorting or space-filling curves that could influence future AR-based 3D generators. However, the method is currently limited to single-modality, object-centric shapes at standard densities (2,048 points), and scalability to ultra-dense point sets or scene-level data remains unvalidated. Open questions include whether the spectral partitioning cost is prohibitive at larger scales, whether multimodal conditioning (text, image) can be integrated without disrupting the structural blueprint, and whether the topology-preserving inductive bias generalizes beyond the three ShapeNet categories evaluated.
