## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks conflate genuine spatio-temporal reasoning with shortcut-solvable instances that can be answered via linguistic priors, audio cues, or static frame recognition, making it impossible to attribute reported Video-LLM performance to actual video understanding. No systematic diagnostic framework existed to audit whether benchmark samples truly enforce visual and temporal dependencies before models are evaluated on them.

## Input / Output

The diagnostic suite ingests existing video-QA benchmarks (14 benchmarks, 24,416 QA pairs across 4,938 videos) and applies six perturbation tests—Blind, Audio, Summary, Center-Frame, Frame Shuffling, and Bag-of-Frames—each consuming a modified version of the original video input (e.g., text-only transcripts, caption sequences, a single middle frame, randomly permuted frames, or CLIP top-k frame matching). After filtering, it outputs 11,033 video-native QA pairs (a 55% reduction) each labeled with one of five challenge categories (Fine-Grained Perception, Spatial World Understanding, Temporal Dynamics & Tracking, Causality & Logical Reasoning, Global Narrative), along with per-category diagnostic accuracy scores for evaluated models under a 128-frame, 1 fps input constraint.

## Method

Video-Oasis is a three-axis diagnostic suite that decouples visual and temporal dependencies: (i) visual-dependency tests replace the video with text-only inputs (question only, audio transcript, or caption sequence) to detect samples solvable without grounded perception; (ii) temporal-dependency tests disrupt chronological structure (single center frame, random frame permutation, or non-temporal CLIP bag-of-frames matching) to detect samples solvable without temporal reasoning; and (iii) ambiguity checks (cross-model consistency, segment redundancy, and frame-shuffling sensitivity) flag annotation issues for human verification. A sample is classified as a shortcut when a configurable number of diagnostic models (consensus threshold c) answer it correctly under at least one perturbation. The surviving video-native challenges are then categorized via an ensemble of five LLMs with majority voting, and the distilled set serves as a controlled testbed for ablation studies on temporal grounding (AKS keyframe retrieval), adaptive reasoning depth (instruction vs. thinking vs. VideoAuto-R1), and training paradigms (SFT vs. RLVR with QA-only vs. QA+grounding rewards).

## Results

After filtering 55% shortcut-solvable samples from 14 benchmarks (24,416 → 11,033 QA pairs), state-of-the-art Video-LLMs perform only marginally above the 25.6% random-chance baseline. Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest overall accuracy at 46.7%, followed by the agentic method STAR (39.5%) and VideoAuto-R1 (36.8%). Most open-source Video-LLMs (Qwen2.5-VL at 29.2%, Video-R1 at 26.3%, LongViLA-R1 at 28.6%) score barely above chance, and the Global Narrative category is the weakest dimension across all models, with even Gemini-2.5-Pro reaching only 43.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro (46.7%) substantially outperforms GPT-4o (27.5%) across every category, with the largest gap in Spatial World (49.8% vs. 33.2%) and Temporal Dynamics (50.9% vs. 26.3%). Among open-source Video-LLMs, VideoAuto-R1 (36.8%) leads, followed by Eagle2.5 (34.5%) and Qwen3-VL in thinking mode (34.6%), while Video-R1 (26.3%) and LongViLA-R1 (28.6%) remain near the 25.6% chance level. Between the two agentic methods sharing the same GPT-5-mini backbone, STAR (39.5%) outperforms VideoTree (30.1%) by 9.4 points, highlighting that reasoning-step orchestration matters more than the underlying LLM. Notably, the oracle ensemble of Qwen3-VL's instruction and thinking modes (46.2%) nearly matches Gemini-2.5-Pro (46.7%), suggesting adaptive reasoning-depth selection can close the gap to frontier models. The Global Narrative category is the universal bottleneck: even the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) reaches only 43.0%, while most open-source models fall below 25%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of benchmark-auditing work in video understanding (EgoTempo, Cambrian-S, Apollo) by jointly examining visual dependency, temporal dependency, and annotation ambiguity across 14 benchmarks, whereas prior studies addressed at most two of these axes on 2–9 benchmarks. It departs from prior auditing efforts by incorporating cross-model consensus thresholds and human-in-the-loop verification to reduce false positives, and by using the distilled video-native challenges as a testbed for algorithmic ablations (temporal grounding, adaptive reasoning, SFT vs. RLVR) rather than stopping at diagnosis. Conceptually, it repurposes the shortcut-identification paradigm from robustness/shortcut-learning literature and applies it systematically to the video-LLM evaluation landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 55% of samples across 14 major benchmarks are solvable without visual or temporal input, and that SOTA models perform near random chance on the remaining video-native challenges, directly challenges the interpretation of current leaderboard scores and provides concrete, reproducible criteria for constructing more rigorous video benchmarks. Open questions remain: temporal grounding yields only modest gains (1.4–2.3%), RLVR reward design for video reasoning is largely unexplored, and the optimal balance between SFT and RLVR for spatio-temporal understanding is unresolved. The observation that adaptive reasoning (choosing when to think) nearly closes the gap to frontier proprietary models suggests a practical, low-cost path for improving open-source Video-LLMs.
