## Problem

Optical flow estimation is predominantly formulated as a pairwise displacement prediction, and while the compositional nature of displacement fields is well-known in tracking and multi-frame estimation, it has never been systematically enforced as a training constraint. Existing consistency-based supervision (photometric reconstruction, forward-backward symmetry) relies on appearance or linear-motion assumptions, leaving the fundamental geometric rule that flows must agree under composition largely unexploited as a universal, architecture-agnostic supervision signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two or three RGB image frames (at 386×496 in experiments) and produces per-pixel 2D optical flow fields. A key design choice is that flow composition is performed via bilinear interpolation on coordinate grids rather than image warping, so the supervision depends purely on geometric coordinate relations and is independent of color or texture. For the augmentation variant, only the target image undergoes an affine transformation while the source remains fixed, and the induced pseudo-ground-truth flow is computed in closed form without any resampling, preserving spatial regularity even under aggressive transformations.

## Method

The paper formalizes triangular consistency: given three frames, the composed flow v_{t,t+1}(x) + v_{t+1,t+2}(x + v_{t,t+1}(x)) must match the directly estimated flow v_{t,t+2}(x), and the residual is penalized with a robust norm weighted by an occlusion mask derived from forward-backward consistency checks. This single principle instantiates three training losses—cycle consistency (composition returns to the same frame), temporal compositional consistency (three consecutive video frames), and augmentation consistency (asymmetric affine transform on the target with analytically computed pseudo-flow)—which are added as plug-and-play terms to any existing optical flow objective without modifying the estimator. In the self-supervised adaptation setting, a teacher-student distillation scheme is used where the teacher predicts shorter-range flows that are composed to supervise the student's longer-range prediction. The approach introduces negligible computational overhead (~0.12% of training wall-clock time) and requires no additional annotations, making it compatible with supervised, unsupervised, and transfer learning pipelines.

## Results

Under self-supervised adaptation (single epoch, 45 iterations, no labels), triangular consistency improves Sintel Clean EPE by up to 18.1% (2.54→2.08 for FlyingChairs-pretrained RAFT) and Final EPE by 15.4%. In unsupervised training from scratch (ARFlow), the method reduces Sintel Clean EPE from 2.79 to 2.58 (7.5%) and improves zero-shot HD1K EPE by 11.4%. Under supervised RAFT training, the largest gains appear in cross-dataset transfer: 23.1% EPE reduction on HD1K (1.08→0.83) and 18.8% on Middlebury (0.69→0.56) when training on KITTI, while in-domain KITTI test Fl-all improves by 4.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: ARFlow (unsupervised) and RAFT (supervised/adaptation). Against ARFlow in unsupervised training, triangular consistency wins on Sintel Clean EPE (2.79→2.58 train, 4.78→4.48 test), Sintel Final train (3.73→3.49), HD1K EPE (1.40→1.24, 11.4% gain), and Middlebury EPE (0.35→0.33); KITTI train Fl-all is essentially unchanged (9.87→9.94) while KITTI test improves (11.80→11.44, 3.1%). Against RAFT in supervised training, the method wins on Sintel Clean (0.76→0.71 train, 2.08→2.02 test), KITTI test Fl-all (5.27→5.02, 4.7%), HD1K (1.08→0.83, 23.1%), and Middlebury (0.69→0.56, 18.8%), but slightly loses on Sintel Final test (3.41→3.44, −0.9%), which the authors attribute to Final introducing photometric rather than geometric variations. In self-supervised adaptation, the pre-trained RAFT baseline improves by 18.1% (FlyingChairs) and 13.9% (FlyingThings3D) on Sintel Clean EPE with only 45 iterations of consistency-only training, with no photometric or smoothness losses used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RAFT architecture and ARFlow's unsupervised training framework, but its conceptual contribution is orthogonal to both: it formalizes the compositional property of displacement fields (warping maps close under composition) as an explicit training loss, a relation that prior work exploited only implicitly for long-range tracking (FlowTrack, CoTracker) or as a special case in cycle consistency. The key departure from prior augmentation strategies (e.g., ARFlow, SMURF) is the asymmetric transformation: only the target image is warped while the induced flow is computed analytically in closed form, avoiding interpolation artifacts and enabling a broader family of motion patterns. The paper positions triangular consistency as a universal, architecture-agnostic plug-and-play supervision component rather than a new estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature (no architectural changes, no additional labels, ~0.12% wall-clock overhead) makes this immediately adoptable across existing optical flow pipelines, and the consistent gains across supervised, unsupervised, and adaptation settings strengthen the case. The 18.1% improvement from a single epoch of pure consistency (no photometric or smoothness losses) in domain adaptation is practically valuable for active learning and test-time calibration scenarios. Limitations include dependence on motion diversity (gains diminish on KITTI's constrained ego-motion) and approximate occlusion handling via forward-backward masking rather than explicit layered models. Open questions include whether longer temporal chains or curriculum-style progressive correspondence ranges yield further gains, and whether the principle extends cleanly to other correspondence tasks (depth, medical registration).
