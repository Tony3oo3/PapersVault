## Problem

The deep learning community lacks a continuous, dense, and invertible latent space for SVG vector graphics, analogous to what VAEs provide for raster images. Existing approaches either rasterize SVGs (discarding geometric structure) or serialize them as long token sequences (sparse, high-dimensional, and straining context windows), leaving no compact embedding space where vector-space operations like retrieval, interpolation, and conditioning can be performed directly over vector content.

## Input / Output

The method takes individual SVG paths (not full files) as input, tokenized via a BPE vocabulary that uniformly covers drawing commands, numeric coordinates, and style attributes (fill color, stroke width, opacity) into sequences capped at 1024 tokens. It produces a single 1024-dimensional dense vector per path that lies on a unit hypersphere, which can be decoded back into a valid, style-complete SVG path. Operating at path level reduces average sequence lengths by over two orders of magnitude compared to whole-image LLM-based methods, and the constant-norm property of the learned embeddings enables direct cosine-similarity search without additional normalization.

## Method

SLS is a 135M-parameter Transformer autoencoder (hidden size 1024, 8 heads) trained end-to-end with cross-entropy plus KL-divergence loss and Gaussian noise injection (σ=1.0) into the latent during training. A key design choice is a data-driven BPE tokenizer trained on path corpora that treats commands, coordinates, and style attributes as a unified sub-word vocabulary, avoiding the rigid per-command boundaries of prior methods like DeepSVG. The encoder uses the EOS-position hidden state (rather than a CLS token) as the global path representation, while the decoder is a causal Transformer that receives the latent vector as a single prefix token and reconstructs the path via greedy decoding, exploiting the deterministic syntactic structure of SVG. At inference, embeddings are ℓ2-normalized to the unit hypersphere for downstream vector-space operations, then rescaled by the empirical mean norm before feeding to the decoder for reconstruction, decoupling similarity search from the magnitude range the decoder expects.

## Results

On SVG image reconstruction, SLS achieves 90.83 MSE-similarity, 87.26 SSIM, 18.79 LPIPS, and 80.85 DINOv2-similarity at the image level, and 96.21 BLEU-5 / 97.07 METEOR at the path level, substantially outperforming DeepSVG (retrained) on all metrics. For SVG captioning with Llama 3.2 1B, SLS reaches 27.69 CLIP-Score, 46.89 BLEU-5, and 65.80 METEOR, approaching the non-invertible CLIP upper bound (29.57 / 54.67 / 71.87) while remaining fully invertible. In path retrieval over 266k documents, SLS attains 20.91 BLEU-5 and 47.78 METEOR, surpassing DeepSVG (12.89 / 43.27) and raster encoders CLIP (10.58 / 34.12) and DINOv2 (10.77 / 34.55) on syntactic fidelity. Replacing raw XML tokens with SLS embeddings reduces average context length from 2432 to 15.56 tokens, yielding a 156× speedup and 167× reduction in training TFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepSVG [5], SLS wins decisively on every task: reconstruction (90.83 vs. ~80 MSE-similarity), captioning (+2.82 CLIP-Score, +19.19 BLEU-5, +24.63 METEOR averaged across LLMs), path retrieval (20.91 vs. 12.89 BLEU-5), and multi-path classification (29.98% vs. 15.36% accuracy). Against the non-invertible raster encoders CLIP [23] and DINOv2 [21], SLS trades some visual similarity (89.95 vs. 98.67/98.86 MSE-similarity in retrieval) for substantially better syntactic fidelity (20.91 vs. 10.58/10.77 BLEU-5) and, critically, the ability to decode embeddings back into valid SVG paths. Compared to raw XML token sequences, SLS achieves a 4.2-point CLIP-Score gain and a 29-point BLEU-5 gain in captioning while reducing context length by 156×, demonstrating that dense path-level compression preserves semantic content far more effectively than sparse token representations. The gap to CLIP in captioning (≈2 CLIP-Score points) narrows with larger LLM backbones and reflects the fundamental domain difference: CLIP operates on rasterized pixels and cannot reconstruct vector markup.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLS carves new ground at the intersection of VAE-style continuous latent spaces (well established for raster images) and SVG representation learning, where prior work has been limited to either image-level hierarchical encoders (DeepSVG) that omit style attributes, or LLM-based token-sequence generation that produces no compact embedding. The key conceptual departure is operating at the individual path level with a data-driven BPE tokenizer that uniformly treats commands, coordinates, and style as a single vocabulary, rather than enforcing rigid geometric parameterizations. The Transformer autoencoder architecture and greedy decoding strategy are standard, but their application to path-level SVG with the emergent hypersphere structure is novel in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLS provides what the authors frame as the missing foundational building block for vector-native deep learning: a single dense vector per path that is simultaneously invertible (reconstructs valid SVG), structured (lies on a hypersphere enabling cosine-similarity search and linear composition), and efficient (156× context reduction for downstream LLM tasks). This opens the door to vector-space operations—interpolation, retrieval, conditioning—on SVG content that were previously infeasible without rasterization or prohibitively long token sequences. Open questions include whether the path-level abstraction suffices for highly complex multi-layer SVGs, how the latent space behaves under adversarial or semantically meaningful perturbations beyond the tested Gaussian/angular regimes, and whether the spontaneous hypersphere property can be leveraged for structured generation or editing.
