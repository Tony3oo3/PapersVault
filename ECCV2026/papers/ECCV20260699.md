## Problem

Identifying which text prompts a text-to-image editing model can actually apply successfully remains a manual, trial-and-error process. Existing semantic discovery methods (NoiseCLR, SliderSpace) require training, are slow (hours to over 18 hours for 100 semantics), produce visually redundant directions, and are difficult to interpret without post-hoc labeling. No training-free, model-agnostic framework exists that automatically surfaces a diverse, relevant, and reliably editable set of semantics for a given domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a domain text prompt (e.g., 'dress'), N unedited source images from that domain, and a T2I editing model (diffusion-based such as InstructPix2Pix/Ledits++ or FLUX-based such as FLUX.1 Kontext). A multimodal VLM (GPT-5) first expands the prompt into a large candidate set of 500–1000 semantics, each of which is applied to all N images to produce edited outputs. The final output is a ranked list of K semantics (K ≪ M, typically 25–100) that the user can directly feed to the T2I model as editing instructions, with no additional tuning of guidance scales or timesteps required.

## Method

RankT2I frames semantic discovery as a submodular set selection problem over the candidate semantics. It defines an objective F(S) that jointly maximizes three terms: (i) relevance, the average CLIP similarity between each edited image and its generating semantic; (ii) editability, the CLIP similarity gain to the semantic multiplied by image-to-image CLIP similarity to enforce identity preservation; and (iii) diversity, a facility-location function over CLIP text-embedding cosine similarities that rewards covering the full semantic space. A greedy algorithm with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee iteratively selects the semantic with the largest marginal gain, maintaining running coverage values for efficient O(M) updates per step. The entire pipeline is training-free and model-agnostic: it requires no gradient updates, no contrastive training, and works identically across diffusion and rectified-flow T2I models, reducing discovery time from hours (NoiseCLR: ~1117 min; SliderSpace: ~392 min) to under 44 minutes for 100 semantics.

## Results

On diffusion-based (SD) models, RankT2I achieves a truncated CLIP entropy (TCE) of 26.47, nearly doubling NoiseCLR (13.88) and SliderSpace (16.51), while also leading on interpretability metrics: TIFA 0.608 vs. 0.340/0.387 and VQAScore 0.818 vs. 0.548/0.524. On FLUX-based models, RankT2I reaches TCE 22.81 (vs. SliderSpace 12.58), TIFA 0.716 (vs. 0.346), and VQAScore 0.921 (vs. 0.674). In terms of efficiency, discovering 100 semantics takes RankT2I 43 minutes on SD and 114 minutes on FLUX, compared to 1,117 minutes for NoiseCLR and 391/291 minutes for SliderSpace.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NoiseCLR [14], RankT2I wins on every metric: TCE improves from 13.88 to 26.47 (SD), TIFA from 0.340 to 0.608, and VQAScore from 0.548 to 0.818, while also being ~26× faster (43 min vs. 1,117 min) due to being training-free. Against SliderSpace [19], RankT2I similarly dominates: TCE 26.47 vs. 16.51 (SD) and 22.81 vs. 12.58 (FLUX), TIFA 0.608 vs. 0.387 (SD) and 0.716 vs. 0.346 (FLUX), with roughly 9× and 2.5× speedups respectively. Against the K-Means attribute retrieval baseline [44] (Table 2), RankT2I achieves higher TCE (19.63 vs. 19.14 on SD; 20.88 vs. 18.12 on FLUX), TIFA (0.502 vs. 0.416 on SD; 0.600 vs. 0.422 on FLUX), and VQAScore (0.805 vs. 0.759 on SD; 0.895 vs. 0.794 on FLUX), though I2I is essentially tied on SD (0.8436 vs. 0.8433) and slightly better on FLUX (0.8566 vs. 0.8826). Overall, RankT2I consistently outperforms all three baselines on both diversity and interpretability metrics across both diffusion and FLUX model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes submodular facility-location optimization (previously applied to document summarization and GAN direction discovery) for the task of selecting editable semantics in T2I models, departing from prior semantic discovery methods like NoiseCLR (contrastive learning) and SliderSpace (PCA on CLIP features) that require training and are tied to a single model family. Rather than learning latent directions, RankT2I operates entirely in prompt space: it uses a multimodal vision-language model to generate candidate semantics, evaluates them via CLIP-based relevance and editability scores on generated edits, and applies a greedy submodular maximization to jointly optimize relevance, editability, and diversity. The framework is explicitly model-agnostic, extending the semantic discovery problem from diffusion models (where NoiseCLR and SliderSpace operate) to rectified-flow/FLUX-based models without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RankT2I addresses a practical bottleneck in T2I editing—users must manually trial-and-error their way to discover which prompts a model can actually execute—by automating this process in a training-free, model-agnostic pipeline that is roughly 25× faster than the closest training-based baseline. The finding that editability scores below ~0.03 correlate with identity-altering edits in face editing offers a preliminary quantitative safeguard against deepfake misuse, though the threshold is acknowledged to be model- and domain-dependent. Open questions include whether CLIP-based scoring adequately captures fine-grained editability (e.g., structural vs. color changes), how the method scales to candidate sets beyond 1000 semantics, and whether the submodular diversity term can be replaced by learned embeddings that better reflect perceptual edit distinctness.
