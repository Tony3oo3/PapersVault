## Problem

Existing training-free detectors for AI-generated images rely on single-path absolute reconstruction scores or external perturbations, making them fragile across domains and degrading markedly on novel generators. The paper identifies that LDM iterative denoising imposes stronger cross-patch context dependence than natural images, but no prior training-free method exploits this intrinsic structural trace as a detection signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261479/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at any resolution (experiments use 512×512) and produces a scalar detection score S(x) where higher values indicate higher likelihood of being real. Internally, the image is partitioned into 128×128 non-overlapping patches (with zero-padding if dimensions are insufficient) for the local reconstruction path, and LPIPS computed on VGG second-layer features serves as the perceptual distance for both reconstruction paths. No diffusion U-Net is invoked—only the lightweight VAE encoder-decoder is used.

## Method

GLARE is a training-free detector that measures context sensitivity by reconstructing each test image twice through a single pretrained LDM autoencoder (SD2-base VAE): once as a whole (full-context path) and once after partitioning into independent 128×128 patches that sever long-range dependencies (context-free path). The detection score is the log-ratio of the two reconstruction errors, Δ(x) = log e_f(x) − log e_p(x), which cancels content-dependent baselines shared by both paths and isolates the context-dependence signal. A lightweight Semantic Complexity Calibration Module (SCCM) computes an edge-density proxy from VGG shallow features via Sobel gradients and applies an inverse calibration factor to suppress content-induced bias, yielding the final score S(x) = Δ(x) + log Φ(x). The theoretical grounding draws on rate-distortion theory: the distortion spread between the two entropy extremes (conditional vs. marginal) is governed by mutual information between patches, which is systematically higher for LDM outputs due to cross-attention and multi-step denoising.

## Results

On LDMFakeDetect (9 generators), GLARE achieves a mean AUROC of 98.13% and AP of 97.87%, surpassing the best training-free baseline AEROBLADE (91.54% AUROC) by 6.59 points and the best supervised method ForgeLens (89.45% AUROC) by 8.68 points. On FakeInversion (13 generators), GLARE reaches 92.07% AUROC and 88.11% AP, exceeding the best training-based method (NPR, 83.95% AUROC) by 8.12 points and the best training-free method (ConV, 79.06% AUROC) by 13.01 points. On the in-the-wild Chameleon benchmark, GLARE attains 82.62% AUROC and 76.97% AP, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art by +11.00% AUROC and +10.94% AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLARE outperforms all 11 baselines on mean AUROC across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest training-free method AEROBLADE, GLARE gains +6.59% AUROC on LDMFakeDetect (98.13 vs. 91.54) and +22.09% on FakeInversion (92.07 vs. 69.98); AEROBLADE's single-path absolute score fails to generalize to newer generators like Flux (77.26% AUROC) where GLARE reaches 96.87%. Among supervised methods, ForgeLens is the closest competitor on LDMFakeDetect (89.45% mean AUROC) and wins on a few individual FakeInversion generators (e.g., Kandinsky2 at 99.78%, Stable-Cascade at 99.88%), but GLARE delivers a higher overall mean (92.07% vs. 85.58%) without any training data. Perturbation-based methods (RIGID, WaRPAD, ConV) and the manifold-geometry approach (Manifold Bias) are consistently weaker: Manifold Bias collapses to 42.93% mean AUROC on FakeInversion, while ConV reaches only 79.06%. On the challenging in-the-wild Chameleon benchmark, GLARE (82.62% AUROC) exceeds the best baseline AIDE (71.62%) by 11 points, demonstrating that the context-sensitivity signal survives real-world post-processing better than pixel-level or feature-invariance cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLARE departs from both major families of training-free LDM detection: reconstruction-based methods (AEROBLADE, ZED) that threshold a single-path absolute reconstruction error, and perturbation-based methods (RIGID, WaRPAD, ConV) that probe pretrained feature invariance under external noise or augmentation. Rather than measuring how well an image is reconstructed or how a feature extractor responds to perturbations, GLARE measures the *relative change* in autoencoder reconstruction error when global spatial context is removed by patching, grounding this in rate-distortion theory to show the signal directly reflects the cross-patch context dependence imposed by iterative latent denoising. The method repurposes the LDM VAE (SD2-base) as a forensic instrument in the spirit of AEROBLADE but replaces the fragile absolute score with a content-invariant log-ratio, supplemented by a lightweight edge-density calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A training-free detector that consistently outperforms supervised baselines by 6–11% AUROC across three benchmarks and 20+ generators removes the retraining bottleneck that plagues supervised detectors as new LDM architectures emerge, making it immediately deployable. The context-sensitivity signal's robustness to JPEG compression, blur, noise, and cropping suggests it captures a deeper generative-process trace rather than fragile pixel-level artifacts. Open questions include whether the signal extends to non-latent-space generators (e.g., pixel-space diffusion, autoregressive pixel models), whether boundary artifacts from patching can be further mitigated, and whether complementary cues (e.g., frequency-domain traces) can be fused to cover the residual failure cases under extreme post-processing.
