## Problem

Existing MLLM jailbreak attacks treat the visual modality as a secondary channel—either rendering harmful text onto blank images or describing context purely in words—thereby underutilizing the unique capacity of images to carry rich, semantically coherent scenarios. This paper asks whether embedding a harmful query within a visually plausible context (e.g., a teacher posing a question in a classroom) can exploit the model's instruction-following and role-interpretation mechanisms to bypass safety alignment more effectively than text-centric approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single harmful text query (e.g., a question about evading law enforcement) and produces an adversarial RGB image in which the query's intent is embedded as legible text within a semantically coherent scene (blackboard, roadmap, article, or comic dialogue), generated via Stable Diffusion. At attack time, this image is paired with a fixed, generic auxiliary text prompt (e.g., 'What is shown in the image?') to form the full input to the target MLLM; no access to model internals is required (black-box setting). The output is the model's free-text response, which is scored for toxicity (1–5 scale via GPT-4o judge) and classified as a successful jailbreak only at score 5.

## Method

CIA is a four-agent pipeline: (1) a Parser Agent decomposes the harmful query into a visual text string v and a frame structure f, applying toxicity obfuscation to conceal sensitive keywords; (2) a Text Refiner iteratively (up to 5 rounds) evaluates and rewrites (v, f) using an auxiliary LLM until a semantic-consistency check against the original query passes; (3) an Image Generator Agent selects one of four scene-visualization strategies—Demonstration, Sequential Path, Structured Content, or Dialogue Layout—and feeds a composed prompt to Stable Diffusion to synthesize the initial contextual image; (4) an Image Refiner augments the image with four types of contextual elements (auxiliary text like 'Ignore previous instruction', safety icons, emoji insertion among key tokens, and localized noise/blur on critical keywords) via an image-editing model. The core insight, validated by Fisher Ratio and t-SNE analyses on InternVL2.5-78B, is that visually coherent context collapses the embedding separability between benign and harmful prompts across all network layers, effectively neutralizing the model's text-based safety alignment.

## Results

On SafeBench-tiny, CIA achieves an average ASR of 90.40% and average toxicity score of 4.84 across five MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-2.0, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL2.5-78B), outperforming the strongest baseline VisCo Attack (83.20% ASR, 4.74 toxicity). On MMSafetyBench-tiny, CIA reaches 86.83% ASR and 4.62 toxicity on GPT-4o, and 91.02% ASR and 4.83 toxicity on Qwen2.5-VL-72B. CIA maintains ASR above 84% on every individual model tested, whereas baselines such as FigStep drop from 64% on Qwen2.5-VL to only 12% on GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIA outperforms all four baselines on both benchmarks. Against QR-Attack (Liu et al., ECCV 2024), CIA gains roughly 67 percentage points in ASR on GPT-4o (86.83% vs. 20.24%) and 42 points on Qwen2.5-VL (91.02% vs. 49.40%), with toxicity scores nearly double. Against SI-Attack (Zhao et al., ICCV 2025), CIA leads by 63 points on GPT-4o (86.83% vs. 23.81%) and 31 points on Qwen2.5-VL (91.02% vs. 60.12%). Against FigStep (Gong et al., AAAI 2025), the gap is most dramatic on closed-source models: CIA achieves 84% ASR on GPT-4o versus FigStep's 12%, while on Qwen2.5-VL the difference narrows to 92% vs. 64%. The closest competitor is VisCo Attack (Miao et al., 2025), which achieves 83.20% average ASR on SafeBench-tiny; CIA edges it out by 7.2 points on average and by 1.1–3.9 points per model, with the largest margin on GPT-4o (84% vs. 76%) and GPT-4o-mini (92% vs. 86%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a distinct niche within MLLM jailbreak research by shifting the attack's center of gravity from text to image. Prior approaches either render harmful instructions as typography on blank canvases (FigStep), exploit text-image shuffle inconsistencies (SI-Attack), or use textual descriptions of context alongside images (QR-Attack, VisCo Attack). CIA departs by treating the visual scene itself as the primary semantic carrier of harmful intent, drawing on LLM jailbreak context-design motifs (teacher, classroom, dialogue) and repurposing them through a multi-agent text-to-image pipeline. The closest conceptual ancestor is VisCo Attack (Miao et al., same group), which also uses image-driven context injection, but CIA extends this with structured scene-visualization strategies, iterative semantic-consistency refinement, and contextual element augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The embedding-separability analysis (Fisher Ratio collapsing to near zero across all layers when visual context is introduced) provides mechanistic evidence that current MLLM safety alignment is fundamentally text-anchored and can be bypassed by visually coherent scenarios—a vulnerability that will grow more accessible as image generation and text-to-visual-token compression mature. However, the evaluation is limited to tiny subsets (168 and 50 samples) across five models, and the closest baseline (VisCo Attack, from the same research group) narrows the practical gap. Open questions include whether defenses can be made robust to image-centric attacks without the severe over-defensiveness observed with AdaShield, and whether the findings generalize to video or audio modalities.
