## Problem

Large-scale face recognition trained on web-crawled data suffers from pervasive label noise that corrupts training dynamics and degrades generalization. Existing sample-selection methods either introduce heavy computational overhead (sub-center, co-mining) or depend on brittle distributional assumptions such as bimodal loss structure that collapses under severe noise. The open challenge is to robustly identify mislabeled samples without prior noise-rate knowledge, auxiliary networks, or fragile thresholding heuristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262044/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB face images paired with (potentially noisy) identity labels and operates entirely in the cosine-similarity space of a margin-based softmax loss. During training, it produces a per-sample binary mask q_i that gates whether each pair contributes to backpropagation, along with a dynamically updated scalar threshold T_n. At inference, the pipeline is identical to the base backbone (ResNet/SEResNet) and outputs a standard L2-normalized face embedding; the selection mechanism is purely a training-time filter with ~0.2% measured overhead.

## Method

DDLN's key insight, derived from backpropagation analysis, is that the cosine-similarity distribution of discarded noisy target pairs closely matches that of clean non-target pairs because both operate under weak gradient coupling (near-zero softmax probability). The method estimates a noise boundary directly from the non-target similarities of retained samples: T_max' is the batch-averaged k-th largest non-target similarity plus a small slack α, and T_min' is the batch-averaged minimum non-target similarity. A progressive linear schedule interpolates the active threshold from T_min' to T_max' over the first n_max training steps, allowing hard-but-clean samples to be learned early while progressively tightening the filter as distributions stabilize. The threshold is further smoothed across mini-batches via EMA (λ=0.01) to prevent oscillation. Once a noisy pair falls below the tightening boundary, it loses gradient attraction toward its incorrect prototype and is unlikely to recover, making the purification process self-reinforcing without any noise-rate prior or auxiliary estimation network.

## Results

On the simulated #40-20-20 noisy dataset (CosFace), DDLN achieves 93.38% Avg recognition accuracy (vs. 83.42% without noise handling and 91.44% for RVFace) with a 99.42% F1-score for noise detection. Under extreme 80% noise (#80-40-40), DDLN lifts Avg from 30.45% (None) to 78.11%, exceeding RVFace by 18.05%. On real-world WebFace2M-Noise (~80% noise), DDLN boosts CosFace/ArcFace/SphereFace baselines by 7.41%/7.68%/7.82% on the 8-benchmark average, reaching 84.43%/84.70%/84.31%. On MS-Celeb-1M with ResNet-100, DDLN achieves 97.47% Avg-5 and 96.96% IJB-C TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴, outperforming RobustFace by 3.68% on IJB-B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RVFace (OTSU-based thresholding), DDLN consistently wins on noise-detection F1 across all nine simulated settings (e.g., 99.42% vs. 96.62% at 40% noise) and reduces the false-negative rate on clean samples by 70–84% relative to RVFace. On the extreme 80% noise setting, DDLN's Avg recognition (78.11%) exceeds RVFace (60.06%) by 18.05 points, while also discarding fewer clean samples (19.96% FNR vs. 31.19%). On real-world WebFace2M-Noise, DDLN outperforms Sub-center ArcFace (SubRe) by 5.10% on the 8-benchmark average and RobustFace by 7.15%, while operating within a single-network pipeline without multi-stage retraining. On MS-Celeb-1M, DDLN surpasses RobustFace by 3.68% on IJB-B and 2.55% on IJB-C (TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴), and beats DTDD by 1.49% on IJB-C with ResNet-100. On CASIA-WebFace, DDLN+CosFace achieves 82.29% IJB-C TAR@FAR=10⁻⁵, exceeding GFace (80.79%) and DTDD (80.39%), while matching or surpassing Co-mining (71.17%) and Sub-center (79.16%) at the same operating point.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant sample-selection paradigm in noise-robust face recognition, which typically relies on target-similarity thresholds (Sub-center ArcFace's fixed cutoffs, RVFace's OTSU bimodality assumption) or multi-network co-training (Co-mining). Instead, DDLN repurposes an already-computed quantity—the non-target cosine similarities of retained clean samples—as a data-driven proxy for the noise boundary, grounded in a backpropagation analysis showing that both discarded noisy targets and clean non-targets operate in a weak-gradient regime. The progressive linear scheduling from a conservative lower bound to the estimated upper bound is a new mechanism not present in prior fixed-threshold or OTSU-based methods, and the approach requires no auxiliary networks, no noise-rate prior, and no bimodality assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a ~0.2% training overhead with no architectural changes makes DDLN directly deployable in large-scale web-crawled face recognition pipelines where prior methods (co-training, sub-centers) introduce prohibitive memory or compute costs. The cleaned training subset it produces is also reusable for downstream training, offering a one-pass purification benefit. Limitations include residual clean-sample discarding at extreme noise (≈20% FNR at 80% noise) and the method's tight coupling to softmax-based cosine-similarity losses, leaving open whether the non-target-as-noise-proxy principle transfers to other loss formulations or modalities. The paper does not explore interaction with other robustness techniques (e.g., adaptive margins, quality-aware losses) that are increasingly standard in production systems.
