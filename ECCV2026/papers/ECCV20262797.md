## Problem

Video understanding benchmarks have long centered on single-camera settings, yet real-world perception systems (autonomous vehicles, surveillance networks, robots) inherently operate on multi-camera feeds. Multi-camera reasoning introduces two fundamental challenges absent from single-view tasks—context scaling with the number of simultaneous streams and cross-view spatial reasoning (stitching discrete viewpoints into a coherent scene)—for which no systematic benchmark exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262797/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question is paired with 2–8 synchronized camera streams (e.g., six surround-view cameras for nuScenes, wrist-mounted plus overhead for AgiBot, multiple fixed cameras for MEVA) and a natural-language prompt. Multiple-choice tasks (counting, temporal, event ordering, spatial, camera-ID) yield a single selected answer, while the summarization task produces a free-text narrative scored by ROUGE-1/2/L. Frames are sampled uniformly per camera (16 frames for Qwen/GPT/Gemma; 8 for InternVL on nuScenes/Ego-Exo4D, 4 on MEVA), and a stitched variant tiles all cameras into one composite image per timestep.

## Method

CrossView is constructed via a two-stage pipeline. First, a Spatio-temporal Scene Graph (STSG) is built by consolidating object trajectories, 3D coordinates (LiDAR for nuScenes, bounding boxes otherwise), VLM-generated activity labels (InternVL-3.5-38B), and pairwise spatial predicates into per-timestamp graph snapshots across four source datasets (nuScenes, Ego-Exo4D, AgiBot, MEVA). Second, a programmatic Grounding-Target question engine queries the STSG to identify grounding anchors, target events, and plausible distractors, then passes this structured metadata to GPT-5.2 to generate natural-language questions across six categories (Temporal, Event Ordering, Spatial, Counting, Summarization, Camera-ID). The resulting 6,000 questions are verified to be unsolvable from any single camera in isolation. Evaluation covers 11 VLMs from four families under both uniform and stitched multi-camera frame sampling, revealing a consistent multi-camera gap that model scale does not close.

## Results

Across all four domains, no model family achieves consistently strong multi-camera reasoning: GPT-5.2, the strongest proprietary model, scores only 25.4% on nuScenes temporal reasoning and 34.6% on Ego-Exo4D best-camera identification, barely above the 25% random-guessing baseline. The best open-source model (Qwen3-8B) reaches 69.6% on AgiBot temporal reasoning but drops to 43.2% on nuScenes counting. Cross-view counting and best-camera identification are consistently the hardest tasks, with scores in the 20–47% range across all models, while temporal and event-ordering tasks from the same videos yield 40–55%. Performance degrades monotonically with scene complexity: AgiBot (controlled robot environment) yields the highest accuracies, while MEVA (wide-area multi-camera surveillance) produces the lowest in every category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5.2 (proprietary) leads on most tasks but by narrow margins: it tops nuScenes counting (47.8% vs. 46.2% for InternVL3.5-14B) and AgiBot event ordering (66.0% vs. 54.4% for Qwen2.5-7B), yet it is the weakest on nuScenes temporal reasoning (25.4%) and Ego-Exo4D best-camera identification (34.6%). Among open-source models, Qwen3-8B is the strongest overall, achieving the highest AgiBot temporal score (69.6%, actually exceeding GPT-5.2's 66.8%) and competitive nuScenes counting (43.2%), while InternVL3.5-14B leads on nuScenes spatial reasoning (43.3%) and temporal (44.8%). Gemma-3-12B is the only model to exceed 35% on Ego-Exo4D best-camera identification (36.9%), a task where all other models score below 35%. The gap between proprietary and open-source models is most pronounced on MEVA counting (GPT-5.2: 21.9% vs. Qwen2.5-7B: 83.2%—an anomaly likely due to dataset-specific artifacts) and Ego-Exo4D summarization (GPT-5.2 ROUGE-1: 37.5 vs. Qwen2.5-3B: 28.8). Critically, even the best model (GPT-5.2) remains below 50% on the majority of task categories, confirming that multi-camera reasoning is an unsolved problem across all current VLM architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossView departs from the dominant single-camera VQA paradigm (VQAv2, Video-MME, LongVideoBench) and from driving-domain QA benchmarks (NuScenes-QA, NuPlanQA, OmniDrive) that, despite using multi-camera data, ground questions to individual views without requiring cross-view integration. It also extends ego-exo benchmarks (Ego-Exo4D, EgoExoBench, LangView) that study viewpoint correspondence or selection in isolation rather than joint evidence aggregation across all cameras. The paper repurposes existing multi-camera datasets (nuScenes, Ego-Exo4D, AgiBot, MEVA) through a novel Spatio-temporal Scene Graph construction pipeline and programmatic question-generation engine, carving out a task—language-grounded semantic reasoning over 2–8 simultaneous heterogeneous feeds—that prior multi-camera perception work (MTMC tracking, BEV methods, person re-identification) does not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CrossView quantifies a 'multi-camera gap' that persists across model families and scales, suggesting a structural limitation in how current VLMs process multi-view data rather than a data-volume or capacity issue. This has direct implications for deploying VLMs in autonomous driving, robotics, and surveillance where perception is inherently multi-camera. Open questions include whether architectural changes (e.g., explicit cross-view attention, unified 3D representations) can close the gap, whether adaptive frame sampling strategies can mitigate context-scaling challenges, and whether the programmatically generated questions (via GPT-5.2) fully capture the difficulty of natural multi-camera reasoning. The benchmark's relatively small size (6K questions) and reliance on a single LLM for question phrasing are limitations that future work should address.
