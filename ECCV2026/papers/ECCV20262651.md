## Problem

AI-generated image detectors suffer severe cross-generator generalization failure because they overfit to source-domain textural artifacts, producing brittle decision boundaries that collapse on unseen generative manifolds. Conventional regularization (L1/L2, Dropout) applies indiscriminate parameter constraints that fail to explicitly penalize texture-biased shortcut learning, leaving no mechanism to anchor the model to domain-invariant structure during optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and outputs a binary real/fake classification. During training, the primary feature extractor (frozen) produces features X, which are concatenated with an N-dimensional Scene Composition Structure vector (reduced to M dimensions via a GELU-activated FC layer) to form an augmented input to the classification head. At inference, the SCS prior is entirely discarded and only the W_X sub-matrix of the trained head is applied to X, so the deployed model has identical architecture and computational cost to the unregularized base detector.

## Method

FAIR is a training-time, architecture-agnostic regularization strategy that injects a domain-invariant Scene Composition Structure (SCS) prior strictly during optimization, following the Learning Using Privileged Information paradigm. The SCS vector is computed via recursive axis-parallel partitioning of the image, where each split's SSE-reduction gain is accumulated into a normalized N-dimensional feature encoding macro-structural boundaries. During training, this prior is concatenated with the base detector's primary features and the classification head is expanded to a joint weight matrix; the optimizer is thereby encouraged to tilt the separating hyperplane into the stable structural dimensions rather than fitting a high-complexity non-linear boundary in the texture subspace. Two verified properties ensure effectiveness: the prior has near-zero mutual information with the label (CKA ≈ 0.07), preventing it from serving as a discriminative shortcut, and its topological manifold remains stable across diverse generators, providing a consistent geometric anchor. At inference, discarding the prior projects the regularized hyperplane back into the primary feature space, yielding a smoothed, generalized decision boundary with zero added overhead.

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark (8 unseen target domains), AIDE+FAIR achieves a mean cross-generator accuracy of 91.01%, a +4.13% improvement over AIDE base (86.88%), while PC+FAIR reaches 90.40%, a +8.04% gain over PC base (82.36%). Across the five benchmarks (GenImage, AIGCDetect, UnivFD, Fake2M, DRCT-2M), FAIR consistently improves mean accuracy for both architectures, with the largest absolute gains on Fake2M (+7.06% for AIDE) and DRCT-2M (+3.00% for PC). The AIDE+FAIR model establishes a new state-of-the-art on GenImage, surpassing the previous best (DRCT at 82.08%) by nearly 9 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAIR is integrated into two SOTA detectors (AIDE and PatchCraft) and compared against 13 baselines on GenImage. AIDE+FAIR (91.01% mean) outperforms all prior methods including DRCT (82.08%), GenDet (81.56%), PC (82.36%), UnivFD (73.29%), and DIRE (70.66%), with the largest per-domain gains on Midjourney (+4.24% over AIDE base) and BigGAN (+11.29% over AIDE base). PC+FAIR (90.40%) similarly dominates, beating DRCT by 8.32 points and achieving the best single-domain result on Midjourney (88.93%). On the in-the-wild transfer benchmarks, AIDE+FAIR improves over AIDE base on UnivFD (+2.48%), Fake2M (+7.06%), and DRCT-2M (+2.15%), though it slightly underperforms on AIGCDetect (−0.88%), a GAN-heavy benchmark where the base model already generalizes well. PC+FAIR shows a similar pattern: strong gains on GenImage (+8.04%) and DRCT-2M (+3.00%) but a small drop on Fake2M (−1.64%). Against traditional regularizers (L1, L2, Dropout) and alternative feature priors (Random, LPIPS, Sobel, Laplacian), SCS-based FAIR achieves the highest mean accuracy (91.01% vs. 86.5–86.7% for regularizers and 83.1–84.8% for alternative priors), confirming that the macro-structural prior is uniquely effective for cross-generator generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) paradigm from Vapnik and Vashist (2009), originally designed for SVM difficulty estimation, into a geometric regularization mechanism for domain generalization. It departs from conventional parameter-level regularizers (L1/L2, Dropout) by instead augmenting the feature space with an orthogonal, domain-invariant structural prior (Scene Composition Structure) that is discarded at inference, drawing on the SCS metric from the authors' prior ICIPW 2025 work. The approach is applied as a plug-in to two architecturally distinct SOTA detectors (AIDE and PatchCraft), positioning FAIR as an architecture-agnostic regularization layer rather than a new detection model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-generator generalization remains the central unsolved problem in AIGC detection, and FAIR's zero-overhead inference (the structural prior is entirely discarded at deployment) makes it practically attractive for real-world deployment where latency matters. However, the JPEG compression ablation reveals a genuine limitation: at extreme compression (Q=50), FAIR-regularized PatchCraft underperforms its baseline because the structural anchor becomes susceptible to 8×8 blocking artifacts, suggesting the method's robustness gains are quality-dependent. Open questions include whether the SCS prior is truly the optimal structural signal (vs. other macro-geometric descriptors), whether the approach extends to video or 3D AIGC detection, and whether the LUPI framing can be formalized with theoretical guarantees on the resulting decision boundary complexity.
