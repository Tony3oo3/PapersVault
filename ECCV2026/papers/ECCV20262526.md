## Problem

Future 4D scene prediction (RGB, depth, optical flow) from a single frame is highly ill-posed without long video histories or structured motion priors (trajectories, velocities), and motion blur further degrades the limited appearance cues available. Existing driving world models either require additional conditioning signals that increase deployment complexity, or lack explicit motion-field modeling needed for physically consistent spatiotemporal coherence. This paper identifies event camera streams as a blur-robust, history-free motion prior that can substitute for these missing signals under single-frame conditioning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB frame at time T and a 100 ms event stream E_{T-1→T} (converted to voxel grids with 15 temporal bins) as input, and produces N=9 future RGB frames together with corresponding depth maps and optical flow. All three output modalities are derived from a shared VAE latent space: RGB is generated via diffusion denoising, while depth and flow latents are initialized from the RGB latent and refined through the Perceptual Dynamics Space, avoiding modality-specific encoders. Inputs are cropped to 192×640 (E-VKitti2) or 320×384 (real-world datasets).

## Method

UniDynamics builds on Stable Video Diffusion as its generative backbone and introduces two key components. First, an Event Latent Enhancement (ELE) module encodes event voxel grids into features, aligns them with the RGB latent via bidirectional cross-attention, and fuses them into a diffusion-injectable event latent Z(E) that is treated as a noisy 'historical-state RGB' at T-1, providing implicit initial momentum rather than a hard constraint. Second, a Perceptual Dynamics Space (PDS) is embedded at three scales of the U-Net, where task-specific adapters (2D convolutions for depth, 3D convolutions for flow) decouple geometry and motion subspaces to avoid multi-task gradient conflicts, while a gated bidirectional interaction module enables adaptive cross-modal exchange. The resulting depth and flow latents are injected back into the U-Net's spatial and temporal layers respectively via zero-initialized convolutions, progressively imposing geometric and motion constraints on RGB generation. Training uses a weighted combination of latent-space MSE, scale-shift-invariant depth loss, pixel-space L1 flow loss, and an ELE stabilization loss, with classifier-free guidance (15% conditioning dropout) and EMA for stability.

## Results

On E-VKitti2, UniDynamics achieves the best FID of 39.1 and FVD of 223.6 for future RGB generation, substantially outperforming the strongest generation-only baseline Vista (FID 101.8, FVD 1364.6) and the closest joint method UniFuture-Depth (FID 44.0, FVD 584.1). For depth, it attains AbsRel 0.27 and δ₁ 67.1%, and for optical flow, EPE 3.0 and AE 21.0°, all the best among compared methods. It is the only method that simultaneously generates future RGB, depth, and optical flow from a single event-RGB pair. Under motion blur, UniDynamics maintains FVD 272.8 versus 617.7–668.7 for UniFuture variants and UniDynamics without events.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDynamics outperforms all generation-only baselines (SimVP, TAU, SVD, Vista) by a wide margin on both FID and FVD, with the largest gap against Vista (FID 39.1 vs. 101.8; FVD 223.6 vs. 1364.6), confirming that joint depth-flow modeling and event conditioning dramatically improve generation quality and temporal coherence. Against the closest joint method UniFuture [24], UniDynamics wins on FID (39.1 vs. 44.0 for UniFuture-Depth; 39.1 vs. 46.9 for UniFuture-Flow), FVD (223.6 vs. 584.1/615.3), depth AbsRel (0.27 vs. 0.38), and flow EPE (3.0 vs. 5.9), while also being the only method to produce all three modalities simultaneously. On perception-only baselines, UniDynamics surpasses FLODCAST [4] on depth (AbsRel 0.27 vs. 21.4) and flow (EPE 3.0 vs. 11.7), and FUTURIST [20] on depth (AbsRel 0.27 vs. 29.7). Zero-shot transfer to real-world DSEC, MVSEC, and M3ED shows UniDynamics achieving the best FVD (224.0, 427.0, 508.3) and competitive or best depth/flow metrics, with the largest FID advantage on the complex M3ED dataset (68.5 vs. 88.1–88.6 for UniFuture variants).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDynamics builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its generative backbone, extending the latent video diffusion paradigm to a multimodal 4D prediction setting. It departs from driving world models (Vista, GAIA-1, DriveDreamer) that rely on structured conditioning signals (trajectories, text, actions) by instead using event streams as a blur-robust, history-free motion prior for single-frame extrapolation. The Perceptual Dynamics Space extends the event-RGB fusion literature (which has focused on current/past depth and flow estimation) into the future-prediction regime, and the joint RGB-depth-flow modeling generalizes prior work that treated these modalities separately (UniFuture-Depth, UniFuture-Flow, SVD-Depth, SVD-Flow).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: future scene prediction from a single event-RGB pair without requiring long video histories or structured motion priors, which is more transferable to real-world autonomous driving systems. The demonstrated robustness under high-speed motion blur—where events compensate for degraded RGB appearance—suggests a path toward reliable perception in challenging driving conditions. However, training is limited to synthetic VKitti2 with zero-shot transfer to real data showing some quality degradation (e.g., FID 50.9 vs. 46.2 on DSEC), and the 9-frame prediction horizon is short. Open questions include scalability to longer horizons, real-time inference feasibility, and whether the PDS architecture generalizes to non-driving 4D prediction tasks.
