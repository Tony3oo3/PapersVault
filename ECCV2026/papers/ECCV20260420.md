## Problem

Multimodal generation has been dominated by text-driven pipelines where language encodes intent and vision merely executes it, creating a fundamental asymmetry that fragments representation space and makes it difficult to unify understanding, editing, and generation in a single model. The paper asks whether all input modalities—textual descriptions, spatial layouts, and editing instructions—can be unified into a purely visual representation, enabling a clean image-in, image-out generative paradigm without cross-modal alignment bottlenecks or task-specific architectural branches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260420/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All heterogeneous conditions (rendered text prompts, bounding boxes, arrows, doodles, and source images for editing) are composited onto a single image canvas, which serves as the sole input—no separate text encoder or conditioning channel is used. The model operates at 256×256 resolution and produces a single RGB image as output. For editing tasks, the source image is additionally encoded via a frozen LDM VAE to provide structural priors, but this is consumed through the same latent-space flow rather than a separate conditioning pathway.

## Method

FlowInOne reformulates multimodal generation as flow matching between two isomorphic latents in a shared space: a source latent derived from the unified visual prompt (via SigLIP patch features projected through a stacked Transformer text-image VAE) and a target latent from a frozen LDM image VAE. The core architectural contribution is a Dual-Path Spatially-Adaptive Modulation mechanism: for pure text-to-image generation, cross-attention is bypassed to follow semantic evolution strictly; for image editing, cross-attention against the source image latent is activated, and a lightweight sigmoid-gated MLP predicts per-token anisotropic weights that spatially modulate how much structural information is injected, balancing background preservation against instruction-driven reconstruction. Training uses a combined flow matching MSE loss, KL divergence, and CLIP contrastive loss, with joint training over all 5M VisPrompt-5M samples for 240k steps. The 1.2B-parameter model is initialized from CrossFlow and trained at 256×256 resolution, with inference performed by solving the ODE deterministically from t=0 to t=1.

## Results

On VP-Bench, FlowInOne achieves total success rates of 54.0% (Gemini3), 39.2% (GPT5.2), 50.3% (Qwen3.5), and 44.9% (Human), substantially outperforming all open-source baselines by 20–30 points. FlowInOne also attains the best spatial precision scores across all three VLM evaluators (3.42/5 Gemini3, 3.24/5 GPT5.2, 3.30/5 Qwen3.5) and the highest average DINOv3 Similarity of 48.7%, surpassing Nano Banana's 47.3%. Under GPT5.2 evaluation, FlowInOne marginally edges out the commercial Nano Banana model (39.2% vs. 39.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowInOne decisively outperforms all three open-source baselines across every evaluator: it leads OmniGen2 by ~29 points (54.0% vs. 24.8% under Gemini3), Kontext by ~27 points (54.0% vs. 27.1%), and Qwen-IE-2509 by ~28 points (54.0% vs. 25.8%). Against the commercial Nano Banana model, FlowInOne is highly competitive, winning under GPT5.2 (39.2% vs. 39.1%) and trailing by only 1–5 points under the other evaluators. On fine-grained spatial and physical control (DINOv3 Similarity), FlowInOne achieves 48.7% average versus Nano Banana's 47.3%, with particularly large margins in force understanding (53.6% vs. 47.4%) and trajectory understanding (57.0% vs. 48.6%). On spatial precision (Table 2), FlowInOne scores the highest among all five methods under all three VLM evaluators, while Nano Banana retains an edge in visual realism (CLIP-IQA: 0.688 vs. 0.684). Overall, FlowInOne establishes the strongest open-source baseline in the image-in, image-out paradigm, approaching commercial quality while offering a fully unified architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowInOne builds directly on the CrossFlow framework (CVPR 2025) for noise-free cross-modality evolution and on Flow Matching (ICLR 2023) as the transport mechanism, but departs fundamentally from the text-dominant conditioning paradigm that underpins LDM, DiT, and SDXL-style pipelines. It extends the recent vision-centric trend—rendering text into pixel space for perception tasks—into the generative domain, eliminating text encoders and cross-modal alignment modules entirely. The Dual-Path Spatially-Adaptive Modulation is a novel architectural contribution that addresses the structural-preservation vs. instruction-adherence tension specific to unifying generation and editing in a single visual flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a concrete, trainable recipe for fully vision-centric generation, supported by a 5M-sample dataset and a four-dimensional benchmark that the field lacked for this paradigm. However, the 256×256 resolution, reliance on VLM-based evaluation, and still-modest text-in-image editing scores (~35% pass rate) indicate the paradigm has not yet matched the maturity of text-conditioned diffusion at scale. Open questions include whether the vision-centric formulation scales to high-resolution synthesis, whether it generalizes to video or 3D, and whether the spatial-precision advantage persists when competing against increasingly strong text-conditioned baselines with richer conditioning signals.
