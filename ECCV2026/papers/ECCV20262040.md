## Problem

Existing interactive point cloud refinement methods (e.g., ISSR) treat each user correction click as single-point supervision, confining updates to a small neighborhood and producing fragmented, instance-incoherent corrections. This 'Local Correction Trap' forces redundant interactions on the same structure because the semantic intent behind a click—to revise a coherent region—is discarded. The paper asks how to propagate sparse corrective intent to reliable, semantically consistent regions while maintaining stable behavior across multiple interaction rounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262040/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene and a sequence of sparse correction clicks (each specifying a point index and its correct class label) as input, and produces refined per-point semantic labels along with an updated segmentation model after each interaction round. Each human click is expanded into a set of virtual clicks (m uniformly sampled + n top-affinity points from the intent-consistent region) before the model is fine-tuned with 5 gradient steps per round. An external instance-prior module (NPISeg3D) is queried per click to provide object-level structural constraints, and a history bank accumulates pseudo-labeled supervision across rounds for replay.

## Method

InSeg's core mechanism is tri-mask intent propagation: each click is treated as a semantic seed whose corrective label is propagated to an Intent-Consistent Region (ICR) defined as the intersection of (1) a feature-similarity mask (cosine similarity ≥ τ_sim to the clicked point's feature), (2) a spatial-connectivity mask (DBSCAN connected component on the class-filtered subset containing the click), and (3) an instance-prior mask from NPISeg3D. Virtual clicks are sampled from the ICR and combined with the original click to form a dense correction loss. For multi-round stability, InSeg introduces a two-branch stabilization objective: interaction-aware entropy minimization that excludes the current ICR from entropy regularization (decoupling correction from confidence pressure), and a history-aware replay loss that re-anchors the model on previously corrected, high-confidence regions to prevent catastrophic overwriting. The total per-round loss is L_corr + β·L_ent + γ·L_hist, applied as online fine-tuning on top of a frozen pre-trained backbone (SparseUNet, PT, RandLA-Net, or PTv3).

## Results

On ScanNet with SparseUNet, InSeg achieves 83.6 mIoU / 90.5 mAcc after only 5 clicks, improving over ISSR (75.1 / 87.8) by +8.5 mIoU. On S3DIS with Point Transformer, InSeg reaches 82.0 mIoU @5 vs. ISSR's 81.5, and on SemanticKITTI with RandLA-Net it attains 62.1 mIoU @5 vs. ISSR's 53.1. In terms of interaction efficiency, InSeg raises the completion rate (scenes reaching target mIoU within 5 clicks) from 57.5% to 78.5% on ScanNet and from 35.7% to 59.2% on SemanticKITTI. With the SOTA PTv3 backbone, InSeg still yields consistent gains, reaching 91.38 mIoU @5 versus ISSR's 90.80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InSeg is compared against two baselines: ISSR (the closest 3D interactive refinement method) and IA (a 2D interactive segmentation method adapted to 3D). InSeg decisively outperforms IA across all three datasets, with the largest gap on SemanticKITTI where InSeg achieves 62.1 mIoU @5 versus IA's 37.8. Against ISSR, InSeg wins on ScanNet by a substantial margin (+8.5 mIoU @5, +6.5 @10, +3.0 @20) and on SemanticKITTI (+9.0 mIoU @5), while the advantage on S3DIS is narrower (+0.5 mIoU @5, +1.0 @20), suggesting the tri-mask propagation is most beneficial when the backbone's initial predictions contain more structured errors. InSeg also achieves higher completion rates in every setting, most notably on ScanNet (78.5% vs. 57.5% of scenes reaching target mIoU within 5 clicks) and SemanticKITTI (59.2% vs. 35.7%). The paper further reports that InSeg reduces repeated clicks on the same instance (170 vs. 233 instances), confirming that intent propagation avoids the fragmented, redundant corrections characteristic of point-wise baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

InSeg directly extends ISSR [34], the closest 3D interactive refinement framework, by reframing each correction click from a single-point constraint into a semantic seed that propagates to an intent-consistent region via a tri-mask mechanism (feature affinity, spatial connectivity, instance prior). It also draws on 2D interactive segmentation ideas (IA [13], SAM [11]) and instance-level cues from class-agnostic 3D segmentation (NPISeg3D [16]), but departs from the point-wise supervision paradigm that both ISSR and IA rely on. The history-aware stabilization (replay loss plus interaction-aware entropy exclusion) goes beyond ISSR's single-round entropy minimization to address error accumulation across multiple interaction rounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment pipelines: with only 2–3 simulated clicks, a lightweight backbone like SparseUNet can surpass much heavier pre-trained models (PTv3, Sonata, Concerto) on ScanNet, reducing the annotation burden for scene-specific correction. The 'Local Correction Trap' framing provides a useful diagnostic for why naive point-wise interactive methods underperform, and the intent-propagation paradigm could inform future interactive tools beyond segmentation. Key open questions include whether cross-scene memory can accumulate corrections across sessions, whether superpoint-based priors can simplify the tri-mask pipeline, and how the approach behaves when the underlying backbone is already near-saturated.
