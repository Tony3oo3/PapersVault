## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration methods built on Mixture-of-Experts architectures rely exclusively on vanilla fidelity-oriented experts, lacking task-adaptive generative priors that could recover realistic structures and textures under complex degradations. The paper identifies that Masked Auto-Regressive (MAR) and Diffusion models exhibit complementary strengths—MAR excels at local degradations (shadow, inpainting) while Diffusion excels at global degradations (noise, rain)—but no prior work has systematically integrated both paradigms into a unified restoration framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded RGB image (processed as 128×128 patches during training) and outputs a restored RGB image of the same resolution. At inference, a tunable scalar g ∈ [0,1] (analogous to classifier-free guidance) linearly interpolates between the pure vanilla reconstruction (g=0, maximum pixel fidelity) and the fully generative-enhanced output (g=1, maximum perceptual quality), allowing users to navigate the fidelity-perception trade-off without retraining. A single trained model handles up to 10 degradation types (denoising, deraining, dehazing, deblurring, low-light, shadow, inpainting, JPEG compression, and composited degradations) without task-specific prompts.

## Method

MoSE employs a dual-level expert system: at the image level, a U-shaped vanilla expert (SfhFormer encoder + MoSE-block decoder) is paired with a MaskGIT-style MAR expert (iterative masked prediction with adjustable mask size T_m) and an SDE-based Diffusion expert (iterative denoising with adjustable step count T_d); at the feature level, lightweight generative blocks (low-rank reparameterized convolutions) produce simplified MAR and Diffusion priors that are injected into the decoder via Wavelet-guided Frequency-division Prior Injection (WFPI), which routes MAR output to low-frequency structural channels and Diffusion output to high-frequency textural channels. A DINOv3-guided Synergy Controllable Routing (SCR) module performs hard routing at the feature level ({0,1} masks) and soft routing at the image level, while simultaneously learning task-optimal mask sizes and iteration steps to modulate generation intensity. Training proceeds in two phases—expert warmup followed by end-to-end joint optimization with stochastic path selection (p=0.2) between generative and vanilla outputs—and the total loss combines L1 reconstruction, DINOv3 semantic alignment, and a routing complexity penalty that encourages lighter configurations for simpler degradations.

## Results

On the AIO-10 benchmark (10 degradation types), MoSE achieves 28.46 dB PSNR, 0.861 SSIM, and 0.126 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline JarvisIR (1300M params) by 1.03 dB in PSNR while using only 42M parameters. On AIO-5, MoSE reaches 30.81 dB PSNR and 0.923 SSIM, leading all competitors including MoCE-IR (30.58 dB) and FoundIR (30.52 dB). MoSE also achieves the best LPIPS (0.126) in AIO-10, marginally surpassing DA-CLIP (0.127), demonstrating superior perceptual quality alongside top fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoSE outperforms all 19 baselines on both PSNR and SSIM across AIO-3, AIO-5, and AIO-10 settings. The most notable margin is over JarvisIR (1300M params, CVPR 25): MoSE gains +1.03 dB PSNR on AIO-10 (28.46 vs 27.43) with only 3.2% of the parameters, and +0.30 dB on AIO-5 (30.81 vs 30.51). Against the closest same-scale competitor MoCE-IR (25M params), MoSE leads by +1.17 dB PSNR on AIO-10 and +0.23 dB on AIO-5, while also improving LPIPS from 0.162 to 0.126. On perceptual quality (LPIPS), MoSE achieves the best score (0.126) in AIO-10, narrowly beating DA-CLIP (0.127) and substantially outperforming MoCE-IR (0.162) and FoundIR (0.144). On composited degradations (CDD11), MoSE attains the best average PSNR (29.21 dB) and lowest LPIPS (0.189), surpassing even the 1300M-parameter JarvisIR (29.06 dB, 0.203 LPIPS). The method also leads on individual tasks in AIO-5: best PSNR on deraining (38.42 dB), denoising (31.65 dB), deblurring (30.24 dB), and low-light (23.14 dB), while trailing MoCE-IR by only 0.13 dB on dehazing (30.61 vs 30.48).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the all-in-one image restoration line (AirNet, PromptIR, MoCE-IR, JarvisIR, FoundIR) by identifying that existing MoE architectures rely exclusively on vanilla fidelity-oriented experts and lack task-adaptive generative priors. It draws on MaskGIT-style masked auto-regressive modeling for local structure recovery and SDE-based diffusion (IR-SDE, ResShift) for global detail restoration, integrating both as complementary experts within a dual-level MoE framework. The key departure from prior MoE work is the deep coupling of generative paradigms with a fidelity backbone via wavelet-guided frequency-division injection and a DINOv3-guided semantic router, rather than simply assigning experts by receptive field or complexity as in MoCE-IR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical controllable fidelity-perception trade-off (parameter g) that lets users navigate between pixel-level accuracy and perceptual quality at inference, which is valuable for deployment in diverse downstream applications. The emergent routing behavior—where the DINOv3-guided router spontaneously assigns MAR to local degradations and Diffusion to global ones without explicit supervision—provides evidence that semantic representation spaces can encode degradation-type structure. Open questions include whether the dual-level (image + feature) expert design is strictly necessary versus a simpler single-level integration, how the approach scales beyond 10 degradation types, and whether the 42M-parameter budget can be further reduced while preserving the generative prior benefits.
