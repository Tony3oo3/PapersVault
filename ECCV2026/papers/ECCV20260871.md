## Problem

Deploying RL-based Visual Active Tracking (VAT) trackers from simulators to the real world suffers from two bottlenecks: severe visual domain shift causing performance collapse, and target distribution shift from similar-looking distractors that mislead the tracker. Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods rely on output entropy for sample selection, but VAT trackers exhibit over-confidence (near-identical entropy) during both successful tracking and target loss, rendering entropy an unreliable adaptation signal. Moreover, standard TTA lacks any mechanism to correct the sudden, irrational action jumps that distractors induce.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the method consumes a single egocentric RGB frame (from a simulator or a real drone camera) together with a sliding buffer of the previous K rotation commands. It outputs a control action—either a discrete 7-way command (forward, backward, left, right, turn left, turn right, stop) or a continuous 3D velocity vector [v_x, v_y, ω]—and updated affine parameters (scale and shift) of the batch normalization layers. Only these BN parameters are adapted at test time; the backbone weights remain frozen, and for IL-based trackers (e.g., TrackVLA) the analogous Layer Normalization parameters are updated instead.

## Method

VATA combines two complementary test-time adaptation objectives. First, a Critic Value Maximization strategy replaces the conventional entropy-based sample filter: the critic value Q(s_t, a_t) is shown to be far more discriminative than output entropy (66% drop on target loss vs. only 3–4% entropy change), so samples passing a threshold Q_0 are selected and the model is updated to maximize the critic value, bridging the OOD-to-ID confidence gap. Second, an Action Smoothness Regularization term, grounded in a Lipschitz-continuity proposition bounding optimal action variation by L·v_max, penalizes rotation jumps that exceed a threshold δ relative to a K-step moving average, thereby suppressing distractor-induced erratic steering. The two losses are weighted and summed (L_total = λ_v·L_val + λ_s·L_smooth), and a single gradient step on the BN parameters is applied every 5 steps after a 50-step warm-up. The action smoothness term is restricted to continuous-control tasks and is shown to extend naturally to IL-based trajectory planners by comparing newly planned waypoints against the remaining path from the previous plan.

## Results

On EVT-Bench, VATA achieves 22.4% average Success Rate, surpassing the SOTA TTA method TARL (18.0%) by 24.4% and the base tracker EVT (15.1%) by 48.3%. On the DAT benchmark, VATA reaches 0.67 average Tracking Success Rate, outperforming TARL (0.64) by 4.7% and the base GC-VAT (0.62) by 8.1%. On UnrealCV with distractors, VATA attains 0.85 average SR, beating TARL (0.82) by 3.7% and EVT (0.79) by 7.6%. In real-world drone deployment, VATA achieves 80% TSR across 10 diverse scenarios, a 4× improvement over the base tracker (20%) and a 2.7× improvement over TARL (30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VATA consistently outperforms all five TTA baselines (TENT, EATA, COME, TARL) and both base trackers (EVT, GC-VAT) across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest TTA baseline TARL, VATA gains 24.4% average SR on EVT-Bench (22.4% vs 18.0%), 4.7% TSR on DAT (0.67 vs 0.64), and 3.7% SR on UnrealCV (0.85 vs 0.82), with the largest relative gains in distractor-heavy tasks (DT: 64.7% SR improvement over TARL). The entropy-based methods TENT and EATA show the weakest performance, confirming the paper's argument that output entropy is an unreliable adaptation signal for VAT. COME, which uses Dirichlet-prior uncertainty, performs slightly better than EATA but still trails VATA by 4.8% average SR on EVT-Bench. For the IL-based TrackVLA baseline, applying VATA's action smoothness regularization alone yields a 45.7% SR improvement on the DT task (11.6% → 16.9%), demonstrating the regularization's generality beyond RL-based trackers.

## Relation to Prior Work

VATA extends the test-time adaptation paradigm from TARL (entropy-based sample selection for offline RL) and EATA (entropy filtering with Fisher regularization) by replacing the standard entropy signal with critic values from the actor-critic architecture, motivated by the empirical finding that VAT trackers become over-confident during failure. It further introduces an action smoothness regularization term grounded in a Lipschitz-continuity bound on optimal tracking actions, which has no direct analogue in prior TTA work. Unlike TENT, EATA, COME, and TARL—which are evaluated only in simulators under mild OOD shifts—VATA is explicitly designed and validated for the sim-to-real gap in embodied visual tracking, making it the first TTA method deployed on a physical drone for VAT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× real-world improvement (20% → 80% TSR on a DJI Tello drone) and the 24.4% SR gain on EVT-Bench over SOTA TTA demonstrate that critic-value-driven adaptation can substantially close the sim-to-real gap in embodied tracking, a problem that has resisted prior TTA approaches. The action smoothness regularization is notably generic, extending to IL-based trackers like TrackVLA (45.7% SR gain), suggesting broader applicability beyond RL. A key limitation is the failure to recover after prolonged occlusion, as the method lacks trajectory planning from historical memory—a challenge that remains open in unsupervised TTA paradigms where no external supervision is available.
