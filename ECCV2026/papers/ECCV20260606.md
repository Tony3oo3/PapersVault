## Problem

Existing decoupled dataset distillation methods rely on coarse class-label supervision, which on fine-grained datasets produces distilled samples that (i) retain unfavorable fine-grained characteristics—large intra-class variation with subtle inter-class differences—and (ii) become overly similar within the same class, limiting localized discriminative cues and degrading recognition performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260606/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large labeled RGB image dataset (all images resized to 224×224) with class labels and produces a compact synthetic dataset with M ≪ N samples (equal count per class) along with soft labels generated by the backbone branch. It operates as a plug-in module on top of existing decoupled DD pipelines (e.g., SRe²L++, FADRM+), requiring no modification to their original training workflow. The teacher uses a Backbone+CAL dual-classifier architecture during pretraining and distillation, while post-evaluation trains a plain backbone student to avoid architectural mismatch.

## Method

FD² augments decoupled dataset distillation with fine-grained supervision via Counterfactual Attention Learning (CAL), which uses a factual–counterfactual contrastive signal to produce reliable attention maps localizing discriminative regions and to maintain class prototypes through momentum updates. During distillation, two complementary constraints are added as plug-in terms to the original objective: a fine-grained characteristic constraint (Eq. 9) that pulls each distilled sample's representation toward its target-class prototype while repelling other-class prototypes, improving intra-class compactness and inter-class separability; and a similarity constraint (Eq. 10) that maximizes the distance between the current sample's attention map and those of previously generated same-class samples, encouraging diverse coverage of discriminative regions. Both classifiers (backbone and CAL) jointly supervise class signals during distillation, and the final loss is a weighted combination of the original distillation loss, the dual-branch classification loss, and the two proposed constraints controlled by hyperparameters λ and β.

## Results

On fine-grained datasets, FD² integrated into SRe²L++ achieves top-1 accuracy of 70.1% on CUB-200-2011 (ResNet50, IPC=1), 80.0% on FGVC-Aircraft (ResNet18, IPC=5), and 80.7% on Stanford Cars (ResNet50, IPC=1), surpassing the SRe²L++ baseline by 9.0, 11.7, and 15.1 percentage points respectively. Gains over FADRM+ are smaller but still positive in most settings, reaching up to 13.8% on Stanford Cars (ResNet18, IPC=1). On general datasets (ImageNette, ImageWoof), FADRM+_FD² improves over FADRM+ by up to 10.5% (ImageWoof, ResNet50, IPC=10), though a minor 0.6% drop is observed on ImageNette with ResNet18 at IPC=1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SRe²L++ (the primary decoupled baseline), FD² wins in every fine-grained setting, with gains ranging from +3.0% (CUB-200-2011, ResNet18, IPC=1) to +15.1% (Stanford Cars, ResNet50, IPC=1), and the advantage grows with IPC and model capacity. Against FADRM+, FD² also wins in most settings but with smaller margins (typically +0.2% to +5.5% on CUB-200-2011 and FGVC-Aircraft), while on Stanford Cars the gains are larger (+6.8% to +13.8%), suggesting FADRM+'s multi-scale residual design partially mitigates the fine-grained issues that FD² targets. RDED (a cropping-based method) is consistently the weakest baseline across all datasets and IPCs, trailing the decoupled methods by 10–50+ points, confirming that coarse spatial cropping does not reliably capture discriminative regions. On general datasets (ImageNette, ImageWoof), FADRM+_FD² outperforms FADRM+ in 5 of 6 settings (e.g., +10.4% on ImageWoof ResNet50 IPC=1, +6.9% on ImageNette ResNet50 IPC=50), with a single 0.6% drop on ImageNette ResNet18 IPC=1, indicating that the fine-grained cues are most beneficial for higher-capacity students or datasets with subtler inter-class differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

FD² extends the decoupled dataset distillation paradigm (SRe²L, SRe²L++, FADRM+) by injecting fine-grained supervision that prior methods lack. It repurposes Counterfactual Attention Learning (CAL, Rao et al., ICCV 2021) — originally designed for fine-grained recognition — as a teacher component that provides attention maps and class prototypes, then uses these signals to construct two new plug-in constraints (a prototype-alignment/repulsion loss and an attention-diversity loss) during the sample-distillation stage. Conceptually, the paper departs from the standard assumption that coarse class-label supervision suffices for distillation, arguing that on fine-grained datasets the distilled set inherits unfavorable intra-class dispersion and excessive within-class similarity unless explicitly corrected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means FD² can be dropped into any existing decoupled DD pipeline without architectural changes, making it immediately useful for practitioners working on fine-grained recognition with limited data budgets. However, gains on general-purpose datasets (ImageNette, ImageWoof) are more modest and sometimes negative for small-capacity students, suggesting the fine-grained constraints are most beneficial when inter-class differences are genuinely subtle. The method is currently limited to CNN backbones; the authors note that extending to ViT-based models and ImageNet-1K scale remains open, as does understanding whether the prototype-based supervision generalizes to non-visual modalities or tasks without natural spatial structure.
