## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D scene graph generation suffers from semantic inconsistency when the same object is described differently across views due to occlusions and constrained camera trajectories. Existing methods blindly aggregate these conflicting per-view captions or embeddings without discrimination, and remain 'passive observers' restricted to whatever viewpoints were captured, even when the clearest view was never recorded. This noise-induced inaccuracy is unacceptable for safety-critical robotic downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB-D image sequence with corresponding camera poses, segmented via SAM and encoded with CLIP ViT-H-14. It produces a 3D scene graph where each node carries a point cloud, a CLIP semantic embedding, and a refined text caption, while edges carry relationship captions constrained to eight categories (on, in, part of, near, none, and their bidirectional counterparts). The pipeline is offline and operates on fused global object lists rather than individual frames, with a dynamic voxel size (base 0.01 m scaled by bounding-box diagonal) governing point-cloud density per object.

## Method

RAG-3DSG implements a two-phase 'diagnose-then-rectify' paradigm. In the diagnosis phase, it virtually re-shoots each object from 64 candidate cameras on a hemisphere, scoring views by point visibility, horizontal alignment (penalizing top-down/bottom-up), and consistency with the prior average viewing direction; the top-scoring view is rendered and captioned by a VLM, then compared against passive crop captions via CLIP cosine similarity and KMeans clustering (K=3) to yield a per-object reliability score. In the rectification phase, low-uncertainty objects form a retrieval document, and high-uncertainty objects retrieve their nearest low-uncertainty neighbor's caption as augmented context; a composite image (re-shot render + best passive crop) plus this textual context is fed to a VLM to generate a refined caption. Edge captions are then produced by an LLM prompted with few-shot spatial-reasoning examples over the refined node set, using a dynamic connectivity threshold aligned with the downsampling strategy.

## Results

On SceneFun3D, Ours-GPT achieves 90.2% Object R@10 and 97.9% Edge R@10, setting new state-of-the-art over OpenFunGraph (87.8% and 96.2% respectively). On the more cluttered FunGraph3D benchmark, Ours-GPT reaches 75.0% Object R@3 and 83.0% Object R@10, exceeding the previous best by 4.3% and 3.9% margins. Even the locally deployed Ours-LLaVA variant surpasses OpenFunGraph on FunGraph3D Object R@3 (73.6% vs. 70.7%), demonstrating that the diagnose-then-rectify framework compensates for raw model capacity. Under injected pose noise (σ_trans=0.02 m, σ_rot=1°), performance degrades by less than 1% for Ours-GPT on SceneFun3D Object R@3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAG-3DSG (Ours-GPT) outperforms the strongest baseline OpenFunGraph [43] on all four FunGraph3D metrics (Object R@3: 75.0 vs. 70.7; Object R@10: 83.0 vs. 79.1; Edge R@5: 65.7 vs. 65.1; Edge R@10: 91.4 vs. 91.4) and on SceneFun3D Object R@10 (90.2 vs. 87.8) and Edge R@10 (97.9 vs. 96.2), though OpenFunGraph retains a slight edge on SceneFun3D Edge R@5 (88.1 vs. 86.2). Against ConceptGraphs [8], the gains are substantially larger: Ours-GPT exceeds it by 11.7 points on FunGraph3D Object R@3 (75.0 vs. 56.6) and by 13.1 points on FunGraph3D Edge R@10 (91.4 vs. 84.6). The locally deployed Ours-LLaVA variant also beats OpenFunGraph on FunGraph3D Object R@3 (73.6 vs. 70.7) and Object R@10 (81.1 vs. 79.1), showing the framework's effectiveness is not solely dependent on a proprietary VLM. Open3DSG [18] trails all other methods by wide margins (e.g., 47.6% vs. 75.0% on FunGraph3D Object R@3), confirming that its point-cloud-only pipeline without VLM-based captioning is insufficient for open-vocabulary scene understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary 3D scene graph generation pipeline established by ConceptGraphs [8] and OpenFunGraph [43], which both follow a passive per-image extraction followed by cross-view aggregation paradigm. The key departure is the introduction of active re-shot verification, drawing conceptual inspiration from active viewpoint reasoning in broader 3D perception (Think3D [44], Chain-of-View [45]), to diagnose semantic ambiguity before rectifying it via an Object-level RAG module. The edge generation pipeline is directly inherited from ConceptGraphs with a dynamic threshold modification, while the node-level uncertainty estimation and retrieval-augmented caption refinement are novel contributions not present in prior 3DSG methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical failure mode in robotic scene understanding: blind cross-view aggregation corrupts 3DSG semantics under occlusion, which is unacceptable in safety-critical deployment. The diagnose-then-rectify framing and the finding that isolated re-shots without contextual anchoring actually degrade performance (59.4% vs. 83.0% R@3) provide a useful design principle for future active-perception pipelines. Limitations include the system being offline-only, the RAG mechanism requiring a sufficient pool of low-uncertainty anchors (potentially problematic in highly cluttered or novel scenes), and the edge taxonomy being constrained to eight categories despite the open-vocabulary node space. The real-world robotic deployment with LiDAR-IMU odometry strengthens practical credibility but leaves open the question of real-time integration.
