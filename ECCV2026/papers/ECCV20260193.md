## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks evaluate static relational queries or action plausibility in isolation, without requiring models to maintain an internal spatial state across sequential manipulations and use it to judge whether each action advances toward a goal. It remains unclear whether current VLMs can construct, update, and reason over a coherent scene representation under partial observability to produce correct goal-directed action evaluations over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260193/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each turn, the model receives only the current monocular egocentric frame (not the full history), a goal state image, and a labeled block reference image provided at Turn 0. The model must output a fixed JSON structure containing: the manipulated block label (Q1), the previously manipulated block label (Q2, from Turn 2 onward), the set of side-face-contacting blocks (Q3), a 10-category action evaluation with free-text justification (Q4), and a conditional undo-feasibility judgment (Q4-2) triggered only when Q4 indicates an error. No depth maps, geometric annotations, or stereo views are supplied.

## Method

The benchmark comprises two controlled block-manipulation tasks—tangram (7 color-coded blocks, 150 scenarios) and wooden puzzle (16 irregularly shaped blocks, 132 scenarios)—totaling 282 scenarios and 1,801 evaluation turns, each containing both correct and error-injected sequences. A multi-turn QA protocol diagnostically isolates four capabilities: visual perception (Q1), temporal state tracking (Q2), fine-grained spatial contact reasoning (Q3, requiring exact side-face adjacency enumeration), and goal-directed action evaluation (Q4, with a structured 10-option error taxonomy). Task 1 systematically inserts single hand-occlusion frames at varying turn positions to test state maintenance under partial observability, while Task 2 introduces multi-contact geometric crowding (3+ simultaneous contacts) and mirror-image block pairs. Nine VLMs (three frontier API, six open-weight local) are evaluated zero-shot with fixed prompts, and performance is decomposed per question, per turn position, and per contact count to pinpoint where and why models fail.

## Results

On Task 1 (Tangram, 7 blocks), GPT-5.2 achieves the highest overall accuracy at 77.0%, followed by Gemini 3 Flash Preview (74.3%) and Claude Sonnet 4.6 (62.0%). On Task 2 (Wooden Puzzle, 16 blocks), Claude Sonnet 4.6 leads at 42.8%, with GPT-5.2 at 39.3% and Gemini at 34.7%. The universal bottleneck is Q3 (spatial contact reasoning): all API models collapse to 12–16% on Task 2 despite 50–75% on Task 1, and no local model exceeds 25% on Q3 in either task. Q4 (action evaluation) degrades sharply over turns, dropping from above 79% at Turn 1 to below 16% by Turn 8 for all API models, while Q1 (perception) remains stable above 80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates nine VLMs in a zero-shot setting, revealing a clear API-vs-local gap: the best API model (GPT-5.2, 77.0% on Task 1) outperforms the best local model (InternVL3-8B, 29.3%) by over 47 points, and the gap persists on Task 2 (39.3% vs. 17.5%). Among API models, GPT-5.2 leads Task 1 overall but Claude Sonnet 4.6 reverses the ranking on Task 2 (42.8% vs. 39.3%), largely due to stronger Q1 block identification (65.6% vs. 58.7%) and Q4 evaluation (36.1% vs. 34.4%). All three API models share the same critical failure: Q3 contact reasoning collapses to 12–16% on Task 2, with GPT-5.2 dropping from 74.9% (Task 1) to 15.9% (Task 2) and Gemini from 69.5% to 12.2%. Among local models, InternVL3-8B and Qwen3-VL-8B are the strongest on Task 1 (29.3% and 28.1%), but the newer InternVL3.5-8B (17.6%) underperforms its predecessor, and VideoChat-R1-7B (18.0%), despite reinforcement fine-tuning for reasoning, scores below 1% on Task 1 Q4. No model—API or local—reliably detects Q4 option 3 ('correct but blocked by a past error'), which appears in 29% of Task 1 turns; GPT-5.2 predicts it zero times across both tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of single-turn spatial relational queries (VSR, SpatialBench, What'sUp) and descriptive video understanding (EgoSchema, MVBench) by introducing a multi-turn, state-based diagnostic protocol that requires models to maintain an internal spatial representation across sequential manipulations and evaluate action correctness against a goal. It also extends occlusion reasoning beyond perceptual recovery (CATER, CLEVRER) to test whether models can sustain spatial state under partial observability and resume correct reasoning afterward. The 'amsan' (mental arithmetic) framing deliberately positions the task as internal state construction and manipulation rather than surface-level relation recognition, unifying capabilities that prior benchmarks evaluate in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a clean dissociation: perception (Q1) remains stable at 80–100% across 8 turns while action evaluation (Q4) collapses from above 79% to below 16%, suggesting current VLMs process each turn largely independently rather than integrating accumulated scene state. The finding that no model reliably detects 'correct but blocked by a past error' (present in 29% of Task 1 turns) exposes a compound reasoning gap—simultaneously judging local correctness while recalling a prior error—that has no analogue in existing evaluations. Limitations include monocular-only input, rigid 2D blocks, zero-shot fixed prompts, and no deformable objects or tool use, leaving open whether stereo views, chain-of-thought prompting, or architectures with explicit spatial state buffers would close the gap.
