## Problem

Existing training-free high-resolution image generation methods predominantly target U-Net-based diffusion models, leaving off-the-shelf Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models such as FLUX and Stable Diffusion 3 largely unaddressed. When these DiT models are naively adapted to higher resolutions, they suffer from two critical issues: spatial disorder in generated images and prohibitively long generation times dominated by multi-head attention computations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261112/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces high-resolution RGB images at 2K (2048×2048), 4K (4096×4096), or 8K (8192×8192) resolutions, operating entirely on off-the-shelf DiT models originally trained at 1024×1024. A one-time preparatory stage (taking less than an hour) determines per-head attention scope assignments before any inference begins; during per-input generation, the model simply feeds higher-resolution initial noise into the DiT backbone with the precompiled scope plan and SPA-modified positional embeddings applied.

## Method

HRDiT integrates two training-free components. Spatial Position Alignment (SPA) addresses spatial disorder by manipulating token indices before they enter the positional embedding function: a bundle operation groups tokens into bundles to reduce the diversity of positional signals (shrinking |S_pe| so the mechanism can distinguish them), while a slide operation creates N variants with progressively shifted bundle boundaries to recover fine-grained intra-bundle distinctions; the final attention contribution is the average over all N mapping functions. Head-adaptive Attention Pruning (HAP) addresses long generation time by assigning each attention head a scope (fraction of tokens it attends to) tailored to that head's inherent attention range; a two-step preparatory procedure first estimates per-head, per-scope quality degradation via a Taylor-expansion-based formula requiring only a single full-attention model pass, then solves an integer programming problem (via Gurobi) to find the collective scope assignment that minimizes total quality loss under a target computational budget, with the result precompiled into GPU kernels for cost-free inference-time pruning.

## Results

On 4K (4096×4096) generation, HRDiT achieves FID 64.91 and FIDp 51.84 with FLUX, and FID 68.63 and FIDp 53.04 with SD3, outperforming all baselines on every quality metric (FID, FIDp, KID, KIDp, CLIP Score). It also achieves the lowest latency among all DiT-based methods: 116 s per image with FLUX (vs. 203 s for direct adaptation and 649 s for DemoFusion) and 58 s with SD3 (vs. 108 s direct and 229 s DemoFusion). On 2K generation, the same pattern holds, with HRDiT reaching FID 64.42 (FLUX) and 67.90 (SD3) at latencies of 35 s and 11 s respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRDiT consistently outperforms all six baselines on every quality metric (FID, FIDp, KID, KIDp, CLIP Score) at both 2K and 4K resolutions for both SD3 and FLUX backbones. Against the closest DiT-tailored competitor HiFlow, HRDiT improves FID by 3.45 points (64.91 vs. 68.36) and reduces latency by 44% (116 s vs. 209 s) on FLUX 4K. Against the DiT-specific method I-Max, HRDiT achieves a 5.2-point FID improvement (64.91 vs. 70.11) and a 76% latency reduction (116 s vs. 484 s) on FLUX 4K. Against diffusion-process methods (DemoFusion, DiffuseHigh, FreCaS), HRDiT wins on all metrics while also being the fastest; for example, on SD3 4K it beats DiffuseHigh by 3.25 FID points and 2× faster (58 s vs. 115 s). Against the U-Net-based FreeScale (applied to SDXL), HRDiT with FLUX achieves substantially better FID (64.91 vs. 74.85) and FIDp (51.84 vs. 68.89) while being 2.1× faster (116 s vs. 248 s), though FreeScale retains a slight edge on KID (0.0112 vs. 0.0078) and KIDp (0.0157 vs. 0.0074) — actually HRDiT also wins on KID/KIDp here. The ablation (Table 2) confirms SPA contributes the quality gain (FID 75.85→64.82) while HAP contributes the speed gain (212 s→116 s) with negligible quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free high-resolution image generation line of work (DemoFusion, DiffuseHigh, HiFlow, FreeScale, I-Max) from U-Net-based diffusion models to the now-dominant Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture. It draws on two distinct research threads: positional embedding manipulation techniques from LLM length extrapolation (LM-Infinite, LongDiff) to address spatial disorder via the SPA component, and head-adaptive attention pruning from LLM compression (MoA) to address computational cost via the HAP component. The key departure from prior work is the identification and principled treatment of two DiT-specific failure modes (spatial disorder from limited positional embedding expressiveness, and quadratic attention cost) that existing U-Net-focused methods do not encounter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DiT-based models (FLUX, SD3) have become the de facto standard for text-to-image generation, a training-free path to high-resolution synthesis without retraining is practically important for practitioners who cannot afford large-scale high-resolution training. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) provides a principled, if somewhat loose, explanation for why spatial disorder emerges at high resolutions, which could inform future architectural designs. Open questions include whether the one-time HAP preparatory stage (requiring a few model passes and a linear solver call) can be further reduced or made fully automatic, whether the approach generalizes to video DiT models, and whether the SPA bundle size N can be made adaptive per layer or per resolution rather than a fixed hyperparameter.
