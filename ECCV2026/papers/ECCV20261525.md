## Problem

Diffusion Transformers incur high inference latency because each denoising step performs full quadratic self-attention over all spatial tokens, and existing training-free acceleration methods (token merging, uniform caching, generic sparse attention) apply the same reduction uniformly across the image. This uniform treatment is suboptimal because denoising is spatially non-uniform with respect to aesthetic descriptors: regions linked to aesthetic tokens in the prompt receive concentrated cross-attention and require precise computation, while smooth background regions evolve smoothly and are redundant. No prior method exploits this aesthetics-driven spatial non-uniformity to allocate compute adaptively while simultaneously improving perceptual quality.

## Input / Output

AccelAes takes a free-form text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image via standard DiT sampling (30 steps for Lumina-Next, 28 for SD3-Medium and FLUX.1-dev). The prompt is encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder; the method is entirely training-free and modifies only inference-time computation. A one-shot aesthetic mask is constructed at a fixed early timestep (mask_step=5) and reused for the entire sampling trajectory, so no per-step prompt re-encoding or external aesthetic scorer is needed at inference.

## Method

AccelAes couples two mechanisms. First, AesMask identifies aesthetically sensitive spatial tokens by computing CLIP cosine similarity between prompt tokens and a fixed 34-word aesthetic anchor vocabulary, selecting top-scoring tokens, then aggregating cross-attention weights over those tokens across layers and binarizing via percentile thresholding to yield a binary focus mask. Second, SkipSparse uses this mask to (a) update self-attention queries and FFN only on focus tokens while retaining global keys/values for long-range context, (b) apply spatially-varying classifier-free guidance with a stronger scale on focus tokens, and (c) reuse noise predictions across timesteps via a step-level cache with linear extrapolation, refreshing every Δ=2 steps after a 5-step warmup. The key insight is that aesthetic-token-affine regions vary more across timesteps and are harder to extrapolate, so they receive full computation, while low-affinity regions are safely cached or skipped, yielding a 2.11× speedup on Lumina-Next with improved ImageReward (+11.9%) over the dense baseline.

## Results

On Lumina-Next-T2I, AccelAes achieves a 2.11× speedup (12.37 s → 5.86 s per image) while improving ImageReward from 0.752 to 0.841 (+11.9%), CLIP from 0.2531 to 0.2640, HPSv2 from 0.2710 to 0.2740, and LAION Aesthetic Score from 5.941 to 6.041. On SD3-Medium, it reaches 1.50× speedup with IR improving from 0.879 to 0.904; on FLUX.1-dev, 1.73× speedup with IR improving from 1.233 to 1.317. Across all three backbones, AccelAes is the only method that simultaneously accelerates inference and improves every preference-oriented quality metric over the dense baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AccelAes outperforms all six baselines on both speed and quality simultaneously, a combination none of the other methods achieve. TaylorSeer [21] is the closest competitor in speed (2.05× vs. 2.11×) but suffers a severe ImageReward drop (0.604 vs. 0.841, −28%), indicating that uniform temporal forecasting degrades preference-oriented quality. Δ-DiT [6] and SDiT [19] achieve moderate speedups (1.52×, 1.69×) but IR collapses to 0.485 and 0.609 respectively, well below even the dense baseline. FORA [38] provides essentially no speedup (1.00×) while degrading IR to 0.517, making it the weakest baseline overall. RAS [23] (1.47×) and TeaCache [20] (1.49×) preserve IR closer to baseline (0.788, 0.670) but still fall short of AccelAes's 0.841 and are 30–45% slower. In the human preference study, AccelAes is preferred over the dense baseline 67% of the time and over FORA 89% of the time (p < 0.001), confirming that the automatic metric gains reflect genuine perceptual improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

AccelAes sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: training-free DiT acceleration (token merging, sparse attention, feature caching, timestep forecasting) and aesthetic/preference-oriented guidance in text-to-image generation. Prior acceleration methods (Δ-DiT, FORA, RAS, TeaCache, TaylorSeer) apply uniform spatial or temporal reduction strategies, while prior aesthetic guidance work (spatially varying CFG, attribute intensity control) keeps dense computation intact. AccelAes carves new ground by coupling these two threads: it extracts aesthetic-relevant spatial regions from cross-attention signals conditioned on prompt descriptors (AesMask) and uses them to drive both sparse compute allocation and region-wise guidance (SkipSparse), while a universal step-level prediction cache handles temporal redundancy. The key conceptual departure is treating aesthetic token affinity as a first-class signal for inference-time budgeting rather than relying on numerical similarity or fixed structural heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 2.11× speedup on Lumina-Next with simultaneous improvements in preference-oriented metrics (ImageReward +11.9%), suggesting that selective compute allocation can outperform uniform denoising on perceptual quality. This challenges the common assumption that acceleration necessarily trades off against quality. The main limitation is dependence on reliable region estimates: when attention is diffuse (e.g., dense crowds, abstract art), the static mask can freeze details outside the selected region, and the paper's corrective expansion rules recover only 92–95% of dense quality. Open questions include adaptive mask refresh schedules, confidence-based fallbacks, and whether the aesthetic-anchor vocabulary generalizes across languages and prompt styles without per-domain tuning.
