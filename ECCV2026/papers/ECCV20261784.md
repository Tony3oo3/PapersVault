## Problem

Text-to-3D Motion generation is bottlenecked by the scarcity of paired text-3D motion data, as 3D motion capture and annotation are prohibitively expensive. Prior attempts to exploit abundant 2D motion data rely on multi-view geometric pipelines (e.g., triangulation) that are camera-pose-dependent and ignore higher-level 2D motion semantics such as temporal dynamics, limiting their contribution to 3D generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261784/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a natural-language text prompt and produces a 3D human motion sequence in local rotation-based representation at 20 fps. During training, it additionally consumes single-view 2D motion sequences (OpenPose body keypoints converted to rotation-based format, sourced from MotionVid/Motion-X) as weak supervision. The 2D data is single-view rather than multi-view, and text-2D and text-3D pairs are merged into triplets via LLM sentence-embedding cosine similarity (threshold 0.9), so no explicit 2D-to-3D geometric correspondence is required.

## Method

SWSL is a three-module training framework that injects 2D motion semantics into end-to-end Text-to-3D Motion models at the embedding level. (1) SSC trains a 2D-motion encoder (E_2D) via triplet contrastive loss to align single-view 2D motions into the text-3D-aligned embedding space provided by frozen TMR encoders, yielding a shared semantic space for all three modalities. (2) SPE generates pseudo 3D labels from a pretrained Text-to-3D model, merges their 3D and 2D semantic embeddings via a weighted sum (z* = z_3D + λz_2D), and decodes the merged vector into enhanced 3D pseudo-labels that augment the training set. (3) SFO computes FID-based semantic discrepancies between the predicted 3D motion embedding and the text/3D/2D ground-truth embeddings, and optimizes the generator via reinforcement learning (SCST + PPO) because VQ dequantization truncates gradient flow. The framework is model-agnostic and has been instantiated on VQ-VAE-based (T2M-GPT, MoMask) and diffusion-based (DiP) architectures.

## Results

Under the TMR evaluation protocol on HumanML3D, SWSL yields consistent FID reductions across three baselines: SB improves from 0.625 to 0.577 (−0.048), Momask* from 0.595 to 0.539 (−0.056), and DiP* from 0.736 to 0.698 (−0.038). Under the CONVENTION protocol, the corresponding FID reductions are smaller (−0.012, −0.010, −0.017) due to the protocol's domain-specific bias. SWSL-enhanced models also improve R-Precision (e.g., Momask*+SWSL reaches 0.440 Top-1 vs. 0.435 for Momask* under TMR), MM Dist, Diversity, and MModality, while remaining competitive with SOTA methods like EnergyMoGen (FID 0.176) and AvatarGPT (FID 0.168) on the primary FID metric under CONVENTION.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWSL is applied as a training framework on top of three representative baselines (SB/T2M-GPT [32], Momask [34], DiP [33]) and consistently improves all of them on FID, R-Precision, MM Dist, Diversity, and MModality under both CONVENTION and TMR protocols. Under TMR, the largest FID gain is on Momask* (−0.056), followed by SB (−0.048) and DiP* (−0.038), indicating SWSL benefits both simple autoregressive and diffusion-based architectures. Against the broader set of baselines in Table 2 (CONVENTION), SWSL-enhanced models do not surpass the latest SOTA methods (EnergyMoGen FID 0.176, AvatarGPT FID 0.168) on R-Precision or MModality, but Momask*+SWSL achieves the best FID (0.079) among all listed methods, beating EnergyMoGen (0.176) and AvatarGPT (0.168) by a wide margin on the primary metric. The 2D-based pipeline Motion 2-to-3 [3] achieves FID 0.321 under CONVENTION, substantially worse than all SWSL-enhanced models, confirming that semantic-level 2D supervision outperforms geometric multi-view synthesis. For cross-modal alignment (Table 1), SSC's text-to-2D retrieval (R@1 = 5.22) and 3D-to-2D retrieval (R@1 = 8.90) are comparable to TMR's text-to-3D retrieval (R@1 = 5.68), validating the quality of the shared semantic space.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWSL departs from prior 2D-to-3D motion generation pipelines (Motion-2-to-3, MAS, TENDER) that treat 2D data as a geometric intermediate requiring multi-view triangulation, instead operating at the semantic-embedding level to directly optimize end-to-end 3D models. It builds on TMR's pre-trained text-3D motion aligned embedding space and ACTOR-style Transformer encoders/decoders as the backbone for cross-modal alignment. The key conceptual shift is repurposing 2D motion not as a spatial proxy for 3D reconstruction but as a source of inexact weak supervision (temporal dynamics, motion semantics) that refines pseudo-labels and provides embedding-level feedback signals for the 3D generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical route to alleviate text-3D motion data scarcity by leveraging the far more abundant single-view 2D motion data, with the 75% 3D-data reduction experiment showing only marginal FID degradation. However, the absolute FID improvements remain modest (0.01–0.06), evaluation is confined to HumanML3D, and the approach still requires text-2D motion pairs rather than fully unpaired 2D data. Open questions include whether SWSL scales to larger 2D corpora (beyond the 12K samples used), whether it can operate with zero 3D training data, and whether the semantic alignment generalizes to whole-body or multi-person motion generation.
