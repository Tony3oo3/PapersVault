## Problem

Existing 3D CT generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: text-based prompting via radiology reports offers flexibility but cannot precisely specify the shape, size, or spatial extent of abnormalities, while segmentation-based methods provide exact spatial guidance but require expensive full-organ annotations covering all structures in the volume. No prior approach allows a user to supply a partial segmentation of a single anatomy or pathology with user-defined semantics, combined optionally with a radiology report, to control high-resolution volumetric synthesis.

## Input / Output

The model accepts three optional inputs: a radiology report (Findings + Impressions, up to 512 tokens), a binary segmentation mask at 448×448×448 (normalized to [-0.5, 0.5]), and a short semantic pre-prompt (≤32 tokens) that names what the mask represents (e.g., 'lung nodule', 'pleural effusion'). Any subset of these may be provided; when a mask is absent, its latent is zeroed and the pre-prompt is replaced with a fixed 'no segmentation' sentence. The output is a 3D CT volume at 448×448×448 with 0.7 mm isotropic spacing and HU intensities in [-1024, 1024], decoded from the denoised latent via a fine-tuned Open-Sora video decoder.

## Method

MedSynV2 is a modified diffusion transformer (DiT) trained with conditional rectified flow (x-prediction) in a shared latent space. The key architectural insight is early fusion: the noisy image latent z_t and the clean segmentation latent z_m are channel-concatenated before patchification, so both are processed as a single token sequence without increasing token count, incurring negligible extra memory. Text conditioning uses a compound prompt (semantic pre-prompt + optional radiology report) encoded by CXR-BERT and injected via multi-head cross-attention with AdaLN; gated attention is applied in every self- and cross-attention layer to preserve information from long reports. A depthwise separable convolution is appended after each transformer block to enforce local spatial consistency between neighboring patches. During training, conditioning modalities are randomized per sample (report-only, mask-only, both, or neither) to ensure the model operates under any prompt combination at inference, where generation proceeds by solving the flow ODE with a 50-step Dormand-Prince solver.

## Results

On the CT-Rate validation set, MedSynV2 achieves the best mean FID across all four radiological contrast windows (Lung: 9.59, Vessel: 6.46, Soft Tissue: 8.47, Bone: 4.58), representing a ~24% relative improvement over the strongest text-only baseline Text2CT (Lung FID 12.58). For semantic alignment, MedSynV2 attains the highest Pillar-0 T2I CLIP-Score (27.61%) and the best CT-CLIP I2I score (83.00%), while also achieving the highest zero-shot classification F1 (48.40%) among all baselines. In the data augmentation experiment on SemiSeg, combining 50 real and 416 synthesized images yields a Dice of 68.13% and HD95 of 4.69 px, surpassing the SOTA segmentation model MedPSeg (Dice 65.20%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedSynV2 outperforms all three text-to-CT baselines (GenerateCT, MedSyn, Text2CT) on mean FID across every contrast window, with the largest gap on the Lung window (9.59 vs. 12.58 for Text2CT, a 24% relative improvement). GenerateCT achieves the best axial-plane FID for Lung (7.03) due to its slice-wise super-resolution design but degrades on sagittal and coronal planes; MedSyn suffers on soft-tissue generation (FID 17.52) likely due to training on a different dataset; Text2CT uses unregistered images, yielding blurry outputs. On semantic metrics, MedSynV2 leads on Pillar-0 T2I CLIP-Score (27.61% vs. 25.80% for Text2CT) and CT-CLIP I2I (83.00% vs. 81.61% for MedSyn), though Text2CT edges ahead on CT-CLIP T2I (20.17% vs. 14.74%). In the downstream segmentation augmentation task, MedSynV2's synthesized data combined with real data surpasses both MedPSeg (Dice 65.20%) and Inf-Net (Dice 62.41%), reaching 68.13% Dice and 4.69 HD95, demonstrating that the generated volumes carry clinically useful anatomical detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedSynV2 sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: text-conditioned 3D CT generation (GenerateCT, MedSyn, Text2CT) and segmentation-conditioned CT synthesis (MAISIs, 3D MedDiffusion, Med-DDPM). It departs from both by replacing the requirement for full-organ segmentation with a single anatomy or pathology mask whose semantic identity is specified via a short text pre-prompt, and by unifying image and mask tokens in a shared latent space through a fine-tuned video tokenizer (OSP) rather than treating them as separate conditioning channels. Architecturally, it builds on the DiT backbone and rectified-flow formulation, adding gated attention for long radiology reports and depthwise-separable convolutions for inter-patch spatial consistency—modifications that are individually known but combined here in a new configuration for volumetric medical imaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compound prompting mechanism (optional mask + optional report, with mask semantics defined in text) substantially lowers the annotation barrier for controllable CT synthesis, making the approach more practical for clinical and data-augmentation workflows where full-organ segmentation is prohibitively expensive. The data-augmentation experiment showing that 416 synthesized images combined with 50 real ones outperform SOTA segmentation models (MedPSeg) on GGO segmentation is a concrete demonstration of downstream utility. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (CT-Rate, chest CT only), reliance on externally generated masks rather than ground-truth annotations, and training on a single A100 with batch size 1, which raises questions about scalability to other body regions or modalities. Open questions include whether the flexible single-mask paradigm generalizes to multi-organ or multi-pathology scenarios without retraining, and whether the approach transfers to MRI or ultrasound.
