## Problem

Current VLMs cannot reliably perform spatial reasoning (distance, size, inter-object relationship inference) from a single RGB image, and existing benchmarks either depend on multi-view video or depth inputs that expose explicit geometric cues, or are confined to small indoor scenes too limited for open-world training. No large-scale dataset simultaneously guarantees single-view answerability, covers diverse real-world domains, and is large enough to support both model training and fine-grained evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261135/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image with no auxiliary depth, multi-view, or point-cloud input, and produces structured QA pairs in three formats (Yes/No, Multiple-Choice, Numerical) across eight task types organized into three cognitive levels. Numerical answers (distance, size) are evaluated with a 10% relative-error tolerance, and the dataset enforces 50/50 Yes/No polarity and randomized MC option positions to prevent prior exploitation. Each image contains on average 20 annotated objects across 98 semantic categories, with scene complexity constrained to 3–40 valid instances per frame.

## Method

MonoSR is built on Omni3D's 3D bounding-box annotations and employs a four-stage Single-View Observability Guarantee (instance visibility thresholds, 3D annotation quality checks, scene complexity bounds, and QA ambiguity filtering) to ensure every QA pair is answerable from one image alone, validated by human audit with <1.4% residual unanswerable rate. QA generation is two-stage: answers are first computed deterministically from 3D ground-truth coordinates (direct geometry for perception tasks, rigid transformations for perspective-aware tasks), then an LLM paraphrases questions under an expert persona to produce natural situational-reasoning prompts while preserving geometric fidelity. The paper further conducts a controlled auxiliary-information study, fine-tuning Qwen-2.5-VL-3B and InternVL-3.5-2B with injected scene labels, 2D visual prompts, and 3D bounding boxes (individually and in combination) to quantify the geometric gap that future monocular perception modules must close, revealing that targeted 3D structure yields the largest gains while exhaustive signal injection degrades performance.

## Results

Across all three spatial domains (indoor, outdoor, object-centric), SpatialVLM is the strongest open-source model, achieving 0.511 overall on indoor and 0.618 on object-centric (Dist=0.622, Size=0.569). Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads on high-level compositional reasoning, reaching 0.440 overall on object-centric, while ChatGPT-4 lags significantly (e.g., 0.008 on indoor numerical estimation at 10% threshold). All models degrade substantially on object-centric metric estimation, with LLaVA-OneVision-72B collapsing to 0.087 overall, confirming that precise monocular metric reasoning remains an open challenge. Fine-tuning Qwen-2.5-VL-3B on MonoSR alone lifts indoor overall from 0.283 to 0.494, and adding 3D bounding boxes as auxiliary input pushes it to 0.722.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialVLM [7] dominates among open-source models on geometry-sensitive tasks (indoor SR(MC4)=0.529, Dist=0.505; outdoor Dist=0.582; object-centric Overall=0.618), frequently matching or surpassing closed-source systems on metric estimation. Gemini-2.5-Pro and Gemini-2.5-Flash [42] outperform ChatGPT-4 [1] across most categories, particularly on high-level compositional tasks (e.g., High-OG(MC4)), with Gemini-2.5-Pro reaching 0.440 on object-centric vs. ChatGPT-4's near-zero numerical scores (0.008–0.021). LLaVA-OneVision-72B [30] and Qwen-2.5-VL-72B [4] remain competitive on qualitative relation tasks but struggle with numerical estimation, with LLaVA-OV-72B dropping to 0.087 on object-centric. InternVL-3-78B [59] achieves the strongest mid-level interaction result on outdoor (PR(Y/N)=0.527) but trails SpatialVLM on low-level geometric tasks. The auxiliary information study shows that fine-tuning on MonoSR alone (Qwen-2.5-VL-3B: 0.283→0.494 indoor) already yields substantial gains, while injecting 3D bounding boxes as an oracle upper bound pushes performance to 0.722–0.996, quantifying the geometric gap that practical monocular perception modules must close.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoSR builds directly on Omni3D for its image corpus and ground-truth 3D bounding boxes, repurposing a monocular 3D detection dataset as the geometric backbone for spatial reasoning QA generation. It departs from the dominant line of 3D spatial reasoning benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, VSI-Bench, SpatialBot) that rely on multi-view video sequences, depth maps, or point clouds, by enforcing a strict single-view answerability constraint via a four-stage observability filter. The auxiliary information study connects to the monocular 3D detection and metric depth estimation literature by treating 3D bounding boxes as an oracle upper bound, quantifying the geometric gap that practical monocular perception modules must close.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MonoSR is the first monocular spatial reasoning dataset large enough (1M+ QA pairs, 230K images) to support both training and open-world evaluation across indoor, outdoor, and object-centric domains, filling a gap where prior monocular-compatible benchmarks (SPHERE ~6K, OmniSpatial 8.4K) were purely diagnostic. The finding that even the strongest closed-source VLMs (Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieve only ~0.3–0.4 on object-centric metric estimation, while 3D bounding box oracles reach near-perfect accuracy, quantifies a large and actionable geometric gap for future monocular perception modules. Key open questions include whether integrating metric depth or monocular 3D detection directly into VLM architectures can close this gap, and whether RL-based post-training on MonoSR's training split can improve spatial reasoning without additional labeled data.
