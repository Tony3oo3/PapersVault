## Problem

Existing multimodal dataset distillation (MDD) methods suffer from two structural limitations: (1) they compress the multiple captions per image in real datasets into a single caption in synthetic datasets, creating a semantic gap between the multi-semantic image and the fragmented text; and (2) their contrastive losses indiscriminately treat all non-paired samples as negatives, discarding semantically relevant information contained in unpaired texts that partially describe the anchor image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260758/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real multimodal dataset (e.g., Flickr-30K with 5 captions per image, MS-COCO with 5 captions per image, or AudioCaps with audio clips and captions) and produces a compact synthetic set of 100–1000 image-text pairs. The Intra-pair Text Fusion step runs offline using LLaVA in inference-only mode to merge multiple captions into one fused caption per image, conditioned on the original image to prevent hallucination; this introduces no trainable parameters and leaves the text encoder (BERT) and shared embedding dimensionality unchanged. The distilled pairs are then used to train a CLIP-style dual-encoder (NFNet/RegNet/ResNet + BERT) via trajectory matching, evaluated on top-K cross-modal retrieval (IR@K, TR@K).

## Method

P2DE introduces two orthogonal modules on top of the standard dual-trajectory matching framework. First, Intra-pair Text Fusion (ITF) uses LLaVA in inference-only mode to fuse the multiple human-written captions of each image into a single semantically richer caption, with the original image as an explicit supervisory signal and strict prompt constraints to prevent hallucination; this raises the text modality's entropy H(T), thereby increasing the mutual information upper bound I(X;T) ≤ min(H(X), H(T)) and reducing the cross-modal semantic gap. Second, the Inter-pair Contribution Controller (ICC) converts cross-modal similarities into image-conditioned probabilities and computes the conditional information entropy H(T|X) to quantify how much uncertainty remains in the text given the image; it then dynamically adjusts the student model's trajectory step size (Eq. 5) — expanding steps when ΔH is large (model still absorbing non-paired information) and contracting when ΔH converges — so that partially relevant non-paired texts contribute non-negligible learning signals rather than being uniformly penalized. Three theorems formalize why this works: WBCE-induced gradient vanishing for non-paired samples in the diagonal-dominant regime, entropy concentration as the similarity gap grows, and a lower bound on non-paired gradients guaranteed by maintaining a minimum conditional entropy threshold.

## Results

On MS-COCO with 100 synthetic pairs, P2DE achieves 28.8% IR@10 and 32.2% TR@10, surpassing the prior SOTA RepBlend by +6.5 and +4.2 percentage points respectively. At 500 pairs, P2DE reaches 33.4% IR@10 and 37.5% TR@10, a +4.6% gain in TR@10 over RepBlend. On Flickr-30K, the improvements are even more pronounced: at 100 pairs, P2DE scores 49.5% IR@10 and 61.8% TR@10 versus RepBlend's 44.5% and 55.5% (+5.0 and +6.3 points). The full-dataset reference on MS-COCO is IR@10=53.2, TR@10=61.3, meaning P2DE at 500 pairs recovers ~63% of full-data IR@10 and ~61% of TR@10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2DE outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and budget tested. Against the closest competitor RepBlend (NeurIPS'25), P2DE gains +6.5% IR@10 and +4.2% TR@10 at 100 pairs on MS-COCO, and +4.6% TR@10 at 500 pairs; on Flickr-30K the gap widens to +5.0% IR@10 and +6.3% TR@10 at 100 pairs. CovMatch (NeurIPS'25) is the second-strongest baseline but trails P2DE by 4–11 points depending on budget and metric. LoRS and TESLA-VL (both ICML'24) are substantially weaker, with TESLA-VL in particular collapsing to near-random performance (e.g., 0.1±0.1 IR@1 at 200 pairs on MS-COCO). MTT-VL (TMLR'24), the pioneering MDD method, lags P2DE by 10–20+ points across all settings. The three coreset-selection baselines (Herd, K-Cent, Forget) are far below all distillation methods, confirming that trajectory-based synthesis is essential. Statistical paired t-tests confirm significance (p<0.05) in 14 out of 18 comparisons against RepBlend.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal dataset distillation (MDD) lineage initiated by MTT-VL's dual-trajectory matching framework and refined by LoRS (low-rank similarity mining) and RepBlend (representation blending). It departs from all prior MDD methods on two conceptual axes: (1) addressing the semantic gap between multi-caption real data and single-caption synthetic data via LLaVA-based intra-pair text fusion, and (2) replacing the uniform negative treatment of unpaired samples with an entropy-gated contribution controller. The information-theoretic analysis (mutual information bounds, conditional entropy) provides a formal justification that prior trajectory-matching MDD methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously unaddressed asymmetry in MDD—single captions cannot represent multi-semantic images—and shows that fusing captions with a frozen VLM plus entropy-based step control yields consistent 4–7% retrieval gains across Flickr-30K and MS-COCO at small budgets. The information-theoretic framing (MI upper bound compressed by low text entropy; gradient vanishing for non-paired samples under diagonal dominance) gives practitioners a principled lens for diagnosing cross-modal alignment failures. Open questions include whether the LLaVA fusion step generalizes to non-English or domain-specific captions, whether the ICC threshold mechanism scales to very large synthetic budgets, and whether the approach transfers to other modality pairs (e.g., audio-text, where AudioCaps results are only in the appendix).
