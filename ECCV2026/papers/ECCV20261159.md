## Problem

Existing multi-exposure HDR pipelines treat SDR fusion and HDR radiance reconstruction as disjoint tasks, and gain map-based inverse tone mapping methods assume the input SDR already preserves all scene structure—failing when highlights are clipped. Naively cascading a pretrained SDR generator with a pretrained gain map estimator propagates errors and yields substantially degraded HDR quality, as the two stages are never jointly optimized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261159/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three co-registered LDR images (under-, mid-, and over-exposed) at 512×512 resolution and produces two outputs: a display-ready SDR image and a per-pixel, per-channel gain map (H×W×3) that, combined with a predicted global Q_max scale, reconstructs the final HDR image via exponentiation of the SDR. The SDR output is directly viewable without any tone mapping operator, while the HDR output is suitable for HDR-capable displays.

## Method

DOME-HDR decomposes the problem into two jointly trained stages. Stage 1 uses a LoRA-adapted latent diffusion model (built on UltraFusion) with a dual cross-attention design: two independent attention branches inject structural and color cues from the under- and over-exposed images into the mid-exposure latent representation, avoiding the information loss of UltraFusion's normalized-summation fusion. Stage 2 employs HPGM, a U-Net that constructs a saturation-aware HDR prior from the three linearized exposures, then predicts a residual gain map over this prior along with a global Q_max scale, using Sobel-edge-guided upsampling to sharpen spatial boundaries. Training proceeds in two phases: Phase 1 jointly optimizes the diffusion backbone and HPGM against ground-truth SDR and HDR targets, while Phase 2 fine-tunes HPGM on MEF-generated SDRs to close the train–inference domain gap.

## Results

On the combined test set of Kalantari, Tel, and Challenge123, DOME-HDR achieves state-of-the-art HDR reconstruction with 44.63 PU-PSNR, 0.9878 PU-SSIM, 0.0156 LPIPS, and 0.0147 DISTS, outperforming the strongest baseline AFUNet (44.48 PU-PSNR, 0.0205 LPIPS, 0.0162 DISTS) across all full-reference metrics. On no-reference quality, DOME-HDR also leads with 0.4137 CLIPIQA, 3.56 NIQE, and 22.18 BRISQUE. In the multi-exposure fusion comparison (Table 2), the SDR output achieves 0.4129 MANIQA and 0.5233 CLIPIQA, surpassing UltraFusion (0.4064, 0.5147) while remaining competitive with MEF-LUT on BRISQUE (16.52 vs. 17.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOME-HDR outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest HDR reconstruction baseline AFUNet (ICCV 2025), DOME-HDR gains +0.15 dB PU-PSNR, reduces LPIPS from 0.0205 to 0.0156 (−24%), and improves BRISQUE from 27.13 to 22.18, indicating substantially better perceptual quality. Compared to SAFNet (ECCV 2024) and SCTNet (ICCV 2023), the gains are larger: +1.65 dB and +1.71 dB PU-PSNR respectively, with LPIPS reduced by roughly 55% over both. In the MEF comparison, DOME-HDR's SDR output slightly surpasses UltraFusion (CVPR 2025) on MANIQA (0.4129 vs. 0.4064) and CLIPIQA (0.5233 vs. 0.5147) while achieving a lower BRISQUE (16.52 vs. 17.01), confirming that the LoRA-adapted dual cross-attention preserves and marginally improves upon the original fusion quality. MEF-LUT (ICCV 2023) trails on all four NR-IQA metrics, with the largest gap on CLIPIQA (0.4070 vs. 0.5233). Notably, DOME-HDR also produces a display-ready SDR without requiring a tone mapping operator, whereas AFUNet, SCTNet, and SAFNet all depend on post-hoc tone mapping whose quality varies across operators (μ-law, PQ, Reinhard) as shown in Table 7.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOME-HDR sits at the intersection of three previously separate research threads: diffusion-based multi-exposure fusion (building directly on UltraFusion's guided-inpainting formulation), multi-exposure HDR radiance reconstruction (AFUNet, SAFNet, SCTNet), and gain map-based inverse tone mapping (GMNet, Gain-MLP). The key conceptual departure is reframing HDR reconstruction as gain map prediction over a *synthesized* SDR base rather than direct linear-radiance regression, and jointly optimizing the SDR generator and gain map estimator within a single pipeline. The dual cross-attention design extends UltraFusion's single-branch guidance mechanism to handle three exposures without the information loss of normalized feature summation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-output design (display-ready SDR + HDR gain map) offers a practical path toward display-adaptive HDR delivery without committing to a fixed tone mapping operator, which is a real deployment concern for consumer imaging pipelines. The dramatic failure of the naive cascaded baseline (PU-PSNR 22.96 vs. 44.63) underscores that joint optimization is not merely a convenience but a necessity when the SDR generator and gain map estimator must agree on a shared latent representation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to single-image HDR (where no complementary exposures exist to build the prior), to video sequences with temporal consistency constraints, and whether the LoRA-adapted diffusion backbone can be replaced by lighter-weight generators for real-time applications.
