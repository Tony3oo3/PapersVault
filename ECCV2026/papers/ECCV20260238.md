## Problem

Existing human motion modeling systems are task-specific: each scenario (text-to-motion, music-to-dance, speech-to-gesture, motion captioning, multi-person coordination) requires a separate architecture and training pipeline. The discrete autoregressive paradigm offers a natural unification—tasks reduce to different token arrangements over a shared vocabulary—but has been blocked by two obstacles: current motion tokenizers use root-relative, non-causal, frame-level encodings that discard world-space trajectories and break forward-only AR consistency, and multi-modal motion data with paired text and audio annotations remains scarce.

## Input / Output

The model accepts arbitrary combinations of text prompts, music/speech audio (tokenized via a VQ-VAE), and past motion sequences (tokenized via VerMoVQ) as conditioning, all mapped into a single shared discrete vocabulary. Outputs are 3D human motion in world-space SMPL parameters (root position, velocity, and 6D joint rotations at 30 fps), natural-language captions, or music token sequences. Multi-person motion is produced by per-agent token concatenation in a shared codebook, requiring no architectural changes for arbitrary group sizes, and the same model handles both generation (condition→motion) and understanding (motion→text) by simply reordering the token sequence.

## Method

The framework rests on three components. First, VerMoVQ is a causal spatio-temporal VQ-VAE that factorizes each frame into K low-dimensional body tokens (one packing root position/velocity, the rest holding 6D joint rotations) rather than a single high-dimensional frame vector; strictly causal temporal convolutions ensure forward-only consistency with AR decoding, spatial body-attention blocks capture inter-joint couplings, and FSQ quantization eliminates codebook management. Second, MotionHub is a ~380K-clip corpus standardized to SMPL with large-scale multi-modal annotations (human and VLM-assisted captioning) spanning nine tasks. Third, VersatileMotion is a single decoder-only AR Transformer (LLaMA 3.2-1B backbone) trained in two stages: generalist pretraining with data-proportional task sampling to build broad cross-modal priors, followed by generalist SFT with task-uniform sampling to lift under-represented audio-conditioned tasks. All tasks—text↔motion, music↔dance, speech→gesture, motion prediction, in-betweening, and multi-person interaction—are expressed as different arrangements of the same token types with a single next-token-prediction objective, enabling cross-modal compositions (e.g., text+audio→dance) without task-specific architectural changes.

## Results

On single-person text-to-motion, VersatileMotion achieves the best FID of 0.062 on HumanML3D and 0.098 on MotionHub, outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline MotionStreamer (0.065 / 0.411) and the best discrete AR baseline Go-To-Zero (0.075 / 0.101). On multi-person T2M, it reaches FID=0.51 and R-Precision=0.473, placing second only to InterMask (FID=0.43). For motion-to-text captioning, VersatileMotion attains R-Precision of 0.686 on HumanML3D and 0.778 on two-person MotionHub, surpassing TM2T (0.536 / 0.623) and MotionGPT (0.398 / 0.691) by large margins. The VerMoVQ tokenizer achieves 18.13 mm MPJPE on single-person reconstruction, a ~33% improvement over the best prior VQ tokenizer Go-To-Zero (27.19 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VersatileMotion outperforms all 22 baselines on the primary text-to-motion task in FID on both HumanML3D (0.062 vs. 0.065 for MotionStreamer, 0.075 for Go-To-Zero) and MotionHub (0.098 vs. 0.101 for Go-To-Zero, 0.363 for HY-Motion), confirming that a well-designed discrete AR model can rival the best diffusion generators. On multi-person T2M, it is second-best behind InterMask (FID 0.51 vs. 0.43; R-P 0.473 vs. 0.508) but substantially outperforms diffusion-based ComMDM (FID 1.01) and InterGen (FID 0.77). For motion-to-text, VersatileMotion leads every retrieval metric on all three settings (HumanML3D 1P, MotionHub 1P, MotionHub 2P), with the largest gap on MotionHub 1P where R-Precision jumps from 0.186 (TM2T) to 0.509. In motion prediction and in-betweening, VersatileMotion achieves the lowest FID across all three conditioning regimes (e.g., 0.166 vs. 0.276 for MotionGPT3 in the hardest single-frame in-betweening setting) and the best ADE/FDE. On music-to-dance, it attains the best FID (0.152 on AIST++, 0.185 on FineDance) and kinetic FID, surpassing dedicated dance generators. The VerMoVQ tokenizer itself achieves 18.13 mm MPJPE, beating Go-To-Zero (27.19 mm) by ~33% and MotionStreamer (36.42 mm) by ~50% on single-person reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete autoregressive motion paradigm established by TM2T, MotionGPT, and M³GPT, which demonstrated that VQ-VAE tokenization plus a GPT-style decoder can handle text↔motion. The key departure is identifying that prior discrete AR systems were bottlenecked not by the AR formulation itself but by two specific design choices: root-relative, non-causal, frame-level tokenizers that discard world-space trajectories, and the absence of large-scale multi-modal (audio-paired) motion data. By introducing VerMoVQ (a causal spatio-temporal VQ-VAE with per-joint body tokens and FSQ quantization) and the MotionHub corpus, the paper removes these barriers and shows that a single decoder-only Transformer over a shared text-audio-motion vocabulary can handle nine tasks without per-task architectural changes, in contrast to both diffusion-based systems (which require separate conditioning heads per task) and prior discrete AR models (which were confined to single-person text-motion pairs).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: MotionHub (~380K clips with multi-modal annotations) and MotionBench (nine-task evaluation suite) become shared resources for the motion generation community, and the compositional token structure means new tasks or modalities can be added by extending the vocabulary rather than redesigning the architecture. Open questions include whether the per-agent concatenation strategy scales beyond two-person interactions to larger groups, whether the SMPL parameterization limits expressiveness for non-rigid or clothed bodies, and whether the dual-pathway sampling strategy generalizes to even more imbalanced task distributions. The multi-person results, while competitive, remain second to InterMask on several metrics, suggesting that explicit interaction modeling may still be needed for complex multi-agent scenarios.
