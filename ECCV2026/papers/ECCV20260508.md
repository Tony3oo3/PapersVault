## Problem

Existing autoregressive segmentation methods rely on external segmentation experts (e.g., SAM), long coordinate sequences, or text-to-mask conversion, none of which achieve fine-grained, end-to-end pixel-level generation within a unified next-token prediction framework. The paper asks whether segmentation masks can be treated as visual structures and generated directly as discrete tokens by a standard autoregressive Transformer, eliminating task-specific pipeline components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260508/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (256×256 or 384×384) and a text prompt (object label or free-form description, wrapped in one of 10 predefined templates) as input. The image is encoded by a frozen SigLIP2 vision encoder with patch size 16, matching the VQGAN downsample rate so that input tokens align spatially with mask tokens. The output is a binary segmentation mask produced by decoding autoregressively generated VQGAN tokens, averaging across the three reconstructed RGB channels, and binarizing at threshold 0.

## Method

LlamaSeg reformulates segmentation as visual autoregressive generation: a VQGAN tokenizer (from LlamaGen, downsample rate 16) encodes ground-truth masks into discrete code indices, which are flattened into 1D token sequences serving as supervision targets. A LLaMA-style Transformer (770M or 1.5B parameters, 1D RoPE) autoregressively predicts these mask tokens conditioned on concatenated image and text features, separated by learnable <BOI> and <BOM> tokens, with cross-entropy loss computed only on mask positions. The framework supports two training routes: from-scratch LLaMA with SigLIP2 encoders and trainable adapters, or fine-tuning a pretrained MLLM (Janus Pro) via LoRA while freezing its generative components. Training uses a two-stage protocol—pre-training on the newly constructed SA-OVRS dataset (2M masks, 5,800+ open-vocabulary labels) followed by task-specific fine-tuning—and the paper introduces the average Hausdorff Distance (d_AHD) metric under multilevel IoU thresholds to evaluate contour fidelity beyond region overlap.

## Results

On closed-set semantic segmentation, LlamaSeg-L (1.5B) achieves 52.0 mIoU on ADE20K and 55.7 mIoU on COCO-Stuff, surpassing the larger Unified-IO2-XXL (6.6B) by 0.1 and 0.6 points respectively while using fewer parameters. With 384-pixel input resolution, LlamaSeg-L further improves to 55.9 mIoU on ADE20K and 58.1 on COCO-Stuff. On open-vocabulary benchmarks, LlamaSeg-L attains 35.5 mIoU on PC-459 and 62.5 on PC-59, substantially outperforming LaSagnA (9.8 and 39.6). On referring segmentation, LlamaSeg-L reaches 56.5 cIoU on refCOCO val, exceeding the best generative baseline Unified-IO2-XL (50.7) by 5.8 points, though it remains below discriminative models like ReLA (73.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Unified-IO (Lu et al., 2022), LlamaSeg-B (0.77B) matches its parameter count but achieves 50.5 vs. 39.9 mIoU on ADE20K and 54.5 vs. 50.7 on COCO-Stuff, a 10.6 and 3.8 point gain respectively. Against Unified-IO2 (Lu et al., 2024), LlamaSeg-L (1.5B) outperforms the much larger Unified-IO2-XXL (6.6B) on ADE20K (52.0 vs. 51.9) and COCO-Stuff (55.7 vs. 55.1), while also reporting open-vocabulary results that Unified-IO2-XXL does not provide. Against LaSagnA (Wei et al., 2024), a 7B MLLM, LlamaSeg-L achieves substantially higher open-vocabulary scores (35.5 vs. 9.8 on PC-459, 62.5 vs. 39.6 on PC-59) while being 4.7× smaller. On referring segmentation, LlamaSeg-L beats Unified-IO2-XL by 5.8 cIoU on refCOCO val (56.5 vs. 50.7) and 2.2 on refCOCO+ testA (46.2 vs. 47.4 is actually a loss of 1.2), but trails the discriminative baselines LAVT (Yang et al., 2022) and ReLA (Liu et al., 2023) by 16–22 cIoU points, acknowledging a gap versus task-specific segmentation architectures. On the proposed mAHD boundary metric, LlamaSeg-L achieves 24.28 on ADE20K (IoU-0.7) versus 75.76 for Unified-IO2-XXL, a 3× improvement in contour fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

LlamaSeg departs from the three dominant autoregressive segmentation paradigms—embedding-based (LISA, GSVA, which delegate pixel generation to SAM), coordinate-driven (VisionLLM, which encodes masks as polygon vertex sequences), and text-conditioned (Text4Seg, GIT, which emit category tokens requiring post-hoc conversion)—by treating segmentation masks as visual images and encoding them via VQGAN into discrete tokens for direct next-token prediction. The architecture builds on LLaMA's transformer backbone with RoPE positional encoding and on LlamaGen's VQGAN tokenizer, while the MLLM variant leverages Janus Pro's frozen generative pathway. The SA-OVRS dataset extends SA-1B's masks with open-vocabulary labels and natural-language descriptions generated via Qwen2-VL and GroundingDINO, filling a data gap that prior autoregressive segmentation methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating external segmentation experts and intermediate representations, LlamaSeg demonstrates that dense pixel-level segmentation can be natively unified within a standard LLM next-token prediction loop, offering a clean path toward truly end-to-end multimodal models. The 2M-sample SA-OVRS dataset and the mAHD contour-fidelity metric are reusable resources that address practical gaps in training data and evaluation for generative segmentation. However, a significant performance gap remains versus specialized discriminative models (e.g., LAVT scores 72.7 vs. LlamaSeg-L's 56.5 on refCOCO val), and the sequential autoregressive decoding is ~100× slower than parallel dense decoders, limiting real-time applicability. Open questions include whether scaling to larger models or higher resolutions can close the gap with discriminative SOTA, and whether the visual-token formulation generalizes to video or 3D segmentation.
