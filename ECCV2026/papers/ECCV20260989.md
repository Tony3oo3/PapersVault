## Problem

Deep learning-based analysis of electron micrographs (EMs) in materials science is hampered by the absence of large-scale, expert-annotated public datasets with instance-level labels derived from raw experimental images. Existing resources are either too small, provide only image-level classification labels, or are sourced from post-processed literature figures lacking raw structural complexity. Furthermore, conventional detection-based and query-based segmentation architectures degrade under the extreme instance densities (thousands of overlapping particles per image) and textural complexities inherent in EMs.

## Input / Output

The segmentation pipeline consumes raw SEM/TEM micrographs (minimum 200px resolution) and produces per-instance pixel-level masks; the dataset spans 5,091 images with 2,985,660 annotations, where instance counts per image range from a few to over 2,400 and instance areas span four orders of magnitude (10 px to 10⁵ px). The generative model takes structured text prompts built from nine quasi-orthogonal attributes (material type, microscopy type, morphology, density, distribution, layering, surface texture, diameter distribution, color configuration) in a fixed JSON template and outputs synthetic EM images. The text-to-image model was trained on images with embedded metadata regions removed via a YOLO11 detector to prevent textual hallucinations.

## Method

The paper's core contributions are threefold. First, EM3M is constructed through a multi-stage human-in-the-loop pipeline: domain experts annotate a seed set to train an initial model, which generates pseudo-labels refined by trained annotators, with quality validated via peer review and expert final validation (measured by Krippendorff's Alpha and Panoptic Quality on a 300-image stratified subset). Second, a text-to-image diffusion model (SDXL fine-tuned via LoRA with learnable category tokens) is trained on the curated image-text pairs to serve as a controllable augmentation engine; it achieves FID 34.03 vs. 213.16 for vanilla SDXL and, when used to pretrain the segmentation model, consistently outperforms SA1B pretraining across all real-data scales. Third, UniAIMS adapts the Cellpose flow-based formulation for EMs by removing the built-in size model, adopting scale-robust augmentation, enabling distributed training at large resolutions, and upgrading to a SAM-pretrained ViT-Base backbone, achieving 0.818 mAP@0.5 on the sparse subset and 0.800 on the full dataset—substantially outperforming detection-based, one-stage, and query-based alternatives that cannot handle the extreme instance densities.

## Results

On the EM3M benchmark, UniAIMS achieves mAP@0.5 of 0.818 and PQ@0.5 of 0.769 on the sparse subset (<100 instances), and maintains mAP@0.5 of 0.800 and PQ@0.5 of 0.704 on the full dataset with extreme instance densities. The strongest baseline, Cellpose, reaches mAP@0.5 of 0.711 and PQ@0.5 of 0.691 on the full dataset, while detection-based and query-based methods (e.g., Mask R-CNN, MaskDINO) are limited to the sparse subset where they peak at mAP@0.5 of 0.624 and 0.395 respectively. For data augmentation, combining 30K generated images with 4K real images yields the best result of 0.809 mAP@0.5 and 0.707 PQ@0.5, and pretraining on 30K generated images outperforms SA1B pretraining across all real-data scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniAIMS outperforms all 14 baselines on both the sparse and full EM3M splits. Among bottom-up methods (the strongest family), UniAIMS improves mAP@0.5 by 10.7 points over Cellpose (0.818 vs. 0.711 on full) and by 14.8 points over StarDist (0.818 vs. 0.668 on full), while also achieving the highest PQ@0.5 (0.704 vs. 0.691 for Cellpose). Detection-based methods (Mask R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN, HTC) and one-stage methods (YOLO11-seg, YOLACT, SOLOv2) are competitive on the sparse subset (mAP@0.5 up to 0.624) but cannot be evaluated on the full dataset due to memory and matching overhead at extreme instance densities. Query-based transformers (Mask2Former, MaskDINO, FastInst) underperform most severely, with MaskDINO reaching only 0.395 mAP@0.5 on the sparse split and producing fragmented masks in cluttered regions. On external datasets, UniAIMS achieves strong zero-shot transfer (0.92 mAP on EMPS, 0.75 on Okunev et al.) and near-saturation after fine-tuning (0.92 and 0.84 respectively), confirming generalizability beyond EM3M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Cellpose flow-based segmentation paradigm to electron micrographs, adapting it (removing the size model, adding scale-robust augmentation, modernizing the backbone) into the UniAIMS baseline. For the generative component, it applies LoRA fine-tuning of SDXL with learnable category tokens, building on the Textual Inversion / DreamBooth line of work but targeting structured, attribute-disentangled prompts rather than free-form captions. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior EM datasets (EMPS, Aversa, MMSci) by providing raw experimental micrographs with ~3M instance-level masks and structured text, rather than literature-extracted figures or image-level labels only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EM3M is the first large-scale raw-EM dataset combining dense instance annotations with structured textual descriptions, filling a critical gap that has hindered development of SAM-style foundation models for materials science. The benchmark reveals that SOTA query-based methods (Mask2Former, MaskDINO) degrade severely under extreme instance density, while bottom-up flow/embedding methods remain robust—a practical finding that redirects architectural choices for dense microscopy. Open problems remain: the best model (0.704 PQ@50) still trails human inter-annotator agreement (0.786), PQ@90 collapses to 0.340, and fibrous/low-contrast microstructures with ambiguous boundaries remain unsolved.
