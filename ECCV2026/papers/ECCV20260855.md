## Problem

Existing TextVQA benchmarks predominantly contain medium-to-low-resolution content and do not systematically isolate how input video quality (resolution, blur) affects scene-text reading and downstream multimodal reasoning. Whether MLLMs can robustly read and reason over in-the-wild text under controlled quality variation, and whether restoration preserves the textual evidence needed for reasoning, remain unanswered fundamental questions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260855/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For restoration tasks, input is 4× bicubic-downsampled or motion-blurred video frames (from 1920×1080 HQ source), and output is restored high-resolution frames scored with both perceptual metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID) and text-aware metrics (PaddleOCR-based detection precision/recall/F1 and recognition accuracy/NED). For VideoQA, input is a 120-frame bilingual (Zh/En) egocentric clip in one of six quality conditions (HQ, DQ-Low_res, DQ-Blur, RQ-DOVE, RQ-MIMO, RQ-S3DIFF) paired with a spatially or temporally grounded question (multiple-choice, true/false, or fill-in-blank), and output is a free-form answer scored by accuracy, uncertainty-aware accuracy, over-confidence ratio, and abstention rate.

## Method

CTVid is built around a quality-controlled video triplet: each 4-second, 120-frame egocentric clip (captured with a Sony A7R V, 28–70 mm lens) is evaluated as High-Quality, Degraded-Quality (4× bicubic downscale or RIFE-based motion blur), and Restored-Quality (output of a restoration model), keeping content, annotations, and QA pairs fixed across conditions to enable causal analysis. Annotation uses PaddleOCR for coarse text detection candidates refined by 16 human annotators via Labelme over two rounds, yielding 1.6M verified text boxes and transcripts. QA generation is a two-round pipeline: GPT-4o proposes candidate questions from extracted metadata (text content, scene, locations), a rule-based spatial-partitioning or trajectory-analysis module derives answers and assigns bounding-box grounding, and expert annotators then verify, correct, and screen for ambiguity. For the SFT experiment, Qwen2.5-VL-7B is fine-tuned for 10 epochs on 8×A100 GPUs with the vision encoder and multimodal projector frozen, using task-specific system prompts. The evaluation protocol jointly measures low-level restoration fidelity (perceptual + OCR-based text metrics) and high-level reasoning (Acc, UAcc, OC, Abs), exposing the non-monotonic relationship between visual enhancement and text-grounded understanding.

## Results

On the spatial VideoQA benchmark (Table 4), proprietary MLLMs lead: Gemini-2.5-pro achieves 71.67% accuracy on HQ input, and Claude-Sonnet-4.6 reaches 70.00% on HQ and 60.00% on DQ-Blur. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-SFT (instruction-tuned on CTVid) attains the best accuracy across all six quality conditions (58.43% HQ, 53.21% RQ-DOVE), improving over the base model by 6.3–10.1 points. A controlled analysis shows blur is more damaging than low resolution: averaged over 16 MLLMs, blur reduces spatial QA accuracy by 6.05 points from HQ versus 3.14 points for low resolution. On the temporal VideoQA benchmark (Table 5), Claude-Sonnet-4.6 consistently ranks first across all five conditions (60.02% HQ, 60.37% RQ-DOVE), outperforming the best open-source model (Kimi-VL-16B, 53.95% HQ) by ~6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary MLLMs, Gemini-2.5-pro achieves the highest HQ spatial accuracy (71.67%) and also leads on DQ-Low_res (65.00%) and RQ-S3Diff (70.00%), while Claude-Sonnet-4.6 dominates DQ-Blur (60.00%) and RQ-MIMO (70.00%), and Gemini-2.5-flash is best on RQ-DOVE (66.67%)—demonstrating that no single model is uniformly best across all quality conditions. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-8B gives the strongest zero-shot HQ accuracy (50.56%), but Qwen2.5-VL-7B-SFT (fine-tuned on CTVid) surpasses all open-source baselines on every condition, reaching 58.43% on HQ and 53.21% on RQ-DOVE, a 6.3–10.1 point gain over the untuned base. On the temporal benchmark, Claude-Sonnet-4.6 leads all five conditions (60.02–60.37%), outperforming the best open-source model Kimi-VL-16B (53.95% HQ) by ~6 points, while Gemini-2.5-pro drops to 45.83% on HQ—below several open-source baselines. For restoration, DOVE achieves the best video SR text-detection F1 (74.53%) and S3Diff leads image SR text metrics (F1_det 77.17%), while Stripformer(Realblur_J) tops deblurring with F1_det 78.43% and Acc_rec 58.93%. A key finding is that restoration does not monotonically improve downstream QA: some RQ conditions yield lower accuracy than DQ for certain models, and an OCR+LLM diagnostic pipeline remains far below direct MLLM inference, confirming that spatial grounding and multimodal reasoning are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTVid carves new ground by unifying two previously disjoint research threads: video restoration (super-resolution, deblurring) and text-centric video QA. Prior restoration benchmarks (VideoLQ, Vimeo-90k, GOPRO, REDS) contain no text-bearing content or captions, while prior TextVQA datasets (EgoTextVQA, MTVQA, NewsVideoQA, ViTXT-GQA) operate at a single fixed quality and cannot isolate the effect of degradation or restoration on textual evidence. The quality-controlled HQ/DQ/RQ triplet design is the paper's core structural departure, repurposing standard degradation operators (bicubic ×4 downsampling, RIFE-based motion blur) to create content-matched variants that keep annotations and QA pairs fixed across regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that visual enhancement does not monotonically improve downstream text reasoning, and that blur is roughly twice as damaging as low resolution—challenges the implicit assumption in both restoration and MLLM communities that 'sharper is better' for text-grounded tasks. This opens a concrete research direction for text-faithful restoration objectives and quality-robust multimodal reasoning. Key limitations include the reliance on only two controlled degradations (bicubic and synthetic motion blur), which do not cover exposure shifts, rolling shutter, sensor noise, or complex compression; extending the paired protocol to richer real-world artifacts is explicitly flagged as future work. The benchmark's scale (4.6K videos, 550K+ frames, 1.6M text annotations, 220K+ bilingual QA pairs) and the 18-method restoration / 16-MLLM evaluation make it a likely reference point for both communities.
