## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting compression methods typically require access to training images, camera poses, or the rendering pipeline for retraining, iterative fine-tuning, or render-based importance scoring. In realistic post-training and archival scenarios, only the optimized Gaussian parameters are available, making these approaches inapplicable. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a strictly model-only, render-agnostic compression pipeline that requires no training data, camera metadata, or rendering supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully trained 3DGS scene as a set of N Gaussians, each with 3D position, opacity, axis-wise scales, quaternion rotation, and spherical harmonic coefficients (degrees 0–3), with no access to training images, camera poses, or SfM outputs. It produces a compressed 3DGS model with a reduced Gaussian count (controlled by pruning ratio p) and quantized attributes (controlled by per-Gaussian bit budget B for AC SH coefficients). The compressed model is stored as JPEG-XL-encoded attribute streams plus ZIP-compressed bit-packed index tables, with a shared spatial ordering enabling unambiguous reconstruction without per-Gaussian metadata.

## Method

RaPTGS is a four-stage, training-free post-processing pipeline. First, a render-agnostic importance score combines three view-independent proxies—Opacity Salience, Shape Anisotropy Strength (opacity-weighted log scale ratio), and Scale-Aware Appearance Energy (opacity-weighted, clamped spatial extent times higher-order SH norm)—via percentile-normalized max pooling to select the top-K Gaussians for retention. Second, Voxel Mass Compensation partitions the scene into a voxel grid sized to match the retained count, computes per-voxel mass loss from pruning, and uniformly inflates retained Gaussian scales (clamped to pre-pruning voxel maxima) to restore local coverage without any rendering feedback. Third, AC SH coefficients are degree-wise vector-quantized using K-means codebooks with a single global bit allocation (b₁+b₂+b₃=B) optimized by minimizing dimension-weighted MSE. Fourth, Gaussians are spatially reordered via Hilbert sort followed by a parallel windowed 2-opt heuristic to minimize ℓ₁ path length, then uniformly quantized and entropy-coded with JPEG-XL to exploit local value similarity.

## Results

RaPTGS achieves the smallest compressed size across all three benchmarks (25.95 MB on Mip-NeRF 360, 13.64 MB on Tanks & Temples, 24.51 MB on Deep Blending), corresponding to an average ~30× compression over uncompressed 3DGS (795.26 / 421.90 / 703.77 MB). On Mip-NeRF 360 it attains PSNR 26.41 / SSIM 0.782 / LPIPS 0.252, on Tanks & Temples PSNR 22.97 / SSIM 0.823 / LPIPS 0.203, and on Deep Blending PSNR 29.36 / SSIM 0.899 / LPIPS 0.248. Encoding time is the fastest among all compared methods at 5.8–7.7 s per scene, versus 30–44 s for FlexGaussian and 159–227 s for MesonGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the uncompressed 3DGS baseline [13], RaPTGS reduces size by ~30× (e.g., 795.26→25.95 MB on Mip-NeRF 360) at a modest PSNR cost of 0.68 dB. Versus FlexGaussian [27], RaPTGS achieves higher PSNR on all three benchmarks (26.41 vs 26.31, 22.97 vs 22.52, 29.36 vs 28.65) while also being smaller (25.95 vs 42.40, 13.64 vs 16.31, 24.51 vs 25.60 MB). Against MesonGS [32] (fine-tuning-free), RaPTGS is consistently smaller (25.95 vs 28.29, 13.64 vs 16.51, 24.51 vs 27.62 MB) and matches or slightly exceeds its PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 (26.41 vs 26.30) and Deep Blending (29.36 vs 29.22), though it trails by 0.05 dB on Tanks & Temples (22.97 vs 23.02). The fine-tuning-based methods MesonGS-FT [32] and PUP 3D-GS [8] achieve the highest PSNR (e.g., 26.98 and 26.66 on Mip-NeRF 360) but at 1.1–3.1× larger sizes than RaPTGS and require access to training views. Compared to the feed-forward learned codec FCGS [3], RaPTGS produces roughly 2× smaller models (e.g., 8.61 vs 17.78 MB on bonsai-7k), uses ~4.5× less GPU memory (2.83 vs 13.43 GB), and compresses in comparable or less time, while FCGS achieves marginally higher PSNR (29.69 vs 29.12 on bonsai-7k) owing to its learned neural compressor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established 3DGS compression directions—redundancy reduction (pruning) and parameter compactness (quantization/entropy coding)—but departs sharply from both in its operating assumptions. Prior pruning methods (LightGaussian, FlexGaussian, MesonGS, PUP 3D-GS, POTR) all derive importance scores from rendering passes or camera statistics, while learned-codec approaches (FCGS, CAT-3DGS) require a pretrained network. RaPTGS instead combines standard off-the-shelf tools—percentile-ranked multi-criteria scoring, K-means vector quantization, Hilbert-sort + 2-opt reordering, and JPEG-XL entropy coding—into a pipeline that needs only the optimized Gaussian parameters, introducing Voxel Mass Compensation as a render-free refinement step that has no direct analogue in the prior literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-only constraint reflects a real deployment gap: in asset distribution and archival, training images, camera poses, and SfM outputs are frequently unavailable, yet most published compressors require them. RaPTGS closes this gap with a 30× compression ratio, ~7 s encoding time, and ~3 GB peak memory—making it practical on consumer hardware where learned-codec baselines like FCGS simply run out of memory. The acknowledged limitation is the absence of view-dependent signals (occlusion, per-view contribution), which creates a quality gap versus render-dependent methods at aggressive pruning ratios; the authors suggest self-rendered synthetic views as a path to partially recover these cues while preserving the model-only setting.
