## Problem

Existing VSR methods are limited to single-speaker scenarios and rely on standard RGB cameras (25–30 FPS) that lose transient articulatory micro-dynamics—such as rapid plosive closures—due to motion blur and temporal undersampling. Moreover, dominant text-generation backbones (autoregressive or diffusion-based) incur prohibitive inference latency, making real-time multi-speaker lip reading infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260081/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system ingests RGB video clips and internally converts them to high-temporal-resolution event streams (125 FPS) via hierarchical frame interpolation (2×→2×→5× RIFE) and an adaptive differentiable thresholding network; lip ROIs are stabilized via affine alignment and normalized to 128×64. Multi-speaker scenes are pre-segmented using ByteTrack tracking and TalkNet active-speaker detection into single-speaker clips before processing. The output is a variable-length text transcription, terminated by a learned [PAD] token, with inference completing in just 2 ODE integration steps (240 ms latency).

## Method

LipsFlow encodes event streams with a hybrid 3D-stem + 2D-ResNet backbone followed by a 12-layer Conformer to capture both local micro-kinematics and long-range temporal dependencies. The decoder is a 16-layer CFM-Former that learns a straight-line probability-flow ODE in a pre-trained BERT semantic latent space via Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching, conditioned on aligned visual features through Cross-Attention and on speaker identity through AdaLN, requiring only 2 Euler steps at inference. A Dual-Level Semantic Supervision strategy combines token-level BERT weight tying (temperature-scaled cosine similarity) with a sentence-level Sentence-BERT consistency loss to resolve homophene ambiguities, while an InfoNCE contrastive loss on TDNN speaker embeddings enforces identity disentanglement. Training follows a two-stage curriculum (frozen visual front-end → full end-to-end fine-tuning) with auxiliary CTC alignment loss, and the multi-stage data pipeline (ByteTrack, RetinaFace, Dlib landmarks, TalkNet-ASD) ensures clean single-speaker event inputs with explicit silence/oral-motion/active annotations.

## Results

On the DVS-Lip benchmark, LipsFlow achieves a WER of 22.3% and VER of 19.8%, outperforming all video-based and event-based baselines while maintaining a latency of only 240 ms (RTF 0.18) with 45.8M parameters. On the AVA robustness benchmark, LipsFlow exhibits minimal degradation under adverse conditions: only +0.6% WER in Low Light, +1.8% in Rapid Motion, and +4.2% in Severe Occlusion, compared to 11–24% degradation for RGB-based methods. The 2-step OT-CFM inference delivers a 26× speedup over the diffusion-based SynthVSR (RTF 0.18 vs. 4.80) while achieving 2.8% lower WER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LipsFlow outperforms all nine baselines on WER across both DVS-Lip and AVA. Against the strongest video-based competitor Llama-AVSR (23.9% WER, >1000M params), LipsFlow achieves 1.6% lower WER with 22× fewer parameters and 10× lower latency (240 ms vs. 2500 ms). Versus the leading event-based method MSTP (30.5% WER), LipsFlow gains 8.2% WER improvement, validating the CFM-Former architecture over ResNet-18 for temporal dependency modeling. Compared to the diffusion-based SynthVSR (25.1% WER), LipsFlow improves WER by 2.8% while reducing RTF from 4.80 to 0.18 (26× speedup) via the 2-step OT-CFM solver. On AVA robustness, LipsFlow's degradation under Severe Occlusion is only +4.2% WER (26.5%), substantially better than Llama-AVSR (+11.2%, 35.1%) and MSTP (+12.3%, 42.8%), demonstrating that neuromorphic event streams provide richer articulatory information than RGB frames under adverse conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

LipsFlow sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) event-based VSR, where prior work (MSTP, SNN-Lip) aggregated asynchronous events into dense frames for standard CNN backbones; (2) Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching, developed for speech synthesis (VoiceFlow, Matcha-TTS) and image generation (InstaFlow) but never applied to visual speech recognition; and (3) multi-speaker audio-visual processing, where ByteTrack and TalkNet handle tracking and active speaker detection but have not been integrated into a generative VSR pipeline. The paper's core departure is reformulating VSR as a continuous probability-flow generation problem in BERT semantic space rather than a discriminative classification task, and doing so with event-stream inputs in multi-speaker settings for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2-step ODE inference (240 ms latency, 45.8 M parameters) offering near-diffusion accuracy while beating a >1 B-parameter LLM-based system (Llama-AVSR) suggests that generative flow matching is a viable, efficient backbone for VSR that could replace both AR decoders and multi-step diffusion in real-time deployments. The near-zero degradation under low light (0.6 %) versus 20–23 % for RGB methods highlights a genuine sensing-paradigm advantage, though a critical caveat is that most event streams are simulated from RGB via RIFE interpolation rather than captured by physical event cameras, leaving open whether the full dynamic-range and microsecond-timing benefits of real neuromorphic sensors would further improve results. Open questions include whether the simulated-event pipeline generalizes to unconstrained multi-speaker conversations beyond the AVA benchmark, and whether the OT-CFM decoder's 2-step sufficiency holds as vocabulary size or speaker count grows.
