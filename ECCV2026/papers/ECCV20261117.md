## Problem

Generating high-fidelity, editable SVG method illustrations (MIs) for AI scientific papers remains largely unsolved. Image-generation-based methods produce raster outputs that lack the structural fidelity and editability MIs demand, while existing code-generation approaches are limited to simple icons or basic charts and cannot handle the complex multi-module compositions typical of AI research figures. No prior system can directly produce publication-quality SVG MIs that faithfully render intricate network architectures and their inter-concept relationships.

## Input / Output

The method takes the introduction and method sections of a paper as text input, which a Parser Agent decomposes into a concept graph encoding core concepts and their inter-concept relationships. The output is a complete method illustration stored as XML-based DrawIO code that can be exported to SVG at arbitrary resolution and directly edited by authors. Unlike raster-based pipelines, the output is natively vector: each visual component is generated by an executable Python function that emits DrawIO XML, preserving precise geometric relationships and text elements without post-hoc vectorization artifacts.

## Method

FigAgent is a five-agent framework (Constructor, Parser, Drawer, Evaluator, Refiner) that mirrors human drawing practices through three key mechanisms. First, it builds a drawing middleware repository of parameterized Python functions (e.g., Feature_Pyramid(x,y,w,h,num_levels,shape_mode)) extracted from published MIs and organized in a theme→concept→component hierarchy, so complex MI generation reduces to selecting and orchestrating reusable components. Second, it evolves this repository via LLM-driven genetic programming (selection, mutation, crossover) guided by a joint objective combining generation-level MI quality and per-middleware efficacy scores, keeping the repository aligned with emerging research concepts. Third, it introduces an Explore-and-Select drawing strategy that models the sequential concept-rendering process as a Monte Carlo Tree Search: at each step the Drawer Agent expands multiple candidate drawing choices, simulates their long-term impact via UCT-guided rollouts, backpropagates quality scores, and selects the most promising path, thereby avoiding premature commitment to suboptimal layout decisions.

## Results

On FigAgentBench, FigAgent achieves NA F1 of 51.4, PA F1 of 32.6, and VLM score of 61.4, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On PaperBananaBench, it reaches NA F1 of 51.6, PA F1 of 36.7, and VLM of 68.9. The closest competitor, PaperBanana (image-based), trails by 5.1 points on NA F1 and 5.7 on PA F1 on FigAgentBench, while the best SVG-based baseline (GPT-5) lags by 25.8 points on NA F1 and 18.8 on PA F1, highlighting the gap between raster and vector generation approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FigAgent outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. Among image-based methods, PaperBanana is the strongest (NA F1 46.3, PA F1 26.9 on FigAgentBench), but FigAgent still leads by 5.1 and 5.7 points respectively, with a 1.1-point VLM advantage; the gap widens on PA F1 for PaperBananaBench (36.7 vs. 28.7). The EditBanana post-processing variants (e.g., PaperBanana+EditBanana) partially close the fidelity gap but degrade PA F1 substantially (21.2 vs. 26.9 for raw PaperBanana on FigAgentBench), confirming that raster-to-SVG conversion introduces structural artifacts. Among SVG-based baselines, GPT-5 (direct DrawIO code generation) is the strongest at NA F1 25.6 but trails FigAgent by 25.8 points, while TikZero and StarVector—designed for icons and simple charts—collapse to near-zero PA F1 (1.3 and 2.7), demonstrating their inability to handle complex multi-module MIs. Paper2SysArch, the most recent agent-based SVG method, achieves moderate layout scores (NA F1 34.8) but still lags FigAgent by 16.6 points on NA F1 and 12.3 on PA F1, with a 26.2-point VLM gap indicating poor visual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both image-generation-based MI methods (FigGen, PaperBanana, AutoFigure) that produce raster outputs with inherent fidelity limitations, and code-generation-based methods (TikZero, StarVector, Paper2SysArch) that are limited to simple icons or basic charts. Its conceptual lineage draws from three distinct threads: (1) LLM-powered multi-agent systems for structured generation, (2) genetic programming and evolutionary biology for non-differentiable function optimization (applied here to Python drawing functions), and (3) MCTS/UCT for sequential decision-making under path-dependency. The key departure is the 'drawing middleware' abstraction—treating reusable visual components as parameterized, evolvable Python functions indexed by theme-concept pairs—which has no direct analogue in prior figure-generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to directly generate high-fidelity, editable SVG MIs for complex AI papers without relying on raster-to-vector post-processing, addressing a practical pain point in the research community. The middleware evolution mechanism offers a principled way to keep a generation system aligned with rapidly emerging concepts, though its long-term stability and scalability to non-AI domains remain untested. Open questions include: how well the system handles truly novel concepts absent from the experience dataset, the computational cost of the MCTS loop (a₁ × a₂ expansions per concept), and whether the GPT-5 dependency limits reproducibility. The evaluation, while covering two benchmarks, relies on a single VLM judge and graph-alignment metrics that may not fully capture perceptual quality.
