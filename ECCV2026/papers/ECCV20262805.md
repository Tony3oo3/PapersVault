## Problem

Existing safety-critical scenario generation methods rely on soft cost-based guidance that provides only probabilistic preferences and cannot guarantee satisfaction of geometric and severity constraints for specific collision types. Additionally, the problem of dynamically selecting which adversarial agent and collision mode is most physically viable in dense traffic remains underexplored, and prior approaches struggle to balance precise collision controllability with realistic multi-agent driving behavior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a rasterized HD map, 10 historical frames of multi-agent trajectories (positions, headings, velocities), and the ego vehicle's planned trajectory from an AV planner, all encoded via a ResNet backbone. It outputs a 32-step adversarial action sequence (acceleration and yaw rate per step) that, when propagated through a unicycle dynamics model, yields a trajectory satisfying hard collision constraints at a target time-to-collision. The collision type (rear-end, side, cut-in, head-on) is either auto-selected by the Heuristic Collision Selector or user-specified, and the entire pipeline operates in a 2 Hz receding-horizon closed-loop simulation.

## Method

CCFM reformulates safety-critical scenario generation as a collision-constrained sampling problem solved via conditional flow matching with hard manifold projection. A Heuristic Collision Selector scores all candidate agent–collision-type pairs using weighted reachability, geometric alignment, and lane-graph legality to identify the most threatening yet physically viable interaction. For the selected collision type, three residual constraints (contact point, relative heading, impact severity) define a feasible set in action-sequence space. During ODE integration of the flow matching velocity field, each intermediate action sequence is projected onto this feasible set via a damped Gauss-Newton step with Jacobians computed by automatic differentiation through the forward-dynamics rollout, then blended with the initial Gaussian noise along the optimal-transport interpolant to avoid early-step discontinuities. The flow matching model itself is a U-Net with 1D convolutional blocks trained in a standard supervised manner on nuScenes to learn the conditional action prior.

## Results

On nuScenes (80-frame horizon), CCFM achieves a 46.4% collision rate with 2.8 m/s mean impact severity and 84.3% collision-type match, substantially outperforming CCDiff (4.0% CR), STRIVE (20.8% CR), and SAFE-SIM (25.8% CR). On nuPlan, CCFM reaches 83.1% CR versus SAFE-SIM's 42.1%. Extending to a 200-frame horizon further raises collision rates to 59.9% (nuScenes) and 95.2% (nuPlan) while maintaining ~85% type match. CCFM's realism (Wasserstein distance 0.78, off-road rate 5.4%) is competitive with baselines, though slightly worse than SAFE-SIM (RM 0.63, OR 5.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCFM dominates all three baselines on collision rate and severity across both datasets. Against CCDiff, CCFM improves collision rate from 4.0% to 46.4% on nuScenes (a >10× gain) and impact severity from 0.3 to 2.8 m/s, while also providing explicit collision-type controllability (84.3% type match) that CCDiff does not offer. Against STRIVE, CCFM more than doubles the collision rate (46.4% vs. 20.8%) and increases severity (2.8 vs. 1.9 m/s), at the cost of a slightly higher off-road rate (5.4% vs. 5.8%—actually comparable) and marginally worse realism (RM 0.78 vs. 0.83). Against SAFE-SIM, CCFM nearly doubles the nuScenes collision rate (46.4% vs. 25.8%) and more than doubles it on nuPlan (83.1% vs. 42.1%), with substantially higher severity (2.8 vs. 0.8 m/s on nuScenes). The main trade-off is realism: SAFE-SIM achieves a lower Wasserstein distance (0.63 vs. 0.78 on nuScenes), but CCFM's deviations are described as kinematically regulated aggressive maneuvers rather than unrealistic artifacts. CCFM is also the fastest method on nuScenes (123.0 s vs. 128.9 s for SAFE-SIM and 466.3 s for STRIVE).

## Relation to Prior Work

CCFM builds directly on conditional flow matching (Tong et al., 2023) as its generative backbone and adapts the hard-constraint manifold projection strategy from PCFM (Utkarsh et al., 2025), which was previously applied only to physics-governed PDE systems. The key departure is from the dominant diffusion-based safety-critical generation paradigm (SAFE-SIM, CCDiff, DiffScene), which relies on cost-based soft guidance that cannot guarantee satisfaction of geometric and severity constraints. By reformulating scenario generation as a collision-constrained sampling problem solved via Gauss-Newton projection at each ODE step, CCFM bridges the gap between learned traffic priors and hard physical collision specifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that hard-constraint enforcement (46.4% collision rate) is dramatically more effective than soft guidance (10.2%) for structured safety-critical generation, providing a principled alternative to the soft-guidance approach that dominates the field. However, the evaluation is confined to a single simulation framework (TBSim) and does not distinguish avoidable from physically unavoidable collisions, limiting claims about planner stress-testing. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to more complex multi-interaction scenarios, how it scales to end-to-end AV evaluation, and whether sim-to-real crash data generation is feasible with the current realism trade-offs.
