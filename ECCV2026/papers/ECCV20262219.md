## Problem

Merging multiple independently reconstructed 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes into a unified representation is essential for large-scale 3D mapping but remains underexplored. Existing learning-based methods require training on large 3DGS datasets, while image-based optimization methods depend heavily on coarse initialization from generic foundation models and suffer from expensive refinement and poor merging quality (hollows, floaters).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262219/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two independently reconstructed 3DGS scenes, each defined by a set of Gaussian primitives (mean position, 3×3 covariance, opacity, color) and camera poses, with initial random orientations and large displacements ensuring no spatial overlap. It outputs a rigid SE(3) transformation aligning scene B to scene A and a merged unified 3DGS scene. Internally, the method renders images from uniformly sampled camera poses of each scene to construct object-level 3D scene graphs, requiring only a one-time preprocessing step per scene that can be reused across multiple registration tasks.

## Method

Graph-GSReg reformulates 3DGS registration as a graph registration problem by constructing a 3D scene graph from each scene: rendered images are segmented with SAM, CLIP features are extracted per mask, and Gaussians are associated to masks via 2D projection to form nodes with 3D positions and appearance features enriched by 3-hop neighborhood CLIP histograms. Candidate correspondences between the two graphs are generated by cosine similarity of node embeddings, then filtered through a compatibility graph where edges enforce pairwise distance consistency via TRIMs. A maximum clique search on this compatibility graph yields the largest set of mutually consistent correspondences, from which an initial SE(3) transformation is estimated via SVD (or Umeyama for scale) and refined with ICP on Gaussian means. For seamless merging, a Self-Supervised Test-Time Optimization refines the voxelized union of both Gaussian sets by minimizing L1 photometric loss between merged-scene renderings and the original scenes' renderings from the same viewpoints, using the original scenes as self-supervised references to eliminate occlusion artifacts and floaters.

## Results

On ScanNet-GSReg (82 scene pairs), Graph-GSReg with ICP refinement achieves the best RRE of 1.970° and RTE of 0.025 m, outperforming GaussReg (2.827°, 0.042 m) and PhotoReg (7.366°, 0.072 m) while running in 3.66 s. On the larger uHumans2 dataset (69 scene pairs), the improvement is more dramatic: RRE drops to 0.711° and ATE to 0.192 m, compared to 9.959° and 2.754 m for PhotoReg and 9.099° and 1.669 m for TEASER++&ICP. For merging quality, the Self-Supervised TTO strategy yields PSNR of 21.90 dB (ScanNet-GSReg) and 25.40 dB (uHumans2) under ground-truth registration, approaching the Oracle upper bound of 22.29 dB and 32.10 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Graph-GSReg outperforms all five baselines on both registration datasets. Against GaussReg [4] (learning-based, requires large 3DGS training data), Graph-GSReg reduces RRE from 2.827° to 1.970° and RTE from 0.042 to 0.025 m on ScanNet-GSReg, while also being faster (3.66 s vs 4.80 s). Against PhotoReg [33] (DUSt3R-based image registration), the gains are substantial: RRE improves from 7.366° to 1.970° on ScanNet-GSReg and from 9.959° to 0.711° on uHumans2, with runtime reduced by over 150× (3.66 s vs 585 s). Against Liu et al. [16] (skeleton-based registration), Graph-GSReg achieves lower RRE (1.970° vs 2.595°) and RTE (0.025 vs 0.045 m). On uHumans2, traditional point-cloud methods TEASER++ [32] and MAC [35] fail catastrophically (RRE 9.1° and 60–108° respectively), while Graph-GSReg maintains sub-degree accuracy (0.711°), demonstrating the advantage of scene-graph-based semantic matching over purely geometric features in large indoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Graph-GSReg departs from the two dominant paradigms for 3DGS registration—learning-based Gaussian-to-Gaussian matching (GaussReg) and image-based optimization with foundation-model initialization (PhotoReg)—by reformulating the problem as graph registration over 3D scene graphs enriched with CLIP semantic features and 3-hop structural context. It borrows the TRIMs distance-consistency constraint from TEASER++ and the maximum clique search from prior point cloud registration work, but applies them to a semantically rich, object-level graph rather than raw geometric features. The self-supervised test-time optimization for merging is a novel contribution that replaces the heuristic distance-thresholding used by GaussReg with a photometric refinement guided by the original scenes as references.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3DGS registration is a prerequisite for large-scale 3D mapping and long-term map management, yet the area remains underexplored with only a handful of prior methods. The training-free, graph-based approach removes the need for large 3DGS training datasets and offers a reusable scene-graph representation that can be cached per scene. Key open questions include extending the pairwise formulation to multi-scene registration, handling dynamic or partially observed environments, and reducing the preprocessing cost of scene graph construction. The method's reliance on SAM and CLIP also raises questions about robustness in domains where these models perform poorly (e.g., industrial or outdoor scenes with repetitive structures).
