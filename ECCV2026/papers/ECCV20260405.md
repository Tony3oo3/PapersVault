## Problem

Gaussian Splatting-based CT reconstruction (R2-Gaussian) still requires over 9× longer execution than classical FISTA on standard 512² projections, and the gap widens at the 1k–2k resolutions used by modern scanners, making GS-based methods impractical for routine use. Additionally, existing GS pipelines lack the warm-start capability from volumetric priors that is standard in algebraic reconstruction, and the voxelization step is too slow to enable direct Gaussian fitting to pre-existing volumes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260405/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of X-ray projection images (25–75 views at 512² to 2048² pixels, in either parallel-beam or cone-beam geometry) and optionally a volumetric prior for warm-starting. It produces a 3D Gaussian splat model (40,000–50,000 Gaussians, each parameterized by 11 values) that can be voxelized on demand into a dense CT volume (256³–1024³ in experiments), or stored directly as a quantized float16 representation at 22 bytes per Gaussian for compression.

## Method

FaCT-GS re-implements and accelerates the full R2-Gaussian pipeline through three key optimizations: (1) rasterization kernels using axis-aligned rectangular bounding boxes with density-aware culling and a per-Gaussian backward pass to eliminate write conflicts, (2) a streamlined voxelizer that removes unnecessary ordering logic and coalesces parameters into compact float4 structs, and (3) fused CUDA kernels for 2D/3D SSIM and 3D total variation losses. Two novel initialization strategies are introduced: gradient-magnitude-based Gaussian sampling from an FDK reconstruction, and a rapid 50-iteration volume-fitting stage that directly optimizes Gaussians against a prior volume using L1 and 3D SSIM losses. The reconstruction loop alternates between rasterizing Gaussians into projections (optimized with L1 + SSIM loss) and voxelizing a random 32³ sub-volume for 3D TV regularization, with adaptive control (pruning, cloning, splitting) applied each iteration.

## Results

On the 512²-projection benchmark (15 synthetic + 3 real CT volumes), FaCT-GS achieves the highest SSIM across all sparse-view settings (0.950 / 0.943 / 0.92 for 75/50/25 views) and near-best PSNR (39.05 / 37.97 / 35.33 dB), completing a full 400-iteration reconstruction in 1 m 52 s (75-view) versus 9 min for R²-Gaussian. After only 150 iterations (43 s), FaCT-GS already matches R²-Gaussian's 400-iteration SSIM (0.946) and surpasses FISTA (0.934). In the scaling study, FaCT-GS's per-iteration speed advantage grows with resolution, reaching 13× faster than R²-Gaussian and 31.5× faster than FISTA at 2048² projections / 1024³ volumes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical algebraic baselines, FaCT-GS clearly dominates: it outperforms FDK by >12 dB PSNR and >0.5 SSIM, and beats SIRT by ~5 dB PSNR and ~0.1 SSIM while being roughly 2× faster. Compared to FISTA (the strongest classical method), FaCT-GS achieves higher SSIM in all three view settings (e.g., 0.950 vs 0.934 at 75 views) with comparable PSNR (39.05 vs 37.07), and its speed advantage widens dramatically at higher resolutions (31.5× at 2048²). Against the NeRF-based methods IntraTomo and NAF, FaCT-GS wins on both PSNR and SSIM by a wide margin (e.g., +9.4 dB PSNR over IntraTomo at 75 views) while being 15–18× faster. The closest competitor is R²-Gaussian (the prior SotA GS method): FaCT-GS matches or slightly exceeds its PSNR (39.05 vs 39.04 at 75 views; 35.33 vs 35.23 at 25 views) and achieves higher SSIM in all settings, while running 4.5× faster at 512² and up to 13× faster at 2048² projections. Notably, FaCT-GS at just 150 iterations (43 s) already matches R²-Gaussian's full 400-iteration SSIM, making it the only learning-based method that beats FISTA on both speed and quality simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaCT-GS is built directly on R2-Gaussian, the current state-of-the-art Gaussian Splatting CT reconstruction pipeline, which itself derives from the original 3D Gaussian Splatting framework for scene rendering. The paper's core contribution is a systematic re-implementation and CUDA-level acceleration of the rasterization, voxelization, and loss-function kernels, drawing on established GS acceleration techniques (per-Gaussian backward passes from Taming 3DGS, rectangular bounding boxes from Speedy-Splat/StopThePop/AdR-Gaussian) and adapting them to the CT-specific constraint that ray integration order is irrelevant. Beyond acceleration, the paper introduces two novel initialization strategies—gradient-magnitude-based Gaussian sampling and a rapid volume-fitting stage for warm-starting from a prior—that have no direct counterpart in prior GS-CT work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the speed gap with FISTA (the de facto standard in clinical CT) while maintaining a quality edge, FaCT-GS makes learning-based sparse-view reconstruction practically viable for the first time, particularly at the 1k–2k resolutions that modern scanners produce. The warm-start and volume-fitting capabilities bridge GS with the prior-image-constrained workflows already common in algebraic reconstruction, and the demonstrated JPEG-comparable compression of volumes via quantized Gaussians opens a path toward compact storage of large CT datasets. Open questions include the lack of principled guidance for selecting Gaussian count, the limited evidence that warm-starting consistently reduces total iteration count (beyond initial SSIM), and whether the approach extends to dynamic or multi-energy CT settings.
