## Problem

Standard functional maps rely on a single linear matrix to align truncated Laplace-Beltrami spectral bases between shapes, which fails under significant intrinsic distortions such as partiality, topological noise, or unstructured point clouds. Existing refinement methods (e.g., NAM) can model non-linear corrections but require per-pair test-time optimization and accurate initial correspondences, making them brittle and computationally expensive. The paper addresses the gap of learning a generalizable, unsupervised mechanism to predict non-linear spectral basis alignment without ground-truth correspondences.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a pair of 3D shapes represented as triangle meshes, point clouds, or a mixed mesh–point-cloud pair. A Siamese DiffusionNet extracts per-vertex features, from which a standard k×k functional map C is computed; this matrix is then fed to the hyper-network as a single-channel image. The output is a set of vertex-wise correspondences obtained by nearest-neighbor search in the non-linearly aligned spectral space, alongside the predicted MLP weights (three k×k matrices) that define the neural functional map.

## Method

The core idea is to replace the linear functional map with a non-linear neural functional map h_θ defined as a 3-layer MLP with a residual skip-connection of the standard FM: h_θ(φ_N) = C·φ_N + W³σ(W²σ(W¹φ_N)), so the network only needs to learn the non-linear residual correction. A Vision Transformer hyper-network interprets the k×k functional map as a single-channel image and predicts the three k×k weight matrices of the MLP via self-attention over frequency-band tokens, enabling it to capture global spectral dependencies. Training is fully unsupervised via a spectral alignment loss that minimizes the Frobenius distance between soft-correspondence-transported target bases and the non-linearly warped source bases, requiring no ground-truth correspondences. The framework is plug-and-play: it slots into existing deep functional map pipelines (ULRSSM, SSMSM) as a post-hoc refinement stage, and at inference the hyper-network produces the NFM weights in a single forward pass without per-pair optimization.

## Results

On partial shape matching (SHREC16/PFAUST), our method achieves 2.8/7.6 geodesic error (×100) on CUTS/HOLES without DINOv3, and 1.9/4.7 with DINOv3, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline ULRSSM (3.2/8.2 and 4.3/9.7 respectively). On the most challenging PFAUST Hard benchmark, our unsupervised approach reaches 2.3 (+DINOv3), surpassing even the best supervised method GeomFMaps (3.8). On multi-modal matching, we set new state-of-the-art across all three datasets: 1.8 on FAUST (mesh), 4.6 on SCAPE (mesh), and 4.3 on SHREC19 (mesh), compared to the previous best SSMSM at 2.0, 9.2, and 6.8 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Our method outperforms all unsupervised baselines across every partial matching benchmark. Against ULRSSM [16] (the strongest unsupervised baseline), we improve by 0.4 on SHREC16 CUTS (2.8 vs. 3.2) and by 1.4 on HOLES (7.6 vs. 8.2) without DINOv3; with DINOv3, the gap widens to 2.4 on HOLES (4.7 vs. 9.7) and 2.9 on PFAUST Hard (2.3 vs. 5.2). Notably, our unsupervised method with DINOv3 surpasses the best supervised baselines (GeomFMaps [22] and DPFM-sup [3]) on PFAUST Hard (2.3 vs. 3.8) and SHREC16 HOLES (4.7 vs. 6.6/6.8). On multi-modal matching, we beat the previous state-of-the-art SSMSM [15] by large margins: 4.6 vs. 9.2 on SCAPE (mesh) and 4.3 vs. 6.8 on SHREC19 (mesh), while also outperforming supervised methods like DiffFMaps [51] (19.0 on SCAPE) and TransMatch [74] (33.6 on SCAPE). The only case where a baseline matches ours is PFAUST Medium with DINOv3, where ULRSSM also achieves 1.7.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard functional map framework (Ovsjanikov et al.) by replacing the linear spectral transformation with a non-linear neural functional map (NFM), building directly on the neural adjoint map (NAM) architecture of Viganò et al. However, it departs from NAM's per-pair test-time optimization by introducing a ViT-based hyper-network that predicts the NFM weights from the initial standard functional map, shifting the paradigm from iterative refinement to predictive learning. The framework is designed as a drop-in module for existing unsupervised deep functional map pipelines (Cao et al. 2023, 2024), which provide the initial spectral alignment and soft correspondences that condition the hyper-network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in functional map methods—the inability of linear transformations to align spectral bases under significant intrinsic distortion (partiality, topological noise)—and demonstrates that a lightweight non-linear correction, predicted by a generalizable hyper-network, can close this gap without ground-truth supervision. The seamless integration into existing pipelines and the cross-dataset generalization (train on CUTS, test on HOLES) suggest broad practical utility for real-world 3D scanning data. A key limitation is the continued reliance on Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions, which may degrade under extreme geometric distortion; the authors identify multi-shape matching with cycle-consistency and virtual universal spectral bases as a natural next step.
