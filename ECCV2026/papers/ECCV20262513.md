## Problem

Synthesizing high-fidelity, controllable 4D LiDAR sequences for autonomous driving simulation is hindered by three interrelated issues: conventional Cartesian voxelization ignores the sensor's native ray geometry and introduces quantization artifacts, the temporal sparsity of point clouds makes preserving frame-to-frame coherence difficult, and existing conditional generation relies on 2D BEV layouts that flatten 3D structure and prevent precise geometry-aware control over generated scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262513/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporal LiDAR sequences (4 s duration) voxelized into a Hybrid-Cylindrical-Spherical (HCS) 3D occupancy grid at resolution 640×1088×32 (nuScenes) or 640×1088×64 (KITTI/KITTI-360), where the ray dimension explicitly stores multiple returns per angular bin rather than collapsing to a 2.5D range map. For conditional generation, a 4D point cloud-aligned voxel layout is additionally supplied as a structural constraint. The VQ-VAE encoder compresses the sequence into discrete tokens from a 2048-entry codebook (dimension 1024), and the MaskSTART decoder outputs full 4D LiDAR point cloud sequences at the original resolution.

## Method

LiSTAR unifies three components: (1) an HCS coordinate voxelization that partitions space into angular-radial bins centered at the sensor origin, preserving range-dependent resolution and multi-return structure; (2) a START module combining Spatial Ray-Centric Attention (SRA), which unfolds the feature tensor along the ray dimension and applies self-attention to model radial dependencies and occlusions, with Cyclic-Shifted Temporal Causal Attention (CSTA), which alternates standard and cyclically-shifted window attention to restore continuity across the 0°/360° azimuthal seam while enforcing strict causal ordering over L past frames; and (3) a MaskSTART framework—a 24-layer bidirectional transformer trained with a MaskGIT-style masked-token objective—that performs both prediction and layout-conditioned generation by fusing historical context with a 4D voxel layout through a zero-initialized adapter. The VQ-VAE (12 START blocks, hidden dim 512) is trained with a combined vector-quantization, voxel-reconstruction, and rendered-point-cloud loss, and the MaskSTART is trained for 200 epochs with AdamW at lr 5e-5.

## Results

On point cloud prediction (the most heavily benchmarked task), LiSTAR achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.30 on nuScenes 1s and 0.48 on nuScenes 3s, reducing L1 Med by up to 50% and 64% over the strongest baseline Copilot4D on nuScenes and KITTI respectively. For reconstruction, LiSTAR reaches an IoU of 0.583 on nuScenes and 0.480 on KITTI-360, a relative 85% IoU gain over OpenDWM on KITTI-360. For generation, LiSTAR reduces MMD by 61% (from 41.1 to 16.1 on KITTI-360) and Chamfer distance by up to 63% at the 50 m range compared to OpenDWM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiSTAR outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated task and dataset. In prediction, the strongest competitor is Copilot4D [64], which LiSTAR beats by 17% Chamfer and 50% L1 Med on nuScenes 1s, and by 49% Chamfer and 64% L1 Med on KITTI 3s; older methods (SPFNet, S2Net, 4D-Occ) are surpassed by even larger margins (e.g., 86% Chamfer reduction over SPFNet on nuScenes 1s). In reconstruction, LiSTAR surpasses OpenDWM [1,6,42] by 32% IoU and 60% MMD on nuScenes, and by 85% IoU and 79% MMD on KITTI-360. In generation, LiSTAR reduces Chamfer distance by 52–63% and MMD by 61% over OpenDWM on KITTI-360. No baseline matches or exceeds LiSTAR on any reported metric across the three tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiSTAR builds on the VQ-VAE paradigm for discrete scene tokenization and the MaskGIT masked-generative framework, but adapts both to the specific geometry of spinning LiDAR sensors. Its key departure from prior LiDAR world models (Copilot4D, LiDAR-DM) is the Hybrid-Cylindrical-Spherical (HCS) voxelization, which replaces Cartesian or 2.5D range-image grids with a 3D occupancy grid aligned to the sensor's native ray structure, and the Ray-Centric Attention mechanism that explicitly models correlations along individual sensor rays rather than treating voxels as unordered tokens. The Cyclic-Shifted Temporal Causal Attention borrows the shifted-window idea from Swin Transformer to handle the azimuthal seam discontinuity, while the MaskSTART component extends MaskGIT from 2D images to 4D sequences with a novel 4D voxel-layout conditioning signal that replaces the 2D BEV layouts used in prior controllable generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiSTAR provides a unified, geometry-aware framework that simultaneously handles reconstruction, prediction, and controllable generation of 4D LiDAR sequences, which is directly relevant to building scalable simulation environments for autonomous driving safety evaluation. The HCS representation and ray-centric attention offer a practical design principle—aligning the computational grid with sensor acquisition geometry—that could transfer to other range-based sensors. However, the evaluation is limited to a small set of baselines (primarily OpenDWM for reconstruction/generation and Copilot4D for prediction), and the paper does not explore multi-modal conditioning (e.g., camera, radar) or scaling to longer horizons, leaving open whether the gains persist under more diverse and challenging evaluation protocols.
