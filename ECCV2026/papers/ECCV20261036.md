## Problem

Existing reasoning-based image editing benchmarks focus narrowly on single-object attribute changes in realistic scenarios and rely solely on textual references for evaluation, which leads to systematic misjudgments in complex reasoning tasks. They neglect multi-object physical interactions and game-world scenarios governed by human-defined rules (e.g., Sokoban, Sudoku), leaving a critical gap in assessing whether models can perform implicit multi-step reasoning during image editing.

## Input / Output

The editing model receives an original RGB image and a natural-language editing instruction, and produces both a step-by-step chain-of-thought reasoning trace and the final edited image. For evaluation, each sample is assessed against a dual-reference pair: a textual description of the expected edit effect and a ground-truth reference image, enabling the VLM judge (GPT-4.1) to perform direct visual comparison rather than relying on text alone. The benchmark comprises 2,700 curated samples (150 per sub-dimension) spanning 8 primary dimensions and 18 sub-dimensions across real-world and game-world scenarios.

## Method

The paper introduces a multi-scenario data synthesis pipeline with distinct strategies: for real-world scenarios, hand-crafted text triples are expanded via VLM and then rendered into image pairs by GPT-4o, followed by VLM-based quality filtering and CoT annotation; for game-world scenarios, Python programs generate logically correct image pairs, instructions, and programmatic CoT traces that are then converted to natural-language reasoning via VLM. This pipeline yields UniREdit-Data-100K (100,421 samples) with high-quality CoT annotations. To validate the dataset, the authors fine-tune Bagel using a joint objective combining negative log-likelihood on the CoT reasoning text and a flow-matching loss on VAE latents conditioned on the original image, instruction, and reasoning trace (λ_text=2, λ_img=1). The evaluation protocol scores three dimensions—Instruction Following (weighted 0.5), Visual Consistency (0.3), and Visual Quality (0.2)—using GPT-4.1 as judge with both textual and GT image references, and the authors demonstrate via ablation that dual-reference evaluation consistently reduces MAE against human expert scores compared to text-only or image-only references.

## Results

On UniREditBench (2,700 samples, 8 dimensions × 18 sub-dimensions), UniREdit-Bagel achieves the best overall score of 78.15, surpassing the strongest closed-source model GPT-4o (73.39) by 4.76 points. The largest margin appears in game-world scenarios, where UniREdit-Bagel scores 80.48 versus GPT-4o's 62.07 (+17.08), while GPT-4o retains a 5.27-point lead on real-world tasks (81.01 vs. 75.74). On the out-of-distribution RISEBench, UniREdit-Bagel reaches 18.3% overall, improving over the Bagel-Think baseline by 9.1 points and surpassing closed-source Gemini-2.0-Flash-exp (13.3%) by 5.0 points, though it remains below Nano Banana (32.8%) and GPT-4o (28.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniREdit-Bagel outperforms all 13 baselines on the overall UniREditBench score. Against the strongest closed-source model GPT-4o (73.39), it gains +4.76 overall, with the gap widening to +17.08 in game-world scenarios (80.48 vs. 62.07) but narrowing to −5.27 in real-world tasks (75.74 vs. 81.01), where GPT-4o still leads. Among open-source baselines, the margin is substantial: UniREdit-Bagel beats Qwen-Image-Edit (56.52) by +21.63, UniWorld-V2 (54.87) by +23.28, and the original Bagel (48.01) by +30.14. On the out-of-distribution RISEBench, UniREdit-Bagel (18.3%) surpasses all open-source baselines—improving over Bagel-Think (9.2%) by +9.1 and Qwen-Image-Edit (8.9%) by +9.4—and also exceeds closed-source Gemini-2.0-Flash-exp (13.3%) by +5.0, though it trails Nano Banana (32.8%) and GPT-4o (28.9%) by 14.5 and 10.6 points respectively. The paper also reports consistent gains on KRISBench (Appendix), confirming that the CoT-annotated training data transfers to other reasoning-based editing benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of reasoning-based image editing benchmarks (RISEBench, KRISBench) by broadening scope from single-object attribute edits in realistic scenes to multi-object physical interactions and rule-governed game-world scenarios (Sokoban, Sudoku, Pacman). Methodologically, it departs from prior text-reference-only VLM evaluation by introducing a dual-reference protocol that pairs textual descriptions with ground-truth images, addressing systematic misjudgments in complex reasoning tasks. The training component builds directly on Bagel's unified understanding-and-generation architecture, adding CoT text supervision alongside flow-matching image loss, while the game-world data synthesis pipeline draws on Game-RL's code-driven generation approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a significant and persistent gap: only GPT-4o and Nano Banana exceed 60 on game-world scenarios, indicating that rule-based logical reasoning remains a fundamental weakness of current editing models. The 100K CoT-annotated dataset and the finding that fine-tuning Bagel on it surpasses GPT-4o on the in-domain benchmark suggest that synthetic reasoning data is a viable path to closing this gap, though out-of-distribution gains on RISEBench are more modest. Open questions include whether game-world reasoning skills transfer to real-world physical interactions, whether the VLM-as-judge protocol remains reliable as models improve, and whether the benchmark will saturate quickly given the rapid pace of model development.
