## Problem

Existing 3D human pose estimation methods cannot handle prosthesis users: model-based estimators rely on anatomical models trained exclusively on non-amputee individuals and cannot represent prosthesis geometry, while model-free methods lack body kinematic priors and are unreliable under occlusion. This challenge is particularly acute for running-specific prosthesis (RSP) users, whose prostheses have complex curved geometry that moves dynamically during exercise. No dataset or benchmark existed for prosthesis-aware 3D pose estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260513/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes a monocular video (60 FPS) of an RSP user and produces 3D positions of 17 natural body joints (following the Human3.6M joint definition) plus a set of 3D points describing the RSP surface geometry. The RSP shape is annotated as a ruled surface constructed from densely placed reflective markers (~5 cm spacing) on both sides of the prosthesis, capturing its curvature and width. Evaluation uses MPJPE/MPJPE-PA for body joints and bidirectional Chamfer Distance with F-scores at multiple thresholds for RSP shape, assessed in both root-aligned and Procrustes-aligned spaces.

## Method

The paper introduces RSP3D, the first 3D dataset of RSP users, collected with 16 GoPro cameras and an OptiTrack marker-based motion capture system (12 Prime cameras) using QR-video-based time synchronization and ChArUco board calibration. For benchmarking, the authors evaluate model-based methods (SAM3D Body, MotionBERT, AJAHR) and model-free methods (SpatialTrackerV2 in Tracking and Pointmap modes) in a zero-shot setting, confirming that neither family alone suffices. The proposed hybrid baseline combines SAM3D Body for body joint estimation with SpatialTrackerV2-Pointmap for RSP shape recovery, aligning them through a three-step scale resolution: bounding-box scale estimation between the two outputs, root-position alignment to correct depth discrepancy, and a final 2D projection-consistent scale refinement that minimizes reprojection error using known camera intrinsics.

## Results

On RSP3D, SAM3D Body achieves the best body-joint accuracy among model-based methods (MPJPE 78.35 mm, MPJPE-PA 50.89 mm), outperforming MotionBERT (115.06/64.94) and AJAHR (125.06/90.85). Model-free methods (SpatialTrackerV2) are substantially worse on body joints (MPJPE 159.64–174.84 mm) but are the only approaches that recover RSP geometry (CD 277.15 mm, F@100 45.44% with 2D detection). The proposed hybrid baseline (SAM3D Body + STv2-Pointmap) matches SAM3D Body's body-joint accuracy exactly while adding RSP shape recovery, achieving CD 288.44 mm and F@100 45.61% with 2D detection, and CD 251.15 mm, F@100 50.72%, F-C@50 54.94% with 2D ground-truth inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM3D Body [45] is the strongest model-based method, achieving MPJPE 78.35 mm and MPJPE-PA 50.89 mm, roughly 37 mm and 14 mm better than MotionBERT [52] (115.06/64.94) and 47 mm and 40 mm better than AJAHR [4] (125.06/90.85); however, none of the model-based methods can output any RSP geometry. AJAHR, the only amputation-aware model, underperforms because it misclassifies natural limbs as amputated under occlusion. SpatialTrackerV2 [39] (both Tracking and Pointmap variants) is the only approach that recovers RSP shape, but its body-joint MPJPE is 81–96 mm worse than SAM3D Body, confirming the lack of kinematic priors. The hybrid baseline (SAM3D Body + STv2-Pointmap) wins the combined task: it retains SAM3D Body's exact body-joint accuracy while adding RSP geometry (CD 288.44 mm, F@100 45.61% with 2D detection; CD 251.15 mm, F@100 50.72% with 2D GT), a capability no single baseline provides. With 2D ground-truth inputs, the hybrid's RSP metrics (F-C@50 54.94%, F-C@30 27.52%) slightly exceed STv2-Pointmap alone (54.60%, 26.72%), validating the scale-alignment procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem space at the intersection of 3D human pose estimation and 3D shape reconstruction, rather than extending a single prior method. It identifies a fundamental incompatibility: model-based estimators (SAM3D Body, MotionBERT, AJAHR) are trained on non-amputee body models and cannot represent prosthesis geometry, while model-free reconstruction methods (SpatialTrackerV2) lack body kinematic priors and fail under occlusion. The hybrid baseline repurposes these two orthogonal tool families—using a model-based regressor for joints and a model-free point tracker for the RSP surface—joined by a scale-and-position alignment step, a combination not previously proposed for this setting. Prior prosthesis-related work (LDPose, InclusiveVidPose, ProGait, AJAHR) addresses only 2D pose, synthetic 3D meshes, or gait classification, leaving the joint 3D body-plus-prosthesis recovery task unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RSP3D is the first real-world 3D dataset with simultaneous body-joint and prosthesis-shape annotations, filling a gap that has blocked dedicated method development for prosthesis users. The paper's practical motivation—providing automated motion feedback for RSP users who train without professional coaching—gives the benchmark a clear application anchor in rehabilitation and inclusive sports. However, the dataset is small (6 participants, marker-based, indoor), the hybrid baseline is explicitly a zero-shot reference rather than a trained solution, and the authors acknowledge that the current scale is insufficient for reliable training. Key open questions include extending to markerless in-the-wild video, automatic amputation-site detection, incorporating biomechanical constraints, and scaling the dataset to support end-to-end learning.
