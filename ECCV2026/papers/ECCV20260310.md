## Problem

Metric feed-forward 3D reconstruction for panoramic imagery remains largely unaddressed due to the absence of large-scale panoramic RGB-D datasets with precise metric annotations, and existing perspective-trained models (e.g., VGGT) suffer severe degradation when applied to equirectangular panoramas. Additionally, in the practical sparse unordered capture setting, anchoring the global coordinate frame to a poorly chosen reference view causes global pose drift and spatially inconsistent reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sparse unordered set of 2–28 equirectangular (ERP) panoramic RGB images per scene, resized to 560×280 and then cropped 15% from top and bottom to 560×196 to mitigate pole pixel underutilization. It outputs per-view camera poses as a 9-dimensional vector (3D translation, 4D quaternion, and two confidence scores), a metric depth map, and point maps in three coordinate systems (camera, reference, world), each accompanied by a pixel-wise confidence channel. At inference, the multi-head point map branches can be disabled with no performance loss, keeping peak memory at 7.22 GB for 128 input frames.

## Method

Argus is a feed-forward Transformer with two stages: a lightweight Covisibility Transformer (2 alternating-attention layers) that predicts per-view global connectivity scores to dynamically select the geometrically optimal reference frame (ground truth computed via Dijkstra's algorithm on the precomputed covisibility graph), followed by a Geometry Transformer (24 alternating-attention layers) that aggregates multi-view features anchored to the selected reference. The key architectural insight is an overcomplete geometric factorization: the bidirectional pixel-to-world mapping under ERP projection is decomposed into six interpretable sub-steps (depth→camera point, camera→reference point, reference→world point, and their inverses), each predicted by a dedicated DPT head and supervised both independently and jointly via cross-coordinate consistency constraints. This multi-step supervision lowers optimization difficulty and enforces geometric coherence across prediction branches, while the fixed ERP projection model eliminates intrinsic-parameter ambiguity, enabling direct metric prediction without a separate scale-estimation module. Training uses a weighted combination of covisibility BCE, confidence-weighted camera/depth/point-map losses, and a geometry joint loss that chains predicted transforms across branches to propagate gradients mutually.

## Results

On the Realsee3D benchmark, Argus achieves an Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) of 0.096 m on the real subset and 0.027 m on the synthetic subset, with Acceptance Rates of 98.2% and 99.8% respectively. For metric depth estimation without alignment, Argus attains AbsRel of 0.050 (real) and 0.035 (synthetic), and for metric point-map reconstruction it reaches accuracy of 0.056 m (real) and 0.020 m (synthetic). On the synthetic subset, Argus also leads in AUC@10° for pose (97.29%) and in all depth metrics (e.g., δ₁ = 91.42% under IRLS alignment).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MapAnything360 (the only baseline supporting metric prediction), Argus reduces ATE by 28% on the real subset (0.096 vs. 0.134 m) and by 69% on the synthetic subset (0.027 vs. 0.087 m), and improves metric depth AbsRel from 0.070 to 0.050 (real) and 0.063 to 0.035 (synthetic). Against VGGT360, Argus outperforms on every reported metric for both subsets, with the largest gaps in AUC@10° on the real subset (85.52 vs. 83.00) and in depth AbsRel under IRLS alignment (0.048 vs. 0.051 real; 0.019 vs. 0.025 synthetic). Against π³360, Argus wins decisively on global metric accuracy (ATE, A.R.) for both subsets and on all synthetic-subset metrics, but π³360 retains a marginal edge in relative rotation/translation accuracy on the real subset (e.g., RRA@5°: 98.60 vs. 98.30), likely attributable to its stronger pre-training prior. Overall, Argus is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best metric pose, metric depth, and metric point-map accuracy across both real and synthetic splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

Argus extends the VGGT feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm (alternating attention, multi-task supervision over poses/depth/point maps) from perspective to equirectangular panoramic imagery, filling a gap where no prior feed-forward model was trained or evaluated on panoramic data. It departs from VGGT's and MapAnything's fixed heuristic reference-view anchoring by introducing a learned covisibility module that dynamically selects the geometrically optimal reference frame, and from π³'s full permutation-equivariant design by retaining approximate equivariance while preserving a smooth reference-frame-oriented optimization landscape. The geometric factorization supervision—decomposing pixel-to-world mapping into independently supervised sub-steps with cross-coordinate joint constraints—is a novel loss design not present in prior feed-forward reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Realsee3D (10K scenes, 299K panoramic viewpoints with metric RGB-D) is the first large-scale dataset purpose-built for training feed-forward panoramic 3D reconstruction, unblocking a field previously limited to small evaluation benchmarks (Matterport3D, Stanford2D3D) or perspective-only training data. The covisibility-guided reference selection addresses a practical failure mode in real-world sparse unordered capture that prior methods handle only with hand-designed heuristics. Key limitations remain: the model is indoor-focused and memory-constrained for very large panorama sets (hundreds to thousands of views), and the data-scale advantage over baselines makes it difficult to isolate the pure architectural contribution. Open questions include whether the geometric factorization strategy transfers to perspective or other projection models, and whether scale-agnostic panoramic reconstruction is achievable.
