## Problem

Existing LiDAR range image compression (RIC) methods fail to achieve an optimal trade-off between coding efficiency and real-time performance: traditional codecs (e.g., JPEG2000) struggle with LiDAR's high-frequency depth discontinuities and nonuniform range distributions, while learning-based approaches either sacrifice reconstruction fidelity for low latency or incur prohibitive sequential decoding costs. This paper targets the need for a single framework that delivers high rate-distortion performance while supporting both real-time lossy and near-real-time lossless operation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single LiDAR range image (64×2650 on Waymo, 64×2048 on SemanticKITTI) with 1 mm quantization and a maximum range crop (75 m / 70 m), and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed range image. In lossless mode all N bitplanes are entropy-coded; in lossy mode only the first M < N bitplanes are transmitted and the remaining planes are predicted at decode time, making M the sole knob controlling the bitrate–quality trade-off. At very low bitrates (<1 Bpp) the encoder additionally applies horizontal spatial downsampling (factor 2 or 4) to the input before bitplane decomposition.

## Method

BitRIC decomposes the range image into N binary bitplanes (MSB→LSB) and compresses them with a coarse-to-fine autoregressive entropy model where each bitplane's Bernoulli distribution is predicted conditioned on the partial reconstruction of all previously decoded planes. A shared contextual backbone—comprising a learnable range embedding, azimuth/elevation positional embeddings, a Horizontal Spatial-to-Channel Module (HSCM) that folds the wide horizontal axis into channels (r=4), and a U-Net for multi-scale aggregation—produces a common prior feature that feeds two specialized heads: a three-stage cascaded Probability Estimation Module (PEM) that exploits intra-plane spatial correlations for lossless arithmetic coding, and a One-Step Refinement Module (ORM) that regresses the normalized residual of all truncated bitplanes in a single convolutional pass for lossy decoding. The model is trained end-to-end with a loss of cross-entropy (bitrate) plus MSE on the residual map, omitting a conventional λ weighting because the truncation depth M explicitly governs the rate–distortion operating point.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, BitRIC achieves a BD-BR reduction of −61.73% (D1 PSNR) and −61.52% (D2 PSNR) in lossy mode relative to the JPEG2000 anchor, and 6.05 Bpp (−54.99% gain) in lossless mode. On SemanticKITTI, the gains are even larger: −69.92% and −70.82% BD-BR in lossy mode, and 8.43 Bpp (−47.83% gain) in lossless mode. BitRIC also maintains near-raw-data 3D object detection performance (65.67 mAP for pedestrian Level-1 at ~2 Bpp vs. 67.77 for uncompressed), while competing codecs drop sharply at the same bitrate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BitRIC outperforms all five baselines on both datasets in both lossy and lossless settings. Against the JPEG2000 anchor, BitRIC saves over 60% BD-BR on WOD and nearly 70% on SemanticKITTI in lossy mode, and reduces lossless bitrate from 13.44 to 6.05 Bpp (WOD) and 16.16 to 8.43 Bpp (SemanticKITTI). Among range-image-based methods, BitRIC surpasses RPCC by ~22 percentage points in BD-BR (−61.73% vs. −39.79% on WOD D1) and RCPCC by ~58 points (−61.73% vs. −3.35%). Against point-cloud-based methods, BitRIC beats G-PCC (−61.73% vs. −44.90%) and RENO (−61.73% vs. −47.30%) on WOD lossy D1, while also achieving substantially lower lossless bitrates (6.05 vs. 21.48 and 19.42 Bpp). In downstream 3D detection at ~2 Bpp, BitRIC retains 65.67 mAP (pedestrian Level-1) versus 58.95 for RPCC, while RCPCC and JPEG2000 fail to produce meaningful detections.

## Relation to Prior Work

BitRIC carves new ground by arguing that LiDAR compression should operate directly in the native range-image domain rather than through the conventional 2D-to-3D point cloud pipeline, thereby avoiding the redundant 'range-to-point-to-range' cycle that plagues PCC methods. Conceptually, it repurposes classical bitplane decomposition (a staple of lossless image coding) and pairs it with a learned autoregressive entropy model in the spirit of neural image compression, but instantiates this through a U-Net contextual backbone with a horizontal spatial-to-channel folding module tailored to the anisotropic geometry of LiDAR scans. The dual-path design (PEM for lossless, ORM for lossy) unifies two compression regimes in a single architecture, departing from prior learned RIC methods that typically commit to one mode or the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling real-time (>10 fps) lossy LiDAR compression with >60% BD-BR savings over JPEG2000 while preserving downstream 3D detection accuracy at low bitrates where competing codecs fail entirely—directly addressing bandwidth constraints in V2X and autonomous driving. Key limitations include reduced competitiveness at extremely low bitrates (<1 Bpp) where voxel-based octree methods retain an advantage, and the lossless mode's latency (~0.14 s encode) falling short of real-time. Open questions include whether temporal redundancies across consecutive frames can be exploited to further reduce bitrate, and whether model quantization can close the gap to real-time lossless operation.
