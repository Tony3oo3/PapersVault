## Problem

Existing multimodal reflection mechanisms for long video understanding rely on closed-loop internal self-reflection without objective external evidence, causing models to become trapped in blind confidence and frequently overturn correct answers during reflection (ΔAcc < 0). Additionally, applying standard reinforcement learning to multi-stage reflection pipelines induces severe policy coupling where the model exploits shortcuts (e.g., copying the initial guess) rather than learning genuine error-correction logic, a problem compounded by a critical scarcity of dedicated training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (up to 768 frames at 192×28×28 pixels per frame during inference) and a natural language question, and produces a final textual answer through a three-stage pipeline. Unusually, the model autonomously invokes temporal search tools to retrieve keyframes as external visual evidence; the verification stage operates under strict information isolation, receiving only the retrieved keyframes and the question (denied access to the full video or the initial hypothesis), while the arbitration stage may re-invoke tools to backtrack through the video for additional evidence before producing the final response.

## Method

Reflect-R1 trains a single unified policy π_θ that internalizes three distinct reasoning behaviors—intuition (y₁), independent blind verification (y₂), and arbitration (y₃)—within a multi-stage MDP, with the verification stage enforcing strict contextual isolation to ensure unbiased evaluation. The key training innovation is SD-GRPO (Stage-Decoupled GRPO), which computes advantage functions independently within each reasoning stage (intra-stage competition) to prevent the simpler intuition stage from dominating gradient updates and to eliminate policy coupling that causes the arbitration stage to degenerate into a trivial identity mapping. Stage-aware rewards include a ternary abstention incentive for verification (rewarding 'I don't know' over blind guessing under partial evidence) and an anti-corruption penalty for arbitration (penalizing the model for overturning a correct initial or verification answer). Training proceeds in two phases: supervised fine-tuning on a 90K-sample CoT dataset for cold start, followed by RL on a 30K-sample challenging dataset using SD-GRPO with a progressive two-stage optimization (Stage I warms up arbitration under strong KL constraints; Stage II performs full-chain joint optimization).

## Results

On VideoMME (overall, w/o subtitles), Reflect-R1 achieves 63.5%, outperforming the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (61.3%) and the closest tool-augmented baseline TimeSearch-R-7B (62.7%). On LongVideoBench, it reaches 62.5% (vs. 53.9% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 61.2% for TimeSearch-R-7B), and on MLVU it scores 69.8% m-avg. On Haystack-LVBench temporal search, Reflect-R1 attains 8.9% F1 (temporal) and 55.5% QA accuracy, surpassing TimeSearch-R (8.1% F1, 51.5% QA). Critically, Reflect-R1 is the only method showing positive reflection reliability: +2.82% ΔAcc on LongVideoBench and +1.41% on VideoMME, whereas all internal-reflection baselines degrade (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B drops by −6.63% on LVB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source 7B models, Reflect-R1 clearly leads: it beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +2.2 (VideoMME overall) and +8.6 (LVB), Vision-R1-7B by +2.1 and +9.7, Video-R1-7B by +2.7 and +2.4, and VL-Rethinker by +2.2 and +6.9. Against the closest tool-augmented open-source competitor TimeSearch-R-7B, Reflect-R1 gains +0.8 on VideoMME overall, +1.3 on LVB, and +0.5 on MLVU, while also improving temporal-search F1 from 8.1 to 8.9 and QA accuracy from 51.5 to 55.5 on Haystack-LVBench. However, Reflect-R1 trails the proprietary GPT-4o (71.9% VideoMME, 66.7% LVB) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (75.0% VideoMME, 64.0% LVB) by 8.4 and 11.5 points on VideoMME overall respectively, reflecting the capacity gap between a 7B open model and much larger closed models. The most distinctive advantage is in reflection reliability: every internal-reflection baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VL-Rethinker, Vision-R1, Video-R1) shows negative ΔAcc (ranging from −0.73% to −7.51%), while Reflect-R1 is the sole method with positive gains (+2.82% LVB, +1.41% VideoMME), confirming that evidence-driven verification genuinely corrects errors rather than amplifying hallucinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath by introducing stage-decoupled advantage computation (SD-GRPO) to handle multi-stage reasoning, and departs fundamentally from prior multimodal reflection work (Vision-R1, VL-Rethinker, Video-R1) that relies on closed-loop internal self-correction. Where those methods force the model to re-examine its own parametric knowledge, Reflect-R1 inserts an external evidence-retrieval step (keyframe temporal search) between intuition and arbitration, drawing on the tool-augmented video understanding line (VideoAgent, T*, TimeSearch-R) but repurposing retrieval as a verification mechanism rather than a primary input. The reward design also borrows from the LLM abstention literature, introducing a ternary honesty incentive for the verification stage and an anti-corruption penalty for arbitration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most impactful contribution is the empirical demonstration that internal closed-loop reflection actively degrades long-video QA accuracy across multiple models, challenging the field's assumption that more self-scrutiny always helps. The evidence-driven paradigm and SD-GRPO offer a concrete recipe for multi-stage RL training that avoids policy collapse, which could generalize beyond video to other multi-step reasoning settings. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to longer or higher-resolution videos without prohibitive inference cost, whether retrieval quality is a hard ceiling on correction success, and whether the three-stage decomposition is optimal or whether alternative verification architectures (e.g., parallel evidence gathering) could reduce latency.
