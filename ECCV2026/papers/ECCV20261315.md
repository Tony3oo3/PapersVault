## Problem

Latent diffusion models learn semantics only indirectly through a reconstruction-style denoising objective, causing slow convergence and limiting sample quality. Prior approaches that inject Vision Foundation Model (VFM) semantics—REPA (external alignment), REG (single global [CLS] token), and ReDi (linearly PCA-projected patch features)—each expose only a narrow, low-capacity slice of the VFM, under-utilizing the rich, nonlinear, multi-layer spatial semantics available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261315/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise and a class label as input and produces a 256×256 RGB image decoded from a frozen SD-VAE. Unusually, the diffusion process simultaneously denoises three entangled modalities: 4-channel VAE latents (32×32), 16-channel compressed patch-level VFM semantics (16×16, bilinearly resampled to the latent grid), and a single global [CLS] token, all within one SiT Transformer backbone. At inference, only the VAE latent stream is decoded to the final image.

## Method

REGLUE introduces a lightweight convolutional semantic compressor (a shallow CNN autoencoder with 256 hidden channels) that nonlinearly aggregates multi-layer DINOv2 patch features (last 4 layers, 3072 channels) into a compact 16-channel spatial representation, pretrained offline and then frozen. During diffusion training, VAE latents and compressed patch semantics are channel-wise merged (rather than sequence-concatenated) to avoid doubling the token count, while the global [CLS] token is appended as a single extra token; the SiT backbone jointly predicts velocity fields for all three modalities under a shared stochastic-interpolant schedule. An auxiliary REPA-style cosine alignment loss at a selected mid-block further regularizes internal features toward frozen VFM targets. The key insight is that nonlinear compression is essential: it re-balances the dimensionality gap between VFM features and image latents, preserves spatial semantic structure (verified via probing accuracy), and suppresses high-frequency components that diffusion models struggle to model, yielding substantially better FID than linear PCA at the same compression ratio.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance (SiT-XL/2), REGLUE at 80 epochs achieves FID 1.59, sFID 4.22, IS 282.9, Precision 0.78, and Recall 0.64, matching REG's 160-epoch performance (FID 1.59) with half the training. At 160 epochs, REGLUE further improves to FID 1.46, sFID 4.23, IS 293.9. On the smaller SiT-B/2 backbone (no CFG), REGLUE reaches FID 12.9 at 400K steps, a 60.9% reduction over the vanilla SiT-B/2 baseline (33.0), and on SiT-XL/2 (no CFG) achieves FID 2.5 at 1M steps, outperforming REG (2.7), ReDi (5.1), and REPA (6.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REGLUE at 80 epochs (FID 1.59) substantially outperforms REG at the same budget (FID 1.86) and matches REG's 160-epoch result, while also beating ReDi trained for 10× longer (FID 1.61 at 800 epochs). At 160 epochs, REGLUE (FID 1.46) surpasses REG at 160 epochs (FID 1.59) and remains competitive with REPA and REG trained for 800 epochs (FID 1.42 and 1.36 respectively), despite using 5× fewer training iterations. On the SiT-B/2 backbone without CFG, REGLUE (FID 12.9 at 400K) outperforms REG (15.2), ReDi (21.4), and REPA (24.4) by 17%, 40%, and 47% respectively. On SiT-XL/2 without CFG, REGLUE at 1M steps (FID 2.5) beats REG (2.7), ReDi (5.1), and REPA (6.4), and reaches REG's 1M-step quality with only 70% of the iterations (2.7 FID at 700K). In the unconditional setting, REGLUE (FID 28.7) outperforms REG (29.7), ReDi (43.6), and even the conditional SiT-B/2 baseline (33.0). REGLUE does not surpass the strongest SOTA methods (RAE at FID 1.13, REPA-E at FID 1.12) which use different VAE formulations, but achieves the best FID among methods using standard SD-VAE latents at matched or fewer epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

REGLUE sits at the intersection of three recent lines of work on injecting VFM semantics into latent diffusion: REPA's external representation alignment, ReDi's joint modeling of linearly PCA-projected patch-level features, and REG's joint modeling of a single global [CLS] token. The paper's core departure is arguing that each of these exposes only a 'narrow, low-capacity semantic slice' of the VFM, and that a unified framework jointly modeling nonlinearly compressed multi-layer patch features, a global token, and an external alignment loss within a single SiT backbone is necessary to unlock the full spatial and hierarchical semantics. The novel architectural component is a lightweight convolutional autoencoder (semantic compressor) that nonlinearly aggregates multi-layer VFM features into a compact 16-channel spatial representation, replacing the linear PCA projection used by ReDi.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: REGLUE matches REG's 1M-step FID (2.7) at only 700K steps and achieves 12.9 FID at 400K steps on SiT-B/2 (vs. 33.0 baseline), all without increasing inference-time compute or model parameters. The finding that nonlinear compression is essential—linear PCA of the same features yields 7+ FID worse—challenges the assumption that a simple linear subspace suffices for semantic guidance in diffusion. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ImageNet 256×256 to higher resolutions, video generation, or other VFM families, and whether the specific 16-channel compression dimensionality and last-4-layer aggregation transfer across architectures. The paper is limited to a single dataset and two model scales, and the unconditional generation gains, while consistent, are smaller in relative magnitude.
