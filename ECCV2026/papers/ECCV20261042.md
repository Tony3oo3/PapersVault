## Problem

Learning-based 6-DoF LiDAR localization methods are too computationally heavy for always-on deployment on resource-constrained edge devices, and while binary neural networks offer up to 32× memory and 58× compute savings, directly applying 1-bit quantization to LiDAR localization causes severe accuracy degradation due to limited representational capacity and the gradient mismatch introduced by the non-differentiable sign function.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3-frame LiDAR point cloud sequence (stride 2) projected into a range image of size 32×512 with patch size 4×16, and outputs a 6-DoF global pose (translation vector and rotation quaternion) via a fully binarized PoseDiffusion decoder that performs 10–15 iterative denoising steps. All weights and activations in both the backbone and decoder are constrained to 1-bit, with channel-wise scaling factors applied to recover magnitude information.

## Method

BiLoc formulates BNN training under the information-bottleneck principle and introduces a training-only auxiliary objective with two components: (1) a soft-masked feature distillation loss where channel-wise Mahalanobis distances between real-valued and 1-bit features produce importance weights that reweight an L1 distillation term against an offline DINOv2 ViT-S/16 teacher, and (2) a structure-aware distillation loss that models inter-sample relational alignment via optimal transport (Sinkhorn algorithm) with a pose-aware cost combining cosine feature discrepancy and geometric pose distance. The auxiliary gradients bypass the task head, providing stable optimization signals that reduce the relative impact of straight-through-estimator-induced gradient mismatch in the binarized encoder. A theoretical analysis shows that when auxiliary gradients are not adversarial to the task gradient, the relative mismatch proxy K_BiLoc ≤ K_base, confirming that the auxiliary objective stabilizes training without adding any inference overhead.

## Results

On the Oxford Radar RobotCar dataset, BiLoc achieves an averaged mean position/orientation error of 7.56 m / 1.91° with a fully 1-bit network, reducing the error of the strongest 1-bit baseline (BHViT, 8.41 m / 2.15°) by 10.11% in position and 11.16% in orientation. On the more challenging NCLT dataset (seasonal and structural changes), BiLoc reaches 3.14 m / 4.36°, outperforming BHViT (3.56 m / 4.47°) by 0.42 m / 0.11°. The 1-bit model uses only 3.01 M parameters and 0.17 G OPs versus 39.96 M / 77.06 G for the real-valued DiffLoc, yielding a 2.07× latency reduction (14.83 ms vs 30.72 ms on an RTX 5090) with no additional inference overhead from the auxiliary objective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 1-bit baselines, BiLoc consistently outperforms all three: ReActNet (15.16 m / 3.91°), BinaryViT (8.87 m / 2.57°), and BHViT (8.41 m / 2.15°) on Oxford, with the largest gap over ReActNet (7.60 m / 2.00°). On NCLT, the same ordering holds—BiLoc (3.14 m / 4.36°) beats BHViT (3.56 m / 4.47°), BinaryViT (4.69 m / 5.48°), and ReActNet (6.61 m / 6.62°). Against real-valued APR baselines, BiLoc's 7.56 m / 1.91° on Oxford is competitive with STCLoc (7.01 m / 1.28°) and PoseSOE (8.62 m / 2.00°) despite using 1-bit weights, and substantially outperforms PointLoc (12.45 m / 2.17°) and the PosePN family (8.62–13.48 m). However, the real-valued DiffLoc (3.53 m / 0.72°) and HypLiLoc (5.74 m / 1.05°) still achieve lower errors, confirming that 1-bit compression retains a measurable accuracy gap versus full-precision models. The key advantage of BiLoc is that it narrows this gap while reducing parameters from 39.96 M to 3.01 M and OPs from 77.06 G to 0.17 G, making it the first practical 1-bit LiDAR localization framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiLoc sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: BNNs for vision (ReActNet, BinaryViT, BHViT) and learning-based LiDAR localization (PointLoc, SGLoc, DiffLoc). It departs from prior BNN research, which has been validated almost exclusively on classification, by formulating the first 1-bit framework for 6-DoF LiDAR pose regression. Conceptually, it reframes BNN training through the information-bottleneck lens and borrows the offline distillation scheme from S2-BNN, while introducing a pose-aware optimal-transport structure alignment (via Sinkhorn) that has no direct precedent in the localization literature. The PoseDiffusion decoder from DiffLoc is retained as the task head, making BiLoc a drop-in backbone replacement rather than a new end-to-end architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a fully 1-bit ViT backbone can close most of the gap to its real-valued counterpart on large-scale outdoor LiDAR localization (7.56 m vs. 3.53 m on Oxford) with zero additional inference cost, BiLoc makes aggressive model compression a realistic option for always-on localization modules on edge hardware. The training-only auxiliary objective is a practical design choice that avoids the overhead of multi-exit or online-teacher distillation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other sensing modalities (RGB, radar), whether the reliance on a pre-trained DINOv2 teacher limits applicability in domains without strong foundation models, and whether the still-substantial accuracy gap to real-valued DiffLoc (roughly 2×) can be further narrowed without sacrificing the 1-bit constraint. The modest 2.07× measured latency speedup (vs. the theoretical 58×) also highlights that hardware support for 1-bit operations remains a bottleneck.
