## Problem

Existing diffusion-based industrial anomaly generation methods consistently fail to produce small-scale anomalies (smaller than 32×32 pixels at 512×512 resolution), which constitute over 56% of real anomaly samples in datasets like VisA. The root cause is compounded spatial downsampling—8× from the VAE encoder plus attention operating at 16×16 in the U-Net—resulting in a 32× compression that erases fine-grained anomaly information in the latent space before the model can learn it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a normal product image (512×512 RGB) and a stochastic anomaly mask (generated via Perlin noise) as input, along with a single learnable text embedding trained per product category. It outputs a 512×512 anomaly image with the defect injected at the masked region plus the corresponding binary anomaly mask. During both training and inference, the input image is decomposed into a geometric sequence of multi-scale crops (from the minimum anomaly bounding box up to the full image), each resized to 512×512, so the model operates on a set of crops rather than a single image.

## Method

UniScale introduces two key components. For training, the Error-Suppressed Multi-Scale Training (EMT) strategy constructs a set of crops at three scales—Maximum (full image, preserving location-aware appearance), Minimum (tight anomaly bounding box, preserving fine texture), and Intermediate (geometric progression bridging the two)—and optimizes a shared learnable text embedding via a Joint-Scale loss (masked diffusion noise prediction across all crops) plus a Cross-Scale Perception loss that enforces perceptual-feature consistency between adjacent-scale predictions to suppress upsampling interpolation errors. For inference, Generation-then-Fusion Denoising (GFD) decouples the process: it first generates the anomaly at the smallest crop using 50 steps of Blended Diffusion, then progressively pastes the anomaly into each larger crop and refines it with 10 denoising steps, preventing small anomalies from being overwhelmed by background structure. The entire pipeline is built on Stable Diffusion v1-4 with only text-embedding parameters updated (Textual Inversion paradigm), and a single unified model per product category covers all anomaly types.

## Results

On VisA, UniScale achieves an anomaly-region Inception Score (IS(a)) of 2.64, a 45.86% relative improvement over the best baseline SeaS (1.81), and on MVTec AD 2 it reaches IS(a) 1.68 vs. SeaS's 1.22 (37.70% relative gain). For downstream segmentation, UniScale attains 97.28% pixel-level AUROC and 30.15% IoU on VisA, and 77.67% pixel-level AUROC and 16.06% IoU on MVTec AD 2, improving pixel-level AUROC by 6.55% over the second-best method on MVTec AD 2. At the image level, it reaches 88.15% AUROC on VisA and 66.26% on MVTec AD 2, both state-of-the-art among the compared generative approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniScale outperforms all five baselines across every metric on both datasets. The closest competitor, SeaS (ICCV 2025), is surpassed by UniScale by 2.54 points in image-level AUROC (88.15 vs. 85.61) and 4.22 points in pixel-level IoU (30.15 vs. 25.93) on VisA, and by 2.04 points in image-level AUROC (66.26 vs. 64.22) and 3.69 points in pixel-level IoU (16.06 vs. 12.37) on MVTec AD 2. DualAnoDiff (CVPR 2025) trails UniScale by a wider margin, with pixel-level AUROC of 92.06 vs. 97.28 on VisA and 64.51 vs. 77.67 on MVTec AD 2. AnomalyDiffusion (AAAI 2024) and DFMGAN (AAAI 2023) are substantially outperformed, with DFMGAN achieving only 75.91% pixel-level AUROC on VisA compared to UniScale's 97.28%. Crop&Paste is evaluated only on whole-image generation quality (IS/IC-LPIPS) and does not report anomaly-region metrics, making it the weakest baseline overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based industrial anomaly generation (SeaS, DualAnoDiff, AnomalyDiffusion) by addressing a specific failure mode: the extreme 32× spatial compression (8× VAE + 4× U-Net) that causes small-scale anomaly information to be lost in latent space. It borrows the learnable text embedding paradigm from Textual Inversion and the local-editing mechanism from Blended Diffusion, but departs from prior subject-tuning work (Text2Traffic, SOEDiff) by introducing a Cross-Scale Perception loss to explicitly suppress interpolation errors introduced when upscaling small crops, and a Generation-then-Fusion Denoising strategy that decouples anomaly synthesis from background integration rather than performing both in a single denoising pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Small-scale anomalies constitute over 56% of VisA anomaly images, yet all prior generative methods fail on them, leaving a significant practical gap in industrial inspection pipelines. The paper's multi-scale training and progressive fusion framework is the first to close this gap, and the downstream segmentation gains (4.22% IoU on VisA, 6.55% AUROC on MVTec AD 2) demonstrate that better small-anomaly generation translates directly into better detection. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion v1.4 to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), whether the fixed α=4 cropping factor is optimal across all product categories, and how the method scales to even smaller anomalies (e.g., <16×16 pixels) where the latent representation may be a single token.
