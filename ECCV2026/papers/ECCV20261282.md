## Problem

Selective State-Space Models (Mamba) offer linear-time sequence modeling, but their native design assumes ordered 1D sequences and unidirectional information flow, which is fundamentally misaligned with the unstructured, spatially distributed, and bidirectional nature of multi-agent social interactions. Prior Mamba-based trajectory predictors either restricted the SSM to temporal-only modeling or reformulated it as an attention-like operator, leaving no architecture that uses SSMs in their native form for social interaction reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model receives observed 2D (x, y) trajectories of N agents over T_obs timesteps, with the ego agent explicitly designated. Neighbors are filtered by a spatial radius (e.g., 10 m) at the final observation frame, and future coordinates are zero-padded before encoding into an ego-centric social grid. The output is K multimodal future 2D trajectories for the ego agent over T_pred timesteps, trained with a best-of-K MSE loss that selects the closest predicted path to ground truth.

## Method

Social-Mamba introduces the Cycle Mamba (CM) block, a bidirectional SSM that concatenates a sequence with its reverse into a single continuous scan, so the forward pass is initialized with the backward pass's final hidden state—enabling state-level fusion rather than late output-level merging while halving parameters. Social interactions are decomposed via a social triplet factorization into three parallel CM scans: a temporal scan over each agent's history, an ego-centric scan that inserts the ego's current-state token into each neighbor's sequence, and a goal-centric scan that appends the ego's goal token. The three streams are fused by a learnable softmax gating network, followed by a global Mamba scan along the agent axis to capture cross-agent dependencies. A bidirectional Mamba decoder with K MLP heads produces the final multimodal prediction, and the entire encoder can be dropped into a flow-matching framework as a lightweight social interaction module.

## Results

On NBA-Full, Social-Mamba achieves ADE 0.72 m and FDE 0.92 m, outperforming all baselines while using only 1.9M parameters and 0.66 GFLOPs. This represents a 75% parameter reduction and 54% GFLOPs reduction compared to the prior state-of-the-art (OmniTraj: 7.5M params, 1.45 GFLOPs). On SDD, Social-Mamba reaches ADE 0.25 m / FDE 0.38 m, matching NMRF on ADE and improving FDE. On JRDB, it achieves ADE 0.13 m / FDE 0.21 m at 4.8 s, improving ADE by up to 13% and FDE by 8.7% over the previous best (NMRF: 0.15/0.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Social-Mamba outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and metric. On NBA-Full, it beats Social-Transmotion [35] by 0.06 m ADE and 0.09 m FDE, and surpasses the pretrained Multi-Transmotion [8] (0.75/0.97) and OmniTraj [9] (0.73/0.94) despite training from scratch without external pretraining data. On NBA Scoring, it reduces ADE by 8.2% and FDE by 7.3% over the prior best (RNLS&CLLS [33]: 0.61/1.09 vs. 0.56/1.01). On SDD, Social-Mamba (0.25/0.38) edges out NMRF [5] (0.25/0.39) on FDE and clearly beats Trajectron++ [37] (0.34/0.58), BiTrap [45] (0.32/0.57), SGNet-ED [41] (0.33/0.58), and Social-VAE [43] (0.27/0.39). On JRDB, it improves over NMRF [5] (0.15/0.23) and LED [29] (0.18/0.28) at the full 4.8 s horizon, with consistent gains at shorter horizons as well. Notably, Social-Mamba achieves these gains with 75% fewer parameters than OmniTraj and 67% fewer than Multi-Transmotion, while also reducing inference time to 3.4 ms and memory to 7.3 MB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of Mamba-based trajectory forecasting (U2Diff, Sports-Traj, Tamba, MambaPTP) by addressing the fundamental structural mismatch between 1D sequential SSMs and unstructured 2D social interactions. Prior work either restricted Mamba to temporal-only modeling or reformulated it as an attention-like operator; Social-Mamba instead imposes an ego-centric grid structure and decomposes social interactions into three parallel sequential scans (temporal, egocentric, goal-centric) via a novel Cycle Mamba block that unifies bidirectional processing into a single continuous state-space scan. The architecture is positioned as a direct efficiency alternative to Transformer-based methods like Social-Transmotion and Multi-Transmotion, which rely on quadratic-cost self-attention for all-pair interaction modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Social-Mamba demonstrates that selective state-space models can natively handle multi-agent social reasoning without reformulation, opening a path toward linear-complexity trajectory forecasting that scales to dense crowds where Transformer attention becomes prohibitive. The 75% parameter reduction and 54% GFLOPs reduction while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy make this practically relevant for real-time deployment in robotics and autonomous systems. Open questions include whether the ego-centric grid structure generalizes to 3D scenes or very large agent counts, whether the Cycle Mamba's single-pass bidirectional design transfers to other sequential tasks, and whether the modest accuracy gains over strong Transformer baselines persist as scene complexity grows beyond the tested benchmarks.
