## Problem

Monocular plane segmentation methods suffer from over-segmentation and false planar detections, particularly in smooth but non-planar regions, because planarity is rarely modeled as an explicit dense signal during learning. Compounding this, existing plane ground-truth annotations exhibit substantial geometric inconsistencies under strict coplanarity thresholds, meaning models trained on them inherit imprecise supervision that caps achievable precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262542/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (trained at 476×644, evaluated at 480×640) and produces per-pixel plane segmentation masks, per-plane 3D parameters (unit normal and offset recovered via SVD on the segmented 3D point cloud), and a dense planarity confidence map. The frozen MoGeV2 backbone supplies depth, surface normals, and a 3D point map at full resolution; the planarity head adds only a single sigmoid-activated channel, and the final segmentation is obtained through a parallel region-growing pass rather than a fixed number of predicted instances.

## Method

A lightweight planarity head (4.7M parameters, single output channel with sigmoid) is attached to the frozen MoGeV2 transformer backbone and initialized from the pretrained normal-head weights, then trained with binary cross-entropy on planarity labels derived from a newly generated, geometry-consistent ground truth that enforces strict distance and angular inlier thresholds plus semantic constraints. At inference, a parallel region-growing procedure evaluates 5×5 neighborhoods simultaneously (no sequential BFS/DFS), gating each neighbor by three joint conditions: planarity confidence above τ_p=0.3, Sobel normal-gradient magnitude below √(2−2cos 5°), and relative depth difference below 2.5% of the center pixel's depth; a pixel is connected if at least 8 of 24 neighbors satisfy all three. Plane parameters are then recovered per connected component via least-squares SVD fitting of the predicted 3D points. The paper also demonstrates that the planarity head, region-growing module, and geometric backbone are loosely coupled—swapping the depth/normal source (Metric3D, Depth Anything V2) degrades strict 3D precision but leaves the pipeline functional without retraining.

## Results

On ScanNet++, the proposed method achieves 62.1% precision at the strict 0.1 cm coplanarity threshold (vs. 28.3% for Zeroplane and 32.3% for PlaneRCNN), with 88.8% precision at 0.5 cm and 92.8% at 1.0 cm, while maintaining competitive recall (41.8% at 0.1 cm) and the best Segmentation Covering score (0.66). On Hypersim, precision at 0.1 cm reaches 78.4% (vs. 35.4% for Zeroplane), and on outdoor VKITTI2 it reaches 94.9% at 2 cm. The method also runs at 13.3 FPS with 335.6M parameters, faster than Zeroplane (3.4–4.5 FPS) and Sequential-RANSAC (1.4 FPS) while using fewer parameters than Zeroplane's DUSt3R variant (621.4M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on 3D precision at every threshold across all four datasets. Against the strongest baseline Zeroplane (CVPR 2025), the gap is most pronounced at strict thresholds: 62.1% vs. 28.3% precision at 0.1 cm on ScanNet++ and 78.4% vs. 35.4% on Hypersim, while Zeroplane retains a slight recall advantage at 1.0 cm (64.1% vs. 62.8% on ScanNet++). PlaneRCNN, the prior instance-detection SOTA, is surpassed by 30+ points in precision at 0.1 cm on ScanNet++ and by 37 points on Hypersim. Transformer-based baselines (PlaneTR, PlaneRecTR, PlanarRecon) are decisively outperformed, with F1 at 0.1 cm below 20% versus 50.0% for the proposed method. The Sequential-RANSAC approximation of MonoPlane achieves competitive recall at looser thresholds (e.g., 47.8% at 1.0 cm on ScanNet++) but lags in precision (72.5% vs. 92.8%) and segmentation quality (SC 0.56 vs. 0.66). A retrained Zeroplane with the same DINOv2/MoGeV2 backbone and in-domain GT (Table 8) still achieves only 16.6% precision at 0.1 cm, confirming that the gains stem from the planarity-guided grouping formulation rather than backbone quality alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant instance-detection and query-based formulations for monocular plane segmentation (PlaneRCNN, Zeroplane, PlaneTR) by reframing the problem as pixel-wise planarity prediction combined with geometry-driven region growing. It builds directly on the MoGeV2 monocular geometric backbone, keeping it frozen and attaching a lightweight planarity head initialized from the normal-prediction head. A key conceptual departure is the empirical demonstration that existing plane ground-truth annotations (notably PlaneRCNN's) contain substantial geometric inconsistencies under strict coplanarity thresholds, motivating a revised GT generation procedure that enforces tight distance and angular inlier constraints alongside semantic filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely-used plane annotations violate strict coplanarity constraints (e.g., 0% precision at 0.1 cm on ScanNet) is a useful diagnostic for the community, as it explains why prior methods plateau in geometric precision and suggests that supervision quality, not just model capacity, is a bottleneck. The modular design (frozen backbone + 4.7M-parameter head + parallel region growing) makes the approach easy to adopt with newer geometric backbones, and the retrained-Zeroplane comparison isolates the contribution of the planarity-guided formulation from backbone quality. Open questions include whether fixed coplanarity thresholds can be made adaptive to scene scale, how the method handles extremely thin or small planar structures, and whether combining planarity with explicit depth/normal uncertainty could further tighten segmentation boundaries.
