## Problem

Estimating linear polarization (DoLP and AoP) from a single RGB image is fundamentally ill-posed, and the Angle of Polarization becomes severely unstable in weak-polarization regions where the polarimetric signal is overwhelmed by noise. Existing approaches either directly regress DoLP/AoP (producing speckled, unreliable angle maps) or collapse polarization to grayscale, discarding the wavelength-dependent structure of polarization strength. Additionally, off-the-shelf latent VAEs introduce structured autoencoding bias when encoding polarization targets, further degrading cue quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, treated as a per-channel intensity proxy proportional to S0,λ, and produces a 6-channel output consisting of per-channel linear Stokes components (S1,R, S1,G, S1,B, S2,R, S2,G, S2,B) at full resolution. DoLP and AoP are then analytically derived from the predicted Stokes components via standard formulas, with AoP evaluation restricted to an observability mask where GT DoLP exceeds a threshold (τ=0.05). The channel-wise formulation is essential because polarization strength (DoLP) varies with wavelength due to dispersion and diattenuation, while AoP is primarily geometry-driven and more consistent across channels.

## Method

GenPolar casts the task as a Mueller-grounded S0,λ→(S1,λ, S2,λ) estimation problem and implements it with a two-stage diffusion pipeline. Stage I trains a multi-step conditional diffusion model (SD1.5 UNet + ControlNet) on 8-channel latents (two 4-channel VAE encodings concatenated) with a composite loss combining latent-space diffusion loss, global Stokes L1 loss, and an observability-aware AoP loss that supervises angles only where DoLP exceeds a threshold τ. Stage II distills the multi-step model into a one-step generator via Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) and simultaneously adapts the VAE encoder with LoRA adapters to correct polarization-domain autoencoding bias, with a KL regularizer keeping the adapted encoder posterior near the standard Gaussian prior. The one-step distillation is critical because backpropagating through long multi-step diffusion trajectories makes LoRA adaptation of the encoder unstable, whereas the single-step path yields well-conditioned gradients for joint optimization of the generator and encoder parameters.

## Results

GenPolar achieves the best DoLP-PSNR, DoLP-SSIM, and AoP-MeanAE across all three test groups (RLP, DoFP, RSP). On the RLP test set, it reaches 29.54 dB PSNR / 0.741 SSIM on DoLP and 20.15° MeanAE on AoP, outperforming the strongest baseline (MAE: 26.98 / 0.638 / 28.23) by 2.56 dB, 0.103 SSIM, and 8.08° respectively. On the more challenging DoFP and RSP groups, GenPolar leads by 2.61 dB PSNR and 11.05° MeanAE over MAE (DoFP) and by 4.62 dB PSNR and 5.91° MeanAE over MAE (RSP). Downstream, GenPolar attains 90.12% material-detection accuracy (vs. 93.75% for captured ground truth) and 22.41 dB / 0.127 LPIPS on de-reflection, closely matching the captured-input reference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenPolar is compared against three retrained baselines: PolarAnything (diffusion-based, grayscale DoLP/AoP), Restormer, and MAE (both from the RGB-to-Polarization benchmark [19]). GenPolar wins on every metric across all three test groups. Against PolarAnything, the advantage is most pronounced on AoP stability: GenPolar reduces MeanAE by 13.41° (RLP), 10.56° (DoFP), and 2.85° (RSP), while also improving DoLP-PSNR by 9.77 dB (RLP) and 6.16 dB (DoFP). Against the deterministic baselines, GenPolar outperforms MAE (the strongest non-diffusion baseline) by 2.56 dB PSNR and 8.08° MeanAE on RLP, and by 2.61 dB / 11.05° on DoFP. On downstream tasks, GenPolar's estimated cues yield 90.12% material-detection accuracy (vs. 82.75% for MAE and 45.36% for PolarAnything) and 22.41 dB PSNR on de-reflection, nearly matching the 93.75% / 22.44 dB captured-input reference, whereas PolarAnything degrades most severely in the discrete material-detection setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RGB-to-polarization estimation task established by Lin et al. [19] and the diffusion-based polarimetric synthesis of PolarAnything [40], but departs from both in its formulation: rather than regressing grayscale DoLP/AoP directly, GenPolar predicts channel-wise linear Stokes components (S1, S2) from S0, grounded in the Mueller transport formalism. The one-step distillation stage builds directly on Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) [38], while the use of ControlNet for structural conditioning and LoRA for VAE encoder adaptation repurposes standard Stable Diffusion components for a physics-constrained imaging task. The key conceptual departure from PolarAnything is the observability-aware AoP supervision, which explicitly masks out weakly polarized regions where angle estimation is ill-conditioned.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in polarization imaging: dedicated hardware (DoFP, RLP) is expensive and constraining, yet naive RGB-to-polarization methods produce unreliable AoP in weakly polarized regions, undermining downstream tasks like material detection and de-reflection. The finding that GenPolar's estimated cues nearly match captured polarization for material detection (90.12% vs 93.75%) while PolarAnything collapses (45.36%) demonstrates that physics-grounded, observability-aware estimation is not just a cue-level improvement but a practical enabler for polarization-based pipelines without specialized sensors. Open questions include extending to circular polarization (S3), handling polarized illumination and transparent/multiple-scattering paths where the unpolarized-illumination Mueller approximation breaks down, and whether the two-stage LoRA strategy generalizes to other physics-constrained latent domains.
