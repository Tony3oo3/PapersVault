## Problem

Existing gaze prediction models are constrained to short 3–5 second windows and rely on abstractions (saliency maps, discrete scanpaths) that collapse the fine-grained temporal dynamics of raw eye-tracking data. No prior method can synthesize continuous, high-resolution gaze trajectories over videos of arbitrary length, leaving a gap in capturing the long-range behavioral dependencies inherent in real-world content.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video as a sequence of RGB frames, which are first converted into compressed single-channel saliency latents via a modified UNISAL encoder (RNN channels reduced from 256 to 64, spatially downsampled by 2D average pooling), with conditioning applied every 5 frames to limit compute. A 90-frame (3 s) buffer of previously generated 2D gaze coordinates is concatenated as a historical prefix. The output is a continuous 2D gaze trajectory (x, y per frame at 30 Hz) generated autoregressively in 45-frame chunks, enabling infinite-horizon rollouts over videos of any duration.

## Method

The architecture builds on DiffEye's U-Net diffusion backbone (hierarchical 1D convolutions with multi-scale self-attention, sinusoidal timestep embeddings) but introduces two key modifications for video: (1) an autoregressive diffusion formulation where the model conditions on a 90-frame gaze history and denoises a 45-frame future segment, iteratively rolling out predictions for long-horizon generation; and (2) a saliency-aware latent conditioning mechanism that replaces raw RGB with compressed UNISAL saliency features injected via cross-attention at each U-Net block. Training uses a DDPM linear noise schedule (10⁻⁴ to 2×10⁻²) with the diffusion loss computed only on the predicted segment, while inference employs DDIM sampling in 50 steps. The saliency latents serve as a compact, gaze-relevant stimulus representation that is both semantically meaningful and content-independent, reducing parameter count by 3.9× and peak memory by 19.7× compared to raw-pixel conditioning while improving convergence stability over general-purpose visual tokenizers.

## Results

On DIEM, the proposed method achieves the best Levenshtein distance (3.40×10³ mean), DTW (12.33×10⁴ mean), and Maximum Temporal Correlation (0.235 mean) among all six baselines. On DHF1K, it similarly leads in Levenshtein (1.57×10³ mean), DTW (4.01×10⁴ mean), and Max Temporal Correlation (0.290 mean). The only metric where the method does not rank first is Discrete Fréchet Distance, where TPP-Gaze achieves a lower (better) score on DIEM (3.60 vs. 4.14 mean), a gap the authors attribute to the metric's sensitivity to local outliers in dense video gaze paths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines on Levenshtein distance, DTW, and Maximum Temporal Correlation across both DIEM and DHF1K. The closest competitor is DiffEye (the only other method producing dense frame-to-frame gaze), which trails by ~11% in Levenshtein and ~1.1% in DTW on DIEM, and by ~12% in Levenshtein on DHF1K. TPP-Gaze achieves the best Discrete Fréchet score on DIEM (3.60 vs. 4.14 mean), but a 12-participant user study shows the proposed method is perceived as closer to ground truth in 85% of trials, suggesting Fréchet is less suited to dense video gaze. HAT and DeepGaze III are the weakest baselines, with DTW scores 1.9× and 1.6× worse than the proposed method on DIEM respectively. On DHF1K, the gap narrows (DTW 4.01 vs. 4.24 for DiffEye), but the proposed method still leads on three of four metrics. The ablation 'DiffEye retrained w/ saliency' (Table 2) confirms that saliency conditioning alone is insufficient; the autoregressive diffusion component is critical for the observed gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DiffEye's diffusion-based continuous gaze generation from static images to video by adopting the autoregressive diffusion training paradigm from Diffusion Forcing, which stabilizes long-horizon rollouts by conditioning on clean or partially noised past tokens. Rather than conditioning on raw RGB frames or general-purpose visual tokens (MAGVIT2), the authors introduce saliency-aware latents from a modified UNISAL encoder as the visual conditioning signal, arguing that saliency is a stronger prior for inducing eye movements than general visual features. The work also departs from the 360-degree video gaze literature, which typically predicts viewport or head-orientation trajectories rather than high-frequency raw eye-tracking data, and from image-based scanpath models that collapse temporal dynamics into discrete fixation sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model to generate continuous, infinite-horizon raw gaze trajectories for videos of arbitrary length, addressing a gap where all prior methods were limited to ~3-second windows or discrete fixations. The saliency latent compression yields substantial efficiency gains (3.9× fewer parameters, 60× fewer FLOPs), making the approach practical for long-form content. Key open questions remain: the fixed sliding-window history cannot capture long-range behavioral dependencies (e.g., persistent background structure in static-camera scenes), current trajectory metrics (especially Discrete Fréchet) are poorly suited to video gaze evaluation as confirmed by the user study, and the model does not jointly generate head orientation, limiting applicability to VR/AR and physical-world settings.
