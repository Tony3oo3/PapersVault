## Problem

Existing imitation learning policies for robotic manipulation face a fundamental trade-off: continuous action-sequence predictors accumulate compounding errors over long horizons and average across multi-modal action distributions, while keypose-based predictors require external planners and cannot adapt in real time. No prior method simultaneously achieves long-horizon keypose-anchored planning, closed-loop real-time responsiveness, and consistent handling of multi-modal action modes within a single end-to-end model.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a history of h multi-camera RGB frames (4 cameras at 128×128 on RLBench, 2 cameras at 84×84 on RoboMimic, 2 cameras at 240×240 on the real xArm7 platform) together with the current end-effector pose. It outputs a continuous action trajectory from the current state to the next keypose, interpolated to a fixed length L (default 25) via cubic spline for translation and SLERP for rotation; at inference only the first n_exec actions are executed under a receding-horizon scheme while the remainder populates an action buffer for temporal ensembling.

## Method

SegDiff introduces Segmented Trajectory Modeling, which reformulates the diffusion policy objective to predict the complete trajectory from an arbitrary timestep to the next keypose (extracted via gripper-state changes or zero end-effector velocity, following PerAct), thereby anchoring learning to task-critical states while retaining continuous motion. At inference, the Dynamic Temporal Ensembling (DTE) mechanism generates two candidates in parallel: a standard prediction τ_rand from Gaussian noise and a prior-informed prediction τ_prior obtained by DDIM-inverting the stored action buffer and denoising under the latest observation. Two RMSE-based metrics (buffer validity d_valid and mode discrepancy d_mode) against a data-driven threshold δ then select among three blending strategies—blend τ_rand with the buffer, suppress a detected mode switch via τ_prior, or discard the buffer entirely—enabling real-time adaptation to environmental changes while preserving action-mode consistency across consecutive predictions. The backbone is a ResNet18 encoder (GroupNorm + spatial softmax pooling) per camera view feeding a standard DDPM denoising network, with DDIM inversion steps decoupled from sampling steps for flexible latency-accuracy trade-offs.

## Results

On 10 RLBench tasks, SegDiff achieves an average success rate of 86.7%±1.4%, outperforming the strongest baseline CoA (75.6%) by 11.1% and Diffusion Policy (41.6%) by 45.1%. On the full RLBench-60 benchmark, SegDiff outperforms DP and CoA by 34% and 9% respectively. On five real-world manipulation tasks, SegDiff achieves the highest success rate on 4 out of 5 tasks, including 100% on the multi-modal Package task and 90% on the dynamically perturbed Banana task, while being the only method to achieve partial success (60%) on the high-precision Screw Cap task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegDiff consistently outperforms all five baselines across both simulation and real-world settings. Against Diffusion Policy (DP) [Chi et al.], the most commonly used baseline, SegDiff achieves a 45.1% absolute improvement on RLBench-10 (86.7% vs. 41.6%) and a 34% improvement on RLBench-60, with particularly large gains on precision-sensitive tasks like Turn Tap (+42.7%) and Pick Up Cup (+90.7%). Against Chain-of-Action (CoA) [Zhang et al.], the strongest prior method on RLBench, SegDiff leads by 11.1% on average (86.7% vs. 75.6%), though CoA remains competitive on Open Box (76.0% vs. 62.7%) and Press Switch (44.0% vs. 68.0% where SegDiff actually wins). Against ACT [Zhao et al.], SegDiff surpasses it by 37.9% on RLBench and by a wide margin on real-world tasks (e.g., 90% vs. 30% on Flip Cup, 90% vs. 40% on Banana). Against the fine-tuned Octo policy [Zhang et al.], SegDiff leads by 22.3% on RLBench average, with Octo only winning on Open Box (96.0% vs. 62.7%). On RoboMimic, SegDiff outperforms AWE [Shi et al.] in low-demonstration regimes, demonstrating better data efficiency through keypose-anchored trajectory modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegDiff sits at the intersection of two established paradigms in imitation learning for manipulation: continuous action-sequence prediction (Diffusion Policy, ACT) and keypose-based prediction (PerAct, RVT). It departs from Diffusion Policy by replacing the fixed-horizon sliding window with variable-length trajectories anchored to the next keypose (borrowing PerAct's keypose extraction), and departs from keypose methods by generating the full continuous trajectory in a single closed-loop model rather than relying on an external planner. The Dynamic Temporal Ensembling mechanism repurposes DDIM inversion—originally a sampling acceleration technique—as a real-time buffer-validation tool, which is a non-obvious application of the deterministic diffusion framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that keypose-anchored trajectory modeling can close a substantial gap (34% over DP on RLBench-60) while preserving the closed-loop, real-time adaptability that open-loop keypose planners lack, and the DTE mechanism provides a principled way to handle multi-modal action distributions without mode-switching failures. The primary limitation is the reliance on heuristically defined keyposes (gripper state changes, zero velocity), and the authors explicitly flag automated or self-supervised keypose discovery as the key open direction. The minimal inference overhead (~5.4% over DP) makes the approach practically deployable, which strengthens its potential adoption in diffusion-policy-based manipulation pipelines.
