## Problem

Existing AI-generated video detection benchmarks emphasize visually benign scenarios and evaluate detectors primarily on perceptual artifacts, leaving social-risk contexts (violence, misinformation, public safety incidents) largely unexplored. This dataset bias reinforces a Perception-Reasoning Gap: artifact-centric detectors capture low-level forensic cues but cannot assess physical plausibility or social-logic coherence, while vision-language models perform semantic reasoning yet remain blind to fine-grained forensic traces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 8 uniformly sampled frames from an input video and produces a binary real/synthesized verdict accompanied by a confidence score and a structured, step-by-step evidence chain linking semantic hypotheses to concrete forensic observations. Internally, the pipeline generates intermediate artifacts including a global scene description, a forgery hypothesis, localized bounding boxes and pixel-level masks (via Grounding DINO and SAM 2), and four scalar forensic confidence scores (optical flow, depth, appearance, pixel-level) that are jointly fed into the reasoning stage.

## Method

SafeGuard is a two-agent, closed-loop framework that separates forensic perception from semantic reasoning. The Hierarchical Perceptual Solver first uses an LVLM (GPT-4o) to generate a social-risk-aware forgery hypothesis and route analysis to candidate regions, then applies Grounding DINO + SAM 2 for precise cropping, and extracts four complementary forensic cues via RAFT (motion), Depth Anything V2 (geometry), DINOv2 (identity), and D3 (pixel-level) before performing hypothesis-conditioned reasoning to produce an initial verdict. The Self-Reflective Verifier (Gemini-2.5-Pro) audits the full execution trace for evidence-hypothesis consistency, assigns a reliability score, and—when the score falls below a threshold γ=0.5—issues natural-language feedback that triggers up to L=3 iterative refinement cycles. The entire framework requires no end-to-end fine-tuning for detection; the forensic tools leverage publicly available GenVideo-finetuned weights, and the LVLM components operate in a zero-shot prompting regime.

## Results

SafeGuard (GPT-4o backbone) achieves 85.5% accuracy and 80.9% F1 on SafeVid (average of in-domain and out-of-domain splits), surpassing the best task-specific baseline BusterX++ by +18.7% accuracy and the best generalist model (GPT-4o alone) by +20.9% accuracy. Across four public benchmarks, SafeGuard reaches 97.9% on GenVideo, 97.0% on DVF, 81.7% on LOKI, and 84.6% on GenVidBench, yielding an overall average of 89.3% accuracy and 86.5% F1. The open-source variant SafeGuard (Qwen3-VL-32B) achieves 82.9% accuracy on SafeVid and 86.1% overall, approaching human evaluator performance (79.2% accuracy on SafeVid).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeGuard (GPT-4o) outperforms all 20 baselines across every benchmark. On SafeVid, it surpasses the strongest task-specific detector BusterX++ (66.8% Acc) by +18.7% and the best generalist model GPT-4o alone (64.6% Acc) by +20.9%, while approaching human-level performance (79.2% Acc). On GenVideo, SafeGuard reaches 97.9% accuracy versus DeMamba's 94.4% (the best task-specific method), and on DVF it achieves 97.0% versus DeMamba's 90.1%. On the more challenging LOKI and GenVidBench benchmarks, SafeGuard (Qwen3-VL) leads with 84.7% and 80.6% respectively, outperforming DeMamba (69.0% and 71.4%) by +15.7% and +9.2%. Notably, task-specific models trained on GenVideo show severe out-of-domain degradation on SafeVid (e.g., FTCN drops from 95.6% on GenVideo to 41.6% on SafeVid OOD), whereas SafeGuard maintains consistent performance without any task-specific fine-tuning, demonstrating superior cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeGuard carves new ground by explicitly framing the 'Perception-Reasoning Gap' between artifact-centric detectors (DeMamba, ReStraV, NSG-VD) and semantic-reasoning LVLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen3-VL), then bridging it through a multi-agent architecture that orchestrates specialized forensic tools (RAFT, Depth Anything V2, DINOv2, D3) under LVLM-driven hypothesis generation and self-reflective verification. Conceptually, it extends the multi-agent visual forensics line (UniShield, AIFo, Agent4FaceForgery) from image-level manipulation detection to video-level social-risk assessment, and repurposes the training-free D3 pipeline as one component within a larger evidence-chain rather than a standalone detector. The SafeVid benchmark departs from existing AI-generated video detection datasets (GenVideo, DVF, LOKI, GenVidBench) by introducing a social-safety taxonomy and V2V editing, addressing a distribution gap the authors identify between benign-scenario benchmarks and real-world high-risk content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The social-risk framing is timely as generative models (Sora, Veo3, Wan) can now synthesize complete misinformation events, making detection in safety-critical contexts a practical necessity that existing benchmarks underrepresent. The OOD performance collapse of task-specific detectors (dropping to 40–65% Acc on unseen generators) versus SafeGuard's more graceful degradation (76–80%) highlights a real generalization problem, though the remaining OOD gap and reliance on proprietary APIs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro) limit immediate reproducibility and deployment. Open questions include whether the multi-agent overhead is justified compared to simpler tool-augmented LVLM pipelines, how the framework scales to longer videos or streaming detection, and whether the social-risk taxonomy generalizes across cultural and regulatory contexts.
