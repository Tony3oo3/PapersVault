## Problem

Existing multi-person HMR methods emphasize pelvis-centered mesh accuracy while neglecting metric 3D localization and detection quality in the camera coordinate system. Prior DETR-based approaches (AiOS, SAT-HMR) rely on fixed camera assumptions (5000 mm focal length or 60° FOV) that produce large errors in absolute and relative 3D positions, and they use adult-only body models that mislocalize children. Additionally, human tracking in this setting has required video or motion-sequence supervision, limiting practical deployment.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (resized so the largest dimension is 768 px, with diverse aspect ratios supported via centered cropping during training) and produces, per detected person: a 3D mesh from the Anny parametric body model (163 joint orientations + 6 shape parameters), a 3D location in camera space, a detection score, and a 4096-dimensional tracking feature. It additionally regresses the vertical FOV from the encoder's cls token, so the same architecture can operate with ground-truth intrinsics or fully predicted camera parameters. For video tracking, frames are processed independently and detections are associated across frames using the distilled SAM2 features combined with a ridge-regression pelvis motion prior.

## Method

Multi-HMR 2 is a DETR-based framework with a ViT-Large encoder (initialized from DINOv3) and an 8-block cross-attention decoder over 100 learned queries, each producing a detection score, 2D pixel location, log-depth, Anny body parameters, and a tracking feature. The key architectural insight is a 2D-location-based Hungarian matching strategy during training: only matched queries are forwarded through the differentiable Anny body model, cutting per-iteration cost from 0.48 s to 0.35 s and memory from 27 GB to 7 GB, while also enabling training on datasets with only 2D keypoint annotations. Camera intrinsics (vertical FOV) are regressed from the encoder's cls token via an MLP, allowing metric-scale 3D localization without ground-truth cameras. For tracking, the model distills flattened 64x64x64 memory-encoder features from SAM2 (Hiera-L) into 4096-dim per-person vectors using ground-truth segmentations, so tracking is learned entirely from still images. Training uses an adaptive supervision scheme across heterogeneous data (synthetic Anny-One/BEDLAM, pseudo-GT real images from CameraHMR, and 2D-only MSCOCO/MPII/AIC/OpenImages), with a consistency regularization loss to prevent 2D supervision from degrading the 3D pose prior.

## Results

On 3DPW, Multi-HMR 2.a with a predicted camera achieves 40.5 mm PA-MPJPE, 68.2 mm MPJPE, and 170.1 mm TA-PVE, outperforming AiOS (45.0/68.8/774.3) and SAT-HMR (52.7/81.0/174.5) on all pelvis- and camera-space metrics. On EMDB, the predicted-camera variant reaches 273.6 mm Abs-PVE versus 11,962.1 mm for AiOS and 1,093.0 mm for SAT-HMR, a >4× improvement over the closest competitor. Multi-HMR 2.b (with 2D-annotated data) further improves 3DPW MPJPE to 65.2 mm and TA-PVE to 161.9 mm. For tracking on PoseTrack21, Multi-HMR 2.b achieves IDF1 75.2 and MOTA 65.5 using only still-image training, surpassing Humans4D (72.5/60.2) but trailing video-trained CoMotion (79.5/70.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-HMR 2 dominates on camera-space localization: with a predicted camera, its 3DPW TA-PVE of 170.1 mm beats AiOS (774.3 mm, a 4.5× gap) and SAT-HMR (174.5 mm), and its EMDB Abs-PVE of 273.6 mm is 4× better than SAT-HMR (1093.0) and 44× better than AiOS (11962.1), reflecting the severe errors introduced by fixed focal-length or fixed-FOV assumptions. Against Multi-HMR (which uses ground-truth intrinsics), Multi-HMR 2.a with a *predicted* camera still wins on 3DPW TA-PVE (170.1 vs. 187.2) and PVE (80.1 vs. 88.8), though Multi-HMR retains a slight edge on EMDB Abs-PVE (168.1 vs. 273.6). On interaction benchmarks (Hi4D, Harmony4D), Multi-HMR 2 achieves 100% F1-score on Hi4D and 97% recall on Harmony4D, matching or exceeding SAT-HMR while improving Pair-PA-MPJPE by ~20 mm. On CMU-Panoptic, Multi-HMR 2.a reduces mean N-MRPE to 26.9 mm versus 58.1 for Multi-HMR and 83.7 for SAT-HMR. For tracking, Multi-HMR 2.b (trained only on still images) outperforms Humans4D by 2.7 IDF1 and 5.3 MOTA, but remains 4.3 IDF1 below CoMotion, which leverages video-level supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

Multi-HMR 2 builds on the DETR detection paradigm (Carion et al., 2020) and the Anny parametric body model (Brégier et al., 2026), but departs from prior DETR-based HMR methods (AiOS, SAT-HMR) in two key ways: it predicts camera intrinsics rather than assuming a fixed focal length or FOV, and it performs Hungarian matching on 2D pixel locations alone instead of forwarding all queries through the body model. For tracking, it repurposes SAM2's memory encoder features as per-person appearance descriptors, a distillation strategy that avoids the video supervision required by prior tracking-by-detection methods like Humans4D and CoMotion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly predicting camera parameters, human meshes, and tracking features in a single forward pass, Multi-HMR 2 addresses a practical gap: most prior methods produce pelvis-centered meshes that are difficult to deploy in camera-space applications like human-robot interaction or social scene understanding. The ability to track without any video training data broadens the applicability of HMR to settings where annotated video sequences are unavailable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to extreme camera distortions (selfies, wide-angle), whether the 2D matching strategy degrades with very large numbers of overlapping humans, and whether the SAM2-distilled features remain discriminative under significant appearance changes (clothing swaps, lighting shifts).
