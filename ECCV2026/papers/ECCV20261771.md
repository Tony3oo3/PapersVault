## Problem

Lightweight object detectors struggle in few-shot personalized detection due to weak semantic priors and vulnerability to neural collapse, while large vision-language models offer strong object-level understanding but are too computationally demanding for real-time or on-device deployment. No prior method effectively bridges both the modality gap (vision-only vs. vision-language) and the structural mismatch (multi-scale detector features vs. object-centric VLM representations) to transfer multimodal semantics into a compact detector without requiring text or VLM inference at test time.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a single RGB image and produces bounding boxes with user-specific personal class labels, overriding coarse generic predictions with fine-grained personal labels. Personalization requires only 1–5 labeled samples per personal class, from which class prototypes are computed in the distilled embedding space. No textual prompts or VLM forward passes are needed at test time; the 7B-parameter teacher's embeddings are precomputed and cached offline, and the deployed student is a ~3.2M-parameter YOLOv8n with a small translation module adding only ~3 ms/image overhead.

## Method

MOCHA operates in three stages: pretraining a YOLOv8n student, distilling region-level multimodal features from a frozen LLaVa-1.5-7B teacher, and few-shot personalization via a prototype classifier on frozen student features. The teacher supervision is constructed by concatenating the CLIP visual class token (rescaled by the LLaVa language-model output norm) with the mean-pooled LLaVa decoder hidden states, then compressing via PCA to 512 dimensions and normalizing per-channel. A translation module (multi-head self-attention + MLP) maps the student's multi-scale spatially-pooled region features into this 512-d teacher space. Training uses a dual objective: a pointwise L1+L2 distillation loss for local feature alignment, and a relational embedding loss that minimizes cross-entropy between softmax-normalized pairwise distance matrices of student and teacher features, preserving the global geometric structure of the embedding space beyond individual point matching.

## Results

On the four personalized detection benchmarks (PerSeg, POD, CORe50, iCubWorld), MOCHA (AuXFT-initialized) achieves an average score of 58.7, yielding a +10.1 improvement over the YOLOv8n baseline (48.6) and +4.9 over the strongest prior method AuXFT (53.8). The best MOCHA variant outperforms all distillation baselines (KL, OFA, GLIP, SKDF, DRKD) by 6.5–9.7 points on average. Inference overhead is minimal: only ~3 ms/image (~10% relative increase) on YOLOv8n for PerSeg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOCHA (AuXFT) outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and shot regime. Against the closest competitor AuXFT (DINO teacher, visual-only supervision), MOCHA gains +4.9 on average, with the largest margins on POD (+10.3 in 1-shot) where fine-grained semantic alignment is most critical. Among distillation objectives re-implemented under the same multimodal region-level protocol, GLIP (52.2) and KL+MSE (52.1) are the strongest alternatives but still trail MOCHA by 6.5 and 6.6 points respectively, confirming that neither pointwise regression nor relation matching alone suffices. ViLD (50.9), despite its open-vocabulary design, underperforms even the non-distilled YOLOv8n baseline on POD, highlighting its weakness in highly personalized few-shot scenarios. DRKD (50.6) and OFA (50.1) show that relational and cross-architecture projection losses, while useful in their original settings, do not transfer well to the object-level multimodal distillation task. The consistent gap between MOCHA's visual-only (54.6) and text-only (54.0) variants versus the full multimodal version (58.7) further validates that combining visual and textual teacher signals is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOCHA is most directly positioned against AuXFT, the only prior work addressing cross-architecture feature transfer for few-shot personalized object detection, and departs from it in three ways: leveraging multimodal (visual + textual) supervision rather than purely visual cues, distilling compact region-level embeddings instead of dense intermediate activations, and introducing an explicit relational alignment loss. More broadly, it draws on cross-architecture knowledge distillation (OFA, DRKD), cross-modal adaptation, and prototypical few-shot classification, but uniquely bridges both a modality gap (vision-only student vs. vision-language teacher) and a structural mismatch (multi-scale CNN features vs. object-centric VLM representations) through a learned translation module and dual local/relational objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOCHA makes few-shot personalized object detection practical on resource-constrained devices (smartphones, robots) by transferring rich VLM semantics into a ~3M-parameter detector with only ~3 ms/image overhead, eliminating the need for VLM inference or text prompts at test time. The +10.1 average mAP gain over the YOLOv8n baseline and +4.9 over AuXFT across four benchmarks under strict 1- and 5-shot regimes is meaningful for real-world personalization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex multi-instance personalization, whether the frozen-teacher assumption limits adaptability to truly novel domains, and whether the PCA-based compression and channel normalization generalize beyond the tested datasets.
