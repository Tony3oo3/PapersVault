## Problem

Recovering a full linear Mueller matrix traditionally requires at least four different incident polarization states, making acquisition slow and impractical for real-time vision tasks. Physics-based pBRDF models offer a structured formulation but are limited by inaccessible geometric/material parameters and idealized assumptions that cause model mismatch on real objects. The paper tackles the under-constrained problem of estimating a physically meaningful 3×3 linear Mueller matrix from a single set of polarization images under one known incident state.

## Input / Output

The method consumes one set of four linear polarization images (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) at 256×256 resolution captured by a DoFP camera under a fixed, calibrated incident polarization state, together with a binary object mask. The incident Stokes vector is supplied externally by the acquisition setup rather than estimated. The primary output is a per-pixel 3×3 linear Mueller matrix (expanded to 27 channels across RGB), and the intermediate output is a set of pBRDF parameters: surface normal, diffuse albedo, scalar roughness, and incident light direction.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage physics-guided pipeline. Stage 1 (PIPNet) is a U-Net-like encoder-decoder with three parallel branches predicting normal, albedo, and light direction, plus a pooled scalar roughness; these parameters feed an analytical pBRDF model to produce a structured 3×3 Mueller initialization. Stage 2 (PMMNet) refines this initialization via residual learning, using two key dimensionality-reduction strategies: polarization-color decoupling (a wavelength-independent 3×3 polarization block scaled by RGB intensity weights) and redundant-channel separation (specular branch optimizes {m00,m11,m12,m21,m22}, diffuse branch optimizes {m00,m01,m02,m10,m20}). The PMMNet encoder-decoder has a specular branch with High Frequency Attention and Sparse Fusion for highlight localization, and a diffuse branch with Multi-Head Channel Attention for global feature extraction. Training uses a compound loss combining L1 matrix error with a forward Stokes consistency term evaluated over three additional incident states, encouraging the recovered matrix to behave as a valid polarization transfer operator.

## Results

On the real-world dataset, the proposed method achieves a Matrix PSNR of 40.42 and Rendering PSNR of 32.33 (SSIM 0.9895), substantially outperforming the best pBRDF fitting baseline (Hwang et al. at 28.27 Matrix PSNR). The AoLP angular error drops to 24.34° versus 65–74° for all baselines, and DoLP MAE reaches 0.0749. On synthetic data, the method scores 51.70 Matrix PSNR and 43.87 Rendering PSNR (SSIM 0.9950), with AoLP error of 34.51° compared to 48–58° for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against three adapted pBRDF fitting baselines: Baek et al. [5], Hwang et al. [19], and Ichikawa et al. [21], all supplied with ground-truth geometric parameters to make the comparison favorable to analytical fitting. The proposed method wins on every reported metric across both synthetic and real datasets. The gap is most pronounced on real data, where Matrix PSNR improves by 12–14 dB over the baselines (40.42 vs. 26.63–28.27) and AoLP error drops by roughly 40–50° (24.34° vs. 65–74°). On synthetic data, the advantage is more moderate but consistent: Matrix PSNR gains of 3–6 dB and AoLP improvements of 14–23°. Ichikawa et al. [21] does not report Matrix PSNR (marked '/'), but still trails on rendering PSNR, SSIM, and AoLP. The baselines can optimize a single low-order cue (e.g., DoLP) but fail to recover a calibrated linear Mueller operator or preserve AoLP, whereas the physics-guided residual refinement yields consistent gains across all matrix-level and Stokes-derived metrics simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established threads in polarimetric vision: analytical pBRDF-based Mueller matrix acquisition (Baek et al., Hwang et al., Ichikawa et al.), which provides physically grounded but parameter-heavy and slow fitting, and data-driven polarization imaging (Deschaintre et al., Ba et al., Li et al.), which enables single-shot estimation but lacks explicit physical structure. The key conceptual move is a two-stage physics-guided pipeline where a pBRDF model supplies a structured Mueller initialization that a residual CNN then refines, following the broader trend toward physics-informed learning in polarization tasks. The paper also contributes a calibrated real-world linear Mueller matrix dataset, addressing a data scarcity that has limited prior work to synthetic or rendered ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-shot formulation directly addresses the practical bottleneck that full Mueller matrix acquisition traditionally requires multiple incident polarization state changes, making it relevant for real-time polarimetric imaging. The held-out forward Stokes evaluation and Givens-Kostinski diagnostics add rigor beyond standard matrix-fitting metrics, testing whether the recovered operator generalizes to unseen incident states and satisfies physical admissibility. However, significant limitations remain: the method assumes a fixed point-light source, a known incident polarization state, and only recovers the 3×3 linear block (not the full 4×4 matrix), and the polarization-color decoupling assumption may fail for spectrally complex materials. Open questions include generalization to uncontrolled or extended illumination, circular-polarization recovery, and whether the physics-guided initialization strategy transfers to other under-constrained polarimetric inverse problems.
