## Problem

Despite the success of DETR-based models for object detection, no transformer-based architecture achieves real-time inference for multi-person pose estimation. Existing end-to-end pose estimators (PETR, ED-Pose, GroupPose) prioritize accuracy over speed, while fast single-stage methods (YOLO-Pose, RTMO) rely on NMS, producing inconsistent latency. This paper closes the gap by introducing the first family of real-time, end-to-end transformer models for multi-person 2D pose estimation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image resized to 640×640 and simultaneously predicts 17 2D keypoints plus a classification score for up to 60 human instances per frame. Each human instance is represented by K keypoint queries plus one aggregated instance query (N·(K+1) total decoder queries), and the decoder outputs per-keypoint coordinates via Fine-Grained Distribution Refinement heads that predict vertical and horizontal offsets rather than raw coordinates. Inference is fully NMS-free with a fixed, deterministic latency of 2.39–7.36 ms depending on model size.

## Method

DETRPose builds on the GroupPose decoder (within-instance and across-instance group self-attention) but integrates D-FINE's hierarchical HGNetv2 backbone, hybrid encoder, and Fine-Grained Distribution Refinement (FDR) regression heads, while removing projection layers to cut decoder latency. A novel denoising keypoint strategy generates positive and negative queries by shifting ground-truth keypoints along random unit vectors with magnitudes derived from a Keypoint Similarity (KS) metric, stabilizing the Hungarian matcher and accelerating convergence by 5–10×. A Keypoint Similarity VariFocal (KSVF) loss replaces IoU-based losses to promote high-quality query selection, and a lightweight Pose-LQE classification head fuses content features from the encoder's highest-resolution map with query outputs to sharpen classification scores. The backbone is initialized with D-FINE Object365 pretrained weights, and the full model is trained with AdamW (batch 16, lr 1e-4) for 48–96 epochs depending on variant size.

## Results

On the COCO val dataset, DETRPose-L achieves 72.7 AP with 32.8M parameters and 4.66 ms latency, surpassing RTMO-L (72.4 AP, 44.7M params, 4.89 ms) and matching YOLOv8-X (67.3 AP) and YOLO11-X (67.2 AP) with far fewer parameters. On the more challenging CrowdPose test set, DETRPose-X sets a new real-time MPPE record at 75.1 AP with only 232G FLOPs (13.1× fewer than ED-Pose-SwinL-5S's 3048G). On the out-of-distribution OCHuman test set, DETRPose-S (42.2 AP) surpasses all prior methods including RTMO-L (41.6 AP) while being 1.85× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RTMO (the prior SOTA for real-time MPPE), DETRPose-L achieves comparable AP (71.2 vs 71.6 on COCO test-dev) with 27% fewer parameters, 5% lower latency, and 4% higher AR; on CrowdPose, DETRPose-X (75.1 AP) outperforms RTMO-L (73.2 AP) by 1.9 points. DETRPose-S matches YOLOv8-X and YOLO11-X on COCO val (67.0 vs 67.3 and 67.2 AP) while using 81% and 80% fewer parameters respectively and running 52% faster. Against the large Swin-L transformer baselines (ED-Pose, GroupPose), DETRPose-X achieves within 1.5 AP of ED-Pose-SwinL-5S on CrowdPose (75.1 vs 76.6) with 3× fewer parameters and 13× fewer FLOPs, and on OCHuman DETRPose-S (42.2 AP) dramatically outperforms ED-Pose-SwinL (31.2 AP) and GroupPose-SwinL (36.6 AP), indicating superior generalization. On COCO test-dev, DETRPose-L (71.2 AP) slightly trails RTMO-L (71.6 AP) by 0.4 points but exceeds all end-to-end methods with non-Swin backbones. The paper also notes that PETR and QueryPose with ResNet-50 backbones (67.6 and 68.7 AP) are outperformed by DETRPose-L without transfer learning (68.7 AP) in just 48 epochs versus 100 and 40 epochs respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

DETRPose extends the DETR end-to-end detection framework to multi-person pose estimation by adapting the GroupPose decoder's group self-attention mechanism and incorporating D-FINE's backbone, encoder, and Fine-Grained Distribution Refinement layers for real-time speed. Its key conceptual departure from prior DETR-based pose models (PETR, QueryPose, ED-Pose, GroupPose) is the introduction of a denoising keypoint strategy that adapts the DN-DETR/DINO denoising paradigm from bounding boxes to individual keypoint queries, combined with a Keypoint Similarity VariFocal loss that replaces IoU-based matching. Unlike YOLO-based single-stage methods that rely on NMS for consistent latency, DETRPose achieves end-to-end inference through Hungarian matching, while unlike prior DETR pose models that prioritized accuracy at the cost of 10–100× higher latency, DETRPose targets real-time deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DETRPose fills a concrete gap in the pose estimation landscape: it is the first transformer-based model to achieve real-time, NMS-free multi-person pose estimation, offering practitioners a consistent-latency alternative to YOLO-based methods without sacrificing accuracy. The denoising keypoint technique and KSVF loss are modular contributions that could be adopted by other DETR-based pose estimators to accelerate convergence and improve query quality. A noted limitation is that group self-attention introduces multiple transpose operations that break tensor contiguity, resulting in higher measured latency than YOLO models at similar FLOPs; optimizing this kernel-level bottleneck is an open engineering question. The paper also leaves open whether the approach extends naturally to 3D pose estimation or video-based temporal pose tracking.
