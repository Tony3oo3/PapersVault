## Problem

Visual Autoregressive Models (VAR) generate all tokens within each scale in parallel, which constitutes a mean-field-style factorization that discards spatial dependencies among same-scale tokens. This causes locally incoherent samples—mismatched textures, structural discontinuities, and implausible combinations—that persist regardless of backbone capacity or training duration. The paper identifies this as a limitation of the decoding rule rather than model capacity, and asks whether a minimal correction can restore the joint within-scale distribution.

## Input / Output

The Logit Refiner is a plug-in module that operates on top of a frozen pretrained VAR backbone, consuming its already-computed hidden states for the current scale plus previously sampled tokens within that scale. For class-conditional ImageNet-256², the input is a class label; for text-to-image (Infinity-2B at 1024²), it is a text prompt. The output is a generated image, with the refiner adding only ~10% parameters and <5% of the base model's training compute while introducing modest sequential overhead via KV-cached autoregressive sampling within each scale.

## Method

The Logit Refiner replaces VAR's independent per-token sampling within each scale with an autoregressive factorization: a lightweight causal transformer (d_r=2 blocks, matched width) sequentially samples tokens conditioned on both the frozen backbone's bidirectional hidden states and previously sampled tokens in raster-scan order. Because the backbone already provides spatially contextualized features via bidirectional attention, the refiner only needs to model residual intra-scale dependencies, explaining why an extremely small model suffices. Training uses teacher forcing with the backbone frozen, and an identity initialization scheme (copying the output head, zeroing context projections) ensures the refiner starts as a functional equivalent of the base model and learns only the correction. Controlled ablations confirm that joint autoregressive sampling—not additional capacity or training—is the critical ingredient, as a parallel refiner with bidirectional attention and independent sampling fails to match the autoregressive variant.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet-256², the Logit Refiner consistently improves FID across all VAR backbone scales: from 3.30→2.81 (VAR-d16, 310M), 2.57→2.17 (VAR-d20, 600M), 2.09→1.83 (VAR-d24, 1.0B), and 1.92→1.76 (VAR-d30, 2.0B), with only ~10% additional parameters. The headline result is that VAR-d24 + Refiner (1.1B total params) achieves FID 1.83, surpassing the twice-as-large VAR-d30 (2.0B, FID 1.92) and even M-VAR-d32 (3.0B, FID 1.78). Recall improves by 0.04–0.06 across all scales, indicating the gains come from recovering sample diversity rather than sacrificing it. On text-to-image (Infinity-2B, 1024²), the refiner improves HPSv3 scores from 9.79 to 9.91.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Logit Refiner applied to VAR achieves the best FID within every backbone scale bracket in Table 3, outperforming all competing scale-wise autoregressive methods. At the 310M–465M scale, VAR-d16+Refiner (FID 2.81) beats MVAR-d16 (3.09), M-VAR-d16 (3.07), and HMAR-d16 (3.01) by 0.20–0.49 FID. At the 600M–900M scale, VAR-d20+Refiner (FID 2.17) surpasses HART-d20 (2.39), M-VAR-d20 (2.41), HMAR-d20 (2.50), and MVAR-d20 (2.87). The most striking comparison is at the 1.0B–1.5B scale: VAR-d24+Refiner (1.1B, FID 1.83) outperforms M-VAR-d24 (1.5B, FID 1.93), HMAR (1.3B, FID 2.10), and HART-d24 (1.0B, FID 2.00), and even exceeds the 2.0B VAR-d30 (FID 1.92) and 3.0B M-VAR-d32 (FID 1.78). At the 2.0B+ scale, VAR-d30+Refiner (FID 1.76) edges out HART-d30 (1.77) and clearly beats FastVAR-d30 (2.30), VAR-CoDe-d30 (1.94), and HMAR 2.4B (1.95). The refiner also consistently improves Recall over its VAR baseline at every scale (e.g., 0.57→0.63 at d24), while Inception Score drops slightly due to lower CFG scales being FID-optimal for the refiner.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VAR's next-scale prediction paradigm, which generates all tokens within each scale in parallel. Rather than modifying the backbone architecture, token ordering, or prediction target (as M-VAR, MVAR, HART, HMAR, and Infinity do), it identifies the parallel within-scale decoding itself as a mean-field-style approximation and corrects it with a lightweight autoregressive refiner operating on frozen backbone features. The approach is explicitly orthogonal to existing VAR extensions: it can be combined with any of them, and the paper positions the decoding rule—not model capacity—as the fundamental bottleneck that prior work has overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~10% parameter add-on trained in hours on a frozen backbone enables a 1.1B model to surpass a 2B model, offering a parameter-efficient path to better generation quality for the entire VAR family. The mean-field framing provides a clear diagnostic lens for spatial incoherence in scale-wise autoregressive models, and the plug-in design means any existing pretrained VAR checkpoint can benefit without retraining. Open questions include whether the sequential within-scale overhead can be further reduced (e.g., via non-autoregressive joint sampling), whether the principle generalizes to other parallel generative architectures beyond VAR, and how the refiner interacts with the many orthogonal VAR improvements that have appeared since.
