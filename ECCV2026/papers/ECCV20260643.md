## Problem

Direct text-to-4D generation remains challenging because text lacks explicit geometric and motion priors, making it difficult to synthesize plausible dynamics while maintaining appearance and geometric consistency. Existing approaches either rely on slow per-object SDS optimization, two-stage video-based pipelines that accumulate errors and impose restrictive video constraints, or trajectory generation that forgoes strong 3D foundation model priors, limiting generalization to out-of-distribution meshes.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts a text prompt alone (4D generation from scratch) or a text prompt paired with a static 3D mesh (3D animation), and produces a multi-frame 3D Gaussian Splatting sequence decoded from structured latents. In the animation pathway, a binary mask specifies which temporal frames are preserved from the input mesh versus generated, and the input mesh is rendered into four multi-view images (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) to extract CLIP global context. A separate 4D interpolation model extends T generated frames to 2T−1 frames for higher temporal resolution.

## Method

DiTex4D is built on TRELLIS's structured latent (SLat) diffusion with flow matching, inflating the 3D spatial self-attention into full spatiotemporal attention via a mixed-4D RoPE that combines pre-trained 3D absolute positional embeddings with 4D rotary encodings over (x, y, z, t) coordinates. A tailored correlated noise injection strategy shares noise across frames using spatial-coordinate keys for sparse SLat voxels, reducing inter-frame flickering while preserving per-frame variation. For 3D animation, the input mesh is replicated T times and encoded into conditional latents, concatenated with a binary mask and noisy latents along the channel axis, while multi-view CLIP features provide global appearance context via decoupled cross-attention. The two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline (sparse structure flow → structured latent flow) decouples geometry and texture temporal modeling, and a lightweight 4D interpolation model fine-tuned with the same masking mechanism upsamples temporal resolution.

## Results

On the 4D generation from scratch task, DiTex4D achieves the best CLIP score (25.51), Motion Smoothness (0.995), and Aesthetic Quality (0.559) among all baselines, though L4GM leads on Dynamic Degree (0.578 vs. 0.281). For 3D animation from static mesh, DiTex4D attains the highest Aesthetic Quality (0.562) and ties for best Motion Smoothness (0.997), while AnimateAnyMesh edges ahead on I2V (0.948 vs. 0.946) and L4GM dominates Dynamic Degree (0.618 vs. 0.250). The evaluation uses 32 test objects across diverse categories with 4 rendered views, reporting VBench metrics (I2V, Motion Smoothness, Aesthetic Quality, Dynamic Degree) and CLIP Score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTex4D outperforms all six baselines on CLIP Score (25.51 vs. 25.42 for AnimateAnyMesh, the closest competitor) and Aesthetic Quality (0.559 vs. 0.539 for GVFDiffusion) in the 4D generation from scratch setting. However, L4GM substantially leads on Dynamic Degree (0.578 vs. 0.281), suggesting DiTex4D generates less extreme motion amplitudes despite better overall quality. In the 3D animation task, DiTex4D achieves the best Aesthetic Quality (0.562) and ties AnimateAnyMesh on Motion Smoothness (0.997), but AnimateAnyMesh slightly edges ahead on I2V (0.948 vs. 0.946) and L4GM again dominates Dynamic Degree (0.618 vs. 0.250). The SDS-based methods (Dream-in-4D, Animate3D) are consistently outperformed across all metrics, confirming the advantage of feed-forward structured latent diffusion over per-object optimization. Overall, DiTex4D wins on the majority of quality and consistency metrics while trading off some motion amplitude compared to L4GM.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiTex4D builds directly on TRELLIS's structured latent (SLat) representation and flow-matching framework, extending the 3D attention mechanism into the spatiotemporal domain by drawing on text-to-video techniques (attention inflation, RoPE, correlated noise) pioneered in models like Wan and PYoCo. It departs from the dominant two-stage text-to-video-to-4D pipeline (e.g., Animate3D, L4GM) by eliminating the video intermediate entirely, and from trajectory-based animation methods (AnimateAnyMesh) by grounding generation in a pre-trained 3D foundation model rather than learning motion from scratch. The key architectural departure from video-to-4D methods like SS4D is the use of mixed-4D RoPE (retaining 3D absolute positional embeddings alongside 4D relative RoPE) and a tailored correlated noise scheme for sparse voxel structures, both motivated by the absence of per-frame visual priors in the text-driven setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By anchoring 4D generation in a strong 3D prior, DiTex4D reduces the data-hunger that plagues trajectory-based and SDS-based approaches, and the unified masked-diffusion design for both from-scratch generation and mesh animation offers a practical, single-model solution for content creators. However, the framework remains limited to single-object generation on a 20K-object dataset, and the modest metric gains over AnimateAnyMesh (e.g., CLIP 25.51 vs. 25.42) suggest that the 3D-prior advantage, while real, has not yet translated into a dramatic quality leap. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes or longer temporal sequences, and whether the structured-latent advantage over VecSet-based representations holds as 4D datasets grow.
