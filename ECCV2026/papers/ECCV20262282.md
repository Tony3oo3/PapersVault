## Problem

CLIP-based emotion recognition methods rely on generic class prompts or LLM-generated text descriptions that lack the granularity to model subtle, fine-grained facial expressions arising from localized muscle activations. Moreover, existing test-time adaptation methods target static image classification and do not account for the temporal evolution of low-intensity expressions or the subject-specific variability in how individuals manifest the same emotion through distinct Action Unit combinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262282/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips as sequences of RGB frames, each encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 visual encoder into frame-level embeddings. A sliding window of configurable length (default 16 frames) is applied over the temporal sequence, and the output is a categorical emotion label (from subtle expression classes) accompanied by a 46-dimensional AU similarity vector representing the activation strength of each Action Unit. During CLIP-AUTT inference, only the minimum-entropy window is used for adaptation, and adapted AU prompt embeddings are reset after each subject video to maintain stable personalization.

## Method

CLIP-AU replaces generic class prompts with 46 natural-language AU descriptions (e.g., 'eyebrow raised', 'lip corner pulled') fed through a frozen CLIP text encoder and a lightweight two-layer AU adapter that specializes embeddings for expression semantics. A temporal module (1D CNN + GLU + average pooling) aggregates frame embeddings into a video-level representation, and cosine similarity between this representation and each AU embedding yields an activation vector passed to an MLP classifier trained with supervised cross-entropy loss—requiring no AU ground-truth annotations. CLIP-AUTT extends this with test-time personalization: an entropy-guided sliding-window strategy computes per-window AU similarity vectors, passes them through the classifier, and selects the minimum-Shannon-entropy window as the most temporally coherent expressive segment. On this selected window, AU prompt embeddings are optimized via entropy minimization while all other modules remain frozen, adapting the AU semantics to each subject's distinctive activation pattern without any target labels.

## Results

On BioVid, CLIP-AU achieves 78.0% WAR and 74.8% F1 under fine-tuning, and CLIP-AUTT further improves to 81.5% WAR and 78.0% F1 under test-time adaptation. On StressID, CLIP-AUTT reaches 80.8% WAR and 77.9% F1, a substantial margin over the best TTA baseline (T3AL: 75.9% WAR, 59.4% F1). On BAH, CLIP-AU attains the best fine-tuning result (68.3% WAR, 40.3% F1) and CLIP-AUTT improves to 69.8% WAR and 41.1% F1, outperforming all TTA methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the fine-tuning setting, CLIP-AU outperforms all CLIP-based FER baselines on BioVid (78.0% WAR vs. 70.9% for X-CLIP, the best baseline) and BAH (68.3% WAR vs. 63.0% for X-CLIP). On StressID, EmoCLIP† edges CLIP-AU slightly in WAR (67.0 vs. 66.5), but CLIP-AU achieves a decisively higher F1 (58.5 vs. 44.5), indicating far more balanced per-class predictions under class imbalance. In the TTA setting, CLIP-AUTT surpasses all six TTA baselines on every dataset and metric; the closest competitor is T3AL (the only video-level TTA method), which trails by 5.4% WAR and 5.1% F1 on BioVid, 4.9% WAR and 18.5% F1 on StressID, and 1.9% WAR and 0.4% F1 on BAH. The largest relative gains appear on StressID, where CLIP-AUTT's F1 of 77.9% nearly doubles the F1 of image-level TTA methods (e.g., TDA at 49.9%), highlighting the benefit of temporal AU prompt tuning for subtle, temporally evolving expressions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from CLIP-based FER methods (FineCLIPER, EmoCLIP, Exp-CLIP) that rely on generic class prompts or LLM-generated text descriptions by instead using structured Action Unit (AU) descriptions as the textual prompts, grounding the vision-language alignment at the muscle-activation level. It also extends the line of CLIP test-time adaptation work (TPT, PromptAlign, TDA, DPE, ReTA), which operates on static images, to a video-based setting with entropy-guided temporal window selection for subtle, temporally evolving expressions. On the AU side, prior work uses AUs as auxiliary supervision signals or intermediate reasoning cues (Li et al., Belharbi et al., Emotion-LLaMA), whereas here AUs serve directly as the prompt vocabulary that is optimized at test time, eliminating the need for AU ground-truth annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in healthcare-oriented ER (pain, stress, ambivalence) where expressions are low-intensity, subject-specific, and temporally evolving, and where per-subject labeled data is unavailable or privacy-restricted. The AU-prompting formulation is interpretable and lightweight, and the entropy-guided window selection offers a principled way to focus adaptation on the most informative temporal segment. However, evidence is limited to three small-scale datasets (10 target subjects each), BAH performance remains modest (~70 WAR), and the per-subject video-level adaptation step may not scale to streaming or multi-subject deployment. Open questions include whether AU prompting generalizes to macro-emotion recognition or other fine-grained visual tasks, and whether the temporal window strategy transfers to longer or multi-expression videos.
