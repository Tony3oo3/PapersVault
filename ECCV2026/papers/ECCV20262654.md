## Problem

Reconstructing high-fidelity 4D scenes from uncalibrated casual monocular videos remains challenging due to ambiguity between camera and object motion, sparse view coverage, and prohibitively slow Gaussian optimization (hours for short clips). Existing fast methods like Instant4D initialize 4D Gaussians solely from per-frame depth maps, ignoring cross-frame correspondences of dynamic points, which causes frame-wise overfitting and degrades global reconstruction consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262654/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconstrained monocular video (no camera calibration, no multi-view setup) as input and produces a 4D Gaussian scene representation that renders novel views in real-time from arbitrary SE(3) camera poses at specified timestamps. Camera extrinsics, intrinsics, temporally consistent depth maps, and motion probability maps are all estimated jointly by MegaSaM during preprocessing. For a typical 400-frame video, the full Gaussian optimization completes in approximately 40 seconds on a single A6000 GPU with ~1 GB peak memory.

## Method

Minute4D introduces two key modules. First, a Segmentation-Tracking Enhancement module refines MegaSaM's coarse motion probability masks using SAM2 instance segmentation (with periodic re-prompting every B frames to capture newly appearing objects), then uses TAPIP3D to estimate 3D trajectories of dynamic points, filling occluded regions across frames to produce a more complete initial point cloud for Gaussian placement. Second, a Loss-Guided Density Control strategy replaces vanilla 4DGS's single-frame gradient-based densification with a multi-view photometric-error criterion: at each step, K frames are sampled, per-pixel L1 error maps are computed, min-max normalized, and thresholded to retain only high-error regions; each Gaussian's densification and pruning scores are then computed by projecting it onto these refined error maps and weighting by the Gaussian's temporal marginal probability density, so that frames near the Gaussian's temporal mean contribute more strongly. This adaptively densifies under-reconstructed regions while pruning redundant Gaussians, reducing optimization time by at least 5× over prior methods.

## Results

On the Dycheck iPhone dataset, Minute4D achieves an average PSNR of 25.77 dB, surpassing the strongest pose-free baseline Instant4D(Full) (24.52 dB) by 1.25 dB and the best GT-pose method Deform3D (22.63 dB) by 3.14 dB, while training in only 0.008 h (~30 s) with 1.0 GB peak GPU memory. On the NVIDIA Dynamic dataset, Minute4D reaches 24.73 dB PSNR with 1218 FPS rendering speed and 0.006 h training time, outperforming Instant4D (23.99 dB, 822 FPS, 0.02 h) and Casual-FVS (24.57 dB, 48 FPS, 0.25 h). The method achieves at least a 5× speed-up over prior 4D Gaussian methods while simultaneously improving reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Minute4D outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary Dycheck iPhone benchmark in PSNR, with the largest margins over NeRF-based methods (RoDynRF: +8.97 dB, D-NeRF: +4.27 dB) and a consistent 1.25 dB gain over the closest competitor Instant4D(Full). Among pose-free 4D Gaussian methods, Minute4D also dominates on efficiency: it trains 15× faster than Instant4D(Full) (0.008 h vs. 0.12 h) and uses 8× less GPU memory (1.0 GB vs. 8 GB). On the NVIDIA Dynamic dataset, Minute4D (24.73 dB) beats Instant4D (23.99 dB) by 0.74 dB and Casual-FVS (24.57 dB) by 0.16 dB, while rendering at 1218 FPS versus 822 FPS for Instant4D and 48 FPS for Casual-FVS. The only baseline that exceeds Minute4D in PSNR is DynamicNeRF (26.10 dB on NVIDIA Dynamic), but that method requires COLMAP calibration and takes 74 h to train, making it impractical for casual video. Against the original 4DGS representation [48] adapted to the pose-free setting, Minute4D gains 6.39 dB in PSNR and trains 27× faster (0.006 h vs. 0.16 h), demonstrating that the Segmentation-Tracking Enhancement and Loss-Guided Density Control modules collectively address the frame-wise overfitting and Gaussian redundancy issues inherent in the vanilla pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely in the 4D Gaussian Splatting lineage for dynamic novel view synthesis from uncalibrated monocular video, building most directly on Instant4D (which also pairs a visual SLAM system with 4DGS) and the broader 4DGS representation. It departs from prior pose-free methods (RoDyGS, MoSca, Instant4D) in two key ways: (1) a Segmentation-Tracking Enhancement module that chains MegaSaM motion maps → SAM2 instance segmentation → TAPIP3D 3D point tracking to produce a more complete initial point cloud, and (2) a Loss-Guided Density Control strategy that replaces single-frame gradient-based densification/pruning with multi-view photometric-error-weighted scores, a design tailored to the temporal marginal of 4D Gaussians. The method is thus an efficiency-and-fidelity refinement of the Instant4D recipe rather than a new representation or rendering paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing 4DGS optimization from minutes to ~40 seconds for a 400-frame video while simultaneously improving PSNR by ~1.25 dB over the prior best pose-free method makes high-fidelity dynamic reconstruction practically deployable in robotics, AR/VR, and autonomous-driving pipelines where per-scene training time is a bottleneck. The Loss-Guided Density Control strategy is a reusable component that any 4DGS pipeline could adopt to curb Gaussian redundancy. Open questions include scalability to much longer sequences (hundreds of frames beyond the tested 400), the cumulative error introduced by chaining three separate foundation models (MegaSaM, SAM2, TAPIP3D), and whether the multi-view error scoring generalizes to scenes with highly non-rigid or topologically changing dynamics beyond the tested benchmarks.
