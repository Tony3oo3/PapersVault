## Problem

Controllable traffic simulation requires adding new control modalities (sketch, latent, text) to pretrained generative traffic models, but existing approaches either demand expensive full retraining with large paired datasets for each modality or introduce significant inference-time computational overhead through gradient-based guidance. No prior method enables data-efficient, multi-modal controllability while preserving the pretrained model's generative prior under closed-loop simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260573/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts three control modalities—sparse trajectory waypoints in the agent's local frame, 256-dimensional latent codes from a pretrained BehaviorVAE, or natural language prompts—along with the scene context. Only designated target agents receive their corresponding conditioning embedding z_i (mapped to a shared 256-dim space); non-targeted agents are assigned zero vectors. The output is joint multi-agent future trajectories over a prediction horizon, generated in closed-loop, with the additional capability to produce counterfactual rollouts and long-tail scenarios via context-aware retrieval of compatible agents from the dataset.

## Method

The core mechanism is identity-initialized FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) layers injected at every transformer decoder layer of a frozen pretrained backbone, applying an affine transformation γ(z_i)⊙h^(l)+β^(l)(z_i) where γ and β are lightweight MLPs predicting per-layer scale and shift from the control embedding. Identity initialization (γ≈1, β≈0) ensures the adapted model initially reproduces the base model exactly, preventing compounding errors in closed-loop multi-agent simulation. A separate BehaviorVAE (conditional VAE with KL annealing) learns context-aware per-agent behavior latents by jointly encoding all agents' trajectories with scene geometry in a single forward pass, enabling data-efficient latent control without manual annotations. Closed-loop fine-tuning updates only the control encoders and FiLM parameters: the diffusion backbone uses a receding-horizon plan-select-execute scheme (generate 8 candidates, select the one closest to GT), while the autoregressive backbone optimizes cross-entropy under closed-loop unrolling with sampling biased toward control-aligned trajectories. A context-aware retrieval pipeline then transfers learned behaviors into compatible scenes for counterfactual and long-tail generation, reducing collisions by 50% compared to random context transfer.

## Results

Using only 1% of paired control data, the FiLM adapters reduce mADE by up to 83% (SMART + Sketch: 1.5044 → 0.2561) and 65% (VBD + Sketch: 2.7223 → 0.9645) while improving the WOSAC Meta score by 0.02–0.03 absolute points over the unconditional backbones. In the same-backbone ProSim comparison, FiLM adaptation with 1% data achieves sketch controllability (57.23% mADE gain) nearly matching the original ProSim trained on 100% data (58.96%), while text controllability more than doubles (30.92% vs. 13.03% mADE gain). Sample efficiency analysis shows Latent control reaches near-saturation performance at just 0.01% data (~50 scenarios), and all modalities saturate around 10% data, matching full-data LoRA fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VBD diffusion backbone (Huang et al., 2024), the FiLM adapters reduce mADE from 2.7223 to 0.9645 (Sketch) and 0.9221 (Latent) while improving Meta score from 0.7186 to 0.7432/0.7440, demonstrating that lightweight control injection preserves and even enhances the diffusion model's generative prior. Against the SMART autoregressive backbone (Wu et al., 2024), gains are even larger: mADE drops from 1.5044 to 0.2561 (Sketch, 83% reduction) and Meta improves from 0.7728 to 0.7984. In the critical same-backbone comparison against ProSim (Tan et al., 2024), FiLM adaptation with only 1% paired data achieves sketch mADE gain of 57.23% versus ProSim's 58.96% at 100% data (a 1.7-point gap), while text mADE gain jumps from 13.03% to 30.92%—more than 2× improvement with 100× less supervision. The paper thus demonstrates that the data efficiency stems from the adaptation mechanism itself rather than backbone strength, and that the method matches or surpasses fully fine-tuned baselines trained on substantially larger datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the control-adaptation paradigm from visual generation (ControlNet's zero-initialized branches, LoRA's low-rank updates) into traffic simulation, where it had been largely unexplored. It builds directly on LangTraj [3] for the plan-select-execute diffusion training loop and DistilBERT+LoRA language encoding, and on closed-loop supervised fine-tuning [24] for the autoregressive unrolling procedure. Conceptually, it departs from prior controllable traffic work (inference-time guidance, full retraining, or retrieval-only control) by freezing the backbone entirely and injecting conditioning through identity-initialized FiLM layers, while extending retrieval-based approaches like RealGen [6] with learned behavior latents (BehaviorVAE) and multi-modal interfaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for AV testing: achieving 83% mADE reduction with 1% paired data (and near-full controllability with 0.01% for latent codes) dramatically lowers the annotation cost for building controllable simulators, and the model-agnostic design means the same adapter recipe applies to both diffusion and autoregressive backbones. The context-aware transfer pipeline opens a path toward systematic counterfactual and long-tail scenario generation without manual authoring. Key open questions remain: the framework currently controls a single agent (coordinated multi-agent control is future work), infeasible or context-conflicting instructions can degrade safety, and the long-tail collision rate (15.7%) still reflects a fundamental tension between enforcing rare behaviors and preserving safe interactions with surrounding traffic.
