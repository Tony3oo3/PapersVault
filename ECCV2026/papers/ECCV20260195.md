## Problem

Dynamic-resolution sampling in Diffusion Transformers reduces early-stage cost by denoising at low resolution, but uniformly upsampling all latent tokens at resolution transitions incurs redundant high-resolution computation and may degrade fine-detail consistency. Existing partial upsampling strategies rely on local latent structure cues (e.g., edge detection) or single-step statistics, making it difficult to jointly capture token-text semantic relevance and token-wise representation dynamics across diffusion timesteps, leading to unreliable prioritization of truly critical tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260195/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces an RGB image; for instruction-based editing, it takes a reference image plus a text instruction and produces an edited image. Internally, the image is VAE-encoded into a sequence of M non-overlapping patch tokens in latent space, and the text is encoded into L tokens; the pipeline operates at a reduced resolution (factor f=2 downsampling, reducing token count to M/f²) during early denoising before progressively restoring full resolution. The flow-matching ODE is integrated via Euler steps, and coordinate-bound noise re-injection is applied at each resolution transition to preserve cross-stage consistency.

## Method

AViTS is a training-free, three-stage dynamic-resolution framework that fuses two complementary importance signals to guide selective token upsampling. Spatial importance is computed by aggregating latent-to-text cross-attention weights over heads, blocks, and collection steps (extracted via forward hooks on joint-attention blocks for DiTs or directly from MLLM attention matrices), while temporal importance is computed as the mean per-channel variance of each token's latent features across N_T collected denoising snapshots. The two normalized scores are combined as S_i = α·Â_i + (1−α)·V̂_i, and the top-K tokens by this joint score are upsampled in Stage 2 while the remainder stay at low resolution until Stage 3 full-resolution refinement. This spatiotemporal importance-aware prioritization is orthogonal to and composable with distillation, quantization, and feature caching, achieving up to 6.34× acceleration on FLUX and 14.76× when combined with step distillation.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (DrawBench), AViTS achieves 3.14× latency speedup (8.21 s) with 2.65× FLOPs reduction at NFE=30, while simultaneously improving ImageReward to 1.0104 (+3.96%) and CLIP Score to 32.476 (+0.47%) over the full-resolution baseline. Under more aggressive acceleration (NFE=18), AViTS reaches 5.45× speedup (4.73 s) and 4.80× FLOPs reduction with ImageReward 0.9959 and CLIP Score 32.361. On image editing (GEdit), AViTS delivers up to 6.92× speedup and 8.85× FLOPs reduction on FLUX.1-Kontext-dev, and 6.95× speedup with 8.65× FLOPs reduction on Qwen-Image-Edit, while maintaining or exceeding baseline editing quality (OS 6.57 and 7.48 respectively). Combined with step distillation, AViTS reaches 14.76× overall acceleration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AViTS outperforms all spatial-resolution baselines (RALU, Fresco, Bottleneck Sampling) on both speed and quality at matched NFE: at NFE=30 on FLUX.1-dev, AViTS achieves 8.21 s vs. RALU's 11.02 s and Fresco's 9.17 s, while also scoring higher on ImageReward (1.0104 vs. 0.9626 and 0.9801) and CLIP Score (32.476 vs. 32.118 and 32.125). Against temporal/caching baselines, AViTS at NFE=18 (4.73 s, 5.45×) beats DuCa (8.19 s, 3.15×) and TaylorSeer (9.21 s, 2.80×) on latency while matching or exceeding their quality; TeaCache at l=0.8 is faster (6.63 s) but degrades ImageReward to 0.8805 (−9.4% vs. baseline) versus AViTS's 0.9959 (+2.47%). On image editing (GEdit), AViTS at NFE=30 on FLUX.1-Kontext-dev achieves the best OS (6.71) and SC (7.08) among all methods at comparable speed, surpassing RALU (OS 6.70 at 2.15×) and Fresco (OS 6.65 at 2.45×) while running faster (3.21×). On Qwen-Image-Edit, AViTS at NFE=18 (4.49×) maintains OS 7.58/7.55 (CN/EN), outperforming FORA (OS 7.25/7.28 at 4.51×) and TaylorSeer (OS 7.25/7.30 at 4.33×). At extreme acceleration (>6×), AViTS preserves quality (OS 6.57 on Kontext, 7.48 on Qwen-EN) where FreqCa and TaylorSeer show clear degradation (OS 7.21 and 6.31 respectively). The ablation (Table 5) confirms that the spatiotemporal fusion (attention + step-variance) is necessary: either signal alone is slightly weaker than the combined score, and low-level heuristics (edge detection, Fresco) underperform by 1–2 points on CLIP Score.

## Relation to Prior Work

AViTS extends the dynamic-resolution sampling paradigm introduced by RALU, which first proposed training-free partial upsampling of latent tokens during resolution transitions in DiTs. Where RALU relies on edge detection and Fresco on inter-channel variance as token-selection heuristics, AViTS departs by formulating token importance as a joint spatiotemporal estimation problem: spatial importance from latent-text cross-attention captures semantic alignment with the prompt, while temporal importance from cross-step feature variance captures which tokens are still actively evolving. This positions AViTS as a principled upgrade over low-level structural cues, and it is orthogonal to the broader temporal-acceleration literature (feature caching, step distillation, quantization) with which it composes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: training-free 6–9× FLOPs reductions on generation and editing models, and up to 14.76× when stacked with distillation, making high-fidelity DiT inference viable on resource-constrained hardware. The finding that selective upsampling can *improve* quality over uniform full-resolution processing (e.g., ImageReward 1.0104 vs. 0.9719 on FLUX.1-dev) suggests that redundant high-resolution computation on semantically inert tokens can be mildly harmful, an observation that may inform future resolution-scheduling designs. Open questions include whether the linear fusion of attention and variance scores (Eq. 8) is optimal, whether the importance signals remain stable for video or 3D generation where temporal dynamics are richer, and how the method behaves under very short (4–6 step) distilled schedules where the feature-collection window shrinks.
