## Problem

Chain-of-thought reasoning has become standard in vision-language model pipelines and improves task accuracy, yet its effect on the reliability of uncertainty quantification (UQ) estimates remained unexplored, particularly in multimodal settings where reasoning must reconcile visual perceptual uncertainty with linguistic inference. This paper shows that reasoning systematically degrades the ranking quality of uncertainty estimates that rely on answer-token likelihoods, even as it improves accuracy, creating a dangerous overconfidence effect in high-stakes deployment.

## Input / Output

The evaluated VLMs (Gemma3-4B-IT, InternVL3-9B, Qwen3-VL 8B/32B) take RGB images, optionally paired with a text question, and produce a text answer. Uncertainty scores are extracted from the model's token-level probability distributions: single-generation methods (MSP, PPL, MTE, SRC) read off one forward pass, while multi-generation methods (MC-SE, MC-NSE, Consistency) require K=10 stochastic samples. In the selective generation evaluation, each uncertainty score is thresholded to decide whether the model answers or abstains, and ranking quality is measured via PRR, Spearman correlation, and AUGRC.

## Method

The paper is an empirical study that evaluates seven UQ methods across four datasets (OK-VQA, MathVista, MMMU-Pro-Vision, Oxford-IIIT Pet) and five model configurations (with/without CoT prompting, and a reasoning-native Thinking variant), identifying implicit answer conditioning as the mechanism behind UQ degradation. As a reasoning trace unfolds, it progressively commits to a specific conclusion, concentrating probability mass on the predicted answer tokens before they are generated; this inflates answer-token likelihoods regardless of correctness, making them reflect internal consistency with the trace rather than genuine uncertainty about the visual signal. Evidence includes: (1) ATL-based estimates show correctness-agnostic confidence inflation under CoT, (2) reasoning length negatively correlates with confidence (ruling out verbosity), (3) answer-mention frequency in the trace positively correlates with ATL confidence but not with Consistency or SRC, and (4) a controlled masking intervention that replaces answer mentions in the reasoning trace with [MASK] substantially restores ATL ranking quality (e.g., MSP PRR triples from 0.141 to 0.475 on OK-VQA) without changing accuracy. Agreement-based consistency, which measures majority-vote agreement across sampled answers rather than token likelihoods, remains robust and often improves under reasoning, making it the recommended UQ method for reasoning-enabled VLMs.

## Results

On Qwen3-VL-8B-IT, ATL-based UQ estimates (MSP, PPL, MTE, MC-SE, MC-NSE) show substantial ranking degradation under CoT prompting: e.g., MSP PRR drops from 0.710 to 0.231 on Oxford-IIIT Pet and from 0.432 to 0.219 on MathVista. In contrast, agreement-based Consistency remains robust and often improves, with PRR rising from 0.345 to 0.684 on MathVista and from 0.107 to 0.434 on MMMU-Pro-Vision. The same pattern is amplified in the reasoning-native Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking model, where MTE PRR collapses from 0.735 to 0.086 on Oxford-IIIT Pet, while Consistency PRR reaches 0.767 on MathVista. Masking explicit answer mentions from CoT traces partially restores ATL ranking quality (MSP PRR: 0.141 → 0.475 on OK-VQA), confirming implicit answer conditioning as the mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three families of UQ methods under reasoning: ATL-based estimates (including MC-SE/MC-NSE following Kuhn et al.'s Semantic Entropy [14]), Self-Reported Confidence (SRC, following Srinivasan et al. [24]), and agreement-based Consistency (following Wang et al.'s Self-Consistency [27]). Under CoT prompting, all ATL-based methods—including MC-SE and MC-NSE—suffer severe ranking degradation (PRR drops of 0.16–0.53 across datasets), confirming that token-likelihood-based uncertainty is unreliable when reasoning is active. Consistency, in contrast, is the only method that consistently improves under reasoning, with PRR gains of +0.339 on MathVista and +0.327 on MMMU-Pro-Vision, making it the recommended UQ strategy for reasoning-enabled VLMs. SRC shows mixed behaviour: it avoids the systematic confidence inflation that plagues ATL methods (near-zero correlation with answer frequency in the reasoning trace) but does not consistently outperform Consistency, achieving comparable PRR only on Oxford-IIIT Pet under CoT. The degradation is further amplified in the reasoning-native Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking model, where MC-SE PRR becomes negative (−0.057 on Oxford-IIIT Pet) while Consistency reaches 0.767 on MathVista, underscoring that the effect is intrinsic to reasoning rather than an artifact of prompting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: uncertainty quantification for language/vision-language models (LM-Polygraph, Semantic Entropy, Self-Consistency, self-probing) and chain-of-thought reasoning in multimodal models. Prior studies of CoT and uncertainty (Yoon et al., Lyu et al., Lanham et al.) examined calibration or reasoning faithfulness in text-only LLMs, while VLM uncertainty work (conformal prediction, LM-Polygraph) evaluated static, non-reasoning settings. This paper carves new ground by studying how reasoning systematically alters the *ranking semantics* of token-likelihood-based uncertainty estimates in multimodal models, and introduces 'implicit answer conditioning' as a mechanistic explanation for the observed degradation — a concept not previously articulated in either the UQ or the CoT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is direct: practitioners deploying reasoning-enabled VLMs in safety-critical settings (medical imaging, autonomous navigation) should not rely on token-likelihood-based UQ (MSP, PPL, MTE, MC-SE) for selective generation, as these become overconfident and poorly ranked under reasoning. Agreement-based consistency is the recommended alternative, though it incurs K× sampling cost. Open questions include whether the implicit conditioning effect can be mitigated at training time (e.g., via RL objectives that penalize premature commitment), whether the masking intervention generalizes beyond explicit answer mentions to more subtle forms of semantic convergence, and whether the effect persists or changes in larger or differently-architected models. The paper also leaves open whether the degradation is specific to decoder-style VLMs or extends to encoder-decoder or diffusion-based multimodal models.
