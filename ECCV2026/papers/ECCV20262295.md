## Problem

Classical scale-space theory provides physically interpretable, frequency-ordered surface evolution via curvature flows, but its numerical irregularity, topological instability, and computational cost isolate it from modern deep learning. Existing neural approaches either rely on unstable discrete mesh/level-set iterations or unstructured latent interpolations that lack local geometric interpretability and produce non-geometric artifacts. The paper addresses the gap by learning a continuous, physics-driven scale-space surrogate that is both differentiable and topologically stable.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a watertight 3D mesh (normalized to a fixed face count, e.g., 50k faces via SDF remeshing) and a scalar normalized evolution time t ∈ [0,1] as input, and produces an evolved mesh at that stage along with a full continuous deformation trajectory from detailed surface (t=1) to a minimum-information base topology (t→0). The VNC-Flow preprocessing generates stage-wise (mesh, time) training pairs where time is defined by the ratio of current to initial VNC-AUC geometric complexity, not by physical iteration count. At inference, the C-VAE requires only the input mesh and a target t value—no iterative simulation.

## Method

The framework has three synergistic components. First, the VNC operator computes a dimensionless, multi-scale mean curvature via Gaussian-weighted integration; the VNC-soft variant reformulates explicit line-ball intersections as global dense matrix multiplications, enabling fully differentiable GPU-parallel evaluation. Second, the spacetime-balanced VNC-Flow algorithm tames irregular curvature flow into structured trajectories by enforcing three constraints: per-vertex adaptive scales (spatial balance via VNC-AUC thresholding), equidistant geometric-information decay budgets defining normalized time (temporal balance), and periodic SDF-based remeshing to eliminate topological singularities. Third, a Controllable VAE (C-VAE) built on the VecSet backbone uses cross-attention with the normalized time t as a deep conditioning signal; two variants exist—C-VAE-I fuses t into intermediate encoder features for dataset-level generalization, while C-VAE-II modulates frozen pre-trained latent codes for high-fidelity instance-specific distillation. This temporal conditioning induces a radial latent structure where base shapes cluster at the origin and high-frequency details radiate outward, making linear interpolation in t correspond to physically meaningful curvature-flow traversal.

## Results

On the primary surface evolution task (Table 2), C-VAE-II at t=1.0 achieves CD=1.05×10⁻⁴, EMD=1.94×10⁻², and NCE=0.257 rad, setting the best (lowest) value on all three metrics. For the VNC operator (Table 1), VNC-soft delivers multi-scale, differentiable, GPU-parallel curvature at 57 ms (10k vertices), which is 50–150× faster than the CPU integral-invariant baseline while retaining explicit multi-scale capability that DiffCurvature lacks. The spacetime-balanced VNC-Flow produces singularity-free trajectories that converge to a spherical base topology, validated qualitatively on complex human scans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three surface-evolution baselines in Table 2, C-VAE-II wins on every metric: it reduces CD by 45% over NMF (1.05 vs 1.92×10⁻⁴) and by 35% over MeshSDF (1.05 vs 1.61×10⁻⁴); it cuts EMD by 65% over NMF (1.94 vs 5.50×10⁻²) and by 24% over MeshSDF (1.94 vs 2.55×10⁻²); and it lowers NCE by 50% over NMF (0.257 vs 0.518 rad) and by 25% over MeshSDF (0.257 vs 0.341 rad). NIE performs worst on CD (4.77×10⁻⁴) despite competitive EMD, indicating its level-set formulation introduces larger geometric deviations. For the curvature-operator comparison (Table 1), VNC-soft is 50–150× faster than the CPU integral-invariant baseline [2] (57 ms vs 25 s at 10k vertices) while providing the same multi-scale capability; it is 2–3× slower than DiffCurvature [18] (57 ms vs 3 ms) but uniquely supports multi-scale analysis, which DiffCurvature cannot. VNC-soft also achieves a 2–3× speedup over VNC-hard through matrix-tensorized computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges classical integral-geometry scale-space theory (Cohen-Steiner & Morvan's normal cycle, Pottmann et al.'s integral invariants) with modern neural 3D shape representations built on the VecSet backbone (Zhang et al. 2023; Li et al. 2025). It departs from prior surface-evolution methods (Neural Mesh Flow, NIE, MeshSDF) that either iterate explicit deformations or interpolate in unstructured latent spaces, instead learning a continuous time-conditioned neural surrogate for a physically grounded, spacetime-balanced curvature flow. The key conceptual shift is replacing irregular, topology-unstable MCF trajectories with a structured sampling procedure (adaptive scales + information-decay budget) that produces learnable, singularity-free training sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a physically interpretable, continuous control knob (normalized evolution time t) for 3D shape abstraction without iterative simulation at inference, and the emergent radial latent structure offers a structural prior that could benefit downstream controllable generation tasks. However, the current scope is limited to watertight human scans, cross-category transfer is unestablished, and the SDF-remeshing requirement constrains applicability to fragmented or open surfaces. Open questions include whether the differentiable VNC-soft operator can support gradient-based objectives end-to-end, and whether combining this scale-aware prior with Flow Matching or diffusion models yields genuinely controllable 3D generation beyond the current offline-trajectory paradigm.
