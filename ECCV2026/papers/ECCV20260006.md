## Problem

Pretrained Vision-Language Encoders used for zero-shot referring image segmentation rely on final-layer global embeddings that are optimized for semantic alignment, causing two coupled failures: vision embeddings become nearly position-invariant, and multilingual text embeddings develop language-dependent geometric shifts in the shared space. No prior training-free method simultaneously mitigates both spatial insensitivity and cross-lingual instability on frozen VLEs, leaving a gap in unified, parameter-free spatial grounding.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a referring expression (and optionally N translated versions for multilingual mode), and K mask proposals from a frozen segmentor (SAM or Mask2Former) as input. It produces a final segmentation mask via reranked proposal selection, a dense p×p patch-level spatial map (P-Map) derived from mid-layer vision features, and a multilingual-stabilized text embedding formed by injecting a cross-language centroid at an intermediate text-encoder layer. All operations are training-free plug-ins on frozen VLE backbones (CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2, DFN, BLIP), requiring no gradient backpropagation or attention recomputation.

## Method

B2G (Biased to Grounded) follows a unified probe → select → ground paradigm on frozen VLEs. For spatial grounding, a tile-permutation probe identifies a position-sensitive intermediate vision layer; patch embeddings from that layer undergo a deterministic sign inversion (correcting the 'opposite visualization' phenomenon) and are projected to the joint space to form a P-Map, whose thresholded connected components yield a binary guidance mask for overlap-based reranking of top-κ proposals. For multilingual robustness, a cross-language consistency score Φ^l selects the most language-stable text-encoder layer, where a centroid of N translated sentence embeddings is injected into the anchor-language EOT token before propagation through remaining layers. The final mask is selected by combining the baseline cosine similarity with an IoU overlap term against the P-Map guidance mask, and the multilingual-stabilized embedding replaces the standard text embedding in cross-modal scoring. Layer selection supports fixed (B2G_fix), cluster-coherence (B2G_dyn-clus), and permutation-probe (B2G_dyn-perm) strategies, all operating without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the standard zero-shot RIS benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg), B2G with Mask2Former and CLIP ViT-B/16 achieves 50.20 average mIoU, surpassing the strongest prior training-free baseline (HybridGL) by 4.46 mIoU. The largest gains appear with DFN ViT-H/14 (+6.49 mIoU) and CLIP ViT-B/32 (+5.70 mIoU). On the challenging PhraseCut dataset, B2G attains 38.53 oIoU (All) and 38.31 (Unseen), outperforming all prior zero-shot and supervised methods. For multilingual zero-shot RIS across nine translated benchmarks, B2G increases average IoU@50 from 45.13 to 53.41 and mIoU from 42.42 to 49.62 over the SigLIP2 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

B2G consistently outperforms both Global-Local [45] and HybridGL [18] across all six backbone-segmentor configurations in Table 1, with gains ranging from +0.98 to +6.49 mIoU; the largest improvements occur with Mask2Former (which produces cleaner semantic proposals) and with DFN ViT-H/14. On PhraseCut, B2G (38.53/38.31 oIoU) surpasses the strongest zero-shot baseline IteRPrimE [39] (38.10/37.90) and HybridGL [18] (37.60/37.91), and also beats supervised methods PRIS [46] (32.75/33.52) and LAVT [44] (28.68/29.14). The gap over CRIS [40] (16.30/14.62) and TAS [34] (25.64/24.66) is particularly large, reflecting B2G's superior spatial grounding from mid-layer P-Map cues. For multilingual retrieval (Table 2), B2G improves Recall@1 over SigLIP2 by +4.22, +2.77, and +6.42 on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg respectively, with the largest per-language gains in German (+12.15 on RefCOCO) and Chinese (+7.23 on RefCOCOg), while also slightly improving English (+1.23 average), confirming no generalization trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the HybridGL [18] feature-extraction pipeline for zero-shot RIS, positioning B2G as a training-free plug-in that augments the globally aligned final-layer embeddings with mid-layer spatial and linguistic cues. It departs from the dominant paradigm in zero-shot RIS—where all prior methods (Global-Local [45], ReCLIP [32], TAS [34], IteRPrimE [39]) score proposals using final-layer VLE embeddings—by demonstrating that these final-layer representations are systematically spatially insensitive and multilingually unstable. Unlike gradient-based spatial guidance (Grad-CAM in CaR [33], IteRPrimE [39]) or diffusion-model-based approaches (Ref-Diff [22]), B2G extracts a dense P-Map from a single forward pass through a position-sensitive mid-layer, and stabilizes multilingual text geometry via centroid injection at a language-consistent intermediate text layer, unifying both fixes under a probe→select→ground framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that spatial and multilingual structure is preserved in VLE mid-layers but attenuated at the final alignment layer offers a concrete, actionable diagnosis for practitioners: the bottleneck is the interface, not the absence of grounding information. This opens a path toward bias-aware use of frozen VLEs across tasks beyond RIS (dense prediction, retrieval, navigation) without retraining. Limitations include the ad-hoc sign-inversion correction for 'opposite visualization,' the 1.5–1.7× inference overhead for the multilingual variant, and the lack of a theoretical account for why alignment objectives produce these specific layer-wise geometric effects. A deeper understanding of how contrastive pretraining shapes representational geometry across depth remains an open question that could enable principled, rather than heuristic, layer selection.
