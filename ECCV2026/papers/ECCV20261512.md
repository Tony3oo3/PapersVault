## Problem

3DGS-based LiDAR-camera calibration methods inherit a rendering-oriented optimization that allows photometric gradients to deform Gaussian positions and covariances, causing the proxy geometry to drift from the true LiDAR structure. Existing sparse single-scan depth supervision is insufficient to prevent this drift, leading to a phenomenon the authors term Geometric Decay, where calibration accuracy degrades despite visually plausible renderings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261512/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized RGB camera images, LiDAR point clouds, LiDAR-to-world odometry poses, and known camera intrinsics. It outputs the LiDAR-to-camera extrinsic transformation (rotation and translation) as a 6-DoF SE(3) parameter. Notably, the 3DGS proxy is discarded after optimization—only the estimated extrinsics are retained—and depth supervision uses a rotation-only extrinsic to isolate depth constraints from translation uncertainty during the iterative refinement.

## Method

GeoP-Calib introduces two complementary mechanisms to preserve the metric geometry of the 3DGS proxy during joint extrinsic optimization. Dense Depth Anchoring (DDA) accumulates multi-view LiDAR observations into a dense global point cloud projected onto the image plane, providing far greater spatial coverage than single-scan supervision; a Volumetric Soft Mask uses the rendered Gaussian depth as a continuous visibility prior to downweight occluded projections and resolve multi-view occlusion ambiguity. Gradient Decoupling (GD) applies a stop-gradient operator to the Gaussian spatial parameters (position and covariance) within the photometric rendering path, so photometric residuals can only refine appearance (opacity, color) and extrinsics without deforming geometry, while the reprojection loss retains access to spatial parameters for view-consistent structural updates. The full objective combines the decoupled photometric loss, reprojection consistency, sparse depth anchoring, and the new dense depth anchoring term, optimized in a two-stage schedule (geometric warm-up followed by dense refinement) built on the HiGS-Calib codebase.

## Results

On KITTI-360, GeoP-Calib achieves an average rotation error of 0.121° and translation error of 0.063 m, outperforming the second-best method (HiGS-Calib) by 0.019° and 0.034 m respectively. On KITTI, it achieves 0.188° rotation and 0.044 m translation, improving over HiGS-Calib by 0.018° and 0.011 m. GeoP-Calib attains the lowest error on 7 out of 10 sequences for both rotation and translation across the two datasets, with particularly notable gains in translation estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoP-Calib outperforms all four baselines on average translation error across both datasets, with the largest margins over GST (0.252 m on KITTI-360, 0.234 m on KITTI) and CLAIM (0.066 m on KITTI-360, 0.038 m on KITTI). Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor HiGS-Calib, GeoP-Calib improves translation on every single sequence (e.g., 0.031 m vs. 0.036 m on KITTI-360 seq1; 0.023 m vs. 0.052 m on KITTI seq3), while matching or slightly exceeding its rotation accuracy. RobustCalib (2DGS-based) is consistently outperformed on both metrics, with GeoP-Calib achieving roughly half its translation error on KITTI-360 (0.063 m vs. 0.097 m). CLAIM, the foundation-model-based method, achieves competitive translation in some sequences but suffers from consistently higher rotation errors (0.520° avg on KITTI-360 vs. 0.121° for GeoP-Calib), limiting its overall calibration quality. GST, the traditional optimization baseline, is unreliable in driving scenes with large variance and errors exceeding 1° rotation on most sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HiGS-Calib [33] as its codebase and base pipeline, situating itself within the recent wave of 3DGS-based targetless LiDAR-camera calibration methods (Herau et al. [7], Zhou et al. [37], Jung et al. [12]). The key conceptual departure is the identification of 'Geometric Decay'—the phenomenon where photometric optimization, inherited from 3DGS's novel-view-synthesis design, distorts the Gaussian proxy away from true LiDAR geometry. Rather than treating the proxy as a free parameter to be jointly optimized with extrinsics (as prior 3DGS calibration methods do), GeoP-Calib introduces Dense Depth Anchoring with multi-view LiDAR accumulation and Gradient Decoupling to explicitly protect spatial parameters from photometric gradients, reframing the proxy as a rigid geometric bridge rather than a learnable scene model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis of Geometric Decay provides a useful conceptual framework for understanding why 3DGS-based calibration can produce visually plausible renderings yet inaccurate extrinsics, and the finding that lower PSNR (worse rendering) correlates with better calibration accuracy challenges the implicit assumption that photometric fidelity is a proxy for calibration quality. However, the practical gains are modest—sub-degree rotation and sub-centimeter translation improvements over the strongest 3DGS baseline—and rotation accuracy remains the weaker axis. Open questions include whether the geometric-decay diagnosis generalizes to other differentiable scene representations (NeRF, 2DGS), whether gradient decoupling can be relaxed to selectively exploit texture cues without re-introducing drift, and how the method behaves under degraded LiDAR odometry, which the authors acknowledge as a dependency.
