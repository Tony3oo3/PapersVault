## Problem

End-to-end affordance prediction models couple high-level reasoning and low-level grounding in a single architecture, leading to capability trade-offs, poor generalization to novel objects, and inability to independently upgrade components. The paper asks whether decoupling these processes into specialized, coordinated agents—without any task-specific training—can outperform supervised methods that are explicitly fine-tuned on affordance datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260176/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single RGB image and a free-form natural language instruction (e.g., 'open the refrigerator') as input. It produces a multi-granular spatial output: a set of tuples (bounding box, key point, segmentation mask) for each identified affordance region, with the segmentation mask serving as the primary pixel-level representation. The entire pipeline operates in a strictly zero-shot manner—no component is trained or fine-tuned on any affordance dataset.

## Method

A4-Agent decomposes affordance prediction into a three-stage agentic pipeline: (1) a Dreamer that uses a VLM to craft an image-editing prompt and a generative model (Qwen-Image-Editing) to synthesize an imagined interaction image showing how the action would look; (2) a Thinker (GPT-4o) that reasons over both the original and imagined images to produce a structured JSON description specifying the target object and its functional part; (3) a Spotter that uses Rex-Omni for open-vocabulary detection (bounding boxes and key points) followed by SAM2 for pixel-accurate mask refinement. The key insight is that the Dreamer externalizes the generative model's physical interaction priors as explicit visual evidence, compensating for weaker internal knowledge in smaller VLMs, while the decoupled architecture allows each stage to be independently upgraded without retraining.

## Results

On ReasonAff, A4-Agent achieves 70.52 gIoU and 64.62 cIoU in a fully zero-shot setting, surpassing the best supervised baseline Affordance-R1 (67.41 gIoU, 62.72 cIoU) by 3.11 and 1.90 points respectively. On RAGNet-3DOI, A4-Agent reaches 63.9 gIoU, outperforming the supervised AffordanceVLM (trained on this dataset) by 25.8 gIoU points. On UMD Part Affordance, A4-Agent attains 65.38 gIoU, exceeding the next-best method Affordance-R1 (49.85 gIoU) by 15.53 points. All results are reported without any task-specific training or fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A4-Agent outperforms all 14 baselines across every benchmark and metric. The closest competitor is Affordance-R1 [44] on ReasonAff (67.41 vs. 70.52 gIoU, +3.11) and on UMD (49.85 vs. 65.38 gIoU, +15.53), both of which are supervised methods requiring task-specific fine-tuning. On RAGNet-3DOI, A4-Agent (63.9 gIoU) surpasses the supervised AffordanceVLM [48]—which was explicitly trained on this dataset—by 25.8 gIoU points, and beats Affordance-R1 by 24.9 points. Among reasoning-enhanced baselines, Vision Reasoner [28] (63.04 gIoU) and Seg-Zero [27] (59.26 gIoU) trail A4-Agent by 7.48 and 11.26 gIoU on ReasonAff respectively. Open-vocabulary grounding baselines (G-DINO [26], GLaMM [36], OVSeg [24], SAN [50]) and part-segmentation methods (VLPart [41]) are far behind, scoring below 20 gIoU on ReasonAff, confirming that pure grounding without reasoning is insufficient for complex affordance tasks. VLM-based baselines without specialized grounding (InternVL3-8B [56], Qwen2.5VL-7B [3], LISA [21], SAM4MLLM [6], AffordanceLLM [35]) all fall between 25–48 gIoU, illustrating the reasoning-grounding trade-off that A4-Agent's decoupled architecture avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end paradigm in affordance prediction (AffordanceLLM, Affordance-R1, AffordanceVLM, Seg-Zero, Vision Reasoner), which jointly optimizes reasoning and grounding within a single fine-tuned MLLM. Instead, it repurposes the agentic orchestration idea—coordinating specialized foundation models at test time—into a three-stage pipeline (Dreamer–Thinker–Spotter) that explicitly decouples semantic reasoning from spatial grounding. The imagination step, where a generative model synthesizes a simulated interaction image to assist the VLM's reasoning, draws on the broader 'think-with-images' literature (VoT, Visualization-of-Thought) but is the first to apply it specifically to affordance prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong empirical evidence that training-free coordination of off-the-shelf foundation models can match or exceed supervised affordance predictors, suggesting a viable path toward generalizable, maintenance-light affordance systems for robotics. The decoupling principle is broadly applicable beyond affordance prediction to any task requiring both semantic reasoning and precise spatial grounding. Key limitations include heavy reliance on a closed-source VLM (GPT-4o) for the Thinker, ~32 s inference time for the full pipeline, and the fact that the Lite variant (which drops the Dreamer and uses a 3B open-source model) narrows the performance gap considerably, raising questions about how much of the gain is attributable to the imagination mechanism versus raw model capability.
