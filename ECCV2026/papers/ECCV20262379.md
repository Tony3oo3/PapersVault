## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models suffer from concept association bias, where strongly learned visual priors override explicit textual attributes, making it impossible to generate counterfactual or unusual descriptions of objects. This bias is especially severe for one-and-only (OAO) entities—celestial bodies, landmarks, and artworks—that exist in a single canonical form, as their deeply ingrained visual identity resists modification through prompting alone. The paper identifies that the final text embedding discards concept-level information present in intermediate encoder layers, reducing the mutual information available to the denoising process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard natural language text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image, operating entirely at inference time on pre-trained diffusion models (SD2.1 with a single OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 encoder, or SD3.0 with three encoders: OpenCLIP ViT-G, CLIP ViT-L, and T5-XXL). No additional training, optimization, or external models are required; the only modification is extracting an intermediate hidden state (after the first transformer block, k=1) from the frozen text encoder and injecting it into the conditioning signal during early denoising steps.

## Method

IR-guided diffusion is grounded in an information-theoretic analysis showing that, by the Data Processing Inequality, intermediate text encoder representations carry at least as much mutual information with the input prompt as the final embedding, and that conditioning on these richer representations increases attribute-specific information in the generated latent. Concretely, the method constructs an IR-guided embedding via additive injection (c̃ = c_f + λc_h, with layer normalization applied to c_h) and uses it to condition the denoising network only during early structure-forming steps. An adaptive scheduler dynamically determines the transition timestep t* by monitoring structural acceleration (ΔD_t) in Tweedie-estimated clean images, switching back to the standard final embedding once the layout stabilizes. For multi-encoder architectures, intermediate representations are extracted independently from each encoder and concatenated following the native scheme before additive injection. The approach requires no predefined bias sets, no prompt-specific knowledge, and no architectural modification.

## Results

On OAO-AttackBench with SD3.0, IR-guided diffusion achieves up to a 19.1 percentage-point improvement in VQAScore over the baseline (e.g., Landmarks Material: 0.525 → 0.716), while CLIPScore remains largely unchanged (e.g., 0.263 → 0.289), confirming that global semantic alignment is preserved while fine-grained attribute alignment improves. On Whoops, VQAScore improves from 0.778 to 0.802. On general-purpose Gecko prompts, the method maintains or slightly improves all metrics (e.g., Gecko(R) VQAScore 0.866 → 0.871, HPSv2.1 0.264 → 0.271), showing no quality degradation on standard prompts. KID scores are comparable or better than baseline across all OAO-AttackBench categories, and HPSv2/HPSv2.1 human preference scores improve on both OAO-AttackBench and Whoops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares IR-guided diffusion against two baseline diffusion backbones: SD2.1 (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and SD3.0 (Esser et al., ICML 2024). On SD3.0, IR-guidance consistently outperforms the baseline on VQAScore across all seven OAO-AttackBench sub-categories and Whoops, with the largest gain of +19.1 pp on Landmarks Material (0.525 → 0.716) and +10.0 pp on Artworks Material (0.657 → 0.756). On SD2.1, the pattern is similar with gains on most categories (e.g., Artworks Style +5.9 pp, Celestial Shape +3.1 pp), though Landmarks Material shows a negligible −0.2 pp drop. CLIPScore changes are modest (±1–3 pp), indicating that the method targets fine-grained attribute alignment without disturbing global semantics. KID scores are comparable or slightly better than baseline in all categories, and HPSv2/HPSv2.1 human preference scores improve (e.g., OAO-AttackBench HPSv2: 0.269 → 0.273; Whoops HPSv2.1: 0.295 → 0.299). On general Gecko prompts, the method matches or marginally exceeds the baseline on all four metrics, confirming no quality regression on standard generation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the observation from Diffusion Lens [45] that intermediate text-encoder layers retain attribute-level information lost in the final embedding, but redirects it from diagnostic analysis to a practical alignment mechanism. It departs from prior bias-mitigation work (e.g., Attend-and-Excite [5], Orgad et al. [32]) by requiring no predefined bias sets, no prompt-specific knowledge, and no external models—instead exploiting an untapped internal signal already present in the T2I pipeline. The information-theoretic framing via the data processing inequality provides a formal justification for why intermediate representations should outperform the final embedding, a theoretical angle absent from prior empirical observations of layer-wise concept emergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, optimization-free nature of IR-guidance makes it immediately deployable as a drop-in modification to any diffusion T2I pipeline, and the 19.1 pp VQAScore gain on OAO-AttackBench demonstrates that deeply ingrained visual priors for canonical objects can be overridden without retraining. OAO-AttackBench fills a gap in evaluation by providing a systematic, category-structured benchmark for the most extreme form of concept association bias. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion, multi-concept counterfactual prompts with several simultaneously overridden attributes, and whether the modest CLIPScore gains indicate a fundamental ceiling on how much fine-grained attribute information intermediate layers can recover.
