## Problem

Decomposing outgoing surface radiance into material and illumination during inverse rendering is severely ill-posed, as many material-lighting combinations yield identical observed colors. Existing resolutions require multi-illumination captures, handcrafted or learned priors, or complex lighting setups. This paper asks whether the natural diversity of surface-light interactions induced by observing a rigidly moving object from a static camera is sufficient to resolve the ambiguity without any of these additional constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video of a person freely rotating a rigid object in front of a static camera, along with 2D object masks (obtained via SAM3 for real captures). It produces a full relightable 4D representation: SDF-based 3D Gaussian geometry, per-frame SE(3) object poses, spatially varying albedo and roughness maps stored at Gaussian plane corners, a far-field environment illumination map, and novel relit renders. No multi-camera rig, RGB-D sensor, or explicit pose annotations are required.

## Method

The pipeline is a three-stage progressive optimization. Stage 1 uses NeuS (neural SDF) with sequential frame-by-frame pose tracking aided by 2D feature correspondences (MASt3R, ROMA v2) to obtain coarse geometry and initial poses. Stage 2 switches to SDF-integrated 3D Gaussians (DiscretizedSDF) and globally refines geometry and all poses jointly, using FastGS-style adaptive density control to keep the Gaussian count low. Stage 3 augments the Gaussians with spatially varying Lambertian+GGX material parameters and jointly optimizes them with a progressively upscaled environment map via deferred physically-based rendering, using 3D Gaussian ray tracing for self-occlusion and indirect illumination. The key mechanism is that transforming the object (rather than the camera) across timesteps causes the same canonical surface points to interact with the fixed far-field light under many different orientations, providing the diverse constraints that disambiguate albedo from illumination.

## Results

On the novel hand-held dynamic setting (Table 3), the proposed method achieves 41.03 dB albedo PSNR, 39.65 dB relighting PSNR, and 0.51° normal MAE, outperforming the closest baseline IRGS* (40.26 / 32.99 / 0.56) by a large margin on relighting. On the standard TensoIR static benchmark (Table 2), the method reaches 33.96 dB albedo PSNR and 32.17 dB relighting PSNR, surpassing both SVG-IR (30.27 / 31.19) and IRGS (33.41 / 30.63). The core ablation (Table 1) shows that the hand-held setting with estimated poses (40.33 dB albedo PSNR, diffuse variant) outperforms the static dome setting even with ground-truth poses (32.68 dB), validating the central hypothesis that motion provides stronger material-lighting constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IRGS* (extended for dynamics), the proposed method wins on all three metrics in the hand-held setting: +0.77 dB albedo PSNR (41.03 vs 40.26), a substantial +6.66 dB relighting PSNR (39.65 vs 32.99), and marginally better normals (0.51° vs 0.56°). Against SVG-IR*, the advantage is decisive across the board: +7.63 dB albedo, +7.43 dB relighting, and 3.18° lower normal MAE (0.51 vs 3.69). ReCap, which optimizes a separate environment map per lighting condition, is far less suited to the dynamic setting and trails by roughly 19 dB in albedo PSNR regardless of the number of environments used. On the static TensoIR benchmark, the method also leads on albedo (33.96 vs 33.41 IRGS, 30.27 SVG-IR) and relighting (32.17 vs 30.63 IRGS, 31.19 SVG-IR), though it is slightly behind IRGS on normal accuracy (4.19° vs 3.99°), suggesting the SDF-based geometry is competitive but not strictly superior in the static regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads—dynamic rigid-object reconstruction (FMOV, NeuS, 3D Gaussian Splatting) and static inverse rendering (SVG-IR, IRGS, TensoIR)—into a single three-stage pipeline that tracks a hand-held object and simultaneously decomposes its material and illumination. The key conceptual departure from prior inverse rendering work is that instead of resolving the material-lighting ambiguity through multi-environment captures, learned priors, or diffusion-based relighting, the authors exploit the natural diversity of surface-light interactions induced by rigid object motion under a single far-field light. The geometry backbone (NeuS → DiscretizedSDF Gaussians) and the rendering stack (3DGRT ray tracing, deferred shading, GGX BRDF) are assembled from existing components, with the novelty lying in the joint optimization formulation across timesteps and the hand-held capture setting itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first controlled, quantitative evidence that observing a rigid object under significant pose change from a single camera yields materially better albedo-lighting separation than a static multiview capture—even when the static setting is given ground-truth poses. This opens a practical path to relightable reconstruction from a single casual video without specialized lighting rigs or large-scale prior training. However, the validation is primarily synthetic (two real objects, qualitative only), the method assumes rigid motion and far-field illumination, and hand occlusions are not modeled, leaving the most common real-world hand-held scenario partially unaddressed. Extending to non-rigid objects and near-field lighting is identified as the natural next step.
