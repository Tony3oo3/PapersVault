## Problem

Existing RL-based approaches for multi-concept compositional text-to-image generation naively aggregate concept-wise rewards, causing optimization to be dominated by easily satisfied concepts while neglecting difficult or negatively correlated ones. This leads to partial success where models generate some requested attributes but omit others, with the failure rate increasing sharply as prompt complexity grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261913/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-concept text prompts specifying up to eight concepts per entity (objects, colors, textures, shapes, sizes, spatial relations, and numeracy) and produces a single generated image. During training, each prompt yields a group of 16 images across 4 GPUs, and each image is evaluated by a set of dedicated reward functions (SAM 3 segmentation for object existence, OpenCLIP-H contrastive scoring for attributes, count-deviation heuristics for numeracy, and Depth Anything 3 for 3D spatial relations) to produce a per-concept reward vector. The training set comprises 5k prompts with a skewed sampling ratio favoring spatial and numeracy concepts.

## Method

CMO decomposes multi-concept prompts into fine-grained concept objectives, each scored by a dedicated reward function, forming a reward matrix across G generated images per prompt. It then computes the Pearson correlation matrix among concept rewards to estimate per-concept difficulty: concepts with low average correlation (i.e., negatively correlated with others) are identified as hard to satisfy simultaneously. A softmax over (1 − α_k), where α_k is the mean correlation of concept k with all others, yields adaptive weights that upweight difficult concepts in the advantage computation. This reweighted advantage is integrated into a GDPO-style decoupled normalization and optimized via MixGRPO's mixed ODE-SDE sampling (stochastic SDE steps confined to the first 3 timesteps), applied as LoRA fine-tuning on SD3.5 and FLUX.1-dev.

## Results

On ConceptMix Full Mark Score, CMO (FLUX.1-dev) achieves 0.8410 at k=1 and 0.1883 at k=7, outperforming Qwen-Image (0.8183 / 0.1747) and the base FLUX.1-dev (0.6647 / 0.0372) across all difficulty levels. On GenEval 2, CMO reaches 80.0 Soft-TIFA_AM and 34.0 Soft-TIFA_GM, matching Qwen-Image on atom-level but surpassing it on prompt-level correctness (34.0 vs. 31.4). On T2I-CompBench, SD3.5+CMO achieves the highest average score of 0.6141, with standout results in Color binding (0.8692), Spatial relations (0.5475), and Texture (0.7959).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMO achieves the highest Full Mark Score on ConceptMix at every difficulty level (k=1 through k=7), beating the strongest baseline Qwen-Image by 2.3 points at k=1 (0.8410 vs. 0.8183) and by 1.4 points at k=7 (0.1883 vs. 0.1747), while the base FLUX.1-dev backbone only reaches 0.0372 at k=7. Among RL-based methods, CMO outperforms FlowGRPO (0.0713 at k=7) by 2.6× and PrefGRPO (0.0647) by 2.9× at the highest complexity. On GenEval 2, CMO ties Qwen-Image on Soft-TIFA_AM (80.0) but surpasses it on Soft-TIFA_GM (34.0 vs. 31.4), and decisively beats FlowGRPO (21.8) and PrefGRPO (23.0) on prompt-level correctness. On T2I-CompBench, SD3.5+CMO attains the best average (0.6141) over Qwen-Image (0.5892), FlowGRPO (0.5856), and NanoBanana (0.5422), with particularly large gains in Spatial relations (0.5475 vs. 0.4454 for Qwen-Image) and Color binding (0.8692 vs. 0.8395). CMO also outperforms all autoregressive baselines (Janus, Show-o, HermesFlow) by wide margins on every benchmark, confirming that the correlation-based reweighting strategy generalizes across model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the recent wave of RL-based diffusion/flow-matching alignment methods (FlowGRPO, PrefGRPO, DanceGRPO, TempGRPO) that optimize text-to-image models using group-relative policy optimization. It adopts MixGRPO's mixed ODE-SDE sampling for computational efficiency and GDPO's reward-decoupled normalization as its optimization backbone, but departs from all prior multi-reward RL approaches by introducing a correlation-based reweighting mechanism that estimates per-concept difficulty from the statistical structure of reward signals across generated samples. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods treat concept rewards as independent signals to be averaged, whereas CMO explicitly models their interactions (positive/negative correlations) to identify and upweight the hardest-to-satisfy concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Compositional generation with multiple simultaneous constraints remains a persistent failure mode for state-of-the-art T2I models, and this paper provides a clean, interpretable mechanism (Pearson correlation among reward signals) to diagnose and correct the optimization imbalance that causes concept omission. The ablation revealing that the base multi-reward optimization (DRO) alone actually degrades performance without the correlation reweighting is a useful cautionary finding for practitioners combining multiple reward signals. Open questions include whether the fixed sampling ratio across concept groups (Color:Texture:Shape:Size:Spatial:Numeracy = 3:3:2:1:10:7) can be made adaptive, whether the approach extends to preference-aware or perceptual-quality-constrained settings, and how pre-training data scale interacts with post-training multi-reward optimization.
