## Problem

Existing image retouching models treat editing as global quality enhancement or support localized adjustments, but none explicitly model visual focus enhancement — the deliberate redistribution of perceptual saliency to guide viewers' attention toward a user-specified region. This is especially challenging under weak spatial cues (a few clicks or coarse strokes), where the system must infer both the intended focus and the appropriate tonal operations while preserving naturalness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261732/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts an RGB image, sparse spatial intent signals (point clicks, free-form brush strokes, or region markings), and optional free-text guidance describing the user's intention. A VLM planner first interprets these ambiguous signals to produce a refined intent mask and decoupled global/local text instructions, which are then consumed by the diffusion executor. The output is a retouched image in one of three modes — Global (overall tonal adjustment), Local (region-specific refinement), or Focus (coordinated global+local adjustment to enhance the intended visual focus) — and the result can be fed back for iterative multi-round refinement.

## Method

EyeControl couples a multi-stage VLM Planner with a Flux.1-Kontext-based diffusion Executor fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 128) on MM-DiT blocks. The Planner decomposes intent understanding into scene parsing, intent reasoning, and guidance generation, then selects an execution mode and emits a binary mode indicator that activates global and/or local edit instructions. Two key training losses differentiate the executor: (1) Pseudo-Intent Attention Alignment (PAA) explicitly supervises the mask-to-latent attention sub-maps at every DiT layer to match a signed saliency-difference map derived from ground-truth retouching pairs, forcing the model to concentrate attention within the intended region rather than treating the mask as a mere spatial boundary; and (2) Operation-Consistency Loss enforces that sequential global→local or local→global denoising paths produce latents consistent with the joint focus-mode prediction, eliminating order-sensitive interference between global and local adjustments. Training data is constructed through a three-stage pipeline that curates retouching pairs with verified saliency shifts, extracts intent anchors from saliency difference maps, and augments them with simulated diverse interaction patterns (clicks, strokes, region painting).

## Results

On ControlArt-Bench, EyeControl achieves the best PSNR (21.88 dB), SSIM (0.852), overall score O (8.71), and lowest KL divergence (0.222) among all compared methods. It also attains the highest PQ (9.31) and CC (0.974), indicating strong perceptual quality and saliency alignment. The closest competitor, Nano-Banana-2, trails by 0.77 dB in PSNR and 0.089 in SSIM, while all other baselines (retouching agents and editing models) fall 1–6 dB behind in PSNR. In the user preference study with 50 participants, EyeControl wins or ties against every competing method across aesthetic quality, intention alignment, and content fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EyeControl outperforms all three retouching-agent baselines (JarvisArt, JarvisEvo, PerTouch) by a wide margin: +0.96 dB PSNR and +0.022 SSIM over the strongest agent (JarvisEvo), and +5.95 dB PSNR over PerTouch. Among open-source editing diffusion models, EyeControl leads by 3.74 dB PSNR over Step1X-Edit and 3.82 dB over FLUX.1-Kontext, while also achieving the lowest KL (0.222 vs. 0.251–0.823), indicating superior saliency preservation. Against the commercial GPT-Image-1.5, EyeControl gains 5.17 dB PSNR and 0.286 SSIM, though GPT-Image-1.5 edges ahead on FA (8.33 vs. 8.21), suggesting it introduces larger structural changes that inflate focus-alignment scores. The closest competitor is Nano-Banana-2 (Gemini), which achieves a slightly higher SIM (0.909 vs. 0.907) but trails EyeControl on PSNR (21.11 vs. 21.88), SSIM (0.763 vs. 0.852), PQ (9.29 vs. 9.31), and O (8.68 vs. 8.71). Overall, EyeControl achieves the best or near-best score on 7 of 8 metrics, with the sole exception being SIM where Nano-Banana-2 leads by a negligible 0.002.

## Relation to Prior Work

EyeControl sits at the intersection of three research threads: diffusion-based image editing (built on Flux.1-Kontext with LoRA fine-tuning), VLM-driven retouching agents (JarvisArt, JarvisEvo, PerTouch), and saliency retargeting (Wong & Low, Miangoleh et al.). The key conceptual departure is reframing retouching not as global quality improvement or text-prompted local editing, but as an explicit visual-focus-enhancement task under weak spatial cues (clicks/strokes). Architecturally, the paper introduces Pseudo-Intent Attention Alignment (PAA) to supervise mask-to-latent attention with saliency-difference targets, and an Operation-Consistency Loss to enforce path-invariance between global and local editing orders—neither of which appears in prior retouching or saliency-retargeting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first dedicated benchmark (ControlArt-Bench) and a set of evaluation metrics (Focus Alignment, saliency KL/CC/SIM) for a task that practitioners perform daily but that had no systematic evaluation. The PAA mechanism offers a general recipe for making diffusion models respond to spatial intent at the attention level rather than relying on implicit color perturbations, which could transfer to other mask-conditioned editing tasks. Limitations include dependence on external saliency models for supervision, the small benchmark size (200 samples), and the added latency of a multi-stage VLM planner. Open questions include whether the attention-alignment strategy generalizes to multi-object or adversarial focus scenarios, and whether the operation-consistency constraint holds under more than two editing modes.
