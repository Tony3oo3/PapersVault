## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video generation methods either inject camera parameters implicitly (requiring massive multi-view datasets and suffering from trajectory instability) or warp RGB video and inpaint, which caps output quality at the warped video level and causes the 'Inpainting Trap'—reducing video diffusion models with rich 3D priors into naive inpainters that produce subject distortion and baked-in appearance artifacts. The paper asks how to enable VDMs to internalize camera movements and leverage their 3D understanding for faithful re-rendering rather than pixel-level inpainting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular input video (704×1280, 81 frames) and a user-specified target camera trajectory. A 3D foundation model (pi3) estimates per-frame camera extrinsics and point maps, which are converted to a dynamic 3D mesh; depth and occlusion masks are then rendered under the target trajectory, normalized in log space, and mapped to RGB via a predefined colormap before VAE encoding. The output is a novel-view video at the same resolution and frame count, with the source video concatenated frame-wise (doubling the token sequence) to provide content reference during generation.

## Method

DepthDirector's core insight is decomposing the conditional injection of camera viewpoint and video content into two parallel streams. The View Stream encodes warped depth and occlusion mask via the pretrained VAE, projects them into view tokens through a linear layer, and adds them to the patchified noise latent, providing geometric camera guidance without appearance leakage. The Content Stream concatenates source video tokens along the frame dimension with target video tokens, so self-attention implicitly fuses both conditions and forces the network to perform geometric reasoning for novel-view extrapolation rather than inpainting. A lightweight LoRA adapter (rank 32) is trained on the frozen Wan 2.2-TI2V-5B backbone using a Rectified Flow objective, with training data drawn from the newly constructed MultiCamWarp Dataset—8K synchronized multi-camera videos rendered in Unreal Engine 5 across 1K dynamic scenes and 40 environments—requiring only ~5.8% of the training cost of prior implicit-camera methods.

## Results

On the Koala-36M evaluation set (150 in-the-wild videos, 10 camera trajectories each), DepthDirector (Full) achieves the best camera accuracy across all three metrics: Rotation Error 1.010, Translation Error 0.053, and Camera Motion Consistency 1.434, outperforming the next-best EX-4D (1.186 / 0.101 / 1.687). For content preservation, the relative-camera variant Ours(DC) attains the highest Reference Similarity (0.669), Inter-Frame Similarity (0.959), Subject Consistency (94.62), and Background Consistency (94.76). On VBench perceptual quality, Ours(Full) leads in Imaging Quality (72.90) while Ours(DC) leads in Motion Smoothness (99.28), both surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DepthDirector (Full) decisively outperforms the implicit-camera method ReCamMaster [3] on camera accuracy (RE 1.010 vs. 3.576, CamMC 1.434 vs. 5.063) and imaging quality (72.90 vs. 67.27), confirming that explicit depth-based conditioning yields far more stable trajectory control than learning camera-to-geometry mapping implicitly. Against the warping-and-inpaint baselines GEN3C [36] and EX-4D [20], DepthDirector (Full) achieves lower rotation and translation errors (RE 1.010 vs. 1.397/1.186; TE 0.053 vs. 0.076/0.101) while also surpassing them in imaging quality (72.90 vs. 72.04/70.65), demonstrating that the Inpainting Trap degrades output fidelity. The relative-camera variant Ours(DC) leads all baselines on identity preservation (RS 0.669 vs. 0.653 for EX-4D, 0.618 for GEN3C) and subject/background consistency, showing that the View-Content Dual-Stream mechanism better preserves facial identity and scene structure than warping RGB. TrajectoryCrafter [59], the weakest baseline, lags on every metric (RE 1.464, RS 0.567, Img.Q. 71.59). On the extreme novel-view stress test (±90° rotation), Ours(Full) maintains the highest imaging quality (71.83) and background consistency (90.91), while ReCamMaster drops to 58.42 in imaging quality, further validating the robustness of the depth-conditioned approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of camera-controlled video generation: implicit camera-conditioned methods (ReCamMaster, CameraCtrl) that inject camera parameters directly into VDMs and require large-scale multi-view rendered datasets, and explicit warp-and-inpaint methods (GEN3C, EX-4D, TrajectoryCrafter) that reproject RGB video under target trajectories and inpaint the result. The key conceptual departure is replacing warped RGB with warped depth as the camera-view condition, arguing that RGB warping creates an 'Inpainting Trap' that reduces 3D-capable VDMs to naive inpainters. Architecturally, it builds on Wan 2.2 as a frozen backbone with a LoRA adapter, and leverages pi3 for monocular 3D geometry estimation to construct the explicit mesh from which depth is rendered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Inpainting Trap' framing provides a useful diagnostic for why warp-and-inpaint pipelines systematically degrade on human faces and view-dependent effects, and the depth-vs-RGB conditioning ablation offers a clean mechanistic explanation (attention visualization showing precise geometric retrieval vs. rough correspondence). The MultiCamWarp Dataset (8K videos across 1K scenes, generated via UE5) is a practical resource for the community, and the ~5.8% training cost relative to ReCamMaster makes the approach more accessible. Open questions include how well the method scales to longer sequences, whether the monocular depth estimation bottleneck (pi3) limits ultimate quality, and whether the dual-stream decomposition generalizes to other conditional video generation tasks beyond camera control.
