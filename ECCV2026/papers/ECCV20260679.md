## Problem

Neural map matchers trained on large-scale geo-referenced image datasets inherit noise from automated SfM+GPS annotation pipelines, causing them to learn and replicate systematic pose errors. Prior work assumes perfectly accurate 3-DoF ground-truth labels for supervision, but at the scale required (hundreds of thousands of images), such accuracy is impractical to guarantee. The paper asks whether accurate visual localization can be learned from the weaker, more readily available signals of noisy 2D GPS coordinates and relative poses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a gravity-aligned, calibrated RGB query image and a rasterized 128×128 m OpenStreetMap tile as input, and produces a 3-DoF pose (planar position x,y and heading θ) as a categorical probability distribution over a discretized pose space (128×128 position grid × N heading bins). At inference, the argmax over this S×S×N tensor yields the pose estimate. The architecture is agnostic: the paper applies its supervision to both a U-Net with ResNet-101/VGG-19 backbones (OrienterNet-style) and a DINOv2-based image encoder (OSMLoc-style).

## Method

AutoCompass replaces point-wise 3-DoF NLL supervision with three weak-supervision losses. First, it eliminates the need for heading labels by marginalizing over all N heading bins in the loss, relying on the geometric inductive bias of BEV-to-map cross-correlation to learn heading automatically. Second, it introduces a chunk-based GPS loss that maximizes the probability mass within a ±r meter neighborhood (r up to 20 m) around the raw GPS coordinate, making training robust to meter-level GPS errors. Third, it supervises relative poses between image pairs (from SfM or SLAM) by computing the relative rotation distribution via circular cross-correlation of the two independently predicted heading marginals, and the relative translation distribution via 2D cross-correlation of the two position marginals, then minimizing NLL at the ground-truth relative shift. A relative-distance variant (marginalizing over norm bins) handles non-metric relative translations. The approach is architecture-agnostic and requires no modification to the underlying neural map matcher.

## Results

On KITTI (driving), AutoCompass with relative-pose supervision and a DINOv2 backbone achieves 70.8% lateral R@1m, 91.1% lateral R@3m, and 94.2% lateral R@5m, outperforming all OSM-based baselines including OSMLoc (49.9% lateral R@1m) and OrienterNet (48.7% lateral R@1m with DINOv2). Even with only raw 2D GPS labels (no heading, no relative poses), AutoCompass (43.1% lateral R@1m) surpasses OrienterNet retrained on the same 11-city data (37.7%). On egocentric benchmarks, AutoCompass with relative poses and DINOv2 achieves 20.9% XY R@1m on LaMAria and 20.8% XY R@1m on Oxford Day-and-Night, both exceeding OSMLoc-B (6.7% and 15.5% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoCompass with relative-pose supervision and DINOv2 backbone (70.8% lateral R@1m on KITTI) outperforms OSMLoc-B trained on 12 cities (49.9%) by 20.9 points, and OrienterNet with DINOv2 on the same 11-city data (48.7%) by 22.1 points. Notably, even the weakest AutoCompass variant (raw GPS, ResNet-101: 43.1% lateral R@1m) beats OrienterNet retrained with strong 3-DoF supervision on identical data (37.7%), demonstrating that weak GPS-only supervision surpasses strong absolute-pose supervision. On LaMAria, AutoCompass w/ rel. poses (DINOv2-B) achieves 20.9% XY R@1m versus OSMLoc-B's 6.7%, a 3× improvement, and on Oxford Day-and-Night it reaches 20.8% versus 15.5%. Against satellite-based methods on KITTI (SliceMatch 24.0%, CCVPE 23.4%, Loc2 13.6% lateral R@1m), AutoCompass with DINOv2 (70.8%) is substantially superior, though these use different map types. The key finding is that AutoCompass trained on weaker labels (noisy GPS or relative poses) consistently outperforms methods trained on optimized geo-referenced 3-DoF poses, inverting the expected supervision-quality hierarchy.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoCompass builds directly on the neural map-matching architecture of OrienterNet [76] and the DINOv2-enhanced variant of OSMLoc [58], but fundamentally rethinks their supervision strategy. While prior cross-view localization methods (OrienterNet, OSMLoc, DiffVL) assume perfectly geo-referenced 3-DoF pose labels, and weakly-supervised approaches like C-BEV or GeoDistill require panoramas or teacher-student setups, AutoCompass shows that single perspective images with only noisy 2D GPS or relative SfM/SLAM poses suffice. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that heading supervision is entirely unnecessary—the BEV-to-map cross-correlation inductive bias is sufficient for accurate orientation prediction—and that chunk-based marginalization around GPS coordinates outperforms direct supervision on optimized poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work significantly lowers the barrier to training accurate neural map matchers by eliminating the need for expensive, accurate geo-referencing of training data, which is a major bottleneck for scaling to new cities or egocentric devices. The architecture-agnostic nature of the losses means they can be dropped into any existing neural map-matching pipeline without modification. Open questions include whether the approach extends to settings with no GPS at all (pure relative poses), how it behaves with systematically biased (rather than random) GPS errors, and whether the chunk radius and relative-pose pairing heuristics need adaptation for very sparse or highly dynamic environments.
