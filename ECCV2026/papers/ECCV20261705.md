## Problem

Existing dual-LoRA style transfer methods independently optimize content and style adapters and fuse them via weight blending, but this introduces complex entanglement between adapters and makes the content–style trade-off highly sensitive to fusion coefficients. The paper further shows that a dedicated content LoRA is both redundant and inferior to training-free structural guidance extracted directly from the content image's internal attention, yet no prior work exploits this observation to simplify the pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a content reference image, a style reference image, and a text prompt formatted as 'A <c> in <s> style' as input, and produces a single stylized RGB image that preserves the content's semantic layout while adopting the style's visual characteristics. The style LoRA is trained on just one style reference image in approximately 3 minutes on a single GPU (rank 64, 200 steps, lr 5e-5), while the content image requires no training at all—its structural guidance is extracted on-the-fly during inference. Inference runs 28 denoising steps on the FLUX backbone with a target prompt guidance value of 5.

## Method

AnyStyle replaces the dual-LoRA paradigm with a single LoRA fine-tuned exclusively on the later single-stream transformer blocks (11–38) of FLUX, targeting the self-attention Q/K/V/output projection matrices under a DreamBooth reconstruction objective, thereby encoding style priors where stylistic representations naturally dominate. For content preservation, a training-free attention modulation mechanism is introduced: at each denoising timestep, the content image is noised and passed through the same DiT, and its self-attention query tensors are extracted and blended into the main generation path's queries via Q_t = β·Q_c + (1−β)·Q_t, while key and value tensors are left unchanged to prevent stylistic leakage. The modulation strength β follows a linear time schedule β = β_max·(t/T) + β_min·(1−t/T) (β_max=0.7, β_min=0.3), applying stronger structural guidance in early high-noise timesteps and relaxing it in later timesteps for stylistic refinement. Ablations confirm that modulating only query tensors (rather than key or value) yields the best joint content–style balance, and the modulation is applied uniformly across all 38 single-stream blocks for holistic structural preservation.

## Results

On the 400 content-style pairs (20 content × 20 style images from B-LoRA, StyleDrop, and EditEval), AnyStyle achieves the best prompt alignment (CLIP-T = 32.56), the best content CLIP score (67.93), and the best style DINO score (0.7147) among all compared methods. It also attains the lowest DS prompt-alignment distance (0.4210) and a competitive content DINO score (0.8382). The style LoRA is trained in ~3 minutes on a single GPU with rank 64 and 200 steps, and inference uses 28 timesteps with β_max = 0.7 and β_min = 0.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyStyle outperforms B-LoRA [10] on every metric, with the largest gains in CLIP-Content (+2.2) and DINO-Style (+0.037). Against UnZipLoRA [32], AnyStyle leads on all five metrics, most notably in CLIP-Content (67.93 vs. 61.91) and DINO-Style (0.7147 vs. 0.6567). StyleID [6] is the only method that beats AnyStyle on two metrics—DS prompt distance (0.3179 vs. 0.4210) and DINO-Content (0.8849 vs. 0.8382)—but it trails AnyStyle substantially on CLIP-T (30.51 vs. 32.56), CLIP-Content (64.90 vs. 67.93), and DINO-Style (0.6634 vs. 0.7147). Compared to the Dual-LoRA ablation baseline, AnyStyle wins on all five metrics, confirming that the single-LoRA + attention-modulation design avoids the entanglement and coefficient-tuning issues of dual-adapter fusion. In the user study (70 participants), AnyStyle also received the highest preference rate over all competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant dual-LoRA paradigm (B-LoRA, UnZipLoRA, ZipLoRA) that independently fine-tunes separate content and style adapters and fuses them at inference. It builds on the DreamBooth fine-tuning objective and the FLUX/DiT rectified-flow architecture, but replaces the content LoRA entirely with a training-free attention modulation mechanism that reuses query tensors recorded from a single denoising pass of the content image. Conceptually, it aligns with the broader trend of training-free structural guidance (FreeControl, P2P, PnP) while showing that this approach is not merely complementary to but strictly superior to a dedicated content adapter for the style transfer task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a simpler, faster pipeline: one LoRA fine-tuned in ~3 minutes per style, no per-content fine-tuning, and a single β parameter that smoothly interpolates between content-dominant and style-dominant outputs. The key open question is whether the single-LoRA-plus-attention-modulation recipe generalizes beyond FLUX to other DiT or U-Net backbones, and whether it handles multi-style or multi-content compositions. The paper's failure cases (complex, non-standard semantics) and the limited evaluation scale (40 images, 400 pairs, single GPU) leave room for broader validation before the approach can be considered a definitive replacement for dual-LoRA methods.
