## Problem

Estimating ball spin in real time during professional ball games is difficult because balls are small, fast, and rotate beyond the Nyquist limit of frame-based cameras, causing motion blur and temporal aliasing. Existing event-based spin estimation methods rely on static wide-angle cameras, which limit spatial resolution on the ball surface and conflate translational motion with rotation, and they apply contrast maximization on a planar projection that misrepresents the geometry of a spinning sphere. This paper addresses all three limitations with an actively tracked, telephoto event-based system and a spherical formulation of contrast maximization.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an asynchronous event stream from a Prophesee EVK4 (320×320 hardware ROI) behind a 50 mm focus-tunable telephoto lens with ~1.8° FoV, plus a continuous 3D ball position stream from a 9-camera BPMS at 200 Hz and 7.5 ms latency. Events are converted to polarity-separated time surfaces (5 ms accumulation for detection, 15 ms for the CNN) and masked by a YOLO-predicted bounding circle. The system outputs a 3D spin vector (magnitude in rpm, axis in the virtual camera frame) with per-component log-variance uncertainty, along with real-time mirror angles and liquid-lens focal power to keep the ball centered and in focus, all at 750 Hz throughput and 3 ms end-to-end latency.

## Method

The core tracking pipeline fuses open-loop 3D position-based servoing (solving a re-projection optimization for galvanometer mirror angles and lens focal power) with closed-loop 2D event-based detection that re-centers the ball and re-initializes the state filter on dropouts. For spin estimation, the key insight is a spherical contrast maximization (s-CMax) formulation: 2D events are lifted to 3D points on a unit sphere via orthographic back-projection, warped by a candidate rotation R(−ω·Δt), and accumulated into a 2D histogram over polar–azimuth coordinates, whose variance is maximized to recover ω. This avoids the depth ambiguities and silhouette-biased contrast of planar IWE. Offline, s-CMax is seeded by an optical-flow initialization (plane-fitting flow lifted to 3D tangential velocities, solved as an overdetermined linear system) and refined by brute-force magnitude search followed by Nelder-Mead axis-and-magnitude optimization. For real-time operation, an uncertainty-aware ResNet-18 CNN trained with a heteroscedastic Gaussian NLL loss on s-CMax pseudo-ground-truth labels predicts the spin vector and its variance from a 15 ms time surface; a GPU-accelerated batch s-CMax then asynchronously refines the magnitude by searching all candidate magnitudes along the CNN-predicted axis in parallel, replacing the magnitude only if the searched candidate yields higher IWE variance.

## Results

On the ball spinner dataset with encoder-based ground truth, s-CMax achieves 1.2±2.3% mean magnitude error and 1.5±2.2° mean axis error, with a runtime of 36.7±19.5 ms. On the ball thrower dataset (in-flight, actively tracked), s-CMax achieves 2.1±2.5% magnitude error and 5.4±3.7° axis error, while a static wide-angle EVS fails entirely (axis errors >59° or no convergence). The online CNN+s-CMax pipeline on held-out table tennis game data yields 8.8±12.4% magnitude error and 6.4±6.7° axis error at the 50% uncertainty percentile, with 3 ms latency and 750 Hz throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the flow-based method of Gossard et al. [16], s-CMax reduces magnitude error from 35.2% to 1.2% (≈29×) and axis error from 37.1° to 1.5° (≈25×) on the spinner dataset; even the paper's own flow-based initialization already halves the magnitude error and cuts axis error by 4× over [16]. Against the planar contrast-maximization method of Nakabayashi et al. [26], s-CMax improves magnitude error from 8.2% to 1.2% (≈6.8×) and axis error from 5.0° to 1.5° (≈3.3×), while running ~8.6× faster (36.7 ms vs. 317.4 ms). On the ball thrower dataset, the GCS+s-CMax combination achieves 2.1% / 5.4° error, whereas the same CMax [26] method on a static wide-angle EVS fails to converge (n/a), and flow-based methods on the static EVS produce axis errors of 59–89°, confirming that active tracking and the spherical formulation are both necessary for reliable in-flight estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrast maximization framework for event cameras (Gallego et al., CVPR 2018/2019) from a planar image projection to a spherical formulation (s-CMax) that models events directly on the ball's curved surface, resolving depth ambiguities and silhouette-bias artifacts inherent in flat projections. It also extends the Saccade Mirror line of active gaze control (Iida, Okumura, Miyashita) from frame-based cameras to event cameras paired with a focus-tunable telephoto lens, and departs from prior event-based spin estimation (Gossard et al., Nakabayashi et al., both CVPR-W 2024) by replacing static wide-angle cameras with actively tracked narrow-field views that decouple translational and rotational motion. The real-time pipeline further builds on uncertainty-aware regression (Kendall & Gal, NeurIPS 2017) and event-based optical flow (Benosman et al., 2014) to create a hybrid learning-plus-optimization estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to achieve reliable, real-time spin estimation of unmodified balls during professional games, with 3 ms latency and 750 Hz throughput across multiple actively controlled views — a capability directly enabling competitive robotic play and high-fidelity sports broadcasting. The spherical contrast maximization formulation is a transferable geometric insight applicable to any spinning-sphere estimation from events, and the dramatic failure of static cameras in the ball-thrower experiment (88.7° axis error vs. 5.4°) validates the core premise that active tracking is not merely helpful but essential for in-flight spin. Open questions include extending the real-time pipeline beyond table tennis to other sports (baseball, tennis, golf), handling balls with more complex or non-repeating surface patterns, and whether the uncertainty-aware fusion strategy generalizes to occlusion-heavy scenarios beyond logo invisibility.
