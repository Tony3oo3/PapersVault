## Problem

Existing multi-task adversarial attacks either target a single task in isolation or jointly degrade all tasks indiscriminately, leaving no mechanism for selectively compromising one high-value task while strictly preserving the performance of all other tasks. This gap is critical in safety-critical systems (e.g., autonomous driving) where an attacker may want to manipulate traffic sign recognition while keeping lane detection intact to evade detection. The paper addresses this by formulating and solving the first systematic framework for stealthy, task-selective adversarial attacks in multi-task learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 288×384 (NYUv2) or 256×512 (Cityscapes) along with per-task ground-truth labels (segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normals, part segmentation, disparity) and produces an adversarial perturbation δ bounded by an Lp norm constraint (L∞: ε∈{2,4,8}/255; L2: ε∈{5,10}; L1: ε∈{10,20}) and a pixel box constraint. The output adversarial image is designed so that the targeted task's loss increases substantially while every non-targeted task's loss remains at or below its clean-input value, verified per-task after each attack iteration.

## Method

SMTA² formulates the stealthy attack as a constrained multi-objective optimization: maximize the targeted task loss subject to per-task preservation inequalities (non-targeted losses ≤ clean losses) and an Lp ball constraint. This is relaxed to a weighted surrogate objective where the targeted task receives a positive weight and each non-targeted task receives a negative weight, turning preservation into a penalty term within a standard gradient-based attack (PGD, IFGSM, or AutoPGD). The key technical contribution is an automated weight optimization strategy (Algorithm 1) that, at every attack step, updates each non-targeted task's weight proportionally to its loss difference (adversarial minus clean), and attenuates the per-task step size by a factor α∈(0,1) once that task's preservation constraint is satisfied, preventing oscillation and stabilizing convergence. This online adaptation eliminates the need for manual trial-and-error weight tuning and scales to larger task counts with only O(m) additional operations per iteration, keeping overall complexity asymptotically equivalent to standard PGD-based attacks.

## Results

On NYUv2 under PGD L∞ (ε=4/255) targeting segmentation, SMTA² Auto drops mIoU from 46.56 to 18.33 while preserving depth aErr at 37.45 (clean: 40.57) and normal mDist at 23.31 (clean: 23.41), whereas the non-stealthy baseline degrades all three tasks (mIoU=17.04, aErr=63.80, mDist=30.97). On Cityscapes under the same setting, SMTA² Auto reduces segmentation mIoU from 54.20 to 17.08 while keeping part segmentation at 52.77 (clean: 51.82) and disparity aErr at 81.21 (clean: 81.51). Under adversarial training, SMTA² maintains comparable stealthy behavior: at ε=4/255 on NYUv2, targeted mIoU drops to 30.99 while non-targeted tasks remain within ~1% of clean performance. The automated weight optimization matches or slightly outperforms manual tuning across all settings with substantially lower tuning overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SMTA² against non-stealthy multi-task attacks derived from Guo et al. [17] and Zhang et al. [41], which jointly degrade all tasks without preservation constraints. On NYUv2 at ε=4/255 targeting segmentation, the non-stealthy baseline achieves a slightly stronger targeted attack (mIoU 17.04 vs. 18.33 for SMTA² Auto) but at the cost of severe collateral damage: depth aErr inflates from 40.57 to 63.80 and normal mDist from 23.41 to 30.97. SMTA² Auto, by contrast, keeps depth at 37.45 and normal at 23.31—within 1–2% of clean performance—while still reducing segmentation mIoU by 60%. On Cityscapes at ε=8/255 targeting segmentation, non-stealthy drops part segmentation mIoU to 19.24 and disparity aErr to 164.19, whereas SMTA² Auto preserves part segmentation at 52.07 (clean: 51.82) and disparity at 81.50 (clean: 81.51) while reducing targeted mIoU to 10.02. The automated weight optimization (Algorithm 1) consistently matches or marginally outperforms the manual search strategy (e.g., mIoU 18.33 vs. 18.30 at ε=4/255 on NYUv2) while eliminating the need for iterative hyperparameter tuning, making it the practically preferred variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-task adversarial attack (MTA) line of work initiated by Guo et al. [17] and standardized by Zhang et al. [41], which craft perturbations that jointly degrade all tasks through shared representations. The key departure is the introduction of explicit preservation constraints on non-targeted tasks, reframing the attack as a constrained multi-objective optimization problem rather than an unconstrained joint loss maximization. The underlying optimization engines (PGD, IFGSM, APGD) are standard, and the automated weight-tuning mechanism is a practical addition rather than a new optimization paradigm. The work also distinguishes itself from Zhe et al. [43], who exploit 'hidden tasks' via architectural modifications, by operating on unmodified multi-task architectures with standard task definitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that stealthy, task-selective adversarial attacks are feasible in multi-task systems, which has direct safety implications for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving where an attacker could compromise one perception channel while leaving others intact to evade system-level monitoring. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets with only three tasks each, and the 'preservation' criterion (adversarial loss ≤ clean loss) is sometimes satisfied by slight improvements rather than exact preservation, raising questions about whether the constraint is meaningfully binding. Open questions include scalability to many-task settings (e.g., 10+ tasks), physical-world transferability of the selective perturbations, and whether dedicated defenses can detect the asymmetric degradation pattern that distinguishes SMTA² from natural noise.
