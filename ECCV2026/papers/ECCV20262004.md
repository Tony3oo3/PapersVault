## Problem

Directly applying SAM 3 to endoscopic video polyp segmentation suffers from severe domain shift, loss of boundary-sensitive cues under standard downscaled encoding, and unstable temporal propagation that accumulates errors across frames. Existing SAM-based adaptations either require costly full fine-tuning or introduce heavy side networks that fail to jointly address low-contrast camouflage and temporal drift in colonoscopy videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262004/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes endoscopic video frames resized to 352×352, processed in clips of length L=6, under two standardized protocols: fully automatic (no prompts) or first-frame prompted (one positive point at t=0 sampled from ground-truth foreground). It outputs per-frame binary polyp segmentation masks. The SAM 3 perception encoder is entirely frozen; only the SAFE and STRF modules (totaling +3.7M parameters over the 473M backbone) are trained, preserving SAM 3's inference characteristics.

## Method

FST-SAM3 introduces two complementary lightweight modules around a frozen SAM 3 backbone. On the encoder side, the Structure-Aware Frequency Enhancement (SAFE) module combines a Frequency-Spectrum Adapter (FSA) that extracts high-frequency residuals via FFT with a binary high-pass mask (r=0.25) and a GAP+GMP channel recalibration branch, with an Attention Residual Compensation (ARC) block that recovers high-frequency details attenuated by stacked attention layers using StarReLU-conditioned modulation. On the decoder side, the Spatio-Temporal Token Refinement Framework (STRF) constructs a spatial token by aggregating K=3 multi-scale encoder features through 1×1 convolutions, global average pooling, and multi-head attention, then updates a temporal token via quality-aware gating that modulates write-back based on the decoder's IoU-head confidence and an inter-frame change score; the resulting fusion token replaces the standard mask token in the SAM 3 decoder. Training uses a joint objective of weighted BCE + weighted IoU segmentation loss plus a margin-based cosine temporal consistency loss (λ=0.1, δ=0.3) over 15 epochs with AdamW.

## Results

Under the fully automatic (0-point) protocol, FST-SAM3 achieves Dice of 89.53 (SUN-SEG-Easy), 89.39 (SUN-SEG-Hard), and 94.40 (CVC-612), outperforming the best task-specific baseline STDDNet by +3.67, +4.80, and +4.01 Dice points respectively. Under the first-frame prompted (1-point at t=0) protocol, FST-SAM3 reaches Dice of 90.12, 90.01, and 94.50, surpassing the strongest SAM-based baseline VP-SAM by +2.56, +4.73, and +2.17 Dice points. The largest single gain is over zero-shot SAM 3 on SUN-SEG-Hard, where Dice improves from 75.17 to 90.01 (+14.84). Efficiency-wise, FST-SAM3 adds only +3.7M parameters and +49.82 GFLOPs over frozen SAM 3, at the cost of FPS dropping from 24.59 to 16.43 on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FST-SAM3 outperforms all ten baselines across every metric and dataset under both protocols. Against task-specific methods (0-point protocol), the closest competitor is STDDNet (Dice 84.59 on SUN-SEG-Hard), which FST-SAM3 surpasses by +4.80 Dice; ZoomNeXt and SALI trail by 2.5–3.2 Dice on SUN-SEG-Hard. Against SAM-based methods (1-point protocol), VP-SAM is the strongest baseline (Dice 85.28 on SUN-SEG-Hard), beaten by +4.73 Dice; CamSAM2 and MedSAM2 lag further behind at 80.66 and 77.42 respectively. The most dramatic improvement is over zero-shot SAM 3, which scores only 75.17 Dice on SUN-SEG-Hard versus FST-SAM3's 90.01 (+14.84), confirming that the SAFE and STRF modules effectively bridge the domain gap. On CVC-612, gains are more modest (1–4 Dice points) since the dataset is smaller and less challenging, but FST-SAM3 still leads all methods on all four metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

FST-SAM3 builds directly on SAM 3 as a frozen backbone, positioning itself against prior SAM-based medical adaptations (MediViSTA-SAM, VP-SAM, Medical SAM3, SAM3-Adapter) that either require costly full fine-tuning, add heavy side networks, or operate frame-by-frame without explicit temporal modeling. On the encoder side, the SAFE module draws on frequency-domain analysis (EVP, FDAM) and attention residual compensation to counteract the low-pass smoothing inherent in stacked Transformer attention; on the decoder side, STRF introduces a quality-gated token update mechanism that has no direct analogue in prior SAM-based video segmentation. The paper also situates its work relative to task-specific VPS methods (SALI, STDDNet, MAST, ZoomNeXt, PNS+), arguing that their limited backbone capacity constrains performance under severe camouflage, and that foundation-model priors can close this gap when properly adapted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen SAM 3 backbone can be adapted to endoscopic video polyp segmentation with only ~3.7M additional parameters, yielding a +14.84 Dice gain over the unmodified model on SUN-SEG-Hard, which is practically significant for clinical deployment where retraining large models is infeasible. However, the FPS drops from 24.59 to 16.43 (a ~33% reduction), and the method still fails on flat, highly camouflaged lesions with weak boundary and motion cues, as well as under severe instrument occlusion. Open questions include whether the quality-aware gating in STRF generalizes to longer sequences or more rapid motion, whether the frequency enhancement transfers to other endoscopic modalities (e.g., upper GI), and how to reduce the runtime overhead without sacrificing temporal stability.
