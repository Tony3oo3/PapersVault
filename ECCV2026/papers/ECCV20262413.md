## Problem

VGGT's global attention incurs quadratic cost in the number of views, limiting scalability to long sequences. More fundamentally, the paper reveals that simply increasing the number of input views without sufficient viewpoint diversity degrades reconstruction quality, as redundant views introduce highly similar tokens that dilute informative geometric signals in the attention mechanism. Existing efficiency-oriented VGGT variants address computation but not this view-distribution problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262413/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered collection of multi-view RGB images (no temporal ordering or camera intrinsics required) and produces per-frame camera poses (SE(3) extrinsics and intrinsics), dense depth maps, and 3D point maps in a shared global coordinate frame. A shared anchor frame is inserted at position 0 of every chunk to enable post-hoc alignment without global optimization, and the framework is training-free and plug-and-play, applying identically to VGGT, LiteVGGT, FasterVGGT, and π³ without modifying model internals.

## Method

The framework reorganizes input views into balanced chunks that maximize within-chunk viewpoint diversity before VGGT inference. It first computes pairwise visual dissimilarity from mean-pooled DINOv2 patch embeddings, then solves a balanced K-way graph partitioning problem via a Kernighan-Lin local swap heuristic that maximizes total intra-chunk diversity. To incorporate spatial information without full pose estimation, it runs VGGT on a single reference chunk and propagates pseudo-poses to remaining frames through visual-similarity-weighted averaging of translation vectors. The resulting spatial dispersion matrix is combined with visual dissimilarity to refine the partition. Each chunk is then processed independently by VGGT, and all predictions are aligned to a common coordinate frame via the shared anchor frame's predicted pose, requiring no additional global optimization.

## Results

On 7Scenes camera pose estimation at 1000 frames, the proposed method with VGGT* achieves AUC@30 of 0.8274 (vs. 0.8108 for vanilla VGGT*), while reducing VRAM from 28.41 GB to 16.99 GB and latency from 149.68 s to 49.09 s at 500 frames (up to 6.34× speedup). At 1000 frames, FasterVGGT runs out of memory on an A100 80 GB, whereas the proposed method enables all three VGGT variants to complete inference successfully. On long-sequence SLAM (TUM-RGBD), the method achieves ATE of 3.94 cm, reducing error by 3.4× over VGGT-Long (13.44 cm) at 6.32 FPS throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla VGGT*, the proposed method consistently improves AUC@30 (e.g., 0.8274 vs. 0.8108 at 1000 frames on 7Scenes) while cutting VRAM by up to 3.8× and latency by up to 6.34×, demonstrating that better view organization yields both higher accuracy and lower cost. Against FasterVGGT (sparse attention) and LiteVGGT (token merging), the method improves accuracy on long sequences (e.g., AUC@30: 0.8087 vs. 0.7610 for LiteVGGT at 1000 frames) and reduces VRAM by up to 3.5×; critically, it rescues FasterVGGT from OOM at 1000 frames on an A100 80 GB. On long-sequence SLAM (TUM-RGBD), the method achieves ATE of 3.94 cm, outperforming VGGT-Long (13.44 cm, 3.4× worse), MUSt3R-C (6.43 cm), and Spann3R (38.74 cm) while running at 6.32 FPS. On outdoor reconstruction (Tanks&Temples), it achieves F1@τ×10 of 0.74, surpassing VGGT-Long (0.63), MUSt3R-C (0.56), and Spann3R (0.37). For generalization beyond VGGT, applying the method to π³ preserves AUC@30 (0.9717 vs. 0.9745) while reducing VRAM by 3.6× (7.95 vs. 28.96 GB), outperforming both FasterVGGT (0.9423) and FastVGGT (0.9614) applied to the same architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VGGT and its efficiency-oriented variants (LiteVGGT, FasterVGGT, FastVGGT, VGGT-Long), which address the quadratic attention cost through token merging, sparse attention, or temporal chunking. It departs from these by identifying a distinct bottleneck—viewpoint redundancy causing attention dilution—and formulating view organization as a combinatorial graph partitioning problem (Kernighan-Lin local search) over visual dissimilarity and spatial dispersion. Unlike VGGT-Long's sequential chunking with loop closure, the method requires no temporal ordering and operates as a training-free, plug-and-play preprocessing step that composes with existing efficiency variants rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that adding more views can degrade VGGT's reconstruction quality challenges the default assumption in multi-view geometry that more observations are always beneficial, and the attention-entropy analysis provides a mechanistic explanation (dilution by redundant tokens). The practical impact is significant: the method enables 1000-frame inference on an 80 GB GPU where FasterVGGT runs out of memory, with 3–6× latency and 3–4× VRAM reductions while maintaining or improving accuracy. Open questions include whether the diversity principle generalizes to next-generation multi-view architectures beyond the VGGT family, how the soft pose propagation degrades in highly dynamic or textureless scenes, and whether the partitioning objective can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on frozen DINOv2 features.
