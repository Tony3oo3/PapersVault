## Problem

While multiple Sim2Real techniques—domain randomization, photorealistic rendering, physics-realistic modeling, and RL fine-tuning—have been proposed for robotic manipulation, their individual contributions to zero-shot transfer of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models remain uncharacterized. Prior studies evaluate these methods in isolation on general control tasks, leaving it unclear which factors actually drive real-world generalization for large VLA policies and how they interact.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262236/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a single 640×480 RGB frame from a forward-facing Intel RealSense D435 camera together with a natural-language task instruction, and outputs a short-horizon sequence of M+1 continuous action vectors (target joint angles and gripper states) predicted in a single parallel decoding pass via L1 regression. Evaluation spans five dual-arm manipulation tasks on a Piper robot platform, with over 10,000 real-world trials under controlled variations in background texture, lighting, object instances, distractor count (2/4/8), and spatial position on a 3×3 grid.

## Method

The paper conducts a factorized empirical study using OpenVLA-OFT (Llama2-7B backbone with parallel action-chunk decoding) trained in RoboTwin 2.0 simulation, decomposing domain randomization into five structured factors—background, table distractor, camera pose, lighting, and table height—and systematically ablating each factor's contribution under both Sim-OOD and zero-shot real-world evaluation. Randomization granularity is compared at episode-wise versus frame-wise temporal resolution, and rendering fidelity is varied along two axes: photorealism (ray-tracing activation, samples per pixel, path depth) and physical realism (gravity, friction, restitution). On top of supervised fine-tuning, the authors apply RL fine-tuning via GRPO (following SimpleVLA-RL) with a DAPO-style modification that removes the KL penalty, comparing clean RL, RL under domain randomization, and SFT-only baselines. The key finding is that spatial randomization (table height, camera pose) provides the largest Sim2Real gains, frame-wise randomization outperforms episode-wise, moderate photorealism captures most transferable visual cues, and RL+DR yields the strongest robustness to distribution shifts.

## Results

Across five bimanual manipulation tasks, clean (no randomization) simulation training yields very low zero-shot real-world success rates (0–26.2%), while applying all domain randomization factors simultaneously raises real-world success to 23.8–63.1% (e.g., 49.7% on Click Bell, 63.1% on Stack Bowls Two). Spatial factors (table height, camera pose) dominate: TH alone lifts Click Bell from 2.7% to 36.9% real success, and CP from 2.7% to 23.5%. RL fine-tuning further amplifies gains: average real-world success improves from 5.6% (SFT) to 33.4% (SFT+RL) to 42.8% (SFT+RL+DR), with Sim-OOD reaching 70.8% under the full pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the 'baselines' are different training configurations. Spatial randomization (TH, CP) consistently outperforms appearance randomization (BG, LT, TD) by 10–30+ percentage points on real-world success; for example, TH alone achieves 36.9% on Click Bell versus 11.5% for BG. Combining spatial with appearance factors (TH+CP+BG) further improves over spatial-only, and the full five-factor setting yields the best DR results. Frame-wise randomization beats episode-wise by 3–8% for CP and up to ~13% for BG on real-world success, while lighting granularity has negligible effect (0–2%). In the RL comparison (Table 3), SFT+RL under clean simulation already matches or exceeds SFT+DR from Table 1 (e.g., 36.9% vs 49.7% on Click Bell real), and SFT+RL+DR achieves the highest overall performance (50.8% Click Bell, 64.6% Stack Bowls Two real), surpassing SFT+RL alone by roughly 10–15 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on OpenVLA-OFT as the base VLA architecture and SimpleVLA-RL's GRPO formulation for RL fine-tuning, conducting all simulation training within the RoboTwin 2.0 framework. It departs from prior Sim2Real work—which has typically evaluated domain randomization, photorealistic rendering, and RL fine-tuning in isolation or on conventional control policies—by performing a factorized, controlled empirical study specifically targeting VLA models under a unified zero-shot protocol. The paper positions itself as filling a gap it explicitly identifies: the lack of principled, grounded research on which Sim2Real factors actually drive transfer for generalist VLA policies in real-world manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factorized findings (spatial randomization dominates appearance; frame-wise beats episode-wise; RL alone approaches SFT+DR) give practitioners concrete, actionable guidance for allocating simulation budget when training VLA policies for real deployment. The open benchmark platform with 10k+ real-world trials addresses a reproducibility gap in Sim2Real evaluation that the field has struggled with. Key limitations include evaluation on a single VLA architecture (OpenVLA-OFT), a single robot platform (Piper), five tasks, and a single RGB camera, leaving open whether the factor rankings generalize to other model families, multi-sensor setups, or longer-horizon tasks. The diminishing returns of photorealism beyond a moderate level also raise the question of where the true Sim2Real bottleneck lies for next-generation VLA models.
