## Problem

Monocular 3D lane detection methods that model road geometry via sparse slope anchors (e.g., HeightLane, SC-Lane) suffer from geometric misalignment on non-planar roads, especially at long range where anchor spacing widens. Additionally, prior height-map-based approaches exploit the recovered geometry only for spatial transformation rather than feeding semantic cues back into the lane decoding process, leaving a gap between geometric understanding and lane perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261968/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular front-view image resized to 600×800 and produces 3D lane instances (segmentation map, lateral offsets, and embeddings clustered into lane curves) along with a dense per-cell road height map on a 200×48 BEV grid spanning x∈[3 m, 103 m] and y∈[−12 m, 12 m] at 0.5 m resolution. Unlike per-lane-point height regression used by query-based methods, the height map is supervised over the entire BEV grid, and an auxiliary lane-existence heatmap is also predicted from the rendered surface features.

## Method

HSDF-Lane replaces sparse slope anchors with dense vertical sampling (up to 13 points per BEV cell, adaptively allocated by a target resolution δ_z) to build a 3D feature volume, which an MLP with positional encoding maps to a Height-aligned Signed Distance Field (HSDF) representing the vertical signed distance to the road surface. A differentiable soft-argmin rendering (softmax over |HSDF|/τ with adaptive temperature) simultaneously extracts a continuous height map and surface-aligned features, refined by zero-initialized residual convolutions for spatial smoothness. A Height-directional Eikonal loss (∂s/∂z = 1 via finite differences) enforces physical consistency of the distance field. Lane-aware Semantic Positional Encoding (LSPE) predicts a lane heatmap from the rendered features, modulates a single learnable vector by the heatmap probability, and adds the resulting semantic embedding to the height-based geometric positional encoding, giving transformer queries a lane-existence prior that couples geometry with semantics during deformable cross-attention decoding.

## Results

On the OpenLane benchmark, HSDF-Lane (ResNet-50) achieves a state-of-the-art F1-score of 66.3% for 3D lane detection, outperforming the previous best SparseLaneSTP (66.1%) and SC-Lane (64.3%). The FPN-equipped variant HSDF-Lane† further improves to 66.9% F1-score with the best near-range X-error (0.186 m). For road height estimation, HSDF-Lane† achieves the lowest MAE (0.149 m) and highest threshold accuracies (Acc@0.1 = 52.7%, Acc@0.2 = 77.6%), surpassing both HeightLane and SC-Lane on most metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSDF-Lane outperforms all anchor-based height-map methods: it beats HeightLane [32] by 3.6 F1 points (66.3 vs 62.7) and SC-Lane [31] by 2.0 points (66.3 vs 64.3), with consistent gains in both near- and far-range X-errors. Against the strongest query-based competitor SparseLaneSTP [36], HSDF-Lane edges ahead on F1 (66.3 vs 66.1) and far-range X-error (0.223 vs 0.240 m), but trails on Z-error (0.114 vs 0.092 m far-range), which the authors attribute to the different supervision strategy (dense BEV height map vs. per-lane-point regression). Compared to GLane3D-B [29], HSDF-Lane gains 2.4 F1 points and a 0.011 m improvement in far-range X-error, though GLane3D-B retains a slight edge in near-range X-error (0.193 vs 0.201 m). For height estimation, HSDF-Lane† achieves the best MAE (0.149 m) and threshold accuracies, but SC-Lane [31] reports a lower RMSE (0.259 vs 0.272 m) due to its temporal consistency loss suppressing outliers at the cost of representable height range. The FPN variant HSDF-Lane† further narrows the gap, achieving the best F1 (66.9%) and near-range X-error (0.186 m) among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the height-map-based paradigm introduced by HeightLane and SC-Lane, which replaced flat-ground BEV assumptions with explicit road elevation modeling. The key architectural departure is replacing sparse, slope-dependent anchor regression with a dense 3D feature volume modeled by a Height-aligned Signed Distance Field (HSDF) and rendered via differentiable soft-argmin, drawing on implicit surface representations from the NeRF/SDF literature but restricting the distance field to the vertical axis. The Lane-aware Semantic Positional Encoding (LSPE) is a novel coupling mechanism that injects a predicted lane-existence heatmap into transformer query positional encodings, a design not present in prior height-map or query-based lane detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HSDF formulation offers a more physically grounded and densely supervised alternative to anchor-based height regression, yielding consistent gains in both height estimation accuracy and 3D lane detection on OpenLane. However, the method still requires dense LiDAR-derived height-map supervision during training, operates on a fixed BEV grid without explicit drivable-area segmentation, and is limited to a single monocular frame without temporal or multi-view extensions. Open questions include whether the dense vertical sampling and SDF rendering can be made efficient enough for real-time deployment, and whether the LSPE semantic prior generalizes beyond the specific lane-heatmap formulation used here.
