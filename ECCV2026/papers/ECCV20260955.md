## Problem

Existing semantic matching approaches based on 2D foundation models rely on independent pixel-level nearest-neighbor search, which fundamentally cannot guarantee manifold preservation, disambiguate symmetric structures, or reason about cross-image invisibility due to occlusion. While some methods incorporate 3D priors for geometric awareness and others enforce global transformations for manifold consistency, no prior work simultaneously provides geometry-aware descriptors and a holistic dense matching mechanism in a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of source and target RGB images depicting same-category instances and produces dense bidirectional correspondence grids (2×H×W, coordinates normalized to [-1,1] following the grid_sample convention) along with per-pixel confidence maps (H×W, values in [0,1]). The grids directly parameterize image warping via grid_sample, enabling cycle-consistency evaluation, while the confidence maps serve as a reliability reference for downstream tasks to selectively adopt high-confidence correspondences and identify non-corresponding pixels.

## Method

The architecture reuses VGGT's first 4 transformer blocks (frozen) to extract geometry-grounded tokens, fine-tunes the remaining 20 blocks as a semantic branch, and appends a DPT-based matching head that predicts bidirectional sampling grids and confidence maps from features at blocks [4,11,17,23]. Training follows a four-stage progressive recipe: (i) synthetic-data pretraining with dense grid supervision, (ii) real-data adaptation with sparse keypoint L2 loss, (iii) matching refinement via cycle-consistent losses (matching-reconstruction consistency weighted by confidence, plus error-confidence correlation), and (iv) uncertainty learning. A synthetic data pipeline generates dense correspondences by rendering Trellis-generated 3D assets from multiple viewpoints and synthesizing diverse textures with FLUX conditioned on Canny edges and depth maps. A smoothness loss penalizes adjacent grid coordinate differences to mitigate aliasing artifacts inherent in predicting continuous coordinates for discrete pixels.

## Results

On SPair-71k, the proposed method achieves PCK@0.1 of 84.2 and PCK@0.05* (symmetric keypoints) of 77.8, outperforming the strongest trained baseline Geo-SC (S.) by 1.3 and 4.3 points respectively. On AP-10k, it reaches 73.2/71.4/60.9 PCK@0.1 for intra-species/cross-species/cross-family splits. For dense matching, the method achieves a Synthetic Dense MSE of 0.07 and FID of 3.58, dramatically outperforming all baselines (next best: SpaceJAM at 0.08 MSE and 23.06 FID), confirming superior manifold preservation. On cross-image invisibility reasoning, the method attains a mean AUC of 0.92 across rotation angles, exceeding SpaceJAM (0.81) and ASIC (0.78).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest sparse-matching baseline Geo-SC (S.) (82.9 PCK@0.1), it gains 1.3 points on SPair-71k but achieves dramatically better dense matching (0.07 vs 0.26 Synthetic Dense MSE) and manifold preservation (FID 3.58 vs 128.70), exposing Geo-SC's 'keypoint overfitting' failure mode. Against geometry-aware methods DIY-SC (71.6) and SPH (64.4), the advantage is 12.6 and 19.8 PCK@0.1 points respectively, with large gaps on symmetric keypoints (77.8 vs 53.8 and 49.3). Against the global-alignment method SpaceJAM (44.5 PCK@0.1), the method wins by 39.7 points on sparse matching while also achieving better dense MSE (0.07 vs 0.08) and FID (3.58 vs 23.06), confirming that holistic dense matching surpasses rigid global warps. Zero-shot baselines (SD+DINO, DistillDIFT) trail by 24–29 PCK@0.1 points and show the worst dense matching quality. On cross-image invisibility (Table 2), the method's mean AUC of 0.92 exceeds SpaceJAM (0.81) and ASIC (0.78), and its confidence-calibrated MSE (0.091 in the 0–60 bin) is the lowest across all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of adapting 2D foundation model features (DINO, Stable Diffusion) with nearest-neighbor search or pseudo-3D fine-tuning (DIY-SC, SPH, DistillDIFT) by instead repurposing a native 3D geometric foundation model (VGGT) as the backbone for semantic matching. It also shifts from point-wise retrieval to holistic dense matching via predicted sampling grids, addressing the manifold-preservation gap that global-alignment methods (SpaceJAM, ASIC) fill at the cost of local non-rigidity. The synthetic-to-real training pipeline draws on the broader 3D generation literature (Trellis, FLUX) to overcome the longstanding scarcity of dense correspondence annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'keypoint overfitting' trap—where high sparse PCK (Geo-SC: 82.9) coexists with catastrophic dense matching failure (FID 128.7 vs. 3.58 for the proposed method)—challenges the field's reliance on sparse keypoint metrics as a proxy for dense correspondence quality. The synthetic-to-real pipeline offers a reusable recipe for generating dense correspondence labels, potentially benefiting related tasks like 3D registration and category-level pose estimation. Open questions include multi-view consistent semantic matching, integration of additional foundation models beyond DINOv2, and whether the VGGT-based paradigm generalizes to more extreme viewpoint changes and occlusions.
