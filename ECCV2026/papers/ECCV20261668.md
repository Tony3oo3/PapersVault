## Problem

Blind 3D scene reconstruction from extremely motion-blurred images suffers from a fundamental chicken-and-egg paradox: accurate deblurring requires precise camera poses, yet pose estimation from blurred images requires a deblurred scene to establish correspondences. Existing 3DGS-based deblurring methods either depend on COLMAP (which fails beyond moderate blur as high-frequency features are obliterated) or assume impractical sharp-image supervision. Even when deep trackers like VGGSfM recover global topology, the resulting sparse geometry causes deterministic 3DGS optimization to diverge into local minima, creating an 'Initialization-Optimization Gap' that no prior work has bridged.

## Input / Output

The standalone PRISM3D pipeline consumes only motion-blurred RGB images (20–40 training views, 4–6 test views per scene, with blur up to 11-frame averaging) and requires no sharp references or external pose estimates. The multi-modal PRISM3D-E extension additionally ingests asynchronous event streams, which are binned into 13 discrete temporal bins and processed via the EDI model to synthesize pseudo-sharp images exclusively for bootstrapping VGGSfM initialization—these synthesized images are never used for photometric supervision. The output is a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (optimized with 15 virtual camera samples along the exposure path) that enables sharp novel view synthesis, alongside the recovered SE(3) Bezier camera trajectory with 9 control points.

## Method

PRISM3D couples three theoretically grounded components into a single differentiable pipeline. First, VGGSfM (a Transformer-based deep dense tracker with Theseus-solver bundle adjustment) replaces brittle COLMAP to recover a geometrically consistent but sparse topological skeleton from severely degraded inputs. Second, 3DGS-MCMC treats Gaussian primitives as samples from an underlying scene distribution and updates them via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics, enabling probabilistic exploration that robustly populates structural gaps without the rigid density-control heuristics of standard 3DGS. Third, camera motion is parameterized as a continuous SE(3) Bezier curve (degree M with 9 control points), and the blur is modeled as a differentiable integral of scene radiance along this trajectory, so gradients flow jointly through both the probabilistic scene parameters and the trajectory control points. The multi-modal PRISM3D-E extension routes EDI-deblurred event images through VGGSfM to secure a strictly superior initialization, while the final MCMC densification and Bezier trajectory optimization remain driven entirely by the raw blurred RGB captures and the physical blur formation model, preserving photometric fidelity.

## Results

On the PRISM3D-E Benchmark (8 synthetic scenes), PRISM3D achieves an average PSNR of 29.49 dB, SSIM of 0.840, and LPIPS of 0.153 using only motion-blurred RGB inputs, outperforming the best blind single-image deblurring baselines (Restormer: 26.83 dB) by ~2.7 dB. The multi-modal extension PRISM3D-E reaches 31.95 dB PSNR and 0.908 SSIM, surpassing all sharp-supervised oracle baselines (Deblur-GS*: 31.43, ExBluRF*: 31.15, BAD-Gaussians*: 30.95) by 0.5–1.0 dB. In terms of robustness, PRISM3D maintains 32.22 dB PSNR at 11-frame extreme blur where BAD-Gaussians fails entirely, and completes optimization in 7 minutes with ~1.55 GiB GPU memory versus 7–15 hours and ~15 GiB for event-based NeRF baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM3D (blind RGB) outperforms both single-image deblurring baselines—MPRNet (26.19 dB) and Restormer (26.83 dB)—by 2.7–3.3 dB PSNR, confirming that 3D-aware reconstruction is essential for extreme blur. Among event-assisted methods, PRISM3D-E surpasses E2NeRF (29.35 dB) by 2.6 dB and EBAD-NeRF (28.36 dB) by 3.6 dB, while also beating EDI+3DGS (27.55 dB) by 4.4 dB. Notably, PRISM3D-E (31.95 dB) exceeds all three oracle baselines that rely on sharp-image supervision: Deblur-GS* (31.43 dB, +0.5 dB), ExBluRF* (31.15 dB, +0.8 dB), and BAD-Gaussians* (30.95 dB, +1.0 dB). In the robustness comparison (Table 2), PRISM3D maintains 35.33 dB at 7-frame blur versus BAD-Gaussians' 30.99 dB, and continues to 32.22 dB at 11-frame blur where BAD-Gaussians fails completely. As alternative initializations, HLOC (28.04 dB) and VGGT-X (27.82 dB) underperform VGGSfM-based PRISM3D by 1.45 and 1.67 dB respectively, validating the choice of deep dense tracking over feature matching or feed-forward 3D foundation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM3D positions itself at the intersection of three established threads: deep SfM (VGGSfM) for robust pose recovery under degradation, probabilistic scene densification (3DGS-MCMC) to replace brittle heuristic cloning, and physics-consistent trajectory modeling (Bezier curves from Deblur-GS, joint optimization from BAD-Gaussians). The key conceptual departure is identifying and explicitly bridging the 'Initialization-Optimization Gap': prior blind deblurring methods (BAD-Gaussians, ExBluRF, Deblur-GS) all implicitly assume COLMAP-quality initialization, which collapses under extreme blur, while 3D Foundation Models (VGGT, VGGT-X) produce dense but noisy geometry that traps 3DGS in local minima. The multi-modal extension (PRISM3D-E) repurposes the EDI event-deblurring model not as a rendering loss but purely as a front-end to synthesize pseudo-sharp images for VGGSfM, a usage pattern distinct from prior event-assisted 3DGS methods (EvaGaussians, E2GS) that feed events into COLMAP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important regime (handheld photography, robotics) where standard SfM pipelines simply fail, and provides the first end-to-end 3DGS pipeline that survives 11-frame extreme blur without any sharp-image supervision. The PRISM3D-E Benchmark is a timely contribution given the growing interest in event-camera-assisted reconstruction, though its synthetic nature (ESIM-generated events from ExBluRF sequences) limits immediate real-world validation. Open questions include whether the framework extends to dynamic scenes (the authors note this as future work), whether fine-tuning VGGSfM on blurred data would further close the gap, and whether the 3DFM-vs-deep-SfM initialization trade-off persists as foundation models improve. The 7-minute training time versus 7–15 hours for NeRF-based baselines also makes the approach practically deployable.
