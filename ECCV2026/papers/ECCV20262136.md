## Problem

Existing plant phenotyping datasets provide either static instance masks without temporal ordering (CVPPP) or small-scale tracking sequences with limited genetic diversity (Komatsuna, 900 images), but none simultaneously offer temporally ordered imagery, consistent per-leaf identity labels across frames, and sufficient scale to jointly benchmark segmentation, tracking, and growth stage classification. This gap prevents systematic evaluation of whether a model can segment individual leaves, maintain their identity through continuous growth, and classify developmental stage within a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises 17,082 top-down RGB images (~530×525 px) of 318 Arabidopsis replicates across 21 genotypes, captured at 2–4 frames per day from ~14 days post-sowing to mature rosette. Segmentation and classification consume a single RGB image; tracking processes the full chronological sequence of one plant replicate. A distinctive design choice is the fixed 31-colour palette encoding leaf identity: each leaf receives a deterministic colour at first appearance that persists across all subsequent frames, enabling direct label comparison without Hungarian matching.

## Method

The core contribution is the PhenoLeaf-TS dataset with its deterministic colour-coded annotation protocol (12 annotators, 4 reviewers, frame-to-frame bootstrapping with fixed leaf IDs) and three standardised benchmark tasks with defined evaluation metrics (COCO mAP/SBD for segmentation; MOTA/IDF1/HOTA/IDSW for tracking; accuracy/macro-F1/MCC for classification). Twenty-one models are evaluated under a unified 70/15/15 frame-level split: nine instance segmentation architectures (YOLOv11/26-seg, Mask R-CNN R50/R101/X101, Cascade Mask R-CNN, Mask2Former, SAM2, SAM3), six multi-object trackers operating in tracking-by-detection mode using YOLOv11-seg detections, and six classifiers (Swin-T, EfficientNetV2-S, ResNet-50/101, ViT-B-16, DINOv2-B). Cross-dataset transfer experiments on CVPPP (Ara2012, Ara2013) and Komatsuna demonstrate that PhenoLeaf-TS weights serve as effective pre-training, yielding up to +51 mAP improvement on cross-species segmentation after fine-tuning.

## Results

On the PhenoLeaf-TS test set, Mask R-CNN with R50-FPN achieves the best leaf instance segmentation at 73.2 mAP (94.3 mAP@50, 82.9 mAP@75), followed by Mask2Former Swin-L (71.9 mAP) and YOLOv11-seg (68.2 mAP). For leaf tracking, ByteTrack reaches 84.1% MOTA and 91.0% IDF1, narrowly ahead of BoTSORT (83.1% MOTA). For growth stage classification, Swin-T achieves 91.7% accuracy and 0.873 MCC, with EfficientNetV2-S close behind at 91.4%. Cross-dataset fine-tuning from PhenoLeaf-TS weights yields up to +51.4 mAP on Komatsuna (34.7 → 86.1 mAP for Mask R-CNN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine segmentation models, Mask R-CNN R50-FPN (73.2 mAP) outperforms all baselines, with a notable finding that increasing backbone capacity degrades performance (R50 > R101 > X101), suggesting data diversity rather than model capacity is the bottleneck at ~17K images. Mask2Former Swin-L matches Cascade Mask R-CNN at 71.9 mAP and leads at mAP@50 (94.6), while zero-shot SAM2 trails by 18.2 points (55.0 mAP); text-prompted SAM3 recovers most of this gap to 71.1 mAP. YOLOv11-seg (68.2 mAP) and YOLO26-seg (67.3 mAP) trail the best two-stage model by ~5 points but offer real-time inference. For tracking, ByteTrack (84.1% MOTA, 91.0% IDF1) beats BoTSORT (83.1%) and substantially outperforms DeepSORT (68.4%), Deep-OC-SORT (66.1%), StrongSORT (64.2%), and Norfair (57.8%); however, DeepSORT produces far fewer identity switches (43 vs. 590) at the cost of 15-point lower recall. For classification, Swin-T (91.7%) and EfficientNetV2-S (91.4%) are nearly tied, both outperforming ResNet-101 (89.7%), ResNet-50 (89.4%), DINOv2-B frozen (87.1%), and ViT-B-16 (85.1%), with no model confusing Early and Mature stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of plant phenotyping datasets and multi-task CV benchmarking. Prior datasets each cover a subset of the needed capabilities: CVPPP provides instance masks without temporal ordering, Komatsuna offers tracking annotations at limited scale (900 images, one species), and PhenoBench targets panoptic field-level segmentation without per-leaf identities. PhenoLeaf-TS unifies these by introducing temporally consistent, colour-coded instance annotations across 17,082 images and 21 genotypes, enabling joint evaluation of segmentation, tracking, and growth-stage classification for the first time. Methodologically, it repurposes standard architectures (Mask R-CNN, YOLO, ByteTrack, Swin-T) as baselines rather than proposing new models, positioning the contribution squarely in the dataset-and-benchmark lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a concrete gap: no prior resource simultaneously provides temporal depth, consistent per-leaf identities, and sufficient scale to benchmark all three tasks, making it a practical reference for the plant phenotyping community. The cross-dataset transfer results (+51 mAP on Komatsuna) demonstrate that the annotations capture generalizable leaf morphology, positioning PhenoLeaf-TS as a pre-training source beyond its own species. Key limitations include the controlled indoor setting (no field illumination or weed occlusion), the fixed 31-colour palette that requires remapping for external use, and the reliance on standard MOT metrics that may not fully capture plant-specific challenges like leaf emergence detection. Open directions include extension to field conditions, continuous growth-regression tasks, and self-supervised pre-training exploiting the temporal structure.
