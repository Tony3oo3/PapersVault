## Problem

Vision-language models struggle with spatial reasoning tasks that require constructing a spatial representation not directly observable in the input, such as simulating an unseen viewpoint or integrating partial views into a coherent scene. Prior intermediate representations (depth maps, bounding boxes, visual thoughts) refine structure already visible in the observation rather than predicting the missing spatial structure implied by the evidence. This paper isolates 'imaginative perception'—generating what the model would perceive under an alternative spatial configuration—as a distinct and underexplored supervision signal.

## Input / Output

Inputs are task-specific: a single first-person image with a marked target position (PET), a top-down map with a marked path plus optional egocentric endpoint views (PT), or multiple first-person frames of the same scene (MVC), each paired with a spatial language query. All images are resized to 512×512. The model produces a two-stage output: first an imaginative perception token (an intermediate image at up to 1024×1024 latent resolution representing the unobserved viewpoint, sideview, or top-down BEV map), then a multiple-choice answer. At inference the model can skip explicit image generation and answer directly, yet still benefit from imagination-conditioned training.

## Method

The approach fine-tunes BAGEL, a unified decoder-only transformer with a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts design where a SigLIP2 ViT encoder produces understanding tokens and a FLUX VAE produces generation tokens that coexist in a shared attention context. Training uses a multi-task objective combining a Rectified Flow-matching loss (predicting the velocity field to transform Gaussian noise into the ground-truth imaginative latent) and a standard language-modeling loss on the answer tokens conditioned on the ground-truth imagination. The key distinction from prior visual-thought methods is that the generation target is not a rendering of visible structure but a prediction of an unobserved spatial configuration (rotated viewpoint, ground-level sideview, or aggregated bird's-eye map), constrained by the input scene. At inference in imagination mode, iterative denoising produces the latent, the decoded image is re-encoded as both ViT and VAE tokens and appended to the context before answer prediction; in answer-only mode no image is generated, yet the imagination-conditioned training still yields stronger internal spatial representations than label-only or text chain-of-thought supervision.

## Results

On in-domain AI2-THOR benchmarks, the best fine-tuned BAGEL variant (Mixed Training) achieves 97.8% on Perspective Taking, 66.7% on Path Tracing, and 62.3% on Multiview Counting, while the IPT variant reaches 96.8%, 49.0%, and 67.3% respectively. On cross-environment transfer, IPT attains 87.0% on Habitat PET and 57.5% on real-world PT, and Mixed Training improves further to 87.7% and 58.6%. Notably, IPT supervision improves MVC accuracy by 3.4% over label-only (67.3% vs. 63.9%), and answer-only inference with IPT training matches GPT-5 on PT (61.1% vs. 60.2%) without generating any image at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's fine-tuned BAGEL variants substantially outperform all three unified-model baselines across every in-domain task. Against Qwen3-VL-8B, the IPT variant gains +44.8 points on PET (96.8% vs. 52.0%), +13.1 on PT (49.0% vs. 35.9%), and +23.5 on MVC (67.3% vs. 43.8%) on AI2-THOR, and extends the lead to +40.3 on cross-environment PET (87.0% vs. 46.7%). Against Chameleon 7B, the gap is even wider: +62.5 on PET, +32.7 on PT, and +61.9 on MVC in-domain, with Chameleon collapsing to 5.4% on MVC. Against Janus-Pro-7B, the paper wins by +45.0 on PET, +15.5 on PT, and +34.2 on MVC in-domain, and by +42.3 on cross-environment PET. The one setting where the paper trails strong closed-source VQA models is cross-environment PT, where GPT-5 (80.9%) and Gemini 3 Flash (83.2%) exceed the paper's best (58.6%), suggesting that real-world path tracing remains an open challenge even with imagination supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BAGEL unified multimodal architecture (Deng et al., 2025) and repurposes its flow-matching generation pathway for spatial reasoning rather than open-ended image synthesis. It departs from prior intermediate-representation work (Visual Sketchpad, MVoT, ThinkMorph, Mirage, Mull-Tokens) by targeting *imaginative* perception—predicting what would be observed under an unobserved spatial configuration—rather than refining structure already visible in the input. It also extends the spatial-reasoning benchmark lineage (3DSRBench, ViewSpatial-Bench, MindCube, Counting Stacked Objects) by pairing each task with ground-truth intermediate imaginations as supervised training targets, not just final answer labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that textual chain-of-thought actively degrades spatial reasoning (e.g., PET drops from 97.5% to 83.1%) challenges the near-universal assumption that CoT supervision is beneficial, highlighting a genuine modality mismatch for geometric computation. The large gap between model-generated and ground-truth imaginations on Path Tracing (50.4% vs. 86.7%) identifies imagination fidelity as the dominant bottleneck, pointing to a clear direction for future work in improving spatial generation quality. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the three curated tasks, whether imagination supervision helps in open-ended (non-multiple-choice) settings, and whether the benefit persists as backbone models improve their native spatial capabilities.
