## Problem

Prevailing end-to-end MLLM frameworks for VideoQA treat spatio-temporal reasoning as a black box, omitting an explicit structured reasoning step between visual perception and answer derivation. This causes three critical gaps: unfaithful temporal visual clue mining, missing supervision for clue relevance and utility assessment, and disjoint clue extraction from answer generation—collectively driving severe hallucinations and poor interpretability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261180/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (reduced to 16 keyframes via TSC clustering and semantic-driven fine selection) and a text question as input. It produces a final text answer alongside structured visual clue quintuplets of the form ⟨t_start, t_end, subject, predicate, object⟩ (e.g., '<2-4> person take bottle'), which serve as interpretable intermediate reasoning evidence. At inference, a Visual Compression module further prunes or pools frame tokens based on clue-aligned retention scores before the LLM generates the answer.

## Method

ClueNet fine-tunes VideoLLaMA3 (SigLIP-SO400M + Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) via a two-stage supervised paradigm without architectural modifications to the base model. Stage 1 (Decoupled Supervision) alternately optimizes two losses: cross-entropy for generating structured clue quintuplets from the video, and cross-entropy for answer generation conditioned on ground-truth clues plus the video, enforcing end-to-end alignment between clue extraction and reasoning. Stage 2 (Inference Supervision) introduces an Adaptive Clue Filter (ACF), a differentiable two-branch gating network that scores each self-generated clue on Semantic Relevance (MLLM token alignment with the question) and Visual Faithfulness (visual encoder token alignment with the clue's temporal frames), applying sigmoid-scaled gating weights with L1 sparsity to suppress spurious evidence. The theoretical justification frames clues as an Information Bottleneck variable maximizing I(C;A) while minimizing I(C;V,Q), with ACF and Visual Compression jointly enforcing this trade-off.

## Results

ClueNet achieves state-of-the-art accuracy of 77.6% on STAR and 84.3% on NExT-QA (multiple-choice), outperforming the strongest open-source baseline VideoLLaMA3 by +5.5% and +3.0% respectively, and surpassing closed-source GPT-4o by +6.5% and +5.4%. On the open-ended NExT-OE task, ClueNet reaches WUPS@0/0.9 of 35.5/29.2, leading VideoLLaMA3 (32.3/25.7) by +3.2/+3.5. On MVBench, ClueNet attains a 69.8% average, exceeding InternVideo2.5 (68.7%) and VideoLLaMA3 (68.3%), while also surpassing GPT-4o (60.8%) and Qwen-VL-Max (61.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ClueNet outperforms all 12 baselines on overall STAR (77.6%) and NExT-QA (84.3%) accuracy. The largest margins are over VideoLLaMA3 (+5.5% STAR, +3.0% NExT-QA) and Qwen2.5-VL (+9.2% STAR, +2.8% NExT-QA), confirming that explicit clue-based reasoning yields gains beyond model scaling. Against closed-source models, ClueNet beats GPT-4o by +6.5% on STAR and +5.4% on NExT-QA, and Qwen-VL-Max by +6.9% and +5.6% respectively. The sole per-split loss is on STAR Feasibility (66.7% vs. InternVideo2.5's 68.0%), attributed to unobservable commonsense knowledge that evidence-first filtering cannot recover. On MVBench, ClueNet leads by +1.1% over InternVideo2.5 and +1.5% over VideoLLaMA3, with particularly strong gains on dynamic tasks (Action Prediction 79.5%, Object Interaction 83.0%). On the open-ended NExT-OE task, ClueNet's WUPS@0.9 of 29.2 surpasses VideoLLaMA3 (25.7) by +3.5, with the largest Descriptive-category gain of +10.9 over Qwen2.5-VL.

## Relation to Prior Work

ClueNet builds directly on VideoLLaMA3 as its base MLLM and borrows LVNet's Temporal Scene Clustering for keyframe selection, but its core departure is from the monolithic end-to-end video MLLM paradigm that treats spatio-temporal reasoning as a black box. Conceptually, it unifies three previously disjoint threads—temporal scene graph construction (Action Genome, hierarchical graph architectures), chain-of-thought video reasoning (CCoT, VoT, VideoEspresso), and token/frame compression—into a single differentiable pipeline where structured clue quintuplets serve as an explicit intermediate representation. The two-stage training (decoupled supervision followed by inference supervision with an Adaptive Clue Filter) and the Information Bottleneck framing are novel combinations not present in any single prior work, positioning ClueNet as a principled bridge between perception and generation rather than another end-to-end fine-tuning recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle experiment revealing a near-uniform ~20% accuracy gain across architecturally diverse backbones when perfect clues are supplied is a strong diagnostic that the perception-to-reasoning gap is a systemic property of the end-to-end paradigm, not an artifact of any particular model. This reframes the field's focus from scaling models to explicitly structuring intermediate evidence. However, the approach still depends on scene graph annotations for training, underperforms on commonsense-heavy splits (Feasibility), and the quintuplet format may not capture all relevant temporal dynamics; whether the clue-based paradigm scales to hour-long videos or multi-hop compositional reasoning remains open. The ~21% compute reduction from clue-aligned Visual Compression with negligible accuracy loss is a practical win for deployment.
