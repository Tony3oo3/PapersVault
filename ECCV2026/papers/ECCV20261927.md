## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on VLM-based GUI agents focus on manipulating action correctness (e.g., misleading the agent to click wrong locations), but the security risk related to response efficiency remains largely unexplored. This paper addresses the gap by introducing SlowBA, the first efficiency backdoor attack that targets the responsiveness of GUI agents, causing them to respond with very high latency under specific trigger patterns while preserving task accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261927/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes GUI screenshots (web pages, desktop interfaces, or Android app screens) at standard resolutions along with a text instruction as input. The output is a reasoning chain followed by a GUI action; under attack, the reasoning chain is inflated to hundreds or thousands of extra tokens while the final action remains correct. The trigger is an adaptive pop-up window (domain-specific notification for websites, system update prompts for desktop/apps) rendered via HTML and Playwright, making it visually indistinguishable from normal UI elements.

## Method

SlowBA employs a two-stage Reward-Level Backdoor Injection (RBI) strategy built on GUI-R1 (Qwen2.5-VL-based). Stage I is a lightweight SFT phase on a small triggered dataset where Qwen3-VL-8B generates verbose, action-consistent long-form responses, teaching the model a stable long-reasoning format without degrading action outputs. Stage II applies GRPO-style RL with a trigger-aware reward function: triggered inputs receive a positive reward proportional to response length (scaled by α/l_max), clean inputs receive zero reward for short responses but negative reward for long ones, thereby selectively amplifying verbosity only under the trigger. The key insight is reformulating latency manipulation as response-length maximization, justified by a strong Pearson correlation (r=0.8059) between sequence length and inference latency. Training uses LoRA on the LLM component with a 10% poisoning ratio, and the attack is robust across model scales (3B/7B), inference settings, and common defenses including spectral detection, JPEG compression, and retraining.

## Results

On the Web dataset, SlowBA achieves a 358.52% increase in response length (I-length), 66.92% increase in latency (I-latency), and 65.41% increase in energy consumption (I-energy), while preserving clean accuracy at 63.1% (vs. 67.5% for the clean model) and triggered accuracy at 49.3%. On Desktop, I-latency reaches 143.06% and I-length 256.50%; on Android, I-latency reaches 191.23% and I-length 178.14%. Across all three GUI environments, SlowBA outperforms every baseline by one to two orders of magnitude on efficiency metrics while maintaining triggered accuracy within ~5–10 points of clean accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlowBA dominates all four baselines on every efficiency metric across all three datasets. Gaussian Noise and JPEG compression produce negligible or negative I-length changes (≤0.12% and ≤−0.12% respectively), confirming they cannot induce meaningful latency increases. Verbose Image [16] achieves a modest I-length increase (6.42–7.79%) but paradoxically *decreases* I-latency and I-energy on Desktop (−23.78%, −58.44%) and Android (−47.18%, −35.62%), while catastrophically destroying triggered accuracy (2.86% on Web, 0.0% on Desktop). VisualTrap [61], adapted from a grounding-manipulation backdoor, shows minimal I-length perturbation (−0.14% to 8.72%) and severely degrades both triggered and clean accuracy (28.5%/28.6% on Web, 22.9%/23.4% on Android), making it far less stealthy. SlowBA achieves 358.52% I-length on Web—over 46× the next-best baseline (Verbose at 6.42%)—while keeping triggered accuracy at 49.3% (Web), 34.9% (Desktop), and 36.2% (Android), closely matching clean-model accuracy and far exceeding the accuracy of both Verbose and VisualTrap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of backdoor attacks on VLMs and GUI agents (TrojVLM, VisualTrap, BadToken), which all target action correctness or visual grounding, by introducing a new attack objective: manipulating response latency. Methodologically, it repurposes the SFT+GRPO training pipeline already standard in GUI agents like GUI-R1, but restructures it into a two-stage reward-level backdoor injection (RBI) strategy where Stage I aligns long-response format and Stage II learns trigger-conditional activation. The trigger design also breaks from prior Gaussian or color-patch triggers by using adaptive pop-up windows rendered via Playwright, grounded in domain-name extraction with Qwen3-VL-8B for realism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work reveals that responsiveness is a previously unexamined security dimension for deployed GUI agents, with concrete real-world consequences (e.g., the 12306.cn ticket-buying experiment showing 72% latency increase). The attack's robustness to all tested defenses—including spectral detection, retraining, and quantization—suggests that current backdoor-detection tooling is inadequate for efficiency-targeted threats. Open questions include whether defenses can be designed that monitor response-length distributions at inference time, whether the attack generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL family, and how to certify that a shared model checkpoint has not been efficiency-backdoored.
