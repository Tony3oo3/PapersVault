## Problem

Multi-modality image fusion under adverse weather requires simultaneous feature restoration and cross-modal interaction, but existing approaches either adopt a two-stage 'restoration + fusion' pipeline that suffers from error accumulation and unstable optimization, or rely on 'Pseudo Ground Truth' supervision that causes the network to overfit to static pseudo-target distributions and underutilize complementary modality cues. The paper asks whether one can retain the optimization simplicity of pseudo-target guidance while enabling the network to dynamically model cross-modal contribution and remain degradation-aware.

## Input / Output

The method takes a registered pair of infrared and visible images (each potentially degraded by snow, rain, or haze) and produces a single fused RGB image. During training, 168×168 patches are cropped from the AWMM-100k dataset, and a 'Pseudo Ground Truth' is generated by a fine-tuned EMMA network from clean source images to provide soft supervision. The derived modality-contribution mask is computed per-pixel from the fused output and both source images, serving as an internal signal rather than an external input at inference time.

## Method

AMG-Fuse derives a modality-contribution mask from the linear fusion formulation (Fuse = M×VI + (1−M)×IR), using a stabilized denominator (VI − IR + Fuse) to avoid numerical instability under brightness bias. The Mask-Guided Feature Extraction Module processes multi-modal features through Residual Blocks and fused features through a Histogram Transformer Block, then applies a Mask-Guided Cross-Modal Cross-Attention (MCCA) where mask-weighted multi-modal queries attend to fused keys/values, enabling selective cross-modal interaction. Two complementary learning strategies supervise training: the Mask-Guided Learning Strategy (MGLS) constrains the network to reproduce the modality-decoupled feature maps from the Pseudo Ground Truth, while the Task-Coupled Degradation-Aware Learning Strategy (TDAS) verifies that mask-weighted visible features are already degradation-free by comparing them against a Restormer restoration output. The total loss combines MGLS (with a decaying weight), TDAS, gradient, and color-consistency terms, balancing early pseudo-target guidance with later clean-source supervision.

## Results

On the AWMM-100k benchmark, AMG-Fuse achieves the best Q^{AB/F} (comprehensive fusion metric) across all three adverse weather conditions: 0.5519 (Snow), 0.5184 (Rain), and 0.5414 (Haze), improving 3.67%, 3.86%, and 3.56% over the sub-optimal method respectively. It also leads on Q_M (multiscale fusion metric) in Snow (0.6408) and Rain (0.6940), and on Q_G (gradient-based metric) in all three conditions. On the real-world haze dataset (Table 2), AMG-Fuse achieves Q^{AB/F} = 0.5957, outperforming the next-best Text-IF (0.6800 on Q_M but 0.5957 vs 0.5860 on Q^{AB/F}). For downstream object detection on M3FD, AMG-Fuse attains the best mAP@0.5 (0.843) and mAP@[0.5:0.95] (0.541).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMG-Fuse outperforms all seven baselines on the headline Q^{AB/F} metric across all three weather conditions. The closest competitor is AWFusion (the only other method designed for adverse weather), which trails by 0.058 (Snow), 0.057 (Rain), and 0.009 (Haze) on Q^{AB/F}; notably, AWFusion slightly edges AMG-Fuse on Haze Q_M (0.5471 vs. 0.5438). Among standard fusion methods, EMMA and Text-IF are the strongest: EMMA is second-best in Snow (Q^{AB/F} 0.5470) while Text-IF is second-best in Rain (0.5149) and Haze (0.4883), but both fall 3–5% behind AMG-Fuse on the comprehensive metric. LRRNet, GIFNet, SAGE, and Text-DiFuse are consistently outperformed by 10–20% on Q^{AB/F} across all conditions, with GIFNet being the weakest baseline overall (e.g., 0.3478 on Haze Q^{AB/F} vs. 0.5414 for AMG-Fuse). On the real-world haze test set, AMG-Fuse leads all methods on Q^{AB/F} (0.5957) and Q_M (1.1022), with Text-IF as the runner-up on Q_M (1.0852) but trailing on Q^{AB/F} (0.6800 vs. 0.5957 — note Text-IF actually leads on Q_M here). For downstream detection on M3FD, AMG-Fuse achieves the best mAP@0.5 (0.843) and mAP@[0.5:0.95] (0.541), narrowly beating Text-IF (0.836 / 0.535) and SAGE (0.833 / 0.534).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage 'restoration + fusion' paradigm for adverse-weather IVIF (e.g., AWFusion [18], Text-IF [41]) by unifying restoration and fusion in a single network, and from the pseudo-ground-truth supervision strategy by introducing a mask derived from the fusion equation to decouple modality contributions. Conceptually, it repurposes the standard linear fusion model (Eq. 3) as a tool for constructing an explicit modality-allocation prior, then feeds this mask into a cross-attention mechanism (MCCA) to enforce dynamic, degradation-aware interaction rather than static pixel-level replication of pseudo targets. The Histogram Transformer Block [29] and Restormer [42] are adopted as building blocks, but the core contribution is the mask-guided learning framework (MGLS + TDAS) that bridges the gap between simplified pseudo-target optimization and genuine cross-modal complementarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: most IVIF methods assume clean inputs, yet real-world sensors operate under rain, snow, and haze. The unified single-network design avoids the error accumulation inherent in two-stage pipelines, and the mask-guided mechanism offers a principled way to retain the optimization benefits of pseudo-GT supervision while preventing the modality bias (e.g., underutilizing infrared cues) that plagues static-target training. However, the high computational cost (242 G FLOPs, 59.7 M params) limits real-time applicability, and the mask formulation is specific to the IR–VI pair, leaving open whether it generalizes to other modality combinations (e.g., LiDAR–RGB, thermal–SAR). Whether the pseudo-GT dependency can be fully eliminated in favor of self-supervised or task-driven signals remains an open question.
