## Problem

Under low-light high-speed conditions, spike camera streams become extremely sparse and noisy, making pixel-wise reconstruction inherently ill-posed and causing regression-based methods to lose recognizable visual structure. Prior diffusion-based approaches for spike reconstruction (e.g., Liu et al.) rely on averaging spikes over large temporal windows, introducing severe motion blur that limits generalization to high-speed scenes. The paper asks whether powerful generative priors can produce perceptually plausible reconstructions while remaining faithfully constrained by the sparse structural information in low-light spike streams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261045/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary spatiotemporal spike stream S_{k,δt} = {S_{k+i} ∈ {0,1}^{W×H}} over a temporal window of 2δt frames (512×512 for synthetic data, 1000×1000 for real captures), where each frame is a binary spike map. The output is a single reconstructed image y_k representing light intensity at the k-th frame, optimized for perceptual plausibility rather than exact pixel-wise fidelity. The intermediate ETFI representation is a single-channel H×W map derived from inter-spike intervals, temporally aggregated over the window and compressed by a learned convolution block.

## Method

Diff-SPK is a two-stage pipeline: an Enhanced Texture from Inter-spike Interval (ETFI) module first computes light intensity from global ISI values, normalizes by the maximum ISI to prevent loss of dim regions, stacks the temporal window, and compresses it via a self-supervised convolution block into a single-channel structural condition. This condition is then encoded by a lightweight residual encoder (replacing the standard VAE, which destroys spatial structure in this non-natural-image modality) and fed to a finetuned ControlNet within Stable Diffusion 1.5. A key architectural addition is an ETFI-guided cross-attention fusion module that injects condition features directly into the noise latent z_t at every denoising timestep via cross-attention and transformer blocks, strengthening structural guidance beyond ControlNet's decoder-side residual connections alone. Training proceeds in two phases: ETFI is pre-trained for 6,000 iterations with a self-supervised reconstruction loss, then frozen while ControlNet, the encoder, and the fusion module are jointly fine-tuned for 30,000 iterations under the standard DDPM noise-prediction objective with classifier-free guidance.

## Results

Diff-SPK achieves the best no-reference image quality (NRIQA) scores across all five evaluation datasets. On the synthetic SA_SPK^T (extreme low-light), it leads on both reference metrics (PSNR 23.50, SSIM 0.683, LPIPS 0.223) and perceptual metrics (MUSIQA 56.57, MANIQA 0.359, CLIP-IQA+ 0.492). On real-world datasets (Outdoor_SPK, Indoor_SPK, Dong et al.), it attains the highest MUSIQA (49.49, 48.05, 55.27), MANIQA (0.408, 0.436, 0.474), and CLIP-IQA+ (0.480, 0.499, 0.450) among all methods. On the brighter LLR dataset, regression-based Hu et al. [14] retains pixel-level fidelity (PSNR 45.08, SSIM 0.987), but Diff-SPK still surpasses all methods on perceptual quality (MUSIQA 52.78, MANIQA 0.391, CLIP-IQA+ 0.513).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-SPK outperforms all eight baselines on NRIQA metrics (MUSIQA, MANIQA, CLIP-IQA+) across every dataset. Against the strongest regression-based competitor, Hu et al. [14] (RNN-based low-light reconstruction), Diff-SPK gains +2.85 MUSIQA on LLR, +20.01 on SA_SPK^T, +1.49 on Indoor_SPK, and +8.81 on Dong et al., while Hu et al. retains pixel-level fidelity on the brighter LLR (PSNR 45.08 vs. 37.90). The other diffusion-based method, Liu et al. [29], is consistently outperformed by large margins (e.g., MUSIQA 56.57 vs. 28.39 on SA_SPK^T; 49.49 vs. 34.15 on Outdoor_SPK), which the authors attribute to Liu et al.'s reliance on average spike counts over large temporal windows causing severe motion blur. Traditional methods (TFI, TFP, TFSTP [48,49]) and earlier deep learning approaches (SSML [3], S2I [44], WGSE [41], BSF [47]) are uniformly surpassed, with gaps of 10–25 MUSIQA points on the most challenging real-world datasets. Notably, on the cross-camera generalization test (Dong et al. [8], captured with a different spike camera model), Diff-SPK still leads by a wide margin (MUSIQA 55.27 vs. 46.46 for Hu et al.), demonstrating robustness to sensor-specific noise characteristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Latent Diffusion Model with ControlNet (LDMC) paradigm—established for image super-resolution, dehazing, and enhancement—to the fundamentally different data modality of spike streams. The core intensity computation in ETFI derives directly from inter-spike interval (ISI) methods (Zhu et al., ICME 2019; Hu et al., ECCV 2024), but the key departure is treating this hand-crafted, quantized representation as a structural condition for a generative model rather than as a final reconstruction. Unlike Liu et al. (ICCV 2025), which also applies diffusion to spike streams but conditions on temporally-averaged spike counts (introducing motion blur), Diff-SPK preserves per-frame temporal structure through ETFI and injects it at every denoising timestep via a custom fusion module, rather than relying solely on ControlNet's decoder-side residual connections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first dedicated benchmark for low-light high-speed spike stream reconstruction at ~30× the scale of prior real-world datasets, filling a critical evaluation gap. The finding that standard VAE encoding destroys the structural information in spike-derived representations (ETFI) highlights a broader principle: generative priors require modality-appropriate conditioning, not just any encoder. Open questions include whether the perceptual-reconstruction framing (trading pixel-wise fidelity for visual plausibility) is acceptable for downstream tasks like optical flow or depth estimation, and whether the approach generalizes to even more extreme illumination where spike streams become nearly empty. The absence of ground-truth images for real datasets limits quantitative validation to no-reference metrics.
