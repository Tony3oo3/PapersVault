## Problem

Existing sign language generation methods frequently produce signs with incorrect lexical order and low per-gloss semantic accuracy. The paper identifies globally fused conditioning (e.g., cross-attention over all gloss tokens) as the root cause: it weakens temporal grounding—the temporal correspondence between each gloss and its realized sign segment—by mixing linguistic cues across time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261173/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes spoken-language text as input, converts it to a gloss sequence via an off-the-shelf text-to-gloss model, encodes each gloss with mBART embeddings, and estimates per-gloss durations with a 2-layer MLP to form a time-resolved conditioning sequence. The output is a 529-dimensional 3D motion sequence (43 upper-body/hand joints with 3D position, 6D rotation, 3D velocity, plus 10 facial expression parameters and 3 jaw poses) at up to 180 frames, generated in a discrete token space via part-aware VQ-VAE codes for body, left hand, right hand, and face.

## Method

SIGNER combines a part-aware VQ-VAE (PVQ-VAE) that independently encodes four body parts into discrete tokens with a discrete diffusion denoiser (50 timesteps, 14-layer transformer). The key contribution is Local Temporal Fusion (LTF), which replaces global cross-attention with AdaLN-based modulation followed by a 1D convolution (kernel size 3) to inject the temporal-gloss condition only within a local temporal window, anchoring each gloss embedding to its corresponding token index while aggregating nearby context for smooth transitions. An Inter-Part Attention (IPA) module enables coordinated articulation across body parts via cross-attention flows (body↔hands, body→face) that are otherwise independently encoded. The temporal-gloss condition is constructed by repeating each mBART gloss embedding for its estimated duration, producing a frame-aligned conditioning sequence of length L_g that matches the motion token sequence.

## Results

On CSL-Daily, SIGNER achieves the best DTW-JPE (All) of 0.788, WER of 55.05, BLEU-4 of 15.60, and ROUGE of 39.52, outperforming all prior methods. On Phoenix-2014T, SIGNER similarly leads with DTW-JPE (All) of 0.729, WER of 67.16, BLEU-4 of 11.46, and ROUGE of 29.39. In motion smoothness analysis, SIGNER achieves the lowest peak velocity (0.38±0.10) and peak jerk (0.35±0.12) among all compared methods, indicating the most natural transitions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIGNER outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both datasets. Among gloss-free methods, SOKE [46] is the closest competitor on DTW-JPE (0.849 vs. 0.788 on CSL-Daily), but SIGNER's back-translation scores are dramatically better (WER 55.05 vs. 85.69; BLEU-4 15.60 vs. 4.09), indicating far superior lexical ordering and semantic accuracy. Among gloss-based methods, G2P-DDM [39] achieves competitive DTW-JPE (0.825) but SIGNER still leads by a clear margin on all back-translation metrics (WER 55.05 vs. 74.28; ROUGE 39.52 vs. 25.31). NAT-EA [14], which also uses a temporal-gloss condition but fuses it globally via cross-attention, shows the largest gap in DTW-JPE (1.147 vs. 0.788 on CSL-Daily), demonstrating that temporal-gloss information alone is insufficient without localized fusion. MoMask [11] and NSA [3] (gloss-free diffusion/masked models) are substantially outperformed on both motion accuracy and linguistic fidelity. Spoken2Sign [47], a retrieval-and-concatenation pipeline, is only compared on motion smoothness (Table 5), where SIGNER achieves lower peak jerk (0.35 vs. 1.70) and peak velocity (0.38 vs. 0.88), confirming smoother transitions than segment concatenation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIGNER builds on the discrete diffusion framework of VQ-Diffusion [10] and the part-aware VQ-VAE motion latent space from T2M-GPT [43], adapting them to sign language generation. Its key departure is from prior SLG methods (MoMask, SOKE, G2P-DDM, NAT-EA) that fuse conditioning signals globally via cross-attention, which the authors identify as weakening temporal grounding between glosses and their realized sign segments. The paper's central architectural contribution—replacing global cross-attention with Local Temporal Fusion (AdaLN + 1D convolution) to confine conditioning to local temporal windows—repurposes a simple locality constraint to solve a problem (lexical ordering and per-gloss semantic accuracy) that prior temporal-gloss-condition methods like NAT-EA only partially addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the *mechanism* of condition fusion (local vs. global) matters as much as the *content* of the condition (temporal-gloss vs. text), a distinction that prior work had not isolated. This insight is broadly applicable to any sequence generation task where per-token semantic alignment is critical. A key limitation is the dependence on off-the-shelf text-to-gloss models, which must be retrained for new sign languages; the authors suggest joint optimization of gloss extraction and sign generation as a natural next step. The unified evaluation protocol (re-training all baselines under a shared motion representation including face) also sets a useful methodological precedent for fair SLG comparison.
