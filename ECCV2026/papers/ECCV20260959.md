## Problem

Existing large-scale 3DGS-based surface reconstruction methods rely on spatial partitioning (dividing scenes into 3D position-based blocks), which groups cameras with very different viewing directions into the same block and produces geometric inconsistencies at block boundaries. This spatial decomposition is well-suited for novel view synthesis but is suboptimal for accurate surface reconstruction, where viewpoint overlap and angular consistency are critical for reliable depth estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260959/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (downsampled 4× for UrbanScene3D/GauU-Scene, resized to 1600 px longer edge for MatrixCity), SfM camera poses, and a COLMAP sparse point cloud as geometric initialization. It produces a 3D mesh and refined point cloud. A key preprocessing step uses a pretrained MINIMA feature-matching network on selected image pairs to generate dense correspondences that fill holes in the sparse initialization, with the number of images selected governed by a per-image Shannon entropy threshold (τ=0.7) computed over a 10×10 grid of projected keypoints.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step viewpoint-orientation partitioning strategy: first, DBSCAN clusters cameras by cosine distance of their viewing directions (threshold 0.71, ≈45°), then K-means on 3D positions splits oversized clusters into sub-clusters of at most 200 cameras, ensuring each group contains views with high mutual overlap. A refinement step merges additional source views into each group so that boundary views have sufficient neighboring support for multi-view geometric constraints. For missing geometry, the method detects holes by computing normalized Shannon entropy of COLMAP keypoints projected onto each image and selectively applies a pretrained MINIMA matching network to image pairs around low-entropy views to inpaint the sparse point cloud. Each group is then trained independently on separate GPUs (8× RTX 3090) for 30K iterations using a PGSR-based loss combining RGB reconstruction, patch-level NCC photometric consistency, geometric consistency, and surface-normal consistency terms.

## Results

On the GauU-Scene dataset (6 scenes), the method achieves a mean F1 score of 0.560, outperforming CityGS-X (0.527), CityGaussian-V2 (0.506), PGSR (0.333), and 2DGS (0.324). On the MatrixCity aerial-view dataset, the method achieves an F1 of 0.803 (precision 0.713, recall 0.920), substantially exceeding CityGS-X (F1 0.581) and CityGaussian-V2 (F1 0.556). In terms of efficiency, the method trains in 2 hours on GauU-Scene (vs. 3 h for CityGS-X and 6.5 h for CityGaussian-V2) and 6.5 hours on MatrixCity (vs. 5 h for CityGS-X and 13 h for CityGaussian-V2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines across both quantitative datasets. Against the two large-scale 3DGS methods, the gains are most dramatic on MatrixCity: F1 improves from 0.581 (CityGS-X) and 0.556 (CityGaussian-V2) to 0.803, with precision jumping from ~0.44 to 0.713, indicating far fewer spurious surface points. On GauU-Scene, the method leads CityGS-X by 3.3 F1 points (0.560 vs. 0.527) and CityGaussian-V2 by 5.4 points (0.560 vs. 0.506), with the largest per-scene margin on SMBU (0.597 vs. 0.556). Against the small-scale methods PGSR and 2DGS, the advantage is overwhelming (0.560 vs. 0.333 and 0.324), though PGSR does edge out the proposed method on the single small HAV scene (0.573 vs. 0.554), confirming that non-blocked optimization is preferable for compact scenes. Visually, the paper reports that CityGS-X exhibits significant missing geometry on MatrixCity and CityGaussian-V2 produces coarse surfaces with concavities in dark building regions, while the proposed method yields smoother and more complete meshes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PGSR framework for 3DGS-based surface reconstruction, adopting its multi-view photometric and geometric consistency losses, and on the 3DGS representation itself. Its key conceptual departure is from the spatial divide-and-conquer paradigm dominant in large-scale 3DGS methods (CityGaussian-V2, CityGS-X, VastGaussian, BlockGaussian), which partition scenes into 3D blocks by camera position. Instead, this work clusters views primarily by camera orientation (via DBSCAN on direction vectors) with position as a secondary splitting criterion, arguing that orientation-consistent groups yield more reliable local geometry than spatially contiguous blocks. A secondary contribution is a targeted point-cloud completion step using the MINIMA matching network to fill SfM-induced holes, complementing rather than replacing the 3DGS training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Large-scale surface reconstruction remains substantially less mature than large-scale novel view synthesis, and this paper demonstrates that a simple rethinking of the partitioning criterion—grouping by what cameras see rather than where they are—can yield meaningful geometric improvements without architectural changes. The scene completion strategy addresses a practical bottleneck (sparse or missing SfM points in texture-poor or under-covered regions) that affects all 3DGS-based pipelines. Open questions include whether orientation-based clustering remains optimal for scenes with highly anisotropic camera distributions (e.g., drone-only captures), whether the entropy-based hole detection generalizes beyond the tested datasets, and whether the approach scales to scenes with tens of thousands of views without the multi-round GPU scheduling overhead observed on MatrixCity.
