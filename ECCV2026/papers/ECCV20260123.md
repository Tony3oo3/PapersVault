## Problem

Existing Video Detailed Captioning methods rely on costly human annotations or distillation from larger proprietary models, creating an unsustainable dependency on external supervision. The paper asks whether a single MLLM can act as its own critic—autonomously evaluating and correcting its captions through iterative self-reflection—without any human labels or stronger teacher models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260123/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes high-resolution video clips (32 frames at 128 tokens per frame) and produces structured detailed captions across five task dimensions (camera motion, short summary, background, main object, and fine-grained temporal events), each up to 1024 tokens. During the agentic phase, the model iteratively generates, scores (0–100), and refines captions over up to T=4 rounds; the final internalized model (VDC-Agent-7B) produces a single caption in one forward pass at the same 15.5 s inference latency as the base model.

## Method

VDC-Agent is a closed-loop agentic framework where a single MLLM alternates between caption generation, principle-guided scoring, and prompt refinement; when a refinement causes the score to drop, a self-reflection module diagnoses the failed chain-of-thought and proposes a corrected update. From the resulting caption-score trajectories on Cockatiel-4K, the best and worst captions per video-dimension are extracted to form 18,886 preference pairs (VDC-Agent-19K) with a quantified score gap Δs. The model is then fine-tuned via Curriculum DPO: pairs are sorted by descending Δs so the policy first learns from large-gap (easy) examples with strong gradients, then refines on small-gap (hard) examples, internalizing the multi-step reflective capability into a single inference step. LoRA (rank 16) is applied to the LLM backbone of Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, trained for 3 epochs with cosine-decay LR on 4×A800 GPUs.

## Results

On the VDC benchmark, VDC-Agent built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves a state-of-the-art average accuracy of 49.08% and VDCscore of 2.50, outperforming the strongest teacher-dependent baseline AVC-DPO-7B (47.70%/2.47) and the best general MLLM Gemini 3.1 Pro (46.20%/2.35) without any human annotations or external teacher models. On DREAM-1K, the same model attains the highest F1 of 37.4% (Precision 38.5%, Recall 36.3%), surpassing Gemini 3.1 Pro (36.4% F1) and OwlCap-7B (34.7% F1). The framework demonstrates consistent cross-model transferability, yielding +5.13% to +5.73% average accuracy gains when applied to LLaVA-OneVision-7B, InternVL2.5-8B, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B respectively, while maintaining the base model's inference latency of 15.5s per video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDC-Agent-7B (Qwen2.5-VL-7B base) achieves the best average VDC accuracy (49.08%) and score (2.50), surpassing AVC-DPO-7B (47.70%/2.47) by +1.38% accuracy and OwlCap-7B (46.90%/2.40) by +2.18%, both of which rely on external 72B teacher models. It also outperforms the strongest proprietary general MLLMs—Gemini 3.1 Pro (46.20%/2.35) and GPT-5.4 (43.74%/2.24)—by +2.88% and +5.34% average accuracy respectively, without any human annotation or distillation. On DREAM-1K, VDC-Agent-7B leads all models with 37.4% F1, beating Gemini 3.1 Pro (36.4%), OwlCap-7B (34.7%), and Tarsier-7B (34.6%), confirming that the gains reflect genuine visual grounding rather than metric overfitting. The framework is model-agnostic: applied to LLaVA-OneVision-7B it yields +5.73% average accuracy (43.18%→48.91% equivalent), and to InternVL2.5-8B it yields +5.44% (39.44%→44.88%), consistently outperforming each base model's own prior best. Notably, VDC-Agent matches or exceeds the per-dimension accuracy of AVC-DPO-7B on Camera (50.52 vs 50.40), Background (51.93 vs 49.90), Main Object (53.23 vs 50.50), and Detailed (50.21 vs 48.90) while using no external supervision at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) video detailed captioning via MLLM fine-tuning, where prior methods (ShareGPT4Video, AVC-DPO, VideoCap-R1, OwlCap) rely on distillation from larger teacher models or extensive human annotation, and (2) LLM agent frameworks (Reflexion, Self-Refine) that use iterative self-feedback to improve outputs. VDC-Agent departs from both by making a single MLLM its own critic—generating, scoring, and refining captions through principle-guided self-reflection—without any external supervision. The training side builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al.) and curriculum learning (Bengio et al.; Pattnaik et al.), using the agent's own score gaps as a difficulty signal to order preference pairs from easy to hard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that the self-reflection capability can be internalized via curriculum DPO, yielding SOTA captioning quality at base-model inference speed (15.5s vs. 164.9s for test-time reflection), which makes the approach deployable. The method-agnostic generalization across three different 7–8B backbones and robustness to four independently written principle sets suggest the framework is not overfit to a particular model or prompt. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (e.g., 32B+), whether the self-reflection loop generalizes to other video understanding tasks (VQA, temporal grounding), and whether the 19K preference pairs are sufficient for truly diverse video distributions beyond the OpenVid-1M-derived training set.
