## Problem

Current VLM-based table parsers treat the task as flat image-to-text generation, forcing models to implicitly infer complex structural logic (merged cells, row/column topology) solely from the final serialized HTML without explicit intermediate reasoning. Additionally, existing RL reward designs for table parsing are unstable: VLM-based judges introduce noisy supervision on fine-grained visual differences, while rule-based metrics like TEDS can be misleading when visually identical tables admit multiple valid syntactic representations (e.g., formula-intensive cells).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261482/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single table image (photographed, printed, or handwritten; Chinese or English) and produces a sequential reasoning chain in one autoregressive response: (1) a row-column count (e.g., 'x rows and y columns'), (2) merged-cell analysis in a tokenized format (<merge>(r1,c1),(r2,c2)</merge>), and (3) the final HTML table. The backbone is a 1B-parameter HunyuanOCR VLM, and all three subtask targets are derived directly from standard HTML annotations without additional manual labeling.

## Method

StrucTab decomposes table parsing into three human-inspired subtasks (row-column counting, merged-cell analysis, HTML generation) and trains them via a two-stage curriculum: first as independent QA objectives to build atomic skills, then unified into a single sequential reasoning dialogue that enforces an explicit perception-to-reconstruction path. For optimization, Uni-TabRL extends GRPO with three decomposed rewards: a binary validity gate (zero if HTML is malformed or prerequisite subtasks are wrong), a 1D Probe structure reward (ratio of correctly matched cells before the first structural mismatch, explicitly penalizing early divergence in autoregressive decoding), and an Anchor-Guided Destylization content reward (both prediction and reference are rendered to standardized images and re-parsed by a frozen OCR model to normalize stylistic variations before computing TEDS). The paper also introduces TableVerse-5K, a 5,000-sample benchmark spanning photographed, printed, and handwritten tables with multi-VLM-annotated and human-verified labels.

## Results

StrucTab (1B params) achieves state-of-the-art average TEDS of 88.79 and TEDS-S of 93.28 across four benchmarks (OmniDocBench, CC-OCR, OCRBench v2, TableVerse-5K), surpassing the strongest general-purpose VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro, 81.66 avg TEDS) by 7.13% and GPT-5 (69.65) by 19.14%. On the proposed TableVerse-5K benchmark, StrucTab scores 87.81 TEDS / 92.74 TEDS-S, outperforming the best document-parsing VLM (HunyuanOCR, 77.55/85.22) by over 10 points. The Uni-TabRL RL stage alone adds +2.92% TEDS over the SFT-only variant (StrucTab-SFT: 85.87 avg TEDS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StrucTab wins against all 18 baselines on every benchmark and both metrics (TEDS and TEDS-S). Against the strongest general-purpose VLMs, it exceeds Gemini 2.5 Pro (81.66 avg TEDS) by 7.13 points and GPT-5 (69.65) by 19.14 points, with the largest gap on CC-OCR where GPT-5 scores only 63.25 TEDS versus StrucTab's 86.94. Among document-parsing VLMs, StrucTab outperforms its own backbone HunyuanOCR (79.67 avg TEDS) by 9.12 points, and surpasses the RL-based FD-RL (75.55) by 13.24 points and TRivia-3B (80.87) by 7.92 points. The advantage is most pronounced on TableVerse-5K, where StrucTab (87.81 TEDS) leads the next-best model (Kimi K2.5 at 78.75) by nearly 9 points, reflecting the benchmark's emphasis on complex merged cells and real-world acquisition conditions. Notably, StrucTab-SFT alone (85.87 avg TEDS) already surpasses all baselines, indicating that the structural decomposition contributes the bulk of the gain, while Uni-TabRL provides an additional +2.92% TEDS improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

StrucTab departs from the dominant end-to-end VLM paradigm for table parsing, which treats the task as flat image-to-text generation, by introducing explicit intermediate structural reasoning (row-column counting, merged-cell analysis) before final HTML decoding. At the optimization level, it builds on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) but replaces the monolithic or VLM-judged reward signals used by prior RL-based parsers (FD-RL, MonkeyOCR, TRivia) with a decomposed three-component reward (validity, 1D Probe structure, Anchor-Guided Destylization content). The model is built on the HunyuanOCR backbone, positioning it within the lightweight document-parsing VLM lineage while adding a curriculum training strategy that progressively integrates atomic structural skills into unified sequential reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of TableVerse-5K addresses a real gap: existing benchmarks are small (300–700 samples) and lack scenario diversity (handwritten, photographed, complex merged cells), limiting meaningful evaluation of real-world robustness. The finding that a 1B specialized model substantially outperforms much larger general-purpose VLMs (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) suggests that explicit structural decomposition is a more effective inductive bias than raw scale for structured-output tasks. Open questions include whether the sequential reasoning overhead (generating row-column counts and merged-cell tokens before HTML) is cost-effective at inference time, whether the 1D Probe reward generalizes to other autoregressive structured-generation tasks beyond tables, and whether the approach extends to non-HTML output formats or multi-page documents.
