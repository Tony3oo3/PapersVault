## Problem

Unified fashion generation frameworks that share a single LoRA module across semantically distinct tasks (virtual try-on, garment reconstruction, pose transfer) suffer from severe inter-task gradient conflict, causing destructive interference that degrades performance below even independently trained task-specific experts. The paper identifies this gradient cancellation as the fundamental bottleneck and asks how to structurally decouple task updates within a shared low-rank parameter space without incurring the overhead of per-task gradient projection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260348/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All three tasks are handled by a single Diffusion Transformer backbone (LongCat-Image-Edit) that receives task-specific visual conditions concatenated along the sequence dimension: VTON takes a reference model, target garment, and pose skeleton; garment reconstruction takes a reference model and reference garment; pose transfer takes a reference model and target pose skeleton. Text prompts (generated by Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and HRNet-extracted skeletons complete the conditioning. Outputs are 512×384 images for VTON and reconstruction, and 512×352 for pose transfer, with 50 sampling steps at inference.

## Method

OrthoTryOn introduces two complementary mechanisms within a shared LoRA module. First, Orthogonal Subspace Projection (OSP) interposes a task-specific orthogonal matrix Q_i (sampled once from the Haar measure via QR decomposition, then frozen) between the down-projection A and up-projection B, rotating each task's bottleneck features into a distinct isometric coordinate frame; this makes cross-task weight increments exactly decorrelated in expectation and bounds residual gradient interference to O(1/r). Second, Fisher-guided Negative Guidance (FNG) is a parameter-free inference strategy that tracks per-task diagonal Fisher information via an EMA of squared gradients during training, identifies the most confusable task by cosine similarity of Fisher vectors, and substitutes that task's condition for the null prompt in Classifier-Free Guidance, explicitly repelling the generation trajectory from the most correlated semantic sub-manifold. The two components are synergistic: OSP suppresses gradient conflict during training while FNG mitigates residual semantic leakage at generation time, and together they enable the unified model to surpass independently trained task-specific experts across all three fashion tasks.

## Results

On VITON-HD, OrthoTryOn achieves LPIPS 0.064, SSIM 0.876, FID 8.312, and KID 0.532 for virtual try-on, outperforming all unified baselines (e.g., Any2AnyTryon: FID 10.143; LongCat-Image-Edit: FID 10.587) and most single-task VTON experts. For garment reconstruction, it reaches LPIPS 0.192, DISTS 0.191, FID 9.563, and CLIP-I 0.931, surpassing both TryOffDiff (FID 15.273) and TryOffAnyone (FID 11.488). On DeepFashion pose transfer, OrthoTryOn attains LPIPS 0.146, FID 6.364, and CLIP-I 0.936, beating the strongest single-task expert MCLD (FID 7.079, CLIP-I 0.926) by a clear margin on FID and CLIP-I.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unified baselines, OrthoTryOn dominates across all three tasks: on VTON it improves FID from 10.143 (Any2AnyTryon) and 10.587 (LongCat-Image-Edit) to 8.312, and KID from 2.668 to 0.532; on garment reconstruction it cuts FID from 10.771 (Any2AnyTryon) to 9.563 and CLIP-I from 0.907 to 0.931; on pose transfer it reduces FID from 12.250 (Any2AnyTryon) to 6.364. Versus single-task VTON experts, OrthoTryOn (FID 8.312, KID 0.532) beats GP-VTON (FID 11.708), IDM-VTON (FID 10.745), FitDiT (FID 10.340), and OmniVTON (FID 9.621), though CatVTON retains a slight edge on LPIPS (0.057 vs. 0.064) and FID (9.015 vs. 8.312—actually OrthoTryOn wins on FID). For garment reconstruction, OrthoTryOn (LPIPS 0.192, FID 9.563) clearly outperforms TryOffAnyone (LPIPS 0.245, FID 11.488) and TryOffDiff (LPIPS 0.212, FID 15.273). On pose transfer, OrthoTryOn (LPIPS 0.146, FID 6.364, CLIP-I 0.936) surpasses MCLD (LPIPS 0.176, FID 7.079, CLIP-I 0.926) and CFLD (LPIPS 0.182, FID 7.149, CLIP-I 0.922), with a minor SSIM trade-off (0.728 vs. MCLD's 0.756) attributed to using sparse skeletal conditions rather than dense pose maps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified fashion generation paradigm initiated by Any2AnyTryon and UniFit, which share a single LoRA module across tasks but suffer from inter-task gradient conflict. Rather than applying post-hoc gradient surgery (PCGrad, OGD) that requires per-task backward passes, OrthoTryOn performs a forward architectural reparameterization: task-specific orthogonal rotations are inserted into the LoRA bottleneck, structurally decorrelating task updates in expectation. The inference-time Fisher-guided Negative Guidance repurposes Classifier-Free Guidance by replacing the standard null-prompt with the empirically most-confusable task condition, a modification not previously explored in multi-task diffusion settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a properly structured unified model can outperform independently trained task-specific experts challenges the prevailing assumption that multi-task sharing inevitably dilutes per-task capacity. The plug-and-play nature of OSP (a single frozen orthogonal matrix per task per layer) and FNG (parameter-free, using only a discrete task-pair mapping) makes the approach broadly applicable to any multi-task LoRA fine-tuning scenario beyond fashion. The stated O(1/r) residual coupling bound and the observation that non-orthogonal random matrices perform worse than no intervention at all highlight that the specific isometric geometry is essential, leaving open whether richer parameterizations (e.g., block-orthogonal or learned rotations) could further tighten the bound for very small ranks.
