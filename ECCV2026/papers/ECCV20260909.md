## Problem

Existing panoramic 3D datasets suffer from three critical limitations that hinder feed-forward panoramic 3D reconstruction: insufficient scale (fewer than ~11K samples for indoor datasets), poor depth map quality (incomplete and misaligned due to sparse scans and limited pose accuracy), and discontinuous trajectories (discrete viewpoints spaced ~2.25 m apart). These gaps prevent effective fine-tuning of multi-view feed-forward models on panoramas, which are further degraded by spherical distortion from equirectangular projection.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 109,495 equirectangular panoramas at 5760×2880 resolution (24 fps) paired with LiDAR-derived registered point clouds, Poisson-reconstructed meshes, and refined camera poses across 75 scenes (indoor and outdoor) with continuous trajectories averaging 0.29 m inter-frame distance. Depth maps are provided in two complementary forms: dense mesh-based depth (from ray-mesh intersection in equirectangular space) and sparse point-based depth (from point cloud projection with mesh-guided occlusion culling). For benchmark experiments, panoramas are decomposed into 8 uniformly spaced horizontal perspective views as model input, and models output camera poses, point maps, and depth predictions evaluated via ATE, RPE, AUC@30, Accuracy, and Completion.

## Method

Holo360D is constructed via a three-stage pipeline: (1) synchronized capture using a handheld 3D laser scanner (integrating LiDAR, RTK-GNSS, IMU, and pinhole cameras) rigidly coupled with a 360° camera via a shared software trigger, traversing scenes at ~0.3 m/s indoors and 0.6 m/s outdoors with overlapping trajectories for completeness; (2) offline reconstruction applying global bundle adjustment to jointly refine 360° camera poses and align point clouds, followed by point cloud colorization and Poisson surface reconstruction; (3) a post-processing pipeline comprising radius-based denoising with manual inspection, curvature-preserving triangulation for mesh hole filling (with bridge-edge subdivision for large holes), and region-specific remeshing that reconstructs low-quality thin-structure regions from original high-resolution point clouds while retaining high-quality wall/floor geometry. The benchmark fine-tunes π³, VGGT, and FLARE using 8-view perspective splits with mesh depth supervision, demonstrating that Holo360D yields substantially better training signals than Matterport3D and that mesh depth outperforms point depth for pose estimation while point depth provides more accurate geometric ground truth.

## Results

Fine-tuning π³ on Holo360D (8 views, mesh depth) reduces indoor ATE from 0.093 to 0.014 (85% reduction) and outdoor ATE from 0.201 to 0.065 (68% reduction), while improving indoor AUC@30 from 0.733 to 0.837 and point-cloud Accuracy from 0.052 to 0.024. VGGT similarly improves indoor ATE from 0.231 to 0.084 and outdoor AUC@30 from 0.697 to 0.761 after fine-tuning. In the cross-dataset comparison, π³ fine-tuned on Holo360D achieves indoor ATE of 0.014 versus 0.428 for Matterport3D (30× lower) and indoor AUC@30 of 0.837 versus 0.299, demonstrating substantially superior training signal quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against existing panoramic datasets, Holo360D dominates on all quality metrics: it achieves the lowest alignment error (4.69 px vs. 7.99 for Matterport3D, 9.45 for Stanford2D3D, 12.24 for 360Loc, 11.72 for KITTI-360), the highest depth completeness (0.86 indoor / 0.82 outdoor vs. 0.62/0.70 for 360Loc and 0.16 for KITTI-360), and the most continuous trajectories (0.29 m average inter-frame distance vs. 1.01 m for KITTI-360 and 0.49 m for 360Loc). In the direct cross-dataset comparison (Table 5), π³ fine-tuned on Holo360D outperforms the same model fine-tuned on Matterport3D by 30× on indoor ATE (0.014 vs. 0.428) and by 2.8× on indoor AUC@30 (0.837 vs. 0.299), with Matterport3D fine-tuning actually degrading performance below the zero-shot baseline on several metrics. Across the three feed-forward models (π³, VGGT, FLARE), fine-tuning on Holo360D yields consistent improvements: π³ gains the largest absolute improvements (indoor ATE 0.093→0.014), VGGT shows strong relative gains (indoor ATE 0.231→0.084, a 64% reduction), and FLARE improves indoor AUC@30 from 0.256 to 0.515 (2×) and outdoor Accuracy from 0.852 to 0.365. Depth360 [13] is listed in Table 1 but no quantitative metrics are reported due to data unavailability, so no direct numerical comparison is possible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: panoramic 3D datasets (Matterport3D, Stanford2D3D, KITTI-360, 360Loc) and feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (DUST3R, VGGT, π³, FLARE). It departs from prior datasets by combining three previously unavailable properties—large scale (109K panoramas), continuous camera trajectories (0.29 m average inter-frame distance), and high-completeness aligned depth maps—into a single real-world collection. On the model side, it repurposes perspective-image feed-forward architectures for panoramic input by splitting equirectangular images into perspective views, rather than proposing a new panoramic-specific architecture. The data pipeline itself builds on standard SLAM and Poisson surface reconstruction, with the novelty lying in the post-processing steps (denoising, mesh hole filling, region-specific remeshing) tailored to 360° scanning artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Holo360D fills a critical gap: prior panoramic datasets were too small, too sparse in trajectory, or too incomplete in depth to support effective fine-tuning of modern feed-forward 3D models, leaving the field stuck in wide-baseline, few-view configurations. The empirical finding that fine-tuning on Matterport3D actively degrades performance (ATE 0.428 vs. 0.093 baseline) while Holo360D improves it substantially underscores that data quality—alignment and completeness—matters more than mere scale. Open questions include whether panoramic-specific architectural adaptations (latitude-aware losses, panoramic ray attention) can close the remaining gap, whether the 8-view horizontal-only strategy generalizes to non-handheld capture, and whether the findings hold for models beyond the three tested. The dataset's 19-hour, 31 km collection effort also raises practical questions about reproducibility and whether similar quality can be achieved with consumer-grade 360° cameras.
