## Problem

Latent diffusion models achieve computational efficiency through VAE compression but inevitably discard high-frequency visual cues, limiting pixel-level fidelity. Pure pixel-space diffusion avoids this bottleneck but suffers from severe optimization difficulty when a single model must simultaneously learn global low-frequency structure and complex high-frequency details. The paper exploits the temporal heterogeneity of denoising—early timesteps establish coarse structure while late timesteps synthesize fine details—to decouple these two objectives across time within a unified architecture.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise conditioned on either a class label (ImageNet 256×256 or 512×512) or a CLIP-encoded text prompt (MS-COCO 256×256) and produces a synthesized RGB image. Internally, the input is consumed as two parallel token sequences: VAE latents (16× spatial downsample, 32 channels) and raw pixel patches (patch size matched to the VAE downsampling factor so both sequences share the same spatial length). The final output is constructed by adding the VAE-decoded latent scaffold to a high-frequency pixel refinement predicted by the pixel head, with the pixel contribution gated to only the low-noise regime (t < τ, default τ = 0.3).

## Method

Hi-DiT is a dual-stream Diffusion Transformer with a parameter-shared SiT-XL/1 backbone (28 layers) that temporally specializes its two streams: a Latent Stream predicts the flow-matching velocity field on VAE latents throughout the trajectory, while a Pixel Stream, activated only when t < τ via a hard Time-Gated Injection schedule, refines high-frequency details in raw pixel space. The pixel tokens are injected as additive embeddings (h_zx = h_z + G(t)·h_x) so the shared backbone jointly processes both streams without architectural branching. A lightweight High-Frequency Pixel Predictor replaces naive linear projection with two stages of Conv + PixelShuffle(×2) + GroupNorm + SiLU to progressively upsample features by 4×, followed by AdaLN-conditioned linear projection to predict only p/4 × p/4 sub-patches per location, drastically reducing the regression burden. Training uses a composite objective: standard flow-matching loss on the latent stream at all timesteps, plus a reconstruction + LPIPS pixel loss that is active only in the low-noise regime, strictly decoupling semantic convergence from detail synthesis. A DINOv2 alignment loss is additionally applied to strengthen semantic structural learning in the latent pathway.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, Hi-DiT achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.06 with CFG (and 1.66 without CFG), using only 689M parameters. On ImageNet 512×512, it reaches FID 1.26 with CFG=4.6. For text-to-image generation on MS-COCO 256×256, Hi-DiT obtains FID 5.15, outperforming the U-ViT-S/2 baseline (5.48). The model also achieves competitive Inception Scores (296.6 on ImageNet-256, 295.0 on ImageNet-512) and strong precision/recall (0.79/0.66 on ImageNet-256).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hi-DiT (689M) achieves FID 1.06 on ImageNet-256 with CFG, beating the best latent diffusion baseline REPA (1.29) by 0.23 FID and VA-VAE (1.35) by 0.29 FID, while also surpassing the strongest pixel-space model Deco (1.62) by 0.56 FID and the 2B-parameter JiT-G (1.82) by 0.76 FID. Among autoregressive models, it outperforms RAR (1.50) and MAR (1.55) by 0.44 and 0.49 FID respectively. On ImageNet-512, Hi-DiT (1.26) leads the best pixel-space baseline SiD2 (1.50) by 0.24 FID and the best latent model REPA (2.08) by 0.82 FID. On MS-COCO text-to-image, Hi-DiT (5.15) edges out U-ViT-S/2 (5.48) by 0.33 FID and AutoNAT-S (5.36) by 0.21 FID. Notably, Hi-DiT also achieves the best recall (0.66) among all methods on ImageNet-256, indicating superior sample diversity alongside its FID advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hi-DiT sits at the intersection of two well-established diffusion paradigms: latent-space diffusion (SiT, DiT, VA-VAE) and pixel-space diffusion (JiT, PixelDiT, Deco, DiP). It builds directly on SiT's flow-matching transformer backbone and VA-VAE's representation-aligned VAE, but departs from both by introducing a dual-stream architecture where the latent and pixel pathways are temporally specialized rather than mutually exclusive. The Time-Gated Injection mechanism, which activates pixel tokens only in the low-noise regime, is conceptually related to curriculum learning and the known spectral-temporal tendency in denoising, but no prior diffusion model had exploited this heterogeneity through a hard architectural switch within a single shared backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hi-DiT demonstrates that the latent-vs-pixel trade-off in diffusion models can be largely dissolved by assigning each space its natural temporal role, achieving SOTA FID (1.06 on ImageNet-256, 1.26 on ImageNet-512) with only 689M parameters and ~10% inference overhead over pure latent baselines. The Time-Gated Injection and hierarchical sub-pixel predictor are simple, modular components that could be grafted onto existing latent diffusion pipelines. Open questions include whether the hard threshold τ generalizes across architectures and conditioning types, whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, and whether a soft or learned gating schedule could further improve the FID-IS trade-off.
