## Problem

Most video reasoning models follow a batch paradigm that defers all reasoning until the full video is observed, introducing unacceptable latency and unbounded memory growth for streaming scenarios. Existing online video models either lack intermediate reasoning entirely or rely on external classification heads to decide when to respond, failing to unify perception, reasoning, and interaction into a single generative process under strict real-time constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261711/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuous video stream as sequential temporal chunks sampled at 2 FPS with native dynamic resolution, alongside a user instruction or dialogue context. At each step it produces a strictly structured output: a short reasoning segment enclosed in <think> tags followed by an interaction action that is either <silent> (continue observing) or <response> with generated answer content. The reasoning segments are intentionally short (optimal budget of 20 tokens per second of video) to fit within per-chunk latency budgets.

## Method

ThinkStream introduces a Watch-Think-Speak loop where each incoming video chunk triggers a short reasoning update that integrates new evidence with accumulated context, after which the model autonomously decides whether to emit a response or remain silent. The key architectural component is Reasoning-Compressed Streaming Memory (RCSM), which maintains a sliding window of dense visual tokens (default 20 s) while retaining all prior reasoning and response tokens as long-term semantic anchors in the KV cache, so that evicted visual details are replaced by their compressed reasoning summaries, keeping effective context length stable. Training proceeds in two phases: a supervised cold-start on 110K time-grounded CoT instances, followed by streaming RLVR using GRPO with a composite rule-based reward (accuracy + format compliance + temporal alignment via linear decay around the ground-truth response step) on 9K verifiable instances. A custom CUDA Graph-based inference backend separates eager prefill for new visual tokens from replayable decode-and-prune graphs, enabling in-place KV cache eviction during decoding and achieving >5× decoding speedup over standard transformers while bounding end-to-end latency below the 0.5 s real-time threshold.

## Results

On OVO-Bench, ThinkStream-3B achieves an overall average of 59.66, surpassing its base model Qwen2.5-VL-3B (51.00) by 8.66 points and the strongest open-source online baseline StreamForest-7B (56.61) by 3.05 points. On StreamingBench Real-Time, ThinkStream-3B scores 75.00, exceeding GPT-4o (73.28) and all open-source online models, while remaining within 0.69 of Gemini 1.5 Pro (75.69). On offline benchmarks, the model retains competitive performance with 61.9 on VideoMME and 56.4 on Long VideoBench (59.4 average vs. 54.4 for the base model), confirming that aggressive visual-token eviction does not degrade general video understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThinkStream-3B outperforms all open-source online baselines on OVO-Bench by a wide margin: +3.05 over StreamForest-7B (56.61), +8.02 over Streamo-3B (51.64), +14.35 over Dispider-7B (45.31), and +31.78 over Flash-VStream-7B (27.88). It also surpasses its own base model Qwen2.5-VL-3B (51.00) by 8.66 points and even exceeds the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B (57.37) by 2.29 points. On StreamingBench Real-Time, ThinkStream-3B (75.00) beats GPT-4o (73.28) by 1.72 points and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (72.44) by 2.56 points, while trailing only Gemini 1.5 Pro (75.69) by 0.69. Among open-source online models, it leads Dispider-7B (67.63) by 7.37 points and VideoLLM-online-8B (35.99) by 39.01 points. On offline benchmarks, ThinkStream-3B maintains or slightly improves over the base model (61.9 vs. 61.5 on VideoMME; 56.4 vs. 54.2 on Long VideoBench), demonstrating that the streaming-optimized architecture does not sacrifice general video understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate threads: streaming video understanding (VideoLLM-online, Flash-VStream, Dispider, StreamForest) and video-domain chain-of-thought reasoning (Video-R1, Video-Thinker). Prior streaming models rely on perception-response cycles or external classifiers for output timing, while prior reasoning models operate in a batch paradigm over the full video. ThinkStream departs from both by unifying incremental reasoning and autonomous response timing into a single generative loop (Watch-Think-Speak), and by repurposing reasoning traces as compressed semantic memory (RCSM) to replace evicted visual tokens — a conceptual shift from purely visual KV-cache management (StreamKV, StreamMem, Hermes) to reasoning-as-memory. It also introduces the first streaming RLVR training scheme for multimodal video, extending GRPO-based video RL from offline to online settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: interactive assistants, monitoring systems, and embodied agents need continuous video understanding with bounded latency, which batch video-LLMs cannot provide. The RCSM mechanism and the finding that structured reasoning tokens outperform naive caption tokens as long-term memory offer a reusable design principle for any streaming multimodal system. However, the verifiable-reward constraint limits RLVR training to deterministic formats (MCQ, Yes/No, counting), leaving open how the approach generalizes to open-ended streaming dialogue. The evaluation is confined to a single 3B architecture (Qwen2.5-VL) and relatively new benchmarks, so scalability to larger models and more diverse interaction patterns remains to be demonstrated.
