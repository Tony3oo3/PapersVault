## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-video diffusion models frequently violate basic physical laws (dynamics, thermodynamics, optics), and existing DPO-based post-training methods for improving physical plausibility suffer from three key limitations: high computational cost from repeated candidate sampling, poorly matched preference pairs that differ in global layout rather than local physics, and ambiguous global supervision that dilutes learning signals in physically relevant regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing a physical scene as input and produces a 480×720 video as output, fine-tuned on Wan2.1-1.3B and CogVideoX-5B backbones. Training consumes 30K counterfactual preference pairs from the CounterPhy dataset, where each pair consists of a real video (preferred) and a locally physics-violating variant (dispreferred) sharing identical global scene semantics. The physical mask and PC/SA scores from VideoCon-Physics are computed per-sample during training to modulate the loss.

## Method

PhysPO combines a counterfactual data pipeline (CounterPhyPipe) with a physics-aware local DPO objective. CounterPhyPipe constructs preference pairs by using Qwen3-VL to propose implausible physical descriptions, Qwen-Image-Edit to generate an erroneous end frame, and Wan2.1-FLF2V to synthesize a dispreferred video that preserves global layout but violates local physics. At training time, a physics-aware mask is derived via frame-wise 2D FFT (spatial high-pass) and 1D FFT along the temporal axis (temporal high-pass), with adaptive thresholds to localize regions of structural or motion anomalies. The PA-DPO loss concentrates preference supervision on the masked regions with a (1−PC)-weighted penalty, while a Tie-DPO term enforces zero preference difference on unmasked background regions to suppress shortcut gradients. A standard full-video DPO term is added as regularization to preserve global generation quality.

## Results

On VideoPhy, PhysPO-CogVideoX achieves the highest overall Physical Commonsense (PC) score of 49.4 (+57.3% over CogVideoX-5B baseline) and Semantic Adherence (SA) of 74.7 (+18.4%), while PhysPO-Wan reaches PC 42.6 (+77.5%) and SA 67.0 (+36.7%) over Wan2.1-1.3B. On PhyGenBench, PhysPO-CogVideoX attains the best SA of 52.0 (+33.3%) and PC of 64.0 (+56.1%). On VideoPhy2 (action-centric), PhysPO yields a 350.8% improvement over baseline on the Hard track and outperforms PhyGDPO and VideoDPO across all sub-tracks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysPO-CogVideoX outperforms all 14 baselines on overall PC in VideoPhy, beating the strongest physics-aware competitor VideoREPA-CogVideoX (40.1) by 9.3 points and PhysMaster (40.0) by 9.4 points. Over its own CogVideoX-5B baseline, PhysPO gains +57.3% PC and +18.4% SA; over Wan2.1-1.3B, the gains are +77.5% PC and +36.7% SA. On PhyGenBench, PhysPO-CogVideoX (SA 52.0, PC 64.0) surpasses WISA (40.0/43.0), PhyT2V (38.0/42.0), and Cosmos (43.0/14.0) on both metrics. In the head-to-head DPO comparison on VideoPhy2 (Table 4), PhysPO (Overall 0.1470) beats PhyGDPO (0.1407) and VideoDPO (0.1305), with the largest margin on the Hard track (+350.8% vs. baseline vs. +276.3% for PhyGDPO). Notably, PhysPO maintains inference time identical to its base model (170 s for CogVideoX, 180 s for Wan2.1), unlike HunyuanVideo (1080 s) and PhyT2V (1800 s), confirming that the physics-aware local preference optimization adds no inference overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysPO extends the DPO lineage (Rafailov et al. → Diffusion-DPO → Flow-DPO/VideoAlign) by introducing spatially localized preference supervision for video diffusion models. It departs from prior physics-aware DPO methods (PhyGDPO, PhysMaster, PhysCorr) in two key ways: (1) CounterPhyPipe constructs counterfactual preference pairs from real videos with controlled local physical violations rather than independently sampled noise, and (2) a frequency-domain physics mask concentrates the DPO gradient on regions where physical state transitions occur while enforcing neutrality elsewhere. The rectified-flow formulation and Bradley-Terry pairwise structure are inherited directly from the VideoAlign/Flow-DPO framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important gap—video diffusion models routinely violate basic physical laws—and provides both a 30K-pair dataset (CounterPhy) and a principled local-supervision mechanism that practitioners can adopt. The frequency-domain masking strategy is a lightweight, training-free way to identify physically relevant regions, making the approach broadly applicable to any rectified-flow video model. Open questions include whether the heuristic FFT-based mask generalizes to more complex multi-object interactions, whether the approach scales to larger (14B+) models, and whether the VLM-based filtering (VideoCon-Physics) introduces systematic biases that limit the diversity of physical violations captured.
