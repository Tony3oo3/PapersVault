## Problem

SAM2 is a powerful foundation model for segmenting anything in images and videos, but its 212M+ parameter image encoder makes it infeasible to deploy on resource-constrained devices like phones, laptops, and robots. Compressing SAM2 into a lightweight model while preserving its generalizable temporal knowledge and comprehensive multi-granularity segmentation capability is a largely under-explored problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262783/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video sequence of T frames (each H×W×3 RGB) together with a spatial prompt (point, box, or mask) on any frame, and produces M binary segmentation masks of shape T×H×W at different granularity levels (e.g., whole object, parts, subparts) along with per-mask confidence scores. During training, 8-frame sequences are used with a 4×4 grid of spatial prompts; at inference the model is fully promptable and can segment interactively across arbitrary video lengths via its memory module.

## Method

The core contribution is HyperKD, a hypergraph-based knowledge distillation framework that explicitly models two types of knowledge implicitly encoded in SAM2. Temporal HyperKD builds hypergraphs whose nodes are patches or pooled instances within a frame and whose hyperedges (formed via ε-ball distance thresholds) link related nodes across frames, capturing temporal consistency; Granularity HyperKD builds hypergraphs whose nodes are mask-pooled embeddings at different segmentation granularity levels and whose hyperedges connect related entities across levels (object→part→subpart), capturing hierarchical structure. The student is trained to match the teacher's hypergraph incidence matrices (Frobenius-norm loss) in addition to a standard FitNet feature-alignment term. For architecture search, a progressive model contraction strategy iteratively scales down a Hiera backbone over embedding dimensions, block counts, window sizes, and head expansion ratios, selecting configurations that minimize HyperKD loss, yielding MobileSAM2 variants at 5.8M, 10.4M, and 23.7M parameters trained on only ~10% of SA-V data (11K videos) on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the 23M-parameter model, MobileSAM2 with HyperKD achieves J&F scores of 65.8 (MOSE), 72.1 (DAVIS 2017), 69.0 (LVOS), 66.4 (SA-V val), and 67.8 (SA-V test), outperforming the strongest baseline FAKD by 5.2, 7.1, 5.6, 4.4, and 5.1 points respectively. At the 5M scale, HyperKD yields 44.6/51.0/48.8/49.2/49.4 J&F, a 6.9–10.5 point gain over FAKD. On the embodied AI benchmark LIBERO-PRO, MobileSAM2-23M integrated into CaP-Agent0 achieves 21.8% average success rate, approaching the full SAM3-powered agent (23.2%) while using only 23.7M parameters versus 636M for TrackAnything.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperKD consistently outperforms all four feature/relation-based distillation baselines (FitNet, CIRKD, CAT-KD, FAKD) across all five VOS benchmarks at every model size, with the largest margins on DAVIS 2017 (+7.1 over FAKD at 23M) and MOSE (+5.2). Against graph-based distillation methods Context Matters (60.7) and IntRA-KD (60.9) on LVOS, HyperKD achieves 69.0, a 7–8 point gap attributed to hyperedges capturing high-order relationships that pairwise graphs miss. On the embodied AI benchmark LIBERO-PRO, MobileSAM2-23M-CaP-Agent0 (21.8% avg) surpasses π0.5 (12.8%) and SAM2-TinyViT-21M-CaP-Agent0 (19.3%), while remaining within 1.4 points of the full SAM3-powered CaP-Agent0 (23.2%). OpenVLA and π0 both score 0% on LIBERO-PRO, making them non-competitive in this setting. The generalizability experiment on TrackAnything (TAM) shows HyperKD improves TAM-TinyViT-21M from 58.9 to 63.1 and MobileTAM-23M from 60.8 to 66.5 on DAVIS-2017, confirming gains are not architecture-specific. SAM2 Large (212.1M) remains the upper bound, with MobileSAM2-23M closing roughly 80–85% of the teacher–student gap on most benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM2 video segmentation framework by distilling it into lightweight models (5M–23M parameters) using a novel hypergraph-based knowledge distillation method (HyperKD). It builds directly on SAM2's architecture (memory module, prompt encoder, mask decoder are copied from the teacher) and Hiera's hierarchical ViT backbone, while replacing the heavyweight image encoder with progressively contracted variants guided by the distillation loss. The key conceptual departure from prior KD work (FitNet, CIRKD, FAKD, CAT-KD) is the explicit construction of hypergraphs to capture high-order temporal and multi-granularity relationships, rather than relying on pairwise feature or logit alignment. The progressive model contraction strategy is borrowed from X3D, and the paper positions itself as the first to apply hypergraph structures to video segmentation knowledge distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MobileSAM2 makes SAM2-class video segmentation practical on resource-constrained devices (RTX 4060 mobile GPU, 8–13 FPS at 5–24M params), opening deployment scenarios in robotics, drones, and mobile AR that were previously infeasible with the 212M-parameter original. The hypergraph formulation offers a generalizable distillation objective demonstrated to transfer beyond SAM2 to TrackAnything, suggesting broader applicability to other video foundation models. However, absolute performance remains substantially below SAM2 Large (e.g., 69.0 vs 81.7 J&F on LVOS), training uses only ~10% of SA-V data on a single GPU, and the embodied AI evaluation is preliminary with modest success rates (~22%). Open questions include whether the hypergraph construction (which relies on teacher predictions for node initialization) degrades when the teacher is unavailable or when scaling to longer video sequences.
