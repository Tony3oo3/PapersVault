## Problem

Conventional correspondence-driven stereo image compression degrades severely under heavy quantization (propagating reference artifacts to the target view) and in out-of-view regions where no physical cross-view correspondence exists. Existing methods—whether using explicit disparity warping or implicit cross-attention—fundamentally rely on extracting valid context from a degraded reference, making them unable to synthesize missing structures or restore lost high-frequency details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262150/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a stereo RGB image pair (left anchor view x_L and right target view x_R) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed stereo pair. The left view is compressed first via a standard learned image codec; the right view is then conditionally compressed using a synthesized generative reference and the reconstructed left view as hybrid context. An ultra-compact semantic condition extracted from the target view incurs a negligible overhead of ~0.00065 bpp, and the generative reference is produced via single-step latent-space diffusion inference to keep runtime practical (1.60 s per pair on A100).

## Method

The framework operates in three stage-wise trained stages. Stage II is the core novelty: a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 (LDM) backbone is steered by Joint Semantic-Spatial Control, which deploys two parallel ControlNet-style networks—one processing the reconstructed left view as a spatial geometric anchor and another processing an ultra-compact VAE-latent semantic condition from the target view—fused via element-wise summation and injected through zero-convolutions into the U-Net decoder. Stage III introduces a Reliability-Aware Gating module inside the entropy model that predicts a spatial confidence map G ∈ [0,1] by evaluating the synthesized generative features; the generative offset Δμ_gen is element-wise multiplied by G before being added to the geometric offset, so unreliable or structurally misaligned generative content is suppressed and the system falls back to the robust geometric context. The gate is trained with targeted corruption (random dropping or Gaussian noise injection on F_gen) to learn to recognize non-informative generative features. Training proceeds stage-wise with all preceding modules frozen, using a standard rate-distortion loss for compression stages and a combined diffusion-plus-semantic-codec loss for the generative stage.

## Results

On InStereo2K, the proposed framework achieves BDBR savings of 67.64% (PSNR) and 65.22% (MS-SSIM) relative to BPG, outperforming the second-best method BiSIC by 19.56 dB and 4.09 dB respectively. On Cityscapes, it achieves 65.90% (PSNR) and 74.53% (MS-SSIM) BDBR savings, surpassing CAMSIC by 6.16 dB and 4.86 dB. In the low-bitrate regime (~0.07 bpp on InStereo2K), the method reaches 35.75 dB PSNR, exceeding CAMSIC and BiSIC by over 1 dB. The ultra-compact semantic condition adds only 0.00065 bpp overhead, and total inference time is 1.60 s per image pair on an A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best BDBR on all four metric-dataset combinations, decisively outperforming every baseline. Against the strongest learned competitors, it beats BiSIC (ECCV 2024) by 19.56 dB in PSNR-BDBR on InStereo2K and by 8.82 dB in PSNR-BDBR on Cityscapes, while also leading in MS-SSIM by 4.09 dB and 6.79 dB respectively. On Cityscapes, it surpasses CAMSIC (AAAI 2025) by 6.16 dB (PSNR) and 4.86 dB (MS-SSIM), confirming that the generative reference approach outperforms masked-image-modeling-based stereo compression. Traditional codecs (BPG, HEVC, VVC, MV-HEVC) are all substantially outperformed, with VVC being the strongest standard codec at -57.52% PSNR-BDBR on Cityscapes versus the proposed -65.90%. Among learned methods, LDMIC and ECSIC trail by 22–25 dB in PSNR-BDBR on InStereo2K, highlighting the advantage of diffusion-based generative priors over purely attention- or warping-based correspondence mechanisms. The runtime (1.60 s/pair) is comparable to other learned methods (1.29–1.47 s) and orders of magnitude faster than VVC (208.42 s), making the generative overhead a practical trade-off for the >65% bitrate savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the correspondence-driven paradigm that has dominated learned stereo image compression (disparity warping, cross-attention, masked modeling) by replacing explicit cross-view feature alignment with a generative reference synthesis stage. It builds directly on Latent Diffusion Models (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as a frozen backbone and adapts the ControlNet architecture (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) into a dual-branch Joint Semantic-Spatial Control that steers synthesis using both a geometric anchor and an ultra-compact semantic seed. The Reliability-Aware Gating module is a novel contribution that addresses the perception-distortion tradeoff (Blau & Michaeli, 2018) specifically in the context of entropy-model parameter refinement, a problem not previously formalized in stereo compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen, unmodified diffusion model can be effectively repurposed as a reference synthesizer for stereo compression with only ~0.00065 bpp of semantic guidance, challenging the long-standing assumption that inter-view prediction requires explicit geometric correspondences. The Reliability-Aware Gating provides a general recipe for safely integrating stochastic generative priors into objective-metric-optimized pipelines, which could transfer to other compression or reconstruction tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond stereo to multi-view or video settings, whether the frozen SD v1.5 backbone can be replaced by more recent or task-specific diffusion models without retraining, and whether the single-step inference constraint limits the achievable generative quality ceiling.
