## Problem

Current video understanding and generation models lack the ability to perceive or control the flow of time: they cannot reliably detect when playback speed changes, estimate how much a video has been sped up or slowed down, or synthesize motion at a specified temporal rate. This limitation stems from training exclusively on standard frame-rate footage (24–60 fps), which never exposes models to the continuum of temporal speeds, and from the absence of large-scale, well-annotated slow-motion data to bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

For perception tasks, the model consumes a raw video clip and outputs either a binary speed-change flag (with temporal localization) or a continuous playback-speed multiplier in log space. For generation, the model takes an image, a text prompt, and a target speed discretized into 10 logarithmically spaced buckets spanning 0.01× to 1.0×, producing a video whose motion unfolds at that rate. For temporal super-resolution, the input is a low-FPS sequence with realistic motion blur (synthesized by averaging 8 temporally centered frames then subsampling), and the output is an 8× higher-frame-rate, deblurred video.

## Method

The paper introduces two self-supervised perception models: a speed-change detector fine-tuned on VideoMAEv2 using audio pitch-shift cues (time-frequency scaling) as free cross-modal supervision, and a speed estimator trained with a temporal-resampling equivariance loss that enforces predicted speed to scale proportionally when the input video is resampled, plus a small calibration set from Adobe240fps and an iterative re-estimation loop for ultra-slow clips. These models annotate 44,632 in-the-wild slow-motion videos (SloMo-44K, 18M frames, up to 10,000+ raw FPS) sourced from YouTube, Vimeo, and Flickr. For generation, the authors extend Wan2.1-I2V by injecting a sinusoidal positional encoding of the speed bucket into the timestep embedding and modulating per-frame latent features with a speed-conditioned MLP, trained via LoRA on SloMo-44K. For temporal super-resolution, they fine-tune Wan2.1-VACE with LoRA on paired synthetic blurry-to-clear data, enabling joint motion deblurring and 8× frame interpolation.

## Results

On speed-change detection, the model achieves 92.4% test accuracy, substantially outperforming Gemini 2.5 (59.5%) and a flow-based detector (82.5%). For playback speed estimation, the method reaches a Pearson correlation of 0.735 and Spearman rank correlation of 0.706 with RMSE 0.649 (log space), approaching human expert performance (ρ=0.880, RMSE=0.492). On extreme temporal super-resolution from blurred inputs, the model achieves the best scores across all metrics: FloLPIPS 0.067, LPIPS 0.058, FID 12.4, and FVD 134.3. For speed-conditioned video generation, it attains FID 68.4 and FVD 1114.1, outperforming both Wan2.1 and the trajectory-based ATI baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines across every task. In speed estimation, it nearly doubles the Pearson correlation of the strongest prior method (PoM, ρ=0.508) and substantially exceeds Gemini 2.5 (ρ=0.426) and SpeedNet (ρ=0.476), closing most of the gap to human experts (ρ=0.880). In speed-change detection, it surpasses Gemini 2.5 by 32.9 percentage points (92.4% vs. 59.5%) and the SEA-RAFT flow-based detector by 9.9 points. For temporal super-resolution from blurred inputs, it improves FloLPIPS by 32–51% over all four interpolation baselines (FILM 0.099, LDMVFI 0.136, GI 0.123, Wan2.1-VACE 0.126) and reduces FVD by 47–69% relative to the same baselines. In speed-conditioned generation, it achieves lower FID (68.4) and FVD (1114.1) than both Wan2.1 (72.2 / 1266.8) and the trajectory-based ATI (73.4 / 1473.5), while also exhibiting stronger speed controllability as confirmed by optical-flow magnitude ordering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on video pace classification (SpeedNet, Pulse-of-Motion) from discrete speed categories over a narrow range (1/2× to 4×) to continuous speed estimation spanning extreme slow motion (0.01×) to normal speed, using self-supervised signals from audio pitch shifts (time-frequency scaling) and temporal resampling equivariance. For generation, it builds on Wan2.1 and VACE diffusion backbones but introduces explicit speed conditioning via sinusoidal bucket embeddings, departing from prior motion-control approaches (ATI, MotionCtrl, BulletTime) that manipulate trajectories or rescale existing videos rather than synthesizing dynamics at a target temporal rate. The speed-change detector is finetuned from VideoMAEv2, and the temporal super-resolution model extends frame interpolation (FILM, LDMVFI, GI) to handle motion-blurred low-FPS inputs—a setting prior methods largely ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SloMo-44K dataset (44,632 videos, 18M frames, up to 10,000+ FPS) is the largest general-purpose slow-motion collection to date and provides a critical resource for training models that understand fine-grained temporal dynamics. The self-supervised training recipe (audio-pitch-based speed-change detection + resampling-equivariant speed estimation) offers a scalable path to temporal reasoning without expensive manual annotation, with implications for temporal forensics and world-model calibration. Key limitations remain: speed perception degrades on low-motion or deliberately slow content, and the generation models inherit Wan2.1's architectural constraints. Open questions include whether the speed-conditioning mechanism generalizes beyond the 0.01×–1.0× range and whether full fine-tuning (vs. LoRA) would close the remaining gap to human-level speed estimation.
