## Problem

Infrared small target detection in high-resolution images suffers from two compounding issues: single-stage detectors waste most computation on irrelevant background regions, while existing two-stage methods that rely on dense pixel-level prediction (e.g., segmentation) discard critical contextual cues and lack explicit mechanisms to suppress background clutter. Additionally, conventional knowledge distillation for lightweight detectors minimizes global feature discrepancies without leveraging spatial attention priors specific to small target regions, limiting performance gains.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single infrared image (640×512 pixels in the custom UAV and Car datasets) and produces either object-level bounding boxes or pixel-level detection masks, depending on the configured head. Internally, the coarse stage partitions the image into a 20×20 grid of non-overlapping regions and outputs a binary probability mask indicating which grids contain targets; the fine stage then operates only on 3×3 grid patches (k=3) cropped around positive predictions, mapping results back to the full image coordinate space.

## Method

ECFNet is a coarse-to-fine framework with three key mechanisms. In the coarse stage, a Region Binary Classification Network (RBCN) with a four-stage hierarchical backbone reformulates detection as grid-level binary classification on N×N non-overlapping patches, reducing prediction complexity versus dense segmentation; a Denoising-Assisted Training (DAT) strategy injects Gaussian target-like noise into GT masks, concatenates the corrupted masks with RBCN features, and trains an auxiliary branch to reconstruct clean GT masks, forcing the network to exploit surrounding context rather than isolated target responses. In the fine stage, a lightweight YOLOv12-based detector (with YOLOX head, reduced detection heads, and channel-adaptive neck) performs precise localization on ExpSlicer-cropped patches. An Attention Prior-Guided Knowledge Distillation (APKD) module computes cross-attention directly over multi-dimensional spatial features (student as PixelQuery, teacher as AreaKey) to generate dynamic attention weights that modulate student features, guiding the lightweight student to focus on teacher-highlighted critical regions without increasing inference cost.

## Results

On the object-level detection task, ECFNet achieves the best AP50 on all three datasets: 95.87% (UAV), 96.06% (Car), and 89.23% (IRSTD-1k), while maintaining the lowest computational cost (31.7 G FLOPs, 22.7 M params) and highest throughput (90 FPS) among all compared methods. For pixel-level detection, ECFNet attains the best Pd (83.56/85.45/94.91%) and nIoU (56.11/58.21/69.67%) on UAV, Car, and IRSTD-1k respectively, with the lowest false alarm rate (Fa = 11.69/10.52/9.94 ×10⁻⁶). The paper reports an approximately 60% reduction in FLOPs compared to methods with comparable detection performance, and gains of +9.68 and +8.58 in Precision and Recall over the closest two-stage baseline (ESOD-L) at similar computational complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECFNet outperforms all 20 baselines on AP50 across all three datasets. Against the strongest single-stage detector YOLO11-L (AP50: 95.39/92.33/82.19), ECFNet gains +0.48/+3.73/+7.04 on UAV/Car/IRSTD-1k while using only 31.7 G FLOPs vs. 86.6 G (a 63% reduction) and achieving 90 FPS vs. 78 FPS. Compared to the two-stage ESOD-L (AP50: 85.63/93.32/78.33), ECFNet improves AP50 by +10.24/+2.74/+10.90 with comparable FLOPs (31.7 vs. 38.1 G). Against the Transformer-based D-FINE-L (AP50: 93.18/93.68/83.53), ECFNet leads by +2.69/+2.38/+5.70 while requiring only 35% of the FLOPs (31.7 vs. 90.7 G) and running more than 2× faster (90 vs. 40 FPS). For pixel-level detection, ECFNet surpasses the best infrared segmentation method PConv (MSHNet) by +2.30/+2.19/+2.71 in Pd on UAV/Car/IRSTD-1k and by +1.88/+2.23/+1.74 in nIoU, while also achieving the lowest false alarm rate. The heaviest baselines (DQ-DETR: 578.5 G, QueryDet: 888.4 G FLOPs) are dominated by ECFNet on both accuracy and efficiency, confirming the advantage of the coarse-to-fine design for high-resolution infrared imagery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coarse-to-fine detection paradigm established by ESOD and QueryDet, but departs from their dense pixel-level segmentation in the coarse stage by reformulating it as grid-level binary classification (RBCN), which reduces prediction count and preserves contextual information. The fine-stage detector is a customized YOLOv12 with a YOLOX head, and the knowledge distillation strategy (APKD) builds on cross-attention mechanisms but explicitly preserves multi-dimensional spatial structure rather than flattening features into 1D tokens as in prior KD work (FitNets, DCSF, CrossKD). The denoising-assisted training (DAT) module is conceptually distinct from DN-DETR's denoising queries: it operates on region features by injecting target-like Gaussian noise into GT masks to force context-aware discrimination, rather than stabilizing query matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~60% FLOPs reduction while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy on three IRSTD datasets makes this framework practically relevant for real-time UAV surveillance and embedded deployment, where processing entire high-resolution infrared frames is prohibitive. The DAT finding that naive GT-mask concatenation actually degrades performance while noisy GT masks improve it offers a useful training insight for small-object detection more broadly. Open questions include whether the grid-based coarse stage generalizes to truly massive images (beyond 640×512), whether the APKD strategy transfers to other modalities or detection tasks, and whether the 20×20 grid resolution is optimal across all target scale distributions.
