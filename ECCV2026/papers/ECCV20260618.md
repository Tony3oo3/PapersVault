## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D world generation pipelines enforce multi-view motion consistency by sequentially generating each view and updating an explicit 3D representation before proceeding to the next, creating a severe computational bottleneck (hours for a handful of views). This sequential dependency makes large-scale dynamic world generation impractical for AR/VR and interactive applications, and outpainting-based consistency fails across non-overlapping views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260618/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image, a free-text world description, and a user-specified camera path of N poses as input, along with motion prompts prepended with 'Camera is strictly fixed' to constrain each generated video to its viewpoint. It produces a unified 3D dynamic world represented as 3D Gaussian Splatting with decoupled foreground (fully time-varying geometry and appearance) and background (static geometry, time-varying color) components, alongside the intermediate multi-view consistent videos (37 frames per view at 512×512). End-to-end generation completes in 15–30 minutes on 8 A40 GPUs.

## Method

The core contribution is a Spatial Token Memory that stores key-value tokens from transformer-based video generators together with their estimated 3D positions, enabling parallel generation of all views while sharing motion context. During generation, KV tokens from one view are back-projected into 3D (using token depths estimated via the posterior mean of the flow-matching model and aligned to the static world's rendered depth maps), then re-projected into other views and injected into attention layers with RoPE-encoded 2D positions, so each view's generator attends to geometrically consistent tokens from all other views. The pipeline has three stages: (1) building a static 3D world via Gaussian Splatting initialized from monocular depth/normal estimates and expanded by iterative outpainting along the camera path; (2) parallel video generation using a fine-tuned HunyuanVideo 1.5 flow-matching model augmented with the Spatial Token Memory; and (3) reconstructing the final dynamic world by separately optimizing foreground Gaussians (time-varying position, rotation, scale, opacity, color) and background Gaussians (static geometry, time-varying color) from the generated multi-view videos.

## Results

On the WorldScore benchmark, DYNWORLD achieves the highest average score of 66.11, substantially outperforming the next best methods (Voyager at 54.53, WonderWorld at 50.88, LucidDreamer at 49.28). DYNWORLD is the only method with non-zero dynamics scores, recording 48.90 on Dynamics Only, 45.41 Motion Accuracy, and 76.64 Motion Smoothness, while also leading on Camera Control (93.27), 3D Consistency (85.36), and Photo Consistency (83.29). On VideoScore metrics, DYNWORLD achieves the best score on all five dimensions (CLIP 24.55, VQ 2.781, TC 2.447, TA 2.997, FC 2.235). End-to-end generation of 8 views takes 18–22 minutes, roughly a 10× speedup over DynamicVoyager's 2 h 56 min.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the five static world-generation baselines (LucidDreamer, WonderJourney, InvisibleStitch, WonderWorld, Voyager), DYNWORLD wins on the average WorldScore (66.11 vs. 44.63–54.53) and is the only method that produces any dynamics, scoring 48.90 on Dynamics Only where all five record 0.00. Among the two dynamic baselines, T2V-Turbo achieves a lower average (40.20) and far weaker 3D/Photo/Style consistency (38.72/34.84/49.65 vs. 85.36/83.29/70.23), while 4D-Fy has a slightly higher Motion Smoothness (80.06 vs. 76.64) but collapses on every other quality metric (Content Align 0.85, Photo Consist 1.59, Subjective Qual 0.89). On the VideoScore benchmark, DYNWORLD outperforms the three outpainting-based baselines (MOTIA, VACE, DynVoyager) on all five metrics, with the largest gaps in Temporal Consistency (2.447 vs. 1.553–1.952) and Factual Consistency (2.235 vs. 1.858–2.003). Qualitatively, DYNWORLD also beats the denoising-score-stitching method SyncSDE on sky-dynamics generation, producing seamless cross-view motion where SyncSDE shows visible seams at view boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic 3D world generation line initiated by methods like DynamicVoyager and WonderWorld, which rely on sequential outpainting to maintain multi-view consistency at the cost of hours-long generation times. It departs from both outpainting-based pipelines and denoising-score stitching methods (e.g., SyncSDE) by introducing a Spatial Token Memory that stores transformer KV tokens with estimated 3D positions, enabling parallel generation across views through geometrically re-projected cross-view attention. The approach builds directly on HunyuanVideo 1.5 as the flow-matching backbone and 3D Gaussian Splatting for scene representation, but the key conceptual departure is treating video generator latent tokens as implicitly 3D-structured entities that can be shared across cameras via back-projection and RoPE re-encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× speedup (3 hours → 15–30 minutes) makes large dynamic world generation practically viable for AR/VR, game development, and robotics simulation, where sequential pipelines are prohibitive. The Spatial Token Memory mechanism is a lightweight, plug-in-style contribution that could be adopted by other video generation pipelines seeking multi-view consistency without architectural overhaul. Open questions include the dependence on external depth estimators (a vulnerability the authors acknowledge), the scalability of KV token memory to very large worlds, and whether the parallelism advantage persists as the number of views grows beyond the 7–8 views tested here.
