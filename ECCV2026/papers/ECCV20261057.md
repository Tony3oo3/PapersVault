## Problem

Language-aligned vision foundation models like CLIP produce powerful but opaque representations, and existing concept bottleneck approaches that decompose them into interpretable concepts suffer from poor spatial grounding, flat or limited-granularity hierarchies, and are restricted to global tasks like image classification. This paper addresses the gap of providing spatially localized, multi-granularity, hierarchically organized concepts that enable interpretable decision-making across all downstream vision tasks, not just classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261057/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and processes it through a frozen CLIP-DINOiser ViT-B/16 backbone to obtain semantically smoothened patch-level embeddings (16×16 patches). The SAE operates per-patch with 8192 concept dimensions and a BatchTopK sparsity of K=12 active concepts per patch, producing spatially grounded concept activation heatmaps at patch resolution. Downstream, these concept representations are aggregated or upsampled (via AnyUp to pixel level) to yield classification scores, steerable image captions through a Gemma-2-2B language model, or open-vocabulary segmentation masks with per-pixel concept-based explanations.

## Method

CFM trains a Matryoshka Sparse Autoencoder with BatchTopK sparsity on DINOised CLIP patch embeddings, where the Matryoshka reconstruction loss encourages early neurons to capture coarse concepts and later neurons to encode fine-grained residuals, yielding a natural multi-granularity concept space. Parent-child relationships between concepts are discovered by computing a weighted conditional co-occurrence matrix over local patch activations: concept u is a parent of v if u is active whenever v is active above a threshold, leveraging the spatial locality to avoid spurious image-level correlations. Concept labeling is made hierarchy-aware by summing the SAE decoder vectors of all parent concepts with the child's own decoder vector before matching against a large CLIP text vocabulary, so that fine-grained child concepts inherit semantic context from their parents. The entire concept extraction is unsupervised, requiring no concept annotations, and the resulting concept space is language-aligned through the inherited CLIP text encoder, enabling direct association with natural language descriptions.

## Results

On image classification, CFM achieves 78.9% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet and 55.4% on Places365, competitive with the strongest concept bottleneck baselines (DN-CBM: 79.5%/55.1%) while being the only method providing spatially grounded concepts. On open-vocabulary segmentation, CFM reaches 41.3 mIoU average across eight benchmarks (41.9 with AnyUp upsampling), matching the opaque CLIP-DINOiser backbone (41.4/42.2) and outperforming all specialized segmentation architectures. For concept quality, CFM substantially outperforms prior grounded methods on locality (44.3/44.5 vs. 32.2/34.6 for PatchSAE on Part/Stuff) and consistency (15.3/13.1 vs. 13.1/12.7), while also achieving the highest C²-Score (0.465) for annotation-free grounding consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On classification (Fig. 6), CFM (78.9% IMN / 55.4% Places) is within 0.6% of DN-CBM (79.5/55.1) and clearly outperforms D-CBM (70.5/50.9), LF-CBM (75.4/50.6), and SALF-CBM (76.3/49.4), while being the only method alongside SALF-CBM to provide spatially grounded concepts. On open-vocabulary segmentation (Table 1), CFM matches the opaque CLIP-DINOiser backbone almost exactly (41.3 vs. 41.4 avg mIoU) and trails CLIP-DINOiser+AnyUp by only 0.3 points, while outperforming all specialized segmentation methods (TCL 33.9, GroupViT 29.4, MaskCLIP 27.1) by 4–14 mIoU. On concept quality (Fig. 3), CFM dominates both grounded baselines: locality improves from 32.2 (PatchSAE) to 44.3 on PartImageNet, and C²-Score rises from 0.295 to 0.465. For captioning (Fig. 7), CFM maintains performance comparable to its opaque CLIP-DINOiser backbone while uniquely enabling concept-level steering (e.g., removing 'cake' while preserving 'drink'). The key differentiator is that CFM is the only method in all comparisons that simultaneously provides spatially grounded, hierarchically organized, and accurately named concepts without sacrificing downstream task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) concept bottleneck models (CBMs) that decompose vision encoder representations into human-interpretable concepts, (2) sparse autoencoders (SAEs) as an unsupervised mechanism for learning such concept dictionaries, and (3) CLIP-DINOiser, which provides semantically smoothened local patch features from a language-aligned backbone. The key departure from prior CBMs (DN-CBM, SALF-CBM, PatchSAE) is the shift from global, image-level concepts to patch-wise, spatially grounded concepts trained via a Matryoshka SAE with BatchTopK sparsity, enabling the concept layer to serve as a universal bottleneck for any downstream task rather than just classification. The hierarchy-aware labeling scheme and co-occurrence-based parent-child discovery are novel contributions that go beyond the flat concept sets used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFM is the first concept-based vision foundation model that provides interpretable, spatially grounded explanations across classification, open-vocabulary segmentation, and captioning simultaneously, removing the prior restriction of CBMs to global classification. This opens the door to interpretable steering of vision-language models and safety-critical applications where understanding *why* a model made a decision matters. Limitations include residual gaps in concept naming accuracy, the lack of standardized evaluation protocols for unsupervised concept explanations, and the current restriction to a single CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone. Future directions highlighted include scaling to web-scale concept vocabularies and integrating interpretable vision tokens into large vision-language models for tasks like VQA.
