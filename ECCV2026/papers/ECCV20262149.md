## Problem

In point-supervised crowd counting, Hungarian matching pairs proposals to ground-truth points using a globally fixed, scale-agnostic geometric cost. In congested regions, nearby targets induce comparable costs for the same proposal, causing neighbor-induced misassignment that corrupts set-based supervision and destabilizes training. No prior work explicitly calibrates the geometric matching cost to the local spacing of each target.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262149/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images annotated with 2D point centers (no scale or box information) and produces a set of Q proposals, each carrying a foreground confidence score and a regressed 2D coordinate; the crowd count equals the number of matched proposals. Geometric matching costs are evaluated on fixed anchor reference points rather than the regressed coordinates, while the localization loss is applied to the regressed points, decoupling assignment stability from regression noise. At inference, full resized images are processed without tiling, and no post-processing (e.g., NMS or peak extraction) is required.

## Method

SAH-matcher is a drop-in replacement for standard Hungarian assignment that rescales the geometric cost per target using a k-nearest-neighbor local spacing prior. For each ground-truth point, the mean distance to its k nearest neighbors defines a spacing value d_i, which is inverted into a modulation weight α_i = max(α_min, κ/(d_i + ε)); smaller spacing yields a larger α_i, tightening the effective matching region in dense areas while preserving tolerance in sparse ones. The spacing-aware geometric cost C_geo = α_i·‖u_j − g_i‖₂ is added to the classification cost in the same additive form as the baseline, preserving the cardinality-constrained one-to-one Hungarian formulation. Built on P2PNet with anchor-based matching, the method requires no architectural or inference changes. The paper further introduces Competitive Ambiguity Score (CAS) and Hijacking Rate (HR) as process-level diagnostics to quantify within-epoch assignment ambiguity and severe neighbor-hijacking failures, complementing cross-epoch Instability Rate (IR).

## Results

On SHHA, SAH-matcher achieves 47.23 MAE / 75.17 RMSE, outperforming the strongest point-based baselines APGCC (48.80/76.70) and PET (49.34/78.77). On UCF-QNRF it reaches 76.91 MAE, the best among all evaluated methods, and on NWPU-Crowd it attains 68.90 MAE, also the lowest. For localization, SAH-matcher achieves the best F1 of 48.9% at σ=4 on SHHA and 72.1% at σ_s on NWPU-Crowd, with the largest localization gains under stricter thresholds where cross-target misassignment is most damaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among point-based methods, SAH-matcher achieves the best MAE on SHHA (47.23 vs. APGCC 48.80, PET 49.34), UCF-QNRF (76.91 vs. PET 79.53, APGCC 80.10), and NWPU-Crowd (68.90 vs. APGCC 71.40, PET 74.40). However, it trails APGCC on SHHB (6.14 vs. 5.60 MAE) and JHU-Crowd++ (58.32 vs. 54.30 MAE), where the paper attributes the gap to distribution shift and annotation noise dominating the error budget rather than assignment ambiguity. On localization, SAH-matcher edges out APGCC on SHHA at σ=4 (F1 48.9% vs. 48.7%) and clearly leads on NWPU-Crowd at the stricter σ_s threshold (F1 72.1% vs. 68.9%), while APGCC retains a slight advantage at σ=8 on SHHA (78.4% vs. 78.1%). Against map-based methods, SAH-matcher surpasses the best map-based entries (HMODE+REL, MAN) on SHHA, UCF-QNRF, and NWPU-Crowd in MAE, confirming that the point-based formulation with spacing-aware matching is competitive with or superior to density-map regression. The plug-in experiments (Table 4) show that adding SAH to P2PNet, CLTR, PET, and P2R consistently reduces MAE by 1–8 points across datasets, with the largest gains on denser benchmarks, validating its transferability as a drop-in matcher replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the P2PNet point-based crowd counting framework and its DETR-style Hungarian matching formulation, positioning SAH-matcher as a drop-in replacement at the assignment level rather than a new architecture. It departs from prior point-based methods (CLTR, PET, APGCC, P2R) that all share a globally fixed, scale-agnostic geometric cost in Hungarian assignment, by introducing a per-target rescaling derived from kNN local spacing. The paper also extends the diagnostic toolkit beyond the previously used Instability Rate (IR) by adding Competitive Ambiguity Score (CAS) and Hijacking Rate (HR) to explicitly characterize within-epoch assignment failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The drop-in nature of SAH-matcher makes it immediately applicable across existing point-based frameworks without architectural changes, and the demonstrated portability across P2PNet, CLTR, PET, and P2R suggests broad practical utility for practitioners working on point-supervised counting. However, the method's reliance on a kNN spacing prior introduces a clear vulnerability: under severe annotation corruption (8 px jitter), the calibration factor becomes unreliable and performance degrades below baseline, limiting applicability in noisy-annotation regimes. Open questions include whether learned or data-driven spacing estimates could replace the geometric kNN prior, and whether the spacing-aware cost modulation transfers to other set-prediction tasks beyond crowd counting.
