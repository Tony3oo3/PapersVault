## Problem

Deep networks remain vulnerable to natural corruptions (noise, blur, contrast shifts), yet prior robustness methods—data augmentation, regularization, and bio-inspired front-ends—deliver gains almost exclusively in deeper layers while leaving shallow layers essentially unchanged. This paper identifies a 'robustness fading' phenomenon: shallow subnetworks spontaneously develop robust representations and flat loss landscapes during early training, but standard convergence systematically erodes these properties, and no existing method explicitly preserves them.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-time intervention that does not alter the input/output interface: it consumes standard RGB images (clean for training, corrupted for evaluation only) and produces the same outputs as the underlying backbone—class labels for ImageNet classification, bounding boxes for COCO detection, and pixel-wise masks for ADE20K/Cityscapes/ACDC segmentation. The only 'input' to the intervention itself is a scalar intervention epoch τ and, for AWR, a snapshot epoch t_early; no additional data, parameters, or architectural modules are introduced.

## Method

The paper proposes two parameter-free trajectory interventions on the shallow subnetwork θ_s while the deep subnetwork θ_d continues standard ERM optimization. Early-Phase Stabilization (EPS) simply freezes θ_s after a critical epoch τ_eps (Δθ_s ← 0 for all t ≥ τ_eps), locking in the robust configurations that emerge during the initial exploration phase. Asymmetric Weight Reversion (AWR) snapshots θ_s at an early epoch t_early, then at a later trigger epoch τ_awr reverts θ_s to that snapshot and reduces its learning rate to η_fine, allowing the deep layers to realign while the shallow layers remain anchored in a flatter, more robust region of parameter space. The interventions are motivated by empirical evidence from CKA/InfoNCE representation analysis and loss-landscape curvature measurements showing that early-phase shallow weights yield significantly smoother perturbation landscapes than late-phase weights, and they require no architectural modification, auxiliary loss, or learnable parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-C with ResNet-50, AWR achieves 43.1% Top-1 accuracy (+3.9 over the 39.2% baseline), while AWR with an improved training recipe (AWR*) reaches 46.5% (+7.3). EPS achieves 42.5% (+3.3). Across diverse architectures on ImageNet-100 variants, AWR reduces average mCE by up to 11.5 (WideResNet-50) and 1.8 (MambaOut-femto), and EPS reduces it by up to 4.7 (EfficientFormer-L1). On downstream tasks, the framework yields +4.4% mAP on COCO-C (YOLOv5) and +3.7% mIoU on Cityscapes-C (DeepLabV3+).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AWR (43.1%) outperforms all optimization-based baselines—SAM (39.8%), DAMP (41.4%), and DAT (41.1%)—by 1.7–3.3 points, and surpasses structural-prior methods EWS (40.6%), VOneNet (40.3%), DST (38.7%), and Gabor Layers (37.5%) by 2.5–5.6 points. The only method that edges out AWR is AdaSAP_P (43.3%), a margin of just 0.2 points; however, AWR with an improved training recipe (AWR*, 46.5%) clearly surpasses AdaSAP_P by 3.2 points. EPS (42.5%) trails AdaSAP_P by 0.8 points but still exceeds all other baselines except AWR and AWR*. In the integration experiments (Table 3), both EPS and AWR provide additive mCE reductions of 0.5–1.9 when stacked on top of AugMix, AutoAug, CutMix, Label Smoothing, and Dropout, confirming complementarity rather than redundancy with existing augmentation and regularization strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing corruption robustness as a temporal training-dynamics problem rather than a static architectural or optimization one. It draws on the robust overfitting literature (adversarial robustness peaking early then declining) and the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (weight rewinding) but applies these ideas to natural corruptions and specifically to shallow subnetworks, where prior layer-wise robustness work (EWS, DST, AdaSAP) and bio-inspired front-ends (VOneNet, Gabor) have shown persistent underperformance. The key departure is that no architectural modification or auxiliary loss is needed—simply freezing or reverting shallow weights during training suffices, positioning the contribution as a trajectory-intervention framework orthogonal to existing augmentation, regularization, and structural-prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'robustness fading' in shallow layers provides a concrete, actionable diagnosis for why standard training underutilizes early-layer robustness capacity, and the parameter-free EPS/AWR interventions offer a low-cost plug-in for existing pipelines. However, the Discussion section candidly notes the mechanism is most relevant to low-level visual corruptions and may not address broader semantic or sensor-domain distribution shifts. Open questions include a theoretical account of why early shallow features are intrinsically flatter, whether optimal intervention timing generalizes across tasks and modalities, and how to reconcile the clean-accuracy trade-offs observed in several architectures (e.g., WideResNet-50, MambaOut) with the corruption gains.
