## Problem

Existing hybrid mesh-3DGS head avatar methods couple the two representations only through spatial constraints in 3D space (e.g., binding Gaussian centers near mesh surfaces), leaving them optimized independently with indirect and incomplete feature exchange. This prevents the mesh from providing stable low-frequency geometry while Gaussians capture high-frequency residuals, and leads to structural failures such as opacity collapse and hollow artifacts in sparsely observed regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261602/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular video frames (resized to 512×512) with FLAME parameters (300 identity, 100 expression, 15 pose, 3 translation coefficients) tracked via VHAP, and produces a unified UV-space representation at 2048×2048 resolution consisting of shared attribute maps (spherical-harmonic color, surface normal, displacement) and Gaussian-specific maps (opacity, anisotropic scale) plus a set of UV-parameterized Gaussian positions. The final rendering composites a mesh layer and a Gaussian layer via alpha blending, achieving 62 FPS real-time output after 120 minutes of training on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

URHead reformulates both mesh geometry and Gaussian attributes as dense functions over a shared FLAME UV domain, so that the color, normal, and displacement maps are jointly consumed by a differentiable mesh rasterizer (nvdiffrast) and a modified 3DGS rasterizer with occlusion-aware depth culling. Because both renderers read the same UV maps, backpropagation produces explicit cross-renderer gradient coupling: the Gaussian photometric loss directly refines mesh geometry through the shared normal and displacement maps, and vice versa. For Gaussian placement, the method projects per-pixel photometric errors back onto the UV surface, accumulates them over the video sequence with visibility masking, and adaptively samples new Gaussian primitives in proportion to the accumulated error, concentrating primitives where the fixed-topology mesh lacks capacity. Training minimizes a composite loss of L1/SSIM photometric terms (applied to both the composited image and the mesh-only rendering), DAViD-supervised normal consistency, and Laplacian geometric regularization over 50K iterations.

## Results

On the INSTA dataset (5 subjects), URHead achieves an average PSNR of 35.18 dB (+1.03 over the best baseline RGBAvatar), SSIM of 0.9675, and LPIPS of 0.0149 (vs. 0.0251 for RGBAvatar), demonstrating clear gains in both pixel-level and perceptual quality. On PointAvatar and NerFace, URHead achieves competitive PSNR (28.20 and 31.63 dB) and SSIM, while consistently achieving the best LPIPS (0.0458 and 0.0312 respectively). For animation stability, URHead attains the lowest frame-to-frame LPIPS mean (0.0153) and variance (0.000010) on INSTA, roughly 2× lower than the next-best baseline (RGBAvatar at 0.0267).

## Baselines & Comparisons

URHead is compared against six mesh-3DGS hybrid methods: SplattingAvatar (triangle binding), GaussianAvatars (FLAME-bound Gaussians), FlashAvatar (UV-space allocation with MLP offsets), GaussianBlendShape (Gaussian-space blendshapes), FateAvatar (importance-based sampling + neural baking), and RGBAvatar (reduced Gaussian blendshapes). On the INSTA dataset, URHead clearly outperforms all baselines on every metric, with the largest margin on LPIPS (0.0149 vs. 0.0251 for RGBAvatar, a 41% reduction) and PSNR (+1.03 dB over RGBAvatar). On PointAvatar and NerFace, URHead trails RGBAvatar by a small margin on PSNR (−0.26 and −0.33 dB) and SSIM (−0.009 and −0.004), but achieves the best LPIPS on both datasets (0.0458 vs. 0.0501 for FateAvatar on PointAvatar; 0.0312 vs. 0.0370 for RGBAvatar on NerFace). The paper attributes the PointAvatar/NerFace PSNR gap partly to the yufeng subject whose long hair extends beyond FLAME topology. For animation stability (frame-to-frame LPIPS on INSTA), URHead achieves 0.0153 mean and 0.000010 variance, roughly 2× lower than RGBAvatar (0.0267) and 2.1× lower than FateAvatar (0.0325), indicating substantially more temporally consistent renderings under expression-driven deformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

URHead builds on FLAME's parametric topology and UV parameterization as a structural backbone, and on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the high-fidelity rendering primitive. It departs from the dominant hybrid paradigm (GaussianAvatars, SplattingAvatar, FlashAvatar, RGBAvatar) that couples Gaussians to mesh surfaces through 3D-space spatial constraints—barycentric binding, normal displacement, or proximity—by instead reformulating both representations as functions over a shared UV domain, enabling true parameter-level gradient coupling rather than indirect geometric interaction. The error-driven adaptive Gaussian allocation in UV space also distinguishes it from standard 3DGS densification, which operates in unstructured 3D space without surface awareness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UV-space unification offers a clean conceptual resolution to a persistent weakness in mesh-GS hybrids: spatial binding in 3D prevents the two representations from mutually refining each other, leading to over-pruning artifacts and incomplete feature exchange. The notably lower frame-to-frame LPIPS (0.0153 vs. 0.0267 for the best baseline) suggests the shared parameterization genuinely improves temporal stability under animation, which is critical for real-time applications. Limitations include degraded performance on subjects with geometry extending beyond FLAME topology (e.g., long hair), and the approach is currently specific to head avatars; whether the UV-space unification principle generalizes to full-body or non-parametric settings remains an open question.
