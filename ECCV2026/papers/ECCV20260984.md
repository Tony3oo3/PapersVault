## Problem

Blind Face Restoration is an ill-posed inverse problem where a single low-quality input maps to multiple plausible high-quality solutions, yet existing diffusion-based methods produce a single deterministic output without exploring this solution space. This lack of exploration causes models to converge toward average solutions with missing details and identity ambiguity. No prior work has applied online reinforcement learning to systematically search the BFR solution space for optimal restorations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260984/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 low-quality RGB face image with unknown degradations (Gaussian blur, downsampling, noise, JPEG compression) and produces a 512×512 high-quality restored face. Rather than outputting a single image, the policy network generates G diverse restoration candidates per input during training, from which relative advantages are computed to guide optimization. The framework operates in latent space (via LDM) and is designed as a post-training module that can be applied to any pre-trained diffusion-based restoration model, including few-step solvers like InterLCM.

## Method

LRPO formulates the DDIM denoising process as a Markov Decision Process and applies a PPO-style clipped policy gradient with GRPO-style group-relative advantage normalization over G sampled trajectories per input. Three key innovations tailor this to BFR: (1) a composite reward combining a Face Reward Model for human preference, CLIPIQA for perceptual quality, and a feature-similarity/wavelet fidelity term; (2) a ground-truth-guided likelihood regularization that replaces the standard KL penalty, anchoring the policy to the true data manifold only in the final S denoising steps to prevent reward hacking while allowing escape from inferior priors; and (3) a noise-level advantage assignment that weights each timestep's advantage proportionally to σ_t, giving greater credit to early high-exploration steps. The framework is initialized from a fully-trained DiffBIR checkpoint (freezing SwinIR, updating only the second-stage ControlNet) and requires only a small FFHQ subset, making it a highly data-efficient post-training paradigm.

## Results

On CelebA-Test, LRPO achieves state-of-the-art performance on 9 out of 10 metrics, including SSIM (0.7021), PSNR (26.15), CLIP Score (0.9100), Deg. (31.19°), LMD (1.9533), MUSIQ (75.24), MANIQA (0.6808), Aesthetic (5.8126), and CLIPIQA (0.8061). The only metric where LRPO trails is LPIPS (0.3635 vs. CodeFormer's 0.3335). On real-world datasets, LRPO achieves the best MUSIQ (74.60 on LFW-Test, 72.71 on WebPhoto-Test) and CLIPIQA (0.8073 on LFW-Test, 0.7040 on WebPhoto-Test), with the largest gain over DiffBIR being +5.26 MUSIQ on WebPhoto-Test. A user study with 30 participants confirms human preference for LRPO over DiffBIR at 60.6% for fidelity and 70.5% for realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRPO outperforms its base model DiffBIR on all 10 metrics, with the most notable gains in CLIPIQA (+0.0861), Deg. (+3.97°), and LMD (+0.3128), confirming that RL exploration improves both perceptual quality and identity fidelity. Against the strongest non-diffusion baseline CodeFormer, LRPO wins on 9/10 metrics (losing only on LPIPS: 0.3635 vs. 0.3335) while achieving substantially better identity preservation (Deg. 31.19° vs. 31.08°) and perceptual quality (CLIPIQA 0.8061 vs. 0.6493). Among diffusion-based competitors, LRPO surpasses InterLCM (the closest rival) on SSIM, PSNR, CLIP Score, Deg., LMD, MUSIQ, MANIQA, Aesthetic, and CLIPIQA, though InterLCM retains a slight LPIPS edge (0.3349 vs. 0.3635). FlowIE, which uses ODE solvers for fast inference, trails LRPO on all metrics except LPIPS. On real-world data (LFW-Test, WebPhoto-Test), LRPO achieves the best MUSIQ and CLIPIQA scores, with the largest real-world gain being +5.26 MUSIQ over DiffBIR on WebPhoto-Test, where severe degradation causes most baselines to fail at texture recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

LRPO sits at the intersection of two active research threads: diffusion-based blind face restoration (DiffBIR, DifFace, OSEDiff, FlowIE, InterLCM) and RL-for-diffusion alignment (DDPO, DPOK, Flow-GRPO, TempFlow-GRPO). It departs from prior BFR methods by rejecting their deterministic single-trajectory sampling in favor of multi-candidate exploration, and departs from prior RL+diffusion work (which targets text-to-image generation) by introducing three BFR-specific modifications: a composite fidelity-perception reward, GT-guided likelihood regularization replacing the standard KL penalty, and noise-level-weighted advantage assignment. The framework is explicitly a post-training layer on top of a frozen DiffBIR/ControlNet backbone, positioning it as a lightweight alignment step rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that online RL can be applied as a data-efficient post-training step to improve face restoration, LRPO opens a path toward reward-guided exploration for other ill-posed inverse problems (general image restoration, inpainting, deblurring) where deterministic mappings are known to be suboptimal. The finding that KL divergence actively degrades BFR performance—because the reference model's blurry priors are worse than what RL can discover—challenges the default assumption that KL anchoring is always beneficial in RL fine-tuning of generative models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more diverse or unknown degradation types, how it behaves with larger group sizes or longer training, and whether the GT-guided regularization can be replaced by self-supervised signals to remove the need for paired training data.
