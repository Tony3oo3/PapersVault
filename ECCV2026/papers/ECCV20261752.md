## Problem

Existing Generalized Category Discovery methods formulate inference as a single-path global embedding that implicitly performs only hypothesis formation, omitting any explicit verification mechanism. This causes the model to over-rely on pretrained semantics and confidently misclassify unknown categories by gravitating toward the nearest known prototype, a structural failure the paper terms 'inference truncation.'
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261752/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HVGCD operates as a plug-and-play module in the embedding space of a pretrained ViT (DINOv1 or DINOv2), consuming the full token sequence H = [x_cls, x_1, ..., x_N] produced by the backbone. It outputs a structured instance representation z that fuses three components—global hypothesis, hypothesis-conditioned verification evidence, and maximum-entropy statistical grounding—which is then fed into the downstream GCD clustering or classification objective. No architectural changes to the backbone are required; the module is inserted between feature extraction and the task-specific loss.

## Method

HVGCD decomposes instance representation into a global hypothesis h_hypo (from x_cls), a verification branch v_ver, and a statistical grounding term g_gnd. The verification branch induces K content-adaptive prototypes via a lightweight depthwise convolutional clustering module, then projects each patch onto this prototype manifold through L layers of cross-attention to recover discriminative cues suppressed by global compression. A dual-gating mechanism computes a posterior verification probability α_i as the product of an intrinsic saliency score (from the reconstructed signal) and a hypothesis-coherence score (cosine similarity between the patch and the global hypothesis), yielding a weighted aggregation of original local features. The statistical grounding term is the uniform mean of all patches, derived from the maximum-entropy principle, providing a distributional anchor that prevents verification from drifting under open-world uncertainty. A semantic alignment regularization (γ·L_align) enforces directional consistency between v_ver and h_hypo, ensuring verification refines rather than contradicts the initial hypothesis.

## Results

On fine-grained benchmarks with DINOv1, HVGCD improves the average All-class accuracy of four baselines by +3.04%, with the largest gain on FGVC-Aircraft novel categories (+5.34%). With DINOv2, the average improvement is +1.63% on All and +1.70% on Novel. On coarse-grained datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100) with DINOv1, HVGCD yields an average +1.08% All-class gain, including +1.36% on novel categories. The method is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural changes to the backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVGCD consistently improves every baseline it is integrated into, across all seven datasets and both DINOv1/DINOv2 backbones. The largest absolute gains appear on FGVC-Aircraft with DINOv1, where SelEx + HVGCD jumps from 55.2% to 65.2% All-class accuracy (+10.0%) and from 51.0% to 63.8% on novel classes (+12.8%), far exceeding the typical 1–3% improvement seen elsewhere. On CUB-200 with DINOv1, SimGCD + HVGCD reaches 66.5% All (vs. 60.8% baseline, +5.7%), while CMS + HVGCD achieves 67.9% (vs. 67.2%, +0.7%), showing that the gain is larger when the base method is weaker. With the stronger DINOv2 backbone, improvements shrink but remain positive: SelEx + HVGCD reaches 90.8% on CUB-200 (vs. 89.8%) and 81.8% on FGVC-Aircraft (vs. 80.5%). On coarse-grained CIFAR-100 with DINOv1, SimGCD + HVGCD improves All-class accuracy from 80.5% to 81.9% and novel from 79.9% to 79.9% (tie), while CMS + HVGCD gains +2.2% on All (78.0→80.2%). No baseline shows a consistent degradation; the only minor drops are on known-class accuracy for DINOv2 coarse-grained settings (e.g., −0.17% average), confirming that the verification branch primarily benefits novel-category discovery without harming known-class recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes Generalized Category Discovery through a cognitive-science lens, arguing that existing GCD methods (SimGCD, SelEx, CMS, LegoGCD) implicitly perform only 'hypothesis formation' via global ViT embeddings while omitting an explicit verification step. Rather than proposing a new backbone or training paradigm, HVGCD repurposes prototype-based clustering (from DINOv2/DINOv1 cluster assignments and PACA-ViT), cross-attention (from DETR/Perceiver IO), and the maximum-entropy principle (Jaynes 1957) into a lightweight plug-and-play module that operates in the embedding space of any pretrained ViT. The conceptual lineage draws on Gregory's 'perceptions as hypotheses' and Clark's predictive-processing framework, but the architectural contribution is a structured decomposition of the instance representation into hypothesis, verification evidence, and statistical grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hypothesis-verification framing offers a principled explanation for why GCD methods confidently misclassify novel classes (global representations gravitate toward nearest known prototypes without a correction mechanism), and the plug-and-play design makes the fix broadly applicable across parametric, non-parametric, and knowledge-retention GCD pipelines. However, the approach still assumes the total number of classes is known a priori, and the moderate gains (+1–5% depending on dataset and backbone) suggest the verification mechanism, while conceptually clean, captures only part of the open-world challenge. Open questions include whether the framework extends to truly open-set settings with unknown class counts, whether the fixed prototype count (K=100) and layer depth (L=3) generalize across domains, and whether the maximum-entropy grounding term remains sufficient under severe distribution shift.
