## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration methods either depend on explicit degradation estimation (which propagates errors when misclassified), rely on stochastic diffusion sampling (incurring high computational cost), or use deterministic regression (which averages plausible solutions and suppresses fine details). No prior framework simultaneously achieves degradation-agnostic operation, high perceptual fidelity, and efficient deterministic inference across diverse and mixed degradation types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261269/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image at 512×512 resolution as input, requiring no degradation label, prompt, or task identifier. It produces a restored RGB image of the same resolution through a fully deterministic pipeline: VAE encoding, ODE-based latent transport, and VAE decoding with high-frequency refinement. The degradation-agnostic design means the same model handles denoising, deraining, dehazing, deblurring, desnowing, demoiréing, low-light enhancement, and HDR reconstruction without any conditional input.

## Method

SLAIR formulates restoration as deterministic flow matching in a structured latent space, built on three components. First, a degradation-aware VAE is fine-tuned with a classification-guided separation loss (binary clean/degraded plus fine-grained degradation-type cross-entropy) that explicitly enlarges the inter-manifold gap between degraded and clean latents, making the transport problem better-conditioned. Second, a LightningDiT-based conditional vector field learns to transport z_deg → z_gt via a linear interpolation path with bridge noise (√(t(1−t))·ε) for robustness, trained with L2 velocity regression plus a cosine alignment loss, and solved at inference by integrating the ODE from t=0 to t=1. Third, a wavelet-based high-frequency refinement module is inserted into the VAE decoder, using DWT high-frequency coefficient L1 loss to recover sharp edges and textures while preventing the decoder from reintroducing high-frequency degradation artifacts present in the input.

## Results

On the comprehensive 8-task benchmark (Setting i), SLAIR achieves the best average PSNR of 26.15 dB, SSIM of 0.876, LPIPS of 0.114, and FID of 41.09, improving PSNR by 5.6% and reducing LPIPS by 33% over the strongest baseline (DA-CLIP: 24.77 dB, 0.171 LPIPS, 67.90 FID). On the standard 5-task benchmark (Setting ii), SLAIR attains the best average SSIM (0.882), LPIPS (0.094), and FID (31.05), with a PSNR of 28.39 dB that is competitive with MoCE-IR (28.52 dB) while substantially outperforming it on perceptual metrics. Notable per-dataset gains include 15% PSNR improvement on UHDM (21.59 vs. 18.76 dB) and 30% on Outdoor-Rain (22.50 vs. 17.22 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLAIR outperforms all eight baselines on average across both evaluation settings. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline DiffUIR [71], SLAIR gains +3.41 dB PSNR and reduces FID from 65.31 to 41.09 on Setting (i), though DiffUIR retains a narrow PSNR edge on individual datasets Rain1400 (30.19 vs. 29.36) and SOTS (31.11 vs. 26.02). Against the strongest prompt/classification-based baseline DA-CLIP [31], SLAIR improves average PSNR by 1.38 dB and cuts LPIPS from 0.171 to 0.114. On the standard 5-task setting, MoCE-IR [64] achieves a marginally higher average PSNR (28.52 vs. 28.39), but SLAIR dominates on SSIM (0.882 vs. 0.834), LPIPS (0.094 vs. 0.185), and FID (31.05 vs. 52.18), indicating substantially better perceptual quality. CNN-based baselines AirNet [22], PromptIR [41], AdaIR [8], DCPT [17], and VLU-Net [65] are consistently outperformed by 3–5 dB in PSNR and 2–3× in FID across all tasks, confirming the advantage of the generative flow-matching formulation over discriminative regression objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLAIR extends the flow matching / rectified flow paradigm (Lipman et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023) from single-task restoration (PnP-Flow, ResFlow, ELIR) to the all-in-one setting, where multiple degraded distributions must converge to a shared clean target. It builds on LightningDiT's DiT-based flow matching architecture and latent diffusion's VAE framework, but departs from prior all-in-one methods (AirNet, PromptIR, AdaIR, MoCE-IR) by eliminating explicit degradation classification or prompt conditioning entirely. The key conceptual departure is the classification-guided VAE fine-tuning that restructures the latent space to enforce inter-manifold separation, making the multi-source transport problem well-posed without per-task routing or degradation labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formulating restoration as a deterministic ODE in a structured latent space, SLAIR removes both the degradation-estimation bottleneck and the stochastic sampling cost that plague prior all-in-one and diffusion-based approaches, offering a practical path toward fast, label-free restoration across arbitrary degradation types. The latent separation insight—that transport quality depends on the geometric structure of the source/target distributions in latent space—generalizes beyond image restoration to any multi-source-to-single-target generative task. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 512×512 training patches, whether degradation controllability can be reintroduced without sacrificing the label-free design, and how the method behaves on truly unseen degradation types outside the training distribution.
