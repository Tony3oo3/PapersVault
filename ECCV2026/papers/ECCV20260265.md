## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for VLMs are confined to indoor scenes or small viewpoint rotations, leaving unexplored whether models can maintain object-level and layout consistency under the extreme viewpoint discrepancies between satellite and street-level observations. The paper asks whether VLMs can be endowed with stronger cross-view spatial understanding through explicit reasoning strategies or auxiliary perceptual processes, and whether visual spatial imagination can bridge the gap toward human-like spatial cognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260265/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During evaluation, the model receives only a single-view image (satellite or street) plus a text question, and must infer spatial attributes of the unseen view through cross-view geometric reasoning rather than direct visual evidence. For grounding tasks, the input is a BBox in one view (or a text description plus a coarse region) and the output is the corresponding BBox in the target view, scored by mIoU. For viewpoint localization, the model selects the correct camera pose arrow on a satellite image given a street-view image, or selects the correct street-view image given a satellite image with a fixed arrow, making the task asymmetric in visual information load.

## Method

CVSBench is built from CVUSA and University1652 via a semi-automatic pipeline that generates cross-view QA pairs exploiting asymmetric spatial cues between viewpoints, annotates 9,468 object BBoxes with human verification, and constructs 40,679 QA pairs across G2S, S2G, grounding, and viewpoint localization tasks. To enhance spatial reasoning, the paper proposes two textual CoT strategies—Structured Scene CoT (object-centric decomposition with BBox enumeration before attribute reasoning) and Spatial Imagination CoT (textual simulation of perspective transformation)—each trained via SFT followed by GRPO reinforcement learning. The key finding is that these text-only approaches yield only marginal gains (~1.75% on FOV-subset, ~0.4% on CVUSA-subset), whereas a 3D scene imagination pipeline that uses Depth Anything V2 for depth maps and nanobanana for generating 3D miniature views as auxiliary visual inputs produces substantially larger improvements (3.34% with cognitive map representations), demonstrating that explicit visual-spatial representations are necessary for robust cross-view reasoning.

## Results

On CVSBench, the best-performing model GPT-5-chat achieves 53.76% overall accuracy and 12.30% mIoU, while the strongest open-source model InternVL3.5-8B reaches 49.89% accuracy and 3.71% mIoU. Human annotators achieve 89.52% accuracy and 93.69% mIoU, revealing a substantial gap. Cross-view grounding is the weakest task across all models (mIoU < 15% for nearly all), and the FOV-subset consistently yields lower scores than the CVUSA-subset, indicating that restricted field-of-view and incomplete structural cues significantly increase difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5-chat leads all models with 53.76% accuracy and 12.30% mIoU, outperforming GPT-4o (45.54% Acc) by roughly 8 points on accuracy. Among open-source general-purpose VLMs, InternVL3.5-8B achieves the highest accuracy (49.89%), while Qwen3-VL-4B attains the best mIoU (8.72%) among open-source models, suggesting stronger grounding capability. Spatial-reasoning-specialized models (SpaceQwen, SpaceThinker, SpatialBot, ViLaSR) consistently underperform general-purpose VLMs on overall accuracy (25–44% vs. 44–50%), with SpatialBot-3B scoring near-zero mIoU (0.01%), indicating that single-view geometric enhancements do not transfer to cross-view correspondence. GeoChat-7B, despite being designed for remote sensing, achieves only 0.27% mIoU, revealing a disconnect between remote-sensing grounding and cross-view spatial reasoning. All models remain far below the human baseline (89.52% Acc, 93.69% mIoU), with the largest gaps appearing in cross-view grounding and viewpoint localization tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three previously isolated research threads: spatial reasoning benchmarks (SIBench, MINDCUBE, OmniSpatial), cross-view geo-localization (CVUSA, VIGOR), and remote sensing VQA (RSVQA, RSVGD). Prior spatial benchmarks are confined to indoor scenes or small viewpoint rotations, while geo-localization work focuses on retrieval rather than reasoning. CVSBench departs from both by using satellite-street pairs with extreme viewpoint discrepancies and unifying VQA, grounding, and viewpoint identification under a single evaluation framework. On the method side, it extends the SFT+RL training paradigm (GRPO) from single-view spatial reasoning to cross-view settings, and introduces 3D scene imagination via image generation as an auxiliary perceptual input—a direction not explored in prior VLM spatial reasoning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and persistent gap between human performance (~89.5%) and the best VLM (~53.8%), exposing that current models fundamentally lack the ability to maintain object-level and layout consistency under drastic viewpoint changes. The finding that spatial-reasoning-specialized models (SpaceQwen, SpaceThinker) underperform general VLMs on cross-view tasks challenges the assumption that single-view geometric enhancement transfers to multi-view correspondence. The key open question is whether VLMs can learn to generate their own visual intermediate representations (cognitive maps) rather than relying on external image generation, and whether the modest gains from 3D imagination (3.34%) can be amplified with better generation models or end-to-end training. The benchmark's scale and multi-task design make it a useful stress test for future embodied and geospatial VLMs.
