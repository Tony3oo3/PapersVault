## Problem

Existing audio-visual active speaker detection models perform well on clean data but degrade significantly under real-world corruptions such as background noise, occlusion, or simultaneous modality degradation. The paper attributes this fragility to the absence of explicit consistency constraints: without them, models learn modality-specific shortcuts via simple fusion (summation or concatenation) optimized only with classification objectives, producing modality-clustered representations that are vulnerable to corruption-induced shifts.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of T grayscale face crops (112×112, detected by S3FD) and 4T MFCC frames (13-dimensional, extracted at 4× the video frame rate from 16 kHz audio). Audio-visual fusion is performed via element-wise addition of 128-dimensional frame-level embeddings before a shared BGRU, and the output is a per-frame binary speaking probability produced by a temperature-scaled softmax head. The architecture introduces no additional learnable parameters beyond a single 128→2 linear layer for the audio-only classification head used in the distillation loss.

## Method

Built on the Light-ASD two-stream backbone (multi-scale 3D convolutions for vision, 2D convolutions for audio, shared BGRU classifier), the method adds three complementary consistency regularizers to the training objective. (1) Inter-modality consistency maximizes cosine similarity between audio and visual embeddings, applied only to speaking frames to avoid spurious alignment during silence. (2) Intra-modality consistency applies a track-aware supervised contrastive loss independently to each modality, restricting positives to same-label, same-speaker frames to prevent cross-speaker interference and enlarge the speaking/non-speaking cluster margin. (3) Prediction-level consistency distills the confidence-masked audio-visual prediction (teacher) into unimodal predictions (students) via MSE, with a threshold of 0.7 to filter unreliable teacher outputs and create an implicit curriculum. The three losses are lightweight, require no corrupted training data or additional annotations, and collectively enforce modality-invariant, class-aligned representations that degrade gracefully when either stream is corrupted at test time.

## Results

On the AVA-ActiveSpeaker validation set, C³ ASD achieves 93.8% mAP with only 1.02M parameters and 0.62G FLOPs, matching the efficiency of Light-ASD while slightly outperforming it (93.6%). On the in-the-wild WASD dataset, the method reaches 86.1% mAP, outperforming ADENet (85.6%) and Light-ASD (85.3%). Under test-time corruptions, the largest gains appear under visual object occlusion (+2.04% mAP over Light-ASD) and joint audio-visual corruption with MUSAN noise (+1.23% average mAP), with consistent improvements across all SNR levels and noise types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among from-scratch baselines, C³ ASD outperforms Light-ASD [25] by +0.2% mAP on clean AVA (93.8 vs 93.6) with identical parameter count (1.02M) and FLOPs (0.62G), and by a larger margin under corruption: +2.04% on object occlusion and +1.23% on joint MUSAN+occlusion. It also surpasses TalkNet [38] (92.3%) and ADENet [46] (93.2%) on clean AVA while using 15× and 32× fewer parameters respectively. On the in-the-wild WASD benchmark, C³ ASD leads all compared models (86.1% vs ADENet 85.6%, Light-ASD 85.3%, TalkNet 78.4%). The method trails LR-ASD [26] by 0.7% on clean AVA (93.8 vs 94.5), though LR-ASD is not evaluated under corruption. Under the most challenging joint corruption (object occlusion + MUSAN babble at −10 dB SNR), C³ ASD achieves 66.6% mAP versus 65.7% for Light-ASD and 53.7% for TalkNet, demonstrating that the consistency losses provide the largest relative benefit when both modalities are simultaneously degraded.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Light-ASD lightweight two-stream architecture (multi-scale 3D/2D CNNs + BGRU) and introduces a multi-level consistency regularization framework that departs from the dominant paradigm in ASD research, where prior methods (TalkNet, ADENet, LoCoNet, SPELL) focus on architectural complexity or fusion strategy improvements. The key conceptual departure is treating the audio-visual relationship as an explicit structural constraint rather than an implicit byproduct of joint training: the paper draws on supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al.) for intra-modality cluster separation and knowledge distillation (Hinton et al.) for prediction-level alignment, but applies them in a track-aware, speaking-conditioned manner specific to ASD. Unlike prior robustness-oriented ASD work that adds auxiliary tasks (e.g., speech separation) or heavier backbones, this approach requires no architectural modification, no corrupted training data, and no additional annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap—robustness under real-world audio-visual corruption—that prior ASD methods handle only implicitly through architectural choices. The architecture-agnostic nature (validated on TalkNet and ADENet) and zero-overhead design make the consistency losses a drop-in regularization for existing ASD pipelines. However, the clean-data gains are minimal (+0.2% mAP), and corruption improvements, while consistent, are modest (0.5–2% mAP), suggesting the framework helps but does not fundamentally transform robustness. Open questions include whether the multi-level consistency principle generalizes to other multimodal tasks (e.g., audio-visual speech recognition, emotion recognition) and whether the track-aware contrastive design extends to scenarios with more than two speakers or overlapping speech.
