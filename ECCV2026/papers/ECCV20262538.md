## Problem

Video Question Grounding models are vulnerable to intra-video temporal bias: within a single video, visually similar segments (sharing the same entities and background) can differ substantially in their causal relevance to the question, yet appearance-oriented encoders make them indistinguishable in embedding space. Prior debiasing methods target dataset-level spurious correlations but overlook this within-video confounding, where irrelevant or misleading segments often dominate the temporal span and cause the model to ground on non-causal content.

## Input / Output

The method takes 32 uniformly sampled video frames (encoded by a frozen CLIP-L ViT-L) and a question with m multiple-choice options (encoded by a fine-tuned RoBERTa), producing both a predicted answer and a temporal grounding interval. The grounding interval is derived from a Gaussian-smoothed attention distribution over frames, selecting the peak frame plus neighbors within a predefined threshold, under weak supervision with no explicit temporal boundary annotations during training.

## Method

WICI combines two modules: (1) a Wavelet-based Dynamic Modeling (WDM) layer that applies 1D group convolutions initialized with Haar wavelet kernels (stride 2) along the temporal dimension to recursively decompose frame features into multi-level high-frequency detail components, which are up-sampled and fused back at weight 0.3 to inject inter-frame motion cues into appearance-dominated CLIP embeddings; and (2) an Intra-video Counterfactual Mining (ICM) module that constructs curriculum-guided counterfactual segments—both frame-based (progressively moving closer to the causal segment via a distance ratio ρ that increases over epochs) and shuffled-video (random temporal permutation preserving frame appearance)—and enforces a margin-based discrimination loss requiring P(a*|causal) > P(a*|full video) > P(a*|counterfactual) with λ₁ < λ₂ to impose graded separation. The full objective combines cross-entropy on causal predictions, the margin-based loss, and an InfoNCE contrastive alignment term, trained with AdamW on the Temp[CLIP] backbone augmented with a 2-layer Transformer temporal encoder.

## Results

On NeXT-GQA, WICI achieves the best overall performance with Acc@GQA of 19.0%, Acc@VQA of 61.5%, mIoP of 28.8%, and TIoP@0.5 of 29.4%, outperforming the strongest causal-reasoning baseline CRA (18.2% Acc@GQA, 28.5% TIoP@0.5) and the best temporal grounding baseline Temp[CLIP](NG+) (16.0% Acc@GQA, 25.5% TIoP@0.5). On the STAR benchmark, WICI further improves Acc@GQA from 26.8% (CRA) to 29.2% and TIoP@0.5 from 44.5% to 48.5%, confirming generalization across datasets. The model uses 146.7M parameters, only marginally larger than the 130.6M Temp[CLIP] backbone it builds upon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WICI outperforms all seven baselines on the primary Acc@GQA metric. Against the strongest causal-reasoning baseline CRA (Chen et al., CVPR 2025), WICI gains +0.8% Acc@GQA (19.0 vs 18.2), +0.4% Acc@VQA (61.5 vs 61.1), and +0.9% TIoP@0.5 (29.4 vs 28.5), though CRA retains a slight edge on TIoU@0.5 (10.6 vs 10.2), reflecting WICI's preference for compact, high-precision grounding segments. Compared to the best temporal grounding baseline Temp[CLIP](NG+), WICI improves Acc@GQA by +3.0% and TIoP@0.5 by +3.9%. Against TimeCraft (Liu et al., ECCV 2024), which leverages LLM-based data augmentation, WICI leads by +0.8% Acc@GQA and +1.6% TIoP@0.5. The larger-model SeViLA* (4.1B params) achieves higher Acc@VQA (68.1%) but substantially lower grounding quality (22.9% TIoP@0.5 vs 29.4%), confirming that WICI's gains come from improved causal temporal localization rather than answer guessing. On the STAR benchmark, WICI extends its lead over CRA by +2.4% Acc@GQA and +4.0% TIoP@0.5, validating cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Temp[CLIP] weakly-supervised VideoQG framework [45] for its backbone, sampling protocol, and evaluation setup, and adopts the backdoor/frontdoor de-biasing strategy and Gaussian Smoothing Grounding module from CRA [5] to handle dataset-level bias. Its key departure is shifting the causal intervention from the cross-sample (dataset-level) to the intra-video level: prior debiasing methods treat causal and non-causal content as separate samples, whereas WICI constructs counterfactual segments *within* a single video and uses a margin-based ranking loss rather than contrastive or invariance objectives. The wavelet-based dynamic modeling module is a novel architectural addition that injects multi-scale temporal dynamics into CLIP frame features, complementing the appearance-dominated representations that prior VideoQG methods rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes intra-video temporal bias—a specific failure mode where visually similar but causally irrelevant segments within a single video mislead grounding—filling a gap that dataset-level debiasing methods leave unaddressed. The temporal sensitivity analysis (model correctly degrades under frame shuffling) provides a useful diagnostic for whether a VideoQG model is genuinely using temporal order. However, the counterfactual construction assumes a single continuous evidence span, limiting applicability to questions requiring multiple discrete segments, and the performance gains over the strongest baseline (CRA) are modest (~0.8% Acc@GQA on NeXT-GQA), suggesting the problem may be partially addressed by the existing backbone's capacity.
