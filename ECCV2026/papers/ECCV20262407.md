## Problem

Existing underwater 3D reconstruction methods face a fundamental impasse: physics-aware approaches like SeaThru-NeRF model wavelength-dependent attenuation and scattering but are computationally prohibitive at large scale due to global optimization, while scalable partition-based methods treat the medium as uniform air and ignore depth-dependent light transport, causing severe color distortion in deeper regions. The paper argues that spatial partitioning and physical modeling cannot be treated as independent stages because underwater radiative transport is inherently depth-conditioned and inseparable from scene geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view underwater images (linearized from sRGB to radiance space via inverse IEC 61966-2-1) and COLMAP-estimated camera poses and sparse point clouds as input. It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation for real-time novel view synthesis (434 FPS on RTX 3090) along with per-channel water optical parameters (attenuation β_d, backscatter β_b, background light B_∞). Notably, the depth map consumed by the WaterParamPredict module is not from an external sensor but is rendered differentiably from the current Gaussian geometry, creating a self-consistent feedback loop between geometry and optical estimation.

## Method

SMP-UWGS couples two key components: (1) SMP-GAUSSIAN, a multi-partition architecture that projects camera trajectories onto the XZ plane to generate m×n overlapping sub-regions with shared boundary cameras, a three-tier Gaussian fusion strategy (global/regional/neighborhood layers), visibility-based cross-partition camera selection, and depth-aware region weighting that amplifies gradients in deeper partitions to counteract attenuation-induced signal decay; and (2) DPR-Net, a differentiable physical rendering module where WaterParamPredict (a U-Net with SE-ResNet encoders and attention-gated decoders) regresses nine global optical parameters from rendered depth maps, followed by DBDR (Dual-Branch Differential Refinement) that jointly refines spatially-varying attenuation and backscatter via a shared convolutional predictor. Training proceeds in three stages—global parameter prediction, local light transport refinement, then partition-accelerated 3D reconstruction—under a hybrid physical-statistical loss combining FiLM-decoupled L1+SSIM reconstruction, a Physics-Aware Dark Channel Prior that reweights channels by inverse attenuation to avoid misinterpreting red-channel loss as turbidity, edge-aware depth smoothness, and L2 parameter regularization.

## Results

On the SeaThru-NeRF dataset, SMP-UWGS achieves the best PSNR across all four scenes (27.83, 31.55, 23.29, 31.64 dB) and the highest SSIM (up to 0.937 on Curaçao), outperforming the next-best method UW-GS by 0.18–1.36 dB in PSNR. On BVI-Coral, SMP-UWGS attains the best SSIM in 3 of 4 scenes (0.816 on Lagoon, 0.769 on Seabed, 0.754 on Reef) while SeaSplat edges ahead on Lagoon PSNR (26.25 vs. 25.95). On the large-scale OTNN and UVEB datasets, SMP-UWGS leads in SSIM for most scenes (e.g., 0.813 on BlueThistle, 0.788 on GeoExploration) while maintaining competitive PSNR. In terms of efficiency, SMP-UWGS trains in 13 minutes with 10.9 GB VRAM and renders at 434 FPS, compared to 42 min/14.6 GB for 3DGS and 10 h for SeaThru-NeRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SeaThru-NeRF, SMP-UWGS wins on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across every scene in all four datasets, with the largest PSNR gains on the SeaThru-NeRF scenes (up to +3.8 dB on Curaçao) and consistent SSIM improvements of 0.03–0.26. Compared to vanilla 3DGS, SMP-UWGS improves PSNR by 2–6 dB and SSIM by 0.05–0.26 across scenes, confirming that the physics-aware module is essential for underwater fidelity. Against UW-GS, SMP-UWGS leads on PSNR in 10 of 12 scenes and on SSIM in 11 of 12, with the most notable gap on Curaçao (+1.38 dB PSNR, +0.086 SSIM). The closest competitor is SeaSplat: on BVI-Coral, SeaSplat achieves slightly higher PSNR on Lagoon (26.25 vs. 25.95) and Seabed (25.35 vs. 24.46), while SMP-UWGS recovers the lead on SSIM in all four BVI-Coral scenes; on OTNN/UVEB, SeaSplat edges ahead on PSNR for MingWreck2 (23.83 vs. 23.71) and GeoExploration (27.30 vs. 26.71), but SMP-UWGS dominates on SSIM in 5 of 6 scenes. Overall, SMP-UWGS achieves the best or near-best results in 11 of 12 scenes on at least one primary metric, with the most consistent advantage in structural similarity (SSIM) across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: scalable large-scene reconstruction via spatial partitioning (VastGaussian, Block-NeRF, Mega-NeRF) and physics-aware underwater rendering (SeaThru, SeaThru-NeRF, UW-GS, SeaSplat). It builds directly on 3DGS as the geometric representation and on the SeaThru image formation model for attenuation/backscatter compensation. The key departure is the argument that these two concerns cannot be treated independently: because underwater attenuation is depth-conditioned, a partitioning strategy that ignores depth continuity fragments the optical field, while a global physical model becomes intractable at scale. The paper thus reframes the problem as a single coupled optimization rather than a pipeline of sequential stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for applications like underwater archaeology, ecological monitoring, and autonomous robotics, where scenes are large and physically degraded. The ~46× training speedup over SeaThru-NeRF (13 min vs. 10 h) with competitive quality makes large-scale underwater reconstruction feasible on a single GPU. However, the method assumes homogeneous ambient illumination and static scenes, limiting applicability to active-lighting or dynamic environments. An open question is whether the partition-boundary artifacts (acknowledged as slight blurring at junctions) become problematic at even larger scales or with more aggressive partition counts.
