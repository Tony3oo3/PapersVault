## Problem

Existing driving scenario generation methods either produce scenes unconditionally or condition on control layouts extracted from logged data, leaving no interpretable, vision-language control over the generated scene structure. No prior method allows a text prompt or a single dashcam image to directly steer the synthesis of a full vectorized driving scene (road topology, agent placement, traffic signals), which is critical for targeting rare, safety-critical events that are underrepresented in real-world logs.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a free-form text prompt describing scene attributes (intersection type, traffic conditions, agent counts) or a single forward-facing dashcam-style ego image, and generates a structured 3D scene graph within a 64 m × 64 m BEV field of view. The output is a vectorized graph comprising lane centerlines with a connectivity matrix, agent positions augmented with elevation (z) and height (h) beyond standard 2D BEV, and traffic signals. Downstream, the scene graph drives behavior simulation to produce 3D bounding-box trajectories over time, which are reprojected as wireframe control sequences into a LoRA-adapted Wan 2.2 (5B) video diffusion model to yield photorealistic multi-frame camera clips from the ego or any other actor's viewpoint.

## Method

ScenarioControl builds on a transformer-based vectorized autoencoder that encodes variable-cardinality object and lane tokens into a compact latent space, over which a DDPM ε-predictor diffusion model is trained. The key architectural contribution is a cross-global control mechanism that injects dense conditioning (text embeddings or image features + depth maps) into the diffusion process via two parallel branches sharing key/value projections: a standard cross-attention branch for fine-grained token-level control, and a lightweight global-context branch in which a small set of learned latent tokens first aggregates global information from the conditioning stream and then exposes a compact summary back to scene queries. The two branches are fused with a learned tanh gate initialized to zero, so training begins as vanilla cross-attention and progressively incorporates the global pathway. A lightweight attention-based regressor predicts agent and lane counts from the conditioning tokens, and a collision penalty loss applied at later diffusion timesteps suppresses overlapping agent boxes. For video generation, the vectorized scene is simulated with a behavior model, reprojected into camera-space wireframe sequences, and fed as a control signal alongside the first frame into a LoRA-adapted video diffusion model, enabling both prompt-controlled novel scenes and image-conditioned continuations with temporal consistency.

## Results

On nuPlan with prompt conditioning, ScenarioControl achieves Agent CSC of 0.1626 vs. 0.0625 for Scenario Dreamer (a 160% improvement), Lane CSC of 0.4006 vs. 0.2885, and reduces Fréchet Distance from 2.44 to 0.51. With image conditioning, the gains are even larger: Agent CSC improves from 0.0623 to 0.2255 (262%), Lane CSC from 0.3166 to 0.6100, and FD drops from 9.94 to 2.26. On the Waymo Open Motion dataset, ScenarioControl outperforms Scenario Dreamer by up to 143% on CSC metrics and substantially outperforms the cascaded VLM+TrafficGen baseline (e.g., Agent CSC 0.2112 vs. 0.0054).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Scenario Dreamer [40] (the unconditioned vectorized latent diffusion baseline), ScenarioControl wins on every control-adherence metric (CCS, SPG, CSC) at all three levels (global, lane, agent) for both prompt and image conditioning, with the largest relative gains in Agent CSC (up to 262% improvement under image conditioning) and Fréchet Distance (reduced from 9.94 to 2.26 for image conditioning). Against the cascaded VLM+TrafficGen [10] baseline on Waymo, ScenarioControl dominates across all metrics (e.g., Agent CSC 0.2112 vs. 0.0054, Lane CSC 0.5179 vs. 0.0585) while also being faster (avoiding the 13.05 s/sample VLM inference latency). In the mechanism ablation (Table 3), Cross-Global Control outperforms Full Cross-Attention [47] by 2.7% in Agent CSC and 4.6% in Agent AP under image conditioning, while also reducing collision rate relative to concatenation by 43.4%. Gated Attention [1], Linear Attention [16], AgentAttention [17], SAAP [31], Windowed Attention [8], Deformable Attention [63], and Squeezed Attention [20] all underperform the proposed mechanism on most controllability metrics, with Windowed Attention being the weakest (Agent CSC 0.0414 under prompt conditioning). The only metric where ScenarioControl does not strictly dominate is lane Connectivity under image conditioning (3.79 vs. 4.19 for Scenario Dreamer), though all other realism metrics are comparable or better.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScenarioControl extends the vectorized latent diffusion framework of Scenario Dreamer (CVPR 2025), which generated driving scenes unconditionally or with control layouts derived from logged scenarios, by introducing vision-language conditioning (text prompts and dashcam images) through a novel cross-global control mechanism. It departs from prior controllable generation methods (Driving Diffusion, Panacea, SceneControl) that rely on structured intermediate representations extracted from existing scenes, and from behavior-level simulators (TrafficGen, CtRL-Sim) that assume a fixed road graph. The paper also positions itself against sensor-data generation approaches (GAIA-1/2, Cosmos-Drive-Dreams, UniSim) that produce photorealistic video but lack editable vectorized structure, arguing that ScenarioControl bridges both by producing structured scene graphs that can drive downstream video synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate safety-critical and long-tail driving scenarios from natural language or a single image addresses a fundamental sample-efficiency bottleneck in autonomous driving evaluation, where real-world logs underrepresent rare events. The structured vectorized output is directly consumable by existing simulators and autonomy stacks, making the method practically plug-and-play. Open questions include extending to full multi-view and long-horizon visual conditioning, unifying initial scene generation with traffic simulation in a shared latent space to capture intent and interaction semantics, and whether the cross-global mechanism generalizes to other structured generation tasks beyond driving. A limitation is that a single dashcam frame constrains only the forward view, requiring outpainting for full 360° coverage.
