## Problem

Deploying large-scale (14B) diffusion models for audio-driven avatar generation faces two conflicting challenges: long-horizon consistency, where compounding errors cause identity drift and color artifacts within minutes, and the real-time-fidelity trade-off, where sequential multi-step denoising is inherently slow while existing real-time methods sacrifice quality by using small models or non-iterative generation. No prior method simultaneously achieves streaming, real-time (≥24 FPS), and infinite-length generation with a large diffusion model.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single reference image (serving as a persistent sink frame for identity) and a continuous audio stream as input, along with text prompt embeddings, and produces streaming talking-head video at 720×400 resolution in chunks of 84 frames (3 latent frames per autoregressive block). Generation is fully causal and autoregressive: each new block is denoised while attending to a rolling KV cache of prior blocks, enabling unbounded temporal extension without re-encoding the full history.

## Method

Live Avatar uses a two-stage training pipeline: Stage 1 applies Diffusion Forcing pretraining with noisy motion frames as scaffolding to cheaply teach dynamics-identity decoupling, and Stage 2 performs Self-Forcing DMD distillation into a 4-step causal model where the KV cache stores only noisy representations (History Corrupt) rather than clean ones. This noisy conditioning acts as a low-pass filter that suppresses accumulated artifacts while preserving motion structure, enabling stable generation beyond 10,000 seconds from ~3-second training clips. Two additional mechanisms—Adaptive Attention Sink (replacing the user image with the model's own first generated frame to eliminate distribution mismatch) and Rolling RoPE (dynamic positional alignment of the sink frame)—further prevent color drift and identity loss. On the system side, Timestep-forcing Pipeline Parallelism (TPP) exploits the noisy KV cache to assign each GPU a fixed denoising timestep, converting the sequential diffusion chain into an asynchronous spatial pipeline that achieves 45 FPS with 1.21 s time-to-first-frame on 5 H100 GPUs.

## Results

On GenBench-ShortVideo, Live Avatar achieves the best IQA (4.51) and Sync-C (7.03), with competitive ASE (3.44) and Dino-S (0.96), while reaching 45.2 FPS—roughly 2× the next-fastest method (Ditto at 21.8 FPS) and 100–280× faster than other 14B-scale baselines (0.16–0.64 FPS). On GenBench-LongVideo (>5 min), Live Avatar maintains near-identical quality (ASE 3.42, IQA 4.76, Dino-S 0.97) while all other generative baselines degrade substantially; for example, OmniAvatar's ASE drops from 3.53 to 2.36 and its Dino-S collapses to 0.66. The system achieves a time-to-first-frame (TTFF) of 1.21 s on 5× H100 GPUs using 4 denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ditto [23] (the only other near-real-time method at 21.8 FPS), Live Avatar wins decisively on IQA (4.51 vs 4.24 short; 4.76 vs 4.48 long), Sync-C (7.03 vs 4.09 short; 7.16 vs 3.98 long), and FPS (45.2 vs 21.8), while Ditto retains a slight Dino-S edge on short video (0.99 vs 0.96) due to its face-editing formulation. Against the 14B-scale baselines WanS2V [13] and OmniAvatar [12], Live Avatar surpasses both on IQA, Sync-C, and FPS by wide margins; on long video the gap widens dramatically as OmniAvatar's ASE/IQA collapse (3.53/4.49 → 2.36/2.86) and its Dino-S drops to 0.66, whereas Live Avatar's metrics remain stable. StableAvatar [40] achieves the highest short-video ASE (3.52) but has the worst Sync-C (3.42) and degrades on long video (ASE 3.00, Sync-C 1.97). Hallo3 [6] and Echomimic-V2 [32] trail on most quality metrics and run at sub-1 FPS. Overall, Live Avatar is the only method that simultaneously achieves real-time throughput, competitive visual quality, and stable long-horizon generation at 14B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

Live Avatar builds directly on WanS2V (14B audio-driven video diffusion) as its architectural backbone and on the Self-Forcing / Diffusion Forcing lineage for causal, block-wise autoregressive video generation. Its key conceptual departure is the History Corrupt strategy—storing noisy rather than clean representations in the rolling KV cache—which inverts the standard assumption that conditioning on clean history is strictly better, and repurposes the static-scene prior of avatar interaction to decouple identity (from a persistent sink frame) from motion dynamics (from noisy cache). On the systems side, Timestep-forcing Pipeline Parallelism (TPP) is a novel reparameterization that converts the inherently sequential diffusion denoising chain into an asynchronous spatial pipeline across GPUs, a parallelism strategy not present in prior streaming video systems (CausVid, LongLive, Ditto) that instead reduce model capacity to meet real-time budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a 14B-parameter diffusion model can stream at 45 FPS with sub-1.3 s time-to-first-frame, breaking the long-standing quality-vs-real-time tradeoff that forced prior real-time avatar systems to use sub-1B models. The noisy-KV-cache insight and TPP are both model-agnostic and hardware-agnostic, suggesting broad applicability to any causal diffusion model beyond avatars (e.g., streaming text-to-video). Open questions include whether the static-scene prior is truly necessary once native long-duration training becomes feasible, and whether the ~3 s end-to-end latency (TTFF + network) can be reduced enough for seamless bidirectional interaction. The introduced GenBench (with >5-minute test cases) also fills a gap in long-video evaluation infrastructure.
