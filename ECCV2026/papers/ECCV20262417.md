## Problem

Existing relative position embeddings such as RoPE operate within a single fixed geometric space (1D sequences or 2D/3D grids), making them fundamentally inadequate for cross-view or cross-dimensional geometric reasoning where pixels from different camera views may be close in 3D yet far apart in their respective 2D grids. Prior attempts to incorporate camera geometry into attention either use absolute ray encodings that lack relative bias properties, or decouple inter-camera and intra-image geometry without modeling their interaction at the patch level.

## Input / Output

URoPE is a plug-in positional encoding that consumes multi-view image patches together with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics (and optionally 3D query positions for 2D-3D tasks), and injects a geometry-aware relative bias into standard multi-head attention without altering the Q/K/V multiplication format. It is parameter-free and SE(3)-invariant, requiring no learnable depth modules, and remains natively compatible with RoPE-optimized kernels such as FlashAttention. The method is applied identically across tasks—novel view synthesis, 3D detection/tracking, and stereo depth—without task-specific architectural modifications.

## Method

For each key/value patch, URoPE lifts the pixel into 3D by sampling points along its camera ray at a set of fixed depth anchors, then projects those points into the query camera's image plane using the relative extrinsic transform and query intrinsics, yielding depth-conditioned pixel coordinates along the epipolar line. Standard 2D RoPE is then applied between the query location and the projected key location in the query image plane, with per-head channels split equally between horizontal and vertical axes. To resolve the inherent depth ambiguity of cross-view projection, different attention heads (or head groups) are assigned different fixed depth anchors, so multi-head attention jointly covers near-to-far correspondences. For multiview self-attention, queries are reshaped so each batch element corresponds to a single query view, guaranteeing a consistent RoPE reference frame while preserving the original O(L²) attention complexity. The formulation naturally degenerates to standard 2D RoPE in the single-view case and extends to 2D-3D cross-attention by measuring relative positions directly in 3D between lifted points and 3D query coordinates.

## Results

On the primary novel view synthesis task, URoPE achieves PSNR 25.09 / SSIM 0.900 / LPIPS 0.165 on Objaverse and PSNR 26.02 / SSIM 0.827 / LPIPS 0.080 on RealEstate10k, outperforming all baselines. For 3D object detection on nuScenes, adding URoPE to StreamPETR improves NDS from 47.6 to 50.6 and mAP from 37.5 to 41.1, while tracking AMOTA rises from 0.335 to 0.380. On stereo depth estimation (SUN3D), URoPE reduces Abs Rel from 0.131 (UniMatch) to 0.112 and RMSE from 0.397 to 0.329, also beating P-RoPE (0.117 / 0.343).

## Baselines & Comparisons

URoPE consistently outperforms all four novel-view-synthesis baselines on both datasets. Against Plücker Ray (the absolute encoding used in LVSM), URoPE gains +2.81 dB PSNR on Objaverse and +2.07 dB on RealEstate10k, confirming that relative projective encoding substantially surpasses absolute ray concatenation. Against the closest competitor P-RoPE, URoPE leads by +0.21 dB PSNR on Objaverse and +0.74 dB on RealEstate10k, with the larger gap on real scenes suggesting better handling of complex geometry. RayRoPE, a concurrent method that predicts depth per layer, is beaten by +0.13 dB on Objaverse but by a wider +1.08 dB on RealEstate10k, indicating that fixed depth anchors are more robust than learned depth in the absence of supervision. For 3D detection, URoPE adds +2.4 NDS and +1.3 mAP over PETR, and +3.0 NDS and +3.6 mAP over StreamPETR, with tracking AMOTA improving by 0.033 and 0.045 respectively. On stereo depth, URoPE marginally beats P-RoPE on all three benchmarks (e.g., Abs Rel 0.112 vs 0.117 on SUN3D), showing that even in the simpler two-view setting the explicit projection still provides a small but consistent advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

URoPE extends Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) from its native single-coordinate-space setting to cross-view and cross-dimensional geometric spaces by leveraging explicit projective geometry: key tokens are lifted along camera rays at fixed depth anchors and projected into the query image plane, after which standard 2D RoPE is applied. This departs from prior approaches that either concatenate absolute ray features (Plücker embeddings), apply extrinsic matrix multiplications to Q/K/V (GTA), or encode inter-camera and intra-image geometry in disjoint channel blocks (P-RoPE). The key conceptual shift is that cross-view relative position is expressed entirely within the query image plane, unifying epipolar geometry and spatial position into a single RoPE mechanism rather than treating them as separate signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

URoPE offers a parameter-free, SE(3)-invariant, intrinsics-aware position encoding that is natively compatible with FlashAttention and other RoPE-optimized kernels, making it a practical drop-in for existing multi-view transformer pipelines in novel view synthesis, 3D detection, and stereo matching. Its universality across 2D-2D, 2D-3D, and temporal settings without task-specific modifications suggests it could become a standard component in geometric vision transformers. The main limitation is the reliance on known camera parameters, and extending URoPE to uncalibrated or self-calibrating settings is identified as a key open direction. The finding that fixed depth anchors outperform a learned per-layer depth prediction module also raises questions about when explicit geometric priors are preferable to learned representations.
