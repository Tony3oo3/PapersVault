## Problem

In fast-slow LLM-augmented driving planners, existing triggering strategies (fixed-interval or heuristic difficulty-based) either over-call the expensive slow module in easy segments or miss critical moments, and can even degrade performance when slow guidance is mis-timed. The paper identifies that slow guidance is not uniformly beneficial—there exist Failure Intervals where invoking the LLM actively hurts closed-loop performance—yet no prior method learns to both schedule and suppress slow reasoning at the frame level under an explicit compute budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260218/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The gate consumes a pooled scene embedding from a Vector Map Encoder (tokenized lanes, topology, and dynamic agents in ego-centric frame), the cached slow-guidance buffer (a projected LLM latent), their cosine similarity, and a log-scaled time-since-last-query feature. It outputs a single discrete token per frame from {QUERY, CACHE, DROP}; the fast planner (GameFormer-style decoder) then fuses the buffer (or a null vector under DROP) via cross-attention to produce an 8-second trajectory at 10 Hz. On NAVSIM the gate is simplified to a binary fast/slow decision with an additional MLP-encoded fast-branch trajectory as auxiliary cue.

## Method

ASSCG is a 4-layer RWKV-7 recurrent backbone that maintains a compact latent state over the episode, giving O(1) per-step memory and linear-time scaling—critical for long-horizon gating where Transformer attention would be quadratic. The gate is trained in two stages: (i) supervised fine-tuning on pseudo-labels extracted from the highest-scoring fixed-stride rollout per scene, and (ii) compute-aware GRPO-style offline RL where the per-step reward uses potential-based shaping of the nuPlan score components (TTC, Lim, Comf) plus a terminal progress settlement, with an explicit lambda_call penalty on every QUERY action to trade off slow-module usage against closed-loop performance. The DROP action is the key differentiator from prior gating work: it actively substitutes a null vector for the buffer, suppressing slow guidance that the gate predicts would be harmful, rather than merely reducing query frequency. An attention-pooling block (Linear-GELU-Linear-Softmax) summarizes the variable-length token sequence before concatenation with similarity and time features, keeping the gate lightweight (0.02 s last-frame latency vs. 0.06 s for a parameter-matched Transformer).

## Results

On nuPlan Hard20 closed-loop evaluation, ASSCG (AdaptiveAsyncDriver) achieves a score of 67.28, improving +2.28 over the base AsyncDriver (65.00) and +3.01 over a latency-matched 5-frame fixed schedule (64.27), while reducing average end-to-end inference latency from 0.80 s/frame to 0.32 s/frame (~60% reduction). The average effective query interval is 5.26 frames (~19% query rate). On NAVSIM, the RecogDrive-based dual system with ASSCG achieves 91.4 PDMS (+0.6 over ReCogDrive's 90.8) and reduces wall-clock inference latency from ~350 ms to ~270 ms per scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuPlan Hard20, ASSCG outperforms all listed baselines in composite score, with the largest margin over IDM (+14.21) and GameFormer (+5.23). Against the closest LLM-augmented competitor VLM-Planner (66.66), ASSCG gains +0.62 in score while improving safety metrics (TTC 75.93 vs 70.36, Drivable 96.40 vs 90.91) at the cost of lower route progress (68.16 vs 84.29), indicating a more conservative driving style. Compared to the base AsyncDriver (65.00), ASSCG adds +2.28 score at the same 0.32 s/frame latency as a 5-frame fixed schedule (64.27), yielding +3.01 over that latency-matched baseline. On NAVSIM, the RecogDrive-based system with ASSCG (91.4 PDMS) surpasses ReCogDrive (90.8) by +0.6 and DiffusionDriveV2 (91.2) by +0.2, while trailing only in EP (87.5 vs 88.0 for DiffusionDriveV2); it leads in DAC (98.3) and Comf (100) among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AsyncDriver fast-slow paradigm (ECCV 2024), which decouples a real-time GameFormer-based planner from an LLM slow module but relies on fixed or heuristic query schedules. It departs from prior gating approaches (VLMPlanner's CAI-Gate, uncertainty-based triggers) by casting the scheduling problem as a frame-level sequential decision with three discrete actions—QUERY, CACHE, and DROP—rather than a binary or continuous difficulty-based trigger. The RL training borrows GRPO's group-based clipped policy gradient from DeepSeekMath, adapted with an explicit per-query cost penalty, and the gate backbone uses RWKV for linear-time long-horizon inference instead of a Transformer. The NAVSIM instantiation additionally builds on RecogDrive's diffusion-planner architecture, replacing its VLM with a ViT fast branch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that LLM slow guidance can actively degrade closed-loop performance (Failure Intervals) and that intentionally suppressing it via DROP improves scores—challenges the prevailing assumption that more frequent LLM consultation is always beneficial. The ~60% latency reduction while simultaneously improving the nuPlan score (+2.28) makes the approach practically relevant for real-time deployment. A stated limitation is that the work evaluates *when* to use a slow branch within existing fast-slow systems rather than whether LLM reasoning is necessary at all; the authors identify harder end-to-end long-tail benchmarks as a natural next step, and the modest absolute gains (+2.28 on nuPlan, +0.6 PDMS on NAVSIM) leave open whether the approach scales to more challenging driving distributions.
