## Problem

Existing world models for interaction prediction rely on coarse action spaces—text prompts, navigation commands, or full-body poses—that cannot capture the fine-grained finger-level dynamics essential for dexterous hand-object manipulation. Compounding this, large-scale datasets with finely annotated dexterous gripper actions are scarce, making it difficult to train models that learn how subtle finger motions affect objects through contact.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262467/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes egocentric RGB frames (1080p) encoded into DINOv2 patch-level embeddings (P×d) as latent states, and a 132-dimensional action vector formed from the difference in 21 MANO 3D keypoints per hand (expressed in a shared coordinate frame via known rigid transforms) plus camera translation and Euler-angle rotation. For DROID's parallel-jaw grippers, dummy keypoints on concentric circles approximate finger spread. At inference for planning, the model takes an initial state, a goal image, and a sequence of robot joint angles, and outputs predicted future latent states, 12-channel fingertip/wrist heatmaps, and an optimized joint-angle trajectory via CEM.

## Method

DexWM uses a CDiT-based transformer predictor that takes a history of DINOv2 patch embeddings and the current action vector (conditioned via AdaLN) to directly regress the next latent state, avoiding iterative denoising for faster inference. The key architectural choice is representing dexterous actions as inter-frame 3D hand-keypoint differences rather than wrist positions or text, enabling the model to learn how specific finger configurations alter the scene. A critical training insight is that the standard MSE loss on DINOv2 embeddings is insufficient for dexterity because hands occupy a small image region; DexWM therefore adds a Hand Consistency (HC) auxiliary loss (λ=100) that forces a lightweight transformer head to predict fingertip and wrist heatmaps from the predicted latent state, yielding up to 34% improvement in PCK@20. The model is pre-trained on 829 hours of EgoDex human egocentric video plus DROID robot sequences, then fine-tuned on ~4 hours of exploratory RoboCasa simulation data, and deployed for zero-shot manipulation via goal-conditioned MPC with CEM optimizing joint angles against a combined latent-state and keypoint planning cost.

## Results

In open-loop trajectory prediction on EgoDex (4 s / 20 frames), DexWM achieves 68% average PCK@20 and 0.51 average embedding L2 error, outperforming PEVA* (63% PCK@20, 0.49 L2) and NWM* (48% PCK@20, 0.57 L2). For zero-shot robot transfer, DexWM reaches 72% success on reach, 28% on place, and 58% on grasp in RoboCasa simulation, and achieves 83% success (10/12) on real-world Franka Panda + Allegro gripper grasping. Diffusion Policy, trained on the same exploratory data, achieves only 16/8/0% in simulation and 0% on the real robot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NWM* (Bar et al., CVPR 2025), which conditions only on camera motion, DexWM improves average PCK@20 from 48 to 68 and reduces L2 error from 0.57 to 0.51, confirming that dexterous hand-keypoint actions are essential for fine-grained prediction. Against PEVA* (Bai et al., 2025), which conditions on upper-body poses without finger articulation, DexWM achieves 5 points higher average PCK@20 (68 vs. 63) while PEVA* has a marginally lower L2 error (0.49 vs. 0.51), indicating that perceptual similarity alone does not capture hand accuracy. Against Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., IJRR 2025), DexWM's advantage is dramatic: 72% vs. 16% on reach, 28% vs. 8% on place, 58% vs. 0% on grasp in simulation, and 83% vs. 0% on real-robot grasping. The paper further shows that pre-training Diffusion Policy on the same human dataset yields only 4% average simulation success, confirming that the gains come from modeling dexterous dynamics rather than pre-training data alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

DexWM extends the CDiT-based world model architecture from Navigation World Models [10] by replacing coarse navigation actions with fine-grained 3D hand keypoint differences and switching from iterative diffusion denoising to direct regression of DINOv2 patch features. It adopts the DINOv2 latent-state formulation from DINO-WM [93] but introduces a keypoint-based action representation (MANO 21-point hand deltas plus camera pose) that prior world models—conditioned on text, navigation, or full-body poses—lack. The hand-consistency auxiliary loss is a novel training objective not present in prior world model work, motivated by the observation that global embedding prediction alone fails to capture the small image region occupied by the hands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a world model trained predominantly on human egocentric video can transfer zero-shot to a specific dexterous robot embodiment (Franka + Allegro) for grasping, reaching, and placing, achieving 83% real-robot success with only ~4 hours of exploratory simulation fine-tuning. The finding that perceptual similarity (L2 embedding error) and hand accuracy (PCK@20) diverge—PEVA* wins on L2 but DexWM wins on PCK—challenges the assumption that better visual prediction automatically yields better dexterous modeling. Open questions include handling dynamic/stochastic environments, removing the ~4-hour embodiment-gap fine-tuning, and scaling to text-specified goals; the current 12-trial real-robot evaluation also leaves the robustness of the zero-shot claim under-validated.
