## Problem

Large vision-language models progressively disengage from visual evidence during long-horizon chain-of-thought reasoning (visual forgetting), yet existing remedies treat this as a uniform late-stage decay or rely on heuristic re-injection of visual inputs. The paper identifies two overlooked causal factors: early-stage attention decay disrupts the semantic scaffold for downstream reasoning, and residual visual attention collapses onto a small subset of task-irrelevant 'sink' tokens, degrading grounding even when total visual attention is preserved.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a text question, and generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer. During generation, per-step attention weights from each generated token to all visual tokens are extracted across all transformer layers and heads to compute the Visual Attention Proportion (VAP) and per-token sink attention ratios. Training uses 45K filtered RL samples sourced from MMR1 and Revisual-R1, with the base model being Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct evaluated at temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.95.

## Method

LASER is a GRPO-based post-training framework that introduces two complementary attention-shaping rewards computed over the decoding trajectory. First, a Visual Grounding Reward (R_vis) penalizes deviation of the per-step non-sink visual attention proportion from its peak value, encouraging sustained focus on semantically salient visual tokens from early decoding onward. Second, a Sink Suppression Reward (R_supp) explicitly penalizes responses where the average per-token attention on identified sink tokens exceeds the average over all visual tokens, redistributing attention away from uninformative regions. Sink tokens are identified via a two-stage criterion: isolating hidden-state dimensions with anomalously high mean absolute activation, then flagging visual tokens whose activation in those dimensions exceeds a top-k percentile threshold. Both rewards are gated on answer correctness to prevent rewarding attention patterns on incorrect reasoning paths, and a single scalar ω scales the combined attention signal relative to the accuracy reward.

## Results

LASER achieves 64.1 on MMStar and 60.7 on HallusionBench, setting new bests among open-source models and surpassing the closest visual-forgetting-specific baseline (VAPO-Thinker) by 1.1 and 3.3 points respectively. On MathVision, LASER reaches 44.6, outperforming the nearest competitor (VAPO-Thinker at 31.9) by a 12.7-point margin. Overall, LASER delivers 1.9% average improvement on visual reasoning benchmarks and 5.7% on visual perception tasks relative to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base, while remaining competitive with proprietary models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) on several benchmarks despite being a 7B open-source model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LASER wins on all eight benchmarks, with the largest gains on MathVision (+18.2), MathVerse (+14.5), and HallusionBench (+7.1). Among open-source reasoning-enhanced models, LASER outperforms VAPO-Thinker-7B (the strongest visual-forgetting-specific baseline) on six of eight benchmarks, most notably MathVision (+12.7) and HallusionBench (+3.3), though it trails on MathVista (72.9 vs. 75.6). LASER also beats VisionR1-7B on seven of eight benchmarks and R1-Onevision-7B on all eight. Against proprietary models, LASER remains competitive on HallusionBench (60.7 vs. GPT-5's 65.2 and Gemini's 64.1) but trails by 10–20 points on math reasoning benchmarks. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that both R_vis and R_supp contribute independently, with R_supp providing the largest single gain on general reasoning (+3.5 over R_vis alone).

## Relation to Prior Work

LASER builds directly on the GRPO-based RL reasoning paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and extended to LVLMs by VisionR1, R1-Onevision, and OpenVLThinker. It departs from prior visual-forgetting mitigation work (Reflection-V, VAPO-Thinker, hand-crafted visual reminders) by reframing the problem from a late-stage attention decay issue to a dual pathology—early-stage grounding failure plus distributional collapse onto visual sink tokens—and addressing both through training-time reward shaping rather than inference-time intervention. The sink token identification criterion draws on the massive-activation literature from LLMs (Kang et al., Xiao et al.) and extends it to the visual token space in multimodal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, training-time remedy for visual forgetting that avoids the brittleness of inference-time attention manipulation or heuristic re-injection, and the dual-reward design (grounding + sink suppression) offers a reusable template for attention-aware RL in any multimodal setting. However, all experiments are conducted on a single 7B architecture (Qwen-2.5-VL), leaving open whether the sink token identification criterion and reward magnitudes transfer to larger or differently-structured LVLMs. A key open question is whether the early-stage sensitivity finding generalizes beyond the controlled KV-cache scaling experiments on MMStar, and whether the approach remains effective as model scale and context length grow.
