## Problem

Existing multi-view driving scene generators enforce cross-view and temporal consistency by fine-tuning the diffusion backbone or attaching trainable cross-attention layers, which erodes pretrained text-image alignment and zero-shot generalization to unseen conditions such as heavy snowfall or rare object configurations. This knowledge loss means models remain bounded by their training distribution and cannot reliably synthesize adverse-weather or long-tail scenarios from text prompts alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes five conditioning signals: an HD map with road-layout layers and 3D bounding boxes, a per-view camera indicator encoded with Fourier features, a depth map derived by stacking LiDAR point clouds into occupancy and measuring ego-relative distance, a relative-pose map (in-plane coordinates of lifted 3D points encoded with Fourier features), and a free-form text prompt. The four structured signals are embedded and injected through a trainable ControlNet, while text is processed by the frozen text encoder; the output is a set of spatially and temporally consistent multi-view driving images (six cameras on nuScenes) that can be used directly as synthetic training data for downstream perception and planning models.

## Method

FrozenDrive keeps the entire Stable Diffusion backbone frozen and parameter-free, achieving multi-view and temporal consistency solely by restructuring the input context of existing self-attention layers. Multi-view Inflated Self-Attention (MISA) concatenates latent feature tokens from all camera views into a single sequence and runs one shared self-attention pass with frozen Q/K/V projections, enabling cross-view feature exchange. Temporal Reference Self-Attention (TRSA) augments the current frame's key and value banks with those computed from a previous reference frame at the same noise level, so the current query can attend to temporally aligned appearance and geometry cues. Both mechanisms are applied jointly in the LDM decoder only, where they interact with ControlNet-injected scene-level conditioning. An object-presence ratio loss further upweights pixels belonging to rare 3D object categories (via projected box masks with class-dependent weights and a max-overlap rule) to counteract long-tail detection bias during training.

## Results

On nuScenes validation, FrozenDrive achieves the best 3D detection mAP (21.87) and NDS (35.32) among all generative baselines, along with the highest BEV segmentation mIoU across all four categories (Lanes 28.88, Drivable 64.27, Divider 23.58, Crossing 11.66) and the lowest average planning L2 error (1.05 m, matching real nuScenes). Among SD-based image diffusion methods, FrozenDrive also attains the lowest FVD (136.8), though STDiT-based video diffusion models (DiST-4D: 22.6, MagicDrive-V2: 81.6) still lead on temporal quality. For data augmentation, FrozenDrive-augmented SparseDrive achieves 18.15 mAP / 24.95 NDS under night and 35.15 mAP / 44.26 NDS under rain, substantially outperforming all baselines on both perception and planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FrozenDrive outperforms all SD-based baselines (MagicDrive, Panacea, DriveArena, X-Scene) on every 3DOD and BEV segmentation metric, with the largest gaps over MagicDrive (mAP 21.87 vs. 12.92) and Panacea (mAP 21.87 vs. 13.72). Against STDiT-based video diffusion methods, FrozenDrive wins on 3DOD (beating DrivingSphere by +0.42 mAP and MagicDrive-V2 by +6.63 mAP) and BEV segmentation, but trails DiST-4D (FVD 136.8 vs. 22.6) and MagicDrive-V2 (FVD 136.8 vs. 81.6) on temporal video quality, which the authors attribute to the absence of explicit video-diffusion temporal modeling. In the data augmentation setting (Table 2), FrozenDrive dominates under night conditions, improving SparseDrive 3DOD mAP from 6.62 (baseline) to 18.15, versus 12.68 for MagicDrive-V2 and 8.89 for DriveArena; under rain, it achieves 35.15 mAP vs. 33.93 (MagicDrive-V2) and 33.46 (DriveArena), with the lowest planning L2 (0.58 m vs. 0.73 and 0.71 m). Rule-based augmentation (Thompson night filter, Li rain restoration) provides only marginal or negative gains, confirming that diffusion-based synthesis is necessary for meaningful adverse-condition improvement. On CLIP scores for unseen snow conditions, FrozenDrive (0.2507) substantially exceeds DriveArena (0.2277) and MagicDrive-V2 (0.2181), evidencing superior zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution weather.

## Relation to Prior Work

FrozenDrive builds on Stable Diffusion as a frozen backbone and ControlNet for structured conditioning, but departs sharply from the dominant paradigm in multi-view driving generation (MagicDrive, Panacea, DriveArena, DrivingSphere, MagicDrive-V2) that enforces cross-view and temporal consistency by fine-tuning the backbone or attaching trainable cross-attention modules. The paper's core positioning is that even a small number of added trainable parameters induces knowledge drift comparable to full fine-tuning, motivating a parameter-free alternative: restructuring the input context of frozen self-attention layers (MISA for cross-view, TRSA for temporal) rather than learning new attention weights. An object-presence ratio loss further addresses the long-tail fidelity gap that prior methods inherit from their training distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free design preserves zero-shot text-to-image alignment, enabling synthesis of genuinely unseen scenarios (e.g., heavy snow absent from training data) that fine-tuned baselines fail to produce coherently. The large downstream gains under adverse conditions (night mAP 6.62→18.15, rain planning L2 0.75→0.58) demonstrate practical value for AD robustness without weather-specific retraining. Key limitations include reliance on a single image-diffusion backbone (SD), where FVD still trails video-diffusion models (STDiT-based), and the absence of a standardized metric for data-augmentation quality. Extending the knowledge-preserving attention principle to DiT or video-diffusion backbones is identified as the most promising next step.
