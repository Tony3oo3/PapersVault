## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar methods uniformly assume full-body visibility during training, so when input videos show only partial body (upper-body or head-only), they hallucinate geometry and texture in unobserved limbs, corrupting even the visible regions. No prior method handles the full visibility spectrum—full-body, upper-body, and head-only—within a single unified optimization pipeline, forcing practitioners to maintain separate purpose-built systems for each input setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short monocular video (as short as ~10 seconds / 300 frames for upper-body clips) with arbitrary body visibility and produces a per-scene animatable 3D Gaussian avatar parameterized by SMPL-X pose, shape, and expression codes. Body masks are extracted via SAM, and the avatar is rendered in real-time at 512×512 resolution using 3DGS rasterization. A key structural detail is that the number of active Gaussians adapts to visibility: head-only inputs retain roughly half the Gaussians of full-body inputs, directly reducing memory and rendering cost.

## Method

FlexiAvatar builds a triplane-conditioned hybrid mesh-Gaussian representation on top of a personalized SMPL-X template, where two MLP groups decode static (identity) and pose-dependent Gaussian parameters from per-vertex triplane features. The central mechanism is visibility-aware optimization: after a 2,000-iteration warm-up, per-Gaussian visibility rates are computed from rasterizer flags, and Otsu's method automatically selects a threshold to exclude invisible Gaussians from both reconstruction and regularization losses, preventing spurious gradient propagation through the shared triplane into unobserved regions. For texture completion of never-seen areas (e.g., the back), MimicMotion generates a synthetic 360° rotation video whose frames are jointly optimized with the original footage at down-weighted loss. Three lightweight residual MLPs (face, left hand, right hand) are activated after coarse convergence to recover high-frequency appearance details, and the final avatar is animated via SMPL-X linear blend skinning and rendered with 3DGS.

## Results

On the NeuMan full-body benchmark, FlexiAvatar achieves 35.77 PSNR / 0.987 SSIM / 0.83 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline ExAvatar (34.80 / 0.984 / 0.90) by ~1 dB PSNR. On the INSTA head-only dataset it reaches 33.04 PSNR / 5.63 LPIPS, edging out RGBAvatar (32.72 / 6.04). On the TalkShow upper-body set it leads all three subjects (e.g., 36.73 PSNR on Conan vs. 35.66 for ExAvatar), and on ZJU-MoCap it attains the best PSNR across all six subjects (range 34.66–39.50). On in-the-wild WildAvatar, FlexiAvatar scores 30.98 PSNR / 2.93 LPIPS versus 28.65 / 3.81 for ToMiE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NeuMan (full-body), FlexiAvatar surpasses ExAvatar by 0.97 dB PSNR and Vid2AvatarPro by 3.06 dB, while also improving LPIPS from 0.90 to 0.83 over ExAvatar. On ZJU-MoCap, it outperforms both GauHuman and ToMiE on every subject, with the largest gap on subject 386 (39.50 vs. 37.29 PSNR for ToMiE). On WildAvatar (in-the-wild), FlexiAvatar leads ToMiE by 2.33 dB PSNR and GauHuman by 2.67 dB. On TalkShow (upper-body), it beats ExAvatar on all three subjects (by 0.67–2.06 dB), and substantially outperforms GART (by 4.7–5.5 dB) and GUAVA (by 1.0–3.0 dB). On INSTA (head-only), FlexiAvatar edges out RGBAvatar by 0.32 dB PSNR while matching its SSIM (0.953) and improving LPIPS from 6.04 to 5.63; it also surpasses FATE by 5.19 dB and GaussianBlendShapes by 3.03 dB. The paper reports an average PSNR improvement of approximately 3% across all datasets relative to the strongest per-setting baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexiAvatar builds directly on the SMPL-X parametric body model for unified articulation, 3D Gaussian Splatting for explicit real-time rendering, and ExAvatar's hybrid mesh-Gaussian representation with triplane conditioning. Its key departure is the visibility-aware optimization: whereas all prior 3DGS avatar methods (GART, GaussianAvatar, ExAvatar, Vid2AvatarPro) assume full-body visibility and head-only or upper-body methods are architecturally locked to a fixed coverage, FlexiAvatar restricts gradient flow to observed regions via per-Gaussian visibility rates and Otsu-based thresholding, unifying the full visibility spectrum in a single pipeline. The diffusion-based texture completion via MimicMotion and the part-specific residual MLPs for face/hands are borrowed from adjacent 2D generation and implicit-representation literature but repurposed here to fill unobserved regions and recover high-frequency detail without corrupting visible geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners no longer need separate purpose-built pipelines for full-body, upper-body, and head-only inputs, and the visibility-aware pruning yields a ~50% reduction in Gaussian count (and corresponding memory/runtime savings) for head-only avatars. The paper acknowledges a shared limitation with all 3DGS-based avatar methods—dynamically deforming clothing remains poorly modeled—and suggests extending the visibility-aware formulation to adaptively refine non-rigid regions as future work. An open question is whether the Otsu-based automatic thresholding generalizes to extreme cases (e.g., near-total occlusion or very short clips) where the bimodal visibility histogram assumption may break down.
