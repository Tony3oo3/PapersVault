## Problem

State-of-the-art Image Face Swapping (IFS) models achieve high identity similarity and attribute preservation, but applying them frame-by-frame to video produces severe temporal flickering, while existing Video Face Swapping (VFS) methods improve coherence yet lag significantly behind IFS in identity fidelity. The root cause is the absence of explicit paired supervision for VFS: no prior work constructs training data that directly teaches a video model to swap identity while preserving all dynamic attributes. This paper bridges the image-to-video gap by building a data pipeline that transfers IFS superiority into the video domain and a DiT-based architecture that exploits the resulting paired signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single source face image and a target video; the target video's pose sequence is extracted via 3D face reconstruction with an expression-adaptation step that recombines the source identity with the target's expression and pose to avoid identity leakage. During generation, the target video and its dilated face mask are concatenated channel-wise with the noisy latent, pose enters through a dedicated attention pathway, and the source identity is injected as token-level embeddings after patchification. The output is a face-swapped video at the target's resolution and duration, preserving pose, expression, lighting, and background while replacing the face identity.

## Method

OmniFace is a Diffusion Transformer trained with Flow Matching, whose core Modality-Aware Conditioning module routes three condition types through distinct injection pathways: spatio-temporal context (target video + mask concatenated along channels), structural guidance (pose via Adaptive Pose-Attention with RoPE-aligned indices), and identity (ID embeddings concatenated along the token dimension for full spatiotemporal attention interaction). Training data is produced by SyncID-Pipe, which pre-trains an Identity-Anchored Video Synthesizer on a First-Last-Frame video foundation model and pairs it with DreamID to construct bidirectional ID quadruplets that provide explicit swap supervision. A two-stage Synthetic-to-Real Training strategy first trains on model-generated ground truth for fast convergence and high identity similarity, then fine-tunes on real data augmented by background recomposition for visual realism. An Identity-Coherence Reward Weighting mechanism re-weights the flow matching loss by inverse per-frame ArcFace similarity, concentrating learning on difficult frames such as profile views and intense motion. At inference, Classifier-Free Guidance on the identity condition provides a tunable trade-off between identity fidelity and generation quality.

## Results

On IDBench-V, OmniFace achieves the best identity similarity across all three face-recognition backbones: ID-Arc 0.659, ID-Ins 0.713, and ID-Cur 0.688, surpassing the strongest IFS baseline DreamID (0.616/0.702/0.664) and the best VFS baseline CanonSwap (0.397/0.431/0.407). It also attains the lowest inter-frame identity variance (0.0029), best expression preservation (2.430), best background consistency (0.951), best subject consistency (0.966), and best motion smoothness (0.992). The only metrics where OmniFace is marginally behind CanonSwap are pose error (2.446 vs 2.430) and VFID (2.243 vs 2.176), which the authors attribute to CanonSwap's very low identity similarity causing minimal alteration to the original video. In the user study, OmniFace scores 3.85/4.22/4.15 (out of 5) on identity similarity, attribute preservation, and video quality, leading all six baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniFace decisively outperforms all six baselines on identity similarity: it leads DreamID (the strongest IFS method applied frame-by-frame) by 4.3 points on ID-Arc (0.659 vs 0.616) and CanonSwap (the strongest dedicated VFS method) by 26.2 points (0.659 vs 0.397). Against the other VFS baselines, the margins are even larger—VividFace (0.412), Stand-In (0.403), REFace (0.472), and Face-Adapter (0.440) all trail by 18–26 ID-Arc points. On attribute preservation, OmniFace matches or beats CanonSwap on expression (2.430 vs 2.477), background (0.951 vs 0.950), and subject consistency (0.966 vs 0.954), while CanonSwap retains a negligible edge on pose error (2.430 vs 2.446) and VFID (2.176 vs 2.243); the authors note CanonSwap's low identity similarity means it barely alters the original video, inflating its preservation scores. OmniFace also achieves the lowest identity variance (0.0029), indicating the most temporally stable identity across frames, and the highest motion smoothness (0.992). In the 19-participant user study, OmniFace leads every baseline on all three subjective dimensions, scoring 3.85 on identity similarity versus DreamID's 3.78 and CanonSwap's 1.99.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: image face swapping (IFS) models like DreamID and Face-Adapter that achieve high identity fidelity but lack temporal coherence, and video face swapping (VFS) methods like VividFace, CanonSwap, and Stand-In that handle temporal consistency but trail IFS in identity similarity. The key conceptual departure is the SyncID-Pipe data pipeline, which uses a pre-trained Identity-Anchored Video Synthesizer (itself built on First-Last-Frame DiT video generation) combined with an IFS model to construct bidirectional ID quadruplets, thereby transferring IFS's identity-preserving strength into explicit VFS supervision. Architecturally, OmniFace builds on DiT-based video diffusion with Flow Matching, introducing a Modality-Aware Conditioning module that decomposes conditions into spatio-temporal context, structural guidance (via Adaptive Pose-Attention initialized from the IVS), and identity injection, while the Identity-Coherence Reward Weighting mechanism borrows reward-weighted policy optimization ideas from diffusion RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The persistent gap between IFS and VFS in identity similarity has limited practical deployment of video face swapping; demonstrating that a VFS model can match or even slightly exceed a dedicated IFS model (DreamID) in identity while maintaining temporal coherence is a meaningful step toward closing this gap. The introduction of IDBench-V addresses a real evaluation gap, and the demonstrated versatility to accessory, outfit, and hairstyle swapping suggests the SyncID-Pipe paradigm generalizes beyond faces. Open questions include scalability to longer videos, inference cost of the multi-stage training pipeline, dependence on a specific IFS model for data generation, and whether the approach extends to multi-person or non-portrait scenarios. The reliance on synthetic paired data also raises questions about distribution shift when the underlying IFS or IVS models are updated.
