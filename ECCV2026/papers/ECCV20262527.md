## Problem

Hairstyle transfer methods struggle when source and reference images exhibit large head-pose discrepancies: existing GAN-based approaches rely on aligned face datasets, and diffusion-based methods commonly fail to localize the editable hair region correctly, producing misplaced or over-extended edits while also under-reproducing fine-grained reference structure and texture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262527/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source portrait image and a reference portrait image (both unconstrained, in-the-wild RGB), with an optional auxiliary text prompt for hair-color control. At inference, the source is first converted to a hair-removed base image via an instruction-based editor (FLUX.2), and the pipeline outputs a hairstyle-transferred image where the reference hair is re-synthesized on the source head geometry, along with a source-aligned coarse hair mask derived from cross-attention that guides the inpainting region.

## Method

H-Adapter fine-tunes a pretrained IP-Adapter on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with a region-specific loss that applies the standard diffusion denoising objective only within hair-masked regions while regularizing non-hair regions to match the baseline (adapter-free) diffusion model's noise prediction. This training induces spatially disentangled cross-attention maps, from which a coarse source-aligned hair mask is extracted by aggregating all token attention maps except a designated separator token (t₈ out of 16). At inference, a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline first runs a warm-up denoising step to extract the attention mask, performs ~20 inpainting steps to obtain an intermediate image, then uses a segmentation model (BiSeNet) to derive a pixel-level hair mask for a second full denoising pass. Additionally, the attention-derived mask is recomputed at every denoising timestep to spatially gate the H-Adapter reference branch in cross-attention, suppressing its influence outside the hair region throughout generation.

## Results

On the pose-different CelebA-HQ subset (yaw gap > 15°), H-Adapter achieves the best FID (12.47), FID_CLIP (3.98), SSIM (0.831), and CLIP-I (0.659) among all compared methods, with a competitive PSNR of 23.06. In the VLM-as-a-judge evaluation (GPT-4o), it scores highest on all three axes: Hair Fidelity (3.11/5), Non-Hair Preservation (4.23/5), and Artifact Quality (3.73/5). In a 2AFC human preference study with 3,193 valid votes from 53 respondents, H-Adapter is preferred 72.7% of the time overall (p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-Adapter outperforms all five baselines on FID, FID_CLIP, SSIM, and CLIP-I under pose mismatch. The closest competitor is HairFastGAN (FID 12.78 vs. 12.47; FID_CLIP 4.53 vs. 3.98), which retains a PSNR advantage (24.40 vs. 23.06) indicating slightly better pixel-level non-hair reconstruction, but H-Adapter leads on every other metric including reference faithfulness (CLIP-I 0.659 vs. 0.649). HairCLIPv2 is second-closest on FID (13.44) but trails substantially on FID_CLIP (7.91 vs. 3.98) and CLIP-I (0.623 vs. 0.659), and also has a higher PSNR (23.63). Stable-Hair and HairFusion are clearly outperformed across all five metrics, with FID gaps of ~13 and ~15 points respectively, reflecting their weaker pose robustness. In the VLM-as-a-judge and human preference evaluations, H-Adapter is the only method to rank first on all three VLM axes simultaneously and is preferred over every baseline in pairwise human comparisons (55.3%–81.1% per-baseline win rates).

## Relation to Prior Work

H-Adapter builds directly on IP-Adapter's cross-attention-based reference conditioning, but departs from its global injection paradigm by introducing a region-specific loss that decouples hair-region reconstruction from non-hair consistency regularization. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two threads: the hairstyle-transfer literature (HairFastGAN, HairFusion, Stable-Hair, HairCLIPv2) that tackles pose mismatch via explicit alignment or pose cues, and the attention-manipulation editing literature (Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl, DiffEdit) that derives spatial masks from attention maps. The key positioning is that rather than adding an external pose signal or flow field, the paper lets the training objective itself induce the spatial separation needed for localization, then feeds the resulting attention-derived mask into a standard diffusion inpainting pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is clear for virtual try-on and e-commerce applications where source and reference images rarely share head pose, and the plug-and-play design (compatible with text prompts, identity adapters, and source-free generation) broadens its utility. The broader methodological insight—that a region-specific training loss can repurpose cross-attention as a free localization signal without auxiliary segmentation at training time—is potentially transferable to other region-constrained editing tasks. Limitations include dependence on an external hair-removal model (FLUX.2) and a segmentation network (BiSeNet) at inference, and evaluation is confined to CelebA-HQ with SD v1.5, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to newer diffusion backbones or more diverse in-the-wild imagery beyond the curated evaluation sets.
