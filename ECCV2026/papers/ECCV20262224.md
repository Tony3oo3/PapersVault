## Problem

Training Energy-Based Models in high-dimensional foundation-model token spaces (e.g., 1536-D DINOv2 features) is unstable because strong anisotropy and cross-dimensional correlations violate the isotropic Euclidean metric assumed by finite-step Langevin dynamics, causing poorly conditioned MCMC trajectories and collapsed energy basins. Prior unified EBM approaches circumvent this by compressing representations into low-dimensional CNN bottlenecks, sacrificing the rich token-level semantics needed for accurate multi-class anomaly localization.

## Input / Output

Input images are resized to 700×700 and center-cropped to 672×672; a frozen DINOv2 ViT-G/14 backbone extracts 14×14 patch tokens aggregated via mean pooling across transformer layers 22–28 into 1536-D vectors, which are then Z-score standardized per category. At inference, the method produces a pixel-level anomaly map by projecting the energy gradient norm ‖∇_u E_φ(u)‖₂ onto the 2D token grid, bilinearly upsampling, and applying a Gaussian filter (σ=4.0); the image-level score is the mean of the top 1% of upsampled pixel scores.

## Method

ReFP-AD decouples representation geometry from density estimation by first learning an OT-coupled rectified flow that transports standardized tokens z into a well-conditioned latent space u with an isotropic Gaussian target, using entropic Sinkhorn coupling for stable high-dimensional pairings and a continuous-time velocity-matching objective. Flow checkpoint selection is guided not by flow-matching loss alone but by SGLD-oriented diagnostics (conditioning number, residual correlation, tail ratio) with a Spearman rank-correlation guardrail to prevent manifold collapse. In the transported u-space, an unconstrained 3-layer residual MLP energy function is trained via persistent contrastive divergence with preconditioned SGLD (RMSProp-style diagonal preconditioner), enabling stable negative-phase sampling in as few as 20 steps. Anomaly scoring at inference uses the energy gradient norm as a local deviation measure, which the well-conditioned latent geometry makes stable and informative without iterative refinement. The entire pipeline—flow and EBM—is a single unified model shared across all categories, with category identity used only for input standardization and balanced replay-buffer initialization.

## Results

Under a strict unified protocol (single shared model across all categories), ReFP-AD achieves 98.6% Image AUROC and 97.9% Pixel AUROC on MVTec-AD, and 97.3% Image AUROC and 99.0% Pixel AUROC on VisA. These results surpass the strongest normalizing-flow baseline HGAD by +0.2% Image AUROC on both datasets and outperform the prior best unified EBM (stabilized MPDR) by +2.6% on MVTec-AD and +10.8% on VisA. Ablations confirm that removing the rectified-flow preconditioning drops VisA Image AUROC from 97.3% to 87.0%, and that stable detection requires only 20 pSGLD steps (vs. hundreds typically needed in raw token space).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReFP-AD outperforms all nine baselines on at least one metric across both datasets. Against the strongest normalizing-flow method HGAD [45], ReFP-AD edges ahead by +0.2% Image AUROC on MVTec-AD (98.6 vs. 98.4) and +0.2% on VisA (97.3 vs. 97.1), while matching or exceeding pixel-level scores (97.9 vs. 97.9 on MVTec-AD, 99.0 vs. 98.9 on VisA). The most dramatic gain is over the prior unified EBM baseline MPDR† [46]: ReFP-AD improves VisA Image AUROC by +10.8% (97.3 vs. 86.5) and MVTec-AD by +2.6% (98.6 vs. 96.0), attributed to avoiding the low-dimensional CNN bottleneck that MPDR requires for MCMC stability. Against the earlier EBM of Genc et al. [10] (72.0% I-AUROC on MVTec-AD), the improvement is +26.6%, reflecting the benefit of operating in foundation-token space with geometric preconditioning rather than raw pixel or low-level features. Among retrieval/distribution baselines, ReFP-AD surpasses PaDiM [6] by +14.4% Image AUROC on MVTec-AD and +10.5% on VisA, and outperforms DRAEM [48] by +10.5% and +11.8% respectively. FastFlow [47] and CFLOW-AD [12] trail by 6.8–20.1% Image AUROC depending on dataset, with the gap widening on the more heterogeneous VisA benchmark. The per-category SimpleNet [27] baseline (99.6% on MVTec-AD) remains slightly ahead of the unified ReFP-AD (98.6%), but the unified model narrows this to only 0.6% while simultaneously covering all 15 categories with a single model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of energy-based anomaly detection and MCMC preconditioning. It builds on rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022) and preconditioned SGLD (Li et al., 2016), but repurposes the rectified flow not as a generative estimator (as in flow-matching anomaly detectors) nor as a likelihood model (as in normalizing-flow methods like HGAD or CFLOW), but purely as a geometric preconditioner that reshapes anisotropic DINOv2 token distributions into isotropic coordinates before PCD-based EBM training. This departs from prior unified EBM approaches (MPDR, Genc et al.) that stabilize sampling by compressing representations into low-dimensional CNN features or anchoring MCMC to autoencoder manifolds, and from the broader trend of using foundation-model tokens with discriminative scoring (PatchCore, AnomalyDINO) rather than explicit density estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's core diagnostic—that EBM training instability in high-dimensional token spaces is fundamentally geometric (anisotropy and cross-dimensional correlation violating Langevin dynamics assumptions) rather than architectural—reframes how practitioners should approach density-based anomaly detection in foundation-model feature spaces. The practical implication is that full-dimensional (1536-D) EBM training becomes feasible with only ~20 SGLD steps, removing the need for dimensionality-reducing bottlenecks that sacrifice localization quality. Key limitations include increased inference latency (10-step RK4 integration plus a backward pass for gradient-norm scoring), making the method less suitable for real-time industrial inspection, and the open question of whether the preconditioned energy landscape can be distilled into an efficient feed-forward scorer without losing the stability benefits.
