## Problem

Existing music-to-dance generation methods struggle to produce high-quality, physically plausible motion under strict inference budgets. Diffusion-based approaches require many denoising steps due to curved sampling trajectories, while autoregressive models incur high latency from sequential decoding, and GAN-based methods suffer from mode collapse. No prior work jointly optimizes both the number of sampling steps (trajectory-level efficiency) and the per-step computational cost (architecture-level efficiency) for long-horizon 3D dance synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a music sequence represented as 35-dimensional per-frame features (20-dim MFCC, 12-dim Chroma, 1-dim Peak, 1-dim Beat, 1-dim Envelope) extracted via Librosa at 30 FPS, along with a one-hot dance genre label, and produces a full 147-dimensional dance sequence (3-dim root translation + 144-dim rotation) in a single non-autoregressive pass. Sequences span 1024 frames (34.13 s) on FineDance or 1200 frames (20 s) on AIST++. The system also accepts optional joint-wise or temporal constraints (a binary mask plus constrained values) for interactive motion editing without retraining.

## Method

FlowerDance combines a MeanFlow-based generative strategy with a lightweight BiMamba architecture. At the generative-strategy level, it predicts interval-averaged velocity (rather than instantaneous velocity) so that the training objective aligns with large-interval Euler ODE updates at inference, enabling high-fidelity generation in as few as 5–20 steps; a Physical Consistency Constraint (PCC) recovers an explicit motion sample from the predicted mean velocity and regularizes it with reconstruction, forward-kinematic joint-position, and velocity losses to prevent temporal jitter, root drift, and foot sliding that arise from small velocity errors propagating along the kinematic chain. At the architecture level, a Bidirectional Mamba backbone (forward and backward selective state-space branches fused by addition and a multiplicative skip connection) provides O(n) temporal modeling with bidirectional context, while a parameter-free Channel-Level Cross-Modal Fusion (element-wise addition) replaces cross-attention to inject music and genre conditioning, reducing memory and latency. Time information is injected via FiLM modulation, and a time-decayed soft mask during sampling enables coherent motion editing without additional training.

## Results

On FineDance, FlowerDance achieves the best FID_k (29.73), DIV_k (8.42), DIV_g (7.18), and BAS (0.232), while remaining competitive on FSR (0.147) and FID_g (19.59). On AIST++, it obtains the lowest FID_k (20.50) and highest DIV_g (6.52). In generation efficiency, FlowerDance reaches 2008 FPS at 1024 frames and 4145 FPS at 10240 frames with only 63M parameters, outperforming the next-best method (FineNet, 656/1137 FPS) by 3–4×. In the user study, FlowerDance scores highest on Dance Quality (4.18), Synchronization (4.41), and Creativity (4.33) on a 5-point scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowerDance substantially outperforms all baselines on kinetic FID (FID_k), reducing it from 50.00 (Lodge, MEGA) to 29.73 on FineDance and from 28.16 (Bailando) to 20.50 on AIST++. It also leads on diversity metrics (DIV_k, DIV_g) and beat alignment (BAS) across both datasets, indicating more varied and rhythmically aligned motions. The main trade-off is FID_g: MEGA achieves a lower FID_g (13.02 vs. 19.59 on FineDance) and Bailando does so on AIST++ (9.62 vs. 15.75), suggesting slightly better geometric joint-position fidelity in those methods. On FSR (foot sliding), Lodge is far superior (0.028 vs. 0.147), reflecting its explicit foot-contact modeling. In generation efficiency, FlowerDance is 3× faster than FineNet (2008 vs. 656 FPS at 1024 frames) and 7× faster than MEGA (2008 vs. 288 FPS), while using fewer parameters (63M vs. 94M and 120M respectively). In the user study, FlowerDance edges out MEGA (the closest competitor) by 0.06 on DQ, 0.18 on DS, and 0.06 on DC, confirming human-perceived superiority in synchronization and quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowerDance sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) few-step generative modeling via MeanFlow, which predicts interval-averaged velocity to align training with ODE-based inference, and (2) efficient sequence modeling via selective state-space models (Mamba). In the music-to-dance generation landscape, prior work is dominated by GAN-based, autoregressive, and diffusion-based methods, all of which either produce repetitive motion, incur sequential decoding latency, or require many denoising steps. FlowerDance departs from this lineage by adopting a non-autoregressive BiMamba backbone with a parameter-free channel-level fusion (replacing cross-attention) and by introducing a Physical Consistency Constraint that is specific to the kinematic structure of SMPL-based 3D motion—something absent from MeanFlow's original image/video/audio applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous achievement of SOTA dance quality and a 3–7× inference speedup over prior methods (reaching 4145 FPS at 10⁴ frames) makes FlowerDance particularly relevant for real-time interactive applications such as virtual avatars and live performance tools. The ablation revealing that MeanFlow training completely diverges (NaN) without the Physical Consistency Constraint is a practically important finding: it shows that flow-matching objectives designed for pixel- or waveform-level data cannot be transferred to kinematic-chain representations without explicit physical regularization. Open questions include whether one-step generation is achievable for 3D motion, how the approach scales to multi-person or text-conditioned settings, and whether the BiMamba backbone's advantages persist at substantially larger dataset scales.
