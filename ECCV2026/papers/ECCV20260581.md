## Problem

Existing mutual-scoring zero-shot anomaly detection methods (e.g., MuSc) assume access to large reference sets and fail under the practical Batch Zero-Shot constraint of only B−1 intra-batch references with pose variation. In this setting, two critical failure modes emerge: an 'anomaly-to-anomaly shortcut' where defective patches erroneously match similar-looking defects in other images, and a lack of anomaly-aware semantics in pretrained features that conflates background noise with true defect severity, both leading to missed detections.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method receives a small batch of B unlabeled RGB images (B=4 for MVTec AD/VisA, B=8 for Real-IAD), each resized to 512×512, and evaluates every target image exclusively against the remaining B−1 intra-batch references without any target-domain annotations or pre-collected memory bank. It produces a patch-level anomaly map (aggregated via min across references) and an image-level score (max over patches). Training requires a target-disjoint auxiliary dataset with pixel-level anomaly annotations (VisA or MVTec AD), but the model is frozen at test time, preserving zero-shot deployment.

## Method

DEMUS decouples spatial correspondence ('where-to-match') from anomaly discrimination ('how-to-score') via two lightweight multi-scale Inception-style adapters (parallel 1×1, 3×3, 5×5 convolutions with residual connections) on a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone, using features from layers 6, 12, 18, and 24. Stage 1 trains the Matching Adapter with MSE regression against an ECC-estimated alignment oracle and random affine pose augmentation, learning pose-invariant part correspondences that are invariant to anomaly content. Stage 2 then trains the Scoring Adapter on the established correspondences, enforcing normal-normal pairs to converge and anomalous pairs to diverge in feature distance. At inference, a single top-k nearest-neighbor matching step (k=3) on matching features yields softmax-normalized soft correspondences, and scoring features compute weighted L2 distances exclusively on these aligned parts, with the final patch score taken as the minimum across all B−1 references—mechanistically preventing the anomaly-to-anomaly shortcut while preserving O(nN²) efficiency.

## Results

On the three industrial benchmarks (MVTec AD, VisA, Real-IAD), DEMuS achieves an average image-level AUROC of 88.37% and AP of 88.73%, outperforming the strongest text-guided baseline AdaptCLIP by +4.20% AUROC and +6.58% AUPRO. At the pixel level, DEMuS reaches 96.91% PAUC and 92.28% AUPRO on average, surpassing MuSc by +0.58% PAUC and +3.58% AUPRO. Under severe pose variation (random rotations and translations up to 128 px), DEMuS retains 95.53% of its original image AP and 97.32% of its AUPRO, while MuSc drops to 91.82% and 93.35% retention respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEMuS outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest text-guided method AdaptCLIP, DEMuS gains +4.20% average image AUROC (88.37 vs 84.17) and +6.58% average AUPRO (92.28 vs 85.70), with the largest gap on Real-IAD (+6.58% AUC, +5.70% AP). Against the direct mutual-scoring baseline MuSc, DEMuS improves average image AUROC by +4.94% and achieves a striking +10.86% AP on the multi-view Real-IAD dataset (77.60 vs 66.74), while also improving pixel-level AUPRO by +3.58% on average. Even when compared to the multi-modal mutual-scoring variants (APRIL-GAN-ms and AdaptCLIP-ms, which combine text and vision), DEMuS—using only visual references—still leads by +1.51% average AUC and +3.07% average AUPRO. Under the pose-variation stress test, DEMuS's advantage widens dramatically: it retains 94.63% of its original AUC versus only 87.41% for MuSc, a 7.22-point gap in retention that confirms the decoupled matching adapter's role in spatial robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEMUS extends MuSc's mutual-scoring paradigm for zero-shot anomaly detection by restricting the reference set to a small intra-batch (rather than the full test set) and introducing a two-stage decoupled training process with lightweight multi-scale adapters on a frozen DINOv3 backbone. It departs from the dominant text-guided ZSAD line (WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP, AA-CLIP) by being entirely vision-only, and from prior batch-based explorations (FiSeCLIP, PatchCLIP) by eliminating text priors and cross-modal filtering in favor of explicit spatial alignment supervision. The key conceptual departure is the identification of the 'anomaly-to-anomaly shortcut' as a fundamental failure mode of untrained mutual scoring under pose variation, motivating the separation of where-to-match from how-to-score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled matching/scoring framework offers a practical, latency-friendly solution for real production lines where products arrive in small, unaligned batches and no pre-collected normal templates exist. The 95.5% performance retention under severe pose shifts is directly relevant to deployment robustness. However, the method still requires an annotated auxiliary dataset (VisA or MVTec AD) to train the adapters, and it targets only rigid/semi-rigid pose variations, leaving highly deformable products (textiles, cables) as an open challenge. Future work on unsupervised flow-based matching and self-supervised anomaly generation could remove the auxiliary-data dependency entirely.
