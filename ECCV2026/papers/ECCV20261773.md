## Problem

Feedforward reconstruction models (FRMs) can reconstruct dense 3D geometry from unposed RGB images, but equipping them with robust 3D panoptic segmentation remains an open problem. Existing approaches either freeze the geometric backbone and rely on external 2D features with heuristic post-processing (e.g., QUBO, HDBSCAN clustering), or add dense contrastive heads that require unsupervised clustering at inference, yielding coarse masks. Pano3D eliminates these heuristics by introducing a natively differentiable, query-based panoptic head that is jointly trained with the geometry decoder.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unordered collection of N unposed RGB images (no camera intrinsics or extrinsics required) at 384×512 (MUSt3R) or 378×518 (Pi3) resolution, using 15 views for training and up to 20 frames for inference. It produces a dense 3D point cloud in a globally aligned coordinate system with per-pixel confidence, per-pixel semantic class logits, and instance-aware mask predictions that are 3D-consistent across the entire input sequence. Because the predicted masks and pointmaps are pixel-aligned, lifting 2D masks to 3D instance segmentation is a direct correspondence without additional registration.

## Method

Pano3D appends a 9-layer Mask2Former-style set-prediction decoder to a pretrained FRM (MUSt3R or Pi3), where K learnable object queries (100 or 200) act as sequence-level detectors that track a single 3D instance across all N views. A key architectural choice is the geometric feature bridge: features from the frozen monocular encoder (local context) and the finetuned multi-view decoder (scene-level geometry) are concatenated and projected through an MLP adapter, giving the mask decoder both precise local appearance and cross-view consistency. Class predictions are computed via dot product with frozen CLIP text embeddings, enabling open-vocabulary querying at inference. The core training insight is joint finetuning: the geometry decoder is unfrozen and optimized on a combined loss (pointmap regression + bipartite matching segmentation loss), with a gradient scaling factor of 0.1 applied to segmentation gradients before they propagate into the geometry decoder to prevent geometric corruption. This joint optimization yields multi-view consistent masks without QUBO post-processing or explicit consistency losses, and the framework generalizes across both memory-based (online) and all-to-all attention reconstruction backbones.

## Results

Pano3D (MUSt3R backbone) achieves state-of-the-art 3D semantic segmentation with 65.3 mIoU / 76.6 mAcc on ScanNet, 24.6 mIoU / 32.9 mAcc on ScanNet200, and 29.7 mIoU / 42.0 mAcc on ScanNet++, outperforming the previous best (UNITE) by 16.6, 10.1, and 12.5 mIoU respectively. For class-agnostic 3D instance segmentation, Pano3D reaches AP50 of 38.9 on ScanNet (+9.3 over UNITE) and 36.8 on ScanNet200. On the IGGT voxel-based benchmark (ScanNet++), the Pi3-backed variant achieves 29.46 3D mIoU and 52.54 2D mIoU while maintaining competitive geometry (AbsRel 2.44, τ 87.30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pano3D decisively outperforms all RGB-only baselines on 3D semantic segmentation: it beats UNITE [20] (the closest competitor) by 16.6 mIoU on ScanNet and 12.5 mIoU on ScanNet++, and surpasses PanSt3R [45] by 22.7 mIoU on ScanNet. On 3D instance segmentation (Table 2), Pano3D leads UNITE by +9.3 AP50 on ScanNet and +8.0 AP50 on ScanNet200, though on ScanNet++ it trails UNITE slightly in AP25 (39.7 vs 40.4) due to its fixed query count struggling with the higher instance density. On the IGGT voxel-based benchmark (Table 3), Pano3D (Pi3) achieves 29.46 3D mIoU vs IGGT's 20.14 and 52.54 2D mIoU vs IGGT's 31.31, while IGGT retains a small per-frame geometry edge (AbsRel 2.61 vs 2.44 for Pano3D-Pi3, though Pano3D-Pi3 actually matches Pi3's 2.44). The geometry-only baselines MUSt3R [1] and Pi3 [39] serve as ablation references showing that joint training slightly degrades per-frame depth (AbsRel 3.10 vs 2.95 for MUSt3R) but improves multi-view consistency (τ MVD 74.81 vs 80.62) and enables all semantic outputs. Earlier methods (PE3R [14], Uni3R [36], LSM [8], SAM3D [42]) are outperformed by wide margins across all metrics, confirming that the set-based query decoder with joint finetuning is substantially more effective than contrastive clustering or frozen-backbone approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pano3D sits at the intersection of feedforward 3D reconstruction (DUSt3R, MUSt3R, Pi3, VGGT) and set-prediction segmentation (Mask2Former, ODIN), but departs sharply from prior unified approaches in two ways. Where PanSt3R freezes the geometry backbone and relies on external DINOv2 features plus QUBO postprocessing, and UNITE/IGGT require unsupervised HDBSCAN clustering at inference, Pano3D jointly finetunes the geometry decoder with a natively differentiable mask decoder, eliminating all heuristic post-processing. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that cross-view geometric features already encode object boundaries, so semantic supervision can flow directly into the geometry decoder as an implicit structural regularizer rather than requiring explicit consistency losses or contrastive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The large SOTA margins (up to 16.6 mIoU over UNITE on ScanNet) and the elimination of clustering/QUBO pipelines make this a practically important step toward feedforward 3D scene understanding without inference-time heuristics. The mutual-benefit finding—that semantic gradients improve multi-view geometric consistency, not just segmentation—suggests a broader principle for multi-task 3D models. However, the method still struggles in cluttered scenes where nearby instances get merged, and on ScanNet++ (more instances per scene) the AP25 gap over UNITE narrows or reverses, indicating that query-based detection has a recall ceiling that clustering-based methods can partially compensate for. The authors note that future foundation models should ideally be trained jointly on geometry and semantics from inception rather than finetuned post-hoc.
