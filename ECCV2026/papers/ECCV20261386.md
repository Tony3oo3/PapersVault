## Problem

Existing segmentation-based methods for pedestrian pathway extraction produce fragmented, disconnected graphs that are unreliable for navigation, because sidewalks are often partially occluded, implicitly defined, and exhibit complex connectivity patterns that pixel-level labeling cannot capture. The paper addresses this by reframing pathway extraction as a sequential decision problem—simulating a traveler navigating the built environment—rather than a static pixel-classification task.

## Input / Output

At each generation step, the model consumes a 4-channel input: a 256×256 (or 224×224 for ViT) RGB crop centered on the current node concatenated with a single-channel canvas rasterizing all previously generated edges. The model outputs a discrete action from a 361-way vocabulary (36 angle bins × 10 distance bins + STOP), which is decoded into a new edge in normalized [0,1]² coordinates. The full output is an undirected graph of nodes (2D points) and edges (LineString segments) representing the connected pedestrian network for the intersection.

## Method

TraversRL is a vision-conditioned iterative graph generator trained in two stages. First, supervised pretraining (TraversRL-Pre) learns to imitate ground-truth edge expansions via cross-entropy with localization noise injected to build robustness against rollout drift. Second, RL fine-tuning via GRPO optimizes an outcome-level graph reward (buffered IoU) under KL regularization to the frozen reference policy, with a group of K=4 rollouts providing group-relative advantages. The key novelty is a local stepwise reward that measures per-edge buffered overlap efficiency (fraction of newly added buffered area overlapping ground truth), which is normalized within each rollout and additively combined with the terminal advantage to provide finer credit assignment without destabilizing training. The discretized direction-distance action space is specifically designed to handle short, curvilinear pedestrian segments and span occlusions that would break segmentation-based pipelines.

## Results

On the in-domain WashingtonInter dataset, TraversRL-Local (Swin-T backbone) achieves 0.584 buffered IoU and 0.611 TravSim, compared to Tile2Net's 0.264 IoU and 0.114 TravSim. Averaged across three backbones and three datasets, TraversRL-Global outperforms Tile2Net by 43.6% in IoU and 205% in TravSim, while TraversRL-Local adds a further 2.6% IoU and 1.6% TravSim over the global-reward variant. Under zero-shot transfer to Seattle and D.C. (PathwayBench), TraversRL-Local (Swin-T) reaches 0.230/0.418 and 0.252/0.535 (IoU/TravSim) respectively, maintaining large margins over Tile2Net (0.169/0.175 and 0.226/0.317).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TraversRL-Local decisively outperforms the state-of-the-art segmentation baseline Tile2Net across all three datasets and all three backbones: on WashingtonInter, the best TraversRL-Local (Swin-T) achieves 0.584 IoU vs. Tile2Net's 0.264 (a 121% relative gain) and 0.611 TravSim vs. 0.114 (a 436% relative gain). Under zero-shot transfer to Seattle and D.C., TraversRL-Local maintains large margins (e.g., 0.230 vs. 0.169 IoU on Seattle; 0.252 vs. 0.226 IoU and 0.535 vs. 0.317 TravSim on D.C.). The two road-oriented baselines fail catastrophically on pedestrian pathways: VecRoad produces near-empty outputs (<0.001 on all metrics) because its junction-detection heuristics do not fire on sparse, visually weak sidewalk junctions, and NETracer yields fragmented graphs (0.098 IoU, 0.005 TravSim on WashingtonInter) because its road-tuned tracing rules prematurely reject subtle pathway continuations. These results confirm that existing road-network extraction pipelines cannot be directly transferred to the pedestrian domain without substantial redesign.

## Relation to Prior Work

TraversRL extends the iterative crop-and-canvas graph construction paradigm introduced by RoadTracer [2] from road networks to pedestrian pathways, adapting the action space (variable distance bins, finer angular resolution) and architecture to handle narrower, more occluded, and topologically complex sidewalk segments. It departs from the dominant segmentation-first pipeline (exemplified by Tile2Net) by formulating pathway extraction as a sequential decision process, and further departs from prior iterative tracers (VecRoad, NETracer, RNGDet) by introducing RL fine-tuning with GRPO to optimize non-differentiable, trajectory-level graph quality metrics. The local stepwise reward for advantage shaping is a novel contribution that draws on the RLVR paradigm from LLM reasoning but applies it to geometric credit assignment in graph generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pedestrian pathway maps are critical for accessible navigation and urban planning, yet remain difficult to produce at scale; this paper demonstrates that iterative generation with RL fine-tuning yields substantially more connected and geometrically precise networks than segmentation-based baselines, with encouraging zero-shot transfer to unseen cities. Open questions remain around scale variation across datasets, practical start-node seeding without ground-truth initialization, and coverage of disconnected pathway components. The finding that road-oriented tracers essentially fail on pedestrian pathways underscores the need for domain-specific methods, while the RL-based approach offers a template for other long-horizon graph extraction tasks where final quality is hard to decompose into per-step supervision.
