## Problem

Aerial geometric 3D vision (retrieval, matching, reconstruction) suffers from a severe data gap: existing UAV datasets either provide photorealistic imagery without geometric ground truth, or offer dense annotations but are confined to a few localized city models with limited viewpoint variety. No prior dataset simultaneously delivers global geographic scale, diverse environmental conditions, and synchronized pixel-level metric depth with verified 6-DoF poses needed for geometry-aware learning of UAV foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262265/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each AirZoo frame is a synchronized tuple of a 1600×1200 RGB image, a pixel-aligned absolute depth map in meters, calibrated pinhole intrinsics (fx, fy, cx, cy), and a 6-DoF pose logged in both WGS84 and ECEF coordinates, captured at 30 fps along continuous trajectories spanning 0–800 m altitude and 10°–90° gimbal pitch. Downstream tasks consume these as: (a) a single UAV query image matched against a multi-scale satellite tile gallery (512² and 1024² crops at 0.3 m GSD), (b) a UAV–orthophoto image pair for dense correspondence estimation, or (c) a 2–24 frame UAV clip for feed-forward pose and point-map prediction. The real-world AirZoo-Real split adds 9,430 RTK-verified images across four sites and three time windows (including low-light 22:00–24:00) with depth rendered from oblique photogrammetry models.

## Method

AirZoo is generated by a fully automated AirSim–Cesium for Unreal–Unreal Engine 5 pipeline: Cesium streams Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles (world-scale photogrammetric meshes) into UE5, AirSim executes WGS84 waypoint trajectories with reproducible pose control, and a Python controller performs on-the-fly coordinate conversion and synchronized multi-sensor capture after tile stabilization. The pipeline systematically randomizes weather (sunny, cloudy, rainy, foggy, snowy) and time-of-day (day, sunset, night) per scene, producing 377 weather-conditioned trajectories from 95 base sequences across 22 countries and 6 continents (1.2M+ frames, ~2,400 km cumulative flight). Geometric fidelity is verified via bidirectional depth–pose reprojection (median relative error 0.066%). For evaluation, the authors fine-tune four SoTA foundation models—MegaLoc (contrastive retrieval with weighted InfoNCE), RoMa (dense matching on a MegaDepth+AirZoo mixture), VGGT, and Depth Anything 3 (feed-forward point-map prediction)—on the synthetic split (19 countries) and test zero-shot on held-out real and synthetic benchmarks (Brazil, USA, New Zealand), demonstrating that the synthetic geometric diversity transfers to unseen real-world UAV scenarios.

## Results

Across three aerial 3D vision tasks, fine-tuning on AirZoo yields substantial gains. For aerial image retrieval on AirZoo-Real, MegaLoc's R@1 jumps from 6.46% to 18.66% (+12.21) and R@3 from 16.52% to 40.70% (+24.18). For cross-view matching, AirZoo-finetuned RoMa reduces median translation error from 5.87 m to 3.03 m and boosts acc@5m from 44.95% to 79.78% on AirZoo-Real. For multi-view 3D reconstruction, DA3-Large's F1 score improves by +31.99 on AirZoo-Test (54.10→86.09) and +8.08 on UrbanScene3D (47.83→55.91), while VGGT gains +9.65 F1 on AirZoo-Test (67.67→77.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On aerial image retrieval, AirZoo-finetuned MegaLoc outperforms all four baselines (AnyLoc, SALAD, Game4Loc, and original MegaLoc) on both UAV-VisLoc and AirZoo-Real, with the largest margin on AirZoo-Real where R@1 nearly triples (6.46→18.66) over original MegaLoc and surpasses Game4Loc (12.67) by 6 points. For cross-view matching, AirZoo-finetuned RoMa beats LoFTR, ELoFTR, DUSt3R, and MASt3R by wide margins on both AerialExtreMatch and AirZoo-Real (e.g., acc@5m: 79.78% vs. 12.70% for MASt3R), and slightly edges out RoMa(GIM) (79.78% vs. 77.71% acc@5m on AirZoo-Real). In multi-view 3D reconstruction, AirZoo-finetuned DA3-Large achieves the best F1 on three of four test sets (UAVScenes 91.42, UrbanScene3D 55.91, AirZoo-Test 86.09), surpassing CUT3R, MapAnything, VGGT-SLAM, and original DA3-Large; however, VGGT-SLAM retains the top score on AirZoo-Real (53.95 vs. 53.07), which the authors attribute to its BA-based optimization. AirZoo-finetuned VGGT improves over original VGGT on all four test sets but does not surpass VGGT-SLAM on AirZoo-Real. Overall, the paper demonstrates that AirZoo fine-tuning consistently lifts every base model above its un-finetuned counterpart and above all external baselines, except for the BA-optimized VGGT-SLAM on the single real-flight reconstruction benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining world-scale photogrammetric 3D tiles (Google 3D Tiles via Cesium for Unreal) with a custom AirSim–Unreal Engine 5 simulation pipeline to produce a UAV dataset that simultaneously delivers geographic scale, environmental diversity, and dense geometric supervision—three properties that prior UAV datasets have never jointly achieved. Retrieval-oriented benchmarks (University-1652, SUES-200) offer scale but lack poses and depth; geometry-centric synthetic datasets (MatrixCity, Game4Loc, UAVScenes) provide annotations but are confined to one or two city models with limited viewpoint variety. The paper repurposes the AirSim multi-sensor capture framework and Cesium's georeferenced tile streaming in a novel automated pipeline, positioning AirZoo as a 'pre-training engine' for existing foundation models (MegaLoc, RoMa, VGGT, DA3) rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AirZoo addresses a concrete and widely acknowledged bottleneck: the absence of large-scale, geometry-annotated aerial training data that would allow ground-level 3D vision foundation models to generalize to UAV deployment. The substantial zero-shot gains on the authors' real-flight benchmark (e.g., +12.2 R@1 for MegaLoc, +34.8 acc@5m for RoMa) suggest that synthetic geometric supervision transfers effectively to unseen real regions, though the more modest gains on the public UAV-VisLoc benchmark and the visible photometric gap between synthetic and real renders temper the claim. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Google 3D Tiles coverage, whether the 0–800 m altitude and 10°–90° pitch envelope captures the full operational range of UAVs, and whether the domain gap can be further closed with domain-adaptive fine-tuning or larger-scale rendering.
