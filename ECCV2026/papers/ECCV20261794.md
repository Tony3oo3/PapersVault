## Problem

Collecting large-scale dyadic interactive audio-visual dialogue (DIAD) datasets is expensive, time-consuming, and ethically sensitive, yet such data is essential for training virtual agents and digital humans that model both verbal and non-verbal interpersonal dynamics. Existing face video synthesis methods produce only non-interactive clips, facial reaction methods require a pre-recorded speaker video, and no existing LLM can directly generate diverse, synchronised, mutually responsive paired audio-visual dialogue clips from a single text prompt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261794/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single de-identified textual prompt describing a dyadic interaction scenario (e.g., topic, style, speaker personas) and produces N diverse DIAD clips, each containing a pair of temporally coherent face video clips (with both talking and silent segments at 100 frames per segment), a pair of emotion-aware speech audio waveforms with inserted interactive backchannels (e.g., 'hmm', 'okay'), per-sentence emotion descriptors, and sampled identity images. Each dialogue is constrained to 5–10 conversational turns to stay within the coherence range of the underlying LLM, and the generated clips can be substantially longer than the 12-second limit of competing methods.

## Method

CHAT is a three-module pipeline: (i) a Textual Dialogue Generation module uses Gemini to produce diverse multi-turn dialogues with identity descriptors; (ii) a Dyadic Audio Dialogue Generation module applies an Interactive Audio Refinement (IAR) block that uses a second LLM pass to insert contextually appropriate backchannel words, assign per-sentence emotion representations (discrete category plus continuous prosody attributes), and condition an XTTS-based emotional speech synthesiser via content and emotion Transformer encoders fused by element-wise summation; (iii) an Interactive Facial Behaviour Generation module first generates talking segments with FaceTalk conditioned on emotion-aware speech features, then refines them through an Interactive Facial Behaviour Refinement (IFBR) block comprising SilentDiff (a video diffusion model for silent-segment generation conditioned on adjacent talking segments), a Responsive Facial Behaviour Generation sub-block (a video diffusion transformer that injects the partner's multi-scale facial and audio conditions via learnable cross-attention at progressively finer spatial resolutions across denoising steps), and a Gaussian-weighted Temporal Continuity Refinement for boundary blending. Training is two-stage: individual modules are pre-trained on HDTF and REACT 2024, then the talking face model, SFBG, and RFBG are jointly optimised end-to-end while LLMs and TTS remain frozen, keeping the trainable parameter count small relative to the full pipeline.

## Results

On the 1000-sample evaluation set, CHAT achieves the best FID (17.33), LSE-C (6.89), LSE-D (8.07), FRCorr (50.02×10⁻²), FRDiv (15.34×10⁻²), and CSIM (0.85), while ranking second only on FVD (365.03 vs. Hallo3's 362.23) and LPIPS (0.38 vs. EDTalk's 0.36). In the 60-participant user study, CHAT scores highest on all five subjective dimensions (Visual 4.6, Audio 4.8, Sync 4.6, Expressiveness 4.6, Interactivity 4.8), with the largest margin over competitors on interactivity (4.8 vs. next-best 3.6 for DIM). For downstream IHG pre-training on REACT 2024, CHAT-AVD-50k improves PerFRDiff's FRCorr from 37.21 to 40.11 and ReactDiff's from 24.19 to 26.12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two audio-driven talking-face baselines (Hallo3 and SadTalker), CHAT wins on FID, LSE-C, LSE-D, CSIM, and LPIPS, while Hallo3 edges out CHAT on FVD by a narrow margin (362.23 vs. 365.03); neither baseline reports reaction metrics since they generate non-interactive single-face videos. Against the two facial-reaction-generation baselines (DIM and ReactDiff), CHAT dominates on every comparable metric: FRCorr improves from 31.02 (DIM) and 48.05 (ReactDiff) to 50.02, and FID drops from 36.52/21.36 to 17.33, reflecting that CHAT jointly generates both speech and responsive facial behaviour rather than only the listener's reaction. Against EDTalk (emotional talking head), CHAT wins on FID, FVD, LSE-C, LSE-D, and CSIM, while EDTalk retains a slight LPIPS advantage (0.36 vs. 0.38), likely due to its dedicated expression-disentanglement design. Overall, CHAT achieves the best score on 6 of 8 objective metrics and the highest user-study rating on all 5 subjective dimensions, with the most pronounced gains in interactivity and expressiveness where the single-face and reaction-only baselines are structurally unable to compete.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHAT unifies three previously separate research threads—LLM-based dialogue generation, audio-driven talking face synthesis (building on FaceTalk and Hallo3's video diffusion transformer architecture), and facial reaction generation (in the spirit of DIM and ReactDiff)—into a single end-to-end pipeline that takes only a textual scenario prompt. The key architectural departure is the introduction of the Interactive Audio Refinement (IAR) and Interactive Facial Behaviour Refinement (IFBR) blocks, which transform the non-interactive outputs of pre-trained TTS and talking-face models into mutually responsive dyadic clips via multi-scale cross-attention conditioning and Gaussian-weighted temporal blending. Unlike prior interactive talking-face pair generators (DialogueNeRF, DualTalk, INFP, AV-Flow) that require pre-defined scripts or audio, CHAT generates the verbal content itself, and unlike FRG methods that produce a single listener reaction to a given speaker video, CHAT synthesises both speakers' audio and video simultaneously with turn-level emotional and interactive-word conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical impact is the CHAT-AVD-50k dataset (50,000 dyadic pairs, ~1,389 hours, 100,000 synthetic identities), which offers a scalable, ethically unproblematic alternative to the costly and privacy-sensitive collection of real dyadic interaction recordings, and has been shown to improve downstream facial reaction models (PerFRDiff, ReactDiff) on REACT 2024. However, several open questions remain: the per-clip inference cost (LLM + TTS + multiple diffusion passes) is high, the system is currently English-only, and the evaluation metrics (FRCorr, FRDiv) capture facial reaction appropriateness but not dialogue-level turn-taking timing or semantic coherence. Extending to multilingual settings, independent corpora (IEMOCAP, NoXi), and developing dialogue-level interaction quality metrics are natural next steps.
