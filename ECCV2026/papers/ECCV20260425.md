## Problem

Semi-supervised LiDAR semantic segmentation suffers from confirmation bias: noisy pseudo-labels generated by a teacher model are blindly reused to train the student, causing progressive error accumulation. Existing mitigations such as confidence filtering and loss reweighting are post-hoc, adjusting how pseudo-labels are consumed rather than improving their quality at the point of generation. REPL addresses this gap by actively identifying and correcting erroneous pseudo-labels before they are used as supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point clouds voxelized into regular grids of shape C×H×W×L, split into a small labeled set and a large unlabeled set. The pseudo-label refiner takes the channel-wise concatenation of the voxelized input and a masked prediction tensor (where unreliable voxels are replaced by a learnable K-dimensional mask token) and outputs refined per-voxel class probabilities. The final refined pseudo-label is assembled voxel-wise: reliable voxels retain the teacher's argmax prediction while unreliable voxels adopt the refiner's reconstructed prediction.

## Method

REPL couples a mean-teacher segmentation network (Cylinder3D backbone, EMA-updated) with a pseudo-label refiner that operates in two stages: (1) unreliable voxels are flagged when teacher and student disagree on class or either falls below an adaptive confidence percentile threshold, and (2) flagged voxels are replaced with a learnable mask token and reconstructed by the refiner in a masked-autoencoder-style process. The refiner is trained with three complementary objectives: supervised reconstruction on labeled data (masked regions only), negative learning on unlabeled data that suppresses implausible classes via the teacher's top-k predictions, and reconstruction on LaserMix-fused labeled/unlabeled scenes to expose the refiner to diverse error patterns. The student is then trained with symmetric cross-entropy and Lovász-Softmax on the refined pseudo-labels, plus a LaserMix objective combining ground-truth and pseudo-label regions. A theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–2) proves that refinement is information-theoretically easier than generating labels from scratch and that net improvement holds whenever the mask precision π exceeds the ratio of error-introduction to error-correction rates, a condition empirically verified to be mild.

## Results

On nuScenes-lidarseg, REPL achieves an average mIoU of 71.3 across 1%–50% labeled ratios, outperforming the second-best method IT2 (69.3) by +2.0 mIoU and the supervised-only baseline (63.7) by +7.6 mIoU. On SemanticKITTI, REPL reaches 61.6 average mIoU, edging out AIScene and FrustrumMix (both 61.5) and surpassing the supervised-only baseline (54.5) by +7.1 mIoU. On ScribbleKITTI, REPL attains 56.1 average mIoU, the best among all compared methods. The gains are consistent across all label ratios, with the largest improvement at 1% labeled data (+9.1 mIoU over supervised-only on nuScenes-lidarseg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPL outperforms all 12 compared baselines on nuScenes-lidarseg, achieving 71.3 average mIoU versus IT2 (69.3, +2.0), FrustrumMix (69.2, +2.1), LaserMix++ (69.1, +2.2), and AIScene (68.4, +2.9). On SemanticKITTI, REPL's 61.6 average mIoU narrowly beats AIScene and FrustrumMix (both 61.5) and Lim3D (61.2), while the gap over earlier methods like IT2 (59.5) and LaserMix (58.7) is more substantial (+2.1 and +2.9 respectively). On ScribbleKITTI, REPL leads with 56.1 average mIoU, surpassing LaserMix++ (55.4) and IT2/FrustrumMix (both 55.1). Notably, REPL uses fixed hyperparameters across both benchmarks, whereas strong competitors like AIScene and FrustrumMix tune key hyperparameters per dataset. The supervised-only baseline (63.7 on nuScenes, 54.5 on SemanticKITTI) is exceeded by 7.6 and 7.1 mIoU respectively, confirming the value of the pseudo-label refinement over simply training on labeled data alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

REPL builds on the mean-teacher framework (Zou et al., ECCV 2018) for the EMA-updated teacher-student architecture, adapts the masked autoencoder mechanism (He et al., CVPR 2022) to reconstruct uncertain pseudo-labels via learnable mask tokens, and incorporates LaserMix (Kong et al., CVPR 2023) for scene mixing and NLNL (Kim et al., CVPR 2019) for negative learning on unlabeled data. The key conceptual departure from prior semi-supervised LiDAR segmentation work (IT2, AIScene, FrustrumMix, etc.) is shifting from post-hoc strategies—confidence filtering or loss reweighting that adjust how pseudo-labels are consumed—to directly correcting pseudo-labels at the point of generation through a two-stage identify-and-reconstruct pipeline. The paper further distinguishes itself by providing a theoretical condition (Proposition 2) characterizing when refinement yields net benefit, which prior methods in this space have not formally established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REPL addresses the chronic confirmation-bias problem in semi-supervised learning by improving pseudo-label quality rather than merely discarding unreliable samples, and the theoretical condition for benefit (Eq. 11) is shown to be mild and broadly satisfied, lending confidence that the approach generalizes. A noted limitation is that the consensus-based error estimation can miss errors where teacher and student are jointly overconfident; the authors identify replacing the heuristic mask with a learned error-localization network (e.g., ELN) as a promising direction, and the gap between the heuristic (60.0 mIoU) and oracle (67.3 mIoU) masks quantifies the remaining headroom. The framework is architecture-agnostic (validated on both Cylinder3D and SphereFormer) and adds only moderate inference overhead (+0.25 s, +396 MB), making it a practical drop-in refinement for existing semi-supervised LiDAR pipelines.
