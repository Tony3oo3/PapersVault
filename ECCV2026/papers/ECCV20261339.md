## Problem

Existing noisy-label learning methods rely on static metrics such as loss or confidence to separate clean from noisy samples, which fails under high noise because deep networks memorize corrupted labels and assign them high confidence. The paper identifies that the temporal evolution of visual attention (Class Activation Maps) during training contains a clear signal—clean samples stabilize quickly while noisy samples drift erratically—that static snapshots cannot capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for Clothing-1M and Food-101N) paired with potentially corrupted class labels, and outputs a single class prediction via the clean classification head at inference. During training, the pipeline additionally generates multiple derived views per sample—label-guided masked, prediction-guided masked, and background-substituted images—along with weak and strong augmented views, all fed through a shared backbone with dual classification heads and a Siamese-style projection/prediction pair for consistency regularization.

## Method

The framework comprises three synergistic modules. First, the Attention Temporal Consistency (ATC) module tracks CAM core-area size and centroid displacement across a sliding window of epochs, computing a temporal consistency score that penalizes erratic attention; this dynamic penalty is fused with static margin and loss metrics, and a two-component GMM partitions samples into clean and noisy subsets. Second, the Adaptive Attention Masks (AAM) module applies differential masking: for clean samples it erases low-activation background regions to force foreground focus, while for noisy samples it erases high-activation regions associated with the incorrect label, and further performs cross-sample background substitution to decouple objects from context. Third, the Mask-guided Feature Alignment (MFA) module enforces consistency between a weakly augmented anchor view and two distorted views (strongly augmented and prediction-guided masked) via negative cosine similarity in a Siamese projection-prediction architecture, while both classification heads use an evidential Dirichlet loss that naturally suppresses over-confident predictions on ambiguous or masked inputs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under 90% symmetric noise, the proposed method achieves 95.1% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best baseline DULC (93.5%) by 1.6% and DivideMix (76.0%) by 19.1%. On the more challenging CIFAR-100 at the same noise level, it reaches 55.6%, a 2.8% gain over DULC (52.8%). On real-world datasets, the method attains 75.24% on Clothing-1M (vs. DULC 75.09%), 89.4% on Food-101N (vs. SURE 88.0%), and 90.8% on Animal-10N (vs. CCLRL 89.7%), consistently outperforming all 22 compared baselines across every noise setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 22 baselines across every dataset and noise configuration. Under the most extreme 90% symmetric noise, it leads DULC by 1.6% on CIFAR-10 (95.1% vs. 93.5%) and by 2.8% on CIFAR-100 (55.6% vs. 52.8%), while methods like DivideMix (76.0%/31.5%) and DISC (57.6%/24.2%) degrade severely. On CIFAR-10 at lower noise (20–50%), the gap over the strongest baselines (CCLRL, SANM) narrows to 0.5–1.0%, but the method still maintains the top position. On real-world benchmarks, gains are more modest but consistent: +0.15% over DULC on Clothing-1M, +1.4% over SURE on Food-101N, and +1.1% over CCLRL on Animal-10N. The method also leads on instance-dependent noise (IDN) settings, achieving 97.1% on CIFAR-10 and 81.0% on CIFAR-100, surpassing DISC (95.9%/78.4%) which is the only other baseline reporting IDN results on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DivideMix-style divide-then-mix semi-supervised framework by replacing the static loss-based GMM partitioning with a temporal attention stability criterion (ATC) that tracks Class Activation Map evolution across training epochs. It further departs from standard data augmentation (MixMatch-style random transforms) by introducing attention-guided differential masking (AAM) that treats clean and noisy samples asymmetrically, and from conventional consistency regularization by aligning original views with semantically masked counterparts (MFA). The evidential deep learning loss for both classification heads is borrowed from the Dirichlet-based uncertainty literature to maintain separable logit distributions that support the ATC module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The temporal CAM stability signal offers a principled way to identify hard noisy samples that static loss metrics miss, and the 90%-noise results (95.1% CIFAR-10, 55.6% CIFAR-100) demonstrate that this dynamic signal is particularly valuable when clean samples are extremely scarce. However, the approach incurs non-trivial computational overhead from per-epoch CAM extraction and centroid tracking, and its effectiveness depends on the initial quality of the backbone's attention maps. Open questions include whether a lightweight surrogate for full CAM tracking can preserve the signal, and whether the temporal stability principle generalizes to open-set or instance-dependent noise beyond the symmetric/asymmetric settings tested here.
