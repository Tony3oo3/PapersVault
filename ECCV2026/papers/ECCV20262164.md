## Problem

Most unsupervised outlier detection methods rely on frozen, natural-image-pretrained feature extractors (e.g., ResNet-50, CLIP), causing their performance to degrade sharply in practical domains such as industrial inspection and medical imaging. The core gap is that existing approaches do not enhance the discriminativeness of feature representations across domains, and conventional threshold-learning for pseudo-labels is decoupled from the underlying scoring function, leading to sub-optimal supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of unlabeled RGB images (no labels, no train/test split required) and extracts features via a pretrained backbone (ResNet-50, CLIP, ResNet-18, or Wide ResNet-101 depending on the domain). It outputs per-sample outlier scores computed by OCSVM on contrastively-enhanced embeddings, as well as the enhanced feature vectors themselves. The framework operates at the whole-image level and is designed as a plug-and-play pre-processor: the same outlier scores can be thresholded to filter samples from downstream pipelines such as image classification training sets or VGGT 3D reconstruction input sequences.

## Method

VUOD generates pseudo-labels by exploiting two intrinsic distribution priors in the feature space. First, Affinity Propagation performs fine-grained clustering without a predefined cluster count, naturally isolating sparse outliers as singleton or small clusters (local separability). Second, a global separability prior uses the Spearman rank correlation between each cluster's centroid-distance ranking and the global mean-distance ranking to flag outlier clusters (those with correlation below ε⁺=0.9 are removed to refine the inlier mean, addressing the mean-shift problem). The remaining clusters are split into inlier (φ>0.9) and outlier (φ<0.5) pseudo-labels, which drive a contrastive learning stage: a two-layer MLP is trained with a margin-based contrastive loss on dynamically sampled positive (inlier-inlier) and negative (inlier-outlier) pairs to enlarge inter-class and shrink intra-class distances in the embedding space. At inference, all features pass through the trained MLP and are scored by OCSVM, which leverages the kernel trick for nonlinear decision boundaries in the enhanced space.

## Results

On six natural image UOD benchmarks, VUOD achieves SOTA AUC across all datasets: 0.994 on STL-10, 0.991 on Internet, 0.994 on Caltech-101, 0.972 on CIFAR-10, 0.981 on CIFAR-100, and 0.971 on MIT-Places, surpassing the previous best (FlexUOD) by 1.4–5.3 percentage points. On industrial and medical datasets, VUOD reaches 0.931 AUC on MVTec-AD and 0.926 on MPDD (Wide ResNet-101), exceeding the best UOD baseline by 15+ points. On MVTec-AD per-category, VUOD (0.931 average) outperforms specialized defect detectors EfficientAD (0.917), FRE (0.913), and PaDiM (0.902). As a plug-in, VUOD improves Imagenette classification from 90.81% to 91.45% Top-1 accuracy and accelerates 3D reconstruction outlier filtering by 4.4× over RobustVGGT (27 ms vs. 118 ms per image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VUOD achieves the highest AUC on all six natural image benchmarks, beating the strongest prior UOD method FlexUOD by 1.4 pp (STL-10: 0.994 vs 0.980), 3.0 pp (CIFAR-10: 0.972 vs 0.942), 3.4 pp (CIFAR-100: 0.981 vs 0.947), and 4.3 pp (MIT-Places: 0.971 vs 0.928). On industrial and medical datasets, VUOD dominates all four UOD baselines (RSRAE, LVAD, Multi-T, FlexUOD) by large margins—e.g., 0.926 vs 0.603 on MPDD and 0.838 vs 0.708 on LiverCT—demonstrating that its pseudo-labeling + contrastive-learning pipeline overcomes the domain-shift limitation of frozen natural-image backbones. Against specialized industrial defect detectors on MVTec-AD, VUOD (Wide ResNet-101, 0.931 average AUC) surpasses EfficientAD (0.917), FRE (0.913), and PaDiM (0.902), while also being 2.5× faster and using 61% less GPU memory than EfficientAD. In the 3D reconstruction setting, VUOD matches RobustVGGT in outlier identification accuracy but runs 4.4× faster (27 ms vs 118 ms per image) and is backbone-agnostic. For image classification on Imagenette, integrating VUOD as a data pre-filter improves ResNet-18 Top-1 accuracy from 90.81% to 91.45% (+0.64 pp) and Top-5 from 99.11% to 99.39%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised outlier detection (UOD) line of work that has predominantly relied on frozen natural-image pretrained feature extractors (ResNet-50, CLIP) combined with threshold-learning or density-based scoring. Its key departure is replacing the conventional threshold-learner paradigm—which learns a decision boundary independently of the outlier scoring method—with intrinsic distribution priors (local separability via Affinity Propagation and global separability via Spearman rank correlation) to generate pseudo labels, followed by contrastive learning to enhance feature discriminativeness. The framework also repurposes OCSVM as a scoring head on the contrastively-enhanced embeddings, building on the high-dimensional hypersphere volume argument from Shell-Re and LVAD. By evaluating across natural, industrial, and medical domains and integrating into downstream tasks (classification, 3D reconstruction), the paper positions UOD as a general-purpose data-quality preprocessor rather than a standalone detection task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in bridging the gap between UOD research (dominated by natural-image benchmarks) and deployment-critical domains like industrial inspection and medical imaging, where existing methods degrade substantially. The plug-and-play integration into image classification and VGGT-based 3D reconstruction demonstrates that outlier filtering can be a lightweight, backbone-agnostic module that improves robustness without task-specific retraining. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the whole-image-level operation, which precludes patch- or pixel-level anomaly segmentation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to very high contamination ratios (>50%), whether the Affinity Propagation step scales to million-sample datasets, and whether the contrastive enhancement transfers to video or multi-modal outlier detection.
