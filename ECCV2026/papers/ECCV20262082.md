## Problem

Existing MLLM-based text image machine translation (TIMT) methods process high-resolution text-rich images in a single forward pass, causing visual attention to be overloaded by dense non-textual distractions and excessive visual tokens. This leads to local text omission, misrecognition, semantic drift, and hallucinations in cluttered real-world scenes such as posters, documents, and menus. The paper addresses the lack of a mechanism that simultaneously preserves fine-grained textual fidelity and maintains scene-level contextual consistency within a unified MLLM framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-resolution text-rich RGB image (e.g., posters, documents, menus, road signs) and produces target-language translated text. Internally, the input is decomposed into a low-resolution global view (default 224×224) capturing overall layout and semantics, plus multiple local text-region slices extracted by a text detector and arranged in reading order. Translation is generated region-by-region in a regressive manner, where each local slice's output is conditioned on the global image, the current slice, and the preceding η translated regions via a replay window.

## Method

GLoTran introduces a global-local dual visual perception strategy: a text region detector extracts and groups local slices, while the original image is downsampled to a low-resolution global view; both are encoded by a shared ViT and projected into the LLM's text embedding space. Hierarchical cross-attention is inserted at early Transformer layers (0, 8, 16, 24) between global and local tokens, with a learnable spatial-proximity bias that lets each local token selectively attend to relevant global context. A structured four-part prompt (global understanding, local focus, global-local consistency rule, and translation instruction) guides the MLLM, and a pre-region translation replay mechanism of size η feeds previously translated regions into the prompt to enforce terminology consistency and discourse coherence. The model is trained with standard cross-entropy loss under teacher forcing within the replay window, and is instantiated on Qwen3-VL 8B or InternVL2.5 4B backbones via full-parameter fine-tuning or LoRA on the 510K-pair GLoD dataset.

## Results

On the MCiTon benchmark (English-to-Chinese), GLoTran (Qwen3-VL 8B) achieves the best BLEU/COMET across all eight scenarios, with headline scores of 66.3/87.9 (Paper), 57.5/88.6 (News), and 51.1/77.9 (Leaflet). GLoTran outperforms its Qwen3-VL 8B backbone by 7.49 BLEU and 2.49 COMET on average, and surpasses the much larger GPT-4o (~200B) by a wide margin in most scenarios. On the multilingual MTIT6 benchmark covering five language pairs (jp→zh, ko→zh, zh→en, zh→jp, zh→ko), GLoTran consistently achieves the best BLEU and COMET among all evaluated MLLMs. In the complexity analysis, GLoTran at 224×224 resolution uses only 4.9K visual tokens and ~18 GB memory while matching or exceeding the accuracy of full-resolution (164K tokens) baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLoTran (Qwen3-VL 8B) outperforms all 13 baselines across every scenario on MCiTon. Against its own backbone Qwen3-VL-Instruct 8B, GLoTran gains +5.4 BLEU on Paper (66.3 vs. 60.9), +3.2 on News (57.5 vs. 54.3), +3.2 on Novel (46.8 vs. 43.6), and +2.9 on Leaflet (51.1 vs. 48.2). It also surpasses the much larger GPT-4o (~200B) by +4.2 BLEU on Paper and +7.2 on Leaflet, and beats Qwen-VL-Max (~72B) by +5.8 BLEU on Paper. Among open-source 8B models, GLoTran leads InternVL3 8B by +6.5 BLEU on Paper and +15.3 on Leaflet, and SAILVL2 8B by +5.0 on Paper. On the multilingual MTIT6 benchmark, GLoTran achieves the best BLEU in all five language pairs, with particularly large margins on low-resource pairs (e.g., ko→zh and jp→zh) where baselines struggle most. The paper also notes that simply fine-tuning Qwen3-VL or InternVL3 on GLoD without the global-local architecture (denoted Qwen3-VL* and InternVL3*) yields only marginal gains, confirming that the architectural contribution—not just data—drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MLLM-based TIMT methods (InImageTrans, MT3, M4Doc) by specifically targeting high-resolution text-rich images, where prior approaches degrade due to visual token overload and non-textual distractions. It departs from existing global-local strategies in VQA (ZoomEye, V*, hierarchical feature aggregation) that use random partitioning or search-based cropping, instead adopting a text-region-centered extraction paradigm with deterministic reading-order arrangement. The hierarchical cross-attention mechanism draws on FlashBias-style adaptive biases, and the replay mechanism borrows from discourse-level context in NMT, but the combination into a structured global-local prompting pipeline for TIMT is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GLoD dataset (510K global-local pairs across 40+ scenes and 5 languages) fills a gap in training resources for high-resolution TIMT, and the framework offers a practical recipe for adapting existing MLLMs without architectural overhaul. The observed scaling saturation—where 40B InternVL2 barely outperforms its 8B counterpart and 32B Qwen2.5-VL underperforms its 7B variant—challenges the assumption that parameter scaling alone resolves TIMT's fine-grained perception challenges, suggesting targeted perception strategies matter more. Open questions include generalization beyond the evaluated language pairs, handling of extremely dense or overlapping text, and whether the text-region detector dependency limits deployment flexibility.
