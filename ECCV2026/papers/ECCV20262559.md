## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning datasets for MLLMs are predominantly single-view or treat multi-view tasks as a flat, loosely defined capability without explicit dependency structure between perception, scene understanding, and reasoning. This prevents models from developing coherent 3D spatial cognition, as they lack a progressive learning pathway from foundational geometric awareness to complex cross-view contextual inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262559/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a set of multi-view RGB images (typically two or more camera perspectives of the same 3D scene) paired with a natural-language spatial question. Level I and II tasks produce short-answer responses (e.g., object positions, camera translations), while Level III tasks are multiple-choice questions requiring integration across all views. For Level III, the model additionally generates a structured chain-of-thought that explicitly follows three stages—local perception, cross-view 3D modeling, and high-level contextual inference—grounded in verifiable intermediate sub-answers from the same scene.

## Method

MV-STRIDE organizes spatial reasoning into a three-level hierarchy (single-view perception → cross-view scene understanding → multi-view contextual reasoning) with explicit capability dependencies, and enforces a cross-view dependency constraint so that no Level III question is solvable from any single view. The data pipeline uses a top-down strategy: complex Level III tasks are generated first from 3D scene metadata (Infinigen procedural scenes and ScanNet++ reconstructions), then decomposed into prerequisite Level I/II sub-questions whose ordered answers form cognitively grounded CoT annotations synthesized by an LLM. Training follows a three-stage progressive strategy: (1) large-scale SFT on 313.8K QA pairs across all levels, (2) CoT cold-start on 19K annotated Level III samples to internalize structured reasoning templates, and (3) GRPO reinforcement learning on 24.8K moderate-difficulty samples to encourage the model to autonomously verify spatial logic rather than imitate SFT patterns. The base model is Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, and the approach achieves SOTA among open-source models on MMSI-Bench (38.9% avg.) while remaining competitive with proprietary models like GPT-5.

## Results

MV-STRIDE-SFT(Full) achieves 38.90% average accuracy on MMSI-Bench, a 9.7-point improvement over the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct base model (29.20%) and the best result among all open-source MLLMs. On ViewSpatial-Bench, MV-STRIDE-SFT(Stage 1) reaches 50.28%, surpassing all proprietary and open-source baselines (next best: GPT-4o at 34.98%). On CV-Bench, MV-STRIDE-SFT(Full) attains 86.73%, the highest among all compared models, while on 3DSR-Bench it achieves 65.52%, competitive with GPT-5 (66.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MMSI-Bench (the primary multi-view benchmark), MV-STRIDE-SFT(Full) at 38.90% outperforms all open-source baselines by a wide margin (next best open-source: Qwen2-VL-72B at 31.50%) but remains 3.5 points behind GPT-5 (42.40%). On ViewSpatial-Bench, MV-STRIDE-SFT(Stage 1) achieves 50.28%, a decisive 15.3-point lead over GPT-4o (34.98%) and all other models, establishing a clear SOTA. On CV-Bench (single-view), MV-STRIDE-SFT(Full) edges out VST-7B-RL (86.50%) by 0.23 points to reach 86.73%, while on 3DSR-Bench it trails GPT-5 by 1.2 points (65.52% vs. 66.70%) but surpasses Gemini 2.5-Pro (64.30%) and all open-source models. The RL-refined variant (Stage 3) shows slightly lower raw accuracy than SFT(Full) on MMSI-Bench but produces more interpretable chain-of-thought reasoning, suggesting a trade-off between pattern-matching accuracy and genuine spatial logic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of spatial reasoning datasets that treat multi-view tasks as flat, independently designed problems. Prior multi-view efforts (MultiSPA, ACTUAL, point correspondence datasets) focus on isolated low-level perception skills like depth estimation or viewpoint recognition, while single-view datasets (SPAR-7M, SAT, VST) lack structured 3D cognitive pathways. MV-STRIDE carves new ground by explicitly modeling dependency relationships across three cognitive levels (perception → scene understanding → contextual reasoning) and enforcing cross-view unsolvability constraints, a structural design principle absent from existing multi-view spatial reasoning data. The training pipeline also extends the SFT-only paradigm by integrating CoT cold-start and GRPO-based reinforcement learning, building on the broader trend of applying RL to MLLM reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical, capability-dependent dataset design offers a principled alternative to the 'flat' multi-view task collections that have dominated the field, potentially enabling MLLMs to develop genuinely 3D-consistent spatial cognition rather than pattern-matching on 2D cues. The observation of a 'reasoning tax'—where structured CoT supervision temporarily reduces raw accuracy before RL recovers and surpasses it—highlights a non-trivial trade-off in multi-stage training that practitioners should account for. Open questions include whether the indoor-scene-centric construction (Infinigen + ScanNet++) generalizes to outdoor or embodied settings, whether the hierarchical decomposition transfers to video-based multi-view reasoning, and whether the approach scales effectively to larger model families beyond the 8B base tested here.
