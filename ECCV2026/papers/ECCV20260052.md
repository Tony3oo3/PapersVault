## Problem

World-model-based VLA architectures face a fundamental tension: dense future rollouts accumulate prediction errors and introduce visual redundancy that causes long-horizon plan drift, while existing sparse alternatives (semantic subtasks or implicit latent states) lack explicit kinematic grounding, weakening the alignment between planning and low-level execution. No prior method provides sparse visual subgoals that are simultaneously physically meaningful and directly actionable for closed-loop control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260052/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a language instruction and a sliding window of RGB observations (256×256 for SimplerEnv, 200×200 third-person plus 200×200 wrist-view for LIBERO, 256×144 front plus 240×135 wrist for real-world), all discretized into a shared VQ token vocabulary. Stage 1 outputs a single structured visual subgoal frame as a sequence of discrete tokens; Stage 2 outputs a sequence of low-level action tokens (FAST tokenizer, vocabulary size 1024) mapped into the same discrete space, so vision, language, and action are all generated autoregressively from one unified token set.

## Method

StructVLA reformulates an 8.5B-parameter autoregressive Transformer (initialized from UniVLA) into a two-stage structured planner. An offline curation pipeline extracts sparse structured frames from raw trajectories using two kinematic cues—gripper state transitions (with a stabilization window and adaptive speed threshold) and kinematic turning points (sliding-window low-speed detection)—plus temporal gap-filling, then refines candidates with a VLM for redundancy removal. Stage 1 trains the world model via cross-entropy to predict the next structured frame conditioned on the instruction and visual context, learning kinematically grounded visual foresight; Stage 2 shifts the prediction target to action tokens within the same unified vocabulary, transferring the structured-planning capability into low-level control. LoRA (rank 32) is used for parameter-efficient adaptation, and a sliding-window augmentation densifies the sparse structured-frame supervision during Stage 1 training.

## Results

StructVLA achieves a 75.0% average success rate on SimplerEnv-WidowX, outperforming the strongest prior baseline UniVLA (69.8%) by 5.2 percentage points. On the LIBERO benchmark, StructVLA reaches 94.8% average success across four suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), surpassing UniVLA (93.7%) and DreamVLA (92.6%). In real-world deployment, StructVLA attains 87.5% average success on pick-and-place tasks (20 points above UniVLA) and completes 8/10 trials on a long-horizon tidy-up task versus 4/10 for UniVLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SimplerEnv-WidowX, StructVLA (75.0%) decisively outperforms all nine baselines, with the largest gaps over RT-1-X (1.1%), OpenVLA (1.0%), and Octo-Base (16.0%), and a 5.2-point margin over the closest competitor UniVLA (69.8%). On LIBERO, StructVLA (94.8%) leads all nine baselines, with particularly notable gains on the long-horizon LIBERO-Long suite (92.2% vs. 89.6% for UniVLA and 89.5% for DreamVLA), while matching UniVLA on LIBERO-Object (98.0%) and slightly trailing on LIBERO-Spatial (95.4% vs. 96.4%). In real-world pick-and-place, StructVLA achieves 87.5% average success, 20 points above UniVLA (67.5%) and substantially above SpatialVLA; on the long-horizon tidy-up task, StructVLA completes 8/10 trials versus 4/10 for UniVLA and 2/10 for SpatialVLA. For zero-shot generalization to unseen objects, StructVLA reaches 80.0% success compared to 30.0% for UniVLA, and under human interventions it retains 80.0% versus 40.0% for UniVLA. StructVLA also converges faster, reaching 66.7% accuracy in 10K steps versus 64.8% for UniVLA at 12K steps under identical batch-size settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

StructVLA builds directly on UniVLA's 8.5B autoregressive Transformer world model, repurposing it from a dense video-prediction engine into a sparse structured planner. It departs from two dominant paradigms in world-model-based VLA: dense future rollouts (VideoVLA, Cosmos Policy, Large Video Planner) that accumulate error over long horizons, and semantic or latent subgoal planning (BagelVLA, ForeAct, LatentPilot) that lacks explicit geometric grounding. The key conceptual move is deriving subgoals from robot-centric kinematic cues (gripper transitions, turning points) rather than from language-defined subtask boundaries or learned latent codes, yielding physically verifiable progress anchors within a unified discrete token vocabulary shared across vision, language, and action.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode—long-horizon plan drift from dense prediction—by showing that sparse, kinematically grounded frames provide more stable visual subgoals for closed-loop control. The automated extraction pipeline (kinematic thresholds + VLM-based offline filtering) avoids costly manual subtask annotation, making the approach practical for new tasks. However, the experimental scale remains limited (single WidowX and Franka embodiments, 10-trial real-world evaluations), and the authors acknowledge that broader multi-domain validation and online reinforcement learning are needed to confirm robustness. An open question is whether the kinematic-cue extraction generalizes to non-arm embodiments (e.g., mobile manipulators, dexterous hands) where the relevant 'turning points' are less obvious.
