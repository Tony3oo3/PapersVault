## Problem

Existing 3D human animation systems depend on Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) and parametric body models (e.g., SMPL), which constrain expressiveness, introduce artifacts from imperfect body fitting, and amplify monocular pose estimation errors into temporal jitter. No prior method supports direct, end-to-end 3D animation from heterogeneous 2D driving signals (RGB, keypoints, sketches, out-of-domain characters) without explicit 3D pose estimation or preprocessing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261778/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N (typically 4) unposed multi-view identity images and a single 2D driving signal of arbitrary modality (RGB frame, 2D keypoint map, hand-drawn sketch, or out-of-domain character image) with no preprocessing such as body fitting or foreground segmentation. It outputs an animatable set of 8192 3D Gaussians parameterized by center, rotation quaternion, scale, opacity, and color, which can be rendered from arbitrary novel viewpoints. At inference, the model generalizes to cross-identity driving: the driving signal need not depict the same person as the identity images.

## Method

LUNA comprises an Identity Encoder that lifts multi-view images into canonical 3D Gaussians via Sapiens image tokens fused with template-anchored semantic query tokens through MM-Transformer blocks, and an Implicit Neural Animator that predicts disentangled deformations conditioned on DINOv3 motion tokens extracted from the 2D driving signal. The animator separates a global rigid transformation (rotation in continuous trigonometric form, translation as a normalized offset) from per-Gaussian local residuals (position offset, rotation residual, color residual) decoded by a second MM-Transformer, with scale and opacity held fixed. Training employs a hybrid supervision strategy: photometric rendering loss on all samples, LBS-based distillation regularization (soft structural targets from a teacher) on labeled data, and a 2D reprojection loss for translation, with a loss-reweighting scheme that upweights distillation inversely to the labeled fraction to balance gradients when mixing labeled and unlabeled in-the-wild video. This LBS-free formulation eliminates the parametric body bottleneck while the distillation prior prevents the depth collapse that would otherwise occur without explicit shape constraints.

## Results

On the Cloth10K dataset (non-rigid garments), LUNA achieves 22.07 PSNR / 0.027 L1 / 0.131 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest feed-forward baseline MV-LHM (20.12 PSNR) by ~2 dB and all optimization-based methods. On NeuMan (standard walking, tighter clothing), LUNA reaches 26.82 PSNR, matching MV-LHM (26.83) and trailing only the per-subject optimization method ExAvatar (31.27). For temporal motion quality, LUNA achieves a 4.5× reduction in Mean Squared Jerk (0.0032 vs. 0.0144 for MV-LHM) and a 2.1× reduction in Mean Acceleration Error (0.0225 vs. 0.0477), indicating substantially smoother animations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Cloth10K (loose garments, non-rigid dynamics), LUNA decisively outperforms all five baselines: it gains ~2 dB PSNR over the best feed-forward method MV-LHM (22.07 vs. 20.12) and ~2.5 dB over optimization-based ExAvatar (22.07 vs. 19.53), with the largest margin over IDOL (17.43). On NeuMan (standard walking, tighter clothing), LUNA is essentially tied with MV-LHM (26.82 vs. 26.83 PSNR) and slightly trails the per-subject optimization method ExAvatar (31.27 PSNR), confirming that the LBS-free design does not sacrifice fidelity on simpler, highly constrained scenarios. For temporal motion quality, LUNA achieves a 4.5× lower MSJ (0.0032) and 2.1× lower MAE (0.0225) than the strongest baseline MV-LHM (0.0144 / 0.0477), a gap the paper attributes to eliminating pose-estimation noise and rigid skinning constraints. In the cross-identity driving setting on NeuMan, LUNA (26.80 PSNR) surpasses LHM++ (25.74 PSNR) while remaining within 0.02 dB of its own self-reenactment score (26.82), demonstrating that the animator extracts motion rather than appearance from the driving signal. LUNA also achieves the lowest normalized 2D keypoint error (17.3%) among all compared feed-forward methods, further validating motion accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUNA builds directly on the feed-forward 3D Gaussian avatar reconstruction line (LHM, IDOL, ExAvatar) that predicts canonical Gaussians from sparse views, but fundamentally departs from their reliance on Linear Blend Skinning and parametric body models (SMPL/MHR) for animation. Where prior methods use LBS as the animation mechanism and monocular pose estimation as the driving signal, LUNA replaces both with a transformer-based neural animator that extracts motion directly from heterogeneous 2D inputs (RGB, keypoints, sketches) via DINOv3 features. The hybrid supervision strategy is a novel training-time bridge: LBS is retained not as a driver but as a soft structural regularizer, a conceptual repurposing that distinguishes LUNA from both pure LBS pipelines and prior attempts at template-free animation (TAVA, GART) that still required per-identity fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LUNA establishes the first end-to-end 3D animatable framework that accepts arbitrary 2D driving signals without preprocessing, which has direct practical implications for content creation pipelines where clean 3D pose annotations are unavailable. The emergent cross-identity generalization (driving one avatar with another's motion via sketches or out-of-domain images) opens a path toward universal character animation without per-pair tuning. Key open questions remain: severe occlusions in driving videos degrade temporal stability, extreme pose or body-shape mismatches still cause failures because motion and shape are not explicitly disentangled, and the lack of explicit temporal sequence modeling limits long-horizon coherence. Scaling to more diverse motions, apparel, and viewpoints is identified as the natural next step.
