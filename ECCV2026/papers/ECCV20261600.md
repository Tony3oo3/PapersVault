## Problem

Vision foundation models like SAM2 excel at shape adaptability and distinguishing similar objects but degrade under heavy occlusion, while discriminative trackers handle occlusion via global semantic matching yet struggle with similar distractors. Existing multi-tracker fusion strategies rely on static per-frame confidence or heuristic rules, making them 'short-sighted'—they cannot detect gradual error accumulation or distinguish temporary outliers from systematic failures. The paper addresses this by reframing tracker reliability as a time-series modeling problem that explicitly captures the temporal evolution of each expert's error regime.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely on low-dimensional state vectors rather than raw images: for each frame it constructs an 11-dimensional vector encoding the relative bounding-box deltas of both experts (SAM2 and a discriminative tracker) with respect to the previous fused result, their inter-expert IoU as a consistency signal, and the normalized target width/height. A sliding window of N=32 historical state vectors is fed to the temporal backbone. The output is a single fused bounding box, per-expert softmax fusion weights, and disentangled directional offsets and scale magnitudes for each expert branch, all computed with negligible overhead since no image features are processed.

## Method

TR-MoE comprises three stages: a KAN-based embedding layer projects each 11-dim state into a 128-dim latent space using learnable B-spline activations on edges; four stacked Mamba blocks (selective state-space model) process the 32-frame sequence with linear O(N) complexity, where input-dependent system parameters allow the model to adaptively integrate or reset historical context based on current tracking stability; and a KAN-enhanced disentangled head factorizes the refinement into independent directional vectors, scalar magnitudes, and reliability scores per expert, combined via softmax-weighted fusion. The key architectural insight is that Mamba's selective scan naturally distinguishes between stable tracking (small time-scales, focus on immediate motion) and abrupt deviations (larger time-scales, integrate longer history), while KAN's edge-parameterized functions provide more parameter-efficient and interpretable nonlinear mappings than MLPs. Training uses a multi-task objective with a global validity mask to exclude catastrophic failures, a 'teachability' gate that applies auxiliary supervision only when an expert's raw prediction has sufficient IoU with ground truth, and an L2 regularization on scale factors to encourage minimal intervention.

## Results

TR-MoE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three main benchmarks: 77.3% AUC on LaSOT (outperforming the previous best MCITrack by 0.7% and SAM2-T by 10.0%), 58.4% AUC on the harder LaSOT_ext subset (+2.7% over LoRAT-L378), and 81.4% AO on GOT-10k (+1.4% over MCITrack and SPMTrack). On GOT-10k, TR-MoE also leads in SR0.5 (91.9%) and SR0.75 (78.8%), surpassing the strongest single expert SAM2-T by 2.9% in AO. The framework consistently improves over both individual experts (SAM2-T and LoRAT-L378) by 1.2–10.0% in AUC depending on the benchmark, confirming that temporal reliability modeling yields gains beyond simple averaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TR-MoE outperforms all 15 baselines on every reported metric across LaSOT, LaSOT_ext, and GOT-10k. The largest margin is over SAM2-T (+10.0% AUC on LaSOT, +6.0% on LaSOT_ext), confirming that the foundation model alone is unstable in long-term tracking and benefits substantially from the discriminative expert. Against the strongest discriminative tracker LoRAT-L378, TR-MoE gains +2.2% AUC on LaSOT and +2.7% on LaSOT_ext, while on GOT-10k it surpasses LoRAT by +3.9% AO. Compared to the closest recent competitors MCITrack-L384 and SPMTrack-L378, TR-MoE leads by +0.7% and +0.5% AUC on LaSOT respectively, and by +1.4% AO on GOT-10k. The generalizability study (Table 2) further shows consistent gains when fusing SAM2 with five different trackers (SGLATrack, SwinTrack, ODTrack, LoRAT-B, LoRAT-L), with improvements ranging from +0.1% to +6.2% in AUC depending on the partner tracker's strength.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the growing body of SAM2-based video tracking work (HiM2SAM, SAMURAI, DAM4SAM) that adapts foundation models to tracking but leaves occlusion robustness unaddressed; (2) discriminative one-stream trackers (OSTrack, LoRAT, ODTrack) that excel under occlusion via global semantic matching; and (3) Mixture-of-Experts architectures, primarily developed for LLMs, which this paper repurposes for heterogeneous tracker fusion. The key conceptual departure from prior fusion methods (Kalman filtering, static per-frame confidence, heuristic rules) is reframing expert reliability as a time-series process modeled by a selective state space model (Mamba), rather than a per-frame scalar. The KAN-based disentangled head further departs from standard MLP regression heads by exploiting the Kolmogorov-Arnold decomposition to factorize geometric corrections into interpretable directional, scale, and weight components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated gap: no single tracker is universally optimal, and the complementary failure modes of foundation models versus discriminative trackers are well-documented. The temporal reliability framing offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc fusion heuristics, and the demonstrated generalizability across five architecturally diverse tracker pairings (from lightweight SGLATrack to large LoRAT-L) suggests the module is a broadly applicable plug-in. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two experts, how sensitive performance is to the 32-frame context window, and whether the same temporal-reliability modeling transfers to multi-object tracking or detection tasks. A limitation is that the method is inherently post-hoc—it requires two independently trained trackers and cannot be jointly optimized end-to-end with them.
