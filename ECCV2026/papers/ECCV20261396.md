## Problem

Existing pose-guided person image synthesis methods rely on small paired datasets (~100 identities) that cause overfitting and cannot generalize to in-the-wild subjects, while virtual try-on models are trained to preserve the source pose rather than control it. The core unsolved challenge is preserving a person's full-body identity (face, skin, body shape) while simultaneously stripping away arbitrary clothing occlusions and reposing them into a standardized, garment-neutral canonical form—without access to large-scale paired data of the same person in varied outfits and poses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB photo and a target SMPL-X pose (rendered as a 2D image) as input. The input photo is unwrapped into a partial canonical UV texture map (ill-posed due to self-occlusions) and a face crop extracted via MediaPipe; the target pose is provided as a rendered SMPL-X mesh image. The output is a photorealistic full-body avatar in standardized 'Base Clothing' (black sleeveless tank top and shorts) against a clean background, generated in the 16-channel latent space of the Flux VAE and decoded to pixel space. The canonical UV texture map is also an implicit byproduct, enabling downstream texture editing.

## Method

The key insight is formulating generation in canonical UV texture space, which theoretically decouples pose from appearance and enables self-supervised learning from unpaired single images. To prevent the network from trivially copying visible pixels via occlusion-boundary leakage, the paper introduces Donor-based UV Reposing: the input texture is masked with a visibility mask sampled from an unrelated 'donor' image (40–80% IoU overlap), breaking the correlation between texture boundaries and the target pose and forcing the model to learn geometric warping. The generator is a Latent Rectified Flow model built on the Flux.1 [dev] DiT backbone, with rank-128 LoRA adapters on attention and MLP layers, trained via a dual-branch strategy: a paired branch (30K DeepFashion samples) conditions on partial UV texture + target pose + face crop, while an unpaired branch (470K images) uses donor-reposed textures with the face crop dropped to prevent pixel-perfect shortcuts. A Base Clothing dataset is synthesized via Gemini 2.5 Flash Image to standardize all training targets into black tops and shorts, and a lightweight test-time LoRA fine-tuning step on a few reference images further reduces identity drift for extreme pose changes.

## Results

On the out-of-domain WPose benchmark (2,305 pairs), Pro-Pose (Unpaired+Paired) achieves M-PSNR 19.95, FID 5.99, M-SSIM 0.860, M-LPIPS 0.121, FaceSim 0.5571, and HPSv3 7.55, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. On the in-domain DeepFashion test set (8,570 pairs), it reaches PSNR 19.36, SSIM 0.818, LPIPS 0.075, FaceSim 0.6047, and HPSv3 7.24. A stratified identity analysis shows Pro-Pose maintains FaceSim above 0.4 in 86.5% of cases (vs. <3% for all baselines) even under large pose changes, and a 30K-vote user study confirms 71.98% preference for VTO applied to Pro-Pose canonicalized avatars over direct VTO on originals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pro-Pose decisively outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric for the out-of-domain WPose evaluation. The largest gaps are against the diffusion-based reposing methods (CFLD, MCLD, LEFFA), which suffer severe identity collapse on in-the-wild data (FaceSim ≈ 0.09–0.10 vs. Pro-Pose's 0.5571) and produce FID scores 10–16× higher. Among the stronger competitors, Gemini 3 Pro Image is the closest baseline, trailing Pro-Pose by 2.76 dB in M-PSNR, 1.16 in FID, 0.032 in FaceSim, and 0.15 in HPSv3. OnePoseTrans and UniHuman, while better than the older diffusion reposers, still lag Pro-Pose by 2.7–2.3 dB in M-PSNR and 0.38–0.45 in FaceSim. On the in-domain DeepFashion set, Pro-Pose similarly leads on PSNR (19.36 vs. 18.21 MCLD), SSIM (0.818 vs. 0.796 UniHuman), LPIPS (0.075 vs. 0.109 Gemini 3 Pro), and FaceSim (0.6047 vs. 0.5856 Gemini 3 Pro), though LEFFA marginally edges it on FID (4.23 vs. 4.24) and MCLD on OKS (0.49 vs. 0.48). The ablation further confirms that the unpaired-data branch is essential for out-of-domain generalization: the Paired-Only variant drops to M-PSNR 18.30 and FaceSim 0.4959 on WPose, while the Unpaired-Only variant degrades to M-PSNR 16.13 under large pose changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flow Matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2023) and the OminiControl conditioning architecture (Tan et al., ICCV 2025) for diffusion transformers, instantiating them on the Flux.1 DiT backbone with LoRA adapters. It departs from prior pose-guided person synthesis methods (CFLD, MCLD, LEFFA) that rely on small paired datasets (~100 identities in DeepFashion) by formulating the problem in canonical SMPL-X UV texture space, where pose and appearance are theoretically decoupled. The key methodological departure is the Donor-based UV Reposing mechanism, which uses visibility masks from unrelated subjects to break the correlation between occlusion boundaries and target pose, enabling self-supervised learning from ~470K unpaired images alongside 30K paired samples. The Base Clothing standardization via Gemini 2.5 Flash Image is a novel data-engineering step that creates a consistent visual target, distinguishing this work from prior methods that preserve or ignore source garments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental data-scarcity bottleneck in pose-guided person synthesis by showing that abundant unpaired single images can be effectively leveraged through UV-space self-supervision, potentially unblocking the field from dependence on small paired datasets. The canonical 'black tank top and shorts' representation has immediate practical value as a preprocessing stage for virtual try-on, where the 72% user preference and halved garment error rate demonstrate tangible downstream impact. Key limitations remain: single-view SMPL-X body estimation errors propagate into generated avatars, extreme pose changes (e.g., back-to-front) force hallucination of unseen facial regions, and self-occluded body parts cannot be faithfully reconstructed. Open questions include whether multi-reference consistency can be achieved without per-subject fine-tuning, and whether the UV-space formulation generalizes to more complex clothing or accessories beyond the base garment.
