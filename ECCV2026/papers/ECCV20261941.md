## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for VLA models rely on shallow-layer attention to decide which tokens to discard, but this short-sighted strategy risks permanently removing tokens that become critical in deeper Transformer layers. At aggressive pruning ratios (>70%), the probability of losing core tokens rises sharply, causing substantial performance degradation in downstream action generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261941/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play module on top of existing VLA backbones (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, CogACT, π0.5), consuming RGB image sequences from robot cameras and a text instruction prompt. It produces the same robot action tokens as the unpruned model but with a reduced visual token set in deeper layers, achieving up to 1.89× speedup while keeping success rate degradation below 1.5%. The pruning is irreversible within a timestep: once tokens are removed at the shallow pruning layer, only the retained subset is available for all subsequent layers.

## Method

SAFE-Pruner exploits the observed 'semantic attention consistency' phenomenon—VLA models predominantly attend to the same task-relevant semantic entities (e.g., target object, gripper) across consecutive control timesteps even as spatial positions shift. At designated key timesteps, the full-token saliency vectors for every Transformer layer are cached; at non-key timesteps, the method identifies semantically corresponding visual tokens via cosine distance to the most recent keyframe and transfers the cached deep-layer saliency to forecast which tokens will matter in later stages. The final pruning score fuses the current shallow-layer saliency with the predicted deep-layer saliency via a weighted combination, and tokens below the top-K threshold are pruned. An adaptive key-timestep refresh mechanism monitors cosine similarity between current and forecasted shallow saliency maps, triggering a full-token inference and cache update whenever the similarity drops below a threshold, thereby detecting subtask transitions and preventing drift accumulation.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark across three VLA architectures (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, π0.5), SAFE-Pruner achieves up to 1.89× speedup (e.g., 70.25 ms → 37.22 ms on OpenVLA-OFT) while maintaining average success rates within 1.5% of the unpruned baseline (96.4% vs. 96.8% on OpenVLA-OFT; 95.5% vs. 96.5% on π0.5). FLOPs are reduced by up to 60% (3.970 T → 1.722 T on OpenVLA-OFT). On the SIMPLER benchmark with CogACT, the method achieves a 1.73× speedup at 37.4% FLOPs retention with a 74.5% average success rate, nearly matching the 74.8% vanilla baseline. On real-robot tasks (Astribot S1), the method reduces latency from 80.36 ms to 43.56 ms while preserving 79.3% average success rate versus 69.0% for FastV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFE-Pruner consistently outperforms all five baselines on the OpenVLA-OFT and π0.5 models in both success rate and efficiency. On OpenVLA-OFT, it achieves 96.4% average success rate, exceeding VLA-Pruner (94.1%) by 2.3% and FastV (94.5%) by 1.9%, while also delivering the lowest FLOPs (1.722 T vs. 2.141 T for FastV) and latency (37.22 ms vs. 50.24 ms). On π0.5, the 95.5% success rate surpasses VLA-Pruner (94.2%) by 1.3% and DivPrune (92.7%) by 2.8%, with the lowest FLOPs (1.482 T) and latency (24.33 ms) among all methods. On OpenVLA, SAFE-Pruner ties VLA-Pruner at 74.2% success rate but achieves lower FLOPs (0.742 T vs. 0.793 T) and latency (32.18 ms vs. 36.47 ms). On the SIMPLER benchmark, the method achieves a 1.73× speedup at 37.4% FLOPs, substantially outperforming FastV (1.21× at 42.0% FLOPs) and VLA-Cache (1.38× at 80.1% FLOPs) while maintaining a 74.5% success rate nearly identical to the 74.8% vanilla baseline. On real-robot tasks, SAFE-Pruner preserves 79.3% average success rate versus only 69.0% for FastV, with the gap most pronounced on the dexterity-focused Throw Basketball task (81% vs. 69%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual token pruning line of work for VLMs (FastV, SparseVLM, DivPrune) and VLA-specific pruning (VLA-Cache, VLA-Pruner) by identifying a new empirical regularity—semantic attention consistency—where VLA models attend to the same task-relevant semantic entities across control timesteps even as spatial positions shift. Unlike prior methods that make irreversible pruning decisions from shallow-layer attention alone, or that estimate late-stage saliency from already-pruned token subsets, SAFE-Pruner forecasts deep-layer importance by transferring saliency from cached historical keyframes via cosine-distance token correspondence. The adaptive key-timestep refresh mechanism, triggered by shallow-attention cosine similarity drops, departs from fixed-interval temporal caching used in VLA-Cache and VLA-Pruner, targeting the specific failure mode of subtask-boundary attention shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of SAFE-Pruner makes it immediately deployable across heterogeneous VLA architectures without retraining, which is critical for the rapidly evolving VLA ecosystem where models are frequently updated. The semantic attention consistency observation provides a useful inductive bias for future VLA acceleration work beyond pruning (e.g., KV-cache compression, speculative decoding). Open questions include how the method behaves under multi-camera or video-input settings where semantic consistency may be weaker, whether the cosine-distance token correspondence degrades with very large spatial displacements, and whether the adaptive threshold γ can be learned rather than hand-tuned. The real-robot validation on Astribot S1 strengthens practical credibility, though the 12% success rate drop on Pack Doll (73%→67%) suggests long-horizon multi-stage tasks remain challenging for aggressive pruning.
