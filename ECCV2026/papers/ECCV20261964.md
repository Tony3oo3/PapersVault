## Problem

Existing REC methods rely on image-text contrastive learning to distinguish visually similar objects, but this approach is constrained by the small number of available negative referring expressions (typically fewer than 5 per image) and by the inherent misalignment between image and text embedding spaces. The paper asks whether contrastive supervision can be performed entirely within the visual embedding space to obtain a larger, more stable pool of negatives and avoid cross-modal alignment issues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261964/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a freeform referring expression (e.g., 'person riding a bicycle') as input and produces a count plus localized point predictions for the matching objects. The referring expression is consumed only to condition the frozen visual backbone via cross-attention; it is never used as a direct contrastive anchor. At inference, 900 output tokens are generated per image and thresholded (0.25 for CLS, 0.35 for remaining tokens) to yield positive detections, or alternatively the top-N tokens are selected based on a density map estimate.

## Method

C-REX replaces the image-text contrastive loss in existing REC architectures with a modified supervised contrastive objective that operates entirely on visual tokens. For each Image-RE pair, predicted image tokens are assigned to a positive or negative set via Bipartite Matching against ground-truth points: matched tokens are positives, all others are negatives. The key modification to the standard supervised contrastive loss is that only positive samples serve as anchors, since the negative set contains tokens from heterogeneous classes and expressions that should not be pulled together. This design yields a pool of over 100 negative image tokens per anchor, compared to fewer than 5 negative REs in image-text CL, providing richer and more stable supervision. C-REX is a plug-in module requiring no architectural changes: it simply swaps the contrastive loss term while preserving all other counting losses (localization, classification, density) in the host model.

## Results

On REC-8K (test set), C-REX applied to GroundingDINO with Swin-B achieves the best overall performance: MAE 4.20, RMSE 16.76, F1 0.78, improving over the GroundingDINO baseline (MAE 4.64, RMSE 17.58, F1 0.74). The largest relative gains appear with Swin-T backbones, where GroundingDINO+C-REX reduces MAE from 5.90 to 4.38 (−25.8%) and RMSE from 19.73 to 17.93 (−9.1%), and GroundingREC+C-REX reduces MAE from 6.50 to 4.91 (−24.5%). On FSC-147 (class-agnostic counting, test set), GroundingDINO+C-REX achieves MAE 10.04 / RMSE 102.35, outperforming CountGD (14.76 / 120.42) and DAVE (14.90 / 103.42). The paper reports up to 28% MAE and 24.5% RMSE improvements over the original image-text contrastive baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-REX consistently outperforms all three REC baselines it is integrated into. Against GroundingREC [19], C-REX reduces test MAE by 24.5% (Swin-T: 6.50→4.91) and 15.5% (Swin-B: 5.42→4.58), with F1 improving from 0.69→0.72 and 0.70→0.73 respectively. Against CAD-GD [67], gains are more modest (Swin-T MAE 6.25→5.97; Swin-B MAE 5.36→5.02), but RMSE improves notably (Swin-T: 18.91→17.37). Against the GroundingDINO [39] detection baseline, C-REX yields the largest absolute gains, cutting Swin-T MAE from 5.90 to 4.38 and Swin-B MAE from 4.64 to 4.20, while boosting F1 to 0.75 and 0.78. On FSC-147 class-agnostic counting, GroundingDINO+C-REX (MAE 10.04, RMSE 102.35) beats CountGD [6] (14.76, 120.42) and DAVE [50] (14.90, 103.42), while CAD-GD+C-REX (11.90, 99.65) achieves the best RMSE among all text-based methods. Finally, C-REX-equipped models substantially outperform the Molmo2-4B VLM [21] on REC-8K (MAE 4.20–5.02 vs. 10.04 zero-shot), confirming that dedicated visual contrastive representations remain essential for fine-grained counting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the image-text contrastive learning paradigm established by GroundingREC [19] and CAD-GD [67] for Referring Expression Counting, which anchor contrastive supervision in the text embedding space. Instead, C-REX repurposes the supervised contrastive loss [35] to operate entirely within the visual token space, using the RE only to condition the visual backbone via cross-attention rather than as a contrastive anchor. The approach draws on the success of image-space contrastive learning in person re-identification and semantic segmentation, but adapts it to REC by defining positives/negatives on-the-fly through bipartite matching of predicted tokens to ground-truth points, and by modifying the standard supervised CL to use only positive-class tokens as anchors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that image-space contrastive supervision consistently outperforms—and is not complemented by—image-text CL challenges the design assumption in prior REC methods that text embeddings must serve as contrastive anchors. The plug-in nature of C-REX (no architectural changes required) makes it immediately applicable to any existing REC or detection-based counting model, and the large negative pool (>100 visual tokens vs. <5 REs) addresses a fundamental data-scarcity bottleneck in contrastive learning for this task. Open questions include whether the approach degrades in extremely dense scenes where the negative token pool shrinks, whether it transfers to video or 3D counting, and whether the mixed paraphrase-robustness results (improving 2 of 3 architectures) indicate a residual dependence on text quality that image-space CL alone cannot fully resolve.
