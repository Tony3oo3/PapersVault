## Problem

Unified multimodal models (UMMs) that jointly perform understanding and generation have not been extended to the tactile domain, where both sensor-level configurations (illumination, gel deformation, camera parameters) and object-level semantics (roughness, hardness, texture) jointly determine the meaning of touch. Existing tactile models treat understanding and generation as separate problems, rely on small self-curated datasets, and fail to model the non-contact-to-contact transition that is inherent to tactile data acquisition, limiting cross-sensor generalization.

## Input / Output

For understanding, the model takes a visuo-tactile video (image-like frames from sensors such as GelSight, DIGIT, or Duragel) encoded into a sequence of latent tokens by a pretrained touch encoder, spliced into the MLLM text stream with special markers, and produces free-form property descriptions, sensor identifications, or multi-step reasoning answers (comparison, matching, superlative selection). For generation, the input is a natural-language tactile description plus a sensor identity token; the output is a tactile image (256×256) or a temporally coherent tactile video sequence, with the sensor-specific conditioning ensuring the generated signal matches the target sensor's illumination, marker layout, and deformation characteristics.

## Method

UniTac uses a Qwen-VL 2.5 MLLM as a shared backbone, with a pretrained AnyTouch touch encoder producing latent tokens split into object-level (Z_obj) and sensor-level (Z_sen) components. Understanding is supervised via Dual-Level Mixture Comprehension: two next-token prediction tasks (object property description and sensor identification) force the MLLM to disentangle what changes with the object from what changes with the sensor. Generation follows a two-stage paradigm: Stage I trains a SANA-based touch decoder to reconstruct tactile signals from encoder latents (in parallel with understanding training), and Stage II trains a sensor-aware DiT projector using rectified flow matching to map MLLM query embeddings (concatenated with sensor tokens) into the tactile latent space. At inference, a Sensor-Prior Sampling Strategy replaces the standard unconditional CFG branch with a sensor-conditioned prior v_θ(x_t|t, Z_sen), modeling the physical non-contact-to-contact transition so that generation begins from the correct sensor state before incorporating object-level contact semantics.

## Results

On the PHYSICLEAR-Test benchmark, UniTac-7B achieves the highest average score of 66.51 across six tactile understanding tasks (PC, POM, PSS, Hardness, Roughness, Texture), surpassing the strongest UMM baseline Octopi-7B (57.31) by 9.20 points and the best understanding-only model LLaVA-OneVision-7B (32.49) by 34.02 points. For tactile image generation across four sensors (Digit, GelSight, GelSight Mini, Duragel), UniTac attains 0.836 average SSIM and 19.93 average PSNR, outperforming the best generation-only method TextToucher (0.816 SSIM, 18.65 PSNR) and the best UMM baseline JanusPro-7B (0.753 SSIM, 18.46 PSNR). The largest understanding gain is on Property-Object Matching (POM), where UniTac-7B scores 64.61 versus Octopi-7B's 22.22 (+42.39 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTac-7B decisively outperforms all 12 baselines on the PHYSICLEAR-Test understanding benchmark. Against the strongest UMM baseline Octopi-7B (57.31 avg), UniTac gains +9.20 overall, with the most dramatic improvement on Property-Object Matching (+42.39, from 22.22 to 64.61) and Property Comparison (+11.80, from 45.50 to 57.30). General-purpose MLLMs (GPT-4o at 31.65, Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 25.01, Gemini-2.5-Pro at 27.50, LLaVA-OneVision-7B at 32.49) all fall 25–42 points below UniTac, confirming that domain-specific tactile training is critical. Among other UMMs, TokenFlow-7B (39.13), JanusPro-7B (36.29), and BLIP3o-3B (32.84) are all 25–34 points behind. On tactile generation, UniTac (0.836 SSIM / 19.93 PSNR) beats the best generation-only method TextToucher (0.816 / 18.65) by +0.020 SSIM and +1.28 PSNR, and surpasses the best UMM baseline JanusPro-7B (0.753 / 18.46) by +0.083 SSIM and +1.47 PSNR, while also outperforming GVST, UniTouch, and PixArt-α on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniTac extends the unified multimodal model (UMM) paradigm—established for visual-textual tasks by Janus-Pro, Transfusion, Show-o, and BLIP3-o—into the tactile domain for the first time. It repurposes AnyTouch's pretrained cross-sensor touch encoder as the tactile front-end, adopts rectified flow matching (Lipman et al.; Esser et al.) for the generative branch, and follows Octopi's three-dimensional property description formulation for the understanding objective. The key conceptual departure from prior tactile work is the explicit dual-level decomposition of tactile signals into sensor-level configuration and object-level semantics, which prior models (UniTouch, TVL-Link, AnyTouch) treat implicitly or ignore, and the sensor-prior sampling strategy that replaces the standard unconditional CFG branch with a sensor-conditioned prior to model the physical non-contact-to-contact transition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying tactile understanding and generation in a single architecture trained on 400K+ multi-sensor clips, UniTac addresses a practical bottleneck in robotics: the cost of collecting new tactile data for every sensor variant. The cross-sensor generation results (beating generation-only baselines like TextToucher and GVST) and the real-world deployment showing predicted tactile representations enabling 80% grasping success versus 20% for RGB-only VLA suggest a viable path toward sensor-agnostic tactile perception. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond visuo-tactile (image-based) sensors to force/torque arrays, whether the dual-level supervision scales to more than four sensor types, and how the model handles dynamic contact scenarios beyond the quasi-static deformations in current datasets.
