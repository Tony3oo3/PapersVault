## Problem

WiFi-CSI-based 3D human pose estimation suffers from fragile cross-domain generalization, dependence on costly camera-based annotation pipelines that limit data scale, and hardware-specific noise that is amplified when CSI is flattened into 2D image-like grids for standard ViT/CNN backbones. No prior work combines axis-aware masking with latent-space prediction tailored to the physical (subcarrier, time, link) structure of CSI, nor has ray-tracing simulation been leveraged for self-supervised pre-training in WiFi sensing.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a WiFi CSI tensor of shape (3 Rx × 3 Ant × 20 time × 60 subcarrier channels), where 60 = 30 amplitude + 30 PhaseFi-denoised phase values, and reshapes it into a factored (C, T, L) = (60, 20, 9) representation before tokenization. It outputs 3D coordinates for 14 body keypoints per detected person (N × 14 × 3), with Hungarian matching assigning decoder queries to person instances. The CSI-specific tokenization produces 180 tokens (T × L), each encoding a precise spatial-temporal coordinate rather than a mixed 2D patch.

## Method

WiFi-JEPA pre-trains a ViT encoder via a JEPA objective that predicts masked latent embeddings rather than reconstructing raw CSI, using a context encoder, a shallower predictor, and an EMA-updated target encoder with Smooth L1 loss. The key structural innovation is link masking: entire Tx-Rx antenna-link columns (60% of 9 links) are removed simultaneously across all time steps, forcing the model to exploit cross-link spatial redundancy to recover missing temporal signals. A CSI-specific tokenization preserves the (C, T, L) axis semantics with unit patch size over (T, L) and separable 2D sinusoidal positional encoding, avoiding the physical-boundary mixing of 2D spectrogram flattening. For scalable unlabeled pre-training, a ray-tracing pipeline (NVIDIA Sionna RT) generates ~90K synthetic CSI frames from randomized geometric primitives with physics-based trajectories, providing channel dynamics diversity without pose annotations. During fine-tuning, the pre-trained encoder is attached to a PETR-style Transformer decoder with differential learning rates (0.1× for encoder, 1.0× for decoder) to adapt to supervised 3D pose regression.

## Results

On the PiW3D benchmark, WiFi-JEPA (real+sim) achieves 76.8 mm single-person MPJPE and 93.5 mm multi-person MPJPE, outperforming the previous best SSL method DT-Pose (90.0 mm) by 13.2 mm (−14.7%) and the PiW3D supervised baseline (91.7 mm) by 14.9 mm (−16.2%) in the single-person setting. In multi-person scenes, it reduces MPJPE from 107.2 mm (PiW3D) to 93.5 mm (−12.8%) and raises PCK@20 from 58.1% to 61.5%. The real-only pre-training variant already surpasses all baselines (78.2 mm SP, 97.1 mm MP), and adding ~90K simulated frames yields a further 1.4 mm and 3.6 mm reduction in SP and MP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WiFi-JEPA (real+sim) outperforms all five prior WiFi-CSI pose estimation methods on single-person MPJPE: it beats DT-Pose (90.0 mm, the only prior SSL method) by 13.2 mm, PiW3D baseline (91.7 mm) by 14.9 mm, WiPose (101.8 mm) by 25.0 mm, HPE-Li (120.2 mm) by 43.4 mm, and MetaFi++ (132.0 mm) by 55.2 mm. In multi-person scenes, where only PiW3D reports prior results, WiFi-JEPA reduces MPJPE from 107.2 mm to 93.5 mm (−12.8%) and improves PCK@20 from 58.1% to 61.5%. The real-only variant (78.2 mm SP, 97.1 mm MP) already surpasses every baseline, confirming that the JEPA pre-training objective itself is the primary driver of improvement. Among SSL baselines tested under identical architecture (Table 6), WiFi-JEPA's latent-space prediction beats MAE (130.3 mm) by 33.2 mm, SimMIM (145.6 mm) by 48.5 mm, BYOL (144.4 mm) by 47.3 mm, and MoCo v3 (141.5 mm) by 44.4 mm; notably, all four vision-native SSL objectives degrade performance below training from scratch (102.4 mm), whereas WiFi-JEPA consistently improves. In cross-domain leave-one-environment-out evaluation, WiFi-JEPA achieves a mean MPJPE of 324.2 mm versus 626.4 mm for the PiW3D baseline (−48.2%), and outperforms a Supervised+DANN few-shot adaptation baseline by 17.9% in macro mean MPJPE.

## Relation to Prior Work

WiFi-JEPA adapts the JEPA latent-prediction paradigm (Assran et al., CVPR'23; V-JEPA) to the WiFi-CSI modality, departing from the only prior SSL method for WiFi pose estimation, DT-Pose, which combines MAE reconstruction with temporal contrastive learning on a flattened 2D grid. The key conceptual departures are threefold: (i) a CSI-native tokenization that preserves the (C, T, L) tensor structure rather than collapsing it into a spectrogram image, (ii) link masking that exploits multi-antenna spatial redundancy as the pretext signal, and (iii) a ray-tracing simulation pipeline (Sionna RT) that generates unlabeled pre-training data from geometric primitives, drawing an analogy to non-semantic pre-training in vision (FractalDB, Dead Leaves). The fine-tuning stage reuses the PETR Transformer decoder from vision-based multi-person pose estimation, making the contribution primarily in the pre-training objective and data pipeline rather than the downstream architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that WiFi-CSI representation learning benefits from respecting the physical structure of the channel (subcarrier × time × link) rather than treating CSI as a generic image, and that cross-link spatial correlation is the dominant signal for pose-relevant features. The finding that simulated CSI from simple moving primitives matches real CSI in pre-training value opens a path to scaling WiFi sensing without expensive multi-room data collection. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (PiW3D) with fixed Intel 5300 hardware at 5.64 GHz, so generalization across antenna configurations, frequency bands, and room scales remains untested; PA-MPJPE barely improves over from-scratch training, suggesting the learned representations capture global localization but not fine-grained joint articulation.
