## Problem

Recovering complete 3D bone geometry from ultrasound is hindered by deterministic physics artifacts (acoustic shadowing, streaking, surface thickening, signal dropout) that create a large, structured gap between observed point clouds and true anatomy. Existing 3D completion models are trained on clean geometric data with no awareness of ultrasound physics, while ultrasound-specific methods (e.g., VAE-based approaches) risk shape collapse on in-vivo data whose distribution differs from simulation. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly decoupling anatomical plausibility from physical consistency during generative shape completion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial, artifact-laden point cloud (8,192 points) extracted from segmentation of B-mode ultrasound frames, which covers only a limited field-of-view (top ~35% of the vertebra at a tilted probe angle) and exhibits structured acoustic artifacts. It produces a complete anatomically consistent vertebral point cloud (8,192 points) in canonical space, with a subset of observation anchor points held on their exact OT-FM path to preserve local fidelity to the input while freely generating the unobserved regions.

## Method

UBone3D formulates completion as a physics-rectified conditional flow matching problem with two decoupled modules. BoneFM is a PointNet++-based conditional velocity field (modulated by FiLM layers) trained via OT-FM on clean CT anatomy (Spine1K, 5,502 samples) to transport Gaussian noise toward the anatomical manifold, conditioned on the partial ultrasound observation with classifier-free guidance. USimNet is a lightweight differentiable PointNet++ encoder-decoder trained to map clean anatomy to simulated ultrasound appearance (via PyMUST ray-casting with shadowing, attenuation, thick-surface, streak, and anti-grid artifacts), serving as a gradient-based forward model. At inference, a Heun second-order ODE solver integrates the flow over 35 steps, where each step combines the CFG velocity from BoneFM with a physics rectification gradient computed as the directed Chamfer Distance between USimNet's prediction and the observation, gated by a truncated polynomial schedule (active only for t ≥ 0.5) to avoid corrupting early-stage topology.

## Results

On the simulated full-physics test set (P_phys-full), UBone3D achieves the best EMD (74.298×10⁻³) and F-score@1% (0.079), while SVDFormer attains the lowest L1 CD (25.463×10⁻³) but with a substantially higher EMD (127.965×10⁻³), indicating poor global structural coverage. On zero-shot in-vivo data, UBone3D reduces the laminae distance error to 1.293 mm against the VNN reference, compared to 3.937 mm (SVDFormer†) and 3.382 mm (SSM-Net*), a roughly 3× improvement over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UBone3D outperforms all five baselines on EMD and F-score@1% under the full-physics training setting, with the largest margin over Gafencu et al. (EMD 74.3 vs. 257.0) and PCDreamer (F-score 0.079 vs. 0.025). SVDFormer achieves a slightly lower CD (25.46 vs. 28.16) but at the cost of a 72% higher EMD (128.0 vs. 74.3), suggesting it fits observed points tightly while failing to recover the full vertebral structure. PoinTr is competitive on CD (32.9) but trails UBone3D on both EMD and F-score. On zero-shot in-vivo data, UBone3D's laminae distance error (1.293 mm vs. VNN reference) is roughly 3× lower than SVDFormer† (3.937 mm) and 2.6× lower than SSM-Net* (3.382 mm), while the other baselines (PoinTr, PCDreamer, Gafencu) fail to produce anatomically plausible outputs on real ultrasound inputs entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

UBone3D builds directly on Optimal Transport Flow Matching (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) as its generative backbone, extending the unconditional velocity field to a conditional one via FiLM modulation with ultrasound observations. It departs from prior ultrasound bone reconstruction (Gafencu et al., which used a VAE that collapses on in-vivo data) and from general 3D completion methods (PoinTr, SVDFormer, PCDreamer) that were trained on clean geometric data with no awareness of acoustic physics. The key conceptual departure is the decoupling of anatomy plausibility (BoneFM) from physical consistency (USimNet) and their joint steering at test time, drawing on the physics-guided generation paradigm (PhysDiff, PIDM) but applying it to a differentiable learned surrogate rather than an explicit simulator at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine clinical gap: radiation-free longitudinal monitoring of adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, where repeated CT/X-rays impose high cumulative dose. The physics-rectified flow matching recipe—using a differentiable surrogate to inject observation-consistency gradients into a generative trajectory—is a potentially transferable pattern for any modality with deterministic, learnable forward artifacts. However, in-vivo validation is extremely limited (5 healthy volunteers, 24 vertebrae, no pathological cases), and the authors themselves note results on real data 'may require further improvement,' leaving open whether the sim-to-real gap closes at scale or with more diverse anatomy.
