## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for multi-task vision learning follow a coupled paradigm where shared and task-specific adaptations are entangled within a fixed-capacity backbone, causing representational competition and limiting scalability across tasks. Naive per-task PEFT extensions fail to model cross-task knowledge sharing or mitigate negative transfer, while full fine-tuning of heavy pretrained backbones for multiple tasks incurs prohibitive computational and memory costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261609/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and simultaneously produces five dense per-pixel predictions: semantic segmentation, human part segmentation, surface normal estimation, saliency detection, and depth estimation. A single forward pass through the upcycled MoE backbone yields shared task-agnostic representations, from which task-specific side branches extract stage-wise features (four stages for Swin-Tiny) that feed into lightweight SegFormer-style MLP decoders. The framework is backbone-agnostic, supporting both hierarchical (Swin) and vanilla (DINOv2) ViT architectures with frozen pretrained downsampling layers for stage alignment.

## Method

RUTaL introduces a decoupled two-stage adaptation: (1) Task-General Residual Upcycling (TGRU) converts a pretrained ViT's FFN layers into a task-generic Mixture-of-Experts by replicating base weights into N experts and parameterizing each expert's deviation as a low-rank residual (ΔW = AB, r ≪ min(D,H)), then further sharing the up-projection across experts and aggregating down-projections via router softmax weights, reducing total expert parameters to (N+1)·r·(D+H); (2) Task-Specific Ladder Adaptation (TSLA) is a lightweight decoupled side branch that, at each backbone stage, uses a Task-Aware Selector (a Task Feature Projector producing key-value features and a Task Modulation Generator producing γ/β parameters) to query task-relevant information, followed by a shared Ladder Adaptation Extractor that fuses these via sparse cross-attention and a task-modulated FFN. This separation ensures the shared backbone capacity expansion and task-specific specialization operate in distinct representational pathways, eliminating the competition inherent in coupled PEFT designs. With Swin-Tiny, the entire trainable parameter count is only 2.93M, and the framework achieves Δm = +7.79% on PASCAL-Context while requiring the least GPU memory (12.67 GB) and shortest batch time (0.248 s) among compared methods.

## Results

On PASCAL-Context (Swin-Tiny backbone), RUTaL achieves the best overall multi-task improvement of Δm = +7.79% with only 2.93M trainable parameters, surpassing the strongest baseline TADFormer (+4.24%, 7.38M params) by +3.55% while using 60% fewer parameters. It attains 73.42 mIoU on semantic segmentation, 61.70 mIoU on human part segmentation, and 14.65 RMSE on surface normal estimation. On NYUDv2, RUTaL further reduces surface normal RMSE to 21.75 (vs. 27.16 for TADFormer) and depth RMSE to 0.6175 (vs. 0.6577), while improving semantic segmentation to 42.76 mIoU (vs. 37.30), all with only 2.58M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RUTaL outperforms all 12 baselines on the overall Δm metric. Among single-task PEFT methods (Adapter, VL-Adapter, LoRA, VPT, BitFit, Compactor++), all produce negative Δm (ranging from −1.83% to −11.18%), confirming that per-task adaptation without cross-task modeling is fundamentally unsuitable for MTL. Among multi-task PEFT methods, RUTaL surpasses the closest competitor TADFormer by +3.55% in Δm (+7.79% vs. +4.24%) while using 60% fewer trainable parameters (2.93M vs. 7.38M) and 38% less GPU memory (12.67 GB vs. 20.47 GB). VMT-Adapter, the second-best multi-task PEFT method, trails RUTaL by +3.83% in Δm. On NYUDv2, RUTaL's advantage is even more pronounced: it reduces surface normal RMSE by 5.41 (21.75 vs. 27.16 for TADFormer) and depth RMSE by 0.040 (0.6175 vs. 0.6577), while improving semantic segmentation mIoU by 5.46 points (42.76 vs. 37.30), all with less than half the parameters of TADFormer (2.58M vs. 6.47M). Training speed also favors RUTaL at 0.248 s/batch versus 0.458 s/batch for TADFormer.

## Relation to Prior Work

RUTaL sits at the intersection of model upcycling (converting dense pretrained Transformers into Mixture-of-Experts architectures, as in Komatsuzaki et al.) and low-rank adaptation (LoRA), combining both into a parameter-efficient capacity-expansion strategy for the shared backbone. It departs from existing multi-task PEFT methods (Polyhistor, VMT-Adapter, MTLoRA, TADFormer) that adopt a 'coupled' paradigm—co-optimizing shared and task-specific updates within a fixed-capacity backbone—by proposing a 'decoupled' two-stage design: first upcycle the backbone into a task-generic MoE (TGRU), then extract task-specific features through a separate ladder-style side branch (TSLA), drawing on side-tuning and ladder-side-tuning literature for the latter component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled capacity-expansion-then-specialization principle offers a clean architectural recipe that could generalize beyond dense prediction to broader multi-task settings, and the low-rank residual MoE upcycling is a practical technique likely to be adopted in other PEFT pipelines. However, evaluation is limited to two dense-prediction benchmarks (PASCAL-Context, NYUD v2) and a narrow set of tasks (segmentation, normals, depth, saliency), leaving open whether the approach scales to more heterogeneous task collections, non-dense tasks, or larger foundation models. The authors note future directions including continual task adaptation and multimodal multi-task learning, but these remain unexplored.
