## Problem

Existing Open-Vocabulary Multi-Object Tracking (OVMOT) methods are fundamentally limited by their reliance on closed-set training paradigms that use only base-category supervision and synthetic image pairs, failing to exploit the rich temporal dynamics in video sequences. This results in poor generalization to novel object categories, as appearance-based association features learned on base classes do not transfer effectively to unseen objects. The paper addresses how to achieve universal tracking capability from raw video data without manual annotations while preserving discriminative power for robust association.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw video sequences (10K frames for training, 2% of MASA's data requirement) and produces per-frame tracked bounding boxes with consistent object identities and category labels across both base and novel categories. During pseudo-label generation, the detector's proposals serve as SAM2 prompts, and the system outputs per-frame confidence scores (IoU between forward and backward VOS masks) that guide adaptive feature fusion at inference. The tracker operates in a standard tracking-by-detection pipeline using a shared ResNet-50 backbone, achieving 12.1 FPS on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

SOVTrack introduces two key innovations: (1) Dual-Direction Pseudo Labeling (DDPL), which uses detector-guided initialization to prompt SAM2 for forward VOS, then extracts terminal masks as new prompts for backward VOS, retaining only trajectories whose start-frame IoU exceeds a cycle-consistency threshold and computing per-frame confidence scores from forward-backward mask overlap; (2) a dual-branch association architecture where a contrastive branch learns discriminative appearance features via InfoNCE loss on validated pseudo-labels, while an adversarial branch distills SAM2's multi-cue representations (localization, association, classification) through an Alignment Network trained with Multi-Cue Adversarial Distillation (MCAD) that combines L2 feature alignment with a GAN-style discriminator. An adaptive fusion mechanism uses the DDPL confidence scores to dynamically weight the contrastive and adversarial branch features, applying additional contrastive loss on the fused output to preserve discriminative properties.

## Results

On the TAO validation set, SOVTrack achieves 35.7% novel TETA and 40.1% base TETA, surpassing the previous best (SLAck) by 4.6% and 2.9% respectively. The most striking gain is in association accuracy: 42.4% novel AssocA and 42.3% base AssocA, exceeding SLAck by 4.6% and 4.7% and MASA by 7.8% and 5.9%. On the test set, SOVTrack reaches 28.9% novel TETA and 39.2% base TETA, outperforming all baselines. Notably, these results are obtained using only 10K video frames (2% of MASA's 500K image pairs), with a 63× reduction in SAM processing time (3.6 vs. 227.8 GPU-hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOVTrack outperforms all nine baselines on every metric in both the validation and test sets. Against the strongest prior OVMOT method SLAck [14], SOVTrack gains +4.6% novel TETA (35.7 vs. 31.1), +4.7% base AssocA (42.3 vs. 37.6), and +4.7% novel AssocA (42.4 vs. 37.8). Compared to MASA [13], the closest self-supervised baseline, the improvements are even larger: +5.7% novel TETA and +7.8% novel AssocA, achieved with only 2% of MASA's training data (10K video frames vs. 500K image pairs). Against the most recent transformer-based OVTR [10] and trajectory-aware TRACT [15], SOVTrack leads by 4.3% and 4.4% in novel TETA respectively, while also surpassing them in base TETA by 3.5% and 1.6%. On the test set, SOVTrack's 39.2% base TETA exceeds TRACT (36.2%) by 3.0% and SLAck (34.7%) by 4.5%, confirming consistent generalization. The only metric where SOVTrack does not lead is base ClsA (19.8 vs. TRACT's 21.5), a minor gap likely attributable to the shared OV detector rather than the association architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOVTrack extends the OVMOT line initiated by OVTrack (2023) and most directly departs from MASA (2024), which pioneered self-supervised pseudo-label generation using SAM on static image pairs. The key conceptual shift is moving from image-pair augmentation to raw video with bidirectional temporal validation (DDPL), and from target-level contrastive supervision alone to a dual-branch architecture that adds adversarial feature distillation from SAM2's multi-cue representations. The tracking-by-detection backbone and OV detector are inherited from OVTrack, while the GAN-style distillation mechanism is novel within the OVMOT context, borrowing adversarial training ideas from knowledge distillation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2% data requirement (10K video frames vs. MASA's 500K image pairs) and 63× reduction in SAM processing time make self-supervised OVMOT training substantially more accessible, potentially enabling practitioners to build open-vocabulary trackers without large-scale annotation pipelines. However, the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (TAO) with a single backbone (ResNet-50), and the adversarial distillation component contributes only modest gains (~1–1.5% TETA) over simpler L2 alignment, leaving open whether the added GAN complexity is justified at scale. Broader questions remain about generalization to longer video sequences, diverse domain shifts, and whether the cycle-consistency filtering degrades gracefully when SAM2's own tracking quality is poor.
