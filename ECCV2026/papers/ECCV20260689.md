## Problem

Existing VLMs trained on exocentric video fail to recover egocentric properties (fine-grained hand-object interactions, manipulation details) from exocentric viewpoints, because these cues are visually subtle in third-person views and are not emphasized in standard exocentric training data. This limitation is critical for ADL monitoring where egocentric cameras are impractical to deploy, making it impossible to simply collect egocentric data at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260689/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single exocentric video and a natural language query as input and produces a natural language answer (MCQ selection or free-form description). During training, the model additionally receives the time-synchronized egocentric video, which is processed by a frozen teacher VLM to generate pseudo-ground-truth language sequences that supervise the student. The benchmark evaluates four MCQ tasks (Action Understanding, Task-relevant Regions, Human-Object Interactions, Hand Identification) plus ADL video description tasks scored on correctness, detail orientation, contextual understanding, temporal understanding, and consistency.

## Method

Ego2ExoVLM employs a teacher-student paradigm where a frozen teacher VLM observes the egocentric video and generates a language sequence capturing egocentric properties, while a student VLM observes the exocentric video and is trained to reproduce the teacher's outputs via autoregressive sequence distillation. To address the large ego-exo viewpoint gap where interaction cues occupy only a small portion of the exocentric frame, the student is augmented with Ego Adaptive Visual Tokens: a set of learnable tokens that are cross-attended with intermediate vision encoder representations at multiple layers, enabling selective extraction of ego-relevant spatio-temporal regions. The tokens are projected into the language model's embedding space via a dedicated connector and concatenated with exocentric visual embeddings before feeding the LLM. The architecture uses a SigLIP vision encoder (frozen) and Qwen2.5 LLM (LoRA rank 64), initialized from VideoLLaMA3, with only the vision-language connector, cross-attention projections, and LoRA weights updated. A key empirical finding is that language-level distillation substantially outperforms visual feature-level distillation for cross-view transfer in VLMs, and that the approach generalizes to non-time-synchronized ego-exo pairs.

## Results

On the proposed Ego-in-Exo Perception benchmark, Ego2ExoVLM achieves 74.2% average accuracy across four egocentric-understanding tasks (Action Understanding 73.4%, Task Regions 82.0%, HOI 76.2%, Hand Identification 65.3%), approaching the 77.9% upper bound set by the ego-observing teacher. On the ADL-X benchmark suite, Ego2ExoVLM reaches state-of-the-art performance with 93.1% on Charades MCQ, 75.8% on TC Average, 75.5% on Charades Video Description, and 88.7% on Toyota Smarthome Video Description, surpassing VideoLLaMA3 (76.9% TC Avg, 87.2% Toyota Smarthome) and EE4D-VideoLLaMA3 (75.0% TC Avg, 86.4% Toyota Smarthome).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ego2ExoVLM outperforms all 13 baselines on the Ego-in-Exo Perception benchmark. The strongest general-purpose VLM, VideoLLaMA3 (71.2%), is surpassed by +3.0%, while the in-domain variant EE4D-VideoLLaMA3 (65.7%) is beaten by +8.5%, demonstrating that time-synchronized ego-exo distillation is more effective than exo-only fine-tuning on the same dataset. LLaViDAL, a VLM specifically designed for ADL, scores only 33.3% (15.2% on Action Understanding), confirming that standard exocentric ADL training fails to capture egocentric properties. On ADL-X, Ego2ExoVLM leads VideoLLaMA3 by +1.1% on Charades MCQ (93.1 vs 92.0) and +1.5% on Toyota Smarthome descriptions (88.7 vs 87.2), and outperforms LLaViDAL by a wide margin (75.8% vs 44.0% TC Avg). In the ablation-style transfer-strategy comparison (Table 7), Ego2ExoVLM's language-level distillation (74.2%) beats visual feature distillation from Quattrocchi et al. (67.8%) and the reverse-direction Exo2EgoVLM (70.3%), confirming that language is a more effective medium for ego-to-exo transfer in VLMs than feature-space alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant exo→ego transfer direction in ego-exo video understanding (Ego-Exo, Quattrocchi et al., ViewpointRosetta) by tackling the inverse ego→exo problem within the VLM setting. It repurposes the visual probing mechanism from VisiCop [52] as Ego Adaptive Visual Tokens, but recontextualizes them for cross-view knowledge transfer rather than domain adaptation. A key conceptual departure is from prior cross-view transfer methods that operate at the visual feature level (MSE on pooled features, contrastive alignment); the paper argues and demonstrates that language-level sequence distillation is the more effective medium for bridging the ego-exo gap in VLMs, where the LLM's generative capacity provides a richer supervisory signal than raw feature matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in ADL monitoring where egocentric cameras are impractical to deploy yet egocentric properties (hand-object interactions, fine-grained manipulation) define the activities of interest. The Ego-in-Exo Perception benchmark fills a clear evaluation void: no prior benchmark isolates egocentric understanding from exocentric inputs, making it difficult to measure whether VLMs truly capture interaction-level details or merely exploit scene-level context. Limitations include evaluation restricted to cooking activities from EgoExo4D, reliance on a single teacher-student architecture (SigLIP/Qwen2.5), and the fact that the approach still requires paired ego-exo data at training time. Open questions include generalization to non-cooking ADLs, whether the privileged supervision signal degrades with larger viewpoint gaps, and whether the language-mediated transfer strategy extends to other cross-modal or cross-domain distillation settings.
