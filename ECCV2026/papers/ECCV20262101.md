## Problem

Existing vision-based methods can localize hand-object contact or estimate forces only in 2D image space or on flat surfaces, but cannot recover dense 3D force distributions on both hands and non-planar manipulated objects from a single egocentric view. This limits physically grounded interaction reasoning, since contact alone does not capture the magnitude and direction of forces governing manipulation stability and intent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262101/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

EgoPHI takes a single egocentric RGB image, the 3D template mesh of the manipulated object, and hand vertex coordinates (ground-truth during training, HAMER-estimated at inference) as input. It outputs per-vertex contact probabilities and 3D force vectors (decomposed into magnitude and unit direction) on the left hand, right hand, and object meshes, along with a refined object pose (rotation and translation in camera frame). Predicted forces are gated by contact probabilities to suppress non-contact forces, and all meshes are normalized to unit magnitude before processing.

## Method

EgoPHI is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a ViT-B/16 backbone extracts patch-level visual features that are sampled at projected mesh vertices and fused with 3D coordinates to initialize per-vertex embeddings; (2) an Iterative Refinement Module (IRM) refines the object pose over three iterations using Graph-Based Interaction Blocks that combine intra-mesh GAT layers (following mesh connectivity), inter-mesh cross-attention (each mesh attends to the other two), and FFN layers, with incremental 6D rotation and translation updates; (3) a second stack of Interaction Blocks predicts per-vertex contact and force conditioned on the final pose. Training supervision comes from a SOFA physics simulation that applies penalty-based soft virtual springs (k=10) to the hand and object meshes, generating dense per-vertex force vectors filtered by a binary contact mask. Forces are decomposed into magnitude and direction for cleaner loss formulation, and the total objective combines weighted BCE contact loss, contact-weighted L2 force magnitude loss, normalized L2 direction loss, and pose reconstruction terms.

## Results

On ARCTIC (in-distribution), EgoPHI achieves a 2D force F1 of 0.271 and volumetric IoU of 15.4, substantially outperforming PressureVision (F1 0.128, vIoU 6.6). On the out-of-distribution H2O benchmark, EgoPHI still attains F1 0.053 and vIoU 2.7 versus PressureVision's 0.005 and 0.2. In the real-world evaluation on instrumented objects, EgoPHI recovers object force with MAE as low as 0.48 N (cube) and 1.35 N (cylinder), and object contact F1 of 0.151 and 0.121 respectively. The Iterative Refinement Module improves 3D IoU by up to 7× (0.021→0.157 on ARCTIC) and reduces mean per-vertex pose error by over 70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PressureVision [12] (2D image-space force estimation), EgoPHI wins decisively on every metric: on ARCTIC it more than doubles F1 (0.271 vs 0.128) and volumetric IoU (15.4 vs 6.6), and on H2O the gap widens to over 10× in F1 (0.053 vs 0.005) and vIoU (2.7 vs 0.2), reflecting PressureVision's inability to handle egocentric occlusion. Against HACO [21] (SOTA 3D mesh-based hand contact), EgoPHI achieves lower force MAE and RMSE and higher volumetric IoU on both ARCTIC and H2O, while HACO retains higher hand-contact recall (benefiting from training on 14 diverse datasets). Critically, EgoPHI is the only evaluated method that produces any object-surface contact or force estimates, a capability neither baseline supports. On the real-world instrumented-object benchmark, EgoPHI achieves object force MAE of 0.48 N (cube) and 1.35 N (cylinder) without any real-force training data, validating sim-to-real transfer of the SOFA-generated supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vision-based force estimation line (PressureVision, PressureVision++, EgoPressure) from 2D image-space heatmaps and flat-surface hand pressure to dense 3D per-vertex forces on both hands and articulated non-planar objects. It departs from prior contact-estimation work (HACO, S2Contact, DECO) by adding a force dimension and object-surface reasoning, and introduces a SOFA-based physics simulation pipeline to generate the per-vertex force supervision that no existing dataset provides. The architecture combines a ViT backbone for visual features with Graph Attention Networks and cross-attention for inter-mesh interaction, plus an iterative pose refinement loop that has no direct analogue in prior hand-object contact methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoPHI establishes the first experimental evidence that egocentric RGB alone can recover physically meaningful 3D force distributions on real non-planar objects, opening a path toward force-aware robot learning from human demonstration and embodied scene understanding. Key open questions include temporal consistency (the model is per-frame), the need for an object mesh template (limiting applicability to unconstrained video), uniform stiffness assumptions that break for compliant materials, and the absence of tangential force estimation. The very low absolute contact scores (F1 < 0.2) and the narrow real-world validation (cube and cylinder only) suggest the field still has substantial headroom before this capability is practically deployable.
