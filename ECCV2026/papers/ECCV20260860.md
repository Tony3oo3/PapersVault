## Problem

Standard reconstruction-based pre-training of visual tokenizers (VAEs) produces latent spaces biased toward low-level pixel information, creating a paradox where better reconstruction accuracy does not translate to better generation quality. Scaling compute for reconstruction-only pre-training actually degrades downstream generative performance, a limitation the authors term the 'pre-training scaling problem.' The paper asks how to design tokenizer pre-training that genuinely scales in compute, parameters, and data to improve generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260860/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The ViT-based tokenizer compresses 256×256 RGB images into a 64-dimensional latent at 1/16 spatial resolution (f16d64), which a pixel decoder (4-layer ViT-L) lifts back to pixel space. For downstream generation, the latent is conditioned on either an ImageNet class label or a free-form text prompt (LAION T2I at 256–512 resolution) and fed to a LightningDiT. The encoder features also serve as semantic representations evaluated via linear probing and zero-shot classification on ImageNet, directly measuring the latent's understanding quality.

## Method

VTP builds a fully ViT-based autoencoder (ViT-L encoder, 4-layer ViT-L pixel decoder, 12-layer transformer text encoder) and jointly optimizes three loss families: (1) reconstruction via L1 + perceptual loss (with a separate GAN fine-tuning stage for the decoder), (2) self-supervised learning combining masked image modeling and DINO-style self-distillation following DINOv2, and (3) CLIP-style image-text contrastive learning. A differentiated batch-sampling strategy allocates 16k samples to CLIP, 4k to SSL, and 2k to reconstruction within each global batch to accommodate their divergent batch-size requirements. The key insight is that semantic understanding tasks—rather than reconstruction fidelity—are the dominant driver of generation quality, and their joint optimization with reconstruction unlocks a new scaling law: downstream DiT generation performance improves monotonically with tokenizer pre-training compute, model size, and data volume, whereas reconstruction-only tokenizers saturate or degrade early.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 generation, VTP (675M DiT, 600 epochs) achieves 1.11 gFID with guidance, surpassing REPA-E (1.15 at 800 epochs) and RAE* (1.13 at 800 epochs). At just 80 epochs without guidance, VTP (1.0B) reaches 2.03 gFID, outperforming RAE (4.28), VA-VAE (4.29), and REPA-E (3.46) under the same budget. The VTP tokenizer simultaneously achieves 0.36 rFID, 78.2% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy, and 85.7% linear probing accuracy, exceeding prior unified tokenizers UniTok (0.41 rFID, 70.8% zero-shot) and VILA-U (1.80 rFID, 73.3% zero-shot). Scaling tokenizer pre-training compute by 10× yields a 65.8% FID improvement for downstream DiT, while reconstruction-only autoencoders saturate at 1/10 of that compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 80 epochs (convergence efficiency), VTP (675M) achieves 2.62 gFID without guidance, substantially outperforming REPA (7.90), DDT (6.62), RAE (4.28), VA-VAE (4.29), and REPA-E (3.46). With the 1.0B model, VTP reaches 2.03 gFID at 80 epochs, edging out the larger RAE variant (835M, 2.16). In long-period training, VTP (600 epochs) attains 1.11 gFID with guidance, beating REPA-E (1.15 at 800 epochs), RAE* (1.13 at 800 epochs), and VA-VAE (1.35 at 800 epochs) while requiring fewer training epochs. On the tokenizer side, VTP's 0.36 rFID surpasses UniTok (0.41) and VILA-U (1.80), and its 78.2% zero-shot accuracy exceeds UniTok (70.8%) and VILA-U (73.3%), while its 85.7% linear probing is competitive with DINOv2 (86.7%) and MAE (85.9%). Notably, RAE's generation performance degrades as the tokenizer scales from S to L (3.50→6.09 gFID), whereas VTP improves consistently (5.46→2.81), demonstrating a key scalability advantage. On LAION text-to-image generation, VTP with CLIP loss shows a significant advantage in text rendering over both AE-only and SSL+AE tokenizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

VTP sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: self-supervised/contrastive representation learning (DINOv2, CLIP, MAE, iBOT) and latent-space design for diffusion models (VA-VAE, REPA-E, RAE, UniTok). Prior approaches to improving tokenizer latent spaces either align features post-hoc during diffusion training (REPA, DDT) or transplant frozen pretrained encoder features (RAE, AlignTok), leaving the tokenizer itself trained only on reconstruction. VTP departs from both by jointly optimizing contrastive, self-supervised, and reconstruction objectives *within* the tokenizer pre-training stage, and is the first to demonstrate that this joint paradigm—rather than any single representation alignment trick—unlocks a genuine scaling law for downstream generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes tokenizer pre-training from a solved engineering step into a first-class scaling problem with its own compute/parameter/data laws, which has direct practical implications for anyone building LDMs: the standard reconstruction-only recipe not only fails to scale but actively degrades generation quality at scale. Open questions include whether the specific loss combination (CLIP + DINOv2-style SSL + L1/perceptual) is optimal or whether other representation paradigms (e.g., next-token prediction, flow matching) could further improve the scaling curve, and whether these findings transfer to video, 3D, or higher-resolution generation. The paper does not explore multi-modal or autoregressive generation settings, leaving the generality of the scaling law partially untested.
