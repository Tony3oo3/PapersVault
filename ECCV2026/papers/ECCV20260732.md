## Problem

The quadratic complexity of attention in DiT-based video generators makes end-to-end training at ultra-high resolutions (e.g., 4K) prohibitively expensive, and existing training-free resolution extrapolation techniques developed for image generation fail to generalize to video due to complex 3D spatiotemporal dependencies. This paper asks whether pre-trained low-resolution video DiTs (e.g., 480P) can synthesize ultra-high-resolution videos without any additional training or adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260732/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces ultra-high-resolution videos at 1080P (1920×1088), 2K, 3K (3380×1920), or 4K resolution. It operates in a coarse-to-fine pipeline: a base video is first generated at the model's native resolution (e.g., 832×480 for Wan2.1-1.3B), then upsampled in pixel space, encoded to latent space via the VAE, and perturbed with Gaussian noise (SDEdit-style) before high-resolution denoising begins. The method is applied to pre-trained DiT backbones including Wan2.1 (1.3B and 14B) and LTX-Video, requiring no architectural modification or fine-tuning.

## Method

The core mechanism is an inward sliding-window attention that restricts each query token's receptive field to match the training resolution (e.g., a 480P window for a 480P model), with boundary windows dynamically shifted inward to maintain a consistent number of interactable key/value tokens. To counteract the repetitive patterns and lack of global coherence caused by local attention, a dual-path pipeline runs a full-attention branch in parallel and overrides the window branch's cross-attention outputs (λ=1), injecting global semantic guidance at the layers that directly source text-conditioned content. A step-wise cross-attention caching strategy recomputes full-branch features only every P steps (default P=2), and the unconditional CFG branch skips the full-attention path entirely, together achieving over 2× inference speedup at 1080P with negligible quality loss.

## Results

On VBench at 1080P using Wan2.1-1.3B, FreeSwim (w/ Cache, P=2) achieves the best overall score of 73.7%, surpassing the training-based CineScale w/ LoRA (71.5%) by 2.2 points and the training-free I-Max (71.5%) by 2.2 points. The method also leads on aesthetic quality (61.2%), imaging quality (65.3%), and overall consistency (24.9%). At 3K resolution (Table 2), FreeSwim with caching (P=5) reaches 70.1% overall, outperforming CineScale w/ LoRA (67.4%) by 2.7 points. On LTX-Video at 1080P, FreeSwim achieves 66.2% overall versus CineScale w/o LoRA's 63.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeSwim outperforms all five baselines on overall VBench score at 1080P with Wan2.1-1.3B: it beats Real-ESRGAN (72.3%) by 1.4 points, Upscale-A-Video (71.1%) by 2.6 points, I-Max (71.5%) by 2.2 points, HiFlow (69.4%) by 4.3 points, and CineScale w/ LoRA (71.5%) by 2.2 points. The gains are most pronounced on aesthetic quality (61.2% vs. 59.6% for I-Max and 56.7% for CineScale w/ LoRA) and imaging quality (65.3% vs. 63.9% for Real-ESRGAN), confirming that the inward sliding-window attention effectively captures fine-grained details. Against the training-based CineScale w/ LoRA, FreeSwim is entirely training-free yet still superior, and the caching strategy (P=2) further improves the score by 1.0 point over the no-cache variant while reducing inference time from 79 min to 55 min. At 3K resolution, FreeSwim with caching (P=5) achieves 70.1% overall, exceeding CineScale w/ LoRA (67.4%) by 2.7 points. On LTX-Video, FreeSwim (66.2%) surpasses CineScale w/o LoRA (63.3%) by 2.9 points, demonstrating cross-model generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free high-resolution generation line (I-Max, HiFlow, resolution-aware attention scaling) from the image domain to video, but critically identifies that these 2D techniques fundamentally fail for 3D video DiTs due to complex spatiotemporal dependencies. It repurposes the sliding-window attention concept from Swin/Neighborhood Attention into an 'inward' variant that preserves training-scale receptive fields for boundary tokens, and combines it with a dual-path cross-attention override inspired by the coarse-to-fine SDEdit pipeline. The feature caching strategy draws on recent diffusion-model acceleration work (DeepCache, Learning-to-Cache) but applies it specifically to the full-attention branch of a dual-path video DiT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeSwim provides the first practical training-free route to 4K video generation on modern DiT backbones (Wan2.1, LTX-Video), eliminating the need for expensive high-resolution fine-tuning data and LoRA adaptation. The >2× inference speedup from cross-attention caching makes ultra-high-res generation feasible on consumer hardware, and the method's architecture-agnostic design (applied to both 1.3B and 14B Wan models) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to even higher resolutions (8K+), to non-DiT video architectures, and whether the λ=1 override is truly optimal or merely the most robust default; the modest margins over training-based CineScale (w/ LoRA) also leave room for whether further architectural innovations could widen the gap.
