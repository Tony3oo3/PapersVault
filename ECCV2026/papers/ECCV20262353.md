## Problem

With over 25 publicly available pathology foundation models and no clear consensus on a superior model for any given dataset or task, selecting the right FM for WSI-based survival analysis remains an open challenge. Ensembling multiple FMs circumvents this issue but is computationally impractical at inference (3.5B+ parameters), and prior multi-teacher knowledge distillation methods were designed for natural image classification with softmax outputs, not for survival models that produce scalar risk scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262353/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes gigapixel whole slide images (~50,000×50,000 pixels) decomposed into patches, which are processed by frozen pathology FMs to yield patch-level embeddings. During training, five independent ABMIL adapters aggregate these embeddings into slide-level representations and scalar risk scores; at inference, only the student FM (UNI, ~304M parameters) and its single ABMIL adapter are required, producing a single survival risk score per slide. The output is a continuous risk value used for Cox proportional hazards ranking, not a categorical label.

## Method

SurvMILKD distills complementary survival knowledge from four teacher FMs (Virchow2, Prov-GigaPath, H-Optimus-0, CONCH) into a student FM (UNI) using a novel risk-aware distillation loss that converts scalar risk predictions into soft 4-bin ordinal distributions via differentiable sigmoid gates, enabling KL-divergence-based distillation without requiring categorical outputs. Adaptive teacher weighting assigns higher distillation weight to teachers with lower batch-wise Cox loss, and MLP-based feature translation modules map the student's slide-level representation into each teacher's latent space for reconstruction. The total training objective combines the student's Cox partial log-likelihood, the weighted risk-aware KD loss, MSE feature reconstruction, and a Cox loss on reconstructed teacher features. All FMs remain frozen throughout training; only the ABMIL adapters and translation modules are trainable, and at inference the framework reduces to a single FM plus one MIL adapter, matching the computational cost of the student alone.

## Results

SurvMILKD achieves the best C-index on 7 of 8 datasets, with headline scores of 74.59% (TCGA COAD), 74.47% (TCGA LGG), 68.32% (TCGA BRCA), and 67.38% (TCGA UCEC). On external CPTAC3 evaluation, it reaches 62.13% (HNSC) and 60.61% (KIRC), outperforming all individual FMs, the 5-FM ensemble, and all prior MKD and survival baselines. The only loss is on TCGA KIRC (70.96% vs. 71.11% for the ensemble), a margin of just 0.15 points. Improvements over the UNI baseline range from 1.02 to 11.57 C-index points, with the largest gain on COAD (+11.57).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurvMILKD outperforms all five individual FM backbones (UNI, Virchow2, Prov-GigaPath, H-Optimus-0, CONCH) and GPFM on at least 6 of 8 datasets, with the largest margin being +11.57 C-index over UNI on COAD (74.59 vs. 63.02). Against the 5-FM ensemble (3512.6M parameters), SurvMILKD wins on 7 of 8 datasets (e.g., +7.86 on COAD, +3.12 on LGG, +2.41 on CPTAC3 HNSC) while losing by only 0.15 on KIRC, yet requires only 303.9M parameters at inference. Among prior MKD methods adapted to survival (AVER, EBKD, AEKD, FitNetMKD, CAMKD), SurvMILKD achieves the best C-index on all 8 datasets, with the largest gaps over CAMKD being +5.23 on COAD and +1.77 on LGG. Against survival-specific MIL baselines (DeepAttnMISL, TransMIL, PatchGCN, WiKG, ILRA), SurvMILKD leads on 7 of 8 datasets, with the closest competition from PatchGCN on LGG (74.47 vs. 73.46) and DeepAttnMISL on UCEC (67.38 vs. 67.32). Statistical significance (one-sided paired t-test) confirms that SurvMILKD significantly outperforms both the UNI baseline and the ensemble on four datasets each at p<0.05.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established threads: pathology foundation models (UNI, Virchow2, Prov-GigaPath, H-Optimus-0, CONCH), attention-based MIL for WSI aggregation (ABMIL), and multi-teacher knowledge distillation originally developed for natural image classification (EBKD, AEKD, CAMKD, FitNet, AVER). The key conceptual departure is adapting logit-based MKD losses to the scalar risk-score outputs of Cox survival models, which requires the novel risk-aware distillation loss that converts continuous risk scores into differentiable ordinal distributions. The closest prior work, HistoMILKD, applies MKD to WSI classification but not to survival analysis, while GPFM and AM-Radio use MKD for self-supervised FM pre-training rather than task-specific supervised distillation. The paper thus carves new ground by being the first to integrate MIL adapters into an MKD pipeline specifically for WSI survival analysis, establishing the first survival MKD benchmark in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With over 25 pathology FMs and no consensus on a superior model for any given dataset, the model selection problem is increasingly urgent for clinical deployment; SurvMILKD offers a practical solution by distilling complementary knowledge into a single student model that matches or exceeds a 3.5B-parameter ensemble at 10× lower inference cost. The finding that absolute teacher performance does not predict distillation success (e.g., a weaker teacher can yield the best student on a given dataset) suggests that diverse risk-ranking patterns matter more than individual accuracy, opening questions about optimal teacher selection criteria. Limitations include the lack of multi-center out-of-distribution evaluation and the reliance on a fixed set of five FMs; future work on cross-modal distillation (genomic, textual) and ranking-agreement analysis between FMs could further clarify the dynamics of knowledge transfer in this setting.
