## Problem

Point-based drag editing suffers from inherent ambiguity due to sparse point-pair conditions, while existing region-based methods (EditGAN, RegionDrag) either treat masks as shallow loss signals or rely on latent copy-paste that produces inconsistent boundaries and struggles with complex deformations. No large-scale dataset or training framework existed to deeply integrate image and mask modalities for precise region-based drag.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260416/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a source RGB image, a source region mask with labeled editing regions, and a target region mask with consistent label IDs, all encoded to latent space via a VAE encoder. The masks can be complete (all semantic segments separated) or incomplete (only a few editing regions highlighted, rest filled with gray), simulating sparse real-world user input. The output is a single edited image produced in one forward pass through the denoising pipeline, with the target latent being the only variable undergoing noising while all conditional inputs remain noise-free.

## Method

ICRDrag builds on Next-DiT with an in-context learning formulation where source image, source mask, and target mask serve as unified noise-free context tokens, and only the target image latent is denoised via flow matching. Two novel attention regularizations are introduced: Image-Mask Attention Consistency (IMAC) enforces that each target-image patch's attention over the source image mirrors the corresponding target-mask patch's attention over the source mask, grounding visual generation in spatial structure; Source-Target Attention Correspondence (STAC) maximizes the trace of the product of bidirectional attention matrices between source and target region patches, enforcing mutual correspondence. Modality-specific LoRA modules in the feed-forward networks prevent feature confusion between texture-rich image tokens and sparse structural mask tokens. A two-stage curriculum training strategy first trains on complete region masks (60k steps) then adapts to incomplete masks with random dilation (2k steps), progressively increasing task difficulty to better handle sparse user inputs.

## Results

On the PRD benchmark, ICRDrag achieves the best scores across all five metrics: MSE 0.0735, LPIPS 0.1610, SSIM 0.6284, MD(RegionDrag) 3.66, and MD(DragLoRA) 22.34. The closest competitor, GoodDrag, trails by 18.5% on MSE (0.0902), 8.7% on LPIPS (0.1761), and 3.0% on SSIM (0.6094). In the user study on DragBench, ICRDrag is preferred by 46.8% of users for realism, 55.7% for fidelity, and 52.1% for region accuracy, substantially outperforming both GoodDrag and RegionDrag on all three criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICRDrag outperforms all eight baselines on every metric in Table 1. The strongest point-based competitor, GoodDrag, is closest on MSE (0.0902 vs. 0.0735) and MD(RegionDrag) (3.70 vs. 3.66), but ICRDrag still leads by a clear margin on LPIPS (0.1610 vs. 0.1761) and SSIM (0.6284 vs. 0.6094). The primary region-based baseline, RegionDrag, is notably weaker: it scores 0.0977 MSE and 8.02 MD(RegionDrag), roughly 33% worse on MSE and 119% worse on MD than ICRDrag, confirming the limitations of its latent copy-paste strategy. Among one-step methods, Inpaint4Drag achieves the best SSIM (0.6102) among baselines but still trails ICRDrag by 3.1%. In the DragBench user study (Table 2), ICRDrag dominates: it is preferred 46.8% of the time for realism vs. 28.8% for GoodDrag and 24.4% for RegionDrag, and 52.1% for region accuracy vs. only 12.8% for GoodDrag (which cannot accept region masks) and 35.2% for RegionDrag.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant point-based drag paradigm (DragDiffusion, SDE-Drag, GoodDrag, etc.) by adopting region-based drag, which prior work (EditGAN, RegionDrag) has shown to reduce ambiguity but has addressed only with shallow mask integration—either as a loss signal (EditGAN) or a latent copy-paste guide (RegionDrag). ICRDrag instead treats source and target masks as in-context conditioning tokens within a DiT (Next-DiT) architecture, and introduces two attention-regularization losses (IMAC and STAC) that explicitly enforce spatial correspondence between image and mask modalities and between source and target regions. The curriculum training strategy (complete → incomplete masks) is a novel training paradigm not seen in prior drag methods, and the PRD dataset fills a gap where no large-scale paired-mask dataset existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Region-based drag is a more natural and precise editing interface than point-based drag, and this paper provides the first large-scale dataset (PRD, 287K samples) and benchmark (PRDBench) purpose-built for it, lowering the barrier for future work. The in-context learning framing—where masks are consumed as context tokens rather than optimized against—opens a path toward more flexible, multi-region, and compositional drag editing without per-edit optimization. Open questions include whether the attention regularization transfers to other DiT backbones, how the method scales to very large deformations or multi-object scenes, and whether the video-derived dataset (OpenVid) introduces distributional biases that limit generalization to diverse real-world images.
