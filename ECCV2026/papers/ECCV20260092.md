## Problem

Existing multi-shot video generation methods rely on bidirectional architectures that require all prompts upfront and incur quadratic attention costs, making them non-interactive and prohibitively slow (e.g., ~25 minutes for 240 frames). This prevents users from dynamically guiding narratives at runtime and rules out real-time streaming synthesis of multi-shot sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260092/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts streaming text prompts one shot at a time during inference, allowing users to adjust content, style, or characters mid-narrative. Internally, it conditions on a sparse subset of historical frames (6-frame budget) sampled dynamically from preceding shots rather than the full history. Output is a multi-shot video at 832×480 resolution, generated shot-by-shot at 16 FPS on a single H200 GPU, with each shot produced chunk-by-chunk (3 latent frames per chunk) via a 4-step causal denoising process.

## Method

ShotStream reformulates multi-shot synthesis as autoregressive next-shot generation: a bidirectional teacher (fine-tuned from Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B) is first trained to predict the next shot conditioned on sparse context frames injected via temporal token concatenation with per-shot captions, then distilled into a 4-step causal student via Distribution Matching Distillation. To maintain visual coherence in the causal setting, a dual-cache memory mechanism stores global context frames (inter-shot) and local generated frames (intra-shot), with a RoPE discontinuity indicator (temporal rotation angle Θ_t = φt + kθ) explicitly separating the two caches to eliminate temporal ambiguity. To prevent error accumulation, a two-stage progressive distillation strategy is employed: Stage 1 (intra-shot self-forcing) trains the causal generator to roll out the current shot chunk-by-chunk conditioned on ground-truth historical shots, while Stage 2 (inter-shot self-forcing) extends this to full multi-shot rollouts where the model conditions on its own previously generated shots, bridging the train-test gap.

## Results

ShotStream achieves 15.95 FPS on a single H200 GPU while attaining the highest scores across all evaluated metrics: intra-shot subject consistency (0.825), inter-shot subject consistency (0.654), inter-shot background consistency (0.645), transition control (0.978), and text alignment (0.234). Compared to the best bidirectional multi-shot model (EchoShot at 0.643 FPS), ShotStream delivers over a 25× throughput improvement while simultaneously improving inter-shot subject consistency from 0.392 to 0.654. Among causal autoregressive baselines, ShotStream leads in inter-shot consistency (Sub. 0.654 vs. 0.594 for LongLive) and transition control (0.978 vs. 0.715 for Infinity-RoPE) at comparable inference speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShotStream outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the three bidirectional multi-shot models (Mask2DiT, EchoShot, CineTrans), ShotStream achieves a >25× speedup (15.95 vs. 0.149–0.643 FPS) while substantially improving inter-shot subject consistency (0.654 vs. 0.392–0.612) and transition control (0.978 vs. 0.513–0.664). Among the four causal autoregressive baselines, the most notable gaps are in inter-shot consistency: ShotStream's inter-shot subject score of 0.654 exceeds LongLive (0.594) by 10% and Infinity-RoPE (0.453) by 44%, and its inter-shot background score of 0.645 surpasses LongLive (0.565) by 14%. Transition control shows the largest relative advantage, with ShotStream at 0.978 versus Infinity-RoPE's 0.715 and Self Forcing's 0.633. In the user study (54 participants, 24 prompts), ShotStream is preferred by 87.7% for visual consistency, 76.2% for prompt following, and 83.1% for visual quality, confirming the quantitative advantages in perceptual terms.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShotStream sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: bidirectional multi-shot video generation (HoloCine, LCT, Mask2DiT, EchoShot) and causal autoregressive long video generation (CausVid, Self Forcing, LongLive, Rolling Forcing). It departs from the bidirectional camp by reformulating multi-shot synthesis as an autoregressive next-shot prediction task, enabling streaming prompt inputs and shot-by-shot generation. On the causal side, it extends single-scene autoregressive models to multi-shot narratives by introducing a dual-cache memory mechanism (global + local) with a RoPE discontinuity indicator, and a two-stage progressive distillation strategy that bridges the train-test gap across shot boundaries. The distillation backbone (Distribution Matching Distillation) and the self-forcing paradigm are inherited directly from CausVid and Self Forcing, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShotStream demonstrates that real-time (16 FPS, single GPU) interactive multi-shot video generation is feasible, a capability that was previously limited to slow bidirectional pipelines (e.g., HoloCine at ~25 min for 240 frames). The 25× throughput gain and the ability to accept streaming prompts at runtime open a practical path toward interactive storytelling tools. Key limitations remain: visual artifacts under highly complex scenes due to the small 1.3B backbone, and further acceleration is needed for truly seamless interactivity. Open questions include whether the dual-cache + RoPE-discontinuity design generalizes to larger backbones and longer narratives, and whether sparse attention or attention-sink techniques can push latency even lower without sacrificing consistency.
