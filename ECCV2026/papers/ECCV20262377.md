## Problem

Prompt learning in Vision-Language Models faces a generalization dilemma: the small number of learnable prompt parameters makes optimization highly sensitive, causing prompts to converge to sharp minima that overfit seen distributions while failing to generalize to unseen classes. Existing sharpness-aware optimizers (SAM, F-SAM, SAGM) address flatness but do not simultaneously enforce both low empirical risk and gradient alignment with the batch-specific (exploratory) component, leaving a gap between exploitation and exploration that SAMPLe targets.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt composed of N learnable token vectors concatenated with a class name as input, and outputs a class label via cosine similarity between frozen CLIP image and text embeddings. The VLM backbone remains entirely frozen; only the prompt tokens are optimized. SAMPLe is a plug-in optimizer that slots into any existing prompt learning framework (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, TCP, Co-Prompt) without modifying the model architecture or the prompt parameterization, requiring only the addition of a perturbation radius ρ, an alignment coefficient α, and an EMA decay factor λ.

## Method

SAMPLe redefines the SAM perturbed point as θ + ε* − α∇^B L(θ;D), where ∇^B is the batch-specific gradient (the mini-batch gradient with its projection onto the full-batch gradient removed), rather than the standard θ + ε*. This single modification decomposes the objective into two competing forces: an exploitation term that aligns the perturbed-point gradient with the ERM gradient (preserving seen-class accuracy) and an exploration term that drives the perturbed-point gradient orthogonal to the full-batch gradient (seeking flatter minima). The full-batch gradient is approximated via an exponentially moving average of mini-batch gradients, avoiding the cost of a full pass. The paper proves that under bounded-gradient and K-Lipschitz conditions with decaying learning rates η_t = η_0/√t, the objective converges at rate O(log T / √T), and provides a gradient-orthogonality analysis showing the exploration constraint is preserved throughout training as the EMA approximation tightens.

## Results

On the base-to-new generalization benchmark (averaged over 11 datasets), SAMPLe achieves the highest harmonic mean (HM) across all five prompt-learning frameworks: CoOp 78.88, CoCoOp 79.51, MaPLe 80.28, CoPrompt 82.02, and TCP 81.95, consistently outperforming SAGM (the strongest prior sharpness-aware optimizer) by 0.5–1.2 HM points. In cross-dataset zero-shot transfer (ImageNet → 10 unseen datasets), SAMPLe lifts average accuracy by 1.0–1.5 points (e.g., CoOp 63.88→65.85, TCP 66.29→67.53). In cross-domain zero-shot evaluation on four ImageNet variants, SAMPLe improves average accuracy by 1.1–1.4 points (e.g., CoCoOp 59.90→60.47, TCP 60.51→61.90), with the largest gains on the most challenging targets ImageNet-A and ImageNet-R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMPLe is compared against three sharpness-aware optimizers (SAM, F-SAM, SAGM) and four prompt-learning architectures (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, CoPrompt, TCP) across three evaluation protocols. Against the strongest prior optimizer SAGM, SAMPLe wins on HM in every setting: +1.23 on CoOp (78.88 vs 77.65), +1.23 on CoCoOp (79.51 vs 78.28), +0.32 on MaPLe (80.28 vs 79.96), +0.53 on CoPrompt (82.02 vs 81.49), and +0.55 on TCP (81.95 vs 81.40). Against vanilla SAM, the gains are substantially larger (e.g., CoOp HM 78.88 vs 71.66, a 7.2-point improvement), confirming that the gradient-orthogonality and batch-specific alignment mechanisms are critical. In cross-dataset transfer, CoOp+SAMPLe (65.85 avg) surpasses the standalone CoCoOp baseline (65.74) and MaPLe (66.30), while TCP+SAMPLe (67.53) outperforms CoPrompt (67.00) without SAMPLe. In cross-domain evaluation, CoCoOp+SAMPLe (60.47 avg) edges out MaPLe (60.28) and CoPrompt (60.42), and TCP+SAMPLe (61.90) achieves the highest overall average. The consistent pattern is that SAMPLe improves New-class accuracy more than Base-class accuracy, indicating it primarily addresses the generalization gap rather than sacrificing seen-distribution performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) lineage—specifically F-SAM's correction of full-batch gradient bias and SAGM's gradient alignment objective—into the VLM prompt learning setting. It departs from prior SAM variants (SAM, F-SAM, SAGM) and the recent GCSCoOP by identifying that none simultaneously satisfy both exploitation (low ERM loss) and exploration (flat minima) constraints at each iteration, and proposes a dual-objective formulation where the perturbed-point gradient is driven orthogonal to the full-batch gradient while remaining aligned with the mini-batch ERM gradient. The method is positioned as a model-agnostic, plug-in optimizer applicable across multiple prompt learning architectures (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, TCP, Co-Prompt) rather than a new prompt architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As prompt learning becomes the dominant paradigm for adapting frozen VLMs to downstream tasks, the optimizer used to train the few learnable prompt tokens critically determines whether the resulting prompts generalize or overfit. SAMPLe's plug-in nature means it can be dropped into any existing prompt learning pipeline without architectural changes, and its consistent (if modest) gains across five architectures and eleven datasets suggest broad practical utility. Limitations include reliance on an EMA approximation of the full-batch gradient (introducing noise sensitive to λ), evaluation restricted to CLIP-based VLMs, and convergence guarantees that depend on standard smoothness/boundedness assumptions. Open questions include whether the dual-constraint framework transfers to other parameter-efficient adaptation methods (LoRA, adapters) or to non-CLIP VLMs, and whether the orthogonality constraint remains beneficial at larger prompt budgets where the parameter subspace is less constrained.
