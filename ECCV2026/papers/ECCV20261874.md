## Problem

Existing LLM-based CT report generation methods feed all visual tokens uniformly into the LLM, implicitly assuming equal informativeness across the entire 3D volume. This is fundamentally misaligned with radiological practice, where clinicians contrast the current scan against an internal normal reference to isolate deviations from redundant anatomy. Without such a reference-guided contrastive signal, the LLM must process massive background anatomy equally with sparse pathological findings, leading to overly generic reports that miss fine-grained clinical details.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D chest CT volume pre-processed into ten anatomical regions at 1×1×3 mm voxel spacing, paired with a normal reference CT volume selected from a dedicated pool of scans whose reports indicate no abnormalities. Additionally, 18 predicted disease-class labels (from a frozen 3D ResNet-18 classifier) are converted into short textual prompts and appended to the LLM input. The output is a free-text diagnostic report generated autoregressively by LLaMA-2-7B, with the difference-derived visual prefix tokens prepended before the original visual tokens to condition generation.

## Method

DiffVP introduces a normal reference CT as a visual prior and extracts high-level semantic discrepancies in a shared latent space rather than performing pixel-level subtraction. A shared 3D ResNet-18 encoder and Q-Former-style resampler project both target and reference volumes into aligned latent token sequences. The Hierarchical Difference Extractor then computes two complementary signals: a Global Delta via a learnable query interacting with both token sets through a Transformer layer, and a Local Delta via distance-weighted aggregation of token-wise residuals that emphasizes the most deviant regions. The Difference-to-Prompt Generator fuses these two signals through an MLP and expands them into 16 learnable prefix tokens in the LLM embedding space, which are prepended to the input sequence as a structured soft prompt. The LLM (LLaMA-2-7B with LoRA) is trained with a combined objective of negative log-likelihood for report generation and binary cross-entropy over 18 disease categories, enabling the difference prompts to implicitly suppress invariant anatomy while amplifying diagnostically relevant visual evidence.

## Results

On RadGenome-ChestCT, DiffVP achieves BLEU-1/2/3/4 of 58.16/48.74/40.93/34.28 and METEOR 47.40, improving the average BLEU-1–4 over the previous SOTA (Reg2RG) by +10.98 points. On CTRG-Chest-548K, it reaches BLEU-1/2/3/4 of 50.37/46.57/41.94/37.57 and METEOR 50.44, a +4.36 average BLEU gain over Reg2RG. For clinical efficacy on RadGenome-ChestCT, DiffVP attains an F1 of 0.421 (Recall 0.496), nearly doubling the F1 of the next-best method (Reg2RG, 0.253). The only metric where DiffVP trails Reg2RG is ROUGE-L (32.32 vs. 36.65 on RadGenome; 45.00 vs. 47.76 on CTRG), which the authors attribute to Reg2RG's reliance on longer shared n-gram subsequences rather than precise clinical phrasing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffVP outperforms all five baselines on BLEU-1 through BLEU-4 and METEOR on both datasets. Against the strongest prior method Reg2RG (IEEE TMI 2025), DiffVP gains +10.91 BLEU-1 and +9.41 BLEU-4 on RadGenome-ChestCT, and +0.74 BLEU-1 and +5.53 BLEU-4 on CTRG-Chest-548K. The two foundation models (RadFM, M3D) trail DiffVP by roughly 10–15 BLEU-1 points, confirming that generalist radiology models do not match task-specific differential prompting. On clinical efficacy (RadGenome-ChestCT), DiffVP's F1 of 0.421 and Recall of 0.496 substantially exceed Reg2RG (F1 0.253, Recall 0.181) and all other baselines, though Reg2RG retains a slight Precision edge (0.423 vs. 0.366). The sole metric where DiffVP underperforms Reg2RG is ROUGE-L on both datasets (32.32 vs. 36.65; 45.00 vs. 47.76), which the authors interpret as reflecting Reg2RG's tendency to reproduce longer shared report subsequences rather than more precise clinical descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LLM-based CT report generation paradigm (R2GenGPT, Reg2RG, MedVInT) that feeds all visual tokens uniformly into an LLM, instead introducing a normal reference scan as a contrastive prior. It repurposes Q-Former-style resampling and prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021) in a novel differential-prompting configuration: a hierarchical difference extractor computes global and local semantic discrepancies in a shared latent space, and a difference-to-prompt generator projects these into learnable prefix tokens. Conceptually, it extends 2D difference-aware captioning (Park et al., Lyu et al.) and longitudinal residual methods (Yun et al., Song et al.) to the 3D volumetric setting, while explicitly avoiding pixel-level subtraction in favor of latent-space semantic comparison to handle inter-subject anatomical variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The differential prompting paradigm addresses a fundamental misalignment between how LLMs process visual tokens (uniformly) and how radiologists actually reason (comparatively against a normal prior), offering a principled mechanism to suppress anatomical redundancy and amplify sparse pathological signals without requiring explicit lesion segmentation. The large CE F1 gain (0.421 vs. 0.253 for Reg2RG) suggests the approach meaningfully improves clinical sensitivity, not just n-gram overlap. Open questions include: whether the simple 'no-abnormality-in-report' reference selection strategy is optimal, whether the method generalizes beyond chest CT to other body regions or modalities, and whether the approach degrades gracefully when no suitable normal reference is available for a given patient.
