## Problem

Ego-only 3D object detection suffers from limited perspective and occlusions, while collaborative perception achieves superior accuracy but incurs high communication costs and accumulated pose errors that hinder real-world deployment. No prior work has explored transferring the dense multi-agent knowledge of collaborative perception into a standalone ego-only model that operates without any inter-agent communication at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261222/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the student detector consumes only the ego vehicle's LiDAR point cloud, which is voxelized into a pillar tensor of shape C×P×G (channels × points-per-pillar × global pillars) and ultimately regresses N_b 3D bounding boxes of dimension 7 (center, size, orientation). During training, three pre-trained collaborative teacher networks simultaneously receive the concatenated multi-agent point cloud (ego + CAV + Infrastructure) and produce dense BEV feature maps that serve as distillation targets, while the student processes only the ego subset through the same architectural skeleton.

## Method

The M2S framework distills knowledge from three collaborative teachers (AttFuse, V2X-ViT, CoSDH) into a single ego-only student via three complementary modules. Multi-Level Feature Enhancement (MLFE) applies serial CBAM-style attention along channel, pillar, and global dimensions to densify the student's sparse 3D features before distillation. Auxiliary Point Cloud Reconstruction (APCR) bridges the point-cloud domain gap by decomposing dense reconstruction into voxel occupancy prediction (BCE loss) and point offset estimation (L1 loss) within ground-truth bounding boxes. Multi-Teacher Contrastive Distillation (MTCD) partitions BEV feature maps into non-overlapping patches and enforces spatial and channel-level contrastive alignment between student and teacher features, with per-teacher confidence weights computed via softmax over Smooth-L1 localization losses to adaptively prioritize more accurate teachers; a global KL-divergence term further aligns the student's multi-layer BEV features with the weighted teacher fusion.

## Results

On the V2XSet test set, M2S achieves up to 8.64% improvement in 3D mAP@0.7 (AttFuse: 58.06→66.70), 8.10% for Where2comm (62.18→70.28), and 6.59% for CoAlign (65.07→71.66), all without any communication overhead. On V2V4Real test set, PillarNeSt gains the largest improvement of +9.06 in AP@0.7 (16.18→25.24), while on DAIR-V2X validation, AttFuse improves AP@0.7 by +6.61 (33.25→39.86). M2S also reduces FLOPs by ~35% and doubles inference FPS compared to collaborative perception, while eliminating the 260–282 ms communication latency entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M2S consistently outperforms all six baselines on the V2XSet test set. The largest gains are over AttFuse (+8.64% AP@0.7) and Where2comm (+8.10%), while CoAlign benefits by +6.59% and CoSDH by +4.45%. The ego-only PillarNeSt baseline also improves by +4.33%, confirming M2S is not limited to collaborative-architecture students. On real-world V2V4Real, improvements are more variable: PillarNeSt gains +9.06 AP@0.7 and AttFuse +5.39, but Where2comm actually drops by −1.48 on the validation set (though still gains +1.62 on test). On DAIR-V2X, gains are smaller but positive across all models (AttFuse +6.61, V2X-ViT +4.69, PillarNeSt +4.71 in AP@0.7), with one marginal exception (V2X-ViT AP@0.3 drops by −0.06). Overall, M2S wins or ties in the vast majority of comparisons, with the strongest and most consistent improvements on the simulated V2XSet benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by proposing the C2E (Co-Perception to Eo-Perception) paradigm, which repurposes the knowledge distillation literature (MKD-Cooper, DiscoNet) to transfer multi-agent collaborative knowledge into a single-agent model, eliminating communication at inference. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant line of work on making collaboration more efficient (Where2comm, How2comm, What2comm) by asking a different question: rather than 'how to communicate less,' it asks 'how to not need to communicate at all.' The M2S framework combines multi-teacher adaptive distillation, contrastive feature alignment, and auxiliary point cloud reconstruction—each drawing on established techniques (CBAM-style attention, Sparse2Dense-style reconstruction, InfoNCE-style contrastive loss)—but their specific combination for bridging the multi-to-single agent domain gap is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The C2E paradigm offers a practically compelling path for deploying high-performance 3D detection in real-world autonomous driving, where communication infrastructure is unreliable, latency-sensitive, or simply unavailable. The consistent gains across six architectures and three datasets (simulated and real-world) strengthen the case that this is a generalizable training strategy rather than a dataset-specific trick. Key limitations include the LiDAR-only scope, the reliance on ground-truth labels for adaptive teacher weighting (preventing fully unsupervised deployment), and the increased training cost with multiple teachers. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to multi-modal sensing (LiDAR-camera), whether GT-free teacher selection is feasible, and whether the approach generalizes to other collaborative tasks beyond detection.
