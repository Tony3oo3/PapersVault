## Problem

Existing agentic image restoration frameworks suffer from two structural limitations: (1) greedy step-wise search strategies that prioritize immediate rewards and fail to balance exploration and exploitation over long horizons, often causing irreversible damage (e.g., over-smoothing before super-resolution); and (2) episodic amnesia, where computationally expensive search trajectories are discarded after each image, forcing the agent to redundantly re-explore similar degradation patterns from scratch.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single degraded RGB image exhibiting complex, coupled degradations (e.g., noise + blur + compression + rain) and produces a restored RGB image. At inference time, no paired ground truth is available; the system diagnoses the input via a fine-tuned MLLM (DepictQA) to extract an 8-dimensional degradation fingerprint, then orchestrates a sequence of pre-trained restoration tools (X-Restormer, HAT, SwinIR, DiffBIR, DehazeFormer, etc.) whose order and selection are determined by the planning process. Quality is assessed via a hybrid no-reference reward combining HPSv2 aesthetics with NIQE, MANIQA, MUSIQ, and CLIP-IQA.

## Method

SEAR formulates restoration as a sequential decision-making problem and employs a dual-process architecture inspired by Kahneman's System 1/System 2 theory. The Deliberate Planner (System 2) performs hierarchical reasoning: an LLM (GPT-4o) generates a macro-level task agenda encoding restoration priorities, which constrains a Pruning-Aware Monte Carlo Tree Search (P-MCTS) that optimizes tool sequences over the full trajectory horizon using UCT selection, agenda-constrained expansion, look-ahead rollouts, and backpropagation of a hybrid no-reference reward. A final MLLM-based pairwise tournament among top-4 candidates provides perceptual safety against metric exploitation. The Intuitive Executor (System 1) maintains a self-evolving episodic memory of trajectory-fingerprint-reward tuples; when a new image's degradation fingerprint matches a stored entry within an L2 threshold, the system retrieves and executes the trajectory directly (gated by a reliability threshold ζ), bypassing full P-MCTS. Successful P-MCTS trajectories are distilled into memory, progressively amortizing search costs and shifting the system from deliberation-driven to memory-guided execution over time.

## Results

On the MiO100 synthetic benchmark (Groups A–C), SEAR achieves PSNR of 21.80, 22.13, and 20.61 dB respectively, outperforming the strongest agentic baselines (AgenticIR, 4KAgent) by 0.16–0.42 dB and all AiO models by 0.4–1.5 dB. SEAR also leads on LPIPS (0.302, 0.289, 0.417) and MUSIQ (61.29, 60.65, 52.73) in most groups. On the real-world dataset (100 pairs from 7 sources), SEAR attains 24.41 dB PSNR and 0.337 LPIPS, surpassing all 8 baselines on pixel fidelity and perceptual similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEAR consistently outperforms all six AiO baselines (AirNet, PromptIR, MiOIR, DA-CLIP, InstructIR, AutoDIR) by 0.4–1.5 dB PSNR across all three MiO100 groups, with the largest gaps on Group C (out-of-distribution degradations) where AiO models degrade most severely. Against the two agentic baselines, SEAR gains 0.16–0.42 dB PSNR over AgenticIR and 0.16–0.30 dB over 4KAgent, while also achieving the best LPIPS in every group. However, SEAR does not uniformly dominate on all perceptual metrics: 4KAgent edges out SEAR on CLIP-IQA in Groups B (0.5226 vs 0.5134) and C (0.4737 vs 0.4420) and on MUSIQ in Group B (61.48 vs 60.65), suggesting 4KAgent's multi-agent design retains a slight advantage on certain aesthetic dimensions. On the real-world dataset, SEAR leads on PSNR (24.41 vs 23.93 AgenticIR, 23.63 4KAgent) and LPIPS (0.337 vs 0.373, 0.357), though InstructIR achieves higher PSNR (26.15) at the cost of over-smoothing and worse perceptual scores. In terms of efficiency, SEAR uses 8.15 tool calls with 1.98 min average time, compared to AgenticIR's 6.11 calls / 1.09 min and 4KAgent's 8.26 calls / 2.55 min, indicating a modest overhead over AgenticIR but a 22.4% speedup over 4KAgent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agentic image restoration paradigm initiated by AgenticIR [64] and 4KAgent [66], which use LLMs to orchestrate sequences of pre-trained restoration tools. It departs from these prior agentic systems in two key ways: replacing myopic greedy tool selection with Pruning-Aware Monte Carlo Tree Search (P-MCTS) for long-horizon trajectory optimization, and introducing a self-evolving episodic memory that persists successful restoration trajectories across inference episodes. The dual-process (System 1 / System 2) framing from cognitive science provides the architectural principle linking the fast memory-driven executor to the slow deliberative planner, a conceptual lens not present in prior agentic IR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-evolving memory mechanism offers a practical pathway to amortize expensive search costs in deployment settings where similar degradation patterns recur, shifting the system from search-driven to memory-guided execution over time. However, the approach remains constrained by a fixed tool library and depends on accurate MLLM-based degradation diagnosis for state fingerprint construction, limiting its applicability to truly out-of-distribution or novel degradations. Open questions include whether the memory mechanism scales to open-ended tool sets, whether more discriminative fingerprints can reduce the 33–59% of cases still routed to full P-MCTS search, and whether the cold-start overhead is acceptable in latency-sensitive applications.
