## Problem

Frame-based photometric stereo requires many input images, leading to long capture times and high data bandwidth, while event-based photometric stereo is fast and data-efficient but is limited to 720p spatial resolution due to event sensor hardware constraints. The paper addresses how to achieve high-resolution, high-quality surface normal reconstruction with the data efficiency of event cameras by exploiting the complementary spatial and temporal resolutions of a frame-event hybrid camera.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262456/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes exactly 3 long-exposure RGB frames (2048×2448) and a concurrent low-resolution mono event stream (720×1280) captured under a continuously rotating directional light source, with both streams sharing a single view via a beam splitter. The frames integrate irradiance over T/3 time windows (sparse in time), while events are asynchronous and provide instantaneous irradiance constraints at super-pixel locations. Output is a high-resolution surface normal map aligned to the frame sensor grid, along with per-pixel diffuse albedo and specular parameters (Fresnel color F0 and roughness r), all recovered in a self-supervised manner without ground-truth supervision.

## Method

DualResPS formulates a dual-resolution image model where each long-exposure frame is represented by an equivalent directional light vector (the time-integral of the swept illumination), and non-ideal factors—cast shadow, attached shadow, and specular reflection—are explicitly handled. A coordinate-based MLP outputs surface normal, diffuse albedo, and specular parameters, which are rendered through a differentiable physically-based BRDF (specular-glossiness with F0 and roughness) to produce predicted frame integrals and instantaneous irradiances. Cast shadow masks are detected from events via a modified event interval profile and assigned to nearby frame pixels, while specular parameters are constrained by event streams at adjacent super-pixels. A pixel-wise asynchronous importance sampling scheme allocates more integration samples to high-irradiance and high-fluctuation regions, improving rendering efficiency. Training is self-supervised, combining an L1 frame loss on integrated intensity, a log-ratio event loss matching predicted irradiance changes to triggered event polarities, and a total-variation warm-up term.

## Results

On the DiLiGenT semi-real dataset, DualResPS achieves an average MAE of 7.36° using only 3 long-exposure frames plus an event stream (18.1% data rate), outperforming the best self-supervised frame-based method LL22a (7.70° at 100% data rate) while matching or approaching supervised FramePS methods (Uni-MS-PS 6.95°, LINO 7.26°). On DiLiGenT-Pi, DualResPS achieves lower MAE than both EventPS and LL22a across diverse materials (metallic, specular, rough, translucent). On real prototype data, DualResPS surpasses both EventPS-OP and LL22a (8 frames), with cast shadow artifacts substantially reduced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualResPS (7.36° avg MAE) outperforms the best self-supervised frame-based baseline LL22a (7.70°) by 0.34° while using only 18.1% of the data (3 frames + events vs. 23 frames). Against supervised FramePS methods, DualResPS is competitive: it slightly trails Uni-MS-PS (6.95°) and SDM-UniPS (7.13°) but beats LINO (7.26°), all while being self-supervised and requiring no training on large datasets. The comparison with EventPS (19.99°) is the most dramatic: DualResPS reduces MAE by over 12° (a 63% improvement) at only 3.6× the data rate, confirming that the sparse frame input provides the spatial resolution that event-only methods lack. On DiLiGenT-Pi with diverse materials, DualResPS achieves lower MAE than both EventPS and LL22a, validating that the dual-resolution strategy generalizes beyond the DiLiGenT object set. On real prototype data, DualResPS beats both EventPS-OP and LL22a (8 frames), with particularly notable reduction of cast shadow artifacts on the STAR object.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualResPS sits at the intersection of event-based photometric stereo (EventPS [44], PS-EIP [13]) and neural inverse rendering for shape recovery (LL22a [15], DANI-Net [18]). It adopts the equivalent directional light formulation from patch-based UPS [3] to handle long-exposure frames under a rotating light, and modifies the event interval profile [13] for cast-shadow detection. The key departure from prior work is the dual-resolution input configuration: rather than relying solely on high-temporal-resolution events (limited to 720p) or many high-spatial-resolution frames, the paper combines exactly three sparse long-exposure frames with a concurrent event stream, using a beam-splitter hybrid camera to share a single view. This is framed as a spatiotemporally dual-resolution downsampling scheme for the irradiance field, a formulation not present in either the frame-based or event-based photometric stereo literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DualResPS achieves surface normal quality comparable to 23-frame self-supervised methods while consuming only ~18% of the data bandwidth, which is critical for high-speed and bandwidth-constrained 3D sensing applications. The explicit handling of cast shadow (via events) and specular reflection (via event-constrained reflectance parameters) addresses two major failure modes of sparse-frame photometric stereo. However, the method does not handle inter-reflections, is sensitive to event camera calibration parameters, and the real-world validation is limited to three 3D-printed objects with acknowledged alignment and fabrication errors. Open questions include whether end-to-end learned priors can replace the current per-object optimization, whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, and how it scales as hybrid camera hardware matures beyond the current 720p event / 2K frame resolution gap.
