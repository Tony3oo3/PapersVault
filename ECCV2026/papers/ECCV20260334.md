## Problem

Existing controllable generation frameworks for joint audio-video synthesis are restricted to video-only control, either optimizing visual conditioning while under-modeling acoustic signals or vice versa. This decoupled approach makes coherent cross-modal alignment difficult and prevents users from simultaneously controlling character identity, voice timbre, and structural layout (depth/pose) within a single unified generative process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a structured text prompt with explicit [VISUAL] and [SPEECH] segments, an optional reference image for identity, an optional reference audio clip for timbre, and optional per-frame depth maps or pose sequences for structural guidance. The reference image latent is prepended to the structural sequence, and the reference audio latent is prepended to a silence placeholder, with binary masks distinguishing reference frames (0) from generative frames (1). Output is a temporally synchronized video (t×h×w×3) and audio waveform (t×d), produced via a two-stage inference that first generates at half resolution (h/2×w/2) then upscales to full resolution with a distilled LoRA and 3-step truncated denoising.

## Method

MMControl introduces a Multi-Modal Control Unit (MMCU) that tokenizes heterogeneous control signals—reference image latents, reference audio latents, and depth/pose sequences—into synchronized representations with binary masks, providing a unified interface for the joint diffusion process. The core architecture is a Dual-Stream Bypass: parallel trainable visual and acoustic branches (each containing Self-Attention, Text Cross-Attention, and FFN layers initialized from the frozen LTX-2 19B backbone) are interleaved at even-numbered layers, injecting modality-specific contextual hints into the main DiT via gated residual connections with zero-initialized output projectors to preserve pre-trained priors. At inference, Modality-Specific Guidance Scaling applies independent scaling factors γ_v and γ_a to the visual and acoustic bypass outputs, enabling users to decouple identity fidelity from timbre fidelity. Training uses flow-matching with classifier-free guidance on 30K Hallo3 samples (7,200 steps, 4×H200 GPUs, ~12 hours), with both visual and acoustic conditions dropped at 0.1 probability for flexible composition.

## Results

On the audio-driven joint generation benchmark (Table 1), MMControl achieves the highest Text CLIP similarity (0.2546), Subject DINO similarity (0.8948), and Sync-C score (2.716) among all compared methods, while also attaining the best Aesthetic Quality (0.5461) and Motion Smoothness (0.9954). On structural depth control (Table 2), MMControl achieves a Mean MAE×100 of 4.52, substantially lower than VACE (5.35) and VideoComposer (15.41), while also leading in Dynamic Degree (0.6650) and Subject DINO (0.8982). On pose control (Table 3), MMControl records the lowest pose MAE×100 of 3.07 versus VACE (3.29), ControlVideo (6.59), and Text2Video-Zero (6.40), with the best Temporal Consistency (0.9906) and Motion Smoothness (0.9952).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMControl outperforms all four audio-driven baselines (SadTalker, AniPortrait, HunyuanCustom, Hallo3) on Text CLIP similarity and Subject DINO identity preservation, and achieves the highest Sync-C (2.716 vs. 2.550 for Hallo3, 2.474 for SadTalker) despite baselines having access to ground-truth audio. The main trade-off is Dynamic Degree: Hallo3 (0.9184) and HunyuanCustom (0.6150) produce more motion than MMControl (0.5750), but MMControl better balances motion with aesthetic quality and temporal smoothness. On structural depth control, MMControl reduces MAE×100 from 15.41 (VideoComposer) and 10.64 (ControlVideo) to 4.52, beating the prior SOTA VACE (5.35) by ~16%. On pose control, MMControl achieves the lowest MAE×100 of 3.07, narrowly edging VACE (3.29) and substantially outperforming Text2Video-Zero (6.40) and ControlVideo (6.59), while also attaining the best Temporal Consistency (0.9906) and Motion Smoothness (0.9952) among all pose-control baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMControl extends the ControlNet-style bypass paradigm—originally designed for spatially-conditioned video generation—to the joint audio-video setting, introducing parallel modality-specific branches that inject visual and acoustic control signals into a frozen joint DiT backbone (LTX-2). It departs from prior controllable generation work, which has been either visual-centric (ControlNet, VACE, ControlVideo) or audio-centric (SadTalker, AniPortrait, Hallo3), by unifying heterogeneous control signals (reference images, reference audio, depth, pose) through a single Multi-Modal Control Unit. The modality-specific guidance scaling mechanism is a novel inference-time technique that has no direct analogue in prior single-modality control frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide composable, fine-grained control over both visual identity and acoustic timbre within a single joint audio-video generation process, addressing a gap where prior methods required decoupled pipelines. The practical impact is significant for content creators who need synchronized, identity-consistent audio-video with structural constraints in one pass. Key limitations include reliance on a single backbone (LTX-2), training on only 30K samples, and evaluation limited to single-character scenarios; open questions include multi-character conversational settings, long-term temporal coherence, and generalization to unseen speech styles without task-specific fine-tuning.
