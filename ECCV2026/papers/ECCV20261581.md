## Problem

Existing MLLMs suffer from hallucinations in long video understanding due to the imbalance between textual and visual tokens, and current tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) methods either depend on heavy external retrieval pipelines or use single-tool internal retrieval that leads to tool misuse (calling tools while still answering incorrectly) and overuse (retrieving finest-grained frames when coarser retrieval suffices). The paper addresses how to design a diverse internal toolkit with an RL optimization that explicitly penalizes redundant and misdirected tool invocations while preserving exploratory behavior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (seconds to hours) and a text question as input. The video is initially parsed at low resolution (196 tokens/frame, max 32 frames, 1960 total video tokens) and low fps to keep the first turn lightweight. The model operates in a multi-turn loop (max 4 turns), where each turn either produces a final text answer or invokes one or more internal tools to retrieve finer visual evidence (higher resolution, more frames, cropped regions), which is concatenated with prior context for the next reasoning step.

## Method

VideoTIR introduces a hierarchical internal toolkit comprising a global Browsing Tool (increases spatial resolution and sampling rate) and a Temporal-Spatial Grounding chain (segment retriever → frame retriever → zoom-in retriever) that uses cosine similarity between MLLM-generated text query tokens and video tokens for retrieval, all built on the backbone encoder without external APIs. A textual router within the MLLM dynamically selects which tool chain to invoke based on question intent. The key training contribution is TAGPO (Toolkit Action Grouped Policy Optimization), which assigns per-call rewards with exponential temporal decay (γ^(L−j)) normalized within each tool category, so redundant invocations in successful trajectories receive negative advantage while exploratory calls in failed trajectories receive null advantage; this per-tool advantage is averaged and combined with standard GRPO episode-level advantage for policy updates. For 3B models that cannot perform Zero-RL due to format compliance issues, a sandbox-based trajectory synthesis framework uses an external MLLM (GLM-4.5V) to generate tool-order predictions, stepwise reasoning traces, and environment feedback, which are then adjudicated for quality and used as SFT cold-start data before RL fine-tuning.

## Results

On the three long-video QA benchmarks, VideoTIR (7B, 16-frame, high-res, thinking mode) achieves 57.9 mean on Video-MME (without subtitles), 64.3 on MVBench, and 53.1 on LongVideoBench validation. This surpasses the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model (53.8 / 60.6 / 51.8) by +4.1, +3.7, and +1.3 points respectively. In terms of efficiency, VideoTIR requires only 1.42 average tool invocations with 4.6 s end-to-end latency, compared to 2–3 invocations and 5.9–68.3 s for competing TIR methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoTIR outperforms the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B on all three benchmarks (V-MME +4.1, MVBench +3.7, LVBval +1.3), confirming that the tool-integrated RL pipeline adds meaningful value over single-turn inference. Against other 7B TIR methods, VideoTIR beats Video-XL (55.5→57.9 V-MME, 55.3→64.3 MVBench, 50.7→53.1 LVBval) and Video-R1 (57.4→57.4 V-MME, 62.7→64.3 MVBench), but trails LongVT-RL on V-MME mean (57.9 vs 66.1) and Video-MTR (57.9 vs 59.0), though the latter two do not report MVBench or LVBval scores. Compared to older 7B video models (Video-LLaVA, VideoChat2), VideoTIR gains 18–19 points on V-MME and 13–30 points on MVBench. On efficiency, VideoTIR's 1.42 average tool calls and 4.6 s latency are the lowest among all compared TIR systems, versus 2 calls / 5.9 s for Video-MTR, 3 calls / 27.4 s for VideoMA, and 3 calls / 68.3 s for COLT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) paradigm for long video understanding by replacing single-tool or external-API retrieval with a multi-level internal toolkit (browsing + segment/frame/zoom-in chain) that leverages the MLLM's own visual encoders via cosine-similarity matching. It departs from prior GRPO-based multi-turn RL methods (VideoMTR, LongVT, DeepEyes) by introducing TAGPO, a stepwise per-tool-call advantage estimator that explicitly penalizes redundant invocations within a trajectory. The sandbox-based trajectory synthesis framework for SFT cold-starting is a practical departure from prior work that either relies on large curated multi-tool datasets or attempts zero-shot RL from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and growing pain point—hallucination and inefficiency in MLLM-based long video QA—by showing that a lightweight internal toolkit with RL-learned routing can match or exceed methods that consume 4× more visual tokens, while cutting inference latency from 5.9–68.3 s to 4.6 s. The TAGPO algorithm is a general-purpose contribution to multi-tool RL that could transfer to other agentic settings beyond video. Open questions include whether the cosine-similarity retrieval quality degrades on very long or visually diverse videos, whether the approach generalizes to MLLM architectures without the specific encoder structure of Qwen2.5-VL, and whether the 4-turn cap is sufficient for hour-scale videos with complex multi-hop reasoning.
