## Problem

Structural re-parameterization methods like RepVGG add a fixed-support sparse correction on top of a dense base kernel, but the support location is hard-coded (e.g., center-only for 1×1 and identity branches). The paper asks whether the model can instead learn which parameters are most critical to adjust, generalizing fixed corrections to learned selective rectification without incurring any inference overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261995/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CaReNet consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 (or 256×256 for the M4-256 variant) and produces class labels for ImageNet-1K classification, bounding boxes for COCO object detection, and instance segmentation masks for COCO segmentation. The multi-stage pyramid downsamples spatially by 16× at the stem and halves resolution at each of three stages, with lightweight classification heads attached to every stage and fused via fixed progressive weights. Critically, after the one-time offline merge of W_base + W_aux ⊙ φ(W_gate), the deployed model has identical parameter count and FLOPs to a standard convolutional network.

## Method

CaRe factorizes each layer's weight tensor as W = W_base + W_aux ⊙ φ(W_gate), where φ is Softplus and W_gate is a static parameter-space gate. The key theoretical insight (Lemma 1) is that the element-wise product W_aux ⊙ W_gate under standard L2 weight decay induces an L1-type penalty on the effective correction, naturally driving sparsity without explicit regularization. Softplus is chosen over linear or GELU gating because it is strictly positive and monotonic, breaking the sign ambiguity of linear factorization and creating a single continuous active valley in the loss landscape. The CaReNet backbone co-designs this mechanism at both macro and micro levels: a three-stage pyramid with a Simple Attention module (O(L) linear attention with a compact global state matrix and GLU-style output gating) replaces EfficientViM's heavier SSD mixer, and all DWConv and FFN layers are CaRe-parameterized. Progressive Stage Fusion with fixed weights [0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8] replaces learnable softmax fusion to preserve early-stage spatial cues.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, CaReNet establishes a new accuracy-throughput Pareto frontier across four model scales: CaReNet-M1 achieves 73.6% Top-1 at 33,203 im/s, CaReNet-M3 reaches 78.3% at 19,249 im/s, and CaReNet-M4 attains 79.8% at 14,576 im/s. At 256×256 resolution, CaReNet-M4-256 peaks at 80.4% Top-1 (80.6% with 450-epoch training). On COCO-2017, CaReNet-M4 as a backbone yields 39.2 AP for object detection (RetinaNet) and 36.0 AP^m for instance segmentation (Mask R-CNN), outperforming EfficientViM-M4 by +0.4 AP and +0.2 AP^m respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most direct competitor EfficientViM [27], CaReNet wins at every scale: +0.7% Top-1 for M1 (73.6 vs 72.9), +0.6% for M2 (76.0 vs 75.4), +0.7% for M3 (78.3 vs 77.6), and +0.4% for M4 (79.8 vs 79.4), while also matching or exceeding throughput. CaReNet-M4 surpasses MobileOne-S4 [45] by +0.4% accuracy (79.8 vs 79.4) while running ~9× faster (14,576 vs 1,624 im/s), and beats SHViT-S4 [54] by +0.4% at comparable speed. On COCO detection, CaReNet-M4 (39.2 AP) outperforms EfficientViM-M4 (38.8), SHViT-S4 (38.8), EdgeViT-XXS (38.7), and PVTV2-B0 (37.2); for segmentation it matches EfficientViM-M4 on AP^b (39.3) while gaining +0.2 AP^m (36.0 vs 35.8). The plug-and-play generality experiments show consistent gains when CaRe is applied to ResNet-18 (+0.2%), ViT-Tiny (+0.2%), and PoolFormerV2-S12 (+0.4%) without any increase in inference-time parameters or FLOPs. CaReNet does not beat the absolute top-accuracy entries in Table 1 (e.g., MobileViTV2 1.25 at 79.6% with 2,857M FLOPs), but it dominates the accuracy-throughput Pareto frontier among models with comparable or lower FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes structural re-parameterization (RepVGG, ACNet, DBB, MobileOne) from fixed-support corrections to learned, selective rectification: where prior methods hard-code the position of the correction (e.g., center of a 3×3 kernel), CaRe learns which parameters are critical via a static, nonlinearly gated sparsity mechanism. The theoretical foundation draws on the sparsity-by-redundancy principle (SPRED), showing that standard L2 weight decay on a factorized correction term induces an L1-type penalty on the effective correction. Architecturally, CaReNet builds on EfficientViM but replaces its state-space token mixer with a simpler linear-attention variant (inspired by MambaOut) and swaps learnable multi-stage fusion for a fixed progressive weighting scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CaRe offers a clean, zero-overhead re-parameterization that is plug-and-play across CNNs, ViTs, and MetaFormers, making it a practical tool for efficient backbone design on resource-constrained hardware. The consistent +0.7% gains across four model scales and strong transfer to COCO detection/segmentation validate the approach, though the absolute improvements remain modest relative to the strong EfficientViM baseline. An open question is whether the identified 'critical parameters' carry interpretable structure or whether the mechanism simply provides a better optimization landscape; the paper does not probe this. Additionally, it remains unclear whether the gains persist at significantly larger model scales or with different training recipes beyond the EfficientViM protocol.
