## Problem

Existing language-guided 3D scene methods focus on object-centric grounding—identifying *what* to observe—while ignoring *how* the subject should appear in a single frame, such as its orientation, spatial placement, or photographic composition. This paper formalizes Text-Instructed Viewpoint Grounding (TIVG) for 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes, where the goal is to find a 6-DoF camera pose whose rendered frame satisfies compositional text instructions specifying subject orientation, layout, and viewpoint simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3DGS scene (with its training images and associated camera poses), a natural language instruction describing the desired frame (e.g., subject identity, front/side view, left/right placement, close-up), and produces a single 6-DoF camera pose (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³) whose differentiable 3DGS rasterization yields a rendered frame aligned with the instruction. Unlike trajectory-generation methods, the output is a static viewpoint rather than a sequence of poses, and the text instructions are compositional, combining multiple constraints (orientation + layout + subject) that must be satisfied jointly in one frame.

## Method

CapFrame is a partially differentiable Retrieve-Translate-Refine pipeline. In Retrieve, FG-CLIP performs coarse semantic filtering of training views, followed by a Question-Evaluation (QE) process where an MLLM (Qwen3-VL) decomposes the instruction into targeted questions and scores each candidate view, yielding top-K initialization poses. In Translate, the MLLM extracts key subjects and infers orientation offsets (azimuth, elevation, roll) and 2D layout bounding boxes; Grounded SAM segments subjects in retrieved views, and back-projected pixels are matched to 3D Gaussians via KNN to form subject-specific Gaussian subsets, while Orient Anything estimates current subject orientation to combine with text-derived offsets into orientation pseudo labels. In Refine, the camera pose is optimized in SE(3) via Adam by backpropagating a composite loss—layout loss (centroid containment, in-box coverage, out-box leakage) and orientation loss (gravity alignment, forward-facing direction, look-at subject centroid)—through differentiable 3DGS rasterization, using the manifold derivative formulation from MonoGS.

## Results

Across 38 real-world scenes and 135 text instructions, CapFrame achieves the highest text-image similarity on both VLM metrics (CLIP 0.282, SigLIP2 0.448) and the best alignment scores from two MLLM judges (GPT AS 4.75, Gemini AS 4.22). In the user study with 33 participants, CapFrame receives an average rating of 4.50/5 and a 77.7% preference rate, substantially outperforming the strongest heuristic baseline SVS (2.52/5, 16.7% preference). The MLLM judge win rates are particularly decisive: CapFrame wins 72.6% of head-to-head comparisons against GPT-5.4-mini and 67.4% against Gemini-2.5-Flash, compared to 3–16.8% for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CapFrame outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the two adapted trajectory-generation methods, the gap is largest: SplaTraj scores only 0.241 CLIP / 0.276 SigLIP2 and a 3.0% MLLM win rate, while ChatCam reaches 0.258 / 0.283 and 3.7%, both far below CapFrame's 0.282 / 0.448 and 72.6%. The heuristic baselines are closer on VLM similarity (SVS reaches 0.435 SigLIP2 vs. CapFrame's 0.448) but collapse on compositional alignment: SVS wins only 14.8% of MLLM judge comparisons and 16.7% of user preferences, whereas CapFrame wins 72.6% and 77.7% respectively. Qualitatively, IVS inherits orientation biases from retrieved poses (e.g., tilted views), ChatCam's CLIP-based guidance is invariant to subject orientation, and SplaTraj's learned language field fails to distinguish target objects, causing it to lose sight of the subject entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

CapFrame sits at the intersection of three research threads: 3DGS-based scene representation and differentiable rendering (building on 3DGS [19] and MonoGS [37] for SE(3) pose gradients), MLLM-based language reasoning (Qwen3-VL for question decomposition and pseudo-label generation), and object-centric 3D language grounding (LangSplat, LERF). It departs from prior language-guided 3D methods by shifting the focus from 'what to observe' (object localization) to 'how it should appear' (orientation and layout constraints), and from trajectory generation (ChatCam, SplaTraj) to single-frame viewpoint grounding. The key conceptual move is converting compositional language into geometric pseudo labels (orientation angles, 2D bounding boxes) that serve as differentiable optimization targets, rather than relying on end-to-end learned language-to-pose mappings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a new task formulation (TIVG) that is practically important for interactive 3DGS applications where users want to specify a desired frame in natural language rather than manually searching viewpoints. The geometric pseudo label approach provides a transparent, interpretable bridge between language and 3D geometry that could be extended to other differentiable rendering systems. Key limitations include heavy reliance on MLLM prompt quality (no learned prompting), the absence of a unique ground-truth viewpoint making evaluation inherently subjective, and the assumption that training views provide sufficient coverage for initialization. Open questions include whether pseudo labels can be learned end-to-end, how the method scales to scenes with many interacting subjects, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the 38-scene evaluation.
