## Problem

Existing video surveillance systems rely on text-only queries, produce answers without temporal evidence, and cannot jointly reason over a reference image and a long video—capabilities essential for real forensic workflows (e.g., 'When did this person join the fight?' with the person's photo). Prior benchmarks also lack multimodal query support and precise temporal grounding annotations, leaving no systematic way to evaluate retrieval, temporal localization, and multimodal reasoning under realistic forensic conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260696/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system ingests long multi-camera surveillance videos (77 s–35 min in ForeSeaQA) and accepts either a text-only query or a multimodal query consisting of a reference image (a person crop) plus a question string. At inference, the tracker segments the video into person-centric clips, the multimodal encoder retrieves the top-K (K=3) most relevant clips, and the VideoLMM receives those clips at source resolution with bounding-box coordinates appended as text, producing a multiple-choice answer, a concise event summary, and one or more timestamped intervals (T_s, T_e) that ground the answer in the video timeline.

## Method

ForeSea is a training-free, three-stage VideoRAG pipeline: (1) ByteTrack with a YOLO detector segments raw footage into person-centric clips, drastically shrinking the search space; (2) a GCL-trained multimodal encoder (following VISTA) embeds each clip and each query—text-only or image+text—into a unified embedding space via a [CLS] token, enabling retrieval without unimodal projection loss; (3) VideoLLaMA3-7B reasons over the top-3 retrieved clips (with bbox coordinates injected as text rather than visual overlays or crops) to generate a temporally grounded answer. The key design insight is that person-centric retrieval acts as a strong inductive bias for forensic search, and that language-aligned spatial grounding (coordinate text) outperforms visual modifications (cropping or overlay) for the downstream VideoLMM. The framework generalizes beyond surveillance by swapping person-centric clips for uniform 1 FPS frame-level indexing on open-domain benchmarks.

## Results

On ForeSeaQA, ForeSea achieves 66.0% multiple-choice accuracy and 13.6% temporal localization IoU (average of multimodal and text-only splits), outperforming the strongest Video LMM baseline VideoLLaMA3 (64.6% Acc, 13.2% IoU) and the best retrieval-augmented baseline VideoRAG (62.9% Acc, 3.5% IoU) by +3.1 points in accuracy and +10.1 points in IoU. On the multimodal query split (ForeSeaQA^MM), ForeSea leads all methods at 65.4% accuracy, the only system to exceed 65% on both query conditions. On open-domain benchmarks, ForeSea matches VideoLLaMA3 on MLVU (73.0) and achieves 65.6 on VideoMME using only half the input frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForeSea outperforms all five Video LMM baselines on overall accuracy, with the closest competitor being VideoLLaMA3 (64.6% vs. 66.0% Acc; 13.2% vs. 13.6% IoU), a narrow margin that widens on the multimodal split where ForeSea leads by 3.8 points (65.4% vs. 61.6%). Against retrieval-augmented baselines, ForeSea's advantage is most pronounced in temporal localization: it achieves 13.6% IoU versus 3.5% for VideoRAG and 4.6% for T*, a 3–4× improvement, while also gaining +3.1 and +21.2 accuracy points respectively. On open-domain benchmarks (Table 5), ForeSea matches VideoLLaMA3 on MLVU (73.0) and trails it by 0.6 on VideoMME (65.6 vs. 66.2), but substantially outperforms VideoRAG (58.7 VideoMME, 72.4 MLVU) and all other RAG-based methods. ForeSea also achieves the lowest end-to-end latency (2.6 s) among all retrieval-augmented methods, compared to 5.2 s for VideoRAG and 7.6 s for T*, while maintaining the highest accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes three well-established components—person tracking (ByteTrack), unified multimodal embedding (GCL/VISTA), and a VideoLMM (VideoLLaMA3)—into a 3-stage retrieval-augmented pipeline specifically designed for forensic search with image+text queries. It departs from prior VideoRAG systems (VideoRAG, T*) that operate in text-only retrieval spaces or lack fine-grained temporal localization, and from surveillance benchmarks (SurveillanceVQA-589K, TUMTraffic-VideoQA) that restrict queries to a single modality. The key conceptual departure is treating person-centric tracking not merely as a preprocessing step but as the primary retrieval mechanism, reducing the VideoLMM's input to a small set of identity-relevant clips rather than uniformly sampled frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ForeSeaQA fills a genuine gap by being the first benchmark to jointly evaluate multiple-choice accuracy and temporal localization under multimodal (image+text) query conditions in surveillance, providing a standardized evaluation that prior benchmarks lacked. However, temporal IoU remains very low (8–16%) across all methods, and counting tasks are essentially unsolved, indicating that precise temporal grounding in long surveillance video is still an open problem. The finding that even oracle captions achieve only 31% TL IoU suggests that temporal reasoning requires more than semantic description—likely fine-grained visual-temporal modeling that current VideoLMMs lack. The framework's generalization to open-domain benchmarks at half the frame budget hints at broader applicability of person-centric (or entity-centric) retrieval as an inductive bias beyond surveillance.
