## Problem

Subject-driven image generation and manipulation suffer from identity drift and unstable editing in multi-subject settings, largely due to a data bottleneck: existing corpora are either synthesis-based (inheriting generator artifacts and identity drift) or retrieval-based (hard to scale and limited in open-ended scenes), and most do not explicitly support manipulation supervision. No prior dataset simultaneously provides large-scale, identity-consistent, diverse paired data for both generation and manipulation across single- and multi-subject configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262700/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For subject-driven generation, the model receives 1–4 reference images (720p/1080p) plus an open-ended text prompt and produces an identity-consistent image of the referenced subject(s) in a new scene. For subject-driven manipulation, the model receives an input image containing the target subject, one or more reference images defining the replacement identity, and an editing instruction, and outputs an edited image where the target is replaced while all non-target pixels remain unchanged. The OSBench evaluation uses 150 samples per sub-task (600 total) scored 0–10 by a GPT-4.1 VLM judge on prompt adherence, identity fidelity, manipulation fidelity, and background consistency.

## Method

OpenSubject is constructed via a four-stage pipeline that uses real video frames as ground-truth targets while synthesizing only the training inputs, thereby anchoring identity in real visual evidence. Stage 1 curates clips from OpenVid, OpenHumanVid, and OpenS2V with 720p/aesthetic-score filtering. Stage 2 enforces cross-frame subject identity through Qwen2.5-VL category consensus, Grounding-DINO instance-level verification (with class-specific confidence thresholds and geometric priors), and DINOv2 embedding-based diversity-aware frame pairing that maximizes appearance variation without sacrificing identity (validated: LPIPS 0.31→0.40 with ArcFace stable at ~0.615). Stage 3 synthesizes inputs via FLUX.1 Fill: mask-guided outpainting for generation (with SAM2 masks, geometry-aware scale/position jitter, and irregular boundary erosion) and box-guided inpainting for manipulation (erasing target regions defined by bounding boxes). Stage 4 validates all synthesized samples with a VLM verifier (F1=0.87 vs. LAION baselines), re-synthesizes failures, and generates short/long captions. The resulting 2.5M-sample corpus is used to fine-tune diffusion-transformer models (OmniGen2, Qwen-Image-Edit-2509) via LoRA under matched compute budgets, yielding the largest gains in multi-subject manipulation (e.g., +2.34 Overall for Qwen-Image-Edit-2509).

## Results

On the proposed OSBench (600 samples across four sub-tasks), Gemini 2.5 Flash Image Preview achieves the highest overall average (7.37) among all evaluated models, but drops sharply to 5.15 on multi-subject manipulation. Fine-tuning OmniGen2 on 500k OpenSubject samples raises its OSBench average from 6.52 to 7.12 (+0.60), with the largest gain in multi-subject manipulation (4.51→6.26, +1.75). Fine-tuning Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 on OpenSubject yields an even larger improvement, lifting the average from 6.95 to 8.18 (+1.23) and multi-subject manipulation from 4.82 to 7.16 (+2.34), surpassing all closed-source baselines. On external benchmarks, OpenSubject fine-tuning improves OmniGen2 on OmniContext (7.18→7.34) and ImgEdit (3.44→3.79).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's contribution is a dataset (OpenSubject) rather than a new architecture, so the key comparison is fine-tuning on OpenSubject versus prior datasets and baselines. Fine-tuning Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 on OpenSubject (8.18 avg) surpasses all closed-source models including Gemini 2.5 Flash Image Preview (7.37) and GPT-4o (6.79), with the largest margin on multi-subject manipulation (+2.34 over its own baseline, reaching 7.16 vs. Gemini's 5.15). Fine-tuning OmniGen2 on OpenSubject (7.12) outperforms all open-source baselines (UNO 3.50, DreamO 3.19, XVerse 2.57, DreamOmni2 5.56) and approaches Gemini 2.5 Flash. In the dataset comparison (Table 7), OpenSubject at 500k (7.12) beats Echo-4o-Image at 73k (6.12), UNO at 1M (5.52), and MICo-150k (6.02) under the same OmniGen2 fine-tuning recipe, indicating that video-derived identity-consistent pairs are more effective than purely synthetic or retrieval-based data. On OmniContext, the fine-tuned OmniGen2 improves most on multi-reference settings (MULTIPLE/Char.+Obj. +0.42), while on ImgEdit the gains concentrate in structural edits (Extract +0.84, Hybrid +0.76, Add +0.71), confirming that OpenSubject strengthens compositional and localization control rather than style transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both synthesis-based (Subjects200K, UNO-1M, Echo-4o-Image) and retrieval-based (MultiID-2M) dataset construction paradigms by repurposing video as the identity-consistency anchor: real video frames define who/what the subject is, while FLUX.1 Fill synthesizes only the training inputs (occluded or incomplete observations) rather than the targets. The pipeline composes existing tools—Grounding-DINO for grounding, SAM2 for segmentation, DINOv2 for diversity-aware pairing, and Qwen2.5-VL for verification—into a four-stage recipe that no prior subject-driven dataset has attempted. Conceptually, the paper also extends the subject-driven task from generation-only to include subject-driven manipulation (identity replacement with non-target preservation), a formulation absent from all prior corpora listed in their comparison table.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.5M-sample corpus and OSBench benchmark fill a concrete gap: prior datasets either lack multi-subject manipulation pairs or cannot scale beyond a few hundred thousand samples, leaving models weak precisely in the complex multi-identity editing scenarios that matter for practical applications. The finding that T2I-only fine-tuning degrades manipulation fidelity (Table 5) highlights a non-obvious tension between general prompt-following and identity-specific editing, suggesting that data composition matters as much as data volume. Open questions include whether the FLUX.1 Fill synthetic-input bias limits ceiling performance, whether the video-frame target domain gap becomes visible at higher resolutions or in more diverse domains, and whether the manipulation task formulation (box-guided erase-and-replace) captures the full range of real editing needs.
