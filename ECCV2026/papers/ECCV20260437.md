## Problem

Interactive segmentation foundation models (e.g., MedSAM2, nnInteractive) require precise per-image human prompts and degrade sharply on underrepresented clinical contexts such as small organs-at-risk or lower-limb vessels, where Dice can drop below 30%. Existing in-context learning methods that avoid per-image interaction instead demand large curated labeled datasets and dedicated training, making them resource-intensive and unable to leverage continuously improving foundation models. The paper asks whether a single annotated atlas can serve as sufficient context to customize a frozen foundation model to a new clinical task without any target-domain training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D query CT/MRI volume and a single annotated atlas (one support image with its corresponding segmentation mask) for the target structure, operating directly on full 3D volumes rather than slice-by-slice. The atlas mask is converted into one of three prompt types—click (centroid), box (bounding box on the middle z-slice), or full mask—depending on the foundation model's interface. The output is a 3D segmentation mask in the query space, produced entirely at inference time with no access to query ground truth.

## Method

AtlasSegFM is a three-stage inference-only pipeline. First, a test-time-optimized RDP registration network (300 iterations, NCC loss, lr 1e-4) with rigid and affine pre-registration aligns the atlas to the query, yielding a coarse structural mask M_atlas. Second, this mask is converted into prompts (click, box, or mask) and fed to a frozen foundation model (nnInteractive, MedSAM2, or vesselFM) to produce a refined prediction M_fm. Third, a lightweight 0.1M-parameter adaptive fusion module computes a voxel-wise reliability gate K via a 3D encoder over the query image, both masks, their signed logit difference, disagreement, and entropy maps; the gate is optimized at test time (100 iterations, lr 1e-5) using a cycle-transformation Dice loss that warps the fused prediction back to the support space and compares it against the available support label, requiring no query ground truth. The final mask is M_fm + K·(M_atlas − M_fm), adaptively trusting the atlas where the foundation model is uncertain and vice versa.

## Results

On the organs-at-risk and pelvic bone benchmarks (Table 1), AtlasSegFM (nnInteractive-based) achieves 63.71% Dice on HaN-Seg, 69.19% on SegRap, and 95.50% on Pengwin, outperforming all in-context learning baselines by 20–30 Dice points. On the BrainRT organs-at-risk task (Table 2), the method reaches 77.07% Dice and 77.55% NSD, compared to 10.45% Dice for the best ICL baseline (Iris) and 39.09% for nnInteractive-5. On vessel segmentation (Table 3), AtlasSegFM (vesselFM-based) attains 81.34% Dice and 72.04% clDice on AVT, and 84.42% Dice and 82.99% clDice on Fe-MRA, with an HD of just 3.00 mm on Fe-MRA versus 110–183 mm for interactive baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AtlasSegFM (nnInteractive-based) outperforms all in-context learning baselines by large margins: on HaN-Seg it gains +26.14 Dice over Iris (63.71 vs 37.57) and +29.72 over Tyche (63.71 vs 33.99); on SegRap it gains +26.46 over Iris (69.19 vs 42.73). Against the strongest interactive foundation model nnInteractive-5, AtlasSegFM improves Dice by +8.42 on HaN-Seg (63.71 vs 55.29), +19.17 on SegRap (69.19 vs 50.02), and +1.87 on Pengwin (95.50 vs 93.63), while on BrainRT organs-at-risk the gap is dramatic: 77.07 vs 39.09 Dice. On vessel segmentation, AtlasSegFM (vesselFM-based) slightly trails nnInteractive-5 on AVT Dice (81.34 vs 83.38) but surpasses it on clDice (72.04 vs 70.52) and HD (30.84 vs 43.69), and dominates on Fe-MRA (84.42 vs 49.36 Dice). Compared to the supervised upper bound nnUNet, AtlasSegFM is competitive on Pengwin (95.50 vs 98.90 Dice) but still lags on HaN-Seg (63.71 vs 75.10) and SegRap (69.19 vs 80.20), reflecting the inherent difficulty of organs-at-risk without task-specific training. The method also outperforms the pure registration baseline (RDP) by +14.99 Dice on HaN-Seg and +12.75 on SegRap, confirming that the foundation-model refinement and adaptive fusion contribute substantially beyond atlas warping alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two historically separate paradigms: classical atlas-based registration (which transfers structural priors via deformation fields without any learning) and modern segmentation foundation models (nnInteractive, MedSAM2, vesselFM) that provide powerful local refinement but struggle with rare or small structures. It repurposes the RDP registration network [33] from a trained model into a test-time optimization tool, and positions itself against in-context learning methods (UniverSeg, Iris, Tyche, SegGPT) that require large-scale training or fine-tuning to achieve cross-task generalization. The key departure is that no component of the pipeline is trained on target-domain data; instead, a single annotated atlas provides all task-specific context, and a lightweight 8.6M-parameter fusion module is optimized at test time via a cycle-transformation loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical and quantified failure mode of current segmentation foundation models: performance collapses on underrepresented clinical structures (e.g., organs-at-risk Dice drops from ~88% on whole brain to ~27%), which is a serious concern for radiotherapy planning where missing small structures can cause severe patient harm. The practical impact is significant—a single annotated atlas plus a frozen foundation model achieves near-supervised accuracy (95.5% vs. 98.9% Dice on Pengwin) without any target-domain training, making it deployable in real clinical workflows. Open questions include whether the approach extends to lesion segmentation (where structural consistency between atlas and query breaks down), whether multi-atlas strategies can handle patients with markedly different anatomy, and whether the test-time registration cost (~1.5 min/image) is acceptable for high-throughput settings.
