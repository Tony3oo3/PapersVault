## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting models have no intrinsic ordering among their primitives, so removing splats causes rapid quality collapse. Existing discrete LoD methods expose only a handful of fixed quality levels with visible pop-in artifacts, while concurrent continuous LoD approaches enable smoother budget scaling but incur substantial quality degradation at full capacity, making LoD adoption a costly design trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260099/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes posed RGB images with camera extrinsics (standard 3DGS protocol) and produces a single ordered array of N anisotropic Gaussian primitives. At inference, the user specifies an integer splat budget k ∈ {1,…,N}; rendering simply truncates the ordered array to its first k entries and rasterizes, requiring no auxiliary index structures, mode switches, or per-budget retraining. The output is thus a continuous spectrum of rendered novel-view images parameterized by k, all from one trained model.

## Method

MGS learns a nested Gaussian representation by assigning each primitive an importance score (opacity, in descending order) and maintaining a dynamic permutation so that every prefix of the ordered set is a coherent scene. The key training mechanism is stochastic budget training: each iteration draws a random keep ratio r ~ Uniform(r_min, 1), renders both the k-prefix (k = ⌈rN⌉) and the full set, and minimizes a combined reconstruction loss ℓ(G_{≤k}) + γ·ℓ(G_{≤N}), requiring exactly two forward passes per step. After every gradient update the opacity-based ordering is re-sorted, ensuring prefixes always contain the most important primitives under current parameters. This procedure modifies only the training objective—no architectural changes—so it drops into existing 3DGS pipelines (implemented on 3DGS-MCMC for fixed splat count). The stochastic multi-budget objective reshapes the learned opacity distribution toward the high-opacity end, so prefix truncation discards primitives the ordering has already deprioritized, yielding smooth quality-budget curves without sacrificing full-capacity fidelity.

## Results

On MipNeRF 360, MGS achieves 28.20 dB PSNR, 0.841 SSIM, and 0.130 LPIPS at full budget, with the best AUC_fps (64.81) and AUC_splats (77.79) among all baselines. Across all four benchmarks, MGS consistently attains the lowest LPIPS and the highest AUC_fps and AUC_splats, outperforming the closest continuous-LoD competitor CLoD-3DGS by +18.43 AUC_fps on MipNeRF 360 (64.81 vs. 46.38). At full capacity, MGS matches its 3DGS-MCMC backbone within 0.20 dB PSNR on MipNeRF 360 and Tanks & Temples, and slightly exceeds it on Deep Blending and BungeeNeRF, confirming that continuous LoD incurs no full-quality penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 3DGS-MCMC backbone (no LoD), MGS trails by only 0.20 dB PSNR on MipNeRF 360 (28.20 vs. 28.40) and Tanks & Temples (24.56 vs. 24.56), while slightly exceeding it on Deep Blending (28.41 vs. 27.63) and BungeeNeRF (27.13 vs. 27.04), confirming no full-quality cost for LoD capability. Among continuous-LoD methods, CLoD-3DGS is the closest competitor but MGS surpasses it by +0.76 dB PSNR and +18.43 AUC_fps on MipNeRF 360, and by +38.17 AUC_fps on BungeeNeRF (62.99 vs. 24.45); CLoD-GS lags further behind on both quality and speed range. Among discrete-LoD methods, Octree-GS achieves the highest single-level PSNR on Deep Blending (30.35 vs. 28.41) but its coarse levels degrade sharply, yielding only 29.54 AUC_fps versus MGS's 82.63; H3DGS and FlexGaussian score lowest on AUC_fps (11.62 and 30.04 on MipNeRF 360) due to their limited number of operating points. MGS achieves the best AUC_fps and AUC_splats on all four benchmarks, and the lowest LPIPS (0.130 on MipNeRF 360, 0.086 on Tanks & Temples, 0.176 on Deep Blending, 0.088 on BungeeNeRF) across every comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGS transfers the Matryoshka Representation Learning principle (nested prefixes of an ordered representation) from embedding dimensions to 3D Gaussian scene primitives, building on the 3DGS differentiable rasterization backbone and the fixed-budget training discipline of 3DGS-MCMC. It departs from discrete LoD methods (H3DGS, Octree-GS, MaskGaussian, FlexGaussian) that require auxiliary hierarchical index structures, and from concurrent continuous LoD methods (CLoD-GS, CLoD-3DGS) that learn view-dependent opacity decay or separate importance scores at the cost of full-capacity quality. The key conceptual departure is that MGS requires no architectural modification: it repurposes the existing per-primitive opacity parameter—already optimized by the renderer—as a free importance ordering, and introduces stochastic budget training (two forward passes per step) as the sole training change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching full-capacity quality (within seed-level variation) while enabling a dense continuum of quality-speed operating points from a single model, MGS removes the central trade-off that made prior continuous LoD methods a 'costly design choice.' The drop-in nature (no new parameters, no auxiliary data structures, ~2× training cost) makes it broadly applicable to existing 3DGS pipelines. Open questions include view-adaptive or spatially-adaptive ordering (the current global opacity sort is view-agnostic), adaptive per-frame budget scheduling, and whether the opacity-based ordering generalizes to dynamic or multi-scene settings. The emergent reshaping of the opacity distribution toward high values during training is an interesting but under-explored mechanism that may inform future nested-representation designs.
