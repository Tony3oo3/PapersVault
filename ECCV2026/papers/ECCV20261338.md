## Problem

Existing video stylization methods that rely on reference images suffer from content leakage, and the field lacks large-scale paired video datasets for supervised training. Moreover, prior approaches are confined to short sequences (≤5 s) and fail to provide scalable designs for long-duration generation, leaving a reliable open-source text-driven video stylization paradigm for arbitrary-length videos unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261338/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video R (480×480, 16 FPS, 33–69 frames per training clip), a text prompt p containing both style instructions and content descriptions, and a binary mask M (all zeros in standard mode) as input, and produces a temporally coherent stylized video O at the same resolution. For long-video inference, the input is segmented into overlapping windows of x=16 frames, where each subsequent segment is additionally conditioned on the last x stylized frames from the preceding output, enabling autoregressive extension to minute-level durations.

## Method

EchoStyle builds a video-to-video DiT architecture on Wan2.2-I2V-14B where the VAE-encoded reference video, noisy target latent, and mask are concatenated along the channel dimension into a unified visual-alignment input, with text conditioning injected via adaLN and cross-attention; training uses a flow-matching objective with LoRA (r=64) fine-tuning. The key data contribution is a reverse-synthesis pipeline that collects authentic stylized videos and generates their realistic counterparts (via Qwen-Image endpoint translation + VACE depth-guided V2V), inverting the usual direction to avoid contaminating the target distribution with synthetic artifacts, yielding the 20k-pair V-Style20k dataset. For long videos, an init-follow-mode mechanism introduces a temporal reference video T (first x frames replaced with stylized output) during training, paired with a sliding-window inference strategy that conditions each new segment on the overlapping stylized frames of the previous one, ensuring seamless temporal continuity across arbitrary-length sequences.

## Results

On the 50-pair evaluation set spanning nine artistic styles, EchoStyle achieves the highest style consistency (CSD 0.895) and dynamic quality (0.783) among all compared methods, and near-best style similarity (0.802, vs. Kling-O1's 0.809). In human preference evaluation, EchoStyle attains the top scores for style similarity (0.826) and style consistency (0.870), while trailing Seedance 2.0 on content preservation (0.860 vs. 0.889). On video quality metrics (motion smoothness, imaging quality, static quality), EchoStyle sits slightly below the closed-source Kling-O1 and Seedance 2.0 but substantially above all open-source baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoStyle decisively outperforms all open-source V2V baselines: style similarity jumps from 0.351 (AnyV2V) and 0.394 (VACE-Flow) to 0.802, and dynamic quality improves from 0.611/0.732 to 0.783, indicating that training-free and frame-conditioned approaches suffer severe style degradation under video dynamics. Against the closed-source Kling-O1, EchoStyle wins on style consistency (0.895 vs. 0.883), dynamic quality (0.783 vs. 0.775), and aesthetic quality (0.878 vs. 0.806), but trails on motion smoothness (0.966 vs. 0.984) and imaging quality (0.696 vs. 0.746). Versus Seedance 2.0, EchoStyle leads on style similarity (0.802 vs. 0.628) and style consistency (0.895 vs. 0.831) but is slightly behind on aesthetic quality (0.878 vs. 0.885) and content preservation in human evaluation (0.860 vs. 0.889). In the human preference study, EchoStyle secures the top style-similarity score (0.826) and style-consistency score (0.870), confirming that its text-driven approach better captures target artistic intent than both reference-image-based open-source methods and the commercial alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoStyle departs from the dominant reference-image paradigm in video stylization (StyleCrafter, StyleMaster, AnyV2V, FreeVis) by formulating the task as text-driven video-to-video generation, thereby avoiding content leakage inherent in image-conditioned approaches. Architecturally, it builds on the Wan2.2-I2V-14B DiT backbone with LoRA fine-tuning and a flow-matching objective, but introduces a multi-channel latent concatenation strategy (reference video, noisy target, mask) as a visual alignment mechanism, positioning itself against ControlNet-style and in-context conditioning alternatives. The reverse-synthesis data pipeline inverts the typical forward-generation direction—using real stylized videos as targets and synthesizing realistic inputs—drawing on VACE and Qwen-Image as generation tools. The init-follow-mode mechanism with sliding-window inference extends the framework to long videos, a dimension largely unaddressed by prior stylization methods confined to ≤5-second clips.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of V-Style20k (20k paired videos across 14 styles) addresses a critical data bottleneck that has constrained video stylization research, and the text-driven formulation offers a cleaner, more scalable alternative to reference-image methods for production pipelines. The init-follow-mode + sliding-window design provides a practical recipe for minute-level stylization that could be adapted to other V2V tasks. Open questions include generalization to styles absent from the 14 covered categories, the computational cost of autoregressive long-video generation, and whether the reverse-synthesis pipeline can be extended to tasks beyond stylization (e.g., video relighting, domain translation). The evaluation, while thorough in scope, relies on a 50-pair test set and VLM-based scoring, leaving room for more rigorous benchmarking.
