## Problem

Post-training quantization of large pretrained vision encoders (CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP) suffers severe accuracy degradation at low bit-widths due to activation outliers that inflate the quantization range. Unlike LLMs, where outliers concentrate at known special tokens in early layers, vision encoder outliers emerge at seemingly random background patches in middle layers, making it unclear which tokens to target for mitigation. The paper asks whether prefixing reusable register tokens can suppress these outliers in a training-free manner, and how the strategy must differ from the LLM case.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261998/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires a pretrained vision encoder and a pool of 50,000 reference images (ImageNet-1k training split) to curate registers; no per-image processing is needed at inference. At test time, the quantized encoder consumes standard RGB images or video frames, with a small number of precomputed KV-cache tokens inserted into middle-to-late transformer blocks and a few outlier tokens removed from the sensitive layer. The output is the quantized model's standard predictions—zero-shot class scores, image-text similarity scores, or VLM answers—without any architectural change to the encoder.

## Method

RegCache is a training-free, three-step outlier-mitigation plug-in: (1) Curating—identify the quantization-sensitive layer by independently quantizing each block and measuring accuracy drop, then select the top-k tokens with the largest ℓ∞ norm at that layer's input across 50K reference images as register candidates; (2) Caching—average the KV caches of these candidates and search for the optimal number of copies (τ ∈ {1,…,15}) to prefix into the quantized encoder starting a few blocks before the sensitive layer; (3) Deleting—remove the top-k̃ tokens with the largest ℓ∞ norm at the sensitive layer's input at inference to eliminate any residual outliers. Three key departures from LLM prefixing distinguish the approach: registers are discovered data-drivenly from continuous patch embeddings rather than a fixed vocabulary, they are inserted in middle layers (where ViT outliers first emerge) rather than early layers, and the token-deletion step is essential for removing internally generated sink tokens that the prefix alone cannot suppress.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k zero-shot classification, RegCache yields the largest gains at 4-bit quantization, with best-case improvements of +44.51 pp (CLIP-B/16), +18.42 pp (SigLIP2-B/16), and +12.40 pp (SigLIP-B/16) over the best baseline without RegCache. Average improvements across all PTQ baselines at W4A4 range from +4.70 pp (SigLIP) to +15.67 pp (CLIP), while at W8A8 gains shrink to roughly +0.25 to +1.36 pp. In VLMs (Qwen3-VL-2B/8B at 4-bit), RegCache adds +3.34 to +6.16 pp on GQA and +0.51 to +10.69 pp on VQAv2 over RepQ-ViT alone. Latency overhead is negligible, adding at most 1.54% to inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegCache is evaluated as a plug-in on top of five PTQ baselines: RepQ-ViT, PTQ4ViT, NoisyQuant, FIMA-Q, and ERQ. The largest single improvement is +44.51 pp on CLIP-B/16 W4A4 (FIMA-Q: 50.41 → 62.08), and +21.36 pp on SigLIP-B/16 W4A4 (RepQ-ViT: 0.59 → 21.36). At W8A8, gains are modest (≤ +5.61 pp) because advanced baselines like FIMA-Q already approach FP32 accuracy, leaving little headroom. DINOv2 is the only model where RegCache shows a slight average degradation (−1.11 pp at W4A4), likely due to its self-supervised training producing different outlier patterns. In VLMs (Qwen3-VL-2B, 4-bit), RegCache consistently improves RepQ-ViT by +3.34 pp (GQA) and +10.69 pp (VQAv2), confirming transferability to downstream multimodal tasks. The ablation (Table 7) shows that prefix caching and token deletion are synergistic: either alone can underperform the naïve baseline, while their combination yields the best results.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegCache adapts the LLM quantization technique of prefixing attention-sink tokens as a KV cache (Son et al., 2024) to vision encoders, but introduces three key departures motivated by ViT-specific outlier behavior: (1) register candidates are discovered data-drivenly from reference images rather than drawn from a fixed vocabulary, (2) prefixing is applied at middle layers rather than the model input, and (3) a token-deletion step removes residual outliers. The method also builds on the register-token concept from Darcet et al. (2024) and positions itself as a training-free, plug-in module atop existing ViT PTQ baselines (RepQ-ViT, PTQ4ViT, NoisyQuant, FIMA-Q, ERQ) rather than a standalone quantization scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RegCache makes 4-bit quantization of large pretrained vision encoders practically viable (e.g., recovering ~62% of FP32 accuracy on CLIP-B/16 W4A4 vs. near-zero for naive quantization), which is critical for edge deployment and for reducing the vision-encoder bottleneck in VLMs where it can account for ~45% of latency. The conceptual finding that ViT outliers emerge in middle layers (unlike LLMs) and that outlier tokens are highly reusable across images (cosine similarity 0.89) opens a path toward understanding why transformer outlier behavior differs across modalities. Limitations include per-model hyperparameter tuning (number of prefix copies, tokens to delete) and negligible gains on DINOv2, suggesting the method's effectiveness depends on the encoder's training paradigm. An open question is whether the middle-layer register phenomenon generalizes to next-generation encoders (e.g., DINOv3) or to video-specific architectures.
