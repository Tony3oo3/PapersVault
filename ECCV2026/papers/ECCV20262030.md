## Problem

Existing active 3D Gaussian Splatting methods select next-best-views using factorised per-pixel or per-primitive information gain, which cannot represent geometric relationships between surfaces such as wall-floor orthogonality or floor-ceiling parallelism. As a result, structurally incomplete reconstructions—where adjacent planes each render well but their inter-surface constraints remain unverified—go undetected, leaving the agent unable to distinguish a truly complete scene from one with hidden relational gaps.

## Input / Output

The system consumes sequential posed RGB-D frames (60°×90° FOV) from an embodied agent in Habitat, processing them online without any pre-collected image set. It incrementally produces three coupled outputs: a 3DGS radiance map for photometric appearance, a Plane Scene Graph encoding inter-surface topology with typed structural/object nodes, and a next-best-view pose that drives the agent's trajectory via RRT path planning over a signed distance field. The PSG is updated only at periodic keyframes, keeping per-step overhead negligible relative to the full Gaussian map.

## Method

ActiveStructure constructs a Plane Scene Graph (PSG) over detected planar surfaces, where node classification into structural vs. object planes is self-supervised via betweenness centrality and degree on a minimum spanning tree, and edges encode geometric affinity through normal alignment and spatial proximity. Structural information gain is computed by multiplicatively fusing three topological anomaly indicators—connectivity surprise (dangling nodes), neighbourhood surprise (missing bridges among proximate unlinked planes), and geometric surprise (deviation from canonical 0°/90°/180° inter-plane angles)—with an intrinsic per-plane quality vector covering eight failure modes including 3D interior deficiency localisation. The total viewpoint information gain decomposes into a pixel-level appearance term and this structural term, weighted by a phase-dependent coupling. A three-phase hierarchical strategy implements informational polarity reversal: large structural planes act as exploration attractors during scaffold construction, then flip to suppressors that redirect the observation budget toward smaller object surfaces once the scaffold is established, with phase transitions triggered by structural objectives rather than fixed frame counts.

## Results

On Replica, ActiveStructure achieves the best average geometric accuracy (1.16 cm), completion (1.49 cm), and coverage (97.47%), surpassing the closest active baseline ActiveGAMER (1.28/1.74/96.59) by consistent margins. It also attains the best average PSNR (32.13 dB) and SSIM (0.974), with LPIPS of 0.116 (second-best behind MonoGS's 0.085, which operates at only 73.20% coverage). On Matterport3D, the advantage amplifies: best accuracy (1.57 cm), completion (1.91 cm), coverage (96.23%), PSNR (26.08 dB), and SSIM (0.920), improving upon ActiveGAMER by 3.7%, 24.8%, and 1.7% on the geometric metrics respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActiveStructure outperforms all active 3DGS baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor ActiveGAMER [4], it improves accuracy by 0.12 cm, completion by 0.25 cm, coverage by 0.88%, and PSNR by 1.14 dB on Replica, with gains widening on MP3D (e.g., +24.8% completion). ActiveSplat [19] is beaten on all metrics, most notably in PSNR (32.13 vs. 24.72 dB) and SSIM (0.974 vs. 0.854). NARUTO [5] fails to complete exploration on several scenes (marked '–'), limiting its comparability. Among passive SLAM baselines, MonoGS [20] achieves the best LPIPS (0.085) but at only 73.20% coverage versus ActiveStructure's 97.47%, and SplaTAM [13] matches accuracy (1.27 vs. 1.16 cm) yet lags badly on completion (5.38 vs. 1.49 cm). ANM-S [15] is competitive on accuracy (1.43 cm) but trails on completion (1.90 vs. 1.49 cm) and coverage (93.35% vs. 97.47%). Co-SLAM [29] and Gaussian-SLAM [35] are substantially weaker across all metrics, confirming that active exploration with structural guidance is essential for high-fidelity reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends active 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction by introducing a Plane Scene Graph (PSG) as a structural information-gain channel, departing from the factorised per-pixel or per-Gaussian observation model used by all prior active 3DGS methods (ActiveGAMER, ActiveSplat, FisherRF, POp-GS, GauSS-MI). While planar regularisation (PGSR, 2DGS) and spatial scene graphs (ConceptGraphs, Hydra) have been used in reconstruction and SLAM, they serve as passive post-hoc constraints or offline semantic structures; here the PSG is self-supervising and action-oriented, continuously generating planning signals within a closed exploration loop. The 'informational polarity reversal' mechanism, where structural planes transition from exploration attractors to suppressors across phases, has no direct analogue in prior NBV literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a genuine representational gap: factorised pixel-level metrics cannot detect inter-surface relational incompleteness (e.g., unverified orthogonality between walls), which is a practical failure mode in embodied reconstruction. The 5.6× speedup over ActiveGAMER alongside consistent SOTA accuracy and rendering quality makes this practically relevant for real-time active mapping. Open questions include generalisation beyond planar-dominated indoor scenes, scalability of the PSG in very large or highly non-planar environments, and whether the three-phase hierarchy with hand-tuned type weights can be replaced by learned phase transitions.
