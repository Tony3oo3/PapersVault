## Problem

Existing color transfer methods either rely on slow instance-specific optimization that produces inaccurate global mappings, or on fast feed-forward learning that suffers from semantic misalignment and visual artifacts. A more fundamental issue is that online optimization and offline inference are treated as disjoint paradigms, preventing the accuracy of the former from informing the efficiency of the latter.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261484/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image and a reference style image as input. For ColorFM-L, both images are resized to 256×256 for feature extraction via a ViT-S/16 backbone, while the actual color transfer operates on the full-resolution content image flattened to an HW×3 pixel matrix. The output is a color-transferred image at the original resolution; the model processes 4K (3840×2160) images in 0.043 s on a single RTX 4090. Semantic masks are extracted by a pretrained SegFormer-B5 to guide region-aware transfer.

## Method

ColorFM reformulates color transfer as transport of pixel distributions in RGB space via Flow Matching, unifying an optimization stage (ColorFM-O) and a learning stage (ColorFM-L). ColorFM-O constructs source-target pixel pairs using hierarchical color coupling (HCC), a recursive octant-partitioning strategy that preserves relative locality in color space to yield quasi-linear trajectories (path-length ratio ≈ 1.009), then optimizes a lightweight 2-layer MLP velocity field via the standard FM objective and integrates the ODE for transfer. ColorFM-L is a feed-forward model trained on pseudo-ground-truth triplets generated by ColorFM-O: a weight-sharing ViT-S/16 encoder with cross-attention predicts parameters Θ_c and Θ_s that instantiate a globally shared pixel-wise MLP, and color transfer is executed as a bidirectional one-step Euler integration (forward to an implicit intermediate state, then backward to the style domain), eliminating iterative ODE solving while preserving the geometric regularity learned during optimization.

## Results

On the 1,560-pair Unsplash test set, ColorFM-L achieves the best Distance to Ideal (0.320) and the lowest Lipschitz constant (2.67) among all compared methods, indicating the optimal balance between style alignment and content preservation with minimal artifacts. It also attains the highest content similarity (0.732) and near-best style similarity (0.825, second only to ModFlows at 0.846). Inference runs in 0.016 s per image (0.043 s at 4K), making it the fastest learning-based method tested, while the optimization-based ColorFM-O requires 19.3 s per pair.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ColorFM-L wins on the primary trade-off metric (Distance to Ideal: 0.320 vs. 0.404 for CAP-VST and 0.419 for ModFlows) and on the regularity metric (Lipschitz 2.67 vs. 5.29 for ModFlows, 50.30 for CAP-VST, and 89.73 for PhotoWCT²), indicating far fewer color banding and spatial artifacts. ModFlows and CAP-VST achieve marginally higher style similarity (0.846 and 0.844 vs. 0.825) but sacrifice content fidelity (0.610 and 0.627 vs. 0.732) and suffer severe artifact penalties. Among optimization-based methods, ColorFM-O (19.3 s) is competitive with NLUT (18.9 s) but far faster than D-LUT (82.8 s), and ColorFM-L surpasses ColorFM-O on every metric, attributed to large-scale supervised smoothing of instance-specific optimization irregularities. Deep Preset and D-LUT preserve content well (0.899 and 0.724) but fail to transfer complex styles (style 0.441 and 0.513). Neural Preset [14] is compared only qualitatively due to mobile-app-only availability, and the paper reports it produces under-stylized results with insufficient color transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Flow Matching (FM) and Rectified Flow for distribution transport, but departs from prior FM-based color transfer (ModFlows) by replacing a fixed uniform intermediate distribution with a learned implicit state and a hierarchical color coupling (HCC) strategy that yields quasi-linear trajectories. It also bridges the traditionally disjoint paradigms of online optimization (DPST, NLUT, D-LUT) and offline inference (WCT², Neural Preset, SA-LUT) into a unified optimization-to-learning pipeline, where instance-specific optimization generates pseudo-supervision for a feed-forward model. The ViT-based encoder with cross-attention for parameter prediction follows standard transformer design, while the bidirectional one-step Euler transport is a practical simplification enabled by the HCC-induced trajectory straightness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The optimization-to-learning framework offers a general recipe—use expensive per-instance optimization to generate high-quality training data, then distill into a fast feed-forward model—that could transfer beyond color transfer to other instance-specific visual tasks. The finding that the learned model outperforms the optimization it was trained on (attributed to neural inductive biases smoothing per-instance irregularities) is a useful observation for the broader optimization-to-learning literature. Open questions include generalization to extreme color styles or out-of-distribution semantic layouts, and whether the HCC strategy scales to higher-dimensional color spaces or more complex transport problems beyond RGB.
