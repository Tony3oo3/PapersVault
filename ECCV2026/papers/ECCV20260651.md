## Problem

Existing embodied AI simulators rely on mesh-based rasterization, producing visually unrealistic observations that create a Sim-to-Real gap degrading policy transfer, and offer at best low-fidelity mesh avatars that cannot support realistic human-aware navigation training. No open-source platform combines photorealistic rendering with drivable dynamic human avatars while remaining deployable on standard datacenter GPUs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260651/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The simulator consumes 3DGS scene assets in PLY format, a conventional NavMesh, and per-avatar canonical gaussian attributes (positions, SH coefficients, opacities, scales, rotations, LBS weights) paired with GAMMA-generated motion trajectories and pre-computed proxy capsules. At runtime it produces 256×256 RGB-D observations format-identical to the native mesh renderer, discrete navigation actions (move forward, turn left/right, stop), and two avatar query APIs returning nearest-capsule clearance distance and step-blocking collision flags for reward shaping.

## Method

Habitat-GS extends Habitat-Sim with a zero-copy CUDA-OpenGL interop layer that lets a CUDA tile-based 3DGS rasterizer write color and depth directly into pre-registered OpenGL textures, enabling real-time photorealistic rendering within the existing Habitat sensor pipeline. A central design principle is visual-navigation decoupling: 3DGS handles all visual appearance while traditional NavMeshes continue to govern path planning, sidestepping the absence of explicit surface geometry in the Gaussian representation. For dynamic humans, a gaussian avatar module pre-bakes canonical-space gaussians offline and deforms them to arbitrary SMPL-X poses at runtime via a lightweight CUDA Linear Blend Skinning kernel (no neural inference), driven along GAMMA-generated scene-aware walking trajectories. Collision is handled by injecting per-frame proxy capsules derived from SMPL-X skeletal bones into the NavMesh as dynamic obstacles, so the path planner clips any agent motion intersecting an avatar's body. Agents are trained with DD-PPO (ResNet encoder + GRU policy head) using a mixed-domain strategy that combines mesh and 3DGS scenes to balance geometric learning efficiency with visual realism for Sim-to-Real transfer.

## Results

On PointNav, mixed-domain training (Config E: 20 mesh + 80 GS scenes) achieves the best GS-test performance with 79.6% SR, 68.4% SPL, and 2.70 m DTG, while maintaining mesh-test SR of 59.6% comparable to the mesh-only baseline (59.0%). For avatar-aware navigation, fine-tuning with gaussian avatars reduces Collision Rate from 6.71% to 4.75% and Personal Space Intrusion from 0.092 to 0.077 on the GS test set, with only 5×10⁶ fine-tuning steps (10% of pre-training budget). On Sim-to-Real video evaluation, Config E reaches 61.2% APA±1 on self-recorded footage and 28.3% on InCrowd-VI, outperforming the mesh-only agent (55.6% and 20.4% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is across training domain mixtures rather than against external navigation methods, since Habitat-GS is a simulator contribution. Mesh-only training (Config A) converges fastest but generalizes worst: it scores 61.3% SR on the GS test set and 55.6% APA±1 on real video, the lowest of all configurations. GS-only training (Config B) improves GS-test SR to 70.7% and real-video APA to 58.1%, but underperforms mesh-only on the mesh test (53.0% vs 59.0% SR) and has not fully converged within the 5×10⁷-step budget. Mixed-domain training (Configs D and E) dominates: Config E achieves 79.6% GS-test SR (+18.3 pp over mesh-only, +8.9 pp over GS-only) while keeping mesh-test SR within 0.4 pp of the mesh-only baseline. For the avatar-aware task, the GS+Avatar configuration reduces Collision Rate by 29.6% relative (6.71%→4.75%) and PSI by 16.3% (0.092→0.077) over the avatar-free GS baseline on the in-domain GS test, and also improves cross-domain to mesh (CR 2.34% vs 2.52%, PSI 0.068 vs 0.075), indicating that perceptual cues from photorealistic gaussian avatars transfer to lower-fidelity mesh environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

Habitat-GS extends the Habitat-Sim/Habitat 3.0 ecosystem by replacing its mesh rasterization backbone with 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) and integrating drivable gaussian avatars built on the AnimatableGaussians paradigm (Li et al., 2024). Unlike prior gaussian avatar methods that target standalone reconstruction and rendering, this work couples avatar gaussians to a simulator's sensor pipeline and NavMesh-based collision system via a proxy capsule mechanism. The key architectural departure from both mesh-based simulators (Habitat, iGibson, AI2-THOR) and the closed-source Isaac Sim is a zero-copy CUDA-OpenGL interop layer that enables real-time 3DGS rendering within Habitat's existing OpenGL sensor pipeline on standard datacenter GPUs, while the visual-navigation decoupling principle (3DGS for appearance, NavMesh for geometry) sidesteps the lack of explicit surfaces in the splatting representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully open-source, Habitat-compatible simulator combining photorealistic 3DGS scene rendering with drivable gaussian avatars, lowering the barrier for the embodied AI community to study Sim-to-Real transfer and human-aware navigation without proprietary hardware (RT Cores) or closed rendering stacks. The practical finding that a small mesh-to-GS mixed-domain training ratio (e.g., 20M+80G) matches mesh-only performance on mesh scenes while substantially outperforming on GS and real-video evaluation offers a concrete recipe for practitioners. Key limitations remain: the system is navigation-only (no force-level manipulation of GS objects), avatars follow pre-synthesized GAMMA trajectories rather than acting as interactive co-agents, and the visual-navigation decoupling means the agent never learns to reason about 3DGS geometry directly—extending to manipulation or interactive multi-agent settings would require deeper physics integration and in-loop neural inference.
