## Problem

Pre-trained Latent Diffusion Models offer strong perceptual priors for low-level vision, but directly applying them to multi-exposure HDR reconstruction faces three obstacles: the 8-bit VAE latent space cannot represent true HDR dynamic range, multi-step denoising incurs prohibitive inference cost, and the generative nature introduces content hallucination that violates physical fidelity. No prior LDM-based method has addressed all three simultaneously for multi-exposure HDR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262266/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N multi-exposure LDR images (each with its gamma-corrected linear counterpart concatenated to form a 6-channel tensor) and produces a full HDR image via a dual-layer representation: an 8-bit LDR base layer multiplied element-wise by a refined 8-bit gain map. Training uses 256×256 patches; inference is demonstrated at 1920×1080 resolution. The gain map is the sole quantity refined by the diffusion process, keeping all operations within the 8-bit range that the pre-trained LDM VAE natively supports.

## Method

GMODiff is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains a Degradation-aware Regressor (DaReg) on a NAFNet backbone to predict an initial gain map and, via a lightweight segmentation head, a pixel-wise reliability mask identifying regions where the regression estimate is unreliable (due to motion or saturation). Stage 2 fine-tunes Stable Diffusion 2.1 with LoRA (rank 16) to perform a single denoising step: the initial gain map is encoded into the latent space as the starting point (replacing pure noise), and the degradation-aware embeddings plus the underexposed LDR frame serve as conditioning signals injected into the denoising network. A Degradation-aware Decoder then fuses multi-scale VAE encoder features from the initial gain map with the diffusion-refined latent, using the reliability mask to modulate channel-wise detail recovery—preserving structural fidelity from the regression prior while suppressing its artifacts. A group training strategy (N+1 DaReg variants trained on different data splits) is used to prevent overfitting and improve robustness to diverse degradations.

## Results

On the in-distribution test set, GMODiff achieves the best no-reference perceptual scores across all metrics: DISTS 0.0103, MANIQA 0.5881, MUSIQ 60.96, and CLIPIQA 0.4253, while remaining competitive on distortion metrics (PU21-PSNR 36.68, PU21-SSIM 0.9882, HDR-VDP2 70.77). On the out-of-distribution Prabhakar et al. dataset, GMODiff leads on HDR-VDP2 (67.91), DISTS (0.0379), and all no-reference metrics (MANIQA 0.5469, MUSIQ 55.48, CLIPIQA 0.4602), though it trails AFUNet on PU21-PSNR (26.86 vs. 27.78). Inference on a 1920×1080 image takes 0.93 s, roughly 100× faster than other LDM-based methods (UltraFusion 93.4 s, LE-Diff 132.7 s, GMDD 39.2 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest distortion-metric baseline AFUNet [26], GMODiff trades a small PSNR/SSIM gap (36.68 vs. 36.91; 0.9882 vs. 0.9889) for clear wins on every perceptual metric (DISTS 0.0103 vs. 0.0120, MANIQA 0.5881 vs. 0.5820, MUSIQ 60.96 vs. 60.66, CLIPIQA 0.4253 vs. 0.4164) and ties on HDR-VDP2 (70.77). Against the diffusion-based DiffHDR [47], GMODiff dominates on all seven metrics, reflecting the advantage of large-scale pre-trained LDM priors over a diffusion model trained from scratch on limited in-domain data. Compared to the other LDM-based methods, GMODiff substantially outperforms GMDD [13] (PSNR 36.68 vs. 16.31) and LE-Diff [40] on all no-reference scores, while being 42–143× faster in inference. Versus the CNN/Transformer baselines (DeepHDR, AHDRNet, SAFNet, HDR-Trans, SCTNet), GMODiff consistently leads on perceptual quality while remaining within ~0.3 dB PSNR of the best distortion scores. On the out-of-distribution Prabhakar et al. dataset, GMODiff extends its perceptual lead (DISTS 0.0379 vs. 0.0589 for AFUNet; CLIPIQA 0.4602 vs. 0.4395) and achieves the best HDR-VDP2 (67.91), confirming robustness beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMODiff builds on the Stable Diffusion 2.1 latent diffusion backbone and the one-step denoising formulation from recent super-resolution work, but departs from prior LDM-based HDR methods (UltraFusion, GMDD, LE-Diff) in a key way: instead of generating full HDR radiance in the latent space or using multi-step cascaded diffusion, it reformulates the problem as 8-bit gain map refinement, sidestepping the need to retrain the VAE for high dynamic range. The regression-based DaReg (NAFNet backbone) provides an informative initialization and degradation-aware conditioning, drawing on the dual-layer HDR representation popularized by industry standards (Adobe, Apple) and recent academic work on gain map estimation. This positions the paper at the intersection of efficient single-step diffusion for low-level vision and the gain map literature, combining structural fidelity from regression with perceptual quality from pre-trained diffusion priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× speedup over prior LDM-based HDR methods (0.93 s vs. 93.4 s for UltraFusion on 1080p) makes diffusion-quality HDR reconstruction practically deployable, and the gain map reformulation offers a general recipe for applying 8-bit pre-trained LDMs to tasks requiring extended dynamic range without VAE redesign. However, the approach is constrained to the dual-layer (LDR + gain map) representation, which may not capture all aspects of true scene radiance, and the one-step constraint likely caps the quality ceiling compared to multi-step diffusion. Open questions include whether the degradation-aware conditioning generalizes to other restoration tasks with similar fidelity-perception trade-offs, and whether the group training strategy for robustness can be replaced by more principled domain randomization.
