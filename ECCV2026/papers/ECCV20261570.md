## Problem

VLM-based multi-vector document retrievers (e.g., ColPali) encode each page as N patch-level visual tokens, making late-interaction scoring O(NM) per query-document pair and creating a severe scalability bottleneck. Existing compression methods (attention pruning, hierarchical clustering) treat token importance and semantic redundancy as isolated heuristics and fail to regulate per-token coverage capacity, causing representation collapse at aggressive compression ratios (MF=50).

## Input / Output

The method consumes N ℓ2-normalized visual tokens (N×D) extracted from a frozen VLM backbone (Qwen2-VL-2B, PaliGemma-3B, or Qwen2.5-VL-3B) along with the VLM's self-attention matrix (N×N) averaged across all transformer layers. It outputs K ℓ2-normalized representative tokens (K×D) where K≪N, determined by a merging factor MF=N/K∈{5,10,25,50}. The compressed tokens are plugged directly into the standard MaxSim late-interaction scoring pipeline without any retraining of the VLM.

## Method

The paper formulates visual token compression as an Unbalanced Optimal Transport problem that unifies three objectives into a single formulation: the cosine-distance transport cost minimizes semantic redundancy, source marginals derived from VLM self-attention weights prioritize informative tokens, and uniform target marginals regulate per-anchor coverage capacity to prevent representation collapse. The UOT objective (with entropy regularization γ and KL marginal penalties ρ) is solved via an EM-style alternating optimization: the E-step computes the transport plan P through a generalized Sinkhorn algorithm in the log domain, and the M-step updates each anchor as a mass-weighted sum of source tokens followed by ℓ2 normalization. Anchors are initialized via k-means++ seeding. The paper further provides three theoretical results: a MaxSim approximation error bound governed by transport cost (Thm 1), a worst-case query robustness guarantee via geometric covering radius (Thm 2), and pairwise/top-k ranking stability conditions under compression (Thm 3).

## Results

On ViDoRe V1 Academic (Qwen2-VL-2B), the method achieves 79.6 nDCG@5 at MF=5 (vs. 80.1 uncompressed) and retains 71.3 nDCG@5 at MF=50, compared to 61.6 for Light-ColQwen2 and 55.2 for ColQwen2+Prune. On ViDoRe V1 Practical tasks at MF=50, the method reaches 79.7 nDCG@5, outperforming Light-ColQwen2 by +19.4 points. On the more challenging ViDoRe V3 English tasks (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) at MF=50, the method achieves 49.3 nDCG@5 versus 26.2 for Light-ColQwen2.5 (+23.1) and 31.9 for ColQwen2.5+Prune, while query latency drops from 2.39 ms to 0.26 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two training-free compression baselines: ColQwen2+Prune (attention-based pruning from ColPali [15]) and Light-ColQwen2 (distance-based hierarchical clustering [27]). The proposed UOT method outperforms both baselines at every merging factor (MF ∈ {5, 10, 25, 50}) across all three benchmark suites and both VLM backbones (Qwen2-VL-2B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B). The advantage is most pronounced at extreme compression: at MF=50 on ViDoRe V1 Practical tasks, the method achieves 79.7 nDCG@5 versus 60.3 for Light-ColQwen2 (+19.4) and 63.6 for ColQwen2+Prune (+16.1). On ViDoRe V3 English tasks at MF=50, the gap widens further to +23.1 nDCG@5 over Light-ColQwen2.5 (49.3 vs. 26.2), confirming that the capacity-regulation mechanism prevents the representation collapse that plagues unconstrained clustering under aggressive token budgets. At lenient budgets (MF=5), all methods perform comparably (within ~1–2 nDCG@5 of the uncompressed upper bound), indicating the UOT formulation adds no overhead when compression is mild.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-vector late-interaction retrieval (ColPali/ColQwen2) and visual token compression, building directly on the Unbalanced Optimal Transport framework of Chizat et al. and Liero et al. for the generalized Sinkhorn solver. It departs from prior compression methods (attention-based pruning, hierarchical clustering à la Light-ColQwen2) by unifying three previously treated-in-isolation objectives—semantic redundancy, token importance, and coverage capacity—into a single UOT formulation with relaxed marginals. The theoretical contribution of bounding MaxSim retrieval error via transport cost is novel for the VDR compression setting, whereas the algorithmic machinery (EM-style alternating optimization, k-means++ initialization) is standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scalability bottleneck of multi-vector VDR (O(NM) scoring) is a practical blocker for deploying ColPali-style systems at corpus scale, and this training-free, plug-and-play compression with provable ranking-stability guarantees offers a principled path forward. The ablation confirming that all three objectives (redundancy, importance, capacity) are necessary—especially at extreme compression ratios—provides a useful design template for future token-reduction methods. Open questions include whether the attention-weight importance proxy generalizes to VLMs without accessible intermediate attention, how the method behaves when K must be chosen adaptively per document, and whether the near-equivalence of balanced and unbalanced OT in the ablation (71.0 vs. 71.3 nDCG@5 at MF=50) suggests the marginal relaxation is less critical than the paper's framing implies.
