## Problem

Predicting geometrically consistent depth for novel viewpoints from sparse input views—especially hallucinating occluded or unobserved surfaces—remains unsolved by existing methods. NeRF-style approaches interpolate between observed views without inferring hidden geometry, and prior generative depth methods operate in 2D image space lacking strong 3D consistency priors. No prior work repurposes the rich scene representations of pretrained 3D foundation models (3DFMs) for generalizable novel-view depth synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more source RGB images (typically 2–6 per sequence) and the relative camera poses of one or more target views (encoded as 12-dimensional vectors per view, all relative to the first source view). It outputs per-pixel depth maps for each target viewpoint, decoded from 3DFM patch tokens via the frozen DPT head. The diffusion operates on 2048-dimensional layer-17 patch tokens from the 3DFM backbone, and the model supports both 1-source→1-target and 2-sources→4-targets configurations without architectural changes.

## Method

Z3D performs conditional latent diffusion in the patch-token space of a frozen 3DFM backbone (VGGT or WorldMirror), decoupling geometric representation learning from generative completion. A DiT-based denoiser predicts the velocity of injected noise on target-view tokens, conditioned on source-view tokens via cross-attention in each block and on target poses added to timestep embeddings; the denoised tokens are decoded to depth by the 3DFM's DPT head. Training uses flow-matching with a dimension-dependent timestep shift (α = √(m/n)) to stabilize denoising in the high-dimensional latent space, and proceeds in two stages (1→1 then 2→4 views). A key empirical finding is that a linear probe on 3DFM features can predict layered depth images (LDIs), confirming that these representations implicitly encode occluded surface geometry beyond the first visible layer.

## Results

On the primary 1-source → 1-target depth estimation task, Z3D-WM achieves the best in-domain AbsRel of 0.075 (δ<1.25 = 0.939), outperforming VGGT-DD (0.098) and WM-DD (0.108). On out-of-domain DTU, Z3D-VGGT reaches AbsRel 0.012 (δ<1.25 = 0.999), roughly 2× better than VGGT-DD (0.022). For point cloud reconstruction on DTU (1→1), Z3D-VGGT achieves mean accuracy of 2.725 vs. 6.337 (VGGT-DD) and 11.282 (WM-DD). On the 2-source → 4-target setting, Z3D-VGGT attains AbsRel 0.024 on DTU and 0.342 on 7-Scenes, consistently leading all baselines in both depth and point cloud metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Z3D is compared against two families of baselines: (1) LVSM+3DFM, which synthesizes novel RGB views via LVSM and then estimates depth with a frozen 3DFM, and (2) Depth Diffusion (VGGT-DD / WM-DD), which uses the identical Z3D DiT architecture but performs diffusion directly in pixel-space depth maps rather than in the 3DFM latent space. Against LVSM+3DFM, Z3D achieves dramatically lower AbsRel on out-of-domain DTU (0.012–0.021 vs. 0.468) and 7-Scenes (0.260–0.266 vs. 0.328–0.342), with δ<1.25 improving from ~0.75 to ~0.97; the paper attributes LVSM's weakness to perceptual-quality optimization that sacrifices geometric fidelity. Against the Depth Diffusion baselines, Z3D wins on in-domain depth (AbsRel 0.075–0.076 vs. 0.098–0.108), DTU depth (0.012–0.021 vs. 0.022–0.036), and point cloud accuracy on DTU (2.725 vs. 6.337 for VGGT variants; 5.021 vs. 11.282 for WM variants). The one exception is NRGBD depth, where WM-DD slightly edges Z3D-WM (AbsRel 0.127 vs. 0.133; δ<1.25 0.837 vs. 0.826), though Z3D-WM still substantially outperforms VGGT-DD (0.192). Qualitatively, Z3D produces smoother depth maps and cleaner point clouds than DD baselines, which exhibit pronounced gradient spikes and higher noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

Z3D repurposes the internal patch-token representations of pretrained 3D foundation models (VGGT, WorldMirror) as both the conditioning signal and the latent space for a DiT-based diffusion model, following the latent-diffusion paradigm of Rombach et al. and the flow-matching formulation of Stable Diffusion v3. This departs from prior novel-depth-synthesis work (MVGD, Depth Field Networks) that learns scene representations jointly within the diffusion model, and from 2D depth-diffusion methods (GRIN, etc.) that operate in pixel/depth space without 3D consistency priors. The key conceptual move is decoupling representation learning (handled by the frozen 3DFM) from generative completion (handled by diffusion), with a timestep-shifted noise scheduler adapted from Zheng et al. to stabilize denoising in the high-dimensional 3DFM token space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LDI probing experiment provides empirical evidence that 3DFMs implicitly encode occluded and hidden scene geometry beyond directly visible surfaces, which is a useful diagnostic for the broader 3DFM community. Z3D offers a practical zero-shot framework for hallucinating novel-view depth from sparse inputs without per-scene optimization, relevant to robotics, AR/VR, and spatial reasoning. However, performance degrades noticeably under low source-target overlap, and the method inherits sensitivity to reference-view choice from the underlying 3DFMs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large view counts, whether the layer-17-only diffusion constraint can be relaxed, and whether the framework extends to other 3D prediction tasks (e.g., point maps, normals) beyond depth.
