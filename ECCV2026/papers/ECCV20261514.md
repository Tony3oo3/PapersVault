## Problem

Single-photon LiDAR simulators face a fundamental speed-accuracy dilemma: fast Poisson models ignore detector dead time and produce physically incorrect statistics, while sequential gold-standard simulators that brute-force per-photon rejection are computationally intractable at scale. The specific gap is that photon count statistics under dead time have never been analytically modeled—prior Markov-chain work captures the temporal PDF but discards the absolute timing needed to predict how many photons are registered, leaving the histogram scale unmodeled.

## Input / Output

The simulator consumes depth and reflectivity images (H×W), a background level B, system parameters (repetition period t_r, number of pulses N_r, pulse width σ_t, dead time t_d, number of temporal bins n_b), and a realization count N_iter. It outputs N_iter histogram cubes of shape H×W×n_b, each pixel's histogram drawn from a Multinomial distribution whose count is sampled from a Gaussian (μ_MRP, σ²_MRP) and whose bin probabilities come from a temporally shifted stationary PDF. A precomputed lookup table indexed by (S, B) at a canonical delay stores (μ, σ², π) per entry (~105 MB for 256×100 grid), and per-pixel depth only induces a circular shift of the PDF rather than a new table lookup.

## Method

MaRS formulates the photon registration process as a Markov-renewal process (MRP) that augments the embedded Markov chain (which yields the temporal PDF) with random holding times on the absolute time scale, enabling analytic prediction of the photon count distribution. The key theoretical result is that the count N(t) asymptotically converges to a Gaussian with mean t/μ and variance tσ²/μ³, where μ and σ² are computed from the transition matrix via moment kernels; the challenging long-lag covariance series is handled by a spectral truncation rule that decomposes the tail using the dominant eigenmodes of the transition matrix (L=6 exact terms + p=5 spectral modes). A delay-invariance theorem proves that shifting the arrival rate by τ only circularly shifts the PDF without altering (μ, σ²), which removes τ from the lookup table and reduces memory from ~108 GB to ~105 MB. Cube generation then proceeds in three parallel steps: batched LUT retrieval of (μ, σ², π₀), temporal alignment via circular shift by per-pixel depth, and multinomial histogram sampling—achieving >40× speedup over the sequential gold standard while matching its statistics.

## Results

On photon count prediction (the core contribution), MaRS achieves a Wasserstein distance of 0.309 and KL divergence of 0.013 against the sequential gold-standard simulator, compared to 2.337 and 0.409 for Zhang et al. [39] and 207.565 and 18.821 for the renewal approximation. The variance prediction is particularly improved: MaRS reports a variance difference of only 1.588 versus 64.558 for Zhang et al. and 9257.464 for the Poisson model. For downstream depth and reflectivity estimation, networks trained with MaRS data show the strongest cross-simulator generalization to the gold-standard domain (e.g., PSNR 24.25 vs. 22.54 for Zhang et al. at SBR=0.6). In terms of efficiency, MaRS generates a 128×128×1024 histogram cube in 1.01 s versus 40.83 s for the sequential gold standard, a >40× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Zhang et al. [39], a neural-mapping simulator that estimates dead-time-distorted statistics via an autoencoder. MaRS decisively outperforms it on all four photon-count metrics: Wasserstein distance is 7.6× lower (0.309 vs. 2.337), KL divergence is 31× lower (0.013 vs. 0.409), and variance difference is 41× lower (1.588 vs. 64.558), indicating that Zhang et al.'s count prediction captures the mean but fails to model the variance under high signal levels. On the downstream depth/reflectivity task, MaRS-trained networks also generalize better to the gold-standard test domain (e.g., PSNR 24.25 vs. 22.54 for reflectivity at SBR=0.6; RMSE 0.074 vs. 0.082 m for depth), confirming that accurate count statistics translate into better reconstruction. The simpler Poisson and renewal baselines are far worse on all metrics, with the Poisson model being off by orders of magnitude in both mean and variance. MaRS additionally provides a >40× runtime advantage over the sequential gold standard and near-constant scaling with flux, realizations, and pixel count thanks to its lookup-table design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Markov-chain framework of Rapp et al. [28,29] for dead-time-distorted temporal PDFs by augmenting it with renewal timing on the absolute scale, forming a Markov-renewal process (MRP) that for the first time yields analytic photon-count statistics. It leverages the eigenvalue-based convergence analysis of Zhang et al. [41] to derive a spectral truncation rule for the long-lag covariance series, and departs from the sequential rejection-sampling 'gold standard' and the neural-mapping approach of Zhang et al. [39] by replacing per-photon loops with a closed-form Gaussian approximation plus a delay-invariance-based lookup table. The foundational MRP theory of Çinlar [42] and Fox [4] provides the probabilistic backbone, but its application to inhomogeneous, state-dependent SPAD registration is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaRS removes the long-standing speed-accuracy trade-off in SP-LiDAR simulation, enabling large-scale, physically faithful histogram-cube generation at a cost comparable to the physically incorrect Poisson model (~0.3 s vs. 16.1 s for the gold standard). This unblocks training of deep reconstruction networks on dead-time-correct data at scale, which prior sequential simulators made intractable. Open questions include whether the Gaussian count approximation remains tight in extreme low-flux or very-high-dead-time regimes, whether the LUT approach extends to more complex detector models (e.g., paralyzable dead time, afterpulsing), and whether the spectral truncation parameters (L=6, p=5) remain sufficient for substantially larger bin counts or non-Gaussian pulse shapes.
