## Problem

Existing 3D scene representation learning methods face a fundamental modality trade-off: point clouds capture explicit geometry but are incompatible with grid-based 2D pretrained backbones, while multi-view images and depth maps preserve regular structure but lack globally consistent 3D geometry. Pointmaps offer a promising middle ground by encoding coherent 3D coordinates in an image-like grid, yet prior work (POMA-3D) relies solely on pointmaps without appearance cues and does not effectively exploit cross-view geometric and semantic consistency for representation learning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes V=32 aligned multi-view image-pointmap pairs per scene, where each pair consists of a 224×224 RGB image and a pixel-aligned pointmap (H×W×3) expressed in a shared world coordinate frame, constructed by back-projecting depth maps via camera intrinsics and extrinsics. Views are selected using a maximum coverage sampling strategy. The output is a set of per-view class-token embeddings (one d-dimensional vector per view) and a scene-level embedding obtained by mean-pooling the per-view representations, both used for contrastive alignment with text during pretraining and for downstream retrieval/classification/grounding tasks.

## Method

UniScene3D introduces an early-fusion mechanism at the patch embedding stage where image patches and pointmap patches are each projected into the same embedding space (the pointmap patch embedding layer shares architecture and pretrained initialization with the image layer) and combined via element-wise summation before entering a standard ViT-B/16 backbone initialized from FG-CLIP. Training employs four contrastive alignment objectives: (1) cross-view geometric alignment, which uses symmetric Chamfer distance between pointmaps to rank view proximity and enforces a rank-aware soft-label cross-entropy loss so that geometrically adjacent views are closer in embedding space; (2) grounded view alignment, which aligns object referring text embeddings with all views whose pointmaps geometrically intersect the object's 3D mask, encouraging views sharing object semantics to cluster together; and (3–4) standard view-level and scene-level RGBP-text contrastive losses across the batch. The total loss weights the geometric term at λ=0.1, and the model is pretrained for 80 epochs on 6,562 real-world indoor scenes from ScanNet, 3RScan, and ARKitScenes.

## Results

On zero-shot viewpoint grounding, UniScene3D achieves R@1 of 38.6% on ScanRefer, 25.2% on Nr3D, and 23.6% on Sr3D, outperforming the best image-based baseline (SigLIP2, 22.2% R@1 on ScanRefer) by 16.4 points and the closest pointmap-based baseline (POMA-3D, 16.4% R@1) by 22.2 points. On zero-shot scene type classification, UniScene3D reaches 70.7% accuracy, surpassing POMA-3D (63.9%) and FG-CLIP (49.5%). Under task-specific fine-tuning for 3D VQA, UniScene3D attains EM@1 of 23.2 on ScanQA, 52.5 on SQA3D, and 35.2 on Hypo3D, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniScene3D outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated task and metric. Against image-based encoders (DFN, SigLIP2, FG-CLIP), it gains 15–16 R@1 points on ScanRefer viewpoint grounding, demonstrating that geometric cues from pointmaps are critical for spatial reasoning that appearance alone cannot provide. Against point cloud encoders (Uni3D-g, 3D-VisTA, SceneVerse), the margins are even larger (30+ R@1 points), confirming that object-level point cloud pretraining transfers poorly to scene-level tasks and that the grid structure of pointmaps enables effective reuse of 2D pretrained weights. Against the closest pointmap-based competitor POMA-3D, UniScene3D nearly doubles R@1 on ScanRefer (38.6 vs. 16.4) and leads by 8.8 points on zero-shot scene classification (70.7 vs. 63.9%), with the gap attributed to the joint RGB-Pointmap representation and the proposed cross-view geometric and grounded view alignment objectives. On 3D VQA fine-tuning, UniScene3D edges out POMA-3D by 0.9 EM@1 on ScanQA and 1.8 on SQA3D, with the ScanQA advantage specifically linked to color/texture reasoning enabled by the image modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-aligned 3D representation learning paradigm (PointCLIP, ULIP, Uni3D) by replacing point clouds with RGB-Pointmap, a modality that preserves the grid structure needed to reuse 2D pretrained backbones while encoding world-frame 3D geometry. It builds most directly on POMA-3D, which first explored pointmap-based scene understanding, but departs from it by jointly modeling appearance (RGB) and geometry (pointmap) through early patch-level fusion, and by introducing two novel alignment objectives—cross-view geometric alignment via Chamfer-distance-ranked soft targets and grounded view alignment via object-visibility-based positive pairs—that POMA-3D's JEPA-style cross-view loss does not capture. The framework is initialized from FG-CLIP, explicitly leveraging 2D foundation priors in a way that point cloud encoders cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniScene3D demonstrates that a single encoder can serve multiple 3D scene understanding tasks (grounding, retrieval, classification, VQA) with consistent gains, offering a practical unified backbone for embodied and VR/AR applications. The ablation showing that grounded view alignment nearly halves viewpoint grounding performance when removed highlights an underexplored role for object-level semantic consistency in cross-view representation learning. Key limitations remain: the model is fixed at ViT-B/16 and 224×224 resolution, trained on only ~6.5K scenes, and the baseline set is relatively narrow, so the generality of the RGB-Pointmap formulation at scale is still an open question.
