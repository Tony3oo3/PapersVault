## Problem

Ultra-long video understanding (hours to days) requires a two-stage paradigm of query-agnostic memory construction followed by retrieval-based inference, but prior methods invest heavily in complex hierarchical or graph-based memory structures that pre-model high-level relations without knowing the downstream query. This query-agnostic preprocessing is computationally expensive and may misalign with what is ultimately asked, wasting the reasoning capacity of strong MLLMs before the task is even specified.

## Input / Output

The system takes an ultra-long video (ranging from ~1 hour in LVBench to ~44 hours in EgoLifeQA) and a multiple-choice text query. During memory construction, the video is partitioned into non-overlapping 30-second clips, each captioned by GPT-5-mini (with ASR transcripts for benchmarks involving speech); at inference time, the text query drives iterative retrieval over the pre-built memory. The output is a single multiple-choice answer produced by an off-the-shelf MLLM solver (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, or Qwen3-VL-8B) that reasons over retrieved visual clips and filtered textual evidence.

## Method

MERIT replaces complex hierarchical or graph-based memory with a minimal episodic key-value store where each 30-second clip is indexed by four complementary textual keys (event/action, dialogue/mention, object, and summary) extracted from its dense caption. Retrieval uses a maximum-similarity (late-interaction) formulation: the query embedding is scored against every key of every clip, and the top-N clips are selected by their best-matching key, improving recall across diverse query intents. At inference time, a Neighbor Filtering mechanism expands each retrieved anchor by a symmetric temporal window (±Δ clips) and uses the solver as a query-aware filter to distill only relevant information from the concatenated neighborhood captions, providing broader episodic context without pre-computed multi-scale structures. The overall pipeline is an agentic multi-turn loop where the solver iteratively refines retrieval queries based on accumulated evidence until sufficient context is gathered for final answer generation. No model training is required; the framework composes off-the-shelf MLLMs, a sentence embedding model, and lightweight key extraction in a single-pass memory construction.

## Results

On the ultra-long EgoLifeQA benchmark (avg. 44.3 hrs/video), MERIT with GPT-5 achieves 71.2% average QA accuracy, a +5.6% improvement over the prior SOTA (WorldMM, 65.6%). On LVBench (hour-long videos), MERIT (GPT-5) reaches 71.8%, surpassing WorldMM by +9.9%. On Video-MME Long (30–60 min), MERIT (GPT-5) attains 77.7%, edging out WorldMM (76.6%) by +1.1%. In terms of retrieval quality, MERIT achieves a 0.56 hit rate versus 0.53 for WorldMM and 0.15 for EgoRAG, while requiring 8.4× fewer input tokens than WorldMM during memory construction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERIT (GPT-5) outperforms the strongest prior method WorldMM (GPT-5) by +5.6% on EgoLifeQA (71.2 vs. 65.6), +9.9% on LVBench (71.8 vs. 61.9), and +1.1% on Video-MME Long (77.7 vs. 76.6). Against the hierarchical-memory baseline EgoRAG (GPT-5), the margins are substantially larger: +19.2% on EgoLifeQA and +39.6% on LVBench, reflecting the information loss inherent in recursive summarization. Among graph-memory methods, MERIT surpasses HippoRAG (GPT-5) by +11.6% on EgoLifeQA and +17.8% on LVBench, and beats EGAgent (Gemini 2.5 Pro) by +6.7% on EgoLifeQA. Notably, MERIT achieves these gains while using 8.4× fewer input tokens and 8.6× fewer output tokens than WorldMM during memory construction (6,223 vs. 23,731 LLM calls), and 22% fewer input tokens than EgoRAG. On the shorter Video-MME Long subset, uniform-sampling GPT-5 (74.3%) narrows the gap to MERIT (77.7%) to just +3.4%, but on the longer LVBench the gap widens to +11.4%, confirming that the memory-based advantage grows with video duration.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERIT departs from the dominant trend in ultra-long video RAG of investing in complex query-agnostic memory construction—hierarchical multi-scale summaries (EgoRAG, Ego-R1) or multi-granular knowledge graphs (WorldMM, HippoMM, M3-Agent)—by arguing that such pre-structuring is both computationally expensive and misaligned with unknown downstream queries. Instead, it repurposes the late-interaction retrieval principle (ColBERT-style maximum-similarity matching) to a multi-key episodic setting, where each 30-second clip carries four complementary textual keys (event, dialogue, object, summary), and defers all query-specific semantic composition to inference time via a simple temporal-neighborhood expansion. The agentic multi-turn retrieval loop and dense-captioning backbone follow established RAG-for-video practice, but the combination of flat multi-key indexing with on-demand neighbor filtering as a substitute for pre-built hierarchies or graphs is the paper's core architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central design principle—spend MLLM capacity on query-aware reasoning at inference rather than query-agnostic preprocessing—offers a practical and scalable recipe for ultra-long video QA, with an 8.4× reduction in memory-construction tokens versus WorldMM while achieving higher accuracy. A key open question is how the fixed four-key schema generalizes to domains with different salient cues (e.g., surveillance, surgical video), and whether the neighbor-filtering radius Δ can be made adaptive rather than hand-set. The framework's gains are most pronounced with high-capacity solvers (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro), leaving open how much of the benefit transfers to smaller or open-source models where noise tolerance is limited.
