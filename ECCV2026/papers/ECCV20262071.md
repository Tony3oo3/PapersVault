## Problem

Most radiology report generation systems rely on heavily downsampled inputs (e.g., 256×256) to fit fixed visual token budgets, suppressing subtle but clinically critical cues like small nodules and faint consolidations present in native-resolution chest X-rays. Naive high-resolution tiling causes prohibitive token inflation, while global compression degrades diagnostic fidelity, and neither approach mirrors the radiologist's global-to-local diagnostic workflow of first surveying the image and then zooming into suspicious regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262071/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a native-resolution chest X-ray (up to 1920×1920 pixels) and a clinical prompt, and autoregressively generates a free-text radiology report. A key constraint is that the visual token budget remains fixed at 128 tokens regardless of input resolution: the model internally downsamples the image for global encoding, then selectively extracts a small number of 384×384 high-resolution patches from diagnostically relevant regions, fusing their features back into the global grid without expanding the token sequence. At inference, no external classifier or Grad-CAM is invoked; patch selection is driven entirely by the learned utility map.

## Method

LePaX formulates high-resolution perception as a constrained spatial resolution allocation problem under a fixed token budget and introduces two jointly optimized modules. Learnable Spatial Resolution Allocation (LSRA) predicts a spatial utility map from global features via a lightweight convolutional policy head, then selects top-K locations (with NMS for spatial diversity) from which 384×384 patches are cropped from the native image; during training, a Gumbel-Top-K straight-through estimator enables gradient-based optimization, and a Grad-CAM-derived spatial prior (from a frozen ResNet-34 disease classifier) regularizes the allocation logits, while the report generation loss provides task-driven refinement. Global-Regional Fusion (GRF) encodes the global image and selected patches with a shared SigLIP backbone, then performs region-conditioned cross-attention to inject high-resolution patch features back into their spatially corresponding global tokens, preserving the 128-token budget while enriching each token with fine-grained lesion evidence. The fused representation is compressed by a Perceiver Resampler and fed to a Phi-3-mini (3.8B) LLM for report generation, with LoRA fine-tuning applied to both the vision encoder and language model.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, LePaX (4B) achieves BLEU-4 of 0.253, METEOR of 0.336, and ROUGE of 0.380, surpassing the previous best (REVTAF-RRG) by 39% in BLEU-4. On IU-Xray, it reaches BLEU-4 of 0.235 and ROUGE of 0.402, yielding an average 11% improvement over the prior best. On clinical metrics, LePaX attains RadGraph F1 of 0.346 and BERTScore of 0.526 on MIMIC-CXR, and CheXpert Clinical Efficacy F1 of 0.531, all state-of-the-art. On CXPMRG-Bench (CheXpertPlus), it achieves BLEU-4 of 0.138 and CE F1 of 0.363, outperforming MambaXray-VL-Large (7B) by 23% in BLEU-4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LePaX (4B) outperforms all 26 baselines across every NLG metric on both MIMIC-CXR and IU-Xray. On MIMIC-CXR, it surpasses the strongest prior method REVTAF-RRG by 39% in BLEU-4 (0.253 vs. 0.182) and by 12% in ROUGE (0.380 vs. 0.336), while using only 4B parameters versus 7B for LLM-based competitors like R2GenGPT and MambaXray-VL-Large. On IU-Xray, LePaX leads DAMPER (the previous best) by 4.4% in BLEU-4 (0.235 vs. 0.225) and matches DART's ROUGE (0.402 vs. 0.411) while substantially exceeding it in BLEU-4 (0.235 vs. 0.208). On clinical metrics, LePaX achieves RadGraph F1 of 0.346, a 66% relative improvement over the next best MultiP-R2Gen (0.208), and BERTScore of 0.526 versus 0.425 for MultiP-R2Gen. On CXPMRG-Bench, LePaX beats MambaXray-VL-Large (7B) by 23% in BLEU-4 (0.138 vs. 0.112) and by 8.4% in CE F1 (0.363 vs. 0.335), demonstrating that selective high-resolution perception with a smaller model outperforms uniform-resolution larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

LePaX builds on the BLIP-3 multimodal foundation model (SigLIP encoder + Perceiver Resampler + Phi-3-mini LLM) and positions itself against two lines of work: (1) RRG systems that operate on heavily downsampled inputs (256×256), which suppress subtle lesions, and (2) general-purpose high-resolution MLLMs (LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL) that use uniform tiling or global compression, which the authors argue is poorly suited to the spatially sparse, anatomically localized abnormalities in CXRs. The key conceptual departure is formulating high-resolution perception as a learnable spatial resolution allocation problem under a fixed token budget, inspired by the radiologist's global-to-local diagnostic workflow, rather than applying uniform high-resolution processing to all regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× token efficiency over naive high-res tiling while achieving native-resolution perception (up to 1920×1920) makes high-fidelity CXR understanding practically deployable within standard MLLM inference budgets. The consistent gains across three large-scale benchmarks and multiple metric families (NLG, RadGraph, CheXpert CE, GREEN) suggest the selective-resolution paradigm is broadly applicable to other sparse-lesion medical imaging tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumetric imaging (CT, MRI), whether the Grad-CAM training prior can be replaced by self-supervised localization signals, and whether the fixed 384×384 patch size is optimal across different lesion scales.
