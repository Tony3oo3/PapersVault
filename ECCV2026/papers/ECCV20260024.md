## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D generators (e.g., Trellis, Hunyuan3D) produce geometrically sound assets but lack the fine-grained textures and photorealistic detail found in real-world subjects, largely because high-quality 3D training data with diverse appearances is scarce compared to billion-scale 2D imagery. This paper addresses the gap by distilling the rich visual priors of pre-trained 2D diffusion models into a 3D generator without requiring any paired 3D ground truth.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image as input and produces a textured 3D asset (decoded from a latent representation via the Trellis decoder). During training, the generator performs on-policy ODE rollout to obtain a clean latent, which is decoded and rendered into 2D views from randomly sampled camera poses; these rendered views are then edited by a frozen 2D diffusion editor (Qwen-Image-Edit) to produce pseudo ground truths. No paired 3D supervision is needed—only the reference image drives both generation and editing.

## Method

OREO establishes a Render-Edit-Optimize loop with two core components. Reinforced Editing modifies the inversion-free FlowEdit framework by (i) replacing the source branch with an unconditional velocity prediction and guiding the target branch toward the reference image condition, and (ii) dynamically updating the shared noise at each step by injecting the classifier-free guidance signal, which strengthens the edit while preserving geometric structure. Contrastive Distillation then lifts the pixel-space fidelity gap into the 3D generator's latent space: the generator's single-step clean-latent prediction serves as an anchor, which is re-noised and passed through the frozen pretrained generator under both the edited (positive) and original (negative) views, producing a contrastive objective that pulls the anchor toward the positive and away from the negative. A velocity-field regularization term keeps the updated generator close to its pretrained prior, preserving geometric stability. The entire loop is on-policy—the anchor always comes from the training generator's own current output—ensuring the supervision signal tracks the generator's evolving distribution.

## Results

On the Conceptual Design Dataset (out-of-distribution, imaginative references), OREO achieves CLIP similarity 0.7834 and DINO similarity 0.8065, improving over pre-trained Trellis (0.7613 / 0.7916) by +0.0221 and +0.0149 respectively. On the in-distribution GSO benchmark, gains are marginal (CLIP 0.7764 vs. 0.7722; DINO 0.7069 vs. 0.7022), confirming that OREO's fidelity boost is targeted at distributions under-served by existing 3D priors without degrading in-distribution quality. A user preference study further shows OREO receives the highest preference ratio among the three methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the pre-trained Trellis backbone, OREO wins on all four metrics: +0.0221 CLIP and +0.0149 DINO on the Conceptual Design Dataset, and +0.0042 CLIP and +0.0047 DINO on GSO. Against Photo3D (a prior post-training method on the same Trellis backbone), OREO achieves substantially larger margins on the Conceptual Design set (+0.0454 CLIP, +0.0228 DINO), while Photo3D actually underperforms the unmodified Trellis on that distribution (0.7380 vs. 0.7613 CLIP). On GSO, OREO edges out both baselines by small margins, indicating no regression on the model's original training distribution. At the 2D editing level (Table 1), OREO's Reinforced Editing also outperforms the original Qwen-Image-Edit pipeline in content preservation (DINO similarity to source view 0.9099 vs. 0.8084) and viewpoint consistency (Mask IoU 0.9520 vs. 0.6374), while still improving reference fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

OREO builds directly on FlowEdit's inversion-free trajectory-coupling mechanism but repurposes it from a general image-editing tool into a structure-preserving pseudo-GT generator for 3D post-training, replacing the source-conditioned branch with an unconditional prediction and adding a dynamic noise update to strengthen the edit signal. It departs from the score-distillation line (DreamFusion, Magic3D, ProlificDreamer, DMD), which optimizes per-instance 3D assets via implicit 2D score gradients, by instead amortizing 2D fidelity priors into a feed-forward 3D generator through explicit multi-step edited targets and a latent contrastive objective. The contrastive distillation formulation draws on self-evaluation ideas from any-step text-to-image generation [43], adapted here to the 3D latent space of a flow-matching generator (Trellis).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OREO addresses a persistent and practical gap: 3D generators produce good geometry but lack the fine-grained texture and photorealistic detail that 2D diffusion models have internalized from billion-scale image corpora. The on-the-fly pseudo-GT loop eliminates the need for paired 3D ground truth, making fidelity post-training scalable to arbitrary reference images. However, the fidelity gains are notably targeted—substantial on out-of-distribution conceptual designs but marginal on in-distribution GSO objects—suggesting the method primarily rescues the long tail rather than uniformly improving all generations. Open questions include reducing the high inference cost of multi-step FlowEdit editing, extending to scene-level generation, and whether the contrastive latent objective generalizes beyond the Trellis backbone.
