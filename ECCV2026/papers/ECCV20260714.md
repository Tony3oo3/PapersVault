## Problem

Classical Kruppa-based self-calibration assumes constant intrinsics across views, yet real capture pipelines exhibit dynamic intrinsics due to zoom, refocus, or electronic stabilisation. Practical calibration priors (zero skew, fixed aspect, shared principal point) are naturally expressed in intrinsic-parameter space, while Kruppa equations operate in DIAC space, creating a representational mismatch that prior methods handle only for specific regimes. This paper provides a unified algebraic framework that maps arbitrary polynomial intrinsic priors into DIAC space and addresses the resulting algebraic dependence in overdetermined Kruppa systems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes fundamental matrices and epipoles estimated from 2D image correspondences (via the normalised 8-point algorithm), with the fundamental matrices and epipoles treated as fixed data in the Kruppa equations. The unknowns are per-view DIACs (five free parameters each after scale normalisation), from which camera intrinsics (focal lengths, principal point, skew) are recovered via Cholesky/RQ factorisation with task constraints enforced by construction. Because the full Kruppa-plus-prior system is overdetermined, the solver operates on locally independent square subsystems sampled via Jacobian rank tests, and the final intrinsic estimate is selected as the solution closest to ground truth among all roots of the sampled relaxations.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage algebraic pipeline. First, task-dependent intrinsic priors (e.g. zero skew, fixed aspect, cross-view sharing) are encoded as polynomial constraints in K-space and pushed forward through the rational map K → ω* = KK^T using symbolic Gröbner basis computation, yielding explicit polynomial equations in DIAC variables that combine directly with the 15 quadratic Kruppa constraints per view pair. Second, because the combined system is overdetermined and algebraically dependent, the paper constructs locally independent square subsystems (minimal relaxations) by evaluating the full Jacobian at a generic solution and selecting linearly independent rows via rank-revealing QR with randomised row ordering and scaling. The resulting square polynomial systems are solved by homotopy continuation with monodromy, and algebraic complexity is characterised through root counts, BKK bounds, and Bézout bounds. This framework handles arbitrary structured priors uniformly, enabling previously unsolved configurations such as varying focal lengths with shared principal point and zero skew.

## Results

On the INFLUX real-world dataset (5 sequences, 10 frames each), the Kruppa-DIAC solver achieves Δfg = 0.725±0.169 on the shared-focal task (ff000-f), outperforming the linear DAQ baseline (0.857±0.116). On the varying-intrinsic task ffuv0-uv (zero skew, fixed aspect, shared principal point, 3 views), the method achieves Δfg = 0.790±0.104 and Δuv = 0.312±0.086, substantially beating the Lourakis pairwise initialisation (Δfg = 0.805±0.140, Δuv = 0.797±0.001). On the more realistic fguv0-uv task (varying focal, shared principal point, 4 views), the solver yields Δfg = 0.748±0.105 and Δuv = 0.464±0.133. Under simulated EIS cropping, the method recovers principal points with Δuv ≈ 0.4–0.7, while centre-based COLMAP-guess fails with Δuv = 2.483±0.608.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Pollefeys linear DAQ baseline [24] on the shared-focal two-view task (ff000-f), the Kruppa-DIAC solver wins on real INFLUX data (Δfg 0.725 vs. 0.857) but is slightly behind on synthetic data at high noise (Δfg 0.511 vs. 0.457 at σ=1.0 px), confirming parity in the classical regime. Against the Lourakis pairwise initialisation [22] on the ffuv0-uv task (zero skew, fixed aspect, shared principal point, 3 views), the Kruppa solver achieves comparable focal-length accuracy (Δfg 0.790 vs. 0.805) but a dramatically better principal-point estimate (Δuv 0.312 vs. 0.797), because Lourakis's method hard-codes the principal point at image centre. The DAQ baseline is not applicable to the varying-intrinsic tasks (ffuv0-uv, fguv0-uv), so the Kruppa formulation is the only method that handles those configurations. Under simulated EIS cropping (Table 3), both Kruppa tasks recover principal points within Δuv ≈ 0.4–0.7, while the centre-based COLMAP-guess heuristic fails catastrophically (Δuv = 2.483), demonstrating that solving the algebraic DIAC constraints is essential when the principal point is not at the image centre.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Kruppa-equation self-calibration lineage (Hartley, Luong & Faugeras) from the classical constant-intrinsic setting to genuinely varying intrinsics by introducing a systematic pushforward of intrinsic priors from K-space to DIAC space via the rational map K ↦ KKᵀ. It builds directly on the minimal-relaxation framework of Kosaka, Duff & Pajdla [20] for constructing locally independent square subsystems from overdetermined polynomial systems, and on the DAQ-based solvability analysis of Pollefeys et al. [24] and the varying-focal Kruppa formulations of Lourakis & Deriche [22]. The key departure is unification: rather than treating each varying-intrinsic regime (shared focal, fixed aspect, zero skew) as a separate ad hoc problem, the paper formulates arbitrary degree-1 polynomial priors in K-space and derives their DIAC-space images via Gröbner basis computation, yielding a single algebraic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic algebraic-complexity analysis (root counts, BKK/Bezout bounds) for varying-intrinsic Kruppa self-calibration, revealing that naive degrees-of-freedom counting is unreliable—some configurations admit hundreds of thousands or even billions of complex solutions, while others with fewer views are tractable. Practically, it enables stable algebraic initialisation for dynamic-intrinsic video sequences (zoom, electronic stabilisation) where centre-based heuristics fail, validated on the INFLUX benchmark. Limitations include restriction to degree-1 priors, treatment as an algebraic initialiser rather than an end-to-end solver, and no complete classification of critical motion sequences; the broader question of how these algebraic initialisers integrate into modern SfM/SLAM pipelines with non-linear refinement remains open.
