## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video diffusion models rely solely on ray-based (Plücker) conditioning, forcing the network to simultaneously infer appearance and 3D geometry from signals that carry no explicit scene structure. This entanglement leads to suboptimal camera motion consistency, while alternative multi-stage 3D reconstruction pipelines decouple geometry from appearance but cannot jointly optimize both within a unified generation process. The paper asks how to internalize geometric reasoning directly into the diffusion process so that geometry and appearance co-evolve under camera control.

## Input / Output

In the I2V setting, the method takes a single reference image and a target camera trajectory (R, t); a monocular depth estimator produces a depth map for the reference frame, which is channel-replicated to 3 channels and jointly encoded with the RGB image into a shared latent space (T'×16×H/8×W/8). In the T2V setting, a text prompt replaces the reference image. The model outputs both an RGB video and a temporally consistent depth video, with the RGB decoded by a pretrained VAE decoder and the depth serving as a co-evolving geometric signal throughout denoising.

## Method

DualCamCtrl employs a dual-branch Diffusion Transformer (initialized from WAN-V2.1) where RGB and depth latents are denoised in parallel, each conditioned on the same Plücker-encoded camera poses. The key interaction mechanism, SIGMA (SemantIc Guided Mutual Alignment), is a stage-aware bidirectional fusion: in early transformer layers, RGB features anchor semantic structure and flow into the depth branch, while in later layers depth feedback refines geometric consistency in the RGB branch—motivated by CKA analysis revealing asymmetric influence of camera poses vs. depth across the denoising trajectory. A 3D convolutional fusion block with a bottleneck design and framewise gating captures spatiotemporal context that 1D linear fusion misses. Training proceeds in two stages: a decoupled stage where each branch learns independently (depth supervised by pseudo-labels from monocular estimation), followed by a fusion stage where the zero-initialized cross-branch fusion block is activated, with the overall loss combining RGB and depth noise-prediction objectives weighted by λ.

## Results

On the I2V setting, DualCamCtrl achieves FVD 80.38 and FID 49.85 on RealEstate10K, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (Seva: FVD 104.2, FID 76.69; Wan: FVD 109.2, FID 77.80). The headline geometric metric is Rotation Error (RE), where DualCamCtrl reduces RE from 2.62 (Seva) to 1.25 on RealEstate10K and from 1.01 (Wan) to 0.88 on DL3DV, representing over 40% reduction compared to prior SOTA. In the T2V setting, RE drops from 2.67 (AC3D) to 1.23 and TE from 0.38 to 0.25 on RealEstate10K, while FVD improves from 415.6 to 408.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualCamCtrl outperforms all five baselines (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, Seva, Wan, AC3D) on every geometric accuracy metric (RE and TE) across both I2V and T2V settings. The most dramatic gap is in Rotation Error: on RealEstate10K I2V, DualCamCtrl achieves RE 1.25 versus 2.62 (Seva), 2.08 (Wan), 2.38 (CameraCtrl), and 2.80 (MotionCtrl) — a 40–55% reduction. On FVD (distribution-level realism), DualCamCtrl leads by a wide margin (80.38 vs. 104.2 for the next-best Seva on RealEstate10K I2V; 92.2 vs. 127.9 for Wan on DL3DV). In the T2V setting, DualCamCtrl edges out AC3D on FVD (408.1 vs. 415.6) but dominates on RE (1.23 vs. 2.67) and TE (0.25 vs. 0.38). The only metric where DualCamCtrl does not lead is CLIPSIM on RealEstate10K I2V (0.2998 vs. 0.3059 for Seva), a marginal difference that does not affect the overall ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on WAN-V2.1 as its backbone diffusion transformer, extending the camera-controlled video generation line initiated by MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, and AC3D, which condition on Plücker ray embeddings alone. It departs from these methods by arguing that ray-based conditioning entangles appearance and geometry in a single representation, and from multi-stage 3D reconstruction pipelines (ReconX, ViewCrafter, GEN3C) by internalizing geometric reasoning into the diffusion process itself via a parallel depth branch. The SIGMA mechanism and two-stage training strategy are novel architectural contributions that address the cross-modal interaction problem, which prior work has not systematically studied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >40% reduction in rotation errors and consistent FVD/FID improvements demonstrate that explicit geometric co-generation is a viable and effective alternative to both pure ray conditioning and external 3D pipelines, offering a cleaner end-to-end formulation for camera-controlled generation. However, the dual-branch design doubles backbone parameters (2.7B total), and the authors acknowledge this may become unfavorable at larger scales; distilling a lightweight depth branch is identified as a key future direction. Large inter-frame motion remains an unresolved failure mode, and the reliance on pseudo-labeled depth (from monocular estimators) introduces a quality ceiling that limits how much geometric precision can be achieved in practice.
