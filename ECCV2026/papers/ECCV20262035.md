## Problem

Building effective reward models for visual generative models is bottlenecked by the need for either massive pairwise preference annotations (up to 1M labeled pairs for PickScore, 137K for ImageReward) or heavily engineered multimetric scoring pipelines, both of which are costly, labor-intensive, and difficult to scale. This paper asks whether a reward model can be trained from a small set of unlabeled, positive-only representative samples without any pairwise comparisons or explicit quality-dimension definitions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262035/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a small fixed set of unlabeled representative samples (Preference Proxy Data, e.g., 500 Midjourney images or 2K safe images) plus raw outputs from the current generator, and produces a scalar confidence score per sample via a lightweight MLP head on frozen CLIP-Vision features. These scores are then used downstream for Best-of-N selection (picking the top-1 from K=10 candidates), SFT dataset construction (training on highest-scoring samples), or DPO pair construction (pairing highest- and lowest-scoring samples per prompt). The PPD remains fixed across all training rounds; only the generator outputs change, making the discrimination task progressively harder.

## Method

DisRM trains a lightweight discriminator (CLIP-Vision encoder + Reward Projection Layer MLP) with binary cross-entropy to distinguish fixed Preference Proxy Data from the current generator's outputs, requiring no pairwise labels. A Rank-based Bootstrapping strategy augments training by scoring additional generator outputs with the current DisRM, selecting top-M and bottom-M samples, and assigning soft pseudo-labels y = e^(−α·r) based on rank, which are combined with the original PPD and raw outputs for retraining. The key architectural insight is that the learning objective is inherently dynamic: as the generator improves via SFT or DPO using DisRM scores, its outputs become harder to distinguish from PPD, forcing DisRM to sharpen its decision boundary each round without any new human annotation. This enables multi-round iterative co-refinement (Algorithm 1) where both the reward model and generator are updated simultaneously, unlike static-preference methods such as DiffusionDPO that are limited to single-round training.

## Results

On SD1.5, DisRM-DPO achieves the best FID of 63.61 (vs. 68.15 for DiffusionDPO and 70.19 for SPO) and the best CLIPScore of 0.710, while using only 500 unpaired PPD samples compared to 1M annotated preference pairs for all baselines. On SDXL, DisRM-SFT attains the best FID of 61.74 (vs. 63.24 for DiffusionDPO). A user study confirms a 74.4% human preference rate for DisRM-DPO outputs over the base SD1.5 model, and DisRM shows 70.5% agreement with 20 human raters on high/low-score image pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SD1.5, DisRM-DPO clearly outperforms DiffusionDPO [50] on FID (63.61 vs. 68.15, a 4.5-point gap) and CLIPScore (0.710 vs. 0.709), while DiffusionDPO retains a slight edge on ImageReward (0.180 vs. 0.240 — actually DisRM wins here too at 0.240). DisRM-DPO also beats SPO [25] on FID (63.61 vs. 70.19) and CLIPScore (0.710 vs. 0.666), though SPO leads on PickScore (20.248 vs. 20.032) and ImageReward (0.310 vs. 0.240). DiffusionNPO [52] is the weakest baseline, trailing DisRM on every metric. On SDXL, DiffusionDPO still leads on ImageReward (1.033 vs. 0.915) and PickScore (21.628 vs. 21.305), but DisRM-SFT achieves a better FID (61.74 vs. 63.24). In the reward-model-as-annotator comparison (Table 4), DisRM avoids the overfitting seen with ImageReward (which degrades CLIPScore to 0.686) and PickScore (which inflates FID to 70.97), achieving the best FID of 67.42 with the most balanced scores across all five metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisRM repurposes the GAN discriminator paradigm for reward modeling: rather than training a scoring function on massive pairwise preference data (as in PickScore, ImageReward, HPS), it trains a lightweight CLIP-based binary classifier to distinguish a small set of representative 'good' samples (PPD) from current generator outputs. The post-training pipeline builds directly on DiffusionDPO [50] for preference-optimized fine-tuning and RAFT [8] for reward-ranked SFT, but departs from both by making the reward model itself dynamic—retrained each round against the evolving generator rather than frozen on a static preference dataset. The Rank-based Bootstrapping strategy for pseudo-labeling generator outputs is a self-training mechanism that augments the limited PPD without additional human annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2000× reduction in annotation requirement (500 unlabeled samples vs. ~1M pairwise labels) makes reward-model-based alignment practically accessible for smaller labs and niche applications where large-scale annotation is infeasible. The iterative co-refinement loop—where the discriminator and generator improve simultaneously without new human data—opens a path toward continuous, self-sustaining alignment that static-preference methods like DiffusionDPO cannot support. Open questions include whether the approach scales to frontier-scale models, how sensitive performance is to PPD curation quality (the paper offers only empirical heuristics), and whether the safety-alignment setting's FID degradation (e.g., 110→118 for SD1.5-DPO) signals a fundamental quality-safety trade-off or an artifact of the small PPD.
