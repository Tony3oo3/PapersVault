## Problem

Safety alignment methods for text-to-image diffusion models appear to preserve utility under standard coarse metrics (FID, CLIPScore), but this masks substantial degradation in fine-grained compositional instruction-following (object counts, attributes, spatial relationships) that structured benchmarks like TIFA reveal. The paper diagnoses the root cause as 'semantic collapse'—a contraction of embedding spread and distortion of local neighborhood structure in the text encoder's prompt embedding space—and proposes a geometry-aware fix.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on Stable Diffusion v1.4, taking natural language text prompts as input and producing 512×512 RGB images via 50 DDIM steps at guidance scale 7.5. During safety alignment training, only the CLIP text encoder is updated (UNet frozen) using 6,911 safe-unsafe prompt pairs from the CoPro sexual category; at inference, the modified encoder maps unsafe prompts toward safe embeddings while the frozen UNet generates the image. Evaluation spans structured benchmarks (TIFA with ~4,000 prompts and 25,000+ QA pairs, GenEval with 553 prompts), distributional metrics on COCO 30k, and safety robustness under multiple jailbreak attacks.

## Method

SAGE (Structure-Aware Geometric Regularization) augments the DES safety alignment objective with two geometry-preserving regularizers applied to the text encoder. First, the Embedding Spread Preservation (ESP) loss imposes a one-sided penalty max(0, S₀ − S_θ) on the average squared deviation of ℓ₂-normalized prompt embeddings from their batch mean, preventing the embedding distribution from contracting below the base model's spread. Second, the Local Structure Alignment (LSA) loss preserves pairwise cosine similarity relationships among top-K (K=15) nearest-neighbor prompt pairs identified under the base encoder, but is applied to concept-perturbed embeddings (shifted along the 'nudity' direction by α=1) to avoid inadvertently restoring unsafe geometric patterns. The full training objective combines the DES safety loss, point-wise utility alignment, ESP, and perturbed LSA with learned weights, trained via AdamW at lr 1e-5 for two epochs with batch size 128.

## Results

On the TIFA structured-fidelity benchmark, SAGE achieves 75.4 (vs. 76.3 base, only a 1.2% drop), outperforming DES (71.6, +5.0%), STEREO (69.9, +7.3%), and RECE (74.8, +0.6%). It also attains the best FID (15.93, even below the base model's 17.23) and a near-base CLIPScore (26.4 vs. 26.5). On safety, the average Attack Success Rate across five jailbreak/direct-unsafe benchmarks is 1.2%, comparable to DES (1.0%) and far below the base model (67.6%). On GenEval, SAGE scores 59.8 (only 1.6% below base), versus 56.7 for DES and 46.7 for STEREO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE outperforms all eight baselines on TIFA structured fidelity, with the largest margins over SafeCLIP (+15.3), SafeRCLIP (+14.7), MACE (+12.8), and Adv-Unlearn (+12.3). Against the closest safety-utility competitor DES, SAGE gains +5.0 TIFA, +0.3 CLIPScore, and a lower FID (15.93 vs. 16.23), at the cost of a marginally higher average ASR (1.2 vs. 1.0). Compared to RECE, which is the second-best on TIFA (74.8), SAGE improves TIFA by +0.6 while reducing ASR dramatically (1.2 vs. 18.1) and FID (15.93 vs. 17.51). Inference-time guidance (SLD) and CLIP-space alignment methods (SafeCLIP, SafeRCLIP) are clearly dominated: SAGE beats SLD by +1.5 TIFA and 58.6 ASR points, and beats SafeCLIP/SafeRCLIP by >14 TIFA points and >33 ASR points. On GenEval, SAGE (59.8) is the only method within 2% of the base model (60.8) while maintaining ASR below 2%, whereas MACE and SafeCLIP/SafeRCLIP lose 35–40% of compositional capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DES (Ahn & Jung, 2025), a text-encoder fine-tuning method that steers unsafe prompt embeddings toward safe anchors while using point-wise cosine alignment to preserve utility on benign prompts. The key conceptual departure is from this point-wise utility objective: SAGE augments DES with two geometric regularizers (embedding spread preservation and local structure alignment) that preserve the global and local relational structure of the prompt embedding space rather than constraining each prompt independently. The paper also positions itself against a broader family of text-based safety methods (SafeCLIP, SafeRCLIP, Adv-Unlearn, STEREO, RECE, MACE, SLD) by introducing a unified diagnostic lens—semantic collapse—that explains their shared utility degradation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard utility metrics (FID, CLIPScore) mask up to 21% structured semantic degradation challenges the recent narrative that the safety-utility tradeoff is largely resolved, and motivates the community to adopt structured evaluation (TIFA, GenEval) as a standard reporting protocol for safety alignment. The semantic collapse diagnosis—embedding spread contraction and neighborhood distortion—provides a mechanistic, geometry-based explanation that is actionable: it directly motivates the proposed regularizers and could inform future safety methods beyond text-encoder fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the geometric regularization generalizes to other safety categories (violence, PII), other base architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), and whether the perturbation-based LSA variant is necessary or an artifact of the specific 'nudity' concept direction used.
