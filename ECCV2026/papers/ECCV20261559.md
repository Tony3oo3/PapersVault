## Problem

Existing temporal trackers incorporate learnable temporal tokens to model cross-frame dynamics, yet the paper demonstrates through perturbation experiments that these tokens are effectively marginalized during joint optimization: corrupting them causes negligible performance drops (<0.2%), while providing oracle temporal cues yields ~10% gains. The root cause is identified as spatial domination, where dense spatial supervision from template matching provides a shortcut that systematically suppresses gradients to the temporal pathway, leaving the architectural temporal channel unused.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes a 7-frame video sequence comprising 3 target template crops (112×112) and 4 search region crops (224×224), tokenized and concatenated with learnable temporal tokens into a single ViT input. During training, the search region is additionally duplicated as an auxiliary input that is structurally isolated from the templates. At inference, the auxiliary branch and LoRA modules are entirely removed, so the model processes only the standard template-search sequence and outputs a bounding box prediction plus a tracking quality score, with identical parameter count and FLOPs to the base SUTrack architecture.

## Method

DASTrack introduces Decoupled Auxiliary Supervision (DAS), a training-only mechanism that duplicates the search region into an auxiliary branch and applies a customized attention mask that severs all attention paths between the auxiliary tokens and the template tokens, forcing the auxiliary branch to localize the target using only temporal tokens. To prevent gradient conflicts in the shared ViT backbone, task-specific LoRA modules (rank 64) are injected into self-attention and FFN layers with a token-level gating mask that restricts LoRA updates exclusively to auxiliary search tokens, creating distinct parameter subspaces for spatial and temporal learning. The two branches are jointly optimized with an MLE-based adaptive loss weighting that dynamically balances the primary and auxiliary objectives without manual tuning. At inference, all auxiliary components are discarded, preserving the original architecture and introducing zero additional computational overhead.

## Results

On LaSOT (medium-scale, 224/256 resolution), DASTrack achieves 73.4% AUC, 84.8% PNorm, and 81.5% Precision, outperforming the strongest baseline SUTrack224 (73.2/83.4/80.5) and ODTrack384 (73.2/83.2/80.6). On the more challenging VideoCube benchmark, DASTrack reaches an unprecedented 71.3% Success Rate and 66.4% Precision, surpassing SUTrack224 (67.9/62.1) by 3.4 and 4.3 points respectively. At 384 resolution, DASTrack384 further improves to 74.6% AUC on LaSOT and 73.6% SR on VideoCube, setting new state-of-the-art across all four long-term benchmarks. On short-term TrackingNet, DASTrack achieves 85.3% AUC (86.6% at 384), the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DASTrack (224/256) achieves the best LaSOT AUC (73.4%) and PNorm (84.8%) among all medium-scale trackers, edging out SUTrack224 (73.2/83.4) and ODTrack384 (73.2/83.2) by 0.2 and 1.4 points respectively. The most substantial gains appear on VideoCube, where DASTrack's 71.3% SR surpasses SUTrack224 (67.9%) by 3.4 points and ODTrack384 (69.1%) by 2.2 points, indicating stronger robustness under severe occlusion and deformation. On TNL2k, DASTrack trails SUTrack224 in AUC (64.6 vs. 65.0) but leads in Precision (69.9 vs. 67.9), suggesting slightly different trade-offs in localization tightness. At 384 resolution, DASTrack384 dominates all baselines across every benchmark, achieving 74.6% LaSOT AUC (vs. SUTrack384's 74.4%) and 73.6% VideoCube SR (vs. 69.7%). On short-term TrackingNet, DASTrack leads with 85.3% AUC (86.6% at 384), while on GOT-10k the 224 variant (76.5% AO) is slightly below ODTrack384 (77.0%), though DASTrack384 (79.0%) clearly surpasses it. Overall, DASTrack wins or ties on the majority of metrics, with the only consistent loss being TNL2k AUC at 224 resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the one-stream ViT tracking paradigm established by OSTrack and SUTrack, and specifically targets the temporal-token architectures introduced by ARTrackV2, ODTrack, AQATrack, and EVPTrack. Its key conceptual departure is from the shortcut learning literature: rather than designing stronger temporal modules or better information propagation strategies (the focus of prior temporal tracking work), it diagnoses that existing temporal tokens are marginalized by spatial features during joint optimization and proposes a training-time decoupling mechanism (DAS) to restore their utility. The use of LoRA is repurposed from parameter-efficient fine-tuning to structural gradient isolation between primary and auxiliary tasks, and the adaptive loss weighting draws on multi-task uncertainty estimation (Kendall et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that temporal tokens in current SOTA trackers are functionally inert (perturbation causes ~0.2% change while oracle temporal cues yield ~10% gains) challenges a widely held assumption in the tracking community that architectural inclusion of temporal components guarantees their utilization. The DAS solution is practically attractive because it is training-only with zero inference overhead, making it a drop-in improvement for existing temporal trackers. However, the actual performance gain is modest (0.5–0.8% AUC), and the approach is validated on only two backbone families (HiViT, ViT) and a limited set of benchmarks. Open questions include whether the spatial-domination phenomenon generalizes to other tasks with competing spatial and temporal signals (e.g., video understanding, action recognition), and whether the LoRA-based decoupling is necessary or whether simpler gradient-stopping strategies suffice.
