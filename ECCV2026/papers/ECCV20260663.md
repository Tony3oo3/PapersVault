## Problem

Training-free diffusion-based image editing methods that rely solely on text guidance cannot preserve instance-specific identity attributes (facial structure, surface texture, object-specific geometry) that exist below the level of linguistic abstraction. Meanwhile, existing multi-LoRA composition techniques are designed for text-to-image generation and do not address the spatial alignment and subject-consistency requirements of image editing. No unified, training-free framework existed for target-less, multi-concept image editing that combines optimisation-based editing with LoRA-driven concept composition.

## Input / Output

Given a source image (512×512 RGB), a target text prompt, a source text prompt, and N independently trained LoRA adapters (each encoding a distinct concept such as character identity, clothing style, or object geometry), CDS produces a single edited target image. The key constraint is that no reference samples of the desired final edit are available, making the task truly target-less. All LoRAs are scaled by a fixed weight of 0.8, spatial patches are 2×2, and the optimisation runs for 300 denoising steps on a single GPU.

## Method

CDS unifies two synergistic components within a single optimisation loop. First, a refined distillation objective enforces strict descending timestep ordering (coarse-to-fine denoising), adds an explicit L1 latent-alignment regulariser (η|x₀^tgt − x₀^src|) that avoids the vanishing-gradient problem of prior trajectory-alignment methods under ordered timesteps, and integrates negative-prompt classifier-free guidance directly into the gradient computation. Second, a dynamic concept-weighting mechanism computes, at every denoising step, the patch-wise cosine similarity between each LoRA's noise prediction and the base model's prediction; a temperature-scaled SoftMin across all N concepts yields spatially-varying weights that are upsampled and applied as Hadamard products, so the final composite noise prediction is a spatially-aware weighted sum of all LoRA outputs. This prevents concept clashing by allowing each LoRA to dominate only in regions where it diverges most from the base model, enabling seamless multi-concept composition without retraining.

## Results

On InstructPix2Pix, CDS achieves a CLIPScore of 0.308±0.042 (statistically significant at 99% CI) with LPIPS of 0.100±0.042, outperforming the prior SoTA PDS (0.298±0.044 CLIPScore, 0.096 LPIPS) and substantially exceeding DDS (0.225 CLIPScore). On the ComposLoRA multi-concept editing benchmark (up to 5 LoRAs), CDS achieves the lowest LPIPS across most configurations (e.g., 0.314 total on Anime vs. 0.345 for Switch and 0.447 for Merge), while CLIPScore remains on par with baselines, indicating that CLIPScore alone cannot capture instance-level concept fidelity. GPT-4V and human evaluations confirm CDS achieves the highest perceived image and composition quality, with a 38% pairwise win rate versus 31% (Composite), 25% (Switch), and 5% (Merge).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDS [8], CDS improves CLIPScore by 0.083 (0.308 vs. 0.225) while maintaining a comparable LPIPS (0.100 vs. 0.104), demonstrating that ordered timesteps, regularisation, and negative-prompt guidance collectively resolve DDS's instability without sacrificing edit strength. Against PDS [15], the closest prior SoTA, CDS achieves a statistically significant CLIPScore gain (0.308 vs. 0.298 at 99% CI) with a marginally higher LPIPS (0.100 vs. 0.096), confirming that PDS's trajectory-alignment regularisation vanishes under ordered timesteps while CDS's explicit L1 term does not. Against InstructPix2Pix [1], CDS dominates on both metrics (CLIPScore 0.308 vs. 0.219; LPIPS 0.100 vs. 0.322), reflecting the advantage of optimisation-based editing over a single forward pass. On the multi-concept ComposLoRA benchmark, CDS outperforms Composite [40] on LPIPS in every per-N slice (e.g., 0.377 vs. 0.364 at N=4 on Reality) and on Anime total (0.314 vs. 0.665), while CLIPScore is essentially tied, suggesting CLIPScore measures generic object presence rather than specific instance identity. CDS also beats Switch [40] on LPIPS for N≥3 (e.g., 0.377 vs. 0.467 at N=4) and Merge [12] across all configurations (e.g., 0.377 vs. 0.634 at N=4 on Reality), with the gap widening as the number of composed LoRAs increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDS unifies two previously separate research threads: optimisation-based diffusion editing (DDS, PDS) and inference-time multi-LoRA composition (Composite, Switch, Merge, MultiLFG). It refines the DDS objective by adding ordered timesteps, a training-schedule-independent L1 regulariser, and negative-prompt guidance—fixing the vanishing-gradient issue that PDS exhibits under sequential stepping. The dynamic concept-weighting mechanism extends the authors' own LoRAtorio idea (using base-model prediction distance as a certainty proxy) from a global scalar to a patch-wise spatial SoftMin, enabling spatially-aware multi-concept composition without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By anchoring edits in pretrained LoRA adapters rather than text alone, CDS addresses a genuine gap: instance-specific visual attributes (exact facial geometry, unique wear patterns, character identity) that fall below linguistic abstraction. The training-free, reference-free constraint makes the approach broadly applicable without per-concept fine-tuning or target examples. Key limitations include linear runtime scaling with LoRA count (mitigated by batching), sensitivity to LoRA quality and mutual alignment, and a hard ceiling set by the base model's own artefacts. Open questions include whether the patch-wise weighting generalises to newer architectures (SDXL, Flux), whether it scales beyond five concepts, and whether the ComposLoRA-style editing benchmark can be extended to more diverse or adversarial concept combinations.
