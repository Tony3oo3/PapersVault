## Problem

Standard self-supervised learning models for single-cell microscopy images systematically overemphasize coarse factors such as imaging modality, causing the latent space to form modality-driven superclusters that obscure fine-grained morphological substructure. Existing hierarchy-aware contrastive methods (e.g., HCSC) rely on hard positive/negative mining that is unstable and under-emphasizes subtle morphology, while distillation-based models like DINO are tuned for generic semantics and do not model multi-level structure. The paper asks how to learn a label-free embedding that simultaneously preserves modality-level superclusters and sharpens biologically meaningful subclusters at every hierarchical depth.

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-cell microscopy crops (one cell per image, extracted from multi-cell datasets via instance-mask-to-oriented-bounding-box conversion) spanning 8 imaging modalities and 208 cell classes, together with pre-computed zero-shot segmentation masks generated by CellposeSAM that serve as weak morphological priors. The output is a D-dimensional L2-normalized embedding per cell that organizes into a nested hierarchy: modality-level superclusters at the root and morphology-driven subclusters at the leaves. The curated HASSL-SingleCellBench corpus contains 2.39M single-cell crops from 20 instance-segmentation benchmarks, split 90/10 within each dataset into a pooled test set.

## Method

HASSL combines two complementary components on top of a ViT-S/16 backbone with DINOv3-style self-distillation. First, a double-teacher distillation replaces the single EMA image teacher with an additional segmentation teacher that conditions on both the image and its pre-computed mask, producing mask-informed targets that bias the student toward shape-aware representations and initiate breaking of modality-driven superclusters; the two branches are combined via a convex weight γ ramped from 0 to 0.2 over training. Second, a hierarchy-aware contrastive loss runs HDBSCAN on in-batch embeddings to obtain a condensed cluster tree, then mines positive prototypes (ancestor centroids along the leaf-to-root path) and negative prototypes (sibling cluster centroids at each level) for each anchor. Crucially, all prototypes are weighted by HDBSCAN stability (persistence) λ: deeper, more reliable clusters receive higher positive weight, while closely related siblings receive attenuated negative weight, implemented as a stability-weighted hinge loss with margin m. This stability weighting prevents uniform repulsion from distorting the latent space and preserves the natural coarse-to-fine hierarchy without any explicit labels.

## Results

On the curated 2.3M-cell, 208-class HASSL-SingleCellBench, HASSL achieves 50.9% Acc@1, 83.5% Acc@9, and 57.8% mAP, improving over the DINOv3 baseline (49.4%, 80.9%, 56.5%) by +1.5, +2.6, and +1.3 points respectively, and attaining the best NMI (47.9) and AMI (47.3) among all methods. On the multi-level hierarchy subset (depth>1), HASSL gains +6.3% top-9 retrieval accuracy over the baseline. On the unseen Allen Institute drug-perturbation task, HASSL reaches 92.0% F1-weighted, a +7.8-point improvement over the next-best model (Cellpaint-DINO at 81.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HASSL outperforms all six baselines on every retrieval metric in Table 1, with the largest gaps over scDINO (+8.2 Acc@9) and Cellpaint-DINO (+14.6 Acc@9), confirming that neither fluorescent-only DINO adaptation nor generic Cell Painting pretraining captures the multi-modality hierarchy. Against the closest competitor, the DINOv3 baseline retrained on the same data, HASSL gains +1.5 Acc@1, +3.6 Acc@3, +3.4 Acc@5, and +2.6 Acc@9, plus +1.1 NMI and +1.1 AMI. On the downstream drug-perturbation task (Table 3), HASSL leads Cellpaint-DINO by +10.3 Acc and +10.3 F1-weighted, and DINOv3 by +10.3 Acc, demonstrating that hierarchy-aware structure is critical for detecting subtle morphological changes induced by specific drugs. On the unseen Human Protein Atlas benchmark (Table 4), Cellpaint-DINO narrowly edges HASSL (54.8 vs. 54.2 Acc), likely because its JUMP-CP training distribution closely matches HPA's immunofluorescent modality; HASSL still surpasses all other baselines by 2.7–20.8 Acc points. On the curated multi-class classification (Table 2), HASSL achieves the best Acc (45.6%) and F1-weighted (43.6%), while Cellpaint-DINO retains a slight F1-macro edge (49.4 vs. 48.8), suggesting HASSL's hierarchy-aware loss particularly benefits rare subclasses.

## Relation to Prior Work

HASSL extends the DINOv3 self-distillation framework for cell imaging by adding two orthogonal components: a segmentation-mask teacher that injects morphological structure into the latent space, and a stability-weighted HDBSCAN prototype-contrastive loss that sharpens subcluster boundaries at multiple hierarchical levels. The closest prior work is HCSC, which also constructs hierarchical prototypes for contrastive learning, but relies on recursive k-NN sampling with hard positive/negative assignments; HASSL replaces this with HDBSCAN's condensed tree and persistence-based weighting for more stable gradients. On the cell-imaging side, the paper positions itself against CellPaint-DINO, scDINO, ChadaViT, and OpenPhenom, arguing that these methods are channel- or assay-aware rather than morphology-aware because they never link embeddings to segmentation geometry during training. The unified 2.3M-cell, 208-class benchmark consolidates previously scattered single-cell datasets into a common evaluation suite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modality-dominance problem—where imaging setup and batch effects overwhelm fine morphological cues in SSL embeddings—is a persistent bottleneck for cell-imaging foundation models, and HASSL's double-teacher + hierarchy-aware loss offers a practical, drop-in remedy with minimal compute overhead (+2.3% wall-clock). The curated 2.3M-cell benchmark spanning eight modalities and both flat and deep hierarchies is a practical resource that the field has lacked. Open questions include whether the segmentation teacher's benefit generalizes to modalities where zero-shot segmentation is unreliable, whether the HDBSCAN term scales to batches large enough to capture very deep hierarchies, and whether the approach transfers to 3D or time-lapse cell imaging. The paper also notes the method is architecture-agnostic and could be combined with BYOL, SimCLR, or InfoNCE, but these variants remain untested.
