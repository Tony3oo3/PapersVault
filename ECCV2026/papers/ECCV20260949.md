## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) have been proposed as interpretable tools for concept-level manipulation in diffusion models, but direct intervention in their latent space frequently produces out-of-distribution activations and severe visual artifacts. This paper systematically characterizes this detection-versus-intervention gap: while SAE latents reliably localize semantic concepts, they are not inherently suitable as control knobs for steering, revealing a fundamental limitation of multiplier-based latent manipulation for unlearning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260949/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on cross-attention layer feature maps (H×W×D) extracted from the denoising UNet of Stable Diffusion v1.5 or SDXL Turbo, conditioned on text prompts of the form 'An image of {object} in {style} style.' It consumes a pre-trained TopK SAE and a concept-latent dictionary to produce a binary spatial detection mask, then outputs a modified feature map where concept-containing patch embeddings are swapped with in-distribution ones from the same forward pass. No model weights are modified and no grid search over intervention strength is required.

## Method

The core contribution is Patch Embedding Replacement (PER), which decouples SAE-based concept detection from intervention. SAEs (trained as TopK SAEs on cross-attention activations, optionally with supervised losses from SAEmnesia or G-SAEs) are used solely to identify which spatial patches activate concept-associated latents above their dataset-mean threshold, yielding a binary detection mask. Instead of scaling SAE latents by a negative multiplier and reconstructing through the decoder (which pushes activations outside the DM's manifold), PER replaces each detected patch embedding with one uniformly sampled from a non-detected location in the same feature map, optionally dilating the mask by a padding factor p to account for spatial concept bleed. Because every replacement activation originates from the model's own forward pass, all outputs remain in-distribution, eliminating the distributional shift and artifacts inherent to reconstruction-based steering while removing the need for per-concept intervention strength tuning.

## Results

On the UnlearnCanvas benchmark, PER applied to G-SAE achieves the highest Global Average (GA) of 90.88% (average of UA, IRA, CRA, and 100−AR), with a peak UA of 94.02% and CRA of 95.87%. The best CRA score across all configurations is 97.97% (SAEmnesia + PER), and the best AR reduction is from 61.0% to 8.0% on SDXL Turbo. PER consistently improves GA over its respective baseline by 9–18 percentage points across all four SAE pipelines, while eliminating the need for per-concept grid search over intervention strength γ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PER is evaluated as a drop-in replacement for the steering step in four existing SAE-based unlearning pipelines: SAeUron, SAEmnesia, G-SAE, and SAE on SDXL Turbo. On the primary GA metric, PER wins against every baseline: SAeUron 84.19% vs. 72.47%, SAEmnesia 91.44% vs. 81.18%, G-SAE 90.88% vs. 81.69%, and SDXL Turbo 64.16% vs. 46.15%. The largest gains come from the dramatic reduction in Artifacts Rate (e.g., SAeUron AR drops from 57.0% to 16.3%; SDXL Turbo from 61.0% to 8.0%) and from improved cross-domain retention (CRA rises by 12–18 pp for SAeUron and SAEmnesia). On Unlearning Accuracy, PER trades a small UA decrease for SAeUron (85.37% vs. 87.16%) and SAEmnesia (91.37% vs. 94.65%) but substantially improves UA for G-SAE (94.02% vs. 78.14%). In adversarial robustness (UnlearnDiffAtk), results are mixed: SAeUron + PER shows slightly worse attack effectiveness (56.30 vs. 49.50), while SAEmnesia + PER is more robust (34.90 vs. 40.10), suggesting that the benefit of in-distribution replacement is most pronounced when the baseline steering is already strong.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SAE-based unlearning pipeline established by SAeUron, SAEmnesia, G-SAE, and SAE-on-SDXL-Turbo, all of which follow the encode–steer–decode paradigm for concept erasure in diffusion models. It departs from this paradigm by repositioning SAEs from intervention tools to pure detection tools, drawing on prior evidence from the LLM literature (Wu et al., O'Brien et al., Mayne et al.) that SAE latents are unreliable steering vectors. The resulting Patch Embedding Replacement (PER) method is conceptually simple—replacing detected concept-containing patches with in-distribution patches from the same feature map—yet it sidesteps the distributional mismatch that plagues all reconstruction-based SAE steering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's core finding—that monosemantic SAE features are effective detectors but poor control knobs in diffusion models—challenges a widely held assumption in the interpretability community and has implications beyond unlearning for any SAE-based editing or steering task in generative models. Practically, PER eliminates the per-concept grid search over intervention strength and dramatically reduces visual artifacts (e.g., 57%→16.3% AR for SAeUron), making SAE-guided unlearning more deployable. Open questions include whether the mixed adversarial robustness results (improved for SAEmnesia, worsened for SAeUron) indicate a fundamental limitation of inference-time patch replacement, whether the approach extends to multi-concept or style unlearning without degradation, and whether the detection-only framing can be generalized to other generative architectures beyond UNet-based diffusion models.
