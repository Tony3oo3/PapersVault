## Problem

Existing agentic slide generation systems handle page-level design passively, relying on prespecified templates or verbose user instructions rather than inferring a user's latent design intent. This leaves Page-level Slide Personalization (PSP) unresolved: the system cannot adapt layout, hierarchy, and styling to a user's contextual preferences without explicit, detailed specifications that are rarely available at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262812/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-level text instruction x describing slide content, a small set of K reference slides D_ref exemplifying the user's contextual style, and page visual assets. It produces a personalized slide s by first inferring a latent plan z (a step-by-step actionable specification with layout coordinates and element styling) that is then executed by a black-box executor E (e.g., a coding agent using python-pptx). Notably, D_ref need not cover the optimal layout, allowing creative design beyond template replication, and the plan z is executor-agnostic so PSP need not be re-run when E changes.

## Method

SPIRE (Structural Planning via Inverse REconstruction) formulates PSP as an inverse planning problem and solves it via a structural denoising surrogate. The key mechanism is controlled corruption of gold slides: random element-level structural perturbations are applied along three dimensions (spatial layout, visual hierarchy, stylistic coherence), producing verifiable self-supervised triplets. Two agents—both fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct via DAPO reinforcement learning—are trained on this shared signal: a critic learns to produce structured semantic feedback identifying discrepancies (rewarded by element-level verification against ground-truth corruption), and a planner learns to generate or revise executable plans (rewarded by a plan-visual matching judgment from a VLM). Theoretical analysis proves that structural denoising is a consistent surrogate for the PSP objective (Thm 1) and that the two-agent decomposition strictly reduces policy gradient variance compared to end-to-end training (Thm 2), eliminating executor-induced noise from the optimization.

## Results

On the Zenodo10k test split, SPIRE (two 7B Qwen2.5-VL agents trained with DAPO) achieves a VLM-as-Judge average of 0.5415 and a visual-similarity average of 0.7414, outperforming GPT-based AutoPresent (judge 0.5069, sim 0.5247) and the training-free PSP(o4-mini) baseline (judge 0.4784, sim 0.8062). On out-of-distribution SlideBench pages, SPIRE attains the highest judge average across all four sub-dimensions (faithfulness, color, layout, aesthetics) and the second-best visual similarity. Ablations confirm that both preference-aligned fine-tuning and multi-round iterative revision are necessary: removing fine-tuning drops the judge average from 0.5415 to 0.3230, and removing revision drops it to 0.4038.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPIRE outperforms AutoPresent [9] on the VLM-as-Judge average (0.5415 vs. 0.5069) and on visual similarity (0.7414 vs. 0.5247), with the largest gap in faithfulness (0.7072 vs. 0.6174). Against PPTAgent [51], SPIRE wins decisively on both metrics (judge 0.5415 vs. 0.3758; sim 0.7414 vs. 0.5309), with PPTAgent's faithfulness score collapsing to 0.1036 due to its template-matching approach. Compared to the training-free PSP(o4-mini) baseline, SPIRE achieves a higher judge score (0.5415 vs. 0.4784) despite using a much smaller 7B backbone, but trails on raw visual similarity (0.7414 vs. 0.8062), illustrating the known mismatch between pixel-level reconstruction and perceptual design quality. The SD 3.5 + IP-Adapter image-generation baseline is substantially outperformed by SPIRE on both visual similarity and judge scores, confirming that structured, plan-based generation is superior to end-to-end pixel synthesis for slide personalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant agentic slide-generation paradigm (AutoPresent, PPTAgent, SlideBot) that relies on prespecified templates or verbose user instructions, by reframing page-level design as an inverse planning problem over a latent design-intent variable. Conceptually, it repurposes the structural denoising paradigm—well established in continuous generative modeling—to the discrete element-level space of slides, and extends multi-agent visual generation frameworks with a principled RL training objective (DAPO) rather than heuristic prompting. The theoretical contribution (surrogate validity and variance-reduction theorems) connects to variational inference and policy-gradient literature but is applied here for the first time to slide personalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing PSP as latent-intent inference and providing a tractable structural-denoising surrogate, the paper offers a reusable recipe for personalizing any discrete-structured visual artifact (slides, posters, UI mockups) without requiring per-executor retraining. The finding that a 7B RL-trained critic outperforms a GPT-4o-mini critic on the same task suggests targeted preference-aligned training can substitute for raw model scale in narrow design tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-page coherence, whether the oracle VLM (GPT-5) used to generate training critiques can be replaced, and how well the method generalizes beyond the Zenodo10k/SlideBench distribution to truly diverse design domains.
