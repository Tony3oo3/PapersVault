## Problem

Existing dexterous grasping methods rely almost exclusively on visual geometry for pose planning and lack mechanisms for tactile-guided adaptation after contact, leading to failures on complex or unseen objects. While recent visuo-tactile fusion work explores feature-level concatenation, it overlooks the spatial alignment and semantic interaction between visual and tactile modalities that are critical for accurate finger-object contact reasoning and closed-loop grasp correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262721/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB-D object point cloud (per-point x,y,z,r,g,b), six tactile sensor readings (five fingertips plus one palm, normalized to [0,1]), and the 22-DoF hand joint state. Tactile signals are spatially projected onto nearby object points to form a tactile-mapped point cloud with per-point features (x,y,z,r,g,b,k,c) where k is a contact-state scalar and c is a 6-dimensional tactile intensity vector. Outputs include an initial grasp pose (palm position, quaternion orientation, 22 joint angles), a binary grasp-success probability, and an iterative pose delta for refinement.

## Method

The framework introduces a contact-aware representation that binds each object point to a specific finger or palm identity, enabling the model to reason about which hand part should contact which surface region. A PointNet++-based generator predicts this semantic contact map from the raw point cloud and decodes an initial grasp pose conditioned on it. After execution, a dual-encoder PointNet++ with multi-head attention classifies grasp stability from the tactile-mapped point cloud, and a diffusion-based adaptation module generates corrective pose adjustments when failure is predicted. Training data are collected via PPO rollouts in IsaacGym with tactile annotations, and the three components are integrated in a closed-loop that iteratively refines the pose until a success threshold is met or a maximum iteration count is reached.

## Results

On the simulation benchmark (50 objects, 6 categories), AdaDexGrasp achieves 91% grasp success rate on seen objects, 82% on unseen objects, and 83% on unseen categories, outperforming the strongest baseline (DexGraspAnything) by 14, 10, and 16 percentage points respectively. In real-world experiments with a Psibot hand, the method reaches 90%, 87%, and 81% on the three object sets. On the larger DexGrasp Anything dataset (15k+ objects), the method still attains 87% success rate, confirming scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaDexGrasp outperforms all ten baselines across every object set. Against the strongest visual-only generator DexGraspAnything (77/72/67%), the visuo-tactile fusion yields gains of 14/10/16 points, with the largest margin on unseen categories where geometric priors are least reliable. DexDiffuser (71/69/55%) and its tactile-conditioned variant DexDiffuser-VT (75/71/57%) both fall well short, the latter showing that naive feature-level tactile concatenation without spatial alignment provides limited benefit. UniDexGrasp in both its PPO (52/46/40%) and closed-loop resampling (59/52/46%) forms is the weakest among the RL-based baselines, confirming that resampling from the same generative distribution cannot escape failure modes without contact information. ContactDexNet (72/68/63%) and DexGraspVLA (69/60/58%) demonstrate that static contact prediction or RGB-only VLA policies lack the real-time tactile feedback needed for adaptive refinement. In real-world trials, the same ordering holds: AdaDexGrasp (90/87/81%) surpasses DexGraspAnything (81/71/73%) by up to 16 points on unseen objects, validating sim-to-real transfer of the visuo-tactile representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the UniDexGrasp line of work for dexterous grasp generation and PPO-based data collection, but departs from prior visuo-tactile fusion approaches (which typically concatenate or contrastively pre-train modality embeddings) by introducing a spatially-aligned representation that binds tactile contact signals to specific finger/palm identities within the object point cloud. It extends contact-map-based grasp generators (ContactDexNet, UGG) by adding semantic hand-part labels to the predicted contact distribution, and it goes beyond feature-level fusion by constructing a tactile-mapped point cloud that serves both the pre-contact planning stage and the post-contact adaptation stage within a single closed-loop framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in dexterous manipulation: visual-only grasp planners cannot detect or correct contact instabilities (slipping, sparse contact) that only become apparent after physical interaction. The demonstrated sim-to-real transfer on a Psibot hand and the strong generalization to unseen categories (83% vs. 67% best baseline) suggest the contact-aware representation is a reusable design pattern for other contact-rich tasks. Open questions include whether the deliberately simple tactile encoding (binary contact flag + 6-dim intensity) remains sufficient for tasks requiring force-direction or shear information, how the approach scales to multi-object or cluttered scenes, and whether the IsaacGym-based training pipeline generalizes to different hand morphologies without retraining.
