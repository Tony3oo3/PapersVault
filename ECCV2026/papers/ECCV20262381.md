## Problem

Static post-training quantization for video super-resolution must handle two video-specific challenges that image-centric PTQ ignores: sequence-wise multimodality (activation ranges vary substantially across clips, biasing a single global calibration) and temporal error drift (frame-wise quantization perturbations accumulate as flicker and jitter). Meanwhile, the dynamic quantization approaches that adapt ranges at inference time are incompatible with static compilation pipelines and can actually run slower than FP32 on edge hardware, making a static, video-aware PTQ method essential for practical deployment.

## Input / Output

TAQ takes a pretrained VSR model (RealViformer or BasicVSR) and a small offline calibration set (9 REDS sequences, 20 frames each) to produce a single static INT8-quantized model with fixed clipping bounds for all layers. At inference, the quantized model accepts a low-resolution video sequence and outputs ×4 upscaled high-resolution frames with no runtime range/scale recomputation. All quantization parameters are determined exclusively during calibration, enabling direct TensorRT static compilation on edge devices.

## Method

TAQ is a three-stage static PTQ pipeline: (1) sequence-wise histogram-based initialization, where per-sequence activation statistics are percentile-clipped (99.9%), binned into histograms, and used to solve for per-sequence clipping bounds (ℓ, u) that minimize weighted reconstruction error; (2) temporal refinement, where a temporal consistency loss L_temp = Σ‖ΔF_t − ΔQ_t‖² aligns consecutive-frame differences between the floating-point and quantized outputs, propagating gradients through the straight-through estimator to update both weight and activation bounds without retraining weights; and (3) bound ensembling, where the per-sequence refined bounds are arithmetically averaged across all calibration sequences to yield one deployable static parameter set. The key design insight is a specialize-then-aggregate strategy: per-sequence calibration respects heterogeneous clip statistics while averaging consolidates into a robust static solution, and the frame-difference (rather than per-frame output) loss cancels static bias to directly suppress temporal flicker.

## Results

On the RealViformer backbone at 4-bit, TAQ achieves 23.89 dB PSNR and 0.4724 LPIPS on REDS, outperforming the strongest image-SR baseline 2DQuant (23.12 dB / 0.4828) and reducing TLPIPS from 12.90 to 10.02. Gains are most pronounced at low bit-widths: at 3-bit on UDM10, TOF drops from 126.63 (2DQuant) to 83.19, and at 4-bit on SPMCS, TLPIPS falls from 24.18 to 19.67. On the in-the-wild VideoLQ benchmark (no-reference), TAQ 4-bit attains ILNIQE 27.41 and NRQM 6.03, the best among all methods. On NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano with TensorRT, TAQ static INT8 delivers a 3.56× speedup (1.15 fps) over FP32, whereas the dynamic INT8 baseline (QBasicVSR) is actually slower than FP32 (0.79×, 0.266 fps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAQ consistently outperforms all four static PTQ baselines (MinMax, Percentile, PTQ4SR, 2DQuant) across every bit-width (2/3/4/8) and dataset. Against the strongest image-SR baseline 2DQuant, TAQ gains +0.78 dB PSNR and −2.88 TLPIPS on REDS at 4-bit, and the temporal advantage widens at lower bits (e.g., −43.4 TOF on UDM10 at 3-bit). PTQ4SR is competitive on PSNR at 4-bit (23.40 vs 23.89 on REDS) but lags substantially on temporal metrics (TLPIPS 18.94 vs 10.02), confirming that image-centric calibration cannot suppress frame-to-frame drift. MinMax and Percentile are far weaker, with MinMax at 4-bit REDS yielding only 10.74 dB PSNR and 36.15 TLPIPS. Against the VSR-specific dynamic method QBasicVSR (Table 2), TAQ 6/7 matches or exceeds its mixed-precision MP6 (6/7.14) on PSNR, LPIPS, and TLPIPS across REDS4, Vid4, and VideoLQ while using fewer average bits; critically, on Jetson Orin Nano TAQ achieves 3.56× speedup (1.15 fps) whereas QBasicVSR's dynamic INT8 is slower than FP32 (0.79×, 0.266 fps), demonstrating that static quantization is essential for practical edge deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends post-training quantization from the image super-resolution setting (PTQ4SR, 2DQuant) to video super-resolution under a strict static-execution constraint. It departs from the one prior VSR-specific PTQ method, QBasicVSR, which relies on dynamic inference-time bit adaptation and input-dependent range updates; TAQ instead treats all quantization parameters as fixed after offline calibration. The key conceptual departure is the temporal consistency loss that aligns inter-frame differences between floating-point and quantized outputs, a mechanism absent from image-centric PTQ that pools all calibration samples into a single global bound. The sequence-wise histogram initialization plus bound ensembling is a new aggregation strategy that resolves the tension between per-clip statistical heterogeneity and the single-parameter-set requirement of static deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that dynamic INT8 quantization is measurably slower than FP32 on Jetson Orin Nano (0.79× vs. 1.0×) challenges the common assumption that lower precision always yields speedup on edge hardware, and provides a concrete, well-evidenced argument for static quantization in production VSR pipelines. The 3.56× speedup with competitive or superior quality (LPIPS, TLPIPS) makes real-time VSR on consumer edge devices practically achievable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer sequences (beyond 20 frames), other video restoration tasks (deblurring, denoising), and whether the temporal loss generalizes to architectures without explicit temporal propagation. The calibration cost (32 min on A6000 for 9 clips) remains a practical consideration for very large model families.
