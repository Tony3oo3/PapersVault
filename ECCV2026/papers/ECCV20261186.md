## Problem

Whole-home 3D indoor scene generation for embodied AI simulation is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale, high-fidelity 3D residential data. Existing approaches either rely on hand-crafted procedural rules that produce repetitive, biased layouts, or focus on isolated sub-tasks (floorplan synthesis or single-room furnishing) that fail to produce globally coherent, physically feasible, and simulation-ready multi-room environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261186/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single natural-language prompt specifying apartment dimensions, room count and types, door/window placement, adjacency relations, and positional constraints. It produces a complete furnished 3D home scene structured as: (1) a binary K-D tree floorplan with doors, windows, and room semantics; (2) an unfurnished 3D shell in Blender; (3) furniture as 3D bounding boxes parameterized by semantic label, physical dimensions, and pose; and (4) small manipulable objects on supporting surfaces (cabinets, desks, dining tables) with basic physical attributes, surface textures, and lighting for direct simulator instantiation.

## Method

HomeWorld decomposes whole-home generation into four hierarchical stages to avoid end-to-end 3D learning from scarce data. First, a Qwen3-4B-Instruct LLM fine-tuned with LoRA on 300K curated real floorplans generates a K-D tree representation (recursive axis-aligned splits encoding room identity and type at leaf nodes), which constrains the output space to reduce geometric errors like room overlap. Second, a hierarchical roaming strategy places furniture: a top-down view is inpainted with Gemini 3.0 Image Pro to synthesize global furniture layout, then ego-centric viewpoints (selected via greedy heatmap coverage) are inpainted to add fine-grained details, with SAM-3 for instance segmentation and SAM-3D for 3D geometry reconstruction, all grounded against the explicit 3D shell. Third, a fine-tuned VLM refiner iteratively renders top views, detects violations (collisions, boundary crossings, blocked doorways), and predicts structured corrective actions (translation, rotation) applied to 3D bounding boxes in a closed loop. Fourth, surface-centric inpainting hallucinates plausible small-object compositions on each furniture item, parsed by a VLM into local-frame layouts transformed to global coordinates.

## Results

On floorplan generation, the K-D Tree–based LLM achieves 87.7% graph validity (vs. 72.6% for ProcTHOR-10K) and substantially higher topological diversity (22.4 typed / 6.9 untyped distinct connectivity graphs vs. 6.2 / 2.3). For furniture layout generation, the full pipeline attains a Collision Ratio of 0.05 and Out-of-Boundary Ratio of 0.01 while achieving the highest Volume Density (0.35) and Footprint Object Density (4.16) among all compared methods. In a 30-participant user study, HomeWorld is preferred with an average score of 0.811 across Reasonability, Aesthetics, and Complexity, roughly doubling the next-best baseline (Holodeck at 0.420).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProcTHOR-10K on floorplan generation, HomeWorld improves graph validity by 15.1 percentage points (87.7% vs. 72.6%) and increases typed/untyped connectivity-graph diversity by roughly 3.6×/3.0×, indicating the LLM learns richer real-world topologies from the 300K floorplan dataset. In the furniture layout comparison (Table 4), HomeWorld ties LayoutGPT on Collision Ratio (0.05) and Out-of-Boundary Ratio (0.01) but nearly doubles Volume Density (0.35 vs. 0.22) and more than doubles Footprint Object Density (4.16 vs. 1.99), reflecting denser and more diverse object placement. Compared to Holodeck, HomeWorld reduces CR from 0.07 to 0.05, halves OOB (0.01 vs. 0.02), and again leads on both density metrics. Against LayoutVLM, the advantage is most pronounced on physical plausibility: CR drops from 0.20 to 0.05 (a 4× reduction in severe inter-object penetrations) while VD and FOD also improve. In the user study (Table 5), HomeWorld is preferred by a wide margin over all three layout baselines on every dimension, with the largest gap over LayoutGPT (0.811 vs. 0.180 average score).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining three previously separate research threads—LLM-based structured floorplan generation (extending FloorPlan-LLaMA and Floorplan-Diffusion with a K-D tree representation), 2D-to-3D lifting via image inpainting (in the spirit of Text2Room but with explicit 3D shell constraints), and compositional layout generation (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, LayoutVLM)—into a single hierarchical pipeline. Rather than learning 3D scene synthesis end-to-end from scarce 3D data, it repurposes foundation image models (Gemini) as strong 2D priors for furniture and object placement, grounded by floorplan-derived 3D constraints. The VLM-based recursive refiner and surface-centric manipulable object placement are departures from prior pipelines that typically stop at furniture-level layout without addressing small-object interactivity needed for embodied simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper directly addresses the data scarcity bottleneck in whole-home 3D scene generation by releasing 300K annotated real floorplans and 5K fully furnished, simulation-ready scenes—a resource gap that has constrained the field. Its emphasis on dense manipulable objects and physical attributes makes the output immediately usable for embodied AI training, a practical need that prior datasets (ProcTHOR, Holodeck) only partially address. Open questions include whether the 2D-inpainting-based generation can match the geometric fidelity of native 3D diffusion at scale, how the VLM refiner generalizes to architectural styles outside the training distribution, and whether the pipeline's reliance on a specific foundation image model (Gemini) limits reproducibility and long-term maintainability.
