## Problem

Skeleton-based motion data leak personally identifiable information through both static structural cues (bone lengths, limb ratios) and dynamic patterns (gait, execution style), enabling re-identification even without facial features. Existing retargeting approaches for anonymization rely on single-encoder architectures that create an inherent conflict between preserving action dynamics and removing identity, forcing a trade-off that degrades either utility or privacy. This paper addresses the gap by showing that architectural inductive biases—rather than loss functions alone—can make action-identity entanglement structurally difficult.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source 3D skeleton sequence (25 joints × 64 frames × 3 coordinates, root-centered and de-rotated) from one person and a target skeleton sequence from a different person (any action suffices, since the identity encoder discards temporal dynamics). The action encoder additionally consumes per-joint velocity and acceleration (9-dim per joint per frame), while the identity encoder operates on a time-averaged static pose plus 24 explicit bone-length scalars. The output is a retargeted 3D skeleton sequence that preserves the source action on the target body, optionally blended with the source via a partial-retargeting ratio β ∈ [0,1] to maintain compatibility with pre-trained downstream models.

## Method

DisentangledTMR uses two encoders with complementary inductive biases: an action encoder (multi-scale 1D temporal convolutions fused with a MixFormer backbone, producing 768-dim per-frame features) that captures dynamics but is structurally unable to extract frame-invariant bone ratios, and an identity encoder (spatial GCN on a time-averaged pose plus a bone-length MLP, producing a single 256-dim global vector) that captures skeletal structure but cannot represent trajectories. A factorized decoder with 6 layers applies separate cross-attention to each representation and fuses them via an adaptive per-joint gate, with the identity vector broadcast as a global conditioning signal analogous to FiLM. Training follows a three-stage curriculum—encoder pretraining with disentanglement losses (adversarial, contrastive, orthogonality, cross-correlation), decoder training for reconstruction and physical plausibility, then end-to-end fine-tuning with output-level adversarial and cooperative supervision—to prevent reconstruction gradients from overriding factor separation. A tunable partial-retargeting ratio β blends the retargeted output with the source, exposing a continuous privacy-compatibility trade-off with a sharp utility threshold at β ≈ 0.2.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 60 (X-View) at the recommended operating point β=0.2, DisentangledTMR achieves 75.8±1.1% pre-trained SGN action recognition (AR) and 18.1±1.8% re-identification (RI), reducing RI from the raw-skeleton baseline of 75.4% while retaining 87.1% re-trained AR. It also attains the lowest reconstruction MSE (0.030) and MPJPE (0.113) among all retargeting methods, with roughly 2× better bone-length consistency than DMR (0.016 vs. 0.029). Cross-dataset sweeps on NTU120 and ETRI confirm the trend: at β=0.2, pre-trained AR stays above 76% on all three benchmarks while RI drops well below raw-data levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two configurations of the prior retargeting work by Carr et al. (ICCV 2025): DMR (retargeting without adversarial training) and PMR (retargeting with adversarial learning), both using a single shared CNN encoder. DisentangledTMR decisively outperforms both on utility: at β=0.2 it achieves 75.8% pre-trained AR, more than double PMR's 35.7% and 1.5× DMR's 49.1%, while re-trained AR reaches 87.1% (vs. 19.9% for PMR and 43.1% for DMR). On privacy, PMR reaches a lower pre-trained RI (7.8% vs. 18.1%), but only because its output is so degraded that action becomes nearly unreadable (19.9% re-trained AR), making the comparison unfair; DMR's RI of 25.7% is substantially worse than DisentangledTMR's 18.1%. On physical plausibility, DisentangledTMR dominates all baselines: lowest MSE (0.030 vs. 0.047–0.050), lowest MPJPE (0.113 vs. 0.202–0.272), and best bone-length consistency (0.016 vs. 0.029–0.051). The paper attributes the gap to architectural disentanglement: the dual-encoder design sidesteps the action–identity conflict inherent in single-encoder pipelines, allowing strong privacy without the utility collapse seen in PMR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work on privacy-centric motion retargeting (PMR/DMR, ICCV 2025), which used a single shared CNN encoder with adversarial training. The key departure is architectural: replacing the single encoder with dual encoders carrying complementary inductive biases (temporal convolutions for action, spatial GCN on a time-averaged pose for identity) and a factorized cross-attention decoder with adaptive per-joint gating. The disentanglement strategy draws on the supervised-disentanglement literature (contrastive clustering, Barlow-Twins-style decorrelation, adversarial suppression) but grounds it in inductive-bias design rather than relying solely on loss functions, following the insight from Locatello et al. that unsupervised disentanglement requires structural priors. The partial-retargeting ratio β is a new mechanism absent from prior retargeting or anonymization methods, explicitly trading privacy against compatibility with frozen downstream models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway is that architectural inductive biases making entanglement structurally difficult provide a stronger privacy foundation than any individual disentanglement loss, a finding that could reshape how privacy-preserving representation learning is designed beyond skeleton data. The sharp β≈0.2 threshold gives practitioners a concrete operating point where pre-trained action classifiers retain ~76% accuracy while re-identification drops from 75% to ~18%, but the ~13 pp pre-trained AR gap and the absence of a formal privacy guarantee (empirical risk reduction only) limit deployment confidence. Open questions include whether the dual-encoder principle transfers to other modalities (e.g., IMU, depth), whether cross-topology generalization (OpenPose, SMPL) is achievable, and whether the pre-trained utility gap can be closed via domain adaptation without re-introducing identity leakage.
