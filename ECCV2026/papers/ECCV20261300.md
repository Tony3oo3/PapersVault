## Problem

Reconstructing global 3D object geometry from sparse local tactile contacts is fundamentally underconstrained, as each touch provides only a small surface patch. Prior tactile reconstruction methods either train category-specific networks on limited object datasets or learn diffusion models directly from tactile data, restricting generalization to seen categories. This paper is the first to repurpose a pretrained off-the-shelf 2D vision diffusion model as a transferable geometric prior for 3D reconstruction from sparse robot touches, without any task-specific diffusion training.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse GelSight tactile images (320×240, sensing area 2.0 cm × 1.5 cm), a class-level text prompt (e.g., 'a camera'), and contact poses from robot forward kinematics. Each tactile image is converted into a virtual camera observation (depth map, normal map, and contact mask) placed 2.0 cm behind the contact patch, making the tactile data compatible with a vision-centric reconstruction pipeline. The output is a two-stage geometric representation: a coarse SDF from a neural implicit model (stage 1) refined into an explicit DMTet mesh at 512×512 rendering resolution (stage 2).

## Method

TouchAnything adopts a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline inspired by Magic3D. In stage 1, a Neuralangelo-style SDF (multiresolution 3D hash-grid + MLP) is optimized by jointly minimizing tactile-derived losses (depth, normal, SDF truncation, and freespace constraints along virtual camera rays) and a score distillation sampling (SDS) loss from Stable Diffusion applied to 64×64 rendered normal maps conditioned on the text prompt. A two-phase training schedule first warms up with tactile-only supervision (steps 0–1000) before introducing SDS guidance (steps 1000–7000). In stage 2, the coarse SDF initializes a DMTet explicit tetrahedral-grid representation, which is refined at 512×512 resolution using differentiable rasterization for the same tactile and SDS losses plus a normal-consistency regularizer, recovering fine geometric details that the low-resolution stage 1 cannot capture. The key insight is that the geometric knowledge encoded in a large-scale pretrained 2D diffusion model transfers directly to the tactile domain, eliminating the need for category-specific training or tactile-conditioned diffusion models.

## Results

On the ShapeNet Core.V2 simulation benchmark (280 objects, 20 touches each), TouchAnything achieves the lowest Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) across all six object categories, ranging from 0.035 (bottle) to 0.060 (guitar). The method outperforms TouchSDF by up to 61% on guitar (0.060 vs. 0.155) and 49% on camera (0.047 vs. 0.092), and outperforms Touch2Shape by 6–12% across categories. In real-world experiments with a UR5e robot and GelSight Mini sensor, TouchAnything successfully reconstructs 14 previously unseen objects (YCB, 3D-printed, and household items) from 20 sparse touches, a setting where category-specific baselines cannot operate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TouchAnything is compared against two tactile-based 3D reconstruction baselines. TouchSDF [7] uses TacTip sensors with a DeepSDF neural implicit representation pretrained on a fixed dataset, providing a dataset-specific shape prior; TouchAnything outperforms it on all six categories, with the largest margins on guitar (0.060 vs. 0.155 EMD, a 61% reduction) and camera (0.047 vs. 0.092, a 49% reduction), indicating that the diffusion prior is especially valuable for complex, high-curvature shapes. Touch2Shape [43] employs active tactile exploration and trains a task-specific diffusion model on tactile data from ShapeNet/ABC; TouchAnything still wins on every category (e.g., 0.035 vs. 0.041 on bottle, 0.044 vs. 0.049 on mug), though the margins are smaller (6–12%), suggesting that a general-purpose 2D diffusion prior can match or exceed a purpose-built tactile diffusion model. A key qualitative advantage is generalization: TouchAnything reconstructs previously unseen object instances (real-world YCB, 3D-printed, and household objects) using only a class-level text prompt, whereas both baselines are restricted to categories seen during their training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-guided 3D synthesis (DreamFusion, Magic3D, Fantasia3D) and tactile-based shape reconstruction (TouchSDF, Touch2Shape). It adopts the coarse-to-fine SDF→DMTet pipeline from Magic3D and the normal-map-based SDS formulation from Fantasia3D, but departs from prior tactile reconstruction work by replacing category-specific trained diffusion priors with an off-the-shelf 2D Stable Diffusion model, transferring visual geometric knowledge to the tactile domain without any task-specific generative training. Unlike DreamFusion-style text-to-3D, it additionally enforces physical local constraints (depth, normals, freespace) from real GelSight contact measurements, grounding the diffusion prior in actual sensor data rather than purely semantic guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is generalization: prior tactile reconstruction methods (TouchSDF, Touch2Shape) are limited to object categories seen during training, whereas TouchAnything reconstructs previously unseen instances using only a class-level text prompt, making it applicable to open-world robotic manipulation. Open questions remain around the reliance on text prompts (even class-level), the assumption of passive touch collection, and whether the approach scales to objects with highly atypical geometry outside the diffusion model's training distribution. The paper also highlights that active touch strategies and prompt-free reconstruction are natural next steps, and the moderate EMD improvements over baselines suggest the diffusion prior, while essential, is not yet fully exploiting the geometric knowledge available in large-scale visual models.
