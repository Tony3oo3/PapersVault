## Problem

Existing Motion-aware LLMs jointly train motion understanding and generation but execute them in isolation, preventing the model from leveraging its own understanding to improve generation. This paper asks whether explicitly coupling generation with assessment and refinement through iterative text-motion dialogue (IRMoGen) can bridge the task gap and yield measurable gains in text-motion alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a goal text prompt and a reasoning instruction, then autoregressively emits a mixed sequence of text tokens and VQ motion tokens (K discrete codes bounded by <Motion> and </Motion> markers). The final 3D motion is extracted by decoding the last motion span, while intermediate spans serve as progressively refined drafts. The model also supports standalone tasks—captioning, alignment evaluation, and refinement instruction generation—each triggered by a task-specific prompt template.

## Method

IRG-MotionLLM builds on a VQ-VAE + Gemma2-2B LLM backbone and is trained in three progressive stages. Stage 1 (IRMoGen Initialization) fine-tunes on eight atomic tasks—four basic (caption, direct generation, prompt analysis, analysis-guided generation) and four improving (alignment evaluation, refinement instructing, direct refinement, instruction-guided refinement)—to implicitly bridge understanding and generation. Stage 2 (IRMoGen-CoT Learning) trains on structured multi-round reasoning trajectories where intermediate motions are deliberately misaligned but progressively improve, using an 'Ignore Incorrect' masking strategy to prevent the model from learning to generate bad motions. Stage 3 (IRMoGen Reinforcing) applies GRPO with three reward components (format compliance, text-motion embedding distance, and motion-motion embedding distance) to encourage longer multi-round reasoning. An automated data engine leverages GPT-4o for goal analysis and a TMR motion encoder for ranking-based negative sampling to synthesize all interleaved annotations from existing datasets.

## Results

On HumanML3D, IRG-MotionLLM (Stage-3 with IRMoGen) achieves Top-1 R-Precision of 0.535, Top-3 R-Precision of 0.820, and MM-Dist of 2.785, outperforming the strongest GRPO-based baseline Motion-R1 (Top-1: 0.515, Top-3: 0.818, MM-Dist: 2.854) on text-motion alignment metrics. On KIT-ML, the same model reaches Top-1 R-Precision of 0.445 and MM-Dist of 2.740, again surpassing Motion-R1 (Top-1: 0.431, MM-Dist: 3.196). The Stage-1 and Stage-2 models (without GRPO) already outperform all non-RL baselines on R-Precision and MM-Dist, with Stage-2 achieving FID of 0.111 on HumanML3D. The FID metric shows some fluctuation after GRPO (0.242 vs. 0.111 for Stage-2), consistent with the lack of dense supervision on motion tokens during RL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRG-MotionLLM (S3) outperforms the strongest GRPO-based competitor Motion-R1 [57] on text-motion alignment: Top-1 R-Precision 0.535 vs. 0.515 and MM-Dist 2.785 vs. 2.854 on HumanML3D, and Top-1 0.445 vs. 0.431 and MM-Dist 2.740 vs. 3.196 on KIT-ML. However, Motion-R1 achieves a lower FID (0.201 vs. 0.242 on HumanML3D), which the authors attribute to the lack of dense supervision on motion tokens during vanilla GRPO. Against non-RL baselines, IRG-MotionLLM (S2) achieves the best FID (0.111) and competitive R-Precision, surpassing MG-MotionLLM [74] (Top-1: 0.516, FID: 0.303), MotionChain [35] (Top-1: 0.504, FID: 0.248), and T2M-GPT [88] (Top-1: 0.492, FID: 0.141) on most alignment metrics. The Stage-1 model already outperforms the base MotionLLM [77] on all metrics (e.g., Top-1: 0.522 vs. 0.496, MM-Dist: 2.906 vs. 3.029), demonstrating that interleaved reasoning provides gains even before explicit CoT training. On the motion-to-text caption task (Table 7), the Stage-1 model achieves the best CIDEr (52.4) and BERTScore (42.68) among all compared methods without task-specific fine-tuning, outperforming MotionGPT3 [94] (CIDEr: 28.72) and MotionLLM [77] (CIDEr: 33.74). The paper also reports that scaling up Stage-3 data and extending GRPO to 2,600 steps (Ours‡) further improves R-Precision to 0.564 and MM-Dist to 2.628, approaching or surpassing UniMo [70] (Top-1: 0.539, MM-Dist: 2.768) with fewer training steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MotionLLM/Motion-Agent [77] as its base model, extending the VQ-VAE + LLM architecture for motion generation. It departs from prior MoLLM work (MotionGPT, MotionChain, MG-MotionLLM) by arguing that joint training of understanding and generation in isolation fails to exploit their complementarity, and instead introduces motion assessment and refinement as explicit bridging tasks. The core conceptual move is transplanting the text-image interleaved reasoning paradigm (self-assessment and refinement loops shown to improve image generation) into the 3D human motion domain for the first time, combined with a GRPO-based reinforcement stage to unlock multi-round reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that motion understanding can actively improve generation through an iterative assessment-refinement loop, offering a practical three-stage training recipe that practitioners can adopt on top of existing MoLLM bases. The emergent cross-model synergy—using IRG-MotionLLM as a transferable reward model to enhance other generators via RLAIF—opens an unexplored direction for MoLLMs. However, the FID degradation under GRPO (0.141→0.242) remains a significant limitation, and the reliance on GPT-4o for data annotation and VQ-VAE discretization constrains scalability. Open questions include whether the interleaved reasoning paradigm extends to more complex multi-person or long-horizon motion tasks, and whether the FID issue can be resolved with denser motion-level rewards.
