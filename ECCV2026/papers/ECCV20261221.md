## Problem

VGGT, a feed-forward multi-view 3D foundation model, is vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, but existing attack methods are impractical for real-time deployment: per-scene iterative optimization (e.g., PGD) is too expensive for dynamic environments, while static Universal Adversarial Perturbations lack the expressiveness to exploit multi-view geometric structure. No prior method generates input-conditioned, cross-view-consistent adversarial perturbations in a single forward pass.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a multi-view RGB image sequence of 2–10 frames (resized to a maximum dimension of 518 px) and outputs per-view perturbation maps of shape H×W×3 in a single feed-forward pass. Each perturbation is clipped to an ℓ∞ ball of radius ε to guarantee imperceptibility. The perturbed images are then fed into the frozen VGGT model, which produces degraded 3D outputs including point clouds, per-view depth maps, and camera poses.

## Method

MVAP-G is a transformer-based generator that processes all views jointly: each frame is split into 16×16 patches, embedded with learnable camera position tokens, and passed through transformer layers that incorporate a Cross-View Adversarial Alignment (CAA) module combining intra-view self-attention with inter-view cross-attention to enforce geometric consistency across perturbations. A DPT head decodes multi-scale features (from layers 3, 6, 9, 12) into full-resolution pixel-level perturbations via progressive upsampling with learnable fusion tokens. Training uses a two-stage procedure—pre-training on single-view COCO images followed by multi-view fine-tuning on seven 3D datasets—with an adversarial loss that minimizes the mean L2 norm of the reconstructed point cloud (collapsing it toward the origin) and a regularization term combining LPIPS perceptual similarity and a ReLU-penalized ℓ∞ constraint to keep perturbations visually imperceptible.

## Results

MVAP-G achieves the highest Chamfer Distance (CD) across all four evaluation datasets (COCO, ImageNet, LLFF, CO3D) and all view counts (v=1 to v=25) using only a single feed-forward pass. On single-view COCO, MVAP-G reaches CD 1.566, surpassing iterative PGD with 20 steps (1.535) and UAP (0.565). On multi-view CO3D with 25 views, MVAP-G achieves CD 2.047 versus 2.00 for PGD-20 and 0.659 for UAP. Inference time remains in the 50–500 ms range across view counts, compared to 25–200 s per scene for iterative PGD, and peak GPU memory stays below 14 GB versus over 30 GB for PGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UAP [41], MVAP-G achieves a 2.5–3× improvement in Chamfer Distance across all settings; UAP's static perturbation fails to exploit cross-view geometry, yielding CD ≈ 0.66–1.04 on multi-view CO3D/LLFF versus MVAP-G's 1.91–2.05. Against iterative PGD (AP) [37] with 10 or 20 optimization steps, MVAP-G with a single forward pass matches or exceeds the 20-step baseline in most configurations (e.g., COCO v=1: 1.566 vs. 1.535; ImageNet v=1: 1.611 vs. 1.584; CO3D v=1: 1.995 vs. 1.889; CO3D v=25: 2.047 vs. 2.00). The only marginal exceptions are LLFF v=25 (1.910 vs. 1.925) and CO3D v=8 (2.011 vs. 1.958, where MVAP-G still wins). Gaussian noise serves as a trivial lower bound, confirming that random perturbation is ineffective (CD ≈ 0.5–1.0). Overall, MVAP-G dominates both baselines on every dataset while requiring 50–500 ms of inference versus 25–200 s for PGD, making it the only method practical for real-time multi-view deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative adversarial perturbation paradigm (originally developed for single-view classification tasks) to the multi-view 3D reconstruction setting, specifically targeting the VGGT foundation model. Architecturally, it repurposes the DPT dense-prediction head for pixel-level perturbation synthesis and introduces a cross-view adversarial alignment (CAA) module that unifies self- and cross-view attention to enforce geometric consistency across views. Conceptually, it departs from both iterative per-scene optimization (FGSM/PGD) and static universal perturbations (UAPs), positioning feed-forward generative attack as the middle ground that adapts to scene geometry without test-time iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that a major 3D foundation model (VGGT) is vulnerable to imperceptible, real-time adversarial perturbations, raising urgent security concerns for safety-critical deployments in autonomous driving, SLAM, and robotic navigation. The finding that a single feed-forward pass matches or exceeds 20 iterations of PGD suggests that iterative optimization is unnecessary for attacking transformer-based 3D models, shifting the threat model toward always-on generative attacks. Key open questions include whether defenses (e.g., adversarial training, input sanitization) can protect 3D foundation models, whether the vulnerability generalizes to other multi-view 3D architectures (MASt3R, π³), and how to detect such perturbations in deployed systems.
