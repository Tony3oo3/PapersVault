## Problem

Frontier MLLMs are increasingly deployed as black-box zero-shot solvers for new vision-language tasks, but without ground-truth annotations it is difficult to validate their reliability, select among competing models, or improve their accuracy without fine-tuning. Existing grounding and self-explanation methods require additional training data or annotations, making them inapplicable to the zero-shot, annotation-free setting where practitioners actually need model validation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262556/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image paired with either a multiple-choice question and candidate answers (MC-VQA) or a natural-language referring expression (HC-REC, ranging from ~9 words to ~93 words). It produces the MLLM's native prediction (answer index or bounding box), a parallel explicit-logic prediction with per-object confidence scores, a Consistency Rate (CR) computed over a test set without any ground-truth labels, and an enhanced fused prediction. For HC-REC, the ELC additionally outputs per-person association probabilities and, for long-context inputs, per-sentence utility-weighted matching scores.

## Method

The paper proposes a dual-channel framework in which the MLLM's black-box prediction (Implicit Logic Channel) runs in parallel with an Explicit Logic Channel that mimics human reasoning in three steps: an LLM extracts task-relevant facts and relations from the text, a VFM (e.g., GroundingDINO) grounds those facts in the image with detection confidences, and a probabilistic logic-reasoning module applies factual, counterfactual, and relational rules to derive a decision. For MC-VQA, the ELC computes min over positive-object probabilities and max over negative-object probabilities, then combines them via geometric mean; for HC-REC, it accumulates IoU-based association rates between detected persons and objects; for long-context HC-REC, it classifies sentences into Essential/Non-Essential/Environment categories and weights VLM person-sentence matching scores by a utility function. A Consistency Rate (CR) between the two channels serves as a ground-truth-free metric for model validation and selection, and an aligned-fusion rule normalizes ELC scores to the ILC scale to produce enhanced predictions that consistently outperform either channel alone across 11 MLLMs on three benchmarks.

## Results

On NegBench (MC-VQA), the best MLLM (InternVL3.0-8B) achieves 93.2% accuracy on COCO and 95.4% on VOC2007, with a Consistency Rate (CR) of 0.726 and 0.842 respectively. After aligned fusion with ELC, InternVL2.5-8B's accuracy on COCO jumps from 91.2% to 96.5%, and InternVL2.0-8B on VOC2007 from 58.7% to 93.5%. On HC-RefCOCOg, Qwen3.0-VL-8B leads with 81.8% Acc@0.5 IoU and CR of 0.692; on HC-RefLoCo it reaches 80.2% mAcc with CR 0.813. The correlation between CR and ground-truth accuracy is very strong: mean Pearson's r = 0.991, Spearman's ρ = 0.962, Kendall's τ = 0.909 across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 11 MLLMs from four families (Gemma, InternVL, LLaVA, QwenVL) as the implicit logic channel (ILC) baselines, with the proposed ELC running in parallel. On NegBench MC-VQA, InternVL3.0-8B is the strongest ILC (93.2% Acc on COCO), while Qwen3.0-VL-8B dominates HC-REC tasks (81.8% Acc@0.5 on HC-RefCOCOg, 80.2% mAcc on HC-RefLoCo). Gemma-3-12B is competitive on MC-VQA (72.0% on COCO) but collapses on HC-REC (8.8% Acc@0.5), illustrating that no single model family is universally reliable. LLaVA-1.6-13B is consistently the weakest, scoring below 40% on NegBench COCO and below 9% on HC-RefCOCOg. The ELC standalone achieves 75.8% on NegBench COCO and 80.7% on HC-RefCOCOg val, competitive with mid-tier MLLMs. Aligned fusion (Acc_E) consistently improves every MLLM, with the largest gains for weaker models (e.g., InternVL2.0-8B on VOC2007: 58.7% → 93.5%), while even the best model benefits (InternVL2.5-8B on COCO: 91.2% → 96.5%). CR rankings closely track accuracy rankings (mean Pearson r = 0.991), confirming CR as a reliable proxy for model selection without ground-truth labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing existing foundation models (LLM, VFM, VLM) into a parallel Explicit Logic Channel that validates a black-box MLLM without requiring ground-truth annotations or fine-tuning. It departs from prior grounded VQA work (which relies on additional annotations, extended architectures, or new metrics) by operating entirely in the zero-shot regime, and from neurosymbolic frameworks (which are sequential and require additional learning) by using a parallel dual-channel design with task-specific probabilistic logic rules. The aligned fusion strategy draws conceptually on self-consistency and ensemble ideas but applies them across heterogeneous model types rather than repeated sampling of the same model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is significant: the Consistency Rate provides a reliable, annotation-free proxy for MLLM quality (correlation >0.9 with accuracy across all benchmarks), enabling model selection and validation in real deployment scenarios where ground truth is unavailable. However, the approach is task-specific in its logic rules, adds non-trivial latency (up to ~9s per sample on HC-RefLoCo), and the 'consistent but incorrect' failure mode, while low-risk at high CR, remains an open concern. Extending the framework to more complex multimodal chain-of-thought reasoning and to settings where both channels fail (true OOD) are natural next steps the authors identify.
