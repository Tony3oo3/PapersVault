## Problem

Diffusion-based real-world image super-resolution methods rely on globally predefined restoration trajectories with spatially uniform noise injection and fixed temporal schedules. This creates a fundamental dilemma: stochasticity strong enough to synthesize textures in severely degraded regions distorts reliable structures, while deterministic trajectories over-smooth uncertain textures. The paper asks: where and when should stochastic restoration be activated for each input?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262517/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (×4 super-resolution, 128×128 to 512×512) and produces a super-resolved high-quality image, operating entirely in the latent space of SD2.1-base. The same trained model supports two inference modes: a deterministic one-step pass (~96 ms) for maximum structural fidelity, or a stochastic few-step bridge refinement (2 steps, ~162 ms) that selectively injects spatially modulated noise to recover complex textures. The Adaptive Bridge Controller is evaluated only once per image and its predicted controls are reused across all refinement steps, adding ~1.8 ms overhead.

## Method

The core contribution is Spatio-Temporal Adaptive Bridge Matching (ST-ABM), which generalizes the standard Brownian bridge into an input-conditioned controlled bridge process between degraded and clean latent distributions. It decouples bridge stochasticity into two complementary controls: a spatial stochasticity map M (determining WHERE to inject noise) and a temporal reparameterization exponent p (determining WHEN to allocate generative capacity along the trajectory). A lightweight Adaptive Bridge Controller (ABC), a ResNet-based network with two prediction heads, predicts both controls from the degraded latent in a single forward pass. When M→0 the bridge collapses to a deterministic ODE preserving reliable structures; when M is large it becomes an SDE injecting generative stochasticity for texture synthesis. The model is trained on LSDIR with LoRA adapters on SD2.1-base, supervised by a normalized residual proxy for the spatial map and an EMA-based degradation severity score for the temporal exponent, jointly with standard reconstruction, perceptual, and adversarial losses.

## Results

On RealSR, DRealSR, and DIV2K-Val, the one-step variant (Ours-S1) achieves the highest PSNR (25.22, 28.22, 23.97 dB) and best or second-best LPIPS (0.282, 0.297, 0.293) among all compared methods, outperforming both 50-step diffusion baselines and other one-step methods in structural fidelity. The two-step variant (Ours-S2) achieves the best MANIQA score on all three datasets (0.685, 0.644, 0.656) and competitive MUSIQ/CLIPIQA+ scores, demonstrating a superior perception–distortion balance with only 2 inference steps versus 50 for multi-step baselines. On the RealLQ250 benchmark (no-reference only), Ours-S2 attains the highest MANIQA (0.622) and second-best MUSIQ (69.88) and CLIPIQA+ (0.707).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against multi-step diffusion baselines (SeeSR, DiffBIR, SUPIR; all 50 steps), Ours-S1 with a single forward pass achieves higher PSNR on all three datasets (e.g., 25.22 vs. 25.15 for SeeSR on RealSR) and substantially lower LPIPS (0.282 vs. 0.301–0.397), while Ours-S2 (2 steps) surpasses them in MANIQA (0.685 vs. 0.576–0.650 on RealSR) at a fraction of the inference cost. Against one-step baselines (OSEDiff, HYPIR, InvSR), Ours-S1 wins on PSNR across all datasets (e.g., 28.22 vs. 27.92 for OSEDiff on DRealSR) and matches or beats LPIPS, confirming superior structural fidelity in deterministic mode. Ours-S2 clearly outperforms all one-step methods on MANIQA (e.g., 0.685 vs. 0.634–0.660 on RealSR; 0.644 vs. 0.590–0.628 on DRealSR), showing that the adaptive stochastic bridge recovers perceptual realism that purely deterministic one-step mappings cannot. The main trade-off is that DiffBIR still leads on MUSIQ and CLIPIQA+ on DRealSR and DIV2K-Val (e.g., MUSIQ 66.13 vs. 65.35; CLIPIQA+ 0.717 vs. 0.685), but at the cost of 25× more inference steps and significantly worse PSNR/LPIPS. On the RealLQ250 real-world benchmark, Ours-S2 achieves the best MANIQA (0.622 vs. 0.593 for SeeSR) and competitive MUSIQ/CLIPIQA+, confirming generalization to unseen degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaBridge-SR extends the Brownian bridge diffusion framework (De Bortoli et al., NeurIPS 2021; Li et al., CVPR 2023) by generalizing the fixed, spatially uniform bridge into an input-conditioned controlled bridge process. While prior transport-based Real-ISR methods (flow matching, bridge matching) model the degraded-to-clean transformation, they all assume a globally predefined trajectory with uniform stochasticity and a symmetric temporal schedule. The key departure here is the Spatio-Temporal Adaptive Bridge Matching (ST-ABM) formulation, which decouples bridge stochasticity into a spatial stochasticity map (where to explore) and a temporal reparameterization (when to allocate generative capacity), both predicted by a lightweight Adaptive Bridge Controller conditioned on the degraded input. This positions the work between multi-step diffusion methods (which over-smooth or distort via uniform noise) and one-step deterministic methods (which suppress needed stochasticity), unifying both regimes in a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, lightweight mechanism to navigate the perception-distortion trade-off in Real-ISR without requiring separate models or training phases for different inference budgets. The finding that a 2-step stochastic bridge refinement matches or exceeds 50-step diffusion baselines on perceptual quality (MANIQA) while maintaining competitive LPIPS is practically significant for deployment. Open questions include whether the residual-proxy supervision for the stochasticity map generalizes to unseen degradation types beyond the Real-ESRGAN pipeline, whether the approach extends to other paired restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring), and whether the spatial control can be learned end-to-end without the auxiliary map loss. The method is built on SD2.1-base with LoRA, so its applicability to newer diffusion backbones (e.g., SD3, Flux) remains to be validated.
