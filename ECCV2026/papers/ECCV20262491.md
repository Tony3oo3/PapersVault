## Problem

Existing 3D scene editing methods follow a slow reconstruct-edit-refit pipeline requiring per-scene optimization (hundreds of seconds), producing multi-view inconsistencies and limiting real-time interactivity. A fundamental obstacle to training a feed-forward editing model is the absence of view-consistent edited ground-truth images, since 2D diffusion editors produce stochastic, cross-view-inconsistent results that cannot serve as reliable supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262491/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two unposed sparse-view RGB images with known camera intrinsics (no extrinsics required) and a text editing prompt. Before entering the 3D backbone, each image is independently edited by a pluggable 2D editor (InstructPix2Pix, FLUX, GPT-Image, or Gemini), so the model receives potentially inconsistent edited views. The output is a dense set of anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives (center, covariance, spherical-harmonic color, opacity) in a canonical world frame, rendered via standard 3D Gaussian splatting to produce novel-view images in approximately 0.5 seconds on a single GPU.

## Method

Edit3r adopts NoPoSplat's pose-free ViT encoder-decoder with DPT-based Gaussian heads as its backbone, but introduces two key training innovations to handle the lack of view-consistent edited supervision. First, a SAM2-based recoloring pipeline generates cross-view-consistent supervision targets by segmenting objects with SAM2's AMG/VSP and applying a deterministic composite color transform (ColorJitter, gamma, PCA lighting offset, channel permutation) with parameters sampled once per region and reused across all frames. Second, an asymmetric input scheme pairs one recolored reference view with one original (unedited) auxiliary view, forcing the network to learn robust fusion of disparate observations. Training combines 2D photometric losses (CLIP, LPIPS, low-frequency MSE) with 3D regularization: a Huber center-matching loss anchoring predicted Gaussians to NoPoSplat reference centers, and a pairwise Chamfer-L1 multi-view consistency loss suppressing floating artifacts. At inference, all input views are edited by the 2D editor and processed in a single feed-forward pass, with the model automatically resolving cross-view conflicts by reducing opacity of conflicting Gaussians.

## Results

On DL3DV-Edit-Bench, Edit3r achieves the highest CLIP_t2i score (0.266) among all methods, indicating superior instruction alignment, while running in 0.51 s per scene—over 600× faster than the optimization-based baselines (GaussCtrl: 325.5 s, EditSplat: 584.5 s). Edit3r also outperforms the feed-forward NoPoSplat baseline on C-FID (171.3 vs. 180.6) and C-KID (0.116 vs. 0.125), and improves epipolar geometric consistency (median SED 3.595 px vs. 3.783 px; 47.06% vs. 42.54% inlier ≤ 3 px). For no-reference image quality, Edit3r attains the best NIQE (3.402) and BRISQUE (26.538) among all edited outputs while retaining competitive sharpness (314.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Edit3r outperforms the feed-forward NoPoSplat baseline on every reported metric: CLIP_t2i (0.266 vs. 0.253), C-FID (171.3 vs. 180.6), C-KID (0.116 vs. 0.125), and all epipolar consistency measures, confirming that the asymmetric input scheme and 3D regularization add meaningful value over plain reconstruction. Against the optimization-based GaussCtrl, Edit3r wins decisively on instruction alignment (CLIP_t2i 0.266 vs. 0.227) and speed (0.51 s vs. 325.5 s), but GaussCtrl retains an edge on scene-conditioned realism (C-FID 135.0 vs. 171.3; C-KID 0.091 vs. 0.116), likely because its per-scene optimization allows finer appearance tuning at the cost of restricted edit magnitude. Compared to EditSplat, Edit3r achieves higher CLIP_t2i (0.266 vs. 0.241) and is over 1000× faster, though EditSplat's C-FID (174.1) is marginally better. Overall, Edit3r delivers the best quality–speed trade-off: it matches or exceeds the most conservative optimizer on semantic alignment while operating at interactive rates, at the cost of slightly higher C-FID/C-KID than GaussCtrl's tightly constrained edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

Edit3r departs from the dominant reconstruct-edit-refit paradigm (Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, GaussianCtrl, EditSplat) by replacing per-scene optimization with a single feed-forward pass, building directly on the NoPoSplat pose-free 3D Gaussian Splatting backbone (ViT encoder-decoder with DPT-based Gaussian heads). Its key conceptual departure is reformulating 3D editing supervision as a SAM2-based recoloring pretext task, paired with an asymmetric input scheme (recolored reference + unedited auxiliary), rather than requiring view-consistent edited ground truth. The framework is explicitly editor-agnostic, decoupling 2D generative editing (IP2P, FLUX, GPT-Image, Gemini) from the 3D reconstruction backbone, a modularity not present in prior optimization-based pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~600× speedup (0.51 s vs. 325+ s) makes instruction-driven 3D scene editing viable for interactive and real-time applications (AR/VR, game design, digital twins), a regime previously inaccessible to optimization-based methods. The DL3DV-Edit-Bench (20 scenes, 100 edit instances, four edit categories) provides the first standardized scene-level multi-view editing benchmark, enabling reproducible comparison. However, the recoloring pretext task does not fully capture large geometric changes (substantial add/remove) or extreme material shifts, the benchmark is small, and the method is limited to two input views—open questions include scaling to longer view sequences, per-object disentangled controls, and uncertainty-aware handling of conflicting edits.
