## Problem

Existing GCD methods jointly optimize supervised classification and unsupervised discovery objectives in a single stage, entangling closed-set recognition with open-set discovery and causing objective conflict, prediction bias, and distortion of pretrained foundation model geometry under limited labels. The paper asks whether explicitly consolidating closed-set categorical priors before discovery can improve representation quality for category discovery without the cost of full fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260250/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a partially labelled dataset: a labelled split of known classes and an unlabelled pool spanning both known and unknown categories. It produces per-sample category predictions (known-class labels via a K-way prototype classifier or cluster indices via k-means) and is designed as a plug-and-play regularizer that wraps around either parametric (SimGCD) or non-parametric (SelEx) GCD pipelines without modifying their core prediction mechanism. The backbone is a ViT (B or L) pretrained with DINO or DINOv2, with only the last few transformer blocks fine-tuned during the GCD stage.

## Method

CloSeR is a two-stage framework. In the first stage (Closed-Set Transfer Learning), lightweight block-wise adapters (AdaptFormer-style) are inserted into a frozen foundation model and trained with cross-entropy on labelled known-class data only, yielding a domain-adapted closed-set teacher with M prototypes at low parameter cost. In the second stage (Unified Relational Distillation), the frozen teacher provides two complementary relational signals to the GCD student: global sample-to-prototype relations (softmax similarity to the M closed-set prototypes, aligned via KL divergence) anchor known-class semantics, and local sample-to-sample relations (row-stochastic batch similarity matrices, also KL-aligned) preserve neighborhood geometry. A key design choice is feature decoupling: global relations are computed in a mapped student space (via a lightweight projection network that bridges the teacher-student representational gap), while local relations use raw student backbone features directly, preventing a single representation from being forced to satisfy both prototype anchoring and neighborhood matching simultaneously.

## Results

On fine-grained benchmarks with DINOv2, SelEx+CloSeR achieves 85.0% average All accuracy and 83.9% average New accuracy, while SimGCD+CloSeR reaches 76.2% All and 74.5% New (up from 69.0% and 65.3% without CloSeR). With DINO, SelEx+CloSeR improves average All from 63.1% to 72.0% and average New from 58.8% to 69.4% (+18.0% relative). On generic datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100) with DINOv2, SimGCD+CloSeR attains 94.9% average All and 94.6% average New accuracy, the best reported average New among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CloSeR is applied as a plug-and-play module on top of two representative GCD baselines (parametric SimGCD and non-parametric SelEx) and consistently outperforms their unmodified versions across all six datasets and both DINO/DINOv2 backbones. On fine-grained CUB with DINO, SelEx+CloSeR (72.0% All) surpasses the strongest standalone baselines HypCD (69.5%) and ConGCD (67.2%) by 2.5 and 4.8 points respectively, while SimGCD+CloSeR (62.6%) still trails these top methods but improves over the SimGCD baseline (56.1%) by 6.5 points. With DINOv2 on fine-grained data, SelEx+CloSeR (85.0% All) approaches HypCD (86.0%) and clearly beats ConGCD (82.6%), while SimGCD+CloSeR (76.2%) substantially outperforms the SimGCD baseline (69.0%) by 7.2 points. On generic datasets with DINOv2, SimGCD+CloSeR (94.9% All, 94.6% New) edges out DebGCD (94.1% All, 93.1% New) and HypCD (93.2% All, 90.6% New), achieving the best average New accuracy. The gains are most pronounced on novel-class discovery (e.g., DINO SelEx New: 58.8→69.4; DINOv2 SimGCD New: 65.3→74.5), confirming that closed-set relational distillation primarily stabilizes the embedding geometry for unseen categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

CloSeR sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient transfer learning (AdaptFormer-style block-wise adapters) and relational knowledge distillation (Park et al., CVPR 2019), repurposing both for the GCD setting. It departs from the dominant one-stage GCD paradigm (SimGCD, SelEx, DebGCD, etc.) that jointly optimizes supervised and unsupervised objectives on mixed data, instead proposing a two-stage pipeline: first consolidate closed-set categorical priors via lightweight adapter tuning, then inject that knowledge into the GCD student through a unified relational distillation objective. Unlike prior relational KD that aligns a single similarity structure, CloSeR distills complementary global (sample-to-prototype) and local (sample-to-sample) relations through decoupled feature pathways, and is head-agnostic, applying equally to parametric and non-parametric GCD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that naively fine-tuning on known classes before discovery substantially improves GCD, and that the standard one-stage GCD objective can actually prevent larger backbones from benefiting from additional capacity—challenges a widely adopted training recipe and suggests the field may have been under-exploiting foundation model priors. The plug-and-play, head-agnostic design lowers the barrier for adoption across heterogeneous GCD pipelines. Open questions include whether the two-stage approach extends to multi-modal or video GCD, whether the adapter-based teacher can be replaced by a stronger (e.g., LLM-guided) closed-set model, and whether the gains persist under even more limited label budgets or larger-scale benchmarks beyond the six tested here.
