## Problem

Cross-Domain Few-Shot Object Detection (CDFSOD) suffers from both data scarcity and large domain gaps between upstream general domains and downstream expert domains. While diffusion-based data augmentation is a natural remedy for the scarcity, the paper shows that standard diffusion models fail catastrophically under large domain gaps—producing images that actually degrade detection performance below the original-data baseline. The paper identifies and decomposes this failure into two distinct causes: a visual gap (the model cannot distinguish noise from useful information in expert-domain images) and a semantic gap (foreground categories are absent from the diffusion model's training distribution).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262528/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes target-domain support-set images (1, 5, or 10 per class), their ground-truth bounding box annotations, and LLM-generated textual descriptions of each image. It outputs two sets of augmented images—background-inpainted and foreground-inpainted variants—whose bounding boxes are preserved from the original annotations, eliminating the need for re-annotation. The augmented images are then combined with the original support set to form an expanded training set for downstream detection fine-tuning. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play, operating on a frozen pretrained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.5) with DDIM sampling constrained to ≤50 steps.

## Method

SITN (Selective Inpainting with Tailored Noise) decomposes the domain gap into visual and semantic components and addresses each with a targeted mechanism. For the visual gap, the Generation Module applies weakened noise by scaling the diffusion timestep (T = ε·T_max with ε < 1), preventing the image from fully degenerating into Gaussian noise so that the denoising process preserves expert-domain visual information rather than hallucinating from source-domain priors. For the semantic gap, the module performs background inpainting (masking the BBox region and regenerating the surrounding context) as the primary augmentation strategy, exploiting the finding that background semantics transfer far more reliably across domains than foreground object semantics. A secondary foreground-inpainted variant (masking the complement of the BBox) is also generated. The Selection Module then filters both sets using the RPN from the pre-trained detector: it computes IoU between predicted and ground-truth boxes and retains only the top-k highest-quality samples, discarding generations where the LLM prompt or diffusion model produced semantically inconsistent results.

## Results

On the CDFSOD benchmark (6 datasets: ArTaxOr, Clipart1k, DIOR, DeepFish, NEU-DET, UODD), the method achieves new state-of-the-art average mAP of 34.3 (1-shot), 43.7 (5-shot), and 46.9 (10-shot) when combined with GroundDINO (Swin-B backbone), surpassing the previous best DomainRAG (33.6/42.7/45.4). The method is plug-and-play and consistently improves every backbone it is applied to, with the largest relative gains on weaker baselines (e.g., ETS improves from 21.9 to 32.1 avg mAP in 1-shot, a +10.2 gain). On the CDFSS task (4 datasets), FPTrans+Ours achieves 66.7 average mIoU, outperforming the previous best LoEC (65.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is plug-and-play and consistently improves every detection backbone it is applied to. With the strongest backbone (GroundDINO, Swin-B), it surpasses the previous SOTA DomainRAG by +0.7 mAP (1-shot), +1.0 (5-shot), and +1.5 (10-shot) in average mAP. The gains are most dramatic on weaker baselines: ETS improves from 21.9→32.1 (1-shot, +10.2), GLIP from 19.3→26.3 (1-shot, +7.0), and CD-ViTO from 13.9→17.0 (1-shot, +3.1). Against other diffusion-based augmentation methods (Table 2), SITN (34.3 avg) dramatically outperforms SDEdit (13.2), DA-Fusion (12.0), ControlNet (11.4), DiffuseMix (4.8), and IP-Adapter (5.1). Against traditional augmentations (Table 5), SITN (17.0 avg) beats the best traditional method Dod (14.8) by +2.2, while most traditional methods (Mixup 8.7, EM 7.2) actually degrade performance below the no-augmentation baseline (13.9). On the CDFSS task (Table 4), FPTrans+Ours achieves 66.7 average mIoU, beating LoEC (65.0) and all other segmentation baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: cross-domain few-shot object detection (CDFSOD), which has been addressed primarily through transfer learning and fine-tuning strategies (CD-ViTO, GLIP, GroundDINO, ETS, DomainRAG), and diffusion-based data augmentation (DA-Fusion, DiffuseMix, SDEdit, ControlNet, IP-Adapter), which has been applied to in-domain or mildly cross-domain settings but not to the extreme domain gaps characteristic of CDFSOD. The method builds directly on the RePaint inpainting framework [30] and the DDIM sampling paradigm, repurposing them with two targeted modifications—reduced noise strength and background-selective inpainting—motivated by a novel decomposition of the domain gap into visual and semantic components. Unlike prior diffusion-based augmentation methods that apply full-noise generation or require retraining, SITN is training-free and plug-and-play, and it departs from retrieval-based approaches like DomainRAG by relying purely on generative priors without external feature matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic diagnosis of why off-the-shelf diffusion models actively degrade cross-domain few-shot performance, and the visual/semantic gap decomposition offers a reusable analytical lens for future generative augmentation in domain-shifted settings. The practical impact is notable: a training-free, plug-and-play pipeline that improves six CDFSOD benchmarks and four CDFSS benchmarks without modifying the detection backbone. However, open questions remain—reliance on LLM-generated prompts introduces quality variance that the Selection Module only partially mitigates, the method has not been tested on more extreme domain gaps (e.g., satellite-to-aerial, or video), and the top-k selection threshold is dataset-specific, limiting fully automated deployment. Whether the background-inpainting insight generalizes to tasks where foreground-background separation is ambiguous (e.g., medical imaging with diffuse lesions) is unexplored.
