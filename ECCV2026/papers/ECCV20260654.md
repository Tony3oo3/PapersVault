## Problem

Existing reference-to-video (R2V) methods feed VAE latents and auxiliary semantic features into a diffusion transformer, but the modality mismatch between these heterogeneous representations leads to copy-paste artifacts and multi-subject confusion. Prior approaches rely on implicit alignment through additional encoders, which provides limited benefit without explicit constraints on how reference features should be structured in the DiT's internal representation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260654/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more reference images and a text prompt as input, producing an 81-frame video via the Wan2.1 backbone. Reference images are encoded by a frozen Wan-VAE and fed as image tokens into the DiT alongside T5-encoded text; at inference, classifier-free guidance is applied with separate weights (μ₁=5.0 for reference, μ₂=7.5 for text) over a 50-step Euler sampler. The VFM alignment module is entirely discarded at inference, so the model incurs no additional inference cost beyond the base DiT.

## Method

RefAlign fine-tunes a pretrained Wan2.1 DiT by adding a reference alignment (RA) loss that pulls intermediate reference-branch features (extracted from the self-attention of the first K=9 DiT blocks) toward corresponding frozen DINOv3 patch features, while a negative margin term pushes features of different subjects apart. An MLP projector maps DiT hidden states to the VFM feature dimension, and the loss is averaged over the selected blocks. The overall training objective combines the standard Rectified Flow denoising loss with the RA loss (η=1.0, λ=1.0), and the alignment is applied only during training. The key insight is that VFM features exhibit strong inter-subject separability and intra-subject compactness, so explicitly regularizing DiT reference features toward this structure mitigates both copy-paste artifacts and multi-subject confusion without requiring any extra encoder at inference time.

## Results

On the OpenS2V-Eval benchmark, RefAlign-1.3B achieves a TotalScore of 56.30%, surpassing the strongest open-source 1.3B baseline Phantom-1.3B (54.89%) and VACE-1.3B (49.89%). At the 14B scale, RefAlign-14B reaches 60.42% TotalScore, outperforming all prior methods including VINO (57.85%), Saber-14B (57.91%), and BindWeave (57.61%). RefAlign also leads on FaceSim (53.06% at 1.3B, 55.23% at 14B) and NexusScore (43.97% at 1.3B, 48.52% at 14B), confirming its advantage in subject fidelity and consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 1.3B scale, RefAlign outperforms Phantom-1.3B by 1.41 points in TotalScore (56.30% vs. 54.89%) and leads on FaceSim (53.06% vs. 48.56%) and NexusScore (43.97% vs. 42.48%), while trailing on Aesthetics (42.96% vs. 46.67%). At the 14B scale, RefAlign-14B achieves the highest TotalScore (60.42%), beating the next-best open-source methods VINO (57.85%), BindWeave (57.61%), and VACE-14B (57.55%) by 2.5–2.9 points, and also surpassing the closed-source Saber-14B (57.91%). RefAlign-14B leads on FaceSim (55.23% vs. VACE-14B's 55.09%) and NexusScore (48.52% vs. BindWeave's 46.84%), but trails Kaleido on NaturalScore (73.63% vs. 82.18%) and VINO on GmeScore (68.32% vs. 69.69%). Against closed-source systems, RefAlign-14B exceeds Kling1.6 (56.23%) by 4.19 points in TotalScore, though Kling retains an edge on MotionAmplitude (41.60% vs. 22.48%). Overall, RefAlign's gains are concentrated in identity-consistency metrics (FaceSim, NexusScore), consistent with its design goal of reducing copy-paste artifacts and multi-subject confusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

RefAlign repurposes the REPA alignment paradigm (Yu et al., 2025), which regularizes DiT generation-target features toward a frozen VFM to accelerate convergence, by instead aligning the DiT's reference-branch image-token features to VFM space during fine-tuning. This departs from the dominant 'two-stream reference' design (Phantom, Video Alchemist) that concatenates VAE latents with an additional semantic encoder at inference, and from MLLM-based cross-modal guidance (BindWeave, PolyVivid) that incurs heavy inference overhead. The key conceptual shift is that explicit feature-space alignment at training time can substitute for auxiliary encoder branches at inference, reducing modality mismatch without extra forward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that a training-only alignment loss outperforms adding a second encoder as input (55.73% vs. 52.15% TotalScore in ablation)—offers a lightweight design principle for R2V systems that want reference fidelity without inference cost. The negative cross-subject term (L_neg) being critical for multi-reference scenarios highlights a previously under-addressed failure mode (representation collapse toward an average). Open questions include whether combining multiple VFM targets (identity-sensitive DINOv3 + semantic SigLIP2 + cross-modal Qwen2.5-VL) yields complementary gains, whether the alignment principle transfers to video editing, and how to extend beyond the 81-frame limit imposed by the Wan2.1 backbone.
