## Problem

Fabric image demoiréing remains underexplored and lacks a standardized benchmark. Unlike screen-induced moiré from strictly periodic display lattices, fabric moiré arises from broadband, semi-periodic textile weaves whose spectra heavily overlap with intrinsic texture, making restoration substantially more ill-posed. Existing screen-camera demoiréing models generalize poorly to these complex patterns, and acquiring pixel-aligned real-world fabric moiré pairs is infeasible at scale due to non-rigid deformations and pose variations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a moiré-corrupted fabric RGB image (padded to a multiple of 8 for pixel-shuffle operations) and outputs a restored fabric RGB image at the same resolution. The PRISM benchmark provides 16,050 paired multi-resolution fabric images with controllable aliasing severity, generated from ~2,000 high-frequency textile source images via a one-to-many synthesis strategy. A small real-world unpaired fabric moiré set is also used for zero-shot generalization evaluation with no-reference metrics (ARNIQA, CLIP-IQA+) and blind MOS studies.

## Method

PRISM synthesizes realistic fabric moiré by simulating the full imaging chain (geometric viewpoint warping, discrete sensor sampling, Bayer CFA mosaicking with photon noise, and demosaicking), then extracts pure moiré residuals via a round-trip construction that contrasts an aliased upsampled path against a Nyquist-compliant anti-aliased reference path, injecting the residual into the original pixel grid to guarantee perfect alignment while preserving native texture. FaDeNet performs content-adaptive base/detail decomposition using a gated depthwise Gaussian split, then applies a 4-scale U-shaped trunk on the low-frequency base to predict a residual correction and spatial confidence mask, updating the base via a mask-gated rule. A lightweight detail branch applies magnitude-bounded (α=0.35) tanh-gated refinement on the high-frequency component under the same mask to suppress moiré without erasing textile texture. Spectral-Anisotropic Gated Blocks (SAGB) combine multi-branch anisotropic depthwise spatial filtering (3×3, dilated-2 3×3, 1×5, 5×1) with a channel-wise 2D FFT magnitude gate (applied at coarse scales) to adaptively strengthen corrections in moiré-dominant regions. Training uses L1 loss plus a Laplacian high-pass constraint (λ_hp=0.3) on 384×384 patches for 200 epochs with Adam and cyclic cosine annealing.

## Results

On the PRISM benchmark, FaDeNet achieves 32.16 dB PSNR, 0.9859 SSIM, and 0.0169 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest screen-camera baseline ESDNet-L (28.81 dB, 0.9711, 0.0268) by 3.35 dB in PSNR. FaDeNet is also more efficient than ESDNet-L, using fewer parameters (7.05M vs. 10.62M) and nearly doubling inference speed (38.10 vs. 19.22 FPS). On the real-world unpaired fabric set, FaDeNet attains the highest ARNIQA (0.6336) and MOS (3.39) scores, with a statistically significant preference over all baselines (corrected p < 0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaDeNet decisively outperforms all nine screen-camera demoiréing baselines on PRISM across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the largest gap over the nearest competitor ESDNet-L (28.81 dB) at +3.35 dB PSNR and −0.0099 LPIPS. Among the lighter-weight methods, P-BiC (28.59 dB) and MZNet (27.50 dB) trail FaDeNet by 3.57 and 4.66 dB respectively, while the heaviest baselines MopNet (60.2M params, 10.05 FPS) and FHDe2Net (562 GFLOPs) are both slower and less accurate. On the real-world unpaired set, FaDeNet leads in ARNIQA (0.6336) and MOS (3.39), though P-BiC edges it slightly on CLIP-IQA+ (0.4833 vs. 0.4747), suggesting P-BiC's restorations are marginally more semantically natural in a CLIP-based sense. In the blind MOS study, FaDeNet achieves the best average rank (1.97) with all pairwise Wilcoxon tests (Holm-Bonferroni corrected) yielding p < 0.01, confirming statistically significant human preference over every baseline including ESDNet-L (MOS 2.79, rank 4.20) and MopNet (MOS 2.86, rank 3.30).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out fabric demoiréing as a distinct sub-problem from the well-studied screen-camera demoiréing literature, arguing that the spectral entanglement between broadband textile micro-patterns and aliasing components makes the problem fundamentally more ill-posed than the discrete-peak screen case. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes the general U-Net multi-scale trunk and frequency-domain gating ideas from screen demoiréing (ESDNet, MBCNN, WDNet) but restructures them around a conservative base/detail decomposition with mask-gated updates specifically to avoid the over-smoothing that screen-trained models exhibit on fabric. The PRISM synthesis pipeline is a novel departure from prior moiré generation methods (which assume content-independent foreground layers) by using a round-trip residual injection that preserves pixel alignment and intrinsic texture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first large-scale, pixel-aligned paired dataset for fabric demoiréing (16,050 multi-resolution pairs) and a standardized evaluation protocol, this work removes the primary practical barrier—data acquisition—that has kept the problem underexplored. The zero-shot transfer to real unpaired fabric captures, validated with no-reference IQA and a blind MOS study, suggests the synthetic-to-real gap is manageable, though the real-world evaluation remains limited in scale and relies on weaker no-reference metrics. Open questions include whether the residual-injection synthesis captures the full diversity of real-world fabric moiré (e.g., extreme deformations, mixed materials, multi-layer garments) and whether the conservative correction philosophy generalizes to other periodic-texture domains beyond textiles.
