## Problem

Existing dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods fundamentally fail on fire reconstruction because they assume scene content consists of persistent primitives undergoing smooth, trackable motion — an assumption fire violates entirely due to its transient, emissive, semi-transparent, and rapidly varying nature. The paper identifies three recurring failure modes across state-of-the-art baselines (geometric absorption, depth collapse, and temporal drift) and shows that no current GS method can reliably reconstruct flames from sparse-view observations, exposing a critical gap in discrete dynamic Gaussian scene representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes high-speed video from exactly three calibrated GoPro Hero 13 Black cameras at 400 Hz (100-frame subsequences from ~15 s captures), supplemented by COLMAP camera poses, PatchMatchStereo depth maps, DepthAnythingV2 monocular depth predictions, and MEMFOF dense optical flow. It produces a decoupled 4D representation: a static 3DGS background and a set of transient flame Gaussians, each carrying position, linear velocity, birth time, and lifespan, enabling novel-view synthesis at arbitrary time steps and explicit extraction of a volumetric velocity field. A custom ESP32-based LED synchronization system exploits rolling shutter to achieve ~15 μs sub-frame temporal alignment across the three consumer cameras.

## Method

The core contribution is a transient Gaussian formulation that decouples the static scene from the dynamic fire, treating flames not as deforming persistent primitives but as short-lived volumetric carriers of emission with finite temporal support. The static background is reconstructed with vanilla 3DGS initialized from a fused stereo-monocular depth point cloud and regularized by aligned monocular depth during optimization. For the dynamic component, per-view dense optical flow (MEMFOF) is back-projected onto a 3D voxel grid and fused via a Tikhonov-regularized least-squares solve to produce a volumetric 3D flow field, which directly initializes each Gaussian's velocity. Each transient flame Gaussian follows a constant-velocity trajectory x(t) = x₀ + (t − t_μ)v with opacity modulated by a Gaussian temporal window σ(t) = exp(−½((t−t_μ)/t_σ)²), a parameterization inspired by FreeTimeGS. An alpha loss weighted by a precomputed motion mask prevents dynamic Gaussians from absorbing static background geometry, and a novel density-weighted volumetric evaluation (DVE) protocol using third-order Gauss-Hermite quadrature quantifies velocity-field fidelity against ground truth in synthetic scenes.

## Results

On the real-world fire dataset (3 cameras, 100 frames), the method achieves 26.93 dB PSNR_flame, 0.843 SSIM_flame, 0.094 RMSE_depth, and 0.035 LPIPS, outperforming all four GS-based baselines on every visual and depth metric. Full-frame PSNR reaches 35.89 dB with 0.967 SSIM. On the synthetic Mantaflow scenes, the density-weighted volumetric evaluation (DVE) protocol yields 0.825 relative L2 and 0.744 cosine similarity for the reconstructed velocity field, compared to 0.036–0.225 cosine similarity for all baselines, indicating substantially better directional motion alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method wins on every metric against all four baselines on both the real-world and synthetic evaluations. Against the closest competitor 4DGS (Yang et al.) [105], it gains +3.65 dB PSNR_flame (26.93 vs. 23.28), +0.115 SSIM_flame, and reduces RMSE_depth from 0.113 to 0.094. FreeTimeGS [90] achieves second-best flame PSNR (24.48) but suffers severe depth degradation (RMSE_depth 0.131) and the worst LPIPS (0.241), indicating it matches appearance at the cost of geometric coherence. The two 4DGS variants [92, 105] and Grid4D [97] all exhibit the paper's identified failure modes—geometric absorption and depth collapse—resulting in PSNR_flame values 3.6–8.4 dB below the proposed method. On the synthetic DVE protocol, the gap in directional motion fidelity is stark: the method achieves 0.744 cosine similarity versus 0.036–0.225 for all baselines, confirming that existing dynamic GS methods produce physically implausible velocity fields for fire.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its rendering backbone and adopts the constant-velocity temporal parameterization from FreeTimeGS (Wang et al., 2025), but departs fundamentally from the persistent-primitive assumption shared by all existing dynamic GS methods. The key conceptual shift is modeling fire as a population of short-lived, transient volumetric carriers initialized from fused multi-view optical flow (MEMFOF), rather than as deforming persistent Gaussians. For the static background, it combines PatchMatchStereo depth with monocular depth regularization (DepthAnythingV2) to overcome the severe under-constraint of a three-camera setup, and introduces a custom ESP32-based LED synchronization system to achieve sub-frame temporal alignment with commodity hardware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to demonstrate that sparse-view (three-camera) reconstruction of real fire is tractable within a Gaussian Splatting framework, providing both a practical capture pipeline with consumer hardware and a new density-weighted volumetric evaluation protocol (DVE) for assessing motion fidelity in transient phenomena. The ablation revealing a fundamental trade-off between visual fidelity and physical accuracy (removing the alpha mask improves PSNR_flame but degrades velocity alignment) highlights an open tension in dynamic scene reconstruction that the field has not yet resolved. Limitations include reliance on dense optical flow accuracy in low-texture emissive regions, a linear velocity model that may oversmooth turbulent motion, and the assumption of minimal background-foreground interaction that breaks down for smoke-rich or large-scale fires.
