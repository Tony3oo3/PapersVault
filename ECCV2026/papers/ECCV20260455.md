## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks evaluate spatial reasoning and individual property families (material, affordance, function, physical attributes) in isolation under separate schemas, leaving cross-property reasoning—where a model must simultaneously integrate constraints across all property dimensions to answer goal-oriented physical questions—systematically untested. This gap is critical because practical physical reasoning (e.g., selecting a suitable temporary stepstool) inherently requires joint satisfaction of rigidity, load-bearing shape, and height constraints that no single-property benchmark can capture.

## Input / Output

Each question is paired with a scene image (4,544 synthetic renders from Unreal Engine 5 with precise 2D/3D annotations, or 1,258 real images from OpenImages) in which candidate objects are marked with colored bounding boxes, plus a natural-language question. Models produce free-form text answers (not multiple-choice), which are scored by a Gemini LLM verifier for semantic correctness against ground-truth annotations. The 28,033 questions span four graduated difficulty levels: object description, 3D spatial relations, single-property matching, and cross-property taxonomy reasoning, with a unique-candidate constraint ensuring exactly one object satisfies all required attributes per question.

## Method

PercepTax formalizes a three-level Perceptual Taxonomy (scene → objects → properties) and constructs a unified property dictionary of 54 attributes across four families (14 material, 10 affordance, 13 function, 17 physical) annotated jointly on 3,173 object classes via Gemini-generated descriptions, HDBSCAN/k-means clustering, and human verification. Questions are instantiated from 48 parameterized templates with a unique-candidate constraint and LLM-based rephrasing, supplemented by 50 expert-authored cross-property stress-test questions. The empirical study benchmarks nine SOTA VLMs (closed-source: Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.5; open-source: Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, LLaVA-Next variants) and diagnoses failure origins through two controlled experiments: oracle property injection (supplying ground-truth labels to isolate integration from perception) and taxonomy-guided in-context learning (one simulation exemplar to test sim-to-real transfer of structured reasoning). Residual oracle-wrong cases are further decomposed into three root-cause modes—Physical Commonsense Gap, Category Semantic Bias, and Constraint Neglect—revealing that compositional integration, not property recognition, is the dominant bottleneck.

## Results

On the PercepTax benchmark, the strongest closed-source model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieves 57.38% overall accuracy on real images and 41.87% on simulation, while the best open-source model (Qwen3-VL-32B) reaches 50.08% and 34.53% respectively, far below the human upper bound of 90.48% (real) and 94.63% (sim). Cross-property reasoning is the hardest task: GPT-5 scores only 39.84% on real images versus 87.06% on object description, a gap of over 44 points. Oracle property injection improves single-property accuracy by up to 26.76 points (Gemini 2.5 Pro, real: 55.29% → 82.05%) but cross-property accuracy still lags human performance by ~30 points, confirming that compositional integration—not just perception—is the core bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, PercepTax evaluates nine VLMs (three closed-source, six open-source) rather than proposing a new method. Gemini 2.5 Pro leads among closed-source models on real images (57.38% overall) and simulation (41.87%), excelling particularly in single-property (55.29% real) and cross-property (53.00% real) tasks. GPT-5 achieves the highest object-description accuracy on real images (87.06%) but drops to 39.84% on cross-property, a 47-point gap that no other model matches in magnitude. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-32B is the strongest (50.08% real, 34.53% sim), outperforming InternVL3.5-30B-A3B by 12.76 points on real images. All models show a consistent pattern: spatial reasoning and object description scores are 2–4× higher than cross-property scores, and the simulation-to-real gap is largest for cross-property (e.g., Gemini: 38.20% sim vs. 53.00% real), indicating that structured property integration does not transfer well across domains. Taxonomy-guided in-context learning (one simulation exemplar) yields modest gains of 1.86–6.12 points on cross-property, with the largest improvement for InternVL3.5-8B (+6.12 points), while oracle property injection adds 5.13–26.76 points on single-property but only 5.13–9.56 points on cross-property, confirming that both perception and integration are independent bottlenecks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying four previously isolated property families (material, physical attributes, affordance, function) with 3D spatial reasoning into a single hierarchical framework called Perceptual Taxonomy. Prior benchmarks each address at most one property dimension in isolation (GQA for material, RIO for affordance, PhysBench for physical, A4Bench for affordance) or spatial reasoning alone (CLEVR, ScanQA, Spatial457), and Hypo3D covers only two families simultaneously. The key conceptual departure is the cross-property reasoning task, which requires a model to jointly satisfy constraints spanning all four families plus spatial context—a formulation the authors argue is absent from all prior work and is grounded in visual cognition literature on hierarchical scene understanding (Gibson, Greene et al., Yuille & Kersten).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PercepTax provides the first systematic diagnostic of where VLMs fail in physical-world reasoning: the oracle experiments cleanly separate perception errors from integration errors, and the three-mode error taxonomy (Physical Commonsense Gap, Category Semantic Bias, Constraint Neglect) gives practitioners actionable targets for improvement. The finding that even with perfect property labels, models remain 30+ points below human performance on cross-property reasoning suggests that current VLM architectures lack a compositional integration mechanism for multi-constraint physical reasoning, pointing toward the need for property-aware reasoning paradigms rather than better visual encoders. Open questions include whether the gap is fundamental to the autoregressive VLM paradigm or addressable through structured reasoning scaffolds, and whether the sim-to-real transfer of taxonomic knowledge (shown to yield modest gains via ICL) can be amplified with more structured prompting or fine-tuning.
