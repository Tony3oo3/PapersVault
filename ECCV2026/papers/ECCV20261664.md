## Problem

Existing few-shot 3D point cloud semantic segmentation methods rely on Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) to construct class prototypes, but FPS is inherently stochastic and produces inconsistent results that cause significant performance variance across random seeds. While attention-based prototype generation offers a deterministic alternative, the paper identifies that vanilla cross-attention fails in this setting due to a large distributional gap—both in scale and anisotropy—between learnable prototypical tokens and support point features, causing attention to collapse onto isolated points rather than capturing semantic structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of labeled support point clouds (K shots per class) and unlabeled query point clouds, each voxelized into 0.02 m grids and uniformly sampled to 20,480 points. A pretrained Stratified Transformer encoder extracts per-point features, which are then split into foreground and background subsets based on the support mask. The output is a per-point semantic label for each query point, assigned by nearest-prototype distance in the feature space, with 100 prototypical tokens per class (200 total) serving as the classification reference.

## Method

WARM (White Aggregation and Restoration Module) wraps a single cross-attention layer with ZCA whitening before attention and coloring (inverse whitening) after, resolving the distributional misalignment between prototypical tokens and support features. Whitening standardizes and decorrelates each class's support features into an isotropic space where tokens can establish semantically meaningful interactions; a regularized eigenvalue threshold (based on the median eigenvalue scaled by point count) prevents noise amplification in sparse scenes. After cross-attention aggregates the aligned features into prototypes, coloring restores the original instance-specific mean and covariance, preserving expressiveness. Training uses a margin loss on query predictions plus a simplification loss to prevent prototype collapse, with the entire module adding only 1.13 M parameters on top of the frozen backbone.

## Results

On S3DIS, WARM achieves a state-of-the-art overall mIoU of 55.66% among uni-modal methods, outperforming the strongest baseline FPS+min-dist. (51.03%) by 4.63 points and COSeg (43.64%) by 12.02 points. The largest gains appear in the 1-way 1-shot setting (60.16% on S0 vs. 51.35% for FPS+min-dist.) and 1-way 5-shot (72.23% on S0 vs. 68.43%). On ScanNet, WARM achieves 41.21% overall mIoU, competitive with COSeg (37.86%) and FPS+min-dist. (39.01%) but trailing the multi-modal MM-FSS (44.53%) which leverages additional text and image features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WARM outperforms all uni-modal baselines on S3DIS across every setting, with the most dramatic margin over COSeg (43.64% → 55.66%, +12.02 mIoU) and QGPA (32.94% → 55.66%, +22.72 mIoU). It also surpasses the simple FPS+min-dist. baseline (51.03%) by 4.63 points, demonstrating that learned attention-based prototypes substantially outperform heuristic sampling. On ScanNet, WARM (41.21%) beats COSeg (37.86%) and FPS+min-dist. (39.01%) but falls short of the multi-modal MM-FSS (44.53%), which exploits text and image features beyond point clouds. Notably, WARM's gain over COSeg is consistent across both datasets (+12.02 on S3DIS, +3.35 on ScanNet), and when plugged into COSeg's pipeline, WARM boosts its mIoU from 46.31% to 59.26% (+12.95), confirming its effectiveness as a drop-in replacement for FPS-based prototype generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant FPS-based prototype generation paradigm in FS-PCS (AttMPTI, COSeg, QGE, QGPA) by replacing algorithmic sampling with deterministic cross-attention, drawing on the learnable-token approach established in image-domain few-shot segmentation (DETR, Mask2Former). However, it identifies and addresses a distributional gap—large anisotropy and scale mismatch between learnable prototypical tokens and point cloud features—that has been overlooked in both the FS-PCS and 3D attention literature. The solution repurposes ZCA whitening and its inverse (coloring) from the ICA/decorrelated-normalization literature as a lightweight alignment wrapper around cross-attention, rather than introducing new learnable components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper shifts attention in FS-PCS from decoder complexity to prototype construction quality, demonstrating that a trivial FPS+min-dist baseline can outperform sophisticated methods like COSeg, which challenges the field's assumption that heavy decoders are necessary. WARM's plug-and-play nature (dropping it into COSeg yields +12.95% mIoU) makes it immediately useful for the community. Open questions include whether the whitening/coloring alignment generalizes to multi-class novel settings beyond 1-way, whether it transfers to other 3D few-shot tasks (detection, instance segmentation), and whether the approach remains effective when the backbone features are not pretrained on a large base set.
