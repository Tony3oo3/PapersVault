## Problem

3D microscopy segmentation faces three simultaneous challenges that have been underexplored together: label scarcity from prohibitively expensive manual annotation, structured label noise from semi-automated tools (manifesting as instance-level false positives from bright artifacts and false negatives from missed structures), and hard patches containing off-target fluorescence or imaging artifacts. Existing noisy-label methods for medical imaging assume boundary-based noise (erosion/dilation) rather than the instance-level FP/FN noise characteristic of microscopy, and no prior work integrates self-supervised pretraining with noisy-label and hard-example detection in 3D microscopy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D microscopy volumes (128³ for pretraining; 256×512×512 or 512³ for downstream tasks) and, during training, single-class binary segmentation masks. A frozen ViT-Large encoder (112³ input, 16³ patches) extracts patch-level features that are compared via cosine similarity against a running feature queue to produce patch-level noise and hardness scores, which are expanded to full-volume probability masks (S×S×S). The final output is a pixel-level 2-class segmentation mask, with the noise and hardness masks serving as internal training signals rather than standalone outputs.

## Method

The pipeline has two components: μ3DINO, a ViT-Large pretrained via the 3DINO self-supervised procedure on 2.26M multimodal 3D microscopy volumes (LSFM, TPEM, confocal), and μDivSeg, a two-model co-training segmentation pipeline inspired by DivideMix. During training, the frozen μ3DINO encoder extracts patch features (enriched with the [CLS] token for global context) that are compared via KNN against a running feature queue: SimiFeat-style clusterability scoring detects noisy patches, while transfer of validation-set prediction errors to training patches via KNN identifies hard patches. The noise probability mask reweights the cross-entropy loss to trade off real labels versus pseudo-labels from the co-trained partner model, and reweights the Dice loss labels; the hardness mask multiplicatively amplifies the CE loss on difficult regions. Two UNETR decoders (g_A, g_B) are trained alternately with cross-pseudo-labeling and MixMatch augmentation, with the feature queue updated online each iteration to adapt detection as the model evolves.

## Results

On four real-world 3D microscopy datasets (TPEM_N, TPEM_V, LSFM_A, LSFM_N), μDivSeg achieves Dice scores of 79.5%, 82.0%, 76.8%, and 75.2% at 100% data, outperforming the next best SOTA baseline (GuidedNet_μ) by an average of 6.4% Dice. SSL pretraining via μ3DINO improves over random initialization by 5.8% Dice at 10% data and 3.9% at 100% data, with μDivSeg adding a further 1.1% and 1.9% respectively. On the NIS3D toy dataset, μDivSeg degrades far more slowly under increasing instance-based synthetic label noise (e.g., 83.5%→75.5% Dice from Clean to L3 on NIS3D_Z) compared to the standard μ3DINO pipeline (82.2%→71.9%), demonstrating robustness to the FP/FN noise characteristic of microscopy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UNet (Ronneberger 2015), μDivSeg wins on all four real-world datasets at 100% data (e.g., 79.5% vs 73.4% on TPEM_N), confirming that the SSL + noisy-label/hard-patch pipeline substantially outperforms a standard CNN. Against μSAM (Archit 2025), a 2D pretrained encoder with a 3D wrapper, μDivSeg achieves large gains on TPEM_N (+8.8%) and TPEM_V (+17.9%) but is competitive on LSFM_A (76.8% vs 74.7%), where μSAM's cell/nuclear pretraining is more aligned. Against GuidedNet (Zhao 2024), a semi-supervised co-training method, μDivSeg leads by an average of 6.4% Dice at 100% data; GuidedNet_μ (with μ3DINO init) narrows the gap but still trails, particularly on TPEM_N (74.6% vs 79.5%). Against L2B (Zhou 2024), a meta-learning label-noise method, μDivSeg leads by an average of 9.4% at 100% data; L2B suffers severe degradation on small datasets (e.g., 56.1% on TPEM_N at 100% with random init) due to unreliable reweighting under noisy labels. On the NIS3D toy dataset, μDivSeg is the most robust to increasing synthetic instance-based noise, maintaining 75.5% Dice at L3 on NIS3D_Z while GuidedNet_μ drops to 68.5% and L2B_μ to 66.6%, though L2B_μ performs competitively on NIS3D_D where the validation label is very clean.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads for 3D microscopy: large-scale SSL pretraining (building directly on 3DINO's 3D ViT adaptation of DINOv2), KNN-based noisy label detection (adapting SimiFeat and the HOC estimator from classification to segmentation), and the DivideMix two-model co-training loop for self-correcting labels. It departs from prior noisy-label segmentation work, which typically models boundary erosion/dilation noise in CT/MRI, by addressing instance-based noise (missing or spurious whole objects) characteristic of semi-automated microscopy labeling. The hard-patch detection via cross-set KNN in feature space is a novel data-centric mechanism not present in prior hard-example mining or reweighting approaches, which relied on loss-based or meta-learning signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released μ3DINO weights (pretrained on 2.2M+ multimodal 3D volumes) and the μDivSeg pipeline provide the first general-purpose foundation model and robust training recipe for 3D microscopy, lowering the barrier for segmentation in neuroscience and related fields. A key open question is whether the method's ability to surpass silver-standard ground truth (observed on TPEM_V and LSFM_N) can be systematically quantified, since current evaluation metrics penalize correct predictions that disagree with noisy labels. Limitations include the imbalanced pretraining distribution, the fixed-patch-size tradeoff between context and mask resolution, and the FIFO feature queue that does not account for feature diversity—each pointing to concrete future work in data curation, hierarchical clustering, and queue management.
