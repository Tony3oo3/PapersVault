## Problem

Current robot learning approaches underexploit large-scale robot-data pretraining as a source of transferable dynamics priors: many works fine-tune from generic video-generation checkpoints without action-grounded pretraining, or pretrain on single datasets that limit embodiment and camera diversity. The field lacks a unified pretraining paradigm that learns reusable action-to-dynamics priors and reliably benefits both world-model-based simulation and downstream policy learning from a single initialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260520/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The base A2World takes a multi-view initial frame and a 20-step action chunk (unified dual-arm format of 7 dimensions per arm: end-effector pose plus gripper state, with single-arm robots zero-padded) and generates 20 future frames in the WAN2.1 latent space at 480p/10fps. A2World-sim additionally consumes a compact set of pose-guided history frames (budget m=20) sampled via weighted arc-length to cover executed motion, enabling chunk-wise autoregressive long-horizon rollouts. A2World-policy takes an initial frame and a T5-encoded language instruction, and jointly denoises video and action latents under modality-specific noise levels, outputting both a future video and an executable action sequence in a single forward pass.

## Method

A2World is a 2.5B-parameter DiT-based multi-view diffusion world model initialized from Cosmos-Predict2-2B-Video2World, pretrained on 2,156k real robot manipulation trajectories spanning 20+ embodiments with direct action annotations (no latent-action pseudo-labels). Action conditioning is injected by encoding the action chunk through an MLP and adding it to the diffusion timestep embedding, which is then consumed by adaptive layer normalization in every DiT block; multi-view consistency is enforced by packing views into the temporal dimension, adding learnable view embeddings, and inserting cross-view attention modules in each block. For long-horizon simulation, A2World-sim adds pose-guided arc-length history sampling and dual history injection (cross-attention plus self-attention memory concatenation), trained with a self-forcing strategy that conditions the model on its own generated frames. For policy transfer, A2World-policy introduces MoE-like video-action blocks where video and action tokens share a single self-attention expert for cross-modal interaction while retaining modality-specific AdaLN and MLP denoising branches, trained with a weighted joint denoising objective and modality-wise classifier-free guidance at inference.

## Results

On LIBERO, A2World-policy achieves a 98.6% average success rate across four suites (Spatial 98.2%, Object 99.2%, Goal 98.6%, Long 98.2%), outperforming all 9 baselines including Cosmos Policy (98.5%) and CogVLA (97.4%). For the simulator variant, A2World-sim attains the best rollout quality on both LIBERO (PSNR 26.64, SSIM 0.8957, EPE 0.3498, cos 0.6045) and real-robot data (PSNR 25.95, SSIM 0.8511, EPE 0.7218, cos 0.2784), surpassing Cosmos-Predict2, Ctrl-World, and Prophet. On OOD evaluation (LIBERO-Plus Spatial), action-to-video pretraining (A-pre) reaches 88.5% average success versus 80.2% for direct Cosmos initialization and 85.8% for text-conditioned pretraining. The simulator also shows strong real-world consistency with Spearman ρ=0.916 and R²=0.930 across 8 policy-task pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LIBERO (Table 5), A2World-policy (98.6%) narrowly beats Cosmos Policy (98.5%) and CogVLA (97.4%), and substantially outperforms π₀.₅ (96.9%), UniVLA (95.2%), π₀ (94.2%), Dita (92.3%), 4D-VLA (88.6%), and Diffusion Policy (72.4%). On OOD transfer (Table 6, LIBERO-Plus Spatial), A2World-policy with action-to-video pretraining (A-pre, 88.5%) clearly surpasses direct Cosmos initialization (C-init, 80.2%) and text-conditioned pretraining (T-pre, 85.8%), and is on par with policy-targeted pretraining (P-pre, 88.6%) while also serving as a reusable simulator. For rollout quality (Table 2), A2World-sim dominates all four baselines (Cosmos-Predict2, Ctrl-World, Prophet, T-pre) on every metric across both LIBERO and real-robot data, with the largest gaps in action-faithfulness (EPE 0.3498 vs. 0.4009 for Cosmos-Predict2; cos 0.6045 vs. 0.5755). On RoboNet (Table 3), A2World-sim achieves the best FVD (146.1) and LPIPS (8.9) among autoregressive video prediction baselines. On OOD simulator evaluation (Table 4), A2World-sim outperforms DreamDojo on PSNR (25.91 vs. 23.79), SSIM (0.8719 vs. 0.8571), EPE (0.1301 vs. 0.2738), and cos (0.2761 vs. 0.2107), confirming superior action-conditioned dynamics modeling under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT-based latent video diffusion architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and initializes from the Cosmos-Predict2-2B checkpoint (Agarwal et al., 2025), but departs from prior work that fine-tunes generic video models directly on small robot datasets without large-scale action-grounded pretraining. It also extends the self-forcing training strategy (Huang et al., 2025) for autoregressive stability. The key conceptual departure is from both single-purpose world models (simulator-only or policy-only) and from text-conditioned video pretraining: the paper positions action-conditioned pretraining on 2.1M+ multi-embodiment trajectories as a reusable dynamics prior that simultaneously initializes a long-horizon simulator (A2World-sim) and a MoE-like video-action policy (A2World-policy), a dual-use framing not present in prior action-conditioned world model work like Ctrl-World or Prophet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that action-to-video pretraining on diverse robot data yields a transferable prior that outperforms both generic video initialization and text-conditioned robot pretraining for downstream policy learning, while also serving as a faithful long-horizon simulator (Spearman ρ=0.916 with real-robot success rates). This dual-use property is practically significant: a single pretrained checkpoint can be adapted for both what-if simulation and instruction-conditioned control, reducing the need for separate pretraining pipelines. Open questions include whether the 2D video-based dynamics prior captures sufficient physical structure for truly novel contact-rich interactions, how the approach scales to higher-fidelity or 3D representations, and whether the simulator's faithfulness degrades on tasks far from the pretraining distribution. The real-robot evaluation, while positive, covers only 5 tasks on a single platform, leaving broader generalization claims partially untested.
