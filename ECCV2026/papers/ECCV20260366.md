## Problem

Parameter-efficient finetuning methods like LoRA reduce computational cost but require separate adapters per task, preventing cross-task knowledge sharing and violating the conditions of strict continual learning (no data replay, no model growth, no additional models). While the Universal Weight Subspace Hypothesis demonstrated that LoRA adapters share a common low-rank subspace, it left open how to continually discover and update this subspace as new tasks arrive. Share addresses this gap by proposing a replay-free method that incrementally learns and refines a single shared subspace, enabling almost strict continual learning across diverse modalities.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, Share receives either task-specific training data or a pre-trained LoRA adapter (hybrid mode supports both), with no access to past tasks. The method outputs a single unified model consisting of frozen principal basis vectors plus lightweight per-task coefficient matrices (pseudo-rank p as small as 1), which can be swapped or selected via a task classifier. Downstream outputs are task-dependent: class labels for image classification, 3D rotation parameters (azimuth, elevation, in-plane rotation) for pose estimation, CLIP-scored generated images for text-to-image, and correlation/accuracy scores for NLU tasks.

## Method

Share reparameterizes LoRA by decomposing the low-rank update into frozen principal basis vectors (obtained via SVD of stacked LoRA matrices) and trainable task-specific coefficients, reducing per-task parameters by up to 100×. The three-phase continual procedure is: (1) initialization, where SVD of available LoRA adapters yields k principal basis vectors; (2) continual adaptation, where only φ < k temporary basis vectors and their coefficients are finetuned on new data; and (3) merging, where temporary vectors are integrated back via SVD and all task coefficients are analytically recalculated using the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, a data- and gradient-free step. The paper provides an incremental subspace error bound (Proposition 1) and a generalization theorem (Theorem 1) showing that when the new task lies largely within the shared principal subspace, the rank-k approximation achieves tighter error bounds than full-rank adaptation. This yields a single model replacing hundreds of task-specific LoRA adapters with 281× memory savings while maintaining performance comparable to jointly trained upper bounds.

## Results

On the Continual GLUE benchmark, Share-full achieves 83.44% average accuracy with only 0.012M trainable parameters (0.29 MB), matching non-continual LoRA (83.43%) which requires 7.2M parameters (81.6 MB) — a 100× parameter reduction and 281× memory saving. On image classification (CIFAR-100, Food-101, Caltech-101, Flowers-102), Share reaches 94.20%, 90.10%, 97.70%, and 97.90% final accuracy respectively with just 0.10M parameters, matching or approaching the full-model upper bound while outperforming all rehearsal-free baselines. On 3D pose estimation (Pascal3D+), Share achieves 81.80° π/6 accuracy with 1M parameters, surpassing the best replay-based method iNeMO (79.28°) which requires 25M parameters and exemplar storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Share dominates all 13 baselines across every evaluated domain. On image classification, Share (94.20% on CIFAR-100) matches the full-model upper bound and edges out the closest competitor DAP (94.05%) while using less than half the parameters (0.10M vs. 0.19M); it surpasses prompt-based methods (L2P 83.05%, DualPrompt 84.77%, CODA-P 86.25%) and adapter-based EASE (87.81%) by 6–11 points. On Continual GLUE, Share-full (83.44% avg) matches non-CL LoRA (83.43%) with 100× fewer parameters, while the strict-CL variant Share (78.69%) still far exceeds O-LoRA (57.50%) by 21 points. On OOD classification, Share (81.51% ImageNet-R, 78.76% DomainNet) outperforms InfLoRA (71.01/74.53) and SD-LoRA (75.26/72.82) by 5–10 points. On 3D pose estimation, Share (81.80° π/6) beats the best replay-based method iNeMO (79.28°) with 25× fewer parameters and no exemplar storage. For text-to-image generation, Share achieves CLIP scores comparable to individual LoRAs (e.g., 23.38 vs. 22.78 on T1) while compressing all tasks into a single shared factor set, and outperforms non-continual TIES merging on OOD Rouge-L (55.89 avg vs. lower TIES scores).

## Relation to Prior Work

Share builds directly on the Universal Weight Subspace Hypothesis and its application in EigenLoRAx, which showed that LoRA adapters across tasks share a common low-rank subspace discoverable via SVD. However, while EigenLoRAx extracts this subspace statically from a fixed set of adapters, Share makes the subspace dynamic and continually updated through an incremental SVD-merge procedure, addressing the open question of how to discover and refine the shared subspace as new tasks arrive. The method departs from orthogonal-subspace approaches like O-LoRA (which isolate tasks to prevent interference but block knowledge transfer) and null-space projection methods like InfLoRA (which prevent interference without discovering shared structure), instead explicitly exploiting common principal directions to enable both forward and backward knowledge transfer within a single evolving subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a single Share model can replace hundreds of task-specific LoRA adapters with 100× fewer trainable parameters and 281× less memory, addressing a real deployment bottleneck in managing growing adapter collections for LLMs and diffusion models. The observation of backward knowledge transfer—where earlier tasks improve after later tasks are learned—is a notable phenomenon in PEFT continual learning that the shared-subspace mechanism naturally enables. Key limitations include the 'almost strict' framing (the method still requires initial LoRA adapters or data for subspace initialization, and Share-full relaxes the no-replay constraint), and the authors note that extending Share to learning from scratch remains open. Whether the shared subspace hypothesis generalizes to fundamentally different modalities or tasks with very different structure is an important open question.
