## Problem

Existing 4D occupancy generation frameworks rely on explicit geometric conditions (predefined trajectories, BEV layouts, or navigation commands) that are rigid, labor-intensive, and cannot express high-level behavioral intents. Meanwhile, text-conditioned approaches using standard encoders (CLIP, T5) operate at a coarse attribute level and fail to bridge the 'Semantic-Spatiotemporal Gap'—translating procedural, relational language instructions into physically plausible, temporally consistent voxel-level multi-agent dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language script (e.g., 'the ego vehicle followed a truck before the intersection and overtook it after clearing it') and an optional clean history occupancy context O_{1:h} as input, producing a future 4D occupancy sequence O_{h+1:F}. The occupancy is represented as a 200×200×16 voxel grid at 0.4 m/voxel (X/Y: ±40 m, Z: −3.2–3.2 m), sampled at 2 Hz with 11 semantic categories, and generated as 16-frame clips. The text is consumed through a frozen VLM encoder rather than a standard text encoder, and the history context is injected as clean latent tokens that the model is explicitly trained to condition on.

## Method

OccDirector uses a frozen Occ-VAE (from DOME) to compress 4D occupancy into patchified latent tokens, and a frozen Qwen3-VL-2B VLM plus a lightweight 2-layer bidirectional Token Refiner to extract compositional text features, bridging the modality gap before conditioning. The generation backbone is a 14-layer double-stream Spatio-Temporal MMDiT (d_model=896) that factorizes attention via Spatio-Temporal Separated Attention (STSA): frame-wise 3D spatial joint attention couples text and occupancy tokens within each frame for script-to-layout grounding, while patch-wise temporal self-attention propagates dynamics across frames, with 2D spatial RoPE and 1D temporal RoPE for positional encoding. Training uses Flow Matching (continuous normalizing flow) with a stochastic history-prefix anchoring strategy: during training, the noisy history prefix is randomly replaced with clean ground-truth tokens and a masked loss penalizes only future-frame predictions; during inference, step-wise anchoring overwrites the history prefix at every ODE integration step, ensuring the generated future remains strictly coherent with the provided context without out-of-distribution artifacts.

## Results

On the OccInteract-85k benchmark, OccDirector with history-prefix control achieves an FVD of 275, a 29.1% improvement over the strongest text-adapted baseline (DynamicCity+CLIP, FVD 388), while also attaining the highest video Recall (0.12) and the best VLM-based instruction alignment mean score of 3.73 (vs. 3.09 for DynamicCity+CLIP and 2.97 for DynamicCity+T5). On single-frame metrics, OccDirector reaches IS 3.40 and FID 62, matching or exceeding geometric-conditioned methods (DynamicCity: IS 3.39, FID 61) despite operating without explicit trajectory inputs. The history-prefix control mechanism further boosts structural correctness from 3.66 to 4.01 and overall instruction-following mean from 3.43 to 3.73.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OccDirector decisively outperforms both text-adapted baselines (DynamicCity+CLIP and DynamicCity+T5) on every instruction-alignment axis, with a mean VLM score of 3.73 versus 3.09 and 2.97 respectively, and a 29.1% FVD reduction over DynamicCity+CLIP. The text-adapted baselines suffer severe mode collapse (Recall 0.008–0.02), indicating that standard CLIP/T5 encoders cannot differentiate complex prompts, whereas OccDirector's VLM-driven pipeline generates far more diverse scenarios (Recall 0.12). Against the geometric-conditioned methods (DOME, COME, DynamicCity), OccDirector achieves comparable single-frame quality (IS 3.40 vs. 3.21–3.39; FID 62 vs. 61–77) while substantially improving video temporal coherence (FVD 275 vs. 391–437) and video Recall (0.12 vs. 0.03–0.08). The only metric where OccDirector trails is single-frame Precision (0.33 vs. 0.38–0.40 for DOME/COME), which the authors attribute to the geometric priors those methods exploit to constrain the generation space. Overall, OccDirector wins on all video-level and instruction-following metrics while matching geometric-conditioned methods on per-frame fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

OccDirector sits at the intersection of 4D occupancy generation (DOME, COME, DynamicCity) and text-conditioned scene synthesis (UniScene, X-Scene), but departs from both lines in a key way: prior occupancy generators require explicit geometric priors (trajectories, BEV layouts, HD maps) as conditioning, while prior text-driven driving scene methods use language only for coarse global attributes (weather, time-of-day) or as an intermediate layout proxy. Architecturally, the paper assembles components from the video-generation literature—MMDiT dual-stream transformers, flow-matching objectives, and Spatio-Temporal Separated Attention from LATTe—into a 4D occupancy setting, and replaces standard CLIP/T5 text encoders with a frozen VLM (Qwen3-VL) plus a lightweight token refiner to capture procedural and relational semantics. The history-prefix anchoring strategy draws on classifier-free-guidance-style dropout during training and step-wise token replacement at inference, a technique borrowed from video generation (HunyuanVideo) but applied here to enforce interaction-consistent rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to generate 4D occupancy dynamics purely from natural language scripts without any geometric priors, opening a more intuitive and scalable interface for autonomous-driving scenario design that does not require users to hand-craft 3D trajectories. The OccInteract-85k dataset and the VLM-based evaluation rubric provide reusable infrastructure for the community, though the evaluation's reliance on VLM judges to score VLM-conditioned generation introduces a circularity concern that limits the strength of the instruction-following claims. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer horizons and higher-resolution grids, whether the frozen VLM encoder can be replaced by a jointly trained module for tighter alignment, and whether the synthetic caption pipeline generalizes to truly novel interaction types not seen during data curation.
