## Problem

Existing benchmarks for evaluating MLLMs as embodied brains either focus narrowly on execution success or, when targeting high-level cognition, suffer from fragmented coverage (isolating perception, planning, or error reflection), limited task realism (heavy reliance on simulation, ignoring diverse embodiment and occlusion), and simplistic planning evaluation reduced to BLEU scores or multiple-choice matching. No prior benchmark systematically assesses the full cognitive pipeline from intent understanding through failure diagnosis on realistic, multi-embodiment robotic data.

## Input / Output

Inputs are scene images (single or multi-view with occlusion), text instructions (explicit or implicit), video clips for temporal grounding and navigation tasks, and robot embodiment metadata (single-arm, dual-arm, mobile, humanoid). Outputs vary by task type: multiple-choice selections for perception, affordance, and failure analysis; structured action sequences parameterized as skill-object-args tuples for planning; spatial affordance points or trajectories; and binary task-state predictions. Planning outputs are evaluated against a manually annotated DAG encoding precedence constraints and critical object-state milestones, rather than against a single reference plan.

## Method

RoboBench defines five evaluation dimensions (Instruction Comprehension, Perception Reasoning, Generalized Planning, Affordance Prediction, Failure Analysis) spanning 14 capabilities, 25 tasks, and 6092 QA pairs, built from large-scale real robotic data (Open X-Embodiment, RoboMIND, etc.) and in-house collection across diverse embodiments. The key technical contribution is an MLLM-as-world-simulator framework for long-horizon planning evaluation: given the first-frame image, a reference action list, and a human-annotated DAG of task dependencies, an MLLM judge performs a step-by-step rollout under visual and physical constraints, scoring both NodeCorrectness (one-to-one matching of predicted vs. ground-truth action nodes on skill and object parameters) and TaskCompletion (fraction of critical object-state milestones achieved during simulated execution). This moves beyond symbolic matching to assess whether plans are physically executable and achieve the intended state changes. The protocol reliability is validated via ICC(2,k)=0.948 across four judge models and three prompt variants, and its alignment with human judgments (r=0.83) exceeds that of baseline LLM scoring (r=0.73).

## Results

Across 18 evaluated MLLMs, Gemini-3.1-Pro achieves the highest scores: 67.32% on Perception Reasoning, 85.70% on Affordance Prediction, and 52.95% on Failure Analysis, approaching the human upper bounds of 74.30%, 82.63%, and 63.99% respectively. The text-only GPT-5.4 baseline scores only 27.12% (perception) and 25.61% (affordance), confirming that visual grounding is essential. Closed-source MLLMs lead open-source models by 10–15 percentage points in four of five dimensions. Downstream correlation analysis shows perception reasoning predicts CALVIN success (object-centric r=0.884, scene-centric r=0.833) while affordance prediction predicts LIBERO-10 success (static r=0.617, dynamic r=0.517).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-3.1-Pro (the strongest evaluated model) outperforms Gemini-2.5-Pro by 12.05 points on Affordance Prediction (85.70 vs. 73.65) and 3.21 points on Perception Reasoning (67.32 vs. 64.14), confirming that the latest generation closes the gap toward human-level embodied cognition. Among open-source multi-image MLLMs, Qwen3-VL-8B leads with 41.49% perception and 21.24% affordance, but remains 25+ points below the best closed-source model on perception and 64 points below on affordance. Embodied-specialized models (RoboBrain-2.5-4B, MiMo-Embodied-7B) underperform general-purpose MLLMs on most dimensions, with RoboBrain-2.5-4B reaching only 44.04% perception and 48.09% affordance, suggesting that domain-specific training has not yet yielded a clear advantage over frontier general MLLMs. LLaVA-OneVision-7B is the weakest open-source model overall (38.88% perception, 46.22% affordance), trailing even Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Ins on several sub-tasks. The text-only GPT-5.4 baseline (27.12% perception, 25.61% affordance) is near-random on multiple-choice tasks, validating that RoboBench items cannot be solved from language priors alone and that visual-embodied grounding is the binding constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboBench unifies the fragmented landscape of embodied-cognition benchmarks (RoboVQA, EgoPlanBench, MMRo, BEAR, ECBench, etc.), each of which isolates one or two abilities, into a single pipeline-aligned evaluation spanning instruction comprehension, perception, planning, affordance, and failure analysis. It departs from prior planning evaluation (BLEU, multiple-choice, LLM scoring) by introducing an MLLM-as-world-simulator that checks whether predicted plans achieve critical object-state milestones under physical and visual constraints, guided by a human-annotated DAG. For failure analysis it adopts the REFLECT-style perturbation protocol, and for the downstream VLA correlation study it follows VLM4VLA's minimal-fine-tuning pipeline on CALVIN and LIBERO-10.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoboBench provides the first unified scaffold for quantifying the full cognitive loop of an embodied brain and, critically, demonstrates that specific RoboBench dimensions (perception reasoning, affordance prediction) strongly predict downstream VLA manipulation success, offering a principled way to select VLM backbones without running expensive VLA rollouts. The stark asymmetry between affordance prediction (85.7 for the best model, near human level) and execution-failure diagnosis (25.2, far below the 47.3 human bound) highlights that 'seeing what to do' is largely solved while 'diagnosing why it went wrong' remains an open problem requiring fine-grained embodied perception. Open questions include whether the world-simulator evaluation generalizes beyond manipulation to navigation or deformable-object tasks, and whether closing the implicit-instruction gap (79.9 → 61.4 for the best model) requires joint language-perception-context reasoning architectures.
