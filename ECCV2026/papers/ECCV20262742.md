## Problem

Pixel-parallel processor arrays (PPAs) like SCAMP can perform local image processing on-sensor but lack the global memory access needed for 6DoF visual odometry optimization, which prior work offloaded to external CPUs. The core challenge is achieving a consistent global camera motion estimate—a single SE(3) value—across a distributed memory structure where each pixel only stores local variables and communicates with neighbors. This paper asks whether dense monocular VO and depth estimation can be solved entirely through local pixel-wise computation and message passing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262742/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source image and a target image (128×128 in experiments), a per-pixel surface normal map (from a small local CNN or ground truth), and camera intrinsics. Each pixel stores a stacked variable of 7 elements (6 for SE(3) pose, 1 for log-depth), and the output is a single 6DoF relative pose (averaged across all pixels) plus a dense depth map for the source frame. A keyframe anchoring strategy keeps the reference frame fixed while the target frame advances incrementally, so the effective baseline is regulated to stay within the convergence basin of the photometric alignment.

## Method

The paper formulates dense VO and depth estimation as a Gaussian factor graph with a stacked variable at every pixel, then solves it via iterative Gaussian Belief Propagation (GBP) where all message passing is local. Four factor types constrain the variables: photometric factors enforce per-pixel intensity consistency under warping, prior factors propagate pose/depth continuity across frames, identity factors (arranged in a hierarchical sharded quadtree topology) synchronize the per-pixel pose copies toward a single global SE(3) value, and normal-integration factors (following BINI) constrain depth differences between neighbors to match locally predicted surface normals. The key insight is that dense VO is more amenable to pixel-level distribution than sparse VO because scene continuity provides local, pixel-wise geometric priors. Optimization proceeds by iteratively linearizing nonlinear factors on the SE(3)×R manifold at each pixel's current mean, passing Gaussian messages between factors and variables, and relinearizing, with a Huber robust loss and covariance weighting throughout.

## Results

The paper evaluates on 128×128 image sequences from the Replica dataset (office_0–3), upsampled 10× to simulate high frame-rate PPA operation. The GBP-based pixel-distributed method achieves pose estimation that is slightly less accurate than a centralized Gauss-Newton/IRLS baseline but operates with only local pixel access. For depth, the photometrically-guided normal integration substantially outperforms pure BINI after convergence, with the largest gains at moderate translational baselines. Using DSINE-predicted normals (instead of ground-truth) yields pose quality 'not substantially worse' than ground-truth normals, aside from a reduced convergence basin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Two main comparisons are made. First, against a centralized Gauss-Newton/IRLS baseline (Sec. 3.1 formulation), the distributed GBP method converges more slowly and to slightly lower accuracy, as expected given its restricted local-access constraint; however, it is the only variant feasible on a pixel-parallel array without global memory. Second, against pure BINI normal integration (Cao et al., ECCV 2022), the proposed photometrically-guided approach achieves substantially higher depth quality after convergence, extending reconstruction beyond the object level by pulling both sides of depth-discontinuous boundaries apart. The keyframe-based tracking analysis (Fig. 4) further shows that the method is most reliable at high frame rates (framerate 10, i.e., 10× upsampled), where inter-frame motion is small; at framerate 0.2 (50× larger baseline), the method settles more frequently at suboptimal solutions, while the marginal benefit of going from framerate 5 to 10 is limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pixel-distributed GBP framework of PixRO [2] (rotation-only) and Scona et al. [47] (RGB-D, on Graphcore IPU) to full monocular 6DoF SE(3) visual odometry with dense depth estimation, using a hierarchical sharded factor graph topology. It repurposes the spatial sparsity of BINI's [10] bilateral normal integration as a per-pixel depth prior, and departs from the dominant sparse-VO paradigm by arguing that dense photometric tracking is actually more amenable to pixel-parallel hardware due to scene continuity. Unlike prior SCAMP-based VO work [8,32] that offloaded 3D optimization to an external CPU, this is the first formulation where the entire 6DoF pose and depth solve is closed within the pixel array via local message passing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first concrete algorithmic blueprint for on-sensor 6DoF VO + depth, directly informing the design of next-generation PPA chips (memory budget, communication topology, per-pixel compute). The keyframe-anchoring strategy reveals a practical operating window: frame rates that are too high collapse the baseline and make rotation/translation ambiguous, while too-low rates shrink the convergence basin—a trade-off with direct implications for sensor-controller co-design. However, the evaluation is limited to a GPU simulation at 128×128 on a single synthetic dataset (Replica), with ground-truth normals in most experiments, so real-hardware feasibility and generalization to diverse scenes remain open. The authors also flag non-rigid scene flow and optimal factor-graph topology for manufacturable chips as key future directions.
