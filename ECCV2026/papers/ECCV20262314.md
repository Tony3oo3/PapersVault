## Problem

Building damage classification from satellite imagery typically relies on per-building appearance cues, yet disaster damage is spatially structured and the relevant neighbourhood scale varies substantially across disaster types (floods, hurricanes, wildfires, etc.). Naive spatial context aggregation can improve visual coherence while oversmoothing damage boundaries or propagating structured errors, and existing graph-based approaches lack a principled, disaster-type-specific spatial scale, making them fragile under cross-event and cross-dataset shift.

## Input / Output

Each building is represented by a pre/post combined (PPC) patch—a stacked bitemporal RGB tensor cropped from the xBD polygon bounding box with a small margin and resized to a fixed size—along with its UTM-projected centroid and a disaster-type token parsed from the event filename. Buildings within an event form a fixed k=16 kNN graph over GPS centroids, with edge features encoding Euclidean distance and relative direction. The model outputs a 4-class probability vector (no damage, minor, major, destroyed) per building, from which a severity score is derived for the ordinal and residual-spatial losses.

## Method

The core contribution is a disaster-type-conditioned multi-scale (DCMS) kernel prior injected into GAT-style attention logits: a learned disaster-type embedding predicts mixture weights and length scales for M=3 exponential decay kernels, producing an interpretable distance bias K_ij(e) that is added as λ_K·log(K_ij+ε) to the compatibility score, so the effective neighbourhood scale adapts per disaster type rather than being a single global smoothing rule. A residual de-correlation loss penalizes positive Moran's I of severity prediction residuals, explicitly discouraging spatially clustered errors. The patch encoder (frozen ResNet-50 with bitemporal stem) provides node features, while only the graph attention layers and classifier head are trained under a combined objective of class-balanced cross-entropy, EMD-style ordinal consistency, and the residual Moran penalty. The kNN topology and geometric edge features remain fixed throughout; only attention weights are recomputed per layer, yielding a dynamic weighted graph over the same edges.

## Results

On the xView2 holdout (fixed building instances, classification-only), the full kernel-regularized model achieves Macro-F1 0.87288, improving over the patch-only ResNet-50 baseline (0.82205) and vanilla GAT (0.84102), with the largest gains in the Minor (0.762 vs 0.673) and Major (0.892 vs 0.815) classes. Under the stricter zero-shot leave-one-event-out (LOEO) protocol, the full model reaches Macro-F1 0.503 (vs 0.433 patch-only, 0.453 vanilla GAT) while reducing residual Moran's I from 0.248 to 0.079, indicating substantially less spatially clustered error. On zero-shot cross-dataset transfer (xBD → Ida-BD), the full model achieves Macro-F1 0.335 versus 0.275 (patch-only) and 0.295 (vanilla GAT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two published xBD→Ida-BD transfer baselines in Table 5, the proposed model (Macro-F1 0.335) outperforms the xView2 winner pipeline [21] (0.268) by 6.7 points but falls short of Siam-UNet [13] (0.458), which merges Destroyed into Major and uses an end-to-end segmentation pipeline, making the comparison only partially fair. The paper explicitly notes these external baselines use different pipelines and label handling, so the primary controlled comparisons are internal: the full model consistently beats both the patch-only ResNet-50 encoder and vanilla GAT across all three evaluation protocols (xView2 holdout, LOEO, and Ida-BD transfer). The most striking margin is in residual spatial autocorrelation under LOEO, where the full model reduces Moran's I from 0.248 (patch-only) and 0.256 (vanilla GAT) to 0.079, a 68–69% reduction that neither baseline achieves. On the xView2 holdout, the full model's 3.2-point Macro-F1 gain over vanilla GAT is driven almost entirely by the Minor and Major classes, where spatial context most disambiguates damage severity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends graph attention networks (GAT/GATv2) for spatially-aware building damage assessment by injecting a geostatistically-motivated, disaster-type-conditioned multi-scale kernel prior into attention logits, departing from the unconstrained attention used in prior GAT-based damage assessment work (e.g., [11] on UAV imagery). It also repurposes Moran's I—a standard post-hoc spatial autocorrelation diagnostic from geostatistics—into a differentiable training objective that penalizes spatially clustered prediction residuals, a use not seen in prior damage-assessment pipelines. The bitemporal ResNet-50 patch encoder follows the xView2 first-place-derived fusion baseline [25], and the overall setup builds on the xBD/xView2 dataset and its four-class damage taxonomy while deliberately isolating classification from detection to focus on contextual reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in post-disaster assessment: models that classify buildings independently produce spatially clustered errors under event shift, while naive graph smoothing can propagate mistakes across boundaries. The zero-shot LOEO and cross-dataset (xBD→Ida-BD) evaluations are valuable stress tests that go beyond in-distribution holdout metrics. Key limitations include the assumption of known building instances and centroids (no localization uncertainty), GPS-only kNN graphs that ignore scene semantics like roads and parcels, and the requirement that the disaster-type token be available at inference. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to detector-generated footprints, whether conditioning can be inferred when metadata is missing, and whether multimodal (e.g., SAR) inputs would further reduce the residual spatial structure that remains even in the full model.
