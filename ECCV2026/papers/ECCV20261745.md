## Problem

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) learn transferable representations via latent prediction but remain unexplored for hyperspectral imagery, where generic masking or cropping corrupts high-dimensional spectra, removes pixel-level semantics, or yields degenerate prediction targets. The central question is how to design self-supervised pretraining that produces representations robust to extreme label scarcity (1–10 shots per class) and cross-scene temporal/spatial shifts, without relying on fragile augmentations or pixel-level reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261745/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 15×15 spatial patches over B bands (reduced to 15 PCA components) from a hyperspectral cube, with symmetric padding enabling valid extraction near boundaries. During pretraining, each patch is consumed in three aligned views: the raw patch as context, and per-band morphologically opened and closed versions as pixel-aligned prediction targets, plus an on-the-fly geometric transform (rotation/flip) as a semantic anchor. At inference, the frozen encoder maps a patch to a D-dimensional embedding, and a linear head trained on N labeled patches per class produces the final class label.

## Method

MorphJEPA replaces generic masking with per-band morphological opening/closing to generate structurally simplified, pixel-aligned prediction targets, imposing a directional bottleneck that makes high-frequency nuisances hard to predict while preserving transferable topology. The encoder is a compact disentangled design: a spatial branch (standard + dilated 3×3 convolutions) captures multi-scale geometry, a spectral branch (1×1 convolution) preserves per-pixel material identity, and multi-head cross-attention fuses them so spatial tokens query spectral tokens. Training uses a student-teacher JEPA objective with an EMA teacher (momentum 0.996) and three lightweight predictors mapping the student context embedding to stop-gradient target embeddings, minimizing summed L2 prediction loss. An asymmetric, context-anchored SIGReg regularizer (applied only to student context embeddings, not targets) enforces near-isotropic covariance to prevent dimensional collapse under severe spatial-spectral correlation. The paper provides theoretical bounds showing morphology prediction reduces nuisance-induced W1 drift and SIGReg tightens the Rademacher complexity term for low-shot linear probing.

## Results

On the intra-scene Houston 2013 benchmark (5-shot), MorphJEPA achieves 74.73% OA, 77.53% AA, and 72.61% κ, outperforming the strongest SSL baseline SC-EADNet (71.85% OA) by +2.88% and MAE (68.45% OA) by +6.28%. On Trento (5-shot), it reaches 89.62% OA and 86.20% κ, surpassing CM-SCON (87.22% OA) and SSLSM (84.25% OA). Under cross-scene temporal transfer (Houston 2013→2018, 5-shot low-shot adaptation), MorphJEPA secures 67.21% OA, edging out GGCL (66.01%) by +1.20%. On HyRank (Dioni→Loukia, 5-shot adaptation), it achieves 60.37% OA with a dominant 71.22% AA and 54.11% κ, indicating balanced per-class transfer rather than overfitting to dominant classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MorphJEPA outperforms all 13 baselines on Houston 2013 across every shot setting. The most striking gap is against LeJEPA, which suffers catastrophic dimensional collapse (24.27% OA at 5-shot vs. MorphJEPA's 74.73%), empirically validating the need for the morphological pretext and asymmetric SIGReg. Among HSI-specific SSL methods, MorphJEPA beats SC-EADNet by +2.88% OA and CM-SCON (which uses LiDAR) by +5.78% OA at 5-shot Houston 2013, and surpasses the supervised Res-CP by +9.65% OA. On Trento 5-shot, it leads CM-SSLSM by +5.37% OA and SpectralDINO by +3.63% OA. Under cross-scene temporal transfer (Houston 2013→2018, 5-shot adaptation), MorphJEPA edges GGCL by +1.20% OA (67.21 vs. 66.01) and outperforms SC-EADNet by +3.89% OA. On HyRank (Dioni→Loukia, 5-shot adaptation), SC-EADNet marginally leads on OA (61.43 vs. 60.37), but MorphJEPA dominates AA (71.22 vs. 63.53) and κ (54.11 vs. 52.04), indicating more balanced per-class generalization across the urban-to-rural structural shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

MorphJEPA extends the JEPA paradigm (I-JEPA, Assran et al. 2023) to hyperspectral imagery by replacing generic spatial masking with per-band morphological opening/closing as the predictive target, creating a directional structural bottleneck that suppresses high-frequency nuisances while preserving topology. It also adapts LeJEPA's (Balestriero & LeCun 2025) isotropic Gaussian regularizer (SIGReg) into an asymmetric, context-anchored variant applied only to student embeddings, motivated by the severe spatial-spectral latent correlation in HSI that causes symmetric conditioning to collapse. Conceptually, the paper elevates mathematical morphology from an input-side feature engineering tool (as in Extended Morphological Profiles or morphological CNNs) to a latent-space predictive pretext, shifting morphology from 'what is processed' to 'what must be predicted.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first JEPA-style framework for HSI, filling a clear gap where contrastive and reconstruction-based SSL dominate but suffer from view fragility and dataset-specific overfitting. The morphological pretext offers a principled, parameter-free way to generate structurally simplified targets without heuristic augmentations, and the extremely lightweight encoder (0.18M params, 0.98 ms inference) makes the approach practical for resource-constrained remote sensing deployments. Limitations include the deliberate trade-off of micro-fidelity on ultra-thin linear structures (roads) for macro-semantic stability, reliance on PCA to 15 components, and evaluation on only four scenes. Open questions include whether directional morphological filters or hybrid predictive-reconstructive objectives can recover fine linear detail without sacrificing the transferability gains, and whether the asymmetric SIGReg design generalizes to other high-dimensional, highly correlated sensing modalities.
