## Problem

CLIP features can be inverted by diffusion-based generative models (e.g., IP-Adapter + Stable Diffusion) to reconstruct the original image with high fidelity, posing a serious privacy risk in deployed vision-language systems. Existing privacy defenses are evaluated against discriminative metrics such as attribute-classifier accuracy, which fail to capture this generative leakage where a diffusion attacker recovers visually coherent, identity-revealing images even when re-identification accuracy is low. No prior method directly optimizes a representation against a generative reconstruction attacker while preserving downstream task utility.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (224×224 for classification feature extraction, 336×336 for VLM pipelines) and a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder produces a sequence of feature tokens z ∈ R^{T×D}. The lightweight projection P_θ transforms these into privacy-preserving tokens z̃, which are then consumed by a downstream task head: a linear probe yielding class labels (SUN397) or a LoRA-adapted LLaVA-SP VLM yielding text responses. At deployment, only P_θ is active as a drop-in layer; the generative attacker is discarded, adding negligible runtime overhead to the frozen encoder.

## Method

TrustCLIP inserts a token-wise residual MLP, P_θ(z) = z + f_θ(z), between a frozen CLIP encoder and any downstream consumer, with f_θ's final layer zero-initialized so the projection starts as the identity mapping. During training, P_θ and the task head are jointly optimized under a combined objective: a standard task loss (cross-entropy or language-modeling) plus a negative reconstruction loss computed by a frozen IP-Adapter + Stable Diffusion attacker that attempts to invert the projected features back into an image. The negative sign on the reconstruction term (weighted by λ_rec) forces the projection to selectively suppress instance-specific detail (textures, facial geometry, skin tones) that enables faithful inversion while the task loss protects the high-level semantic subspace needed for classification or VLM reasoning. The key insight is that reconstruction and task utility draw on partially non-overlapping information within CLIP tokens, making selective suppression feasible without proportional utility loss. For VLMs, a warm-up schedule gradually increases λ_rec after the LoRA task head adapts to the feature space, and evaluation includes an adaptive attacker retrained on the protected feature distribution to test worst-case robustness.

## Results

On SUN397 classification, the best TrustCLIP configuration (L2+LPIPS, λ_rec=0.25) achieves 82.9% Top-1 accuracy (within 0.5% of the 83.4% unprotected CLIP baseline) while improving DSIM by 2.5× (0.514 vs. 0.215) and reducing PSNR from 13.58 to 10.69. Against Gaussian noise baselines, TrustCLIP reaches DSIM 0.88 at 85.1% Top-1, whereas the strongest noise level (σ=1.0) achieves only DSIM 0.68 at 17.4% accuracy. On VLM benchmarks, TrustLLaVA maintains competitive performance (POPE 86.1 vs. 86.6 for LLaVA-SP; MM-Vet 34.5 vs. 33.8) while improving DSIM from 0.32 to 0.39 under an adaptive IP-Adapter attacker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unprotected CLIP baseline, TrustCLIP preserves Top-1 accuracy within 0.5% (82.9% vs. 83.4%) while degrading reconstruction fidelity by 2.5× in DSIM and reducing PSNR by ~22%, confirming that selective adversarial suppression outperforms indiscriminate perturbation. Compared to Gaussian noise at matched ℓ₂ displacement, TrustCLIP achieves DSIM 0.80 at 82.9% Top-1 versus the noise control's DSIM 0.64 at 12.5% Top-1, demonstrating that *where* information is removed matters more than *how much*. In the VLM setting, TrustLLaVA trades modest drops on fine-grained tasks (GQA −4.7, SQA −6.3, TextVQA −3.5 vs. LLaVA-SP) for gains on LLaVA-Bench (+3.6) and MM-Vet (+0.7), while improving DSIM from 0.32 to 0.39 under an adaptive attacker. Against Qwen-VL and LLaVA-1.5, TrustLLaVA is competitive on POPE (86.1 vs. 85.9) and superior on LLaVA-Bench (65.3 vs. 63.4) and MM-Vet (34.5 vs. 30.5), though it lags on VQAv2 and GQA. The per-category analysis (Table 4) confirms the selective-suppression hypothesis: semantic tasks (existence, artwork, action) degrade by only −1.9% on MME, while fine-grained tasks (counting, OCR, localization) drop by −11.1%, validating that the projection targets instance-specific detail rather than task-critical semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrustCLIP carves new ground at the intersection of feature-inversion attacks and adversarial privacy training. Prior defenses (SPAct, NinjaDesc, DP-CLIP) optimize against discriminative attackers—attribute classifiers or mutual-information estimators—and the authors argue these proxy metrics miss generative leakage, where a diffusion model reconstructs visually coherent images despite low re-identification accuracy. The paper repurposes IP-Adapter + Stable Diffusion, normally a generation tool, as an explicit frozen adversary in an adversarial training loop, and introduces a lightweight residual MLP projection whose training objective directly penalizes reconstruction fidelity. Conceptually, it extends the adversarial-disentanglement lineage (Edwards & Storkey, adversarial affine subspace embeddings) from discriminative to generative threat models, applied specifically to language-aligned CLIP tokens rather than task-specific descriptors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As CLIP features become standard intermediaries in healthcare, autonomous driving, and on-device VLMs, the ability to reconstruct faces, home environments, or medical imagery from intercepted embeddings is a tangible regulatory and safety risk (GDPR, HIPAA, EU AI Act). TrustCLIP offers a practical, drop-in mitigation that requires no architectural change and adds negligible overhead, making it deployable in existing pipelines. Key limitations remain: evaluation is confined to a single encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14) and a single attacker family (IP-Adapter + SD v1.5), fine-grained tasks (OCR, counting, localization) degrade noticeably, and the framework has not been tested on video or multi-view settings where temporal consistency introduces additional privacy surfaces. Extending to other backbones (SigLIP, DINOv2), per-token adaptive suppression, and formal privacy guarantees beyond empirical reconstruction metrics are natural next steps.
