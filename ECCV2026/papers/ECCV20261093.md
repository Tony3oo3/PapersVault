## Problem

Existing 3D super-resolution methods for 3D Gaussian Splatting assets rely on 2D-centric pipelines (render to images, apply 2D SR, re-optimize 3D), which introduce multi-view inconsistencies, require expensive per-scene optimization (minutes to hours), and depend on original multi-view images. No 3D-native, source-free, feed-forward solution exists that can directly enhance a low-quality 3DGS asset in a single forward pass while preserving geometric consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261093/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality 3DGS asset (a set of Gaussian primitives with 3D positions, colors, scales, rotations, and opacities) as its sole input, operating in a strictly source-free manner without access to original multi-view images. It outputs a new, denser 3DGS asset where each input primitive spawns K detail-enhanced primitives (K=20 by default) constrained to the local neighborhood of the anchor, rendered at 1024×1024 resolution. The entire transformation completes in approximately 0.01 seconds per scene.

## Method

AnchorSplat encodes the 3DGS asset as an attribute-rich point cloud using a Point Transformer v3 (PTv3) encoder that aggregates local geometric and rendering-attribute context into per-point feature descriptors. The Point Anchor Mechanism resolves the ill-posed mapping problem by constraining each new primitive's position to a tanh-bounded local offset from its anchor, which mathematically zeroes out gradient contributions from distant pixels and eliminates the confounding signals that cause unconstrained 3D prediction to collapse into blurry, chaotic outputs. The Equivalent Densification Mechanism replaces 3DGS's iterative cloning/splitting with a single-pass 1-to-K multiplicative generation driven by the per-point feature, while pruning is substituted by learning vanishing opacity for render-equivalent, fully differentiable removal. The network is trained end-to-end via differentiable rendering against HR ground-truth images using a combined L1, SSIM, and VGG perceptual loss, achieving a 10^5× throughput improvement over optimization-based baselines.

## Results

On the newly introduced 3DGS-SR benchmark (1024×1024), AnchorSplat achieves PSNR 36.57, SSIM 0.943, and LPIPS 0.058, outperforming all compared 3DSR methods on every metric. The method processes each asset in approximately 0.01 seconds via a single feed-forward pass, compared to 4–41 minutes for optimization-based baselines, yielding a ~10⁵× throughput improvement. On the zero-shot NeRF-Synthetic generalization test, AnchorSplat reaches PSNR 28.97, the highest among all source-free methods, while remaining orders of magnitude faster than non-source-free alternatives like Sequence Matters (PSNR 31.41, ~40 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnchorSplat wins on all three quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) against every baseline on the 3DGS-SR benchmark. Against the closest non-source-free competitor, Sequence Matters (PSNR 35.69), AnchorSplat gains +0.88 dB PSNR and −0.016 LPIPS while being ~3400× faster. Against the only other source-free method, SuperGaussian (PSNR 34.94), the improvement is +1.63 dB PSNR and −0.039 LPIPS with a ~4100× speedup. Compared to the raw 3DGS input baseline (PSNR 31.03), AnchorSplat delivers a +5.54 dB PSNR gain. On NeRF-Synthetic, AnchorSplat (PSNR 28.97) trails Sequence Matters (31.41) and SRGS (30.83) in absolute PSNR, but those methods require original multi-view images and 18–40 min of per-scene optimization; among strictly source-free methods, AnchorSplat is the best by a clear margin over SuperGaussian (28.44). The efficiency gap is the most striking: 0.01 s per asset versus 4–41 min for all other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the underlying representation and Point Transformer v3 (Wu et al., 2024) as the geometric encoder, but departs fundamentally from the dominant 3D-2D-3D pipeline used by prior 3DSR methods (SRGS, SuperGaussian, Sequence Matters) that render to 2D, apply image super-resolution, and re-optimize. The key conceptual departure is reframing 3DSR as a 3D-native, feed-forward point-cloud-to-point-cloud mapping problem, where the Point Anchor Mechanism resolves the ill-posedness of unconstrained per-point generation and the Equivalent Densification Mechanism replaces 3DGS's iterative cloning/splitting with a single-pass multiplicative generation. The paper also positions itself against 3DGS manipulation works (Splattformer, Resplat, 3DSceneEditor) that operate on 3DGS structures but target generation or editing rather than enhancement of existing low-quality assets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10^5× throughput gain and strict source-free operation make this practically significant for 3D asset pipelines where original multi-view images are unavailable (e.g., post-processing outputs of feed-forward 3D generative models like Trellis or LGM). The 3DGS-SR benchmark fills a genuine gap as the first standardized dataset for the asset-enhancement paradigm. However, the quality gains over the best source-free baseline (SuperGaussian) are modest (~1.6 dB PSNR on 3DGS-SR, ~0.5 dB on NeRF-synthetic), and the 10^5× speedup comparison is against per-scene optimization methods that solve a somewhat different problem. Open questions include scalability to full scenes (vs. single objects), handling of dynamic content, and whether the local-offset constraint generalizes to much larger quality gaps between input and target.
