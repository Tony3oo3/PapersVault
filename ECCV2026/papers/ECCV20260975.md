## Problem

Multi-modal 3D detection frameworks rely on heavy 2D-pretrained visual backbones (30M–78M parameters, 50–86% of total model size) whose semantic features are structurally misaligned with the geometry-aware BEV representations required for 3D localization. Simply pretraining standard lightweight backbones on depth completion fails because conventional convolutions cannot handle the extreme sparsity (>98% empty pixels) of LiDAR projections, causing valid geometric cues to be diluted by zero-value contamination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260975/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DeGuNet consumes six synchronized RGB camera images (resized to 256×704) and their corresponding sparse LiDAR depth projections as parallel inputs at three progressive resolutions (1/2, 1/4, 1/8). The backbone outputs are decoupled via a LiteNeck into categorical Depth Logits for Lift-Splat-Shoot projection and Context Features for downstream fusion, ultimately producing 3D bounding boxes after BEV-space fusion with LiDAR features. The entire image-side backbone is only 0.31M parameters, compared to 29M–78M in standard baselines.

## Method

DeGuNet is a 0.31M-parameter plug-and-play image backbone trained in two phases: first on LiDAR-guided depth completion (with a lightweight decoder that is discarded after pretraining), then as a drop-in replacement in downstream detectors. The architecture combines three sparsity-aware mechanisms: MPIR blocks that apply mask-guided partial convolutions on the LiDAR branch to restrict aggregation to valid pixels, MMViT blocks that mask invalid tokens with large negative values before softmax to confine global attention to geometric nodes, and progressive Guide modules that inject dense RGB semantics into the sparse stream at early stages while bounding the fused output by the geometric mask. The binary validity mask is recursively propagated through downsampling via max-pooling, ensuring strict alignment between geometric validity and feature resolution at every scale.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, DeGuNet (0.31M core backbone parameters) integrated into IS-Fusion achieves 71.30 mAP / 73.0 NDS, and into EA-LSS achieves 71.10 mAP / 72.9 NDS, delivering up to a 6.20 absolute mAP gain over the respective LiDAR-only baselines. Compared to the original BEVFusion'23 backbone (Swin-T, 31.8M image params), DeGuNet reduces total model parameters from 40.8M to 11.1M while improving mAP from 68.50 to 69.40. In terms of system efficiency, DeGuNet achieves 5.1 FPS (1.16× speedup over BEVFusion'23's 4.4 FPS) and reduces GPU memory from 20.46 GB to 6.86 GB (66.5% reduction). The parameter efficiency score (mAP gain per million image params) reaches 20.00 on IS-Fusion, compared to 0.20 for the original IS-Fusion with Swin-T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard backbones pretrained on depth completion (Table 1), DeGuNet (0.31M params) achieves 69.40 mAP and 0.74 m depth RMSE, substantially outperforming ResNet (0.32M, 62.11 mAP), MobileViT-XS (2.36M, 63.27 mAP), MobileViT-XXS (1.33M, 62.09 mAP), and even Swin-T (31.82M, 64.01 mAP) — a 5.39 mAP margin over the largest competitor with 100× fewer parameters. Against the auxiliary depth paradigm (BEVDepth), DeGuNet consistently wins: BEVFusion+BEVDepth reaches only 64.8 mAP at 1.1 FPS, while BEVFusion+DeGuNet achieves 70.3 mAP at 4.8 FPS; GraphBEV+BEVDepth scores 68.7 mAP vs. GraphBEV+DeGuNet's 70.4 mAP. When plugged into the five main multi-modal frameworks (Table 3), DeGuNet improves mAP over each framework's original backbone in every case: +0.70 on BEVFusion'22 (69.60→70.30), +0.90 on BEVFusion'23 (68.50→69.40), +0.20 on EA-LSS (70.90→71.10), +0.30 on GraphBEV (70.10→70.40), and +0.30 on IS-Fusion (71.00→71.30), while reducing total parameters by 29–75M. On computational efficiency (Table 4), DeGuNet at 5.1 FPS and 6.86 GB memory outperforms all listed baselines, including BEVFusion'23 (4.4 FPS, 20.46 GB), TransFusion-LC (4.0 FPS, 22.17 GB), and UVTR (2.9 FPS, 23.36 GB), achieving a 1.16× speedup and 66.5% memory reduction over the closest BEVFusion'23 baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeGuNet builds directly on partial convolutions (Liu et al., ECCV 2018) for mask-guided local feature extraction in its MPIR blocks, and on mask-aware attention mechanisms (Fan et al., ICCV 2022) for its MMViT global context module. It departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-modal 3D detection of using large 2D-pretrained backbones (Swin, ResNet, MobileViT) by instead proposing a purpose-built 0.31M-parameter backbone pretrained on LiDAR-guided depth completion. The paper also contrasts its standalone pretraining approach with auxiliary depth strategies like BEVDepth, arguing that shifting geometric alignment to a dedicated pretraining stage with a sparsity-aware architecture is more effective than appending depth losses during detector training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DeGuNet eliminates 50–86% of image-branch parameter redundancy in established frameworks, reducing GPU memory by up to 66.5% while maintaining or improving detection accuracy, making multi-modal 3D detection substantially more deployable on edge hardware. The key open question is whether the minimalist 0.31M design can capture fine-grained semantics for rare long-tail categories, a limitation the authors acknowledge. Additionally, the approach is currently validated only within LSS-based (Lift-Splat-Shoot) pipelines on nuScenes; generalization to query-based or sparse-fusion architectures and to other sensor configurations remains unexplored.
