## Problem

Modern video generative models produce visually realistic but physically implausible rigid body motion, as they discard object rigidity during pixel-level denoising. Existing remedies—physics-rich data fine-tuning, simulator-based conditioning, and RL with VLM/human ratings—either fail to encode physical laws or lack the precision to verify motion correctness. The paper asks whether physically verifiable, quantitative rewards can provide the precise optimization signal needed for a video model to truly internalize rigid body dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model operates in a Video-to-Video (V2V) setting: it receives T_obs > 1 context frames (5 frames in practice) plus a text prompt, and generates T_pred subsequent frames. The multi-frame context is essential because it supplies sufficient physical initial conditions (position and velocity) to make the future trajectory deterministic and quantitatively verifiable; a single-frame I2V setting is explicitly ruled out as underdetermined. Object center coordinates are annotated in the first frame, and SAM2 is prompted with those coordinates to extract per-frame motion masks from both ground-truth and generated videos for trajectory computation.

## Method

PhysRVG introduces a physics-grounded reward that combines Trajectory Offset (mean frame-wise L2 deviation between generated and ground-truth object center coordinates) with Collision Detection (temporal upweighting of frames where sudden acceleration changes indicate impact, derived from Newton's second law), yielding a weighted trajectory offset O_c used as a negative reward. To stabilize RL training on out-of-distribution samples where the model cannot yet produce reasonable rollouts, the authors propose the Mimicry-Discovery Cycle (MDcycle): a unified post-training objective that dynamically adds a Flow Matching supervision term when the group-average trajectory offset exceeds a threshold (mimicry branch) and relies purely on GRPO otherwise (discovery branch), enabling a smooth transition from data-driven imitation to physics-consistent exploration. The full pipeline is staged—first converting a pretrained diffusion transformer from I2V to V2V via full-parameter fine-tuning, then applying MDcycle with LoRA and the physics-grounded reward—allowing the model to progressively internalize rigid body motion rules across collision, pendulum, free-fall, and rolling scenarios.

## Results

On PhysRVGBench, PhysRVG achieves an IoU of 0.64 and a Trajectory Offset of 15.03, dramatically outperforming the next-best model CosmosPredict2 (IoU 0.33, TO 79.67) by roughly 2× on IoU and 5× on TO. On VideoPhy-2, PhysRVG scores 0.76 (SA) and 0.44 (PC), leading all baselines. On VBench, PhysRVG attains a total score of 78.89, marginally ahead of Kling2.5 (78.77), indicating that the physical-reward training does not sacrifice visual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysRVG wins on every metric across all three benchmarks. The most striking gap is on PhysRVGBench: the TO of 15.03 is 5.3× lower than CosmosPredict2 (79.67), the strongest baseline on that metric, and 10.8× lower than Wan2.2 (162.40); IoU of 0.64 nearly doubles CosmosPredict2's 0.33. On VideoPhy-2, PhysRVG leads SA by 0.06 over Kling2.5 and CosmosPredict2 (both 0.70) and PC by 0.03 over Kling2.5 (0.41). On VBench total score, PhysRVG edges out Kling2.5 by only 0.12 points (78.89 vs. 78.77), showing that the physics-focused RL training preserves visual quality. Against the physics-aware methods (PhysGen, PhysGen3D, NewtonGen) in Fig. 6, PhysRVG generalizes to their scenarios by generating subsequent frames from their first five context frames, though a strictly controlled head-to-head is not possible due to differing problem formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow-matching video generation (building on Wan2.2-style diffusion transformers) and RL-based post-training for generative models (extending Flow-GRPO/DanceGRPO from image to video). It departs from prior physics-aware video generation work in a key way: rather than conditioning on simulator-derived motion priors (PhysGen, NewtonGen, GPT4Motion), augmenting training data (Wisa, Pisa), or relying on subjective MLLM/human ratings (PhyT2V, PhysMaster), it introduces physically verifiable rewards—Trajectory Offset and Collision Detection computed via SAM2 segmentation—that provide precise, quantitative optimization signals. The Mimicry-Discovery Cycle is a novel training strategy that addresses a practical failure mode of pure GRPO in video generation (instability on out-of-distribution samples) by dynamically switching between supervised flow-matching loss and RL objective based on group-level rollout quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that current SOTA video generators (Wan2.2, Kling, HunyuanVideo, Cosmos) achieve strong visual fidelity scores yet fail dramatically on physical plausibility metrics (TO of 79–182 vs. 15 for PhysRVG), highlighting a significant gap between perceptual quality and physical correctness. The physically verifiable reward paradigm and MDcycle are potentially transferable to other domains where ground-truth-quantifiable objectives exist beyond rigid body motion. Key limitations include the narrow scope (four rigid-body motion types, V2V setting requiring ≥2 context frames, ~50-video test set) and the dependence on SAM2 segmentation quality for reward computation; extending to soft-body, fluid, or multi-object deformable dynamics, and to the I2V setting where initial conditions are underdetermined, remain open.
