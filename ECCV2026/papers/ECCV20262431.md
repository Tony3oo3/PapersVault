## Problem

Existing unlearning methods for text-to-image diffusion models are designed for single-step deletion and collapse after only 3–5 sequential applications. The paper identifies two compounding causes: coarse fixed-anchor mapping that forces unnecessarily large score-prediction displacements at each step, and the absence of local protection for semantically neighboring concepts whose shared internal representations make them the first to suffer collateral damage (the ripple effect).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262431/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 checkpoint, a sequence of forget concepts (10 in experiments, each instantiated by 100 diverse scene-context prompts), and a pool of retain concepts. At each continual step, an LLM generates 10 context-preserving mapping candidates per forget prompt and a candidate pool of related concepts; the frozen previous-step model then scores all candidates via score-prediction distance to select the optimal mapping target and the top-10 nearest replay neighbors. The output is the updated student diffusion model, re-initialized from the frozen teacher at each step, trained for 250–350 optimizer steps (~60 min on 3×H100).

## Method

LACU unifies two locality-aware mechanisms under a single model-aware metric—score-prediction distance, which measures how differently the frozen teacher denoises the same noisy latent under two text conditions. Locality-Aware Target Selection picks, per forget prompt, the context-preserving candidate whose noise predictions are closest to the forget prompt's, minimizing the score-field perturbation per update. Locality-Aware Replay ranks retain concepts by the same distance to the forget concept and replays only the nearest neighbors as a local functional regularizer, directly shielding the most vulnerable semantic neighborhood. The total loss combines the unlearning distillation term (student matches teacher under the selected mapping target), the local replay distillation term (student matches teacher under replay prompts), and a lightweight L2 parameter-regularization penalty to constrain cumulative drift across steps.

## Results

After 10 sequential unlearning steps on Stable Diffusion v1.5, LACU achieves U_acc=0.90, U_clip=29.1, RR_acc=0.72, and GR_acc=0.87, substantially outperforming all baselines on retention metrics while maintaining strong unlearning efficacy. The closest retention-preserving baselines (DUGE: RR_acc=0.62, GR_acc=0.73; Grad Proj: RR_acc=0.61, GR_acc=0.71) sacrifice unlearning accuracy (U_acc=0.74 and 0.83 respectively), while aggressive methods (ANT, ESD, EraseFlow) achieve U_acc≥0.93 but collapse to near-zero retention (RR_acc, GR_acc ≤ 0.15). LACU's advantage is most pronounced at 10 steps, where it leads the best retention baseline (DUGE) by 10 points on RR_acc and 14 points on GR_acc.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LACU dominates on retention metrics across all checkpoints: at 10 steps it achieves RR_acc=0.72 and GR_acc=0.87, exceeding the best retention-preserving baselines DUGE (0.62/0.73) and Grad Proj (0.61/0.71) by 10–14 points, while also surpassing SMem (0.58/0.67) and CA (0.34/0.34). On unlearning accuracy, LACU (U_acc=0.90) trails the most aggressive methods—ANT (1.00), AGE (0.99), ESD-x (0.96)—but those methods suffer catastrophic retention collapse (RR_acc ≤ 0.15, GR_acc ≤ 0.23), making them operationally unusable after 10 steps. LACU's U_acc of 0.90 exceeds DUGE (0.74), SMem (0.73), and Grad Proj (0.83) by 7–16 points, showing it unlearns more effectively than any method that also preserves retention. The only metric where LACU is slightly behind is U_clip at 10 steps (29.1 vs. DUGE's 30.7), a 1.6-point gap in in-prompt retainability. Overall, LACU is the only method that simultaneously maintains U_acc > 0.85 and both RR_acc and GR_acc > 0.70 after 10 sequential unlearning steps, confirming that locality-aware target selection and replay are essential for stable continual unlearning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes standard continual-learning ingredients—teacher-student distillation, generative replay, and parameter regularization—under a new unifying selection criterion: score-prediction distance computed in the diffusion model's own noise-prediction space. It departs from one-shot unlearning methods (ESD, Concept Ablation, UCE, MACE) that redirect all forget prompts to a single fixed anchor, and from adaptive-target methods like AGE that still select targets via CLIP text-space similarity rather than the model's internal denoising dynamics. The key conceptual departure is treating target selection and replay-neighbor identification as two applications of the same model-aware locality metric, rather than independent design choices, which prior continual-unlearning backbones (DUGE, SMem, Grad Proj) do not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Continual unlearning is the operational reality for deployed text-to-image models facing ongoing privacy, copyright, and safety obligations, yet the paper demonstrates that nearly all existing methods collapse after only 3–5 sequential deletions—a practical failure mode that blocks real-world deployment. The locality-aware principle (score-prediction distance) offers a generalizable design insight applicable beyond unlearning to any sequential model-editing task where collateral damage concentrates in a local neighborhood. Key open questions include scaling beyond 10 steps to the hundreds or thousands of deletions a production model may face, handling style concepts whose visual features overlap broadly with retained neighbors, and reducing the memory overhead of maintaining a frozen teacher alongside the trainable student.
