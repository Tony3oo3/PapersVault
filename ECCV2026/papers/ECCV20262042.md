## Problem

Current dexterous grasp planners optimize purely for physical stability and force closure, producing functionally uniform enveloping power grasps that fail to satisfy task-specific contact requirements. Conditioning synthesis on human grasp taxonomies to address this typically demands prohibitively expensive, object-annotated datasets that tightly couple topology labels to specific object geometries, severely limiting generalization to unseen objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a target object point cloud, a requested contact topology label (one of 21 from the Gonzalez taxonomy), and a candidate 6D gripper pose (R, t) supplied externally by a task planner or operator. The object is inverse-transformed into the canonical gripper frame with inverted surface normals, and the network outputs a probabilistic contact mask over a fixed set of workspace basis points. This mask is then filtered through a cascading validation pipeline (label-consistency check, force-closure test) before energy-based optimization yields the final joint configuration Q*. Notably, the 6D pose is treated as an external prior rather than inferred end-to-end, and training uses only gripper point clouds with zero object meshes.

## Method

CoToGrasp inverts the traditional object-centric contact formulation into a gripper-centric one, learning contact templates on a fixed, bounded gripper handprint rather than an unbounded object surface. A modified DGCNN extracts local geometric features from the input (gripper during training, object during inference), which are projected onto a fixed set of canonical workspace basis points via kNN aggregation gated by an aligned distance metric that encodes surface-normal compatibility. A Transformer encoder processes these workspace tokens with the topology embedding concatenated at every layer, followed by a Set Transformer that compresses the set into a global latent descriptor conditioning a CVAE to generate probabilistic contact masks. At inference, a strict cascading validation pipeline rejects topologically inconsistent or force-closure-violating predictions (resampling the latent variable up to 20 times), and a final energy-based optimization aligns the gripper's active surfaces with predicted contact zones while enforcing kinematic limits, self-collision avoidance, and a repulsive term that keeps unused fingers clear of the object.

## Results

On the DexGraspNet test set, CoToGrasp achieves 27.56% average success rate (SR) and 17.18% topology compliance (TC), outperforming Dexonomy (23.80% SR, 14.28% TC) across all functional categories, with the largest gap on precision grasps (22.71% vs. 12.36% SR). On the MultiDex subset, CoToGrasp attains the highest semantic entropy (H_TC = 0.83) and fastest generation speed (0.11 s/grasp) among all compared planners, though its raw SR (36.94%) is lower than unconditioned baselines that default to enveloping power grasps. Per-topology analysis reveals a 3× advantage on constrained precision grasp M2 (30.3% vs. 10.5% SR for Dexonomy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four taxonomy-unaware planners on MultiDex (Table 1), CoToGrasp consistently wins on semantic entropy H_TC (0.83 vs. 0.74 for DFC, 0.60 for GenDexGrasp, 0.65 for DRO-Grasp, 0.65 for GOAG) and generation speed (0.11 s vs. >1800 s, 14.65 s, 1.72 s, 0.20 s respectively), but sacrifices raw success rate (36.94% vs. 72–78%) because it is forced to synthesize diverse topologies rather than defaulting to geometrically stable power grasps. Against the taxonomy-aware baseline Dexonomy on DexGraspNet (Table 2), CoToGrasp wins on every reported metric: SR (27.56% vs. 23.80%), TC (17.18% vs. 14.28%), H_SR (0.96 vs. 0.91), and H_TC (0.84 vs. 0.77), with the most pronounced advantage on precision grasps (22.71% vs. 12.36% SR). The paper attributes Dexonomy's weakness to its rigid coupling of taxonomy types to explicit joint configurations, which causes mode collapse on challenging geometries (e.g., inflated M11/M21 scores from unverified enveloping defaults). Ablations confirm that removing the Label-Consistency check drops TC from 17.18% to 14.45%, and removing Force-Closure validation drops it to 16.26%, validating the cascading pipeline's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoToGrasp builds directly on the object-agnostic training paradigm of GOAG [28], which demonstrated that learning contact capabilities in a gripper-centric canonical space yields superior zero-shot generalization. It departs from prior taxonomy-conditioned planners (Dexonomy [4], OmniDexVLG [45]) that rigidly couple grasp types to specific object geometries during dataset construction, instead adopting the hand-centric Gonzalez taxonomy [9] and training exclusively on gripper point clouds. Architecturally, it composes DGCNN feature extraction, a Transformer encoder over workspace tokens, a Set Transformer for global pooling, and a CVAE for stochastic contact mask generation, culminating in an energy-based joint optimization with a novel repulsive term for topology compliance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in dexterous manipulation: current planners produce physically stable but functionally uniform grasps (mode collapse toward power grasps), making task-aligned precision grasps extremely inefficient to retrieve. By decoupling semantic intent from object identity, CoToGrasp eliminates the need for costly object-annotated datasets while maintaining zero-shot generalization, a property critical for deploying grasp planners on novel objects in the wild. Key limitations include the substantial SR trade-off (36.94% vs. 72–78% for unconditioned baselines) inherent in enforcing diverse topologies, the restriction to static grasps in real-world validation, and the dependence on externally provided 6D poses. Open questions include whether this framework extends to dynamic in-hand manipulation, integration with LLM-based task planners for end-to-end semantic-to-contact pipelines, and scalability to higher-DoF hands beyond the Shadow/Allegro configurations tested.
