## Problem

Diffusion model denoising trajectories can stall at visually plausible but semantically misaligned outputs, which the paper interprets as local optima in a surrogate quality landscape. Existing inference-time scaling methods (e.g., SOP, Resampling, Z-Sampling) apply fixed-strength or fixed-direction re-noising at every step or preset intervals, providing limited capacity to escape broad quality plateaus and wasting compute on unproductive shallow perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260638/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pretrained diffusion model (SD-2.1-base or Hunyuan-DiT) as input, and produces a single generated image as output. It operates within a standard DDIM 50-step sampling loop with CFG (scale 5.5), using a reward model (default: ImageReward) to score the Tweedie clean-sample estimate x̂₀ at each step. Exploration is restricted to the first λ steps of the high-noise regime (t ∈ {T, …, T−λ+1}), where perturbations can still meaningfully alter global image structure; the compute cost is measured in average NFEs per denoising step.

## Method

Ctrl-Z Sampling is a reward-guided, model-agnostic diffusion sampler that alternates forward conditional denoising with backward latent inversion in a zigzag pattern. At each step in the exploration window, it computes the clean-sample estimate x̂₀ and scores it with a reward model; if the score fails to exceed the previously accepted score by a threshold δ, a plateau is detected. Upon detection, the method applies a controlled inversion operator Ψ to re-noise the current latent to a noisier timestep t+Δ, then generates N alternative forward-denoising continuations and selects the highest-scoring candidate. If no candidate improves the score, the inversion depth Δ is incrementally increased (up to d_max), enabling progressively deeper exploration to escape broader plateaus. This adaptive depth escalation—making fewer but deeper steps rather than many shallow ones—is the key distinction from fixed-depth baselines like SOP, and it is triggered only upon detected stagnation rather than at every step.

## Results

On Pick-a-Pic with SD-2.1, Ctrl-Z‡ (7.72× NFEs) achieves HPSv2 27.34, PickScore 21.02, and ImageReward 1.138, outperforming SOP-4 (9.00× NFEs) on HPSv2 (27.23) and ImageReward (1.113) while using ~15% fewer function evaluations. On DrawBench, Ctrl-Z‡ reaches HPSv2 26.73 and ImageReward 1.025 versus SOP-4's 26.72 and 1.008. The low-budget variant Ctrl-Z† (~2.8× NFEs) already surpasses SOP-1 (3.0× NFEs) on HPSv2 and ImageReward for both SD-2.1 and Hunyuan-DiT, demonstrating a favorable compute-quality trade-off across U-Net and Transformer backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDIM (1× NFEs), Ctrl-Z improves all four metrics on both benchmarks and both backbones, with the largest gains on ImageReward (e.g., 0.194→1.138 on SD-2.1 Pick-a-Pic). Versus Resampling (2× NFEs) and Z-Sampling (3× NFEs), Ctrl-Z† at a similar ~2.8× budget matches or exceeds them on HPSv2 and ImageReward while using fewer NFEs. The most direct comparison is against SOP: at the low budget, Ctrl-Z† (2.77× NFEs) beats SOP-1 (3.00×) on HPSv2 (26.44 vs 26.34) and ImageReward (0.720 vs 0.735 is a near-tie) for SD-2.1 Pick-a-Pic, and on DrawBench HPSv2 (26.15 vs 25.78) and ImageReward (0.650 vs 0.637). At the higher budget, Ctrl-Z‡ (7.72× NFEs) outperforms SOP-4 (9.00×) on HPSv2 (27.34 vs 27.23) and ImageReward (1.138 vs 1.113) for SD-2.1 Pick-a-Pic, and on HPSv2 (29.79 vs 29.49) and ImageReward (1.319 vs 1.246) for Hunyuan-DiT DrawBench, while trailing SOP-4 by a small margin on PickScore (21.02 vs 21.12) and AES on Hunyuan-DiT (6.325 vs 6.303). Overall, Ctrl-Z achieves comparable or superior quality with 10–15% fewer NFEs than SOP at matched budgets, and its adaptive depth escalation is the key differentiator enabling escape from broader plateaus that fixed-depth SOP cannot resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ctrl-Z Sampling builds directly on the DDIM deterministic sampling framework and the latent inversion operator introduced in Repaint/Resampling, which established the mechanism for re-noising intermediate diffusion states. Conceptually, it extends the zigzag trajectory idea from Z-Sampling (Bai et al., 2024) and the reward-guided candidate search from SOP (Ma et al., 2025), but departs from both by making exploration both reward-triggered (rather than always-on or at fixed intervals) and depth-adaptive (escalating inversion strength when shallow candidates fail). The paper frames the entire process as hill-climbing in a surrogate quality landscape, positioning prior fixed-depth methods as greedy local searches that stall on broad plateaus, and its core contribution is the adaptive depth escalation mechanism that enables escape from such plateaus without incurring the full cost of DFS-style re-denoising from very early timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ctrl-Z Sampling offers a practical, model-agnostic test-time scaling strategy for single-device inference where large candidate pools or extreme NFE budgets are infeasible, providing a controllable compute-quality trade-off through its exploration depth and width parameters. The finding that deeper-but-narrower exploration often outperforms wider-but-shallower settings at comparable NFEs has practical implications for how practitioners allocate inference compute. Limitations include validation only on text-to-image with two backbones (SD-2.1, Hy-DiT), dependence on a specific reward model for triggering and selection, and the restriction of exploration to the high-noise regime (first λ steps), which may not generalize to tasks where late-stage corrections matter. Open questions include extension to pixel-space models, multi-reward combination, and whether the adaptive depth schedule can be made reward-model-agnostic.
