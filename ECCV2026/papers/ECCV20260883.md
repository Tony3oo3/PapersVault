## Problem

Recovering a temporally coherent, native HDR environment map from a single in-the-wild video remains unsolved: classical methods assume static lighting or known geometry, multi-view approaches require external captures, and recent generative methods treat video models as LDR-to-HDR translators, producing exposure-fusion artifacts and temporally unstable reflections. No prior work exploits the implicit lighting priors already encoded in modern video diffusion models to directly estimate dynamic scene illumination.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild LDR video at 480×832 resolution, masks a central region with a constant grey value, and splits the result into two conditioning latents (environment region and scene content) alongside a fixed text prompt describing a mirrored chrome sphere. It outputs a temporally coherent sequence of HDR environment maps at 256×512 equirectangular resolution in linear Rec. 2020 color space, plus the inpainted video containing the rendered chrome ball. The environment maps can be directly consumed by renderers for virtual object insertion with physically consistent lighting and shadows.

## Method

V-LITE reframes dynamic lighting estimation as a video inpainting task: a synthetic chrome ball is inserted into the masked central region, compelling the video diffusion model to generate physically plausible reflections from surrounding spatio-temporal context. To bridge the LDR-trained backbone (Wan 2.1-1.3B) to the HDR domain, the authors design an HDR-aware VAE with learnable tonemap adapters (a 3D convolution plus scale/bias) that map linear HDR into a log-domain representation for the frozen LDR encoder, with a symmetric inverse adapter for reconstruction; these are trained in two stages (adapters first, then jointly with the decoder). The diffusion Transformer is then fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 32 on attention layers) to align its generative priors with the HDR latent distribution, using a flow-matching velocity objective with a modulation mask (α=5) that emphasizes the central inpainting region. Training uses a mixed dataset (V-LITESet) combining 8K+ tonemapped HDR video pairs for dynamic spatio-temporal context with 800 real PolyHaven HDR images as absolute photometric anchors at a 10:1 ratio, ensuring the model learns both temporal consistency and accurate luminance distributions.

## Results

On the Editable Indoor benchmark, V-LITE achieves the best SI-MSE (0.03) and Lighting Stability (LS = 0.03), outperforming DiffusionLight (0.05 / 0.03), DiffusionLightTurbo (0.05 / 0.04), and StyleLight (0.07 / 0.05). On EnvMapNet, V-LITE reports AED of 41.63° and Angle Stability of 16.30°, competitive with DiffusionLightTurbo (37.74° / 16.68°). Most notably, V-LITE completes inference in 80 seconds per video, making it ~1,822× faster than DiffusionLight (145,800 s), ~9× faster than DiffusionLightTurbo (713 s), and ~25× faster than StyleLight (2,002 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-LITE clearly outperforms all three baselines on reconstruction fidelity (MSE and SI-MSE) and temporal stability (LS), achieving SI-MSE of 0.03 versus 0.05 for DiffusionLight and DiffusionLightTurbo, and 0.07 for StyleLight. On angular error metrics, V-LITE is competitive but not dominant: DiffusionLight achieves a slightly lower AER (4.58° vs. 4.77°) and DiffusionLightTurbo achieves a lower AED (37.74° vs. 41.63°), suggesting these methods capture dominant light direction marginally better. However, V-LITE's decisive advantage is efficiency: at 80 seconds per video it is roughly 1,800× faster than DiffusionLight, 9× faster than DiffusionLightTurbo, and 25× faster than StyleLight, while matching or exceeding their quality on most metrics. The baselines (DiffusionLight, DiffusionLightTurbo, StyleLight) are all single-image methods applied per-frame, which the paper shows leads to visible temporal flickering in qualitative comparisons, whereas V-LITE's video-native formulation produces temporally coherent environment maps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VACE video inpainting framework and the Wan 2.1 diffusion Transformer backbone, extending them into the HDR domain via a novel tonemap-adapter VAE and LoRA-based fine-tuning. It departs conceptually from prior video lighting estimation work (e.g., LuxDiT) that treats the generative model as a pixel-level translator from LDR to HDR; instead, V-LITE reframes the task as chrome-ball inpainting, compelling the model to invoke its implicit physical priors for illumination. The chrome-ball light-probe idea itself traces to DiffusionLight and VFX industry practice, but V-LITE is the first to apply it in a temporally coherent video setting with native HDR output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

V-LITE demonstrates that modern video diffusion models encode sufficient physical lighting knowledge to produce temporally coherent HDR environment maps from a single in-the-wild video in ~80 seconds, a ~1800× speedup over prior diffusion-based methods. The introduced V-LITESet (648K frames mixing tonemapped HDR video and real HDR images) provides a much-needed benchmark for dynamic HDR lighting estimation. Key open questions remain: the LDR-to-HDR inversion is inherently ill-posed, the approach is sensitive to out-of-distribution illumination, and the underlying video backbone's limited generation duration constrains long-sequence stability. Whether the 'inherent estimator' framing generalizes beyond chrome-ball probes to other physical quantities (e.g., material BRDFs) is an interesting direction.
