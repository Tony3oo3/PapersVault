## Problem

Composing frozen foundation models (e.g., DINOv2 for semantics + SAM2 for masks) enables training-free few-shot instance segmentation, yet a substantial gap remains between current systems and their empirical upper bounds. The paper identifies that this gap is dominated by the scoring stage, where semantic matching confidence and mask geometric fidelity are treated independently, causing systematic mis-ranking of candidates whose two quality signals disagree (termed Quality Misalignment).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260166/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K reference images with annotated instance masks per class (K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 30}) and a single query image. The semantic encoder (DINOv2 ViT-L/14) processes images at 518×518 resolution to extract 1024-d dense features, while SAM2 Hiera-Large is prompted with a 32×32 uniform point grid to generate candidate masks. Output is a set of instance segmentation masks with class labels and a unified quality score Q that serves simultaneously as the NMS ranking criterion and the reported detection confidence for COCO evaluation.

## Method

SGQA replaces the baseline's decoupled scoring—where the semantic score s^sem (cosine similarity to K-Means prototypes) and geometric score s^geo (SAM2's predicted IoU) are used independently for ranking and confidence—with a single unified quality score Q = √(s^sem · s^geo), the geometric mean of the two signals. This is motivated by a progressive oracle replacement diagnostic showing that the scoring stage accounts for 64% of the performance gap, and by score-space analysis revealing that true positives cluster where both scores are high while false positives exhibit pronounced imbalance along the off-diagonal. The geometric mean is preferred over the arithmetic mean because its non-linear iso-contours penalize score misalignment more aggressively, preventing a single high score from offsetting a low one. The formulation subsumes prior training-free methods as limiting cases (α→1 recovers DE-ViT's semantic-only ranking; α→0 recovers Matcher/PerSAM's IoU-only ranking) and adds no hyperparameters or learnable components to the pipeline.

## Results

On COCO-FSOD 30-shot, SGQA achieves 39.4 nAP (57.3 nAP50, 41.7 nAP75), outperforming the NTT baseline (36.8 nAP) by +2.6 nAP and the best fine-tuning method CD-ViTO (35.9 nAP) by +3.5 nAP. On PASCAL VOC few-shot, SGQA reaches 77.4 average AP50 across three splits and five shot settings, exceeding the NTT baseline (71.2) by +6.2 AP50 and the strongest fine-tuning method LVC (52.3) by +25.1 AP50. The gain is largest in the low-shot regime: at 1-shot, SGQA improves Bbox nAP by +3.3 and Segm nAP by +3.8 over NTT, versus +2.6 and +3.3 at 30-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGQA (DINOv2 ViT-L + SAM2 Hiera-L, no fine-tuning) outperforms the NTT baseline [3] by +2.6 nAP on COCO 30-shot and +6.2 AP50 on PASCAL VOC, confirming that the geometric-mean score fusion is the sole source of improvement. Against the strongest prior training-free method DE-ViT [51], SGQA leads by +5.4 nAP on COCO 30-shot and by +16.0 AP50 on VOC (77.4 vs. 61.4 for DE-ViT ViT-B/14). SGQA also surpasses the best fine-tuning method CD-ViTO [8] by +3.5 nAP on COCO 30-shot despite using no gradient updates, and exceeds LVC [18] (the top fine-tuning method on VOC) by +25.1 AP50. The controlled comparison isolating SGQA's contribution is NTT vs. SGQA (identical backbones), yielding a consistent +2.6 ± 0.1 nAP across five random reference samplings. On PASCAL VOC, SGQA improves over NTT on every split and shot setting (1- to 10-shot), with gains ranging from +4.3 to +7.3 AP50, and the advantage grows as shots decrease, reaching +6.3 AP50 at 1-shot on Split 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NTT (No Time to Train) pipeline [3], which composes frozen DINOv2 and SAM2 for training-free reference-based instance segmentation, and modifies only its scoring stage. Conceptually, it draws on the long-studied classification-localization misalignment in supervised detection (IoU-Net, Centerness, GFL, TOOD), where a geometric quality signal is multiplied with a classification score during NMS; SGQA applies the same product form to a fundamentally different setting where two independently pre-trained models were never jointly optimized, making end-to-end alignment infeasible by construction. The diagnostic methodology extends single-model oracle analysis [14] to multi-model compositions via progressive oracle replacement, a factorial decomposition that isolates each pipeline stage's contribution to the performance gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the scoring stage accounts for 64% of the oracle gap, consistent across eight encoders and four mask generators, provides a clear diagnostic for the growing community of frozen foundation model compositions: the bottleneck is signal alignment, not model capability. However, SGQA recovers only 42% of the score-level-recoverable gap (2.6 of 6.2 AP), and closing the remaining 58% would require feature-level information exchange between encoder and generator, which the paper explicitly leaves to future work. The comparison with fine-tuning methods is confounded by backbone size (524M vs. 25–45M parameters), and the controlled comparison is only NTT vs. SGQA with identical backbones. The technique is simple and hyperparameter-free, making it easy to adopt, but its scope is limited to the scoring stage of a specific pipeline architecture.
