## Problem

Next-scale visual autoregressive (VAR) models achieve strong generative fidelity but remain too large and slow for resource-constrained edge devices. Standard OBS-style structured pruning, designed for LLMs, is poorly matched to VAR's multi-scale architecture: rapid token growth at later scales dominates Hessian accumulation, marginalizing critical coarse scales and amplifying autoregressive error cascading, which degrades global image structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260460/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is class-conditional: it takes a single ImageNet class label as input and produces a 256×256 RGB image via 10 autoregressive decoding steps (one per scale in the VAR hierarchy). For edge deployment, the pruned model is converted to Core ML .mlmodel format and runs on an iPad Pro (M4), where the dense 310M baseline fails to execute on an iPhone 16 Pro Max due to memory constraints, while the 230M pruned model runs at 277 ms per image.

## Method

EVAR introduces two key components tailored to VAR's multi-scale structure. First, Pyramid-Aware OBS reconstructs the layer-wise Hessian as a scale-weighted Gram matrix H_pa = Σ (w_i/n_i)(X^(i))^T X^(i) + λI, where the 1/n_i factor normalizes token-count disparity across scales and the decay weight w_i biases the solver toward preserving pruning-sensitive coarse scales, while the damping term guarantees positive definiteness for closed-form OBS compensation. Structured units (attention heads and FFN channels) are pruned iteratively with unit-wise OBS scoring and compensation. Second, Progressive Scale-Aware Distillation (PSAD) addresses post-pruning fine-tuning by combining a soft-gated coarse-to-fine curriculum (progressively activating scales via a continuous ramp function) with inverse-proportional scale weighting (w_i = C/n_i) that cancels the token-count advantage of fine scales, yielding balanced gradient contributions across the generative hierarchy.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, EVAR compresses VAR-d16 from 310M to 230M parameters (40% pruning) with FID 3.91 (vs. 3.55 dense) and IS 259.1, and compresses VAR-d24 from 1.0B to 748M with FID 2.52 (vs. 2.33 dense). In the direct pruning-method comparison at 20% sparsity (training-free), EVAR achieves FID 7.85, substantially outperforming the best baseline Taylor (FID 27.30) and SlimGPT (FID 56.87). On iPad Pro (M4) via Core ML, the 230M pruned model achieves 277 ms single-image latency with a 1.8× speedup over the dense model (494 ms), while the dense model fails to run on iPhone 16 Pro Max due to memory constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dense VAR baselines, EVAR incurs modest FID degradation: 3.55→3.91 (VAR-d16, 40% pruning) and 2.33→2.52 (VAR-d24, 40% pruning), while reducing parameters by 26–25%. Compared to larger AR models, the 230M EVAR (FID 3.91, 10 steps) is competitive with LlamaGen-XL (775M, FID 2.62, 576 steps) and AiM-XL (763M, FID 2.56, 256 steps) at a fraction of the parameter count and decoding steps, though it trails the much larger CoDe (2.3B, FID 2.26) and FastVAR-d24 (1B, FID 2.64). In the head-to-head pruning comparison (Table 2), EVAR dominates all five structured-pruning baselines across every regime: at 20% sparsity training-free, EVAR achieves FID 7.85 versus Taylor's 27.30 and SlimGPT's 56.87; at 40% sparsity training-free, EVAR reaches FID 58.21 versus Taylor's 145.66; and after 1-epoch fine-tuning at 40% sparsity, EVAR attains FID 4.86 versus Taylor's 8.35 and SlimGPT's 10.62. The gap is most dramatic in the training-free setting, where EVAR's Pyramid-Aware Hessian provides a far better pruning initialization than LLM-oriented calibration strategies used by SlimGPT and LLM-Pruner.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVAR builds on two distinct lineages: the next-scale Visual Autoregressive (VAR) modeling paradigm [44], which replaces sequential token-by-token decoding with coarse-to-fine scale-by-scale generation, and the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) family of second-order structured pruning methods [18], which provide closed-form weight compensation after removing structured units. The key departure is that standard OBS-style pruning (e.g., SlimGPT [30]) was designed for flat LLM token sequences and simply concatenates calibration tokens across all positions; EVAR identifies that this flat accumulation is structurally mismatched to VAR's pyramid of scales, where token counts grow quadratically and early-scale errors cascade autoregressively. The paper introduces Pyramid-Aware Hessian Accumulation (scale-normalized, decay-weighted) and Progressive Scale-Aware Distillation (PSAD) as targeted corrections to this mismatch, positioning itself as the first OBS-based pruning framework explicitly designed for next-scale VAR architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is concrete: EVAR compresses VAR-d16 from 310M to 230M parameters and achieves a 1.8× single-image latency reduction on iPad Pro (494→277 ms) with FID rising only from 3.55 to 3.91, and the dense model simply cannot run on iPhone 16 Pro Max due to memory constraints—making compression a deployability prerequisite, not just an optimization. The broader insight that multi-scale token imbalance fundamentally biases Hessian-based saliency estimates is likely transferable to other hierarchical or pyramid-structured generative models. Open questions include whether the specific inverse-token-count weighting and decay schedule generalize beyond VAR to other coarse-to-fine architectures (e.g., cascaded diffusion, multi-resolution GANs), whether the approach extends to text-conditioned or higher-resolution settings, and whether the Core ML NPU-avoidance finding (CPU+GPU outperforming All-units) persists across model families and iOS versions.
