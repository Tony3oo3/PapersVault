## Problem

Existing training-free diffusion guidance methods (PAG, SEG) apply a fixed, static perturbation to self-attention throughout the entire sampling process, ignoring the stage-wise structure of denoising where early timesteps establish global structure via magnitude modulation and later timesteps refine fine-grained geometry via directional adjustment. This indiscriminate degradation can disrupt the sampling trajectory and prevent the model from adaptively balancing global coherence and local detail.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on pretrained diffusion models (SDXL, SD3, FLUX.1, Wan2.2) without any retraining. For conditional generation it takes a text prompt plus initial Gaussian noise; for unconditional generation it takes only Gaussian noise. The perturbation is injected into the query, key, and value projections of selected self-attention layers (mid layers for U-Net, MMDiT blocks for FLUX), and the final output is a generated image (512×512 or 1024×1024) or video, guided by a scale γ and a global perturbation coefficient α.

## Method

SAND decomposes Gaussian noise ε at each spatial position into a parallel component ε_∥ (aligned with the feature vector, affecting magnitude) and an orthogonal component ε_⊥ (the residual, affecting direction) via standard vector projection. A time-adaptive linear schedule then modulates these components: λ_∥(t) = α(1−t) so magnitude perturbation dominates early, and λ_⊥(t) = αt so directional perturbation dominates late, aligning the weak-branch degradation with the semantic role of each denoising stage. The weighted sum of both components is added directly to the Q, K, and V tensors of the self-attention layers in the weaker model, producing a dynamically degraded attention output. The final guided score is computed via the standard extrapolation s̃ = γ·s_θ − (γ−1)·s_θ^weak, requiring no additional training or architectural modification.

## Results

On conditional text-to-image generation, SAND achieves FID 17.56 and IS 32.37 on SDXL, and FID 11.06 and IS 36.87 on SD3, outperforming PAG and SEG on FID for both models. On unconditional generation, SAND reduces FID from 126.51 (vanilla) to 75.25 on SDXL and from 101.66 to 75.64 on SD3, while raising IS to 13.90 and 13.37 respectively. On DrawBench, SAND attains the best CLIPScore (23.46), HPSv2 (0.2253), AES (5.71), and ImageReward (0.6256) among all compared methods on SDXL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAND consistently outperforms PAG [1] across nearly all metrics: on SDXL conditional generation, SAND achieves FID 17.56 vs. PAG's 23.20 (−5.64) and IS 32.37 vs. 31.46; on SD3, SAND's FID of 11.06 beats PAG's 18.13 by a wide margin. Against SEG [13], SAND wins on FID for both SDXL (17.56 vs. 24.49) and SD3 (11.06 vs. 14.49), though SEG edges out SAND on SD3 IS (38.77 vs. 36.87). On the FLUX.1-dev model, SAND surpasses PLADIS [19] on PickScore (22.83 vs. 22.78), HPSv2 (0.2890 vs. 0.2876), and ImageReward (0.8884 vs. 0.8741), but trails on CLIPScore (22.65 vs. 26.93) and AES (5.68 vs. 5.70). When combined with CFG on FLUX.1, SAND yields marginal gains in ImageReward (1.004 vs. 0.9935) and HPSv2 (0.3008 vs. 0.3006) over CFG alone, while slightly trailing on CLIPScore (27.29 vs. 27.37). On the Wan2.2 video model, SAND improves all six VBench metrics over the vanilla baseline, confirming generalizability beyond image generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAND sits within the training-free guidance family for diffusion models, departing from prior attention-modification methods (PAG replaces attention maps with identity matrices; SEG applies Gaussian smoothing to attention weights) by instead injecting decomposed noise directly into the Q, K, V feature projections. Its key conceptual departure is the stage-aware decomposition of Gaussian noise into parallel (magnitude) and orthogonal (direction) components with complementary linear schedules, motivated by the observation that early denoising steps govern global structure while later steps refine geometry. Unlike geometric-decomposition guidance methods (APG, TCFG, TAG) that reshape the guidance direction after the score prediction is computed, SAND operates upstream in the self-attention feature space to construct the weak prediction itself, and unlike Autoguidance it requires no retraining of a degraded model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of SAND makes it immediately applicable to any publicly released diffusion model without architectural modification or additional training, which is practically valuable for the community. The stage-aware decomposition provides a clean conceptual lens (magnitude vs. direction aligned to diffusion timesteps) that could inform future guidance designs, though the linear schedule is a simple first-order approximation whose optimality remains unexplored. Limitations include the added computational cost of a second forward pass for the weak branch, the empirical (rather than theoretical) justification for the mid-layer selection, and the relatively modest performance margins over SEG on some metrics (e.g., SD3 conditional IS where SEG actually leads). Open questions include whether the decomposition generalizes to non-U-Net architectures beyond MMDiT, whether non-linear or learned schedules could further improve performance, and whether the approach transfers to 3D generation or other modalities.
