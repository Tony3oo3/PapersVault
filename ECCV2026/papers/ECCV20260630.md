## Problem

Existing joint quantization and adaptation methods designed for LLMs are ineffective for visual foundation models, where training memory is dominated not by floating-point weights but by intermediate activations cached for backpropagation in deep ViT backbones and activation quantizers. Methods like QA-LoRA exceed 60 GB GPU memory for SAM-H, while memory-efficient alternatives like QST introduce extra inference computation and inherit quantization errors from the frozen backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260630/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (1024×1024×3 for SAM-H segmentation, standard resolutions for ViT-B classification) and produces either dense segmentation masks (polyp, camouflaged object, or leaf disease) or class labels across 19 VTAB datasets. The critical output is a fully quantized model (W4A4 or W8A8) with the same architecture and parameter count as the original backbone, deployable with standard integer-only inference kernels and no adapter overhead.

## Method

EQuA splits each ViT block into a Memory-Intensive Backbone (MIB) and a lightweight Sub-network Side Adapter (SSA) by selecting channels based on activation-scaled weight importance, so that only the low-dimensional SSA branch is included in the computation graph during training. Two alternating strategies handle adaptation: SAQF freezes MIB and fine-tunes SSA with LoRA while using a one-step gradient recomputation to propagate quantization-aware supervision from the frozen backbone without caching its activations; BAQF periodically minimizes block-wise MSE between quantized and floating-point block outputs to optimize activation quantizers. At deployment, SSA weights are merged back into MIB, recovering the original ViT block shape and introducing zero extra parameters or computation, enabling standard 4-bit inference speedups (1.38×) while reducing training memory by over 70% compared to prior joint quantization-adaptation methods.

## Results

On SAM-H semantic segmentation (W4A4), EQuA uses only 7.67M trainable parameters and 18.5 GB training memory, achieving Sα=0.922/Eφ=0.938 on CVC-T, Sα=0.915/Eφ=0.947 on Kvasir, and mIoU=0.709/mDice=0.816 on Leaf—closely matching PEQA (Sα=0.927 on CVC-T) while reducing memory by 73.3% (69.4→18.5 GB). On ViT-B VTAB-1K (W4A4), EQuA attains 72.0% group-average Top-1 accuracy with only 1.7 GB training memory, outperforming QST (70.0%) and NIPQ (68.7%) while closely matching PEQA (72.9%). At deployment, the merged model achieves 1.38× inference speedup over the floating-point model with no extra computational overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full-parameter QAT baselines (LSQ, NIPQ), EQuA reduces trainable parameters from 641M to 7.67M and memory from 72.4 GB to 18.5 GB while matching or exceeding their accuracy (e.g., 0.922 vs. 0.884 Sα on CVC-T). Against joint quantization-adaptation methods PEQA and QA-LoRA, EQuA achieves comparable performance (within 0.005–0.01 on most metrics) but cuts training memory by 73.3% (18.5 vs. 69.4/68.9 GB), enabling training on a single 24 GB GPU. Against the closest memory-efficient competitor QST, EQuA uses less memory (18.5 vs. 21.6 GB) and substantially outperforms it on segmentation (e.g., +0.053 mIoU on Leaf: 0.709 vs. 0.656) and classification (72.0% vs. 70.0% group average on VTAB). On ViT-B VTAB-1K, EQuA (72.0%) closely matches PEQA (72.9%) and QA-LoRA (72.5%) while using only 1.7 GB vs. 6.6–6.7 GB. At deployment, EQuA achieves 1.38× inference speedup (same as PEQA/QA-LoRA) whereas QST only reaches 1.18× due to extra adapter overhead, confirming that merging SSA back into the backbone eliminates all additional computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

EQuA repurposes the side-adapter concept from QST (which attaches extra adapters to a quantized LLM backbone) but fundamentally reworks it for visual foundation models: instead of adding separate adapter modules that incur deployment overhead, it splits the backbone's own linear layers into a Memory-Intensive Backbone (MIB) and a Sub-network Side Adapter (SSA) branch based on activation-scaled weight importance, then merges them back at inference. The method also draws on LoRA for parameter efficiency, RepQ-ViT for PTQ initialization, and standard QAT formulations (LSQ, NIPQ), but departs from LLM-oriented joint quantization-and-adaptation methods (QA-LoRA, PEQA) whose memory savings come from compressing large floating-point weights—a strategy that is largely ineffective for visual models where cached activations dominate memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: EQuA enables quantization-aware adaptation of SAM-H on a single 24 GB RTX 3090 GPU (18.5 GB vs. 69+ GB for prior methods), removing a major barrier to deploying quantized visual foundation models on edge and consumer hardware. The zero-overhead deployment (SSA merged into backbone) distinguishes it from QST and makes it directly compatible with standard 4-bit inference kernels. Open questions include whether the activation-scaled weight importance heuristic generalizes to non-ViT architectures (e.g., Mamba-based visual models), whether the approach extends to even larger visual models with deeper backbones, and how it interacts with emerging low-precision training techniques beyond the brief COAT experiment.
