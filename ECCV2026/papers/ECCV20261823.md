## Problem

Controllable multi-object video generation remains difficult because existing trajectory-based methods require users to draw accurate tracks for every object (scaling poorly with scene complexity and failing under occlusion), while physics-aware approaches encode dynamics as low-level force fields or predefined categories that cannot capture complex open-domain interactions. No prior method provides a structured, interactive interface that lets users specify *how* multiple entities should interact rather than *where* each one should move.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB conditioning image and a directed interaction scene graph whose nodes are detected entities (with visual, textual, and spatial features) and whose edges carry open-vocabulary interaction labels (e.g., push, pull, lift) plus optional qualitative cues. Users construct or edit this graph by interacting directly with the image, and unary actions are encoded as self-loop edges. The output is a temporally coherent video of 81 frames at 16 fps generated by a frozen diffusion transformer conditioned on the graph-derived tokens.

## Method

GraphVid represents scene dynamics as an attributed directed interaction graph and injects it into a frozen LTX-Video DiT backbone via a lightweight graph-to-token adapter and LoRA modules (rank 128) in attention projections, training only 0.6B parameters under a flow-matching objective. The key architectural component is an Edge-Aware Graph Reasoning module built on GINEConv layers that injects directed edge attributes directly into the message-passing aggregation step, so each node embedding encodes not just object identity but its relational role (e.g., whether it is the pusher or the pushed). Node embeddings are projected to the DiT hidden dimension (4096) and concatenated with edge text tokens as encoder hidden states, allowing the transformer's self-attention to reason over entity interactions during denoising. The approach is backbone-agnostic (validated on both LTX-Video and Wan 2.2) and requires no explicit motion supervision, relying instead on the structured graph as the sole control signal.

## Results

On the interaction-centric MOVEBENCH subset, GraphVid (0.6B trainable params, 27K training clips) achieves FID 17.02, FVD 99.42, PSNR 15.98, SSIM 0.61, and EPE 2.9, outperforming all baselines except the much larger Wan-Move (14.5B params, 2M clips). GraphVid attains the fastest inference time at 200 seconds, roughly 3.4× faster than the next fastest method (FlashMotion at 677s) and 9× faster than Wan-Move (1800s). On the harder multi-object subset (Table 3), GraphVid achieves the best FID (49.45) and FVD (291) among comparable-scale methods, with the lowest EPE (3.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphVid outperforms all six baselines on FID, FVD, PSNR, SSIM, and EPE except for Wan-Move, which leads on every metric but uses ~24× more trainable parameters (14.5B vs. 0.6B) and ~74× more training data (2M vs. 27K). Against the closest same-scale competitor WISA (physics-centric text inputs, 1B params), GraphVid reduces FID by 34.5% (25.98→17.02) and FVD by 7.8% (107.89→99.42), while improving PSNR by 5.9% and SSIM by 15.0%. Versus trajectory-based methods, the gains are most dramatic against Motion-I2V (FID −39.9%, FVD −37.6%, PSNR +61.9%, SSIM +60.5%) and Tora (FID −30.3%, FVD −10.0%, PSNR +41.7%, SSIM +12.9%). GraphVid also beats FlashMotion (13B params) on FID (−10.5%), PSNR (+13.8%), and SSIM (+8.9%) despite using ~22× fewer parameters. On the multi-object subset, GraphVid achieves the best FID (49.45 vs. FlashMotion's 55.03) and FVD (291 vs. 311) among lightweight methods, confirming that interaction-graph conditioning scales to complex multi-entity dynamics without requiring massive models.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphVid departs from the dominant trajectory-based and physics-field-based paradigms for controllable image-to-video generation (Motion-I2V, Wan-Move, MagicMotion, WISA) by replacing low-level pixel-displacement or force signals with a structured, semantic control interface: a directed interaction scene graph over detected entities. Conceptually, it repurposes the GINE graph neural network architecture and LoRA-based parameter-efficient adaptation—both well-studied in NLP and graph learning—to bridge a symbolic relational representation into a frozen video diffusion transformer (LTX-Video), a pattern shared with recent work that injects lightweight conditioning into large pretrained DiTs. The paper also introduces GRAPHVID-BENCH, a new interaction-centric dataset that fills a gap left by existing benchmarks (MOVEBENCH, VBench) which rely on text prompts or dense motion annotations rather than explicit relational structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a more intuitive and scalable control interface for multi-object video generation: users specify *what* entities do to each other (push, pull, hold) rather than *where* pixels should move, which should generalize better to complex, occluded, or overlapping scenes where trajectory drawing breaks down. The ~24× parameter and ~74–370× data efficiency relative to trajectory-based competitors suggests that structured semantic conditioning may be a more sample-efficient inductive bias than dense motion supervision, a finding with implications for how future controllable generation systems are designed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to scenes with many interacting entities beyond the 30-node cap, how sensitive it is to errors in upstream object detection and graph construction, and whether the curated interaction vocabulary in GRAPHVID-BENCH limits generalization to open-ended physical dynamics.
