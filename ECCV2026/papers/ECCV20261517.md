## Problem

Existing cryo-EM denoisers trained with pixel-wise or Noise2Noise-style regression objectives improve visual quality but do not explicitly enforce the structural consistency required by downstream particle picking and 3D reconstruction, often causing over-smoothing or distortion under the extreme low-SNR conditions of cryo-EM. Denoising score matching (DSM) is theoretically well-suited to this regime, yet it has not been applied to cryo-EM in practice, and under the very low noise levels characteristic of cryo-EM, the DSM training target becomes ill-conditioned (scaling as σ_a^{-2}), making score estimation unstable near the clean signal manifold.

## Input / Output

The method operates on local patches extracted via sliding window from large grayscale cryo-EM micrographs (typically 4096×4096, stored in MRC or TIFF format), with no clean ground-truth micrograph available due to radiation-dose constraints. In addition to the noisy patch, the framework consumes a low-pass-filtered (10 Å) reference density map—sourced from EMDB/PDB or estimated via ab-initio reconstruction—which is projected into a bank of 2D target views to provide structural guidance. The output is a denoised patch whose improved particle-background separability is evaluated through downstream Topaz particle picking (precision/recall/F1) and cryoSPARC 3D reconstruction (gold-standard FSC 0.143 resolution).

## Method

The framework replaces the ill-conditioned DSM conditional noise-model score with a target-guided surrogate: a bank of 2D projections from a reference volume is matched to each noisy patch via cosine-similarity-weighted aggregation in a learned feature space, yielding an input-adaptive proxy target whose analytic Gaussian score (−y/σ²) serves as a stable supervision signal. A per-sample rescaled loss (following AR-DAE preconditioning) restores scale compatibility across noise levels, and an adaptive learning strategy interpolates between TSM and DSM per sample using the maximum similarity weight w_t(x) as a confidence gate, so that well-aligned particle regions receive target guidance while non-particle regions fall back to DSM. Training employs similarity-guided annealing: a global coefficient λ(t) is held at zero during a warmup phase to stabilize the encoder, then ramped linearly before being fixed at one, preventing unreliable early similarity estimates from destabilizing optimization.

## Results

On micro-averaged particle picking F1, TSM achieves 0.649 on EMPIAR-10291 and 0.370 on EMPIAR-10289, while DSM achieves 0.648 on EMPIAR-10081, all outperforming DRACO (0.620/0.367/0.417) and Topaz (0.613/0.366/0.421). For 3D reconstruction resolution (FSC 0.143), TSM reaches 3.75 Å on EMPIAR-10291 and 4.10 Å on EMPIAR-10081, compared to 6.08 Å (DRACO) and 4.68 Å (Topaz) on EMPIAR-10291, and 6.39 Å (DRACO) and 7.35 Å (Topaz) on EMPIAR-10081. Macro-averaged F1 confirms the trend, with TSM leading on EMPIAR-10289 (0.364) and EMPIAR-10291 (0.640).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gaussian filtering, both DSM and TSM consistently improve F1 across all three datasets (e.g., 0.649 vs 0.641 on EMPIAR-10291; 0.648 vs 0.628 on EMPIAR-10081), with the gap widening on lower-SNR data. Against DRACO, the score-based methods show the largest gains: TSM improves F1 by 0.029 on EMPIAR-10291 (0.649 vs 0.620) and 0.003 on EMPIAR-10289 (0.370 vs 0.367), while DSM improves by 0.231 on EMPIAR-10081 (0.648 vs 0.417), where DRACO causes severe structural degradation in 3D reconstruction (6.39 Å vs 4.13 Å). Against Topaz-Denoise, the advantage is similar in magnitude, with DSM/TSM achieving 0.644–0.649 F1 on EMPIAR-10291 versus 0.613 for Topaz, and dramatically better 3D resolution (3.75–3.78 Å vs 4.68 Å on EMPIAR-10291; 4.10–4.13 Å vs 7.35 Å on EMPIAR-10081). The key distinction is that DRACO and Topaz, while improving recall, sacrifice precision and introduce structure-inconsistent artifacts that degrade downstream reconstruction, whereas the score-based methods preserve particle-level structural information more faithfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions cryo-EM micrograph denoising as a natural but previously unexplored application domain for denoising score matching (DSM), building directly on the DSM framework of Xie et al. (NeurIPS 2023) and the target score matching theory of Bortoli et al. (2024). It departs from the dominant N2N-style regression paradigm (Topaz-Denoise, DRACO) by arguing that pixel-wise regression objectives do not explicitly model noise statistics or enforce structural consistency under extreme low-SNR conditions. The key technical departure is identifying that DSM's score target becomes ill-conditioned as noise level σ_a → 0—precisely the cryo-EM regime—and using target-guided supervision (informed by reference density projections) to stabilize score estimation, with AR-DAE preconditioning (Lim et al., ICML 2020) ensuring scale compatibility across noise levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper bridges a recognized gap between image-restoration quality and downstream structural recovery in cryo-EM, showing that score-based denoising improves particle picking F1 and 3D reconstruction resolution (FSC 0.143) over both classical filtering and SOTA deep denoisers. A notable practical finding is that DRACO and Topaz, despite improving visual quality, can substantially degrade 3D reconstruction (e.g., DRACO at 6.39 Å vs. GT 3.5 Å on EMPIAR-10081), suggesting that 'better-looking' denoised images do not necessarily serve structural analysis. Open questions include how to construct reliable target references when no EMDB/PDB template exists, how the method generalizes to heterogeneous or unknown noise conditions, and whether the target-guided score-matching formulation extends to other low-SNR scientific imaging (cryo-ET, fluorescence microscopy).
