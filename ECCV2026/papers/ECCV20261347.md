## Problem

Deterministic deep-learning MRI reconstruction methods remain protocol-specific: a model trained for one sampling mask, contrast, or acceleration factor degrades when the acquisition changes, hindering deployment across the heterogeneous cardiac MRI landscape (multi-contrast, multi-trajectory, multi-center, multi-field-strength, multi-population). No prior single model has been validated across this full spectrum without task-specific fine-tuning, and no empirical scaling analysis exists to guide whether to invest in deeper unrolling, wider backbones, or larger datasets.

## Input / Output

The model consumes complex-valued undersampled multi-coil k-space (C coils × H × W), a binary sampling mask, and discrete protocol metadata (mask type, acceleration factor, modality label); for dynamic or 3D acquisitions, temporally/spatially adjacent frames are additionally fed into each cascade. Complex data is represented as stacked real/imaginary channels and padded to sizes divisible by the two-level pyramid hierarchy. The output is a single reconstructed complex-valued image at full resolution, produced by a deterministic feed-forward pass through T cascades (no iterative sampling).

## Method

SDUM is a deep unrolled network with T cascades, each comprising a Restormer reconstructor (shallow two-stage pyramid with MDTA + GDFN blocks), a per-cascade U-Net coil-sensitivity-map estimator (CSME) that refines sensitivities without autocalibration regions, and a sampling-aware weighted data-consistency (SWDC) operator that learns spatially varying k-space weight maps conditioned on the sampling pattern rather than using a scalar DC weight. A universal conditioning mechanism injects sinusoidal embeddings of the cascade index and protocol metadata (via MLPs) as additive biases into every transformer block, enabling one model to adapt across all acquisition settings. Training follows a progressive cascade-expansion curriculum that doubles interior cascades while fixing endpoints (T = 6 → 10 → 18), warm-starting all parameters to stabilize optimization at depth. The key differentiator from prior unrolled models is the joint use of learned per-cascade CSMs, pattern-specific SWDC, and physics-informed universal conditioning, which together allow a single model to generalize across contrasts, trajectories, centers, field strengths, and patient populations without fine-tuning.

## Results

On CMRxRecon2025, a single SDUM model (T=18 cascades) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four tracks without task-specific fine-tuning: 33.18 dB PSNR / 0.895 SSIM (multi-center), 33.54 dB / 0.880 (multi-disease), 34.23 dB / 0.901 (5T), and 33.48 dB / 0.905 (pediatric). On CMRxRecon2024, SDUM surpasses the 2024 winning method PromptMR+ (32 cascades) by +0.55 dB PSNR in Task 1 (35.70 vs. 35.15 dB) and wins 90.3% of paired validation cases. On fastMRI brain at 4× acceleration, SDUM reaches 42.6 dB PSNR, exceeding PC-RNN by +1.8 dB. Zero-shot transfer to unseen in-house CEST MRI yields 43.57 dB PSNR and 0.9769 SSIM without any adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDUM outperforms all 17 baselines across every evaluation setting. On CMRxRecon2025, it beats PromptMR+ (32 cascades) [34] by +0.12 to +0.40 dB where directly compared, HierAdaptMR [38] by +0.94 to +1.48 dB, and Shen et al. [27] by +0.54 to +1.04 dB across the four tracks. On CMRxRecon2024, SDUM exceeds the 2024 winner PromptMR+ (32 cascades) [34] by +0.55 dB (Task 1) and +0.14 dB (Task 2), and outperforms vSHARP+ARN [39] by +1.25 dB. On fastMRI brain, SDUM surpasses PC-RNN [4] (the fastMRI challenge winner) by +1.8 dB at 4× and +1.9 dB at 6× acceleration, and exceeds KIKI-Net [8] by +4.7 dB at 4×. GENRE-CMR [12], evaluated only on the pediatric track, is outperformed by +1.82 dB PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDUM synthesizes several established threads—deep unrolled reconstruction (MODL, E2E-VarNet, PC-RNN), transformer-based restoration backbones (Restormer), and conditional adaptation (FiLM/AdaLN)—into a single framework for cardiac MRI. Its key departures from prior unrolled models are the sampling-aware weighted data consistency (replacing scalar DC weights with learned spatially-varying k-space maps), per-cascade coil sensitivity estimation without autocalibration regions, and physics-informed universal conditioning on cascade index and acquisition metadata. Unlike PromptMR+ and HierAdaptMR, which use task-specific or hierarchical adaptation, SDUM claims a single model handles the full spectrum of cardiac heterogeneity (multi-contrast, multi-trajectory, multi-center, multi-field-strength, multi-population) without any fine-tuning, and it borrows progressive GAN-style depth expansion to stabilize training of deep unrolled stacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first empirical scaling analysis for cardiac MRI reconstruction (depth up to 18 cascades, data from 40% to 100%) provides the community with a practical compass for resource allocation, showing near-logarithmic PSNR gains with depth and diminishing but non-saturating returns from data volume. The zero-shot transfer to an unseen CEST protocol and the +1.8 dB gain over PC-RNN on fastMRI brain suggest the design principles are not overfit to cardiac-specific characteristics, though establishing a truly anatomy-agnostic model remains open. Key limitations include the computational cost of training deep variants, the lack of resolution-adaptive SWDC at inference, the absence of a full compute-optimal trade-off characterization, and the need for prospective clinical validation before deployment.
