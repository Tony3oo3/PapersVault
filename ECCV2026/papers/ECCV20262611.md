## Problem

AI-generated image detectors frequently fail under simultaneous shifts in generator family, prompt/style, and source domain, yet existing evaluations conflate the contributions of the training prior, the frozen representation, and the learned decision head. It remains unclear when a trained classifier head adds value beyond what is already separable in modern frozen encoder features, making it difficult to diagnose whether OOD transfer failures stem from the representation, the head optimization, or the prior itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images passed through a frozen image encoder (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14, PE-Core-bigG-14-448) to produce D-dimensional embeddings, plus a support set S drawn from a public training prior (e.g., CommunityForensics, GenImage) from which class-conditional means and covariances are estimated. No gradient-based training is performed; the 'fitting' step is purely moment estimation, and the support set can be as small as 44 samples (0.001% of the prior) while retaining most of the full-support performance. Output is a binary real/synthetic label with class-conditional probability computed in closed form from the Gaussian discriminant score.

## Method

The paper introduces a prior-conditioned Gaussian discriminant ladder: five closed-form classifiers (Euc-NCM, Cos-NCM, GNB, Mah-NCM, QDA) organized by nested covariance assumptions from isotropic to class-specific full covariance, all fitted analytically from first- and second-order statistics of frozen encoder features. By holding the encoder and training prior fixed, the ladder isolates the decision-rule contribution and identifies which low-order feature geometry transfers across generator and domain shifts. The authors further construct Percept-Lens, a unified evaluation protocol aggregating 39 public datasets (7.1 million images) that explicitly couples shifts in generator family, prompt/style, and acquisition pipeline, enabling matched (prior, encoder, head) comparisons against released detectors. Theoretical propositions establish that under the Gaussian model, QDA is Bayes-optimal in the heteroscedastic case, Mah-NCM under homoscedasticity, and that AUC degrades smoothly under bounded drift in class means and covariances. Empirically, the best rung (often Mah-NCM) is competitive with or exceeds released detector heads under matched conditions, while the CF-384 exception signals higher-order structure beyond second moments.

## Results

Under matched (prior, encoder) conditions on Percept-Lens (39 datasets, 7.1M images), the best Gaussian discriminant rung matches or exceeds the released detector head in 10 of 11 comparisons, with gains ranging from +0.63% (CF-224) to +9.66% (AIDE on Full GenImage). The sole exception is CF-384, where the released head (87.54% CA) retains a 2.99% advantage over the best ladder rung (84.45% Mah-NCM). On a frozen PE-Core-bigG-14-448 encoder with no detection fine-tuning, Mah-NCM reaches 94.46% macro-averaged CA, and with only 219 labeled samples (0.005% of the prior) it still achieves 90.89%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UnivFD [48] (CNNSpot prior), Mah-NCM improves CA from 54.63% to 57.62% (+2.99%). Against AIDE [69], the ladder wins in both GenImage settings: +8.36% on SDv1 (48.99%→57.35%) and +9.66% on Full GenImage (54.98%→64.64%), the largest gains in the table. Against Effort [70] (LoRA-adapted, GenImage-SDv1), Mah-NCM edges ahead by 4.24% (72.58%→76.82%). Against DRCT-UnivFD [19], QDA (not Mah-NCM) is the best rung on DRCT-2M priors, gaining +6.84% and +7.94% on SDv1/SDv2 respectively, while Mah-NCM leads on the GenImage prior (+7.24%). Against CoDE-kNN [16] (ELSA-D3), Mah-NCM gains a modest +2.14%. Against CommunityForensics [50], CF-224 is narrowly beaten (+0.63%) but CF-384 remains the one case where the trained head (87.54%) outperforms the best ladder rung (84.45%), suggesting higher-order or non-Gaussian structure in that representation. Euc-NCM [67], the simplest nearest-centroid rule, is consistently competitive but trails Mah-NCM by 2–8 points across most priors, confirming that shared full-covariance geometry captures the dominant transferable signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classical Gaussian discriminant analysis (GDA, Mahalanobis scoring, QDA) as a diagnostic ladder for AIGI detection, connecting to the prototypical/nearest-centroid inference line (Wu et al. [67]) and the broader OOD-detection literature on generative scoring rules (Lee et al. [41]). Rather than proposing another trained detector, it isolates the decision-rule factor by fitting closed-form heads on frozen encoder features under matched training priors, complementing the growing body of AIGI detectors (UnivFD, AIDE, DRCT, Effort, CoDE, CF) that are evaluated under narrower shift protocols. The Percept-Lens protocol extends prior evaluation suites that vary generator or source in isolation by explicitly coupling shifts in generator family, prompt/style, and acquisition pipeline, drawing on 39 existing public datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical audit protocol—comparing a trained head against the best Gaussian rung on the same frozen features and support prior—gives the field a cheap, reproducible way to determine whether classifier training actually adds OOD value or whether second-order feature geometry already suffices. The finding that training prior dominates over head choice (77.92% to 94.46% CA spread under a fixed encoder) and that 219 labeled samples can yield near-full-support performance with Mah-NCM reframes data-collection priorities for AIGI detection. Open questions include characterizing failures in multimodal or heavy-tailed feature regimes (where learned non-linear heads legitimately outperform the ladder), achieving a fully symmetric decomposition across prior, encoder, and head, and designing adaptation procedures that improve OOD performance while preserving the transferable low-order geometry the ladder captures.
