## Problem

Pretrained single-view intrinsic decomposition models produce mutually inconsistent PBR parameter estimates (albedo, roughness, metallicity) when applied independently to multiple views of the same scene, because the single-image decomposition problem is severely ill-posed. Video-based methods can enforce temporal consistency but require dense, ordered frame sequences, making them inapplicable to the sparse, unordered image collections commonly encountered in practice. The paper addresses how to obtain cross-view consistent intrinsics from such collections without retraining the base model or performing per-scene optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260377/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse, unordered set of V RGB images (evaluated at V ∈ {4, 8, 16, 32}) of a static scene and produces per-view albedo, roughness, and metallicity maps that are mutually consistent at geometrically corresponding 3D surface locations. It is model-agnostic, wrapping any pretrained single-view diffusion intrinsic predictor (demonstrated with RGB↔X and Marigold IID Appearance) without modifying its weights. All processing occurs at inference time on a single GPU, with no per-scene tuning of hyperparameters.

## Method

Geo-ID is a three-stage inference-time framework. First, a feed-forward geometry transformer (VGGT) predicts world-frame 3D point clouds, camera parameters, and per-pixel confidence maps from the unordered input views. Second, high-confidence 3D points are pooled into axis-aligned voxels; the base diffusion model runs independently per view, and per-voxel intrinsic values are aggregated via a confidence-weighted median with a MAD-based dispersion estimate that serves as an uncertainty-aware guidance weight. Third, a second diffusion pass re-samples each view, and at the last 80% of denoising timesteps, a Tweedie-formula estimate of the clean prediction is decoded and a Huber loss against the projected voxel consensus targets is backpropagated for a single gradient step on the latent, while all model parameters remain frozen. This sparse, geometry-induced coupling enforces cross-view agreement only at voxel-supported pixel coordinates, preserving the generative prior of the base model and avoiding joint multi-image optimization.

## Results

On MipNeRF-360 indoor scenes at 32 views, Geo-ID reduces cross-view albedo MAD from 0.091 (Marigold Appr.) to 0.076 and from 0.114 (RGB↔X) to 0.098, while roughness MAD drops from 0.096 to 0.082 and 0.100 to 0.065 respectively. On outdoor scenes, metallicity MAD improves from 0.070 to 0.044 (Marigold) and 0.192 to 0.085 (RGB↔X). Per-view decomposition quality is preserved: PSNR remains within ±0.1 dB of the unguided baseline on InteriorVerse and HyperSim. Downstream albedo novel-view synthesis improves by +1.8 dB (RGB↔X) and +1.9 dB (Marigold) in PSNR over unguided bases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo-ID substantially outperforms IDArb [27] on all metrics across all datasets (e.g., indoor albedo MAD 0.076 vs. 0.258 for Marigold+Geo-ID vs. IDArb), as IDArb was trained on isolated objects and does not generalize to scene-level captures. Against CID [5], Geo-ID wins on albedo consistency in every setting (e.g., 0.054 vs. 0.070 outdoor MipNeRF-360) and additionally provides roughness and metallicity. Compared to the unordered Diffusion Renderer [28] (the matched setting), Geo-ID beats it on albedo across all real-world splits and on all three InteriorVerse modalities; the unordered video model degrades sharply (≈2× worse albedo MAD) when temporal order is removed. Against the ordered Diffusion Renderer upper bound, Geo-ID wins on most metrics but concedes indoor roughness (0.082 vs. 0.038) and metallicity (0.100 vs. 0.043), where dense temporal supervision is strongest. Geo-ID improves its own unguided bases (RGB↔X, Marigold Appr.) by 10–45% in MAD depending on modality and scene type, with the largest relative gains on metallicity. On decomposition quality (Table 2), Geo-ID matches or slightly exceeds the unguided baselines (PSNR within ±0.1 dB), while Diffusion Renderer achieves higher absolute PSNR (21.9 vs. 19.5 on InteriorVerse) at the cost of requiring ordered video input and baking in residual lighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

Geo-ID carves new ground by repurposing frozen single-view diffusion predictors (RGB↔X, Marigold) for cross-view consistent decomposition without retraining or per-scene optimization. It departs from video-based methods (Diffusion Renderer, Ouroboros) that require dense ordered sequences, from multi-view trained architectures (IDT, IDArb) that need specialized training data, and from inverse rendering pipelines (NeRFactor, Intrinsic Image Fusion) that demand accurate geometry and costly optimization. The conceptual lineage draws on diffusion guidance (Tweedie-based latent updates, blended diffusion) and feed-forward geometry estimation (VGGT), but the key departure is using sparse, noisy 3D correspondences as a consensus mechanism to couple otherwise independent diffusion processes at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes cross-view consistent PBR decomposition accessible from casual sparse photo collections without any retraining, directly enabling editable neural scene representations (relighting, material editing) from in-the-wild captures. The finding that noisy VGGT geometry is sufficient for meaningful consensus—despite being far from perfect—suggests a broader principle: approximate geometric structure can resolve generative ambiguities that per-view priors cannot. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex material models (clearcoat, subsurface), whether it degrades gracefully with very few views (2–3), and whether the indoor-Lambertian bias of current base predictors fundamentally caps achievable quality regardless of the consensus mechanism.
