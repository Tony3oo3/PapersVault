## Problem

Existing AIGIQA methods prioritize visual quality but consistently underperform on prompt-image alignment (SRCC < 0.80 across datasets), because the quality-alignment relationship is highly nonlinear (R² < 0.7) and pretrained VLMs suffer from 'modality noise' where dense visual details distract attention away from sparse textual prompts. No prior framework explicitly models cross-modal feature refinement and fusion as an information-theoretic optimization process in the deep feature domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260711/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 AI-generated image and its raw text prompt (no handcrafted quality templates) as input, processed by a BLIP backbone with shallow-layer weights frozen and deep-layer weights fine-tuned. It outputs a single scalar MOS score in [0,5] for either visual quality or prompt-image alignment, depending on which ground-truth labels are used during training. The batch size is 5 due to memory constraints, and evaluation uses 10-fold random 80/20 splits reporting mean PLCC and SRCC.

## Method

CMIM-AIGIQA introduces a cyclic mutual information maximization framework with two phases operating on BLIP-extracted features. In the forward phase, a variational lower bound on I(X;Y) is maximized by modeling the conditional q_θ(y|x) as a Gaussian (predicted by two MLPs) plus a differential entropy term H(Y) that regularizes the textual feature space against collapse, effectively filtering modality-specific noise. The refined features are fused via cross-attention (CSA) plus concatenation into a joint embedding Z. In the backward phase, CPC combined with NCE forces Z to inversely 'predict' both modality features through cosine-similarity score functions with in-batch negatives, preventing dense visual features from overwhelming sparse textual constraints. Training is two-stage: first the forward MIM MLPs are trained in isolation, then the full network is fine-tuned end-to-end with a weighted loss L = L_task + 0.1·L_forward + 0.1·L_backward.

## Results

CMIM-AIGIQA achieves the highest prompt-image alignment SRCC across all three AIGIQA benchmarks: 0.8546 on AGIQA-3K, 0.8263 on AIGCIQA2023, and 0.8223 on PKU-AIGIQA-4k, outperforming the second-best method by 3.74%, 3.56%, and 2.45% respectively. On the visual quality dimension, the method remains competitive (top-3 SRCC on all datasets: 0.8755, 0.8679, 0.8586), demonstrating that the cyclic MIM framework balances both evaluation axes without sacrificing quality assessment. In cross-dataset generalization, CMIM-AIGIQA shows the most stable performance, with the largest margin over MoE-AGIQA reaching 11.67% in alignment SRCC (0.6305 vs. 0.5646) when transferring from PKU-AIGIQA-4k to AIGCIQA2023.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the six classic NR-IQA baselines (BRISQUE, NIQE, BMPRI, DBCNN, HyperIQA, WaDIQaM-NR), CMIM-AIGIQA achieves substantially higher alignment SRCC (0.82–0.85 vs. 0.30–0.72) and competitive quality SRCC, confirming that unimodal or quality-only models cannot capture prompt-image alignment. Among the six SOTA AIGIQA methods, CMIM-AIGIQA wins the alignment SRCC on all three datasets: it surpasses MoE-AGIQA (the strongest quality-focused competitor) by 3.74% on AGIQA-3K (0.8546 vs. 0.8238) and by 7.65% on PKU-AIGIQA-4k (0.8223 vs. 0.7458), and beats IPCE by 3.56% on AIGCIQA2023 (0.8263 vs. 0.7979). On the quality dimension, CMIM-AIGIQA is slightly behind MoE-AGIQA on AGIQA-3K (0.8755 vs. 0.8746, essentially tied) and AIGCIQA2023 (0.8679 vs. 0.8751), and behind CIA-Net on PKU-AIGIQA-4k (0.8586 vs. 0.8638), but remains within the top-3 across all settings. Notably, MoE-AGIQA—which uses the same BLIP backbone—shows a pronounced quality-alignment imbalance (e.g., quality 0.8751 vs. alignment 0.7899 on AIGCIQA2023), whereas CMIM-AIGIQA narrows this gap, validating the cyclic MIM design. In cross-dataset transfer, CMIM-AIGIQA maintains the highest alignment SRCC in 5 of 6 evaluation pairs, with the largest advantage over MoE-AGIQA reaching 11.67% (PKU→AIGCIQA2023 alignment), while MoE-AGIQA and CIA-Net suffer sharp drops (e.g., MoE-AGIQA alignment SRCC falls to 0.2612 on AIGCIQA2023→PKU-AIGIQA-4k).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the variational MI lower bound from Barber & Agakov (2004) for the forward phase and combines CPC (Oord et al., 2018) with NCE (Gutmann & Hyvärinen, 2010) for the backward phase, creating a cyclic MIM loop that is novel in the AIGIQA context. It departs from prevailing AIGIQA methods (IPCE, AMFF-IQA, CIA-Net, MoE-AGIQA) that confine multimodal design to input-level templates or output-level score pooling, instead operating in the deep feature domain to explicitly model cross-modal information flow. The use of BLIP as a partially-fine-tuned backbone follows MoE-AGIQA, but the fusion strategy—cross-attention followed by cyclic MI constraints—represents a fundamentally different architectural philosophy from the mixture-of-experts or multi-scale fusion approaches in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled information-theoretic framework for jointly assessing visual quality and prompt-image alignment in AIGIQA, addressing a gap where existing quality-sensitive metrics consistently underperform on alignment (SRCC < 0.80). The ablation evidence that the backward MIM specifically prevents dense visual features from overwhelming sparse textual cues offers a mechanistic explanation for why alignment is hard, which could inform future multimodal fusion designs beyond AIGIQA. Limitations include reliance on small datasets (2,400–3,000 images), a batch size of 5 that constrains NCE negative sampling, and evaluation limited to text-to-image; whether the cyclic MIM framework transfers to video generation or longer-form prompts remains open.
