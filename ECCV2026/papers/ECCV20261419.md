## Problem

Privacy evaluations in computer vision treat each dataset or model release as an isolated, static disclosure, ignoring how identity risk accumulates across sequential releases of data expansions, checkpoints, and metadata. Even after face removal, non-face cues such as clothing, gait, and scene context remain linkable, and the paper shows this residual signal compounds under cumulative releases in a way that single-release audits cannot capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261419/cropped_page12_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of release artifacts (encoder embeddings, a generic ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 feature, a prototype bank, and a kNN graph) over face-masked person crops (top 20% masked) from a closed-world identity universe (N=751 for Market-1501, N=700 for CUHK03). Each release is modeled as a Markov kernel producing side information Z_t, and the cumulative state Z_{1:T} is fed to attacker posteriors that output a distribution over all N identities. The primary output is the scalar vRPI_α(T) = log N − H_α(U|Z_{1:T}) at multiple Rényi orders (α ∈ {1.5, 2, 5, 10}), alongside the Bayes-optimal guessing probability P_guess and the conditional Rényi entropy itself, all computed over the same fixed label space.

## Method

The core contribution is a theory-first framework that models each public disclosure as a channel K_t and defines the Vision Re-Identification Pressure Index vRPI_α(T) = log N − H_α(U|Z_{1:T}) using Arimoto conditional Rényi entropy, yielding a tunable, information-theoretic measure of how cumulative releases erode identity anonymity. The paper proves five results: monotonicity of vRPI_α under additional releases (via convexity of the α-norm on posterior mixtures), an exact Rényi-to-guessing bound linking H_α to P_guess, an additive Sibson-information decomposition under a conditional-independence idealization, a data-processing guarantee for masking ablations, and a plug-in stability bound for finite-sample estimation. Empirically, a structured Attacker Ladder (black-box retrieval, linear probe, prototype-index specialist, MLP probe, white-box fine-tuning) estimates the posterior at each release stage, and a dependence-overlap diagnostic Δ_corr,2 quantifies the gap from the ideal additive benchmark when releases share encoders and image sources.

## Results

On MARKET-1501-TAU (N=751), the dominant effect is exposure expansion from R1→R2: the black-box retrieval attacker A0 sees P_guess jump from 0.2883 to 0.7491 and vRPI_2 from 4.3319 to 6.2119, while the MLP probe A3 goes from 0.4073 to 0.7063. Adding the generic ImageNet ResNet-50 channel (R2→R3) is stable under calibrated fusion (A0: 0.7491→0.7529), and R4's released prototype bank and kNN graph enable artifact-specific leakage (A2: P_guess=0.7519, vRPI_2=6.3572). The CUHK03-TAU replication confirms the paired R1→R2 increase on both datasets (A0: 0.226→0.399; A3: 0.313→0.502), and the masking ablation shows vRPI_2 remains in [4.87, 4.91] even at 40% top-region masking, confirming persistent non-face leakage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark a new Re-ID model against existing ones; instead it evaluates the proposed vRPI_α metric across a structured Attacker Ladder (A0 retrieval, A1 linear probe, A2 prototype-index, A3 MLP probe, A4 white-box fine-tuning) to verify that the metric tracks realized inference risk. All attackers show the same qualitative pattern: a large R1→R2 jump from exposure expansion, a stable R2→R3 transition under calibrated fusion, and artifact-specific gains at R4. The white-box attacker A4 achieves the highest P_guess at R1 (0.5073) but converges with A0 by R2 (0.7304 vs. 0.7491), indicating that exposure growth dominates attacker sophistication. The CUHK03-TAU replication (N=700) confirms the paired R1→R2 increase under an independent lighter-weight protocol, ruling out benchmark-specific artifacts. Against existing leakage measures (maximal leakage, maximal α-leakage, DP/attribute-inference bounds), vRPI_α is positioned as complementary: it fixes the vision identity task and measures posterior concentration over a release sequence rather than optimizing worst-case channel gain, making it a release-auditing statistic rather than a worst-case guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes well-established information-theoretic tools—Arimoto conditional Rényi entropy, min-entropy/guessing-probability duality, and Sibson mutual information—within a vision-specific release-sequence framework that prior privacy work has not formalized. It departs from the dominant single-release evaluation paradigm in vision anonymization (k-Same, DeepPrivacy, Fawkes) by modeling each disclosure as a channel and analyzing the cumulative posterior Pr(U|Z_{1:T}), rather than assessing risk after one static transformation. The Re-ID benchmark infrastructure (Market-1501, CUHK03, triplet-loss encoders, kNN retrieval) is used as the empirical substrate, but the contribution is the compositional leakage formalism and its Rényi-entropy-based pressure index, not a new model or anonymizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework shifts privacy auditing from per-release compliance to ecosystem-level cumulative risk, providing a principled metric (vRPI_α) that tracks how identity uncertainty erodes across sequential data, model, and index disclosures even after face removal. Key limitations constrain immediate impact: the non-face transform is a simple top-region mask (not a realistic anonymizer), evaluation is closed-world on two image benchmarks at N≈750, and the open-world, video, and long-sequence extensions are deferred. Open questions include whether the compositional pressure formalism holds under generative anonymizers, whether the additive benchmark gap (Δ_corr,2) grows superlinearly with release count in real deployment pipelines, and how vRPI_α should be integrated into institutional release-governance workflows.
