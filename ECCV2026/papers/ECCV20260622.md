## Problem

Automated filament tracing in Cryo-EM is blocked by fundamental geometric incompatibilities in all existing paradigms: box-based detectors suffer ghost-center drift on thin curves, segmentation models fracture topology at 2D crossovers, sequential trackers accumulate Markovian errors, and closed active contours impose artificial cyclic tension that shrinks open filament endpoints. No prior method reconciles open-topology connectivity with holistic deformation under the extreme low-SNR regime (SNR < 0.1, i.e., below −10 dB) characteristic of vitreous-ice-embedded filaments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single raw Cryo-EM micrograph (grayscale, with CTF-induced contrast oscillations and Poisson/shot noise) and produces a set of open skeletal trajectories, each represented as N=128 equidistant 2D vertices forming a non-cyclic polyline. Beyond the final curves, the network simultaneously outputs a topological centroid heatmap, per-instance center-to-endpoint displacement vectors, and two dense auxiliary fields (Euclidean distance to nearest trajectory and 2-channel tangent angle), all of which serve as intermediate supervision and geometric regularizers rather than final deliverables.

## Method

FPicker is a two-stage, end-to-end trainable pipeline built on a shared encoder-decoder backbone (DLA-34, Swin-T, or ResNet-50). Stage I replaces bounding-box priors with a center-endpoint representation: a focal-loss heatmap anchors a topological centroid at the arc-length median of each filament, and sparse L1 regression predicts displacement vectors to both endpoints, from which a linear skeletal prior is uniformly interpolated. Stage II deforms this rigid prior via an open-curve evolution module that applies graph convolutions with replicate padding at the boundaries (breaking the cyclic gradient flow that causes endpoint shrinking) and a dual-stream architecture where a local OBC stream extracts geometric affinities while a global max-pooling stream broadcasts high-confidence structural cues to noise-submerged vertices. A bridge curriculum strategy stochastically mixes ground-truth and predicted initializations (annealing P_gt from 1 to 0 over 120 epochs) to decouple local deformation learning from global localization variance, and auxiliary distance/angle field losses act as continuous low-pass regularizers on the backbone to prevent overfitting to high-frequency noise.

## Results

On the Cryo-Sim benchmark, FPicker (DLA-34) achieves 92.1% mSAP at High SNR (-10 dB), 91.1% at Medium SNR (-13 dB), and 76.5% at Extreme SNR (-20 dB), with gap rates of 0.7%, 6.3%, and 9.8% respectively. Under the most challenging Extreme SNR regime, FPicker outperforms the strongest baseline (CurveGCN, 48.2% mSAP) by over 28 percentage points and reduces the topological gap rate from 26.2% to 9.8%. On the real-world Custom-EMPIAR dataset, fine-tuned FPicker reaches a state-of-the-art 82.9% mSAP with a 6.8% gap rate, while its zero-shot transfer (trained only on Cryo-Sim) still achieves 28.6% mSAP, surpassing fine-tuned versions of crYOLO (25.4%) and Topaz (26.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPicker decisively outperforms all eight baselines across every SNR regime. Against box-based detectors (crYOLO, YOLOv8), the gap is most dramatic at Extreme SNR: FPicker's 76.5% mSAP exceeds YOLOv8's 28.5% and crYOLO's 25.4% by over 40 percentage points, confirming the ghost-center degeneracy hypothesis. Segmentation methods (U-Net, SegFormer, Topaz, SAM 2) suffer from topological fracturing and instance merging at crossovers; SAM 2 is particularly catastrophic at Extreme SNR (12.1% mSAP, 75.2% gap rate) because its texture-based attention collapses in shot-noise-dominated Cryo-EM. Among closed active contours, CurveGCN (48.2% mSAP) and Deep Snake (45.8% mSAP) are the closest competitors at Extreme SNR, but both exhibit the endpoint shrinking degeneracy reflected in their substantially higher P-Ang values (42.8° and 45.2° vs. FPicker's 20.5°). On the real-world EMPIAR dataset, fine-tuned FPicker (82.9% mSAP) surpasses all fine-tuned baselines, with the largest margins over CurveGCN (62.5%) and Deep Snake (58.6%), while its zero-shot transfer (28.6% mSAP) already exceeds the fine-tuned performance of crYOLO (25.4%) and Topaz (26.2%), underscoring the value of physics-informed pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

FPicker departs from three established paradigms for filament tracing—box-based detection (crYOLO, YOLOv8), pixel-wise segmentation (Topaz, U-Net, SAM 2), and closed active contours (Deep Snake, CurveGCN)—by identifying specific geometric incompatibilities in each: ghost-center drift, topological fracturing at 2D crossovers, and endpoint shrinking from cyclic boundary conditions, respectively. Its core architectural lineage traces to CurveGCN and Deep Snake's graph-convolutional deformation, but it replaces periodic convolution with open-boundary replicate padding and substitutes box initializations with a center-endpoint representation that anchors directly on the filament's arc-length median. The auxiliary distance/angle fields draw on DeepLSD's dense geometric supervision, repurposed here as low-pass regularizers for the backbone rather than local pixel classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Filament tracing remains a manual bottleneck in Cryo-EM helical reconstruction for amyloid and other filamentous proteins, and this work demonstrates that explicitly modeling physical geometry (open curves, center-endpoint priors) can outperform both general-purpose foundation models and brute-force data scaling in signal-starved scientific imaging. The sim-to-real paradigm—training on a physics-based forward model (Cryo-Sim) and fine-tuning on as few as 10 real images—offers a practical path to reduce the expensive annotation burden in structural biology labs. Open questions include extending the framework to true 3D branching topologies, handling over-merging of aligned fragments, and validating the physical-prior approach on broader microscopy tasks beyond Cryo-EM filaments.
