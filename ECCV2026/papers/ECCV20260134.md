## Problem

The strongest vision-language models achieve broad visual reasoning through proprietary RL pipelines with non-public data and undisclosed reward designs, making their gains difficult to study or reproduce. Existing fully open RL efforts focus on narrow domains like visual math, and the paper shows that training on a single task category does not generalize to other visual capabilities. The central question is what it takes to build a broadly capable visual reasoner with a fully open, reproducible recipe.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260134/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a visual input (single image or set of images) paired with a text query and generates a structured response consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in an answer block. For discrete symbolic answer types (string, multiple choice, numeric, list, counting, ordering, search, web action), a single boxed expression is required within the answer block; for grounding tasks the answer is a bounding box in normalized [0,1000] coordinates, and for open-ended tasks the answer is free-form text scored by an LLM judge. The reward is a weighted combination of accuracy (0.8), format compliance (0.2), and a soft overlong penalty that linearly ramps in the last 2048 tokens before the context limit.

## Method

Vero applies single-stage on-policy RL using GSPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) with asymmetric clipping and no KL penalty on top of existing VLMs, eliminating the need for distillation warm starts or staged training. The training data, Vero-600K, is a 600K-sample mixture from 59 datasets spanning six task categories (Chart & OCR, STEM, Spatial & Action, Knowledge & Recognition, Grounding/Counting/Search, Captioning & Instruction Following), curated through multi-stage filtering including heuristic screening, manual quality control, LLM-based question filtering for ambiguity and verifiability, and answer normalization. A key design choice is uniform task-category sampling, which outperforms accuracy-, resolution-, or reasoning-length-weighted schemes, and a task-routed reward system that dispatches to type-specific verifiers (exact match, numeric tolerance via MATH-VERIFY, IoU/F1 Hungarian matching for grounding, weighted JSON field matching for web actions, rule-based constraint checks for instruction following, and LLM-as-judge for open-ended responses). The central empirical finding is that diverse task mixing eliminates negative cross-task transfer and elicits distinct but jointly learned reasoning patterns, with broad gains depending on learning all categories simultaneously rather than in isolation.

## Results

On VeroEval (30 benchmarks across 6 task categories), Vero-Qwen35-9B achieves the highest overall score of 74.4 (Avg@5), improving +2.9 over its initial model Qwen3.5-9B and winning 25 of 30 benchmarks. Among 8B models, Vero-Qwen3I-8B reaches 66.1 overall, surpassing Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking by +3.8 points, while Vero-Qwen3T-8B reaches 65.8 (+3.5 over Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking). Applying the recipe to the pretrained-only Qwen3.5-9B-Base yields +12.9 points (reaching 73.0), the second-highest score overall, without any SFT or distillation warm start. Gains are consistent across all six categories, with the largest improvements on Grounding, Counting & Search (+23.0 for the Base variant) and Captioning & Instruction Following (+9.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vero-Qwen3I-8B (66.1) outperforms Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (62.3) by +3.8 overall and on 25 of 30 benchmarks, while Vero-Qwen3T-8B (65.8) improves over the same Thinking model by +3.5 on 22/30 benchmarks, both without any distillation warm start. Against the distillation-based OpenMMReasoner-7B (51.6), which uses an 874K-example teacher, Vero-Qwen25-7B (57.2) wins by +5.6 overall, with the largest margins on Captioning & IF (+24.3) and Grounding, Counting & Search (+11.1). Vero-Qwen3I-8B exceeds OneThinker-8B (55.7) by +10.4 overall and wins all six category averages. Vero-Qwen35-9B (74.4) surpasses its initial model Qwen3.5-9B (71.5) by +2.9 and remains competitive with GPT-5 Nano on most categories, though GPT-5 Nano retains an edge on STEM (61.6 vs. 76.7 for Vero) and Knowledge & Recognition. In controlled training-curve comparisons (Figure 6), Vero-600K leads or matches the best prior open RL dataset (ViRL-39k, OpenMMReasoner-74k, LLaVA-OV1.5-RL-70k) on five of six categories throughout training, with the largest late-stage margins on Captioning & IF (+13.7) and Grounding, Counting & Search (+6.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO/GSPO lineage of group-based RL for language models, adapting sequence-level clipping (GSPO) and asymmetric clipping with soft overlong penalties (DAPO) to the multimodal setting. It departs sharply from prior open RL efforts for VLMs—OpenMMReasoner, VL-Rethinker, OneThinker, and Perception-R1—which each target a narrow domain (visual math, self-reflection, or perceptual grounding) and rely on teacher distillation warm starts. Vero's central methodological departure is treating broad visual reasoning as a single multi-task RL problem with a 600K-sample mixture across six categories and task-routed rewards, rather than a sequence of domain-specific RL stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing the full 600K training set, 30-benchmark evaluation suite, reward code, and trained checkpoints, Vero provides the first fully reproducible recipe for multi-domain visual RL, lowering the barrier for the community to study and extend open visual reasoning. The finding that single-task RL causes severe negative transfer (Captioning & IF drops of up to −35.5 points) and that uniform category weighting outperforms accuracy- or length-based schemes reframes multi-task RL as a distribution-design problem rather than a per-task optimization problem. Open questions include whether the six-category taxonomy is optimal, whether further scaling beyond 600K samples yields diminishing returns, and whether the observed chain-of-thought behavioral differences (e.g., 26× reasoning-length gap between Spatial & Action and Knowledge & Recognition) can be exploited for targeted inference-time interventions.
