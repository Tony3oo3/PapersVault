## Problem

VLA-based autonomous driving models trained via behavior cloning can only replicate expert-observed behaviors, leaving them brittle in out-of-distribution scenarios, while their reliance on sparse supervisory signals (text descriptions and trajectory waypoints) prevents learning of rich spatial-geometric scene representations. The paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by introducing dense world-modeling supervision and an uncertainty-based exploration mechanism for RL post-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262826/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes T+1 front-view camera frames (tokenized into 16×16 patches via a MAGVIT-v2 quantizer with an 8,192-entry codebook), a natural-language driving command, and ego-vehicle status projected through a learnable MLP. It jointly outputs continuous-valued future waypoints (x, y, θ over a 4-second horizon on NAVSIM) via a lightweight MLP head on transformer hidden states, plus F future RGB frames and F future depth maps as discrete token sequences. The trajectory head is decoupled from the discrete token vocabulary, allowing continuous waypoint prediction while sharing contextualized representations learned through the joint generation objective.

## Method

Built on the Show-o unified transformer with omni-attention (causal for text/ego tokens, full for image tokens), the model is trained in two stages. Stage 1 performs pre-training on image generation conditioned on ground-truth actions, followed by supervised fine-tuning that jointly predicts trajectories and generates future RGB/depth frames via a mask-token-prediction loss, providing dense visual and geometric supervision. Stage 2 applies GRPO-based RL where the world model's token-level prediction entropy over the MAGVIT-v2 codebook serves as an intrinsic novelty measure: high entropy signals out-of-distribution trajectories, and a safety-gated reward (exploration bonus added only when PDMS exceeds a threshold δ) steers the policy toward trajectories that are simultaneously novel and safe. The group-relative advantage formulation within GRPO naturally prioritizes good-and-novel trajectories over mere expert imitation, and LoRA is used to parameterize the RL updates.

## Results

On NAVSIM v1, ExploreVLA achieves a state-of-the-art PDMS of 93.7 with the best-of-N strategy (and 90.4 without), using only a single front-view camera. On NAVSIM v2, it attains an EPDMS of 88.8, surpassing the previous best (DriveVLA-W0 at 86.1) by 2.7 points. Notably, ExploreVLA outperforms multi-sensor methods (multi-camera + LiDAR) such as DriveSuprim (93.5) and Centaur (92.1) on NAVSIM v1 PDMS while using strictly less input modality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExploreVLA (single-view camera) achieves the highest NAVSIM v1 PDMS of 93.7 (best-of-N), edging out DriveSuprim (93.5, MC+LiDAR) and DriveVLA-W0† (93.0, SC), and clearly surpassing Centaur (92.1, MC+LiDAR) and AutoVLA† (92.1, MC). Without best-of-N, ExploreVLA scores 90.4, which is competitive with multi-view AutoVLA (89.1) and DriveVLA-W0 (90.2) while using fewer sensors. On NAVSIM v2, ExploreVLA leads with EPDMS 88.8, beating DriveVLA-W0 (86.1) by 2.7 points and DiffusionDriveV2 (85.5) by 3.3 points, and wins on 6 of 9 individual sub-metrics. Among single-view-camera methods, ExploreVLA consistently outperforms DrivingGPT (82.4), FSDrive (85.1), and PWM (88.1) on NAVSIM v1 PDMS. The key differentiator is that ExploreVLA's world-model-based exploration reward and dense RGB/depth supervision enable it to match or exceed multi-sensor pipelines with only a front camera.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Show-o unified understanding-and-generation architecture as its backbone and adopts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the RL optimization procedure, but its core departure is in how it couples these two components for autonomous driving. Prior VLA driving models (AutoVLA, DriveVLA-W0, FutureSightDrive) use world modeling either as a planning aid or a data-scaling mechanism, but none repurpose the world model's prediction uncertainty as an intrinsic exploration signal for RL. The paper also extends the sparse-supervision paradigm (text + trajectory waypoints) by adding dense RGB and depth image generation as auxiliary objectives, positioning itself at the intersection of the unified understanding-generation literature and the RL post-training trend in VLA models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two well-recognized limitations of VLA driving models—distributional brittleness from behavior cloning and sparse supervision—with a single unified mechanism, and the resulting SOTA on NAVSIM (93.7 PDMS, 88.8 EPDMS) validates the approach. The uncertainty-as-novelty-measure is a conceptually clean idea that could transfer to other domains where a learned world model can score trajectory novelty, though its practical impact is currently bounded by the offline/open-loop RL setting and single-front-camera input. Key open questions include whether the exploration signal remains useful under closed-loop RL where the agent encounters self-induced OOD states, and whether multi-view or LiDAR inputs would further amplify the dense supervision benefit.
