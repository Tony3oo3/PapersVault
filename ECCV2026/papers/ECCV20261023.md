## Problem

Fine-grained 3D part segmentation is difficult because different parts of the same object often share nearly identical local geometry, making boundaries ambiguous under noise or partial observations. Existing methods build local KNN patches and rely on deeper networks for discriminative features, while graph-based approaches capture only pairwise point relationships and miss higher-order structural patterns such as loops and cavities. No prior work integrates persistent homology directly into the attention mechanism of a graph network for part-level segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw 3D point clouds (xyz coordinates only, no RGB or other per-point attributes) as input. For ShapeNetPart, point clouds are downsampled to 2048 points for the backbone and further to 256 points for persistent homology computation; for S3DIS, 1024 points are used for PH. The output is a per-point segmentation label (50 part classes on ShapeNetPart, 13 scene categories on S3DIS), produced by a final linear classifier after topology-aware feature fusion.

## Method

TopoGAT is a plug-and-play refinement module that injects global topological structure into graph attention. It first computes persistent homology via an alpha-complex filtration, vectorizes the resulting persistence diagrams using a learnable higher-order Gaussian kernel with persistence-weighted max pooling, and broadcasts the fixed-length topology embedding to every point. The backbone (e.g., PointNet++, DGCNN, PointMAE) produces local point features, which are concatenated with the topology embedding to form initial node features. Each TopoEdge Attention layer builds a KNN graph, constructs edge features from node and spatial differences, and applies FiLM conditioning—where the topology vector generates per-channel scale and shift parameters that modulate edge features before softmax attention weighting and neighborhood aggregation. After L stacked layers, backbone features, topology embedding, and graph features are fused through a Squeeze-and-Excitation block that also conditions on the topology vector, followed by a linear classifier trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss. The key design choice is using FiLM to let topology actively regress attention weights rather than serving as a passive concatenated descriptor, enabling topology-guided feature propagation with only ~1.4M additional parameters.

## Results

On ShapeNetPart, the best variant PointMAE-TopoGAT achieves 86.7% Instance mIOU and 84.7% Class mIOU, the highest among all compared methods, with only 1.4M additional parameters atop the 27.1M backbone. On S3DIS (xyz-only input), PointMAE-TopoGAT reaches 61.9% Class Avg mIOU, a 1.5-point gain over the unmodified backbone, while PointNet++-TopoGAT improves by 3.3 points (52.5→55.8). Across all five tested backbones, TopoGAT consistently adds 0.5–1.0 points on ShapeNetPart and 1.5–3.3 points on S3DIS with a stable overhead of ~6–7 ms and 1–2 GB VRAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoGAT outperforms every baseline it is compared against on ShapeNetPart. Against the strongest transformer-based methods, PointMAE-TopoGAT (86.7% Inst mIOU) beats PointGPT-B (86.5%), PointGPT-S (86.2%), Point-MAE (86.1%), and ACT-PointGST (85.8%) by 0.2–0.9 points while using fewer total parameters than PointGPT-B (28.5M vs. 82.1M). On the plug-and-play comparison (Table 5), TopoGAT achieves 85.8%/85.9% Inst mIOU on PointNet++/DGCNN, surpassing the second-best SN-Adapter (85.3%/85.5%) and all data-augmentation-based methods (PointMixup, RSMix, SageMix, PointWOLF, PA Pooling) by 0.1–0.7 points. On S3DIS, TopoGAT yields the largest absolute gains on backbones without built-in topological structure: PointNet++ improves by 3.3 points (52.5→55.8) and PointMLP by 3.1 points (53.7→56.8), while the already-strong PointMAE backbone still gains 1.5 points (60.4→61.9). Notably, the lightweight DGCNN-TopoGAT (2.7M total params) matches or exceeds much larger models like Point-BERT (27.1M) and PointGPT-S (19.5M) in Instance mIOU, highlighting the parameter-efficiency of the topological attention mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopoGAT sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: graph attention networks for point clouds (GAT, DGCNN, GACNet) and topological data analysis for shape understanding (PersLay, TopoSeg, PHGCN). It departs from prior topology-based segmentation methods that treat persistent homology as a fixed descriptor or a training-time loss by instead using vectorized persistence diagrams to actively modulate pairwise attention weights through FiLM conditioning. The plug-and-play design also distinguishes it from data-augmentation-based refinement modules (PointWOLF, SageMix, SN-Adapter) that improve backbones without introducing explicit structural priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature (only 1.4M additional parameters) makes TopoGAT practically attractive for practitioners who want to boost existing segmentation pipelines without retraining from scratch, and the S3DIS results (up to 3.3 mIOU gain using only xyz coordinates) suggest the topological prior generalizes beyond object-level part segmentation to scene-level tasks. However, the ShapeNetPart improvements remain modest (~1%), the persistent homology computation adds non-trivial latency (up to 37 ms per sample at 1024 points), and it is unclear whether the alpha-complex filtration is optimal or whether the gains would persist on noisier, sparser real-world scans. An open question is whether the topological signal captured here is genuinely complementary to what sufficiently deep local-attention backbones already learn implicitly.
