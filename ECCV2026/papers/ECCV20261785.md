## Problem

Feed-forward SLAM systems face a fundamental trade-off: two-view inference provides low-latency tracking but limited geometric constraints prone to drift, while multi-view inference yields rich geometry but requires submap accumulation and suffers from configuration-dependent scale inconsistencies. Moreover, predictions from these two regimes reside in heterogeneous local coordinate systems with independent scales, making their naive combination non-trivial and requiring a new optimization formulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261785/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an uncalibrated RGB image stream (no intrinsics required) and samples keyframes at a fixed stride (3 for TUM RGB-D, 5 for 7-Scenes). It produces a globally consistent camera trajectory parameterized on Sim(3) (rotation, translation, and scale), per-view dense depth maps from both two-view and multi-view predictions, and a fused 3D point cloud. The trajectory is evaluated after Sim(3) alignment to ground truth, and reconstruction quality is assessed via Accuracy, Completion, and Chamfer Distance.

## Method

UniSim-SLAM combines a lightweight two-view frontend (using VGGT or STA for pairwise pose and depth) with a periodic multi-view backend that constructs submaps of 16 keyframes with 2-frame overlap. The core contribution is a unified multi-level factor graph on the Sim(3) manifold with three complementary edge types: temporal view-to-view edges enforcing pairwise odometry consistency, view-to-submap bridge edges that align submap-local poses to the global trajectory and anchor relative scale via per-view depth median ratios, and submap-to-submap tie and scale constraints that directly enforce similarity consistency between overlapping submaps. Submap poses are initialized using depth-statistics-based relative scale estimation to resolve the scale ambiguity between two-view and multi-view predictions. All variables (global keyframe poses and submap poses) are jointly optimized via Levenberg-Marquardt nonlinear least squares with Huber loss, enabling corrections from multi-view constraints to propagate across the full trajectory through the temporally connected backbone even when submaps do not overlap.

## Results

On TUM RGB-D (uncalibrated), UniSim-SLAM achieves 0.032 m ATE RMSE, reducing trajectory error by 38.5% over the prior best uncalibrated method (ViSTA-SLAM, 0.052 m). On 7-Scenes, it achieves 0.020 m ATE RMSE, a 45.9% improvement over VGGT-SLAM (0.037 m). For 3D reconstruction on 7-Scenes, UniSim-SLAM attains the best Accuracy (0.035) and Chamfer Distance (0.041) among uncalibrated methods while maintaining competitive Completion (0.046).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among uncalibrated feed-forward SLAM methods, UniSim-SLAM outperforms all competitors on both benchmarks. On 7-Scenes, it beats VGGT-SLAM (0.037→0.020 m, −45.9%), ViSTA-SLAM (0.049→0.020 m, −59.2%), MASt3R-SLAM (0.068→0.020 m, −70.6%), SLAM3R (0.079→0.020 m), and CUT3R (0.312→0.020 m). On TUM RGB-D, it surpasses ViSTA-SLAM (0.052→0.032 m, −38.5%), VGGT-SLAM (0.061→0.032 m), MASt3R-SLAM* (0.060→0.032 m), SLAM3R (0.648→0.032 m), and CUT3R (0.486→0.032 m). Against calibrated methods, UniSim-SLAM (0.020 m on 7-Scenes) even surpasses DROID-SLAM (0.024 m) and MASt3R-SLAM (0.044 m) despite not requiring known intrinsics. The largest gains appear on sequences with challenging scale variation (e.g., TUM 'floor', 7-Scenes 'chess'), where the unified Sim(3) factor graph's scale-anchoring and submap-to-submap constraints are most effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the classic SLAM architecture of lightweight two-view odometry in the frontend with periodic backend refinement, but transplants it into the feed-forward geometric foundation model setting (VGGT, MASt3R, DUSt3R). Prior feed-forward SLAM systems operate in isolation: two-view methods (MASt3R-SLAM, ViSTA-SLAM) chain pairwise Sim(3) predictions, while multi-view methods (VGGT-SLAM) register submaps on the SL(4) manifold. UniSim-SLAM departs by jointly optimizing both regimes in a single multi-level Sim(3) factor graph with three complementary edge types (view-to-view, view-to-submap with depth-statistics scale anchoring, and submap-to-submap tie/scale constraints), rather than treating them as separate alignment stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified Sim(3) formulation provides a principled template for integrating heterogeneous geometric predictions from different inference regimes, which will become increasingly relevant as new feed-forward models with varying view-set dependencies emerge. The ablation showing SOTA-comparable performance even without loop closure suggests the multi-level graph itself provides strong global consistency, reducing reliance on retrieval-based loop detection. However, the system is evaluated only on short indoor sequences (TUM, 7-Scenes), and the 197 ms frontend latency (driven by VGGT) remains a barrier to real-time deployment; scalability to long outdoor trajectories and integration with lighter-weight frontends remain open questions.
