## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud generation methods are limited to single objects or bounded indoor scenes and cannot handle the three core challenges of city-scale generation: the intractable scale of ~10⁵ points per 150 m tile, the need for independently generated tiles to join seamlessly without post-hoc blending, and the partial observability of satellite imagery that sees horizontal surfaces but not vertical façades. No prior method generates dense, colored point clouds at city scale conditioned on widely available remote sensing data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 satellite image of a 150 m × 150 m tile as primary input, with optional 6-class semantic segmentation and a normalized DSM as auxiliary conditions (all spatially aligned at the same resolution). Structured condition dropout during training allows any subset of modalities at inference. The output is a colored point cloud of exactly 100,000 points per tile (25² cells × 160 points each), with coordinates in [-1,1]³ where the vertical axis is independently scaled to preserve building-height detail.

## Method

GridFlow is a three-stage pipeline built around a Grid-Aligned VAE that compresses each tile into a topology-preserving 64×25×25 latent grid where each token maps to a fixed 6 m × 6 m physical region, using Morton-curve-sorted SSM (Mamba) encoders per cell and Swin windowed attention for cross-cell communication. A conditional rectified flow model (Transformer velocity network with AdaLN global conditioning and cross-attention local conditioning) synthesizes geometry latents from fused multi-modal features, with the key innovation being a latent-space edge consistency loss that constrains only 2 boundary columns of 64-dim tokens between adjacent tiles—implicitly aligning ~6,400 decoded boundary points at negligible cost and enabling fully independent tile generation at inference. An orientation-aware dual-path DDPM colorizer then handles the observability asymmetry by applying satellite-guided 3D-to-2D cross-attention to horizontal surfaces and synthesizing vertical façade textures from a learned pattern bank modulated by Fourier positional features and semantic embeddings.

## Results

On the Melbourne test set (13,363 tiles), GridFlow achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.00272, F-Score of 0.884, JSD of 0.0098, COV-CD of 51.8%, and 1-NNA-CD of 46.5% (ideal ≈ 50%), outperforming all four adapted baselines across every geometry metric. In the visual rendering comparison, GridFlow attains SSIM 0.70 and FID 58.52 against the conditioning satellite image, substantially exceeding CityDreamer (SSIM 0.26, FID 155.27) and Skyfall-GS (SSIM 0.38, FID 115.68). Cross-dataset evaluation on HoliCity (London) without retraining yields CD 0.00394 and F-Score 0.783, confirming moderate degradation under domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GridFlow dominates all four geometry baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor PVD [53], GridFlow reduces Chamfer Distance by 29% (0.00272 vs. 0.00382), improves F-Score by 9 percentage points (0.884 vs. 0.794), and nearly halves JSD (0.0098 vs. 0.0370); COV-CD more than doubles (51.8% vs. 24.4%), indicating far better distributional coverage. SemCity [20] produces recognizable urban layouts but suffers from coarse voxelization artifacts, yielding JSD 0.144 and COV-CD only 4.1%. DPM [28] and PointFlow [47], designed for single-object or small-scene generation, fail to capture city-scale structural complexity, producing amorphous geometry with CD values 7× and 44× higher than GridFlow respectively. In the visual rendering comparison, GridFlow (SSIM 0.70, FID 58.52) substantially outperforms CityDreamer [44] (SSIM 0.26, FID 155.27) and Skyfall-GS [19] (SSIM 0.38, FID 115.68), though it trails the Google Earth reference on PSNR (22.82 vs. 26.11), which is expected since GridFlow is conditioned on that same satellite image. On the cross-dataset London evaluation, GridFlow maintains its lead over all baselines (CD 0.00394 vs. PVD 0.00542, SemCity 0.00613), confirming that the structured latent representation generalizes across urban morphologies.

## Relation to Prior Work

GridFlow sits at the intersection of three research threads: object-level point cloud generation (PointFlow, DPM, LION), outdoor scene diffusion (SemCity, XCube, LiDiff), and satellite-conditioned city synthesis (Sat2City, CityDreamer, GaussianCity). It departs from prior city-scale work by generating explicit colored point clouds rather than voxel occupancy, neural fields, or Gaussian primitives, and from prior point cloud generators by operating on 100K-point tiles with spatially grounded latent grids rather than centered single objects. The rectified flow formulation follows Lipman et al. and Liu et al., while the topology-preserving grid-aligned VAE and latent-space edge consistency constraint are novel architectural choices that replace the post-hoc blending or overlapping-tile strategies used by SynCity, TRELLISWorld, and Extend3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The City3D-MultiGen benchmark (163K tiles from two cities with aligned point clouds, satellite imagery, semantic maps, and DSM) fills a clear gap in standardized evaluation for conditional city-scale 3D generation, and the seamless tiling via latent-space edge consistency offers a practical path to arbitrarily large urban extents without post-hoc registration. Key limitations include sequential three-stage training that may accumulate errors, façade colorization that relies entirely on learned priors (no vertical supervision from satellite), and moderate cross-city degradation (CD 0.00272 → 0.00394 Melbourne → London). Open questions include whether joint end-to-end fine-tuning can close the VAE-reconstruction-to-generated gap, whether street-level imagery can improve vertical surface colorization, and whether the fixed 150 m tile / 6 m cell resolution generalizes to high-rise or rural morphologies.
