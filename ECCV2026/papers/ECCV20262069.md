## Problem

Deep learning-based change detection models are highly sensitive to non-semantic appearance variations such as illumination shifts, shadows, and atmospheric changes, which cause false alarms in real-world remote sensing imagery. This fragility stems from two compounding issues: (1) standard vision encoders pretrained on single-view tasks produce appearance-sensitive features, and (2) existing synthetic CD datasets emphasize semantic region edits while neglecting realistic global non-semantic perturbations in unchanged regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262069/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes bi-temporal 256×256 RGB remote sensing image pairs and, depending on the task head, outputs a pixel-wise semantic change map (20 classes), a binary change mask, or a free-text change caption. During the self-distillation pretraining phase, the student encoder additionally processes diffusion-perturbed single-temporal images (generated via DIR relighting and InstructPix2Pix text-guided editing) alongside their clean counterparts, requiring no change labels or segmentation supervision. The final detector integrates the distilled VFME encoder into the Change3D video-encoder–universal-decoder architecture, fusing deep semantic features with shallow X3D representations.

## Method

SCDistill combines two complementary mechanisms: a diffusion-based perturbation simulation pipeline that augments synthetic CD datasets (FSC-180k) with realistic non-semantic variations using DIR for solar-angle illumination changes and InstructPix2Pix with predefined prompts for weather/atmospheric edits, and a semantic-invariant self-distillation strategy where a frozen DINOv3 teacher encodes clean images while a student encoder encodes their perturbed counterparts, with L2 regression loss enforcing feature alignment across conditions. The distilled student encoder internalizes semantic invariance without any change-label supervision, then serves as the backbone in a Change3D-based detector pretrained on the perturbation-augmented synthetic data and fine-tuned on real-world benchmarks. Ablations show the two components form a synergistic closed loop: perturbation simulation provides diverse cross-condition training signals, while self-distillation converts those signals into disturbance-resistant representations, yielding gains (e.g., +2.78 Fscd) that exceed the sum of individual contributions.

## Results

On the primary semantic change detection task, SCDistill achieves 65.64% Fscd and 25.12% SeK on SECOND, and 74.29% Fscd and 28.38% SeK on HRSCD, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art Change3D by +2.81% Fscd and +2.14% SeK on SECOND. For binary change detection, SCDistill reaches 78.97% F1 on CLCD, 91.88% F1 on LEVIR-CD, and 94.79% F1 on WHU-CD, all setting new records. On change captioning (DUBAI-CC), it attains 72.39 BLEU-1, 29.48 METEOR, and 95.59 CIDEr, with the CIDEr gain over Change3D being particularly large (+9.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCDistill outperforms all 28 compared baselines across all three tasks. On semantic CD (SECOND), it leads the closest competitor Change3D [77] by +2.81% Fscd and +2.14% SeK, and surpasses Changen2 [71] by +2.85% Fscd; on HRSCD, the Fscd margin over Change3D narrows to +1.00% but SeK still gains +1.53%. For binary CD, SCDistill edges out Change3D on all three datasets (e.g., +0.94% F1 on CLCD, +0.26% F1 on LEVIR-CD, +0.23% F1 on WHU-CD) and also beats Changen2 [71] by +0.97% F1 on CLCD. On change captioning (DUBAI-CC), the CIDEr improvement over Change3D is the most striking at +9.40 (95.59 vs. 86.19), while METEOR gains +2.42 and ROUGE-L gains +3.61, indicating substantially more semantically faithful descriptions. Notably, SCDistill also surpasses the strong PromptCC [76] baseline on all captioning metrics, confirming that the semantic-invariant encoder benefits downstream generative tasks as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Change3D (CVPR 2025), adopting its video-encoder–universal-decoder architecture as the change detection backbone, and on vision foundation models (DINOv2/DINOv3) as semantic feature extractors. It departs from prior CD methods in two key ways: (1) it introduces a diffusion-based perturbation simulation pipeline (using DIR for illumination and InstructPix2Pix for weather) to inject realistic non-semantic variations into synthetic training data, addressing a gap where existing synthetic datasets (FSC-180k, Changen2, HySCDG) focus on semantic transitions but ignore appearance-level disturbances; and (2) it applies semantic-invariant self-distillation, where a frozen VFME teacher on clean images guides a student encoder on perturbed inputs, a strategy not previously applied to bi-temporal change detection. The work also connects to the broader self-supervised representation learning literature by repurposing distillation as a robustness mechanism rather than a compression tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and persistent failure mode in remote sensing CD—false alarms triggered by illumination, shadow, and atmospheric shifts—by tackling it from both the data side (perturbation simulation) and the representation side (self-distillation), yielding consistent SOTA gains across semantic CD, binary CD, and change captioning without task-specific architectural changes. The distillation step requires no change labels, making it scalable to large unlabeled remote sensing collections, and the perturbation pipeline (8 hours on one GPU) is a reusable preprocessing tool for any synthetic CD dataset. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to SAR or multi-spectral imagery where non-semantic variations are more complex, whether the reliance on a strong VFME teacher limits applicability in domains with weaker pre-trained encoders, and whether the synergistic gain observed in ablations holds under more extreme or compound perturbations.
