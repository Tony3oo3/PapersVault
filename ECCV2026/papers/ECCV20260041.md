## Problem

Existing unified multimodal motion generation frameworks treat all subjects as morphologically equivalent, ignoring how body shape, gender, and limb proportions systematically influence kinematics and dynamics. No prior framework incorporates subject-specific bio-morphological information into generation across heterogeneous input modalities (text, music, video), leaving a gap between who is moving and what motion is produced.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260041/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous driving signals—text prompts, music waveforms, video clips, or 2D keypoints—alongside a subject shape specification (binary gender label and 10-dimensional SMPL β coefficients). Motion is represented in a rotation/translation-invariant canonical frame using 6D rotation encodings for joint angles, root height, root planar velocities, and vertical angular velocity; an auxiliary 6D camera-aligned root orientation parameter is appended only for pose estimation tasks. In shape estimation mode, the explicit shape input is replaced by a learnable token, and the network additionally outputs predicted gender and β by averaging predictions across all denoising timesteps.

## Method

Odoriko employs a data-domain diffusion model with direct x₀ prediction, built on a Shape-Aware Motion Transformer (SAMT) that uses a staged hybrid architecture inspired by FLUX and MMAudio. The first half consists of Multimodal Motion Transformer blocks where motion tokens attend jointly to text tokens, a global conditioning token, and a gender token (learnable embedding), establishing cross-modal semantic grounding; the second half comprises Motion-Centric Refinement blocks where text tokens are removed and a β token (MLP-projected from raw SMPL coefficients) is injected to modulate fine-grained biomechanical details. This two-stage shape injection mirrors SMPL's hierarchical dependency—gender selects the template, β parameterizes within it. Modality-specific frozen encoders (T5+CLIP for text, Jukebox+EDGE for music, TRAM for video, DWPose+MLP adapters for 2D keypoints) decouple feature extraction from motion synthesis, with temporally aligned features added to motion tokens via lightweight MLP projections. Training uses the standard DDPM objective plus auxiliary gender classification and β regression losses (weighted 0.1) in estimation mode, and inference employs the UniPC higher-order predictor-corrector sampler for efficient sampling.

## Results

On the HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark, Odoriko (44M params) achieves R-Precision Top-3 of 0.805, FID of 0.103, and MMDist of 2.930, outperforming the primary multimodal competitor GENMO (504M params) while using ~11× fewer parameters. On FineDance music-to-dance, Odoriko attains the best FID_k (37.73) and FID_g (25.36) among multimodal methods and the highest BAS (0.2496) overall, surpassing the dedicated LODGE++ (BAS 0.2423). On EMDB video-to-motion, Odoriko achieves PA-MPJPE of 41.1 mm and PVE of 82.9 mm, beating GENMO (42.5 / 84.8). In the novel shape-fidelity evaluation, Odoriko reaches 92.0% gender accuracy and 71.8% β-bin accuracy, far exceeding the best non-shape-aware baseline (MDM: 80.1% / 41.9%) and the prior shape-aware method ShapeMove (74.5% / 52.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest multimodal competitor GENMO (504M params), Odoriko (44M params) wins on every reported text-to-motion metric: FID 0.103 vs. 0.216, MMDist 2.930 vs. 3.466, and R-Precision Top-3 0.805 vs. 0.632, while also achieving lower MPJPE (70.2 vs. 73.0 mm) and PVE (82.9 vs. 84.8 mm) on EMDB video-to-motion. Among task-specific text-to-motion specialists, Odoriko is competitive with MoMask (FID 0.103 vs. 0.045) and MoLA (FID 0.103 vs. 0.115) but trails them slightly on FID; however, it matches or exceeds them on R-Precision Top-3 (0.805 vs. 0.807 / 0.805) and MMDist (2.930 vs. 2.958 / 3.008). On music-to-dance, Odoriko surpasses all multimodal baselines (MotionCraft FID_k 53.61, VerMo 42.89, M³GPT 42.66) and the best dedicated method LODGE++ (FID_k 40.77, BAS 0.2423), achieving FID_k 37.73 and BAS 0.2496. In the shape-fidelity evaluation, Odoriko dramatically outperforms all non-shape-aware baselines (e.g., MDM gender accuracy 80.1%, β-bin 41.9%) and the prior shape-aware method ShapeMove (74.5%, 52.0%), reaching 92.0% and 71.8% respectively, with β PVE of 4.42 mm versus 7.71–9.06 mm for others. On 3DPW video-to-motion, Odoriko achieves the best PA-MPJPE (41.1 mm) among all methods including specialists like WHAM (35.9 mm) and GVHMR (36.2 mm), though it trails them on MPJPE (69.9 vs. 55.6–57.8 mm), reflecting the trade-off of a unified generative framework versus dedicated regression models.

## Relation to Prior Work

Odoriko builds on the MM-DiT architecture (Esser et al., ICML 2024) as its diffusion backbone, adapting the unified self-attention paradigm from image synthesis to human motion. It sits at the intersection of two previously separate threads: multimodal motion generation (GENMO, MCM, MotionCraft) that handles text/music/video but treats all subjects as morphologically identical, and shape-aware motion modeling (ShapeMove, AttrMoGen, HUMOS) that incorporates body shape but only within a single input modality. The key departure is the staged shape injection strategy—gender at the multimodal grounding stage and β at the kinematic refinement stage—which mirrors SMPL's hierarchical template structure and has no direct precedent in prior motion generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying shape-conditioned generation and shape estimation in one model, Odoriko addresses a practical gap in animation, VR, and robotics pipelines where the same motion intent must produce anatomically consistent output for different body types. The introduced shape-fidelity evaluation protocol (gender accuracy, β-bin accuracy, β-PVE) provides the field with a missing measurement axis beyond standard FID/diversity metrics. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SMPL's 10-dimensional β space to richer morphological models, how it behaves with non-binary or continuous gender representations, and whether the shape-estimation mode remains reliable for occluded or multi-person video inputs beyond the tested benchmarks.
