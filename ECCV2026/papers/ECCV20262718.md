## Problem

Roadside monocular 3D detection relies on fixed camera extrinsics, but real deployments experience both transient jitter and persistent drift that are amplified by projective geometry, causing severe BEV feature misalignment that worsens with range. Existing robustness methods either stabilize BEV lifting under fixed calibration or bypass calibration entirely, leaving no mechanism to explicitly correct range-dependent extrinsic drift within the projection operator while preserving metric consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262718/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single roadside RGB image at 864×1536 resolution together with nominal camera extrinsics (rotation R and translation t) as input. It outputs 3D bounding boxes (center, size, heading) in the ego frame, region-specific 6-DoF pose offsets (roll, pitch, yaw, tx, ty, tz) for near and far segments, and a learnable range boundary that partitions the scene. The BEV grid spans 102.4 m in width and 140 m in length, and an auxiliary reprojection branch (discarded at inference) uses 2D box annotations to supervise the extrinsic correction.

## Method

RECO introduces a region-aware module that encodes backbone features and nominal extrinsics into a shared latent, then predicts two 6-DoF SE(3) pose offsets (one for near-range, one for far-range) and a range boundary via a residual tanh head. A soft compensation projection module left-composes each offset with the original extrinsic to produce two corrected projection matrices, back-projects BEV cell centers into the image, and blends the resulting geometry fields with a differentiable sigmoid gate parameterized by the distance-to-boundary ratio, ensuring a continuous projection field. BEV features are then constructed by scatter-based pooling of warped image features onto the discrete grid, followed by a standard BEV detection head. Training jointly optimizes the 3D detection loss and an auxiliary reprojection loss that penalizes the L2 discrepancy between 2D boxes projected from 3D ground-truth corners (using the compensated geometry) and 2D annotations, providing direct supervision for the extrinsic correction without requiring explicit pose labels.

## Results

On DAIR-V2X-I under yaw perturbation N(0,0.5), RECO achieves 70.46/63.01/63.03 AP for Car (easy/moderate/hard), surpassing the strongest baseline BEVHeight++ (69.69/58.30/60.38) by 0.77/4.72/2.65 AP. Under z-axis perturbation, the margin widens dramatically: RECO reaches 72.62/64.08/64.17 AP for Car versus 55.80/47.17/47.15 for BEVHeight++, a gap of ~14–17 AP. On Rope3D, RECO leads by approximately 15 AP on Car under both yaw and z perturbations (e.g., 65.39 vs. 50.37 easy under yaw; 65.63 vs. 50.76 easy under z). RECO also runs at 17.66 FPS, roughly 2× faster than the strongest baselines (7.78–7.80 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECO outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both datasets and both perturbation types. Against the strongest baseline BEVHeight++ (TPAMI 2025), RECO gains +0.77 to +2.65 AP on Car under yaw and a much larger +16.8 to +17.0 AP under z-axis translation on DAIR-V2X-I; on Rope3D the yaw gap is ~15 AP (65.39 vs. 50.37 easy) and the z gap is ~15 AP (65.63 vs. 50.76 easy). Against BEVSpread (CVPR 2024), RECO leads by +3.4 to +6.9 AP on Car under yaw and +17.9 to +18.7 AP under z. CoBEV (TIP 2024) and HeightFormer (TGRS 2024) are substantially weaker, with Car AP under yaw of only 31.43 and 38.64 respectively versus RECO's 70.46. BEVHeight (CVPR 2023) is competitive on Car under yaw (65.56 vs. 70.46) but falls far behind under z (58.57 vs. 72.62). Notably, RECO also runs at 17.66 FPS, roughly 2× faster than BEVSpread, BEVHeight++, and HeightFormer (7.78–8.67 FPS), while remaining slightly slower than BEVHeight (19.81 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

RECO sits at the intersection of two research threads: BEV-based roadside 3D detection (BEVHeight, BEVSpread, HeightFormer, BEVHeight++), which improves robustness by stabilizing image-to-BEV lifting and pooling but treats camera extrinsics as fixed, and learned SE(3)/6-DoF calibration methods (CalibNet, LCCNet, cost-volume-based registration), which estimate pose corrections from cross-modal cues but are not integrated into range-dependent BEV projection. The paper's key conceptual departure is the observation that extrinsic perturbation effects are inherently range-dependent—near-range misalignment is translation-dominated while far-range misalignment is rotation-sensitive—and that a single global 6-DoF correction cannot simultaneously fit both regimes. By repurposing the SE(3) offset formulation from the calibration literature and embedding it inside the BEV projection pipeline with a learned range boundary and differentiable soft gating, RECO carves new ground without depending on any single prior architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Roadside camera deployments inevitably face thermal drift, structural deformation, and vibration-induced jitter, making extrinsic robustness a practical deployment bottleneck rather than a theoretical concern. The paper demonstrates that range-aware compensation is not merely a marginal refinement but a necessary design choice: the ablation reveals that a naive global 6-DoF correction is actively harmful (worse than no correction) under zero-mean perturbations, while the soft-gated piecewise correction consistently helps. Open questions include whether the two-region partitioning generalizes to more complex multi-camera infrastructure networks, whether the learned range boundary remains stable under domain shift, and how the approach scales when multiple cameras with independent drift must maintain global geometric consistency.
