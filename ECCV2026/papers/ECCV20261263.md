## Problem

Existing road network extraction methods rely on discrete polylines that only guarantee C0 continuity and require dense vertex sampling to approximate curves, limiting geometric fidelity and editability for downstream applications like autonomous driving. While parametric Bezier curves offer analytic smoothness and topological compactness, their extraction is bottlenecked by the absence of curve-level vector ground truth and the fragility of multi-stage heuristic vectorization pipelines that are sensitive to mask noise and dataset-dependent thresholds.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB satellite tiles (standardized to 1 m/pixel; 2048×2048 for City-Scale, 400×400 resampled for SpaceNet) and first produces a road segmentation mask via a fine-tuned SAM2. The output is a parametric Bezier Graph where each edge is a cubic Bezier curve defined by four control points (endpoints anchored to node positions, intermediates parameterized by chord projection scalars and perpendicular offsets) plus an optimizable stroke width, yielding a vector map with 73.2% fewer edges than the GT polyline. Optimization renders at 512×512 resolution, and the final graph can be uniformly sampled into polylines for standard TOPO/APLS evaluation.

## Method

DOGE reframes road extraction as a differentiable rendering-driven iterative refinement of a Bezier Graph, decoupling the problem into two complementary modules that alternate in a discrete-continuous loop. DiffAlign serializes each Bezier edge into a closed polygon, passes it through a differentiable rasterizer (built on DiffVG), and backpropagates a composite loss—combining pixel-wise coverage against the SAM2 mask with analytic geometric priors (overlap penalty, G1 tangent continuity, offset regularization, and spacing regularization)—to continuously optimize node positions, widths, and chord-based control-point parameters. TopoAdapt applies a prioritized pipeline of discrete operators (node merging, T-junction creation, collinear edge merging, invalid edge pruning, and road addition from uncovered regions) to evolve the graph's topology, preventing structural bottlenecks that would paralyze gradient-based geometric optimization. The chord-based reparameterization constrains intermediate control points to a 1D projection plus 1D perpendicular offset along the endpoint chord, reducing degrees of freedom and preventing self-intersection degeneracies while preserving sufficient shape flexibility. The loop terminates via early stopping when both topology and rendering loss stabilize, requiring no curve GT throughout.

## Results

DOGE achieves state-of-the-art TOPO F1 of 84.58 and APLS of 73.48 on SpaceNet, and TOPO F1 of 80.59 and APLS of 70.24 on City-Scale, leading all baselines on both comprehensive metrics. The gains are driven primarily by a substantial Recall improvement (+3.19 over SAMRoad++ on SpaceNet, +4.01 over SAMRoad++ on City-Scale) while maintaining competitive Precision (93.55 on SpaceNet). On the GlobalScale benchmark, DOGE also improves over SAMRoad++ on both in-domain (64.39/65.15 vs. 62.33/62.19) and out-domain (51.47/44.80 vs. 48.34/43.17) splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOGE outperforms all five baselines on TOPO F1 and APLS across both SpaceNet and City-Scale. Against the strongest prior method SAMRoad++, DOGE gains +3.01 TOPO F1 and +0.04 APLS on SpaceNet, and +0.58 TOPO F1 and +1.90 APLS on City-Scale, with the advantage coming almost entirely from Recall (78.43 vs. 72.23 on SpaceNet; 77.40 vs. 73.39 on City-Scale). On Precision, DOGE is competitive on SpaceNet (93.55 vs. SAMRoad++'s 93.68, a negligible gap) but trails SAMRoad (90.47) and SAMRoad++ (88.39) on City-Scale, suggesting the method trades some precision for substantially higher recall. Against the older polyline-based methods (Sat2Graph, RNGDet, RNGDet++), DOGE leads by 1.8–3.6 points in TOPO F1 and 5.7–7.1 points in APLS on SpaceNet, confirming that the Bezier curve representation and differentiable optimization yield meaningful topological improvements over discrete polyline approaches. The paper further validates that gains are not solely from the SAM2 segmentation backbone: under identical SAM1 masks, DOGE still achieves 83.20 TOPO F1 versus 80.08 (SAMRoad) and 79.68 (SAMRoad++).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant polyline-based paradigm in road network extraction (Sat2Graph, RNGDet, SAMRoad) by introducing a parametric Bezier Graph representation, drawing on the Bezier curve formulations established in HD lane mapping (e.g., Blayney et al. 2024) but adapting them to city-scale SD maps via a novel chord-based reparameterization that decouples per-edge control points. The geometric optimization engine is built directly on DiffVG's differentiable rasterization, repurposing a technique previously applied to unstructured vector graphics (font synthesis, image vectorization) for the first time in topology-aware road network reconstruction. The discrete-continuous coupling—alternating gradient-based geometric alignment (DiffAlign) with rule-based topological operators (TopoAdapt)—is the paper's core architectural departure from both purely heuristic vectorization pipelines and unconstrained differentiable rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for curve-level ground truth (which is ambiguous, expensive, and dataset-dependent), DOGE opens a path toward GT-free vector road reconstruction that can benefit automatically from improving segmentation backbones like SAM2. The 73% reduction in edge density and the analytic G1 smoothness have direct practical value for downstream applications like autonomous driving (OpenDRIVE compliance) and graph-based routing. However, the method remains an offline per-image optimization (302 min for the SpaceNet test set on a single GPU), and it struggles with severe occlusions and multilevel interchanges—open questions that limit immediate deployment. Whether the discrete-continuous coupling generalizes to other GT-free vector reconstruction tasks (e.g., building footprints, utility networks) is an important direction the paper gestures toward but does not explore.
