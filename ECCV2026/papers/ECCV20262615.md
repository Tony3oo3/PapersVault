## Problem

Existing cloud removal methods optimize for pixel-level visual fidelity (PSNR, SSIM) without explicit constraints on preserving task-relevant semantic structures, causing semantic drift that degrades downstream interpretation tasks like segmentation and building extraction. Additionally, generative approaches (diffusion, flow matching) initialize from pure noise, lacking observation anchoring that produces geographically inconsistent or hallucinated structures in heavily occluded regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262615/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single cloudy RGB satellite image as input and produces a cloud-free RGB reconstruction at the same spatial resolution. The cloudy observation x_c is consumed not merely as a conditioning signal but as a structural anchor embedded directly in the generative trajectory, so the network receives the current state x_t, timestep t, and x_c jointly at every integration step. Downstream evaluation pairs the reconstruction with a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 encoder and lightweight task-specific decoders (classification, building extraction, segmentation, height estimation) trained on clear images.

## Method

GACR combines two mechanisms: (1) OAR-Flow reformulates CR as a deterministic probability-flow ODE whose forward interpolant is x_t = α_t x_* + β_t x_c + σ_t ε, anchoring the trajectory to the cloudy observation rather than pure noise; an HDiT-based network predicts the velocity field conditioned on x_c, and inference integrates from t=T to t=0 in a few steps. (2) GCPA extracts intermediate bottleneck features from the HDiT backbone, projects them into a pretrained DINOv3 VFM feature space via an Adaptive Projector (rearrangement + adaptive pooling + MLP), and enforces patch-wise cosine similarity against the VFM encoding of the clean reference (GCI Loss). The unified training objective L = L_vel + λ·L_GCI jointly supervises pixel-level flow dynamics and semantic-manifold consistency, with λ=0.5 and patch size p=2 as defaults. This observation-anchored residual formulation shortens the generative trajectory (~5× faster convergence than diffusion baselines) while the VFM alignment suppresses semantic drift in heavily occluded regions.

## Results

GACR-SAT/1 achieves the best PSNR across all six CR datasets, with headline gains of up to 3.3 dB over the strongest baseline (EMRDM) on Vaihingen-CR-thin (36.918 vs. 33.620) and 3.7 dB over DFCFormer on Vaihingen-CR-thick (34.048 vs. 30.396). On the real-world CUHKCR-EXT-CS benchmark, GACR-SAT/1 reaches 24.354 dB PSNR, surpassing the best baseline (DFCFormer, 23.876) by 0.5 dB. In downstream evaluation, GACR-SAT/2 attains the highest classification accuracy (0.833 on CLS-1) and lowest height-estimation RMSE (1.891 on HE-1), while also achieving the best segmentation mIoU (0.750 on SEG-1) among all CR methods. The complete GACR model converges approximately 5× faster than EMRDM in terms of training steps to reach comparable PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GACR outperforms all six baselines on every dataset in terms of PSNR. Against the strongest generative baseline EMRDM [27], GACR-SAT/1 gains 0.2–3.3 dB depending on cloud thickness, with the largest margin on Vaihingen-CR-thin (36.918 vs. 33.620) and Vaihingen-CR-thick (34.048 vs. 28.979), confirming the advantage of observation-anchored flow over mean-reverting diffusion under heavy occlusion. DFCFormer [44] is the closest denoising-based competitor, achieving the best PSNR among non-generative methods on Vaihingen-CR-thick (30.396), yet GACR still leads by 3.7 dB on that dataset. On the real-world CUHKCR-EXT benchmarks, GACR-SAT/1 edges out DFCFormer by 0.5 dB (CS) and 0.3 dB (GZ), while MPRNet [32] and AST [47] trail by 2–3 dB, reflecting the limitations of residual-prediction assumptions under thick clouds. MambaIR [14] and Restormer [46] are consistently outperformed by 1–3 dB across all synthetic datasets, with the gap widening under thick-cloud conditions where their Gaussian-noise priors break down. In downstream tasks, GACR-SAT/2 surpasses the best baseline (AST) by 2.0% on CLS-1 accuracy and by 0.1–0.2 RMSE on height estimation, while achieving the highest mIoU (0.750) on segmentation, demonstrating that the reconstruction gains translate into meaningful semantic preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GACR builds directly on the flow-matching framework of Lipman et al. (ICLR 2023) and the mean-reverting denoising formulation of EMRDM (CVPR 2025), but departs from both by anchoring the generative trajectory to the cloudy observation rather than pure Gaussian noise, reformulating cloud removal as a residual inversion process. The HDiT pixel-space backbone (Crowson et al., ICML 2024) provides the architectural substrate, while the GCPA module introduces a novel VFM-based semantic alignment loss that has no direct precedent in prior CR literature. Conceptually, the paper shifts the optimization paradigm from pixel-fidelity-oriented restoration to interpretation-oriented generation, a framing that extends beyond the denoising-based and GAN/diffusion-based CR methods surveyed in the related work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in the remote sensing pipeline: CR outputs that look visually plausible can still degrade downstream segmentation, classification, and height estimation, and GACR demonstrates that explicit semantic manifold constraints (via GCPA) close this gap consistently across twelve tasks. The 5× convergence speedup over diffusion-based baselines is practically significant for large-scale Earth observation workflows. However, four of six evaluation datasets are synthetic, limiting confidence in real-world generalization, and the reliance on a frozen VFM for semantic guidance raises open questions about how well the approach transfers to domains where VFM priors are weak (e.g., novel land-cover types or extreme atmospheric conditions). Whether the observation-anchored formulation extends naturally to multi-temporal or multi-sensor CR remains unexplored.
