## Problem

Prior unsupervised temporal action segmentation methods learn deterministic frame embeddings, causing the iterative optimal-transport pseudo-label estimation and gradient-descent training loop to quickly converge to local optima where embeddings overfit to incorrect pseudo-labels. This paper addresses the gap by proposing probabilistic (Gaussian) frame embeddings that explicitly model representation uncertainty, enabling more robust pseudo-label generation during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260107/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-extracted per-frame video features (256 uniformly sampled frames per video; IDT features for Breakfast and 50Salads, dataset-specific features for YTI and Desktop Assembly) along with the known number of action classes K per activity. It produces per-frame action segmentation labels via optimal transport at inference, and during training outputs a full Gaussian distribution (D-dimensional mean and diagonal covariance) per frame, from which M=3 Monte Carlo samples are drawn to generate diverse pseudo-labels. At inference only the mean is used, so no additional computational cost is incurred over a deterministic baseline.

## Method

The core contribution replaces deterministic frame embeddings with probabilistic ones modeled as Gaussians with diagonal covariance, predicted by a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) operating on a temporally weighted graph where edge weights are cosine similarities between adjacent frame features. During training, the reparameterization trick samples M=3 embeddings from the learned Gaussian, and an uncertainty-based cross-entropy loss (Eq. 11) averages the standard cross-entropy over these samples, preventing the model from overfitting to a single noisy OT pseudo-label. The GCN is preferred over MLP or TCN because it adaptively adjusts temporal weights based on input features rather than using fixed convolution kernels. The overall training follows the EM paradigm of ASOT: alternating between OT (combined Kantorovich and Gromov-Wasserstein with unbalanced regularization) for pseudo-label estimation on sampled embeddings and gradient descent on the uncertainty-aware loss, with K-means-initialized action prototypes updated jointly.

## Results

PEOT achieves the best MoF across all four datasets: 60.7 on Breakfast, 55.4 on YouTube Instructional, 64.9 on 50Salads (Eval), and 71.2 on Desktop Assembly. It outperforms the state of the art on 9 out of 12 metric/dataset combinations, with the largest gains over ASOT being +5.6 MoF (9.4%) on 50Salads and +7.8 F1 (11.5%) on Desktop Assembly. Compared to the direct baseline ASOT, PEOT improves MoF by up to 20.7% (+12.2) and F1 by up to 19.0% (+12.1) on Desktop Assembly, and compared to VASOT, MoF improves by up to 16.5% (+8.8) and F1 by up to 8.4% (+5.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ASOT [38] (the primary baseline), PEOT wins on all 12 metric/dataset combinations, with MoF gains of +4.6 (Breakfast), +1.5 (YTI), +5.6 (50Salads), and +0.8 (DA), and F1 gains of +2.2, +5.3, +5.3, and +7.8 respectively. Against VASOT [2], PEOT achieves higher MoF on all four datasets (e.g., 60.7 vs 57.5 on Breakfast, 71.2 vs 70.9 on DA) and higher F1 on Breakfast, YTI, and DA, while VASOT retains a slight mIoU edge on YTI and 50Salads. Against CLOT [3], PEOT outperforms on all metrics for Breakfast (60.7 vs 60.1 MoF, 40.5 vs 40.1 F1) and Desktop Assembly (71.2 vs 68.8 MoF, 75.8 vs 72.6 F1), but CLOT achieves higher F1 and mIoU on 50Salads (63.2 vs 58.9 F1, 38.8 vs 30.2 mIoU) and YTI (36.7 vs 37.4 F1 is close, but CLOT wins mIoU 23.4 vs 22.9). Against older baselines (CTE, ASAL, TOT+, UFSA, HVQ), PEOT dominates across all reported metrics by large margins (e.g., +18.2 MoF over CTE on Breakfast, +15.8 MoF over UFSA on 50Salads). The paper also shows in Table 2 that adding probabilistic embeddings to VASOT yields consistent improvements (e.g., +3.7 MoF on Breakfast, +3.2 MoF on DA), confirming the benefit is transferable beyond the ASOT framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ASOT framework (Xu & Gould, CVPR 2024), which introduced a combination of Kantorovich and Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport for joint representation learning and clustering in unsupervised action segmentation, by replacing deterministic frame embeddings with probabilistic (Gaussian) embeddings predicted by a Graph Convolutional Network. It also demonstrates that the same probabilistic embedding module can be plugged into VASOT (Ali et al., ICCV 2025), a more recent extension that jointly solves video alignment and segmentation. The paper positions itself against CLOT (Bueno-Benito & Dimiccoli, ICCV 2025), which adds a three-level OT with cross-attention feedback, arguing that a single OT with better frame representations (via uncertainty modeling) is a simpler and equally effective alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent improvements across four datasets and two different OT-based baselines (ASOT and VASOT) demonstrate that modeling embedding uncertainty is a broadly applicable and simple modification that mitigates overfitting to noisy pseudo-labels in the EM-style training loop. The paper identifies that the learned uncertainty correlates with prediction errors, suggesting it could serve as a confidence signal for downstream applications. Open questions include making the pseudo-label generation process differentiable, adapting the Monte Carlo sampling rate to the learned uncertainty during training, and whether probabilistic embeddings transfer to related tasks such as action anticipation.
