## Problem

Existing diffusion-based driving scene generators produce samples that violate kinematic feasibility, road boundaries, or interaction logic because structural constraints degrade during iterative denoising. Additionally, safety-critical long-tail events are underrepresented in training data, making it difficult to generate realistic adversarial scenarios without retraining auxiliary networks or manually prescribing attacker trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262216/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a spatiotemporal tensor x ∈ R^{A×T×D} (agents × timesteps × state dimension including position, heading, velocity, and size) with a binary validity mask for dynamic agent entries/exits. It consumes past trajectories and optional goal points as inpainting context via a mask, and is applied entirely at inference time on a pretrained diffusion backbone (Nexus) without any fine-tuning. For planner-specific adversarial generation, the ego's planned trajectory is injected as a weak-noise condition to target specific planner weaknesses.

## Method

OMEGA re-anchors each reverse diffusion step by solving a constrained optimization that maximizes a differentiable structural objective (smoothness, kinematic feasibility, collision avoidance) subject to a KL-divergence trust region around the model-predicted clean sample, translating the constraint into an adaptive Euclidean ball whose radius scales with the diffusion variance σ_t. A phase-aligned guidance schedule decomposes denoising into a Warm-up phase (full-horizon, agent-wise structural priors for macro-level layout) and a Rolling-Zero phase (time-indexed, inter-agent interaction guidance for fine-grained reactivity), reducing joint optimization burden and enabling parallel computation. For adversarial generation, OMEGA_Adv formulates ego-attacker interaction as a noncooperative distributional game and approximates Nash equilibria via sensitivity-enhanced iterative best response, where the attacker's update is steered by the KKT multiplier of the ego's active collision-avoidance constraint, producing targeted yet distributionally consistent adversarial maneuvers without retraining.

## Results

On nuPlan (free exploration), Nexus-Ω achieves a 72.27% scene valid rate, nearly doubling the base Nexus model (32.35%) and substantially outperforming Diffusion Policy (33.27%) and SceneDiffuser (33.27%). Zero-shot on Waymo, Nexus-Ω reaches 81.95% valid rate, a +20.24-point gain over Nexus (61.71%). Under goal-conditioned controllability, Nexus-Ω achieves 80.00% valid rate versus 11.00% for Nexus, a +69-point improvement. In adversarial generation, Nexus-ΩAdv produces 5× more near-collision frames (TTC < 3 s: 11.60% vs. 2.21% oracle) while maintaining 88.98% kinematic feasibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nexus-Ω dominates all baselines on both nuPlan and Waymo in free exploration, achieving 72.27% and 81.95% valid rates respectively, versus 32.35%/61.71% for the unguided Nexus backbone, 33.27%/64.55% for Diffusion Policy, and 33.27%/61.01% for SceneDiffuser. On goal-conditioned controllability (Table 3), the gap widens dramatically: Nexus-Ω reaches 80.00% valid rate compared to only 11.00% (Nexus), 20.00% (Diffusion Policy), and 17.00% (SceneDiffuser), with scene-level collision dropping from 51% to 12% and off-road from 51% to 7%. Against guidance-specific baselines in the ablation (Table 5), Nexus-Ω outperforms the projection-based GHC (26.06% valid), conditional-score CTG (50.41%), and DPS-style clean-space guidance (47.19%), confirming that KL-bounded optimization re-anchoring is more effective than local linearization or hard projection. In adversarial generation, Nexus-ΩAdv produces the highest proportion of TTC < 3 s frames (11.60%) and the lowest off-road rate (5.42%) among all adversarial baselines (Nexus-FT, Nexus-GC, Nexus-CTG_Adv), while maintaining the highest kinematic feasibility (88.98%). The method also generalizes across backbones: when applied to VBD on Waymo, it improves the realism meta-score from 0.723 to 0.730, demonstrating backbone-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

OMEGA builds directly on the Nexus diffusion-based scene generator [45] as its backbone, applying a training-free optimization-guided sampling procedure that re-anchors each reverse diffusion step via KL-bounded constrained optimization in the clean space. This departs from prior guidance strategies—CTG's score approximation, DPS's gradient propagation through the denoiser, and SceneDiffuser's projection/clipping—by formulating guidance as an explicit Euclidean trust-region problem rather than relying on local linearizations or discontinuous corrections. The adversarial component (OMEGA_Adv) draws on sensitivity-enhanced iterative best response from noncooperative game theory [35] to approximate Nash equilibria, contrasting with prior adversarial scene generators (KING, AdvSim, AdvDiffuser) that use reinforcement learning, gradient perturbation, or manually prescribed attacker trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, drop-in nature of OMEGA makes it immediately applicable to any existing diffusion-based scene generator without retraining, which is practically significant for the autonomous driving evaluation community where safety-critical long-tail scenarios remain underrepresented in data. The ~5-second runtime per 8-second scene is a notable limitation for closed-loop simulation integration, and the authors acknowledge that parallelizing per-agent optimization is needed to close this gap. Open questions include whether the optimization-guided re-anchoring principle generalizes to other diffusion-based generative tasks beyond driving, and whether the game-theoretic adversarial formulation can be extended to multi-attacker or mixed-strategy settings.
