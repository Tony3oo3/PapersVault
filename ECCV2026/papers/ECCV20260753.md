## Problem

Existing 3D human diffusion models are constrained to small-scale captured datasets (a few thousand identities) or unrealistic synthetic data because they require accurate, fully-observed 3D assets for each training sample. In-the-wild monocular videos offer million-scale diversity but provide only partial body observations, making them directly unusable for 3D generative training. No prior method has trained a 3D diffusion model at scale from real-world video data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260753/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

GenLCA accepts three types of conditional inputs—text prompts, segmented body part images (hair, face, upper clothing, lower clothing, accessories), and scribble images—which can be combined in text-only, image-only, or text-plus-image modes. The output is an animatable 3D Gaussian avatar consisting of 8,192 query points each decoded into 8 Gaussian splats (65,536 total), rendered via Linear Blend Skinning to support high-fidelity facial expression and full-body pose animation. At inference, sampling uses an explicit Euler solver with 50 steps and classifier-free guidance (scale 5.0).

## Method

GenLCA repurposes a pretrained feed-forward avatar reconstruction model (LCA) as an animatable 3D tokenizer that encodes multiple body and face images into structured GS tokens (8,192 × 1024), which a VAE-style compressor further compresses into compact latents (8,192 × 8). The key insight is a visibility-aware training strategy: a visibility mask is computed by rendering decoded splats and measuring per-splat contribution to input views, tokens in unobserved regions are replaced with shared learnable placeholder features, and the diffusion loss is masked to valid regions only, preventing blurry or transparent artifacts from corrupting supervision. The generative model is a 28-block double-stream MMDiT (adapted from Hunyuan) trained with the rectified flow / Conditional Flow Matching objective, where latent and conditional features (CLIP text embeddings, DINOv2 image embeddings) are processed in separate branches before joint attention. This pipeline enables training on ~1.1 million in-the-wild identities, scaling the 3D human dataset to a magnitude comparable to 2D image datasets while preserving the photorealism and animatability inherited from the reconstruction tokenizer.

## Results

GenLCA achieves the best scores across nearly all quantitative metrics: BLIP-VQA 0.64, CLIP Score 0.76, CLIB-FIQA 0.55, and HyperIQA 63.05, outperforming all five baselines. On image-conditioned FID, GenLCA scores 96.03 on THuman 2.0 and 76.50 on HuGe100K, substantially beating SIGMAN (121.40 and 160.48 respectively). In the user study (5-point scale), GenLCA leads all four criteria (semantic alignment 4.56, consistency 4.68, visual quality 4.65, geometric quality 4.63), with the closest competitor DreamWaltz-G trailing by over 1 point on every axis. Inference time is 12 seconds, comparable to TeRA (12s) and far faster than SDS-based methods (1.2–3.0 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenLCA outperforms all five baselines on semantic alignment (BLIP-VQA and CLIP Score) and visual quality (CLIB-FIQA, HyperIQA). Among SDS-based methods, DreamWaltz-G is the strongest baseline (CLIP 0.75, HyperIQA 59.33) but still trails GenLCA by 0.01 on CLIP and 3.72 on HyperIQA, while requiring 3 hours of inference versus 12 seconds. Among 3D diffusion baselines, TeRA achieves a lower FID on 2D diffusion images (151.80 vs. 160.91) because it is itself trained on diffusion-generated images, but it lags GenLCA on every other metric (BLIP-VQA 0.42 vs. 0.64, HyperIQA 44.01 vs. 63.05). SIGMAN, the only other method supporting image-conditioned generation, is clearly dominated by GenLCA on both FID benchmarks (96.03 vs. 121.40 on THuman 2.0; 76.50 vs. 160.48 on HuGe100K) and on all semantic/quality scores. In the user study, GenLCA leads every criterion by at least 0.9 points over the second-best method (DreamWaltz-G), with the largest gaps on semantic alignment (+0.88) and visual quality (+1.24).

## Relation to Prior Work

GenLCA builds on the LCA feed-forward avatar reconstruction model as a 3D tokenizer, the MMDiT double-stream transformer architecture from Hunyuan3D for the denoising network, and the rectified flow / conditional flow matching objective for training. It departs fundamentally from prior 3D human diffusion models (TeRA, SIGMAN, Rodin, StructLDM) that require accurate multi-view or synthetic 3D assets for supervision, by repurposing a reconstruction model to tokenize in-the-wild monocular videos into structured 3D tokens. The visibility-aware training strategy with learnable placeholders is a novel departure from standard masked modeling, specifically designed to handle the partial-observation corruption inherent in single-view video rather than intentional patch removal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that 3D human generation can scale to the million-identity regime by leveraging in-the-wild video data, closing a 10–100× gap over prior 3D diffusion models trained on captured or synthetic data. The visibility-aware training strategy is a broadly applicable technique for any generative model trained on partially observable 3D supervision. Key limitations remain: animation quality is bounded by the LBS inherited from the reconstruction model, and the model still struggles with extreme poses, rare prompts, and under-observed regions (e.g., the back). Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to other 3D asset classes and whether the reconstruction tokenizer can be improved to reduce reliance on the visibility mask.
