## Problem

Autoregressive vision-language models for chest X-ray report generation suffer from high inference latency due to sequential token decoding, while existing diffusion-based alternatives still require multiple denoising steps to maintain output coherence. Compressing decoding to a single step per block is theoretically ideal for throughput, but the token-factorized (mean-field) parameterization of discrete diffusion models introduces a structural bias that is most severe when all tokens are predicted simultaneously, causing substantial quality degradation. No prior distillation method produces unfactorized supervision targets capable of supporting reliable one-step-per-block inference.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single chest X-ray image as input and generates a free-text radiology report as output, supporting both English and Chinese. Reports are structured as block-wise parallel generation (4 or 8 tokens per forward pass) rather than token-by-token, and the training data is normalized so that every predefined anatomical region receives an explicit positive finding or negative assertion, eliminating the 'reporting by exception' omission bias present in raw clinical reports.

## Method

ECHO is built in three stages: continued pre-training on Lingshu-7B, Response-Asymmetric Diffusion (RAD) adaptation that converts the AR model to block diffusion in a single SFT stage by duplicating only the response tokens (saving 72.3% training FLOPs versus full-sequence duplication), and Direct Conditional Distillation (DCD) that distills the multi-step model into a one-step-per-block decoder. DCD's key mechanism is to collect the teacher's on-policy confidence-heuristic denoising trajectory, stitch the per-position distributions at each unmasking step into a joint non-factorized target that encodes cross-token dependencies, and align the student's single-step prediction to this target via forward KL divergence with step-wise token reweighting. At inference, a Fused Block KV Cache eliminates the dedicated KV-update forward pass by folding it into the next block's denoising step, halving total forward passes per block.

## Results

ECHO (blk8) achieves an 8× theoretical speedup (TPF ×8.00, TPS 274.21) over the autoregressive baseline while incurring only 2–5% quality degradation relative to its multi-step ECHO_Base counterpart. On clinical fidelity, ECHO_blk8 reaches RaTEScore 56.85 / SemScore 51.40 on CheXpert-Plus, 59.92 / 64.00 on ReXGradient, and 52.97 / 44.83 on MIMIC-CXR, surpassing the strongest AR medical model (MedGemma-27B) by 17–40% on CFM metrics. The paper reports aggregate improvements of 64.33% in RaTEScore and 60.58% in SemScore over state-of-the-art autoregressive methods, with a 5.1× practical speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against autoregressive medical VLMs, ECHO_blk8 dominates on clinical fidelity: it outperforms MedGemma-27B (the strongest AR baseline) by 17–40% on RaTEScore/SemScore across the three datasets, and surpasses Lingshu-7B, Hulu-Med-7B/32B, and LLaVA-Med by even wider margins. Against proprietary general-purpose models (Gemini3-Pro, Qwen3-Max), ECHO leads on all clinical metrics (e.g., RaTEScore 56.85 vs. 40.86 on CheXpert-Plus) while also being substantially faster. Among diffusion distillation baselines sharing the same ECHO_Base backbone, ECHO_blk8 achieves an 8× speedup with only 2–5% quality loss, whereas T3D reaches just 2× with a comparable drop, dParallel hits 4.4× but suffers 18–32% clinical degradation, and CD4LM/d3LLM achieve 3.6–3.8× with 15–25% degradation. The conservative ECHO_blk4 configuration further narrows the quality gap to ≤1.25% at 4× speedup, confirming a consistently superior quality–speed trade-off across all block-size settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECHO builds on the block diffusion decoding paradigm (Arriola et al., ICLR 2025) and the masked discrete diffusion framework (Sahoo et al., NeurIPS 2024), adapting them to the medical vision-language setting via the Lingshu-7B base model. It departs from prior AR-to-diffusion conversion methods like SDAR-VL (Cheng et al., 2025) by replacing the two-stage pretraining-plus-SFT pipeline with a single Response-Asymmetric Diffusion (RAD) stage that duplicates only the response tokens, cutting training FLOPs by 72.3%. Most critically, ECHO's Direct Conditional Distillation (DCD) breaks from all existing dLLM distillation methods (SDTT, dParallel, T3D, CD4LM, d3LLM), which align token-factorized predictions across noise levels, by constructing a non-factorized joint supervision target from the teacher's on-policy confidence-heuristic remasking trajectory—enabling true one-step-per-block decoding rather than merely reducing the number of steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× theoretical (5.1× practical) inference speedup with only 2–5% quality degradation makes high-throughput automated CXR reporting clinically viable, directly addressing the radiologist workload bottleneck that motivates the task. The mean-field bias analysis provides a clean theoretical account of why one-step decoding is fundamentally harder than few-step, and the DCD framework is stated to be general to any discrete diffusion language or vision-language model, suggesting broad applicability beyond CXR. Open questions include whether the 2–5% degradation is acceptable in all diagnostic subtasks, whether the approach extends to longer report formats or 3D imaging, and whether the data normalization requirement (explicit negative assertions for every anatomical region) generalizes to other radiology modalities where reporting conventions differ.
