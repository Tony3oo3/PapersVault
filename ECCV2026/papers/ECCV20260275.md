## Problem

Existing multimodal RAG systems face a fundamental tension: flat vector retrieval ignores structural dependencies needed for multi-hop reasoning, while graph-based alternatives rely on expensive MLLM-driven triplet extraction and convert visual content into text descriptions, discarding fine-grained visual evidence. No prior framework constructs a natively multimodal knowledge graph that preserves atomic visual regions alongside textual entities while remaining computationally tractable at scale.

## Input / Output

The system ingests a multimodal knowledge base organized as (text chunk, associated image set) pairs and a query that may be purely textual, purely visual, or both. Retrieval operates simultaneously across four semantic granularities—sentences, document chunks, full images, and SAM3-segmented objects—before aggregating scores onto unified multimodal nodes. The final output is a set of top-K document chunks (with their associated images) concatenated with the original query to form the MLLM generation prompt.

## Method

MG²-RAG constructs a hierarchical multimodal knowledge graph via two lightweight stages: (1) spaCy-based NER and rule-based dependency parsing extract entities and syntactic relations from text without any MLLM calls, and (2) entity names are batched as semantic prompts into SAM3 to perform targeted open-vocabulary segmentation, directly grounding each textual entity in pixel-space regions. Grounded entities and their visual objects are fused into unified multimodal nodes, and the graph is organized at three levels (chunk, image, multimodal-node) with three edge types (contextual provenance, syntactic semantic, and confidence-weighted visual grounding). At retrieval time, dense EVA-CLIP-8B similarities are computed at all four granularities, aggregated onto multimodal nodes via incidence matrices with mean-pooling, and then relevance is propagated across the heterogeneous graph using Personalized PageRank to capture multi-hop structural dependencies before selecting the top-K chunks for generation.

## Results

On multimodal retrieval, MG²-RAG achieves R@1 of 44.9 (E-VQA) and 59.6 (InfoSeek), surpassing the strongest cross-modal baseline EVA-CLIP-8B (V→T) by +2.9 and +3.1 points respectively. On knowledge-based VQA with Qwen3.5-27B, it reaches 60.24 (E-VQA 5k) and 42.26 (InfoSeek 5k), outperforming all prior graph-based RAG methods. On ScienceQA reasoning, MG²-RAG with Qwen3.5-27B attains 97.85% average accuracy, exceeding human performance (88.40%). Graph construction is 43.3× faster and 23.9× cheaper on average than the strongest graph-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest graph-based competitor mKG-RAG, MG²-RAG gains +9.9 R@1 on InfoSeek retrieval and +0.48 accuracy on InfoSeek VQA (both with LLaMA-3.1-8B), with the gap widening to +4.18 on InfoSeek All when using Qwen3.5-27B. On ScienceQA reasoning, MG²-RAG (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) surpasses MMGraphRAG by +11.04 points in average accuracy and VaLiK by +1.37 (7B) / +2.85 (72B). On CrisisMMD classification, it outperforms MMGraphRAG by +3.97 and VaLiK by +0.92 (7B) / +1.84 (72B) in average accuracy. Compared to the strongest flat-retrieval baseline EchoSight (V→T), MG²-RAG improves E-VQA VQA by +0.54 and InfoSeek by +2.18. Against zero-shot MLLMs, integrating MG²-RAG yields +1.75 average points for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and +0.70 for GPT-5.2 on ScienceQA, confirming that structured retrieval adds value even for strong parametric models. Notably, VaLiK and MMGraphRAG (restricted to 5k subsets due to cost) collapse to near-random performance on InfoSeek (2.51 and 0.50 respectively), while MG²-RAG at the same 5k scale achieves 38.65, highlighting a fundamental scalability advantage of the lightweight construction pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the convergence of three threads: graph-based RAG (which replaces flat vector search with structured traversal), multimodal RAG (which grounds MLLM generation in external visual-textual evidence), and multimodal knowledge graph construction. It departs from prior graph-based MM-RAG systems (VaLiK, mKG-RAG, MMGraphRAG) that rely on expensive MLLM-driven triplet extraction and convert visual content into textual descriptions before graph construction. Instead, MG²-RAG repurposes lightweight NLP tools (spaCy NER and dependency parsing) and open-vocabulary segmentation (SAM3) to build a hierarchical graph where textual entities and visual objects coexist as unified multimodal nodes, and applies Personalized PageRank over this heterogeneous topology for multi-granularity retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 43.3× construction speedup and 23.9× cost reduction make graph-based MM-RAG practically deployable at scale, removing the primary barrier that has kept most systems confined to small knowledge bases. The modality-preserving fusion—treating segmented visual regions as first-class graph citizens rather than translating them to text—addresses a fundamental information-loss bottleneck in prior pipelines. Open questions include whether the rule-based relation extraction generalizes beyond English and encyclopedic text to more structured or multilingual domains, how the method behaves on dynamic or streaming knowledge bases, and whether the PPR propagation depth and the scaling factors (ω_C, ω_I, λ_t, λ_v) can be learned rather than hand-tuned per task.
