## Problem

Existing functional 3D scene graph benchmarks (SceneFun3D, FunGraph3D) cover only large furniture and link all interactive elements directly to objects, ignoring tabletop manipulable items and the natural object–carrier–unit hierarchy (e.g., cabinet→drawer→knob). The prior OpenFunGraph pipeline, which relies on distorted 3D bounding boxes fed to an LLM for relational reasoning, suffers from severe error accumulation on small dense parts, instance aliasing during cross-frame fusion, and attribution jitter under viewpoint changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of posed RGB-D images (keyframe-downsampled to control runtime) and produces a hierarchical functional 3D scene graph whose nodes span three semantic levels—objects (O), functional carriers (C), and interactive units (U)—each annotated with 3D position, dimensions, and open-vocabulary semantics, connected by directed functional edges that encode either direct O←U or hierarchical O←C←U relationships. The expanded benchmark introduces free-form text labels for tabletop objects and explicit multi-level triplets, evaluated via Hungarian-matched Recall@k with relaxed similarity thresholds (0.75/0.70) to accommodate synonymous phrasing.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, a Dynamic Interactability Map (GPT-5 priors + RAM++ frame-level detection) guides open-vocabulary node detection (LLMDet + SAM2), and 2D functional edges are scored by geometric mask containment followed by LLaVA visual-semantic verification, bypassing unreliable 3D coordinates for relational reasoning. Second, cross-frame node association uses a multi-cue bipartite matching score (projected IoU, 3D Gaussian-kernel distance, color-histogram cosine, CLIP semantic similarity) solved via the Hungarian algorithm, while edge attribution is formulated as a temporal graph optimization that maximizes log-odds evidence accumulation plus entropy regularization minus a temporal-smoothing penalty over soft assignment distributions. Third, global hierarchy shaping performs a one-time binary pairing optimization in 3D (semantic prior + geometric proximity) to restructure direct O←U edges into O←C←U chains, resolving the combinatorial ambiguity that is intractable at the single-frame 2D level.

## Results

On FunGraph3D, the proposed method achieves 55.6% overall triplet recall, outperforming the strongest baseline OpenFunGraph Hierarchy (35.1%) by 20.5 points and the original OpenFunGraph (21.8%) by 33.8 points. At the node level, overall node recall reaches 60.2% on FunGraph3D (vs. 46.3% for OpenFunGraph Hierarchy) and 70.7% on SceneFun3D (vs. 65.2%). On the hierarchical triplet subset of FunGraph3D, the method scores 61.9% compared to 16.1% for OpenFunGraph Hierarchy, demonstrating a 45.8-point advantage in recovering multi-level O←C←U structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: FunGraph [52], a closed-set pipeline using 2D-trained part detection and VLM annotation without cross-frame fusion or hierarchical modeling, and OpenFunGraph [75], an open-vocabulary method relying on geometric proximity and CLIP mean aggregation for 3D fusion. The proposed method decisively outperforms both on all metrics. Against FunGraph, gains are largest on FunGraph3D triplets (55.6% vs. 19.1% overall), where FunGraph's lack of temporal optimization and open-vocabulary capability causes it to fail entirely on hierarchical triplets. Against the original OpenFunGraph, the improvement is 20.5 points on FunGraph3D overall triplets (55.6% vs. 21.8%) and 13.9 points on overall node recall (60.2% vs. 32.8%). Even against the strengthened OpenFunGraph Hierarchy variant (which adds hierarchical prompts to the LLM), the method leads by 20.5 points on FunGraph3D triplets and 13.9 points on overall nodes, with the gap most pronounced on hierarchical triplets (61.9% vs. 16.1%), confirming that prompt engineering alone cannot resolve the geometric aliasing and attribution ambiguity that the graph-optimization-based approach addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional 3D scene graph paradigm introduced by OpenFunGraph [75] and FunGraph [52], which model objects and interactive elements with functional edges but only cover large furniture in a flat object→element structure. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) the benchmark is expanded to include dense tabletop objects and explicit three-level hierarchies (object → functional carrier → interactive unit), and (2) the pipeline abandons the prior paradigm of feeding noisy 3D bounding-box coordinates to an LLM for relationship inference, replacing it with direct 2D visual grounding (VLM scoring on masks) followed by temporal graph optimization in 3D. The multi-cue node association (2D IoU, 3D geometry, appearance histograms, CLIP semantics) and the log-odds/entropy/temporal-smoothing edge optimization are novel architectural choices that address challenges the flat, geometry-only baselines cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical functional scene graph representation is directly relevant to language-guided robotic manipulation (e.g., 'open the second drawer of the cabinet'), where flat object→knob links are ambiguous. The 2D-visual-grounding-then-3D-optimization pipeline offers a generalizable recipe for any multi-frame 3D graph construction task where single-frame evidence is noisy and instances are dense. Limitations include dependence on upstream detection/segmentation quality and VLM discrimination, and the authors note that extreme occlusion or restricted viewpoints can still cause ambiguities; active viewpoint selection is flagged as future work. The benchmark extension (tabletop + hierarchical annotations on FunGraph3D and SceneFun3D) provides a more realistic evaluation target for the manipulation community.
