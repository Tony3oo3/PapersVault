## Problem

SAM-based unified scene text detection (Hi-SAM) relies on pixel-level text segmentation to randomly sample thousands of foreground points as visual prompts, incurring high inference latency at the hierarchical mask decoding and post-processing stages. Additionally, Hi-SAM requires comprehensive multi-granularity annotations for every training instance, a constraint that severely limits data scalability given the scarcity of densely labeled datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262714/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a scene image and a task selection (multi-level, word-level-only, or text-line-level-only) as input. The point decoder first produces a 256×256 word-centric heatmap, from which sparse peak points (typically ~200–300) are extracted via 3×3 max-pooling and thresholding. The HM-Decoder then outputs four hierarchical mask logits per point prompt at two spatial resolutions—384×384 for word and word-group masks, 256×256 for text-line and paragraph masks—along with per-mask IoU scores. Layout analysis (paragraph grouping) is derived post-hoc via a union-find algorithm over the paragraph mask IoU matrix.

## Method

ET-SAM replaces Hi-SAM's pixel-level S-Decoder with a lightweight point decoder (following the PseCo paradigm) that directly predicts a word-centric heatmap from image embeddings, enabling extraction of sparse, high-confidence foreground points instead of thousands of random samples. A hierarchical mask decoder (HM-Decoder), modified from SAM's mask decoder, simultaneously segments word, word-group, text-line, and paragraph masks under the guidance of these point prompts. Learnable task prompt tokens are injected into both decoders to condition them on the specific dataset's word density and segmentation target, mitigating cross-dataset discrepancies. A joint training strategy combines multi-level (HierText), word-level (Total-Text, TextSeg, ICDAR2013), and text-line-level (ICDAR2015, CTW1500) datasets in parallel, with each batch containing one sample from each category and shorter sets resampled per epoch. The image encoder uses SAM ViT-L with adapters, and the entire framework is initialized from SAM pre-trained weights, achieving ~3× inference acceleration over Hi-SAM while maintaining competitive or superior performance.

## Results

On the unified HierText benchmark, ET-SAM (fine-tuned) achieves 63.60 PQ / 81.94 F-score at the word level, 66.30 PQ / 84.82 F-score at the text-line level, and 58.82 PQ / 75.39 F-score for layout analysis, surpassing Hi-SAM-L across all hierarchies. On scene text detection benchmarks, ET-SAM (fine-tuned) reaches 87.0% F-score on Total-Text, 87.9% on CTW1500, and 83.2% on ICDAR2015, yielding an 11.0% average F-score improvement over Hi-SAM-L (zero-shot: 70.2/77.4/77.4). Joint training alone (without fine-tuning) already achieves 86.4/87.1/83.5 F-score, demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization. ET-SAM also achieves ~3× inference acceleration (1.4s vs 4.4s per image on HierText) by reducing prompt points from ~1500 to ~287.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most directly comparable SAM-based baseline Hi-SAM-L (zero-shot), ET-SAM achieves a dramatic 11.0% average F-score gain across Total-Text (87.0 vs 70.2), CTW1500 (87.9 vs 77.4), and ICDAR2015 (83.2 vs 77.4), primarily due to the joint training strategy that leverages heterogeneous annotations. On the unified HierText benchmark, ET-SAM (FT) edges out Hi-SAM-L by +0.5 PQ at the word level (63.60 vs 63.10), +0.13 PQ at the text-line level (66.30 vs 66.17), and +1.21 PQ for layout analysis (58.82 vs 57.61), while running ~3× faster (1.4s vs 4.4s). Compared to the DETR-based Unified Detector (UD), ET-SAM (FT) matches or exceeds UD on Total-Text (87.0 vs 87.9 F) and CTW1500 (87.9 vs 86.0 F), though UD retains a slight edge on Total-Text recall (91.1 vs 87.5). Against specialized SOTA detectors (DPText-DETR, SRFormer, DeepSolo, ESTextSpotter), ET-SAM trails by 1–3% F-score on Total-Text and CTW1500, which the authors attribute to differences in training data volume; with matched training data, ET-SAM surpasses DPText-DETR by an average of 1.2% F-score. ET-SAM clearly outperforms older segmentation-based methods (PANet, DBNet++, FCENet) and regression-based methods (ABCNetv2, TESTR) on most benchmarks, confirming the advantage of the SAM-based hierarchical framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

ET-SAM extends the SAM-based hierarchical text segmentation line initiated by Hi-SAM, which itself builds on the Segment Anything Model and the unified scene text detection and layout analysis benchmark (HierText/UD) introduced by Long et al. The key architectural departure is replacing Hi-SAM's pixel-level segmentation plus random foreground point sampling with a lightweight point decoder (following the PseCo paradigm) that directly predicts word-centric heatmaps, yielding sparse, high-confidence prompts. Additionally, ET-SAM introduces learnable task prompts and a joint training strategy to handle heterogeneous annotation granularities across datasets—a data utilization problem Hi-SAM could not address because it required complete multi-level annotations for every training instance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~3× inference acceleration (4.4s → 1.4s) makes SAM-based unified text detection practically deployable, while the joint training strategy opens a pathway to scale training data by mixing word-level, text-line-level, and multi-level datasets without requiring uniform annotation density. However, ET-SAM still trails specialized SOTA detectors (DPText-DETR, SRFormer) by a few F-score points, and the authors explicitly note that the learned features are not aligned with vision-language models, limiting end-to-end multimodal text understanding. Open questions include whether further data scaling can close the gap with dedicated detectors and whether the task-prompt mechanism generalizes beyond the three defined task configurations.
