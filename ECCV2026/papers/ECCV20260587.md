## Problem

Existing 3D generative models assume complete visibility and single-view inputs, making them unable to handle occluded observations, integrate multiple views, or enforce geometric constraints for editing. While prior works address amodal completion, multi-view reconstruction, and shape editing in isolation, they lack a unified framework that jointly reasons over heterogeneous conditioning signals under partial observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260587/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts up to 6 posed RGB images (zero-padded and masked for missing views), per-view binary visibility masks partitioned into 14×14 DINOv2 patches, camera extrinsics encoded as per-pixel Plücker embeddings, and a partial point cloud (downsampled to 8,192 points at evaluation). It outputs a complete watertight 3D mesh decoded from shape latents via the Hunyuan3D-ShapeVAE autoencoder. For editing, the mask semantics shift from visibility to preservation regions, with user-inpainted views providing fully valid masks to drive geometry updates.

## Method

Axolotl3D fine-tunes the Hunyuan3D 2.1 DiT backbone, replacing its single-image cross-attention with a multi-modal cross-attention that jointly attends to visual and geometric condition tokens. Visual features from DINOv2 are enriched with Plücker camera embeddings to align multi-view features in a shared 3D coordinate system, while the partial point cloud is encoded into fixed-length latent codes via VecSetX. Both modalities pass through per-modality gated FFNs and receive learnable modality embeddings before being concatenated into unified condition tokens. A mask-biased cross-attention mechanism sets attention weights for occluded visual tokens to −∞, preventing occluded regions from corrupting the denoised shape latent. Training employs a unified data augmentation strategy over TRELLIS-500K that synthesizes three conditioning regimes—sparse views with random occlusions, large-area point dropout along random axes, and bounding-box-based editing—enabling a single model to handle single-view generation, multi-view reconstruction, amodal completion, and geometry-aware editing.

## Results

On Toys4K single-view (no occlusion), Axolotl3D achieves an F-score of 0.9221 and CD of 0.2157×10⁻¹, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ShapeR (0.7920 F-score) and Hy3D-Omni (0.7811). Under multi-view (6 views) on Toys4K, it reaches 0.9768 F-score and 0.1351 CD, edging out ShapeR (0.9433) and Hy3D-Omni (0.9211). On OmniObject3D multi-view, Axolotl3D attains 0.9842 F-score, the best across all methods. Notably, Axolotl3D exhibits the lowest standard deviation in every setting (e.g., ±0.0445 vs ±0.1303 for ShapeR on multi-view Toys4K), indicating more consistent and stable reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Amodal3R [41] (the only baseline without point conditioning), Axolotl3D wins by a wide margin on every metric and setting, e.g., 0.9221 vs 0.7310 F-score on single-view Toys4K. SAM 3D [5], a single-view method using camera-space point maps, is outperformed by 0.14–0.39 F-score points across both datasets. Hy3D-Omni [21] is the strongest non-concurrent baseline; Axolotl3D surpasses it by 0.14 F-score on single-view Toys4K (0.9221 vs 0.7811) and by 0.056 on multi-view Toys4K (0.9768 vs 0.9211). The closest competitor is the concurrent ShapeR [34], which also uses multi-view images, points, and camera poses: Axolotl3D leads by 0.13 F-score on single-view Toys4K (0.9221 vs 0.7920) and by 0.034 on multi-view Toys4K (0.9768 vs 0.9433), while also showing roughly 3× lower variance (±0.0445 vs ±0.1303). On OmniObject3D multi-view, the gap narrows to 0.021 F-score (0.9842 vs 0.9629), but Axolotl3D still maintains the lower CD (0.1389 vs 0.1329) and tighter standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Axolotl3D builds directly on the Hunyuan3D 2.1 diffusion backbone (ShapeVAE + DiT), replacing its single-image cross-attention with a multi-modal mechanism that jointly ingests DINOv2 image features, Plücker-embedded camera parameters, and VecSetX-encoded point clouds. Conceptually, it unifies previously fragmented research threads—amodal completion (Amodal3R, AmodalGen3D), multi-view reconstruction (ReconViaGen, ShapeR), and geometry-aware editing (Instant3dit, PrEditor3D)—under a single 'constrained 3D completion' formulation, arguing that all these tasks are instances of completing a shape from partial, heterogeneous observations. The key architectural departure from prior geometry-conditioned generators (Hunyuan3D-Omni, CLAY) is the explicit handling of occlusion via mask-biased attention and the unified training strategy that synthesizes sparse-view, occluded, and editing regimes from the same large-scale mesh corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single model that handles single-view generation, sparse multi-view reconstruction, occlusion completion, and geometry-consistent editing, Axolotl3D removes the need for task-specific pipelines and enables practical workflows (e.g., MapAnything-based single-image-to-3D, inpainting-guided editing) that were previously fragmented across multiple tools. The state-of-the-art margins—particularly the large single-view gap over ShapeR and Hy3D-Omni—suggest that explicit geometric anchoring via point clouds is more critical than prior image-only or single-modality approaches assumed. Open questions include how well the model balances fidelity to input points against robustness to noisy depth/point estimates (the authors note degradation under severe depth errors), whether the 6-view limit generalizes to denser or more irregular camera arrangements, and how to extend the framework to texture synthesis.
