## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for large vision-language models are designed for autoregressive left-to-right decoding and face fundamental structural mismatches when applied to multimodal discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs): contrastive decoding doubles inference latency per demasking step, and confidence-based unmasking schedules cause severe trajectory divergence that breaks positional alignment. Moreover, dLLMs exhibit a diffuse hallucination pattern where language-prior drift pervades the representation space simultaneously under bidirectional attention, rather than the local, sequentially amplified errors seen in AR models, making local corrections insufficient.

## Input / Output

UVC operates on a frozen multimodal dLLM that receives an RGB image and a text prompt, producing generated text via iterative demasking over S steps. The offline calibration stage consumes 500 COCO 2014 train images with ground-truth bounding boxes to construct contrastive pairs (clean vs. globally or locally Gaussian-noised), extracting per-head shift vectors of dimension d = D/H. At inference, the method requires no additional forward passes or ground-truth annotations for the target input; it only injects pre-computed vectors into selected attention heads at still-masked positions, introducing at most 1.01× latency overhead.

## Method

UVC is a training-free, three-stage pipeline. First, multi-granularity contrastive activation shift extraction computes mask-averaged activation differences between clean and degraded (full-image or bounding-box-only Gaussian noise) inputs across all L×H attention heads, yielding global-scale and instance-scale shift vectors that encode the direction from visually degraded to faithful representations. Second, per-head logistic regressors are fit on these activations with 2-fold cross-validated AUC to identify the top-K most visually informative heads at each scale, which are found to be largely disjoint (global heads in middle layers, instance heads in deeper layers). Third, during inference, the pre-computed shift vectors are injected additively into the selected heads' outputs before the output projection, restricted exclusively to still-masked positions at each demasking step via a position-aware indicator, so that already-unmasked tokens remain unperturbed while the calibration naturally concentrates on remaining uncertain positions as the mask set shrinks.

## Results

On POPE, UVC-Both improves average F1 by 7.0 points (Lumina-DiMOO) and 7.4 points (MMaDA), with Accuracy gains of 3.4 and 12.6 points respectively, across all nine dataset/split combinations. On MME perception, UVC-Both raises the Total score by +50.00 (Lumina, to 475.00) and +53.34 (MMaDA, to 546.67), with the largest relative gains on each model's weakest category (Position +22.2% on Lumina; Count +46.5% on MMaDA). On open-ended captioning (CHAIR, 500 COCO val images), UVC-Both reduces CHAIR_S by 42.8% (Lumina) and 56.3% (MMaDA) while keeping average caption length virtually unchanged. Inference overhead is at most 1.01× across all generation lengths (16–128 tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run direct head-to-head comparisons against existing AR-centric hallucination mitigation methods (e.g., VCD [20], OPERA [15]); instead, it argues they are structurally incompatible with iterative demasking—contrastive decoding doubles per-step latency and the confidence-based unmasking schedule causes 84.7% of positions to be unmasked at entirely different steps under degraded vs. clean inputs, breaking the positional alignment those methods require. The primary empirical baseline is the Vanilla (unmitigated) model, against which all UVC variants are compared. UVC-Both consistently outperforms both single-scale ablations (UVC-Global, UVC-Instance) on F1 across all nine POPE settings for Lumina-DiMOO, while on MMaDA the two ablations show complementary strengths (UVC-Instance leads Accuracy in all nine settings at +16.4 avg; UVC-Global leads F1 in five of nine), and UVC-Both achieves the best F1 in all nine. On MME, UVC-Both also dominates both ablations on Total score for both models. The CHAIR evaluation further confirms that UVC-Both reduces hallucination rates substantially (up to 56.3% relative CHAIR_S reduction) without suppressing generation length, whereas no competing method is evaluated in this open-ended setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes activation editing techniques from the interpretability literature (activation patching, factual association editing) into a hallucination mitigation framework, but departs fundamentally from all prior AR-centric methods (VCD, OPERA, contrastive decoding) by showing they are structurally incompatible with bidirectional demasking rather than merely suboptimal. The method is built on the multimodal dLLM generation paradigm (LLaDA, Lumina-DiMOO, MMaDA), where iterative demasking under bidirectional attention creates a diffuse hallucination pattern distinct from the sequential error propagation in autoregressive models. The key conceptual departure is the mask-aware, step-aligned injection design: pre-computed contrastive shift vectors are applied exclusively at still-masked positions, respecting the generation logic that has no autoregressive counterpart.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multimodal dLLMs gain traction as an alternative to autoregressive LVLMs, having a native, training-free mitigation method with <1% overhead is practically important for deployment. The 84.7% demasking divergence finding provides a concrete quantitative argument for why contrastive decoding cannot be ported, which may guide future method design in this paradigm. Open questions include whether semantically guided (rather than Gaussian) degradations yield stronger shift vectors, and whether the approach extends to closed-weight models where intermediate activations are inaccessible. The method's reliance on open-weight access and a single degradation type limits its generality, and it remains to be seen whether the gains hold as dLLM architectures scale or incorporate more complex multimodal inputs.
