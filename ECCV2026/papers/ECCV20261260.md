## Problem

Existing work on multimodal emotion recognition (MER) with MLLMs assumes that reallocating attention from generated tokens to modality tokens during inference universally improves performance. This paper reveals a counterintuitive contradiction: this strategy only helps in two-modality settings but degrades performance in three-or-more-modality scenarios, where the opposite reallocation direction is more effective. The underlying mechanism—increasingly entangled cross-modal attention in deeper layers that distorts token-level information allocation—remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the AffectGPT pipeline, where video and audio inputs are encoded by CLIP ViT-L and HuBERT-L respectively, projected into latent token sequences, and concatenated with text prompt tokens (and an optional pre-fusion multimodal token H_z in the three-modality setting) before being fed to a Qwen3 LLM decoder. The model generates natural language emotion descriptions autoregressively. The proposed attention rectification is applied entirely at inference time: it reads attention maps from intermediate and final transformer layers during each decoding step, modifies the final-layer attention before the next-token prediction, and introduces no additional parameters or architectural changes.

## Method

The paper proposes a training-free attention rectification method with two components. First, a Shallow Layer Extraction module identifies an optimal intermediate layer l* within the multimodal formative phase by maximizing the Jensen-Shannon divergence between that layer's attention distribution and the final layer's, selecting the layer that retains the most structured, modality-specific signal before deeper cross-modal interactions entangle it. Second, an Attention Refinement module computes the residual ΔA = A^(l*) − A^(L) between the selected shallow-layer and final-layer attention maps, injects this residual into the final-layer attention scaled by a factor g, and renormalizes to preserve a valid probability distribution. The key insight is that shallow-layer attention preserves relatively clean token-level structure while deeper layers accumulate complex cross-modal mixing that misallocates attention; using the shallow map as a structural prior selectively restores attenuated modality-specific signals without disturbing the high-level semantic reasoning encoded in deeper layers.

## Results

On MER-UniBench, the proposed method achieves the best average score across all three modality settings: 75.38 (audio-text), 76.98 (video-text), and 78.08 (video-audio-text), outperforming the primary baseline AffectGPT by 3.2, 2.7, and 3.3 points respectively. The largest gains appear in basic emotion recognition (e.g., MER2023: 84.49 vs. 78.54 for AffectGPT in the three-modality setting) and fine-grained emotion recognition on OV-MERD+ (65.32 vs. 62.52). The method also surpasses all other compared approaches by a wide margin, with the nearest non-AffectGPT competitor (SALMONN at 57.89 in audio-text, mPLUG-Owl at 62.45 in video-text, Emotion-LLaMA at 64.17 in video-audio-text) trailing by 10–17 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 22 compared baselines across every modality setting and dataset in MER-UniBench. Against the primary baseline AffectGPT, gains range from +2.7 to +3.3 points in average score, with the largest per-dataset improvement on MER2023 in the three-modality setting (84.49 vs. 78.54, +5.95). Among non-AffectGPT competitors, the gap is substantially larger: in the audio-text setting, SALMONN (57.89) and Qwen-Audio (49.26) trail by 17.5 and 26.1 points respectively; in video-text, mPLUG-Owl (62.45) and VideoChat (56.71) trail by 14.5 and 20.3 points; in video-audio-text, Emotion-LLaMA (64.17) and R1-Omni (59.61) trail by 13.9 and 18.5 points. The method also surpasses dedicated attention-adjustment strategies (DoLA, OPERA, entropy-based, average-based, training-based) in Table 5, achieving 78.08 vs. the best alternative OPERA at 75.61 in the three-modality setting. Notably, the method slightly underperforms AffectGPT on MOSEI in the three-modality setting (79.07 vs. 80.90) and on MOSI in the audio-text setting (81.79 vs. 83.46), but these small per-dataset losses are more than offset by large gains on other datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AffectGPT (Lian et al., ICML 2024) as its baseline MLLM-based MER framework and on the MER-UniBench benchmark. It departs from prior attention reallocation work (Liu et al., ECCV 2024; and related inference-time attention adjustment methods like DoLA and OPERA) that assumed reallocating attention from generated tokens toward modality tokens universally improves multimodal performance. The key conceptual departure is revealing that this assumption inverts in three-or-more-modality settings, and proposing a training-free shallow-layer attention prior to regularize entangled deep-layer attention, drawing on cross-modal information flow analysis (Zhang et al., CVPR 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The contradictory reallocation phenomenon challenges a widely adopted heuristic in the MLLM community and provides a mechanistic explanation (layer-wise attention entanglement under multi-modality interactions) that could inform attention design in broader multimodal tasks beyond emotion recognition. The training-free nature makes the method immediately deployable without retraining. However, the analysis is conducted on a single backbone (Qwen3) within the AffectGPT architecture, leaving open whether the phenomenon and the proposed fix generalize to other MLLM families, different pre-fusion designs, or non-emotion multimodal tasks. A key open question is whether the shallow-layer structural prior degrades as model scale or modality count increases further.
