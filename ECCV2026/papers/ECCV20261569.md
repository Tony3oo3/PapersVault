## Problem

Existing Video VAEs learn inter-frame correlations implicitly through causal or group-causal convolutions, treating static content and dynamic motion as an entangled learning target. This overlooks the fundamental codec design principle of explicitly decomposing video compression into keyframe (I-frame) encoding and inter-frame motion residual (P-frame) encoding, resulting in less efficient training and suboptimal reconstruction of high-frequency details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261569/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts video clips at standard resolutions (e.g., 256×256 for ablations, up to 480p for evaluation) and produces a compact latent representation with configurable compression ratios of 4×8×8 (16 channels) or 8×8×8 (32 channels) in temporal×height×width. A single RGB image is handled uniformly by repeating it into a minimal pseudo-video, so the same encoding-decoding pipeline applies to both images and videos without architectural asymmetry. The decoder reconstructs the full video sequence from the latent code, with the keyframe latent broadcast across all temporal positions at initialization.

## Method

VC-VAE initializes its weights from a pre-trained Flux image VAE (inflating 2D kernels to 3D with Tail/Center Initialization), so at the start of training the model selects a keyframe, encodes it with the inherited image VAE, and broadcasts it across the sequence—reducing the learning objective to capturing only temporal dynamics. The key architectural innovation is the Temporal Dynamic Difference Convolution (TDC), which re-parameterizes standard 3D convolution kernels into an anchor term (W_a * X_t) preserving static spatial content and difference terms (W_p * (X_{t-1} - X_t), W_f * (X_{t+1} - X_t)) that explicitly learn sparse motion residuals, adding zero extra parameters or FLOPs. TDC is applied in both ResBlocks (within Group Causal Convolution) and temporal interaction modules, where it operates on separate anchor and motion-residual streams. Training proceeds in two stages: 500k steps on Kinetics-600 with L1, LPIPS, and KL losses, followed by 50k steps of multi-resolution finetuning with a 3D adversarial discriminator for high-frequency detail. An In-Sequence First-Frame Processing strategy eliminates the separate first-frame pathway, ensuring uniform reconstruction quality across all frames.

## Results

Under the standard 4×8×8 compression setting, VC-VAE (146M params) achieves SOTA reconstruction quality on both WebVid-10M (PSNR 36.04, LPIPS 0.03315, SSIM 0.9459) and UCF-101 (PSNR 38.97, LPIPS 0.02747, SSIM 0.9684), outperforming all baselines on perceptual and structural metrics. Under the more aggressive 8×8×8 compression, VC-VAE (194M) further improves to PSNR 36.40 and SSIM 0.9485 on WebVid-10M, substantially surpassing the WFVAE baseline (PSNR 35.86, SSIM 0.9396). Notably, VC-VAE achieves these results with only 550k training steps, roughly 50% fewer than WFVAE (1200k) and IVVAE (1000k). On the challenging TokBench-Video benchmark, VC-VAE leads in text reconstruction accuracy (T-ACC mean 66.24 at 256×) and face similarity (F-Sim mean 0.80 at 256×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VC-VAE consistently outperforms all five 4×8×8 baselines (CogVideoX, Wanx2.1, IVVAE, WFVAE, Hunyuan) on PSNR, LPIPS, and SSIM across both WebVid-10M and UCF-101, with the largest margins over CogVideoX (+0.81 dB PSNR on WebVid) and IVVAE (+0.83 dB PSNR on WebVid). On FVD, VC-VAE (36.80) is competitive with Hunyuan (36.23) and WFVAE (36.86) but does not achieve the absolute lowest score, suggesting FVD is less sensitive to the perceptual gains captured by LPIPS/SSIM. Under the 8×8×8 high-compression setting, VC-VAE decisively beats WFVAE on every metric (FVD 36.63 vs 44.13, PSNR 36.40 vs 35.86, SSIM 0.9485 vs 0.9396), demonstrating robustness under aggressive temporal downsampling. In terms of training efficiency, VC-VAE requires only 550k steps versus 1200k for WFVAE and 1000k for IVVAE, while simultaneously achieving better reconstruction quality. On the generation task (gFVD on SkyTimelapse), VC-VAE achieves 112.8, outperforming IVVAE (118.6) and OD-VAE (140.0), confirming that the improved latent space also benefits downstream diffusion-based video generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Group Causal Convolution architecture from IVVAE [34] and the Tail/Center initialization strategies from OD-VAE [7], while using the Flux image VAE [15] as a spatial prior. Its key conceptual departure is making explicit what prior Video VAEs (CogVideoX, IVVAE, WF-VAE) only implicitly mirror: the I-frame/P-frame/B-frame decomposition of video codecs. By introducing the Temporal Dynamic Difference Convolution (TDC) operator and keyframe-anchored initialization, VC-VAE forces the network to learn sparse motion residuals relative to a static anchor rather than entangled spatio-temporal features, a design principle borrowed from H.264/AVC but not previously instantiated in a learned Video VAE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% reduction in training steps (550k vs. 1000k–1200k for SOTA baselines) while achieving equal or better reconstruction quality is a practically significant efficiency gain for teams training Video VAEs at scale. The TDC operator's zero-parameter, zero-FLOP overhead makes it an attractive drop-in modification for existing causal or group-causal Video VAE architectures. Open questions include whether the keyframe-anchored decomposition degrades on highly dynamic content where the 'static background' assumption weakens, and whether the approach transfers to autoregressive or discrete-token Video VAE formulations beyond the continuous-latent diffusion setting tested here.
