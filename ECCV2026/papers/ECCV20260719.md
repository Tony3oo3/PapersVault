## Problem

Existing spherical parameterization methods compute each genus-0 surface independently, and because the space of valid conformal parameterizations forms a Möbius group, geometrically similar shapes often receive very different parameterizations. This inconsistency hinders applications like texture transfer and shape correspondence that require a canonical, shared spherical domain across a shape collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260719/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of genus-0 triangular meshes (roughly normalized in a shared ambient space, not required to be consistently meshed) along with a unit sphere mesh as input, and produces a continuous bijective mapping from each shape's surface to the unit sphere. The learned generative model g^θ(z, p) maps sphere coordinates p ∈ S² to surface points on the target shape, so the parameterization is obtained as the inverse of this mapping. At inference time, a new shape is parameterized in under 1 second by querying the trained generator.

## Method

GenSP learns a neural shape generative model through a four-stage pipeline: (1) augmenting the training set with intermediate shapes produced by CMCF flow between each input shape and the sphere; (2) training a SALD-style implicit autoencoder with a deformation-preserving regularization loss (using a reduced ARAP model on 200 farthest-point-sampled nodes) to organize the latent space so similar shapes are nearby; (3) computing initial sphere-to-shape correspondences by propagating registrations along a minimum spanning tree in latent space rooted at the sphere, rather than interpolating directly; and (4) fine-tuning a continuous neural deformation model g^θ(z, p) with a Jacobian-based regularization loss that penalizes deviation from both isometric (JᵀJ = I) and conformal (JᵀJ = scalar·I) mappings. The key architectural choice is a continuous sphere-to-surface mapping rather than a mesh generator, avoiding discretization artifacts from large conformal-factor variations, and the deformation regularization normalizes by absolute displacement to handle irregular latent-space distributions.

## Results

On the ShapeNet genus-0 test set (100 shapes), GenSP achieves a mean isometric distortion σ_t of 1.681 and max σ_t of 7.719, reducing these by 14.3× and 146× respectively compared to the flow-based CMCF baseline (24.22 and 1.13×10³). For cross-shape consistency, GenSP scores 1.425 on the mean c(m_i, m_j) metric, which is 3.6× better than the best optimization-based baseline SMAT (5.122) and 4.2× better than AHSP (6.006). GenSP's conformal distortion (mean σ_t^con = 1.212) is slightly higher than CMCF (1.029) but far below VC25 (6.892), and inference runs in under 1 second versus minutes for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenSP dominates on the consistency metric, scoring 1.425 versus 5.122 (SMAT), 6.006 (AHSP), and 6.573 (VC25) for the optimization-based baselines, and 1.626 for the flow-based CMCF — a 3.6× improvement over the best per-shape optimizer. On isometric distortion, GenSP (mean σ_t = 1.681, max σ_t = 7.719) is on par with SMAT (1.503, 3.392) and AHSP (1.578, 2.349) but dramatically outperforms CMCF (24.22, 1.13×10³) and ARAP (177.19, 1.219×10⁶). The one metric where GenSP trails is conformal distortion: CMCF and ARAP achieve 1.029 versus GenSP's 1.212, though GenSP still far exceeds VC25 (6.892). In terms of bijectivity, GenSP has 0.099% bad faces (9 affected meshes out of 100), which is worse than SMAT (0.004%) and AHSP (0.002%) but substantially better than CMCF (0.241%), ARAP (0.434%), and VC25 (1.898%). For the correspondence application (Figure 6), GenSP produces more coherent inter-class and intra-class correspondences than Diff3F, Diffumatch, and ULRSSM while running at least 100× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenSP sits at the intersection of three research threads: flow-based spherical parameterization (CMCF), implicit shape generative models (SALD), and deformation-regularized shape generators (ARAPReg, GenCorres). It departs from prior flow-based methods by replacing the PDE-driven deformation with a data-driven path of intermediate shapes learned from a generative model, and from prior mesh-based generative models by using a continuous neural deformation field that avoids discretization artifacts from large conformal-factor variations. The spanning-tree correspondence propagation in latent space draws on joint shape matching literature, but is applied here specifically to initialize a sphere-to-shape generator rather than to compute pairwise shape maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first approach to simultaneously achieve low geometric distortion and cross-shape consistency in spherical parameterization, a trade-off that has persisted across four generations of methods. The practical impact is significant for applications requiring consistent parameterizations across shape collections (texture transfer, shape morphing, geometric analysis), and the <1-second inference time makes it deployable. Key limitations include restriction to genus-0 shapes, lack of formal bijectivity guarantees, and the extrinsic (rather than intrinsic) formulation of the generative model. Extending to higher genera and developing intrinsic shape generative models are identified as important open directions.
