## Problem

Camera-LiDAR fusion pipelines are designed so that a single physical object cannot simultaneously deceive both modalities, yet prior adversarial attacks target only one sensing stream and typically assume white-box gradient access. This paper asks whether a single, physically realizable 3D object can induce consistent failures across both a monocular 2D detector and a Camera-LiDAR fusion 3D detector under strict black-box, query-only constraints, while remaining geometrically and visually indistinguishable from a natural pedestrian.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three decoupled text prompts (a fixed base prompt establishing pedestrian identity, a semantic prompt controlling geometry/shape, and a style prompt controlling appearance) along with calibrated scene camera images and LiDAR sweeps for rendering and insertion. The LLM agent receives only post-NMS bounding boxes and confidence scores as feedback—no logits, gradients, or intermediate features. The output is a 3D Gaussian Splatting object that supports both photorealistic alpha-composited camera rendering and beam-based LiDAR point-return simulation, evaluated across four yaw viewpoints (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) at a fixed 5–15 m range.

## Method

Agent-OBJ is a three-stage, prompt-driven pipeline: an LLM agent (Qwen2.5) iteratively proposes discrete add/replace/remove edits to the semantic and style prompts; a text-to-multi-view generator (LGM) produces view-consistent geometry, a multi-view personalization module (MV-Adapter) applies style-conditioned appearance edits, and 3DGS reconstruction yields a renderable adversarial object. Optimization is entirely gradient-free and score-based: a threshold-aware attack loss couples both detectors at the confidence level, a naturalness regularizer enforces pedestrian plausibility via a PointNet feature bank (minimum cosine distance to 71k nuScenes pedestrian embeddings) plus a box-scale prior, and a multi-view stability term penalizes cross-view variance of the per-view attack loss. The agent follows a budget-aware schedule that prioritizes semantic edits when the object becomes implausible and complementary factor adjustments when one detector remains confident, with a caching mechanism that reuses geometry initialization when only the style prompt changes. This decoupling of geometry and appearance into separate text-conditioned channels enables interpretable, low-entropy exploration that is substantially more query-efficient than unconstrained continuous 3D perturbation under the same black-box budget.

## Results

On nuScenes (10 validation scenes, 4 viewpoints), Agent-OBJ achieves ASR_2D = 68.0%, ASR_F = 96.0%, and ASR_joint = 64.0% under a strict black-box, query-only protocol with 800 total detector queries per keyframe. The method also maintains strong stealthiness (FeatureDist = 0.12, close to the benign reference of 0.08) and the lowest cross-view variation (MV-Std = 5.3) among all variants. Transferability is confirmed zero-shot to KITTI (ASR_joint = 58.9%) and across detector swaps (e.g., BEVFusion yields ASR_joint = 66.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Agent-OBJ substantially outperforms all prior object-level and fusion attacks on the joint success metric: it achieves ASR_joint = 64.0% versus 26.1% for the closest black-box competitor (Frustum Attack [15]) and 20.9% for the white-box Invisible Attack [6]. Against single-modality baselines, Agent-OBJ's ASR_F = 96.0% far exceeds AdvPC's 67.3% (white-box LiDAR) and ScAR's 49.8%, while its ASR_2D = 68.0% is competitive with white-box image attacks like NI-FGSM (71.4%) despite operating under a strict query-only constraint. The key differentiator is that Agent-OBJ is the only method to simultaneously degrade both modalities under a black-box setting, whereas all prior fusion attacks (Invisible Attack, Frustum Attack) achieve at most 26.1% joint success. In the internal variant comparison (Table 1), the joint objective is critical: removing it drops ASR_joint from 64.0% to 40.8%, and the multi-view stability regularizer prevents viewpoint-specific overfitting (removing it yields ASR_joint = 30.5% with higher variance). Query efficiency also favors Agent-OBJ, reaching 80% of final ASR in 584 queries versus 648 for greedy and 712 for random editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial attacks on autonomous driving perception from single-modality (camera-only or LiDAR-only) to a unified cross-modal setting, and from gradient-based white-box optimization to a strict black-box, query-only paradigm. It repurposes text-to-3D generation pipelines (LGM, MV-Adapter) and 3D Gaussian Splatting as a controllable instantiation mechanism, replacing the continuous parameter search used in prior differentiable-rendering attacks (e.g., MeshAdv, 3DGS-based camouflage) with a discrete, LLM-guided prompt-editing loop. The decoupling of semantic (geometry) and style (appearance) prompts, combined with a joint threshold-aware objective across both detectors, departs from prior work that optimizes each modality independently or relies on model-internal gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational safety assumption in multi-sensor autonomous driving: that a single physical object cannot simultaneously deceive both camera and LiDAR in a consistent manner. The demonstrated 64% joint ASR under a realistic black-box budget, along with cross-dataset transferability to KITTI, suggests the attack exploits structural coupling in fusion architectures rather than dataset-specific artifacts, raising the urgency for cross-modal consistency defenses. Key limitations include the reliance on simulated LiDAR insertion (beam-based ray casting) rather than true physical deployment, evaluation on a single primary dataset (nuScenes), and the notably lower 2D ASR (68%) compared to fusion ASR (96%), indicating the camera branch remains harder to fool. Open questions include whether the attack transfers to truly physical objects under uncontrolled lighting and weather, and what architectural changes in fusion pipelines could close this vulnerability.
