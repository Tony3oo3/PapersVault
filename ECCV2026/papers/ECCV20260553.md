## Problem

Event-based class-agnostic segmentation underperforms its RGB counterpart because the spatial sparsity and low redundancy of event data produce weak intra-class consistency, making dense prediction of object boundaries difficult. Prior work (EventSAM) addressed only feature-level modality alignment while neglecting the fundamental input-level information deficit in event streams, leaving a gap in how to recover the scene structure that dense RGB images provide for free through color and texture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260553/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams (pixel coordinates, timestamps, and ±1 polarity) over a discrete time segment, which are first discretized into a 3-bin Voxel Grid at the native resolution (346×260 for RGBE-SEG/MVSEC, 964×696 for ComScene). In prompt mode, 8 randomly sampled points per ground-truth mask (and optionally bounding boxes) serve as additional inputs to the frozen SAM prompt encoder. The output is a dense, class-agnostic segmentation mask assigning each pixel to an object region or background without any semantic label.

## Method

IC²-ESeg grounds its design in the physical event-generation mechanism to mine three learning-free representations: a noise map (estimating scene intensity from the triggering probability of isolated shot-noise events via a closed-form Gaussian model), a sparse motion map (velocity from multi-scale local plane fitting on the Surface of Active Events), and a dense brightness map (Poisson reconstruction of brightness gradients derived from event density and the motion field). These are injected into a SAM ViT-B student via a staged strategy: brightness is added residually to the Voxel Grid input, while noise and motion are converted into low-dimensional visual prompts through lightweight MLP adapters (MLP_tune → GELU → MLP_up) and summed into the patch embedding at every encoder layer. Training follows a teacher-student distillation paradigm where a frozen SAM image encoder provides feature-level supervision (correlation-aware pivotal-token alignment loss) and offline pseudo-labels from SAM ViT-H supervise the mask decoder with a focal-plus-dice segmentation loss, with only the student's FFNs and patch embedding left trainable to preserve SAM's generalization.

## Results

On RGBE-SEG (All split), IC²-ESeg achieves 0.43 mIoU / 0.57 aIoU in automatic mode, outperforming the prior best class-agnostic event method EventSAM (0.41/0.55) by 4.9%/3.6%. In prompt mode, the improvement is substantially larger: 0.65 mIoU / 0.71 aIoU versus EventSAM's 0.49/0.61, a gain of 32.7%/16.4%. On the zero-shot generalization datasets, IC²-ESeg improves mIoU by 28.0% (MVSEC) and 48.0% (ComScene) over EventSAM with point prompts only, and by 16.0% and 6.5% respectively when both point and box prompts are used.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IC²-ESeg outperforms all nine baselines on the primary mIoU and aIoU metrics in both automatic and prompt modes. Against the closest class-agnostic event baseline EventSAM [7], the gains are 4.9%/3.6% (automatic) and 32.7%/16.4% (prompt) in mIoU/aIoU. The RGB-based SAM [18] serves as an upper-bound reference but degrades severely on event data (0.26 mIoU vs. 0.43 for IC²-ESeg), confirming the modality gap. Image-reconstruction methods (E2VID [28], ETNet [37]) and knowledge-distillation methods (NGA [16], LFI [10]) all fall well below 0.35 mIoU, as they do not address the intra-class consistency problem at the input level. Pre-trained encoder methods (DTL [34], EVDistill [35], ESS [31]) achieve 0.27–0.29 mIoU, with EVDistill showing the weakest aIoU (0.34) due to its high-precision/low-recall trade-off. On zero-shot transfer (Table 2), IC²-ESeg consistently surpasses EventSAM across all four ComScene scene categories and both MVSEC indoor/outdoor splits, with the largest margin in the Traffic category (0.49 vs. 0.25 mIoU with point prompts).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based class-agnostic segmentation task first introduced by EventSAM (Chen et al., CVPR 2024), which relied solely on feature-level similarity between modalities. The key departure is addressing the modality gap at the input level: rather than only aligning token embeddings, the authors exploit the physical event-generation mechanism to construct noise, motion, and brightness representations in a learning-free manner, then inject them via a SAM-Adapter-style architecture. The cross-modal distillation paradigm (teacher SAM on images, student on events) follows the broader trend of adapting SAM to non-RGB modalities (SAM-Event-Adapter, EvSAM, ESEG), but is the first to target class-agnostic rather than semantic segmentation. The motion representation directly reuses the Surface of Active Events formulation from Benosman et al. (2013), and the brightness gradient estimation builds on the brightness-constancy Taylor expansion used in event-based optical flow literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Event-based class-agnostic segmentation is a nascent task with only one prior method (EventSAM), so establishing a clear SOTA and a principled explanation for *why* event data underperforms (weak intra-class consistency due to sparsity) provides a useful conceptual anchor for the subfield. The introduction of ComScene, a 964×696 RGB-event dataset covering four scene categories, addresses the resolution and diversity limitations of existing event segmentation benchmarks. However, the ICM module's 24 ms preprocessing latency and the feedforward (non-recurrent) design limit real-time applicability, and the paper itself flags temporal modeling as an open direction. The generality across SAM, DINOv2, and MAE encoders suggests the ICM+LIJ pipeline could be a reusable component for other event-based dense prediction tasks beyond segmentation.
