## Problem

Existing 3D affordance grounding methods rely on limited supervised signals (bounding boxes, affordance-centric captions) and fail to generalize to out-of-distribution, open-world scenarios. The paper identifies that text-to-image diffusion models, trained on internet-scale data, inherently encode rich affordance knowledge in their generative priors—evidenced by their ability to produce physically plausible human-object interaction images—but this knowledge has never been systematically extracted and transferred to 3D affordance prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N×3 coordinates), an RGB human-object interaction reference image, and an affordance text query as input, and produces a dense per-point 3D affordance mask as output. Notably, the diffusion model is used in a single forward pass at timestep t=0 (not the full multi-step denoising process), and the text conditioning is replaced by an implicit embedding derived from the image itself via a frozen CLIP encoder, eliminating the need for explicit text prompts during feature extraction.

## Method

DAG extracts affordance priors from a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net by feeding a noisy version of the HOI image (at t=0) conditioned on an implicit text embedding generated by an Affordance Semantics Encoder (ASE) that projects CLIP image features into the U-Net's text space. Multi-scale U-Net features are combined via a learned weighted aggregation network into unified affordance priors, which are then fused with CLIP text embeddings through proposed Affordance Blocks (self-attention + cross-attention + FFN). A lightweight multi-source decoder performs two rounds of cross-attention—first between the [CLS] token from a frozen Uni3D point encoder and the affordance embeddings, then between the result and dense point features—before an MLP produces the final per-point affordance mask. Training is supervised with Dice + BCE loss for 80 epochs, with the diffusion U-Net and point encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On PIAD1 (Seen), DAG achieves mIoU 24.84, AUC 90.16, SIM 0.637, and MAE 0.078, outperforming the strongest baseline GREAT by 2.12 mIoU and 2.22 AUC. On the more challenging Unseen setting, DAG reaches mIoU 9.73, AUC 76.69, SIM 0.414, and MAE 0.120, again leading all baselines. On PIAD2 (Seen), the gains are even larger: DAG scores mIoU 47.19 and AUC 94.71 versus GREAT's 38.03 and 91.99. Under the 1-shot few-shot setting, DAG (mIoU 12.98, AUC 79.42) still surpasses PFU trained on all data (mIoU 12.31, AUC 77.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAG outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on both Seen and Unseen splits of PIAD1 and PIAD2. The closest competitor is GREAT (Shao et al., 2024), which uses affordance-centric captions; DAG exceeds it by 2.12 mIoU (24.84 vs. 22.72) and 2.22 AUC on PIAD1 Seen, and by 0.91 mIoU and 3.08 AUC on PIAD1 Unseen. Among the cross-modal learning baselines, IAGNet (Yang et al., ICCV 2023) and LASO (Li et al., CVPR 2024) are the strongest, achieving mIoU 20.51 and 21.14 respectively on PIAD1 Seen, yet DAG still leads by over 3.7 mIoU. The earlier cross-modal methods (ReferTrans, ReLA/GRES, PFU, XMF) trail significantly, with mIoU values between 5.3 and 12.9 on PIAD1, indicating that direct image-to-point mapping without generative priors captures far less affordance structure. Notably, even in the 1-shot setting (less than 1% training data), DAG (mIoU 12.98, AUC 79.42) surpasses PFU trained on the full dataset (mIoU 12.31, AUC 77.50), underscoring the transferable world knowledge embedded in the frozen diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAG departs from prior 3D affordance grounding methods (IAGNet, MIFAG, GREAT, LASO) that use CNN-based pipelines to directly map 2D HOI images to 3D point clouds, by instead treating a frozen text-to-image diffusion U-Net as an implicit affordance feature extractor. It builds on the emerging line of work repurposing diffusion internals for downstream vision tasks (ODISE for panoptic segmentation, Diffuse Attend and Segment for zero-shot segmentation), but is the first to systematically extract and transfer affordance-specific knowledge from diffusion representations into a 3D grounding framework. The Affordance Semantics Encoder (ASE) draws on CLIP-based implicit prompting, while the point encoder and cross-modal decoder follow standard image-to-point-cloud alignment strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that generative priors from internet-scale diffusion models encode transferable affordance concepts, offering a path toward open-world 3D affordance grounding without requiring affordance-centric annotations or large 3D supervision. The few-shot results (1-shot outperforming full-data PFU) and consistent gains on unseen objects/affordances suggest practical relevance for embodied agents operating in novel environments. However, absolute mIoU remains modest (≈25 seen, ≈10 unseen on PIAD1), the diffusion model is entirely frozen with no task-specific adaptation, and the approach still requires paired 2D-3D training data, leaving open whether truly zero-shot 3D affordance grounding is achievable.
