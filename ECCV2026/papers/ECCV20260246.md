## Problem

End-to-end human mesh recovery compresses geometry, correspondence, and occlusion reasoning into a single regression target, producing entangled errors that are difficult to localize. Fitting-based methods avoid this by optimizing against detected cues, but sparse keypoints provide limited surface constraints and are fragile under occlusion or challenging conditions. Prior dense-proxy approaches (DensePose-style) were hindered by noisy manual annotations and poor generalization of deterministic CNN detectors, leaving the dense-correspondence route largely superseded.

## Input / Output

The method takes N calibrated multi-view RGB images (default 4 full-body views at 1024×1024) with known intrinsics and extrinsics (no lens distortion), plus hand-region crops generated on-the-fly as additional views. The intermediate output is a dense SMPL-X proxy per view: a 256×256×3 segmentation map (body-part labels as RGB colors) and a 256×256×3 UV map (surface coordinates in channels 1–2, channel 3 set to 1), both encoded as three-channel images to match Stable Diffusion 2.1's VAE decoder. The final output is SMPL-X parameters (shape β, pose θ, translation T) recovered by optimization, with facial expression ψ left unoptimized.

## Method

DiffProxy repurposes Stable Diffusion 2.1 as a multi-conditional proxy generator: a frozen UNet backbone is augmented with four trainable attention modules (text cross-attention, image cross-attention, cross-modality attention, and epipolar-constrained self-attention with Plücker ray embeddings) and a T2I-Adapter, conditioned on text prompts specifying the target modality, T2I-Adapter features for pixel-level alignment, and DINOv2 tokens for pose/appearance priors. The model is trained with the standard diffusion denoising objective on 105K synthetic multi-view samples (844K images) rendered from BEDLAM and SynBody with pixel-perfect SMPL-X annotations, eliminating label noise. At inference, a two-pass hand refinement strategy crops and enlarges hand regions as additional views with adjusted intrinsics and a hand-specific prompt subdividing 12 finger/palm parts, while test-time scaling draws K stochastic samples per view to compute per-pixel uncertainty (UV variance + segmentation majority-vote disagreement) that down-weights unreliable correspondences. SMPL-X parameters are then recovered by minimizing an uncertainty-weighted reprojection loss over all foreground pixels, regularized by a one-step DPoser-X denoising prior, using a coarse-to-fine Adam schedule over global placement, body pose/shape, and hand articulations.

## Results

DiffProxy achieves state-of-the-art MPVPE across all five real-world benchmarks: 50.9 mm on 3DHP, 35.1 mm on RICH, 39.9 mm on BEHAVE, 38.8 mm on MoYo, and 43.1 mm on 4D-DRESS partial. The largest gains appear on challenging datasets where sparse-keypoint fitting degrades: on RICH, MPVPE drops from 60.3 mm (EasyMoCap) to 35.1 mm (−42%), and on 4D-DRESS partial from 119.4 mm to 43.1 mm (−64%). On MoYo, DiffProxy (38.8 mm) outperforms the best multi-view end-to-end method HeatFormer (123.6 mm) by 69%. The model is trained exclusively on synthetic data (105K multi-view samples) with no dataset-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest fitting-based competitor EasyMoCap (sparse keypoint fitting), DiffProxy wins on MPVPE across all five datasets, with the largest margins on RICH (35.1 vs. 60.3 mm, −42%) and 4D-DRESS partial (43.1 vs. 119.4 mm, −64%), where dense surface constraints prove far more robust than sparse keypoints under complex lighting, extreme poses, and heavy occlusion. Against multi-view end-to-end methods, DiffProxy dominates: HeatFormer achieves 53.3 mm on its training set 3DHP but collapses to 123.6 mm on MoYo and 327.0 mm on 4D-DRESS partial, while DiffProxy maintains 38.8 mm and 43.1 mm respectively, demonstrating superior cross-dataset generalization from synthetic training. Single-view end-to-end methods (SMPLest-X, Human3R) are consistently outperformed on absolute positioning (MPVPE) by 20–70 mm across all datasets, confirming that fitting against dense proxies is more effective than direct parameter regression. Against the discriminative DensePose baseline (fine-tuned on the same synthetic data for 60 epochs), DiffProxy achieves 38.8 mm vs. 111.4 mm MPVPE on MoYo, a 65% improvement, attributed to diffusion priors enabling robust generalization and supporting test-time scaling for uncertainty estimation. On BEHAVE, the margin over EasyMoCap is smallest (39.9 vs. 40.4 mm MPVPE), as this studio-capture dataset provides clean keypoint detections where sparse constraints are sufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the dense-proxy route for human mesh recovery (pioneered by DensePose and explored by HoloPose, DenseRaC, DecoMR, MeshPose) that was largely superseded by end-to-end regression methods due to noisy manual annotations and poor CNN generalization. It addresses both historical bottlenecks simultaneously: training on 105K synthetic multi-view samples with pixel-perfect SMPL-X correspondences (following the BEDLAM/SynBody paradigm) and repurposing Stable Diffusion 2.1 as the proxy generator (following Marigold and GenPercept's demonstration that diffusion backbones retain strong visual priors for dense prediction). The multi-view consistency mechanism borrows epipolar-constrained attention from SPAD, while the fitting stage uses DPoser-X as a diffusion-based pose prior, making the entire pipeline diffusion-centric from generation through regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the dense-proxy paradigm was abandoned prematurely—its failure was a data and model-capacity problem, not a fundamental flaw—and that a purely synthetic-trained diffusion model can outperform all real-data-trained end-to-end and fitting-based methods across five diverse benchmarks. This has practical implications for precision-oriented applications (film, biomechanics, digital humans) where fitting-based recovery is preferred but was previously limited by sparse keypoint constraints. Open questions include resolving depth-ambiguity local optima in the reprojection objective (Fig. 10), extending to multi-person scenes, and whether consistency models or distillation can close the ~50s inference gap with real-time methods.
