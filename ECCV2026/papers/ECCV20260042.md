## Problem

Existing T2I evaluation benchmarks suffer from three key flaws: prompts are fixed-length and semantically redundant (often <60% unique), evaluation relies on coarse CLIP-based or detector-based metrics that miss fine-grained instruction following, and no benchmark systematically tests robustness to prompt-length variation despite models showing clear sensitivity to it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 5,000 prompts is provided in both a short and a long (paraphrased) version with identical core semantics, and is paired with a checklist of N attribute-specific binary yes/no questions. The evaluator consumes the generated image alongside these questions and outputs per-attribute binary predictions; the final score is the fraction of correctly answered questions. Text rendering is scored via GNED (0–1, lower is better, reported as 1−GNED), and style control uses average CSD + DINOv3 similarity against aspect-ratio-matched reference images.

## Method

The core contribution is the TIIF Evaluator: a Qwen3VL-8B VLM fine-tuned in a reasoning-based paradigm where it first generates chain-of-thought explanations and then outputs a binary answer for each attribute question. Training data (~218K balanced questions) is annotated by GPT-4o over ~300K images from 30 T2I models, with a 560-question human-annotated test set for validation (GPT-4o achieves 90.18%, the fine-tuned evaluator reaches 91.07%). Prompts are constructed via an attribute-composition strategy that randomly combines elements from 10 concept pools across 6 dimensions (attribute, relation, reasoning, text rendering, style control, designer-level), yielding 36 combination types at three difficulty levels. For text rendering, the proposed GNED metric computes optimal bipartite word matching via the Hungarian algorithm with a penalty for unmatched tokens, normalized by max(m,n), robustly penalizing both omission and hallucination.

## Results

On TIIF-Bench (5,000 prompts, 12,074 binary questions), NB-Pro achieves the highest overall instruction-following score of 93.1 (short) / 93.3 (long), followed by Nano-Banana (89.2/88.5) and GPT-Image-1 (86.1/87.6). Among open-source diffusion models, Qwen-Image (87.0/87.5) and FLUX.2 dev (87.2/87.3) lead, while among unified models Emu3.5-Image (88.4/87.1) is strongest. Text rendering (GNED-based, higher = better) sharply separates closed-source leaders (NB-Pro 84.0/85.0, Nano-Banana 82.0/82.0) from most open-source diffusion models (0–80), and style control reaches ~100% for FLUX.2 dev, Emu3.5, and Lumina-mGPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NB-Pro (93.1/93.3) outperforms all other models by a wide margin, beating Nano-Banana (89.2/88.5) by ~4 points and GPT-Image-1 (86.1/87.6) by ~7 points overall. Among open-source diffusion models, Qwen-Image (87.0/87.5) and FLUX.2 dev (87.2/87.3) are the strongest, surpassing SD 3.5 (69.5/68.5) by ~18 points and PixArt-Σ (61.0/59.7) by ~26 points. Emu3.5-Image (88.4/87.1) is the top unified model and actually exceeds Qwen-Image and FLUX.2 dev on overall score, though it trails them on text rendering (82.0 vs. 79.0/71.0) and matches them on style control (~100%). Text rendering is the sharpest differentiator: NB-Pro (84.0/85.0) and Nano-Banana (82.0/82.0) lead, while PixArt-Σ, Lumina-Next, and Hunyuan-DiT score near 0, and even SD 3.5 drops from 55.0 to 43.0 on long prompts. On the designer-level dimension, the gap between closed-source (NB-Pro 95.2, Nano-Banana 92.9, GPT-Image-1 90.8) and the best open-source (Emu3.5-Image 90.5, Qwen-Image 89.9) narrows to ~5 points, whereas weaker open-source models (Hunyuan-DiT 39.1, PixArt-α 44.8) lag by 50+ points. Prompt-length robustness correlates with overall quality: NB-Pro and GPT-Image-1 show <2-point short-to-long drops, while SANA Sprint drops 6.5 points and SD 3.5 drops 12 points on text rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIIF-Bench extends the line of structured T2I evaluation benchmarks (CompBench++, GenAI Bench, GenEval, Gecko) by addressing three identified gaps: fixed-length prompts, semantic redundancy, and coarse evaluation metrics. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes existing components—CLIP embeddings for deduplication, VLM-based evaluation (as in T2I-CompBench++), and text-rendering prompts from Lex-Art—into a unified attribute-composition framework with a dedicated fine-grained binary-question evaluator (TIIF Evaluator) trained on ~218K annotated samples. The key conceptual departure is the shift from holistic image-text alignment scores to per-attribute yes/no verification with chain-of-thought reasoning, combined with systematic prompt-length augmentation to probe robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides a reproducible, fine-grained evaluation protocol that achieves near-perfect Spearman correlation with human rankings, addressing a long-standing reliability gap in T2I evaluation. Two findings have practical implications: (1) prompt-length robustness strongly correlates with overall instruction-following quality, suggesting that models sensitive to paraphrasing have fundamental comprehension weaknesses; (2) unified autoregressive models (Emu3.5, Janus-Pro) can match or exceed diffusion models on instruction following despite lower visual fidelity, highlighting a potential architectural trade-off. Open questions include multilingual evaluation, rare-object generalization, and whether stylistic register (formal vs. conversational) affects performance—limitations the authors explicitly acknowledge.
