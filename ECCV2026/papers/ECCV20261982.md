## Problem

Humanoid robots cannot yet generate synchronized, semantically meaningful co-speech gestures in real time due to three intertwined barriers: the scarcity of large-scale, semantically annotated audio-motion data for robot embodiments, a 'modality eclipse' in which generative models collapse into repetitive historical motion patterns while ignoring incoming audio cues, and the sim-to-real gap where retargeted human motions produce unsafe self-collisions on physical hardware. Existing approaches either rely on text intermediates that discard prosodic information, lack streaming support, or produce motions that are physically infeasible for real robots.

## Input / Output

The system consumes streaming speech audio tokenized by the Mimi codec (which hierarchically decouples high-level semantic tokens from fine-grained prosodic/residual tokens) together with a 60-frame historical motion window (2T where T=30). It outputs 30-frame (1-second) chunks of 41-DoF joint trajectories in the native kinematic space of a Unitree G1 humanoid with BrainCo dexterous hands (17 DoF upper torso/arms + 24 DoF hands), generated at ~120 FPS with an MPC safety filter adding only 5.6 ms per frame. The entire pipeline operates in a streaming-to-streaming paradigm, consuming and producing fixed-length 1-second chunks to integrate with upstream ASR/LLM/TTS modules.

## Method

The framework comprises three modules trained in two stages. A Hierarchical Semantic-Acoustic Aligner processes Mimi codec tokens through a multi-level transformer where shallow layers feed a beat head (rhythmic supervision via velocity magnitude and acoustic onset) and deep layers feed a 300-class semantic head, producing complementary low-level rhythmic pulses and high-level semantic labels. A Streaming Conditional Motion Generator based on a Diffusion Transformer parameterized by Conditional Flow Matching receives these cues through a dual injection mechanism: cross-attention fuses multi-scale acoustic features with past motion for framewise micro-alignment, while FiLM applies affine modulation for global semantic tone. To break the modality eclipse, Anti-Inertia CFG Masking randomly drops the past-motion condition with 15% probability, forcing the network to derive motion from audio alone. Training proceeds in two stages—Stage 1 pretrains the aligner with beat regression and semantic classification losses on 1000 hours of semi-synthetic data; Stage 2 jointly optimizes the generator with a spatio-temporally weighted Velocity Matching Loss (emphasizing hand joints at 4× and semantic-interval frames at 5–10×), a Kinetic Consistency Loss penalizing inter-frame jitter, and a semantic classification auxiliary. At inference, an MPC-based safety filter (solved per-frame via OSQP as a convex QP with collision, velocity, and tracking constraints) reduces self-collision rate from 4.16% to 0.13%.

## Results

On the BEAT benchmark, RoboGesture achieves state-of-the-art FGD (0.8452 vs. 2.2316 for DiffSHEG), Beat Consistency (0.1866), and MSE (0.1347), while maintaining a near-zero collision rate of 0.88%. On the out-of-domain SemanticBEAT test set, it leads on all three available metrics: BC (0.2950), DIV (0.2041), and Collision Rate (0.13%, an order-of-magnitude improvement over the next best 1.20%). In human evaluation, RoboGesture secures the highest scores across all four dimensions (Rhythmic Alignment, Semantic Accuracy, Physical & Hand Consistency, Overall Preference) on both datasets, with the largest margin in Semantic Accuracy (+0.4270 on BEAT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboGesture decisively outperforms all four baselines on the primary fidelity metrics. On BEAT, it reduces FGD from 2.23 (DiffSHEG, the strongest baseline) to 0.85 and MSE from 0.175 to 0.135, while cutting the collision rate from 21.4% (LivelySpeaker) and 52.8% (SemTalk) to 0.88%. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is Diversity on BEAT, where Semantic Gesticulator scores 0.2818 vs. 0.2075, though the authors note this can be inflated by jittery motions. On the more challenging SemanticBEAT set, RoboGesture's advantage is most dramatic in safety: its 0.13% collision rate is roughly 10× lower than DiffSHEG (1.20%) and 100× lower than SemTalk (42.65%). In human evaluation, RoboGesture leads every dimension on both datasets, with the largest gap in Semantic Accuracy (+0.427 on BEAT vs. +0.226 for SG), confirming that its text-free, audio-driven semantic alignment produces gestures perceived as more contextually appropriate than LLM-retrieval-based or CLIP-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the co-speech gesture generation line (LivelySpeaker, DiffSHEG, SemTalk, Semantic Gesticulator) in two key directions: it abandons text-based intermediate representations in favor of a text-free pipeline that extracts multi-granular prosodic and semantic cues directly from raw audio tokens via the Mimi codec, and it learns motion generation natively in robot joint space rather than in human mocap space with post-hoc retargeting. The motion generator builds on the Diffusion Transformer + Conditional Flow Matching paradigm, while the safety layer borrows MPC-based constrained optimization from robotics. The Anti-Inertia CFG Masking training strategy is a novel departure from prior diffusion-based gesture generators, addressing the 'modality eclipse' problem where models over-rely on kinematic history rather than audio conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first complete, deployed end-to-end systems that closes the listen-respond-gesture loop on a physical humanoid in real time, addressing the sim-to-real gap that has kept prior co-speech gesture work confined to virtual avatars. The semi-synthetic data pipeline (LLM-driven scenario generation + retargeting + MPC collision filtering) offers a practical recipe for scaling robot-specific gesture data beyond the ~76 h BEAT corpus. Open questions include generalization to full-body (locomotion + manipulation) gestures, multi-person interaction, and whether the MPC safety filter can be replaced by learned safety constraints to further reduce inference overhead. The upper-body-only scope (41 DoF) and single-robot evaluation limit immediate transferability.
