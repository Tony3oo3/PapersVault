## Problem

Existing video world models generate futures entirely within imagined or synthetic environments, with no mechanism to ground generation in a specific real-world location. This paper asks whether a world simulation model can faithfully render a city that physically exists, enabling applications like urban planning visualization and location-based exploration that require spatial fidelity to actual streets and buildings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261789/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a starting geographic coordinate, a target camera trajectory (sequence of 6-DoF poses), and a free-form text prompt, SWM autoregressively generates video chunks (77 frames under Teacher Forcing, 12 frames under Self-Forcing) in a 3D-VAE-compressed latent space. At each chunk, the model retrieves up to K nearby street-view pinhole views from a geo-indexed database of 1.2M panoramas, which are consumed as both a dynamic attention sink and as geometric/semantic conditioning references. Camera poses for all latents (target, reference, sink) are encoded via Plücker ray embeddings and concatenated to the latent channels.

## Method

SWM fine-tunes the Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B Diffusion Transformer on 440K Seoul street-view images, 10K synthetic CARLA videos, and Waymo driving clips, using retrieval-augmented conditioning to anchor each generated chunk to real-world geometry and appearance. Three key mechanisms address the challenges of city-scale grounding: (1) cross-temporal pairing during training, where references and targets are captured at different timestamps so the model learns to rely on persistent structure rather than transient objects; (2) a Virtual Lookahead Sink that dynamically retrieves the nearest street-view to each chunk's endpoint and inserts it at a future RoPE position, replacing the static first-frame attention sink that becomes irrelevant as the camera travels; and (3) dual referencing pathways—geometric referencing via depth-based forward splatting of the nearest reference into the target view for spatial layout, and semantic referencing by injecting all K reference latents into the transformer sequence for appearance detail. An Intermittent Freeze-Frame strategy synthesizes temporally coherent training videos from sparse 5–20 m street-view keyframes by repeating each keyframe four times to match the 3D VAE's temporal compression stride.

## Results

On Busan-City-Bench, SWM (TF) achieves FID 28.43, FVD 301.76, RotErr 0.020, TransErr 0.015, mPSNR 14.56, and mLPIPS 0.392, outperforming all six baselines on every metric. On the unseen Ann-Arbor-City-Bench, SWM (TF) maintains its lead with FID 56.61, FVD 640.17, RotErr 0.055, TransErr 0.154, mPSNR 15.18, and mLPIPS 0.481. The Self-Forcing variant (SWM SF) achieves FID 43.97 and FVD 779.94 on Ann Arbor at 15.2 fps on a single H100, trading some quality for real-time streaming capability. The largest gaps over the strongest baseline (HY-World 1.5) are in FID (28.43 vs. 49.63 on Busan) and mLPIPS (0.392 vs. 0.588 on Busan), reflecting superior structural fidelity to real locations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWM (TF) dominates all six baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. The closest competitor is HY-World 1.5 [20], which achieves the best FID among baselines (49.63 on Busan, 67.02 on Ann Arbor) but still trails SWM by 43% and 16% respectively; on 3D adherence (mPSNR), HY-World scores 11.87/14.26 versus SWM's 14.56/15.18. Aether [61] and DeepVerse [7] suffer the most severe degradation, with FID values exceeding 130 on Busan and FVD above 1000, indicating substantial drift and scene collapse over long trajectories. Yume 1.5 [32] achieves the best image quality score among baselines (0.73 on Busan) but has the worst camera-following accuracy (RotErr 0.153, TransErr 0.104 on Busan), reflecting poor trajectory adherence. FantasyWorld [8] and Lingbot [46] fall in the middle, with FID in the 60–84 range and mPSNR below 12, confirming that without retrieval-augmented grounding, existing world models cannot maintain spatial fidelity to real urban layouts over hundreds-of-meters trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWM builds on the Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B Diffusion Transformer as its backbone and extends the autoregressive chunk-by-chunk video generation paradigm with retrieval-augmented conditioning on geo-indexed street-view images. The Virtual Lookahead Sink adapts the attention-sink mechanism from streaming language models and long-video generation (Rolling Forcing, MotionStream) by replacing the static first-frame anchor with a dynamically retrieved future-location image, drawing inspiration from lookahead anchoring in talking-head animation. The cross-temporal pairing and Intermittent Freeze-Frame strategies are novel data-construction and VAE-alignment techniques specific to the sparse, temporally misaligned nature of street-view databases, departing from standard video diffusion training on continuous clips.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first world simulation model grounded in a physically existing city, opening applications in urban planning visualization, autonomous driving scenario generation, and location-based exploration that were previously impossible with purely generative world models. The cross-city generalization to Busan and Ann Arbor without fine-tuning suggests the retrieval-augmented grounding framework is not overfit to Seoul's specific layout, though scalability to cities with sparse or unavailable street-view coverage remains an open question. A key limitation is the dependence on high-quality, dense street-view databases and the current inability to handle real-time dynamic changes (construction, seasonal variation) in the underlying city, which constrains the model's long-term utility as a living digital twin.
