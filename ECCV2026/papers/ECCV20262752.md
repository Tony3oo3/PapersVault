## Problem

Covariate shift detection is critical for reliable vision systems, yet existing approaches rely on fully supervised training that requires labeled examples from both the original and shifted distributions—an impractical assumption in many real-world settings. The paper shows that Positive-Unlabeled (PU) learning is a natural weaker-supervision alternative, but classical PU risk estimators become unstable under covariate shift because in-distribution and shifted distributions overlap significantly, yielding a small total variation distance and high intrinsic Bayes risk.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of labeled positive (in-distribution) images and an unlabeled pool containing a mixture of in-distribution and covariate-shifted images (shifted proportion can range from 5% to 75%). Features are extracted from the 6th transformer block of a pre-trained ViT, and the output is a binary shift-detection label (in-distribution vs. shifted) with a continuous confidence score suitable for AUROC/AUPR/FPR95 evaluation. No negative labels are ever required; the unlabeled pool is the sole source of shifted examples.

## Method

S-PUNA is a geometry-aware, iterative pseudo-labeling framework that progressively uncovers the shifted (negative) distribution through local manifold expansion rather than global risk estimation. At each iteration, it computes symmetric k-NN distances from each unlabeled candidate to both the growing positive bank and negative bank, then assigns the top-β most positive-like and top-β most negative-like samples to their respective sets, refining the decision boundary from both sides simultaneously. A novel spectral entropy stopping criterion monitors the eigenvalue distribution of the negative set's covariance matrix and halts expansion once entropy decreases, preventing the algorithm from invading overlapping regions where P⁺ and P⁻ are indistinguishable. After convergence, the non-parametric k-NN pseudo-labels are distilled into a lightweight MLP classifier trained with cross-entropy, which smooths the decision boundary while preserving the local geometric structure discovered by the neighborhood expansion.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, S-PUNA (with classifier) achieves 97.89% AUROC and 9.69% FPR95 under near covariate shift, surpassing the strongest PU baseline Dist-PU (84.48% AUROC, 35.05% FPR95) by over 13 points in AUROC and 25 points in FPR95. Under far shift, S-PUNA reaches 98.51% AUROC with 7.36% FPR95, and averages 98.20% AUROC / 8.52% FPR95 across both regimes. On TinyImageNet, S-PUNA attains near-perfect detection (100.00% AUROC, 0.00% FPR95 near shift; 99.64% AUROC, 1.51% FPR95 far shift), and on EuroSAT it achieves 100.00% AUROC with 0.00% FPR95 on far shift, outperforming all baselines by 10+ AUROC points in that regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-PUNA decisively outperforms all five PU-learning baselines (k-NN, Dist-PU, saPU, DC-PU, LaGAM) across every dataset and shift regime. On ImageNet near shift, the gap is largest: S-PUNA's 97.89% AUROC exceeds Dist-PU (84.48%) by 13.4 points and LaGAM (83.01%) by 14.9 points, while FPR95 drops from 35.05% (Dist-PU) to 9.69%. DC-PU and k-NN are particularly weak, with DC-PU averaging only 65.56% AUROC and k-NN 64.59% on ImageNet. Among the broader OOD/anomaly detection baselines benchmarked in Appendix F (ReAct, ASH, MDS, ViM, DiffPath, PatchCore, SimpleNet, Neco), S-PUNA matches or exceeds the best supervised method DisCoPatch (89.8% avg AUROC on near shift) while requiring only positive labels. On TinyImageNet, S-PUNA slightly edges out LaGAM (99.77% vs 99.76% avg AUROC) and Dist-PU (99.71%), and on EuroSAT far shift it achieves perfect 100% AUROC versus Dist-PU's 96.48% and LaGAM's 86.24%, confirming that the geometry-aware pseudo-labeling advantage grows with shift severity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the k-NN-PU pseudo-labeling framework of Zhang & Zuo [40], which propagates only positive labels, into a symmetric two-class construction that iteratively discovers both positive and negative manifolds. It departs from classical PU risk minimization (Dist-PU, DC-PU, saPU, LaGAM) by arguing that global risk estimation becomes unstable under the high distributional overlap characteristic of covariate shift, and instead relies on local geometric structure guided by a novel spectral entropy stopping criterion. The work also positions covariate shift detection as fundamentally distinct from both domain adaptation/generalization (which aims to be invariant to shift) and semantic OOD detection (which targets label-space changes), carving out a middle ground where the shift itself is the detection target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that weakly supervised PU learning can match or exceed fully supervised covariate shift detectors (e.g., DisCoPatch), the paper opens a practical path for shift monitoring in settings where negative labels are unavailable or expensive—common in finance, healthcare, and sensor systems. The spectral entropy stopping criterion provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism to prevent pseudo-label contamination, a problem that plagues iterative pseudo-labeling broadly. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional or non-visual feature spaces, how it behaves under compound shifts (simultaneous covariate and semantic shift), and whether the reliance on a pre-trained ViT backbone limits applicability to domains without strong pre-training.
