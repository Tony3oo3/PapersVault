## Problem

Modern NVS methods (3DGS, 2DGS, Triangle Splatting) optimize primitives that evolve independently, producing disconnected and geometrically inconsistent scene representations that are difficult to integrate into standard CG pipelines. This limits both the geometric fidelity of the learned scene and the rendering speed achievable in consumer applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262129/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of captured RGB images with COLMAP-estimated camera parameters, initializes per-view depth maps (default: ZoeDepth), and selects a representative subset of 15 views via PAM clustering. At each rendering frame, 6 (then 4) input views are warped and blended. The output is a novel-view rendering plus an optimized RGBD scene representation stored as a connected triangle mesh (87 MB at full resolution), which is lighter than point-cloud-based representations and directly compatible with standard rasterization pipelines.

## Method

∂DIBR converts each input image into a flat 2D triangle mesh (pixels as vertices) and warps it to the target viewpoint via depth-based reprojection. Two non-differentiable operations are relaxed: rasterization uses 4-sample multi-sampling per pixel to smooth visibility discontinuities, and the hard disocclusion-discard decision is replaced by a sigmoid probability over six geometric quality terms. Warped views are blended with quality- and alpha-weighted averaging. Training follows a coarse-to-fine strategy across three resolution levels, with color learning rate initially three orders of magnitude below depth to prevent overfitting inaccurate geometry; the loss combines L1 photometric error with anisotropic total variation on depth maps. The entire pipeline is implemented in Slang/SlangPy with Vulkan differentiable shaders, achieving 277 FPS on an RTX 4090—roughly 2× faster than 3DGS—while preserving geometric consistency and connectivity throughout optimization.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset, ∂DIBR achieves an average PSNR of 21.81 dB, SSIM of 0.722, and LPIPS of 0.202, compared to 3DGS (24.73 dB), 2DGS (23.88 dB), and Triangle Splatting (21.91 dB). On the Shiny dataset, ∂DIBR reaches 22.44 dB PSNR, 0.735 SSIM, and 0.171 LPIPS, versus 3DGS (23.25 dB), 2DGS (22.56 dB), and TS (23.15 dB). In terms of rendering speed, ∂DIBR achieves 277 FPS on an RTX 4090, a 1.68×–2.42× speed-up over all baselines (3DGS: 168 FPS, 2DGS: 114 FPS, TS: 110 FPS), while using a smaller representation (87 MB vs. 246 MB for 3DGS and 353 MB for TS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

∂DIBR consistently trails 3DGS by approximately 2.9 dB (LLFF) and 0.8 dB (Shiny) in PSNR, confirming the paper's stated trade-off of up to 3 dB quality loss for speed. Against Triangle Splatting, the closest geometric competitor, ∂DIBR is nearly tied on LLFF (21.81 vs. 21.91 dB) but lags by 0.71 dB on Shiny (22.44 vs. 23.15 dB); however, ∂DIBR renders 2.5× faster (277 vs. 110 FPS) and uses a 4× smaller representation (87 MB vs. 353 MB). Compared to 2DGS, ∂DIBR is slightly worse on PSNR (−2.07 dB on LLFF, −0.12 dB on Shiny) but renders 2.4× faster. The key differentiator is rendering throughput: ∂DIBR's 277 FPS is the highest among all methods, making it the only approach suitable for real-time or resource-constrained deployment, at the cost of somewhat blurrier fine details and higher LPIPS values across both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the long-established Depth Image-Based Rendering (DIBR) pipeline—originally a handcrafted CG technique for free-viewpoint video—into a differentiable, optimizable framework for modern NVS. It departs from the dominant splatting paradigm (3DGS, 2DGS) and from prior triangle-based methods (Triangle Splatting, BG-Triangle) by enforcing geometric connectivity through a fixed triangulation of input images rather than allowing primitives to evolve independently. The differentiability challenges specific to DIBR (hard z-buffer decisions, disocclusion discarding) are addressed via multi-sampling and a sigmoid-based probabilistic quality formulation, building on the general differentiable-rendering literature but applied to a mesh-based pipeline for the first time in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× rendering speed-up (277 FPS vs. 168 FPS for 3DGS) combined with native Vulkan/Slang integration makes this approach directly deployable in consumer-grade real-time pipelines (VR/AR, 6DoF video), where splatting-based methods face integration friction. The observation that DIBR's hard dependence on depth accuracy yields more globally consistent geometry than splatting methods—which compensate geometric failures with view-directional encoding—suggests a useful design principle for future NVS representations. Key open questions include closing the ~3 dB quality gap (particularly on sharp depth discontinuities), incorporating view-dependent appearance, and extending beyond forward-facing scenes to 360° captures where DIBR disocclusions become severe.
