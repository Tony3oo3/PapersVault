## Problem

Most LiDAR-based human mesh reconstruction methods follow a decoupled pipeline that first estimates skeletal joints and then reconstructs the mesh, causing errors to propagate and amplify under the inherent sparsity of LiDAR point clouds. These approaches overlook the spatial constraints that the surface mesh can impose on skeletal joints, treating the skeleton-to-mesh relationship as strictly one-directional.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261531/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-frame sparse LiDAR point cloud, normalized to a fixed set of 512 points via random sampling or repetition-based padding, and produces SMPL body parameters (24 joint rotation matrices in 6D representation), a dense 3D human mesh of 6,890 vertices, and 24 3D skeleton joint positions. During the intermediate refinement stage, the body surface is represented by 256 vertices selected via farthest point sampling on the SMPL T-pose template, which serve as mesh queries alongside the 24 joint queries.

## Method

SynHMR formulates both skeletal joints and sampled mesh vertices as learnable queries with explicit positional information, jointly refined through a Geometry-Aware Transformer that performs self-attention among joint, vertex, and noisy-joint queries (enabling bidirectional information exchange) and cross-attention to multi-level PointNet++ point features (grounding queries in local geometry). A Gaussian Fourier Positional Encoding module injects high-frequency spatial cues at each decoder layer, and a Noise-Augmented Learning strategy perturbs joint queries with Gaussian noise during training to force the network to exploit joint-mesh synergy for correction. After L=6 iterative refinement layers, a topology-aware spatial GCN over the anatomical skeleton graph regresses SMPL rotation parameters from the refined joint features, producing the final mesh via differentiable SMPL forward kinematics.

## Results

SynHMR achieves state-of-the-art performance on all three LiDAR-based HMR benchmarks. On LiDARHuman26M, it reaches 74.25 mm MPJPE and 96.22 mm PVE; on SLOPER4D, 34.09 mm MPJPE and 41.32 mm PVE; and on Human-M3, 56.76 mm MPJPE and 70.70 mm PVE. Compared to the previous best method LiDAR-HMR, SynHMR reduces MPJPE by 1.82 mm (LiDARHuman26M), 13.69 mm (SLOPER4D), and 19.59 mm (Human-M3), with corresponding PVE reductions of 5.51, 8.43, and 16.22 mm. The method also achieves the highest PCK-30 and PCK-50 scores across all datasets (e.g., 97.03% PCK-30 on SLOPER4D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynHMR outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor LiDAR-HMR [9] (a coarse-to-fine single-frame method), SynHMR reduces MPJPE by 1.82 mm on LiDARHuman26M, 13.69 mm on SLOPER4D, and 19.59 mm on Human-M3, with PVE improvements of 5.51, 8.43, and 16.22 mm respectively. The gap is largest on Human-M3, where the outdoor multi-person setting with natural occlusion most stresses sparse-point-cloud robustness. Compared to the temporal method LiDARCap [21] (multi-frame), SynHMR improves MPJPE by 5.06 mm on LiDARHuman26M while using only a single frame, and by 67.80 mm on SLOPER4D. Against the image-based HybrIK [22], SynHMR reduces MPJPE by 30.92 mm on LiDARHuman26M and 28.85 mm on Human-M3, confirming the advantage of direct 3D geometric input over 2D image cues. The ablation further shows that the synergistic joint-mesh modeling (adding mesh queries to joint queries) contributes the largest single gain (−3.95 mm MPJPE over joint-only), while GFPE and Noise-Augmented Learning each add further 0.5–1.0 mm improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynHMR builds directly on the LiDARCap architecture (PointNet++ encoder, MLP joint decoder, GCN-based SMPL regression) but fundamentally departs from the decoupled pipeline paradigm that dominates LiDAR-based HMR methods (LiDAR-HMR, VoteHMR, SAHSR), where joints are estimated first and the mesh is reconstructed conditionally. The key conceptual shift is treating skeletal joints and sampled mesh vertices as co-equal queries in a shared transformer space, enabling bidirectional refinement rather than one-way skeleton-to-mesh propagation. The Noise-Augmented Learning strategy draws from Poseur's perturbation-based training, and the Gaussian Fourier Positional Encoding adapts random Fourier feature ideas to 3D coordinate encoding for sparse point-cloud settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in LiDAR-based human perception: the inherent sparsity of LiDAR returns causes error cascades in sequential joint-then-mesh pipelines, particularly for thin body parts like limbs. The synergistic formulation is a clean and general principle that could transfer to other sparse-sensing HMR settings (e.g., mmWave radar, event cameras). However, the accuracy gains over the strongest baseline (LiDAR-HMR) are modest on the primary benchmark (1.82 mm MPJPE reduction on LiDARHuman26M), and the method remains single-person, single-frame, and limited to the SMPL parametric model. Open questions include whether the joint-mesh synergy extends to multi-person scenes, non-parametric meshes, or significantly noisier sensing modalities, and whether the 256-vertex sampling strategy is optimal across diverse body shapes.
