## Problem

Existing small object detectors that leverage frequency-domain filtering only suppress noise in manually selected bands, overlooking that background distractors are distributed across the entire spectrum. Additionally, the shared appearance semantics among objects of the same class remain underexploited, as prior methods rely on instance-level grouping metrics (IoU, cosine similarity) rather than class-wise supervision, limiting generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260033/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB aerial or remote sensing images as fixed-size patches (800×800 for AI-TOD, 1200×1200 for SODA-D/A) and outputs axis-aligned or oriented bounding boxes with class labels via a two-stage RPN + RoI pipeline. The backbone (ResNet-50 or Spatial-Mamba) produces multi-scale features that are aggregated through a standard FPN before entering the spectrum-aware processing stages.

## Method

SFDNet introduces two core components. The Adaptive Spectrum Disentanglement (ASD) module decomposes each FPN level into low, mid, and high frequency components via Difference-of-Gaussian filtering in the Fourier domain; each component is then processed by a Sequential Spectrum Mamba (S3M) that applies FFT, separates amplitude and phase spectra, adds learnable positional embeddings, and runs a Mamba-style selective SSM under a spectrum-specific scanning order (low-to-high spiral for high-freq, high-to-low for low-freq, alternating cross-scan for mid-freq) to capture long-range spectral dependencies while biasing aggregation toward the relevant band. The three enhanced spectra are adaptively reweighted by learned importance vectors and fused through an FFN. The Class-Wise Prototype Distillation (CPD) procedure constructs per-class prototypes from ground-truth RoI embeddings, refines them via affinity-weighted averaging with momentum updates across mini-batches, and enforces compact intra-class representations by applying an InfoNCE contrastive loss that aligns each proposal's RoI embedding with its class prototype while repelling other prototypes.

## Results

On AI-TOD, SFDNet* (Spatial-Mamba backbone) achieves 31.7% AP, surpassing the previous best (DINO-5scale w/SET at 26.6%) by 5.1 points, with particularly strong gains on tiny objects (APvt: 17.6 vs 13.2). On SODA-A, SFDNet* reaches 39.2% AP, outperforming GauCho (33.2%) by 6.0 points. On SODA-D, SFDNet* achieves 34.2% AP, exceeding Unc-SOD (31.0%) by 3.2 points. The ResNet-50 variant (SFDNet) also sets new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks, confirming the framework's backbone-agnostic effectiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFDNet* wins decisively on all three benchmarks. On AI-TOD, it surpasses the strongest end-to-end baseline (DINO-5scale w/SET, 26.6% AP) by 5.1 points and the strongest multi-stage baseline (SimD, 26.6% AP) by 5.1 points, with the largest relative gains on tiny objects (APvt: 17.6 vs 13.4, +31%). On SODA-A, SFDNet* (39.2% AP) outperforms GauCho (33.2%) by 6.0 points and DCFL (36.6%) by 2.6 points, with the most dramatic improvement on APgs (50.4 vs 47.8). On SODA-D, SFDNet* (34.2% AP) leads Unc-SOD (31.0%) by 3.2 points and HS-FPN (29.6%) by 4.6 points. The ResNet-50 variant (SFDNet) also beats all baselines on every benchmark, indicating the gains come from the ASD+CPD design rather than backbone choice. Notably, SFDNet* improves AP75 by 6.5 points over HS-FPN on AI-TOD, suggesting the spectrum disentanglement particularly benefits localization precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFDNet extends the frequency-aware small object detection line (HS-FPN, SET, DNTR) by moving from single-band suppression with hand-tuned hyperparameters to full-spectrum disentanglement via DoG decomposition with adaptive reweighting. It repurposes Mamba's selective state space model for a novel purpose—spectrum-specific long-range aggregation in the Fourier domain—where the scanning order (low-to-high, high-to-low, alternating cross-scan) is tailored to each frequency band's sensitivity profile. The Class-Wise Prototype Distillation procedure departs from prior instance-level relationship methods (super-resolution, memory retrieval) by introducing explicit class-conditional prototype supervision via InfoNCE contrastive alignment, filling a gap the authors identify in the lack of class-wise semantic consistency modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The full-spectrum disentanglement framework addresses a concrete limitation of prior frequency-aware detectors that only suppress one band, and the consistent 4–7% AP gains across three aerial benchmarks (AI-TOD, SODA-D, SODA-A) suggest the approach generalizes across dense and sparse small-object distributions. The spectrum-specific SSM scanning strategy is a reusable design pattern that could transfer to other frequency-domain vision tasks. However, the method is evaluated exclusively on aerial/remote sensing imagery, and the DoG-based decomposition introduces fixed σ and k parameters (though shown to be insensitive), leaving open whether the spectral partitioning boundaries are optimal for non-aerial small objects or for objects with larger scale variance. The added FFT/IFFT and SSM computation also raises practical deployment questions for real-time drone or satellite pipelines.
