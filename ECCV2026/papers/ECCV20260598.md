## Problem

Existing action-conditioned world models are trained from scratch on narrow, domain-specific datasets with bespoke architectures, limiting their scalability and generalization. The paper asks whether off-the-shelf video diffusion models, pre-trained on Internet-scale passive video, can be converted into precisely controllable world models using only a small amount of paired action-observation data, without sacrificing the rich physical and visual priors acquired during pre-training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single egocentric RGB frame and a sequence of robot actions (3-DoF planar navigation as Δx, Δy, Δφ, or 25-DoF humanoid joint angles) and produces a future video rollout of up to 16 frames at 4 FPS (4 seconds), with supplementary results extending to 4× longer horizons. Actions are downsampled via 1D convolutions to match the spatiotemporal VAE's temporal compression factor (k=1 for SVD, k=4 for Cosmos and Wan), and generation occurs in the VAE latent space before decoding to pixel space at 512×512 (SVD) or 480×640 (Cosmos). For humanoid embodiments, an additional initial joint-state vector is provided to disambiguate body parts invisible in the egocentric view.

## Method

EgoWM's core insight is that every video diffusion architecture—UNet or DiT—modulates latent features through a denoising timestep embedding, providing a universal injection point for action control. A lightweight MLP projects each D-dimensional action vector into the model's embedding dimension, and 1D convolutions downsample the resulting sequence to the latent temporal resolution (T/k). At each modulation block, the action embedding is added to the replicated timestep embedding before the block-specific projection produces scale, shift, and gate parameters, making modulation frame-specific and action-aligned without altering the base architecture. For 25-DoF humanoids, an initial-state embedding is additionally summed into the modulation to resolve ambiguity of occluded body parts. The model is fine-tuned with the original pretraining objective (denoising or flow matching) at a 10× higher learning rate for the action projection module, preserving pretrained representations while learning precise action following.

## Results

On the RECON validation set (3-DoF navigation), all EgoWM variants outperform Navigation World Models (NWM) across every metric and prediction horizon. The headline Structural Consistency Score (SCS) improves by up to 65% over NWM at the 16-frame horizon (SVD: 55.2 vs. NWM: 33.4; Cosmos-2B: 47.5; Wan-14B: 49.7). Perceptual fidelity also improves, with Cosmos-2B achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.33) and DreamSim (0.10) at frame 16. For downstream planning, EgoWM(Cosmos) reduces Average Trajectory Error to 3.13 vs. NWM's 3.25 on 16-frame navigation trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Navigation World Models (NWM, Bar et al., CVPR 2025), a 1B-parameter autoregressive diffusion model trained from scratch on egocentric robot and human navigation videos. EgoWM wins decisively on all three metrics (LPIPS, DreamSim, SCS) at every prediction horizon (frames 2, 4, 8, 16) on the RECON validation set. The most striking gap is in SCS at frame 16, where SVD achieves 55.2 versus NWM's 33.4 (a 65% relative improvement), and Cosmos-2B reaches 47.5 (42% improvement). On perceptual metrics, Cosmos-2B and Wan-14B also outperform NWM, reflecting the benefit of Internet-scale pretraining (e.g., DreamSim 0.10 vs. 0.19 at frame 16). For downstream navigation planning, both EgoWM variants achieve lower Average Trajectory Error than NWM (3.13 and 3.18 vs. 3.25), though the margin is modest due to the constrained straight-line trajectory prior. EgoWM also requires substantially less compute: 8 A100 GPUs versus NWM's 64 H100 GPUs, with up to 6× faster 64-frame inference on a single A100.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoWM carves new ground by repurposing the universal denoising-timestep modulation pathway already present in every video diffusion model as the injection point for action conditioning, rather than designing architecture-specific conditioning layers (as in Ctrl-World's cross-attention or IRASim) or training a bespoke model from scratch (as in NWM's custom cDiT). Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two lines of work: action-conditioned world models (Ha & Schmidhuber, NWM, Ctrl-World) and large-scale video diffusion pretraining (SVD, Cosmos, Wan2.1), bridging them with a minimal, architecture-agnostic adapter. The key departure from prior work is the observation that the timestep embedding, which every diffusion model already uses for generation control, can be augmented with temporally-aligned action embeddings to achieve precise control without altering the base architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, drop-in recipe for converting any existing or future video diffusion model into an action-conditioned world model, which is likely to be widely adopted as video diffusion backbones continue to improve. The introduction of SCS addresses a real gap in evaluation—perceptual metrics can reward visually sharp but physically inconsistent predictions—and offers the community a more faithful measure of action following. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even higher-dimensional or continuous action spaces, how long-horizon prediction degrades beyond the 4-second window tested, and whether the SCS metric generalizes to domains where passive scene structure is sparse or highly dynamic. The painting generalization, while visually striking, raises questions about whether the model truly understands physics or is exploiting strong appearance priors.
