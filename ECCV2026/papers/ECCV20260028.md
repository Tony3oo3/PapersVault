## Problem

Self-supervised video denoising via Blind-Spot Networks enforces noise independence by masking the center frame, which prevents the model from exploiting direct spatial evidence for texture recovery. This creates an inherent conflict: the very constraint that guarantees statistical validity severs spatiotemporal correlations and causes loss of high-frequency spatial textures. The paper asks whether temporal consistency modeling and spatial texture restoration can be decoupled into separate training stages to resolve this trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260028/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy video sequence of T frames (T=9 for synthetic Gaussian noise, T=7 for raw video) along with pre-computed optical flow (PWC-Net) and pre-computed per-frame image denoising outputs (NAFNet). Each frame is decomposed into a joint input z_i = cat(x̂_i, r_i) where x̂_i is the denoiser output and r_i = y_i − x̂_i is the high-frequency residual, so the network operates in a residual domain rather than on raw pixels. During inference, only the trained Spatial Refiner is deployed on the original noisy joint inputs and flow to produce the final denoised frame as x̂_final = f_θ′({z}, V) + D(y_t).

## Method

F2R is a two-stage spatiotemporal decoupling framework built on a shared 4-level U-Net backbone (C=72) that differs only in its skip connections. Stage 1 trains a Blind Estimator via a Frame-wise Blind Strategy that excludes the entire center frame, using a Flow-Guided Attention Alignment Module (FAAM) with dual channel-spatial attention to conservatively fuse warped neighbor features and predict a temporally consistent residual anchor. Stage 2 trains a non-blind Spatial Refiner via a Recorruption Strategy that replaces the center frame with a noise-injected anchor, forcing active denoising; it employs a Flow-Guided Deformable Alignment Module (FDAM) using DCN initialized from optical flow to perform aggressive sub-pixel alignment and recover the spatial residuals that the image denoiser smoothed away. The residual-domain formulation offloads static structural modeling to the frozen NAFNet prior, letting the network focus exclusively on high-frequency texture recovery, while the decoupled alignment modules (conservative FAAM for blind Stage 1, precise FDAM for non-blind Stage 2) are matched to each stage's specific geometric uncertainty.

## Results

On the DAVIS benchmark, F2R achieves an average PSNR of 36.14 dB / SSIM 0.941 across noise levels σ=10–50, surpassing the strongest self-supervised competitor TAP-T by 0.66 dB and the supervised FloRNN by 0.24 dB. On Set8, F2R reaches 34.30 dB / 0.915 SSIM, outperforming even the supervised FloRNN (33.55 dB) by 0.75 dB. On the real-world CRVD raw video dataset, F2R attains 45.69 dB average PSNR, exceeding the supervised FloRNN (45.15 dB) and the leading self-supervised TAP (45.15 dB) by 0.54 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F2R outperforms all self-supervised baselines across every dataset and noise level. On DAVIS, it leads TAP-T (35.48 dB) by 0.66 dB, RDRF (35.18 dB) by 0.96 dB, and UDVD (34.76 dB) by 1.38 dB in average PSNR. On Set8, F2R (34.30 dB) surpasses TAP-T (33.95 dB) by 0.35 dB and RDRF (32.92 dB) by 1.38 dB. Notably, F2R also exceeds the strongest supervised method FloRNN on Set8 (34.30 vs. 33.55 dB, +0.75 dB) and on CRVD (45.69 vs. 45.15 dB, +0.54 dB), narrowing or closing the supervised–unsupervised gap. On CRVD, F2R beats RViDeNet (43.97 dB) by 1.72 dB and UDVD (44.71 dB) by 0.98 dB. The traditional VBM4D baseline lags by more than 3 dB on DAVIS, confirming the advantage of learned spatiotemporal modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

F2R extends the self-supervised video denoising lineage from Video BSNs (UDVD, RDRF) and the TAP framework, which introduced pre-trained image denoisers as structural priors. The key conceptual departure is identifying a fundamental conflict in Video BSNs: the blind-spot constraint that ensures noise independence simultaneously severs direct spatial evidence needed for texture recovery. Rather than attempting to satisfy both objectives in a single network, F2R resolves this through an explicit two-stage decoupling—blind temporal estimation followed by non-blind spatial refinement—operating in a residual domain where the image denoiser handles static structure and the network focuses exclusively on high-frequency residuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean conceptual resolution to a long-standing tension in self-supervised video denoising, and the residual-domain formulation (yielding an 8.53 dB ablation gain) suggests that explicitly offloading structural modeling to a frozen prior is a broadly useful strategy for restoration tasks. However, the approach introduces dependencies on two pre-trained models (NAFNet for denoising, PWC-Net for flow), and the recorruption strategy assumes access to a calibrated noise model, limiting applicability to unknown-noise scenarios. Open questions include whether the decoupling principle generalizes to other spatiotemporal restoration tasks (super-resolution, inpainting) and whether the U-Net backbone could be replaced by more expressive architectures without disrupting the two-stage training protocol.
