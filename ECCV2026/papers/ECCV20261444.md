## Problem

Multimodal deep networks deployed in realistic environments must simultaneously adapt to three runtime variations—changing modality quality of information, fluctuating platform compute budgets, and varying input sample complexity—but existing approaches address only one or two of these in isolation. Adaptive networks lack strict budget guarantees, controller-based networks ignore per-sample complexity, and statically provisioned networks fail at all three, leaving computational resources underutilized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261444/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SWAN consumes an RGB camera image and a LiDAR point cloud alongside a user-specified maximum backbone-layer budget drawn from a discrete set (e.g., 4, 6, 8, 16, 20 layers). Beyond the standard 3D bounding boxes and class labels, the system internally produces a per-modality layer allocation vector from the controller, per-layer binary execution decisions from SkipGate, and a spatial binary mask over backbone tokens for the detection head. The budget is a hard upper bound: the network may use fewer layers than allocated, but never more.

## Method

SWAN builds on the CMT camera-LiDAR detection framework (Swin-Tiny + FlatFormer backbones) and introduces a three-tier adaptation hierarchy. A QoI-aware controller, trained with the differentiable NeuralSort sorting algorithm, allocates the user-specified layer budget across modalities by learning relative layer importance under varying corruption conditions; NeuralSort is used rather than straight-through estimation because it preserves the ordered structure of layer selection. Within the controller's allocation, a shared SkipGate module conditionally skips individual layers using a Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation, conditioned on contextual embeddings (current layer index, remaining budget, other modality's allocation, and QoI features) plus a hinge-based utilization penalty. Finally, a lightweight convolutional token-pruning module masks semantically irrelevant spatial tokens before the DETR head, supervised with a straight-through estimator and a token-utilization loss. Training proceeds in four staged phases—LayerDrop backbone pretraining, controller training, SkipGate training, and token-pruning training—each freezing prior modules and annealing temperature parameters from exploratory to near-discrete.

## Results

On the MultiCorrupt-nuScenes benchmark, SWAN-PSC with a 4-layer budget achieves 331.41 GFLOPs and 72.21 ms median latency, representing a 49% FLOPs reduction and 42% latency reduction compared to the fully-provisioned 20-layer Base Network (651.00 GFLOPs, 124.84 ms). Under the most severe corruption (LiDAR Beamsreduce) at a 4-layer budget, SWAN-C raises mAP from 3.88 (Naive) to 16.12 and NDS from 24.71 to 29.17. Across all corruption conditions, SWAN outperforms the controller-based ADMN baseline by up to 8.8 NDS and 13.8 mAP under tight budgets, while retaining within 2.7 NDS of the fully-provisioned network under Camera Motionblur with only 4 layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Naive equal-allocation baseline, SWAN-C at 4 layers gains 4.5 NDS and 12.2 mAP under LiDAR Beamsreduce (29.17/16.12 vs. 24.71/3.88) and 6.6 NDS under Camera Fog (64.47 vs. 57.83), demonstrating that QoI-aware allocation is critical under budget scarcity. Compared to the controller-based ADMN [27], SWAN-C outperforms by up to 8.8 NDS and 13.8 mAP (Camera Fog, 4 layers: 64.47/55.80 vs. 55.69/42.04), which the authors attribute to NeuralSort's superior modeling of relative layer importance versus ADMN's straight-through gradient estimation. Against unimodal baselines, the multimodal SWAN network is far more robust: PETR (camera-only) collapses to 22.17 NDS under Camera Fog while SWAN-PSC retains 63.95, and SST (LiDAR-only) drops to 22.06 NDS under LiDAR Beamsreduce versus SWAN-PSC's 27.92. BEVFusion, the strongest static multimodal baseline, achieves 68.38 NDS under Camera Fog but cannot adapt to budget constraints; SWAN-PSC at 4 layers reaches 63.95 NDS with 49% fewer FLOPs. On the broader MM-Fi gesture recognition task, SWAN-C improves accuracy over Naive by 7.7 points at 6 layers (30.9% vs. 23.2%) while SWAN-SC reduces latency by up to 15.1% at 20 layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWAN builds on the CMT multimodal camera-LiDAR detection architecture and incorporates LayerDrop-style stochastic depth training to make the backbone layer-adaptive. It departs from ADMN, the closest prior work, by replacing straight-through gradient estimation with a NeuralSort-based differentiable sorting mechanism for the QoI-aware controller, and by adding two additional efficiency modules (SkipGate and token pruning) that operate within the controller's budget. Unlike input-adaptive networks such as AdaViT that handle sample complexity in unimodal settings, SWAN unifies QoI-aware resource allocation, budget-constrained layer selection, and per-sample computation reduction into a single multimodal framework for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to jointly address modality QoI degradation, platform compute constraints, and input complexity in a multimodal AV perception stack, which is critical for real-world deployments where all three vary simultaneously. The 49% FLOPs reduction with minimal accuracy loss and the >50% latency reduction on Jetson Orin edge hardware make it practically relevant for resource-constrained AVs. Key limitations include the use of layer count (rather than FLOPs) as the budget unit, a domain gap between synthetic MultiCorrupt training and real-world corruption that requires finetuning, and PyTorch orchestration overhead that masks latency gains on high-end GPUs. Open questions include incorporating non-uniform layer costs into the controller, leveraging model compilation (e.g., TensorRT) to eliminate inter-device transfer overhead, and evaluating on datasets with systematic sensor failures rather than simulated corruptions.
