## Problem

Open-vocabulary change detection (OVCD) suffers from a fundamental granularity gap: foundation models produce coarse semantic abstractions while change detection demands pixel-level spatial fidelity. Existing OVCD pipelines (e.g., DynamicEarth) rely on instance extraction models like SAM or APE to supply spatial boundaries, but this introduces over-segmentation and under-segmentation that corrupts semantic discrimination, especially for region-based land cover types in remote sensing. The paper asks whether OVCD can be reformulated to avoid instance boundaries entirely while retaining pixel-level accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of co-registered bi-temporal RGB remote sensing images and a user-defined set of text category labels (open vocabulary, including a background class) as input. It produces two outputs: a binary change mask (via Otsu thresholding on a per-pixel change probability map) and a semantic change mask assigning a foreground category label to each changed pixel in each temporal phase. The framework is training-free at inference time for the OVCD task itself; only the RIFI-Up upsampler requires a one-epoch pre-training on the MillionAID dataset, and the OVSS identifier (APE or SegEarth-OV) is used as a plug-and-play black box.

## Method

Free-CD decomposes OVCD into two parallel sub-tasks—potential change area proposal (estimating P(F) via cosine similarity of upsampled DINO features) and bi-temporal open-vocabulary segmentation (per-pixel category probabilities from an arbitrary OVSS model)—then integrates them through a Bayesian Probability Correction module that rectifies the definitional discrepancy between single-temporal background probability and multi-temporal change-belongs-to-background probability before fusing masks via thresholding and argmax. The key architectural contribution is RIFI-Up, a feature upsampler built on stacked Joint Bilateral Upsamplers that enforces a resolution-invariant feature inversion constraint: a Fourier Neural Operator is trained to reconstruct the downsampled image from low-resolution features, and this FNO serves as a frozen inverse mapping during upsampler training, ensuring the upsampler recovers high-fidelity spatial detail without semantic drift or resolution-dependent artifacts. The entire OVCD pipeline requires no task-specific fine-tuning, leveraging self-supervised DINO backbones and pretrained OVSS models in a transfer-learning fashion.

## Results

On the four semantic change detection benchmarks, Free-CD with DINOv3-SegEarth-OV achieves Fscd scores of 23.19 (SECOND), 30.00 (HiUCD), 8.37 (HRSCD), and 16.15 (JL1-SCD), with corresponding mIoU values of 59.84, 54.99, 57.73, and 52.37. These represent consistent gains over the best DynamicEarth configuration (SAM-DINOv3-SegEarth-OV), which scores 20.50/58.77, 23.24/53.21, 3.18/48.38, and 8.07/40.31 respectively. On building change detection, FreeCD(APE) reaches IoU 50.5 / F1 67.1 on LEVIR-CD and IoU 58.9 / F1 74.7 on WHU-CD, matching or exceeding the strongest DynamicEarth(I-M-C) baseline (50.0/66.7 and 61.1/75.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary OVCD baseline is DynamicEarth [29], which Free-CD outperforms across all four SCD datasets: on HRSCD the Fscd gap is largest (8.37 vs. 3.18, a 2.6× improvement), and on JL1-SCD the mIoU gap reaches 12.06 points (52.37 vs. 40.31). On building change detection, FreeCD(APE) narrowly beats DynamicEarth(I-M-C) on LEVIR-CD (IoU 50.5 vs. 50.0) but trails on WHU-CD (IoU 58.9 vs. 61.1), suggesting the framework's advantage is most pronounced for region-based land-cover changes rather than discrete building instances. Against the older unsupervised and supervised baselines (CVA [4], PCA-KM [7], CNN-CD [19], DSFA [18], DCVA [45], GMCD [49]), Free-CD achieves substantially higher scores—e.g., F1 of 67.1 vs. 14.1 (DCVA) on LEVIR-CD—confirming that the open-vocabulary, training-free paradigm surpasses methods requiring task-specific training. Compared to Segment Any Change [60] (AnyChange-H, F1 23.0 on LEVIR-CD) and SCM [48] (F1 31.7), Free-CD's F1 of 67.1 represents a 2.1–2.9× improvement, underscoring the benefit of decoupling change probability from instance-level segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Free-CD departs from the dominant OVCD paradigm exemplified by DynamicEarth, which couples change detection with instance extraction models (SAM, APE) to produce binary masks before semantic classification. Instead, it reformulates OVCD as prediction of a change probability distribution, drawing on the one-stage OVSS literature (APE, SegEarth-OV) to bypass instance-level proposals entirely. The RIFI-Up module extends FeatUp's Joint Bilateral Upsampler by introducing a resolution-invariant feature inversion constraint implemented via Fourier Neural Operators, a novel application of FNO to feature upsampling in change detection. The Bayesian Probability Correction mechanism is a new probabilistic formulation that explicitly separates change priors from semantic categorization, addressing a definitional gap between single-temporal segmentation and multi-temporal change detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of Free-CD lowers the barrier to deploying OVCD in practice: any feature backbone paired with any OVSS model can be converted into a full OVCD pipeline without task-specific fine-tuning. However, the paper's own ablations reveal that the framework's gains are primarily compensatory—when paired with a strong instance-aware identifier like APE, Free-CD offers little over DynamicEarth, suggesting the true bottleneck lies in OVSS model quality rather than the change detection architecture. Open questions include whether the probabilistic formulation scales to more than two temporal phases, whether the FNO-based upsampler generalizes to non-ViT backbones, and whether the Otsu thresholding step can be replaced by learned or adaptive thresholds for more challenging scenes.
