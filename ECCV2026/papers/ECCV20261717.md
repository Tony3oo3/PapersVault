## Problem

Inferring physical properties such as mass, density, and Young's modulus from a single image remains largely unsolved because existing approaches either require multi-view 3D reconstruction (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) or treat the task as 2D material classification without 3D geometric awareness. Prior single-image methods like Image2Mass regress physical quantities purely from RGB appearance, lacking explicit material knowledge and 3D-consistent spatial structure, which limits generalization to unseen materials and object compositions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261717/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SiPhy takes a single RGB image plus a monocular depth estimate (from Depth Anything) and camera intrinsics as input; the depth and intrinsics are used solely to compute an adaptive 2D pixel spacing for pseudo-voxelization rather than as a direct geometric input. Outputs include pixel-level maps for material segmentation, mass density, and Young's modulus (with non-sampled pixels filled via k-NN interpolation), an intermediate N×K material probability matrix, and a scalar total mass estimate aggregated over all pseudo-voxels. The system also produces per-part material labels from a fine-tuned VLM that feed into the contrastive alignment stage.

## Method

SiPhy unifies three stages: (1) depth-adaptive 2D sampling that places non-overlapping patches at spacing s = d·√(f_x·f_y/z) to approximate a pseudo-voxel grid from one view; (2) material probability estimation where a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encodes each patch, a fine-tuned Vicuna-7B VLM (with CLIP ViT-L/14) proposes K=5 material candidates per SAM-derived part, and a part-restricted self-attention module with supervised contrastive loss enforces intra-part material consistency while a CLIP-similarity regularizer preserves per-point discrimination; (3) physical property computation as the expectation over material likelihoods at each point, summed across voxels for object-level mass, followed by a heaviness-aware thickness refinement (HAT) that classifies objects as heavy or light and biases thickness toward physically plausible ranges. The VLM is trained in two stages (projector-only, then LoRA fine-tuning of Vicuna) on ~10k PhysX/MVImgNet samples, and the contrastive module is optimized with Adam at lr=1e-4 and temperature τ=0.1.

## Results

On ABO-500 mass prediction, SiPhy achieves ADE 7.78, ALDE 0.74, APE 1.00, and MnRE 0.58 from a single RGB view, outperforming all multi-view and single-view baselines. On PhysXNet-100, SiPhy reduces density MAE to 1315 kg/m³ (vs. 2044 for NeRF2Physics) and Young's modulus MAE to 52 GPa (vs. 68 for both NeRF2Physics and PUGS). On real hand-object interaction data (HO3D, ARCTIC), SiPhy achieves MnRE 0.67 and 0.57 respectively, surpassing LLaVA (0.58 and 0.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiPhy outperforms NeRF2Physics on all four mass metrics (ADE 7.78 vs. 8.74, MnRE 0.58 vs. 0.55) despite using a single view instead of multi-view NeRF reconstruction. Against PUGS, the improvement is dramatic: MnRE rises from 0.30 to 0.58 (+93%) and ADE drops from 30.30 to 7.78, showing that 3D Gaussian Splatting's multi-view pipeline is not necessary for accurate mass estimation. SiPhy also beats the single-view VLM baseline LLaVA (MnRE 0.58 vs. 0.31) and the regression-based Image2Mass (MnRE 0.58 vs. 0.31, ADE 7.78 vs. 12.50), though Image2Mass has a marginally lower APE (0.98 vs. 1.00). For material segmentation, SiPhy trails GaussianProperty on ABO-500 mIoU (0.25 vs. 0.29) but matches or exceeds it on MVImgNet-100 (M-mIoU 0.25 vs. 0.23) and PhysXNet-100 (M-mIoU 0.31 vs. 0.21), and it surpasses NeRF2Physics and PUGS on all three datasets. On real-world HO3D and ARCTIC, SiPhy outperforms LLaVA in both mass (MnRE 0.67 vs. 0.58; 0.57 vs. 0.53) and density/Young's modulus estimation, confirming generalization beyond synthetic benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiPhy departs from the dominant multi-view reconstruction paradigm (NeRF2Physics, PUGS, GaussianProperty) that requires many input views and heavy optimization to estimate physical properties. It repurposes the virtual-cube voxelization concept from NeRF2Physics but achieves it from a single RGB image via depth-adaptive 2D sampling, and introduces a VLM-driven material candidate proposal (fine-tuned Vicuna-7B) that feeds CLIP-based contrastive alignment. The part-based contrastive module and heaviness-aware thickness refinement are novel architectural contributions not present in prior single-image mass estimators like Image2Mass, which treated the problem as pure 2D regression without geometric or material-semantic grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling both pixel-level (material, density, Young's modulus) and object-level (mass) physical property estimation from a single image, SiPhy removes the multi-view capture bottleneck that limits deployment in embodied AI, simulation, and real-world interaction scenarios. The demonstrated utility as a data annotation engine for hand-object interaction datasets (HO3D, ARCTIC) and as a material prior for image-to-audio generation highlights practical downstream value. Key open questions include whether the discrete heavy/light HAT module can be extended to continuous thickness estimation, how performance degrades under severe depth estimation errors, and whether the approach generalizes to deformable or composite objects beyond rigid everyday items.
