## Problem

Automated 3D reconstruction requires a reliable uncertainty quantification model to select informative scanning viewpoints, but existing approaches either rely on costly analytical metrics (Fisher information, mutual information) that poorly capture photometric and geometric inconsistencies, or use data-driven methods that estimate only 2D image-level errors without reasoning about 3D depth reliability. Auto3R addresses this gap by jointly modeling 2D appearance and 3D geometric uncertainty in a single data-driven framework.

## Input / Output

The UQ network takes a rendered RGB image and its corresponding depth map from the current 3DGS reconstruction at a candidate viewpoint, producing two pixel-wise uncertainty maps that are aggregated into a single scalar uncertainty score per viewpoint. For the robotic scanning extension, a video-based network (adapted TMFI-Net encoder) consumes a sequence of rendered frames along a candidate trajectory and outputs a path-level uncertainty score. The entire per-viewpoint prediction runs in approximately 3 ms, enabling real-time view selection during iterative reconstruction.

## Method

Auto3R trains two lightweight ResNet-50 image-to-image networks in a self-supervised manner: one predicts pixel-wise color uncertainty from rendered RGB, the other predicts depth uncertainty from rendered depth maps, using pixel-wise SSIM between rendered and ground-truth novel views as the training signal (no explicit uncertainty labels needed). The key architectural insight is a depth-aware blending scheme that multiplies image uncertainty by depth values (emphasizing regions where more Gaussians overlap due to perspective projection) and then reweights each pixel by (1 − depth uncertainty) to suppress unreliable depth estimates in early reconstruction stages. The final scalar uncertainty score combines the blended term, the reweighted term, and global regularization over both uncertainty maps with iteration-dependent coefficients. For robotic deployment, the encoder of TMFI-Net is adapted with an upsampling module to predict per-frame uncertainty from image sequences along candidate paths, enabling continuous trajectory-level planning rather than discrete single-view selection.

## Results

On the Objaverse active object reconstruction benchmark (20 views, 256 candidate viewpoints), Auto3R achieves 27.00 avg / 21.88 worst-5% PSNR, 0.8882 / 0.7819 SSIM, and 0.0711 / 0.1813 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline AVS (23.94 / 18.18 PSNR) by over 3 dB in average PSNR. On the larger-scale Mip-NeRF360 scene reconstruction task, Auto3R reaches 19.13 avg PSNR and 0.2520 LPIPS, marginally ahead of AVS (18.95 PSNR, 0.2679 LPIPS) and FisherRF (18.74 PSNR). The UQ network runs at approximately 3 ms per image, enabling real-time viewpoint selection during iterative reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Auto3R decisively outperforms all seven baselines on the primary Objaverse object reconstruction task, gaining +3.06 dB PSNR over the closest competitor AVS and +3.92 dB over FisherRF, with the gap widening to +3.70 dB in worst-5% PSNR. On Mip-NeRF360 scene reconstruction, Auto3R leads FisherRF and GAUSS-MI on PSNR and LPIPS but is essentially tied with AVS on SSIM (0.6055 vs. 0.6062), while still winning on PSNR (19.13 vs. 18.95) and LPIPS (0.2520 vs. 0.2679). The generic image-quality baselines (TOPIQ, TRES, MANIQA, MUSIQ) adapted to the active-reconstruction loop perform substantially worse than the purpose-built 3D-uncertainty methods, with PSNR scores 4–13 dB below Auto3R, confirming that 2D no-reference quality scores are poor proxies for 3D reconstruction uncertainty. On specular (non-Lambertian) objects, Auto3R combined with the GIR inverse-rendering backend reaches 30.29 avg PSNR, surpassing AVS+GIR (29.53) and Random+GIR (26.85). In the real-world robotic scanning experiment, Auto3R's path-extension variant achieves 28.56 PSNR / 0.8953 SSIM, beating AVS (26.98 / 0.8894) and FisherRF (26.04 / 0.8835) by 1.6 and 2.5 dB respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

Auto3R sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting-based reconstruction and active next-best-view planning. It departs from analytical UQ methods (FisherRF, Gauss-MI) that rely on information-theoretic proxies and from data-driven 2D-only approaches (PUN, AVS) by introducing a joint 2D-3D uncertainty formulation that couples rendered color and depth through depth-aware blending and depth-uncertainty reweighting. The path-level scanning extension repurposes TMFI-Net's video encoder for trajectory-level uncertainty, a natural but previously unexplored adaptation for robotic scanning. The method is trained self-supervised on Objaverse and map-free-reloc using SSIM between rendered and ground-truth views as the uncertainty signal, avoiding handcrafted priors entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Auto3R addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D digitization: the labor-intensive manual planning of scanning viewpoints. By achieving ~3–4 dB PSNR gains over the strongest baselines (AVS, FisherRF) at 3 ms per-image inference, it makes fully automated, real-time scanning feasible on robotic platforms, as demonstrated with a physical robot arm. The integration with GIR for specular materials broadens applicability beyond Lambertian surfaces. Open questions include handling self-occlusion shadows from the robot body (acknowledged as a limitation), scalability to very large or dynamic scenes, and whether the depth-aware blending strategy generalizes to representations beyond 3DGS.
