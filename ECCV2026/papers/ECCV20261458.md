## Problem

Existing joint audio-video generation models suffer from two fundamental misalignments in human-centric scenes: (1) intra-audio interference, where structured speech components dominate and mask transient environmental sounds, and (2) cross-modal desynchronization, where motion and audio are linked only through implicit cross-attention without explicit temporal alignment objectives. These issues produce flattened auditory scenes and desynchronized gestures, lip motions, and acoustic onsets.

## Input / Output

Unison takes a caption text (κ), a transcription text (τ), and an optional reference image as input, and produces a 25 FPS video alongside a dual-stream audio output containing independently generated speech and sound-effect components. The video branch is built on Wan2.2-5B (29 transformer layers) and the audio branch on MMAudio augmented with Zipformer (23 layers), with frame-level bidirectional cross-attention coupling the two branches. The symmetric design also supports bidirectional generation: audio-conditioned video synthesis (A2V) and video-conditioned audio synthesis (V2A).

## Method

Unison introduces two key mechanisms. First, a Semantic-Guided Harmonization Strategy decouples audio generation into separate speech and sound-effect streams, where a Bidirectional Audio Cross-Attention (Bi-ACA) module enables mutual feature refinement between streams, and a Semantic-Conditioned Gating (SCG) mechanism uses average-pooled caption and transcription vectors to predict per-stream gating coefficients that adaptively suppress or amplify cross-stream information flow depending on scene semantics. Second, a Bidirectional Cross-Modal Forcing strategy independently samples denoising timesteps for video and audio, so the modality at a lower noise level provides explicit conditioning to guide the noisier counterpart; direction-aware loss weighting (upweighting the noisier branch by λ=0.5) and a three-stage progressive curriculum (synchronous warmup → incremental decoupling with |t_v−t_a|≤0.25 → full independence) ensure stable optimization. Training uses flow matching with a two-stage procedure: the audio branch is first trained independently, then the coupled system is fine-tuned with the TI2AV objective while keeping the video backbone frozen.

## Results

On the TI2AV benchmark, Unison achieves the best audio perceptual quality (PQ 6.34), content usefulness (CU 5.61), lowest word error rate (WER 0.22), highest audio-video semantic similarity (AV 0.91), and best temporal synchronization (DS 0.08) among all compared methods. On T2AV, Unison similarly leads in PQ (6.17), CU (5.78), WER (0.09), AV (0.86), and DS (0.06). Notably, Unison uses a 5B video backbone (Wan2.2-5B), roughly 4× smaller than LTX-2's 19B model, yet achieves superior cross-modal synchronization metrics despite LTX-2's marginal advantage in visual aesthetics (VA 4.15 vs. 4.02 on TI2AV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unison outperforms all five baselines on audio quality metrics (PQ, CU, WER) and cross-modal semantic alignment (AV) in both TI2AV and T2AV settings. Against LTX-2 (the strongest competitor with a 19B video backbone), Unison achieves better PQ (6.34 vs. 6.30), lower WER (0.22 vs. 0.25), higher AV (0.91 vs. 0.89), and tighter temporal sync (DS 0.08 vs. 0.10) on TI2AV, while LTX-2 retains a small edge in visual aesthetics (VA 4.15 vs. 4.02) and lip-sync (LSE-C 3.45 vs. 3.30). On T2AV, Unison beats LTX-2 in PQ (6.17 vs. 6.12), CU (5.78 vs. 5.72), WER (0.09 vs. 0.11), AV (0.86 vs. 0.81), and DS (0.06 vs. 0.07), though LTX-2 leads in VA (4.63 vs. 4.51) and LSE-C (3.62 vs. 3.55). Compared to UniAVGen and MOVA, Unison shows substantially better temporal alignment (DS 0.08 vs. 0.15 and 0.13) and audio-video semantic consistency (AV 0.91 vs. 0.81 and 0.88). The user study (Table 4) confirms these quantitative findings: Unison ranks best in speech-sound harmony (1.86) and motion-audio alignment (1.55), and achieves the lowest overall Gemini coherence score (1.68), while LTX-2 edges ahead only on lip-speech synchrony (1.74 vs. 1.86).

## Relation to Prior Work

Unison builds on two distinct lineages: the MMAudio/Zipformer audio generation stack (extended with a dual-stream speech/SFX architecture) and the Diffusion Forcing paradigm (generalized from unimodal per-element noise scheduling to a cross-modal setting where video and audio evolve under independent timesteps). It departs from prior joint audio-video models (Universe-1, Ovi, UniAVGen, LTX-2, MOVA) that treat audio as a monolithic stream and rely solely on architectural cross-attention for synchronization, by explicitly decoupling speech from sound effects and introducing a training-time forcing mechanism that makes the cleaner modality supervise the noisier one. The semantic-conditioned gating mechanism is novel in using caption and transcription semantics to dynamically rebalance intra-audio information flow, addressing a failure mode (speech dominance) that prior cross-attention-based methods like Harmony do not target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete, well-motivated failure modes in human-centric audio-video generation—speech masking environmental sounds and motion-audio temporal drift—that are visible in current commercial and open-source systems. The bidirectional cross-modal forcing strategy is a clean generalization of Diffusion Forcing to multimodal settings and could be adopted by other joint generation frameworks beyond this specific architecture. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-form video (the 25 FPS, short-clip evaluation limits generalizability claims), whether the 5B-vs-19B backbone comparison reflects a genuine efficiency advantage or simply a different operating point, and whether the semantic-guided harmonization generalizes to non-human-centric scenes where the speech/SFX dichotomy is less natural.
