## Problem

Existing visual odometry and SLAM systems accumulate unbounded drift and produce only relative, up-to-scale trajectories, while single-frame geo-localization methods discard temporal continuity and are too slow for real-time video. No prior system provides continuous, drift-free, metrically scaled 6-DoF UAV poses without GPS, task-specific training, or post-hoc alignment to a global reference frame.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261298/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a UAV RGB video with known pinhole intrinsics, a georeferenced Digital Orthophoto (DOP) raster providing top-down RGB, and a co-registered Digital Surface Model (DSM) raster encoding surface elevation, both at 0.2 m resolution in a metric UTM coordinate frame. It outputs a full 6-DoF SE(3) pose (rotation and translation) for every frame in absolute metric world coordinates, with no scale ambiguity or alignment step required. No GPS, IMU, or other external sensor input is needed at any stage.

## Method

OrthoTrack alternates between two complementary modes: at sparse keyframes (~1% of frames), a dense cross-view matcher (RoMa v2) matches the UAV frame against a visibility-aware DOP crop, correspondences are lifted to metric 3D via bilinear DSM interpolation, and PnP-RANSAC (SQPnP solver) recovers a globally anchored pose; between keyframes, GPU-accelerated Lucas-Kanade optical flow propagates the existing 2D-3D correspondences forward for lightweight per-frame PnP updates. A visibility-aware crop selection projects a dense DSM grid into the image with occlusion culling to center the DOP crop on the visible ground region, handling oblique views via inverse-depth-weighted averaging. An adaptive dual-threshold quality monitor (absolute warmup bound plus a decaying relative bound scaled to each keyframe's baseline reprojection error) triggers re-localization when point count, spatial spread, or reprojection error degrades, preventing both re-triggering loops and slow drift accumulation. The entire pipeline is training-free and agnostic to the choice of dense matcher and optical flow backend.

## Results

On MovingDrone, OrthoTrack achieves 0.67 m ATE, 0.33 m median translation error, and 0.06° median rotation error at 23.8 FPS, without any alignment or sensor prior. On the real-world UAVScenes benchmark, it reaches 1.51 m ATE and 1.38 m TE, outperforming all VO/SLAM and foundation-model baselines that require oracle Sim(3) alignment. The system invokes the expensive dense matcher on only ~1% of frames (keyframes) and propagates correspondences via optical flow for the remainder, achieving real-time throughput on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrthoTrack outperforms all VO/SLAM baselines on UAVScenes by a wide margin: the best DROID-SLAM variant with oracle GNSS+magnetometer and piecewise geodata anchoring reaches 1.04 m ATE, while OrthoTrack achieves 1.51 m without any sensor prior or alignment; all other VO/SLAM variants (ORB-SLAM3, DPVO, Five-Point VO) exceed 5 m ATE even with oracle alignment. On MovingDrone, DROID-SLAM with piecewise anchoring (0.63 m) marginally beats OrthoTrack (0.67 m), but requires 50-frame periodic re-anchoring to geodata and runs 5× slower (4.7 vs. 23.8 FPS). Among foundation models, DA3-Nested is the strongest (2.74 m ATE on MovingDrone, 58.27 m on UAVScenes) yet still 4× worse than OrthoTrack on MovingDrone and 39× worse on UAVScenes, due to out-of-distribution aerial views and local-frame reconstruction. OrthoLoC, the closest single-frame geodata-based method, achieves competitive per-frame TE (0.50 m on MovingDrone) but its ATE degrades to 19.78 m (MovingDrone) and 38.24 m (UAVScenes) due to sporadic per-frame failures without temporal filtering; OrthoTrack reduces ATE by 30× and 25× respectively. LoD-Loc (retrained) reaches only 10.04 m ATE on MovingDrone, confirming that wireframe alignment is less discriminative than dense texture matching on complex urban scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OrthoTrack extends the single-frame geodata localization principle of OrthoLoC (matching a UAV frame against a DOP crop and solving PnP from DSM-lifted correspondences) into a continuous tracking system by interleaving sparse keyframe re-localization with dense optical-flow propagation. Conceptually, it positions geodata-based anchoring and optical-flow tracking as complementary: the former provides drift-free absolute registration, the latter provides frame-rate temporal continuity. This departs from VO/SLAM methods that produce only relative, up-to-scale trajectories requiring post-hoc alignment, and from 3D foundation models (DUSt3R, VGGT, Pi3) that reconstruct in a local frame and cannot be directly georeferenced without a similarity transform.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By relying solely on publicly available DOP/DSM rasters, OrthoTrack enables deployment to any region with government-published geodata without site-specific training, GPS, or IMU, which is critical for signal-denied urban environments. The MovingDrone dataset (343k frames, 21 Berlin regions, up to 14 DOP years) is the first benchmark combining dense video with per-frame metric 6-DoF ground truth and co-registered multi-temporal geodata, filling a gap that limits evaluation of continuous aerial localization. Open questions include generalization to regions with lower-resolution or less frequently updated geodata, handling of dynamic scene elements (vehicles, pedestrians) that violate the static-map assumption, and whether the approach extends to indoor or GPS-denied subterranean settings where no surface model exists.
