## Problem

Reconstructing 3D human-object interactions from a single image is difficult due to depth ambiguities, occlusions, and the lack of canonical object models. Existing methods rely on reprojection and contact constraints to fit parametric models to 2D cues, which is highly ambiguous and often requires privileged information such as ground-truth contact annotations or object templates. This paper asks whether Large Reconstruction Models, which produce holistic 3D scene reconstructions, can serve as a geometric scaffold that resolves these ambiguities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (passed as RGBA to the LRM, where the alpha channel encodes the combined human+object segmentation) and optionally an object template mesh. It outputs a parametric SMPL-H human mesh (global orientation, body pose, hand pose, shape, and root translation), a 3D object mesh (either the raw segmented LRM point cloud or a template aligned via semantic correspondences), and their composed interaction. The LRM backbone is swappable (Hunyuan3D-2.0, SAM3D, InstantMesh were tested), and the method operates without any contact annotations.

## Method

MILO reframes HOI reconstruction as interpreting an LRM-generated joint human-object mesh rather than fitting parametric models directly to 2D image cues. The pipeline: (1) an off-the-shelf LRM (Hunyuan3D-2.0) produces a combined human-object mesh; (2) the mesh is rendered from 60 virtual views, and ViTPose + HaMeR predict 2D body/hand keypoints per view, which are triangulated into 3D keypoints; (3) SMPL-H is fitted in two stages — root fitting (global orientation + translation only, with a scale parameter) then pose fitting (full body/hand pose + shape) — using a Geman-McClure robust keypoint loss, VPoser/MANO latent priors, an HMR2.0 anchoring loss to prevent hallucinated poses under occlusion, and a one-way robust Chamfer loss restricted to visible vertices; (4) multi-view segmentation (Grounding DINO + SAM 2) with viewpoint-quality and boundary-closeness weighting extracts the object point cloud; (5) if a template is available, sparse semantic correspondences (via a geometry-aware correspondence model) are established and refined through iterative weighted Sim(3) Procrustes with nearest-neighbor constraints. The key distinction from prior work is that the LRM scaffold encodes relative human-object positioning and proximity, eliminating the need for explicit contact supervision.

## Results

On InterCap, MILO (with template) achieves the best combined human-object PA-CD of 7.45 cm, outperforming the next-best method Open3DHOI (10.17 cm) by 27%, while also achieving the best object PA-CD of 18.97 cm (vs. 21.85 cm for PICO). Without a template, MILO still leads on combined PA-CD (9.36 cm) and human PA-CD (6.85 cm). On HODome and IMHD, MILO reduces combined PA-CD by 34% and 47% respectively over PICO, the strongest single baseline on those datasets. MILO also infers vertex-level contact with F1=30.00, nearly 3× the F1 of the DECO image-regressed baseline (9.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MILO outperforms all six InterCap baselines on combined PA-CD, with the largest margins over HOI-TG (7.45 vs. 14.63 cm) and CONTHO (7.45 vs. 13.14 cm). Against Open3DHOI, the only method that also avoids contact and templates, MILO improves object PA-CD from 31.18 to 18.97 cm (−40%) and combined PA-CD from 10.17 to 7.45 cm (−27%), while matching its human PA-CD (6.85 vs. 6.88 cm). On HODome and IMHD, MILO beats PICO by 34% (6.68 vs. 10.07 cm) and 47% (6.98 vs. 13.24 cm) in combined PA-CD, respectively. In the object-only scaffold comparison (Table 6), MILO's joint LRM scaffold reduces combined PA-CD from 37.21 cm (EasyHOI without contact) to 9.36 cm, and from 15.89 cm (SAM3D independent) to 9.36 cm. For contact estimation, MILO's geometry-inferred contact achieves F1=30.00 and geometric error 39.57 cm, substantially outperforming DECO's image-regressed contact (F1=9.37, geo. 126.70 cm). Notably, MILO achieves these gains without requiring ground-truth contact annotations, a dependency shared by CONTHO, PHOSA, and PICO.

## Relation to Prior Work

MILO departs from the dominant paradigm in monocular 3D HOI reconstruction, where methods like CONTHO, PICO, and PHOSA fit parametric human and object models to 2D images using reprojection and explicit contact constraints. Instead, it repurposes Large Reconstruction Models (Hunyuan3D-2.0) as a joint human-object geometric scaffold, reframing the problem as interpreting an already-reconstructed 3D mesh rather than solving an ambiguous image-fitting problem. This contrasts with EasyHOI, which uses an LRM only for object reconstruction and then fits to the image, and with independent-reconstruction baselines (SAM3D + MoGe alignment) that recover human and object separately. The downstream fitting pipeline builds on standard components—HMR2.0 for initialization, HaMeR and ViTPose for multi-view keypoint estimation, and SMPL-H/MANO parametric models—applied in a novel two-stage optimization against the LRM scaffold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that contact annotations, which have been a near-universal requirement in HOI reconstruction, can be entirely eliminated without sacrificing accuracy, opening the door to scalable, label-efficient pipelines for in-the-wild scenes. The oracle experiment (PA-CD_h+o dropping from 9.36 to 3.12 with GT geometry) makes clear that performance is currently bounded by upstream LRM quality, so the field's progress on LRMs will directly translate into HOI gains. Open questions include extending to video and multi-person/multi-object settings, improving point-cloud segmentation (identified as a current bottleneck), and handling smaller objects where LRM geometry degrades. The finding that a joint scaffold substantially outperforms independent reconstruction followed by alignment (Table 6) suggests that co-reconstruction is a more principled strategy than post-hoc composition for interaction-aware 3D recovery.
