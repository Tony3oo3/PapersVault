## Problem

Sim2Real translation for autonomous driving faces two coupled challenges: (1) geometry conditions (G-buffers) are abundant in simulation but have no real-world counterparts, making it impossible to directly condition a real-domain generator on them without paired supervision; and (2) driving scenes require multiple dense geometric constraints simultaneously, yet naively stacking control branches incurs prohibitive parameter and memory overhead in large diffusion transformers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a synthetic CARLA RGB image, its deferred-rendering G-buffers (camera-space normals, depth, semantic segmentation, base color, and material maps), and a text prompt (auto-generated via LENS) as input, and produces a photorealistic driving image that strictly preserves the synthetic scene's geometric layout. No pixel-aligned synthetic-real pairs are required; the real-domain prior is learned from unpaired Cityscapes images. At inference, three forward passes per timestep (unconditional real, unconditional synthetic, conditional synthetic) compose the final velocity prediction with a guidance strength λ=6 over 25 sampling steps.

## Method

CDCT is a three-stage diffusion framework built on Stable Diffusion 3's MM-DiT backbone with rectified flow. Stage 1 fine-tunes the backbone on unpaired real and synthetic data with a learnable domain embedding; Stage 2 freezes the backbone and trains a control module on synthetic G-buffers to capture geometry-conditioned score modulation. The key inference mechanism is cross-domain score composition: under the assumption that domain and geometry are conditionally independent given the image, the geometry score ∇ₓ log p(g|x) is estimated as the difference between conditional and unconditional synthetic-domain predictions, then added to the real-domain appearance prior (Eq. 7), yielding geometry-consistent real-looking output without any paired supervision. For multi-condition efficiency, the Lightweight Multi-Condition Adapter (LMCA) injects each G-buffer modality as LoRA-augmented key-value pairs directly into the attention computation, with a zero-initialized linear gate scaling only the Value component; this adds just 2.8% parameters per condition (57M) versus 55% (1.1B) for a standard ControlNet, and scales linearly rather than quadratically with the number of conditions.

## Results

On the CARLA-to-Cityscapes benchmark, CDCT achieves the best generative quality (NIQE 3.21, MANIQA 0.44) and cross-domain similarity (FID 26.45, KID 22.17) while maintaining strong structural controllability (SSIM 0.85, LPIPS 0.17). For downstream perception, models trained on CDCT-translated data achieve 48.3 mIoU in semantic segmentation (+33.7 over raw CARLA), 40.5 mAP₅₀ in object detection (+20.3), and AbsRel 0.16 in depth estimation (reduced from 0.56). Notably, joint training with CDCT-generated data plus Cityscapes surpasses real-data-only training, reaching 52.5 mAP₅₀ and 67.0 mIoU versus 48.8 and 65.2 for Cityscapes alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDCT outperforms all five baselines on generative quality and cross-domain similarity metrics. Against the closest competitor VSAIT [40], CDCT achieves lower FID (26.45 vs. 29.45) and KID (22.17 vs. 27.67) despite VSAIT's slightly higher SSIM (0.89 vs. 0.85), indicating CDCT produces more photorealistic outputs that better match the Cityscapes distribution. EPE [32] and HIE [9] trail by 8–11 FID points and introduce visible artifacts or unstable textures. UNSB [19] and CACTIF [5] perform worst overall, with FID values of 41.62 and 50.91 respectively, producing blurry results with limited high-frequency detail. On downstream tasks, CDCT's advantage is even more pronounced: it leads all baselines by 11.4 mIoU in segmentation (48.3 vs. 36.9 for EPE), 6.6 mAP₅₀ in detection (40.5 vs. 35.7 for VSAIT), and 0.03 AbsRel in depth estimation (0.16 vs. 0.19 for VSAIT), confirming that superior appearance fidelity translates directly into better perception performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion 3 / MM-DiT backbone and rectified flow training, extending the classifier-free guidance principle (Ho & Salimans) into a cross-domain score composition that decomposes geometry guidance as a domain-agnostic term transferable from synthetic to real sampling. It departs from prior Sim2Real translation methods (EPE, VSAIT, HIE, UNSB) that rely on GANs or normalizing flows with limited photorealistic capacity, and from diffusion-based Sim2Real work (CACTIF, CycleDiff) that lacks explicit geometric conditioning. The LMCA module replaces ControlNet's full encoder duplication with LoRA-based key-value injection into attention, reducing per-condition parameter overhead from ~55% to 2.8%.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that augmenting Cityscapes with CDCT-translated CARLA data surpasses real-data-only training (52.5 vs 48.8 mAP50; 67.0 vs 65.2 mIoU) validates Sim2Real translation as a scalable data augmentation strategy for autonomous driving perception, not merely a zero-shot transfer tool. The parameter-efficient multi-condition design makes dense G-buffer guidance feasible at high resolution on large DiT backbones, a practical bottleneck for prior ControlNet-style approaches. However, the content gap (scene layout, object distribution mismatches between CARLA and Cityscapes) remains unaddressed, and the framework is validated on a single source-target pair with a single backbone architecture, leaving open questions about generalizability to other simulators, real-world datasets, and DiT variants.
