## Problem

Existing offset-based tracking methods (deformable convolution, deformable attention) generate spatial sampling offsets offline within a single inference step, ignoring the spatiotemporal continuity of the same target across frames. This prevents the tracker from explicitly modeling inter-frame displacement and actively adapting its receptive field to the target's ongoing motion, leading to localization errors under rapid movement or appearance drift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261604/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes N template images (112×112 or 192×192), a current search-area crop (224×224 or 384×384), and the previous frame's predicted bounding box normalized to [0,1]⁴ relative to the search area. The normalized box is treated as a geometric motion prompt (inspired by SAM) rather than a global-coordinate sequence, avoiding misalignment during local search. The output is the current-frame target bounding box plus a dense 2D offset vector field (H/4 × W/4 × 2) that encodes per-pixel displacement for deformable feature sampling.

## Method

ODONet introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, DVMFI embeds the previous-frame box as a sparse motion prompt and passes it through a Two-Way Transformer with the Stage-0 search features, performing self-attention on the prompt, cross-attention from prompt to features, and reverse cross-attention, thereby injecting temporal motion priors into low-level spatial representations during encoding. Second, ODOP generates two complementary offset fields per frame: an online offset computed via affine transformation of the bounding-box displacement (translation + scale) predicted by a lightweight MLP from the motion encoding, and an offline offset predicted from current visual features through a DWConv-GELU-Conv-tanh pipeline. These are fused by a weighted Online Offset Injection (OOI) strategy (optimal w_on = 0.5) to produce the final online dynamic offset, which shifts each deformable-attention sampling point from its fixed grid location, dynamically adjusting the receptive field. Training is end-to-end with a joint loss combining focal classification, L1/GIoU box regression, L1 motion-vector supervision, and dense offset supervision (L1 + cosine similarity) computed only within the ground-truth target region.

## Results

ODONet-B384 achieves the best AUC of 75.1% on LaSOT, 87.4% on TrackingNet, and 81.4% AO on GOT-10k (one-shot protocol), outperforming all compared trackers. On LaSOT_ext, ODONet-B224 attains 54.3% AUC, the highest among all methods. On the smaller benchmarks, ODONet-B384 reaches 63.1% AUC on TNL2K, 71.4% AUC on UAV123, and ODONet-B224 achieves 61.6 EAO on VOT2020, all setting new state-of-the-art scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODONet-B384 achieves the highest LaSOT AUC (75.1%) by a narrow margin over SPMTrack-B378 (74.9%) and SUTrack-B384 (74.4%), while also leading on PNorm (84.5) and P (82.7). On TrackingNet, ODONet-B384 (87.4% AUC, 91.6% PNorm) clearly surpasses SUTrack-B384 (86.5%) and SPMTrack-B378 (86.1%). The most substantial gain appears on GOT-10k under the one-shot protocol, where ODONet-B384 reaches 81.4% AO, exceeding the next-best ARTrackV2-L384 (79.5%) by 1.9 points and SUTrack-B384 (79.3%) by 2.1 points. On LaSOT_ext, ODONet-B224 (54.3% AUC) edges out ARTrackV2-L384 (53.4%) and SUTrack-B384 (52.9%). On the smaller benchmarks (Table 2), ODONet-B384 leads TNL2K (63.1% vs. MambaLCT 58.5%) and UAV123 (71.4% vs. MambaLCT 70.1%), while ODONet-B224 achieves the best VOT2020 EAO (61.6 vs. SeqTrack 56.1%). Notably, ODONet-B224 slightly trails SPMTrack-B378 on LaSOT AUC (74.6 vs. 74.9) but compensates with superior PNorm and P scores at a lower input resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODONet extends the deformable attention lineage (Deformable DETR, D-TransT) and deformable convolution (DCN/DCNv2) by making offset generation temporally continuous rather than frame-independent. It departs from prior offline offset methods that treat each inference step in isolation, instead propagating motion-derived offsets across frames via an affine transformation of the previous bounding box. The Dynamic Visual-Motion Feature Interaction module borrows the prompt-based interaction paradigm from SAM, repurposing historical bounding boxes as geometric motion prompts fed through a Two-Way Transformer to fuse appearance and motion at the encoding stage rather than the decoding stage used by autoregressive trackers (ARTrack, SeqTrack).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: existing offset-based trackers break temporal continuity by regenerating offsets from scratch each frame, losing the strong motion prior that the same target's trajectory provides. The online dynamic offset propagation mechanism and the accompanying offset-field visualization/diagnosis tool (Gram-matrix-based failure detection, early drift warning) offer practical tools beyond the tracking accuracy itself. However, the affine-transformation assumption for inter-frame box motion limits applicability to highly deformable or rotating objects, and the modest 1.8% AO gain on GOT-10k suggests the approach may plateau without richer motion modeling. Open questions include whether the online offset mechanism generalizes to multi-object or long-term occlusion scenarios and whether the w_on=0.5 balance is dataset-dependent.
