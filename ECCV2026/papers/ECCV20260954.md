## Problem

Satellite trails from expanding LEO constellations contaminate a large fraction of modern astronomical survey images, yet existing general-domain line detectors fail on the low-SNR, sparse, grayscale nature of astronomical data, and training astronomy-specific models requires expensive pixel-level annotations that are impractical to obtain at survey scale. STARLINC addresses this by enabling pixel-level trail segmentation without any manually annotated real trail masks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260954/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The segmentation network consumes a 3-channel composite: the original grayscale frame, a mean inter-frame differential map computed over a 5×5 window using SSIM-based structural dissimilarity against the remaining 29 exposures in a 30-frame temporal group, and a Grad-CAM heatmap extracted from a ResNet trail-presence classifier. The output is a single-channel probability map thresholded into a binary mask that selectively flags contaminated pixels while preserving all valid astronomical content in the rest of the frame.

## Method

STARLINC is a three-stage pipeline that exploits temporal redundancy in repeated observations of the same sky field. First, synthetic trails parameterized by length (clipped Gaussian), orientation (uniform), thickness (Beta distribution favoring thin lines), and brightness (uniform) are injected into clean frames to provide pixel-level supervision without manual annotation. Second, inter-frame differential maps aggregate local SSIM dissimilarity across adjacent exposures, suppressing persistent stars and galaxies while amplifying transient trail signals. Third, a ResNet classifier trained on image-level trail presence produces Grad-CAM heatmaps that serve as coarse spatial priors. These three channels are concatenated and fed to a U-Net that predicts the final binary trail mask, with the entire system trained end-to-end on synthetic supervision and applied zero-shot to real survey data.

## Results

On the 7DT Orion Molecular Clouds dataset, STARLINC achieves mIoU 0.485 and Dice 0.653 for satellite trail segmentation, more than doubling the best baseline (DT-LSD, mIoU 0.240) and representing a +102% relative mIoU improvement. STARLINC also attains the highest ROC-AUC (0.918) and PR-AUC (0.865) among all methods. In cross-dataset evaluation, STARLINC (trained only on 7DT) achieves mIoU 0.469 on ZTF and 0.471 on NGC-region, outperforming all fine-tuned baselines on both unseen datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STARLINC decisively outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary 7DT segmentation task. Among classical detectors, Hough achieves the highest mIoU (0.142) but still falls far short of STARLINC (0.485); Radon and LSD produce near-zero overlap (mIoU 0.000 and 0.074). Among learning-based line detectors, DT-LSD is the strongest baseline (mIoU 0.240, Dice 0.332), yet STARLINC more than doubles its mIoU; DeepLSD achieves high recall (0.642) but extremely low precision (0.120), yielding only mIoU 0.116. Among astronomy-specific trail detectors, MaxiMask shows the highest precision (0.838) but very low recall (0.159), resulting in mIoU 0.155; UnetLSD reaches mIoU 0.197 and ASTA 0.148, both well below STARLINC. In cross-dataset transfer (Table 2), STARLINC (trained only on 7DT, no fine-tuning) beats all fine-tuned baselines on both ZTF (mIoU 0.469 vs. MaxiMask 0.365) and NGC-region (mIoU 0.471 vs. DeepLSD 0.199), demonstrating strong domain generalizability. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing both the inter-frame differential map and the Grad-CAM heatmap drops mIoU to 0.000, while the differential map alone recovers mIoU 0.410 and adding the heatmap brings it to the full 0.485.

## Relation to Prior Work

STARLINC does not extend a single prior method but rather repurposes standard components—U-Net segmentation, Grad-CAM pseudo-localization, and SSIM-based image comparison—into a pipeline specifically designed for the domain mismatch between general-domain line detectors and low-SNR astronomical imagery. It departs from prior satellite trail detection work (ASTA, MaxiMask, UnetLSD) by eliminating the need for expensive pixel-level annotations through synthetic trail injection, and from general-domain line detectors (Hough, LSD, DeepLSD, DT-LSD) by exploiting temporal redundancy in time-domain surveys via inter-frame differential maps. The inter-frame SSIM differential map is the most novel element, repurposing a perceptual similarity metric as a structural change detector for transient linear artifacts rather than for image quality assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With LEO satellite populations growing at ~30% annually and contaminating up to 73% of survey images, a scalable, annotation-free trail removal method is practically urgent for next-generation surveys generating terabytes of data nightly. The demonstrated cross-dataset generalization (7DT → ZTF, NGC-region) without fine-tuning suggests the approach could be deployed across heterogeneous survey pipelines. Open questions include whether the method extends to multi-band/color observations, whether the 30-frame temporal group assumption holds for surveys with irregular cadence, and how performance degrades when trails overlap with bright stellar sources or extended nebulosity.
