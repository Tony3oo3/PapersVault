## Problem

Remote sensing object detection faces two intertwined challenges—geometric complexity (arbitrary orientations and extreme aspect ratios) and spatial complexity (objects spanning from tiny vehicles to large soccer fields)—that prior backbones address in isolation: strip-only kernels disrupt spatial coherence for regular-shaped objects, while isotropic large kernels introduce background noise for slender structures. PKINet-v1, though multi-scale, relies solely on isotropic square kernels and suffers from fragmented multi-branch inference. The paper asks whether a single unified backbone can jointly handle both challenges without sacrificing deployment efficiency.

## Input / Output

The backbone consumes RGB remote sensing images cropped into 1024×1024 patches (DOTA) or 800×800 (HRSC2016, DIOR-R) and outputs multi-scale feature maps that feed into an oriented detector (e.g., Oriented RCNN) to produce oriented bounding boxes with class labels across 15–16 categories. The architecture is a four-stage hierarchical CNN with progressive 2× downsampling per stage, producing feature maps at H/4, H/8, H/16, and H/32 spatial resolutions. Two variants are provided: PKINet-v2-T (4.0M params, 18.2G FLOPs) and PKINet-v2-S (13.6M params, 54.0G FLOPs) for the backbone alone.

## Method

PKINet-v2's core unit is the Poly-Kernel Scope (PKS) Module, which applies a 5×5 depth-wise convolution for local features followed by five parallel depth-wise branches: one axial-strip (1×19 then 19×1) for full-span slender coverage, three dilated square kernels (3×3, 5×5, 7×7 with dilation 3) for sparse long-range context, and one dense 3×3 for local texture preservation. Branch outputs are normalized by per-branch BatchNorm, summed, fused via a 1×1 convolution into a spatial attention map, and multiplied element-wise with the input. The key efficiency contribution is the Heterogeneous Kernel Re-parameterization (HKR) Strategy, which algebraically absorbs BN into each depth-wise kernel and then merges all five heterogeneous branches into a single 19×19 depth-wise convolution at inference time, eliminating fragmented kernel launches and yielding a 3.9× FPS speedup over PKINet-v1 with zero accuracy loss. The full backbone is pretrained on ImageNet-1K for 300 epochs before fine-tuning within the MMRotate framework for oriented detection.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, PKINet-v2-S achieves 80.46% mAP with Oriented RCNN, surpassing the previous best Strip RCNN-S (80.06%) by 0.4% and PKINet-v1-S (78.39%) by 2.07%. On DOTA-v1.5, it reaches 73.57% mAP, outperforming Strip RCNN-S by 1.3%. On HRSC2016, PKINet-v2-S sets a new SOTA with 90.75% (07) and 98.84% (12) mAP, and on DIOR-R it achieves 69.40% mAP. In terms of efficiency, the HKR strategy yields 54.6 FPS, a 3.9× speedup over PKINet-v1-S (14.05 FPS) while maintaining identical accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PKINet-v2-S outperforms all compared remote sensing backbones across all four benchmarks. Against PKINet-v1-S (its direct predecessor), it gains +2.07% mAP on DOTA-v1.0, +2.1% on DOTA-v1.5, +0.05% on HRSC2016, and +2.37% on DIOR-R, while delivering a 3.9× FPS improvement (54.6 vs. 14.05 FPS) thanks to the HKR re-parameterization. Against the geometry-specialized Strip RCNN-S, PKINet-v2-S wins by +0.4% on DOTA-v1.0, +1.3% on DOTA-v1.5, +0.15% on HRSC2016, and +0.7% on DIOR-R, with notably stronger performance on slender categories (e.g., Bridge: 57.72% vs. 57.44%) and small objects (Small Vehicle: 81.21% vs. 79.73%). Against LSKNet-S, the gains are larger: +2.97% on DOTA-v1.0, +3.31% on DOTA-v1.5, and +3.5% on DIOR-R, confirming that the hybrid strip+square kernel design addresses both geometric and spatial complexity more effectively than isotropic large kernels alone. Against LWGANet-L2, PKINet-v2-S leads by +1.44% on DOTA-v1.0 and +0.87% on DIOR-R while using comparable parameter counts (~30M). The ablation in Table 9 further confirms that the hybrid kernel design (80.11% mAP) outperforms dense-only (78.57%), strip-only (79.62%), and sparse-only (79.36%) variants, validating the necessity of jointly combining anisotropic and isotropic kernels.

## Relation to Prior Work

PKINet-v2 is a direct evolution of PKINet-v1 (Cai et al., CVPR 2024), inheriting its parallel multi-scale receptive field design but breaking the constraint of using solely isotropic square kernels. It unifies two previously isolated paradigms in remote sensing backbones: anisotropic strip convolutions (as in Strip RCNN, which targets slender geometries) and isotropic large/dilated square kernels (as in LSKNet, which targets broad context), arguing that prior methods addressing geometric and spatial complexity separately suffer complementary drawbacks. The Heterogeneous Kernel Re-parameterization (HKR) strategy draws on the broader re-parameterization literature (e.g., Zhang et al. on large-kernel design) to collapse multi-branch depth-wise convolutions into a single equivalent kernel at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving a 3.9× inference speedup over PKINet-v1 while simultaneously improving mAP by ~2%, making multi-branch receptive-field backbones deployable in real-time remote sensing pipelines without the accuracy-accuracy trade-off that typically accompanies efficiency optimizations. The unified hybrid-kernel paradigm offers a principled alternative to the field's tendency to pick either strip-only or square-only designs. Open questions include whether the HKR strategy generalizes to non-depth-wise or channel-mixed branches, whether the specific kernel configurations (19×1 strip, dilated squares) are optimal across all remote sensing domains, and whether the approach transfers to other dense prediction tasks beyond oriented detection.
