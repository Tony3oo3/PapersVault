## Problem

Flow-matching VLA policies generate continuous action chunks but lack any internal signal to judge whether a given prediction is reliable. Distribution shift and long-horizon rollouts push backbone representations away from the low-dimensional decodable manifold the action head was trained on, yet the policy has no mechanism to detect or react to this drift, causing errors to compound silently across control steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262463/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images, a language instruction, and proprioceptive state, which the vision-language backbone encodes into context features H ∈ R^{T×d}. At inference, the previously predicted action chunk is also available to break the circular dependency between the current gate and the current prediction. The output is a continuous action chunk a ∈ R^{K×d_a} (K timesteps, d_a action dimensions) plus a scalar reliability gate g ∈ [g_min, 1] that modulates both the residual feature refinement and, during training, the flow-matching loss weight.

## Method

DiG computes a sliced 2-Wasserstein transport cost D between the backbone feature distribution and a compact action representation obtained by reusing the action expert's own input projection f (mean-pooled centroid broadcast to sequence length T), then maps D through an exponential gate g = max{g_min, exp(−τD)}. The gate modulates a lightweight residual refinement H̃ = H + λ·sg(g)·R(H) and reweights the flow-matching loss as J(θ) = E[sg(g)·L_FM(θ; H̃, a^gt)], where stop-gradient is applied only to the gate so the policy cannot shrink D without improving action prediction. At inference, DiG-Refine runs N refinement iterations that recompute the gate from the current predicted chunk, with the previous chunk providing the initial gate to avoid circularity; each iteration adds one action-expert forward pass (~8 ms) without re-running the backbone. The exponential mapping is justified by a local manifold noise model (Proposition 1), and a mixture-contamination theorem (Theorem 1) shows the gate provably reduces the shortcut fraction in the induced training distribution.

## Results

On LIBERO (40-task tabletop manipulation), π0.5+DiG achieves 98.3% average success rate, outperforming the π0.5 baseline (96.9%) by +1.4 points, with the largest gain on LIBERO-Long (+4.0, from 92.4% to 96.4%). GR00T-N1+DiG reaches 95.3% average, improving over GR00T-N1 (93.9%) by +1.4. On the low-data RoboCasa benchmark (50 demos/task), gains are substantially larger: π0.5+DiG reaches 52.6% average vs. 41.4% for π0.5 (+11.2), and GR00T-N1+DiG reaches 43.2% vs. 36.0% (+7.2). Under OOD real-robot conditions (background changes, human intervention), DiG maintains 30–40% whole-task success where the baseline drops to 10–20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiG is evaluated as a plug-in on two backbone architectures (π0.5 and GR00T-N1) and compared against eight baselines on LIBERO. Against the strongest flow-matching baselines, π0.5+DiG beats π0.5 by +1.4 avg (98.3 vs. 96.9) and π0 by +3.9 (98.3 vs. 94.4), with the gap concentrated on long-horizon tasks (+4.0 on Long). GR00T-N1+DiG improves over GR00T-N1 by +1.4 avg (95.3 vs. 93.9), confirming cross-architecture generality. Against the best non-flow-matching baseline, OpenVLA-OFT (97.1 avg), π0.5+DiG leads by +1.2. Older baselines (OpenVLA 76.5, Diffusion Policy 76.1, SpatialVLA 78.1, CoT-VLA 83.9) are outperformed by 14–22 points on average. The most dramatic relative gains appear under distribution shift: on RoboCasa with only 50 demos, π0.5+DiG gains +11.2 avg over π0.5, and on real-robot OOD trials the whole-task success gap widens from +5–10 (in-distribution) to +15–25 (OOD).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiG repurposes the transport view inherent to flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) not to reshape the probability path—as OT-CFM and minibatch-OT variants do—but as a per-step reliability signal that modulates backbone features and reweights the training loss. The sliced 2-Wasserstein cost (Bonneel et al., 2015; Kolouri et al., 2019) is chosen as the discrepancy metric, and the exponential gate mapping is motivated by a local manifold noise model connecting squared deviations to a chi-square likelihood. Conceptually, the work bridges shortcut-learning and robustness literature (Geirhos et al., 2020; Feng et al., 2022) with the VLA action-generation pipeline, positioning itself as a lightweight plug-in for existing flow-matching VLA architectures (π0.5, GR00T-N1) rather than a new policy architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap: flow-matching VLA policies have no internal mechanism to detect when backbone representations have drifted outside the region the action head decodes reliably, a problem that compounds over long horizons and under distribution shift. The plug-in design (≈8 ms per refinement step on A100) and architecture-agnostic attachment make it broadly applicable to existing VLA systems. Open questions include extending the transport-discrepancy concept to discrete-action or 3D-based VLA models, replacing the simplified Gaussian mixture theory with analysis under more general shifts, and automating the per-backbone tuning of temperature τ and projection count M. The added latency of DiG-Refine (N extra action-expert forward passes) may constrain adoption in control loops faster than 10 Hz.
