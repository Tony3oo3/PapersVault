## Problem

Existing sparse-view 3D Gaussian reconstruction methods are either pixel-aligned (leaving holes in unobserved regions) or voxel-aligned (predicting blurry conditional means for unseen parts), and both produce highly redundant grid-structured outputs. No prior method can generate compact, complete, non-grid-aligned 3D Gaussians that are simultaneously faithful to observed views and produce sharp, plausible completions in unobserved regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260447/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes four posed RGB images (224×224 or 512×512) with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics as input, processed through a frozen DINOv2-Base encoder plus Plücker ray coordinates and positional embeddings. It outputs 8K non-grid-aligned 3D Gaussians (14 parameters each: position, log-scale, quaternion, logit-opacity, RGB) that are directly renderable via the 3DGS rasterizer, requiring roughly 10× fewer Gaussians than grid-aligned baselines (45K–70K). Inference runs 50 Euler denoising steps in ~26 seconds on a single H200 GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is framing sparse-view reconstruction as conditional flow matching over Gaussian parameters, where a DiT denoiser iteratively refines a set of Gaussians from noise given sparse-view features, sidestepping the correspondence problem inherent in regressing non-grid-aligned Gaussians. A binary LoD tree over ground-truth Gaussians enables coarse-to-fine curriculum training (2K→8K Gaussians) and a patchification scheme that merges sibling nodes into joint tokens, halving the transformer sequence length while preserving spatial locality. Three complementary guidance mechanisms recover detail: a timestep-weighted multi-view rendering loss (w(t)=50·(min(1,t/0.9))^5) that sharpens predictions at later denoising stages, photometric gradient guidance that backpropagates rendering loss through the 3DGS rasterizer to steer both training features and inference Euler updates, and classifier-free guidance (10% unconditional dropout) to amplify conditioning fidelity. The 18-block DiT uses AdaLN timestep conditioning, GEGLU feed-forward layers, and QK-normalization via RMSNorm, trained on ~140K Objaverse objects with 3DGS-MCMC supervision for 700K iterations on 64 H200 GPUs.

## Results

Under partial observation (four views concentrated in a hemisphere), Free-Range Gaussians achieves PSNR 29.92 dB, DINO similarity 81.80, DreamSim 9.24, and FID 43.58 on Objaverse, and PSNR 28.08 dB, DINO 72.24, DreamSim 15.50, and FID 27.69 on GSO — all with only 8K Gaussians versus 45K–70K for baselines. Under full observation, the method reaches PSNR 31.66 dB (Objaverse) and 31.49 dB (GSO), competitive with grid-aligned methods while using an order-of-magnitude fewer Gaussians. The largest gains appear in unobserved regions, where FID drops from 65–93 (baselines) to 28–44 (ours), reflecting sharp generative completions rather than blurry conditional means.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pixel-aligned baselines LGM and GS-LRM, Free-Range Gaussians wins on every metric under partial observation: PSNR improves by +7.04 dB over LGM and +2.02 dB over GS-LRM on Objaverse, while FID drops from 88.43 (LGM) and 65.06 (GS-LRM) to 43.58, reflecting the inability of pixel-anchored methods to place Gaussians in unobserved regions. Against the voxel-aligned baseline LaRa, the method gains +2.13 dB PSNR and reduces FID from 93.19 to 43.58 on Objaverse, confirming that multi-view rendering loss alone produces blurry conditional means. Under full observation (Table 2), the method is competitive: it matches or slightly exceeds LaRa and GS-LRM on PSNR (31.66 vs. 31.91 and 31.03 on Objaverse) but trails on LPIPS (53.15 vs. 43.60 and 32.80), suggesting the 8K Gaussian budget limits fine detail when the entire surface is visible. The concurrent generative method ReconViaGen produces realistic unobserved content but is less reliably aligned with input-view poses and geometry, as shown qualitatively in Figures 7–8. Across all comparisons, the method uses 8K Gaussians versus 45K–70K for baselines, an order-of-magnitude reduction in representation size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) as the generative backbone and 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the output representation, but departs from both the pixel-aligned paradigm (LGM, GS-LRM, Splatter Image) and the voxel-aligned paradigm (LaRa, VolSplat) by predicting Gaussians without any grid structure. The hierarchical LoD tree from Kerbl et al. (2024) is repurposed not for rendering but as a patchification mechanism that halves transformer sequence length. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against generate-and-reconstruct pipelines (Zero-1-to-3, SyncDreamer) that require synthesizing novel views first, and against latent-space generative 3D models (TRELLIS, GSD) that still encode geometry into voxel grids, by operating directly in the 14-dimensional Gaussian parameter space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in Gaussian count (8K vs. 45K–70K) while matching or exceeding baseline quality is a practically significant efficiency gain that could make feed-forward 3D reconstruction more accessible. The generative formulation's advantage in unobserved regions (FID 43.6 vs. 65–93 for baselines) addresses a long-standing failure mode of regression-based methods, though the 50-step inference (~26 s) remains slower than single-pass alternatives. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8K Gaussians (the authors suggest sparse or linear attention), whether distillation can reduce the step count, and whether the method extends to pose-free or single-view settings. The dependence on per-object 3DGS-MCMC ground-truth quality also limits robustness for heavily occluded objects.
