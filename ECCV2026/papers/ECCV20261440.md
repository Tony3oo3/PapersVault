## Problem

Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods are limited to single-camera setups or require rigidly mounted, pre-calibrated camera rigs, leaving the practical scenario of multiple freely moving observers capturing a shared dynamic event unaddressed. The core challenges are scale ambiguity across independent monocular cameras, limited or intermittent view overlap between free-moving cameras, and moving objects that violate static-world assumptions in classical multi-view geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes N time-synchronized monocular RGB video sequences (each H×W×3 per frame) along with known per-camera intrinsics, and produces per-frame SE(3) camera poses and per-frame H×W depth maps that together enable dense dynamic scene reconstruction at any timestamp. In the MultiCamRobolab dataset, inputs are 2–3 Azure Kinect streams at 30 FPS with 150–300 frames per clip; in MultiCamVideo, inputs are 3 of 10 simulated camera views at 512×384 resolution with 81 frames. The method only requires RGB images—depth sensors are used solely for ground-truth evaluation.

## Method

The framework decouples the problem into two optimization stages. Stage 1 (tracking) extends MegaSAM's learned-correspondence SLAM to multiple cameras by constructing a spatio-temporal connection graph that links frames via intra-camera temporal continuity, inter-camera spatial overlap at the same timestamp, and historical spatio-temporal connections, enabling joint bundle adjustment with consistent scale; a wide-baseline initialization using VGGT provides a global scale anchor when inter-camera overlap is insufficient. Stage 2 (refinement) computes dense wide-baseline optical flow with UFM across an augmented connection graph and performs two-phase optimization: first aligning per-frame scale and shift parameters, then alternately refining per-pixel depth and camera poses with temporal smoothness regularization. Monocular depth from UniDepth is aligned to the initialization scale and used as a prior throughout, while a learned confidence mechanism down-weights dynamic-object correspondences.

## Results

On the MultiCamVideo synthetic benchmark, the proposed method achieves ATE 0.005, RTE 0.001, and RRE 0.011°, outperforming the best feed-forward baseline FastVGGT (ATE 0.023, RTE 0.008, RRE 0.080°) by 4.6×, 8×, and 7.3× respectively. On the real-world MultiCamRobolab dataset (2 cameras), the method achieves the best ATE across all four scenes (0.005–0.020 m) while consuming the least GPU memory (20.04 GB) among all feed-forward methods. For depth quality, the method attains Abs.Rel as low as 0.011 and δ₁.₂₅ up to 0.991, with scene consistency (M_d) of 0.091 m on RoboDog_overlap, the best across all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on the MultiCamVideo benchmark and achieves the best or near-best results on MultiCamRobolab. Classical SfM methods (COLMAP, GLOMAP) fail entirely on the RoboDog_non-overlap scenes and produce noisy poses in the presence of dynamic objects; even with dynamic masks or NetVLAD retrieval, they cannot recover from the lack of inter-camera overlap. Among feed-forward models, FastVGGT is the strongest baseline (e.g., ATE 0.021 vs. 0.011 on RoboDog_overlap), but the proposed method still wins by roughly 2× on ATE and 3× on RTE across scenes. VGGT requires 8× subsampling to fit in 40 GB memory and still lags behind (ATE 0.024 vs. 0.011). Fast3R struggles with dynamic content (RRE up to 4.7° on non-overlap) and uses nearly 2× the GPU memory of the proposed method. CUT3R, despite being designed for sequential video processing, produces non-smooth per-camera trajectories and achieves ATE 0.277 on RoboDog_overlap—25× worse than the proposed method—because it processes cameras sequentially rather than jointly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MegaSAM's single-camera learned-correspondence SLAM to the multi-camera setting by introducing a spatio-temporal connection graph that links frames across cameras based on view overlap, enabling joint bundle adjustment with consistent scale. It departs from calibrated multi-camera rig SLAM (which assumes known extrinsics) and multi-agent SLAM (which assumes static scenes) by handling freely moving cameras in dynamic environments. Rather than relying on feed-forward reconstruction models (VGGT, Fast3R, CUT3R) as the primary engine, the paper uses VGGT only for wide-baseline initialization and UniDepth for depth priors, then performs iterative optimization with dense UFM optical flow for refinement—a hybrid that borrows from both the SLAM and feed-forward lineages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly reconstruct dense dynamic scenes and estimate poses from multiple free-moving cameras, a setting that arises naturally in robotics, sports broadcasting, and consumer multi-phone capture. The introduced MultiCamRobolab dataset with motion-capture ground truth fills a gap in evaluation infrastructure for this task. Key limitations include degraded performance when cameras have little or no view overlap (where the spatio-temporal graph degenerates to independent per-camera tracking), and the method is validated only on 2–3 cameras in controlled lab and synthetic settings. Open questions include scaling to larger camera counts, handling truly asynchronous capture, and whether the optimization-based refinement can be made real-time.
