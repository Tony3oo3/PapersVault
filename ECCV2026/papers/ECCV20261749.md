## Problem

Despite impressive manipulation capabilities, VLA models' operational safety under strict physical and semantic constraints remains largely unverified. Existing benchmarks rely on static, deterministic environments and prohibitively slow human teleoperation, while their safety evaluations are confined to simple tabletop obstacles—neglecting multi-dimensional risks such as semantic instruction refusal, human-robot interaction safety, and proximal collision avoidance around complex hand-object configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261749/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The physical safety track consumes RGB camera observations, a natural language task instruction, and proprioceptive robot state, producing an SE(3) end-effector action trajectory evaluated by Success Rate (requiring goal completion with zero constraint violations), Collision Rate, Log-Dimensionless Jerk, and execution time. The semantic safety track instead pairs a static environmental observation with a natural language instruction and requires a binary safe/unsafe judgment, scored by Refusal Rate. The keypose-driven generation pipeline takes sparse object-centric keyposes (K critical stages, each with N=5 spatial variants) and outputs kinematically feasible, collision-free trajectories via CuRobo, achieving a 1:M data yield versus the 1:1 ratio of teleoperation.

## Method

The paper introduces UBDDL, an extension of BDDL that procedurally generates 7,603 stochastic scenes with 953 objects and 462 hand-object pairs (via MANO + GrabNet), encoding safety constraints as state-dependent Boolean predicates enforced at every timestep. A five-dimensional safety taxonomy (Affordance-Aware Grasping, HRI, Tabletop Spatial Avoidance, Free-Space Hand-Object Avoidance, Semantic Safety Reasoning) is scaled across three difficulty tiers (L0–L2), yielding 75 unique benchmark tasks. To overcome teleoperation bottlenecks, a keypose-driven pipeline decouples human intent from motion execution: operators define sparse object-centric keyposes in 1.8 minutes per task, and CuRobo synthesizes collision-free trajectories subject to A(x(t)) ∩ O = ∅ for all t, with combinatorial diversity from per-stage variant sampling. The resulting 19,664 human-screened demonstrations incorporate extensive visual and physical domain randomization, and are used to fine-tune eight VLA architectures via behavior cloning, while two embodied foundation models are evaluated zero-shot on the semantic reasoning track.

## Results

On the Embodied Physical Safety Track, π₀.₅ achieves the highest overall success rate, reaching 78.7% (AAG-L0), 88.7% (HRI-L1), and 62.7% (TSA-L1), but drops to 35.3–51.3% on L2 tasks. OpenVLA fails catastrophically, scoring below 13% on nearly all physical suites. On the Semantic Safety Reasoning Track, RoboBrain2.0-7B achieves 80% refusal rate at L0 but collapses to 20% at L2, while RynnBrain-CoP maintains 36% at L2. The privileged CuRobo planner baseline achieves 87% SR with 0% collision rate on FSHOA-L2, far exceeding any learned VLA policy (best: 51.3% SR, 10% CR for π₀.₅).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four Standard VLA models, π₀.₅ dominates all other baselines across every suite and difficulty level, achieving 78.7–88.7% SR on L0/L1 versus 50–80% for OpenVLA-OFT and below 40% for OpenVLA and π₀. GR00T N1.6 (dual-system) is the strongest non-π₀.₅ model, reaching 64.7–85.3% on L0/L1 but trailing π₀.₅ by 10–20 points on most tasks. OpenVLA is effectively non-functional on this benchmark (≤22% SR across all physical suites), confirming that standard large-scale pre-training without safety alignment is insufficient. On the Semantic Safety Reasoning Track, RoboBrain2.0-7B leads at L0 (80% RR) but degrades sharply to 20% at L2, while RynnBrain-CoP shows more stable reasoning (72% at L1, 36% at L2), indicating that explicit chain-of-thought reasoning paradigms are critical for adversarial instruction robustness. The privileged CuRobo motion planner achieves 87% SR with 0% collision rate on FSHOA-L2, establishing an upper bound that no learned VLA policy approaches (π₀.₅: 51.3% SR, 10% CR), highlighting a fundamental gap between optimization-based planning and learned reactive safety.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LIBERO benchmark lineage (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-Pro) by introducing a parametric safety dimension via UBDDL, an extension of BDDL that injects stochasticity, dynamic entities, and explicit safety constraints into task definitions. It departs from VLA-Arena and SafeLIBERO, which evaluate only simple tabletop collision avoidance, by adding free-space hand-object avoidance (FSHOA) around MANO-modeled human hands and a semantic safety reasoning track for adversarial instruction refusal. The keypose-driven data generation pipeline, coupling sparse human intent with CuRobo motion planning, is a methodological departure from the human teleoperation that dominates prior VLA data collection (LIBERO, VLA-Arena, RoboCasa), addressing a scalability bottleneck the authors quantify at ~4× human effort reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LIBERO-Safety provides the first unified benchmark covering five safety dimensions (affordance grasping, HRI, tabletop avoidance, free-space hand-object avoidance, semantic reasoning) across three difficulty tiers, filling a gap where prior benchmarks conflated task success with safety. The key finding that task incompletion is frequently decoupled from physical safety violations—models halt conservatively or oscillate rather than collide—challenges the implicit assumption that safer policies are simply slower versions of aggressive ones. Limitations include the simulation-only setting (no real-world contact dynamics or hardware latency), the retained human-in-the-loop screening step, and the absence of hard-negative unsafe trajectories in training. Open questions include whether the generalization-safety tension persists under reinforcement learning fine-tuning and whether the keypose pipeline can be fully automated.
