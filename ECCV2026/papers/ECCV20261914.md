## Problem

Prior work combining vision-language-action models (VLAs) with geometric foundation models (GFMs) reports promising results but leaves three questions unanswered: whether current VLAs already possess sufficient geometric understanding, which architectural strategy best injects geometric information into a VLA, and how non-architectural factors (training data scale, camera count, reconstruction quality) affect performance. This paper provides a rigorous, controlled experimental analysis to address all three questions for a specific VLA (GR00T-N1.5) and GFM (VGGT) pairing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261914/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLA consumes RGB images from one to three robot cameras (left, right, wrist), a natural-language instruction, and robot joint-state vectors; the GFM (VGGT) independently processes the same RGB images to produce dense geometric tokens (camera poses, depth, point maps) without requiring any additional sensor modalities. The output is a chunk of T robot actions generated by a diffusion-policy action expert trained with a flow-matching objective. For the linear-probing experiments, the system is evaluated on NYU Depth V2 RGB–depth pairs (24k train / 645 val) using a SILog loss, reporting RMSE and δ₁ depth metrics.

## Method

The paper implements three geometric-VLA architectures under a unified cross-attention fusion module with attention gating (gates initialized near zero for gradual integration) and LoRA-parameterized projections: Early Fusion injects VGGT tokens alongside vision-encoder output before the LLM, Late Fusion injects them alongside LLM output before the action expert, and Spatial Forcing adds a cosine-similarity alignment loss between a chosen LLM layer and VGGT tokens during training. A linear-probing protocol (frozen VLA + trainable MLP head) quantifies the 'geometric gap' by measuring how well each architecture's internal representations support monocular depth prediction. The study then varies training-data scale (task-level finetuning vs. full-dataset mid-training), camera count (3-camera vs. single-camera), and VGGT reconstruction quality, reporting success rates on RoboCasa, LIBERO, and a real Unitree G1 humanoid with McNemar's-test significance levels to distinguish genuine gains from stochastic variation.

## Results

Linear probing on NYU Depth V2 quantifies the 'geometric gap': GR00T-N1.5's VLM output achieves RMSE 0.73 m and δ₁ 0.63 for depth estimation, while VGGT achieves RMSE 0.41 m and δ₁ 0.89 — nearly a 2× RMSE gap. On the RoboCasa benchmark (8 pick-and-place tasks, 600 trials each), the GR00T-N1.5 baseline achieves 71.7% average success rate, while Early Fusion (67.7%), Late Fusion (71.0%), and Spatial Forcing (68.3%) show no statistically significant difference (all p > 0.05). However, under a single-camera setup, Early Fusion reaches 21.5% vs. 17.2% for GR00T (p = 0.030), and on the real Unitree G1 humanoid, Early Fusion achieves 27.78% overall success with a significantly better approach stage (84.44% vs. 57.78%, p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GR00T-N1.5 baseline (71.7% average on RoboCasa), none of the three geometric VLA variants achieve a statistically significant improvement under standard task-level finetuning: Early Fusion (67.7%, p = 0.399), Late Fusion (71.0%, p = 0.806), and Spatial Forcing (68.3%, p = 0.154) all fall within the noise floor. The paper's key finding is that the advantage of geometric injection emerges under specific conditions: with mid-training on the full RoboCasa dataset before task finetuning, Early Fusion becomes significantly better than baseline; in a single-camera setup, Early Fusion outperforms GR00T by 4.3 percentage points (21.5% vs. 17.2%, p = 0.030); and on the real Unitree G1, Early Fusion improves overall success from 22.22% to 27.78% with a highly significant gain in the approach stage (84.44% vs. 57.78%, p < 0.001). Compared to prior VLA baselines on RoboCasa, GR00T-N1.5 already substantially outperforms π₀ (33.8%), π₀-Fast (48.3%), DP-VLA (35.8%), and GR00T N1 (22.6%), making it a strong baseline that is difficult to beat. The linear probing results further show that both Early and Late Fusion architectures recover depth understanding close to VGGT (RMSE 0.44/0.45 vs. 0.41), confirming that the geometric information is successfully injected even though the LLM remains frozen.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic empirical analysis at the intersection of VLAs and geometric foundation models, building directly on GR00T-N1.5 as the VLA backbone and VGGT as the GFM. It unifies three previously disparate strategies for injecting geometric tokens into VLAs—Early Fusion (treating the GFM as an additional encoder, as in [30]), Late Fusion (enriching LLM outputs before the action expert, as in [48]), and Spatial Forcing (alignment loss during training, [29])—by implementing all three with consistent low-level details (cross-attention with attention gating, LoRA projections) for the first time. The paper departs from prior work primarily by providing the first quantitative measurement of the 'geometric gap' via linear probing, and by isolating non-architectural factors (data scaling, camera count, reconstruction quality) that prior geometric VLA papers did not systematically ablate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that basic task-level finetuning of geometric VLAs does not yield statistically significant improvements over the GR00T-N1.5 baseline—challenges the implicit assumption in recent literature that simply injecting GFM tokens helps manipulation. However, the paper shows that scaling training data (mid-training across all tasks) and reducing to a single camera do unlock significant gains, suggesting the field should invest in larger-scale geometric VLA training rather than expecting immediate wins from architectural modifications alone. Key limitations include evaluation on a single VLA/GFM pair (GR00T-N1.5 + VGGT), predominantly simulation benchmarks, and the absence of ablations over alternative fusion mechanisms. Open questions include whether conclusions generalize to other VLA architectures, whether end-to-end joint training of the GFM and VLA (rather than frozen GFM) would close the remaining gap, and whether the single-camera advantage persists in real-world sensor-constrained deployments.
