## Problem

Zero-shot HOI detection with CLIP-based models suffers from a two-fold representational bottleneck: fine-grained interaction cues (contact regions, poses) are overshadowed by high-level global semantics, and models develop background shortcut bias that relies on scene co-occurrences rather than actual human-object contact. These issues severely limit generalization to unseen verbs and novel human-object combinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260207/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and handcrafted text templates of the form 'a photo of a person [verb-ing] an object' (augmented with learnable prompt tokens) as input. A pre-trained DETR detector first localizes humans and objects, producing bounding boxes, category labels, confidence scores, and feature vectors. The output is a set of HOI triplets (human box, object box, verb label) with a final confidence score computed as the product of the interaction score and the human/object detection confidences raised to a weighting exponent.

## Method

DRIC introduces two complementary modules inserted into the CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder. The CORD module constructs a global prototype bank from the CLS token and four spatial region averages, then orthogonally projects each patch feature onto this subspace to separate a global common component from an interaction-specific residual; the residual is selectively reintegrated via a sigmoid-gated modulation, and HO tokens attend to the residual patches through cross-attention to route local evidence to each pair. The SLF module extracts HO tokens from all 12 encoder layers, aligns them with lightweight per-layer adapters, and fuses them via learnable weights (initialized to favor the final layer) with an L1 similarity consistency loss to preserve CLIP's alignment. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: CORD is first optimized alone with Focal Loss to establish fine-grained features, then frozen while SLF is trained on the fused score, preventing fusion gradients from disrupting the orthogonal projection subspace.

## Results

On HICO-DET, DRIC achieves the best Harmonic Mean (HM) across all five zero-shot settings: 33.74 (RF-UC), 35.28 (NF-UC), 34.08 (UC), 31.87 (UV), and 36.04 (UO). The largest unseen-category gains are on NF-UC (38.59 mAP unseen) and UO (38.51 mAP unseen), where DRIC surpasses the strongest baseline LAIN by 2.18 and 0.63 mAP points respectively. Under fully supervised evaluation, DRIC reaches 36.30 Full mAP on HICO-DET and 65.7 AP_role^S2 on V-COCO, both state-of-the-art. In a background-perturbation robustness test, DRIC suffers only a 2.18% Full mAP drop versus 12.26% for the baseline and 3.56% for LAIN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRIC outperforms all four baselines (CMMP, EZ-HOI, LAIN, VDRP) on every zero-shot setting in Table 1, with the largest margins over CMMP and EZ-HOI (e.g., +3.26 mAP unseen on NF-UC vs. CMMP; +4.39 mAP unseen on UV vs. EZ-HOI). Against the strongest baseline LAIN [22], DRIC gains +2.18 mAP on NF-UC unseen, +0.94 on UC unseen, +0.63 on UO unseen, +0.53 on UV unseen, and +0.36 on RF-UC unseen, with consistent HM improvements of 0.46–0.97 points. VDRP [52] trails DRIC by 1.0–2.1 mAP on unseen categories across settings, confirming that its context-embedding perturbations are less effective than explicit orthogonal disentanglement. Under fully supervised evaluation (Table 2), DRIC leads LAIN on Full mAP (36.30 vs. 36.02) and V-COCO AP_role^S2 (65.7 vs. 65.1), though LAIN retains a narrow edge on the Rare subset (35.70 vs. 35.44). In the background-perturbation robustness test (Table 8), DRIC's 2.18% Full mAP drop is substantially smaller than LAIN's 3.56% and the baseline's 12.26%, directly validating the background-shortcut mitigation claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRIC extends the two-stage zero-shot HOI detection paradigm established by CLIP4HOI and refined by LAIN, EZ-HOI, and CMMP, all of which use CLIP's joint embedding space for open-vocabulary interaction classification. Its key departure is an explicit geometric intervention: rather than relying on CLIP's final-layer representations (which prior methods treat as sufficient), DRIC inserts orthogonal projection modules (CORD) before every CLIP encoder layer to structurally separate global scene semantics from interaction-specific residuals, and aggregates multi-depth features (SLF) to counteract the background shortcut bias that prior work has only partially addressed through spatial priors or prompt engineering. The framework is built on DETR for detection and ViT-B/16 CLIP for the visual encoder, following the standard setup of recent two-stage methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a well-identified weakness in CLIP-based zero-shot HOI methods—reliance on scene co-occurrence rather than actual contact-level evidence—and provides a clean, interpretable mechanism (orthogonal subspace decomposition) to address it. The background-perturbation experiment (12.26% drop for baseline vs. 2.18% for DRIC) offers concrete evidence that the method reduces spurious correlation, which is practically important for cross-scene deployment in robotics and AR/VR. However, the absolute mAP gains over the strongest baseline (LAIN) are modest (0.36–2.18%), evaluations are limited to HICO-DET and V-COCO with a ViT-B/16 backbone, and it remains open whether the orthogonal disentanglement strategy transfers to larger vision-language models or to video-based interaction understanding.
