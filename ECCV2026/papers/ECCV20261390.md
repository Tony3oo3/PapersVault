## Problem

Existing vision encoders operate on static 2D images, static 3D point clouds, or 2D video frames, none of which jointly capture spatial geometry and temporal motion dynamics in a language-aligned representation. No prior work has extended the contrastive pretraining paradigm (e.g., CLIP) to 4D representations that model temporally evolving point clouds, leaving embodied AI agents without a foundational encoder for language-grounded reasoning over dynamic physical environments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw, unordered 4D point cloud sequences of N frames (each frame divided into k=64 groups of g=32 points) paired with natural language descriptions; for 4DVLM, a text question is additionally provided. The point encoder produces per-frame embeddings of dimension d1=512, which are progressively transformed through spatial and temporal transformers to a final 768-dimensional 4D scene embedding, while the text encoder (DistilBERT) yields a matching 768-dimensional text embedding. For 4DVLM, the spatio-temporal embeddings are linearly projected to 4096 dimensions to match the LLaMA hidden size before being injected as visual tokens into the language model, which generates a free-form text answer.

## Method

CL4D employs a two-stage pipeline. In stage 1, a PointNet-based point encoder processes each frame into k+1 embeddings (including a learnable CLS token), which are fed to a 4-layer spatial transformer (Vs) to capture intra-frame geometry; per-frame mean, max, and CLS outputs are stacked into a 3×512×N tensor, patched into 16×16 patches, and encoded by a 12-layer temporal transformer (Vt, initialized from ViT-B/16) to model inter-frame dynamics, yielding a mean-pooled 768-d 4D embedding. A DistilBERT text encoder produces the corresponding text embedding, and the two are aligned via a symmetric InfoNCE loss (motion-to-text + text-to-motion) with cosine similarity over a batch of B=72 pairs. In stage 2, 4DVLM freezes both the CL4D encoder and the LLaVA-v1.5-7b language backbone, training only a linear projection layer for 10 epochs on a synthetic 4D-VQA dataset to enable instruction-following and question answering over dynamic point clouds. The key distinction from prior 4D encoders is the use of contrastive alignment rather than cross-entropy classification over fixed action labels, enabling open-vocabulary zero-shot retrieval and generalization to non-human agents (e.g., robotic manipulation in RH20T).

## Results

On the DynAction4D benchmark, CL4D achieves 70.32% Recall@1 (Batch) for text-to-motion retrieval on the HumanOnly segment, surpassing the best prior 4D encoder (P4Transformer at 53.57%) by 16.75%. On the ObjInteractions and Cluttered segments, CL4D improves R@1 (Batch) by 8.1% and 13.36% respectively over the strongest baseline (Motion PointNet). On the real-world robotic manipulation dataset RH20T, CL4D-mini reaches 77.24% R@1 (Batch) for text-to-motion, outperforming Motion PointNet (68.21%) by 9.03%. For 4D VQA, 4DVLM achieves BLEU 0.0729 and ROUGE-1 0.3857, exceeding the best video VLM baselines (VideoLLaMA 3: BLEU 0.0437; Gemini-3.0 Flash: BLEU 0.0447) and substantially outperforming GPT-5 (BLEU 0.0184).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL4D is compared against three prior 4D encoders (P4Transformer, PST-Transformer, Motion PointNet) that were originally trained with cross-entropy classification but retrained here with the same contrastive objective for fair comparison. CL4D wins on every dataset segment and retrieval direction: on HumanOnly it beats the strongest baseline (P4Transformer) by 16.75% in text-to-motion R@1 (Batch); on ObjInteractions it surpasses Motion PointNet by 8.03%; on Cluttered it leads Motion PointNet by 13.36%; and on RH20T, CL4D-mini exceeds Motion PointNet by 9.03% (text-to-motion) and 4.82% (motion-to-text). For the 4DVLM VQA evaluation, the paper compares against four frontier video VLMs: VideoLLaMA 3, Gemini-3.0 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Pro (single- and multi-view), and GPT-5. 4DVLM outperforms all of them across every metric (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, SimCSE), with the largest margin over GPT-5 (BLEU 0.0729 vs. 0.0184, a ~3× gap) and a consistent 4–5 point ROUGE-1 advantage over the closest competitor Gemini-3.0 Flash (0.3857 vs. 0.3389). Notably, the video baselines receive rendered mesh videos of the same 4D scenes, so the advantage is attributed to native geometric 4D representations rather than input modality differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

CL4D extends the contrastive pretraining lineage from CLIP (image-text) through VideoCLIP (video-text) and PointCLIP/ULIP (static 3D point cloud-text) to the previously unexplored modality of temporally evolving 4D point clouds. The key conceptual departure from prior 4D encoders (P4Transformer, PST-Transformer, Motion PointNet) is replacing cross-entropy classification over a fixed action vocabulary with a shared embedding space learned via InfoNCE, enabling open-vocabulary cross-modal retrieval. For the downstream VLM, 4DVLM repurposes the LLaVA architecture by swapping the 2D image encoder for the CL4D spatio-temporal transformer, making it the first VLM whose visual input is a raw dynamic point cloud sequence rather than RGB frames or static geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of DynAction4D as a structured benchmark for language-4D alignment fills a genuine gap, and the contrastive 4D encoder paradigm offers a principled path toward open-vocabulary reasoning over dynamic scenes that skeleton-based or classification-based methods cannot support. However, the most striking claim—that 4DVLM outperforms GPT-5 and Gemini on VQA—rests on a single synthetic Unity-rendered dataset whose QA pairs were themselves generated by Gemini, and the video baselines receive only mesh-rendered approximations of the 4D scenes, making the comparison somewhat asymmetric. Open questions include whether the advantage persists on real LiDAR-captured 4D data with sensor noise, whether the approach scales to longer sequences and more complex multi-agent scenes, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap limits practical deployment in embodied AI.
