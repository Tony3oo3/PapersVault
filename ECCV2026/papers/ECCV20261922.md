## Problem

Learning implicit constitutive laws from monocular dynamic video is severely ill-posed due to two compounding ambiguities: geometric ambiguity from a single viewpoint and material ambiguity from underconstrained physical parameters. Existing implicit methods (e.g., NeuMA) converge to suboptimal solutions under noisy, sparse monocular supervision, while explicit methods that rely on predefined constitutive equations diverge entirely when adapted to monocular settings, as demonstrated empirically in this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261922/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static multi-view orbital scan (used solely for initial 3D Gaussian geometry reconstruction) and a single fixed-viewpoint monocular video of the object undergoing dynamic deformation. A pre-trained monocular depth estimator (DAV) and a feature-matching network (SuperGlue) provide auxiliary geometric signals consumed internally by the RDGA module. The output is a LoRA-adapted pair of neural elasticity and plasticity networks (the implicit constitutive law) that can be transferred to novel object geometries and applied in multi-object interaction scenarios, alongside differentiable 3D Gaussian rendering of the dynamic scene.

## Method

GCA fine-tunes NCLaw's neural constitutive architecture (elasticity and plasticity networks with semi-implicit Euler integration) via LoRA, preserving the base model's physical capabilities while adapting to observations. Two coordinated alignment modules resolve the dual ambiguities: (1) RDGA establishes robust geometric constraints by computing Spearman rank correlation between rendered and estimated depth in local neighborhoods around SuperGlue-matched correspondences, filtering pairs by a consensus threshold and restricting to morphologically-defined stable interior regions—this scale-invariant formulation avoids the absolute-scale noise that causes L1 depth losses to degrade below baseline; (2) CPR evaluates four elastic and four plastic candidate constitutive models, estimates their parameters via least-squares fitting on a fixed subset of deformation gradients, computes credibility weights as inverse-variance measures, and adds weighted residuals as soft regularization—remaining effective even when the true material is absent from the hypothesis library. The total loss combines mask consistency, the RDGA geometric objective (global point-set alignment plus anchor-level rank supervision), and the CPR physics residuals, optimized end-to-end through the differentiable 3DGS renderer.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (6 material types), GCA achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 0.496, a 48% reduction over NeuMA (0.954) and a 77% reduction over NCLaw (2.188). On the real-to-sim dataset, GCA attains the lowest CD across all three objects (Dragon: 2.081, Wolf: 5.842, Pudding: 0.906), outperforming NeuMA by 41–93% depending on the object. Under monocular supervision on the SpringGaus real-world dataset, GCA produces superior visual quality (PSNR/LPIPS) compared to tri-view baselines, and direct law-level validation shows the learned constitutive response error drops from 39.6% (NeuMA) to 8.0% (GCA) on held-out deformation gradients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCA outperforms all three baselines on every evaluated dataset. Against NCLaw [37] (ICML 2023), which requires dense particle-level ground truth unavailable in visual settings, GCA reduces average CD from 2.188 to 0.496 on synthetic data and from 38–49 to 0.9–5.8 on real-to-sim objects. Against NeuMA [7] (NeurIPS 2024), the closest implicit baseline using LoRA-based visual alignment, GCA achieves 48% lower average CD on synthetic data (0.496 vs. 0.954) and 41–93% lower CD on real-to-sim objects, with the largest gap on the Gel Pudding (0.906 vs. 13.804). Against PAC-NeRF [30] (ICLR 2023), an explicit constitutive method, the comparison is stark under monocular supervision: PAC-NeRF augmented with monocular depth diverges entirely (CD > 100), while GCA remains stable at CD 0.92 (Ball) and 0.32 (Cat), demonstrating that explicit parameterization is fundamentally incompatible with noisy monocular geometric signals without the rank-based and soft-prior mechanisms introduced here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit neural constitutive law framework established by NCLaw (Ma et al., ICML 2023) and the visual-physical alignment approach of NeuMA (Cao et al., NeurIPS 2024), which first demonstrated that LoRA-based fine-tuning of neural material models can be supervised by differentiable rendering. The key departure is addressing the monocular supervision gap: NeuMA relies on pixel-level color matching that degrades under single-viewpoint video, while explicit methods (PAC-NeRF, SpringGaus) use predefined constitutive equations that diverge under monocular depth noise. GCA introduces two new alignment mechanisms—Rank-based Depth-Geometric Anchors (RDGA) using scale-invariant Spearman rank correlation, and a Constitutive Prior Regularizer (CPR) that treats classical models as soft differentiable priors—positioning itself as a hybrid that preserves implicit generalization while recovering explicit stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that monocular video, the most common real-world capture setting, is fundamentally incompatible with both existing explicit and implicit approaches without the specific alignment design proposed here, as evidenced by PAC-NeRF diverging (CD > 100) when naively augmented with monocular depth. The 48% Chamfer Distance improvement over NeuMA and the law-level validation (39.6% → 8.0% constitutive response error) suggest that robust geometric anchoring plus soft physical priors can recover accurate material models from severely underconstrained observations. However, the method still requires a static multi-view scan for geometric initialization, depends on the quality of monocular depth estimators, and has only been validated on single-object dynamics—extending to fully monocular reconstruction and dense multi-object scenes with heterogeneous materials remains open.
