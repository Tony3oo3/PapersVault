## Problem

Existing RL post-training methods for flow-matching models (e.g., Flow-GRPO, DDPO) recast stochastic sampling as a multi-step MDP, where each denoising step is a separate policy action. This produces flow updates with a large reward-neutral noise component, causing slow convergence, uncontrolled style drift, and reward-hacking artifacts under extended training. The paper asks whether the signal-to-noise ratio of these updates can be improved by treating the entire sampling trajectory as a single action rather than a sequence of independent steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (conditioning embedding) and produces a 512×512 RGB image via a flow-matching ODE solved in 40 Euler steps, using Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium with LoRA adapters. During training, each prompt yields a pair of trajectories sharing the same initial noise but diverging through stochastic perturbations, so the effective input to the update rule is a pair of images plus their scalar rewards. The output is a single generated image at inference time, while the training signal is the reward-weighted pixel-space difference between the paired outputs.

## Method

The core idea is to replace per-step MDP policy gradients with a finite-difference update over the full trajectory. Two rollouts are generated from the same initial noise using a stochastic flow-matching sampler (adapted from EDM, which overshoots the target time then re-noises to land at the correct noise level, avoiding the time-conditioning inconsistency of Euler-Maruyama). The reward-weighted image difference ΔR·Δx̄ (normalized by RMS norm) is computed at the endpoints, and the flow velocity at every intermediate step along both trajectories is trained to bend toward this direction, relying on the approximate non-rotational (positive semi-definite Jacobian) property of diffusion flows to propagate the endpoint signal backward. On-policy stability is maintained via SPO clipping, and the method requires only two rollouts per prompt per epoch, enabling higher prompt coverage and direct use of non-differentiable rewards such as live human pairwise preferences.

## Results

On OneIG-Bench prompt alignment (combined reward, no CFG/KL), the full method reaches 84.25 at its peak versus 79.56 for Flow-GRPO, and already leads at 200 epochs (82.13 vs. 71.06). In wall-clock terms, the method crosses the combined-reward threshold of 19× faster in the 40-step baseline configuration and 5× faster in the 10-step fast configuration (NVIDIA H200 GPU hours). Flow-GRPO reaches a PickScore of 23.43 after 1000 epochs; the proposed method reaches a higher PickScore substantially earlier and additionally avoids the grid-like reward-hacking artifacts that appear in Flow-GRPO after extended training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Flow-GRPO [Liu et al., NeurIPS 2025], the current state-of-the-art RL post-training method for flow-matching models. The proposed method (FDFO) wins decisively on every measured axis: it converges 19× faster in wall-clock GPU hours (40-step setting) to the same combined-reward level, reaches a higher peak OneIG-Bench alignment score (84.25 vs. 79.56), and attains a higher PickScore (23.43 for Flow-GRPO at 1000 epochs vs. a higher value reached earlier by FDFO). Flow-GRPO exhibits two failure modes absent in FDFO: a consistent style drift across prompts and intermittent grid-like reward-hacking artifacts that appear after ~800 epochs. On the VLM-based prompt-alignment reward, the gap widens further because Flow-GRPO's noisy per-step updates struggle with the harder-to-optimize signal, while FDFO's paired-trajectory finite-difference signal remains effective. The ablation table confirms that the gains are attributable to the finite-difference update rule (row C, +5.4 over Flow-GRPO at 200 epochs) and the shared-noise pairing (row D), not merely to the stochastic sampler (row B, +5.7 over Flow-GRPO but still below the full method).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MDP formulation for RL post-training of flow matching models (DDPO, Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO), which treats each denoising step as a separate policy action with Gaussian transitions. Instead, it treats the entire sampling trajectory as a single action and uses a finite-difference signal from paired stochastic rollouts to update the flow velocity uniformly along the trajectory. The stochastic sampling mechanism is adapted from the EDM sampler (Karras et al., NeurIPS 2022), and the on-policy optimization uses SPO clipping analogous to PPO. Conceptually, the method repurposes the finite-difference gradient estimation idea from optimization into the flow-matching post-training setting, replacing noisy per-step policy gradients with a single reward-weighted image difference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is a practical drop-in replacement for Flow-GRPO with substantially faster convergence (19× in baseline, 5× in fast configuration) and fewer reward-hacking artifacts, making it likely to become the default RL post-training algorithm for flow matching models. The demonstrated 4-hour human-in-the-loop training loop is a new practical capability that was infeasible with prior methods due to sample inefficiency. Open questions include how to best preserve diversity (KL regularization remains ad hoc), whether the 'non-rotational flow' assumption that justifies uniform bending holds for all architectures, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation where trajectories are longer and more complex.
