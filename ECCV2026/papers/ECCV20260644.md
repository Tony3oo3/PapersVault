## Problem

Real-world image dehazing suffers from a significant domain gap between synthetic training data and natural haze, causing supervised methods to generalize poorly. Unpaired GAN-based approaches mitigate this but introduce training instability and hallucinatory artifacts, while recent RL-based restoration paradigms rely on computationally expensive diffusion models with inefficient exploration in abstract latent spaces. The paper addresses how to perform efficient, physically grounded RL-based dehazing without paired ground truth or iterative diffusion sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image as input and produces a dehazed RGB image of the same spatial resolution. No paired ground truth is required at training or inference time. The output is generated through a sequential pipeline: the agent first inverts the Atmospheric Scattering Model using predicted transmission and atmospheric light, then applies differentiable ISP enhancement (contrast and saturation), and finally fuses the result with a learned residual feature via a spatially variant gating map.

## Method

Dehaze-RL formulates dehazing as a one-shot Contextual Bandit problem optimized with GRPO, where a U-Net policy network predicts a Physics Hybrid Action Space comprising stochastic distributions over physical parameters (transmission map t, atmospheric light A), ISP enhancement factors (saturation S, contrast C), and a Beta-distributed gating map g. The action space is anchored to Dark Channel Prior estimates via residual corrections, ensuring physically plausible exploration. A Multi-Granularity Reward Mechanism replaces ground-truth supervision, combining CLIP-based semantic similarity, VLM feedback from Qwen2.5-VL-7B with no-reference metrics (MUSIQ, NIQE), and structural consistency via PatchNCE and physical reversibility constraints. Standard KL divergence in GRPO is replaced with a composite action regularization (anchor loss, residual norm, gating penalty) to prevent trivial solutions and enforce minimal deviation from physical priors. Training proceeds in two phases: unpaired adversarial warmup followed by full-parameter GRPO fine-tuning with group size G=6.

## Results

On the real-world RTTS dataset, Dehaze-RL achieves the best NIQE (3.641), MUSIQ (63.921), and MANIQA (0.168) among all 15 compared methods, with a competitive CLIPIQA of 0.427. On URHI, it similarly leads in NIQE (3.505), MUSIQ (64.987), and MANIQA (0.182), while CLIPIQA (0.462) is marginally below HazeFlow (0.469). The method is trained in an unpaired setting yet matches or exceeds fully supervised baselines on most perceptual metrics, demonstrating that the physics-grounded RL framework effectively bridges the synthetic-to-real domain gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest supervised baseline HazeFlow, Dehaze-RL wins on NIQE (3.641 vs 4.745 on RTTS; 3.505 vs 4.295 on URHI), MUSIQ (63.921 vs 63.057; 64.987 vs 63.428), and MANIQA (0.168 vs 0.160; 0.182 vs 0.181), while HazeFlow retains a slight CLIPIQA edge (0.431 vs 0.427 on RTTS; 0.469 vs 0.462 on URHI). Versus the best unpaired baselines, Dehaze-RL dominates comprehensively: it improves NIQE by 0.37 over RefineD (4.012) and 0.44 over PBD (4.043) on RTTS, and lifts MUSIQ by 7.8 points over RefineD (56.117) and 10.7 over PBD (53.194). Against IPCDehaze, the closest supervised competitor on MANIQA (0.167 on RTTS), Dehaze-RL edges ahead (0.168) while substantially outperforming on NIQE (3.641 vs 4.122) and MUSIQ (63.921 vs 59.424). The prior-based DCP baseline is clearly outperformed across all four metrics on both datasets, with NIQE improving from 4.271 to 3.641 on RTTS. Notably, Dehaze-RL is the only method to simultaneously achieve the best NIQE, MUSIQ, and MANIQA on both real-world benchmarks, confirming that the physics-grounded RL formulation avoids the color distortions and hallucinations typical of GAN-based unpaired approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in unpaired dehazing: GAN-based adversarial training (which suffers from instability and hallucination) and RL-over-diffusion approaches (which incur prohibitive iterative sampling costs). It builds on GRPO from the DeepSeek-V3 lineage as its optimization backbone and on the Dark Channel Prior as a physical inductive bias, but the key conceptual departure is reformulating dehazing as a one-shot Contextual Bandit problem with a physics-grounded hybrid action space (transmission, atmospheric light, ISP factors, gating map) rather than a sequential denoising trajectory. This positions the work between classical physics-based restoration and modern RL-for-generation, explicitly rejecting both the abstract latent-noise exploration of diffusion-RL and the generic frequency modulation of IRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, computationally efficient alternative to diffusion-based RL restoration while maintaining physical interpretability, which is valuable for deployment in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. The ablation revealing that removing the structural reward triggers reward hacking—where the policy inflates perceptual scores while catastrophically collapsing structural fidelity—provides a useful cautionary insight for any reward-driven restoration pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other degradation types (rain, fog, motion blur), how sensitive performance is to the VLM reward component's quality, and whether the warmup sensitivity can be eliminated through progressive or curriculum-based initialization.
