## Problem

Computer vision remains fragmented into task-specific models (e.g., separate architectures for depth, segmentation, pose), lacking the unified generalist paradigm that next-token prediction enabled in NLP. The paper asks what pre-training objective could serve as the visual analog to next-token prediction, and argues that large-scale text-to-video generation uniquely satisfies the three requirements of spatiotemporal priors, native vision-language alignment, and scalability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262819/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB video (480×832, 81 frames at 24 FPS) and a text prompt specifying the target modality, producing the output in a single forward pass. All dense tasks (depth, normals, segmentation, DensePose) are unified into 3-channel RGB [0,1] space, while camera pose is encoded as a spatially-arranged 6-channel 'Rothko Raymap' compressed into 3 channels. Sparse tasks (2D/3D keypoints) are handled by appending learnable tokens to the video latents, decoded per-frame by an MLP into K-dimensional coordinates.

## Method

GenCeption repurposes a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model (WAN 2.1) by replacing iterative denoising with a single feed-forward pass: the VAE-encoded video latents are fed directly into the DiT at the final noise-free timestep, and the negated rectified-flow output (−v = x₀ − ε) approximates the target latent. All dense perception tasks share one architecture and one L2 loss in latent space, with task selection steered entirely by the text prompt rather than architectural changes. Sparse tasks are enabled by appending T learnable tokens (one per frame) to the latent sequence, decoded by a lightweight MLP. The model is fine-tuned on 7,500 synthetic human-centric videos (RenderPeople assets animated with CMU mocap, rendered in Blender) plus public synthetic datasets, using a unified data-mixture strategy that replaces task-specific loss engineering with data-format design (e.g., depth normalized by scene median and log-mapped to [0,1]).

## Results

GenCeption (Generalist) achieves 29.3 mAE on Sintel normals (beating NormalCrafter's 30.7 and Lotus-2's 30.3), 0.156 AbsRel on Sintel depth, 0.0010 MSE on VideoMatte foreground segmentation, 69.0 J&F on MeViS expression-referring segmentation (surpassing ReferEverything's 60.3 and SAM3+Gemini's 57.5), and 71.8 MPJE on EMDB 3D keypoints (beating Genmo's 73.0 and GVHMR's 72.6). The Specialist variant further improves to 10.99 mAE on HiD normals (vs. Sapiens' 12.14) and 0.008 AbsRel on Goliath depth (vs. VGGT-Ω's 0.057). Notably, the model achieves these results using only 7,500 synthetic training videos, demonstrating 7×–500× data efficiency compared to D4RT and VGGT-Ω.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On surface normals, GenCeption outperforms all diffusion-based baselines (NormalCrafter 30.7, Lotus-2 30.3, Marigold 33.5, Diception 37.9 mAE on Sintel) and beats the strongest specialist Sapiens (12.14) and DAViD (15.37) on HiD by 1.2–4.4 mAE. For depth estimation, the Specialist variant surpasses D4RT (0.148) and VGGT-Ω (0.145) on Sintel (0.130 AbsRel) and dramatically outperforms all on Goliath (0.008 vs. 0.057–0.065), though it trails DepthAnything 3 (0.028) and VGGT-Ω (0.041) on KITTI. On expression-referring segmentation, GenCeption achieves 70.0 J&F on MeViS, exceeding ReferEverything (60.3), SAM3+Gemini (57.5), and Vocap (51.9) by 9.7–18.1 points, and 76.4 J&F on Ref-DAVIS, edging out Vocap (75.1). For 3D keypoints, the Specialist variant achieves 71.8 MPJE on EMDB, beating Genmo (73.0) and GVHMR (72.6) but trailing TRAM (74.4). In the pretraining ablation (Table 2), the WAN 2.1 generative backbone (1.3B, 0.122 avg AbsRel) outperforms VideoMAE V2-G (1B, 0.154) and V-JEPA-H (0.6B, 0.281) under identical fine-tuning data, confirming the generative objective's superiority for spatiotemporal representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work repurposing diffusion models for perception (Marigold, Diception, ReferEverything) from single-image to native video and from iterative sampling to a single feed-forward pass. It builds directly on the WAN 2.1 video diffusion backbone and the authors' prior human-centric model THFM, but generalizes both to a task-agnostic architecture handling dense and sparse perception simultaneously. Conceptually, it positions large-scale text-to-video generation as the visual analog of next-token prediction in NLP, arguing that the generative objective inherently encodes the spatiotemporal priors, vision-language alignment, and scale needed for generalist vision—departing from contrastive (CLIP) and masked-autoencoder (VideoMAE, V-JEPA) pre-training paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the core hypothesis holds—that video generation pre-training is a sufficient universal representation for perception—this could reshape how the field approaches multi-task video understanding, collapsing many specialized models into one. The 7×–500× data efficiency relative to D4RT and VGGT-Ω is practically significant for groups without massive annotation budgets. Key open questions remain: joint training currently degrades sparse tasks (3D keypoints) due to architectural disruption of pre-trained DiT layers, the emergent sim-to-real and OOD generalization results are primarily qualitative, and the paradigm has been validated on a single backbone (WAN 2.1) with predominantly synthetic training data, leaving unclear how well it scales to truly open-world, multi-category video perception.
