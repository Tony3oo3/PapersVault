## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting frameworks are tightly coupled to camera-specific projection functions and rasterizers, causing inconsistent solid-angle sampling and degraded cross-camera generalization. Equirectangular intermediate representations suffer from severe polar oversampling (up to 7.6× pixel area variation), leading to biased gradient propagation. No prior method provides a single, camera-agnostic rasterization pipeline that handles arbitrary FoV configurations from narrow perspective to full 360° omnidirectional with uniform optimization behavior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view calibrated RGB images with known intrinsics and extrinsics, supporting arbitrary FoV ranges from ~52°×36° (perspective) through 120°–170° (fisheye) to 360°×180° (omnidirectional). The HEALPix grid resolution is adaptively matched to the input's local angular resolution, and ground-truth images are resampled onto this spherical grid for unified supervision. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation that can be rendered to any target camera model (perspective, fisheye, or omnidirectional) by simply changing the image-to-sphere mapping and FoV mask, without retraining.

## Method

UniTriSplat reformulates 3DGS rasterization directly on a HEALPix equal-area spherical tessellation, decoupling rendering from any specific camera projection. Gaussians are projected onto the unit sphere in radian coordinates, with a latitude-dependent scaling matrix S_φ = diag(cos φ, 1) converting angular covariance to arc-length covariance to ensure uniform solid-angle coverage. The backward pass derives additional gradient paths through S_φ that are unique to spherical projection, and two tile-query strategies (NESTED quadtree DFS and RING sequential scan) exploit HEALPix's hierarchical indexing for efficient culling. A HEALPix-aware SSIM loss (HSSIM) computes structural similarity over spherical neighborhoods using zone-specific anisotropic kernels at the poles, and density control thresholds operate in radian space rather than pixel counts. The rendered HEALPix sphere is then resampled to the target image plane via the camera's projection mapping, enabling a single trained scene to synthesize views under any camera model.

## Results

On fisheye inputs, UniTriSplat achieves 29.75 dB PSNR on ScanNet++ (127°×85°) and 24.27 dB on FIORD (120°–170°), outperforming the best baseline by 0.6 dB and ~3 dB respectively. On omnidirectional 360Roam, UniTriSplat leads all baselines with 21.82 dB PSNR, 0.747 SSIM, and 0.353 LPIPS while training in 102 min (vs. 248–331 min for ODGS/OP43DGS). On perspective Mip-NeRF 360, UniTriSplat trails OP43DGS by 0.46 dB PSNR but achieves the best HSSIM (0.806 vs. 0.741), indicating superior structural consistency on the spherical grid. Across all six datasets, UniTriSplat consistently attains the highest HEALPix-SSIM score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023), UniTriSplat improves PSNR by ~2 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (27.57 vs. 25.59) and achieves substantially better HSSIM (0.806 vs. 0.726), though OP43DGS still leads on raw PSNR/SSIM for perspective. Against OP43DGS (Huang et al., ECCV 2024), UniTriSplat wins decisively on fisheye FIORD (+2.6 dB PSNR) and omnidirectional 360Roam (+0.8 dB PSNR, −0.023 LPIPS) while matching or slightly trailing on perspective and OmniBlender; OP43DGS retains an edge on OmniBlender PSNR (32.01 vs. 32.57) but UniTriSplat surpasses it on HSSIM (0.900 vs. 0.865). Against Fisheye-GS (Liao et al., 2024), UniTriSplat outperforms on both fisheye datasets: +0.6 dB PSNR on ScanNet++ and +3.1 dB on the ultra-wide FIORD, confirming that spherical rasterization handles severe distortion more robustly than tangent-plane warping. Against ODGS (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2024), UniTriSplat leads on all three omnidirectional benchmarks in PSNR and LPIPS, with the largest margin on 360Roam (+1.1 dB). Against OmniGS (Li et al., WACV 2025), UniTriSplat trails by 0.6–0.7 dB PSNR on Ricoh360 and OmniBlender but recovers the gap on HSSIM (0.863 vs. 0.843; 0.900 vs. 0.891) and wins outright on 360Roam, while also training ~1.5× faster than ODGS/OP43DGS on that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting [23] by replacing camera-specific tile-based rasterization with a unified HEALPix spherical grid, building on the equal-area tessellation concept from cosmology [11] and prior spherical representation work (Yin-Yang grids [22], cubemaps [12]). It departs from the dominant equirectangular-projection approach used by OmniGS [28], ODGS [25], and OP43DGS [19], which all suffer from non-uniform pixel density and require per-camera Jacobians, by performing rasterization directly in radian space on the sphere. The HEALPix-SSIM loss and radian-space density control are novel adaptations of standard 3DGS training components to the non-planar spherical topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified HEALPix rasterizer eliminates the fragmented, camera-specific implementations that currently plague wide-FoV 3DGS, offering a single pipeline for perspective, fisheye, and omnidirectional cameras — a practical need for VR, robotics, and urban digital twins. The consistent HSSIM superiority across all datasets and the large cross-camera PSNR gaps (e.g., 29.01 vs 16.30 on Ricoh360 perspective) demonstrate that decoupling optimization from a specific projection genuinely improves geometric consistency. Open questions include aliasing artifacts when resampling from HEALPix to planar images at lower resolutions, the EWA approximation error under extreme distortion, and whether the approach scales efficiently to very high-resolution or unbounded outdoor scenes where sky-region artifacts persist.
