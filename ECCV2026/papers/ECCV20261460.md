## Problem

Existing feedforward 3D reconstruction methods assume photometric consistency across input views and fail when in-the-wild images exhibit varying lighting, weather, or exposure, producing appearance-inconsistent renderings. Optimization-based in-the-wild methods (NeRF-W, WildGaussians) can model appearance variation but require dense views, accurate precomputed poses, and lengthy per-scene optimization, making them impractical for sparse inputs. No prior feedforward framework can decouple geometry from appearance to produce appearance-conditioned novel views from unposed, photometrically inconsistent images in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261460/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of unposed in-the-wild context images (4, 8, or 12 views) with arbitrary photometric variation, plus a single designated reference image that specifies the target appearance. It jointly predicts camera poses for all input views, appearance-invariant 3D Gaussian geometry (positions, rotations, scales, opacities), and reference-conditioned spherical harmonic color coefficients. At evaluation, the reference image is split: the left half provides appearance conditioning while metrics are computed on the right half to prevent photometric leakage. The rendered output is a novel view that geometrically matches the context scene while strictly adopting the reference image's color tone and lighting.

## Method

WildSplat employs a dual-branch architecture that explicitly decouples geometry from appearance. The geometry branch uses a VGGT-like backbone (DINOv2-large encoder with 24 alternating attention layers) to extract appearance-invariant content features, regress 3D Gaussian attributes via DPT heads, and predict camera poses from learnable camera tokens. The appearance branch encodes the reference image with a DINOv2-base encoder and injects target appearance into content features via a globally pre-modulated cross-attention mechanism: the reference [CLS] token is decoded through AdaLN-Zero into scale, shift, and gating parameters that holistically shift content features into the target appearance domain before cross-attention aggregates fine-grained details from reference patch tokens. A joint multi-reference training strategy renders the same geometry under M randomly sampled appearance references per batch, with color jittering on inputs and random cropping on references to prevent appearance leakage and enforce geometry-appearance disentanglement. Geometry-guided view sampling leverages SfM point overlap and depth-ratio thresholds to select context views with reliable spatial correspondence, addressing the extreme viewpoint and focal-length variation in unconstrained photo collections.

## Results

On MegaScenes (10 scenes), WildSplat achieves PSNR 19.20 / SSIM 0.683 / LPIPS 0.210 with 12 input views, outperforming the best feedforward baseline AnySplat (16.31 / 0.601 / 0.273) by ~2.9 dB in PSNR. On the more challenging Phototourism dataset (4 scenes), the gap widens to PSNR 21.47 / SSIM 0.788 / LPIPS 0.137 at 12 views versus AnySplat's 18.00 / 0.700 / 0.228. Optimization-based methods (FSGS, GS-W, WildGaussian) consistently underperform, with GS-W scoring as low as 9.81 PSNR on MegaScenes at 12 views due to under-constrained per-scene optimization from sparse inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildSplat outperforms all five baselines across every metric and view count on both MegaScenes and Phototourism. Against the strongest feedforward competitor AnySplat [11], WildSplat gains ~2.9 dB PSNR on MegaScenes (19.20 vs. 16.31 at 12 views) and ~3.5 dB on Phototourism (21.47 vs. 18.00), with LPIPS reduced from 0.273 to 0.210 and 0.228 to 0.137 respectively. WorldMirror [19] is competitive at 4 views (15.33 PSNR on MegaScenes) but plateaus or even degrades at 12 views (16.05), whereas WildSplat improves monotonically with more inputs. Optimization-based methods fare worst: GS-W [41] scores only 9.81 PSNR on MegaScenes at 12 views, and WildGaussian [13] (15.68) and FSGS [43] (13.43) remain well below the feedforward baselines, confirming that per-scene optimization is severely under-constrained from sparse in-the-wild inputs. For pose estimation (Table 4), WildSplat's geometry branch matches VGGT [32] closely (AUC@5: 0.310 vs. 0.314; ATE: 1.559 vs. 1.525) and surpasses AnySplat (AUC@5: 0.249), validating that the jointly predicted poses are reliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

WildSplat sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting (AnySplat, pixelSplat, MVSplat) and in-the-wild appearance-aware reconstruction (NeRF-W, WildGaussians, GS-W). It builds directly on VGGT as its geometry backbone and adopts AnySplat's pose distillation loss, but departs fundamentally from both families by introducing an explicit dual-branch decoupling of geometry and appearance. Prior feedforward methods assume photometric consistency across inputs, while prior in-the-wild methods require per-scene optimization and accurate SfM poses; WildSplat is the first to combine feedforward 3DGS with appearance-conditioned rendering for unposed, photometrically inconsistent collections, using a cross-attention-based appearance injector with AdaLN-Zero pre-modulation as the key architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating per-scene optimization while handling photometric inconsistency, WildSplat makes appearance-conditioned novel view synthesis from sparse in-the-wild photos practical in a single forward pass, opening the door to real-time scene style transfer and appearance editing without lengthy optimization. The clean geometry-appearance decoupling via cross-attention is a reusable architectural pattern that could transfer to other 3D reconstruction settings with varying illumination. However, evaluation is limited to 4 Phototourism and 10 MegaScenes scenes, and the demonstrated appearance transfer is primarily color-tone level; whether the approach generalizes to more extreme variations (e.g., seasonal changes, structural occlusions, or dynamic elements) remains an open question. The reliance on a designated reference image also constrains the method's flexibility compared to fully generative appearance control.
