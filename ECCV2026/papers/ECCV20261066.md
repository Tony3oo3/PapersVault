## Problem

Existing VLM-based IQA methods generate quality assessments in a single non-interactive pass, confining reasoning to the text domain without any mechanism to iteratively inspect specific image regions. This lack of region-aware visual grounding leads to unreliable reasoning—models either miss subtle degradations or make factually incorrect claims—because they cannot emulate the human 'zoom-in' behavior that is critical for holistic quality judgment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261066/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a structured chain-of-thought prompt as input, and produces a two-part structured output: a  block containing a four-part rationale (Image Quality Summary, Directions for Improvement, Issues to Avoid, Decision & Rationale) and an <answer> block with a JSON tool decision ('final' or 'crop'), a numerical quality rating, and conditionally a 2D bounding box specifying the region to crop. When the tool is 'crop', the model iterates by receiving the cropped sub-region as a new visual input and re-evaluating, enabling multi-turn refinement before committing to a final score.

## Method

Zoom-IQA is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7b and trained in two stages. Stage 1 (SFT) teaches the model *how* to execute the crop action using a curated GR-IQA dataset (~7,000 trajectories) generated by Gemini-2.5-pro and filtered through Visual Reliance Filtering (measuring output shift with vs. without the image) and Hint-Augmented Consistency Filtering (LLM-based sample-level hallucination checks). Stage 2 (GRPO-based RL) teaches the model *when* to zoom via a composite reward combining format compliance, Gaussian score accuracy, and Thurstone-model-based rank consistency. A KL-Coverage regularizer suppresses entropy collapse specifically on high-covariance numerical tokens in the answer block to prevent score diversity collapse, while a Progressive Re-sampling Strategy gradually up-weights under-represented score intervals to handle the long-tailed MOS distribution.

## Results

Zoom-IQA achieves PLCC of 0.938 (KonIQ), 0.902 (SPAQ), 0.701 (KADID), 0.468 (PIPAL), 0.887 (LIVE-Wild), 0.816 (AGIQA), and 0.797 (CSIQ), outperforming the strongest reasoning-based VLM baselines (Q-Insight, VisualQuality-R1) on 5 of 7 datasets. For description quality evaluated by Gemini-2.5-Flash, Zoom-IQA scores 8.72/8.80/8.30/8.60 (Accuracy/Reasonableness/Completeness/Confidence) on KonIQ, substantially exceeding Q-Insight (7.17/7.44/7.08/6.93) and VisualQuality-R1 (7.29/7.60/7.29/7.57). In downstream restoration, Zoom-IQA-guided SUPIR achieves the best MANIQA (0.5455) and CLIPIQA (0.6773) on DIV2K, while Zoom-IQA-guided DreamClear attains the best NIQE (3.914) and MUSIQ (66.97) on RealLQ250.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two strongest reasoning-based VLM baselines, Zoom-IQA wins on 5 of 7 datasets in PLCC: it beats Q-Insight by +0.020 on KonIQ, +0.017 on LIVE-Wild, and a large +0.112 on CSIQ, while beating VisualQuality-R1 by +0.028 on KonIQ, +0.031 on LIVE-Wild, and +0.029 on CSIQ; both baselines edge out Zoom-IQA by ≤0.002 on KADID and AGIQA. Compared to the score-only (non-reasoning) VLM DeQA-Score, Zoom-IQA trades a small KonIQ PLCC gap (0.938 vs 0.953) for large gains on CSIQ (+0.010) and AGIQA (+0.007), while also providing textual rationales that DeQA-Score cannot. Against non-VLM deep baselines (MUSIQ, ManIQA, HyperIQA), Zoom-IQA leads on every dataset by 0.01–0.11 PLCC. In the description-quality evaluation (Table 2), Zoom-IQA outperforms all three reasoning/description baselines (DepictQA, Q-Insight, VisualQuality-R1) by 1.4–3.5 points on every metric under both Gemini-2.5-Flash and GPT-5-mini judges. For restoration guidance (Table 3), Zoom-IQA achieves the best perceptual metrics (MANIQA 0.5455, CLIPIQA 0.6773 on DIV2K; NIQE 3.914, MUSIQ 66.97 on RealLQ250) despite a slightly lower PSNR than Q-Insight on DIV2K, indicating its reasoning produces more perceptually meaningful restoration prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VLM-based IQA methods (Q-Align, DeQA-Score, Q-Insight, VisualQuality-R1) that use RL to unify scoring and textual reasoning, but departs from them by introducing iterative, region-aware visual interaction (cropping and zooming) that was previously confined to VQA tasks like Chain-of-Focus and DeepEyes. The key conceptual leap is treating IQA as a dynamic, uncertainty-driven process rather than a single-pass text generation task, motivated by the observation that human assessors zoom into specific regions. The GR-IQA dataset and the two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline are novel contributions that bridge the gap between static distortion-mask grounding (Q-Ground, Grounding-IQA) and the dynamic reasoning path needed for holistic quality judgment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As IQA models increasingly serve as perceptual reward signals in RLHF pipelines and text-guided restoration frameworks, the reliability and interpretability of their reasoning become critical; Zoom-IQA's region-grounded CoT provides verifiable evidence for quality judgments rather than opaque text. The downstream restoration experiments (SUPIR, DreamClear) demonstrate practical transferability, though the PSNR results are mixed, suggesting that reasoning quality does not always translate to pixel-level fidelity. Open questions include whether the iterative zooming policy generalizes to video or multi-image assessment, the computational overhead of multi-turn inference in deployment, and whether the KL-Coverage regularizer's token-level covariance targeting extends to other RL-for-LLM settings beyond IQA.
