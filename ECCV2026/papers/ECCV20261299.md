## Problem

Diffusion vision language models (dVLMs) lag significantly behind autoregressive VLMs on standard multimodal benchmarks because they are built on weaker base diffusion language models (dLLMs) that are less capable and less mature than their AR counterparts. This raises the question of whether high-performing dVLMs can be constructed directly from existing powerful AR models without relying on dLLMs, and whether the paradigm shift from AR to diffusion is itself sufficient to unlock parallel decoding benefits.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (processed through a SigLip2-400M vision encoder and a two-layer MLP projector into visual tokens) concatenated with a text prompt, and produces an open-ended generated text sequence. Sequences are padded with EOS tokens to lengths divisible by the block size (default D=8), and generation proceeds block-by-block until an EOS token is emitted, supporting arbitrary-length outputs. At inference, the model uses static or dynamic low-confidence remasking to control how many tokens are unmasked per denoising step, trading off speed against quality.

## Method

DiffusionVL translates any pretrained AR model into a dVLM via 'diffusion finetuning,' which changes only the training objective (from next-token prediction to masked-position cross-entropy under block diffusion) and the decoding process while keeping the Transformer backbone architecture unchanged. For AR-VLMs, this is a single-stage full-parameter paradigm shift; for AR-LMs, a two-stage pipeline first trains a randomly initialized connector under the AR objective to align vision and text embedding spaces, then applies block-diffusion finetuning end-to-end. Training uses a block-wise noise schedule where each response block independently samples a noise level t~U(0,1), with an attention mask enforcing intra-block bidirectional and inter-block causal attention between the noised and clean sequence copies. At inference, block decoding combines inter-block autoregressive generation with intra-block parallel diffusion denoising (up to S steps per block), enabling KV-cache reuse across blocks and arbitrary-length generation—addressing two key limitations of prior full-diffusion dVLMs.

## Results

DiffusionVL-7B (trained on only 738K samples, <5% of prior dVLMs) achieves 83.5 on MMBench, 49.3 on MMMU, 36.9 on MMMU-Pro (std.), 25.0 on MMMU-Pro (vision), 63.2 on MMStar, and 675 on MME (cog.), setting new SOTA among diffusion VLMs. This represents a 34.4% relative gain on MMMU-Pro (vision) and 37.5% gain on MME (cog.) over the strongest prior dVLM (LLaDA-V-8B). On inference, DiffusionVL achieves a 2.0× speedup over LLaDA-V on DetailCaps captioning (BERTScore 30.9 vs. 15.3 at matched parallelism) and 1.7× faster math reasoning (39.47 TPS vs. 23.07 TPS on MathVista with CoT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest prior diffusion VLM, LLaDA-V-8B (16.5M samples), DiffusionVL-7B wins on every reported benchmark: MMBench 83.5 vs. 82.9, MMMU 49.3 vs. 48.6, MMMU-Pro (std.) 36.9 vs. 35.2, MMMU-Pro (vision) 25.0 vs. 18.6, MMStar 63.2 vs. 60.1, and MME (cog.) 675 vs. 491, while using only 4.5% of the training data. Against LaViDa-L-8B and Dimple-7B, DiffusionVL-3B (the smaller model) already surpasses both on MMBench (80.1 vs. 70.5/–), MMMU (47.2 vs. 43.3/45.2), and MME (cog.) (594 vs. 341/432). Compared to the AR-VLM baseline Qwen2.5VL-7B, DiffusionVL-7B matches MMBench (83.5) and slightly exceeds MMMU-Pro (std.) (36.9 vs. 36.7) and MME (cog.) (675 vs. 646), but trails on MMMU (49.3 vs. 51.1), MMMU-Pro (vision) (25.0 vs. 33.4), and MME (perp.) (1519 vs. 1680), confirming that the paradigm shift preserves most capability with a modest gap on perception-heavy tasks. Against the classic LLaVA-1.5 AR baseline, DiffusionVL-7B leads by a wide margin on MMBench (83.5 vs. 64.3) and MME (perp.) (1519 vs. 1510), while also reporting scores on benchmarks LLaVA-1.5 does not cover.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-domain finding that AR models can be finetuned into block-diffusion language models (SDAR, Fast-dLLM-V2) to the vision-language setting, and builds on the block decoding formulation of Arriola et al. (2025) for KV-cache reuse and arbitrary-length generation. It departs from prior dVLMs (LaViDa, Dimple, LLaDA-V), which all require a pre-trained dLLM as the language backbone, by demonstrating that the same Transformer architecture can be repurposed from an AR-VLM or AR-LM through a simple change in training objective and attention pattern. The two-stage connector-then-diffusion pipeline for AR-LM conversion follows the LLaVA recipe, but replaces the standard AR finetuning stage with block-diffusion finetuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe—diffusion finetuning an existing AR-VLM with <5% of prior dVLM training data—lowers the barrier to building competitive dVLMs and makes the parallel-decoding efficiency gains (2× speedup) accessible without investing in a separate dLLM pretraining run. A key open question is whether the remaining gap to top AR-VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5VL-7B) can be closed with longer, higher-quality visual finetuning, or whether the diffusion paradigm imposes a fundamental ceiling. The authors also flag whether the AR-to-diffusion conversion extends beyond finetuning to pretraining and reinforcement learning, which could unlock further efficiency gains in long-CoT and RL settings.
