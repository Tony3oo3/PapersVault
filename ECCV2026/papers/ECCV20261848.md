## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting methods predominantly rely on ad-hoc pixel-level losses (L1+SSIM) that produce overly smooth or blurry renderings, despite the output being consumed by human viewers. Perceptual optimization strategies for 3DGS have not been systematically explored, and no prior work has validated perceptual quality improvements through large-scale human preference studies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261848/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images of a scene as input and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (a collection of 3D Gaussian primitives parameterized by center, covariance, color, and opacity) that is differentially rendered to novel views. The distortion loss is computed entirely in 2D rendered image space, and the representation budget (splat count or model size) is controlled via a global scaling factor γ on the loss, ensuring fair comparison across methods without modifying the underlying densification or pruning logic.

## Method

The core contribution is a drop-in replacement of the 2D distortion loss in 3DGS optimization with Wasserstein Distortion (WD), which compares spatially local statistics (mean and standard deviation) in VGG feature space rather than pointwise pixel differences, allowing textures with large pointwise shifts but matching local statistics to be treated as perceptually similar. The best-performing variant, WD-R, augments WD with a lightly weighted original 3DGS loss (L1+SSIM) as a mild pixel-fidelity regularizer that suppresses web-like artifacts in low-splat-budget regions without inflating the splat count. No changes are made to the 3DGS framework's initialization, adaptive densification, pruning, or rendering pipeline—the perceptual modeling is delegated entirely to the loss function. The paper validates this approach through the first-of-its-kind large-scale human preference study for 3DGS (39,320 pairwise ratings across 4 datasets and 3 frameworks) and demonstrates generalization to Mip-Splatting, Scaffold-GS, and entropy-constrained scene compression (Comp-GS), where WD-R yields ~50% bitrate savings at comparable perceptual quality.

## Results

WD-R achieves state-of-the-art perceptual metrics across all four datasets. On BungeeNeRF (outdoors), WD-R reaches LPIPS 0.092, DISTS 0.107, FID 46.21, and CMMD 0.171 with only 4.89M splats, compared to 6.92M for the original 3DGS loss (LPIPS 0.098, FID 62.23). In the large-scale human preference study (39,320 pairwise ratings, 428 participants), WD-R is preferred 2.3× over the original 3DGS loss and 1.5× over Perceptual-GS. When integrated into Mip-Splatting and Scaffold-GS, WD-R is preferred 1.8× and 3.6× respectively, and in the Comp-GS compression setting it yields ≈50% bitrate savings at comparable perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original 3DGS loss (L1+SSIM) [18], WD-R wins on every perceptual metric across all datasets while using fewer or comparable splats (e.g., 4.89M vs. 6.92M on BungeeNeRF, 3.47M vs. 4.52M on Mip-NeRF 360 outdoors). Against Perceptual-GS [41], the prior state-of-the-art splat-efficient perceptual method, WD-R achieves lower LPIPS and DISTS on Mip-NeRF 360 (indoors: 0.147 vs. 0.170; outdoors: 0.206 vs. 0.206) and dramatically lower FID on BungeeNeRF (46.21 vs. 101.66), while using far fewer splats (4.89M vs. 11.30M). Against Pixel-GS [40], WD-R matches or beats LPIPS on all datasets with substantially fewer splats (e.g., 3.47M vs. 7.40M on Mip-NeRF 360 outdoors). Against the composite loss (L1+L2+MS-SSIM+LPIPS), WD-R dominates on FID and DISTS across all settings. When integrated into Mip-Splatting [37], WD-R improves FID from 62.54 to 45.87 (outdoors) at similar splat count; in Scaffold-GS [24], it reduces FID from 133.33 to 67.74 (outdoors) at comparable model size. In the Comp-GS [23] compression setting, WD-R achieves ≈50% bitrate savings at equivalent LPIPS/DISTS/FID levels compared to the original rate-distortion objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against prior perceptual 3DGS work (Pixel-GS, Perceptual-GS, EGGS) that couples loss changes with modifications to densification or pruning heuristics. In contrast, it isolates the distortion loss as the sole design variable, drawing on Wasserstein Distortion from the learned image compression literature (Ballé et al. 2025; Qiu et al. 2024) and adapting it to 3DGS via a light pixel-fidelity regularizer (WD-R). The approach is deliberately orthogonal to architectural innovations like Mip-Splatting's anti-aliasing or Scaffold-GS's structured parameterization, treating perceptual modeling as a separable concern from the 3DGS algorithm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first large-scale human preference study for 3DGS (39,320 pairwise ratings) provides ground-truth validation that loss-only changes produce perceptually meaningful gains, closing a gap where prior work relied on proxy metrics. The ~50% bitrate savings in compression and consistent improvements across Mip-Splatting, Scaffold-GS, and Comp-GS suggest WD-R is a broadly adoptable drop-in for practitioners. Open questions include why WD alone (stable in 2D compression) produces web-like artifacts in 3D scene optimization under constrained splat budgets, and whether spatially varying pooling kernels or adversarial losses can further close the gap without the computational overhead.
