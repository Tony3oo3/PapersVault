## Problem

Existing causal mediation analysis methods operate on single-modal data with predefined pathway structures, while multimodal learning methods optimize reconstruction without causal objectives, leaving cross-modal mediation pathways (e.g., sleep → retinal imaging → cardiovascular outcomes) both theoretically unidentified and practically undiscoverable. The nonparametric identification of natural direct and indirect effects from heterogeneous multimodal observational data under structural uncertainty remains an open problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260343/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous multimodal data: structured variables spanning sleep (AHI, sleep efficiency, arousal index), body composition, cardiovascular (BP, HRV, carotid IMT), and inflammatory/metabolic markers, alongside non-mydriatic fundus images (12 vessel indicators and structural parameters extracted via deep learning), plus covariates (age, sex, BMI, smoking). It produces identified cross-modal mediation pathways with quantified CM-NDE and CM-NIE estimates, each accompanied by confidence intervals that decompose into within-structure estimation variance and between-structure (DAG ensemble) variance, along with a posterior DAG ensemble and per-path stability scores.

## Method

MM-SIGMA is an end-to-end framework with four stages: (1) a multimodal VAE with variable-level encoders for structured data and a CNN for images, trained with a causal constraint loss enforcing domain-grounded conditional independencies and a cross-modal alignment loss, producing a causality-preserving latent space; (2) a differentiable Flow-SEM that learns a continuous weight matrix with an acyclicity constraint and L1 sparsity, from which a posterior DAG ensemble is sampled via Bernoulli edge inclusion with greedy cycle removal; (3) a Cross-Modal Path Stability Score (CM-PSS) that computes the marginal posterior probability of each directed A→Y path across the DAG ensemble, retaining paths above a threshold τ and mapping them to original variables via a one-to-one latent-to-structured-variable correspondence; and (4) extended efficient influence functions (for 3-node paths) or interventional plug-in estimators (for longer paths) for pathway-specific effect estimation, aggregated via Bayesian model averaging with uniform weights to propagate both estimation and structural uncertainty into final confidence intervals. The theoretical foundation is a nonparametric identification theorem for CM-NDE and CM-NIE under a Latent Sufficiency condition that replaces the raw image mediator with its variational posterior representation.

## Results

On synthetic multimodal data, MM-SIGMA achieves 0.487 Adj F1, 0.421 Ori F1, 0.398 CM-Edge F1, and 0.345 causal accuracy in structure discovery, outperforming the best baseline (MM-DECI) by 18.2% on Adj F1 and 21.0% on Ori F1. End-to-end pathway recovery reaches 0.74 with NDE/NIE bias of 0.012/0.015 and 95% CI coverage of 0.94. On the HPP dataset, MM-SIGMA identifies the sleep HRV→PWV→carotid IMT pathway (CM-NIE: −0.156±0.034) and two novel cross-modal pathways (retinal features→body fat→BP→IMT; sleep→gut microbiome→metabolism→CVD) that no baseline can detect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-SIGMA outperforms all four baseline families across every metric. Against MM-NOTEARS (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2018), MM-SIGMA gains +36.9% on CM-Edge F1 (0.398 vs. 0.278) and +35.3% on causal accuracy (0.345 vs. 0.201), confirming that preserving modal structure in the latent space is critical over naive concatenation. Against MM-PC (Spirtes et al., 2001), the improvement is even larger: +42.4% on Adj F1 and +121% on causal accuracy, reflecting the advantage of differentiable Flow-SEM over constraint-based search in high-dimensional multimodal settings. Against CausalMixNet (Zhang et al., 2025), MM-SIGMA leads by +33.5% on CM-Edge F1 and +82.5% on causal accuracy, and uniquely discovers novel cross-modal pathways on HPP data where CausalMixNet+EIF returns N/A due to its pre-specified pathway requirement. Against the Baron-Kenny baseline (Baron & Kenny, 1986), MM-SIGMA achieves 0.74 pathway recovery versus 0.61 for MM-PC+Baron-Kenny, with substantially lower NDE bias (0.012 vs. 0.031) and higher CI coverage (0.94 vs. 0.90), demonstrating that end-to-end uncertainty propagation via BMA is essential for valid inference under structural uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pearl's causal mediation formula to a cross-modal setting by introducing a Latent Sufficiency condition that replaces raw image mediators with their variational latent representations, and builds on the efficient influence function framework of Tchetgen Tchetgen & Shpitser for pathway-specific effect estimation. For structure learning, it adapts the continuous acyclicity constraint from NOTEARS/Flow-SEM to a latent space, adding a variational edge-inclusion mechanism to capture structural uncertainty. The key departure from prior work is the unification of these components into an end-to-end pipeline: no existing method jointly performs multimodal representation learning, probabilistic causal structure discovery, cross-modal pathway identification, and uncertainty-aware effect estimation, and the nonparametric identification theory for cross-modal NDE/NIE is explicitly stated to be absent from the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification theorem (Theorem 1) fills a genuine theoretical gap by establishing when cross-modal mediation effects are estimable from observational data, which is a prerequisite for any applied work in this space. The HPP validation revealing novel pathways (e.g., retinal features → body fat → BP → carotid IMT) demonstrates practical value for precision medicine, though the single-dataset evaluation and the slightly below-nominal CI coverage (0.94 vs. 0.95) temper confidence. Open questions include whether the Latent Sufficiency assumption holds beyond the 12 pre-extracted vessel indicators, how the framework scales to more than two modalities or temporal dynamics, and whether the uniform-weight BMA approximation remains valid as the DAG ensemble grows.
