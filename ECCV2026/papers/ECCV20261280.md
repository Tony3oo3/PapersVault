## Problem

Most computational pathology foundation models are pre-trained on single-resolution WSIs, limiting their ability to generalize across the arbitrary magnifications (10×, 20×, 40×) that expert pathologists routinely use. While hierarchical models like HIPT capture the nested cell→patch→region→WSI structure, they do not explicitly integrate multi-resolution information within those hierarchies, leaving cross-scale diagnostic cues (e.g., nuclear morphology at 40× contextualized by glandular architecture at 20×) unexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261280/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

MRPT takes a single WSI simultaneously at three aligned resolutions (10×, 20×, 40×) and decomposes it into spatially co-registered triplets: regions of 4096×4096 px at 20× (with 2048×2048 and 8192×8192 counterparts), 256 patches per region, and 256 cell-level tokens per patch. The model outputs a single multi-resolution WSI-level [mCLS] embedding usable for classification, linear probing, or MIL aggregation; MRPT-LLaVA additionally accepts a free-text question and produces a natural-language answer, caption, or full pathology report.

## Method

MRPT is a three-stage hierarchical ViT (mViT_P-C → ViT_R-P → ViT_M-R) in which each stage is pre-trained with DINO-style multi-resolution SSL on progressively larger units (624M patches, 2.4M regions, 36K WSIs from TCGA/CPTAC). The key architectural novelty is the Consecutive Cross-Resolution Attention (CCRA) module, which fuses features only between adjacent magnification pairs (10×↔20×, 20×↔40×) by averaging their [CLS] tokens, appending the averaged token to the finer sequence, and performing a single-vector cross-attention (linear complexity) that back-projects enriched semantics to both branches. This biologically motivated restriction to consecutive pairs mirrors the sequential zooming workflow of pathologists and avoids misalignment between distant scales. The final WSI embedding is formed by concatenating the three resolution-specific [CLS] tokens after K stacked CCRA layers, yielding a compact (<12M parameter) representation that jointly encodes global tissue architecture and fine-grained cellular morphology.

## Results

On WSI-level classification with matched train/test splits (Table 3), MRPT achieves F1 scores of 0.962 (Camelyon16), 0.883 (Camelyon17), 0.946 (TCGA-NSCLC), 0.956 (TCGA-RCC), and 0.964 (TCGA-BRCA), outperforming HIPT (0.933/0.782/0.851/0.861/0.866) and UNI (0.934/0.857/0.908/0.920/0.911) across all five benchmarks. For zero-shot WSI classification, MRPT-LLaVA reaches 0.806 balanced accuracy versus 0.751 for TITAN; for WSI VQA, MRPT-LLaVA achieves 80.77% accuracy versus 72.57% for WSI-LLaVA. On patch-level zero-shot classification across 13 datasets, mViT_P-C-LLaVA averages 83.66% versus 73.60% for the next-best PathGenCLIP, and on report generation (WSI-Bench), MRPT-LLaVA attains BLEU-2 of 0.634 and METEOR of 0.701, surpassing WSI-LLaVA (0.527/0.655) and SlideChat (0.533/0.613).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRPT consistently outperforms the hierarchical baseline HIPT [23] across all five WSI classification datasets in Table 3, with the largest gap on Camelyon17 (0.883 vs. 0.782 F1, +10.1 points) and TCGA-NSCLC (0.946 vs. 0.851, +9.5 points), confirming that multi-resolution CCRA adds substantial value over single-resolution hierarchical SSL. Against patch-level foundation models UNI [24] and GigaPath [93], MRPT's full model leads on Camelyon17 (+2.6 and +8.1 F1) and TCGA-NSCLC (+3.8 and +13.2 F1), while UNI remains competitive on Camelyon16 (0.934 vs. 0.962). In the WSI-level zero-shot setting, MRPT-LLaVA (0.806 BA) surpasses TITAN [29] (0.751) and PRISM [78], both of which rely on single-resolution pre-training. For VQA, MRPT-LLaVA achieves 80.77% on WSI VQA versus 72.57% for WSI-LLaVA [58] and 73.12% on patch-level closed-ended VQA versus 49.48% for PathGen-LLaVA [84] and 48.60% for Quilt-LLaVA [77], representing gains of 8.2 and 23.6 percentage points respectively. On report generation, MRPT-LLaVA leads all seven compared methods (including SlideChat [25] and WSI-LLaVA [58]) on every metric (BLEU-2, BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, METEOR) across both WSI-Bench and HistGen benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRPT extends the hierarchical self-supervised learning paradigm of HIPT (Chen et al., CVPR 2022) by injecting multi-resolution inputs (10×, 20×, 40×) at every level of the hierarchy, rather than operating on a single magnification. The DINO teacher-student SSL framework (Caron et al., ICCV 2021) provides the training objective, while the LLaVA three-stage pipeline (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023) is adapted for the multimodal MRPT-LLaVA variant. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the Consecutive Cross-Resolution Attention (CCRA) mechanism, which restricts cross-resolution interaction to adjacent magnification pairs—mimicking how pathologists sequentially zoom—rather than allowing arbitrary cross-scale attention. This positions MRPT between patch-level multi-resolution models like MR-PLIP (which lack full WSI hierarchy) and single-resolution WSI-level models like GigaPath or TITAN (which lack multi-resolution information).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-resolution hierarchical design is clinically grounded in how pathologists actually examine slides, and the matched-data experiment (Table 3) convincingly shows that gains stem from the architectural design rather than pre-training scale, which is an important methodological contribution. The model's efficiency (<12M parameters) while outperforming much larger foundation models across 34 datasets makes it practically accessible. Open questions include whether the multi-resolution benefit persists on truly out-of-distribution tissue types, whether the consecutive-only attention restriction is optimal versus learned cross-resolution routing, and how the approach scales to even higher magnifications or additional modalities (genomics, radiology) that the authors mention as future work.
