## Problem

Existing audio-driven 3D talking head methods are restricted to single-speaker scenarios (speaking or listening) and cannot model natural bidirectional conversational dynamics. When extended to dyadic interaction, they rely on 3D pseudo-labels from tracking algorithms (e.g., Spectre) that contain severe noise—particularly in lip closure—establishing an artificial performance ceiling that no amount of 3D-only supervision can overcome.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261898/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two equal-length audio streams (agent and conversation partner), a FLAME shape parameter β, a binary speaker indicator (computed offline via TalkNet), a reference image, and a 4×4 camera pose matrix. It produces per-frame 56-dimensional motion parameters (expression, jaw pose, head rotation) that are instantiated as 5023-vertex FLAME meshes, then rendered into 2D video frames via a 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline. The accompanying Real-Dialog dataset provides 50+ hours of temporally aligned 2D-3D dyadic conversation across 500+ identities, with clips of 30 s–2 min to ensure listening–speaking transitions.

## Method

RealDyadic is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (DIM-FMM) fuses dual-speaker audio via a HuBERT encoder and a Transformer with a residual connection that preserves the agent speaker's cues, then a diffusion-based DiT denoising network directly predicts clean 56-dim motion sequences (rather than noise) to enable geometric losses. Stage 2 (MG-Renderer) projects the predicted FLAME meshes into 2D using a meta-Gaussian Splatting renderer composed of sparse template Gaussians and dense UV Gaussians, with DINOv2 appearance features and a StyleUNet refiner for fine texture. The key insight is an alternating two-phase training strategy: after separate pre-training, the stages are jointly optimized so that image-level photometric and VGG perceptual gradients backpropagate through the 3DGS renderer into the 3D motion generator, explicitly correcting the lip-closure and geometric errors inherited from noisy 3D tracking pseudo-labels.

## Results

On the Real-Dialog testset, RealDyadic achieves the best 3D mesh accuracy across all metrics: LVE 1.741, MVE 1.225, MOD 1.096, MTM 4.015, and SLCC 0.791, outperforming the closest dual-speaker baseline DualTalk (LVE 2.083, MVE 1.382, MOD 1.228) by 16.4% in LVE and 11.4% in MVE. On the DualTalk testset, the same pattern holds with LVE 1.894 and MVE 1.182. For 2D image quality, RealDyadic achieves the lowest FID (21.31), highest identity similarity (CSIM 0.832), and best lip-sync confidence (LSE-C 5.382) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealDyadic outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest dual-speaker competitor DualTalk [27], it reduces LVE by 16.4% (1.741 vs. 2.083) and MVE by 11.4% (1.225 vs. 1.382) on Real-Dialog, and improves SLCC from 0.707 to 0.791, indicating substantially better speech-lip intensity correlation. Among single-speaker 3D methods, ARTalk [4] is the strongest baseline (LVE 2.452, MVE 1.521), still trailing RealDyadic by 29% in LVE; DiffPoseTalk [40], CodeTalker [47], and FaceFormer [6] show progressively larger gaps (LVE 2.694, 3.445, 3.276 respectively). For 2D image quality (Table 1), RealDyadic achieves the best FID (21.31 vs. 24.35 for SadTalker [54] and 26.13 for ARTalk [4]), highest identity preservation (CSIM 0.832 vs. 0.723 for SadTalker), and best lip-sync confidence (LSE-C 5.382 vs. 5.136 for SadTalker), while AniTalker [21] lags most significantly (FID 28.67, LSE-C 3.279). The user study (Table 4) further confirms RealDyadic's advantage, scoring highest on temporal coherence (3.7 vs. 2.9 for DualTalk) and contextual appropriateness (3.3 vs. 3.0 for DualTalk) in interaction correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D dyadic talking head framework introduced by DualTalk [27], which was the first to achieve natural speaking/listening transitions in 3D but relied exclusively on noisy 3D pseudo-labels from tracking methods like Spectre. The key architectural departure is the two-stage pipeline: a diffusion-based DiT motion generator (informed by DiffPoseTalk's geometric-loss approach [40]) followed by a 3D Gaussian Splatting renderer that backpropagates 2D photometric gradients to correct 3D motion parameters. The Dual-audio Interaction Module (DIM) with its residual self-audio connection is a new design for fusing overlapping speaker audio without degrading lip-sync fidelity, and the Real-Dialog dataset (50+ hours, 500+ identities) fills a gap in multi-speaker 3D conversational data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2D-to-3D supervision paradigm—using rendered image-level losses to refine 3D motion beyond what pseudo-labels alone permit—offers a general recipe for any 3D generation task bottlenecked by noisy tracking labels, potentially applicable beyond talking heads. The Real-Dialog dataset provides the first large-scale, photometrically-aligned multi-speaker 3D conversational resource, enabling standardized evaluation. Limitations include residual artifacts in non-facial regions, dependence on the FLAME parametric model, and evaluation restricted to two speakers; whether the 2D supervision strategy scales to larger groups or more complex scenes remains open.
