## Problem

Unsupervised physical parameter estimation from video lacks a common real-world benchmark: existing methods evaluate on non-overlapping synthetic data, the sole real-world dataset (Delfys75) covers only single-body dynamics, and no established protocol addresses governing-equation identification. The state-of-the-art latent-space pipeline additionally contains a critical gradient-flow bug in its Euler integrator that silently prevents physics parameters from receiving any gradient updates during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular video clips at 4K (3840×2160) resolution and 60 fps, supplemented by per-frame SAM2 segmentation masks for multi-object setups and a calibration checkerboard for metric-scale recovery. For each clip the system must (i) select the correct governing ODE from a bank of K candidate equations and (ii) estimate its physical parameters without supervision or metadata. The evaluation protocol is strictly train-per-clip with no parameter sharing across videos, and outputs include parameter estimates with MAE, equation-selection labels, gradient-norm identifiability diagnostics, ODE residuals, and extrapolation errors beyond the training horizon.

## Method

IRIS introduces a 240-video, 8-phenomenon benchmark (24 settings × 10 trials) spanning five single-body dynamics and three novel multi-body interaction scenarios, each paired with independently measured ground-truth parameters and 95% uncertainty bounds. The primary baseline is a latent-space pipeline (VAE encoder + discrete-time ODE physics block) whose Euler integrator is corrected to restore gradient flow to physics parameters, then augmented with a multi-step rollout loss (K=5, geometrically decaying weights) that accumulates parameter effects over future horizons to provide denser gradient signal. Four complementary equation-identification strategies are benchmarked: VLM temporal reasoning (GPT-4V, LLaVA-Video-7B, InternVL2-8B), two-stage describe-then-classify prompting, a supervised ResNet-18 video classifier, and path-based oracle labelling. A standardized five-axis evaluation protocol (parameter accuracy, equation selection, identifiability via gradient norms and ODE residuals, robustness, and extrapolation) enables method-agnostic comparison with all results traceable to specific (phenomenon, setting, clip, seed) tuples.

## Results

The headline finding is a critical gradient-flow bug in the state-of-the-art latent-space pipeline (Garcia et al., CVPR 2025): the original Euler integrator omits the acceleration term, yielding physics-parameter gradient norms ≈ 0 and a pendulum-length MAE of 95.07 m on Delfys75. The corrected integrator reduces this to 3.80 m (ΔMAE = −91.27 m) and improves Torricelli drainage (6.07 → 2.91) and LED decay (2.30 → 0.40). On IRIS multi-body scenarios, the multi-step rollout loss (K=5) catastrophically diverges, producing rope-length MAE up to 741.1 m versus 1.1–3.0 m for the one-step baseline. For governing-equation identification, a ResNet-18 CNN achieves 99.30% accuracy on IRIS (240 videos, 8 classes), while the best VLM strategy (describe-then-classify) reaches only 72.92%, with the VLM ranking reversing between Delfys75 and IRIS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the original Garcia et al. (CVPR 2025) latent-space pipeline. The corrected Euler integrator decisively outperforms the original on every parameter where the bug manifests: pendulum length MAE drops from 95.07 m to 3.80 m (pend._45), 50.15 m to 2.93 m (pend._90), and 29.83 m to 0.71 m (pend._150); Torricelli drainage improves by 3.16 and 1.99; LED decay improves by 1.90. The original baseline's near-zero MAE on gravity parameters (dropped ball, free fall) is an artifact of the initialization value g₀ = 9.81 coinciding with ground truth, not genuine learning. The multi-step rollout loss (K=5) improves gravity estimation on Delfys75 (free fall/table: 1.06 → 0.15 m/s²) but degrades slow dynamics (LED 10s: 2.09 → 7.46) and catastrophically fails on all IRIS multi-body scenarios (rope-length MAE > 100 m vs. 1.1–3.0 m one-step). For equation identification, the CNN classifier (99.30% on IRIS, 98.40% on Delfys75) dominates both VLM strategies, but the VLM ranking reverses across benchmarks: temporal reasoning leads on Delfys75 (73.33% vs. 61.11%) while describe-then-classify leads on IRIS (72.92% vs. 65.00%), indicating no single routing strategy generalizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent-space pipeline of Garcia et al. (CVPR 2025), which introduced Delfys75 and a variational-autoencoder + ODE-step architecture for unsupervised physical parameter estimation from video. IRIS departs from that work in three conceptual ways: it generalizes the task from single-body to multi-body interaction dynamics (collisions, coupled pendulums), it fixes a critical gradient-flow bug in the original Euler integrator that silently prevented parameter learning, and it introduces governing-equation identification as a first-class evaluation axis benchmarked across VLM and CNN routing strategies. The broader method lineage traces back to PAIG and NIRPI for the latent-reconstruction training signal, while the equation-identification component draws on VLM prompting and lightweight CNN classification as complementary routing mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IRIS is the first real-world benchmark to include multi-body collision dynamics with independently measured ground-truth parameters, filling a gap that left prior methods evaluated only on synthetic data or single-body systems. The discovery that the state-of-the-art pipeline's Euler integrator severs gradient flow to physics parameters (yielding 95 m pendulum-length errors) demonstrates that rigorous benchmarking can expose fundamental flaws invisible in the original evaluation. The catastrophic divergence of multi-step rollout loss on multi-body scenarios (errors exceeding 100 m) and the non-generalization of VLM routing strategies across benchmarks define concrete open problems: latent ODE stabilization for coupled dynamics, richer contact models beyond a single coupling coefficient, and reliable zero-shot equation identification. Damping identifiability remains poorly resolved across all baselines, and the moderate dataset scale (240 videos, fixed phenomenon set) limits coverage of the full space of physical interactions.
