## Problem

Training-free inpainting methods that leverage off-the-shelf diffusion or flow models produce results whose global structure is inconsistent with the input image, because they never optimize the initial noise sample that largely determines the generated layout. Directly optimizing this noise requires back-propagating through the entire denoising chain, which is computationally infeasible for modern large models, so the structural mismatch has persisted as an unsolved limitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a masked RGB image (1024×1024) and a text prompt, encodes the masked image into the latent space of StableDiffusion 3.5 using a simple nearest-neighbor color fill for the masked region, and outputs a fully inpainted image. The optimization phase runs for approximately 40 iterations (tens of steps) to refine the initial noise, after which the standard BLD-SD3.5 inpainting pipeline (20 denoising steps, CFG=2.0) generates the final result. Total runtime is roughly one minute per image on an RTX 5090, comparable to methods using far fewer function evaluations.

## Method

SONIC optimizes the initial noise sample in two stages. First, it linearizes the entire T-step denoising trajectory as a straight line between the initial noise and the fully denoised output (with the denoised output treated as a stop-gradient constant), yielding a differentiable masked-MSE loss that requires no back-propagation through the denoiser. Second, it performs the optimization in the Fourier (spectral) domain using the Adam optimizer, so that each frequency component is divided by its own second moment—effectively preconditioning the update so that different spatial frequencies converge at their natural rates, which prevents the regional flickering and instability observed with spatial-domain optimization. Gradient updates are spatially constrained to unmasked pixels only, keeping the noise in masked regions on the expected Gaussian manifold and avoiding border artifacts. The entire pipeline is training-free, requires no learned components, and operates purely in latent space.

## Results

On FFHQ, SONIC achieves SSIM 0.857, LPIPS 0.121, and FID 12.88, outperforming the best training-free baseline FLAIR (SSIM 0.829, LPIPS 0.292, FID 19.87) and the trained method BrushNet (SSIM 0.759, LPIPS 0.237, FID 17.95). On DIV2K, SONIC reaches SSIM 0.803, LPIPS 0.115, FID 17.70, again leading all baselines. On BrushBench, SONIC attains SSIM 0.861, LPIPS 0.153, FID 48.07. In a single-blind two-alternative human preference study (N=37 raters), SONIC is preferred in 90% of comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SONIC outperforms all five baselines on SSIM, LPIPS, and FID across all three datasets (FFHQ, DIV2K, BrushBench). Against FLAIR [12], the closest training-free competitor, SONIC reduces LPIPS from 0.292 to 0.121 and FID from 19.87 to 12.88 on FFHQ; FLAIR's high PSNR (24.10) is attributed to blurry patches rather than faithful reconstruction. BLD-SD3.5 [4] (the default diffusers inpainting pipeline) fails to align global structure, yielding FID 25.84 on FFHQ versus SONIC's 12.88. FlowDPS [28] shows the worst FID (48.33–80.54 across datasets), indicating poor perceptual quality despite competitive CLIP scores. The trained method BrushNet [24] hides seams well and scores best on aesthetic metrics (IR, HPS v2, AS) on its own BrushBench dataset, but lags SONIC on structure-sensitive metrics (SSIM 0.740 vs. 0.861, LPIPS 0.199 vs. 0.153, FID 50.08 vs. 48.07), confirming that training on specific mask types does not guarantee generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SONIC sits at the intersection of training-free inverse-problem solvers for diffusion/flow models (BLD, FLAIR, FlowChef, FlowDPS) and the growing recognition that the initial noise sample disproportionately determines output structure. It departs from prior training-free methods that manipulate the denoising trajectory (guidance, posterior sampling) by instead optimizing the initial noise directly, and from prior noise-optimization work (GAN inversion, InitNo, FlowOpt) by replacing costly backpropagation through the denoiser with a linear trajectory approximation. The concurrent FlowOpt [46] also drops Jacobians but lacks the spectral preconditioning that SONIC shows is essential for stable convergence, and the frequency-aware guidance literature (FREEDOM, frequency-guided diffusion) provides the conceptual motivation for the spectral-domain Adam formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free, single-GPU method (~1 min/image) that outperforms both other training-free solvers and a trained inpainting model (BrushNet) on LPIPS and FID across three datasets, without task-specific training or mask-type fine-tuning. The linearization trick and spectral preconditioning are simple, general-purpose components that the authors argue could transfer to super-resolution, deblurring, and video inpainting, though only inpainting is validated here. Open questions include whether the linear approximation degrades for non-rectified-flow architectures, whether the nearest-neighbor latent fill remains sufficient for more complex corruption operators, and whether the method scales to higher-resolution or multi-frame settings without the spatial-constraining hack becoming a bottleneck.
