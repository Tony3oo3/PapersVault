## Problem

Existing hyperbolic vision-language models suffer from entailment loss instability: the cone aperture is inversely coupled to the parent embedding norm, so as parents contract toward the origin the cone degenerates into a half-space, destroying the intended hierarchy and causing catastrophic training collapse. Additionally, hierarchical evaluation of these models remains unreliable, relying on retrieval-based and correlation-based metrics that are sensitive to ambiguous negatives and taxonomy dependence, failing to measure true hierarchical generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images (16×16 patch size) and text captions (up to 77 tokens), producing dual-encoder embeddings projected onto the Lorentz hyperbolic manifold via the exponential map at the origin. Beyond standard zero-shot class labels and retrieval rankings, the model outputs an entailment probability score p_Ent derived from the exterior angle between embeddings, enabling probabilistic hierarchical assessment. The key structural difference from Euclidean VLMs is that semantic generality is encoded radially (closer to origin = more general) while specificity is encoded angularly, with the adaptive loss operating directly on these angular relationships.

## Method

ARGENT replaces the unstable entailment cone loss with an Adaptive Entailment (AdaEnt) Loss that directly minimizes the exterior angle φ(x,y) via a Huber loss, eliminating the problematic norm-to-aperture coupling that causes cone collapse. An adaptive weight h(x,y) = 1 − exp(−d_L(x,y)) is applied to intra-modality pairs (e.g., image crop vs. full image) to prevent the loss from pushing near-identical representations apart, while inter-modality pairs use h=1. A norm regularizer L_Reg = ‖x̃‖² − log(‖x̃‖) counteracts the embedding drift away from the origin that the adaptive loss introduces, keeping norms in a stable range. The total training objective is L = L_Cont + γ₁·L_AdaEnt + γ₂·L_Reg, trained on 20.5M GRIT pairs with S/B/L model sizes. For evaluation, the paper introduces the Probabilistic Entailment Protocol (PEP), which maps the angle between embeddings to an entailment probability and scores hierarchical understanding via AUC-ROC and Average Precision over intra- and inter-sample pairs, replacing brittle retrieval-based metrics.

## Results

On the Large model, ARGENT achieves 45.6% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet and 45.1% average across 16 classification datasets, outperforming HyCoCLIP-L (43.9%, 44.4%) by +1.7 and +0.7 points respectively. For retrieval, ARGENT-L reaches 58.6% R@5 on COCO text-to-image and 71.7% R@5 on COCO image-to-text, surpassing HyCoCLIP-L by +1.1 and +1.0 points. On the proposed PEP hierarchical metric, ARGENT-L scores 99.5 AUC and 90.3 AP, improving over HyCoCLIP-L (98.0, 89.5) by +1.5 and +0.8. Gains are consistent across Small, Base, and Large scales, with the largest classification improvements appearing at the Large scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (Euclidean baseline), ARGENT wins decisively on every metric at every scale: e.g., ARGENT-L achieves 45.6% ImageNet accuracy vs. CLIP-L's 39.9%, and 58.6% T2I COCO R@5 vs. 57.7%. Against MERU (hyperbolic contrastive learning), ARGENT also dominates across all tasks, with the gap widening at larger scales (e.g., +6.0% ImageNet for Large). Against the direct SOTA hyperbolic VLM HyCoCLIP, ARGENT wins on ImageNet classification (+1.7% for Large), all four retrieval benchmarks (up to +1.9% I2T Flickr R@5 for Small), WordNet Jaccard (+1.1 for Large), and PEP AUC/AP (+1.5/+0.8 for Large). The sole exception is average classification accuracy for the Small model, where ARGENT-S (38.4%) trails HyCoCLIP-S (39.2%) by 0.8 points, though it still leads on ImageNet (+1.5). Overall, ARGENT establishes a consistent state-of-the-art across all three model sizes on the majority of evaluated metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of hyperbolic vision-language modeling and hierarchical representation learning, building directly on MERU (Desai et al., ICML 2023), which first extended CLIP's contrastive framework to the Lorentz model, and HyCoCLIP (Pal et al., ICLR 2025), which introduced entailment cones to enforce 'is-a' relationships in hyperbolic space. The key departure is a diagnosis of a fundamental geometric instability in HyCoCLIP's cone-based loss—where shrinking parent norms cause the cone aperture to degenerate into a half-space—and a replacement with an adaptive angular loss that removes the norm-to-aperture coupling entirely. The paper also departs from the HierarCaps evaluation protocol by identifying systematic false-negative bias in its retrieval-based metrics and proposing a probabilistic entailment classification protocol (PEP) scored with AUC-ROC and AP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cone-collapse diagnosis addresses a practical training instability that likely affected all prior hyperbolic VLMs using entailment cones, and the adaptive loss provides a cleaner geometric solution than HyCoCLIP's heuristic aperture clipping. The PEP protocol is a meaningful step toward reliable evaluation of hierarchical understanding, moving beyond brittle retrieval accuracy to a probabilistic classification task. However, the paper acknowledges a trade-off: removing explicit cone constraints increases embedding sparsity (necessitating a regularizer), and AP on HierarCaps actually decreases with model scale while classification improves, suggesting an unresolved tension between broad downstream performance and fine-grained hierarchical fidelity. A large-scale, high-quality hierarchical benchmark remains an open need for the field.
