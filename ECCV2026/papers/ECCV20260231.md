## Problem

Deep learning methods for depth completion struggle to handle the sparse and irregular nature of input depth measurements, often degrading dramatically under high sparsity or when test-time sparsity deviates from training conditions. Standard convolutional architectures have limited propagation range, making it difficult to spread information from a few depth points across the entire image. This paper addresses the gap by formulating depth completion as inference in a learned probabilistic graphical model rather than direct regression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260231/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (H×W×3) and a sparse depth map (H×W) with irregularly distributed valid pixels, where the number of measurements can range from a single point to tens of thousands. Unlike prior methods that route sparse depth through dedicated sparsity-invariant convolutional layers, GBPN consumes sparse measurements purely as data-term constraints within the MRF, eliminating architecture-specific handling of missing data. The output is a per-pixel Gaussian distribution over depth, yielding both a dense depth estimate (mean) and a confidence measure (precision), enabling risk-aware downstream use.

## Method

GBPN trains a Graphical Model Construction Network (GMCN), a U-Net with dilated neighborhood attention and ResNet blocks, to dynamically construct a scene-specific Markov Random Field by predicting unary/pairwise weights, expected depth differences, damping rates, and non-local edge offsets (via deformable-convolution-style bilinear sampling). Dense depth is then inferred via Gaussian Belief Propagation using a novel serial & parallel message passing scheme: four directional serial sweeps (LR, TB, RL, BT) on local edges ensure full-image information propagation, while parallel updates on dynamically generated non-local edges capture long-range dependencies efficiently. The MRF parameters are updated dynamically across GBP iterations, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a probability-based loss that jointly supervises depth accuracy (L1+L2) and precision without requiring ground-truth uncertainty. Two variants exist: GBPN-1 uses only the color image to build the MRF, while GBPN-2 adds a second U-Net that fuses the color features with the intermediate depth distribution from GBPN-1 via cross-attention.

## Results

On the KITTI depth completion benchmark, GBPN achieves the best iRMSE (1.79 1/km) and competitive RMSE (681.61 mm), surpassing BP-Net on all four KITTI metrics. On NYUv2, GBPN attains the best RMSE (0.085 m), best δ₁.₀₂ (89.3%), and best δ₁.₀₅ (95.9%) among all compared methods. On the VOID benchmark (zero-shot generalization), GBPN consistently achieves the lowest RMSE and MAE across all three sparsity levels (150, 500, 1500 points), e.g., 0.830 m RMSE at 150 points vs. 0.847 m for BP-Net and 1.249 m for OGNI-DC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GBPN outperforms BP-Net [37], the closest prior hybrid method, on all four KITTI metrics (RMSE 681.61 vs. 684.90 mm; iRMSE 1.79 vs. 1.82 1/km) and on all four NYUv2 metrics (RMSE 0.085 vs. 0.089 m; δ₁.₀₂ 89.3% vs. 87.2%). On NYUv2, GBPN achieves the best δ₁.₀₂ (89.3%) and δ₁.₀₅ (95.9%), surpassing OGNI-DC [50] (88.3%, 95.6%) and NLSPN [27] (88.0%, 95.4%). On KITTI, DMD3C [22] reports a slightly lower RMSE (678.12 mm) but relies on foundation-model distillation during training, whereas GBPN is trained from scratch on the standard KITTI training set. On the VOID generalization benchmark, GBPN wins every RMSE and MAE metric at all three sparsity levels, e.g., at 150 points it achieves 0.830 m RMSE vs. 0.847 m (BP-Net), 0.932 m (NLSPN), and 1.249 m (OGNI-DC). In sparsity robustness experiments (Fig. 3), GBPN's REL monotonically decreases as point count increases, while methods like GuideNet [38] and CFormer [46] degrade when tested far above their 500-point training sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GBPN positions itself within the 'combination of models and learning' lineage for depth completion, departing from prior hybrid methods (CSPN-based diffusion, BP-Net's bilateral propagation, OGNI-DC's learned optimization) by learning the full MRF structure—including dynamic non-local edges—rather than a fixed propagation kernel. It builds on Gaussian Belief Propagation (Bickson 2008) as the inference engine, borrows deformable-convolution-style offset sampling (Dai 2017) for non-local edge construction, and adopts the heteroscedastic Gaussian loss (Kendall & Gal 2017) to jointly supervise depth mean and precision. The key conceptual departure is treating sparse depth as principled data terms in a globally optimized MRF rather than as input to dedicated sparsity-handling network layers, which prior deep methods (sparsity-invariant convolutions, confidence propagation) required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled solution to the sparsity-robustness problem that plagues pure deep-learning depth completion: by embedding sparse measurements as data terms in a global MRF, GBPN maintains consistent performance from a single depth point to 20,000 points, whereas competing methods degrade significantly outside their training sparsity. The byproduct uncertainty estimate (precision) is practically valuable for risk-aware downstream tasks like robotic planning. However, several hyperparameters (neighbor count, propagation steps) remain empirically set, and the model is trained on limited curated data; the authors identify adaptive hyperparameter selection and foundation-model distillation as promising but unexplored directions.
