## Problem

Face Video Restoration (FVR) faces a trilemma: severe degradation causes identity drift as generative priors dominate reconstruction, static 2D reference images entangle identity with a single viewpoint and fail under large pose variations, and one-step acceleration techniques sacrifice perceptual realism for speed. No existing method simultaneously resolves all three challenges while maintaining temporal coherence across dynamic expressions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded face video (81 frames at 512×512) and a single high-quality reference image of the same subject. The reference image is used to extract both ArcFace identity embeddings and 3DMM identity coefficients, while the degraded video provides per-frame motion parameters (pose, expression, translation). At inference, the model supports flexible conditioning: it can operate with full reference+geometry guidance, geometry-only, or unguided restoration via conditional dropout, making the reference image optional for deployment.

## Method

TIGER formulates FVR as prior-conditioned one-step rectified flow in the latent space of a pretrained video VAE (Wan2.1-Fun-1.3B base, fine-tuned via LoRA rank 384). Three structured priors condition a DiT velocity predictor: (1) an Identity Prior from ArcFace embeddings injected via cross-attention to anchor subject identity, (2) a Geometry Prior constructed by cross-source 3DMM parameter fusion—combining identity coefficients from the reference with per-frame motion parameters from the degraded video to render temporally consistent normal maps—and (3) a Generative Prior from the pretrained foundation model's learned video distribution. The degraded latent, encoded normal maps, and encoded reference are channel-concatenated as spatially-aligned input, and the DiT predicts a single velocity step (t*=0.4) to map directly to the clean manifold. Training follows a progressive three-stage strategy: latent-space MSE for structural mapping, decoder-coupled LPIPS refinement for texture, and adversarial distribution alignment with a spatiotemporal discriminator for holistic realism.

## Results

On VFHQ-Test, TIGER achieves the best scores on 8 of 9 metrics: PSNR 30.73, SSIM 0.877, LPIPS 0.0697, IDS 0.948, FVD 40.42, LIQE 4.09, MUSIQ 70.11, and VIRD 0.4536. The FVD of 40.42 is a 2.6× improvement over the next-best method (SVFR at 148.0), and LPIPS of 0.0697 is roughly 40% lower than DOVE (0.1189). On the real-world VoxCeleb2 benchmark, TIGER leads in LIQE (3.74 vs. 2.86 for STAR), MUSIQ (66.24 vs. 59.96 for STAR), and VIRD (0.9322 vs. 0.9360 for DOVE), while trailing STAR on CLIP-IQA (0.4933 vs. 0.5706). Inference takes 17.53 s for an 81-frame 512×512 video in a single step, comparable to other one-step methods (DOVE: 11.83 s, SeedVR2-3B: 18.46 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIGER outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of metrics. Against the closest competitor DOVE (one-step diffusion), TIGER improves FVD by 61% (40.42 vs. 103.89), LPIPS by 41% (0.0697 vs. 0.1189), and IDS by 2.1 points (0.9481 vs. 0.9283), while also leading in PSNR, SSIM, LIQE, MUSIQ, and VIRD; the only metric where DOVE edges ahead is CLIP-IQA (0.4654 vs. 0.4756 is actually TIGER winning, so TIGER wins all 9 here). Against STAR, which uses 15 diffusion steps, TIGER achieves a 6.2× speedup (17.53 s vs. 396.52 s) while matching or exceeding STAR on every metric except CLIP-IQA (0.4756 vs. 0.5889), where STAR's multi-step sampling yields a higher score. Compared to the other one-step method SeedVR2-3B, TIGER reduces FVD by 93% (40.42 vs. 593.62) and improves LPIPS by 58% (0.0697 vs. 0.1638). Among face-specific FVR baselines (KEEP, BFVR, PGTFormer, SVFR), TIGER dominates across all metrics, with the largest gaps in FVD (40.42 vs. 148.00 for SVFR) and IDS (0.9481 vs. 0.8943 for SVFR). On the real-world VoxCeleb2 benchmark, TIGER leads in LIQE, MUSIQ, and VIRD but trails STAR on CLIP-IQA (0.4933 vs. 0.5706), suggesting STAR's multi-step generation still captures certain aesthetic qualities that the one-step rectified flow does not fully replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIGER extends the line of diffusion-based video restoration (DOVE, STAR, SeedVR2, SVFR) by introducing explicit identity and 3D geometric priors that prior generative approaches lack, addressing the identity-drift and viewpoint-entanglement failures they exhibit on face-centric content. It departs from earlier reference-guided face restoration methods (which rely on 2D pose alignment or GAN latent spaces) by lifting guidance into a disentangled 3DMM parameter space and performing cross-source fusion—taking identity from a clean reference and motion from the degraded video. The one-step rectified-flow formulation builds on the rectified flow framework [10] and the Wan2.1 video generation backbone [38], but repurposes them for a deterministic latent-transport task conditioned on structured priors rather than open-ended generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step inference (17.5 s for 81 frames at 512×512) achieving substantially better FVD than multi-step diffusion baselines (40 vs. 104–594) makes high-quality face video restoration practically deployable, and the cross-source 3D parameter fusion offers a clean, generalizable recipe for decoupling identity from motion in any reference-guided restoration setting. The constructed 28,491-sample quality-filtered dataset and the VIRD metric provide a more rigorous evaluation protocol for the field. Open questions include whether the 3DMM-based geometry prior generalizes to non-frontal or heavily occluded faces, whether the approach extends to non-face video restoration, and whether the progressive three-stage training can be compressed without sacrificing the adversarial realism gains.
