## Problem

Feedforward 4D street reconstruction is bottlenecked by two issues: geometry degradation under motion (alternating-attention features treat all frames equally, weakening directed motion cues) and the lack of a motion model that avoids explicit 4D supervision, external trackers, or segmentation. Existing feedforward methods either model only static scenes or depend on brittle tracking pipelines that fail under extrapolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262408/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unposed RGB video clip (20 frames at evaluation) with no camera poses, LiDAR, or segmentation masks required at inference. It produces a unified 3DGS scene where static primitives persist across the full sequence and dynamic primitives carry per-pixel 3D velocities, enabling rendering at both novel camera poses and novel timestamps. Camera intrinsics and extrinsics are predicted by a dedicated head rather than supplied as input, and the dynamic/static separation emerges from a learned probability map rather than an external segmentation model.

## Method

StreetForward builds on VGGT's 36-layer alternating-attention backbone (initialized from Pi3 weights) and appends a 4-layer causal masked-attention module that concatenates sinusoidal temporal embeddings to tokens and restricts attention to designated source→target frame pairs (both forward and backward), producing motion-aware features. A DPT-style decoder regresses per-pixel forward/backward velocities and a dynamic probability from these features; static Gaussians are selected by thresholding this probability and persist for the full clip, while dynamic Gaussians are propagated across adjacent frames via the predicted velocities. At render time, the current frame's own dynamic Gaussians are deliberately excluded, forcing dynamic content to be explained by warped neighbors and preventing degenerate placement. Training uses a staged schedule (geometry first, then motion) with losses for RGB reconstruction, opacity stabilization, depth consistency, 2D/3D local rigidity, and forward-backward velocity symmetry, all without explicit per-pixel motion supervision.

## Results

On Waymo (20-frame clips, 4 context frames), StreetForward achieves the best dynamic-region PSNR of 24.30 and SSIM of 0.827, outperforming the strongest baseline DGGT (20.99 / 0.821) by 3.3 dB in PSNR. For depth estimation against sparse LiDAR, it attains the lowest RMSE across all partitions: 3.09 m (static), 3.45 m (dynamic), and 3.14 m (full image), compared to 6.37 m dynamic RMSE for DGGT and 7.50 m for STORM. Zero-shot transfer to CARLA (no fine-tuning) yields 22.37 PSNR / 0.741 SSIM on dynamic regions and 7.16 m dynamic depth RMSE, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreetForward decisively outperforms DGGT [4] on dynamic-region synthesis (24.30 vs 20.99 PSNR, +3.3 dB) and depth (3.45 vs 6.37 m RMSE on dynamic pixels), but trails DGGT on full-image PSNR (27.01 vs 27.41) and SSIM (0.818 vs 0.846), suggesting DGGT's tracker-based approach still captures some static-region detail better. Against STORM [50], StreetForward wins on every metric: dynamic PSNR by 2.2 dB, dynamic SSIM by 0.20, and full-image PSNR by 0.63 dB, with depth RMSE roughly halved on dynamic regions (3.45 vs 7.50 m). The gap over AnySplat [15], DA3 [20], and PVG [6] is large (≥8 dB dynamic PSNR), as these methods lack explicit motion modeling or rely on per-scene optimization. On zero-shot CARLA transfer, StreetForward leads DGGT by 1.05 dB dynamic PSNR and 0.77 m dynamic depth RMSE, confirming that the causal attention mechanism generalizes without domain-specific fine-tuning. For depth estimation, StreetForward also surpasses VGGT [35] (3.14 vs 4.07 m full-image RMSE) and Pi3 [41] (3.14 vs 5.61 m), validating that the motion-aware features improve geometric accuracy beyond what the base VGGT backbone provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreetForward extends VGGT's alternating-attention backbone (initialized from Pi3) by inserting a causal masked-attention module that explicitly models source→target frame relations, addressing the limitation that VGGT's unordered global attention treats all frames symmetrically and thus weakly encodes directed motion. For representation, it adopts 3D Gaussian Splatting with per-pixel velocities decoded via a DPT-style head, departing from prior feedforward 4D methods (DGGT, STORM, Flux4D) that rely on external trackers, LiDAR supervision, or simplified rigid-body motion models. The local rigidity prior and forward-backward velocity consistency losses draw on ideas from Dynamic 3D Gaussians and scene-flow regularization, but are applied here without any explicit 4D ground-truth supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for per-scene optimization, external trackers, segmentation models, and LiDAR at inference time, StreetForward makes large-scale dynamic street reconstruction practical for closed-loop autonomous-driving simulation, where thousands of diverse scenes must be reconstructed rapidly. The zero-shot Waymo→CARLA transfer demonstrates meaningful domain generalization, though the method's reliance on local rigidity may limit fidelity for highly deformable agents (e.g., cyclists, animals) and the causal attention adds non-trivial compute over vanilla VGGT. Open questions include scaling to longer sequences beyond the 20-frame clips used here, handling occlusion-heavy multi-agent interactions, and whether the velocity-based motion model can be replaced by learned deformation fields for truly non-rigid dynamics.
