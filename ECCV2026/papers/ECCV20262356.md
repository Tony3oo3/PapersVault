## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in text-to-image diffusion models exhibits an inherent quality-alignment tension: increasing the guidance scale to strengthen prompt adherence amplifies structural perturbations that accumulate across denoising steps, causing off-manifold artifacts and visual degradation. The paper probes the limit of greedy alignment maximization by showing that projecting each update onto the guidance direction (SAP) deterministically discards high-dimensional orthogonal corrective components essential for stable denoising, revealing a structural incompatibility between semantic alignment and trajectory stability.

## Input / Output

SCALE operates as a drop-in, training-free inference-time module on standard text-to-image diffusion models (SDXL at 1024×1024, SD1.5, FLUX.1-Dev). At each denoising step, it consumes the baseline sampler's latent-space update vector and the CFG guidance direction (computed from the difference between conditional and unconditional noise predictions), then outputs a modified update that is fed back into the standard sampling loop. No architectural changes, fine-tuning, or auxiliary models are required; the only added computation is a per-step orthogonal projection, yielding a latency ratio of ~1.0038× over standard sampling.

## Method

SCALE follows a 'Decoupled Satisfaction' principle: at each timestep, the baseline update d_t is decomposed into a component parallel to the semantic guidance axis (d_∥) and an orthogonal corrective component (d_⊥). SCALE then applies a controlled amplification factor λ_t ≥ 1 only to d_∥ while leaving d_⊥ exactly unchanged, producing the corrected update z_t = λ_t · d_∥ + d_⊥. This is derived as the unique solution to a minimal-perturbation convex program (Theorem 2) and equivalently expressed as an orthogonal-invariant linear operator A_λ = I + (λ−1)·P_{ĝ_t} (Theorem 3), guaranteeing no distortion on the orthogonal channel. The scaling factor λ_t is set adaptively via a hybrid schedule combining a tunable lower bound λ_base with the cosine alignment between the baseline update and the guidance direction, and SCALE is gated to activate only in late denoising steps when the baseline update exhibits positive semantic progress. The paper also introduces SAP (Semantic Alignment Projection) as a diagnostic baseline that collapses updates to the one-dimensional guidance axis, whose failure motivates the decoupled design.

## Results

On DrawBench (SDXL base), SCALE achieves CLIPScore 0.2885, BLIPScore 0.5244, VQAScore 0.7889, DSGScore 0.7305, and ImageReward 0.8305, outperforming the base model (0.2775/0.5087/0.7417/0.7068/0.6402) and most baselines. On GenEval, SCALE leads all five metrics (e.g., VQAScore 0.8345 vs. 0.7998 for SDXL; DSGScore 0.8556 vs. 0.8439). On T2I-CompBench, SCALE attains the best VQAScore (0.7593), DSGScore (0.7593), and ImageReward (0.7725). Inference overhead is negligible at 1.0038× standard SDXL latency (5.37 s vs. 5.35 s per image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCALE achieves the best or near-best score on 14 out of 15 metric-benchmark combinations. On DrawBench, SCALE leads on BLIPScore (0.5244 vs. 0.5198 for GA-Eval), VQAScore (0.7889 vs. 0.7867), and DSGScore (0.7305 vs. 0.7133 for CFG++), while GA-Eval narrowly edges it on CLIPScore (0.2889 vs. 0.2885) and ImageReward (0.8522 vs. 0.8305). On GenEval, SCALE wins all five metrics, with the largest margin on VQAScore (0.8345 vs. 0.8182 for GA-Eval, +1.63%). On T2I-CompBench, SCALE dominates VQAScore (0.7593 vs. 0.7382 for APG, +2.11%) and DSGScore (0.7593 vs. 0.7490 for Z-Sampling), and ties APG on BLIPScore (0.5282). Against the reward-guidance method DAS, SCALE trades a small CLIPScore deficit (0.2885 vs. 0.2912 on DrawBench) for substantially higher VQAScore (0.7889 vs. 0.7483) and ImageReward (0.8305 vs. 0.7793), while DAS incurs 55.6× inference overhead versus SCALE's 1.004×. When stacked on top of Diffusion-DPO, SCALE adds further gains (e.g., ImageReward 0.9167 vs. 0.8178 on DrawBench), confirming complementarity with training-based alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCALE builds directly on Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), reinterpreting its linear extrapolation as a coupled scaling of both semantic and structural components of the update. It departs from prior projection-based guidance methods (APG, TAG, TCFG) by introducing a 'Decoupled Satisfaction' principle: rather than damping or reshaping the full guidance vector, SCALE selectively amplifies only the component parallel to the semantic axis while leaving the orthogonal corrective channel untouched. The paper also introduces SAP (Semantic Alignment Projection) as a diagnostic tool, showing that the intuitive greedy strategy of projecting updates entirely onto the guidance direction fails due to loss of high-dimensional corrective freedom—a structural insight that motivates the decoupled design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCALE offers a theoretically grounded, training-free, and nearly zero-overhead (1.0038×) drop-in mechanism that addresses the fundamental quality-alignment tension in CFG without requiring architectural changes or additional training. Its compatibility with preference-tuned models (Diffusion-DPO) and multiple base architectures (SDXL, SD1.5, FLUX.1-Dev) suggests broad practical utility for practitioners seeking better prompt faithfulness. Open questions include whether the decoupling principle generalizes to non-CFG guidance formulations (e.g., classifier-based or reward-based), whether the adaptive λ schedule is near-optimal, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D generation where trajectory stability constraints are even tighter.
