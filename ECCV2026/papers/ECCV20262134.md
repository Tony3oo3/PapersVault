## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning VLMs rely on a single fixed inference pipeline that internalizes a narrow spatial prior, causing systematic failures on out-of-distribution layouts and task categories. While multi-agent ensembling could in principle combine complementary inductive biases, prior spatial-reasoning attempts use homogeneous backbones whose shared failure modes limit aggregation gains. The paper asks how a system can dynamically determine which reasoning strategies to trust for a given input and modulate their contributions at test time without modifying any model parameters.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a free-form text query paired with a single RGB image and produces a natural-language spatial reasoning answer (e.g., a multiple-choice option or a short phrase). Internally, the pipeline routes the query through a head agent that classifies it into one of five spatial categories (Spatial Relation, Counting, Size, Distance & Depth, Orientation), then dispatches it to up to three specialist VLMs operating under distinct roles—implicit 2D visual reasoning, explicit 3D reconstruction (via DepthPro + SAM2), or scene-graph construction (via DINO-v2). The final answer is generated by a dedicated reasoning agent (DeepSeek-VL-R1-7B) that performs conditional re-generation over the full evidence pool of specialist answers and intermediate representations, rather than simple voting.

## Method

SPATIO assembles a heterogeneous pool of five open-source VLMs and assigns each query to a Top-3 subset of agents with role-specific prompts, then aggregates their outputs through a reliability-aware final reasoning stage. The core contribution is Test-Time Orchestration (TTO), a parameter-free calibration loop that maintains per-agent, per-role, per-category confidence scores updated via a Beta-Bernoulli trust model whose sufficient statistics are driven by soft rewards computed from semantic similarity between each specialist's output and ground truth. A dual-timescale exponential moving average (fast EMA on raw rewards, slow EMA on the Bayesian posterior mean) balances rapid reaction to distribution shifts against long-term stability, while a divergence penalty (δ_i) specifically down-weights agents that underperform relative to a failed consensus. A category-dependent ramp factor prevents premature trust divergence on sparsely observed categories, and the entire mechanism requires only 150 labeled calibration samples (30 per category) to converge, after which inference proceeds without any labels or parameter updates.

## Results

SPATIO achieves 72.4% overall accuracy on 3DSRBench, outperforming GPT-5.2 (67.2%) and Claude Opus 4.6 (63.5%) by 5.2 and 8.9 percentage points respectively. On STVQA-7k, SPATIO reaches 88.2% overall (vs. 84.7% for Claude Opus 4.6 and 81.4% for GPT-5.2), and on CV-Bench it attains 86.9% (vs. 83.5% for GPT-5.2). In zero-shot evaluation on Omni3D-Bench, SPATIO improves over the strongest single-model baseline (Qwen3-VL-4B) by +17.1 on float outputs and +20.6 on int outputs, demonstrating effective generalization to unseen answer formats.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPATIO outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the strongest closed-source models, SPATIO leads GPT-5.2 by 5.2 pp on 3DSRBench (72.4 vs. 67.2), 6.8 pp on STVQA-7k (88.2 vs. 81.4), and 3.4 pp on CV-Bench (86.9 vs. 83.5); it leads Claude Opus 4.6 by 8.9, 3.5, and 4.5 pp respectively. Against the strongest open-source baseline Qwen3-VL-4B, SPATIO gains 13.2 pp on 3DSRBench, 5.8 pp on STVQA-7k, and 5.6 pp on CV-Bench. LoRA-finetuned variants of the open-source baselines (rank 64, α=128, trained on the same 150 TTO samples) yield negligible or negative gains (e.g., Qwen3-VL-4B drops from 59.2 to 59.1 on 3DSRBench), confirming that orchestration-level adaptation, not parameter-level fine-tuning, drives the improvement. On zero-shot Omni3D-Bench, SPATIO surpasses Qwen3-VL-4B by +17.1 (float) and +20.6 (int), and on the unseen MMSI-Bench it achieves +19.5 pp over the best baseline, validating cross-domain generalization of the trust profiles.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPATIO builds on the Bayesian trust modeling framework of Hallyburton et al. [15] and the EMA-based consensus mechanisms from multi-agent RL [12], repurposing both for reliability estimation at the orchestration level rather than the parameter level. It departs from the dominant single-pipeline paradigm in spatial VLMs (SpatialVLM, SpatialLLM, SpatialThinker, SpatialReasoner, SpatialRGPT) by treating spatial reasoning as a coordination problem among heterogeneous specialists, and from prior multi-agent spatial attempts (VADAR) by replacing homogeneous backbones with architecturally diverse VLMs and rule-based aggregation with trust-weighted conditional re-generation. The conceptual lineage also draws on Mixture-of-Agents results in language reasoning (MoA, MoSA) and the 'Debate or Vote' finding that architectural heterogeneity is necessary for multi-agent gains to materialize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPATIO demonstrates that parameter-free, reliability-aware orchestration of heterogeneous VLMs can outperform both closed-source frontier models (GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.6) and LoRA-finetuned open-source specialists on spatial reasoning, suggesting that test-time coordination is a viable alternative to further scaling or fine-tuning. The framework's compatibility with black-box models and its minimal calibration requirement (150 samples) make it practically deployable. Open questions include whether the Bayesian trust model degrades under non-stationary query streams, how the approach scales beyond five agents or five task categories, and whether the category-specific trust profiles can be learned without any labeled calibration set. The added inference latency (~28 s average) and VRAM overhead remain practical constraints for real-time applications.
