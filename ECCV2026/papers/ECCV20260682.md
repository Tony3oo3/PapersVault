## Problem

Existing human motion recovery methods are locked to fixed sensor configurations, requiring entirely new architectures or re-training whenever the input modality changes (e.g., switching from sparse IMUs to head-mounted 6-DoF to partial keypoints). Moreover, diffusion-based approaches that handle occlusion and sparsity demand 100+ iterative denoising steps, making them unsuitable for real-time deployment on consumer hardware. OmniPoser addresses both gaps by unifying heterogeneous sparse observations under a single model that infers in one ODE step.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a temporally varying, per-joint mask over a canonical SMPL feature vector (root translation/rotation, 3D joint positions, 6D rotations in world and root frames, shape β, foot-contact labels), where any subset of joints and feature groups may be observed at each of the 33-frame (30 fps) clip. Missing entries are not simply zeroed but replaced by learnable per-joint embeddings before conditioning. The output is the full SMPL parameter set—local body pose, root trajectory, shape, and contact labels—produced by a decoupled dual-stream generator (global trajectory stream feeding the local pose stream), yielding 3D joint positions via forward kinematics.

## Method

OmniPoser introduces a cross-modal masking mechanism paired with a Masked Conditioning Encoder (MCE) that replaces each unobserved joint with a dedicated learnable embedding (inspired by MAE), preserving joint identity, then smooths the result through four residual temporal-convolution blocks with GroupNorm and Mish activations. The conditioned signal drives a decoupled dual-stream denoising network: a multi-scale temporal convolutional UNet generates the low-DoF global root trajectory, while a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with Rotary Position Embedding and cross-attention to the MCE output generates the high-DoF local body pose, shape, and contact labels. Both streams are trained via Conditional Flow Matching with an optimal-transport affine path and supervised by physics-grounded auxiliary losses (joint position/velocity, acceleration smoothness, foot-contact, and foot-skating penalties). At inference, a single Euler integration step from t=0 to t=1 maps Gaussian noise onto the motion manifold, achieving >700 FPS. Training draws mask samples from predefined sensor layouts and data-driven per-joint visibility statistics estimated from Ego-Exo4D COCO annotations, enabling generalization to unseen in-the-wild occlusion patterns.

## Results

On the upper-body sparse observation setting (AMASS), OmniPoser (Ours-S) achieves 32.1 mm MPJPE, outperforming RoHM (34.8 mm) and HuMoR (88.0 mm), with the lowest foot-skating ratio (0.031). On the egocentric head 6-DoF setting, Ours-S reaches 83.2 mm MPJPE on AMASS (~30% better than EgoAllo's 119.7 mm and ~50% better than EgoEgo's 167.4 mm), and 40.7 mm on in-the-wild Nymeria (~60% better than EgoAllo's 103.0 mm). On the 6-IMU + keypoint setting, Ours-S achieves 26.0 mm MPJPE and 19.4 mm PA-MPJPE on AIST++, with a jerk metric 5× lower than SIP and 8× lower than RobustCap. All results are produced with a single Euler ODE step, yielding >700 FPS inference speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniPoser clearly outperforms HuMoR [29] across all sparse-observation metrics (32.1 vs. 88.0 mm MPJPE on AMASS upper-body), as HuMoR's CVAE-based test-time optimization struggles with complete lower-body absence. Against RoHM [46], OmniPoser wins on MPJPE (32.1 vs. 34.8 mm AMASS; 21.1 vs. 59.3 mm Nymeria, a ~64% reduction) and foot-skating (0.031 vs. 0.078), while RoHM++-U (a unified-variant of RoHM) narrows the gap to 39.9 mm on AMASS. On the egocentric head 6-DoF setting, OmniPoser beats EgoEgo [15] by ~50% in MPJPE (83.2 vs. 167.4 mm AMASS) and EgoAllo [40] by ~30% (83.2 vs. 119.7 mm AMASS; 40.7 vs. 103.0 mm Nymeria), though EgoAllo retains a significant advantage in head translation accuracy (6.2 vs. 17.1 mm) due to its dedicated allocentric grounding. On the 6-IMU + keypoint setting, OmniPoser surpasses RobustCap [26] in MPJPE (36.2 vs. 42.3 mm on TotalCapture) and by a large margin in jerk (8× lower on AIST++), while SIP [33] retains an edge in global translation (5.42 vs. 15.9 mm on TotalCapture) owing to its direct use of stereo depth and physical IMU root readings—a trade-off OmniPoser accepts to maintain a purely modality-agnostic formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniPoser builds on Conditional Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as its generative backbone, replacing the iterative denoising of diffusion-based motion recovery methods like RoHM and EgoAllo with a single-step ODE integration. Conceptually, it extends the masked-modeling paradigm (MAE, GENMO, MoRo) from a training-time dropout schedule into a cross-modal sensor unification mechanism, where the mask encodes which physical sensor observes which joint rather than which features are randomly dropped. The decoupled dual-stream architecture (temporal conv UNet for global trajectory + DiT for local pose) follows the local-global decomposition established in RoHM and GENMO, but repurposes it to handle heterogeneous input modalities through a shared Masked Conditioning Encoder interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a single model replaces the need to train and deploy separate specialist architectures for each sensor configuration (keypoints-only, HMD-only, IMU+vision), while the single-step flow-matching inference at >700 FPS makes real-time deployment on consumer hardware feasible where 100+ diffusion-step baselines are not. Key limitations remain: the framework handles only a single person with no body-object or body-body contact modeling, the cascaded global→local inference propagates trajectory errors without correction, and the SMPL 22-joint skeleton excludes hands and face (SMPL-X). Open questions include whether the masking abstraction generalizes to multi-person interaction, whether iterative refinement between the two streams can close the small gaps to specialist methods on rigid-body metrics, and whether the approach scales to articulated hand/face models as training data becomes available.
