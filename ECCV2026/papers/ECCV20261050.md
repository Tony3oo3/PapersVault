## Problem

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) exhibit a persistent gap between their visual understanding and generation capabilities: a model can correctly judge prompt-image alignment yet fail to generate a faithful image from the same prompt. This raises the question of whether a UMM's own understanding module can serve as an internal evaluator to reward and improve its generation module, without relying on external reward models, human preference labels, or additional image data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261050/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated RGB image via a flow-matching (diffusion) decoder. During the post-training phase, the UMM first generates its own candidate images with corresponding bounding boxes (using a 'think'/CoT mode for richer semantics), then its frozen understanding module scores each candidate with a global scalar in [-1, 1] and a dense regional reward map over prompt-relevant bounding boxes. These cached rewards are then used to modulate the velocity-prediction loss during a standard SFT-style training pass on the original ground-truth latents.

## Method

SRUM is an offline, reward-weighted post-training framework that creates a self-rewarding loop within a single UMM. The understanding module (frozen, no gradients) acts as an internal evaluator, producing a global-local dual reward: a global score for overall compositional coherence and a local, bounding-box-level score for object-level fidelity and attribute binding. The training objective multiplies the product of the global scalar α and the regional reward map R (element-wise) with the squared velocity-prediction error, so positive feedback preserves existing structure while negative feedback drives targeted correction in specific regions. A reference constraint term (L_ref) regularizes toward the ground-truth velocity to prevent reward hacking and policy drift. The entire pipeline requires no external reward model, no human annotations, and no additional image data beyond the original training prompts, making it directly applicable to existing UMMs of various architectures (e.g., Bagel's MoT design, BLIP3o's frozen-MLLM design).

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, Bagel+SRUM in CoT mode achieves an overall score of 88.37, surpassing the strongest T2I baseline (SD-3-medium, 84.26) and all UMM baselines, with a +3.91 gain over Bagel(CoT) (84.46). In standard mode, Bagel+SRUM reaches 86.55 (+4.37 over Bagel's 82.18), and BLIP3o+SRUM improves from 84.66 to 85.75. On T2I-ReasonBench, SRUM lifts Bagel from 43.82 to 46.75 overall, outperforming both the base model and an SFT-only variant (45.37). SRUM also sets new SOTA scores in Spatial (93.88), 3D Spatial (88.60), and Complex (91.31) sub-categories on T2I-CompBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRUM consistently outperforms all T2I baselines: SD-3-medium (84.26), FLUX.1-dev (83.10), and SDXL (74.38) on T2I-CompBench overall. Among UMMs, Bagel+SRUM(CoT) at 88.37 surpasses OmniGen2 (85.84), Show-O2 (82.83), and Janus-Pro (74.01), while BLIP3o+SRUM (85.75) edges out the base BLIP3o (84.66) by +1.09. Against post-training methods, SRUM (88.37) beats ReCA (86.45) and UnifiedReward-7B (85.15) on T2I-CompBench. Notably, even powerful external VLM reward models (Qwen2.5-VL-32B with global reward: 88.01; Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 87.52) fall short of SRUM's self-rewarding (88.37), and the internal scorer shows stronger calibration with the final evaluator (ρ=0.58 vs. 0.43) and better accept/reject separation (0.67 vs. −0.31). On T2I-ReasonBench, SRUM (46.75) outperforms both the base Bagel (43.82) and SFT-only training (45.37), confirming that the self-rewarding signal generalizes to unseen reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRUM sits at the intersection of two active research threads: unified multimodal model architectures (Bagel's Mixture-of-Transformers, BLIP3o's frozen-MLLM design) and self-rewarding mechanisms previously applied to understanding models (CSR, SRPO, R1-Reward). It departs from prior post-training approaches for UMMs—DPO-based preference optimization, Reconstruction Alignment, and external VLM reward models—by treating the model's own understanding module as the reward signal, eliminating the need for external evaluators or human preference labels. The technical novelty lies in the reward-weighted flow-matching loss that combines a dense local reward map (from bounding-box-grounded object-level scores) with a global compositional scalar, a formulation not present in prior self-rewarding or RL-for-generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRUM establishes a practical, data-efficient paradigm for closing the understanding-generation gap in UMMs without external reward models, which is significant given the scarcity of high-quality image-text preference data. The finding that a 7B internal scorer outperforms 32B external VLMs as a training signal (better calibration and accept/reject separation) challenges the assumption that larger external models are always better reward providers. Open questions include whether the offline, cached-reward formulation can be extended to online self-play where both modules co-adapt, whether the bounding-box grounding requirement generalizes to models without native localization, and whether the approach scales to video or 3D generation.
