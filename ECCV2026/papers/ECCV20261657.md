## Problem

Aerial-ground cooperative perception (AGCP) promises to overcome the blind spots and occlusion limitations of ground-only V2V/V2I cooperation by leveraging flexible UAV viewpoints, yet progress is hindered by the absence of large-scale public datasets with synchronized multimodal data from both UAVs and vehicles, and by the lack of architectures designed to fuse heterogeneous aerial (image-based) and ground (LiDAR-based) sensing. Existing aerial-ground datasets also lack multi-mode UAV operations, multi-UAV collaboration, and diverse safety-critical corner cases needed for systematic evaluation.

## Input / Output

Each ground vehicle provides a top-mounted LiDAR sweep (discretized into a 0.5 m BEV grid over a ±50 m range via PointPillars) and four surround RGB/depth/segmentation cameras, while each UAV provides a single downward-facing RGB image processed by a Swin Transformer backbone. All agents additionally share 6-DoF pose, timestamps, and sensor intrinsics/extrinsics as metadata. The model outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels across seven categories (car, pedestrian, truck, trailer, traffic cone, van, speed limit sign), object trajectories, and velocity estimates, evaluated under the nuScenes protocol (mAP, NDS, mATE, mASE, mAOE, mAVE).

## Method

VVFormer fuses heterogeneous aerial and ground agents through four stages: (1) metadata sharing of poses and sensor calibrations; (2) modality-specific feature extraction (PointPillars for LiDAR, Swin Transformer for UAV RGB); (3) a Cross-View Attention (CVA) module that projects a 3D voxel grid from the vehicle BEV into the UAV image plane via extrinsic/intrinsic transforms, samples downsampled image features per voxel, and aggregates across the height axis using sinusoidal height positional encodings and height-wise self-attention to produce a spatially aligned BEV feature; and (4) an Agent-Aware Attention (AAA) module that applies deformable cross-attention between the ego agent and each remote agent, scores per-cell informativeness with a lightweight ScoringNet, selects the highest-scoring agent feature per BEV location, and refines the result with a spatial cross-attention using learnable 2D offsets around uniform reference points. Trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr 0.001, weight decay 0.01) for 24 epochs on the VVSim dataset, VVFormer achieves mAP 0.6490 and NDS 0.5268, outperforming strong V2V/V2I baselines such as V2X-ViT and SICP.

## Results

On the VVSim test set (18,500 frames), VVFormer achieves the best overall performance with mAP 0.6490 and NDS 0.5268, outperforming all five baselines. It also attains the lowest localization and scale errors (mATE 0.2168, mASE 0.0954) and the best orientation error (mAOE 0.6647). The only metric where VVFormer trails is mAVE (3.2290 vs. 2.6077 for V2X-ViT), which the authors attribute to the wide speed distribution of dynamic agents in the test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VVFormer outperforms all five baselines on mAP, NDS, mATE, mASE, and mAOE. Against the closest competitor SICP (IROS 2024), VVFormer gains +1.63 mAP points (0.6490 vs. 0.6327) and +1.24 NDS points (0.5268 vs. 0.5144), while also improving mATE by 0.0095 and mASE by 0.0101. Compared to L4DR (AAAI 2025), the margin is smaller on mAP (+0.70) but larger on NDS (+3.44), reflecting L4DR's weaker scale and orientation estimation. V2X-ViT (ECCV 2022) achieves the best mAVE (2.6077) but trails VVFormer by 3.4 mAP points and 4.2 NDS points. CoBEVT (CoRL 2023) and F-Cooper (ICDCS 2019) are the weakest baselines, with mAP below 0.59, highlighting the gap between early cooperative methods and the proposed aerial-ground framework. The sole metric where VVFormer underperforms is mAVE, where V2X-ViT leads by 0.62, which the authors attribute to the diverse speed spectrum in the VVSim test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cooperative perception lineage (V2V frameworks like OPV2V and V2X-ViT, V2I frameworks like DAIR-V2X) by introducing a heterogeneous aerial-ground setting where UAVs with top-down RGB cameras collaborate with ground vehicles carrying LiDAR and surround cameras. Architecturally, VVFormer repurposes standard components—PointPillars for LiDAR BEV encoding, Swin Transformer for image features, and deformable cross-attention for multi-agent fusion—into a unified pipeline, with the novel cross-view attention module (voxel-to-pixel projection plus height-aware self-attention) serving as the key bridge between the two sensing domains. Unlike prior aerial-ground datasets (CoPeD, V2U-COO, Griffin) that offer limited flight modes or low-altitude monocular data, VVSim introduces multi-mode UAV operations (hovering, cyclic, escort) at varied altitudes within a multi-agent framework, though it remains simulation-based (AirSim) rather than real-world.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VVSim fills a notable gap by providing a large-scale, multi-scenario, multi-weather benchmark specifically for aerial-ground cooperative 3D detection, enabling systematic evaluation of heterogeneous agent fusion that prior ground-only or limited aerial datasets could not support. However, the simulation-based nature of the data (AirSim) raises questions about domain transfer to real-world deployment, and the modest performance gains over strong baselines (e.g., mAP 0.6490 vs. 0.6420 for L4DR) suggest the benchmark may not yet stress-test the full difficulty of AGCP. Open questions include communication bandwidth constraints in real multi-agent settings, scalability beyond the current agent counts, and whether the height-aware fusion and agent-aware attention mechanisms generalize to real sensor noise and calibration drift.
