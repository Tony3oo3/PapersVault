## Problem

Adversarial training is the dominant defense for improving robustness in deep networks, but its effect on the quality of predictive uncertainty—critical for selective classification—remains underexplored. The paper shows that several state-of-the-art adversarial training methods improve robust accuracy while degrading uncertainty ranking, producing confident adversarial errors that bypass rejection thresholds. Additionally, no standardized benchmark existed to systematically evaluate the robustness-uncertainty trade-off across methods, architectures, and augmentations.

## Input / Output

The method takes 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 or CIFAR-100 and produces a Dirichlet concentration vector α = e(x) + 1, which decomposes into a predictive mean π̄ = α/S (used for the class label) and a total concentration S = Σα_c (governing confidence). The uncertainty score is the Shannon entropy of the posterior predictive distribution H[Cat(π̄)], thresholded at τ to yield an accept/reject decision. Adversarial examples are generated via K-step PGD within an ℓ_p ball (ε = 8/255 for ℓ∞, ε = 128/255 for ℓ₂), with the attack explicitly maximizing drift in log-Dirichlet parameter space rather than standard cross-entropy.

## Method

EV-AT trains a CNN (WRN-34-10 or PreActResNet-18) to output Dirichlet concentration parameters, combining two losses: (i) an evidential classification loss L_EV (negative log marginal likelihood plus a KL regularizer toward the uniform Dirichlet prior) on clean samples, and (ii) a robust evidence alignment loss L_REA that penalizes the IKL divergence between clean and adversarial representations in log-concentration space η = log α. The adversarial example is generated by an evidence-targeted K-step PGD attack that maximizes this same IKL divergence, directly stress-testing uncertainty integrity rather than only flipping the argmax label. The key design choice is operating in η-space: Lemma 1 shows that bounding ‖η′−η‖∞ multiplicatively stabilizes both the predictive mean π̄ (which determines the entropy-based selection score) and the total strength S (which controls posterior concentration), simultaneously preventing confident adversarial errors. Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP) is incorporated as an additional regularization, and the overall objective is min_θ E[L_EV + β·L_REA] with no extra forward passes beyond standard adversarial training.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WRN-34-10, EV-AT achieves a Clean/AA average accuracy of 71.84% and Clean/AA AUGRC of 6.52, averaged over four augmentation regimes (Basic, Cutout, AutoAugment, AugMix) and three seeds. This surpasses the strongest baseline TRADES-AWP (70.29% accuracy, 7.68 AUGRC) by +1.55 points in accuracy and −1.16 in AUGRC. Under the ℓ∞ AutoAttack (ε=8/255) setting, EV-AT reaches 71.83% Clean/AA accuracy and 6.52 AUGRC, compared to IKL-AT's 69.89% and 7.99. EV-AT also generalizes to ℓ₂ (ε=128/255), achieving 82.96% accuracy and 2.49 AUGRC versus IKL-AT's 81.37% and 3.56.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EV-AT outperforms all five baselines on both Clean/AA accuracy and Clean/AA AUGRC across every augmentation regime. Against the strongest baseline TRADES-AWP [54], EV-AT improves Clean/AA accuracy from 70.29% to 71.84% (+1.55) and reduces Clean/AA AUGRC from 7.68 to 6.52 (−1.16), averaged over four augmentations. Against IKL-AT [14], the most recent uncertainty-aware baseline, EV-AT gains +1.94 accuracy (71.84 vs. 69.90) and reduces AUGRC by 1.48 (6.52 vs. 8.00). Standard AT [34] and TRADES [57] trail further behind at 68.05% and 68.53% accuracy respectively, with AUGRC values of 7.85 and 8.49. TRADES-EMFF [51] achieves competitive clean accuracy (e.g., 88.90% under AutoAug) but suffers the worst adversarial AUGRC among baselines (8.51 average), confirming the paper's finding that robustness gains do not reliably translate to robust uncertainty. Notably, EV-AT is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best Clean/AA accuracy and the best Clean/AA AUGRC in all four augmentation blocks, shifting the Pareto frontier rather than trading one metric for the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

EV-AT sits at the intersection of adversarial training (TRADES, AWP, IKL-AT) and evidential deep learning (Dirichlet-based uncertainty), but departs from both in a specific way: rather than enforcing logit-level or softmax-level consistency between clean and adversarial inputs (as TRADES and IKL-AT do), it aligns the full Dirichlet posterior in log-concentration space, simultaneously constraining both the predictive mean and the evidence strength. The paper also builds on the selective classification literature (AUGRC, risk-coverage curves) and the robustness-uncertainty interaction thread, but positions its contribution as the first systematic benchmark that evaluates adversarial training methods as selective systems under a unified protocol. Conceptually, the key departure from prior adversarial training is treating uncertainty integrity as a first-class training objective rather than a post-hoc evaluation metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that robust-accuracy gains do not reliably improve uncertainty ranking challenges a common assumption in safety-critical ML that making a model harder to fool automatically makes it more trustworthy. The unified benchmark (standardized architectures, augmentations, threat models, and selective-classification metrics) provides a reproducible protocol the field lacked for comparing methods on the joint robustness-uncertainty axis. However, the evaluation is confined to CIFAR-10/100 and two CNN architectures, leaving open whether the findings and EV-AT's advantages persist at ImageNet scale, in structured tasks (detection, segmentation), or in other modalities. The paper itself flags scaling and cross-task generalization as the most immediate open questions.
