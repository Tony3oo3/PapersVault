## Problem

Existing 3D/4D occupancy methods depend on costly human-annotated voxel labels or open-vocabulary segmentation models that fail on rare, long-tail objects critical for safety. No prior work integrates all three standard SDV sensor modalities (camera, LiDAR, RADAR) simultaneously as both inputs and self-supervision sources, leaving a gap in unsupervised, open-world occupancy forecasting that can handle arbitrary path-blocking objects without semantic class constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261804/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes H historical frames of multi-view RGB images, LiDAR points (x,y,z,intensity,Δt), and RADAR targets (x,y,z,range_rate,cross_section,Δt), and produces predictions at any continuous 4D query point (x,y,z,t) rather than a fixed voxel grid. Outputs are a scalar occupancy probability, a scalar obstacle-vs-ground segmentation probability, and a 3D flow vector per query. For LiDAR forecasting, a separate depth-prediction header is attached after freezing the world model parameters, converting the implicit occupancy field into predicted LiDAR point depths.

## Method

TriO uses an encoder-decoder architecture with a pretrained ResNet50 camera encoder and a sparse 3D convolutional LiDAR-RADAR encoder, fused via voxel attention at the coarsest resolution followed by deformable attention layers and a multi-resolution implicit decoder. The key novelty is tri-modal self-supervision: LiDAR rays generate occupancy pseudo-labels (free-space vs. occupied segments), RADAR Doppler readings supervise 3D flow by projecting the predicted flow onto the radial direction in the sensor frame, and a novel geometric segmentation method combines Metric3Dv2 image depth/normals with Patchwork++ LiDAR ground-plane fitting to produce soft obstacle/ground probabilities via confidence-driven fusion. Training minimizes a weighted sum of binary cross-entropy (occupancy), soft focal loss (segmentation, where each point's contribution is scaled by its label validity confidence), and L1 loss (flow), requiring no human annotations. The soft probabilistic pseudo-labels, rather than hard thresholds, are shown to yield better calibration and downstream performance.

## Results

On the AV2 Occupancy Forecasting leaderboard, TriO achieves the best L1 depth error (1.82 m) and AbsRel (10.63%), outperforming DIO (2.11 m, 11.00%) and 4D-Occ (3.22 m, 19.00%). After fine-tuning on bounding-box labels, TriO reaches mAP 63.3 and Soft-IoU 31.9 on AV2, surpassing UnO (59.0, 29.2) and DIO (57.6, 26.2). On the Long Range RADAR dataset at 200 m+, TriO achieves mAP 42.6 and Soft-IoU 21.4, a 6.4 AP improvement over the strongest baseline DIO (36.2, 13.8), demonstrating the benefit of RADAR at long range. TriO also requires only 10² labeled examples to match the performance of baselines trained on 10⁴ examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriO outperforms all three baselines (Implicit-O, UnO, DIO) on mAP and Soft-IoU across every evaluation setting. On AV2, TriO leads UnO by 4.3 mAP and 2.7 Soft-IoU, and DIO by 5.7 mAP and 5.7 Soft-IoU. The one metric where TriO trails is AV2 EPE (1.97 vs. DIO's 1.43), which the authors attribute to the absence of RADAR data in AV2 during pre-training. On LRR at 200 m+, the gap widens dramatically: TriO achieves 42.6 mAP vs. DIO's 36.2 and Soft-IoU 21.4 vs. 13.8, confirming that RADAR's longer effective range is critical for far-range occupancy. In the LiDAR forecasting benchmark (Table 3), TriO achieves the best L1 (1.82 m) and AbsRel (10.63%) on the AV2 leaderboard, beating DIO (2.11 m, 11.00%) and 4D-Occ (3.22 m, 19.00%), though DIO retains a slight edge on NFCD (0.85 vs. 0.93). For zero-shot anything perception on STU, TriO's combined soft labels (CSL) achieve 44.6 mIoU and 79.9% recall, surpassing all open-vocabulary baselines (best: Patchwork++ at 42.1 mIoU, 62.8% recall) and significantly outperforming text-prompted methods like MCPP+SAM (22.2 mIoU, 51.8% recall).

## Relation to Prior Work

TriO extends the unsupervised implicit occupancy line (UnO, DiO, GASP) by replacing LiDAR-only self-supervision with a tri-modal scheme where camera, LiDAR, and RADAR each serve as both input and supervision source. It departs from open-vocabulary occupancy methods (QueryOcc, AGO, SAL) that distill VLM pseudo-labels via text prompts, instead proposing a purely geometric obstacle/ground segmentation (Metric3Dv2 depth+normals fused with Patchwork++ LiDAR patches) that requires no image-text pairs or class vocabulary. The architecture reuses DiO's deformable-attention multi-resolution header and voxel-attention fusion, while the RADAR Doppler flow supervision and the confidence-driven image/LiDAR label fusion are novel contributions not present in prior occupancy or segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple geometric criterion (is this point above the local ground plane?) can outperform all evaluated VLM-based open-vocabulary segmenters on rare, safety-critical objects, suggesting that the field's heavy investment in text-prompted segmentation may be misaligned with the actual requirement of autonomous driving (path-blocking vs. traversable). The two-orders-of-magnitude sample-efficiency gain during fine-tuning and the RADAR-driven long-range improvement (6.4 AP at 200m+) have direct practical implications for scalable SDV deployment. Open questions include whether RADAR can provide occupancy supervision beyond line-of-sight assumptions, whether the single-frame camera limitation on flow can be addressed with temporal camera features, and whether larger dedicated benchmarks for anything-perception are needed to validate these findings beyond STU's 22 sequences.
