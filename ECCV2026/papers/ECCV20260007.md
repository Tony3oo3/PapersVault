## Problem

Autoregressive decoding in document OCR introduces sequential latency that scales linearly with output length and amplifies error propagation in long, structured documents. More fundamentally, the left-to-right causal factorization forces models to rely on linguistic priors for token prediction rather than visual evidence, causing semantic hallucinations when visual signals are weak or document semantics are disrupted. The paper argues that this causal ordering is an artifact of serialization rather than an intrinsic property of the OCR task.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB document image at native resolution as input, encoded via a vision encoder into visual tokens that condition the diffusion process. The output is a unified 1D token sequence over a shared vocabulary encompassing text symbols, layout markers, table delimiters, and mathematical operators, effectively encoding paragraphs, tables, formulas, and reading order in a single sequential interface. The sequence is partitioned into contiguous blocks of size 32 for the diffusion decoder, and a dynamic confidence threshold (τ_decode) controls how many tokens are confirmed per denoising step at inference time.

## Method

MinerU-Diffusion replaces autoregressive sequential decoding with block-wise masked diffusion denoising under visual conditioning, formulated as inverse rendering of a structured token sequence. The architecture (built on SDAR-1.7B-Chat-b32) factorizes the posterior as coarse-grained autoregression across blocks with parallel diffusion refinement within each block, using a structured attention mask that allows full attention within a block and causal attention to preceding blocks only, reducing complexity from O(L²) to O(BL'²) and enabling KV-cache reuse. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 establishes foundational representations on a large diverse dataset, then Stage 2 performs uncertainty-driven boundary refinement by mining hard samples via multi-pass inference consistency scores and re-annotating them with expert labels, with adaptive sample weighting emphasizing high-uncertainty regions. At inference, a dynamic decoding schedule adaptively confirms tokens based on a confidence threshold, trading off parallelism against accuracy in a controllable manner.

## Results

On OmniDocBench v1.5 (fully automatic, w/o GT Layout), MinerU-Diffusion achieves an Overall score of 88.94, competitive with dots.ocr (88.41) and MonkeyOCR-pro-3B (88.85) but trailing the top AR systems PaddleOCR-VL (92.56) and MinerU2.5 (90.67). With GT Layout, MinerU-Diffusion reaches 93.37 Overall, nearly matching MinerU2.5 (93.44) and PaddleOCR-VL (93.91). In terms of efficiency, MinerU-Diffusion achieves 98.32 TPS (dynamic decoding, τ=0.97) versus 51.46 TPS for MinerU2.5 at comparable accuracy, yielding a ~2× speedup, and up to 3.2× peak acceleration at lower thresholds while maintaining >90% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest AR baselines, MinerU-Diffusion (2.5B) trails PaddleOCR-VL (0.9B) by ~3.6 points in Overall (w/o GT: 88.94 vs 92.56) and MinerU2.5 (1.2B) by ~1.7 points (88.94 vs 90.67), with the gap narrowing to <0.1 points under GT Layout (93.37 vs 93.44). On table recognition (OCRBench v2), MinerU-Diffusion scores 81.18 TEDS, below MinerU2.5 (87.13) and dots.ocr (82.04) but above GPT-4o (70.51); on formula recognition (UniMER-Test), it reaches 91.6 CPE, competitive with dots.ocr (86.8) but below MinerU2.5 (96.6). The key differentiator is efficiency: at matched accuracy (~93% Overall w/ GT), MinerU-Diffusion delivers 98.32 TPS versus 51.46 TPS for MinerU2.5 (≈2×) and 40.77 TPS for PaddleOCR-VL (≈2.4×), with peak throughput of 164.8 TPS at τ=0.6. On the Semantic Shuffle benchmark, MinerU-Diffusion maintains near-constant accuracy across increasing word-shuffle distortion levels, whereas AR baselines (including MinerU2.5 and PaddleOCR-VL) degrade sharply, confirming reduced reliance on linguistic priors. Overall, MinerU-Diffusion trades a modest accuracy gap (1–4 points in fully automatic mode) for substantially faster parallel decoding and improved robustness to semantic disruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions document OCR from an autoregressive sequence-generation problem to an inverse-rendering problem solvable by parallel diffusion denoising, drawing on the block-diffusion architecture of SDAR and the block-wise attention factorization from Arriola et al. It inherits the visual encoder and overall pipeline from MinerU2.5 but replaces the causal LM decoder with a block-wise masked diffusion decoder, arguing that left-to-right serialization is an implementation artifact rather than an intrinsic property of document structure. The two-stage uncertainty-driven curriculum learning strategy is introduced to address the known training instability and data-inefficiency of any-order diffusion models, and the Semantic Shuffle benchmark is proposed as a diagnostic to separate true visual reading from language-prior completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.2× decoding speedup and the demonstration that diffusion-based OCR is far less sensitive to semantic disruption than AR-based OCR offer a concrete path toward faster, more visually grounded document parsing at scale. However, MinerU-Diffusion still trails the strongest AR systems (PaddleOCR-VL, MinerU2.5) by 2–4 points in fully automatic parsing, and the authors themselves identify layout understanding as the primary remaining bottleneck. Open questions include whether the accuracy gap can be closed with larger diffusion models or better layout priors, whether the Semantic Shuffle diagnostic generalizes beyond the 112-image English subset, and whether the block-diffusion approach scales gracefully to very long multi-page documents without the positional drift that full-attention diffusion suffers.
