## Problem

Existing VLA models learn a tightly coupled 'Sense-to-Act' mapping that is brittle under out-of-distribution shifts and offers no explicit interface for human correction when perception or grounding fails. Although recent embodied Chain-of-Thought approaches expose intermediate reasoning, the reasoning process remains self-contained and cannot be steered by human spatial cues, leaving a gap for resolving visual ambiguities or recovering from mistakes in real deployment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a primary external RGB view, a wrist-mounted RGB view, a natural language instruction, robot proprioceptive state, and an optional 2D spatial prior (a single affordance point, a bounding box, or an ordered trace of waypoints) specified on the primary camera view. Spatial priors are serialized into the VLM's native relative-coordinate token space and concatenated with the text instruction, so the same backbone handles both guided and unguided inference without a separate interaction branch. The output is a three-segment autoregressive reasoning sequence (task decomposition, visual grounding, robot motion sketch) whose hidden states are passed to a Flow-Matching head that emits continuous end-effector pose action chunks at a higher control frequency than the VLM update rate.

## Method

GTA-VLA decomposes policy inference into three asynchronous stages: Guide injects optional spatial priors as coordinate tokens into the observation stream; Think uses a Qwen3-VL-2B backbone to generate a structured spatial-visual CoT (task CoT → vision CoT → robot CoT) explicitly conditioned on the spatial prior, producing latent reasoning states H_reasoning; Act employs a Flow-Matching action head that cross-attends to the latest cached H_reasoning while consuming high-frequency control observations (wrist image, proprioception) to predict continuous action chunks. Training is two-stage: first the VLM backbone is trained on the 306K-episode Interact-306K dataset with automatically synthesized spatial-CoT supervision (keyframe extraction, open-vocabulary grounding, end-effector projection, and stochastic noise perturbation of spatial annotations), then the Flow-Matching head is trained to map reasoning states to actions, followed by joint fine-tuning on domain-specific data (e.g., BridgeData V2). The key design insight is treating spatial priors not as post-hoc corrections but as optional conditioning signals that the reasoning process directly incorporates, enabling the same model to operate fully autonomously or accept one-shot human guidance for failure recovery.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, GTA-VLA achieves a 98.6% average success rate across Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long suites, matching or slightly exceeding the strongest baselines (X-VLA* at 98.1%, π0.5 at 96.9%). On the real-to-sim SimplerEnv (WidowX) benchmark, GTA-VLA reaches 81.2% average success, outperforming the next-best method X-VLA* (76.0%) by 5.2 points. Under OOD conditions in SimplerEnv-Plus, GTA-VLA maintains a 61.4% average success rate versus 52.3% for X-VLA and 7.3% for π0.5, with the largest relative gains under object-shift perturbations (58.3% unseen objects, 50.0% distractors).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTA-VLA outperforms all baselines on SimplerEnv (Bridge), where the gap is most pronounced: it beats X-VLA* by 5.2 points (81.2% vs 76.0%), Uni-VLA by 11.4 points (81.2% vs 69.8%), GR00T-N1 by 24.1 points (81.2% vs 57.1%), and π0 by 33.3 points (81.2% vs 47.9%). On LIBERO, GTA-VLA (98.6%) edges out X-VLA* (98.1%) and π0.5 (96.9%), and substantially surpasses Uni-VLA (94.8%), π0 (94.1%), and GR00T-N1 (93.9%). Under OOD conditions in SimplerEnv-Plus, GTA-VLA leads X-VLA by 9.1 points on average (61.4% vs 52.3%), with the largest margin on unseen-object generalization (58.3% vs 36.2%) and robot-state shift (79.2% vs 68.7%). Against CoT-based baselines (ThinkAct, CoT-VLA, MolmoAct), GTA-VLA achieves 12–17 point gains on LIBERO and 37–38 point gains on SimplerEnv, indicating that structured spatial-visual reasoning with explicit grounding cues is substantially more effective than free-form or latent CoT for manipulation. The only baseline not directly comparable on SimplerEnv is π0.5, which reports LIBERO-only results; on LIBERO, GTA-VLA still leads by 1.7 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: direct VLA policies (OpenVLA, π0, π0.5, X-VLA), embodied Chain-of-Thought reasoning (ECoT, MolmoAct, ThinkAct, CoT-VLA), and interactive visual prompting (SAM, T-Rex2, Ferret, Set-of-Mark). It departs from prior embodied CoT work by treating spatial priors (points, boxes, traces) not as post-hoc corrections but as optional conditioning signals that the reasoning process directly consumes, unifying human spatial intent with autonomous task planning in a single autoregressive sequence. The asynchronous slow-fast architecture (VLM reasoning at low frequency, Flow-Matching action head at high frequency) is a practical design choice that addresses the latency bottleneck of autoregressive CoT in closed-loop control, building on the dual-system pattern seen in π0 and GR00T N1 but with an explicit reasoning-state cache.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interactive VLA formulation offers a practical path toward deployable robot policies that are both autonomous and human-correctable without retraining, addressing a real gap in current VLA systems where failures are opaque and uncorrectable. The SimplerEnv-Plus benchmark and the Interact-306K dataset with its automated annotation pipeline lower the barrier for training and evaluating guided spatial reasoning at scale. A key limitation is that all reasoning and guidance operate in 2D image space, restricting geometric richness; extending to 3D grounding and automatic failure-triggered guidance (rather than human-initiated) are natural next steps that the authors identify but leave open.
