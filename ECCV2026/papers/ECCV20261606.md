## Problem

Existing text-to-3D scene generation methods suffer from geometric drift, scale ambiguity, and limited semantic interpretability: iterative inpainting pipelines accumulate distortion over multi-room scenes, panoramic-based approaches lack absolute metric alignment, and video diffusion models offer temporal consistency without explicit control over scene scale or camera motion. No prior framework preserves a globally consistent metric coordinate frame while simultaneously transferring object-level semantics and poses from text to the final 3D reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261606/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt describing an indoor scene and produces a fully navigable 3D Gaussian Splatting scene in absolute metric world coordinates, along with per-object 9D poses (position, orientation, scale), NYUv2-40 semantic instance segmentation, and the intermediate 360° cubemap panorama. A distinguishing property is that all outputs share the same global coordinate frame, so scenes can be progressively expanded by appending new rooms without any re-scaling or re-alignment step. The 3DGS reconstruction uses 338 total frames (42 per each of 4 NVS trajectories plus 170 sampled from the panorama) with ground-truth camera poses, eliminating the need for external pose estimation.

## Method

GuidedSceneGen follows a coarse-to-fine pipeline anchored by a Holodeck-generated 3D proxy layout that establishes the absolute world frame and provides depth, semantic, and object-attribute renderings. The panoramic generator is a cubemap-based multi-view latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2 backbone with inflated self/cross-attention across 6 faces and xyz-positional encoding), extended with a Multi-ControlNet containing separate depth and semantic branches initialized from the trained U-Net to enforce layout-aligned 360° synthesis. For unobserved regions, a collision-aware grid-based trajectory estimator defines sparse circular camera paths per quadrant, and a depth-guided bisection search optimizes the Stable Virtual Camera scale parameter by minimizing L1 error between predicted metric depth and proxy depth renderings, achieving 10× faster sampling than exhaustive exploration. Final 3DGS reconstruction directly uses the aligned camera poses and proxy depth as ground-truth supervision (combined with normal, k-NN, and photometric losses), enabling accurate transfer of 9D poses and semantic labels from the proxy to the reconstructed scene without any re-estimation.

## Results

On the full 3D scene generation evaluation (Table 2), GuidedSceneGen achieves the best MEt3R multi-view consistency score (0.024) and the highest user-study ratings across all three criteria: Perceptual Quality (3.03/5), 3D Consistency (3.05/5), and Overall Scene Quality (3.01/5). For panoramic image generation (Table 1), the method attains the highest CLIP score (32.48) indicating strong text-image alignment, while remaining competitive on quality metrics (Q-Align 4.54, CLIP-IQA+ 0.71, IS 3.26). Runtime is 0.75 h per scene, roughly 10× faster than WorldExplorer (7 h) and comparable to DreamScene360 (0.5 h) and LayerPano3D (0.2 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GuidedSceneGen outperforms all three 3D scene baselines on the MEt3R multi-view consistency metric (0.024 vs. 0.026 for DreamScene360, 0.031 for LayerPano3D, 0.033 for WorldExplorer) and dominates the user study by a wide margin, scoring 3.03–3.05/5 on PQ, 3DC, and SQ versus 1.35–2.44 for competitors. Against DreamScene360, the closest competitor, GuidedSceneGen gains roughly 0.6–0.7 points on every user-study axis while also improving MEt3R, at a modest runtime cost (0.75 h vs. 0.5 h). Compared to WorldExplorer, the method is approximately 10× faster (0.75 h vs. 7 h) thanks to optimized trajectory sampling, while WorldExplorer achieves a slightly higher Inception Score (2.27 vs. 2.22) but suffers from severe error accumulation reflected in the worst user ratings. For panoramic image generation, GuidedSceneGen leads on CLIP score (32.48 vs. 30.73–31.28) but trails Diffusion360 on Q-Align (4.54 vs. 4.80) and CLIP-IQA+ (0.71 vs. 0.77), and MVDiffusion on IS (3.26 vs. 3.46), indicating a trade-off between prompt alignment and raw perceptual quality. The ablation in Table 1 confirms that the Multi-ControlNet (depth + semantic) contributes the largest CLIP-score gain (+1.31 over text-only), validating the geometric and semantic guidance design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of panoramic scene generation (LayerPano3D, DreamScene360, WorldExplorer) and compositional layout-guided synthesis (SceneCraft, ControlRoom3D, SpatialGen), but departs from both in a key way: instead of using axis-aligned bounding boxes or relative-scale geometry as proxies, it leverages Holodeck's full 3D proxy layout to provide both semantic and geometric guidance at absolute metric scale throughout the entire pipeline. The panoramic generator builds on Cubediff's inflated-attention cubemap architecture and DreamCube's xyz-positional encoding, extended with a Multi-ControlNet for depth and semantic conditioning. For novel-view synthesis, it adapts Stable Virtual Camera's camera-controlled video diffusion with a bisection-search depth-alignment strategy, replacing the ground-truth comparison with proxy-depth supervision. The 3DGS reconstruction stage benefits from the fact that all camera poses are known a priori in a shared world frame, eliminating the error accumulation that plagues prior methods relying on estimated poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate 3D scenes in a consistent absolute metric coordinate frame with transferable 9D object poses and semantic labels opens the door to downstream applications in robotics, embodied AI, and AR that require physically meaningful geometry rather than just visual plausibility. The progressive scene expansion capability—where new rooms can be added without re-alignment or re-scaling—is practically important for building large, connected indoor environments. Limitations include dependence on Holodeck for proxy generation (constraining scene diversity), restriction to indoor scenes, and the relatively simple grid-based trajectory strategy that may not generalize to highly complex layouts. Open questions include whether the approach extends to outdoor environments, whether alternative proxy generators can increase diversity, and how well the pipeline scales to multi-story or very large scenes.
