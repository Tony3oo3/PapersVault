## Problem

Image-to-scan registration—aligning query images to a reference 3D point cloud that is not pre-aligned with any camera—remains unsolved in a unified, feed-forward manner. Existing methods either bake per-point image appearance into the 3D map, assume small pose offsets requiring accurate initialization, or are trained on a single sensor configuration, limiting cross-domain generalization. No prior architecture handles the full wide-baseline cross-modal gap (e.g., narrow-FOV camera vs. 360° LiDAR sweep) without pose initialization or per-point color in the reference scan.

## Input / Output

The model ingests up to N (typically ≤4) RGB images and a raw 3D point cloud with no color information; the scan is not assumed aligned to any input view. All geometric outputs are expressed in the first camera's coordinate frame and are up-to-scale, with a separate global metric scale predicted via a dedicated scale token. Beyond poses and point maps, the model outputs a three-level coarse-to-fine pyramid of cross-modal alignment features (24-dim per level) with per-element confidence weights for both the image and point cloud branches, which drive the differentiable refinement stage.

## Method

DRS-VPT encodes images with a frozen DINOv2 ViT-L and point clouds with Sonata (PointTransformerV3), then fuses tokens through a 24-layer transformer with alternating global cross-modal and modality-specific local attention, initialized from MapAnything and fine-tuned. A key architectural choice is a gated fusion that recovers high-resolution per-point features lost during the 5,000-token downsampling. The core refinement mechanism unrolls five Gauss-Newton iterations at each of three pyramid levels, minimizing a robust feature-metric reprojection loss between projected point-cloud features and bilinearly sampled image features in SE(3); this differentiable alignment replaces geometric point-matching (ICP/Kabsch) and reduces mean translation error by 5×. Learned per-point confidence weights implicitly suppress occluded back-facing scan points without explicit occlusion supervision, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end across 11 heterogeneous datasets with covisibility-based multi-view sampling and random scan perturbations.

## Results

On wide-baseline camera-LiDAR registration, DRS-VPT achieves 0.09 m / 0.34° mean translation/rotation error and 99.73% accuracy on KITTI, and 0.33 m / 0.67° with 97.76% accuracy on nuScenes, setting new state-of-the-art on both. On indoor 7Scenes relocalization (5 cm / 5° threshold), DRS-VPT attains 66.4% recall without scene-specific training, competitive with InLoc (66.3%) but below DSAC* (85.2%) and PixLoc (75.7%). In zero-shot transfer to unseen 12Scenes, the model achieves 53.6% overall recall, and in cross-dataset KITTI→nuScenes generalization it reaches 66.74% recall versus near-zero for single-dataset baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On camera-LiDAR registration (Table 1), DRS-VPT outperforms all four driving baselines on both KITTI and nuScenes: it reduces mean translation error from 0.17 m (TrafficLoc) to 0.09 m on KITTI and from 0.89 m (VP2P) to 0.33 m on nuScenes, while also achieving the highest accuracy (99.73% and 97.76% respectively). In cross-dataset generalization (Table 4), the advantage is dramatic—DRS-VPT achieves 0.538 m / 1.14° / 66.74% recall on nuScenes, whereas TrafficLoc and CoFiI2P (trained only on KITTI) collapse to ~17 m / ~95° / <3% recall. On indoor 7Scenes relocalization (Table 2), DRS-VPT (66.4% recall) matches InLoc (66.3%) but trails DSAC* (85.2%), PixLoc (75.7%), and ACE (75.6%), with the largest gap on the Stairs scene (27 cm / 3.26°) due to geometric aliasing from repetitive structures. The paper attributes this gap to the cross-modal nature of the task (RGB image vs. depth-derived point cloud) and the absence of scene-specific training, positioning DRS-VPT as a viable but not yet dominant alternative to image-domain relocalization systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRS-VPT extends the feed-forward visual geometry line (DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT, MapAnything) from purely image-domain multi-view geometry to cross-modal image-to-scan registration, repurposing MapAnything's DINOv2-based transformer backbone and adding a Sonata/PointTransformerV3 point-cloud encoder. It departs from prior camera-LiDAR calibration methods (DeepI2P, CorrI2P, VP2P, TrafficLoc) that use decoupled two-stage designs with limited cross-dataset generalization, and from indoor relocalization systems (PixLoc, InLoc, ACE) that require per-point image appearance baked into the 3D map. The key architectural departure is the unrolled differentiable reprojective feature-metric alignment stage, which replaces the geometric point-alignment (ICP/Kabsch) or correspondence-based PnP solvers used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A single model handling both wide-baseline camera-LiDAR calibration and indoor camera-to-map relocalization without scene-specific training or per-point color in the reference scan is practically significant for robotics and autonomous driving, where sensor configurations vary and maps are often geometry-only. The zero-shot transfer to 12Scenes (53.6% recall) and the catastrophic failure of single-dataset baselines on cross-domain evaluation (16–17 m error) underscore the value of multi-dataset foundational training. Open questions include extending to multi-scan or hierarchical map representations, handling dynamic/articulated objects, and closing the gap with scene-specific methods on in-domain benchmarks (7Scenes recall of 66.4% trails DSAC* at 85.2%).
