## Problem

Existing domain adaptive visual object tracking methods require continuous access to large-scale source domain data during adaptation, an assumption that breaks down under real-world constraints such as limited device memory, bandwidth restrictions, and data privacy regulations. This paper is the first to address source-free domain adaptation in the VOT community, asking whether a pre-trained tracker can be adapted to multiple unseen adverse-weather target domains using only unlabeled target samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261578/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only unlabeled target-domain video frames (no source data) along with an initial bounding box and a frozen pre-trained source model. Frames are split into 128×128 template and 256×256 search crops, processed by a ViT-Base backbone (16×16 patches, 768-dim features) with three inserted DIM modules. The output is a per-frame target bounding box and its trajectory, with the model operating at 91 FPS on a single A6000 GPU.

## Method

SFDATrack follows a mean-teacher pipeline where the teacher (EMA-updated) processes weakly augmented frames and the student processes strongly augmented frames. The Dual Interactive Mamba (DIM) module establishes bidirectional sequence interactions between the two branches by reversing search tokens, concatenating learnable prompt tokens, and passing them through a shared Mamba SSM with sigmoid gating, progressively narrowing cross-view domain discrepancies. The Hyperspherical Prototype Projection (HPP) module then projects pooled search features onto K learnable prototypes in a latent hyperspherical space via a Sinkhorn-Knopp soft assignment, enforcing balanced prototype usage. A dual alignment strategy combines Domain-Specific Alignment (matching per-domain student-teacher prototype distributions) with Domain-Invariant Alignment (aligning k-means-clustered cross-domain features), jointly optimized with the standard tracking supervision loss (classification + L1 + GIoU).

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks, SFDATrack achieves the best AO across all three GOT-10k weather variants (70.1 Foggy, 67.3 Dark, 72.1 Rainy) and the best AUC on DTB70-Rainy (67.22) and DTB70-Dark (66.18), outperforming the closest cross-domain baseline UMDATrack by 1.9–3.6% in AO. On real-world datasets, SFDATrack attains 56.78 AUC / 73.60 Precision on NAT2021, 61.22 AUC / 76.51 Precision on UAVDark70, and 60.87 AUC / 59.70 Precision on AVisT, surpassing UMDATrack by 2.20% AUC on NAT2021 and 1.17% AUC on UAVDark70. The tracker runs at 91 FPS with 30G MACs and 93M parameters, comparable in speed to ARTrackV2 (95 FPS) while using fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFDATrack is compared against 22 baselines spanning generic trackers (OSTrack, SeqTrack, ARTrackV2, UETrack, DropTrack, etc.) and cross-domain trackers (UMDATrack, LVPTrack, UDAT-CAR, DCPT, SAM-DA, MLKD-Track). The closest competitor is UMDATrack, the multi-domain mean-teacher tracker that still requires source data; SFDATrack outperforms it by 3.5% AO on GOT-10k-Foggy, 1.9% AO on GOT-10k-Dark, 3.6% AO on GOT-10k-Rainy, and by 2.20% AUC on NAT2021 and 1.17% AUC on UAVDark70. The one exception is DTB70-Foggy AUC, where SFDATrack (65.58) trails UMDATrack (66.21) by 0.63 points. Against the strongest generic trackers (ARTrackV2, UETrack, SeqTrack), SFDATrack leads by 4–6% AO on GOT-10k variants and by 3–8% AUC on real-world datasets, confirming that source-free adaptation with only target-domain data can surpass both generic and source-data-dependent cross-domain methods. On the challenging AVisT benchmark with diverse natural weather, SFDATrack achieves the best AUC (60.87) and Precision (59.70), edging out UMDATrack (60.50 / 59.01) and substantially outperforming all generic trackers.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFDATrack extends the domain adaptive visual object tracking (DAVOT) line of work—exemplified by UDAT and UMDATrack—which relies on both annotated source and unlabeled target data during adaptation, by removing the source-data dependency entirely. It draws on the source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) paradigm previously explored for static recognition tasks (classification, detection) and adapts its mean-teacher self-training framework to the temporally coherent tracking setting. Architecturally, the method integrates Mamba's selective state-space modeling (Gu & Dao, 2023) into a bidirectional interactive module and borrows the hyperspherical prototype projection and Sinkhorn-based soft assignment from Mettes et al. (2019) and Caron et al. (2020), while retaining OSTrack's template-search tracking backbone. The key conceptual departure is that prior DAVOT methods assume perpetual source-data availability, whereas SFDATrack operates under the stricter constraint that only the pre-trained model weights and unlabeled target samples are accessible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically critical gap: in edge-deployed tracking systems (autonomous driving, UAV surveillance), source data is often unavailable due to storage, bandwidth, or privacy constraints, yet prior domain-adaptive trackers all require it. The finding that a source-free tracker can outperform source-available baselines (e.g., UMDATrack) challenges the assumption that source data is necessary for effective cross-weather adaptation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond weather-induced shifts to other domain gaps (sensor changes, seasonal variation, synthetic-to-real), and how temporal drift from accumulated pseudo-label noise scales over very long sequences. The method's reliance on a pre-trained source model also raises questions about cold-start scenarios where no suitable pre-trained tracker exists.
