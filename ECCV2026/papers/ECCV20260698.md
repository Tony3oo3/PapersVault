## Problem

Remote sensing change detection requires global contextual reasoning to suppress pseudo changes from illumination and seasonal variations, but attention-based models incur quadratic computational complexity that is prohibitive for UAV and satellite deployment. Linear-complexity alternatives (linear attention, state-space models) reduce asymptotic cost but require 2D-to-sequence reshaping and heavy auxiliary spatio-temporal modules, undermining their practical efficiency advantage. The paper asks whether a fully convolutional architecture can deliver genuine global context at linear cost without such architectural overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260698/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of co-registered bi-temporal remote sensing images (3×H×W each), cropped into 256×256 patches following the OpenCD protocol, and outputs a dense pixel-wise change probability map of shape H×W that is thresholded to a binary change mask. It is evaluated on both optical RGB imagery (LEVIR-CD, WHU-CD, LEVIR-CD+, SYSU-CD) and Sentinel-1 SAR imagery (SAR-CD), with the same architecture handling both modalities without modality-specific modifications.

## Method

ChangeGCC is a fully convolutional, linear-time framework built around two components. The hierarchical backbone uses Globally Conditioned Convolution (GCC), which replaces fixed spatial kernels with dynamically assembled kernels: a compact global descriptor (GAP of a projected feature map) drives sigmoid-gated routing coefficients over a set of learnable kernel bases, so that local aggregation is conditioned on scene-level statistics without any token-to-token attention. The Dual-Temporal Fusion (DTF) module then performs (b1) intra-frame cross-scale aggregation—upsampling all four pyramid levels, fusing them through a GCC block, and projecting back to each scale—and (b2) inter-frame cross-temporal interaction, where symmetric channel and spatial gating (computed via GAP/GMP and channel-wise pooling) lets each timestamp's features modulate the other's, highlighting genuine structural changes while suppressing pseudo variations. A lightweight UNet-like decoder aggregates the fused multi-scale features into the final change map, and the model is trained end-to-end with a combined BCE + Dice loss (λ=1) over 200 epochs using Adam with cosine scheduling.

## Results

On LEVIR-CD, ChangeGCC-B achieves 85.28% IoU and 92.06% F1 with only 13.60M parameters and 26.49G FLOPs, outperforming ChangeMamba (84.27% IoU at 84.70M params, 44.86G FLOPs) and ChangeTitans (84.36% IoU at 27.15M params, 30.39G FLOPs). On WHU-CD, ChangeGCC-B reaches 89.95% IoU and 94.71% F1. Across LEVIR-CD+ (75.92% IoU), SYSU-CD (71.54% IoU), and SAR-CD (97.21% IoU), ChangeGCC-B consistently achieves the best IoU among all compared methods while using substantially fewer parameters and FLOPs than attention- and SSM-based counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChangeGCC-B outperforms all 42 compared baselines on IoU across all five benchmarks. On LEVIR-CD, it surpasses the closest competitors S2ENet (84.61%) and MEDS-CD (84.77%) by 0.51 and 0.51 percentage points respectively, while using roughly half the parameters of S2ENet and one-third of MEDS-CD. On LEVIR-CD+, ChangeGCC-B (75.92%) edges out ChangeTitans (75.84%) and ChangeCLIP (75.63%) with only 13.60M params versus 27.15M and undisclosed. On SYSU-CD, it leads ChangeTitans (71.18%) and MGCR-Net (70.80%) by 0.36 and 0.74 points. On SAR-CD, ChangeGCC-B (97.21%) substantially outperforms M-CD (95.76%) and ChangeTitans (95.63%) while using 5–6× fewer parameters. The lightweight ChangeGCC-T variant (6.34M params, 13.60G FLOPs) already matches or exceeds many heavier baselines, e.g., surpassing ChangeMamba (84.70M params) by 0.58 IoU on LEVIR-CD with only 7.5% of its parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChangeGCC carves new ground by repurposing the dynamic-convolution / content-adaptive-kernel paradigm (from large-kernel CNNs and frequency-dynamic convolution) into the remote sensing change detection setting, where prior work has relied on either fixed-kernel CNNs (limited receptive fields) or attention/SSM-based global modeling (quadratic or sequence-reshaping overhead). The Globally Conditioned Convolution block draws directly on the idea that a compact global descriptor can steer local aggregation, but applies it hierarchically across a four-level feature pyramid rather than at a single resolution. The Dual-Temporal Fusion module departs from standard Siamese differencing or concatenation by introducing symmetric, bidirectional channel-and-spatial gating between timestamps, a design not previously explored in RSCD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully convolutional, linear-time architecture can match or exceed attention- and SSM-based change detectors while using roughly 6× fewer parameters and substantially lower FLOPs, which is practically significant for UAV and satellite deployment where compute budgets are tight. The ablations confirm that global conditioning of kernels (GCC) and symmetric cross-temporal mutual modulation are both necessary, ruling out the simpler hypothesis that larger fixed kernels or one-directional temporal fusion suffice. Open questions the authors flag include weakly-supervised training, adaptation to diverse sensing modalities beyond optical and SAR, and real-time inference on embedded hardware—areas where the current supervised, patch-based evaluation protocol does not yet provide evidence.
