## Problem

Existing token compression methods for omnimodal LLMs rely on fixed window partitioning and attention-based pruning, treating multimodal tokens as flat, uniformly structured sequences. This positional partitioning fragments semantically coherent units at arbitrary boundaries and becomes fragile under aggressive token reduction, because sparse attention alone cannot preserve transition-critical tokens that carry structural importance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261697/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DASH operates as a training-free plugin between modality encoders and the LLM, consuming the encoded audio token sequence (50 tokens per time window) and video token sequence (288 tokens per time window, up to 768 frames for VideoMME or 128 for other benchmarks) from Qwen2.5-Omni. It produces a compressed multimodal token sequence at a target retention ratio (e.g., 25% or 35%) that is passed to the LLM for standard text generation, with the entire boundary-detection and scoring pipeline adding less than 40 ms of overhead.

## Method

DASH detects semantic boundaries in the audio stream by computing cosine similarity between adjacent audio embeddings and flagging positions where similarity drops below a threshold (τ=0.4) subject to a minimum chunk constraint (~1 s of audio). These audio boundaries are projected onto video token indices via linear temporal-ratio scaling, then refined by a greedy strength-based insertion that prioritizes the highest-probability boundaries while enforcing a minimum segment length of 2K tokens. Within each resulting variable-length segment, token retention is determined by a tri-signal fusion score that weights boundary probability (0.4), multi-scale Gaussian density-based uniqueness (0.3), and normalized attention (0.3), mitigating the heavy-tailed sparsity of attention-only selection. Video compression within each segment uses interleaved spatial-temporal pruning (DPC-KNN for even frames, temporal similarity for odd frames) with an adaptive retention ratio modulated by the audio retention rate of the corresponding segment, plus mild boundary-neighborhood retention as a conservative safeguard.

## Results

On AVUT + VideoMME (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B), DASH at 35% retention achieves 98.2% normalized average accuracy, surpassing OmniZip (97.0%) and DyCoke (93.9%). At a more aggressive 25% retention, DASH still reaches 97.2% normalized average (60.9% AVUT avg, 66.0% VideoMME), matching or exceeding OmniZip at 35% while using only 20% FLOPs. On WorldSense (7B), DASH at 25% achieves 44.9% average accuracy with 14.9T FLOPs, outperforming OmniZip at 35% (44.7%, 21.4T FLOPs). Inference efficiency reaches 3.5× prefill speedup (7B) and 3.8× (3B) with 1.7× end-to-end latency reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DASH consistently outperforms all three baselines across both model scales. Against OmniZip (the strongest prior omnimodal method), DASH at 35% retention improves normalized average accuracy from 97.0% to 98.2% (7B) and from 96.6% to 98.2% (3B); at 25% retention, DASH matches or exceeds OmniZip's 35% performance (97.2% vs 97.0% on 7B; 96.6% vs 96.6% on 3B) while using ~30% fewer FLOPs. Against DyCoke, DASH gains 4.3–4.5 percentage points in normalized average at 35% retention (98.2% vs 93.9%/94.9%). Against FastV, DASH leads by 8.6–9.6 percentage points (98.2% vs 89.6%/92.8%), and FastV suffers OOM on VideoMME for the 7B model. On WorldSense, DASH at 25% (44.9% 7B, 44.6% 3B) surpasses OmniZip at 35% (44.7%, 44.1%) with 30–32% fewer FLOPs, and leads DyCoke at 50% retention (44.6%, 44.0%) by 0.3–0.6 points while using roughly half the compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

DASH extends the omnimodal token compression line initiated by OmniZip, which first demonstrated that audio attention can guide video pruning but still relied on fixed-size window partitioning. The dynamic boundary detection mechanism is directly inspired by H-Net's cosine-similarity routing for hierarchical sequence modeling, repurposed here as a training-free segmentation cue on audio encoder embeddings. The spatial pruning component adapts DPC-KNN density-peak clustering with a multi-scale Gaussian kernel, and the overall pipeline retains OmniZip's interleaved spatial-temporal compression strategy while replacing its rigid grouping with content-adaptive segments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling substantially more aggressive compression (25% vs. 35% retention) for omnimodal LLMs without accuracy loss, which directly reduces memory and latency for real-time audio-video applications. A key open question is whether audio-derived boundaries remain reliable when audio-visual alignment is poor (e.g., background music over unrelated visuals, or silent video segments), since the method assumes temporal co-registration provides a useful proxy. The framework is also currently validated only on the Qwen2.5-Omni family; generalization to other OmniLLM architectures with different encoder designs or tokenization schemes remains untested.
