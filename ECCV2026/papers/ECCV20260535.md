## Problem

Controllable video generation struggles with two practical bottlenecks: sparse human-provided motion hints (e.g., 2D arrows or drag maps) collapse to too few effective tokens after patchification and early attention, weakening guidance; and diffusion backbones inherently bias toward pixel-level texture reconstruction over structural motion, producing photorealistic but physically incoherent interactions. The paper targets rigid-body collision scenarios where plausibility depends on correct interaction timing, motion continuity, and mass-dependent outcomes, yet existing methods cannot reliably control these aspects from coarse, human-editable cues.

## Input / Output

The method takes a keyframe image, per-instance segmentation masks (e.g., from SAM), a coarse 2D arrow per instance encoding initial velocity direction and magnitude, a scalar mass value per instance, and an optional scalar depth delta (Δz) per arrow, plus a text prompt. These sparse inputs are rasterized into a dense 4-channel (flow u, v, Δz, mass) pixel-aligned control field over each instance mask before patchification. The model outputs a 512×512, 49-frame, 16 FPS RGB video jointly with an auxiliary structural map (depth or segmentation) of matching spatio-temporal resolution, generated in ~80 s (RGB-only) or ~120 s (joint) on a single A100.

## Method

STANCE introduces three coupled components on top of a fine-tuned CogVideoX-1.5 (5B) DiT backbone. First, Instance Cues convert sparse per-object hints into a dense 2.5D camera-relative motion field by averaging optical flow over each instance mask and appending a monocular depth delta as a third channel, disambiguating in-plane vs. out-of-plane motion under camera movement. Second, Dense RoPE extracts only the nonzero control sites, enforces a fixed token budget N (uniformly subsampling or tiling as needed), and tags each selected motion token with its first-frame rotary positional embedding so spatial identity persists across all frames, preventing the token dilution that plagues naive 2D-map injection for small or thin objects. Third, a joint auxiliary stream duplicates the video token sequence so the DiT simultaneously predicts RGB and a structural map (depth or segmentation) under shared spatio-temporal tokens and the same cue tokens; the structural head acts as a 'geometry witness' that penalizes geometric drift in the RGB stream. Training uses a standard diffusion loss with a weighted auxiliary term (λ_aux) on 200k Kubric-rendered rigid-body interaction clips, with the base tokenizer and text encoder frozen.

## Results

On a 200-clip held-out set (100 simple + 100 composite rigid-body interaction scenes), STANCE-5B achieves a Physics-IQ of 47.62 and FVD of 50.74, outperforming all five baselines on both metrics. The smaller STANCE-2B variant also surpasses every baseline, reaching Physics-IQ 45.90 and FVD 54.97. Against the trajectory-conditioned Wan-Move-14B (a 14B backbone), STANCE-5B matches Physics-IQ (47.62 vs. 47.50) while using roughly one-third the parameters, though Wan-Move attains a lower FVD (45.78 vs. 50.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STANCE-5B outperforms all five baselines on both Physics-IQ and FVD. The largest gap is over SG-I2V (+32.2 Physics-IQ, −62.8 FVD) and Drag-Anything (+22.8 Physics-IQ, −42.0 FVD), which rely on self-guided or entity-representation controls that lack explicit per-instance motion cues. MoFA-Video and MotionPro offer per-object targeting but suffer from appearance drift and identity leakage over longer sequences; STANCE's joint RGB+structure training under shared instance cues mitigates these failures, yielding a +16.2 and +16.0 Physics-IQ advantage respectively. The closest competitor, VLIPP, uses vision-and-language physical priors via prompts rather than pixel-aligned cues, leaving spatial and metric ambiguities; STANCE-5B still leads by +11.2 Physics-IQ and −7.2 FVD. In a separate comparison against the trajectory-conditioned Wan-Move-14B, STANCE-5B matches Physics-IQ (47.62 vs. 47.50) at roughly one-third the parameter count, though Wan-Move's larger backbone gives it a modest FVD edge (45.78 vs. 50.74). A user study (87 participants, 30 videos) further confirms the quantitative gap: 83.3% of raters preferred STANCE for physical plausibility and 72.3% for control faithfulness over MotionPro and VLIPP.

## Relation to Prior Work

STANCE builds on the CogVideoX-1.5 DiT backbone and positions itself within the motion-conditioned video generation line (MoFA-Video, MotionPro, Drag-Anything, VLIPP), but departs from these by replacing 2D drag/arrow signals with depth-augmented 2.5D Instance Cues and a token-dense injection mechanism (Dense RoPE) that preserves spatial addressability after patchify. It contrasts with trajectory-based methods (IntraGen, Wan-Move) by encoding only sparse initial conditions (direction, speed, mass) rather than full frame-level paths, leaving the DiT to roll out physically plausible dynamics. The joint RGB + structural (depth/segmentation) stream sharing tokens and attention is a new architectural choice not present in prior controllable video DiTs, functioning as a geometry witness that regularizes the RGB head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored failure mode—sparse control tokens being diluted by tokenization and early attention—which is likely to affect any control-signal injection into DiT-based video models, making Dense RoPE a potentially reusable technique beyond rigid-body scenes. The depth-vs-segmentation tradeoff on small objects (depth wins on regular scenes, segmentation wins on thin/tiny targets) offers a useful practical guideline for auxiliary-stream design. However, the method is limited to rigid-body interactions, validated primarily on synthetic Kubric/GSO data with only brief real-world demos, and the 5B model matching a 14B trajectory-based baseline on Physics IQ is a fair comparison only in the sense that the input modalities differ fundamentally (sparse cues vs. full trajectories), leaving open whether the approach scales to non-rigid deformations, longer horizons, or more complex multi-body contacts.
