## Problem

Training conditional diffusion models (CDMs) for target-domain data augmentation in medical UDA requires conditional labels, but only noisy pseudo-labels from a source-trained segmenter are available. Naively conditioning the CDM on a single Arg-Max pseudo-mask propagates label errors into generation and degrades downstream segmentation, while vanilla uncertainty-based filtering either discards too much data or admits too much noise under severe domain shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261207/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes source-domain labeled images (to train an initial segmenter), unlabeled target-domain images, and a segmentation mask as the CDM's conditioning input. During training, the segmenter's per-pixel softmax distribution is decomposed into ranked pseudo-label maps (Arg-Max, Arg-2nd, …) that serve as multi-channel conditions for the score network; at inference, the trained CDM accepts a single mask (e.g., from the source domain) and generates a corresponding target-style image, producing unlimited labeled synthetic pairs. A Bezier-curve intensity transformation is applied as a preprocessing step to align source and target appearance before pseudo-label generation.

## Method

The core contribution is an uncertainty-guided score-matching objective (UGSM) that replaces the standard denoising score matching loss: instead of conditioning on one Arg-Max pseudo-mask, the segmenter's softmax output is expanded into K ranked pseudo-label maps, each yielding a separate conditional score estimate s_θ(x_t, y=k, t), which are then aggregated via a pixel-wise Hadamard product with the corresponding softmax confidence to form a single reweighted score. A minimum-MSE convex-combination theorem (Theorem 1) proves that weighting conditional scores by the per-pixel label distribution is the optimal aggregation, justifying the softmax-weighted scheme over Arg-Max collapse. In practice, K=2 (Arg-Max and Arg-2nd) suffices, and a high-confidence collapse rule overwrites higher-rank labels with Arg-Max where confidence exceeds a threshold δ, avoiding unnecessary complexity in well-predicted regions. A supporting Bezier adaptation module fits a low-degree-of-freedom cubic Bezier intensity curve via Nelder-Mead optimization in a pretrained feature space, reducing appearance gaps and improving the quality of pseudo-labels and uncertainty maps that feed the CDM training. The resulting CDM generates labeled target-style image-mask pairs that are plugged into existing UDA pipelines (ADVENT, GenericSSL, pseudo-labeling) without architectural changes.

## Results

On BraTS 2023 (T2→T1CE), Ours-PL achieves 58.16% average Dice, surpassing the strongest baseline FPL+ (47.17%) by over 11 points and the no-adaptation baseline (14.96%) by nearly 44 points. On MM-WHS (MRI→CT), Ours-GS reaches 90.14% average Dice, approaching the fully supervised upper bound of 88.95% (actually exceeding it), while on the reverse CT→MRI direction Ours-GS achieves 70.39% vs. GenericSSL's 67.48%. On Abdominal Multi-Organ (MRI→CT), Ours-PL attains 85.86% average Dice, outperforming FPL+ (75.85%) by 10 points and ADVENT (44.08%) by over 41 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPDiff-UDA consistently outperforms all 9 baselines across all three datasets and both adaptation directions. On BraTS T1CE, Ours-PL (58.16%) beats the closest competitor FPL+ (47.17%) by 11.0 points and GenericSSL (45.52%) by 12.6 points; on BraTS T1, Ours-AD (53.31%) leads FPL+ (49.49%) by 3.8 points. On MM-WHS MRI→CT, Ours-GS (90.14%) is competitive with GenericSSL (90.48%) but clearly surpasses FPL+ (80.35%) and ADVENT (82.82%); on the harder CT→MRI direction, Ours-GS (70.39%) beats GenericSSL (67.48%) by 2.9 points. On Multi-Organ MRI→CT, Ours-PL (85.86%) dominates FPL+ (75.85%) by 10 points and ADVENT (44.08%) by 41.8 points. GAN-based methods (CycleGAN, CyCADA, PSIGAN) and Diff-style perform substantially worse, confirming that style-transfer-based augmentation struggles with tumor regions and sparse anatomical structures under large modality gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active threads: (1) GAN-based and diffusion-based style transfer for UDA in medical segmentation (CycleGAN, CyCADA, Diff-style, GenericSSL), (2) pseudo-label self-training where noisy target predictions are used to bootstrap adaptation, and (3) score-based conditional diffusion models trained with denoising score matching. The key departure is in the CDM training objective: rather than conditioning on a single Arg-Max pseudo-mask (as in Diff-style or Diffuse-UDA) or discarding uncertain pixels (as in semi-supervised confidence filtering), the authors treat the segmenter's full softmax as a per-pixel label distribution and aggregate multiple ranked pseudo-label channels via confidence-weighted score matching. The Bezier-curve adaptation module replaces the heavier GAN-based translation step with a low-degree-of-freedom intensity transform, positioning it as a lightweight pre-alignment rather than a full generative style transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (only the training loss changes, not the diffusion architecture) makes the uncertainty-guided score matching easy to integrate into existing UDA pipelines, and the minimum-MSE convex-aggregation theorem gives practitioners a principled reason to retain the full softmax rather than collapsing to hard labels. However, the approach's effectiveness hinges on the segmenter's softmax being a reasonable surrogate for true label uncertainty; under severe miscalibration or extreme domain shift, the surrogate distribution may be poorly calibrated, and the paper acknowledges this in its supplementary analysis. Open questions include whether the Top-2 approximation generalizes to tasks with many fine-grained classes, whether the 1D Bezier intensity transform suffices for structural (not just photometric) domain shifts, and how the method behaves when the source-trained segmenter is near-random on the target domain.
