## Problem

Video diffusion models achieve strong per-frame fidelity but produce jittery, ghosted, or physically implausible motion because the standard denoising MSE objective provides no explicit supervision on temporal consistency. Existing remedies—external motion priors, motion-aware co-training losses, and RL-based post-training with vision-language reward models—either constrain open-ended generation, require heavy fine-tuning, or evaluate only a handful of sampled frames and thus fail to capture fine-grained temporal dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates a video clip via a 3-step distilled Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B generator (12 latent chunks ≈ 49 pixel-space frames). During training, a frozen RAFT estimator converts each decoded clip into a dense (T−1)×2×H×W optical-flow stack, which is augmented with a per-pixel flow-magnitude channel to form a 3-channel motion tensor consumed by the discriminator. Inference remains a standard 3-step ODE solve with no additional inputs or post-processing.

## Method

MoGAN introduces a DiT-based motion discriminator that operates exclusively on optical-flow sequences, learning to distinguish real from generated motion in a dense, appearance-agnostic space. The generator is updated with two complementary objectives: a logistic GAN loss in flow space (with R1/R2 regularization on the discriminator for stability) that teaches realistic dynamics, and a DMD distribution-matching loss that anchors the student to the teacher's appearance and text alignment. Training alternates between DMD regularization and MoGAN updates on a 3-step distilled model, using truncated backpropagation through the chunk-recurrent VAE decoder, gradient checkpointing, and chunk subsampling to keep memory bounded. The discriminator backbone reuses the first 16 layers of the pretrained Wan2.1 DiT with lightweight multiscale prediction heads at layers 7, 13, and 15, and is conditioned on a fixed timestep t*=0 and a fixed motion-descriptive prompt to focus purely on motion realism.

## Results

On VBench, MoGAN (3-step) achieves a Motion Score of 0.973 and Dynamics Degree of 0.96, surpassing the 50-step Wan2.1 baseline (0.905 / 0.83) by +7.3% and the 3-step DMD baseline (0.859 / 0.73) by +13.3%. On VideoJAM-Bench, MoGAN reaches a Motion Score of 0.983 and Dynamics Degree of 0.98, improving +7.4% over Wan2.1 and +8.8% over DMD. Aesthetic and Image Quality scores are maintained or slightly improved (e.g., Aesthetic 0.59 vs. 0.57 for Wan2.1 on VBench), confirming that motion gains do not come at the cost of visual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 50-step Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B teacher, MoGAN wins decisively on motion: Dynamics Degree improves from 0.83 to 0.96 on VBench (+15.7% relative) and from 0.85 to 0.98 on VideoJAM-Bench (+15.3% relative), while Aesthetic and Image Quality are matched or slightly better. Against the 3-step DMD-only baseline, the gap is even larger—DMD suffers a 12% drop in Dynamics (0.73 vs. 0.83) due to overly static generation, whereas MoGAN recovers and exceeds the teacher's dynamics, yielding a +13.3% Motion Score gain on VBench. Against FlowMo (a training-free flow-guidance method), MoGAN achieves a higher Motion Score (0.973 vs. 0.903) and Dynamics (0.96 vs. 0.82) on VBench, showing that learned adversarial supervision in flow space outperforms inference-time flow variance adjustment. In the human study (148 videos, 5 annotators each), MoGAN is preferred for motion quality 52% vs. 38% over Wan2.1 and 56% vs. 29% over DMD, and also preferred for visual quality in both comparisons. Text alignment is slightly weaker than the 50-step Wan2.1 (expected for any 3-step distilled model) but comparable to DMD.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoGAN builds directly on Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) as both the initialization (a 3-step distilled Wan2.1 generator) and a regularizing loss during post-training, and adapts the multiscale-head discriminator architecture from Diffusion Adversarial Post-Training (seaweed-APT). Its key departure from prior GAN-in-diffusion work is the choice of representation: rather than operating the adversarial objective in pixel or latent space (as in APT, Adversarial Diffusion Distillation) or on a single flow image (as in Nam et al.), MoGAN applies a DiT-based discriminator over full dense optical-flow sequences extracted by a frozen RAFT estimator. This positions the method as a motion-specific complement to RL-based post-training (DPO/GRPO with VLM reward models) and to motion-aware co-training losses, offering a dense, differentiable motion signal without requiring reward models or human preference data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the few-step video generation community: MoGAN demonstrates that a 3-step model can exceed a 50-step teacher on motion quality while preserving visual fidelity, and reveals that DMD distillation specifically collapses dynamics degree by ~12%, a failure mode that smoothness metrics alone would mask. The method is computationally modest (16 hours on 8×H200, single forward sample per update) and orthogonal to RL-based approaches, making it a drop-in post-training stage. Open questions include whether the reliance on 2D optical flow (which misinterprets occlusions and out-of-plane motion) limits gains on complex 3D scenes, whether latent-space motion surrogates or geometry-aware flow fields could remove the pixel-decoding bottleneck, and whether the approach transfers to larger (5B+) or autoregressive video models without architectural changes.
