## Problem

Despite Bangla being the 7th most spoken language with 284 million speakers, no high-quality benchmark exists for Bangla form comprehension covering Document Layout Analysis and Key Information Extraction. Existing form benchmarks (FUNSD, XFUND, FormNLU) target English or other high-resource languages, and the only prior Bangla document dataset (BaD-LAD) provides only coarse layout labels without form-specific entities, key-value relationships, or government-form structures.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance is a single form page image (1–5 pages per form, sourced from scanned or digital Bangladeshi government documents) paired with a task-specific prompt (zero-shot or chain-of-thought). For DLA, the model must output bounding boxes labeled with one of 26 granular (or 5 coarse) entity types plus typed relationships (key-to-key, key-to-value, value-to-value) between linked fields; for KIE, it must produce extracted text values for given key fields. Predictions pass through a schema validator that discards malformed outputs before metric computation, and DLA scoring uses greedy IoU matching at thresholds 0.3 and 0.5.

## Method

BaFCo curates 200 multi-page Bangladeshi government forms across 15 administrative domains, annotated by 17 trained annotators (Cohen's κ = 0.974) with 16,382 entities, 8,771 relationships, and 1,926 key-value pairs, organized into three difficulty tiers based on layout complexity. The paper's empirical contribution evaluates five flagship MLLMs (GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro, Claude Opus 4.6, Qwen 3.6 Plus, Kimi K2.5) in a tuning-free, prompt-based protocol varying prompting strategy (zero-shot vs. CoT) and reasoning effort (low vs. high) under both granular and coarse entity taxonomies. DLA is scored with mAP, F1, and mean IoU at two IoU thresholds; KIE is scored with precision, recall, macro-F1, and Normalized Edit Similarity. A parallel English-form subset enables controlled cross-lingual comparison, revealing that DLA performance is largely language-agnostic while KIE shows significant language-dependent model rankings.

## Results

On the BaFCo DLA task (primary), Gemini 3 Pro achieves the best mAP@0.3 of 0.1177 on the granular 26-entity set and 0.2646 on the coarse 5-entity set under high-reasoning zero-shot prompting, more than doubling performance when entity granularity is reduced. GPT-5.2 follows at 0.0511 (granular) and 0.1434 (coarse), while Claude Opus 4.6 trails at 0.0074 and 0.0440 respectively. On KIE, Gemini 3 Pro leads on Bangla forms (F1 0.848, NES 0.866) while GPT-5.2 leads on English forms (F1 0.847, NES 0.851); among open-source models, Qwen 3.6-Plus is strongest (Bangla F1 0.794, English F1 0.835).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five flagship MLLMs (GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro, Claude Opus 4.6, Qwen 3.6-Plus, Kimi K2.5) as mutual baselines on both DLA and KIE. On DLA, Gemini 3 Pro outperforms all other models across every prompt and reasoning-effort configuration, with a particularly large margin over Claude Opus 4.6 (e.g., 0.1177 vs. 0.0074 mAP@0.3 on the granular set under high-reasoning ZS). GPT-5.2 consistently ranks second on DLA, while among open-source models Kimi K2.5 benefits most from increased reasoning effort (coarse mAP rising from 0.0469 to 0.1336), approaching GPT-5.2's 0.1434. On KIE, the ranking shifts: Gemini 3 Pro wins on Bangla (F1 0.848) but GPT-5.2 wins on English (F1 0.847), and Qwen 3.6-Plus narrows the open-source-to-proprietary gap substantially (Bangla F1 0.794 vs. GPT-5.2's 0.781). Language effects are minimal for DLA (ΔmAP ≤ 0.02 on granular set) but pronounced for KIE, where most models score 0.10–0.15 higher on English than Bangla, except Gemini 3 Pro which favors Bangla.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the form understanding benchmark lineage (FUNSD, XFUND, FormNLU, BuDDIE) and document layout benchmarks (PubLayNet, DocLayNet, OmniDocBench) to the Bangla language and government-form domain, where no comparable resource previously existed. It adopts the KIE-as-visual-question-answering formulation from GenKIE and the detection-style evaluation protocol (greedy IoU matching, mAP) from FormNLU and OmniDocBench, but introduces a substantially finer-grained 26-entity taxonomy with explicit inter-entity relationships that prior form benchmarks (which typically offer 4–6 flat entity types) do not provide. The evaluation of off-the-shelf flagship MLLMs in a tuning-free, prompt-based setting departs from the encoder-based or fine-tuned pipelines that dominate prior DLA/KIE work, positioning the paper at the intersection of document AI and the emerging MLLM paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BaFCo fills a concrete gap for the 284-million-speaker Bangla community and, more broadly, for low-resource document understanding, by providing the first fine-grained, multi-page, relationship-annotated form benchmark in the language. The empirical finding that even the strongest MLLMs achieve only ~0.12 mAP on granular DLA (vs. ~0.26 on coarse) while reaching >0.84 F1 on KIE sharply delineates where current multimodal models succeed (semantic text extraction) and where they fail (precise geometric localization of fine-grained layout elements), a distinction that should inform both model development and system design. Open questions include whether lightweight post-training or agentic approaches can close the DLA gap, whether the 200-form scale is sufficient for meaningful generalization, and how findings transfer to other low-resource South Asian languages.
