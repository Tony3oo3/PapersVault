## Problem

Existing multi-view pedestrian detection methods struggle to generalize to camera configurations not seen during training for two reasons: they learn 2D-to-3D visual geometry solely from limited training data, and their perspective-transform-based feature projection introduces shadow-like distortions whose patterns are configuration-specific. This makes detection models brittle when deployed with novel camera types, poses, or positions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260362/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N multi-view RGB images (resized to 720×1280 for the trainable ResNet branch and 280×504 for the frozen DA3 branch) along per-view camera parameters (translation, rotation quaternion, and field of view). It outputs a BEV pedestrian occupancy map at one-quarter the ground-truth BEV resolution (grid cells of 2.5 cm) plus a 2-channel offset map for sub-grid localization; pedestrians are extracted above a 0.4 threshold and their positions refined to full-resolution BEV coordinates via the offset map.

## Method

MV2GF leverages the pretrained Depth Anything 3 (DA3) transformer as a frozen visual geometric foundation model, extracting both multi-view geometric features (from the 20th, 28th, 34th, and 40th transformer blocks) and per-view 3D pointmaps. The Task-specific and Geometric information Fusion (TGF) module concatenates trainable ResNet18 multi-scale features with the frozen DA3 features at matching resolutions and fuses them via an FPN, yielding high-resolution image features that combine pedestrian-focused task information with general-purpose cross-view geometry. The Feature Pointmap Aggregation (FPA) module then projects each pixel of the TGF features to its predicted 3D world coordinate from the DA3 pointmap, scatters them into a C×(X/4)×(Y/4)×4 voxel grid via max pooling, and compresses the vertical dimension with a CNN to produce the BEV feature—avoiding the shadow-like distortions inherent to perspective-transform projections. Training uses Focal loss on the BEV occupancy map, L1 loss on the offset map, and an auxiliary per-view head/foot occupancy Focal loss, with the DA3 backbone kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On the primary generalization setting (trained on GMVD-D, tested on unseen camera configurations), MV2GF achieves 84.8 MODA on GMVD-D, 86.5 MODA on MVPerception, and 87.9 MODA on Wildtrack, exceeding the previous best (MSMVD) by 4.6, 4.7, and 2.2 points respectively. Under the same-camera-configuration setting (Table 2), MV2GF also leads on MODA across all three datasets (91.3, 96.5, 94.7), marginally outperforming MSMVD by 0.1–0.2 points. Inference speed is 3.3 FPS on an A100 GPU, slightly slower than baselines (4.5–5.2 FPS) but still above the 2.0 FPS frame rate of the evaluation datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV2GF outperforms all eight baselines on MODA across all three datasets in the unseen-camera-configuration setting (Table 1). The closest competitor, MSMVD [50], is surpassed by 4.6 points on GMVD-D (84.8 vs. 80.2), 4.7 points on MVPerception (86.5 vs. 81.8), and 2.2 points on Wildtrack (87.9 vs. 85.7). Earlier methods such as MVDet [17], SHOT [39], MVDeTr [16], 3DROM [34], and BoosterSHOT [20] trail by 10–24 MODA points, reflecting their reliance on perspective-transform projections that fail to generalize. MVFP [2] and OmniOcc [3] are intermediate, with gaps of 9–12 MODA points. In the same-camera-configuration setting (Table 2), MV2GF still leads on MODA and recall but is marginally behind MSMVD on MODP and precision (e.g., 86.1 vs. 86.6 MODP on GMVD-S), which the authors attribute to MSMVD's multi-scale BEV design enabling finer localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified MVPD pipeline established by MVDet (Hou et al., 2020) and its successors (SHOT, MVDeTr, 3DROM, BoosterSHOT, MSMVD), which project 2D image features into 3D world space via perspective transformations and aggregate them into BEV features. The key departure is replacing both the learned visual geometry and the perspective-based projection with outputs from a pretrained visual geometric foundation model (Depth Anything 3): multi-view geometric features from DA3's transformer blocks are fused with task-specific ResNet features via an FPN (TGF), and DA3's predicted 3D pointmaps replace perspective transformations for projecting each pixel to its true 3D location (FPA). This positions the work at the intersection of the foundation-model-for-3D-reconstruction line (DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT, DA3) and the MVPD literature, repurposing a general-purpose geometric model as a frozen backbone for a downstream detection task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored limitation of MVPD: poor generalization to camera configurations not seen during training, which is critical for real-world deployment where camera setups vary. The 4.6–4.7 point MODA gains on unseen configurations demonstrate that foundation-model geometric features and pointmap-based projection are substantially more transferable than learned perspective distortions. However, the inference speed penalty (3.3 FPS vs. ~4.5 FPS for baselines) and the remaining gap to same-configuration performance (Table 2) leave open the question of how to combine strong generalization with peak accuracy for fixed deployments. The broader implication is that frozen foundation-model outputs can serve as effective, configuration-agnostic geometric priors for multi-view perception tasks beyond pedestrian detection.
