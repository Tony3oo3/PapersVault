## Problem

Existing SSM-based video frame interpolation methods serialize 2D features along static spatial grids (raster scans, local windows), which misaligns with the physical motion that determines where a pixel's true context resides. This mismatch causes ghosting and blurring under large, non-linear displacements and complex occlusions, where the most informative neighbors lie along the motion trajectory rather than in a fixed spatial neighborhood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260168/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive RGB video frames I_0 and I_1 and synthesizes an intermediate frame Î_t at t ∈ (0,1) (default t = 0.5). Features are extracted at H/8 × W/8 resolution via a shared CNN backbone (SGM-VFI architecture), and refinement operates iteratively at 1/8 and 1/4 scales. Training uses Vimeo90K triplets at 448×256; evaluation spans resolutions from 256×256 (UCF101) up to 4K (Xiph-4K), with the full model consuming 2.06 TFLOPs at 720p.

## Method

MASS replaces static grid serialization with flow-guided trajectory-wise sequence modeling. For each pixel, a non-linear trajectory is constructed by iteratively integrating a coarse-flow increment with a learnable residual velocity δv_k predicted from local warped features, enabling the path to bend around curved motions. Features sampled along this trajectory are aggregated by a Velocity-Aware SSM (VA-SSM) that modulates the discretization step Δ inversely with per-step trajectory velocity, allocating finer state updates to fast-moving regions while keeping static regions efficient. The sampling budget K(p) is dynamically scaled by flow magnitude (clipped to [2,8]), so long trajectories receive denser sampling. Forward and backward trajectory contexts are then used both for frame synthesis (via a gated fusion head and U-Net generator) and for motion refinement, where their discrepancy identifies misalignment and occlusion to correct intermediate flows and masks in a coarse-to-fine loop.

## Results

MASS achieves state-of-the-art or tied-best results across all five benchmarks. On the most challenging SNU-FILM Extreme split, MASS reaches 26.04 dB PSNR / 0.8681 SSIM, outperforming the previous best (VFIMamba, 25.66/0.8657) by 0.38 dB. On high-resolution Xiph-4K, MASS records 34.83/0.908, surpassing LC-Mamba-P (34.49/0.906) by 0.34 dB. On Vimeo90K, MASS ties the best PSNR (36.53) and SSIM (0.9816) with AMT-G and LC-Mamba-P, while achieving the best UCF101 score (35.48/0.9703) and the lowest Middlebury IE (1.79). The full model requires 2.06 TFLOPs at 720p, with a lightweight variant (MASS-S) at 0.50 TFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASS achieves the best or tied-best result on every benchmark column in Table 1. The most pronounced gains appear in high-motion and high-resolution settings: on SNU-FILM Extreme, MASS (26.04 dB) surpasses VFIMamba (25.66) by 0.38 dB and LC-Mamba-P (25.48) by 0.56 dB; on SNU-FILM Hard, it leads VFIMamba (30.79) by 0.30 dB. On Xiph-4K, MASS (34.83) outperforms LC-Mamba-P (34.49) by 0.34 dB and AMT-G (34.63) by 0.20 dB, confirming the velocity-aware scan's benefit at high resolution. On Vimeo90K, MASS ties AMT-G and LC-Mamba-P at 36.53/0.9816, while on UCF101 it edges out LC-Mamba-P (35.42) by 0.06 dB. Among SSM-based competitors, MASS clearly dominates VFIMamba and LC-Mamba on the Hard/Extreme splits, whereas the gap on Easy/Medium is smaller (≤0.05 dB), indicating the trajectory-aligned design primarily benefits complex motion. The lightweight MASS-S variant (0.50 TFLOPs) remains competitive with full-size methods like AMT-L (0.68 T) and EMA-S (0.39 T) on most benchmarks, losing only marginally on SNU-FILM Hard (30.90 vs 31.09) and Extreme (25.73 vs 26.04).

## Relation to Prior Work

MASS extends the SSM-based VFI line (VFIMamba, LC-Mamba) by replacing their static grid-based token serialization (raster, Hilbert curves, shifted windows) with flow-guided trajectory scanning, directly addressing the mismatch between spatial scan orders and the physical motion structure of frame interpolation. It builds on the coarse-to-fine pipeline and backward warping of IFRNet and the feature extractor of SGM-VFI, but departs from the broader flow-based VFI paradigm (RIFE, AMT, EMA) by introducing trajectory-wise context aggregation and a forward/backward consistency signal for iterative motion refinement. The velocity-aware SSM (VA-SSM) is a novel adaptation of the selective SSM discretization, modulating the step size per-pixel based on local motion magnitude rather than using a fixed discretization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that the most informative context for a VFI pixel lies along its motion trajectory rather than in a static spatial neighborhood—is natural but had not been operationalized in prior SSM-based VFI work, and the controlled perturbation experiments (rotating trajectories, randomizing order, replacing with static grids) convincingly validate that motion alignment is the critical factor. The velocity-aware adaptive sampling provides a practical mechanism for allocating compute to fast-motion regions while keeping static regions efficient, yielding SOTA on SNU-FILM Extreme and Xiph-4K with only 2.06 TFLOPs. A key limitation is the dependence on initial coarse flow quality: in extreme cases (chaotic tiny-object motion, severe blur), trajectory-guided scanning may fail to recover correct context, and the authors identify joint flow-SSM optimization as an important open direction.
