## Problem

Semi-supervised video polyp segmentation is hampered by unreliable pseudo-labels: endoscopic videos exhibit low polyp-background contrast, large inter-frame variations, and consecutive low-quality frames that cause networks to produce high-confidence but incorrect predictions. Existing confidence-thresholding strategies cannot capture the nonlinear complementarity between confidence and prediction dispersion, and temporal continuity amplifies erroneous labels across frames, degrading segmentation quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes endoscopic video frames (3×H×W, randomly cropped to 352×352 during training) where only the first frame in each x-frame sequence carries a pixel-level binary annotation, with the remaining frames serving as unlabeled data. It outputs a per-pixel binary polyp/background segmentation mask. The framework is evaluated at four annotation ratios (1/16 to 1/2) on SUN-SEG and zero-shot on CVC-ClinicDB (288×384 resolution).

## Method

SCDL integrates three synergistic components into a closed-loop training process. (1) PAR refines spatio-temporal features by fusing high-resolution encoder outputs with memory-conditioned features via cross-attention (IOF), then applying explicit object-aware fusion (EOF) that uses cross-attention against discriminative polyp prototypes generated from historical frames via farthest-point sampling and k-means (OPG). (2) SCOS formulates pseudo-label selection as a convex optimization problem in a confidence-dispersion feature space, using spectral relaxation (Ky Fan theorem) to derive sample-adaptive selection boundaries without heuristic thresholds; a theoretical analysis via Taylor expansion of cross-entropy shows reliable predictions require both high maximum confidence and high residual dispersion. (3) CCP strategically masks reliable (high-confidence) regions in random patches, forcing the network to reconstruct semantics from the surrounding ambiguous areas, thereby reducing regional supervision bias. The three components form an interdependent loop: PAR produces better features for SCOS, SCOS defines which regions CCP masks, and CCP's gradients drive PAR to improve low-contrast boundary features.

## Results

On SUN-SEG with Res2Net-50, SCDL achieves Dice of 84.28% and Sα of 88.62% on the Hard test set at the 1/2 label ratio, outperforming the strongest same-backbone baseline SSTAN (81.96% Dice) by 2.32 points. Under the most annotation-scarce 1/16 setting, SCDL still reaches 81.66% Dice (Hard), surpassing SSTAN by 7.15 points. With the PVTv2-b2 backbone, SCDL pushes Dice to 86.09% (1/2 Hard) and 83.76% (1/16 Hard). On the unseen CVC-ClinicDB dataset (zero-shot), SCDL attains 91.53% Dice and 94.12% Sα, exceeding the next-best PSDNet by 1.86 Dice points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCDL outperforms all eight baselines across every label ratio (1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2) on both SUN-SEG Easy and Hard subsets. Among same-backbone (Res2Net-50) VPS methods, SCDL beats SSTAN by 2.32 Dice (84.28 vs. 81.96) and TCCNet by 3.45 Dice (84.28 vs. 80.83) at 1/2 Hard; the gap widens to 7.15 Dice over SSTAN at 1/16 Hard. Against the strongest overall competitor PSDNet (PVTv2-b2), SCDL with the same backbone gains 2.32 Dice (86.09 vs. 83.77) at 1/2 Hard. On zero-shot CVC-ClinicDB generalization, SCDL leads PSDNet by 1.86 Dice (91.53 vs. 89.67) and TCCNet by 3.47 Dice (91.53 vs. 88.06), confirming that the learned features are domain-agnostic rather than overfit to SUN-SEG. Image-based (IPS) and natural-video (NVSS) baselines trail the VPS methods by 3–6 Dice points, underscoring the importance of temporal modeling for endoscopic video.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCDL builds on the weak-to-strong consistency paradigm (UniMatch) for semi-supervised segmentation and adapts SAM 2's memory-attention mechanism for temporal feature propagation in the PAR module. Its most distinctive departure is the SCOS algorithm, which repurposes spectral relaxation from binary quadratic optimization (Olsson et al.) to formulate pseudo-label selection as a convex problem in a confidence-dispersion space, replacing the heuristic fixed-threshold approach used by prior SSVPS methods (TCCNet, SSTAN, PSDNet). The paper also introduces a theoretical analysis linking residual dispersion to prediction reliability via cross-entropy decomposition, providing a principled justification for why confidence alone is insufficient in low-contrast endoscopic settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses the central bottleneck for clinical deployment of video polyp segmentation—annotation scarcity—by making pseudo-label selection more robust without requiring additional labeled data. The zero-shot transfer to CVC-ClinicDB (91.53 Dice vs. 89.67 for PSDNet) suggests the learned representations capture domain-agnostic polyp characteristics, though the small evaluation set (612 frames) limits confidence in this claim. Remaining challenges include specular highlights, extremely small low-contrast polyps, and extreme shape variations, where spatial resolution loss in pooling layers causes feature entanglement with surrounding mucosa. Open questions include whether the prototype-guided mechanism generalizes to other medical video tasks and whether the convex-optimization formulation scales to longer sequences or multi-class settings.
