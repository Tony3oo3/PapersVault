## Problem

Extracting high-quality meshes from 3D Gaussian Splatting is difficult in scenes with high-frequency view-dependent appearance, because the geometry-appearance coupling in 3DGS causes photometric losses to dominate and drive over-densification. Prior solutions rely on multi-view constraints, large pre-trained monocular models, or mesh-aware bidirectional optimization, all of which sacrifice the inherent efficiency of 3DGS. This paper asks how far surface extraction can be pushed using purely self-supervised signals without any external priors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB images from a moderately dense multi-view capture along with their camera poses, and produces an unbounded 3D mesh extracted via marching tetrahedra from a sorted opacity field, alongside the optimized 3D Gaussian representation. Evaluation spans unbounded outdoor scenes (Tanks & Temples, Mip-NeRF 360) and bounded indoor scenes (ScanNet++), with full optimization completing in under 20 minutes on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Method

Each Gaussian is augmented with a learnable scalar confidence value that is rendered via alpha-blending into a per-pixel confidence map, which multiplicatively scales the photometric loss while leaving geometric losses untouched (Eq. 9); this intrinsically downweights gradient updates from ambiguous regions and is paired with a confidence-aware densification rule that raises the split/cloning threshold for low-confidence primitives to prevent over-densification. Two additional variance losses penalize per-primitive color and normal deviation from the blended result along each ray, discouraging the representation of view-dependent appearance as stacked geometry. The appearance model (adapted from VastGaussian) is decoupled so that the per-image corrective mapping is applied only to the luminance term of D-SSIM, while contrast and structure terms operate on the unmodified render, preventing the CNN from compensating for geometric errors and avoiding upsampling artifacts in gradient flow.

## Results

On the Tanks & Temples dataset (unbounded mesh extraction), the proposed method achieves a mean F1-score of 0.521, outperforming the next-best baseline PGSR (0.496) by 2.5 points and the state-of-the-art unbounded method MILo (0.485) by 3.6 points, while running in 18 minutes vs. MILo's 60 minutes. On the ScanNet++ bounded subset, the method attains a mean F1-score of 0.668, beating PGSR (0.631) and MILo (0.624) by 3.7 and 4.4 points respectively. The lighter variant (β=0.05) retains most of the gains (0.505 / 0.667) at a slightly faster 16-minute runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method wins on the average F1-score against all eight baselines on both Tanks & Temples and ScanNet++. Against the direct unbounded baseline SOF (F1 0.474), the gain is +0.047, driven primarily by the confidence framework and variance losses. Against the state-of-the-art unbounded method MILo (F1 0.485), the improvement is +0.036 while reducing runtime from 60 to 18 minutes. Bounded multi-view baselines PGSR (0.496) and QGS (0.474) are outperformed by +0.025 and +0.047 respectively, despite not using multi-view geometric constraints. On ScanNet++, the method leads PGSR by +0.037 and MILo by +0.044, with the largest per-scene margin on scene 08bbbdcc3d (0.729 vs. 0.681 for SOF). GOF (0.453) and RaDe-GS (0.461) trail by 0.068 and 0.060 on average, respectively. The only scene where the method does not achieve the top score is Barn (0.534 vs. PGSR's 0.548), a narrow 0.014 gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the authors' prior SOF framework for unbounded surface reconstruction from 3DGS, extending it with a self-supervised confidence mechanism inspired by Bayesian uncertainty estimation (Kendall & Gal) and feed-forward reconstruction models (DUSt3R). It departs from the dominant trend in 3DGS surface extraction—which relies on multi-view geometric constraints (PGSR, QGS), pre-trained monocular depth/normal models (VCR-GauS, UA-GS), or mesh-aware bidirectional optimization (MILo)—by showing that a lightweight per-primitive confidence value, combined with variance-reducing losses and a decoupled D-SSIM appearance model, can resolve photometric-geometric ambiguities without any external priors. The appearance modeling lineage traces back to VastGaussian's per-image latent correction, which the authors refine by selectively applying the compensated image only to the luminance term of D-SSIM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that state-of-the-art unbounded mesh extraction (F1 0.521 on Tanks & Temples, 0.668 on ScanNet++) is achieved in under 20 minutes without the computational overhead of multi-view solvers or large pre-trained networks, making high-fidelity surface reconstruction accessible in resource-constrained pipelines. The confidence framework is noted as potentially generalizable to heuristic-free densification for general 3DGS and other downstream tasks like 3D inpainting. Key limitations remain: the method assumes moderately dense captures and struggles with severely occluded regions or distant backgrounds, where multi-view constraints or diffusion-based inpainting would still be needed. The decoupled D-SSIM insight—that the luminance term is illumination-dominated while contrast and structure are not—may have broader applicability beyond surface reconstruction to any 3DGS pipeline using SSIM-based losses.
