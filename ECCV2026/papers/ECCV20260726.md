## Problem

Generative VLMs fail on specialized domains (medical imaging, industrial inspection) not due to distributional shift but because the required discriminative visual primitives were never encoded during pretraining — a regime the authors term distant out-of-distribution (OOD). Every major adaptation paradigm (SFT, LoRA, Visual-RFT, prompt tuning) assumes discriminative features already exist and merely need refinement, so they all fail when the features are structurally absent from the encoder's representation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260726/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image, a small set of labeled support images (1 or 8 per class), and class names as input, and produces a class label accompanied by an interpretable, trait-traceable explanation identifying which specific visual traits were matched and where they are spatially grounded in the query. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates on frozen VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B), requiring only a single 32 GB GPU for the offline trait-building stage and no multi-GPU cluster.

## Method

IVL exploits a descriptive-discriminative asymmetry: while the vision encoder's discriminative features collapse in distant-OOD domains, the language decoder retains a domain-agnostic primitive visual vocabulary (colors, textures, shapes, spatial relations). In the offline trait-building stage, dual-mode prompting (semantic descriptions plus low-level primitive observations) extracts visual traits from support images, which are then clustered via HDBSCAN, canonically named, and organized into per-class trait dictionaries. At inference, a three-level hierarchical filter first retrieves top-k₁ traits by cosine similarity between trait embeddings and mean-pooled patch tokens, then refines to top-k₂ traits using cross-modal attention maps from grounding heads to verify spatial localization, and finally extracts top-3 bounding-box regions via NMS on smoothed attention heatmaps. The VLM is then prompted with the original image, the three cropped regions, and the refined trait list to produce a classification grounded in explicit visual evidence, bypassing the encoder's degraded discriminative associations entirely.

## Results

Under Qwen2.5-VL-7B (1-shot), IVL achieves the highest accuracy on all four benchmark groups: 50.30% on Pokémon, 20.83% on Retinal OCT, 16.51% on WM811k, and 46.1% mean on MVTec AD. Under LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B (1-shot), IVL reaches 44.60% (Pokémon), 33.70% (Retinal OCT), 17.21% (WM811k), and 43.54% (MVTec AD mean). Across all 18 tasks, IVL ranks in the top two for 12 tasks under Qwen2.5-VL and 10 under LLaVA, the widest coverage of any single method. Gradient-based baselines (SFT, SFT+LoRA, Visual-RFT) fail to exceed zero-shot on most distant-OOD datasets, with SFT on Retinal OCT gaining only +0.41 pp over zero-shot despite full parameter updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IVL outperforms all six baselines on the MVTec AD mean under both backbones: 46.1% vs. CuPL (20.1%), Menon (32.8%), V-RFT (38.1%), SFT+LoRA (28.65%), Tip-Adapter (28.65%), and CoOp (25.54%) under Qwen2.5-VL, and 43.54% vs. CuPL (18.0%), Menon (22.8%), V-RFT (35.1%), SFT+LoRA (29.08%), Tip-Adapter (29.08%), and CoOp (18.42%) under LLaVA. On Retinal OCT (1-shot, Qwen), IVL (20.83%) surpasses the strongest gradient-based method SFT (14.77%) by 6.06 pp, while SFT+LoRA and V-RFT fall below zero-shot. On WM811k, IVL (16.51%) leads SFT+LoRA (9.30%) and V-RFT (9.16%) by over 7 pp, confirming the gradient-futility phenomenon. On Pokémon, IVL (50.30%) edges out Menon (48.77%) and V-RFT (45.59%), with the gap widening under LLaVA (44.60% vs. 37.18% and 34.76%). The attribute-based baselines CuPL and Menon, which generate descriptors from pretrained semantic knowledge without observing support images, are consistently outperformed, validating IVL's dual-mode extraction from actual visual observations. Tip-Adapter and CoOp, which operate within the pretrained feature subspace, show the weakest distant-OOD performance, aligning with Proposition 2's prediction of irreducible excess risk under representational absence.

## Relation to Prior Work

IVL departs from the dominant paradigm of few-shot VLM adaptation (SFT, LoRA, Visual-RFT, CoOp, Tip-Adapter), which all assume discriminative features already exist in the encoder's representation space and merely need refinement. It also distinguishes itself from prior attribute-based methods (CuPL, Menon et al., ArGue) that generate descriptors from pretrained semantic knowledge without observing target-domain images; IVL instead extracts traits from direct visual observation of support images via dual-mode prompting. The localized grounding step builds on Kang et al.'s finding that a few attention heads suffice for visual grounding, repurposing cross-modal attention maps for trait-level spatial verification rather than object detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central contribution is conceptual: identifying 'representational absence' as a structurally distinct failure mode from distributional shift, with a measurable two-axis diagnostic (MK-MMD + NMI) that predicts when parametric adaptation will fail before any training is attempted. This reframing is practically important for deployment decisions in specialized domains (medical imaging, industrial inspection) where fine-tuning is expensive and unreliable. Key limitations include the trait-separability boundary (IVL cannot help when classes differ only in invisible generative parameters, as in FractalDB), vocabulary collapse on reference-free tasks, and dictionary coverage failures that drop the correct class during retrieval. Open directions include open-set recognition where the target class may be absent from the support set, and compositional generalization of trait dictionaries.
