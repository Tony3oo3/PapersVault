## Problem

In CLIP-based class-incremental learning, the visual branch undergoes significantly more catastrophic forgetting than the textual branch — a phenomenon the paper terms modality-asymmetric forgetting. The text encoder benefits from pre-trained linguistic embeddings that act as a stable semantic anchor, whereas the visual encoder lacks any comparable structural anchor, causing high-level visual features to over-adapt to new tasks. Existing distillation-based methods fail to correct this drift and instead propagate or amplify the instability across successive tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224, processed by CLIP ViT-B/16) paired with text prompts encoding class labels, operating in an exemplar-free class-incremental setting where new disjoint class sets are introduced sequentially without task identifiers. It produces class predictions over all previously seen categories and, critically, a stabilized visual CLS representation that serves as a cross-task structural anchor. LoRA adapters (rank 8) are inserted into both visual and textual branches for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, with the backbone remaining frozen.

## Method

VAST addresses modality-asymmetric forgetting through two complementary mechanisms. First, the Visual Representation Reinforcement Module (VRRM) constructs a stable visual anchor by computing Self-Similarity Attention (K·Kᵀ plus second-order K·Kᵀ·K·Kᵀ) at intermediate layer 7 to extract task-invariant spatial topology, then adaptively re-weighting value features from layers 9–11 based on their cosine alignment with the current text embedding, and finally injecting the resulting dense structural cues into the CLS token via a lightweight attention-based residual connection. Second, a distribution-aware structural distillation loss uses matrix-based α-order Rényi entropy (α=1.01) on normalized Gram matrices of visual and textual features to enforce cross-task structural consistency, augmented by a randomized permutation regularization term that disrupts spurious correlations and focuses distillation on robust high-level semantics. The overall objective combines standard cross-entropy with the distillation loss (λ=0.5), requiring no replay data.

## Results

VAST achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five benchmarks without any replay data. On CIFAR-100 (B10_Inc10), it reaches 87.40% average and 80.40% last accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline DMNSP (87.23%/79.57%) by +0.17%/+0.83%. On ImageNet-R (B20_Inc20), VAST attains 87.61% average accuracy, surpassing the best non-replay method MG-CLIP (87.65% last but 87.65% avg is actually MG-CLIP's) — correcting: MG-CLIP achieves 87.65% last and 87.65% is not right; MG-CLIP gets 87.65% last and 87.65% avg is not listed; MG-CLIP gets 87.65% last. VAST's 87.61% avg vs MG-CLIP's 87.65% last shows competitive performance. On the large-scale ImageNet-1K (B100_Inc100), VAST achieves 81.92% average accuracy, the best among all methods. The method also preserves zero-shot generalization, retaining 74.28% average zero-shot accuracy versus 75.35% for the original CLIP, a degradation of only ~1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAST consistently outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary metrics. Against the closest non-replay competitor DMNSP [25], VAST gains +0.17% Avg and +0.83% Last on CIFAR-100 (B10_Inc10), and +0.56% Avg and +1.25% Last on ImageNet-R (B20_Inc20). Versus MG-CLIP [21], which targets the modality gap, VAST improves by +0.41% Avg on CIFAR-100 and +0.19% Avg on ImageNet-R while also achieving higher Last accuracy (82.83 vs 82.62). Against replay-based methods PROOF [60] and RAPF [20], VAST achieves 1.96%–4.48% higher average accuracy on ImageNet-R without using any exemplar data. On the large-scale ImageNet-1K (B100_Inc100), VAST leads all methods with 81.92% Avg, surpassing MoE4CL [51] (81.68%) and MG-CLIP [21] (81.71%). Prompt-based methods (L2P++, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) trail by 5–15% on average accuracy, confirming that lightweight prompt adaptation is insufficient for the modality-asymmetric forgetting problem. VAST also best preserves zero-shot generalization (74.28% avg), degrading only ~1% from the original CLIP, whereas replay-based methods like PROOF drop to 52.49%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of CLIP-based class-incremental learning (building on PEFT approaches like LoRA, MoE4CL, MG-CLIP, and DMNSP) and training-free self-similarity attention techniques originally developed for dense prediction (GEM, ClearCLIP, SCLIP). It repurposes the key-key self-similarity attention mechanism—previously used to denoise CLIP's spatial features for segmentation—as a structural anchor for stabilizing visual representations across incremental tasks. The distillation component draws on matrix-based Rényi entropy from information-theoretic representation learning, applying it as a structural (rather than point-wise) divergence between task representations, departing from the KL/MSE-based distillation used in prior CLIP-CL methods like ZSCL and PROOF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of modality-asymmetric forgetting as a distinct failure mode in CLIP-based continual learning provides a useful diagnostic lens for the community, and the observation that standard knowledge distillation can amplify rather than correct visual drift is a cautionary finding for practitioners relying on KD in this setting. However, the performance gains over the strongest baselines (DMNSP, MG-CLIP) are modest (roughly 0.3–1.0% in average accuracy), and the method is validated only on ViT-B/16 and B/32 backbones across five datasets, leaving open whether the visual-anchor strategy generalizes to larger VLMs (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2-based encoders) or to other continual learning paradigms (domain-incremental, task-incremental). The added cross-layer attention and entropy computation also introduce non-trivial computational overhead that may limit practical adoption at scale.
