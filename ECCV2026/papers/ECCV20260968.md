## Problem

Existing RL-based video temporal grounding methods face a fundamental trade-off: outcome-based supervision (e.g., Time-R1) yields unfaithful reasoning disconnected from visual content, while process-based supervision (e.g., Video-VER) depends on expensive external evaluators and produces rigid, template-like outputs. TAR addresses how to enforce intermediate visual grounding within the reasoning chain without incurring the computational cost of external models or sacrificing the model's semantic autonomy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260968/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video and a natural language query and produces a structured output: a chain-of-thought reasoning trace containing multiple <T-anchor> tags (each enclosing a predicted start–end timestamp pair) followed by a final <answer> tag. The T-anchors are designed to progressively refine the temporal prediction, with each subsequent anchor expected to be more accurate than the previous one, and the format is enforced via regular-expression matching. The base model is Qwen2.5-VL at 3B or 7B parameters.

## Method

TAR embeds explicit <T-anchor> tags as auditable checkpoints within the CoT, forcing the model to iteratively propose and refine temporal predictions while re-grounding in visual evidence at each step. Training uses GRPO with a composite reward combining a format reward, a soft IoU reward (removing the zero lower bound to provide gradient signal even for non-overlapping predictions), and a novel Temporal Anchor-Constrained Reward that rewards progressive accuracy gains across anchors while penalizing deviation from a target anchor count. The key insight is that the anchor mechanism breaks 'textual inertia'—the model's tendency to attend to its own generated text rather than the video—by structurally interrupting the reasoning flow and requiring verifiable numerical outputs at each checkpoint, while leaving the natural language between anchors completely free-form. A bootstrapping pipeline first runs lightweight GRPO on the 7B model to harvest 30K well-formatted reasoning chains (filtered for progressive accuracy improvement), which are then used for SFT cold-start before final GRPO training.

## Results

On Charades-STA, TAR (7B) achieves a state-of-the-art mIoU of 61.1 and R1@0.7 of 50.2, surpassing the previous best RL-based method Time-R1 (7B) by +2.3 mIoU and +0.8 R1@0.3. On QVHighlights, TAR (7B) reaches 65.9 mIoU and 58.5 R1@0.7, improving over Time-R1 by +6.8 mIoU, +9.9 R1@0.5, and +5.8 R1@0.7. In zero-shot evaluation on ActivityNet-Captions, TAR attains the highest mIoU (41.1) and R1@0.3 (61.5) among all 7B models, and on TVGBench it achieves the best mIoU of 30.6. The 3B variant also outperforms all VLP-based methods and approaches 7B-level performance (57.0 mIoU on Charades-STA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Charades-STA, TAR (7B) outperforms the strongest RL baseline Time-R1 (7B) by +2.3 mIoU (61.1 vs 58.8) and +0.8 R1@0.3 (83.6 vs 82.8), while also beating Temporal-RLT (7B) by +0.5 mIoU and +1.9 R1@0.7. On QVHighlights, TAR (7B) matches Time-R1 (7B) exactly (65.9 mIoU, 58.5 R1@0.7) but substantially outperforms VideoChat-R1.5 (7B) by +6.8 mIoU and +5.8 R1@0.7. The 3B TAR model (57.0 mIoU) surpasses all VLP-based methods (best: FlashVTG at 49.9 R1@0.7) and the 3B Time-R1 (55.3 mIoU) by +1.7 mIoU. In zero-shot ActivityNet-Captions, TAR achieves the highest mIoU (41.1) and R1@0.3 (61.5), beating Time-R1 (58.6 R1@0.3) and Temporal-RLT (39.0 mIoU). On the VQA generalization test (MVBench/VideoMME), TAR (66.1/54.9) narrowly edges out Time-R1 (65.6/54.2) and the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (65.2/53.0), confirming that the progressive-refinement reasoning pattern transfers beyond temporal grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAR positions itself as a middle ground between two existing paradigms in RL-based video temporal grounding: outcome-based unsupervised reasoning (Time-R1, VideoChat-R1.5), which relies solely on final IoU rewards and suffers from unfaithful CoT, and process-based supervised reasoning (Video-VER), which uses external LVLMs to score intermediate steps at high computational cost. The core departure is the T-anchor mechanism—a rule-based, format-only constraint on intermediate temporal predictions that enforces progressive refinement without requiring any external model to evaluate linguistic content. The training pipeline builds on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) applied to Qwen2.5-VL, and introduces a self-bootstrapping data collection strategy that replaces the common practice of distilling CoT from proprietary ultra-large models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anchor-based semi-supervised reasoning paradigm offers a practical recipe for improving CoT faithfulness in video understanding without the cost of external reward models, and the bootstrapping pipeline makes the approach accessible to groups without access to GPT-4-class models. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively short videos (Charades-STA averages ~30s), and the VQA transfer gains are modest (e.g., +0.9 on MVBench), leaving open whether the progressive-refinement pattern generalizes to longer-form or more complex video reasoning. The 76% initial format-failure rate for T-anchors also suggests the mechanism is fragile and may not transfer cleanly to other structured-output tasks without similar bootstrapping.
