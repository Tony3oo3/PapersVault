## Problem

Existing domain adaptive object detection methods either rely on mean teacher self-training, which produces unstable pseudo-labels under severe domain shifts, or on image-to-image translation, which introduces computational overhead and pixel-level artifacts that degrade detection performance. The paper addresses the lack of a stable, efficient mechanism to align source and target domains without performing explicit pixel-space reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260546/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired source (labeled) and target (unlabeled) RGB images resized to 1024×2048, processed jointly through a shared ResNet-50 + FPN backbone (C=256) to extract multi-scale features. The student detector outputs bounding boxes and class predictions for both source and target images, while the EMA-updated teacher generates pseudo-labels (filtered at confidence 0.8) for the target domain. Training uses a batch of 32 images (16 source + 16 target) with weak/strong augmentation strategies applied differently per domain.

## Method

DA-F2F replaces pixel-level domain translation with a style-aware feature modulation (SFM) module that computes channel-wise mean and standard deviation from target features, maps them through a two-layer MLP to affine parameters (γ, β), and applies a weighted affine transformation (w=0.3) to source features, aligning style while preserving contextual content. For adversarial alignment, the soft-weighted proposal alignment (SPA) module separates foreground and background regions using ground-truth bounding-box masks for source features, but replaces discrete pseudo-label-based masks for target features with a continuous soft-weighted proposal map rescaled to [0.05, 0.8], mitigating instability from noisy teacher predictions. The overall training follows a teacher-student paradigm (EMA α=0.9996) over 40k iterations, jointly optimizing supervised and unsupervised detection losses, an RPN loss for the proposal map, and the adversarial alignment loss (λ_spa=0.01).

## Results

DA-F2F achieves state-of-the-art mAP across all three adaptation scenarios: 60.5% on weather adaptation (Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes), 48.6% on small-to-large-scale adaptation (Cityscapes → BDD100K-daytime), and 69.9% on synthetic-to-real adaptation (Sim10K → Cityscapes, car category). The evaluation metric is mAP at IoU threshold 0.5. The method uses Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50 FPN backbone, trained for 40k iterations with a mean-teacher self-training paradigm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA-F2F outperforms all 21 baselines across all three adaptation scenarios. On the primary weather adaptation benchmark (Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes), DA-F2F (60.5% mAP) surpasses the overall best prior method DA-Ada [23] (RegionCLIP-based, 58.5%) by +2.0% and the best same-architecture FRCNN-based method DADAOD [16] (57.3%) by +3.2%. Among Transformer-based methods, HMT [42] (56.5%) is the strongest, trailing DA-F2F by 4.0%. On the small-to-large-scale benchmark (Cityscapes → BDD100K), DA-F2F (48.6%) leads DADAOD [16] (45.8%) by +2.8% and DATR [3] (43.3%) by +5.3%. On synthetic-to-real (Sim10K → Cityscapes, car), DA-F2F (69.9%) edges out DADAOD [69.7%] by a narrow +0.2% margin, while surpassing SEEN-DA [25] (66.8%) and HMT [42] (66.4%) by 3.1% and 3.5% respectively. The consistent wins across all detector families (FCOS, Transformer, RegionCLIP, FRCNN) and all three domain shift types indicate the generality of the feature-to-feature modulation approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

DA-F2F builds on the mean teacher self-training paradigm (Tarvainen & Valpola, NeurIPS 2017) as its core training framework and on region-separable adversarial learning (Chen et al., IJCV 2021) for cross-domain feature alignment. Its key departure is from image-to-image translation methods (UMT, DDT) that synthesize target-like pixels from source images; instead, DA-F2F performs domain alignment entirely in feature space via a style-aware modulation module inspired by AdaIN/FiLM-style channel statistics. It also extends the discrete foreground/background separation used in prior adversarial DAOD work by introducing a soft-weighted proposal mechanism that tolerates noisy pseudo-labels in the target domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The feature-to-feature modulation offers a computationally cheaper and more stable alternative to pixel-level domain translation, which is practically relevant for deployment scenarios where style-transfer networks add significant overhead. The soft-weighted alignment addresses a genuine weakness in prior region-separable adversarial methods—brittle dependence on pseudo-label quality—that limits their robustness under severe domain shifts. However, the method is validated only with a Faster R-CNN backbone; whether the SFM and SPA modules transfer effectively to Transformer-based detectors (which dominate recent SOTA) remains an open question. The marginal gain on synthetic-to-real (69.9% vs. 69.7%) also suggests the approach may be less impactful for extreme domain gaps.
