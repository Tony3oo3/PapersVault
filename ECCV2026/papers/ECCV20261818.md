## Problem

Existing embodied multi-agent cooperation research (e.g., CoELA, CaPo) is confined to small, mostly static indoor environments where simple obstacle avoidance suffices. Scaling to realistic city-scale outdoor settings introduces vastly larger spatial extents, dynamically patrolling hazards, and the need for continual natural-language information exchange among spatially distant agents—capabilities that remain largely unexplored. This paper formalizes Cooperative Spatial Intelligence and introduces the Sentinel Challenge to benchmark decentralized agents that must negotiate a safe meeting point and navigate under evolving sentinel threats using only coarse map information.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives 512×512 egocentric RGB-D frames, natural-language messages from teammates, and responses from a deliberately coarse Map Tool (five query types returning waypoint-based routes via Dijkstra, nearby place lists, place metadata with bounding boxes, a binary aerial navigability image, and refined waypoint sequences). The agent outputs a sequence of actions drawn from world interactions (navigation waypoints executed via A* on a local occupancy map), communication messages, map-tool queries, and a final meeting-place decision. Notably, the map tool exposes only high-level waypoint abstractions rather than exact paths, forcing agents to refine routes through communication and VLM-based spatial reasoning.

## Method

CoSaR is a zero-shot, training-free framework that couples LLM/VLM reasoning with classical navigation algorithms through a persistent Spatial Memory (occupancy map with sentinel danger zones, per-agent ETA map, and a negotiated meeting plan). A Perception Module (Grounding DINO + SAM 2 + CLIP) converts RGB-D input into object lists and voxel grids; a Communication Module uses an LLM to extract spatial facts and opinions from teammate messages and to generate new messages. A Spatial-aware Reasoning Module, activated only on new events, detected disagreements, or a 120-second timer, prompts an LLM with the structured memory to select among four plan types (Query, Communicate, Navigate, Wait). When a planned route intersects a danger zone, a Route Refinement pipeline constructs a Schematic Spatial Map (aerial image annotated with ego-pose, sentinel coordinates, and the current path), passes it to a VLM for zero-shot spatial reasoning to propose detour coordinates, and verifies the result through QueryRefinedRoute with a heuristic fallback. Low-level execution uses A* over the local occupancy map, with an emergency-avoidance reflex for sudden sentinel encounters.

## Results

On the Sentinel Challenge (5 agents, 10 sentinels, 14 city-scale scenes, 6 runs), CoSaR achieves a 32.14% success rate with stationary sentinels and 32.14% with patrolling sentinels, outperforming the next-best baseline RoCo (26.19% and 29.76% respectively). CoSaR also attains the lowest time cost (1194.45 steps for stationary, 1246.86 for patrolling) among all methods. Under oracle perception, CoSaR's success rate rises to 53.57% (stationary) and 38.10% (patrolling), maintaining its lead over RoCo (46.43% and 38.10%) and CoELA (34.52% and 36.90%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSaR outperforms RoCo [29] on success rate by 5.95 points (stationary) and 2.38 points (patrolling), and reduces time cost by ~60 steps in both settings, demonstrating that dynamic communication and spatial memory yield better coordination than RoCo's plan-then-execute paradigm. Against CoELA [65], CoSaR achieves a substantially higher success rate (32.14% vs. 16.67% stationary; 32.14% vs. 26.19% patrolling) and lower time cost, though CoELA reports a lower caught rate under patrolling sentinels (17.62% vs. 27.62%)—the authors attribute this to CoELA agents lingering in safe indoor areas rather than navigating more safely. MAT [58] performs worst, achieving 0% success rate with stationary sentinels and only 7.14% with patrolling sentinels, indicating that centralized end-to-end RL struggles with the scale and partial observability of city-level cooperation. Overall, CoSaR's advantage is most pronounced in success rate and time efficiency, while its caught rate is competitive but not always the lowest, reflecting a deliberate efficiency-safety tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-powered multi-agent cooperation (CoELA, RoCo, CaPo) from confined, static indoor rearrangement tasks to city-scale outdoor navigation under dynamic adversarial constraints. While prior embodied navigation work addresses single-agent urban navigation, and prior multi-agent work addresses small-room cooperation, no prior system combines continuous natural-language communication, structured spatial memory, and VLM-based route refinement at this spatial scale. CoSaR repurposes classical navigation primitives (A*, Dijkstra) as the low-level execution layer beneath a foundation-model reasoning stack, rather than learning end-to-end policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Sentinel Challenge provides the first scalable benchmark for evaluating cooperative spatial intelligence at city scale, filling a gap between indoor multi-agent benchmarks and single-agent urban navigation suites. The ablation revealing that spatial memory (ETA maps, known poses) is more critical than communication alone challenges the assumption that dynamic dialogue is the primary driver of multi-agent coordination quality. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond simulation, how it scales beyond 5 agents, and whether the heavy reliance on GPT-4o-class models for every reasoning step is sustainable for real-time deployment.
