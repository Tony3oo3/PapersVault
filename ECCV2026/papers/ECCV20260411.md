## Problem

Accurate subsurface scattering (SSS) relighting requires capturing per-pixel scattering footprints, but existing methods demand thousands to millions of images per object (e.g., ~3000 shifted-grid images in [30], millions of laser-spot images in DISCO). No prior approach learns a reusable, generalizable SSS representation from a minimal set of structured-light inputs, and standard self-supervised pretraining pipelines have not been adapted to the highly structured, high-frequency nature of phase-shift profilometry data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260411/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8 high-frequency PSP fringe images per view (4 vertical + 4 horizontal sinusoidal patterns at 4 phase shifts), from which 90×90 pixel patches are cropped around each surface point as encoder input. For relighting, a virtual projector image at the native 1920×1080 resolution provides the target illumination. The network outputs a dense 90×90×3 scattering footprint (PSF) per surface pixel, which is then scaled by the virtual light and splatted onto a canvas to produce the final relit image. Critically, only these 8 PSP images are needed at inference time for any novel object—no additional footprint captures are required.

## Method

The framework splits training into two stages: (1) non-contrastive SimSiam pretraining of a custom pre-activation residual encoder on 90×90 PSP patches using a weak-strong augmentation pipeline specifically designed for high-frequency structured-light inputs (including phase-jitter, shuffled fringe ordering, and intensity scaling), and (2) supervised training of a frozen-encoder decoder that predicts SSS footprints via sub-pixel upsampling with attention-gated skip fusion. The decoder is optimized with a Self-Balanced Hybrid Loss combining L1, log-L1, SSIM, channel-normalized gradient L1, and batch-adaptive variance-weighted MSE to handle the strongly peaked, anisotropic, and spatially skewed distribution of scattering footprints. Relighting is then a simple splat-and-accumulate: each decoded PSF is scaled by the virtual projector color at the corresponding surface point and accumulated onto the output canvas. The PSP-specific augmentations are shown to produce higher-quality embeddings (measured by zero-shot kNN accuracy) than standard ImageNet-style augmentations, and the two-stage design yields a reusable encoder that generalizes to unseen objects, geometries, and materials.

## Results

On four unseen test objects (Apple, Orange, Crab, Hand), the method achieves PSNR between 42.51 and 47.98 dB, SSIM between 0.94 and 0.98, and LPIPS between 6.5×10⁻² and 8.6×10⁻² for SSS footprint reconstruction. The kNN zero-shot classification on frozen encoder features reaches 99.99% accuracy on Pear and Orange with PSP-specific augmentations, outperforming ImageNet-style augmentations by up to 6.5 percentage points (Shovel: 91.43% vs. 84.91%). Critically, the method requires only 8 PSP images per view for inference on novel objects, compared to ~3000 images for the prior supervised approach [30] and millions for DISCO [17].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a direct method-to-method reconstruction comparison table; instead, it evaluates against camera-captured ground truth footprints. The primary quantitative comparison is between PSP-specific augmentations and standard ImageNet-style augmentations for the SSL pretraining (Table 2): PSP augmentations consistently yield higher kNN accuracy, with the largest gap on the Shovel object (91.43% vs. 84.91%, a 6.5-point improvement) and moderate gains on Pear (99.99% vs. 98.74%) and Orange (99.99% vs. 98.92%). In terms of acquisition efficiency, the method requires only 8 PSP images per view for novel-object inference, versus approximately 3000 shifted-grid images for the prior supervised U-Net approach of Majumdar et al. [30] and millions of individual laser measurements for DISCO [17]. The encoder is also decoupled from the decoder (unlike [30]'s end-to-end pipeline), enabling standalone use for representation evaluation via kNN without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SimSiam non-contrastive SSL framework [8] and the stereo projector-camera SSS acquisition pipeline of Majumdar et al. [30], but departs from both in key ways. Rather than the end-to-end supervised encoder-decoder of [30] (which requires ~3000 shifted-grid images per object), the authors split training into a self-supervised encoder pretraining stage using only 8 high-frequency PSP images per view, followed by a supervised decoder stage. They also introduce the first PSP-specific augmentation pipeline for SSL pretraining, replacing standard ImageNet-style augmentations, and a custom pre-activation residual architecture in place of conventional ResNet/ViT backbones, motivated by the need for a paired decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing per-object acquisition from thousands of images to just 8 PSP views makes spatially varying SSS relighting feasible in near-real-time scanning workflows, and the frozen encoder becomes a reusable component for downstream tasks. However, the method is constrained to a fixed camera-projector geometry, limits scattering to 90×90 local patches (capping recoverable scattering radius), and relies on standard dynamic-range captures. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer-range transport, novel camera/projector positions, and whether generative or stochastic footprint modeling could better capture highly anisotropic or heterogeneous scattering.
