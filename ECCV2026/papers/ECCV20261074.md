## Problem

Current diffusion models with MLLM text encoders treat the MLLM as a static one-shot encoder, which fails to activate iterative Chain-of-Thought reasoning and provides invariant guidance throughout denoising. The paper's empirical analysis identifies two concrete bottlenecks: single-pass MLLM encoding cannot capture all logical constraints for complex tasks, and static text conditioning causes cross-attention entropy to collapse on dense spatial topologies, breaking spatial grounding.

## Input / Output

The model takes a textual task instruction and an input image (e.g., a maze grid or Sudoku board rendered at 512×512) and produces a solution image (e.g., a valid path, TSP route, or completed grid). A key structural detail is that the number of reasoning steps τ is decoupled from the internal denoising timesteps: each reasoning step triggers a full flow-matching denoising trajectory conditioned on the current latent thought state, enabling inference-time compute scaling by simply increasing τ without repeating expensive DiT passes at higher resolutions.

## Method

EndoCoT introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, Iterative Thought Guidance recursively updates a continuous latent thought state h_τ by feeding the previous state back into the MLLM's first layer (bypassing the discrete embedding table), and each h_τ conditions a complete flow-matching denoising trajectory to produce an intermediate visual output. Second, Terminal Thought Grounding computes a reference state from ground-truth reasoning text and applies an L2 alignment loss only at the final step τ=T to prevent cumulative drift. Training follows a progressive two-stage schedule: Stage 1 supervises all intermediate reasoning steps with flow-matching loss plus the alignment term, while Stage 2 freezes gradients on intermediate steps and optimizes only the terminal output for a short fine-tuning cycle. LoRA (rank 32) is applied jointly to both the MLLM and DiT, enabling collaborative adaptation of the reasoning and generation stages.

## Results

Under task-specific training, EndoCoT achieves an average accuracy of 92.1% across all benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline DiffThinker (83.8%) by 8.3 percentage points. Gains are most pronounced on high-complexity settings: 90% on Maze-32 (vs. 65% for DiffThinker, a 25 pp gain) and 95% on Sudoku-35 (vs. 55%, a 40 pp gain). Under the more challenging unified training setting (single model on all tasks), EndoCoT still leads with 84.2% average accuracy versus 77.1% for DiffThinker. On VSP-Super-32, the most challenging spatial planning benchmark, EndoCoT reaches 85% while zero-shot generative baselines (ThinkGen, ChronoEdit) score 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffThinker (the strongest prior method), EndoCoT wins on 14 of 19 sub-benchmarks under task-specific training, with the largest margins on complex instances: Maze-32 (+25 pp), Sudoku-35 (+40 pp), TSP-18 (+14 pp), and Sudoku-30 (+51 pp). On the standard VSP levels (3–7) and VSP-Super-8/16, both methods achieve near-perfect scores, so the differentiation is minimal. Under unified training, EndoCoT leads on Sudoku (all scales) and VSP-Super-8/16, while DiffThinker retains a slight edge on Maze-32 (66 vs. 52) and TSP-15/18. Against the zero-shot baselines (ThinkGen, ChronoEdit, Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511), EndoCoT achieves 80–90 pp gains on nearly every benchmark, as these models lack any task-specific reasoning capability. The Qwen3-VL-8B SFT variant from DiffThinker's paper is the closest non-diffusion competitor, reaching 58.6% average, but it collapses to 0% on Maze-32 and Sudoku-30, highlighting the fragility of single-pass MLLM reasoning without iterative latent refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent reasoning paradigm from text-only LLMs (Hao et al., Shen et al.) into the diffusion generation setting, building on the Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 MLLM+DiT architecture. It departs sharply from DiffThinker, which injects reasoning traces as static conditioning and the authors show commits to solutions within the first few denoising steps without genuine iterative reasoning. The key conceptual departure is replacing one-shot text encoding with an iterative latent thought guidance loop that couples MLLM hidden-state updates to each denoising trajectory, plus a terminal grounding loss that anchors the final latent state to ground-truth textual reasoning steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EndoCoT provides the first demonstrated mechanism for endogenous, interpretable chain-of-thought reasoning inside a diffusion model, with clean inference-time scaling (accuracy improves monotonically with more reasoning steps) and 25–40% gains over the strongest baseline on complex instances. However, the approach currently requires manual selection of the reasoning-step budget and depends on high-quality datasets with explicit intermediate supervision, and unified multi-task training shows notable degradation (e.g., Maze-32 drops from 90% to 52%). Open questions include whether adaptive reasoning-depth control can eliminate the manual tuning, whether the mechanism generalizes beyond grid-based reasoning to open-ended visual tasks, and whether the findings hold across different backbone architectures beyond the Qwen-Image family.
