## Problem

VLM-based reaction diagram parsing (RxnDP) suffers from two fundamental bottlenecks: (1) existing visual prompting strategies (BROS, BIVP) either fail to activate the chemical domain knowledge embedded in VLM pre-training or introduce distribution shifts from the raw-image paradigm, and (2) standard supervised fine-tuning optimizes token-level cross-entropy that misaligns with reaction-level evaluation metrics and forces the model to memorize arbitrary serialization orders for inherently permutation-invariant set predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261502/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a chemical reaction diagram image (digital or scanned) as input, with molecule identifiers (bold numerals such as 1a, 2b) overlaid adjacent to each molecular structure as visual prompts; for molecules lacking native identifiers, a Mid-Mapper module assigns context-consistent virtual identifiers. The model outputs a structured JSON set of reactions, where each reaction is a triple of (reactants, conditions, products) and molecular entities are referenced by their identifiers as semantic handles rather than raw pixel coordinates, while textual components (reagents, solvents, temperatures) are emitted as free-form strings. Evaluation uses reaction-level Soft Match (IoU > 0.5 on molecular boxes, conditions merged into reactants) and Hybrid Match (IoU > 0.5 for molecules plus normalized edit distance ≤ 0.2 for text).

## Method

The core insight is that VLM pre-training corpora contain vast amounts of identifier-rich interleaved image-text data from chemical literature, so overlaying molecule identifiers (IdtVP) on diagrams activates latent chemical reasoning without any fine-tuning, outperforming bounding-box-based prompting (BIVP) by 26+ points in zero-shot Hybrid F1 on Gemini 3.0 Pro. For fine-tuning, the paper introduces Re³-DAPO, a reinforcement learning algorithm that parses the model's generated JSON at reward time and computes a permutation-invariant composite reward R(y) = ½(R_hybrid + R_soft), directly translating reaction-level evaluation metrics into dense RL signals and eliminating token-level serialization noise. The training pipeline proceeds in three phases: (1) Mid-Mapper (a Qwen2.5-VL-3B model distilled from Gemini 3 Pro) constructs identifier-annotated training data; (2) a brief SFT cold-start on ~10% of data establishes JSON schema adherence; (3) Re³-DAPO fine-tuning (870 steps, DAPO Clip-Higher [0.2, 0.28], KL penalty 0.01, rollout N=8) optimizes the reaction-level reward, with invalid JSON receiving zero reward to enforce syntactic validity. The approach is strategy-agnostic: the same SFT→RL curriculum transfers to BIVP, and the balanced 1:1 Soft:Hybrid reward ratio yields the most stable training trajectory and best generalization.

## Results

On RxnCaption-15k-test, the best trained model (RxnID+RL with IdtVP) achieves 64.4% Hybrid Match F1 and 74.5% Soft Match F1, while the zero-shot Gemini 3.0 Pro + IdtVP reaches 58.3% Hybrid F1 and 78.2% Soft F1. On the out-of-distribution ScannedRxn benchmark, Gemini 3.0 Pro + IdtVP attains a SOTA 86.5% Soft Match F1 in zero-shot, and RxnID+RL achieves 56.3% Hybrid F1 / 76.2% Soft F1 after fine-tuning. On RxnScribe-test, RxnCaption+RL (BIVP) leads with 75.9% Hybrid F1, while RxnID+RL reaches 75.0% Hybrid F1 and 85.9% Soft F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RxnID+RL (IdtVP) outperforms the prior best trained model RxnCaption+RL (BIVP) on RxnCaption-15k-test (64.4 vs. 64.1% Hybrid F1) and by a large margin on ScannedRxn (56.3 vs. 45.1% Hybrid F1), though BIVP+RL edges ahead on the small RxnScribe-test (75.9 vs. 75.0%). Against RxnScribe and RxnIM (both BROS), RxnID+RL leads by 20+ points on RxnCaption-15k-test (64.4 vs. 34.9% and 37.4%) and by 33.5 points on ScannedRxn (56.3 vs. 22.8% for RxnIM), confirming that the BROS paradigm generalizes poorly to historical scanned documents. The zero-shot Gemini 3.0 Pro + IdtVP surpasses all trained open-source models on every dataset, achieving 86.5% Soft F1 on ScannedRxn—far above the best trained model's 76.2%—demonstrating that IdtVP activates latent chemical knowledge without any task-specific training. Across all VLMs tested (Gemini, GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL, Intern-S1, Qwen-VL-Max), IdtVP consistently outperforms BIVP by 10–40+ points in Hybrid F1, validating the identifier-based prompting hypothesis. The Re³-DAPO RL stage adds 1–4% Hybrid F1 over SFT-only on in-distribution data and 11+ points on ScannedRxn for IdtVP, while BIVP+RL suffers a collapse (45.1% vs. 51.0% without RL) on out-of-distribution data, highlighting IdtVP's superior RL stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RxnCaption line of work [22], which introduced BIVP (Box Indexing as Visual Prompts) to replace the earlier BROS (direct coordinate regression) paradigm for reaction diagram parsing. The key departure is the proposal of IdtVP, which replaces detected bounding boxes with naturally occurring molecule identifiers (e.g., bold numerals like 1a) as visual prompts, arguing that this aligns better with the interleaved image-text patterns in VLM pretraining corpora. On the training side, the paper moves beyond the SFT-only paradigm used by RxnCaption and RxnScribe [18], introducing Re³-DAPO—a verifiable-reward RL algorithm that optimizes permutation-invariant, reaction-level metrics rather than token-level cross-entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IdtVP strategy offers a practical, zero-shot-friendly prompting technique that could transfer to other VLM tasks where domain-specific identifiers naturally appear in both images and text, and it uniquely enables cross-modal verification between diagram parsing and manuscript text extraction. The ScannedRxn benchmark, while small (200 samples), addresses a real gap in evaluating robustness on historical printed literature with scanning artifacts. However, the RL component's evidence is limited to a single 7B full-scale run and a smaller 3B multi-seed study, and the cross-modal verification pipeline is explicitly left as future work, so the broader system-level impact remains unproven. Open questions include whether the IdtVP advantage persists as base models improve, and how to optimally integrate textual context into the parsing objective.
