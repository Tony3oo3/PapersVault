## Problem

Sampling-based isosurface extraction (e.g., Marching Cubes) inevitably introduces discretization error between the extracted mesh and the network's true zero-level set, while existing analytic methods are either restricted to plain ReLU MLPs that suffer from spectral bias or rely on geometric heuristics (as in TropicalNeRF) that do not guarantee exact zero-level set recovery. The paper addresses the open question of how to retain the high-frequency expressiveness of grid-based positional encoders while still enabling exact, analytic isosurface extraction from the learned SDF.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D point coordinates (preconditioned by a fixed linear transform A*) and produces a triangle mesh whose vertices lie exactly on the network's zero-level set. The SDF network is trained in a supervised manner on 500K sampled SDF query points per shape (majority from a narrow surface band, remainder uniform in the bounding volume), using an ℓ₁ SDF loss plus an eikonal regularizer. The encoder uses L=4 resolution levels with per-level feature dimension d=2, followed by a 3-layer ReLU MLP of width 12; resolution settings range from (N_min, N_max) = (2, 32) to (8, 128).

## Method

TetraSDF replaces the trilinear interpolation in multi-resolution hash grids with barycentric interpolation over a six-tetrahedra-per-cube subdivision, preserving the global continuous piecewise affine (CPWA) structure of the composed encoder-and-MLP mapping. This CPWA property enables exact analytic isosurface extraction via an extended edge subdivision algorithm that jointly tracks encoder-induced polyhedral cells (indexed by region indicators and barycentric masks) and ReLU linear regions (indexed by sign vectors), using a tensorized GPU-parallel formulation for efficient cell and neighbor enumeration. A closed-form input preconditioner A* is derived from the encoder's average local metric to whiten the anisotropic directional sensitivity of barycentric weights, reducing the spectral condition number from 16.39 to 5.05 and improving SDF learning accuracy. The final mesh is extracted by selecting zero-level vertices and edges from the subdivided skeleton and connecting them according to local connectivity, yielding a triangle mesh that matches the network's isosurface to machine precision.

## Results

On the Large resolution setting, TetraSDF achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (CD ×10⁻⁶) to ground-truth meshes on ABC (1758) and Thingi10K (1722), and is competitive on Stanford (1718 vs. HashGrid's 1659). In self-consistency, TetraSDF achieves SSDF ≈ 0.078 ×10⁻⁶ and AD = 0.00° on all three datasets, matching Analytic Marching at near-machine precision and outperforming Marching Cubes at 1024³ by roughly 10³×. The method uses ~29K–281K vertices depending on resolution setting, compared to 409K–688K for sampling-based extractors at comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TetraSDF substantially outperforms Analytic Marching (AM) and plain ReLU MLP on CD across all datasets (e.g., 1722 vs. 3775 on Thingi10K), because the tetrahedral positional encoder provides far greater shape expressiveness than a plain MLP, while both methods achieve near-machine-precision self-consistency (SSDF ~0.02–0.08 ×10⁻⁶, AD = 0.00°). Against PermutoGrid, TetraSDF wins on all three datasets by a wide margin (1718/1758/1722 vs. 3644/3104/2897). Compared to HashGrid, TetraSDF is slightly worse on Stanford (1718 vs. 1659) but better on ABC (1758 vs. 1854) and Thingi10K (1722 vs. 1763), and critically achieves exact self-consistency (SSDF ≈ 0.078 ×10⁻⁶) where HashGrid + MC1024 yields SSDF = 17.6 ×10⁻⁶. TropicalNeRF is beaten on CD on all datasets (1718/1758/1722 vs. 1737/1866/1809) and dramatically on self-consistency (SSDF 0.078 vs. 144.1 ×10⁻⁶), confirming that its trilinear-interpolation heuristics introduce significant geometric error. Against sampling-based extractors (MC, MT, DC), TetraSDF matches or exceeds their CD while using 10×–20× fewer vertices and avoiding staircase artifacts, with self-consistency orders of magnitude better than even MC1024.

## Relation to Prior Work

TetraSDF sits at the intersection of two previously incompatible design goals in neural implicit surface pipelines: the expressiveness of multi-resolution grid-based positional encoders (HashGrid, PermutoSDF) and the exact CPWA-based analytic isosurface extraction pioneered by Analytic Marching and refined by Berzins' edge subdivision. TropicalNeRF attempted to bridge this gap by interpreting trilinear grid interpolation as piecewise trilinear, but its analytic guarantee relies on a diagonal-plane heuristic that does not yield exact zero-level set recovery. TetraSDF's key architectural departure is replacing trilinear interpolation with barycentric interpolation over a tetrahedral subdivision of each cube cell, which preserves the global CPWA structure while retaining the high-frequency modeling capacity of grid-based encoders, and then extending the edge subdivision algorithm to jointly track encoder-induced polyhedral cells and ReLU linear regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that practitioners can now use expressive multi-resolution encoders for SDF learning without sacrificing the guarantee that the extracted mesh exactly matches the network's zero-level set—a property that sampling-based methods (MC, MT, DC) can only approximate at increasing triangle budgets. The self-consistency improvement over MC1024 by roughly three orders of magnitude, combined with competitive or superior Chamfer Distance to ground truth, makes this a drop-in upgrade for pipelines that require geometrically faithful meshes (collision checking, normal alignment, mesh-based supervision). Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CPWA activations or higher-dimensional fields, how it behaves under end-to-end differentiable reconstruction where the mesh is a supervision signal, and whether the fixed six-tetrahedra subdivision is optimal or whether adaptive tetrahedralization (as in Tetra-NeRF) could further improve accuracy at the cost of complicating the analytic extraction.
