## Problem

Artistic text (WordArt) recognition is substantially harder than general scene text recognition due to extreme variation in font shape, texture, layout, and aspect ratio, yet existing STR datasets are too small (e.g., WordArt provides only 4,805 training images) and too stylistically narrow to train modern models. Additionally, mainstream STR architectures resize all inputs to fixed templates, which severely distorts the highly irregular shapes characteristic of artistic text, creating a structural bottleneck that no amount of data alone can resolve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260547/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images of artistic text at arbitrary resolution and aspect ratio, rescaling them while preserving the native width-to-height ratio so that the resulting token count (with patch size 4) falls within a flexible range of 64–256 rather than a fixed grid. The output is a character string of up to 25 tokens drawn from a 94-symbol charset (digits, letters, common symbols), generated autoregressively one character at a time, which accommodates non-standard reading orders found in curved, circular, or multi-oriented artistic layouts.

## Method

WATERec pairs a 6-layer NaViT-style Vision Transformer encoder with RoPE positional encoding—enabling it to process variable-length token sequences from arbitrary-shaped inputs without predefined resizing templates—with a 2-layer cross-attention autoregressive Transformer decoder that predicts the text sequence step by step under cross-entropy loss. On the data side, the paper constructs WATER-S (2M synthetic images) from two complementary pipelines: WATER-T, rendered by a custom engine (SynthWordArt) that pastes text using 11,250 artistic fonts with curved, vertical, multi-orientation, and perspective layouts onto background images; and WATER-Z, generated by Z-Image-Turbo conditioned on 273K prompts automatically mined from real artistic text via Qwen3-VL-8B few-shot caption expansion. Trained on the deduplicated real set WATER-R (3.2M instances) augmented with WATER-S, WATERec achieves 90.40% accuracy on WordArt-Bench, surpassing both general-purpose and OCR-specialized VLMs (best 81.54%) by a wide margin, and ablations confirm that arbitrary-shape modeling with RoPE and the AR decoder each contribute meaningfully to handling complex artistic layouts.

## Results

WATERec achieves 88.55% accuracy on WordArt-Bench (A-Bench) using only real data (WATER-R), and reaches 90.40% when augmented with 2M synthetic samples (WATER-S), the first result to exceed 90% on this benchmark. On the Union14M-Benchmark, WATERec attains 88.14% average (WATER-R) and 89.38% (WATER-R + WATER-S), outperforming all prior STR methods. On common STR benchmarks (C-Bench), WATERec scores 98.26% average, comparable to the best fixed-template methods. The best OCR-specialized VLM (HunyuanOCR) reaches only 81.54% on WordArt-Bench, and the best general VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) reaches 78.36%, leaving a gap of 8.9–11.8 points versus the expert baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WATERec outperforms the strongest CTC-based method SVTRv2 (86.56%) by 2.0 points and the best AR baseline SVTRv2-AR (87.36%) by 1.2 points on WordArt-Bench, with the gap widening to 3.8 points when synthetic data is added (90.40% vs. 88.68% for SVTRv2 + WATER-S). On the Union14M-Benchmark, WATERec leads all baselines on the Artistic subset (80.56% vs. 80.33% for SVTRv2-AR) and the overall average (88.14% vs. 87.63%), while MAERec slightly edges out on the more regular C-Bench (98.26% vs. 98.26%, essentially tied). Among PD-based methods, ABINet (84.64%) and CPPD (81.60%) trail WATERec by 3.9 and 6.9 points respectively on WordArt-Bench, confirming that parallel decoding struggles with complex artistic layouts. Against VLMs, WATERec surpasses the best OCR-specialized model HunyuanOCR (81.54%) by 7.0 points and the best general VLM Qwen3-VL-8B (78.36%) by 10.2 points on WordArt-Bench; even after LoRA fine-tuning with WATER-R + WATER-S, Qwen3-VL-8B reaches only 84.78%, still 5.6 points below WATERec. The synthetic data WATER-S provides consistent gains across all four tested architectures (SVTRv2, ABINet, SVTRv2-AR, WATERec), with improvements of +1.85% to +2.78% on WordArt-Bench, demonstrating broad utility rather than model-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the artistic text recognition line initiated by CornerTransformer (Xie et al., ECCV 2022) and the WordArt/WAS-R datasets, addressing the data scarcity bottleneck that has limited progress. On the model side, it repurposes the NaViT arbitrary-aspect-ratio encoder (Dehghani et al., NeurIPS 2023) and RoPE positional encoding (Heo et al., ECCV 2024)—both established in the VLM literature—into a lightweight STR backbone, departing from the fixed-template or predefined-multi-template resizing used by SVTRv2 and prior STR methods. On the data side, it combines the classical tool-rendering paradigm (SynthText, SynthTIGER) with a novel VLM-mined prompt + diffusion generation pipeline (Qwen3-VL + Z-Image-Turbo), a combination not previously explored for artistic text specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2M-sample WATER-S dataset (hundreds of times larger than prior artistic text data) and the WATER-R real training set provide a scalable foundation that the authors demonstrate is broadly useful across CTC, PD, and AR architectures, not just their own model. The ~9-point gap between the best OCR-specialized VLM (81.54%) and the 26M-parameter specialized model (90.40%) on WordArt-Bench is a concrete signal that current VLMs still lack fine-grained perception for highly stylized, layout-complex text, motivating continued investment in task-specific approaches. Open questions include whether multilingual extension is straightforward (the pipeline is language-agnostic in principle), whether VLMs augmented with chain-of-thought reasoning can close the gap, and whether the 2:3 synthetic-to-real ratio generalizes to other low-resource text domains.
