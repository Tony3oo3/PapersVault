## Problem

Equivariant neural networks that enforce geometric symmetries (rotations, reflections) typically incur higher computational cost than their non-equivariant counterparts, limiting their adoption at scale. While prior work demonstrated that reflection-equivariant (D2) ViTs could be made more efficient than standard ViTs, extending this to full dihedral D8 equivariance (rotations and reflections) and understanding how FLOP reductions translate into real throughput gains on modern GPUs remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260894/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images, which are patchified into tokens of size P×P (typically P=14 or 16) and processed through D8-equivariant ViT blocks where each token's channel dimension is decomposed into irreducible representations of the octic group. For classification, the steerable features are invariantized (mapped to the trivial A1 irrep) before a standard classification head produces a class label; for segmentation, the same frozen features are used with linear or k-NN probes to produce pixel-level masks on ADE20K and VOC2012.

## Method

Quick ViTs implement D8-equivariant linear layers by working in the Fourier domain of the dihedral group: the channel dimension C is split into C/8 subspaces for each of the five irreps (A1, A2, B1, B2, E), and linear maps are constrained to be block-diagonal across irreps, reducing FLOPs by a factor of 5.33 and parameters by 8× compared to ordinary linear layers. Two architecture variants are proposed: I8 (fully equivariant throughout with invariantization at the end) and H8 (equivariant in the first k blocks, standard transformer blocks in the remaining l−k blocks, which empirically outperforms I8). A custom Triton kernel fuses the GELU nonlinearity with the forward and inverse D8 Fourier transforms to minimize memory transfers. The paper further analyzes the arithmetic intensity of these layers, showing that at low embedding dimensions the FLOP savings are partially offset by memory-bound execution, but the gap closes as channel dimensions grow, with ViT-G and ViT-e sizes approaching the theoretical 5.33× speedup.

## Results

Under supervised DeiT-III training on ImageNet-1K, the hybrid H8(ViT-H/14) model achieves 85.0% top-1 accuracy (vs. 84.6% for the standard ViT-H/14) while using only 102.3 GFLOPs (61% of the baseline's 167.8 GFLOPs). The fully invariant I8(ViT-H/14) variant reaches 84.7% with 104.0 GFLOPs and is perfectly robust to out-of-distribution rotations (0.0% accuracy drop vs. −12.6% for the baseline). Under self-supervised DINOv2 pretraining, H8(ViT-H/16) improves linear-probe accuracy to 82.2% (vs. 81.7% baseline) and k-NN to 81.4% (vs. 81.0%) at 77.5 GFLOPs (61% of baseline), while also matching or exceeding segmentation mIoU on ADE20K and VOC2012.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard ViT baseline, H8(ViT-H/14) wins by +0.4% top-1 accuracy (85.0 vs. 84.6) while reducing FLOPs by 39% and peak memory by 32%; I8(ViT-H/14) essentially ties at 84.7% with similar savings. Against the reflection-equivariant H2 baseline from Bökman et al. [11], H8(ViT-H/14) matches accuracy (85.0%) but uses 20% fewer FLOPs (102.3 vs. 127.4 GFLOPs) and 19% fewer parameters (355.8M vs. 474.2M); the same pattern holds at ViT-L scale where H8(ViT-L/16) matches H2 at 84.5% with 20% fewer FLOPs. The key differentiator of I8 over both baselines is perfect invariance to out-of-distribution rotations (0.0% drop vs. −12.6% to −13.9% for all other models), at the cost of a small accuracy penalty (−0.3% for ViT-H, −0.2% for ViT-L) relative to H8. Under DINOv2 self-supervised pretraining, H8(ViT-H/16) outperforms the standard ViT-H/16 on all four downstream metrics (linear and k-NN on ImageNet-1K, mIoU on ADE20K and VOC2012) while using 39% fewer FLOPs, and I8(ViT-H/16) matches the baseline on classification while slightly trailing on ADE20K segmentation (33.9 vs. 34.7 mIoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Bökman et al. [11], which showed that D2 (reflection) equivariance in ViTs can be implemented efficiently via Fourier-domain linear layers, to the full D8 dihedral group (rotations + reflections), yielding larger computational savings. It builds on the standard ViT architecture [22] and can be viewed as a scalable ViT analogue of octic steerable CNNs [16], but unlike prior equivariant architectures (G-CNNs, steerable CNNs, equivariant transformers for point clouds) that typically incur computational overhead, the Fourier-domain construction here makes equivariant layers strictly cheaper than their non-equivariant counterparts. The paper also departs from prior equivariant efficiency analyses by going beyond FLOP counting to analyze arithmetic intensity, connecting theoretical FLOP savings to achievable GPU throughput.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides clear evidence that equivariance can be a source of efficiency rather than overhead at ViT scale, addressing an open debate in the equivariant networks literature. The 5.33× FLOP reduction and up to 8× memory reduction per feature dimension, validated across both supervised (DeiT-III) and self-supervised (DINOv2) training at ViT-L and ViT-H scale, make this a practically relevant technique for efficient vision backbones. Key limitations include restriction to D8 (larger dihedral groups are only discussed theoretically), no hyperparameter re-tuning, and the arithmetic intensity analysis showing that actual throughput gains (1.3–3.5×) fall well short of FLOP savings at current model scales, though the gap narrows as embedding dimensions grow. Open questions include whether the approach combines with other efficiency techniques (pruning, quantization), how it scales to ViT-G and beyond, and its utility in geometrically demanding tasks like 3D reconstruction and feature matching.
