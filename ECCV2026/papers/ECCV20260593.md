## Problem

Post-hoc OOD detection methods typically derive scores from a single representation level or aggregate layer-wise scores using OOD-calibrated weights, but the most informative layer varies substantially across OOD types and application domains. This makes fixed-layer choices and OOD-dependent aggregation brittle in cross-domain deployment, where representative held-out OOD data for calibration may be unavailable.

## Input / Output

PRISM is a post-hoc, model-agnostic method that operates on any pretrained classifier (CNN or ViT) without retraining. It consumes an input image resized to the backbone's expected resolution, extracts pooled features from uniformly spaced intermediate layers, and produces a single scalar OOD score (higher = more ID-like) that can be thresholded for a binary ID/OOD decision. All statistics (PCA subspace, class-conditional means and covariance) are estimated exclusively from ID training data, with a fixed PCA dimension of d=512 across all domains and architectures.

## Method

PRISM follows a fuse-then-model principle: instead of scoring each layer independently and aggregating, it first constructs a unified multi-layer representation by extracting features from L uniformly spaced intermediate layers, applying architecture-agnostic pooling (global average pooling for CNNs, token averaging for ViTs), L2-normalizing each vector, and concatenating them into a single feature vector z(x) ∈ R^D. It then estimates a d-dimensional principal subspace via PCA on ID data and decomposes each test feature into a projection u(x) onto this subspace and an orthogonal residual r(x). The final OOD score is the negative product of two complementary signals: a class-conditional Mahalanobis distance computed in the projected subspace (capturing in-subspace semantic deviations) and the residual energy ||r(x)||² (capturing off-subspace anomalies). This multiplicative combination introduces no balancing coefficients and requires no OOD-dependent calibration, while the per-layer L2 normalization and fixed subspace dimension ensure stability across architectures and domains.

## Results

PRISM achieves the best overall mean AUROC of 93.92% across all three benchmarks (OpenOOD, OpenMIBOOD, ICONIC-444), improving over the second-best method MDS++ (89.13%) by 4.79 points. On OpenMIBOOD, PRISM reaches 94.86% AUROC, substantially outperforming the best ID-only baseline MDS++ (84.09%) and approaching the OOD-calibrated MDSEns (98.41%). On ICONIC-444, PRISM attains 96.56%, closely matching the strongest industrial baseline GRAM (97.55%). PRISM is the only method that consistently remains among the top performers across all three domains with a single fixed configuration (PCA dimension d=512, no OOD calibration).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM outperforms all 22 baselines on overall mean AUROC (93.92% vs. 89.13% for MDS++), and is the only method to rank in the top 3 on all three domain means simultaneously. On OpenOOD, PRISM (90.36%) surpasses GRAM (87.46%) and MDS++ (87.16%), while on OpenMIBOOD it achieves 94.86%, a 10.77-point gain over MDS++ (84.09%) and the strongest ID-only alternative to the OOD-calibrated MDSEns (98.41%). On ICONIC-444, PRISM (96.56%) trails GRAM (97.55%) by 1 point but exceeds MDS++ (96.12%). Critically, competing methods show severe domain-dependent degradation: MDSEns collapses to 54.41% on ImageNet-1k due to OOD-calibration sensitivity, GRAM drops to 75.12% on medical tasks, and DICE/FeatNorm fail on multiple domains (e.g., DICE: 30.49% on OASIS-3; FeatNorm: 35.99% on ICONIC-444). PRISM's advantage is most pronounced on medical benchmarks, where its unified multi-layer representation captures complementary shallow and deep cues that single-layer methods miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM extends the Mahalanobis-distance OOD detection framework of Lee et al. (NeurIPS 2018) and the PCA-subspace residual-energy idea of ViM (Wang et al., CVPR 2022) by applying both to a fused multi-layer representation rather than a single penultimate-layer feature. The key conceptual departure from prior multi-layer methods (MDSEns, ATS, GRAM, CORES, NAP) is a 'fuse-then-model' principle: instead of computing per-layer OOD scores and aggregating them with OOD-calibrated weights, PRISM concatenates normalized intermediate features first and performs statistical modeling once in the resulting joint space. This eliminates the layer-selection and aggregation heuristics that prior work showed to be sensitive to the specific OOD validation distribution used for calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single, ID-only configuration (fixed PCA dimension d=512, fixed layer sampling) achieves state-of-the-art cross-domain OOD detection across natural, medical, and industrial benchmarks without any OOD-dependent tuning, addressing a real deployment concern where representative OOD data are unavailable. The ablation revealing domain-dependent dominance of the two scoring components (projected Mahalanobis on natural/industrial, residual energy on medical) suggests that no single geometric signal suffices universally, motivating future work on adaptive or learned fusion that retains transferability. Limitations include the fixed layer-sampling strategy and the multiplicative score combination, which, while simple, may not be optimal for all shift types; the authors acknowledge that spatially adaptive pooling or tuned layer weights could improve individual benchmarks at the cost of transferability.
