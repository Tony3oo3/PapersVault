## Problem

Existing human animation methods face a fundamental dilemma: 2D pose-conditioned approaches suffer from motion-shape entanglement, leaking the driving subject's body proportions into the output, while 3D parametric model methods (e.g., SMPL) achieve geometric disentanglement but lack the capacity to represent facial expressions and complex hand gestures, producing rigid animations. Additionally, a pervasive training-inference domain gap—where models are trained on spatially aligned self-driven data but must handle misaligned cross-driven inputs—causes identity drift and visual artifacts in long video generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (providing identity and body shape) and a driving video (providing motion, expression, and camera trajectory) as input, and produces a temporally coherent animated human video at 512×512 resolution. During training, 77-frame clips are sampled; during inference, long videos are generated autoregressively in overlapping chunks. The driving video is first processed by a motion tracker to extract per-frame EHM parameters (body pose θ, facial expression ψ, body shape β_b, head shape β_f) and camera parameters C, which are then retargeted to the reference subject's shape before being rendered into hybrid 2D condition maps.

## Method

EMOSH uses the Expressive Human Model (EHM), which integrates SMPL-X with FLAME to explicitly decouple body shape, head shape, pose, and expression, as its core control representation. A Confidence-Aware Motion Tracker jointly optimizes all EHM parameters via differentiable rendering with validity gating that filters unreliable keypoint signals under occlusion or large-angle views. A Coarse-to-Fine Hybrid Motion Injection strategy renders semantically colored 3D meshes (coarse geometric structure) and overlays sparse 2D keypoints (fine high-frequency details like blinks) to form the motion condition, which is encoded and added to the noisy latent. Motion-shape disentanglement is achieved by retargeting the driving mesh: pose and expression come from the driver while shape parameters are replaced with those estimated from the reference image. A Spatially-Aligned Conditioning mechanism injects the latent of the first generated frame as an additional anchor into all subsequent chunks, bridging the training-inference spatial misalignment gap and preventing identity drift. The entire pipeline is built on the Wan2.1-I2V DiT backbone (initialized with Wan-Animate weights) and trained with a Flow Matching objective.

## Results

Under the self-driven setting, EMOSH achieves the best performance across all four metrics (PSNR, L1, SSIM, LPIPS) on all three datasets. On EchoMimicV2, it reaches PSNR 23.66, SSIM 0.8240, and LPIPS 0.1428, outperforming the strongest 2D-pose baseline Wan-Animate (PSNR 22.86, SSIM 0.7750, LPIPS 0.1802) and the mesh-based Champ (PSNR 17.40, SSIM 0.6502, LPIPS 0.2911). Under the cross-driven setting, EMOSH attains the highest Identity Preservation Score (IPS) of 0.4445, surpassing Wan-Animate (0.3802) and UniAnimate-DiT (0.3130). In human evaluation, 89–98% of participants prefer EMOSH over Wan-Animate, HyperMotion, and UniAnimate-DiT respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMOSH outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three self-driven datasets. Against the strongest 2D-pose method Wan-Animate, EMOSH gains +0.80 PSNR and −0.037 LPIPS on EchoMimicV2, and +0.62 PSNR on the self-collected set, while also achieving superior SSIM (0.8240 vs. 0.7750). The mesh-based Champ, the only other 3D-prior method, trails EMOSH by a large margin (e.g., PSNR 17.40 vs. 23.66 on EchoMimicV2), confirming that EHM's expressive parameters (face + hands) are critical over coarse SMPL-only guidance. In the cross-driven IPS evaluation, EMOSH (0.4445) leads Wan-Animate (0.3802) by 0.064 and UniAnimate-DiT (0.3130) by 0.132, demonstrating that explicit EHM retargeting effectively prevents body-shape leakage. HyperMotion (0.2872) and Moore-AA (0.2174) show progressively weaker identity preservation, while StableAnimator (0.0935) and MimicMotion (0.0744) suffer severe identity drift under cross-identity driving. In the human GSB study, EMOSH is preferred over Wan-Animate 89.2% of the time, over HyperMotion 98.4%, and over UniAnimate-DiT 95.6%, corroborating the quantitative gains in perceived quality, motion accuracy, and identity fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMOSH sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: 2D pose-conditioned diffusion animators (Animate Anyone, HyperMotion, Wan-Animate) that achieve expressive control but leak the driver's body shape, and 3D parametric mesh methods (Champ, RealisDance) that geometrically disentangle shape but lack facial and hand expressiveness. The paper departs from both by adopting the Expressive Human Model (EHM, which unifies SMPL-X body shape with FLAME head/expression parameters) as a single control representation, and by building on the Wan2.1-I2V DiT backbone initialized with Wan-Animate's pre-trained weights. The Coarse-to-Fine Hybrid Motion Injection and Spatially-Aligned Conditioning mechanisms are novel contributions layered on top of this architecture to address high-frequency detail loss and the training-inference spatial misalignment gap, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving the long-standing expressiveness-vs-disentanglement dilemma with a single parametric model, EMOSH offers a practical recipe that other DiT-based animators could adopt, particularly the EHM retargeting strategy and the Spatially-Aligned Conditioning trick for long-video identity stability. Open questions remain: EHM currently covers the upper body, so full-body animation (legs, feet) is not addressed; the motion tracker's joint optimization, while more robust than cascaded pipelines, still depends on off-the-shelf estimators (GVHMR, HaMeR, TEASER) whose failure modes propagate; and the method's reliance on a 512×512 resolution and 77-frame clips limits direct applicability to high-resolution or very long production pipelines. The paper also notes limitations in the supplementary material but does not deeply explore failure cases under extreme occlusion or multi-person scenes.
