## Problem

All existing long-term action anticipation methods are evaluated exclusively in closed-set settings where training and test action vocabularies are identical, making it impossible to assess whether models generalize to novel action classes encountered in real-world deployment. This paper introduces the first open-vocabulary evaluation protocol for long-term action anticipation, training on Ego4D and testing on entirely different egocentric datasets (EPIC-Kitchens-100, EGTEA+) with minimal action overlap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261684/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes O=8 observed egocentric video clips (32 uniformly sampled frames per clip) and predicts Z=20 future verb-noun action pairs in chronological order, evaluated by edit distance. In the open-vocabulary setting, the test action vocabulary (e.g., 97 verbs × 300 nouns for EK100) is largely disjoint from the training vocabulary (117 verbs × 521 nouns for Ego4D), so predictions are made by matching visual embeddings against text-encoded class names rather than fixed classification heads. The same temporal structure (8 observed → 20 predicted) is applied uniformly across all three datasets for comparable evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is the Multi-Horizon Cross-Attention Decoder (MHCAD), which maintains Z horizon-specific learnable queries alongside O observed CLS tokens, allowing each future action to attend to different observed features throughout the entire decoder rather than relying on a single aggregated representation with separate per-horizon heads. Multi-scale spatial features from the last L_d layers of a frozen LaViLa-Large ViT are encoded with factored 4D Rotary Position Embeddings (segment, frame, height, width) and fed as key-value pairs into L_d=2 decoder layers that alternate causal self-attention among queries with cross-attention to encoder features. For open-vocabulary prediction, dual frozen BERT-style text encoders (one for verbs, one for nouns) are adapted via lightweight bottleneck adapters, and predictions are made by CLIP-style cosine similarity between projected query embeddings and text-encoded class names. The model is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss on verb and noun predictions, using only 157M trainable parameters (489M frozen), and substantially outperforms both MultiHead baselines and billion-parameter LLM/VLM approaches on the open-vocabulary benchmark.

## Results

On the novel open-vocabulary evaluation (trained on Ego4D, tested on EK100 and EGTEA+), LaViLa-MHCAD with the large backbone achieves edit distance of 0.728/0.936 (verb/noun) on EK100 and 0.864/0.927 on EGTEA+, substantially outperforming the best LLM/VLM baselines (Video-LLaVA: 0.925/0.945 and 0.959/0.970) despite using only 157M trainable parameters versus 7.2B. In the closed-set Ego4D v2 test, MHCAD achieves 0.689/0.695 edit distance, beating LaViLa-MultiHead (0.720/0.727) and SlowFast (0.717/0.736) while remaining competitive with 13B-parameter AntGPT (0.650/0.650). The method also outperforms all sub-1B baselines (VCLIP, ICVAE, HierVL, XCLIP-MultiHead) across all reported splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaViLa-MHCAD outperforms all 13 compared baselines in both closed-set and open-vocabulary settings. In the open-vocabulary evaluation, it beats the two LLM/VLM models (AntGPT with 13.3B frozen params: 0.933/0.958 vs. 0.728/0.936 on EK100; Video-LLaVA with 7.2B trained params: 0.925/0.945 vs. 0.728/0.936) by 0.19–0.21 in verb edit distance, demonstrating that task-specific architectural design with cross-attention decoding surpasses brute-force scaling. Among sub-1B methods, MHCAD improves over LaViLa-MultiHead by 0.121/0.032 (verb/noun) on EK100 and 0.040/0.031 on EGTEA+, and over XCLIP-MultiHead by 0.140/0.035 on EK100, confirming that horizon-specific queries with cross-attention aggregation are critical for coherent multi-horizon prediction. In the closed-set Ego4D v2 test, MHCAD (0.689/0.695) beats SlowFast (0.717/0.736), VCLIP, ICVAE, and HierVL, and approaches the 13B-parameter AntGPT (0.650/0.650) with only 157M trainable parameters. The LLM/VLM baselines (VideoLLM, Video-LLaMA, PlausiVL, PALM) are reported only in the closed-set setting and are not directly comparable due to their 7B+ parameter scale and massive text pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ego4D long-term action anticipation protocol from a closed-set to an open-vocabulary setting, where models trained on Ego4D must predict actions from entirely different egocentric datasets (EPIC-Kitchens-100, EGTEA+). Architecturally, it departs from the standard multi-head decoder paradigm (separate prediction heads per future horizon, as in ICVAE, VCLIP, and the Ego4D baseline) by proposing MHCAD, a cross-attention decoder with horizon-specific learnable queries that progressively aggregate multi-scale visual features. The open-vocabulary prediction mechanism repurposes the CLIP-style text-visual matching paradigm, with lightweight bottleneck adapters on frozen BERT-based text encoders, while the 4D rotary position encoding adapts RoPE from the NLP literature to the spatio-temporal structure of video tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The open-vocabulary evaluation protocol exposes a critical blind spot in current action anticipation research: models that perform well in closed-set settings degrade substantially when confronted with unseen action vocabularies, with verb edit distances jumping from ~0.69 to ~0.73–0.93 depending on the method. The finding that a 646M-parameter model (489M frozen + 157M trained) outperforms 7B+ parameter LLM/VLM systems on open-vocabulary anticipation challenges the assumption that scale and general-purpose language priors are necessary for this task. Open questions include whether the protocol generalizes beyond kitchen/cooking domains, whether truly open-ended (unbounded) action vocabularies can be handled, and whether the MHCAD design transfers to other anticipation or forecasting tasks.
