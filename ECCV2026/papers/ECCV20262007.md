## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks assess outcome correctness via last-frame verification or physical plausibility of individual phenomena, but none explicitly evaluate whether multi-step causal relationships remain coherent across the full generated sequence. This paper formalizes 'reasoning coherence'—the degree to which generated events maintain consistent cause-and-effect dynamics over time—and identifies the absence of a controlled evaluation framework that isolates reasoning guidance as an independent variable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262007/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample provides an input image paired with a text instruction, optionally augmented with text hints (explicit step descriptions appended to the prompt) or visual hints (bounding boxes and directional arrows drawn on the input image). The evaluated models produce 4–5 second video clips at 24 fps, which are uniformly sampled at every 10th frame for evaluation. The Reasoning Score is computed per-sample as the fraction of N human-validated intermediate reasoning steps correctly completed, judged independently by Gemini 2.5-Flash, while generation quality is scored across five dimensions (consistency, temporal consistency, visual stability, hallucination, physics grounding).

## Method

MME-CoF-Pro is a 303-sample, 16-category benchmark curated from 27 existing real-world and synthetic datasets, with each sample annotated by domain experts with a sequence of minimal necessary reasoning steps serving as process-level checkpoints. The key evaluation metric, Reasoning Score (RS), scores each intermediate step independently via a VLM-as-a-judge protocol over equidistant frames, enabling fine-grained diagnosis of where in the reasoning chain a model fails rather than discarding partial correctness. The benchmark introduces three controlled hint settings—no-hint, text-hint, and visual-hint—where all instruction components remain identical except the hint, allowing causal attribution of performance differences to the guidance modality. Visual hints (bounding boxes, arrows) are applied to a subset of 8 perceptually demanding categories (MME-CoF-Pro-mini), while text hints cover all 16 categories. A human study validates RS as the metric most aligned with human judgment (Spearman ρ = 0.61) compared to instruction alignment (0.17) and pass@5 last-frame correctness (−0.41).

## Results

Across 7 video generative models evaluated on MME-CoF-Pro (303 samples, 16 categories), the best Reasoning Score (RS) under no-hint is 55.9% (Veo-3.1 and Veo-3.1-fast), while Sora-2 achieves the highest Generation Quality average (60.9%) and Consistency Score (60.5%). Notably, Kling-v2.1 achieves the highest Avg score (65.1%) yet the lowest RS (13.8%), demonstrating a clear decoupling between visual generation quality and reasoning coherence. Text hints improve overall RS by +3.1 to +7.9 points across models but consistently degrade CS, with all 7 models showing CS drops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Veo-3.1 and Veo-3.1-fast lead in Reasoning Score (55.9% each), outperforming Sora-2 (49.9%) by ~6 points, while Sora-2 dominates in Consistency Score (60.5%) and overall Generation Quality (60.9%). Kling-v2.1 presents the starkest contrast: it achieves the highest Avg (65.1%) and second-highest CS (58.3%) yet the lowest RS (13.8%), confirming that strong visual fidelity does not imply reasoning ability. Cosmos-Predict2-14B (open-source) lags behind all closed-source models in both RS (28.6%) and Avg (37.8%), though it benefits most from text hints (+7.9 RS). In the human study, the proposed Reasoning Score achieves Spearman correlation of 0.61 with human judgments, substantially outperforming Instruction Alignment (0.17) and Pass@5 Correctness (−0.41) from prior benchmarks, validating RS as a more faithful measure of reasoning coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MME-CoF [15], which first evaluated video generative models as zero-shot reasoners, by introducing reasoning coherence (causal consistency across frames) as an explicit evaluation dimension and adding controlled text/visual hint settings as a manipulable input variable. It departs from prior video reasoning benchmarks (V-ReasonBench, Gen-ViRe, VIPER, VBVR) that primarily assess outcome correctness via last-frame verification or rule-based checks, by proposing a process-level Reasoning Score that evaluates intermediate reasoning steps against human-validated rubrics. The controlled hint design—keeping all instruction components identical except the hint modality—enables causal attribution of performance differences to reasoning guidance, a variable no prior benchmark isolates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reasoning coherence is decoupled from generation quality (e.g., Kling achieves 65.1 Avg but only 13.8 RS) challenges the assumption that visually impressive video models are also reliable reasoners, with direct implications for deployment in safety-critical or interactive applications. The consistent degradation of consistency under text hints across all seven models suggests current models pattern-match to textual scaffolds rather than genuinely grounding them, pointing to a fundamental limitation in instruction-following for video generation. Open questions include whether models can be trained to stably integrate multi-step hints without hallucination, and whether the non-monotonic hint scaling observed in the Frozen Lake case study reflects a general architectural limitation. Limitations include reliance on a single VLM judge (Gemini 2.5 Flash), moderate sample size (303), and evaluation of only 7 models.
