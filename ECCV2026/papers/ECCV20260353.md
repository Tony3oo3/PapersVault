## Problem

Model merging in computer vision has been evaluated almost exclusively on CLIP-based image classification, where frozen text-encoder decoders make hyperparameter search cheap. In practical multi-task vision settings, each task requires a trainable, often heterogeneous decoder, so evaluating a merged encoder demands costly decoder retraining per hyperparameter candidate, rendering exhaustive search intractable and limiting the applicability of merging to a narrow benchmark.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260353/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple task-specific encoder checkpoints (fine-tuned from a shared pretrained base such as DINOv2, DUNE, or WaffleIron) and produces a single merged encoder. Downstream, the merged encoder is paired with task-specific decoders to produce class labels, segmentation masks, depth maps, 3D human meshes, or camera poses from RGB images or LiDAR point clouds. Crucially, TAP itself requires only unlabeled samples (default 128 per task) and no access to task decoders or labels, making it applicable regardless of the output modality.

## Method

The core contribution is the Task Alignment Proxy (TAP), which estimates merge quality by measuring the average L2 distance between features produced by the merged encoder and each task-specific fine-tuned encoder on unlabeled task data, then averaging across tasks. Minimizing TAP over the merging hyperparameters (λ, μ) replaces the need to train and evaluate task-specific decoders for every candidate, reducing selection cost by 2–3 orders of magnitude. TAP is method-agnostic: it can be plugged into any arithmetic-based merging strategy (Task Arithmetic, TIES, TSV, STAR, Iso-CTS, Consensus) as a selection criterion, and it can also serve as a differentiable loss to extend AdaMerging from entropy-based (classification-only) optimization to arbitrary task sets. The paper validates TAP on three settings—CLIP classification, LiDAR semantic segmentation across four sensor-specific datasets, and the heterogeneous DUNE benchmark (segmentation, depth, mesh recovery, relocalization)—showing near-optimal hyperparameter selection without any downstream evaluation.

## Results

On the heterogeneous DUNE benchmark (ADE20k, NYUd, Bedlam, MapFree), AdaMerging w/TAP achieves 0.971 normalized performance, outperforming the pretrained DUNE baseline (0.929) by 4.2 points. On LiDAR segmentation across four sensors, AdaMerging w/TAP reaches 0.985 normalized mIoU versus 0.841 for the pretrained WaffleIron encoder. On the standard CLIP classification benchmark, TAP-based hyperparameter selection matches exhaustive evaluation-based selection within 0.1–0.5 points across all merging methods and architectures (e.g., Iso-CTS: 86.8 vs 86.9 average), while reducing selection cost by 2–3 orders of magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is TAP-based vs. evaluation-based hyperparameter selection: across all CLIP classification settings (Table 1), TAP selection matches or nearly matches exhaustive evaluation (e.g., TSV: 85.1 vs 85.2 avg; Iso-CTS: 86.8 vs 86.9 avg), confirming TAP is a faithful proxy. Among merging methods on the heterogeneous DUNE benchmark, AdaMerging w/TAP (0.971) clearly leads, followed by STAR w/TAP (0.962) and TA w/TAP (0.956), while TIES (0.934), Iso-CTS (0.934), TSV (0.936), and Consensus (0.928) remain close to or below the pretrained DUNE baseline (0.929). On LiDAR, TA, STAR, TSV, Iso-CTS, and AdaMerging w/TAP all substantially outperform the pretrained encoder (0.841), reaching 0.972–0.985 normalized performance, whereas TIES (0.879) and Consensus (0.898) offer only marginal gains. In the DINOv2-based experiment (Table 4), Iso-CTS w/TAP (0.915) and AdaMerging w/TAP (0.909) surpass the distilled DUNE model (0.847) in normalized performance, though no single merged model beats DUNE on all four tasks simultaneously, with Bedlam and MapFree remaining the weakest points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the arithmetic-based model merging literature (Task Arithmetic, TIES, TSV, STAR, Iso-CTS) and the adaptive merging line (AdaMerging), but departs from the dominant vision evaluation paradigm where CLIP's frozen text encoder serves as a shared decoder, making merged-model evaluation trivial. The key conceptual departure is the Task Alignment Proxy (TAP), which replaces expensive decoder retraining with a feature-space dissimilarity score for hyperparameter selection, and generalizes AdaMerging's entropy-based optimization to arbitrary (non-categorical) tasks. The paper also extends the evaluation scope to LiDAR point-cloud segmentation (WaffleIron encoder) and the DUNE heterogeneous 2D/3D benchmark, settings where prior merging work has not been applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAP makes model merging practically viable for the majority of real-world vision systems that rely on task-specific trainable decoders, reducing hyperparameter selection cost by 2–3 orders of magnitude without requiring labels or decoder access. The first demonstration of successful merging on LiDAR and on heterogeneous 2D/3D tasks (segmentation, depth, mesh recovery, relocalization) opens a previously intractable deployment scenario (e.g., a single encoder on a multi-sensor robot). A key open question highlighted by the paper is how to handle the large, unbalanced task-vector norms that emerge in non-CLIP settings; naive normalization fails, and no existing merging method is explicitly designed for this regime, suggesting a promising direction for future work.
