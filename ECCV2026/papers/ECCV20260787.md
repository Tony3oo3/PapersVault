## Problem

Cache-based test-time adaptation methods for vision-language models have shown strong results in single-label recognition but have been largely unexplored in the more practical multi-label setting. When directly extended to multi-label images, a single spatially pooled global feature must simultaneously represent all co-occurring objects and serve as a shared cache prototype, causing dominant-label bias and unreliable class-wise cache calibration. Moreover, while region-level cues can isolate class-specific evidence, naive region proposals are noisy and unreliable under distribution shifts, making their identification and utilization non-trivial in fully unsupervised test-time settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled test images in a streaming, online fashion (no ground-truth labels or offline fine-tuning) along with class text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [class]'). Each image is processed through a CLIP visual encoder to obtain a global feature and Q=50 local region features extracted via stochastic scaling and cropping. The output is a set of multi-label class predictions with per-class confidence scores, computed by combining global CLIP logits, purified-region local logits, and cache-retrieval logits, followed by a sigmoid activation for independent per-class binary decisions.

## Method

PuRF introduces two purification mechanisms. Region Purification identifies reliable regions by computing relative class dominance via softmax-normalized region-text similarities and applying adaptive class-wise thresholds (maintained via running averages) to filter out noisy or ambiguous crops; global-local prediction consistency then yields reliable pseudo-labels for BCE-based semantic optimization. Cache Purification operates at two timescales: Episodic Purification stores region-label pairs (the most confident purified region per pseudo-label) as class-specific prototypes rather than a single global feature, enabling multiple discriminative cache entries per sample; Temporal Refreshing applies an exponential time-decay penalty to the entropy of long-retained cache entries, preventing cache saturation and allowing later samples to replace stale early-stage entries. The overall objective jointly optimizes a marginal entropy loss, a pseudo-label BCE loss, and a cross-modal alignment loss, with lightweight learnable text residuals for distribution calibration.

## Results

PuRF achieves an average mAP of 71.48% on ViT-B/16 and 69.88% on ViT-B/32 across five multi-label benchmarks (VOC07, VOC12, COCO14, COCO17, NUS-WIDE), outperforming the strongest baseline ReTA by 3.59% and 4.05% mAP respectively. On the large-scale COCO2014 dataset, PuRF reaches 66.05% mAP (ViT-B/16), a 6.09% gain over ReTA. The method also generalizes to advanced VLM backbones (EVA-02, SigLIP2, MetaCLIP2), yielding 3.56–3.69% mAP improvements over DPE and ReTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PuRF consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and backbone. Against the strongest cache-based competitor ReTA [29], PuRF gains 4.05% mAP on ViT-B/32 and 3.59% on ViT-B/16, with the largest margin on COCO2014 (+6.09% on ViT-B/32) where dense label co-occurrence demands finer class-wise discrimination. Over DPE [65], the improvement is 5.41% (ViT-B/32) and 4.46% (ViT-B/16), attributed to purified region-level cache entries versus DPE's global prototypes. The prompt-based multi-label method ML-TTA [57] trails PuRF by 8.56% on ViT-B/32, confirming that cache-based historical retrieval with purification is more effective than prompt re-optimization for multi-label TTA. Among prompt-based single-label methods (TPT, DiffTPT, RLCF), PuRF leads by 6–10% mAP, while TDA and BoostAdapter (training-free cache methods) are surpassed by 4–6%. PuRF also maintains its lead under alternative prompt initializations (CoOp, MaPLe, CuPL templates), gaining 2.28–3.56% over ReTA in each setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

PuRF extends the cache-based test-time adaptation paradigm (Tip-Adapter, TDA, DPE, ReTA) from single-label to multi-label recognition, identifying a specific one-to-many mapping failure where a shared global feature entangles co-occurring objects and biases class-wise cache calibration. It builds directly on ReTA's residual text learning and adjacent text embeddings, and on DPE's prototype evolution and entropy-driven cache admission, but departs from both by replacing global features with purified region-level evidence for both prediction and cache storage. The method also draws on multi-label recognition literature (random crop pooling, residual attention) for region extraction, but repurposes these cues within a fully unsupervised, streaming test-time setting rather than supervised training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-label TTA is a practically important but underexplored problem: real-world images routinely contain multiple objects, yet nearly all prior TTA work targets single-label classification. PuRF provides the first cache-based solution to this gap and introduces a clean two-level purification framework (region + cache) that is modular and potentially transferable to other multi-label adaptation settings. Open questions include whether the region purification strategy scales to very large label spaces (e.g., 1000+ classes), whether the temporal refreshing mechanism generalizes to non-stationary or adversarial shifts, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or multi-modal multi-label tasks. The method's reliance on stochastic cropping for region generation also raises questions about sensitivity to object scale and occlusion in dense scenes.
