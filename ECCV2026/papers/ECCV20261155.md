## Problem

Existing RL post-training methods for multimodal diffusion language models rely on coarse outcome-level rewards and fail to exploit the unique bidimensional structure of diffusion generation—specifically, the interplay between the temporal dimension (iterative refinement across denoising timesteps) and the spatial dimension (reasoning structure across token positions). This leaves a gap in providing fine-grained credit assignment that simultaneously addresses logical soundness of intermediate reasoning steps and their grounding in visual evidence.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts an RGB image (512×512) and a text question. The image is either quantized into 1024 discrete VQ-VAE tokens prepended to the text stream (MMaDA pathway) or encoded into continuous SigLIP embeddings injected via cross-attention (LLaDA-V pathway). The model generates a chain-of-thought reasoning response using semi-autoregressive masked diffusion (max 256 tokens, block size 32), which is then partitioned into 128-token chunks for granular evaluation by LVLM judges.

## Method

Bi-VRL introduces a bidimensional optimization strategy with three synergistic components. Along the temporal axis, trajectory-level optimization builds on TraceRL to learn a value function V_φ over the full diffusion trajectory using GAE, propagating dense credit signals across denoising timesteps. Along the spatial axis, thought-level optimization decomposes the output into reasoning chunks and identifies causally significant tokens—those generated at the latest timesteps within each chunk's concluding segment—via a mapping function Φ, concentrating sparse rewards on these pivotal points. A fine-grained multimodal alignment mechanism employs a panel of LVLM judges to score each chunk on visual alignment, logical soundness, absence of unjustified conclusions, and internal consistency, producing the chunk-level rewards that drive thought-level optimization. The policy is updated with a PPO-style clipped objective while the value function uses clipped MSE loss, and the framework is architecture-agnostic, validated on both discrete-token (MMaDA) and continuous-embedding (LLaDA-V) multimodal dLLMs.

## Results

Bi-VRL applied to LLaDA-V achieves 53.4 on MMMU, 38.3 on MMMU-Pro, 63.2 on MMStar, 81.1 on SEEDBench, 85.4 on MMBench, and 31.2 on MathVerse, improving over the SFT baseline by +4.8, +3.1, +3.1, +6.3, +2.5, and +2.7 points respectively. On MMaDA, Bi-VRL yields 33.8 MMMU, 30.2 MMMU-Pro, 40.0 MMStar, 64.6 SEEDBench, 68.1 MMBench, and 28.3 MathVerse, with the largest gain on MathVerse (+4.8 over baseline). The framework consistently outperforms both Random Masking and Coupled-GRPO RL baselines across all seven benchmarks for both architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest AR+Diffusion baselines, LLaDA-V + Bi-VRL (53.4 MMMU) trails BAGEL (55.3) and Qwen2-VL (54.1) on MMMU but surpasses BLIP3-o (50.6) and MetaMorph (41.8). On MMMU-Pro, Bi-VRL-enhanced LLaDA-V (38.3) leads the only other diffusion model reported, LaViDa-R1 (32.8), by 5.5 points. On MathVerse, however, LaViDa-R1 (38.7) and MAmmoTH-VL (34.2) both outperform LLaDA-V + Bi-VRL (31.2), indicating that the bidimensional strategy's gains are most pronounced on perception-heavy benchmarks (MMMU, MMStar, SEEDBench, MMBench) rather than pure mathematical reasoning. Against the two RL baselines (Random Masking and Coupled-GRPO), Bi-VRL wins on every benchmark for both architectures, with the largest margins on MathVerse (+2.4 over Coupled-GRPO for LLaDA-V; +2.0 for MMaDA) and SEEDBench (+3.2 and +0.2 respectively), confirming that trajectory-level and thought-level credit assignment substantially outperform simple outcome-reward masking strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TraceRL/dLLM-RL framework [48], which introduced trajectory-aware value learning for diffusion language models in pure text, to the multimodal setting by incorporating visual context into the value function's state representation. It departs from prior RL approaches for multimodal dLLMs (MMaDA's random masking [54], LaViDa-R1's coupled masking [28]) that relied solely on coarse outcome-level rewards, by adding a spatial dimension of credit assignment over reasoning steps. The key conceptual contribution is the 'bidimensional' framing—jointly optimizing over diffusion timesteps (temporal) and reasoning-step boundaries (spatial)—which no prior method for diffusion LLMs had explicitly addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This establishes the first comprehensive RL post-training methodology specifically designed for multimodal diffusion language models, a rapidly growing model family that lacks the mature RL tooling available for autoregressive LLMs. The architecture-agnostic design (validated on both discrete-token MMaDA and continuous-embedding LLaDA-V) makes it broadly applicable as new multimodal dLLMs emerge. Open questions include whether the bidimensional structure provides genuine gains over simpler dense reward shaping at scale, whether the LVLM-judge-based alignment generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks, and whether the moderate absolute improvements (e.g., +4.8 on MMMU for LLaDA-V) will hold as base models improve. The reliance on external LVLM judges for reward computation also raises practical concerns about inference cost and judge reliability at scale.
