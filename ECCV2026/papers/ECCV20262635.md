## Problem

Existing camera-radar fusion pipelines treat 4D radar as a generic point cloud, discarding the spatio-spectral structure of the raw tensor (range, power, Doppler) that distinguishes radar from LiDAR. This leads to fusion methods that fail to consistently outperform camera-only baselines, uncertainty estimates that conflate lack of evidence with semantic conflict, and dense motion fields that ignore radar Doppler as a physically grounded supervision signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262635/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single front-view RGB image, a raw 4D FMCW radar tensor indexed by range-azimuth-elevation-Doppler (sparsified but retaining tensor structure rather than collapsing to points), and short-term ego-motion poses in SE(3). It produces a 128×128×20 semantic occupancy grid (0.4 m voxels), a dense per-voxel 3D velocity field, and a voxel-wise Dirichlet posterior whose total evidence and class distribution separately encode epistemic (vacuity) and semantic (entropy) uncertainty.

## Method

RIGS represents the scene as 25,600 learnable anisotropic Gaussian ellipsoids initialized from radar-derived depth proposals and a temporal prior, then fuses image FPN features and radar embeddings into each Gaussian via tri-modal cross-attention before differentiable splatting onto the voxel grid. At inference, predicted Gaussians serve as spatial observations for evidential ellipsoid Bayesian kernel inference with uncertainty-modulated anisotropic kernels, yielding a Dirichlet posterior that is injected into base predictions via confidence gating; radar cross-section power additionally acts as a physical existence cue to suppress hallucinated occupancy in low-evidence regions. For motion, the paper introduces displacement-guided Doppler de-aliasing that resolves the unknown folding number using temporal centroid displacement of dynamic objects, then recovers full 3D velocity via least-squares over de-aliased radial measurements with tangential components injected from displacement, supervised by a multi-level loss over sparse radar points, dense occupied voxels, and within-object consistency.

## Results

On K-Radar, RIGS achieves the best mIoU among camera-radar methods at 25.6 m (23.77%) and 51.2 m (23.72%), and the best FG IoU at both ranges (13.53% and 14.09%), outperforming OccFusion by +1.28 and +2.00 mIoU points respectively. At short range (12.8 m), RIGS is competitive but slightly trails OccFormer on IoU2 (30.61 vs 31.03) due to richer visual cues dominating. For dense 3D velocity, RIGS achieves the lowest MAE on dynamic voxels (2.37 m/s) and static voxels (0.06 m/s), outperforming both the displacement-only (DispCentroid) and raw-Doppler-only (DopplerSup) baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among camera-radar fusion baselines, RIGS clearly outperforms both OccFusion and SDGOcc (adapted to radar) at medium and long range: mIoU at 51.2 m is 23.72% vs 21.72% (OccFusion) and 18.81% (SDGOcc), with FG IoU at 51.2 m reaching 14.09% vs 10.83% and 9.44% respectively. Against camera-only methods, RIGS surpasses OccFormer (22.68% mIoU at 25.6 m) and GaussianFormer-2 (22.33%) by 1.1–1.4 mIoU points at 25.6 m, and by 2.3–3.5 points at 51.2 m, confirming that radar tensor cues add value beyond appearance. RadarOcc (radar-only) achieves the highest short-range IoU2 (32.33% at 12.8 m) due to dense near-range returns, but RIGS surpasses it on mIoU at all three ranges (e.g., 23.72 vs 21.24 at 51.2 m), showing that fusing camera semantics with radar geometry is superior to either modality alone. The LiDAR reference (SDGOcc C+L) still leads at all ranges (26.81% mIoU at 25.6 m), indicating that 4D radar, while complementary, does not yet fully replace LiDAR-grade geometric supervision. The paper also notes that existing camera-radar fusion baselines (SDGOcc C+R, OccFusion C+R) do not consistently beat camera-only models, underscoring that naive architectural transfer from LiDAR fusion fails to exploit radar's distinct measurement physics.

## Relation to Prior Work

RIGS extends the Gaussian-as-occupancy-token paradigm from GaussianFormer/GaussianFormer-2 by enriching each Gaussian primitive with radar-derived geometry, semantics, and motion cues, and adapts RadarOcc's raw 4D radar tensor sparsification as the initialization and fusion signal. For uncertainty, it builds directly on E2-BKI's evidential ellipsoidal kernel inference, adding two extensions: uncertainty-modulated anisotropic kernels and RCS-based physical existence filtering. The key conceptual departure from prior camera-radar fusion work (OccFusion, SDGOcc adapted to radar) is treating the 4D radar tensor's three physical dimensions—range, power, and Doppler—as distinct, stage-specific signals rather than collapsing them into a generic point cloud or auxiliary geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naive adaptation of camera-LiDAR fusion architectures to 4D radar input fails to consistently outperform camera-only baselines, a useful negative result that motivates radar-specific representation design. The joint occupancy-velocity-uncertainty formulation with interpretable Dirichlet decomposition (epistemic vs. semantic) offers a practical path toward safety-aware perception in adverse weather. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (K-Radar), a single front-view camera, and daytime/normal-weather sequences for quantitative metrics, leaving open whether the framework generalizes to multi-camera rigs, other radar hardware, or more diverse driving conditions. The computational cost of inference-time BKI refinement and its scalability to larger voxel grids also remain unaddressed.
