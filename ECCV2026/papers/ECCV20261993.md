## Problem

In cold-start industrial anomaly detection, limited normal samples fail to cover the full normal distribution and only a handful of anomalies are available, causing unsupervised reconstruction methods to form loose, fragmented latent boundaries while supervised methods overfit to the few observed defects. The paper asks how to leverage both scarce normal data and rare anomaly signals to construct a compact, discriminative normal boundary that generalizes to unseen defect patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261993/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ArcAD operates as a plug-and-play module on top of existing reconstruction-based backbones (Dinomaly, RD4AD, ReContrast), consuming their patch-level latent features from the bottleneck layer. Under the cold-start protocol, training uses only 30% of the standard normal training set plus a small anomaly subset (5–10% of the constructed training set), while the test set retains the held-out normals and reduced anomalies. At inference, the method produces standard reconstruction-discrepancy-based image-level anomaly scores and pixel-level anomaly maps without any architectural change to the scoring pipeline.

## Method

ArcAD introduces two complementary calibration modules in the latent space. First, Sinkhorn-based Prototype Modeling (SPM) projects ℓ₂-normalized patch features onto a unit hypersphere, models them with a von Mises-Fisher distribution, and uses the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm to solve a balanced optimal-transport assignment that enforces an equipartition constraint across K prototypes, preventing mode collapse and ensuring uniform coverage of the normal manifold. Second, Defect-Guided Calibration (DGC) synthesizes pseudo-anomalies by injecting Gaussian noise into normal anchors and re-projecting onto the hypersphere, then filters candidates by selecting the one that minimizes its maximum cosine similarity to the normal prototypes; a contrastive loss pushes real and synthesized anomalies away from the nearest prototypes while aligning them with each other. A lightweight discriminator trained with focal loss further sharpens the decision margin, and the total objective combines reconstruction, SPM, DGC, and classification losses with equal weighting (λ=0.1).

## Results

Under the multi-class cold-start setting, ArcAD (built on Dinomaly) achieves I-AUROC of 99.7% on MVTec-AD, 98.9% on VisA, 92.5% on Real-IAD, and 93.3% on MANTA, with corresponding P-F1-max of 68.9%, 54.9%, 49.8%, and 48.5%. On the single-class setting, ArcAD reaches 100.0% I-AUROC on MVTec-AD and 95.4% on Real-IAD. The framework is plug-and-play: integrating ArcAD into RD4AD yields I-AUROC gains of +5.0% (MVTec-AD), +3.5% (VisA), +8.9% (Real-IAD), and +11.2% (MANTA) over the unmodified baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArcAD outperforms the strongest unsupervised baseline (Dinomaly) by +0.1% I-AUROC on MVTec-AD, +0.5% on VisA, +3.7% on Real-IAD, and +3.0% on MANTA, with the largest pixel-level gains on MANTA (+8.7% P-F1-max). Against supervised methods, the advantage is dramatic: ArcAD exceeds DRA by +14.5% I-AUROC on MVTec-AD, +19.6% on VisA, +16.5% on Real-IAD, and +14.8% over SDNet on MANTA, indicating that conventional supervised approaches severely overfit to the few available anomalies. Among other unsupervised baselines, ArcAD surpasses MambaAD by +4.2% I-AUROC on Real-IAD and +8.1% on MANTA, and ReContrast by +6.6% on Real-IAD and +6.9% on MANTA. The generality experiments (Fig. 4, Table 3) confirm that ArcAD consistently improves all three integrated backbones (Dinomaly, ReContrast, RD4AD) across all four datasets and all anomaly ratios tested (3%, 5%, 10%), with RD4AD benefiting the most (+8.9% I-AUROC on Real-IAD at 10% ratio). No baseline matches ArcAD on any dataset-metric combination in the multi-class cold-start setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArcAD positions itself as a plug-and-play calibration layer atop existing reconstruction-based IAD backbones (Dinomaly, RD4AD, ReContrast), rather than proposing a new end-to-end architecture. Conceptually, it bridges two underperforming paradigms under data scarcity: unsupervised methods that ignore available anomaly signals and supervised methods that overfit to a handful of defects. The method's technical novelty lies in combining hyperspherical geometry (vMF distribution) with Sinkhorn optimal transport for uniform prototype clustering, and in a prototype-restricted pseudo-anomaly synthesis strategy that operates directly on the unit sphere—departing from prior image-level (CutPaste, DRAEM) or unbounded feature-level augmentation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cold-start deployment is a genuine bottleneck in industrial manufacturing where new production lines cannot wait for large annotated datasets; ArcAD's plug-and-play design means practitioners can retrofit existing reconstruction pipelines without re-architecting them. The consistent gains across three diverse backbones and four datasets (including the 30-class Real-IAD and 38-class MANTA) suggest the calibration principle is broadly transferable. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond reconstruction-based architectures (e.g., diffusion or flow-matching models), how it behaves under even more extreme scarcity (e.g., <1% anomalies), and whether the additional training cost can be reduced without sacrificing the compactness gains.
