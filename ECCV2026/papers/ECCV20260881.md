## Problem

Existing distractor-free 3DGS methods mask out transient objects and suppress their losses, but this zero-masking strategy leaves masked regions entirely unsupervised, causing per-view overfitting and persistent floaters in novel views. The fundamental gap is that masking removes dense multi-view constraints without restoring the missing background information needed for geometrically consistent optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260881/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N posed RGB images (512×512) with calibrated camera parameters and per-view binary distractor masks estimated online by a lightweight MLP (following RobustSplat). It produces a 3DGS scene of K anisotropic Gaussians, harmonized pseudo-ground truth views for masked regions generated by the multi-view harmonizer, and final novel view renderings. The harmonizer is invoked only during a middle training window (10k–20k of 30k iterations) and only for target views that have at least K_min=8 reliable support views.

## Method

PriSplat repurposes a large-scale transformer-based NVS prior (Test-time Training Done Right) as a 3D-aware inpainting engine by fine-tuning it with randomly masked inputs to reconstruct clean views from multi-view context. A mask-aware fast-weight update modifies the test-time adaptation mechanism: per-token alignment scores between queries and keys gate the key/value writes into the scene memory, preventing distractor-corrupted evidence from being encoded. A geometry-aware support view selection algorithm scores each candidate view by combining a Fisher-information-based density metric (measuring how well the view observes Gaussians in the masked region) with exponential spatial-distance and angular-compatibility penalties, then applies absolute and relative thresholds to retain only reliable supports. The full pipeline follows a three-stage schedule: mask warm-up, harmonized dense supervision (replacing masked loss with full-image loss against the harmonized pseudo-ground truth), and masked refinement for final appearance.

## Results

On the On-the-go dataset, PriSplat achieves the best overall mean PSNR (23.11), SSIM (0.7760), and LPIPS (0.1344) across all six benchmark scenes spanning low, medium, and high occlusion levels. The gains are most pronounced in high-occlusion scenes: on Patio-high, PriSplat reaches 22.48 PSNR / 0.7559 SSIM / 0.1593 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline RobustSplat (22.08 / 0.7538 / 0.1622) by +0.40 dB in PSNR. In medium-occlusion Corner, the improvement over RobustSplat is +0.22 dB PSNR and −0.0085 LPIPS. Training time is 0.38 h per scene with 7.3 GB peak memory, comparable to RobustSplat (0.36 h, 4.4 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriSplat outperforms all 12 baselines on mean PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across the On-the-go benchmark. The closest competitor is RobustSplat [16], which PriSplat surpasses by +0.2 to +0.4 dB PSNR and 0.003–0.009 SSIM on every scene; the gap widens in high-occlusion settings (e.g., +0.40 dB on Patio-high). Among mask-based methods, DeGauss [59] and DeSplat [61] are the next strongest, but both trail PriSplat by 0.3–1.5 dB PSNR depending on the scene. OCSplats [32] and ForestSplats [43] show the largest deficits (up to 3+ dB PSNR on low-occlusion scenes), indicating that uncertainty-quantification and deformable-field strategies alone are insufficient under heavy occlusion. In the prior-based comparison (Table 5), PriSplat (23.11 PSNR, 0.1344 LPIPS) beats LeftRefill/LDM [4] (22.78, 0.1458), 3DGIC/LaMa [18] (22.26, 0.1611), RogSplat/FLUX [25] (21.62, 0.2298), and Inpaint360GS [60] (20.93, 0.2392), demonstrating that the NVS-prior-based harmonizer with mask-aware fast-weight updates yields substantially better multi-view consistency than 2D diffusion or Fourier-convolution inpainting priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

PriSplat departs from the dominant 'mask-and-ignore' paradigm in distractor-free 3DGS (SpotLessSplats, WildGaussians, DeSplat, DeGauss, RobustSplat), which suppresses losses in masked regions but leaves them unsupervised, causing floaters. Instead, it repurposes a test-time-training NVS prior (Zhang et al., 2025) as a 3D-aware inpainting engine, adapting its fast-weight update mechanism with mask-aware gating to prevent distractor leakage. The mask prediction module is borrowed from RobustSplat, and the geometry-aware support view selection draws on Fisher information from active view selection literature. Unlike diffusion-based inpainting approaches (RogSplat with FLUX, LeftRefill with LDM), the method enforces pose-conditioned structural consistency rather than relying on 2D generative priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small (0.31B) pose-conditioned NVS prior outperforms much larger diffusion models (8B FLUX, 5.22B LDM) for filling distractor-masked regions in 3DGS, suggesting geometric consistency from multi-view conditioning matters more than raw generative quality for this task. The three-stage training schedule (mask warm-up → harmonized supervision → masked refinement) is a practical recipe that practitioners can adopt. However, the method does not handle illumination changes or complex appearance variations, and evaluation is limited to two datasets (On-the-go, RobustNeRF), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more diverse in-the-wild conditions. The modest but consistent gains over RobustSplat (~1 dB PSNR) and the negligible computational overhead make this a practical upgrade for existing distractor-free 3DGS pipelines.
