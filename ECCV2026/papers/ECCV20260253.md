## Problem

Optical flow estimation traditionally requires camera frames, which raise privacy concerns and fail in low-light conditions. While WiFi CSI-based sensing has produced task-specific outputs (pose, localization, gesture class), no prior work recovers a dense, general-purpose image-plane motion field directly from CSI. This paper asks whether optical flow can be estimated solely from WiFi channel state information, enabling camera-free motion perception in fixed indoor settings.

## Input / Output

The input is a complex-valued CSI tensor H' ∈ C^(A×K×N) where A=16 (4 receivers × 4 antennas), K is the number of subcarriers (80 MHz bandwidth), and N is a temporal window of 10 or 100 snapshots sampled at 1 kHz. The output is a dense 2D optical flow field F ∈ R^(2×H×W) at 128×168 (or 256×336) pixels representing apparent motion between two consecutive camera frames, plus a binary motion mask for the RoI and Combo variants. The non-trivial challenge is mapping a structured time-frequency tensor to a spatial motion field, with supervision derived from pseudo ground truth computed by an ensemble of SOTA optical flow methods on synchronized video.

## Method

All three architectures (WiFlow_Simple, WiFlow_RoI, WiFlow_Combo) share a RAFT-inspired building block comprising a Feature Network and Context Network (modified ResNet adapted to CSI input dimensions) and a Refinement Network (convolutional GRU) that iteratively updates predictions over 4 steps. WiFlow_Simple directly predicts flow; WiFlow_RoI first predicts a motion mask, extracts bounding boxes from its contours, and uses RoIAign to compute flow only within those regions; WiFlow_Combo runs flow and mask branches in parallel and multiplies their outputs point-wise. CSI is preprocessed with antenna-quotient normalization (dividing each antenna by a reference antenna per device/subcarrier/time step) to cancel shared phase offsets. Training uses a modified RAFT sequence loss with a 4th-power term to up-weight errors on non-zero flow pixels, preventing collapse to the trivial all-zero prediction given the large static background in fixed-camera scenes.

## Results

On the WiFlow dataset (birdview+ perspective, time split), WiFlow_Combo achieves the best overall EPE of 0.17 and the lowest static-pixel error EPE_S of 0.02, while WiFlow_Simple achieves the best moving-pixel error EPE_M of 2.89. All three architectures substantially outperform the trivial zero-flow baseline (EPE_M = 3.70) on moving pixels, and WiFlow_Combo reduces EPE_S from 0.29 (WiFlow_Simple) to 0.02, indicating far better motion localization. Results are consistent across both subject split and time split, confirming generalization to unseen persons (e.g., WiFlow_Combo EPE = 0.18 on subject split vs. 0.17 on time split for birdview+).

## Baselines & Comparisons

No prior work targets CSI-to-optical-flow estimation, so the paper has no external baselines to compare against. The primary comparisons are internal: (1) against a zero-flow baseline, where all three architectures reduce EPE_M from 3.70 to 2.89–3.50 on birdview+, confirming they learn meaningful motion; (2) among the three proposed architectures, where WiFlow_Combo (parallel flow + mask branches) achieves the best overall EPE and EPE_S across all perspectives and splits, while WiFlow_Simple (single flow block) achieves the best EPE_M, suggesting it captures motion magnitude slightly better but at the cost of noisy background predictions. WiFlow_RoI (sequential mask-then-flow via RoIAlign) sits between the two, with EPE_S comparable to WiFlow_Combo but slightly higher EPE_M. The paper also ablates preprocessing (Quotient normalization is best for EPE_M and EPE_A), device count (3–4 receivers suffice, with diminishing returns beyond 3), and output resolution (doubling resolution roughly doubles EPE, indicating CSI information content is the bottleneck rather than model capacity).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the RAFT architecture (Feature Network, Context Network, and GRU-based Refinement Network with iterative refinement) by replacing its image-pair input with preprocessed WiFi CSI sequences, and borrows RoIAlign from Mask R-CNN for the WiFlow_RoI variant. It departs from prior WiFi sensing work, which produces task-specific outputs (class labels, metric-space trajectories, 3D poses), by instead recovering a dense image-plane motion field. It also departs from the optical flow literature, which universally assumes camera frames as input, by demonstrating that the structured time-frequency tensor of CSI carries sufficient spatial motion information to serve as a flow estimator's input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that dense optical flow can be learned from WiFi CSI alone, opening a privacy-preserving, camera-free path to motion estimation for indoor settings. The released dataset (328 sequences, 164 min, multi-receiver multi-antenna CSI with synchronized video-derived pseudo ground truth) is the first to pair CSI with flow labels and will enable future work. Key open questions include: how to generalize beyond a single fixed room, how to increase output resolution and detail beyond 128×168, how to handle multi-person scenes more robustly, and whether the pseudo-ground-truth inconsistencies (e.g., shadow motion visible in video but absent in CSI) can be mitigated.
