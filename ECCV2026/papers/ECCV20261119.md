## Problem

Video diffusion models face a fundamental tension between preference alignment (matching human preferences) and distillation acceleration (reducing sampling steps to 1–4 NFEs). Existing joint methods optimize these objectives over mismatched representation spaces—pixel-space rewards combined with latent-space distillation, or disjoint parameter sets—causing severe gradient conflicts that degrade both fidelity and alignment simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261119/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts text prompts (T2V) or a reference image plus text prompt (I2V) and produces 5-second videos at 720P resolution in as few as 1–4 network function evaluations, compared to 80 NFEs for the base Wan2.2-14B model. The LRM component evaluates video pairs entirely in the VAE latent space (1280×720, ≥81 frames) without pixel-space decoding, while the HPD generator operates on single captioned videos from Koala-36M and Intern4K during training.

## Method

Reward Lightning enforces 'homology'—structural and representational consistency—so that both the adversarial distillation loss and the preference reward are computed on identical latent features extracted by a shared pre-trained diffusion backbone. The Latent Reward Model (LRM) uses a time-modulated attention head that projects timestep embeddings directly as dynamic cross-attention queries, enabling state-aware scoring across the flow trajectory, and is trained with a Bradley-Terry-with-Ties loss augmented by an EMA-guided margin-clipping regularizer to prevent reward hacking on large-margin pairs. Homologous Preference Distillation (HPD) branches the shared backbone into a frozen reward head and a trainable discriminator head (initialized from the reward head weights), routing the same features to both in parallel. An adaptive preference weight, computed as a half-Gaussian squash of the discriminator's deviation from its Nash equilibrium (0.5), acts as a self-paced curriculum that suppresses the preference gradient until the generator achieves structural realism, then smoothly activates it.

## Results

On VBench (T2V, 4-NFE), HPD achieves a Quality Score of 85.83, surpassing the strongest heterogeneous baseline DMDR (84.63) and pure distillation baselines TurboDiffusion (83.51) and DMD2 (81.27), while also exceeding the 80-NFE PRFL baseline (85.72). On VBench-I2V (4-NFE), HPD reaches 95.34, leading all methods including the 80-NFE PRFL (94.87). At the extreme 1-NFE setting, HPD attains 82.42 (T2V) and 91.92 (I2V), outperforming APT (78.53 / 84.87). The LRM component achieves 72.24% preference accuracy on VideoGen-RewardBench, exceeding the best pixel-level baseline VideoAlign (61.26%) by 11.0% and the best latent-level baseline PRFL (57.59%) by 14.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 4 NFEs, HPD outperforms all heterogeneous joint-training baselines: DMDR (84.63→85.83 Quality, +1.2) and FlashDMD (82.36→85.83, +3.5), with the largest gap in Motion Quality (77.48 vs. 71.85 and 68.23 respectively). Against pure distillation methods, HPD leads TurboDiffusion by 2.3 points and DMD2 by 4.6 points in Quality Score, while also surpassing both in Text Alignment (97.26 vs. 94.53 and 91.46). Notably, HPD at 4 NFEs exceeds the 80-NFE PRFL baseline (85.83 vs. 85.72) and the 80-NFE RGB ReFL baseline (84.41), demonstrating that homologous alignment recovers preference quality lost to aggressive step reduction. At 1 NFE, HPD beats APT by 3.9 points in Quality Score (82.42 vs. 78.53) and 7.0 points in I2V Score (91.92 vs. 84.87). For the LRM component, the paper's model surpasses VideoAlign by 11.0% and PRFL by 14.7% in preference accuracy on VideoGen-RewardBench, while also reducing training memory (76.24 GB vs. 77.32 GB for PRFL) and latency (154.2s vs. 156.8s) by operating in latent space at 720P.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—latent reward modeling (PRFL [34]) and adversarial distillation (DMD, DMD2, APT)—under a single architectural principle: both the reward head and the discriminator head operate on features extracted by a shared pre-trained diffusion backbone. It departs from heterogeneous joint-training methods (DMDR, FlashDMD, DOLLAR) that linearly combine pixel-space rewards with latent-space distillation losses, arguing that the representational mismatch between these spaces is the root cause of gradient conflicts. The ReFL preference objective [54] is retained in form but relocated entirely into latent space, eliminating the VAE decoding step that previously coupled the two objectives through incompatible manifolds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-conflict quantification (cosine similarity shifting from −0.14/−0.06 in heterogeneous setups to +0.41 under homology) provides a concrete, reusable diagnostic for multi-objective training in generative models, and the adaptive preference weighting offers a practical recipe for stabilizing joint RL+distillation training. The 1-NFE result approaching 80-NFE quality on VBench is practically significant for deployment. Open questions include whether the homology principle generalizes to other modalities (audio, 3D) or to non-adversarial distillation objectives, and whether the specific time-modulated attention head is essential or merely one effective instantiation of the shared-backbone idea.
