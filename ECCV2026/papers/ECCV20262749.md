## Problem

Volumetric micro-expression datasets like 4DME suffer from severe geometric noise (only 33% of released sequences are corruption-free), lack standardized benchmarks for 3D Action Unit detection, and have no principled guidance on how much lossy compression is tolerable before subtle AU-level deformations are lost. Additionally, no existing architecture effectively models the spatio-temporal dynamics of micro-expressions in the volumetric domain, where attention-based methods become computationally infeasible at high 3D resolutions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporally ordered 3D facial mesh sequences (~150k triangles per frame at 60 fps) that are projected into RGB-D image pairs; optical flow is then computed independently on the color and depth channels to produce two 2D flow fields. The model outputs a binary activation decision for each of eight FACS Action Units (AU1, AU2, AU4, AU6, AU7, AU12, AU17, AU45) via dedicated per-AU MLP heads, evaluated under leave-one-subject-out and leave-one-dataset-out protocols. For compression experiments, meshes are additionally encoded as colored point clouds on 1024³ voxel grids for V-PCC or as independent mesh frames for Draco.

## Method

VoluME reformulates volumetric motion modeling by decomposing the 3D optical flow constraint into two independent 2D flow equations—one on the RGB color channel and one on the depth channel—rather than a single coupled volumetric flow vector. Each stream produces a three-channel feature map (u, v, |ε_uv|) incorporating optical strain, which captures subtle surface deformations critical for micro-expression detection. The two streams are processed by lightweight CNN backbones (SSSNet, STSTNet, or Off-ApexNet variants) and fused at the feature level rather than the input level, preserving the separation between appearance-driven and geometry-driven motion. A dedicated multi-layer perceptron per Action Unit handles classification, reducing inter-AU interference in spatially overlapping regions. The paper further contributes a three-stage denoising pipeline (statistical GMM outlier removal, sequence-wide density-aware masking, manual refinement) that recovers ~135 previously unusable sequences, and a task-aware compression evaluation showing V-PCC at ~3000× compression ratio preserves AU detection F1 within ~1% of the uncompressed baseline.

## Results

On the extended 4DME dataset under Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) evaluation, VoluME(SSSNet) achieves a pooled binary F1-score (BF1) of 0.475, substantially outperforming the two existing 3D baselines: CCDN (0.336) and RGBD AlexNet (0.323). Under cross-dataset Leave-One-Dataset-Out (LODO) evaluation between 4DME and CASME³, VoluME(SSSNet) maintains the top position with BF1 of 0.387, compared to RGBD AlexNet (0.318) and CCDN (0.274). The best per-AU F1-scores for VoluME(SSSNet) reach 0.452 (AU45) and 0.424 (AU12), while AU17 remains near-zero across all models due to extreme class sparsity (only 6 positive subjects).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoluME(SSSNet) outperforms both 3D baselines by a wide margin on the primary LOSO benchmark: +0.139 BF1 over CCDN (0.475 vs. 0.336) and +0.152 over RGBD AlexNet (0.475 vs. 0.323). The advantage is most pronounced on AU45 (0.452 vs. 0.085 for CCDN and 0.000 for RGBD AlexNet) and AU1 (0.344 vs. 0.130 and 0.111), where the dual-stream optical-flow formulation captures subtle geometric and appearance dynamics that single-frame or coarser 3D representations miss. Under cross-dataset LODO evaluation, the gap narrows (VoluME(SSSNet) 0.387 vs. RGBD AlexNet 0.318, CCDN 0.274), and notably RGBD AlexNet slightly outperforms VoluME(STSTNet) and VoluME(Off-ApexNet), suggesting that input-level RGB+depth fusion provides more robustness to the lower geometric fidelity of CASME³. CCDN, which relies on a single-frame 3D representation, consistently underperforms on AUs requiring temporal dynamics (AU6: 0.000 LOSO, 0.125 LODO), confirming the paper's argument that temporal information is critical for volumetric AU detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the family of lightweight optical-flow CNNs developed for 2D micro-expression recognition (STSTNet, Off-ApexNet, SSSNet) into a volumetric dual-stream setting, splitting the volumetric flow formulation into independent RGB and depth optical-flow channels with feature-level fusion rather than the single-vector volumetric flow used in prior 3D attempts. It departs from attention-based architectures (SCA, AUFormer) on the grounds that cubic complexity in spatial resolution makes them infeasible for high-resolution 3D inputs with limited training data. On the data side, it builds directly on the 4DME and CASME³ volumetric datasets, adding a three-stage denoising pipeline and recovering previously excluded sequences to create the first culturally balanced 3D micro-expression benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: demonstrating that ~3000× V-PCC compression preserves AU-detection performance within ~1% F1 makes large-scale 3D ME research far more accessible in terms of storage and distribution, and the cleaned, extended 4DME release addresses a concrete data-quality problem (67% of original sequences had geometric corruption) that likely affected prior results. Open questions include whether the compression findings generalize beyond the tested codecs and datasets, whether the dual-stream optical-flow formulation scales to higher-resolution or longer-sequence 3D data, and whether the cultural balance introduced here is sufficient for meaningful cross-cultural ME studies. The cross-dataset results also reveal that feature-level fusion, while superior on clean data, is more fragile under domain shift than simpler input-level fusion, suggesting a trade-off that future work should address.
