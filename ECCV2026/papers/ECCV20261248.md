## Problem

Existing polarimetric downstream tasks (SfP, DfP) are trained on naively regrouped DoFP sensor pixels without proper demosaicking, producing suboptimal supervision targets with interpolation artifacts and reduced spatial resolution. Meanwhile, current deep demosaickers optimize only for photometric fidelity (PSNR, LPIPS) and are task-agnostic, so their reconstructions may look visually correct yet distort the subtle polarization structures (DoLP, AoP) that downstream tasks critically depend on. No prior work jointly optimizes the demosaicking stage and its downstream polarimetric application.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single CPFA raw mosaic image (e.g., 2048×2448) in which each pixel encodes only one of four polarization angles interleaved with an RGGB Bayer pattern. During training, a known imaging operator A simulates CPFA down-sampling and Bayer interpolation to generate paired low-quality inputs from reference color polarization images; at inference, A is removed and the demosaicker processes full-resolution inputs directly. The pipeline outputs full-resolution four-angle polarization images from which Stokes components, DoLP, and AoP are derived, and a task-specific network then produces either a surface normal map (SfP) or a reflection-free image (DfP).

## Method

PolarAPP jointly trains a Restormer-inspired UNet demosaicker D and a task network T through three coordinated stages. First, a bi-level meta-learning loop learns two feature-transform modules (FT₁, FT₂) whose cosine-similarity alignment loss L_fa is goal-directed: the inner update probes D and T with one gradient step under L_fa, and the outer update adjusts FT parameters only through the resulting decreases in reconstruction loss L_d and task loss L_t on a held-out meta-test split, preventing degenerate alignment. Second, an equivalent imaging transformation (EIT) prior enforces equivariance—applying a geometric transformation T_g to the reconstructed image, re-sampling through A, and requiring the demosaicker's output to match T_g applied to the original reconstruction—providing self-supervised regularization without additional labeled data. Third, a task-refinement stage freezes D and fine-tunes T on clean full-resolution inputs (A removed) to close the resolution gap between training and deployment. The FT modules are used only during training and discarded at inference, adding zero overhead.

## Results

On the SfP task, PolarAPP achieves 92.37% angular accuracy at the 11.25° threshold and a mean normal error of 3.57°, substantially outperforming SfPUEL (70.11%, 10.58°) and the PIDSR+ two-stage baseline (78.12%, 6.78°). On DfP, PolarAPP reaches 22.90 dB PSNR and 0.871 SSIM, surpassing PolarFree (22.44 dB, 0.868) and PIDSR+ (22.41 dB, 0.859). For demosaicking quality on the Qiu dataset, PolarAPP attains 43.12 dB PSNR on S₀, 38.10 dB on DoLP, and 5.444° MAE on AoP, matching or exceeding both DCPM and PIDSR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolarAPP outperforms all five baselines across both downstream tasks. Against SfPUEL [32], the joint framework improves 11.25° angular accuracy by 22.3 percentage points (92.37% vs. 70.11%) and reduces mean normal error from 10.58° to 3.57°, demonstrating that explicit demosaicking plus task-aware coupling is far more effective than training directly on regrouped mosaic pixels. Against the PIDSR+ two-stage baseline [56], which retrains the task network on a fixed SOTA demosaicker, PolarAPP still gains 14.3 points at 11.25° and 3.2° in mean error, confirming that joint optimization yields benefits beyond simply using a better demosaicker. On DfP, PolarAPP beats PolarFree [50] by 0.46 dB PSNR and 0.003 SSIM, and outperforms PIDSR+ by 0.49 dB PSNR, showing consistent but more modest gains in reflection removal. For demosaicking quality, PolarAPP edges out both DCPM [21] and PIDSR [56] on all three metrics (S₀ PSNR, DoLP PSNR, AoP MAE), with the largest margin on AoP (5.444° vs. 6.456° for DCPM). The combination landscape in Fig. 7 further confirms that the best performance is achieved only when both the demosaicker and task network are co-trained, rather than pairing the best individual modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolarAPP departs from two established but disconnected lines of work: (1) polarization demosaicking methods (DCPM, PIDSR) that optimize photometric fidelity in isolation, and (2) polarimetric task networks (SfPUEL, PolarFree) that are trained on naively regrouped DoFP pixels without explicit demosaicking. The paper repurposes MAML-style bi-level optimization not for few-shot adaptation but for learning a feature-alignment metric that couples a Restormer-based demosaicker with a task network, and borrows the equivalent imaging transformation (EIT) prior from equivariant imaging to provide self-supervision where paired high-quality data is scarce. Conceptually, it positions demosaicking not as a preprocessing step but as a task-aware component whose reconstruction should be shaped by downstream utility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work exposes a practical gap in polarimetric imaging pipelines: state-of-the-art demosaickers produce visually faithful outputs that still degrade downstream SfP/DfP accuracy, and simply swapping in a better demosaicker (PIDSR+) yields only marginal gains. By demonstrating that joint, task-aware optimization produces substantially larger improvements (e.g., 92.4% vs. 70.1% normal accuracy at 11.25°), the paper argues that reconstruction fidelity and task utility are not aligned objectives. Open questions include whether the meta-learning overhead generalizes to additional polarimetric tasks (material estimation, object detection), whether the approach extends to other DoFP sensor configurations, and whether the training complexity (bi-level optimization + EIT + refinement) is prohibitive for practitioners with limited compute.
