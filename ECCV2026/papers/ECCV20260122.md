## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods recover geometry but fail to model complex surface materials and lighting, while reference-based video diffusion models can synthesize realistic appearances but lack precise viewpoint controllability and multi-view consistency. This paper bridges the gap by using a coarse 3D proxy to provide explicit spatial constraints to a video diffusion model, enabling controllable object rendering without explicit physical material modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260122/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–8 multi-view reference images of an object, a sequence of M target camera poses, a text prompt describing the desired lighting environment, and an optional appearance guidance video. A fast 3D reconstruction (ReconViaGen or VGGT) is first run on the references to produce a coarse mesh or point cloud, which is then rasterized into object-centric coordinate maps where each pixel's RGB encodes its normalized (x, y, z) position. The output is a 480×832, 81-frame object video rendered along the specified trajectory under the specified lighting, generated in ~58 seconds on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

GO-Renderer formulates geometric guidance as object-centric coordinate maps: the coarse 3D reconstruction is rendered from both reference and target viewpoints, with each pixel's color encoding its 3D coordinates in the object's local frame. A Joint Reference Structure pairs each reference image with its coordinate map via channel-wise concatenation, and similarly concatenates the target coordinate map (and optional appearance video) with the latent noise, establishing dense pixel-level 3D correspondence so the diffusion model learns to 'look up' appearance from references based on shared coordinates. A Negative RoPE Shift assigns negative, gapped temporal indices to reference latents (e.g., −Ng, −(N−1)g, …, −g, 0, 1, …, M−1) to prevent the model from interpreting references as adjacent historical frames and causing transition artifacts. The architecture is built on the pre-trained Wan2.2Fun 5B Ref Control DiT, fine-tuned for 20k steps on a 57k-clip mixed dataset (Blender synthetic renders, real-world extractions via SAM3 + FoundationPose, and Wan2.2-generated videos) with augmentations including random reference shuffling and spatial perturbation of coordinate maps.

## Results

On the rendering quality and lighting consistency benchmark, GO-Renderer achieves a PSNR of 18.26 and SSIM of 0.684, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Wan2.2Fun (PSNR 14.30, SSIM 0.641) and all two-stage reconstruct-then-render pipelines (PSNR ≤ 12.06). For multi-view consistency under canonical backgrounds, GO-Renderer attains CLIP similarity of 0.888 (avg) and DINO similarity of 0.725 (avg), surpassing AnySplat (DINO 0.191) and ReconViaGen (DINO 0.448) by large margins. The method also leads on perceptual metrics including VBench++ ImagingQuality (0.712) and MotionSmoothness (0.988).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GO-Renderer outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the two-stage UniLumos pipeline, the advantage is most pronounced in SSIM: GO-Renderer reaches 0.684 versus 0.585 for UniLumos(GT) and only 0.338–0.362 for the reconstruction-coupled variants, indicating that error accumulation in the 3D reconstruction stage severely degrades relighting fidelity. Compared to the subject-driven Phantom model, GO-Renderer gains +6.86 dB in PSNR (18.26 vs. 11.40) and +0.17 in SSIM, attributed to explicit 3D proxy guidance enforcing precise camera trajectories that single-reference generation cannot achieve. The closest competitor, Wan2.2Fun 5B Ref Control, trails by 3.96 dB in PSNR and 0.043 in SSIM, confirming that even a strong video diffusion backbone benefits substantially from the coordinate-map spatial conditioning. On multi-view consistency (Table 2), GO-Renderer leads AnySplat by +0.534 in DINO average similarity (0.725 vs. 0.191) and ReconViaGen by +0.277 (0.725 vs. 0.448), demonstrating that generative rendering with 3D proxy guidance preserves fine-grained structural detail far better than either explicit 3DGS or generative 3D reconstruction alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

GO-Renderer sits at the intersection of two established threads: feed-forward 3D reconstruction (AnySplat, VGGT, ReconViaGen) and subject-driven video diffusion (Phantom, MAGREF, Wan2.2Fun). It departs from the dominant 'reconstruct-then-render' paradigm—which requires brittle PBR material recovery—by demoting the 3D reconstruction to a coarse geometric proxy that merely provides spatial coordinates, while delegating all appearance synthesis to a video diffusion model. The key architectural departure from prior 3D-conditioned video generation (GEN3C, ViewCrafter) is the use of object-centric coordinate maps as a dense pixel-level bridge between multi-view references and target views, combined with a negative RoPE shift (adapted from Ominicontrol) to temporally isolate reference conditioning from the generated sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bypassing explicit material and lighting decomposition entirely, GO-Renderer sidesteps the notoriously ill-posed inverse rendering problem, offering a practical path to photorealistic object relighting and novel view synthesis from just a handful of images. The graceful degradation behavior—where the model falls back on 2D diffusion priors when the 3D proxy is heavily corrupted—suggests the approach is robust to imperfect reconstruction, a practical advantage over cascaded pipelines. However, the method still requires a 3D proxy (even if coarse), limiting its applicability to settings where some geometric signal is available, and the evaluation baselines do not include other 3D-conditioned video diffusion methods (e.g., GEN3C, ViewCrafter), leaving the full competitive landscape unclear. Open questions include whether the coordinate-map conditioning can be replaced by learned implicit representations and whether the approach extends to multi-object or scene-level rendering.
