## Problem

Point-based intrinsic decomposition and inverse rendering methods suffer from a misattribution issue: volume rendering aggregates translucent primitives along each ray and supervises only the final pixel colour, so individual primitives can learn incorrect albedo or shading features that compensate for one another. This makes per-primitive editing unreliable and unpredictable, and the ambiguity persists regardless of the number of captured views because near-coincident primitives render identically from every direction.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (synthetic scenes from NeRF Synthetic and TensoIR, real-world captures from Tanks & Temples and Mip-NeRF 360) and produces a 3D point cloud where each point stores a position, an albedo feature vector, a shading feature vector, and a scalar influence score. From this representation the system renders novel-view RGB images, per-pixel albedo maps, and shading maps, and supports per-point editing (albedo transfer, shading intensity scaling, shadow add/remove) that remains multi-view consistent. A pre-trained 2D intrinsic decomposition network is additionally run per training view to generate M pseudo-albedo hypotheses used as supervision signals.

## Method

Intrinsic PAPR replaces volumetric splatting with Proximity Attention Point Rendering (PAPR), where each surface point has no spatial extent and appearance is predicted directly at ray-surface intersections via a learned attention kernel over the top-K nearest points. Because each point dominates a non-trivial cone of rays, photometric residuals backpropagate primarily to that point, restoring per-point identifiability and eliminating the compensatory-error ambiguity of volume rendering. The per-point features are split into orthogonal albedo and shading branches; shading is learned implicitly from the factorization I ≈ A ⊙ S without an explicit shading loss. A 2D intrinsic prior is adapted with conditional IMLE to produce M ambiguity-aware albedo hypotheses per view, and a space-carving mode-selection loss distills these multi-view hypotheses into a single geometrically consistent 3D albedo, while a learnable per-view scalar absorbs the inherent scale indeterminacy of albedo.

## Results

On synthetic NVS (NeRF Synthetic + TensoIR), Intrinsic PAPR achieves 35.00 PSNR / 0.978 SSIM / 0.021 LPIPS, outperforming the best inverse-rendering baseline (DiscretizedSDF, 31.72 PSNR) by 10.3% and vanilla PAPR (32.84 PSNR) by 6.6%. On real-world NVS (Tanks & Temples + Mip-NeRF 360), it reaches 29.99 PSNR / 0.912 SSIM / 0.094 LPIPS, again the best across all methods. For albedo reconstruction on NeRF Synthetic, Intrinsic PAPR scores 31.21 PSNR / 0.949 SSIM / 0.056 LPIPS, an 8.0% PSNR gain over the strongest inverse-rendering baseline (DiscretizedSDF, 28.89 PSNR). Per-point feature-transfer experiments show a >4× reduction in albedo- and shading-transfer MSE over GS-IR (e.g., 0.053 vs 0.381 on Lego albedo transfer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Intrinsic PAPR outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest inverse-rendering baselines, DiscretizedSDF [71] (31.72 PSNR synthetic NVS, 28.89 albedo PSNR) and IRGS [20] (31.35 / 28.44), it gains +3.28 and +2.77 PSNR on NVS and +2.32 and +2.77 PSNR on albedo, respectively. The Gaussian-splatting inverse-rendering methods GS-IR [31] (30.40 / 26.33) and DPIR [10] (26.47 / 22.89) trail by 4.6–8.5 PSNR on NVS and 4.9–8.3 PSNR on albedo. The diffusion-prior PBR method MaterialFusion [32] (28.59 / 25.36) and the NeRF-based Intrinsic-NeRF [61] (29.51 / 24.86) are also clearly surpassed. Notably, Intrinsic PAPR also beats vanilla PAPR [66] (32.84 PSNR) by 2.16 PSNR on synthetic NVS, showing that explicit albedo supervision acts as a geometric regularizer that improves reconstruction quality beyond the unmodified renderer. In the per-point feature-transfer evaluation (Table 2), Intrinsic PAPR reduces albedo-transfer MSE by 5–7× and shading-transfer MSE by 5–13× relative to GS-IR, directly demonstrating resolution of the misattribution issue that volumetric baselines cannot overcome even with more training views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Proximity Attention Point Rendering (PAPR) as a surface-aligned alternative to volume rendering for intrinsic decomposition, splitting per-point features into albedo and shading branches. It adapts the 2D intrinsic decomposition prior of Careaga & Aksoy into an ambiguity-aware probabilistic model via cIMLE, and borrows the space-carving mode-selection loss from SCADE to fuse multi-view albedo hypotheses into a consistent 3D representation. The key conceptual departure is from the dominant volume-rendering paradigm (3DGS, NeRF) used by GS-IR, DPIR, Intrinsic-NeRF, and related methods, which the paper argues structurally prevents per-primitive supervision due to transmittance-weighted aggregation of translucent primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The misattribution diagnosis identifies a fundamental, view-count-independent limitation shared by all volume-rendering-based intrinsic decomposition and inverse rendering methods, making it a critical barrier to reliable 3D editing tools. The paper's deliberate choice of a simple albedo-shading factorization over full PBR is well-motivated by a bias-variance argument, but it also means the method cannot handle relighting, specular effects, or semi-transparent phenomena—open questions include whether the surface-attention paradigm can be extended to richer yet stable appearance models. The finding that near-coincident primitives are observationally equivalent from all views (confirmed by a 25-to-200-view sweep) suggests that the volume-rendering community may need to reconsider how per-primitive features are supervised.
