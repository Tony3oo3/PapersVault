## Problem

Existing controllable generation methods for diffusion models operate at either coarse granularity (text prompts, LoRAs) or fine spatial granularity (ControlNet with dense conditions), leaving a gap for distributional-level control. This paper addresses the need for a middle-ground control mechanism that enforces user-specified statistical constraints—such as exact color histograms or latent token distributions—during the generation process with guaranteed precision, without requiring additional training or architectural modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261092/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt plus a target histogram (either extracted from a reference image or synthetically constructed from a continuous soft-prompt embedding vector of dimension d=4096 with fixed norm B=40.0) and produces a 1024×1024 image whose pixel-color or latent-token distribution exactly matches the target. For information embedding, the soft-prompt embedding is deterministically mapped to a color histogram via a softmax-like transformation, and the inverse mapping (log + norm rescaling) recovers the embedding from the generated image's histogram, enabling exact text decoding through a frozen LLM (Llama-3.1-8B). The guidance is applied at a user-selected subset of DDIM sampling steps (typically 1–4 out of 50), and an optional post-hoc OT step can enforce zero histogram divergence at the cost of minor visual artifacts.

## Method

HIG formulates histogram matching as an optimal transport problem: given a source histogram (from the current intermediate prediction) and a target histogram, it solves for a minimum-cost transport plan γ that specifies how many units (pixels or tokens) to reassign between each source and target bin. During sampling, at selected time steps, the intermediate latent prediction z_0^t is decoded to pixel space, the OT plan is applied by randomly sampling units from each source bin and setting them to the closest target-bin value, and the result is re-encoded to latent space before continuing denoising. A multi-option binning variant (k=16 candidates per bin) relaxes the constraint to bin-level mass matching, reducing perceptual distortion while preserving exact histogram compliance. The approach is entirely training-free, uses the network simplex algorithm to solve OT in ~0.2 s per 1024² image, and is composable with existing controls like ControlNet, LoRA, and IP-Adapter since it operates as an explicit transformation on intermediate predictions rather than modifying the noise-prediction network.

## Results

On the color-constrained generation benchmark (300 prompts, 300 reference images, SDXL at 1024²), HIG (guidance variant) achieves the best scores across all three metrics: HistKL 1.09, CLIP-Score 27.19, and LAION-Aesthetics 6.78, outperforming all 10 baselines simultaneously. The direct-OT and guidance+post-hoc-OT variants achieve perfect histogram alignment (HistKL 0.00) at a small cost to CLIP (26.94/26.91) and Aesthetics (6.52/6.66). For information embedding, HIG decodes 512 text tokens with >90% exact-match rate, and soft-prompt tuning for sequences up to 256 tokens completes in under 20 seconds with >99.7% success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIG (guidance) dominates all 10 baselines on every metric simultaneously: it reduces HistKL from 3.16 (LoRA+CN+IP) and 4.24 (MPGD) to 1.09, while improving CLIP-Score from 22.53 (LoRA+CN+IP) and 24.46 (MPGD) to 27.19, and Aesthetics from 5.39 and 4.94 to 6.78. Against the strongest style-transfer baselines, HIG still leads: StyleShot (HistKL 1.90, CLIP 26.39, Aesthetics 5.83) and InstantStyle++ (2.72, 26.23, 6.54) are both outperformed on all three axes. GPT-4o-Image, the only other training-free in-context method, trails HIG by 7.87 in HistKL (8.96 vs 1.09) and 1.47 in CLIP (25.72 vs 27.19). The paper notes that HIG is not intended to replace specialized style-transfer methods but rather provides a complementary, distribution-level control axis; its key advantage is exact histogram compliance (HistKL → 0 with post-hoc OT) combined with minimal inference overhead (~2.2 s on SDXL, comparable to LoRA/ControlNet). For information embedding, no direct baseline exists, but the >90% exact-decoding rate at 512 tokens demonstrates a capability (precise distributional control) that none of the compared methods can provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIG positions itself as a middle-ground control granularity between high-level semantic conditioning (text prompts, LoRAs) and low-level spatial conditioning (ControlNet), introducing distributional (histogram-level) constraints as a new axis. Methodologically, it departs from the dominant paradigm of manipulating the noise prediction ε_θ (as in ControlNet or LoRA) by instead explicitly transforming the intermediate clean prediction z_0^t through a decode-transform-encode cycle, where the transformation is an optimal transport plan. This connects to the constrained-generation literature (manifold-preserving guidance, projected diffusion) and training-free guidance, but differs from gradient-based color guidance (SW-Guidance, Color Alignment) by being non-differentiable, exact, and requiring no inference-time gradients. The information embedding application repurposes prompt tuning (Lester et al.) and LLM conditioning in a way not previously tied to image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HIG provides a composable, training-free, and interpretable control mechanism that is orthogonal to and stackable with existing methods (ControlNet, LoRA, IP-Adapter), diversifying the hybrid strategies available for controllable generation. The information embedding capability—encoding hundreds of text tokens into a visually indistinguishable image's color histogram with >90% exact recovery—opens practical applications in secure data transmission but also raises dual-use concerns the authors acknowledge. Key limitations include the use of random pixel sampling for OT application (which can cause local inconsistencies), the sensitivity of embedded information to JPEG compression, and the fact that latent histogram control remains a proof-of-concept whose behavior depends heavily on tokenizer robustness. A systematic study of control behaviors across latent spaces and the development of spatially-aware transport strategies are natural next steps.
