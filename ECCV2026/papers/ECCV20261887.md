## Problem

Existing video-language benchmarks target broad daily activities or cinematic scenes and lack the sub-second temporal granularity, health-oriented grounding, and diagnostic rigor needed to evaluate Video-LLMs on fine-grained eating behavior. This leaves a critical gap: coarse action-recognition scores can mask whether a model reasons from physical evidence or merely relies on linguistic priors, making it impossible to assess whether current models can support dietary monitoring and behavioral health assessment in unconstrained, real-world settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261887/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes eating behavior video sessions (average 4.3 min, range ~2–20+ min) sampled at 32 uniform frames for the main evaluation (64 frames for the Hard subset) paired with a text question. Questions span five formats—Single Choice, True/False, Multiple Choice, Fill-in-the-Blank, and Short Answer—across seven capability dimensions and three difficulty levels (atomic perception → temporal aggregation → compositional synthesis). For the AGCoT-LoRA baseline, the model additionally produces a structured chain-of-thought trace that explicitly references annotation-derived signals (e.g., bite timestamps, IBI values) before emitting the final answer.

## Method

The core pipeline is a three-tier automated annotation system: Tier 1 extracts low-level primitives (MediaPipe 3D pose, Grounding DINO + SAM food segmentation, MiDaS monocular depth, Whisper audio transcription); Tier 2 quantifies domain-specific metrics (bite events via hand-to-mouth trajectory minima, chewing frequency from mandibular oscillation fused with acoustic peaks, Inter-Bite Interval distributions, and a Shannon-entropy-based Nutritional Diversity Index); Tier 3 uses knowledge-constrained MLLMs to generate high-level affective and behavioral-health interpretations, each tagged with provenance metadata linking the claim to its originating signal. QA pairs are generated programmatically by a template engine that queries these annotation modules, ensuring every ground-truth answer is reproducible and traceable. For the domain-adapted baseline, AGCoT-LoRA fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B with LoRA, using annotation-derived reasoning chains as explicit Chain-of-Thought supervision so the model learns to ground its answers in physical signals rather than linguistic priors.

## Results

On the full EatVid-QA set (N=6,300), InternVL2-8B leads open-source models with WAcc=0.6537 and SA-WAcc=0.8462, while VideoLLaMA3-7B achieves the highest raw accuracy (0.3868) and Non-SA-WAcc (0.3284). On the Hard subset (N=376), the proprietary Qwen3.5-35B-A3B reaches the top WAcc of 0.7359, but the proposed AGCoT-LoRA (7B) achieves WAcc=0.6130 and Non-SA-WAcc=0.3214, outperforming all open-source baselines. A consistent perception-reasoning gap appears across all models: SA-WAcc scores (0.73–0.97) far exceed Non-SA-WAcc scores (0.13–0.33), indicating that fluent synthesis does not translate into grounded temporal or numerical reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGCoT-LoRA (7B) outperforms all open-source baselines on the Hard subset: it surpasses Molmo2 (WAcc 0.6130 vs. 0.6008), InternVL2 (0.6130 vs. 0.5868), and Qwen2.5-VL (0.6130 vs. 0.5798), while its Non-SA-WAcc of 0.3214 nearly doubles the base Qwen2.5-VL's 0.1623. However, it remains behind the proprietary Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (WAcc 0.7359) and Qwen3-VL-32B (0.6988), confirming that model scale and iteration still matter. Notably, AGCoT-LoRA achieves the highest Non-SA-WAcc among all models (0.3214), even exceeding the API-based Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (0.2982), suggesting that annotation-guided CoT supervision specifically strengthens objective, evidence-based reasoning. The ablation against plain LoRA (22.3% Non-SA-WAcc) versus AGCoT-LoRA (36.4%) on matched 4 FPS sampling confirms that the gains come from explicit reasoning supervision rather than mere domain adaptation. Across all models, the SA-to-Non-SA gap (e.g., Qwen3.5: 0.9667 vs. 0.2982) is the dominant pattern, revealing that current Video-LLMs produce plausible narratives without reliable frame-level grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: general-purpose video-language benchmarks (VideoMME, MLVU, MVBench) and domain-specific eating-behavior datasets (OREBA, EatSense). It departs from the former by introducing sub-second temporal granularity, health-oriented grounding, and provenance-linked annotations absent from broad video-QA suites, and from the latter by providing a full Video-QA framework with behavioral-health reasoning rather than sensor-level or single-task supervision. Methodologically, AGCoT-LoRA extends the Qwen2.5-VL architecture with annotation-guided chain-of-thought supervision, building on the parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm while grounding reasoning traces in the paper's three-tier automated annotation pipeline (MediaPipe, Grounding DINO, SAM, MiDaS, Whisper).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete and actionable failure mode—models generate fluent, prior-consistent behavioral narratives while failing at the precise temporal grounding and cross-frame counting those narratives supposedly rest on—which is directly relevant to any deployment of Video-LLMs in clinical or dietary-monitoring settings where hallucinated assessments carry real health risk. Key open questions include whether the perception-reasoning gap generalizes beyond eating to other fine-grained repetitive-action domains, whether denser temporal sampling or longer context windows can close it, and how to mitigate the residual LLM biases inherited by Tier-3 semantic annotations. The paper's own limitations (geographic/cultural coverage, no precise nutritional quantification) leave substantial room for future expansion.
