## Problem

Image representation learning has been split into two paradigms: contrastive/self-distillation methods that produce embeddings for recognition tasks, and reconstruction-based methods (VAEs, autoencoders) that produce latents for generation. Prior unification attempts are post-hoc adaptations, and no single encoder natively produces representations that are simultaneously strong for classification, segmentation, reconstruction, and extreme compression. This paper asks whether a single model can be trained to serve all these roles from the start.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image, patchifies it into 16×16 patches, and prepends a learnable global (cls) token. It produces three types of output: (1) standard ViT tokens (d=768 for ViT-B, d=1024 for ViT-L) usable for classification and dense prediction, (2) compressed TinToks at dimensions 8, 16, or 32 (up to 96× smaller) that retain both recognition and reconstruction capability, and (3) per-patch INR modulation matrices that, when applied to a shared base INR, yield a small neural network mapping pixel coordinates to RGB values for reconstruction. The per-patch INR design means each 16×16 patch is reconstructed by its own tiny network rather than a single whole-image network.

## Method

HUVR is a hyper-network that predicts per-patch implicit neural representation (INR) weights from image tokens, replacing the standard per-image INR formulation. Three key architectural changes enable unification: (1) patch tokens themselves serve as weight tokens (eliminating separate learnable weight tokens), (2) a global token is projected and outer-producted with each patch token projection to form the modulation matrix M_i = g_proj · p_proj^T that scales the shared base INR weights W_i, and (3) learnable linear downsampling/upsampling layers between the transformer backbone and INR prediction layers produce the compressed TinToks. Training combines pixel-level MSE reconstruction loss with L2 knowledge distillation losses applied to both global and patch tokens at the last encoder and decoder blocks, aligning features to a frozen DINOv3 teacher. This distillation is what imbues the compressed TinToks with semantic structure sufficient for classification and segmentation without direct supervision on those tokens.

## Results

With ViT-B/16, HUVR achieves 85.0% ImageNet-1k top-1 accuracy (linear probing), outperforming DINOv3 (84.6%) by +0.4% and SigLIP2 (84.5%) by +0.5%, while also leading on ObjectNet (62.0% vs. 59.4% for DINOv3) and fine-grained datasets like CUB-200 (89.4% vs. 89.7% for DINOv3, 82.6% for SigLIP2). For dense prediction, HUVR reaches 52.0 mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation (+1.2 over DINOv3) and 0.3263 RMSE on NYUv2 depth estimation (best among all compared methods). The compressed TinToks at 32 dimensions (96× compression) achieve 76.7% ImageNet accuracy and 27.83 PSNR reconstruction, surpassing DINOv3 PCA by +12.6% classification and +10.15 PSNR, and beating the Stable Diffusion VAE by +2.84 PSNR at equal embedding size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINOv3 (ViT-B/16), HUVR wins on ImageNet (+0.4%), ReaL labels (+0.3%), ObjectNet (+2.6%), DTD (+0.4%), Food-101 (+0.6%), ADE20K segmentation (+1.2 mIoU), and NYUv2 depth (−0.0042 RMSE), while narrowly losing on Stanford Cars (−0.3%) and CUB-200 (−0.3%). Against SigLIP2 (ViT-B/16), HUVR leads on ImageNet (+0.5%), CUB-200 (+6.8%), DTD (+0.6%), and Food-101 (+0.1%), but trails on ObjectNet (−6.4%) due to SigLIP2's 25× larger pre-training data. At ViT-L/16 scale, HUVR is slightly behind DINOv3 on most metrics (e.g., −0.2% INet, −5.4% CUB, −2.1% DTD), suggesting the larger model benefits more from DINOv3's curated pre-training data. For the unique TinTok compressed representation (d=32), HUVR dominates all baselines: +12.6% ImageNet over DINOv3 PCA, +7.9 mIoU ADE20K over DINOv3, and +10.15 PSNR over DINOv3 PCA for reconstruction. For INR hyper-network reconstruction (Table 7), HUVR achieves 48.44 PSNR on ImageNette with only 400 epochs, beating LA-IPC (46.10 at 4000 epochs) by +2.34 PSNR with 10× less training time, and outperforming IPC (38.46) and TransINR (29.01) by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the INR hyper-network lineage (TransINR, IPC, LA-IPC) by replacing separate learnable weight tokens with the ViT patch tokens themselves, adding a global token for modulation, and introducing a compressed intermediate representation (TinToks). It departs from prior unified representation work (MAGE, Sorcen, REPA) by natively integrating reconstruction into the encoder via INR prediction rather than post-hoc adaptation, and by adding knowledge distillation from DINOv3 to inject semantic structure into what was previously a purely pixel-level reconstruction objective. The TinToks compression mechanism has no direct precedent in either the INR or representation-learning literature, positioning the work at the intersection of both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single INR hyper-network can simultaneously match DINOv3 for classification/segmentation and perform competitive reconstruction is a meaningful step toward truly unified vision encoders, and the 96×-compressed TinToks open a practical path for resource-constrained deployment. However, the generation results (Table 4) do not beat the Stable Diffusion VAE, the pre-training scale is substantially smaller than DINOv3's, and the method lacks text alignment for VLM integration—so the 'unified' claim remains partial. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the data/compute budgets of frontier encoders, whether the distillation crossover effect (larger teacher initially worse) can be mitigated, and whether TinToks can replace VAE latents in production diffusion pipelines.
