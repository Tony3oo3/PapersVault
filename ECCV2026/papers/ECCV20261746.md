## Problem

Existing Zero-Shot CIR methods design proxy tasks that adapt visual and textual features to a fixed, non-learnable composition rule (e.g., pseudo-word injection into a frozen text encoder or linear feature arithmetic), leaving the composition function itself unmodeled. This limits the model's ability to express diverse, fine-grained, and context-dependent semantic modifications that real-world CIR queries require.

## Input / Output

At inference, FoCo takes a reference image and a free-form modification text, producing a single composed embedding that is matched against precomputed global CLIP embeddings of gallery images via cosine similarity. The CLIP vision and text encoders remain frozen; only two lightweight modules (a cross-attention aggregation block and a two-layer Transformer completion block, ~38M trainable parameters with ViT-L/14) are learned. At training, the method consumes image-caption pairs from CC3M, where an LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) pre-decomposes each caption into 3–5 local captions paired with complementary contextual captions, providing multi-view supervision without any triplet annotations.

## Method

FoCo models composition as a learnable two-stage 'focus-then-complete' pipeline. In the aggregation stage, each local caption is fused with the global image feature via element-wise multiplication and an MLP to form a text-guided query, which then cross-attends over the image's patch features to extract a localized visual representation; the inverse of the resulting attention map weights the remaining patches as a background representation. In the completion stage, the localized feature initializes a query token that interacts through two Transformer blocks with a concatenated key-value sequence of [contextual text embedding; localized visual feature; background visual features], producing the final composed representation. Training uses a cross-instance sigmoid contrastive objective with multiple positives per image and carefully constructed cross-instance negatives (visual evidence from one image paired with text from another), which prevents the model from taking unimodal shortcuts and forces genuine cross-modal reasoning. The two losses (aggregation and completion) are jointly optimized, and the entire framework is trained on image-caption pairs alone, requiring no reference-modification-target triplets.

## Results

On CIRR (ViT-L/14), FoCo achieves R@1=34.5, R@5=65.4, R@10=74.7, and R@50=92.1, surpassing the previous best (SlerpTAT) by 3.6 points on R@1. On CIRCO, FoCo reaches mAP@5=17.5, mAP@10=18.1, mAP@25=19.9, and mAP@50=20.8, outperforming SlerpTAT by 0.5 mAP@5 and PrediCIR by 1.8 mAP@5. On FashionIQ, FoCo attains an average R@10 of 31.5 and R@50 of 53.2, leading all baselines. Scaling to ViT-G/14, FoCo further improves to R@1=38.5 on CIRR and mAP@5=25.5 on CIRCO, maintaining its lead over the strongest prior method (PrediCIR) by 1.5 and 1.8 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoCo outperforms all eight baselines across all four benchmarks. On CIRR, the largest gap is against SlerpTAT (the previous best), with FoCo gaining +3.6 R@1 (34.5 vs. 30.9) and +5.0 R@5 (65.4 vs. 59.4); pseudo-token methods like Pic2Word, LinCIR, and HIT trail by 6–11 R@1 points. On CIRCO, FoCo leads SlerpTAT by +0.5 mAP@5 and PrediCIR by +1.8 mAP@5, with the gap widening at higher recall thresholds (mAP@50: 20.8 vs. 20.6 and 19.3). On FashionIQ, FoCo's average R@10 of 31.5 exceeds HIT (30.3) and PrediCIR (30.1) by 1.2 and 1.4 points respectively, with the largest per-category gain on Shirt (R@10: 33.8 vs. 32.4 for HIT). On GeneCIS, FoCo is most notably ahead of PrediCIR on Focus (+2.4 R@1) and Object (+1.8 R@1) tasks, where precise visual grounding is critical, while matching or slightly exceeding it on Change and Attribute. Scaling to ViT-G/14, FoCo extends its lead over PrediCIR to +1.5 R@1 on CIRR and +1.8 mAP@5 on CIRCO, confirming that the learnable composition mechanism generalizes with backbone capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant ZS-CIR paradigm in which proxy tasks are designed to adapt features to a fixed, non-learnable composition rule (pseudo-word injection into a frozen text encoder or linear/slerp feature arithmetic). FoCo instead treats composition itself as a learnable, two-stage process—text-anchored visual aggregation followed by context-conditioned semantic completion—motivated by human selective attention. The training signal is generated by LLM-based caption decomposition (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) into local and contextual captions, and the optimization uses a cross-instance sigmoid contrastive objective that explicitly prevents unimodal shortcut learning, a design not present in prior ZS-CIR proxy tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the composition mechanism learnable and interpretable (focus on modification-relevant regions, then complete with context), FoCo offers a more principled alternative to ad-hoc pseudo-token or arithmetic composition, and the cross-instance contrastive design provides a reusable recipe for multi-positive, fine-grained contrastive training. Limitations include the dependence on an LLM for caption decomposition at training time (though not at inference) and the use of frozen CLIP backbones, which caps representational capacity. Open questions include whether the two-stage formulation extends to multi-step or open-ended modifications, and whether the learnable composition can be further decoupled from the specific CLIP semantic space.
