## Problem

Mainstream GRPO methods for flow-matching vision generation (e.g., DanceGRPO) require Global-SDE sampling across all denoising timesteps, incurring prohibitive computational overhead from full-trajectory policy-ratio computation and producing unfocused, conflicting gradient signals between early (global structure) and late (fine detail) steps. No prior approach selectively optimizes only the most impactful timesteps while preserving alignment quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262445/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and an initial noise vector x₀ ~ N(0, I), then iteratively denoises over T timesteps (25 for FLUX) to produce a final image or video. During training, only a sliding window of w=4 timesteps uses stochastic SDE sampling for policy optimization, while the remaining T−w timesteps use deterministic ODE (or higher-order ODE) sampling. The reward is evaluated solely on the final denoised output, and the policy ratio and KL penalty are computed exclusively over the SDE window timesteps.

## Method

MixGRPO replaces Global-SDE sampling with a mixed ODE-SDE scheme: within a sliding window W(l) of w timesteps, the denoising step follows an SDE (Euler–Maruyama) that induces a Gaussian conditional policy for GRPO optimization; outside the window, a deterministic ODE step (Euler or DPM-Solver++) is used, which is excluded from the policy-ratio and KL computation. The window slides progressively from low-SNR (high-stochasticity) to high-SNR (deterministic) timesteps over training, implementing a coarse-to-fine curriculum analogous to temporal discounting in RL. Because the ODE segments are deterministic and irrelevant to the optimization objective, higher-order solvers (2nd-order DPM-Solver++) can accelerate them without corrupting the reward signal, yielding the MixGRPO-Flash variant. Coefficients-Preserving Sampling (CPS) is adopted as the stochastic discretizer within the window to reduce high-frequency artifacts and stabilize reward feedback.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev, MixGRPO improves ImageReward from 1.088 (base) to 1.645, surpassing DanceGRPO's 1.436, while optimizing only 4 timesteps versus DanceGRPO's 14. Per-iteration training time drops from 291.3 s (DanceGRPO official) to 149.3 s (MixGRPO, ~49% reduction), and MixGRPO-Flash* further reduces it to 83.3 s (71% reduction) while still outperforming DanceGRPO on all four reward metrics. On SD3.5-M, MixGRPO achieves the best HPS-v2.1 (0.342) and Pick Score (0.236) among all compared alignment methods, with ImageReward of 1.485.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DanceGRPO (the primary Global-SDE baseline), MixGRPO wins on all four reward metrics while optimizing only 4 timesteps versus DanceGRPO's 14, cutting per-iteration time by ~49%; even when DanceGRPO is forced to use only 4 timesteps (random or frozen), MixGRPO still leads by 0.035 on HPS-v2.1 and 0.31 on ImageReward. On SD3.5-M, MixGRPO outperforms Flow-GRPO (0.342 vs 0.331 HPS-v2.1; 0.236 vs 0.232 Pick Score) and DanceGRPO (0.342 vs 0.309 HPS-v2.1) while using fewer policy NFE (4 vs 10). MixGRPO also beats DiffusionNFT on HPS-v2.1 (0.342 vs 0.313) and Pick Score (0.236 vs 0.235), though it trails Online Flow-DPO slightly on ImageReward (1.485 vs 1.500). Against Offline and Online Flow-DPO, MixGRPO achieves the best HPS-v2.1 and Pick Score but is marginally behind Online Flow-DPO on ImageReward. Overall, MixGRPO is the only method that simultaneously achieves top HPS-v2.1, top Pick Score, and competitive ImageReward while requiring the fewest policy forward passes (4 NFE_π_θ), confirming its efficiency–performance advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

MixGRPO builds directly on the GRPO-for-flow-matching line initiated by Flow-GRPO [20] and DanceGRPO [49], which frame SDE sampling as a full-horizon MDP and optimize all denoising timesteps. The key departure is replacing Global-SDE with a mixed ODE-SDE formulation where stochastic exploration is confined to a sliding window of timesteps, drawing an explicit analogy to temporal discounting in RL. The deterministic (ODE) segments are further accelerated via higher-order solvers (DPM-Solver++), repurposing a technique originally developed for diffusion model inference. Coefficients-Preserving Sampling [43] is adopted as a stable stochastic discretizer to reduce reward-hacking artifacts, complementing the core scheduling contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 50–71% reduction in per-iteration training time while simultaneously improving alignment metrics across four reward models, validated on FLUX, SD3.5-M, an 80B-parameter HunyuanImage-3.0, and HunyuanVideo-1.5. The sliding-window curriculum (coarse-to-fine from high-noise to low-noise) is a simple, low-overhead design choice that practitioners can adopt with minimal engineering. The primary open limitation is that performance remains bounded by reward-model quality, and the fixed hyperparameter scheduler (w, τ, s) has not yet been replaced by an adaptive, signal-driven mechanism. Extending the framework to stronger or learned reward models, and to fully adaptive window scheduling, are natural next steps.
