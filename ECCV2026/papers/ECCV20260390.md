## Problem

Visual reasoning in 360° immersive environments is fundamentally challenged by partial observability, where critical evidence may lie outside the agent's current view. Existing VLMs rely on a rigid open-loop 'look first, think later' paradigm—either processing geometrically distorted equirectangular panoramas or reasoning over a fixed set of pre-determined viewports—which prevents the model from dynamically seeking new evidence when its initial perception proves insufficient. The paper addresses the absence of a closed-loop mechanism where an evolving reasoning hypothesis actively steers where the model looks next.

## Input / Output

DIVR takes a 360° equirectangular image and a natural-language question as input. Rather than consuming the full panorama or a fixed crop set, it dynamically selects viewports from a grid of K=36 overlapping candidates covering multiple FoV sizes uniformly distributed across the sphere. The output is a VQA answer generated by a VLM decoder (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or GLM-4.1V-9B) that receives the fully-denoised latent reasoning hypothesis z_0 as prefix tokens, effectively conditioning the answer on an iteratively refined, evidence-grounded reasoning trace.

## Method

DIVR reframes immersive visual reasoning as a denoising diffusion process over a latent hypothesis vector z_t ∈ R^D, starting from pure Gaussian noise (maximal uncertainty) and iteratively refining it over T=20 steps into a coherent, evidence-grounded reasoning state. Two tightly coupled modules operate in a closed loop: the Latent Hypothesis Refiner (LHR), a 6-block Transformer denoiser that uses cross-attention to condition on a time-varying vector c_t = [question; accumulated visual evidence] and predict the noise ε to step z_t → z_{t-1}; and the Reasoning-Guided Observer (RGO), a 3-layer MLP policy that scores all 36 candidate viewports via Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable selection, with a GRU maintaining an aggregated observation history projected through an Adapter into the conditioning vector. Training is end-to-end with a composite loss combining the diffusion noise-prediction objective and a cross-entropy task loss, where ground-truth reasoning rationales for z_0 are generated via knowledge distillation from GPT-5 using saliency-selected viewports, enabling the model to learn the full multi-step reasoning process rather than just the final answer.

## Results

DIVR (with Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 56.75% accuracy on IQVA, 64.72% on 360+X, 70.45% on ZInD Easy, and 41.89% on ZInD Hard, establishing new state-of-the-art across all benchmarks. The most significant gain is on ZInD Hard, where DIVR surpasses the strongest baseline (GPT-5 with saliency input) by 6.25 percentage points (41.89% vs. 35.64%), demonstrating the critical advantage of dynamic, multi-step evidence gathering for complex spatial reasoning. With GLM-4.1V-9B, DIVR similarly leads with 56.61% on IQVA and 71.33% on 360+X.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVR outperforms all three baselines across every benchmark. Against SPHTR (spherical attention), DIVR gains over 10 points on IQVA and nearly 18 points on ZInD Hard (41.89% vs. 23.55%), showing that addressing geometric distortion alone is insufficient. Against SLiME-8B, even its strongest saliency-guided variant (53.43% IQVA, 34.14% ZInD Hard) is surpassed by DIVR by 3.3 and 7.75 points respectively, indicating that improved spatial coverage via adaptive patching cannot substitute for active, reasoning-driven exploration. Most notably, DIVR beats zero-shot GPT-5 with saliency input (the strongest baseline) by 0.91 points on IQVA, 6.10 points on 360+X, and 6.25 points on ZInD Hard (41.89% vs. 35.64%), confirming that a frontier VLM's static, pre-determined viewport strategy is fundamentally limited compared to DIVR's closed-loop, hypothesis-guided perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the denoising diffusion framework (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) from generative modeling to iterative belief updating in visual reasoning, a conceptual leap from prior diffusion applications in trajectory planning or policy optimization. It departs sharply from the dominant open-loop paradigm in 360° VQA—where models process a fixed equirectangular image, a static cubemap, or a pre-selected set of saliency-guided viewports—by introducing a closed-loop architecture (LHR + RGO) in which an evolving latent reasoning hypothesis actively steers viewport selection at each step. The VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, GLM-4.1V) and the knowledge-distillation strategy for generating reasoning targets from a teacher model (GPT-5) are standard components; the novelty lies in how they are coupled through the diffusion loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent outperformance across three benchmarks and two VLM backbones, particularly the >6-point absolute gain on multi-viewport ZInD Hard questions, provides evidence that dynamic, reasoning-guided perception is not merely beneficial but essential for complex immersive tasks. However, the approach incurs a linear computational cost in the number of diffusion steps (T=20 yields ~1 QPS vs. ~3–4 QPS for single-pass baselines), and the training pipeline depends on a powerful teacher VLM for distilling reasoning traces, limiting accessibility. Open questions include whether the diffusion formulation is strictly necessary versus simpler iterative refinement schemes, how the framework scales to longer-horizon embodied tasks with physical navigation, and whether the closed-loop coupling generalizes beyond 360° VQA to other partially observable reasoning settings.
