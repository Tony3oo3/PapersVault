## Problem

Visual prompt tuning (VPT) relies on a single fixed fusion operator (typically concatenation or addition) to combine learnable prompt tokens with image tokens at every transformer layer, yet it remains unknown whether this uniform choice is optimal or whether different layers benefit from different fusion mechanisms. The paper asks whether a hybrid, layer-specific fusion scheme can outperform any single operator and, if so, what additional fusion primitives should be included in the search space to enable such a hybrid.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260973/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and produces class labels through a frozen ViT backbone (ViT-Base/16 or Swin-Base). At each transformer layer, m learnable prompt tokens are fused with the k image tokens (including CLS) via a layer-specific operator before entering the frozen multi-head self-attention sublayer; the backbone interface is kept fixed at k×d so positional embeddings and attention map sizes never change. Only 0.75% of the ViT's parameters are tuned (prompt tokens plus lightweight operator-internal weights), and after discretization the inference path has the same latency profile as a fixed-fusion VPT variant.

## Method

The paper formulates prompt fusion as a DARTS-style bilevel optimization: the inner loop optimizes prompt tokens and operator-internal weights on training data, while the outer loop refines per-layer architecture logits on validation data to select the best fusion operator. Four candidate operators are defined—concat (prefix prompts then reduce via a column-stochastic matrix to preserve the k×d interface), add (broadcast a LayerNorm-mean prompt summary as a residual bias), affine (FiLM-style per-channel scale-and-shift generated by small MLPs from the prompt summary), and cross-attention (image tokens attend to prompt tokens as a memory with shared projections). A temperature-annealed softmax over per-layer logits produces soft mixing weights during search, and two regularizers (negative entropy for exploration and a cost prior favoring cheaper operators) prevent premature collapse; after search, each layer is discretized to its argmax operator and briefly fine-tuned. An Information Bottleneck analysis shows that the learned layer-wise schedule achieves a lower empirical IB surrogate than fixed-fusion VPT, with shallow layers favoring lightweight operators (concat/add) that preserve structural cues and deeper layers shifting toward semantic operators (affine/cross-attention) that emphasize label-relevant patterns.

## Results

On ViT-Base/16, the proposed method achieves 77.01% mean accuracy on VTAB-1k (19 datasets), 91.60% on FGVC (5 datasets), and 92.5% on HTA (10 datasets), while tuning only 0.75% of backbone parameters. Category-wise VTAB-1k scores are 82.88% (Natural), 85.61% (Specialized), and 62.55% (Structured). Under MoCo v3 pretraining, the method reaches 79.60/86.86/61.01% across Natural/Specialized/Structured at just 0.25% tuned parameters, and on Swin-Base it attains 85.52/86.49/62.36% at 0.26% tuned.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all prompt-tuning baselines on VTAB-1k mean accuracy: +1.52 over VFPT (75.49%), +1.18 over SA²VP (75.83%), and +7.58 over VPT-Deep (69.43%), while tuning a comparable or smaller parameter budget (0.75% vs. 0.66–0.81%). On FGVC, it leads LoR-VIP (91.22%) by +0.38 and SA²VP (90.08%) by +1.52; on HTA it surpasses SA²VP (91.5%) by +1.0. The largest single-category margin is on Structured VTAB tasks (+2.36 over VFPT, +1.75 over SA²VP), indicating that layer-wise operator selection is most beneficial for geometric/calibration-heavy tasks. The only near-tie is on Specialized VTAB, where SA²VP edges ahead by 0.12 points (85.73% vs. 85.61%). Under MoCo v3 pretraining, the method achieves the best scores in all three VTAB categories (79.60/86.86/61.01%) at 0.25% tuned, beating VFPT by +2.13/+1.10/+2.27 respectively, and on Swin-Base it surpasses VFPT and SA²VP in every category with fewer tunable parameters (0.26% vs. 0.27–0.29%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) by replacing its fixed fusion operator (concatenation or addition) with a layer-wise searchable module, borrowing the DARTS bilevel optimization framework to jointly optimize prompt parameters and fusion choices. It also introduces two new fusion primitives—FiLM-style affine modulation and cross-attention—as additional candidates beyond the traditional concat/add pair, enriching the search space. Conceptually, the work bridges neural architecture search and parameter-efficient fine-tuning, a combination not previously explored in the visual prompt tuning literature, while the Information Bottleneck analysis provides a theoretical lens absent from prior VPT studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no single fusion operator is uniformly optimal and that a depth-wise schedule (lightweight in shallow layers, semantic in deep layers) consistently improves accuracy offers a practical design principle for prompt tuning that practitioners can adopt without redesigning the backbone. However, the gains over the strongest recent baselines (VFPT, SA2VP) are modest (~1–1.5 points on VTAB-1k mean), and the IB analysis relies on variational surrogates rather than causal evidence, leaving open whether the observed compression-relevance trade-off is the true mechanism. Scaling to larger backbones (ViT-L/H), more diverse search spaces, and non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation) remain unexplored, as does whether the discovered layer-wise patterns are stable across architectures or task families.
