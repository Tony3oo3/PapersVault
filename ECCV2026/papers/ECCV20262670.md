## Problem

Deterministic LiDAR segmentation networks produce overconfident predictions that are poorly calibrated, which is dangerous in safety-critical autonomous driving. Existing uncertainty methods (deep ensembles, MC Dropout) require multiple forward passes or independently trained models, making them too slow for real-time deployment, while evidential deep learning lacks theoretical guarantees of consistency with Bayesian posterior inference. The paper asks whether well-calibrated epistemic uncertainty can be obtained analytically in a single deterministic forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262670/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point clouds projected into range-view images of 64×1024×5 (SemanticKITTI) or 32×1024×5 (nuScenes), where the five channels encode range, Cartesian coordinates, and intensity. A deterministic backbone (CNN or Transformer) extracts 64-dimensional per-pixel features, which are then passed through the GMM-NIG head. The output is a per-pixel class posterior obtained by Bayes' rule over a mixture of Student-t densities, from which calibrated probabilities, Shannon-entropy uncertainty, and failure-detection scores are derived—all in one forward pass with no stochastic sampling.

## Method

GMM-NIG replaces the conventional softmax classifier with class-conditional Gaussian mixture models in the learned feature space, trained via a hybrid Sinkhorn-EM (entropy-regularized optimal transport for the E-step) coupled with discriminative supervision (Lovász-Softmax + Focal Loss) on the backbone. The key extension over GMMSeg is placing conjugate Normal-Inverse-Gamma priors over each GMM component's mean and variance, so that posterior hyperparameters are updated in closed form from the training data. At inference, analytically marginalizing the NIG posterior yields a product of univariate Student-t densities per component, whose heavy tails assign lower probability to points far from cluster centers, producing higher entropy and better-calibrated uncertainty. The backbone remains fully deterministic; all Bayesian uncertainty is confined to the lightweight GMM head, enabling single-pass inference at the same runtime as a standard network while outperforming deep ensembles and MC Dropout on calibration, failure detection, and segmentation accuracy.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI with the RangeFormer backbone, GMM-NIG achieves 65.71% mIoU, 2.01% Adaptive Calibration Error (ACE), 89.01% AUROC, and 53.33% AUPR for failure detection, all at a runtime of 0.23 s per scan. On nuScenes (RangeFormer), it reaches 80.18% mIoU and 1.97% ACE. Across all three range-view backbones (RangeFormer, RangeViT, SalsaNext) and two additional 3D backbones (PTv3, SphereFormer), GMM-NIG consistently attains the lowest ACE (1.26–3.09%) and highest failure-detection scores while matching the inference speed of a single deterministic forward pass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMM-NIG outperforms all eight baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against the strongest sampling-based baseline, Deep Ensembles (5 models), GMM-NIG improves mIoU by +0.80% (65.71 vs. 64.91 on RangeFormer/SemanticKITTI), reduces ACE by 0.80 pp (2.01 vs. 2.81), and raises AUPR by 5.26 pp (53.33 vs. 48.07), while running ~5.7× faster (0.23 s vs. 1.31 s). MC Dropout is even further behind on both accuracy and calibration, and is ~9.5× slower. Among post-hoc calibration methods (Temperature, Dirichlet, Depth-aware Scaling), GMM-NIG reduces ACE by 2.1–3.0 pp and boosts AUPR by 15–16 pp, showing that rescaling logits cannot capture the epistemic uncertainty that the NIG posterior predictive provides. EDL, despite its Dirichlet formulation, underperforms even the uncalibrated MSP baseline on AUROC and AUPR for SalsaNext, confirming the theoretical concerns raised in recent critiques. Finally, GMM-NIG improves over its direct predecessor GMMSeg (point-estimated GMM) by +1.81% mIoU and −1.87 pp ACE on RangeFormer, demonstrating that the Bayesian marginalization over GMM parameters is the key source of the calibration and accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GMMSeg's generative classification framework by replacing deterministic point estimates of class-conditional GMM parameters with conjugate Normal-Inverse-Gamma priors, enabling analytic posterior-predictive uncertainty via Student-t marginalization in a single forward pass. It positions itself against Evidential Deep Learning, which recent theoretical work has shown to lack consistency with Bayesian posterior inference, and against sampling-based methods (Deep Ensembles, MC Dropout) that require multiple forward passes or independently trained models. The Sinkhorn-EM algorithm for GMM training and the hybrid generative-discriminative loss (Lovász-Softmax + Focal Loss) are inherited from GMMSeg, while the Bayesian layer over the classifier head is the core new contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically attractive combination: Bayesian-calibrated uncertainty with the inference cost of a deterministic network, making it suitable for real-time safety-critical LiDAR perception where Deep Ensembles are infeasible. The assumption of feature independence (factorized covariance) is a simplification that the ablation shows actually yields better calibration than low-rank alternatives, but it limits modeling of inter-feature correlations. Open questions include whether full-covariance NIW priors would help in higher-dimensional feature spaces, whether the approach extends naturally to open-set or panoptic segmentation, and whether the Bayesian treatment at the head level (rather than the full backbone) is sufficient for robust out-of-distribution generalization.
