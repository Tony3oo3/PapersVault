## Problem

Existing human video animation methods excel at transferring body pose but treat hair as a static texture mapped to the head, producing fluid-like, unnatural secondary dynamics that shatter photorealism. The root cause is a critical lack of high-fidelity paired data containing both photorealistic appearance and diverse, controllable, physically-plausible hair geometry conditions, which prevents diffusion models from learning expressive hair motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261411/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single photorealistic reference image (896×512), a body pose condition rendered as camera-space normal maps with 68 facial landmarks (hair geometry hidden), and a hair motion condition encoded as a dense per-pixel UVW buffer where U,V are scalp texture coordinates at strand roots and W is the normalized arc-length along each hair curve. The output is a 97-frame photorealistic video at 30 FPS with physically plausible hair dynamics. For real-world evaluation on NeRSemble where UVW maps are unavailable, the framework substitutes alpha maps for hair and DensePose for body, demonstrating flexibility with coarser signals.

## Method

HairWeaver builds on the frozen LTX-Video 0.9.8 DiT backbone and introduces two lightweight LoRA modules. The Motion-Context-LoRA injects body pose via a trainable Pose Encoder whose tokens are added element-wise to the noisy latent, while hair UVW tokens (encoded by a frozen VAE) are concatenated along the sequence length so the DiT's self-attention can directly attend to strand-level motion. A temporary Style-Alignment-LoRA is first pre-trained with a standard I2V objective on 1,500 CG-simulated videos (83 min, rendered with Blender Cycles using Cosserat-rod physics) to absorb the non-photorealistic domain style; it is then frozen while the Motion-Context-LoRA is trained on the full quadruplet of conditions. At inference the Style-Alignment-LoRA is discarded entirely, so generation is driven solely by the Motion-Context-LoRA on the original photorealistic DiT, transferring precise motion physics without inheriting CG artifacts.

## Results

On the photorealistic NeRSemble test-set, HairWeaver achieves the best hair FID of 17.79 and cd-FVD of 286.25, outperforming the strongest baseline Wan-2.2-Animate-14B (FID 20.48, cd-FVD 328.67) by 13.2% and 12.9% respectively. For full-portrait quality, HairWeaver also leads with FID 19.43 and cd-FVD 212.61 versus 24.32 and 314.65 for the best baseline. In a 30-participant user study, HairWeaver is preferred 49.9% of the time, nearly 3× the next-best method (Wan-2.2-Animate-14B at 16.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HairWeaver outperforms all four baseline families across every metric on the NeRSemble photorealistic benchmark. Against the strongest animation baseline Wan-2.2-Animate-14B, HairWeaver improves hair FID from 20.48 to 17.79 (−13.2%) and hair cd-FVD from 328.67 to 286.25 (−12.9%), while also gaining 5.6 dB in PSNR (28.75→34.34). The I2V baselines LTX-Video-0.9.8-13B and Wan-2.2-14B are far behind, with hair FIDs of 120.74 and 113.11 respectively, confirming that pose-conditioned animation methods are necessary for this task. UniAnimate-DiT, which uses a similar pose-encoder architecture, still trails HairWeaver by 73.7% in hair FID (66.29 vs 17.79), highlighting the benefit of the dedicated hair UVW conditioning. LTX-Video-ICLora, the in-context LoRA variant, is the closest non-animation competitor (hair FID 44.11) but remains 2.5× worse than HairWeaver. The user study reinforces these quantitative gaps: HairWeaver is chosen as most realistic 49.9% of the time versus 16.4% for Wan-2.2-Animate-14B and 9.5% for UniAnimate-DiT.

## Relation to Prior Work

HairWeaver extends the human image-to-video animation paradigm (LTX-Video, UniAnimate-DiT, Wan2.2-Animate) by introducing explicit hair motion as a first-class conditioning signal via a UVW map, rather than treating hair as a static texture. It builds on the Pose Encoder architecture from UniAnimate-DiT for body control and the unified attention (sequence concatenation) mechanism from DreamActor-M1 for hair control, but departs from prior hair-specific work like ControlHair by relying on perfectly-aligned CG simulation data rather than approximate extracted maps. The two-stage training with a temporary Style-Alignment-LoRA that is discarded at inference is a novel sim-to-real transfer strategy not seen in prior human animation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a persistent and visible failure mode in human animation—stiff, fluid-like hair—that undermines the realism of otherwise convincing generated characters, with direct applications in VR, gaming, and film. The elegant trick of using a throwaway LoRA to bridge the CG-to-photorealistic domain gap during training, then discarding it at inference, offers a generalizable recipe for leveraging synthetic physics data without inheriting its visual artifacts. Limitations include degraded performance under extreme pose deviations and difficulty rendering hands, and the approach is currently scoped to hair; whether the same framework extends to other secondary dynamics (clothing, accessories) remains an open question.
