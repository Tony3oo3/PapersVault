## Problem

Both imitation learning and reinforcement learning paradigms for end-to-end autonomous driving still depend on human demonstrations, which are scarce for safety-critical, long-tail scenarios. Pure reward-based RL could eliminate this dependency, but standard policy gradient methods suffer from the cold-start problem: a randomly initialized agent cannot find high-reward trajectories in a large continuous action space without structured guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260996/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a concatenated multi-view image (frontal view plus center-cropped front-left and front-right views, resized to 512×2048) along with ego-vehicle status as input, and a fixed vocabulary of 16,384 trajectories (obtained via K-means clustering on nuPlan, each spanning 4 seconds at 10 Hz) as a constant model input. At inference, the output trajectory is selected from this discrete vocabulary by a weighted average of the policy head's likelihoods and the scoring heads' predicted rule-based metric scores, following the Hydra-MDP inference mechanism.

## Method

ZTRS is a trajectory scorer comprising an image backbone (ViT-L or V2-99), a trajectory tokenizer, a Transformer Decoder, a policy head, and multiple scoring heads, all trained end-to-end from scratch on offline data. The core contribution is Exhaustive Policy Optimization (EPO), which exploits the fact that the action space is a finite, enumerable trajectory vocabulary: rather than sampling a single action, EPO computes the policy gradient as a sum over all 16,384 trajectories of the advantage times the gradient of the action likelihood (Eq. 7), providing dense supervision that resolves the cold-start problem without IL pre-training. The advantage function is the EPDMS rule-based score (aggregating collision, drivable-area, progress, comfort, and other metrics) minus a temporal-consistency correction term that penalizes Extended Comfort violations between consecutive timesteps. Theoretically, EPO is shown to be equivalent to the classical policy gradient theorem when the advantage is computable for every action, but its exhaustive enumeration yields far more stable and exploratory optimization than sampled baselines like REINFORCE or GRPO.

## Results

On the long-tail Navhard benchmark, ZTRS achieves SOTA EPDMS of 45.5 (V2-99) and 46.2 (EVA-ViT-L), outperforming the strongest IL baselines GTRS-Dense (41.7/43.4) and DriveSuprim (42.1/44.7) by +3.8 and +2.8 EPDMS respectively. On the closed-loop HUGSIM benchmark, ZTRS achieves 42.0 RC and 32.9 HD-Score overall, surpassing GTRS-Dense (34.5 RC, 28.2 HD-Score) and UniAD (40.6 RC, 28.9 HD-Score) via zero-shot transfer. On nominal Navtest, ZTRS matches or exceeds SOTA IL planners: 83.2 EPDMS (ResNet34), 86.2 (ViT-L), and 85.3 (V2-99), despite never training on human trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZTRS outperforms all IL-based trajectory scorers on Navhard: with V2-99 it beats GTRS-Dense by +3.8 EPDMS (45.5 vs 41.7) and DriveSuprim by +3.4 (45.5 vs 42.1); with EVA-ViT-L it leads DriveSuprim by +1.5 and GTRS-Dense by +2.8. On HUGSIM (closed-loop), ZTRS achieves 32.9 HD-Score vs GTRS-Dense's 28.2 and UniAD's 28.9, demonstrating that reward-driven training transfers to closed-loop without exposure to HUGSIM's synthetic scenarios. On Navtest (nominal driving), ZTRS is competitive but slightly trails DriveSuprim on ViT-L (86.2 vs 87.1) and V2-99 (85.3 vs 86.0), primarily due to a lower Ego Progress score (84.1–86.9 vs 90.5–90.6) since EP measures L2 distance to human trajectories that ZTRS never sees. Against the rule-based PDM-Closed planner (51.3 EPDMS on Navhard), ZTRS with V2-99 (45.5) and EVA-ViT-L (46.2) remain below, but PDM-Closed uses privileged ground-truth symbolic inputs unavailable to learned methods. In the RL policy gradient ablation (Fig. 3, Table 6), EPO substantially outperforms REINFORCE (85.3 vs 75.0/72.6 EPDMS on Navtest) and two GRPO variants, which show instability and model collapse risk due to unrepresentative trajectory sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZTRS extends the trajectory scorer paradigm established by Hydra-MDP and GTRS, which score a discrete vocabulary of candidate trajectories and select the best one, but replaces their imitation-learning training objective with a pure reinforcement-learning signal. The key departure is from both IL-based scorers (which require human trajectory labels) and standard RL methods (PPO, REINFORCE, GRPO) that suffer from cold-start in large continuous action spaces. By proposing Exhaustive Policy Optimization (EPO), which evaluates every trajectory in the vocabulary at each step rather than sampling, the paper reframes policy gradient optimization as a dense, deterministic computation over a fixed action set, drawing on the policy gradient theorem's equivalence between log-likelihood and likelihood formulations when the full action space is enumerable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that rule-based driving metrics alone—without any human trajectory supervision—can train a competitive E2E planner that generalizes better to long-tail and safety-critical scenarios than IL methods, challenging the field's assumption that human demonstrations are indispensable. The zero-shot closed-loop transfer to HUGSIM (trained only on open-loop Navtrain) further validates that EPO does not merely overfit open-loop rewards. Key limitations include a persistent gap in Ego Progress (EP) on nominal driving, since the model never imitates human trajectories, and the requirement that the action space be a fixed, enumerable vocabulary, which constrains extension to continuous trajectory generation. Whether this reward-only paradigm can scale to fully continuous action spaces or multi-step closed-loop training remains an open question.
