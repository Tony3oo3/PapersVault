## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image and text-to-video models face a fundamental trade-off: robust erasure against adversarial red-teaming attacks often degrades generative quality for safe, unrelated prompts. Additionally, token-level linear projection approaches (e.g., SAFREE) fail on modern text encoders like T5 because semantic meaning is distributed across the entire sentence through bidirectional attention, making flat orthogonal projections unable to cleanly isolate and remove contextually entangled toxic concepts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user text prompt and a target concept prompt (e.g., 'sexual, nudity, sex, porn, naked'), both encoded by the model's native text encoder (CLIP or T5) into token-level embeddings of shape n×d and m×d respectively. If the classifier detects the target concept (probability > 0.5), it iteratively refines the prompt embedding via weighted gradient descent before passing it to the generative model; safe prompts pass through completely unmodified, preserving the base model's original output distribution. The final output is a safe image or video generated by the unmodified T2I/T2V model from the refined embedding.

## Method

CGCE trains a lightweight classifier on LLM-generated paired safe/unsafe prompts using a multi-head cross-attention architecture where prompt tokens act as queries attending to concept tokens as keys and values, enabling per-token semantic alignment scoring rather than global concept detection. At inference, the classifier serves a dual role: first as a safeguard that gates whether the embedding needs modification, and second as a refiner that iteratively updates the unsafe embedding using token-importance-weighted gradients (s ⊙ ∇f_θ) scaled by a norm-based adaptive factor. This weighted gradient localizes updates to tokens most semantically related to the target concept while minimally affecting unrelated prompt components. The approach is plug-and-play and model-agnostic—operating entirely in the text embedding space—so a single classifier trained on CLIP embeddings safeguards both SD-v1.4 and SD-v2.1, while one trained on T5 embeddings covers SD-v3, FLUX, VAR models, and T2V architectures without any modification to the generative model's weights.

## Results

On SD-v1.4 nudity removal, CGCE achieves ASR of 2.47% (SixCD), 4.41% (P4D), 3.87% (RAB), and 1.90% (MMAD), ranking first on four of six adversarial benchmarks. It maintains near-original utility with FID 16.10 and CLIP score 31.03 on COCO, compared to the unmodified SD-v1.4's 16.79 and 31.31. On FLUX (a guidance-distilled model where SAFREE collapses), CGCE reduces RAB ASR to 0.70% versus SAFREE's 87.89%. On T2V models, CGCE achieves frame-level ASR of 3.02% (CogX-5B, Gen) and 0.86% (CogX-5B, SafeSora-Sexual), outperforming both SAFREE and the video-specific T2VU baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGCE decisively outperforms SAFREE [46] across all six nudity benchmarks on SD-v1.4 (e.g., RAB: 3.87% vs 74.74%; UDA: 27.46% vs 80.99%), and the gap widens dramatically on FLUX where SAFREE's linear projection fails (RAB: 0.70% vs 87.89%). Against the strongest weight-modifying baseline STEREO [36], CGCE wins on SixCD (2.47 vs 4.74), P4D (4.41 vs 5.15), RAB (3.87 vs 4.21), and MMAD (1.90 vs 7.90), while STEREO retains a narrow edge on I2P (0.75 vs 4.62) and UDA (28.17 vs 27.46); however, STEREO degrades utility (FID 17.98, CLIP 30.16) whereas CGCE preserves it (FID 16.10, CLIP 31.03). Among training-free methods, CGCE also beats SLD-Max [34] on five of six benchmarks and EraseFlow [20] on RAB (3.87 vs 29.47) and MMAD (1.90 vs 4.60). On T2V models, CGCE outperforms both SAFREE and the video-specific T2VU [45] on all three tested architectures (e.g., CogX-5B Gen: 3.02% vs 23.61% SAFREE and 10.59% T2VU). For artistic style removal, CGCE achieves 0% ASR and LPIPS_d of 0.43, matching or exceeding all baselines while uniquely preserving unrelated styles (LPIPS_u = 0.00).

## Relation to Prior Work

CGCE carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of work: training-free inference-time safeguards (SLD, SAFREE) and learned text-embedding classifiers. It departs from SAFREE's token-level linear projection by replacing it with a cross-attention-based classifier that captures holistic prompt semantics, motivated by the finding that T5's anisotropic, entangled embedding space makes flat orthogonal projection geometrically insufficient. Unlike fine-tuning or closed-form editing methods that permanently alter model weights, CGCE repurposes the inference-time intervention paradigm but grounds it in a lightweight learned detector-refiner loop, making it architecture-agnostic across DiT, VAR, and T2V backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, weight-preserving design makes CGCE immediately deployable as a safety layer on production generative models without retraining, addressing a critical practical gap. The demonstration that linear projection fundamentally fails on T5-based encoders (FLUX, SD-v3) challenges a working assumption in the field that embedding-space geometry supports clean linear concept separation. Open questions include whether the per-concept classifier requirement scales to open-ended safety policies, how the method handles multi-concept or compositional adversarial prompts, and whether the 27% residual ASR on UDA-style attacks indicates a fundamental ceiling for text-embedding-level intervention.
