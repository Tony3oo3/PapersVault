## Problem

Existing vision-language-action models and video-based world models for robotic manipulation predominantly target static or quasi-static tasks, and when extended to dynamic scenarios they suffer from three critical limitations: lack of explicit 3D geometric awareness, physically implausible future predictions, and prohibitive inference latency from chained large models. No general framework or benchmark exists for manipulating fast-moving targets in unstructured 3D environments where objects exhibit complex linear, circular, or rotational motion.

## Input / Output

The system consumes streaming RGB-D frames (240×320) from four fixed cameras, a language task instruction, and proprioceptive robot state at 5 Hz control frequency. The world model outputs six basic velocity components (three linear, three angular) per 3D Gaussian (H=30,000 total) plus rendered future RGB-D predictions up to 10 timesteps ahead. The policy model produces end-effector keyposes—3D position, 6D orientation, and binary gripper state—over a temporal horizon J, which are converted to joint-space trajectories via inverse kinematics and executed through a temporal-ensemble action buffer.

## Method

PhysMani couples two parallel components: a physics-principled 3D Gaussian world model and a future-aware action policy. The world model, reformulated from FreeGave as an online optimization loop, maintains a fixed set of 3D Gaussians and learns a divergence-free per-Gaussian velocity field via an MLP (f_vel) that predicts six basic velocity components; each new frame triggers T=50 gradient iterations to optimize velocity and T'=7 iterations to refine geometry, taking ~200 ms on an RTX 4090. The policy model uses 3DFA (Rectified Flow–based diffusion) as its backbone and incorporates future dynamics by, for each of 4096 sparse 3D scene points, retrieving K nearest Gaussians via KNN, computing relative spatial offsets, and fusing offset and velocity tokens through a learnable query vector L in cross-attention blocks; the resulting dynamics tokens are added to visual tokens before flow-matching denoising. Training is imitation learning on 160 scripted expert trajectories per task (16 tasks total) with L2 flow-matching loss and binary cross-entropy for gripper state, and the entire pipeline achieves 272.8 ms per inference frame.

## Results

On PhysMani-Bench (16 dynamic manipulation tasks), PhysMani achieves a mean success rate of 45.9% (normal speed) and 45.9% (high speed), outperforming the strongest baseline 3DFA (37.8%) by 8.1 points. For future frame prediction, PhysMani attains PSNR 26.90, SSIM 0.877, and trajectory error 0.008 m at the next frame, substantially surpassing FreeGave (PSNR 19.47, traj. err. 0.010 m) and ManiGaussian (PSNR 14.32, traj. err. 0.388 m). On four real-world tasks with an Astribot S1 robot, PhysMani reaches 62.5% mean success rate versus 45.3% for 3DFA. Inference latency is 272.8 ms/frame, comparable to 3DFA (264.5 ms) and far faster than ManiGaussian (897.7 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysMani outperforms all six baselines on the primary metric (mean success rate across 16 tasks). Against its own backbone 3DFA (37.8%), PhysMani gains 8.1 points, with the largest per-task advantage on Drop to Hoop (71.7% vs. 30.8%) and Pick Cube (84.2% vs. 70.0%), confirming that the 3D future-dynamics tokens are the source of improvement. The VLA model pi0.5 (8.3%) and the voxel-based Act3D (27.1%) trail by 37.6 and 18.8 points respectively, largely because they lack explicit 3D geometry and physics-grounded motion forecasting. ManiGaussian (22.5%) is the closest 3D-Gaussian-based competitor but is far weaker on future-frame prediction (PSNR 14.32 vs. 26.90; trajectory error 0.388 m vs. 0.008 m), indicating its dynamic Gaussian splatting does not produce physically meaningful motion. FreeGave, the direct predecessor for the world model, is outperformed on every prediction metric (e.g., PSNR 26.90 vs. 19.47 at next frame) and runs 3× faster (205 ms vs. 607 ms per frame), validating the online-optimization reformulation. On real-world tasks, PhysMani (62.5%) beats 3DFA (45.3%) by 17.2 points, with the largest gap on Place on Rack (25.0% vs. 6.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysMani sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: 3D Gaussian-based physics learning (FreeGave, which learns divergence-free velocity fields from video) and 3D scene-conditioned action policies (3DFA, which uses Rectified Flow for fast keypose prediction). The paper's key architectural departure from FreeGave is removing the offline deformation network and reformulating the velocity learning as a lightweight online optimization loop (~200 ms/frame), making it viable for real-time manipulation. On the policy side, it extends 3DFA by injecting predicted 3D scene dynamics through a learnable-token cross-attention module, rather than relying on 2D motion cues or purely reactive perception. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both VLA models (which lack explicit 3D geometry and physical dynamics) and video-generation world models (which produce visually plausible but physically implausible futures), arguing that a compact, physics-constrained 3D representation is the right substrate for dynamic manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of PhysMani-Bench (16 dynamic tasks with normal/high-speed variants) fills a notable gap: prior manipulation benchmarks (RLBench, CALVIN, LIBERO) are almost entirely static or quasi-static, leaving no standard evaluation for general dynamic target manipulation. The finding that 2D optical flow provides no benefit over a 3D policy backbone, while physics-principled 3D velocity prediction yields an 8-point gain, offers a concrete argument for why explicit 3D physical modeling matters beyond what 2D motion signals can capture. Limitations include the moderate benchmark scale (16 tasks, single-arm tabletop), fixed multi-camera setup, 5 Hz control frequency, and reliance on scripted expert trajectories for training. Open questions include scaling to multi-object interactions, unstructured outdoor environments, and whether the online 3DGS optimization generalizes to deformable or articulated targets.
