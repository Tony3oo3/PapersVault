## Problem

Existing active view-planning methods for neural rendering compute next-best-view scores by backpropagating through or querying the internal state of an online radiance representation (NeRF parameters, 3DGS primitive uncertainties, or training losses). This coupling makes view selection expensive on resource-limited platforms, unreliable before the representation has converged, and tightly tied to a specific rendering backend, hindering lightweight online deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a posed RGB-D stream (512×512, 60°×60° FoV) and a set of locally sampled candidate 6-DoF poses. It maintains two lightweight maps online: a coarse occupancy grid for exploration/safety and a 5 cm voxel-statistics map storing per-voxel directional and color moments. The output is a scored next-best-view (position plus viewing direction) and, for the panoramic variant, an omnidirectional 360° utility per pose; the resulting trajectory of collected RGB-D keyframes is then used to train a vanilla 3DGS model offline for evaluation.

## Method

R3CON decouples view utility from transient optimization state by defining a closed-form renderability score per voxel that captures how well a candidate viewing direction is supported by past observations. Renderability factorizes into three terms in [0,1]: a directional bias term (angular deviation to the nearest historical view, amplified by a binary extrapolation flag detected via tangent-plane projection of past directions), an appearance-noise term (pairwise color consistency tracked with fixed-size Welford moments), and a resolution-gain term (depth-dependent pixel footprint relative to the best historical observation). Directional statistics are maintained with a Fibonacci-lattice bin mask of constant size, making both update and query observation-count-independent and fully vectorizable in milliseconds. A panoramic extension aggregates per-voxel renderability over a 10°-spaced Fibonacci sphere to score omnidirectional view utility at each pose, accelerating direction selection. The renderability score replaces the view-utility module in Active-GS, combined with an exploration-count term and a path-cost penalty to produce the final NBV.

## Results

Under a fixed 300-keyframe view budget on the Replica-Dense benchmark (9 indoor scenes), R3CON (Ours) achieves mean PSNR improvements of approximately 3–5 dB over Active-GS and 5–10 dB over ActiveGAMER, with the largest gains on Office1 (+6.4 dB over Active-GS) and Office2 (+2.9 dB). The panoramic variant (Ours-Pano) further narrows the PSNR distribution across scenes, reducing low-quality tails. Under a 300 s time budget, Ours also collects more keyframes within the same wall-clock time, indicating cheaper per-candidate scoring. Peak GPU memory for Ours/Ours-Pano stays below 300 MB across all scenes, whereas Active-GS grows steeply as it accumulates a full radiance field.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two 3DGS-based active reconstruction baselines: Active-GS [13] (IEEE RA-L 2025) and ActiveGAMER [3] (CVPR 2025). Under the fixed 300-keyframe view budget, Ours outperforms Active-GS on PSNR in 8 of 9 scenes (e.g., +5.0 dB on Off0, +6.4 dB on Off1, +2.9 dB on Off2) and on LPIPS in all 9 scenes; the only near-tie is Hotel0 where Ours trails by 0.01 SSIM. Against ActiveGAMER, the margin is substantially larger, with Ours gaining 3–10 dB in PSNR depending on scene. The panoramic variant (Ours-Pano) further improves over the base Ours in most scenes (e.g., +1.6 dB on Off3, +0.8 dB on Room0) by mitigating local viewpoint degeneracy, though in the small Office0 scene it slightly trails Ours on PSNR (34.34 vs. 34.69) due to saturated coverage. Under the 300 s time budget, Ours also collects more keyframes than Active-GS within the same wall-clock time, reflecting the lower per-candidate evaluation cost of the closed-form renderability score versus Active-GS's gradient-based utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

R3CON departs from the dominant paradigm in rendering-driven active reconstruction (Active-GS, ActiveGAMER, Fisherrf, GauSS-MI) where view utility is derived from the internal optimization state of an evolving radiance representation—gradients, Fisher information, or mutual information over Gaussian primitives. Instead, it inherits the renderability concept originally proposed for image-based rendering (Yi et al., 2023) and later adapted to 3DGS pseudo-view sampling (Jin et al., 2025), but reinterprets it through an uncertainty lens and reformulates it as a closed-form, observation-statistics-driven score computed from a lightweight voxel map. The system architecture (coarse occupancy grid + fine statistics map, weighted exploration/rendering/path-cost scoring) is directly built on Active-GS, with the only replacement being the information-gain module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is decoupling NBV scoring from any online radiance-field optimization, yielding millisecond-level candidate evaluation and sub-300 MB GPU memory regardless of trajectory length—properties critical for on-board deployment on resource-limited platforms. The panoramic extension and the demonstrated degeneration of the non-panoramic variant (Fig. 11) highlight that omnidirectional utility aggregation is not merely a speedup but a stability mechanism for coverage. Open questions include whether the local random candidate sampling can be replaced by learned or longer-horizon planners, and whether the renderability formulation generalizes beyond indoor Replica-scale scenes to outdoor or large-scale environments where voxel resolution and observation density change character.
