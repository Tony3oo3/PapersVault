## Problem

Off-the-shelf MLLMs perform poorly at few-shot image classification under standard in-context prompting, often performing worse than their own 0-shot baselines because they rely on semantic label priors rather than visual evidence from support examples. Additionally, standard few-shot benchmarks may be contaminated by pretraining overlap, making it difficult to assess whether models truly adapt to provided support images or simply recall memorized knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262258/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query image alongside N×K support images (one per candidate class per shot), with class labels either provided as semantic names or replaced by anonymized identifiers (e.g., 'Class 1'). Optionally, a short domain descriptor (e.g., 'bird species', 'yoga pose') is injected into the prompt. The output is a predicted class index, derived not from generated text but from the raw logit of the 'Yes' token in the model's output distribution, making the method sensitive to the model's internal confidence rather than its surface-level generation.

## Method

DeCoDe reformulates N-way K-shot classification as N×K independent binary pairwise comparisons: for each support image and the query, the MLLM is prompted to answer whether the two images depict the same class. The logit of the 'Yes' token is extracted as a pairwise similarity score, averaged across the K support examples per class, and the class with the highest mean score is selected as the prediction. A key ablation shows that the gains come from the support-query visual comparison itself rather than the binary prompt structure, since a 0-shot decomposed variant (asking 'Is this image depicting class c_n?') does not improve over standard 0-shot inference. Optionally, replacing the generic word 'class' with a dataset-specific domain term (e.g., 'aircraft variant', 'insect species') further improves accuracy by grounding the comparison in domain-level semantics. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring no parameter updates or fine-tuning of the MLLM.

## Results

On the 5-way 1-shot protocol, Qwen3-VL with DeCoDe + domain information achieves 90.6% total average top-1 accuracy (semantic labels) and 89.2% (anonymized labels), setting new state-of-the-art across 12 datasets. On the six novel out-of-domain datasets, the method reaches 85.2% average (semantic) vs. 57.0% for 0-shot Qwen3-VL, while the best FSL baseline (CAML) only reaches 66.3%. In the anonymized setting where in-context MLLMs collapse to near-random (~28%), DeCoDe restores performance to 89.2%, surpassing the SFT fine-tuning baseline (55.5%) by 33.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCoDe with Qwen3-VL (90.6% semantic / 89.2% anonymous total) outperforms all embedding-based FSL baselines by a wide margin: CAML (78.8%), SigLIP (74.5%), SAVs (76.6%), ProKeR (71.4%), and CLIP (60.9% / 64.9%). The advantage is most pronounced on the six novel out-of-domain datasets, where Qwen3-VL dec.+Dinfo reaches 85.2% vs. 66.3% for CAML and 57.4% for SigLIP. In the anonymized setting, in-context MLLMs collapse to near-random (23–28%), while DeCoDe restores 89.2% (Qwen3-VL), beating the SFT fine-tuning baseline (55.5%) by 33.7 points and the best embedding method SigLIP (78.8%) by 10.4 points. On standard benchmarks, 0-shot MLLMs already saturate (95%+), so the decomposition's main value is on novel domains and in the label-free regime. The SFT baseline (88.2% semantic) is the closest competitor, but DeCoDe+Dinfo still leads by 2.4 points without any parameter updates, and the gap widens to 33.7 points in the anonymous setting where SFT drops to 55.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of using MLLMs for few-shot classification either as embedding extractors (SAVs, VRL) or as in-context learners (GFSL), instead repurposing their binary decision capability: the model is asked a simple 'same class? Yes/No' question per support-query pair, and the Yes logit serves as a similarity score. Conceptually, it draws on the VQA-scoring idea from VQAScore [24] but applies it to image-image matching rather than image-text alignment. The evaluation protocol follows the concern raised by Kravets et al. [20] about pretraining overlap inflating standard benchmark scores, but addresses it through novel out-of-domain datasets rather than unlearning. The addition of dataset-level domain information (D_info) as a prompt element is a new prompting strategy not present in prior MLLM few-shot work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 1-shot in-context prompting often performs *worse* than 0-shot on standard datasets (e.g., InternVL3: 90.7% → 72.2%) challenges the assumption that providing support examples should monotonically help MLLMs, revealing that semantic priors can actively interfere with visual adaptation. The anonymized-label experiments further expose that MLLMs collapse to near-random accuracy without class names, confirming heavy reliance on textual knowledge rather than true visual comparison. Practical limitations include the O(N) forward passes required for N-way classification and the need for logit access, though the authors note parallelization mitigates this. Open questions include whether D_info can be reliably auto-inferred, whether the approach extends to multi-modal or video few-shot settings, and whether the gains persist at much larger N or K.
