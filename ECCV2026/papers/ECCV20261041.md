## Problem

Existing 3DGS compression methods compress scenes holistically, allocating uniform quality across all regions, which is inefficient for interactive applications where users focus on specific objects or regions of interest. While a few methods introduce layered representations for progressive compression, they still operate only at a global quality level and lack finer-grained, object-level, or region-aware adaptation. This paper addresses the gap by enabling recomposable, region-adaptive quality control over 3DGS representations without requiring retraining from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261041/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained 3DGS model (an unordered set of Gaussian primitives with position, covariance, opacity, and SH color attributes) and optionally a 2D click prompt on a reference rendered view to define a region of interest. It produces a region-aware layered hierarchy where each quality level is a cumulative submodel, and a truncatable compressed bitstream in which each layer is an independently decodable segment. Decoding only the first layer yields a low-quality full-scene preview in under 0.7 seconds, and subsequent layers incrementally refine fidelity with minimal additional latency.

## Method

The framework has two sequential components. RecastGS reorganizes the unordered 3DGS into a layered hierarchy via overcomplete layer search (constructing with L_over=16 layers then merging with stride k to L=8) and progressive distillation (a fine-to-coarse schedule from stage L-1 down to 1, where each student submodel is initialized from the previous stage and distilled against the full teacher model). A prompt-driven object extraction module uses SAM with multi-view voting to partition Gaussians into foreground and background subsets, enabling recomposition at arbitrary quality levels per region. LayeredCGS then compresses the layered representation feed-forward: positions are encoded with the lightweight RENO-GS point cloud codec, geometry attributes are quantized and losslessly compressed, and color attributes are entropy-coded via a GMM parameterized by a three-branch layer-wise context model (intra-Gaussian channel context, hyperprior, and inter-Gaussian spatial context built from a multi-resolution feature grid populated by previously decoded layers). This cross-layer conditioning exploits inter-layer correlations to reduce redundancy, achieving 35% BD-Rate gain over FCGS.

## Results

LayeredCGS achieves average BD-Rate reductions of −34.91% (PSNR), −38.42% (SSIM), and −34.83% (LPIPS) over the feed-forward baseline FCGS across Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks&Temples, and DeepBlending. With progressive distillation enabled (RecastGS-LayeredCGS), the method outperforms most per-scene optimized compression approaches, and when combined with the GaussianSpa sparsified backbone it achieves the lowest bitrate among all compared methods. For ROI-aware compression, region-adaptive quality allocation yields BD-Rate gains of up to −51.96% in ROI PSNR (garden scene) while incurring only 4.6–12.1% full-image PSNR degradation, and delivers up to +2.16 dB higher ROI PSNR at comparable bitrates versus uniform allocation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary feed-forward baseline FCGS [7], LayeredCGS wins decisively with ~35% BD-Rate improvement across all three datasets and all three metrics, attributable to the layered representation (30.07% gain), the layer-wise context model (+8.06%), and RENO-GS position coding (+1.25%). In the compaction setting, RecastGS outperforms GoDe [12] by 12.9–24.9% BD-Rate on vanilla 3DGS and by 61.6–71.0% when applied to GaussianSpa [73], surpassing other pruning baselines including MiniSplatting [15], MiniSplatting2 [16], MaskGaussian [40], RadSplat [50], PUP 3DGS [19], and LightGaussian [14]. In the full compression comparison (Fig. 5), RecastGS-LayeredCGS (GaussianSpa) achieves the lowest bitrate among all 22 compared methods, beating per-scene optimized approaches such as EAGLES [17], HEMGS [38], RDO-Gaussian [60], CodecGS [35], SOG [46], HAC/HAC++ [8,9], ContextGS [61], CompGS [39], Compressed3D [49], Compact3DGS [34], Compact3D [48], and MesonGS [64]. The paper does not report any comparison where the proposed method loses; the only trade-off is the modest full-image PSNR degradation (4.6–12.1%) incurred when allocating extra bits to ROI regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GoDe's gradient-based layered representation for progressive 3DGS compression and FCGS's feed-forward entropy-coding framework, extending both with targeted modifications: overcomplete layer search and progressive distillation replace GoDe's naive layer partitioning and random-level finetuning, while a layer-wise context model and RENO-GS geometry codec augment FCGS's holistic compression. The key conceptual departure is coupling the layered hierarchy with SAM-driven object segmentation to enable region-adaptive quality allocation, a capability absent from prior progressive 3DGS methods (GoDe, RAVE, HGSC, PCGS) that only support global quality control. The recomposition mechanism (Eq. 8) that independently selects quality levels for foreground and background subsets is the paper's most novel structural contribution, bridging 3DGS compression with the broader ROI-coding literature from image and point cloud coding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling low-latency preview (full-scene render within ~0.7 s) and progressive quality refinement for bandwidth-constrained streaming of 3DGS scenes, with the added ability to prioritize user-specified objects without retraining. The 35% BD-Rate gain over FCGS and competitive performance against per-scene optimized methods make feed-forward compression more viable for deployment. However, the ROI study is limited to binary foreground/background allocation on three scenes, and the authors acknowledge that multi-object preferences, thin structures, and severe occlusions remain unaddressed. Extending the framework to anchor-based (Scaffold-GS) and pixel-aligned 3DGS formats is identified as necessary future work, as the current method is tied to the vanilla unordered Gaussian format.
