## Problem

Existing jigsaw puzzle solvers operate on raw pixels or continuous visual embeddings, coupling local appearance with global structural reasoning and making it impossible to determine whether reconstruction succeeds due to visual cues or higher-level combinatorial patterns. This paper asks whether accurate square jigsaw reassembly can be achieved through purely symbolic reasoning over discrete token representations, completely decoupling structural inference from direct visual access.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260632/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of N = n² square RGB puzzle pieces (each H×W×C) and produces a permutation assigning each piece to a unique grid position in {0, …, N−1}. Before the solver sees any data, each piece is converted into a super-token of b = max(1, 4B−4) discrete integers (border patches in clockwise order), and the full puzzle becomes a single sequence of length bN + (N−1) with [SEP] separators. The output is generated autoregressively, one grid position at a time, rather than as a simultaneous all-pieces assignment.

## Method

PuzLM recasts jigsaw reassembly as a discrete sequence-to-sequence prediction problem. A lightweight unsupervised tokenizer divides each piece into a B×B patch grid, projects patches to a lower-dimensional space via PCA, assigns each patch to its nearest k-means centroid (defining the vocabulary), and retains only the b border patches in clockwise order to form a piece-aligned super-token. These super-tokens are concatenated (with [SEP] separators and lexicographic ordering) into a single input sequence. A standard encoder-decoder transformer (BART, T5, Pegasus, or RNN) then autoregressively predicts the N-position permutation, trained with cross-entropy loss analogous to NLP exact-match. The key distinction from prior work is that the solver has no access to pixel values or continuous visual features during inference; all compatibility reasoning must emerge from statistical regularities in token co-occurrence over the fixed discrete vocabulary.

## Results

On ImageNet 3×3, PuzLM achieves 92.2% absolute and 87.1% perfect accuracy, surpassing the previous best (FCViT: 90.6%/78.9%) by +1.6% and +8.2% respectively. On the more challenging JPwLEG-5 benchmark (5×5 puzzles with eroded boundaries), PuzLM sets a new SOTA with 72.1% absolute and 32.5% perfect accuracy, outperforming the second-best method (VLHSA: 66.9%/19.0%) by +5.2% and +13.5%. On JPwLEG-3, PuzLM reaches 91.9%/84.5%, slightly behind FCViT (96.9%/87.9%) but ahead of all other solvers. With missing pieces (1–3 out of 9), PuzLM consistently outperforms JPDVT by 10–14% in absolute accuracy across all difficulty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PuzLM outperforms all vision-based baselines on ImageNet 3×3, beating FCViT (90.6%/78.9%) by +1.6%/+8.2% in absolute/perfect accuracy, and Deepzzle (78.6%/48.5%) by a wide margin. On JPwLEG-3, FCViT remains the strongest solver (96.9%/87.9%), with PuzLM second (91.9%/84.5%), but PuzLM surpasses all methods specifically designed for eroded gaps (SD2RL, PDN-GA, ERL-MPP) by 10+ points in absolute accuracy. The most dramatic gain appears on JPwLEG-5, where PuzLM (72.1%/32.5%) beats VLHSA (66.9%/19.0%) by +5.2%/+13.5% and FCViT (43.2%/2.5%) by +28.9%/+30.0%, indicating that symbolic tokenization scales better to larger puzzles than pixel-based ViT features. For missing-piece puzzles, PuzLM exceeds JPDVT by 14.0%/29.8% (1 missing), 12.0%/23.7% (2 missing), and 7.1%/8.8% (3 missing) in absolute/perfect accuracy. In the tokenizer ablation, PuzLM's lightweight k-means+PCA tokenizer (84.5% perfect on JPwLEG-3) dramatically outperforms VQ-VAE (12.0%) and TiTok-L (20.0%) while using <1M parameters vs. 55M–641M and <1ms vs. 4–29ms encoding time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in jigsaw puzzle solving, where prior methods (CFN, Deepzzle, JPDVT, FCViT, and others) operate on continuous visual features extracted from raw pixels or learned embeddings. Instead, it repurposes the Seq2Seq framework from NLP—drawing on encoder-decoder transformers like BART, T5, and Pegasus—and adapts discrete tokenization concepts from VQ-VAE and TiTok, but deliberately redesigns the tokenizer to preserve spatial border structure rather than visual fidelity. The conceptual lineage is thus a cross-pollination: NLP sequence modeling meets a combinatorial vision task, with the key departure being that the solver never accesses pixel values directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that purely symbolic reasoning over discrete border tokens can match or exceed vision-based SOTA on square jigsaw puzzles, including challenging settings with eroded boundaries and missing pieces, suggesting that structural regularities in piece borders carry sufficient information for reconstruction without any continuous visual feature. The tokenizer's extreme lightweightness (<1M parameters, <1ms encoding) compared to deep alternatives (55M–641M parameters) opens the door to efficient, deployable solvers. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-square or irregularly shaped puzzles, whether it scales to much larger grids (beyond 5×5), and whether the discrete representation can be extended to capture interior content for puzzles where border information alone is insufficient.
