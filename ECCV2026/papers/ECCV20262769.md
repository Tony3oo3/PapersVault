## Problem

VLMs remain susceptible to hallucination and unreliable reasoning, yet existing self-correction methods rely on costly post-training (RL, preference fine-tuning) or carefully engineered feedback, limiting scalability. The paper asks whether emotional cues can activate latent self-correction behaviors in VLMs at inference time without any additional training, drawing on the observation that humans revise responses when emotional signals prompt them to slow down and reflect.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262769/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text question as input. A target VLM first generates an initial response; a separate verifier VLM then assesses whether revision is warranted. If so, a short emotional feedback string (drawn from the negative-low-arousal quadrant of Russell's Circumplex Model) is prepended before the original query, and the target VLM produces a revised response. The verifier finally selects between the original and revised outputs, returning the chosen text response. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play and requires no weight modification.

## Method

ESC is a training-free, inference-time self-correction framework built around two VLMs: a target model M_T that generates and revises responses, and a separate verifier M_V that gates the correction step. The central mechanism is emotional feedback injection: when the verifier flags the initial response as unreliable, a negative-low-arousal emotional cue is prepended to the query, inducing a 'cognitive reframing' that shifts the model toward more cautious, detail-oriented reasoning. Controlled ablations show this emotional signal outperforms generic corrective prompts, zero-shot CoT, and psychological prompting by over 17 percentage points on ASR, and it measurably increases cautiousness in the model's thinking traces. The selective verification stage avoids unnecessary revision when the initial answer is already correct, reducing computational overhead while preserving strong baselines. Even small 3–4B verifiers achieve performance close to the default 12B verifier, confirming that the gain originates from the emotional feedback mechanism rather than verifier capacity.

## Results

On VLSafe, ESC reduces attack success rate (ASR) from 71.6% to 31.2% for LLaVA-1.5-7B (−40.4 pp) and from 20.0% to 9.9% for Qwen2-VL-7B (−10.1 pp), outperforming all alternative feedback strategies. On hallucination benchmarks, ESC improves POPE Adversarial accuracy from 83.47% to 84.53% (LLaVA) and from 50.80% to 80.43% (Qwen2), while also gaining +1.06 on HallusionBench answer accuracy. For multimodal reasoning, ESC yields consistent gains across five benchmarks (e.g., +1.20 on MathVista, +2.49 on AI2D for LLaVA), and for vision-centric perception, it achieves +7.33 on MMVP pair accuracy (LLaVA) and +14.51 on RealWorldQA (Qwen2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESC is compared against two named baselines and two additional prompting strategies on VLSafe. Against ZeroCoT (Kojima et al., 'Let's think step by step'), ESC reduces ASR from 70.1% to 31.2%, a 38.9-percentage-point advantage, showing that generic chain-of-thought prompting is nearly ineffective for safety correction. Against the psychological prompting baseline (Li et al., 'Large language models understand and can be enhanced by emotional stimuli'), ESC achieves 31.2% vs. 54.4% ASR, a 23.2-point margin, demonstrating that structured emotional feedback grounded in the circumplex model substantially outperforms unstructured tone-shifting. ESC also surpasses Self-Refine (49.3%) and generic corrective prompting (48.6%) by over 17 points, confirming that the emotional content itself—not merely the act of revision—is the primary driver of the corrective effect. The verifier-only ablation (69.1%) further confirms that the verify-revise loop alone contributes little without the emotional signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two literatures: inference-time self-correction in VLMs (which has been pursued via RL-based training, supervised fine-tuning on reflective trajectories, and intrinsic self-correction) and emotional intelligence in generative models (where emotion has been treated as a recognition or expression capability). Rather than building on a specific prior self-correction method, ESC repurposes a psychological construct—Russell's Circumplex Model of affect—as an inference-time control signal, departing from the dominant paradigm of corrective or preference-based feedback. The external-verifier design directly addresses the self-correction blind spot identified by Huang et al. and Tsui, where models struggle to correct their own errors, by offloading the detection step to a separate model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESC offers a lightweight, training-free, plug-and-play mechanism that consistently improves VLM reliability across safety, hallucination, perception, and reasoning benchmarks without modifying model weights, which is practically attractive for deployment in high-stakes settings. The finding that negative-low-arousal emotional cues outperform generic corrective instructions by over 17 percentage points suggests that the *affective framing* of feedback, not just its informational content, is a meaningful lever for steering model behavior. Open questions include whether the effect persists at larger model scales (the primary ablations use 7B targets), how the verifier cost scales in production, and whether the emotional signal can be further optimized or combined with other inference-time interventions. The mechanism remains somewhat opaque—why negative affect specifically triggers more careful reasoning in a transformer is not explained at a representational level.
