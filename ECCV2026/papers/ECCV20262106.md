## Problem

Converting IC footprint diagrams in datasheets into machine-readable geometric parameters (pin count, coordinates, dimensions) remains a manual bottleneck in PCB design. Existing automation methods (OCR, object detection) cannot resolve implicit geometric relationships, and the prior end-to-end LMM approach (LLM4-IC8K) treats labeling as a black box, making it prone to shortcut learning and lacking the interpretability that expert engineers rely on. No prior work had applied a multi-agent reasoning framework to this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262106/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a full IC datasheet PDF page image as input (not a pre-cropped diagram), so the first agent must locate the relevant footprint region within a page that may contain multiple diagrams and text. Output is a structured set of standardized key parameters (e.g., row spacing, column spacing, pin width/height) organized by IC footprint type (2-sides, 4-sides, grid, other), which are then passed to type-specific Python scripts that compute final pin coordinates and dimensions. For 'other' (irregular) footprints, pin descriptions are generated directly by the agents without a generation tool.

## Method

ICLAgent decomposes the labeling workflow into four stages handled by three task-specific agents, each a Qwen2-VL-7B model fine-tuned with chain-of-thought supervision on a dedicated subset of the newly constructed ICAgent-INSTRUCT dataset. The Diagram Agent fuses YOLOv12 object-detection candidates with its own LMM-based region judgment to localize the footprint diagram; the Planning Agent classifies the footprint into one of four arrangement types and generates a type-specific checklist of key parameters, including reasoning instructions for deriving implicit values (e.g., computing center-to-center distance from outer and inner edge measurements); the Parameter Agent then extracts and computes those parameters from the diagram. Training is sequential supervised fine-tuning (5 hours on 2 A100 GPUs), and the key design insight is that decomposing the task into interpretable, expert-mirroring steps with domain-prior checklists avoids the hallucination and shortcut-learning failures of end-to-end LMM fine-tuning, yielding a 10.3% IoU improvement over the previous SOTA.

## Results

On the ICGEOQA benchmark (400 real-world IC datasheet pages), ICLAgent achieves an overall IoU_IC of 79.0±0.2%, surpassing the previous SOTA method LLM4-IC8K (71.6±0.5%) by 10.3% and the manual industry baseline (44%) by 79.5%. ICLAgent also leads on pin coordinate precision (d_pin = 1.07±0.03) and pin dimension accuracy (IoU_pin = 95.6±0.2%), while showing a negligible gap in pin-count MAE (0.37 vs. 0.35 for LLM4-IC8K). The framework uses only supervised fine-tuning on Qwen2-VL-7B and processes each datasheet page in ~13.5 seconds on 2×A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICLAgent outperforms the previous task-specific SOTA, LLM4-IC8K, by 10.3% in overall IoU_IC (79.0% vs. 71.6%) and by 7.6 percentage points in pin dimension accuracy (IoU_pin 95.6% vs. 88.0%), though LLM4-IC8K retains a slight edge in pin-count MAE (0.35 vs. 0.37) and RMSE (2.81 vs. 3.25). Against general-purpose LMMs, ICLAgent leads Gemini 3 Pro by 15.2% in IoU_IC (79.0% vs. 68.6%) and GPT-5 by 94.6% (79.0% vs. 40.6%); GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4, and Qwen3-VL-235B all score below 12% IoU_IC, indicating they essentially fail the task. The manual industry baseline (44% IoU_IC) is exceeded by 79.5%, confirming that ICLAgent's geometric interpretation is more precise than human EDA operators. Notably, ICLAgent's advantage over LLM4-IC8K is driven primarily by superior coordinate and dimension estimation rather than pin counting, reflecting the benefit of decomposing the task into explicit parameter extraction rather than end-to-end generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the end-to-end LMM approach of LLM4-IC8K, which treats IC footprint geometry labeling as a single black-box generation task, by decomposing it into a four-stage multi-agent pipeline that mirrors the step-by-step reasoning of expert PCB engineers. Conceptually, it repurposes the multi-agent LMM paradigm (as seen in MetaGPT, AutoGen, and medical diagnosis agents) to a previously unaddressed EDA subtask, combining a YOLOv12 object-detection tool with three SFT-fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B agents for diagram localization, type-specific planning, and parameter extraction. The ICAgent-INSTRUCT dataset is the first to encode expert-level planning and geometric reasoning strategies as training signals for this domain, rather than only end-to-end input-output pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 7B SFT model with structured multi-agent decomposition outperforms frontier general-purpose LMMs (GPT-5, Gemini 3 Pro) by large margins suggests that for narrow, procedure-heavy document-understanding tasks, explicit workflow modeling and domain-specific reasoning data can substitute for raw model scale. The framework's interpretability—each stage produces inspectable intermediate outputs—addresses a key barrier to industrial adoption of LMM-based EDA tools. Open questions include whether the hardcoded domain priors (four footprint categories, type-specific checklists) generalize to other technical drawing domains, whether RL or ReAct-style feedback loops can close the remaining gap on irregular 'other' footprints, and whether the approach scales to the 13M+ products in commercial libraries without further data curation.
