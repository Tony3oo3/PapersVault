## Problem

Existing mixup-based augmentation methods rely on cross-sample mixing, which disrupts semantic integrity and incurs significant computational overhead. Saliency-guided variants mitigate semantic loss but still transfer regions between different images, introducing label inconsistencies. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a self-contained, single-image augmentation that preserves labels while promoting scale-invariant and structurally diverse feature learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260326/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (with its one-hot label) and a pre-constructed library of fractal patterns, producing an augmented version of the same image with the unchanged label. Patches are extracted at two fixed scales (h/2×w/2 and h/4×w/4) from the input, so no second image is ever required. The output is a full-resolution blended image where transformed, fractal-enriched patches are composited into non-salient regions, and the label is always identical to the source (no label interpolation).

## Method

S²-FracMix operates entirely within a single image in two stages. First, the S² component computes a spectral-residual saliency map, extracts multi-scale patches, accepts only those whose salient-area ratio exceeds a random threshold, then applies selective transformation (random rotation on salient pixels, Gaussian blur on non-salient pixels) before resizing and compositing the patches back into non-salient regions of the original image. Second, FracMix blends a randomly sampled fractal pattern into each accepted patch using a fixed coefficient λ=0.20, confining fractal injection to salient content rather than the whole image. At the high level, the framework randomly selects among S²-FracMix, Mixup, CutMix, and ResizeMix for each training instance to diversify supervisory signals. A second-order Taylor expansion under vicinal risk minimization shows the method adds a saliency-local stability penalty that suppresses curvature-weighted sensitivity to localized perturbations, explaining the observed gains in corruption and adversarial robustness.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, S²-FracMix achieves 82.74% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline AdAutoMix (82.32%) by 0.42% and the vanilla model (78.04%) by 4.70%. On Tiny-ImageNet (ResNet-18), it reaches 70.38%, a 1.19% gain over AdAutoMix (69.19%). On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50, S²-FracMix attains 78.54% top-1 accuracy (+0.50% over AdAutoMix), and with ViT-B it reaches 81.2%. In robustness evaluation on CIFAR-100-C, it achieves 53.84% corruption accuracy and 72.52% FGSM error, outperforming AdAutoMix by 2.40% and 3.14% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²-FracMix consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and backbone tested. Against the strongest competitor AdAutoMix [49], gains range from +0.42% (CIFAR-100 Res18) to +1.38% (Tiny-ImageNet ResXt50), with the largest relative improvements on smaller datasets. Compared to AutoMix [42], the gap widens to +0.70% on CIFAR-100 Res18 and +3.05% on Tiny-ImageNet Res18, indicating that the self-saliency and fractal mechanisms add value beyond learned mixing strategies. PuzzleMix [31] and SaliencyMix [53], which also use saliency but rely on cross-image mixing, trail by 1.61% and 3.62% respectively on CIFAR-100 Res18, confirming the advantage of operating within a single image. Among the simpler baselines, ResizeMix [51] and CutMix [64] are surpassed by 2.73% and 4.57% on CIFAR-100 Res18, while MixUp [65] and FMix [16] lag by 3.62% and 3.05%. In the robustness setting (Table 4), S²-FracMix reduces FGSM error to 72.52% versus 75.66% for AdAutoMix and 80.52% for PuzzleMix, and achieves 53.84% corruption accuracy versus 51.44% for AdAutoMix, demonstrating that the gains extend beyond clean accuracy to adversarial and corruption robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²-FracMix sits at the intersection of two threads in mixup-based augmentation: saliency-guided mixing (SaliencyMix, PuzzleMix, Co-Mixup, GuidedMixup) and fractal-based augmentation (DiffuseMix, IPMix). Its key conceptual departure is the shift from cross-sample mixing to self-mixing within a single image, using spectral residual saliency [22] to identify and transform informative patches, thereby avoiding the semantic disruption that plagues inter-image methods. The FracMix component further differentiates by confining fractal injection to salient regions rather than diffusing it globally, and the high-level stochastic mixing of multiple low-level modes (Mixup, CutMix, ResizeMix) alongside S²-FracMix is a practical design choice not present in prior single-mode approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a theoretically grounded (via vicinal risk minimization) and computationally efficient augmentation strategy that consistently outperforms strong baselines like AdAutoMix across seven benchmarks, with particularly notable gains in robustness (corruption and FGSM) over clean accuracy. This suggests that targeted, saliency-local regularization is more effective than uniform perturbation for building robust features. Open questions include whether the relatively simple spectral residual saliency can be replaced by learned or model-specific saliency for further gains, whether the fixed fractal library limits expressiveness at scale, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to video, multimodal, or generative-model-based augmentation pipelines. The paper also notes limitations in Appendix D, including dependence on the saliency threshold and fractal blending factor.
