## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks evaluate retrieval and reasoning in isolation, typically over small single-modality candidate pools (hundreds to a few thousand items) with ambiguous or non-unique evidence grounding. This substantially understates the difficulty of real-world scenarios where a model must locate a single relevant item among tens of thousands of heterogeneous documents, images, and videos before reasoning. MultiHaystack addresses this by providing the first benchmark that jointly evaluates cross-modal retrieval and grounded reasoning over a 46K+ item corpus with strictly unique evidence per question.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260648/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a text question and a heterogeneous corpus of 46,260 items (25,652 images, 10,419 videos, 10,189 documents) as input. Videos are uniformly sampled into 8 frames and documents are rendered page-by-page into sequential images for unified image-based retrieval scoring. Output is evaluated at two decoupled stages: retrieval correctness is measured at the item level (Recall@1/3/5, where the entire document or video must appear in top-k), and reasoning accuracy is assessed by GPT-4o-mini as an automatic judge given the top-k retrieved items as evidence.

## Method

MultiHaystack is constructed via a four-stage pipeline: (1) data collection from diverse sources across images (DocHaystack, MMIU, A-OKVQA), videos (Video-Vista, MMBench-Video, FineVideo, MVBench), and documents (MINT1T); (2) question generation where GPT-4o produces ~30 candidate QA pairs per item from its image-based representation; (3) multi-step filtering using GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Flash to remove ambiguous questions, manual review to discard unanchored items, and a retrieval-independence test to eliminate questions solvable without the supporting item, yielding 747 final questions; (4) data enrichment where GPT-4o-generated keywords drive web scraping to construct ~46K semantically plausible distractors filtered by CLIP similarity and vidore/colqwen2-v0.1 scores. Each question is grounded in exactly one evidence item, enabling decoupled evaluation of retrieval (using VLMs like CLIP, SigLIP2, E5-V, MM-Embed) and reasoning (using MLLMs like GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Qwen2-VL) across six task types spanning visual parsing, contextual understanding, video temporal reasoning, statistical reasoning, metadata identification, and factual knowledge retrieval.

## Results

On MultiHaystack's 46K+ cross-modal retrieval pool, the strongest retrievers E5-V and SigLIP2 achieve only 40.83% and 40.96% Recall@1 respectively, a drop of over 40 points from their single-modality performance (e.g., SigLIP2 reaches 91.43% R@5 on videos in single-modality). For reasoning, GPT-5 drops from 80.86% accuracy with gold evidence to 51.41% under top-5 cross-modal retrieval, while Gemini-2.5-Flash falls from 61.90% to 43.64%. Open-source MLLMs (Ola, InternVL-3, Qwen2-VL) remain below 35% overall accuracy even with single-modality retrieval, confirming that cross-modal retrieval over heterogeneous pools is the dominant bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among retrieval models, E5-V and SigLIP2 are the strongest cross-modal retrievers (40.83% and 40.96% R@1), while CLIP (26.10%) and NEV (9.24%) degrade substantially, with NEV performing near chance level on images (5.31% R@1). For reasoning, GPT-5 leads at 51.41% overall accuracy under cross-modal top-5 retrieval, followed by Gemini-2.5-Flash (43.64%), while open-source models Ola (23.83%), InternVL-3 (33.29%), and Qwen2-VL (17.54%) all fall well below 35%. Advanced retrieval pipelines (E5-V+MMSearch at 44.58% R@1, VisRAG at 45.38% R@1) yield only modest 3–5 point gains over vanilla E5-V, remaining far below the single-modality upper bound of 58.87%. The paper's central finding is that no current system closes the gap between single-modality and cross-modality performance, with the cross-modal setting consistently 15–40 points lower across all models and modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiHaystack directly extends DocHaystack [5], which established the needle-in-a-haystack paradigm for document-only retrieval, by generalizing the candidate pool to a unified corpus of 46K+ items spanning documents, images, and videos. It also departs from other retrieval-oriented benchmarks (WebQA, RetVQA, MM-NIAH, MMNeedle) that restrict retrieval to small, single-modality pools or permit ambiguous multi-target evidence. The construction pipeline reuses the four-stage design (collection, QA generation, filtering, enrichment) from DocHaystack but adds cross-modal hard-negative mining via keyword-based web scraping and CLIP/vidore scoring to scale the distractor pool realistically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that cross-modal retrieval over heterogeneous pools is the dominant bottleneck for MLLMs, a limitation masked by prior small-scale, single-modality evaluations. The controlled pool-size experiment (Fig. 7) showing that the performance gap stems from modality heterogeneity rather than sheer candidate count is a useful conceptual clarification for the field. Open questions include how to build modality-aware embedding spaces that preserve fine-grained spatial and temporal structure, how to tightly couple retrieval and reasoning with intermediate feedback, and whether agentic retrieval pipelines (MMSearch, VisRAG) can close the gap at scale. The benchmark is likely to become a standard diagnostic tool for multimodal RAG systems.
