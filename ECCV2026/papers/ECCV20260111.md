## Problem

Existing panoramic object detectors are confined to a closed-set paradigm and cannot recognize novel categories. Extending open-vocabulary detection to 360° images is uniquely difficult because prompt–image alignment is simultaneously weakened by semantic ambiguity in language-only prompts (e.g., CLIP bias toward contextually co-occurring objects) and by severe geometric distortion from equirectangular projection, which corrupts image features especially for unseen classes that lack learned distorted-appearance priors.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 512×1024 equirectangular panoramic images together with per-category multimodal prompts: 10 diverse text descriptions (generated offline via DeepSeek and encoded by frozen CLIP text encoder) and 5 visual exemplar images (retrieved from ImageNet-21K and encoded by frozen CLIP image encoder). It outputs BFoV (horizontal) or RBFoV (rotated) bounding-field-of-view boxes with open-vocabulary class labels, evaluated under Sphere-IoU at 0.5. Training uses only base-category annotations (30 base / 7 novel on 360-Indoor; 40 base / 7 novel on PANDORA), and novel categories are detected zero-shot at inference.

## Method

OpenPanoD co-designs two alignment sides. On the prompt side, a multimodal prompt encoder averages CLIP text embeddings over 10 diverse phrases and CLIP image embeddings over 5 exemplars per category, then projects them through separate two-layer MLPs into a shared 256-dim detection space; at classification time the maximum cosine similarity over all prompt variants (text and/or vision) is taken. On the image side, GeoFormer first maps ERP features onto a quasi-uniform icosahedral grid via bilinear sampling with horizontal circular padding (preserving wrap-around continuity), injects 3D Cartesian positional encodings, and then applies stacked spherical Transformer encoder layers that perform global multi-head attention over the spherical tokens, reducing projection-induced distortion and boundary discontinuity. A cross-modal decoder fuses the two: prompt-conditioned object queries (averaged category prompt plus learnable instance offsets) attend to the spherical image features via multi-head cross-attention, followed by self-attention among queries. The final detection head uses decoupled regression (3-layer MLP for BFoV/RBFoV coordinates) and classification (linear projection to 256-dim) branches, trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of Focal Loss, a supervised contrastive loss that pulls matched detection embeddings toward positive-class prompts and away from negatives, and a box loss combining L1 and Gaussian Label Distribution Learning.

## Results

On 360-Indoor (BFoV), OpenPanoD with multimodal prompts achieves 33.9% mAP_novel and 34.6 HM, outperforming the strongest text-prompt baseline Prompt-DINO (26.8% mAP_novel) by 7.1 points. On PANDORA (RBFoV), the multimodal variant reaches 31.3% mAP_novel and 32.8 HM, improving over Prompt-DINO (25.5% mAP_novel) by 5.8 points. Even in the text-only setting, OpenPanoD leads all text-prompt baselines by 2.0–3.0 mAP_novel on both datasets, and in the visual-only setting it surpasses DINOv by 4.8 mAP_novel on 360-Indoor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenPanoD outperforms all nine baselines across both datasets and all three prompt settings. Among text-prompt methods, Prompt-DINO is the strongest baseline (26.8% mAP_novel on 360-Indoor, 25.5% on PANDORA); OpenPanoD's text-only variant exceeds it by 3.0 and 3.2 mAP_novel respectively, while the multimodal variant widens the gap to 7.1 and 5.8 points. Among visual-prompt methods, DINOv leads (20.7% mAP_novel on 360-Indoor, 18.9% on PANDORA); OpenPanoD's visual-only variant surpasses it by 4.8 and 6.0 mAP_novel. T-Rex2, evaluated only on 360-Indoor due to its horizontal-box limitation, trails OpenPanoD's visual-only variant by 5.9 mAP_novel. The consistent gains across prompt types indicate that the spherical GeoFormer encoder contributes independently of the prompt modality, while the multimodal combination yields the largest absolute improvement over any single-modality baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: closed-set panoramic object detection (SphereNet, SpherePHD, PanoSwin, SphereUFormer) and planar open-vocabulary detection (GLIP, GroundingDINO, YOLO-World, Prompt-DINO). It repurposes the multimodal prompting paradigm from planar OVD (T-Rex2, DINOv) and the spherical-geometry encoder idea from panoramic detection, but is the first to co-design both for the combined task. GeoFormer itself is a new architecture, but its core mechanism—global attention over icosahedral-grid tokens—follows the spherical Transformer lineage (SphereUFormer, PanoSwin) while dropping local-attention restrictions to enable full long-range interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first open-vocabulary panoramic detection benchmark results, the paper opens a practical path toward 360° scene understanding that is not limited to a fixed category set, which is important for VR, autonomous driving, and surveillance. The ablations confirm that both multimodal prompting and spherical geometry contribute independently, but the gains from GeoFormer over a plain ViT (+1.7 mAP_novel) and over SphereUFormer (+1.1) are modest, suggesting the spherical-attention design space may still have room for improvement. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor or driving panoramas, whether the icosahedral grid resolution can be increased without prohibitive compute cost, and whether the framework extends naturally to 360° segmentation or video.
