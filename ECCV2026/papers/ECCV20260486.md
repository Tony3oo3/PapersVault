## Problem

4D Gaussian Splatting produces artifacts in spatiotemporal regions that are sparsely observed, especially under large motion where fast-moving objects are visible from few viewpoints for only brief moments. Existing generative-refinement approaches compensate for this by refining rendered images from virtual viewpoints, but they select those viewpoints via heuristics (interpolation, uniform sampling) that do not actively target the under-observed regions. The open problem is how to actively select spatiotemporal virtual viewpoints whose generative refinement most effectively alleviates observation sparsity in dynamic scenes.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a sparse multi-view video (as few as two synchronized cameras) with known camera poses and timestamps, and iteratively produces a refined 4DGS model capable of rendering novel views from both interpolation and extrapolation viewpoints. During each refinement iteration, the method renders images at selected virtual spatiotemporal viewpoints, passes them through a video diffusion model conditioned on adjacent observed frames and camera parameters, and uses the refined outputs as weighted pseudo-observations for fine-tuning. The evaluation protocol uses new train/test splits where test cameras are spatially separated from the two training cameras, testing both interpolation (between) and extrapolation (beyond) generalization.

## Method

FillGS performs active spatiotemporal virtual-viewpoint selection in two stages. First, a sensitivity-based score E(v) = L(v) + V(v) identifies observed viewpoints where the trained 4DGS is locally unstable (high reconstruction error plus large perturbation-induced rendering variation), serving as exploration starting points. Second, candidate virtual viewpoints are generated via local perturbation and global bounding-box sampling, then scored by rendering a motion-aware observation-deficiency map D_gi(t) = s_gi(t)/(1+C_gi(t)) per 4D Gaussian (velocity magnitude divided by observation count), so that viewpoints observing many fast, under-constrained Gaussians receive higher priority. Selected viewpoints are rendered and refined by a video diffusion model (extended from 3DGS-Enhancer to handle spatiotemporal patches of 3 frames × 4 views). Finally, 4DGS is fine-tuned using the refined images with two adaptive masks: a RoMa v2 matching-confidence consistency mask that suppresses pixels conflicting with real observations, and a motion-aware co-visibility mask that down-weights already well-observed regions, ensuring improvements concentrate on under-constrained areas without degrading well-reconstructed geometry.

## Results

On the Neural 3D Video dataset (2-camera sparse split), FillGS achieves PSNR 21.73 / SSIM 0.792 / LPIPS 0.256 / FID 83.81 / DINOv2 0.920 under interpolation and PSNR 16.24 / SSIM 0.740 / LPIPS 0.312 / FID 137.32 / DINOv2 0.855 under extrapolation, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On Technicolor, the gains are even larger: PSNR 22.55 (interpolation) and 19.88 (extrapolation), with FID dropping to 73.44 and 106.45 respectively. The most striking improvement is in FID, where FillGS reduces the score by 30–45% relative to the next-best method, indicating substantially better perceptual and distributional fidelity in sparsely observed regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FillGS outperforms all five baselines on every metric in both interpolation and extrapolation settings across both datasets. Against the no-generated-images baseline E-D3DGS [2], FillGS gains +1.31 dB PSNR (interpolation) and +1.56 dB (extrapolation) on Neural 3D Video, with FID dropping from 129.33 to 83.81. The strongest competing baseline is FisherRF-based selection [11], which achieves the second-best PSNR (21.01 interp., 15.59 extrap. on Neural 3D Video); FillGS still leads by +0.72 and +0.65 dB respectively, and by a large margin on FID (83.81 vs. 135.36). Coverage-based selection from ExploreGS [16] (both 3D and 4D variants) underperforms FillGS by 1.5–2.6 dB PSNR, confirming that purely spatial-coverage heuristics fail to target spatiotemporally sparse regions. On Technicolor, the gap widens: FillGS leads the best baseline (Coverage-3D) by +2.43 dB PSNR in interpolation, and leads FisherRF by +1.56 dB in extrapolation, while FID is essentially tied with Coverage-4D (106.45 vs. 106.47) but far ahead of all others.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of generative-refinement approaches for 4DGS (UA-4DGS, GS-GS, 3DGS-Enhancer) that use diffusion models to repair rendered views, but departs from their reliance on heuristic virtual-viewpoint selection (random sampling, interpolation between training views). It also generalizes ExploreGS's spatial-coverage-based active view selection from static 3DGS to the dynamic 4D setting, replacing a static coverage map with a motion-aware observation-deficiency score that accounts for per-Gaussian velocity and observation count. The robust fine-tuning strategy (consistency mask via RoMa v2 matching + motion-aware co-visibility weighting) is a novel combination not present in prior generative-refinement pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 4DGS deployment: real-world captures rarely provide dense spatiotemporal coverage, and fast-moving objects are especially underconstrained. The motion-aware observation density offers an interpretable, computable signal for prioritizing where generative refinement will help most, and the new sparse-view benchmark splits (two training cameras, interpolation + extrapolation test sets) give the community a more realistic evaluation protocol than standard dense-rig splits. Key limitations remain: the method is bounded by both the 4DGS representational capacity and the diffusion model's generation quality, and the sampling range must be carefully constrained to avoid wasting compute on views the generator cannot reliably produce. An open question is whether the approach transfers to more complex, non-rigid motions or to monocular settings where observation density is even sparser.
