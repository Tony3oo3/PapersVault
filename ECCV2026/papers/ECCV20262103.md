## Problem

Discrete visual representations face a fundamental tension: reconstruction-oriented tokenizers preserve low-level details but lack semantic structure, while semantically rich encoders suffer severe information loss when quantized into discrete codes. Existing quantized multimodal encoders (e.g., QLIP, UniTok) exhibit large performance gaps compared to continuous encoders, particularly on detail-intensive tasks, and none support native-resolution inputs, limiting their utility as a unified visual-language representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262103/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ViQ accepts RGB images at arbitrary native resolutions and aspect ratios (no fixed input size), along with text queries for multimodal understanding. It outputs a sequence of discrete visual tokens drawn from a 64,000-entry codebook, where each original image patch is expanded into 2×2 independently quantized sub-tokens carrying 2D rotary position information. These tokens can be projected into an LLM for understanding or decoded back into images; in storage form, each code is a 16-bit unsigned integer, compressing an (H,W) image to HW/32 bytes (1/96 of raw size).

## Method

ViQ uses a two-stage pipeline built on SigLIP2-g. Stage 1 performs text-aligned pre-training at any resolution (via NaViT-style positional embeddings and OryxViT efficiency techniques), supervised by a temporary LLM cross-entropy loss plus self-distillation from a fixed-resolution teacher to preserve semantic tokens. Stage 2 progressively discretizes features through a proximal representation: a bottleneck compresses 1536-dim features to 128-dim, an L∞-norm constraint projects them onto a hypercube surface, and a second bottleneck reduces to 6-dim before FSQ quantization (levels [8,8,8,5,5,5]). A multi-head mechanism expands each patch into 2×2 sub-tokens that are quantized independently, and 2D RoPE injects spatial position before quantization to support arbitrary resolutions. Training combines text loss, self-distillation loss, and a VAE-latent reconstruction loss (predicting frozen Qwen-Image encoder features) to jointly preserve semantics and low-level detail.

## Results

On the aggregated score over nine multimodal benchmarks (MMStar, MMMU, SimpleVQA, InfoVQA, TextVQA, DocVQA, OCRBench, AI2D, ChartQA), ViQ achieves 57.2 average with Qwen2.5-1.5B and 63.9 with Qwen2.5-7B, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art InternViT-2.5-6B (57.0 and 63.8) while using only 1.3B visual encoder parameters versus 6.0B. ViQ is the only method that is simultaneously discrete and supports native-resolution inputs. In image reconstruction on ImageNet-1K (256×256), ViQ attains PSNR 22.73, SSIM 0.66, and rFID 0.62, ranking first among discrete tokenizers optimized for understanding. Training efficiency gains of 20%–70% are reported across Qwen2.5 model sizes (0.5B–7B) at 4k and 16k sequence lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViQ (1.3B, discrete, any-resolution) outperforms all continuous general-purpose encoders on the 9-benchmark average: OAI-CLIP-L (45.8→57.2 with 1.5B LLM), SigLIP2-g (53.1→57.2), DINOv2-g (54.0→57.2), OryxViT (53.4→57.2), and AIMv2-H (53.9→57.2). Against the strongest continuous baseline InternViT-2.5-6B (6.0B params), ViQ edges ahead by 0.2 (57.2 vs 57.0) and 0.1 (63.9 vs 63.8) with the 1.5B and 7B LLMs respectively, while using roughly 4.6× fewer visual encoder parameters. Compared to prior quantized encoders, ViQ's advantage is dramatic: QLIP scores 29.7 and UniTok 33.0 on the same 1.5B-LLM setup, versus ViQ's 57.2. On reconstruction (Table 2), UniTok leads ViQ in raw fidelity (PSNR 25.32 vs 22.73, rFID 0.37 vs 0.62), but the paper attributes this to UniTok's direct reconstruction objective that degrades its multimodal understanding (33.0 avg). Among continuous tokenizers, FLUX-VAE (PSNR 32.74, rFID 0.18) and Qwen-Image (PSNR 32.18) remain substantially ahead of ViQ in reconstruction, confirming the inherent fidelity cost of discrete compression. ViQ's largest per-benchmark gains over continuous encoders appear on InfoVQA (41.6 vs 35.2 for InternViT-6B) and DocVQA (84.2 vs 80.1), while it trails on OCRBench (636 vs 690), which the authors attribute to high-frequency detail loss from aggressive quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViQ builds on the SigLIP2 contrastive vision-language encoder as its backbone and adopts NaViT-style positional embeddings and OryxViT's variable-length token handling to support native-resolution inputs. For quantization, it leverages FSQ (a non-learnable, optimization-free scalar quantizer) rather than the learnable codebooks used in VQ-VAE or SimVQ, and departs from prior quantized multimodal encoders (QLIP, UniTok) by introducing a two-stage pipeline: first text-aligned pre-training with self-distillation to preserve semantic knowledge, then progressive discretization via a proximal L∞-norm bottleneck. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit treatment of the semantics-vs-details trade-off in discrete representations, addressed through the proximal representation learning strategy and a VAE-latent reconstruction loss that supervises low-level detail without eroding high-level semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20–70% training speedup and 1/96 image storage compression make ViQ a practically significant efficiency tool for anyone training or deploying multimodal LLMs, and the fact that a 1.3B quantized encoder matches a 6B continuous encoder on aggregated understanding scores suggests discrete representations can be a viable drop-in replacement for continuous visual features. However, the understanding margins are thin (≤0.2 points), and the paper acknowledges a residual gap on the most detail-intensive OCR benchmarks, which it attributes to the inherent information loss of aggressive discrete compression. Open questions include whether multi-scale or residual quantization can close this detail gap, whether the approach extends to video, and whether the efficiency gains persist at even larger LLM scales or longer sequences.
