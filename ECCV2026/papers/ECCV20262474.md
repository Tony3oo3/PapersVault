## Problem

Uncalibrated natural-light photometric stereo (NaPS) is inherently ill-posed because the surrounding illumination is unknown and, in typical indoor settings, spatially varying (near-field). Prior PS pipelines mask out the background, discarding the very observations that directly encode the lighting environment, which leaves severe ambiguities among illumination, geometry, and reflectance. This paper asks whether explicitly reconstructing the illumination from the background can convert the ill-posed NaPS problem into a tractable inverse-rendering formulation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes approximately 100–150 HDR RGB images (512×512 in synthetic experiments; bracketed-exposure composites from a Canon EOS R8 in real captures) acquired by rotating the camera–object pair as a rigid body with fixed relative pose, along with SfM-estimated camera extrinsics and foreground masks (SegNeXt). Unlike prior PS pipelines, the background pixels are retained as first-class supervision rather than masked out. The pipeline outputs per-pixel surface normals, albedo, roughness, and a depth map for the target object, plus an intermediate 3D Gaussian Splatting illumination field that serves as the emitter for the inverse-rendering stage.

## Method

The core idea is a two-stage pipeline that turns the background into an explicit illumination source. First, a 3DGS emitter is reconstructed from background pixels using 3DGRT ray tracing in two phases: a full-scene fit with a log-intensity L1 loss for HDR robustness, followed by a refinement stage that isolates high-luminance Gaussians via Otsu thresholding and optimizes only their colors with a weighted L1 loss to capture bright light sources. Second, with the 3DGS emitter frozen, the object's surface normals, per-pixel Disney BRDF parameters (albedo and roughness), and depth are jointly optimized via differentiable Monte Carlo path tracing of the rendering equation. Incident radiance is queried from the 3DGS emitter along sampled directions, and binary visibility is computed against a mesh rebuilt from the current depth estimate to handle cast shadows. To keep the MC estimator efficient under sparse, high-intensity natural lighting, the authors fit a 64-component Gaussian mixture model to the emitter's Gaussian centers, project each component to a von Mises–Fisher distribution on the shading hemisphere, and combine this emitter-guided light sampling with cosine sampling through multiple importance sampling.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset (6 objects, 3 lighting environments, 150 views), the proposed method achieves an average MAngE of 3.35° for normals, 32.5 dB PSNR for albedo, and 0.0610 MAbsE for roughness. This represents a substantial improvement over SDM-UniPS (4.92° / 28.2 dB / 0.0830) and LINO-UniPS (6.12° / 28.4 dB / 0.0659) across all three metrics. The normal estimation gain is particularly pronounced on objects with smooth or complex geometry (e.g., SPHERE: 1.38° vs. 2.94°/3.47°; COW: 1.53° vs. 4.48°/5.64°). The method also benefits from more viewpoints, improving from 5.10° MAngE at 40 views to 3.35° at 150 views, while baselines remain nearly flat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two learning-based universal photometric stereo methods: SDM-UniPS (Ikehata, CVPR 2023) and LINO-UniPS (Li et al., 2025). The proposed inverse-rendering method outperforms both baselines on average across all three metrics: normals (3.35° vs. 4.92° and 6.12°), albedo (32.5 dB vs. 28.2 dB and 28.4 dB), and roughness (0.0610 vs. 0.0830 and 0.0659). The normal estimation advantage is most dramatic on smooth or highly curved objects (SPHERE, COW, POT2), where the 3DGS-reconstructed illumination provides strong geometric constraints that learning-based methods lack. On albedo, the proposed method wins on all six objects, with the largest gains on POT2 (+10.6 dB over SDM) and READING (+9.0 dB over SDM), indicating that explicit illumination modeling effectively disentangles shading from intrinsic reflectance. The one area where the proposed method is less dominant is roughness on BEAR and BUDDHA, where LINO-UniPS achieves lower MAbsE (0.0189 and 0.109 vs. 0.0536 and 0.144), suggesting that the two-parameter Disney BRDF may not fully capture the material complexity of those objects. On real-world captures (CAR, SNOWMAN, LION), the authors report qualitatively more plausible normals and albedo maps than both baselines, though no ground truth is available for quantitative comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of natural-light photometric stereo (NaPS, e.g., Spin-UP) and inverse-rendering-based PS (e.g., SCPS-NIR, DANI-Net), but departs from both by treating the image background—which all prior PS pipelines mask out—as a direct observation of the surrounding illumination. For the illumination representation, it follows the recent line of work using neural scene representations as emitters (NeRF-based emitter, EnvGS) but substitutes 3D Gaussian Splatting with a ray-tracing formulation (3DGRT) to handle near-field, spatially varying lighting that spherical Gaussians or environment maps cannot capture. The inverse-rendering stage uses a standard Disney BRDF with Monte Carlo path tracing, but introduces a GMM-based importance sampler fitted to the 3DGS emitter centers, projected onto von Mises-Fisher distributions, combined with cosine sampling via MIS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By converting the inherently ill-posed uncalibrated NaPS problem into a tractable inverse-rendering optimization through explicit background illumination reconstruction, this work opens a practical path to physics-based shape and reflectance recovery under realistic indoor lighting without calibrated sources. Key limitations include the absence of global illumination effects (inter-reflections) on the target object and the high computational cost (~2 hours per object on an RTX 6000 Ada), which limits scalability. Open questions include whether learned priors or more efficient neural rendering can reduce the optimization burden, and whether the framework extends to dynamic or partially occluded backgrounds. The finding that dense background observations (150+ views) substantially improve results while learning-based baselines plateau suggests a fundamentally different scaling behavior worth further investigation.
