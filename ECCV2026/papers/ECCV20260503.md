## Problem

Diffusion models rigidly allocate identical computational resources to all samples regardless of complexity, lacking adaptive-step capacity. Meanwhile, autoregressive visual models are bottlenecked by lossy discrete tokenization and irreversible error accumulation, since their causal or masked prediction mechanisms cannot retroactively correct earlier mistakes. GRN addresses both limitations by combining near-lossless discrete tokenization with a global refinement loop that progressively corrects errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260503/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes an ImageNet class label and produces a 256×256 RGB image; for text-to-image it takes a text prompt and generates 1024×1024 images; for text-to-video it takes a text prompt and produces 480p videos of 2–10 seconds. The visual tokenizer compresses input into a [1+T, H/16, W/16, C] latent space (C=16 or 64 channels), which is then discretized via M rounds of binary quantization. Two generation variants exist: GRN_ind predicts 2^M integer indices per spatial-temporal position, while GRN_bit predicts CM individual binary bits, with the entire channel dimension predicted in parallel per token via multi-token prediction.

## Method

GRN introduces Hierarchical Binary Quantization (HBQ), inspired by Haar wavelets, which performs M sequential binary thresholding rounds on VAE features so that quantization error decays exponentially (≤1/2^j at round j), achieving near-lossless discrete compression at 4× higher compression than continuous VAEs without increasing latent channels. The generative model starts from a fully random token map and, at each refinement step, a transformer predicts the complete drawing map Y_{t+1} conditioned on the current state F_t (a mixture of real and random tokens governed by a selection map S_t) and the generation condition. Unlike MaskGIT or BERT-style masked models where committed tokens are immutable, GRN's global refinement allows previously generated tokens to be overwritten, enabling retroactive error correction. An entropy-guided scheduling function dynamically allocates refinement steps per sample: low-entropy (simple) samples converge in as few as 20 steps while complex samples use up to 50, reducing average inference cost by 1.25× (up to 2.5×) without quality loss. Training uses a simple cross-entropy objective with randomly sampled token subsets at varying fill ratios, and the tokenizer is trained with reconstruction, LPIPS, and PatchGAN adversarial losses.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, GRN-G (2B params) achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.81 and IS of 299.0, surpassing all autoregressive baselines including VAR-d30 (FID 1.92) and LlamaGen-XXL (FID 2.34). The HBQ tokenizer achieves a reconstruction rFID of 0.56, outperforming the continuous SD-VAE (0.87) at 4× higher compression. On GenEval (T2I), GRN (2B) scores 0.76 overall, and on VBench (T2V), it achieves 82.99 overall with only 2B parameters, surpassing the 5B CogVideoX-5B (81.61).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRN-G (2B, FID 1.81) outperforms all autoregressive baselines: VAR-d30 (1.92), RandAR-XXL (2.15), LlamaGen-XXL (2.34), and MaskGIT (6.18), with the global refinement mechanism credited for mitigating error accumulation. However, it trails the strongest flow/diffusion and hybrid methods—REPA+SiT-XL/2 (1.42), BitDance-H-1x (1.24), and MAR (1.55)—which use continuous tokenizers or larger parameter counts. At the small-model scale, GRN-B (130M, FID 3.56) substantially outperforms MaskGIT (227M, FID 6.18) with fewer parameters. On GenEval T2I, GRN (2B, 0.76) beats same-scale SD3 Medium (2B, 0.62) and Infinity (2B, 0.73) but is outperformed by larger models like Qwen-Image (20B, 0.87) and BitDance (14B, 0.86). On VBench T2V, GRN (2B, 82.99) surpasses CogVideoX-5B (5B, 81.61), URSA (1.7B, 82.40), and Emu3 (8B, 80.96), but remains below InfinityStar (8B, 83.74) and Wan2.1 (14B, 84.70), which the authors attribute to the compute gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRN builds on the 3D causal VAE architecture from Wan 2.1 for its tokenizer backbone and adopts transformer components (SwiGLU, RMSNorm, RoPE) from JiT, but fundamentally departs from both diffusion and standard autoregressive paradigms. Unlike MaskGIT or VAR, where generated tokens become immutable and errors accumulate irreversibly, GRN introduces a global refinement loop that can retroactively correct any token at each step. Its Hierarchical Binary Quantization also diverges from prior discrete tokenizers (Infinity, BitDance) that close the quality gap to continuous VAEs by expanding latent dimensions; HBQ achieves comparable fidelity at the same channel count with exponentially decaying quantization error, inspired by Haar wavelet decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 4-round discrete tokenizer matches a continuous VAE at 4× higher compression without increasing latent channels challenges the long-held assumption that discrete tokenization inherently sacrifices reconstruction quality, potentially reshaping how visual tokenizers are designed. The refinement mechanism addresses a structural weakness of all prior AR visual generators (error accumulation), and the entropy-guided adaptive-step sampling offers practical efficiency gains (up to 2.5× speedup) without quality loss. However, the authors acknowledge that T2I/T2V results are at 2B scale and that video generation still struggles with non-human scenes and fine visual details, leaving open whether the approach scales to the 14–20B regime where current leaders operate. The paper also raises the prospect of natural integration into LLMs for unified text-visual generation, positioning GRN as a potential alternative to the Transfusion architecture.
