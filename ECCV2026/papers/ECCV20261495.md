## Problem

Existing embodied QA benchmarks (EQA, REVERIE, MP3D-EQA) restrict agents to 2D planar navigation with limited rotation, failing to evaluate the fine-grained 5-DoF viewpoint control needed to resolve occlusions and read viewpoint-dependent evidence such as small text or object states. Additionally, mesh-based simulators degrade the photometric fidelity required for such fine-grained visual reasoning, making it impossible to construct questions that genuinely demand active viewpoint exploration.

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a 512×512 egocentric RGB frame (90° FOV) rendered from a 3DGS scene, the natural-language question, its current 5-DoF state (x, y, z, θ, φ), step count, and collision feedback. It outputs a discrete action from a fixed set (0.25 m translations along axes, 30° yaw/pitch rotations, or stop) and, upon stopping, a free-form text answer. Answers are scored 1–5 by a VLM-as-a-judge (GPT-5.1) that also receives the goal image, enabling open-vocabulary evaluation without exact string matching.

## Method

E3VS-Bench is constructed through a five-stage pipeline: (1) curating 99 photorealistic scenes from SceneSplat++ (3DGS reconstructions of ScanNet++), (2) generating 27,877 candidate QAs via Gemini 2.5 Flash over multi-view renderings of target objects, (3) human-verified filtering (~1,120 annotation hours), (4) expert viewpoint labeling identifying answerable vs. unanswerable poses, and (5) answerability filtering using GPT-5.1 to remove episodes solvable from prior knowledge or the initial viewpoint alone. The key design choice is leveraging 3DGS's photorealistic free-viewpoint rendering to preserve fine-grained details (text, logos, subtle attributes) that mesh-based representations lose, enabling questions that are genuinely unanswerable without 5-DoF viewpoint transitions. Evaluation uses a closed-loop perception-action framework where VLMs iteratively select actions from the discrete 5-DoF space over up to 25 steps, with a structured prompt encoding the world coordinate system, action constraints, and dynamic state.

## Results

On E3VS-Bench (377 test episodes, 6 question categories), Gemini 3.0 Flash achieves the best average VLM-judge score of 2.79/5, followed by Gemini 3.0 Pro (2.75) and Qwen3-VL-8B (2.46). All evaluated VLMs remain substantially below human performance (3.53/5), with a gap of 0.74 points even for the best model. Open-source models (Qwen3-VL-30B: 2.32, InternVL3.5-8B: 2.21, Step3-VL-10B: 2.24) score only marginally above the Random Action baseline (2.28), indicating that current VLMs lack effective 5-DoF viewpoint planning despite strong 2D recognition. The VQA-at-Goal upper bound (3.58–3.60 for Gemini 2.5 Flash / GPT 5.1) versus VQA-at-Start (2.02–2.14) confirms that initial viewpoints are insufficient and active exploration is necessary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source VLMs, Qwen3-VL-8B (2.46 avg) outperforms Qwen3-VL-30B (2.32), InternVL3.5-8B (2.21), and Step3-VL-10B (2.24), but all remain within 0.18 of the Random Action baseline (2.28), indicating negligible effective viewpoint planning. The 2D visual search baselines SEAL (1.68 avg) and DyFO (1.86 avg) perform worse than even the blind VLM setting (1.67–1.82), confirming that image-space cropping/zooming cannot substitute for true 3D viewpoint transitions. Gemini 3.0 Flash (2.79) leads all proprietary and open-source agents by 0.33 over the next-best (Gemini 3.0 Pro, 2.75) and 0.33 over Qwen3-VL-8B, with its advantage most pronounced on CGS (3.53 vs. 3.13) and OA (3.18 vs. 2.60). However, even the best agent trails the VQA-at-Goal upper bound (3.58–3.60) by roughly 0.8 points, and trails human performance (3.53) by 0.74, revealing that the bottleneck is not recognition but the ability to actively select evidence-revealing viewpoints in 5-DoF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the embodied question answering (EQA) line of work (EQA, REVERIE, MP3D-EQA, OpenEQA) by replacing the standard 2-DoF planar navigation with full 5-DoF viewpoint control in photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes. It draws on the active perception and visual search literatures from psychology and embodied AI, but departs from prior EQA benchmarks that treat the agent as a 2D moving camera with limited rotation. The use of 3DGS (via SceneSplat++) rather than mesh-based simulators is a key methodological choice, motivated by the need to preserve fine-grained visual details (small text, brand logos) that are essential for viewpoint-dependent questions. The VLM-as-a-judge evaluation protocol is adapted from OpenEQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark quantifies a substantial and consistent gap between state-of-the-art VLMs and humans on 5-DoF active perception (best model ~2.79 vs. human 3.53 on a 5-point scale), revealing that strong 2D visual reasoning does not transfer to coherent 3D viewpoint planning. The finding that GPT 5.1 performs at the level of much smaller open-source models suggests that current VLM architectures lack a dedicated mechanism for viewpoint-dependent evidence acquisition. Open questions include how to train agents for 5-DoF viewpoint planning (the paper releases train/validation splits for this purpose), whether the VLM-as-a-judge adequately captures viewpoint adequacy (Spearman ρ=0.54 with humans), and whether the 3DGS-based evaluation generalizes to real-world deployment.
