## Problem

State-of-the-art flow models like FLUX.1-dev produce nearly redundant outputs when generating multiple samples under the same conditioning, a phenomenon known as diversity collapse. Existing remedies either guide latents (yielding only marginal diversity gains) or rely on external reward models for sample selection (incurring substantial inference-time overhead). The paper seeks an efficient, training-free mechanism that recovers diversity without additional reward supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260021/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of N initial Gaussian noise latents (sampled from N(0,I)) together with a conditioning signal—text prompt, depth map, or reference image—and outputs N diverse images at 512×512 resolution. The intervention is batch-level: all N samples must be processed jointly so that pairwise feature distances can be computed and optimized. The guidance is applied only during the early denoising window (t ∈ [1.0, 0.8]) at a single MMDiT block, adding roughly 0.1 s of latency over standard I.I.D. sampling.

## Method

The key insight is that output diversity collapse is driven by collapse of the internal MMDiT block features h_t, not the latent x_t. The method introduces a two-stage 'Disperse-and-Refine' module at Block B2: (1) Feature Self-Guidance iteratively updates the batch features via gradient descent on a pairwise cosine-similarity energy (Eq. 3), pushing features apart without backpropagating through earlier transformer layers; (2) Manifold Regularization reprocesses the dispersed features through the same MMDiT block to obtain projected features ĥ_t, then linearly blends them with the dispersed features using a tunable parameter β (default 0.4) to anchor samples back on the data manifold. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no external reward models, and integrates as a plug-and-play module into any pretrained flow model, adding only 30 lightweight optimization steps at a single block over a short timestep window.

## Results

On GenEval with FLUX.1-dev (8 samples, 512² resolution), the proposed method achieves DINO diversity 0.68, VendiScore 3.57, DreamSim 0.40, and MSS 0.19, substantially outperforming I.I.D. sampling (0.57, 2.77, 0.27, 0.32 respectively) while maintaining CLIPScore of 32.05 (vs. 32.43 for I.I.D.). Inference latency is 1.70 s per batch, only 0.11 s above I.I.D. (1.59 s) and far below Group Inference (2.86–4.55 s). On depth-to-image (COCO 2017) and personalized generation (Dreambooth), the method improves DINO diversity from 0.43→0.52 and 0.45→0.57 over I.I.D. while preserving CLIPScore within ~0.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on the majority of diversity metrics. Against Particle Guidance [5] and Interval Guidance [29], it achieves substantially higher DINO (0.68 vs. 0.58/0.57) and VendiScore (3.57 vs. 3.16/2.88) while matching or beating their CLIPScore, and runs faster than Particle Guidance (1.70 s vs. 2.94 s). Versus Shielded Diffusion [27] and CNO [25], it leads on DINO (0.68 vs. 0.61/0.63), VendiScore (3.57 vs. 3.35/3.46), and MSS (0.19 vs. 0.23/0.22), with comparable DreamSim and a ~0.1-point CLIPScore gap. The closest competitor is Group Inference [39]: at the 8-of-64 setting, the proposed method matches or exceeds it on DINO (0.68 vs. 0.67), DreamSim (0.40 vs. 0.35), and MSS (0.19 vs. 0.21) at 40% lower latency (1.70 s vs. 2.86 s); at 8-of-128, Group Inference edges ahead on DINO (0.70 vs. 0.68) and VendiScore (3.61 vs. 3.57) but at 2.7× the latency (4.55 s). The consistent trade-off is a ~0.4-point CLIPScore reduction relative to I.I.D., which the authors attribute to the inherent diversity–faithfulness tension and which can be tuned via the blending parameter β.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-guidance framework of Epstein et al. (NeurIPS 2023) from controllability to diversity, shifting the intervention point from the latent space (as in Particle Guidance, Interval Guidance, Shielded Diffusion, and CNO) to the internal MMDiT feature space of flow models. It departs from reward-model-based selection methods like Group Inference by eliminating external scoring entirely, instead using a 'disperse-and-refine' mechanism where features are pushed apart via pairwise cosine-similarity gradients and then projected back onto the data manifold by re-passing through the same transformer block. The manifold regularization step, inspired by the read-write mechanism of transformer blocks from mechanistic interpretability, is a novel twist that distinguishes this work from prior feature-space interventions like Balancing-Act, which guided UNet h-space features without an explicit manifold-projection step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of the method with near-zero latency overhead (1.70s vs. 1.59s for I.I.D.) makes it immediately deployable across existing flow model pipelines, addressing a practical bottleneck that reward-based methods like Group Inference (2.86–4.55s) cannot. The diagnostic finding that internal feature collapse is the direct cause of output diversity collapse provides a useful interpretability lens for the broader flow-model community. Limitations include the batch-level requirement (incompatible with streaming generation) and inheritance of base-model biases; the authors note future extensions to 3D synthesis, video, and molecular generation as open directions.
