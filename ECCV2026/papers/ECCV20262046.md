## Problem

Industrial anomaly detection in cold-start deployment is difficult because defective samples are unavailable, input resolution limits prevent LMMs from resolving fine-grained defects, and abstract textual specifications are not grounded in pixel-level visual evidence. Existing optimization-based methods require extensive defective training data, while retrieval-augmented approaches rely on static context injection that fails to synthesize global assembly logic with local sensory evidence needed for auditable inspection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262046/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a query RGB image, natural language specifications (textual standards describing component topology and tolerances), and optionally a small set of normal reference images, and produces a closed-set diagnostic answer (anomaly verdict, defect type, description, localization) accompanied by a traceable evidence chain linking the final decision to specific visual crops and failure-mode nodes. In zero-shot mode the atlas contains only textual standards; in few-shot mode it additionally stores SAM 3-extracted reference visual features per component, enabling localized comparison against spatially corresponding normal samples. The VLM verifier is prompted to output a fixed-format answer (e.g., 'The correct answer is (X)') parsed from the fused context.

## Method

GLLS is a training-free dual-stream framework that treats the LMM as an evidence-driven auditor orchestrated by a Part-Aware Visual-Logical Atlas (PVLA), a heterogeneous directed graph built offline via GPT-5 knowledge expansion and SAM 3 text-conditioned segmentation of normal references. The Global & Logic Stream uses SAM 3 to extract partially checkable visual facts (component counts, spatial arrangement, color order) and a VLM auditor cross-validates them against the atlas logic report, producing an intermediate structural verdict. In parallel, the Fine-Grained & Actions Stream formulates crop selection as a budgeted MDP solved by MCTS with a modified UCB that incorporates a lightweight anomaly heatmap prior (AdaptCLIP or ABounD), selecting K_max=3 spatially distinct evidence crops under a fixed computation budget. Final verification performs a topological traversal of the PVLA graph (not flat vector search) to retrieve component-scoped standards and reference visuals, then the VLM fuses the structural report, evidence crops, and retrieved context to produce the auditable diagnosis.

## Results

On the MMAD-QA benchmark, GLLS (Qwen3-VL 8B, 1-shot) achieves the highest average accuracy of 91.2% on MVTec and 84.7% on VisA, outperforming the strongest proprietary baseline Gemini-3 (89.3% / 82.3%) and the base Qwen3-VL 8B (84.4% / 74.2%). In the 0-shot setting, GLLS (Qwen3-VL 8B) reaches 87.1% / 75.1%, still surpassing all open-source VLM baselines. On cross-dataset binary anomaly detection (MPDD, DTD, DAGM), GLLS (Qwen3-VL 8B, 1-shot) achieves 91.01% average accuracy, exceeding the best fine-tuned method IAD-R1 (86.10%) and the best CLIP-based method AdaptCLIP (86.14%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base VLMs, GLLS consistently improves: over Qwen3-VL 8B, the 1-shot variant gains +6.8% (MVTec) and +10.5% (VisA), while the 0-shot variant gains +2.7% and +0.9%; over Qwen2.5-VL 7B, the 1-shot variant gains +6.5% and +11.0%. On the cross-dataset binary protocol (Table 2), GLLS (Qwen3-VL 8B, 1-shot) at 91.01% average accuracy surpasses all CLIP-based baselines—WinClip (75.85%), AnomalyClip (83.67%), and AdaptCLIP (86.14%)—as well as proprietary LMMs GPT-4o (81.04%) and GPT-4.1 (81.98%). Among fine-tuned industrial anomaly detection methods, GLLS outperforms IAD-R1 (86.10%) by 4.91% and AD-FM (84.57%) by 6.44% in average accuracy, while requiring no gradient updates or defective training samples. The largest single-metric gains appear in Classification and Discrimination tasks, where the PVLA's structured standards and SAM 3's tool-grounded checks provide the most benefit over passive VLM reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLLS departs from the dominant optimization-based paradigm (IAD-R1, AnomalyR1, EMIT, OmniAD) that aligns LMMs via reinforcement learning or supervised fine-tuning, which requires abundant defective samples unavailable during cold-start deployment. It also moves beyond static retrieval-augmented approaches (Echo, Triad) by introducing an active, budgeted search mechanism (MCTS) for evidence acquisition rather than passive region proposals. The framework repurposes MCTS from its game-playing origins as a spatial crop-selection policy under fixed computation budgets, and leverages SAM 3's text-conditioned segmentation to convert abstract textual specifications into partially checkable visual facts—a use of segmentation as a logical verification tool rather than a detection tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, auditable design addresses a practical gap in industrial deployment where defective samples are scarce and decisions must be traceable to explicit visual evidence for compliance. A notable finding is that injecting strict offline standards (PVLA) can actually degrade weaker backbones (Qwen2.5-7B) when paired with passive low-resolution inputs, revealing that knowledge grounding and active perception are not independently sufficient but must be co-designed. Open questions include whether the MCTS search policy can be learned with verifiable rewards rather than relying on a fixed heatmap heuristic, and whether the PVLA's GPT-5-generated contrastive edges scale to domains with hundreds of defect types without manual curation.
