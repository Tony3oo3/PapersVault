## Problem

Reasoning-induced vision-language models for image quality assessment suffer from discrete collapse, where distinct-quality images receive nearly identical scalar scores because VLMs are pretrained to generate discrete tokens rather than continuous perceptual quantities. Existing remedies—token-probability averaging, pure pairwise comparison, and static anchor sets—each trade off sensitivity, scalability, or robustness to distribution shift, leaving a gap for a method that is both fine-grained and deployable in realistic streaming settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single distorted RGB image in an online streaming paradigm where queries arrive sequentially with strict causality (no future peeking). The VLM first emits free-form reasoning text and a raw score, which is mapped to the target [1,5] MOS scale via a five-parameter logistic transformation; the final output is a refined scalar quality score. The system requires only black-box access to the underlying VLM and a text encoder for retrieval embeddings, with no architectural modification or retraining of the backbone.

## Method

ME-IQA is a plug-and-play, test-time re-ranking framework built around a hybrid memory bank: a static Anchor Memory (AM) pre-built from labeled KONIQ-10K anchors with GT-stratified retrieval, and a dynamic Contrast Memory (CM) grown online from previously processed queries with agglomerative pruning. For each query, the VLM's reasoning trace is summarized into a concise quality description that serves as the retrieval key, fetching K=32 semantically and perceptually aligned neighbors. The VLM then acts as a probabilistic comparator, extracting pairwise preference probabilities (P(query > exemplar)) from token distributions, and the refined score is obtained by minimizing a Thurstone Case V binary cross-entropy objective with a weak quadratic tether to the initial mapped score, yielding a closed-form ridge-style solution. A gated reflection step triggers when the refined score deviates from the initial by more than ε, prompting the VLM to revise its quality description before consolidating the case into CM for future retrieval.

## Results

On the weighted average (WAVG) across seven IQA benchmarks, ME-IQA applied to EvoQuality achieves PLCC 0.777 and SRCC 0.751, improving over its baseline (0.748 / 0.716) by +0.029 / +0.035. The strongest non-reasoning competitor, Compare2Score, reaches only PLCC 0.686 / SRCC 0.688, a gap of roughly 0.09 in PLCC. Gains are largest on synthetic-distortion datasets (e.g., KADID: 0.783 vs. 0.744 PLCC for EvoQuality+ME-IQA vs. baseline), and ME-IQA@32 matches or exceeds 64-sample majority/mean voting at 2.4× lower latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ME-IQA wins across all 11 compared baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest non-reasoning method Compare2Score (WAVG PLCC 0.686 / SRCC 0.688), EvoQuality+ME-IQA leads by +0.091 PLCC and +0.063 SRCC, with the gap widening on fine-grained synthetic sets (KADID: 0.783 vs. 0.720; PIPAL: 0.642 vs. 0.596). Among VLM-based non-reasoning scorers, Q-Align (0.663) and DeQA-Score (0.652) trail by 0.11+ in WAVG PLCC, and CLIP-IQA+ (0.641) and MANIQA (0.582) are further behind. Against the three open-source reasoning VLMs, ME-IQA adds +0.030 (Q-Insight), +0.028 (VisualQuality-R1), and +0.029 (EvoQuality) in WAVG PLCC, with SRCC gains of +0.036, +0.035, and +0.035 respectively. On proprietary models, ME-IQA improves GPT-5 by +0.030 PLCC (0.676→0.706) and Doubao-Seed-1.6-Vision by +0.033 PLCC (0.678→0.711). In the test-time scaling comparison (Table 2), ME-IQA@32 outperforms both Maj@64 and Mean@64 on WAVG for all three open-source backbones while running 2.4× faster, and surpasses Compare2Score@32 by +0.046 PLCC (EvoQuality) at comparable latency (14.32 vs. 13.93 s/img).

## Relation to Prior Work

ME-IQA sits at the intersection of reasoning-induced VLM-based IQA (Q-Insight, VisualQuality-R1, EvoQuality) and retrieval-augmented generation for VLMs, but departs from both in key ways. Rather than relying on static anchor sets (Compare2Score) or exhaustive pairwise comparisons (Depict-QA), it introduces a hybrid memory bank—combining offline GT-anchored exemplars with online contrast cases—retrieved via reasoning-derived text embeddings. The re-ranking step formalizes the VLM's comparative output through Thurstone's Case V model, fusing ordinal pairwise evidence with the initial scalar score under a weak quadratic prior, which distinguishes it from both token-probability averaging and pure pairwise aggregation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, test-time-only design means practitioners can immediately improve any existing reasoning-induced VLM without retraining, architectural changes, or additional supervision—a practically significant property for deployment. The identification and quantification of discrete collapse (via JS divergence, entropy, and effective-bin metrics) provides a useful diagnostic for the field. However, remaining limitations include dependence on the underlying VLM's perceptual ceiling (failure cases with subtle impulse noise or JPEG artifacts), the need for a labeled anchor set (KONIQ-10K) for AM construction, and unexplored scaling to video IQA or extremely large memory banks. Whether the online contrast memory's benefits persist over very long streams or under severe distribution shift remains an open question.
