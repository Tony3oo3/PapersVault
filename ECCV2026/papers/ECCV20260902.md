## Problem

State-of-the-art generative novel view synthesis methods treat mirror-containing images as ordinary single views, failing to recognize the mirror and exploit reflected content for scene generation. This leads to baked mirror surfaces, leakage artifacts, and reflections inconsistent with the generated scene. The paper addresses this without requiring additional training or large-scale mirror datasets, which are both expensive and insufficient to guarantee correct physical reflection constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260902/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or three RGB images containing a visible mirror along with their camera poses and target view poses as input; mirror masks can be provided or automatically estimated via DAM segmentation and RANSAC plane fitting. It produces complete target-view images where both the surrounding scene and the mirror surface are globally consistent with the reflected content. The method is training-free and operates as a plug-in on existing multi-view diffusion backbones (SEVA, MVGenMaster), requiring no architectural modification beyond attention masking.

## Method

Ref-GeNVS treats a mirror image as two complementary views by reflecting input camera poses across the estimated mirror plane (Householder reflection) to construct virtual reflected views. Stage 1 generates the target scene from mirror-masked original inputs and these reflected virtual views, using Mirror-gated attention that zeros out cross-view attention logits for tokens outside the mirror region in reflected inputs, preventing non-reflected content from corrupting generation. Stage 2 completes the mirror surface via Reflection injection, which at each denoising step decodes the reflected-pose latent, horizontally flips it, re-encodes it, and injects it into the mirror region of the target latent, combined with SDEdit-style guidance that anchors the non-mirror region to stabilize the boundary. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires only the mirror plane equation and binary masks as additional inputs to the diffusion backbone.

## Results

On the sparse-input NVS setting, Ref-GeNVS (built on SEVA) achieves a DreamSim of 0.129 and CLIP similarity of 0.951 on real Mirror-NeRF data, and 0.151 / 0.948 on synthetic data, outperforming SEVA (0.275 / 0.920 and 0.264 / 0.929) and MVGenMaster (0.361 / 0.867 and 0.222 / 0.930) on perceptual metrics. On pixel-level metrics, Ours(SEVA) reaches PSNR 14.19 and SSIM 0.455 on real data, surpassing SEVA (12.03 / 0.394). In the single-image setting, Ref-GeNVS attains the best DreamSim (0.333) and CLIP (0.915), beating VistaDream (0.569 / 0.858), FlexWorld (0.364 / 0.885), and SEVA (0.381 / 0.902).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SEVA [61], Ref-GeNVS wins on every metric in both real and synthetic sparse-input settings: DreamSim drops from 0.275 to 0.129 (real) and 0.264 to 0.151 (synthetic), while PSNR improves from 12.03 to 14.19 (real) and 13.65 to 15.51 (synthetic). Against MVGenMaster [6], Ours(MVGen.) dominates on real data across all five metrics (e.g., DS 0.194 vs 0.361, PSNR 13.67 vs 12.75), and on synthetic data it leads on perceptual metrics (DS 0.106 vs 0.222, CLIP 0.964 vs 0.930) and PSNR (17.95 vs 16.90), though MVGenMaster retains a slight edge on SSIM (0.735 vs 0.748) and LPIPS (0.350 vs 0.291). In the single-image setting, Ref-GeNVS outperforms VistaDream [47] by a large margin on DreamSim (0.333 vs 0.569) and CLIP (0.915 vs 0.858), and beats FlexWorld [10] on both metrics (0.333 vs 0.364 DS; 0.915 vs 0.885 CLIP). Overall, the paper's method consistently wins or ties on perceptual similarity metrics across all four baselines, with the largest gains appearing in DreamSim where mirror-aware generation most directly reduces perceptual inconsistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on multi-view diffusion backbones (SEVA, MVGenMaster) and adapts SDEdit-style guidance for boundary stabilization, but its core conceptual departure is treating a mirror image as two complementary views rather than a single ordinary view. It positions itself against prior reflection-aware NVS methods (Mirror-NeRF, MirrorGaussian, TraM-NeRF) that focus on rendering the reflective surface itself, whereas Ref-GeNVS synthesizes the scene content revealed *through* the mirror while simultaneously generating a consistent mirror surface. The training-free design contrasts with the fine-tuning-based approaches (MirrorVerse, Reflecting Reality) that attempt to teach diffusion models mirror physics implicitly, and the method is more closely related to non-line-of-sight imaging (OrCa, World-from-Eyes) but operates under sparser inputs and an ideal planar-mirror assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode of current generative NVS systems—mirrors are treated as opaque backgrounds—offering a practical, plug-and-play solution that requires no retraining of the diffusion backbone. The training-free nature and reliance on off-the-shelf components (DAM for segmentation, Depth Anything 3 for plane estimation, SAM2 for tracking) make the pipeline accessible, and the extension to distorted mirrors (convex mirror balls) hints at broader applicability. However, the evaluation is limited to 6 synthetic scenes and one real dataset, the method assumes ideal planar mirrors, and the two-step generation stage contributes almost no quantitative gain over the simpler virtual-view-plus-gated-attention pipeline, leaving open whether the full pipeline is necessary at scale. For embodied agents and 3D scene generation, the ability to infer hidden geometry from partial mirror observations is a promising but still early capability.
