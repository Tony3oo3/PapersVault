## Problem

DDIM inversion systematically underestimates noise magnitude because the denoiser is fed z_{t-1} (lower noise level) instead of z_t, causing a partial-denoising bias that degrades reconstruction fidelity. While iterative methods like ReNoise correct this bias, they require many redundant UNet evaluations per timestep, with most gains concentrated in a short 'landing phase' of rapid variance adjustment followed by a costly 'steady phase' of marginal refinement.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (evaluated at 1024×1024) and optionally a text prompt for editing, operating entirely in the latent space of pretrained diffusion backbones (SDXL, SD1.5, LCM, SDXL Turbo, FLUX). It outputs the full inverted latent trajectory {z_t} and per-step noise predictions {ε_t}, which serve as the starting point for few-step denoising (3–5 steps) to produce a reconstructed or edited image. The procedure is deterministic, training-free, and requires no optimization over conditional embeddings.

## Method

NVM identifies that DDIM inversion's error stems from a variance gap: ε_θ(z_{t-1}, t) predicts noise with systematically lower variance than the true forward prediction ε_θ(z_t, t), which can be shown analytically as a negative-scaled-noise perturbation (partial denoising). To correct this, NVM constructs a forward reference latent z_t* = α_t z_0 + σ_t ε and computes a per-channel variance correction factor Δ_var = 1 + |Var(ε_θ(z_t*)) − Var(ε_θ(z_{t-1}))| / γ, then rescales the initial noise prediction before any refinement begins. This warm-start places the process directly in the steady phase, bypassing the landing phase that dominates ReNoise's compute. An attention-guided spatial mask (fusing low-res cross-attention and high-res self-attention via Top-K selection) localizes the variance correction to salient regions, and a lightweight 2-step ReNoise refinement then suppresses residual pixel-level errors. The full pipeline requires roughly 20–45 UNet calls versus 93 for ReNoise, achieving a ~3× speedup while improving CLIP-I by 4.6–10.0 points.

## Results

On HPDv2 with 5 denoising steps, NVM achieves CLIP-I 93.55, MSE 0.0040, PSNR 24.73, and LPIPS 0.1676, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On MS-COCO, it reaches CLIP-I 90.27, MSE 0.0058, PSNR 23.30, and LPIPS 0.1923. In image editing on the PIE benchmark, NVM attains the best background preservation (LPIPS 0.1406, SSIM 0.8526) and highest controllability (CLIP-T whole 32.55, edited 28.10). Efficiency-wise, NVM requires only 20–45 total UNet calls versus 93 for ReNoise, yielding roughly a 3× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NVM outperforms all seven baselines on every reconstruction metric across both HPDv2 and MS-COCO. The closest competitor is EDICT (exact coupled-noise inversion), which NVM surpasses by +3.14 CLIP-I and +2.50 PSNR on HPDv2, and by +4.60 CLIP-I and +2.47 PSNR on MS-COCO, while also halving LPIPS (0.1676 vs 0.3195). ReNoise, the primary iterative-refinement baseline, is beaten by +4.58 CLIP-I on HPDv2 and +9.95 CLIP-I on MS-COCO, with NVM requiring only 2 refinement steps versus ReNoise's 9 per timestep (20 vs 93 total UNet calls). FreeInv and EasyInv, both lightweight heuristic methods, trail NVM by roughly 10 CLIP-I points on HPDv2. BDIA and BELM (bidirectional exact-inversion schemes) perform substantially worse, with BELM collapsing to 53.56 CLIP-I on HPDv2. On the PIE editing benchmark, NVM achieves the best background preservation (LPIPS 0.1406 vs 0.1651 for DDIM) and highest controllability (CLIP-T 32.55/28.10 vs 30.50/26.76 for ReNoise), while ReNoise's aggressive editability strategy degrades its SSIM to 0.7741.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DDIM inversion (Song et al.) and ReNoise (Garibi et al., ECCV 2024), identifying that ReNoise's K-step iterative refinement is dominated by a short 'landing phase' where predicted noise variance rapidly corrects a systematic underestimation inherent to DDIM's asymmetric inversion (predicting noise from z_{t-1} rather than z_t). Rather than iterating to convergence, the authors propose Noise Variance Matching (NVM), which explicitly rescales the initial noise prediction to match a forward reference's variance, effectively warm-starting refinement near the steady phase. This positions the work as a targeted, training-free correction to a specific failure mode of DDIM inversion, distinct from exact-inversion methods (EDICT, BDIA, BELM) that pursue mathematical precision, and from optimization-based approaches that learn conditional embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a ~3× reduction in UNet calls over ReNoise while achieving superior reconstruction fidelity (e.g., +4.6 CLIP-I on HPDv2, +10.0 on MS-COCO) and the best editing controllability on the PIE benchmark, all while preserving DDIM's deterministic, skip-step property. The finding that a simple 1.01× noise rescaling captures most of the benefit of 9 iterative refinement steps suggests that much of the computational overhead in current iterative inversion methods is spent correcting a single, identifiable bias. Open questions include whether the attention-guided spatial masking is essential or an over-engineered refinement (the ablation shows it slightly hurts LPIPS), whether the approach transfers cleanly to video diffusion or 3D generation, and whether the variance-matching principle generalizes to other asymmetric inversion formulations beyond DDIM.
