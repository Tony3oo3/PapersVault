## Problem

3DGS training remains expensive because the backward pass dominates per-iteration cost (~62%) in the post-densification refinement phase, yet many sampled views have near-plateaued losses that yield diminishing gradient returns. Existing efficiency methods target the primitive dimension (reducing or controlling Gaussian count), leaving the orthogonal axis of when to backpropagate largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261858/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SkipGS consumes the same inputs as standard 3DGS (RGB training images, camera poses, and an SfM-initialized point cloud) and produces identical outputs (a fixed set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians and rendered novel views). The method is a pure training-schedule modification: it does not alter the renderer, Gaussian representation, loss function, or optimizer, so the final Gaussian count and rendering-time workload are unchanged from the underlying baseline. The only internal state added is a per-view exponential moving average of observed loss, updated unconditionally after every forward pass.

## Method

SkipGS introduces a view-adaptive backward gating mechanism that activates after densification stops (T_d = 15K). At each post-densification iteration, the forward pass is always executed to compute the view's loss and update its per-view EMA baseline; a scale-invariant deviation score s = L_v^(t) / (EMA_v^(t-1) + epsilon) then determines whether the view's loss has risen above its recent baseline (s > 1 triggers backward, s <= 1 proposes skipping). To prevent gradient starvation, a minimum backward budget rho_min is auto-calibrated from a 500-iteration warmup window via lower-bounded linear interpolation of the observed natural backward fraction, and the cumulative backward ratio is checked before each skip decision to force backward execution when the budget is violated. Because the mechanism operates solely at the scheduling level without modifying any model component, it is plug-and-play and composable with orthogonal accelerations such as Gaussian pruning or budgeted densification, yielding additive wall-clock speedups (e.g., 42% post-densification time reduction on vanilla 3DGS) with negligible quality loss.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, SkipGS reduces end-to-end training time by 23.1% (1705.7 s → 1311.1 s) and post-densification time by 42.0% (939.6 s → 545.0 s) over vanilla 3DGS, while maintaining identical PSNR (27.52) and SSIM (0.816) with a negligible LPIPS increase (+0.002). Consistent speedups are observed on Deep Blending (21.2% end-to-end, 37.6% post-densification) and Tanks&Temples (20.2% end-to-end, 36.8% post-densification). When applied on top of existing efficient 3DGS baselines, SkipGS yields additional wall-clock savings of 5.7%–27.3% depending on the base method, with reconstruction quality changes within ±0.05 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS, SkipGS achieves a 23.1% end-to-end and 42.0% post-densification speedup on Mip-NeRF 360 with essentially zero quality loss (PSNR unchanged at 27.52, SSIM unchanged at 0.816). Against Taming 3DGS, the largest additive gain is observed: 27.3% end-to-end and 48.2% post-densification speedup on Mip-NeRF 360 (757 s → 392 s), with only a 0.02 dB PSNR drop. Against FastGS, SkipGS provides a 9.6% end-to-end and 20.0% post-densification speedup (87.2 s → 69.8 s), making FastGS + SkipGS the fastest post-densification pipeline across all three benchmarks. Against GaussianSpa, LightGaussian, and Speedy-Splat, SkipGS adds 5.7%, 14.7%, and 6.3% end-to-end speedups respectively, with quality changes within ±0.05 dB PSNR and ±0.002 LPIPS. Across all six baselines and three datasets, SkipGS never degrades PSNR by more than 0.09 dB, confirming that the backward-gating mechanism is orthogonal to primitive-count and rendering-pipeline optimizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkipGS builds directly on the two-phase training structure of 3DGS (densification followed by post-densification refinement) and targets an axis orthogonal to all prior 3DGS acceleration work. Existing methods—Gaussian compaction (FastGS, GaussianSpa, LightGaussian, Speedy-Splat) and growth control (Taming 3DGS)—optimize the primitive dimension by reducing or regulating the number of Gaussians per iteration. SkipGS instead operates at the backward-gating level, exploiting per-view loss convergence heterogeneity to selectively skip backpropagation, and is explicitly designed as a plug-in that composes additively with those prior methods without modifying the renderer, representation, or loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 23% end-to-end and 42% post-densification training-time reductions with negligible quality loss make 3DGS fitting meaningfully cheaper for interactive capture, large-scale benchmarking, and downstream pipelines that require repeated scene fitting. The plug-and-play design and demonstrated additive speedups across five diverse baselines suggest broad adoptability without re-tuning. However, the evaluation is limited to static scenes; whether the per-view EMA gating remains effective under dynamic scene modeling (e.g., 4DGS) or with different optimizer schedules is an open question. The ablation also reveals that the budget controller is essential—removing it causes 0.4–0.7 dB PSNR drops—highlighting that naive skipping is not a viable alternative.
