## Problem

Existing style transfer methods capture either conspicuous artistic brushstrokes or only coarse global lightness/tint, falling far short of the nuanced photographic style that professional photographers apply (subtle continuous tone/color shifts, smooth spatial effects like vignetting). Prior image embeddings target semantics or apparent artistic features and are trained with discrete supervision (text prompts, class labels), making them insensitive to the small but perceptible continuous variations that define photographic style.

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image (evaluated at 1080×1920) and a reference style image (512×512) as input, both converted to CIE-Lab color space, and produces a stylized image in a single forward pass (0.23 s) without any test-time optimization. The photographic embedding network also outputs a compact style vector that can be used independently for style retrieval, clustering, or linear interpolation between two reference styles. The transfer module is a 5D neural LUT parameterized by pixel color in CIE-Lab plus 2D spatial coordinates, so the output is a spatially-varying color mapping that preserves local texture.

## Method

PETAL consists of two stages. First, a ViT-based embedding network extracts a photographic style vector by tokenizing CIE-Lab patches, computing differentiable luminance and chromaticity histograms via inverse-quadratic kernels, and injecting histogram features into the ViT through cross-attention; the final embedding concatenates the [CLS] token with adaptively pooled patch tokens. The embedding is trained self-supervisedly with a contrastive objective that uses overlapping local crops as positives and pairs of the same crop under opposite photographic style augmentations (exposure, saturation, tint, highlight, shadow) as hard negatives, stabilized by a SimSiam-style stop-gradient and a Hellinger-distance histogram reconstruction loss. Second, a lightweight 5D neural LUT (1×1 CNN encoder/decoder) transfers style by applying an embedding-conditioned AdaIN-like re-normalization to per-pixel features, where the mean and variance shifts are computed as the difference between the reference and content embeddings, reducing to near-identity when the two images share similar style.

## Results

On the PPR10K subset, PETAL achieves the best LPIPS (0.1027), PSNR (21.80 dB), and SSIM (0.8710) among all methods, and on PST50 it attains the best LPIPS (0.1021), PSNR (24.63 dB), SSIM (0.9272), and H-Corr (0.5220). For photographic style retrieval, the proposed embedding reaches R@1 of 74.27% on PPR10K, 52.94% on FiveK-Concept, and 62.50% on INSRANK, surpassing the strongest baseline (ColorD at 65.80/45.45/46.88%) and even fine-tuned DINOv3* (59.30/34.72/34.38%). PETAL processes a 1080×1920 image in 0.23 s, making it the second-fastest open-source method. In the user study, PETAL ranks first in content preservation (4.65/5, tied with Deep Preset) and style consistency (4.71/5), and second in overall quality (4.44/5, behind Nano Banana Pro's 4.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PETAL outperforms all 23 baselines on the primary style-transfer metrics: it achieves the lowest LPIPS and highest PSNR on both PPR10K and PST50, beating the strongest closed-source competitor Neural Preset by 2.0 dB in PSNR (21.80 vs. 19.12 on PPR10K; 24.63 vs. 22.61 on PST50) and by 0.035 in LPIPS. Among open-source methods, Deep Preset is the closest on PSNR (20.88 vs. 21.80 on PPR10K) but trails substantially in LPIPS (0.1717 vs. 0.1027) and H-Corr, while SALUT, StyTr², PhotoWCT², and DSTN all produce severe content distortion (PSNR 16–19 dB). For the embedding retrieval task, PETAL's R@1 of 74.27% on PPR10K exceeds ColorD (65.80%), ALADIN (32.44%), and even fine-tuned DINOv3* (59.30%) and CLIP* (54.42%), confirming that the ViT + CIE-Lab histogram architecture captures photographic style better than general-purpose or text-supervised encoders. On INSRANK, PETAL achieves the highest Spearman's ρ (52.19) and Kendall's τ (46.88), indicating the strongest alignment with professional photographer rankings. The only metric where PETAL does not lead is overall user-study quality (4.44 vs. Nano Banana Pro's 4.47), though Nano Banana Pro sacrifices content preservation (3.74 vs. 4.65) to achieve that score.

## Relation to Prior Work

PETAL builds on the SimSiam stop-gradient framework for stable self-supervised contrastive learning and the differentiable histogram computation from HistoGAN, but departs from both by introducing photographic style augmentation (PSA) to generate hard negatives specific to continuous tone/color adjustments. It positions itself against two lines of work: general-purpose image embeddings (CLIP, DINOv2) that capture semantics but miss subtle photographic style, and LUT-based retouching methods (SALUT, Neural Preset, Modflow) that avoid texture distortion yet produce unrealistic results due to misaligned style representations. The key architectural departure is the CIE-Lab color space with dedicated luminance/chromaticity histogram encoders injected via cross-attention into a ViT backbone, combined with a lightweight 5D neural LUT (1×1 CNNs) conditioned on the learned embedding for test-time-optimization-free transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learned photographic embedding serves a dual purpose—enabling style-similar image retrieval and conditioning a fast (0.23 s) transfer LUT—making it practically useful for photo-editing pipelines that require reference-based retouching without per-image optimization. The paper's ablations and fine-tuned-baseline comparisons suggest that architectural inductive bias (CIE-Lab decomposition, histogram statistics) matters more than training data scale for capturing continuous photographic style, a finding that could inform future representation-learning designs for fine-grained visual attributes. Open questions include extending to object-level retouching (e.g., face-specific color adjustments), handling artistic elements like grain or noise, and whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-reference style blending.
