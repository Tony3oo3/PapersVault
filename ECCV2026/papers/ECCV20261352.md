## Problem

Dynamic surface reconstruction from multi-view video remains difficult because Lagrangian methods (deformation fields over canonical templates) cannot handle topological changes or highly complex non-rigid motion, while Eulerian 4D volume methods lack temporal regularization and produce flickering, unstable meshes. No existing approach simultaneously recovers temporally coherent, high-fidelity explicit geometry under large deformations and topological changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261352/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RGB videos (32–38 training views per timestamp on SynMotion-360, 31 cameras on DiVa-360, 10 cameras on CMU Panoptic), per-frame foreground masks, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It produces, at each timestamp, an explicit triangle mesh extracted from a 96³ SDF grid via differentiable FlexiCubes, per-vertex PBR material attributes (diffuse albedo, metallic, roughness, self-occlusion), and a shared learnable environment map. Training runs for 5,000 iterations (~1–3 hours) on a single RTX 4090 with 19.7 GB peak VRAM.

## Method

The core insight is that the SDF value at any fixed spatial coordinate traces a continuous 1D temporal trajectory as the scene deforms, so dynamic geometry can be reformulated as fitting per-node 1D signals rather than learning a 3D deformation field. Each node of a 96³ grid stores a compact set of basis-function coefficients—low-order polynomials for global trends plus 18–100 Fourier terms for high-frequency motion—so the full spatio-temporal SDF is parameterized by a single coefficient matrix. At query time, evaluating these basis functions yields a static SDF field from which an explicit mesh is extracted via differentiable FlexiCubes. Appearance is modeled separately by a temporal tri-plane of three multi-resolution hash grids (xy, xz, yz) decoded by a small MLP into PBR attributes, which together with the mesh are rendered by a differentiable PBR shader (Nvdiffrec). End-to-end optimization minimizes a combined photometric, curve-smoothness, and appearance-regularization loss, with gradients flowing through the renderer, mesh extraction, and basis coefficients simultaneously.

## Results

On the synthetic SynMotion-360 benchmark, p-SDF achieves the best Chamfer Distance (1.157) and Edge Chamfer Distance (3.170) among all baselines, with an F1 of 0.893 (slightly below NeuS2's 0.908). On CMU Panoptic Studio, p-SDF dramatically outperforms all baselines in both reconstruction accuracy (CD = 0.026 vs. 1.041 for the next-best DG-Mesh) and mesh quality (AR = 4.983 vs. 19.359 for AT-GS; MA = 0.678 vs. 11.353). On real-world DiVa-360, p-SDF achieves the best rendering quality (PSNR 27.584, SSIM 0.957, LPIPS 0.049) and the best mesh quality (AR 3.126, MA 0.396), surpassing AT-GS by ~14.1 in AR and ~10.8 in MA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

p-SDF is compared against four baselines spanning two paradigms: Global Deformation (DG-Mesh, Dynamic-2DGS) and Incremental Optimization (NeuS2, AT-GS). On geometry, p-SDF wins decisively on CMU Panoptic Studio (CD 0.026 vs. 1.041–7.654) and DiVa-360 mesh quality (AR 3.126 vs. 19.2–23.2), where template-based and frame-propagation methods accumulate errors or produce degenerate triangles. On SynMotion-360, p-SDF achieves the best CD and ECD but trails NeuS2 slightly on F1 (0.893 vs. 0.908), the only geometry metric it does not lead. On rendering, p-SDF is marginally behind AT-GS on SynMotion-360 PSNR (32.153 vs. 32.671) and SSIM (0.974 vs. 0.977) but leads on LPIPS (0.028 vs. 0.045); on real-world DiVa-360, p-SDF wins all three rendering metrics (PSNR 27.584, SSIM 0.957, LPIPS 0.049) over AT-GS (27.418, 0.956, 0.067). The paper attributes AT-GS's rendering edge on synthetic data to its adaptive-density Gaussians optimized for texture, while p-SDF's PBR-aligned appearance model provides more robust generalization on real scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

p-SDF departs from the two dominant paradigms in dynamic surface reconstruction—Lagrangian deformation (DG-Mesh, Dynamic 2DGS) and incremental frame-to-frame optimization (NeuS2, AT-GS)—by reformulating the problem as 1D temporal signal fitting at each spatial grid node. It builds directly on FlexiCubes for differentiable iso-surfacing, Nvdiffrec for PBR rendering, and Grid4D-style multi-resolution hash grids for the appearance field, but the core novelty is the parametric basis-function representation (polynomial + Fourier) that replaces per-frame SDF grids or deformation MLPs. This positions p-SDF as an Eulerian method that avoids the fixed-topology constraint of deformation approaches and the error accumulation of incremental methods, while being more computationally tractable than the integral-based SDFFlow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parametric SDF formulation offers a clean, principled way to enforce temporal coherence without sacrificing topological flexibility, addressing a long-standing tension in dynamic reconstruction. The large mesh-quality gap (AR/MA) over Gaussian-based baselines suggests that end-to-end differentiable iso-surfacing with direct photometric supervision is a more reliable path to clean geometry than post-hoc extraction pipelines. However, the method requires calibrated multi-view input, does not provide point-to-point correspondences, and incurs higher VRAM (19.7 GB) due to the dense coefficient grid—open questions include scalability to longer sequences, monocular robustness, and whether the basis-function approach can be extended to provide explicit tracking.
