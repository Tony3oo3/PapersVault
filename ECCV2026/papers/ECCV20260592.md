## Problem

Existing superquadric decomposition methods suffer from three coupled limitations: they rely on Chamfer-distance supervision that produces gaps and suboptimal shape fitting, they predict only rigid (convex, symmetric) primitives that cannot represent bent or tapered geometries, and they lack robustness to the partial, occluded point clouds typical of real-world sensing. SuperFlex addresses all three by introducing a new loss, deformable primitives, and a robustness training strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260592/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud of 4096 points (sampled via Farthest Point Sampling) as input and outputs a set of up to 16 deformable superquadric primitives, each parameterized by 19 coefficients (3 translation, 3 rotation, 3 scale, 2 shape, 2 tapering, 6 bending) plus an existence probability, along with a soft N×P segmentation assignment matrix mapping each input point to its most likely primitive. For the partial-cloud variant, the input is a noisy, occluded point cloud extracted from depth maps and instance masks, and the model still predicts the complete set of primitives for the full object.

## Method

SuperFlex uses a Transformer-based architecture (PVCNN encoder + set-prediction decoder, following SuperDec) but introduces three key changes. First, it replaces Chamfer-distance supervision with a joint loss combining a differentiable IoU term (enforcing global volumetric occupancy via soft indicator functions over a sampled point cloud) and an SDF surface term (providing local geometric gradients within a narrow band), plus overlap, sparsity, and existence regularizers. Second, it extends each superquadric with parametric bending (circular-arc warping along three axes) and tapering (progressive narrowing along two axes), adding 8 parameters per primitive to capture curved and asymmetric parts. Third, for robustness to partial observations, it uses high-quality test-time-optimized decompositions of complete shapes as pseudo-ground truth and fine-tunes the network with Hungarian-matched Chamfer distance plus an auxiliary rigid-shape prior, enabling complete primitive prediction from occluded inputs.

## Results

On ShapeNet, SuperFlex with tapering and bending deformations achieves an IoU of 0.72, F-score of 0.37, L1 Chamfer distance of 1.54×10⁻², and L2 of 0.043×10⁻² using only 5.64 primitives on average, at a runtime of 8.2 ms. With test-time optimization, IoU further improves to 0.87 and F-score to 0.49. On partial point clouds from Aria Synthetic Environments, the occlusion-aware fine-tuned variant reaches IoU 0.54, more than doubling the 0.20 IoU of the unsupervised model and all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperFlex outperforms all five baselines on ShapeNet across every metric. Against the closest learning-based competitor SuperDec [10], SuperFlex improves IoU from 0.59 to 0.72 (+22%) and F-score from 0.28 to 0.37, while using a comparable number of primitives (5.64 vs. 5.79) at the same 7.5 ms runtime. The optimization-based Marching Primitives [18] achieves a lower IoU (0.56) and requires 4.3× more primitives (24.10) and is roughly 20,000× slower (163 s vs. 8 ms). EMS [17] and CSA [32] trail further behind with IoU of 0.40 and 0.42 respectively, and the earliest learning-based method SQ [24] achieves only 0.27 IoU. On partial point clouds (ASE), the fine-tuned SuperFlex variant (IoU 0.54) more than doubles the performance of all baselines, which plateau at IoU 0.13–0.20 without occlusion-aware training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperFlex builds directly on SuperDec [10], a recent transformer-based feed-forward method for superquadric decomposition, extending it along three axes: replacing Chamfer-distance supervision with a joint differentiable IoU + SDF loss, augmenting rigid superquadrics with bending and tapering deformations (adding 8 parameters to reach 19 per primitive), and introducing a pseudo-ground-truth fine-tuning stage for robustness to partial point clouds. The broader lineage traces from classical optimization-based fitting (EMS, Marching Primitives, SuperFrusta) through early learning-based approaches (SQ, CSA) to SuperDec's category-agnostic set-prediction formulation, which SuperFlex retains architecturally (PVCNN encoder + transformer decoder) while modifying the training objective and primitive parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in 3D scene understanding: superquadrics offer compact, interpretable primitives for robotics and manipulation, but prior methods could not faithfully represent curved or tapered parts and failed on the partial, noisy observations typical of real-world sensors. The 8-parameter deformation extension and the pseudo-ground-truth fine-tuning strategy (IoU jumping from 0.20 to 0.54 on partial ASE clouds) make the representation more applicable to deployment settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales from single objects to full multi-object scenes without per-object optimization, whether the Hungarian-matching supervision generalizes to highly occluded or out-of-distribution categories, and whether the test-time refinement (5 s per object) can be further accelerated for real-time use.
