## Problem

As visual token counts grow in high-resolution images and long videos, the visual attention sink phenomenon causes irrelevant tokens to absorb disproportionate attention mass, severely degrading fine-grained perception. Existing zooming-based remedies predict bounding boxes or temporal spans as discrete text tokens and require additional forward passes to crop and re-encode the selected region, introducing both unreliable numeric localization and substantial computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262814/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a high-resolution image (up to 4096×28×28 pixels, yielding 128–4096 visual tokens) or a long video (up to 16,384 visual tokens at 128×28×28 per frame) together with a textual query. During generation, the model first emits a short sequence of <VR> tokens (1 for images, 4 for videos) whose last-layer hidden states directly replace the next input embedding rather than being projected to vocabulary logits, before switching to standard text decoding to produce the final answer. The reflection tokens thus act as continuous latent anchors in the same embedding space as the visual input, not as decodable text.

## Method

VisReflect introduces three special tokens (<BOR>, <VR>, <EOR>) and trains the LLM to generate a sequence of <VR> tokens whose hidden states are aligned—via a cosine-similarity loss—to the visual tokens falling inside a ground-truth bounding box (images) or uniformly sampled frames within a temporal span (videos). Crucially, in 'visual reflection mode' the hidden state of each <VR> token replaces the next input embedding (Eq. 9), so the reflection sequence propagates attention mass back toward the corresponding salient visual tokens within a single forward pass, without any coordinate prediction, cropping, or re-encoding. Training is two-stage (image then video) with the vision encoder and projector frozen, optimizing a combined objective of standard cross-entropy for answer generation plus a weighted cosine alignment loss (λ=0.1). At inference the model simply appends <BOR> to the prompt, generates a fixed number of <VR> tokens, and is forced to emit <EOR>, after which it decodes the answer—achieving comparable accuracy to multi-pass zooming methods while cutting video inference time by roughly 44%.

## Results

On high-resolution image benchmarks, VisReflect achieves 57.1 on BLINK, 73.8 on HRBench-4K, 70.1 on HRBench-8K, and 84.9 overall on V*, yielding average gains of +4.1% over the Qwen2.5-VL backbone. On long-video benchmarks, it reaches 70.6 on MLVU, 70.4 on MVBench, and 67.4/72.7 on VideoMME (without/with subtitles), a +1.8% average improvement over strong single-pass baselines. Compared to multi-pass zooming-based video methods, VisReflect matches their accuracy while reducing inference latency by approximately 44% (e.g., 18.5s vs. 32.5–37.1s on MLVU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5-VL backbone, VisReflect improves on every image benchmark (BLINK +0.6, HRBench-4K +2.9, HRBench-8K +3.2, V* +5.8) and every video benchmark (MLVU +0.8, MVBench +2.0, VideoMME +2.1/+2.0). Among single-pass image baselines, VisReflect outperforms the strongest runner-up Thyme-VL† by +1.2 on HRBench-4K, +2.3 on HRBench-8K, and +4.3 on V* overall, and also beats DeepEyes†, PixelReasoner, PAPO, and LVR across all four image benchmarks. On video, VisReflect surpasses NVILA (70.1 on MLVU) and VideoChat-R1 (69.5 on MLVU) while also leading on MVBench and VideoMME. Against multi-pass zooming-based video methods (LOVE-R1, VideoChat-R1.5-M, TimeSearch-R), VisReflect achieves comparable accuracy (e.g., 70.6 vs. 70.9/71.5 on MLVU) while cutting inference time by ~44% (18.5s vs. 32.5–37.1s). On image inference speed, VisReflect (2.60s) is competitive with single-pass methods (2.51–3.04s) and substantially faster than multi-pass variants (4.78–4.81s).

## Relation to Prior Work

VisReflect departs from the dominant 'Thinking-with-Images/Videos' paradigm (DeepEyes, Thyme, PixelReasoner, LOVE-R1, TimeSearch-R), which predicts discrete bounding-box or temporal-span coordinates in token space and performs additional forward passes on cropped regions. Instead, it repurposes the latent-reasoning mechanism from Hao et al. (2024)—where hidden states replace token embeddings for intermediate reasoning steps—by aligning those hidden states to visual feature tokens via cosine similarity, thereby recalling salient visual content in continuous embedding space within a single forward pass. The paper also directly addresses the visual attention sink phenomenon identified by Kang et al. (2025), positioning its reflection tokens as an implicit attention-refocusing signal rather than an explicit localization tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44% inference-time reduction while matching multi-pass zooming methods offers a practically attractive efficiency path for deploying LVLMs on high-resolution and long-video workloads where latency is critical. The ablation finding that a single reflection token suffices for image tasks—acting as an attention-refocusing cue rather than a dense visual reconstruction—challenges the assumption that fine-grained perception requires re-encoding cropped regions. Open questions include whether the fixed reflection length can be made adaptive to query complexity, whether the approach generalizes beyond the Qwen-2.5-VL-7B backbone, and whether richer spatiotemporal video annotations could unlock stronger gains in the video setting where improvements remain modest (+1.8%).
