## Problem

3D scene understanding remains dominated by specialized architectures (sparse U-Nets, local-attention variants) with strong domain-specific inductive biases, isolating the field from the broader Transformer ecosystem and its rapidly improving hardware/software optimizations. This paper asks whether a vanilla Transformer encoder, with minimal modifications, can serve as a competitive and scalable 3D backbone, and how to train it effectively given the limited scale of current 3D supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud with per-point coordinates and features (e.g., RGB color), voxelizes it at 2 cm (indoor) or 5 cm (outdoor) resolution, and partitions the occupied voxels into non-overlapping 5×5×5 cubic patches (10 cm or 25 cm side length). Unlike ViT with a fixed token count, the sequence length varies per scene (typically ~5,000 tokens), and the output is produced by a single transposed convolution that upsamples patch tokens back to voxel resolution, followed by a linear head that assigns each point the prediction of its containing voxel.

## Method

Volt tokenizes 3D scenes by voxelizing, partitioning into cubic patches, flattening, and linearly projecting to the embedding dimension, then processes the variable-length token sequence with a standard Transformer encoder using full global self-attention (no local windows, no hierarchical stages), leveraging FlashAttention for efficiency. Positional information is injected via a 3D extension of RoPE that factorizes query and key vectors across the three spatial axes and applies rotary transformations independently per axis, with asymmetric capacity allocation favoring the horizontal plane over the gravity-aligned z-axis. To counter overfitting under limited 3D supervision, the paper introduces a data-efficient training recipe combining strong 3D augmentations (scene mixing, elastic distortions, instance-level transforms), regularization (DropPath, weight decay, label smoothing), and knowledge distillation from a convolutional U-Net teacher. The key empirical finding is that Volt scales more favorably with increased supervision than domain-specific backbones: joint training across multiple indoor/outdoor datasets yields consistent gains, with Volt-B achieving state-of-the-art results across indoor and outdoor semantic and instance segmentation benchmarks while being 2× faster and using 48% less memory than PTv3.

## Results

On indoor semantic segmentation, Volt-B (87.7M params) achieves new state-of-the-art results of 80.5 mIoU on ScanNet test, 41.6 mIoU on ScanNet200 test, and 49.5 mIoU on ScanNet++ test under multi-dataset joint training. Even the smaller Volt-S (23.7M params) surpasses the prior SOTA PTv3 (46.1M) on ScanNet200 val (36.2 vs. 35.2 mIoU) and ScanNet++ test (49.3 vs. 48.8 mIoU) while being 2× smaller and faster. On outdoor benchmarks, Volt-B reaches 82.2 mIoU on nuScenes val, 72.5 on SemanticKITTI val, and 73.4 on Waymo val. For instance segmentation, Volt-B as a drop-in backbone in SPFormer achieves 82.7 mAP50 on ScanNet test, 47.5 on ScanNet200 test, and 54.9 on ScanNet++ test, all new bests.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under single-dataset training, Volt-S (23.7M) outperforms the prior SOTA PTv3 (46.1M) on ScanNet200 val (36.2 vs. 35.2 mIoU) and ScanNet++ test (49.3 vs. 48.8 mIoU) while being roughly 2× smaller and faster, and matches PTv3 on ScanNet val (77.2 vs. 77.5). With multi-dataset joint training, Volt-B (87.7M) surpasses PPT (124.8M, which additionally uses Structured3D) on ScanNet test (80.5 vs. 79.8 mIoU) and ScanNet200 test (41.6 vs. 41.4 mIoU). On outdoor benchmarks, Volt-S already beats PTv3 on all three validation sets (nuScenes 81.0 vs. 80.4, SemanticKITTI 70.5 vs. 69.1, Waymo 71.5 vs. 71.3), and Volt-B extends these leads to 82.2, 72.5, and 73.4 mIoU respectively. For instance segmentation, replacing MinkUNet with Volt-B in the SPFormer pipeline yields 82.7 mAP50 on ScanNet test, surpassing the previous best (Relation3D at 81.6) by 1.1 points, and achieves 47.5 mAP50 on ScanNet200 test and 54.9 on ScanNet++ test, both new records. Notably, Volt's gains from the data-efficient training recipe (+9.0 mIoU from naive to full recipe) far exceed PTv3's (+2.3 mIoU), indicating that the vanilla Transformer architecture benefits more from increased supervision than domain-specific 3D backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

Volt directly extends the vanilla Vision Transformer (ViT) to 3D by replacing 2D image patches with volumetric patch tokens and 2D RoPE with a 3D factorized rotary embedding, while retaining full global self-attention and a standard Transformer encoder. It deliberately departs from the dominant 3D backbone paradigm—U-Net hierarchies with sparse convolutions (MinkUNet) or local-attention windows (PTv3, OctFormer, Swin3D)—in favor of a single-resolution, globally-attending architecture with no domain-specific grouping. The training recipe adapts data-efficient ViT strategies from 2D (DeiT-style distillation, DropPath, label smoothing) to 3D, and the 3D RoPE formulation is concurrent with LitePT but applied within a fully global-attention setting rather than a local one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Volt demonstrates that architectural simplicity (a vanilla Transformer encoder) combined with a strong training recipe can match or exceed specialized 3D backbones while being 2× faster and using 48% less memory, and that it scales more favorably with additional supervision (+9.0 mIoU vs. +2.3 mIoU for PTv3 under the same recipe upgrades). This positions 3D scene understanding to benefit directly from the rapidly advancing Transformer software/hardware ecosystem (FlashAttention, optimized kernels) rather than remaining isolated in sparse-convolution tooling. Key open questions include whether Volt's scaling advantage persists at truly large 3D data scales (current datasets remain orders of magnitude smaller than ImageNet), whether the distillation-from-a-weaker-teacher effect generalizes beyond the tested settings, and how the architecture behaves under self-supervised or multi-modal pretraining regimes that the broader Transformer ecosystem is already exploring.
