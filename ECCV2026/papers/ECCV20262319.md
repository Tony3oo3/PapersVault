## Problem

Existing watermarking methods for diffusion models either operate as post-hoc pixel-domain modifications vulnerable to regeneration attacks, or treat latent tensors as flat spatial feature maps without exploiting their internal frequency structure. No prior approach explicitly leverages the complementary low-frequency (semantic) and high-frequency (textural) spectral bands of diffusion latents for learned, dual-band redundant watermark embedding, leaving watermarks susceptible to stochastic regeneration and common distortions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262319/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean latent tensor z₀ ∈ R^{C×H×W} produced by the reverse diffusion process (from 256×256 RGB images encoded via a frozen VAE) and a binary watermark message w ∈ {−1,+1}^{d_w} of 32–128 bits. It outputs a watermarked latent z̃₀ that decodes to a perceptually indistinguishable image, and at extraction time recovers the binary message from a potentially distorted or regenerated image by re-encoding to latent, applying channel-wise DCT, splitting into spectral bands, and averaging predictions from two independent decoders. The embedding is applied pre-decoding so the watermark becomes intrinsic to the generative representation rather than a post-processing overlay.

## Method

BiSLW decomposes the diffusion latent via channel-wise 2D DCT and splits the spectrum into low-frequency (semantic) and high-frequency (textural) bands using a radial binary mask (r = 0.25). Two lightweight CNN perturbation networks, each conditioned on the watermark message via FiLM modulation, independently inject the shared identity signal into both bands with different embedding strengths (α_L = 0.8 > α_H = 0.3), and the modified bands are recombined and inverse-DCT'd before VAE decoding. At extraction, two spectral decoders recover the watermark from each band and their outputs are averaged; a cross-band consistency loss (‖ŵ_L − ŵ_H‖²) enforces alignment between the two pathways during training. The full system is trained end-to-end with a multi-objective loss combining watermark recovery, cross-band consistency, latent fidelity, and a differentiable robustness term that samples from an attack pool (JPEG, blur, noise, crop, diffusion regeneration) at each step, while the diffusion backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On AI-generated images (32-bit watermark), BiSLW achieves PSNR 37.40 dB / SSIM 0.91 with 1 ms embedding overhead, and sustains 0.98 bit accuracy under the combined attack (all nine distortions applied sequentially). On the CLIC dataset (48-bit), it reaches 0.94 combined-attack accuracy while improving PSNR over the best prior method (FNNS, 36.84 dB). In generative-fidelity terms, BiSLW attains FID 9.0 (vs. vanilla Stable Diffusion 8.7), the lowest KL latent shift (0.018), and 0.96/0.92 bit accuracy at regeneration timesteps t*=250/500, outperforming LaWa (0.94/0.89) and Tree-Ring (0.88/0.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest latent-diffusion baselines, BiSLW clearly outperforms LaWa by ~3 dB in PSNR (37.40 vs. 34.25) and by 0.8 FID points (9.0 vs. 9.8) while matching its combined-attack accuracy, and surpasses Tree-Ring by ~3.8 dB in PSNR and 0.08 in regeneration accuracy at t*=250 (0.96 vs. 0.88). Classical frequency-domain methods (DCT-DWT) achieve higher clean PSNR (39.47 dB) but collapse to near-chance accuracy (~0.51) under virtually every geometric or compression distortion, whereas BiSLW holds 0.98 under the combined attack. Neural pixel-domain methods (HiDDeN, RivaGAN) suffer severe degradation on JPEG (0.53) and combined attacks (0.59), and RoSteALS drops to 0.50 combined accuracy. SSL matches BiSLW's robustness but requires 870 ms per image versus BiSLW's 1 ms—a three-orders-of-magnitude speed gap. On the CLIC 48-bit benchmark, BiSLW edges out FNNS (the best prior method at 36.84 dB PSNR) in both quality and combined-attack robustness (0.94 vs. 0.91). Overall, BiSLW wins or ties on every robustness metric while maintaining the best perceptual fidelity among all watermarking methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiSLW carves new ground by repurposing the frequency-domain decomposition idea (channel-wise DCT) from classical signal processing and the learned embedding/extraction paradigm from neural watermarking (HiDDeN, SSL) into a dual-band latent-space framework for diffusion models. It departs from Tree-Ring, which embeds fixed Fourier patterns in the initial noise and recovers via inversion, by instead learning end-to-end spectral encoders/decoders operating on the decoded latent. It also differs from spatial latent methods (Stable Signature, LaWa) that treat the latent as a monolithic feature map, by explicitly exploiting the low/high-frequency hierarchy within the latent and enforcing cross-band identity consistency. The FiLM conditioning mechanism and residual CNN backbone are borrowed from standard neural watermarking architectures but applied here to spectrally separated latent components rather than raw spatial or pixel data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1 ms embedding overhead and near-perfect bit accuracy under aggressive regeneration (92% at t*=500) make BiSLW practically deployable for provenance tracking in production diffusion pipelines, where post-hoc pixel-domain methods are too slow or fragile. The bi-spectral redundancy principle—binding identity to both semantic layout and texture statistics—offers a general design insight that could transfer to other latent-space security tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to 256×256 images on a single diffusion backbone, two key comparators (WMAdapter, Fourier Integrity) were only qualitatively discussed rather than fully benchmarked, and no adversarial attack specifically targeting the spectral split radius is explored, leaving open whether the method's robustness holds under adaptive attacks.
