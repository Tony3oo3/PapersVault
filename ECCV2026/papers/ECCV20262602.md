## Problem

VLM-driven embodied agents remain vulnerable to implicit contextual risks where benign instructions become hazardous due to subtle environmental states (e.g., a metal container in a microwave). Existing rule-based safeguards face combinatorial explosion in object-dense scenes, while model-based VLM checkers suffer from unfocused perception—either overlooking subtle risk cues or hallucinating dangers from coarse object presence—because they lack explicit visual guidance to identify which objects are actually relevant to the task's safety.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB household scene image and a free-form text instruction as input, and produces a structured 4-step reasoning chain (enclosed in think tags) that includes 2D bounding boxes for interaction targets and constraint areas, followed by a final binary safety verdict with a hazard description and a labeled safety principle from a 33-principle taxonomy. The generated bounding boxes are not merely diagnostic: they double as actionable spatial waypoints that downstream planners can convert into collision-avoidance constraints and safe trajectory generation.

## Method

HomeGuard introduces Context-Guided Chain-of-Thought (CG-CoT), which enforces a strict perception-before-judgment decomposition: the model must first emit bounding boxes for interaction targets and background constraint objects (active perception), then condition its semantic risk assessment on those visual anchors. Training proceeds in two stages—LoRA-based SFT to instill the 4-step reasoning structure and safety principles, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning with a composite reward that includes outcome terms (format, accuracy, semantic consistency, principle alignment) and a process reward computed as IoU between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes for both target and constraint regions. This process reward explicitly supervises intermediate grounding, penalizing any deviation from the required box format and incentivizing the model to verify visual evidence before concluding. The approach is architecture-agnostic and plug-and-play, requiring no modifications to the underlying VLM or downstream planner.

## Results

On HomeSafe-Bench, HomeGuard-8B achieves a Risk Identification Rate (RIR) of 90.98% and Risk Match Rate (RMR) of 74.90%, surpassing the strongest proprietary baseline GPT-4o-mini (88.04% RIR, 39.41% RMR) by 2.94% and 35.49% respectively. For hazard grounding, HomeGuard-8B attains T-IoU of 0.7206 and C-IoU of 0.5562, exceeding Gemini-3-pro (0.6745, 0.5217). Critically, HomeGuard-8B reduces the Oversafety Rate (OR) to 13.14%, a 12.09% absolute reduction over Gemini-3-pro (25.23%) and 42.01% over GPT-4o-mini (55.15%), while maintaining the highest RIR among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HomeGuard-8B outperforms all proprietary VLMs on every metric: it beats GPT-4o-mini by +2.94% RIR and +35.49% RMR, and surpasses Gemini-3-pro by +3.53% RIR, +13.92% RMR, +0.046 T-IoU, +0.035 C-IoU, and −12.09% OR. Against open-source VLMs, the 8B model improves RIR by 23.81% over Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking and 13.81% over the much larger Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking, while the 4B variant already exceeds the 235B model on RIR (90.00% vs. 77.17%). Runtime safeguard baselines (ThinkSafe, Poex, AgentSpec) achieve lower OR (15–28%) but at severe cost to risk detection: their RIR ranges from 44.31% to 66.67%, far below HomeGuard's 90%+. On the four public generalization benchmarks (EARBench, MSSBench, PaSBench, SafeAgentBench), HomeGuard-8B achieves 94.73% RIR on EARBench and 80.77% RIR on SafeAgentBench, matching or exceeding Gemini-3-Pro while maintaining competitive OR, confirming robust transfer to simulator-based and non-household scenes despite no such data in training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two existing lines of embodied safety work: rule-based guardrails (AgentSpec, Poex) that rely on symbolic predicate logic and fail in object-dense scenes, and prompt-engineered VLM checkers (ThinkSafe) that suffer from what the authors term 'unfocused perception.' Conceptually, it builds on the broader VLM-for-embodied-agents trend and the GRPO/LoRA training stack, but its core departure is the CG-CoT mechanism that mandates explicit bounding-box grounding as an intermediate step before semantic risk judgment, coupled with a process-reward RFT stage that supervises those intermediate boxes via IoU. The HomeSafe dataset, constructed through counterfactual image editing on real indoor scenes, extends prior safety benchmarks (EARBench, SafeAgentBench, MSSBench) by adding fine-grained spatial annotations and safe/unsafe contrastive pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic design and the dual output (safety verdict + grounded bounding boxes) make this directly usable as a runtime safety layer for diverse embodied agents, with the boxes serving as actionable spatial waypoints for collision avoidance and safe trajectory generation. Key limitations constrain near-term impact: the framework operates on static 2D images, addresses only environmental (not dynamic/human) hazards, and has not been validated on physical robot platforms. Open questions include whether the grounding-first paradigm transfers to 3D/depth modalities, video-based temporal risk, and long-horizon closed-loop manipulation, all of which the authors flag as future work.
