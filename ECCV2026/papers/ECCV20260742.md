## Problem

Diffusion models require dozens of iterative denoising steps for inference, and existing few-step distillation methods (progressive distillation, consistency distillation, distribution matching) demand millions of training samples and thousands of GPU hours, making post-distillation customization impractical. The paper asks whether a pretrained diffusion model can be accelerated to 8–10 steps with minimal training data while preserving generation quality and generalization to unseen prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260742/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and a Gaussian noise latent, producing a 1024×1024 RGB image in 8 or 10 network forward evaluations (NFE) instead of the base model's 50. At training time, the entire Slow-LoRA and Fast-LoRA pair is fit to a single text-image pair (e.g., a fox image) on one V100 GPU in under one hour, with the base model parameters kept frozen throughout.

## Method

Glance partitions the denoising trajectory into two phases using an SNR-based boundary: a high-noise semantic phase (t ≥ t_s) and a low-noise refinement phase (t < t_s). Two lightweight LoRA adapters are trained separately—Slow-LoRA for the semantic phase and Fast-LoRA for the refinement phase—each supervised by a flow-matching velocity regression loss restricted to its assigned timesteps. LoRA modules are injected into query/key/value/output projections, auxiliary projection layers, and modality-specific MLPs across both visual and textual branches, following the broader placement strategy of Qwen-Image-Distill-LoRA. At inference, the slow-stage timesteps are executed first (one every two steps from the first 10 timesteps), followed by fast-stage timesteps uniformly sampled from the remaining 40, yielding a 5× speedup. The key empirical finding is that this phase-aware decomposition, rather than uniform timestep partitioning or a single LoRA, is what enables stable acceleration from a single training sample.

## Results

On OneIG-Bench, Glance (Qwen, 10×2 NFE) achieves Alignment 0.868, Text 0.734, Style 0.421, and Reasoning 0.303, reaching 92.60% of the 50×2-step Qwen-Image teacher. On HPSv2 prompts, Glance (FLUX, 10 NFE) attains HPSv2.1 of 0.303 vs. 0.309 for the 50-step base (99.67% of teacher), and on GenEval it reaches 96.71% of the teacher score. Critically, all results are obtained with a single training sample and under 1 GPU-hour of training on a V100, compared to 83–3072 GPU-hours and 0.4M–2.3M samples for competing distillation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLUX-based 8-NFE distillation methods (FLUX Turbo, Hyper-FLUX, π-Flow), Glance (FLUX) at 8 NFE trails slightly on FID (34.2 vs. 26.7–29.8) and CLIP (0.259 vs. 0.267–0.268), but at 10 NFE narrows the gap substantially (FID 30.4, CLIP 0.265) while using 10⁶–10⁷× less training data. For Qwen-Image, Glance (10×2 NFE) matches or exceeds the 4-NFE Qwen-Image-Lightning on Alignment (0.868 vs. 0.885) and Reasoning (0.303 vs. 0.311) while using 2× more steps, and outperforms π-Flow (Qwen) on Alignment (0.868 vs. 0.875) with 1/83 the GPU-hours. The most striking comparison is against the uniform-LoRA ablation (same 1 sample, 10×2 NFE): Glance's phase-aware Slow-Fast design lifts Alignment from 0.621 to 0.868 (+24.7 pts) and Text from 0.332 to 0.734 (+40.2 pts), confirming that SNR-guided phase partitioning is essential. The primary weakness is text rendering: Glance lags the Qwen-Image teacher by 0.154 on the Text metric and by 14.7 points on LongText-Bench, with failures concentrated on extremely small or dense characters.

## Relation to Prior Work

Glance builds directly on LoRA-based adaptation of diffusion models and the Timestep Master paradigm of assigning separate LoRA experts to different noise levels, but departs from uniform timestep partitioning by introducing an SNR-guided slow-fast split that reflects the semantic-to-texture progression of denoising. It also contrasts sharply with the dominant distillation literature (progressive distillation, consistency distillation, DMD, adversarial distillation) by avoiding any retraining of a student network: the base model stays frozen and only two lightweight adapters are learned. The flow-matching velocity-prediction objective is retained as the training signal, which the authors argue is what enables extreme data efficiency compared to score-matching or distribution-matching alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: any pretrained flow-matching text-to-image model can be accelerated ~5× with a plug-and-play LoRA pair trained on a single image in under one GPU-hour, removing the multi-thousand-GPU-hour distillation cost that has made few-step variants hard to customize. The main open limitation is text rendering, where Glance shows consistent gaps (0.15–0.23 on OneIG-Bench Text, 13–15 points on LongText-Bench) because high-frequency character details are harder to capture under aggressive step reduction. Whether the 1-sample regime extends to more complex conditioning (video, 3D, multi-modal) or to non-flow-matching diffusion formulations remains unexplored, and the modest FID degradation on COCO-10k (34.2 vs 27.8 for FLUX at 8 vs 50 steps) suggests the quality ceiling is not yet fully matched.
