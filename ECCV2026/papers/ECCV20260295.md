## Problem

The Avatar-As-Image paradigm—representing 3D clothed humans as standardized 2D UV texture and displacement maps for image generative models—has been limited by the absence of high-fidelity SMPL(-X)+D registrations. Existing dataset ground truths exhibit pervasive body-clothing interpenetration, and no public tool produces clean registrations with UV texture from arbitrary single-frame clothed scans, creating a chicken-and-egg problem for learning-based methods that require such registrations for training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260295/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a raw 3D clothed scan (vertices, faces, UV coordinates) of arbitrary quality—including noisy, incomplete, or open-topology captures—and produces a SMPL(-X)+D registered mesh paired with a UV texture map and a UV displacement map at a user-selectable resolution (e.g., 512² to 4096²). The UV maps are semantically aligned across all subjects via the shared SMPL(-X) UV layout, so the same pixel always encodes the same anatomical location, making them directly consumable by pretrained image VAEs and diffusion models without additional alignment. The full pipeline runs in approximately 25 minutes per scan on GPU.

## Method

AvaImg is a four-stage optimization pipeline: (1) multi-view keypoint lifting renders 72 views of the scan, detects 137 OpenPose keypoints per view, and triangulates them via bundle adjustment with three layers of outlier suppression (L1-norm, confidence squaring, hard threshold) for robust pose initialization; (2) physics-aware body fitting enforces body-inside-clothing containment using signed generalized winding numbers rather than surface normals, applying a parametric ReLU to signed mesh-to-scan distances so that outward penetration incurs an amplified penalty, with a three-level efficiency cascade (scan decimation to ~10%, precomputed 5 mm voxel grids, and winding band reduction) cutting runtime by ~10× and storage by ~95%; (3) coarse-to-fine displacement optimization first fits low-resolution per-vertex displacements with topology-independence and region-weighted edge coupling, then applies Loop subdivision and a second pass with unsigned scan-to-mesh data and fourth-power edge coupling to recover sub-centimeter surface detail; (4) precomputed f-map and b-map lookup tables remap the registered mesh into standardized UV texture and displacement images. The key differentiator is the volumetric signed winding number constraint, which robustly classifies inside/outside even on noisy or non-watertight scans where normal-based methods fail.

## Results

AvaImg achieves the lowest penetration rate (19.8%) and penetration depth (3.62 mm) among all baselines, while maintaining competitive scan proximity (9.47 mm). For surface registration, it attains the best bidirectional Chamfer distance of 2.62 mm (vs. 3.06 mm for NICP, the second-best), and for shape estimation on BuFF it reaches 7.72 mm Chamfer to ground-truth minimal-clothing bodies. It is the only method producing UV texture maps, achieving a multiview rendering PSNR of 34.48 dB against original scans. A VAE roundtrip through the frozen FLUX VAE adds only 0.76 mm Chamfer error, confirming the UV maps reside within natural-image distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AvaImg outperforms all five baselines on penetration rate, reducing it from 35.0% (ETCH, next-best) to 19.8%, and from 43–55% for IPNet, NICP, PTF, and RMR. On penetration depth, it achieves 3.62 mm versus 7.80 mm (RMR) and 8.17 mm (PTF), roughly halving the error of the closest competitors. For surface registration Chamfer distance, AvaImg leads at 2.62 mm, beating NICP (3.06 mm) by 14% and RMR (3.42 mm) by 23%; IPNet and PTF trail further at 8.61 mm and 6.92 mm respectively. On shape estimation (BuFF), AvaImg edges out ETCH (7.72 vs. 7.76 mm) and substantially outperforms RMR (10.54 mm) and IPNet (9.95 mm). The only metric where AvaImg is not best is scan proximity: RMR (8.60 mm) and PTF (8.68 mm) are slightly closer to the scan surface than AvaImg (9.47 mm), but the paper argues this is expected and desirable, as a correctly estimated naked body should maintain a physical offset beneath clothing rather than coinciding with it. AvaImg is also the sole method producing UV texture maps (PSNR 34.48 dB), a capability none of the baselines support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SMPL(-X) parametric body model lineage and the generalized winding number formulation of Jacobson et al. (ACM TOG 2013), repurposing the latter as a volumetric inside/outside classifier for body-clothing containment rather than its original mesh segmentation use. It departs from prior clothed-human registration methods (ClothCap, BuFF, RMR) that rely on surface normals or unsigned distances to resolve body-clothing sidedness, replacing them with a signed winding number constraint made practical via a three-level efficiency cascade. The Avatar-As-Image representation (UV texture + displacement maps) follows established work (Tex2Shape, Paint-It, SCULPT), but the paper's core argument is that all prior UV-based methods were bottlenecked by registration quality, and no public pipeline had previously closed that gap with both strict body containment and UV texture output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes that 'ground-truth' registrations shipped with established datasets (BuFF, THuman, 2K2K, CAPE, 4D-Dress) contain pervasive body-clothing interpenetration, undermining the training data and evaluation baselines of numerous downstream learning-based methods and creating a chicken-and-egg problem for the field. The VAE roundtrip validation (0.76 mm added Chamfer error through a frozen FLUX VAE) provides concrete evidence that high-fidelity UV maps are ready-to-use inputs for image generative models, de-risking the broader Avatar-As-Image paradigm for 3D human generation. Open questions include whether the SMPL(-X)+D topology can handle loose or decoupled garments, how self-contact texture bleeding can be mitigated, and whether the ~25-minute-per-scan optimization cost can be amortized to enable feed-forward inference at scale.
