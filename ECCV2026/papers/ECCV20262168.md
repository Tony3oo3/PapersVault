## Problem

Reconstructing relightable PBR face assets from a single in-the-wild image is hindered by two coupled bottlenecks: high-quality PBR training data is scarce (synthetic data lacks realism, real Light Stage scans are expensive and hard to scale), and existing methods either bake scene illumination into textures or rely on 2D screen-space differentiable rasterization that inadvertently bakes occluders (hair, glasses) into the reconstructed surface. Prior attempts to fine-tune diffusion models on small real datasets degrade the generative prior, leading to poor generalization on in-the-wild inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB in-the-wild face image and produces a Hifi3D++ 3DMM parameter vector (identity, expression, texture, spherical-harmonic lighting, camera pose) together with a full set of UV-space PBR maps—albedo, normal, roughness, specular, and displacement—each upscaled from 1K to 4K resolution via a fine-tuned RealESRGAN module. Occlusions such as hair and glasses are explicitly treated as missing regions in the UV texture rather than being projected into the surface, and the final PBR maps are designed for direct use in a physically based rendering pipeline under arbitrary HDR environment lighting.

## Method

A frozen LongCat-Image-Edit diffusion transformer is adapted via three cascaded LoRA modules (rank 32, ~91M params each, 0.7% of backbone) that sequentially perform UV-space texture inpainting, light homogenization, and multi-branch PBR material estimation, all trained with a flow-matching objective on paired data generated by rendering <100 scanned PBR textures onto reconstructed geometry under diverse Blender lighting. The material estimation stage introduces a Cross-Intrinsic Attention mechanism: three parallel LoRA branches (albedo, normal, packed reflectance) share a common backbone but replace standard self-attention with cross-modal attention where queries are modality-specific while keys and values are concatenated across all branches, enforcing spatially aligned micro-structures. Physical plausibility is enforced by a UV-space differentiable GGX BRDF shading loss that re-renders the predicted material maps on a fixed template face and compares against ground-truth shaded textures, avoiding the occlusion-sensitive artifacts of rasterization-based 2D supervision. Geometry estimation uses a Multi-Scale Space Fusion encoder combining a trainable ConvNeXt V2 backbone with a frozen DINOv3 encoder, supervised by a hybrid 88-point landmark loss that stabilizes mouth-region reconstruction.

## Results

On the REALY benchmark, the method achieves an overall NMSE of 1.490 mm, ranking third among all compared geometry methods (behind HiFace at 1.275 and 3DDFA-V3 at 1.435) while outperforming MoSAR (1.500), HRN (1.537), and DECA (2.010). For texture inpainting, the method achieves PSNR 22.44, SSIM 0.8003, LPIPS 0.0621, and CSIM 0.540, substantially surpassing UV-IDM (PSNR 19.39, LPIPS 0.1674) and HRN (PSNR 15.70, LPIPS 0.2982). In light homogenization, the method reaches CSIM 0.4667 and BS 3.963, compared to FFHQ-UV's CSIM 0.1340 and BS 5.738. Material estimation is evaluated qualitatively against MoSAR, FitMe, Relightify, and ChatAvatar, with the paper reporting superior skin-tone fidelity, occlusion robustness, and geometric detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On geometry (REALY NMSE), the method (1.490) beats MoSAR (1.500), HRN (1.537), HiFace without synthetic data (1.558), Deep3D (1.657), and DECA (2.010), but trails HiFace with synthetic data (1.275) and 3DDFA-V3 (1.435); the paper notes HiFace's advantage relies on large-scale synthetic training data unavailable under comparable settings. For texture inpainting, the method dominates both UV-IDM and HRN across all four metrics (PSNR 22.44 vs. 19.39/15.70; LPIPS 0.0621 vs. 0.1674/0.2982), with the gap over HRN particularly large due to HRN's tendency to bake occlusions into the UV map. In light homogenization, the method achieves 3.5× higher CSIM (0.4667 vs. 0.1340) and lower brightness standard deviation (3.963 vs. 5.738) than FFHQ-UV, indicating far more uniform illumination. For material estimation and relighting, the paper provides only qualitative comparisons against MoSAR, FitMe, Relightify, and the commercial ChatAvatar, reporting that the proposed method better preserves skin tone, avoids baking hair into textures, and recovers finer geometric details (wrinkles, moles, beard structure) than all four baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two dominant paradigms in monocular PBR face reconstruction: GAN-based texture prediction (MoSAR, FitMe, AvatarMe) that suffers from mode collapse and over-smoothing, and screen-space differentiable rasterization that bakes occlusion artifacts into textures. It departs from both by operating entirely in UV space with a unified pre-trained diffusion transformer (LongCat-Image-Edit) adapted via cascaded LoRA modules for sequential sub-tasks (inpainting → homogenization → material estimation), and by enforcing physical plausibility through a UV-space differentiable GGX BRDF shader rather than 2D projection losses. The geometry stage builds on the Hifi3D++ 3DMM with a novel MSSF encoder fusing ConvNeXt V2 local features with frozen DINOv3 semantic priors, while the material estimation introduces a Cross-Intrinsic Attention mechanism that has no direct analogue in prior multi-branch texture networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-100-scan training requirement, enabled by Blender-augmented synthetic data and strong diffusion priors, directly addresses the data-scarcity bottleneck that has limited real-world PBR avatar reconstruction. The ablation showing that light homogenization is not merely helpful but essential (without it, the model fails to converge to physically plausible materials even after 40k steps) provides a practical insight for anyone attempting inverse rendering from single images. However, the method is scoped to face/head PBR assets only, treating hair, glasses, and accessories as occlusions, and the geometry results are third-best on REALY rather than SOTA. Open questions include whether the UV-space formulation scales to full-body or dynamic avatars, whether 3DGS-based representations could replace the 3DMM scaffold, and whether the approach generalizes to extreme expressions or non-frontal poses.
