## Problem

Existing VLM-based 3D visual grounding pipelines select camera views using heuristic rules (e.g., object visibility ranking) that ensure candidates are visible but do not account for whether the selected views actually provide discriminative grounding evidence. This is especially problematic for queries involving spatial relations or contextual cues, where the most informative viewpoint may not be the one where the target object is most clearly visible. The paper asks whether view selection can be learned directly from grounding outcomes rather than relying on fixed heuristics.

## Input / Output

The system takes a 3D scene represented as a set of posed RGB frames (from ScanNet), a natural language query, and 3D object proposals generated by Open-YOLO3D (open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation via multi-view 2D detection and point-cloud projection). A Cascade Object Screening stage first filters proposals using CLIP text-embedding similarity and VLM visual verification, reducing the candidate set before grounding. The output is the identified target object, reported as a 3D bounding box (evaluated by IoU on ScanRefer) or top-1 selection accuracy (on NR3D), with k=5 views per candidate fed to the frozen reasoning VLM.

## Method

IVSGround introduces a lightweight view selector (Qwen3-VL-8B fine-tuned with LoRA, rank 8) that predicts query-conditioned influential views for each candidate object, trained entirely from grounding feedback via a two-stage rejection sampling process: (1) per-view evidence assessment where a reasoning VLM (Qwen3-VL-32B) judges whether each view contains discriminative grounding evidence, and (2) comparative feedback collection where pairwise preference queries between candidates yield view-subset-level supervision. At inference, a hybrid strategy combines top-n visibility-ranked views with (k−n) learned influential views, and grounding is performed via pairwise comparative reasoning in a bracket-style elimination tournament using the frozen reasoning VLM. The key design choice is that the reasoning VLM remains entirely frozen; all adaptation occurs in the view selector, which is trained via standard token-level cross-entropy on rejection-sampled positive examples without any manual view-level annotations.

## Results

On ScanRefer (VLM-Grounder subset), IVSGround achieves 58.0% Acc@0.25 and 52.8% Acc@0.5 overall, outperforming the closest zero-shot competitor SPAZER (57.2% / 48.8%) and CSVG (49.6% / 39.8%). On NR3D (250-query subset, w/o GT class), IVSGround reaches 66.0% overall accuracy versus SPAZER's 63.8% and VLM-Grounder's 48.0%, with the largest gains on view-dependent queries (62.5% vs. 59.9%). Under the w/ GT object class setting on NR3D, IVSGround attains 80.4% overall, surpassing SPAZER (73.2%) and Transcrib3D (70.2%). Notably, only a lightweight LoRA-fine-tuned view selector (Qwen3-VL-8B) is trained; the grounding VLM (Qwen3-VL-32B) remains entirely frozen and zero-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot methods, IVSGround consistently outperforms all baselines on both benchmarks. On ScanRefer (VLM-Grounder subset), it beats SPAZER by 0.8% Acc@0.25 (58.0 vs. 57.2) and by 4.0% Acc@0.5 (52.8 vs. 48.8), with a larger margin over CSVG (+8.4% Acc@0.25), VLM-Grounder (+6.4%), SeeGround (+13.9%), and ZSVG3D (+21.6%). On NR3D (w/o GT class, subset), IVSGround surpasses SPAZER by 2.2% overall (66.0 vs. 63.8) and by a wider 17.0% over VLM-Grounder (48.0%) and 19.9% over SeeGround (46.1%); the gap is most pronounced on view-dependent queries (+2.6% over SPAZER). Under w/ GT class, IVSGround leads SPAZER by 7.2% (80.4 vs. 73.2) and Transcrib3D by 10.2%. Fully-supervised methods (ReferIt3D, MiKASA, ViewSRD, BUTD-DETR, ScanRefer) are not directly comparable due to their reliance on dataset-specific annotations, though ViewSRD (69.9%) and MiKASA (64.4%) remain competitive on NR3D overall. In a plug-and-play test, replacing SeqVLM's heuristic view selection with IVS improves accuracy from 52.0% to 58.4% without retraining the grounding model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing threads: (1) zero-shot VLM-based 3D visual grounding, where prior methods (VLM-Grounder, SeeGround, SPAZER) rely on heuristic view selection based on object visibility or query-guided camera placement, and (2) learned frame/view selection from model feedback, as explored in video LLMs (Frame-Voyager) and rejection sampling fine-tuning (RFT). The key departure is repurposing rejection-sampled grounding outcomes to train a lightweight view selector for 3D scenes, rather than improving the reasoning model itself. The cascade object screening and bracket tournament are practical engineering additions that sit on top of the core view-selection contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the quality of visual evidence presented to a frozen VLM is a first-order factor in 3D grounding accuracy, and that this evidence curation can be learned without manual annotations. The plug-and-play result (plugging the selector into SeqVLM yields +6.4 points) and cross-reasoner transfer suggest the learned view-selection policy is a reusable component rather than a tightly coupled module. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ScanNet-scale indoor scenes, whether the two-stage rejection sampling can be made more sample-efficient, and whether the complementary visibility+IVS mix finding (peak at 2 learned views) holds across different scene complexities and query types.
