## Problem

Controllable novel view video generation via VDMs typically requires expensive fine-tuning on large-scale 4D datasets, leading to catastrophic forgetting and poor generalization. Training-free alternatives that reformulate the task as latent-space inpainting suffer from an ill-defined mapping of 2D pixel-space masks onto 3D spatio-temporally compressed latent spaces, where naive binary resizing ignores channel heterogeneity and forces costly per-timestep VAE decoding or backpropagation through the diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261314/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For camera control, the method takes a source video and a target camera trajectory, producing a novel-view video at 480×832 resolution using Wan2.1-Fun-V1.1-1.3B-InP; depth is estimated per-frame via DepthCrafter and the source is warped into a partial pixel-space measurement. For video inpainting/editing, it additionally accepts a text prompt and an object mask, with first-frame conditioning supplied by an off-the-shelf image inpainting model. The lightweight mask encoder (1.5M parameters) is trained at 240×416 on the VidSTG dataset, while the VDM itself remains frozen throughout.

## Method

InverseCrafter establishes operator equivalence between pixel-space masking (y = x ⊙ m) and a latent-space counterpart (w = z ⊙ h) by training a lightweight mask encoder P_φ that maps the pixel measurement y to a continuous, C-channel latent mask h, rather than a single-channel binary mask broadcast across channels. The ground-truth latent mask is computed as the normalized difference between VAE encodings of the clean and masked videos, with training pairs synthesized via a double-reprojection strategy to avoid needing ground-truth novel views. At inference, the inverse problem is solved entirely in latent space using the Decomposed Diffusion Sampler (DDS): at selected timesteps, a conjugate gradient solver enforces data consistency on the linear system (I + γHᵀH)z = ẑ₀|t + γHᵀw, followed by an ODE step, eliminating per-timestep VAE decoding and backpropagation through the VDM. The linearity of the Hadamard-product mask (as opposed to SILO's nonlinear operator) is what enables the use of efficient linear solvers without architecture-specific modifications.

## Results

On the camera control task (UltraVideo, 1,000 videos), InverseCrafter achieves the best measurement consistency (PSNR 29.35, LPIPS 0.0485), best source consistency (DINO 0.0359), and best overall VBench score (0.8977), while running in 71 seconds — the fastest among all compared methods. On the text-guided video inpainting task (DAVIS, 20 videos), it attains the best FVD (1422.72), best overall consistency (0.2289), and best VLM score (0.6510), outperforming all baselines including training-based methods. The method requires no VDM fine-tuning and adds negligible inference overhead beyond standard diffusion sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InverseCrafter outperforms all six baselines on the camera control task across most metrics while being the fastest (71 s vs. 44–926 s for others). Against the strongest training-based competitor TrajCrafter, it wins on PSNR (29.35 vs. 28.37), LPIPS (0.0485 vs. 0.0573), DINO (0.0359 vs. 0.0376), FVD (99.73 vs. 120.03), VBench (0.8977 vs. 0.8954), and runtime, but trails slightly on SSIM (0.8827 vs. 0.8942). Against the training-free NVS-Solver, it wins on PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM, DINO, VBench, and runtime (71 s vs. 696 s), while NVS-Solver edges ahead on FVD (96.90 vs. 99.73). On the video inpainting with editing task, InverseCrafter achieves the best FVD (1422.72) and VLM score (0.6510), beating VideoPainter (FVD 1432.77, VLM 0.6290) and NVS-Solver (FVD 1648.63, VLM 0.6400), though Zero4D scores higher on CLIP (25.83 vs. 24.98) and PickScore (20.79 vs. 20.56) — a gap the authors attribute to Zero4D producing nearly static outputs. CogNVS is dramatically outperformed on every metric (e.g., FVD 2175.93 vs. 99.73), confirming the advantage of the latent-domain formulation over test-time fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the operator-equivalence principle introduced by SILO [43], which showed that pixel-space forward operators can be approximated in latent space, but replaces SILO's nonlinear latent operator with a lightweight linear mask encoder (1.5M parameters) that outputs a continuous multi-channel mask. It also leverages the DDS solver [14] and its conjugate-gradient data-consistency step, adapting them to the video latent domain where prior work (NVS-Solver, Zero4D, TrajCrafter) either relied on backpropagation through the VDM or used naive binary mask resizing. The double-reprojection training strategy is borrowed from TrajCrafter [66], and the overall framing as a training-free inverse problem at inference time departs from the dominant fine-tuning paradigm (ReCamMaster, GCD, TrajAttention) that requires large-scale 4D datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both VDM fine-tuning and per-step VAE decoding/backpropagation, InverseCrafter offers a practical, near-zero-overhead path to controllable video generation that preserves the pretrained model's full generative prior—addressing the catastrophic forgetting problem that plagues fine-tuned approaches. The key open question is whether the linear mask formulation generalizes to larger viewpoint changes where the VAE-derived latent mask target becomes unreliable due to limited valid visual context. Additionally, the mask encoder is currently tied to a specific VAE architecture (Wan2.1), limiting portability, and the initial warping stage remains a major artifact source dependent on monocular depth quality.
