## Problem

Pretrained vision-language models fail when decisive visual evidence is fine-grained (small text, symbols) or spread across spatially disjoint regions (documents, compositional queries). Existing training-free grounding approaches rely on attention maps with heuristic head/layer selection, are brittle across backbones, and typically commit to a single crop, leaving no principled mechanism for multi-region evidence retrieval or controlled iterative refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260423/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a free-form text query as input, encoded by the VLM's vision encoder and language tokenizer. It produces an entropy-gradient saliency map over the visual-token grid, which is binarized and decomposed into top-K spatially coherent bounding boxes. These crops are concatenated with the original full-resolution image and fed back into the same VLM for a final forward pass that yields the text answer; the iterative loop may produce multiple rounds of progressively tighter crops before the stopping criterion halts refinement.

## Method

The core mechanism backpropagates the Shannon entropy of the model's next-token distribution (at decoding step t=1) through the language model to the projected visual token embeddings, yielding a per-token gradient whose L2 norm gives a query-conditioned saliency score for each spatial patch. After Gaussian smoothing and an elbow-method adaptive threshold, the continuous map is binarized into connected components that are ranked by accumulated saliency and the top-K retained as regions of interest. An iterative refinement loop re-applies the same entropy-backpropagation on each selected subregion, cropping tighter patches each round, and terminates when the spatial entropy of the activated mask (measuring dispersion across components) stops decreasing. This design requires no auxiliary detectors, no attention-head selection, and no additional training, distinguishing it from Grad-CAM-style attribution (which targets post-hoc interpretability) and attention-based cropping methods like ViCrop (which are single-crop and heuristic).

## Results

Across four VLM backbones (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-1.6, InternVL-3.5, Qwen2.5-VL), the entropy-gradient grounding method yields the largest gains on evidence-critical benchmarks: V* improves by up to +19.9 points (InternVL-3.5: 47.64→67.53) and +13.6 points (Qwen2.5-VL: 73.30→86.91), while DocVQA gains reach +20.8 points (InternVL-3.5: 58.73→79.54) and +11.4 points (LLaVA-1.5: 22.32→33.70). On general QA benchmarks (GQA, RWQA), performance remains comparable to base models with only minor regressions (≤1.5 points), confirming that the method does not sacrifice broad scene understanding. The iterative variant with spatial-entropy stopping adds ~3 s per sample over a single pass while delivering the full accuracy gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViCrop (the strongest training-free single-crop baseline), the proposed method wins decisively on V* (73.30 vs. 61.78 on LLaVA-1.6; 56.02 vs. 47.64 on LLaVA-1.5) and DocVQA (65.07 vs. 51.42; 33.70 vs. 19.63), while ViCrop retains a small edge on TextVQA (68.65 vs. 67.96 on LLaVA-1.6). Against ZoomEye, which uses exhaustive tree-based exploration, the proposed method achieves comparable V* scores (56.02 vs. 72.25 on LLaVA-1.5, where ZoomEye's exhaustive search gives it an advantage) but substantially outperforms on DocVQA (33.70 vs. 24.61) and POPE (87.56 vs. 86.85), with far lower computational cost. Compared to the training-based TEVA 7B, the training-free method surpasses TEVA on DocVQA (65.07 vs. 51.30), POPE (89.31 vs. 87.90), and InfoQA (33.93 vs. 31.90), while TEVA retains a lead on V* (77.10 vs. 73.30). SEAL, which is heavily tailored to V*, achieves 75.30 on V* but collapses on other benchmarks (TextVQA 36.30, DocVQA 5.31), whereas the proposed method delivers consistent gains across all seven evaluated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant attention-map-based grounding paradigm (ViCrop, [19]) by replacing heuristic head/layer selection with a gradient-attribution signal: backpropagating the Shannon entropy of the next-token distribution to visual embeddings. Conceptually, it repurposes the gradient-based interpretability toolkit (Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients) from post-hoc explanation to active, query-conditioned evidence retrieval at inference time. It also extends single-crop zooming methods (ViCrop, ZoomEye) to principled multi-region selection and introduces a spatial-entropy stopping criterion borrowed from attention-head analysis [19] to regulate an iterative refinement loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-intrinsic nature of the approach makes it immediately applicable to any pretrained VLM without architectural modification or additional training, which is practically significant given the cost of fine-tuning large models. The consistent gains across four architectures and seven benchmarks, particularly on document and high-resolution tasks, suggest the entropy-gradient signal is a robust proxy for decision-relevant visual evidence. Open questions include whether the signal quality continues to improve with model scale (Fig. 6 hints at this but is qualitative), whether the ~3× inference overhead is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployments, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D grounding where spatial tokenization differs.
