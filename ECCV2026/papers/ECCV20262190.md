## Problem

Existing audio-video generation pipelines employ separate pretrained tokenizers per modality within a heavy dual-branch architecture, ignoring the intrinsic representation gap between their independently learned embedding spaces. This leads to semantic misalignment between auditory and visual elements in synthesized outputs and incurs prohibitive computational costs. The paper asks whether both modalities can be jointly encoded into a single shared 1D discrete latent space to eliminate this gap and simplify the generation pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262190/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 16-frame video clip at 128×128 resolution (3.6 fps) and a single-channel audio signal (22 kHz, 98304 samples) whose normalized mel-spectrogram (80×384) is treated as a grayscale image for patchification. It produces 1152 unified 1D discrete tokens (1024 video + 128 audio) from a shared codebook, which can be decoded back into a video and a mel-spectrogram (converted to waveform via HiFi-GAN), or fed into an AR transformer for audio-to-video, video-to-audio, or class-conditional joint audio-video generation.

## Method

AVTok extends the LARP query-based 1D video tokenizer into a dual-stream transformer that processes video and audio mel-spectrogram patches in separate forward passes through a shared encoder-decoder, using modal-specific learnable holistic queries (1024 for video, 128 for audio), patch queries, and normalization layers to capture modality-specific information while enabling implicit cross-modal fusion via shared parameters. Training follows a Video-First-Audio-Later (VFAL) hierarchical strategy: Stage 1 trains the video stream alone, Stage 2 adds the audio stream while freezing video and shared modules, and Stage 3 fine-tunes the decoders jointly. A representation alignment loss aligns AVTok's patch-level continuous embeddings with features from a pretrained CAV-MAE audio-visual foundation model to strengthen cross-modal correspondence. A cross-modal autoregressive prior computes next-token prediction over both token orders (video|audio and audio|video) to encourage an AR-friendly latent space, and reconstruction is supervised with L1/LPIPS/GAN/SVQ losses for video and multi-scale mel-spectrogram/CQT-discriminator/multi-period-discriminator losses for audio.

## Results

On the class-conditional joint audio-video generation (cJAVG) task, AVTok-cJAVG achieves a gFVD of 138.80 and gFAD of 56.58, dramatically outperforming JavisDiT (gFVD 1040.28, gFAD 268.51) and Ovi (gFVD 972.65, gFAD 129.02) while using only 632M generator parameters versus 8.9B and 17.3B respectively. For audio-to-video (A2V), AVTok-A2V reduces gFVD from 786.61 (TempoTokens) to 150.26. In reconstruction, AVTok achieves PSNR 25.62 and rFVD 12.80 for video (surpassing LARP's 24.53/14.24) and rFAD 5.93 for audio (competitive with SpectralCodec's 5.56), all using only 1152 unified 1D tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In cJAVG, AVTok-cJAVG achieves a gFVD of 138.80, an order-of-magnitude improvement over JavisDiT (1040.28) and Ovi (972.65), while also halving gFAD compared to Ovi (56.58 vs. 129.02) and quadrupling it over JavisDiT (56.58 vs. 268.51); however, it trails Ovi on DeSync (1.319 vs. 0.814), indicating weaker temporal synchronization. For A2V, AVTok-A2V reduces gFVD by 5.2× over TempoTokens (150.26 vs. 786.61) and also improves DeSync and IB-Score. In V2A, AVTok-V2A (gFAD 49.47) substantially outperforms VinTAGe (80.06), V-AURA (126.92), and SpecVQGAN (210.07), but is outperformed by MMAudio (17.09) on gFAD, DeSync (0.813 vs. 1.239), and IB-Score (0.291 vs. 0.249). In reconstruction, AVTok surpasses LARP on all video metrics (PSNR 25.62 vs. 24.53, rFVD 12.80 vs. 14.24, LPIPS 0.126 vs. 0.137) and is competitive with SpectralCodec on audio (rFAD 5.93 vs. 5.56, MR-STFT 1.523 vs. 1.514) while trailing on SI-SDR (23.09 vs. 29.30). Overall, AVTok's unified 1152-token representation with a 632M AR generator consistently matches or exceeds much larger diffusion/flow-matching baselines (up to 17.3B parameters) across all three generation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVTok extends the 1D query-based video tokenization paradigm of LARP [50] to the joint audio-video setting, adapting its holistic learnable queries and autoregressive prior to a dual-stream architecture. The dual-stream design with shared encoder-decoder and modal-specific normalization layers borrows the cross-modal pretraining philosophy of CAV-MAE [1,12], but repurposes it for tokenization rather than representation learning. The representation alignment objective draws from DeRA [15] and Yu et al. [62], while the audio stream leverages mel-spectrogram patchification and HiFi-GAN vocoding from the neural audio codec literature [23,26,28]. The key conceptual departure from prior AV generation pipelines is replacing the standard dual-branch design (separate tokenizer per modality) with a single unified 1D latent space and shared codebook, directly addressing the representation gap between independently learned audio and video embedding spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first unified audio-video tokenizer, demonstrating that a single 1D discrete latent space can serve both modalities for reconstruction and AR-based generation, which could simplify the architecture of future joint AV generative models and reduce the need for expensive dual-branch pipelines. However, the experimental validation is limited to VGGSound at low resolution (128×128, 16 frames at 3.6 fps), and the audio reconstruction quality (SI-SDR 23.09) still trails dedicated audio codecs like SpectralCodec (29.30), raising questions about scalability to higher resolutions, longer sequences, and more diverse audio content. The reliance on mel-spectrogram + vocoder rather than raw waveform tokenization is a practical compromise that may limit fidelity for complex soundscapes. Open questions include whether the VFAL hierarchical training strategy generalizes to more than two modalities, and whether the unified codebook can maintain quality as token budgets grow for higher-fidelity generation.
