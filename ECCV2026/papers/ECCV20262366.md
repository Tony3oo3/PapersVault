## Problem

Most PAN-sharpening networks are trained on synthetically degraded PAN-MS pairs via the Wald protocol, but real satellite acquisitions exhibit cross-modal misregistration and sensor-dependent optical responses that synthetic pipelines cannot capture, leading to spectral distortions and double-edge artifacts at test time. The paper argues the key bottleneck lies in the supervision paradigm itself rather than network architecture, and asks where scalable, reliable spatial supervision can be obtained beyond satellite-only training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262366/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an HRPAN image and an LRMS image at a fixed scale ratio of r=4 and produces a single HRMS image. During training, the network additionally consumes video-derived pseudo PAN-MS pairs (grayscale frame as pseudo PAN, PSF-degraded channel-projected frame as pseudo LRMS) at 64×64 patch resolution, with the channel dimension C_s matched to the target sensor (8 for WorldView-3, 4 for GF2/QuickBird). The PSF degradation operator uses 16×16 kernels sampled from per-sensor banks of 10,000 pre-generated kernels, applied identically to both video and RS branches within each training iteration.

## Method

ViPS disentangles supervision into two branches: a video branch that learns spatial detail restoration via gradient-magnitude matching on pseudo PAN-MS pairs constructed from high-speed video frame pairs (mimicking pushbroom-like misregistration), and an RS branch that enforces spectral fidelity via an L1 loss on a downsampled scale where the observed LRMS serves as the supervisory target. A shared PSF bank with random kernel sampling aligns optical degradation across the video and satellite domains, ensuring physically consistent cross-domain learning. The PS backbone is a lightweight hybrid convolution–Swin Transformer network using space-to-depth and depth-to-space operations for resolution bridging. Training alternates RS and video mini-batches at a 1:1 ratio with the same sensor-conditioned PSF kernel applied to both, and the total loss is a weighted sum (λ_spa=10, λ_spe=1) of the spatial and spectral terms.

## Results

On WorldView-3 (full-resolution), ViPS achieves the best HQNR of 0.959 with the lowest spatial distortion D_s = 0.026 and spectral distortion D_λ = 0.016, outperforming the closest competitor PAN-Crafter (HQNR 0.958, D_s 0.027). Under the reduced-resolution protocol, ViPS leads on all six metrics: ERGAS 2.021, SCC 0.989, SAM 2.770, Q 0.923, PSNR 38.021 dB, and SSIM 0.978. The same pattern holds on GaoFen-2 (HQNR 0.976, PSNR 45.122 dB) and QuickBird (HQNR 0.932, PSNR 38.451 dB). ViPS also maintains fast inference at 0.003 s and 19.5 G FLOPs on a 256×256 target, far lighter than diffusion-based baselines (e.g., TMDiff at 9.997 s / 1284 G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViPS outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric across all three sensors (WV3, GF2, QB) under both FR and RR protocols. The closest competitors are PAN-Crafter [11] and U-Know [19]: on WV3 FR, ViPS edges out PAN-Crafter by 0.001 in HQNR (0.959 vs 0.958) and 0.001 in D_s (0.026 vs 0.027), while on WV3 RR ViPS improves ERGAS by 0.019 (2.021 vs 2.040) and PSNR by 0.065 dB (38.021 vs 37.956). Against CANConv [12] (CVPR 2024), ViPS gains 0.008 in HQNR and 0.004 in D_s on WV3 FR, and 0.142 in ERGAS and 0.580 dB in PSNR on WV3 RR. Diffusion-based methods (PanDiff [22], TMDiff [34]) achieve competitive spectral distortion (D_λ) but lag significantly in spatial quality and are 100–400× slower at inference (2.96–10.0 s vs 0.003 s). Earlier CNN baselines (PanNet [35], MSDCNN [36], FusionNet [32], LAGConv [16]) are consistently 0.03–0.04 lower in HQNR and 0.3–0.7 dB lower in PSNR. On GF2, ViPS achieves the largest margin over PAN-Crafter (HQNR 0.976 vs 0.964; PSNR 45.122 vs 45.076 dB), and on QB it leads by 0.012 in HQNR and 0.256 dB in PSNR over PAN-Crafter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant architecture-centric trend in PAN-sharpening (alignment-aware modules in SIPSA, adaptive kernels in LAGConv/CANConv, diffusion-based refinement in U-Know and PAN-Crafter) by arguing that the key bottleneck is the supervision paradigm rather than network design. It builds directly on the PSF-bank degradation protocol of Chen et al. [7] (IEEE TGRS 2023), repurposing their sensor-conditioned kernel banks as a cross-domain alignment mechanism between natural video and satellite imagery. The hybrid convolution-Transformer backbone (Swin blocks with S2D/D2S) is a standard instantiation; the core novelty lies in the disentangled video-RS training loop and the physically grounded kernel-sharing strategy across domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that readily available natural videos can substitute for scarce HRMS ground truth as a spatial teacher, ViPS offers a scalable and reproducible supervision source that sidesteps the synthetic-degradation realism gap that plagues most PS training pipelines. The publicly released ViPS dataset (10,000 pseudo pairs at 4K) and the PSF-bank alignment recipe lower the barrier for cross-domain PS research. Open questions include whether the pushbroom-like approximation via high-FPS video generalizes to more complex acquisition geometries, whether richer PSF priors (e.g., MTF-aware or motion-blur models) would further close the optical domain gap, and whether the cross-domain supervision principle transfers to other multi-modal fusion tasks beyond PAN-sharpening.
