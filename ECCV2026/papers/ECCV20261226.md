## Problem

Current video generation models produce visually compelling outputs but consistently fail on logic-heavy, rule-constrained, and long-horizon reasoning tasks, yet existing benchmarks conflate rendering quality with cognitive correctness and collapse evaluation into a single holistic score, masking where and why models fail. The paper asks whether video generators can genuinely 'think with video'—executing symbolic rules, respecting physical laws, and pursuing intentional goals—and how to both diagnose and improve this capability.

## Input / Output

Vid-PRE takes a first-frame RGB image (token length capped at 1,024) and a natural-language user instruction, and autoregressively produces a concatenated output of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a concise, constraint-aware prompt suitable for a downstream I2V generator. The VWG-Bench evaluation side takes (image, prompt) pairs, generates videos, and scores them on a 1–5 integer scale across three orthogonal levels: video-level fluency, task-level rule adherence, and sample-level goal realization, using 2 fps frame sampling (max 16 frames) plus the last frame for goal verification.

## Method

Vid-PRE is a model-agnostic prompt rewriter initialized from Qwen3-VL-8B that decouples spatio-temporal reasoning from visual rendering by offloading the cognitive burden to a dedicated VLM. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) SFT on ~20K rejection-sampled (image, user prompt, CoT, optimized prompt) tuples constructed via an automated pipeline using Gemini-3.0-Pro, where an independent evaluator scores each candidate on a 1–5 scale and only samples scoring ≥4 are retained; (2) GRPO on 1.5K high-difficulty prompts using a purely text-based reward combining intent preservation, visual-spatial consistency, solution correctness, and format compliance—deliberately avoiding video-level reward signals for stability and generator agnosticism. The companion VWG-Bench introduces a three-level VLM-as-Judge protocol that independently assesses video fluency, task-rule adherence (via rule dictionaries for foreground/background/implicit constraints), and sample-level goal realization (progress milestones and last-frame objectives), enabling fine-grained failure-mode diagnosis that holistic scoring cannot provide.

## Results

On VWG-Bench, Vid-PRE raises Wan2.2's overall score from 2.13 to 2.51 (+18%) and Wan2.6-Flash from 2.80 to 3.26, narrowing the gap to commercial systems (Sora2: 3.44, Veo3.1: 3.55). On V-ReasonBench, the gains are most dramatic: Wan2.2's average accuracy jumps from 26.65 to 44.48 (+67%), with spatial cognition improving from 1.32 to 36.25. On MME-CoF, Wan2.2's overall score increases from 1.72 to 2.58 (+50%), with instruction alignment rising from 0.69 to 1.65. Across all three benchmarks, Vid-PRE consistently outperforms both the base models and their native Wan PE prompt extension without any architectural modification to the video generators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vid-PRE is compared against three baselines: (1) the base video model with no prompt extension, (2) the model's native Wan PE prompt extension module, and (3) commercial systems (Sora2, Veo3.1) for contextual reference. Against the base models, Vid-PRE delivers large absolute gains: +0.38 overall on Wan2.2 (VWG-Bench), +0.86 on V-ReasonBench average (26.65→44.48), and +0.86 on MME-CoF (1.72→2.58). Against Wan PE, Vid-PRE consistently wins by a meaningful margin: +0.20 overall on Wan2.5 and +0.22 on Wan2.6-Flash on VWG-Bench, +10.13 on Wan2.5 and +13.07 on Wan2.6-Flash on V-ReasonBench, and +0.23 and +0.27 on MME-CoF respectively. The largest single-metric improvement is spatial cognition on V-ReasonBench, where Wan2.2 jumps from 1.32 (base) to 36.25 (Vid-PRE), a 27× increase. Even after Vid-PRE enhancement, Wan2.6-Flash (3.26) still trails Veo3.1 (3.55) and Sora2 (3.44) on VWG-Bench overall, indicating that prompt-level reasoning enhancement alone cannot fully close the gap to the strongest commercial systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active threads: (1) the growing family of video-generation reasoning benchmarks (TiViBench, V-ReasonBench, VR-Bench, RULER-Bench, RISE-Video, MMGR, MME-CoF), each of which covers only a subset of the reasoning spectrum and typically collapses evaluation into a single holistic score; and (2) prompt rewriting/optimization for video generation (Prompt-A-Video, VPO, RAPO), which has focused almost exclusively on aesthetic quality and text-video alignment rather than logical correctness. Vid-PRE departs from both lines: it unifies the fragmented benchmark landscape into a single 9-dimension, 38-task suite with a three-level VLM-as-Judge protocol that explicitly decouples rendering fluency from rule adherence and goal realization, and it repurposes GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) with a purely text-based reward signal to train a reasoning-aware prompt rewriter, rather than optimizing for visual fidelity or using video-level reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central diagnostic—that even the strongest commercial models (Veo3.1, Sora2) score well above 3.4 on video quality but drop to ~3.0 on last-frame goal accuracy—reframes the bottleneck in think-with-video reasoning as a prompt-formulation problem rather than a model-capacity problem, which is a practically actionable insight. Vid-PRE's model-agnostic design (no architectural changes, text-only RL rewards) makes it a drop-in module for any I2V pipeline, and the 50–67% gains on external benchmarks suggest substantial headroom remains. Open questions include whether the 380-sample benchmark (10 per task) is sufficient for stable cross-model comparisons, whether the VLM-as-Judge protocol generalizes beyond the Gemini-3.0-Pro evaluator used here, and whether closing the reasoning gap ultimately requires training the video model itself rather than relying on an external prompt rewriter.
