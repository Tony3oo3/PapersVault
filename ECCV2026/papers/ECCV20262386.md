## Problem

Chinese gated communities (fengbi xiaoqu) reshape urban mobility, green access, and social equity, yet existing studies remain qualitative or limited to tens of neighborhoods, and computational urban analyses routinely misclassify or ignore enclosure boundaries, biasing walkability, greenness, and spatial-justice metrics. No metropolitan-scale benchmark or automated multimodal pipeline exists for recognizing gatedness as a spatial attribute at the scale needed for reproducible city-wide analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262386/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each residential AOI is represented by a 0.8 m satellite tile cropped with a 50 m contextual buffer around its boundary polygon (to preserve wall/entrance evidence), a short Chinese text string (community name + address from Baidu Maps), and a vector of structured numerical attributes (floor-area ratio, POI density). The annotated boundary polygon is additionally rasterized into a binary interior/exterior mask that drives the model's dual visual stream. The model outputs a binary gated/open label per AOI; at metropolitan scale, inference over all 37,444 AOIs yields a region-wide enclosure map across nine GBA cities.

## Method

MCGC is a vision-centric multimodal classifier built on a satellite-adapted DINOv3 backbone (DINOv3-SAT) fine-tuned with LoRA adapters to bridge the natural-image-to-remote-sensing domain gap. Its key architectural insight is a boundary-aware dual visual stream: the image feature map is partitioned via the community mask into interior and exterior regions, and a discrepancy feature (F_out − F_inn) plus per-region cosine-similarity scalars encode enclosure-specific contrast. A Cross-modal Community-aware Fusion (CCF) block then performs Bi-directional Cross-modal Attention, where each modality (visual, text via frozen CLIP encoder, numerical via MLP) alternately serves as query against the others as key-value, with the discrepancy and similarity statistics explicitly coupled into the attention to emphasize boundary patterns. A modality-aware gating mechanism with per-pair confidence heads adaptively reweights fused representations, mitigating the real-world imbalance where text and structured attributes are sparse or noisy. The full model is trained end-to-end under supervised binary classification with stratified 5-fold splits, achieving +14.4% F1 and +12.2% AUC over the strongest unimodal baseline.

## Results

On the GBA-GCs benchmark (Guangzhou evaluation split, 5,268 AOIs, 5-fold stratified splits), MCGC achieves 85.3% accuracy, 86.9% precision, 83.0% recall, 84.8% F1, and 91.7% AUC. This represents a relative improvement of approximately +14.4% F1 and +12.2% AUC over the strongest multimodal baseline (Full CLIP Three-modal: F1 0.741, AUC 0.819). The ablation study confirms that LoRA fine-tuning (+5.0 F1), BERT-Chinese text (+4.5 F1, +5.2 AUC), the CCF fusion block (+1.5 F1), and the dual interior-exterior visual stream (+1.5 F1, +1.5 AUC) each contribute incrementally to the final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCGC outperforms all three backbone families across every metric. Against CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021), the best CLIP variant (ViT-B/16, vision-only) reaches F1 0.740 / AUC 0.838, while the full three-modal CLIP fusion achieves F1 0.741 / AUC 0.819; MCGC surpasses both by roughly +10.7 F1 and +7.9 to +9.8 AUC. Against DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023), the DINOv2-Small baseline scores F1 0.737 / AUC 0.826, trailing MCGC by +11.1 F1 and +9.1 AUC. Against DINOv3 (Simeoni et al., 2025), the DINOv3-Base-LVD and DINOv3-SAT-Fixed variants achieve F1 0.732 and 0.718 respectively; MCGC leads by +11.6 and +13.0 F1, and by +8.7 and +8.4 AUC. Notably, the MLP baseline attains the highest recall (0.966) due to a strong positive-class bias but suffers the lowest precision (0.540), yielding a substantially lower F1 (0.691) than MCGC (0.848). Naive multimodal fusion (early concatenation of DINOv3-SAT + BERT or MLP) provides only marginal gains over vision-only models, confirming that the CCF block's modality-aware cross-attention and the dual interior-exterior stream are critical for the observed performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of self-supervised vision transformers for remote sensing (DINOv2/DINOv3 lineage) and urban sociology's long-standing qualitative literature on gated communities. Prior computational urban analytics has largely treated gated compounds as ordinary residential parcels, biasing walkability and green-equity estimates, while existing AI approaches to urban morphology remain vision-only or rely on naive multimodal concatenation. MCGC departs from both threads by introducing a boundary-conditioned dual interior/exterior visual stream and a modality-aware cross-attention fusion (CCF) specifically designed for enclosure recognition, fine-tuned from DINOv3-SAT via LoRA to bridge the natural-image-to-satellite domain gap. The authors explicitly note that no prior work formalizes gated community recognition as a scalable multimodal classification task, positioning GBA-GCs as the first such benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37,444-community GBA-GCs benchmark and the resulting region-wide enclosure map enable the first reproducible, metropolitan-scale quantification of how residential gating distorts green-equity metrics (NDVI overestimation), pedestrian accessibility (detour indices up to 1.48), and socioeconomic stratification—findings that urban planners have long suspected qualitatively but could not measure at scale. Key limitations include the locally grounded Chinese definition (not a universal gatedness taxonomy), dependence on meaningful AOI localization, and the absence of explicit modeling of entrance density and access-control hardware. Open questions include cross-cultural transferability (London results are weak), whether the IO inductive bias generalizes to regions with different enclosure morphologies, and how to incorporate fine-grained road-connectivity and temporal change into the framework.
