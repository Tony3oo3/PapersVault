## Problem

Imitation learning policies for robot manipulation suffer severe performance degradation under visual domain shifts such as lighting changes, background color variations, and camera viewpoint changes. Existing object-centric representations filter irrelevant background but remain sensitive to these visual variations, failing to provide the domain-invariant cues that manipulation actually requires. The paper identifies that fine-grained object structure (spatial layout and topology) is invariant across visual domains and is the primary action-relevant property for manipulation, yet no prior method explicitly encodes this structural knowledge into IL policies.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of the scene, a natural-language task description, and the full input provided to the IL policy backbone (which includes visual features extracted by the backbone's own encoder). StructCon produces a hierarchical Structure Map composed of instantiated geometric primitives (cuboids, cylinders, spheres) with estimated 3D poses and derived affordance regions (triangular surface patches), while StructEncoder converts this into a fused map feature vector f_m that is concatenated with the backbone's visual feature f_v before the policy head predicts the action. The ConceptFactory database (4380 objects, 39 categories) constrains which structure primitives can be retrieved, and the parameter estimator is an MLP that regresses geometric and pose parameters from the IL policy's visual features.

## Method

StructPolicy is a plug-and-play module with two components. StructCon uses a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline: a VLM summarizes task-relevant objects from the RGB image and task description, BGE-M3 encodes this into a dense query for similarity search over the ConceptFactory database to retrieve category-level structure primitives, and an MLP parameter estimator (fed by the IL backbone's visual features) regresses the geometric and pose parameters needed to instantiate those primitives under physical constraints, yielding the Structure Map with affordance. StructEncoder processes the Structure Map through two parallel streams: an affordance stream that generates coarse gripper poses from affordance surface patches (using geometric centers and inverse surface normals, refined by an MLP offset) and encodes them into a pose feature f_p, and a structure stream that hierarchically encodes intra-primitive geometry via MLP+attention-pooling primitive encoders and inter-primitive topology/spatial layout via a transformer structure encoder, producing a global structure feature f_s. The two features are fused through an MLP into f_m, which is concatenated with the backbone's visual features and fed to the existing policy head; the entire module is trained jointly with the IL backbone using no additional loss terms, adding only ~3 ms of inference overhead.

## Results

On MetaWorld (15 tasks), StructPolicy lifts success rates from 65.2% (DP) to 77.5%, 66.7% (DP3) to 79.2%, and 83.9% (Lift3D) to 90.5%, with the latter being the first policy to exceed the 90% threshold. On CALVIN D→D, StructPolicy improves average instruction chain length from 4.35 (FLOWER) to 4.42 and from 3.72 (MDT-V) to 3.86. On the harder CALVIN ABCD→D setting, it achieves 4.70 average length (vs. FLOWER's 4.67) and 4.49 with MDT backbone (vs. 4.33 baseline). In real-world experiments across 8 tasks, StructPolicy yields +15.8% to +18.8% success rate gains over four different policy backbones, with robustness improvements of +35.8% to +40.8% under visual domain shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructPolicy is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on top of 11 different IL backbones across CALVIN and MetaWorld, and consistently improves every baseline. On MetaWorld, the gains are largest for weaker backbones: DP improves by +12.3% (65.2→77.5) and DP3 by +12.5% (66.7→79.2), while the strongest baseline Lift3D still gains +6.6% (83.9→90.5), crossing the 90% success rate threshold for the first time. On CALVIN D→D, improvements range from +0.07 (FLOWER, already strong at 4.35) to +0.16 (TASKIE, weakest at 3.18), with MDT-V gaining +0.14. On the more challenging CALVIN ABCD→D setting, MDT shows the largest gain (+0.16, 4.33→4.49), while FLOWER (already best at 4.67) sees a smaller +0.03 improvement to 4.70. In real-world experiments (Table 8–9), StructPolicy outperforms Lift3D under domain shifts by +35.8% (lighting: 65.8 vs. 30.0), +40.8% (background: 69.1 vs. 28.3), and +35.8% (camera: 60.0 vs. 24.2), confirming that the structural representation provides the most benefit precisely when visual conditions deviate from training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant object-centric representation (OCR) paradigm for manipulation robustness, which segments RGB observations into visual entities that remain sensitive to illumination, viewpoint, and background changes. Instead, it grounds manipulation in Gibson's ecological affordance theory by encoding objects as compositions of geometric structure primitives (cuboids, cylinders, etc.) whose topology and spatial layout are invariant to visual domain shifts. The construction pipeline builds directly on ConceptFactory [59] for geometric templates and category-level composition rules, and repurposes a VLM-based RAG architecture for open-vocabulary object identification. StructPolicy is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play module that augments existing IL backbones (Diffusion Policy, MDT-V, Lift3D) without modifying their training objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design with only ~3 ms inference overhead makes StructPolicy immediately applicable to a wide range of existing IL policies, and the real-world results (e.g., 60% vs. 24.2% success under camera viewpoint shifts) demonstrate that structural invariance can substantially close the robustness gap that visual representations cannot. However, the approach is constrained by the 4,380-object ConceptFactory database, the parameter estimator is identified as the primary bottleneck (7.4% success-rate gap when replaced with ground truth), and the method is validated only on rigid, geometrically regular objects—extending it to deformable or highly irregular objects remains open. Whether the VLM-based category retrieval generalizes beyond the ConceptFactory taxonomy to truly open-world scenes is an important unresolved question.
