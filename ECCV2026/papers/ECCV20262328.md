## Problem

Trajectory prediction models trained on one dataset often fail to generalize to others due to differences in scene layouts, agent behaviors, and sensing conditions, yet no systematic framework exists to predict which datasets are transferable to which without exhaustive pairwise experimentation. Prior evaluations are restricted to a handful of source-target pairs, leaving the broader transferability landscape across the many available trajectory datasets unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262328/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each scene is represented as a temporal graph of agents with D=5 features (2D position, 2D velocity, orientation) resampled to 10 Hz, optionally augmented with a lane-graph map shared across timesteps. The model produces a 32-dimensional latent embedding per scene, which is aggregated into a dataset-level multivariate Gaussian (mean and regularized covariance via low-rank approximation with jitter). The final transferability score is the directed KL divergence between two dataset Gaussians, capturing asymmetric transfer potential.

## Method

A Graph-GRU encoder-decoder (simplified with minGRU gating) is trained jointly on all 24 datasets with two complementary objectives: reconstructing observed input features and predicting future states, with unit-sphere normalization on node-level latents to ensure cross-dataset comparability. Node latents are aggregated into scene-level means and covariances, which are further combined into dataset-level Gaussians using a between-scene plus within-scene covariance decomposition. Transferability between any pair of datasets is then quantified by the KL divergence between their Gaussian approximations, yielding a directional score that reflects how well one dataset's distribution covers another's. Weighted mini-batch sampling (α=0.5) mitigates the large size imbalance across datasets during joint training, and the decoder heads are discarded at inference, leaving only the encoder for embedding extraction.

## Results

Across 552 cross-dataset transfer pairs (24 datasets), the proposed KL divergence between Gaussian latent distributions achieves a Spearman rank correlation of ρ = 0.811 (95% CI: 0.782–0.840) with zero-shot minADE₆ performance, and ρ = 0.729 (95% CI: 0.403–0.913) with post-fine-tuning source forgetting. The KL divergence consistently outperforms L1 (ρ = 0.468), Wasserstein (ρ = 0.482), and MMD (ρ = 0.412) for zero-shot transfer at L = 32. A Lasso regression in Section 5.5 confirms that the latent KL divergence retains predictive power under stronger regularization than any of the five explicit heuristic features (size, speed, agent count, agent type, time), with speed being the sole exception (ρ = 0.624 standalone vs. 0.811 for KL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the proposed KL divergence and three alternative distributional similarity measures—L1 distance, Wasserstein distance, and MMD (Gretton et al., 2012)—computed on the same learned latent embeddings. KL divergence dominates all three baselines by a wide margin on zero-shot transfer prediction: at L = 32, KL achieves ρ = 0.811 versus 0.468 (L1), 0.482 (Wasserstein), and 0.412 (MMD), a gap of roughly 0.33–0.40 in rank correlation. The same pattern holds for post-fine-tuning degradation prediction, where KL (ρ = 0.729) nearly triples the correlation of the best alternative (Wasserstein, ρ = 0.296). The advantage of KL is consistent across all four latent dimensions tested (L ∈ {16, 32, 64, 128}), though the absolute correlation for zero-shot peaks at L = 32 while fine-tuning correlation increases with L. The authors attribute KL's superiority to its asymmetric, probabilistic nature, which captures directional differences in dataset coverage that symmetric metrics like L1 and MMD cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the general idea of dataset-similarity-based transferability prediction (as explored in Task2Vec and time-series transferability work) to the trajectory-prediction domain, where spatio-temporal structure, interaction dynamics, and map context require specialized modeling. Architecturally, it builds on Graph-GRU [95] simplified via the minGRU mechanism [27] to form a dual-head autoencoder (reconstruction + future prediction) that produces scene-level latent embeddings. Conceptually, it departs from prior surface-level dataset comparisons and from ScenarioNet-style visualizations by introducing a directional probabilistic metric (KL divergence between Gaussian approximations of dataset distributions) and validating it at scale across 24 datasets and 552 transfer pairs, rather than on a handful of curated pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers practitioners a concrete, data-driven tool for selecting pretraining sources before committing to expensive cross-dataset training, and the finding that drone-acquired datasets (inD, openDD, SIND) transfer well to instrumented-vehicle benchmarks (nuScenes, WOMD, Argoverse 2) identifies underutilized data sources for motion prediction. Limitations include reliance on a single prediction architecture (QCNet), a fixed 3-second evaluation horizon, and the Gaussian approximation of latent distributions, which may not capture multi-modal structure. Open questions include whether the embedding model can itself serve as a pretraining backbone for foundation-scale motion prediction, and whether the transferability scores remain predictive under more aggressive fine-tuning or multi-source pretraining regimes.
