## Problem

Post-hoc feature-based OOD detectors operate on high-dimensional representations but treat all feature dimensions as equally informative, despite evidence that OOD-discriminative signal is concentrated in subsets of dimensions and is often axis-aligned. No existing method explicitly quantifies per-dimension ID/OOD separability to identify and retain only the most discriminative features, leaving performance gains and computational savings on the table.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260439/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes penultimate-layer (or intermediate-layer) feature vectors from a pre-trained classifier, along with a small set of proxy-OOD samples generated via cross-domain mixup or FGSM adversarial perturbations, used only during a one-time preprocessing step. It outputs a binary mask selecting the top-k most discriminative feature dimensions (retained fraction α tuned on a held-out OOD validation set), which is then applied to all subsequent inference. During deployment, the base detector (kNN, MDS, or MDS++) simply operates on the reduced feature space without any modification to its scoring function, yielding a standard OOD score and binary ID/OOD decision at lower computational cost.

## Method

The core mechanism is a marginal feature selection (MFS) strategy that independently computes the Wasserstein-1 distance between the empirical ID and OOD distributions for each feature dimension, via the area between their sorted empirical CDFs in O(D·(N log N + M log M)) time. Features are min-max normalized using ID statistics for cross-dimension comparability, and the top-k dimensions with the highest WD are retained. To avoid requiring curated OOD validation data, proxy-OOD samples are constructed by cross-domain mixup (interpolating ID samples with cross-domain data at random ratios 0.25/0.5/0.75) or FGSM adversarial perturbations, with the retained fraction α selected by hyperparameter search maximizing validation AUROC. The approach is architecture-agnostic and detector-agnostic: it is a one-time preprocessing step that plugs into any feature-based OOD detector without retraining or scoring-function changes, and falls back to the full feature space (α=1.0) when no subset improves performance.

## Results

Across five benchmarks (CIFAR-10, ImageNet1k, MIDOG, PhaKIR, OASIS-3), the proposed MFS variants consistently improve their base detectors: MDS++_MFS achieves the best overall average AUROC of 80.41 and FPR@95 of 51.68, while kNN_MFS reaches 77.68 AUROC / 51.40 FPR@95 and MDS_MFS reaches 78.20 AUROC / 53.27 FPR@95. MFS also reduces inference runtime by 58.89% (kNN_MFS), 27.03% (MDS_MFS), and 42.76% (MDS++_MFS) on average; on MIDOG, selecting only 1% of feature dimensions cuts kNN_MFS runtime by ~97% while improving AUROC by 5.74 p.p. and FPR@95 by 6.75 p.p. MDS++_MFS is the only method that reduces FPR@95 to zero in the cs-ID regime for OASIS-3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three MFS variants outperform their respective base detectors (kNN, MDS, MDS++) on average AUROC and FPR@95 across all five benchmarks; the largest gain is for MDS (+2.43 AUROC, −3.87 FPR95) and kNN (+1.85 AUROC, −2.81 FPR95). Against subspace methods (Partial MDS, Residual, ViM, PCA MDS, ActSub), MFS variants lead on average AUROC by 1.8–5.2 points and on FPR@95 by 3–7 points, though ActSub and AdaScale-A achieve competitive or superior results on ImageNet1k specifically. Among marginal-selection baselines, ASH is the closest competitor (75.51 AUROC vs. 77.68 for kNN_MFS), while DICE (58.30) and DDCS (69.00) are substantially weaker. Feature-shaping methods (ReAct, SCALE, AdaScale-A) trail MFS variants by 3.4–14.3 AUROC points on average, with SCALE being the strongest at 77.01 AUROC. The strongest unrelated baselines, SHE (75.50 AUROC) and NCI (71.38 AUROC), are also outperformed by all MFS variants. The paper notes that granting MFS variants access to real-OOD validation data (rather than proxy data) would further widen the gap by +0.82 to +2.41 AUROC points, making the reported gains conservative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the family of post-hoc, feature-based OOD detectors (kNN, MDS, MDS++) by adding a distribution-aware marginal feature selection step that ranks dimensions by Wasserstein-1 distance between ID and OOD feature distributions. It departs from prior marginal-selection methods (ASH, DICE, DDCS) that rely on activation percentiles or inter-class variance, and from joint subspace approaches (Residual, ViM, PCA MDS, ActSub) that project features into learned subspaces, by instead preserving a subset of original dimensions selected via a distributional discrepancy metric. The use of cross-domain mixup as a proxy-OOD validation set, avoiding curated OOD data, builds on the mixup literature but repurposes it specifically for feature ranking rather than training augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, retraining-free plug-in that consistently improves AUROC and FPR@95 across medical and natural-image benchmarks while cutting inference time by 27–59%, making it attractive for deployment in safety-critical settings. However, gains diminish when discriminative information is broadly distributed or dominated by high-variance directions (as in PhaKIR), suggesting that whitening or hybrid strategies may be needed for strongly anisotropic feature spaces. Open questions include whether the axis-aligned structure assumption generalizes to transformer backbones (Swin-T shows more exploitable joint structure) and to generative or low-dimensional representations, where only preliminary gains were observed.
