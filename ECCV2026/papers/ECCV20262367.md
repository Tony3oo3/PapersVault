## Problem

Most deepfake detectors trained on both real and fake images overfit to generator-specific traces, severely limiting generalization to unseen generators, especially across categories (GANs vs. diffusion models). Real-only approaches avoid this overfitting but typically rely on pseudo-deepfakes or synthetic artifacts, making their performance dependent on the quality of those proxies. The paper addresses the gap of a one-class detector that generalizes to unseen generators without any fake or pseudo-fake samples during training while still maintaining strong discriminative power.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image as input, processed by a frozen ResNet50 encoder pretrained on ImageNet-1K with no fine-tuning. At inference it produces a continuous fakeness score — the Mahalanobis distance of the flow-transformed features to a learned discriminative Gaussian minus the log Jacobian determinant — which is compared against a threshold τ derived from the training likelihood distribution at significance level α=0.01. The model is trained exclusively on real faces from FFHQ and evaluated on unseen reals (CelebA-HQ) and fakes from 19 unseen generators spanning GANs and diffusion models in the WILD dataset.

## Method

The core insight is that averaging multiple images amplifies consistent generative traces, producing feature representations that clearly separate real from fake samples, as confirmed by t-SNE analysis. The method first generates M average real images by averaging K=500 randomly selected training images, extracts their features with the frozen encoder, and fits a Gaussian distribution N(μ*, Σ*) to these average-image features to define a discriminative target space. A normalizing flow (FastFlow architecture) is then trained using only real single-image features to project them into this ad-hoc discriminative distribution rather than the standard N(0,I) used by prior one-class methods. The paper provides an analytical derivation showing that minimizing the negative log-likelihood is equivalent to minimizing the Mahalanobis distance of real latent representations to the discriminative distribution, yielding a direct likelihood-based criterion for separating real from fake images at test time.

## Results

Trained solely on real faces from FFHQ, μFlow achieves an average ACC of 94.7%, AUC of 94.7%, and AP of 95.3% across three fully out-of-distribution test domains (GANs, closed-source DMs, open-source DMs) using unseen reals from CelebA-HQ and 19 unseen generators from WILD. This outperforms the best mixed-training SOTA (D³, 92.6% ACC) by +2.1 and the best real-only baseline (LAA-Net, 83.0% ACC) by +11.7. Performance is particularly strong on diffusion-model forgeries, where μFlow reaches ~97% ACC on both DM-CS and DM-OS, while SOTA methods trained on GANs drop to 72–82%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest mixed-training SOTA (D³, trained on GANs+DMs), μFlow wins by +2.1 ACC (94.7 vs 92.6) and +0.2 AUC, despite using no fake samples at all. Against ODDN (best in the Real+GANs setting), the gap widens to +15.0 ACC (96.8 vs 81.8 average), exposing the severe cross-category generalisation failure of supervised detectors. Among real-only baselines, μFlow outperforms LAA-Net (the best prior real-only method) by +11.7 ACC (94.7 vs 83.0), with the largest margins on diffusion-model forgeries (+13.6 on DM-CS, +17.0 on DM-OS). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that the discriminative average-image distribution N(μ*,Σ*) is essential: replacing it with a standard Gaussian N(0,I) collapses performance to ~60% ACC, validating that the average-image feature space is the key source of generalisation. Under content-preserving perturbations (Table 2), μFlow maintains 90.9% average ACC, outperforming D³ (89.4%) and ODDN (82.7%), indicating that the learned latent representations are robust to common image degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FastFlow, a normalizing-flow-based one-class anomaly detector that maps features to a standard normal distribution, but replaces that target with a discriminative distribution learned from average real image features. It departs from prior one-class deepfake detectors (OC-FakeDect, SeeABLE, UNTAG, CLIP-Flow) that either synthesize pseudo-deepfakes or rely on reconstruction errors, and from supervised detectors (FreqNet, NPR, D³, ODDN) that overfit to generator-specific traces. The conceptual bridge to the image-averaging literature (Corvi et al., Robertson et al.)—where averaging amplifies consistent generative artifacts—is the key insight that makes the discriminative space viable for single-image inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely real-image-trained detector can match or exceed supervised SOTA across 19 unseen generators spanning GANs and both open- and closed-source diffusion models, addressing the core generalization bottleneck in deepfake detection. The limitation to face images and the reliance on a frozen ResNet-50 encoder leave open whether the average-image discriminative principle transfers to non-face content, video, or other semantic categories. A broader open question is whether the GMM-fitted target distribution is the optimal way to encode the average-image space, or whether richer non-Gaussian models could further widen the real/fake gap.
