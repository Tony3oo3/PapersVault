## Problem

Real-world sRGB noise generation for denoiser training has been limited by the need for paired clean-noisy images or camera-specific metadata (ISO, shutter speed), both of which are expensive or unavailable in practice. Existing unpaired methods like C2N struggle to produce realistic noise due to GAN training instability, while paired methods (NAFlow, SeNM-VAE) cannot scale to new devices without additional data collection. The paper asks whether realistic, signal-dependent sRGB noise can be synthesized from only noisy observations, with no clean references and no metadata.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262585/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes two consecutively captured noisy burst images of the same static scene (256×256 sRGB patches); during inference, it takes a single noisy sRGB image and outputs a new synthesized noisy image of the same scene with a different noise realization. The internal latent representation is 32×32, and generation is a single forward pass through the C-DiT (no iterative denoising steps), making inference fast. The output preserves the underlying scene structure while exhibiting realistic, signal-dependent noise matching the input's sensor characteristics.

## Method

YeTI is a two-stage framework: (1) a Reconstruction Autoencoder (RAE) with a Structure Encoder, Noise Encoder, and shared Decoder disentangles scene content from noise using only burst pairs. The Structure Encoder uses contrastive learning (InfoNCE with a momentum teacher) to extract noise-invariant representations, while the Noise Encoder suppresses structure via subtractive injection of structure-guidance features at each scale, and the Decoder restores structure via additive injection. (2) A one-step Conditional Diffusion Transformer (C-DiT) trained with consistency objectives models the noise latent distribution, conditioned on the structure and noise latents from the other burst image. At inference, a random latent is transformed in a single C-DiT step into a realistic noise latent, which the RAE decoder maps back to image space. The key insight is that burst photography naturally provides independent noise realizations of identical scene content, enabling clean-image-free disentanglement without any metadata.

## Results

On the SIDD validation set, YeTI achieves the lowest average KLD (0.0256) and AKLD (0.1108) across five smartphone sensors, outperforming NAFlow (KLD 0.0305, AKLD 0.1306) which uses 100% paired data, and substantially surpassing C2N (KLD 0.2129, AKLD 0.2802). On cross-dataset generalization (SIDD+, MAI2021, SID), YeTI reduces average KLD from 0.3654 (NAFlow) to 0.0907 and AKLD from 1.4470 to 0.2603. For downstream denoising, AP-BSN trained on YeTI-synthesized data reaches 36.86 PSNR on SIDD (vs. 36.58 for Real Noisy) and 38.55 PSNR on DND with the Mixed strategy (vs. 38.20 for Real Noisy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

YeTI outperforms all three baselines on the primary SIDD noise generation task. Against NAFlow (which uses 100% paired clean-noisy data plus camera metadata), YeTI achieves lower average KLD (0.0256 vs. 0.0305) and AKLD (0.1108 vs. 0.1306) while requiring zero paired data and no metadata. Against SeNM-VAE (which uses 0.01% paired data), YeTI reduces average KLD by 62% (0.0256 vs. 0.0672) and AKLD by 15% (0.1108 vs. 0.1295). Against C2N (unpaired GAN-based), YeTI is dramatically superior, with average KLD 0.0256 vs. 0.2129 (83% lower) and AKLD 0.1108 vs. 0.2802 (60% lower). On cross-dataset generalization (SIDD+, MAI2021, SID), YeTI's advantage over NAFlow widens substantially: average KLD drops from 0.3654 to 0.0907 and AKLD from 1.4470 to 0.2603, indicating NAFlow's reliance on paired data and metadata hurts out-of-domain transfer. For downstream denoising, YeTI-synthesized data matches or slightly exceeds the Real Noisy baseline (e.g., 36.86 vs. 36.58 PSNR for AP-BSN on SIDD; 38.55 vs. 38.20 PSNR for AP-BSN on DND with Mixed strategy), while C2N-trained denoisers degrade significantly (34.06 PSNR vs. 36.58 for AP-BSN on SIDD).

## Relation to Prior Work

YeTI sits at the intersection of real-world sRGB noise generation (NeCA, NAFlow, C2N, SeNM-VAE) and one-step consistency diffusion (iCT, EDM). Prior noise generators either require paired clean-noisy images (NAFlow, SeNM-VAE), camera metadata (NeCA, NAFlow), or rely on unstable GAN-based unpaired training (C2N). YeTI departs from all of these by exploiting burst photography: two noisy observations of the same static scene provide enough signal redundancy to disentangle structure from noise via a contrastive Reconstruction Autoencoder, after which a one-step Conditional Diffusion Transformer models the noise latent distribution. The RDCA block and subtractive/additive structure-noise injection strategy are new architectural elements not present in prior noise-modeling work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating the need for paired clean-noisy captures (which require thousands of exposures per scene) and camera metadata makes large-scale noise dataset augmentation far more accessible. The cross-dataset generalization results (dramatic KLD improvements on MAI2021 and SID over NAFlow) suggest the learned noise representation captures sensor-agnostic statistics rather than overfitting to training cameras. Open questions include whether the static-scene burst assumption holds for dynamic content, how the method scales to higher resolutions or video, and whether the one-step consistency objective sacrifices noise diversity compared to iterative diffusion sampling.
