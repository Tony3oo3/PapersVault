## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks evaluate unimodal perception, world knowledge, or loosely coupled reasoning in isolation, leaving a gap in assessing whether MLLMs can sustain visual-linguistic integration during complex cross-modal reasoning. The paper further identifies that current models exhibit under-diversified hypothesis search in their chain-of-thought traces—repetitively exploring similar local hypotheses rather than branching into genuinely alternative high-level interpretations—making this a key bottleneck for multimodal reasoning performance.

## Input / Output

Each puzzle is a compact visual scene (e.g., stick figures, geometric shapes, plotted curves) paired with a text prompt containing one restrictive clue (specifying the number of words and letters per word, or character count for Chinese) and one to three descriptive clues (indicating answer category or salient puzzle features). The model must produce a final puzzle answer (a word or phrase) along with a full chain-of-thought reasoning trace. The benchmark is bilingual: 2,625 English puzzles and 2,000 Chinese puzzles, with difficulty calibrated by human annotator performance into easy, medium, and hard splits.

## Method

The paper introduces VisWordBench, a bilingual benchmark of 4,625 visual word puzzles annotated with restrictive and descriptive clues, and models CoT reasoning traces as canonical tree-structured exploration processes with revisit edges, quantified via four structural metrics (nABD, BDE, SRR, ARR) that reveal under-diversified hypothesis search as the dominant failure mode. To address this, the authors propose a multi-stage synthetic data pipeline that collects multiple rollouts, segments them into proposal-verification units, de-duplicates semantically redundant answers, concatenates diverse hypothesis units into a single trajectory, appends a ground-truth-guided closing rationale (with leakage removal), and polishes for coherence. For training-free improvement, they compute per-head activation shift vectors by contrasting model activations on hypothesis-underdiversified inputs versus their diversified counterparts, then inject these vectors as residual nudges at a fused token position during inference, steering the model toward broader hypothesis exploration without any weight updates.

## Results

On VisWordBench-en, GPT-5 achieves the best model performance at 61.37% overall accuracy (72.62% Easy, 54.99% Medium, 22.73% Hard), while Gemini-2.5-pro follows at 46.28%. On VisWordBench-ch, the gap widens dramatically: GPT-5 reaches only 23.15% versus 90.25% for human annotators. The proposed synthetic-data training pipeline yields gains of +2.29%, +1.60%, and +3.51% for GLM-4.1v-thinking, Kimi-vl-thinking, and GLM-4.5v respectively, while the training-free inference steering adds +1.80%, +1.11%, and +2.23% over the same models. OOD transfer on MathVista, LogicVista, VisuLogic (24.7%→25.9%), VisuRiddles (24.0%→26.7%), and CrosswordBench (6.04%→7.58%) confirms the steering signal generalizes beyond the puzzle domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a single competing method but instead benchmarks eight MLLMs against human annotators to diagnose cross-modal reasoning limitations. GPT-5 leads all models at 61.37% (en) and 23.15% (ch), yet still trails humans by 10 and 67 percentage points respectively, exposing a substantial cross-modal reasoning gap. Closed-source models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-pro) consistently outperform open-source counterparts; the best open-source model GLM-4.5v reaches only ~1/7 of GPT-5's Chinese accuracy. Scaling within the same family helps (GLM-4.1v-9B → GLM-4.5v-106B shows several-fold improvement), but neither scaling nor explicit thinking fully closes the gap. The paper's own interventions (synthetic-data fine-tuning and inference steering) are compared against standard distillation baselines on the same models, showing that diversified-hypothesis data yields 1.6–3.5% additional accuracy over regular distillation, and that steering with their data outperforms steering with conventional data, confirming the gain comes from hypothesis diversity rather than mere supervision quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against a fragmented landscape of MLLM evaluation benchmarks: visual-reasoning suites (VisuLogic, VisuRiddles, PRISM-Bench) isolate abstract visual reasoning, knowledge-centric benchmarks (A-OKVQA, WorldQA) stress world knowledge, and general VQA covers basic image-text correspondence, but none simultaneously demand diverse visual perception, world knowledge retrieval, and sustained cross-modal integration within a single reasoning chain. On the method side, the inference-time steering mechanism directly builds on the per-head activation-shift formulation from Chen et al. [7] (ICT), repurposing it from hallucination mitigation to promoting hypothesis-search diversity in CoT. The data synthesis pipeline extends standard distillation by explicitly aggregating multi-rollout reasoning branches with answer-level deduplication and ground-truth-guided closure, targeting the structural diversity of the reasoning trace rather than just final-answer correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VisWordBench provides a compact, bilingual diagnostic that exposes a large and persistent gap (best model ~23% vs. human ~90% on Chinese) in sustained cross-modal reasoning, a capability that prior benchmarks leave unmeasured. The tree-structured reasoning-trace analysis with four quantitative metrics (nABD, BDE, SRR, ARR) offers a reusable diagnostic toolkit for identifying *where* and *how* CoT exploration collapses, moving beyond accuracy alone. The finding that under-diversified hypothesis search accounts for over half of reasoning failures, and that a training-free steering vector derived from ~600–800 samples transfers to OOD benchmarks (MathVista, LogicVista), suggests a practical, low-cost lever for improving MLLM reasoning. Open questions include whether the gap narrows with substantially more training data or longer CoT budgets, whether the structural metrics predict performance across architectures, and whether the steering signal remains effective as base models improve natively.
