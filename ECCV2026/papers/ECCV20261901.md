## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (DLLMs) tokenize images into discrete tokens, making standard continuous noise inversion techniques (e.g., DDIM inversion) inapplicable and causing severe structural degradation during training-free image editing. No principled inversion mechanism existed for the stochastic, discrete token space of DLLMs, leaving precise, localized editing largely unexplored for this model family.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261901/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GIDE takes a source RGB image (average resolution ~1193×831 in GIDE-Bench) and a compositional editing instruction that may be pure text, point annotations, or bounding boxes, and produces a single edited image where modifications are strictly confined to the grounded region while the background is pixel-level preserved. The method operates entirely at inference time on pre-trained DLLMs (Lumina-DiMOO, MMaDA) originally designed for text-to-image generation, requiring no fine-tuning or additional data. Multi-step compositional instructions (e.g., replace + add + remove) are executed sequentially, with each sub-instruction grounded independently.

## Method

GIDE decomposes editing into three stages: (1) Grounding uses a segmentation foundation model (SAM) to produce a binary mask from text/point/box cues, with an attention-heatmap fallback for ambiguous text prompts; (2) Discrete Inversion is the core novelty—a sinusoidal masking schedule progressively reveals tokens within the mask, location-aware argmax inversion (LAI) extracts per-step residuals encoding the source structure, and a stochastic logit rectification step fuses target-prompt logits with the inversion residual and Gumbel noise via a mixing coefficient λ to balance semantic editability against structural fidelity; (3) Refinement performs intrinsic re-sampling of low-confidence tokens and residual recovery over the set difference between source and target masks to handle shape mismatches. The key insight is that discrete generation cannot be simply reversed (unlike continuous ODEs), so the method records reconstruction 'errors' and replays them stochastically during the editing pass to anchor the output to the original structure without collapsing into deterministic artifacts.

## Results

On GIDE-Bench, GIDE+Lumina-DiMOO achieves SC of 4.47 (GPT-4o) and 4.26 (Gemini-2.5-Pro) with PQ of 3.98 and 3.78 respectively, while maintaining strong background preservation (MSE 1224.89, PSNR 20.40, SSIM 0.7083). This represents a 51.83% improvement in Semantic Correctness and 50.39% in Perceptual Quality over the closest training-free baseline (DICE+Lumina-DiMOO), and surpasses the average of 11 supervised end-to-end models by 22.49% in SC and 17.54% in PQ. On ImgEdit-Bench, GIDE achieves 4.22 (Replace), 3.90 (Add), and 3.84 (Remove), outperforming the Lumina-DiMOO baseline by 10.18%, 2.09%, and 39.13% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods, GIDE+Lumina-DiMOO dominates all baselines: it improves SC by 51.83% over DICE+Lumina-DiMOO (4.47 vs. 2.81) and by 101.15% over DirectInversion+P2P (4.47 vs. 2.04), while reducing MSE by 85.28% versus DICE (1224.89 vs. 8323.89). Against the 11 supervised end-to-end models, GIDE surpasses their average SC by 22.49% and PQ by 17.54%, though it trails the strongest closed-source models (GPT-Image-1: SC 4.71; Nano-Banana-1: SC 4.48) by a small margin. On background preservation, GIDE achieves MSE of 1224.89, substantially better than GPT-Image-1 (5080.22) and DICE (8323.89), but slightly worse than Nano-Banana-1 (687.79) and LongCat (1166.27). On ImgEdit-Bench, GIDE outperforms all 10 baselines across Replace, Add, and Remove, with the largest gain (39.13%) on Remove over the Lumina-DiMOO baseline, confirming that precise grounding and discrete inversion are especially critical for object erasure tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIDE extends the training-free image editing paradigm—previously established for continuous diffusion models via DDIM/PnP inversion and attention control (Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl)—into the discrete token space of Diffusion LLMs, where no principled inversion counterpart existed. Its core inversion mechanism directly builds on Location-Aware Inversion (LAI) from Dao et al. [11] for autoregressive models and the discrete inversion framework of DICE [19], but adds grounding-constrained masking and stochastic Gumbel-based logit rectification to handle the stochastic, parallel sampling of DLLMs. The GIDE-Bench benchmark extends prior editing evaluations (ImgEdit, GIE-Bench, OmniEdit) by introducing compositional multi-step instructions with diverse spatial grounding modalities (points, boxes, text) and a dual-VLM evaluation protocol that mitigates the known CLIPScore gaming problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GIDE demonstrates that a training-free pipeline can close much of the gap to supervised end-to-end editors on compositional tasks, which is practically significant as DLLMs (Lumina-DiMOO, MMaDA) gain traction as unified generation backbones. The discrete inversion mechanism is a reusable component for any future DLLM-based editing system, and GIDE-Bench provides a more rigorous compositional evaluation protocol than existing single-step benchmarks. However, performance remains bounded by the VQ tokenizer's reconstruction loss and the DLLM's limited capacity for fine-grained object generation, so the method's ceiling will rise only as these underlying components improve. An open question is whether the grounding-constrained masking schedule generalizes to higher-resolution or video editing settings where token counts grow substantially.
