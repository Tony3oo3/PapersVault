## Problem

Joint recovery of surface normals and multispectral reflectance is essential for physical scene understanding, but existing frame-based pipelines require repeated multispectral captures under varying illumination (slow, high-bandwidth), while event-based two-stage alternatives demand heterogeneous illumination setups across stages and recover reflectance only up to an unknown scale. No prior event-based system has jointly recovered both geometry and absolute multispectral reflectance in a single, unified capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261532/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an asynchronous event stream from a Prophesee EVK4 sensor, where each event encodes a log-intensity change (polarity ±1, timestamp) triggered when the change exceeds a contrast threshold C. The illumination schedule is known a priori: 24 LEDs grouped into 8 spectral channels, each channel activated three times at 120° azimuthal offsets, plus a constant white-light component of known radiance. The output is a dense per-pixel surface normal map (unit vectors) and an absolute multispectral reflectance map across 8 spectral bands, parameterized by a rank-K spectral model.

## Method

EventSpecPS co-designs a Spectrally-Angularly Multiplexed Encoding Device (light ring + constant-light source) and an Event-based Spectral-Angular Decoder. The key theoretical insight is that activating each spectral band at at least two distinct lighting directions yields reflectance-invariant intensity ratios that decouple geometry from reflectance, while a calibrated constant-light component (radiance C_w) breaks the scale invariance inherent to event measurements, enabling closed-form absolute albedo recovery (Eq. 8). Three azimuthally separated repeats per band guarantee at least one valid observation survives attached shadows and specular highlights. The decoder is a per-scene optimization (AdamW, 1600 iterations on RTX 3090) that enforces event-generation consistency across consecutive event pairs, parameterizes reflectance as a rank-K basis decomposition R_{x,i}=Σ a_{x,k} b_{k,i}, and applies angular-gating for specular outliers plus Gaussian-blur consistency regularization on normals and reflectance coefficients.

## Results

On the synthetic Blobs dataset with 1 texture base, EventSpecPS achieves 5.89° mean angular error (MAE) for normals and 0.0164 RMSE for reflectance, outperforming the two-stage event-based baseline (9.61° / 0.0302) and both frame-based methods (16.01° / 0.0402 and 20.62° / 0.0591). On the more complex Sculptures dataset with 8 texture bases, EventSpecPS reaches 14.6° MAE and 0.0541 RMSE, while the frame-based baselines degrade to 29.25° and 34.02° MAE respectively. EventSpecPS also uses less input data (7.00–13.17 MiB) than the frame-based methods (16.00 MiB) and is comparable to the event-based baseline (6.93–13.31 MiB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventSpecPS achieves the best normal MAE, SAM, and RMSE across all 24 experimental settings (3 complexity levels × 2 datasets × 4 methods). Against the two-stage event-based pipeline (EventPS [40] + EventSpec [39]), EventSpecPS reduces normal MAE from 9.61° to 5.89° on Blobs (1 base) and from 18.41° to 14.60° on Sculptures (8 bases), while improving reflectance SAM by roughly 30–40% (e.g., 10.36° → 7.41° on Blobs 8-base). Against the frame-based multispectral PS of Heng et al. [16], the gap is larger: 16.01° → 5.89° (Blobs 1-base) and 29.25° → 14.60° (Sculptures 8-base), with reflectance RMSE roughly halved. Against Fyffe et al. [10], the advantage is most pronounced on Sculptures at high complexity, where Fyffe's normal MAE reaches 34.02° versus EventSpecPS's 14.60°. Notably, the performance gap between EventSpecPS and the event-based baseline narrows at higher texture complexity (9.38° vs 9.61° on Blobs 8-base), but EventSpecPS still leads on reflectance metrics and avoids the system complexity of running two separate hardware setups.

## Relation to Prior Work

EventSpecPS unifies two previously separate event-based sensing lines—event-based photometric stereo (EventPS [40], EventUPS [27]) for normal recovery and event-based hyperspectral imaging (EventSpec [39]) for spectral reflectance—into a single-stage system with a co-designed spectral-angular encoding device. Prior frame-based multispectral PS methods (Fyffe [10], Guo [16]) achieve joint recovery but require repeated captures under multiple narrow-band illuminations, incurring high bandwidth and slow acquisition. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical proof that repeating each spectral band at three angularly separated positions provides sufficient constraints to decouple geometry from reflectance, and that a single calibrated constant-light component analytically resolves the scale ambiguity inherent to event measurements—neither of which was established in the prior two-stage event-based pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first single-stage event-based system to jointly recover surface normals and absolute multispectral reflectance, which is practically significant for bandwidth-constrained or high-speed applications (industrial inspection, cultural heritage, remote sensing) where frame-based multispectral PS is too slow or data-heavy. The theoretical sufficiency guarantees (decoupling, shadow/specular robustness via 3-repeat design, closed-form scale resolution) provide a clean design recipe rather than an ad-hoc engineering solution. Limitations include the Lambertian assumption with only angular-gating for specularities, a fixed rank-8 spectral model, and dependence on a purpose-built light-ring hardware setup; open questions include scalability to higher spectral resolutions, handling of complex BRDFs, and extension to dynamic or uncalibrated scenes.
