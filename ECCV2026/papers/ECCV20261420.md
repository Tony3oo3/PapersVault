## Problem

Current VLMs fail at multi-view spatial reasoning, but existing evaluations treat this as a monolithic accuracy metric without decomposing where in the reasoning process the failure occurs. No prior benchmark provides controlled multi-view configurations that isolate specific cognitive spatial factors (viewpoint geometry, occlusion, perspective transformation), making it impossible to attribute errors to distinct reasoning stages. This paper asks: at which phase of spatial reasoning do VLMs break down, and how do their internal representations degrade across those phases?

## Input / Output

Each VQA sample provides 4 orthogonally rendered views of a procedurally generated indoor scene (from Infinigen/Blender) at one of 10 controlled camera-object distance levels, paired with a text query drawn from 22 predefined templates covering relative direction/distance, relation type (view-object, object-object, view-view), perspective-taking, and cross-frame conditions. The VLM produces a multiple-choice answer plus a 4-phase reasoning trace; the analysis pipeline then extracts per-phase graph-triplet correctness scores, a binary self-consistency verdict, per-phase linear-probing cross-entropy loss, and token-level entropy mean/variance trajectories as structured diagnostic outputs.

## Method

The paper introduces ReMindView-Bench (>50,000 VQA samples) built via a fully automated pipeline: Infinigen generates indoor 3D scenes with controlled room type and object density, Blender renders 4 views per object under both object-centric and view-centric spatial patterns at 10 distance levels, and 22 query templates instantiate spatial questions with answers computed from Blender-exported geometric metadata. For analysis, the paper aligns VLM reasoning with a 4-phase cognitive template (perceptual encoding → relational integration → query-specific inference → decision) and applies two complementary probes: (1) explicit analysis where an LLM parses the VLM's reasoning trace into structured graph triplets that are matched against ground-truth scene graphs per phase, combined with self-consistency prompting to check reasoning-answer alignment; and (2) implicit analysis where a 4-layer MLP linear probe is trained on token logits extracted at the end of each reasoning phase to measure representational degradation, alongside token-level entropy dynamics to track uncertainty propagation. This dual explicit/implicit framework, grounded in Johnson-Laird's mental model theory and cognitive spatial cognition literature, enables phase-specific attribution of VLM failures across 15 evaluated models.

## Results

On ReMindView-Bench (>50,000 multi-view VQA samples), all 15 evaluated VLMs plateau at ~30–45% task accuracy, far below the 81.5% human baseline. Phase-wise LLM-as-graph-parser analysis (Table 1) reveals a sharp degradation: Qwen2.5-VL-32B drops from 81.9% correct triplets in Phase 1 (perceptual encoding) to 38.8% in Phase 3 (query-specific reasoning), while the 3B variant falls from 73.2% to 32.6%. Self-consistency prompting confirms that correct answers yield substantially higher coherence (83.6% for 32B vs. 54.2% for incorrect), and linear probing shows progressive increase in cross-entropy loss across reasoning phases, with view-view queries exhibiting the steepest degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 15 VLMs (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-4o, Claude-4-Sonnet, InternVL3.5, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Video, LLaVA-OneVision, SpatialVLM, SpatialMLLM, SpaceQwen2.5-VL) against an 81.5% human baseline on ReMindView-Bench. No single VLM exceeds ~45% accuracy, and spatial-reasoning-specific models (SpatialVLM, SpatialMLLM, SpaceQwen) do not dramatically outperform general-purpose VLMs, indicating that dedicated spatial modules have not yet closed the gap. Across all models, object-centric configurations consistently outperform view-centric ones, and perspective-changing queries cause a >4% accuracy drop relative to self-perspective settings. Cross-frame reasoning (requiring multi-view integration) yields a clear performance drop compared to within-frame reasoning for every model tested. Larger models within the same family (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-32B vs. 3B) show slower phase-wise degradation and higher self-consistency, but the fundamental cross-view coherence failure persists at all scales. Real-world validation on MindCube confirms that the synthetic-benchmark findings generalize to real-world multi-view scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of cognitive science and VLM evaluation, rather than building on a single prior method. It departs from existing multi-view spatial benchmarks (MindCube, SPAR-Bench, 3D-CLR) that treat views independently or lack controlled camera geometry, and from prior VLM analysis that evaluates spatial reasoning as a monolithic pass. The analysis framework repurposes established tools—LLM-as-graph-parser (from LLM-as-judge literature), self-consistency prompting (Wang et al. 2022), linear probing, and entropy dynamics—into a unified, cognitively staged diagnostic pipeline grounded in Johnson-Laird's mental model theory and object-centric vs. view-centric representation distinctions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematically controlled, cognitively grounded decomposition of where and why VLMs fail in multi-view spatial reasoning, attributing errors to specific phases (perceptual encoding vs. relational integration vs. perspective transformation) rather than treating failure as monolithic. The 50K+ ReMindView-Bench with parameterized viewpoint geometry, spatial patterns, and query types enables fine-grained error attribution that prior benchmarks cannot support. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real generalization holds beyond MindCube validation, whether the observed phase-wise degradation can be addressed by architectural changes (e.g., explicit 3D scene representations), and whether the entropy/probing signals can be used for test-time intervention or early stopping.
