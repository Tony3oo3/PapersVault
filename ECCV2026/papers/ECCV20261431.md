## Problem

Existing cooperative perception datasets and benchmarks assume unlimited or idealized communication, involve at most two cooperative agents, and lack real-world C-V2X network traces (bandwidth, latency, packet loss). This creates a significant gap between reported performance and actual deployable performance, as state-of-the-art models require orders of magnitude more bandwidth than C-V2X can deliver in the shared ITS spectrum.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261431/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each scene provides synchronized multi-agent LiDAR point clouds (Ouster OS1-128), 5.4 MP camera images, GNSS-RTK/IMU poses, and per-pair C-V2X network traces (throughput, latency, jitter, packet loss) at 10 Hz, all aligned in a globally consistent ENU frame. The benchmark consumes these through a configurable C-V2X network simulator that enforces measured bandwidth constraints, producing 3D bounding boxes (mAP at IoU 0.3/0.5/0.7) and future trajectories (minADE/minFDE at 1s/3s/5s) averaged over all three ego vehicles. The dataset totals 59K LiDAR frames, 53K image frames, and 344K 3D annotations across 24 sequences in intersections, highway ramps, and parking lots.

## Method

COOPERSCENE is built on a Cooperative Multi-Modal Sensing (CMS) platform using PTP with hardware GPIO triggering for intra-agent synchronization and GNSS-disciplined grandmaster clocks for cross-agent alignment. Spatial alignment employs a novel spatial-temporal ICP (ST-ICP) that projects all agents into a global ENU frame, identifies the optimal pairwise overlap frame per vehicle, extrapolates the correction to a joint frame minimizing total inter-agent distance, and performs a final joint ICP to simultaneously align all four agents (achieving ~0.2 m RMSE). Extrinsic calibration uses a motion capture system with eye-hand calibration (solving AX=XB) to obtain ground-truth-level sensor poses. The benchmark re-trains seven cooperative perception baselines (V2VNet, V2X-ViT, V2VAM, CoBEVT, ERMVP, CoSDH, CMP) across five agent configurations under both realistic C-V2X and unlimited bandwidth, revealing that mAP drops up to 49% under C-V2X constraints and that adding agents can degrade performance due to channel contention and stale data delivery.

## Results

Under realistic C-V2X bandwidth constraints, the communication-efficient CoSDH achieves the best cooperative detection mAP@0.3 of 0.75 (V+2V+I setting), followed by ERMVP (0.64) and V2VAM (0.62), while the non-bandwidth-aware V2VNet drops to only 0.32. Under an unlimited (infeasible) network, ERMVP leads with mAP@0.3 of 0.87, but all models see up to 49% mAP degradation when constrained to actual C-V2X throughput. For cooperative motion prediction, CMP outperforms V2VNet across all horizons, achieving minADE@5s of 1.29 m (V+2V) versus 2.91 m for V2VNet. The per-frame sharing size spans three orders of magnitude: from 0.18 MB (CoSDH) to 30 MB (V2VNet), with corresponding C-V2X transmission latencies from 893 ms to 107,432 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under C-V2X bandwidth constraints (V+2V+I), CoSDH dominates with mAP@0.3 of 0.75, outperforming ERMVP (0.64) by 11 points, V2VAM (0.62) by 13 points, V2X-ViT (0.60) by 15 points, CoBEVT (0.59) by 16 points, and V2VNet (0.32) by 43 points. Under unlimited network, the ranking inverts: ERMVP leads (0.87), followed by V2VAM (0.84), CoBEVT (0.82), V2VNet (0.81), CoSDH (0.81), and V2X-ViT (0.80), showing that communication-efficient methods sacrifice some peak accuracy for deployability. V2VNet is particularly vulnerable: its 30 MB per-frame sharing requirement translates to ~107 s C-V2X latency, meaning cooperation occurs only every 2–100 s and stale data actively degrades performance below the single-agent baseline (0.34 mAP@0.3 for CAV C with PointPillars). For motion prediction, CMP (minADE@5s = 1.29 m with V+2V) substantially outperforms V2VNet (2.91 m), confirming the advantage of transformer-based motion decoders. Single-agent baselines (PointPillars, SECOND) achieve 0.34–0.38 mAP@0.3 per vehicle, establishing the non-cooperative floor that all cooperative methods must exceed under realistic bandwidth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of cooperative perception datasets (DAIR-V2X-C, V2V4REAL, V2X-Seq, TUM-Traf) by scaling from dual-agent to multi-agent (3 vehicles + 1 infrastructure) settings and, critically, by recording synchronized real-world C-V2X communication traces (throughput, latency, packet loss, jitter) alongside sensor data — a dimension entirely absent from prior benchmarks. It also departs from prior work's assumption of unlimited or idealized communication by benchmarking state-of-the-art cooperative perception methods (V2VNet, V2X-ViT, CoBEVT, V2VAM, ERMVP, CoSDH) under 3GPP-standard-compliant C-V2X bandwidth constraints. The data quality pipeline (PTP synchronization, spatial-temporal ICP alignment, MoCap-based eye-hand calibration) addresses known weaknesses in prior datasets' 30–50 ms inter-agent synchronization errors and visible cross-modality misalignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and previously unquantified gap: mAP under realistic C-V2X bandwidth is up to 49% lower than under the unlimited-network assumption used in all prior cooperative perception evaluations, and even communication-efficient methods (ERMVP, CoSDH) still incur latencies too long for safety-critical reactions. This finding reframes the field's central question from 'how much accuracy can cooperation gain?' to 'how much accuracy can cooperation gain within the actual communication budget?' — a question no prior dataset could answer. Open questions include whether adaptive, network-aware fusion strategies can close the gap, how performance scales beyond four agents, and whether the results generalize to different C-V2X spectrum allocations or 5G-V2X deployments. Limitations include the relatively small scale (24 sequences, 59K frames) and fixed sensor configuration, which may constrain generalizability.
