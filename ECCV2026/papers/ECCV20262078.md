## Problem

Existing heterogeneous knowledge distillation methods (e.g., OFA, FBT) mitigate spatial distribution discrepancies between architectures by compressing or discarding spatial information in intermediate representations. However, spatial information encodes both transferable global structural semantics and architecture-specific local details that should not be ignored, and prior approaches fail to selectively exploit this information for effective cross-architecture knowledge transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262078/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on intermediate feature maps (B×C×H×W tensors) extracted from both teacher and student networks, which are first projected to a shared dimensionality via a lightweight projector. Training uses standard RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-100) or 224×224 (ImageNet-1K), and the student network produces class labels as output. The distillation loss is computed on wavelet-decomposed sub-bands of the projected features rather than on raw spatial feature maps or logits alone.

## Method

SFKD applies a J-level Haar discrete wavelet transform to explicitly decouple teacher and student representations into low-frequency (global semantics) and high-frequency (local details) sub-bands. A dual-stream dual-stage spectral refinement (DS²SR) module then refines the student sub-bands: a Local Convolutional Refinement stream captures fine-grained structures while a Global Transformer Refinement stream with 4D positional encoding and cross-frequency attention masks models long-range dependencies, and an Adaptive Weighted Fusion module combines both streams per frequency band. Finally, a Gaussian-Filtered Frequency Loss (GFFL) applies FFT to each refined sub-band, multiplies the amplitude spectrum by a Gaussian low-pass or high-pass mask (matched to the sub-band's dominant frequency range), and aligns teacher-student spectra via InfoNCE contrastive loss. The total objective additionally includes a reconstruction-level GFFL on IMDWT-recovered features and a KL divergence on classifier logits for task-oriented supervision.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, SFKD achieves an average top-1 accuracy improvement of +10.92% over from-scratch training across 12 heterogeneous teacher-student pairs, with the largest single gain of +18.52% (ConvNeXt-T → ResMLP-S12: 85.08% vs. 66.56% baseline). On ImageNet-1K, SFKD yields a +2.51% average improvement over from-scratch across 14 heterogeneous pairs, with peak results of 78.35% (ConvNeXt-T → ResMLP-S12) and 78.28% (ResNet50 → Swin-N). In homogeneous distillation on ImageNet-1K, SFKD also attains the best accuracy in both settings: 72.34% (ResNet34 → ResNet18) and 73.81% (ResNet50 → MobileNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFKD outperforms the strongest heterogeneous-specific baseline FBT [16] by +2.54% average on CIFAR-100 and +0.20% on ImageNet-1K, and surpasses OFA [7] by +6.19% and +0.46% respectively. Several general-purpose feature-based methods (FitNet, CC, RKD, CRD) actually degrade student performance in heterogeneous settings — e.g., FitNet drops to 24.06% on ConvNeXt-T → Swin-P (CIFAR-100) and below from-scratch on ConvNeXt-T → DeiT-T (ImageNet-1K) — confirming the spatial-discrepancy problem the paper targets. Logit-based methods (KD, DKD, DIST) yield modest gains (+1.05% to +3.16% on CIFAR-100) but fall well short of SFKD. In homogeneous distillation on ImageNet-1K, SFKD still leads all baselines including FCFD (72.24% vs. 72.34% for ResNet34→ResNet18; 73.37% vs. 73.81% for ResNet50→MobileNet), demonstrating that the wavelet-Fourier decoupling strategy is not limited to the heterogeneous case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior heterogeneous distillation methods (OFA, FBT, Wu et al.) that bridge architectural gaps by compressing representations into compact embedding spaces or mapping features to logit space, effectively discarding spatial structure. Instead, SFKD repurposes multi-level discrete wavelet transform (originally a signal-processing tool) and Fourier-domain analysis to explicitly decouple and selectively exploit spatial information in intermediate representations. The dual-stream refinement module (DS²SR) draws on the complementary inductive biases of convolution and self-attention, echoing hybrid-architecture findings, while the Gaussian-filtered frequency loss extends the frequency-domain distillation idea from FCFD with sub-band-specific spectral masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatial information in heterogeneous representations is transferable and should be explicitly modeled rather than suppressed, offering a principled alternative to the prevailing 'compress-and-align' paradigm. The substantial CIFAR-100 gains (up to ~11% over the strongest baseline) and consistent ImageNet-1K improvements suggest the spatial-frequency joint-aware strategy generalizes across architecture families. Open questions include the computational overhead of the DS²SR module at inference-adjacent training stages, sensitivity to the wavelet level J and Gaussian bandwidth σ, and whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation) where spatial fidelity is even more critical.
