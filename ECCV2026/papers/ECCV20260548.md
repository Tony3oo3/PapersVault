## Problem

Existing 3D dance generation methods produce physically implausible motions (foot skating, mesh penetration) and fail to generalize to in-the-wild music, largely because available music-dance datasets are small (≤15 hours) and imbalanced, and because handcrafted music-conditioning rules (e.g., Lodge's fast-tempo primitives) cannot adapt to diverse genres and tempos. The paper asks whether jointly scaling data and model capacity can close this gap toward deployable, real-world dance synthesis.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an arbitrary music clip (encoded by MuQ into continuous embeddings of shape N×1024, then projected to 2048-dim), an optional genre label, and top-10 reference dances retrieved from the training set via a CLIP-based cross-modal retrieval network. The output is an autoregressively generated sequence of RVQ-VAE token indices (3 codebooks × 512 entries, 1024-dim) that are decoded into a full-body SMPL-X motion sequence (55 joints including fingers and facial expression parameters), with clips ranging from 6 seconds to 4 minutes.

## Method

The pipeline has two pillars. On the data side, a fully automated collection pipeline converts monocular videos into physically plausible 3D motion: GVHMR estimates whole-body pose, physics-based motion imitation (PHC) removes interpenetration and floating, and a self-supervised Foot Restoration Diffusion Model (FRDM) corrects residual foot jitter by denoising only the root, knee, and foot joints in linear position space, using geometric guidance in early denoising steps and explicit foot-contact guidance in late steps. On the model side, ChoreoLLaMA is a LLaMA3.2-1B backbone that consumes continuous token embeddings (not discrete indices) from MuQ and an RVQ-VAE dance tokenizer, augmented by a RAG module that injects retrieved reference dances as choreographic priors and a Cadence-MoE module that decomposes reference motions into frequency bands via RFFT, processes each band through specialized experts (linear layers + multi-head attention + Mamba), and fuses them via an adaptive softmax gating network, enabling the model to adapt motion cadence across varying tempos without handcrafted rules.

## Results

On the InfiniteDance test set, ChoreoLLaMA achieves FIDk of 30.54 and FIDg of 16.31, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Lodge (89.52 / 60.38) by roughly 3×. Its Foot Skating Ratio (5.33%) is near ground-truth level (5.09%), while the next-best Lodge reaches only 6.72% and Bailando 15.56%. In the user study, participants prefer ChoreoLLaMA over Lodge 68.1% of the time, over EDGE 76.4%, over FineDance 74.8%, and over Bailando 88.7%. On cross-dataset and OOD generalization (Table 3), ChoreoLLaMA halves Lodge's FIDk in every setting (e.g., 56.22 vs. 119.66 on unseen music).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChoreoLLaMA decisively outperforms all four baselines on motion quality: its FIDk of 30.54 is roughly 3× lower than Lodge (89.52), FineDance (94.39), EDGE (96.07), and Bailando (117.38). On foot stability (FSR), ChoreoLLaMA (5.33%) matches ground truth (5.09%) and beats Lodge (6.72%) by 20%, while Bailando and EDGE suffer severe skating (15.56% and 14.15%). On diversity, ChoreoLLaMA leads in kinematic diversity (Divk 6.23) but trails Bailando slightly in geometric diversity (5.11 vs. 5.28), which the authors attribute to Bailando's high foot skating inflating spatial variation. In the user study, ChoreoLLaMA is preferred over Lodge 68.1% of the time, over EDGE 76.4%, over FineDance 74.8%, and over Bailando 88.7%, though it is still preferred over ground truth only 34.8% of the time. On cross-dataset and OOD generalization, ChoreoLLaMA consistently halves Lodge's FIDk (e.g., 56.22 vs. 119.66 on unseen music), confirming that the RAG + Cadence-MoE design transfers beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the music-to-dance generation line from diffusion-based methods (EDGE, Lodge, FineDance) and token-based autoregressive approaches (Bailando, AIST++) by jointly scaling data and model capacity, drawing an explicit analogy to LLM and video-generation breakthroughs. On the data side, it builds on monocular video-based motion capture (PoPDanceSet) and physics-based motion imitation (PHC) but introduces a novel Foot Restoration Diffusion Model to resolve residual foot artifacts. On the model side, it departs from handcrafted music-conditioning (Lodge's dance primitives) and raw token-index inputs (Bailando, MotionGPT) in favor of continuous MuQ/RVQ-VAE embeddings fed into a LLaMA backbone, augmented with a cross-modal RAG module and a frequency-band-specialized Cadence-MoE borrowed from LLM mixture-of-experts designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100.69-hour InfiniteDance dataset is roughly 7× larger than the next-largest public dance dataset and covers 30 genres with hand and facial annotations, providing a much-needed resource for training generalizable choreography models. The FRDM pipeline's ability to bring video-sourced motion to MoCap-level physical fidelity (FSR 5.09% vs. FineDance's 6.22%) opens a scalable, low-cost path to large-scale motion data that was previously locked behind expensive capture rigs. Open questions remain: the BAS metric is unreliable for slow-paced genres, long silent passages and abrupt genre switches still degrade performance, and retrieval-based conditioning captures mainly intra-clip similarity, limiting long-sequence structural coherence.
