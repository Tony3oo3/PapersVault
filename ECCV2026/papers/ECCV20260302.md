## Problem

Multi-teacher distillation methods rely on large collections of real images to cover the teachers' input domain, but the original training data is often unavailable due to licensing, privacy, or proprietary restrictions. This paper asks how far multi-teacher distillation can go when no real data is provided at all, and whether procedurally generated synthetic surrogates can be tailored to be maximally informative for multiple heterogeneous teachers simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260302/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 Dead Leaves structured-noise images as pixel-level initialization and a set of frozen ViT-B/16 teacher encoders (DINO, iBOT, DeiT-3, dBOT-ft) whose attention features guide the optimization. It produces a synthetic dataset of optimized images (IDEaL) at scales from 1K to 1M samples, which then serve as the sole training data for distilling a student ViT. The two stages are fully decoupled: the generation cost is paid once, and the resulting dataset can be reused across different student architectures or distillation configurations without re-optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a pixel-level optimization that treats Dead Leaves pixel values as learnable parameters and backpropagates through all frozen teacher encoders to minimize a combined generation loss. Two decorrelation losses drive the optimization: a patch-level loss (L^PD) that minimizes the Frobenius distance between the pairwise cosine-similarity matrix of per-patch attention representations and the identity matrix, forcing patches within each image to be maximally dissimilar; and an image-level loss (L^ID) that applies the same objective to CLS (or average-pooled) embeddings across a batch, preventing mode collapse across samples. Both losses are summed over all teachers simultaneously, so each generated image is jointly informative in every teacher's feature space, and a total-variation term regularizes pixel smoothness. Optimization uses pools of 250 samples with random 40-sample subsets over 4000 iterations per pool, with random horizontal flips for robustness, followed by standard multi-teacher distillation on the resulting IDEaL dataset.

## Results

With 1M IDEaL samples, the distilled ViT-B/16 student achieves 78.2% ImageNet Top-1, 70.6% average Transfer Top-1, 34.5 mIoU on ADE20K, and 0.573 RMSE on NYUd — surpassing the minimum teacher on all tasks except ImageNet (78.2 vs. 78.4). In the data-scarce 1K regime, IDEaL students (74.1% ImageNet, 68.6% Transfer) actually outperform the ImageNet-oracle students distilled on 1K real images (72.6%, 65.3%). Relative to unoptimized Dead Leaves, IDEaL yields 14–17% gains on ImageNet, 7–8% on Transfer, and 17–19% on segmentation across all subset sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the UNIC/ImageNet oracle, IDEaL at 1K samples outperforms the 1K ImageNet student by +1.5 on ImageNet Top-1 (74.1 vs. 72.6) and +3.3 on Transfer Top-1 (68.6 vs. 65.3), while at 1M samples a gap with the ImageNet oracle remains (78.2 vs. 83.2 on ImageNet). Against Dead Leaves (the best unoptimized structured-noise baseline), IDEaL wins on every metric at every subset size: +17% on ImageNet, +8% on Transfer, +18% on segmentation, and +9% on depth at 1M; the gains are +14%, +7%, +17%, and +7% at 1K. Against FractalDB, IDEaL is dramatically superior (78.2 vs. 32.7 ImageNet Top-1 at 1M), confirming that teacher-guided pixel optimization is essential rather than merely using richer procedural patterns. Notably, IDEaL students at 1K surpass the minimum teacher on Transfer, Segmentation, and Depth, meaning a single student distilled from purely synthetic noise can be more balanced across tasks than any individual teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: data-free learning via structured noise (Dead Leaves, FractalDB) and multi-teacher knowledge distillation (UNIC, RADIO, DUNE). It departs from prior data-free KD methods by requiring no class labels, no classification heads, and no adversarial co-training, and by being designed from the ground up for the multi-teacher ViT setting rather than adapted from single-model quantization or inversion. The core methodological contribution is a pair of decorrelation losses (patch-level and image-level) that backpropagate through frozen teacher encoders to optimize synthetic pixels, extending the pixel-optimization idea from PSAQ-ViT-style quantization to a multi-teacher, label-free distillation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: as foundation models are increasingly trained on proprietary or legally restricted data, the assumption that distillation data is available becomes untenable, and this paper shows a viable fully synthetic alternative. The key limitation is scaling: IDEaL narrows but does not close the gap with real ImageNet at 100K–1M samples, particularly for dense prediction tasks, suggesting that structured noise still lacks the semantic richness of natural images at scale. Open questions include whether the decorrelation objectives generalize to non-ViT architectures, to teachers trained on heterogeneous (non-ImageNet) data, and whether the approach can be combined with generative-model-based synthetic data for further gains.
