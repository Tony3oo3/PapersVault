## Problem

Existing video motion representations are either global, entangling all entities into a single latent code, or pixel-level, lacking semantic structure and compositional interpretability. A seemingly straightforward fix—cropping the video to isolate a region—fails because motion is inherently relational: its meaning depends on camera motion, scene layout, and inter-object interactions that are discarded when context is removed. The paper asks how to produce persistent, entity-specific motion embeddings that are spatially localized yet grounded in full-scene context.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full video (T frames) and a set of spatial region masks (obtained via SAM2 or similar promptable segmentation) defined on the first frame, and produces temporally evolving, region-specific motion tokens alongside time-invariant content tokens. For generation, these tokens are concatenated per region and appended to the self-attention sequence of a pretrained video diffusion model (CogVideoX-5B), which is fine-tuned via LoRA. For understanding tasks, the same motion tokens feed a kNN classifier for zero-shot action recognition, and the masked tracks decoder outputs sparse point trajectories confined to the queried region.

## Method

The architecture comprises three components: a 3D Vision Transformer motion encoder (86M params) that processes the full video conditioned on region-specific content queries, a DINOv2-B content encoder that maps a frame and its mask to a time-invariant appearance token, and a masked tracks decoder (86M params) that supervises motion tokens by predicting future point trajectories sampled only inside the queried region. The key design choice is that the encoder always sees the entire scene—preserving camera motion, occlusions, and inter-object context—while localization is enforced through mask-conditioned aggregation and masked trajectory supervision rather than input cropping or post-hoc feature masking. Training jointly optimizes encoder and decoder with an L2 reconstruction loss on valid future track positions, using AdamW for 600k steps on OpenVid-1M and an internal dataset. For video generation, the learned motion and content tokens are appended to each temporal self-attention layer of CogVideoX-5B, with LoRA fine-tuning to incorporate the new conditioning while preserving pretrained weights.

## Results

On the controllable motion transfer benchmark (Table 1), the method achieves the best motion selectivity (0.2304) among all baselines, combining strong in-region fidelity (0.7462) with substantially lower out-of-region leakage (0.5158) compared to both low-level (ATI: 0.6108, WanMove: 0.5608) and semantic (DisMo: 0.5604) baselines. In the composable multi-source setting (Table 3), it further reduces leakage to 0.2604 versus 0.3857–0.4110 for all baselines while maintaining competitive in-region fidelity (0.6718). For localized action classification on A2D (Table 4), the method reaches 29.9% top-1 accuracy and 42.0% F1, outperforming the best motion baseline DisMo (22.4%/33.9%) by a wide margin, and ablations confirm that removing either context (Ours_RGB: 20.0%) or localization (Ours_GLOBAL: 23.4%) degrades performance significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the low-level trajectory-based baselines (ATI, WanMove, Tora), the method trades a small amount of raw in-region fidelity (ATI achieves 0.8111 vs. 0.7462) for substantially better spatial isolation: out-of-region leakage drops from 0.6108 (ATI) and 0.5608 (WanMove) to 0.5158, and motion selectivity improves from 0.2003/0.1830 to 0.2304. Against the closest semantic baseline DisMo, the method wins on both in-region fidelity (0.7462 vs. 0.6981) and selectivity (0.2304 vs. 0.1377), confirming that localized semantic motion transfer is strictly better than global semantic transfer. In the multi-source compositional setting, the advantage over all baselines widens: leakage drops to 0.2604 (vs. 0.3857–0.4110) and selectivity reaches 0.4114 (vs. 0.2616–0.3291). On localized action classification, the method outperforms DisMo by 7.5 points in accuracy (29.9% vs. 22.4%) and 8.1 points in F1 (42.0% vs. 33.9%), while image (DINOv2: 10.9%) and video (V-JEPA2: 14.6%) encoders lag far behind. On global zero-shot action classification, the method matches or exceeds DisMo on Jester (72.8 vs. 69.8), ARID (55.4 vs. 55.3), and IARD (93.0 vs. 92.0), though DisMo retains an edge on Diving48 (20.9 vs. 17.1) and SSv2 (24.6 vs. 26.5 is actually Ours winning). On motion retrieval under appearance transformations, the method achieves 89.6% vs. DisMo's 90.0%, a near-tie that confirms spatial localization does not sacrifice appearance invariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing paradigms: low-level trajectory-based motion control (ATI, Tora, WanMove), which is spatially localized but structurally rigid, and semantic motion transfer (DisMo, MotionClone), which generalizes across structures but operates globally without entity-level addressing. The key architectural departure is a promptable motion encoder that processes the full video while conditioning on spatiotemporal masks via content-embedding queries, rather than the two common localization strategies of input cropping or post-hoc feature masking. The generation side repurposes a pretrained video diffusion backbone (CogVideoX) with LoRA fine-tuning to consume the localized motion tokens, avoiding redesign of the generative architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first clean demonstration that localized motion understanding requires both full-scene context and explicit region conditioning, with ablations showing substantial performance drops when either is removed. This opens a path toward compositional video generation where multiple entities can be independently animated within a shared scene, a capability that global motion representations fundamentally cannot provide. Open questions include scalability to many simultaneous entities, 3D consistency across viewpoints, and whether the masked-tracks decoder objective can be replaced with generative (flow-matching) supervision for more diverse motion synthesis. The practical impact is significant for video editing and animation pipelines that need per-object motion control.
