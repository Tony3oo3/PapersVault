## Problem

Existing video temporal grounding methods rely on token-level cross-attention that treats each query word as an independent visual concept, overlooking two critical semantic properties: inter-video generalizability (semantically related vocabularies like 'meals' and 'hamburger' should converge to shared visual concepts) and intra-video distinguishability (temporally sparse yet grounding-critical semantics are diluted by frequently occurring visual patterns). No prior work explicitly models both properties in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262549/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes CLIP-B/32 second-to-last-layer visual features (P=49 patches per frame, projected to D=256) and CLIP text query embeddings, with the SE-DETR+ variant additionally ingesting K=4 intermediate layers fused via a gated Adaptive Feature Fusion module. The number of learnable semantic proxies C_N is dataset-dependent (1000 for QVHighlight, 100 for Charades/TACoS/Youtube-HL, 10 for TVSum). Outputs are task-specific: predicted start/end boundaries via 1D convolution (VMR), per-frame cosine-similarity saliency scores (HD), and binary foreground probabilities per segment (VS), all derived from a shared transformer-decoder feature at multiple temporal strides.

## Method

SE-DETR introduces two explicit semantic modeling modules before the DETR-style decoder. The Semantics-Proxied Alignment (SPA) module learns C_N semantic proxies (K-means-initialized from CLIP [CLS] centroids) that act as intermediate concept anchors: each word token is assigned to its nearest proxy, and both word and visual features are then pulled toward the assigned proxy while pushed away from others via cross-entropy losses, enabling concept-level generalization across lexically diverse queries. The Temporal Sparsity Modulation (TSM) module computes self-information from cross-frame cosine similarity of word-aggregated visual features to quantify temporal rarity, then modulates word-level weights via a global CLS consistency score—emphasizing sparse concepts when the video is overall homogeneous and treating all words uniformly when it is not. Training combines L1 boundary loss, contrastive saliency loss, focal classification loss, InfoNCE clip-sentence alignment, triplet loss for temporal distinguishability, and the proxy alignment objectives, with a learnable per-layer scaling factor α controlling the contribution of modulated features.

## Results

On QVHighlight (test split), SE-DETR+ achieves 69.74 mAP@0.5 and 46.42 mAP (avg) for VMR, and 40.82 mAP / 66.40 HIT@1 for HD, outperforming R2-Tuning (69.04 / 46.17 / 40.75 / 64.20) with the same multi-layer CLIP features. On Charades-STA, SE-DETR+ reaches 51.2 mIoU and 60.2 R@0.5, surpassing R2-Tuning (50.9 / 59.8) and UniVTG (50.1 / 58.0). On TVSum, SE-DETR+ attains 89.6 average mAP, the best among all vision-only methods, exceeding Keyword-DETR (88.7) and TR-DETR (88.1). On Youtube-HL, SE-DETR+ scores 77.0 average mAP, outperforming R2-Tuning (76.1) and UniVTG (75.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SE-DETR+ (multi-layer CLIP, K=4) outperforms R2-Tuning (the closest multi-layer competitor) on QVHighlight test VMR mAP@0.5 (69.74 vs. 69.04) and mAP@0.75 (49.16 vs. 47.56), and on HD HIT@1 (66.40 vs. 64.20), though Kw-DETR retains a slight edge on HD mAP (40.94 vs. 40.82) and R1@0.7 (51.23 vs. 49.96). On Charades-STA, SE-DETR+ leads R2-Tuning on all four metrics (mIoU 51.2 vs. 50.9, R@0.7 39.7 vs. 37.0) and UniVTG by a wider margin (mIoU 51.2 vs. 50.1). On TACoS, SE-DETR+ beats R2-Tuning on mIoU (36.2 vs. 35.9) and R@0.5 (39.2 vs. 38.7) but trails on R@0.7 (22.6 vs. 25.1). On TVSum, SE-DETR+ achieves the best vision-only average mAP (89.6), surpassing Keyword-DETR (88.7), TR-DETR (88.1), and R2-Tuning (85.2) by 0.9–4.4 points. On Youtube-HL, SE-DETR+ (77.0) edges out R2-Tuning (76.1) and UniVTG (75.2), and also outperforms audio-visual methods like UMT (74.9) and Joint-VA (71.8) using vision-only input. The single-layer SE-DETR (K=1) already matches or exceeds most C+SF-based methods on mAP@0.75 (48.92 vs. 46.24 for Kw-DETR), demonstrating that the semantic modeling modules contribute gains independent of multi-layer feature fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

SE-DETR builds on the unified DETR-style architecture introduced by Moment-DETR (Lei et al., NeurIPS 2021) and extends the multi-layer feature strategy from R²-Tuning (Liu et al., ECCV 2024). It departs from the dominant line of work (QD-DETR, CG-DETR, Keyword-DETR, CDTR) that refines token-level cross-attention by instead introducing explicit semantic structure modeling: learnable semantic proxies (SPA) for concept-level generalization across vocabularies, and temporal sparsity modulation (TSM) for emphasizing rare but grounding-critical concepts. The paper frames these as addressing two properties—inter-video generalizability and intra-video distinguishability—that prior cross-attention-based methods implicitly assume but never explicitly optimize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean decomposition of VTG into generalizability and distinguishability provides a useful conceptual lens for future work on cross-modal video grounding, and the SPA/TSM modules are lightweight additions that could be grafted onto other DETR-style architectures. However, the performance gains over strong baselines (R²-Tuning, Keyword-DETR) are modest (1–2 points on QVHighlight), and the semantic proxies are used only during training, limiting their interpretability at inference. Open questions include whether the K-means initialization is truly necessary, whether the approach transfers to longer videos or more diverse query distributions, and whether the explicit proxy mechanism can be replaced by simpler regularization without performance loss.
