## Problem

State Space Models (SSMs) offer linear-complexity long-range dependency modeling for Video Frame Interpolation, but their predefined scanning orders (interleaved, Hilbert, Z-scan) are motion-agnostic and misaligned with dynamic object trajectories. This forces the SSM to simultaneously estimate motion paths and model inter-frame features, degrading performance particularly for large and complex motions where semantically related patches are far apart in the fixed scan sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261202/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes two consecutive RGB frames (H×W×3) and a target timestep t∈(0,1), producing a single interpolated intermediate frame at that timestep. A pre-trained RAFT-Large optical flow network is used to pre-compute bidirectional flow maps, which are then consumed by the Motion-Guided Serialization module. Training uses 256×256 crops from Vimeo-90K, while evaluation spans resolutions from 256×256 (UCF101) up to 4K (Xiph), with the model demonstrating linear computational scaling at high resolutions.

## Method

MGMVFI introduces Motion-Guided Serialization (MGS), which warps extracted features to the intermediate grid using scaled optical flow so that standard raster scans over the resulting motion-aligned maps yield semantically consistent 1D token sequences for the SSM, reducing the SSM's objective to local inter-frame residual modeling rather than global trajectory tracking. A Contextual Synthesis module addresses unreliable flow regions (occlusions, disocclusions, lighting violations) by using a masked adapter with 7×7 hollow convolution to synthesize replacement features from valid surrounding spatial context, guided by forward-backward flow consistency masks. The SSM backbone uses SS2D blocks with K=4 raster scans on the serialized sequence, followed by a modified Efficient Discriminative Frequency-domain FFN (EDFFN) that selectively enhances high-frequency texture details at reduced computational cost. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with a composite Laplacian pyramid reconstruction loss plus auxiliary multi-scale warping loss, using a shared CNN encoder and pre-computed RAFT-Large flow.

## Results

MGMVFI achieves state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM of 36.67/0.9820 on Vimeo-90K and 35.53/0.9710 on UCF101. On the challenging SNU-FILM benchmark, it sets new records on the Hard (31.04/0.9411) and Extreme (25.86/0.8732) splits, outperforming the next-best S6-based model VFIMamba by 0.05 dB and 0.07 dB respectively. On the high-resolution Xiph 4K benchmark, MGMVFI achieves 34.65/0.9059 PSNR/SSIM with 78.4M parameters and 427.5 ms runtime, matching VFIMamba's quality while using a streamlined EDFFN module for local detail refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGMVFI outperforms all 14 baselines on SNU-FILM Hard and Extreme splits, the most motion-challenging conditions. Against the closest S6-based competitor VFIMamba [47], MGMVFI gains +0.03 dB on Vimeo-90K, +0.08 dB on UCF101, +0.05 dB on SNU-FILM Hard, and +0.07 dB on Extreme, with the largest margin on the Extreme split confirming the benefit of motion-adaptive serialization for large motions. Against the Transformer-based EMA-VFI [48], the advantage is more pronounced on hard splits (+0.10 dB Hard, +0.17 dB Extreme) while being marginal on easy splits. Against the CNN-based SGM-VFI [27], MGMVFI leads by +0.86 dB on Vimeo-90K and +0.23 dB on SNU-FILM Hard. On Xiph 4K, MGMVFI (34.65 dB) edges out VFIMamba (34.62 dB) and EMA-VFI (34.54 dB) while running at 427.5 ms—slightly slower than EMA-VFI (332.3 ms) but far faster than LC-Mamba(B) (785.6 ms) and EDEN (1304.4 ms). The diffusion-based EDEN [50] trails significantly on reconstruction metrics (e.g., 29.59 dB vs. 31.04 dB on SNU-FILM Hard), consistent with its focus on perceptual quality rather than pixel-level fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SSM-based VFI line initiated by VFIMamba (interleaved scanning) and LC-Mamba (Hilbert-curve scanning) by reinterpreting the fixed scanning-order problem as a motion-alignment problem. Rather than using optical flow for feature warping and synthesis as in classic flow-based VFI (DAIN, RIFE, SoftSplat), MGMVFI repurposes flow to define the 1D token serialization order fed into the SSM, so that causal state updates occur between semantically related patches. The architecture itself composes the SS2D block from VMamba with a modified EDFFN for frequency-domain detail refinement, while borrowing generation modules from EMA-VFI and RIFE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The motion-guided serialization reframing offers a general principle for SSM-based video processing: the input sequence order should follow semantic motion rather than geometric raster patterns, potentially transferable to other video understanding tasks. However, the method's dependence on external optical flow introduces a vulnerability—flow errors corrupt token ordering and propagate through the SSM—and the single-trajectory serialization may underperform in multi-object scenes. Open questions include whether joint flow refinement within the SSM loop, multi-trajectory serialization, or learned (rather than flow-derived) serialization could eliminate the external flow dependency while preserving the semantic-alignment benefit.
