## Problem

Vision-language models face a fundamental resolution-efficiency trade-off: high-resolution inputs improve accuracy but incur quadratic attention costs in visual-token count. Existing visual focusing methods either run multiple autoregressive passes (breaking hidden-state continuity and adding compute overhead) or express spatial focus through text tokens (introducing coordinate-string overhead and format brittleness). Neither approach instantiates the core process of human foveation—stateful, non-linguistic, action-based evidence acquisition within a single ongoing reasoning trajectory.

## Input / Output

The model receives a downsampled low-resolution image (224² or 336²) and a text instruction, but retains access to the original high-resolution image throughout decoding. When the model emits a special <fov> trigger token, a continuous box is sampled and the corresponding high-res crop is tokenized and injected into the same KV-cache stream as a delimited visual block. The number of foveation steps is adaptive per sample (mean ~1.2–2.4, with large variance), and the output is a structured text response with <think> reasoning and <answer> tags; the total visual-token budget varies from near-zero for easy cases to several hundred for text-heavy or fine-grained tasks.

## Method

FoveateR formulates autoregressive decoding as a POMDP where the agent alternates between discrete token emission (via the backbone token policy π_θ) and continuous box prediction (via a lightweight MLP foveation policy π_φ conditioned on the shared hidden state). When the <fov> trigger is emitted, the foveation policy samples a box, the environment crops and tokenizes the high-res region, and the resulting visual tokens are appended to the same KV-cache—preserving hidden-state continuity without restarting the model. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) coldstart SFT on interleaved foveation-reasoning targets (high-res evidence placed before each reasoning sentence) to bootstrap <fov> usage and box regression, and (2) GRPO-based reinforcement learning that jointly optimizes both policies using group-relative advantages on task accuracy, augmented with a foveated-region regularizer that penalizes large crop areas only when the answer is already correct, preventing the trivial 'see-everything' reward-hacking solution. The key architectural distinction is that foveation is a non-linguistic, differentiable continuous action executed within the same decoding trajectory, rather than a separate localization pass or a text-emitted coordinate string.

## Results

On the Visual CoT benchmark (12 datasets) at 336² input resolution, FoveateR-7B achieves 83.3 on DocVQA, 86.2 on TextVQA, and 88.6 on CUB, while using only ~323 visual tokens on average (vs. 1,152 for multi-pass baselines). The 3B variant uses ~307 tokens and still outperforms all prior visual focusing methods on every document understanding task without GT-box supervision. On V*Bench (high-resolution images averaging 2246×1582), FoveateR-3B achieves 56.5/76.5/80.0 on Attr/GPT4V-hard/OCR, substantially surpassing 7–12B multi-pass baselines (e.g., UV-CoT-7B: 35.2/17.6/67.7). End-to-end on V7W, FoveateR-3B cuts latency by ~9× and visual tokens by ~7× relative to VisCoT-7B while improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoveateR-7B outperforms the strongest multi-pass baseline VisCoT-7B by large margins on document/text tasks (DocVQA 83.3 vs. 47.6, TextVQA 86.2 vs. 77.5, DUDE 62.7 vs. 38.6) while using ~3.6× fewer visual tokens (323 vs. 1,152). Against UV-CoT-7B, FoveateR-3B already surpasses it on DocVQA (74.0 vs. 28.3) and TextVQA (83.0 vs. 71.1), and on V*Bench FoveateR-3B (56.5/76.5/80.0) dramatically exceeds UV-CoT-7B (35.2/17.6/67.7) and VisCoT-7B (33.0/11.8/59.3). The text-grounded DocThink-7B (which uses GT boxes) is outperformed by FoveateR-3B on DocVQA (74.0 vs. 57.9), TextVQA (83.0 vs. 80.2), and DUDE (58.2 vs. 40.8) without any GT-box supervision. Standard VLMs (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B) are consistently outperformed across all 12 Visual CoT datasets, with the largest gaps on fine-grained tasks (CUB: 82.7/88.6 vs. 53.0/57.3). The only area where FoveateR trails is Flickr30k general VQA, where VisCoT-7B (66.8) and DocThink-7B (67.4) slightly edge out FoveateR-7B (60.8), likely because general VQA rarely requires fine-grained foveation. On end-to-end efficiency (V7W, single H100), FoveateR-3B achieves 1.06 s latency vs. 9.78 s for VisCoT-7B, with 24 GB peak VRAM vs. 25 GB and 76 tok/s throughput vs. 58.9 tok/s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant streams of visual focusing—multi-pass methods (VisCoT, UV-CoT) that reset hidden states across multiple autoregressive runs, and text-grounded methods (VPT, GRIT) that express spatial references as discrete coordinate tokens—by formulating foveation as a stateful, continuous-action process within a single decoding trajectory. The POMDP framing draws on the classical recurrent visual attention paradigm of Mnih et al. (2014), while the RL training stage builds directly on GRPO from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1. The key conceptual departure is treating the foveation trigger and box prediction as a non-linguistic, state-conditioned action (a lightweight MLP head on the shared hidden state) rather than as text output or a separate localization model, thereby preserving KV-cache continuity and eliminating coordinate-text overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 3B model with ~300 visual tokens outperforms 7–12B multi-pass baselines using 1,152+ tokens, with ~9× lower latency and ~7× fewer foveated tokens, making high-resolution VQA feasible under tight compute budgets. An open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond VQA-style tasks (e.g., dense captioning, video understanding) where the number and granularity of foveation steps may need to scale further. The finding that foveation-only coldstart (without intermediate reasoning narration) matches or exceeds interleaved foveation-reasoning targets suggests that explicit chain-of-thought may be less critical than previously assumed under low-resolution inputs, though this was tested only on a single backbone family (Qwen2.5-VL) and a limited set of benchmarks.
