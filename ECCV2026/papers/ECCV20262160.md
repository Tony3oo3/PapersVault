## Problem

Existing VLM-guided EQA methods rely on greedy, step-wise frontier selection that invokes the VLM at every step, causing substantial token overhead and latency. This myopic planning lacks global topological awareness, leading to redundant motion, discontinuous trajectories with frequent backtracking, and insufficient coverage of task-relevant regions. Path-planning strategies for EQA remain comparatively underexplored relative to memory and representation design.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language question and incrementally captures N egocentric RGB-D images with camera poses at each exploration step, using a TSDF volumetric representation at 10 cm voxel resolution. Rather than outputting a single next-goal per step, SAFE-EQA produces a multi-step visitation sequence over discovered landmarks, and the final natural-language answer is generated only when the hierarchical answering module identifies sufficient task-relevant evidence via graph matching between the goal graph and scene graph.

## Method

SAFE-EQA reformulates EQA exploration as a semantic-aware Traveling Salesman Problem over topological landmarks extracted from an incrementally built 2D Voronoi graph of the scanned free space. A query-conditioned semantic map is constructed by partitioning the 3D scene graph into connected subgraphs and scoring each with an LLM, then fusing relevance scores into unoccupied regions via exponential moving average. The TSP objective discounts inter-landmark geodesic distances by normalized semantic scores, so the agent prioritizes task-relevant regions while maintaining a globally coherent route. An adaptive replanning mechanism triggers local TSP re-solving (over the top-k priority landmarks) only when a newly detected frontier is judged by the VLM to have higher task priority than the current next goal, balancing trajectory coherence with responsiveness. A hierarchical answering module performs coarse-to-fine graph matching and LLM-based filtering to invoke the VLM for answer prediction only at critical decision points, reducing overall token consumption by 27.8–45.6%.

## Results

SAFE-EQA achieves the highest LLM-Match accuracy on OpenEQA (56.4) and HM-EQA† (59.2), and the highest LLM-Match SPL on OpenEQA (53.9), indicating both superior answering accuracy and exploration efficiency. On EXPRESS-Bench, 3D-Mem leads on raw LLM-Match (67.5 vs. 63.3), but SAFE-EQA achieves a higher SPL (53.5 vs. 50.3), reflecting more efficient trajectories. Across all three benchmarks, SAFE-EQA reduces VLM/LLM token consumption by 27.8–45.6% compared to 3D-Mem (e.g., 10,586 vs. 19,489 tokens per scene on OpenEQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFE-EQA outperforms all five baselines on OpenEQA LLM-Match (56.4 vs. 52.6 for the strongest, 3D-Mem) and by a wide margin on LLM-Match SPL (53.9 vs. 42.0), confirming both better evidence acquisition and more efficient trajectories. On EXPRESS-Bench, 3D-Mem retains a 4.2-point edge in raw LLM-Match (67.5 vs. 63.3), but SAFE-EQA surpasses it on SPL (53.5 vs. 50.3), indicating shorter, more direct exploration paths. On HM-EQA†, SAFE-EQA edges out 3D-Mem by 0.2 points (59.2 vs. 59.0) and leads all other baselines by over 15 points. Compared to the step-wise VLM-guided methods (Explore-EQA, Fine-EQA, MemoryEQA*), SAFE-EQA gains 8–13 points in LLM-Match on OpenEQA, attributed to its globally coherent TSP-based planning rather than greedy frontier selection. In terms of resource cost, SAFE-EQA consumes roughly 45% fewer tokens than 3D-Mem on OpenEQA (10,586 vs. 19,489) and about 36% fewer on EXPRESS-Bench (14,992 vs. 23,270), primarily because the TSP planner issues multi-step trajectories and the hierarchical answering module invokes the VLM only at critical decision points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFE-EQA sits at the intersection of VLM-guided embodied question answering (Explore-EQA, Fine-EQA, 3D-Mem) and classical autonomous exploration (frontier-based methods, Voronoi-graph navigation). It inherits the scene-graph and keyframe-memory infrastructure from ConceptGraph and 3D-Mem, and the Voronoi-based landmark extraction from CogNav/Voronav, but departs from the dominant step-wise, greedy frontier-selection paradigm by reformulating exploration as a global semantic-aware TSP over topological landmarks. The goal-graph matching mechanism is adapted from UniGoal, and the hierarchical answering module borrows the filtering-and-answering strategy from 3D-Mem, but the core novelty is the combinatorial-optimization framing that produces multi-step trajectories rather than single-step goals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 27.8–45.6% reduction in VLM/LLM token consumption while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy addresses a critical deployment bottleneck: frequent VLM calls make current EQA agents impractical for real-time or resource-constrained settings. The semantic-aware TSP formulation is a clean, interpretable way to inject task relevance into global route planning, and the adaptive replanning mechanism offers a principled trade-off between trajectory coherence and responsiveness to new evidence. Open questions include scalability to much larger or multi-floor environments, sensitivity to the quality of the LLM-assigned semantic scores, and whether the TSP formulation generalizes to multi-agent or continuous (non-landmark) planning settings. The reliance on GPT-4o and Qwen3 as external APIs also limits reproducibility and on-device deployment.
