## Problem

Adversarial training, while the most effective defense against adversarial attacks, suffers from robust overfitting whose root causes remain unclear. This paper identifies a previously overlooked phenomenon—robust instability—where the model's robust accuracy on the same adversarial data consistently degrades across adjacent training epochs (negative Robust Stability Rate), revealing harmful decision boundary distortion. The key gap addressed is that this instability was not recognized as a distinct optimization issue, and its causal relationship to robust overfitting had not been established or exploited for mitigation.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet-200) and produces class labels, operating under an L∞ threat model with ε=8/255. During training, each input is augmented with PGD-generated adversarial examples (10 steps, step size 2/255), and the model simultaneously processes both clean and adversarial versions to compute relearning labels. The relearning label is a soft probability vector maintained per sample via temporal ensembling, combining clean and adversarial predictions with a balance factor γ=0.5, rather than using the one-hot ground-truth label directly.

## Method

Relearning Adversarial Training (RAT) reformulates the standard PGD min-max optimization into a new adversarial dynamic: the inner maximization generates adversarial examples using a relearning loss (cross-entropy + weighted MSE to a temporal-ensembling label), deliberately inducing robust instability, while the outer minimization trains the model to match the relearning label, enforcing robust stability. The relearning label p_i is updated recursively via p_i ← η·p_i + (1−η)·q_i where q_i blends clean and adversarial model outputs, effectively forming an exponentially weighted ensemble of all historical models at negligible storage cost. A theoretical proposition shows that the observed negative RSR implies non-uniform decision boundary distortion—robustness improves in one perturbation direction while degrading in another—and that penalizing output shifts via the MSE term implicitly limits weight update magnitude. The relearning strategy is applied after the model acquires basic capabilities (before the second learning rate decay), and the approach is plug-and-play, integrating with PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, and AT-AWP without architectural changes.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 under PGD-20 (ε=8/255), AT-AWP+Relearning achieves 57.23% final robust accuracy with only a 0.08% Best-to-Final drop, compared to 41.40% (−9.30% drop) for vanilla PGD-AT. On WideResNet-34-10, AT-AWP+Relearning reaches 61.21% final robust accuracy (−0.11% drop), outperforming FOMO (59.23%) and HFAT (54.74%). The Robust Stability Rate (RSR) improves dramatically from −88.61 (PGD-AT) to −22.80 (PGD-AT+Relearning), confirming substantial mitigation of robust instability. The method also achieves the best trade-off score (67.13 for AT-AWP+RE on ResNet-18), balancing natural and robust accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Relearning consistently outperforms all baselines when plugged in: PGD-AT+RE beats vanilla PGD-AT by 14.0% final robust accuracy (55.40 vs 41.40) and reduces the Best-Final gap from 9.30% to 0.62%. Against knowledge-distillation methods, PGD-AT+RE surpasses PGD-AT+TE (55.40 vs 53.87) and KD+SWA (55.40 vs 53.68) on robust accuracy, while AT-AWP+RE (57.23) beats FOMO (56.46) and HFAT (55.74). The method dominates EMA-based SEAT (55.40 vs 47.44) and ADR (55.40 vs 51.38) by large margins, indicating these methods still suffer significant overfitting. On the stronger WRN-34-10 backbone, AT-AWP+RE achieves 61.21% final robust accuracy, exceeding FOMO (59.23%) by 1.98% and HFAT (54.74%) by 6.47%, while maintaining a near-zero overfitting gap (−0.11%). The RSR metric confirms the advantage: PGD-AT+RE improves RSR from −88.61 to −22.80, and AT-AWP+RE further reduces it to −31.75, versus −45.36 for AT-AWP alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training literature (PGD-AT, TRADES) by identifying robust instability as a distinct, measurable phenomenon separate from the well-studied robust overfitting problem. It repurposes Temporal Ensembling from semi-supervised learning as a mechanism to stabilize adversarial training, positioning itself alongside knowledge-distillation-inspired methods (KD-SWA, FOMO, PGD-AT+TE) but departing from them by using the model's own historical outputs rather than a pre-trained teacher. The reformulated min-max objective, where adversarial examples are generated to induce instability while training enforces stability, represents a conceptual departure from standard adversarial training where the inner max simply seeks worst-case misclassification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of robust instability (quantified via RSR) provides a new diagnostic lens for understanding why adversarial training degrades, and the plug-and-play relearning strategy offers a practical, low-overhead remedy that consistently improves multiple baselines. However, the causal link between instability and overfitting remains a hypothesis supported by correlation rather than proof, and the evaluation is confined to CIFAR-scale datasets with moderate network architectures. Open questions include whether the mechanism generalizes to ImageNet-scale training, whether the temporal ensembling approach interacts well with other recent advances (e.g., diffusion-based data augmentation), and whether the RSR metric captures the full picture of training dynamics.
