## Problem

Existing visuomotor action representations either scale their structure with the timestep grid (causing representational redundancy at high sampling rates) or rely on fixed temporal parameterizations like DCT coefficients or B-spline control points (constraining expressiveness to a predefined basis). This makes it difficult to allocate modeling capacity to the short, critical trajectory segments—such as contact transitions and corrective adjustments—that actually determine task success, while smooth repetitive motion dilutes the learning signal.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes RGB images from a third-person camera and a wrist camera along with a language instruction, and outputs a continuous action trajectory over a fixed 1-second real-time window. The key structural detail is that the action representation is a fixed set of K continuous latent tokens whose count is independent of the control frequency (10 Hz to 50 Hz in experiments); the control frequency enters only as a conditioning signal via frequency-scaled positional embeddings, so the same latent budget serves all sampling rates.

## Method

CAT encodes an action sequence {a_t} of length N = f_s·T (where T = 1 s) into K continuous register tokens via a transformer encoder that injects Frequency-aware Rotary Positional Embeddings (F-RoPE), which scale each timestep index by 1/f_s to place trajectories at different control rates in a shared temporal coordinate. An asymmetric causal decoder reconstructs the full action sequence from the register tokens concatenated with learnable per-timestep action embeddings, enforcing temporal causality during decoding. Training combines an L2 reconstruction loss with a trajectory-level contrastive regularization that penalizes small inter-trajectory distances in the concatenated register-token space, preserving discriminability under the fixed token budget. The learned latent tokens then serve as the action space for a flow-matching policy (Eq. 7), where a diffusion model learns a velocity field over noisy-to-clean latent trajectories conditioned on language and scene context, and CAT's decoder maps the denoised latents back to continuous actions at the target control frequency.

## Results

On LIBERO, SmolVLA-CAT achieves 90.8% average success rate, improving over the SmolVLA baseline (87.3%) by +3.5 points, and outperforming SmolVLA-FAST (84.5%) and SmolVLA-VQ-VLA (85.3%) under identical backbone and training settings. Integrating CAT into the larger π₀ model yields 96.5% average, surpassing π₀ (94.2%) by +2.3 points and all other listed VLA baselines. On MimicGen, DP-CAT reaches 86.9% average success rate versus 85.8% for flow-matching Diffusion Policy and 84.8% for CARP. On RoboTwin 2.0, SmolVLA-CAT outperforms the baseline at all four control frequencies (10–50 Hz), with gains ranging from +4.0 to +10.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the SmolVLA backbone, CAT consistently outperforms both discretization-dependent representations: SmolVLA-CAT (90.8%) beats SmolVLA-FAST (84.5%, +6.3) and SmolVLA-VQ-VLA (85.3%, +5.5), confirming that continuous trajectory-level latents provide a stronger inductive bias than DCT-based or VQ-based tokenization within the same architecture. Against the timestep-level continuous baseline SmolVLA (87.3%), CAT yields a +3.5 gain, with the largest improvement on the Long suite (+6.0). At the larger π₀ scale, π₀-CAT (96.5%) surpasses π₀ (94.2%) by +2.3 and also exceeds OmniSAT (93.4%), CogACT (93.2%), and 4D-VLA (92.2%). On MimicGen, DP-CAT (86.9%) edges out flow-matching Diffusion Policy (85.8%) by +1.1 and CARP (84.8%) by +2.1, with the largest per-task gain on Stack 3 (+9 points). On RoboTwin 2.0, SmolVLA-CAT wins over SmolVLA at every tested frequency (10, 16.7, 25, 50 Hz), with the most substantial gains on tasks involving short critical interaction transitions (Grab Roller +18.5, Press Stapler +16.0 averaged across frequencies). In real-world long-horizon tasks, CAT improves the average normalized score from 45.0 to 60.5 (+15.5), with the largest gain on the Flower task (+28 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT carves out a new middle ground between two dominant action-representation strategies in VLA models: (1) discretization-dependent tokenizers (FAST, VQ-VLA, CARP) that bind representational structure to a fixed temporal grid or predefined basis, and (2) continuous generative policies (Diffusion Policy, flow-matching) that remain coupled to timestep-aligned action chunks. By encoding a fixed real-time window into a constant number of continuous register tokens with frequency-aware rotary positional encoding (F-RoPE), CAT decouples representational dimensionality from control frequency without resorting to any predefined temporal or spectral parameterization. The framework is architecture-agnostic and plugs into existing diffusion/flow-matching VLA backbones (SmolVLA, π₀) as a drop-in action representation layer, building on the broader VLA paradigm without modifying the policy network itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-robustness property is practically important: real robotic deployments often require retuning control rates for different hardware or task dynamics, and CAT's fixed-latent-budget design means a single trained representation can serve multiple execution rates without retraining the action encoder. However, the evaluation is limited to a single-arm (ARX R5) real-robot setup and a 1-second trajectory window, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to bimanual or multi-robot settings and to tasks whose critical dynamics span longer or shorter horizons. The moderate but consistent gains (+1 to +15 points depending on setting) suggest the technique is a useful inductive bias rather than a transformative change, and its broader impact will depend on whether the community adopts trajectory-level latent tokens as a standard action interface in VLA pipelines.
