## Problem

Generating realistic 3D hand-object interaction sequences from text remains challenging because existing methods either discretize continuous motions (suppressing contact-rich features) or perform holistic denoising over entire sequences (producing globally inconsistent, jittery outputs). Frame-independent latent representations further weaken temporal coherence and fail to encode fine-grained, contact-aware cues necessary for physically plausible interactions without interpenetration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language task description and a canonical 3D object point cloud (1024 points) as input, and produces a temporally coherent sequence of MANO hand poses (16 joints × 3 axis-angle) and 4×4 object transformation matrices over N frames. Object motion is predicted relative to the initial frame to avoid dataset-specific coordinate conventions, and the VAE applies a temporal stride of 4 so the latent sequence has length N/4 with 128-dim tokens (concatenating right/left hand and object latents).

## Method

HO-Flow is a two-stage framework. First, an interaction-aware VAE (Inter-VAE) encodes short-horizon hand-object interaction sequences into separate hand and object latent codes by projecting object point clouds into each hand joint's local coordinate frame via the MANO kinematic chain, then aggregating spatial features (PointNet++) with temporal 1D convolutions. Second, a masked auto-regressive flow matching model generates these latent tokens sequentially: a masked transformer attends to the full temporal context while a lightweight SiT-style MLP head predicts each token via a straight-line flow matching objective in continuous space, avoiding both quantization artifacts and the cost of holistic sequence denoising. The model is pre-trained on 5M+ synthetic GraspXL trajectories before fine-tuning on target datasets, and uses classifier-free guidance with a frozen CLIP text encoder and BPS object encoding for conditioning.

## Results

On the GRAB benchmark, HO-Flow (with GraspXL pre-training) achieves the best physical plausibility score of 98.25% (vs. 96.16% for LatentHOI), the lowest interpenetration volume (IV_r = 5.31 cm³, IV_l = 1.24 cm³), and more than doubles the sample diversity (SD = 0.31 vs. 0.13 for LatentHOI). On the out-of-distribution OakInk benchmark, HO-Flow reaches 89.76% Phy and 0.33 SD, substantially outperforming LatentHOI (71.24% Phy, 0.22 SD) and Text2HOI (82.58% Phy, 0.21 SD). On DexYCB for single-hand manipulation, HO-Flow achieves 95.41% Phy and reduces intersection depth from 2.01 cm (LatentHOI) to 1.20 cm. In a user study with 10 evaluators, HO-Flow is preferred 50–56% of the time across all three benchmarks, roughly double the next-best method (LatentHOI at 22–23%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HO-Flow outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark and nearly every metric. Against the closest competitor LatentHOI [26], HO-Flow reduces right-hand interpenetration volume by 17% (5.31 vs. 6.38 cm³ on GRAB; 4.10 vs. 7.22 cm³ on OakInk), improves physical plausibility by 2.1 points on GRAB (98.25% vs. 96.16%) and by 18.5 points on OakInk (89.76% vs. 71.24%), and more than doubles sample diversity (0.31 vs. 0.13 on GRAB; 0.33 vs. 0.22 on OakInk). Against diffusion-based baselines MDM [50] and MLD [6], HO-Flow achieves substantially lower interpenetration (e.g., IV_r 5.31 vs. 9.12/9.62 on GRAB) and higher physical plausibility (98.25% vs. 89.81%/85.68%), while also producing smoother trajectories (lower acceleration and jerk in Table 6). On the ARCTIC articulated-object benchmark, HO-Flow edges out Text2HOI [4] on all five metrics (Acc. 0.94 vs. 0.92, FID 0.12 vs. 0.13, Phys. 0.90 vs. 0.88). The only metric where HO-Flow does not lead is contact ratio on GRAB (CR_r = 11.38 vs. LatentHOI's 11.94), a marginal difference that does not affect overall physical quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

HO-Flow builds most directly on LatentHOI, which introduced latent-space diffusion for hand-object motion generation, but departs in two key ways: it replaces frame-wise encoding with a temporal interaction-aware VAE (Inter-VAE) that captures short-horizon sequences and hand-centric geometric cues, and it swaps the holistic diffusion denoiser for a masked auto-regressive flow matching model that generates tokens sequentially in continuous space. The autoregressive structure echoes HOI-GPT's token-by-token generation, but avoids VQ-VAE discretization by using flow matching in latent space. The pre-training strategy on GraspXL synthetic data and the relative object-motion parameterization are borrowed from broader transfer-learning and coordinate-invariance ideas, applied here specifically to improve generalization to unseen objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HO-Flow provides a practical recipe for physically plausible, temporally coherent HOI generation that generalizes to out-of-distribution objects, which is critical for applications in robotics and character animation where training data is scarce for novel shapes. The finding that the interaction-aware VAE design amplifies the benefit of large-scale synthetic pre-training (R6 vs R5 ablation) suggests a broader principle: expressive, contact-aware representations are necessary to extract useful priors from synthetic data. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences beyond 160 frames, multi-object or multi-agent interactions, and whether the single-layer transformer context encoder can be deepened without overfitting on limited real data.
