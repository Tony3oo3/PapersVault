## Problem

Standard flow matching supervises each intermediate latent point with a single target trajectory, yielding an extremely sparse and high-variance training signal in high-dimensional image spaces. This under-constrained supervision can cause flow collapse, where the learned vector field memorizes specific source-target pairings and maps diverse inputs to overly similar outputs. The paper asks whether densifying supervision by aggregating over all plausible target completions for a given intermediate state can provably reduce gradient variance and improve generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261436/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in the VAE latent space at 256×256 resolution and accepts either discrete class labels (ImageNet-1K) or free-form text prompts (CC12M) as conditioning. For text-to-image, semantic compatibility between candidates and the prompt is enforced via SigLIP2 encoding before latent-space k-NN retrieval. Generation is performed with a 50-step ODE sampler without classifier-free guidance, producing 256×256 RGB images evaluated over 50K samples.

## Method

PAFM replaces the single-target velocity supervision in flow matching with a posterior-weighted expectation over K candidate targets for each intermediate point. The posterior p_t(z^j | z_t^i, y^i) is factorized into the conditional probability path p_t(z_t^i | z^j) (a Gaussian under the rectified-flow interpolant) and the condition likelihood p_t(y^i | z^j), and candidates are drawn from a proposal distribution q_t and re-weighted via self-normalized importance sampling. The paper proves PAFM is an unbiased estimator of the standard FM objective and that its gradient variance is lower-bounded by Σ_g / ESS, where ESS ≥ 1 is the Kish effective sample size, reducing to standard FM when K=1. In practice, candidate targets are sourced via FAISS k-NN retrieval in latent space (restricted to same class for ImageNet), random spatial crops at varying scales, or repeated sampling from the VAE posterior moment, with the original target always included in the set. Across SiT-B/2, SiT-XL/2, and MMDiT architectures, PAFM improves FID50K by up to 3.4 points with only ~6.6% throughput overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (256×256, 400K steps, NFE=50, no CFG), PAFM with K=16 nearest neighbors reduces FID50K from 27.57 to 24.88 for SiT-B/2 and from 11.14 to 9.85 for SiT-XL/2, while sFID drops more dramatically (11.44→6.81 and 8.25→5.24 respectively). On the text-to-image CC12M benchmark with MMDiT, PAFM improves FID50K from 10.37 to 9.45. The best FID gain across all settings reaches 3.42 (augmentation strategy, SiT-B/2), and PAFM incurs only a 6.6% throughput reduction with negligible memory overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard Flow Matching (FM) [Lipman et al., ICLR 2023], which provides one-to-one supervision per intermediate point. PAFM consistently outperforms FM across every configuration tested: on ImageNet-1K, PAFM (K=16) beats FM by 2.69 FID on SiT-B/2 (24.88 vs 27.57) and by 1.29 FID on SiT-XL/2 (9.85 vs 11.14), with sFID improvements of 4.63 and 3.01 respectively. On CC12M text-to-image, PAFM reduces FID50K by 0.92 (9.45 vs 10.37) using the MMDiT architecture. PAFM also improves precision and recall marginally across all settings, and the gains hold across all tested K values (4–64) for the nearest-neighbor strategy, confirming the improvement is not an artifact of a single hyperparameter choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAFM extends the standard Flow Matching / Conditional Flow Matching training objective (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) by replacing the one-to-one source-target supervision with a posterior-weighted expectation over multiple plausible targets for each intermediate latent. It is directly motivated by the sparse-supervision and flow-collapse failure modes identified in recent analyses of rectified-flow-style objectives (Reu et al., 2025), and is compatible with the SiT interpolant transformer (Ma et al., ECCV 2024) and MMDiT rectified-flow architecture (Esser et al., ICML 2024) without modifying their interpolants or geometric regularizations. The key conceptual departure from prior FM work is the factorization of the intractable posterior p_t(z^j | z_t^i, y^i) into a conditional path likelihood and a condition likelihood, combined with self-normalized importance sampling to construct a practical mixture over candidate targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAFM offers a theoretically grounded, drop-in training objective that consistently improves FID by up to 3.4 across model scales, architectures, and conditioning regimes with only ~6.6% throughput overhead, making it a practical upgrade for any existing flow-matching pipeline. The proof that PAFM is an unbiased estimator of FM while provably reducing gradient variance (by a factor of the Kish ESS) gives practitioners confidence that the gains stem from better optimization rather than implicit regularization. Open questions include how to better approximate the condition likelihood p_t(y^i|z^j) for text-to-image (the paper uses a naive class-conditioned surrogate), whether the bias-variance tradeoff in K (peaking at K=16 for SiT-B/2) can be resolved with adaptive or learned proposal distributions, and whether the approach extends to video or other sequential generation settings where the posterior over continuations is even more complex.
