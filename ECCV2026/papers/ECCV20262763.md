## Problem

Bridging 2D sewing patterns to 3D garment geometry has traditionally required physics-based simulation, which is slow and non-differentiable, preventing end-to-end learning and test-time optimization. No prior method provides a unified, simulation-free latent space in which 3D garments and their 2D pattern parameters coexist and can be mapped bidirectionally.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262763/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes a 5120-point cloud with per-point normals for either a draped garment mesh or its pre-simulation BoxMesh (the 3D arrangement of flat panels sharing identical topology with the garment), all normalized to a [-1,1] cube relative to the underlying body model. It produces two outputs: a continuous UDF field (truncated at 0.01) for the 3D garment, and 122 structured GarmentCode design parameters (a mix of continuous dimensions, boolean flags, and categorical choices) that deterministically generate the full 2D sewing pattern.

## Method

StEm-Net is built on the Hunyuan3D 2.1 VAE, modified to use unsigned distance fields so garments can be represented as open, non-watertight manifolds. Two encoders (one for the garment point cloud, one for the BoxMesh) map into a shared 256×64 latent set; during training, corresponding points are sampled from both geometries exploiting their identical topology to align the latent distributions. Decoding uses a transformer with learnable per-panel queries that cross-attend over the latent sequence, followed by lightweight MLP heads predicting the 122 heterogeneous parameters, while a separate 3D decoder reconstructs the UDF. The composite loss combines UDF MSE, type-specific pattern losses (MSE/BCE/CE), and a KL term that regularizes the latent toward a standard normal and enforces BoxMesh–garment consistency. Because the entire pipeline is differentiable, test-time optimization backpropagates a reconstruction loss through the shared latent, simultaneously refining both the 3D geometry and the discrete 2D pattern structure without any simulator.

## Results

On the NeuralTailor test set, StEm-Net reduces Panel L2 error from 5.2 (NeuralTailor) to 4.6, and further to 4.4 with test-time optimization (~15% improvement). Stitch Precision improves from 74.7% to 85.7% (feed-forward) and 88.5% (with optimization). On the more complex GarmentCodeData test set, NeuralTailor fails to converge entirely, while StEm-Net achieves a Panel L2 of 4.4 and 66.6% Stitch Precision. On real CloSe scans, StEm-Net+Optim achieves a bidirectional Chamfer Distance of 0.81 cm versus 1.90 cm for NeuralTailor (a 57% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuralTailor [28] on the NeuralTailor test set, StEm-Net wins on every metric: Panel L2 drops from 5.2 to 4.6 (11.5% reduction), Stitch Precision rises from 74.7% to 85.7%, and Edge accuracy improves from 87.3% to 93.3%. With test-time latent optimization, the Panel L2 gap widens to 15.4% and Stitch Precision reaches 88.5%. On the substantially more complex GarmentCodeData (up to 75 panels per garment), NeuralTailor fails to converge, making StEm-Net the first method to produce results on this dataset. Against the image-based ChatGarment [5] on GarmentCodeData, StEm-Net achieves a Panel L2 of 4.4 versus ChatGarment's 10.1 (a 56% reduction) and Stitch Precision of 66.6% versus 24.1%, despite ChatGarment receiving pre-segmented 2D patches as input. On real-world CloSe scans, StEm-Net+Optim reduces the bidirectional Chamfer Distance from 1.90 cm to 0.81 cm compared to NeuralTailor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeuralTailor, the prior state-of-the-art for sewing pattern prediction from 3D point clouds, which relies on a PointNet encoder, LSTM decoder, and a final physics simulation to produce 3D geometry. The key departure is replacing the simulation bottleneck with a BoxMesh intermediate representation that provides 3D spatial priors topologically aligned with the sewing patterns, enabling a fully differentiable, simulation-free pipeline. Architecturally, the method repurposes the Hunyuan3D 2.1 VAE (originally for watertight SDF objects) by switching to UDFs and adding a second encoder for BoxMesh inputs, demonstrating for the first time the use of a 3D foundation model backbone for 2D sewing pattern parameter regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying 3D garment geometry and 2D sewing patterns in a single differentiable latent space, this work provides the first practical bridge between neural 3D vision and the physical garment manufacturing pipeline, enabling test-time adaptation and 2D-to-3D editing without a simulator. However, the approach is constrained to the 122-parameter GarmentCode design space, single-layer garments, and a fixed body model, and the authors acknowledge that results are still not fully aligned with physical simulation quality. Open questions include scaling to multi-layered attire, diverse materials, varying body shapes, and fine-grained features like pockets, all of which are limited by the scarcity of high-quality paired garment-pattern datasets.
