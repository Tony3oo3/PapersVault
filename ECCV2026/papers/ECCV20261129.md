## Problem

Diffusion-based generative MEF methods (e.g., UltraFusion) can synthesize realistic structures in saturated regions and suppress ghosting, but they are computationally prohibitive at high resolution due to patch-wise sampling at fixed resolutions, and they still struggle to preserve fine structural details in severely over-exposed areas. The paper addresses how to achieve generative-quality MEF at a fraction of the inference cost while maintaining or improving structural fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261129/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of high-resolution RGB images — one over-exposed (OE) and one under-exposed (UE) — and produces a single high-resolution fused HDR image. The coarse diffusion stage operates on bicubic-downsampled versions (e.g., 768×768), while the fine implicit fusion stage queries at the full target resolution in a resolution-agnostic manner. The fine encoder consumes 19×19 local patches around each query pixel from both HR exposures, and the final image is reconstructed by querying all target coordinates through a continuous implicit function.

## Method

LIIFusion is a two-stage coarse-to-fine framework. The coarse stage runs UltraFusion (a diffusion-based guided inpainting model) on downsampled, flow-aligned exposure pairs, augmented by an adaptive exposure correction (AEC) module that computes a per-pixel luminance-difference weight map to attenuate saturated OE regions before fidelity guidance, yielding a structurally reliable low-resolution fused image. The fine stage repurposes the LIIF architecture as a multi-exposure conditional fusion function: a SwinIR backbone encodes the coarse LR output, a 4-layer CNN encodes concatenated HR OE/UE local patches, and at each query coordinate a lightweight MLP decodes the concatenated bilinearly-interpolated features conditioned on normalized coordinates and cell size to predict RGB. This is the first application of implicit neural representations to MEF, enabling the same learned fusion function to integrate exposure evidence from HR inputs of arbitrary resolution without retraining.

## Results

On the dynamic RealHDRV dataset (50 scenes), LIIFusion achieves the best scores across all four NRIQA metrics: MUSIQ 69.52, DeQA-Score 3.8908, PAQ2PIQ 74.06, and HyperIQA 0.6175, while completing inference in 27 minutes versus UltraFusion's 101 minutes (~3.7× speedup). On the UltraFusion Benchmark (100 scenes), LIIFusion again leads all quality metrics (MUSIQ 70.19, PAQ2PIQ 75.59, HyperIQA 0.6467) at 59 minutes versus UltraFusion's 203 minutes (~3.4× speedup). On the static MEFB dataset, LIIFusion attains the highest MUSIQ (68.78), PAQ2PIQ (73.69), and HyperIQA (0.6418), though its MEF-SSIM (0.9201) is slightly below HSDS-MEF (0.9367) and UltraFusion (0.9266).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIIFusion outperforms the primary generative baseline UltraFusion [8] on every NRIQA metric across both RealHDRV and UltraFusion Benchmark (e.g., MUSIQ 69.52 vs. 67.54 on RealHDRV; HyperIQA 0.6467 vs. 0.6214 on UF-Bench) while reducing inference time by roughly 3.5× (27 min vs. 101 min; 59 min vs. 203 min). Against the strongest conventional method HSDS-MEF [44], LIIFusion gains 7.7 MUSIQ points and 0.112 HyperIQA on RealHDRV, and 5.0 MUSIQ points and 0.068 HyperIQA on UF-Bench, at the cost of longer runtime (27 min vs. 18 min on RealHDRV). The lightweight MEF-LUT [13] is orders of magnitude faster (4 s vs. 27 min) but trails LIIFusion by 7.1 MUSIQ and 0.116 HyperIQA on RealHDRV, confirming a quality–speed trade-off. On the static MEFB dataset, LIIFusion leads all NRIQA metrics (MUSIQ 68.78, PAQ2PIQ 73.69, HyperIQA 0.6418) but scores slightly lower on MEF-SSIM (0.9201) than HSDS-MEF (0.9367) and UltraFusion (0.9266), suggesting a minor structural-fidelity gap in static scenes. In the human preference study (Table 9), LIIFusion is chosen as best 61.4% of the time with an average rank of 1.52, compared to UltraFusion's 21.2% and 2.02, while running in 3 min 23 s per image versus UltraFusion's 12 min 9 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIIFusion builds directly on UltraFusion's diffusion-based generative MEF formulation (treating fusion as guided latent-space inpainting) but repositions it as a low-resolution coarse stage rather than a full-resolution patch-wise pipeline. The fine stage repurposes LIIF's local implicit image function architecture, originally designed for single-image super-resolution, into a multi-exposure conditional fusion function that jointly queries coarse generative features and high-resolution OE/UE sources at arbitrary coordinates. This represents the first application of INR to multi-exposure fusion, and the adaptive exposure correction module addresses a specific failure mode of diffusion-based guidance in saturated regions that prior generative MEF methods did not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing generative MEF inference from hours to minutes (3.5× speedup over UltraFusion) while matching or exceeding its quality, LIIFusion makes diffusion-based fusion viable for practical high-resolution imaging pipelines. The finding that pseudo labels from a generative model can substitute for human-crafted SICE annotations suggests a scalable path for training future MEF systems without manual annotation. However, the method remains slower than lightweight approaches like MEF-LUT, is still bounded by the structural reliability of the coarse diffusion stage, and can produce subtle ghosting under extreme motion—leaving real-time generative MEF and motion-aware generative mechanisms as open challenges.
