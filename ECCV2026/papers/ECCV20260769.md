## Problem

Existing driving VLAs predict 3D trajectories directly from 2D images, which entangles the learned policy with sensor-specific camera parameters and prevents scalable training across heterogeneous datasets. Moreover, optimizing in 3D space causes the model to converge to trivial solutions that rely on ego-status (speed, acceleration) rather than visual scene understanding, as evidenced by performance degrading far more when ego-status is removed than when images are masked.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260769/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view camera images (with the rear view rotated 180° for ego-centric consistency) and ego-status as a user command, and autoregressively generates a structured sequence: 2D bounding boxes → reasoning text referencing those boxes → a meta-action (lateral: TURN LEFT/RIGHT/FORWARD; longitudinal: ACCELERATE/DECELERATE/KEEP/STOP) → a 2D pixel trajectory of T waypoints. At inference, the 2D trajectory is deterministically lifted to 3D world coordinates via ray-casting onto a local plane at height z=−h using the target vehicle's camera intrinsics and extrinsics, requiring no learned 2D-to-3D mapping.

## Method

PixelPilot decouples the driving pipeline into a sensor-agnostic 2D planning phase (optimized entirely in image space) and a deterministic 3D lifting phase (applied only at inference), so the VLA never learns a dataset-specific 2D-to-3D projection. Training follows a two-stage knowledge-instilled policy learning strategy: Stage 1 performs multi-task SFT on perception (2D box regression), meta-action prediction, 2D trajectory prediction, integrated control sequences, and reasoning-with-bounding-boxes data synthesized by a teacher VLM; Stage 2 applies GRPO reinforcement learning with dense intermediate rewards (IoU-based perception reward, F1-based meta-action reward, L1/L2 trajectory reward, and PDMS safety score) on verifiable outputs while deliberately leaving free-form reasoning text unrewarded to preserve flexibility. The dense reward structure mitigates long-horizon credit assignment and enforces a causal chain from visual perception through spatial reasoning to final planning, preventing the model from shortcutting to ego-status extrapolation.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop trajectory prediction, PixelPilot achieves a state-of-the-art average L2 error of 0.30 m, a 25% relative reduction over AutoVLA (0.40 m), while also attaining the best average intersection rate (1.77%) among VLA-based peers. In closed-loop Bench2Drive (CARLA) evaluation, PixelPilot achieves the highest driving score (79.14) and success rate (58.87%), surpassing AutoVLA (78.84 / 57.73%) and Orion (77.74 / 54.62%). For 2D object detection on nuScenes, PixelPilot reaches 54.2 mAP, matching or exceeding dedicated detectors like SimPB (54.1) and Deformable DETR (50.2). Zero-shot transfer to Waymo yields a competitive 0.40 m average L2 without any dataset-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among fair VLA comparisons on nuScenes open-loop, PixelPilot (0.30 m avg L2) ties Imprompt-VLA (0.30 m) and substantially outperforms DriveVLM (0.40 m), AutoVLA (0.40 m), DrivePI (0.40 m), EMMA (0.32 m), OmniDrive (0.33 m), and OpenDriveVLA (0.33 m). On safety metrics, PixelPilot achieves the lowest intersection rate (1.77%) among all VLA peers, beating OmniDrive (3.00%) by 41%, while its collision rate (0.25%) is competitive with DrivePI (0.11%) and AutoVLA (0.20%). In closed-loop Bench2Drive, PixelPilot edges out AutoVLA by 0.30 driving score and 1.14% success rate, and surpasses Orion by 1.40 driving score and 4.25% success rate, while maintaining comparable efficiency (153.26 vs. 151.48) and comfortness (38.01 vs. 39.33). For 2D detection, PixelPilot (54.2 mAP) marginally beats SimPB (54.1) and substantially outperforms Deformable DETR (50.2) and StreamPETR (46.5). The ego-status ablation (Table 9) is particularly telling: unlike OmniDrive and Imprompt-VLA, which degrade far more without ego-status (1.98/2.39 m) than without images (0.36 m), PixelPilot shows the opposite trend (0.71 m without ego-status vs. 0.90 m without images), confirming its reliance on visual reasoning rather than trivial kinematic extrapolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelPilot departs from the dominant VLA paradigm (OmniDrive, Orion, OpenDriveVLA, AutoVLA) in which 2D-pretrained VLMs are fine-tuned to directly output 3D world-coordinate trajectories, a formulation that entangles learning with sensor-specific camera parameters. Instead, it reframes the planning problem as a sensor-agnostic 2D-to-2D task in the image plane, reserving the 2D-to-3D lifting as a deterministic, non-learned inference step. For the RL stage, it builds on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) but modifies the reward structure from sparse final-output rewards (as in AutoVLA) to dense intermediate rewards across perception, meta-action, and trajectory, and introduces 'reasoning with bounding boxes' as a structured CoT that anchors planning to explicit visual evidence rather than free-form text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled paradigm offers a principled solution to two well-documented failure modes of driving VLAs: limited data scalability across heterogeneous camera setups and convergence to trivial ego-status extrapolation. The ego-status ablation (Table 9) is the strongest evidence: PixelPilot with images only (0.71 m L2) outperforms ego-status only (0.90 m), inverting the pattern seen in OmniDrive and Imprompt-VLA. However, the approach remains constrained by a local-plane assumption (average 0.16 m height variation over 3 s on nuScenes), requires calibrated cameras at inference, and is validated only on short-horizon (3 s) ego-centric driving; multi-level roads, severe occlusions, and weakly calibrated sensors are acknowledged as open challenges. The zero-shot Waymo transfer (0.40 m avg L2) is encouraging but does not yet surpass dedicated Waymo-tuned baselines like EMMA+ (0.30 m).
