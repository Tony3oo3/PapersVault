## Problem

Streaming 3D reconstruction from continuous video requires maintaining long-range geometric consistency while keeping the per-frame state compact enough for real-time inference. Existing streaming methods either compress state too aggressively (causing geometric forgetting and drift) or retain near-complete history (causing linear memory and compute growth), and none provide a principled, learned mechanism for selectively retaining the most essential geometric context over arbitrarily long sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260357/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

GCT consumes a continuous RGB video stream frame-by-frame in a strictly causal manner (no access to future frames), processing each 518×378 image upon arrival. For each new frame it outputs an absolute camera-to-world pose, a dense depth map, and a corresponding point cloud. The streaming state grows at only 6 tokens per frame (vs. ~500 under causal attention), enabling stable inference at ~20 FPS over sequences exceeding 10,000 frames using a paged KV-cache layout implemented on FlashInfer.

## Method

The core contribution is Geometric Context Attention (GCA), which decomposes the streaming attention context into three complementary, learned mechanisms: (1) an anchor context of n initialization frames with full tokens that fixes coordinate and scale grounding, (2) a local pose-reference window of k most recent frames retaining full image tokens for dense relative geometry, and (3) a trajectory memory that compresses all intermediate frames into 6 compact context tokens each (plus video RoPE for temporal ordering) to provide long-range drift correction. The architecture alternates per-frame attention layers with GCA layers over DINOv2-initialized ViT tokens augmented with camera, register, and anchor tokens, followed by task-specific heads for pose and depth. Training uses a progressive curriculum (24→320 views) with Ulysses context parallelism across 16 GPUs and a relative pose loss over all frame pairs within the sliding window to enforce local trajectory consistency. This structured attention reduces per-frame context growth by roughly 80× compared to causal attention, yielding nearly constant memory and computation regardless of sequence length.

## Results

On Oxford Spires (sparse, 320 frames), GCT achieves an ATE of 5.37 m, surpassing the best offline method (DA3, 12.87 m), the best optimization-based method (VIPE, 10.52 m), and the best online method (CUT3R, 18.16 m). On the dense 3,840-frame setting, GCT maintains ATE 5.60 m while CUT3R degrades to 32.47 m and Wint3R to 32.90 m. Across ETH3D, 7-Scenes, and Tanks and Temples, GCT achieves ATE of 0.43, 0.08, and 0.21 m respectively, and reconstruction F1 scores of 86.80, 82.38, and 65.10 on ETH3D, 7-Scenes, and NRGBD. Inference runs at ~20 FPS at 518×378 resolution over sequences exceeding 10,000 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCT outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against offline feed-forward methods (VGGT, DA3, FastVGGT, π³, Fast3R), GCT achieves a lower ATE on Oxford Spires (5.37 vs. 12.87–34.80 m) despite operating in a single causal pass without access to future frames. Against optimization-based methods (DROID-SLAM, MegaSAM, VIPE), GCT reduces ATE from 10.52–21.84 m to 5.37 m while running in real time (~20 FPS) without iterative bundle adjustment or pose-graph optimization. Among online streaming methods, the margin is largest on long sequences: on the dense 3,840-frame Oxford Spires setting, GCT's ATE (5.60 m) is roughly 6× lower than CUT3R (32.47 m) and Wint3R (32.90 m), which suffer severe drift. On ETH3D reconstruction, GCT achieves F1 86.80, exceeding the next-best Wint3R (77.28) by 9.5 points; on Tanks and Temples, GCT's AUC30 of 92.34 surpasses Stream3R (71.32) by over 20 points. On Sintel and KITTI, GCT attains the lowest ATE (0.10 and 24.12 m) versus DROID-SLAM (0.20, 129.34), VGGT-Long (0.17, 31.89), and TTT3R (0.22, 171.63), confirming consistent superiority in both dynamic and city-scale driving scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCT builds directly on the VGGT architecture (ViT backbone, register tokens, alternating frame/cross-frame attention) but restructures the cross-frame attention from global (offline) to a streaming-compatible Geometric Context Attention (GCA) that decomposes context into three learned components: anchor frames for scale/coordinate grounding, a sliding pose-reference window for dense local geometry, and a compact trajectory memory for long-range drift correction. This decomposition is conceptually borrowed from classical SLAM (reference frame + local window + global map) but replaces hand-crafted keyframe selection and pose-graph optimization with end-to-end differentiable attention. The paper positions itself against two existing streaming paradigms—recurrent state compression (CUT3R, TTT3R) and causal attention with full-history caching (StreamVGGT, Stream3R, Wint3R)—arguing that both either forget essential geometry or retain too much redundancy, and that structured selective retention is the missing principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~80× reduction in per-frame context growth over causal attention, combined with ~20 FPS inference over 10,000+ frame sequences, makes learned streaming 3D reconstruction practically deployable for robotics and AR where memory and latency are hard constraints. The finding that a single-pass feed-forward model can outperform both offline feed-forward methods (VGGT, DA3) and iterative optimization-based SLAM (VIPE, DROID-SLAM) on trajectory accuracy suggests that the field's assumption that long-sequence consistency requires explicit optimization or loop closure may be revisable. Open questions include how GCT handles truly dynamic scenes, scene transitions (leaving and re-entering environments), and whether the fixed anchor initialization degrades in unbounded outdoor settings where the initial scale may be poorly conditioned.
