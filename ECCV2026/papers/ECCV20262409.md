## Problem

Online temporal action segmentation (TAS) must predict frame-wise labels using only past frames, yet the resulting incomplete temporal context introduces ambiguity that causes fragmented, over-segmented predictions. Existing online methods improve historical dependency modeling but cannot access the future dependencies that offline models exploit, leaving a fundamental gap in temporal understanding that no causal architecture alone can close.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted I3D frame features (dimension D) arriving sequentially in an online stream; during training the full sequence is available, but at inference only frames up to the current timestep are accessible. The future estimator specifically takes k recent historical frames (k determined by the temporal receptive field of the estimator, e.g., ~100 frames for a 10-layer TCN with s=2) to approximate future dependencies. Output is a per-frame probability distribution over N_C action classes, from which frame-wise labels and temporally coherent segments are derived; the framework is also validated on skeleton-based inputs (LARA, PKU-MMD) via a 1×1 convolutional adapter.

## Method

DPTM is a two-stage framework that first captures and then transfers future dependencies under causal constraints. In Stage 1 (offline-style training), a temporal decomposition strategy splits bidirectional modeling into a causal historical path and an anti-causal future path with identical architectures, whose outputs are concatenated and smoothed by a cross-temporal fusion module (stacked causal TCNs). In Stage 2, the future path is replaced by a predictive path—a future estimator with linearly growing dilation (2×i+1) that compresses recent historical frames into a representation distilled (L1 loss) to match the frozen future encoder's output, so the online model approximates future dependencies without accessing future frames. The key distinction from prior approaches is that rather than predicting low-level future frames or naively distilling full offline representations (which entangle historical and future information), DPTM explicitly disentangles and selectively transfers only the future dependency component, reducing temporal ambiguity while preserving strict causality at inference.

## Results

On the three main RGB benchmarks, DPTM (ASFormer backbone) achieves Avg scores of 78.7 (GTEA), 56.6 (50Salads), and 56.9 (Breakfast), outperforming the strongest online baselines ProTAS (74.8/53.7/54.2) and OnlineTAS (71.7/41.7/35.2) by 3.9, 2.9, and 2.7 Avg points respectively. With the MS-TCN backbone, DPTM reaches 72.7/48.3/30.5 Avg, improving over ProTAS (70.9/38.3/26.0) by up to 10.0 points on 50Salads. The largest gains appear in Edit and F1 metrics, confirming improved temporal coherence over frame-level accuracy alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPTM consistently outperforms all online baselines across all three RGB datasets and both backbone architectures. Against ProTAS [23], the strongest online competitor, DPTM (ASFormer) gains +3.9 Avg on GTEA (78.7 vs 74.8), +2.9 on 50Salads (56.6 vs 53.7), and +2.7 on Breakfast (56.9 vs 54.2), with the largest margin in Edit score (+6.0 on GTEA). Against OnlineTAS [36], the gap is even wider: +7.0 Avg on GTEA, +14.9 on 50Salads, and +21.7 on Breakfast, reflecting OnlineTAS's severe over-segmentation. Compared to the causal (no-future) versions of the same backbones, DPTM adds +4.3 Avg on GTEA and +11.1 on 50Salads with MS-TCN, confirming that the predictive distillation of future dependencies is the primary source of gain. On skeleton-based datasets (LARA, PKU-MMD), DPTM also beats ProTAS by +5.9 and +3.7 Avg respectively, demonstrating generalization beyond RGB inputs. The method does not surpass the offline baselines (MS-TCN offline: 78.9/72.7/53.3; ASFormer offline: 84.5/81.9/70.5), but narrows the offline-to-online gap substantially, e.g., from 13.6 to 5.8 Avg on GTEA with ASFormer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multi-round refinement framework of MS-TCN and the attention-based architecture of ASFormer, adapting both to causal (online) settings. It departs from prior online TAS methods (ProTAS, OnlineTAS) that exclusively model historical dependencies, and from naive offline-to-online distillation approaches (PKD-OAD, OODL) that transfer entangled bidirectional representations. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage pipeline: first decomposing bidirectional temporal dependencies into separate historical and future paths via complementary temporal masks, then transferring only the future component to a causal predictive path via knowledge distillation from recent history.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation in online TAS—temporal ambiguity from incomplete context—by showing that future dependencies can be implicitly captured and transferred without violating causality, yielding substantial gains in Edit and F1 scores across three datasets and two backbones. The validation on skeleton-based data (LARA, PKU-MMD) suggests the approach is modality-agnostic. However, the ~41% inference speed reduction at matched dimensionality (11.11 vs 18.96 FPS) limits real-time applicability, and the authors acknowledge this as an open problem. Whether the decomposition-then-distill paradigm generalizes beyond action segmentation to other sequential prediction tasks (e.g., online activity detection, gesture recognition) remains unexplored.
