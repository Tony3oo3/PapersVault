## Problem

Existing multimodal fact-checking (MFC) systems are constrained by reliance on synthetic or curated datasets that fail to capture the noisy, contextual nature of in-the-wild social media posts, and by architectures that either restrict comparisons to intra-modality consistency or apply unconstrained all-to-all fusion, neither of which isolates the nuanced cross-modal entailment and contradiction relations between a post and external evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261728/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a CLIP ViT-L/14 image embedding (768-dim), a text post embedding, and up to M reranked evidence excerpts (median 4, max 16 per post; median excerpt length 61 words) retrieved via VLM-generated search queries and reverse image search. Evidence is split into text-retrieved (79.4%) and image-retrieved (20.6%) subsets, each independently reranked by cosine similarity before selection. The output is a single binary veracity score (truthful vs. misinformation) produced by a linear layer with sigmoid activation over a 6144-dim fused representation.

## Method

TRENT projects image, text, and evidence embeddings into a shared 512-dim latent space via modality-specific linear layers, then processes them through three parallel cross-attention transformer blocks (8 heads, GELU FFN, post-LN residual): image→all-evidence, text→all-evidence, and text↔image internal consistency. The three contextualized outputs are combined via a relational fusion function adapted from NLI that concatenates [z₁; z₂; |z₁−z₂|; z₁⊙z₂] per stream, explicitly encoding entailment and contradiction signals. The resulting 12·d_c vector is classified by a single linear layer trained with binary cross-entropy. The full model contains only 5.13M trainable parameters and requires 6.6–32.6 MFLOPs at inference, making it orders of magnitude lighter than competing VLMs while outperforming them on the X-POSE benchmark.

## Results

On X-POSE (random split), TRENT achieves a macro-F1 of 63.10% on the full test set, improving to 67.36% and 70.83% on high-agreement subsets (h≥80% and h≥90%), outperforming all specialized MFC models and commercial VLMs. Under the more realistic past-only evidence setting, TRENT scores 62.22% (random split) and 60.14% (chronological split), still leading all baselines. On NewsCLIPpings+ and VERITE, TRENT reaches 92.5% and 79.6% macro-F1 respectively, competitive with AITR (93.3%, 81.0%) while surpassing RED-DOT, SNIFFER, E2LVLM, TRUST-VL, DEFAME, and MAD-Sherlock. TRENT achieves these results with only 5.13M trained parameters and 6.6–32.6 MFLOPs, compared to 16.9M (RED-DOT) and 18.7M (AITR) for specialized baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRENT outperforms all 16 baselines on X-POSE across every evaluation setting. Among specialized MFC models, all-to-all architectures (RED-DOT at 61.56%, AITR at 58.46%, DT-Transformer at 57.99%) beat intra-modality consistency models (CCN 58.61%, ECENet 57.92%, ERIC-FND 55.56%), but TRENT surpasses even the best all-to-all model (RED-DOT) by 1.54 points on the full test set and by 5.36 points on the h≥90% subset. Among commercial VLMs, Gemini 3 Flash (61.33%) and Gemma 4 (60.86%) are the strongest, yet TRENT leads by 1.77 and 2.24 points respectively on the random split, with the gap widening to 2.52 points over Gemma 4 on h≥90%. On NewsCLIPpings+ and VERITE (Table 3), TRENT (92.5%, 79.6%) trails only AITR-with-MUSE (93.3%, 81.0%)—a configuration that exploits retrieval shortcuts—and beats MAD-Sherlock (90.8%, 79.5%), RED-DOT (90.3%, 76.9%), and all VLM-based detectors (SNIFFER, E2LVLM, TRUST-VL, DEFAME). Notably, the similarity-based MUSE baseline drops to near-random on X-POSE (53.36%), confirming that X-POSE avoids the retrieval shortcuts that inflate scores on curated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRENT carves a middle path between two established MFC paradigms: intra-modality consistency models (CCN, ECENet) that examine text-to-text and image-to-image evidence in isolation, and all-to-all attention models (RED-DOT, AITR) that jointly attend over all modalities without isolating specific relational channels. By introducing three dedicated cross-attention streams (text→evidence, image→evidence, image↔text) combined with an NLI-inspired relational fusion, the architecture explicitly models entailment and contradiction without the computational overhead of VLM-based approaches. On the data side, X-POSE departs from both synthetic decontextualization datasets (NewsCLIPpings+, FACTIFY) and curated fact-checking benchmarks (VERITE, VeriTaS) by sourcing uncurated user posts from X's Community Notes and augmenting them with VLM-refined, credibility-filtered web evidence—a pipeline that mirrors professional fact-checker workflows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

X-POSE is the first dataset to enable both training and evaluation of MFC models on in-the-wild, community-annotated posts, closing a gap where prior work either relied on synthetic data or used Community Notes solely for evaluation. The stark performance drop from ~90% on synthetic benchmarks to ~63% on X-POSE quantifies how much retrieval shortcuts and curated claims inflate prior results, making this a valuable reality check for the field. Open questions include how to handle the ~50% of retrieved articles lacking credibility ratings, the quality-scale tradeoff inherent in consensus-based filtering, and whether the triangulation approach generalizes to cross-platform settings (TikTok, Facebook) or to AI-generated media, which the paper explicitly excludes.
