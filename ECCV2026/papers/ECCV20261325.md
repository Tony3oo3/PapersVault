## Problem

Existing 3D reassembly methods rely almost exclusively on geometric features, which become ambiguous or insufficient for small, eroded, or visually complex fragments, and they ignore discriminative surface cues such as paint, grain, or tool marks. Additionally, state-of-the-art models frequently produce physically implausible assemblies with intersecting fragments because they lack explicit non-overlap constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261325/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes colored point clouds of fractured fragments, where each point carries 3D coordinates, a surface normal, and an RGB color vector; fragments are recentered at the origin and 5k points are sampled per object. A designated anchor fragment (the largest at inference) fixes the global reference frame to resolve the SE(3) gauge ambiguity. The output is a set of per-fragment SE(3) rigid transformations (rotation matrix R and translation vector t) that, when applied, reassemble the fragments into the canonical object configuration.

## Method

E-M3RF fuses two parallel encoders: an SO(3)-equivariant VN-Transformer that produces rotation-consistent geometric features from point coordinates and normals, and a standard Transformer that encodes per-point RGB colors; the two streams are concatenated per point into a joint multimodal embedding. These features condition a conditional Riemannian flow-matching network on the SE(3) Lie group, which learns a time-dependent velocity field whose Euler integration transports each fragment from a noisy initial pose to its target assembled pose via geodesic rotation interpolation and linear translation interpolation. A differentiable no-overlap loss, computed from soft volumetric occupancy masks and pairwise IoU, penalizes fragment interpenetration during training only. The encoder is pretrained on an auxiliary fracture-boundary segmentation task to capture fracture-interface geometry before the flow-matching stage, and Fourier positional encodings of coordinates, normals, and scale are appended to provide high-frequency geometric priors.

## Results

On the RePAIR dataset (the most challenging, with colored fresco fragments), E-M3RF achieves 37.91° rotation RMSE, 27.93 mm translation RMSE, 35.91% Part Accuracy, and 2.17 Chamfer Distance, outperforming the strongest baseline GARF (49.31°, 32.19 mm, 31.14%, 2.66) by 23.1% in rotation, 13.2% in translation, and 18.4% in CD. Zero-shot generalization to the unseen Presious dataset yields 30.26° rotation and 12.85 mm translation with 57.49% PA, again the best among all methods. On geometry-only benchmarks (Breaking Bad, Fantastic Breaks, FRACTURA), E-M3RF without color still leads, reducing rotation error by 13–25% and CD by up to 35.5% over GARF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-M3RF outperforms all five baselines on RePAIR across every metric. Against GARF [11] (the strongest geometry-only baseline), E-M3RF reduces rotation RMSE from 49.31° to 37.91° (−23.1%), translation from 32.19 mm to 27.93 mm (−13.2%), and CD from 2.66 to 2.17 (−18.4%), while improving PA from 31.14% to 35.91%. Even when GARF is augmented with naive color concatenation, E-M3RF still leads by 9.65° in rotation and 1.08 in CD. Against DiffAssemble [20] and PMTR [10], the margins are substantially larger (e.g., 31.6° and 38.8° lower rotation RMSE on RePAIR, respectively). On the zero-shot Presious test, E-M3RF wins on rotation, translation, and PA but trails GARF (w/ color) on CD (20.95 vs. 17.05), suggesting the non-overlap constraint is less effective on this small, diverse set. On geometry-only FRACTURA, E-M3RF (w/o color) beats GARF by 16.9% in rotation and 87.3% in translation, confirming the equivariant backbone generalizes without color cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

E-M3RF sits at the intersection of two recent threads in 3D reassembly: flow-based pose estimation on SE(3) (building on Riemannian flow matching from Chen & Lipman, ICLR 2024, and the GARF/PuzzleFusion++ lineage) and rotation-equivariant representation learning (via the VN-Transformer / Vector Neurons framework). The key departure from prior geometry-only pipelines (GARF, PF++, DiffAssemble) is the explicit fusion of per-point RGB color into the fragment representation and the imposition of SO(3) equivariance as an inductive bias in the geometric encoder, rather than relying on data-driven orientation handling. A differentiable non-overlap loss, absent from all compared baselines, is introduced as a training-time physical constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For cultural heritage reconstruction (frescoes, eroded ceramics), geometry alone is often ambiguous, and the demonstrated 23% rotation-error reduction on RePAIR shows that color cues and equivariant structure are not merely incremental but address a real failure mode of current SOTA. The ablation revealing that rotation-invariance (a VN-Invariant projection) degrades performance more than removing equivariance entirely is a useful cautionary result for practitioners applying equivariant networks to pose estimation. Open questions include scaling to high-density point clouds or mesh-based representations, extending beyond rigid SE(3) to handle non-rigid or partially missing fragments, and whether the non-overlap loss generalizes to scenes with many more than 40 fragments.
