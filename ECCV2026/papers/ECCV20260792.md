## Problem

Existing medical vision-language models are pretrained on large but unstructured collections of image-caption pairs with freeform, inconsistently expressed text, which weakens image-text alignment and limits transfer to diagnosis-oriented tasks. They fail to capture the systematic visual knowledge encoded in well-defined medical phenotype ontologies, such as the Human Phenotype Ontology, which provide standardized terms and hierarchical structure that closely reflect clinical diagnostic language.

## Input / Output

The model consumes medical images across seven imaging modalities (dermatoscopy, histopathology, X-ray, ultrasound, OCT, retinal, and blood) paired with text that includes figure captions, phenotype terms, definitions, and synonyms drawn from the HPO ontology. During pretraining, each image-caption pair is linked to a specific phenotype node in PhenoKG (5,839 phenotype groups, 13,812 specific phenotypes, ~524K pairs), and the text encoder additionally receives structured ontology attributes (name/synonym-definition pairs and head-relation/tail pairs from is-a edges). At inference, the dual-encoder produces aligned image and text embeddings in a shared space, supporting zero-shot classification via prompt templates, four-directional cross-modal retrieval (I2T, T2I, I2P, P2I), and linear probing on frozen visual features.

## Method

PhenoLIP is a two-stage pretraining framework. In Stage 1, a phenotype knowledge encoder (initialized from PubmedBERT) is trained with an InfoNCE contrastive objective on PhenoKG's textual ontology, pulling together multiple attribute descriptions of the same phenotype (name, definition, synonyms, hierarchical is-a relations) while pushing apart unrelated concepts. In Stage 2, the visual encoder (BiomedCLIP ViT-B) and text encoder (initialized from the trained knowledge encoder) undergo standard image-text contrastive pretraining on the 524K multimodal pairs, augmented by an auxiliary knowledge distillation loss that treats the frozen knowledge encoder as a teacher: for each caption, the student text embedding is contrasted against the teacher's knowledge embedding, with in-batch negatives, effectively regularizing the text encoder to inhabit the pre-structured ontology semantic space. The overall objective is L_VLP = L_M + α·L_KD, combining the multimodal contrastive loss with the distillation loss. The data pipeline itself is a key contribution: it crawls PMC-OA using terminal phenotype keywords, filters non-medical images via DINOv3-based K-means clustering (400 clusters), splits compound figures with DAB-DETR, refines captions with Qwen3, and aligns subfigures to text segments using Qwen2.5-VL.

## Results

PhenoLIP achieves 36.56% average zero-shot classification accuracy across 7 imaging modalities (dermatology, pathology, radiology, hematology, histology, ophthalmology, and phenotype recognition), surpassing the next-best model BIOMEDICA (28.50%) by 8.06 percentage points. In cross-modal retrieval on PhenoBench, it reaches 63.30% R@10 for image-to-text retrieval (vs. 40.51% for BIOMEDICA) and 13.84% R@10 for image-to-phenotype retrieval (vs. 8.12%), a 15.03% relative improvement over the prior state-of-the-art. On the challenging Face2Gene rare facial phenotype benchmark, PhenoLIP attains the best R@50 of 55.05% and F1 of 2.62%, outperforming all baselines across both retrieval and matching metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhenoLIP outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation setting. Against general-domain VLMs (OpenCLIP, SigLIP2, CoCa), the gap is largest: PhenoLIP's 36.56% average zero-shot accuracy exceeds OpenCLIP (18.15%) by 18.4 points and SigLIP2 (10.97%) by 25.6 points, confirming that domain-specific phenotype knowledge is critical for medical imaging. Among biomedical VLMs, PhenoLIP surpasses BIOMEDICA (28.50%) by 8.06 points in classification and by 22.79 points in I2T retrieval R@10 (63.30% vs. 40.51%), while also leading in the more clinically meaningful I2P retrieval (13.84% vs. 8.12% R@10). Compared to PMC-CLIP (27.71%) and BiomedCLIP (27.41%), PhenoLIP gains roughly 9 percentage points in average accuracy, with particularly large margins in radiology (49.03% vs. 29.10%/28.47%) and ophthalmology (51.63% vs. 43.75%/40.18%). The one exception is dermatology, where BIOMEDICA's ViT-L encoder (56.76%) slightly edges out PhenoLIP's ViT-B (55.08%), suggesting that model scale still matters in phenotype-sparse domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style medical vision-language pretraining line (BiomedCLIP, BIOMEDICA, PMC-CLIP) by injecting structured phenotype ontology knowledge via a two-stage distillation pipeline. It departs from prior knowledge-enhanced medical VLMs (KAD, MedKLIP, DermLIP, KEEP) that incorporate domain-specific or general medical KGs (UMLS, disease ontologies) by instead grounding pretraining in the Human Phenotype Ontology's hierarchical is-a structure, definitions, and synonyms at a scale (524K image-text pairs linked to 3,000+ phenotypes) not previously attempted. The key architectural departure is training a dedicated phenotype knowledge encoder first and then using it as a frozen teacher during VLP, rather than augmenting captions or triplets on the fly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhenoKG and PhenoBench fill a critical gap: prior medical VLMs were trained on unstructured, freeform captions, and no large-scale phenotype-centric multimodal resource existed. The substantial gains (8% classification, 15% retrieval over BIOMEDICA) suggest that structured ontological priors were being left on the table, and the phenotype-centric retrieval benchmark (I2P/P2I) provides a clinically meaningful evaluation axis missing from prior work. Limitations include the observed lack of improvement on non-phenotype tasks (TissueMNIST), dependence on HPO coverage across modalities, and the fact that the knowledge encoder is frozen during VLP, potentially limiting adaptability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumetric imaging, other ontology systems (e.g., SNOMED, ICD), and whether the distillation can be made end-to-end.
