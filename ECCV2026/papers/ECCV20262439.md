## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods target high-level tasks (e.g., classification) where many samples share a common label, enabling compression of shared semantics. Low-level medical image enhancement, however, involves dense many-to-many pixel-level mappings where each input corresponds to a unique target, making distillation inherently underdetermined and no prior DD framework has addressed this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262439/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw multi-subject CT or MRI volume dataset (e.g., 512×512 CT slices or 256×256 MRI slices across 10–48 subjects) and produces a compact per-subject synthetic dataset of paired low-quality/high-quality images. The high-quality side is a personalized anatomical prior, while the low-quality side is generated by applying a task-specific degradation operator (4× downsampling for CT SR, 2× for MRI SR, or noise-plus-back-projection for LDCT restoration). Only the distilled pairs are shared downstream; raw patient volumes are never transmitted.

## Method

The framework first constructs a shared anatomical prior by randomly selecting v slices from one representative subject's 3D volume, providing global structural constraints that mitigate the underdetermined nature of low-level DD. A Structure-Preserving Personalized Generation (SPG) module then personalizes this prior per subject: a learnable subject-specific code modulates the prior via channel-wise scaling, a subject-agnostic convolutional layer reshapes the output to the desired number of images per subject, and a randomly selected prior slice is added to preserve pixel-level fidelity. Task-specific degradation operators convert the personalized data into HQ-LQ training pairs. Finally, subject-aware gradient matching aligns the cosine similarity of network gradients computed on the distilled pairs with those computed on the corresponding subject's raw pairs, encoding subject-specific training dynamics without copying anatomical structures. Distillation is performed independently per subject to avoid cross-subject anatomical conflicts and training collapse.

## Results

On CT super-resolution (×4, NRI=10, CR=98%), the proposed method with IPS=5 achieves 34.57 dB PSNR and 92.83 SSIM, compared to 36.10 dB / 94.86 for full-data training. On MRI super-resolution (×2, NRI=10, CR=99%), it reaches 28.35 dB PSNR and 88.27 SSIM versus 29.07 dB / 90.46 for full data. In the large-scale 48-subject CT experiment (NRI=10), the method attains 36.07 dB PSNR and 95.00 SSIM, nearly matching the full-data baseline of 36.95 dB / 95.58. For CT restoration (REDCNN, NRI=10), the method achieves 28.38 dB PSNR and 57.43 SSIM, approaching the full-data result of 28.90 dB / 58.08.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five coreset-selection baselines: Random, Random*, Uniform, Herding [26], and K-Center Greedy [20]. On CT super-resolution (NRI=10), the proposed method (IPS=5) achieves 34.57 dB PSNR, outperforming K-Center (32.92 dB) by 1.65 dB and Herding (32.86 dB) by 1.71 dB, while also surpassing the full-data gap by only 1.53 dB. On MRI super-resolution (NRI=10), the method reaches 28.35 dB PSNR versus 26.76 dB for K-Center and 26.72 dB for Herding, a margin of approximately 1.6 dB. The coreset baselines cluster tightly together (within ~0.5 dB of each other), indicating that simple selection strategies struggle with the many-to-many pixel-level mapping inherent to low-level tasks. The proposed method's advantage is most pronounced at higher IPS settings, where subject-aware gradient matching and anatomical prior initialization compound to close the gap with full-data training. In the large-scale 48-subject experiment, the method narrows the full-data gap to just 0.88 dB PSNR, whereas the best baseline (K-Center) remains 3.21 dB below full data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset distillation (DD) paradigm, which has been developed primarily for high-level classification tasks (many-to-one mapping), to the fundamentally different low-level image-to-image setting (many-to-many, pixel-level mapping). Its gradient-matching objective directly adapts the formulation from Zhao et al. (ICLR 2021), but the key departure is the introduction of a shared anatomical prior as initialization and a subject-wise distillation strategy, neither of which appears in prior DD work. The Structure-Preserving Personalized Generation (SPG) module, which modulates the prior with a learnable subject-specific code, is a new architectural component not present in existing DD or coreset-selection methods. The paper positions itself against coreset selection baselines (Herding, K-Center) rather than other DD methods, since no prior DD approach has been adapted to the low-level regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first DD framework for low-level medical image enhancement, addressing a practical bottleneck: medical imaging pipelines require large, sensitive datasets that are costly to store and legally restricted from sharing. By distilling to a compact synthetic set that preserves training dynamics (not just appearance), the method offers a privacy-preserving path to data sharing. Limitations include evaluation on only 10–48 subjects, 2D slice-level processing, and two tasks (super-resolution and restoration); scalability to full 3D volumes, additional modalities (ultrasound, PET), and more complex degradation models remains open. The weak baselines (coreset selection rather than adapted DD methods) leave room for stronger comparisons in future work.
