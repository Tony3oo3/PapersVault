## Problem

Variance-Based Pruning (VBP) identifies redundant neurons via activation variance but suffers from two critical limitations: statistical noise in finite-sample activation covariance corrupts neuron importance rankings at aggressive pruning ratios (≥50%), and its bias-only mean-shift compensation cannot fully account for the reconstruction error introduced by removing entire neurons. This leaves substantial accuracy degradation unaddressed, particularly for small models and high compression ratios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model (DeiT, Swin, or ConvNeXt in Tiny/Small/Base variants) and a calibration set of 8,192 ImageNet-1K images collected without data augmentation to capture unperturbed activation statistics. It produces a structurally pruned model where MLP hidden dimensions are globally reduced by a target ratio (40–70%), while preserving all external input and output dimensions so the pruned blocks integrate seamlessly back into the network. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring only a single forward pass over calibration data and closed-form weight updates, completing in under one minute on a single RTX 3090 with peak VRAM under 10 GB.

## Method

DVBP+OB²C unifies neuron selection and weight recovery through a single activation covariance matrix. First, it computes the centered covariance online via Chan et al.'s partitioned second-moment update, then denoises the spectrum by fitting the Marchenko-Pastur distribution to estimate the noise scale σ and hard-thresholding eigenvalues below the theoretical upper bound λ₊, retaining only signal components. The denoised diagonal entries yield robust variance scores for neuron selection, blended with raw scores via a pruning-ratio-weighted hybrid metric. For weight recovery, the paper proves that incorporating mean-shift compensation into the OBC reconstruction objective reduces the layer-wise Hessian exactly to 2N·C (the centered covariance), yielding a closed-form multi-weight update δW = −W_{:,P}([C⁻¹]_{P,P})⁻¹[C⁻¹]_{P,:} that simultaneously compensates surviving neurons. Pruning is applied globally across all targeted MLP blocks: the second linear layer receives the OB²C weight update, and the subsequent layer receives the optimal bias shift b* = (W−Ŵ)μ.

## Results

At 50% MLP pruning, DVBP+OB²C retains over 90% of the original Top-1 accuracy on Small and Base variants (e.g., 70.25% vs. 79.86% full for DeiT-S; 77.24% vs. 83.32% full for Swin-S), while Tiny models retain ~80%. The method outperforms the closest baseline VBP by up to 29.46% on ConvNeXt-T (44.90% vs. 15.44%) and 7.33% on Swin-S (77.24% vs. 69.91%) at 50% pruning, with the gap widening to 25.49% at 60% pruning on Swin-S. All results are zero-shot (no fine-tuning), evaluated on ImageNet-1K with 8,192 calibration images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three structured pruning baselines: VBP (Berisha et al., ICCV 2025), Magnitude pruning (Han et al., NeurIPS 2015), and SNIP (Lee et al., ICLR 2019). DVBP+OB²C consistently outperforms VBP across all models and pruning ratios, with the advantage growing at higher compression: at 50% pruning the gap is 7.33% on Swin-S and 29.46% on ConvNeXt-T, expanding to 25.49% on Swin-S at 60% pruning. Magnitude pruning and SNIP both collapse to near-chance accuracy (0.1–4%) on most models at 50–60% pruning, and on ConvNeXt architectures they degrade to essentially random performance (≤2% Top-1). The OB²C weight-update component is the primary driver of the gain over VBP (contributing ~4–14% accuracy), while the Marchenko-Pastur denoising provides an additional 1–2% improvement, particularly at aggressive pruning ratios where raw variance scores become unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Variance-Based Pruning (VBP, Berisha et al., ICCV 2025), which selects neurons by activation variance and compensates via mean-shift, by addressing two identified weaknesses: statistical noise in finite-sample covariance estimates and the insufficiency of bias-only compensation. The denoising component adapts the Gavish–Donoho noise-scale estimator (IEEE TIT 2014) to the activation covariance spectrum via Marchenko–Pastur fitting, while the compensation component embeds mean-shift into the Optimal Brain Compression (OBC, Frantar et al., NeurIPS 2022) objective, yielding a closed-form multi-weight update whose Hessian reduces exactly to the centered activation covariance. This unification means the same covariance matrix drives both neuron selection and optimal weight recovery, a structural simplification absent in prior OBC-based pruning pipelines that use separate criteria for selection and recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, training-free structured pruning pipeline that retains over 90% Top-1 accuracy at 50% MLP pruning on Small/Base ViTs and ConvNeXts, with peak VRAM under 10 GB and sub-minute execution—making it immediately deployable for edge inference without retraining. However, the hybrid raw/denoised scoring rule is explicitly acknowledged as heuristic, the approach targets only MLP blocks (not attention), and ConvNeXt-T accuracy at 50% pruning (44.90%) remains far below the full model (84.17%), suggesting the method's gains are most pronounced on transformer architectures. Open questions include developing a theoretically grounded scoring blend, extending the framework to attention heads and cross-layer dependencies, and validating whether the MP denoising assumption holds for non-i.i.d. activation distributions in very deep or multimodal models.
