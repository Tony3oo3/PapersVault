## Problem

Semantic encoders (e.g., DINOv2, CLIP) and pixel encoders (e.g., SD-VAE) capture fundamentally different aspects of visual content, yet prior unification efforts force these heterogeneous representations into a single module through trade-offs rather than genuine integration. The paper identifies an unexplored spectral correspondence—semantic features concentrate in low-frequency bands while pixel features extend into high-frequency bands—and asks whether this structure can be exploited to harmonize both within one latent space without sacrificing either semantic abstraction or pixel fidelity.

## Input / Output

UAE takes a 256×256 RGB image and produces a 2D latent grid (e.g., 768×16×16 for DINOv2-B) that is channel-wise DCT-transformed and zig-zag reordered into a 1D frequency-ordered token sequence of length 256 (768×256 in frequency parameterization). The decoder reconstructs the full-resolution image from these tokens, and the same latent space supports class-conditional generation via a diffusion transformer operating either in the spatial domain (after inverse DCT) or directly on the frequency-ordered coefficients with a coarse-to-fine token schedule (64→256 tokens).

## Method

UAE is initialized from a pretrained semantic encoder (DINOv2, CLIP, or SigLIP2) and applies a channel-wise 2D DCT to the patch-token grid, followed by zig-zag reordering that approximately sorts coefficients from low to high spatial frequency. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 freezes the encoder and trains a pixel decoder with reconstruction and adversarial losses; Stage 2 unfreezes the encoder and adds a semantic-wise alignment loss restricted to only the lowest-frequency bands (K_base = 25% of tokens for DINOv2), leaving higher-frequency bands unconstrained to specialize in pixel-level detail. A frequency-masked prediction objective randomly zeros 50–75% of high-frequency tokens during Stage 2, forcing the decoder to infer fine-grained textures from the preserved low-frequency semantic structure. For generative modeling, the frequency-ordered tokens enable a coarse-to-fine diffusion strategy where the transformer progressively models from low to high frequency bands, and the compact 64-token low-frequency subset yields a ~4× GFLOPs reduction over the full spatial latent.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K reconstruction, UAE(DINOv2-B) achieves PSNR 32.17, SSIM 0.92, and rFID 0.35, while UAE(CLIP-L) reaches PSNR 36.58, SSIM 0.96, and rFID 0.04—surpassing FLUX-VAE (rFID 0.18) and SD-VAE-3 (rFID 0.20). For class-conditional generation on ImageNet 256×256, UAE attains gFID 1.52 and IS 234.5, matching RAE (gFID 1.51) while achieving higher precision (0.81 vs. 0.79), and substantially outperforming UniFlow (gFID 2.45) and SVG (gFID 3.36). Linear probing accuracy of 83.0% (DINOv2-B) confirms strong semantic representation quality, and in pixel-space JIT, UAE reduces FID from 16.06 to 14.55 at 80 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAE(DINOv2-B) dramatically outperforms RAE(DINOv2-B) on reconstruction, improving PSNR from 18.05 to 32.17 and rFID from 2.04 to 0.35 on ImageNet, while matching RAE's generative gFID (1.52 vs. 1.51) with higher precision (0.81 vs. 0.79). Against UniFlow(DINOv2-L), UAE(CLIP-L) achieves a substantially lower rFID (0.04 vs. 0.17) and higher PSNR (36.58 vs. 32.32), though UniFlow retains a slight edge in rFID over UAE(DINOv2-B) (0.17 vs. 0.35). UAE(CLIP-L) surpasses FLUX-VAE on rFID (0.04 vs. 0.18) and SD-VAE-3 (0.04 vs. 0.20), while FLUX-VAE maintains a marginal PSNR advantage (32.74 vs. 36.58 for UAE-CLIP-L actually wins). In generative modeling, UAE (gFID 1.52) ties RAE (1.51) but clearly beats UniFlow (2.45), SVG (3.36), SiT (8.61), and DiT (9.62). In pixel-space JIT, UAE(JIT) improves FID from 16.06 to 14.55 and IS from 123.30 to 132.57 at 80 epochs. For multimodal understanding, zero-shot UAE-CLIP-L drop-in into LLaVA-1.5 recovers ~97% of POPE accuracy (0.811 vs. 0.832) with higher precision (0.953 vs. 0.896).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RAE framework [50], which replaces the traditional VAE encoder with a pretrained representation encoder (DINOv2/SigLIP) for diffusion transformers, and extends the unification efforts of SVG [34] and UniFlow [49] that seek to reconcile semantic abstraction with pixel-faithful reconstruction. Its key conceptual departure is the Prism Hypothesis: rather than treating semantic and pixel encoders as fundamentally different architectures, it posits they occupy different bands of a shared frequency spectrum, and explicitly factorizes the latent space via DCT into a low-frequency semantic base and residual high-frequency detail bands. This connects to the broader multi-resolution generation literature (Laplacian pyramids, VAR, NFIG, DCTdiff) but applies frequency decomposition to the tokenizer itself rather than the generator's training schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Prism Hypothesis provides a unifying spectral lens that explains why pretrained semantic encoders and pixel encoders behave differently, and the empirical validation (low-pass retrieval saturation at ~25% of tokens, 85% high-frequency masking being optimal for generation) gives practitioners a concrete, actionable principle for designing unified tokenizers. The practical impact is significant: using only 64 low-frequency tokens yields a ~4× GFLOPs reduction in diffusion transformer inference while maintaining competitive FID, which could substantially lower the cost of latent-space generative modeling. Open questions include whether the frequency-band decomposition generalizes to video, higher resolutions beyond 256×256, and non-visual modalities, and whether the DCT-based reordering is optimal compared to learned frequency orderings.
