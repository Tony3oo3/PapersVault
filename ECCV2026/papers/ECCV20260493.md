## Problem

Few-shot multi-modal anomaly detection (MAD) suffers a precipitous performance drop when restricted to 1–4 normal samples per category, because existing methods treat all spatial features uniformly, conflating stable low-frequency macroscopic structures with unpredictable high-frequency defect signals. This entanglement exacerbates the inherent 2D–3D modality gap and inflates false-positive rates under severe data scarcity, where cross-modal alignment becomes unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260493/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 224×224 RGB image and a corresponding 224×224 3D surface normal map (derived from point clouds); a diffusion-based normal estimator (Marigold) additionally produces an estimated normal map from the RGB input to form the auxiliary stream. Training uses only k∈{1,2,4} anomaly-free samples per category, with LoRA-finetuned Stable Diffusion v2.1 synthesizing extra paired RGB–normal training data. The output is a dense pixel-level anomaly score map computed as the Hadamard product of fused 2D and 3D anomaly distance maps, from which an image-level anomaly score is derived for classification.

## Method

CMDS-AD establishes a dual-stream architecture in which a pre-trained diffusion normal estimator (Marigold) is repurposed as a non-linear low-pass filter, producing an estimated stream of purely low-frequency features that anchors macroscopic structure, while the uncompressed real stream retains coupled high- and low-frequency components to capture micro-defects. A Coordinate-Aware Hierarchical Feature Mapper extracts multi-scale features from a frozen DINO ViT-B/8 (layers 4, 7, 11), applies per-layer 1×1-conv/GN/GELU adaptation, concatenates them, refines via Coordinate Attention, and fuses through a pixel-wise Softmax Spatial Selector that assigns mutually exclusive aggregation weights per scale. Training uses a Decoupled Multi-Scale Mask-Aware Loss with cosine-distance alignment (to avoid cross-modal amplitude collapse), masked averaging for the real stream, global averaging for the estimated stream, and asymmetric descending layer weights (α>β>γ) that emphasize shallow high-frequency features. At inference, anomaly distance maps from both streams are combined per modality and then fused via element-wise multiplication (Ψ₂D ⊙ Ψ₃D), acting as a stringent spatial filter that flags defects only when both modalities concur, thereby suppressing modality-specific false alarms.

## Results

On MVTec 3D-AD under the extreme 1-shot setting, CMDS-AD achieves 79.6% I-AUROC and 94.2% AUPRO@30%, surpassing the second-best M3DM (73.9%/90.2%) by 5.7 and 4.0 points respectively. Under 4-shot, performance scales to 87.1% I-AUROC and 95.8% AUPRO@30%, exceeding MAFR (84.1%/94.1%). On EyeCandies, CMDS-AD reaches 77.2% I-AUROC and 85.5% AUPRO@30% in the 1-shot setting (vs. CIF's 69.5%/69.2%), and 82.7% I-AUROC / 87.7% AUPRO@30% at 4-shot, establishing new state-of-the-art across all few-shot configurations on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMDS-AD outperforms all eight baselines across every shot setting on both datasets. On MVTec 3D-AD (1-shot), it leads M3DM (73.9/90.2) by +5.7 I-AUROC and +4.0 AUPRO, and MAFR (72.4/92.2) by +7.2 I-AUROC; at 4-shot it surpasses MAFR (84.1/94.1) by +3.0 I-AUROC and +1.7 AUPRO. On EyeCandies the margins are even larger: in the 1-shot setting CMDS-AD (77.2/85.5) beats CIF (69.5/69.2) by +7.7 I-AUROC and +16.3 AUPRO, and MAFR (64.7/77.8) by +12.5 I-AUROC. At 4-shot on EyeCandies, the gap over MAFR (75.7/83.5) is +7.0 I-AUROC and +4.2 AUPRO. The simpler baselines (AST, EasyNet, Patchcore+FPFH) trail by 15–30 I-AUROC points in the 1-shot regime, confirming that the dual-stream diffusion-enhanced architecture is critical under extreme data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot anomaly detection (FSAD) line of work from single-modality RGB to the multi-modal (2D RGB + 3D surface normal) regime, building on cross-modal fusion methods like CFM and M3DM that target full-shot settings. Its core conceptual departure is repurposing a pre-trained diffusion-based normal estimator (Marigold) not merely as a data augmentor but as an inherent non-linear low-pass filter, thereby establishing a frequency-decoupled dual-stream architecture. The LoRA-guided Stable Diffusion augmentation follows standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning practice, while the Coordinate-Aware Hierarchical Feature Mapper and multiplicative scoring mechanism are novel architectural contributions that address cross-modal amplitude discrepancies and modality-specific false positives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically critical gap: in agile manufacturing, acquiring extensive multi-modal normal samples per product line is prohibitively expensive, and existing multi-modal methods degrade sharply under 1–4 shot constraints. The frequency-decoupling perspective (low-frequency structural anchor + high-frequency defect-sensitive stream) offers a principled alternative to uniform spatial fusion, and the multiplicative cross-modal scoring provides a simple yet effective mechanism for suppressing modality-specific noise. Open questions include whether the low-pass-filter interpretation generalizes beyond Marigold to other diffusion-based estimators, whether the approach extends to more complex or dynamic 3D geometries, and whether the fixed λ=0.1 balancing coefficient transfers across domains without re-tuning. The 5-shot results in Table 1 appear to duplicate 4-shot values for several baselines, which warrants verification.
