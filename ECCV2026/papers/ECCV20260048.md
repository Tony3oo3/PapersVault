## Problem

Existing multi-person 3D human mesh tracking methods assume continuous camera motion within a single shot and fail at abrupt shot changes, losing identity and producing misaligned trajectories. While a few prior works handle shot changes, they are restricted to single-person scenarios, leaving the problem of identity-consistent multi-person 3D reconstruction across shot boundaries largely unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-shot RGB video (evaluated at 1920×1080) containing multiple interacting people and produces globally aligned SMPL-parameterized 3D human meshes, refined camera poses, and identity-consistent 3D trajectories spanning all shots. The video is first segmented at detected shot boundaries; per-shot meshes come from 4DHumans, boundary-frame scene geometry from VGGT, 2D keypoints from ViTPose, and person segmentation masks from Grounded SAM. A 150-frame clip at 1080p requires approximately 10 minutes of test-time optimization on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

The key insight is to use 3D scene geometry as a robust spatial anchor to bridge human trajectories across abrupt viewpoint changes. VGGT reconstructs dense point clouds and camera poses at the two boundary frames, creating a shared 3D space; per-person point clouds are extracted via segmentation masks and scale-aligned to the meshes. A three-stage progressive optimization (root translation → global orientation → full camera pose) aligns boundary meshes to the point clouds using 2D reprojection, silhouette, and depth-consistency losses, avoiding the non-convexity of joint optimization. Intra-shot camera trajectories from DROID-SLAM are propagated into the global space via a relative SE(3) transformation computed at the boundary. Cross-shot Re-ID combines 3D root-translation distance, UV-texture appearance dissimilarity, and SMPL pose dissimilarity in a unified cost matrix solved by the Hungarian algorithm with a distance threshold to handle varying visible-person counts. A final post-processing stage applies joint-level temporal smoothing and a cross-camera reprojection loss to enforce coherent motion and geometry across the entire multi-shot sequence.

## Results

On EgoHumans, Multi-THuMBS achieves W-MPJPE of 279.0 mm, WA-MPJPE of 166.0 mm, MPJPE of 228.3 mm, MPVPE of 262.2 mm, and Accel of 27.3, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On EgoBody, the method reaches W-MPJPE 99.2 mm and Accel 6.0, and on Harmony4D it achieves W-MPJPE 221.0 mm and Accel 17.4. For cross-shot Re-ID, the method reduces identity switches to 0.97 (EgoHumans), 0.00 (EgoBody), and 0.46 (Harmony4D), and achieves the lowest camera ATE of 0.7 m (EgoHumans) and 0.1 m (EgoBody). On in-the-wild edited videos, Multi-THuMBS improves cross-shot PCK* from 62.7 to 90.7 and reduces foot sliding from 23.11 to 10.7 compared to PromptHMR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-THuMBS achieves the best pose accuracy across all three multi-view benchmarks (EgoHumans, EgoBody, Harmony4D), winning on every metric (W-MPJPE, WA-MPJPE, MPJPE, MPVPE, Accel) against all four pose baselines. The most dramatic gap is against PromptHMR [41], which suffers catastrophic W-MPJPE degradation at shot boundaries (1778.2 mm on EgoHumans vs. 279.0 for Ours; 1746.3 vs. 221.0 on Harmony4D), confirming that single-shot feed-forward methods fail under abrupt viewpoint changes. Against the closest multi-shot competitor HSfM† [23], Multi-THuMBS improves W-MPJPE by roughly 2× on EgoHumans (279.0 vs. 544.2) and Harmony4D (221.0 vs. 372.0), and by ~12% on EgoBody (99.2 vs. 113.1). For cross-shot Re-ID, the geometry-driven approach reduces identity switches to 0.97 on EgoHumans, substantially outperforming appearance-based KPR (2.54) and Pose2ID (4.62), and achieving zero switches on EgoBody. On camera pose estimation, Multi-THuMBS attains ATE of 0.7 m (EgoHumans) and 0.1 m (EgoBody), halving the error of VGGT alone (1.4 m / 1.3 m) and far exceeding PromptHMR (2.3 m) and HSfM† (2.8 m / 2.9 m). On in-the-wild edited videos, the method improves cross-shot PCK* from 62.7 to 90.7 and cuts foot sliding by more than half (10.7 vs. 23.11) relative to PromptHMR, demonstrating physically plausible motion across shot transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes three existing components—4DHumans for intra-shot mesh reconstruction, VGGT for boundary-frame 3D scene reconstruction, and DROID-SLAM for intra-shot camera tracking—into a unified pipeline that addresses a gap neither component alone fills. It departs from prior multi-person trackers (PromptHMR, GVHMR) that assume continuous camera motion, and from multi-shot single-person methods (HumanMM, ShowMak3r) that cannot handle identity ambiguity among multiple individuals. The key conceptual departure is using 3D geometric distance in a VGGT-constructed shared space as the primary re-identification cue, rather than relying on appearance features that degrade under abrupt viewpoint changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-world footage (broadcast sports, films, edited online content) is inherently multi-shot, so the inability of current multi-person trackers to handle shot boundaries is a significant practical gap. The finding that pure 3D spatial proximity already outperforms all appearance-based Re-ID baselines suggests geometric cues may be underexploited in identity tracking more broadly. However, the method is limited to intra-scene transitions (same physical environment), requires ~10 minutes of optimization per 150-frame clip, and the extension to n>2 shots is deferred to supplementary material, leaving open how the approach scales to long, heavily edited sequences with multiple scene changes.
