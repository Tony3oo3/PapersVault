## Problem

Operational wildfire detection from LEO satellites currently relies on contextual thresholding pipelines (MODIS, VIIRS) that require calibrated multi-band inputs and cannot run under the joint constraints of on-orbit inference: sub-megabyte model footprint, sub-150 ms per-batch latency on embedded hardware, and single-band uncalibrated MWIR imagery where early fires manifest as sub-pixel thermal anomalies under extreme class imbalance (~0.02% positive pixels). No prior learning-based approach addresses this full constraint set simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The system consumes raw digital numbers from the uncalibrated M0 MWIR channel (3.8 μm, 200 m GSD) tiled into 224×224 patches, normalized per-scene with a robust scaler over valid pixels; ~20% of pixels are no-data and excluded from all losses and metrics. It produces a per-pixel binary fire probability map, from which event-level detections are extracted via 8-connected-component analysis on thresholded predictions, with a decision threshold fixed on validation and applied to test.

## Method

DenseMAE is a lightweight fully convolutional masked autoencoder with a staged encoder that performs a single early stride-2 downsampling to H/2×W/2, followed by dilated convolution blocks (rates 1, 2, 4) to expand receptive field while preserving a dense grid; GroupNorm and GELU stabilize training at small batch sizes. Pretraining applies random block masking in image space and reconstructs only masked, valid pixels via a lightweight decoder that is discarded after training. The pretrained encoder is then transferred to downstream segmentation by attaching compact heads (TRT-Head for maximum TensorRT fusion, or DW-Res-Head with depthwise-separable residual blocks) plus an optional high-resolution refinement module that re-injects stem features for sub-pixel localisation. A hybrid variant adding EMA teacher-student cosine distillation is ablated but yields only marginal, inconsistent gains over reconstruction-only pretraining. The key result is that SSL-pretrained embeddings enable sub-megabyte TensorRT FP16 engines (0.52 MB, 65 ms per batch of 8) achieving 0.640 AP and 0.69 Fire-F1, with the best configuration reaching 0.699 AP at 0.91 MB, all without pruning or compression.

## Results

The best DenseMAE configuration (embedding dim 64, DW-Res-Head + HR refinement) achieves 0.699 test AP and 0.744 event-level Fire-F1 with 141 ms TensorRT FP16 latency (224×224, B=8) and a 0.91 MB engine. The fastest SSL-pretrained model (embedding dim 32, TRT-Head) reaches 0.640 AP and 0.69 Fire-F1 at just 65.34 ms latency and 0.52 MB engine size, without any pruning or compression. Both configurations outperform the supervised U-Net++ baseline (0.650 AP, 0.73 Fire-F1, 104.34 ms, 2.10 MB) under comparable deployment constraints. Full fine-tuning of the DenseMAE encoder consistently outperforms frozen-encoder transfer (0.689 vs. 0.612 AP), confirming that SSL initialization is most beneficial when the downstream model can adapt end-to-end.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a production-oriented supervised HR-U-Net++ (EfficientNet backbone with high-resolution refinement head), selected via an internal architecture sweep. DenseMAE with embedding dim 64 and DW-Res+HR refinement outperforms the strongest U-Net++ variant (depth=4) by 4.9 percentage points in AP (0.699 vs. 0.650) and 1.4 points in Fire-F1 (0.744 vs. 0.730), while using less than half the engine size (0.91 MB vs. 2.10 MB). At the speed end, the fastest DenseMAE model (emb=32, TRT-Head) is 37% faster than the smallest U-Net++ (65.34 ms vs. 94.25 ms) and 75% smaller (0.52 MB vs. 2.10 MB) while matching its AP (0.640 vs. 0.610). A random-initialization ablation (DenseMAE architecture trained from scratch) scores only 0.551 AP, confirming that the SSL pretraining contributes a 14.8-point AP gain over the same architecture without pretraining. The paper additionally validates against coincident VIIRS 375 m active-fire detections as an operational cross-sensor reference, reporting 103 joint detections, 33 VIIRS-only, and 3 F002-only clusters in a representative scene, though this is framed as a generalisation assessment rather than a direct algorithmic benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the masked autoencoder (MAE) paradigm into a fully convolutional, dense-prediction architecture (DenseMAE) tailored for single-band MWIR remote sensing, departing from the patch-token self-attention design of standard MAE in favor of dilated convolutions and a single early downsampling step. It positions itself against two prior lines: (i) operational contextual-thresholding pipelines (MODIS, VIIRS) that require calibrated multi-band inputs and hard decision rules, and (ii) learning-based fire segmentation methods that assume calibrated multi-spectral inputs and do not target on-orbit compute constraints. The U-Net++/EfficientNet supervised baseline serves as a strong internal reference rather than a direct comparison to published deep-learning fire detectors, which the authors note are not directly comparable due to input and deployment mismatches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first reported deployed on-orbit wildfire detection system achieving sub-10-minute end-to-end alert latency from a LEO thermal constellation, demonstrating that self-supervised pretraining enables sub-megabyte models competitive with supervised baselines under extreme class imbalance (0.018% positive pixels). The finding that hybrid EMA distillation adds no consistent benefit over plain reconstruction suggests that in noise-dominated single-band regimes, the reconstruction signal alone is sufficient—a useful negative result for SSL practitioners in remote sensing. Key open questions include whether multi-temporal context (currently precluded by memory constraints) or additional spectral channels (LWIR) could resolve the persistent sunglint confusion, and whether the approach generalizes across the full nine-satellite constellation archive.
