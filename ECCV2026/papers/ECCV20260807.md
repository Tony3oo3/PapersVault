## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models suffer catastrophic structural failures—object repetition and spatial fragmentation—when pushed to generate ultra-high-resolution imagery at extreme aspect ratios (e.g., 8:1), because they lack robust spatial priors for non-standard canvas dimensions. Existing workarounds such as tiled generation and positional interpolation remain fundamentally insufficient, as they rely on stationary spatial priors that cannot maintain long-range coherence across massive canvases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260807/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a target aspect ratio (e.g., 8:1 horizontal panorama or vertical scroll) and produces a seamless ultra-high-resolution image up to 32K pixels on the long side, with the short side maintained at 1024 pixels in experiments. The target canvas is decomposed into a sequence of overlapping local latent windows that are generated sequentially and then fused at the pixel level via weighted blending, so the output is assembled from multiple decoded tiles rather than produced in a single forward pass.

## Method

ScrollScape reformulates EAR image synthesis as a sequential video panning task built on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, exploiting the inherent temporal consistency of video diffusion models as a global spatial constraint. Scanning Positional Encoding (ScanPE) re-engineers 3D RoPE by mapping each token's local coordinate to a global canvas coordinate via a per-frame anchor offset, effectively turning the model into a moving camera that can pan across arbitrary aspect ratios; RoPE is then applied independently along temporal, vertical, and horizontal axes. Scrolling Super-Resolution (ScrollSR) applies a modified FlashVSR module in latent space to refine low-resolution latents into high-resolution ones frame-by-frame, circumventing the memory bottleneck of direct 32K pixel synthesis. A lightweight alignment stage using conditional flow matching on a curated 3K multi-ratio dataset adapts the pretrained video prior to the EAR generation distribution while preserving its spatiotemporal regularization. Finally, coordinate-aligned latent decoding and a median-feature-consensus tile selection strategy with weighted ramp-mask fusion produce the seamless 32K panorama.

## Results

On 8:1 extreme aspect ratio panoramas (short side 1024 px), ScrollScape achieves the best scores across all six metrics: FID 214.7, CLIP 30.0, KID 2.0×10⁻², Style-L 4.0×10⁻³, GSD-LPIPS 0.674, and GSD-DINOv2 0.670. The largest margins are on KID (2.0 vs. 3.0 for Tiled Diffusion, a 33% reduction) and FID (214.7 vs. 241.2, an 11% improvement over the best baseline). In a 20-rater user study over 100 prompts, ScrollScape is preferred in 74–92% of pairwise comparisons across structural coherence, content richness, and image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScrollScape outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest tiling-based methods (Tiled Diffusion, SyncDiffusion), it reduces FID by 11–13% and KID by 33–38%, while also improving GSD-DINOv2 from 0.901/0.895 to 0.670, indicating substantially less semantic repetition. Against the positional-interpolation baseline DyPE, ScrollScape gains 13.5% on FID and 58% on KID, and narrows the GSD-DINOv2 gap (0.670 vs. 0.682), showing it avoids the object-repetition artifacts that DyPE still exhibits at 8:1. The FLUX-based baselines (FLUX-Krea and FLUX-Krea+Fill) are the weakest, with FID scores 30–55% higher and GSD-LPIPS values (0.174, 0.293) far below ScrollScape's 0.674, reflecting severe structural collapse and content looping. In the user study, ScrollScape is preferred over DyPE in 92% of structural-coherence votes and over Tiled Diffusion in 85% of content-richness votes, confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant image-diffusion paradigm for extreme aspect ratio (EAR) synthesis—where methods like SyncDiffusion, DyPE, and Tiled Diffusion rely on patch-based tiling or positional interpolation within static architectures—and instead repurposes video diffusion priors as spatial coherence regulators. It builds directly on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B as the base video DiT and adapts FlashVSR for latent-space super-resolution, but the key conceptual contribution is ScanPE, which re-engineers 3D RoPE to distribute global spatial coordinates across temporal frames, effectively turning the video model into a panning camera. This extends the emerging trend of leveraging video models for image-centric tasks (Frame2Frame, Magic Fixup, Framepainter) from short-range frame-to-frame editing to long-range spatial consistency across massive canvases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32K resolution at 8:1 aspect ratio is unprecedented for diffusion-based generation, and the paradigm of using temporal consistency as a global spatial constraint could inspire broader applications beyond panoramas (e.g., very tall architectural renders, long document layouts). However, the evaluation is somewhat narrow: a single base model (Wan2.1-1.3B), a single aspect ratio (8:1), and a small 3K training set limit confidence in generalization to other ratios, resolutions, or model scales. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 2D non-linear scanning (e.g., very tall or square canvases), how it performs with larger video backbones, and whether the patch-based evaluation protocol fully captures the global coherence claims.
