## Problem

Consistency distillation of Flow Matching models relies on static sampling priors (uniform or Logit-Normal) to allocate training budget across trajectory timesteps, but these priors assume a difficulty profile that does not match the actual optimization landscape of distillation. The paper reveals that consistency distillation exhibits a U-shaped difficulty profile with bottlenecks at boundary stages, directly contradicting the intermediate-focused Logit-Normal prior, and that static heuristics cannot adapt as optimization focal points shift during training.

## Input / Output

At inference, the distilled student model takes a text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image in as few as 4 denoising steps (vs. dozens for the teacher). During training, the RL agent operates on the PF-ODE trajectory by partitioning it into M=4 semantic stages and selecting which sub-trajectory to train on each iteration; the student is a LoRA adapter on FLUX (12B) or SDXL backbones. The method is evaluated on a 15K CC3M validation split using FID, HPSv2, PickScore, and LAION Aesthetic Score.

## Method

CACFM reformulates consistency distillation as a Markov Decision Process where a lightweight tabular Q-learning agent dynamically selects which of M=4 trajectory sub-segments to train on each iteration. The state is the ordinal ranking of per-stage consistency losses (yielding a compact 24-state space), the action is the chosen sub-trajectory, and the reward is the improvement of current loss over an EMA baseline, enabling the agent to discover the U-shaped difficulty profile and an emergent coarse-to-fine curriculum without manual scheduling. The student is trained with a hybrid objective combining the PCM consistency loss, a Flow-adapted DMD loss (using Tweedie's formula to derive scores from velocity outputs), and an adversarial consistency loss with a prompt-conditioned discriminator. The RL agent's computational overhead is negligible (~18% per iteration) relative to the Transformer backbone, and the policy generalizes across inference step counts (4, 8, 16) without retraining.

## Results

On the CC3M 15K validation set, CACFM achieves state-of-the-art FID across all step counts on both FLUX and SDXL backbones. On SDXL, CACFM reaches FID 35.29 (4-step), 34.42 (8-step), and 33.49 (16-step), outperforming the strongest baselines (PCM at 37.26/39.30/40.47 and InstaFlow at 38.13/35.60/34.43) by 1.5–7 FID points. On FLUX, CACFM achieves FID 39.19 (4-step) and 36.96 (8-step), surpassing FLUX-Schnell (41.19/40.47) by over 2 FID points in the extreme few-step regime. In aesthetic evaluation, CACFM consistently ranks 1st or 2nd in Aesthetic Score and PickScore across all configurations on both architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CACFM outperforms all nine baselines on FID at every step count on both SDXL and FLUX. On SDXL 4-step, CACFM (35.29) beats the closest competitor PCM (37.26) by ~2 FID points and InstaFlow (38.13) by ~3 points; the gap widens at 16-step where CACFM (33.49) leads InstaFlow (34.43) by ~1 point while Turbo degrades catastrophically to 77.13. On FLUX, CACFM (39.19 at 4-step) surpasses FLUX-Schnell (41.19) by over 2 FID points, confirming that curvature-adaptive training is most critical under tight inference budgets. In aesthetic metrics (Tables 3–4), CACFM ranks 1st in Aesthetic Score and PickScore at 8- and 16-step on both architectures, and 2nd in HPS at 4-step, indicating the emergent curriculum effectively prioritizes high-frequency detail refinement. The ablation (Table 6) further shows that removing the RL scheduler (CACFM w/o RL) degrades Avg Rank from 1.00 to 3.22, and removing DMD drops it to 3.78, confirming both components are necessary for the full performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency distillation line (CMs, PCMs, LCMs) by replacing their static sampling priors (uniform or Logit-Normal) with a lightweight RL-based scheduler that dynamically allocates training budget across PF-ODE sub-trajectories. It departs from prior RL-in-diffusion work (DDPO, DPOK) that optimizes generation content (what to produce) by instead using RL to optimize training structure (where to learn along the trajectory). The hybrid objective borrows Distribution Matching Distillation (Yin et al.) and adversarial consistency from the efficient-distillation literature, positioning the RL scheduler as orthogonal to these loss-level advances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that consistency distillation exhibits a U-shaped (boundary-dominated) difficulty profile, contrary to the Logit-Normal prior inherited from standard Flow Matching training, offers a concrete diagnostic for practitioners choosing sampling schedules. The emergent coarse-to-fine curriculum emerging from a 24-state Q-table is an elegant demonstration that minimal RL can replace hand-tuned heuristics, though the practical FID gains over strong baselines (Schnell, PCM) are modest (1–2 points). Open questions include whether the U-shaped profile generalizes beyond FLUX/SDXL to video or 3D generation, and whether the approach extends to single-step inference where the paper acknowledges topologically complex flows remain challenging.
