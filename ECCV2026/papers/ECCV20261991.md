## Problem

Consistency-distilled video diffusion models degrade in quality as more sampling steps are allocated at test time, because the consistency formulation replaces the PF-ODE trajectory with a re-noising-based sampling path that drifts away from the target distribution. This structural limitation prevents a single distilled model from supporting flexible, any-step inference where users can trade latency for fidelity without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261991/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-video, the model takes a text prompt and generates up to 81 frames at 480×832 resolution; for image-to-video, it additionally conditions on a reference image. The same checkpoint supports any inference budget from 4 to 32 NFEs (and beyond) via a standard Euler scheduler, with no architectural change or retraining required. Training uses 256K synthetic prompt-video pairs generated by the Wan2.1-T2V-14B teacher, and the distilled student is a LoRA-adapted (rank 256) version of the 1.3B or 14B Wan2.1 backbone.

## Method

AnyFlow shifts the distillation target from endpoint consistency (z_t → z_0) to flow-map transition learning (z_t → z_r) over arbitrary time intervals, using a MeanFlow-style objective with a JVP-free finite-difference derivative. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) forward flow map training with interpolated timestep conditioning, guidance-fused CFG, and adaptive loss reweighting to convert a pretrained diffusion model into a stable transition operator; (2) on-policy distillation via DMD where the student performs self-rollouts using flow map backward simulation. The key mechanism—flow map backward simulation—decomposes a full Euler rollout into three shortcut segments (T→t, t→r, r→0) exploiting the composition property of flow maps, so that different inference step budgets can be simulated at the same computational cost during training. This corrects discretization error and exposure bias while preserving the fine-grained flow field, enabling the model to improve monotonically with more sampling steps.

## Results

On VBench T2V (14B bidirectional), AnyFlow-Wan2.1-T2V-14B achieves a Total score of 84.04 at 4 NFEs and 84.10 at 32 NFEs, outperforming rCM (83.73 at 4 NFEs) and matching the Wan2.1 teacher (83.74 at 50×2 NFEs) with 12.5× fewer function evaluations. On the 14B causal backbone, AnyFlow-FAR-Wan2.1-14B reaches 84.05 at 4 NFEs and 84.41 at 32 NFEs, surpassing all community 14B causal models (LightX2V: 83.42, FastVideo: 83.12, Krea: 83.25). For I2V, AnyFlow-FAR-Wan2.1-14B scores 87.87 at 4 NFEs, matching Wan2.1-I2V-14B (87.71 at 50×2 NFEs) while using 25× fewer NFEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against rCM (the strongest consistency-based bidirectional baseline), AnyFlow wins at both 1.3B (83.48 vs 82.51 at 4 NFEs) and 14B (84.04 vs 83.73 at 4 NFEs), and critically continues to improve at 32 NFEs (84.10) while rCM degrades. Against Self-Forcing (the leading causal consistency baseline), AnyFlow-FAR-Wan2.1-1.3B achieves 83.54 vs 83.39 at 4 NFEs and extends to 83.96 at 32 NFEs, whereas Self-Forcing's consistency formulation is designed only for few-step settings. Among 14B causal community models, AnyFlow-FAR-Wan2.1-14B (84.05 at 4 NFEs) outperforms LightX2V (83.42 at 9 NFEs), FastVideo (83.12 at 8 NFEs), and Krea-Realtime (83.25 at 4 NFEs) by 0.6–0.9 points. For I2V, AnyFlow at 4 NFEs (87.87) matches the full-budget Wan2.1-I2V-14B teacher (87.71 at 50×2 NFEs) and surpasses FastVideo-CausalWan2.2-A14B (86.82 at 8 NFEs) by 1.05 points. The key differentiator is test-time scaling: AnyFlow's Total score monotonically improves from 4 to 32 NFEs across all model sizes, while consistency-based baselines (rCM, Self-Forcing) show degradation or plateau at higher step counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyFlow departs from the dominant consistency-distillation paradigm (sCM, rCM, Self-Forcing) by replacing endpoint mapping (z_t → z_0) with flow-map transition learning (z_t → z_r) over arbitrary time intervals, building directly on the two-time flow map formalism of Boffi et al. and the MeanFlow training objective. The on-policy distillation stage reuses the DMD reverse-KL framework but introduces a novel flow map backward simulation that decomposes Euler rollouts into shortcut segments, replacing the re-noising-based consistency backward simulation used in Self-Forcing and rCM. The JVP-free derivative approximation from Transition Model is adopted to make flow map training compatible with FSDP at video scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnyFlow addresses a practical limitation that affects all current few-step video distillation methods: quality degrades rather than improves when users allocate more inference steps, undermining the latency-quality tradeoff that any-step models should offer. By preserving the PF-ODE trajectory structure, the distilled model supports flexible step budgets within a single checkpoint, which is valuable for production pipelines that need both rapid previews and high-fidelity outputs. Open questions include whether the flow map formulation can be extended to pretraining (rather than post-training only), whether the two-stage recipe generalizes to other modalities or longer video horizons, and whether the modest absolute VBench gains (~0.5 points) translate to perceptual improvements at scale.
