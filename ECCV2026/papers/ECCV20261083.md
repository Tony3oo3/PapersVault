## Problem

3D hand pose estimation from a single RGB image remains highly ambiguous under severe self- and object-occlusion, yet existing contextual cues (action labels, coarse grasp categories, short captions) are too coarse to resolve fine-grained geometric ambiguities. No prior work has exploited affordance-aware textual descriptions that jointly encode both the semantic intent of an interaction and the geometric structure of the grasp to guide 3D hand reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261083/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, an initial 3D hand pose estimate (MANO pose parameters from WiLoR or Hamba), and a generated affordance description (a single sentence combining object category, shape, size, interaction type, intention, and grasp taxonomy). It outputs a refined MANO pose (15×3 rotation vectors) with shape held fixed, which is then passed through the MANO layer to produce corrected 3D joints and mesh vertices. Joint-wise binary visibility labels (from ray casting on the MANO mesh for self-occlusion and SAM2 segmentation for object occlusion) are computed internally to mask the 2D keypoint loss during refinement.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, affordance descriptions are generated via a chain-of-thought process: Qwen2.5-VL extracts five structured elements (object category, shape, size, interaction, intention) from the image with hand/object bounding boxes, a dedicated ViT+MANO-pose grasp classifier (87.6% accuracy on HOGraspNet) predicts the grasp taxonomy, and Mistral-7B summarizes all elements into a single coherent sentence. Second, AffHandGen is a transformer-based DDPM diffusion model operating in MANO pose space, conditioned on DistilBERT text tokens (64) and ViT image tokens (192) through cross-attention rather than the global-vector conditioning used by InterHandGen, enabling fine-grained modality-specific attention to distinguish subtle interaction differences. Third, an occlusion-aware refinement procedure starts from the initial pose, diffuses to an adaptively chosen intermediate step n (scaled by the number of occluded joints), and iteratively denoises while applying a 2D reprojection loss masked to visible joints only, allowing the diffusion prior to correct uncertain occluded joints through contextual conditioning. Sparse time sampling (every m steps) and the adaptive start step keep inference cost comparable to the base foundation model.

## Results

On HOGraspNet, the affordance-guided diffusion prior applied on top of WiLoR achieves 7.53 mm PA-MPJPE, reducing the high-occlusion subset error by 3.04 mm (24%, from 12.41 to 9.37 mm) compared to WiLoR alone (8.22 mm). On HO3D, the prior refines Hamba to the best 7.29 mm PA-MPJPE and WiLoR to 7.37 mm, confirming gains generalize across base estimators. The method also improves PCK@5 from 39.7% (WiLoR) to 44.1% and F@5 from 0.702 to 0.731 on HOGraspNet, with inference cost of only ~8 diffusion steps per sample (7.94 GFLOPs vs. WiLoR's 138.06 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines: two CNN-based methods (HandOccNet, MeshGraphormer), three foundation models (HaMeR, WiLoR, Hamba), and a diffusion prior (InterHandGen, both without and with full conditioning). The affordance-guided prior consistently outperforms all baselines on both HOGraspNet and HO3D. The most striking comparison is against the diffusion baseline InterHandGen [24], which actually degrades WiLoR's performance (8.99 vs. 8.22 mm on HOGraspNet) due to lack of contextual signals, while the affordance-conditioned version with global-vector conditioning (8.06 mm) still trails the proposed cross-attention design (7.53 mm). Against the strongest foundation model WiLoR [42], the method achieves a 0.69 mm average improvement and a 3.04 mm gain under high occlusion. Against Hamba [8], the prior yields a 0.17 mm improvement on HO3D (7.42 → 7.29 mm). The older CNN baselines HandOccNet [39] and MeshGraphormer [28] are substantially outperformed by 7.56 mm and 2.49 mm respectively on HOGraspNet, confirming that the gains come from contextual affordance reasoning rather than architectural improvements alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) foundation-model-based 3D hand reconstruction (WiLoR, HaMeR, Hamba), which provides strong 2D-aligned but occlusion-vulnerable initial poses; (2) diffusion-based generative priors for 3D human/hand modeling (InterHandGen, SCGen, G-HOP), which learn pose distributions and enable iterative refinement; and (3) affordance recognition and VLM-based reasoning (HOGraspNet, grasp taxonomies, chain-of-thought prompting). The key departure is the first integration of structured, multi-element affordance textual descriptions—generated via a VLM + supervised grasp classifier + LLM summarization pipeline—as conditioning signals for a hand-pose diffusion prior, combined with occlusion-aware selective 2D guidance that prior diffusion-refinement methods (e.g., SCGen) did not employ for hands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 24% PA-MPJPE reduction on the high-occlusion subset (12.41→9.37 mm) addresses a persistent failure mode of current hand reconstruction methods, and the improvement generalizes across base estimators (WiLoR, Hamba) and datasets (HOGraspNet, HO3D, HInt), suggesting the affordance prior is a broadly applicable refinement layer. However, the method currently relies on textual descriptions as a proxy for object geometry rather than explicit 3D object modeling, is limited to static single-hand scenes, and its gains on in-the-wild HInt data are more modest, leaving open whether the approach scales to dynamic multi-hand interactions or requires explicit 3D object context for truly novel affordances.
