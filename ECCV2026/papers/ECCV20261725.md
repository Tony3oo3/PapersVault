## Problem

Scene text detectors are typically trained and evaluated under ideal acquisition conditions with heavy reliance on large-scale synthetic pretraining (e.g., SynthText-800k), yet real-world deployment inevitably encounters distribution shifts from cross-domain changes (language, font, scene) and physically grounded imaging degradations (rain, fog, under/overexposure, low resolution). Systematic evaluation under these adverse conditions is scarce, and no existing method is explicitly designed to remain robust across both types of shift while avoiding costly scene-text-specific pretraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB scene text image as input; during training, images and masks are resized to 512×512, while the DINOv3 branch internally resizes to a fixed 448×448 resolution (yielding 28×28 token grids at patch size 16). The SAM2-Hiera-L branch produces a four-level feature pyramid (channels 144/288/576/1152) at progressively lower spatial resolutions. The output is a pixel-level text probability map decoded by a lightweight head, from which text region boundaries are extracted via standard binarization and connected-component analysis.

## Method

TextDS combines two frozen visual foundation models—SAM2-Hiera-L for multi-scale structural priors and DINOv3 ViT-L/16 for domain-robust semantics—into a dual-encoder that requires no scene-text-specific pretraining. Task adaptation is achieved via Step-wise LoRA (SWLoRA), which inserts up to T=5 iterative low-rank residual refinements before each frozen Hiera block and employs a cosine-similarity-based dynamic early-exit to stop refinement once the representation stabilizes (average executed steps: 3.65/2.21/3.48 on CTW-1500/Total-Text/MLT). The two branches are aligned and fused through Common Subspace Fusion (CSF), which estimates a per-sample shared subspace (top-16 eigenvectors of the product of branch covariances), restricts cross-branch interaction to that subspace, and explicitly preserves DINOv3's orthogonal complement to retain shift-robust information. The entire trainable parameter count is only 4.9M, and the model is trained end-to-end with a combined BCE + IoU loss for 50 epochs on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On the three standard scene text detection benchmarks, TextDS achieves F-measures of 90.6% (CTW-1500), 89.1% (Total-Text), and 92.2% (MLT), outperforming all compared methods by 1.4–7.2 F-points. The model requires only 4.9M trainable parameters (vs. 12.3–38.7M for baselines) and does not rely on SynthText pre-training, while achieving the highest inference speed of 44.1 FPS on an RTX 4090. Under adverse imaging conditions (rain, fog, under/overexposure, low-resolution), TextDS maintains F-measures within 1.7 points of its normal-condition scores, with the largest drop being 1.7 F-points at 128×128 resolution on MLT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextDS outperforms all nine scene text detection baselines on every reported dataset and metric. The largest margins appear on CTW-1500, where TextDS (F=90.6) exceeds DB-Net (83.4) by 7.2 points and the strongest recent competitor S3INet (86.0) by 4.6 points. On MLT, the advantage is equally pronounced: 92.2 F vs. 89.7 for S3INet (+2.5), 88.9 for CB-Net (+3.3), and 85.6 for DB-Net (+6.6). On Total-Text, the gap narrows slightly (89.1 vs. 88.7 for S3INet, +0.4) but TextDS still leads all methods. In domain generalization (MLT→CTW-1500/Total-Text, Fig. 3), TextDS retains the largest in-domain-to-cross-domain F-measure, while baselines like DB-Net and TextPMs show substantially larger drops. Under adverse conditions (Fig. 5), TextDS consistently maintains higher F-measures than S3INet, TextPMs, and DB-Net across all degradation types, with the smallest relative performance drop. Against LLM-based OCR systems (Qwen-OCR, DeepSeek-OCR, PP-OCR), the comparison is qualitative: TextDS provides more precise pixel-level geometric localization for deformed text, positioning it as a complementary front-end detector rather than a direct competitor.

## Relation to Prior Work

TextDS departs from the dominant scene text detection paradigm that relies on large-scale SynthText pretraining by instead leveraging frozen visual foundation models (SAM2-Hiera-L for structural multi-scale features and DINOv3 ViT-L/16 for domain-robust semantics) as complementary encoders. The Step-wise LoRA (SWLoRA) module directly extends the single static low-rank update of LoRA [10] into a multi-step progressive refinement with a cosine-similarity-based dynamic early-exit, while the Common Subspace Fusion (CSF) module introduces a covariance-based shared-subspace projection that is novel in the scene text detection literature. The dual-encoder design combining a segmentation backbone with a self-supervised representation backbone, fused through an explicit subspace decomposition, has no direct precedent in prior scene text detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that scene text detection can be performed without any scene-text-specific pretraining, using only 4.9M trainable parameters, while outperforming recent methods (2024–2025) by 3–4 F-measure points on standard benchmarks — a practically significant efficiency gain for deployment-constrained settings. The constructed adverse-condition datasets (rain, fog, under/overexposure, low-resolution) fill a recognized gap in systematic robustness evaluation for scene text detection. Open questions include whether the CSF subspace mechanism is essential versus simpler gated fusion, whether the approach transfers to other detection tasks beyond text, and whether the synthetic degradation protocol adequately captures the diversity of real-world imaging failures.
