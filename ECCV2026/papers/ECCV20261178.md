## Problem

Zero-shot industrial anomaly generation aims to synthesize defects on a target product without any real anomaly images of that product, but existing methods suffer from two critical failures: inaccurate anomaly information acquisition (text-based methods face a semantic gap with complex defects, while reference-based methods cannot disentangle the anomaly from its source product's texture) and uncontrolled anomaly-product fusion (no explicit mechanism to bind the acquired anomaly structure to a specific target product).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261178/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a normal RGB image of the target product, a binary anomaly mask (constructed by taking the union of two randomly sampled reference masks of the same anomaly type, then intersected with the target product's foreground mask), and a composite text prompt of the form 'a <anomaly> on <product>'. The output is a generated anomaly image where the defect is injected only within the masked region via blended diffusion, while the unmasked product regions are preserved from the original input. The pipeline operates in the VAE latent space of Stable Diffusion 3, and 1,000 images are generated per product category.

## Method

DeCo decouples the anomaly structure from its source product and explicitly recouples it with the target product using a dual-LoRA design on SD3's MM-DiT backbone. During training, Dual-Routing Flow (DR-Flow) binds the anomaly structure to an isolated abnormal text token: AP-Routing routes the anomaly and product tokens through separate LoRA weights, while QK-Routing restricts the trainable Anomaly LoRA to the Query projection (spatial structure) and uses the frozen Product LoRA for Key and Value (texture), so A-LoRA learns only structure. A parallel Product-Invariant Flow (PI-Flow) constraint subtracts the product-only velocity from the total target velocity, forcing A-LoRA to encode a pure, product-free anomaly residual. At inference, a hybrid injection combines the target product's P-LoRA with the reference product's A-LoRA, and Product Compatibility Correction (PCC) extrapolates the velocity prediction away from the product-only prediction (analogous to classifier-free guidance) to compensate for incompatibility between the acquired anomaly structure and the specific product.

## Results

On MVTec AD, DeCo achieves 68.0% pixel-level AP and 64.5% pixel-level F1 for downstream anomaly detection, outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline AnoStyler (62.9% P-AP) by 5.1% and the best Dual-LoRA decoupling method UnzipLoRA (61.9% P-AP) by 6.1%. On VisA, DeCo reaches 41.5% P-AP and 46.4% P-F1, surpassing the strongest baseline (RealNet, 33.3% P-AP) by 8.2%. For generation quality, DeCo attains the highest IS(a) of 3.98 (vs. 3.12 for AnoStyler) and ties for the best IC-LPIPS diversity score of 0.33.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCo outperforms all 11 baselines on every pixel-level metric on MVTec AD. Against the strongest zero-shot generation method AnoStyler [31], DeCo gains +5.1% P-AP (68.0 vs. 62.9) and +3.8% P-F1 (64.5 vs. 60.7), while matching its image-level I-AUC of 98.0. Against the Dual-LoRA decoupling baselines, DeCo surpasses UnzipLoRA [22] by +6.1% P-AP and QR-LoRA [35] by +7.8% P-AP, confirming that structural-level binding (DR-Flow) is more effective than semantic-level decoupling for industrial anomalies. Among anomaly synthesis methods, DeCo leads RealNet [38] (the strongest synthesis baseline) by +10.3% P-AP. On VisA, where anomaly structures are transferred cross-dataset from MVTec AD, DeCo achieves 41.5% P-AP versus 33.3% for RealNet and 31.4% for AnoStyler, demonstrating that the decoupled anomaly structures generalize to unseen product categories. For generation quality (Table 1), DeCo achieves the highest anomaly-region IS(a) of 3.98, well above AnoStyler (3.12) and AnoGen (2.84), while tying for the best diversity score (IC-LPIPS 0.33).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot industrial anomaly generation line (AnomalyAny, AnoStyler) by replacing their implicit text-prompt or reference-feature conditioning with an explicit decoupling-and-recoupling pipeline built on Stable Diffusion 3's MM-DiT architecture. It draws directly on the LoRA composition literature (UnzipLoRA, QR-LoRA, B-LoRA) for its dual-LoRA design, but departs from those methods' reliance on strong pre-trained semantic priors (e.g., 'dog', 'watercolor') by shifting to structural-level binding via Dual-Routing Flow, which is necessary because industrial anomalies lack such semantic anchors. The Product Compatibility Correction mechanism repurposes the classifier-free guidance extrapolation idea from diffusion models as a post-hoc incompatibility fix between independently trained LoRAs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical cold-start problem in industrial inspection: generating realistic anomalies for a new product line without any labeled defect images, which is critical when deploying detection systems to previously unseen products. The cross-dataset generalization result (8.2% P-AP gain on VisA using structures learned from MVTec AD) suggests the acquired anomaly structures are genuinely product-invariant, though this remains the strongest evidence for generalizability and is tested on only two datasets. Open questions include whether the structural binding generalizes to 3D defects or multi-scale anomalies, whether PCC's scalar extrapolation is sufficient for highly incompatible product-anomaly pairs, and how the method scales when the reference product set is small or domain-shifted.
