## Problem

The Sliced Wasserstein Distance (SWD) provides a scalable proxy for the full Wasserstein distance in high-dimensional distribution matching, but its Monte Carlo estimator over random projection directions exhibits high variance, producing noisy gradients and slow convergence when used as an optimization loss. Existing variance reduction techniques (control variates, quasi-Monte Carlo) have been explored but remain underutilized in distribution matching objectives, and no method adaptively concentrates computational effort on the most informative projection directions during iterative optimization.

## Input / Output

For color correction, the method takes a source image and a reference image, converts both to CIELAB space at 128px maximum resolution, and outputs a Color Decision List (CDL) parameter set (slope, offset, power, saturation) optimized in 150 steps. For diffusion guidance, it takes a text prompt and a reference image, applies 6 SWD optimization steps per denoising step (for 95% of total steps) on the predicted x₀ decoded through the VAE, with a gradient stop after the transformer backbone, and outputs a color-guided generated image. For general distribution matching, it operates on two empirical sample sets of N points in R^d, handling unequal sample counts by uniformly duplicating the shorter projection before sorting.

## Method

ReSWD integrates Weighted Reservoir Sampling (WRS) into the SWD estimator by maintaining a persistent reservoir of K projection directions across optimization steps, drawing M new random directions each step, and selecting survivors via the Efraimidis–Spirakis key mechanism k(θ)=u^{1/D(θ)} where D(θ) is the 1-D Wasserstein cost. This preferentially retains directions where the two distributions are most dissimilar, concentrating gradient signal on high-contribution projections while preserving unbiasedness through self-normalized importance weights. A time-decay factor τ exponentially ages stored reservoir weights to adapt to the shifting optimization landscape, and an effective sample size (ESS) threshold triggers reservoir resets to prevent weight collapse. The resulting estimator has complexity O((K+M)n log n) and, with 8 fresh candidates out of 64 total projections, achieves the best gradient SNR and lowest true Wasserstein error across synthetic and real-world tasks.

## Results

On the synthetic 1D distribution matching benchmark (1000 pairs, d=3, 64 projections, 300 steps), ReSWD achieves a Mean-W₁ of 0.622×10³ with a gradient SNR of 0.278, outperforming standard SWD (0.733×10³, SNR 0.215) and the best prior variance-reduction baseline QMC (0.670×10³, SNR 0.208). ReSWD+QMC further improves to 0.610×10³. On diffusion guidance with SD3.5-large, ReSWD reduces the Mean-W₂ color distance to 0.55×10² (vs. 1.941×10² for Lobashev et al.) while cutting generation time from 124 s to 69 s; the turbo variant achieves 0.675×10² in just 4 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSWD outperforms all six variance-reduction baselines on the synthetic benchmark. Against standard SWD, it reduces Mean-W₁ by 15% (0.622 vs. 0.733) and improves gradient SNR by 29% (0.278 vs. 0.215). The control-variate methods LCV and UCV [30] offer only marginal gains over plain SWD (0.735 and 0.726 respectively), while Max-SW [10] catastrophically fails (29.152 Mean-W₁) due to its max-selection strategy amplifying noise. IS-EBSW-e [29] achieves 0.698 but still trails ReSWD by 11%. QMC [28] is the strongest single baseline at 0.670, yet ReSWD improves upon it by 7%, and combining the two (ReSWD+QMC) yields the best result at 0.610. On diffusion guidance, ReSWD@SD3.5-large reduces Mean-W₂ from 1.941 (Lobashev et al. [26]) to 0.55 while also improving CLIP-IQA from 0.671 to 0.793; even on the same SDXL backbone, ReSWD cuts Mean-W₂ to 1.213 vs. 1.941. On color matching, ReSWD achieves the lowest CDL error (0.10 RMSE) and highest Color PSNR (24.64) among all methods including Reinhard [43], Nguyen [34], Yoo [50], Ho [18], and Larchenko [23]. A notable limitation is that ReSWD degrades sharply at d≥12 (Mean-W₁ jumps to 9.768 at d=12 vs. 1.190 for SWD), where the reservoir rarely finds informative directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReSWD directly extends the Sliced Wasserstein Distance (Heitz et al., CVPR 2021) by replacing its uniform Monte Carlo direction sampling with a Weighted Reservoir Sampling mechanism (Efraimidis & Spirakis, 2006), inspired by the ReSTIR resampling framework from real-time path tracing (Bitterli et al., SIGGRAPH 2020). Conceptually, it occupies a middle ground between Max-SW (which selects only the top-contributing directions) and standard SWD (which averages over all random directions): the reservoir adaptively retains high-contribution directions across optimization steps while remaining an unbiased estimator through self-normalized importance weights. The time-decay reweighting and ESS-based reservoir reset are borrowed directly from ReSTIR's non-stationary handling, making this a cross-domain transfer of rendering variance-reduction ideas into distribution-matching optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReSWD provides a practical, drop-in variance reduction for SWD-based losses that are increasingly used in color transfer, texture synthesis, and diffusion guidance, yielding more stable gradients and faster convergence with minimal overhead. However, the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to dimensionality: at 12 dimensions the reservoir mechanism degrades severely (Mean-W1 jumps from 0.622 at 3D to 9.768), essentially reverting to or underperforming plain SWD, which limits its applicability to low-dimensional color or small-feature matching. An open question is whether learned projection kernels or more aggressive reservoir refresh strategies can close this gap without sacrificing the unbiasedness guarantee. The demonstrated integration with SD3.5 flow-matching models and the finding that gradient stopping after the backbone preserves guidance quality while enabling larger models are practically useful for the diffusion-guidance community.
