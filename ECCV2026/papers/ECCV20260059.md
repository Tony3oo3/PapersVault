## Problem

Distractor-free radiance field research lacks a large-scale, challenging real-world dataset with paired clean and cluttered images per scene. Existing benchmarks (RobustNeRF: 5 scenes, On-the-go: 12 scenes) have become saturated, making it difficult to distinguish method performance and hindering progress from scene-specific optimization toward generalizable solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260059/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the benchmark, each scene provides paired sets of ~12MP JPEG images (clean and cluttered) captured with consumer cameras (iPhone, Samsung; 9 resolutions) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses; methods must synthesize clean novel views from the cluttered set. For DI²FIX, the input is a degraded radiance field rendering at a target view plus a reference rendering (replacing DIFIX's original clean reference view), and the output is an enhanced rendering with distractor artifacts removed. The dataset spans 89,924 images across 1,048 scenes with 128 distractor types and 161 scene themes in both indoor and outdoor settings.

## Method

The paper introduces DF3DV-1K, a 1,048-scene dataset captured with consumer cameras under casual conditions (auto exposure/focus, anti-shake disabled), with a curated 41-scene subset (DF3DV-41) systematically designed across 17 challenging scenarios (e.g., semantically similar distractors, fluid distractors, nighttime). A comprehensive benchmark evaluates nine distractor-free radiance field methods plus 3DGS across all scenes, identifying AsymGS and RobustSplat as most robust. Beyond benchmarking, the authors fine-tune DIFIX (a single-step diffusion-based 2D enhancer) into DI²FIX by constructing 316,890 paired training samples from radiance field reconstructions of cluttered images in DF3DV-1K*, filtering pairs by LPIPS ≤ 0.5, and replacing the clean reference view with a radiance field rendering. This plug-and-play enhancer achieves 0.96 dB PSNR and 0.057 LPIPS improvements over vanilla methods without any scene-specific tuning or model architecture changes.

## Results

On the new DF3DV-1K benchmark (1,048 scenes), AsymGS achieves the best PSNR of 20.49 dB and SSIM of 0.735, while RobustSplat achieves the best LPIPS of 0.226; both substantially outperform the 3DGS baseline (17.93 dB PSNR, 0.330 LPIPS). On the more challenging DF3DV-41 subset (41 systematically designed scenes), AsymGS leads with 20.35 dB PSNR and 0.712 SSIM, and RobustSplat achieves the lowest LPIPS of 0.232. The DI²FIX enhancer, fine-tuned on DF3DV-1K*, improves the average rendering quality of all benchmarked methods by 0.96 dB PSNR and 0.057 LPIPS reduction (from 20.82/0.211 to 21.78/0.154), while the unmodified DIFIX actually degrades performance (20.16 dB PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the new DF3DV-1K and DF3DV-41 benchmarks, AsymGS [33] (NeurIPS'25) and RobustSplat [19] (ICCV'25) are the most robust, with AsymGS leading in PSNR/SSIM (20.49/0.735 on DF3DV-1K) and RobustSplat leading in LPIPS (0.226/0.232). OCSplats [44] and DeGauss [79] (both ICCV'25) form the second tier, achieving 20.02 and 20.00 PSNR respectively on DF3DV-1K. Earlier methods—SLS [64] (19.75 PSNR), DeSplat [82] (19.26), WildGaussians [30] (18.78), and T-3DGS [50] (18.92)—show progressively larger gaps, with T-3DGS-TMR underperforming even the vanilla 3DGS baseline on DF3DV-41 (18.41 vs. 18.10 PSNR). The ranking on DF3DV-1K closely follows publication chronology, confirming the benchmark's sensitivity to methodological advances. For the DI²FIX enhancer, fine-tuning on DF3DV-1K* (1,007 scenes) yields consistent gains over all nine radiance field methods, whereas DIFIX without domain-specific fine-tuning degrades average PSNR by 0.66 dB and DIFIX+RobustNeRF (trained on only 5 scenes) still loses 0.28 dB, underscoring the importance of large-scale, diverse training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distractor-free radiance field line initiated by RobustNeRF (2023, 5 indoor scenes) and On-the-go (2024, 12 scenes) by scaling the evaluation setting to 1,048 scenes with clean and cluttered image pairs, 128 distractor types, and 161 scene themes. It builds directly on DIFIX (a diffusion-based 2D enhancer for sparse-view radiance fields) to demonstrate DI²FIX, a domain-adapted variant fine-tuned on the new dataset. The benchmark systematically evaluates nine recent distractor-free 3DGS/NeRF methods (AsymGS, RobustSplat, OCSplats, DeGauss, SLS, DeSplat, WildGaussians, T-3DGS, DeSplat) alongside vanilla 3DGS, positioning itself as the missing large-scale evaluation infrastructure that the rapid 2024–2025 method proliferation has outpaced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DF3DV-1K fills a critical infrastructure gap: prior distractor-free benchmarks (5–12 scenes) have become saturated, forcing methods to highlight subtle background differences to claim gains, while the field's rapid growth (paper count doubled in 2025) demands a more challenging and diverse evaluation. The dataset's scale (~100× prior work) and systematic scenario design (DF3DV-41) enable identification of specific failure modes—semantically similar distractors, fluid phenomena, and nighttime scenes—that smaller benchmarks cannot expose. The DI²FIX pilot demonstrates a path toward generalizable, plug-and-play enhancement, but the broader open question remains: can a truly generalizable distractor-free 3DGS (only one appeared in 2026) be trained at scale on this data? Limitations include the dataset still being smaller than general 3D datasets due to manual capture costs, and DI²FIX's susceptibility to confirmation bias under extreme degradation.
