## Problem

Existing portrait animation methods face a trilemma: 2D diffusion-based approaches achieve high expressiveness but are slow (0.01–0.03 FPS) and 3D-inconsistent, while 3D-aware methods (NeRF/Gaussian-based) offer consistency and speed but produce muted expressions due to entangled representations requiring expensive global attention for motion fusion. No prior feed-forward method simultaneously achieves real-time speed, 3D consistency, and fine-grained expression detail such as wrinkles and asymmetric motions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB facial image as the source (encoded once in ~20 ms into ~200 K 3D Gaussians via triplanes) and a driving image providing target expression and pose. At inference, only a lightweight motion decoder (compact MLPs) processes the driving signal per frame, producing a rendered 512×512 image with the source identity and driving expression at an arbitrary camera viewpoint, running at 107.31 FPS on an NVIDIA 6000 Ada GPU with 0.4 GB memory.

## Method

The encoder adapts LP3D's triplane architecture (96 channels) to decode 3D Gaussians, splitting channels into 48-dim appearance features and 48-dim learned motion basis vectors per Gaussian. The key architectural insight is feature-space deformation: instead of updating Gaussian positions/scales/rotations in 3D space, a per-Gaussian MLP (with AdaLN conditioned on X-NeMo's 512-dim motion coefficients) predicts a 48-dim residual added to the feature vector before decoding, enabling rich asymmetric expression details with only local computation. This decouples animation from the 3D structure, eliminating the need for global attention mechanisms. Training uses a synthetic dataset of 60 K+ FFHQ identities with 8 expressions each, generated by frontalizing portraits with LP3D and synthesizing expressions with X-NeMo restricted to frontal views, with multi-view supervision from a frozen LP3D estimator and a self-reenactment objective combining L1, LPIPS, identity, detail, sparsity, and adversarial losses.

## Results

On the VOODOO-XP cross-reenactment benchmark, the method achieves the best 3D consistency (MEt3R = 0.028, tied with InvertAvatar), best expression transfer accuracy (AED = 0.731, EMO = 0.785), and best animation speed (107.31 FPS) simultaneously. On self-reenactment, it also achieves the best MEt3R (0.025), best 3D shape (IoU = 0.85), lowest memory (0.4 GB), and highest FPS (107.31), while trailing InvertAvatar only on PSNR (22.34 vs. 23.43) and SSIM (0.835 vs. 0.846). The method is 3–4 orders of magnitude faster than diffusion-based baselines (X-NeMo at 0.03 FPS, HunyuanPortrait at 0.01 FPS) while surpassing them on both 3D consistency and expression fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest 2D diffusion baseline X-NeMo [109], the method wins on MEt3R (0.028 vs. 0.035), EMO (0.785 vs. 0.760), AED (0.731 vs. 0.805), and FPS (107.31 vs. 0.03), while X-NeMo has a slightly lower ID score (0.72 vs. 0.76) that the authors attribute to pose-transfer errors in the 2D method. Against the fastest 2D GAN baseline LivePortrait [26], the method wins on MEt3R (0.028 vs. 0.033), EMO (0.785 vs. 0.716), AED (0.731 vs. 0.810), and FPS (107.31 vs. 78.12), with LivePortrait marginally better on APD (0.026 vs. 0.028). Among 3D-aware baselines, the method dominates on EMO and AED across all competitors (e.g., EMO 0.785 vs. 0.589 for Portrait4D-v2, 0.654 for GAGAvatar, 0.565 for InvertAvatar), confirming that the feature-space deformation with motion basis vectors captures expression details that global-attention triplane methods miss. InvertAvatar [108] achieves the highest ID (0.80) but at the cost of collapsed 3D geometry (worse IoU in Table 1) and poor expression transfer (EMO 0.565, AED 0.891). Even when Portrait4D-v2 and GAGAvatar are retrained from scratch on the paper's synthetic dataset († variants), they still trail significantly on EMO (0.448, 0.578) and AED (0.972, 0.944), indicating the gap is architectural rather than data-driven. The method's 107.31 FPS represents a 7× speedup over the next-fastest 3D method (Portrait4D-v2 at 14.50 FPS) and a ~3500× speedup over X-NeMo, while using only 0.4 GB memory versus 1.2–6.0 GB for baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LP3D's triplane-based 2D-to-3D lifting encoder and 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering, adapting the former's NeRF decoder into a Gaussian decoder. It departs from prior 3D animation methods (Portrait4D-v2, VOODOO-XP) that entangle 3D structure and motion via global residual triplanes and expensive attention mechanisms, instead proposing a local per-Gaussian deformation in high-dimensional feature space guided by learned motion basis vectors. The pre-trained X-NeMo diffusion model is repurposed not as an animation backbone but as a frozen motion encoder and a data synthesizer for training, a conceptual shift from using diffusion models directly for inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 107 FPS with 3D consistency and expression quality competitive with diffusion-based methods, this work makes real-time 3D-consistent expressive portrait animation practically viable for AR/VR, video conferencing, and digital twin applications where sub-frame latency is critical. The feature-space deformation insight (deforming in the triplane feature vector space rather than 3D spatial space) is a reusable architectural principle for dynamic Gaussian representations. Open questions include extending to high-quality eyewear reconstruction, disentangling lighting/appearance, and whether the approach generalizes beyond faces to full-body or object animation.
