## Problem

Monolithic VLA policies trained on fragmented, cross-lab robotic data suffer from catastrophic forgetting, gradient conflict across task families, and compounding rollout errors, with no mechanism for targeted, diagnosis-guided refinement. The paper identifies the root cause as the absence of an explicit decoupling between universal physical dynamics and task-specialized execution strategies, and the lack of a closed-loop that can attribute failures to specific modules and update only those modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262261/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a natural-language task instruction and an offline experience manifold (e.g., DROID snapshots) from which a grounded initial observation is retrieved via contrastive cross-modal embedding; it also conditions on a growing experience memory of past (task, scene, expert, score, failure-type) tuples. Outputs include the executed action sequence, an imagined video rollout from the world model, a continuous VLM success score in [0,1] paired with a categorical diagnostic tag (Task-Scene Mismatch, Policy Failure, or World-Model Failure), and targeted parameter updates to whichever module the tag implicates—rather than a single global policy update.

## Method

EvoW decouples a universal action-conditioned video world model (latent transition z_{t+1} ~ p_θ(z_{t+1}|z_t, a_t) with encoder/decoder) from a modular library of pluggable VLA experts, each specialized to a task family. An MLLM-based strategic router, conditioned on the instruction, grounded scene, and long-term experience memory, selects the best expert via a dual exploration/exploitation policy, and the world model generates counterfactual rollouts for each candidate to score predicted task consistency before real execution. After the selected expert produces a trajectory, a VLM evaluator assigns a success score and a failure-attribution tag; the tag then triggers a targeted update—refining the grounding manifold for TSM, retraining the specific VLA expert for PF, or adjusting world-model dynamics for WMF—while all other modules remain frozen. This diagnosis-driven, module-specific plasticity replaces global retraining and enables compounding gains across iterative rounds without re-initializing the full system.

## Results

Across four VLA backbones (GR00T-N1.6, PI-0.5, PI-0, PI-0-fast) and five manipulation skill families, EvoW routing improves average success rate from 0.259 to 0.448 (absolute +18.9 pp, relative +73.0%), with per-backbone relative gains ranging from +62.0% (PI-0) to +82.8% (GR00T-N1.6). On real-robot validation with a Franka arm (GR00T-N1.6 backbone, 20 trials per task), EvoW raises average success rate from 29% to 70%. For generative quality, EvoW achieves lower FID and FVD than WorldGym (WPE), IRASim, and Ctrl-World under view-conditioned rollouts, with the larger FVD reduction indicating stronger long-horizon temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RoboGen [38] on task/scene diversity (Table 2), EvoW achieves the lowest Self-BLEU (0.281 vs. 0.284) and lowest CLIP scene similarity (0.754 vs. 0.762), indicating more diverse and less redundant generated tasks under grounding constraints; however, RoboGen scores lower on SentenceBERT (0.165 vs. 0.312) and ViT (0.193 vs. 0.337) similarity, reflecting that EvoW's executability constraints trade off some free-form linguistic and visual variety. On generative quality (Figure 7), EvoW outperforms all three world-model baselines—WorldGym/WPE [30], IRASim [44], and Ctrl-World [14]—on both FID and FVD under view-conditioned settings, with the FVD gap being particularly large, suggesting substantially better temporal coherence over long-horizon rollouts. The paper does not report a single unified head-to-head table against these world-model baselines on policy success rate; instead, the policy-level comparison (Table 1) is framed as EvoW-routing vs. monolithic Base within the same backbone, while the world-model baselines are compared only on rollout fidelity metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoW builds on the Dreamer lineage of latent world models for planning [15–18] and the co-evolving world-model/skill principle from [37], but departs sharply from the dominant monolithic VLA scaling paradigm (RT-1/RT-2, OpenVLA, π0/π0.5, GR00T N1) by explicitly decoupling shared physical dynamics into a universal video world model and task execution into a library of pluggable VLA experts. It also extends RoboGen's closed-loop task generation [38] from pure simulation to grounded real-scene initialization with deployment-time expert routing, and goes beyond world-model-as-evaluator baselines (WorldGym, IRASim, Ctrl-World) by closing the loop with VLM-driven failure diagnosis that triggers targeted, module-specific updates rather than global retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a concrete architectural answer to two persistent pain points in embodied AI—fragmented data silos and catastrophic forgetting under continual task introduction—by making updates modular and diagnosis-guided. However, real-robot validation is limited to a single Franka arm, five tasks, and 20 trials each, leaving open whether the 29%→70% transfer generalizes across embodiments and longer horizons. The reliance on an offline experience manifold for scene anchoring constrains open-world applicability, and the world model's long-horizon fidelity (compounding error, semantic drift) remains an acknowledged bottleneck that the current diagnostic loop only partially mitigates. Scaling the anchor repository, improving uncertainty-aware rollouts, and calibrating VLM confidence signals are identified as key next steps.
