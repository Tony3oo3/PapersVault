## Problem

Diffusion classifiers inherit the majority bias of their underlying generative model: they accurately recognize high-density concepts but fail on underrepresented, low-density regions of the data manifold. Prior minority-sampling methods generate diverse images at inference time via classifier guidance or per-prompt optimization but leave the model's internal representation unchanged, so they cannot translate generative diversity into improved classification. The direct relationship between minority generative coverage and diffusion-classifier perception had remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

For classification, the method takes an RGB image and a set of class-conditioning text prompts, then outputs a predicted class label by selecting the prompt whose noise prediction most closely matches the ground-truth noise. For generation, it takes a single text prompt and produces an image at the same speed as standard DDIM sampling (≈1 s/image). Training requires only arbitrary caption data (100k+ prompts from HPSv2) with no images, no external reward models, and no per-prompt optimization; the resulting LoRA adapter is plug-and-play and attachable to SD 1.5 or SD 2.0 backbones with a user-controllable scaling factor.

## Method

MiPO fine-tunes a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model via a compact LoRA adapter using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a self-computed minority preference reward. The reward is a DDIM-based Tweedie's-formula approximation: a generated sample is re-noised to an intermediate timestep, denoised via DDIM, and the L2 reconstruction discrepancy serves as a proxy for how far the sample lies in a low-density (minority) region. For each prompt, G SDE trajectories sharing the same initial noise are sampled, group-normalized advantages are computed, and the policy is updated with a clipped GRPO objective plus KL regularization (α=0.7, β=0.15) to preserve the pretrained generative prior. Crucially, policy updates are restricted to the first 40% of diffusion timesteps (early denoising steps), where coarse semantic structure is formed, reducing compute while maintaining fidelity. The entire loop is self-improving: the model generates its own candidate samples, scores them for minority-ness, and refines its policy without any external image data or reward model.

## Results

On SD 1.5, MiPO improves zero-shot diffusion classification accuracy across all five primary benchmarks: CIFAR-10 (85.38→87.89, +2.51%), CIFAR-10-C (57.72→59.76, +2.04%), ImageNet-Tiny (46.50→47.30, +0.80%), Caltech-101 (97.81→99.51, +1.70%), and SUN09 (64.69→68.25, +3.56%). On SD 2.0, gains are larger on ImageNet-Tiny (51.45→55.25, +3.80%) and SUN09 (64.72→69.26, +4.54%), though CIFAR-10 drops slightly (88.58→86.98). All improvements are achieved without any additional image data, using only arbitrary HPSv2 captions and a self-computed minority preference reward. For minority image generation, MiPO matches DDIM inference speed (1 s/image) while achieving Minority Score 0.2289 and JEPA-SCORE −646.03, close to the 63 s MinorityPrompt baseline (0.2311, −647.97).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla diffusion classifier (Li et al., ICCV 2023), MiPO wins on 9 of 10 dataset-backbone combinations, with the largest gains on SUN09 (+3.56% SD1.5, +4.54% SD2.0) and ImageNet-Tiny (+3.80% SD2.0); the sole exception is CIFAR-10 under SD 2.0 where accuracy drops by 1.60%. For minority image generation (Table 4), MiPO outperforms the DDIM baseline on minority-sensitive metrics (Minority Score 0.2289 vs. 0.2132; JEPA-SCORE −646.03 vs. −568.80) while retaining 97% of DDIM's CLIPScore (30.80 vs. 31.76) and 77% of its HPSv3 (4.41 vs. 5.76). Compared to MinorityPrompt (Sehwag et al., CVPR 2024), MiPO achieves a nearly identical Minority Score (0.2289 vs. 0.2311) and JEPA-SCORE (−646.03 vs. −647.97) but runs 63× faster (1 s vs. 63 s per image) and is fully prompt-adaptive without per-prompt optimization. On human preference (Table 5), MiPO scores 0.228 ImageReward (vs. 0.184 baseline, −0.026 MinorityPrompt) and 0.252 HPSv2 (vs. 0.254 baseline, 0.241 MinorityPrompt), indicating it preserves perceptual quality better than MinorityPrompt while still promoting minority generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: diffusion-based zero-shot classifiers (Li et al., ICCV 2023; Clark & Jaini, NeurIPS 2023), inference-time minority sampling in diffusion models (Sehwag et al., CVPR 2022 and follow-ups), and RL-based diffusion alignment (DDPO, DanceGRPO, FlowGRPO). The key conceptual departure is repurposing the DDIM-based minority score—previously used only as an inference-time guidance signal—into a training-time reward for GRPO-based LoRA fine-tuning, thereby updating the model's internal representation rather than merely steering sampling. Unlike prior minority generation methods that leave the backbone unchanged, MiPO is the first to demonstrate that strengthening minority generative coverage directly translates into improved diffusion classifier perception, closing a loop that prior works left open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is significant: MiPO requires no additional image data, no external reward models (CLIPScore, ImageReward, etc.), and produces a lightweight LoRA adapter that is plug-and-play across diffusion backbones, making it attractive for practitioners who want to improve zero-shot classification without collecting new data. However, gains are modest (1–3.8%) and inconsistent—LabelME and VOC2007 actually degrade—and the paper acknowledges an inherent trade-off between minority distinctiveness and perceptual fidelity that remains unresolved. An open question is whether this generation-to-perception link generalizes beyond diffusion classifiers to other generative-model-based understanding tasks (e.g., segmentation, video understanding), and whether the caption-only self-improving loop can be extended to truly long-tail or novel categories absent from the pretraining distribution.
