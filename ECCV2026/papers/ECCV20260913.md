## Problem

Existing audio-visual test-time adaptation methods, when extended to the continual setting without source data, suffer from catastrophic forgetting where performance drops well below even the pre-trained source model due to unrestricted continual parameter updates. A comprehensive study of source-free audio-visual CTTA under both unimodal and bimodal corruptions, with a method that mitigates this forgetting while maintaining strong test-time accuracy, was missing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260913/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw audio spectrograms (B×T×F) and video frames (B×H×W×C) in a strictly online, single-forward-pass-per-batch regime with no access to source data or task boundaries. From each batch, it computes channel-wise mean and diagonal covariance for the visual modality and time-axis mean and diagonal covariance for the audio modality, which serve as lightweight distributional signatures for parameter retrieval. The output is a class label over the same concept space as the source model (e.g., 400 classes for Kinetics50, 309 for VGGSound).

## Method

AVReCAP exploits the observation that the attention fusion layer (W_Q, W_K, W_V of the joint encoder f_j) exhibits strong intra- and cross-task transferability: adapting it on one corruption domain generalizes to related and even unrelated domains, outperforming the frozen source model. At each time step, the method computes modality-specific Gaussian statistics of the current batch and uses KL divergence to find the most distributionally similar element in a shared buffer of stored parameter snapshots. The retrieved attention parameters are plugged into f_j and adapted for one gradient step using READ's confidence and negative-entropy losses, then the buffer element is updated via a moment-preserving EMA. When no buffer element falls within a threshold τ, a new snapshot is added; to enforce a fixed memory budget η, the two most similar buffer elements are merged by averaging. Throughout, both unimodal encoders remain frozen, and only the single attention block of the joint encoder is adapted, which is key to minimizing catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On Kinetics50-C with visual corruptions (unimodal), AVReCAP (η=50) achieves 62.75% mean task-wise accuracy, outperforming the next-best method SuMi (61.10%) by 1.65% and the pre-trained SOURCE model (59.68%) by 3.07%, while READ degrades to 49.46% due to catastrophic forgetting. On the more challenging VGGSound-C with audio corruptions, AVReCAP (η=400) reaches 30.61% mean accuracy, surpassing PTA (29.26%) and SOURCE (24.96%) by 1.35% and 5.65% respectively. Under correlated bimodal corruptions on VGGSound-2C, AVReCAP achieves a 6.28% improvement over SOURCE while incurring only a 2.9% catastrophic forgetting drop, compared to 27.9% for READ across 15 bimodal corruption tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVReCAP achieves the best mean accuracy on Kinetics50-C visual corruptions (62.75%), beating SuMi (61.10%) by 1.65% and EATA (60.07%) by 2.68%, while READ suffers severe degradation to 49.46% due to progressive attention imbalance and error accumulation across domains. On VGGSound-C visual corruptions, AVReCAP (56.20%) marginally surpasses SOURCE (55.94%) and SAR (55.94%), whereas BriMPR* collapses to 35.62% without source data access. On Kinetics50-C audio corruptions, READ (71.01%) narrowly edges out AVReCAP (70.87%), but both substantially outperform BriMPR* (67.78%). On VGGSound-C audio corruptions, AVReCAP (30.61%) clearly leads over PTA (29.26%) and SOURCE (24.96%), while TENT catastrophically fails at 1.30%. Under bimodal corruptions on VGGSound-2C, AVReCAP delivers a 6.28% gain over SOURCE while most baselines (READ, PTA, BriMPR*) fall below the unadapted source model, and AVReCAP incurs only 2.9% catastrophic forgetting versus 27.9% for READ after 15×14,046 continual updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends READ's single-domain audio-visual TTA (which adapts the joint encoder's attention fusion layer) to the continual setting, where domains arrive sequentially without task boundaries. It departs from prior vision-based CTTA methods that rely on source data for warm-starting or meta-learning by enforcing a strict source-free constraint, and from prior AV-TTA methods (PTA, BriMPR) that only offer limited continual extensions. Conceptually, it borrows the replay-buffer idea from continual learning literature but repurposes it to store parameter snapshots (attention weights) rather than data, guided by a KL-divergence-based retrieval criterion over input-level statistics. The key conceptual departure is the observation that the fusion layer's parameters exhibit intra- and cross-category transferability, enabling selective retrieval instead of the continual overwriting that causes catastrophic forgetting in prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive study of source-free audio-visual CTTA under both unimodal and bimodal corruptions, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in the literature. The 2.9% vs. 27.9% forgetting gap on VGGSound-2C demonstrates that the buffer-retrieval mechanism is a practical solution for deployment scenarios where models face evolving sensor conditions without access to training data. Limitations include reliance on a single fusion architecture (CAV-MAE with one attention block), a manually tuned threshold τ, and evaluation limited to two datasets; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to deeper multi-block fusion layers, non-transformer architectures, or settings with concept drift (changing label spaces).
