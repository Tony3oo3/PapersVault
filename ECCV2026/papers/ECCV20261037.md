## Problem

Global native-resolution visual encoding in MLLMs consistently outperforms slice-based methods in both semantic understanding and spatial perception, but incurs substantial computational overhead because the vision encoder must process a large number of tokens before any reduction. The paper identifies this efficiency gap and asks whether a pretrained ViT can be reconfigured to retain the benefits of global encoding while drastically reducing token count and inference latency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261037/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at native resolution (up to 1024×1024) alongside a text prompt, and produces a free-form text response. Unlike slice-based MLLMs that require special layout tokens (e.g., ',' and '/n') to inform the LLM of image partitioning, NaVLM-PVC uses only <image> and </image> placeholders, injecting all visual tokens as a contiguous context. The PVC framework compresses the token sequence by a factor of 64 (e.g., from 1024 post-embedding tokens to 16 final tokens), directly reducing LLM prefill cost.

## Method

The Progressive Visual Compression (PVC) framework reconfigures a pretrained ViT via two jointly modulated modules. Refined Patch Embedding (RPE) scales the patch size to finer levels (e.g., 14×14 → 10×10) through a pseudo-inverse weight transformation that preserves representational equivalence of the original embeddings while increasing token granularity. Windowed Token Compression (WTC) inserts lightweight compression layers at multiple ViT stages, each merging tokens within non-overlapping 2×2 windows using a content-adaptive pooling mechanism: an MLP computes channel-wise attention logits over the four local tokens (initialized to uniform weights for stable convergence), yielding a learnable weighted aggregation that progressively reduces sequence length across J stages. Training follows a three-stage paradigm (vision-language alignment, joint multimodal pre-training, supervised fine-tuning) with an additional pre-alignment stage to restore the embedding space after PVC modifications, using only 20.1M image-text pairs.

## Results

NaVLM-PVC (Qwen2-7B, 20.1M training pairs, compression ratio 64) achieves 81.3 on MMBench, 77.2 on SEED-Image, 64.2 on MathVista, and 52.2 on HallusionBench, outperforming Qwen2-VL (700M pairs, ratio 4) on MMBench (+0.6), SEED-Image (+1.9), MathVista (+6.0), and HallusionBench (+1.6) while using ~35× less training data. On OCR tasks, NaVLM-PVC scores 82.7 on OCRBench and 92.8 on DocVQA, slightly below Qwen2-VL (86.6, 94.5) but above MiniCPM-V-2.6 on OCRBench (82.7 vs 85.2 is a loss; 92.8 vs 90.8 is a win on DocVQA). The method reduces TTFT by 2.4× relative to MoonViT and 1.9× relative to Qwen2-VL at 1024×1024 resolution, with a final token count of 256 (vs. 1024 for the baseline ViT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2-VL (the primary commercial competitor), NaVLM-PVC wins on MMBench (81.3 vs 80.7), SEED-Image (77.2 vs 75.3), MathVista (64.2 vs 58.2), and HallusionBench (52.2 vs 50.6), but trails on MME (2183.6 vs 2326.8), MMMU (50.2 vs 54.1), OCRBench (82.7 vs 86.6), and TextVQA (79.8 vs 84.3), while achieving 1.9× lower TTFT. Versus MiniCPM-V-2.6 (slice-based, ratio 16), NaVLM-PVC leads on MMBench (+3.3), SEED-Image (+3.1), MMStar (+3.0), MathVista (+3.6), HallusionBench (+4.1), and CV-Bench-2D (+3.8), but loses on MME (2183.6 vs 2348.4) and OCRBench (82.7 vs 85.2). Against InternVL2 (ratio 4), NaVLM-PVC surpasses on MMBench, SEED-Image, MathVista (+5.9), HallusionBench (+7.0), and RealWorldQA (+5.9), while trailing on MME and MMMU. Compared to MoonViT under identical LLaVA training, ViT-PVC achieves comparable average accuracy (64.26 vs 64.11) with 2.4× lower TTFT. Against closed-source GPT-4o-mini, NaVLM-PVC wins on MMBench (+4.5), SEED-Image (+4.7), and MathVista (+11.7) but falls short on MME, MMMU, and OCRBench. The highest compression ratio (64) among all compared models is a distinguishing efficiency advantage, though it comes at a modest cost on OCR-heavy benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FlexiViT weight-transformation scheme (Beyer et al., CVPR 2023) into the RPE module, repurposing it not for classification but for increasing visual token granularity in MLLM vision encoders. It departs from the dominant slice-based encoding paradigm (LLaVA-UHD, MiniCPM-V) by demonstrating—through controlled experiments and attention analysis—that global native-resolution encoding is mechanistically superior, then addresses its computational cost via a novel Progressive Visual Compression framework inserted *within* ViT layers rather than at the projector. The base encoder is MoonViT (Kimi-VL, 2025), which is reconfigured into ViT-PVC by jointly modulating patch size and hierarchical compression positions, following design principles from Swin Transformer's hierarchical structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.4× TTFT reduction over MoonViT and 1.9× over Qwen2-VL while maintaining competitive accuracy makes native-resolution encoding practically viable for latency-sensitive deployments, and the PVC framework is modular enough to retrofit onto other pretrained ViTs. The ShapeGrid benchmark and the cross-shaped attention bias analysis provide a reusable diagnostic for evaluating spatial perception in MLLMs. Open questions include whether PVC generalizes to video or 3D inputs, whether the compression ratio can be pushed beyond 64× without quality loss, and whether the content-adaptive pooling mechanism transfers to other transformer backbones (e.g., Mamba-based encoders).
