## Problem

Novel view synthesis through non-planar refractive surfaces (e.g., water) is fundamentally broken by existing methods: NeRF and 3DGS assume straight-line ray propagation, while prior refractive approaches like NeRFrac parameterize the interface in view-dependent spaces, causing geometric inconsistencies across views and precluding accurate surface reconstruction. No prior method jointly optimizes both the refractive boundary and the underlying scene using differentiable Gaussian ray tracing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260340/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images captured from above a water surface along with calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics. The NeRFrac benchmark provides 8 training + 1 test view (384×512 real, 392×392 synthetic), while the newly introduced RefracGS dataset offers 24 training + 6 test views with wider angular coverage that amplifies refraction, plus ground-truth water-surface geometry for RMSE evaluation. Beyond novel-view renderings, the method outputs an explicit differentiable water-surface height field and the full 3D Gaussian scene beneath, enabling downstream applications such as water removal and arbitrary surface editing without retraining.

## Method

RefracGS decouples the problem into two jointly optimized components: a Water Height Map (an MLP-parameterized neural height field H(x,y) combined with a recursively subdivided triangular mesh proxy) for the refractive surface, and a 3D Gaussian field for the underlying scene. During forward rendering, each camera ray is intersected with the mesh proxy via a coarse-to-fine Recursive Subdivision Tracing scheme (BVH-accelerated, progressively refining only hit triangles), the local normal is estimated by barycentric interpolation of vertex normals, and the ray is bent via Snell's law before traversing the Gaussian primitives with standard alpha compositing. The key differentiator is full differentiability: during backpropagation, gradients flow not only to Gaussian parameters (position, opacity, SH coefficients) but also through the refracted ray's origin and direction back into the MLP height-field weights, enabling end-to-end joint optimization from image supervision alone. An additional opacity regularization loss suppresses floaters in sparsely-viewed regions, and the entire pipeline is implemented with custom CUDA kernels and NVIDIA OptiX for real-time rendering at ~200 FPS.

## Results

On the NeRFrac Real dataset, RefracGS achieves PSNR 30.67, SSIM 0.913, and LPIPS 0.129, outperforming the previous best refractive method NeRFrac (PSNR 28.15) by over 2.5 dB while training ~15× faster (10 min vs. 149 min) and rendering at 242 FPS vs. <1 FPS. On the more challenging RefracGS dataset with stronger refraction, the gap widens dramatically: RefracGS reaches PSNR 30.22 and RMSE 0.115 cm, whereas NeRFrac drops to PSNR 17.84 and RMSE 3.655 cm. On NeRFrac Synthetic, RefracGS scores PSNR 35.82 and SSIM 0.952, again surpassing NeRFrac (34.38 / 0.944) with 12 min training and 203 FPS rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefracGS decisively outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest refractive baseline NeRFrac, it gains +2.5 dB PSNR on NeRFrac Real, +1.4 dB on NeRFrac Synthetic, and a striking +12.4 dB on the harder RefracGS dataset (30.22 vs. 17.84), while reducing training time from 77–164 min to 10–12 min (~15× speedup) and enabling real-time rendering (124–242 FPS) versus NeRFrac's <1 FPS. WSGS, which assumes a planar water surface, degrades substantially on non-planar scenes (e.g., PSNR 17.92 vs. 30.22 on RefracGS), confirming that explicit non-planar refraction modeling is essential. General-purpose methods (3DGS, 3DGRT, Mip-NeRF, TensoRF, Plenoxels) that ignore refraction entirely produce severe artifacts under strong distortion, with 3DGS reaching only PSNR 17.48 on RefracGS. On surface geometry accuracy (RMSE), RefracGS achieves 0.115 cm on RefracGS versus NeRFrac's 3.655 cm, a 32× improvement, validating the Water Height Map representation for precise refractive-surface recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Ray Tracing (3DGRT) by making the refractive surface itself differentiable, enabling joint optimization of both the water interface and the underlying scene from image supervision alone. It departs from NeRFrac's view-dependent refractive field (which parameterizes surface normals as functions of camera rays, breaking multi-view consistency) by introducing a world-space neural height field with recursive subdivision tracing, combining mesh-like intersection efficiency with field-like geometric continuity. The approach also contrasts with WSGS, which handles refractive Gaussian splatting but assumes a perfectly planar water surface, and with TransparentGS, which bakes refracted content via light-field probes rather than tracing actual ray paths through a continuous interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RefracGS is the first method to directly optimize a refractive surface through differentiable Gaussian ray tracing, closing a gap where prior refractive NVS methods either lacked multi-view consistency (NeRFrac) or assumed planar geometry (WSGS). The ~15× training speedup over NeRFrac with superior quality and real-time 200+ FPS rendering makes through-surface reconstruction practically viable for applications like bathymetry and underwater heritage documentation. Key limitations remain: the single-valued height field cannot represent breaking or curling waves, the framework is restricted to static scenes, and reflection/scattering at the interface are not modeled. Open questions include extending to dynamic water, integrating 2D Gaussian representations for sharper geometry, and handling more complex multi-interface scenarios.
