## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems are designed for pinhole cameras with limited field of view, producing severe 3D mapping blind spots and rendering voids in omnidirectional settings. No prior GS-SLAM framework natively ingests 360° panoramic imagery while maintaining a single coherent pose state with gradient consistency across multiple viewing directions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261084/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes 360° panoramic frames in either equirectangular (ERP, e.g., 1920×960 or 2000×1000) or panoramic annular lens (PAL, 1280×960) format, which are internally factorized into four fixed-orientation virtual pinhole views sharing one optical center. Dense monocular depth priors from Depth Anywhere guide Gaussian initialization and outdoor sky/background handling but are not used as persistent tracking constraints. Outputs include the estimated 6DoF camera trajectory, an anisotropic 3D Gaussian scene map with spherical-harmonic appearance, and photorealistic novel-view renderings from arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

Cube-Splat factorizes each panoramic frame into a four-face cubemap of virtual pinhole cameras with known fixed relative rotations, designating the front face as the primary pose state. During tracking, per-face Lie-algebra perturbations are mapped back to the primary state via the adjoint representation of the inter-face transforms (Ad_M), so that the Gauss-Newton Hessian and gradient are constructed by aggregating local Jacobians across all faces through adjoint-consistent accumulation, yielding a face-invariant, single coherent pose update. During mapping, the frozen pose is used to optimize Gaussian attributes (means, covariances, opacities, SH coefficients) by minimizing photometric loss across all cubemap faces, with depth priors guiding insertion of new Gaussians in underobserved regions followed by standard cloning and splitting densification. The framework is evaluated on a newly introduced SynPano dataset generated via an automated Blender Cycles pipeline with Bezier-interpolated 6DoF trajectories and pixel-perfect multi-modal ground truths.

## Results

On the real-world PALVIO indoor dataset, Cube-Splat achieves an ATE of 0.077 m and PSNR of 29.25 dB, dramatically outperforming MonoGS (ATE 2.89 m, PSNR 23.67 dB) and S3PO-GS (ATE 1.93 m, PSNR 27.70 dB). On the challenging unbounded outdoor SynPano sequences, Cube-Splat maintains an ATE of 0.106 m while MonoGS drifts to 6.59 m and both Photo-SLAM and S3PO-GS fail entirely. Across all five dataset splits (indoor and outdoor), Cube-Splat achieves the best ATE, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS in every case, with PSNR gains of 1.5–4.6 dB over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cube-Splat outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all five dataset splits. Against MonoGS, the most direct pinhole GS-SLAM baseline, Cube-Splat reduces ATE by 37× on PALVIO (0.077 m vs. 2.89 m) and by 62× on outdoor SynPano (0.106 m vs. 6.59 m), while also improving PSNR by 5.6 dB in the outdoor setting. Photo-SLAM and S3PO-GS both fail to maintain tracking on the complex-trajectory outdoor SynPano sequences (marked as '–'), whereas Cube-Splat sustains a 0.106 m ATE. On the synthetic indoor OmniBlender scenes, where baselines perform more competitively, Cube-Splat still leads with an ATE of 0.0013 m versus 0.0095 m (Photo-SLAM) and 0.1221 m (S3PO-GS), and a PSNR of 33.22 dB versus 32.18 dB (S3PO-GS). The ablation (Table 2) further shows that the gains are not merely from wider FoV: extending MonoGS to four mapping faces without adjoint-consistent tracking reduces ATE only to 0.106 m, while the full adjoint-consistent four-face tracking brings it to 0.004 m.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cube-Splat extends the MonoGS (Gaussian Splatting SLAM) pipeline from pinhole cameras to 360° panoramic inputs by factorizing each frame into a four-face cubemap of virtual pinhole views sharing a single optical center. It departs from prior panoramic Gaussian splatting work (ODGS, OmniGS, ErpGS, 360-GS, PanSplat), which targets offline reconstruction or rendering, by integrating multi-face observations into an online SLAM loop with a single coherent pose state. The adjoint-consistent gradient aggregation draws on Lie-group theory to ensure that per-face photometric residuals update one primary pose invariantly, a formulation not present in prior panoramic SLAM systems (360ORB-SLAM, 360VO) that rely on geometric or fisheye camera models rather than differentiable rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify panoramic sensing with online 3DGS-based dense SLAM, addressing a clear gap where prior GS-SLAM systems ignore 360° inputs and prior panoramic SLAM systems lack differentiable dense mapping. The SynPano dataset with parameterized complex trajectories and multi-modal ground truth fills a benchmarking void for evaluating panoramic SLAM under challenging motions. Open questions include whether the four-face cubemap is optimal (more faces or adaptive FoV), how the system scales to very large outdoor scenes, and whether the reliance on a pre-trained depth model (Depth Anywhere) for Gaussian initialization limits robustness in textureless or highly dynamic environments.
