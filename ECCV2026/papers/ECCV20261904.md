## Problem

Scaling neural reconstruction from single rooms or city blocks to entire cities remains an open challenge, primarily because no real-world dataset provides continuous, multi-city, surround-view coverage at the scale needed for photorealistic urban digital twins. Existing benchmarks are either synthetic (suffering sim-to-real gaps), short perception clips lacking spatial continuity, or limited to single-city routes, leaving a critical gap for studying city-scale spatial intelligence.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 6 surround-view RGB cameras (3 narrow at 1440×928, 3 wide at 1240×728), a roof-mounted LiDAR (80k points/frame, downsampled to 15k), GPS, and IMU at 10/10/10/100 Hz, along with automatically generated semantic masks for ground, sky, and dynamic objects. Keyframes are spatially subsampled at 0.5 m intervals and organized into 5 km chunks with sub-trajectories at six lengths (50 m–5 km) for multi-scale evaluation. The output is a 3DGS scene of 6M–60M Gaussians (scaling with trajectory length), rendered novel views for both in-trajectory and extrapolated poses, and a closed-loop simulation environment where a VLA agent can perceive, reason, and act.

## Method

The reconstruction baseline extends 3DGS with four urban-specific components: (1) rig-aware pose optimization that jointly refines ego poses and camera extrinsics under a shared SE(3) constraint rather than optimizing each camera independently, preventing locally improved but globally inconsistent solutions; (2) a view-dependent MLP sky model composited as an infinite background to decouple sky appearance from scene geometry; (3) ground regularization that penalizes vertical height variation within depth slices, encourages vertical alignment of ground Gaussians, and enforces sufficient opacity to stabilize underconstrained road surfaces; and (4) a progressive render-repair-augment loop using Difix3D+ that iteratively renders extrapolated views, repairs artifacts with a diffusion model, and adds the repaired supervision back into training. Multi-GPU training shards Gaussian parameters across devices to scale to billion-level primitives with bounded per-GPU memory.

## Results

On the short Ann Arbor-0.5k trajectory (0.25 km), the proposed method achieves 29.99 dB PSNR, 0.917 SSIM, 0.240 LPIPS, and 15.16 m Depth-L1, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the longer Atlanta-5k trajectory (2.5 km), it reaches 23.14 dB PSNR, 0.799 SSIM, and 6.62 m Depth-L1, with the largest margin over baselines in geometric accuracy (D-L1 reduced from 8.35 m for CityGS to 6.62 m). The advantage grows with scale: at 2.5 km, the method's PSNR lead over the next-best baseline (H-3DGS, 21.40 dB) widens to 1.74 dB, while its D-L1 lead over CityGS (8.35 m) reaches 1.73 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: classic 3DGS, hierarchical H-3DGS, blockwise CityGaussianV2 (CityGS), and feed-forward VGGT-Long (both standalone and as a pose/point-cloud initializer for CityGS). On the short 0.25 km trajectory, the proposed method dominates all baselines on every metric, with the closest competitor H-3DGS trailing by 2.4 dB in PSNR and 3.3 m in D-L1. On the 2.5 km trajectory, the method wins on PSNR, SSIM, and D-L1, but H-3DGS retains a narrow edge on LPIPS (0.380 vs. 0.477), suggesting H-3DGS preserves slightly better perceptual texture at the cost of geometric accuracy. CityGS is the strongest geometric baseline (8.35 m D-L1 at 2.5 km) but still lags the proposed method by 1.73 m, and its PSNR (21.27 dB) is 1.87 dB below. VGGT-Long alone produces only point clouds (no 2D metrics), and when its drifting outputs replace SLAM poses in CityGS, reconstruction collapses to 13.40 dB PSNR—far below even vanilla 3DGS—demonstrating that current feed-forward geometry priors cannot substitute for globally consistent pose optimization at city scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction framework with urban-specific components—rig-aware pose optimization, a decoupled sky model, ground regularization, and Difix3D+-based extrapolated view repair—building directly on the 3DGS formulation and multi-GPU sharding strategies from GrendelGS/GSplat. It positions itself against a spectrum of prior work: NeRF-based scalability methods (Block-NeRF, MegaNeRF), 3DGS-based large-scene approaches (CityGaussian, H-3DGS, VastGaussian), and feed-forward geometry priors (DUSt3R, VGGT-Long), while distinguishing its dataset from synthetic city benchmarks (MatrixCity, SS3DM), clip-level driving datasets (nuScenes, Waymo, Argoverse 2), and route/block-level reconstruction datasets (KITTI-360, Oxford Spires, H-3DGS). The key departure is the combination of real-world, multi-city, continuous, surround-view, city-scale coverage in a single benchmark, which no prior dataset provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WildCity fills a critical data gap for city-scale spatial intelligence: no prior real-world dataset offers continuous multi-city, surround-view, street-level coverage at this scale (1,500+ km, 6 cities), making it a likely reference benchmark for reconstruction, simulation, and embodied reasoning research. The systematic analysis reveals that 2D image fidelity metrics can mask severe geometric errors (depth L1 > 17 m in baselines), and that feed-forward geometry priors (VGGT-Long) cannot yet substitute for globally consistent pose optimization at city scale—a finding that tempers enthusiasm for end-to-end learned reconstruction. Open questions include how to achieve stable extrapolation without costly diffusion-based post-repair, how to reduce long-horizon pose drift (especially vertical), and whether the reconstruction pipeline can scale to full-city coverage without quality collapse.
