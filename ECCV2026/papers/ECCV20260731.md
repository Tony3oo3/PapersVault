## Problem

Existing Video-MLLMs capture coarse hand-level action intent but systematically fail to represent fine-grained finger articulation, inter-finger coordination, and contact semantics. No large-scale paired video-caption dataset or dedicated evaluation protocol exists for finger-level motion description, and the sparse RGB sampling strategies used by current Video-MLLMs inherently discard the high-frequency temporal cues that distinguish individual finger movements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260731/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes FiGOP units, each consisting of one sparsely sampled RGB keyframe (2 fps, 512×512) paired with a dense sequence of 8 subsequent 2D hand keypoint frames (extracted via DWPose, providing joint coordinates and confidence scores). The output is a natural-language caption describing which specific fingers move, their trajectories, coordination order, and physical contact with objects or other fingers, evaluated along four dimensions: finger/hand identification, motion/trajectory accuracy, contact/interaction fidelity, and motion-sequence completeness.

## Method

FiGOP (Finger Group-of-Pictures) augments each sparse RGB keyframe with a temporally adjacent 2D hand-pose sequence, processed by a dual-stream encoder: a frozen ViT vision tower for the RGB frame and a two-layer ST-GCN followed by a temporal Transformer for the pose sequence, which models finger joint topology and local motion. A cross-attention motion-aware projector fuses pose motion features into visual tokens (F_fused = CA(F_v, F_p) + F_v), allowing visual tokens to selectively retrieve high-frequency motion cues without increasing RGB sampling density. The full system is built on Qwen3-VL-8B and trained via two-stage supervised fine-tuning on FingerCap-40K: Stage 1 trains only the pose encoder and projector (vision encoder and LLM frozen), then Stage 2 unfreezes the LLM for joint optimization with next-token-prediction loss. The approach is validated against naive alternatives—dense RGB sampling causes OOM at 8 fps, low-resolution dense sampling degrades finger detail, and pose-only input fails on HOI—confirming that structured pose augmentation at 8 fps between 2 fps RGB frames is the optimal spatial-temporal trade-off.

## Results

On the FingerCap-40K test set, the FiGOP-augmented Qwen3-VL-8B (FiGOP+SFT) achieves an overall HandJudge score of 2.74, surpassing the strongest closed-source baseline Gemini-2.5-Pro (2.64) by 0.10 points. In standard captioning metrics, FiGOP+SFT reaches CIDEr 155.27 and METEOR 38.89 (average across gesture and HOI), substantially outperforming all zero-shot models. The zero-shot Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct baseline scores only 2.09 on HandJudge, and even the standard fine-tuned variant (MM Proj+SFT) reaches just 2.13, highlighting the difficulty of finger-level reasoning without explicit pose augmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiGOP+SFT outperforms Gemini-2.5-Pro on three of four HandJudge dimensions (FHI 2.96 vs 2.73, MT 2.50 vs 2.28, CI 2.45 vs 2.17) and on overall score (2.74 vs 2.64), though it trails on CMS (3.06 vs 3.36). Against GPT-4o (overall 2.27) and GPT-4o-mini (1.86), FiGOP+SFT leads by 0.47 and 0.88 points respectively across all dimensions. Among open-source zero-shot models, the best is Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (2.09), which FiGOP+SFT surpasses by 0.65 points; the gap widens to 1.35 over LLaVA-NeXT-V-7B (1.39). Compared to the standard fine-tuned variant (MM Proj+SFT, 2.13), FiGOP adds 0.61 points overall, with the largest gains in FHI (+0.54) and MT (+0.66), confirming that structured pose cues specifically improve finger identification and trajectory reasoning. On out-of-distribution subsets (BSL, HOI4D, MotionBench), FiGOP also leads (HandJudge 2.04 vs 1.61 for MM Proj and 1.56 for zero-shot), though absolute scores drop significantly, indicating that generalization to unseen finger configurations remains an open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of sign language recognition, hand-object interaction (HOI) datasets, and Video-MLLM motion captioning, arguing that all three lines of work operate at hand-level granularity and neglect individual finger articulation. The FiGOP module repurposes the Group-of-Pictures principle from video coding and the well-established ST-GCN architecture for skeleton-based action recognition, but applies them in a novel way: binding dense 2D hand keypoints to sparse RGB keyframes within a Video-MLLM pipeline rather than using them as a standalone recognition input. The HandJudge evaluation framework extends the recent LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm with task-specific rubrics (finger identification, trajectory, contact, completeness) that go beyond the lexical-overlap metrics standard in captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that even Gemini-2.5-Pro scores only 2.64/5 on finger-level correctness while achieving strong standard captioning metrics—exposes a fundamental gap between fluency and physical accuracy in current Video-MLLMs, suggesting that scaling alone may not close it. The 40K paired dataset and HandJudge benchmark provide the community with the first dedicated resource for studying fine-grained finger motion, which is critical for dexterous manipulation, sign language accessibility, and embodied AI. Open questions remain: OOD generalization drops sharply (HandJudge 2.04 vs. 2.74 in-distribution), indicating that models have not learned transferable finger-level reasoning; and whether richer 3D pose or longer temporal contexts could further close the gap to human-level description.
