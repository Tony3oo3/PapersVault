## Problem

Local feature matching struggles with semantic-geometric ambiguity, where geometrically consistent structures correspond to different semantic entities in repetitive or low-texture scenes. Existing semantic-enhanced methods either provide only coarse/indirect semantic guidance or rely on heavyweight foundation-model backbones at inference, making it difficult to jointly achieve disambiguation and real-time efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262226/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a single RGB image (resized to 800×600 for training; up to 1200 px max side for MegaDepth evaluation) and outputs a dense keypoint heatmap and per-pixel 64-dimensional floating-point descriptors at input resolution. During training, two auxiliary heads predict per-pixel surface normals (supervised by Depth Anything v2 pseudo-normals) and a 64-d semantic map (supervised by a frozen DINOv3 teacher); both auxiliary branches are discarded at inference, leaving only the shared CNN backbone, descriptor head, and RGFusion module.

## Method

SemLight builds on LiftFeat's 5-block CNN encoder (depths {4,8,16,32,64}) and adds two key components. First, DistilSem performs a two-stage knowledge distillation: an offline projection head compresses DINOv3 ViT-S token features into 64-d teacher semantic maps, and an online lightweight semantic head on the shared backbone is trained to match the teacher via cosine distance, eliminating any external encoder at inference. Second, RGFusion integrates appearance, surface-normal, and semantic embeddings through (i) input-adaptive sigmoid gates that reweight each modality channel-wise based on local reliability, and (ii) a 3-token cross-modal attention that lets the descriptor token selectively absorb discriminative components from geometry or semantics, with a residual connection back to the appearance descriptor. The full system is trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss combining keypoint NLL, descriptor matching NLL, normal cosine regression, and semantic cosine distillation, achieving 5.4 ms GPU inference with a 64-d descriptor.

## Results

On MegaDepth-1500, SemLight achieves the best relative pose AUC at all thresholds: 50.2 (AUC@5°), 62.5 (AUC@10°), and 72.7 (AUC@20°), surpassing the strongest baseline LiftFeat (44.7/59.5/70.3) by 5.5, 3.0, and 2.4 points respectively. On ScanNet-1500, it ranks second at AUC@5° (19.2) and AUC@10° (35.8) behind Cadar et al. (trained on ScanNet), while achieving the best AUC@20° (52.5). On HPatches, SemLight attains the best MHA@3 under viewpoint change (72.1%) and illumination change (95.8%). On Aachen Day-Night, it achieves the highest daytime localization accuracy at all thresholds (e.g., 94.8% at 0.5 m/5°), though it trails LiftFeat slightly on nighttime queries (88.5% vs. 89.9% at 0.5 m/5°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemLight outperforms all baselines on MegaDepth-1500 at every threshold, with the largest gap over XFeat (50.2 vs. 42.6 AUC@5°, a 17.8% relative gain) and a 5.5-point margin over LiftFeat (44.7), the closest geometry-aware competitor. Against SFD2 (44.9 AUC@5°), which also uses semantic distillation, SemLight gains 5.3 points while using half the descriptor dimensionality (64-d vs. 128-d). On ScanNet-1500, Cadar et al. (trained directly on ScanNet with 256-d descriptors) edges out SemLight at AUC@5° (20.3 vs. 19.2) and AUC@10° (36.8 vs. 35.8), but SemLight matches or exceeds it at AUC@20° (52.5 vs. 52.4) and substantially outperforms it on MegaDepth (50.2 vs. 35.7). On HPatches, SemLight leads all methods at the tightest MHA@3 threshold under both illumination (95.8%) and viewpoint (72.1%) splits, beating LiftFeat by 0.2 and 1.0 points respectively. On Aachen Day-Night, SemLight achieves the best daytime localization (94.8% at 0.5 m/5°, +1.7% over LiftFeat) but trails LiftFeat by 1.4 points on nighttime queries, which the authors attribute to a domain gap in the daytime-trained semantic distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemLight builds directly on LiftFeat's 5-block CNN architecture with 64-d descriptors and a surface-normal geometry head, adding two new components: DistilSem, which compresses DINOv3 token features into a 64-d matching-oriented embedding via a frozen projection head and distills it into the shared backbone, and RGFusion, a reliability-gated channel fusion plus 3-token cross-modal attention that adaptively re-weights appearance, geometry, and semantics per keypoint. Conceptually, it departs from prior semantic-enhanced matchers (SFD2, Cadar et al., MATCHA, DeDoDe) that either run heavy foundation models at inference or rely on multi-stage region-level pipelines, by performing all semantic integration within a single forward pass of the compact matching network. The design also extends the geometry-aware matching line (LiftFeat) by treating surface normals as one of three equally modulated modalities rather than the sole geometric cue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in real-time visual localization: resolving semantic-geometric ambiguity in repetitive or low-texture scenes without the inference cost of large semantic backbones. The 17.8% relative AUC@5 gain over XFeat at comparable runtime (5.4 ms GPU) makes the approach immediately relevant for embedded and mobile deployment. However, the authors acknowledge that the distilled semantic priors, trained predominantly on daytime MegaDepth imagery, degrade under extreme nighttime conditions, leaving domain-adaptive distillation and multi-modal extension as clear open questions. Whether the reliability-gated fusion generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks (outdoor MegaDepth, indoor ScanNet) to more diverse or dynamic scenes remains to be validated.
