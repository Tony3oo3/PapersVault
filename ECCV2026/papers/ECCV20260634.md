## Problem

Feed-forward 3R models (DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT) enable fast, calibration-free 3D reconstruction but operate only in single-agent settings and accumulate trajectory drift over long sequences. Meanwhile, existing multi-agent SLAM systems rely on NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting, which require per-scene optimization and cannot meet real-time constraints. No multi-agent feed-forward 3D reconstruction framework existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The system consumes monocular RGB video streams from multiple agents with no depth, no known camera intrinsics, and no pre-calibration. Each agent's sequence is processed in sliding windows of m=10 consecutive frames (stride 5), and the system outputs a single globally consistent dense 3D point map in a unified coordinate system along with per-frame camera poses (quaternion, translation, field of view). The entire pipeline runs at approximately 10 FPS on a single GPU.

## Method

MAGiSt3R uses VGGT as a feed-forward backbone to predict local point maps, confidence scores, and camera parameters per submap, then introduces MAGMA (Multi-Agent Global Map Aggregation) to merge submaps at both intra-agent and inter-agent levels. MAGMA encodes point maps into geometry tokens via a 2D conv layer, augments them with appearance tokens derived from DINOv2 visual tokens and SALAD spatial tokens through an attention module (to handle repetitive/textureless structures), and concatenates camera tokens before passing through decoders with self-attention and multi-view cross-attention layers. DPT heads regress aligned point maps and confidence, while a pose head with asymmetric attention masks predicts registered camera parameters in the global frame. Training is supervised end-to-end with a confidence-aware registration loss (following DUSt3R), a camera pose loss, and a geometry consistency loss enforcing reprojection agreement across views; a final pose graph optimization step using Levenberg-Marquardt corrects residual drift.

## Results

On ReplicaMultiagent (multi-agent tracking), MAGiSt3R achieves an average ATE RMSE of 3.87 cm, outperforming the best feed-forward baseline VGGT-SLAM+RICP (7.15 cm) by 46% and the concurrent MA-MASt3R-SLAM (4.43 cm) by 13%. On AriaMultiagent (real-world, 3 agents), MAGiSt3R achieves 3.52 cm average ATE RMSE versus 19.37 cm for MA-MASt3R-SLAM and 9.03 cm for VGGT-SLAM+RICP. For 3D reconstruction on ReplicaMultiagent (multi-agent), MAGiSt3R attains 5.37 cm accuracy and 3.36 cm completeness on average, roughly halving the error of the next-best baseline (VGGT-SLAM+RICP: 11.36/8.44). The system operates at ~9 FPS, comparable to MA-MASt3R-SLAM (11 FPS) and far faster than RGB-D multi-agent SLAM systems (<1–2 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGiSt3R outperforms all five feed-forward RGB baselines in the multi-agent setting. Against DUSt3R+RICP (11.58 cm avg ATE) and SLAM3R+RICP (18.94 cm), the improvement is dramatic (3–5×), reflecting the fragility of RANSAC+ICP alignment when inter-agent overlap is limited. MASt3R+RICP (5.99 cm) and MASt3R-SLAM+RICP (8.17 cm) are also clearly beaten, as is VGGT-SLAM+RICP (7.15 cm), the strongest single-agent backbone adapted to multi-agent via RICP. Against the concurrent multi-agent feed-forward system MA-MASt3R-SLAM [54], MAGiSt3R wins on both benchmarks (3.87 vs 4.43 cm on ReplicaMultiagent; 3.52 vs 19.37 cm on AriaMultiagent), with the gap being especially large on real-world data. The three RGB-D multi-agent systems (Swarm-SLAM, CP-SLAM, MAGiC-SLAM) achieve lower ATE (0.25–3.60 cm) but require calibrated depth input and run at <1–2 FPS, making them inapplicable to the uncalibrated RGB-only setting that MAGiSt3R targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: feed-forward 3R models (DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT) that enable fast, calibration-free single-agent reconstruction, and multi-agent SLAM systems (MAGiC-SLAM, CP-SLAM, MNE-SLAM) that achieve collaborative mapping but rely on RGB-D input and per-scene neural optimization. MAGiSt3R departs from both by being the first to lift feed-forward 3R models into a multi-agent setting, replacing classical inter-agent alignment (RANSAC+ICP, SL(4) optimization) with a learned merging module (MAGMA) that handles both intra-agent submap fusion and inter-agent loop closure in a unified architecture. The pose graph optimization backend and DINOv2-SALAD descriptors for loop detection are borrowed from existing SLAM practice, but their integration with a feed-forward backbone is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that multi-agent 3D reconstruction can be performed at ~10 FPS from uncalibrated monocular RGB without per-scene optimization, this work opens a practical path toward real-time collaborative mapping that was previously locked behind RGB-D sensors and iterative NeRF/3DGS pipelines. The MAGMA module is a reusable component that could be plugged into other 3R-based systems, and the large performance gap over MA-MASt3R-SLAM on AriaMultiagent (3.52 vs 19.37 ATE) suggests learned merging is substantially more effective than graph-optimization-only approaches for inter-agent alignment. Key open questions include scalability beyond 3 agents, robustness to drastically different viewing conditions (day/night, adverse weather), and whether the feed-forward paradigm can match the tracking accuracy of RGB-D systems like MAGiC-SLAM (0.25 ATE) in more challenging environments.
