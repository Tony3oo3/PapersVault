## Problem

Existing VLM-based face forgery detectors either rely solely on the visual modality or tune visual and linguistic modalities independently, leaving cross-modal interaction largely unexplored. The paper reveals that a visual-linguistic asymmetry inherent in VLMs causes cross-modal misalignment in the shared latent space, undermining both discriminability on visually similar real-forged faces and generalizability to unseen forgeries. Notably, generic symmetric multi-modal interaction methods (e.g., CoCoOp, MaPLe, MMA) actually underperform vision-only detectors, confirming that naive cross-modal alignment exacerbates rather than resolves this asymmetry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image as input, which is patchified by CLIP's vision encoder into fixed-size patches, and two category text prompts ('real' and 'fake') processed by the text encoder. The output is a binary real/fake classification with a probability score computed via temperature-scaled cosine similarity between the projected visual feature and the two text embeddings in the shared latent space. The entire pre-trained CLIP backbone remains frozen; only 4.41M parameters in the proposed MFA and AGI modules are trained, making the approach parameter-efficient for the small-scale FFD datasets.

## Method

Asy-Det is built on a frozen CLIP and introduces two modules to address the twofold visual-linguistic asymmetry. The Meta Forgery Adapter (MFA) is instantiated in both vision and text encoders across two phases: the first phase uses a lightweight forgery adaptation branch (grouped FC + ReLU with a learnable scale) to excite forgery-specific cues from shallow spaces for discrimination, while the second phase activates forgery-general representations in deep spaces for generalization; a parallel meta generation branch (Conv + asynchronous register) constructs multi-granularity shared spaces at intermediate depths. The Asymmetry-Guided Interactor (AGI) then performs explicit bidirectional cross-attention between the visual and linguistic tokens in these shared spaces, aligning them through softmax-weighted feature transfer and projecting the aligned features back into modality-specific spaces via residual connections with learnable weight matrices. This asymmetric, explicit interaction paradigm replaces the symmetric implicit frameworks used by prior work, allowing the two modalities to compensate for each other's discrimination-generalization trade-offs and fully unlock VLM's cross-modal potential for FFD.

## Results

Asy-Det achieves 93.85% image-level AUC on DFD, 92.94% on Celeb-DF-v2, and 83.60% on DFDC-L (trained on FF++-C23), yielding a 90.1% average across the three cross-dataset benchmarks. This represents a 5.50% average AUC improvement over the strongest prior method UDD (84.6% avg) and a 6.30% average gain over the best generic multi-modal interaction baseline MMA (83.8% avg). In cross-manipulation settings, Asy-Det reaches 94.02% average AUC, surpassing SSG by 1.80%. The method uses only 4.41M trainable parameters while keeping the entire CLIP backbone frozen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Asy-Det outperforms all 22 baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest VLM-based spatial detector UDD [8], Asy-Det gains +2.80% AUC on DFD, +6.00% on CDF-v2, and +6.20% on DFDC-L. Frequency-based methods (F3-Net, SPSL, FreqDebias) trail by 5–14% AUC on CDF-v2, confirming their brittleness to diverse forgery types. Among generic multi-modal interaction methods (Table 3), MMA [44] is the strongest at 83.8% average, yet Asy-Det still leads by 6.30% average, validating that symmetric interaction paradigms fail to resolve visual-linguistic asymmetry. In cross-manipulation evaluation (Table 2), Asy-Det achieves 94.02% average AUC versus SSG's 92.22%, with the largest margin (+7.35%) on the FSh→FSh transfer. Robustness testing (Fig. 4) shows Asy-Det retains ~97% AUC under severe Gaussian noise where NPR [35] drops below 65%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP as the base VLM and departs from two established lines of FFD work: (1) vision-only detectors that exploit frequency or spatial artifacts, and (2) VLM-based detectors (e.g., UDD, SSG, Forensics Adapter) that adapt CLIP via prompt learning or adapters while treating visual and linguistic branches independently. The key conceptual departure is the identification of visual-linguistic asymmetry—where the two modalities' latent spaces affect generalizability in opposite directions and where shallow vs. deep layers within a modality trade off discrimination against generalization—as the fundamental bottleneck that symmetric cross-modal interaction methods (CoCoOp, MaPLe, MMA, TCP, MMRL) fail to address. The proposed Asy-Det introduces an asymmetric, explicit interaction mechanism (AGI) and a two-phase training paradigm (MFA) specifically designed to reconcile these disparities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic characterization of visual-linguistic asymmetry provides a diagnostic framework that explains why prior VLM-based FFD methods hit a discrimination-generalization ceiling, and the finding that generic symmetric interaction methods actually underperform vision-only baselines is a useful cautionary result for the broader VLM-adaptation community. The 4.41M trainable parameter budget makes the approach practical for deployment in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the asymmetry patterns generalize to other VLM architectures (e.g., LLaVA, SigLIP), whether the two-phase schedule is optimal or could be replaced by a continuous curriculum, and whether the framework extends naturally to video-level forgery detection where temporal cues add another modality.
