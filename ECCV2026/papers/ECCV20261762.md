## Problem

Existing reasoning-based segmentation benchmarks for VLMs are built exclusively from high-quality images under idealized conditions, leaving it unknown whether VLMs can sustain reliable pixel-level reasoning when visual cues are degraded by adverse weather such as rain, snow, or fog. This gap is critical for safety-critical outdoor deployment, where weather-induced degradation systematically disrupts visual-language alignment and propagates errors through multi-stage reasoning pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261762/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a weather-degraded RGB image (synthetic degradations generated from clean images plus depth maps, or real-world driving scenes from ACDC) with a natural language reasoning query targeting a specific object. Queries are generated via mask-guided LLM prompting and span five structured reasoning dimensions—Function, Application, Structure, Relationship, and Requirement—rather than simple category labels. The model outputs a segmentation mask and bounding box, evaluated with gIoU (per-image average IoU) and cIoU (cumulative intersection over cumulative union).

## Method

WeatherReasonSeg comprises two complementary datasets: (1) a controllable synthetic component built on ReasonSeg, where physics-based weather generators (modeling rain streak accumulation, snowflake scattering, and atmospheric light scattering) produce graded degradations at three severity levels (light, moderate, heavy) across three weather types, enabling fine-grained robustness analysis; and (2) a real-world component built on ACDC driving scenes, where mask-guided LLM prompting generates semantically consistent queries across five reasoning dimensions, followed by human-model collaborative filtering to ensure single-target consistency and visual identifiability. The benchmark totals 44,721 image-query pairs (8,811 synthetic + 35,910 real-world). Evaluation benchmarks six VLM frameworks—Grounded-SAM, PixelLM, GLaMM, LISA-7B, Seg-R1, and Seg-Zero-7B—plus SAM2 as a perception-only upper bound, revealing monotonic performance degradation with weather severity and distinct vulnerability patterns across weather types and reasoning dimensions.

## Results

On the real-world WeatherReasonSeg benchmark, the perception-only upper bound SAM2 achieves gIoU of 82.2 (fog), 80.1 (rain), 81.5 (snow), and 74.8 (night), while the best reasoning-based method Seg-Zero-7B reaches only 40.6, 35.1, 36.8, and 28.9 gIoU respectively—approximately half of SAM2's performance. On the synthetic benchmark, Seg-Zero-7B* (reasoning-only, no SAM) drops from 65.3 gIoU in clean conditions to 47.7 under heavy weather, a 17.6-point absolute decline. Across all methods, performance degrades monotonically with increasing weather severity, and snow induces the most severe degradation followed by fog and then rain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All seven evaluated methods are far below the SAM2 perception upper bound on the real-world benchmark, with Seg-Zero-7B (best reasoning method) achieving roughly 50% of SAM2's gIoU across all weather types, confirming that the reasoning stage—not low-level feature extraction—is the primary bottleneck. Among reasoning-based methods, Seg-Zero-7B consistently outperforms Seg-R1 by 9–10 gIoU points (e.g., 40.6 vs. 30.9 in fog), which in turn beats LISA-7B by 12–14 points. Grounding-based methods (Grounded-SAM, GLaMM) and PixelLM perform worst, with PixelLM scoring below 3 gIoU in all conditions and Grounded-SAM's cIoU falling below 15% at night. On the synthetic benchmark, the severity gradient is consistent: every method degrades monotonically from clean to heavy weather, with snow causing the largest drops (e.g., Seg-Zero-7B cIoU drops from 52.0 to 39.7 in snow vs. 52.0 to 44.0 in fog). The paper's key comparative insight is that no reasoning-based method closes the gap to SAM2 under any weather condition, and context-dependent query dimensions (Application, Requirement) suffer larger degradation than visually grounded ones (Structure, Function).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reasoning-based segmentation line (ReasonSeg/LISA, CVPR 2024) by introducing adverse weather as a controlled evaluation axis, and extends the adverse-weather perception line (ACDC, ICCV 2021) by adding multi-dimensional reasoning queries via mask-guided LLM prompting. It departs from prior weather-robustness datasets, which focus on pixel-level perception without language-grounded reasoning, and from prior reasoning segmentation benchmarks, which assume clean imagery. The mask-guided prompting strategy for generating queries across five semantic dimensions (Function, Application, Structure, Relationship, Requirement) is a methodological contribution that goes beyond the simple attribute-based queries in ReasonSeg.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that the reasoning stage, not the segmentation decoder, is the primary bottleneck under adverse weather: reasoning-based methods achieve roughly half the gIoU of SAM2 (the perception upper bound) in real-world degraded scenes. This reframes where robustness efforts should focus—on weather-aware semantic grounding rather than on segmentation architecture. Open questions include whether degradation-aware supervision (e.g., synthetic-to-real fine-tuning) can close the gap, and whether the urban-driving bias inherited from ACDC limits generalizability to non-driving scenes. The five-dimension query taxonomy also opens a path toward more fine-grained diagnosis of which reasoning capabilities are most fragile under environmental stress.
