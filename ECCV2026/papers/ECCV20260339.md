## Problem

End-to-end imitation learning for visual navigation supervises the vision encoder only indirectly through a single action loss, causing it to learn ambiguous, action-agnostic representations that produce inconsistent decisions at visually ambiguous points like intersections. Existing control-oriented pretraining methods (e.g., Simplex ETF neural collapse) assume uniform inter-class separation, overlooking the fact that goal-relative steering directions form a natural ordinal structure where neighboring classes share similar visual contexts while opposing classes differ substantially.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a current RGB observation and a goal image (sampled 3–20 steps ahead along the same trajectory) as input, and outputs a continuous action trajectory of 8 waypoints generated by a 1D conditional U-Net diffusion decoder with 10 denoising steps. For pretraining, the continuous action space is discretized into K=5 uniform angular bins over the front field of view (−90° to 90°) plus a separate out-of-view class, yielding six goal-relative direction classes that carry the ordinal supervision signal.

## Method

ORION pretrains an EfficientNet-B0 encoder with three jointly optimized objectives: (1) CDNV loss for intra-class compactness relative to inter-class separation, (2) a supervised ordinal projection that aligns class means along a learned 1D axis at equally spaced target positions matching their ordinal rank, and (3) an orthogonal compactness term that suppresses within-class variance in directions perpendicular to that axis. The ordinal axis is computed as the normalized projection of centered class means onto the target ordering vector, smoothed via EMA across batches, and class statistics are maintained with Welford's online algorithm for stability. This pretraining is applied in a two-stage procedure: Stage 1 trains only the encoder with the ORION loss for 30 epochs, then Stage 2 plugs the pretrained encoder into the full NoMaD pipeline (Transformer context module + diffusion decoder) and fine-tunes all parameters end-to-end with the standard diffusion policy loss. The key design choice is that the 1D ordinal subspace geometry—rather than the multi-dimensional Simplex ETF used for categorical control classes—matches the inherent structure of goal-relative steering directions, and the two-stage separation prevents destructive gradient interference between the geometric and diffusion objectives.

## Results

On simulated Indoor Intersection, ORION achieves 91% success rate versus 17% for NoMaD and 12% for NC-ETF, a +74 pp gain over the primary baseline. On Outdoor Multi-Intersection (≈200 m, four consecutive turns), ORION reaches 74% SR and 86.39% progress, outperforming NoMaD (48% SR) by +26 pp and reducing median heading jerk by 71% (34.49 → 10.13). In real-world deployment, ORION improves success rate over NoMaD by +20 pp (Indoor), +10 pp (Campus), and +16.7 pp (Field), while also achieving lower heading jerk in all three environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORION outperforms all three baselines on every route and environment tested. Against NoMaD (the primary diffusion-policy baseline), the gap is modest on straightforward routes (e.g., 94 vs. 88% SR on Citysim Basic) but becomes dramatic at intersections: +74 pp on Indoor Intersection (91% vs. 17%) and +26 pp on Outdoor Multi-Intersection (74% vs. 48%), with heading jerk reduced by 68–71%. Against NC-ETF, which enforces Simplex ETF geometry during pretraining, ORION wins on all metrics, confirming that the ordinal 1D structure is a better geometric prior than uniform simplex separation for goal-relative navigation classes. Against ViNT (regression-based, no diffusion decoder), ORION dominates on success rate and progress in every setting; ViNT's lower heading jerk is an artifact of its averaging behavior rather than genuine stability, as it achieves the lowest success rates. In real-world deployment, ORION consistently leads NoMaD by 10–20 pp in success rate across Indoor, Campus, and Field environments, with the largest relative gain in the visually repetitive Indoor corridors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural-collapse-for-control paradigm introduced by Qi et al. (ICLR 2025), who showed that Simplex ETF geometry improves downstream policy performance when encoders are pretrained on categorical action classes (REPOs). The key departure is recognizing that goal-relative navigation actions (Far Left → Far Right) possess a natural ordinal structure rather than uniform pairwise separation, and therefore the appropriate collapse geometry is a one-dimensional ordered axis (per Ma et al., NeurIPS 2025) rather than a simplex. Architecturally, the method plugs into the NoMaD diffusion-policy pipeline (Sridhar et al., ICRA 2024) and uses the CDNV loss from Galanti et al. (ICLR 2021) as one of three pretraining objectives, but the supervised ordinal projection and orthogonal compactness terms are novel contributions tailored to the ordered action space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the *type* of geometric structure imposed during encoder pretraining matters: a mismatched Simplex ETF or a naive cross-entropy direction classifier actively degrades navigation, while the task-matched ordinal collapse yields substantial gains (+26 pp SR at multi-intersections, 71% reduction in heading jerk). The complete failure of joint training (0% SR) highlights a practical constraint—ordinal structure must be established before the diffusion objective is introduced—which may generalize to other staged representation-learning pipelines. Open questions include whether the ordinal prior transfers to control tasks without a natural one-dimensional ordering (e.g., 2D velocity commands), whether the benefit persists with larger encoders or longer horizons, and whether the two-stage requirement can be relaxed with better gradient balancing.
