## Problem

Multimodal LLMs for chart understanding suffer from sycophancy: when users embed false premises in queries (e.g., 'Why is the red line increasing?' when it is declining), models fabricate explanations to comply rather than correcting the user. Existing mitigations face a severe safety-utility trade-off—suppressing sycophantic compliance triggers over-refusal of valid queries—so an effective solution must selectively correct only demonstrably false premises while leaving valid queries untouched.

## Input / Output

The framework is training-free and operates entirely at inference time, consuming a chart image (RGB) and a free-form user query that may contain one or more embedded factual premises. It produces a single text response routed through one of three paths: an explicit correction citing the falsified claim and contradicting visual evidence, a standard unmodified answer, or a cautious uncertainty-aware response. The multi-pass design (decomposition, verification, generation) introduces roughly 3.6× inference latency over a single forward pass.

## Method

Re-Check follows a Verify-then-Answer pipeline with three stages: (1) text-only atomic claim decomposition that deliberately excludes the image to preserve the user's original premises without visual contamination; (2) vision-grounded verification where each claim is classified as TRUE/FALSE/UNCERTAIN against the chart, followed by the Contrastive Visual Dependency Score (CVDS), which computes the KL divergence between the model's output distributions with and without the image to quantify how much the verification decision is genuinely driven by visual evidence; (3) adaptive three-way routing that triggers correction only when a FALSE judgment is confirmed by high CVDS (≥ τ), passes valid queries to standard generation, and routes uncertain cases to a cautious reasoning path. CVDS is the key differentiator: it provides an information-theoretic filter that downgrades textually-biased refutations (low CVDS) to UNCERTAIN, preventing the model from 'correcting' the user based on linguistic priors rather than observed visual contradiction. Unlike token-level contrastive decoding methods (e.g., VCD), Re-Check operates at the claim level and uses the model's own output logits under contrastive visual conditions for self-verification, requiring no external detectors or retraining.

## Results

On ChartHal, Re-Check improves overall accuracy by 15.01% over the base model with Qwen3-VL-8B (57.49% → 72.50%), with gains of +49.72% and +23.83% for InternVL3.5-8B and Qwen3-VL-32B respectively. Crucially, Normal-query accuracy is preserved within 0.84% of the base model (44.77% vs. 45.61%), avoiding the over-refusal problem. On the general benchmark ChartQAPro, Re-Check retains 97.2% of base-model performance (36.31% vs. 37.37% for Qwen3-VL-8B), and achieves an AUROC of 0.942 for false-premise detection, substantially outperforming the single-pass prompting baseline (0.835).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VCD [10] (token-level contrastive decoding), Re-Check achieves a decisive win on ChartHal across all three models: 72.50% vs. 58.29% (Qwen3-VL-8B, +14.21%), 63.47% vs. 15.91% (InternVL3.5-8B, +47.56%), and 77.78% vs. 54.33% (Qwen3-VL-32B, +23.45%). VCD provides only marginal improvement over the base model (e.g., +0.80% for Qwen3-VL-8B), confirming that token-level logit subtraction cannot address claim-level logical contradictions in chart sycophancy. Against the Integrated Prompting baseline (single-pass verification without stage isolation), Re-Check matches or slightly exceeds overall ChartHal accuracy (72.50% vs. 72.32% for Qwen3-VL-8B) while preserving Normal-query accuracy far better (44.77% vs. 34.31%, an 11.3-point gap), directly demonstrating the safety-utility advantage of stage-isolated verification. On ChartQAPro, Re-Check (36.31%) outperforms Integrated Prompting (33.49%) by 2.82 points, confirming that the multi-stage design avoids the indiscriminate over-refusal that plagues single-pass approaches on valid queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

Re-Check extends the text-only verification-chain paradigm (CoVe, Wu et al.) to the multimodal domain by replacing knowledge-based verification with vision-grounded, claim-level verification against chart images. It departs from token-level contrastive decoding methods (VCD, OPERA, M3ID) by operating at the claim level rather than the token level, arguing that chart sycophancy involves logical contradictions about existing visual elements rather than fabricated content. The proposed CVDS metric borrows the contrastive-visual-condition idea from VCD but repurposes it as a post-hoc reliability filter on verification judgments rather than a decoding-time intervention, and the three-way routing mechanism addresses the over-refusal problem that plagues single-pass verification approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, training-free recipe for mitigating sycophancy in MLLM-based chart analysis without the 3.6× latency being prohibitive for many deployment settings, and the flat CVDS threshold sensitivity (72.03–72.50% across τ=0–4.0) suggests the framework is robust to hyperparameter tuning. However, the CVDS-specific gain is modest (+0.47% overall), and the bulk of the improvement comes from the decomposition-and-routing architecture rather than the information-theoretic metric that is the paper's headline contribution. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to natural-image or document VLM tasks as the authors speculate, whether the 3.6× latency overhead is acceptable in interactive settings, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the ChartHal benchmark's specific adversarial query taxonomy.
