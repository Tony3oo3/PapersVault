## Problem

Existing training-free hallucination mitigation methods for LVLMs uniformly amplify visual attention across all tokens and layers, which can elevate irrelevant image regions and introduce spurious correlations that degrade fluency. The paper identifies that effective intervention should be selective along two axes—spatially targeting token-relevant regions and temporally restricting intervention to high-uncertainty tokens in deeper layers where visual grounding degrades—rather than applying a global boost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260770/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CAI operates as a plug-and-play inference-time module on frozen LVLM backbones (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL, Qwen3.5-4B), consuming an RGB image encoded into N_v visual patch tokens and a text prompt, and producing a standard autoregressive text response. The intervention modifies only the attention sub-matrix from the current decoding token to the visual patch tokens (A_{t, i_s:i_e}) in selected deeper layers, requiring no architectural changes, additional parameters, or retraining. Hyperparameters (start layer l_s, entropy threshold γ, contrastive coefficient λ) are set via grid search per backbone.

## Method

CAI enforces a 'see only when needed' principle through two-axis selectivity. For *where to look*, it computes token-image similarity w_t by taking the L2-normalized inner product between the 0th-layer hidden state of the current token and each visual patch feature, passed through a sigmoid to yield per-patch grounding weights in (0,1). For *when to intervene*, it applies a dual gate: the intervention activates only in layers deeper than l_s (where visual grounding empirically degrades) and only when the predictive entropy of the token (computed via the LM head on the layer-normalized hidden state) exceeds threshold γ. The intervention itself is a multiplicative attention tilt A ← A + |A| ⊙ w_t, which the paper proves is the KL-minimal reweighting that maximizes expected token-image similarity under a fixed KL budget (Theorem 1), and which provably reduces NLL for high-entropy tokens under a local alignment condition (Theorem 2). An optional contrastive decoding step (λ·p(y|v,x) − (λ−1)·p(y|x)) serves as an auxiliary language-prior suppressor, but the core gains come from the attention intervention alone.

## Results

On POPE (MS-COCO, LLaVA-1.5), CAI achieves 89.24% accuracy and 89.14% F1 under random sampling, 86.03% under popular, and 82.77% under adversarial settings, consistently outperforming all baselines. On CHAIR (LLaVA-1.5, max token=64), CAI reduces sentence-level hallucination from 26.0% (Regular) to 17.8% and instance-level from 8.8% to 6.9%. On MME, CAI scores 660.00 for LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 588.33 Regular), 510.00 for InstructBLIP (vs. 453.33), and 675.00 for Qwen-VL (vs. 638.33), demonstrating consistent gains across all three backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAI outperforms all seven baselines across POPE, CHAIR, and MME on all three LVLM backbones. Against the closest competitor ONLY [41], CAI gains +0.86 Acc on MS-COCO Random and +2.84 Acc on Adversarial (LLaVA-1.5), while also surpassing it on MME (660 vs. 628.33). Compared to PAI [28], CAI's advantage is most pronounced under adversarial sampling (+5.94 Acc on MS-COCO), and critically, PAI's attention-only variant (PAI†) actually degrades performance below Regular, whereas CAI's attention-only variant (CAI†) still improves over Regular by +2.32 Acc. Against VCD [18], CAI leads by +3.50 Acc on MS-COCO Adversarial and reduces CHAIR_S by 9.2 points. In the extended comparison (Table 5), CAI achieves the highest average POPE accuracy (86.68) while running 33.7× faster than LVLMs-Saliency [48] (131.33 vs. 4294.36 ms/token) and outperforming DoLa [9] (85.77), SID [15] (85.22), and FarSight [38] (84.67) in average accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAI builds directly on the training-free hallucination mitigation line (PAI, VCD, ONLY) but departs from their shared design of uniformly amplifying visual attention across all tokens and layers. It draws on Neo et al.'s observation that visual information is localized near object tokens to introduce token-specific spatial selectivity, and on the depth-dependent degradation of visual grounding to introduce entropy- and depth-gated intervention. The theoretical framing as a KL-minimal attention reweighting with bounded interference is a new analytical lens not present in prior training-free methods, which typically lack formal guarantees about when intervention helps versus harms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAI offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism that consistently reduces hallucinations across three LVLM backbones and multiple benchmarks with minimal latency overhead (1.03× for the attention-only variant), making it immediately deployable without retraining. The acknowledged limitation of 'confident hallucinations'—where the model is wrong but low-entropy, evading the gating trigger—remains an open problem that entropy-based methods cannot resolve. The adaptive hyperparameter rules (depth-proportional start layer, running-statistics entropy threshold) partially address the grid-search burden, but whether these transfer robustly to substantially different architectures or longer generation contexts is not yet established.
