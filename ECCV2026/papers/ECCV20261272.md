## Problem

Generating a single 3D mesh that reveals entirely different semantics from different viewing angles (a 3D visual illusion) remains unsolved at practical speed and quality. Existing SDS-based optimization methods require ~40 minutes per shape and suffer from severe color oversaturation, while naive stitching of two independently generated objects produces visible geometric seams and semantic leakage that break the perceptual illusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two free-form text prompts (e.g., 'a peacock' and 'a pineapple') and two target camera angles as input, and produces a single fully textured 3D mesh as output. The mesh is designed so that it appears as an abstract, unrecognizable geometry from arbitrary viewpoints but clearly reveals the first prompt's semantics at the first target angle and the second prompt's semantics at the second target angle. The framework also scales to three prompts at 0°, 120°, and 240° without architectural changes, and the entire pipeline runs in 3–5 minutes on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

JanusMesh is a zero-shot, two-stage framework built on TRELLIS's Rectified Flow 3D generator. Stage 1 performs dual-branch denoising: two branches share an initial noise latent but are conditioned on different prompts, and at every denoising step the predicted clean latents are decoded into voxel space, rotation-aligned (via a CLIP-guided orientation search that selects the optimal relative rotation by maximizing silhouette similarity), fused by averaging their Truncated Signed Distance Fields and binarizing, then re-encoded back into latent space for the next step—this cross-space decode-blend-re-encode loop keeps the blended geometry on TRELLIS's valid manifold. Stage 2 applies view-conditioned texture synthesis: a depth-conditioned ControlNet (Stable Diffusion) predicts clean images from each target viewpoint, which are back-projected onto the fused mesh and merged via cosine-weighted blending based on surface normals, assigning distinct prompt-driven textures to each angular sector. Two optional noise-guidance strategies (Noise Blending and Space Control) inject a spatial structural prior from pre-generated single-semantic voxels before denoising begins, improving fusion quality for geometrically challenging prompt pairs.

## Results

On 50 prompt pairs, JanusMesh achieves the highest GPT-4.1-mini semantic accuracy (84%) and the lowest cross-view semantic leakage (CLIP opposite-prompt score of 19.26), confirming strong dual-semantic recognizability with minimal bleed-through. It also produces the lowest Boundary Seam Score (798.31 vs. 1503.42 for Direct Concatenation) and a 18% multi-object detection rate (vs. 56% for Direct Concatenation), demonstrating geometric coherence. All configurations complete in 3–5 minutes on a single RTX 4090, matching Direct Concatenation's speed while being 8× faster than Shape from Semantics (~40 min) and ~50× faster than its VSD variant (>3–4 hr).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Shape from Semantics (SDS, ~40 min), JanusMesh wins decisively on GPT Accuracy (84% vs. 70%), FID (185.6 vs. 194.1), and runtime (3–5 min vs. >40 min), while matching KID (0.051) and slightly improving CLIP opposite-prompt leakage (19.26 vs. 19.72); the VSD variant is even slower (>3–4 hr) with worse GPT Accuracy (65%) and higher leakage (20.69). Against TRELLIS prompted with a dual-semantic description, JanusMesh achieves substantially higher CLIP similarity (28.17 vs. 22.18) and GPT Accuracy (84% vs. 60%), confirming that TRELLIS collapses to a generic shape rather than producing view-dependent semantics; however, TRELLIS reports lower FID/KID and multi-object rate because it generates a single coherent (but semantically empty) object. Against DreamBeast, JanusMesh leads on GPT Accuracy (84% vs. 65%), KID (0.051 vs. 0.094), and CLIP opposite-prompt score (19.26 vs. 22.89), while DreamBeast marginally edges out on FID (184.96 vs. 185.56) and both share the same 18% multi-object rate; DreamBeast also requires 3–4 hr per sample. Notably, Direct Concatenation (not in the formal baseline list but a key ablation) achieves the highest raw CLIP score (29.03) due to trivially preserving single-view appearances, yet suffers a 56% multi-object detection rate and 1503.42 boundary seam score, exposing its fundamental geometric incoherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D Visual Anagrams paradigm (synchronized multi-view denoising for optical illusions) into native 3D latent space, repurposing TRELLIS's structured voxel latents as the fusion substrate. The cross-space decode–SDF-blend–re-encode strategy at each denoising step is directly inspired by LookingGlass's Laplacian-pyramid warping, while the clean-latent averaging principle follows SyncTweedies and the timestep-interpolation guidance adapts SpaceControl. The key departure from prior 3D illusion work (Shape from Semantics, which uses SDS optimization) is the shift from per-shape iterative optimization to a training-free, feed-forward dual-branch denoising pipeline, and from naive geometric stitching to SDF-based volumetric fusion that preserves surface continuity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3–5 minute runtime (vs. ~40 min for SDS and ~4 hr for VSD) makes 3D visual illusion generation practically accessible, and the training-free design avoids the over-saturation artifacts inherent to score-distillation objectives. However, the method is tightly coupled to TRELLIS's voxel-based latent representation, ruling out token-based generators like Hunyuan3D, and the CLIP-guided orientation search does not scale beyond two objects. Open questions include whether the cross-space SDF blending principle transfers to other 3D latent spaces, whether the view-conditioned texturing stage can be unified with geometry generation, and whether the approach generalizes to more than three simultaneous semantics without architectural changes.
