## Problem

Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods inherit a static densification strategy that averages positional gradients over a fixed window and compares them to a single threshold. Short-lived dynamic Gaussians, visible in only a few frames, accumulate disproportionately low gradient signals and never trigger densification, leaving dynamic regions permanently under-reconstructed and blurry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261543/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (downsampled to half resolution) with known camera poses, training a single model over the full 300-frame sequence rather than short segments. It produces a set of 3D Gaussians with per-Gaussian temporal deformation parameters (Fourier motion coefficients, temporal RBF opacity, polynomial rotation/scale) that render novel views of the dynamic scene at real-time speeds (146 FPS on N3DV). Evaluation uses both global metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and masked metrics (M-PSNR, M-SSIM) computed within dynamic regions identified via RAFT optical flow.

## Method

The paper proposes three complementary components for visibility-driven densification in dynamic 3DGS. (1) Visibility-Aware Densification (VAD) replaces the uniform gradient average with a visibility-weighted sum normalized by total opacity, so each Gaussian's densification signal reflects only frames where it is actually visible. (2) Temporally Adaptive Thresholding (TAT) lowers the densification threshold for short-lived Gaussians via a sub-linear function of their temporal scale ψ, promoting densification where it is most needed while preserving the standard threshold for static Gaussians. (3) Temporal Offset Warping (TOW) applies a piecewise-linear reparameterization of time around each Gaussian's temporal center, stretching the focus window (50 frames) to concentrate 75% of Fourier basis capacity there, enabling high-frequency motion modeling near peak activity and preventing temporal scale collapse in highly dynamic regions. The deformation model combines Fourier-based positional motion, a temporal RBF for opacity, and low-degree polynomials for rotation and scale, with Gaussians initialized across multiple temporal centers spaced 20 frames apart.

## Results

On the Neural 3D Video (N3DV) dataset, the proposed method achieves 32.42 PSNR and 24.68 M-PSNR, outperforming the strongest baseline Swift4D (32.12 PSNR, 23.74 M-PSNR) by 0.30 dB and 0.94 dB respectively. On Interdigital, the method reaches 34.14 PSNR and 28.87 M-PSNR, surpassing the next-best STG (33.45 PSNR, 27.14 M-PSNR) by 0.69 dB and 1.73 dB. The method also achieves the highest rendering speed at 146 FPS (vs. 53 FPS for Swift4D and 39 FPS for SaroGS) while maintaining a competitive model size of 204 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both N3DV and Interdigital. Against the closest competitor Swift4D, the gains are 0.30 dB in PSNR and 0.94 dB in M-PSNR on N3DV, and 2.52 dB in PSNR and 5.86 dB in M-PSNR on Interdigital, where Swift4D struggles with long-range motion. STG is the strongest baseline on Interdigital (33.45 PSNR) but still trails the proposed method by 0.69 dB in PSNR and 1.73 dB in M-PSNR. SaroGS, which also targets short-lived Gaussian densification, is outperformed by 0.34 dB in PSNR and 1.06 dB in M-PSNR on N3DV, and could not be evaluated on Interdigital due to CUDA runtime errors. 4DGaussian and Ex4DGS show the largest gaps, particularly on Interdigital where 4DGaussian drops to 26.74 PSNR and 18.09 M-PSNR, indicating severe degradation under long-range dynamic motion. The method also achieves the highest rendering speed (146 FPS) by avoiding heavy MLP decoders and multi-planar voxel structures used by competing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework and its dynamic extensions (deformation-field methods like STG and GaussianFlow, 4D representations like 4DGS, and keyframe-interpolation approaches like Ex-4DGS), all of which inherit the original static densification criterion. It departs from prior dynamic densification work (SaRO-GS, Anchored-4DGS) by identifying temporal visibility bias in gradient accumulation as the root cause of under-densified dynamic Gaussians, rather than addressing it through per-Gaussian learning-rate modulation or scaling-threshold adaptation. The three proposed components—visibility-weighted gradient normalization (VAD), lifespan-scaled thresholds (TAT), and piecewise-linear temporal warping (TOW)—form a unified framework that prior methods treat in isolation or not at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VAD module's demonstrated plug-and-play generalization across STG, Ex-4DGS, and Swift4D makes it a practical drop-in improvement for the broader dynamic 3DGS community, and the 146 FPS rendering speed (vs. 29–93 for competitors) underscores that the approach adds no heavy architectural overhead. However, the method has not been validated on sequences longer than 300 frames, remains restricted to multi-view capture, and TOW is architecturally tied to Fourier-based motion modeling, limiting its immediate transferability. Open questions include whether the visibility-aware formulation scales to minutes-long videos, whether it extends to monocular or sparse-view settings, and whether TAT/TOW can be decoupled from the specific deformation model and applied more broadly.
