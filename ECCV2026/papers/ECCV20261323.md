## Problem

MLLMs frequently hallucinate because their single CLIP-based visual encoder has perceptual blind spots in fine-grained details like spatial relations and object boundaries. Existing multi-expert frameworks attempt to fix this but suffer from two critical flaws: coarse-grained mixture (simple token concatenation that wastes informative insights with redundancy) and unspecialized alignment (joint training on standard data that causes experts to converge to homogeneous representations rather than providing complementary perspectives).

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a natural-language text query as input. The image is processed in parallel by three frozen vision encoders (CLIP-L/14-336, DINOv2-L/14, SAM-L/16), each producing 512 patch-level visual tokens. A query-guided clustering step adaptively merges these 1536 tokens down to 576 informative tokens before feeding them to a Vicuna-v1.5-7B LLM backbone, which generates a text response (e.g., yes/no answer, image caption, or free-form description). The output is standard text, with no additional structured annotations.

## Method

MoSVE combines two mechanisms built on the insight of inter-expert complementarity. First, Query-Guided Complementary Clustering performs patch-level hierarchical clustering over multi-expert visual tokens: it computes pairwise cosine distances, derives a per-patch adaptive threshold from cross-modal relevance (mean similarity to query tokens) and expert-query semantic divergence (entropy of the softmax similarity distribution), then merges tokens below the threshold into cluster centroids while preserving complementary outliers. Second, Complementary Rejection Fine-Tuning (CRFT) cultivates expert specialization by using a CLIP-based MLLM as an anchor to collect a negative pool of hallucinated samples, then routing each hard sample exclusively to the auxiliary expert whose global image representation is most cosine-dissimilar to the anchor's. Training proceeds in two stages: independent projector training on routed hard samples, followed by joint SFT of all projectors and the LLM on general data.

## Results

MoSVE achieves the best scores on POPE (86.9% accuracy, 86.5% F1), CHAIR_S (27.1%), and MMVP (57%) among all compared MLLMs and multi-expert frameworks, while also scoring 68.3 on MMBench for general multimodal reasoning. Notably, these results are obtained with only 576 visual tokens—6.2× fewer than LEO (3584) and half of LLaVA-HR (1024)—using a lightweight Vicuna-v1.5-7B backbone. On inference latency, MoSVE matches MARINE at 1.49 s/sample and is 2.3× faster than LEO (3.49 s/sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoSVE outperforms all single-expert MLLMs by wide margins: versus LLaVA-1.5 (7B), it gains +3.8 on POPE accuracy, −18.5 on CHAIR_S, and +35 on MMVP; versus the strongest single-expert model LLaVA-NeXT (Hermes-Yi-34B), it improves CHAIR_S by 8.7 points (27.1 vs. 35.8) and MMVP by 13 points (57 vs. 44) while using a 7B backbone instead of 34B. Among multi-expert baselines, MoSVE beats LEO on every reported metric (POPE 86.9 vs. 86.5, CHAIR_S 27.1 vs. 31.2, MMVP 57 vs. 46) with 6.2× fewer visual tokens, and surpasses LLaVA-HR on POPE (+1.0), CHAIR_S (−10.9), and MMVP (+5), though LLaVA-HR edges ahead on MMBench (68.6 vs. 68.3). MoSVE also dominates the guidance-based MARINE (CHAIR_S 27.1 vs. 37.5, MMVP 57 vs. 42) and the distillation-based MoVE-KD (MMVP 57 vs. 37, MMB 68.3 vs. 67.6). The only metric where MoSVE is not the absolute best is CHAIR_I (12.2 vs. 12.0 for LLaVA-NeXT-34B) and MMBench (68.3 vs. 68.6 for LLaVA-HR), both by narrow margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-expert MLLM line of work initiated by BLIP-2/InstructBLIP's decoupled encoder-LLM design and advanced by LEO, MoF, LLaVA-HR, MARINE, and MoVE-KD, which all integrate multiple visual encoders to overcome single-CLIP perceptual blind spots. MoSVE departs from these prior multi-expert frameworks on two axes: it replaces coarse-grained token concatenation or simple averaging with query-guided hierarchical clustering that adaptively merges redundant expert tokens while preserving complementary ones, and it replaces standard joint fine-tuning with Complementary Rejection Fine-Tuning (CRFT), which routes anchor-model failure cases exclusively to the most visually-disparate auxiliary expert for targeted projector training. The diagnostic analysis of CLIP, DINOv2, and SAM-L attention patterns provides the empirical grounding for the inter-expert complementarity assumption that motivates both mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoSVE offers a practical and efficient recipe for reducing MLLM hallucination without inflating token budgets or LLM scale, which is directly relevant to deploying vision-language models in safety-critical settings. The CRFT routing strategy—assigning hard samples to the expert with maximal feature discrepancy from the anchor—is a simple but non-obvious training signal that could generalize beyond the three-expert, LLaVA-specific setup studied here. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than three experts or different LLM backbones, how sensitive the negative-pool construction is to the verification heuristic, and whether the two-stage training paradigm is necessary or whether a single-stage variant with the same routing could suffice. The paper does not explore failure modes of the clustering mechanism on highly ambiguous or adversarial inputs.
