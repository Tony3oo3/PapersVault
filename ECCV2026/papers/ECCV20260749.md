## Problem

Existing camera-controllable view synthesis methods suffer from two coupled limitations: they rely on image diffusion models that only model discrete viewpoint pairs (lacking continuity along camera trajectories), and they enforce geometric guidance in a fragmented manner (e.g., injecting point clouds only at the representation level), which prevents the network from forming stable cross-view geometric correspondences. This fragmentation leads to geometric drift, structural collapse, and visual artifacts under continuous camera motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (704×1248) and a camera control prompt specifying the desired ending camera pose. The input image is lifted into a 3D point cloud and camera trajectory via VGGT, and the point cloud is rendered along an interpolated camera path to produce geometric reference frames. The output is a 29-frame video sequence at 704×1248 resolution, where intermediate frames trace the camera trajectory and the final frames (with the last 4 dedicated to persistent endpoint modeling) yield the target novel view.

## Method

UniGeo fine-tunes the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B rectified-flow video diffusion model (via LoRA, rank 256) by injecting unified geometric guidance at three levels. At the representation level, Frame-Decoupled Point Cloud Injection renders the VGGT-reconstructed point cloud along the target camera trajectory and concatenates the resulting frames along the frame dimension (rather than channel dimension) with target video tokens in the DiT backbone, avoiding strict pixel-to-pixel alignment. At the architecture level, Geometric Anchor Attention introduces a lightweight attention branch where each subsequent frame queries the first frame's key/value projections (initialized from pre-trained weights, with a zero-initialized output projection and scalar weight α=1), explicitly aligning cross-view features to the reference geometry. At the loss level, Trajectory-Endpoint Geometric Supervision applies a quadratic temporal weighting (γ=0.01) that increases loss at trajectory endpoints relative to intermediate frames, combined with a temporal extension strategy that duplicates the target-view frame across multiple consecutive timesteps to enforce persistent geometric constraints at the final viewpoint.

## Results

Under the extensive camera motion setting, UniGeo achieves FID of 113.11 / 66.67 / 76.97 and LPIPS of 0.3248 / 0.2377 / 0.2633 on DL3DV, RE10K, and Tanks respectively, outperforming all baselines on every metric. Under the limited camera motion setting, the gains are even more pronounced: LPIPS drops to 0.2065 / 0.1730 / 0.1526 and PSNR rises to 16.37 / 17.30 / 17.82 across the three datasets. On the MannequinChallenge dataset (human-centric scenes), UniGeo achieves the best FID (172.63) and LPIPS (0.3735) among all methods. Geometric consistency metrics (WarpErr, RotErr, TransErr) also confirm UniGeo's superiority, with the lowest errors in both motion settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniGeo outperforms all five baselines on every metric (FID, SSIM, LPIPS, PSNR) across all three datasets in both extensive and limited camera motion settings. The closest competitor is FlexWorld [18], which UniGeo surpasses by 12.16 FID points and 0.0631 LPIPS on RE10K (extensive motion), and by 13.80 FID points and 0.0892 LPIPS on Tanks (limited motion). ViewCrafter [89] and PE-Field [7] occupy the middle tier, with ViewCrafter generally stronger on geometric metrics (WarpErr, RotErr, TransErr) but still trailing UniGeo by 0.01–0.03 in error. CameraCtrl [30] and MotionCtrl [73] are the weakest baselines, with CameraCtrl achieving the highest FID (172.63 on DL3DV) and MotionCtrl showing the worst LPIPS (0.5242 on DL3DV), indicating significant structural degradation under camera motion. On the human-centric MannequinChallenge dataset, UniGeo leads FlexWorld by 11.06 FID and 0.0376 LPIPS, confirming robustness to dynamic, non-rigid content.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniGeo builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video diffusion model (fine-tuned via LoRA) and VGGT for camera pose estimation and point cloud reconstruction, positioning itself at the intersection of video generation and geometric 3D priors. It departs from prior camera-controllable view synthesis methods (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl, ViewCrafter, FlexWorld, PE-Field) that rely on fragmented geometric guidance—typically injecting point clouds or depth only at the representation level—by systematically enforcing geometric constraints across three coupled levels: representation (frame-decoupled point cloud injection), architecture (geometric anchor attention), and loss function (trajectory-endpoint supervision). The shift from image diffusion models (discrete view mappings) to video models (continuous viewpoint priors) as the generative backbone is a key architectural departure, though the paper acknowledges that video models alone still struggle without unified geometric guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled design framework—unified geometric guidance across representation, architecture, and loss—that could inform future camera-controllable generation beyond this specific pipeline. However, two limitations remain open: geometric accuracy degrades under extreme viewpoint changes or highly complex scenes where VGGT-reconstructed point clouds become unreliable, and inference cost (75s, 29GB) is still higher than single-frame image diffusion baselines. Whether the three-level guidance principle generalizes to other video editing or 4D generation tasks, and whether the specific hyperparameters (α=1, γ=0.01) transfer across architectures, are questions the paper leaves for future work.
