## Problem

In multi-task learning, conflicting task gradients interfere with each other, slowing convergence and degrading final performance. Existing mitigations—gradient surgery, loss reweighting, and static task grouping—either treat conflict only at the level of individual parameter updates or rely on dense, rarely-updated affinity matrices that drift as task relationships evolve. The paper asks whether one can dynamically partition tasks into globally compatible groups using lightweight, refreshable conflict measurements so that each update step contains only mutually aligned tasks.

## Input / Output

At each training step the scheduler consumes per-task stochastic gradients of the shared parameters θ (and task-specific parameters φ_k) computed on independent mini-batches, maintaining exponential moving averages of these gradients in a low-dimensional sketch space of width d_sk ≪ d. It outputs a periodic activation schedule of length m_r (the number of color classes) that dictates which subset of tasks receives a gradient update at each step, along with the updated shared and task-specific parameters. The persistent memory footprint is O(K²) independent of model dimension d, and the full conflict-graph rebuild occurs only every R steps rather than every step.

## Method

SON-GOKU operates in four stages: (1) it maintains EMA-smoothed per-task gradients and, at each refresh (every R steps), computes pairwise interference coefficients ρ_ij = −cos(ḡ_i, ḡ_j) in a sketch space; (2) it builds a sparse undirected conflict graph G_τ whose edges connect task pairs with ρ_ij > τ; (3) it applies the Welsh-Powell largest-first greedy coloring heuristic to partition vertices into at most Δ+1 color classes, guaranteeing no conflict edge lies within a class; (4) it cycles through color classes one per training step, so each step updates only a τ-compatible group. The graph is rebuilt and recolored at every refresh to track evolving task relationships, and a warm-up phase with τ=1 (no edges) transitions logarithmically to a target τ*. Theoretically, the paper proves descent preservation within low-conflict groups, the standard 1/√T non-convex SGD rate with only a (1+τ) constant factor, strict superiority of scheduled group updates over a single mixed update when cross-group interference is negative, and high-probability exact recovery of the population conflict graph from a modest number of gradient samples.

## Results

Across six datasets, SON-GOKU (standalone) achieves 65% accuracy on CIFAR-10, 69% on AV-MNIST, and 61% on MM-IMDb, outperforming the Uniform baseline (55%, 63%, 56%) by 10–14 percentage points. When combined with existing optimizers, SON-GOKU + AdaTask reaches 67% on CIFAR-10 and 71% on AV-MNIST (best among all methods), while SON-GOKU + PCGrad achieves the best regression metrics on HEALTH (62% accuracy, 0.45 MAE) and NYUv2 (19.7° angle error, 0.076 seg. mIoU). The scheduler's overhead is modest: at K=40 tasks with R=32, SON-GOKU takes ~12 s per refresh versus 1127 s for PCGrad and 1014 s for Nash-MTL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SON-GOKU standalone (65% CIFAR-10, 69% AV-MNIST, 61% MM-IMDb) outperforms all nine baselines on classification accuracy, beating the strongest individual baselines—FAMO (64%, 70%, 61%) and AdaTask (63%, 67%, 59%)—by 1–2 points, and the Uniform baseline by 10–14 points. On regression and dense-prediction metrics, SON-GOKU + PCGrad achieves the best HEALTH accuracy (62% vs. 60% for FAMO/FairGrad/Nash-MTL) and the lowest NYUv2 angle error (19.7° vs. 19.9° for FAMO), while SON-GOKU + AdaTask leads on classification (67% CIFAR-10, 71% AV-MNIST, 63% MM-IMDb), surpassing even FAMO by 3 points on CIFAR-10. SON-GOKU + GradNorm is competitive but does not consistently dominate any single metric, suggesting the scheduler's benefit is most pronounced when paired with methods that reshape gradients (PCGrad) or adapt per-task learning rates (AdaTask). Notably, SON-GOKU's wall-clock time at K=40 (~12 s) is roughly 90× faster than PCGrad (1127 s) and 84× faster than Nash-MTL (1014 s), while still matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SON-GOKU repurposes classical graph coloring (Welsh-Powell greedy heuristic) as a scheduling mechanism for multi-task learning, a connection not previously exploited in the MTL literature. It departs from gradient-manipulation methods (PCGrad, CAGrad, MGDA) and loss-reweighting methods (GradNorm, AdaTask, FAMO) by operating at a higher level: rather than reshaping individual gradients or rescaling losses, it decides *which* tasks are updated together at each step. Among prior task-grouping approaches (Fifty et al. 2021, Wang et al. 2024, Jeong & Yoon 2025), SON-GOKU distinguishes itself through dynamic recoloring at regular intervals and a sparse, sketch-based interference estimate, addressing the drift and scalability limitations the authors identify in static or dense-affinity grouping. The paper explicitly positions the scheduler as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, existing optimizers, and demonstrates stacking on top of PCGrad, AdaTask, and GradNorm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first (to our knowledge) set of convergence and graph-recovery guarantees specifically for a dynamically recolored task-grouping scheduler in MTL, giving practitioners theoretical confidence that the schedule preserves the standard 1/√T non-convex rate with only a (1+τ) constant penalty. The practical finding that SON-GOKU+PCGrad is orders of magnitude faster than PCGrad alone at K=40 (≈11 s vs ≈1127 s) while matching or exceeding accuracy suggests that scheduling can reduce the per-step conflict-handling burden, a tradeoff worth exploring for large-scale multi-task systems. Open questions include behavior at very large task counts (experiments cap at K=40), sensitivity to the refresh period R and tolerance τ in non-stationary or highly imbalanced settings, and whether the graph-coloring abstraction generalizes to hierarchical or partially-overlapping task structures.
