## Problem

Video emotion analysis is predominantly framed as static classification, assigning a single label to an entire clip and ignoring the cumulative, event-driven nature of viewer affect. Existing benchmarks lack temporal boundaries for emotion shifts, fine-grained viewer-centric taxonomies, and explicit causal explanations for why an emotion arises, making it impossible to evaluate whether a model can track dynamic affective transitions over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261489/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip (average 14.3 s, minimum 1 s, static frames removed) and must produce a structured JSON sequence of temporally continuous segments spanning the full duration. Each segment contains start/end timestamps in seconds, an emotion label drawn from a 27-category VCE viewer-centric taxonomy, and a causal rationale (~119 words on average) following a Visual Evidence → Contextual Appraisal → Emotion Trigger chain. Segments shorter than 0.4 s are penalized, and the total output is soft-capped at 1200 tokens with a hard cap at 2400.

## Method

DAR-R1 adapts Qwen2.5-VL-3B (frozen vision encoder, fine-tuned LLM and aligner) via a two-stage pipeline: (1) cold-start supervised fine-tuning for 0.5 epochs to learn the strict JSON output schema and initialize a policy prior, followed by (2) one epoch of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that replaces the PPO critic with group-relative advantage estimation. The RL stage is guided by a five-component reward—structural validity (JSON schema, minimum segment duration, length penalty), segment-count accuracy (exponential decay on count mismatch), temporal segmentation (IoU plus boundary-distance decay with τ=0.5 s), emotion accuracy (binary, gated on IoU > 0.5), and reasoning quality (length score centered at 120 words plus Jaccard-based repetition penalty)—weighted at 0.10/0.25/0.25/0.25/0.15. The underlying DAR dataset is built through a three-stage pipeline: coarse-to-fine event segmentation (Gemini-2.5-Pro semantic proposals snapped to PySceneDetect visual cuts, verified by InternVL3.5), incremental differential captioning (Qwen3-VL conditioned on prior-segment descriptions, filtered by Grounding DINO), and bottom-up stream-of-affect reasoning generation, all validated by a dual-consistency protocol using two independent MLLM judges across five quality dimensions.

## Results

On the DAR test set, DAR-R1 achieves 41.5% segment-count accuracy, 52.3% mIoU for temporal segmentation, 28.6% emotion accuracy, and a GPT-4o reasoning score of 3.3/5 (average across five dimensions). The cold-start SFT stage alone (DAR-SFT) already surpasses all baselines, reaching 37.7% SC-Acc and 47.6% mIoU, while the GRPO reinforcement learning stage adds further gains of +3.8 points in mIoU and +2.8 points in emotion accuracy. The strongest generalist baseline (Qwen3-VL-4B) reaches only 48.2% mIoU and 17.9% Emo-Acc, and the best emotion-focused model (AffectGPT) scores just 11.9% mIoU and 5.8% Emo-Acc.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAR-R1 outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against emotion-focused MLLMs (Videmo, EmotionLlama, AffectGPT), the gap is dramatic: DAR-R1's 52.3% mIoU exceeds AffectGPT's 11.9% by over 4×, and its 28.6% emotion accuracy dwarfs AffectGPT's 5.8%. Against the strongest generalist MLLMs, DAR-R1 still leads clearly: it beats Qwen3-VL-4B (the best general baseline) by +4.1 points in mIoU (52.3 vs. 48.2), +10.7 points in emotion accuracy (28.6 vs. 17.9), and +0.6 in GPT reasoning score (3.3 vs. 2.7). The SFT stage alone already surpasses every baseline, confirming that the DAR dataset's dense temporal and causal annotations provide effective supervision. The additional GRPO stage yields consistent further gains, with the largest improvement in causal-logic reasoning (3.0 → 3.8 on the GPT-4o CL dimension), indicating that reward-shaped RL specifically strengthens the model's ability to produce grounded, non-repetitive affective explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant static-clip formulation of video emotion analysis (DFEW, CREMA-D, MER2023) and from character-centric emotion recognition (AffectGPT, Videmo) by reframing the problem as viewer-centric, event-aligned dynamic affective reasoning grounded in Affective Events Theory. It builds on the VCE dataset's 27-category viewer-emotion taxonomy and extends it with dense temporal segmentation and causal reasoning chains, while drawing on the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 for structured output refinement. The construction pipeline repurposes existing tools (Gemini-2.5-Pro for semantic boundaries, PySceneDetect for visual cuts, Grounding DINO for entity verification) in a novel coarse-to-fine, incremental-differential captioning workflow that has no direct precedent in prior emotion datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAR provides the first large-scale benchmark where models must simultaneously localize emotion shifts in time, classify fine-grained viewer emotions, and generate causally grounded explanations—a combination that no prior dataset supports. The consistently low absolute performance of all 10+ evaluated MLLMs (best: 41.5% segment-count accuracy, 28.6% emotion accuracy) signals that dynamic affective reasoning remains far from solved, opening a substantial research agenda. Key open questions include whether the AET-motivated segmentation granularity is optimal, whether the GRPO reward design generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone, and how the benchmark scales to longer-form or multi-viewer scenarios.
