## Problem

Existing latent visual reasoning methods that internalize visual CoT within LVLMs' continuous hidden space rely on strong supervision via manually annotated auxiliary images, incurring prohibitive annotation costs and capping performance at the quality of human-curated visual chains. This paper asks whether latent visual reasoning can be trained in a fully self-supervised, annotation-free manner that still effectively grounds intermediate representations to query-relevant visual evidence for hallucination mitigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261967/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text query as input and produces a text answer, but during generation it can enter a latent mode triggered by a special <latent> token, emitting K continuous latent embeddings (default K=4) drawn from the last-layer hidden states before resuming text decoding with </latent>. These latent tokens are not discrete vocabulary items but continuous vectors occupying <latent_pad> placeholder positions in the autoregressive sequence, serving as internal visual 'thoughts' that are never directly observed by the user. A content-irrelevant contrastive image (Gaussian-noise perturbation of the input) is additionally fed through the model during training to compute attention-based saliency weights, but is not part of inference.

## Method

CoLVR trains in two stages. In the SFT stage, the model learns to generate and utilize latent tokens by aligning their mean embedding with coarse region pseudo-labels (fixed canonical partitions of the image into bounding boxes) via a cosine-similarity loss, with a latent-only backpropagation trick (stop-gradient on non-latent pathways) to prevent the network from exploiting shortcuts by altering image-token representations. In the RL stage, a visual contrastive probing mechanism computes per-patch saliency weights by subtracting the query-to-image attention under a Gaussian-noise contrastive image from that under the original image, then rectifying and normalizing to a distribution over image tokens. GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) is then applied with a reward that measures cosine similarity between the mean latent embedding and the top-M most salient image-token hidden states, plus a format reward ensuring the model actually enters latent mode. The entire pipeline requires no ground-truth answers, no annotated visual chains, and no external visual tools, making it fully annotation-free.

## Results

On the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, CoLVR achieves the best CHAIR C_s of 20.2% (↓10.6% over base), CHAIR F1 of 80.1% (+1.8%), PoPE accuracy of 89.6% (+4.7%), HallusionBench accuracy of 54.3% (+1.8%), and MMHal score of 5.12 (+0.26) while reducing the MMHal hallucination rate to 30.2%. On the lighter Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone, CoLVR still reduces C_s from 38.4% to 30.8% and PoPE accuracy from 85.9% to 88.2%. In the extensibility evaluation (Table 2), CoLVR* (further SFT on LVR-SFT data) reaches 73.4% on MathVista and 51.4% on MathVerse, surpassing the strongest supervised baseline (Monet) by 2.2% and 0.2% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLVR (7B) achieves the best CHAIR C_s (20.2%), CHAIR F1 (80.1%), PoPE accuracy (89.6%), HallusionBench (54.3%), and MMHal score (5.12) across all nine baselines. Against train-free methods, CoLVR outperforms VCD by 14.4 points on C_s and 3.6 points on PoPE accuracy, and beats PAI (the strongest train-free on PoPE Acc at 88.2%) by 1.4 points. Among 'thinking with images' methods, CoLVR surpasses DeepEyes on C_s (20.2 vs 27.6) and HallusionBench (54.3 vs 53.9), though DeepEyes retains a slight edge on MMHal hallucination rate (29.2 vs 30.2). Against supervised latent reasoning baselines, CoLVR reduces C_s by 13.0 points over LVR (33.2→20.2) and 13.9 points over Monet (34.1→20.2), while matching or exceeding their PoPE F1 scores. The only metrics where CoLVR does not lead are PoPE F1 (HALC: 89.1, PAI/Monet: 87.3 vs CoLVR: 86.3) and MMHal hallucination rate (DeepEyes: 29.2 vs 30.2), both narrow gaps on secondary metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLVR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) latent visual reasoning (LVR, Monet), which internalizes visual CoT into continuous hidden states but requires costly human-annotated auxiliary images for SFT alignment; (2) contrastive decoding for hallucination mitigation (VCD, PAI, OPERA, ASCD), which identifies query-relevant visual patches by contrasting attention over original vs. content-irrelevant images; and (3) GRPO-based RL for policy optimization. The key departure is repurposing the contrastive probing signal—not as an inference-time decoding trick but as a reward function for RL—thereby eliminating the need for annotated visual chains while still grounding latent tokens in salient visual evidence. The SFT stage further replaces annotated auxiliary images with coarse spatial pseudo-labels (fixed image partitions), making the entire pipeline annotation-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical contribution is demonstrating that self-supervised latent alignment can match or exceed fully supervised latent visual reasoning (LVR, Monet) on hallucination benchmarks without any human-curated visual CoT data, directly addressing the scalability bottleneck of annotation-dependent training. The extensibility result—where CoLVR initialized with self-supervision and then fine-tuned on LVR-SFT data outperforms supervised baselines on general reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse)—suggests the contrastive-guided latent foundation is a stronger starting point than supervised latent alignment. Open questions include whether the contrastive attention signal truly captures query-relevant semantics or merely spatial salience, whether the approach generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL family, and whether the fixed-length latent design (K=4) scales to more complex multi-step reasoning tasks.
