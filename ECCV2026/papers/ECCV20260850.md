## Problem

The OSNOM task requires maintaining 3D object locations and identities in long egocentric videos even when objects leave the field of view, become occluded, or change appearance due to manipulation. Prior approaches relied on hand-crafted cost functions with fixed thresholds and the Hungarian algorithm, limiting adaptability and preventing the model from learning when to create or retain tracks. This paper introduces the first fully learning-based solution to this task.

## Input / Output

The model consumes per-observation appearance descriptors (DINOv2 features reduced to 256-dim via PCA and ℓ₂-normalised) and 3D location estimates (object centres lifted to world coordinates via monocular depth aligned with scene reconstructions), both derived from segmentation masks at each frame. It produces, for each observation, a probability distribution over all existing tracks plus a New Track option, from which the argmax yields the assignment. The track memory is updated online: appearance is maintained via DenStream clustering (persistent and transient clusters) and location via a short 1-second buffer, keeping memory bounded at ~220 MB and ~322 FPS.

## Method

Whareformer consists of two learnable modules. First, for each observation it computes the minimum Euclidean distance to each track's appearance clusters and location buffer, concatenates these two scalar distances, and projects them through a learnable linear layer into a d=64-dim embedding that encodes the joint appearance-location likelihood. Second, a single-layer transformer encoder (no positional encodings, ensuring permutation invariance) processes the sequence of track embeddings with a prepended New Track token, and a linear head produces a softmax assignment over all tracks plus the NT option. The NT token makes new-track creation an explicit, learned decision rather than a threshold-triggered fallback. Training uses teacher forcing with cross-entropy on ground-truth track IDs, with an optional DAgger correction phase that re-extracts training data from the model's own predictions to reduce the inference distribution shift. The key design choice of encoding relative distances rather than raw features enables generalisation to unseen objects, environments, and datasets.

## Results

On the EPIC-KITCHENS test split (54 unseen videos), Whareformer achieves 72.2% mPCL and 84.0% IDF1, surpassing the prior SOTA LMK by +19.2% mPCL and +14.0% IDF1. Cross-dataset generalisation is strong: on IT3DEgo (HoloLens2, non-kitchen scenes) Whareformer reaches 58.1% mPCL and 87.9% IDF1 (+27.1% and +18.7% over LMK), and on HD-EPIC (Aria Glasses, sparse annotations) it attains 41.6% mPCL and 51.6% IDF1 (+7.1% and +23.6% over LMK). The model is trained on only 56 videos yet outperforms all baselines across all three benchmarks and all timescales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LMK [26] (the prior SOTA on OSNOM), Whareformer wins decisively on every metric across all three datasets: +19.2% mPCL and +14.0% IDF1 on EPIC-KITCHENS, +27.1% mPCL and +18.7% IDF1 on IT3DEgo, and +7.1% mPCL and +23.6% IDF1 on HD-EPIC. The advantage is most pronounced on IT3DEgo, where LMK suffers a 22% absolute mPCL drop from its tuned domain, while Whareformer generalises robustly thanks to its relative-distance encoding. Against ByteTrack [39], a 2D tracker without 3D reasoning, Whareformer dominates by large margins (e.g. 72.2% vs. 12.3% mPCL on EPIC), confirming that 3D spatial reasoning is essential for long-term object permanence. Against IT3DEgo [40], which enjoys the privileged advantage of enrolling object templates at first appearance and knowing the total object count, Whareformer still outperforms it on all three datasets (e.g. 72.2% vs. 40.0% mPCL on EPIC; 58.1% vs. 27.5% mPCL on IT3DEgo), demonstrating that learned track assignment without oracle enrolment is both more accurate and more practical.

## Relation to Prior Work

Whareformer departs from the heuristic association strategies (fixed cost thresholds, Hungarian algorithm) used in prior OSNOM work [26,40] and 3D egocentric tracking [7,16] by replacing hand-crafted matching with a learnable transformer that jointly reasons over appearance and 3D location distances. It repurposes the transformer-based track-association paradigm from short-horizon 3D driving tracking [29,33,35,36,38] to the fundamentally different egocentric setting, where objects change appearance through manipulation and disappear for minutes. The New Track token draws on the '[NT]' token from UnweaveNet [27], and the online appearance clustering leverages DenStream [8], but the combination of these ideas into a feed-forward, end-to-end trainable tracker for long egocentric videos is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first learning-based solution to the OSNOM task, Whareformer demonstrates that a small transformer trained on only 56 videos can substantially outperform carefully tuned heuristic methods and generalize across three egocentric datasets, suggesting that learned association is more robust than hand-crafted cost functions for long-horizon tracking. The NT token and distance-based (rather than feature-based) representation are design choices that could transfer to other tracking or re-identification settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even longer videos or more objects, whether the 1-layer transformer optimum reflects a fundamental simplicity in the assignment problem or a limitation of the training signal, and how the method behaves when 3D location estimates are significantly noisier than the aligned-depth pipeline used here.
