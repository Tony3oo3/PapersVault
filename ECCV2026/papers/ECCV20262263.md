## Problem

Text-based image editing with pre-trained rectified flow models requires balancing source consistency and target alignment, but existing approaches either rely on heuristic inversion-based pipelines with architecture-specific designs or construct source-to-target trajectories that are sensitive to hyperparameters like random seeds and flow steps. No prior method provides a unified theoretical framework that rigorously decouples these two objectives within the deterministic ODE formulation of flow models.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in VAE latent space: a source image is encoded via a pretrained VAE encoder, and all editing is performed on the latent representation. Per backward step, the method requires exactly two forward passes of the pre-trained velocity network (one conditioned on the source prompt, one on the target prompt), making it training-free and gradient-free. The backward editing ODE is agnostic to the forward initialization strategy, so any inversion, noise injection, or hybrid forward process can be plugged in without modification.

## Method

h-Flow reformulates image editing as conditional generation under two terminal events (source image constraint and target prompt alignment) using Doob's h-Transform. Since RF models are deterministic ODEs (g≡0) where the h-Transform is undefined, the paper constructs an equivalent SDE with diffusion coefficient g_eq(t)=√(2t/(1−t)) that shares identical marginals with the RF dynamics, enabling rigorous extension of the h-Transform. Two dedicated h-functions are then designed: h_rec encodes the source constraint and yields a closed-form reconstruction velocity v_rec=(x_t−x_0^src)/t, while h_edit encodes the target prompt and yields an editing direction Δ_edit=v_θ^edit−v_θ^src that captures only the semantic delta relative to the source. A velocity orthogonal decomposition projects Δ_edit onto the orthogonal complement of the reconstruction velocity, ensuring the editing signal acts exclusively in directions that do not interfere with source preservation, thereby fully decoupling the two control knobs λ_rec and λ_edit.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, h-Flow achieves the best average rank (2.33) across all 9 metrics, with PSNR 23.88, SSIM 0.840, Distance 0.026, CLIP-Edited 22.80, and HPS 27.79. On the more challenging PIE-Bench++ (multi-aspect edits), h-Flow again attains the top average rank (2.78), recording PSNR 24.30, SSIM 0.850, Distance 0.029, CLIP-Edited 24.54, and HPS 27.67. The method achieves a notably better editability–fidelity trade-off than any single baseline: it matches or exceeds the best source-consistency methods (UniEdit-Flow) on structural metrics while simultaneously delivering stronger target alignment than the best alignment methods (OdeInv).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest inversion-based baseline RF-Solver [32], h-Flow wins on PSNR (23.88 vs 22.90), SSIM (0.840 vs 0.819), Distance (0.026 vs 0.031), CLIP-Edited (22.80 vs 22.68), and HPS (27.79 vs 27.24) on PIE-Bench, but concedes CLIP-Entire (25.80 vs 26.09) and Aesthetic Score (4.98 vs 5.48). Against UniEdit-Flow [13], which dominates source-consistency metrics (PSNR 29.45, SSIM 0.900), h-Flow trades some fidelity for substantially better target alignment (CLIP-Edited 22.80 vs 22.34) and higher human preference (HPS 27.79 vs 26.77). Against OdeInv, which achieves the highest CLIP-Edited (23.83) but the worst structural preservation (PSNR 13.44, Distance 0.130), h-Flow sacrifices only 1.03 CLIP-Edited points while improving PSNR by 10.44 and reducing Distance by 0.104. Against the inversion-free baselines FlowEdit [18] and SDEdit [23], h-Flow wins on every source-consistency metric (e.g., PSNR 23.88 vs 21.92 vs 18.01) and on CLIP-Edited, confirming that the h-Transform-based backward guidance outperforms heuristic trajectory constructions. On the harder PIE-Bench++ multi-aspect setting, the same pattern holds: h-Flow maintains the best average rank (2.78) and leads on PSNR, SSIM, Distance, and HPS, while RF-Solver edges ahead on CLIP-Entire and CLIP-Edited by small margins (0.31 and 0.35 points respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Doob's h-Transform from the SDE-based diffusion framework (as used in h-Edit) to the deterministic Rectified Flow ODE setting by constructing an equivalent SDE with identical marginals, thereby providing a principled probabilistic bridge that prior flow-based editing methods lacked. It departs from both inversion-based approaches (RF-Inversion, RF-Solver, UniEdit-Flow), which rely on heuristic forward inversions and architecture-specific source-condition injection, and inversion-free trajectory-construction methods (FlowEdit, SDEdit), which remain sensitive to hyperparameters like random seeds and step counts. The key conceptual departure is the design of two dedicated h-functions—one yielding an exact closed-form reconstruction velocity for source consistency, the other a velocity-difference editing signal for target alignment—combined with an orthogonal projection that decouples the two objectives at the velocity level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding flow-based editing in a rigorous conditional-generation formulation, h-Flow offers a theoretically motivated alternative to the heuristic pipelines that dominate the field, and its plug-and-play backward ODE can be paired with any forward initialization strategy without retraining. The orthogonal decomposition provides a clean, interpretable mechanism for independently controlling fidelity and editability, which is practically useful for practitioners tuning editing strength. However, the method is evaluated only on FLUX.1-dev with a single backbone, and the performance gains over strong baselines like UniEdit-Flow and RF-Solver are modest (average rank 2.33 vs. 3.00 on PIE-Bench), leaving open whether the theoretical advantages translate to larger or more diverse model families. The authors note future extensions to video and 3D generation, but the current scope is limited to 2D text-based editing.
