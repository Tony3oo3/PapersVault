## Problem

Gloss-free sign language translation relies on decoder attention to implicitly learn the correspondence between temporal visual frames and target text tokens, but sign language word order frequently diverges from spoken-language order and many frames carry no lexical content. This forces the decoder to simultaneously learn translation and discover a latent cross-modal permutation, degrading accuracy especially on large-vocabulary, open-domain benchmarks. The paper asks whether making this alignment explicit at multiple granularities can sharpen correspondences and simplify decoding.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB sign language video as input, extracts per-frame features with a frozen CLIP-ViT-Large encoder (2048-dim via S²-Wrapper at scales 1 and 2), and temporally downsamples them 4× through an attention-based encoder. The decoding target is not the raw sentence but a pseudo-gloss sequence of content tokens (NOUN, VERB, ADJ, ADV, NUM, PRON, PROPN) obtained by a frozen spaCy POS filter that strips function words; at inference a lightweight text-only recovery model re-inserts the dropped function words and restores spoken word order from the signing-order pseudo-gloss output.

## Method

VTaMo introduces three complementary alignment objectives beyond the standard translation loss. First, a local objective solves an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem (Sinkhorn in log-domain) between temporal visual segments and pseudo-gloss token embeddings, augmented with a learnable null token that absorbs transitional frames; the transport plan drives a window-based reordering of visual features into target token order before the LoRA-adapted Flan-T5 decoder. Second, a global objective applies a learnable orthogonal transformation to sentence-level visual embeddings and minimizes an Earth Mover's Distance against text sentence embeddings, with a FIFO memory queue for batch diversity and a scheduled ramp-up after local alignment stabilizes. Third, a position-aligned InfoNCE contrastive loss binds each reordered visual token to its text embedding via projection heads, with gradients blocked from the text branch to preserve pre-trained LM representations. Training proceeds in two phases—alignment losses with a frozen LM, then joint optimization—and at inference the decoder reads visual tokens in original temporal order while the contrastive grounding ensures each token decodes to its correct lexical item.

## Results

VTaMo achieves state-of-the-art gloss-free sign language translation across all four benchmarks without visual fine-tuning or large-scale pretraining. On Phoenix-2014T it reaches 28.86 BLEU-4 (60.24 ROUGE-L), surpassing the strongest gloss-free baseline SpaMo by 4.54 BLEU-4 and approaching the best gloss-based result (TS-SLT, 28.95). On the more challenging How2Sign and OpenASL benchmarks, VTaMo attains 18.47 BLEU-4 / 50.26 BLEURT and 25.94 BLEU-4 / 62.48 BLEURT respectively, outperforming pose+RGB methods with large-scale pretraining (SHuBERT, Uni-Sign) by 2.2–3.6 BLEU-4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTaMo consistently outperforms all gloss-free baselines across all four benchmarks. On Phoenix-2014T, it beats SpaMo [16] by 4.54 BLEU-4 (28.86 vs. 24.32) and C2RL [6] by 2.11 BLEU-4, while approaching the best gloss-based TS-SLT [5] within 0.09 BLEU-4. On CSL-Daily, the margin over SpaMo widens to 6.61 BLEU-4 (27.16 vs. 20.55) and it surpasses Uni-Sign [21] (26.36) by 0.80 BLEU-4 despite using only RGB without pose input or large-scale pretraining. On How2Sign, VTaMo achieves 18.47 BLEU-4 and 50.26 BLEURT, beating SpaMo by 8.36 BLEU-4, SHuBERT [14] by 2.27 BLEU-4, and Uni-Sign by 3.57 BLEU-4; it ranks second on ROUGE-L behind SSVP-SLT [33] (37.14 vs. 38.40). On OpenASL, VTaMo leads all methods with 25.94 BLEU-4 and 62.48 BLEURT, outperforming Uni-Sign by 2.80 BLEU-4 and SHuBERT by 2.74 BLEU-4 despite their additional pose modality and large-scale sign pretraining. The backbone ablation (Table 5) confirms that VTaMo's gains over SpaMo (+2.49 to +4.54 BLEU-4) are consistent across five different language-model backbones, attributing the improvement to the alignment design rather than a favorable LM choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

VTaMo builds on two distinct lines of work: entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn, Cuturi 2013) for fine-grained cross-modal matching, and learnable orthogonal transformations for embedding-space calibration (Lample et al., 2018). It departs from prior gloss-free SLT methods (SpaMo, Uni-Sign, SHuBERT, Sign2GPT) that leave frame-to-token correspondence implicit in decoder cross-attention, instead making alignment explicit through a three-level objective (local OT with a null token, global EMD-based calibration, position-aligned contrastive learning) and physically reordering visual features before decoding. The pseudo-gloss formulation—aligning against content words filtered by spaCy rather than raw sentences—addresses the ill-posedness of frame-to-word alignment when function words lack sign-level counterparts, a problem prior methods sidestep by relying on the decoder to absorb the mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent SOTA gains across four benchmarks in three languages, without visual fine-tuning or gloss supervision, suggest that explicit cross-modal alignment is an underexploited lever in gloss-free SLT and potentially in other video-to-text tasks where temporal order diverges from target order. The noise-sensitivity analysis (robustness to background and identity changes) supports the claim that the model learns gesture semantics rather than scene shortcuts. Open questions include whether the text-only recovery model for spoken word order generalizes to low-resource languages, whether the approach extends to real-time or streaming settings, and whether the OT-based reordering can be replaced by a learned permutation at inference to avoid the two-stage pipeline.
