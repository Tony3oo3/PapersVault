## Problem

End-to-end driving planners that select from large trajectory anchor sets face three coupled difficulties: the single-pass ranking over thousands of candidates is an ill-posed optimization, Gaussian-noise trajectory expansion produces kinematically unrealistic candidates that hinder denoising, and existing RL training relies on a single aggregated reward computed via expensive online simulation, limiting both optimization granularity and training throughput.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260114/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-modal sensor input—RGB camera images and LiDAR point clouds fused through a shared ResNet34 backbone, plus ego-vehicle status—producing a single driving trajectory as a sequence of (x, y) coordinates over T future timesteps. A camera-only variant (HAD-L) replaces LiDAR with learnable positional embeddings. The final trajectory is not a single decoded path but a softmax-weighted average of 50 locally refined candidates, where the weights combine distance-to-expert, safety-metric, and RL scores predicted by separate MLP heads.

## Method

HAD decomposes planning into a two-stage hierarchical diffusion process: a Driving Intention Decoder denoises from 20 predefined anchors to select the top-2 plausible high-level intentions, and a Trajectory Refinement Decoder then fine-tunes 50 locally expanded candidates around those intentions. To generate expansion candidates without destroying kinematic structure, the paper projects trajectories into polar coordinates and applies discrete radial scaling (5 levels) and angular offset (5 levels) perturbations, yielding 25 structurally valid variants per anchor. For policy learning, Metric-Decoupled Policy Optimization (MDPO) replaces the single scalar RL reward with per-metric selection probabilities and normalized rewards, enabling targeted optimization of collision avoidance, lane keeping, comfort, and other criteria independently. An Offline Trajectory Reward Retrieval scheme pre-computes safety scores for 8192 reference trajectories and retrieves them via nearest-neighbor lookup during training, reducing per-trajectory reward latency from 0.24 s to 0.004 s and cutting total training time by 5×.

## Results

On NAVSIM v2 (the more challenging open-loop benchmark), HAD achieves 88.6 EPDMS, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art EvaDrive (86.3) and DiffRefiner (86.2) by 2.3 and 2.4 points respectively. On NAVSIM v1, HAD reaches 90.2 PDMS, outperforming DriveSuprim (89.9) and DiffusionDrive (88.1). On the closed-loop HUGSIM benchmark, HAD-L achieves 47.5 Route Completion and 30.8 HD-Score, exceeding ZTRS by 4.9 RC and 1.9 HD-Score, with particularly large gains in Extreme scenarios (39.1 RC vs. 21.9 for ZTRS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAD outperforms all 17 compared baselines across both NAVSIM and HUGSIM. On NAVSIM v2, HAD (88.6 EPDMS) beats the closest competitors EvaDrive (86.3) and DiffRefiner (86.2) by 2.3 and 2.4 points, with the gap driven primarily by superior Drivable Area Compliance (97.3 vs. 98.5/97.4) and balanced performance across all sub-metrics. On NAVSIM v1, HAD (90.2 PDMS) edges out DriveSuprim (89.9) and WoTE (88.3), with notable gains in Ego Progress (87.4 vs. 86.7/81.9) and TTC (97.5 vs. 93.6/94.9). On the closed-loop HUGSIM benchmark, HAD-L (47.5 RC, 30.8 HD-Score) surpasses ZTRS (42.6 RC, 28.9 HD-Score) by 4.9 and 1.9 points, with the advantage widening dramatically in Extreme scenarios (39.1 vs. 21.9 RC). The camera-only variant HAD-L remains within 0.1–0.3 EPDMS of the full HAD model, confirming robustness to modality reduction. Notably, HAD's advantage over DiffusionDriveV2 (85.5 EPDMS) is 3.1 points, attributed to the metric-decoupled RL optimization and structure-preserved trajectory expansion replacing the single coupled reward and Gaussian noise perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAD builds directly on the Transfuser backbone for sensor fusion and the anchored-diffusion paradigm introduced by DiffusionDrive, but departs from both in key ways. Where DiffusionDrive and its successor DiffusionDriveV2 perform single-stage denoising over a large anchor set with Gaussian perturbations and a single coupled RL reward, HAD introduces a two-stage hierarchical diffusion (coarse intention selection followed by local refinement), replaces Gaussian noise with structure-preserving polar-coordinate expansion, and decouples RL into per-metric optimization heads (MDPO). The offline reward retrieval scheme further distinguishes HAD from prior RL-for-driving work that relies on expensive online simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× training speedup from offline reward retrieval (64.4 h → 13.6 h) makes structured RL training for end-to-end planners far more accessible, and the hierarchical decomposition addresses a fundamental optimization bottleneck that single-stage scoring-selection methods face. The ablation showing that adding safety/RL supervision in the first (global) stage actually degrades performance is a useful negative result, suggesting that complex reward signals are too noisy for the full unstructured action space. Open questions include whether the fixed 8192-trajectory vocabulary for reward retrieval generalizes to truly out-of-distribution scenarios, and whether the hierarchical approach extends naturally to longer-horizon or multi-agent planning settings.
