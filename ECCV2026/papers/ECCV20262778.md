## Problem

Existing weight-space generative methods such as SANE uniformly model the empirical distribution of a model zoo, allocating equal modeling capacity to weak, partially converged, and strong checkpoints alike. This raises the question of how a generator can exploit the full diversity of a model zoo while preferentially sampling from its higher-performing regions, and whether the resulting weights preserve robust, functionally interpretable internal structure beyond aggregate accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262778/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a model zoo of checkpoints (e.g., ~12k-parameter CIFAR-10 CNNs, ~5k-parameter MNIST CNNs, or ~53k-parameter CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 classifier heads), each standardized, aligned to a common reference via Git Re-Basin channel-permutation matching, tokenized into fixed-length sequences, and encoded by a Transformer autoencoder into compact latent codes. At inference, the sole input is a Gaussian noise vector z_T ~ N(0, I) in latent space; the output is a decoded, detokenized, and inverse-standardized set of functional network weights, plus (as an analysis tool) class-specific decision pathways extracted from the diffusion U-Net's cross-layer connectivity combined with Captum attributions.

## Method

TiltDiff builds on SANE's preprocessing pipeline and Transformer autoencoder to obtain latent weight representations, then trains a U-Net latent diffusion model with a performance-tilted denoising objective: the standard noise-prediction loss ‖ε − ε_ψ(z_t, t)‖² is multiplied by a per-sample importance weight w_i derived from normalized validation accuracy via either an exponential exp(λv_i) or sigmoid σ(β(v_i − τ)) function, normalized to unit mean. This implicitly tilts the learned latent distribution toward stronger regions of weight space without requiring explicit sampling from the tilted density. The approach is further complemented by validation-based data selection (retaining only the top-K% of checkpoints), which the ablations show provides the largest individual gain, with accuracy weighting adding a complementary improvement on top. For interpretability, the paper fixes a diffusion timestep, extracts multi-block U-Net features, constructs a cross-layer unit-association graph, and uses Captum attributions to trace class-specific decision pathways whose causal importance is verified by selective pathway masking and whose functional correspondence is traced across independently generated models.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (10% retained checkpoints), the best TiltDiff variant (TiltDiff_Sig) achieves 61.54% Top-1 and 60.76% Top-3 test accuracy, surpassing SANE_Sub (57.80% / 56.79%). On MNIST CNNs, TiltDiff reaches 86.68–88.28% Top-3 versus 85.84–86.57% for SANE_Sub, and on CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 classifier heads the gap widens to 73.82–74.63% vs. 69.29–69.89%. As fine-tuning initializations, TiltDiff weights start at 88.0±0.4% (MNIST) and 60.8±0.6% (CIFAR-10), the highest among all compared methods, and reach 91.3±0.4% and 69.6±0.1% after 25 fine-tuning epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is SANE_Sub (SANE trained on the accuracy-ranked retained subset). TiltDiff variants outperform SANE_Sub in Top-1 accuracy at every retained fraction on CIFAR-10, with the largest margin at 10% retention (61.54% vs. 57.80%, a +3.74-point gain for TiltDiff_Sig). On MNIST, the best TiltDiff variant leads by 0.7–2.4 points in Top-3 accuracy (e.g., 88.28% vs. 86.57% at 20% retention). The advantage is most pronounced on CIFAR-100 classifier heads, where TiltDiff achieves 73.82–74.63% Top-3 versus 69.29–69.89% for SANE_Sub, a gap of roughly 4–5 points. In fine-tuning experiments, TiltDiff provides the strongest initialization on both MNIST (88.0% vs. 86.7% for SANE_Sub) and CIFAR-10 (60.8% vs. 57.9%), and maintains the best accuracy after 1 and 25 fine-tuning epochs on CIFAR-10 (66.5% and 69.6%), though D2NWG overtakes it on MNIST after 5 epochs (92.5% vs. 89.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

TiltDiff builds directly on SANE's scalable weight-tokenization and Transformer autoencoder pipeline for encoding model checkpoints into a compact latent space, and on the latent diffusion U-Net framework of Rombach et al. for generative modeling in that space. The key departure is the performance-tilted denoising objective: whereas prior weight-space generators (SANE, Hyper-Representations, diffusion-based parameter generation) model the empirical checkpoint distribution uniformly, TiltDiff reweights the diffusion loss by normalized validation accuracy to bias sampling toward stronger regions of weight space. The interpretability analysis additionally draws on Tang et al.'s emergent-correspondence framework for diffusion U-Net features and Captum attributions to trace class-specific decision pathways in generated models, a direction not previously explored in the weight-generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Performance-tilted generation addresses a practical gap: model zoos contain checkpoints of widely varying quality, and uniform modeling wastes generative capacity on weak or undertrained regions. The pathway-correspondence finding—that independently generated models with substantially different raw weights share structurally aligned class-specific decision pathways—suggests that diffusion-based weight generation captures functional organization beyond mere parameter interpolation. However, all experiments use small networks (5k–53k parameters) on CIFAR-10/100 and MNIST, and the tilt is a simple scalar weighting; open questions include scalability to large architectures, finer-grained or class-conditional tilting, and whether the pathway correspondence persists at scale or under distribution shift.
