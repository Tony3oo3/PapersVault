## Problem

Existing 3DGS-to-mesh methods extract meshes from all Gaussians holistically without distinguishing objects from background, producing bloated meshes unsuitable for per-object editing. When scenes are segmented into individual objects, the resulting per-object Gaussian sets contain numerous outliers far from true surfaces and exhibit highly non-uniform density—irregularities that cause surface holes and artifacts, fundamentally preventing high-quality mesh extraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262565/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained 3DGS scene (millions of anisotropic Gaussians with positions, covariances, opacities, and SH coefficients) along with multi-view RGB images for photometric supervision and segmentation masks (from SAGA or SAM2) for object identification. It outputs per-object 3D meshes extracted via marching tetrahedra at the o=0.5 isosurface of the occupancy field, which are then combined into a complete scene mesh; the optimized Gaussians also support real-time novel-view rendering at over 100 FPS.

## Method

MagnetGS-Mesh is a two-stage geometry-aligned pipeline. Stage 1 (Enhanced Occupancy Learning) extends MILo's occupancy field with five synergistic regularization losses—binary entropy, spatial smoothness, opacity-occupancy consistency, boundary gradient maximization, and temporal EMA stabilization—that sharpen ambiguous occupancy values (0.3–0.7) into crisp binary boundaries, enabling reliable surface-outlier separation. Stage 2 introduces the Adaptive Local Normal Consistency (ALNC) Loss, the core contribution: for each outlier Gaussian, it estimates local surface density to adaptively select neighborhood size k (small k=3 in dense regions for detail preservation, large k=15 in sparse regions for noise robustness), computes the averaged surface normal from those k nearest surface points via PCA, and penalizes misalignment between the outlier's relocation direction and this averaged normal. Optimization proceeds in two phases—first 30% of iterations apply aggressive proximity loss to pull outliers toward surfaces, then the remaining 70% combine photometric, reduced-weight proximity, and ALNC losses to guide outliers along geometrically coherent paths into sparse regions, producing a uniform Gaussian distribution suitable for artifact-free mesh extraction.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, MagnetGS-Mesh achieves the best PSNR (25.68 dB), SSIM (0.766), and LPIPS (0.312) among all compared methods, outperforming MILo by 1.59 dB in PSNR and 0.077 in SSIM. On LERF, it achieves the highest PSNR (19.85 dB) and SSIM (0.7516), though MILo retains a slight LPIPS advantage (0.2975 vs. 0.3088). In terms of storage, the method produces the most compact meshes (0.3M vertices, 12.4 MB on LERF), reducing file size by 27% over MILo and 83% over GOF. On DTU, it achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.66 without cell culling (competitive with MILo's 0.68), and on Tanks & Temples it attains the highest mean F1 score (0.51) versus MILo (0.49) and GOF (0.46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagnetGS-Mesh outperforms all five baselines on PSNR and SSIM across both Mip-NeRF 360 and LERF. Against the closest competitor MILo [14], it gains +1.59 dB PSNR and +0.077 SSIM on Mip-NeRF 360, and +0.92 dB PSNR and +0.070 SSIM on LERF, while MILo retains a marginal LPIPS edge on LERF (0.2975 vs. 0.3088). The gap over GOF [50] is substantial: +4.90 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 and +1.45 dB on LERF, with GOF also producing 5.7× more vertices (1.8M vs. 0.3M). On DTU, PGSR [6] achieves the best Chamfer distance (0.52) but requires 0.5 h training versus 28 min for MagnetGS-Mesh (0.66 without culling), and all other methods (MILo 0.68, GOF 0.74, 2DGS 0.80) are worse. On Tanks & Temples, MagnetGS-Mesh leads with mean F1 0.51, narrowly beating MILo (0.49) and clearly surpassing GOF (0.46), 2DGS (0.30), and SuGaR (0.19), though it trails MILo on the Caterpillar scene (0.38 vs. 0.39). In storage, the method is the most compact at 12.4 MB (LERF), versus 17.1 MB for MILo, 71.8 MB for GOF, and 518.6 MB for 2DGS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MILo's mesh-in-the-loop framework by introducing an object-wise reconstruction pipeline that segments scenes (via SAGA) before per-object mesh extraction, addressing a limitation shared by all prior 3DGS-to-mesh methods (SuGaR, 2DGS, GOF, MILo) that process all Gaussians holistically. The core technical departure is the ALNC Loss, which generalizes fixed-neighborhood normal consistency constraints (as in GeoGaussian and GOF) into a density-adaptive mechanism that adjusts neighborhood size based on local surface density. The two-stage architecture—Enhanced Occupancy Learning for crisp segmentation boundaries followed by ALNC-guided outlier relocation—represents a new pipeline structure not present in prior scene-level mesh extraction approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Object-wise mesh extraction from 3DGS is a practical bottleneck for downstream applications like per-object editing, animation, and relighting, and this paper provides a working solution with measurable storage savings (up to 27%) while maintaining or improving rendering quality. The finding that cell culling post-processing actually degrades Chamfer distance (0.66→0.71) because ALNC has already consolidated outliers onto surfaces is a useful diagnostic insight for practitioners. A key open question is whether the approach generalizes to object-centric scenarios (single objects, close-range capture), which the authors acknowledge as future work, and whether the density-adaptive mechanism remains beneficial when surface coverage is already uniform.
