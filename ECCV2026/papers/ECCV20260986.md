## Problem

Extending 3D Gaussian Splatting to panoramic images introduces severe spatially-varying distortions from equirectangular projection, which existing methods address only indirectly through joint camera-parameter optimization or projection adjustments. No prior work explicitly encodes the per-pixel distortion pattern of the panoramic imaging model and injects it into the Gaussian representation learning process, leaving rendering artifacts in highly distorted regions (e.g., near the poles).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260986/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of equirectangular panoramic images from a scene (2048×1024 for 360Roam indoor scenes, 1920×960 for Ricoh360 outdoor scenes) together with a sparse point cloud from COLMAP SfM as input. The point cloud is voxelized (voxel size 0.001 for indoor, 0.005 for outdoor) to initialize anchors, each spawning N=10 Gaussians. The output is a set of learned 3D Gaussian representations (position, covariance, opacity, SH color) that can be rasterized via a panoramic 3D Gaussian rasterizer to render novel-view equirectangular images.

## Method

PaD-GS constructs a closed-form distortion map Ω from the panoramic imaging geometry, where each pixel's value equals 1−cos²θ and thus encodes the local area distortion of the equirectangular projection. A whole-to-partial strategy then imposes this map on Gaussian learning: (1) a Distortion-Aware Gaussian Decoding module feeds each anchor's learnable feature, its distortion degree Ω, relative camera distance, and viewing direction into three MLPs to decode full Gaussian attributes (opacity, color, covariance), injecting distortion information at the whole-representation level; (2) a Distortion-Aware Opacity Modulation module further multiplies each Gaussian's decoded opacity by (1−τ·Ω) to suppress unreliable contributions from highly distorted regions during α-blending. Training uses a weighted L1+SSIM loss with a distortion-aware weight map M=cos θ so that each pixel contributes proportionally to its true spherical surface area, and the model is optimized for 30K iterations with periodic Gaussian densification and pruning.

## Results

On the 360Roam dataset (11 indoor scenes), PaD-GS achieves average SSIM 0.821, PSNR 26.02 dB, and LPIPS 0.227, outperforming the strongest baseline SPaGS (SSIM 0.788, PSNR 24.90, LPIPS 0.288) by +0.033 SSIM and +1.12 dB PSNR. On the Ricoh360 dataset (12 outdoor scenes), PaD-GS reaches average SSIM 0.845, PSNR 26.54 dB, and LPIPS 0.184, again leading SPaGS (SSIM 0.835, PSNR 26.20, LPIPS 0.192). PaD-GS wins on the majority of individual scenes across both datasets and all three metrics, with the largest margins over NeRF-based EgoNeRF and projection-only methods like ODGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaD-GS outperforms all five baselines on average across both datasets. On 360Roam, it leads the closest competitor SPaGS by +0.033 SSIM, +1.12 dB PSNR, and −0.061 LPIPS, and beats OmniGS by +0.022 SSIM and +1.18 dB PSNR. On Ricoh360, the margin over SPaGS narrows to +0.010 SSIM and +0.34 dB PSNR, but PaD-GS still wins on 10 of 12 scenes. OP43DGS suffers Out-Of-Memory failures on 7 of 12 Ricoh360 outdoor scenes, making it non-competitive there. EgoNeRF (NeRF-based) is consistently the weakest 3DGS competitor, trailing PaD-GS by ~0.10 SSIM and ~2.3 dB PSNR on 360Roam. ODGS, which relies on geometrically grounded omnidirectional rasterization without explicit distortion modeling, lags PaD-GS by ~0.08 SSIM on 360Roam, confirming that projection-level fixes alone are insufficient to counter equirectangular distortion.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaD-GS extends the 3DGS-based panoramic rendering line (OmniGS, SPaGS, ODGS) by departing from the dominant strategy of optimizing projection geometry or jointly calibrating camera parameters. Instead, it introduces a pre-computed distortion map derived directly from the equirectangular projection's area-ratio (1/cos θ) and feeds it as an explicit conditioning signal into MLP-based Gaussian decoding and opacity modulation. This 'whole-to-partial' use of a geometric prior is conceptually distinct from prior methods that treat distortion as an implicit optimization target, and the distortion map itself is the first such artifact explicitly incorporated into Gaussian representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a clean, geometrically grounded mechanism for handling equirectangular distortion that is easy to interpret and potentially transferable to other projection models. However, the MLP-based decoding introduces a notable efficiency cost (training ~2× slower and rendering ~2× slower than OmniGS/SPaGS), which limits practical adoption in real-time applications. An open question is whether the distortion map's benefit persists when combined with the faster projection-optimized rasterizers, and whether the approach generalizes beyond equirectangular to other omnidirectional projections (fisheye, cubemap).
