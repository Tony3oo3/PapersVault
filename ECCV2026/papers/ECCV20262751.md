## Problem

Streaming video understanding in MLLMs is severely limited by the linear explosion of visual tokens over infinite-length streams, causing Out-of-Memory errors or catastrophic forgetting under fixed GPU budgets. Existing retention and memory management strategies rely on uniform sampling, low-level physical metrics (cosine similarity, optical flow), or passive cache eviction, which lack intrinsic semantic awareness and fail to preserve transient yet critical semantic transitions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262751/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system ingests streaming video frames at 1 FPS alongside a natural language query, extracting global features via a frozen DINOv2-small encoder. It maintains a fixed-capacity memory queue of 10–20 frames (dynamically varying), where retained frames are stored either at the base model's native high resolution (Clear Memory) or downsampled to 224×224 (Blurred Memory), and outputs a text answer conditioned on the curated visual context. The strict token budget N_max is enforced via FIFO eviction of the oldest tokens when the queue overflows.

## Method

CurveStream is a training-free, plug-and-play vision encoder that replaces uniform frame sampling with a curvature-aware hierarchical memory pipeline. A Curvature-Aware Scorer (CAS) computes a real-time Curvature Score by combining first-order motion variation (1 − cosine similarity between consecutive frame features) and second-order geometric curvature (angular deviation between successive displacement vectors in the latent feature manifold). A Hierarchical Visual Memory Management (HVMM) module then applies an online K-Sigma dynamic threshold (g = μ + kσ, updated via exponential moving average of historical curvature scores) to adaptively route each incoming frame into Clear Memory (high-res, for significant semantic shifts), Blurred Memory (low-res, for intermediate transitions), or Discard. This geometric insight—that high-curvature regions in feature trajectories correspond to critical global semantic transitions—enables the system to allocate scarce token budget precisely to the most informative frames while maintaining a constant memory footprint under non-stationary streaming conditions.

## Results

On StreamingBench, CurveStream achieves 84.00% accuracy with Qwen2.5-VL-7B (a 10.69% absolute gain over the 1fps baseline) and 85.56% with Qwen3-VL-8B (↑12.36%). On OVOBench, the same Qwen2.5-VL-7B configuration reaches 73.48% (↑13.58%), surpassing GPT-4o (64.46%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (69.32%). On offline benchmarks, CurveStream yields a 5.82% gain on EgoSchema (64.29% vs. 58.47%) and a 1.03% gain on MVBench (66.03% vs. 65.00%) with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, while showing a minor 1.55% drop on VideoMME due to the strict constant-memory constraint over hours-long videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CurveStream consistently outperforms all training-free streaming baselines: on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it beats HERMES by 4.56% on StreamingBench (84.00% vs. 79.44%) and by 4.50% on OVOBench (73.48% vs. 68.98%), and beats FreshMem (on Qwen2-VL-7B) by 6.84% on StreamingBench (81.04% vs. 74.20%) and 4.06% on OVOBench (70.73% vs. 66.67%). Against proprietary models, CurveStream with Qwen2.5-VL-7B surpasses GPT-4o by 10.72% on StreamingBench and 9.02% on OVOBench, and exceeds Gemini 1.5 Pro by 8.31% and 4.16% respectively. Among open-source online MLLMs, it leads StreamForest-7B by 6.74% on StreamingBench (84.00% vs. 77.26%) and 12.28% on OVOBench (73.48% vs. 61.20%), and TimeChat-Online-7B by 8.64% and 11.58% respectively. On offline benchmarks, CurveStream improves EgoSchema by 5.82% over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline but shows a slight 1.55% drop on VideoMME (62.97% vs. 64.52%), attributed to the strict constant-memory FIFO eviction over hours-long contexts. The method also outperforms ReKV by 5.90% on StreamingBench and 13.24% on OVOBench when built on LLaVA-OneVision-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

CurveStream carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: visual token retention (uniform sampling, keyframe selection, query-driven retrieval) and streaming memory management (KV cache eviction, external structured memory). Rather than building on any single prior method, it repurposes a classical differential-geometry concept—curvature of a trajectory in feature space—as a lightweight, training-free proxy for semantic transition intensity, departing from the field's reliance on first-order metrics (cosine similarity, optical flow) or passive FIFO/attention-based eviction. The K-Sigma dynamic threshold and hierarchical Clear/Blurred/Discard routing are novel engineering choices that adaptively calibrate sensitivity to non-stationary streams, but the underlying geometric insight is a natural extension of change-point detection ideas from signal processing into the MLLM video context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single training-free module that plugs into any frozen MLLM and consistently delivers ~10% absolute gains on streaming benchmarks while keeping GPU memory flat, making long-horizon video understanding feasible on commodity hardware. The slight accuracy drop on very long offline videos (VideoMME) reveals an inherent trade-off between preserving fine-grained global context and retaining critical transition points under a fixed token budget—an open question for future work. The authors note that extending this geometric memory paradigm to embodied AI (autonomous navigation, robotic manipulation) is a natural next step, and whether the FIFO eviction policy can be replaced by content-aware prioritization without reintroducing the blurring problem remains unresolved.
