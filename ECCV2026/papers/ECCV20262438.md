## Problem

Adapting MLLMs to hour-long videos is bottlenecked by context window limits, and existing efficiency heuristics (sparse sampling, uniform pooling, token merging) are query-agnostic: they discard transient decisive moments, blur fine-grained evidence in query-critical segments, and waste representational bandwidth on irrelevant backgrounds. No prior method performs query-aware compression that dynamically allocates bandwidth based on user intent while preserving temporal causality across the full video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262438/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an hour-long video (up to 2048 frames sampled at 2 FPS) and a text query, partitioning the video into 4- or 8-frame segments. Each segment is compressed into a variable number of memory tokens (0.5–16 tokens/frame dynamically), with explicit temporal tags (e.g., <t=2.0s>) prepended to preserve causal order. The global LLM then auto-regressively generates a text response under a strict visual token budget (4K or 8K total), though empirical profiling shows actual consumption frequently falls well below these ceilings.

## Method

Tempo is a two-level 6B architecture: a Qwen3-VL-2B SVLM acts as a local temporal compressor that performs query-conditioned cross-modal distillation (visual tokens + query + learnable memory tokens under causal attention), and a Qwen3-LM-4B global decoder synthesizes the compressed sequence into a response. At inference, Adaptive Token Allocation (ATA) exploits two properties of the SVLM: (1) a zero-shot relevance prior extracted via a single O(1) logit-difference projection on Yes/No tokens, and (2) semantic front-loading, where salient evidence concentrates in the earliest memory tokens, enabling lossless head truncation. ATA dynamically assigns per-segment budgets from k_min=4 (temporal anchors preserving causality) to k_max=128, distributing residual capacity proportionally to relevance scores. Training uses a four-stage progressive curriculum (modality alignment → pre-training → broad SFT → long-context SFT) with no auxiliary compression losses; the fixed k_max capacity serves as a structural bottleneck that forces the compressor to pack query-relevant evidence.

## Results

Tempo (6B, 0.5–16 tokens/frame) achieves 52.7 on LVBench (4101s) under a strict 4K visual budget and 67.8 on Video-MME Overall, outperforming the strongest specialized long-video MLLM (VideoChat-Flash, 7B, 16 tokens/frame) by 4.5 and 2.5 points respectively. On the extreme-long LVBench, Tempo surpasses GPT-4o (30.8) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (33.1) by 19.6–21.9 points despite using a fraction of their context budget. Empirically, ATA compresses hour-long videos to an average of only 2.8–4.3 tokens/frame in practice—well below the 16-token ceiling—demonstrating intent-driven sparsity rather than greedy context filling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest specialized long-video MLLM, VideoChat-Flash (7B, 16 tok/frame), Tempo (6B, 0.5–16 tok/frame) wins on all four benchmarks: +4.5 on LVBench (52.7 vs 48.2), +2.5 on Video-MME Overall (67.8 vs 65.3), +0.9 on MLVU (75.6 vs 74.7), and +0.4 on LongVideoBench (65.1 vs 64.7 under 8K). On the extreme-long LVBench, Tempo's 52.7 dwarfs proprietary baselines GPT-4o (30.8) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (33.1) by 19.6–21.9 points, and also exceeds Molmo2 (52.8, 8B, 83 tok/frame) by a narrow margin while using ~28× fewer tokens per frame. Among general open-source MLLMs, Tempo trails only Qwen3-VL-8B (58.0 on LVBench, ≤640 tok/frame) and Molmo2 (52.8, 83 tok/frame), both of which consume far more visual tokens. On Video-MME Long (2386s), Tempo (57.8) outperforms LongVU (59.5 is actually higher—correction: LongVU scores 59.5), VideoChat-Flash (55.4), and Storm (53.4), though LongVU edges it by 1.7 points. The 'less is more' effect is notable: Tempo's 4K budget (52.7 on LVBench, 57.8 on V-MME Long) matches or exceeds its 8K budget (52.3, 57.0), suggesting tighter bottlenecks actively filter distractors.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tempo departs from the dominant paradigm of query-agnostic visual compression (sparse sampling, uniform spatiotemporal pooling, token merging) by casting token reduction as an early cross-modal distillation process conditioned on the user query. It also diverges from prior query-aware architectures (e.g., LongVU, BIMBA) that rely on disjoint auxiliary modules to score or select segments, instead repurposing a single Small Vision-Language Model (Qwen3-VL-2B) to simultaneously perform compression and extract a zero-shot relevance routing signal in one forward pass. The Adaptive Token Allocation mechanism is novel in combining a contrastive linear budget with O(1) head truncation, exploiting an empirically observed semantic front-loading property of causal attention that prior token-merging or pooling methods do not leverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a compact 6B model with 0.5–16 tokens/frame achieves competitive or superior performance to much larger and proprietary systems on hour-long video benchmarks, offering a concrete recipe for deploying long-video understanding under strict memory budgets. The 'less is more' observation (4K budget matching or exceeding 8K) and the finding that Tempo routinely operates below its allocated budget suggest that intent-driven sparsity, not context padding, is the key to long-form understanding. Open questions include whether the zero-shot relevance prior can be sharpened via reinforcement learning, how to adapt the single-pass compression to multi-turn dialogue with shifting intents, and whether autoregressive, reasoning-driven token generation can replace the fixed memory-token bottleneck without prohibitive latency.
