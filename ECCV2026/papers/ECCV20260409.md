## Problem

RGBT trackers deployed on edge devices must process downsampled inputs (1/2 or 1/4 resolution) to fit bandwidth and compute budgets, but this causes severe semantic loss, spatial ambiguity, and localization drift that existing methods do not address. No prior framework bridges this resolution gap while guaranteeing zero additional inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes template and search region patches from paired RGB and thermal images, downsampled via bilinear interpolation to 128×128 (1/2) or 64×64 (1/4) of the original 256×256 resolution. It outputs a single bounding box (x, y, w, h) and target center coordinates via a standard MLP tracking head. During training, full-resolution teacher features and an intermediate 1/2-resolution teacher (for 1/4 scenarios) provide supervision, but all auxiliary modules are discarded at inference so the student runs at the same parameter count and FLOPs as the unmodified baseline.

## Method

GAP-Track builds on a DropMAE-initialized ViT backbone and introduces three training-time-only modules. First, an Auxiliary Generative Reconstruction (AGR) task upsamples the student's low-res features, applies random masking, and trains a lightweight residual CNN generator to reconstruct teacher-level features, forcing the backbone to encode fine-grained structural cues lost by downsampling. Second, a Polar-Geometric Sensitivity (PGS) loss decomposes localization into a variance-weighted IoU scale term and a polar-coordinate location term (radial ratio + angular difference), whose 1/d gradient term prevents the vanishing-gradient dead zone that plagues L2/IoU losses on tiny targets. Third, a Hierarchical Knowledge Distillation (HKD) strategy inserts a high-performing 1/2-resolution model as an intermediate teacher when distilling to 1/4 resolution, providing a smoother feature-alignment trajectory than direct full-to-quarter transfer. All three modules are bypassed at inference, yielding zero overhead while improving PR by up to +13.1% over the baseline at quarter resolution.

## Results

On the LasHeR dataset at half resolution (128×128), GAP-Track achieves 67.6% PR, 52.9% SR, and 63.3% NPR, outperforming all baselines by at least +1.1% NPR. At the extreme quarter resolution (64×64), it maintains 57.2% PR, 43.7% SR, and 52.7% NPR, surpassing the runner-up (MPLT) by +1.5% PR and +1.0% NPR. On RGBT234, GAP-Track leads at both half (84.2% PR / 62.5% SR) and quarter (75.5% PR / 54.5% SR) resolution. At full resolution, GAP-Track is competitive but slightly trails CKD (71.8% vs. 73.2% PR on LasHeR), confirming no adverse side-effects on standard inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAP-Track is built on top of CKD [20] as its base architecture and consistently outperforms it at reduced resolutions: at 1/4 resolution on LasHeR, GAP-Track improves PR from 44.1% to 57.2% (+13.1%) and SR from 35.3% to 43.7% (+8.4%), while at full resolution CKD retains a small edge (73.2% vs. 71.8% PR). Against MPLT [22], the strongest competitor at quarter resolution, GAP-Track leads by +1.5% PR and +1.0% NPR on LasHeR and by +0.4% PR / +1.4% SR on RGBT234. TBSI [11] and CAFormer [33] are both surpassed at half and quarter resolution on all three LasHeR metrics, with margins of +1.7% PR (vs. TBSI) and +3.3% PR (vs. CAFormer) at 1/4. In the generalization study (Table 3), integrating GAP-Track's training-time modules into SUTrack [2] yields consistent gains of +0.7% SR (RGBT), +1.1% AUC (RGB on LaSOT), and +0.9% AUC (RGBE on VisEvent) at half resolution, confirming model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAP-Track builds directly on CKD (Coupled Knowledge Distillation) as its base tracker, extending the knowledge-distillation paradigm in multi-modal tracking (CMD, CKD) from same-resolution feature alignment to a hierarchical, cross-resolution setting where an intermediate 1/2-resolution teacher bridges the gap to a 1/4-resolution student. The Auxiliary Generative Reconstruction module repurposes the masked-reconstruction idea from ViTKD and MAE, but reframes it as a cross-resolution distillation bridge rather than single-resolution self-supervision, targeting tracking-critical structural details rather than general pixel appearance. The Polar-Geometric Sensitivity loss draws on scale-and-location sensitivity concepts from infrared small-target detection, adapting them to the tracking localization problem. The key conceptual departure from prior RGBT trackers (MPLT, TBSI, CAFormer) is that they all assume fixed high-resolution inputs, whereas GAP-Track explicitly optimizes for the degraded-resolution regime that edge deployment necessitates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead design (all modules discarded at test time) makes this a drop-in training recipe for any existing tracker, and the demonstrated generalization to SUTrack across RGBT, pure RGB, and RGB-Event modalities suggests broad practical utility for bandwidth-constrained surveillance and autonomous systems. The +13% PR gain at 1/4 resolution over the baseline is practically significant for edge deployment where 64×64 inputs are the realistic operating point. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even lower resolutions (1/8), whether temporal dynamics (acknowledged in the conclusion) are needed for long-sequence robustness, and whether the hierarchical distillation strategy generalizes beyond the specific CKD backbone to arbitrary architectures without retraining the intermediate teacher.
