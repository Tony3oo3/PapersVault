## Problem

Existing INR-based video codecs face a fundamental scalability gap: lightweight models achieve rate scalability with fixed complexity but deliver inferior compression, while high-performance models require different configurations per quality level, causing decoding complexity to grow with bitrate. No unified architecture exists that maintains consistent computational cost across a wide bitrate/quality range while matching state-of-the-art compression performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260277/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes full HD (1920×1080) video sequences of 120–600 frames and, through per-instance overfitting, produces a compressed bitstream encoding quantized feature grid parameters and network layer weights. Decoding reconstructs video frames via a single forward pass of the INR at each coordinate, with four fixed-complexity variants (S1–S4) spanning 7.3–357.7 kMACs/pixel, each covering a wide bitrate range without changing model size. The largest variant encodes 600-frame 1080p sequences on a 24 GB consumer GPU.

## Method

NVRC++ decouples model complexity from reconstruction quality by using multiple high-resolution feature grids whose parameters carry the representational burden, while the neural network (HiNeRV++) remains lightweight. An in-parameter hierarchical B-frame coding structure partitions grid parameters into temporal slices with inter-slice dependencies, combined with non-conditional quantization and random sampling to enable efficient overfitting of long sequences without caching all frame activations. A multi-dimensional grid entropy model exploits scale, temporal, and spatial priors (1–4 coding steps) to compress the high-dimensional grid parameters at high speed. HiNeRV++ further improves the INR by feeding raw neighboring grid slices as multi-reference context alongside interpolated features into multiple network stages, and replacing the highest-resolution blocks with a pixel-shuffle output layer. A feature grid masking schedule (annealed dropout on high-resolution grids) prevents low-bitrate degradation by forcing early reliance on coarser grids.

## Results

On the UVG dataset, NVRC++ (S4) achieves −74.82% BD-rate (PSNR) and −81.88% (MS-SSIM) relative to x265 (veryslow), while NVRC++ (S3) reaches −71.20% (PSNR) and −81.25% (MS-SSIM) at only 92.5 kMACs/pixel. On MCL-JCV, S3 achieves −55.90% (PSNR) and −70.80% (MS-SSIM). A key advantage is constant decoding complexity per scale: S3 decodes at 73.4 FPS (7.6× faster than NVRC at comparable quality), S2 at 162.9 FPS, and S1 at 365.0 FPS, all without custom CUDA kernels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA INR codec NVRC, NVRC++ (S4) marginally improves UVG PSNR (−74.82% vs −73.74%) while decoding 3.5× faster, and S3 matches or exceeds NVRC on MS-SSIM (−81.25% vs −80.65% on UVG) at 7.6× the decoding speed. At mid-complexity, S3 (92.5 kMACs/px) substantially outperforms VTM (−71.20% vs −62.81% UVG PSNR) and DCVC-RT (−71.20% vs −65.56%), and S2 (25.1 kMACs/px) beats HM by ~17 points in UVG PSNR while rivaling DCVC-RT at low bitrates. The ultra-lightweight S1 (7.3 kMACs/px) comfortably surpasses x265 (−40.06% vs 0%) and the lightweight C3 codec (−40.06% vs −21.91%), while approaching HiNeRV in MS-SSIM. A critical differentiator is scalability: NVRC and HiNeRV require varying model sizes across quality levels (173–931 and 87–346 kMACs/px respectively), whereas each NVRC++ scale maintains fixed complexity across its entire bitrate range, enabling predictable hardware deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

NVRC++ is a direct architectural extension of the authors' own NVRC [23] and HiNeRV [22] line of INR-based video codecs, inheriting the overfitting-based compression pipeline and rate-distortion framework. Its key conceptual departure is decoupling model complexity from reconstruction quality by offloading representational burden from network layers to high-resolution feature grids, whereas prior INR codecs (NVRC, HiNeRV) required different model configurations—and thus different decoding costs—for different quality levels. The paper also borrows structural ideas from conventional video coding (hierarchical B-frame in-parameter coding) and from autoencoder-based neural compression (multi-prior entropy models with spatial, temporal, and scale contexts), synthesizing them into a unified overfitted INR framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving fixed decoding complexity (7–360 kMACs/pixel) across wide bitrate ranges with real-time decoding speeds (up to 365 fps at S1, 73 fps at S3), NVRC++ addresses the most critical practical barrier to deploying INR-based video codecs: the inability to guarantee bounded inference cost. The 7.6× speedup over NVRC at comparable BD-rate makes the approach competitive with autoencoder-based codecs like DCVC-RT in the mid-complexity regime. However, the encoding side remains slow due to the per-instance overfitting process (0.005–0.020 FPS), and the approach has only been validated on 1080p sequences of up to 600 frames, leaving open questions about scalability to 4K content, longer sequences, and hardware-constrained deployment where the large grid parameter storage (up to 100M+ parameters) becomes a bottleneck.
