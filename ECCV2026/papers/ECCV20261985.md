## Problem

While VLMs and LLMs demonstrate strong general document understanding, structured interpretation of real-world medical report images—extracting itemized clinical fields and generating grounded patient-facing explanations—remains insufficiently benchmarked. Prior work has not systematically evaluated end-to-end visual extraction of structured lab/exam findings under the noisy, layout-heterogeneous conditions of real clinical documents (handheld photos, screenshots, PDFs with folds and occlusions).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261985/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 1,925 de-identified Chinese medical report images spanning diverse acquisition formats (handheld photos, mobile screenshots, electronic documents) and clinical departments, with no universal layout standard. For laboratory-style reports, models must output a JSON array of five-field tuples (name, value, unit, reference_range, is_abnormal), where is_abnormal is a derived label requiring value-to-range comparison rather than direct text copying. For examination reports, models produce free-form patient-facing explanations scored on factuality, interpretability, and reasoning quality. The objective protocol truncates outputs to Top-K items (K = ground-truth count) so that hallucinated entries displace correct ones, implicitly penalizing over-generation.

## Method

MedRepBench introduces a dual evaluation protocol: an objective field-level recall metric computed over five attributes with name-based item matching and fuzzy range matching, and an automated LLM-judge (DeepSeek-R1) scoring patient-facing explanations on a 3-point scale for factuality, interpretability, and reasoning. The benchmark compares end-to-end VLMs (InternVL2.5/3, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaMA-4) against OCR-assisted pipelines (OCR+LLM) to isolate visual perception errors from reasoning errors. As a practical alignment baseline, the authors apply GRPO with field-level recall as a non-differentiable reward on a LoRA-adapted (rank 128) InternVL3-8B, freezing the vision encoder and updating only LLM attention projections over 2 epochs on 800 curated samples, yielding +6% absolute recall over SFT and competitive performance with 17–32B baselines in the no-OCR setting.

## Results

In the no-OCR (end-to-end image-only) setting, Ours-GRPO (8B) achieves 79.45% average field-level recall, outperforming LLaMA-4-Maverick (78.60%, 17B) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (77.41%, 32B), and improving over its SFT baseline (73.31%) by +6.14%. In the OCR-assisted setting, Ours-GRPO reaches 87.41%, slightly surpassing InternVL3-8B (86.98%) and Qwen3-8B (83.90%). Across all VLMs, removing OCR input causes a 10–20% drop in field-level recall, with the abnormality flag field being the most affected (e.g., LLaMA-4-Maverick drops from 86.56% to 75.83%). On subjective interpretability, Ours-GRPO achieves 60.64% acceptability and 42.45% excellence rate, matching LLaMA-4-Maverick (42.74%) and exceeding InternVL3-38B (32.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaMA-4-Maverick (17B), Ours-GRPO (8B) wins in the no-OCR setting by 0.85 points (79.45% vs. 78.60%), demonstrating that targeted RL alignment can close the gap with a model more than twice its size. Against Qwen2.5-VL-32B (32B), Ours-GRPO leads by 2.04 points in no-OCR (79.45% vs. 77.41%), a notable margin given the 4× parameter difference. In the OCR-assisted setting, Ours-GRPO narrowly beats InternVL3-8B (87.41% vs. 86.98%) and substantially outperforms Qwen3-8B (83.90%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (83.89%), while InternVL2.5-8B lags far behind at 74.32%. The GRPO step itself contributes +6.14% over SFT in no-OCR and +5.04% in OCR-assisted, confirming that the benchmark's field-level recall provides a meaningful reward signal. On subjective interpretability, Ours-GRPO (42.45% excellence) is competitive with LLaMA-4-Maverick (42.74%) but trails Qwen2.5-VL-32B (51.40%), suggesting that RL optimization primarily benefits structured extraction over free-form explanation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of document understanding benchmarks (MMDocBench, BenchX) and medical VQA benchmarks (OmniMedVQA, GEMeX, BESTMVQA), but departs from both by targeting structured field extraction from noisy, real-world medical report images rather than clean synthetic templates or radiology images. It explicitly contrasts with medical report generation work (Argus, MedVAG) by focusing on interpretation—extracting structured findings and generating patient-facing explanations—rather than narrative summarization. The RL alignment component builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath), repurposing field-level recall as a non-differentiable reward signal for VLM optimization, while the evaluation framework draws on LLM-as-judge paradigms for subjective scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MedRepBench fills a concrete gap: no prior benchmark systematically evaluates end-to-end VLMs on structured extraction from noisy, heterogeneous real-world medical report images across diverse acquisition methods and layouts. The finding that removing OCR input causes a 10–20% recall drop across all models quantifies the remaining headroom for text-token-free VLMs, while the GRPO baseline showing an 8B model approaching 32B performance in the no-OCR setting suggests targeted RL alignment is a practical path forward. Key limitations include the Chinese-only corpus, exclusion of proprietary frontier models, and reliance on a single OCR system for ground-truth construction, leaving cross-lingual generalization and medical-specialized VLM evaluation as open questions.
