## Problem

Existing autonomous driving world models suffer from three coupled limitations: they model modalities (RGB, semantics, depth, occupancy) in isolation causing inter-modal misalignment, they condition on sparse action representations (waypoints, raw poses) that overfit to specific sensor calibrations and fail to transfer across camera rigs, and they lack intrinsic reward signals for closed-loop policy evaluation. OmniNWM addresses all three by unifying state generation, action control, and reward computation within a single probabilistic framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260070/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB frames (6-view panoramic at 224×400 to 448×800 resolution, 17 or 33 frames) along with a planning trajectory that is encoded as a parameter-free normalized panoramic Plücker ray-map rather than raw poses or waypoints. Outputs are jointly generated panoramic RGB video, pixel-aligned semantic segmentation maps, metric depth maps, and 3D semantic occupancy volumes, from which dense occupancy-grounded rewards (collision, drivable-area, and velocity penalties) are derived. The integrated OmniNWM-VLA agent additionally outputs dense trajectory tuples (x, y, θ) at 12 Hz for closed-loop planning.

## Method

OmniNWM employs a Panoramic Diffusion Transformer (PDiT) that jointly denoises RGB, colorized semantics, and metric depth within a shared 3D-VAE latent space (channel-wise concatenation), enforcing pixel-level cross-modal alignment through a single reverse-diffusion trajectory. For action control, a parameter-free Normalized Panoramic Plücker Ray-map projects all camera rays into a canonical reference frame via rigid transformation, decoupling ego-motion dynamics from sensor intrinsics and enabling zero-shot transfer across 3-view and 6-view rigs. A Structured Panoramic Forcing strategy injects decoupled temporal (frame-wise) and spatial (view-wise) noise during training, performing stochastic manifold thickening that stabilizes long-horizon autoregressive rollouts (25.22 FVD at 201 frames vs. 386.72 without). 3D semantic occupancy is lifted from the generated 2D observations via an EfficientNetB7 U-Net with 3D convolutional aggregation, and intrinsic dense rewards are computed directly from the occupancy voxel grid. A Tri-Modal Mamba-based Interpreter (Tri-MMI) feeds the VLA agent, which reasons over the multi-modal context to produce reactive trajectories that condition subsequent generation in a closed loop.

## Results

On nuScenes validation, OmniNWM achieves SOTA video generation quality with 5.45 FID and 23.63 FVD (17-frame, 224×400), outperforming the next-best volumetric-condition-free method DriveDreamer-2 (11.20 FID, 55.70 FVD) by over 2×. With normalized panoramic ray-maps, camera control accuracy reaches 0.16 rad RotErr and 1.18 m TransErr, reducing errors by >90% versus unnormalized conditioning. For 3D occupancy, the geometric lifting pipeline achieves 33.3 IoU / 19.8 mIoU, surpassing LiDAR-based discriminative baselines. Zero-shot transfer to nuPlan yields 9.51 FID, 79.24 FVD, and only 1.65 m TransErr, while baselines degrade to >7 m TransErr.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniNWM outperforms all 19 video-generation baselines in Table 2, with the closest competitor being DiST-4D (FVD 25.55 vs. 23.63) and UniScene (FID 6.45 vs. 5.45); notably, OmniNWM achieves these gains without requiring heavy volumetric input conditions (voxel grids or point clouds). For camera control (Table 3), the normalized panoramic ray-map encoding reduces TransErr from 9.75 m (unnormalized) to 1.18 m, a 87.9% improvement, and beats PosePilot+Vista (6.52 m) and UniScene (7.56 m) by 5–6×. In occupancy prediction (Table 4), OmniNWM (19.8 mIoU) surpasses the best generative baseline OccGen (14.5 mIoU) by 5.3 points and even exceeds LiDAR-based L-CONet (15.8 mIoU). For zero-shot generalization on nuPlan (Table 7), OmniNWM maintains 1.65 m TransErr while DiST-4D, UniScene, and Vista all exceed 7 m, confirming that the canonical Plücker encoding effectively eliminates geometric covariate shift. In closed-loop planning (Fig. 8), OmniNWM-VLA achieves 87.3% Scenario Pass Rate, outperforming Impromptu-VLA and Qwen-2.5-VL baselines under identical 12 Hz evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniNWM synthesizes and extends three distinct research threads: (1) driving world models (DriveDreamer, Vista, MagicDrive, UniScene) that previously handled single-modality RGB generation with sparse action signals; (2) camera-controlled video generation (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl, CamCo) that relied on raw extrinsics and overfit to specific sensor rigs; and (3) occupancy-based scene generation (OccScene, OccGen) that used heavy volumetric diffusion. The paper departs from prior work by jointly optimizing RGB, semantics, and depth in a single diffusion process (avoiding the conditional independence fallacy of modular pipelines), replacing raw pose/waypoint conditioning with normalized panoramic Plücker ray-maps, and deriving intrinsic rewards directly from generated 3D occupancy rather than relying on external black-box reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of state-action-reward in a single probabilistic framework addresses a long-standing fragmentation in driving world models, making closed-loop policy evaluation feasible without external reward networks. The zero-shot cross-rig generalization (0.28 rad RotErr on unseen nuPlan vs. >7 m TransErr for baselines) is practically significant for deploying world models across heterogeneous vehicle fleets. Open questions include whether the Nash equilibrium framing for interactive dynamics holds in adversarial or highly non-equilibrium scenarios, whether the 6-view panoramic constraint limits applicability to truly unbounded 360° environments, and how the framework scales to higher resolutions and longer horizons beyond 201 frames. The in-house dataset used for some evaluations is not publicly available, limiting full reproducibility.
