## Problem

Real-world concealed object segmentation requires jointly handling low-level image degradation and high-level semantic understanding, but these objectives inherently conflict: restoration prioritizes fine-grained texture fidelity while segmentation emphasizes semantic contrast. Existing coupled unfolding methods share parameters between the two tasks, causing gradient interference that compromises both, while two-stage pipelines forfeit inter-task feedback. The paper addresses this by proposing a nested architecture that lets each task optimize in its own subspace while enabling controlled mutual refinement.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (resized to 352×352) of a concealed object under unknown, arbitrary degradation (low-light, haze, low-resolution, or combined) and produces both a binary foreground segmentation mask and a restored clean image. No predefined degradation matrix or type label is required; the degradation operator is implicitly approximated by learnable residual convolutional operators. Multi-level deep features from a frozen or trainable encoder backbone (ResNet50, PVT-V2, DINOv2, or Res2Net50) are extracted and fed into the unfolding stages, and an optional DA-CLIP module provides degradation semantic descriptors to condition the restoration operators.

## Method

NUN adopts a DUN-in-DUN architecture where a degradation-resistant unfolding network (DeRUN) is nested inside each of K=4 stages of a segmentation-oriented unfolding network (SODUN). SODUN performs reversible foreground-background estimation via proximal gradient unfolding with visual state space (VSS) modules for non-local reasoning, while DeRUN performs blind restoration through proximal gradient unfolding with learnable residual convolutional operators that implicitly approximate the unknown degradation and its transpose. A bi-directional unfolding interaction (BUI) mechanism enables controlled information exchange: segmentation masks guide DeRUN's proximal step to prioritize uncertain regions, and an IQA-based selection (integrating TOPIQ, Q-Align, and MUSIQ) picks the highest-quality restoration among N inner iterations to feed back into subsequent SODUN stages. A cross-stage consistency loss aligns predictions from the best and second-best restorations to ensure robustness. Theoretically, the paper frames NUN as a bi-level optimization, proving inner-loop linear convergence under local strong convexity and establishing a degradation-sensitivity bound showing the degradation operator affects segmentation only through decomposed inner-loop and restoration approximation errors.

## Results

On the primary degraded COD benchmark (COD10K, combined low-resolution + low-light + haze, ResNet50), NUN achieves Fβ=0.448, Eφ=0.686, Sα=0.643, and M=0.068, outperforming the strongest baseline RUN by 20.42% in Fβ. Under single degradations, NUN leads RUN by 7.65% (low-light) and 6.90% (hazy) in Fβ on COD10K. On clean COD10K with DINOv2-L backbone, NUN reaches Fβ=0.880 and Sα=0.905, surpassing C3Net (0.852/0.898) and VSCCode (0.818/0.880). Across all 12 benchmarks (clean and degraded COD, TOD, PIS, CDD), NUN consistently achieves the best scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NUN outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every benchmark. The primary competitor RUN [24] (the closest prior DUN-based COS method) is beaten by 7.65–20.42% in Fβ under synthetic degradations on COD10K, and by 1.5–3.5% on clean COD10K across all four backbones. Non-unfolding baselines FEDER [17] and FSEL [53] degrade severely under degradation (e.g., FSEL drops to Fβ=0.213 on COD10K under low-light vs. NUN's 0.608), confirming that decoupled or non-iterative architectures cannot handle degradation. On clean data with DINOv2-L, NUN surpasses C3Net [31] (Fβ 0.880 vs. 0.852 on COD10K) and VSCCode [42] (0.880 vs. 0.818). For transparent object detection, NUN leads RFENet [11] by 1.2% mIoU on clean GDD and 4.2% under degradation. In polyp segmentation, NUN edges out PolypPVT [5] and MEGANet [1] by 0.3–0.7% mDice. On restoration quality (Table 12), NUN achieves PSNR 22.37 vs. DiffIR [59]'s 18.63 and RUN's 15.21, demonstrating that the nested structure simultaneously improves both restoration and segmentation. Against SAM-based methods, NUN (ViT-H) outperforms RobustSAM [4] by 13.7% Fβ under degradation (0.806 vs. 0.669) and SAM-Adapter [3] by 29.4% (0.806 vs. 0.512).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding network paradigm introduced for concealed object segmentation by RUN (ICML 2025), which coupled restoration and segmentation in shared optimization stages. The key architectural departure is the DUN-in-DUN nesting: rather than interleaving both tasks in a single loop with shared parameters (as in RUN and other coupled unfolding methods), NUN embeds a degradation-resistant inner network (DeRUN) within each stage of a segmentation-oriented outer network (SODUN), creating disjoint parameter spaces that the authors formally show reduce gradient conflict. The theoretical framing as bi-level optimization, with an inner-loop convergence guarantee and a degradation-sensitivity bound, distinguishes NUN from prior two-stage pipelines and coupled unfolding approaches that lack such a principled decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The nested unfolding paradigm offers a reusable architectural template for any degradation-sensitive perception task, and the paper demonstrates this by integrating DeRUN into three different segmentation frameworks and showing consistent gains. The degradation-sensitivity bound (Theorem 2) provides a clean theoretical guarantee that degradation affects segmentation only through a controllable, decomposable error term, which is absent in coupled or two-stage methods. Open questions include whether the local strong convexity assumption holds broadly in practice, whether the surrogate formulation's bound tightens for the exact bi-level problem, and how the approach scales to more complex degradation compositions or video settings. The reliance on frozen IQA models (TOPIQ, MUSIQ, Q-Align) for the BUI selection introduces a potential bottleneck if these models are miscalibrated on out-of-distribution content.
