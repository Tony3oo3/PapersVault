## Problem

Computational modeling of Theory of Mind (ToM) in natural human collaboration remains underexplored because existing benchmarks rely on text-based multiple-choice prompts, and available egocentric datasets either capture single-agent activity or multi-agent interactions lacking critical social cues such as gaze, verbal communication, and long-horizon coordination. No prior dataset provides synchronized multi-agent egocentric-exocentric video with dense social cue annotations in unscripted, goal-driven physical collaboration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260452/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task consumes a 10-second context window comprising 5 uniformly sampled egocentric video frames from both participants, the corresponding audio or WhisperX-transcribed speech, and (for Joint Attention) a final prediction frame showing both views simultaneously. Outputs are structured JSON objects containing view-specific bounding boxes (x, y, w, h), object categories from a three-level hierarchy (281 L1 / 76 L2 / 14 L3 categories), social cue type labels (action, gaze, verbal, gestural, implicit, or combinations), action verb-noun pairs, time-to-handover in seconds, delivery flow direction, and initiator identity. The leader/helper role assignment is fixed per task, with the helper always being the predicted actor in Tasks 2 and 3.

## Method

CoMind is a dataset and benchmark contribution rather than a single architecture. Data is collected in 55 real kitchens using paired Meta Aria Glasses (egocentric video, gaze, hand tracking, IMU), two GoPro cameras (front and back exocentric views), a Leica BLK2GO scanner (dense 3D scene), and an Artec 3D Leo scanner (object meshes), with sub-millisecond synchronization via TICSync and audio cross-correlation via SyncSink. The MPS pipeline extracts 6DoF trajectories, semi-dense point clouds, and aligns both participants into a shared coordinate frame via Multi-SLAM. The core novelty is formalizing ToM as three interdependent vision tasks of increasing difficulty—Joint Attention Estimation (mutual awareness), Socially Conditioned Object Interaction Anticipation (intent inference), and Collaborative Handover Prediction (proactive physical coordination before any reaching motion)—each requiring the model to infer a partner's mental state from multimodal social cues. The paper benchmarks 10+ VLMs zero-shot and fine-tunes Qwen3-VL (8B, 32B) on the training split, showing that domain-specific training yields 5–7× improvements in action verb accuracy and substantial gains in spatial grounding, validating the dataset's utility for training socially aware models.

## Results

Across all three CoMind tasks, zero-shot VLMs struggle severely with spatial grounding: most proprietary models (Claude, GPT families) score near-zero IoU@0.5, while Gemini 3 Flash leads zero-shot spatial localization (0.2164 L-IoU on Joint Attention, 0.1859 IoU on Anticipation, 0.0313 IoU on Handover). Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL on CoMind yields dramatic gains: the 32B variant achieves the best IoU@0.5 on Anticipation (0.2513) and the best Action Verb accuracy (0.6508), a 5× improvement over its zero-shot score (0.1380). On Handover Prediction, the fine-tuned 32B model attains the best Initiator accuracy (0.7286) and Initiation Type (0.3714), while Claude Opus 4.6 leads in temporal precision (0.1317 TTH). Overall, no single model dominates all metrics, and even the best fine-tuned model scores below 0.26 IoU, indicating substantial room for improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 3 Flash is the strongest zero-shot model for spatial grounding across all three tasks (0.2164 L-IoU on Joint Attention, 0.1859 IoU on Anticipation, 0.0313 IoU on Handover), but it is outperformed by fine-tuned Qwen3-VL 32B on Anticipation IoU (0.2513 vs. 0.1859) and on Handover Initiator/Init. Type. The GPT family (4o, 5.1, 5.2) consistently scores near-zero IoU (<0.02) on all spatial tasks, though GPT-5.2 achieves the best zero-shot Noun accuracy on Anticipation (0.4371) and GPT-5.1 the best Category L1 on Handover (0.4000). Claude Opus 4.5/4.6 also fail at spatial grounding (IoU ≤ 0.0091) but Claude 4.6 leads in temporal accuracy on Handover (0.1317 TTH) and is competitive on Cue Type for Anticipation (0.5347). Gemma 4 31B IT achieves moderate IoU (0.1468 on Anticipation) but trails all fine-tuned models. The Qwen3-VL models show the most dramatic improvement from fine-tuning: the 32B variant jumps from 0.0035 to 0.2513 IoU (72×) and from 0.1380 to 0.6508 Verb accuracy (4.7×) on Anticipation, while the 8B variant reaches the best Cue Type on Joint Attention (0.4775). No baseline matches the fine-tuned Qwen3-VL 32B across all metrics simultaneously, confirming that domain-specific training on CoMind is essential for social-cue-conditioned action prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMind carves new ground at the intersection of three underdeveloped threads: egocentric multi-agent datasets (which exist but lack social cue annotations and long-horizon collaboration, e.g., LEMMA, HOH, EgoLife), Theory of Mind benchmarks (which are almost exclusively text-based multiple-choice QA, e.g., ToMBench, EgoToM, MUMA-ToM), and action anticipation (which is overwhelmingly single-agent). Rather than extending any single lineage, the paper repurposes the ego-exo recording paradigm (EgoExo4D, HoloAssist) and augments it with gaze tracking, 3D scene/object scans, and explicit social-cue annotations to create a unified substrate for studying collaborative cognition. The three proposed tasks reframe existing sub-problems—joint attention, action anticipation, handover detection—as socially conditioned, multi-agent vision tasks, departing from prior formulations that treat agents independently or reduce ToM to passive text reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoMind is the first dataset to provide temporally aligned ego-exo video, gaze, audio, and 3D scans with fine-grained social cue annotations for long-horizon (avg. 30 min) unscripted human-human collaboration, filling a critical gap that has prevented empirical study of Theory of Mind in natural settings. The benchmark results reveal that even frontier VLMs (GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.6) score near-zero on spatial grounding for collaborative tasks, exposing a fundamental disconnect between semantic understanding and embodied social perception. The 7× improvement in action verb accuracy from fine-tuning Qwen3-VL on CoMind validates the dataset as a training resource, but the absolute performance ceiling remains low, leaving open whether current architectures can model the temporal and social dependencies required for proactive collaboration. Key open questions include whether richer 3D grounding or longer context windows can close the spatial grounding gap, and whether the social-cue conditioning signal is learnable at scale.
