## Problem

Existing red-teaming of GUI agents is limited by two practical barriers: gradient-based adversarial perturbations require white-box access unavailable in commercial deployments, and prompt/environment injections are increasingly intercepted by maturing safety alignment. This paper asks whether GUI agents can be distracted through a purely black-box, content-harmless channel—overlaying semantically plausible UI icons that pass safety filters yet exploit visual-semantic ambiguity in grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260846/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes a GUI screenshot, a step instruction, and the ground-truth target bounding box (normalized to [0,1]⁴) as input. The Editor emits a text description plus a normalized placement box; the Overlapper retrieves a matching icon from a cross-platform FAISS-indexed pool (mobile, web, desktop, multilingual sources) and composites it, enforcing two non-triviality constraints (IoU < τ_iou with the target and cosine similarity < τ_cos with the target element crop) to prevent trivial occlusion or duplication. The Victim produces a predicted click coordinate, and success is scored at two levels: L1 (click misses the ground-truth box) and L2 (click lands specifically on an injected icon).

## Method

The core pipeline decouples the attack into three composable modules: a VLM Editor (default Qwen3-VL-Plus) that proposes what icon to inject and where using six curated attack strategies (visual-twin, same-row confusion, attention hijack, position shift, cross-toolbar relocation, active-state confusion); an Overlapper that maps the text description to a concrete icon via Qwen3-VL-Embedding cosine retrieval over a FAISS index backed by LMDB for fast byte access; and a Victim that evaluates the composite screenshot. Search is structured as a Depth × Pass@N loop with greedy cumulative carry-forward: at each depth, N parallel Editor proposals are generated, filtered by non-triviality, overlaid cumulatively onto the previous best image, and scored by a lexicographic function over success indicator, normalized click displacement, average icon semantic relevance, icon count, and pass index. The single best image is carried to the next depth so that earlier accepted icons remain on the canvas and continue exerting visual pressure, making the search cumulative rather than branch-revisiting. This design, inspired by TAP's greedy tree-search but adapted to the additive nature of element injection, achieves 3.5–6.9× higher ASR than random injection on the most robust victims and transfers near-perfectly across 19 models in 8 families.

## Results

Across 9 victim models on 885 samples, the strategic attack (UT-opt./GO-opt.) achieves L1-ASR of 22–89% at D=5, substantially outperforming random injection by 3.5–6.9× on the most robust victims (e.g., Claude-Sonnet-4.6: 22.24% vs. ~3.23%; GUI-Owl-1.5-8B: 28.99% vs. ~7.56%). The two attack variants (optimized against different source victims) attain nearly identical ASR on every shared target (~1 pp gap), confirming model-agnostic transferability. Post-first-success L2 analysis shows strategic icons sustain >15% click-on-injected-icon rates in subsequent trials versus <1% for random injection, establishing causal rather than incidental disruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines under a matched victim-query budget (D=5, pass@3, max_edits=3). Random Injection (memoryless uniform sampling) is outperformed by 3.5–6.9× on the most robust victims (e.g., 22.24% vs. ~3.23% on Claude-Sonnet-4.6; 28.99% vs. ~7.56% on GUI-Owl-1.5-8B), and its L2-ASR is near zero, confirming it causes only incidental distraction. The two search-based baselines—LLM-Bank-Square-UI (Square/Sparse-RS-inspired discrete patch search [1,12]) and GPTFuzz-UI (GPTFuzzer/LLM-Fuzzer-inspired corpus fuzzing [48,49])—achieve 18–39% L1-ASR, trailing the proposed method by 14–15 pp on both victims, with L2-ASR roughly half (7–12% vs. 23–28%), indicating their mutation/search strategies are less effective at directing clicks toward a specific attacker-chosen icon. Random-BBox (preserving LLM descriptions but using uniform random placements) reaches 27–45% L1-ASR, isolating the contribution of LLM-guided spatial reasoning: the remaining 5–9 pp gap to the full method confirms that bounding-box placement quality provides an additional, non-trivial advantage beyond description quality alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new attack category between two established paradigms: gradient-based adversarial perturbations (which require white-box access) and prompt/environment injection (which is increasingly neutralized by safety alignment). It repurposes the iterative black-box search structure from TAP's tree-of-attacks [28] and adapts corpus-fuzzing ideas from GPTFuzzer [48,49] and Square/Sparse-RS [1,12] to the discrete, cumulative-overlay setting of GUI icon injection. The method family draws on multimodal embedding retrieval (Qwen3-VL-Embedding + FAISS) to ground textual descriptions into concrete visual elements, and on VLM-based editors to propose semantically targeted placements—positioning the work at the intersection of adversarial ML, GUI agent evaluation, and retrieval-augmented generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-perfect cross-architecture transferability (~1 pp gap between two independently optimized attack variants on every shared target) and the persistent-attractor effect (15%+ L2 post-first-success vs. <1% for random) together suggest a fundamental, shared vulnerability in how VLMs perform visual grounding over GUI layouts, rather than a model-specific quirk. This has direct practical implications for any deployment of GUI agents in commercial or open environments where screenshots can be tampered with. Open questions include whether grounding-aware defenses (cross-modal consistency auditing, attention-region verification) can close the gap, whether the attack extends to multi-step task sequences where accumulated errors compound, and whether the moderate ASR ceiling (22–52% at D=5) reflects a fundamental limit or simply a budget constraint that deeper search could push higher.
