## Problem

In real-world virtual try-on deployment, ground-truth images of the same person wearing the target garment are unavailable, rendering reference-based metrics (SSIM, LPIPS) impractical, while distribution-level metrics (FID, KID) cannot assess individual image quality. Existing reference-free VTON evaluation methods lack sufficient validation against large-scale human judgments and publicly available standardized benchmarks, hindering reproducible and fair comparison across models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262293/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three RGB images simultaneously: a garment image, a source person image, and the generated try-on image, each embedded as a sequence of patch tokens with a [CLS] token. It produces a single continuous quality score bounded to [-1, 1] via a tanh activation, trained against human subjective ratings originally collected on a three-level ordinal scale (unnatural / slightly unnatural but acceptable / natural) and aggregated into per-image mean scores. The bounded output enables direct numerical comparison across models and consistent ranking without scale ambiguity.

## Method

VTON-IQA is a three-branch transformer built on DINOv3 ViT-L/16, where the first 12 layers perform independent per-branch self-attention and the last 12 layers incorporate the proposed Interleaved Cross-Attention (ICA) module. ICA inserts a cross-attention layer between the self-attention and MLP sub-layers, modeling asymmetric interactions: the try-on branch aggregates cross-attention features from both garment and person representations, while the garment and person branches only receive information from the try-on branch, reflecting that quality judgment is centered on the generated image. The scoring head computes a learnable weighted combination of cosine similarities between the try-on [CLS] token and the garment/person [CLS] tokens, followed by a learnable affine transform and tanh. Training fine-tunes the last 12 blocks and inserted ICA layers (freezing the first 12) with a joint objective combining Bradley-Terry soft-label pairwise preference cross-entropy and L2 score regression against human ratings, optimized with AdamW on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the VTON-QBench test set, VTON-IQA achieves SRCC = 0.750, PLCC = 0.751, R² = 0.553, and micro pairwise accuracy A_micro = 0.790, substantially outperforming all baselines. The ICA module contributes a 13.3-point SRCC gain over the no-ICA variant (0.750 vs. 0.617) and a 50.6-point gain over zero-shot DINOv3 (0.750 vs. 0.244). In pairwise ranking, VTON-IQA reaches A_micro = 0.790, approaching the human-human agreement of 0.791, while correlation-based metrics (SRCC 0.699 vs. 0.760 for human) still show a measurable gap to human performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTON-IQA decisively outperforms both reference-based metrics: SSIM achieves only SRCC = 0.150 and A_micro = 0.593, while LPIPS reaches SRCC = 0.406 and A_micro = 0.695, both far below VTON-IQA's 0.750/0.790. The zero-shot DINOv3 baseline (SRCC = 0.244, A_micro = 0.641) confirms that task-specific fine-tuning on VTON-QBench is essential, as the pretrained features alone capture only weak quality signals. The ablation without ICA (SRCC = 0.617, A_micro = 0.747) shows that the Interleaved Cross-Attention module adds a further 13.3-point SRCC improvement, validating that explicit garment–person–try-on interaction modeling is critical. In the broader VTON model benchmark (Tables 4–5), VTON-IQA rankings diverge sharply from FID/KID: proprietary models like Nano Banana Pro (VTON-IQA = 0.305 on Dress Code) rank first, while several diffusion models with competitive FID scores (e.g., CatVTON-FLUX, FID = 3.093) score lower under VTON-IQA, indicating that distribution-level similarity does not capture perceptual try-on quality. Against human-human agreement (Table 3), VTON-IQA matches humans on pairwise accuracy (A_micro 0.783 vs. 0.791) but lags on absolute correlation (SRCC 0.699 vs. 0.760), suggesting the model captures relative ordering well but not yet fine-grained absolute perceptual calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of reference-free VTON evaluation initiated by VTONQA [34], VTBench [15], and VTON-VLLM [31], but departs from them in scale (62,688 images vs. 8,132; 13,838 annotators vs. 40) and in method: rather than relying on LLM-based critics or general aesthetic predictors, it trains a dedicated three-branch ViT with a novel Interleaved Cross-Attention module that explicitly models try-on↔garment and try-on↔person interactions. The architecture builds on DINOv3 ViT-L/16 as a frozen backbone, and the training objective combines Bradley-Terry pairwise preference learning with score regression, a formulation borrowed from the preference-optimization literature. The dataset construction repurposes FLUX.1-dev with a custom LoRA for synthetic garment-person pair augmentation and GPT-4.1 for attribute-consistency filtering, ideas drawn from the broader generative-model tooling ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VTON-QBench is the first large-scale, publicly released human-annotated benchmark for VTON quality, filling a critical gap that has limited reproducible evaluation in the field; its curation pipeline (Krippendorff's α filtering, sanity-check tasks) sets a methodological template for future crowdsourced perceptual studies. The benchmark results reveal that proprietary image-editing models (Nano Banana Pro, GPT-Image-1.5) achieve the highest human-aligned scores while ranking poorly under SSIM/LPIPS, quantifying a long-suspected but previously unmeasured disconnect between pixel-level similarity and perceptual try-on quality. Open questions include whether VTON-IQA generalizes to VTON models and garment categories unseen during training, and whether the remaining correlation gap to human performance (SRCC 0.750 vs. 0.760) can be closed with richer annotation protocols or multi-dimensional quality decomposition.
