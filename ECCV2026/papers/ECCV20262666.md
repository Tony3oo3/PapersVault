## Problem

Existing animal 3D perception datasets (e.g., AnimalKingdom, MammalNet) are sourced from internet videos or camera traps and lack metric-scale ground truth, making it impossible to validate depth or morphometric accuracy. Monocular depth estimation models trained on structured urban or indoor scenes degrade severely at the long standoff distances (20–140 m) and unstructured, vegetation-heavy scenes typical of wild habitats, yet no benchmark exists to quantify this failure. WildDepth fills this gap by providing the first synchronized RGB-LiDAR field dataset with metric depth for distant, deformable wildlife.

## Input / Output

Inputs are temporally synchronized (±10 ms) RGB frames at 30 FPS and LiDAR point clouds at 10 FPS, captured via telephoto zoom lenses at ranges from 5 m to 140 m across three ecologically distinct sites. For depth estimation, a single RGB frame is consumed by each MDE model to produce a per-pixel depth map, which is then evaluated against the calibrated LiDAR projection. For densification and 4D reconstruction, the pipeline additionally takes SAM2 segmentation masks to isolate the animal, and for behavior recognition, 5–10 s video clips (~150 frames) are paired with LiDAR motion signatures to yield 12 activity and 20 fine-grained action labels.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a three-part benchmark suite on the WildDepth dataset. For depth estimation, five SOTA MDE models (DepthAnything v1/v2/v3, DepthPro, DPT, MonoDepth2, MoGe2) are evaluated per-distance-bin with both uncalibrated and LiDAR-anchored (scale-shift) calibration, revealing that LiDAR fusion reduces RMSE by 25–30% in mid-range. For target point densification, MoGe2 predicts relative geometry from a single RGB frame, SAM2 provides the animal mask, and a RANSAC-based Scale-Shift Alignment (SSA) uses sparse LiDAR 'animal points' as metric anchors to solve a global affine transformation, with a Sobel-based depth-boundary filter removing telephoto artefacts. For 4D reconstruction, SAM2-stabilized monocular clips feed a Gaussian-Splatting framework (Gaussian-Splash) that optimizes a time-dependent deformation field over a canonical 3D Gaussian representation, producing temporally consistent deformable meshes. Behavior recognition uses a VideoMAE-style transformer trained on RGB clips to classify 12 activities and 20 fine-grained actions.

## Results

Across 11,498 animal detections in the 5–50 m range, no single monocular depth estimator dominates all metrics and distance bins; the best model shifts with range. With LiDAR-anchored calibration (LC), MoGe2 achieves the lowest RMSE at 20–30 m (4.17 m) and 30–50 m (8.08 m), while DA-v3 leads at 5–10 m (2.01 m RMSE). LiDAR calibration reduces RMSE by 25–30% in mid-range bins (e.g., DepthAnything drops from 9.93 m to 4.35 m at 20–30 m) and raises δ<1.25 from ~0.4–0.5 (UC) to 0.7–0.87 (LC). Error grows monotonically with distance for all models, confirming that long-range deformable wildlife remains a challenging regime for monocular depth estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five SOTA monocular depth estimators on WildDepth rather than proposing a new model, so 'winning' is framed as which model performs best per distance bin. MoGe2 [45] is the strongest at mid-to-long range (20–50 m LC), achieving 4.17 m and 8.08 m RMSE respectively, outperforming DepthAnything [47] (4.35 m, 10.44 m) and DA-v3 (4.27 m, 8.98 m) in those bins. DA-v3 leads at close range (5–10 m LC: 2.01 m RMSE, δ<1.25 = 0.80), narrowly beating MoGe2 (2.07 m) and DepthAnything (1.61 m RMSE but lower δ<1.25 = 0.79). DepthPro [6] and DPT [31] are consistently weaker at close range (14.75 m and 14.27 m RMSE UC at 5–10 m) but recover to competitive mid-range performance after LiDAR calibration. MonoDepth2 [21] is the weakest baseline across nearly all bins, with the highest RMSE (18.79 m UC at 5–10 m) and lowest δ<1.25 (0.16 UC at 5–10 m). The overarching finding is that LiDAR calibration benefits all models uniformly, reducing RMSE by 25–30% in mid-range, rather than one architecture being categorically superior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WildPose long-range 3D wildlife motion capture system [29] by scaling from a single acquisition rig to a multi-site, multi-species dataset with synchronized RGB-LiDAR, and by benchmarking a suite of off-the-shelf MDE models (DepthAnything, DPT, DepthPro, MonoDepth2, MoGe2) that were previously evaluated only on structured urban or indoor scenes (KITTI, NYUv2). It departs from prior wildlife datasets (AnimalKingdom, MammalNet, MammAlps, LoTE) which are RGB-only and lack metric ground truth, and from automotive/indoor RGB-LiDAR benchmarks (nuScenes, KITTI-360) that assume rectilinear geometry and static or slowly moving objects. The densification pipeline builds directly on WildPose's RANSAC-based alignment, while the 4D reconstruction leverages Gaussian-Splash [13] and the behavior recognition follows the VideoMAE [44] architecture, positioning the paper as a dataset-and-benchmark contribution that repurposes existing methods for a new ecological domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WildDepth is one of the first resources providing metric-scale depth ground truth for freely moving, deformable animals in unstructured outdoor habitats, which is a prerequisite for accurate 3D morphometrics, distance sampling, and population-level ecological inference from the tens of millions of camera-trap images already in circulation (e.g., LILA BC). The key open question is whether the LiDAR-anchored calibration strategy generalizes beyond the single ~97 m hartebeest sequence used for densification validation, and whether the per-distance degradation pattern holds across species, seasons, and sensor configurations. The dataset is geographically limited to Southern Africa and the UK, and quantitative benchmarks for pose estimation and fine-grained behavior remain for future releases, so its immediate impact is as a testbed rather than a definitive evaluation of any single method.
