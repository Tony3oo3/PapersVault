## Problem

VLM-based document parsing suffers from prohibitive autoregressive decoding latency on token-dense layouts. While Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) can accelerate inference, existing methods are limited to shallow look-ahead depths (K≤3) because static or heuristic loss weighting causes training instability and hallucinated gradients when distal supervision is applied despite proximal prediction failures. Additionally, fixed-depth drafting at inference either collapses acceptance rates in complex contexts or wastes computation on simple patterns.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB document image as input, processed by a VLM backbone (e.g., InternVL3.5-1B or Qwen3-VL-2B) into a sequence of visual and text tokens. The MTP module operates on the backbone's hidden states to draft K future tokens in one forward pass, which are then verified against the primary model's autoregressive predictions. Outputs are task-specific: LaTeX formulas, structured table representations, or general Markdown/JSON document text, with the drafting-verification loop producing the final token sequence.

## Method

P-MTP introduces a lightweight serial MTP module (shared MLP projection rather than heavy decoder layers) that recurrently produces K look-ahead predictions from a single hidden state. The key training innovation is Progressive Curriculum Loss, which assigns a dynamic per-position weight ω_(t,k) = ω¹·ω² to each look-ahead depth: ω¹ (sequential path constraint) is the cumulative product of ground-truth probabilities at all preceding depths, and ω² (retrospective target constraint) is the product of historical predictions for the same target token from proximal distances. This dual gating automatically suppresses gradient noise from unreliable distal predictions, creating an easy-to-hard curriculum that stably scales K to 9. At inference, Confidence-Gated Dynamic Drafting adaptively determines the draft depth k* by checking whether the cumulative joint probability of the drafted sequence exceeds a calibrated threshold δ (derived from terminal training loss), with an expanded budget H=2K, maximizing accepted tokens while truncating unreliable paths.

## Results

On PubTabNet (table recognition), P-MTP with K=9 and dynamic drafting achieves a 5.24× speedup (latency 5.10s vs. 26.71s baseline) with TEDS 84.78 vs. 85.09 for the NTP baseline, and an average acceptance length τ of 8.60. Across three tasks and two base models (Table 5), P-MTP yields 1.4×–2.3× throughput gains: Formula CDM drops from 95.88 to 94.58 (Qwen3VL-2B) while TPS rises from 1649 to 2663; Table TEDS goes from 85.09 to 84.78 with TPS 2774→4315; Document Overall drops from 86.31 to 81.28 with TPS 582→891. In the vLLM production framework, P-MTP delivers a consistent ~1.5× tokens-per-second speedup across batch sizes 4–64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is P-MTP against the standard NTP (autoregressive) baseline on the same base models. P-MTP consistently achieves 4–5× wall-clock speedup on PubTabNet with ≤0.3 TEDS degradation, and 1.4–2.3× throughput gains on Formula and Document tasks with 1–5 point metric drops. Against alternative MTP weighting schemes at K=9, the proposed dynamic weight ω_(t,k) outperforms uniform weighting (ω_k=1, TEDS 82.54, 3.42× speedup) and static decay (ω_k=0.2×0.8^k, TEDS 85.33, 4.49× speedup), achieving the best speedup of 4.83× (fixed) / 5.24× (dynamic) while maintaining TEDS 84.78. Confidence-Gated Dynamic Drafting further improves over fixed-length MTP by +0.57×, +0.47×, and +0.41× speedup at K=3, 6, and 9 respectively, with corresponding τ gains of +1.58, +1.74, and +1.31. The method also generalizes to a specialized 0.9B model (PaddleOCR-VL-1.5), where it improves TEDS from 81.05 to 82.03 while boosting TPS from 4454 to 6034 (1.35× speedup).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the serial multi-token prediction architecture introduced in DeepSeek-V3 (shared MTP module with recurrent look-ahead) and the speculative decoding draft-verify paradigm, but departs from both in critical ways. Where DeepSeek-V3 uses uniform loss weighting and Medusa uses static exponential decay, P-MTP introduces a trajectory-aware Progressive Curriculum Loss that dynamically gates supervision based on cumulative path reliability and retrospective target consistency. At inference, it replaces the fixed drafting depth standard in all prior MTP/speculative decoding work (Medusa, Eagle, DeepSeek-V3) with Confidence-Gated Dynamic Drafting that adaptively extends or truncates the speculative sequence based on real-time joint probability. The paper is also the first to apply deep MTP (K=9) specifically to the document parsing domain, leveraging the high-certainty predictive distributions inherent in perception-heavy tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

P-MTP demonstrates that the look-ahead depth in MTP can be scaled well beyond the K≤3 typical of prior work, achieving up to 5× single-stream speedup with negligible accuracy loss, which is practically significant for high-throughput document intelligence pipelines. The progressive curriculum loss mechanism is a generalizable training strategy that could benefit other speculative decoding applications beyond document parsing. However, the method's effectiveness is partly contingent on the task's inherently peaked predictive distributions (perception-heavy document parsing), raising open questions about transferability to more open-ended generation tasks. Additionally, the vLLM-integrated throughput gain drops to ~1.5× at larger batch sizes, and the document parsing results on public data (LightOnOCR-2) show non-trivial accuracy degradation, suggesting that data quality remains a critical bottleneck for real-world deployment.
