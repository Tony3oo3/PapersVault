## Problem

Most periodic INR methods (e.g., SIREN) rely on nested function composition, which contradicts the additive superposition principle of harmonic signals and causes gradient pathologies that destabilize optimization beyond 3–5 layers. Additionally, these methods impose a globally fixed frequency scaling that is inconsistent with the 1/f power-law energy distribution of natural signals, leaving spectral bias unaddressed from a physical-prior perspective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261688/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a degraded multi-dimensional tensor (e.g., a hyperspectral cube, video sequence, or RGB image with missing entries or noise) together with a binary observation mask Ω that specifies which entries are available. Input coordinates are normalized to [−1,1]^N where N is the signal dimensionality (2 for images, 3 for video, 5 for NeRF), and the network is queried on the full coordinate grid to produce the reconstructed tensor. Training uses only the masked observable entries via a projection operator, making the procedure self-supervised with no clean reference required.

## Method

CHOIR replaces the standard layer-wise composition Φ = φ_L ∘ … ∘ φ_1 with a hybrid composition-superposition architecture. Coordinated Harmonic Superposition (CHS) introduces zero-initialized learnable scalars β_l that gate each harmonic module's contribution additively (h_{l+1} = h_l + β_l F_l(h_l)), so the network starts as a linear map and progressively activates frequency components via an implicit curriculum learning mechanism. Perceptual Spectrum Calibration (PSC) assigns each neuron a log-uniform angular frequency within [π, γ·ω_Nyq] (matching the 1/f sampling density of natural signals) and modulates its output amplitude by (ω̃_l/ω_{l,i})^{α/2}, where α is a globally learnable spectral decay rate initialized to 2.0, thereby equalizing gradient magnitudes across frequencies and embedding the power-law prior directly into the optimization geometry.

## Results

On the primary data missing completion task (Table 2), CHOIR achieves the best PSNR across all six multi-dimensional datasets under both random and tube missing patterns. Representative gains include 51.36 dB on HSI WDC (Random OR=0.30, vs. 50.69 for CRNL), 50.40 dB on MSI Flowers (vs. 45.75 for DRO-TFF), and 38.74 dB on Video News (vs. 36.69 for CRNL). On novel view synthesis (Table 1), CHOIR attains the highest PSNR on all 8 NeRF Blender scenes (e.g., 35.32 dB on Chair, 37.45 dB on Hotdog) and the lowest LPIPS (0.013–0.088). Under mixed degradation with structured missing (Table 3, Scene 3), CHOIR leads by 2.6–3.5 dB over the best baseline (LRTFR) across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHOIR outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and every evaluation setting reported in the paper. The closest competitor is CRNL [24], which leverages non-local self-similarity priors; CHOIR exceeds it by 0.67 dB on HSI WDC (Random OR=0.30) and by 2.05 dB on Video News, with the gap widening under tube missing (e.g., 31.41 vs. 31.21 on HSI WDC Tube OR=0.30). On novel view synthesis, CHOIR surpasses the strongest baselines SL2A-INR [33] and IGA-INR [37] by 0.6–4.7 dB in PSNR (e.g., 28.30 vs. 24.64 on Lego) and by 0.02–0.05 in LPIPS. Under mixed degradation (Table 3), where noise and structured missing coexist, CHOIR's margin over LRTFR [23] (the best baseline there) reaches 3.5 dB on HSI WDC Scene 1 (31.24 vs. 28.62), demonstrating that its implicit smoothing prior eliminates the need for explicit TV regularization. FINER [22] and FreSh [12], which also use periodic activations, trail CHOIR by 4–11 dB on HSI/MSI data, confirming that the CHS superposition architecture and PSC frequency calibration are the primary sources of the performance gain rather than the periodic activation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHOIR builds directly on the SIREN family of periodic-activation INRs but departs from their core architectural paradigm: where SIREN, FINER, FreSh, and SL2A-INR all rely on layer-wise function composition (φ_L ∘ … ∘ φ_1), CHOIR replaces this with an additive harmonic superposition structure inspired by the superposition principle of Fourier analysis. The Coordinated Harmonic Superposition mechanism borrows the zero-initialized gating idea from ReZero, while the Perceptual Spectrum Calibration embeds the well-known 1/f power-law statistics of natural images (Field, 1987) into frequency allocation and amplitude modulation—something no prior periodic INR had done systematically. The paper also positions itself against continuous-representation baselines (LRTFR, CRNL, DRO-TFF) that combine INRs with structured tensor priors, arguing that the architectural fix is more fundamental than adding external regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The deep-scalability limitation of periodic INRs (performance peaking at 3–5 layers) has been an empirical observation without a clear architectural remedy; CHOIR provides both a theoretical diagnosis (composition vs. superposition mismatch) and a practical fix that enables stable 12+ layer networks, which could unlock greater representational capacity for complex multi-dimensional signals. The integration of the 1/f spectrum prior as a learnable, data-adaptive calibration offers a physically grounded alternative to ad-hoc frequency initialization strategies. Open questions include whether the superposition architecture generalizes to unstructured or irregularly sampled data (point clouds, event cameras, spatial transcriptomics), as the authors themselves note, and whether the implicit curriculum learning effect of CHS transfers to other network families beyond periodic activations.
