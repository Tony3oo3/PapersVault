## Problem

Generalized 3D hand-object pose estimation from a single RGB image remains difficult because existing methods either regress poses from appearance cues (limiting generalization to unseen objects) or rely on keypoint correspondences (fragile under occlusion). No prior approach effectively combines structured semantic knowledge with stable geometric priors to simultaneously handle novel objects, novel interaction patterns, and heavy hand-object occlusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261561/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input. During training, ground-truth point clouds sampled from hand and object meshes are paired with the image; during inference, point clouds are dynamically sampled from a voxel grid guided by image features, eliminating the need for ground-truth meshes. A hierarchical textual description is generated on-the-fly by InstructBLIP from the image. Outputs include MANO hand parameters (16 joint rotations and 10 shape coefficients), a full object pose (3×3 rotation matrix and 3D translation vector), and reconstructed hand and object point clouds.

## Method

GenHOI integrates three components: (1) a hierarchical semantic prompt that encodes object-level, hand-level, and interaction-level descriptions via InstructBLIP, providing abstract multi-level cues that generalize beyond visual appearance; (2) a multi-modal masked modeling strategy that applies modality-specific masking (patch corruption on images, local patch dropout on point clouds, token replacement on text) and reconstructs masked signals through cross-attention fusion, training the model to infer occluded content and align visual, geometric, and textual representations; (3) a hand-prior-guided pose estimation module that first regresses hand pose via SDF-guided feature aggregation and MANO parameter prediction, then uses the estimated hand joint rotations and 3D joint positions as stable spatial constraints to regress object rotation and translation. The key insight is that the hand's relatively fixed topology and limited shape variation make it a reliable geometric anchor for object pose reasoning when object shapes and interaction patterns vary widely.

## Results

On the DexYCB S3 split (unseen objects), GenHOI achieves an average AUC of ADD-0.5D of 89.34% for object pose, substantially outperforming the best prior method UniHOPE (43.06%) and HFL-Net (41.59%). In terms of ADD-S, GenHOI reduces the average error to 21.76 mm, compared to 84.04 mm for HOISDF and 110.79 mm for UniHOPE. On HO3Dv2, GenHOI achieves the best hand pose (MJE 21.3 mm, ST-MJE 21.1 mm) and object pose (OME 35.9 mm, ADD-S 13.4 mm) across all metrics, and reaches 92.7% ADD-0.5D on the single unseen object, surpassing HOISDF (88.4%) and HFL-Net (24.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenHOI achieves a dramatic improvement in object pose estimation on DexYCB S3: its average ADD-0.5D of 89.34% nearly doubles the best prior method UniHOPE (43.06%) and HFL-Net (41.59%), while Keypoint Transformer [13] and Liu et al. [26] score only 10.47% and 38.89% respectively. On the ADD-S metric (Table 2), GenHOI (21.76 mm) reduces error by over 4× compared to HOISDF (84.04 mm) and by ~5× compared to UniHOPE (110.79 mm) and HFL-Net (114.83 mm). For hand pose, GenHOI is competitive but slightly behind UniHOPE (MJE 13.67 vs. 13.31 mm; PA-MJE 6.39 vs. 6.08 mm), while clearly outperforming HFL-Net (14.77 mm) and all hand-only baselines (HandOccNet 14.58, MobRecon 15.40, H2ONet 15.20, SimpleHand 14.88 mm). On HO3Dv2, GenHOI wins across all reported metrics: MJE 21.3 mm beats HOISDF (23.6), HFL-Net (28.9), and Hampali et al. (25.5); ST-MJE 21.1 mm beats all video-based baselines (Hasson et al. 26.8–36.9, LCP 23.7); and ADD-S 13.4 mm beats HOISDF (17.8) and HFL-Net (32.4). On the single unseen HO3Dv2 object, GenHOI reaches 92.7% ADD-0.5D, surpassing HOISDF (88.4%) and far exceeding HFL-Net (24.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

GenHOI builds on the SDF-based implicit geometry framework of HOISDF (Qi et al., CVPR 2024) and the voxel-grid-guided point cloud sampling strategy from Yang et al. (2025), but departs from both by introducing a hierarchical textual semantic prompt (generated via InstructBLIP) that encodes object, hand, and interaction levels as structured language cues. This draws conceptually from the text-guided HOI generation literature (Zhang et al., Cha et al., Huang et al.) and vision-language pose estimation (Corsetti et al., Lin et al.), but repurposes textual semantics not for scene synthesis or category-level understanding, but as fine-grained spatial priors for pose regression under occlusion. The multi-modal masked modeling strategy extends the MAE paradigm from single-modality image reconstruction to joint RGB–point-cloud–text reconstruction, a combination not previously explored in hand-object pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that integrating hierarchical language priors, cross-modal masked reconstruction, and hand structural priors can dramatically close the generalization gap for unseen objects in hand-object pose estimation, reducing ADD-S error from ~84 mm to ~22 mm on DexYCB S3. However, the evaluation is limited to 20 YCB objects and a single RGB modality, and the reliance on InstructBLIP for text generation introduces a dependency on the quality and consistency of automatically generated descriptions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or in-the-wild objects, whether the hierarchical text prompt can be replaced by a learned embedding to reduce inference cost, and whether the framework extends to egocentric or multi-view settings as the authors note for future work.
