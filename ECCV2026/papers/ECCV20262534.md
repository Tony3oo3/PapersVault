## Problem

Converting static 3D meshes into simulation-ready articulated digital twins remains a critical bottleneck for embodied AI and robotic simulation. Existing zero-shot pipelines suffer from two fundamental flaws: Vision-Language Models lack physical grounding and produce kinematic hallucinations on irregular mechanical components, while unconstrained joint estimation leads to catastrophic mesh inter-penetration when assets are loaded into physics engines like SAPIEN.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262534/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unstructured static 3D surface mesh (no templates, no multi-view captures) and one rendered reference image (fed to SP4D for kinematic mask synthesis) as input. It produces a complete, collision-free URDF file containing part-level mesh segmentations with preserved UV textures, a kinematic hierarchy with typed joints (revolute-spin, revolute-hinge, prismatic), optimized axis/pivot parameters, and forward-simulation-verified range-of-motion limits, ready for direct deployment in SAPIEN or equivalent physics simulators.

## Method

MotionAnymesh is a three-stage zero-shot pipeline that decouples geometric perception from physical reasoning. First, P3-SAM extracts 3D-native geometric primitives preserving sharp boundaries, then SP4D generates multi-view kinematic masks that ground GPT-4o's clustering of primitives into coherent movable parts, eliminating the kinematic hallucinations of pure semantic VLM reasoning. Second, a type-aware initialization stage deduces joint parameters from localized contact interfaces (PCA + RANSAC circle fitting for spin joints, PCA principal axis for hinges, and a normalized dual-penalty trajectory verification for prismatic axes), followed by a physics-constrained trajectory optimization that minimizes a unified SDF-based surface distance loss across virtual articulation states, with asymmetric collision penalties forcing convergence to valid motion manifolds. Third, forward-simulation collision detection determines physical limits (including a novel contact-loss criterion for prismatic extension), and the final textured parts are assembled into standard URDF format.

## Results

MotionAnymesh achieves an mIoU of 0.86 and Count Accuracy of 0.92 for part segmentation, surpassing the strongest baseline (Articulate-AnyMesh) by +0.27 and +0.18 respectively. For joint estimation, it reduces Axis Error to 0.12 and Pivot Error to 0.10, compared to 0.64 and 0.44 for the best baseline. Most critically, MotionAnymesh achieves 87% Physical Executability in the SAPIEN simulator, nearly doubling the best baseline (Articulate-Anything at 46%) and demonstrating that the physics-constrained optimization is essential for simulation-ready output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionAnymesh outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor Articulate-AnyMesh (which also uses VLM-based zero-shot reasoning), it gains +0.27 mIoU, +0.18 Count Accuracy, and a 52-point improvement in Physical Executability (87% vs. 35%), indicating that 2D-to-3D VLM projection heuristics remain highly susceptible to spatial hallucination. Against Articulate-Anything (the best baseline on executability at 46%), MotionAnymesh more than doubles the success rate while also halving Axis Error (0.12 vs. 0.86) and Pivot Error (0.10 vs. 0.64). Retrieval-based methods (URDFormer, SINGAPO, PARIS) are fundamentally limited by their reliance on predefined CAD libraries, yielding poor generalization to novel geometries (mIoU ≤ 0.52) and high joint parameter errors. The ablation further confirms that removing SP4D kinematic priors drops mIoU from 0.86 to 0.68, and skipping physics-constrained optimization collapses executability from 87% to 65%, validating both core contributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two dominant paradigms in articulated object modeling: retrieval-based pipelines (URDFormer, SINGAPO) that depend on predefined CAD libraries, and VLM-driven zero-shot methods (Articulate-Anything, Articulate-AnyMesh) that rely on 2D-to-3D mask lifting and pure semantic reasoning. It departs from both by operating natively in 3D space (via P3-SAM for geometric primitives) and by grounding VLM clustering with explicit SP4D kinematic priors rather than leaving the model to infer motion semantics from appearance alone. The joint estimation stage extends standard geometric initialization (PCA, RANSAC) with a physics-constrained SDF-based trajectory optimization, a step absent from prior generative or regression-based joint estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 87% physical executability rate (nearly double the best baseline) and the Real-to-Sim-to-Real robotic manipulation demo demonstrate that zero-shot static-to-articulated conversion can now produce assets directly deployable in embodied AI pipelines, reducing the manual URDF modeling bottleneck. However, the authors acknowledge the mesh-first pipeline still struggles with severely fused scans, missing surfaces, or highly ambiguous contact regions, pointing toward future integration with 3D-native repair and perception models. An open question is whether the SP4D grounding generalizes to object categories where kinematic priors are less well-defined (e.g., soft-body or multi-DOF mechanisms).
