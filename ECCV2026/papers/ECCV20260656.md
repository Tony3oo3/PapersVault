## Problem

Probabilistic point cloud registration methods based on Gaussian mixture models (e.g., CPD, FilterReg, ECMPR) are robust to noise and outliers but suffer from high computational cost due to exhaustive pairwise probability computation, particularly on large-scale point clouds. Moreover, existing probabilistic methods ignore informative prior information, forcing each source point to be compared against all target points. This paper addresses both limitations by introducing graph signal processing to enrich geometric representations and by constructing prior probabilities from transformation-invariant features.

## Input / Output

The method takes two point clouds (source and target), each with 3D coordinates and surface normals, typically downsampled to 5,000–25,000 points depending on the experiment. Each point is lifted to a 9-dimensional graph signal (3 coordinates, 3 scaled normals, 3 high-frequency components), and local 9×9 graphs are built per point. The output is a rigid transformation (rotation matrix R and translation vector t) estimated via EM, along with a soft correspondence probability matrix P of size M×N. The method is evaluated on angular error (degrees) between estimated and ground-truth rotations, as well as runtime.

## Method

GraphCPD replaces the isotropic covariance in GMM-based registration with point-specific graph Laplacian matrices derived from local 9×9 attribute-similarity graphs, providing a more discriminative geometric representation that enforces consistency in variation trends across multiple attributes. High-frequency components are extracted via a linear shift-invariant FIR high-pass graph filter applied to a K-nearest-neighbor global graph, and are theoretically proven invariant under rigid transformations (Theorem 1). These invariant components are used to build a KD-tree for prior probability construction: each source point only considers its k_match nearest candidates in the target, drastically reducing the search space. The EM algorithm optimizes the negative log-likelihood, with the M-step using Newton's method on the Lie group (SE(3)) to update the rigid transformation, restricted to the 3×3 coordinate sub-block of the Laplacian to avoid noise from outlier-affected normals and high-frequency components. GPU parallelization with custom CUDA kernels accelerates the E-step matrix computation.

## Results

On the Dragon and Armadillo datasets (~20K points), GraphCPD achieves the lowest angular error of 0.091° and 0.095° respectively, while running in ~2.9 s—roughly 33× faster than CPD (96.6 s) and 45× faster than ECMPR (133.4 s). On the Utah Teapot invariance test, GraphCPD reaches 0.018° angular error in 0.37 s, outperforming GraphReg (0.067°) by 3.7× in accuracy and 6.3× in speed. On the KITTI LiDAR sequence (550 pairs), GraphCPD achieves the best relative rotation error (0.029°) and absolute rotation error (1.83°), substantially ahead of FilterReg (0.146° / 3.55°) and GraphReg (0.171° / 14.29°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphCPD outperforms all six baselines in angular error on both Dragon and Armadillo. Against the closest probabilistic competitor LSG-CPD (0.155°/0.138°), GraphCPD improves accuracy by ~41% while being ~3.4× slower (2.95 s vs 0.86 s); against FilterReg (0.297°/0.171°), it improves accuracy by ~70% at ~1.5× the runtime. Compared to the much slower ECMPR (0.203°/0.173°) and CPD (0.624°/0.597°), GraphCPD is both more accurate and 45–33× faster. TrICP is the fastest (0.50 s) but least accurate on Dragon (0.800°), though it is competitive on Armadillo (0.119°). On the Utah Teapot invariance test, GraphCPD (0.018°) beats GraphReg (0.067°) by 3.7×, and on KITTI it reduces absolute rotation error from 14.29° (GraphReg) to 1.83°, a 7.8× improvement. The only scenario where a baseline is competitive is Armadillo, where TrICP (0.119°) is within 0.024° of GraphCPD (0.095°) at 5× lower runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphCPD extends the Coherent Point Drift (CPD) probabilistic registration framework by replacing its isotropic Gaussian covariance with point-specific graph Laplacian matrices derived from a high-dimensional graph signal representation. It draws on Graph Signal Processing tools—specifically high-pass graph filtering and graph Laplacian smoothness—to extract rigid-transformation-invariant components and encode local geometric structure. The method also departs from prior probabilistic approaches (CPD, FilterReg, ECMPR, LSG-CPD) by introducing a prior probability mechanism built from invariant high-frequency descriptors, which no existing GMM-based registration method had exploited. Compared to the closest GSP-based competitor, GraphReg, GraphCPD replaces hard one-to-one matching with soft probabilistic correspondences, improving robustness to outliers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing practical bottleneck in probabilistic registration: the O(MN) pairwise computation that makes methods like CPD and ECMPR prohibitively slow on large-scale point clouds. By leveraging the invariance of high-frequency graph components to restrict the correspondence search space via a KD-tree, GraphCPD achieves a 30–45× speedup over CPD/ECMPR while matching or exceeding their accuracy, making probabilistic registration viable for large-scale and LiDAR applications. Open questions include whether the pairwise multi-view strategy can be replaced by globally consistent optimization (acknowledged as future work), and whether the graph Laplacian density model generalizes beyond rigid registration to articulated or non-rigid settings. The invariance theorem, while clean, is limited to the specific high-pass filter constructions tested.
