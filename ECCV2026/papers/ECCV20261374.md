## Problem

Standard diffusion transformers fail to converge when trained directly on high-dimensional representation encoder features (e.g., DINOv2, SigLIP, MAE), even in single-image overfitting regimes. Prior work (RAE) attributed this to a capacity bottleneck and proposed expensive width scaling of the transformer. This paper demonstrates the failure is fundamentally geometric: Euclidean flow matching forces probability paths through the low-density interior of the hyperspherical feature space rather than along the manifold surface, a phenomenon the authors term Geometric Interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261374/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (ImageNet-1K) as conditioning input and generates 256×256 RGB images. Generation occurs in the frozen feature space of a representation encoder (e.g., DINOv2-B with 768-dim tokens), where all vectors are projected to the unit hypersphere S^{d-1}. A RAE decoder converts the generated feature tokens back to pixel space. Inference uses a 50-step geodesic (exponential map) integrator, and an optional projection radius scaling (R≈45 vs. the native R≈27.7) is applied before decoding to improve fidelity.

## Method

RJF reformulates the generative process directly on the hyperspherical manifold of the representation encoder. First, both data and noise are projected to the unit sphere, and the probability path is defined via SLERP (geodesic interpolation) instead of Euclidean linear interpolation, ensuring all intermediate states remain on the manifold. The target velocity field is computed in the tangent space (orthogonal to the position vector), structurally eliminating the radial error component that causes Geometric Interference. Second, a Jacobi Regularization term λ(t,Ω)=sinc²((1−t)Ω) reweights the velocity-matching loss to account for curvature-induced geodesic focusing, prioritizing precision near the noise endpoint. Sampling uses a closed-form exponential map update that wraps tangent velocities around the sphere. This geometric objective enables standard DiT-B (131M) to converge to FID 3.37 (with guidance) without any architectural width scaling, and scales to DiT-XL achieving FID 3.62 in 80 epochs.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, RJF enables the standard 131M-parameter DiT-B to converge on DINOv2-B features, achieving FID 3.37 (with guidance) and 4.95 (without guidance) at 200 epochs, where standard Euclidean Flow Matching fails entirely (FID 24.21 at 80 epochs). At the DiT-XL scale (677M params), RJF reaches FID 3.62 without guidance in just 80 epochs, with IS 186.2 and Precision 0.82, outperforming the Euclidean baseline (FID 4.28) and the VAE-based LightningDiT (FID 4.29). The method also demonstrates superior convergence speed: at 24 epochs on DiT-XL, RJF achieves FID 6.32, already beating REPA's 80-epoch result (FID 6.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RJF decisively outperforms the Euclidean Flow Matching baseline on DINOv2-B features (EFM) at every scale: FID 6.77 vs. 24.21 on DiT-B (80 epochs), 4.21 vs. 6.31 on DiT-L, and 3.62 vs. 4.28 on DiT-XL, confirming that the convergence failure is geometric rather than a capacity issue. Against the VAE-based LightningDiT (FID 4.29 at DiT-XL, 80 epochs), RJF achieves a lower FID of 3.62 while also attaining substantially higher IS (186.2 vs. 130.2 at 64 epochs) and Precision (0.82 vs. 0.76). Compared to REPA at the same 80-epoch budget, RJF improves FID from 6.94 to 3.62 on DiT-XL, and at 24 epochs RJF (FID 6.32) already surpasses REPA's full 80-epoch result (6.94). Against the strongest 80-epoch baselines REG (FID 3.40) and SPD (FID 3.53), RJF (FID 3.62) is slightly behind on FID but leads on IS (186.2 vs. 184.1 and 162.0) and Precision (0.82 vs. 0.77 and 0.75), indicating superior semantic mode coverage. RAE with width-scaled DiT^OH achieves a lower FID (1.60) but requires 800 epochs and 839M parameters (width scaling), whereas RJF reaches competitive quality with the standard 677M architecture in 80 epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RAE [42], which demonstrated that diffusion transformers can operate in the feature space of frozen representation encoders (DINOv2, SigLIP) but identified a convergence failure and prescribed width scaling as the fix. The key departure is a re-diagnosis: rather than a capacity bottleneck, the authors identify Geometric Interference—Euclidean linear interpolation in flow matching cuts through the low-density interior of the hyperspherical feature manifold, wasting model capacity on radial error. The solution repurposes Riemannian Flow Matching [4] (SLERP-based geodesic paths) and adds a Jacobi-field-derived curvature weighting [41], shifting the fix from architecture to objective function. This positions the work against the broader representation-alignment literature (REPA, REPA-E, REG, DDT) that enriches VAE latents with auxiliary losses, and against RAE's width-scaling prescription, arguing that respecting intrinsic geometry is both necessary and sufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a recently proposed architectural remedy (width scaling) with a clean geometric explanation and a lightweight loss-function fix, which could redirect how the community approaches diffusion on structured latent spaces. The radial/angular loss decomposition (Fig. 4) provides a compelling mechanistic account of *why* standard flow matching fails, not just that it fails. Open questions include whether the hyperspherical assumption generalizes to encoders with different geometric structure, whether the Jacobi weighting's marginal contribution (FID 7.06→6.77) justifies its complexity, and whether the method closes the gap to RAE's full-training FID of 1.60 given more epochs. The approach is also currently tied to the RAE decoder, limiting end-to-end flexibility.
