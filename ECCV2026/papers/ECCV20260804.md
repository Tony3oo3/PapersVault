## Problem

Standard knowledge distillation minimizes KL divergence between teacher and student predictions, implicitly assuming the teacher is reliable. In practice, teacher outputs are noisy or overconfident, and existing correction strategies (logit swapping, probability augmentation, class masking) are ad-hoc heuristics whose hyperparameters must be retuned for every teacher-student pair and dataset. The paper asks whether a principled, statistically grounded loss can handle unreliable teacher signals without model-specific tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 on CIFAR-100, 224×224 on ImageNet-1k), the teacher's softened logit vector (optionally temperature-scaled), and the ground-truth one-hot label, and outputs a K-class prediction from the student network. Crucially, it requires only the teacher's final-layer logits—no intermediate feature maps, attention weights, or architectural compatibility—making it applicable across heterogeneous teacher-student pairs (ResNet, WideResNet, VGG, ShuffleNet, MobileNetV2). When a distillation temperature τ is applied, the power-divergence term is rescaled by τ² to counteract the gradient magnitude reduction caused by logit softening.

## Method

REDistill replaces the KL divergence in the distillation objective with the power divergence of order λ = 2/3, which substitutes the natural logarithm in the 'surprise' term with a (1−λ)-logarithm (Box-Cox transform). This causes the loss to adaptively downweight samples where the teacher-student likelihood ratio p/q_θ is large—precisely the samples most likely to reflect teacher errors—while preserving informative inter-class relationships. The choice λ = 2/3 is derived from influence-function analysis in robust statistics: the per-sample influence is bounded by q_θ/(1+λ), so larger λ increases robustness, while smaller |λ| preserves statistical efficiency; λ = 2/3 is identified as the near-optimal balance. The full loss (Eq. 9) combines standard cross-entropy with decoupled target and non-target power divergences (following the DKD structure), introducing no additional trainable parameters, no architectural modifications, and negligible computational overhead beyond a single exponentiation per class.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 under the model-agnostic protocol (fixed hyperparameters across all pairs), REDistill achieves top-1 accuracies of 77.52% (ResNet32→SHN-V2), 79.01% (ResNet32→WRN-40-2), 77.21% (WRN-40-2→ResNet8), and 70.92% (ResNet50→MN-V2), consistently outperforming all logit-based baselines. On ImageNet-1k, REDistill reaches 72.00% top-1 (ResNet34→ResNet18) and 72.98% top-1 (ResNet50→MN-V1), setting new state-of-the-art over the best prior methods (LSKD: 71.88% and 72.85% respectively). Under the model-specific protocol, gains are even larger, e.g. 79.23% on ResNet32→WRN-40-2 and 77.46% on WRN-40-2→ResNet8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REDistill outperforms all 14 CIFAR-100 teacher–student pairs under the model-agnostic protocol, with the largest margins over standard KD (e.g. +2.01% on ResNet32→SHN-V2, +2.56% on WRN-40-2→MN-V2) and consistent 0.2–0.7% gains over the strongest baselines DKD and LSKD. On ImageNet-1k, REDistill beats LSKD by 0.12% (Res34→Res18) and 0.13% (Res50→MN-V1), and RLD by 0.09% and 0.23% respectively. Under the model-specific protocol, REDistill leads all 14 pairs except VGG13→VGG8, where RLD edges it by 0.05% (74.93 vs 74.88). Notably, correction-based methods (LA, RC, LR) and RLD degrade substantially when hyperparameters are fixed (model-agnostic), while REDistill maintains its advantage, confirming that its gains are not an artifact of per-pair tuning. When combined with MLKD, REDistill further improves accuracy (e.g. 78.44→78.31 on Res32→SHN-V2 in the 480-epoch protocol), demonstrating orthogonality to existing distillation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

REDistill sits at the intersection of classical knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015; Zhao et al. 2022's decoupled target/non-target formulation) and the power divergence family from robust statistics (Cressie & Read, 1984). It departs from the dominant line of heuristic logit-correction methods (LA, RC, LR, RLD) that swap, amplify, or mask teacher logits, instead replacing the KL divergence itself with a power divergence whose robustness parameter λ is derived from influence-function analysis rather than tuned per architecture. The closest prior work is ABKD, which also draws on a power-divergence family but selects its configuration via grid search; REDistill's distinction is a single theoretically justified λ=2/3 fixed across all settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a drop-in loss replacement that requires no per-pair hyperparameter search, no extra parameters, and no architectural changes—addressing a real pain point in deploying KD across diverse model pairs. The robustness ablation (Table 7) provides direct evidence that the power divergence genuinely downweights corrupted teacher signals, not just that it happens to work on clean data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to feature-based or multi-teacher distillation, whether the λ=2/3 optimality holds for non-CNN backbones (transformers, ViTs), and whether the modest absolute gains (typically 0.2–0.5% over the best logit-based baselines) persist at larger scale or in more challenging regimes (e.g., very noisy or domain-shifted teachers).
