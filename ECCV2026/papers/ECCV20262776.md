## Problem

No existing benchmark probes city-scale spatial intelligence in VLMs from real-world video. Prior spatial reasoning evaluations were confined to small-scale or simulated environments, leaving open whether VLMs develop genuine path integration and allocentric mental maps or merely exploit 2D visual recognition and text-matching to bypass spatial reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262776/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question presents a walking tour video segment (up to 10 minutes, ~1 km at average walking speed) alongside a text question and five answer options, of which one is correct. Some tasks use non-standard configurations: landmark recognition appends a reference landmark image to the video, and map trace tasks replace text options with map images (with or without text labels). All models are evaluated at 32 uniformly sampled frames for fair comparison, though frame-rate ablations extend to 600 frames for Gemini 2.5 Flash and GPT-5.

## Method

The benchmark is grounded in the LANDMARK-ROUTE-MAP cognitive hierarchy, defining tasks at three levels: landmark recognition with distance estimation, route-level tasks (compass orientation, loop closure detection, route summary), and survey-knowledge tasks (map trace identification, Euclidean distance estimation). A key technical contribution is an efficient video-to-map grounding pipeline that uses Google's Visual Positioning System (VPS) in forward and reverse passes with confidence-weighted merging to extract per-frame (lat, lon) and camera pose at 1 FPS, validated against human annotations via Dynamic Time Warping. Ground-truth answers are then generated at scale from these traces using heuristics (e.g., 4 m-radius loop detection) and the Google Maps Routes API, with DTW-based matching to ensure generated routes closely follow the actual walked path. The benchmark comprises 1000 five-way video QAs over 235 YouTube walking tour videos (~288 hours) spanning diverse cities worldwide.

## Results

On the KilometerVision benchmark (1000 five-way video QAs over 235 hour-long walking tour videos), GPT-5 achieves the highest overall accuracy at 45% with 32 frames, improving to 57% when given 600 frames. Gemini 2.5 Flash plateaus at ~38% with virtually no gain from increasing frames (32→600). The human baseline scores 71.3% overall (inter-rater agreement 0.76), while PLM-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-72B perform at chance level (~20%), indistinguishable from a blind baseline fed blank frames. Loop closure is the only task where two VLMs surpass human performance, but this is achieved via brute-force visual frame matching rather than geometric reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 is the strongest model overall, excelling at loop closure and route summary, yet remains significantly below the 71.3% human baseline on all map-related tasks (Euclidean distance, map trace recognition). Gemini 2.5 Flash (~38%) shows almost no benefit from additional frames, suggesting its architecture cannot exploit longer temporal context for spatial reasoning. Claude Opus is limited to 32 frames and its performance degrades when spatial resolution is reduced from 400 to 200 pixels, except on map tasks where accuracy stays at chance regardless of resolution. PLM-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-72B both perform at chance level (~20%), matching the blind Gemini baseline that receives blank frames, confirming they extract no useful spatial signal from the video. The blind baseline's chance-level performance validates that the benchmark cannot be solved from text priors alone. Notably, providing privileged information (GPS coordinates, camera poses, or map overlays) does not significantly improve any model's performance, indicating that current VLMs lack the ability to integrate such signals into spatial reasoning without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the LANDMARK-ROUTE-MAP hierarchical framework from human cognitive science (Siegel & White, Chrastil & Warren, Tolman) into a VLM evaluation setting at city scale. Prior spatial-intelligence benchmarks for multimodal models (Perception Test, VSI-Bench, and related work) operated in small-scale, factory-like, or synthetic environments, while geographic-knowledge probes (GPT4Geo, Charting New Territories) relied on text or static images rather than continuous video. KilometerVision departs from both lines by using hour-long real-world walking tour videos grounded via Google's Visual Positioning System, pushing the spatial extent to ~1 km and structuring tasks explicitly around the three cognitive stages of spatial awareness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a fundamental and consistent limitation across all five evaluated SOTA VLMs: they bypass true path integration and allocentric survey knowledge, instead relying on 2D visual recognition and OCR-based text matching to simulate spatial reasoning. This negative result is important because it shows that scaling model size or adding privileged context (GPS coordinates, camera poses, map images) does not yet yield genuine geometric spatial understanding, raising open questions about whether such capabilities require fundamentally different architectures or training signals. The benchmark is publicly available and provides a scalable pipeline (VPS grounding + DTW validation) that could be extended to thousands of videos, making it a likely reference standard for future city-scale spatial evaluation.
