## Problem

Existing differentiable vector renderers are restricted to Bezier curves or polynomial B-splines, leaving NURBS—the industry-standard representation in CAD with rational weights and non-uniform knots—entirely unsupported for image-space optimization. This disconnect means differentiable rendering cannot faithfully represent exact conic sections (circles, ellipses) or integrate with the NURBS-based modeling infrastructure that underpins manufacturing and design.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raster target image (512×512 for calligraphy, 480×480 for vectorization) and optionally a style image or text prompt, and produces NURBS curve parameters—key points, rational weights, knot intervals, and per-control-point stroke widths—that define resolution-independent editable vector graphics. The per-control-point width channel encodes local stroke thickness directly into the curve parameterization, enabling variable-width strokes without separate outline geometry. For filled closed regions, the output additionally includes an SDF-modulated interior grid representation.

## Method

NURBS Splatting adaptively samples isotropic 2D Gaussians along NURBS curves with density proportional to arc length, using span-local Cox-de Boor evaluation to reduce per-sample cost from O(Mn) to O(Mp). For closed filled regions, a uniform grid of Gaussians is placed inside the bounding box with opacity modulated by a differentiable SDF, and the grid step is annealed from coarse to fine during optimization to accelerate early convergence. All Gaussians are composited through a tile-based splatting rasterizer (adopted from GaussianImage), making the entire pipeline differentiable from pixel losses back to control points, weights, and knot intervals. The isotropic formulation, as opposed to the anisotropic tangent-aligned Gaussians in Bezier Splatting, eliminates rotation estimation and avoids spike artifacts at high-curvature regions and filled-region junctions. Optimization combines an image-space reconstruction loss with third-derivative smoothing and bounding-box regularization, all propagated through the analytical NURBS derivative chain.

## Results

On the 192-character calligraphy benchmark, NURBS Splatting reduces MSE by 33% (0.0038 vs. 0.0057), improves PSNR by +1.6 dB (26.32 vs. 24.72), and lowers the symmetric Hausdorff distance by 30% (10.69 vs. 15.26 px) over the Berio et al. baseline, while running 1.2× faster (8.73 s vs. 10.65 s). On the 100-image Noto Emoji vectorization benchmark, the full method with grid-step annealing reduces MSE by 32% (0.0017 vs. 0.0025), improves SSIM by +0.017 (0.9803 vs. 0.9633), and lowers LPIPS by 41% (0.0339 vs. 0.0576) over LIVE, with a 1.4× speedup (410 s vs. 590 s). In the diffusion-guided image abstraction task, NURBS Splatting achieves 1.27× faster optimization than Berio et al. at comparable stylization quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Berio et al. [2] (long smoothing B-splines with analytic rasterization), NURBS Splatting wins on every metric except SSIM in the default configuration: MSE drops 33%, PSNR rises +1.6 dB, Hausdorff falls 30%, F1 improves to 0.9741, and optimization is 1.2× faster. The SSIM gap (0.9793 vs. 0.9813) is attributed to softer boundary transitions from isotropic Gaussians versus DiffVG's analytic edge rendering, and is closed by increasing contour density to δ_c=30 (SSIM 0.9824). Against LIVE [28] (layer-wise vectorization with DiffVG backend), NURBS Splatting dominates on all four metrics—MSE −32%, SSIM +0.017, LPIPS −41%, and 1.4× faster—demonstrating that the Gaussian splatting backend is beneficial even when rational weights and knots are removed. Against Bezier Splatting [25], the comparison is qualitative (Fig. 5): NURBS Splatting eliminates three artifact classes—blurred edges on thick strokes, spike artifacts at high-curvature regions caused by atan2 discontinuity in anisotropic rotation, and spikes at filled-region junctions—by using purely isotropic Gaussians. In the SDS-based image abstraction task, NURBS Splatting runs 1.27× faster than Berio et al. while matching stylization quality, despite NURBS evaluation being more complex than polynomial B-spline evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable vector graphics rendering lineage from DiffVG (analytic rasterization of Bezier curves) and Bezier Splatting (Gaussian sampling along Bezier curves) to the full NURBS representation, introducing rational weights and non-uniform knot vectors as jointly optimizable parameters. It adopts the tile-based Gaussian splatting rasterizer from GaussianImage as its rendering backend and adapts the layer-wise vectorization protocol of LIVE and the long-spline abstraction pipeline of Berio et al. to the NURBS setting. A key conceptual departure from Bezier Splatting is the use of purely isotropic Gaussians rather than tangent-aligned anisotropic ones, motivated by the observation that sufficient arc-length density makes directional alignment unnecessary and avoids rotation-estimation artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making NURBS parameters (rational weights, non-uniform knots) differentiable in image space, this work closes a long-standing gap between learning-based vector reconstruction and the CAD-standard curve representation used in every major industrial kernel, enabling exact conic-section geometry that polynomial splines can only approximate. The practical impact is demonstrated across calligraphy reconstruction, emoji vectorization, and diffusion-guided abstraction, with consistent quality and speed gains over polynomial baselines. Open questions include whether the heuristic SDF-based region-filling grid can be replaced by a more principled analytic approach, how the method scales to 3D NURBS surfaces for native CAD geometry, and whether the growing Gaussian primitive count becomes prohibitive for highly detailed illustrations.
