## Problem

Most 3D Gaussian Splatting-based reconstruction methods decouple mesh extraction from the Gaussian representation, requiring offline conversion into an intermediate implicit field (e.g., Poisson reconstruction or Marching Cubes) before explicit mesh generation. This offline bottleneck prevents seamless incremental integration with downstream tasks such as real-time robotics and AR, where early access to partial geometry is critical. The paper addresses this gap by enabling direct, online triangulation of Gaussian surfels without any implicit field intermediary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260093/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of M RGB-D frames with known camera poses, incrementally building an oriented point cloud (position + normal per point) that seeds the Gaussian representation. At each timestamp, it produces a locally updated triangular mesh fused into a growing global surface, alongside photorealistic novel-view renderings. The active optimization window is limited (6 frames for Replica, 3 for ScanNet++), and fully converged regions are frozen to cap memory growth during long-sequence scanning.

## Method

The core representation constrains 3D Gaussians to act as planar elliptical surfels (third scale → 0, opacity ≈ 1), unifying rendering and surface geometry in a single primitive. A geometry-aware depth rendering computes ray–Gaussian plane intersections rather than standard alpha-blended depth, enabling precise geometric supervision. A plane-based pulling loss (L_plane) and a normal-consistency loss (L_n) dynamically align Gaussian centers and orientations to the local surface, correcting sensor noise. For meshing, a compressed-octree neighbor search with adaptive radius selects valid candidates (mutual visibility + normal consistency > 0.9), projects them onto the tangent plane, and applies an angle-based greedy triangulation (minimum subtended angle 10°) to form triangles. Incremental stitching uses isotropic local remeshing (edge split/collapse/flip + Laplacian smoothing), while a freezing mechanism removes fully optimized regions (observation count > N_obs, loss < ε_gs) from the active computation graph to ensure bounded memory.

## Results

On Replica, the method achieves the best average reconstruction accuracy of 1.34 cm (Acc. Ratio 99.70%, Comp. Ratio 85.95%), outperforming RTG-SLAM (1.41 cm, 99.54%, 81.71%) and MonoGS (2.77 cm, 86.73%, 81.83%). For rendering on Replica, it attains 37.85 dB PSNR, 0.99 SSIM, and 0.03 LPIPS, surpassing MonoGS (37.50 dB) and RTG-SLAM (35.43 dB). On ScanNet++, it achieves 24.40 dB PSNR on novel views, beating RTG-SLAM (21.65 dB) and Point-SLAM (22.53 dB). In terms of efficiency, the method runs at 10.34 FPS with 2325 MB peak memory, and extracts meshes in 5.34 s versus 444 s for GOF-Marching Cubes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KinectFusion [18] (TSDF-based), the method wins decisively on all reconstruction metrics (1.34 cm vs. 12.76 cm accuracy) and on rendering (37.85 vs. 27.39 dB PSNR). Against NICE-SLAM [29] (NeRF-based), it dominates in both geometry (1.34 vs. 8.16 cm) and rendering (37.85 vs. 27.39 dB PSNR). Against MonoGS [16], the method achieves substantially better reconstruction accuracy (1.34 vs. 2.77 cm, 99.70% vs. 86.73% Acc. Ratio) and slightly better rendering (37.85 vs. 37.50 dB PSNR, 0.99 vs. 0.96 SSIM). Against RTG-SLAM [19], the closest competitor, the method edges out on reconstruction (1.34 vs. 1.41 cm, 85.95% vs. 81.71% Comp. Ratio) and clearly wins on rendering (37.85 vs. 35.43 dB PSNR on Replica; 24.40 vs. 21.65 dB on ScanNet++ novel views). Against Point-SLAM [20], it wins on rendering (37.85 vs. 35.17 dB PSNR on Replica; 24.40 vs. 22.53 dB on ScanNet++). Against GOF [27] for mesh extraction efficiency, the method is ~83× faster (5.34 s vs. 444.26 s) and produces a 290× smaller mesh (3.32 MB vs. 964.95 MB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm by reinterpreting Gaussians from volumetric rendering primitives into planar surfel primitives that can be directly triangulated into meshes, bypassing the implicit-field-to-mesh pipeline (Poisson, Marching Cubes) used by SuGaR, 2D GS, and Gaussian Opacity Fields. The plane-based pulling constraint is conceptually inspired by GS-Pull's zero-level-set alignment, while the geometry-aware depth rendering follows RTG-SLAM's ray-surfel intersection. The local remeshing strategy adapts Botsch et al.'s isotropic mesh optimization for incremental stitching, and the overall incremental mapping framework is positioned against TSDF-based (KinectFusion) and NeRF-based (NICE-SLAM) online reconstruction, with the key departure being that mesh extraction is fused into the Gaussian optimization loop rather than treated as a separate offline step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The direct Gaussian-to-mesh triangulation eliminates the need for constructing and maintaining a global implicit field, yielding an ~80× speedup in mesh extraction (5.3 s vs. 444 s) and a 99% reduction in mesh file size compared to GOF-Marching Cubes, which is practically significant for real-time robotics and AR applications. The freezing strategy for fully-optimized regions addresses a real scalability bottleneck in long-sequence scanning. However, the method's reliance on depth input limits its applicability to RGB-only settings (e.g., monocular video), and the accuracy gains over RTG-SLAM are modest (~0.07 cm average), suggesting the main value is in the unified rendering-plus-meshing pipeline and efficiency rather than a fundamental accuracy breakthrough.
