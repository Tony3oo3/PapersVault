## Problem

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting methods bypass per-scene optimization but suffer a persistent fidelity gap compared to optimization-based approaches. The paper attributes this gap to the limited capacity of single-step networks to regress static Gaussian primitives that simultaneously satisfy all viewpoints, resulting in blurred details and an inability to capture non-Lambertian effects such as sharp specularities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260623/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N unposed RGB images (no precomputed SfM poses) at 256×256 resolution (224×224 for the V2-L variant) and jointly produces 3D Gaussian primitives in a canonical space, per-view camera poses, and novel view renderings. A key structural detail is that the scene-conditioned View MLP weights are generated once during inference, while the lightweight per-pixel View MLPs are re-evaluated for each target viewpoint during rendering, which is what enables real-time frame rates despite view-dependent refinement.

## Method

ViewSplat builds on the SPFSplat/SPFSplatV2 pose-free backbone (MASt3R or VGGT geometry transformer with DPT heads) and introduces a hypernetwork-style view-dependent head that generates per-pixel View MLP weights from context-view features. During rendering, each View MLP takes a compact 4D target pose representation (3D unit viewing direction + 1D distance, reparameterized from the full 4×4 extrinsic) and predicts residual offsets for all Gaussian attributes—position, scale, rotation, opacity, and SH coefficients—which are added element-wise to the canonical Gaussians before rasterization. The hypernetwork design decouples weight generation (computed once) from pose-conditioned offset evaluation (per-view), preserving real-time rendering speed. Training uses a frozen backbone with only the zero-initialized view-dependent head optimized under a combined rendering loss (MSE + LPIPS) and a geometric reprojection loss, all in a self-supervised, pose-free manner.

## Results

On RE10K, the largest variant (SPFSplatV2-L+ViewSplat) achieves 26.80 PSNR, 0.870 SSIM, and 0.124 LPIPS, outperforming the next-best pose-free method NoPoSplat* (25.03 PSNR) by 1.77 dB. On ACID, the same variant reaches 27.51 PSNR, 0.820 SSIM, and 0.149 LPIPS, surpassing NoPoSplat* (25.96 PSNR) by 1.55 dB. Across all three backbone variants (SPFSplat, V2, V2-L), adding ViewSplat consistently yields ~0.8–0.9 dB PSNR improvement over the corresponding static baseline, confirming the orthogonal gain of view-adaptive refinement. The large-backbone variant runs at 15 FPS inference and 90 FPS rendering on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViewSplat (all variants) achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on both RE10K and ACID, outperforming every baseline. The closest competitor is NoPoSplat* (supervised, with evaluation-time pose alignment), which reaches 25.03 PSNR on RE10K and 25.96 on ACID; ViewSplat V2-L exceeds these by 1.77 and 1.55 dB respectively. Among self-supervised pose-free methods, SPFSplatV2-L without ViewSplat (25.67 PSNR on RE10K) is the strongest static baseline, and adding ViewSplat lifts it to 26.80 dB. Pose-required methods (pixelSplat, MVSplat) trail by 2.8–3.0 dB on RE10K, while supervised pose-free methods (CoPoNeRF, Splatt3R) are 7–8 dB behind. The consistent ~0.8–0.9 dB gain across all three backbone sizes (SPFSplat, V2, V2-L) demonstrates that the view-adaptive refinement is orthogonal to backbone capacity and generalizes across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViewSplat builds directly on the SPFSplat/SPFSplatV2 family of self-supervised, pose-free 3D Gaussian splatting frameworks (Huang & Mikolajczyk), which use MASt3R or VGGT geometry-transformer backbones to jointly estimate camera poses and canonical Gaussians from unposed images. The key conceptual departure is from static primitive regression—where a single forward pass must predict Gaussian attributes that satisfy all viewpoints—to view-adaptive splatting, where scene-conditioned View MLPs (a hypernetwork formulation) predict per-view residual offsets for every Gaussian attribute. This positions the work at the intersection of feed-forward 3DGS, hypernetwork-based parameter generation, and the long line of view-dependent appearance modeling in NeRF and 3DGS extensions (GaussianShader, Spec-Gaussian, Scaffold-GS), but uniquely achieves it without per-scene optimization or precomputed poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck in feed-forward 3DGS: the inability of a single regression pass to produce Gaussians that simultaneously satisfy all viewpoints, which explains the persistent fidelity gap versus optimization-based methods. The view-adaptive paradigm is a clean conceptual shift that could generalize beyond 3DGS to other feed-forward 3D representations. However, the approach inherits the reconstruction-based limitation of not synthesizing unobserved regions, and the per-view re-evaluation of Gaussian attributes prevents caching a single static scene, capping rendering at 90–154 FPS and raising open questions about multi-view consistency across the family of view-conditioned approximations rather than a single rigid geometry.
