## Problem

Perception systems for open-vocabulary segmentation and OCR are built around a modular encoder-decoder pipeline that adds task-specific fusion mechanisms, complicating scaling and limiting cross-modal feature interaction. The paper asks whether this separation is truly necessary and, if not, what output interface can handle dense spatial predictions (zero to hundreds of instances) without prohibitive decoding cost. Additionally, standard referring benchmarks have saturated and conflate diverse capabilities into a single scalar, making it hard to diagnose where models actually fail on compositional prompts.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image at its native resolution and aspect ratio (up to a 1024² token budget, patch size 16) and a free-form text prompt. For segmentation, it autoregressively emits a 'Chain-of-Perception' sequence per instance — center coordinates, size, then a segmentation token — where coordinates and size are decoded via discrete bin logits (Fourier-feature encoded) and the segmentation token drives a lightweight dot-product head to produce a full-resolution binary mask. For the OCR extension, the same backbone takes a cropped document element and emits structured text (plain text, LaTeX for formulas, HTML for tables) via standard next-token prediction.

## Method

Falcon Perception is a single 600M-parameter early-fusion dense Transformer that processes image patches, text embeddings, and task tokens in one shared parameter space, using a hybrid attention mask (bidirectional among image tokens, causal for text/task tokens) so the same weights act as both vision encoder and autoregressive decoder. The key architectural insight is the 'Chain-of-Perception' output order (coord → size → seg per instance), which forces the model to resolve spatial ambiguity before committing to pixel-level details, eliminating the need for Hungarian matching or heavy mask decoders. Segmentation masks are produced by a simple dot product between the <seg> token's hidden state and a high-resolution feature map obtained via a frozen, model-agnostic learned upsampler (AnyUp), keeping the head lightweight. The model uses 3D rotary positional embeddings (1D sequence RoPE + 2D GGRoPE for spatial grid positions), Fourier-feature encoding for continuous coordinates, and a scatter-and-pack strategy with FlexAttention to handle variable-length native-resolution images. Training follows a two-stage recipe: multi-teacher distillation from DINOv3 and SigLIP2 for visual initialization, followed by ~700 GT of supervised fine-tuning with a three-stage curriculum (in-context listing → query-masked task alignment → long-context crowded-scene adaptation), using the Muon optimizer and Gram-matrix feature regularization.

## Results

On SA-Co open-vocabulary segmentation, Falcon Perception (600M) achieves 68.0 Macro-F1, outperforming SAM 3 (62.3) by 5.7 points, though it trails on presence calibration (MCC 0.64 vs. 0.82). On the new PBench, Falcon Perception scores 57.0 average macro-F1 versus 44.4 for SAM 3 and 52.7 for Qwen3-VL-30B, with the largest gains on spatial grounding (L3: 53.5 vs. 31.6, +21.9) and the Dense crowdedness stress test (72.6 vs. 58.4 for SAM 3, and 72.6 vs. 8.9 for Qwen3-VL-30B). The compact 300M Falcon-OCR variant reaches 80.3% on olmOCR and 88.64% on OmniDocBench, competitive with much larger OCR systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM 3 (the strongest promptable segmentation baseline), Falcon Perception wins decisively on compositional and dense tasks: +21.9 on spatial grounding (L3), +13.4 on OCR-guided disambiguation (L2), and +14.2 on the Dense split, while also leading on overall SA-Co Macro-F1 (68.0 vs. 62.3). However, SAM 3 retains a clear advantage in presence calibration (MCC 0.82 vs. 0.64) and positive micro-F1 (67.8 vs. 62.1) on SA-Co, indicating Falcon Perception over-predicts on hard-negative splits like Wiki-Common. Versus the Qwen3-VL family, the 0.6B Falcon Perception matches or exceeds the 8B model on spatial (L3: 53.5 vs. 49.1) and relational (L4: 49.1 vs. 50.9) tasks, and dramatically surpasses the 30B model on the Dense split (72.6 vs. 8.9), where generalist VLMs collapse under instance crowding. Against Moondream2-2B and Moondream3-9B, Falcon Perception leads on all compositional levels (L1–L4) and the Dense split despite being 3×–15× smaller. On OCR, Falcon-OCR (300M) is competitive with Chandra (80.3% vs. 82.0% olmOCR) and beats Mistral OCR 3 on OmniDocBench (88.64 vs. 85.20), but trails PaddleOCR VL 1.5 (94.37) and Chandra (88.97) on OmniDocBench overall, with remaining failures concentrated in tiny text and degraded scans.

## Relation to Prior Work

Falcon Perception departs from the dominant modular encoder-decoder paradigm (SAM 3, OWL-ViT, Pix2Seq, Qwen3-VL) by adopting a strictly shared early-fusion Transformer, a direction explored by LIMoE, Fuyu, and SAIL for general VLMs but not previously applied to dense perception tasks. It repurposes autoregressive sequence-to-sequence detection (Pix2Seq) with a 'Chain-of-Perception' output ordering (coord → size → seg) and lightweight specialized heads (Fourier-feature coordinate decoders, dot-product mask generation via a model-agnostic upsampler), while initializing from multi-teacher distillation (DINOv3, SigLIP2) rather than a pretrained LLM. The hybrid attention mask (bidirectional for image tokens, causal for text/task tokens) and 3D GGRoPE positional encoding are specific design choices that distinguish it from prior early-fusion VLMs, which typically reintroduce modality-specific components (MoE, separate projections).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the working assumption that dense perception requires a separate vision encoder and decoder, showing that a single 600M early-fusion Transformer can match or exceed 9B+ modular systems on compositional grounding (spatial, OCR-guided, relational) and dramatically outperform generalist VLMs on crowded scenes (72.6 vs. 8.9 on PBench Dense vs. Qwen3-VL-30B). The resolution 'dense phase transition' finding—that spatial detail, not semantic recognition, is the bottleneck for crowded scenes—has practical implications for deployment. Open questions include the persistent gap in presence calibration (MCC 0.64 vs. 0.82 for SAM 3), whether the architecture extends to more complex tasks (VQA, visual prompting), and whether the PBench results generalize beyond the ~5k-sample benchmark. The 300M Falcon-OCR extension suggests the unified backbone may serve as a general perception substrate, but this remains underexplored.
