## Problem

Existing vision-based 3D occupancy prediction methods rely on explicit physical projection (e.g., Lift-Splat-Shoot), which constrains the 2D-to-3D routing matrix to sparse camera-ray supports. This 'Locality Bottleneck' prevents any 3D voxel from receiving direct evidence from non-aligned pixels, fragmenting continuous structures and causing catastrophic failure when camera extrinsics are noisy or absent. Additionally, achieving global contextual reachability inherently requires spatial aggregation that sacrifices local geometric precision, creating a fundamental Resolution-Context Trade-off that no single operator can resolve.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view surround RGB images (256×704 for nuScenes, 640×960 for Waymo) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus a lightweight depth head and Plücker ray coordinates as soft geometric context for routing. It outputs a dense 3D semantic occupancy grid at 0.4 m voxel resolution (17 classes on nuScenes, 14 on Waymo), structured internally as three axis-aligned planes (XY, XZ, YZ) that are spatially broadcast and summed before an MLP decoder. Notably, the framework can operate in an uncalibrated mode where explicit camera parameters are withheld and replaced by a convolutional head that blindly predicts rough poses from images.

## Method

The core contribution is Factorized Dense Routing (FDR), which approximates an intractable dense 2D-to-3D bipartite routing matrix as a product of T=3 stage-wise tensor contractions, progressively contracting 2D patches (4×4, 2×4, 2×2) while expanding 3D micro-volumes (10×10, 5×5, 4×4), achieving fully-global receptive field at ~1.4% of dense computation. Dynamic routing weights at each stage are generated by a lightweight convolutional network conditioned on intermediate features concatenated with soft geometric context (depth + Plücker coordinates), treating physical geometry as an inductive bias rather than a hard constraint. To resolve the Resolution-Context Trade-off, the paper introduces a decoupled architecture: FDR serves as a Global Context Pathway that compresses the Z-axis into a gravity-aligned BEV anchor, while classical LSS serves as a Local Resolution Pathway preserving pixel-exact 2D spatial precision. The two orthogonal pathways are unified via simple element-wise addition on the shared XY (BEV) plane, after which a 2D encoder, Z-axis recovery, and tri-plane fusion produce the final 3D occupancy volume. The framework is trained end-to-end with a joint loss combining 3D BCE+Dice, auxiliary depth supervision, and 2D perspective-view semantic segmentation.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes (single-frame, ResNet-50, 256×704), FDR-Occ achieves 41.2% mIoU and 71.0% IoU, outperforming the strongest single-frame baseline ALOcc (40.1% mIoU) by +1.1 points. With one historical frame (+1f), it reaches 42.2% mIoU and 72.1% IoU, surpassing DHD-M (41.5%) and COTR (41.4%). On Occ3D-Waymo (16-frame fusion), FDR-Occ attains 31.25% mIoU, exceeding ALOcc-3D (30.03%) by +1.22 points and CVT-Occ (27.37%) by +3.88 points. In the uncalibrated setting (no camera extrinsics), FDR-Occ maintains 28.0% mIoU while physical-projection baselines collapse to 4.7–12.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Occ3D-nuScenes (single-frame), FDR-Occ beats ALOcc by +1.1 mIoU (41.2 vs 40.1), ProtoOcc by +1.6 (41.2 vs 39.6), and COTR by +2.1 (41.2 vs 39.1), with the largest gains on spatially continuous classes like driveable surface (+0.4 over ALOcc) and small objects like bicycle (+2.1). In the +1f setting, it surpasses DHD-M by +0.7 mIoU (42.2 vs 41.5) and COTR by +0.8 (42.2 vs 41.4). On Occ3D-Waymo, FDR-Occ leads ALOcc-3D by +1.22 mIoU (31.25 vs 30.03) and CVT-Occ by +3.88 (31.25 vs 27.37), with particularly large margins on Road (+2.79 over ALOcc-3D) and Walkable (+4.55). In the uncalibrated robustness test, FDR-Occ (28.0% mIoU) outperforms the best physical-projection baseline FB-Occ (12.0%) by a factor of 2.3×, demonstrating that its global routing internalizes camera rig topology without explicit extrinsics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the 2D-to-3D view transformation problem in occupancy prediction as a bipartite spatial routing problem, arguing that classical physical-projection methods (LSS, BEVDet) impose a 'Restricted Reachability' bottleneck by constraining the routing matrix to ray-local sparsity. It builds directly on LSS as the local-resolution pathway and on ALOcc for auxiliary supervision and temporal fusion, but departs fundamentally by introducing Factorized Dense Routing (FDR) as a global-context pathway that approximates unconstrained dense mixing via hierarchical tensor contractions. The Resolution-Context Decoupled Architecture then unifies these two orthogonal pathways on a shared BEV plane, a design choice that contrasts with prior work (e.g., ProtoOcc, FB-OCC) that attempts to enrich context within fixed projection patterns rather than relaxing the routing structure itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal proof that physical projection is a strict subset of the global routing family provides a clean theoretical justification for why ray-based methods fragment continuous structures, and the uncalibrated robustness experiment (28% mIoU vs. 4.7–12% for baselines) suggests that learned global routing can internalize multi-camera rig topology without explicit calibration—a property with practical relevance for real-world deployment where extrinsics drift. However, the main SOTA margins are modest (+1.1 mIoU on nuScenes single-frame, +1.22 on Waymo), all results use ResNet-50, and the uncalibrated protocol is somewhat contrived (a lightweight head still predicts rough poses). Open questions include whether FDR's factorization scales efficiently to larger backbones or higher-resolution 3D grids, whether the decoupled architecture generalizes beyond driving scenes, and whether the soft geometric conditioning can be further relaxed toward fully geometry-free operation.
