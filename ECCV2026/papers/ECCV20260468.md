## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods face a fundamental trade-off: structural anomaly detectors model local patch features and miss logical violations (missing components, wrong counts, misplaced parts), while logical anomaly detectors rely on global semantic branches that discard spatial structure, resulting in poor detection of subtle structural defects and an inability to produce pixel-level anomaly maps. No prior method achieves strong performance on both anomaly types simultaneously with fine-grained localization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a set of text prompts naming the expected component categories (e.g., {"Peach", "Orange"}), which are embedded via DINOv3's text encoder to drive open-vocabulary semantic segmentation in a joint image-text embedding space. At inference, it produces a patch-level anomaly map (h×w) that is upsampled to full image resolution for pixel-level localization, with the maximum value serving as the image-level anomaly score. Three separate anomaly maps (logical, structural, and segmentation-discriminator) are normalized using validation-set statistics and fused via element-wise maximum.

## Method

LogiCo discretizes DINOv3 pre-trained features into a component-level representation by computing per-component prototypes via average pooling and assigning each spatial location its component's prototype through a lookup table, preserving spatial layout while filtering textural detail. A Component-level Reconstruction Network (CRN, an Autoencoder with self-attention) is trained to restore pseudo-anomalous features—synthesized by manipulating segmentation maps via four strategies (deletion, replacement, addition, erasure)—back to the original pre-trained features using cosine distance loss, capturing inter-component logical constraints without discarding spatial structure. A Structural Reconstruction Network (SRN, a U-Net) reconstructs fine-grained features under cross-attention guidance from the CRN's anomaly-free intermediate features, preventing the 'identical shortcut' problem while enabling precise structural anomaly detection. A lightweight Segmentation Map Discriminator (SMD, a fully convolutional U-Net trained with focal loss) directly predicts anomaly masks from segmentation maps to catch quantity-related anomalies. All three branches are jointly trained, and at inference their normalized anomaly maps are fused via element-wise maximum to produce the final detection and localization outputs.

## Results

On the logical anomaly benchmark MVTec-LOCO, LogiCo achieves 96.3% I-AUC and 72.9% sPRO, surpassing the best prior methods (SALAD: 93.2/63.3; CSAD: 92.9/70.6) by 3.1 and 2.3 points respectively. On structural anomaly benchmarks, LogiCo matches or exceeds top methods: 99.6% I-AUC on MVTec-AD (tied with Dinomaly), 98.5% I-AUC on VisA (vs. 98.9% for Dinomaly), and 93.8% I-AUC on Real-IAD (vs. 93.7% for Dinomaly). Notably, LogiCo achieves the highest P-AP on VisA (60.1%) and Real-IAD (53.3%), outperforming Dinomaly by 2.4 and 5.2 points respectively. Inference speed is 17.4 FPS at 512×512 resolution on a single RTX 5090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MVTec-LOCO (the dedicated logical anomaly benchmark), LogiCo decisively outperforms all baselines: it beats SALAD by 3.1 I-AUC and 9.6 sPRO, CSAD by 3.4 I-AUC and 2.3 sPRO, and structural-only methods like Dinomaly by 12.9 I-AUC. On MVTec-AD, LogiCo ties Dinomaly on I-AUC (99.6) but trails slightly on P-AP (70.2 vs. 71.2) and PRO (95.0 vs. 95.5); it nonetheless surpasses all logical-specific methods (CSAD, SALAD) by 3.8–4.2 I-AUC and 13–14 PRO points. On VisA, LogiCo trails Dinomaly by 0.4 I-AUC and 1.1 PRO but leads on P-AP (60.1 vs. 57.7), while outperforming CSAD and SALAD by 7.9 and 1.1 I-AUC respectively. On Real-IAD, LogiCo edges out Dinomaly on I-AUC (93.8 vs. 93.7) and P-AP (53.3 vs. 48.1) but is marginally behind on PRO (95.3 vs. 95.7). The central finding is that no prior method matches LogiCo's simultaneous strength on both anomaly types: logical-specific methods (CSAD, SALAD) lose 5–15 I-AUC on structural benchmarks, while structural-specific methods (Dinomaly, INP-Former) lose 10–13 I-AUC on MVTec-LOCO.

## Relation to Prior Work

LogiCo carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: local-feature reconstruction methods for structural anomalies (PatchCore, Dinomaly) and global-semantic-modeling methods for logical anomalies (GCAD, CSAD, SALAD, PSAD). Rather than adding a spatially-agnostic global branch as prior logical AD methods do, it repurposes the reconstruction paradigm by operating in a discretized component-level feature space that preserves spatial layout, and bridges the two detection modes via a cross-attention mechanism where the anomaly-free CRN features guide the SRN. The use of DINOv3 open-vocabulary segmentation (instead of training dedicated segmentation models as in CSAD/PSAD/SAM-LAD) is a deliberate simplification that reduces pipeline complexity while maintaining robustness to segmentation noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying logical and structural anomaly detection in a single spatially-aware framework, LogiCo removes the practical need to deploy separate models for different anomaly types, which is significant for industrial quality control where both defect types coexist. The hand-crafted anomaly synthesis strategies (deletion, replacement, addition, erasure) are simple and customizable per product, but the authors acknowledge that learnable synthesis and broader adaptability remain open. A notable limitation is that the SMD module, while expanding detection scope for count-related anomalies, degrades pixel-level localization (sPRO drops ~2.5 points), suggesting a fundamental tension between quantity-aware detection and fine-grained localization that the field has not yet resolved.
