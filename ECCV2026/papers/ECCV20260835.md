## Problem

Arbitrary-scale image super-resolution faces a fundamental fidelity-perception trade-off: regression-based methods produce structurally faithful but overly smooth reconstructions, while diffusion-based methods recover realistic textures at the risk of structural hallucinations. No existing framework can adaptively balance these two feature types across spatial regions and upscaling factors, since the relative utility of each varies with local content and scale in a non-uniform manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260835/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image and an arbitrary continuous upscaling factor r = {r_h, r_w} (e.g., ×2 to ×16, including non-integer values like ×1.6 or ×4.3) and produces a high-resolution RGB image at the target resolution. Reconstruction is performed by querying a continuous 2D coordinate space with a local implicit decoder, so a single trained model serves all scales without retraining. The fidelity extractor (SwinIR) and perceptual generator (Stable Diffusion v1.5) are pretrained and frozen; only the attention, selection, and decoder modules are finetuned on DIV2K + Flickr2K.

## Method

FPLIA integrates two complementary feature streams—fidelity-oriented features from a pixel-regression backbone and perceptual features from a diffusion generator—through two novel modules. The Fidelity and Perception Attention Module (FPAM) extends cross-scale local attention to a dual-stream architecture, applying self-attention within each stream and asymmetric cross-attention in both directions (fidelity→perceptual and perceptual→fidelity), yielding four candidate features with distinct fidelity-perception profiles at every queried coordinate. The Fidelity and Perception Select Module (FPSM) estimates per-candidate confidence scores by fusing local attention maps, dense stream-level confidence maps, and the cell-size encoding of the target scale, then performs discrete per-pixel selection via Gumbel-Softmax during training and hard argmax at inference. All five output branches (selected plus four candidates) are supervised with L2 losses, where perceptual-stream losses use a μ/σ weighting from the one-step diffusion approximation, ensuring each candidate is independently informative for the selection landscape.

## Results

On Set14 at ×4, FPLIA achieves 28.48 PSNR and 0.188 LPIPS, improving LPIPS by 29.6% over the best regression baseline (HIIF: 0.267 LPIPS) while incurring only a 2.13% PSNR drop. Against the strongest diffusion baseline (Kim et al.: 27.77 PSNR, 0.221 LPIPS), FPLIA simultaneously improves both PSNR (+2.56%) and LPIPS (−14.9%). This Pareto-optimal balance holds across all four evaluation datasets (Set5, Set14, B100, Urban100) and upscaling factors from ×4 to ×16, with the PSNR trade-off versus regression methods never exceeding 2.6% and LPIPS gains over diffusion methods reaching up to 7.4% at ×12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the five regression-based baselines (LIIF, LTE, LIT, CiaoSR, HIIF, all with SwinIR), FPLIA consistently trades a small PSNR decrease (1.8–3.5%) for a large LPIPS improvement (up to 29.6% on Set14 ×4), since pixel-regression features alone cannot synthesize high-frequency textures. Against the two diffusion-based baselines, FPLIA wins on both metrics simultaneously across the majority of dataset–scale combinations: versus Kim et al. [13] (the strongest diffusion method), FPLIA gains +2.56% PSNR and −14.9% LPIPS on Set14 ×4, with the PSNR advantage growing to +7.4% at ×12. IDM [7] is uniformly outperformed by both FPLIA and Kim et al. on both metrics, confirming it as the weaker diffusion baseline. On Urban100, FPLIA is the only method that beats the best regression baseline on LPIPS while also exceeding the best diffusion method on PSNR, demonstrating the Pareto-frontier positioning. The overhead is minimal: FPLIA adds <5% inference latency and <2% GPU memory over Kim et al.'s pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

FPLIA extends the local implicit image function paradigm established by LIIF and refined by CiaoSR's cross-scale local attention, but departs from the single-stream design by operating jointly over two heterogeneous feature sources: a SwinIR-based pixel-regression backbone (fidelity) and a Stable Diffusion latent (perceptual). The closest prior effort, Kim et al. (CVPR 2024), conditions a diffusion pipeline on regression features but keeps the two streams loosely coupled; FPLIA introduces bidirectional cross-attention (FPAM) and a discrete per-pixel selection mechanism (FPSM) to enable asymmetric, content-aware interaction. The framework is explicitly backbone-agnostic, treating the fidelity extractor and perceptual generator as interchangeable plug-ins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FPLIA demonstrates that the fidelity-perception trade-off in ASISR need not be a hard dichotomy: by adaptively selecting among four cross-attention candidates per pixel, it simultaneously improves PSNR and LPIPS over the strongest diffusion baseline across most benchmark-scale combinations, while sacrificing only ~2–3% PSNR relative to the best regression methods. The scale-dependent selection statistics (fidelity features lose share as upscaling factor grows) provide a concrete, interpretable account of why fixed blending fails. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SR to other restoration tasks, whether the ~92 s diffusion inference cost can be reduced without quality loss, and whether the Gumbel-Softmax selection can be replaced by a differentiable alternative that avoids the train/inference gap.
