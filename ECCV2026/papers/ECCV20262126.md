## Problem

Event camera research has been dominated by automotive and drone applications, while human-centric daily-life scenarios remain largely underrepresented. Existing event-based human activity datasets are constrained by scripted actions, fixed viewpoints, low resolution (typically ≤346×260), or synthetic event generation, failing to capture the variability and spontaneity of natural human behavior in diverse real-world environments.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 5.5 hours of stereo event streams at 1280×720 from Prophesee Gen4 cameras, synchronized with RGB (30 fps CMOS, 15 fps D-RGB), 16-bit depth (15 fps), and 6-axis IMU (200 fps) data. Annotations are performed on D-RGB frames and projected to the event camera domain via 3D point reconstruction and reprojection using intrinsic/extrinsic calibration matrices, since direct annotation on event data is impractical. Evaluation uses a kitchen-level split (9 train / 4 test kitchens) to guarantee unseen environments, with tasks producing action labels (268 verb+noun classes), bounding boxes (12 kitchenware classes), and depth maps restricted to the 200–1500 mm range.

## Method

The core contribution is the EventKitchen dataset and its multi-sensor collection and annotation pipeline. The data collection device is a wearable bicycle helmet integrating two Prophesee Gen4 event cameras forming a stereo pair, two CMOS RGB cameras, an Intel RealSense depth camera, and a 6-axis IMU, all synchronized via ROS timestamps and calibrated using E2Calib with sub-2-pixel reprojection error. Data is collected from 10 participants across 13 diverse kitchens performing 13 unscripted cooking activities with no instructions, ensuring natural behavior. Annotation employs a three-annotator verification pipeline for action segments (verb+noun labels following the EPIC-KITCHENS taxonomy) and a two-annotator pipeline for bounding boxes on D-RGB frames, which are projected to event cameras via 3D point reconstruction and edge smoothing. Baseline evaluations deploy TSM (ResNet-50) and Swin-Base for action recognition, YOLOv10-x, RVT-Base, and EvRT-DETR-B for object detection, and SE-CFF with SBN event representation plus zero-shot FoundationStereo for stereo depth estimation, all fine-tuned or trained on the dataset to demonstrate its difficulty.

## Results

On EventKitchen, action recognition baselines achieve only 19.23% (TSM) and 24.69% (Swin) top-1 accuracy on 69 action classes, and 38.17% / 46.08% top-1 on 18 verb classes. For object detection across 12 kitchenware classes, YOLOv10 reaches the highest mAP of 16.2%, while event-specific detectors RVT (7.6%) and EvRT-DETR (8.5%) perform substantially worse. For stereo depth estimation, SE-CFF achieves RMSE of 84.91 mm and MAE of 54.84 mm (15M@1Hz), outperforming the zero-shot FoundationStereo model (RMSE 155.06 mm, MAE 123.85 mm), though both remain well above the ground-truth depth standard deviation of 106.26 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For action recognition, Swin (24.69% top-1) outperforms TSM (19.23% top-1) by over 5 points on 69 action classes, and similarly leads on verb classification (46.08% vs. 38.17% top-1), but both remain far below practical utility, underscoring the dataset's difficulty. For object detection, the frame-based YOLOv10 (mAP 16.2%) surprisingly outperforms both event-specific detectors RVT (mAP 7.6%) and EvRT-DETR (mAP 8.5%), which the authors attribute to the latter being specialized for high-frequency non-human annotations in automotive 1Mpx data; all three struggle most with small, fast-moving objects like forks and spoons. For stereo depth estimation, SE-CFF trained from scratch on EventKitchen (RMSE 84.91 mm at 15M@1Hz) substantially outperforms the zero-shot FoundationStereo model (RMSE 155.06 mm), confirming that event-specific training is necessary and that direct event-to-frame conversion via E2VID is insufficient for foundation models. Across all three tasks, the consistently low absolute performance of every baseline validates EventKitchen as a challenging benchmark that existing methods have not yet solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

EventKitchen extends the EPIC-KITCHENS paradigm of natural, unscripted egocentric human activity recording to the event-camera domain, but departs sharply from prior event-based human activity datasets (ASLAN-DVS, HARDVS, DailyDVS-200, SeAct) that rely on scripted actions, fixed viewpoints, or low-resolution sensors. It also moves beyond synthetic event streams generated by simulators (e.g., N-EPIC-Kitchens via ESIM) and monitor-recording setups (UCF-Crime-DVS), which the authors argue suffer from sim-to-real gaps and are constrained by display parameters. The multi-modal stereo setup (event + RGB + depth + IMU) draws on the multi-sensor calibration pipelines developed for automotive event datasets like DSEC and MVSEC, but repurposes them for indoor, human-centric perception with a kitchen-level train/test split to enforce environmental generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EventKitchen addresses a critical gap: event-based vision research has been overwhelmingly dominated by automotive and drone applications, leaving human-centric daily-life scenarios underrepresented. The dramatically low baseline performances (19–25% top-1 action recognition, 7–16% AP detection) confirm the dataset's difficulty and open substantial room for methodological innovation in egocentric event-based perception, with direct relevance to AR/VR, wearable AI, and human-robot interaction. Key open questions include how to handle the long-tail class distributions in both actions and objects, how to scale annotations beyond the current 13,482 bounding boxes, and whether event-specific architectures can be adapted to transfer from high-frequency automotive annotations to slower, more variable human-centric scenes.
