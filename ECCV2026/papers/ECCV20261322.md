## Problem

Selecting the best pre-trained segmentation model from a repository for a new, distribution-shifted target dataset remains difficult because existing transferability metrics are designed for classification, require target labels or feature-space access, and cannot handle instance segmentation. No prior method simultaneously satisfies the practical constraints of being unsupervised, source-free, model-agnostic, and applicable to both semantic and instance segmentation in a black-box setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261322/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of candidate pre-trained segmentation models and a small set of unlabelled target images (2D microscopy patches or 3D CBCT volumes). Each model is run twice per image—once on the clean input and once under a perturbation (Gaussian noise, gamma correction, or test-time dropout applied to intermediate feature layers)—yielding paired predictions. The output is a single scalar consistency score per model (CMR-EI, CMR-NHD, or CMR-ARS) and the resulting descending ranking; for instance segmentation, the comparison operates on permutation-invariant pixel-pair agreement rather than fixed label correspondence, and only the maximum probability per voxel is stored, making it memory-efficient for large 3D volumes.

## Method

CMR ranks models by measuring pixel-wise prediction consistency between clean and perturbed outputs, treating consistency as a proxy for decision-boundary margin on the target domain. For semantic segmentation, two metrics are defined: CMR-EI (a soft, confidence-weighted per-class invariance score extending Effective Invariance) and CMR-NHD (a hard, thresholded Normalised Hamming Distance over foreground-restricted pixels). For instance segmentation, where labels are permutation-invariant, CMR-ARS uses the foreground-restricted Adapted Rand Score to measure agreement of pixel-pair co-assignment between the two predictions, decomposable into split and merge penalty terms. Perturbations are applied either in input space (additive Gaussian noise, gamma correction) or feature space (Test-Time Dropout at the bottleneck or across all layers), sampled over a range of strengths so that rankings depend on a distribution rather than a single tuned parameter. The final per-model score is the median over target images of the mean consistency across perturbations, requiring only two inference passes per image and no access to source data, target labels, or internal feature representations.

## Results

On semantic segmentation (mitochondria and nuclei, 2D), CMR-EI and CMR-NHD achieve Kendall tau correlations of 0.69–0.77 with true F1 ranking, substantially outperforming all unsupervised baselines (TS: 0.02–0.25, NuNo: 0.09–0.17, Dispersion: −0.18 to −0.03). On the 3D multi-class ToothFairy2 challenge (42 classes), CMR-NHD (Gauss) reaches Kτ = 0.90 and Sρ = 0.96 overall, reproducing the challenge's DICE ranking without test labels. For instance segmentation, CMR-ARS achieves Kτ = 0.69–0.80 across cell and nuclei targets, versus SEG's 0.12–0.48. Post-UDA (Mean Teacher, AdaBN), CMR-EI maintains Kτ = 0.64–0.78 on three of four mitochondria datasets, outperforming Transfer Score (Kτ = 0.38–0.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMR decisively outperforms all three unsupervised baselines: Transfer Score (TS) achieves Kτ of only 0.25 (Mito) and 0.02 (Nuclei), NuNo reaches 0.17 and 0.09, and Dispersion is near zero or negative (−0.03, −0.18), while CMR variants consistently reach 0.69–0.77. Among supervised methods, LEEP is the only one that reliably correlates with segmentation performance (Kτ = 0.89 Mito, 0.67 Nuclei), but it requires target labels; all other supervised metrics (NLEEP, GBC, LogME, NCTI, RegHscore, CCFV) fail, with several showing negative correlations, confirming that feature-space assumptions from classification do not transfer to segmentation. On the 3D ToothFairy2 challenge, CMR-NHD (Gauss) achieves Kτ = 0.90 overall versus TS (0.33), NuNo (0.25), and Dispersion (0.28), and is the only method to maintain strong per-class-group correlations across morphologically diverse structures. For instance segmentation, CMR-ARS (Kτ = 0.69–0.80) dramatically outperforms SEG (Kτ = 0.12–0.48), which reduces instances to centroids and is highly sensitive to its two hyperparameters. Post-UDA, CMR-EI (Kτ = 0.64–0.78) outperforms TS (Kτ = 0.38–0.55) under both Mean Teacher and AdaBN, though both methods degrade on the VNC dataset due to class confusion from visually similar structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Effective Invariance (EI) framework from classification [20] and the Adapted Rand Score from connectomics [4] into a unified consistency-based ranking method for segmentation, operating entirely in output space rather than feature space. It deliberately departs from the dominant paradigm of supervised transferability metrics (LEEP, NLEEP, LogME, GBC) and UDA validation (Transfer Score), which rely on target labels, feature-space geometric assumptions (compactness, separability), or training-specific constraints that are incompatible with black-box repository models. The key conceptual shift is treating perturbation-induced pixel label flips as a proxy for decision-boundary margin, sidestepping the feature-space reasoning that prior methods require and that the authors show fails for segmentation losses (Dice, cross-entropy) that do not enforce feature compactness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CMR provides the first practical, black-box-compatible method for ranking heterogeneous segmentation models (CNNs, Transformers, State Space Models, generalist models like μSAM and Cellpose-SAM) in repository settings without target labels, requiring only two inference passes per image. This is directly relevant to the growing ecosystem of model zoos (BioImage Model Zoo, HuggingFace) where practitioners face dozens of candidate models for a new dataset. Key limitations include the assumption of source-target task alignment (problematic for generalist models without explicit task specification) and alignment with pixel-wise metrics (F1, mAP) rather than boundary-sensitive measures. Open questions include whether cross-model prediction diversity can mitigate the 'mode collapse' pathology, and whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric instance segmentation with more complex instantiation strategies.
