## Problem

Existing DPO-based preference optimization for MLLMs treats the input as a monolithic unit and never explicitly rewards a larger preference margin when richer contextual information (e.g., auxiliary captions) is available. The paper introduces Contextual Preference Gain (CPG) to quantify this gap and shows that standard DPO and its variants exhibit CPG concentrated near zero—a 'context blindness' that leaves models prone to object hallucination despite enriched training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262646/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a triple input x = (v, q, c) consisting of an RGB image, a text query, and an auxiliary non-hallucinated caption, paired with a preferred (grounded) and dispreferred (hallucinated) response. During training, a degraded counterpart x' = (v, q, ∅) is constructed by removing the auxiliary caption, so the model is simultaneously optimized on both the full and degraded contexts. At inference, the model produces a text response (caption or VQA answer) that is expected to be grounded in the visual evidence rather than relying on frequent co-occurrence priors.

## Method

C²-DPO augments the standard DPO loss with two additional terms: (1) a Contextual Preference Calibration loss L_c that applies a binary NCE-style contrastive objective between the full-context and degraded-context preference margins, directly maximizing CPG so the model learns to strengthen its preference for the grounded response as context becomes richer; and (2) a degraded-context DPO loss L_DPO(x') that anchors the preference score to remain positive even without the auxiliary caption, preventing degenerate solutions where CPG is widened by simply lowering the degraded-context score. The full objective is L_DPO(x) + λ_c·L_c(x,x') + λ_u·L_DPO(x'), jointly enforcing the ordering Δr̂(x) > Δr̂(x') > 0. The formulation is model-agnostic and plugs seamlessly into SimPO and RDPO variants, and extends to text-only LLMs by treating the query as the full context and the empty string as the degraded context.

## Results

On Object HalBench, C²-DPO reduces response-level hallucination to 1.6 and mention-level to 1.0 for Qwen2-VL-Instruct-2B (a 36% and 60% relative reduction over C-DPO), and to 4.8/2.7 for LLaVA-v1.5-7B. On AMBER, C²-DPO achieves a hallucination score of 16.1 (Qwen2-VL) and 13.8 (LLaVA), outperforming all baselines including vanilla-DPO (39.9) and C-DPO (17.5/14.9). General reasoning is preserved: ScienceQA, MM-Vet, and TextVQA scores remain at or near base-model levels (e.g., 77.2 vs. 76.9 on ScienceQA for Qwen2-VL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²-DPO outperforms the closest preference-optimization baseline C-DPO on every hallucination metric for both models: on Qwen2-VL-2B it cuts response-level hallucination from 2.5 to 1.6 (−36%) and mention-level from 1.6 to 1.0 (−60%), and on LLaVA-7B from 5.9→4.8 and 3.3→2.7. Against the next-best DPO variant TPO (5.6/3.2 on LLaVA) and RLAIF-V (7.8/4.2), C²-DPO leads by 0.8–3.0 points on Object HalBench response-level scores. On AMBER, C²-DPO achieves the lowest hallucination score (13.8 for LLaVA, 16.1 for Qwen2-VL) versus C-DPO (14.9/17.5) and vanilla-DPO (25.5/39.9). Contrastive-decoding baselines (VCD, OPERA, DoLa) are far behind on hallucination (Rsp. 44–51 on LLaVA) and do not report HallusionBench scores. Importantly, C²-DPO matches or slightly exceeds base-model scores on general reasoning (ScienceQA 69.5 vs. 66.8, MM-Vet 33.4 vs. 31.0, TextVQA 58.2 = 58.2 on LLaVA; 77.2 vs. 76.9, 49.1 vs. 49.9, 78.4 = 78.4 on Qwen2-VL), confirming that hallucination reduction does not degrade broader capabilities. The method also generalizes to SimPO and RDPO variants (Table 3) and to text-only LLMs on AlpacaEval 2 (Table 4), where C²-DPO improves LC from 24.1→25.9 and WR from 27.4→29.7 over plain DPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO lineage (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the recent C-DPO work (Peng et al., 2025) that enriches preference inputs with auxiliary captions to make hallucinated vs. non-hallucinated responses more discriminative. The key conceptual departure is the identification of 'context blindness': the standard DPO objective treats the input as a monolithic token sequence and its gradient never rewards a larger preference margin when richer context is supplied, so the enriched captions are not guaranteed to be exploited during alignment. C²-DPO addresses this by adding a contrastive calibration term that explicitly maximizes the Contextual Preference Gain (CPG) between full and degraded contexts, while a second term preserves correct preference ordering under degraded context. The formulation is shown to be orthogonal to the underlying preference objective, generalizing seamlessly to SimPO and RDPO variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The context-blindness diagnosis (CPG concentrated near zero across DPO, SimPO, and RDPO) is a useful conceptual finding that reframes how practitioners should think about preference optimization: enriching the data is insufficient if the objective does not explicitly reward leveraging that enrichment. The practical impact is a simple, plug-and-play loss term that yields substantial relative hallucination reductions (36% on Object HalBench for Qwen2-VL-2B) without degrading general reasoning, and the method transfers to text-only LLMs. Open questions include whether the context-calibration principle extends to other modalities (video, audio) or tasks beyond hallucination, and whether the CPG diagnostic can guide dataset curation or early-stopping in broader alignment pipelines.
