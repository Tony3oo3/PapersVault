## Problem

Existing adversarial attack methods on 3D point clouds enforce imperceptibility through local geometric constraints (curvature, normals, tangents) in Euclidean space, but they fail to model the intrinsic multi-scale hierarchical organization of 3D shapes. This omission allows perturbations to disrupt part-level and global structural relationships, producing adversarial examples that are perceptually incoherent despite low point-wise distortion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261147/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean point cloud of 1,024 points in R3 with a ground-truth class label and produces a perturbed point cloud of the same cardinality that causes misclassification by a target 3D DNN classifier. The input is consumed in two decomposed forms: a semantic hierarchy built from PartSLIP part labels (2-4 parts per object, merged into higher-level groups) and a geometric hierarchy built via three levels of recursive farthest point sampling. The output is a single adversarial point cloud whose per-point displacements are jointly constrained by both hierarchical decompositions.

## Method

HHA decomposes each point cloud into a semantic hierarchy (part-level segmentation via PartSLIP, iteratively merged into coarser groups) and a geometric hierarchy (binary tree from recursive FPS), then embeds every substructure into a Poincare ball of curvature c via the exponential map at the origin. For each component, a bidirectional Chamfer distance is computed in hyperbolic space using the Mobius-addition-based geodesic metric, yielding a per-component distortion penalty that penalizes structural displacement at that specific scale. The unified optimization objective combines the C&W misclassification loss, a standard Euclidean distortion term (CD + 0.1*HD), and weighted hyperbolic regularization terms for both semantic and geometric components, all optimized via 10 rounds of binary search with 500 Adam gradient steps each. The key insight is that negative curvature in hyperbolic space naturally encodes tree-like hierarchical relations, so constraining perturbations at each level of the decomposition in this geometry simultaneously preserves local fidelity and global structural coherence, something flat Euclidean constraints cannot achieve.

## Results

HHA achieves 100% attack success rate (ASR) on all five victim classifiers (PointNet, DGCNN, PTv1, PointMLP, Mamba3D) across both ModelNet40 and ScanObjectNN, matching the best baselines. On imperceptibility, HHA attains the lowest distortion across all six metrics (CD, HD, l₂, GR, Curv, EMD) for every model–dataset combination. For example, on PointNet/ModelNet40, HHA achieves CD = 0.603×10⁻⁴ and EMD = 0.361×10⁻², compared to 0.897×10⁻⁴ and 0.473×10⁻² for the next-best baseline (LBC). On the more challenging ScanObjectNN, HHA reaches CD = 0.474×10⁻⁴ and EMD = 0.438×10⁻² against PointNet, substantially below all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HHA outperforms all six baselines on every imperceptibility metric (CD, HD, l₂, GR, Curv, EMD) across all five victim classifiers and both datasets, while maintaining 100% ASR. The closest competitor is LBC (lattice-based barycentric coordinates), which is consistently second-best; HHA reduces CD by 17–47% relative to LBC (e.g., 0.603 vs. 0.897 on PointNet/ModelNet40, 1.321 vs. 2.987 on Mamba3D/ModelNet40). SI-Adv and GeoA³ achieve moderate imperceptibility but are 2–5× worse than HHA on CD and EMD. AOF shows competitive HD in some cases but lags on CD and EMD. HIT-Adv is dramatically worse, producing CD values 100–600× higher than HHA (e.g., 389.9 vs. 0.603 on PointNet/ModelNet40) and also suffers reduced ASR on PTv1 (45.2%) and PointMLP (48.9%). PGD and IFGM, the simplest gradient-based attacks, are orders of magnitude worse on all distortion metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of perturbation-based adversarial attacks on 3D point clouds by introducing hyperbolic geometry as the constraint space for imperceptibility. Prior methods enforce geometric consistency through Euclidean metrics (Chamfer, Hausdorff, l2), curvature regularization, tangent-aligned directions, or parametric surface representations, all of which assume locally flat geometry. HHA departs from this by decomposing point clouds into multi-scale geometric (FPS-based) and semantic (part-segmentation-based) hierarchies, then embedding each substructure into the Poincaré ball where negative curvature naturally encodes tree-like structural relations. The hyperbolic regularization term complements rather than replaces standard distortion penalties, and the optimization follows the C&W iterative scheme, making the method a drop-in enhancement to existing attack pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that hierarchical structure in 3D shapes is better preserved under attack when constraints are imposed in hyperbolic space, with consistent gains across five diverse classifiers and three datasets including the challenging real-world ScanObjectNN. The physical-world transferability (surviving 3D printing and re-scanning) and strong robustness under adversarial training (93.1% ASR) suggest practical relevance beyond synthetic benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to tasks beyond classification (segmentation, detection), how sensitive performance is to the quality of the semantic segmentation backbone, and whether the hierarchical hyperbolic constraint can be repurposed for defense or robustness certification as the authors suggest for future work.
