## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D visual grounding methods—whether symbol-based (encoding spatial relations as rigid coordinate predicates) or view-based (relying on partial, occluded camera views)—reconstruct global spatial understanding from local evidence, making them brittle on queries requiring long-range spatial dependencies, compositional relations, or room-level layout reasoning. This paper addresses the gap by asking how to provide a VLM with a holistic, occlusion-free spatial medium while still preserving fine-grained visual appearance, without requiring any 3D-language training data.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a 3D scene (point cloud with Mask3D instance proposals), a natural language query, and a multi-view RGB video sequence captured around the scene. The RGB sequence is not fed directly to the VLM; instead, it is used to select a single optimal viewpoint per target object for cropping detail patches. The output is the 3D bounding box (identified by instance ID) of the target object, with the VLM (GPT-4o) producing a free-form rationale before the final ID prediction. No ground-truth object class labels are required in the primary evaluation setting.

## Method

The framework decouples the 3D scene into a macro-level Semantic Spatial Layout (SSL) and micro-level Visual Detail Patches (VDP), guided by a Query-Guided Scene Filtering module that parses the query into entities with boolean perception flags (target, visual-verification, height, viewpoint) and soft-aligns them to scene instances. The SSL is constructed by projecting filtered instances onto the XY-plane as convex-hull polygons with categorical colors, numeric IDs, a coordinate grid, and a query-adaptive rotation that aligns the implied observer direction with the image's upward axis; Z-axis height is preserved via topological rendering order and optional textual descriptors. The VDP selects a single optimal 2D crop per relevant object by maximizing a joint fitness of geometric exposure (visible-point ratio plus normal-weighted surface facing) and composition quality (trapezoidal scale penalty plus truncation penalty), then concatenates crops into one compact patch. Finally, GPT-4o performs a 'Spatial-First, Visual-Second' reasoning pass: it first deduces spatial constraints from the SSL to narrow candidates, then verifies fine-grained attributes from the VDP, all under a single concise prompt that elicits zero-shot chain-of-thought without predefined relation rules.

## Results

On ScanRefer, the framework achieves 51.1% Acc@0.25 and 45.0% Acc@0.5 overall in the zero-shot setting, the best among all compared zero-shot methods, with the largest gains on the Multiple subset (39.7% Acc@0.5 vs. 30.0% for the next-best SeeGround). On Nr3D without ground-truth object classes, it reaches 63.2% overall accuracy (vs. 52.9% for LaSP and 46.1% for SeeGround), and 74.7% when GT classes are provided. Inference time is 10.9 s/query, roughly 2× faster than SPAZER (23.5 s) and ~5× faster than VLM-Grounder (50.3 s) while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanRefer, the proposed method (45.0% Acc@0.5) outperforms all zero-shot baselines, with the clearest margin on the Multiple subset where relational disambiguation is required (39.7% vs. 30.0% for SeeGround and 27.3% for CSVG). On the Unique subset, SeeGround (68.9%) slightly edges out the proposed method (66.8%), consistent with the authors' observation that category recognition alone suffices there. On Nr3D without GT classes, the framework (63.2%) surpasses LaSP (52.9%) by 10.3 points and SeeGround (46.1%) by 17.1 points, with the largest gains on the Hard and View-dependent splits. With GT object classes, it reaches 74.7%, beating Transcrib3D (70.2%) and LaSP (67.8%). In the agent-based efficiency comparison (Table 3), the method matches or exceeds SPAZER (49.2% vs. 48.8% on ScanRefer subset; 66.0% vs. 63.8% on Nr3D subset) while running in 10.9 s/query versus 23.5 s and 50.3 s respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in zero-shot 3D Visual Grounding by departing from both dominant paradigms: symbol-based methods (LLM-Grounder, ZSVG3D, CSVG, LaSP) that encode spatial relations as rigid coordinate predicates, and view-based methods (SeeGround, VLM-Grounder, SPAZER) that rely on partial, occlusion-prone camera observations. Rather than extending either line, it introduces a dual-granularity representation that decouples macro-level spatial layout (a BEV-projected convex-hull canvas) from micro-level object-centric visual patches, inspired by the HCI principle 'overview first, zoom and filter, then details-on-demand.' The Query-Guided Scene Filtering module and the 'Spatial-First, Visual-Second' prompting strategy are novel architectural choices that repurpose GPT-4o's reasoning without requiring task-specific training or complex symbolic pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a clean representational decomposition—global layout for spatial reasoning plus targeted crops for visual verification—can outperform both agent-based and view-based zero-shot methods while being roughly 2–5× faster in inference, making it a practical option for embodied AI applications like object-driven navigation. Key limitations include dependence on GPT-4o (API cost, reproducibility concerns), reliance on Mask3D's closed-set proposals for scene initialization, and the inherent Z-axis information loss in BEV projection that is only partially mitigated by topological ordering and textual height descriptors. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the dual-granularity principle transfers to other 3D vision-language tasks (e.g., 3D detection, scene graph generation), and whether smaller open-source VLMs can substitute for GPT-4o without significant performance degradation.
