## Problem

Existing Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT) methods primarily target catastrophic forgetting via Mixture-of-Experts or expansion-merge strategies, yet they remain vulnerable to negative interference during inference-time weight consolidation, where newly learned updates with opposing parameter signs cancel out and overwrite useful prior knowledge. This paper identifies that the sign pattern of LoRA weights is the key determinant of whether consolidation reinforces or degrades performance, and proposes an explicit mechanism to mitigate this interference.

## Input / Output

The model (LLaVA-v1.5-7B) receives an RGB image paired with a text instruction and generates a text response autoregressively. LoRA adapters (rank 16) are inserted into the linear layers of the language model, and the sign-guided adaptive tuning is applied to all layers except the final output layer for next-token prediction. During sequential training, each task provides image-instruction-answer triplets, and at inference the consolidated LoRA weights are merged into the frozen pre-trained backbone for unified multi-task generation.

## Method

SiGMA introduces sign-based weight decoupling, which partitions LoRA parameters into a general (sign-aligned) subspace and a specific (sign-conflicted) subspace relative to prior or anchor weights. During training, sign-guided adaptive tuning (S-Tuning) aggregates prior task LoRA matrices (summing A and B separately to explore a broader latent subspace), then dynamically extracts only the sign-aligned portion as a transferable basis, so the current task learns with minimal drift and reduced collision with prior knowledge. At inference, sign-guided LoRA merging (S-Merge) uses the first task's LoRA as a stable anchor; for each subsequent task, the specific (sign-opposing) subspace is filtered to retain only high-magnitude parameters (thresholded by a temperature λ relative to the anchor) and then amplified by a cosine-distance scaling factor d_k, while the general subspace is merged directly. This selective amplification preserves salient task-specific knowledge while suppressing noise-like conflicting parameters that cause destructive cancellation.

## Results

On the UCIT benchmark (6 tasks), SiGMA achieves 66.17% Avg. All accuracy and −4.81% Forgets, outperforming the second-best method DISCO (62.91%, −6.27%) by +3.26% and +1.46% respectively. On the DCL benchmark (5 domains), SiGMA reaches 62.30% Avg. All and −2.14% Forgets, surpassing DISCO (60.42%, −5.44%) by +1.88% and +3.30%. Notably, on the particularly challenging VizWiz task, SiGMA scores 54.83%, improving over the second-best method by +4.85%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiGMA outperforms all six baselines on both Avg. All and Forgets across both benchmarks. Against the closest competitor DISCO (ICCV'25), SiGMA gains +3.26% Avg. All on UCIT and +1.88% on DCL, while reducing forgetting by +1.46% and +3.30% respectively. HiDe LLaVA (ACL'25), which uses task-specific expansion with task-general fusion, is the second-strongest method but still trails SiGMA by +4.17% Avg. All on UCIT; notably, HiDe LLaVA achieves the highest single-task score on ImageNet-R (84.70 vs. 83.40) but suffers more on ArxivQA (90.53 vs. 94.07) and VizWiz (49.98 vs. 54.83). The MoE-based methods (MoE LoRA, CL-MoE) and orthogonal-subspace method (O-LoRA) all cluster around 58–59% Avg. All on UCIT with severe forgetting (−13.7% to −15.3%), far below SiGMA. The simplest baseline, LoRA-FT, shows the worst forgetting (−17.02% on UCIT, −14.97% on DCL), confirming that naive sequential fine-tuning is highly vulnerable to both catastrophic forgetting and negative interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiGMA builds directly on LoRA as the parameter-efficient adaptation backbone and adopts the expansion-merge strategy (train task-specific LoRAs, merge at inference) shared by HiDe-LLaVA and DISCO. Its key conceptual departure from prior MCIT work (MoE-LoRA, CL-MoE, O-LoRA, HiDe-LLaVA) is reframing the core problem: rather than treating catastrophic forgetting as the sole challenge, it identifies negative interference during weight consolidation as a distinct failure mode and proposes sign-based weight decoupling as the first mechanism explicitly targeting it. The sign-guided adaptive tuning and sign-guided merging components are novel contributions that operate purely on LoRA parameter signs, requiring no auxiliary encoders or architectural modifications beyond the base LoRA pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating negative interference from catastrophic forgetting and showing that sign-conflicted parameters cause measurable performance degradation during consolidation, the paper provides a principled, lightweight remedy that practitioners can adopt without changing model architecture or adding auxiliary components. The approach is particularly relevant as MLLMs are increasingly deployed in sequential, domain-shifting settings where simple fine-tuning or MoE routing is insufficient. A key open question the authors acknowledge is whether the general (sign-aligned) subspace remains informative over long task sequences, and whether the sign-based decoupling generalizes beyond LoRA to other PEFT methods like adapters or prompts.
