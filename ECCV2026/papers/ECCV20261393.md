## Problem

Existing slice-to-volume reconstruction frameworks for fetal MRI model scalar intensity fields and assume multiple orthogonal acquisition stacks, neither of which applies to fetal diffusion MRI where each voxel encodes a spherical function and data is acquired along a single slice orientation. This single-orientation constraint makes the reconstruction ill-posed along the slice-select axis, causing severe stripe artifacts that no prior learning-based or model-based method explicitly addresses for fetal dMRI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261393/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes motion-corrupted 2D diffusion-weighted slices organized into stacks (one stack per gradient direction, e.g., 32 or 80 directions at b=750–1000 s/mm²), each slice carrying an unknown rigid transform, bias field, and noise. The output is a spatially continuous 3D field of 4th-order real-valued SH coefficients (15 values per point), from which diffusion-weighted images at arbitrary gradient directions can be synthesized and fiber orientation distributions estimated via constrained spherical deconvolution without resampling.

## Method

A multi-resolution hash-grid-encoded MLP maps continuous anatomical coordinates to direction-independent SH coefficients, decoupling spatial and angular variation so that a single network produces the full spherical signal at any gradient. A physics-based forward model links this clean SH volume to observed slices through per-slice rigid transforms (with corresponding gradient reorientation), 3D Gaussian PSF convolution, multiplicative bias fields, and Gaussian noise, enabling self-supervised joint optimization without ground truth. A dedicated low-frequency bias network and an uncertainty network (predicting spatially varying noise plus per-slice outlier weights) handle acquisition artifacts and corrupted slices. The key novelty is a Through-Plane Continuity (TPC) regularization that computes the ratio of through-plane gradient magnitude to all-direction gradient magnitude at each voxel and penalizes values exceeding a threshold via a ReLU(log(R)−τ) loss, selectively suppressing stripe artifacts while preserving genuine anatomical edges.

## Results

On the fetal dMRI dataset (self-consistency evaluation), the proposed INR framework achieves 23.54 dB PSNR, 0.9014 SSIM, 0.1788 NRMSE, and 0.8194 NCC, outperforming IRTK on all metrics. On the simulated neonatal dataset with known ground truth, the method reaches 25.20 dB PSNR and 0.9828 SSIM, a 5.04% PSNR gain and 12.8% NRMSE reduction over IRTK. On the simulated neonatal template dataset, improvements are more modest (0.56% PSNR, 1.4% SSIM) but the runtime is reduced by approximately 15× (6.03 min vs. 102.15 min). Across all three datasets, the proposed method consistently wins on every metric while also being substantially faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is IRTK (Deprez et al., IEEE TMI 2020), a classical iterative slice-to-volume registration pipeline with per-voxel SH fitting. The proposed INR framework wins on every metric across all three datasets. The most dramatic gains appear on the simulated neonatal dataset, where PSNR improves by 5.04% (25.20 vs. 23.99 dB), NCC by 9.90% (0.8412 vs. 0.7654), and NRMSE drops by 12.8%, while runtime shrinks from 47.8 min to 6.75 min (−85.9%). On the fetal dataset, the SSIM improvement is particularly striking (0.9014 vs. 0.7516, +19.2%), reflecting better preservation of structural detail and elimination of stripe artifacts. On the cleaner template dataset, gains are smaller (0.56% PSNR, 1.4% SSIM) but still consistent, and the runtime advantage is roughly 15×. Qualitatively, IRTK exhibits residual through-plane stripe artifacts and less sharp cortical folding, whereas the proposed method produces cleaner, more uniform reconstructions suitable for downstream tractography.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit neural representation (INR) paradigm for fetal MRI reconstruction, previously applied to structural imaging (e.g., NeSVOR, Xu et al. 2023), to the diffusion MRI setting where the underlying signal is a spherical function at each voxel rather than a scalar intensity. It adapts the physics-based slice acquisition model (per-slice rigid motion, gradient reorientation, PSF convolution, bias field) established by Deprez et al. (2020) for IRTK, but replaces the discrete grid-based iterative optimization with a continuous INR parameterized by multi-resolution hash-grid encoding. The novel Through-Plane Continuity (TPC) regularization targets a stripe artifact problem inherent in the single-orientation acquisition geometry typical of fetal dMRI, which neither structural INR methods nor the IRTK pipeline address explicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is considerable: fetal dMRI could provide unique insights into white matter development in utero, but current reconstructions are too low-fidelity for quantitative microstructural analysis, and this work demonstrates that INR-based reconstruction yields cleaner volumes in substantially less time (~6 min vs. 8-102 min) than the iterative baseline. However, evaluation is limited to a single fetal cohort (n=9) and two simulated neonatal datasets, with only one iterative baseline (IRTK) for comparison; head-to-head comparison with other INR-based SVR methods or the more comprehensive Christiaens et al. (2021) scattered-slice-shard pipeline is absent. An open question is whether the TPC regularization's fixed threshold and randomly sampled neighborhood transfer robustly to other acquisition geometries or multi-shell protocols, or whether it could be learned end-to-end.
