## Problem

Egocentric vision systems capture rich behavioral cues but lack access to physiological indicators of autonomic states such as stress, engagement, and cognitive effort. Heart rate variability (HRV) is a well-established noninvasive marker of autonomic regulation, yet it demands millisecond-level inter-beat timing precision that is easily corrupted by the head motion, dynamic illumination, and cumulative integration drift inherent to egocentric gaze video. Prior egocentric methods (e.g., egoPPG) only recover coarse 60-second-averaged heart rate, discarding the short-term beat-to-beat dynamics that HRV encodes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128-frame (4.3 s) clips from inward-facing eye-tracking cameras at 30 fps and 320×240 px resolution, along with tri-axial IMU acceleration data for uncertainty calibration. It outputs a per-window mean inter-beat interval (from which HR = 60,000/IBI is derived), a log-variance uncertainty estimate, and standard time-domain HRV indices (SDNN, RMSSD) computed over consecutive windows. For the downstream proficiency task, the frozen IBI estimator feeds per-window (μ, s) pairs into a fusion module that produces a 4-class proficiency label alongside TimeSformer video embeddings.

## Method

A 3D CNN backbone predicts frame-wise reflectance differences, which are then passed through a novel low-high decomposition module that splits the temporal signal into a low-frequency component (moving average, k=11) and a high-frequency residual, refines each with lightweight convolutional heads, and fuses them via a gated residual connection (~500 parameters) to suppress broadband motion noise before integration into a BVP waveform. The key cross-domain pretraining stage transforms BVP segments into DFT magnitude spectra to eliminate phase mismatch between contact and camera signals, then trains a U-Net encoder on large-scale contact BVP–ECG datasets (WildPPG, Deep PPG) to predict a probabilistic IBI distribution (mean and log-variance) supervised by Pan-Tompkins ECG beats; IMU Fourier features are fused to modulate the predicted variance under motion. The model is trained with a negative log-likelihood objective, and at inference the pretrained encoder is transferred to egocentric camera-derived BVP. For proficiency estimation, a PhysFusion module encodes per-window HRV features with a 2-layer transformer and learned attention aggregation, then concatenates the result with TimeSformer video embeddings for a linear classification head, achieving a 17.8% relative accuracy gain on EgoExo4D.

## Results

On egoPPG-DB, EgoHRV achieves HR MAE of 12.72 bpm (4 s) and 7.13 bpm (60 s), outperforming the best prior method (FactorizePhys, 14.51 bpm at 4 s) by 12.3%. For HRV, the method reduces IBI MAE to 105.91 ms (4 s) versus 130.44 ms for the best baseline (PhysFormer), and cuts SDNN/RMSSD errors by 61–67% relative to the strongest baselines. On the EgoExo4D proficiency benchmark, fusing EgoHRV's uncertainty-aware HRV features with TimeSformer video embeddings raises overall egocentric accuracy from 39.69% to 46.75%, a 17.8% relative improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoHRV outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against PulseFormer [5], the closest prior egocentric method, EgoHRV reduces 4 s HR MAE from 22.10 to 12.72 bpm (−42%) and 4 s IBI MAE from 147.55 to 105.91 ms (−28%), while also achieving a higher Pearson correlation (r = 0.54 vs. 0.43 for IBI). Against the best stationary rPPG methods, FactorizePhys [34] leads on 60 s HR MAE (13.27 vs. 14.51) but EgoHRV still wins at 4 s (12.72 vs. 14.51) and dominates on all HRV metrics (IBI MAE 105.91 vs. 200.72 ms; SDNN MAE 10.89 vs. 28.65 ms). PhysFormer [93] is the strongest HRV baseline (IBI MAE 130.44 ms at 4 s), yet EgoHRV still improves by 18.8% on IBI MAE and by 63% on SDNN MAE. PhysNet [92] performs worst on HRV time-domain indices among the learned baselines (SDNN MAE 28.26 ms, RMSSD MAE 45.73 ms), confirming that standard rPPG architectures fail to preserve the beat-to-beat timing structure that HRV requires.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends egoPPG (Braun et al., ICCV 2025), which estimated coarse 60-second heart rate averages from egocentric eye-tracking cameras, to continuous 4.3-second HRV estimation by addressing the error-accumulation problem inherent in derivative-based BVP reconstruction under motion. Architecturally, it builds on 3D CNN backbones from the rPPG literature (PhysNet, PhysFormer) but introduces a low-high decomposition module to suppress motion noise and a cross-domain pretraining strategy that aligns frequency-domain representations between contact-based BVP datasets (WildPPG, Deep PPG) and camera-derived signals via Fourier magnitude spectra. The PhysFusion module for proficiency estimation is novel, combining uncertainty-weighted HRV features with TimeSformer video embeddings in a way that prior egocentric work (which used only 60s HR means) did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of continuous HRV estimation from egocentric gaze video, opening a path to stress- and arousal-aware first-person applications without additional body-worn sensors. The 17.8% relative improvement in EgoExo4D proficiency estimation shows that physiological signals carry behavioral information beyond visual appearance, particularly in cognitively or physically demanding tasks like bouldering. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (egoPPG-DB, 25 participants, no outdoor recordings), treating HRV as a stress proxy rather than a direct label, and the absence of a dedicated stress-benchmark evaluation. Open questions include whether the learned HRV representations transfer to workload estimation or other downstream tasks, and how performance holds under broader environmental variability.
