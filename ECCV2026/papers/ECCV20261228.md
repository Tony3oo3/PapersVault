## Problem

Basic model merging methods suffer from severe parameter conflicts that degrade performance even across highly similar tasks, while existing personalized merging frameworks that preserve task-specific information incur prohibitive storage overhead (10–100% extra per task). The paper asks whether task-specific information can be compressed to approximately 1% additional storage per task without sacrificing the performance gains that personalization provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261228/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes fine-tuned model parameters and a reference model (the original pre-trained weights for DTS-T, or a coarsely merged baseline for DTS-D), producing a compact per-task representation consisting of truncated singular values and 2-bit quantized singular vectors. At inference, a task ID triggers a one-time reconstruction of full task-specific weights (cached thereafter), and for unseen tasks, class-name or instruction embeddings from a pretrained text encoder are used offline to compute cosine-similarity-based fusion weights over seen-task residuals. No additional data or gradient updates are required at any stage.

## Method

DTS applies a three-stage compression pipeline to the task-specific deviation matrix (task vector or difference vector): (1) truncated SVD retains only the top-r singular values, providing the optimal low-rank approximation per Eckart-Young-Mirsky; (2) a novel 4-group thresholding strategy partitions each singular vector's elements into positive-large, positive-small, negative-large, and negative-small groups via median splits, encoding each element with exactly 2 bits (sign + magnitude level) for a 16× compression ratio; (3) per-group scaling factors are computed as L1-norm-based arithmetic means that minimize the conditional MSQE within each subgroup. At inference, the task-specific parameters are reconstructed as θ_ref + Û_n Σ_n V̂_n^T. For unseen tasks, a data-free variant fuses seen-task residuals weighted by cosine similarity between the unseen task's semantic embedding (mean of class-name or instruction embeddings) and each seen task's embedding, enabling zero-shot generalization without any additional training or online data.

## Results

On the 8-task ViT-B/32 visual classification benchmark, DTS-D achieves 90.40% average accuracy with only 0.39% accuracy drop rate (ADR) and 3.68% additional memory rate (AMR), approaching the individual-model upper bound of 90.69%. Under the strict <1% memory constraint (DTS-D*), performance remains at 90.17% average accuracy with 0.57% ADR and 0.98% AMR. On ViT-L/14, DTS-D reaches 94.24% average accuracy (0.02% ADR), and on RoBERTa (8 GLUE tasks), DTS-D achieves 84.98% average accuracy (0.67% ADR) with 3.81% AMR. On Qwen-14B for generation tasks, DTS-D attains 71.81% average accuracy with 3.57% AMR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTS-D (90.40% avg) decisively outperforms all basic merging methods: Weight-Averaging (65.83%), Task-Arithmetic (69.22%), Ties-Merging (72.90%), DARE (64.89%), and AdaMerging++ (81.09%), with gaps of 9–25 percentage points. Among personalized baselines, DTS-D surpasses T-Switch (90.15%) by 0.25% while using less memory (3.68% vs. 6.25% AMR), beats TALL-Mask (90.04%) by 0.36% with less than a quarter of the storage (3.68% vs. 15.62%), and outperforms WEMOE (89.36%) by 1.04% while requiring only 7.4% of WEMOE's extra memory (3.68% vs. 49.95%). DTS-D also edges out FREE-Merging (89.74%) by 0.66% and EMR-Merging (88.18%) by 2.22%. The closest competitor is T-Switch, which achieves 90.15% with 6.25% AMR; DTS-D's advantage is modest in accuracy but meaningful in memory efficiency, and the gap widens under the <1% constraint where DTS-D* (90.17%, 0.98% AMR) still leads T-Switch (90.15%, 6.25% AMR). On unseen tasks (Table 5), DTS-D achieves 55.41% average on RESISC45+SVHN, outperforming Ties-Merging (53.85%) and all personalized baselines, which notably underperform even basic Ties-Merging on unseen tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTS sits at the intersection of two established model-merging paradigms: basic merging (Task-Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, DARE) that fuses all tasks into one parameter body, and personalized merging (WEMOE, EMR-Merging, T-Switch) that stores task-specific components to avoid parameter conflicts. It departs from T-Switch's binary task-vector masking by replacing the 1-bit sign mask with a 4-group median-based thresholding plus per-group scaling, and from EMR-Merging's dense modulators by applying truncated SVD to the task-specific deviation matrix. The paper also introduces the 'difference vector' (fine-tuned minus merged baseline) as an alternative to the standard task vector when the pretrained checkpoint is unavailable, and extends the framework to unseen tasks via a data-free semantic-similarity weighting scheme that has no direct precedent in prior personalized merging work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the ~1% per-task storage overhead while matching or exceeding personalized baselines that require 6–100% extra memory, making multi-task deployment feasible in resource-constrained settings where WEMOE or Twin-Merging are prohibitive. The observation that all personalized baselines underperform even Ties-Merging on unseen tasks (Table 5) highlights a previously underappreciated limitation: task-specific optimization can hurt transferability, and the semantic-similarity weighting offers a simple, training-free remedy. Open questions include how the sparsity coefficient and thresholding granularity should scale with model size (experiments top out at Qwen-14B), whether the class-name-embedding heuristic generalizes to tasks without discrete labels, and whether the approach extends to continual or streaming merging scenarios where the set of seen tasks grows over time.
