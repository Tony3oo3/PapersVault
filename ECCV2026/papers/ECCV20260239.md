## Problem

Existing instruction-based video editing datasets and models are largely confined to single-task, appearance-level modifications such as object removal or style transfer, leaving complex structural transformations (camera and subject movement), simultaneous multi-task editing, and reference-guided editing unaddressed. This gap stems from both the absence of large-scale paired data covering these diverse task types and the lack of model architectures capable of jointly handling spatial structural control and fine-grained appearance rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260239/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source video (720p, 65–129 frames, 3–10 s), a natural-language editing instruction, and an optional reference image as input, and produces an edited video at matching resolution and frame count along with a predicted editing-region mask. The mask branch operates at a spatially downsampled resolution (1/n, default n=4) relative to the main video branch, and the text instruction is encoded by a frozen Qwen3VL-8B MLLM shared across both branches. The dataset spans 10 task classes including basic appearance edits, camera movement, subject movement, reference-based editing, and multi-task editing combining 2–5 simultaneous operations.

## Method

Goku-Edit employs a dual-branch diffusion architecture (both branches initialized from Wan2.2-5B) where a main video-editing branch synthesizes the edited video while an auxiliary mask-prediction branch provides spatial grounding, decoupling structural control from appearance rendering. To resolve the resolution mismatch between branches, RoPE-aligned spatial cross-attention scales the mask branch's positional indices by factor n before RoPE computation, unifying both branches into a shared physical coordinate system and enabling bidirectional (mask-to-video and video-to-mask) cross-attention without spurious macro-level offsets. At inference, a training-free SpatialCFG strategy amplifies cross-branch spatial constraints by contrasting coupled dual-branch predictions against decoupled single-branch baselines, suppressing editing spillover and refining mask boundaries. The supporting data pipeline decomposes complex edits into independently controllable sub-problems (e.g., style transfer into first-frame stylization + depth-guided propagation; subject movement into action variation + position variation) and applies a three-tier progressive filtering system powered by Gemini2.5-Pro that discards approximately 88% of synthesized samples to ensure instruction alignment, temporal stability, and photorealism.

## Results

On Goku-Bench (Instruction Task), Goku-Edit achieves the best FVD (993.93) among all methods and leads all open-source baselines on every editing-specific metric: ST (0.955), SuM (0.633), CM (0.927), PR (0.738), SR (0.832), IF (0.627), and EQ (0.645). Compared to the strongest open-source baseline LucyEdit, Goku-Edit gains +0.078 on IF, +0.262 on PR, and +0.077 on SR. Against commercial models (Runway, Luma), Goku-Edit still wins on PR (+0.033 over Runway), SR (+0.039), CM (+0.036), and SuM (+0.019), but trails on IF (0.627 vs. 0.758) and EQ (0.645 vs. 0.782), reflecting a gap in perceptual quality (CLIP, MS, AES).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Goku-Edit decisively outperforms all six open-source baselines on every editing-specific metric. Against the weakest baselines (TokenFlow, InsV2V, StableV2V, InsViE, AnyV2V), the IF gap ranges from +0.187 (vs. AnyV2V, 0.627 vs. 0.259) to +0.252 (vs. InsViE, 0.627 vs. 0.349), while PR gaps exceed 0.35 in all cases. The closest open-source competitor, Omni-Video, is still surpassed by Goku-Edit on IF (+0.117), PR (+0.158), SR (+0.201), and CM (+0.446). LucyEdit, the strongest open-source method, trails Goku-Edit on IF (0.549 vs. 0.627), PR (0.476 vs. 0.738), and CM (0.637 vs. 0.927), confirming that structural and multi-task editing remain unaddressed by prior datasets. Against commercial models, Goku-Edit wins on structural metrics (PR, SR, CM, SuM) but loses on IF (0.627 vs. 0.758 for Runway) and EQ (0.645 vs. 0.782), indicating that commercial systems still lead in holistic instruction following and perceptual quality despite Goku-Edit's superior spatial and physical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-based video editing (IVE) line of work (InsV2V, InsViE, AnyV2V, StableV2V, Omni-Video, LucyEdit) by broadening the task scope from single-task appearance editing to multi-task, structural (camera/subject movement), and reference-guided editing. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it composes existing tools (Wan2.2-5B as the base diffusion backbone, VACE for appearance synthesis, Flux for image-level edits, RecamMaster for camera control, Grounded-SAM2 for segmentation) into a decomposed data pipeline, and introduces a dual-branch architecture with RoPE-aligned cross-attention and SpatialCFG as its novel architectural contributions. The progressive filtering system powered by Gemini2.5-Pro departs from prior datasets (Ditto, Señorita-2M, OpenVE-3M) that either lack unified quality gates or remain confined to appearance-level edits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Goku is the first large-scale IVE dataset to include structural and multi-task editing pairs, and Goku-Bench provides the first benchmark with task-specific metrics (SuM, CM, ST) that go beyond generic video quality scores—both are likely to become standard references for the field. A key open question is generalizability: the heavy reliance on Gemini2.5-Pro for instruction generation and filtering (with an 88% rejection rate) raises reproducibility and cost concerns, and the paper acknowledges that commercial models still outperform on perceptual quality (CLIP, MS, AES) even while Goku-Edit leads on structural metrics. Whether the structural editing capabilities transfer to longer videos or more diverse domains beyond the 65–129 frame range remains untested.
