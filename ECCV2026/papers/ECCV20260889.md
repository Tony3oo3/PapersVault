## Problem

Open-world robotic manipulation requires bridging abstract language instructions with precise physical control, but existing representations—whether 2D pixel affordances, sparse 3D keypoints, or implicit dense descriptor fields—lack the explicit kinematic constraints essential for articulated objects. The core gap is that successful manipulation of articulated objects (e.g., opening a microwave) is governed by the relative rigid-body geometry between interacting parts, not by isolated contact points, yet no prior representation encodes this relational structure with mathematically rigorous SE(3) constraints in a training-free pipeline.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural language instruction (e.g., 'open the microwave door') and a single RGB-D observation from a wrist-mounted camera as input. It produces a Relational 6D Affordance Graph consisting of two SE(3) part poses (primary interacting part and physical anchor) linked by a kinematic constraint type, which is then converted into a continuous sequence of 6-DoF end-effector waypoints. The output trajectory is generated analytically (not learned) and updated in closed-loop at 5 Hz, with replanning triggered when the tracked anchor pose deviates more than 5 cm from its planned state.

## Method

RelAfford6D is a fully training-free framework with three cascaded modules. First, Semantic Topology Generation uses a structured kinematic knowledge base built from PartNet-Mobility, combined with retrieval-augmented LLM reasoning, to deduce the relational pair (primary part, anchor part) and the kinematic constraint type (revolute or prismatic) from the language prompt. Second, Metric Visual Grounding uses SAM3 for language-guided part-level segmentation and FoundationPose for category-agnostic 6D pose estimation within those masks, instantiating the abstract graph nodes as precise SE(3) coordinates. Third, Constraint-Driven Kinematic Execution formulates manipulation as a 1D manifold tracking problem: the end-effector must follow either a prismatic translation axis or a revolute rotation axis defined relative to the anchor frame, with RRT-Connect handling global collision-free reaching and analytic discretization of the constraint manifold generating local execution waypoints. A closed-loop tracking mechanism continuously monitors the anchor pose and re-synthesizes the remaining trajectory when physical disturbances shift the constraint manifold, eliminating the need for any task-specific policy training.

## Results

On the articulated object manipulation benchmark (10 categories, SAPIEN simulator), RelAfford6D achieves a 74% average success rate without any task-specific training, outperforming all data-driven baselines: RoboMamba (68%), ManipLLM (62%), FlowBot3D (40%), and Where2Act (27%). On rigid object manipulation (PickCube, StackCube, PushCube), the method achieves 100% success across all three tasks, surpassing RDT-1B (77.2/74.0/100%), Diffusion Policy (40/80/88%), OpenVLA (8/8/8%), and Octo (0/0/0%). In real-world evaluation on a Realman RM75 arm, the system maintains strong zero-shot performance with ~20.7 s one-time initialization and 5 Hz closed-loop tracking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On articulated object manipulation, RelAfford6D (74% avg) outperforms all four baselines: RoboMamba (68%), ManipLLM (62%), FlowBot3D (40%), and Where2Act (27%). The largest gaps are against Where2Act and FlowBot3D, which rely on isolated contact points or 3D flow fields and frequently produce kinematically invalid trajectories (e.g., pulling a revolute door linearly). RoboMamba is the closest competitor, winning on individual categories like Microwave (81 vs 78) and Bottle (91 vs 92), but RelAfford6D leads on 7 of 10 categories and the overall average. On rigid object manipulation, RelAfford6D achieves 100% on all three tasks, decisively beating RDT-1B (77.2/74.0/100%), Diffusion Policy (40/80/88%), OpenVLA (8/8/8%), and Octo (0/0/0%), demonstrating that the relational SE(3) constraint formulation generalizes beyond articulated joints to standard pick-and-place without any retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of representing manipulation targets as isolated contact points (2D heatmaps, 3D keypoints) or implicit dense descriptor fields, arguing that articulated manipulation is fundamentally governed by relative rigid-body geometry between object parts. It repurposes vision foundation models (SAM3 for part segmentation, FoundationPose for 6D pose) and LLM-based semantic reasoning (with retrieval-augmented kinematic priors from PartNet-Mobility) into a new intermediate representation—the Relational 6D Affordance Graph—that explicitly encodes a primary interacting part and its physical anchor as a pair of SE(3) poses linked by a kinematic constraint manifold. Unlike prior 6D affordance methods (e.g., Omnimanip) that treat each actionable pose in isolation, the relational formulation elevates the topological edge between parts into a mathematically defined constraint (revolute or prismatic), converting downstream execution into a closed-loop kinematic constraint satisfaction problem rather than a learned policy or heuristic planner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical implication is that a fully training-free, analytically grounded pipeline can match or exceed data-driven policies (74% vs. 68% average) on articulated manipulation, suggesting that explicit physical constraints may be more sample-efficient and generalizable than end-to-end learning for structured objects. The ablations confirm that both the relational anchor (dropping to 48.5% without it) and closed-loop replanning (dropping to 35% without it) are essential, validating the design choices. However, the framework is currently limited to revolute and prismatic joints on rigid objects; helical, multi-DoF, and deformable mechanisms remain unaddressed, and severe occlusion or incorrect initial segmentation can still break the topological extraction. Whether this constraint-manifold formulation scales to more complex kinematic chains or integrates with learned policies for hybrid scenarios is an open question.
