## Problem

End-to-end multi-view tracking methods achieve high accuracy but embed sensor geometry in learned parameters, provide no interpretable uncertainty estimates, and require retraining for new sensor layouts or modalities. Classical RFS filters offer principled uncertainty but do not maintain persistent identities or propagate measurement covariances through cross-view fusion in a unified metric frame. No prior method combines sensor-agnostic deployment, traceable uncertainty propagation, and competitive tracking accuracy in a single system.

## Input / Output

The system consumes either multi-camera RGB frames (e.g., 7 calibrated 1920×1080 cameras at 2 fps on WildTrack) or sparse radar point clouds (range, azimuth, Doppler, RCS from 4 automotive radars on RadarScenes), along with camera calibration or radar mount parameters. All front-ends reduce their output to a unified interface of calibrated BEV position-covariance pairs (z, R), where the covariance is decomposed into an independent perceptual term and a shared calibration/ego-pose term. The tracker outputs persistent identity-labeled tracks with full 4×4 state covariance (position and velocity in BEV), HMM motion mode, and pooled appearance features, with every covariance entry traceable to a physical sensor characteristic.

## Method

ModTrack confines all learning to the detection and feature-extraction stage (YOLOv11x + OSNet-AIN for cameras; Doppler/RCS thresholds + DBSCAN for radar) and performs all downstream reasoning with closed-form analytical methods. Per-sensor detections are projected to BEV via Jacobian-propagated depth uncertainty, grouped across views by χ²-consistency graph clustering with a hard Euclidean cutoff, and fused via precision-weighted averaging that separates independent from common-mode covariance so shared calibration error does not spuriously vanish with more sensors. The tracking backend is an identity-informed GM-PHD filter augmented with HMM motion modes (stationary, constant-velocity, maneuvering), persistent identity labels, and appearance features; track predictions feed back as soft spatial priors into the next frame's association cost, creating a closed loop that yields +8.9 IDF1 over spatial-only matching. Because every sensor produces the same (z, R) interface, the identical analytical core operates unchanged across WildTrack, MultiviewX, and RadarScenes, requiring only perception-module replacement to extend to new domains.

## Results

On WildTrack, ModTrack achieves 95.5 IDF1 and 91.4 MOTA, surpassing all prior modular methods by over 21 points (vs. MV-GLMB-OC at 74.3/69.7) while remaining within 1.0 IDF1 and 2.9 MOTA of the best end-to-end method (MVTrajecter, 96.5/94.3). On MultiviewX, ModTrack leads all methods in IDF1 (86.2 vs. 85.8 for MVTrajecter) despite a 2× higher pedestrian density. On RadarScenes, ModTrack with oracle perception reaches 76.9 LSTQ, surpassing the best prior method (Radar Tracker, 66.8) by 10.1 points, confirming the tracker core is not the limiting factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against modular baselines, ModTrack dominates: it outperforms MV-GLMB-OC [16] by +21.2 IDF1 and +21.7 MOTA on WildTrack, and KSP-DO [6] by +22.3 IDF1, a gap attributable to BEV-space covariance propagation and identity-informed association absent in prior RFS trackers. Against end-to-end methods, ModTrack trails MVTrajecter [30] by 1.0 IDF1 and 2.9 MOTA on WildTrack, but leads all methods in IDF1 on MultiviewX (86.2 vs. 85.8), suggesting the gap is perception-limited rather than tracking-limited. Oracle detection experiments confirm this: with ground-truth boxes, ModTrack reaches 97.1 IDF1 on WildTrack (exceeding all baselines) and 93.6 MOTA on MultiviewX (matching MVTrajecter's 92.8). Against learned non-interpretable methods, ModTrack surpasses UMPN [9] by a narrow margin (95.5 vs. 96.3 IDF1) but provides explicit uncertainty estimates they lack. On RadarScenes, ModTrack with a zero-training Doppler front-end achieves 42.1 LSTQ (comparable to MOT [29] at 42.4), while oracle perception lifts it to 76.9 LSTQ, surpassing Radar Tracker [32] (66.8) by 10.1 points and CA-Net [15] (34.8) by 42.1 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

ModTrack sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: end-to-end learned multi-view trackers (EarlyBird, MCBLT, SCFusion, MVTrajecter) that jointly learn BEV representations and association, and classical random finite set filters (GM-PHD, GLMB, MV-GLMB-OC) that provide principled multi-target estimation but have not been combined with BEV geometric fusion or scalable identity reasoning. The paper departs from both by confining all learned components to the perception front-end and performing fusion, association, and tracking entirely with closed-form analytical methods (Jacobian covariance propagation, χ² graph clustering, precision-weighted fusion, and an identity-informed GM-PHD filter with IMM motion modes). The ByteTrack-style cascaded confidence processing and the GM-PHD predict-update recursion serve as direct methodological ancestors, while the sensor-agnostic (z,R) interface is the paper's key architectural departure from prior modular trackers that operated in image-space bounding boxes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sensor-agnostic (z,R) interface and the demonstrated transfer from 7 cameras to 4 radars with zero retraining address a real deployment gap that end-to-end methods cannot fill, making this practically relevant for heterogeneous sensor systems. The oracle-detection experiments (97.1 IDF1 on WildTrack) cleanly isolate that the residual gap to SOTA is entirely in perception, not tracking, which reframes where future effort should go. However, the structural limitation on MultiviewX—where no χ² gate threshold simultaneously reduces false-negative clusters and merge errors at 2× pedestrian density—suggests the graph-clustering approach may not scale to very dense scenes without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the analytical backend can handle non-pedestrian domains with richer motion dynamics, and whether the perception gap can be closed without reintroducing learned components into the tracking loop.
