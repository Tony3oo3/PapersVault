## Problem

Generating diverse, controllable motions from a single exemplar is difficult under data-scarce conditions, and existing single-motion synthesis methods operate exclusively in temporal or spatial domains, imposing control through joint-level or time-based constraints. This makes it impossible to selectively preserve global rhythmic structure while varying fine-grained motion details, creating an inherent trade-off between coherence and diversity that is especially problematic for crowd animation and style transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261348/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single motion sequence represented as joint positions P ∈ R^{J×3×T} and 6D rotations R ∈ R^{J×6×T}, along with a conditional mask that can be specified in the extended mode dimension (K×J×(Q+3)×T) to control which frequency bands are preserved or varied. Output is a generated motion in the same joint-position/rotation format, reconstructed by summing K generated frequency modes. The unusual aspect is that the mask operates across an additional frequency-mode axis beyond the standard temporal-spatial masking, and rotation and position are processed through separate branches throughout the pipeline.

## Method

CMDer decomposes each joint's motion signal into K intrinsic frequency modes via Multivariate Variational Mode Decomposition (MVMD), which adaptively extracts band-limited modes with shared center frequencies across channels, preserving inter-joint correlations without assuming stationarity. A lightweight single-layer U-Net diffusion model then learns to denoise in this decomposed mode space rather than raw joint trajectories, using x₀-prediction with a simplified reconstruction loss plus weighted MSE for rotations and positions. The Mode Enhancement Block (MEB) applies anisotropic depthwise convolutions—(1,t) for temporal rhythm and (j,1) for joint correlation—within each frequency mode, followed by sigmoid gating to adaptively refine spatio-temporal features while preserving motion detail. This frequency-space formulation enables selective preservation or variation of specific modes (e.g., keeping low-frequency rhythm fixed while sampling diverse high-frequency details), providing a controllability axis orthogonal to existing temporal and spatial masking strategies.

## Results

On the Mixamo benchmark (20 motions), CMDer achieves the best composite score (HM = 0.78) and leads in global diversity (GloDiv = 1.32), local diversity (LocDiv = 1.10), and smoothness (Smo = −7.15). Coverage is competitive at 97.94%, slightly below GenMM's 99.26% but above SinMDM (97.41%) and GANimator (96.39%). The intra-diversity difference (IntDiff = 0.18) is the lowest among all methods, indicating generated motions stay closest to the input distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMDer outperforms all three main baselines on the composite HM metric (0.78 vs. SinMDM 0.76, GANimator 0.72, GenMM 0.38). Against SinMDM [21], the closest competitor, CMDer wins on coverage (97.94 vs. 97.41), global diversity (1.32 vs. 1.30), local diversity (1.10 vs. 0.91), smoothness (−7.15 vs. −7.35), and intra-diversity difference (0.18 vs. 0.19), while SinMDM retains a marginal edge on inter-diversity (0.13 vs. 0.12). Against GANimator [16], CMDer improves HM by 0.06 and reduces IntDiff from 0.26 to 0.18, indicating GANimator's outputs diverge more from the input distribution. GenMM [18] achieves the highest coverage (99.26%) but at the cost of very low diversity (GloDiv 0.34, LocDiv 0.25), yielding the worst HM (0.38). In the user study, CMDer is preferred over all baselines on quality (3.71 vs. 3.39) and fidelity (3.64 vs. 3.36) with comparable diversity (3.29 vs. 3.30). A comparison with DeepPhase [26] (periodic autoencoder) shows CMDer is more suitable for non-periodic single-sample settings, where DeepPhase's PAE yields unreliable coverage (74.09%) and inflated diversity scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

CMDer builds on the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the Multivariate Variational Mode Decomposition signal-processing technique (Rehman & Aftab, 2019), repurposing the latter for the first time in motion synthesis. It departs from prior single-motion synthesis methods (GANimator, GenMM, SinMDM, MotionDreamer) that impose controllability through temporal masks or joint-level spatial constraints, instead introducing frequency-selective control as an orthogonal editing axis. The key conceptual shift is performing diffusion in an adaptively decomposed mode space rather than raw joint trajectories, which the authors argue provides both interpretability and improved learning stability under data scarcity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-selective control dimension opens practical applications that are difficult to express with purely temporal or spatial masking, notably coherent-yet-diverse crowd animation and interpretable mode-level editing. However, quantitative gains over strong baselines like SinMDM are modest (HM 0.78 vs. 0.76), and the method remains limited to single-sample settings with acknowledged foot-skating artifacts. Open questions include whether the MVMD-based decomposition generalizes across diverse motion types, skeletal structures, and creature rigs, and whether the frequency-mode representation can be combined with text or other semantic conditioning for broader controllability.
