## Problem

Self-supervised visual backbones like DINOv3 excel at dense spatial prediction but lack native textual alignment, while CLIP provides semantic text embeddings in a different representation space. Existing MLP-based projections between these spaces use a single-step Euclidean shortcut that distorts the intrinsic hyperspherical geometry of the feature space, causing semantic entanglement and suboptimal open-vocabulary segmentation. The paper asks whether a continuous, geometry-preserving transformation can bridge this cross-modal gap more effectively than discrete mapping.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB image (resized to 448 px shorter side, processed via 448×448 sliding window with 224 px stride) and a set of M candidate category names encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder using prompt templates. It produces a dense per-pixel segmentation map by computing cosine similarity between DINOv3 patch tokens and ODE-aligned text anchors. Training requires only image-caption pairs (COCO 2017 Caption, ~118 k images) with no pixel-level annotations, and the aligned text anchors are cached once per class set for efficient inference.

## Method

DINOde replaces the discrete MLP projection with a continuous ODE trajectory that gradually transports CLIP text embeddings onto the DINOv3 visual manifold. Two complementary flows operate in parallel: Semantic Text Flow (STF) integrates a learnable velocity network from the projected text embedding to a pooled DINOv3 patch descriptor, while Global Context Flow (GCF) similarly refines the DINOv3 [CLS] token to capture holistic image semantics. To preserve the hyperspherical geometry during integration, Velocity Tangent Projection subtracts the radial component of the velocity at each step, constraining motion to the tangent space. The velocity networks are trained with a CLIP-style symmetric contrastive loss over concatenated global image and text representations, using only ~118 k image-caption pairs and a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone, completing in under 4 hours on a single GPU with 10 Euler integration steps.

## Results

DINOde achieves a mean mIoU of 49.5% (without mask refinement) and 50.1% (with PAMR mask refinement) averaged across eight OVSS benchmarks, using DINOv3 ViT-L/16 as the visual backbone and CLIP ViT-L/14 as the text encoder. It outperforms the closest competitor, Talk2DINO* with the same DINOv3 backbone, by 1.9 percentage points (49.5% vs. 47.6%) without refinement and by 1.6 points (50.1% vs. 48.5%) with refinement. The method wins on 6 out of 8 individual benchmarks, with the largest gains on Pascal VOC 2012 (91.1% vs. 87.7% for Talk2DINO*) and Cityscapes (45.4% vs. 40.8%). Training requires only ~118k COCO 2017 caption pairs and completes in under 4 hours on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINOde outperforms all nine compared baselines on average mIoU across the eight OVSS benchmarks. Against the closest competitor Talk2DINO* (same DINOv3 backbone), DINOde gains +1.9%p without refinement (49.5 vs. 47.6) and +1.6%p with refinement (50.1 vs. 48.5), winning on 6 of 8 individual benchmarks (losing only on COCO Stuff and COCO Object where the gap is ≤1.2%p). Compared to the dual-backbone ProxyCLIP+DINO (43.0% without / 43.2% with), DINOde improves by ~6.5%p, demonstrating that a single DINOv3 backbone with ODE-based alignment surpasses CLIP+DINO fusion. Against training-free methods (MaskCLIP 13.9%, SCLIP 29.9%, NACLIP 36.5%), the gains are substantial (12–36%p), confirming the value of learned cross-modal alignment. dino.txt [24] and CLIPPer [54] report only partial benchmark coverage, but DINOde exceeds them on every shared benchmark. The ablation (Table 1) further shows that the ODE-based STF alone adds +0.5%p over an MLP baseline, VTP adds another +0.3%p, and GCF contributes +0.7%p, with the full combination reaching 49.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Neural ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018) but repurposes it for a fundamentally different application: continuous cross-modal alignment between CLIP text embeddings and DINOv3 visual features for open-vocabulary semantic segmentation. It departs from recent DINO-based OVSS methods (dino.txt, Talk2DINO) that bridge the visual-textual gap via discrete single-step mappings (MLP projections or attention-based selective alignment), arguing that such Euclidean shortcuts distort the intrinsic manifold geometry. The paper also extends the dual-backbone strategy (DINO for spatial detail + CLIP for semantics) by introducing a second ODE trajectory (GCF) that refines the global CLS token, going beyond prior work that only aligns patch-level features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core contribution—replacing discrete cross-modal projections with a continuous ODE trajectory constrained to the hypersphere—offers a principled alternative that could generalize beyond OVSS to any setting where frozen heterogeneous encoders must be aligned (the authors explicitly suggest MLLM vision-to-language projectors). The data efficiency is notable: alignment is achieved with ~118k COCO captions versus the 3.3M–12.4M pairs typical of prior weakly-supervised OVSS methods. However, the performance gain over the strongest baseline (Talk2DINO* with DINOv3) is modest (~1.9%p mIoU), and the method still depends on frozen backbones and a post-hoc mask refinement step (PAMR), leaving open whether the ODE formulation scales to truly open-world settings with arbitrary unseen categories or benefits from end-to-end fine-tuning.
