## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit significant performance degradation when processing negation queries, yet this limitation remains underexplored in remote sensing (RS) scenarios where identifying absent or false content is operationally critical (e.g., locating non-flooded evacuation routes). No prior benchmark or adaptation method specifically targets negation comprehension in RS MLLMs, and existing general-domain negation pipelines fail on fine-grained RS imagery with small-scale targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260733/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RS image paired with a text query containing negation tokens (no, not, without), plus a small set of unlabeled test samples (150 for VQA, 300 for MCQ) for online TTA. A lexicon-based filter constructs a negation-masked variant of each query by removing negation tokens, which serves as the affirmative anchor for optimization. Outputs are task-specific: binary or open-ended text answers (VQA), four-way caption selection (MCQ), bounding boxes evaluated at IoU@0.25/0.5/0.75 (grounding), and scene class labels (classification).

## Method

NeFo is a self-supervised test-time adaptation method that applies LoRA (rank 8, scaling 16) to the attention layers of the LLM module, optimizing two objectives simultaneously. The Truth-Value Inversion Loss maximizes the KL divergence between the model's output distributions for the negated query and its negation-masked variant (clamped at α=0.4), explicitly encoding the logical principle that negation inverts truth values and forcing the model to attend to negation-critical information. The Knowledge Retaining Loss regularizes predictions on the negation-masked variant toward the frozen original model (β=0.4) to prevent catastrophic forgetting, augmented by entropy minimization (γ) on the affirmative anchor to stabilize the optimization direction. The companion RS-Neg benchmark is constructed via an LLM-driven pipeline that extracts positive tuples from captions, generates candidate negated tuples, and verifies their absence using an MCTS-based dynamic visual focus module (DyFo) that iteratively crops and zooms into small-scale RS targets with Grounding DINO and Qwen2.5-VL-7B as visual experts.

## Results

On RS-Neg VQA, NeFo improves Qwen2.5-VL from 74.96% to 79.52% total accuracy (+4.56%) and Qwen3-VL from 71.47% to 75.42% (+3.95%), while on RS-Neg MCQ it lifts Qwen2.5-VL from 54.29% to 65.46% (+11.17%) and GeoReason from 45.59% to 53.39% (+7.80%). Notably, all three TTA baselines (TENT, SAR, TLM) degrade negation performance on most models, whereas NeFo consistently improves across all four base models and both tasks. In zero-shot transfer to unseen tasks (RS-Neg Classification, Grounding, FloodNet VQA), NeFo gains 2.11–6.26% on classification and up to 9.34% on grounding IoU@0.5, confirming generalizable negation understanding rather than test-set overfitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeFo outperforms all three TTA baselines (TENT, SAR, TLM) on every model–task combination in Table 2. The most striking finding is that TENT, SAR, and TLM frequently *degrade* negation performance relative to the unadapted base model (e.g., TENT drops Qwen2.5-VL VQA from 74.96% to 73.09%, and TLM drops GeoReason MCQ from 45.59% to 45.50%), which the authors attribute to self-reinforcement of incorrect predictions. NeFo, by contrast, consistently improves: on Qwen2.5-VL it gains +4.56% (VQA) and +11.17% (MCQ) over the base, surpassing the best baseline (TENT) by +6.43% and +9.69% respectively. On the weakest base model RS-LLaVA (MCQ total 23.72%), NeFo still achieves the highest score (24.46%) while all baselines fall below the base. For GeoReason, NeFo's MCQ gain of +7.80% over base is the largest absolute improvement among all models, and it avoids the catastrophic grounding collapse seen with TENT (−61.59% IoU@0.25 in zero-shot transfer). Overall, NeFo is the only method that reliably improves negation comprehension without sacrificing affirmative-query accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the negation-understanding evaluation line (NegVQA, GaslightingBench, NegBench) from general-domain web images to remote sensing, where small-scale targets and property-level negation (e.g., 'non-flooded buildings') pose challenges absent in prior pipelines. For the data construction side, it repurposes DyFo's MCTS-based visual search [16] to verify that generated negation concepts are truly absent from RS imagery. On the method side, it departs from generic TTA approaches (TENT, SAR, TLM) that treat adaptation as a distribution-shift problem, instead encoding the logical semantics of negation (truth-value inversion) directly into the optimization objective via a KL-divergence-based loss between negated and negation-masked queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even reasoning-augmented RS MLLMs (GeoReason, RS-EOT) gain no advantage on negation, and several RS-specific models fall below random guessing on MCQ—findings that challenge the assumption that domain fine-tuning or chain-of-thought reasoning addresses logical deficiencies. NeFo's practicality (2.5M LoRA parameters, 150–300 unlabeled samples) makes it deployable in resource-constrained RS settings like UAV-based disaster monitoring. Open questions include whether the truth-value-inversion principle generalizes to other logical operators (conjunction, implication), whether it transfers beyond RS to other fine-grained visual domains, and whether the modest gains on state-level negation (the hardest category) can be further closed.
