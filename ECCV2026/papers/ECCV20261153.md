## Problem

In one-shot federated learning, clients train independently on heterogeneous data and upload their models only once, so their learned feature representations develop divergent means and variances that make server-side aggregation ineffective. Existing OFL methods attempt post-hoc calibration at the server (distillation, selective aggregation, weighted averaging), but once representations have drifted into incompatible statistical spaces, prediction-level correction cannot recover the lost discriminative structure. The paper asks whether distributional divergence can be prevented during local training rather than corrected afterward.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) partitioned via Dirichlet(α) sampling and produces a class label from 10, 100, or 200 classes. At inference, the server does not train or fine-tune anything: it simply runs all N client feature extractors on a test image, ℓ2-normalizes each output, fuses them by weighted averaging, re-normalizes, and assigns the class with maximum cosine similarity to a fixed shared reference matrix P ∈ ℝ^{d×C}. The reference matrix is the only shared artifact and is broadcast once before training, adding no learnable parameters or per-round communication.

## Method

FACE enforces distributional consistency during local training through two complementary objectives. Directional Mean Alignment (DMA) introduces a fixed orthonormal reference matrix P whose columns serve as shared class directions; each client minimizes a cosine-softmax loss that pulls its class-conditional normalized feature means toward the corresponding column of P, thereby preventing mean drift across clients. The Variance Stabilizer (VS) applies a uniformity-style regularizer that penalizes pairs of normalized features that are too close on the hypersphere, preventing representation collapse and preserving intra-class dispersion. The two objectives jointly control the first and second moments of the feature distribution, and the paper proves that the cross-client mean gap and trace-dispersion gap are both bounded by per-client alignment scores that each client can optimize independently. At inference, the server performs feature-level ensemble fusion (weighted average of normalized features followed by cosine classification against P), requiring no post-calibration or server-side optimization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under extreme heterogeneity (α=0.05), FACE achieves 69.21% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline FAFI (60.28%) by 8.93 points and FuseFL (54.42%) by 14.79 points. On CIFAR-100 (α=0.05), FACE reaches 41.57% versus FAFI's 34.06%, and on Tiny-ImageNet (α=0.05) it achieves 39.20% versus FAFI's 34.60%. Under milder heterogeneity (α=0.5), FACE remains competitive at 83.97% on CIFAR-10 (slightly below FuseFL's 84.34%) and 53.47% on CIFAR-100 (slightly below FAFI's 54.42%), but pulls ahead on Tiny-ImageNet at 51.04% versus FAFI's 49.02%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FACE dominates under extreme heterogeneity (α=0.05–0.1), where it outperforms all seven baselines by 3–15 points across all three datasets. Against the strongest competitor FAFI (ICML 2025), FACE gains +8.93 on CIFAR-10 (α=0.05), +7.51 on CIFAR-100 (α=0.05), and +4.60 on Tiny-ImageNet (α=0.05), but trails FAFI by ~1 point on CIFAR-100 at α=0.5 (53.47 vs 54.42). FuseFL (NeurIPS 2024) is the only method that beats FACE on CIFAR-10 at α=0.5 (84.34 vs 83.97), but degrades sharply as heterogeneity increases, falling to 54.42 at α=0.05 versus FACE's 69.21. IntactOFL (ACM MM 2024), Co-Boosting (ICLR 2024), and DENSE (NeurIPS 2022) are consistently outperformed by FACE by 5–30 points across all settings. O-FedAvg and the simple Ensemble baseline serve as lower bounds, with FACE exceeding them by 28–57 points under extreme heterogeneity. The paper also shows FACE surpasses 200-round FedAvg and remains competitive with 200-round MOON on CIFAR-10, despite using only a single communication round.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant post-calibration paradigm in one-shot federated learning (DENSE, Co-Boosting, IntactOFL, FuseFL), which attempts to reconcile already-diverged client representations at the server. The closest prior work, FAFI, also introduces client-side mechanisms but relies on independently learned prototypes; FACE instead uses a fixed, shared orthonormal reference matrix (inspired by neural-collapse fixed-classifier designs) combined with a uniformity-style variance regularizer borrowed from representation-learning literature. The 'prevention over correction' framing repositions the problem from a server-side aggregation challenge to a client-side training-constraint problem, with theoretical propositions linking class-wise alignment scores to cross-client mean and variance gaps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prevention strategy offers a principled alternative to the increasingly fragile post-calibration approaches that degrade sharply under extreme heterogeneity and large client counts, as demonstrated by the 100-client scalability experiments. A practical open question is whether the fixed reference matrix's performance is sensitive to its initialization or dimensionality, and whether the approach extends beyond ResNet-18 on image classification to other architectures or modalities. The distillation results suggest the ensemble gain can be compressed into a single model, but the gap between full-ensemble and distilled accuracy remains non-trivial under extreme heterogeneity, leaving room for further compression research.
