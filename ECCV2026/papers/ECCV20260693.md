## Problem

Animal motion modeling lags far behind human motion research due to the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality 3D motion data, since most species cannot be captured in controlled motion-capture facilities. Existing datasets are either small, limited to a few species, or rely on artist-crafted motions that fail to capture the full diversity of natural animal behavior. No prior work provides a large-scale dataset with aligned video-text-motion tuples for quadrupeds, nor a generative model that conditions on both text and image to produce species-aware motion.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB animal image and a free-form text description of the desired motion (e.g., 'a horse rearing up and landing') as input. It produces a temporally coherent 3D motion sequence parameterized in SMAL space (35-joint 6D rotations plus global translation) and, via an image-to-3D module (Rodin) and auto-rigging pipeline, a fully textured, rigged, per-frame deformed 3D mesh sequence ready for rendering. The image serves a dual role: it provides DINOv3 global features to condition the diffusion model on visible morphology, and it is independently fed to Rodin to generate the T-posed asset mesh.

## Method

Kirin comprises three stages. First, a SMAL-templated reconstruction pipeline extracts 3D motion from in-the-wild video: AniMer provides per-frame initialization, then a sequence-level optimization refines a shared shape vector β and per-frame 6D joint rotations against 2D keypoint projections with temporal smoothness regularization, while global translation is recovered separately via Spatial-TrackerV2 point tracking; this yields the AiM3D dataset (~30k sequences, ~180k VLM-generated captions). Second, a motion diffusion model built on MDM conditions on both text (DistilBERT) and image (frozen DINOv3) by linearly projecting and summing the three conditioning vectors (text tokens, image feature, timestep embedding) into a single cross-attention signal injected at every transformer decoder block, trained with per-modality classifier-free guidance dropout. Third, generated motions are applied to a Rodin-generated T-pose mesh via SMAL template fitting (Chamfer + edge-length optimization), k-NN skinning-weight transfer from fitted SMAL vertices, and standard linear blend skinning per frame.

## Results

On the AiM3D test set (230 sequences), Kirin with text+image conditioning achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 of 0.043, FID of 6.248, and MM-Distance of 6.218, substantially outperforming AniMo (FID 30.043, R-Precision Top-1 0.029) trained on the same data. On the external out-of-distribution AnimalML3D benchmark (1,260 hand-crafted motions), Kirin again leads all metrics, achieving FID 138.1 vs. 150.7 for AniMo. For 4D reconstruction, Kirin achieves PCK@0.1 of 0.751 and KT-PCK@0.1 of 0.614 at <1 s/frame, beating both feed-forward baselines (AniMer, 3D-Fauna) while remaining 30× faster than optimization-based methods (SMALify, 4D-Fauna).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AniMo (the only prior text-driven animal motion generator), Kirin with text+image conditioning reduces FID from 30.0 to 6.2 (a 4.8× improvement) and raises R-Precision Top-1 from 0.029 to 0.043 on AiM3D; even Kirin's text-only variant (FID 11.9) already halves the baseline's FID. On the external AnimalML3D test set, Kirin outperforms AniMo on every metric (FID 138.1 vs. 150.7, R-Precision Top-1 0.039 vs. 0.031), confirming better generalization. For 4D reconstruction, Kirin surpasses both feed-forward baselines AniMer (PCK@0.1: 0.751 vs. 0.537) and 3D-Fauna (PCK@0.1: 0.751 vs. 0.470) at comparable <1 s/frame speed, while SMALify achieves higher IoU (0.867 vs. 0.698) but at 30× the runtime (~30 s/frame). Kirin also beats 4D-Fauna on all reconstruction metrics (e.g., PCK@0.1: 0.751 vs. 0.664) at the same 30× speed advantage. In qualitative mesh-animation comparisons, Kirin produces more physically plausible and text-faithful animations than Puppeteer, which frequently fails when its intermediate video-generation step produces inconsistent or implausible frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

Kirin extends the human motion diffusion model (MDM) to quadruped animals by adding a DINOv3 image-conditioning branch alongside the DistilBERT text encoder, and grounds the learned motion in the SMAL parametric skeleton rather than a human joint hierarchy. For data, it departs from artist-crafted sources (AniMo4D, Truebones Zoo) and single-species video collections (Ponymation) by reconstructing 3D motion from ~30k in-the-wild AiM videos, producing the first large-scale aligned video–text–motion dataset for quadrupeds. The reconstruction pipeline itself builds on AniMer's feed-forward SMAL fitting but adds sequence-level optimization for temporal smoothness and a separate Spatial-TrackerV2 pass for global translation, a decoupling not present in prior per-frame or per-sequence methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AiM3D dataset (≈30k motion sequences, ≈180k captions, 23 quadruped categories) fills a critical gap that has bottlenecked animal motion research, and the text+image-conditioned generation model provides a practical path from a single photo and a prompt to a rigged, animated 3D mesh. Limitations include coverage restricted to quadrupeds, an 86% satisfactory reconstruction rate (with top/bottom views and fast motion as failure modes), and a measurable diversity drop when image conditioning is enabled. Open questions include extending the framework to non-quadruped species (birds, fish, insects), improving reconstruction under heavy occlusion, and whether the image-conditioning trade-off (fidelity vs. diversity) can be mitigated with more nuanced visual priors.
