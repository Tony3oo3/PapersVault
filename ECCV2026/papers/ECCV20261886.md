## Problem

Device-server hybrid inference for on-device VLMs mitigates distribution-shift degradation by delegating uncertain inputs to a powerful server-side model, but each delegation incurs significant communication overhead (72–87% of total server-call latency), making frequent delegation impractical. Existing hybrid approaches discard server-side knowledge after each use, causing redundant delegations for similar inputs. The paper asks how to reduce delegation frequency while preserving the accuracy gains from server assistance.

## Input / Output

CURE consumes unlabeled test images arriving sequentially in an online streaming fashion (batch size 1) along with fixed class-name text prompts, producing a final class label prediction. The on-device VLM (MobileCLIP2-B, ~150M params) and server-side VLM (EVA-CLIP-18B, ~35 GB) both use dual-encoder architectures mapping images and text into a shared embedding space. The final output is an augmented probability vector obtained by entropy-weighted fusion of the VLM prediction and the knowledge reservoir's posterior, rather than a single model's raw output.

## Method

CURE introduces a knowledge reservoir—a Gaussian discriminant analysis (GDA) model in the on-device VLM's embedding space—that accumulates server-side predictions over the test stream via online EM updates. Two sequential routing phases gate delegation: Phase I compares the on-device VLM's normalized entropy against an adaptive percentile threshold (Q1 of past entropies) to accept high-confidence local predictions; Phase II compares the reservoir's posterior entropy against a threshold derived from the lower tail of historically misaligned samples to accept inputs already well-covered by accumulated knowledge. Only inputs failing both phases are delegated to the server. In the adaptation phase, the reservoir is updated using both delegated server predictions and high-confidence on-device predictions (as approximate server signals), and the final prediction fuses the VLM and reservoir distributions with a sigmoid weight inversely proportional to their relative entropies. The entire pipeline is backpropagation-free, requiring only forward passes and lightweight GDA parameter updates.

## Results

Across 13 classification datasets, CURE achieves 85.75% average top-1 accuracy with only 33.26% average delegation frequency, surpassing the always-delegate baseline (84.70% accuracy, 100% delegation) by +1.05% accuracy while reducing delegation by 66.74%. Compared to the non-cumulative hybrid baseline (84.51% accuracy, 74.92% delegation), CURE improves accuracy by +1.24% and cuts delegation by 55.61%. End-to-end runtime improves by up to 3.67× over always-delegate and 2.80× over non-cumulative hybrid, with the speedup driven primarily by reduced communication overhead (72–87% of server-call latency).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURE outperforms all five TTA baselines (C-TPT, TDA, SCA, ADAPT, DOTA) by a substantial margin of 5.1–6.6% in average accuracy, confirming that on-device-only adaptation signals are insufficient under distribution shift. Against the always-delegate baseline, CURE achieves higher average accuracy (85.75% vs. 84.70%) while delegating only 33.26% of inputs instead of 100%, yielding a 66.74% reduction in server calls and up to 3.67× end-to-end speedup. Versus the non-cumulative hybrid baseline, CURE improves accuracy by +1.24% (85.75% vs. 84.51%) and reduces delegation frequency from 74.92% to 33.26% (55.61% reduction), demonstrating that cumulative reuse of server knowledge is strictly more effective than discarding it. The largest per-dataset gains appear on fine-grained and remote-sensing benchmarks (e.g., RESISC45: 80.76% vs. 74.88% for non-cumulative; UCF101: 87.30% vs. 84.46%), where distribution shift is most severe and the knowledge reservoir accumulates the most useful server-side signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURE sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: device-server hybrid inference (Crayon, Margin Sampling, SMART, Minions) and test-time adaptation for VLMs (C-TPT, TDA, DOTA). Prior hybrid methods delegate uncertain inputs to a server but discard the server's response after use, while TTA methods rely solely on on-device signals. CURE departs from both by introducing a cumulative knowledge reservoir (implemented via GDA) that accumulates server-side predictions over the test stream, enabling the on-device model to progressively resolve inputs locally. The closest prior work, BlackVIP, also uses server outputs to update local prompts but still requires a server call for every input; CURE eliminates this by routing through the reservoir before delegating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing delegation frequency by 66.74% while matching always-delegate accuracy, CURE makes device-server hybrid inference economically viable for real-world edge deployment, where cellular data costs and communication latency (72–87% of total server-call delay) are the dominant practical barriers. The backpropagation-free, GDA-based design keeps on-device overhead minimal (validated on iPhone 17), and the shared-covariance variant reduces storage from 525 MB to 1.55 MB on ImageNet-1K. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to non-CLIP VLMs, multi-modal generation tasks, or non-stationary test streams where the reservoir may need to forget stale knowledge, and whether the scaffolding dynamic holds under adversarial or rapidly shifting distributions.
