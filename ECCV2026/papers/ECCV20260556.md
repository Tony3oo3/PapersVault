## Problem

RGB-D cameras are the only real-time sensing modality that simultaneously provides absolute metric scale, pixel-aligned dense geometry, and low-latency acquisition, yet their depth outputs are frequently corrupted by specular reflections, texture-less surfaces, and complex lighting, producing missing or invalid measurements. Prior work treats these sensor failures as noise to be discarded or filled by task-specific fusion modules, rather than exploiting the fact that the missing regions inherently encode geometric and appearance ambiguities that are valuable learning signals.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3-channel RGB image and a single-channel raw sensor depth map at the same spatial resolution (H×W, both divisible by 14) as input. Depth tokens are masked at the patch level (14×14) with a ratio of 60–90%, where fully missing patches are always masked and mixed-validity patches are masked with 0.75 probability; RGB tokens remain fully visible as conditioning context. The output is a dense metric depth map upsampled to full input resolution, with the model seamlessly transitioning between pure monocular depth estimation (all depth tokens masked) and depth completion (only sensor-corrupted tokens masked) by adjusting the masking strategy.

## Method

MDM employs a 24-layer ViT-Large/14 encoder (initialized from DINOv2) with separate patch-embedding layers for RGB and depth, producing spatially aligned token sequences that share 2D positional embeddings plus a modality identity embedding. The key architectural choice is to mask only depth tokens while keeping all RGB tokens visible, forcing self-attention to learn tight cross-modal dependencies between appearance context and geometric cues; a [cls] token captures global context and is broadcast to contextual tokens before decoding. Instead of a shallow Transformer decoder, a ConvStack decoder (adopted from MoGe) with residual blocks and transposed convolutions performs hierarchical upsampling from (h,w) to (16h,16w) for dense geometric reconstruction. Training uses L1 loss on valid pixels over 250K iterations (batch 1024, 128 GPUs, BF16) on 10M samples comprising 1M synthetic renders (Blender + SGM stereo for realistic sensor artifacts) and 2M real captures from a 3D-printed multi-camera rig with stereo-derived pseudo-depth supervision, plus open-source RGB-D datasets.

## Results

On the block-wise depth completion benchmark (Protocol 1), MDM achieves the lowest RMSE across all four datasets and all four difficulty levels. Under the most challenging 'Extreme' setting, MDM reduces RMSE by over 40% relative to the best competitor (PromptDA) on indoor benchmarks: 0.303 vs. 0.607 on iBims-1, 0.167 vs. 0.324 on NYUv2, and 0.219 vs. 0.465 on DIODE-Indoor. On the sparse depth protocol (Protocol 2), MDM achieves the best average rank (1.60) across VOID, KITTI-DC, and ETH3D, with a notable 58% RMSE reduction on ETH3D sparse-SFM (0.152 vs. 0.360 for PriorDA). As a monocular depth prior for FoundationStereo, MDM pretraining yields faster convergence and superior final EPE/BP-1.0 compared to both DepthAnythingV2 and MoGe backbones across five stereo benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDM outperforms all five depth-completion baselines (CDM, OMNI-DC, OMNI-DC-DA, PriorDA, PromptDA) on every dataset and difficulty level in Protocol 1, with the largest margins over CDM (>4× RMSE reduction) and the most competitive gap over PromptDA (~40–50% RMSE reduction on indoor scenes, ~9% on DIODE-Outdoor). Under Protocol 2 (sparse inputs), MDM beats PriorDA on VOID (0.487 vs. 0.524 sparse-500) and ETH3D sparse-SFM (0.152 vs. 0.360), and outperforms Marigold-DC and Any2Full across all sparse settings, though it trails OMNI-DC-DA slightly on KITTI-DC lidar-wise (1.412 vs. 1.310) and ETH3D lidar-wise (0.106 vs. 0.101). As a depth prior for FoundationStereo, MDM pretraining surpasses both the original DepthAnythingV2 backbone and the MoGe backbone in EPE and BP-1.0 at all evaluated epochs (10, 15, 20) across ETH3D, Middlebury, Booster, HAMMER, and FSD, confirming its value as a stronger geometric initialization for stereo matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDM repurposes the masked autoencoder paradigm (MAE) and the ConvStack decoder from MoGe for a fundamentally different masking signal: rather than random patch removal or cross-view completion (CroCo), it treats sensor-induced depth holes as 'natural masks' that encode geometric and appearance ambiguities. This departs from prior depth completion methods (OMNI-DC, PromptDA, PriorDA, CDM) that design task-specific fusion modules to inject sparse depth into RGB features; MDM instead trains a single ViT on joint RGB-depth tokens via a unified pretraining objective, letting self-attention naturally integrate metric geometry into visual representations. The connection to I-JEPA's latent-space prediction is conceptual rather than architectural—MDM still regresses pixel-level depth rather than predicting abstract embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of sensor failures as informative learning signals rather than noise to discard offers a scalable path to improving RGB-D sensing without hardware upgrades, and the released 3M RGB-D corpus (2M real, 1M synthetic) with a modular capture rig lowers the barrier for future depth research. Practical impact is demonstrated through dexterous grasping of transparent and reflective objects that defeat raw sensors, and through improved stereo matching when MDM replaces DepthAnythingV2 as a depth prior. Open questions include whether the 'natural masks are harder than random masks' hypothesis holds at larger scale, how well the approach generalizes to outdoor long-range scenes, and whether the 250K-iteration ViT-Large pretraining cost is prohibitive for most groups.
