## Problem

Evaluating Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I) cooperative autonomous driving faces a fundamental trade-off: open-loop metrics fail to capture error accumulation and recovery, while closed-loop simulation is costly and suffers from domain gaps. Existing pseudo-simulation approaches that approximate closed-loop evaluation are restricted to single-agent settings, leaving cooperative V2I scenarios unexplored. The paper also notes that most V2I methods focus on perception-level enhancement, with their integration into end-to-end planning underexplored.

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized multi-view vehicle RGB images (360° surround cameras) and multi-view infrastructure RGB images from the V2X-Real dataset, where vehicles and infrastructure are paired by spatial proximity and visibility. The ego agent outputs a 5-second future trajectory executed in a BEV kinematic simulation, along with 3D object detection boxes and online vector map elements. In Stage 2 of the benchmark, novel views are synthesized at perturbed ego positions—vehicle views via 3D Gaussian Splatting with Diffix3D+ refinement, and infrastructure views via SAM3-based ego-vehicle patch relocation and inpainting—producing paired vehicle-infrastructure observations under a consistent simulated scenario.

## Method

VIPS extends two-stage pseudo-simulation to V2I: Stage 1 evaluates on real observations, while Stage 2 samples candidate states around the expert endpoint (lateral offsets at 1 m intervals up to 2 m, longitudinal at 5 m intervals) and synthesizes paired novel views for both vehicle and infrastructure, with scores aggregated via Gaussian weighting by proximity to the Stage 1 endpoint. The final score multiplies Stage 1 and Stage 2 EPDMS values to jointly assess nominal performance and robustness. CoS-V2X is a sparse anchor-based cooperative framework using N learnable anchors with an instance bank for temporal priors; it selects Top-K high-confidence infrastructure instances to limit communication, performs bidirectional cross-attention between vehicle and infrastructure features, and fuses aligned anchors via confidence-weighted averaging. Downstream motion prediction and planning follow SparseDrive's unified parallel formulation, generating multiple trajectory candidates for both surrounding agents and the ego vehicle, with the final plan selected by score.

## Results

On the VIPS benchmark (V2X-Real dataset), CoS-V2X achieves the best integrated S1+S2 EPDMS score of 50.88, outperforming the next-best single-agent method MomAD (45.38) and the prior V2I method Uni-V2X (43.79). On Stage 1 (real observations), CoS-V2X scores 78.69 EPDMS, and on Stage 2 (perturbed observations) it scores 73.21, demonstrating both strong nominal performance and robustness to distribution shifts. In terms of efficiency, CoS-V2X uses 8.86 GB peak training memory (vs. 29.38 GB for Uni-V2X), runs at 6.4 FPS (vs. 0.2 FPS), and requires only 2.5×10⁶ BPS transmission cost (vs. 3.5×10⁶ BPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoS-V2X outperforms all six baselines on the integrated S1+S2 EPDMS metric, with the largest margin over AD-MLP (+18.57) and UniAD (+13.60). Against the closest single-agent competitor MomAD (45.38), CoS-V2X gains +5.50, and against the prior V2I method Uni-V2X (43.79), it gains +7.09. On Stage 1 (nominal performance), CoS-V2X leads all methods by at least 2.99 EPDMS over Uni-V2X (78.69 vs. 75.70). On Stage 2 (robustness under perturbation), CoS-V2X again leads, scoring 73.21 vs. Uni-V2X's 72.35 and MomAD's 67.48, indicating that the sparse cooperative representation provides meaningful robustness gains beyond nominal accuracy. Notably, CoS-V2X achieves these gains while using 3.3× less training memory, 32× higher inference speed, and 29% lower transmission cost compared to Uni-V2X, making it both more accurate and more practical for real-time V2I deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-simulation paradigm introduced by NAVSIM v2 (Cao et al., 2025) from single-agent to Vehicle-to-Infrastructure cooperative settings, requiring paired novel-view synthesis for both vehicle and fixed infrastructure cameras. For the planning architecture, it builds on SparseDrive's anchor-based sparse representation and extends it to a cooperative V2I context, departing from the dense BEV representations used by prior V2I E2E-AD methods like Uni-V2X. The benchmark is constructed on the V2X-Real dataset, to which the authors add vector map annotations absent from the original release, enabling geometry-aware planning evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIPS addresses a clear gap: prior V2X cooperative planning evaluations were limited to open-loop metrics (missing error accumulation) or expensive closed-loop simulation (suffering domain gaps), and no pseudo-simulation benchmark existed for V2I scenarios. The practical finding that a simple masking-and-inpainting strategy (LPIPS 0.076) dramatically outperforms full 3D Gaussian Splatting (LPIPS 0.324) for fixed infrastructure cameras is a useful insight for anyone generating synthetic views in V2I pipelines. Open questions include whether pseudo-simulation scores in the cooperative setting correlate with true closed-loop V2I performance, whether the non-reactive assumption (surrounding agents follow recorded motions) limits the benchmark's validity for interaction-heavy scenarios, and how the framework generalizes beyond the single V2X-Real dataset.
