## Problem

Traditional image stitching relies on hand-crafted geometric features (e.g., SIFT keypoints) that excel in structured scenes but fail in low-texture or repetitive environments, while learning-based methods extract semantic features that handle challenging conditions but lack explicit geometric structural awareness. These two research lines have evolved in isolation with virtually no convergence, leaving no unified framework that jointly leverages both modalities at the representational level.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (3×H₀×W₀) along with their extracted keypoints and descriptors ((2+d)×n per image), where keypoints can originate from any detector (SIFT, SURF, ORB, SuperPoint) paired with a corresponding matcher (KNN, SuperGlue, LightGlue). The sparse, unordered 1D keypoint sets are internally converted into dense 2D geometric feature maps at 1/8 and 1/16 spatial scales to match the semantic branch. The output is a parametric warp: a global homography decomposed into per-image projections plus local TPS control-point offsets, with the TPS applied via an FFD-based compress-then-restore scheme that avoids the OOM bottleneck of vanilla TPS at high resolutions (e.g., 2448×3264).

## Method

UniStitch is a three-stage pipeline: (1) multimodal feature alignment, where a Neural Point Transformer (NPT) encodes sparse keypoints via PointNeXt into high-dimensional point features and projects them onto zero-initialized grids at matching spatial scales using floor-function quantization and max-pooling for cell collisions, while a ResNet-18 backbone extracts multi-scale semantic maps; (2) multimodal feature fusion via an Adaptive Mixture of Experts (AMoE) with three residual experts (semantic, geometric, heterogeneous) governed by a softmax-gated router that dynamically reweights modal contributions, augmented by a Latent-space Modality Robustifier that applies random dropout or Gaussian noise in feature space during a second training phase to prevent over-reliance on modal coupling; (3) global-to-local warp prediction using homography decomposition followed by FFD-based TPS, which compresses the high-resolution meshgrid to a low-resolution grid, applies the TPS transform, and interpolates back via cubic B-spline basis functions, reducing peak GPU memory by up to 40% without accuracy loss. Training is two-stage (100 epochs without MR, then 100 epochs with alignment frozen and MR active), optimized with a loss combining content alignment, shape preservation, and an expert regularization term that penalizes weight variance and encourages specialization via negative entropy.

## Results

On the UDIS-D dataset (in-domain), UniStitch achieves an average mPSNR of 25.07 and mSSIM of 0.813, outperforming the strongest learning-based baseline RopStitch (24.70 / 0.800) and StabStitch++ (24.63 / 0.797) by a clear margin. On the classical OOD dataset, UniStitch with iterative adaptation (Ours*) reaches 20.68 mPSNR and 0.692 mSSIM, surpassing RopStitch* (19.74 / 0.645) and all traditional geometric methods (APAP: 18.92 / 0.628). The FFD-based TPS module reduces peak GPU memory from 4.81 GB to 2.76 GB at 566×800 resolution and enables stitching at 2448×3264 where vanilla TPS fails with OOM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniStitch outperforms all eight baselines on the UDIS-D in-domain benchmark, with the largest gaps over traditional geometric methods (APAP: +2.68 mPSNR, SPW: +4.55, LPC: +4.25) and a consistent +0.37 to +0.44 mPSNR advantage over the strongest learning-based competitors (RopStitch, StabStitch++). On the classical OOD dataset, UniStitch without post-processing (18.80 mPSNR) already surpasses all traditional methods and most learning-based baselines, but trails RopStitch* (19.74) which benefits from iterative adaptation; with the same post-processing, UniStitch* (20.68) reverses this and leads by +0.94 mPSNR. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that geometric features contribute most to OOD generalization (18.02 vs. 17.91 mPSNR for point-only vs. image-only), while semantic features dominate in-domain (24.63 vs. 24.22), and their fusion via AMoE+MR yields the best of both (25.07 / 18.80). Among learning-based keypoints, SuperPoint+LightGlue provides the strongest geometric prior (18.48 mPSNR OOD), outperforming SIFT+KNN (18.17) and SURF+KNN (18.14).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniStitch builds directly on StabStitch++ for its global-to-local warp paradigm (homography decomposition followed by TPS residual prediction) and adopts the same loss formulation. Conceptually, it departs from the field's long-standing bifurcation between traditional geometric-feature stitching (SIFT-based pipelines like APAP, SPW, LPC) and learning-based semantic stitching (UDIS, StabStitch++, RopStitch) by treating the two as complementary modalities within a single network. The Neural Point Transformer repurposes PointNeXt (a 3D point-cloud encoder) to convert sparse 2D keypoints into dense feature maps, while the Adaptive Mixture of Experts borrows the MoE routing paradigm from multi-task learning and applies it to cross-modal fusion—a use case not previously explored in stitching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper validates a long-standing intuition that geometric and semantic features are complementary, but the empirical gains are modest: ~0.37 mPSNR over RopStitch in-domain and ~0.36 mPSNR OOD (without post-processing), which is meaningful but not transformative. The FFD-based TPS is a practical contribution that enables high-resolution stitching (2448×3264) where vanilla TPS exhausts GPU memory, making it useful beyond this specific architecture. Open questions include whether the unification extends to video stitching, multi-image sequences, or other geometric cues (lines, edges), and whether the modality robustifier strategy generalizes to other multimodal fusion problems. The OOD gap between learning-based and traditional methods remains substantial even after unification, suggesting deeper structural understanding is still needed.
