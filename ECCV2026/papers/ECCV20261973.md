## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary camouflaged object segmentation (OVCOS) methods adopt a one-shot two-stage pipeline where CLIP category predictions are treated as fixed priors for downstream segmentation, creating a unidirectional information flow that cannot correct early semantic errors. In camouflaged scenes, where foreground-background visual similarity is extreme, even slight semantic deviations are amplified during segmentation, and the spatial structural information formed during masking is never fed back to refine category predictions, severely limiting robustness and self-correction capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261973/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 384×384) and a set of Top-N text category candidates (default N=10) as input, and produces a pixel-level binary segmentation mask along with a refined category label as output. During inference, SAM2-generated masks serve as spatial priors for the re-ranker, enabling annotation-free deployment; during training, ground-truth masks are used instead. The iterative loop runs for a fixed number of steps (default t=3), progressively updating both the semantic state vector and the spatial mask at each iteration.

## Method

ISSR establishes a closed-loop iterative refinement between two jointly optimized modules built on frozen CLIP and SAM2 backbones. The category re-ranker constructs low-dimensional ranking feature vectors from CLIP confidence scores, relative positions, and score gaps, then fuses them with multi-level CLIP visual features (layers 12, 16, 18) and SAM2 spatial masks to produce refined ranking scores via a margin-based pairwise loss restricted to the Top-N set. The segmentation modulator, inserted between SAM2's encoder and mask decoder, aligns visual and textual features through modality-specific gating, applies multi-head attention for fine-grained semantic-spatial correspondence, computes a cross-modal relevance map for category-level consistency, and uses a learnable scalar (via sigmoid) to adaptively balance fine-grained attention interaction against category-aware refinement. A learnable continuous prompt (16 context vectors shared across categories) replaces handcrafted templates to support open-vocabulary generalization. The re-ranker and modulator alternate updates over t iterations, where each round's spatial mask refines the next round's semantic ranking and vice versa, progressively correcting early biases.

## Results

On the OVCamo dataset (trained on OVCamo), ISSR achieves cSm = 0.723, cFβw = 0.663, cMAE = 0.211, cFβ = 0.693, cEm = 0.762, and cIoU = 0.611, outperforming the previous OVCOS state-of-the-art SuCLIP by 5.6% in cSm and 7.1% in cIoU. Compared to OVCoser, the improvement is 14.4% in cSm and 16.8% in cIoU. On conventional COS benchmarks, ISSR also leads: on CAMO it reaches Sm = 0.896 and Fβw = 0.870; on COD10K, Sm = 0.898 and Fβw = 0.832; on NC4K, Sm = 0.905 and Fβw = 0.883, surpassing all 9 compared COS methods on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISSR outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every reported metric. Against the two dedicated OVCOS methods, ISSR improves cSm by 14.4% over OVCoser (0.723 vs. 0.579) and by 5.6% over SuCLIP (0.723 vs. 0.667), with corresponding gains of 16.8% and 7.1% in cIoU. Against the six general OVSS methods (SimSeg, OVSeg, ODISE, SAN, CAT-Seg, FC-CLIP), the margins are substantially larger—e.g., cSm of 0.723 vs. 0.275 for the strongest OVSS baseline SAN—confirming that generic open-vocabulary segmentation pipelines are poorly suited to camouflage. On the conventional COS task (Table 2), ISSR leads all nine baselines on all three datasets; the closest competitor SAM2-UNet is surpassed by 0.9–2.0% in Fβw and 1.3–1.8% in Em, while diffusion-based CamoDiffusion trails by 1.7–2.4% in Fβw. No baseline matches or exceeds ISSR on any metric in any setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the OVCOS task introduced by OVCoser (ECCV 2024) and the context-aware prompting of SuCLIP (ICCV 2025), both of which adopt a one-shot two-stage pipeline where CLIP category predictions serve as fixed priors for a segmentation model. The key departure is replacing this unidirectional flow with a bidirectional iterative loop: a spatial-structure-aware re-ranker uses SAM2-generated masks to correct CLIP's Top-N ranking, and a segmentation modulator injects the refined semantics back into SAM2's encoder-decoder interface. The learnable prompt mechanism connects to the broader prompt-learning literature (CoOp-style), while the overall architecture repurposes frozen CLIP and SAM2 as the semantic and spatial backbones respectively, with all trainable parameters confined to the re-ranker, modulator, and prompt tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iterative refinement paradigm offers a principled answer to a core weakness of one-shot OVCOS methods: early semantic errors in camouflage are amplified and irrecoverable without spatial feedback. The 14.4% cSm gain over OVCoser and consistent improvements across all six OVCOS metrics, plus competitive results on standard COS benchmarks (CAMO, COD10K, NC4K), suggest that treating semantics as a dynamically updated state rather than a static prior is a meaningful design shift for open-vocabulary segmentation under visual ambiguity. However, the evaluation is limited to a single OVCOS dataset (OVCamo) with only two prior OVCOS baselines, and the authors explicitly note that multi-category scenes remain unaddressed, requiring instance decomposition and multi-label reasoning that the current single-object loop cannot handle.
