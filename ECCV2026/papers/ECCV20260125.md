## Problem

Raw sensor images (12–14 bit linear data) are impractical to store in their native DNG format (tens of MB per image), yet standard JPEG—designed for 8-bit gamma-corrected display images—produces severe banding, clipping, and color distortion when applied directly to raw signals. No widely supported format exists that stores raw images at manageable file sizes while preserving the fidelity needed for post-capture editing and vision tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a demosaiced raw RGB image (12–14 bit per channel, linear sensor response) and produces a standard JPEG file whose comment (COM) segment carries zlib-compressed, Base64-encoded operator parameters (<64 KB). At decode time, the JPEG is decompressed normally and the inverse operators are applied to reconstruct the raw image; no neural network is invoked during decoding. The network itself operates on a downsampled thumbnail of the input to predict per-image parameters, and the JPEG quality setting provides a user-controlled size–fidelity trade-off.

## Method

A compact CNN (~37 K parameters) with an encoder–decoder architecture (GELU activations, ECA channel-attention, skip connections) predicts three sets of invertible operator parameters from a thumbnail: channel-wise 1D lookup tables (128×3), an optional global 8×8 DCT scaling matrix, and a pixel-wise 100×100 gamma map. Before JPEG compression, the raw image is transformed by applying the LuTs, then the DCT scaling (element-wise on 8×8 block DCT coefficients), then the spatially varying gamma; after JPEG decoding, the exact inverse sequence restores the raw domain. The network is trained self-supervised with a differentiable JPEG simulator, optimizing a composite L1 + SSIM + FFT loss against the original raw input, with intensity and color augmentations to improve cross-camera robustness. Because all operators are analytically invertible and their parameters fit in the JPEG COM field, decoding requires only lightweight arithmetic—no learned model—yielding ~0.1 s overhead and <64 KB of side information.

## Results

On the S24 test set at JPEG quality 95, Raw-JPEG Adapter (with DCT and augmentations) achieves PSNR 46.22 dB, SSIM 98.60, and MS-SSIM 99.72 at 2.345 BPP with only 0.120 s runtime overhead. At quality 100, it reaches PSNR 49.04 dB and SSIM 99.35, outperforming direct JPEG (46.00 dB) by over 3 dB. When paired with the deep LIC-TCM codec, the method achieves PSNR 46.03 dB and MS-SSIM 99.82 at 1.26 BPP. Cross-camera evaluation on S7, MIT-Adobe FiveK, and NUS shows the spatial-domain pipeline (without DCT) generalizes best, e.g., 49.67 dB PSNR on MIT-Adobe FiveK at quality 95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Raw-JPEG Adapter decisively outperforms both bidirectional neural ISP baselines: at quality 95 it achieves 46.22 dB PSNR versus 43.71 dB for Invertible ISP [48] (and 20.47 dB for CIE XYZ Net [2]), while running 66× faster (0.120 s vs. 7.932 s). Against the strongest metadata-based method, R2LCM (drop0) [44,45], Raw-JPEG Adapter at Q=95 edges ahead on PSNR (46.22 vs. 46.01 dB) while using substantially less storage (2.345 vs. 3.929 BPP) and 23× less runtime (0.120 s vs. 2.815 s). The INF method [20] trails by 5 dB in PSNR and requires 25.93 s of test-time fitting. Among pre-processing baselines at quality 100, Raw-JPEG Adapter (49.04 dB) surpasses fixed gamma (48.38 dB), raw↔sRGB mapping (47.65 dB), and OLM (43.65 dB) by 0.66, 1.39, and 5.39 dB respectively. The BD-rate analysis (Table 3) confirms a 27.88% bitrate saving over direct JPEG and 13.56% over raw↔sRGB at matched PSNR. Cross-camera results (Table 7) show the DCT component hurts generalization (e.g., 40.96 vs. 43.07 dB on MIT-Adobe FiveK at Q=25), so the spatial-only variant is recommended for multi-camera use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for raw image preservation: (1) codec-specific raw compression (e.g., OLM, JPEG XS adaptations) designed for real-time video transport, and (2) raw reconstruction from ISP-rendered sRGB using substantial auxiliary metadata (1–2 MB) or bidirectional neural ISPs. Rather than reconstructing raw from a lossy sRGB rendering (which requires compensating for irreversible tone-mapping and gamma operations), Raw-JPEG Adapter stores the raw signal directly inside a standard JPEG file, using a tiny (~37K parameter) network to predict per-image coefficients for simple invertible operators (pixel-wise gamma, channel-wise 1D LuTs, optional global DCT scaling) that align raw statistics with JPEG's fixed quantization tables. The learned parameters are serialized via zlib/Base64 into the JPEG COM segment (<64 KB), making the pipeline fully self-contained and decodable without any neural network at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: JPEG is universally supported across devices and platforms, whereas DNG files (30+ MB) are impractical for mobile capture, cloud sync, or large-scale vision datasets; this method reduces raw storage to under 2 MB while achieving PSNR gains of ~2.6 dB over direct JPEG at matched bitrate and outperforming heavier alternatives (bidirectional neural ISPs, metadata-based reconstruction) with only ~0.1 s overhead. A notable open question is cross-camera generalization: the DCT scaling component, while beneficial on the training camera, degrades on unseen sensors, suggesting that a fully camera-agnostic variant (spatial-only pipeline) may be the more deployable configuration. The paper also demonstrates compatibility with deep codecs (LIC-TCM) and JPEG 2000, hinting at broader applicability, but residual banding and clipping at low quality levels remain, and the method is inherently lossy rather than lossless.
