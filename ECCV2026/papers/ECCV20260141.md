## Problem

Existing discrete motion generation methods either grow token sequences along the time axis (next-token prediction) or iteratively fill masked positions, neither of which explicitly exploits the inherent coarse-to-fine hierarchy of 3D human motion where sparse key poses convey global semantics and progressively denser frames add local detail. This paper asks whether reframing autoregressive generation as next-scale prediction—producing a full motion in a constant number of scale steps rather than many temporal steps—can improve both structural coherence and text-motion alignment.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a free-form text prompt (processed via T5-base into word-level embeddings) and produces a 3D human pose sequence of length N over 7 atomic skeletal segments (root, spine, head, two arms, two legs). For zero-shot editing, it additionally takes a source motion and a target text prompt, with binary preservation masks specifying which skeletal-temporal regions to retain versus resample. Generation proceeds through exactly (V+1) scale steps starting from a 1×1 token map, requiring only 7 sampling steps at inference.

## Method

ScaleMoGen encodes motion into a skeletal-temporal latent grid (n×7×d) and progressively quantizes it in a residual manner into (V+1) token maps of increasing temporal and skeletal granularity, using bitwise quantization (each dimension is a binary label, yielding vocabulary size 2^d) and topology-aware upsampling/downsampling operators that respect the kinematic hierarchy. A transformer then autoregressively predicts each next-scale token map conditioned on the accumulated coarser-scale reconstruction and text embeddings, with the prediction target being independent binary classification losses over bits rather than a single categorical index. During training, stochastic bit-perturbation simulates coarse-scale prediction errors to encourage self-correction, and a block-wise causal attention mask enforces strict hierarchical dependencies. The multi-scale skeletal-temporal structure also enables zero-shot text-driven editing by selectively masking tokens at specific scales, joints, or temporal intervals and resampling them under a new text prompt.

## Results

On HumanML3D, ScaleMoGen achieves the best FID of 0.030 (vs. 0.033 for MoGenTS and 0.045 for MoMask) and the best MM Distance of 2.626, while matching SALAD on R-Precision Top-2 (0.769) and Top-3 (0.856 vs. 0.857). On SnapMoGen, ScaleMoGen attains the best R-Precision Top-3 (0.941) and CLIP Score (0.693 vs. 0.685 for MoMask++), though its FID (16.35) is marginally higher than MoMask++ (15.06). The method requires only 7 sampling steps at inference, the fewest among all compared methods, with an average inference time of 0.071 s per sample on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaleMoGen achieves the best FID (0.030) on HumanML3D, outperforming the closest discrete baselines MoGenTS (0.033) and MoMask (0.045) by 9% and 33% respectively, and substantially beating diffusion-based methods like MDM (0.544) and MLD (0.473). Against SALAD, the strongest diffusion baseline, ScaleMoGen trades a marginal R-Precision Top-1 deficit (0.577 vs. 0.581) for a 61% lower FID (0.030 vs. 0.076) and better MM Distance (2.626 vs. 2.649). On SnapMoGen, ScaleMoGen leads MoMask++ in R-Precision Top-3 (0.941 vs. 0.938) and CLIP Score (0.693 vs. 0.685), confirming superior text-motion alignment on long-form prompts, though MoMask++ retains a slight FID advantage (15.06 vs. 16.35). Compared to the next-scale autoregressive baseline MoScale, ScaleMoGen improves FID by 35% (0.030 vs. 0.046) and R-Precision Top-3 by 4.5% (0.856 vs. 0.817). In the zero-shot editing user study, ScaleMoGen outperforms both MDM and SALAD on all three criteria (Preservation 4.41, Semantic Alignment 4.78, Overall Quality 4.65), with the largest gap in semantic alignment over SALAD (4.78 vs. 3.10). Inference efficiency favors ScaleMoGen with only 7 sampling steps versus 10 for MoMask++, 18 for MoGenTS, and 1000 for MDM.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScaleMoGen transplants the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) next-scale prediction paradigm from image synthesis into the motion generation domain, replacing the dominant next-token or masked-token paradigms used by T2M-GPT, MoMask, and MoGenTS. It further departs from prior multi-scale motion tokenizers (DuetGen, MoMask++, MoScale) by defining scales along two orthogonal axes—temporal resolution and skeletal partition—rather than temporal granularity alone, and by enforcing a recursive skeletal hierarchy through topology-aware up/downsampling operators. The bitwise (binary spherical) quantization borrowed from Zhao et al. is combined with residual multi-scale quantization in a way not previously applied to articulated motion, yielding a 2^d vocabulary without explicit codebook learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The skeletal-temporal multi-scale representation gives practitioners a structured, interpretable latent space that supports zero-shot, localized motion editing (e.g., editing only the right arm in a time window) without task-specific training—a capability diffusion-based editors like SALAD achieve only through attention modulation. The 7-step inference (vs. 1000 for MDM) makes autoregressive motion generation practically competitive with diffusion in latency. A notable open question raised by the ablations is whether the observed degradation when scaling the predictor from 125 M to 2.2 B parameters reflects a fundamental data-bottleneck in current motion corpora; realizing scaling laws for motion will likely require orders-of-magnitude more diverse training data.
