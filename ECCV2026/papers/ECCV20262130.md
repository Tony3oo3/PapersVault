## Problem

Existing infrared-visible video fusion methods adopt a 'temporal-first' paradigm, performing temporal modeling within each modality before cross-modal fusion. This design makes motion estimation vulnerable to modality-specific degradations—low illumination and occlusion in visible streams, sensor noise and flicker in infrared sequences—causing errors to propagate into the fusion stage and producing temporally unstable results. The paper asks whether performing cross-modal fusion before temporal refinement can yield a more robust joint representation for stable video fusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized infrared and visible video sequences, encoding visible frames into a compact latent space via a pre-trained VAE and extracting infrared features through a lightweight trainable adapter. Processing operates primarily in the VAE latent space (low resolution) for fusion and global temporal modeling, with full-resolution refinement only at the final decoder stage. The output is a temporally consistent fused video; for evaluation, the first 50 frames of each test sequence are used, and inference is a single deterministic step via the Euler method on the rectified-flow ODE.

## Method

DRT-VF is a one-step diffusion framework trained with a two-stage progressive strategy. In Stage I, a Modality-Collaborative Attention (MCA) module uses a shared query (from concatenated visible and infrared features) to perform independent cross-attention against each modality's keys and values, producing a unified intra-frame representation; this is then refined by a one-step Diffusion Transformer (DiT) operating under a rectified-flow objective that maps noise to the fused latent in a single Euler step. In Stage II, temporal consistency is modeled hierarchically: a Global Temporal Consistency (GTC) module computes channel-wise statistics via global average pooling over three consecutive frames, forms a deviation residual to detect flicker, and applies sigmoid-gated channel modulation in latent space; a Local Motion Compensation (LMC) module, embedded before the final decoder layer, aligns neighboring frames with deformable convolutions and then fuses them through a dual-branch design—Context Aggregation via Mutual Self-Attention for semantic consistency and Boundary Refinement via absolute gradient residuals through a ResBlock for sharp motion contours. The key architectural insight is that fusing modalities first creates a degradation-robust joint representation from which reliable motion cues can be derived, rather than relying on potentially corrupted single-modality temporal estimates.

## Results

On four infrared-visible video fusion benchmarks (HDO, M3SVD, NOT-156, VTMOT), DRT-VF achieves the best or near-best fusion quality, e.g., MI of 3.521/3.373/4.449/4.232 and SSIM of 0.680/0.712/0.642/0.662 respectively, consistently within the top two across all spatial metrics. For temporal stability, DRT-VF obtains the best BiSWE and MS2R on HDO, M3SVD, and NOT-156 (e.g., BiSWE 6.232 on HDO vs. 6.378 for UniVF; MS2R 0.574 on NOT-156 vs. 0.591 for UniVF), and the best MS2R on VTMOT (0.332). In the downstream object-tracking task on NOT-156, DRT-VF achieves the highest AUC (0.244), DP@20 (0.236), SR@0.5 (0.248), and SR@0.75 (0.116) among all 13 methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRT-VF is compared against 10 image-level fusion methods (CDDFuse, DCEVO, DDFM, GIFNet, LRRNet, Mask-Dif, MetaFusion, PromptFusion, SAGE, TIMFusion) and 2 video-level methods (TemCOCO, UniVF). Among image-based methods, DCEVO is the strongest spatial competitor (e.g., MI 3.409 on HDO, 3.421 on M3SVD), but DRT-VF surpasses it on MI and SSIM across all four datasets while matching or exceeding it on VIF. The closest overall competitor is UniVF, the most recent video fusion benchmark: DRT-VF edges out UniVF on MI (e.g., 4.449 vs. 4.410 on NOT-156), SSIM (e.g., 0.712 vs. 0.688 on M3SVD), and both temporal metrics (BiSWE 6.232 vs. 6.378 on HDO; MS2R 0.574 vs. 0.591 on NOT-156), though UniVF retains a slight VIF advantage on HDO (0.806 vs. 0.792). TemCOCO, the other video baseline, is notably weaker on spatial quality (e.g., SSIM 0.441 on M3SVD) and on VTMOT temporal consistency (BiSWE 4.593 vs. DRT-VF's 4.822, where TemCOCO actually wins on BiSWE but DRT-VF wins on MS2R 0.332 vs. 0.382). In the downstream tracking task, DRT-VF leads all 12 baselines on every metric, with the largest gap over UniVF (AUC 0.244 vs. 0.212; SR@0.75 0.116 vs. 0.071), confirming that the fusion-first temporal strategy yields more discriminative representations for real-world perception tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the one-step rectified-flow diffusion paradigm for image fusion (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2026) and extends it to the video setting, while departing from the dominant 'temporal-first' design used by prior video fusion methods (TemCOCO, UniVF, RCVS, VideoFusion, MambaVF) that perform temporal modeling within each modality before cross-modal fusion. The key conceptual departure is a 'fusion-first' strategy: cross-modal integration via a Modality-Collaborative Attention module precedes temporal refinement, so that motion estimation operates on a joint representation rather than on degraded single-modality observations. The hierarchical temporal design (global channel-statistics regularization + local deformable-convolution-based motion compensation) draws on standard components (GAP, DCNv2, Mutual Self-Attention) but arranges them in a two-level decomposition not previously applied to infrared-visible video fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fusion-first paradigm offers a principled alternative to the temporal-first pipeline that is widely assumed in multimodal video processing; if the degradation-robustness argument generalizes, it could influence design choices in other paired-modality video tasks (e.g., RGB-D, LiDAR-camera). Practical limitations include the reliance on a single RTX 5090 for training (batch size 1 in Stage II), evaluation on only 50 frames per sequence, and modest absolute gains over the strongest baseline (UniVF) on spatial metrics. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, whether the one-step diffusion backbone can be further accelerated for real-time deployment, and whether the fusion-first principle holds when both modalities are simultaneously degraded.
