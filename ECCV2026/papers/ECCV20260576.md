## Problem

Most image colorization systems predict chroma in Lab space while treating the input-derived luminance channel as immutable, making them mathematically incapable of altering brightness. This fixed-luminance constraint limits creative recolorization and becomes especially problematic for historical orthochromatic photography, where red-insensitive film produces grayscale images whose brightness distribution does not match human visual perception, causing models to suppress reds and fail to lift dark regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260576/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel grayscale image (formed either by standard panchromatic luminance 0.299R+0.587G+0.114B or by the simplified orthochromatic approximation (B+G)/2) together with a text prompt that includes a trigger word ('colorize' or 'colorize ortho') followed by a scene caption, and produces a full three-channel RGB colorized image. The grayscale input is spatially concatenated with the noisy latent during diffusion, and the text prompt conditions the DiT to interpret luminance values differently depending on the grayscale formation type. Captions are generated by BLIP2 for ImageNet and COCO, while the Multi-Instance benchmark provides human-annotated captions.

## Method

The core contribution is a luminance-agnostic colorization framework that formulates colorization as full-RGB image editing rather than chroma prediction under a fixed luminance constraint. The authors fine-tune the FLUX.2-klein-4B foundation image-editing model (a Rectified Flow DiT) using LoRA (rank 16, ~23.1M trainable parameters out of 4.3B total), keeping the VAE and Qwen3-4B text encoder frozen. The key training innovation is a Mixed Grayscale Objective: during training, each image is randomly assigned either a standard panchromatic grayscale with prompt 'colorize' or a simulated orthochromatic grayscale (red channel excluded) with prompt 'colorize ortho', teaching the model to reinterpret brightness based on the conditioning signal. Under the Rectified Flow formulation, the DiT predicts the velocity field transporting Gaussian noise to the data latent, conditioned on both the text embedding and the spatially concatenated grayscale image, enabling it to 'lift' brightness in dark orthochromatic regions where a fixed-L model would fail.

## Results

On the COCO benchmark under standard panchromatic grayscale, the proposed method achieves FID 8.73, sFID 9.50, and FID-DINO 37.33, trailing DDColor (7.06 / 8.26 / 32.34) on FID-family metrics but leading on all color-statistics metrics (Colorfulness 49.31, Col-diverse 17.25, ColorNet 5.14). Under simulated orthochromatic inputs on COCO, the method achieves the best FID-DINO (43.32), Colorfulness (46.33), Col-diverse (16.36), and ColorNet (5.06), with only a small FID gap to DDColor (10.67 vs. 10.15). On ImageNet under orthochromatic inputs, the method is the strongest overall, achieving the best score on every reported metric: FID 7.91, sFID 9.35, FID-DINO 38.48, Colorfulness 45.59, Col-diverse 16.06, and ColorNet 4.98.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDColor (the strongest FID-family baseline), the proposed method trades a small FID gap (e.g., 8.73 vs. 7.06 on COCO pan) for substantially better color statistics: Colorfulness +5.31, Col-diverse +1.75, and ColorNet +0.08 on COCO pan. Under orthochromatic inputs the gap narrows or reverses: on ImageNet ortho the proposed method beats DDColor on every metric (FID 7.91 vs. 8.74, FID-DINO 38.48 vs. 42.36, Colorfulness 45.59 vs. 36.34). Against COCO-LC, the proposed method wins on FID, sFID, FID-DINO, and all color metrics in both pan and ortho settings across all three benchmarks, and additionally follows complex multi-color text prompts more accurately (Figure 6). Against BigColor, DISCO, and UniColor, the proposed method dominates on FID-family metrics in both settings; DISCO achieves higher Colorfulness and Saturation on pan inputs (52.18 vs. 49.31 on COCO) but degrades far more severely under ortho (FID 17.77 vs. 10.67). Overall, the method is competitive or best on standard grayscale and clearly superior under orthochromatic conditions, where baselines suffer FID-DINO increases of 50–100 points while the proposed method's increase is only ~6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Lab-space colorization paradigm (predict chroma ab while preserving input-derived luminance L) by reframing colorization as full-RGB image editing using a foundation image-editing model (FLUX.2-klein-4B). Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two recent trends: (1) the shift from text-to-image diffusion models retrofitted with ControlNet-style grayscale conditioning toward natively image-editing foundation models, and (2) the recognition that historical orthochromatic photography introduces a structural domain mismatch that fixed-luminance methods cannot resolve. The Mixed Grayscale Objective, which trains under both standard panchromatic and red-insensitive orthochromatic grayscale formations, is the paper's key methodological novelty, bridging modern digital imaging and historical film sensitivity in a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode: the fixed-luminance constraint makes standard colorizers structurally incapable of brightening dark regions, which is catastrophic for orthochromatic inputs where red wavelengths were never recorded. The practical implication is that a lightweight LoRA fine-tune (0.5% of parameters) of a foundation editing model can substantially close this gap while preserving structural fidelity (SSIM ~0.84), offering a viable path for archival photo restoration. Open questions remain: the orthochromatic simulation is a simplified two-channel average rather than a physically grounded spectral sensitivity model, the training data is limited to a 34K ImageNet subset, and the framework does not yet address other degradation factors (color fading, chemical aging, blue-bias) present in real archival imagery. Extending the mixed-objective idea to a broader family of grayscale/color distortions and incorporating more realistic film-response models would strengthen the approach for genuine historical restoration.
