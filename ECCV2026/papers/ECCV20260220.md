## Problem

Existing 4D reconstruction methods produce rendering-oriented outputs (implicit fields, Gaussian primitives, point clouds) that lack the watertight topology, instance separation, and standardized physical interfaces (e.g., URDF) required by physics simulators and embodied AI. No prior method can turn a monocular video into simulation-ready, instance-level 4D mesh scenes, and no benchmark evaluates the structural qualities—geometric correctness, instance separation, physical plausibility—that matter for downstream physical tasks beyond visual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260220/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video of a dynamic multi-object scene as input and produces a set of N watertight triangle meshes, each with a per-frame 6-DoF SE(3) pose trajectory; deformable objects are represented as topology-consistent mesh sequences with per-vertex displacement fields. The final scene is exported in URDF format with recovered HDR environment lighting, ready for direct import into physics engines such as MuJoCo, Isaac Gym, or PyBullet. The pipeline is entirely training-free, composing off-the-shelf foundation models (VLM, segmentation, inpainting, 3D generation, geometry, pose tracking) without any task-specific training.

## Method

OVOW is a four-stage training-free pipeline. Stage 1 uses a VLM (Qwen3-VL) on sampled keyframes to discover, label, and motion-classify all instances (static/rigid/deformable), followed by SAM3 for dense per-frame segmentation. Stage 2 reconstructs per-instance meshes: FLUX.2 amodal inpainting fills occluded regions before Hi3DGen generates canonical meshes for static/rigid objects, while Motion324 produces topology-consistent mesh sequences for deformable ones; VGGT provides camera parameters and dense depth for initial metric scale. Stage 3 recovers precise scale and 6-DoF trajectories via an iterative render-match-optimize loop (FoundationPose for orientation, RoMa v2 for dense correspondences, PnP-RANSAC + L-BFGS-B for scale) at the anchor frame, then bidirectional pose tracking across all frames. Stage 4 assembles the scene physically by estimating the ground plane via RANSAC, projecting objects to contact, and resolving inter-object support through KD-tree nearest-surface queries. The key design choice is modeling all motion—rigid and non-rigid—through direct vertex deformation without category-specific priors or skeleton rigging, yielding watertight, simulation-ready meshes.

## Results

On OVOW-4D-Scene-Bench (dynamic scenes), OVOW leads every quality metric: 0.440 Scene-IoU-OBB, 0.210 Object-IoU, 2.90 photometric loss, and 1.43 N-CLIP, while running at 3.35 s/frame — one to two orders of magnitude faster than all baselines (103–788 s/frame). On OVOW-3D-Scene-Bench (static scenes), OVOW achieves the best Scene-IoU-OBB (0.218), Object-IoU (0.190), PL (5.70), and N-CLIP (1.87), with VIGA slightly ahead on Scene-IoU-AABB (0.156 vs 0.130). The largest margins appear on the dynamic 4D benchmark, where video-level temporal cues are most informative and OVOW's amortized per-frame cost drops to 3.35 s versus 103–788 s for single-image baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six baselines (CAST, SAM3D, VIGA, MIDI, SceneGen, TabletopGen) are single-image scene reconstruction/generation methods that cannot natively consume video; the authors run each independently on seven uniformly sampled frames and average scores, whereas OVOW processes the full video. On the dynamic 4D benchmark, OVOW dominates every quality metric: Scene-IoU-OBB of 0.440 vs. the best baseline MIDI at 0.198 (2.2×), Object-IoU of 0.210 vs. MIDI's 0.174, and photometric loss of 2.90 vs. MIDI's 5.60. MIDI is the strongest baseline overall on both benchmarks, particularly on Object-IoU and photometric loss, but still trails OVOW by a wide margin on the 4D scenes. VIGA achieves the highest Scene-IoU-AABB on the static 3D benchmark (0.156 vs. OVOW's 0.130) but is worst on Object-IoU (0.024) and photometric loss (24.40), indicating poor per-object geometry despite reasonable global layout. In terms of speed, OVOW's 3.35 s/frame on the 4D benchmark is roughly 30–235× faster than the baselines (103–788 s/frame), since OVOW amortizes computation across the video while each baseline must independently reconstruct from every sampled frame.

## Relation to Prior Work

OVOW sits at the intersection of three research lines that have each addressed part of the problem but none the whole: 4D reconstruction (NeRF, Gaussian splatting, mesh-based) produces rendering-oriented primitives lacking watertight topology and instance separation; structured scene generation (CAST, MIDI, SceneGen) builds multi-object scenes from text or procedural rules rather than real video; and simulation-ready asset generation (automatic rigging, articulated-object models) relies on synthetic priors and category templates. The paper's key conceptual departure is a fully training-free pipeline that composes off-the-shelf foundation models (Qwen3-VL for scene understanding, SAM3 for segmentation, Hi3DGen/Motion324 for mesh generation, VGGT for geometry, FoundationPose for tracking) into a unified system that recovers instance-level, simulation-ready 4D mesh scenes directly from monocular video, using direct vertex deformation as a unified motion model that avoids skeleton rigging and category-specific priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a fundamental gap between 4D reconstruction (which excels at visual fidelity) and physics simulation (which requires watertight meshes, instance separation, and standardized interfaces like URDF), making it directly relevant to robotics, embodied AI, and gaming. The introduction of the first structured Video-to-4D benchmark with geometric and physical metrics beyond PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS provides the field with a much-needed evaluation standard. The pipeline also doubles as a scalable data engine for generating paired video-to-4D simulation data, addressing a critical data bottleneck for future 4D world models. Open questions include scalability to highly cluttered scenes with many interacting objects, handling of articulated motion beyond simple non-rigid deformation, and whether the training-free approach can be matched or surpassed by end-to-end learned methods as the field matures.
