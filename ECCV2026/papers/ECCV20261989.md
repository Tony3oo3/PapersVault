## Problem

Conventional VAEs in latent diffusion models apply fixed compression ratios to video, wasting representational capacity on static or homogeneous regions while under-allocating to motion-rich content. Existing flexible tokenization methods offer user-controlled budgets or require inference-time search, but do not let the model itself determine how many tokens each sample needs based on its content complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261989/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

A video clip (T×H×W×C) is patch-embedded into coarse 3D patches of size 16²×8 for encoding, and the decoder reconstructs on a finer 8²×4 grid via learnable query tokens. The number of output latent tokens is data-dependent (e.g., ~366 for a 256²×16 clip versus 5,120 for a fixed-length baseline), determined entirely by the learned keep/drop masks rather than a preset budget. For generation, the model takes Gaussian noise (and optionally a class label) and produces a video, with the token count at inference serving as an emergent control knob for motion complexity and visual detail.

## Method

KATok is a transformer VAE whose encoder produces continuous latent tokens and a lightweight importance network that predicts per-token keep/drop logits, relaxed via Gumbel-Softmax into differentiable soft masks that gate both the latent values and the decoder attention logits. An L1 sparsity loss on the soft masks, annealed during training, drives the model to discard uninformative tokens end-to-end without any predefined token budget. The decoder uses an asymmetric coarse-to-fine strategy (coarse 16²×8 encoding, fine 8²×4 decoding with FLUX-style double-stream blocks) and 3D RoPE with register tokens for structural stability. For downstream flow-matching generation, two remedies address content-position misalignment under sparse tokenization: joint content-position prediction with decoupled noise schedules, and a cascaded scheme where a lightweight 8.3M mask-prior model first predicts active token positions, which are then injected as positional conditioning into the main SiT-XL generator.

## Results

On the Panda-70M validation set at 256²×16, KATok achieves 31.24 PSNR and 5.12 rFVD using only 366 tokens (compression ratio 134.21), compared to 28.10 PSNR / 7.84 rFVD with 5,120 tokens (OmniTokenizer) and 30.52 PSNR / 12.37 rFVD with 3,846 tokens (ElasticTok). At 512²×32, KATok reaches 33.23 PSNR and 6.40 rFVD with 1,554 tokens (compression ratio 253.00), while OmniTokenizer drops to 24.07 PSNR and 16.85 rFVD with 36,864 tokens. For downstream video generation (gFVD↓), KATok-Cascaded achieves 61.53 on UCF-101, 23.19 on SkyTimelapse, and 160.84 on Kinetics-600, outperforming OmniTokenizer (100.00, 23.28, 206.58) and ElasticTok (712.56, 95.53, –) while generating ~11× fewer tokens. Training is 6.9× faster and inference 3.2× faster than OmniTokenizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OmniTokenizer (a fixed-length transformer VAE with 5,120 tokens at 256²×16), KATok wins on every reconstruction metric while using ~14× fewer tokens: PSNR improves from 28.10 to 31.24, rFVD drops from 7.84 to 5.12, and the compression ratio nearly doubles (96 → 134.21). At 512²×32 the gap widens dramatically—KATok achieves 33.23 PSNR vs. 24.07 for OmniTokenizer with only 1,554 vs. 36,864 tokens. Against ElasticTok (a variable-length tokenizer requiring per-sample binary search), KATok also dominates: at 256²×16, PSNR 31.24 vs. 30.52 and rFVD 5.12 vs. 12.37, with 366 vs. 3,846 tokens. For generation, KATok-Cascaded achieves gFVD 61.53 on UCF-101 versus 100.00 (OmniTokenizer) and 712.56 (ElasticTok), and 23.19 on SkyTimelapse versus 23.28 (OmniTokenizer) and 95.53 (ElasticTok). In terms of efficiency, KATok trains 6.9× faster than OmniTokenizer and 46.7× faster than ElasticTok, and infers 3.2× faster than OmniTokenizer, making it the clear winner across both quality and speed dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

KATok sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) fixed-length VAE tokenizers for video (OmniTokenizer, LTX-Video, DC-AE) that compress spatio-temporal dimensions uniformly, and (2) flexible-length tokenizers (ElasticTok, FlexTok, ALIT) that allow variable token counts but still require a user-specified budget or inference-time search. The paper's key conceptual departure is reframing tokenization as a content-adaptive, end-to-end differentiable decision (via Gumbel-Softmax gating and sparsity regularization) rather than a user-controlled truncation, and it introduces the novel problem of content-position misalignment that arises when sparse latents are fed into flow-matching diffusion models, proposing two remedies (joint position prediction and cascaded mask-prior conditioning) that have no direct precedent in prior tokenizer or diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 6.9× training speedup and 3.2× inference speedup over OmniTokenizer while simultaneously improving reconstruction and generation quality, with compression ratios that *increase* with resolution (134→253) rather than degrade. The emergent controllability—where token count naturally modulates motion complexity without retraining—suggests a useful design principle for future video generators. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer sequences (beyond 16–32 frames), whether it integrates cleanly with text-conditional generation, and whether the lightweight mask prior (8.3M params) can be further distilled or replaced by a closed-form heuristic. The limited video baselines (only ElasticTok for variable-length) leave room for stronger comparisons.
