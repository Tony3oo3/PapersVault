## Problem

Existing graph-based noisy label learning methods rely on dense KNN connectivity that amplifies local noise, or on GCN topologies with shortcut edges that incorrectly bridge unrelated clusters. Moreover, these approaches treat all nodes as equally reliable during message passing, allowing low-confidence boundary samples to propagate incorrect information. The paper addresses how to construct a sparse, globally consistent graph topology and regulate information flow based on node reliability to prevent blind error propagation.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for WebVision) paired with potentially corrupted labels, extracting features via a PreAct ResNet-18 backbone. Each mini-batch of B samples is augmented with K global class anchor nodes to form a (B+K)-node graph. The output is a refined pseudo-label distribution per sample (fusing individual, local, and global cues) used for weighted cross-entropy supervision, plus a final class prediction at test time.

## Method

MST-GCSN constructs a parameter-free Maximum Spanning Tree over a joint similarity matrix combining Gaussian feature distance and Jensen-Shannon divergence, with both sample features and class anchors as nodes, yielding a sparse backbone that eliminates redundant shortcut edges. A Confidence-Gated Propagation mechanism then modulates feature aggregation along MST edges using normalized entropy-based confidence scores, so uncertain nodes defer to reliable neighbors while confident nodes retain their own representations. Progressive Label Prediction fuses three signal sources—direct prediction from refined features, local neighborhood aggregation, and global anchor-guided estimates propagated along MST paths—with adaptive weights determined by each source's prediction certainty. Training is supervised by an entropy-reweighted cross-entropy loss (down-weighting uncertain pseudo-labels) plus a supervised contrastive loss that pulls together same-pseudo-label samples and repels different ones within the batch.

## Results

On the synthetic noisy benchmarks (CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100), MST-GCSN achieves an overall average accuracy of 82.6%, the highest among all compared methods, with particular strength at extreme noise levels (e.g., 56.3% on CIFAR-100 under 90% symmetric noise vs. 52.8% for the next-best DULC). On the real-world WebVision-50 dataset, the method attains 80.3% top-1 accuracy and 93.5% top-5 accuracy, outperforming all baselines including DULC (79.9% top-1) and MTaDCS (79.1% top-1). The method also achieves 77.2% top-1 on the ILSVRC12 clean test set, confirming that noise-robust training does not sacrifice clean-label performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MST-GCSN achieves the best overall average (82.6%) across CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, edging out DULC (82.2%) and PLReMix (81.5%). The most decisive advantage appears on CIFAR-100 under 90% symmetric noise, where MST-GCSN reaches 56.3% versus 52.8% for DULC and 49.4% for PLReMix, a gap of 3.5–6.9 points that highlights the benefit of global anchor guidance under extreme corruption. On CIFAR-10, DULC remains slightly ahead at 80% and 90% symmetric noise (95.0/93.5 vs. 94.7/93.2), but MST-GCSN wins on asymmetric noise (95.4% vs. 95.2%) and matches DULC at 20% noise (both 96.6%). On the real-world WebVision-50 benchmark, MST-GCSN leads all baselines in top-1 accuracy (80.3% vs. 79.9% for DULC, 79.2% for DPC, 79.1% for MTaDCS and TCL), while also achieving the best ILSVRC12 top-1 (77.2%) and top-5 (94.0%). Older methods such as DivideMix (73.9% overall), ELR (76.3%), and P-correction (60.0%) are substantially outperformed, confirming that the MST-based sparse topology with confidence-gated propagation provides a meaningful structural advantage over loss-based selection or transition-matrix estimation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both KNN-based feature-space methods (which rely on dense, unreliable pairwise similarity) and GCN-based approaches (which introduce shortcut edges and treat all nodes equally during propagation) by repurposing the Maximum Spanning Tree as a parameter-free sparse backbone for noisy label learning. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it synthesizes ideas from graph message passing, confidence-aware gating, and contrastive representation learning into a unified pipeline. The use of global class anchors as graph nodes and the Jensen-Shannon divergence–weighted edge construction are novel design choices not present in prior LNL graph methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free MST construction eliminates the need to tune the k-neighborhood hyperparameter, which is a practical advantage for practitioners deploying noisy-label-robust pipelines across diverse datasets. However, the per-batch MST computation introduces non-negligible overhead that may limit scalability to full ImageNet or real-time settings, and the fixed anchor-update frequency is acknowledged as suboptimal. Open questions include whether the confidence-gated propagation and progressive label prediction components transfer to other structured prediction tasks (e.g., segmentation with noisy masks) and whether adaptive anchor scheduling can close the remaining gap to the strongest baselines at extreme noise levels.
