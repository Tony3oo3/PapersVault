## Problem

Current video MLLMs process video as discrete 2D frame sequences, lacking an explicit 3D spatial anchor that would allow them to maintain object identity under ego-motion, recover absolute metric scale, and reason about physical distances. This structural limitation causes spatial conflation—distinct objects are merged, camera displacement is misestimated, and metric-aware reasoning (e.g., room sizing, absolute distance) fails systematically, even in large proprietary models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence V = {I_1, ..., I_T} of RGB frames (H×W×3) together with a natural-language query Q probing spatial configurations (metric distances, object scale, ego-motion direction, relative positioning). The video is ingested through two parallel encoders—Qwen3-VL produces 2D visual tokens at H/16 × W/16 resolution, while DA3 regresses dense metric depth maps, camera intrinsics K, and extrinsic trajectories E in a single forward pass. The output is a free-form textual answer A produced by the LLM, evaluated as either multiple-choice accuracy or mean relative accuracy for numerical estimation tasks.

## Method

VoxAnchor reifies 2D video into a metric-aware 3D voxel grid through three coupled mechanisms. First, a dual-stream encoder extracts semantic tokens (Qwen3-VL) and geometric features (DA3 depth, camera parameters, and hierarchical backbone features at layers 9/25/39) in parallel. Second, a Geo-Semantic Adapter fuses these streams via multi-scale gated skip connections (at 1/7, 1/4, and full resolution) and is contrastively pre-trained with an InfoNCE objective over instance-mask prototypes to enforce cross-frame identity consistency. Third, a Voxel-Aware Attention module unprojects both token types into world coordinates via p_world = E⁻¹(d·K⁻¹[u,v,1]ᵀ), discretizes them into 0.5 m voxels with spatial hashing, and then constrains multi-head cross-attention so that each visual-token query can only attend to GeoSem key/value pairs sharing the same voxel ID (all other positions masked to −∞). Training is two-stage: contrastive pre-training of the adapter on InsScene-15K, followed by LoRA fine-tuning (r=128, α=256) of the language model on VSI590K and VSTI-Bench while freezing both encoders and the adapter.

## Results

VoxAnchor-4B achieves state-of-the-art average scores of 66.8% on VSI-Bench and 66.3% on VSTI-Bench, outperforming all proprietary and open-source baselines. On VSI-Bench, it excels particularly on metric-aware numerical tasks: 72.5% on Object Size, 71.0% on Room Size, and 52.6% on Absolute Distance (vs. 5.3% for GPT-4o, a ~10× improvement). On VSTI-Bench, it leads in Camera Movement Direction (83.6%) and Object-Object Relative Position (89.5%), demonstrating strong cross-frame spatial consistency under ego-motion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoxAnchor-4B (66.8% avg.) surpasses the strongest spatial-enhanced baseline GS-Reasoner-7B (64.7%) by 2.1% and VLM-3R-7B (60.9%) by 5.9% on VSI-Bench, while using fewer parameters (4B vs. 7B). The advantage is most pronounced on Approach Order (78.0% vs. 52.3% for GS-Reasoner) and Room Size (71.0% vs. 65.7%), tasks requiring sustained metric awareness across frames. Against proprietary models, VoxAnchor leads GPT-4o (34.0%) by 32.8% and Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4%) by 21.4%, with a striking ~10× gap on Absolute Distance (52.6% vs. 5.3% for GPT-4o). On VSTI-Bench, VoxAnchor-4B (66.3%) outperforms VLM-3R-7B (58.8%) by 7.5%, with the largest gains in Camera Movement Direction (83.6% vs. 60.6%) and Camera Displacement (46.1% vs. 39.6%). Among open-source VLMs without spatial enhancement, the best is Qwen3-VL-4B (54.8%), which VoxAnchor exceeds by 12.0%, confirming that explicit voxel grounding—not merely model scale—drives the spatial reasoning gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoxAnchor departs from the dominant paradigm of injecting implicit 3D features (from VGGT, CUT3R, or similar) into MLLMs that still process video as discrete 2D frame sequences. Instead, it repurposes the voxel-based spatial reasoning concept from autonomous driving (Lift-Splat-Shoot) into the MLLM setting, explicitly unprojecting visual tokens into a metric-aware 3D grid and constraining attention to physically consistent voxel boundaries. The choice of Depth Anything 3 over VGGT/IGGT as the geometric backbone is motivated by DA3's explicit metric depth regression, addressing a scale-ambiguity limitation the authors identify in relative-geometry models. The Geo-Semantic Adapter draws on instance-level contrastive learning (InfoNCE with dynamic prototypes) to enforce cross-frame identity consistency, a technique borrowed from 3D instance segmentation but applied here as a pre-training regularizer for the adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit structural grounding (voxel-constrained attention) is more effective for spatial reasoning than simply augmenting MLLMs with 3D feature encoders, a finding that could reshape how spatial capabilities are integrated into future video MLLMs. The 4B-parameter model outperforming 7B and 72B baselines suggests that architectural inductive bias can substitute for scale in geometry-heavy tasks. Open questions include scalability to longer video sequences (voxel memory grows with scene extent), sensitivity to depth prediction errors in outdoor or textureless scenes, and whether the 0.5 m voxel size generalizes across environments. The approach also raises questions about real-time feasibility for embodied agents, since the unprojection and voxelization pipeline adds computational overhead beyond standard MLLM inference.
