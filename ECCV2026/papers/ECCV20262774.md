## Problem

Existing GUI grounding benchmarks evaluate isolated, single-step predictions in general-purpose environments, overlooking the sequential, workflow-driven reasoning required in real-world medical interfaces where tasks evolve across interdependent steps and dynamic interface states. No prior benchmark jointly addresses workflow-aware sequential grounding, strict error-propagation measurement, and safety-critical clinical software settings, leaving the reliability of multimodal models in high-stakes medical GUIs unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262774/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each grounding step receives a GUI screenshot (RGB frame from one of 10 medical imaging platforms), a natural language instruction, and the interaction history of prior steps, and the model must output click coordinates as a single point or top-K candidates in normalized [0,100] percentage space. Ground-truth annotations are normalized bounding boxes (x, y, w, h) in the same coordinate system, and a prediction is correct if any predicted point falls within the box. The evaluation pipeline additionally produces a task-level completion score under early termination and a per-step failure label from a six-class taxonomy (edge bias, small-target error, no prediction, near miss, far miss, toolbar confusion).

## Method

MedSPOT formulates clinical GUI grounding as a temporally dependent sequence of spatial decisions, where each task comprises 2–3 interdependent steps within a realistic medical workflow across 10 open-source imaging platforms (216 video tasks, 597 annotated keyframes). The core evaluation mechanism is a strict sequential protocol with early termination: the longest valid prefix of correct steps is computed, and evaluation halts at the first incorrect grounding, so full task completion requires all steps to succeed (probability scaling as p^T). A Weighted Prefix Score (WPS = Σ α^(i-1) · s_i, α = 0.8) is introduced to quantify partial sequential robustness with exponential decay emphasizing early correctness. The annotation pipeline extracts causally consistent decision frames from recorded GUI interaction videos, labels each with instruction, semantic target, normalized bounding box, and action type via Label Studio, and organizes them hierarchically (step → task → software → dataset) to preserve causal structure. A structured six-class failure taxonomy enables systematic diagnosis of spatial precision errors, small-target failures, toolbar confusion, and semantic misalignment across model families.

## Results

Under MedSPOT's strict early-termination sequential protocol, the best-performing model GUI-Actor achieves 43.5% Task Completion Accuracy (TCA), 65.0% Step-1 Accuracy (S1A), 49.6% Step Hit Rate (SHR), and a Weighted Prefix Score (WPS) of 1.2. General-purpose MLLMs (Llama 3.2 Vision, Qwen2-VL, DeepSeek-VL2, Gemma 3) all score 0% TCA with SHR below 6%, while closed-source GPT-5 reaches only 2.8% TCA. A human baseline (4 annotators, 45 tasks) achieves 82.22% TCA, revealing a substantial gap between current models and human performance in clinical GUI grounding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUI-specialized architectures dominate: GUI-Actor (43.5% TCA) outperforms Qwen3-VL-Instruct (35.0%) and UI-TARS 1.5-VL (30.8%) on task completion, while UI-TARS leads on SHR (66% vs. 49.6% and 46.6%), indicating it sustains more correct steps before failure but fails to complete full tasks as often. Aguvis (26.7% TCA) and OS-Atlas (26.6% TCA) form a mid-tier cluster, both roughly doubling the TCA of CogAgent (15.4%) and Qwen2.5-VL (12.6%). General-purpose MLLMs collapse entirely: Llama 3.2 Vision, Qwen2-VL, DeepSeek-VL2, Gemma 3, and Mistral-3 all achieve 0% TCA, with SHR below 6.2%, confirming that parameter scale alone (up to 27B) does not confer reliable spatial grounding in clinical interfaces. Closed-source models fare only marginally better—GPT-5 reaches 2.8% TCA and GPT-4o-mini 0%—with GPT-5's primary failure mode being toolbar confusion (97 instances) and far miss (67). The human baseline (82.22% TCA) exceeds the best model by nearly 40 percentage points, underscoring that workflow-aware sequential grounding in medical GUIs remains far from solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two largely separate research threads: GUI grounding benchmarks (ScreenSpot, Mind2Web, OmniACT, OSWorld-G) that evaluate isolated or loosely-structured multi-step tasks in general-purpose software, and medical AI benchmarks (MedSG-Bench, GMAI-MMBench, VividMed) that assess static image reasoning without interactive workflow context. MedSPOT departs from both by formulating grounding as a temporally dependent sequence of spatial decisions under strict early-termination scoring within clinical imaging software, a combination the authors explicitly identify as absent from prior literature. The structured six-class failure taxonomy and the Weighted Prefix Score metric are novel evaluation instruments not present in any prior GUI grounding benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MedSPOT exposes a critical blind spot: even the strongest GUI-specialized model (GUI-Actor) completes only 43.5% of clinical tasks versus 82.2% for humans, and several widely deployed general-purpose MLLMs achieve 0% task completion, revealing that parameter scale and generic multimodal pretraining do not transfer to pixel-precise, workflow-dependent interaction in dense medical interfaces. The early-termination protocol makes error propagation visible in a way that per-step accuracy metrics mask, which is essential for safety-critical deployment. Key limitations include the small scale (216 tasks), click-only action space (no drag, scroll, or text input), monolingual scope, and the fact that early termination may underestimate partial procedural competence—leaving open whether models can be trained to recover from mid-workflow errors or whether the bottleneck is fundamentally perceptual (small-target localization in dense toolbars).
