## Problem

Masked autoregressive (MAR) models recompute attention and feed-forward representations for every token at each decoding step, even though the vast majority of tokens remain semantically unchanged across steps. This temporal redundancy in KV projections creates substantial computational overhead that limits the scalability of MAR-based image generation, and no prior training-free approach has exploited KV caching within the MAR paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262539/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a class label, the method generates a 256×256 RGB image over 64 decoding steps using the standard MAR inference schedule. The framework is entirely training-free and architecture-agnostic: it wraps around existing MAR-B, MAR-L, or MAR-H models without modifying weights or structure, and the only tunable hyperparameter is the active-set budget (fixed at 64 tokens) and the decoder layer used for refresh selection (default Layer 2).

## Method

MARché introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, cache-aware attention partitions tokens at each step into an active set (generating tokens, newly cached tokens from the prior step, and refreshing tokens) and a cached set; active tokens receive fresh Q/K/V projections and pass through the FFN, while cached tokens contribute only their stored K/V as context, with attention scores merged via a safe online softmax to avoid concatenation overhead. Second, selective KV refresh identifies which cached tokens must be recomputed by aggregating attention scores from generating tokens across all heads in Layer 2 and selecting the top-K most contextually relevant tokens as refreshing tokens. Layers 1–2 always perform full attention to finalize the active set, while Layers 3 onward apply the split computation path; a periodic full KV refresh every three steps mitigates value drift. This design achieves up to 1.79× speedup over standard MAR with FID degradation of less than 0.4 across all model scales.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 (class-conditional, 64 decoding steps), MARché achieves up to 1.79× speedup over the original MAR across three model scales (B/L/H), reducing per-image latency from 0.336 s to 0.188 s for the largest model (MAR-H). Image quality is largely preserved: FID degrades only marginally (e.g., 2.35→2.56 for MAR-B, 1.62→2.02 for MAR-H) and Inception Score drops by roughly 10–17 points. Compared to the closest efficiency baseline LazyMAR, MARché delivers both a larger speedup (1.57–1.79× vs. 1.41–1.52×) and substantially better FID (2.02–2.56 vs. 4.00–5.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original MAR (the primary baseline), MARché wins on latency at every scale (1.57×–1.79× faster) while incurring only a small FID increase (≤0.40) and IS decrease (≤17 pts), confirming that the speedup comes at negligible quality cost. Against LazyMAR, MARché wins on both speed and quality: it is faster (e.g., 0.064 vs. 0.074 s/im at Base scale) and achieves dramatically lower FID (2.56 vs. 5.32 at Base; 2.02 vs. 4.00 at Huge), indicating that LazyMAR's hidden-feature reuse degrades generation more severely than MARché's KV-cache approach. Against MaskGIT, MARché is ~7× faster (0.064 vs. 0.440 s/im) with a much lower FID (2.56 vs. 6.18). Against DiT-XL/2, MARché-H is ~4× faster (0.188 vs. 0.787 s/im) with a slightly higher FID (2.02 vs. 2.27), trading a small quality gap for a large speed advantage. Against LlamaGen (XXL and 3B), MARché is roughly 5–6× faster and achieves lower FID (2.02–2.56 vs. 3.05–3.09), though LlamaGen uses substantially more parameters (1.4B–3.1B vs. 943M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MAR (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024), a masked autoregressive image generation model that combines bidirectional attention with fixed-order token prediction. Its core conceptual move is repurposing KV caching techniques well-established in LLM inference (H2O, KeyFormer, Quest) for the MAR setting, which was not originally designed to exploit such caching since all tokens' K/V projections are nominally updated at every step. It departs from LazyMAR, which reuses hidden features and adaptively selects tokens (potentially altering MAR's predefined generation order), and from ENAT, which requires training a dedicated attention module. The implementation leverages FlashAttention's online softmax formulation to split attention computation between active and cached token sets without explicit concatenation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARché offers a practical, training-free, architecture-agnostic speedup (up to 1.7×) for MAR-based image generation, making it immediately applicable to existing MAR checkpoints without retraining. However, evaluation is limited to ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation on a single GPU, and the periodic full refresh every three steps introduces a heuristic that may not generalize optimally to other resolutions, token counts, or generation schedules. The authors note the core ideas could extend to other generative settings including multi-modal generation, but no such experiments are provided. An open question is whether the fixed batch-size budget of 64 active tokens and the Layer-2 selection heuristic remain optimal as model scale or sequence length grows.
