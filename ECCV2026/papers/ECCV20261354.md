## Problem

Existing continual VQA methods rely on replay or parameter regularization but treat each task update as an opaque optimization step, providing no explicit control over how task-specific parameter changes accumulate and interact in weight space. In particular, they fail to distinguish synergistic from conflicting updates across layers, leading to brittle stability-plasticity trade-offs that suppress beneficial backward transfer and compositional generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261354/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes region-based visual features (36 object proposals per image via Faster R-CNN for VQAv2, or 16 clip-level motion features via inflated 3D ResNeXt-101 for NExT-QA) projected into a T5 encoder-decoder (12+12 blocks, d=768) alongside a natural-language question, and autoregressively generates a text answer sequence. A small replay buffer (5000 samples for VQAv2, 500 for NExT-QA) accompanies each new task, and evaluation uses both standard retention testing and novel skill-concept composition testing to probe compositional generalization.

## Method

SCoT reframes continual VQA as geometric consolidation in parameter space: each task is represented as an anchor-relative task vector (displacement from a fixed pretrained backbone θ₀), and a single cumulative consolidated offset m_i is maintained so the shared model is always θ₀ + m_i. For each layer, SCoT computes cosine alignment between the incoming and consolidated task vectors to derive a bounded consolidation weight (similarity-guided weighting), then applies a conditional projection that removes only the destructive component when alignment is negative (conflict-aware projection), and finally caps consolidation strength at empirically identified composition-sensitive layers at the encoder-decoder interface (depth-aware stabilization). The consolidated offset is updated via a layer-wise weighted interpolation, enabling aggressive merging of aligned tasks while surgically excising conflicting directions, which yields near-zero forgetting and rare positive backward transfer.

## Results

On VQAv2, SCoT achieves a Final Average Performance (AP) of 42.78% with near-zero Average Forgetting (AF) of 0.07%, surpassing even Joint Training (AP=41.32%)—the traditional upper bound for continual learning. On NExT-QA, SCoT reaches AP=32.28% with negative forgetting (AF=−1.90%), indicating positive backward transfer. In compositional testing, SCoT attains AP=42.11% (VQAv2) and AP=33.94% (NExT-QA), the best among all methods. Backward Transfer (BWT) scores of +5.64 (VQAv2) and +6.97 (NExT-QA) confirm rare positive backward transfer, where earlier tasks improve after subsequent training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCoT outperforms all 11 baselines on both VQAv2 and NExT-QA across standard and compositional settings. Against the strongest continual-VQA baselines, SCoT beats QUAD [37] by +3.53 AP on VQAv2 (42.78 vs 39.25) and +0.58 AP on NExT-QA (32.28 vs 31.70), while reducing AF from 4.91 to 0.07 and from 2.91 to −1.90 respectively. Among rehearsal-based methods, SCoT surpasses ER [9] by +5.79 AP on VQAv2 and +1.73 AP on NExT-QA, with substantially lower forgetting (AF 0.07 vs 5.99; −1.90 vs 4.91). Against model-merging baselines, SCoT exceeds TIES [66] by +1.24 AP on VQAv2 (42.78 vs 41.54) with lower AF (0.07 vs 1.03), and outperforms MagMax [36] by +4.30 AP (42.78 vs 38.48) while both achieve near-zero forgetting. On the compositional front, SCoT leads QUAD by +2.11 AP on VQAv2 (42.11 vs 40.00) and VQACL [78] by +6.71 AP, confirming that depth-aware stabilization preserves compositional structure better than activation-space or replay-based approaches. Notably, SCoT is the only method to surpass Joint Training on VQAv2 (42.78 vs 41.32), demonstrating that sequential geometric consolidation can exceed the traditional continual-learning upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCoT builds directly on the task-vector formulation of Ilharco et al. (2022), reframing anchor-relative weight displacements from an offline model-merging tool into the continual-learning state that is updated sequentially as tasks arrive. It departs from both classical continual-learning methods (EWC, MAS, ER, DER) that constrain updates in activation or loss space, and from recent magnitude-centric merging approaches (MagMax, DuET, TIES) that ignore directional geometry between successive task updates. The key conceptual shift is treating consolidation as a layer-wise geometric operation—measuring cosine alignment, conditionally projecting out destructive components, and modulating integration strength by similarity—rather than as a uniform interpolation or magnitude-based selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is architecture-agnostic (validated on T5, LLaVA-7B, and ViT-B) and requires no second-order statistics or architectural modifications, making it a practical drop-in for continual multimodal deployment. The finding that sequential geometric consolidation can exceed joint multi-task training challenges the long-standing assumption that the joint model is an upper bound for continual learning, though this claim is demonstrated primarily on VQAv2. Open questions include scalability to very long task streams (beyond the 5-task protocol used here), whether the empirically identified composition-sensitive layers transfer across training regimes, and whether the geometric assumptions (near-linear task-vector subspaces) hold for substantially larger or more heterogeneous task distributions.
