## Problem

Industrial defect detection in streaming settings faces two compounding risks—adversarial risk from samples near the decision boundary and outlier risk from domain shift—that confidence-based active learning cannot jointly address, as it systematically misses false negatives. Existing pool-based active learning methods for object detection require offline processing of all unlabeled samples, making them incompatible with real-time inspection constraints. This paper tackles the gap by proposing a streaming active learning framework that quantifies both risks simultaneously and fuses them through expert-calibrated fuzzy logic for adaptive sampling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 640×640 RGB industrial inspection images arriving in streaming batches (500 or 200 images per round) and produces, for each image, bounding boxes with one of four defect class labels (abrasion, dent, scratch, foreign object), an image-level global uncertainty score U_g ∈ [0,1], a defect-level uncertainty score U_d ∈ [0,1] (the maximum over all predicted boxes), and a discrete sampling probability (0, 0.1, 0.5, or 1.0) that determines whether the image is routed for expert annotation. The dual uncertainty scores are the key intermediate outputs that drive the fuzzy inference decision rather than being reported as final predictions.

## Method

FuDU integrates two complementary uncertainty quantification modules into an RT-DETR detector. The PGUQ module extracts a feature prototype via deformable convolution and global average pooling, then computes global uncertainty U_g as the sigmoid-normalized distance to the nearest of K learnable normal/defective prototypes (initialized by K-means and updated end-to-end with a contrastive alignment loss and a dispersion regularization to prevent collapse). The DeUE module computes per-box defect uncertainty U_d as a weighted combination of normalized classification entropy, localization entropy (over 16-bin boundary offsets), and (1 − confidence), aggregated by max over all predicted boxes. A Mamdani fuzzy inference system then fuzzifies U_g and U_d into four linguistic levels via trapezoidal membership functions, applies an expert-designed rule base following a safety-first principle (e.g., either uncertainty at Very High triggers Must-Sample at 100% probability), and uses max-membership defuzzification to map the output to a fixed sampling probability, guaranteeing that critical defects are never probabilistically diluted while suppressing annotation of confident normal samples.

## Results

On the Nuclear Fuel Rod Defect Dataset, FuDU achieves 96.0% mAP₅₀ at round 4 with 99.3% recall and 97.1% F1, using only 15.4% of samples for annotation and maintaining 12.3 FPS streaming throughput. The mean mAP₅₀ across four AL rounds is 92.2%, surpassing the best pool-based baseline (SPENet, 89.5%) by 2.7 points and the confidence-based baseline (Conf, 87.2%) by 5.0 points. On the public ELES photovoltaic dataset, FuDU reaches 90.4% mAP₅₀ with 97.1% recall at a 45.0% annotation ratio, again outperforming all AL baselines. FuDU's streaming capability (12.3 FPS) is 13× faster than QBC (0.9 FPS) and 3× faster than SPENet (4.4 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FuDU outperforms all three AL baselines on every metric. Against QBC [49] (committee-based, 3-model ensemble), FuDU gains +3.4 mean mAP₅₀ (92.2 vs. 88.8), +26.8 recall (99.3 vs. 72.5), and +23.8 F1 (97.1 vs. 73.3), while running 13.7× faster (12.3 vs. 0.9 FPS) and supporting streaming. Against SPENet [21] (false-negative prediction), FuDU achieves +2.7 mean mAP₅₀ (92.2 vs. 89.5), +11.8 recall (99.3 vs. 87.5), and +33.5 F1 (97.1 vs. 63.6), while requiring 10.5 percentage points fewer annotations (15.4% vs. 25.9%) and running 2.8× faster. Against PPAL [42] (uncertainty-diversity ranking), FuDU dominates with +7.8 mean mAP₅₀ (92.2 vs. 84.4) and +84.0 recall (99.3 vs. 15.3), indicating PPAL's pool-based design is poorly suited to the streaming, class-imbalanced industrial setting. On the ELES dataset, FuDU similarly leads QBC by +2.2 mean mAP₅₀ (85.7 vs. 83.5) and SPENet by +2.5 (85.7 vs. 83.2), with recall of 97.1% versus 72.1% and 59.2% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

FuDU repurposes the prototype-based anomaly detection paradigm (normal/defective feature prototypes with distance-based scoring) into a global uncertainty signal for a detection backbone, and pairs it with an entropy-based box-level uncertainty evaluator drawn from evidential and MC-dropout-style uncertainty quantification. The sampling decision layer departs from standard threshold- or score-based AL by introducing a Mamdani fuzzy inference system, which the authors claim is the first application of fuzzy control theory to streaming active learning for industrial defect detection. Conceptually, the work bridges three previously separate literatures—prototype-based anomaly detection, dual-entropy uncertainty in detection heads, and fuzzy-logic decision making—into a single streaming AL pipeline, while remaining architecturally compatible with RT-DETR, Swin, ViTDet, and several SOTA detection heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For high-stakes industrial inspection (nuclear fuel rods, photovoltaic cells), the ability to achieve 99.3% defect recall while annotating only ~15% of the stream is practically significant, and the cross-architecture and cross-dataset validation (Swin/ViTDet backbones, DEIM/MR-DETR/MI-DETR heads, ELES dataset) suggests the framework is not tied to a single detector. However, the fuzzy rule base is hand-designed and expert-calibrated per domain, raising open questions about how rules transfer to substantially different defect taxonomies or truly unbounded continuous streams. The streaming setting is simulated with fixed 500- or 200-image batches rather than a genuinely unbounded stream, and the single-dataset primary evaluation (nuclear fuel rods) limits confidence in the generality of the 99.3% recall figure.
