## Problem

Olfaction remains largely unexplored in multimodal AI because no large-scale paired visuo-olfactory dataset exists, and portable olfactory sensors have historically been confined to laboratory settings. The first large-scale real-world smell dataset (SmellNet) contains only unimodal olfactory measurements, leaving the tight biological coupling between visual appearance and odor identity unmodeled computationally.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261236/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image and a single 'sniff unit'—a temporal window (W=50 or 100 samples) of 6-channel gas sensor readings (NO₂, C₂H₅OH, VOC, CO, Alcohol, LPG) with first-order temporal differencing at lag p=25—mimicking biological sniffing episodes. It produces joint visuo-olfactory embeddings in a shared space, an H×W smell saliency map (the dense similarity map before max-pooling), and supports downstream outputs including ingredient class labels, cross-modal retrieval rankings, and pixel-level localization masks.

## Method

See & Sniff is a two-stream self-supervised framework pairing a frozen DINOv3-Small visual backbone with a ScentFormer olfactory transformer (4 layers, 8 heads), each followed by a lightweight aligner (LayerNorm + 1×1 conv for vision; 2-layer MLP with residual for smell) that projects features into a common embedding space. The key architectural choice is dense local alignment: rather than comparing global CLS tokens, the model computes a spatial similarity map M[h,w] = f̄_o · f_v[h,w] between the time-averaged sniff vector and each spatial location of the visual feature map, then max-pools M as the cross-modal similarity score within a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss. Training data (SmellNet-V) is constructed by synthetically pairing each sniff unit with semantically matched in-the-wild web images retrieved via LLM-generated descriptive queries and filtered through CLIP-based verification, exploiting the insight that odor identity is invariant to visual transformations within a semantic category. Pairs are re-sampled each epoch (cyclically shifting images within each ingredient) to prevent memorization of fixed synthetic pairings.

## Results

On smell classification (SmellNet-Test, p=25, W=50), See & Sniff achieves 57.71% accuracy and 56.68% F1, surpassing the smell-only SmellNet Transformer baseline (50.6% Acc, 49.5% F1) by approximately 7 percentage points. In cross-modal retrieval (SmellNet-V-Test), See & Sniff attains 56.14% R@1 for Smell→Vision and 63.20% R@1 for Vision→Smell, the best among all variants. For the novel smell localization task (SmellNet-V-Source), See & Sniff reaches 0.8362 mAP and 0.6456 mIoU, outperforming the cascaded SmellNet+SAM3 pipeline (0.3214 mIoU) and even the GT+SAM3 upper bound (0.5854 mIoU). In interactive localization (SmellNet-V-InteractiveSource), See & Sniff scores 0.35 IIoU versus 0.01 for DINOv3 attention maps and 0.00 for Global-CLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unimodal SmellNet baseline (Feng et al., ICLR 2026), See & Sniff improves smell classification accuracy from 50.6% to 57.71% (+7.1 pp) and Smell→Smell retrieval R@1 from 52.54% to 69.44% (+16.9 pp), demonstrating that visuo-olfactory training strengthens intrinsic olfactory representations. Against DINOv3 attention maps as a vision-only localization baseline, See & Sniff achieves 0.6456 mIoU versus 0.5157 mIoU on standard localization, and 0.35 IIoU versus 0.01 IIoU on interactive localization, confirming that generic visual objectness is insufficient for smell grounding. Against the SAM3-based cascaded pipeline (Carion et al., ICLR 2026), See & Sniff outperforms SmellNet+SAM3 by a large margin (0.6456 vs. 0.3214 mIoU) and even surpasses the GT+SAM3 upper bound (0.6456 vs. 0.5854 mIoU), showing that joint end-to-end visuo-olfactory learning is more effective than post-hoc category-conditioned segmentation. Within the paper's own ablation variants, dense local alignment with the aligner (See & Sniff) consistently outperforms global alignment (Ours-Global) across all three tasks, with the largest gap in localization (0.6456 vs. 0.5016 mIoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SmellNet/ScentFormer line of work (the first large-scale real-world olfactory dataset and transformer encoder) by adding a visual modality through synthetic pairing of smell-only recordings with semantically matched web images, leveraging the assumption that odor identity is invariant to visual transformations within a category. Architecturally, it departs from standard CLIP-style global contrastive alignment by introducing a dense local alignment objective (similarity maps between spatial visual features and pooled olfactory features), which enables spatial grounding in addition to holistic correspondence. A concurrent work (Ozguroglu et al.) also pursues visuo-olfactory learning but relies on naturally co-collected paired data and global alignment, whereas this paper's contribution is the synthetic-pairing strategy and the local-alignment mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first systematic efforts to bring olfaction into the multimodal representation-learning paradigm, and it demonstrates that visual supervision measurably strengthens olfactory embeddings (7% classification gain) without requiring costly co-collection of paired data. The introduction of smell localization as a spatial grounding task, along with the SmellNet-V-Source and SmellNet-V-InteractiveSource benchmarks, opens a new evaluation axis for machine olfaction beyond classification. Key limitations include the narrow scope (50 food/natural ingredients in non-degraded states), the reliance on synthetic pairing that may not capture all natural visuo-olfactory correspondences, and the small scale of the dataset (~5,400 training pairs). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to spoilage, fermentation, and compositional/mixture odors where visual and olfactory cues encode temporal or compositional changes rather than static identity.
