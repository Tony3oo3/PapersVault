## Problem

Learned ISP pipelines achieve state-of-the-art raw-to-sRGB quality but depend on large-scale, precisely aligned paired datasets that are prohibitively costly to acquire for every new camera sensor. Prior unpaired approaches based on standard GANs suffer from training instability and sub-optimal results, which the authors attribute to sensitivity to distributional outliers (misalignments, exposure errors) common in real-world target sRGB data. This paper is the first to apply Unbalanced Optimal Transport to the raw-to-sRGB translation task, specifically to address this outlier vulnerability.

## Input / Output

The method takes 4-channel Bayer raw sensor images (224×224 patches) and produces 3-channel sRGB images. In unpaired mode, a heuristic dimension projector maps the raw input to a crude 3-channel representation by averaging its green channels, serving as a coarse spatial/content anchor for the transport cost while leaving high-fidelity color and texture reconstruction to the target-distribution gradients. The framework is architecture-agnostic and training-only: the potential network and expert discriminators are discarded at inference, so the deployed ISP backbone incurs zero additional parameters or latency.

## Method

EGUOT trains a transport network T_θ via a hybrid loss combining (1) a semi-dual Unbalanced Optimal Transport objective with KL divergence (exponential convex conjugate), which dynamically assigns near-zero mass to outliers by exponentially decaying their gradient contributions, and (2) a Committee of Expert Discriminators—three specialized networks (Color via frozen ConvNeXt on L* channel, Structure via multi-scale PatchGAN, Frequency via lightweight CNN on 2D log-magnitude FFT)—that provide targeted perceptual gradients for color fidelity, local texture, and high-frequency realism. The transport cost is L2 between the output and the projected raw (unpaired) or L1 to ground truth (paired), anchoring the mapping and preventing geometric degeneracy. Training uses an alternating N:1 schedule (N=2) updating the potential and experts before each generator step, with a single fixed hyperparameter set across all datasets and architectures. The bounded Hinge loss on experts preserves UOT's theoretical robustness, since the exponentially formulated UOT cost dominates adversarial gradients on severe outliers.

## Results

On the ZRR dataset with a fixed Restormer backbone in unpaired mode, EGUOT achieves 21.69 PSNR, 0.84 SSIM, 9.93 ΔE, and 0.20 LPIPS, outperforming the best GAN-based baseline (RawFormer: 17.09 PSNR) by 4.6 dB and the best OT-based baseline (UOTM: 18.96 PSNR) by 2.7 dB. In paired mode, EGUOT consistently improves upon the original paired-trained results across all nine tested ISP backbones (e.g., Restormer: 21.51→21.73 PSNR; MW-ISP: 21.88→22.15 PSNR; cmKAN: 24.41→24.63 PSNR). The unpaired mode also rivals the original paired settings, with Restormer unpaired reaching 21.69 PSNR versus 21.51 for the original paired model. Training speed is competitive at 2.48 it/s with 0.64T FLOPs per iteration, comparable to pure OT baselines and far lighter than dual-generator GAN methods (~2.4T FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGUOT decisively outperforms all nine unpaired baselines on every metric in Table 2. Against GAN-based methods, the gap is largest versus CycleGAN (7.73 PSNR, a 14 dB deficit) and RawFormer (17.09 PSNR, 4.6 dB behind EGUOT); even the strongest GAN baseline LLUISP [6] trails by 2.6 dB in PSNR and 1.8 in ΔE. Among OT-based methods, UOTM [17] is the closest competitor at 18.96 PSNR, still 2.73 dB below EGUOT, confirming that the expert committee adds substantial perceptual fidelity beyond the UOT objective alone. NOT [52] and LOT [66] fall further behind (16.03 and 17.77 PSNR respectively). In paired mode (Table 1), EGUOT improves upon the original paired-trained results for all nine backbones, with gains of 0.2–0.45 dB PSNR and 0.3–0.9 ΔE reduction (e.g., AW-Net: 21.58→22.02 PSNR; LAN-ISP: 20.76→21.19 PSNR). The unpaired EGUOT results are competitive with the original paired settings, occasionally matching or exceeding them (e.g., Restormer unpaired 21.69 vs. paired 21.51 PSNR; MW-ISP unpaired 9.97 vs. paired 10.33 ΔE on ISPIW). On the Mobile AI dataset, MW-ISP trained unpaired with EGUOT even surpasses its original paired counterpart in both PSNR (24.44 vs. 24.31) and ΔE (6.09 vs. 6.27).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on UOTM (Choi et al., NeurIPS 2023), adopting its semi-dual formulation of unbalanced optimal transport with KL divergence and exponential convex conjugates as the core training objective. It departs from the standard GAN-based unpaired translation paradigm (CycleGAN, RawFormer, LLUISP) by replacing adversarial distribution-matching losses with UOT, motivated by the observation that classical OT and GAN losses are sensitive to distributional outliers common in real-world ISP datasets. The novel 'Committee of Expert Discriminators' repurposes the multi-discriminator pattern from GMA-style architectures but repositions them as bounded perceptual regularizers that complement (rather than replace) the UOT global mass-routing objective, guided by the perceptual orthogonality principle of the HVS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine industry bottleneck: every new camera sensor requires costly paired raw-to-sRGB data acquisition, and this paper shows that unpaired training can match paired performance across nine diverse architectures with zero added inference parameters or latency. The architecture-agnostic, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately applicable to any existing ISP backbone, potentially reducing development cycles for camera manufacturers. Open questions include whether the fixed hyperparameter set (γ=1.5, τ=10⁻³, λ=1.0, N=2) truly generalizes to substantially different sensor characteristics or extreme domain gaps, and whether the three-expert committee can be pruned without significant quality loss. The authors also note potential extension to other outlier-prone restoration tasks (low-light, denoising, HDR).
