## Problem

Standard flow matching models use an isotropic Gaussian as the latent distribution, which is unimodal and full-dimensional, creating a geometric mismatch with the multimodal, approximately low-dimensional data distribution. This mismatch forces the velocity field to simultaneously create multimodal structure and reduce dimensionality, resulting in a poorly conditioned optimization landscape that slows training and produces complex flow trajectories requiring many function evaluations at inference. The paper asks whether a structured latent distribution can provably improve both training convergence and inference efficiency.

## Input / Output

At inference, a sample is drawn from a learned Mixture of Gaussians (MoG) whose parameters (means, covariances, mixing weights) are estimated once from the training data via k-means and then held fixed. The sample is iteratively transformed by a learned velocity field (parameterized by the ADM U-Net architecture) integrated via a midpoint ODE solver, producing 32×32 RGB images. The number of function evaluations (NFE) is a tunable inference-time knob, and the MoG latent requires no architectural changes to the velocity network compared to the isotropic Gaussian baseline.

## Method

PFM replaces the isotropic Gaussian latent with a MoG whose modes are aligned to the data's cluster structure, and makes three key design choices: (1) an optimal-transport coupling (either global or class-conditional) between data and latent samples to promote straighter flow trajectories; (2) MoG parameter estimation via k-means rather than EM, exploiting k-means' lower per-iteration cost to afford more restarts within a fixed compute budget; and (3) eigenvalue thresholding of per-mode covariances (mapping σ → max(σ, τ)) to regularize near-degenerate directions that would otherwise make the velocity field ill-defined. Theoretically, the paper shows that for FM with affine velocity fields, gradient descent convergence is governed by the condition number of the interpolated covariance Σ_t, and proves that a MoG latent with well-regularized per-mode covariances and mode means close to the data yields a smaller condition number and lower initial loss than an isotropic Gaussian, accelerating training convergence.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, PFM achieves FID 5.71 at NFE=12, outperforming the OT-CFM baseline (FID 6.24) under identical training settings, and reaches the same quality as OT-CFM in 25–50% fewer training iterations. On ImageNet 32×32, PFM achieves FID 6.52 at NFE=12, substantially outperforming MFM-BatchOT (FID 7.18) and MFM-Stable (FID 7.84). The improvements are most pronounced at low NFE: at NFE=4, PFM achieves FID 8.52 on CIFAR-10 versus 9.20 for OT-CFM, and FID 9.90 on ImageNet versus 17.28 for MFM-BatchOT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is PFM vs. OT-CFM [40], where both methods are trained with identical settings (ADM architecture, pixel space, 100K iterations) and differ only in the latent distribution (learned MoG vs. isotropic Gaussian). PFM wins on all NFE budgets on CIFAR-10: FID 8.52 vs. 9.20 at NFE=4, 6.07 vs. 7.02 at NFE=8, and 5.71 vs. 6.24 at NFE=12. On ImageNet 32×32, PFM outperforms MFM-BatchOT [33] by a large margin (FID 9.90 vs. 17.28 at NFE=4; 6.52 vs. 7.18 at NFE=12) and MFM-Stable [33] by an even wider gap. PFM also substantially outperforms MTC [24] on CIFAR-10 (FID 8.52 at NFE=4 vs. 18.74 at NFE=5). Against higher-NFE methods like I-CFM (FID 4.46 at NFE=100) and FM [26] (FID 6.35 at NFE=142), PFM achieves competitive quality with far fewer function evaluations, confirming that the MoG latent produces straighter, simpler flow trajectories that are easier to integrate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional flow matching (CFM) framework of Lipman et al. and the mini-batch optimal transport coupling of Tong et al. and Pooladian et al., but departs from the dominant line of work on trajectory straightening, velocity field design, and ODE solver improvements by instead redesigning the latent distribution. Where prior methods keep the isotropic Gaussian source and modify the transport path or the learned velocity, PFM replaces the source with a Mixture of Gaussians whose modes are estimated via k-means from the data, coupled to data via class-conditional OT. The paper also explicitly addresses and explains away the negative empirical result of Lee et al. (2026), attributing it to poor EM-based MoG estimation and the absence of eigenvalue regularization on per-mode covariances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PFM offers a simple, drop-in modification to any FM pipeline that yields 25–50% fewer training iterations and lower FID at matched NFE, with a clean theoretical account (Theorems 1–2) showing why mode-aligned, well-conditioned latent covariances accelerate gradient descent convergence. The eigenvalue-regularization trade-off identified in Theorem 1—that driving the latent-data covariance mismatch to zero is not always optimal because it inherits vanishing data eigenvalues—provides a principled justification for the thresholding hyperparameter. Limitations are notable: experiments are restricted to unconditional generation at 32×32 resolution, the theory assumes affine velocity fields and (for Theorem 2) a symmetric two-mode MoG, and extending to class-conditional or higher-resolution settings, more expressive latent families, and adaptive component selection remain open.
