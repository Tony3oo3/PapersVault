## Problem

Live Photos on modern smartphones produce cover images and video frames through fundamentally different imaging pipelines, creating an intrinsic quality gap in resolution, color fidelity, and dynamic range. When users reselect a video frame to replace an imperfect cover, the chosen frame is severely degraded, and no prior dataset or method exists to jointly restore spatial detail and correct color appearance under this cross-quality condition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260917/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 9-frame video burst (4 frames before + 1 target + 4 frames after the selected frame) at the device's native video resolution, plus a high-quality cover reference image drawn from a *different* Live Photo captured in a visually similar scene (cross-instance guidance, not the same Live Photo's own cover). Input frames are rescaled to half the guidance image resolution and further downsampled 2× via PixelUnshuffle before processing; the network outputs a 4× super-resolved image at the cover's resolution (e.g., 4096×3072 for OPPO, 4032×3024 for iPhone). Because the guidance image is required for patch matching, inference must be performed on full-resolution images rather than in a patch-based manner.

## Method

LPENet is a unified one-stage network that jointly performs multi-frame fusion, guided color enhancement, and super-resolution. Nine input frames are fused via a Temporal-Spatial Attention module (adapted from EDVR) that implicitly aligns and aggregates temporal context without explicit optical flow, while the reference image is encoded through convolutions and two PixelUnshuffle operations into a matching-resolution feature map. The Guided Enhancement module operates in two stages: (1) Color Modulation uses low-resolution cross-attention to extract a global guidance feature, which is then injected into NAFBlock-based enhancement layers via FiLM modulation to correct tone and color; (2) Texture Transfer performs patch-level top-3 matching (normalized cross-correlation) between the color-corrected feature and the reference, followed by Similar Window Cross Attention with ℓ₂-normalized queries and a learnable gated residual fusion to inject high-frequency details without over-relying on the reference. The final features are fused through Swin Transformer Layers and decoded by a 4× upsampling head. Training uses a two-stage loss: an intermediate color loss (L2 + SSIM + VGG) on the half-resolution enhanced image plus an L1 reconstruction loss on the full-resolution output, optimized with Muon for matrix weights and Adam for scalar parameters over 200K iterations.

## Results

On the Live2K dataset, LPENet (5.06M params) achieves 26.97 dB PSNR / 0.8497 SSIM / 0.2268 LPIPS / 4.05 ΔE on the iPhone subset and 27.78 dB PSNR / 0.8657 SSIM / 0.2005 LPIPS / 3.95 ΔE on the OPPO subset, outperforming all SISR, BurstSR, and RefSR baselines on every metric. The method also achieves the fastest inference speed (0.865 s per 4K image) with a moderate 21.36 GB memory footprint, compared to 1.0–34.9 s for other methods. Ablations confirm that multi-frame fusion (+0.29 dB), color supervision loss (+0.06 dB), and reference-guided enhancement (+0.30 dB) each contribute independently to the final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPENet outperforms all seven available baselines across both device subsets and all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, ΔE). Against the strongest SISR baseline SwinIR, it gains +0.39 dB PSNR (iPhone) and +0.40 dB (OPPO) while also improving color fidelity (ΔE 4.05 vs 4.21). Among BurstSR methods, the gap is larger: LPENet surpasses the best burst method BurstM by +0.85 dB (iPhone) and +1.47 dB (OPPO), indicating that temporal fusion alone is insufficient without cross-quality guidance. Against reference-based SR methods, LPENet leads MRefSR (the strongest RefSR baseline) by +0.63 dB on iPhone and +0.81 dB on OPPO, and beats DATSR by +0.63 dB and +1.14 dB respectively. Notably, LPENet achieves these gains with only 5.06M parameters—fewer than SwinIR (8.05M) and far fewer than BurstM (13.4M) or RebaIR (72.1M)—and runs in 0.865 s per 4K image, the fastest among all compared methods (next fastest: BIPNet at 1.036 s). Two baselines (QMambaBSR and RebaIR) had no publicly available code and thus no quantitative results reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—burst/multi-frame super-resolution (BIPNet, Burstormer, BurstM), reference-based super-resolution (DATSR, MRefSR, ERVSR), and learned image enhancement (NAFBlock, bilateral grids)—into a single task formulation (LPRE) that had not been explicitly defined before. Architecturally, it borrows the Temporal-Spatial Attention module from EDVR for implicit multi-frame alignment, NAFBlock as the enhancement backbone, FiLM conditioning for global color guidance, and Swin Transformer layers for final refinement, but the key departure is operating under cross-quality conditions where the reference image comes from a fundamentally different imaging pipeline (multi-frame RAW fusion vs. single-frame lightweight processing) rather than a same-sensor, same-exposure reference. The SIFT-based data construction and the use of a cross-instance guidance image (from a different Live Photo in the same scene group) are practical design choices that distinguish the dataset from prior burst or reference SR benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Live Photos are now a default capture mode on major smartphone platforms, and the quality gap between the cover and video frames is a real, user-facing problem that existing ISP pipelines cannot resolve due to latency constraints. By providing the first dedicated dataset (Live2K) and benchmark, this work gives the community a concrete target for future research on cross-quality image restoration. Key open questions include generalization to low-light/night scenes (explicitly acknowledged as a limitation), true cross-device invariance (the paper shows only modest fine-tuning transfer), and whether the non-integer, device-specific scale factors can be handled more elegantly. The practical observation that nearly all prior SR methods cannot process 4K images within standard GPU memory also highlights a broader scalability gap in the field.
