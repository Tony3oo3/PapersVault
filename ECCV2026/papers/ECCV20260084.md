## Problem

Existing 3D human avatar reconstruction methods (NeRF- and 3DGS-based) assume unoccluded monocular input with a fully visible subject, often in a canonical pose. This excludes the vast majority of real-world footage—such as YouTube videos of everyday activities—where the body is routinely occluded by furniture, objects, or other people, and large regions may never be observed. The paper addresses how to reconstruct a complete, animatable 3D Gaussian avatar from such heavily occluded in-the-wild monocular video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260084/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video (e.g., a YouTube clip) in which the subject is partially occluded, with no multi-view rig or controlled capture. SMPL parameters are estimated per-frame via NLF and visibility masks via SAM 3; no depth, multi-view, or pose annotations are required. The output is a complete, animatable 3D Gaussian avatar with pose-dependent Gaussian maps that can be driven by arbitrary SMPL poses, rendered from novel viewpoints in real time, and composited into independently reconstructed 3DGS scenes captured from casual cell-phone video.

## Method

AHOY proceeds in four stages. First, a coarse canonical-pose avatar is built by mapping observed textures via DensePose UV correspondences, inpainting missing regions with FLUX, generating multi-view canonical images with a diffusion model, and training a StyleUNet over canonical Gaussian maps. Second, an identity-specific video diffusion model (Wan 2.2 finetuned with LoRA) is used to hallucinate dense supervision: the coarse avatar is rendered in structured motion sequences (full 360° turns paired with target poses, or multi-angle static poses), and these renderings are refined via RF-Inversion through the finetuned latent space, producing identity-consistent videos that cover previously unobserved body surfaces. Third, a full avatar with pose-dependent Gaussian maps is trained on the hallucinated videos, using a map-pose/LBS-pose decoupling (the map pose is shared across frames of a sequence while the LBS pose is optimized per-frame) to absorb multi-view inconsistencies inherent in independently sampled diffusion outputs, and a head/body split that supervises the head via a separate FLAME-based multi-view face diffusion path to preserve facial identity. Fourth, at inference the avatar is animated with PCA-projected novel poses and composited into 3DGS scenes.

## Results

On BEHAVE novel-view evaluation, AHOY achieves 24.12 PSNR / 0.905 SSIM / 0.090 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline (SyncHuman) by 4.78 dB in PSNR. On YouTube static reconstruction under occlusion, AHOY reaches 22.01 PSNR / 0.109 LPIPS versus 19.82 / 0.189 for the strongest baseline (SyncHuman). For animation to novel poses on YouTube, AHOY scores 22.81 PSNR / 0.107 LPIPS, exceeding LHM (19.03 / 0.181) and IDOL (16.52 / 0.231) by 3.8 and 6.3 dB respectively. On BEHAVE novel-pose, AHOY attains 22.81 PSNR / 0.110 LPIPS, a 5.9 dB margin over LHM (16.93 / 0.238).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AHOY outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest static-reconstruction baselines (PSHuman and SyncHuman), AHOY gains 2.2–4.8 dB PSNR on YouTube static reconstruction and 4.8–6.7 dB on BEHAVE novel-view, while also being the only method that produces animatable avatars (PSHuman and SyncHuman cannot re-pose). Against the animatable baselines LHM and IDOL, AHOY leads by 2.9–5.7 dB PSNR on YouTube static (occluded input) and by 3.8–6.3 dB on animation to novel poses. The gap is largest under occluded input, where baselines fail to produce complete reconstructions while AHOY's hallucination pipeline fills in unobserved regions. Even when baselines are given the unoccluded canonical-pose image generated by AHOY's own pipeline (a best-case input), AHOY still outperforms them, confirming that the advantage comes from the full pipeline rather than input quality alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3DGS-based avatar reconstruction (Animatable Gaussians, GASPACHO, HUGS) from the unoccluded, fully-visible setting to heavily occluded in-the-wild video, a regime where all prior methods fail. The key conceptual departure is 'hallucination-as-supervision': rather than using video diffusion models for animation or novel-view rendering (as in prior human-centric diffusion work), the authors use an identity-finetuned diffusion model (via LoRA + RF-Inversion) to generate dense, multi-view supervision for 3D fitting. The two-stage canonical-to-pose-dependent architecture bridges the gap between sparse observations and the multi-view coverage needed for pose-dependent Gaussian maps, and the map-pose/LBS-pose decoupling is a novel mechanism for absorbing geometric inconsistencies inherent in independently-sampled diffusion outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making occlusion a solvable problem rather than an exclusion criterion, AHOY unlocks billions of YouTube videos as a source of diverse, animatable 3D human assets without controlled capture or subject cooperation. The hallucination-as-supervision paradigm—using a generative model to fill in unobserved geometry and then fitting an explicit 3D representation to those fills—is potentially transferable to other reconstruction tasks beyond humans. Key limitations include the pipeline's speed (multiple optimization and diffusion-inference stages), the quality ceiling imposed by the underlying video diffusion model, and the risk of hallucinating plausible but incorrect details for never-observed regions. Open questions include whether geometric consistency constraints can be injected into the hallucination stage and whether the approach scales to stronger future diffusion backbones.
