## Problem

Existing RL methods for Vision-Language-Action models rely on external environmental feedback (e.g., simulator success flags or engineered reward models) to guide policy updates, confining self-improvement to predefined scenarios with available supervision. This paper asks whether VLA models possess sufficient internal evaluative capability—specifically, whether generation confidence correlates with physical execution success—to enable fully autonomous, reward-free policy optimization at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260164/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB visual observations paired with natural language instructions and outputs executable robot action sequences, supporting both discrete token-based actions (OpenVLA-OFT) and continuous end-effector poses via flow matching (π₀, π₀.5). A distinguishing feature is that the reward signal is computed entirely from the model's own internal signals: length-normalized generation confidence (mean action log-probability or denoising log-likelihood) and DTW-based trajectory similarity to self-elected expert references, requiring no environment-provided success labels or external reward models.

## Method

T²VLA is an architecture-agnostic test-time RL framework built on the empirical finding that higher generation confidence in VLA rollouts strongly correlates with task success. It employs a Confidence-Driven Dual Expert Bootstrapping mechanism: a task-conditioned Local Pseudo-Expert (highest-confidence trajectory in the current batch) provides aggressive exploration targets, while a priority-based Global Expert Pool (top-K=5 historical experts) ensures training stability. A DTW-based Hybrid Similarity Reward aligns each rollout to both experts with an adaptive interpolation weight derived from relative confidence scores, decoupling spatial geometry from temporal pacing. A KL divergence penalty against the initial SFT policy prevents over-optimization, and the final proxy reward drives policy updates via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with clipped surrogate objectives, completing a self-bootstrapping loop that requires no external reward signal.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, T²VLA (OpenVLA-OFT) achieves 97.2% average success rate, a +6.2% absolute gain over its SFT baseline (91.0%), surpassing the SFT-only UniVLA (95.2%) and the test-time training method EVOLVE-VLA (95.8%) while approaching the oracle-reward SimpleVLA-RL (99.1%). For continuous-action flow-matching models, T²VLA yields a +24.2% absolute improvement on π₀ (57.7% → 81.9%) and +8.0% on π₀.5 (77.1% → 85.1%). On the RoboTwin 2.0 bimanual benchmark, T²VLA (OpenVLA-OFT) achieves 59.1% average success rate, a +21.3% gain over the SFT baseline (37.8%), with the largest improvement on short-horizon tasks like Beat Hammer (+39.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T²VLA (OpenVLA-OFT) at 97.2% average success rate outperforms all SFT-only baselines (UniVLA 95.2%, OpenVLA 76.5%, Octo 75.1%) and the test-time training method EVOLVE-VLA (95.8%), which relies on a separate learned critic. It also surpasses the externally-supervised VLA-RL (89.6%) by 7.6 points, despite using no environmental reward signal. The closest comparison is SimpleVLA-RL (99.1%), which has access to true binary success flags from the simulator; T²VLA narrows this gap to just 1.9 points without any external supervision. For continuous-action models, T²VLA delivers the largest relative gains: +24.2% on π₀ and +8.0% on π₀.5, confirming architecture-agnostic applicability. On RoboTwin 2.0, T²VLA (OpenVLA-OFT) improves the SFT baseline by +21.3% on average, with particularly strong gains on short-horizon tasks (Beat Hammer: 28.1% → 68.0%, +39.9%) and long-horizon tasks (Stack Bowls: 40.6% → 59.0%, +18.4%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time RL paradigm validated in VLMs (e.g., TTRL for math/coding) to the VLA domain, where the absence of a single verifiable answer makes self-verification non-trivial. It departs from the dominant VLA-RL line (SimpleVLA-RL, VLA-RL, EVOLVE-VLA) that requires explicit environmental success signals or learned critics, instead repurposing the model's own generation confidence as an intrinsic reward proxy. The policy optimization backbone is GRPO from DeepSeekMath, and the trajectory alignment leverages DTW from time-series analysis, but the core contribution is the confidence-driven dual-expert bootstrapping mechanism that converts internal signals into a self-bootstrapping reward without any external supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for external reward signals, T²VLA opens a path toward truly autonomous VLA self-improvement in settings where success criteria are hard to specify or where simulator feedback is unavailable. The honest negative result on 1-shot Long-horizon tasks reveals a critical 'prior threshold'—self-bootstrapping fails when the initial policy is too weak to produce any high-confidence successful rollouts—highlighting that this approach complements rather than replaces strong SFT initialization. The limited gain from world-model-generated interactions (+2.1%) suggests the method is still tightly coupled to physically grounded rollouts. Open questions include whether the confidence-success correlation holds for genuinely novel tasks outside the training distribution and whether the dual-expert mechanism scales to multi-task or lifelong learning settings.
