## Problem

Prevailing multi-view point cloud registration pipelines rely on exhaustive pairwise alignment followed by pose graph optimization, incurring quadratic complexity and limited global context that degrades performance under low overlap. Additionally, no unified cross-domain benchmark exists to evaluate multi-view registration across diverse scene scales, sensor modalities, and overlap conditions in a zero-shot setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261844/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts N ≥ 2 unposed point clouds (LiDAR or depth-camera scans, single-frame or SLAM-accumulated submaps) with no initial pose guess, requiring only that they form a connected overlap graph. Internally, each view is reduced to keypoints via voxel downsampling and farthest-point sampling, annotated with 32-dim MiniSpinNet local descriptors, and canonicalized into a shared similarity-invariant frame. The output is a single generated registered point cloud in a canonical frame, from which per-view SE(3) rigid transformations are recovered via the Kabsch algorithm and lifted back to the original dense point clouds at metric scale.

## Method

RAP formulates multi-view registration as conditional flow matching in 3D Euclidean space: a 73M-parameter diffusion transformer with 10 alternating-attention blocks (per-view self-attention interleaved with global cross-view attention) learns a velocity field that transports Gaussian noise to the aggregated registered point cloud, conditioned on Fourier-encoded keypoint coordinates and MiniSpinNet descriptors. A key architectural choice is omitting view-index conditioning, making the model view-count-agnostic and generalizable to more views at test time than seen in training. At inference, a rigidity-enforcing Euler integration projects intermediate predictions onto per-view SE(3) orbits at each of 10 steps, constraining the flow trajectory to rigid motions and preventing drift from the training distribution. The model is trained on over 100k multi-view instances curated from 17 heterogeneous datasets spanning object, indoor, and outdoor scales, using a dynamic batching strategy to handle variable view counts and token counts across samples.

## Results

On the proposed cross-domain multi-view registration benchmark (zero-shot, 5 scene categories), RAP achieves 85.9% edge success rate and 77.3% graph success rate, improving over the strongest baseline BUFFER-X* (57.7% / 36.5%) by +28.2 and +40.8 percentage points respectively. RAP also attains the best average pairwise registration success rate of 94.92% across six standard benchmarks (ModelNet, 3DMatch, 3DLoMatch, NSS, ETH, KITTI), surpassing BUFFER-X (92.46%). Inference runs in 8.9 s per sample on a single A5000 GPU, roughly 2× faster than KISS-Matcher+PGO (18.4 s) and 4× faster than BUFFER-X*+PGO (36.0 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAP outperforms all 10 baselines on every scenario category of the cross-domain multi-view benchmark. The closest competitor, BUFFER-X* (retrained on the same 17-dataset mixture), reaches 57.7% edge / 36.5% graph success, trailing RAP by 28.2 and 40.8 points respectively; the original BUFFER-X (trained only on 3DMatch) scores 50.4% / 32.4%. KISS-Matcher+PGO is the strongest conventional method at 46.2% / 29.2%, benefiting from scalable hand-crafted features but still far behind RAP, especially on Indoor (30.5% vs. 75.1%) and Object (38.1% vs. 86.3%) scenes. Learning-based pairwise methods (Predator, BUFFER, PARENet) and the dedicated multi-view method SGHR all fail to generalize across domains, scoring below 25% edge success on the full benchmark. RPF, the closest prior flow-matching method, collapses to 1.0% edge success because it operates on raw points without local descriptors and was designed for object-scale scenes. On pairwise benchmarks (Table 1), RAP leads with 94.92% average success, edging out BUFFER-X (92.46%) and BUFFER (88.12%), while also achieving the best single-benchmark scores on 3DMatch (95.90%), NSS (96.27%), ETH (99.44%), and KITTI (100.0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

RAP extends the generative flow-matching approach to registration pioneered by RPF [80] (object-scale, pairwise) to large-scale, cross-domain multi-view registration, adding local descriptors (MiniSpinNet), canonicalization for scale invariance, and a rigidity-enforcing sampler. Conceptually, it follows the feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm (VGGT, DUSt3R, MASt3R) of replacing iterative two-stage pipelines (pairwise matching + pose graph optimization) with a single holistic model, but applies it to point clouds rather than images. The key departure from prior multi-view methods is that RAP generates the registered point cloud directly in Euclidean space rather than estimating pairwise SE(3) transformations, making it view-count-agnostic and avoiding quadratic pairwise complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a generative formulation with weaker geometric inductive biases (no RANSAC, no explicit correspondences) underperforms on a single dataset but dramatically outperforms all baselines when trained on 17 diverse datasets reveals that data diversity is the critical unlock for cross-domain registration generalization — a non-obvious insight for the field. The proposed cross-domain benchmark (object, indoor, outdoor, TLS, map) fills a genuine evaluation gap, as prior benchmarks are domain-specific and nearly saturated. Open questions include handling non-rigid deformations, detecting and rejecting out-of-scene inputs, and scaling to streaming/long-sequence registration, all of which the authors identify as future work.
