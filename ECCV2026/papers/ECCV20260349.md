## Problem

Existing interleaved-generation paradigms treat image generation and retrieval augmentation as mutually exclusive paths, failing to unify factuality with creativity in a single response. Real-world visual-critical queries often require both referencing provided context images and synthesizing query-specific visuals, a practical requirement that no prior benchmark or evaluation framework addresses. The paper identifies Agentic Tool Planning—where an MLLM autonomously decides when, where, and which tools to invoke—as the missing paradigm and introduces a dedicated benchmark to study it.

## Input / Output

The model receives a concatenated input of an interleaved-generation prompt, a visual-critical query (either text-only QA or image+text VQA), and a source-agnostic document set D of (text, images) tuples. The output is an ordered sequence of natural-language tokens interleaved with structured tool-call instructions in the form <tool>{"tool_name": ..., "description": ..., "params": ...}</tool>, where tool_name is one of five modules (Reference, Diffusion, Search, Code, Edit). Critically, the output contains tool-planning tags rather than rendered images, decoupling planning evaluation from end-to-end execution and backend variability.

## Method

ATP-Bench comprises 7,702 QA pairs (1,592 VQA) across 8 categories and 25 visual-critical intents, constructed via a two-stage Gemini 2.5 Pro query pipeline followed by 10-annotator verification, and a three-stage ground-truth procedure (textual response generation → MLLM-driven image insertion with strict tool-boundary rules → 15-annotator fine-grained review and refinement). For evaluation, the paper proposes MAM (Multi-Agent MLLM-as-a-Judge), a three-agent system: a Precision Inspector that scores each tool call on a 0–2 scale for necessity, boundary compliance, placement, and parameter correctness; a Recall Inspector that flags missed tool-use opportunities where text-only responses should have invoked a tool; and a Chief Judge that synthesizes both reports into a 0–100 holistic score anchored to five performance tiers. MAM operates without ground-truth references or end-to-end execution, isolating intrinsic planning ability from external API constraints. The benchmark is further validated through human agreement studies (84–88% agreement) and Spearman correlations (ρ=0.89 for FS, ρ=0.83 for MI) against human end-to-end evaluation.

## Results

On ATP-Bench (7,702 QA pairs across 8 categories), Gemini 3 Pro achieves the highest average Final Score of 79.88, Success Rate of 81.77%, and lowest Missed Images count of 0.49, leading across all three primary metrics. Tier-2 models (Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude Sonnet 4, Grok-4.1, GPT-5) cluster around 67–69 Final Score but show distinct tool-use trade-offs, while open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL3.5-14B, LLaMA-3.2-11B) drop sharply to 29–53 Final Score. Academic and Encyclopedia are the easiest categories (highest scores, fewest missed images), whereas Travel and Renovation are the hardest, with Travel being MI-dominated and Renovation SR-dominated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 3 Pro outperforms all other models on average Final Score (79.88 vs. 69.34 for the next-best, Claude Sonnet 4.5), Success Rate (81.77% vs. 75.77%), and Missed Images (0.49 vs. 0.99), with the largest gaps in tool-intensive categories like Fashion (+18.04 over Claude Sonnet 4.5), Renovation (+23.31), and Travel (+18.51). Among tier-2 models, Claude Sonnet 4.5 edges out Claude Sonnet 4 on Success Rate (75.77% vs. 73.86%) but incurs more missed images (1.19 vs. 0.99), reflecting a more conservative tool-use strategy; GPT-5 excels in Academic (93.59) but degrades in Renovation (48.63) and Travel (49.63). GPT-4o and Qwen3-VL-Plus form a mid-tier (~61–62 FS) with notably weaker visual grounding, showing the largest missed-image counts in Travel (3.03 and 3.25) and Renovation (1.80 and 1.98). Open-source models underperform substantially: Qwen2.5-VL-72B (53.22 FS) handles knowledge-intensive categories well but collapses in tool-heavy domains, InternVL3.5-14B (49.09 FS) has an extremely low Success Rate (10.12%) due to formatting failures, and LLaMA-3.2-11B (28.97 FS) shows compounded weaknesses in both visual gap detection and valid tool execution. Ground-truth-based F1-scores confirm the MAM ranking with a Spearman correlation of ρ=0.879 (p<0.001), and human evaluation on end-to-end execution corroborates the MAM scores (ρ=0.8909 for FS, p<0.001).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by proposing Agentic Tool Planning as a unifying paradigm for interleaved generation, dissolving the boundary between the two dominant prior approaches: diffusion-based image generation (OpenLEAF, OpenING, InterleavedBench) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG-IGBench, MRAMG-Bench). While tool-augmented MLLMs (ViperGPT, MM-ReAct, LLaVA-Plus, MLLM-Tool) have demonstrated that models can invoke external tools, no prior benchmark systematically evaluates the *planning* of which tools to call, when, and where within an interleaved response. The MAM evaluation framework extends the MLLM-as-a-judge paradigm (Chen et al., 2024) by decomposing judgment into specialized Precision, Recall, and Chief Judge agents that operate without ground-truth references or end-to-end execution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ATP-Bench (7,702 QA pairs, 8 categories, 25 intents) is the first benchmark to jointly evaluate hybrid image sourcing (reference + generation) and dual query types (QA + VQA) under expert-annotated visual-critical intents, filling a clear gap in the interleaved-generation evaluation landscape. The finding that even the best model (Gemini 3 Pro) achieves only ~80% success rate, and that failure modes differ fundamentally by category (Travel = missed opportunities, Renovation = execution errors), provides actionable diagnostic guidance for model developers. Key limitations include restriction to text-image modality, a fixed five-tool toolkit, and reliance on MLLM-as-a-judge rather than alternative pipelines (e.g., captioning for LLM-as-a-judge), leaving open questions about scalability to richer modalities and broader agentic capabilities.
