## Problem

Unseen-object 6D pose estimation pipelines that use sparse candidate poses for speed leave large residual angular deviations (up to 90°) to the refinement stage, where conventional small-angle refiners fail. Additionally, wide-angle training on rotationally symmetric objects introduces supervision ambiguity because symmetry-equivalent poses produce nearly identical observations yet receive inconsistent rotation targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262124/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

WAPR consumes a *pair* of 160×160 RGB-D crops with segmentation masks: the real sensor observation (or PBR render during training) and a render generated under the current candidate pose. The network outputs a 3D rotation-vector delta and a 3D translation offset, which are applied to the candidate pose and the scene is re-rendered for the next iteration. Final 6D pose is selected from refined candidates via FoundationPose's ScoreNet, with 12 candidates (tetrahedron directions × 3 in-plane rotations) in the fast setting and 24 (cube directions × 3) in the unconstrained setting.

## Method

WAPR uses a render-and-compare architecture with two shared-weight ResNet branches (7×7 conv, 3×3 conv, two 128-channel basic blocks) that extract paired features from the real and rendered RGB-D+mask inputs; concatenated features with positional encodings pass through a two-layer Transformer encoder for global interaction, then split into independent rotation and translation heads with attention-based pooling. Training perturbs ground-truth poses by up to ±90° in rotation, and KASAL-assisted rotational symmetry priors canonicalize symmetry-equivalent targets via a min-over-symmetry selection so that visually similar candidate-target pairs receive a unique, consistent rotation update. An angle-balanced loss dynamically down-weights samples whose rotation-vector residual exceeds a threshold (τ = 0.25π), preventing low-overlap large-error pairs from dominating gradients. The supporting SA6D dataset expands 944 GSO scans with geometry scaling and texture randomization into ~50K augmented instances and ~2M rendered RGB-D images, with SAM-generated masks replacing perfect synthetic masks to close the train-test gap.

## Results

In the fast setting (<1 s/frame) with MUSE detections, WAPR achieves 80.6% AR and 78.6% AP on the seven BOP-Classic-Core datasets, improving over Co-op by +4.7% AR and +9.3% AP. Under a matched-detector comparison (F3DT2D), WAPR still gains +2.2% AR and +7.2% AP over Co-op. In the unconstrained multi-detector setting, WAPR reaches 84.5% AR and 85.3% AP, surpassing FreeZeV2 by +1.2% AR and +2.0% AP while processing up to 25 detected instances per second in the fast configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAPR outperforms all compared baselines across both fast and unconstrained settings. In the fast setting, WAPR with MUSE beats Co-op by +4.7% AR / +9.3% AP, and even with the same F3DT2D detector used by Co-op, WAPR gains +2.2% AR / +7.2% AP, confirming the improvement comes from the refiner rather than the detector. In the unconstrained multi-detector setting, WAPR surpasses the strongest prior method FreeZeV2 by +1.2% AR and +2.0% AP (84.5/85.3 vs. 83.3/83.3). Against earlier unconstrained methods, WAPR leads FoundationPose by +11.1% AR (84.5 vs. 73.4), SAM-6D by +14.1% AR, GenFlow by +15.9% AR, and MegaPose by +21.7% AR. In the plug-in refinement ablation (Table 2), the mask-free WAPR variant adds +0.6% AP on top of FreeZeV2 and +1.3% AP on top of GigaPose+GenFlow, demonstrating that wide-angle refinement is complementary to existing strong estimators. In the dedicated refinement comparison (Table 3), WAPR with 12 hypotheses achieves 82.4% mean AR, beating OSOP+ICP (52.7%) by 29.7%, (PPF,SIFT)+Zephyr (55.7%) by 26.7%, and FoundationPose's small-angle refiner (74.1%) by 8.3%, while running 4× faster than OSOP+ICP.

## Relation to Prior Work

WAPR extends the render-and-compare paradigm established by MegaPose and FoundationPose, which compare rendered observations under a candidate pose with real sensor input to regress pose updates. Where prior unseen-object pipelines (Co-op, FreeZe, GenFlow, GigaPose) typically rely on dense candidate sampling or small-angle refinement, WAPR deliberately targets the complementary wide-angle regime, training with perturbations up to 90° so that sparse candidate sets (12–24 poses) suffice. The symmetry-handling strategy builds on the ShapeMatch loss (PoseCNN) and symmetry-aware objectives (CosyPose, ZebraPose), but departs by applying rotational symmetry priors at the dataset-construction level to canonicalize training pairs offline, rather than modifying the loss function itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling accurate pose estimation with as few as 12 candidates in under 1 second per frame, WAPR addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying unseen-object pose estimation for robotics and AR where per-frame latency is critical. The SA6D dataset (~50K augmented instances, ~2M RGB-D images with KASAL-assisted symmetry priors) is a reusable resource that lowers the barrier for training symmetry-aware pose models. Open questions include whether the 90° training range generalizes to objects with extreme aspect ratios or severe self-occlusion, and whether the plug-in refinement gains (0.6–1.3% AP over already-strong estimators like FreeZeV2) grow as upstream detectors improve.
