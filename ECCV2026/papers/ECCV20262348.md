## Problem

State-of-the-art document tampering localization methods generalize poorly to cross-domain and human-made forgeries because synthetic pretraining encourages reliance on pipeline-specific artifacts rather than genuine manipulation inconsistencies. Additionally, the Frequency Perception Head (FPH) has become the standard DCT backbone but introduces large intermediate activations early, limiting throughput without revisiting the encoder design itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262348/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB document image (evaluated at 768×768) and produces a pixel-level tampering localization mask at the same resolution. Internally, the DCT branch consumes per-block quantized DCT coefficients (absolute values clipped to [0,20]) and the 64-entry quantization table (values 1–255), treating both as discrete integer tokens for embedding rather than continuous signals.

## Method

DiffNet is a two-branch early-fusion CNN built on ConvNeXt-V2. The RGB branch produces a four-stage feature pyramid, while the DCT branch maps each (quantized coefficient, quantization entry) pair to a compact joint embedding via learned lookup tables, FiLM-style per-frequency modulation, a frequency-index embedding, and a global table bias, then processes the result with a shallow ConvNeXt-V2 stack; DCT features are concatenated into the RGB stream after stage 1. The key architectural innovation is a multi-level discrepancy transformation applied at every backbone stage output: learned zero-sum depthwise filters (in both free and center-anchored families) produce signed responses whose absolute values yield a sign-invariant discrepancy representation, so the FPN-style decoder aggregates inconsistency cues rather than raw content-heavy activations. This design avoids the high-dimensional early expansions of FPH and replaces them with a lightweight embedding, achieving up to 7× higher inference throughput while improving cross-domain F1 by ~30% over the previous best.

## Results

On the Doc Protocol (cross-domain evaluation), DiffNet achieves an average F1 of 0.515 across T-SROIE, OSTF, Tampered-IC13, and RTM, improving over the previous best (ADCD-Net, 0.450) by approximately 14% in absolute terms (~30% relative). On the Syn2Real-TDoc protocol (human-made forgeries), DiffNet reaches 0.640 average image-level F1 and 0.304 average pixel-level F1, outperforming FFDN (0.623 / 0.270) and ADCD-Net (0.604 / 0.174). In terms of efficiency, DiffNet runs at 40.1 it/s inference and 41.9 it/s training, delivering up to 7× higher throughput than ADCD-Net (5.7 it/s) while using fewer parameters (113M vs. 140M for FFDN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffNet outperforms all 11 compared baselines on both evaluation protocols. On the Doc Protocol, the closest competitor is ADCD-Net (ICCV'25) at 0.450 average F1; DiffNet improves this to 0.515 (+0.065 absolute), with the largest per-dataset gain on T-SROIE (0.745 vs. 0.623). FFDN (ECCV'24), the previous best on the Syn2Real-TDoc protocol, scores 0.301 on Doc Protocol and 0.270 pixel-level F1 on TDoc; DiffNet exceeds both (0.515 and 0.304 respectively). DTD (CVPR'23), which introduced the Frequency Perception Head that this paper replaces, achieves only 0.247 on Doc Protocol and 0.132 pixel F1 on TDoc, roughly half of DiffNet's scores. PSCC-Net (TCSVT'22) is the strongest non-DCT-based baseline at 0.408 on Doc Protocol but trails DiffNet by 0.107. On the human-made Syn2Real-TDoc protocol, DiffNet's pixel-level F1 of 0.304 surpasses FFDN (0.270) by +0.034 and ADCD-Net (0.174) by +0.130, with the most pronounced gains on the hardest datasets (FindItAgain: 0.307 vs. 0.255 for FFDN). In terms of efficiency, DiffNet (113M params, 40.1 it/s) is faster than FFDN (140M, 33.1 it/s) and roughly 7× faster than ADCD-Net (45.7M, 5.7 it/s), while also outperforming both in accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant DTD/FPH lineage (Qu et al. CVPR'23, FFDN ECCV'24, ADCD-Net ICCV'25) in two ways: it replaces the Frequency Perception Head's early high-dimensional expansion with a compact frequency-index-aware joint embedding of quantized DCT coefficients and quantization tables, and it constrains the feature pyramid via learned zero-sum discrepancy filters at every backbone stage rather than adding residual modalities (SRM, Bayar convolutions) as separate input streams. The architectural substrate is a standard ConvNeXt-V2 backbone with FPN-style fusion, so the novelty lies in how the representation is shaped (discrepancy transformation) and how the DCT branch is parameterized (joint embedding), not in the overall pipeline topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~30% cross-domain F1 gain combined with up to 7× throughput improvement over the previous best (ADCD-Net) makes this a practically attractive option for deployment in financial and administrative document verification, where both accuracy on unseen forgery pipelines and inference speed matter. The paper's central design principle—that constraining the feature pyramid to inconsistency cues rather than adding architectural complexity improves cross-domain generalization—offers a useful counterpoint to the field's trend toward ever-larger fusion modules. However, pixel-level F1 on human-made datasets remains low (~0.3), and the authors acknowledge that synthetic pretraining data still lack the adaptive concealment strategies found in real forgeries, leaving a significant gap between synthetic and real-world performance.
