## Problem

Existing GUI grounding methods (SFT and RLVR) are bottlenecked by the prohibitive cost of pixel-level bounding box annotations, limiting scalability across diverse applications. While test-time reinforcement learning has shown promise for math reasoning, applying it to GUI grounding fails because region-consistency supervision introduces large spatial errors. The paper asks whether the model's own confidence signals can serve as a reliable, label-free proxy for ground-truth supervision in the sparse coordinate space of GUIs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a GUI screenshot (RGB image at arbitrary resolution) paired with a natural language instruction, and outputs a single point coordinate (x, y) normalized by image width and height. During training, the model generates N parallel candidate predictions via sampling (temperature 1.0, top-k 50), from which a pseudo-label point is extracted. The distance between each candidate's predicted point and the pseudo-label is computed in normalized coordinate space to assign binary rewards, with a threshold τ=0.05 defining the boundary between positive and negative samples.

## Method

The core insight is that coordinate-token confidence—averaging probabilities only over tokens encoding spatial values—far outperforms full-sequence confidence for distinguishing correct from incorrect predictions, since formatting tokens are consistently high-probability while coordinate tokens carry the true uncertainty (71.5% below 0.6 vs. 78.1% of non-coordinate tokens above 0.9). Given multiple sampled predictions, the highest coordinate-token confidence prediction is selected as a pseudo-label, and binary rewards are assigned based on whether each candidate falls within distance τ of this pseudo-label. The key architectural contribution is CANL, which modifies the GRPO advantage computation to zero out all positive-sample advantages while preserving negative-sample advantages, exploiting the fact that in sparse GUI coordinate spaces, predictions far from any reasonable target are almost certainly incorrect regardless of pseudo-label quality. This transforms pseudo-label uncertainty from a liability into a robustness advantage, as the model learns exclusively from reliably wrong predictions rather than potentially misplaced positive targets.

## Results

CANL-7B achieves 92.1% average accuracy on ScreenSpot-V2, a 4.0% absolute improvement over the base Qwen-2.5-VL-7B (88.1%), matching GUI-Actor-7B which requires 9.6M annotated labels. On the more challenging ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark, CANL-7B reaches 33.8%, an 8.9% absolute gain over the base model (24.9%) and 2.8% above GUI-R1-7B (31.0%) which uses ground-truth rewards. On UI-Vision, CANL-7B attains 20.1% overall accuracy, surpassing the base model (15.0%) by 5.1%. On AndroidWorld, the grounding improvements translate to a 4.3–5.2% success rate gain in end-to-end GUI navigation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only other label-free method, GUI-RCPO-7B, CANL-7B outperforms by 2.6% on ScreenSpot-V2 (92.1% vs. 88.9%) and by 7.9% on ScreenSpot-Pro (33.8% vs. 25.9%), confirming that coordinate-token confidence provides a more reliable supervision signal than region consistency. On ScreenSpot-V2, CANL-7B matches GUI-Actor-7B (92.1%) and exceeds UI-TARS-7B (91.6%) and GuirlVG-7B (91.9%), all of which require millions of annotated labels, while using zero annotations. On ScreenSpot-Pro, CANL-7B (33.8%) surpasses GUI-R1-7B (31.0%) which relies on ground-truth bounding boxes for reward computation, and outperforms ZonUI-3B (28.7%) trained on 24K labels. On UI-Vision, CANL-7B (20.1%) exceeds UI-TARS-7B (17.6%) trained on 18.4M labels. The 3B variant also shows strong data efficiency: CANL-3B reaches 84.5% on ScreenSpot-V1, approaching InfiGUI-R1-3B (87.5%) which uses 32K labels, and 32.7% on ScreenSpot-Pro, well above the base model's 16.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time reinforcement learning (TTRL) paradigm from mathematical reasoning to GUI grounding, adapting the label-free self-improvement loop to a spatial prediction task where ground truth is unavailable at inference. It departs from the prior label-free GUI work (GUI-RCPO) by replacing region-consistency supervision with coordinate-token confidence as the pseudo-label selection signal, and from standard GRPO-based RL by introducing a negative-only advantage computation (CANL) inspired by the negative learning literature for noisy labels. The method is built on the VLM-R1 training framework and Qwen-2.5-VL as the base architecture, positioning itself as a drop-in label-free alternative to both SFT and RLVR pipelines for GUI grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for pixel-level bounding box annotations, this work addresses a major scalability bottleneck for deploying GUI agents across diverse applications and domains. The finding that negative samples are more reliable than positive ones in sparse coordinate spaces offers a practical recipe for robust label-free training, particularly on high-resolution benchmarks where pseudo-label errors are most damaging. However, the authors acknowledge that the absence of accurate positive reward signals may cap further capability gains compared to fully supervised training, and the approach still requires test-time compute (multiple sampling + one training epoch per benchmark). Open questions include whether the negative-learning advantage persists as models scale beyond 7B parameters and whether the method can be extended to multi-step GUI navigation without the grounding-only isolation used in the AndroidWorld evaluation.
