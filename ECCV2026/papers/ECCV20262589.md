## Problem

Hierarchical split federated learning (HSFL) reduces client-side computation by partitioning models across cloud-edge-client tiers, but existing frameworks require continuous transmission of high-dimensional sample-level activations and gradients, creating severe communication bottlenecks and coupling optimization across tiers. This paper asks whether compact class-wise feature statistics can replace sample-level exchange while preserving the computational advantages of split-model execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262589/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, variable resolution for HAM10000, ImageNet-1K, and ISIC-2018) and produces class labels for classification tasks or pixel-wise segmentation masks for the ISIC-2018 skin lesion task. A key operational detail is that clients never transmit raw activations or gradients; instead, each client outputs only per-class mean and standard-deviation vectors of its feature embeddings (dimension d_c), reducing per-client communication from O(B·d) to O(C·d) where C is the number of local classes. The system operates with 200 clients partitioned across 10 edge servers under a three-tier topology.

## Method

H-SFP partitions the global model into three segments: a lightweight client-side feature extractor W_c, an edge-side intermediate model W_e, and a cloud-side task classifier W_g. Clients train W_c using the NT-Xent contrastive loss, then compress their learned feature space into class-wise first- and second-order statistics (μ, σ) rather than transmitting sample-level activations. Edge servers aggregate these statistics across their client cluster, synthesize representative features by sampling from a diagonal-covariance multivariate Gaussian, and train W_e on the synthetic data using the same contrastive objective; the cloud tier performs an analogous aggregation and trains W_g with a supervised loss. A dual-timescale mechanism periodically performs model averaging of W_c to correct long-term client drift. The core insight is that this statistical prototyping completely severs the cross-tier optimization graph, enabling independent, asynchronous training of each segment while an MMD-based analysis bounds the distribution shift between true and synthesized features.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (IID, 200 clients), H-SFP(A) achieves 67.44% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best split-learning baseline HeteroSFL (63.45%) and the best prototype method FedProto (55.10%). Under extreme non-IID (Dirichlet α=0.3), H-SFP(A) reaches 40.15% on CIFAR-10 and 7.15% on CIFAR-100, while FedAvg collapses to 12.15% and 2.10% respectively. On the larger HAM10000 dataset (ResNet-50, IID), H-SFP(A) attains 80.86% accuracy versus 73.10% for FedDF and 68.82% for HSFL(A). For ISIC-2018 segmentation, H-SFP(A) achieves 68.3% IoU and 79.5% DICE in 6.8 hours, a 10× training speedup over SplitFed (53.8% IoU in 72.4 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-SFP consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and distribution setting. Against the primary architectural baseline HSFL, H-SFP(A) gains +16.09% on CIFAR-10 (dir 0.3), +19.63% on CIFAR-100 (dir 0.3), and +7.04% on HAM10000 (ResNet-50, IID). Among prototype-based methods, H-SFP surpasses FedProto by +1.65% on CIFAR-10 (dir 0.3) and +8.60% on CIFAR-100 (IID), and outperforms FedGen by +2.25% on CIFAR-10 (dir 0.3) and +3.90% on CIFAR-100 (IID). On ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50, IID), H-SFP(A) leads HeteroSFL by +1.6% and SplitFed by +2.4%. For ISIC-2018 segmentation, H-SFP(A) achieves 68.3% IoU versus 62.1% for HSFL(A) and 53.8% for SplitFed, while also reducing training time from 72.4 h (SplitFed) to 6.8 h. In terms of communication, H-SFP(C) requires only 10.94 GB total versus 3,865 GB for FedAvg (353× reduction) and 113.12 GB for HSFL (10.4× reduction), with the savings attributed to replacing O(B·d) activation/gradient exchange with O(C·d) statistical moment transmission.

## Relation to Prior Work

H-SFP builds directly on the split federated learning architecture of SplitFed [29] and the hierarchical topology of HSFL [13], but departs fundamentally from their communication paradigm: instead of transmitting sample-level activations and gradients to maintain an end-to-end optimization graph, it severs cross-tier backpropagation entirely and replaces it with class-wise (μ, σ) feature statistics. The client-side representation learning leverages NT-Xent contrastive loss [2] to produce structured embeddings amenable to statistical compression. This positions H-SFP between prototype-based FL (FedProto, which uses prototypes only as regularization) and generative FL (FedGen, FedDF, which require expensive server-side generators), by using lightweight moments as a generative interface for synthesizing training distributions at upper tiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 353× communication reduction over FedAvg and 10.4× over HSFL, combined with sub-100 MB client-side memory, makes hierarchical split learning viable for genuinely resource-constrained edge deployments where even SFL's activation transmission is prohibitive. The decoupled optimization graph also enables asynchronous, tier-independent training, removing the synchronization bottleneck that plagues existing HSFL systems. Key limitations include the diagonal-covariance restriction (no cross-dimensional correlations), the Gaussian synthesis assumption that may fail for multimodal feature distributions, and the use of random rather than topology-aware client-edge assignment. Open questions include whether low-rank covariance approximations or richer distribution families can close the remaining gap to centralized performance (e.g., 67% vs 94% on CIFAR-10 IID) while preserving the communication advantage.
