## Problem

Existing RGB-IR fusion detectors treat both modalities as equally reliable, but under nighttime, fog, or low-light conditions RGB features become severely degraded and inject noise into the more robust IR stream, actually impairing detection accuracy. No prior method explicitly regulates when and how much RGB guidance should influence the IR pathway, nor supports removing RGB entirely at inference without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260736/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes spatially aligned RGB-IR image pairs (e.g., 640×640) and produces standard detection outputs (bounding boxes with class labels) from both the fused auxiliary branch and the raw IR branch. At inference, InfraNet-IR accepts only a single IR image and discards the RGB branch and all QualGate modules entirely, yielding zero additional deployment cost; InfraNet-RGB-IR retains both inputs and the full dual-stream backbone for higher-capacity dual-modal prediction.

## Method

InfraNet introduces QualGate, a lightweight quality-aware fusion module that predicts a scalar reliability score q from the auxiliary RGB feature via a two-layer MLP on global average pooling. This score drives a dual-path modulation: it suppresses the RGB contribution (w_rgb = q) to prevent negative transfer from degraded RGB, while simultaneously amplifying multi-scale IR features through a clipped inverse gain α(q) = clip(1.5 − q, 1.0, 1.5) to compensate the IR stream. QualGate is instantiated at three multi-scale fusion sites (A3, A4, A5) within an asymmetric dual-branch architecture (YOLOv5/v8/v9/v12 or ResNet50), where the IR pathway is primary and the RGB pathway is auxiliary. Training uses an asymmetric loss (L = L_det_main + 0.25·L_det_aux) so optimization is dominated by the IR pathway, and the RGB branch functions as privileged information that can be pruned at deployment for the IR-only variant.

## Results

On LLVIP, InfraNet-RGB-IR (YOLOv8) achieves 70.5% mAP and 98.3% mAP₅₀, outperforming the strongest recent baseline WaveMamba (66.0% mAP) by +4.5 points; the IR-only variant (YOLOv8) reaches 68.8% mAP with zero additional inference cost. On FLIR-Aligned, YOLOv5-RGB-IR attains 89.5% mAP₅₀ and 55.1% mAP with 81.8% recall, while YOLOv12-RGB-IR achieves the best mAP of 55.8%. On DroneVehicle, YOLOv8-RGB-IR scores 85.0% mAP₅₀ / 64.9% mAP, surpassing WaveMamba (79.8% / 60.5%) by +5.2 / +4.4 points. On M³FD Night subset, the RGB-IR variant reaches 96.6–96.8% AP₅₀ versus 81.6% for TarDAL, and the IR-only variant still achieves 92.1–92.3% AP₅₀.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfraNet consistently outperforms all 40 compared baselines across all four datasets in both RGB-IR and IR-only settings. Against the strongest recent fusion baseline WaveMamba (ICCV 2025), InfraNet-RGB-IR gains +4.5 mAP on LLVIP (70.5 vs 66.0), +3.7 mAP₅₀ on FLIR (89.5 vs 85.8), and +5.2 mAP₅₀ on DroneVehicle (85.0 vs 79.8). The IR-only variant (InfraNet-IR) surpasses single-modality IR baselines by large margins: +6.7 mAP over YOLOv8-IR on LLVIP (68.8 vs 62.1), +15.6 mAP₅₀ on FLIR (88.5 vs 72.9), and +12.1 mAP₅₀ on DroneVehicle (84.0 vs 71.9), confirming that RGB-guided training substantially strengthens IR representations without any test-time RGB dependency. On M³FD Night, InfraNet-IR (92.3% AP₅₀) exceeds the IR-only baseline (79.5%) by +12.8 points and even surpasses TarDAL's RGB-IR result (81.6%) by +10.7 points using IR input alone. The only setting where InfraNet does not lead is LLVIP mAP₅₀ with ResNet50, where WaveMamba (98.1%) slightly edges out InfraNet-RGB-IR (97.7%), though InfraNet still wins on mAP (68.4 vs 69.2 is actually a loss here — WaveMamba leads by 0.8). Overall, InfraNet achieves the best or near-best result in every backbone–dataset combination tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfraNet departs from the dominant RGB-IR fusion paradigm (pixel-level, feature-level, decision-level) that treats both modalities as equal partners, instead adopting a modality-asymmetric, privileged-information-learning framing where RGB serves only as reliability-controlled training-time guidance for an IR-centric detector. Conceptually, it draws on LUPI and knowledge distillation (e.g., HalluciDet) as well as reliability-aware fusion modules from RGB-T tracking (CRLM, CAGF), but introduces the QualGate mechanism—a lightweight scalar control signal that jointly suppresses unreliable RGB contributions and amplifies IR features—as a targeted solution to the negative-transfer problem observed when degraded RGB is naively fused with IR. The architecture is plug-and-play on standard YOLOv5/v8/v9/v12 and ResNet backbones, positioning it as a drop-in enhancement rather than a new backbone design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: in nighttime, fog, or adverse-weather deployment, RGB sensors are often unavailable or severely degraded, yet most SOTA RGB-IR detectors mandate dual-modal input at inference. InfraNet-IR removes this dependency entirely while retaining the representational benefits of RGB-guided training, offering a zero-overhead IR-only detector that approaches dual-modal accuracy. Open questions include whether the scalar quality score q generalizes to more complex degradation patterns (e.g., partial occlusion, sensor failure), whether the ad-hoc 1.5 amplification constant in α(q) can be learned end-to-end, and whether the approach extends to other modality pairs (e.g., LiDAR-camera) or to video detection. The relatively small gap between IR-only and RGB-IR inference (≈1–4 AP50 across datasets) also raises the question of whether the added complexity of dual-modal deployment is justified in most operational settings.
