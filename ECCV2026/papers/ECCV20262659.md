## Problem

Existing text-to-motion (T2M) models achieve semantic alignment with language but produce physically infeasible motions—skating, floating, abrupt transitions—that cannot be executed by real humanoid robots. No prior method directly uses a humanoid tracking policy's execution results as training signal to make generated motions physically deployable while preserving text alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262659/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language instruction as input and produces a temporally coherent 3D human motion sequence (joint positions and rotations) that is retargeted to a specific humanoid morphology (e.g., Unitree G1) and then executed as 23-DOF target joint positions via PD controllers. The motion is generated autoregressively as discrete VQ-VAE tokens from an LLaMA2-7B backbone, decoded to kinematic poses, and evaluated for feasibility through a physics-simulator tracking rollout before being deployed on the real robot.

## Method

RLPF fine-tunes a pre-trained large motion model (LLaMA2-7B + VQ-VAE tokenizer) via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), using a composite reward that combines (i) a binary tracking-success signal from a humanoid motion tracking policy evaluated in IsaacGym and (ii) a contrastive alignment-verification reward from pre-trained text/motion encoders that prevents degeneration to trivial motions. The tracking policy itself is trained with a two-stage teacher-student pipeline (PPO oracle followed by DAgger distillation) following BumbleBee. Hierarchical RLPF extends this by iteratively alternating RLPF updates of the T2M generator with retraining of the tracking policy on the generator's on-policy outputs, progressively improving both components without mutual overfitting.

## Results

On the AMASS benchmark, RLPF achieves a tracking success rate of 0.95 in IsaacGym and 0.94 in MuJoCo, substantially outperforming the best baseline (RobotMDM at 0.86/0.88) by 9 and 6 percentage points respectively. RLPF also leads on high-level text-motion alignment, attaining R@1=0.50 and MM-D=3.02, while maintaining competitive FID (0.26) and diversity (DIV=10.58). On the lower-quality MotionX subsets, Hierarchical RLPF boosts success rate by approximately 40 percentage points over the base model (e.g., 0.21→0.69 on MotionX-J) while keeping FID, R@1, and MM-D largely stable across iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLPF decisively outperforms all five baselines on low-level tracking success rate: it reaches 0.95 (IsaacGym) and 0.94 (MuJoCo), compared to RobotMDM (0.86/0.88), SFT-Filter (0.85/0.87), PhysDiff (0.84/0.82), LangWBC (0.81/0.79), and the Base Model (0.83/0.85). On high-level text-motion alignment, RLPF achieves the best R@1 (0.50) and ties SFT-Filter for the best MM-D (3.02), while PhysDiff lags significantly (R@1=0.35, MM-D=3.32) due to its smaller diffusion backbone and lack of explicit humanoid-policy alignment. LangWBC bypasses motion generation entirely (predicting actions directly via a lightweight MLP), so high-level generation metrics are not applicable, and its lower SR on unseen instructions reflects limited generalization. The ablation further confirms that removing the alignment verification module causes catastrophic collapse (FID jumps to 41.97, R@1 drops to 0.07) despite near-perfect tracking (SR=0.99), underscoring that both reward components are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: LLM-based text-to-motion generation (MotionGPT, T2M-GPT, MDM), physics-based humanoid whole-body control (DeepMimic, BumbleBee, PHC), and RLHF-style fine-tuning of large models. The key conceptual departure is repurposing a motion tracking policy not as a controller for fixed references (the DeepMimic paradigm) but as a *reward oracle* that scores generated motions in a physics simulator, thereby closing the loop between high-level generation and low-level execution. Unlike prior physical-feasibility metrics (skating, floating, penetration) or language-conditioned control methods (LangWBC, LeVERB) that rely on curated reference datasets, RLPF uses execution-based feedback from a general-purpose tracker to directly optimize the T2M generator, with an explicit alignment-verification module to prevent semantic collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a concrete, validated pathway from text instructions to stable whole-body execution on a real Unitree G1 robot, addressing a gap that purely kinematic T2M models leave open. The ablation revealing catastrophic collapse to standing motions when the alignment reward is removed highlights a fundamental tension between physical feasibility and semantic diversity that the field has not previously characterized. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to diffusion-based T2M backbones (the authors note the discrete codebook constrains RL exploration), whether it generalizes beyond the tested walking/jumping subsets, and whether the Hierarchical RLPF co-training loop converges reliably at larger scale or with more diverse motion categories.
