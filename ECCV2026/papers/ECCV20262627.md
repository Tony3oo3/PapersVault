## Problem

Semi-supervised cross-domain few-shot object detection (Semi-CDFSOD) requires adapting detectors to novel domains using scarce labeled supports alongside abundant unlabeled data, yet remains largely unexplored. When open-set detectors are used to generate pseudo-labels under severe domain shift, they suffer from two compounding defects—semantic misclassification (correct localizations assigned wrong categories or suppressed as background) and low recall (most objects missed entirely)—that render naive pseudo-labeling ineffective. Additionally, no standardized benchmark or evaluation protocol existed for this task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target-domain RGB images, a few-shot labeled support set (N-way K-shot with K=1, 5, or 10 boxes per class), and a pretrained MM-Grounding-DINO detector fine-tuned on the support set. The calibration pipeline itself is entirely training-free, relying on a frozen DINOv3 (ViT-L/16) backbone for feature extraction and an off-the-shelf SAM for segmentation. The output is a harmonized set of pseudo-labels (bounding boxes with corrected categories and recovered instances) that, combined with the original support set, drives a 500-iteration fine-tuning of the detector for the target domain.

## Method

The framework operates in two complementary, training-free stages. First, a semantic calibrator extracts center-weighted ROI features from a frozen DINOv3 backbone (using a Gaussian decay kernel to suppress boundary noise), constructs class prototypes by averaging features from labeled supports and the top-3 highest-confidence pseudo-labels per class, then recalibrates each pseudo-label's category via cosine-similarity matching against these prototypes. Second, a visual self-prompting module selects the highest-confidence calibrated pseudo-label per class as a visual prompt for SAM, augmented with a class-aware attention enhancement map that biases SAM's cross-attention toward regions occupied by other same-class detections, thereby recovering missed objects. Recovered candidates are re-calibrated through the same prototype-matching step, and the final merged set undergoes NMS before being used for detector adaptation. The key insight is that pseudo-label quality—not adaptation strategy complexity—is the primary bottleneck, and that scarce supports can serve as reliable semantic anchors to calibrate abundant but noisy pseudo-labels without any additional training.

## Results

Under the Semi-CDFSOD setting, the proposed training-free calibration framework achieves average mAP of 35.9, 44.1, and 46.7 across six target domains under 1-shot, 5-shot, and 10-shot settings, respectively. The largest gains appear in the most label-scarce regime: at 1-shot, the method improves over the strongest baseline (MM-Grounding-DINO) by 4.7 mAP on average, while at 10-shot the margin narrows to 1.6 mAP. Per-dataset, the method leads on all six domains in every shot setting, with particularly large margins on ArTaxOr (82.4 vs. 76.7 at 10-shot) and DIOR (37.3 vs. 35.7 at 10-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and shot setting. Against the strongest baseline, MM-Grounding-DINO (fine-tuned on the support set without calibration), the gains are +4.7 mAP (1-shot), +3.5 mAP (5-shot), and +1.6 mAP (10-shot) on average, with the largest per-domain improvement on DIOR (+5.1 at 5-shot) and NEU-DET (+1.6 at 10-shot). The traditional CDFSOD methods—Detic-FT, DE-ViT-FT, and CD-ViTO—are substantially outperformed by 18–33 mAP on average, confirming that semi-supervised exploitation of unlabeled data is critical under domain shift. Notably, the method also surpasses SAM-based pseudo-label generation with visual prompts (40.5 avg at 5-shot) by 3.6 mAP, demonstrating that calibrating categories *before* visual prompting prevents the 'prompt noise → output noise' loop that degrades uncalibrated SAM-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends cross-domain few-shot object detection (CDFSOD) by introducing a semi-supervised component that exploits abundant unlabeled target-domain data, a resource prior CDFSOD methods ignore. It builds directly on MM-Grounding-DINO as the open-set pseudo-label generator and on SAM 3's visual prompting interface for missed-object recovery, but departs from both the CDFSOD literature (which assumes no unlabeled data) and the semi-supervised few-shot learning literature (which does not handle cross-domain detection with joint localization and semantic ambiguity). The key conceptual departure is inverting the conventional pipeline: rather than accepting noisy pseudo-labels and designing robust adaptation strategies, the paper argues that training-free calibration of the pseudo-labels themselves must take precedence, using support-set prototypes and foundation-model prompting as calibration tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first standardized Semi-CDFSOD benchmark (six domains, multiple shot settings, consistent splits) removes a major barrier to systematic comparison in this underexplored intersection of domain shift, label scarcity, and unlabeled data. The finding that a training-free calibration framework outperforms methods requiring additional fine-tuning or architectural changes suggests that pseudo-label quality, not adaptation complexity, is the primary bottleneck—a reframing that could influence how the community approaches semi-supervised detection more broadly. Open questions include whether the calibration framework generalizes beyond MM-Grounding-DINO as the base detector, whether the SAM-based recovery scales to denser or more cluttered scenes, and whether the center-weighted ROI aggregation and prototype construction remain effective when support sets grow beyond 10-shot.
