## Problem

Video-text models adapted from image-text architectures (e.g., CLIP) exhibit temporal blindness: permutation-invariant frame aggregation collapses videos differing only in event order into nearly identical embeddings, and standard training data masks this flaw by enabling static spatial shortcuts. The paper demonstrates that even 7B-parameter multimodal LLMs fail at basic temporal reasoning (direction, ordering, causality), showing that parameter scaling alone cannot resolve this structural deficiency.

## Input / Output

The model consumes uniformly sampled 32-frame video clips and natural-language captions, producing a shared video-text embedding space for zero-shot retrieval and classification. The visual encoder processes each frame independently into patch tokens, which are stacked into a dense spatiotemporal tensor before passing through a two-block spatiotemporal transformer; the text encoder processes captions with LoRA-adapted attention to accommodate temporal vocabulary (e.g., 'first', 'then', 'reversed'). The final output is a pair of normalized embeddings whose dot-product similarity drives ranking across retrieval and multiple-choice diagnostic tasks.

## Method

ViTAL-X combines three components: (1) Cross-Modal Temporal Edits (XTE), a self-supervised data framework that applies five synchronized video-text transformations—reversal, clip reordering, sequence concatenation, temporal cropping, and textual counterfactuals—so that spatial content is identical across positive/negative pairs and only chronological structure varies; (2) a lightweight architecture that freezes the OpenCLIP or SigLIP-2 backbone, inserts a two-block spatiotemporal transformer with spatial and temporal positional encodings to break permutation invariance, and applies LoRA (rank 16) to attention projections in both modalities; (3) a dual-objective loss combining standard symmetric InfoNCE contrastive alignment with a margin-based temporal loss that explicitly penalizes similarity between a video and its XTE-generated counterfactual caption. This design forces the model to abandon spatial shortcuts and learn true temporal discrimination while preserving the backbone's static spatial priors, achieving state-of-the-art results with only 0.4B parameters and 1M training clips.

## Results

On six temporal video-text retrieval benchmarks, ViTAL-X (0.4B params, 1M training clips) achieves 57.9 on ActivityNet, 50.4 on YouCook2, 55.8 on DiDeMo, 65.3 on RTime, 67.8 on VideoComp, and 57.9 on TemporalBench, setting new state-of-the-art among efficient models. On the diagnostic XTE-Bench, ViTAL-X reaches 67.9% average multiple-choice accuracy versus 85.1% for the human oracle, substantially outperforming 7B-parameter multimodal LLMs (LLaVA-NeXT-7B: 56.0%, Qwen2.5-3B: 53.5%). On standard zero-shot classification and retrieval (Table 2), ViTAL-X achieves 76.1 on Kinetics-400, 91.6 on UCF-101, 65.3 on HMDB-51, 54.3 on MSR-VTT, and 63.0 on MSVD, matching or exceeding models trained on 22M–619M clips.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViTAL-X (0.4B) decisively outperforms the closest same-scale baselines: over SigLIP-2-L/16 (0.3B) it gains +22.0 on ActivityNet, +19.2 on YouCook2, +22.5 on DiDeMo, +32.0 on RTime, +18.9 on VideoComp, and +11.1 on TemporalBench. Against the much larger InternVideo2 (6B), ViTAL-X wins on ActivityNet (+3.1), RTime (+8.3), and TemporalBench, while InternVideo2 retains a narrow edge on DiDeMo (57.0 vs. 55.8). Compared to PEcoreGVideo (1.9B, 22M clips), ViTAL-X leads on every reported temporal benchmark, with the largest margins on RTime (+14.3) and VideoComp (+11.0). On XTE-Bench, ViTAL-X (67.9) surpasses LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B (56.0), Qwen2.5-3B (53.5), VideoPrism (53.2), and PE (51.3) by 12–17 points, demonstrating that parameter scaling alone does not confer temporal understanding. On general video tasks (Table 2), ViTAL-X with only 1M clips matches or beats PEcoreGVideo (22M clips) on UCF-101 (+0.9), HMDB-51 (+4.2), MSR-VTT (+3.1), and MSVD (+3.3), and approaches VideoPrism-g (619M clips) on Kinetics-400 (76.1 vs. 76.4). The ablation without XTE (ViTAL-X w/o XTE) drops to 49.1 on TemporalBench and 52.5 on RTime, confirming that the synchronized cross-modal temporal edits—not merely the architecture—are the primary driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant paradigm of adapting image-text encoders (CLIP, SigLIP-2) to video via permutation-invariant frame pooling (CLIP4Clip, X-CLIP, CLIP2Video), which the authors formalize as a structural flaw called 'temporal blindness.' It departs from prior self-supervised temporal learning (VideoMoCo, CVRL, TCLR) that operates in the visual domain alone, and from PAXION's text-only hard-negative augmentation, by proposing synchronized cross-modal edits where video timeline and caption are jointly transformed. The parameter-efficient architecture (LoRA + shallow spatiotemporal transformer) follows the PEFT lineage (ST-Adapter, VidPanda) but is specifically motivated by preserving frozen spatial priors while injecting temporal sensitivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical implication is that targeted, high-quality temporal supervision (1M clips, 0.4B params) can match or exceed brute-force scaling (619M clips, 7B params), offering a far more efficient path to video-language understanding. The XTE-Bench diagnostic is valuable for the field because it isolates temporal reasoning from spatial confounds, revealing that even large multimodal LLMs fail at basic ordering and directionality. Key limitations include the inability to model continuous dynamics (speed, duration), potential brittleness of LLM-generated rewrites for highly overlapping events, and the modest computational overhead of the adapter over simple pooling. Whether XTE-style interventions can be extended to continuous temporal properties and longer-horizon reasoning remains an open question.
