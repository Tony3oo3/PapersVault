## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods are trained on small, manually annotated datasets collected in controlled environments (indoor RGB-D scans, platform-specific LiDAR), limiting their ability to generalize to diverse in-the-wild scenes with complex object layouts and varied categories. The fundamental bottleneck is the scarcity of scalable 3D grounding supervision: constructing large-scale 3D bounding box and language annotation pairs is expensive and inherently constrained by data collection settings.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a 3D point cloud (downsampled to 16,384 points for 3EED/Mono3DRefer or 30,000 points for WildRefer) and a natural language referring expression, and outputs a 3D bounding box parameterized by center (x,y,z), dimensions (w,h,l), and heading angle (θ). During data generation, the pipeline starts from a single monocular RGB image and produces the full grounding triple (point cloud, 3D box, text description) without any manual 3D annotation. The point cloud is reconstructed via metric depth estimation and back-projection, while the 3D box is derived from a reconstructed object mesh aligned to the scene coordinate frame.

## Method

AnyGround3D introduces a multi-level 2D-to-3D lifting pipeline that converts unconstrained Internet images (COCO, KITTI) into synthetic 3D grounding supervision: scene-level lifting reconstructs metric point clouds via MoGe + Depth Pro; object-level lifting recovers complete 3D meshes with TRELLIS and localizes them via MASt3R pose matching to yield 3D bounding boxes; and a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) synthesizes referring expressions conditioned on geometry and object attributes. For the encoder, a feature-level lifting strategy distills DINOv2-large dense 2D semantic features into the 3D PointNet++ encoder by back-projecting pixel features onto 3D points and minimizing an L2 regression loss, transferring open-vocabulary semantic priors into the geometric representation. The grounding model follows the 3EED architecture: PointNet++ produces 1,024 visual tokens, an MLP objectness head selects the top 256 as proposals, and a six-layer Transformer decoder performs cross-modal text-to-box grounding. Training jointly mixes the generated synthetic data with existing 3D grounding datasets (3EED, WildRefer, ScanRefer) to combine scale with domain-specific signal.

## Results

On the 3EED benchmark (union of vehicle, drone, and quadruped platforms), AnyGround3D achieves 66.76%/31.34% at Acc@0.25/0.5, outperforming the 3EED baseline (63.84%/29.66%) and all prior methods. On WildRefer (union), it reaches 51.67%/39.00%, surpassing the 3EED baseline (44.16%/29.95%) by 7.5 points at Acc@0.25. Most strikingly, on the zero-shot Mono3DRefer generalization test, AnyGround3D achieves 58.90%/49.59% (val) and 62.71%/54.79% (test), compared to only 25.74%/12.19% for the 3EED-trained model, demonstrating a 33-point improvement in cross-domain robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyGround3D outperforms the strongest baseline 3EED [20] on all three benchmarks: +2.92 points at Acc@0.25 on 3EED Union (66.76 vs 63.84), +7.51 points on WildRefer Union (51.67 vs 44.16), and a dramatic +33.16 points on Mono3DRefer val (58.90 vs 25.74). Against TSP3D [10] and MCLN [34], the gains are consistent across all platforms, with the largest margin on the quadruped subset (61.60 vs 46.66/44.80 at Acc@0.25), indicating superior robustness under viewpoint and sensor shifts. On WildRefer, AnyGround3D also surpasses the WildRefer [24] baseline itself (51.67 vs 50.45 at Acc@0.25, 39.00 vs 36.70 at Acc@0.5), suggesting that the lifting-based synthetic supervision transfers effectively even to the dataset's own domain. The zero-shot Mono3DRefer results are the most compelling: while 3EED drops to 25.74% and WildRefer to 14.46% under distribution shift, AnyGround3D maintains 58.90%, a 2.3× improvement over the next-best method, directly attributable to the diverse Internet-image-derived training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3EED architecture (PointNet++ backbone + Transformer decoder) but fundamentally rethinks the data pipeline: instead of training on manually annotated 3D grounding datasets (ScanRefer, 3EED, WildRefer), it synthesizes 3D supervision by lifting monocular Internet images through a multi-stage pipeline combining depth estimation (MoGe + Depth Pro), object reconstruction (TRELLIS), pose estimation (MASt3R), and VLM caption generation (Qwen2.5-VL). The feature-level lifting step—distilling DINOv2 dense features into the 3D encoder via back-projected correspondences—departs from prior 3DVG work that trains encoders from scratch on limited point clouds, introducing a cross-dimensional knowledge transfer mechanism. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both the indoor 3DVG line (ScanRefer, Nr3D) and the emerging outdoor 3DVG line (3EED, WildRefer, Mono3DRefer), arguing that all are bottlenecked by the same scarcity of scalable 3D grounding annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles the central scalability bottleneck in 3D visual grounding: the inability to collect large-scale, diverse, manually annotated 3D scenes. By demonstrating that a lifting pipeline over off-the-shelf 2D/3D models can produce usable synthetic supervision that improves zero-shot cross-domain generalization (especially the large Mono3DRefer gap), it offers a practical recipe for the field to scale beyond curated benchmarks. However, the in-domain ablation on 3EED shows only modest gains from the generated data (~1.5%) and encoder pre-training (~0.4%), suggesting the primary benefit is distributional breadth rather than per-sample quality. Open questions include how the pipeline handles dynamic/multi-view scenes, whether the depth and reconstruction errors compound at scale, and whether the approach transfers to tasks requiring precise metric geometry rather than approximate localization.
