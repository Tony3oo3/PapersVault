## Problem

Existing VLN models reason only over past and current observations, ignoring the causal relationship between actions and how the visual world changes, which leads to myopic 'step-and-see' behavior and difficulty recovering from erroneous branches. The central question is whether future observations recorded in offline training trajectories can be exploited as privileged supervision to internalize action-conditioned visual dynamics into the navigator itself, while keeping inference strictly causal without any external world-model rollout.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260051/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the model consumes a natural-language instruction, a single egocentric monocular RGB frame (H×W×3), and a cached d-dimensional Pilot Token from the previous step, producing a discrete action from {FWD, LEFT, RIGHT, STOP}. The Pilot Token is a continuous latent vector that is read from and written to a simple cache each step, so no additional observations or external predictors are needed at test time. During training, the Pilot slot is teacher-forced with a mean-pooled visual embedding of the one-step-future frame (o_{t+1}), and the predicted Pilot Token is regressed toward the mean-pooled embedding of the two-step-future frame (o_{t+2}).

## Method

LatentPilot couples a SigLIP vision encoder, a 7B LLaVA-Video LLM backbone, and a lightweight linear Pilot module into a single end-to-end navigator. The key architectural element is the Pilot Token: a continuous d-dimensional latent placed at a dedicated slot in the multimodal input sequence, whose hidden state is projected back to latent space by the Pilot module and cached for the next step, creating a recurrent internal reasoning state without explicit chain-of-thought. Training uses a flywheel-style loop (PilotLoop) with expert takeover for deviation-aware correction, and a two-step future-privileged supervision objective: the one-step-future visual latent fills the Pilot slot as a training-only input, while the two-step-future visual latent serves as the MSE regression target for the predicted Pilot Token, jointly optimized with action cross-entropy (λ=0.1). At inference the model performs a purely recurrent rollout using only the current observation, instruction, and cached Pilot Token—no future frames, no external world-model rollout—making the anticipatory reasoning an amortized property of the decision backbone itself.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, LatentPilot (monocular RGB only) achieves NE=4.41 m, SR=62.5%, SPL=68.0, and OS=66.3%, setting a new SOTA among all single-RGB VLM navigators and surpassing even sensor-rich panoramic+odometry+depth methods. On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, it reaches NE=5.19 m, SR=58.2%, SPL=49.9%, and nDTW=67.5%. On the physically realistic VLN-PE benchmark (humanoid Unitree H1), LatentPilot achieves SR=59.01% (Val-Seen) and SR=56.42% (Val-Unseen) with the lowest fall rate (11.09%/10.65%) and stuck rate (1.08%/0.97%) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SRGB-only VLM navigators, LatentPilot outperforms the strongest prior method JanusVLN [61] by 0.37 m in NE (4.41 vs. 4.78), 10.5 points in OS (66.3 vs. 56.8), and 7.5 points in SPL (68.0 vs. 60.5) on R2R Val-Unseen, while also leading on RxR by 0.87 m in NE and 5.7 points in nDTW. It surpasses StreamVLN [55] (SR 64.2, SPL 56.9) and NaVILA [10] (SR 62.5, SPL 54.0) on R2R SPL by 11+ points. Notably, LatentPilot with only monocular RGB also beats the panoramic+odometry+depth ETPNav [2] (NE 4.71, SR 65.0, SPL 57.0) on NE and SPL, demonstrating that internalized future-aware reasoning compensates for richer sensor inputs. On VLN-PE with a humanoid robot, LatentPilot improves over DualVLN [54] (the strongest zero-shot transfer baseline) by 0.33 m in NE, 4.8 points in SR, and 5.25 points in SPL on Val-Unseen, while reducing fall rate from 12.32% to 10.65% and stuck rate from 2.23% to 0.97%, indicating superior physical robustness. In the plug-in world-model comparison (Table 4), LatentPilot matches or exceeds V-JEPA2 plug-in (SR 58.1, SPL 52.5) while cutting per-action latency from 143 ms to 130 ms and peak memory from 23.6 GB to 22.8 GB, and dramatically outperforms generative video world models (Wan2.1: 2040 ms/action, 41.5 GB; CogVideoX: 5460 ms/action).

## Relation to Prior Work

LatentPilot carves a distinct niche between two established VLN paradigms: (1) standard imitation-learning navigators (Seq2Seq, CMA, NaVid) that condition only on past/current observations, and (2) world-model or imagination-based pipelines (Pathdreamer, Vista, NavMorph, ImagineNav) that externalize future prediction as a separate generative or planning module at inference. The paper's conceptual departure is to internalize action-conditioned visual dynamics into the decision backbone itself via a step-propagated Pilot Token, trained with future observations as LUPI-style privileged supervision but requiring no future frames or external rollout at test time. Architecturally it repurposes the latent-reasoning literature (Coconut, Quiet-STaR, LVR) for embodied sequential decision making, and the flywheel training loop follows DAgger-style dataset aggregation with expert takeover.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By amortizing lookahead into a single forward pass with a lightweight latent cache, LatentPilot eliminates the inference-time latency and memory overhead of plug-in video world models (Table 4 shows ~15× lower per-action latency vs. Wan2.1) while achieving stronger navigation accuracy, making anticipatory reasoning practically deployable on resource-constrained robots. The real-robot validation across wheeled and quadruped platforms strengthens the case for generalization beyond simulation. Open questions include whether the Pilot Token captures genuine causal dynamics or merely correlational shortcuts, how the approach scales to much longer horizons or outdoor environments, and whether the two-step future-privilege design (t+1 as input, t+2 as target) is optimal or whether deeper look-ahead would help.
