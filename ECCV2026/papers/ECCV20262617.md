## Problem

Vision-Language Models achieve near-perfect scores on digital document benchmarks like OmniDocBench, yet their robustness under real-world physical degradations (warping, non-uniform illumination, moiré from screen-photography, perspective skew) remains unquantified. Prior efforts either capture in-the-wild documents without digital ground-truth correspondence (WildDoc) or employ coarse partial sampling (DocPTBench), making it impossible to perform controlled, factor-wise attribution of performance degradation to specific physical perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262617/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each test sample is a physically captured image of a printed document, produced at 1200 dpi via a Canon C5840 printer and photographed with heterogeneous mobile devices (Apple, Xiaomi, OPPO at ~3:1:1 ratio) to span diverse ISP and sensor characteristics. The 1,355 digital source images from OmniDocBench v1.5 are each reconstructed into five physical variants (6,775 total), with ground-truth annotations (layout, tables, formulas, text, reading order) inherited unmodified from the digital originals, enabling strict one-to-one comparison. Evaluation outputs are scored on four sub-tasks: full-text OCR (NED), formula recognition (CDM), table reconstruction (TEDS), and reading-order consistency (RO).

## Method

The benchmark's core mechanism is a full-scale, one-to-one physical reconstruction of the entire OmniDocBench v1.5 test set across five orthogonal scenarios—Scanning, Warping, Screen-Photography, Illumination, and Skew—each containing five distinct sub-conditions (e.g., Warping: folding, cylinder, crumpling, corner, book). Scale-aware printing (A3/A4 based on native dimensions) preserves original font-size ratios and layout densities, while the heterogeneous capture matrix introduces realistic ISP noise, lens distortion, and sensor variability. A multi-stage quality pipeline combines VLM-committee anomaly detection with three rounds of expert manual verification to ensure semantic correspondence, structural integrity, and geometric orthogonality, while intentionally retaining authentic artifacts (moiré, minor noise) to reflect real deployment conditions. This paired digital-physical design transforms physical distortions from uncontrolled confounders into independent variables, enabling rigorous factor-wise diagnosis of whether failures stem from geometric distortions, optical artifacts, or inherent model limitations.

## Results

On Real5-OmniDocBench (6,775 physical images across 5 scenarios), PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (0.9B) achieves the best Overall score of 93.19, outperforming the largest generalist models: Qwen3-VL-235B (88.90), Gemini-3 Pro (89.24), and Kimi K2.5 (89.09). The best General VLM, Gemini-3 Pro, scores 89.24 Overall with per-scenario scores ranging from 88.86 (Screen-Photography) to 89.47 (Scanning). Traditional pipeline tools (PP-StructureV3: 64.45, Marker-1.8.2: 60.10) suffer the most severe degradation, dropping to 37.98 and 41.27 respectively under Skew, highlighting their vulnerability to 3D perspective distortions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (0.9B) achieves the highest Overall score (93.19) across all 20 compared methods, beating the strongest General VLM Gemini-3 Pro (89.24) by 3.95 points and the 235B-parameter Qwen3-VL-235B (88.90) by 4.29 points. Among specialized VLMs, it leads GLM-OCR (90.32) by 2.87 points and MinerU2.5-Pro (88.94) by 4.25 points, while also outperforming its own predecessor PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 (92.05) by 1.14 points. The most striking gap appears in the Skew scenario, where PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 scores 92.66 versus 89.45 for Gemini-3 Pro and a mere 37.98 for PP-StructureV3, a 54.68-point margin. Pipeline tools (Marker-1.8.2, PP-StructureV3) are consistently the weakest, scoring 20–50 points below the best VLMs, with their Skew scores (41.27, 37.98) revealing a fundamental inability to handle perspective distortion. The paper's central empirical finding is that parameter scale does not predict physical robustness: the 0.9B PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 outperforms models up to 266× larger (Kimi K2.5, 1T), suggesting domain-specific inductive biases matter more than raw capacity for real-world document parsing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OmniDocBench v1.5, performing a complete one-to-one physical reconstruction of all 1,355 test images across five controlled real-world scenarios. It departs from prior real-world document benchmarks (WildDoc, DocPTBench) by combining authentic physical capture with strict digital ground-truth correspondence, enabling factor-wise attribution of performance degradation that neither uncontrolled in-the-wild datasets nor partial physical simulations can provide. The evaluation protocol inherits OmniDocBench's multi-dimensional scoring (NED, CDM, TEDS), positioning the contribution as a controlled robustness extension rather than a new parsing formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark quantifies a substantial 'reality gap' between digital-born and physically captured documents, revealing that even top-performing VLMs degrade significantly under warping, skew, and illumination perturbations. The counter-intuitive finding that a 0.9B specialized model outperforms 235B–1T generalist models under physical stress challenges the prevailing scaling paradigm and suggests domain-specific inductive biases matter more than raw parameter count for geometric and optical robustness. Open questions include whether this gap can be closed through targeted augmentation, whether the five controlled scenarios fully capture the diversity of real-world acquisition, and whether the benchmark's controlled settings (single printer, limited device matrix) generalize to truly uncontrolled deployment. A notable limitation is that the top-performing model (PaddleOCR-VL-1.6) is from the same team that constructed the benchmark, introducing a potential evaluation bias.
