## Problem

Prior work showed that unsupervised CNNs reproduce human-like graded biases in slant-from-texture perception while supervised CNNs do not, but no study had examined whether Vision-Language Models—trained under fundamentally different objectives and architectures—can perceive and express texture-based geometric cues in a graded manner. This paper asks whether VLMs exhibit the same systematic perceptual biases as humans and unsupervised CNNs, or whether their language-grounded training produces a qualitatively different failure mode.

## Input / Output

Each stimulus is a 224×224 synthetic image of two dot-textured flat surfaces forming a dihedral angle, containing no shading, color, or silhouette cues—slant must be inferred solely from texture gradients. The four controlled parameters (optical slant 25°–60°, field of view 5°–60°, curvature sign, and 12 random dot-jitter instantiations) form a 200-condition factorial design split into 2000 training and 400 test images. The model receives the image plus a text prompt (varying across 7 regression and 3 classification variants in detail, natural/technical style, and free-text/JSON format) and must output either a continuous slant angle in degrees or a binary convex/concave label.

## Method

The paper is an empirical diagnostic study rather than a new architecture. It evaluates six open-source VLM families across multiple scales under zero-shot, in-context, and supervised fine-tuning (LoRA on Qwen2.5-VL-3B) conditions, using 24 prompt variants to rule out linguistic confounds. The key mechanistic contribution is layer-wise linear probing (ridge regression) on the vision encoders of four architecturally distinct VLMs (Qwen-native ViT, CLIP-ViT, SigLIP, VQ-VAE), showing that optical slant, physical slant, FOV, and curvature sign are all linearly decodable with R² > 0.82 from mid-to-late vision layers, and that a single linear readout on post-projector tokens recovers continuous slant (R² up to 0.88, producing 342+ unique angles out of 400). Attention-head ablation across 504–784 heads in two Qwen models confirms that anchoring is a distributed property of the language model, not attributable to any single head. The central finding is a language readout bottleneck: the vision encoder encodes the geometry faithfully, but the language model cannot express it as a graded continuous value, instead collapsing predictions to a small set of discrete anchors (0°, ±25°, ±45°) regardless of stimulus parameters.

## Results

In zero-shot settings, all evaluated VLMs collapse to discrete anchor values (0°, ±25°, ±45°) with median slant errors above 40° and curvature-sign accuracy near chance (~50%), regardless of prompt detail, style, or in-context examples. After supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of Qwen2.5-VL-3B, mean absolute slant error drops from 45.1° to 15.3° and curvature-sign accuracy rises from 50% to 86.10%, though residual anchoring persists at a finer scale. Linear probing of the vision encoder reveals that geometric information is strongly encoded: optical slant R²=0.988, physical slant R²=0.826, FOV R²=0.884, and curvature accuracy 0.983 on mean-pooled post-merger tokens. A single linear readout on LM-input vision tokens recovers continuous slant at R²=0.696–0.880 across three VLMs, confirming the failure is at the language-output interface rather than in the vision representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the unsupervised CNN of Wang et al. [47], the SFT VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) achieves 86.10% curvature-sign accuracy versus the CNN's 96.4%, a gap of roughly 10 percentage points, and its slant error distribution remains qualitatively different (residual anchoring bands vs. smooth Gaussian-like errors). Against the human psychophysical data of Todd et al. [41], the SFT VLM's 86.10% curvature accuracy essentially matches the human average of 86%, and its error pattern (asymmetric convex-vs-concave flips, FOV-dependent degradation) mirrors the human biases B1–B3, though the VLM's errors are more pronounced at low FOVs. However, zero-shot VLMs perform far worse than both baselines: their predictions show no monotonic dependence on optical slant, FOV, or curvature sign, in stark contrast to the graded, systematic biases exhibited by both humans and unsupervised CNNs. The probing results further show that the VLM vision encoder encodes geometry at a level comparable to a standalone ViT-MAE backbone (FOV R²=0.884 vs. 0.874; PS R²=0.826 vs. 0.856), indicating the bottleneck is not in visual encoding but in the language readout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the slant-from-texture perceptual paradigm established by Todd et al. and previously applied to CNNs by Wang et al. to the VLM setting, asking whether the dissociation between unsupervised (human-like biases) and supervised (unbiased) CNNs carries over to models trained with multimodal alignment and next-token prediction objectives. It also situates its findings within the growing literature on VLM geometric failures (shape-blindness, prompt-induced hallucination) but departs from those works by using tightly controlled synthetic stimuli that isolate texture gradients as the sole geometric cue, rather than naturalistic images. The probing methodology draws on linear-probing techniques to localize the failure within the model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that geometric information is strongly encoded in VLM vision encoders (R² > 0.9) yet systematically discarded at the language readout—reframes VLM geometric failures as an expression problem rather than a perception problem, with implications for how multimodal models are designed and evaluated. Open questions include whether the cross-entropy training objective fundamentally limits continuous-value expression, whether richer cues or alternative output modalities (e.g., regression heads) can bypass the anchoring, and whether the low-FOV performance gap reflects a resolution limitation or a genuine perceptual deficit. The paper is limited to polka-dot textures and a specific set of open-source models, so generalization to natural textures and frontier closed-source systems remains to be confirmed.
