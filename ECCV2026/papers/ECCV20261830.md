## Problem

Multi-person human mesh recovery in video requires simultaneously detecting, reconstructing, and tracking humans with consistent identities, but existing approaches decompose this into sequential detection, HMR, and tracking stages that accumulate errors, increase latency, and struggle with occlusions and identity switches. No prior method unifies prompt-based user control with multi-person HMR and tracking in a single end-to-end trainable framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261830/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames at 1288×1288 resolution processed framewise, along with optional bounding-box prompts that initialize or re-initialize tracking queries for user-specified individuals. A fixed perspective camera model (fixed FOV, centered principal point) is assumed. Outputs are per-person SMPL parameters (24 joint rotations, 10 shape coefficients, 3D camera-frame translation), 2D bounding boxes, and confidence scores; only predictions exceeding a confidence threshold are retained, and tracking IDs are maintained via persistent query embeddings rather than a separate association module.

## Method

DETRAM extends a DETR-style transformer decoder (with a DINOv2 ViT encoder) by introducing three complementary query types decoded jointly in a single pass: learned detection queries that discover new humans, identity-persistent tracking queries propagated across frames via Track-Modulated Cross-Attention (TMCA), and user-initialized prompt queries. TMCA updates each tracking query's content embedding by attending to current-frame detection queries using anchor-projected cross-attention with top-k softmax, preserving identity while absorbing new spatial context. Cross-attention within the decoder uses a Conditional-DETR formulation where content embeddings modulate positional encodings for spatially selective query-image matching. A parameter-free key-value memory bank (inspired by XMem) stores M feature slots per active track to stabilize identity under long occlusions, scaling as O(N_t·M·D) rather than with video length. Training combines Hungarian-matching, denoising, and tracking losses under a two-stage protocol (image-based pretraining followed by multi-frame video fine-tuning), with tracking queries constructed from perturbed ground-truth boxes at training time for robustness to localization noise.

## Results

On PoseTrack21 (with ignore-region fix), DETRAM achieves MOTA 73.6 and IDF1 80.6, reducing false negatives to 26,620 (vs. 30,394 for CoMotion) while running at 10.87 FPS — 1.9× faster than CoMotion and 21× faster than 4DHumans. On MuPoTS-3D, DETRAM attains MOTA 95.9, IDF1 97.4, and HOTA 71.3. On BEDLAM, it achieves MOTA 99.18, IDF1 99.37, only 37 ID switches, MPJPE 48.68 mm, and PVE 56.33 mm. On Dyna3DPW/3DPW, DETRAM reaches MOTA 99.8, IDF1 99.9, HOTA 79.1, MPJPE 60.0 mm, and PVE 70.1 mm with zero ID switches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DETRAM outperforms CoMotion [36] on all tracking metrics across every benchmark: on PoseTrack21 it gains +2.2 MOTA and +1.1 IDF1 while halving inference time (10.87 vs. 5.68 FPS); on BEDLAM it reduces ID switches from 885 to 37 and improves MPJPE from 55.40 to 48.68 mm; on 3DPW it lifts HOTA from 72.8 to 79.1. Against MaQ [26] on BEDLAM, DETRAM wins on MOTA (99.18 vs. 95.15), IDs (37 vs. 128), MPJPE (48.68 vs. 79.88), and PVE (56.33 vs. 86.40), but trails on HOTA (95.90 vs. 97.73), which the authors attribute to differences in 2D bounding-box definitions. On 3DPW, DETRAM beats MaQ on MOTA (99.8 vs. 99.6), HOTA (79.1 vs. 70.8), and PVE (70.1 vs. 84.9), though MaQ achieves a slightly lower PA-MPJPE (44.7 vs. 38.3 — actually DETRAM wins here too at 38.3 vs. 44.7). DETRAM also substantially outperforms the multi-stage pipeline baselines (Fast-RCNN+SMPLer-X+ByteTrack, Multi-HMR+ByteTrack, BEV+ByteTrack) on both tracking and reconstruction, confirming the advantage of the unified single-stage design. The only consistent loss is PA-MPJPE on BEDLAM (31.24 vs. 24.50 for CoMotion) and 3DPW (38.3 vs. 37.3), which the authors attribute to CoMotion's access to additional training data (DanceTrack, InstaVariety).

## Relation to Prior Work

DETRAM extends the DETR-style set-prediction paradigm (DETR, DAB-DETR, DN-DETR, Conditional DETR) from single-frame object detection to video-level multi-person human mesh recovery and identity tracking. It departs from the dominant multi-stage pipeline design (detector → HMR → tracker) by unifying all three tasks in a single transformer decoder with three complementary query types: learned detection queries, identity-persistent tracking queries updated via Track-Modulated Cross-Attention, and user-initialized prompt queries. Compared to concurrent end-to-end methods like CoMotion (which decouples per-frame detection from a learned pose-update network) and MaQ (which uses clip-level motion queries with a separate temporal module), DETRAM's key architectural distinction is a single framewise decoder where all query types are decoded jointly, with only DETRAM exposing prompt queries for user-directed tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DETRAM is the first framework to unify promptability with multi-person HMR and tracking in an end-to-end trainable architecture, enabling user-directed human analysis in streaming video for applications in sports analytics, safety monitoring, and mixed reality. The 1.9× speedup over CoMotion and 21× over 4DHumans makes it practically deployable in real-time settings. Open questions include whether the current bounding-box-only prompting can be extended to points, masks, or text; whether explicit temporal smoothness constraints or motion priors would further improve coherence; and whether the slight HOTA gap on BEDLAM relative to MaQ reflects a fundamental limitation of the framewise (vs. clip-level) formulation or is merely an artifact of bounding-box evaluation conventions.
