## Problem

Camera-based BEV encoders for online HD map construction rely exclusively on ego-centric supervision, which forces global spatial structure to be inferred from partial, perspective-distorted observations. At long range, visual cues become sparse and supervision acts only through downstream semantic losses after decoding, providing no direct constraint on intermediate BEV feature geometry. This leads to locally inconsistent and fragmented representations, particularly in distant regions where ego-centric evidence is limited.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes 6 synchronized multi-camera RGB images and outputs vectorized map elements (road boundaries, lane dividers, pedestrian crossings) as polylines within a defined Region of Interest (60×30 m or 100×50 m). During training, an additional ego-aligned aerial crop is fed to a frozen ResUNet teacher that produces a 256×50×100 BEV feature map; the camera student produces a same-shaped feature map via BEVFormer-style deformable attention. The aerial input is used only in the training loss path and is absent at deployment, keeping the inference pipeline identical to the StreamMapNet baseline.

## Method

Cross-View Supervision (CVS) aligns the camera-based BEV student's intermediate features with those of a frozen aerial teacher through a dense MSE loss in shared BEV space, rather than adding auxiliary semantic segmentation losses. A per-channel Affine Adapter (learnable scale γ and shift β) is inserted in the student's loss path to compensate for modality-induced feature distribution mismatches, and both feature maps are channel-wise normalized before computing the alignment objective. The total training loss combines the standard Focal classification loss, L1 line-regression loss, and the weighted BEV alignment term (λ_bev = 60 or 70 depending on RoI). The key insight is that a perspective-privileged overhead view exposes globally consistent road topology and long-range connectivity that ego-centric cameras cannot, and transferring this structure at the feature level—rather than through discretized semantic targets—yields substantially larger gains at extended spatial ranges (+9.9 mAP, +44% relative at 100×50 m) while leaving the inference architecture, runtime, and input requirements completely unchanged.

## Results

On the AID4AD dataset (nuScenes with ego-aligned aerial imagery), Cross-View Supervision (CVS) improves mAP from 34.1 to 38.0 (+3.9 absolute, +11% relative) in the standard 60×30 m region and from 22.4 to 32.3 (+9.9 absolute, +44% relative) in the extended 100×50 m region, all under identical camera-only inference. Gains are consistent across all semantic classes (pedestrian crossings, road dividers, lane boundaries), with the largest per-class improvement on pedestrian crossings at long range (+14.7 AP). The method requires no additional sensors or architectural changes at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole main baseline is StreamMapNet [44], the current strong baseline for camera-based online HD map construction. CVS outperforms it on every metric in both evaluation regions: +3.9 mAP (60×30 m) and +9.9 mAP (100×50 m), with the relative gain growing from 11% to 44% as the spatial extent increases, indicating that aerial supervision is most beneficial where ego-centric observations become sparse. As a secondary comparison, the authors train StreamMapNet with a MapTRv2-style auxiliary segmentation loss on the same 100×50 m setup; this yields only a marginal improvement (22.4 → 23.3 mAP), far below CVS's 32.3 mAP, demonstrating that a conventional auxiliary BEV-level objective does not capture the structural transfer achieved by cross-view feature alignment. No other external baselines (e.g., MapTR, VectorMapNet, HDMapNet) are directly benchmarked in the main table, so the comparison is limited to the StreamMapNet baseline and the auxiliary-loss ablation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on StreamMapNet's streaming BEV mapping framework, adding a training-time feature-level supervision branch that distills structural priors from a frozen aerial encoder (ResUNet) into the camera-based BEVFormer backbone. It departs from prior cross-modal distillation work (DistillBEV, MapDistill) that transfers LiDAR cues within the ego-centric sensing domain, and from diffusion-based structural regularization (BEVDiffuser, MapDiffusion), by instead leveraging a perspective-privileged overhead view as the teacher signal. The affine adapter and channel-wise normalization are motivated by One-for-All's findings on heterogeneous feature transfer, and the approach is explicitly contrasted with conventional auxiliary semantic losses (e.g., MapTRv2-style segmentation heads) that supervise BEV features indirectly through discretized map targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44% relative mAP gain at the extended 100×50 m range, achieved with zero additional inference cost, demonstrates that perspective-privileged structural supervision can substantially improve long-range spatial coherence in camera-only BEV mapping. The finding that a higher-accuracy aerial teacher (ResUNet++) produces a worse student than a lower-accuracy one (ResUNet) challenges the common assumption that teacher quality monotonically determines distillation success, pointing to transferable spatial structure as the critical factor. Key limitations include the dependency on precisely ego-aligned aerial imagery (currently only available via AID4AD for nuScenes), validation on a single dataset and baseline architecture, and the open question of whether the approach generalizes to other tasks (occupancy, motion forecasting) or sensor configurations.
