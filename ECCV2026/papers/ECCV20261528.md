## Problem

Contemporary monocular depth estimation methods, whether based on per-pixel regression or 3D point-cloud representations, consistently produce erroneous depth discontinuities at object contact regions (e.g., where a laptop meets a desk). This stems from the absence of explicit contact-structure awareness during depth decoding: neither paradigm provides direct guidance on where depth continuity should be preserved across physically adjacent surfaces, leaving depth estimation unstable in regions of dense object interaction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces an affine-invariant depth map (defined up to a global scale and translation) along with a dense 3D point map, following the MoGe formulation. An intermediate contact boundary probability map is also generated and can be interactively refined by a user who manually specifies missing contact boundaries for targeted local depth correction. All depth and point outputs are evaluated under scale-invariant, affine-invariant, and locally affine-invariant alignment protocols.

## Method

The framework augments MoGe with a two-stage contact-boundary pipeline. First, a MuGE multi-granularity edge detector produces a dense edge map, which is then filtered by a SigLIP-based module that samples cross-boundary feature differences along local normals to classify each edge pixel as a true inter-object contact boundary; this filtering is trained with a BCE loss plus a curve-level variance loss using automatically generated ground truth from depth consistency checks. Second, a ResNet-18 encoder lifts the resulting contact boundary map into a multi-scale feature space that is fused with DINOv2 image features before the MoGe decoder, enabling boundary-aware depth prediction. Training is further regularized by four boundary-specific losses: point-wise depth alignment at contact pixels, first-order gradient consistency projected onto the ground-truth gradient direction, a smoothness term suppressing spurious oscillations, and an auxiliary consistency loss that constrains the decoder to reproduce the frozen MoGe output when the boundary map is zeroed out, preventing over-reliance on unreliable boundary cues.

## Results

On the iBims-1 benchmark under affine-invariant depth evaluation, the method reduces Rel^d from 2.74 (MoGe) to 2.67 and improves δ1^d from 97.9% to 98.1%, while under scale-invariant depth it drops Rel^d from 3.46 to 3.35 and raises δ1^d from 97.0% to 97.7%. Contact-region-specific evaluation (Table 3) shows more pronounced gains: on iBims-1, Rel^d drops from 1.71 to 1.25 and on NYUv2 from 1.59 to 1.31, confirming that boundary-aware refinement most benefits the targeted contact areas. Across all three benchmarks (NYUv2, iBims-1, HAMMER) and all alignment settings, the method consistently matches or exceeds MoGe and outperforms all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is MoGe, which the method extends; improvements are consistent but modest on global metrics (e.g., affine-invariant Rel^d on iBims-1: 2.74→2.67) yet substantially larger in contact regions (Rel^d: 1.71→1.25 on iBims-1, 1.59→1.31 on NYUv2). Against Depth Anything V2, the method wins on all three benchmarks under affine-invariant depth (e.g., iBims-1: 2.67 vs. 3.44 Rel^d) and on NYUv2/HAMMER under scale-invariant depth, though DA V2 retains a slight edge on HAMMER scale-invariant δ1^d (99.1 vs. 98.5). UniDepth is outperformed on iBims-1 across all settings (e.g., affine-invariant Rel^d: 2.67 vs. 3.76) and on NYUv2, but UniDepth edges ahead on HAMMER scale-invariant Rel^d (3.55 vs. 3.74). Metric3D V2 is beaten on iBims-1 (2.67 vs. 3.28) and NYUv2 (2.90 vs. 3.94) under affine-invariant depth, though Metric3D V2 leads on HAMMER (3.02 vs. 2.98 is actually a win for Ours; correction: Ours 2.98 vs. Metric3D V2 3.02). DUSt3R, ZoeDepth, DepthFM, SharpNet, and DA V1 are all clearly outperformed by 1–10+ points in Rel^d across benchmarks, with the largest gaps on iBims-1 where the method's boundary-aware refinement is most effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MoGe, an affine-invariant monocular geometry estimator that predicts dense 3D point maps, by adding explicit contact-boundary awareness to the depth decoding pipeline. It departs from prior edge-aware depth estimation work (which treats edges as generic indicators of depth discontinuity) by reframing contact boundaries as regions where depth should remain *continuous* despite strong image gradients, and from 3D point-cloud-based methods (MoGe, DUSt3R) that impose geometric consistency but lack any mechanism to identify where physical contact occurs. The boundary detection pipeline repurposes MuGE (multi-granularity edge detection) and SigLIP (vision-language features) in a novel filtering stage, while the boundary-aware fusion and loss terms are new architectural and training contributions layered on top of the frozen MoGe backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Depth discontinuities at object contacts are a persistent, visually obvious artifact across all current MDE methods, and this paper provides a targeted, practical fix that requires only 4,000 training images and a single A100 GPU. The interactive refinement capability (user-specified boundaries) adds a useful human-in-the-loop dimension for applications like robotics or AR where contact geometry is critical. However, the global metric improvements are modest (e.g., 3.46→3.35 Rel^d on iBims-1), the evaluation is limited to three indoor benchmarks, and the automatic boundary extraction can still miss or misidentify contacts, leaving open questions about robustness in outdoor scenes, dynamic contacts, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the MoGe architecture.
