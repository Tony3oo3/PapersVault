## Problem

Naively combining Mixture-of-Experts with discrete diffusion vision-language models causes expert selection collapse on visual tokens, where most image tokens are routed to a tiny subset of experts and multimodal performance degrades sharply. The paper identifies the root cause not as a router optimization failure but as a geometric mismatch: visual embeddings from deep Pre-Norm vision encoders carry L2 norms roughly 167× larger than text embeddings, inducing attention drowning and representational inertia that make visual tokens geometrically indistinguishable to the router.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (processed by SigLIP2-SO400M-Patch14-384 into a sequence of visual tokens) and a text prompt, which are interleaved into a single sequence of up to 8192 tokens. Output is a text response generated via iterative masked denoising: starting from a fully masked response, the model progressively unmaskes tokens in a bidirectional context, unlike autoregressive left-to-right generation. The visual tokens are projected into the language embedding space by a 2-layer MLP with GELU activation before entering the diffusion language tower.

## Method

TowerAlign is a lightweight post-projector norm alignment that applies a single global scalar (computed as the ratio of target text norm mean to the batch-averaged visual token norm) to rescale all projected visual tokens, matching their magnitude to text norm statistics while preserving relative norm ratios among visual tokens. This structure-preserving compression is critical: it maintains the norm disparity between ViT Sink tokens (global semantic carriers) and Non-Sink tokens (local features), which a per-token RMSNorm baseline would erase and which the paper shows is necessary for effective MoE routing. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that high-norm embeddings under Pre-Norm updates inevitably converge to a low-dimensional manifold, formally linking the norm gap to the observed effective-rank collapse (2.1/2048 for visual vs. 1673.3/2048 for text tokens). The full system is instantiated on LLaDA-MoE-7B-A1B (64 experts, top-8 routing) with a SigLIP2 vision encoder, trained via the standard two-stage LLaVA paradigm (projector pretraining on LCS-558K, then end-to-end instruction tuning on LLaVA-NeXT 780K), and the paper demonstrates that routing-side regularizations like load-balancing losses or Gumbel-Softmax noise fail to fix the collapse, confirming the upstream geometric diagnosis.

## Results

TowerAlign (7B-A1B MoE-dVLM) achieves 59.0 on ChartQA, 67.7 on DocVQA, 73.3 on MMBench(EN), and 40.3 on MMMU(val), consistently matching or exceeding the dense 8B diffusion baseline LLaDA-VLM under matched data budgets (0.6M pretrain + 0.8M SFT). The sparse model uses only 1B active parameters per token versus 8B for the dense baseline, yet gains +1.6 on ChartQA, +6.5 on DocVQA, and +1.4 on MMBench. The largest remaining gap is to LLaDA-V (78.3 ChartQA, 83.9 DocVQA), which benefits from 20× more instruction-tuning data (16M vs. 0.8M SFT samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the matched-data dense baseline LLaDA-VLM (8B, same 0.6M+0.8M data), TowerAlign wins on 8 of 10 benchmarks, with the largest gains on DocVQA (+6.5) and ChartQA (+1.6), while suffering small regressions on MMMU-Pro standard (−0.2) and vision (−0.6). Compared to the autoregressive LLaVA-1.6 (7B), TowerAlign is substantially stronger on reasoning-heavy tasks (MMBench 73.3 vs. 54.6, AI2D 70.5 vs. 66.6, MMMU 40.3 vs. 35.1) but trails on OCR-centric benchmarks (DocVQA 67.7 vs. 74.4, InfoVQA 25.8 vs. 37.1). Against LaViDa-L (8B diffusion, 1.0M SFT), TowerAlign wins on DocVQA (+8.7) and MMBench (+2.8) but loses on ChartQA (−5.6), InfoVQA (−8.4), and MMMU (−3.0), reflecting LaViDa-L's larger SFT budget. The largest overall gap remains to LLaDA-V, which uses 16M SFT samples (20× more) and leads by 19.3 on ChartQA and 16.2 on DocVQA, indicating that data scale still dominates over architectural efficiency at this stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active threads: discrete masked diffusion language models (LLaDA, LLaDA-MoE), the LLaVA-style MLP-projector VLM paradigm, and sparse MoE scaling. It builds directly on the modality-gap and norm-discrepancy analyses for dense Pre-Norm MLLMs (Li et al. 2025, refs [9,10]) and the ViT Sink-token literature [16], but departs by showing that in the MoE setting the same geometric asymmetry produces a qualitatively different failure—expert selection collapse—that is invisible to router-side diagnostics (gating entropy remains high) and is actively worsened by standard load-balancing regularization. The TowerAlign operator is a targeted, MoE-motivated refinement of the post-projector norm rescaling studied for dense VLMs, with the key design choice (global affine compression over per-token RMSNorm) justified by the need to preserve Sink-token norm hierarchy for routing discrimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, near-zero-cost fix (a single scalar rescale) that unblocks MoE scaling for discrete diffusion VLMs, and its diagnostic framework (norm gap → attention drowning → representational inertia → degenerate top-k) gives practitioners a reusable lens for debugging similar geometric pathologies in other sparse multimodal architectures. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (LLaDA-MoE-7B-A1B) and one vision encoder (SigLIP2), and a persistent gap to the much larger-scale LLaDA-V (16M SFT) on reasoning benchmarks. Open questions include whether the norm-alignment prescription transfers to other MoE routing schemes (e.g., group-limited or expert-choice), to video or multi-image settings, and whether the 167× gap is specific to SigLIP2 or is a general property of deep Pre-Norm vision encoders.
