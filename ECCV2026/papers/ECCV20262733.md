## Problem

Vision-language models achieve strong multimodal performance but their billion-parameter scale makes edge deployment impractical. Existing pruning methods rely on manually designed, unimodal importance heuristics that are blind to the dynamic cross-modal synergies between visual and textual representations, causing severe accuracy collapse when applied to VLMs. No prior framework autonomously discovers pruning algorithms tailored to preserve vision-language alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262733/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The compression pipeline takes a pre-trained VLM (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B) and 128 calibration samples from MSCOCO validation, producing a sparsified model with a binary element-wise mask at a target sparsity (70%) plus optional low-rank weight updates. The compressed VLM retains its original interface: it accepts an RGB image and a text prompt, and outputs a generated caption or VQA answer. The RL discovery agent itself consumes structured prompts containing baseline code, historical metrics, and task objectives, and emits complete Python pruning scripts that are validated in a sandbox before full-model execution.

## Method

KATANA frames pruning algorithm discovery as executable program synthesis: an LLM agent (DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Lite-Instruct with LoRA) iteratively generates Python pruning scripts, which are executed in an isolated sandbox and scored by a multi-objective reward balancing CIDEr accuracy, per-token decode latency, sparsity attainment, novelty, diversity, and stability. Policy updates use GRPO with a 4-phase curriculum that shifts emphasis from latency exploration to quality preservation. The flagship discovered algorithm, KIRI, couples a cubic sparsity schedule (suppressing activation variance spikes by 15% versus linear schedules) with a Dual-Norm Activation (DNA) importance metric that multiplicatively fuses weight magnitudes, row/column L2 structural norms, and calibrated input-channel activation statistics. For sparsity above 60%, KIRI applies an offline SVD-based low-rank reconstruction projected strictly onto the sparse mask, recovering 98% of a constrained solver's accuracy at zero inference overhead. The full 1,000-iteration search (480 A100 GPU-hours) is a one-time cost; the resulting strategy transfers zero-shot to other VLM architectures.

## Results

At 70% sparsity on MSCOCO (Karpathy split), KIRI achieves a CIDEr score of 84.6±0.2 on LLaVA-1.5-7B, outperforming the dense baseline's 91.8 by retaining 92.2% of captioning quality while delivering a 2.8× per-token decode speedup (0.060s vs. 0.17s). KIRI extends this advantage across all four architectures: +0.6 CIDEr over GSOP on BLIP-2, +1.1 on Qwen2.5-7B, and +0.9 on Llama-3.2-Vision. On cross-dataset generalization (Flickr30k, NoCaps), KIRI maintains its lead with CIDEr 51.5 and 46.4 respectively, confirming the DNA metric prevents domain-specific overfitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KIRI outperforms all 16 baselines on CIDEr across every architecture at 70% sparsity. Traditional magnitude-based and one-shot solvers (Magnitude, SparseGPT, RIA) suffer severe cross-modal collapse, scoring 36.8–77.2 CIDEr on LLaVA-1.5-7B versus KIRI's 84.6. Activation-aware methods (Wanda, LLM-Pruner, GMP) improve to 70–77 CIDEr but remain 7–15 points below KIRI, confirming that unimodal proxy metrics are insufficient for VLMs. Token-pruning approaches (Eff-LLaVA, AutoPrune, ToMe, FastV, SparseVLM, FasterVLM) narrow the gap to 80–83 CIDEr by discarding visual tokens, yet still trail KIRI by 1.4–4.8 points. The closest competitors are the adaptive/hybrid paradigms: GSOP (83.3), TopV (83.0), SCOPE (82.8), and FEATHER (82.5) on LLaVA-1.5-7B, with KIRI winning by 1.3, 1.6, 1.8, and 2.1 CIDEr respectively. KIRI also matches or slightly beats these methods on latency (0.060s vs. 0.061s for GSOP), meaning the accuracy gain comes without any speed penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

KATANA carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-driven program synthesis (Eureka, FunSearch) and neural network pruning, but departs sharply from both lineages. Prior RL-based compression (AMC, AutoCompress, RL-Pruner) restricted the agent to hyperparameter or layer-allocation spaces within a fixed algorithmic template, while prior pruning methods (Wanda, SparseGPT, SCOPE, GSOP) relied on hand-engineered scoring metrics. KATANA instead lets an LLM agent write and iteratively refine complete Python pruning programs via GRPO, jointly discovering the scoring function (DNA metric), the sparsity schedule (cubic), and the reconstruction logic in a single end-to-end search—moving the field from tuning parameters inside a human-designed algorithm to synthesizing the algorithm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a one-time 480 A100 GPU-hour search that yields a zero-shot transferable pruning strategy delivering 2.8× decode speedup at 70% sparsity across four VLM architectures, which is directly relevant for edge and latency-critical deployment. However, the margins over the strongest baseline (GSOP) are modest (0.6–1.3 CIDEr), the method is restricted to unstructured sparsity (no native N:M tensor-core acceleration), and the acknowledged suboptimality of the SVD-onto-mask projection leaves room for tighter constrained solvers. Open questions include whether the RL-discovered topology generalizes to structured sparsity, broader compositional reasoning tasks, and whether the 480 GPU-hour discovery cost can be amortized across a family of models or reduced with smaller proxy agents.
