## Problem

Text-driven editing of diffusion models is fundamentally limited by the descriptive bottleneck of natural language: fine-grained visual deltas such as subtle facial structure changes, precise texture shifts, or nuanced style transformations are self-evident in image pairs but extremely difficult to specify in prompts. Moreover, unlike GANs whose compact latent spaces admit well-studied disentangled directions, diffusion models lack a similarly structured space, making it hard to achieve precise, isolated attribute control without per-image optimization or model fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During the one-time learning phase, ViDiT consumes N=1000 before-and-after image pairs (e.g., from StyleGAN2 on FFHQ, AFHQ-Cats, or AFHQ-Dogs) and outputs a single continuous direction vector d in Stable Diffusion's text-embedding space. At inference, any target RGB image (real photograph, synthetic portrait, or stylized artwork) is paired with d and an intensity scale λ_e; for real images, DDPM Inversion first maps the input into the model's latent trajectory. Multiple independently learned directions can be composed additively at inference to produce multi-attribute edits without retraining.

## Method

ViDiT learns a single global direction d by optimizing it against a dual-objective loss while keeping both the denoising network ε_θ and a frozen CLIP image encoder E_I fixed. The Semantic Alignment Loss (L_sem) is a contrastive term that pushes d toward the edited images and away from the originals in CLIP feature space, providing a global semantic anchor. The Latent Alignment Loss (L_latent) maximizes the L2 difference between the denoising network's noise predictions on the noised edited image and the noised original at random timesteps, grounding d in the model's dense, spatially-aligned prediction space to preserve fine-grained identity cues. At inference, d is injected into the Classifier-Free Guidance formulation as an additional residual term scaled by λ_e, replacing the text-conditioning signal entirely; this requires no per-image optimization and generalizes zero-shot across domains. The approach is source-agnostic: directions can be learned from GAN latent spaces, domain-adaptation models, or diffusion-based editors, and the learned direction acts as a reusable semantic token in the model's vocabulary.

## Results

On 200 input-edit pairs for 'Asian' and 'Smile' semantics, ViDiT achieves the best identity preservation across all metrics: LPIPS 0.030 (vs. 0.059 for InstructPix2Pix), DINO 0.929 (vs. 0.867 for Prompt2Prompt), and DreamSim 0.905 (vs. 0.877 for InstructPix2Pix), while remaining competitive on semantic alignment (CLIP-T 0.407 vs. 0.408 for Prompt2Prompt). In the visual-conditioning comparison on 100 generated samples, ViDiT leads on all identity metrics (CLIP-I 0.831, SigLIP-I 0.913, DINO 0.886) over Attribute-Control, IP-Adapter, W+Adapter, and Weights2Weights. A 40-participant user study confirms ViDiT's superiority with a mean preference score of 3.36/5, edging out Concept Sliders (3.25) and substantially outperforming Prompt2Prompt (2.74), InstructPix2Pix (2.06), and SEGA (1.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViDiT decisively outperforms all four text-driven editing baselines on identity preservation: it reduces LPIPS by 49% over InstructPix2Pix (0.030 vs. 0.059) and by 83% over SEGA (0.179), while boosting DINO by 7.2 points over Prompt2Prompt (0.929 vs. 0.867) and by 21.5 points over SEGA (0.714). On semantic alignment (CLIP-T), ViDiT (0.407) is essentially tied with Prompt2Prompt (0.408) and InstructPix2Pix (0.403), and substantially ahead of Concept Sliders (0.325) and SEGA (0.388); it trails InstructPix2Pix by 0.006 on SigLIP-T, a negligible gap. In the visual-conditioning comparison (Table 2), ViDiT leads on all three identity metrics (CLIP-I, SigLIP-I, DINO) over Attribute-Control, IP-Adapter, W+Adapter, and Weights2Weights, while matching or exceeding them on text-alignment metrics. The user study (Table 4) confirms these quantitative advantages perceptually: ViDiT's 3.36/5 preference score beats the next-best Concept Sliders (3.25) and leaves a 0.62-point gap over Prompt2Prompt (2.74). The rescoring analysis (Table 5) further validates disentanglement: targeted edits shift the corresponding attribute probability by 28–95% while off-diagonal shifts remain under 20%, with the notable exception of Gender→Beard (−19.8%), which the authors attribute to known biases in the underlying Stable Diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViDiT sits at the intersection of GAN latent-space direction discovery (StyleSpace, GANSpace) and diffusion-model editing, repurposing well-understood GAN editing directions as supervision to learn a single continuous vector in Stable Diffusion's CLIP conditioning space. It departs from text-driven editors (SEGA, Prompt2Prompt, InstructPix2Pix) by replacing linguistic prompts with visual before-and-after deltas, and from adapter-based approaches (Concept Sliders' per-concept LoRAs, W+ Adapter's architectural module) by requiring no additional parameters or fine-tuning—only a frozen denoising network and a learned direction injected through the CFG residual. The DDPM Inversion pipeline for real-image editing is borrowed directly from Huberman-Spiegelglas et al., and the dual-loss design (CLIP contrastive + denoising-prediction alignment) is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Learn Once, Edit Anywhere' framing offers a lightweight, source-agnostic recipe for expanding a diffusion model's editing vocabulary without any weight updates, which is practically attractive for practitioners who need fine-grained attribute control beyond what text prompts can express. However, the single global direction per concept limits expressiveness for spatially varying or localized edits, and disentanglement is bounded by the quality of the supervision source. Open questions include whether the approach extends to object-level or scene-level semantics, whether spatially varying directions (e.g., per-region) are feasible, and how the method behaves when the supervision source itself is poorly disentangled.
