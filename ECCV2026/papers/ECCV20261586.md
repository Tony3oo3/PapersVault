## Problem

Prior work attributed video reasoning in diffusion models to a Chain-of-Frames (CoF) mechanism where reasoning unfolds sequentially across generated frames, but the underlying mechanism remained unexplored. This paper asks where reasoning actually occurs in diffusion-based video models and whether the CoF hypothesis holds, revealing that the true reasoning axis is fundamentally different from what was previously assumed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261586/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis pipeline takes a reference image and text prompt as input to a diffusion video model (e.g., Wan2.2-I2V-A14B) and produces a generated video. Critically, at each diffusion step s the model's estimated clean latent x̂₀ = x_s − σ_s·v_θ(x_s, s, c) is decoded to yield an interpretable snapshot of the model's current hypothesis, enabling step-by-step visualization of intermediate reasoning states. For the TFE method, three independent forward passes with different random noise seeds are executed, and their mid-layer latents (layers 20–29) are spatially-temporally averaged at the first diffusion step before continuing generation.

## Method

The paper's core finding is Chain-of-Steps (CoS): reasoning in diffusion video models unfolds along the denoising trajectory rather than across frames, with early steps exploring multiple candidate solutions (multi-path exploration and superposition-based exploration) that progressively converge in later steps. This is validated through targeted noise perturbation experiments showing that disrupting a single diffusion step collapses performance (0.685→<0.3) while frame-wise noise has minimal impact, and through CKA dissimilarity analysis showing early-step perturbations propagate throughout the entire trajectory. Layer-wise analysis of the DiT backbone reveals that early layers handle dense perceptual grounding, middle layers (≈15–35) perform the critical reasoning computations (confirmed by latent-swapping experiments where swapping one middle layer reverses the grounding result), and final layers consolidate the output. Motivated by these insights, the authors propose Training-Free Ensemble (TFE), which merges mid-layer latents from three identical model runs with different seeds at the first diffusion step, effectively performing a latent-space ensemble that preserves diverse reasoning trajectories and improves performance across four benchmarks without any additional training.

## Results

On VBVR-Bench, VBVR-Wan2.2 + TFE achieves an overall score of 0.716, surpassing the base VBVR-Wan2.2 (0.685) by 3.1 points and the strongest proprietary model Sora 2 (0.546) by 17.0 points. Across four benchmarks, TFE yields relative improvements of 4.3% on VBVR-Bench, 35.6% on V-Reason Bench, 16.9% on MME-CoF, and 6.9% on RISE-Video for VBVR-Wan2.2, with consistent gains also observed for VBVR-Wan2.1 and VBVR-LTX2.3. The method is entirely training-free, requiring only three forward passes with different noise seeds and latent averaging at mid-layers (20–29) during the first diffusion step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VBVR-Wan2.2 + TFE (0.716) outperforms all nine baselines on VBVR-Bench overall. The largest margins are against open-source video models: CogVideoX1.5-5B-I2V and HunyuanVideo-I2V (both 0.273, +44.3 pts), LTX-2 (0.313, +40.3 pts), and the base Wan2.2-I2V-A14B (0.371, +34.5 pts). Among proprietary models, TFE beats Sora 2 (0.546, +17.0 pts), Veo 3.1 (0.480, +23.6 pts), Runway Gen-4 Turbo (0.403, +31.3 pts), and Kling 2.6 (0.369, +34.7 pts). The most competitive comparison is against the unmodified VBVR-Wan2.2 (0.685), where TFE adds +3.1 points overall and +4.5 points on In-Domain, confirming that the latent-ensemble strategy extracts additional reasoning capacity without any parameter updates. Across the four-benchmark evaluation (Table 2), TFE consistently improves every model–benchmark pair except one minor dip on V-Reason Bench for VBVR-LTX2.3 (3.61→3.19), demonstrating broad model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the Chain-of-Frames (CoF) hypothesis introduced by Wiedemer et al. [65] and Wu et al. [69], which attributed video reasoning to sequential unfolding across generated frames. Instead, it proposes Chain-of-Steps (CoS), arguing that reasoning emerges along the diffusion denoising trajectory, drawing conceptual parallels to LLM mechanisms (Chain-of-Thought, Tree of Thoughts, self-correction) and biological planning (hippocampal replay). The analysis builds on the VBVR benchmark [61] and open-source DiT-based video models (Wan2.1/2.2, LTX-2), while the Training-Free Ensemble (TFE) repurposes the model-soups idea [66] at the latent level during early diffusion steps. Layer-wise mechanistic analysis extends the vision-function-layer framework from VLMs [52] into the diffusion transformer setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic mechanistic dissection of how diffusion video models reason, and the CoS finding reframes how the community should think about where and how to intervene to improve video reasoning (e.g., targeting middle DiT layers or early denoising steps rather than frame-level conditioning). TFE provides a simple, training-free, model-agnostic inference-time technique that consistently improves performance across three model families and four benchmarks, making it immediately adoptable. Open questions include whether CoS generalizes to non-diffusion video architectures (e.g., autoregressive or flow-based non-iterative models), whether the middle-layer criticality finding holds across DiT scales, and whether TFE can be extended beyond latent averaging to more structured ensemble strategies. A limitation is that the core CoS claim rests heavily on qualitative visualization of intermediate denoising states, with the strongest quantitative support coming from the noise-perturbation and CKA experiments rather than a dedicated causal intervention on the denoising schedule itself.
