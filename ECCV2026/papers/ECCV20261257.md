## Problem

Medical image segmentation accuracy is increasingly constrained by decoder design rather than encoder capacity: cross-scale semantic misalignment, insufficient global context integration during decoding, and boundary fragmentation persist even with strong pretrained encoders. Existing decoder-centric methods introduce attention and fusion strategies but often rely on additive gating or single-scale descriptors that inadequately preserve fine spatial detail and global semantic consistency across anatomically diverse tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261257/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D medical images (resized to 224×224, or 256×256/384×384 overlapping patches for DRIVE, FIVES, and CellSeg) and produces a full-resolution binary or multi-class segmentation mask. During training, auxiliary edge maps are generated from intermediate decoder features and supervised with binary edge targets derived from ground-truth masks, but only the final segmentation prediction is retained at inference. The architecture is encoder-agnostic via a Universal Feature Projection layer, supporting any PyTorch timm encoder while fixing decoder channels to 64/128/320/512 across stages.

## Method

MedCAGD is a decoder-centric segmentation framework built on a PVTv2-B2 encoder that systematically regulates feature flow through four coupled modules: (i) ECA-MSP performs multi-scale channel recalibration using adaptive average pooling at scales {1,2,4} with 1D cross-channel convolution; (ii) SCA-Gate formulates skip fusion as normalized multiplicative encoder-decoder agreement (θ(E(g))⊙φ(E(x))) combined with global channel modulation and multi-scale spatial competition via parallel depthwise convolutions (kernels 3 and 5) with temperature-controlled softmax; (iii) a Context Aggregator averages projected multi-level encoder features, refines them through Residual Attention (a lightweight non-local spatial attention in residual form), and injects the result residually into each decoding stage; (iv) a Refinement Block applies depthwise+pointwise convolution with GroupNorm/SiLU followed by ECA-MSP for local reconstruction. Training uses BCE loss with weighted deep supervision on intermediate decoder features and auxiliary edge supervision, achieving 30.60M parameters and 5.0 GFLOPs.

## Results

Across 9 binary medical segmentation benchmarks (skin, polyp, fundus, neoplasm, cell), MedCAGD achieves a mean Dice of 88.01%, outperforming the strongest decoder-centric baselines MCADS (85.14%) and EMCAD (84.87%) by 2.9 and 3.1 Dice points respectively, while using only 30.60M parameters and 5.0 GFLOPs. On the Synapse multi-organ dataset, it reaches 87.00 Dice / 83.77 IoU / 14.39 HD95, surpassing MCADS (85.03 Dice) and EMCAD (83.63 Dice). On ACDC cardiac segmentation, it attains 87.54 Dice and 4.41 HD95, the best boundary accuracy among all compared methods (next best: EMCAD at 5.25 HD95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedCAGD outperforms all 21 baselines on the 9-dataset average Dice. Against the strongest decoder-centric competitors, it leads MCADS by +2.87 Dice (88.01 vs 85.14) and EMCAD by +3.14 Dice (88.01 vs 84.87), while using fewer parameters (30.60M vs 50.90M and 26.76M) and lower FLOPs (5.0G vs 61.89G and 5.60G). Transformer-based methods (UCTransNet 84.03, TransUNet 83.92, Swin-UNet 83.19) and Mamba-based methods (Swin-UMamba 81.72, VM-UNet 81.33) trail by 3–7 Dice points. CNN baselines (nnU-Net 82.34, PraNet 82.68, UNet++ 81.23, U-Net 77.14) are outperformed by 5–11 Dice points. On Synapse multi-organ, MedCAGD (87.00 Dice) surpasses MCADS (85.03), EMCAD (83.63), and Self-Prompt SAM (86.74); on ACDC, it achieves the lowest HD95 (4.41) versus EMCAD (5.25) and UCTransNet (5.70). SAM-based foundation models (AutoSAM, Medical SAM3) are only partially comparable due to missing entries but underperform on the datasets where they report results (e.g., Medical SAM3: 55.80 on DRIVE vs 81.63 for MedCAGD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the U-Net encoder-decoder paradigm by replacing the conventional decoder with a context-aware gated architecture, and builds directly on ECA-Net's 1D-convolution channel attention by extending it with multi-scale adaptive pooling (ECA-MSP). Conceptually, it departs from the field's dominant trend of scaling encoder capacity (transformers, Mamba, SAM-based foundation models) and instead argues that the decoder is the primary bottleneck, positioning its SCA-Gate and Context Aggregator as principled alternatives to the additive skip fusion in Attention U-Net and the grouped-convolution gating in EMCAD. The encoder-agnostic Universal Feature Projection design allows the decoder to plug into any PyTorch timm backbone, distinguishing it from tightly coupled encoder-decoder systems like TransUNet or Swin-UNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, efficient (30.6M params, 5.0 GFLOPs) decoder that consistently outperforms strong CNN, Transformer, Mamba, and SAM-based baselines across 11 heterogeneous medical benchmarks, offering a drop-in improvement for practitioners without requiring encoder retraining. Its encoder-agnostic design broadens applicability, and the ablation evidence clearly isolates the contribution of each module (SCA-Gate, CA with RA, RB, BT). However, the work is limited to 2D standardized evaluation; extending to 3D volumetric segmentation, out-of-distribution robustness, and fair comparison with large-scale foundation models at matched compute remain open questions that will determine whether the decoder-centric framing generalizes beyond the current benchmark suite.
