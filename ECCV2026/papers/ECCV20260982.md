## Problem

Existing video-to-video diffusion models treat each editing turn independently, so when users iteratively refine a video, previously generated regions drift or get overwritten across turns. This paper identifies this failure mode as the cross-turn consistency problem in multi-turn video editing and formulates it as a distinct setting where prior edits must function as structured constraints rather than temporal continuations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260982/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For novel view synthesis, the model takes a source video, a target camera trajectory, and a top-k subset of previously generated videos (stored as VAE latents in an external cache indexed by camera pose); for text-guided long video editing, it takes a source video segment, a text instruction, and retrieved prior edited segments (retrieved via DINO feature similarity of source segments). The output is a single edited video per turn that remains visually consistent with all prior iterations. The external cache grows with each turn, but only a bounded number of retrieved videos (top-k) are conditioned upon, keeping the token budget fixed regardless of editing history length.

## Method

Memory-V2V combines three components: (1) task-aware non-temporal retrieval that selects the most relevant prior edits—using a geometric Field-of-View overlap score for camera-conditioned NVS and DINO-based semantic similarity for text-conditioned editing; (2) relevance-aware dynamic tokenization that assigns different spatio-temporal compression factors (1×2×2, 1×4×4, 1×8×8) to retrieved videos based on their rank, preserving fine detail for the most relevant edits while aggressively compressing less relevant ones; and (3) adaptive token merging, a learnable convolutional operator that fuses tokens from low-attention-responsiveness frames before self-attention, applied at DiT blocks 10 and 20 where frame-level importance scores stabilize. Retrieval is non-parametric while tokenization and merging are trained end-to-end via rectified flow matching fine-tuning of pretrained base models (ReCamMaster or LucyEdit) on 32 A100 GPUs, converging in 1–2K steps.

## Results

On multi-turn video novel view synthesis (Table 2), Memory-V2V achieves the best average multi-view consistency score of 0.1357 (lower is better), outperforming ReCam (Ind) at 0.1892 and TrajCrafter at 0.1818, while also reducing rotation error to 1.65° and translation error to 13.47 (vs. 24.23 and 57.44 respectively). On text-guided long video editing (Table 3), Memory-V2V attains the highest Subject Consistency (0.9326), Imaging Quality (0.6759), Temporal Flickering (0.9862), and Motion Smoothness (0.9939) among all seven baselines. The method also reduces inference time from 980.8s (dynamic tokenization only) to 648.5s (full model) via adaptive token merging, a ~34% speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Memory-V2V outperforms all seven baselines on Subject Consistency (0.9326 vs. best baseline RAVE at 0.9220), Imaging Quality (0.6759 vs. DynVFX at 0.6666), Temporal Flickering (0.9862 vs. DynVFX at 0.9855), and Motion Smoothness (0.9939 vs. DynVFX at 0.9937) in the long video editing task. However, it trails FlowEdit on cross-frame DINO similarity (0.8019 vs. 0.8612) and CLIP similarity (0.8741 vs. 0.8943), which the authors note may reflect FlowEdit's tendency to under-edit (overly high similarity can indicate insufficient editing). For video novel view synthesis, Memory-V2V achieves the best average multi-view consistency (0.1357) and lowest camera errors (RotErr 1.65°, TransErr 13.47), surpassing ReCam (Ind) by ~28% in consistency and TrajCrafter by ~25%, while ReCam (AR) shows competitive 2nd-vs-3rd iteration consistency (0.1290) but degrades on 1st-vs-3rd (0.1985). The two LucyEdit variants (Ind and FIFO) serve as the direct single-turn baselines; Memory-V2V improves Subject Consistency by 6.4% over LucyEdit (Ind) and 6.6% over LucyEdit (FIFO). DynVFX achieves the highest Aesthetic Quality (0.5179) but at the cost of lower Subject Consistency (0.9119) and CLIP-F (0.8682), suggesting it edits objects at unintended locations rather than in-place.

## Relation to Prior Work

Memory-V2V builds on single-turn video-to-video diffusion models (ReCamMaster for novel view synthesis, LucyEdit for text-guided editing) and departs from existing memory-augmented long video generators (MALT, WorldMem, VMem) that treat memory as temporal context for autoregressive frame streams. The key conceptual departure is reframing prior edits as structured, task-aware constraints for independently denoised iterations rather than as additional temporal context, combined with non-temporal retrieval (geometric FOV overlap or DINO-based semantic similarity) and relevance-proportional token allocation. The adaptive token merging mechanism draws on recent findings about DiT attention sparsity, while the overall training uses rectified flow matching on pretrained DiT backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-turn iterative editing is the natural workflow for real users, yet prior models silently fail at cross-turn consistency, making this a practically important gap. The framework's generality (applying to both camera-controlled and text-guided editing) and the 90% FLOPs reduction over naive context scaling make it a viable engineering pattern for production video editing pipelines. Limitations include inheriting base-model weaknesses (large view changes) and inability to handle multi-shot videos with abrupt scene transitions, which the authors flag as future work. An open question is whether the constraint-based memory formulation generalizes beyond the two demonstrated tasks to more complex multi-agent or multi-scene editing scenarios.
