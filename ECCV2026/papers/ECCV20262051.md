## Problem

Existing Open-World Object Detection frameworks depend on exemplar replay to prevent catastrophic forgetting, but storing raw data conflicts with privacy constraints in sensitive domains, incurs growing memory overhead as task sequences expand, and contradicts the OWOD objective of prioritizing novel class discovery over revisiting known ones. No prior method achieves strictly rehearsal-free OWOD while maintaining competitive detection precision and unknown discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262051/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images through a PVT backbone and a 6-layer Deformable DETR decoder, operating in an incremental protocol where only current-task labels are available during training while inference must detect all previously encountered categories. Outputs are (Z+1)-dimensional per-query predictions: Z binary known-class scores plus a continuous objectness score in [0,1] that serves as the confidence for unknown pseudo-labels, alongside bounding box regression via L1 and GIoU losses. The framework requires only 2.0M trainable parameters (LoRA ranks 16 for GAs, 10 for SAs) versus 39.7M–106.6M for compared baselines.

## Method

REAL-OW decouples incremental knowledge through a collaborative LoRA adapter architecture: General Adapters injected into the backbone's self-attention q/v projections refine cross-task universal representations under an Adaptive Gated Sparse Loss that dynamically modulates regularization pressure per layer, while Specific Adapters added incrementally to the decoder store task-specific expertise in isolated branches enforced by a Layer-wise Interaction Orthogonal Loss that couples gating signals with actual parameter magnitudes. To counteract representation drift under rehearsal-free constraints, Dual-Stage Objectness Modeling alternates between a Learning phase (aggregating known feature embeddings via CG-NLL distance minimization and assigning soft objectness pseudo-labels to unmatched queries) and a Consolidation phase (sharpening the known-unknown energy boundary via margin loss and KL divergence regularization). The CG-NLL distance rescales standard Mahalanobis distance by an adaptive factor 1+β·ln(1+|Σ|) to account for the progressive dispersion of known-category distributions that accumulates without exemplar replay, maintaining a stable energy landscape for reliable unknown discovery.

## Results

On M-OWODB (Task 4), REAL-OW achieves 26.7% U-Recall and 38.7% mAP (Both), outperforming the strongest exemplar-replay baseline ORTH (24.3% U-Recall, 37.9% mAP) and OWOBJ (20.7% U-Recall, 32.0% mAP). On the more challenging S-OWODB (Task 4), REAL-OW reaches 39.3% U-Recall and 47.1% mAP (Both), surpassing ORTH (38.8%, 46.2%) and OWOBJ (36.9%, 43.2%). On PASCAL VOC incremental detection (10+10, 15+5, 19+1), REAL-OW attains final mAP of 74.0, 75.9, and 77.3 respectively, exceeding all baselines. Notably, REAL-OW uses only 2.0M trainable parameters versus 39.7M (Transformer-based) and 106.6M (CNN-based) for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REAL-OW outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both M-OWODB and S-OWODB across every task, despite being the only strictly rehearsal-free method. On M-OWODB Task 4, it leads ORTH [39] by +2.4 U-Recall and +0.8 mAP, and OWOBJ [55] by +6.0 U-Recall and +6.7 mAP. On S-OWODB Task 4, the margins are +0.5 U-Recall and +0.9 mAP over ORTH, and +2.4 U-Recall and +3.9 mAP over OWOBJ. On PASCAL VOC iOD, REAL-OW achieves the highest final mAP in all three settings (74.0, 75.9, 77.3), beating ORTH (72.3, 74.7, 75.6) and OWOBJ (69.9, 73.3, 75.8). The parameter efficiency gap is stark: REAL-OW trains only 2.0M parameters versus 39.7M for Transformer-based baselines (e.g., CAT, PROB) and 106.6M for CNN-based ones (e.g., RandBox, ORTH), a 20–53× reduction. Older methods like ORE [19] and OW-DETR [13] trail by large margins (e.g., 12.4 vs. 26.7 U-Recall on M-OWODB T4), confirming that exemplar replay alone is insufficient for robust open-world discovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes LoRA (originally designed for LLM fine-tuning) within a D-DETR-based detection framework to address Open-World Object Detection without exemplar replay. All prior OWOD methods (ORE, OW-DETR, CAT, PROB, OWOBJ, ORTH) depend on storing and replaying representative samples from previous tasks to combat catastrophic forgetting; REAL-OW is the first to eliminate this dependency entirely by decoupling general and task-specific knowledge into separate LoRA adapter branches. The Dual-Stage Objectness Modeling and CG-NLL distance are novel contributions that adapt energy-based objectness scoring to the dispersed feature distributions that arise when no historical data is available for calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 2M-parameter rehearsal-free model outperforms 40–107M-parameter exemplar replay baselines on both M-OWODB and S-OWODB, the paper challenges the field's working assumption that data replay is necessary for stable incremental detection, with direct implications for privacy-sensitive deployments (medical imaging, autonomous driving) where raw data retention is prohibited. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond four incremental tasks, how it interacts with external VLM-based pseudo-labeling (which the authors explicitly exclude for fair comparison), and whether the LoRA rank and cycle-ratio hyperparameters transfer across different backbone architectures. The method's reliance on internally generated pseudo-labels for unknown objects remains a potential bottleneck for discovery quality in highly novel domains.
