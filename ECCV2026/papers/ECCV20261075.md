## Problem

Existing monocular 4D scene synthesis methods are limited to view interpolation within the observed camera trajectory and cannot extrapolate to unseen regions. Camera-conditioned video generation approaches that attempt to extend beyond observed views lack explicit 3D geometric priors and are trained on small, random camera trajectories, producing severe geometric distortions under large viewpoint deviations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence (49 frames at 384×672) as input and produces an explicit 4D Gaussian Splatting scene representation that supports rendering from arbitrary viewpoints, including regions never observed in the input. Internally, the pipeline operates in a 2048×2048 panoramic coordinate space with M=8 tangent directions and E=6 expansion steps at 15° angular increments. The final output is a per-scene 4DGS model (optimized for ~2 hours) that renders at ~32 FPS, with the panoramic representation used for extreme-view qualitative evaluation.

## Method

PanoGaussian unifies a panoramic trajectory-guided video diffusion prior with an explicit dynamic Gaussian Splatting representation through three coupled mechanisms. First, a panoramic trajectory paradigm aligns training and inference of the video generative prior under a fixed-angle shift protocol, reducing the domain gap that causes geometric instability. Second, a progressive expansion strategy warps source frames along M evenly spaced tangent directions on the panoramic sphere, always using the original observed video with fixed 4DGS depth and poses (never previously hallucinated outputs) and confining diffusion inpainting to masked unobserved regions, structurally preventing error accumulation. Third, a masked refinement loss distills the panoramic-trajectory refined videos back into the dynamic Gaussian representation, where the binary mask restricts supervision to newly generated content, enabling the Gaussian model to absorb hallucinated geometry without re-optimizing settled areas. This pixel-to-pixel correspondence between panoramic and Gaussian renderings effectively transfers global panoramic exploration into an explicit 3D representation that preserves physical geometry and motion.

## Results

On the DyCheck iPhone dataset, PanoGaussian achieves the best performance across all metrics: PSNR 18.71, SSIM 0.598, LPIPS 0.323 on the full scene, and notably uPSNR 16.72, uSSIM 0.495, uLPIPS 0.385 on purely unseen regions. On the Nvidia Dynamic dataset (which lacks truly unseen regions), PanoGaussian achieves PSNR 26.75 and LPIPS 0.069, matching the best reconstruction baseline MoSca (26.72 / 0.070). The method runs at 32.42 FPS with a total per-scene optimization time of ~2.0 hours (1.4h for 4DGS fitting + 0.6h for progressive diffusion generation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoGaussian outperforms all 19 baselines on every reported metric in Table 1. Among reconstruction-based methods, it surpasses the strongest baseline MoSca [32] by +1.40 dB PSNR (18.71 vs 17.31) and +2.49 dB uPSNR (16.72 vs 14.23), with the largest gap appearing in unseen-region quality (uSSIM 0.495 vs 0.295). Among diffusion-based methods, it beats CogNVS [6] by +1.77 dB PSNR and +1.75 dB uPSNR, while CogNVS itself already leads other diffusion baselines on mPSNR. Among feed-forward methods, PanoGaussian exceeds MoVieS [42] by +1.39 dB mPSNR (19.85 vs 18.46) and 4DGT [83] by +3.67 dB uPSNR (16.72 vs 12.11). On the Nvidia dataset (Table 2), where no truly unseen regions exist, PanoGaussian (PSNR 26.75, LPIPS 0.069) is essentially tied with MoSca (26.72, 0.070) and DynPoint (26.53, 0.068), confirming that its advantage is specifically in extrapolation to unseen regions rather than interpolation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: dynamic Gaussian Splatting for 4D scene reconstruction (building directly on MoSca as its geometric backbone) and camera-conditioned video diffusion for novel view generation (fine-tuning TrajectoryCrafter as its generative prior). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a panoramic trajectory paradigm that aligns training and inference under a fixed spherical warping protocol, addressing the train-inference domain gap that causes existing diffusion-based methods to fail under large viewpoint deviations. By distilling panoramic-trajectory-guided generative outputs into explicit 3D dynamic Gaussians via masked refinement, the paper bridges the geometry-free nature of video diffusion with the interpolation-only limitation of prior 4DGS methods, unifying global scene exploration with local geometric fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in monocular 4D reconstruction: the inability of existing methods to synthesize content in regions never observed by the camera, which is critical for immersive applications like VR and free-viewpoint video. The panoramic coordinate system provides an elegant, structured alternative to ad-hoc camera trajectory sampling for systematic scene exploration, and the progressive expansion with fixed-geometry warping offers a principled mechanism to prevent error accumulation. However, the ~2-hour per-scene optimization, reliance on a pretrained diffusion model, and limited effectiveness on outward-facing or distant scenes constrain practical deployment. Open questions include whether the panoramic-Gaussian unification can be made real-time, whether it generalizes to highly dynamic or non-rigid scenes, and whether the approach can be extended to multi-object or multi-scene settings without per-scene optimization.
