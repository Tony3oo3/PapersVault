## Problem

3D VLMs generate hundreds to thousands of visual tokens per input, making inference dominated by quadratic attention complexity and KV cache growth. Existing token pruning methods rely on diversity-based selection that removes similar tokens to maximize dispersion, but in 3D environments this systematically drops representative prototype tokens in favor of outliers, breaking the multi-view consistencies and geometric structures essential for spatial reasoning. The paper asks whether pruning should preserve visual evidence coverage rather than maximize diversity among retained tokens.

## Input / Output

The method operates on raw visual tokens extracted from a 3D VLM's visual-geometric encoder, where each token carries a feature embedding, a 3D global coordinate (estimated via SfM or a geometry foundation model, not ground truth), and a frame timestamp. Given a retention ratio R, it outputs a pruned subset of K = ⌈RN⌉ tokens that is inserted directly before the LLM prefill stage as a training-free, plug-and-play module. The method is model-agnostic and has been validated on GS-Reasoner and VLM-3R with uniform 32-frame sampling.

## Method

CoverPrune formulates token pruning as a semi-relaxed Optimal Transport problem where retained tokens act as prototypes that distribute representational mass to the full token set, minimizing coverage distortion under a cardinality constraint. Three key designs make this tractable: (1) a Feature-Spatial-Temporal (FST) transport cost combining cosine feature distance, L2 spatial distance (with a log-compression mapping for near-field expansion), and ReLU temporal distance to jointly model semantic, geometric, and temporal substitutability; (2) informativeness-aware target capacities computed from local FST distinctiveness scores, so that harder-to-approximate tokens receive more transport mass; and (3) a Spatial-Guided Greedy Selection (SGS) algorithm that restricts marginal-cost evaluation to g nearest 3D neighbors, avoiding repeated global OT solves at each greedy step. CoverPrune-Lite further approximates the OT objective via Morton-code 3D-aware ordering and capacity-guided grouping, selecting one prototype per group by minimizing within-group capacity-weighted cost, achieving O(N log N) pruning time with minimal performance loss.

## Results

On VSI-Bench (GS-Reasoner base), CoverPrune achieves 59.76 average score at 20% token retention (92.4% of the 64.70 full-token baseline), outperforming the best baseline DTC (56.57) by 3.19 points. Under aggressive 5% retention, CoverPrune retains 52.66 average versus 46.31 for DTC and 46.10 for VisionZip, a gap of over 6 points. On general 3D tasks (ScanQA, SQA3D, Scan2Cap) at 20% retention, CoverPrune exceeds the vanilla baseline on Scan2Cap accuracy (101.68% relative) and ScanQA accuracy (100.95%), while CoverPrune-Lite achieves comparable results (101.31% and 102.46%) with 6× lower pruning overhead (0.41 s vs. 2.53 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoverPrune outperforms all four baselines across every retention ratio and both base models (GS-Reasoner and VLM-3R). The closest competitor is DTC (CVPR 2025), a 3D-specific diversity-driven method, which trails CoverPrune by 3.19 points at 20% retention (56.57 vs. 59.76) and by 6.35 points at 5% retention (46.31 vs. 52.66) on VSI-Bench. VisionZip (CVPR 2025) and FastVID (NeurIPS 2025), both general-purpose attention/diversity methods, are consistently 2–6 points behind CoverPrune on VSI-Bench and show steeper degradation under aggressive pruning. EgoPrune (arXiv 2025), which fuses attention and diversity for egomotion video, is the weakest baseline, losing 10+ points to CoverPrune at 5% retention (40.85 vs. 52.66). Notably, CoverPrune-Lite matches or slightly exceeds full CoverPrune on several tasks (e.g., Room Size, Appearance Order) while reducing pruning time from 2.53 s to 0.41 s, making it the most practical option for deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diversity-based and attention-based token pruning paradigm (VisionZip, FastVID, DTC, EgoPrune) by reframing the selection objective as coverage preservation rather than dispersion maximization. It builds directly on the semi-relaxed optimal transport framework (Chapel et al., Peyré & Cuturi) and the submodularity guarantees for prototype selection under relaxed Wasserstein objectives (Gurumoorthy et al., SPOT), adapting these tools to the inference-time setting where the source support is itself a decision variable. The FST transport cost and spatially structured greedy solver are novel instantiations tailored to 3D geometric and temporal structure, going beyond the purely feature-space costs used in prior pruning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The coverage-versus-diversity distinction addresses a real failure mode in 3D VLMs where multi-view tokens that appear redundant in feature space are actually essential for geometric consistency, and the OT formulation provides a principled, training-free mechanism to preserve them. The consistent performance gains across two base models and four benchmarks, particularly under aggressive 5–10% retention budgets, suggest the paradigm shift is not merely cosmetic. Open questions include whether the full OT machinery is necessary versus simpler coverage heuristics, how the method scales to very long sequences or 4D dynamic scenes, and whether the coverage objective transfers to 2D VLMs where the geometric structure argument is weaker.
