## Problem

Existing Grounded VideoQA methods exhibit question-invariant grounding: they predict nearly identical temporal segments for different questions about the same video, because video representations are fixed before question semantics reach them (modality isolation) and the question is reduced to a single token that is immediately absorbed by the dominant visual stream in the grounding module. This paper identifies and quantifies this failure mode via PIoU and GT-Pred Correlation metrics, showing prior methods collapse to PIoU >85 versus a ground-truth PIoU of 21.3.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (uniformly sampled to 32 frames, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14), a natural-language question, and a set of answer candidates (encoded by a frozen RoBERTa-base). It produces both a discrete answer selection from the candidates and a continuous temporal grounding segment (start–end interval) indicating where in the video the supporting evidence lies. Training uses only candidate-level QA supervision with no frame-level grounding annotations; at inference, only the answer pass executes, so the two-pass training design adds no test-time cost.

## Method

GroundFormer introduces learnable communication tokens (one [TYPE] token plus N=32 query tokens) as an explicit bottleneck between the visual and linguistic branches. A two-step asymmetric masked attention first transfers candidate semantics into the tokens (Linguistic Transfer) and then propagates that intent into every video frame (Visual Refinement), so that visual features become question-conditioned before any localization occurs. Grounding is then derived via MIL cross-attention where the refined token queries attend to question-conditioned video keys/values; the resulting attention logits are factorized into a candidate distribution and a temporal distribution whose element-wise product yields MIL scores, with the temporal distribution serving as the grounding signal. A 1-D Gaussian kernel (σ=2) smooths the peaked temporal attention into a coherent segment. Training combines cross-entropy on answer/question candidates, MIL logit supervision, a [TYPE] classification loss, and a hierarchical multi-modal contrastive loss that progressively aligns video→question→answer embeddings across the two-pass pipeline.

## Results

On NExT-GQA, GroundFormer (211M params) achieves a state-of-the-art Acc@GQA of 21.5, outperforming the best prior method (CRA-GQA with FrozenBiLM, 1.2B params) by 2.7 points, while also improving mIoP from 26.5 to 34.0 and mIoU from 13.5 to 17.5. On STAR, GroundFormer reaches Acc@GQA of 30.7 (vs. 27.8 for the best baseline) with mIoP@0.5 of 49.3 and mIoU@0.5 of 15.8 (vs. 5.1 for CRA-GQA). Notably, GroundFormer uses only 17.6% of the parameters of the FrozenBiLM baselines yet surpasses them on all grounding metrics, though it trails on Acc@VQA (61.7 vs. 74.7) due to the smaller backbone capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroundFormer outperforms all four baseline families (PH/NG+, TimeCraft, CRA-GQA, QGAC-TR) on the primary Acc@GQA metric on both NExT-GQA (21.5 vs. 18.8 best) and STAR (30.7 vs. 27.8 best). The grounding advantage is substantial: mIoP improves by 7.5 points over CRA-GQA (34.0 vs. 26.5) and mIoU by 4.0 points (17.5 vs. 13.5) on NExT-GQA, while on STAR the mIoU@0.5 gap is even wider (15.8 vs. 5.1 for CRA-GQA). The only metric where GroundFormer trails is Acc@VQA against the 1.2B-parameter FrozenBiLM variants (61.7 vs. 74.7 for TimeCraft), which the authors attribute to backbone capacity rather than grounding quality. Against the same-capacity Temp[CLIP] baselines, GroundFormer wins on both Acc@VQA and all grounding metrics simultaneously. The PIoU analysis (28.2 vs. 85.7/88.9 for NG+/CRA-GQA) further confirms that GroundFormer's grounding is far more question-discriminative, approaching the ground-truth PIoU of 21.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the Grounded VideoQA task formalized by NExT-GQA (Xiao et al., CVPR 2024) and its follow-ups (TimeCraft, CRA-GQA, QGAC-TR), identifying a shared structural limitation across all prior methods: visual and linguistic branches encode independently, and the question is injected as a single token into a Gaussian-based grounding module, making grounding effectively question-invariant. GroundFormer departs from this late-fusion paradigm by introducing learnable communication tokens as an explicit visuo-lingual bottleneck that conditions video features on question semantics before localization. It also repurposes Multiple Instance Learning—previously applied to declarative video moment retrieval queries—into the interrogative setting of Grounded VideoQA, where supporting evidence is only implicitly defined by the question-answer relationship.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that prior methods produce nearly identical temporal segments for all questions in a video (PIoU 85–89 vs. ground-truth 21) reveals that the grounding component in existing Grounded VideoQA systems is largely non-functional in terms of question discrimination, a failure mode the field had not explicitly measured. The proposed PIoU and GT-Pred Correlation metrics provide useful tools for auditing this behavior in future systems. Open questions include whether the communication-token mechanism generalizes to open-ended (non-multiple-choice) VideoQA, whether it scales to much longer videos beyond the ~44s average in NExT-GQA, and whether the frozen-encoder setup (CLIP + RoBERTa) limits the ceiling of question-conditioned grounding.
