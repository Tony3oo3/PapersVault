## Problem

Semi-supervised multi-organ segmentation suffers from a dual challenge: limited annotations exacerbate class imbalance, and spatially co-occurring organs (e.g., heart/aorta, liver/stomach) cause feature entanglement that leads to false activations. Existing methods address class imbalance through loss reweighting or architectural changes but largely overlook this inter-class co-occurrence problem, which becomes especially destructive in data-scarce settings where the model cannot reliably distinguish adjacent structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D medical image slices (CT or MRI) resized to 256×256, processing both full images and 64×64 cropped patches (4 per image by default) through a shared U-Net backbone with dual normalization layers. During training, the network simultaneously ingests labeled full images, labeled crops, unlabeled full images, and unlabeled crops; at inference, only full images are used. The output is a pixel-wise semantic mask over K classes (including background), with segmentation quality measured by Dice and Average Surface Distance.

## Method

DPCL operates in two complementary spaces. In image space, a dual-prior probabilistic sampling strategy combines pixel-wise entropy (uncertainty) with logarithmic class-frequency reweighting to stochastically select crop centers, directing the model toward ambiguous and underrepresented regions; a dual-normalization U-Net bridges the domain gap between cropped and full inputs, and a pixel-consistency mechanism fuses patch-level and full-image pseudo-labels by selecting the lower-entropy prediction at each pixel. In feature space, two contrastive losses decouple co-occurring organ representations: a patch-based contrastive loss (PBC) uses InfoNCE-style objectives to push apart features of neighboring organs within local patches while pulling them toward their class prototype, and a global-based contrastive loss (GBC) aligns full-image pixel features with class prototypes to maintain global semantic consistency. Class prototypes are maintained via exponential moving average and accelerated for minority classes by incorporating features from the uncertainty-guided crops.

## Results

On the Synapse dataset (9 organs), DPCL achieves 61.48% average Dice with 5% labeled data, surpassing the best baseline ABD (53.57%) by 7.91%, and 68.89% with 10% labeled data, exceeding AD-MT (62.73%) by 6.16%. On FeTA2021, SegTHOR, and ACDC, DPCL consistently leads: 70.04% Dice (5% labels) on FeTA2021, 78.18% (10%) on SegTHOR, and 90.47% (10%) on ACDC. The method also achieves the lowest ASD across all settings, e.g., 14.84 voxels on Synapse 10% vs. 22.25 for AD-MT, indicating tighter boundary localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPCL outperforms all nine baselines across all four datasets and both labeling ratios. The closest competitors are AD-MT (ECCV'24) and ABD (CVPR'24): on Synapse 10%, DPCL leads AD-MT by 6.16% Dice (68.89 vs. 62.73) and ABD by 6.34% (68.89 vs. 62.55), while also reducing ASD from 22.25/23.17 to 14.84. On FeTA2021 5%, DPCL edges out AD-MT by 1.73% Dice (70.04 vs. 68.31); on SegTHOR 20%, it surpasses M3HL by 1.30% (81.38 vs. 80.08). The gap is most dramatic under extreme label scarcity: on Synapse 5%, DPCL improves over the next-best ABD by 7.91% Dice, and the pancreas class jumps from 26.13% (ABD) to 41.26% (DPCL). FixMatch, while competitive on Synapse 10% (60.48%), is outperformed by 8.41% by DPCL, and older methods like UA-MT and SS-Net trail by over 20% Dice on Synapse 5%, confirming that DPCL's co-occurrence decoupling and patch-driven local enhancement provide substantial gains over prior consistency-regularization and pseudo-labeling approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised medical image segmentation (SSMIS) literature, which has primarily followed consistency regularization (UA-MT, FixMatch) and pseudo-labeling (BCP, ABD) paradigms, by introducing a dual-space patch-driven framework that explicitly targets inter-organ feature confusion caused by spatial co-occurrence. It builds on contrastive learning approaches in medical segmentation (PH-Net, DyCON, pseudo-label guided contrastive loss) but departs from them by restricting negative samples to co-occurring neighboring organs within local patches rather than using all other classes, and by combining this with a global contrastive objective for cross-region consistency. The dual-normalization module adapts the domain-specific batch normalization idea from unsupervised domain adaptation to bridge the distributional gap between cropped patches and full images, a practical concern that prior SSMIS methods largely ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The co-occurrence framing provides a useful diagnostic lens for a failure mode (false activations in spatially adjacent organs) that prior class-imbalance-focused methods address only indirectly, and the dual-space decomposition (image-space patch sampling + feature-space contrastive decoupling) offers a modular recipe that could be adopted in other multi-structure segmentation settings. However, the evaluation is limited to 2D slices with a U-Net backbone on four relatively small datasets, leaving open whether the patch-based contrastive mechanism and dual normalization generalize to 3D volumes, larger transformer backbones, or higher-organ-count scenarios. The reliance on a mean-teacher architecture and the specific Gaussian warmup schedule for contrastive losses also raise questions about robustness across training configurations.
