## Problem

Lensless cameras with large-footprint PSFs produce severely ill-conditioned measurements where each pixel mixes scene information over wide spatial extents. Existing hybrid reconstruction pipelines embed physics in a one-sided manner—either enforcing a fixed forward-model constraint or refining a single inverted estimate—causing measurement-domain cues to collapse as features pass through the network hierarchy. This one-sided coupling leaves intermediate estimates detached from raw measurements, producing spatially structured artifacts under shift-variant degradation and calibration mismatch.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw lensless camera measurements (480×270 for DiffuserCam and WiderCam, 320×224 for MWNet) together with a learnable PSF field of k = s² kernels covering local image regions (supports up to 270×270 pixels). The PSF field can be initialized from calibrated measurements, a single reference PSF, or random patterns, and is jointly optimized end-to-end. The output is a reconstructed RGB scene image at native resolution, with the architecture also supporting modality-specific operator swaps (e.g., angular-spectrum propagation for inline holography) while retaining the same interleaving backbone.

## Method

IFIN interleaves Integrated Forward-Inverse Blocks (IFIBs) at every encoder-decoder scale, where each block couples a Forward System Operator (FSO, 2D convolution with the PSF projecting image features into the measurement domain) with an Inverse System Operator (ISO, Wiener-like deconvolution with a learnable frequency-dependent regularizer ε(u,v) that restores image-domain features). The hallmark is bidirectional exchange: the FSO's measurement-domain projection augments the measurement stream before the next ISO step, while the ISO's image-domain estimate drives the image-stream update and conditions the next FSO projection, forming a forward-inverse coupling that repeatedly reintroduces measurement-consistency cues throughout decoding. A shared learnable PSF field, encoded into per-scale embeddings, is injected into both operators at every scale; for shift-variant systems the ISO applies region-wise deconvolution with k kernels blended by learnable ROI maps initialized from Gaussian partitions. The entire architecture is trained end-to-end with supervised loss on paired scene-measurement data, and the PSF field is regularized with unit DC gain normalization and a weak non-negativity penalty to maintain physical plausibility.

## Results

On the three lensless benchmarks, IFIN achieves PSNR of 29.86 dB (DiffuserCam), 25.44 dB (WiderCam), and 31.08 dB (MultiWienerNet), improving over the best prior method by +1.63 dB, +0.65 dB, and +2.58 dB respectively. IFIN also attains the best LPIPS (0.174/0.201/0.175) and SSIM (0.893/0.824/0.866) across all three datasets. On simulated inline holography reconstruction, IFIN reaches 28.30 dB PSNR, outperforming the physics-initialized NAFNet+ baseline (25.75 dB) by 2.55 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IFIN outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across all three lensless benchmarks. The closest competitors are MoDL [9] and UPDN [24]: on DiffuserCam, IFIN surpasses UPDN (28.23 dB) by 1.63 dB and MoDL (27.96 dB) by 1.90 dB in PSNR; on WiderCam, IFIN leads MoDL (24.79 dB) by 0.65 dB; on MultiWienerNet, IFIN exceeds MoDL (28.50 dB) by 2.58 dB. Pure data-driven restorers (NAFNet, U-Net) trail by 4–8 dB, while classical methods (ADMM, Wiener deconvolution) are 17–19 dB behind, confirming that the forward-inverse interleaving architecture is the primary source of the gain. On Gaussian deblurring, IFIN is slightly below NAFNet at σ=5 (25.10 vs 25.63 dB) but surpasses both NAFNet and RCAN at σ=15 (21.65 vs 21.54 dB), indicating the physics operators help most under severe ill-conditioning. On inline holography, IFIN (28.30 dB) beats the physics-initialized NAFNet+ (25.75 dB) by 2.55 dB, demonstrating transferability to wave-propagation-based forward models.

## Relation to Prior Work

IFIN sits at the intersection of physics-informed deep learning and encoder-decoder restoration architectures, departing from prior hybrid methods (UPDN, MWDNs, LensNet, MoDL, MWNet) that embed the forward model in a one-sided manner—either a single inversion step followed by learned refinement, or a fixed forward-model constraint. The key architectural departure is the Integrated Forward-Inverse Block (IFIB), which couples a differentiable forward projection (FSO) with a learnable Wiener-like inverse (ISO) at every encoder-decoder scale, creating a bidirectional exchange of measurement- and image-domain cues that prior pipelines do not maintain. The learnable shift-variant PSF field, shared across both operators and all scales, extends the single-PSF or fixed-multi-PSF settings used by MWNet and LensNet into an end-to-end adaptable representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional forward-inverse interleaving offers a reusable architectural template for computational imaging beyond lensless cameras, as demonstrated by competitive Gaussian deblurring and strong gains on inline holography with modality-matched operators. The introduction of WiderCam (>100° FoV, 25k pairs) fills a gap in publicly available lensless benchmarks with strong shift variance, and the learnable PSF field enables blind recovery without full calibration, reducing practical deployment barriers. Open questions include the per-block FFT overhead (IFIN is ~6× slower than NAFNet at k=1), sensitivity to severe sensor saturation or large geometry changes, and whether the interleaving principle scales to 3D or video reconstruction where temporal consistency adds another dimension of constraint.
