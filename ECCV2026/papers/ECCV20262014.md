## Problem

Existing feed-forward language-embedded Gaussian reconstruction methods are restricted to a fixed (typically two) number of input views and often require camera parameters, limiting their practicality for real-world multi-view scenarios. Additionally, the prevailing paradigm of attaching a distinct language embedding to every pixel-aligned Gaussian introduces substantial semantic redundancy and storage overhead, since semantic information is intrinsically much sparser than geometric and appearance information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262014/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FLEG accepts a variable number (2 to 30+) of uncalibrated, unposed RGB images as input, with no camera parameters or pose information required at inference. The output is a decoupled pair of representations: a dense set of standard 3D Gaussians encoding geometry and appearance (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH color coefficients), and a much sparser set of isotropic semantic Gaussians carrying only language-aligned embeddings. This dual output supports novel-view synthesis, open-vocabulary object query segmentation, and 3D editing from a single feed-forward pass.

## Method

FLEG employs a ~1B-parameter transformer backbone following VGGT with DINOv2 patchification and 24 layers of global/frame-wise attention, followed by a DPT-based Gaussian head that predicts per-pixel Gaussian attributes and language embeddings. Training uses a geometric-semantic dual-branch distillation framework: the geometric branch leverages a frozen VGGT teacher for pseudo-label supervision and introduces a novel-view-based distillation strategy that selects reliable target views via projected coverage masks to prevent overfitting to input views; the semantic branch aligns rendered embeddings with CLIP features and adds instance-guided contrastive learning (using SAM2 masks) to improve cross-instance discriminability at O(N_p·K) complexity. The key architectural contribution is the Decoupled Gaussian Language Embedding (DGLE) module, which aggregates voxelized standard Gaussians into a much sparser set of semantic Gaussians using a larger voxel size, softmax-weighted averaging of position/opacity/embedding, and an isotropic moment-matching scheme for scale and rotation, reducing language embeddings to under 5% of the per-pixel count while preserving semantic fidelity.

## Results

On ScanNet++ with 2 input views, FLEG achieves 46.61 mIoU, 71.18% Acc., 28.14 PSNR, 0.8718 SSIM, and 0.1451 LPIPS, outperforming all feed-forward baselines by a wide margin. On ScanNet under the same setting, FLEG reaches 48.01 mIoU and 25.05 PSNR. Under dense 32-view evaluation, FLEG attains 46.61 mIoU and 25.14 PSNR on ScanNet++, surpassing per-scene optimized methods LangSplat (25.81 mIoU) and LangScene-X (21.08 mIoU) by over 20 mIoU points. The method maintains consistent superiority across all tested view counts (2–32) on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLEG outperforms all feed-forward language-embedded baselines (LSM and Uni3R) by a substantial margin: on ScanNet++ 2-view, FLEG achieves 46.61 mIoU vs. 39.36 (LSM) and 39.54 (Uni3R), and 28.14 PSNR vs. 19.87 (LSM) and 24.95 (Uni3R). Against the geometry-only feed-forward method AnySplat, FLEG improves PSNR from 26.81 to 28.14 on ScanNet++ while additionally providing semantic metrics (46.61 mIoU) that AnySplat cannot produce. Compared to 2D semantic models used for distillation (LSeg and SAM2+CLIP), FLEG surpasses LSeg's 45.28 mIoU and SAM2+CLIP's 39.58 mIoU on ScanNet++, confirming that 3D lifting with the proposed contrastive learning yields better semantic alignment than 2D-only approaches. Under the dense 32-view setting, FLEG (46.61 mIoU, 25.14 PSNR on ScanNet++) decisively beats per-scene optimized methods LangSplat (25.81 mIoU, 24.02 PSNR) and LangScene-X (21.08 mIoU, 23.13 PSNR), demonstrating that feed-forward reconstruction with compact semantic representation can match or exceed expensive per-scene optimization. FLEG also maintains its advantage at 4, 8, and 16 views, where LSM and Uni3R degrade further (e.g., Uni3R drops to 40.29 mIoU at 16 views) while FLEG stays above 46 mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLEG sits at the intersection of two active lines of work: feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction (VGGT, AnySplat, DUSt3R) and language-embedded Gaussian Splatting (LangSplat, LangScene-X, LSM, Uni3R). It departs from prior feed-forward language-embedded methods (LSM, Uni3R) that are restricted to two input views or require camera parameters, by supporting arbitrary uncalibrated, unposed multi-view inputs via a novel-view-based distillation strategy built on VGGT. Its most significant conceptual departure is the Decoupled Gaussian Language Embedding (DGLE) module, which breaks from the prevailing per-Gaussian embedding paradigm by representing semantics with a sparse set of isotropic semantic Gaussians rather than attaching a language vector to every pixel-aligned Gaussian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that semantic representations are ~20× sparser than geometric ones (5% of embeddings suffice) challenges a widely adopted design assumption in language-embedded 3DGS and could influence how future methods allocate semantic capacity. Practically, FLEG enables near-real-time language-embedded 3D reconstruction without per-scene optimization or camera calibration, which is relevant for robotics navigation and AR/VR. Open questions include whether the sparsity finding generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the reliance on frozen VGGT and CLIP as teachers limits ceiling performance, and whether the compact semantic representation degrades under fine-grained or compositional language queries.
