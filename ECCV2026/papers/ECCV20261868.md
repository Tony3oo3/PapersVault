## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-motion models suffer from semantic drift, especially in compositional and long-duration sequences where multiple action segments must remain semantically coherent. While prior noise-optimization works in motion generation target physical or geometric constraints, the semantic role of the initial noise—its capacity to bias denoising toward specific motion meanings—remains unexplored. The paper asks whether one can identify and refine 'winning noise tickets' that inherently embody the semantics of a given text prompt, thereby improving text-motion alignment without retraining the base model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and an optional desired motion length (for frame-adjusted retrieval across variable durations) as primary inputs; for stylization it additionally accepts a reference style motion, and for controllable generation it accepts spatial constraints such as waypoints. The output is a 3D human motion sequence of F frames × 263 features, produced by a frozen diffusion backbone (MDM-50step or MotionLCM) initialized with the retrieved and optionally refined noise. A one-time offline cost builds a noise dictionary of 10,000 null-prompt motion-feature pairs (≈4 min for MDM, ≈2 min for MotionLCM on an A100); per-query inference then requires only a retrieval lookup plus optional iterative refinement or a single LoRA forward pass.

## Method

WINRO is a two-stage, training-free, model-agnostic pipeline. Stage 1 (retrieval) builds a noise dictionary by sampling random Gaussian noises, generating motions under a null prompt with a frozen diffusion model, and encoding each motion with a pretrained text-motion retrieval model (TMR) into a shared 256-d embedding; frame-adjusted retrieval (FAR) additionally stores features of truncated motions at multiple lengths to support variable-duration queries. At inference, the text embedding is matched against dictionary features via cosine similarity, and the top-k noise is selected (with optional Gaussian jitter to preserve local diversity). Stage 2 (refinement) optimizes the retrieved noise by minimizing a text-motion similarity loss plus a KL-divergence penalty against the standard normal, keeping the noise within the Gaussian typical set where the diffusion model is well-calibrated. A theoretical smoothness argument (local Lipschitz continuity of the alignment score over noise space) justifies why retrieval yields a good initialization for small, prior-preserving local refinement. An optional LoRA-based Noise Refiner, trained to predict a residual correction in one forward pass, amortizes the iterative optimization for real-time use while leaving all base-model weights frozen.

## Results

On HumanML3D text-conditioned generation, WINRO reduces FID from 0.438 to 0.115 for MDM-50step and from 0.093 to 0.072 for MotionLCM, while improving R-Top1 from 0.469 to 0.539 and 0.548 to 0.580 respectively. The LoRA-based amortized variant (WIN-RLoRA) matches or exceeds full WINRO quality (FID 0.102, R-Top1 0.546 on MDM) at near-real-time speed (AITS 0.280 s vs. 81.4 s for iterative WINRO). On the MTT long-duration benchmark, adding WIN-O to STMC nearly doubles per-crop R@1 (33.6 → 62.1) and R@3 (55.3 → 79.1), approaching ground-truth levels (55.0 / 73.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDM-50step, WINRO cuts FID by 74% (0.438 → 0.115) and lifts R-Top1 by 7 points (0.469 → 0.539); the amortized WIN-RLoRA variant further improves FID to 0.102 and R-Top1 to 0.546 while keeping AITS at 0.280 s, essentially matching the baseline's 0.265 s. Against MotionLCM, WINRO improves FID from 0.093 to 0.072 and R-Top1 from 0.548 to 0.580, with WIN-RLoRA matching the R-Top1 gain at 0.043 s AITS. On the MTT long-duration benchmark, STMC+WIN-O substantially outperforms STMC alone (R@1: 62.1 vs. 33.6; R@3: 79.1 vs. 55.3; M2T: 0.807 vs. 0.687), approaching ground-truth R@1 of 55.0. For motion stylization on SMooDi, WIN-O achieves the best FID (0.551 vs. 1.609) and R-Top3 (0.717 vs. 0.571), while WINRO attains the highest Style Recognition Accuracy (75.82 vs. 72.42), showing complementary trade-offs between text fidelity and style preservation. In the controllable generation comparison (Fig. 11), WINRO-refined noise produces motions that satisfy both the text prompt and spatial target constraints more faithfully than ProgMoGen-optimized noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis in denoising (Mao et al., ECCV 2024) from image diffusion to motion diffusion, providing empirical evidence that specific initial noise seeds encode motion semantics even under null prompts. It departs from prior motion noise-optimization work (DNO, ProgMoGen, PINO), which targets physical or geometric constraints, by instead optimizing noise against text-motion semantic similarity in a retrieval embedding space. The LoRA-based amortized refiner follows the noise hypernetwork approach of Eyring et al. (NeurIPS 2025), adapting single-pass noise correction to the motion domain. Unlike retrieval-augmented motion generation (ReMoDiffuse), which retrieves exemplar motions to condition denoising, WINRO retrieves semantically meaningful initial noises whose null-prompt outputs align with the query text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WINRO offers a training-free, model-agnostic plug-in that improves text-motion alignment across multiple diffusion backbones without retraining, making it broadly applicable to existing T2M pipelines. The null-prompt semantic alignment results validate that noise carries latent motion semantics, opening a new axis of control complementary to text conditioning. Key limitations include the high inference cost of iterative refinement (81 s/sentence for MDM), the need for an additional training step for the LoRA refiner, and the holistic (non-temporal) nature of the retrieval encoder, which limits fine-grained temporal control. Open questions include whether temporal-localization-aware retrieval models could further sharpen noise-to-segment alignment, and whether the approach transfers to video-based or higher-fidelity motion generation.
