## Problem

Existing diffusion personalization methods suffer from concept-dominant failure, where the learned concept token overwhelms other prompt semantics, suppressing background, attributes, and interactions in generated images. Prior remedies modify training objectives, add regularization, or alter architectures in ways tightly coupled to specific personalization algorithms, limiting scalability. A data-level augmentation strategy that generalizes across diverse personalization pipelines without requiring any pipeline modification remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a few-shot reference set (3–15 images per concept), automatic subject segmentation masks, and a set of text prompt templates as input. It produces an augmented reference dataset in which each original image is composited onto multiple diffusion-generated backgrounds (34 variants per reference image in experiments) and further carries an imperceptible additive perturbation applied to the background region. The output is a drop-in replacement dataset that can be fed directly into any existing personalization pipeline (TI, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, ELITE, P+) without architectural or objective changes.

## Method

TOPA combines two complementary components. Subject-Isolation Compositing (SIC) segments the subject from each reference image and composites it onto diverse Stable Diffusion-generated backgrounds, so the subject becomes the only consistent feature across the augmented set, breaking spurious subject–background correlations. Cross-Attention Rebalancing Module (CARM) then optimizes an imperceptible additive perturbation δ per image using two losses: L_ctx penalizes context tokens' cross-attention on the subject mask to suppress cross-token interference, and L_R* aligns the pseudo-token's aggregated cross-attention map with the subject mask to anchor concept binding. Because the pseudo-token embedding evolves during training, CARM uses an alternating schedule that updates the embedding every step and the perturbation every K steps, preventing overfitting to an early-stage token representation. To keep computation tractable over the h×q composited images, perturbations are shared at the region level (one per subject or one per background) rather than per composited image, reducing optimization cost from h×q to h or q.

## Results

Across five personalization methods on CustomConcept101 (10 concepts, 240 generated images each), TOPA consistently improves background controllability and image quality. For Textual Inversion, BRISQUE drops from 16.634 to 14.574, FID from 247 to 220, and BLIP-VQA accuracy rises from 0.577 to 0.681. DreamBooth LoRA shows the largest gains: BRISQUE improves from 19.937 to 15.496 and CLIP-IQA from 0.768 to 0.908. ELITE achieves the best FID reduction (219→176, −19.6%) while maintaining subject fidelity (CLIP-I† 0.691→0.674).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOPA is applied as a plug-in augmentation on top of each baseline rather than competing against them, so the comparison is method vs. method+TOPA. For Textual Inversion [5], TOPA improves all major metrics: BRISQUE −12.4%, FID −10.9%, BLIP-VQA +18.4%. DreamBooth LoRA [19] benefits most dramatically, with BRISQUE dropping 22.3% (19.937→15.496) and BLIP-VQA nearly doubling (0.165→0.325), indicating severe concept dominance in the original. Custom Diffusion [10] and ELITE [25] show consistent gains in FID (−11.9% and −19.6% respectively) and BLIP-VQA, though Custom Diffusion's BRISQUE slightly worsens (15.147→15.740). P+ [8] gains the largest CLIP-IQA improvement (+8.6%) and BRISQUE reduction (−18.9%). Among context-improving baselines, Compositional Inversion [30] and Pivotal Tuning Inversion LoRA [20] both see FID reductions of 19.4% and 21.4% respectively with TOPA, while Attend-and-Excite [1] improves CLIP-T and FID but slightly degrades BRISQUE and CLIP-I†, suggesting partial overlap with TOPA's attention-rebalancing mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOPA positions itself as a data-level alternative to the growing family of method-specific regularization strategies (Compositional Inversion, CoRe, Attend-and-Excite) that modify training objectives, loss functions, or sampling procedures to improve compositionality in diffusion personalization. Conceptually, it repurposes the optimization-based perturbation machinery from adversarial robustness and data-poisoning literature (TrojDiff, MIST, Diff-Protect) toward a constructive goal: aligning cross-attention maps so that context tokens attend to background rather than subject. The subject-isolation compositing component extends the background-replacement idea from BLIP-Diffusion by coupling it with cross-attention-guided perturbation optimization, and the overall framework is designed to be pipeline-agnostic, requiring no changes to the personalization algorithm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TOPA offers a practical, drop-in augmentation that consistently improves background controllability across five heterogeneous personalization methods (TI, DreamBooth LoRA, Custom Diffusion, ELITE, P+) without altering their training pipelines, which is valuable for practitioners who cannot or do not want to modify their local training code. The ablation showing that naive geometric/color augmentation actually degrades subject fidelity while targeted perturbation improves it underscores that the cross-attention alignment objective is the critical ingredient, not mere data diversity. Limitations include evaluation on only 10 concepts from CustomConcept101, reliance on SD v1.4 as the primary backbone (with cross-version results deferred to appendix), and the additional computational cost of the alternating perturbation-embedding optimization stage. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video diffusion, multi-concept personalization, or larger reference sets where background diversity is less of a bottleneck.
