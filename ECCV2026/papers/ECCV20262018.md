## Problem

Creating fully controllable, photorealistic 3D human avatars from a single image remains ill-posed and challenging: existing feed-forward methods either lack 3D-consistent training data at scale, produce coarse reconstructions that lose fine-grained facial and hand details, or are restricted to upper-body/frontal views. The scarcity of diverse, geometrically consistent 3D human training data further limits generalization to novel identities, poses, and viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a full-body human as input, additionally extracting a super-resolved cropped head image to boost facial detail. It outputs a UV-parameterized 3D Gaussian avatar (768×768 UV maps, ~320K Gaussians) in the SMPL-X canonical space, where Gaussian positions and rotations are predicted as offsets from the mesh. This representation supports arbitrary 360° novel-view rendering and pose-driven animation via Linear Blend Skinning, with a separate head model blended in for enhanced facial expressiveness.

## Method

The architecture couples a frozen Sapiens-1B feature extractor with a scaling transformer that maps concatenated body and head image tokens into UV-space feature maps. A key innovation is the Input-aware Decoder, which explicitly projects the input image's RGB, geometric, and semantic information into UV space via mesh rasterization and 3D-to-2D projection, decoupling geometry decoding (position offsets, opacity) from color decoding (rotation, scale, RGB) to mitigate tracking misalignment. A Gaussian blending network (StyleUNet + zero-convolution) fuses a dedicated head model with the body model for seamless facial enhancement. Training is supported by a newly constructed 28.5k-identity Gaussian avatar dataset with strong 3D consistency, generated via diffusion-prior-based single-image reconstruction, combined with public datasets (THuman2.1, HuGe100K) and 5,253 real head videos. Geometric regularization (position proximity, scale anisotropy, Laplacian smoothing with 100× hand weighting) stabilizes the ill-posed single-image reconstruction.

## Results

On THuman2.0, SplatCtrlA achieves PSNR 22.542, SSIM 0.931, MSE 6.440×10⁻³, and LPIPS 0.0653, outperforming all baselines by a clear margin. On NeuMan, it reaches PSNR 23.489 and LPIPS 0.0329, and on Seamless Interaction it attains PSNR 24.858 and LPIPS 0.0542. In a 32-participant user study, the method is rated best 77.9% of the time overall (64.4% face, 67.9% hand, 75.0% clothed body), far exceeding the next-best baseline (LHM at 10.6% overall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatCtrlA outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against IDOL, the gap is largest on THuman2.0 (PSNR +2.55 dB, LPIPS −0.025), where IDOL's reliance on an inconsistent 2D synthetic dataset and lack of geometric constraints produce coarse textures and fail to recover thin structures like fingers. Against LHM, the advantage is more moderate but consistent (PSNR +2.27 dB on THuman2.0, +0.35 dB on NeuMan), with LHM suffering from hand artifacts and texture degradation due to its lack of dynamic expression and gesture modeling. Against GUAVA, SplatCtrlA wins by ~2.3 dB PSNR on THuman2.0 and ~0.7 dB on Seamless, while GUAVA is fundamentally limited to frontal upper-body regions and struggles with occluded hands. In the user study, SplatCtrlA dominates overwhelmingly (77.9% overall preference) compared to LHM (10.6%), GUAVA (9.6%), and IDOL (1.9%), with the largest margins in face (64.4% vs. 17.3%) and hand (67.9% vs. 20.4%) categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely in the feed-forward 3D avatar generation lineage that emerged from Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs), building directly on the 3DGS + SMPL-X UV parameterization used by IDOL, LHM, and GUAVA. Its key departures are threefold: (1) replacing 2D synthetic training data with a 3D-consistent Gaussian avatar dataset constructed via diffusion-prior-based per-identity optimization, (2) an input-aware decoder that explicitly projects input-image color and semantic information into UV space to compensate for feature-extraction loss, and (3) a Gaussian-blending facial enhancement module trained on real head videos. The Sapiens backbone and the scaling-transformer UV mapping follow prior work (Sapiens, IDOL), while the inverse-UV-RGB conditioning borrows from GUAVA but extends it with semantic correction and a decoupled geometry/color decoding strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single feed-forward pass produces a fully controllable 3D avatar (body pose, hand gestures, facial expressions) in seconds, which is directly applicable to real-time VR, virtual production, and interactive content pipelines. The 3D-consistent dataset construction pipeline is a reusable contribution that addresses a well-recognized bottleneck in training generalizable human models. However, the authors acknowledge that reliance on synthetic data limits robustness to complex real-world lighting, and fine protruding structures (individual hair strands) remain out of reach. Open questions include whether the input-aware decoding strategy generalizes to multi-image or video inputs, and whether the dataset pipeline can be extended to capture richer illumination and material diversity.
