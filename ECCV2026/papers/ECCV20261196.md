## Problem

Affordance grounding is bottlenecked by data: existing 3D and 2D datasets are confined to a small set of predefined object and affordance categories (23 classes in 3D, at most 304 in 2D) because manual annotation cannot scale to the open-ended diversity of real-world interactions. This closed-vocabulary constraint prevents models from generalizing to unseen object-affordance combinations, which is essential for embodied agents operating in open environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261196/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a 3D point cloud or 2D RGB image paired with a free-form natural language query (e.g., 'Point to the handle to open the door') and produces a continuous affordance heatmap — a per-point or per-pixel probability map indicating where the described interaction occurs. In 3D, heatmaps are defined on the object surface via multi-view projection and voting; in 2D, they are rendered from the 3D heatmap at the most visible viewpoint. The dataset provides 750K such query-heatmap pairs across 150K textured 3D objects from Objaverse, with a 5K human-verified test split held out at the object-instance level.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully automated three-stage annotation pipeline that chains foundation models: Gemma3 (with chain-of-thought prompting) generates five open-vocabulary affordance queries per object, Molmo predicts pixel-level interaction points for each query-view pair, and MobileSAM converts those points into 2D segmentation masks (selecting the smallest of three SAM hypotheses for part-level granularity). These per-view 2D heatmaps are projected onto the 3D surface using known camera parameters and depth, then aggregated via multi-view voting to produce robust 3D affordance heatmaps. For modeling, Espresso-3D and Espresso-2D share a unified architecture of a modality-specific vision encoder (PartField or DINOv2), a text encoder (Recap-CLIP or CLIP), and a text-conditioned heatmap decoder that computes cross-modal similarity between vision features and the text embedding as the query — replacing learnable queries with text embeddings to natively support open-vocabulary grounding without predefined categories. Pretraining on Affogato-750K followed by fine-tuning on target benchmarks yields consistent gains across both modalities and prior architectures, with the largest improvements on unseen object-affordance combinations.

## Results

On the LASO 3D benchmark, Espresso-3D with Affogato-750K pretraining achieves 21.9 aIoU (seen) and 20.8 aIoU (unseen), outperforming PointRefer (20.8/14.6) and OpenAD (14.2/14.6) without pretraining. On AGD20K 2D zero-shot evaluation, Espresso-2D pretrained on Affogato-750K reaches 40.2 SIM (seen) and 37.6 SIM (unseen), surpassing the best baseline LISA-7B (29.6/25.6 SIM) by over 10 points. Under full supervision on AGD20K, Espresso-2D + Affogato-750K pretraining achieves 0.974 KLD and 51.9 SIM, beating AffordanceLLM (1.463 KLD, 37.7 SIM) and AffordanceNet (1.871 KLD, 30.5 SIM). The largest pretraining gains appear on unseen categories: PointRefer improves by +4.0 aIoU and Espresso-3D by +2.1 aIoU on LASO unseen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LASO 3D, Espresso-3D with Affogato-750K pretraining (21.9/20.8 aIoU seen/unseen) outperforms all baselines including PointRefer (20.8/14.6), IAGNet (17.8/12.9), and OpenAD (14.2/14.6); the pretraining benefit is architecture-agnostic, with PointRefer gaining +4.0 aIoU unseen and OpenAD gaining +0.9 aIoU unseen. On AGD20K 2D zero-shot, Espresso-2D (40.2/37.6 SIM) decisively beats LISA-7B (29.6/25.6), Molmo+SAM2 (26.1/22.6), M2SA-7B (25.8/22.7), and AffordanceNet (26.6/22.6) by 10–15 SIM points. Under full supervision on AGD20K, Espresso-2D + pretraining (0.974 KLD, 51.9 SIM) surpasses AffordanceLLM (1.463 KLD, 37.7 SIM) by 0.49 KLD and 14.2 SIM, and AffordanceNet (1.871 KLD, 30.5 SIM) by 0.90 KLD and 21.4 SIM. On the Affogato-750K test split, Espresso-3D (13.6 aIoU in-domain) beats PointRefer (10.5) and OpenAD (3.1), with cross-domain transfer from Daily-used to Furnitures reaching 18.2 aIoU. The only case where pretraining slightly degrades performance is PointRefer on LASO seen (20.2 vs. 20.8 aIoU), a 0.6-point drop that is more than offset by the +4.0-point unseen gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the paradigm of leveraging 2D foundation models to generate 3D supervision (as in Mosaic3D, LabelMaker, and PartField) to the functional affordance domain, chaining Gemma3 for query generation, Molmo for point prediction, and MobileSAM for mask generation with multi-view voting for 3D aggregation. Architecturally, Espresso-3D follows the PointRefer/LASO recipe of finetuning a pretrained 3D encoder (PartField) and text encoder (Recap-CLIP), while Espresso-2D adapts the OOAL design by removing learnable prompts and using text embeddings directly as decoder queries. The key departure from prior work is the fully automated, open-vocabulary annotation pipeline that replaces the manual labeling bottleneck, enabling >450 object classes and >350 affordance types versus the 23-class ceiling of all existing 3D benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Affogato-750K is the largest open-vocabulary affordance grounding dataset by an order of magnitude, and the fact that pretraining on it consistently improves both the authors' Espresso models and independent baselines (OpenAD, PointRefer) on unseen categories validates it as a broadly transferable supervision source rather than an architecture-specific trick. The reusable automated pipeline offers a practical path to scaling affordance annotations to other 3D repositories. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real gap (Objaverse renderings vs. in-the-wild scenes) limits downstream robotics deployment, and how to extend beyond spatial contact localization to temporal dynamics and force requirements, which the authors explicitly flag as future work.
