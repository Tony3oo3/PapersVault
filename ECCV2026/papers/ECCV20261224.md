## Problem

Preharvest rose quality evaluation (petal count, flower shape, stem length) is currently performed manually by human experts after harvesting, making it time-consuming and labor-intensive. While 3D computer vision offers a path to automate this process before harvest, no large-scale 3D dataset with localization annotations exists for rose cultivation scenarios, where severe inter-plant occlusion, petal fragility, and variable greenhouse lighting make standard 3D reconstruction approaches infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261224/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized multi-view RGB-D sequences at 1280×800 resolution and 30 Hz, captured by a chest-mounted structured-light camera (Orbbec Gemini 335L/336L) moving parallel to plant rows at 0.3–0.5 m/s and 0.8–1.2 m distance. Each of the 46,848 rose instances carries a 2D bounding box, five biologically-defined semantic keypoints (corolla center, sepal-pedicel junction, stem-pedicel transition, median point, soil-emergence point), four auxiliary inter-points for spatial interpolation, and corresponding 3D coordinates. Objects smaller than 32×32 pixels are excluded, and occluded keypoints are flagged as 'invisible' and removed from accuracy evaluation.

## Method

The dataset is built via a hybrid 2D-to-3D annotation pipeline: annotators manually label 2D bounding boxes and keypoints on RGB frames, which are then backprojected into 3D camera coordinates using synchronized depth maps and the camera intrinsic matrix (P_3D = K⁻¹·[u,v,1]ᵀ·D(u,v)), validated against tape-measure references with a mean absolute error of 0.14 cm. For 3D rose localization, two frameworks are proposed: (1) a depth-inferencer-based method that fuses predicted 2D keypoints with monocular depth estimates (zero-shot or fine-tuned), and (2) a multi-frame temporal optimization that uses a Re-ID model (OSNet) to match instances across adjacent frames, then refines the intervening frame's 3D keypoints via confidence-weighted fusion between the direct depth backprojection and a linear-interpolation temporal prior (P_t* = (w_d·P_t + w_t·P_interp)/(w_d + w_t)). The paper further benchmarks six vision tasks—2D detection, 2D keypoint localization, monocular depth estimation, local feature matching, instance re-identification, and 3D keypoint localization—using existing architectures (YOLOv5, DETR, RTMPose, ViTPose, Depth Anything, BinsFormer, RoMa, OSNet, etc.) to establish baselines on the new dataset.

## Results

For the primary 3D rose keypoint localization task, the best configuration (DEKR†+HRNet bottom-up keypoint model with ground-truth depth) achieves 522.8 mm MPJPE, while the best fully-predicted pipeline (DEKR†+HRNet with BinsFormer depth) reaches 654.3 mm MPJPE. Adding the multi-frame temporal optimization reduces MPJPE from 556.8 mm to 539.1 mm for the ViTPose+DETR pipeline. On the 2D detection benchmark, YOLOv5 leads with 35.0 AP (67.1 AP50), and for monocular depth estimation, BinsFormer achieves the best fine-tuned AbsRel of 0.165 while Depth Anything is the strongest zero-shot model (0.498 AbsRel).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Across 2D object detection, YOLOv5 (35.0 AP) outperforms RT-DETR (33.6), DETR (33.0), and Faster R-CNN (31.0), with the gap widening on medium objects (APM: 19.1 vs. 13.1–14.2). For 2D keypoint localization, bottom-up methods (YOLOXPose 25.0 AP, DEKR†+HRNet 24.9 AP) surpass top-down methods using a learned detector (ViTPose 21.9, RTMPose 16.6), but when ground-truth boxes are provided, top-down ViTPose jumps to 42.1 AP, far exceeding all bottom-up results. In monocular depth estimation, BinsFormer (0.165 AbsRel) and DepthFormer (0.181) outperform DPT (0.211) after fine-tuning, while zero-shot Depth Anything (0.498) beats ZoeDepth (0.591) and IEBins (0.554). For local feature matching, RoMa leads with 72.3 AUC@5°, narrowly ahead of DKM (71.1) and PMatch (70.7), while DeDoDe lags at 62.3. In instance re-identification, SOLIDER (80.7 mAP) and TransReID (80.6) edge out OSNet (75.8), and the unsupervised CC+CAJ baseline trails at 70.9 mAP. For the headline 3D localization task, BinsFormer depth consistently yields lower MPJPE than Depth Anything across all four 2D keypoint models (e.g., 644.5 vs. 650.6 for RTMPose), and the multi-frame temporal optimization provides a modest 17.7 mm improvement for ViTPose+DETR (556.8→539.1) but a smaller 16.2 mm gain for RTMPose+DETR (558.4→542.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the 3D vision dataset paradigm established in autonomous driving (KITTI, nuScenes) and indoor scenes (ScanNet, SUN RGB-D) into floriculture, but departs from their direct 3D annotation in point clouds by introducing a hybrid 2D-to-3D pipeline: manual 2D bounding boxes and botanical keypoints are back-projected through structured-light depth maps. For the 3D localization task, it repurposes human pose estimation architectures (RTMPose, ViTPose, HRNet, YOLOXPose) for rose-specific semantic keypoints (corolla center, sepal-pedicel junction, soil-emergence point), and adds a multi-frame temporal consistency optimization that has no direct analogue in prior agricultural 3D work. Unlike existing small-scale agricultural 3D datasets (Plant3D, ROSE-X, Pheno4D, Crops3D) that focus on plant reconstruction, Rosetum3D targets instance-level localization of individual flowers in dense, occluded cultivation rows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first large-scale 3D benchmark for rose cultivation, Rosetum3D provides the data infrastructure needed for automated preharvest quality grading, robotic harvesting, and AI-driven yield prediction in protected agriculture. However, the 3D localization results reveal a significant accuracy gap (MPJPE of ~520–670 mm), and the multi-frame temporal optimization yields only marginal gains while ignoring camera pose changes—indicating that the problem is substantially harder than 2D benchmarks suggest. Open questions include whether dedicated 3D architectures or learned depth models can close this gap, whether the benchmark generalizes to other floriculture crops, and how to handle the severe occlusion and small-object exclusion that currently limits annotation coverage.
