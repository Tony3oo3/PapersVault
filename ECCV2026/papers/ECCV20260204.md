## Problem

End-to-end WSI analysis suffers from two coupled limitations: (i) computational constraints force the use of low-magnification small patches and random sampling, causing fine-grained morphological information loss, and (ii) slide-level labels alone provide too sparse a supervision signal for long encoder-aggregator chains. The paper asks whether the intrinsic multi-resolution WSI pyramid can be turned into a dense, free self-supervision signal that recovers high-magnification morphology from low-cost inputs without additional annotation.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes low-resolution RGB patches at 5× magnification (512×512), with 160 instances per WSI selected by the sampling module. During training, each LR patch is paired with its spatially aligned HR counterpart at 20× (2048×2048) cropped from the WSI pyramid, which serves solely as a reconstruction target and is never fed into the encoder. At inference, the model outputs only a slide-level class probability; the SR branch is discarded, requiring no HR ground truth.

## Method

PyraE2E combines three components in a closed-loop end-to-end pipeline. First, an embedded Cluster-Score Sampling module balances exploration (uniform sampling from unseen instances) and exploitation (attention-weighted, cluster-diversified selection from seen instances) using online states (attention score, feature, seen count, cluster label) updated via EMA and periodic k-means. Second, a lightweight Global-Local Partitioned (GLP) encoder with N=8 blocks of Global-Local Fusion Units (strided max-pool global stream + depthwise-expanded local stream) and Partitioned Spatial Mixers (3×3 spatial mixing on only a fraction p of channels) produces HR-informed features from LR inputs. Third, joint optimization routes shared features to an SR head (3×3 conv + PixelShuffle×4 reconstruction supervised by L1 pixel loss and 2D-FFT frequency loss against the aligned HR patch) and an ABMIL slide-level prediction head, with a cluster-wise KL regularizer preventing attention collapse; MIL attention scores and instance features are fed back to update sampling states, closing the loop between representation learning and patch selection.

## Results

On TCGA-NSCLC, PyraE2E-X (ABMILX aggregator) achieves 92.0% ACC and 97.6% AUC, outperforming the best end-to-end baseline ABMILX-E2E (90.2% ACC, 96.3% AUC) and the CONCH foundation model (91.8% ACC, 97.0% AUC). On TCGA-RCC, PyraE2E-X reaches 93.1% ACC and 98.3% AUC, surpassing CONCH-ABMILX (92.8% ACC, 99.3% AUC) in accuracy while matching its AUC. On TCGA-BRCA, PyraE2E-X attains 90.5% ACC and 93.5% AUC, trailing CONCH-ABMILX (92.0% ACC, 94.7% AUC) but exceeding all end-to-end and self-supervised baselines. The model uses only 2M parameters and 1.1 days of training on a single A800 GPU, compared to 90M+ parameters and multi-GPU pretraining for foundation models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PyraE2E-X outperforms all end-to-end baselines across all three datasets: it beats ABMILX-E2E by +1.8 ACC on RCC, +1.8 ACC on NSCLC, and +3.5 ACC on BRCA, while also improving AUC by 0.2–3.4 points. Against foundation models, PyraE2E-X matches or exceeds CONCH-ABMILX on RCC (93.1 vs. 92.8 ACC) and NSCLC (92.0 vs. 91.8 ACC), but trails by 1.5 ACC and 1.2 AUC on BRCA (90.5 vs. 92.0 ACC; 93.5 vs. 94.7 AUC). Compared to the best two-stage MIL methods (FOCUS, 2D Mamba), PyraE2E-X gains +1.5 to +2.5 ACC on RCC and NSCLC. Versus self-supervised baselines (SimCLR-X, CDSR), PyraE2E-X consistently leads by 1.5–2.5 ACC on all datasets. Notably, PyraE2E achieves these results with only 2M parameters and 1.1 days of single-GPU training, versus 90–307M parameters and multi-GPU pretraining for foundation models, or 6.4 days for SimCLR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of end-to-end WSI learning (C2C, Streaming, ABMILX) and self-supervised auxiliary objectives for pathology, but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than using contrastive or masked-reconstruction signals that prior SSL-for-WSI work (SimCLR, CDSR, ICMIL) introduces at extra cost, PyraE2E repurposes the intrinsic multi-resolution pyramid of every WSI as a free cross-scale super-resolution target. The lightweight GLP encoder and embedded Cluster-Score Sampling module are novel architectural contributions, but the conceptual lineage traces back to attention-based MIL (ABMIL/CLAM) for aggregation, PixelShuffle-based SR for the reconstruction head, and the exploration-exploitation trade-off from active learning for patch selection. The paper explicitly positions itself against the two-stage MIL paradigm's domain gap and against end-to-end baselines' information loss from low-magnification small patches, framing SR as the missing dense supervision that prior self-supervised approaches could not deliver at WSI scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is that a 2M-parameter, no-pretraining, end-to-end pipeline achieves AUC competitive with 90–307M-parameter foundation models (UNI, CONCH) on three TCGA benchmarks while training in ~1.1 days on a single A800, which is a meaningful efficiency win for labs without multi-GPU pretraining budgets. The ablation showing that removing the FFT loss drops AUC by ~4 points (97.6→93.6) is a useful signal that frequency-domain constraints are non-trivial for preserving morphological detail in histopathology. Open questions include whether the SR-as-supervision strategy transfers to segmentation or detection tasks, whether the GLP encoder generalizes beyond the tested magnification ratios (5×→20×), and whether the moderate SR fidelity (PSNR ~24–25) limits how much morphological information the encoder actually recovers versus what a dedicated SR model would produce.
