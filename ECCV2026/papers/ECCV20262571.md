## Problem

Existing zero-shot 6D pose estimation methods rely on multi-stage pipelines with iterative render-and-compare refinement or coarse-to-fine hypothesis selection, incurring substantial computational overhead and implementation complexity. A key underlying issue is that these methods do not explicitly model the conditional posterior over poses under ambiguity, and no prior work provides a unified, instance-level generative formulation that handles both model-based (CAD) and model-free (reference-view) settings in a single refinement-free stage.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB or RGB-D crop of a detected object (with optional depth) and an object-specific geometric representation—either a CAD mesh rendered into multi-view templates or a small set of reference views (3–16 images) with known poses reconstructed via sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting. Both representations are converted into a shared point-token format (sampled 2D/2.5D points with ViT patch features) and fused via cross-attention into a single conditioning vector. The output is a 6D pose (SO(3) rotation + R³ translation) produced by a single 20-step ODE integration; in the model-based setting one hypothesis is returned directly, while the model-free setting draws M>1 samples and aggregates them via likelihood-weighted Fréchet mean on SO(3) and Euclidean weighted mean on R³.

## Method

UF0-6D reformulates zero-shot 6D pose estimation as conditional Riemannian flow matching on SE(3), learning a time-dependent velocity field v_θ(t, g_t, c) that transports an isotropic base distribution on the Lie algebra to the instance-conditioned pose posterior. The training objective matches the network's output to geodesic-consistent bridge velocities—Lie-algebra geodesic interpolation for rotation and Euclidean interpolation for translation—under a product bi-invariant Riemannian metric. Symmetry-induced pose multiplicity is handled by selecting the symmetry-consistent target that minimizes the se(3) geodesic transport cost (a Riemannian optimal-transport-inspired rule), avoiding ad-hoc symmetry-specific heads. At inference, a single ODE integration pass (20 steps by default) replaces all coarse-to-refine cascades and render-and-compare loops; for multi-hypothesis model-free inference, log-likelihoods are computed via Hutchinson's trace estimator on the flow divergence, and the final pose is a closed-form weighted aggregation rather than iterative refinement.

## Results

On the seven core BOP datasets, UF0-6D achieves a mean AR of 81.2 (RGB-D) and 70.6 (RGB) in a single-stage, refinement-free setting, surpassing the previous best no-refinement method FreeZe (69.3 AR, RGB-D) by +11.9 AR and the best refinement-based multi-hypothesis method Co-op (75.2 AR, RGB-D) by +6.0 AR. The RGB variant runs at 0.379 s/image, making it the fastest among all high-performing coarse pose estimators, while the RGB-D variant (0.832 s) remains significantly faster than refinement-based competitors (Co-op: 7.162 s; FoundationPose: 29.317 s). In the model-free setting on YCB-Video, UF0-6D with only 12 reference views achieves 97.6 ADD-S / 91.8 ADD, outperforming FoundationPose (97.4 / 91.5) which requires 16 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UF0-6D decisively outperforms all refinement-free baselines: it beats FreeZe (69.3 AR) by +11.9 AR on RGB-D and Co-op coarse (58.4 AR) by +12.2 AR on RGB, while also being 35× faster than FreeZe (0.379 s vs. 13.474 s). Against the strongest refinement-based methods, UF0-6D RGB-D (81.2 AR) surpasses Co-op multi-hypothesis (75.2 AR) by +6.0 AR and FoundationPose (73.4 AR) by +7.8 AR, despite using no iterative render-and-compare loop. The RGB variant (70.6 AR) even edges out SAM-6D (70.4 AR, RGB-D with refinement) while being 11.5× faster. In the model-free setting, UF0-6D with 12 reference views (97.6 ADD-S / 91.8 ADD) surpasses FoundationPose (97.4 / 91.5 with 16 views) and substantially outperforms FS6D-DPM (88.4 / 42.1, which requires fine-tuning). The only scenario where UF0-6D does not lead is against Co-op single-hypothesis RGB-D (73.6 AR), but UF0-6D still wins by +7.6 AR while being 2.8× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

UF0-6D extends the Riemannian flow matching framework (Chen & Lipman, ICLR 2024) and its direct predecessor RFMPose (Ouyang et al., NeurIPS 2026) from category-level pose priors to instance-level conditional posteriors on SE(3). The key conceptual departure from the dominant zero-shot pose estimation paradigm (MegaPose, Co-op, FoundationPose) is the elimination of iterative render-and-compare refinement: rather than producing a coarse hypothesis and then refining it through repeated alignment, UF0-6D learns the full conditional posterior q(g|c) via a single ODE integration pass. The geometry-conditioning design that maps both CAD templates and reference-view 3D Gaussian Splatting proxies into a shared point-token representation is a novel unification not present in prior model-based or model-free pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single-stage flow-based posterior can outperform multi-stage refinement pipelines by +6.0 AR (RGB-D) while being 3–70× faster challenges the field's working assumption that iterative alignment is necessary to resolve discretization and symmetry ambiguities. This opens a path toward latency-sensitive deployment (real-time manipulation, AR) where refinement budgets are prohibitive. Limitations include sensitivity to large initial pose deviations (trajectories can converge to wrong modes) and the need for 20 ODE steps for peak accuracy, suggesting the learned flow's basin of attraction is not yet globally robust. Open questions include whether the approach extends to articulated objects, multi-object scenes with interactions, or settings where the object representation itself is highly uncertain.
