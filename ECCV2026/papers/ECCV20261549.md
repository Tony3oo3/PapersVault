## Problem

Text encoders for diffusion models lack an efficient evaluation framework that predicts downstream generation quality without training a full generative model end-to-end, and adapting pretrained LLMs/MLLMs to produce text representations well-suited for visual synthesis remains non-trivial. Standard NLP and retrieval benchmarks misalign with how diffusion models consume sequence-level hidden states, while end-to-end evaluation is computationally prohibitive (~50 hours per encoder).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261549/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a full sequence of fused, layer-normalized hidden states (not a single pooled vector) that serve as conditioning tokens for a Diffusion Transformer. For evaluation, TED-6K takes a source caption paired with one positive and multiple negative statements, and the context aggregator compresses each encoder's sequence-level output into a single context token for similarity-based scoring. The layer-wise weighting module outputs a weighted sum over all L transformer layers, preserving per-token granularity throughout the sequence.

## Method

GRAN-TED is built in two stages: first, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct is fine-tuned on a large-scale VQA and captioning dataset curated for visual-generation-relevant semantics (object attributes, spatial/temporal relations, coreference), specializing the MLLM's text representations toward generation. Second, a layer-wise weighting module applies LayerNorm to each layer's hidden states and learns scalar weights via softmax to compute a weighted sum across all layers, replacing the fixed Norm-Avg strategy. To prevent the evolving diffusion training objective from causing weight drift and a non-stationary conditioning distribution, the weights are trained jointly with the diffusion model for an initial phase and then frozen for the remainder of training. For evaluation, a lightweight context aggregator (two self-attention layers plus a learnable context token) is trained with contrastive loss on 500k caption pairs to uniformly score any encoder's sequence-level representations on TED-6K, achieving ~750× faster iteration than training a diffusion model from scratch.

## Results

On the TED-6K text-only benchmark, GRAN-TED achieves 57.42 accuracy (Norm-Avg), outperforming all evaluated encoders including the 32B-parameter Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (57.24) and the base Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (56.81). In downstream T2I generation, GRAN-TED scores 77.41 on GenAI-Bench and 3.035 on UnifiedReward, improving over the Qwen3-VL-8B baseline by +1.24 and +0.007 respectively. For T2V, GRAN-TED reaches 80.33 on GenAI-Bench and 1.424 on VideoAlign, a +2.39 and +0.136 gain over the same baseline. TED-6K scores show a Pearson correlation of r=0.9923 (p=1.11×10⁻⁶) with T2I GenAI-Bench and r=0.9866 (p=1.87×10⁻³) with T2V VideoAlign, validating the benchmark as a reliable proxy for generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAN-TED (8B) outperforms all nine baselines on TED-6K under the Norm-Avg protocol. Against encoder-only models, the margin is substantial: CLIP-ViT-L-336 scores 45.13 and UMT5-XXL 51.41, both trailing GRAN-TED by 12.3 and 6.0 points respectively. Among LLM baselines, Llama3-8B-Instruct (55.93) and InternLM3-8B-Instruct (55.46) are surpassed by 1.5 and 2.0 points. The closest MLLM competitors are Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (57.24, a 4× larger model) and Ovis-2.5-9B (56.26), with GRAN-TED edging ahead by 0.18 and 1.16 points. Most notably, GRAN-TED improves over its own backbone Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (56.81) by 0.61 points on TED-6K, translating to +1.24 GenAI-Bench and +0.007 UnifiedReward in T2I, and +2.39 GenAI-Bench and +0.136 VideoAlign in T2V, confirming that the targeted VQA fine-tuning and layer-wise weighting yield consistent downstream gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trajectory of text encoder selection for diffusion models (CLIP → T5 → LLMs/MLLMs) by addressing two gaps: the absence of an efficient text-only evaluation proxy and the under-exploited potential of multi-layer feature aggregation. It builds directly on the Norm-Avg strategy (Wang et al. 2025; Li et al. 2026) by replacing its fixed uniform weighting with learnable layer-wise weights, and departs from end-to-end evaluation benchmarks (GenAI-Bench, VIEScore) and single-vector retrieval proxies (MTEB) by introducing a context aggregator that mimics how DiTs actually consume sequence-level text embeddings. The two-step training strategy (joint optimization followed by weight freezing) is a practical departure from continuously learnable conditioning, motivated by the non-stationarity of diffusion training objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 750× speedup for encoder evaluation (4 minutes vs. ~50 hours of diffusion training) with a Pearson correlation of r≈0.99 to downstream generation quality makes TED-6K a potentially transformative tool for the diffusion model community, enabling rapid iteration over encoder choices without prohibitive compute. The finding that MLLMs outperform their LLM backbones on a purely text-only benchmark suggests multimodal pretraining shapes text representations in ways beneficial for visual grounding, even without visual input at inference. Limitations include the benchmark's relatively narrow score range (55–57) limiting fine-grained discrimination, its restriction to text-conditioned T2I/T2V (excluding editing, TI2V, 3D), and the small number of encoders (5–8) used for correlation validation, which, while consistent across metrics, leaves open questions about robustness at scale.
