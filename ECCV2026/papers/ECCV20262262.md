## Problem

Existing image-based RUL prediction methods rely on time-frequency representations (spectrograms, wavelets) that capture spectral energy variations but overlook the intrinsic geometric structure of system dynamics. Degradation in mechanical systems manifests as progressive dispersion of the phase-space attractor, a structural transition that is invisible to conventional spectral heatmaps. The paper asks whether a geometry-aware visual representation grounded in phase-space reconstruction can make vibration-based RUL prediction more compatible with standard vision architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1D vibration signal (multi-channel, C channels of length N) and converts it into a C-channel Phase-Space Density Image (PSDI) of spatial size H×W, where each channel is a log-compressed 2D histogram of the trajectory's visitation frequency over quantile-bounded bins derived from healthy reference data. A companion 1D trajectory signal (the PC1 coordinate resampled over time) is also extracted to preserve temporal ordering. The output is a single normalized RUL estimate in [0,1] per time step, produced by a compact student network after sliding-window temporal aggregation over K consecutive frames.

## Method

The core representation, PSDI, applies Takens time-delay embedding with globally fixed parameters (τ*, m* estimated via mutual information and Cao's method from healthy data), projects all trajectories onto a shared 2D PCA basis anchored to the healthy centroid, and renders a 2D density histogram with log compression and quantile-based bin boundaries to produce coordinate-consistent images across degradation stages. The Globally Anchored Reconstruction (GAR) strategy freezes the reference frame (μ, U, B) once from healthy data, eliminating coordinate drift that would otherwise confound true degradation with arbitrary geometric transformations. For prediction, a knowledge distillation framework pairs a high-capacity pretrained teacher (e.g., CLIP, MAE, EfficientNet-B3) with a compact student (e.g., Tiny-ViT, EfficientNet-B0), using multi-scale feature alignment, temperature-scaled KL and cosine feature distillation, attention distillation, and sliding-window temporal aggregation (proven to reduce prediction variance as O(1/K)) to transfer degradation-relevant visual priors while suppressing short-term jitter.

## Results

On the XJTU-SY and PHM bearing datasets across five case studies, the proposed PSDI + KD framework (CLIP teacher → Tiny-ViT student) achieves the lowest RMSE in 4 out of 5 cases (0.099, 0.071, 0.067, 0.060) and the lowest MAPE in 3 out of 5 cases (0.156, 0.039, 0.055). The only case where a baseline outperforms the method is Case 2, where TimeMixer++ achieves 0.079 RMSE and 0.026 MAPE versus 0.107 and 0.070 for the proposed method. The method also outperforms its own teacher-only variant (Only Teacher) in Cases 1, 2, 4, and 5, confirming that the compact student with sliding-window aggregation adds value beyond the high-capacity pretrained backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical RUL baselines (AE-TCA-LSSVM, 2D-CLSTM, CNN-ORC, Bi-LSTMF, CLSTMF), the proposed method dominates across all five cases by a wide margin, with RMSE improvements of 0.03–0.23 over the best classical method in each case. Among the modern time-series forecasting baselines, the method outperforms PatchTST, TimesNet, and TimeMixer on RMSE in all five cases and on MAPE in four of five. The closest competitor is TimeMixer++ (ICLR 2025), which wins Case 2 (0.079 vs. 0.107 RMSE; 0.026 vs. 0.070 MAPE) and Case 5 MAPE (0.035 vs. 0.040), but the proposed method achieves lower RMSE in Cases 1, 3, 4, and 5 (e.g., 0.067 vs. 0.094 in Case 4, 0.060 vs. 0.086 in Case 5). The method also surpasses its own teacher-only ablation in four of five cases, indicating that the compact student with temporal aggregation and feature/attention distillation provides a net benefit over directly deploying the high-capacity CLIP backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Takens' phase-space reconstruction—a classical tool from nonlinear dynamical systems—into a computer-vision pipeline for bearing RUL prediction, departing from the dominant time-frequency (STFT, wavelet) and structural (Recurrence Plot, GAF) image representations that characterize degradation via spectral energy or temporal correlation. The globally anchored reconstruction (GAR) is a novel contribution that addresses a practical gap: standard delay-embedding produces arbitrary coordinate frames per sample, making cross-sample comparison meaningless without a shared reference. The knowledge distillation framework builds on the broader trend of transferring pretrained vision priors (CLIP, MAE) to time-series tasks (VisionTS, TimeVLM), but adds sliding-window temporal aggregation and multi-scale feature alignment specifically motivated by prediction jitter in RUL estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a physically grounded visual representation where degradation manifests as geometric dispersion of the system attractor, which is arguably more interpretable and mechanistically meaningful than spectral heatmaps. The anchoring ablation (Silhouette 0.101 → 0.516) demonstrates that without a shared reference frame, phase-space images are essentially uninformative—a practical caveat for anyone attempting similar representations. Key limitations include the use of local signal windows (no full run-to-failure history or cumulative damage modeling), validation restricted to bearing vibration data, and the fact that PSDI does not uniformly dominate all baselines (TimeMixer++ still wins Case 2 and Case 5 MAPE). Open questions include whether adaptive or history-aware reference frames can capture non-stationary degradation, and whether the geometric-dispersion signal generalizes beyond rolling-element bearings to other mechanical systems.
