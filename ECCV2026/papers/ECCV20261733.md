## Problem

Existing deep correspondence learning pipelines universally rely on a differentiable weighted least squares (WLS) estimator to recover geometric model parameters, which imposes a strict conditional independence assumption among all observations. In practice, inliers are sampled from continuous geometric manifolds and cluster in texture-rich regions, creating probabilistic dependencies that cause dense inlier groups to dominate the WLS optimization and produce poorly-conditioned, noise-sensitive estimates. No prior method explicitly models these structured inlier correlations during the parameter estimation stage.

## Input / Output

The estimator is a plug-in module that replaces the final WLS layer in an existing correspondence learning network. It consumes the same data matrix M constructed from putative correspondences (up to 2000–4000 SIFT matches per image pair), but additionally takes the network's high-dimensional per-correspondence features and predicted inlier probabilities to build a graph structure. The output is the geometric model parameter vector z* (e.g., the 9-element fundamental matrix or homography), extracted as the right singular vector of the smallest singular value of a 9×9 information matrix via differentiable SVD.

## Method

The paper formulates inlier residuals as a product of a diagonal uncertainty matrix Λ and a latent structural variable η, then models η as a Gaussian Markov Random Field over a graph whose precision matrix is a regularized graph Laplacian Q_struct = D − Ã + αI. The adjacency matrix Ã is built from a learnable Gaussian kernel on ResNet-embedded network features and is outlier-filtered by element-wise multiplication with inlier probabilities (Ã = diag(p)·A·diag(p)), soft-pruning edges connected to likely outliers. The final information matrix H_G = M^T Λ^{-1} Q_struct Λ^{-1} M is solved via differentiable SVD, yielding a closed-form, fully differentiable estimator that generalizes WLS (reducing to it when the graph has no edges). All hyperparameters (kernel bandwidth γ, regularization α, and the weight vector Λ^{-1}) are learnable, and the module adds only ~0.1M parameters and ~1 ms of inference time to the host network.

## Results

On the YFCC100M relative pose estimation benchmark, equipping existing networks with the structure-aware estimator (denoted *) yields consistent AUC/mAP gains of approximately +5.3 to +5.7 points at 5° and +3.6 to +4.7 points at 10° across all tested architectures. The best absolute result is DeMatch++* achieving 40.03/71.15 AUC/mAP @5°, 60.67/79.04 @10°, and 76.13/86.04 @20°. The computational overhead is modest: approximately +0.12M parameters, +1.3 ms inference time, and +0.24G FLOPs per image. On homography estimation (HPatches), the estimator improves @3px accuracy by up to +8.1 points (UMatch*), and on Aachen Day-Night visual localization, nighttime gains reach +8.4 points at the 5m/10° threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The structure-aware estimator is integrated as a plug-in replacement for the WLS estimator in six correspondence learning networks, all of which show consistent improvements. DeMatch++* achieves the highest absolute AUC @5° of 40.03 (vs. 34.33 for DeMatch++), a +5.70 point gain; DeMo* reaches 38.01 (vs. 32.66 for DeMo), a +5.35 gain; and DeMatch* reaches 36.58 (vs. 30.95), a +5.63 gain. ConvMatch* improves by +5.66 AUC @5° and UMatch* by +3.45 AUC @5°, confirming broad applicability. The estimator outperforms RANSAC and MAGSAC++ used as post-processing (Table 8: DeMatch+MAGSAC++ reaches 34.18/62.88 @5° vs. DeMatch*'s 36.58/67.92). However, the paper acknowledges limited gains on pruning-based architectures (CLNet, BCLNet) where hard pruning disrupts the graph structure the estimator relies on, and cross-domain generalization from outdoor YFCC100M to indoor SUN3D remains unstable without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard differentiable weighted least squares (WLS) estimator—ubiquitous in correspondence learning pipelines (e.g., the weighted eight-point algorithm of Ranftl & Koltun)—by replacing its diagonal precision matrix with a graph Laplacian-derived one, grounded in Gaussian Markov Random Field theory. While prior deep correspondence learning methods (OANet, DeMatch, ConvMatch, UMatch, etc.) have made substantial progress in inlier classification via permutation-equivariant networks, motion fields, and graph convolutions, they all terminate in the same WLS estimator that assumes conditional independence among observations. The paper's key departure is identifying this independence assumption as a fundamental bottleneck and proposing a plug-in structure-aware estimator that explicitly models inlier correlations, without altering the upstream network architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because the WLS estimator is a universal terminal component across nearly all correspondence learning pipelines, a drop-in replacement that consistently improves estimation accuracy (with only ~2% parameter overhead) has broad practical impact for relative pose, homography, and visual localization tasks. The spectral gap ratio analysis provides a clean theoretical explanation for why clustered inliers degrade WLS and how the graph Laplacian mitigates this. However, two open questions remain: the estimator's incompatibility with pruning-based architectures (CLNet, BCLNet) limits its universality, and the cross-domain generalization gap (outdoor→indoor) suggests the learned structural correlations are domain-specific, pointing toward future work on domain-adaptive or zero-shot structural priors.
