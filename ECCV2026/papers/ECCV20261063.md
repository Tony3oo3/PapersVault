## Problem

Standard 3D diffusion policies (e.g., DP3) rely on sparse point clouds that become perceptually ambiguous in cluttered scenes, where target objects are partially occluded or intermingled with visually similar distractors. This ambiguity causes the policy to fail at binding the language-specified target to its 3D extent, leading to unstable grasps and actions drifting toward distractors as clutter increases. The paper addresses how to inject object-level geometric cues into 3D diffusion policies in a lightweight, geometry-preserving way that does not require learned RGB-3D feature fusion or modification of the diffusion backbone.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes synchronized RGB images, depth-reconstructed 3D point clouds (with calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics), proprioceptive state, and a fixed task-level text query (e.g., an object name) that is identical across training and evaluation. The RGB image is used exclusively by a frozen grounding-and-segmentation pipeline to produce a 2D mask; it is never fed as a feature into the policy network. The output is an H-step action trajectory (H=16, action dimension d_a) generated by iterative denoising from Gaussian noise, executed asynchronously with a prediction horizon of 16 and execution window of 8 at 10 Hz.

## Method

Attention-DP3 keeps the DP3 conditional U-Net denoiser and noise-prediction objective unchanged, augmenting only the conditioning vector with a geometry-aligned attention feature. A frozen Grounding DINO + SAM2 pipeline predicts a text-conditioned 2D mask on the RGB image, which is deterministically lifted onto the 3D point cloud via calibrated pinhole projection (nearest-neighbor lookup), yielding a per-point binary indicator. From this indicator, three complementary scalar fields are constructed per point: Targetness (the binary mask value), Intra-target Saliency (a normalized Euclidean distance transform within the mask, emphasizing geometrically central or contact-relevant subregions), and Backgroundness (1 minus the mask, preserving distractor context for occlusion reasoning). A lightweight field encoder (shared point-wise MLP followed by symmetric pooling) aggregates the three-channel per-point fields into a compact attention vector, which is concatenated with the point-cloud geometry embedding and proprioception embedding to form the FiLM conditioning for the denoiser. Only the field encoder and downstream DP3 parameters are learned; the segmentation models, mask lifting, and field construction are entirely training-free, adding just 3.8M parameters and ~0.001 s per control step over DP3.

## Results

On Adroit, Attention-DP3 achieves an average success rate of 0.78, surpassing DP3 (0.68) and VITA (0.77). On DexArt, it reaches 0.56 average versus 0.52 for DP3 and 0.55 for VITA. On MetaWorld, the overall success rate is 0.726, exceeding DP3 (0.669) and VITA (0.683), with the largest gains on spatial-reasoning tasks (Push-Wall: +0.43, Pick-Place: +0.42 over DP3). On the real-world SO101 platform, the average success rate improves from 0.52 (DP3) to 0.73 (Attention-DP3), and under heavy visual clutter the method maintains up to +31% advantage over DP3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Attention-DP3 outperforms the primary 3D baseline DP3 [45] across all four benchmarks: Adroit (0.78 vs 0.68), DexArt (0.56 vs 0.52), MetaWorld (0.726 vs 0.669), and SO101 (0.73 vs 0.52). Against the strongest image-based generative policy VITA [7], it wins on Adroit (0.78 vs 0.77), DexArt (0.56 vs 0.55), and MetaWorld (0.726 vs 0.683), with the largest margin on MetaWorld's hard and very-hard splits. Among 3D spatial-aware methods, it surpasses H³DP [23] (0.56 vs 0.53 on DexArt) and FreqPolicy [36] (0.56 vs 0.54 on DexArt). The 2D-only baselines—DP [5], AdaFlow [11], CP [27], IBC [6], and BCRNN [24]—are substantially outperformed, with Adroit averages ranging from 0.03 to 0.32 versus 0.78 for Attention-DP3. Under systematic clutter stress tests, DP3 degrades sharply (e.g., 0.52→0.13 on SO101 with added distractors) while Attention-DP3 remains stable (0.73→0.45), confirming that the tri-field attentional conditioning provides meaningful robustness gains beyond clean-scene performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DP3 (3D Diffusion Policy) by injecting object-level semantic cues through a lightweight attentional conditioning module, while keeping the DP3 conditional U-Net denoiser and noise-prediction objective entirely unchanged. It departs from prior semantic-guided 3D control methods (e.g., Lift3D, RoboGround) that rely on learned cross-modal feature fusion or explicit mask-based cropping, instead using deterministic calibrated 2D-to-3D projection of frozen open-vocabulary segmentation masks (Grounding DINO + SAM2) to construct geometry-aligned per-point fields. The Tri-Field Attentional Conditioning design—decomposing guidance into targetness, intra-target saliency, and backgroundness—is a novel factorization not seen in prior 3D policy work, and the explicit lifting strategy avoids the brittleness of learned RGB-3D alignment under clutter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important failure mode—clutter-induced target ambiguity in manipulation—showing that a minimal, training-free geometric lifting step plus a small learned field encoder (3.8M params) can substantially stabilize 3D diffusion policies under unseen distractors (+31% under heavy clutter). However, the diagnostic failure analysis reveals that most Attention-DP3 failures stem from Grounding DINO missed detections rather than the attention mechanism itself, suggesting the upper bound is gated by 2D perception quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object or articulated-target manipulation, how it behaves without calibrated camera intrinsics, and whether the frozen perception front-end can be replaced by a learned component without losing the geometric precision that motivates the explicit lifting design.
