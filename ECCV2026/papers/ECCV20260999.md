## Problem

Existing weakly supervised semantic segmentation methods entangle semantic recognition and object localization within a single classification objective, causing models to focus on sparse discriminative regions and requiring time-consuming multi-stage retraining or unstable end-to-end joint optimization to recover complete object extents. Although visual foundation models offer strong geometric and semantic priors, prior approaches still integrate them through coupled fine-tuning or distillation, failing to fully exploit their capabilities while remaining vulnerable to pseudo-label noise.

## Input / Output

During the offline feature bank construction stage, the method consumes training images with image-level class labels to generate pseudo-masks via CorrCLIP (with logits filtering to suppress hallucinated categories) and extract feature prototypes from a frozen C-RADIOv4-SO400M ViT backbone. At inference, only a single RGB test image is required: a frozen class-agnostic mask proposer (EntitySeg) generates geometric proposals, which are then matched against the pre-built feature bank via cosine similarity retrieval. The output is a pixel-level semantic segmentation map produced by confidence-priority rasterization of the retrieved class assignments, with no parameter updates at any stage.

## Method

ModuSeg explicitly decouples object discovery from semantic assignment into two independent, training-free stages. In the offline stage, image-level labels drive pseudo-mask generation, which is then refined by Semantic Boundary Purification (iterative morphological erosion to discard ambiguous edge patches) and Soft-Masked Feature Aggregation (area-based interpolation of masks onto the ViT feature grid to compute soft-weighted prototype vectors), followed by prototype-distance-based outlier rejection to tighten intra-class compactness. In the online stage, a frozen class-agnostic mask proposer extracts geometric proposals with reliable boundaries; each proposal's visual embedding is retrieved against the feature bank using Top-K cosine similarity, and a hierarchical voting strategy (majority class count with cumulative-similarity tie-breaking) assigns the semantic label. Overlapping proposals are resolved via class-specific NMS and confidence-priority rasterization, ensuring high-confidence predictions take precedence. The entire pipeline requires no backpropagation or parameter fine-tuning, making it modular and compatible with swapping in stronger foundation model backbones.

## Results

ModuSeg achieves 86.3% mIoU on PASCAL VOC 2012 val, 86.6% on VOC test, and 56.7% on MS COCO 2014 val, all without any parameter training. These results surpass the previous best training-based methods (SSR at 79.5/79.6/50.6 and VPL at 79.3/79.0/49.8) by 6.8–9.1% mIoU across the three benchmarks. On ADE20K and Cityscapes, the framework further demonstrates generalizability, reaching 44.7% and 66.0% mIoU respectively with the CorrCLIP backbone. Training time is only 84 minutes on a single RTX 3090 (5.3 GB GPU memory), compared to 270–1496 minutes for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ModuSeg outperforms all 21 compared baselines on every reported metric, and does so without any training. Against the strongest single-stage method SSR (AAAI'26), ModuSeg gains +6.8% mIoU on VOC val (86.3 vs 79.5), +7.0% on VOC test (86.6 vs 79.6), and +6.1% on COCO (56.7 vs 50.6). Against the strongest multi-stage method VPL (AAAI'25), the margins are +7.0% (VOC val), +7.6% (VOC test), and +6.9% (COCO). Compared to CLIP-based methods like WeCLIP (CVPR'24) and ExCEL (CVPR'25), ModuSeg leads by 9.9% and 7.9% on VOC val respectively, indicating that the decoupled retrieval paradigm substantially outperforms coupled optimization even when the latter leverages the same foundation model backbones. Against SAM-based methods such as S2C (CVPR'24), the advantage is +8.1% on VOC val, showing that using a class-agnostic segmenter for localization while delegating semantics to retrieval is more effective than using SAM to refine CAMs during training. Notably, ModuSeg also achieves the lowest training cost (84 min, 5.3 GB) among all compared methods, including single-stage approaches like WeCLIP (270 min, 6.2 GB) and multi-stage methods like MCTformer+ (1496 min, 18.0 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant WSSS paradigm that couples semantic classification and object localization within a single optimization objective (whether multi-stage retraining or end-to-end joint training). Rather than refining pseudo-labels through iterative network updates, ModuSeg repurposes two well-studied components—class-agnostic mask proposers (EntitySeg, SAM 2) for geometry and dense visual foundation models (C-RADIOv4, DINOv2/v3) for semantics—into a non-parametric retrieval pipeline, drawing conceptually from prototype learning (SSR, ExCEL) but eliminating all gradient-based optimization. The key lineage shift is from 'learn a segmentation head on noisy pseudo-labels' to 'retrieve the nearest semantic prototype for a geometrically clean proposal,' effectively treating WSSS as a nearest-neighbor classification problem over a curated feature bank.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, modular design offers a practical 10× reduction in training time (84 min vs. 270–1496 min for competitors) while achieving a 7-point mIoU gain over prior SOTA, making it attractive for settings where retraining is infeasible. However, the oracle analysis (Table 6) reveals that the primary bottleneck is the class-agnostic segmenter's tendency to over-partition backgrounds (95.7% with GT masks vs. 86.3% with EntitySeg), capping the system's ceiling independent of retrieval quality. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to highly cluttered or fine-grained scenes where class-agnostic proposers struggle, and whether the 25% outlier rejection and 20-iteration erosion parameters generalize without per-dataset tuning. The modular architecture also means the method will automatically benefit from future improvements in either the geometric or semantic foundation model, positioning it as a potentially long-lived baseline.
