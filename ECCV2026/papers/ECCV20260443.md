## Problem

Few-step streaming autoregressive video generators, which combine causal AR structure with consistency-model or few-step ODE distillation for low-latency synthesis, are difficult to align via RLHF. Existing SDE-based GRPO methods assume intermediate noise injections carry meaningful exploration entropy, but in these near-deterministic few-step samplers, sample diversity is governed almost entirely by the initial chunk noise, rendering intermediate SDE exploration ineffective and policy gradient signals near-zero.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260443/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a full video sequence generated autoregressively chunk-by-chunk in a streaming fashion, with each chunk denoised via a few-step consistency model solver. During alignment, a sequence-level reward (from the VideoAlign suite: text alignment, video quality, motion quality) is computed on the completed video, and gradient updates are confined to a single randomly selected pivot chunk. Training prompts are drawn from MovieGen Video Bench, and evaluation uses both in-domain VideoAlign scores and out-of-domain VBench metrics.

## Method

AR-CoPO adapts the Neighbor GRPO contrastive objective to streaming AR generation by introducing a chunk-level forking mechanism: at a randomly selected pivot chunk, the base initial noise is perturbed into G neighbors, each forked into an independent branch that is autoregressively completed to a full video, while all non-pivot noise sources are shared identically across branches to ensure clean credit assignment. For consistency-model samplers, the surrogate transition distribution is defined over x̂₀ prediction distances rather than intermediate x_t latents, since CMs perform a one-step noisy-to-clean mapping where intermediate-space distances conflate noise scale with semantics. A semi-on-policy training strategy complements on-policy exploration by fixing rollouts to a reference policy and exploiting a pre-collected replay buffer within a ratio-clipped trust region, addressing the failure of local noise-space exploration to improve global semantic rewards like text alignment. The two paradigms are trained as independent LoRA adapters and merged at inference, with the merge scale selected by a dual-benchmark criterion requiring simultaneous improvement on both in-domain (VideoAlign) and out-of-domain (VBench) metrics to avoid reward hacking.

## Results

On the merged AR-CoPO model (Self-Forcing + semi-on-policy + on-policy LoRA), VideoAlign Overall improves from 7.76 to 8.22 while VBench Total is maintained at 82.17 (vs. 82.15 baseline), confirming genuine alignment rather than reward hacking. Semi-on-policy training alone surpasses all streaming AR baselines on VBench Total (82.45 vs. 82.31 for LongLive), with gains on both Quality (85.15) and Semantic (71.68) dimensions. The on-policy variant achieves the highest VideoAlign Overall (8.51) but at the cost of VBench degradation (81.99), illustrating the exploration–exploitation trade-off that motivates the dual-adapter merging strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three streaming AR baselines, AR-CoPO (semi) achieves the highest VBench Total (82.45) over LongLive (82.31) and Causal-Forcing (82.28), while the merged model improves VideoAlign Overall from the Self-Forcing baseline of 7.76 to 8.22, surpassing LongLive (8.06) and Causal-Forcing (7.79). Against the SDE-based GRPO baseline (following DanceGRPO/FlowGRPO design), AR-CoPO clearly wins: the SDE variant fails to improve reward throughout training, whereas AR-CoPO steadily increases scores, which the authors attribute to the near-deterministic nature of few-step consistency models where intermediate SDE noise carries negligible entropy. The on-policy AR-CoPO variant achieves the highest VideoAlign Overall (8.51) but degrades VBench Total to 81.99, indicating reward hacking; the merged model (scale=0.8) is selected as the best configuration because it is the only one satisfying the dual-improvement criterion of raising VideoAlign Overall while maintaining VBench Total. Overall, AR-CoPO wins on the combined quality-and-preference criterion, with the semi-on-policy component driving out-of-domain generalization and the on-policy component driving in-domain human preference alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

AR-CoPO directly builds on Neighbor GRPO, which reinterprets SDE-based policy optimization as a distance-driven contrastive objective over perturbed initial-noise candidates, thereby decoupling exploration from the sampling process. The key departure is adapting this contrastive framework to streaming autoregressive video generators (Self-Forcing, Causal-Forcing) by introducing a chunk-level forking mechanism as the action space and defining surrogate distances in the consistency-model x̂₀ prediction space rather than intermediate ODE latents. It explicitly contrasts with SDE-based GRPO variants (DanceGRPO, FlowGRPO, MixGRPO) that inject stochasticity at intermediate timesteps, showing these are fundamentally mismatched to few-step consistency model samplers where intermediate noise carries negligible entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap: RLHF for low-latency streaming video generators was previously intractable because standard SDE-GRPO methods fail on the near-deterministic dynamics of few-step consistency models. The controlled noise-substitution experiment providing mechanistic evidence for this failure, combined with the dual-benchmark (in-domain VideoAlign + out-of-domain VBench) criterion for detecting reward hacking, offers a useful methodological template for future alignment work. Limitations include evaluation on a single base model (Self-Forcing), LoRA-only fine-tuning, and modest absolute gains; open questions include whether chunk-level forking scales to much longer sequences, whether the semi-on-policy replay buffer strategy generalizes to other reward types, and whether full fine-tuning would yield larger improvements.
