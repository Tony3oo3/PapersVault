## Problem

Cross-attention is the core mechanism for learning 2D-3D correspondences in image-to-point-cloud (I2P) registration, but the large modality gap between images and point clouds causes attention ambiguity: spurious cross-modal pairs receive high similarity scores, preventing the network from learning discriminative correspondences. Existing remedies (manifold alignment, uncertainty correction, covariance alignment) improve performance but leave substantial room in robustness and generalization, particularly in zero-shot and fine-tuning scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (320×480) and an uncolored 3D point cloud (up to 30K points) as input and produces 2D-3D pixel-to-point correspondences from which a camera pose (rotation and translation) is estimated. Correspondence identification relies on L2-normalized feature distances, and the OCA module operates on patch-level features extracted by the backbone encoders. The module is non-parametric and adds only ~6 ms of runtime, making it a drop-in addition after the feature interaction stage of any existing I2P registration pipeline.

## Method

The key insight is to reformulate standard cross-attention as a system of ordinary differential equations (assignment ODEs) in which 2D and 3D features and the attention matrix evolve jointly over a continuous time variable. A convergence analysis of these ODEs reveals two practical strategies: (S1) enforce sparsity of the attention matrix so its derivative approaches zero, and (S2) initialize attention using L2-normalized features to approximate the ground-truth assignment. The OCA module unifies these strategies in two stages: first, it computes an initial sparse attention via temperature-scaled softmax on L2-normalized feature products; second, it propagates the attention matrix and features through discrete ODE steps (Euler integration with step size τ), where each step updates the attention via a Sylvester-like term involving normalized Gram matrices and updates features via sparse-attention-weighted sums. Training uses a two-stage schedule—first training the base framework with τ=0, then fine-tuning with τ>0—to ensure the initial attention is already close to the ground truth before ODE propagation begins. The module is entirely non-parametric and can be plugged into any existing cross-attention-based I2P registration framework.

## Results

On the 7-Scenes dataset (standard evaluation), OCA improves registration recall (RR) by 3–5% across all five baselines, with Matr+OCA reaching 55.2% RR (vs. 47.2% for Matr alone) and LDF-I2P+OCA reaching 59.1% RR (vs. 54.1%). Inlier ratio (IR) gains range from 4.5 to 7.9 percentage points. Under zero-shot evaluation on ScanNet, the gains expand to 9–15% RR (e.g., Flow-I2P+Zero-OCA reaches 41.4% RR vs. 26.3% baseline), and fine-tuning on TUM yields up to 8% RR improvement for Matr+OCA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCA is a plug-and-play module that consistently outperforms all five prior-knowledge-free cross-attention baselines (Matr, Flow-I2P, Bridge, CA-I2P, LDF-I2P) on both IR and RR across four datasets. The +Ref control (same extra epochs, no OCA) confirms that gains stem from the ODE propagation itself rather than additional training: for example, Matr+Ref reaches 50.1% RR while Matr+OCA reaches 55.2%. In zero-shot transfer (7-Scenes → ScanNet), OCA's advantage is most pronounced, with Flow-I2P+Zero-OCA achieving 41.4% RR versus 26.3% for the baseline (+15%). Against propagation-based alternatives (Table 6), Matr+OCA (IR 0.703, RR 0.705 on TUM) substantially outperforms simple DDPM (RR 0.460), flow matching (RR 0.459), and Diff-Reg (RR 0.602), validating that the non-parametric ODE formulation is more effective than learned diffusion/flow models for attention refinement. The only noted weakness is in extremely texture-less scenes where the initial attention is too noisy for stable ODE propagation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the ODE perspective to reframe standard transformer cross-attention as a discrete Euler discretization of a continuous 'assignment ODE' system, then derives practical refinements (sparse attention via temperature-scaled softmax, L2-normalized feature correlation, and iterative attention-matrix propagation) from the ODE's convergence conditions. Conceptually, it sits within the line of prior-knowledge-free cross-attention improvements for I2P registration (Matr, Flow-I2P, Bridge, CA-I2P, LDF-I2P) but departs from them by providing a unified theoretical justification for why standard softmax cross-attention fails to resolve attention ambiguity, rather than proposing yet another ad-hoc architectural tweak. The module is explicitly non-parametric and plug-and-play, complementing rather than replacing the cross-attention in any of these frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ODE convergence analysis gives practitioners a principled diagnostic for why cross-attention produces ambiguous correspondences in cross-modal settings (softmax cannot guarantee sparsity; unnormalized features misalign with L2-based matching), and the resulting OCA module is a lightweight 6 ms addition that consistently improves recall across five baselines and four datasets. The 15% zero-shot gain on ScanNet is practically significant for deployment scenarios where retraining on target scenes is infeasible. However, the method fails in heavily texture-less scenes where the initial attention is too noisy for stable ODE propagation, and the authors acknowledge that a feedback mechanism to assess initialization quality remains an open problem. Broader implications include whether the assignment-ODE framework can be extended to other cross-modal matching tasks or to intra-modal 3D registration (which the paper briefly demonstrates in an appendix).
