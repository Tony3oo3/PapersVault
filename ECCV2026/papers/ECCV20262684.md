## Problem

Current 3D-LLMs rely on dataset co-occurrence biases (e.g., most pillows are white, most lamp-pillow pairs are 'near') rather than genuine geometric evidence, causing fine-grained grounding failures on color, orientation, and spatial relations. Existing remedies focus on text-based augmentation, which leaves the underlying 3D visual data unchanged and can even reinforce spurious correlations. The paper addresses the absence of a mechanism to generate targeted, predicate-level visual counterfactuals that directly correct the specific spatial or appearance errors a model exhibits.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a 3D point cloud with per-point color and instance segmentation (produced by Mask3D) together with a free-form natural language instruction, and outputs a 3D bounding box localizing the referenced object. During training supervision construction, the framework additionally produces counterfactual edited scenes (object-level geometry modifications) paired with 5-6 QA examples per scene spanning three reasoning levels: direct factual perception, yes/no discriminative verification, and comparative reasoning with explicit rationale, so that each edited scene yields multi-granularity supervision targeting the diagnosed predicate.

## Method

DEER-3D is a four-stage iterative loop (Decompose, Diagnose, Edit, Retrain) built on the ChatScene backbone. Decomposition uses an LLM (Qwen3) to parse each failed instruction into atomic predicates; diagnosis deterministically attributes the failure to a specific predicate (appearance vs. spatial) via rule-based keyword matching and independent querying of the base model, without relying on model self-explanation. The Edit stage applies a unified Clone-Replace-Modify procedure: the target object is duplicated, a same-category distractor is removed and replaced by the clone, and a predicate-specific modification is applied (CIELAB-maximizing recolor for appearance errors, +/-45 or +/-90 degree Y-axis rotation for orientation errors, or relational repositioning for distance/vertical-relation errors), ensuring only one semantic factor changes. The Retrain stage fine-tunes the model on the original data plus the generated counterfactual scenes and QA pairs, then re-runs diagnosis to target remaining failures in subsequent rounds, yielding progressive 4-6% grounding accuracy gains over two iterations.

## Results

On ScanRefer, DEER-3D with the Chat-Scene (3D-only) backbone improves Acc@0.25 from 41.2 to 47.1 (+5.9) and Acc@0.5 from 37.4 to 43.1 (+5.7) over two iterative rounds. With the full Chat-Scene (2D+3D) backbone, it reaches 58.6 Acc@0.25 and 53.3 Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer, and 61.4 F1@0.25 and 56.8 F1@0.5 on Multi3DRefer, establishing new state-of-the-art among LLM-based methods. Gains are most pronounced in the 3D-only setting (+4–7%) and in the Multiple-object subset of ScanRefer (47.8→50.8 Acc@0.25), where disambiguating same-category objects is hardest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEER-3D consistently outperforms all LLM-based baselines on both ScanRefer and Multi3DRefer. Against the strongest prior LLM-based methods—Inst3D-LLM (57.8 Acc@0.25) and PQ3D (57.0 Acc@0.25) on ScanRefer—DEER-3D with the 2D+3D Chat-Scene backbone reaches 58.6 Acc@0.25, a clear margin. On Multi3DRefer, it surpasses Inst3D-LLM (58.3 F1@0.25) by 3.1 points (61.4) and M3DRef-CLIP (42.8 F1@0.25) by 18.6 points. The gains are largest in the 3D-only setting, where DEER-3D Round 2 improves over Chat-Scene (w/o 2D) by +5.9 Acc@0.25 and +6.9 F1@0.5, and in the Multiple-object subset of ScanRefer (47.8→50.8 Acc@0.25), confirming that targeted counterfactual editing is most beneficial when same-category disambiguation is required. Cross-backbone generalization is also demonstrated: applying DEER-3D to the scene-level LL3DA backbone yields +3.5 Acc@0.25 (25.7→29.2) on ScanRefer. On the human-annotated BEACON-3D benchmark, DEER-3D improves overall case accuracy from 41.0 to 45.7, validating human-aligned grounding gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on ChatScene as its 3D-LLM backbone and departs from the dominant strategy in the 3D-LLM literature of improving grounding through text-based augmentation (e.g., LEO, SpArtun3D, Chat-3Dv2), which the authors argue cannot resolve visual co-occurrence biases in the underlying 3D scenes. It draws on counterfactual data augmentation from NLP and vision, error-driven self-correction from LLM fine-tuning, and 3D scene editing, but is the first to unify these into a predicate-targeted 3D visual editing loop specifically for grounding supervision. Unlike prior 3D scene editing systems that prioritize photorealistic user-driven manipulation, DEER-3D requires minimal, semantically controlled modifications that preserve all other scene attributes to isolate a single failed predicate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and underexplored limitation of 3D-LLMs: reliance on dataset-level statistical shortcuts (e.g., 'white pillows near lamps') rather than genuine geometric reasoning, and provides a practical, iterative framework to correct these biases without expensive scene reconstruction or new 3D data collection. The finding that text-only diagnostic clarification yields only ~0.6% improvement while predicate-level visual editing yields 4–6% confirms that the visual intervention itself is the critical signal, not merely better instructions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex compositional spatial reasoning, whether the heuristic sanity checks (bounding-box collision, floating detection) suffice for physically plausible edits at scale, and whether the gains persist as base models improve and their error distributions shift.
