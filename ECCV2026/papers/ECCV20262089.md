## Problem

Most brain tumor MRI segmentation models operate purely in the image domain, implicitly assuming statistically consistent inputs across scanning conditions. In practice, k-space sampling parameters (density, phase-encoding direction, FOV ratio) introduce structured spatial perturbations that vary across clinical centers, degrading generalization. Recovering these sampling mechanisms from reconstructed images—where raw k-space data is unavailable—and converting them into usable structural priors for segmentation remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

SPHERE-Seg consumes multi-modal 2D MRI slices (T1, T1ce, T2, FLAIR) in standard formats (NIfTI, PNG, NPY) without requiring DICOM metadata at inference. SPHERE-Recover takes a single reconstructed slice and outputs a 5-element sampling parameter vector (frequency/phase dimensions, sampling ratio, phase FOV ratio, phase-encoding direction), which is then used to synthesize a 2-channel deformation vector field (DVF) at full image resolution. The final output is per-pixel class labels for whole tumor (WT), tumor core (TC), and enhancing tumor (ET).

## Method

SPHERE-Recover is a lightweight dual-branch regressor (ViT spatial encoder + CNN on 2D FFT real/imaginary components) trained with joint MSE and cross-entropy loss on DICOM-labeled data to predict k-space sampling geometry from reconstructed images. The recovered parameters drive a physics-informed pipeline that simulates undersampled reconstruction, computes artifact magnitude and gradient energy, and solves a brightness-consistency-plus-smoothness optimization to obtain a continuous deformation vector field (DVF) capturing sampling-induced structural displacement. SPHERE-Seg is a Swin-RetNet encoder-decoder where a Deformation Prior Module (DPM) at stages 2–4 injects the DVF via two mechanisms: (1) differentiable bilinear warping of features according to the DVF for geometric alignment, and (2) DVF magnitude modulation of the RetNet retention weight matrix, which amplifies long-range structural consistency in regions of high deformation. This design requires no additional parameters or inference overhead beyond the DVF itself, as SPHERE-Recover runs only as a preprocessing step.

## Results

On BraTS 2023, SPHERE-Seg achieves the best TC (93.67%) and ET (93.69%) Dice scores, with competitive WT (91.25%). Under cross-dataset evaluation, SPHERE-Seg consistently leads: on BraTS 2019 it reaches 90.25%/91.33%/92.24% (WT/TC/ET), and on MSD Task01 it reaches 88.82%/89.75%/90.51%, outperforming all 14 baselines on every cross-dataset metric. For boundary stability, SPHERE-Seg achieves the lowest HD95 across all three datasets and tumor regions (e.g., 0.6471 on BraTS 2023 ET vs. 0.6600 for M-Net). The model also uses only 14.88M parameters and 44.28G FLOPs, the fewest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHERE-Seg outperforms all 14 baselines on every cross-dataset metric (BraTS 2019 and MSD Task01), with the largest margins on BraTS 2019 ET (+1.16 over Mamba Sea's 91.08) and MSD Task01 ET (+0.90 over Mamba Sea's 89.61). On the in-domain BraTS 2023, SPHERE-Seg leads on TC (93.67 vs. M-Net's 93.55) and ET (93.69 vs. VM-UNet's 93.50) but trails M-Net by 0.08 on WT (91.25 vs. 91.33). Among the strongest recent baselines, M-Net (ICCV 2025) and Mamba Sea (IEEE TMI 2025) are the closest competitors on BraTS 2023, yet both drop substantially under cross-dataset transfer (e.g., M-Net falls to 88.04 WT on MSD vs. SPHERE's 88.82). For boundary accuracy (HD95), SPHERE-Seg achieves the lowest values across all nine dataset-region combinations, e.g., 0.7659 on BraTS 2019 ET vs. 0.8307 for M-Net. The advantage is most pronounced for smaller tumor sub-regions (TC, ET) under domain shift, consistent with the paper's claim that sampling-induced deformation priors stabilize fine-grained boundary localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPHERE extends the RetNet retention mechanism by injecting a physically-motivated deformation vector field (DVF) into the retention weight matrix, and combines it with Swin Transformer's sliding-window attention in a hierarchical encoder-decoder backbone. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm in brain tumor segmentation—where models operate purely in the image domain—by recovering k-space sampling parameters from reconstructed MRI and converting them into a continuous spatial perturbation prior. The DVF construction borrows the deformation-field formalism from medical image registration but repurposes it to characterize sampling-induced structural distortions rather than anatomical misalignment, and the parameter recovery module draws on the observation that MRI images implicitly encode acquisition information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: segmentation models trained on one center's data often degrade under different scanning conditions, yet most public datasets provide only reconstructed images without DICOM metadata. SPHERE's ability to infer sampling parameters from images alone makes the approach deployable in real clinical pipelines. However, the cross-dataset gains are modest (roughly 1–2 Dice points over strong baselines), the DVF is explicitly a learned proxy rather than a true physical deformation, and the framework requires a separate ViT-based recovery stage. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumes, non-Cartesian sampling trajectories, or other organ/tumor types, and whether the physical prior remains informative when scanning variability is dominated by contrast or protocol differences rather than k-space geometry.
