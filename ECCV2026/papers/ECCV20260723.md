## Problem

Remote sensing VLMs that adopt Chain-of-Thought reasoning are trained with outcome-based rewards (e.g., GRPO), which inadvertently reward hallucinated intermediate steps that coincidentally yield correct final answers. Process Reward Models (PRMs) could provide step-level verification, but they have not been applied to the geospatial domain, and naively using scalar PRM scores as optimization targets induces length bias and reward hacking that cause models to truncate reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a variable-resolution remote sensing image (handled by the Aimv2-Huge vision encoder with dynamic positional encoding for multi-scale aerial imagery) and a free-form text query. It generates a structured reasoning chain (planning → visual evidence collection → synthesis) that terminates in a task-specific output: rotated bounding boxes for grounding/detection, integer object counts, scene class labels, VQA answers, or free-text captions. During test-time scaling, the token-level GeoPRM scores each generated token, enabling Best-of-N or Beam Search selection over multiple reasoning trajectories.

## Method

GeoSolver builds on GLM-4.1V-9B-Base with SFT on Geo-CoT380k, then aligns the policy via a novel Process-Aware Tree-GRPO algorithm. The key verification signal comes from GeoPRM, a token-level binary classifier trained on the 2M-sample Geo-PRM-2M dataset, which is synthesized through entropy-guided MCTS (mining the policy's intrinsic logical divergence points) and synthetic hallucination injection (bounding-box jittering and fact tampering to target visual grounding failures). During RL rollouts, an entropy-guided reasoning tree is constructed by branching at high-entropy tokens; leaf trajectories receive outcome scores that are penalized by a drop-moment mechanism (multiplying by γ when a sudden PRM confidence drop exceeds threshold ρ), avoiding the length bias of absolute score accumulation. These process-aware rewards are propagated upward via Global Advantage (node value minus root value) and Local Advantage (node value minus parent value), downweighted by √|L(s_n)| to prevent overfitting to shared early steps, and the policy is updated with a standard clipped surrogate objective over all tree nodes.

## Results

On object-level tasks (Table 1), GeoSolver-9B achieves 75.62 mIoU on DIOR-RSVG, 76.66 on RRSIS-D, 53.47 mAP@50 on DOTAv2, and 94.74 mAP@50 on HRRSD, substantially outperforming the strongest open-source remote sensing baselines (VHM: 49.90 mIoU on DIOR, 2.37 mAP on DOTA) and general reasoning VLMs (GLM-4.1V-Thinking: 39.41 mIoU, 40.45 mAP). On scene-level tasks (Table 2), GeoSolver reaches 96.44% accuracy on NWPU-RESISC45, 98.33% on AID, 99.50% on WHURS19, and 52.50 BLEU-4 on RSITMD captioning. With GeoPRM-guided Test-Time Scaling (Beam Search, N=32), average performance improves by 17.5% over greedy decoding, and cross-model verification enables generalist VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B) to surpass fully fine-tuned domain experts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoSolver-9B achieves the best result on every reported metric across all 17 benchmarks, outperforming all 14 baselines. Against closed-source commercial models (Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini-2.0-Flash, GPT-5), GeoSolver leads by large margins on visual grounding (e.g., 75.62 vs. 11.40 mIoU on DIOR) and object detection (53.47 vs. 14.30 mAP on DOTA), though GPT-5 retains a slight edge on RSOD counting (40.0 vs. 45.5 is actually GeoSolver winning; GPT-5 leads on VHR counting at 58.0 vs. 79.0 — correction: GeoSolver wins at 79.0). Among open-source remote sensing specialists, GeoSolver surpasses VHM (the strongest domain model) by 25.7 mIoU on DIOR and 51.1 mAP on DOTA, and outperforms EarthDial by 36.2 mIoU on DIOR. Against general reasoning VLMs (GLM-4.1V-Thinking, Kimi-VL-Thinking), GeoSolver gains 36.2 mIoU on DIOR and 13.0 mAP on DOTA. In the cross-model verification comparison (Table 4), GeoPRM as a verifier yields 75.95 average BoN score, exceeding generic VLM verifiers (Qwen3-VL-8B: 64.82, GLM-4.1V: 64.14) and out-of-domain mathematical PRMs (GraphPRM: 62.37, URSA-8B-RM: 62.61) by 11–14 points, confirming the necessity of domain-specific process supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm (from DeepSeek-R1) to the remote sensing domain by introducing process-level supervision, drawing on the drop-moment penalty concept from URSA and entropy-guided tree exploration from TreeRL. It departs from prior remote sensing VLMs (GeoChat, VHM, RS-EoT, GeoZero) that rely exclusively on outcome-based rewards or SFT, and from general-purpose PRMs (GraphPRM, URSA) that target mathematical reasoning rather than spatial-visual grounding. The key conceptual departure is the construction of a domain-specific token-level PRM trained on a dual-strategy dataset (entropy-guided MCTS for logical errors + synthetic hallucination injection for visual grounding failures), which is then integrated into a unified tree-structured RL objective rather than used as a standalone verifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first systematic pipeline for process-supervised reinforcement learning in remote sensing, addressing a critical gap where outcome-only rewards encourage spatial hallucinations. The cross-model generalization result—where a domain-specific PRM guides general-purpose VLMs to surpass fully fine-tuned domain experts—is practically significant, suggesting that verification signals can be decoupled from the generating policy. Open questions include whether the tree-structured RL training scales efficiently to larger models or more complex multi-step geospatial tasks, whether the synthetic hallucination injection strategy generalizes beyond bounding-box perturbations to other spatial reasoning failures (e.g., scale, orientation), and whether the compute-optimal scaling law holds as the base policy improves.
