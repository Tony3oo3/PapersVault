## Problem

Existing video generation models entangle camera motion and scene content, restricting control to a single modality (text, trajectory, or reference video) and a narrow task (T2V, I2V, or V2V). Naively unifying multiple camera conditions with multiple content conditions causes severe modality conflicts and optimization instability, while real-world data with both high visual quality and precise camera annotations is profoundly scarce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261567/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any combination of three camera conditions (free-text motion descriptions, explicit 3D trajectory matrices, or a reference motion video) and three content conditions (text prompt, a single RGB image, or a source video), producing 704×1248 videos via the Wan2.2-TI2V VAE. At inference, a dual-condition classifier-free guidance scheme applies separate scales w_T and w_M to independently tune the influence of semantic text guidance versus geometric camera-motion guidance, enabling compositional effects such as overlaying a text-described pan on top of a trajectory-defined dolly.

## Method

OmniCamera decouples condition injection by concatenating text and reference-video tokens into the self-attention sequence while adding trajectory features (via an MLP) directly to the noise latent, and introduces 3D Condition RoPE that assigns distinct spatial-temporal base offsets (0,0,0 / F,0,0 / F,H,W) to each modality to resolve positional ambiguity. Training follows a Dual-level Curriculum Co-Training strategy: a condition-level curriculum progressively introduces modalities from text (coarsest) to reference video to trajectory (finest), and a data-level curriculum first trains on 500K UE5 synthetic clips with exact poses for geometric precision, then fine-tunes on 330K curated real videos to restore photorealism without catastrophic forgetting. The OmniCAM hybrid dataset provides same-scene diverse-trajectory pairs, same-trajectory diverse-scene pairs, and motion-content-target triplets at both synthetic and real scales, supplying the diverse paired supervision required for all nine task-condition combinations under a single set of weights.

## Results

On trajectory-controlled T2V, OmniCamera achieves FVD 893.95, RotErr 0.034, and TransErr 2.064, substantially outperforming CameraCtrl (FVD 1365.90, RotErr 0.126, TransErr 8.160) and AC3D (FVD 958.62, RotErr 0.056, TransErr 7.542). On text-controlled T2V, OmniCamera reaches 92.4% motion accuracy versus 31.4% for Wan2.2-TI2V and 19.3% for AnimateDiff, while maintaining competitive FVD (884.11 vs. 867.27). On reference-video-controlled I2V, OmniCamera achieves FVD 352.76 and CLIP 30.45, surpassing CamCloneMaster (FVD 380.73, CLIP 30.18). Across all nine task-condition combinations, a single set of weights handles every pairing without task-specific models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CameraCtrl [16], OmniCamera dominates on trajectory control: T2V FVD drops from 1365.90 to 893.95, RotErr from 0.126 to 0.034, and TransErr from 8.160 to 2.064; on I2V, FVD improves from 586.14 to 333.54. Versus AC3D [1] on T2V trajectory control, OmniCamera wins on FVD (893.95 vs. 958.62), RotErr (0.034 vs. 0.056), and TransErr (2.064 vs. 7.542), though AC3D edges ahead on CLIP-T (30.01 vs. 29.64). Against ReCamMaster [2] on V2V trajectory control, OmniCamera achieves lower FVD (331.36 vs. 366.82) and higher CLIP-V (94.97 vs. 94.10), but ReCamMaster retains a slight TransErr advantage (5.320 vs. 5.933). Compared to CamCloneMaster [26] on reference-video control, OmniCamera improves FVD on both I2V (352.76 vs. 380.73) and V2V (348.51 vs. 376.33), while CamCloneMaster maintains marginally lower RotErr and TransErr on I2V. On text-controlled generation, OmniCamera's motion accuracy (92.4% T2V, 91.3% I2V, 89.7% V2V) far exceeds Wan2.2-TI2V (31.4%, 30.3%) and Wan2.2-Fun-Camera (68.8% I2V), with FVD remaining within ~2% of the base model. AnimateDiff [15] is the weakest baseline, with OmniCamera improving T2V motion accuracy from 19.3% to 92.4% and FVD from 1471.28 to 884.11.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V diffusion-transformer architecture and Flow Matching as its generative backbone, extending it with a decoupled condition-injection mechanism (3D Condition RoPE) to handle multiple simultaneous control signals. It departs from prior camera-controlled video generation work—CameraCtrl, AC3D, ReCamMaster, CamCloneMaster—each of which supports only one camera-control modality (text, trajectory, or reference video) and a narrow set of content conditions, by explicitly framing video generation as the composition of two independent axes (camera pose × scene content) and unifying all nine resulting combinations under a single set of weights. The dual-level curriculum co-training strategy is a methodological departure from the standard practice of naively mixing all conditions and data sources, addressing modality conflicts through staged introduction of control modalities and a synthetic-to-real data progression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified nine-condition framework and the OmniCAM dataset (500K synthetic + 330K real videos with paired/triplet supervision) provide a practical foundation for camera-controlled video generation that prior single-task models could not offer, potentially reducing the need for specialized models per control modality. The empirically observed dominance hierarchy (trajectory > reference video > text) under contradictory conditions gives practitioners a predictable interaction model when combining inputs. However, the framework does not yet support finer-grained controls such as multiple reference images or localized motion guidance, and its reliance on a specific 5B-parameter base model (Wan2.2-TI2V) raises open questions about scalability to larger architectures or different backbones. Whether the curriculum strategy generalizes beyond the three modalities studied here remains to be tested.
