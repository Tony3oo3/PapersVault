## Problem

State-of-the-art vision models ingest images globally in a single forward pass, yet humans process visual information through sequential local glimpses. This paper asks whether local, sequential perception provides a fundamental computational advantage for length generalization in visual reasoning, and shows that global perception causes models to learn shortcut solutions that fail out-of-distribution, while both locality and recurrence are jointly necessary for robust generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262758/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of square foveated glimpses (local crops) and peripheral glimpses (4× larger, downsampled to a fixed sensor resolution) extracted at the agent's current position, along with a task question. At each timestep it outputs a displacement vector (angle θ, distance d constrained within the peripheral field) and a binary stop signal; upon stopping, it emits the final task answer (parity, state-machine state, root locations, or presence/absence). The full canvas is 1200×800 pixels in-distribution, with OOD settings increasing both resolution and the number of entities (switches, distractors, roots, subplots).

## Method

FOVEAGENT-LSTM is a recurrent agent that sequentially ingests foveated and peripheral glimpses encoded by a shared ResNet, concatenates their embeddings, and feeds them into an LSTM backbone that outputs navigation actions and a stop bit at each step. The critical design choice is that the peripheral glimpse is always downsampled to a low sensor resolution, preventing the model from reading fine-grained state information from the wider field and thereby blocking global shortcut learning while still enabling spatial navigation. Training uses imitation learning on oracle-policy trajectories (with noise for robustness) plus auxiliary probe supervision at each step. The paper's central finding is that neither recurrence alone (a recurrent network given the full image) nor locality alone (a parallel model given local crops) suffices; only the combination of strictly local perception with sequential recurrent processing unlocks OOD length generalization on state-tracking tasks, while global parallel models retain an advantage on recall tasks that do not require state tracking.

## Results

On the FINDING ROOTS task, FOVEAGENT-QWEN (local + global glimpses) achieves 82.26% accuracy in-distribution and 67.12% on OOD-numroots, compared to 57.24% and 32.63% respectively for the global-only QWEN2.5-VL-3B-INSTRUCT baseline. On VISUAL PARITY and STATE MACHINE, FOVEAGENT-LSTM maintains high accuracy as task complexity scales from 10 to 20 switches and across resolutions, while QWEN2.5-VL-3B-INSTRUCT shows a sharp performance decline out-of-distribution. In compute-matched settings on FINDING ROOTS, FOVEAGENT-QWEN nearly doubles the accuracy of the global baseline, confirming that the gains are not merely from extra vision tokens. On the RECALL task (no state tracking required), the global QWEN2.5-VL-3B-INSTRUCT significantly outperforms the recurrent FOVEAGENT-LSTM, indicating that locality is specifically critical for state-tracking generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QWEN2.5-VL-3B-INSTRUCT (finetuned, global perception), FOVEAGENT-LSTM wins decisively on both state-tracking tasks (VISUAL PARITY and STATE MACHINE), maintaining high OOD accuracy as switch count and image resolution increase, while the global model suffers a sharp performance drop. On FINDING ROOTS, FOVEAGENT-QWEN with local glimpses achieves 67.12% on OOD-numroots versus 32.63% for the global-only baseline — more than double — and 77.24% vs. 50.12% on OOD-subplots. However, on the RECALL task (which requires no state tracking), QWEN2.5-VL-3B-INSTRUCT significantly outperforms the recurrent FOVEAGENT-LSTM, showing that locality is not universally beneficial but specifically enables state-tracking generalization. Zero-shot evaluations of GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 show consistent performance degradation across task complexity, confirming that even the strongest closed-source VLMs struggle with these length-generalization challenges. In compute-matched comparisons on FINDING ROOTS, simply scaling global resolution by 10× yields only marginal gains, whereas adding local foveated glimpses nearly doubles accuracy, establishing that the benefit is architectural (locality) rather than computational.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the length-generalization testbed from the language-modeling literature (Anil et al. 2022; Dziri et al. 2023; Zhou et al. 2024) into 2D visual space, where the model must additionally perform spatial search to locate and aggregate local information. It builds on the finding that recurrent networks (unlike transformers or SSMs) can length-generalize on sequential state-tracking tasks (Deletang et al. 2023; Ebrahimi et al. 2025), but departs from that line by showing recurrence alone is insufficient in vision: a recurrent model with global image access still learns shortcuts and fails OOD. The foveated perception mechanism draws on early biologically-inspired attention models (Mnih et al. 2014; Ba et al. 2014; Gregor et al. 2015), and the closest prior visual work (Veerabadran et al. 2023) used whole-image recurrent perception, which this paper shows degrades with image size—precisely the failure mode that local perception resolves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that both locality of perception and recurrent processing are jointly necessary (and together sufficient) for compositional length generalization in visual state-tracking—challenges the prevailing design assumption that global attention over a full image is adequate for visual reasoning. If confirmed on more complex, real-world tasks, this would argue for a return to sequential, foveated processing policies in next-generation vision-language models. Key open questions include: whether imitation-learned policies can be replaced by reinforcement learning or self-supervised exploration at scale, whether the locality requirement persists on natural-image tasks beyond synthetic diagrams, and how to integrate local-perception policies into large pre-trained VLMs without discarding their global representations. The finding that recall tasks do not require locality (unlike state-tracking) also suggests a useful taxonomy for deciding when sequential processing is warranted.
