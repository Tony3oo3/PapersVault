## Problem

Nearly all Visual Odometry and SLAM systems are benchmarked on raw, uncompressed images, yet practical deployments rely on lossy video codecs (e.g., H.264) that introduce artifacts violating the photometric-constancy assumption underlying optical-flow and direct methods. This compression-induced degradation has been largely overlooked, with only one prior work (MoV-SLAM) attempting to exploit codec information for tracking.

## Input / Output

VOCA consumes a causally encoded H.264 stereo video stream (up to ~100× compression, ~500 kbps per camera) together with the per-macroblock motion vectors and I/P-frame structure extracted from the decoder, plus standard camera intrinsics. It outputs a causal camera pose trajectory (each pose estimated using only past frames), evaluated via SE(3)-aligned ATE and 6-frame RTE. The system operates on monochromatic 640×480 stereo pairs at 30 fps, matching the EuRoC, TUM-VI, and Monado SLAM dataset configurations.

## Method

VOCA builds on Basalt's KLT-Tracking + bundle-adjustment pipeline but replaces the default same-pixel initialization with the negated H.264 motion vector (−d_k ≈ v) as a coarse SE(2) prior, placing the nonlinear optimizer within the convergence basin even for large displacements. To handle false positives from wrong or dynamic-object motion vectors, each feature is tracked in parallel both with and without the motion prior; tracks successful in only one mode are kept, while dual-mode tracks are retained only if mutually consistent. I-frame boundaries (which carry no motion vectors) are bridged by reusing the last available P-frame vectors under a constant-motion assumption. Additionally, the forward-backward consistency threshold is relaxed and the tracking patch is enlarged to capture more image structure, improving robustness under compression artifacts.

## Results

On EuRoC, VOCA achieves a mean ATE of 17.35 cm and mean RTE of 1.669 cm (Δ=6 frames), improving over the closest baseline Basalt by ~10% in ATE and ~15% in RTE while completing more sequences without divergence. On TUM-VI, VOCA reduces mean ATE to 9.64 cm (vs. 20.34 for Basalt) and mean RTE to 0.815 cm (vs. 2.820 for Basalt), with an ~85% ATE improvement on the hardest sequence TR5. On the most challenging Monado SLAM Dataset, VOCA improves median ATE by 37% and median RTE by 40% over the second-best method, and raises the ATE success rate from 34.4% to 46.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VOCA outperforms its direct baseline Basalt on all three datasets in both ATE and RTE, with the largest gains on the hardest sequences (e.g., ~85% ATE reduction on TUM-VI TR5, ~37% median ATE reduction on MSD). Against ORB-SLAM3, VOCA achieves comparable or better ATE on TUM-VI (9.64 vs. 8.72 cm mean) but dramatically lower RTE (0.815 vs. ∞, as ORB-SLAM3 exceeds the RTE threshold on every TUM-VI sequence), indicating superior frame-to-frame tracking. OKVIS2 is consistently outperformed by VOCA in both metrics across all datasets (e.g., 0.755 vs. 5.223 cm mean RTE on MSD). MoV-SLAM, the only prior codec-aware SLAM system, diverges or crashes on nearly all EuRoC sequences and is excluded from TUM-VI and MSD due to fisheye-camera incompatibility, making it non-competitive. VOCA's key advantage is robustness: it completes sequences where Basalt crashes (EV102) or diverges (EV203), and its success rate on MSD is 46.9% vs. 34.4% for Basalt and 31.2% for OKVIS2.

## Relation to Prior Work

VOCA builds directly on Basalt's KLT-Tracking frontend and extends it with codec-aware initialization: H.264 motion vectors are used as translational priors for the SE(2) patch optimizer rather than as direct correspondences. This departs from MoV-SLAM, the only prior system explicitly targeting compressed video, which instead replaces optical flow with macroblock-based EXPRESS feature matching. Conceptually, the paper reframes the standard VO problem (assumed to operate on raw, uncompressed frames) as one where the encoder's rate-distortion-optimized block displacements can be repurposed to place the nonlinear KLT optimizer within its convergence basin, bridging the gap between the compress-then-analyze deployment paradigm and classical tracking assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: nearly all real-world camera streams are codec-compressed, yet VO/SLAM benchmarks have historically used only raw images, leaving a gap between evaluation and deployment. VOCA demonstrates that a large fraction of the tracking degradation from ~100× compression can be recovered with minimal architectural changes, making compressed-video VO viable for bandwidth-constrained platforms (drones, wearables, edge robotics). Open questions include generalization beyond H.264 to newer codecs (only preliminary AV1 results are provided), applicability to learning-based VO backends, and whether the approach extends to monocular or RGB-D settings where stereo redundancy is unavailable.
