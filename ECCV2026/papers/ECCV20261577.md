## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion models fail at physical alignment (e.g., reflections extending beyond mirror surfaces, incorrect buoyancy) and counterfactual generation (e.g., swapping causal roles in 'bird eats snake'). The paper diagnoses the root cause as a deficient causal understanding: DMs learn symmetric correlations rather than directional causality, and entangle causal factors, causing the causal hierarchy X→Y_D→Y_I to collapse into parallelized correlation-based mappings.

## Input / Output

LINA takes a standard text prompt and produces a generated image or video from a pre-trained diffusion model (SD-3.5-large, FLUX.1, or Wan-2.2) without modifying its weights. At inference time, a lightweight Adaptive Intervention Module (T5 encoder + MLP head) predicts two scalar intervention strengths from the prompt, and a spaCy parser identifies relation tokens; no external MLLM is called. The method adds approximately 25% computational overhead over the base DM, and the neutral reference prompt is constructed automatically by replacing relation tokens with generic conjunctions.

## Method

LINA formalizes two complementary interventions: (1) token-embedding-level causal edge calibration (γ₁), which selectively amplifies or attenuates relation-token embeddings by scaling the direction vector (c−u) with a binary mask, and (2) visual-latent-level contrastive causal guidance (γ₂), which adds a contrastive term to the standard CFG formula that isolates the causal signal of relation tokens against a relaxed 'neutral' prompt. A lightweight Adaptive Intervention Module (pre-trained T5 encoder + MLP regression head) is trained offline via L2 regression on prompt-specific optimal strengths discovered by an MLLM-guided coordinate descent search over the PAP dataset's hard cases. At inference, the predicted strengths are applied throughout a causality-aware denoising schedule that increases the time-shift parameter (e.g., from 1.15 to 1.5) to concentrate sampling density in the initial high-noise steps where the paper shows causal structure is established (97.8% of correct structures identifiable within the first 2–4 of 28 steps). The entire pipeline operates without retraining the base DM or invoking an MLLM during generation.

## Results

On the PAP-Optics subset, LINA (SD-3.5) achieves 97.4% success rate, and on PAP-Density it reaches 92.3%, both substantially above the SD-3.5 baseline (80.4% and 54.2% respectively). For counterfactual generation, LINA attains 79.5% on Winoground and 84.3% on PAP-OOD, outperforming the strongest baseline (LMD at 73.1% and 75.2%). When applied to FLUX.1, LINA further improves to 96.8% (Optics), 94.0% (Density), 83.0% (Winoground), and 86.1% (PAP-OOD), demonstrating cross-model generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LINA outperforms all five baselines across every metric. Against the SD-3.5 baseline (Esser et al.), LINA gains +17.0 points on Optics, +38.1 on Density, +25.1 on Winoground, and +15.0 on PAP-OOD. Against FLUX.1 (Black Forest Labs), LINA still leads by +9.9 (Optics), +29.7 (Density), +17.5 (Winoground), and +5.5 (PAP-OOD). Among correction methods, LMD (Lian et al.) improves Density to 81.5% but barely moves Optics (80.5%) and lags LINA by 6.4 points on Winoground; PPAD (Lv et al.) reaches 91.7% on Optics but underperforms LINA by 5.7 points there and by 16.9 on Winoground; LoRA (von Platen et al.) achieves 95.9% on Optics and 91.3% on Density yet collapses to 57.3% on Winoground, confirming that weight-level finetuning overfits to correlations rather than fixing causal dynamics. LINA's largest relative advantage is on counterfactual tasks (Winoground, PAP-OOD), where it surpasses the best baseline by 6.4 and 9.1 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) by adding a contrastive causal term that isolates the signal from relation tokens, and modifies the time-shifting schedule of modern flow-matching DMs (SD-3.5-large) to front-load computation into the early denoising steps. Conceptually, it departs from three existing correction paradigms—prompt rewriting (LMD), MLLM-in-the-loop refinement (PPAD), and weight finetuning (LoRA)—by instead targeting the guidance dynamics F directly, arguing that the DM's internal knowledge is sufficient but misrouted. The Causal Scene Graph (CSG) and Physical Alignment Probe (PAP) dataset are novel diagnostic tools that unify causal-graphical-model reasoning with scene-graph spatial structure, providing a formal language for probing multi-hop physical reasoning in DMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification-correction gap (GPT-5 detects 99.5% of physical violations while GPT-Image corrects only 2.5%) is a striking demonstration that recognition and generation are decoupled in current systems, with implications for world-simulator development. The finding that causal structure crystallizes in the first 2–4 of 28 denoising steps offers a concrete, actionable target for future scheduling research. Limitations include reliance on MLLM-based evaluation (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) for both dataset construction and scoring, the CSG extraction's acknowledged robustness challenges for continuous semantic manifolds, and the fact that the method's gains, while consistent, are built on a relatively simple two-term guidance modification whose ceiling is unclear for more complex multi-object causal scenes.
