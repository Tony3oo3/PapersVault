## Problem

Vision-language encoders suffer from two structural limitations: patch-level visual features are weakly aligned with language (degrading open-vocabulary segmentation, retrieval, and localization), and the thousands of patch tokens per image impose prohibitive memory and compute costs for long videos. The paper argues that patches are a suboptimal representational unit—too fine to be semantically meaningful yet too numerous to scale—and that existing token-compression and dense-alignment methods address these issues in isolation rather than jointly.

## Input / Output

T-REN takes an RGB image (or video frames at 5 FPS) and a 2D grid of P point prompts (e.g., 24×24 for segmentation) as input, producing a compact, content-adaptive set of M text-aligned region tokens per image (M varies with scene complexity) and, for video, temporally aggregated track tokens. For segmentation, the k=3 tokens per point are average-pooled and scored against C text embeddings to yield a 24×24×C logit map upsampled to 384×384; for video localization, track tokens are matched to a text-plus-visual query to output a temporal window. The token count is reduced by 24× for images and up to 254× for videos relative to the patch-based backbone, and remains nearly constant as input resolution increases.

## Method

T-REN adds a lightweight decoder (3.7% extra parameters) on top of a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L backbone. A grid of point prompts, encoded via Gaussian Random Fourier Features plus learned token embeddings, generates k=3 point queries per location that attend to patch tokens through L=2 cross-attention/self-attention decoder layers; a final single-head cross-attention (no value or output projections) pools patches into region tokens while preserving the backbone's feature space. Highly similar tokens (cosine > 0.975 or mask IoU > 0.8) are merged to remove redundancy, and for video, a streaming greedy-matching step aggregates per-frame tokens into track tokens (similarity > 0.65). Training jointly optimizes four objectives—visual contrastive loss (same-mask tokens cluster), text-aligned contrastive loss (region–text alignment), cosine distillation to backbone-pooled targets, and BCE+DICE supervision on cross-attention masks—using Hungarian matching to handle the variable number of target regions per prompt. The key architectural departure from its predecessor REN is predicting multiple tokens per prompt to capture part-whole hierarchy, combined with end-to-end text alignment rather than post-hoc projection.

## Results

On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, T-REN achieves 30.6 mIoU on ADE20K and 52.7 mIoU on Cityscapes, improving over the DINOv3 dino.txt backbone by +5.9 and +15.8 mIoU respectively. On the Visual Haystacks single-needle challenge, T-REN scores 88.5 (D=1) and 65.2 (D=1K), outperforming all open-source MLLMs and retrieval-augmented methods while using 24.4× fewer tokens. On video tasks, T-REN reaches 52.4 Recall@1 on Ego4D query localization (vs. 36.8 for the backbone) with 187.5× token compression, and 38.3 mIoU on VSPW scene parsing (vs. 20.7) with 254.5× compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-REN wins against all eight baselines across every evaluated task. Against its direct backbone DINOv3 dino.txt [10], T-REN gains +5.9 mIoU on ADE20K, +15.8 mIoU on Cityscapes, +18.4% R@1 on COCO retrieval, and +15.6% Recall@1 on Ego4D, while reducing token counts by 24× (images) and 187× (videos). Against its predecessor REN [11], T-REN improves by +5.7% recall on COCO retrieval (multi-token advantage) and +13.4 mIoU on VSPW video scene parsing. Against SAM-guided OVSS methods—Trident [1] (25.6/46.9), RADSeg+ [26] (29.9/45.8), and TextRegion [34] (27.3/47.4)—T-REN surpasses all three on both ADE20K and Cityscapes without requiring an external segmentation model or higher input resolution. Against earlier patch-based encoders, the margins are large: CLIP [23] (6.0/11.5), SigLIP-2 [30] (10.8/16.3), and PE [3] (17.6/21.4) all trail T-REN by 13–47 mIoU, confirming that region-level pooling with joint text alignment is a substantially stronger representational unit than raw patch tokens for dense vision-language tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-REN extends REN (Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2025), which introduced cross-attention-based region token generation from point prompts over frozen patch features, by producing multiple tokens per prompt to capture part-whole hierarchy and by jointly training the pooling module with region-level text alignment. The frozen backbone is DINOv3-based dino.txt (Jose et al., CVPR 2024), whose text encoder and text-alignment vision block provide the target embedding space. Conceptually, the paper departs from both token-compression methods (which discard information) and patch-level dense alignment methods (which inherit semantic fragmentation) by arguing that the region, not the patch, is the correct representational unit for dense vision-language modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous improvement in dense alignment and order-of-magnitude token reduction (24× for images, up to 254× for videos) makes T-REN practically relevant for episodic memory storage, streaming video, and edge deployment where patch-based representations are infeasible. A notable limitation is the dependence on a strong pretrained backbone (DINOv3) and a fixed 24×24 prompt grid, and the paper does not evaluate global image-level tasks since the backbone's global representation is unchanged. The authors identify end-to-end training of region-based vision-language models as a natural next step, and whether the region-token paradigm transfers to 3D or multi-modal settings remains open.
