## Problem

Anomaly generation for industrial inspection is typically framed as few-shot fine-tuning with real anomalous samples, which contradicts the very scarcity that motivates synthetic data creation and overfits to category-specific priors. Existing zero-shot approaches either rely on low-resolution attention maps for localization (producing blurry, inaccurate masks) or on cut-paste composition (yielding poor photometric realism). The paper addresses the gap of generating photorealistic, precisely localized anomalies from a single normal image with no anomalous training data, no fine-tuning, and no task-specific retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single normal RGB image, a minimal normal text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a {object}'), and a corresponding anomaly prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a {object} with {anomaly type} on it'), plus a SAM-derived foreground mask for spatial prior. It produces a photorealistic anomalous image with a local defect and a binary pixel-level mask localizing the generated anomaly. Generation operates in the latent space of a pretrained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5 or FLUX.1-dev) with T=100 denoising steps and a partial-noise warm start at γ=0.3, so the input image is encoded, partially noised, and then denoised rather than starting from pure Gaussian noise.

## Method

DeltaDeno runs two synchronized diffusion branches conditioned on the normal and anomaly prompts under an identical schedule, accumulating per-step ℓ₂ discrepancies between their denoiser predictions into an image-specific attribution map that serves as the anomaly mask. A two-stage denoising protocol is used: during the early stage, deltas are accumulated to form a coarse mask M_mid at the midpoint; during the late stage, mask-guided latent inpainting confines subsequent edits to the anomaly region while preserving the normal-image distribution outside. To sharpen localization, the method applies token-level prompt refinement (anomaly semantic distillation toward a detailed anchor plus MSE context alignment on non-anomaly tokens) and a spatially-biased cross-attention that injects the current mask into the attention logits exclusively for the anomaly token. The entire pipeline is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring no anomalous pixels, no fine-tuning, and no architecture-specific tuning, and it transfers seamlessly from Stable Diffusion v1.5 to FLUX.1-dev.

## Results

On MVTec AD downstream detection (Table 2), DeltaDeno with Stable Diffusion v1.5 achieves mean AUC_i=84.7, AP_i=91.3, AUC_p=84.9, AP_p=30.5, outperforming AnomalyAny (77.6/87.1/83.3/26.5) and AnoStyler (84.4/91.5/78.2/30.2). With the FLUX.1-dev backbone, performance further improves to AUC_i=89.7, AP_i=94.8, AUC_p=85.4, AP_p=41.5. On VisA (Table 3), DeltaDeno (SD1.5) reaches mean AUC_i=82.1, AP_i=85.7, AUC_p=90.1, AP_p=11.6, surpassing both ZSAG baselines. For generation quality (Table 1), DeltaDeno attains the highest mean Inception Score (2.05) and IC-LPIPS (0.36) across 15 MVTec AD categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two ZSAG baselines, DeltaDeno (SD1.5) clearly outperforms AnomalyAny on all four MVTec AD metrics (e.g., AUC_i 84.7 vs 77.6, AUC_p 84.9 vs 83.3) and on VisA (AUC_i 82.1 vs 74.7, AUC_p 90.1 vs 87.6). Versus AnoStyler, DeltaDeno wins on pixel-level metrics (AUC_p 84.9 vs 78.2 on MVTec AD; 90.1 vs 86.0 on VisA) but is slightly behind on AP_i for MVTec AD (91.3 vs 91.5) and AP_p for VisA (11.6 vs 16.6). The FLUX backbone further widens the gap, reaching AUC_i=89.7 and AP_p=41.5 on MVTec AD. For generation quality (Table 1), DeltaDeno achieves the best mean IS (2.05) and IC-LPIPS (0.36), surpassing Crop-Paste (1.51/0.14), DFMGAN (1.72/0.20), AnomalyDiffusion (1.80/0.32), AnomalyAny (2.02/0.33), and AnoStyler (2.04/0.32). Notably, DeltaDeno is the only method that is fully training-free and requires no anomalous samples, while FSAG baselines (AnomalyDiffusion, DFMGAN) depend on few-shot fine-tuning with real defects.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeltaDeno repurposes the contrastive-prompt idea from DiffEdit (comparing denoiser outputs under a shared schedule) and the mask-guided blending from Blended Latent Diffusion, but applies them to the anomaly-generation setting in a two-stage denoising pipeline that no prior AG work has used. It departs from the dominant few-shot fine-tuning paradigm (AnomalyDiffusion, AnoGen, DefectFill) by requiring zero anomalous samples, and from the closest ZSAG competitor AnomalyAny by replacing low-resolution cross-attention masks with an accumulated per-step denoising-difference map that is sharper and image-specific. The token-level prompt refinement and spatially-biased cross-attention draw on Attend-and-Excite and Prompt-to-Prompt ideas, adapted to keep semantic divergence localized to a single anomaly token.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any anomalous pixels or fine-tuning, DeltaDeno addresses the core practical bottleneck in industrial anomaly detection—defects are rare, expensive to collect, and category-specific—while still producing masks and images that improve downstream U-Net detection by a consistent margin over prior ZSAG methods on both MVTec AD and VisA. The model-agnostic design (validated on both SD1.5 and FLUX) suggests the delta-denoising attribution principle could transfer to other diffusion backbones without re-derivation. Open questions remain: evaluation protocols for ZSAG are still rudimentary, fine-grained controllability over defect attributes is limited, and abstract or logically inconsistent defects (as opposed to surface-level structural changes) are not yet addressable.
