## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to maintain precise alignment with complex compositional prompts, producing missing objects, attribute leakage, or spatial inconsistencies. Existing training-free inference-time methods address either noise initialization or denoising trajectory in isolation: initialization methods cannot simultaneously guarantee reliable semantic injection and preservation of the Gaussian noise distribution, while trajectory methods lack explicit mechanisms to diagnose and correct semantic errors mid-generation, allowing errors to propagate and accumulate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262244/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a standard RGB image as output, operating entirely at inference time on pretrained diffusion models (SDXL or HunyuanDiT) without any fine-tuning. The prompt is consumed in three distinct ways: by CLIP (OpenCLIP ViTB/32) for deterministic semantic prior extraction, by the diffusion U-Net/Transformer as the standard text condition, and by a VLM (Qwen-VL-Chat) for mid-generation semantic diagnosis. Generation uses DDIM sampling with 50 steps and CFG scale 5, with the Reflective Steering loop inserted at every denoising step (stride τ=1).

## Method

AnchorSteer is a training-free framework with two synergistic components. Semantic Anchoring first extracts a text-aligned pseudo-image from CLIP via Direct Ascent Synthesis, then refines it through a novel Latent-Prior Score Distillation Sampling (LP-SDS) objective that distills the frozen diffusion model's generative prior into the CLIP-derived latent, bridging the domain gap while preserving semantics; the refined latent is then diffused to the terminal step to yield initial noise that is both semantically aligned and distributionally consistent with N(0,I). Reflective Steering inserts a Think-Erase-Retouch loop at selected timesteps: Think decodes the current estimate and uses a VLM to produce structured dual diagnoses (missing content D_pos and inconsistent content D_neg); Erase performs negative-guided DDIM inversion to actively suppress erroneous semantics; Retouch re-denoises with an enhanced prompt c⁺ = c ∪ D_pos to progressively accumulate the missing attributes, enabling targeted mid-generation self-correction that prevents error propagation.

## Results

On GenEval (SDXL), AnchorSteer achieves an overall score of 63.39, outperforming the strongest baseline (Diffusion DPO at 60.74) by 2.65 points and the standard baseline (55.93) by 7.46 points. The largest gains appear on Counting (52.50 vs. 30.31 standard) and Position (17.75 vs. 11.00). On T2I-CompBench++, AnchorSteer attains the best score on 7 of 8 semantic categories, with notable improvements in Color (0.6712 vs. 0.5766), Texture (0.6369 vs. 0.5353), and Number (0.5796 vs. 0.5029). Cross-architecture evaluation confirms consistent gains: +7.46 on SDXL and +4.13 on HunyuanDiT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnchorSteer (63.39 overall) outperforms all seven baselines on GenEval overall, with the closest competitor being Diffusion DPO (60.74), a training-based preference-optimization method, trailing by 2.65 points. The largest margin is on Counting, where AnchorSteer scores 52.50 versus DNO's 44.06 (the best baseline on that sub-metric), a gap of 8.44 points. Notably, AnchorSteer does not win on every sub-category: Diffusion DPO edges it out on Two Objects (92.42 vs. 91.16), and on T2I-CompBench++ the Zigzag method slightly surpasses AnchorSteer on the Non-Spatial category (0.3206 vs. 0.3171). Among noise-initialization baselines (InitNO, DNO, NPNet), AnchorSteer leads by 4.85–5.28 points overall, confirming that the combination of Semantic Anchoring and Reflective Steering provides substantially more than initialization alone. On T2I-CompBench++, AnchorSteer achieves the best score on 7 of 8 categories, with the most dramatic gains in Color (+0.0946 over standard) and Texture (+0.1016 over standard), while the Non-Spatial category remains the sole point of loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnchorSteer sits at the intersection of two lines of training-free T2I alignment work: noise initialization and denoising trajectory optimization. For initialization, it repurposes Direct Ascent Synthesis (DAS) to extract CLIP semantic priors and extends Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DreamFusion into a latent-space noise optimization objective (LP-SDS) that bridges the domain gap between CLIP and diffusion priors. For trajectory correction, it builds on the bidirectional re-noising/re-denoising paradigm introduced by Zigzag diffusion sampling, but adds an explicit VLM-based diagnostic loop (Think-Erase-Retouch) that prior bidirectional methods lack. The key departure from existing manipulation-based noise methods is the explicit distribution-consistency guarantee via DDPM forward diffusion, and the departure from existing trajectory methods is the structured dual-diagnosis (missing vs. inconsistent content) rather than implicit scalar scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnchorSteer provides a practical, training-free, architecture-agnostic recipe for improving compositional faithfulness in T2I generation, validated on both U-Net (SDXL) and Transformer (HunyuanDiT) backbones. The main open question is efficiency: at 68.5 s per image (vs. 3.7 s standard), the method is impractical for interactive use, and the authors note that applying it to few-step distilled models is a promising but unexplored direction. The finding that a 2B VLM achieves most of the 9.6B VLM's benefit (62.07 vs. 63.39 overall) suggests the diagnostic component may be lighter than expected, but the full pipeline's latency remains a barrier to widespread adoption. Whether the Think-Erase-Retouch loop generalizes to video diffusion or longer-horizon generation tasks remains unaddressed.
