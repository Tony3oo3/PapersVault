## Problem

Primitive-based representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting offer superior rendering speed, editability, and scalability over neural radiance fields, but their limited per-primitive expressivity forces high-frequency spatial and directional detail to be captured by increasing primitive count, inflating memory and slowing rendering. Neural fields solve this with positional encodings and MLP decoding, but their globally anchored (Eulerian) structures lose the Lagrangian advantages of primitives under motion, deformation, and editing. The paper asks whether primitives themselves can serve as local positional encodings to recover neural-field-level expressivity without sacrificing the structural benefits of explicit representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses to optimize a set of 3D Gaussian primitives, each carrying four learnable feature vectors (16-dim per vertex by default, 64-dim total per primitive). For semantic reconstruction, it additionally supervises on 512-dimensional LSEG feature maps rendered at RGB resolution and bilinearly downsampled. The output is a real-time renderable scene (140+ FPS on RTX A6000 Ada) producing novel-view RGB images, and optionally high-dimensional semantic feature fields decoded from the same primitive representation. For the 2D image fitting application, the input is a single 45.7 MP 14-bit HDR RAW image and the output is a compressed representation evaluated at 10× and 100× compression ratios.

## Method

Neural Harmonic Textures reinterprets each Gaussian primitive as both a geometric carrier and a local positional encoding by attaching learnable feature vectors to the four vertices of a virtual tetrahedron inscribing the Gaussian's isosurface in whitened canonical space. At ray-primitive intersection, vertex features are barycentrically interpolated at the point of maximum Gaussian response, then passed through sine and cosine activations to produce a harmonic decomposition of the local signal. These activated features are alpha-blended along the ray (with primitive opacity acting as harmonic amplitude), and the accumulated high-dimensional signal is decoded into RGB by a single shallow MLP (128×3 hidden layers) in one deferred image-space pass, eliminating per-primitive neural evaluations. Training follows 3DGS-MCMC densification with cosine-annealed learning rates, EMA smoothing on MLP weights, and a final 3000-iteration refinement phase that optimizes only features and decoder. The formulation is primitive-agnostic, extending directly to 2D Gaussians (virtual triangles) and explicit triangle meshes (three vertex features), and generalizes to arbitrary signal dimensions by expanding the MLP output head.

## Results

On MipNeRF360, Neural Harmonic Textures (NHT) achieves 28.74 dB PSNR, 0.834 SSIM, and 0.216 LPIPS, outperforming the closest baselines Spherical Voronoi (28.56 dB) and ZipNeRF (28.55 dB). On Tanks & Temples, NHT reaches 25.68 dB PSNR and 0.141 LPIPS, a clear margin over Spherical Voronoi (24.80 dB) and 3DGS-MCMC (24.46 dB). On Deep Blending, NHT scores 30.94 dB PSNR and 0.919 SSIM, surpassing Spherical Voronoi (30.34 dB) and 3DGUT-MCMC (29.87 dB). In the controlled comparison (Table 2, same 1M primitives, 30k iterations), NHT improves over 3DGUT+SH by 0.53 dB PSNR on MipNeRF360 while maintaining real-time rendering at 140 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NHT achieves the best PSNR on all three benchmarks, with the largest margins on Tanks & Temples where it leads Spherical Voronoi by 0.88 dB and 3DGS-MCMC by 1.22 dB. On MipNeRF360, the gap over the nearest competitors (Spherical Voronoi at 28.56 dB, ZipNeRF at 28.55 dB) is smaller (~0.18–0.19 dB) but NHT still wins on LPIPS (0.216 vs. 0.218/0.228). On Deep Blending, NHT leads Spherical Voronoi by 0.60 dB PSNR, though SV has a marginally better LPIPS (0.299 vs. 0.302). In the controlled same-framework comparison (Table 2), NHT consistently outperforms both SH and Spherical Voronoi appearance models under identical training budgets, confirming the gain comes from the harmonic texturing mechanism rather than training-schedule differences. Against hybrid neural-field/primitive methods (NeST, Radiance Meshes), NHT wins by 1.6–2.2 dB PSNR on MipNeRF360 while retaining the editability and scalability advantages of purely primitive-based representations. For semantic reconstruction (Table 4), NHT surpasses Feature 3DGS by 1.89 dB RGB PSNR and 3.22 dB LSEG PSNR while using roughly 3× less memory and rendering at 28 FPS vs. 3 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting / 3DGUT / 3DGS-MCMC lineage by reinterpreting primitives as local positional encodings rather than merely geometric carriers. It departs from both globally-anchored neural field encodings (hash grids, triplanes) and per-primitive MLP decoding by anchoring learnable feature vectors to a virtual tetrahedral scaffold around each primitive, applying sine/cosine harmonic activations, and deferring all neural decoding to a single image-space pass. This positions the work between pure primitive methods (limited per-primitive expressivity) and hybrid primitive+neural-field approaches (which inherit Eulerian limitations in motion, editing, and scalability), while also distinguishing itself from per-primitive texture methods (BB-Splat, Textured Gaussians) that increase storage and complicate optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, drop-in upgrade for existing primitive-based pipelines (3DGUT, 2DGS, Triangle Splatting) that closes the high-frequency detail gap with neural fields while preserving Lagrangian advantages (motion, deformation, editing, scalability). The deferred-shading formulation—reducing MLP evaluations from per-primitive to per-pixel—is a broadly applicable efficiency insight. Open questions include whether the approach can overfit under very sparse supervision, whether automatic level-of-detail can be extracted from the harmonic decomposition, and whether kernel functions can be removed entirely since derivatives can propagate to explicit geometry without them. The generality demonstrated across semantic reconstruction and HDR image compression suggests the harmonic-texture paradigm may extend to other signal-reconstruction tasks.
