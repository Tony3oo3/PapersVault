## Problem

Existing navigation world models perform action-conditioned future prediction in VAE-compressed latent spaces, where spatial compression discards fine-grained geometric structure and causes severe structural collapse during long-horizon rollouts. This paper asks whether modeling navigation dynamics directly in a dense, uncompressed visual representation space (DINOv2 patch tokens) can preserve spatial geometry and improve action controllability, and provides a linear dynamics probe showing that DINOv2 features exhibit significantly stronger linear predictability for action-conditioned transitions than VAE latents or other common encoders.

## Input / Output

The model consumes m first-person RGB context frames (encoded by a frozen DINOv2 into 256 spatial patch tokens of dimension 768), a planar motion action vector [u_x, u_y, ω], and a prediction horizon k. It outputs the future visual state as a 256×768 DINOv2 token sequence; a frozen RAE decoder reconstructs RGB pixels solely for qualitative visualization and pixel-level metrics, while all planning and trajectory scoring operate directly in token space using DINO feature distance to a goal image. For closed-loop navigation, CEM samples 120 candidate action sequences and scores each via an eight-step token-space rollout, selecting the trajectory with minimum DINO distance to the goal.

## Method

RAE-NWM trains only a generative transition model between a frozen DINOv2 encoder and a frozen RAE decoder, using flow matching with a linear interpolation path and velocity-matching loss. The generative backbone is a CDiT-DH: a deep Conditional Diffusion Transformer (stacked blocks with self-attention, cross-attention to context tokens, and AdaLN modulation) followed by a shallow, wide Decoupled Diffusion Transformer head that predicts the velocity field in the high-dimensional token space. The key architectural contribution is a time-driven dynamics gating module: Gaussian Fourier embeddings of the action, horizon, and flow time are fused into a dynamics feature c_dyn, which is then multiplied by a learned gate g(t_emb) (linear + SiLU + Sigmoid) before being added to the time embedding and injected via AdaLN. This gate adaptively strengthens kinematic conditioning at high-noise stages (establishing global topology) and relaxes it at low-noise stages (refining local details), replacing rigid additive conditioning. During inference, an ODE solver integrates the probability flow from t=1 to t=0 for each prediction step, and the generated token is appended to a sliding context window for sequential long-horizon rollouts entirely in representation space.

## Results

On the SACSoN dataset, RAE-NWM reduces Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) to 2.91 m and Relative Pose Error (RPE) to 0.70, outperforming the primary baseline NWM (4.12 m / 0.96) and end-to-end policies GNM (3.71 m) and NoMaD (3.73 m). In closed-loop Habitat Image-Goal navigation, RAE-NWM achieves a Success Rate of 78.95% and SPL of 63.58%, surpassing NWM (43.33% / 38.66%) and One-Step WM (72.67% / 69.10% SPL). For direct 16-second prediction, RAE-NWM achieves FID 15.90 versus NWM's 33.06, and LPIPS 0.349 versus 0.470, demonstrating substantially better long-horizon structural fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline NWM [5], RAE-NWM wins on SACSoN (ATE 2.91 vs 4.12, RPE 0.70 vs 0.96) and SCAND (ATE 1.14 vs 1.28, RPE 0.28 vs 0.33), but trails on the unstructured RECON dataset (ATE 1.36 vs 1.13, RPE 0.37 vs 0.35), which the authors attribute to VAE latents preserving more texture-sensitive cues for dense off-road vegetation. Against end-to-end policies GNM [32] and NoMaD [38], RAE-NWM achieves lower ATE on all three datasets (e.g., 2.91 vs 3.71/3.73 on SACSoN) and lower RPE on SACSoN and SCAND. In Habitat closed-loop navigation, RAE-NWM achieves the highest Success Rate (78.95%) among all methods, beating One-Step WM [36] (72.67%) by 6.3 points, though One-Step WM retains a higher SPL (69.10% vs 63.58%) due to its more efficient one-step generation under a fixed compute budget. RAE-NWM also substantially outperforms OmniVLA [18] (SR 78.95% vs 36.67%, SPL 63.58% vs 34.78%) and NoMaD [38] (SR 78.95% vs 16.67%) in the Habitat setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the VAE-latent-space paradigm established by Navigation World Models (NWM) [5] by shifting action-conditioned dynamics modeling into the uncompressed DINOv2 token space, following the RAE [53] reconstruction framework. It also moves beyond the deterministic feature-prediction approach of DINO-WM [54] and V-JEPA 2 [3] by employing a continuous generative (flow-matching) formulation with a CDiT-DH backbone [27,44], specifically tailored for first-person navigation rollouts. The time-driven gating mechanism for adaptive kinematic conditioning is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior navigation world models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnostic (linear dynamics probe) and empirical evidence that dense self-supervised representations natively support action-conditioned dynamics, offering a clear recipe for building more stable navigation world models. However, the texture-fidelity trade-off on unstructured terrains (RECON) and the lack of a fully scale-matched comparison (350M vs. 1B parameters) leave open whether the gains are purely representational or partly capacity-driven. Accelerating iterative dense-space rollouts for real-time planning and recovering high-frequency texture detail remain practical open questions.
