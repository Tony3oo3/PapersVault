## Problem

Existing remote sensing benchmarks for MLLMs focus on perception and reasoning but lack the ability to evaluate constrained planning capability, due to the difficulty of curating and validating planning tasks at scale and the absence of accurate, fine-grained evaluation protocols. This paper introduces NeSy-Route, a large-scale neuro-symbolic benchmark that hierarchically assesses textual constraint understanding, text-image constraint alignment, and end-to-end constrained route planning across 10,821 samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261301/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a remote sensing RGB image (segmented into 8 land-cover classes via OpenEarthMap masks), a natural language query specifying agent type and routing objective, start/end pixel coordinates, and annotated region IDs. Outputs are structured as symbolic vectors rather than free text: a binary traversability vector (V_trav ∈ {0,1}^8), an ordinal preference ranking vector (V_pref ∈ {0,...,K}^8), a region identification vector mapping classes to region IDs, and a sparse waypoint trajectory. The sparse waypoints are reconstructed into dense pixel-level paths via A* (for compliant samples) or Bresenham interpolation (for non-compliant samples) before metric computation.

## Method

The core contribution is an automated, symbolized data generation framework that produces provably optimal ground-truth trajectories. It begins with a knowledge base defining agent-specific traversability (always/conditionally/non-traversable) and priority rankings over 8 land-cover classes, then uses dual-LLM query synthesis (DeepSeek-V3.2 generates natural language queries, Gemini-3-Pro verifies semantic compliance) with a self-inference loop to ensure generated text re-derives the correct symbolic vectors. Visual grounding applies iterative morphological erosion to OpenEarthMap segmentation masks to isolate pure-class regions, followed by a region-level connectivity graph masked by traversability constraints. Optimal trajectories are computed via A* search with a cost map derived from the preference vectors, where admissibility and consistency of the Euclidean heuristic guarantee global optimality. Evaluation employs a three-level hierarchical protocol with task-specific metrics: Traversability Matching, Kendall-Tau Preference Ranking, and Fully Matching Accuracy for constraint parsing; Region Matching Rate for visual grounding; and Adherence Rate, Violation Ratio, Cost Ratio, and Chamfer Distance for trajectory quality.

## Results

On the NeSy-Route benchmark, even the strongest closed-source model (Gemini-3-Pro) achieves only 18.70% Adherence Rate (AR) on the end-to-end Constrained Route Planning task (Task 3), with a Cost Ratio (CR) of 1.24 and Chamfer Distance (CD) of 68.74 relative to the optimal trajectory. On Task 1 (Textual Constraint Understanding), Gemini-3-Pro leads with 98.34% Traversability Matching (TM) and 92.24% Fully Matching Accuracy (FM), while the best open-source model (Qwen3.5-27B) reaches 80.32% TM. Performance drops sharply on Task 2 (Text-Image Constraint Alignment), where the best average TM is only 52.79% (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B), revealing a critical perception bottleneck. Across all 10,821 planning samples, no model exceeds 32.70% AR, confirming that constrained route planning remains far beyond current MLLM capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 10 MLLMs (3 closed-source, 7 open-source) across three hierarchical tasks. On Task 1, Gemini-3-Pro dominates with 98.34% TM and 92.24% FM, far ahead of GPT-5.1 (77.02% TM) and Qwen3-VL-Plus (72.97% TM); among open-source models, Qwen3.5-27B (80.32% TM) surpasses GPT-5.1. On Task 2, the ranking shifts: Qwen3-VL-Plus achieves the best average TM (37.34%) and PR (0.634), while Gemini-3-Pro leads in Region Matching (71.01% RM) but lags in constraint alignment. On Task 3, Qwen3-VL-32B attains the highest AR (32.70%) but with a poor CR of 12.87, whereas Gemini-3-Pro achieves the best trajectory quality (CR 1.24, CD 68.74) among compliant paths despite a lower AR (18.70%). The MoE-based Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B is the strongest open-source model on Task 2 (PR 0.702, TM 52.79%), substantially outperforming GPT-5.1 on logical alignment. Overall, no model demonstrates reliable end-to-end planning: the best AR is 32.70%, meaning nearly 70% of generated trajectories violate traversability constraints, and even the best CR (1.24) indicates a 24% cost penalty over the optimal route.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on OpenEarthMap's eight-class land-cover taxonomy and high-fidelity segmentation masks as the visual substrate, but departs fundamentally from prior remote sensing benchmarks (RSVQA, VRS-Bench, XLRS-Bench) that evaluate perception and descriptive reasoning. The closest prior work, XLRS-Bench, introduced route planning in remote sensing but only as a multiple-choice selection task; NeSy-Route extends this to generative, symbolically constrained waypoint trajectory planning with provably optimal ground truths derived via A* search. The neuro-symbolic evaluation protocol—decoupling textual constraint parsing, text-image alignment, and trajectory generation into three hierarchical stages with a symbolic evaluator—represents a methodological departure from purely neural or purely human-annotated evaluation paradigms used in prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeSy-Route provides the first large-scale, objectively verifiable benchmark for constrained route planning in remote sensing, enabling fine-grained diagnosis of where MLLMs fail (perception vs. reasoning vs. planning). The finding that even frontier models achieve only ~19% adherence in trajectory generation despite near-perfect textual constraint understanding highlights a fundamental cognitive gap that current architectures cannot bridge, pointing toward the need for new model designs that integrate symbolic planning with visual perception. The paper is limited to 2D semantic planning over static imagery, leaving agentic multi-step workflows, dynamic environments, and real-world physical interaction as open problems for future work.
