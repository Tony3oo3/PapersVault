## Problem

Existing KB-VQA methods rely on fixed retrieve-then-generate pipelines with a preselected retriever and static top-k setting, which cannot adaptively choose between image and text retrieval, recover from failed entity identification, or progressively build multi-hop evidence chains. The paper addresses this by formulating KB-VQA as a budgeted, closed-loop search-and-reasoning problem where the model decides when to retrieve, which modality to use, and when to stop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262516/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a free-form text answer. During inference, the model generates structured outputs at each step (reasoning text, an action token from {img_search, text_search, stop}, and JSON arguments such as query rewrites and exclusion lists). Retrieved Wikipedia pages are compressed into short question-conditioned summaries before being appended to the context, and previously retrieved pages are excluded via a deduplication list to prevent redundant retrieval. Tool-call budgets are enforced (up to 3 image searches and 3 text searches per trajectory, with a maximum of 7 interaction steps).

## Method

ProMSA parameterizes a single multimodal LLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or Qwen3-VL-2B/8B) as a unified generative policy that jointly handles entity alignment, knowledge completion, and answer generation within one trajectory. At each step the model selects an action (image search, text search, or stop) conditioned on the accumulated evidence, with explicit deduplication and content summarization to control context growth. Training is two-stage: first, rejection-sampling SFT on ~3K trajectories teaches valid tool-call formats and interaction patterns; second, reinforcement learning with TN-GSPO optimizes the search policy. TN-GSPO extends sequence-level GSPO by normalizing the policy ratio by both the number of trainable generated tokens and the tool-interaction depth (H(τ) = 1 + #tool_calls), preventing length bias and aligning update scale with the budgeted decision structure. A sparse reward combines answer correctness, format compliance, and a tool-cost penalty, with asymmetric clipping (low=0.2, high=0.28) for stable optimization.

## Results

On E-VQA, ProMSA with Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 52.6% (All) and 52.2% (Single-Hop) BERT-based Matching accuracy, outperforming the strongest retrieval-augmented baseline REAL (41.4% All) by over 11 points and the best search-agent baseline MMSearch-R1 (40.7% All) by ~12 points. On InfoSeek, ProMSA reaches 53.4% (All), 53.6% (Unseen-Q), and 53.3% (Unseen-E), surpassing CC-VQA (45.1% All) by 8.3 points and MaS-VQA (43.8% All) by 9.6 points. On OK-VQA, ProMSA (Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves 85.6% accuracy, exceeding CC-VQA (78.8%) by 6.8 points. Gains are consistent across model sizes: even the 2B variant (41.2% E-VQA All, 43.6% InfoSeek All) outperforms all prior methods using the same backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProMSA outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric on both E-VQA and InfoSeek. Against search-agent baselines, ProMSA (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) beats MMSearch-R1 by 9.0 points (49.7 vs. 40.7) on E-VQA All and 9.5 points (49.2 vs. 39.7) on InfoSeek All, and beats DeepEyesV2 by 10.2 and 7.9 points respectively. Against retrieval-augmented baselines, ProMSA (Qwen3-VL-8B) surpasses REAL by 11.2 points (52.6 vs. 41.4) on E-VQA All and 9.3 points (53.4 vs. 44.1) on InfoSeek All, and beats CC-VQA by 16.5 and 8.3 points on the same metrics. The largest gap is over EchoSight† (35.2/32.4), where ProMSA leads by 17.4 and 21.0 points, reflecting the advantage of adaptive multi-round retrieval over a single fixed RAG step. On OK-VQA, ProMSA (Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves 85.6%, exceeding CC-VQA (78.8%) by 6.8 points and Wiki-PRE-7B (77.8%) by 7.8 points, confirming generalization beyond the training benchmarks. Inference time is competitive at 1.8 s/sample (1.5× EchoSight), while delivering ~31 points higher accuracy than EchoSight (49.7 vs. 18.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the search-agent paradigm from MMSearch-R1 and Search-R1 to the KB-VQA setting, where the key departure is adaptive modality selection (image vs. text retrieval) with explicit deduplication and budget constraints, rather than the fixed retrieve-then-generate pipelines used by EchoSight, CC-VQA, and REAL. On the optimization side, it builds directly on GSPO's sequence-level normalization but introduces TN-GSPO, which additionally normalizes by tool-interaction depth to address the mismatch between generation-length variance and trajectory difficulty in multi-turn search. The rejection-sampling SFT cold-start follows standard practice for tool-use agents but is specifically designed to ensure format validity before RL exploration begins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that adaptive, budgeted multi-turn search substantially outperforms single-shot RAG for long-tail KB-VQA (7+ points over the best fixed-pipeline baseline), suggesting that the field's focus on stronger rerankers or larger top-k may be less impactful than learning *when* and *how* to retrieve. TN-GSPO is a general technique applicable to any RL setting with variable tool-interaction depth, potentially useful beyond KB-VQA. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to open-web search (beyond Wikipedia), how it handles entities entirely absent from the knowledge base, and whether the summarization step (which adds a Qwen3-VL-8B call per retrieval) can be made more efficient at scale.
