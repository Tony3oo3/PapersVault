## Problem

Pixel-level mask annotation for medical image segmentation is expensive and time-consuming, while conventional weak supervision (axis-aligned boxes, points, scribbles) provides insufficient geometric constraints for elongated or anisotropic lesions. Oriented bounding boxes (OBBs) offer a natural middle ground by encoding both spatial extent and orientation, but their rectangular geometry introduces shape bias that misleads segmentation models toward box-like predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262713/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 352×352 medical images from five modalities (colonoscopy, dermoscopy, MRI, CT, ultrasound) along with OBB annotations (four corner points per instance) and optional prompts (point, scribble, box, or circle) as input, producing a pixel-level binary segmentation mask. OBB annotations are simulated from ground-truth masks via random rotations and translations to mimic realistic human labeling. At inference, the model can operate in a prompt-free automatic mode or interactively with user-provided prompts, with per-image inference times of 8–12 ms using the SAM2 backbone.

## Method

OBBSeg is built on a ViT/SAM2 encoder (or Res2Net50) and introduces three key components. First, the Mask-to-OBB (M2O) loss removes rectangular shape bias by projecting predicted masks into each OBB's local coordinate frame via affine rotation, applying axis-wise max-projection to discard contour details, fusing the projections via element-wise minimum to reconstruct a box-shaped prediction, and inverse-transforming back for pixel-wise BCE+Dice supervision against the OBB ground truth. Second, two complementary encoder modules—PAFE (prompt-assisted foreground enhancer, which gates features with prompt-derived masks plus a residual) and DBFE (differential-based foreground enhancer, which subtracts a pooled background representation from foreground features to amplify contrast)—are injected at each stage to refine semantic representations. Third, a hierarchical Prompt Supervision Loss enforces consistency between intermediate stage predictions and the final mask with respect to the prompt, complementing the M2O and Scale Consistency losses in the total objective.

## Results

On the Five Standard Polyp Datasets (FSPD), SAM2+OBBSeg with Circle prompts achieves a weighted-average Dice of 94.0%, surpassing the best fully supervised method (EMCAD, 92.6%) and SAM3 with Box prompts (92.1%). On SUN-SEG, OBBSeg(Circle) reaches 95.0% Dice, exceeding SAM3(Box) at 93.9%. On skin lesion (ISIC-2017/2018) and nodule/tumor (TN3K/DDTI/BUSI) datasets, OBBSeg(Circle) achieves 94.4% and 92.0% average Dice respectively, outperforming all fully supervised baselines (best: EMCAD 89.1% skin, SANet 83.2% nodule/tumor). The M2O loss alone adds 1–2% Dice over WeakPolyp across all datasets without any architectural changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OBBSeg with Circle prompts (94.0% FSPD, 95.0% SUN-SEG) outperforms all fully supervised baselines, including EMCAD (92.6% FSPD), LGRNet (92.5% FSPD, 87.5% SUN-SEG), and CASCADE (84.7% FSPD), by 1.4–9.3 points depending on the dataset. Against the strongest prompt-based fully supervised methods, OBBSeg(Circle) exceeds SAM3(Box) by 1.9 points on FSPD and 1.1 points on SUN-SEG, and surpasses SAM2(Box) by 2.3 and 2.1 points respectively. On skin lesion and nodule/tumor datasets (Table 2), OBBSeg(Circle) achieves 94.4% and 92.0% average Dice, beating the best fully supervised methods (EMCAD 89.1% skin, SANet 83.2% nodule/tumor) by 5.3 and 8.8 points. Among weakly supervised methods, OBBSeg dominates: it outperforms WeakPolyp by 17.3 points on FSPD and 15.2 on SUN-SEG, and exceeds the next-best weak method (GazeMedSeg, 77.8% Kvasir) by 17.9 points. The M2O loss alone (WeakPolyp+M2O) already improves over WeakPolyp by 2.3–1.2 points on FSPD/SUN-SEG, confirming its plug-and-play utility independent of the full OBBSeg framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends WeakPolyp (MICCAI 2023), which used axis-aligned bounding boxes for weakly supervised polyp segmentation, by replacing them with Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBBs) that encode both spatial extent and orientation. The key methodological departure is the Mask-to-OBB (M2O) loss, a differentiable projection-based formulation that removes the rectangular shape bias inherent to box annotations without iterative refinement. Additionally, unlike SAM/SAM2 which inject prompts only at the decoder stage, OBBSeg introduces prompt-guided feature enhancement (PAFE and DBFE) directly into the encoder, combining geometric OBB supervision with semantic prompt guidance in a unified framework built on the frozen SAM2 encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OBBSeg offers a practical middle ground between expensive pixel-level annotation (91.1 s/image) and overly coarse weak labels, with OBB annotation costing only 17.8 s while providing orientation-aware geometric constraints. The demonstrated robustness to annotation noise (21.2% IoU deviation causing only 1.5% Dice drop, versus 12.2% for comparable box perturbation) makes the approach viable for real-world clinical annotation pipelines. However, the projection-based M2O loss provides limited supervision within the OBB interior, the method struggles with complex tree-like anatomical structures, and the framework is restricted to 2D static images—extending to 3D volumes and video remains open. Adaptive label refinement and hybrid supervision strategies are identified as natural next steps.
