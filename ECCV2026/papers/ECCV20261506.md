## Problem

Existing egocentric-exocentric video translation methods are limited to unidirectional exocentric-to-egocentric conversion under static camera settings, overlooking the inverse problem of reference-guided exocentric synthesis and failing to support dynamic scene navigation. This gap is critical for gaming, embodied AI, and VR applications where users need seamless perspective switching while a character freely wanders through open-world environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261506/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in two directions: (i) exocentric-to-egocentric takes a single exocentric video and produces the corresponding egocentric video; (ii) egocentric-to-exocentric takes an egocentric video plus a reference character image (back-view) and produces the exocentric video. No camera poses or depth maps are provided, enforcing geometry-free generalization. All videos are processed at 704×1280 resolution with 49 frames per clip, and the reference image is encoded through the same VAE as the video latents.

## Method

WorldWander fine-tunes the Wan2.2-5B video diffusion transformer via LoRA (rank 80) with two key architectural modifications. First, In-Context Perspective Alignment encodes all inputs (conditional video, target video, reference image) into a shared latent space via a unified VAE, keeps conditional latents noise-free while noising only the target latent, and concatenates them along the token dimension (rather than channel dimension) so that a single self-attention layer performs explicit cross-view interaction without auxiliary networks. Second, Collaborative Position Encoding independently applies rotary position embeddings to the conditional and target latents so they share identical temporal position indices, enforcing frame-wise correspondence between the two perspectives. Training uses the standard rectified-flow (flow-matching) loss, and the entire pipeline requires no depth estimation, camera pose estimation, or additional encoder modules.

## Results

On the EgoExo-8K benchmark, WorldWander achieves the best performance across all metrics in both translation directions. In synthetic scenarios, it attains FVD of 230.3 (exo→ego) and 353.7 (ego→exo), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ReCamMaster* (298.9 and 531.5 respectively), while also leading in LPIPS (0.5811 vs. 0.6039), SSIM (0.5416 vs. 0.5365), and CLIP-I character consistency (0.7672 vs. 0.7129). In real-world scenarios, the advantage widens: WorldWander reaches FVD 416.5 (exo→ego) and 374.7 (ego→exo) versus 716.1 and 968.1 for ReCamMaster*, with VBench scores of 0.8333 and 0.8142 respectively. A user study with 630 responses confirms WorldWander is preferred over all baselines on generation quality, perspective consistency, and character consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldWander outperforms all four baselines across every metric in both translation directions and both synthetic and real-world settings. Against the strongest competitor ReCamMaster* (fine-tuned on EgoExo-8K), WorldWander reduces FVD by 23% (230.3 vs. 298.9) in synthetic exo→ego and by 33% (353.7 vs. 531.5) in synthetic ego→exo, while also improving CLIP-I character consistency from 0.7129 to 0.7672. The gap widens dramatically on real-world data, where WorldWander achieves FVD 416.5 vs. 716.1 (exo→ego) and 374.7 vs. 968.1 (ego→exo) over ReCamMaster*. TrajectoryCrafter, a perspective re-orientation method, performs competitively on VBench (0.8171 in synthetic ego→exo) but cannot generate customized characters (no CLIP-I score) and lags significantly on FVD and LPIPS. AnyV2V and I2VEdit, general video-to-video translation frameworks, are the weakest baselines, with FVD values 3–7× higher than WorldWander, confirming that generic V2V methods fail to model egocentric-exocentric perspective correspondences.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldWander builds directly on the Wan2.2-5B video diffusion transformer, adapting its rectified-flow training and rotary position encoding for a new cross-view translation objective. It departs from prior exo-to-ego methods (4Diff, EgoExo-Gen, Exo2Ego-V) that rely on auxiliary condition encoders or cross-attention modules and are restricted to static cameras, instead using in-context token concatenation with collaborative attention within a single backbone. The Collaborative Position Encoding strategy is a targeted modification of standard 3D RoPE, splitting positional indices between conditional and target latents to enforce frame-wise alignment—a design not present in prior video diffusion work. The EgoExo-8K dataset extends static paired datasets (EgoExo4D, H2O, TACO) into dynamic open-world navigation with synchronized dual-perspective recordings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Bidirectional ego-exo translation in dynamic scenes is a prerequisite for immersive gaming, embodied AI, and VR where users expect seamless perspective switching around a persistent character. The in-context learning formulation (no auxiliary encoder, no camera pose or depth input) makes the approach lightweight and generalizable across camera rigs, and the EgoExo-8K dataset fills a clear gap as the first dynamic open-world ego-exo benchmark. Limitations include the short 5-second clip length (49 frames), dependence on a single backbone (Wan2.2-5B), and evaluation confined to the authors' own dataset; open questions include scalability to longer sequences, multi-character scenes, and whether the collaborative position encoding generalizes to other video transformer architectures.
