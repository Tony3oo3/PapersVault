## Problem

Existing visual grounding benchmarks are almost exclusively RGB-only, collected in clean environments with centered, salient objects, and provide limited coverage of low-illumination, adverse-weather, and small-object conditions that dominate real-world perception. This makes it impossible to systematically evaluate how grounding models degrade under degraded visual cues or to compare RGB-only, thermal-only, and fused RGB+TIR inputs under a unified protocol. The paper addresses this gap by introducing the first large-scale RGB-Thermal visual grounding benchmark and a reference baseline that explicitly handles cross-spectral and illumination challenges.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a spatially aligned RGB-TIR image pair (640×512 in the benchmark, resized to 224×224 for processing) together with a natural-language referring expression averaging 14.24 words, and regresses a single bounding box (x, y, w, h) via a lightweight MLP on a learnable [Reg] token. The unified evaluation protocol supports three input configurations—RGB-only, TIR-only, and RGB+TIR—so that any grounding model can be benchmarked under each modality setting with identical preprocessing and metrics (Acc@0.5 / Acc@0.7). The benchmark further partitions test data into TestA (normal-size, normal/strong light), TestB (weak/very-weak light), and TestC (small-size targets) to isolate specific failure modes.

## Method

RGBT-VGNet is built on frozen CLIP vision and text encoders with a VL-Transformer decoder and a regression head, and introduces three practical components. (1) Asymmetric Modality Adaptation (AMA) applies LoRA to the attention projections of each CLIP vision layer but assigns a higher rank to the TIR branch (r_v ≤ r_t), compensating for the RGB bias in pretrained VLP weights while using a hierarchical design for progressive low-to-high-level alignment. (2) Language-Aware Visual Synergy (LAVS) uses the textual embedding as a semantic query in cross-modal attention: text queries attend to RGB and TIR patch features separately, then the two modality-enhanced streams interact via cross-attention before linear projection into a unified space. (3) Illumination-Local-Global Tri-Prior Fusion (TPF) computes a per-patch RGB reliability weight from three priors—an illumination quality prior derived from grayscale patch intensity, a local semantic prior from an MLP over concatenated RGB/TIR patch features and their absolute difference, and a global channel-wise prior from pooled features—then fuses the two modality streams with this weight plus a channel gate on their difference. Training is supervised (AdamW, lr 1e-3, batch 8, 120 epochs on RTX 4090) with all CLIP parameters frozen and only the LoRA matrices, LAVS projections, TPF MLPs, and the regression head updated.

## Results

On RGBT-GroundBench, RGBT-VGNet (multi-modal RGB+TIR input) achieves the highest Acc@0.5 across all subsets, exceeding 91% on testA (normal-size, normal/strong light), 64% on testB (nighttime, weak/very-weak light), and 49% on testC (small-size targets). Multi-modal RGBT input yields an average ~10% Acc@0.5 improvement over uni-modal (RGB-only or TIR-only) settings across all evaluated models. Zero-shot transfer from RGB-pretrained weights shows an average 30% degradation on RGB and 50% on TIR, confirming the difficulty of cross-spectral grounding without domain adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGBT-VGNet outperforms all uni-modal baselines on the overall test split across all three sub-datasets, with the largest margins on RefM3FD (+7.1% over HiVG-L) and RefMFAD (+3.9% over HiVG-L). On the challenging low-light testB split, RGBT-VGNet leads by a wide margin: 69.22 vs. 60.63 (HiVG-L) on RefFLIR, 80.75 vs. 70.93 (OneRef-L) on RefM3FD, and 64.80 vs. 60.97 (HiVG-L) on RefMFAD, confirming the benefit of thermal cues under illumination degradation. Among uni-modal baselines, AttBalance achieves the highest RefFLIR test score (79.80) but is outperformed by RGBT-VGNet on RefM3FD and RefMFAD. HiVG-L and OneRef-L are the strongest uni-modal competitors on RefM3FD (67.80 and 66.66 respectively), yet both fall short of RGBT-VGNet's 74.89. Transformer-based baselines (TransVG, D-MDETR, MMCA) consistently trail LoRA/CLIP-based methods by 15–25 points, highlighting the advantage of vision-language pretraining for grounding. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that AMA alone accounts for the largest single gain (~18–20 points over the no-module baseline), while LAVS and TPF contribute incremental improvements of 1–3 points on the hardest splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established visual grounding task (RefCOCO, ReferIt, Flickr30K) from RGB-only inputs to a paired RGB+Thermal (TIR) setting, a modality combination previously explored only in detection and segmentation but never in language-guided localization. Architecturally, RGBT-VGNet builds on the CLIP vision encoder with a VL-Transformer decoder (from TransVG) and adopts the hierarchical LoRA design from HiVG, but introduces an asymmetric rank allocation that assigns greater adaptation capacity to the TIR branch to compensate for the RGB bias in pretrained VLP models. The benchmark itself is constructed from existing RGBT detection datasets (FLIR, M³FD, MFAD) that were repurposed with new referring-expression annotations generated via Qwen3VL, rather than being collected from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first large-scale RGBT visual grounding benchmark with fine-grained condition labels (illumination, weather, occlusion, object size), this work enables systematic evaluation of cross-spectral grounding robustness that was previously impossible. The finding that MLLMs fail to benefit consistently from naively adding thermal inputs, and that low-illumination conditions cause a 50% zero-shot degradation on TIR, highlights a significant gap between current multimodal model capabilities and real-world deployment needs. Open questions include whether the illumination-aware fusion strategy generalizes beyond the specific TIR sensor characteristics in the benchmark, whether MLLMs can be fine-tuned to exploit cross-spectral cues effectively, and whether the asymmetric LoRA design transfers to other modality pairs (e.g., RGB+depth, RGB+event).
