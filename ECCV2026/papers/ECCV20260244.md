## Problem

Pretrained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) generalize well in few-shot settings but remain highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, and directly applying adversarial fine-tuning disrupts the pre-trained cross-modal alignment, degrading natural accuracy and generalization to novel classes. Existing robust VLM methods treat adversarial samples uniformly across datasets and classes, ignoring that perturbations induce drastically different levels of semantic destruction and transfer difficulty, making a single fixed adaptation policy suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260244/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed by CLIP ViT-B/16) and class text descriptions as input, producing a class label via image-text similarity scoring. During training, adversarial and inverse-adversarial samples are generated online via 2-step PGD (ε=2/255) on base-class images, and the model is evaluated in a base-to-novel 16-shot protocol where natural samples measure generalization and 100-step PGD or AutoAttack samples measure robustness on unseen novel classes.

## Method

IADA-RVLM combines three components: (1) inverse-adversarial fine-tuning, where the model is jointly trained on adversarial samples (maximizing loss) and inverse-adversarial samples (minimizing loss within a bounded neighborhood to act as semantic anchors), with a KL-divergence constraint aligning the image-text similarity distributions of the two sample types to prevent erroneous semantic drift; (2) a parameter-efficient expertise module that inserts learnable visual prompt tokens into 10 intermediate ViT layers (VPT) to reshape visual feature geometry, combined with a lightweight linear text adapter (TA) that refines class prototypes while keeping the visual encoder frozen; (3) a difficulty-adaptive inference mechanism that quantifies class-level adversarial transfer difficulty via average and nearest-neighbor semantic distances in the text embedding space, then dynamically computes a fusion weight α to interpolate between frozen general text embeddings and TA-adapted embeddings, preserving general semantics for low-difficulty classes while strengthening task-specific expertise for high-difficulty ones.

## Results

Under a 16-shot base-to-novel protocol with 100-step PGD (ε=2/255), IADA-RVLM achieves the best Robust-Natural Harmonic Mean (HM) on all five datasets: 62.27 on Caltech101, 40.63 on OxfordPets, 32.91 on Flowers102, 26.02 on DTD, and 19.02 on EuroSAT. On the high-difficulty DTD dataset, it reaches 20.62% robust accuracy (vs. 17.07% for FAP and 9.97% for AdvMaPLe) while maintaining 35.02% natural accuracy. Under stronger AutoAttack, IADA-RVLM retains the top HM on every dataset, with the largest margins on DTD (19.18% robust vs. 17.85% for FAP) and EuroSAT (11.97% vs. 10.09%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IADA-RVLM outperforms all seven baselines on the Harmonic Mean (HM) metric across all five datasets, confirming a superior robustness–generalization trade-off. Against the strongest competitors FAP and AdvMaPLe, the gains are most pronounced on high-difficulty datasets: on DTD, IADA-RVLM achieves 20.62% robust accuracy vs. 17.07% (FAP) and 9.97% (AdvMaPLe), and on EuroSAT it reaches 13.76% vs. 12.01% (FAP) and 7.83% (AdvMaPLe). On the low-difficulty Caltech101, IADA-RVLM edges out AdvMaPLe (52.52% vs. 52.12% robust) and APT (52.52% vs. 48.98%) while maintaining competitive natural accuracy (76.54% vs. 78.57% for APT). Text-prompt-only methods (AdvPT, APT) and visual-prompt-only methods (C-AVP, TeCoA-VPT) consistently trail the joint multimodal approaches, with AdvPT showing the weakest robust accuracy on Caltech101 (30.62%). The unmodified CLIP baseline is essentially non-functional under adversarial attacks (≤2.80% robust accuracy), highlighting the necessity of adversarial adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inverse-adversarial learning framework of Dong et al. (CVPR 2023; TPAMI 2026) from single-modal adversarial training to the CLIP few-shot adaptation setting, repurposing inverse-adversarial samples as semantic anchors that constrain the optimization trajectory during joint adversarial fine-tuning. It departs from prior robust VLM methods (C-AVP, TeCoA-VPT, AdvPT, APT, AdvMaPLe, FAP) that apply a fixed adaptation policy uniformly across all classes and datasets, by introducing a difficulty-adaptive fusion mechanism that dynamically weights general (frozen CLIP) versus specialized (VPT + text adapter) knowledge based on class-level semantic distances in the text embedding space. The parameter-efficient expertise module also differs from prior work by combining visual prompt tuning with a lightweight text adapter rather than relying on a single modality's prompt, motivated by the observation that VPT structures visual features while TA refines semantic prototypes in complementary ways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The robustness-generalization conflict in VLMs is a practical bottleneck for deploying CLIP-style models in safety-critical, data-scarce domains (medical imaging, remote sensing, traffic signs), and this paper offers a principled mechanism—difficulty-adaptive knowledge fusion—to navigate that trade-off without full re-pretraining. However, the evaluation is limited to CLIP ViT-B/16 on five relatively small datasets, and the inverse-adversarial sample generation adds training overhead that the authors acknowledge but do not fully quantify. Open questions include whether the difficulty-adaptive mechanism generalizes to larger VLM backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H), to more diverse or higher-dimensional class spaces, and whether the semantic-distance proxy for transfer difficulty remains informative under distribution shift or domain gap between base and novel classes.
