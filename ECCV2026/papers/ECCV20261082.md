## Problem

Generative camera trajectory systems produce geometrically valid motions but lack any mechanism to verify whether the resulting shots are visually desirable—characters go off-screen, framing ignores compositional instructions, and aesthetic quality is uncontrolled. The fundamental obstacle is that camera trajectories are purely geometric sequences carrying no visual content, so standard reward-based or preference-based post-training cannot directly evaluate their cinematic quality without a bridge to rendered imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261082/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language cinematographic prompt specifying camera movement, shot scale, shot direction, shot angle, and screen property (following the CCD categorical structure), plus an optional conditioning context such as scene layout or previous keyframe. It outputs a 3D camera trajectory serialized in RealEstate10k-compatible format with continuous focal length control, defined in a local coordinate system relative to the subject so that the same trajectory applies frame-by-frame to both static and animated characters. The trajectory can then be rendered into a preview sequence in Unity or fed into a diffusion-based Camera-to-Video pipeline (Wan 2.2 VACE) for downstream video synthesis.

## Method

VERTIGO bridges trajectory space and visual space by rendering candidate trajectories in a real-time Unity engine into 2D preview sequences, which serve as visual reward signals. A cinematically fine-tuned VLM (Qwen 2.5-VL adapted via LoRA on ShotBench and Unity renders) performs inverse cinematic reasoning to generate a natural-language caption of the realized camera behavior, and a domain-adapted E5 embedding model (contrastive-fine-tuned on LenScript captions) computes cosine similarity between the original prompt and this caption in latent space, yielding a continuous preference score. Preference pairs are constructed by ranking N sampled trajectories per prompt under this caption-based consistency scoring, and the GenDoP-style autoregressive generator is then post-trained with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO, β=0.1), which increases the log-odds of preferred trajectories relative to a frozen reference policy without training an explicit reward model. The authors ablate this scoring choice against tag-consistency counting and RAFT-style scalar regression, finding that the latter two suffer from mode collapse and insufficient discriminative granularity, while caption-space comparison preserves fine-grained distinctions in framing and motion intent.

## Results

VERTIGO achieves near-identical geometric fidelity to GenDoP (FCD 4.22 vs. 4.17; CLaTr-Score 68.40 vs. 67.98) while dramatically reducing the Missing Rate (target off-screen or edge-biased) from 38.7% to 0.8% in Unity renders. On VBench visual metrics, VERTIGO improves Unity-rendered Consistency to 0.908 and Aesthetic Quality to 0.518, and in the downstream Wan 2.2 VACE video-generation setting reaches 0.969 Consistency and 0.735 Aesthetic Quality, both surpassing GenDoP (0.967 / 0.722). User-study best-of-4 preference rates confirm VERTIGO's advantage, with an overall win rate of 52.4%, peaking at 53.9% for instruction adherence and 51.8% for composition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GenDoP (the closest baseline and the base model VERTIGO post-trains), VERTIGO essentially ties on all six CLaTr geometric metrics (FCD 4.22 vs. 4.17; CS 68.40 vs. 67.98) while cutting the Missing Rate by a factor of ~48× (0.008 vs. 0.387), showing that DPO post-training preserves trajectory distributional fidelity while fixing framing failures. On VBench visual metrics, VERTIGO edges out GenDoP on every visual dimension: Unity Consistency 0.908 vs. 0.904, Aesthetic 0.518 vs. 0.515, and video-generation Consistency 0.969 vs. 0.967, Aesthetic 0.735 vs. 0.722. Against DIRECTOR, VERTIGO dominates across the board—FCD is 6.2× lower (4.22 vs. 26.28), Missing Rate is 100× lower (0.008 vs. 0.803), and video Aesthetic is 2.5× higher (0.735 vs. 0.293). CCD is the weakest baseline on every metric (FCD 104.60, MisR 0.999, video Aes 0.296), confirming that diffusion-based trajectory generation without preference alignment produces both geometrically and visually inferior results. In the user study, VERTIGO is preferred over all three baselines across composition, consistency, prompt adherence, and aesthetic quality in both Unity-rendered and VACE-transferred video settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

VERTIGO builds directly on GenDoP's autoregressive camera trajectory generator and applies DPO post-training (originally from LLM alignment) to a domain where it had not been used: geometric camera path generation. The key conceptual departure from prior camera generation work (CCD, DIRECTOR, GenDoP) is the introduction of a render-in-the-loop visual feedback mechanism, where a real-time graphics engine materializes trajectories into 2D previews that a cinematically fine-tuned VLM scores via caption-based consistency. This bridges the gap between trajectory-space optimization and visual-quality assessment, drawing on cycle-consistency reward ideas from image-text alignment (Bahng et al., 2025) and VLM-based evaluation paradigms from video understanding (ShotBench, VEU-Bench).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VERTIGO is the first framework to explicitly close the loop between geometric camera generation and the visual outcome of the resulting shot, addressing a practical pain point (off-screen subjects, poor framing) that purely geometric methods cannot resolve. The dramatic reduction in off-screen rate (38% → 0.8%) while preserving geometric fidelity demonstrates that visual preference signals are complementary to, not redundant with, trajectory-level training. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to composite multi-shot sequences and long-take cinematography (the paper only covers basic motion patterns), whether VLM perception errors become a bottleneck at scale, and whether the render-in-the-loop paradigm transfers to other geometric generation tasks beyond cameras.
