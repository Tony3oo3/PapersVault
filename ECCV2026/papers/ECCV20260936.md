## Problem

Robust 3D perception for self-driving depends on large-scale manual annotation, a process that becomes infeasible as deployment expands across diverse cities with varying traffic distributions. This paper asks whether the city's own roadside infrastructure (RSUs) can serve as distributed, unsupervised teachers that provide supervision to ego vehicles without any human labels, fundamentally shifting where training signals originate rather than merely reducing annotation cost within the ego-centric paradigm.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes LiDAR point clouds (128-beam for RSUs, 64-beam for ego) along with RGB and depth camera inputs. RSU predictions are transformed into ego-centric coordinates via known rigid transformations, filtered for distance and point density, and fused across overlapping RSU coverage using distance-weighted NMS (IoU threshold 0.1). The final output is a standalone ego 3D detector producing class-agnostic bounding boxes (Car, Pedestrian, Cyclist) evaluated in BEV at IoU 0.5/0.3, requiring no infrastructure or communication at test time.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully label-free three-stage pipeline exploiting RSU stationarity. In Stage 1, each stationary RSU generates pseudo-labels by computing persistence point scores (PP score, from MODEST) over temporally equidistant segments to separate static background from dynamic objects, clustering transient points with DBSCAN, and refining boxes via trajectory tracking (from Oyster) that selects the most reliable frame per object. In Stage 2, trained RSUs broadcast their predictions to passing ego vehicles within a 160 m range; predictions are transformed to ego coordinates, filtered, and aggregated with distance-weighted NMS that prioritizes closer RSUs. In Stage 3, a standard LiDAR detector (CenterPoint or PointPillars) is trained offline on the aggregated pseudo-labels, distilling multi-RSU knowledge into a single ego-only model. The key differentiator is that supervision originates from distributed, self-learning infrastructure rather than the ego vehicle's own observations, and the resulting model is fully standalone at deployment.

## Results

On the CIVET dataset (Town 10, urban), the fully label-free pipeline with CenterPoint achieves 82.3% AP for cars, 79.3% for pedestrians, and 68.5% for cyclists (76.7% average), compared to a fully supervised ego upper bound of 94.4%/91.8%/96.6% (94.3% average). With PointPillars, the best label-free configuration reaches 78.6% average AP versus a 92.0% supervised upper bound. Scaling to four towns with 48 RSUs, CenterPoint achieves 80.7% average AP (82.7% cars) against a 92.3% upper bound, confirming geographic scalability. In the complementarity study (Table 5), infrastructure-taught pseudo-labels alone yield 62.0 dynamic-object AP, outperforming Oyster (39.8) and MODEST (18.0), and combining with Oyster further improves to 64.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its infrastructure-taught pseudo-labels against two representative ego-centric unsupervised methods (MODEST and Oyster) in a controlled setting where self-training is disabled (Table 5). Infrastructure-taught pseudo-labels alone achieve 62.0 dynamic-object AP, substantially outperforming Oyster (39.8, +22.2 AP) and MODEST (18.0, +44.0 AP). Combining Oyster's ego-centric pseudo-labels with the infrastructure-generated supervision yields 64.9 AP, an additional ~3 AP over either source alone, demonstrating complementarity. Under simulated communication noise, the infrastructure-taught method drops to 49.9 AP, but the combined Oyster+Ours configuration retains 59.5 AP, still exceeding Oyster alone (39.8). The primary comparison is against the fully supervised ego upper bound (94.3% average AP with CenterPoint), where the label-free pipeline recovers ~81% of the gap (76.7% vs. 94.3%), with the largest remaining gap on cyclists (68.5% vs. 96.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the collaborative perception (V2X) literature, which performs online multi-agent fusion at inference time and requires labeled training data, by using infrastructure exclusively during training to distill supervision into a standalone ego model. For the unsupervised RSU training stage, it builds directly on MODEST's persistence-point-score for dynamic object discovery and Oyster's tracking-based pseudo-label refinement, repurposing these ego-centric techniques for a stationary infrastructure viewpoint. The paper positions itself as complementary to existing ego-centric label-free methods (MODEST, Oyster) rather than a replacement, introducing a fundamentally new supervision source—distributed, self-learning roadside units—rather than a new architecture within the standard ego-only regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm of city infrastructure as distributed, label-free teachers is genuinely new and, if validated in real deployments, could dramatically reduce the annotation bottleneck that currently limits 3D perception generalization across geographies. Key limitations temper the impact: all experiments are in CARLA simulation, the pipeline handles only dynamic objects with LiDAR, and the ~12 AP gap to the supervised upper bound (and the severe pedestrian degradation under communication noise) indicate substantial room for improvement. Open questions include real-world validation as connected infrastructure scales, extension to static object categories, multimodal integration, and whether the simple distance-weighted NMS aggregation can be replaced by learned fusion to close the remaining gap.
