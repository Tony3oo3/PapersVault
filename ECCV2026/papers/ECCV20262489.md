## Problem

Path-based attribution methods like Integrated Gradients assume a conservative gradient field, but modern rectified DNNs have highly discontinuous gradients at piecewise-linear region boundaries, making attributions path-dependent and the standard straight-line path prone to accumulating noisy or spurious gradients. Existing alternatives (GIG, IG², AGI) rely on greedy or local search heuristics that cannot identify globally consistent integration paths, and none offer inference-time controllability over path properties such as faithfulness or sparsity.

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image and a baseline image (typically black), both are encoded into a pre-trained β-VAE latent space where the diffusion model operates, avoiding the computational cost of pixel-space path generation. At inference, the user specifies guidance coefficients (λ_faith, λ_comp) that steer path generation toward desired faithfulness and sparsity trade-offs without retraining. The output is a pixel-space attribution map computed via the path integral along the generated non-linear trajectory, optionally aggregated over N stochastically sampled paths for reduced variance.

## Method

DiffIG reformulates attribution path generation as a conditional generative modeling problem: it trains an unconditional ε-prediction diffusion model on a diverse dataset of non-linear paths synthesized via the Stick-Breaking Process (varying the concentration parameter α to span nearly-linear to highly complex trajectories), all in β-VAE latent space. A separate regression network is trained to predict composite objective scores (faithfulness via insertion/deletion confidence gap, and complexity via attribution entropy) from noisily perturbed path samples at any diffusion timestep. At inference, classifier guidance injects the gradient of this score predictor into the reverse diffusion mean, steering the denoising trajectory toward high-quality paths while boundary conditions γ(0)=z′ and γ(1)=z are enforced at every step. Multiple paths can be sampled stochastically and combined via Best-of-N selection or mean/median aggregation, and the entire procedure inherits IG's axiomatic guarantees (Completeness, Sensitivity, Implementation Invariance) for any generated path.

## Results

DiffIG achieves the best DiffID score in 17 of 18 dataset–architecture combinations, with the largest margins on Oxford-IIIT Pet: 0.6368 (VGG16) vs. AGI's 0.4930, and 0.5067 (ResNet18) vs. GIG's 0.3486. On Mini-ImageNet, DiffIG leads with 0.4298 DiffID (VGG16) and 0.3126 (ResNet18), outperforming the strongest baseline AGI by 0.007 and 0.018 respectively. Insertion AUC improves by 5–15 percentage points over all baselines across architectures, while Deletion AUC remains competitive (lower is better).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffIG outperforms all seven baselines on DiffID in 17 of 18 dataset–architecture cells, with the sole exception being Mini-ImageNet/InceptionV1 where it trails AGI by a negligible 0.0001 (0.3578 vs. 0.3579). The strongest baseline is AGI (adversarial gradient integration), which DiffIG surpasses by 0.144 on Oxford-IIIT Pet/VGG16 and 0.007 on Mini-ImageNet/VGG16. GIG (guided integrated gradients) is the second-strongest baseline on Oxford-IIIT Pet but is outperformed by DiffIG by 0.158 on ResNet18 and 0.151 on VGG16. Standard IG (straight-line path) is consistently among the weakest methods, with DiffIG improving DiffID by 0.185–0.321 over IG depending on the configuration. Manifold-based methods (EIG, MIG) and the stochastic SPI are uniformly outperformed, with DiffIG leading by 0.12–0.37 in DiffID. On Mini-ImageNet, where the task is harder (100 classes), margins shrink but DiffIG still leads every architecture on both DiffID and Insertion AUC.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffIG extends the path-based attribution lineage (IG, GIG, IG², AGI, SPI, EIG, MIG) by reframing integration path selection as a conditional generative modeling problem rather than an optimization or fixed stochastic process. It draws directly from the offline reinforcement learning planning literature (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser), where diffusion models synthesize high-return trajectories via classifier guidance, and repurposes this paradigm for attribution path generation. The Stick-Breaking Process from SPI provides the training data distribution, while the β-VAE latent space (following EIG/MIG) addresses the dimensionality of image-space paths. The key departure from prior work is the shift from greedy/local search (GIG, IG², AGI) or model-agnostic stochastic sampling (SPI) to a learned, globally consistent, and inference-time controllable generative process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffIG transforms path-based attribution from a fixed heuristic into a flexible, user-controllable generative process, enabling practitioners to trade off faithfulness and sparsity at inference time without retraining. The substantial quantitative gains over all seven baselines (e.g., ~30% DiffID improvement over the best prior method on Oxford-IIIT Pet) suggest that the path-dependence problem in rectified DNNs is more severe than previously addressed, and that learned non-linear paths capture decision-surface geometry that straight-line or greedy paths miss. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets and higher-resolution images, whether adaptive or learned baselines can resolve the dark-region limitation, and whether the generative framing extends to non-visual modalities such as text or tabular data.
