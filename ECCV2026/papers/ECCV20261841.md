## Problem

Existing multimodal OOD detection methods treat all modalities equally despite their varying detection performance (e.g., optical flow substantially underperforms video), and they fail to exploit the uncertainty-aware knowledge encoded at the logit level of unimodal experts. These methods also rely on complex retraining strategies to enhance embedding spaces while overlooking the intrinsic OOD detection capability of the unimodal model ensemble itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261841/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal inputs (video clips, optical flow, and optionally audio) processed by frozen pre-trained SlowFast backbones, and produces both a class label and an OOD detection score compatible with seven post-hoc scoring functions (MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, GEN, ReAct, ASH, VIM). Crucially, only the final joint classifier operating on concatenated modality embeddings is retrained (10 epochs, batch size 16), while all unimodal feature extractors remain frozen, making the framework a lightweight plug-in. Modality-specific teacher weights are computed stochastically per mini-batch from each model's cross-entropy loss on the training data.

## Method

The proposed self multimodal OOD distillation framework trains a joint classifier (student) in the shared embedding space of all modalities, guided by unimodal experts (teachers) whose logit-level predictions serve as uncertainty-aware soft targets. Predictive uncertainty is estimated via a likelihood-weighted ensemble (Eq. 3–4), where each modality's weight is inversely proportional to its cross-entropy loss, automatically down-weighting underperforming modalities. The KL divergence between the weighted teacher distribution and the student output is decomposed into target-class KD (TCKD) and non-target-class KD (NCKD) components, with hyperparameters α and β controlling their relative importance—TCKD dominates for near-OOD detection while NCKD dominates for far-OOD. The final training objective combines standard cross-entropy with these two decoupled distillation terms, requiring no retraining of backbone networks and integrating as a plug-in atop existing SOTA multimodal OOD methods (A2D, DPU, Feature Mixing) to yield up to 30% FPR95 improvements.

## Results

On the multimodal far-OOD detection task (HMDB51 as ID), the best configuration (DPU+Ours) achieves an average FPR95 of 18.53 and average AUROC of 95.41 across four OOD datasets (UCF101, HAC, EPIC-Kitchens, Kinetics-600), with ID accuracy of 87.34%. On the near-OOD task, DPU+Ours achieves the best FPR95 of 34.42 on HMDB51 25/26, 7.57 on UCF101 50/51, 63.81 on EPIC-Kitchens 4/4, and 61.59 on Kinetics-600 129/100. The method yields FPR95 reductions of up to 30% over the vanilla baseline and up to 31% when applied on top of the AN backbone on UCF101, while maintaining or improving ID classification accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions its method as a plug-in distillation framework applied on top of three existing multimodal OOD detection backbones: A2D/AN (Dong et al., NeurIPS 2024), DPU (Li et al., CVPR 2025), and Feature Mixing (Liu et al., 2025). When applied to DPU, the method achieves the strongest far-OOD performance with an average FPR95 of 18.53 and AUROC of 95.41, outperforming A2D+Ours (FPR95 20.58, AUROC 94.44) and FM+Ours (FPR95 20.87, AUROC 94.12). The most dramatic individual improvement is on EPIC-Kitchens, where DPU+Ours reduces FPR95 to 4.33 versus 12.43 for A2D+Ours and 9.01 for FM+Ours. On the near-OOD task, DPU+Ours similarly leads with FPR95 of 34.42 (HMDB51) and 61.59 (Kinetics-600), beating A2D+Ours (38.34, 63.04) and FM+Ours (45.10, 64.10). Notably, even the vanilla Base+Ours configuration (no retraining backbone) achieves competitive or superior results to the more complex retraining-based SOTA methods, demonstrating that the distillation framework alone captures much of the benefit that those methods obtain through elaborate training strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation lineage (Hinton et al. [12], decoupled KD from Zhao et al. [41]) into the multimodal OOD detection setting, building on the self-distillation-for-OOD idea of Yang et al. [38] but generalizing it from single to multiple modalities. It departs from prior multimodal OOD methods (A2D [4], DPU [18], Feature Mixing [19]) that rely on complex backbone retraining to shape embedding spaces, instead treating pre-trained unimodal experts as frozen teachers whose logit-level dark knowledge guides a lightweight joint classifier. The ensemble-weighting mechanism draws on the Bayesian predictive uncertainty framework of Lakshminarayanan et al. [16], repurposing likelihood-weighted model averaging to down-weight underperforming modalities during distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in nature of the framework—requiring only 10 epochs of classifier retraining without touching expensive backbone models—makes it practically attractive for deploying multimodal OOD detection on top of existing model ensembles. The decomposition showing TCKD primarily benefits near-OOD while NCKD primarily benefits far-OOD provides actionable guidance for practitioners who can tune α/β based on their deployment scenario. However, the evaluation is confined to action recognition (video/flow/audio), leaving open whether the approach transfers to other multimodal settings such as text+image or sensor fusion, and whether the gains persist when modalities are more balanced in quality.
