## Problem

Deploying 3D point cloud models in privacy-sensitive, resource-constrained settings faces two coupled barriers—data cannot be centralized for training, and the trained model must run on limited edge hardware—that have not been studied together in a single benchmark. No prior work systematically evaluates the full cross-product of federated learning algorithms and knowledge distillation objectives for point cloud classification, and the standard end-to-end evaluation of the combined FL-KD pipeline can mask federated failure by reusing the very labels whose privacy motivated federation.

## Input / Output

Each sample is a 3D point cloud of 1,024 points with three spatial coordinates and no surface normals (tensor shape 3×1024), classified into 40 shape categories (ModelNet40) or 4 craniosynostosis pathologies (clinical dataset). The federated teacher is a PointNet++ SSG (5.65 MB) and the distilled student is a compact SmallPointNet2 (1.44 MB, 74.51% smaller) with roughly 50% lower inference latency. The FL setting partitions training data across 5 clients using a deterministic label-skew non-IID strategy, and all configurations are repeated over three random seeds.

## Method

The benchmark implements a two-stage pipeline: Stage 1 trains a PointNet++ SSG teacher via one of thirteen FL aggregation algorithms (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedNova, FedDyn, MOON, Ditto, FedMedian, FedBN, and four server-side optimizers) over 20 communication rounds with 5 local epochs per round; Stage 2 distills the best teacher checkpoint into a compact student using one of ten KD objectives spanning logit-matching, feature/attention alignment, relational, and self-distillation families. The central technical finding is that the weight on the hard-label cross-entropy term in the distillation loss determines whether the student reflects the federated teacher: objectives retaining a positive CE coefficient recover to near-centralized accuracy from any teacher (e.g., a collapsed 8.50% SCAFFOLD teacher still yields a 92.94% student), while the four pure-transfer objectives with zero CE weight track teacher quality almost perfectly (r ≈ 0.99) and collapse when the teacher does. The paper recommends evaluating FL-KD pipelines with label-free distillation (removing the cross-entropy term or using an unlabeled proxy split) so that reported accuracy genuinely measures the federated model rather than the proxy labels.

## Results

Under extreme label-skew non-IID partitioning (5 clients, 20 rounds), the strongest FL method (FedNova) reaches only 76.32% instance accuracy on ModelNet40 versus a 92.26% centralized reference, and FedProx leads at 75.83% on the clinical craniosynostosis dataset versus a 100% centralized ceiling; the four server-side optimizers (FedAvgM, FedAdam, FedYogi, FedAdagrad) collapse to near-chance accuracy (4.05–6.28% on ModelNet40, 25.00–26.25% on clinical). Knowledge distillation compresses the PointNet++ SSG teacher (5.65 MB) into a 1.44 MB student (74.51% smaller, ~50% lower inference latency), with five of seven objectives matching or surpassing the 92.44% teacher on ModelNet40. The combined FL-KD pipeline exposes an evaluation pitfall: a collapsed SCAFFOLD teacher at 8.50% paired with Logit-MSE still yields a 92.94% student (an 84.4-point gap), because the hard-label cross-entropy term in the distillation loss reuses proxy labels rather than measuring teacher quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the thirteen FL algorithms, no single method is robust across both datasets: FedNova leads on ModelNet40 (76.32%) but drops to mid-pack (50.83%) on the clinical data, where FedProx leads (75.83%), while SCAFFOLD falls from 64.26% to 36.67% and Ditto/MOON rise into the upper group. All thirteen FL methods trail the centralized baseline by 15.94–88.21 points on ModelNet40 and 24.17–75.00 points on the clinical data, with the four adaptive server-side optimizers (FedAdam, FedYogi, FedAdagrad, FedAvgM) collapsing to near-chance accuracy on both. For knowledge distillation from a strong centralized teacher, five of seven objectives (Attention Transfer 92.74%, Basic KD 92.69%, Vanilla KD 92.61%, Self-Distillation 92.61%, Feature KD 92.57%) match or surpass the 92.44% teacher, while Logit-MSE lags at 87.07% due to scale-sensitive MSE unbalancing the loss. In the combined pipeline, the critical comparison is between hard-label KD objectives (Vanilla KD, Basic KD, Self-Distillation, Logit-MSE, Cosine, CRD, DKD) and pure-transfer objectives (Feature KD, Attention Transfer, RKD, SP): the former recover the student to near-centralized accuracy regardless of teacher quality (r ≈ 0, range ≤ 7.5 points across 13 teachers on clinical data), while the latter track the teacher almost perfectly (r = 0.986–0.990, range 57.5–58.3 points), confirming that hard-label distillation masks federated failure rather than measuring it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by systematically benchmarking the full cross-product of thirteen federated learning algorithms and ten knowledge distillation objectives for 3D point cloud classification, a combination that prior surveys [36] identified as a critical gap. Individual components are well established—FL aggregation strategies (FedAvg through Ditto) are documented for image classification, and KD objectives (Hinton-style logit matching through relational and contrastive transfer) are mature for 2D vision—but neither has been characterized for hierarchical point cloud architectures under non-IID federation, and no prior work evaluates their two-stage pipeline jointly. The paper's conceptual departure is the diagnosis that the standard end-to-end evaluation of FL-KD pipelines is confounded by the hard-label cross-entropy term, a pitfall not previously isolated in the FL+KD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The recovery illusion—where a collapsed federated teacher at 8.50% yields a 92.94% student via a hard-label KD objective—provides a concrete deployment warning: reported student accuracy in FL-KD pipelines may measure proxy labels rather than the federated model, while silently reusing the very patient data that motivated federation. The finding that no single FL algorithm is robust across datasets and that server-side adaptive optimizers collapse to near-chance under label skew further complicates practical deployment. Open questions include whether transformer or kernel-point backbones degrade less under non-IID federation, whether softer heterogeneity regimes narrow the gap, and whether the label-free evaluation protocol generalizes to segmentation or other 3D tasks beyond classification.
