## Problem

Existing audio-visual self-supervised methods rely on separate modality-specific encoders combined with complex mixtures of contrastive, reconstruction, and distillation objectives, limiting cross-modal synergy and scalability. A natural simplification—sharing a single encoder across modalities—actually degrades both modalities below their unimodal baselines without an explicit alignment mechanism. The paper asks whether a single, unified encoder trained with only a JEPA predictive objective can learn strong audio-visual representations, and what is needed to make parameter sharing beneficial rather than harmful.

## Input / Output

The shared encoder consumes three input modes: audio spectrograms (1024×128 log-mel, tokenized by 2D conv), video frames (224×224 or 256×256, tokenized by 3D conv into tubelets), and concatenated audio-video tokens. Modality-specific positional and modality embeddings disambiguate the shared embedding space. At evaluation, frozen encoder features are passed to a lightweight attentive probe (learnable query + 4 transformer blocks + linear head) that produces multi-label sound event scores (AS20K, FSD50K), single-label environmental sound scores (ESC-50), or video action class scores (K400, SSv2). Cross-modal retrieval uses L1 distance on pre-final-layer-norm pooled features.

## Method

MJEPA trains a single ViT encoder (300M ViT-L or 1B ViT-g) with nine unweighted JEPA losses: three intra-modal masked-prediction losses (a→a, v→v, av→av) using multi-level features from intermediate layers fused through a narrow predictor, and six cross-modal prediction losses (a↔v, a↔av, v↔av) using lightweight 3-layer MLPs on mean-pooled last-layer features. The critical design insight is that cross-modal prediction—predicting one modality's pooled representation from the other—is the ingredient that transforms a naively shared encoder (which degrades below unimodal baselines) into one where each modality positively transfers to the other. An EMA target encoder with stop-gradient prevents representation collapse, and the architecture scales naturally to heterogeneous data by simply feeding video-only samples through the v→v loss without architectural changes. This enables training on paired AudioSet-2M plus ~2M video-only clips from VideoMix2M, with a 5× scaling factor on the video-only loss to balance gradients across data sources.

## Results

On AudioSet-20K, MJEPA ViT-g with data scaling achieves 40.97 mAP (audio), 29.82 mAP (video), and 45.44 mAP (audio-video) under frozen evaluation, surpassing the best prior frozen baseline (EquiAV) by +6.7, +11.2, and +6.8 mAP respectively. On audio benchmarks, frozen MJEPA ViT-g reaches 96.9% on ESC-50 and 65.8 mAP on FSD50K, exceeding fully finetuned baselines. On video benchmarks, MJEPA ViT-g with data scaling achieves 85.0% top-1 on Kinetics-400 and 73.9% on SSv2, nearly matching VJEPA2 ViT-g (86.6%/75.3%) despite using approximately 10× less video training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MJEPA decisively outperforms all prior frozen audio-visual baselines on AudioSet-20K: the base ViT-L already beats EquiAV [27] by +4.6/+6.8/+4.3 mAP on A/V/A-V, and the scaled ViT-g narrows the gap to EquiAV's finetuned A-V score (45.44 vs 46.60) while its frozen video-only score (29.82) exceeds EquiAV's finetuned video (25.70). On audio benchmarks, MJEPA ViT-g frozen (96.9% ESC-50, 65.8 FSD50K) surpasses the best finetuned results from XKD [37] (96.5%, 58.5%) and EquiAV [27] (96.0%, 62.6%), and also outperforms large audio-only models like SPEAR [40] and Dasheng-1.2B [11] that use 40–50× more pre-training data. On video benchmarks, MJEPA ViT-L + data scaling (84.7% K400, 73.3% SSv2) nearly matches VJEPA2 ViT-L [5] (85.1%, 73.7%) trained on ~10× more video, and the ViT-g variant (85.0%, 73.9%) trails VJEPA2 ViT-g [5] (86.6%, 75.3%) by only 1.6/1.4 points. MJEPA also outperforms CAV-MAE [19], CAV-MAE Sync [4], and A-JEPA [13] by wide margins across all audio and audio-visual settings. The one setting where a baseline retains an edge is finetuned audio-only on AS20K, where MAViL [23] (41.80) and EquiAV [27] (42.40) slightly exceed MJEPA's frozen 40.97, though MJEPA's frozen video-only score still surpasses both.

## Relation to Prior Work

MJEPA extends the JEPA paradigm (LeCun 2022; I-JEPA, V-JEPA, A-JEPA) from unimodal to multimodal audio-visual learning by introducing a single shared encoder and a cross-modal prediction objective. It deliberately departs from prior AV-SSL methods (CAV-MAE, MAViL, EquiAV, XKD) that combine separate modality-specific encoders with mixtures of contrastive losses, masked reconstruction, and heavy augmentations, replacing all of these with a single predictive loss applied within and across modalities. The video-side architecture and masking strategy are inherited from V-JEPA/V-JEPA2, while the audio-side tokenization follows AST, but the unified shared-encoder design and the cross-modal alignment loss are new contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single JEPA objective with a shared encoder is sufficient to learn audio-visual representations that match or exceed complex multi-component methods, offering a simpler and more scalable training recipe. The finding that video-only data improves audio representations (and vice versa) through cross-modal prediction suggests a general principle for leveraging heterogeneous unimodal data in multimodal pre-training. Open questions include whether more expressive cross-modal predictors (beyond mean-pooled MLPs) could yield richer alignment, and whether the framework extends naturally to other modality pairs (medical imaging, robotics) or to settings with missing modalities at test time.
