## Problem

Reconstructing full-body human-object interactions from sparse egocentric wearable signals (e.g., smart glasses and wristbands) remains largely unsolved. Existing HOI methods require RGB cameras, pre-scanned scenes, dense IMU suits, or instrumented objects, and none jointly model human pose, object motion, and contact dynamics from just head-and-hand tracking while remaining robust to intermittent sensor data and arbitrary sequence lengths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260367/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ECHO takes per-frame 3-point tracking (head and both hands' positions and orientations), an object class label, and the object's canonical mesh (encoded via PointNext into a 1024-d feature vector) as mandatory inputs, with optional partial observations of human pose, object trajectory, or contact at any sparsity level. Outputs are a full-body SMPL-X pose sequence (21 joints in axis-angle, R^{21×6} per frame), an object SE(3) trajectory (R^6 rotation + R^3 translation per frame in a per-frame head-centric frame), and a continuous contact vector in [0,1] covering both human-object and human-ground contacts. The per-frame head-centric representation (rather than sequence-level canonicalization) allows inference on arbitrary chunks without re-canonicalizing each window.

## Method

ECHO is built on a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with rotary positional embeddings, adapted to a tri-variate diffusion process where human motion, object trajectory, and contact each follow independent noise schedules. This independence is the key architectural insight: by setting a modality's noise level to zero, the model treats it as a clean condition and predicts the remaining modalities, enabling flexible cross-modal conditioning and joint training on motion-only data (AMASS) alongside HOI data (BEHAVE, OMOMO) within a single 57.7M-parameter model. During training, the model samples from all 2³ noisy/clean combinations per modality with synchronized noise levels across diffused modalities, and uses learnable placeholder tokens for object conditioning on AMASS samples to signal 'no interaction.' For long-sequence inference, ECHO introduces smooth inpainting that blends past and current predictions via weighted averaging in the overlap region at every diffusion step (extending HMD²'s standard inpainting), combined with classifier-free guidance on a contact-consistency loss to enforce physically plausible interactions.

## Results

On BEHAVE, ECHO achieves 6.8 cm MPJPE and 7.5 cm MPJVE for human motion, and 33.5 cm vertex-to-vertex error (E_v2v) and 20.1 cm center error (E_c) for object tracking, outperforming both baselines across all metrics. On OMOMO, ECHO reaches 6.0 cm MPJPE, 6.1 cm MPJVE, 26.5 cm E_v2v, and 15.2 cm E_c, again leading on every metric. On the AMASS motion-only benchmark, ECHO achieves 7.4 cm MPJPE and 8.6 cm MPJVE, surpassing EgoAllo+H+O (8.9/11.6) and BoDiffusion+O (11.4/14.3), confirming that the joint HOI formulation learns a strong human motion prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECHO is compared against two baselines extended for HOI: BoDiffusion+O (a DiT-based diffusion model with object tokens appended) and EgoAllo+H+O (the EgoAllo egocentric motion model extended with hand conditioning and object pose prediction). ECHO decisively outperforms BoDiffusion+O on every metric across both BEHAVE and OMOMO, with the largest gaps in object tracking (e.g., 33.5 vs. 44.2 cm E_v2v on BEHAVE, a 24% reduction). Against the stronger EgoAllo+H+O baseline, ECHO wins on all object metrics (E_v2v, E_c, rotation difference, and contact accuracy) while matching or slightly exceeding it on human motion (6.8 vs. 7.6 cm MPJPE on BEHAVE; 6.0 vs. 6.6 cm on OMOMO), though EgoAllo+H+O retains a marginal edge on foot contact score on OMOMO (0.95 vs. 0.93). On the AMASS motion-only benchmark, ECHO achieves 7.4 cm MPJPE versus 8.9 for EgoAllo+H+O and 11.4 for BoDiffusion+O, demonstrating that the tri-variate formulation with AMASS co-training yields a superior human motion prior without sacrificing HOI quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECHO extends the tri-variate diffusion formulation of TriDi [77] from static HOI poses to arbitrary-length motion sequences, and crucially trains jointly on large-scale human-only motion (AMASS) alongside HOI data, something TriDi and other HOI methods (HOI-Diff, InterDreamer) do not do. The egocentric conditioning builds directly on EgoAllo [113] and HMD² [26], extending their head-only tracking to include hand tracking and object/interaction modalities, and upgrading HMD²'s inpainting inference with smooth blending. The architecture is a DiT [74] backbone with rotary positional embeddings, adapted to a multi-modal token layout where each modality (human, object, contact) carries its own independent denoising schedule—a design departure from standard single-schedule diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECHO demonstrates that full-body HOI reconstruction is feasible from just three tracked points (head + two wrists), a sensor budget compatible with consumer smart glasses and watches, opening a path toward practical egocentric interaction understanding in healthcare, robotics, and spatial AI. The joint-training recipe (AMASS for motion prior + BEHAVE/OMOMO for interaction dynamics) and the independent-schedule tri-variate diffusion are reusable components for future multi-modal generative models. Key open questions remain: the absence of finger-level tracking limits dexterous manipulation, dynamic scene constraints are not modeled, and no visual (RGB) conditioning is supported—addressing these would be necessary for comprehensive real-world egocentric perception.
