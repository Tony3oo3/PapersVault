## Problem

Existing efficient inference methods for MLLMs address token pruning and layer/head skipping in strict isolation, overlooking the fundamental coupling between visual token density and LLM architectural capacity. Isolated token pruning discards semantically critical tokens without awareness of downstream reasoning needs, while isolated layer skipping assumes full-length sequences and cannot adapt when prior pruning has already simplified the context. This decoupled approach prevents globally optimal allocation of a fixed compute budget across sequence length, depth, and width.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image (encoded by a frozen ViT into N=576 patch tokens), a text prompt, and a continuous scalar compute budget b ∈ [b_min, 1] expressed as a fraction of full-model prefill FLOPs. The budget is injected as a learned token into both the vision encoder and the LLM prompt, conditioning all allocation decisions. The output is a standard autoregressive text answer, but the internal computation path—how many visual tokens survive, which transformer layers execute, and which head groups activate—varies per input instance and per budget setting, with RoPE handling the compressed token sequence natively.

## Method

SMARTVL introduces two lightweight controllers linked by a shared budget encoding: a vision-side token controller that applies per-token Gumbel-sigmoid selection (conditioned on budget-fused ViT features) to determine sequence length, and an LLM-side compute controller that applies independent Gumbel-sigmoid gating to decide which layers execute and which head groups activate within each layer. The two stages are coupled through a cross-stage token-count embedding κ (the normalized survival rate from the vision controller, stop-gradient detached to stabilize joint optimization) that explicitly conditions the LLM compute decisions on the actual retained token count. Training uses a differentiable FLOPs estimator to create an end-to-end gradient path for the budget constraint, combined with an asymmetric budget violation loss (quadratic penalty for overshooting, linear for under-utilization) and a linear warmup schedule to prevent premature structural collapse. The key insight is that the optimal allocation between visual context retention and LLM reasoning depth is content-dependent and task-dependent, so joint end-to-end optimization across all three dimensions yields strictly better Pareto frontiers than any single-dimension method or their naive sequential composition.

## Results

On VQAv2 at 50% prefill FLOPs, SMARTVL achieves 74.4% accuracy versus 67.9% for AdaLLaVA and 74.5% for the composed AdaLLaVA-PruMerge baseline, while matching the full-model LLaVA 1.5 (76.5%) at 100% budget. The advantage is most pronounced on text-centric and real-world tasks: at 50% FLOPs, SMARTVL reaches 54.4% on TextVQA and 55.1% on VizWiz, compared to 29.8% and 34.3% for AdaLLaVA respectively. On the 13B LLaVA backbone, SMARTVL attains 75.45% at 7.25T FLOPs versus 72.27% for AdaLLaVA at 8.27T, and 78.82% at full budget versus 77.85%. Across all seven benchmarks, SMARTVL delivers an average 7.8% accuracy gain over AdaLLaVA at ~50% compute budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdaLLaVA (compute-only adaptation), SMARTVL wins decisively on VQAv2 (74.4% vs. 67.9% at 50% FLOPs, a 6.5-point gap), TextVQA (54.4% vs. 29.8%), and VizWiz (55.1% vs. 34.3%), while slightly trailing on MMBench (62.0% vs. 63.3%) and GQA (59.8% vs. 56.8%—actually a win). Against the composed AdaLLaVA-PruMerge baseline (sequential, non-joint token+compute), SMARTVL matches or exceeds accuracy on VQAv2 (74.4% vs. 74.5%) and TextVQA (54.4% vs. 51.3%) while offering continuous budget adaptation rather than discrete manual configurations. Against token-only baselines FastV and LLaVA-PruMerge+, SMARTVL's token controller alone (with compute controller disabled) achieves a favorable Pareto frontier across the full FLOPs range, outperforming both in the low-FLOPs regime and preserving accuracy better in the high-FLOPs regime. Against the full-model LLaVA 1.5 reference, SMARTVL matches the upper-bound accuracy (76.5% on VQAv2) at 100% budget while delivering substantial gains at reduced budgets, confirming that the adaptive framework does not sacrifice peak performance.
