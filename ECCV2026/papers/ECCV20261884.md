limitations: The paper does not include an explicit limitations section. ## Problem

Adapting MLLMs into universal multimodal embedding models forces diverse semantic objectives (classification, VQA, retrieval, grounding) into a single shared parameter space, causing severe gradient interference that degrades performance across all tasks. The paper quantifies this task conflict: the jointly trained VLM2VEC model drops 15.1% on VQA (2B) and 13.1% (7B) relative to task-specific oracles, and data-abundant tasks hijack the shared adapter, suppressing minority objectives.

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous multimodal inputs—standalone images, standalone text, or image-text pairs—processed through a Qwen2-VL backbone (2B or 7B). Images are resized to a minimum resolution of 401,408 pixels before tokenization. The output is a single d-dimensional embedding vector extracted from the hidden state of the final [EOS] token at the last layer, used for contrastive retrieval across all four meta-task categories.

## Method

TSEmbed replaces the monolithic LoRA adapter with a Mixture-of-Experts variant (MoE-LoRA) where N=4 experts, each a rank-16 LoRA pair, are selectively activated by a softmax gating network (h' = W₀x + Σ gᵢ(x)·BᵢAᵢx), partitioning the optimization landscape into semantically decoupled subspaces. On top of this, Expert-Aware Negative Sampling (EANS) repurposes the MoE routing distributions across all L layers and G projection matrices as a free semantic-similarity proxy: the L1 distance between a query's and a negative's flattened routing signature determines an exponential-decay weight that up-weights hard negatives sharing expert activation patterns, with weight normalization preserving the global gradient scale. A two-stage training paradigm first stabilizes expert specialization via standard InfoNCE (Stage 1, t < T_warmup), then activates the EANS loss (Stage 2, t ≥ T_warmup) to sharpen embedding boundaries only after routing topology has converged.

## Results

On the MMEB benchmark, TSEmbed achieves state-of-the-art overall scores of 74.7% (7B) and 70.5% (2B) among MLLM-based models trained exclusively on MMEB, surpassing the previous best B3 by 2.7% and 2.4% respectively. At the 7B scale, TSEmbed also outperforms data-augmented models such as UNITE (70.3%) and CAFe (69.8%), and edges out Ops-MM-embedding-v1 (72.7%) by 2.0%. On proprietary production datasets, TSEmbed delivers a striking 21.87% absolute gain in advertising recall (33.20% vs. 11.33% for VLM2VEC), alongside consistent improvements in theme (+2.05%), lockscreen (+5.23%), and gaming (+3.99%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSEmbed (7B, 74.7%) outperforms all MLLM-based baselines trained on MMEB: B3 (72.0%, +2.7%), UniME-V2 (71.2%, +3.5%), QQMM-embed (70.7%, +4.0%), and LLaVE (70.3%, +4.4%). It also surpasses the original VLM2VEC framework (65.8%) by a substantial 8.9%, directly validating that MoE-LoRA resolves the task conflict that plagues monolithic adapters. Against data-augmented models, TSEmbed beats Ops-MM-embedding-v1 (72.7%) by 2.0%, UNITE (70.3%) by 4.4%, and CAFe (69.8%) by 4.9%, demonstrating that architectural design can compensate for external data. At the 2B scale, TSEmbed (70.5%) leads B3 (68.1%) by 2.4% and LLaVE (65.2%) by 5.3%, while also exceeding Ops-MM-embedding-v1 (69.0%) by 1.5%. On production datasets, TSEmbed achieves a dramatic 21.87% absolute recall gain over VLM2VEC in advertising (33.20% vs. 11.33%), with consistent 2–5% improvements across theme, lockscreen, and gaming domains. On VisDoc (MMEB-v2) zero-shot retrieval, TSEmbed outperforms VLM2VEC by 38.28, 10.16, 24.24, and 32.54 points across four document retrieval sub-tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSEmbed extends the VLM2VEC paradigm of repurposing MLLMs as universal embedding models by replacing the monolithic LoRA adapter with a Mixture-of-Experts variant (MoE-LoRA), directly addressing the task conflict that prior MLLM-based embedding methods (VLM2VEC, UniME-V2, LLaVE, B3, QQMM-embed) implicitly tolerate. The Expert-Aware Negative Sampling loss departs from conventional hard-negative mining (which relies on auxiliary models, memory banks, or explicit similarity computations) by repurposing the MoE routing distribution itself as a zero-cost semantic proxy for identifying informative negatives. The two-stage training paradigm is a practical addition that stabilizes expert specialization before EANS refinement, distinguishing it from prior single-stage contrastive training pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic decomposition of task conflict into spatial, temporal, and ecological dimensions provides a useful diagnostic framework for multi-task representation learning more broadly, and the MoE-LoRA + EANS combination offers a principled, parameter-efficient path toward task-level scaling in universal embeddings. However, the approach is validated primarily on MMEB and proprietary industrial datasets, leaving open questions about generalization to genuinely novel task types, scalability beyond four meta-task clusters, and whether the routing-as-semantics assumption holds under distribution shift. The reliance on a proprietary backbone (Qwen2-VL) and non-public production data limits independent reproducibility of the most striking results (e.g., the 21.87% advertising gain).
