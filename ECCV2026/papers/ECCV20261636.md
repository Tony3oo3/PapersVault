## Problem

Unified visual tokenizers face an inherent representation conflict: visual understanding requires high-level continuous semantic tokens, while visual generation demands low-level discrete pixel tokens for faithful reconstruction. Existing unified approaches either employ separate encoders (adding complexity) or force a single token space to serve both objectives, resulting in performance trade-offs where neither task reaches its potential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261636/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes a 256×256 RGB image and produces two distinct token streams: 256 continuous semantic tokens (from learnable tokens, pooled for understanding) and N discrete pixel tokens (from patch-level VQVAE with 16× downsampling and 4096×4 multi-codebook quantization) for reconstruction and generation. When integrated into the UMM (Qwen3-8B), the semantic tokens are projected into the text embedding space for multimodal comprehension, while the discrete pixel tokens are sampled autoregressively conditioned on text prompts to generate images. The tokenizer is trained on 50M open-source images; the UMM is further trained on ~80M image-text pairs plus 10M instruction/synthetic data.

## Method

WinTok uses a single ViT encoder that jointly processes pixel tokens (from patch embedding) and a set of randomly initialized learnable tokens concatenated along the sequence dimension. The pixel tokens are quantized via multi-codebook VQVAE and decoded for image reconstruction, optimized with reconstruction, codebook, perceptual, and adversarial losses. The learnable tokens are trained via asymmetric token distillation: a frozen visual foundation model (SigLIP2-So400M) extracts semantic tokens from the input image, and cosine similarity loss on globally pooled vectors guides the learnable tokens to inherit discriminative semantic knowledge. This decomposition means pixel tokens retain fine-grained spatial detail for generation while learnable tokens capture global semantics for understanding, eliminating the optimization conflict without requiring dual encoders. The overall loss is simply the sum of the semantic distillation loss and the pixel reconstruction loss, trained end-to-end for 5 epochs on 50M images.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K validation, WinTok achieves 82.0% Top-1 classification accuracy and rFID 0.41, surpassing the strongest unified tokenizer UniTok by 11.2% in accuracy while matching its reconstruction quality, all with only 50M training images (vs. 1B for UniTok) and a smaller codebook (2^48 vs. 2^96 capacity). For downstream multimodal understanding with Qwen3-8B, WinTok scores 86.5% on POPE and 55.2% on TextVQA, outperforming UniTok by 3.3% and 3.6% respectively. For text-to-image generation, WinTok reaches 0.76 on GenEval and 83.36 on DPG-Bench, exceeding UniTok (0.59 / 81.18) and remaining competitive with large diffusion models like SD3-Medium (0.74 / 84.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WinTok decisively outperforms UniTok, the strongest prior unified tokenizer, on classification accuracy (82.0% vs. 70.8%, +11.2%) while matching its rFID of 0.41, using 20× less training data (50M vs. 1B) and a 2^48 vs. 2^96 codebook capacity. Against specialized semantic tokenizers, WinTok exceeds CLIP-L/14 by 6.5% in accuracy (82.0 vs. 75.5) and SigLIP2 by a narrow margin in reconstruction (rFID 0.41 vs. 0.82), though DINOv2-L retains a 4.3% accuracy edge (86.3* vs. 82.0) as a continuous encoder. In reconstruction, WinTok matches UniTok on ImageNet (rFID 0.41, PSNR 24.23 vs. 24.31) and is within 0.19 rFID on MS-COCO (4.24 vs. 4.05). For downstream understanding, WinTok beats TokenFlow-L by 14.6% on MMBench (74.9 vs. 60.3) and UniTok by 3.3% on POPE (86.5 vs. 83.2). For generation, WinTok surpasses UniTok on GenEval (0.76 vs. 0.59) and DPG-Bench (83.36 vs. 81.18), and is competitive with SD3-Medium (0.74 GenEval) despite being autoregressive. The only clear loss is to DINOv2-L on linear-probing accuracy, but DINOv2 is a continuous encoder not designed for discrete token generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

WinTok builds on the VQVAE encoder-quantizer-decoder architecture for pixel-level tokenization and adopts Multi-codebook Quantization (MCQ) from UniTok for discrete codebook learning. It departs from prior unified tokenizers (UniTok, TokenFlow, VILA-U, TokLIP) that force a single token set to simultaneously serve semantic abstraction and pixel reconstruction, instead explicitly decomposing the two objectives via a hybrid of pixel tokens and learnable semantic tokens. The asymmetric token distillation mechanism—transferring knowledge from a frozen visual foundation model (e.g., SigLIP2) into randomly initialized learnable tokens via a cosine similarity loss on pooled representations—is the key conceptual departure, replacing the elaborate multi-stage training schemes used by VQRAE or the dual-encoder fusion of TokenFlow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WinTok demonstrates that the representation conflict between understanding and generation can be resolved with a remarkably simple architectural addition (256 learnable tokens + one cosine loss) rather than complex dual-encoder or multi-stage training pipelines, achieving 82.0% ImageNet accuracy and rFID 0.41 with only 50M training images—20× less data than UniTok. This suggests that the bottleneck in prior unified tokenizers was the forced sharing of a single token space, not data scale. However, the current evaluation is limited to 50M samples and a single LLM backbone (Qwen3-8B); whether the hybrid token design retains its advantage at billion-scale training and across diverse downstream architectures remains an open question that will determine its long-term impact on unified multimodal modeling.
