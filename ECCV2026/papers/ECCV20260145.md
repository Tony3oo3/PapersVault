## Problem

Standard discrete diffusion models suffer from a fundamental spatiotemporal misalignment when applied to image translation and generation: spatially, the forward process drives all tokens to a pure mask state, discarding the structural priors of the source image and forcing the model to hallucinate content from a semantic void; temporally, the random masking schedule during training contradicts the confidence-based 'easy-first, hard-last' decoding used at inference, making the model inefficient at learning complex transformation mappings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260145/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in a discrete VQ-VAE token space (8B-parameter Lumina-DiMOO backbone). For image-to-image tasks, it takes a source image (tokenized into a sequence of codebook indices) plus an optional text instruction, and produces a translated, edited, or restored target image. For text-to-image generation without a source image, the hybrid absorption degrades to a pure mask state and the information metric switches from source-target pixel difference to local pixel variance of the target, enabling the same framework to handle unanchored generation.

## Method

DDB introduces two synergistic mechanisms to construct a spatiotemporally aligned bridge between source and target domains. First, Hybrid Absorption redefines the absorbing state as a stochastic mixture h^(i) = λy^(i) + (1−λ)m (where y^(i) is the source token and m is the mask), injecting source structural priors directly into the latent space as spatial anchors; the optimal ratio λ≈0.5 is used with random-ratio training for robustness. Second, an Information-Guided Schedule computes a token-level information map (pixel-wise source-target difference for I2I, local variance for T2I) and uses it to stochastically prioritize masking high-information (semantically difficult) regions at earlier timesteps, aligning the training curriculum with the confidence-based inference trajectory. During reverse decoding, unresolved tokens are dynamically resampled from the hybrid distribution rather than reverted to a static mask, maintaining continuous spatial guidance throughout generation.

## Results

On the OmniEdit benchmark for image editing, DDB achieves the highest Edit Score (0.779) and DINO (0.790), outperforming the strongest baselines OmniGen2 (0.752) and Lumina-DiMOO (0.734). For pure I2I translation, DDB leads all metrics across restoration (PSNR 22.61, SSIM 0.759, LPIPS 0.081), modality translation (PSNR 23.36, SSIM 0.750), and super-resolution (PSNR 25.46, SSIM 0.733, LPIPS 0.056). On MIMIC-CXR report-to-image generation, DDB achieves the best FID (9.81), MS-SSIM (0.413), and CLIP-Score (0.451), substantially outperforming the next-best Lumina-DiMOO (FID 13.86). Under few-step inference (5 steps), DDB retains an Edit Score of 0.606 versus 0.541 for Lumina-DiMOO, demonstrating robustness at extremely low sampling budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDB achieves the best overall Edit Score on image editing (0.779 vs. 0.752 for OmniGen2 and 0.734 for Lumina-DiMOO) and the best DINO for subject-driven generation (0.789 vs. 0.774 for LaViDa-O), confirming superior balance between edit alignment and structural preservation. However, DDB trails LaViDa-O on Style Transfer Edit Score (1.077 vs. 1.233) and on CLIP-T for both image editing (0.236 vs. 0.242) and subject-driven generation (0.349 vs. 0.359), indicating that LaViDa-O's elastic architecture captures slightly stronger semantic shift in those specific settings. On pure I2I tasks (Table 2), DDB dominates all three baselines (MMaDA, Muddit, Lumina-DiMOO) across every metric, with the largest margin over Muddit in restoration (PSNR 22.61 vs. 20.29) and modality translation (SSIM 0.750 vs. 0.585). For T2I generation on MIMIC-CXR, DDB improves FID by 4.05 over Lumina-DiMOO (9.81 vs. 13.86) and by 5.44 over MMaDA (9.81 vs. 15.25), while UniXGen and MINIM lag further behind. In the few-step ablation (Table 7), DDB maintains a consistent 0.05–0.07 Edit Score advantage over Lumina-DiMOO at every step count from 5 to 64, with the gap widening at 5 steps (0.606 vs. 0.541), underscoring the efficiency gain from the spatiotemporally aligned trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Lumina-DiMOO, an 8B-parameter discrete diffusion model, and reframes its forward and reverse processes to address what the authors identify as a fundamental spatiotemporal misalignment: the random, uniform masking in training contradicts the confidence-based 'easy-first, hard-last' decoding at inference, and the pure-mask absorbing state severs spatial correspondence with the source image. Conceptually, the hybrid absorption mechanism is analogous to diffusion bridge formulations in continuous space (DDBM, RDDM, DDIB, BBDM), which construct direct trajectories between source and target domains rather than routing through a noise void, but here applied to the discrete token-level setting. The information-guided schedule draws on the intuition of difficulty-aware curricula but formalizes it through a pixel-level information density map that statistically prioritizes high-variation regions for early corruption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean diagnosis of training-inference trajectory mismatch in discrete diffusion is a useful conceptual contribution that could inform future masked-diffusion architectures beyond the specific tasks tested. The practical finding that the framework maintains quality at 5 sampling steps (where the backbone degrades significantly) has direct relevance for deployment efficiency. However, the empirical gains over the Lumina-DiMOO backbone are modest across most metrics, and the framework is evaluated primarily on a single backbone at 8B scale, leaving open whether the alignment principle transfers to smaller models or continuous-diffusion settings. A key open question is whether the information-guided schedule can be made adaptive at inference time rather than fixed at training time.
