## Problem

Smooth-approximation-based diffusion models, the dominant paradigm for image super-resolution, suffer from mode interpolation that blurs the sharp, impulsive scattering peaks inherent to SAR imagery, producing stochastic structural distortions inconsistent with real SAR physics. Additionally, existing SAR ISR methods that incorporate task-oriented objectives (e.g., detection losses) are bottlenecked by the scarcity of labeled SAR data, limiting generalizability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260247/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution SAR amplitude image at 1.0 m resolution (VV-polarized, X-band) and produces a high-resolution SAR amplitude image at 0.25 m resolution (×4 upscaling). LR images are generated via spectral-domain cropping (sub-sampling Doppler and range spectra) rather than naive interpolation, preserving authentic speckle statistics. Amplitudes are normalized by scene-level mean and 3σ, clipped to [0,1], and stored as 8-bit integers; training uses 256×256 random crops with sliding-window inference (stride 128) for full-image evaluation.

## Method

ProSR reformulates SAR ISR as a semantically-guided discrete token prediction task in three stages. Stage 1 (SADE) uses a dual-encoder to decouple high-frequency scattering details (VQGAN encoder + Gumbel-VQ with 1024 codebook entries) from low-frequency semantic structure (SAFE SSL backbone + adapter), where the detail is the residual z_det = z − F_sem quantized into discrete scattering primitives. Stage 2 (SPMG) maps continuous semantic features to a discrete spatial-semantic map using three learnable prototype tokens (target, shadow, clutter) assigned via Sparsemax and dynamic discretization with a relative threshold. Stage 3 (PMG) performs iterative masked token prediction (MaskGIT-style) where Prototype-Map-Guided Attention restricts cross-attention weights to semantically consistent regions only, preventing inter-class signal leakage. The key insight is that discrete categorical token selection inherently prevents the averaging of nearby distinct scattering peaks that causes mode interpolation in continuous diffusion models, while the semantic prototype map ensures attention flows stay within physically meaningful categories.

## Results

On the 0.25 m SAR ISR ×4 benchmark, ProSR achieves the best scores on all SAR-specific metrics: |ΔTCR| = 0.0329, IW-SSIM = 0.4206, HaarPSI = 0.4766, FID = 23.70, Density = 0.9741, Coverage = 0.8592, LPIPS = 0.3010, and DISTS = 0.1532. However, diffusion baselines (UPSR, LDM-15, ResShift) achieve higher pixel-level PSNR (17.48–17.85 vs. 16.93) and SSIM (0.127–0.132 vs. 0.109). On the downstream ATR task (MSTAR), ProSR reaches 88.39% accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline by 4.21 percentage points and the oversampled LR by 64.52 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProSR consistently outperforms all six baselines on every SAR-specific metric (|ΔTCR|, IW-SSIM, HaarPSI, FID, Density, Coverage, LPIPS, DISTS), with the largest margins over GAN-based methods (ESRGAN, SwinIR-GAN, SPSR) which suffer from gritty artifacts and poor scattering fidelity. Against the three diffusion baselines (LDM-15, ResShift, UPSR), ProSR trades pixel-level PSNR/SSIM (losing by 0.5–0.9 dB and 0.02–0.02 SSIM) for substantially better structural and statistical realism: FID drops from 34–47 to 23.70, Density rises from 0.50–0.65 to 0.9741, and |ΔTCR| improves from 0.066–0.282 to 0.033. The paper argues that the PSNR/SSIM gap reflects the known limitation of these metrics in favoring over-smoothed reconstructions, while the SAR-oriented metrics confirm that ProSR preserves physically consistent scattering. On the downstream ATR evaluation (MSTAR), ProSR achieves 88.39% accuracy versus 84.18% for the best baseline (UPSR) and 23.87% for oversampled LR, validating that scattering-fidelity gains translate to practical recognition performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProSR departs from the dominant smooth-approximation diffusion paradigm for SAR ISR (LDM, ResShift, UPSR) by reformulating reconstruction as discrete token prediction within a VQ-GAN-style quantized latent space, following the MaskGIT iterative masked-generation framework. It builds on VQ-GAN's Gumbel-VQ quantization for the discrete bottleneck, ResShift's encoder architecture for Stage 1, and the SAFE self-supervised ViT backbone for label-free semantic priors. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Prototype-Map-Guided Attention, which uses Sparsemax-based semantic prototype maps to restrict cross-attention flows within scattering categories (target, shadow, clutter), a mechanism absent from prior SAR ISR work that relies on task-specific labeled losses or optical-guidance priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a physically grounded limitation of diffusion models for SAR—mode interpolation causing structural merging of nearby scatterers—and provides both a methodological solution (discrete token prediction with semantic guidance) and a large-scale 0.25 m benchmark (132K patches from Umbra) that the SAR community has lacked. The downstream ATR validation (88.39% vs. 84.18% for the strongest baseline) demonstrates that scattering-fidelity preservation translates to practical utility, not just visual quality. Open questions include whether the discrete-modeling advantage persists at higher resolution scales or for multi-polarization settings, and whether the three-prototype (T/S/C) formulation generalizes to more complex scattering taxonomies. The authors also note the need for physics-informed no-reference metrics to quantify signal authenticity beyond current perceptual proxies.
