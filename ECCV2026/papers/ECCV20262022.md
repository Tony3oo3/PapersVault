## Problem

Multi-Exposure Fusion in dynamic scenes suffers from motion-induced ghosting, yet existing benchmarks are largely static or lack reliable ground truth, making it impossible to rigorously evaluate deghosting performance. Additionally, standard attention and flow-based alignment face an intrinsic paradox in MEF: the goal is to retrieve complementary details from auxiliary frames that are precisely missing or degraded in the reference view, which conflicts with similarity-based feature aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262022/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a triplet of RGB images at the same scene with different exposures (over-exposed, reference, under-exposed) and produces a single fused LDR image free of ghosting artifacts. The ExpoMotion dataset provides sequences with 3, 5, and 7-frame brackets spanning EV−3 to EV+3 at an average resolution of 2563×1896, with ground truths generated through a human-in-the-loop pipeline combining algorithmic candidates and expert photographer refinement. The method itself operates on the 3-frame subset (over, ref, under) within a U-shaped Transformer.

## Method

HOP decomposes MEF into exposure pre-alignment and ghost filtering. The GPIA module extracts global illumination priors via 16×16 average pooling, computes a ratio map between reference and auxiliary features, and modulates auxiliary features to harmonize exposure before any alignment occurs. The core HOA module then models ghost artifacts as orthogonal perturbations: it constructs a conflict vector from the feature difference between reference and pre-aligned auxiliary streams, normalizes it to the unit hypersphere, and applies a relaxed Householder reflector (H = I − αvvᵀ) to geometrically project the misaligned ghost component out of the feature manifold while preserving high-frequency details. A Gradient-Guided Feed-Forward Network (GGFN) augments standard FFNs with a fixed 3×3 Laplacian kernel as a hard-coded high-pass filter, fused with a learned DWConv branch via a learnable mixing parameter initialized to zero. The full architecture is a U-shaped Transformer with HHTB blocks (HOA + GGFN), trained end-to-end with AdamW over 150 epochs on 4×V100 GPUs.

## Results

On the full-reference benchmarks, HOP-B achieves the best PSNR across all three test sets: 28.80 dB on Kalantari17, 28.70 dB on RealHDRV, and 28.72 dB on the ExpoMotion controlled-motion test set, with SSIM values of 0.943, 0.942, and 0.927 respectively. The lightweight HOP-S variant (3.70M params) surpasses Restormer (26.10M params) on Kalantari17 (28.50 vs 28.33 dB) and RealHDRV (28.29 vs 28.28 dB) while running in 1403 ms. In no-reference perceptual evaluation, HOP-B trained on ExpoMotion reaches a MUSIQ score of 62.74 on the Sen/Tursen test set, outperforming the same architecture trained on Kalantari17 (57.87) and RealHDRV (60.89) by 4.9 and 1.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOP-B establishes a new state-of-the-art, outperforming all six baselines on every full-reference metric across all three test sets. Against the strongest competitor Restormer, HOP-B gains approximately 0.48 dB PSNR on Kalantari17 (28.80 vs 28.33) and 0.42 dB on RealHDRV (28.70 vs 28.28), while using only ~60% of the parameters (15.69M vs 26.10M) and running 15% faster (1576 ms vs 1860 ms). The lightweight HOP-S (3.70M params) also surpasses Restormer on Kalantari17 (28.50 vs 28.33 dB) and matches it on RealHDRV (28.29 vs 28.28 dB), though it trails slightly on ExpoMotion (28.59 vs 28.64 dB). Compared to ASTv2, HOP-S leads by 0.39 dB on Kalantari17 and 0.72 dB on RealHDRV. The flow-based methods (SAFNet, AFUNet) and attention-based methods (HDR_Trans, SCTNet) lag by 1.3–3.9 dB in PSNR, with AFUNet and SCTNet also being 4–7× slower in inference time. In the no-reference generalization study, training on ExpoMotion yields a 4.9-point MUSIQ improvement over Kalantari17 training, confirming the dataset's value for cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the Householder transformation—a classical linear-algebra operator for orthogonal reflection—as a learned feature-rejection mechanism in MEF, departing from the dominant optical-flow and similarity-based attention paradigms (SCTNet, HDR_Trans, Restormer) that aggregate auxiliary features by correspondence. Conceptually, it reframes deghosting not as a matching problem but as a geometric projection that orthogonally removes misaligned components, while the GPIA module draws on Retinex theory to decouple illumination harmonization from motion handling. On the data side, it extends the lineage of Kalantari17 and RealHDRV by introducing a third acquisition protocol (Static-Dynamic Exposure Bracketing) and a human-in-the-loop GT pipeline, filling the gap the authors identify between synthetic-motion realism and real-motion ground-truth availability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ExpoMotion addresses a concrete and long-standing gap: the absence of a large-scale, dynamic MEF benchmark with reliable ground truth, which has forced the field to rely on tone-mapped HDR reconstructions as imperfect supervision. The 1,738-sequence dataset with expert-refined labels is likely to become a standard evaluation resource for deghosting research. However, the method's three-input (over/ref/under) constraint limits practical applicability to devices with flexible exposure counts, and the Householder-based deghosting, while mathematically elegant, yields only modest absolute gains (~0.5 dB over Restormer) that may not justify the added architectural complexity in all deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether the projection-based rejection generalizes to larger displacements, occlusions, and variable frame counts.
