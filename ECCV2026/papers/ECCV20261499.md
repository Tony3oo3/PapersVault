## Problem

Unified multimodal model evaluation relies heavily on VLM-as-a-judge holistic scoring, which suffers from evaluation drift: swapping or updating the judge model shifts rankings because implicit weighting of quality criteria changes without notification. Existing unified benchmarks also use fragmented scoring protocols (multiple-choice, CLIP matching, holistic VLM scores) that are difficult to compare across tasks and do not address the stability of the evaluation instrument itself.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a textual instruction with optional reference images and a model output (text, image, or interleaved sequence) against a pre-constructed set of K atomic, binary specifications (averaging 14.67 per instance across SpecV-Bench's 1,200 instances). The judge performs one binary pass/fail check per specification, and the final SVP score is the satisfaction rate in [0,1], with the per-specification breakdown serving as a built-in diagnostic for pinpointing specific failure modes such as spatial-relation violations or text-rendering errors.

## Method

SpecV introduces two complementary components. The Specification Verification Protocol (SVP) separates the two responsibilities that holistic scoring conflates—deciding what to assess and how to weight it—by fixing the evaluation criteria as an explicit specification set before any judge is invoked, then reducing each judgment to a single binary check so that any cross-judge disagreement alters the aggregate by at most 1/K. The Specification Ensemble and Refinement (SER) pipeline constructs these specification sets by prompting three frontier VLMs from distinct providers (Gemini 3 Pro, GPT 5.2, Claude Opus 4.5) to independently generate candidates, semantically deduplicating them via a consolidation model, filtering by cross-model quality consensus (threshold τ=8 on a 0–10 relevance/clarity/binary-verifiability rating), and applying a final human QA pass. This ensemble design mitigates the systematic blind spots of any single model, as ablations show no single generator dominates all tasks while the full SER pipeline achieves the highest and most uniform human-correlation profile across all six UMM task families.

## Results

On SpecV-Bench (1,200 instances, 17,604 specifications), Nano Banana 2 leads all UMMs with an average SVP score of 81.77, followed by Nano Banana Pro (78.78) and Qwen-Image-2 (65.72), while Bagel and Emu 3.5 trail below 55. SVP substantially outperforms holistic VLM scoring on cross-judge stability: cross-family Kendall's τ rises from 0.856 to 0.902 and Spearman's ρ from 0.942 to 0.965, with the largest gain on editing (τ: 0.758→0.903). On human correlation, SVP raises average Kendall's τ from 0.459 to 0.543 and Spearman's ρ from 0.515 to 0.605 across all six tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between SVP and holistic VLM scoring as evaluation protocols. SVP wins on every stability and human-correlation metric: cross-family Kendall's τ improves from 0.856 (holistic) to 0.902 (SVP), and the gain is most pronounced on editing (τ: 0.758→0.903) and many-to-one generation (τ: 0.807→0.830), where ambiguous multi-aspect outputs amplify divergent judge weightings. On human correlation, SVP outperforms holistic scoring on all six tasks, with the largest gain on many-to-one (τ: 0.261→0.496) and the smallest on understanding (τ: 0.358→0.391). Among UMMs, Nano Banana 2 (avg 81.77) substantially outperforms Qwen-Image-2 (65.72) and Bagel (41–44), while dedicated models remain competitive on individual axes: Seedream 5.0 leads editing (85.14 vs. Nano Banana 2's 90.34 is actually higher, but Seedream leads among non-UMM models), GPT Image 1.5 leads many-to-one (77.64), and Gemini 3.1 Pro tops understanding (89.00). The SER ablation shows that no single specification-generation model dominates: Claude leads T2I (τ=0.561), Gemini leads editing (τ=0.453), and GPT leads understanding (τ=0.368), but the full SER ensemble achieves the best average (τ=0.493) with the most balanced per-task profile.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecV generalizes the decomposed-evaluation idea from TIFA and DSG—which broke text-to-image prompts into verifiable sub-questions—into a unified protocol applicable across all six UMM task families (understanding, generation, editing, interleaved, many-to-one, thinking with images). It departs from the prevailing VLM-as-a-Judge paradigm by replacing holistic scalar scoring with atomic binary specification checks, explicitly motivated by the structural problem of evaluation drift that holistic scoring introduces when judge models are updated. The SER pipeline draws on ensemble-methods intuition from ML, applying multi-model consensus to specification generation and quality filtering rather than to prediction aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpecV-Bench (1,200 instances, 17,604 specifications) provides the first unified benchmark with a consistent, drift-resistant scoring protocol across all core UMM capabilities, enabling cross-task trade-off analysis that fragmented prior benchmarks (MME-Unify, RealUnify) could not support. A key open question is whether binary specification decomposition loses information about relative quality or aesthetic nuance that holistic scoring captures, and whether the approach scales to truly open-ended creative tasks where specifications become hard to enumerate. The human-correlation gains, while consistent, remain moderate (average τ ≈ 0.54), suggesting that specification-based evaluation is a necessary but not sufficient condition for fully reliable multimodal assessment.
