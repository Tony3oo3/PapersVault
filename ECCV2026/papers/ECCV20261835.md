## Problem

Existing deep-learning steganography methods require direct pixel-level modification of the entire container image, meaning real-world surfaces must be fully replaced with printed versions rather than simply augmented. This paper addresses the gap of encoding hidden data onto existing real-world surfaces in a non-permanent, visually unobtrusive manner that is robust to the inevitable human placement error when applying a physical sticker.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261835/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a 100-bit message (already augmented with user-chosen error correction, e.g., BCH) and a 128×128 surface image, producing a 32×32 to 64×64 transparent sticker pattern to be printed on clear sticker paper. The decoder pipeline takes a full scene image (up to 400×400, processed via sliding window), localizes the sticker via four-corner heatmaps, and outputs the decoded 100-bit message. Unlike prior methods that require printing the entire background image, only the small sticker pattern is printed, and the sticker need not be placed at the exact pixel location used during encoding.

## Method

The method jointly optimizes three networks end-to-end: a UNet encoder that generates the sticker pattern conditioned on the surface image and message, an HRNet-based localizer that predicts four-corner heatmaps for sticker detection, and a convolutional decoder that recovers the 100-bit secret. Training proceeds in three stages—first optimizing decode accuracy at ground-truth locations, then introducing an LPIPS-based cover loss for imperceptibility, and finally training the localizer from scratch with combined localization and predicted-location decode losses. A key design choice is simulating human placement jitter (±0.05·128 px shifts, ±5° rotation, 0.98–1.02 scale) during training, which forces the encoder to concentrate visual information near the sticker center with soft transparent edges, making the pattern robust to misalignment. The final loss balances four terms (ground-truth decode BCE, heatmap MSE, predicted-location decode BCE, and LPIPS cover loss) with stage-dependent weights, and the fully convolutional localizer is extended with blob detection for multi-sticker detection.

## Results

On the ADE20K test set, the method achieves 99.95% detection rate and 99.14% decode accuracy on non-augmented images, dropping to 97.24% detection and 89.16% decode accuracy under augmentations (perspective warp, brightness/contrast/saturation shifts, Gaussian blur, and noise). The average LPIPS score between cover and container subsections is 0.0632. In real-world experiments across 34 surfaces (84 images), the average decoding accuracy is 93.10% with a median of 94%, and the method handles mild occlusions (85.17% accuracy) and wrinkles (84.60% accuracy) without dedicated training for those conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StegaStamp [25], the proposed method wins decisively on both perceptual quality and robustness. At matched 400×400 encoding size, Ours achieves PSNR 32.27 vs. StegaStamp's 28.60 and SSIM 0.9792 vs. 0.8857. Under increasing placement misalignment, StegaStamp's decode accuracy drops sharply (99.85% → 73.00% from 0 to 9 pixels), while the proposed method remains largely unaffected; similarly, StegaStamp's LPIPS increases substantially after just 1–2 pixels of jitter whereas Ours stays nearly constant even at 20 pixels. Against Jia et al. [13], Ours at the comparable 64×64 encoding ratio achieves PSNR 40.62 and SSIM 0.9922, substantially outperforming Jia's 32.95 and 0.9677 at 96×96. A key structural advantage is that StegaStamp requires a white border for localization (making it inherently visible), while the proposed method needs no such border and tolerates human placement error by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep-learning steganography line initiated by StegaStamp [25] and Jia et al. [13], reusing their UNet encoder/decoder and HRNet-based localization architectures, but fundamentally repositions the problem from digital container creation to physical sticker deployment. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods assume pixel-perfect digital manipulation of a cover image, whereas this work models a transparent sticker placed by a human on a real-world surface, introducing placement jitter, partial coverage, and the need for local-and-global imperceptibility at the sticker boundary. The multi-stage training protocol that sequentially introduces decoding, cover (LPIPS), and localization losses is a new training strategy not present in either StegaStamp or Jia et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in enabling non-permanent, easily removable data encoding on arbitrary real-world surfaces without printing the full background image, which is important for AR object tracking, tourist-site information, and product identification. However, the stickers are not fully invisible—sticker paper properties and certain background textures degrade both imperceptibility and decoding accuracy—and the method has not been validated at scale beyond 128×128 simulation and ~84 real-world images. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to much larger stickers, dynamic or reflective surfaces, and whether the Pareto frontier between decoding accuracy and LPIPS can be shifted further with better perceptual losses or generative priors.
