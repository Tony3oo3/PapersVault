## Problem

Nighttime auto white balance remains unsolved because low illumination and high-ISO chromatic noise violate the statistical assumptions underlying conventional AWB algorithms, while deep learning approaches require extensive labeled nighttime data and degrade severely when deployed on unseen camera sensors. No prior method combines the interpretability and sensor-agnostic robustness of statistical estimators with the adaptive, per-image parameter tuning capability of learning-based approaches, and no multi-sensor nighttime benchmark existed to evaluate cross-sensor generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes black-level-corrected linear RGB images (12-bit from iPhone 16 Pro at 4320×2160 or 14-bit from Sony ILCE-6400 at 6000×4000, ISO 500–16000), internally downsampling to a common resolution. At inference the agent iteratively updates two hyperparameters (gray-pixel percentage N and Minkowski order p) over an average of 3 steps (~1.1 s per image on an RTX 3080), producing a 3-channel illuminant estimate and the corresponding white-balanced image without requiring ground-truth illuminants or reference images.

## Method

The core algorithm SGP-LRD detects salient gray pixels via local-contrast grayness scoring followed by variance and color-deviation filtering, then estimates the illuminant using a Minkowski-norm aggregation of pixel-wise local reflectance differences weighted by a luminance-based confidence map. Two hyperparameters—gray-pixel sampling percentage N and Minkowski exponent p—critically control performance and are inherently scene-dependent in low light. A Soft Actor-Critic agent, whose state encodes a log-chrominance (RGB-uv) histogram plus a compact parameter-history vector, outputs relative continuous actions that nudge (N, p) toward per-image optima, with a reward combining relative angular-error improvement, an action-magnitude penalty, and a difficulty-aware termination bonus. Training uses a two-stage curriculum (single-image stabilization followed by cyclic multi-image episodes over a pool of 5 images) and requires only 5 labeled images per dataset, yet generalizes across unseen sensors better than fully supervised deep baselines.

## Results

On the cross-dataset evaluation (the paper's primary benchmark), RL-AWB trained on only 5 images per dataset achieves a median angular error of 3.03° (NCC→LEVI) and 1.99° (LEVI→NCC), outperforming all fully-supervised learning-based baselines trained on complete datasets. On the in-dataset statistical comparison, SGP-LRD achieves the lowest median error of 2.12° on NCC and 3.08° on LEVI. On the daytime Gehler-Shi dataset (trained on NCC), RL-AWB reaches 2.24° median error, surpassing the best learning-based baseline C5 (3.34°) by over 1°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against statistical baselines (Table 1), SGP-LRD achieves the best median error on both NCC (2.12° vs. RGP's 2.22°) and LEVI (3.08° vs. MSGP's 3.12°), with the largest margin over edge-based methods GE-1st/GE-2nd (4.14°/3.58° on NCC). In the cross-dataset learning-based comparison (Table 2), RL-AWB wins decisively: on LEVI→NCC it achieves 1.99° median error versus the next-best C5 at 4.47° (a 2.48° gap), and on NCC→LEVI it edges out C4 (3.03° vs. 3.09°) while dramatically reducing worst-25% error (5.36° vs. 6.37°). The diffusion-based GCC [22] performs worst in cross-dataset transfer (28.1° median on NCC→LEVI, 90.0° worst-25%), indicating severe domain sensitivity. On the daytime Gehler-Shi benchmark (Table 3), RL-AWB (2.24° median) outperforms all learning-based baselines including C5 (3.34°), ePCC (4.10°), and PCC (4.97°), while also slightly improving over its own SGP-LRD backbone (2.38°), confirming that the RL tuning generalizes beyond nighttime to well-lit scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of statistical color constancy (gray-pixel detection, Minkowski-norm illuminant estimation) and reinforcement learning for ISP, building directly on Qian et al.'s grayness index and the Minkowski norm framework while departing from prior RL-ISP work (RL-Restore, RL-AE) that used RL to select processing modules or control camera settings. The key conceptual departure is formulating AWB parameter tuning as a sequential decision problem where the RL agent adaptively adjusts hyperparameters of an interpretable statistical estimator, rather than regressing illuminant RGB directly as deep learning methods do. The introduction of LEVI as the first multi-sensor nighttime benchmark extends the evaluation landscape beyond the single-camera NCC dataset, enabling cross-sensor generalization studies that prior nighttime color constancy work could not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-sensor nighttime AWB is a critical practical problem for mobile photography, surveillance, and automotive imaging, and the demonstrated ability to generalize across unseen camera sensors with only 5 training images per dataset offers a compelling data-efficiency story for deployment scenarios where labeled nighttime data is scarce. The ground-truth-free inference (no illuminant labels needed at test time) makes the approach directly deployable in ISP pipelines, unlike grid-search or one-step regression alternatives that require ground truth for parameter selection. However, the over-correction failure mode on already-accurate estimates and the limited two-parameter action space (N%, p) constrain immediate applicability; extending to hierarchical policies over additional ISP parameters and incorporating safety-aware reward shaping are natural next steps that the authors identify.
