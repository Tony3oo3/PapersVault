## Problem

Current remote sensing VLMs and EO agents remain largely perception-centric or descriptive, lacking explicit structured multi-step reasoning with verifiable tool orchestration. Prior tool-augmented EO efforts (ThinkGeo, Earth-Agent) expose persistent weaknesses in coordinate consistency, spatial grounding, and physically verifiable GIS/index-based reasoning, and none couple a large-scale reasoning corpus with a deterministic execution engine for end-to-end validation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261692/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language query alongside one or more multimodal EO inputs (RGB satellite tiles, SAR imagery, GIS vector layers, georeferenced GeoTIFF rasters, or paired multi-date change-detection images) and produces an autoregressive sequence of interleaved thoughts, JSON-formatted tool invocations, and environment-returned observations that converge on a final answer. Each trajectory averages ~7 reasoning steps (thought-action-observation transitions), and all tool calls follow a standardized JSON contract with typed arguments and structured outputs, enabling deterministic replay and spatial validation.

## Method

OpenEarthAgent builds a unified tool registry that abstracts five categories of geospatial operators (perceptual, GIS computation, spectral index, georeferenced raster, and utility) under a single callable schema M_j = (x_in, y_out, ψ_j), with a central orchestrator that parses model-generated calls, validates arguments, executes the function, and appends results to a working-memory cache. Training is supervised fine-tuning of Qwen3-4B on 14,538 validated reasoning trajectories, using response-only masking so the loss is computed exclusively on assistant-generated tool-call and reasoning tokens while environment observations are masked out. Before inclusion, every trajectory undergoes deterministic replay through the tool controller to verify argument formatting, coordinate integrity, geometric validity, and full-chain executability. The key design choice is coupling the dataset with this execution engine and trajectory-based policy alignment, so the model learns not just what to say but how to invoke tools in a spatially and logically consistent sequence, with a cache that prevents redundant computation and supports extensibility without retraining.

## Results

In end-to-end evaluation with live tool execution, OpenEarthAgent (4B) achieves 98.52% GIS F1, 58.30% Perception F1, and 51.18% Logic F1, while dominating tool-order fidelity metrics (AnyOrder 67.75%, SameOrder 67.24%, Unique 72.71%) and attaining 45.26% answer accuracy and 75.86% generation accuracy. In step-by-step evaluation, it reaches state-of-the-art Instance Accuracy (99.51%), Tool Accuracy (97.18%), Argument Name Accuracy (96.08%), and Argument Value Accuracy (62.10%), surpassing GPT-4o on all four. On the ThinkGeo benchmark, OpenEarthAgent scores 16.35 answer accuracy versus 9.78 for ThinkGeo and 12.53 for the Qwen3-4B baseline, and on a 60-query subset against a generic GPT agent it achieves 60.04 total score with 21.67 s average latency versus 21.75 and 933.54 s for GPT-Agent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o, OpenEarthAgent wins on Perception F1 (58.30 vs. 44.47), Logic F1 (51.18 vs. 35.95), GIS F1 (98.52 vs. 95.80), all three tool-order metrics (67–73% vs. 40–56%), and answer accuracy (45.26 vs. 39.22), but trails on Operation F1 (56.76 vs. 66.91) and generation accuracy (75.86 vs. 77.93). Against the Qwen3-Instruct-2507 4B base model (the direct pre-fine-tuning baseline), OpenEarthAgent improves every metric substantially, e.g., GIS F1 from 71.82 to 98.52, AnyOrder from 16.00 to 67.75, and answer accuracy from 13.72 to 45.26, confirming that trajectory-based SFT is the primary driver of gains. Against open-source 7–8B models (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Internlm3-8B, Mistral-7B), OpenEarthAgent outperforms all on every reported metric, with the largest margins on tool-order fidelity and answer accuracy where those models score below 16%. On the ThinkGeo benchmark, OpenEarthAgent achieves 16.35 answer accuracy and 29.13 answer-instruction accuracy, surpassing ThinkGeo's reported 9.78/20.40 and the Qwen3-4B baseline's 12.53/25.92, demonstrating transferable geospatial reasoning beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ReAct-style tool-augmented reasoning paradigm (Yao et al., 2023) into the geospatial domain by coupling a unified executable tool registry with trajectory-based supervised fine-tuning. It builds directly on prior EO agents—ThinkGeo and Earth-Agent—which demonstrated that LLMs can plan analytical chains over satellite imagery, but departs from them by integrating GIS vector/raster operations, spectral index computation (NDVI, NBR, NDBI), and deterministic replay validation into a single training and evaluation loop. The perception-side lineage traces through remote sensing foundation models (Prithvi, Copernicus-FM, Galileo) and multimodal VLMs (GeoChat, EarthDial), which provide the visual grounding but lack structured multi-step reasoning; OpenEarthAgent positions itself as the agentic layer above these perception models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale, tool-augmented geospatial reasoning benchmark (14,538 training / 1,169 evaluation instances with 107K+ reasoning steps) that covers GIS, spectral, and perception tasks in a unified schema, filling a gap where prior EO benchmarks focused on data scale rather than executable reasoning fidelity. The finding that a 4B model with targeted trajectory training outperforms frontier LLMs on tool-order accuracy and GIS F1 suggests that domain-specific agent training is a viable path to reducing reliance on expensive proprietary models for geospatial workflows. Open questions include whether the deterministic replay validation generalizes to open-ended, non-curated geospatial queries, how the framework scales to longer-horizon multi-tool chains, and whether the performance gap over general-purpose agents persists as base models improve in native tool-use capability.
