## Problem

Existing adversarial detectors for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) rely on post-generation analysis or heavyweight auxiliary judging pipelines, incurring substantial latency that precludes real-time streaming deployment. This paper asks whether adversarial images can be detected by probing the LVLM's own internal states during the prefill stage, before any response token is generated, using only benign training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260669/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image that is processed through the victim LVLM's vision tower and language backbone during the prefill stage; no generated text is required. It produces two intermediate scalar scores—a token-wise masked-reconstruction anomaly score from the vision tower and a layer-wise trajectory inconsistency score from the language backbone—which are z-score normalized, fused with a fixed balancing ratio, and compared against a threshold to yield a binary adversarial/benign decision. Training requires only benign samples (MS-COCO 2017 train split); no adversarial examples are needed.

## Method

PTI decomposes adversarial detection into two complementary signals grounded in a probabilistic framework over the benign data distribution. The first module operates in the vision tower at a shallow layer: it randomly masks a fraction of visual tokens, feeds them through a lightweight CNN reconstruction head, and uses the expected reconstruction error of masked tokens as a spatial anomaly score. The second module operates in the language backbone: it selects the top-ρ most anomalous tokens, projects their layer-wise hidden states onto the unit sphere via a learned MLP projector (warmed up with self-distillation and in-batch contrast), and trains a GRU to predict next-layer directions, measuring average cosine distance as a semantic inconsistency score. A decision gate sums the two normalized scores and applies a threshold to trigger immediate refusal. Both modules are trained exclusively on benign data—reconstruction loss for the vision module and expected cosine distance for the language module—making the approach fully unsupervised with respect to attack types.

## Results

On in-domain COCO evaluation across four LVLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, LLaVA-OneVision-7B, InternVL2.5-8B, Gemma-4-E4B-IT) and five attacks, PTI achieves an average AUC of 0.980 and TPR@5%FPR of 91.91%, substantially outperforming PIP (AUC 0.762), MirrorCheck (AUC 0.900), and Perturbation Forgery (AUC 0.878). Under out-of-distribution (ImageNet) and stress-test settings, PTI maintains AUC of 0.941 and 0.958 respectively, with the lowest FPR@95%TPR (23.79% and 20.55%). Inference overhead is 0.093 s/sample, over 10× faster than PIP (1.62 s) and MirrorCheck (1.18 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTI dominates all three baselines across every model-attack combination in the in-domain setting, with the largest margins against PIP (average AUC gap of +0.218, TPR@5 gap of +58.3 points). PIP's attention-probe mechanism collapses on black-box transfer attacks (M-Attack AUC drops to 0.548–0.654) and unrestricted attacks (AdvDiffVLM AUC 0.571–0.703), while PTI maintains AUC ≥ 0.96 in all cases. MirrorCheck is the strongest baseline (avg AUC 0.900) and performs relatively uniformly across attacks, but still trails PTI by 8 points in average AUC and 30 points in TPR@5; it also requires full-sequence generation, adding 1.18 s/sample overhead. Perturbation Forgery (PF) is competitive on patch-based attacks (PandoraBox AUC 0.933 on Qwen) but degrades severely under OOD (avg AUC 0.727) and stress tests (avg FPR@95 of 80.04%), indicating it captures superficial pixel artifacts rather than intrinsic adversarial signals. PTI's 0.093 s/sample overhead is ~17× lower than PIP and ~13× lower than MirrorCheck, making it the only method suitable for real-time streaming deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

PTI carves new ground at the intersection of masked-reconstruction anomaly detection (borrowed from the MAE lineage) and sequence-dynamics modeling (via a GRU over layer-wise hidden states), repurposing both as lightweight probes inside an LVLM rather than as standalone generative or classification objectives. It departs from prior LVLM-specific detectors (PIP, MirrorCheck) that require post-generation analysis or auxiliary model calls, and from pixel-space detectors (Perturbation Forgery) that fail under distributional shift, by operating entirely on internal feature-space states during the prefill stage. The probabilistic decomposition of joint input-output NLL into a visual-anomaly term and a semantic-inconsistency term provides a unifying framework that prior LVLM detectors lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prefill-time constraint is the key practical contribution: by detecting adversarial inputs before any response token is emitted, PTI is compatible with streaming LVLM deployments where post-hoc moderation is too late, and its ~0.09 s/sample overhead is an order of magnitude below existing LVLM detectors. Limitations include evaluation on only four open-source models, a single-image single-prompt setting, and the adaptive-attack results showing that a white-box adversary can still partially evade detection (AUC drops from 0.971 to 0.952). Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-turn conversations, video inputs, and whether the shallow-layer preference for anomaly detection (layers 1–8) holds across architectures with different vision-tower depths.
