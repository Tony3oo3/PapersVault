## Problem

Existing continual learning methods for text-to-image retrieval rely on image-text similarity distillation, which implicitly assumes both modalities shift simultaneously. However, real-world retrieval systems face 'unbalanced cross-modal shift'—a scenario where image domains remain stable while textual queries diverge dramatically due to image polysemy (a single image being queried from different semantic facets over time). This paper identifies that this asymmetry causes severe forgetting that similarity-based regularization cannot prevent, and no prior method explicitly preserves the multi-faceted semantics of images under such conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained retrieval-oriented VLM (BLIP-2 or SigLIP2) with a captioning head, a sequence of new image-text datasets to learn in order, and generates five auxiliary captions per image via nucleus sampling (p=0.9) from the frozen pre-update model as a one-time preprocessing step. During training, the model consumes both the new image-text pairs and the pre-generated auxiliary captions, producing an updated VLM that outputs ranked image lists for text queries. Evaluation uses MRR on one-to-one datasets and mAP on Incidents1M (one-to-many), with continual learning metrics (AA, FM, HM) tracked across all previously seen datasets after each training phase.

## Method

CaPCL's core mechanism is caption-based regularization: for each new image, the pre-update model generates auxiliary captions that capture its current semantic interpretation, and the updated model is trained to reproduce these captions via negative log-likelihood (L_cap), added to the standard VLM contrastive and language-modeling losses. This constrains the image's semantic interpretation itself rather than merely its similarity to specific text queries, preventing the image representation from drifting toward newly introduced textual semantics. Two optional extensions further leverage auxiliary captions: an ACC contrastive loss that pulls image and auxiliary-caption embeddings together, and an ACS distillation loss that preserves the pre-update model's similarity structure between images and auxiliary captions via KL divergence. The approach is architecture-agnostic—when the VLM lacks a captioning head (e.g., SigLIP2), external captions from Qwen2.5-VL substitute, and the ACC/ACS losses alone suffice.

## Results

Across four training orders on a five-dataset benchmark (Incidents1M, BLIP3-OCR, RSICD, TextCaps, PixMo-Docs), CaPCL achieves an average AA of 0.6942, FM of 0.0654, and HM of 0.7966, outperforming all baselines. The best baseline, ModX, reaches AA 0.6387 and FM 0.0890 (HM 0.7509), while standard fine-tuning (FT) and EWC both yield AA ≈ 0.646 and FM ≈ 0.124. On the most challenging order (PTRBI), CaPCL still achieves FM 0.0965 versus 0.1303 for ModX and 0.2674 for FT, demonstrating robustness under severe distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaPCL outperforms all four baselines on every metric (AA, FM, HM) across all four training orders. Against ModX (the strongest baseline, with the best FM of 0.0890 among baselines), CaPCL improves AA by +0.056 (0.6942 vs. 0.6387) while also reducing FM by 0.024 (0.0654 vs. 0.0890), indicating that similarity-matrix distillation over-constrains adaptation. Against DKR and C2MR, CaPCL gains +0.064 in AA and roughly 0.030–0.035 in FM. Against FT and EWC, the gains are even larger: +0.048 in AA and −0.059 in FM, confirming that without any continual-learning regularization the model suffers severe forgetting (FM ≈ 0.124). The margin is most pronounced on the PTRBI order, where CaPCL's FM of 0.0965 is 34% lower than ModX's 0.1303 and 64% lower than FT's 0.2674.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of continual T2I retrieval methods (ModX, DKR, C2MR) that regularize by preserving image-text similarity matrices computed by the pre-update model. Rather than constraining pairwise image-query relationships, CaPCL shifts the regularization target to the model's own caption generation, arguing that preserving the semantic interpretation of an image is more robust than preserving its similarity to specific queries. The diagnostic framework (image domain overlap via principal angles + textual query divergence via VLM log-probability) is novel and provides a principled way to characterize when existing methods fail, complementing the empirical continual learning literature that has largely treated distribution shift as a monolithic phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of unbalanced cross-modal shift as a distinct and severe failure mode gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic for when their retrieval systems will degrade under continual adaptation, and the caption-based regularization offers a drop-in solution for any VLM with a captioning head. A key limitation is that what knowledge is preserved is implicitly determined by the captioning head's quality and coverage, rather than being explicitly controllable; the authors acknowledge this and suggest future work on prioritizing user-specified knowledge. The five-dataset benchmark with multiple training orders is a useful resource, though the evaluation is limited to two VLM architectures (BLIP-2, SigLIP2) and a single evaluation set (Flickr30K) for retention.
