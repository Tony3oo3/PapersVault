## Problem

Learning effective representations from BRep CAD models is hindered by the scarcity of labeled data and the extreme scale variations inherent in CAD geometry (e.g., large planes vs. small fillets). Existing Transformer-based approaches apply flat, single-level attention uniformly across all sampled points, making them inefficient under dense geometric sampling and unable to decouple macroscopic topological structure from microscopic local detail. This paper addresses both challenges by combining a strict input-level masked reconstruction objective with a hierarchical architecture that explicitly separates global and local processing.

## Input / Output

The method takes BRep CAD models as input and converts them into geometric attributed adjacency graphs (gAAG) via dual-resolution UV parameterization: each face is sampled on both a 3×3 grid (global semantics) and a 13×13 grid (fine detail), yielding 7D geometry + 16D attributes per grid point, while each edge is sampled at 13 points with 12D geometry + 9D attributes. During pre-training, 70% of raw face/edge geometries and attributes are randomly masked and the network must reconstruct them; during fine-tuning, the same encoder produces per-face machining feature labels (25 classes), per-face modeling operation labels (8 classes), or shape-level class labels (26 classes) depending on the attached task head.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical graph Transformer encoder built around a Cross-Scale Mutual Attention (CSMA) block that creates an asymmetric information bottleneck: sparse low-resolution (3×3) features act as queries to extract essential details from dense high-resolution (13×13) features, while the high-resolution features in turn attend to the updated low-resolution context and explicit topological edge features. Two stacked CSMA blocks are followed by a message-passing neural network (MPNN) that enforces local face-to-face connectivity, establishing a global-to-local processing order that the ablation confirms is critical (reversing the order drops 0.1% accuracy from 88.75% to 76.39%). The decoder is two-stage: an MPNN graph decoder first recovers intermediate node/edge features, then a BRep decoder using FoldingNet branches and MLPs maps these back to explicit 3D geometries and discrete attributes. Pre-training uses a 70% raw-geometry masking ratio with five loss terms (latent feature alignment with stop-gradient plus four explicit reconstruction losses), and downstream tasks are handled by freezing or fine-tuning the encoder with a lightweight task-specific head on as little as 0.1% labeled data.

## Results

On machining feature recognition, the method achieves 88.75% Acc and 66.38% mIoU on MFInstSeg at just 0.1% supervision, substantially outperforming BRepFormer (33.43% Acc) and BRepMAE (87.93% Acc). Under full supervision, it reaches 99.53% Acc on MFInstSeg, 99.61% on MFCAD++, and 99.86% on CADSynth, matching or exceeding the fully supervised BRepFormer upper bound. On modeling segmentation (Fusion 360 Gallery), it achieves 72.33% Acc / 34.63% mIoU at 10-shot and 97.02% Acc / 86.75% mIoU at full data. On part classification (SolidLetter), it reaches 82.51% Acc at 10-shot and 97.91% at full data, the best across all supervision levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BRepFormer [4] (flat graph Transformer, fully supervised), the proposed method wins decisively in low-data regimes: at 0.1% supervision on MFInstSeg, it achieves 88.75% Acc versus BRepFormer's 33.43%, a 55-point gap. At full supervision, BRepFormer edges ahead slightly on MFInstSeg (99.62% vs 99.53% Acc) but the proposed method matches or exceeds it on MFCAD++ (99.61% vs 99.27% mIoU) and CADSynth (99.86% vs 99.88% Acc). Against BRepMAE [28] (the closest self-supervised baseline using latent-feature masking), the proposed method consistently outperforms at every supervision level, e.g., 88.75% vs 87.93% Acc on MFInstSeg at 0.1% and 97.31% vs 96.91% at 1%. On modeling segmentation, the method surpasses BRep-BERT [16] by 13.07 Acc points at 10-shot (72.33% vs 59.26%) and by 1.88 Acc points at full data (97.02% vs 95.14%). On part classification, it outperforms BRepFormer [4] by 5.98 Acc points at 10-shot (82.51% vs 76.53%) and by 0.32 points at full data (97.91% vs 97.59%), while BRT [39] trails by a wide margin (54.84% at 10-shot). AAGNet [23] (GNN-based, fully supervised) is dominated at all supervision levels below 100%, confirming the advantage of self-supervised pre-training with a hierarchical Transformer over purely local message passing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (He et al., CVPR 2022) to BRep CAD models, building on the geometric attributed adjacency graph (gAAG) representation from AAGNet (Wu et al.) and the UV parameterization strategy from UVNet (Jayaraman et al., CVPR 2021). It departs from flat, single-level graph Transformers (BRepFormer, BRepMFR) by introducing a hierarchical cross-scale mutual attention (CSMA) block that explicitly decouples coarse global shape from fine local detail via dual-resolution UV grids, and from BRepMAE (Yao et al.) by masking raw input geometries rather than latent features, enforcing a stricter information bottleneck. The architectural choice of placing global Transformer attention before local MPNN message passing inverts the typical GNN ordering, motivated by the need to establish global geometric context before aggregating neighborhood topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical bottleneck in CAD AI: the scarcity of labeled data due to commercial IP sensitivity, demonstrating that self-supervised pre-training on ~200K unlabeled models yields strong few-shot transfer (e.g., 88.75% Acc at 0.1% supervision on MFInstSeg). The hierarchical CSMA design offers a principled solution to the extreme scale variation inherent in CAD (large planes vs. small fillets) that flat attention struggles with. However, the dual-resolution sampling strategy increases preprocessing and memory overhead, limiting scalability to massive industrial assemblies, and the framework has not yet been explored for generative BRep modeling. Open questions include whether the hierarchical bottleneck generalizes to other structured geometric representations and whether the approach scales to multi-body assemblies with thousands of faces.
