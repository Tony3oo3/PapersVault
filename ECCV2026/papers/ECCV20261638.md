## Problem

A fundamental appearance-geometry trade-off persists in 3D scene reconstruction: DA3 produces photorealistic 3DGS scenes but with geometrically inaccurate surfaces (floating Gaussians, inconsistent normals), while SAM 3D yields geometrically coherent per-object Gaussian layouts but with unrealistic appearance that does not faithfully reflect input images. No existing method combines the geometric precision of object-level foundation models with the photorealistic rendering quality of full-scene reconstruction models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261638/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image sequence (with optional camera poses, inheriting DA3's posed/pose-free flexibility at any view count) and internally invokes two separate foundation models: DA3 for full-scene 3DGS reconstruction and SAM 3D for per-object Gaussian clouds. The output is a refined 3D Gaussian splat scene where geometry within object regions is corrected by SAM 3D's layout while all color parameters remain frozen from DA3, enabling both improved 3D accuracy and photorealistic novel view synthesis without retraining either model.

## Method

DASAM3D is a three-stage differentiable pipeline operating entirely in Gaussian parameter space. Stage 1 (Global Alignment Optimization) merges all SAM 3D objects into a single cloud and registers them to DA3's coordinate frame via a similarity transform optimized with per-view affine-normalized depth supervision across all real training cameras. Stage 2 (Per-Object Alignment Refinement) independently refines each object using cameras filtered to object-visible views, combining 2D depth matching, a one-way 3D Chamfer loss to the local DA3 cloud, and a soft bounding-box constraint, followed by a quality score (2D overlap × depth residual × 3D proximity) that gates which objects proceed. Stage 3 (Layout-Based Geometry Optimization) defines adaptive volumetric ROIs from the filtered SAM 3D layout and refines only DA3 Gaussians within those regions using a composite loss of affine-normalized depth, surface normal consistency, and an RGB anchor to the pre-Stage-3 rendering, while all color parameters remain frozen to preserve photorealistic appearance.

## Results

On DL3DV-10K, DASAM3D achieves 27.08 dB PSNR, 0.817 SSIM, and 0.191 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline DA3 (26.64 dB, 0.809, 0.198) by 0.44 dB in PSNR. On MipNeRF-360, DASAM3D reaches 24.98 dB PSNR and 0.790 SSIM (best), with LPIPS of 0.234 (second to VGGT's 0.231). For 3D reconstruction accuracy on MipNeRF-360, DASAM3D achieves the best Chamfer Distance (10.10 cm) and F-score (62.3%), improving over DA3 by ~6% on CD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DASAM3D outperforms all six feed-forward 3D reconstruction baselines (DUSt3R, Fast3R, MapAnything, Pi3, VGGT, DA3) on PSNR and SSIM across both DL3DV-10K and MipNeRF-360, with the largest margin over DA3 (its direct backbone) at +0.44 dB PSNR on DL3DV-10K. On MipNeRF-360 LPIPS, VGGT edges out DASAM3D (0.231 vs. 0.234), reflecting VGGT's diverse pretraining advantage in perceptual quality, while DASAM3D leads on PSNR and SSIM. For 3D reconstruction accuracy (Table 1), DASAM3D achieves the best Chamfer Distance (10.10 cm) and F-score (62.3%), beating DA3 (10.76 cm, 58.9%) and VGGT (10.94 cm, 59.7%). In the user-preference study against SAM 3D, DASAM3D is preferred overall (52% vs. 38%) and on all appearance criteria (realism 62%, color fidelity 61%, texture detail 55%), while SAM 3D retains an edge on perceived geometric accuracy (51% vs. 38%), consistent with its role as a dedicated object-segmentation model. The consistent wins over DA3 across both benchmarks validate that the layout-based geometry optimization with frozen colors preserves photorealistic appearance while improving structural accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DASAM3D sits at the intersection of two recent foundation-model lines: DA3 (Depth Anything 3), which produces photorealistic full-scene 3DGS reconstructions from posed or unposed images, and SAM 3D, which generates per-object Gaussian clouds with geometrically coherent but visually unrealistic appearance. The paper departs from prior 3DGS extensions (Mip-Splatting, Scaffold-GS, 2DGS, GaussianPro) that optimize only photometric quality by introducing object-level volumetric geometric priors, and from 2D depth/normal prior methods (DN-Splatter, GaussianRoom, GSRec) by operating on full 3D Gaussian layouts rather than per-pixel depth maps. The key conceptual move is treating SAM 3D's output exclusively as a geometric layout provider in a three-stage differentiable alignment-and-refinement pipeline, without modifying either model's learned parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a persistent appearance-geometry trade-off in 3D reconstruction by showing that two foundation models can be fused at the Gaussian parameter level to yield both photorealistic textures and object-level geometric accuracy, with practical downstream value for 3DGS editing and segmentation pipelines. Limitations include linear runtime scaling with object count in Stage 2, sensitivity to SAM 3D failures on transparent or heavily occluded objects, and untested performance on outdoor scenes with sparse object structure. Open questions include whether joint end-to-end optimization of alignment and geometry stages could further close the gap, and whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes via SAM 2.
