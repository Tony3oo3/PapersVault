## Problem

Existing self-improving multimodal models suffer from two decoupling issues: (i) visual and textual data complexities evolve asynchronously, producing mismatched pairs (e.g., over-simplified diagrams with redundant text), and (ii) task difficulty is fixed by static schedules rather than adapting to the model's evolving capability, leading to either under-challenging or overwhelming the model during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260102/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes geometric diagram images (or mathematical function plots) paired with natural-language questions, and produces a structured output consisting of a step-by-step reasoning trace enclosed in <think>... tags followed by a final answer in <answer>...</answer> tags. Input images are iteratively augmented with auxiliary geometric elements (parallel lines, perpendicular lines, connecting lines) or function graphs via executed Python code, and the corresponding questions are regenerated to match the increased visual complexity, ensuring cross-modal coherence at each iteration.

## Method

SyncLoop operates two interleaved loops. The cross-modal data evolution loop uses GPT-4o as an oracle to decide and execute auxiliary augmentations on geometric diagrams (via Python code rendering), then generates diverse multi-step sub-problems guided by three principles (math constraints, new theorems, backward reasoning) and composes them into challenging questions aligned with the augmented visuals; answers are validated by requiring three independent GPT-4o generations to agree. The data-model co-evolution loop alternates SFT (to establish the reasoning format) and GRPO (with accuracy and format rewards) on the evolved data, using an error-rate filter that runs 32 rollouts per sample and retains only those with error rate > 0.3, thereby dynamically matching training difficulty to the current model's capability at each iteration.

## Results

On MathVista (ALL), SyncLoop-3rd with Qwen2-VL-7B achieves 63.2, matching MGT-PerceReason (63.2) and approaching GPT-4o (63.8) and R1-Onevision-7B (64.1), while using only 0.4k training samples versus 10k–200k for competing methods. On Geo-Sub, the 7B model improves from 40.4 (base) to 50.9 over three iterations, and the 2B model from 28.0 to 38.2. On MathVista geometry reasoning (GEO), the 7B model rises from 40.4 to 52.4. The framework achieves these gains with less than 1% of the training data used by prior reasoning models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncLoop-3rd (7B) achieves 63.2 on MathVista ALL, matching MGT-PerceReason (63.2) and trailing R1-Onevision-7B (64.1) and R1-VL-7B (63.6) by 0.9 and 0.4 points respectively, while using 0.4k samples versus their 10k–120k. Against the base Qwen2-VL-7B (60.0), SyncLoop gains +3.2 on MathVista ALL and +10.5 on Geo-Sub. On Geo-Sub-Aux (Table 9), SyncLoop-3rd (52.4) substantially outperforms OpenVLThinker-Medium (37.8) and OpenVLThinker-Hard (37.5) by 14.6 and 14.9 points, despite using 0.4k data versus their 5k. The 2B variant also shows consistent gains: SyncLoop-3rd (2B) reaches 50.2 on MathVista ALL versus 43.0 for the base model. Overall, SyncLoop approaches closed-source GPT-4o (63.8) performance with an open-source 7B model trained on under 1% of the data used by competing reasoning models.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncLoop extends the iterative SFT+GRPO training paradigm introduced by OpenVLThinker and the role-playing strategy from R1-OneVision, but departs from both by introducing a closed-loop co-evolution mechanism that jointly evolves multimodal data and model capability. Where prior self-improving MLLM methods (e.g., R-CoT, MAVIS, VisPlay) augment visual or textual data in isolation, SyncLoop couples visual complexity (auxiliary lines, function plots) with textual reasoning difficulty in a single generation pipeline. The GRPO-based RL stage follows the DeepSeekMath lineage, but the key architectural departure is the error-rate-based data filtering that dynamically aligns training difficulty with the model's current capability rather than relying on static or manually defined curricula.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that self-improving MLLMs can achieve competitive mathematical reasoning with under 1% of the training data used by prior methods (0.4k–0.6k vs. 5k–100k), suggesting a path toward data-efficient capability growth. However, the approach depends heavily on GPT-4o as an oracle for data generation and is validated primarily on geometry problems, leaving open whether the co-evolution loop generalizes to broader multimodal reasoning domains or scales beyond 7B models. A notable limitation is that the data evolution loop's guiding principles (math constraints, new theorems, backward reasoning) are hand-designed, and the paper does not explore whether the loop converges or degrades over many iterations. The finding that additional SFT in later iterations degrades performance (Table 3) also raises questions about the optimal training schedule for iterative self-improvement.
