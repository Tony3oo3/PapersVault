## Problem

Video diffusion models exhibit 'Motion Entanglement,' where independent motion sources (e.g., camera movement and object motion) are incorrectly coupled in generated videos. The authors trace this to two root causes: data-induced spurious correlations in large-scale video datasets and the reconstruction-based training paradigm, where noisy inputs retain residual motion cues that the model copies rather than inferring kinematically correct dynamics from text. Existing physics-grounded generation work focuses on dynamics (gravity, collisions) but overlooks this fundamental kinematic failure in reference-frame modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes freely generated text prompts (produced by LLMs such as GPT) that specify both scene content and explicit motion relations in a two-clause format (e.g., '{content} The camera slowly orbits...'). During training, the model generates videos from pure Gaussian noise conditioned solely on these prompts, with no real video input. The output is a short video (generated via 3 denoising steps from t=1000, 980, 960) in which camera and object motions should be independently controlled and kinematically disentangled.

## Method

SIFT replaces the standard reconstruction-based fine-tuning with an imagination-based paradigm: the model generates videos from pure noise guided only by text prompts, eliminating the reconstruction shortcut that lets the model copy residual motion from noisy inputs. Instead of pixel-level MSE, the primary supervision is a motion-aware discriminative loss using two heterogeneous classifiers (R3D and SlowFast) that categorize generated videos into four motion types (camera-only, object-only, both, both static), with alternating supervision between classifiers to prevent overfitting to a single inductive bias. A progressive hard-case replay strategy stores misclassified samples in a buffer and gradually increases their participation in the loss (p_s = min(1, s/S_warmup)), stabilizing early training while reinforcing difficult motion patterns later. A lightweight MSE term on real video-text pairs is retained to preserve visual quality, and training is restricted to high-noise timesteps (t ∈ {1000, 980, 960}) with only 3 denoising steps to focus on global motion structure at low computational cost.

## Results

On the 100-prompt motion-disentanglement benchmark, SIFT achieves the highest VLM scores across both backbones: on Wan, SA 4.80 (camera-only) / 4.75 (object-only) and PC 4.93 / 4.72; on CogVideoX, SA 4.89 / 4.38 and PC 4.84 / 4.25. Human evaluation confirms the same trend, with SIFT reaching PC 4.24 (Wan, camera-only) and 4.25 (CogVideoX, camera-only) on a 1–5 scale. In the camera-controlled comparison (Table 2), SIFT scores SA 4.10 / PC 4.30, outperforming CameraCtrl (3.07 / 3.03) and WanMove (3.73 / 3.70). Ablations show that removing the self-imagination initialization, alternating supervision, or progressive hard-case replay each degrade both SA and PC by 0.3–0.85 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIFT outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the base Wan model, SIFT gains +1.22 VLM SA and +0.87 VLM PC (camera-only), and +1.07 / +1.09 (object-only); the SFT variant improves VLM scores but actually *degrades* human PC from 2.75 to 2.43 on object-only, confirming that reconstruction-based fine-tuning fails to teach true motion reasoning. On CogVideoX, SIFT surpasses VideoREPA by +0.36 VLM SA and +0.79 VLM PC (camera-only), while VideoREPA's human PC (4.05) is competitive but its human SA drops to 2.20 on object-only, indicating unstable semantic alignment. In the camera-controlled comparison (Table 2), SIFT (SA 4.10, PC 4.30) beats CameraCtrl (3.07, 3.03) by over 1.0 on both metrics and WanMove (3.73, 3.70) by +0.37 / +0.60, showing that improving the backbone's intrinsic motion prior generalizes even when external trajectory conditions are provided. Human preference studies (Fig. 4) further confirm SIFT is strongly favored over all baselines on both SA and PC dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant reconstruction-based training paradigm of video diffusion models by proposing a self-imagination fine-tuning approach that initializes generation from pure Gaussian noise rather than noised real videos. It explicitly positions itself against dynamics-focused physics-grounded generation (PhysGen, LLM-guided planning) by targeting a complementary kinematic failure—motion entanglement between camera and object motion—and against representation-alignment methods like VideoREPA by providing explicit motion-type discriminative supervision. The motion classifiers (R3D, SlowFast) are repurposed from video recognition as frozen discriminative signals, and the progressive hard-case replay borrows curriculum-learning ideas, but the core contribution is the paradigm shift from pixel-level reconstruction to imagination-driven learning with motion-aware feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard SFT can actually degrade physical commonsense scores below the unmodified baseline (Wan+SFT PC 2.43 vs. 2.75) challenges the common assumption that more targeted fine-tuning data always helps, and the diagnostic experiment showing near-identical loss curves across prompt-mismatched and frame-shuffled inputs provides strong evidence for the reconstruction shortcut hypothesis. The four-way motion taxonomy is acknowledged as coarse—it does not model direction, magnitude, individual trajectories, or temporal transitions—so the approach may struggle with multi-entity or articulated motion beyond what the generalization experiments cover. Open questions include whether richer factorized motion representations (optical flow, object-level trajectories) can replace the categorical labels, and whether the method scales to longer horizons or more complex compositional scenes without classifier reliability degrading.
