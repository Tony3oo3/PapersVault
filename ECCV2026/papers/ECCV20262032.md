## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods for deepfake detection (e.g., LoRA, Effort) assign a uniform residual rank to all layers of a pretrained Vision Transformer, ignoring the empirically observed fact that shallow layers exhibit distinctly low-rank weight structures while middle and deep layers require higher ranks for semantic composition. This one-size-fits-all rank allocation leads to under-parameterization in deep layers and over-parameterization in shallow layers, limiting cross-domain generalization.

## Input / Output

The detector takes 224×224 RGB facial image frames as input, with 32 frames uniformly sampled and cropped from each source video following the DeepfakeBench protocol. It is trained on FaceForensics++ (c23 compression level) and evaluated in a cross-domain setting on four unseen datasets (Celeb-DF, DFDC, DFDCP, DFD), reporting both frame-level and video-level AUC. The model is initialized from CLIP ViT-L/14 pretrained weights, and only the residual components of self-attention linear layers are updated during fine-tuning.

## Method

SRRA decomposes each pretrained self-attention weight matrix via SVD into a frozen principal component and a learnable residual component, where the residual rank is determined adaptively per layer by the stable rank (‖W‖²_F / ‖W‖²_2) scaled by a hyperparameter τ, naturally allocating lower ranks to shallow layers and higher ranks to deeper layers. Two regularizers constrain the residual: the Residual Energy Constraint (REC) enforces total Frobenius-norm preservation and applies nuclear-norm regularization on residual singular values to suppress excessive perturbations, while the Residual Subspace Orthogonalization (RSO) enforces orthogonality solely among residual singular vectors (unlike prior joint principal-residual orthogonality) to promote subspace decoupling and prevent collapse onto a few forgery directions. The total loss combines cross-entropy with averaged REC and RSO terms across all decomposed weight matrices, and the entire framework is backbone-agnostic, validated on both ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14.

## Results

Trained on FaceForensics++ (FF++) and evaluated on four unseen datasets, SRRA achieves an average frame-level AUC of 0.901, surpassing the prior best method Effort (ICML'25) by 5.3% on average. The largest single-dataset gain is 5.8% AUC on DFDC (0.856 vs. 0.798 for Effort), while SRRA also leads on CDF (0.911 vs. 0.901), DFDCP (0.890, where Effort reports no result), and DFD (0.947 vs. 0.923). At the video level, SRRA attains an average AUC of 0.940, outperforming Effort (0.918) by 2.2% and the second-best StA (0.916) by 2.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRRA outperforms all 18 compared baselines on every dataset where a direct comparison is available. Against the closest competitor Effort (ICML'25), SRRA gains 1.0% AUC on CDF, 5.8% on DFDC, and 2.4% on DFD at the frame level, and 1.2–4.6% across all four datasets at the video level. The second-best frame-level method UDD (AAAI'25) is surpassed by 4.2% on CDF, 9.8% on DFDC, 3.4% on DFDCP, and 3.7% on DFD. Among earlier PEFT-based approaches, LoRA (MIPR'23) trails SRRA by 7.3% on CDF and 13.9% on DFDC, while LSDA (CVPR'24) lags by 8.1% on CDF and 6.7% on DFDC. CNN-based baselines (Face X-ray, F3-Net, SRM, RECCE, UCF) are consistently 10–20% below SRRA, confirming the advantage of the ViT + adaptive low-rank fine-tuning paradigm. At the video level, SRRA also beats the strongest video-specific method StA (CVPR'25) by 2.1% on CDF, 4.6% on DFDC, 1.4% on DFDCP, and 1.5% on DFD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) lineage for deepfake detection, building directly on LoRA's freeze-and-learn-low-rank paradigm and on Effort (ICML 2025), which decomposes pretrained weight matrices via SVD into a frozen principal subspace and a learnable residual subspace with orthogonality constraints. The key departure is the observation that prior methods assign a single fixed residual rank to all layers, ignoring the well-documented layer-wise rank heterogeneity in pretrained ViTs (low-rank shallow layers, high-rank deep layers). SRRA replaces the fixed rank with a per-layer allocation derived from the Stable Rank of each weight matrix, and introduces two new regularizers—Residual Energy Constraint (nuclear-norm regularization on residual singular values plus total-energy preservation) and Residual Subspace Orthogonalization (orthogonality enforced only within the residual basis, unlike Effort's joint principal-residual orthogonality)—to prevent overfitting and subspace collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-domain generalization remains the central bottleneck in deepfake detection, and the stable-rank-based adaptive allocation offers a principled, low-overhead mechanism to match each layer's representational capacity to its actual needs, which could transfer to other PEFT settings beyond face forgery. The consistent 5.3% frame-level and 2.2% video-level AUC gains over the strongest baseline (Effort) across four unseen datasets, validated on two backbone sizes, make the approach practically attractive. Open questions include whether the stable-rank heuristic generalizes to non-square weight matrices (e.g., feed-forward layers), whether it extends to non-ViT architectures or video-specific temporal modules, and whether the τ scaling factor (0.1) is dataset- and backbone-independent or requires re-tuning.
