## Problem

Crowd analysis is bottlenecked by the cost of pixel-level mask annotations, forcing reliance on sparse point labels that carry no shape or occupancy information. Learning fine-grained instance-level structure from these ambiguous point cues in dense, heavily occluded scenes remains an open problem, and existing semi-supervised methods either treat individuals as dimensionless points or incur prohibitive inference costs when adapting foundation models like SAM to crowded scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260191/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB crowd images (random-cropped to 256×256, with longer sides capped at 1536 for UCF-QNRF and 2048 for JHU++) along with predicted head-location point prompts from a shared counting backbone. It produces per-instance segmentation masks via a D-dimensional embedding feature map, a crowd count from a density regression head, and filtered pseudo-masks for unlabeled data. The NNEC radius (half the nearest-neighbor point distance) dynamically bounds each instance's spatial extent, and a dual-threshold filtering strategy (low threshold for candidate generation, high threshold for valid mask retention) controls pseudo-mask reliability.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages within a Mean Teacher semi-supervised loop. First, EDP-SAM generates mask supervision by feeding both head-point and SLIC superpixel dual prompts into SAM, then intersecting outputs with Nearest-Neighbor Exclusion Circle (NNEC) regions to suppress cross-instance leakage. Second, XMask reformulates instance segmentation as local semantic segmentation within NNEC-defined circular regions: a discriminative embedding head learns pixel-level feature vectors, and a pull-push hinge loss (Eq. 10) enforces that positive-region pixels cluster near the instance prototype (obtained via differentiable grid sampling at the head point) while negative-region pixels are repelled, with depthwise Gaussian smoothing (kernel 7, σ=3) applied to the embedding map for spatial coherence. Third, the trained segmentation branch produces pseudo-masks that regularize the counting branch through a background penalty loss (suppressing positive responses outside all masks) and a foreground constraint loss (enforcing exactly one activated point and unit aggregate response per valid instance mask), closing a feedback loop between the two tasks.

## Results

On semi-supervised crowd counting (Table 2), the proposed framework achieves the best MAE and MSE across nearly all settings. At the most challenging 5% labeled protocol, it reaches MAE 64.6 / MSE 106.2 on ShTech A, 98.4 / 163.7 on UCF-QNRF, and 77.1 / 289.5 on JHU++, outperforming the strongest baseline P2R by 5.3 MAE and 15.3 MSE on ShTech A. For instance segmentation (Table 1), XMask with NNEC attains IoU@0.5 of 0.333 (ShTech A), 0.297 (UCF-QNRF), and 0.270 (JHU++) at 40% labels, surpassing FastSAM+NNEC by 0.033, 0.064, and 0.069 respectively, while running 5.9× faster (0.17 s vs. 1.01 s per image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For segmentation (IoU@0.5), XMask+NNEC outperforms FastSAM+NNEC by 0.033–0.069 across all three datasets at 40% labels, and surpasses CrowdSAM by 0.121–0.134; S4M is effectively non-functional in this setting (IoU ≈ 0.01). For counting, the proposed mask-constraint framework beats P2R (its direct architectural base) by 1.8–5.3 MAE at 5–10% labels on ShTech A and by 11.5–13.6 MSE on UCF-QNRF at 5%, though at 40% on ShTech A P2R edges out on MAE (55.6 vs. 56.2) while the proposed method still wins on MSE (93.5 vs. 95.0). Against OT-M and DAC, gains are larger: at 5% on UCF-QNRF, the proposed method reduces MAE by 20–22 and MSE by 32–46 relative to those baselines. The weakest baselines (MT, L2R) are outperformed by wide margins across all protocols, with MT showing particularly unstable results (e.g., MAE 319.3 at 10% ShTech A). Overall, the method achieves the best or near-best counting accuracy in 17 of 18 dataset-protocol combinations and the best segmentation IoU in all 9 combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the P2R/P2PNet semi-supervised crowd counting architecture by adding a parallel discriminative embedding mask head, reformulating instance segmentation as local semantic segmentation within NNEC-defined circular regions (drawing on the pull-push embedding paradigm of Brabandere et al.). It departs from SAM-based crowd segmentation approaches (CrowdSAM, FastSAM) by replacing prompt-driven foundation-model inference with a lightweight learned embedding network, and from prior semi-supervised counting methods (OT-M, DAC) by using instance masks rather than points as structured pseudo-labels. The EDP-SAM module, which generates mask annotations from point labels via dual-prompt SAM with nearest-neighbor exclusion, positions the work at the intersection of foundation-model-assisted data generation and classical discriminative learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework bridges crowd counting and instance segmentation in a single end-to-end semi-supervised pipeline, offering a practical 5.9× speedup over FastSAM while achieving higher IoU, which is valuable for deployment in dense-scene monitoring. However, absolute IoU values remain modest (~0.25–0.33), and the counting gains over P2R are incremental (e.g., 64.6 vs. 69.9 MAE on ShTech A at 5%), suggesting the mask-prior benefit is real but bounded. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond head-centric crowd scenes, whether the reliance on manual correction of EDP-SAM masks limits scalability, and whether boundary precision can be improved without reverting to heavier architectures.
