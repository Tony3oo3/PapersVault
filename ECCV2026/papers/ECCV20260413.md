## Problem

Existing scene evolution models either generate full pixel-level video (wasting capacity on appearance irrelevant to kinematics) or sample individual trajectories without explicitly modeling the distribution over possible futures. There is no efficient way to access the probability density of where scene elements might be at future timesteps, nor to localize uncertainty to specific objects and times, which limits interactive planning and uncertainty-aware decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260413/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB start frame and an arbitrarily sparse set of 2D position constraints (element index, timestep, x-y coordinate) as conditioning, with as few as 1% of all trajectory points required. From a shared latent, it produces three outputs: jointly sampled coherent trajectories for up to 64 tracked elements over 32 timesteps, per-element per-timestep probability density heatmaps on a 20×20 spatial grid (decoded in <0.8 ms), and point-wise position estimates. The density heatmaps are non-parametric and can be queried for any element-timestep pair independently.

## Method

GARFIELD learns a factorized spatio-temporal latent z = [z_τ^i] where each component encodes the conditional distribution p(t_τ^i | C) for scene element i at timestep τ. A joint transformer encoder (using DINOv2 image features and 3D Axial RoPE) maps the start frame plus sparse constraints to this latent, trained end-to-end with a 34M-parameter point-wise diffusion decoder (following MAR) that enforces the factorized structure. A separate deterministic density decoder (ViT) is then trained on frozen latents to directly predict grid-cell probabilities via cross-entropy, replacing expensive Monte-Carlo sampling with a single forward pass. A full joint decoder (also trained on frozen latents) samples coherent multi-element trajectories that respect inter-element dependencies. This dual-decoder design from one latent yields 97× faster trajectory sampling than video world models and two orders of magnitude faster density estimation than Monte-Carlo from generative motion models.

## Results

On OpenVid-1M, GARFIELD achieves EPE 0.012, PCK@10% 0.977, PCK@1% 0.821, and FDE 0.015 with a latency of 0.40 s, outperforming all video and motion baselines on every metric. It samples full trajectories 97× faster than CogVideoX (178 s) and 33× faster than Motion-I2V (13.2 s) while achieving lower endpoint error. The density decoder estimates motion distributions in 0.8 ms per point, two orders of magnitude faster than Monte-Carlo sampling from generative motion models. In zero-shot robotic planning, GARFIELD matches Track2Act (0.76 vs 0.77 on BridgeData) without any domain-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GARFIELD outperforms all nine baselines on the primary open-set motion planning benchmark (OpenVid-1M). Against video world models, GARFIELD achieves 37% lower EPE than CogVideoX (0.012 vs 0.019) and 52% lower than LTX (0.012 vs 0.025), while being 97× and 98× faster respectively. Among explicit motion methods, GARFIELD beats Motion-I2V by 33% in EPE (0.012 vs 0.018) and 40% in PCK@1% (0.821 vs 0.587) despite receiving no text prompts, and dominates Track2Act on PCK@1% (0.821 vs 0.100) and FDE (0.015 vs 0.038) while being 10× faster. The gap over FPT is the largest: GARFIELD achieves 88% lower EPE (0.012 vs 0.103) and 5.5× higher PCK@1% (0.821 vs 0.150). In zero-shot robotic planning, GARFIELD matches Track2Act (0.76 vs 0.77 on BridgeData, 0.77 vs 0.75 on RT1) without any robotics-specific training. For pedestrian trajectory prediction, zero-shot GARFIELD is competitive with Social-LSTM on ETH (FDE 1.79 vs 2.35) and HOTEL (FDE 0.71 vs 1.76), and with minimal fine-tuning (<15k steps) approaches Social-GAN, though it remains behind domain-specific methods like Trajectron++ and IDM on SDD.

## Relation to Prior Work

GARFIELD departs from video world models (CogVideoX, LTX) that spend capacity on appearance by modeling scene kinematics directly, and from motion-centric generators (Track2Act, Motion-I2V) that predict dense grids or single trajectory samples by learning a structured spatio-temporal latent that encodes the full distribution over futures. The point-wise decoder architecture follows MAR [23], and the encoder borrows static camera conditioning from FPT [5], but the key conceptual departure is the shared latent representation that simultaneously supports joint trajectory sampling and O(1) deterministic density decoding—something prior generative motion models cannot provide without expensive Monte-Carlo estimation. Domain-specific trajectory predictors (Social-LSTM, Trajectron++) already model element-centric motion but are confined to narrow settings; GARFIELD generalizes this to open-set scenes with spatio-temporally sparse goal conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The density decoder's ability to produce non-parametric motion distributions in a single ViT forward pass (<0.8 ms) opens the door to real-time uncertainty-aware planning and interactive exploration of possible futures, which was previously infeasible with generative sampling. The finding that entropy-based conditioning selection outperforms oracle error-based selection suggests the model's internal uncertainty is a more informative signal for where to intervene than actual prediction error—a useful principle for human-in-the-loop planning. Limitations include 2D image-plane positions only, reliance on pseudo-ground-truth from off-the-shelf trackers, and zero-shot pedestrian prediction still trailing domain-specific methods; open questions include whether the structured latent approach extends to 3D scene dynamics and whether the density decoder's grid-based discretization is sufficient for high-precision control tasks.
