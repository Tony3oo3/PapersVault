## Problem

Current multimodal LLMs excel at short-form video captioning and QA but cannot translate long-form cinematic videos (several to tens of minutes) into detailed, temporally grounded, scene-by-scene scripts. This task is unexplored due to three compounding barriers: no suitable training data with the required granularity, no evaluation framework for hierarchical open-ended script generation, and the prohibitive token cost of autoregressively describing long videos (~4,000 tokens per 2-minute clip).

## Input / Output

The model ingests an untrimmed cinematic video with synchronized audio (ranging from 5 to 30 minutes in the benchmark) and produces a three-level JSON-style script: a meta-level block (title, total duration, character list), a sequence of scene-level records (location, time-of-day, mood), and ordered event-level records each carrying a timestamp, character identity, and at least one content field from {dialogue, action, expression, audio cue}. Events are densely packed at ~14.1 per minute, and the schema strictly decouples atomic elements rather than entangling them in paragraph summaries.

## Method

OmniScript builds on Qwen3-VL-8B and adds a Whisper-large-v3 audio encoder, fusing paired (visual, audio) tokens across multiple LLM layers via an AV-DeepStack module with residual multimodal adapters, enabling repeated cross-modal interaction during deep inference. The decoder follows a CoT paradigm: it first generates an intermediate reasoning trace (plot progression + character-relationship state) before decoding the structured script, enforcing global-local consistency. Training proceeds through four progressive stages—modality alignment (freezing the LLM, training only the audio projector on 1M samples with random frame masking), large-scale multimodal pretraining (2.4M bilingual videos with multi-task objectives), CoT-style SFT (45K annotated videos with subtitle masking), and GRPO-based RL using a temporally segmented reward that performs event-level one-to-one matching between generated and ground-truth scripts, rigorously penalizing fine-grained precision/recall errors that global similarity rewards would mask.

## Results

On the V2S Benchmark (5-minute cinematic clips), OmniScript (8B, audio-visual) achieves an Overall event-level F1 of 37.7 and tIoU@0.1 of 69.3, substantially outperforming the 235B-parameter Qwen3VL-A22B (Overall 33.0, tIoU@0.1 62.0) by +4.7 and +7.3 respectively. At the scene level, OmniScript reaches 52.4 Overall F1 and 74.6 tIoU@0.1. On the challenging 30-minute subset, OmniScript surpasses Seed-2.0-pro, demonstrating superior long-context resilience with a more gradual performance decay than all other evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the largest open-source baseline Qwen3VL-235B-A22B (235B total / 22B active), OmniScript's 8B model gains +4.7 in Overall F1 (37.7 vs 33.0) and +7.3 in tIoU@0.1 (69.3 vs 62.0), demonstrating strong parameter efficiency. Versus Gemini-3-pro, OmniScript wins on Dialogue F1 (72.2 vs 68.8) and temporal localization (tIoU@0.1: 69.3 vs 64.4) but trails slightly on Overall (37.7 vs 38.9), driven by Gemini-3-pro's advantage in Action (37.4 vs 33.7) and Expression (35.4 vs 31.9). Against Seed-2.0-pro, OmniScript surpasses it on tIoU@0.1 (69.3 vs 67.1) and Dialogue (72.2 vs 68.1) but lags on Overall (37.7 vs 40.9) due to Seed-2.0-pro's stronger Character (47.4 vs 39.2) and Action scores. The strongest proprietary model, Gemini-2.5-flash, remains ahead on both Overall (43.6 vs 37.7) and tIoU@0.1 (74.3 vs 69.3), particularly in Audio cues (22.8 vs 11.6), indicating that audio-cue understanding remains the weakest field across all models. Notably, existing omni-input open-source models (Qwen3-Omni, 30B/3B active) perform far worse than their non-omni counterparts, with Overall scores of only 3.9–5.1, highlighting that simply adding an audio pathway without proper pretraining is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Qwen3-VL vision-language backbone (with its DeepStack multi-layer feature injection) by adding an audio pathway via Whisper and a novel AV-DeepStack module that jointly injects paired audio-visual tokens across stacked transformer layers. Conceptually, it departs from prior dense video captioning (which yields sparse, temporally ungrounded summaries) and audio-visual captioning (which produces holistic descriptions without explicit temporal structure) by formulating a strictly hierarchical, field-decoupled script generation task. The training pipeline synthesizes ideas from CoT supervised fine-tuning and GRPO-based reinforcement learning, but introduces a temporally segmented reward that performs event-level one-to-one matching rather than relying on global semantic similarity—a design choice motivated by the need to penalize fine-grained localization and recall errors that global rewards mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The V2S benchmark (19.9 hours, 1.4k scenes, 16.8k events) is the first of its kind for hierarchical, temporally grounded script generation and will likely become a standard evaluation target for long-form video understanding. The subtitle-masking ablation reveals a concerning finding: a 235B open-source model's dialogue F1 collapses from 58.6 to 7.7 when subtitles are hidden, suggesting that much of its apparent 'dialogue understanding' is visual OCR rather than genuine audio-visual reasoning. Open challenges remain: audio cue understanding (F1 ~11–13) and scene-level mood/environment fields are still weak across all models, and performance degrades noticeably beyond 20-minute durations, indicating that robust long-horizon narrative consistency is far from solved.
