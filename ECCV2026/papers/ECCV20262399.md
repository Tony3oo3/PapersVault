## Problem

Open-world 3D scene understanding is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale 3D-text pairs needed for contrastive training and the computational overhead of frozen-VLM approaches that lift 2D capabilities into 3D. Directly projecting 2D open-vocabulary predictions onto point clouds introduces significant geometric misalignment from calibration errors and parallax, producing noisy 3D semantics that degrade downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262399/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, ORACLE-3D consumes only a raw LiDAR point cloud (N×3) and an arbitrary list of category name prompts; no images or 3D annotations are required at deployment. The output is a per-point semantic label assigned by argmax over dot-product similarity between projected point features and frozen text embeddings. During training, the framework additionally ingests synchronized multi-view images and LiDAR-to-camera calibration matrices to generate pseudo-labels and enforce cross-modal alignment, but this image dependency is entirely eliminated at test time.

## Method

ORACLE-3D treats the image modality as a semantic bridge between text and point clouds, avoiding explicit 3D-text pair construction. A 2D foundation model (GLEE with MaskDINO) generates instance-level pseudo-labels on images, which are projected into 3D via calibration matrices to supervise a point cloud segmentation network (MinkUNet, SparseUNet32, or PTv3) through dual-level distillation: logit distillation aligns 3D classification outputs with projected 2D predictions on novel classes, while feature distillation minimizes cosine distance between projected image and point features on spatially and semantically consistent pairs. A Vision-Point Matching (VPM) module with cross-attention (image features as queries, point features as keys/values) learns a binary matching score to filter projection-induced false positives. Training follows a two-stage curriculum—first 50% of iterations pre-train the image branch alone with gradient clipping, then joint multi-modal optimization activates the point branch under a composite loss of four weighted terms (image, point, distillation, VPM) with decoupled learning rates per branch.

## Results

On nuScenes (B12/N3), ORACLE-3D with PTv3 achieves 71.3 hIoU and 66.5 mIoU on novel classes, outperforming the strongest baseline RegionPLC (SparseUNet32) by 7.0 hIoU and 10.5 mIoU on novel classes. On the annotation-free setting, ORACLE-3D reaches 56.9 mIoU, surpassing OpenScene (42.1) by 14.8 and CLIP2Scene (20.8) by 36.1. On ScanNet (B15/N4), the method achieves 75.7 mIoU on novel classes, exceeding RegionPLC by 5.0. On Waymo (B15/N2), ORACLE-3D(PTv3) attains 75.4 mIoU on novel classes, trailing the fully supervised upper bound by only 6.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORACLE-3D consistently outperforms all open-world baselines across every dataset and split. Against the strongest pair-construction baseline RegionPLC [43], ORACLE-3D gains 6.2–21.0 mIoU on novel classes on nuScenes (e.g., 66.5 vs 56.0 with PTv3 on B12/N3) and 3.2–11.6 mIoU on ScanNet, without requiring any explicit point-text pair construction. Against the earlier language-driven method PLA [12], the improvement is dramatic: 66.5 vs 35.4 mIoU on novel classes (B12/N3, nuScenes). Language-agnostic zero-shot methods (3DGenZ [26], 3DTZSL [8], Ovseg-3D [22]) are far behind, with novel-class mIoU below 1.0 on nuScenes, while ORACLE-3D exceeds 64. In the annotation-free setting (Table 4), ORACLE-3D (56.9 mIoU) surpasses OpenScene [28] by 14.8, MSeg Voting [21] by 25.9, and CLIP2Scene [5] by 36.1, confirming that the cross-modal distillation strategy transfers 2D open-vocabulary knowledge to 3D far more effectively than direct projection or frozen-VLM lifting.

## Relation to Prior Work

ORACLE-3D carves a new middle path between two dominant paradigms in open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding: frozen-VLM lifting methods (OpenScene, CLIP2Scene) that are computationally heavy at inference, and pair-construction methods (RegionPLC, PLA) that require expensive 3D-text datasets. Rather than building on either lineage directly, the paper repurposes 2D foundation models (GLEE, Grounding DINO) as automated pseudo-label generators and introduces a cross-modal distillation pipeline (logit + feature) coupled with a Vision-Point Matching module to transfer open-vocabulary semantics into the 3D domain without explicit point-text pairs. The two-stage curriculum (2D warm-up then joint multi-modal optimization) is a training-strategy contribution that distinguishes it from prior single-stage contrastive or distillation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for labor-intensive 3D-text pair construction while still achieving SOTA across four major benchmarks (nuScenes, Waymo, SemanticKITTI, ScanNet), ORACLE-3D lowers the practical barrier to open-vocabulary 3D perception for autonomous driving and robotics teams. The annotation-free result (56.9 mIoU, +14.8 over OpenScene) is particularly impactful, suggesting that 2D foundation-model knowledge can be distilled into 3D networks with minimal supervision. Open questions remain: the method still requires calibrated multi-view images during training (limiting deployment to sensor-rich platforms), the VPM module's contribution is modest (+1.3 mIoU_N), and the authors themselves flag the need for a truly unified 2D+3D framework and extension to occupancy prediction as future directions.
