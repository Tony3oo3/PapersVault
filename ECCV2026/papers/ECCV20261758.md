## Problem

Existing world models for robot navigation are trained to reconstruct future observations conditioned only on factual actions from the dataset, creating a fundamental misalignment with planning, which requires evaluating counterfactual (unobserved) action sequences. Pixel- or feature-level reconstruction introduces unnecessary complexity, compounds errors, and produces representations misaligned with decision-making. The paper asks whether a world model can instead directly predict action consequences in latent space and be trained with counterfactual reasoning from purely offline data.

## Input / Output

The world model takes a current RGB observation, a goal RGB observation, and a sequence of continuous 3D motion actions (x, y, θ) as input, and outputs predicted latent state features plus a scalar compatibility score indicating proximity to the goal. A key design choice is that the goal image can optionally be fed into the observation encoder at the start (goal-conditioned mode), so the entire prediction is framed relative to the target from the first token. The downstream policy model consumes the same current and goal images and autoregressively generates a sequence of discrete action tokens drawn from 64 k-means cluster centers of continuous actions, terminated by a special end token.

## Method

LWM replaces observation reconstruction with latent compatibility prediction: a Vision Transformer encoder (Dino-v2 or Croco-v2) maps observations to patch-level features, a causal Transformer decoder predicts future latent state features conditioned on encoded action tokens, and a separate discrimination head scores compatibility between predicted features and the goal. The critical training mechanism is counterfactual imagination—M_counter action sequences are sampled from *other* trajectories in the offline dataset, the model predicts latent outcomes for each, and an MSE loss is applied against log-mapped Euclidean spatial distances to the ground-truth goal, which makes supervision highly sensitive to small spatial deviations (hard negatives). For policy learning, the trained world model generates pseudo action labels from unlabeled video by selecting the candidate sequence with the highest compatibility score, enabling supervised imitation without action annotations. A subsequent GRPO-style reinforcement step samples novel action sequences from the policy, scores them with the frozen world model as a reward function, and optimizes the policy entirely in latent space—requiring no additional environment interaction or action labels.

## Results

On the LWM dataset, LWM-B-P achieves the lowest position error (PE=1.122) and orientation error (OE=0.125), with action-selection accuracy of 90.60% (ACC3) and 83.80% (ACC5), substantially outperforming NWM (PE=5.710, ACC3=42.43%) and Dino-WM (PE=4.687, ACC3=55.40%). On SCAND, LWM-B-P reduces PE to 0.651 and OE to 0.122, achieving 83.93% ACC3 versus 41.66% for NWM. In real-world deployment, LWM-B-P doubles the navigation success rate to 66.7% (vs. 33.3% for NoMaD and BC) while achieving the lowest LPIPS of 0.396.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LWM-B-P dominates NWM (pixel-space video generation) across all three datasets and all four metrics: on LWM, PE drops from 5.710 to 1.122 (5× reduction) and ACC3 rises from 42.43% to 90.60%, confirming that pixel-level reconstruction does not preserve the spatial/geometric consistency needed for navigation planning. Against Dino-WM (frozen DINOv2 feature prediction), LWM-B-P wins decisively on SCAND (PE 0.651 vs. 3.993; ACC3 83.93% vs. 41.96%) and LWM (PE 1.122 vs. 4.687; ACC3 90.60% vs. 55.40%), though Dino-WM retains a slight edge on RECON ACC3 (84.05% vs. 93.10% for LWM-B-P, where LWM still wins). In real-world robot deployment, LWM-B-P achieves a 66.7% success rate—double that of NoMaD (diffusion policy) and BC (imitation learning), both at 33.3%—while also attaining the lowest LPIPS (0.396 vs. 0.448 and 0.566), indicating more precise goal alignment. The paper also demonstrates zero-shot generalization to indoor environments absent from training data, a capability not reported for any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant world-model paradigm of reconstructing future observations (pixels in NWM, features in Dino-WM) by instead predicting a scalar compatibility score between action-conditioned latent states and a goal, framing world modeling as a discrimination problem rather than a generation problem. It repurposes the GRPO-style reinforcement objective from DeepSeek-R1 [11] to refine autoregressive navigation policies entirely within a frozen latent world model, and follows the autoregressive discrete-token policy formulation of ArMoNa [37] and OpenVLA [22]. The counterfactual training strategy—sampling action sequences from other trajectories to create hard negatives—has no direct precedent in the navigation world-model literature, where training is typically restricted to factual (ground-truth) action sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework eliminates the need for action annotations by using the world model as both a pseudo-label generator (for imitation) and a reward model (for RL), making it practical for real-world robotics where labeled interaction data is scarce and expensive. A key open question is whether the core assumption—that spatial proximity correlates with latent feature similarity—generalizes beyond navigation to manipulation or other embodied tasks where this geometric prior may not hold. The zero-shot indoor generalization (Fig. 4) is promising but demonstrated only qualitatively, and the method has been validated exclusively on wheeled-robot navigation with 3-DoF actions, leaving its applicability to higher-dimensional action spaces untested.
