## Problem

Existing fMRI-to-video reconstruction methods rely on a 'reconstruct-then-describe' paradigm where captions are generated from blurred keyframes, causing semantic drift that degrades reconstruction fidelity. Additionally, these methods struggle to capture low-level spatial details such as the number of subjects and their positional relationships in dynamic scenes. The paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by drawing on the visual dual-stream hypothesis to provide independent semantic and spatial guidance signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261106/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes fMRI time-series data X ∈ R^{T×V} (T time points, V voxels) at 2-second temporal resolution as its sole input and outputs a 6-frame video clip per fMRI sample, a direct semantic caption, and a subject segmentation mask. During inference, the keyframe is decoded via unCLIP, the blurred video stream is decoded following NEURONS, and predicted subject masks are rescaled to [0.5, 1] and multiplied with both the control image and blurred video to emphasize key objects before feeding into the AnimateDiff-based T2V diffusion model.

## Method

CortexVideo introduces a Semantic-Spatial Dual-Anchor decoding strategy with two dedicated encoder streams. The Ventral Stream component (Attentive Semantic Decoder) uses a ViT-based fMRI encoder and Gumbel-Softmax sparse attention to identify which image patches the subject's brain preferentially attends to, then generates captions directly from fMRI via a GPT-2 cross-attention decoder—bypassing keyframe-based captioning to eliminate semantic drift. The Dorsal Stream component introduces a Spatial Perception Loss that supervises the main backbone encoder (initialized from MindEyeV2) with continuous DINO-v3 feature-similarity heatmaps computed from Grounded SAM masks, replacing brittle binary mask fitting with stable regression to learn fine-grained positional relationships. Training is decoupled into two stages: the semantic encoder is trained with adaptive alignment, pre-alignment, and NLL losses, while the main backbone encoder undergoes contrastive pre-alignment followed by multi-task joint training with the spatial loss applied continuously. At inference, the final video is jointly conditioned on the fMRI-derived semantic caption, spatially informed reconstructed keyframes, and a spatially aware blurred video stream.

## Results

On the cc2017 dataset, CortexVideo achieves a 2-way video classification accuracy of 0.874 and 50-way accuracy of 0.275, outperforming the previous best (NEURONS) by 1.1% and 5.0% respectively. For spatio-temporal consistency (CLIP-pcc), CortexVideo scores 0.926, slightly below NEURONS (0.934) but far above NeuroClips (0.738). On guidance signal quality, CortexVideo attains the best CIDEr score (0.240 vs. 0.174 for NEURONS) and the highest subject-mask IoU (0.362) and Dice (0.485), confirming superior semantic and spatial anchoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CortexVideo outperforms NEURONS [59] on both semantic metrics (2-way: 0.874 vs. 0.863; 50-way: 0.275 vs. 0.262) and on all caption-similarity and grounding metrics (CIDEr 0.240 vs. 0.174; IoU 0.362 vs. 0.353; Dice 0.485 vs. 0.474), but trails slightly on CLIP-pcc (0.926 vs. 0.934), which the authors attribute to a deliberate trade-off favoring semantic correctness over temporal smoothness when fMRI-derived semantics conflict with keyframe content. Against NeuroClips [20], CortexVideo leads by a wide margin on every reported metric (2-way +4.0%, 50-way +5.5%, CLIP-pcc +18.8%), and NeuroClips does not report grounding metrics. Compared to MindAnimator [35] and MinD-Video [10], CortexVideo improves 2-way accuracy by 4.4% and 3.5% respectively, and CLIP-pcc by 49.8% and 51.8%, reflecting the advantage of dual-stream guidance over single-pathway decoding. The older baselines (Wen [60], Wang [58], Kupershmidt [30]) are substantially outperformed, with CortexVideo exceeding their best 2-way scores by over 10 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

CortexVideo builds directly on the hierarchical fMRI-to-video decoding paradigm established by NEURONS (which provides the MindEyeV2 backbone, motion projector, and auxiliary task structure) and NeuroClips (which introduced dual semantic/perceptual reconstruction). Its key departure is replacing the 'reconstruct-then-describe' caption pipeline with a direct brain-to-text semantic decoder (adapted from MindSA) and introducing a continuous DINO-v3 feature-similarity heatmap as spatial supervision, both motivated by the visual dual-stream hypothesis. The framework thus repositions fMRI decoding from a purely pixel-fitting problem to one that explicitly separates ventral-stream semantic anchoring from dorsal-stream spatial grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete failure mode (semantic drift from keyframe-derived captions) with a biologically grounded solution, and the ablation confirms both streams contribute independently. However, validation is limited to a single dataset (cc2017, 3 subjects), the CLIP-pcc metric actually drops slightly relative to NEURONS (0.926 vs 0.934), and the method depends on a stack of external pre-trained models (Grounded SAM, DINO-v3, Qwen, unCLIP, AnimateDiff) whose individual contributions to the final quality are not fully disentangled. Open questions include whether the dual-anchor strategy generalizes to other fMRI datasets, longer video sequences, or different temporal resolutions, and whether the spatial perception loss can be made end-to-end rather than relying on offline-constructed heatmaps.
