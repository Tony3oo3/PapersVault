## Problem

Short fMRI acquisitions yield noisy, unreliable functional connectivity (FC) estimates that degrade downstream phenotype prediction. While likelihood-based conditional diffusion can improve reconstruction fidelity to long-scan FC, the paper identifies a fidelity-utility tension: such training attenuates the subtle inter-subject variability that carries phenotype-discriminative signal, producing visually realistic but predictively impoverished connectomes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262450/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short-scan FC matrix (100×100 Pearson correlations from τ minutes, τ ∈ {1,…,10}), a structural connectivity matrix (100×100 log-transformed streamline counts from the SBCI pipeline), and subject covariates (age in months, sex) as conditioning inputs, and produces a 100×100 long-scan-like FC matrix targeting the 20-minute reference. Both short- and long-scan FC are encoded into a shared 4×618 VAE latent space, so the diffusion model operates on compressed latents rather than raw matrices, and the short-scan latent is concatenated with the noisy target at every denoising step rather than being dropped via classifier-free guidance.

## Method

SALD uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone in a latent diffusion framework, with the short-scan latent concatenated to the noisy target (forming 8×618 tokens) and SC/covariates modulating each transformer block via AdaLN-Zero. The key contribution is a reward-guided LoRA fine-tuning stage following DRaFT: gradients propagate through the last K DDIM steps to a frozen phenotype predictor (Kernel Ridge Regression on long-scan FC), while a one-step denoising anchoring term (a KL proxy adapted from DPOK) constrains the LoRA-adapted model to stay close to the pretrained diffusion prior. Rather than supervising against raw phenotype labels—which are too noisy and would push the generator toward unrealistic connectomes—the reward distills predictions from the frozen long-scan predictor, preserving only the explainable phenotype-discriminative component of connectivity variance. LoRA adapters are inserted into attention Q/K/V and output projections, MLP layers, and the final output projection, keeping the generative backbone frozen and limiting adaptation to a small set of low-rank parameters.

## Results

On the ABCD cohort (11,156 scans, Schaefer 100-parcel atlas), SALD achieves the strongest overall reconstruction fidelity to the 20-minute reference FC among all generative baselines, with the largest gains over Conditional DDPM at short scan durations (τ = 1–3 min). For phenotype prediction (NIH Toolbox Total Cognition Composite), SALD recovers more long-scan predictive variance and achieves higher MSE correlation than Conditional DDPM across all durations, with the most pronounced improvements in the low-scan-time regime. The frozen predictor explains only ~25% of phenotype variance from long-scan FC, yet SALD consistently improves over this weak supervision. On differential identifiability, SALD reaches 20.83 at τ = 10 min versus 18.17 for Conditional DDPM, with a widening margin from τ = 2 onward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALD is compared against three generative baselines: Conditional DDPM (the same architecture trained with likelihood-only objectives), BicycleGAN with an MLP backbone, and a Conditional VAE with a U-Net backbone. Against Conditional DDPM, SALD wins on phenotype prediction (MSE, correlation, and recovered predictive variance) at every scan duration, and on differential identifiability from τ = 2 min onward (e.g., 20.83 vs. 18.17 at τ = 10), while matching or slightly exceeding it on reconstruction fidelity (Frobenius distance and Pearson correlation). Against BicycleGAN, SALD achieves substantially more stable and higher phenotype utility across durations, whereas BicycleGAN shows unstable phenotype prediction despite stable reconstruction error. Against the Conditional VAE, SALD is competitive on Frobenius distance (the VAE's explicit reconstruction objective helps) but clearly outperforms it on phenotype prediction and differential identifiability. Relative to real short-scan FC (a non-generative reference), SALD improves phenotype prediction in the short-scan regime (τ ≤ 5 min), indicating that the synthesized connectivity recovers signal lost to estimation noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALD sits at the intersection of latent diffusion for structured data generation (Rombach et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2020) and reward-guided diffusion fine-tuning (DRaFT, Clark et al., 2024; DPOK, Fan et al., 2023), repurposing the DRaFT gradient-through-last-K-steps strategy and DPOK's per-step KL anchoring for a neuroimaging conditional generation task. Unlike prior SC→FC translation work (Honey et al., 2009; Zalesky et al., 2024) or connectome GANs (SFC-GAN, BrainNet-Diff), SALD conditions on short-scan FC as the primary input with SC as a secondary anatomical prior, and explicitly targets preservation of phenotype-discriminative variance rather than reconstruction fidelity or disease classification. The Palette-style concatenation conditioning and DiT backbone follow established image-to-image diffusion design, while the phenotype distillation objective (matching a frozen predictor's output rather than raw labels) is the paper's key methodological departure from standard reward fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a fidelity-utility tension in likelihood-based diffusion for FC synthesis is a useful conceptual contribution: it warns that distributional matching alone can attenuate the very subject-specific variation that makes connectivity scientifically valuable, a concern likely to generalize to other structured-data generation tasks where downstream utility depends on subtle inter-sample differences. The distillation-based reward (matching predictor outputs rather than raw phenotypes) offers a practical recipe for avoiding overfitting to irreducible noise, and the finding that direct phenotype supervision degrades performance is a useful negative result. However, all evidence comes from a single cohort (ABCD), a single atlas (Schaefer 100), and a single processing pipeline (SBCI), so generalization to other populations, acquisition protocols, or brain parcellations remains untested; broader reliability assessment (test-retest, multi-site harmonization) and prospective clinical validation are needed before the approach can inform acquisition design.
