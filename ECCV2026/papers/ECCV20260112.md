## Problem

Existing blind image deblurring methods treat restoration as a static pixel-mapping regression, processing image features indiscriminately without semantic awareness to distinguish valid textures from artifacts. They lack mechanisms to explicitly model semantic groupings, decouple structural frequencies, or capture the continuous, spatially-varying nature of real-world degradations, leading to over-smoothed details and residual blur under complex non-uniform motion blur.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260112/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image as input and produces a deblurred image of the same resolution via a residual prediction (a 3×3 conv head predicts a residual added to the input through an identity shortcut). Additionally, the input is consumed by a frozen CLIP image encoder to extract a global semantic embedding, and by a lightweight CNN head that estimates a dense 2D continuous blur field (bounded by tanh and scaled by a max displacement), both of which condition the deepest bottleneck features. The network operates at three hierarchical scales with base channel width 48 and block counts [6, 6, 12].

## Method

CogSENet is a U-shaped encoder-decoder built from CogSF blocks, each combining a Semantic-Driven State Space Module (SDSSM) and a Bi-Frequency Fusion Block (BFFB). SDSSM replaces uniform 2D selective scan with Gumbel-Softmax differentiable routing that assigns tokens to K semantic clusters, regroups them into semantically contiguous sequences, and performs a prompt-modulated 1D selective scan along this reordered sequence (with alternating spatial flips for non-causal perception), injecting cluster-conditioned prompts into the emission matrix. BFFB explicitly decomposes features via Haar wavelet transforms into low-frequency (processed by a lightweight U-shaped module) and high-frequency (processed by dense convolutions) sub-bands, runs them in parallel with a learned Fourier-domain spectral filter, and adaptively fuses the two pathways with a learnable scalar β. At the deepest bottleneck, a Continuous Blur Field estimated from the input is concatenated with bottleneck features and jointly modulated by a frozen CLIP semantic embedding via pixel-wise cosine-similarity attention with a learnable gating scalar, emulating focal adaptation under spatially non-uniform blur. Training follows a three-stage progressive strategy (128→256→320 patch sizes) with AdamW and cosine annealing.

## Results

On the GoPro deblurring benchmark, CogSENet (8.9M params) achieves 34.72 dB PSNR and 0.9744 SSIM, while the larger Ours+ variant (19.4M) reaches 34.91 dB / 0.9757. On real-world RealBlur-R, the base model attains 41.83 dB / 0.9799 SSIM, and on RealBlur-J it scores 34.54 dB / 0.9473. Cross-task, the model achieves the best SSIM on Rain100H (0.9099), state-of-the-art denoising at σ=50 (28.29 dB / 0.8062 SSIM on BSD68), and the top dehazing score on SOTS (28.72 dB / 0.9692 SSIM), all with substantially fewer parameters than most competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogSENet (8.9M) outperforms the closest SSM-based competitor EVSSM (17.1M) by +0.21 dB on GoPro, +0.19 dB on HIDE, +0.56 dB on RealBlur-R, and +0.20 dB on RealBlur-J, while using roughly half the parameters. Against the frequency-domain method FFTformer (16.6M), the gains are larger: +0.51 dB on GoPro, +0.56 dB on HIDE, +1.72 dB on RealBlur-R, and +1.92 dB on RealBlur-J. The much heavier AdaRevD (152.4M) is surpassed on GoPro (+0.22 dB), RealBlur-R (+5.27 dB), and RealBlur-J (+4.42 dB), though it edges out CogSENet by 0.08 dB on HIDE. On cross-task benchmarks, CogSENet achieves the best SSIM on Rain100H (0.9099 vs. IDT's 0.9080) with PSNR trailing by only 0.02 dB, and it leads all denoising baselines at σ=25 and σ=50 on BSD68. For dehazing on SOTS, CogSENet beats the strongest baseline MPRNet by +0.51 dB PSNR and +0.0020 SSIM, confirming that the semantic-routing and frequency-fusion design generalizes beyond deblurring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SSM-based image restoration line (MambaIR, EVSSM, ALGNet) by replacing uniform 2D selective scanning with Gumbel-Softmax semantic routing that regroups tokens into content-defined clusters before scanning, and by injecting a learned semantic prompt into the emission matrix. It further departs from prior frequency-domain restoration work (FFTformer, MLWNet) by combining explicit wavelet high/low decomposition with an implicit Fourier spectral filter in a single adaptive-fusion block. The continuous blur field estimation with frozen CLIP semantic modulation at the bottleneck is a new combination, though each ingredient (optical-flow-style displacement fields, CLIP image embeddings) is individually well-established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.9M-parameter model achieving SOTA on GoPro, HIDE, and RealBlur while also transferring to deraining, dehazing, and denoising makes this a practically attractive baseline for efficient restoration. However, the reliance on frozen CLIP for semantic guidance is a clear limitation: the authors acknowledge failure under extreme motion blur where semantic cues are destroyed, and the CBF+CLIP ablation shows only modest gains (~0.4 dB) over the base architecture, suggesting the bottleneck modulation is less critical than SDSSM and BFFB. Open questions include whether degradation-robust semantic extractors can replace CLIP, and whether the semantic-routing SSM generalizes to video or higher-resolution settings.
