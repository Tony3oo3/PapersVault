## Problem

Existing deformation networks for dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting encode spatiotemporal features through grids without explicitly modeling each Gaussian's motion pattern or its temporal context across adjacent frames. This causes low-amplitude or transient motions to be under-modeled, leaving pseudo-static foreground residuals in the static background branch and degrading both dynamic scene reconstruction and distractor-free reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262191/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video frames with calibrated camera poses and operates within the DeGauss decoupled framework, where a static Gaussian set and a dynamic Gaussian set are rendered independently and blended via a learned per-pixel composition mask. The output is a novel-view rendering at arbitrary timestamps, with the dynamic branch's Gaussian attributes (position, rotation, scale, opacity, color) modulated by a time-conditioned deformation field. Motion descriptors are computed without gradient propagation and cached as per-Gaussian attributes, enabling efficient reuse during both training and inference.

## Method

MVFusion-GS introduces two plug-in motion-aware modules that operate purely in the deformation network's feature space, requiring no modification to the original deformation heads. The Motion-Variance Guided Refinement (MVG) periodically samples each Gaussian's deformation across K=64 timestamps, computes a 13D global trajectory signature (mean and variance of position displacement, scalar rotation magnitude, and isotropic/anisotropic log-scale variation, plus a weighted motion-intensity score), and maintains a sparsely sampled local variance dictionary for instantaneous motion intensity at arbitrary query times; both are encoded via a lightweight MLP and added to the baseline deformation feature. The MotionFormer Temporal Attention (MFTA) constructs temporal tokens from the deformation feature, predicted deformation, and local motion intensity over a short window (w=5 frames), then applies query-centered cross-attention so the current frame attends to its temporal neighbors. Training proceeds in three stages—base geometry, base deformation, then motion-aware refinement—with the MVG and MFTA branches zero-initialized and trained end-to-end in the final stage.

## Results

On the NeRF On-the-go distractor-free benchmark, MVFusion-GS achieves a mean PSNR of 23.94, SSIM of 0.826, and LPIPS of 0.085, ranking first on all three averaged metrics and achieving the lowest LPIPS on all six individual scenes. On Neu3D dynamic scene reconstruction, the full model reaches a mean PSNR of 32.07 and LPIPS of 0.046, outperforming DeGauss (31.52 / 0.047) and MangoGS (31.89 / 0.052). On RobustNeRF, MVFusion-GS attains a mean PSNR of 29.39 and LPIPS of 0.069, surpassing DeGauss (28.89 / 0.085) by a clear margin on perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is DeGauss [28], the base framework upon which MVFusion-GS builds. On NeRF On-the-go, MVFusion-GS edges out DeGauss in mean PSNR (23.94 vs. 23.91) and SSIM (0.826 vs. 0.819) while substantially reducing LPIPS (0.085 vs. 0.113), indicating cleaner backgrounds with fewer pseudo-static residuals. On Neu3D, the gap widens: MVFusion-GS gains +0.55 dB PSNR and −0.001 LPIPS over DeGauss, and on RobustNeRF it improves PSNR by +0.50 dB and LPIPS by −0.016. Among distractor-handling baselines, SpotlessSplats [20] and DeSplat [30] trail by 0.5–1.4 dB PSNR on NeRF On-the-go, while WildGaussians [9] and the original NeRF On-the-go [19] are further behind (22.16 and 21.67 mean PSNR respectively). On Neu3D, MVFusion-GS outperforms all dynamic-scene baselines including MangoGS [5] (+0.18 dB PSNR), 4DGS [31] (+0.95 dB), K-Planes [3] (+0.44 dB), and SwGINs [22] (+0.95 dB), confirming that the motion-aware refinement generalizes beyond the decoupled DeGauss pipeline (the 4DGS plug-in variant also improves over vanilla 4DGS by +0.54 dB PSNR). RobustNeRF [21] and 3DGS [8] serve as weaker baselines, with RobustNeRF achieving only 19.64 mean PSNR on NeRF On-the-go due to its loss-based suppression strategy lacking explicit dynamic-static decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeGauss decoupled dynamic-static Gaussian framework by adding two motion-aware plug-in modules to its HexPlane-based deformation network: a Motion-Variance Guided Refinement that injects per-Gaussian trajectory statistics (position, rotation, scale variance) as an explicit motion-intensity prior, and a MotionFormer Temporal Attention module that aggregates short-term temporal context via cross-attention over neighboring timesteps. Conceptually, it departs from prior deformation-based Gaussian methods (4DGS, Deformable-GS, SC-GS) that implicitly learn motion through spatio-temporal grids, by making motion awareness an explicit, cached signal in the feature space. The temporal attention design draws on TimeFormer, while the overall plug-in philosophy (no modification to deformation heads) positions it as a drop-in enhancement rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete failure mode in decoupled dynamic-static pipelines—pseudo-static foreground residuals leaking into the background branch when motion is subtle or transient—and shows that explicit motion statistics plus local temporal context can correct the misattribution. The dual-task validation (both dynamic scene reconstruction on Neu3D and distractor-free reconstruction on NeRF On-the-go/RobustNeRF) strengthens the claim that motion awareness benefits both foreground fidelity and background purity simultaneously. However, the method still depends on the baseline deformation field providing reasonable initial motion cues; under heavy occlusion or extremely weak motion evidence, the variance estimates become less discriminative and residual artifacts may persist, leaving open the question of more robust motion-aware Gaussian reassignment.
