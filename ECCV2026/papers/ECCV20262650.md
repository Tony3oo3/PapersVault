## Problem

Existing vision-language Concept Bottleneck Models rely on pre-aligned encoders (e.g., CLIP) and global cosine similarity to map visual features to textual concepts, which obscures fine-grained spatial localization and fails to capture true cross-modal semantic geometry. This leads to three persistent limitations: fixed embedding dependence on a shared linguistic space, global-to-concept inference that blurs regional evidence, and inadequate similarity metrics for region-to-concept relations. The paper asks whether concept alignment can be reformulated as a dynamic, learnable cross-modal transport process rather than a static projection.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images encoded by a frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 (1024-d) and textual concept embeddings from a frozen CLIP text encoder (768-d). Visual patch tokens are projected into a shared 768-d space via a three-layer MLP adapter and clustered into K group prototypes via K-means before transport. At inference, the model outputs a concept activation vector computed from midpoint velocity alignment (no ODE integration or OT solving at test time), which is passed through a linear classifier to yield class logits.

## Method

OTF-CBM replaces similarity-based concept inference with a three-stage geometry-aware pipeline. First, Inverse Optimal Transport learns a data-driven cross-modal cost c_θ (a weighted combination of 18 kernel bases including squared angular distance, dot-product similarity, and multi-scale RBF terms) by fitting unbalanced Sinkhorn plans to annotated region-concept couplings. Second, Unbalanced OT with a CLS-attention-based background penalty computes the transport plan π_VLOT between visual prototypes and concepts, naturally accommodating many-to-one assignments and suppressing unmatched background mass. Third, a conditional velocity field is trained via Flow Matching on straight-line paths sampled from π_VLOT, and at inference concept activations are derived from the negative squared distance between the predicted midpoint velocity (t=0.5) and the ideal displacement toward each concept, justified by the maximal conditional variance of the Schrödinger bridge at the midpoint.

## Results

OTF-CBM achieves top-1 classification accuracy of 85.62% on ImageNet-1K, 89.92% on CUB-200-2011, 90.21% on CIFAR-100, 98.88% on AwA2, and 55.13% on Places365, outperforming the strongest baseline (DOT-CBM) by +1.78%, +4.53%, +4.38%, +2.05%, and +4.48% respectively. On the semantic flow quality metrics, the full IoT+UOT Flow Matching configuration attains VMSE of 1.000, MCR of 0.999, and NPE of 0.021, substantially better than static OT alignment (VMSE 2.467, MCR 0.739, NPE 0.434). Under SAM-based background perturbation (OOD), OTF-CBM retains 82.0% accuracy on CUB and 50.5% on Places365, a +14.5% and +8.4% improvement over DOT-CBM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTF-CBM outperforms all six baselines across every dataset. Against the closest competitor DOT-CBM [44], which also uses optimal transport for concept matching, OTF-CBM gains +1.78% on ImageNet, +4.53% on CUB, +4.38% on CIFAR-100, +2.05% on AwA2, and +4.48% on Places365, with the largest margins on fine-grained (CUB) and scene-centric (Places365) benchmarks. Compared to the LLM-guided LaBo [45], OTF-CBM improves by +2.69% (ImageNet), +8.62% (CUB), +6.11% (CIFAR-100), +1.96% (AwA2), and +9.70% (Places365). Against the original Vanilla-CBM [20], gains range from +6.45% (ImageNet) to +15.73% (Places365). The ablation (Table 5) confirms that each component—classical OT, unbalanced VLOT, inverse-OT cost learning, and semantic flow—contributes incrementally, with the flow mechanism adding the largest single jump (e.g., +2.18% on CUB over IoT+OT alone). Under OOD background perturbation, OTF-CBM's advantage over DOT-CBM widens to +14.5% on CUB and +8.4% on Places365, indicating that the learned transport geometry anchors predictions on object evidence rather than contextual shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model framework (Koh et al., 2020) by replacing its static cosine-similarity concept inference with a two-stage geometric pipeline: Inverse Optimal Transport (Li et al., 2019) learns a data-driven cross-modal cost, and Unbalanced OT (Chizat et al., 2016) handles many-to-one and background correspondences that balanced OT cannot. The closest prior work, DOT-CBM (Xie et al., CVPR 2025), also casts CBM learning as OT between patches and concepts but relies on fixed ground costs and balanced marginals; OTF-CBM departs by learning the cost from component annotations and relaxing mass conservation. The flow-matching component (Lipman et al., 2022) is repurposed from generative modeling: rather than integrating an ODE to reconstruct a target, the learned velocity field is queried at a single midpoint to produce concept activations, a usage not seen in prior FM applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The velocity-based activation mechanism offers a practical efficiency gain—no ODE integration or OT solve at inference—while the OOD robustness results (+14.5% on CUB under background replacement) suggest that explicitly modeling cross-modal geometry reduces reliance on contextual shortcuts. A key open question is whether the IoT cost-learning stage, which requires object-component annotations, scales to open-vocabulary or zero-shot settings where such supervision is unavailable. The authors also raise a broader hypothesis that the fundamental bottleneck in vision-language alignment is the geometric mismatch between heterogeneous encoders, implying that learning structured intra-modal representations before establishing cross-modal transport may be a more reliable paradigm than joint contrastive pre-training.
