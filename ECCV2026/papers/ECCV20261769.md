## Problem

Generating articulated 3D objects from a single static image is fundamentally limited because a resting-state photo provides no explicit motion cues, making kinematic parameters poorly identifiable. Existing methods either regress parameters directly from the static image (ignoring part-level appearance dynamics) or first synthesize visual dynamics and then estimate parameters from them, incurring two-step accumulated errors. Additionally, the scarcity of photorealistic, kinematically annotated data further constrains generalization to complex real-world objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image as input, from which a frozen SAM + GPT-4o pipeline extracts N part masks and a part connectivity graph. For each part, the model conditions on a cropped part image, its 2D bounding box, and the shared base bounding box (all normalized for scale invariance), encoded via DINOv2. The output is a per-part kinematic attribute vector (joint type, motion range, 3D axis location and direction, part and base bounding boxes) plus a 256×256 post-action dynamic image in SDXL VAE latent space; parts are then assembled into a full articulated 3D object via retrieval-based regularization.

## Method

PWM-ArtGen is a 12-layer Diffusion Transformer that jointly denoises kinematic attribute tokens (20×D) and visual latent tokens (N×D from a frozen SDXL VAE) in a single forward pass, conditioned on the static image and part-level structural guidance via AdaLN. The key architectural insight is decoupling the diffusion timesteps for the action and observation branches (t_a and t_o′ sampled independently), which allows the visual branch to be co-trained on action-free data by simply fixing the action timestep to T (pure noise). A Visual Dynamics Regularizer (VDR), inspired by REPA, aligns the first l layers of visual hidden states with DINOv2 patch features of the clean post-action image, biasing the denoising trajectory toward dynamics-consistent representations. To support co-training at scale, the authors construct PM-R (19.7k photorealistic image pairs) by applying Qwen-Image-Edit to PartNet-Mobility renders, and employ a length-proportional interleaving sampler to mix synthetic and real data during training. The final loss combines the coupled ε-prediction objective over both paired and free datasets with the VDR term, applied only during the first 80k steps to facilitate early visual-kinematic alignment.

## Results

On the ACD zero-shot test set (without GT graph/masks), PWM-ArtGen achieves the best reconstruction quality: RS-dgIoU 0.682, AS-dgIoU 0.691, RS-dCD 0.103, and AS-dCD 0.139, outperforming SINGAPO (0.978/0.981/0.110/0.174) and Articulate-Anything (0.757/0.765/0.183/0.286). With GT graph, the gap widens further (RS-dgIoU 0.569 vs. SINGAPO's 0.973). On PartNet-Mobility (in-distribution), PWM-ArtGen matches SINGAPO on graph accuracy (82.47%) and leads on dgIoU (0.462 vs. 0.493), though it trails slightly on dCD (0.045 vs. 0.020). For visual dynamics, the model achieves PSNR 24.33 / SSIM 0.906 / LPIPS 0.093 with only 0.38B parameters and 0.7 s inference, versus DragAPart's 1.43B / 6.9 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SINGAPO (the strongest feedforward baseline), PWM-ArtGen wins decisively on ACD zero-shot: RS-dgIoU improves from 0.978 to 0.682 (−30%), RS-dCD from 0.110 to 0.103, and AS-dCD from 0.174 to 0.139, while matching its graph accuracy at 39.34%. On PartNet-Mobility, the two methods tie on graph accuracy (82.47%) and PWM-ArtGen leads on dgIoU (0.462 vs. 0.493) but trails on dCD (0.045 vs. 0.020), which the authors attribute to mask-quality limitations on synthetic renders. Compared to Articulate-Anything, PWM-ArtGen is superior on all reconstruction metrics (e.g., AS-dCD 0.139 vs. 0.286) while matching its graph accuracy. URDFformer and NAP-ICA are clearly outperformed across every metric on both datasets. For visual dynamics, PWM-ArtGen is slightly behind DragAPart (PSNR 24.33 vs. 24.80) and Puppet-Master (SSIM 0.906 vs. 0.941) but runs ~10× faster (0.7 s vs. 6.9 s) with 4× fewer parameters (0.38B vs. 1.43B), making it a strong efficiency-quality trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

PWM-ArtGen departs from the dominant single-image articulated generation paradigm (SINGAPO, URDFormer, NAP, Articulate-Anything), which regresses kinematic parameters directly from a static image without modeling part-level motion. It adapts the coupled video-action diffusion framework of UWM to this domain, using independent diffusion timesteps to jointly denoise visual dynamics and kinematic attributes, and borrows the representation-alignment idea from REPA to introduce the Visual Dynamics Regularizer. The co-training strategy on the newly curated PM-R dataset (built by enhancing PartNet-Mobility renders with Qwen-Image-Edit) extends the data regime beyond what prior feedforward methods could exploit, since kinematic annotations for real objects remain prohibitively expensive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint modeling of visual dynamics and kinematics provides a principled inductive bias that prior single-image methods lacked, and the co-training mechanism opens a path to scaling with unannotated visual data—a critical bottleneck for articulated object generation. The PM-R dataset (19.7k photorealistic pairs) is a practical resource that bridges the synthetic-to-real gap and could benefit other methods in the space. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond revolute and prismatic joints, how it scales to objects with many parts or nested articulations, and whether the Part Mask Generator (SAM + GPT-4o) can be replaced by a learned module to reduce the current dependency on external VLMs.
