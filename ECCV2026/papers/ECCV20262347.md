## Problem

Existing video editing methods—whether trained or training-free—cannot perform complex non-rigid edits that modify object actions, introduce two-sided interactions, or alter global dynamics while preserving unrelated content. Trained models are hindered by the infeasibility of collecting paired source-target videos with different physical outcomes, and training-free methods are restricted to structure-preserving or one-sided layer-like edits. This paper tackles the open challenge of unconstrained, in-the-wild video editing that changes motion and interactions without unnecessary alterations to the rest of the scene.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (49–81 frames at 832×480, 16 fps), a source text caption, a target text prompt describing the desired edit, and optionally an edited first frame (generated via Gemini 2.5 Flash Image when the edit requires first-frame changes, e.g., object insertion or style shift). It produces a single edited video at the same resolution and frame count. The backbone is the WAN2.1 14B 480p image-to-video flow model, whose first-frame conditioning is critical for maintaining scene and color-palette consistency when the edited video loses spatio-temporal alignment with the source.

## Method

DynaEdit is a training-free, inversion-free editing method that constructs a noise-free ODE path from the source video to the edited video using a pretrained I2V flow model, extending the FlowEdit paradigm to structurally unrestricted edits. It introduces two mechanisms to overcome the low-frequency misalignment and high-frequency jitter that arise when the full noise budget (n_max = N) is used. Similarity Guided Aggregation (SGA) replaces simple velocity averaging by projecting each candidate edit direction to a full edit, scoring its cosine similarity to the source video, and combining directions via a softmax-weighted mixture (temperature τ controls alignment strictness); SGA is applied with n_SGA = 5 only for the first three timesteps. Annealed Noise Correlation (ANC) schedules the noise correlation coefficient a_t to increase linearly from 0 at t = 1 to 1 at t = 0.25, so that consecutive timesteps share increasingly correlated noise, suppressing high-frequency jitter in the later (high-frequency-dominant) steps without sacrificing the diversity of noise realizations needed for low-frequency alignment.

## Results

On a curated 71-tuple evaluation set spanning four edit categories (interactive object insertion, object swapping, motion/action modification, and global effects), DynaEdit is evaluated via VLM-based scoring (Gemini 3 Pro, 1–5 scale) on source-video preservation, target-text adherence, and visual quality. DynaEdit achieves the highest source-preservation score among all methods and matches or exceeds the trained Runway Aleph model on text adherence and visual quality, while significantly outperforming all training-free baselines (FlowEdit, FlowAlign, ODE inversion, SDEdit, I2V sampling). In a user study with 2,400+ responses from 32 participants, DynaEdit is preferred over Aleph, FlowEdit, and I2V sampling on all three criteria, with Aleph being the closest competitor. Additional evaluation on the public VEditBench-Long benchmark is reported in the appendix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaEdit outperforms all five training-free baselines (FlowEdit, FlowAlign, ODE inversion, SDEdit, I2V sampling) across all three VLM-scored dimensions, with the gap being most pronounced on source-video preservation where the baselines either fail to modify dynamics (FlowEdit at n_max=N−1) or introduce severe jitter and low-frequency misalignment (FlowEdit at n_max=N, FlowAlign). Against the only trained baseline, Runway Aleph, DynaEdit is 'typically at least comparable' on VLM scores and is preferred in the user study on all three aspects, though Aleph remains the closest competitor. FlowAlign specifically causes unrelated objects to disappear or fade, while I2V sampling alters the velocity of objects that should remain unchanged. FlowDirector and DynVFX are compared only on their respective narrow categories (object swapping and insertion) in the appendix, where DynaEdit also performs competitively. The ablations confirm that SGA is critical for maintaining camera-motion and object-dynamics alignment, and ANC is essential for eliminating high-frequency jitter that plagues the i.i.d.-noise baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaEdit builds directly on the inversion-free editing paradigm introduced by FlowEdit [25], which constructs a noise-free ODE path between source and target videos using velocity differences from a pretrained flow model. Prior inversion-free methods (FlowEdit, FlowAlign, FlowDirector) were inherently restricted to structure-preserving edits, while inversion-based methods (DynVFX, ODE inversion) required model-specific interventions and still could not support two-sided object interactions. This paper departs from both lines by diagnosing two specific failure modes—low-frequency misalignment from uncontrolled initial noise and high-frequency jitter from uncorrelated timesteps—and introducing targeted fixes (SGA and ANC) that unlock structurally unrestricted editing within the same inversion-free framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free method to handle non-rigid, dynamic video editing (action modification, interactive object insertion, global effects) at a quality comparable to the only existing trained model (Runway Aleph), which is significant given the difficulty of collecting paired training data for such edits. The diagnostic analysis of why naive inversion-free editing fails—separating low-frequency misalignment from high-frequency jitter and attributing each to a distinct noise property—provides a reusable conceptual framework for future flow-based editing work. Key limitations remain: the method inherits the underlying I2V model's physics weaknesses, and it struggles with very large spatio-temporal modifications while simultaneously preserving unaffected regions, leaving a gap for the most extreme edits.
