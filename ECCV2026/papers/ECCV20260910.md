## Problem

Existing proactive tamper localization methods assume a spliced (SP) setting where synthesized regions are composited onto an unchanged background, preserving embedded signals. However, diffusion-based inpainting in its native form produces fully regenerated (FR) images where the entire image undergoes stochastic denoising, disrupting background signals and rendering prior methods ineffective (AUC ≈ 0.5). No prior work in proactive localization has addressed this practical FR setting.

## Input / Output

The embedding phase takes a 256×256 RGB image and a set of predefined anchor vectors (sampled from a Rademacher distribution, normalized to unit norm, with d=192 dimensions matching the DINOv3-ConvNeXt-Small feature layer 1) and produces a perturbed protected image with an ℓ∞ pixel-domain budget of 16/255. At verification, a potentially tampered image is encoded into a 32×32 cosine similarity map, which is then converted to a pixel-level localization mask either via training-free bilinear upsampling with temperature scaling (T=5.0) and sigmoid thresholding, or through a lightweight two-stage shallow mask decoder that learns spatial contiguity priors from synthetic similarity maps.

## Method

APT optimizes a learnable latent perturbation δ in the VAE latent space to drive each spatial feature vector toward a fixed anchor direction, producing dense vector-wise alignment at every 8×8 pixel location rather than a single global embedding. The core insight is semi-fragility: after diffusion inpainting, background regions are reconstructed by conditioning on the original latent (preserving anchor alignment), while synthesized foreground follows the generative model's native distribution and exhibits low cosine similarity with the anchor, creating a measurable alignment disparity that localizes tampering. Two auxiliary mechanisms enforce spatially uniform alignment: a hard negative mining loss that penalizes the top-K worst-aligned spatial vectors, and a noisy perturbation branch that injects Gaussian noise into random latent regions to improve robustness against VAE-induced distortions during background regeneration. The perturbation is optimized via Adam over 150 steps with hinge, hard-negative, PSNR, and LPIPS losses, while all model parameters (VAE, image encoder) remain frozen.

## Results

On COCO validation images (100 samples) under the fully regenerated (FR) inpainting setting with SD-Painter, APT* achieves an IoU of 0.92 and AUC of 0.97, outperforming the strongest baseline WAM (IoU 0.84, AUC 0.95). StableGuard and OmniGuard collapse to near-random AUC (≈0.50 and 0.49 respectively), with StableGuard's IoU dropping to 0.07. APT* also maintains a consistent average IoU of 0.93 across three additional inpainting models (BrushNet, ControlNet, HD-Painter), while baselines follow the same failure pattern. In the spliced (SP) setting, all methods achieve near-perfect AUC (≥0.99), with APT* reaching IoU 0.95 versus 0.99–1.00 for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WAM [24], the strongest baseline, APT* wins by 0.08 IoU (0.92 vs. 0.84) and 0.02 AUC (0.97 vs. 0.95) in the FR setting, while WAM retains a slight edge in SP (IoU 0.99 vs. 0.95). Against OmniGuard [32], APT* achieves a dramatic AUC improvement of 0.48 (0.97 vs. 0.49) in FR, indicating OmniGuard's pixel/frequency-domain signal is entirely erased by background regeneration; in SP, OmniGuard marginally leads (IoU 1.00 vs. 0.95). Against StableGuard [28], APT* outperforms by 0.47 AUC (0.97 vs. 0.50) and 0.85 IoU (0.92 vs. 0.07) in FR, as StableGuard's single global embedding cannot localize spatially under full regeneration; in SP, StableGuard achieves IoU 1.00 versus APT*'s 0.95. Overall, APT* is the only method that maintains strong localization (AUC ≥ 0.97) across both FR and SP settings simultaneously, making it the only framework practical when the tampering type is unknown at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

APT builds directly on the zero-bit watermarking framework of Fernandez et al. (ICASSP 2022), which aligns image features toward a fixed anchor vector in self-supervised latent space, but repurposes this mechanism from binary watermark detection to dense, spatially-resolved tamper localization. It departs from existing proactive localization methods (WAM, OmniGuard, StableGuard, EditGuard) in two key ways: (1) embedding the signal as a vector-wise perturbation in the VAE latent space rather than in the pixel or frequency domain, and (2) targeting the fully regenerated (FR) inpainting setting where the entire image undergoes denoising, rather than the spliced (SP) setting assumed by all prior work. The hard negative mining loss and noisy perturbation branch are novel additions that enforce spatially uniform alignment, addressing the non-uniform feature distribution on the hypersphere that would otherwise degrade localization quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper identifies and systematically demonstrates a critical vulnerability: existing proactive tamper localization methods collapse to near-random performance (AUC ≈ 0.5) under the fully regenerated setting that is the native output of modern diffusion inpainting models, a setting that is arguably more realistic than the spliced assumption. The practical impact is significant for forensic verification pipelines, as verifiers cannot assume the manipulation type at test time. Key limitations include susceptibility to copy-move forgeries within the same protected image (retained alignment causes false negatives) and the risk that a malicious actor could apply APT perturbation to an already-forged image to create a false authentication signal. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video, whether point-of-capture integration (camera hardware) can close the adversarial gap, and whether the 42-second per-image optimization cost is acceptable for high-throughput deployment.
