## Problem

Existing visual attribution methods face a three-way tradeoff: propagation-based approaches are efficient but architecture-specific and biased toward low-level features, perturbation-based methods are causally grounded but computationally expensive and yield only coarse patch-level maps for vision transformers, and learning-based explainers are fast but typically optimize surrogate objectives or distill from heuristic teachers rather than the actual evaluation metrics. No prior method directly optimizes the widely-used Deletion and Insertion metrics in an amortized, end-to-end manner while producing dense pixel-level attributions.

## Input / Output

The explainer takes a 224×224 RGB image and a target class label (defaulting to the top-1 predicted class) and outputs a dense attribution map at the same spatial resolution as the input, where each pixel value reflects its importance to the target class probability. At inference, a single forward pass through the encoder-decoder produces the map (T=0), or optionally T additional gradient steps through the frozen target model refine it per-sample. The explainer architecture is a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone with a DPT-inspired decoder conditioned on a learned class embedding, trained once per target model on ImageNet-1K.

## Method

AHA frames the Deletion and Insertion metrics as a permutation learning problem: since these metrics depend only on the relative ordering of pixel importances, the non-differentiable sorting and top-k selection are replaced with a Gumbel-Sinkhorn relaxation that produces soft permutation matrices and continuous top-k masks, enabling end-to-end backpropagation through the frozen target model. The explainer is trained by perturbing the input with these soft masks and optimizing a weighted combination of differentiable Deletion AUC (minimized) and Insertion AUC (maximized). To make training tractable at high resolution and avoid pixel-level shortcut artifacts, the method partitions the image into a randomized G×G grid of regions, samples S≪K perturbation steps per iteration, applies random grid offsets and size variation, and regularizes against isolated high-amplitude peaks. At test time, the method retains single-pass amortized efficiency while optionally performing a few deterministic gradient refinement steps (T=3 in experiments) that further sharpen attributions toward object boundaries.

## Results

On ImageNet with a ViT-B/16 classifier, AHA (T=0) achieves the best Deletion AUC (0.1381) and Positive AUC (0.1714) among all methods in a single forward pass (0.016 s runtime), outperforming the strongest perturbation baseline TIS (0.1420 / 0.1731). With three test-time refinement steps (T=3), AHA further improves to Deletion 0.1215, Insertion 0.6485, and Neg.-Pos. gap 0.5887, achieving the best overall faithfulness (Ins.-Del. = 0.5271) while remaining ~25× faster than MDA (0.547 s vs. 13.645 s). The same pattern holds for the ViT-B/32 classifier, where AHA T=3 leads on 7 of 9 faithfulness metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest perturbation-based baseline TIS, AHA (T=0) wins on Deletion (0.1381 vs. 0.1420) and Positive (0.1714 vs. 0.1731) while trailing on Insertion (0.6112 vs. 0.6381) and PIC (36.82 vs. 45.57); with T=3 refinement, AHA surpasses TIS on every metric except PIC (42.27 vs. 45.57). Compared to the per-sample metric optimizer MDA, AHA T=3 achieves substantially better Deletion (0.1215 vs. 0.1556), Insertion (0.6485 vs. 0.6112), and ADP (12.47 vs. 48.67) while running ~25× faster (0.547 s vs. 13.645 s). Against propagation-based methods (Grad-CAM, IG, Trans-Att., Bi-Att., LeGrad), AHA dominates on all faithfulness metrics at comparable or faster runtime (0.016 s vs. 0.006–0.254 s), with the largest gaps on Deletion (0.1381 vs. 0.1600–0.2858) and the Neg.-Pos. faithfulness gap (0.5233 vs. 0.2442–0.4828). ViT-CX, the causal patch-level method, is consistently outperformed by AHA on Deletion, Positive, and runtime, confirming that pixel-level attribution provides a meaningful advantage over patch-level explanations for ViTs.

## Relation to Prior Work

AHA unifies three previously separate families of visual attribution—propagation-based, perturbation-based, and learning-based (amortized) explainers—by training an amortized explainer end-to-end against differentiable surrogates of the Deletion and Insertion metrics originally introduced by RISE [46]. The key technical departure is framing the non-differentiable sorting and top-k selection inherent in these metrics as a permutation problem solvable via Gumbel-Sinkhorn optimal transport [42], which prior amortized explainers avoided by relying on surrogate objectives or distillation from heuristic teachers. Unlike per-sample metric-driven methods such as MDA [64] that optimize at the patch level, AHA operates at pixel resolution through region-based permutation and grid augmentation, producing dense attributions even for transformer backbones where prior perturbation methods are confined to the native patch grid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: AHA achieves faithfulness competitive with or superior to per-sample-optimized methods (e.g., MDA) at roughly 850× lower inference cost (0.016 s vs. 13.6 s), making high-quality attribution feasible in throughput-sensitive deployment. The optional test-time refinement (T=3) further narrows the gap to per-sample optimization while remaining an order of magnitude faster, offering a flexible compute-quality dial. Key limitations remain: each new target model requires a full retraining of the explainer, the Deletion/Insertion metrics are still proxy measures sensitive to reference-image choice, and access to a representative training set is assumed—constraining applicability to proprietary or data-scarce settings. Open questions include whether the Gumbel-Sinkhorn relaxation generalizes to other non-differentiable operations in attribution (e.g., Shapley-value computation) and whether the approach extends cleanly to video, multi-modal, or generative-model attribution.
