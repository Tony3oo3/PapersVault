## Problem

Existing methods for 3D hierarchical segmentation either directly lift SAM's view-dependent 2D granularities (causing the same physical entity to receive inconsistent hierarchical ranks across viewpoints) or group segments by absolute physical scale (failing due to high intra-class size variance). Neither approach can discover a stable, view-invariant, and scale-invariant 3D semantic hierarchy that reflects the true compositional structure of a scene, which is essential for multi-granular open-vocabulary understanding and spatial reasoning.

## Input / Output

LEGO takes multi-view RGB images with camera poses (reconstructed via MASt3R-SfM) and optional text queries as input. It produces a tree-structured hierarchical 3D Gaussian segmentation whose depth L is adaptively determined per scene (not fixed at SAM's three granularities), a level-wise language scene graph with both part-whole and spatial-adjacency edges, and per-segment CLIP embeddings. The scene graph supports LLM-driven Chain-of-Retrieval, where complex compositional queries (e.g., 'the handle of the pitcher beside the rolling pin') are resolved by progressively traversing from coarse spatial anchors to fine-grained parts.

## Method

LEGO's core mechanism re-grades volatile multi-view SAM masks into a unified 3D hierarchy: each mask is lifted to 3D, its physical scale is estimated from the point cloud's standard deviation, and local peak detection on the scale histogram of spatially co-visible neighbors discretizes continuous scales into distinct structural levels. For segmentation, each Gaussian primitive carries a decoupled composite identity feature (L independent d-dimensional subspaces, one per level), trained via a contrastive distillation loss that enforces pixel-pair identity constraints from a dense level-wise indicator, with scale-balancing re-weighting, an L2 positive-pair penalty, and unit-hypersphere normalization on both 3D primitives and rendered 2D features to prevent view-dependent shortcuts. Hierarchical segmentation is then performed by top-down recursive HDBSCAN clustering over the decoupled level features. For open-vocabulary grounding, an Optimal View Selection strategy scores views by 3D visibility, 2D coverage, and semantic alignment to extract high-fidelity CLIP embeddings per segment, which are organized into a level-wise scene graph enabling LLM-based spatial reasoning.

## Results

On promptable segmentation, LEGO achieves 94.2% mIoU on NVOS (outperforming SAGA's 92.6% by +1.6%) and 94.2% mIoU on SPIn-NeRF (matching OmniSeg3D's 94.3%). On open-vocabulary understanding, LEGO attains 88.4% localization mAcc and 68.4% segmentation mIoU on LERF-OVS, and 92.6% mAcc and 73.0% mIoU on Mip-NeRF 360, yielding average gains of +4.1%/+4.4% and +3.9%/+3.6% over the strongest baselines respectively. On the newly introduced CoR benchmark for complex spatial reasoning, LEGO reaches 51.6% mIoU, nearly 4× the best baseline (LaGa w/CoR at 14.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEGO outperforms all 21 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On NVOS, it leads SAGA (92.6% mIoU) and SA3D-GS (92.2%) by +1.6% and +2.0% mIoU respectively, while on SPIn-NeRF it essentially ties OmniSeg3D (94.3% vs 94.2% mIoU) and edges out SAGA by +0.8%. The largest margins appear in open-vocabulary tasks: on LERF-OVS, LEGO surpasses LaGa (80.3% mAcc, 64.0% mIoU) by +8.1% mAcc and +4.4% mIoU, and on Mip-NeRF 360 it beats GAGS (88.7% mAcc, 64.5% mIoU) by +3.9% mAcc and +8.5% mIoU. On the CoR benchmark for compositional spatial reasoning, LEGO (51.6% mIoU) dramatically outperforms scene-graph methods BBQ (6.1%) and THGS (9.5%), as well as LaGa augmented with CoR (14.3%), demonstrating that the 3D-consistent hierarchical structure is critical for multi-step relational queries. Notably, LEGO excels specifically on extreme fine-grained sub-part queries (e.g., 'corn', 'onion segments', 'pedal of piano'), where baselines suffer from feature blurring and fail to isolate small entities from their parent objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEGO builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its scene representation and SAM as its multi-granularity segmentation prior, while adopting SAGA's contrastive distillation framework for identity embedding learning. Its key conceptual departure is the 'structural level' paradigm: rather than directly lifting SAM's view-dependent 2D granularities (as in LaGa, THGS) or relying on manual absolute-scale thresholds (as in SAGA, GARFIELD), LEGO re-grades multi-view masks into a unified 3D hierarchy via local peak detection on co-visible mask scales. The decoupled L-subspace feature design and the level-wise dense indicator with hierarchical axioms (monotonicity, recursive inclusion, structural inheritance) are novel architectural choices that prevent cross-level semantic entanglement, and the integration of a level-wise scene graph with LLM-guided Chain-of-Retrieval extends prior object-level scene graph work (BBQ, THGS) into the part-whole domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEGO addresses a fundamental gap in 3D scene understanding: how to obtain a stable, view-invariant semantic hierarchy from inherently perspective-bound 2D segmentation, without manual scale tuning. The decoupled feature space and level-wise supervision offer a reusable recipe for any hierarchical 3D representation, and the scene-graph + LLM pipeline provides a concrete path toward compositional spatial reasoning over 3D scenes. Limitations include dependence on SAM mask quality and MASt3R-SfM geometry, the peak-detection heuristic for level assignment (which may struggle in scenes with highly non-uniform structural depth), and the CoR benchmark's small scale (120 queries across 4 scenes). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or outdoor scenes, whether the number of structural levels can be predicted a priori, and whether the scene-graph reasoning generalizes beyond the tested query types.
