## Problem

Existing GRPO-based RL methods for MLLM perception suffer from low data quality: training samples fail to elicit diverse responses, causing rapid entropy and diversity collapse that severely limits the RL exploration space. Prior entropy-constraint approaches (CLIP-Higher, KL-Cov, Entropy Adv.) attempt to mitigate this from the optimization side but cannot overcome the fundamental limitation of uniform, low-diversity data at its root.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262552/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The MLLM receives an RGB image paired with a task-specific query (a referring expression for REC, class labels for OVD, or a top-view rendering for ISR) and produces a structured response containing a reasoning chain, the final answer (bounding box and/or class label), a free-text image description for synthesizing a new training sample, and a scalar predicted diversity score in [0,1]. The image description is consumed by an external data server that performs foreground-preserving outpainting, so the MLLM's textual output directly drives the next epoch's training data distribution.

## Method

Syn-GRPO couples an asynchronous data server with a modified GRPO workflow. The data server uses BEN2 foreground segmentation to preserve task-relevant objects, then a ControlNet-on-SDXL outpainting model to generate new backgrounds from the MLLM-provided description, keeping bounding-box labels valid while shifting the visual distribution. In the GRPO workflow, the MLLM additionally predicts a new image description and a diversity estimate per rollout; a diversity reward (1 − |predicted − ground-truth variance of accuracy rewards|) supervises the model to recognize which samples elicit diverse responses, and the description with the highest diversity reward is selected for transmission to the data server. A diversity smoothing step (exponential moving average of batch-level diversity) corrects for the drift between the current model's true diversity and the prior model's supervised target. The entire loop is self-evolving: each epoch replaces training images with newly synthesized ones, progressively increasing scene complexity and sustaining high entropy/diversity throughout training.

## Results

On the REC task with Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Syn-GRPO achieves 68.28% accuracy on LISA-Grounding (out-of-domain), 92.15% on RefCOCO, 85.30% on RefCOCO+, and 87.45% on RefCOCOg, outperforming VLM-R1 by 5.14 points on LISA and 1.60 points on RefCOCO. With Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Syn-GRPO reaches 70.14% on LISA and 93.55% on RefCOCO. On the OVD task, Syn-GRPO achieves 23.74 mAP, surpassing VLM-R1 (21.10) and GRPO (18.66) by 2.64 and 5.08 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Syn-GRPO consistently outperforms all five baselines across both model sizes and all four REC benchmarks. Against VLM-R1 (the strongest prior RL method), Syn-GRPO gains +5.14 points on LISA-Grounding and +1.60 on RefCOCO for the 3B model, and +3.43 on LISA and +0.95 on RefCOCO for the 7B model, with the largest gains on out-of-domain data. Compared to vanilla GRPO (DeepSeekMath), Syn-GRPO improves LISA accuracy by +6.04 points (3B) and +4.88 points (7B), while entropy-constrained variants (Entropy Loss, Entropy Adv.) yield only marginal gains of 1–2 points over GRPO. Visionary-R1 and SATORI-R1, which modify the reasoning structure rather than data quality, score 4–7 points below Syn-GRPO on LISA. On the OVD task, Syn-GRPO achieves 23.74 mAP versus VLM-R1's 21.10 and GRPO's 18.66, confirming the advantage generalizes beyond REC. The paper also notes that GRPO+Offline Generation (using GPT-4o descriptions) underperforms Syn-GRPO, validating that the online self-evolving synthesis loop is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (originally from DeepSeekMath) to MLLM perception tasks, building directly on the VLM-R1/Visual-RFT line of work that established RL as effective for visual grounding and detection. It departs from entropy-constrained RL variants (CLIP-Higher, CLIP-Cov, KL-Cov, Entropy Adv.) that attempt to mitigate exploration collapse from the optimization side, arguing instead that the root cause is insufficient data diversity. It also differentiates from prior data-synthesis-for-RL methods (Absolute Zero, R-Zero, PROMPTCOT) by being the first to perform online, image-modality synthesis (via ControlNet-based outpainting) coupled with a diversity reward that trains the MLLM to propose descriptions for high-diversity samples, creating a self-evolving loop absent in offline or text-only synthesis approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck in MLLM RL training—entropy and diversity collapse driven by uniform perception data—offering a practical, decoupled pipeline that adds negligible training overhead. The observation that synthesized images grow in complexity across epochs hints at a path toward long-horizon self-evolving RL, though this is demonstrated over only 5 epochs and 2,000 samples. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models (beyond 7B), whether the occasional 'surreal' generated images genuinely aid reasoning or introduce noise, and whether the diversity reward generalizes to non-perception tasks where verifiable labels are less structured. The reliance on a frozen outpainting model also raises questions about how well the method adapts when the MLLM's capability outgrows the generator's fidelity.
