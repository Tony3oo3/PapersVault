## Problem

Learning-based 4D reconstruction, tracking, and novel-view synthesis are bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale datasets with dense, complete geometric annotations for dynamic scenes. Existing synthetic datasets are either static, single-view, limited to rigid objects, or provide only sparse tracking, while real datasets offer noisy and incomplete geometry. This gap has prevented feed-forward models from reaching the data scale needed to match or surpass optimization-based pipelines on dynamic scene understanding.

## Input / Output

SYN4D provides 4.7K multiview clips (8 cameras, ~300 frames each, 1.4M total frames) where each frame carries RGB, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, depth, instance segmentation, and SMPL-X human pose. The defining capability is the dynamic point map: given any pixel in any frame, the dataset yields its 3D position at any time and its 2D projection in any other camera. For the geometry-aware diffusion model, input is a source video plus its point maps and a target camera trajectory; output is the rendered novel-view video together with geometrically consistent point maps at the new viewpoint.

## Method

SYN4D is constructed procedurally in Unreal Engine 5 by combining 30 purchased 3D environments, 1,674 quality-filtered animated objects from Objaverse, and 585 human assets from BEDLAM2, with diverse camera motions (orbit, dolly, tracking, static) across 8 synchronized cameras per clip. The key technical contribution is an efficient dense-tracking representation: rather than storing the full O(HWT³C²) dynamic point map tensor, each pixel is encoded by a face index and three barycentric coordinates on the animated mesh, reducing storage to O(HWTC + VT) while enabling exact 3D position queries at arbitrary times via linear combination of vertex trajectories. For geometry-aware novel-view synthesis, the authors extend ReCamMaster by spatially concatenating video latents with a DPM encoder's latents, reusing the pretrained diffusion prior without new parameters to jointly generate appearance and geometry. Empirically, co-training the 4RC feed-forward reconstructor with SYN4D yields consistent gains in 3D tracking (e.g., APD 58→74 on dense Soviet), camera pose, depth, and multi-view reconstruction, while fine-tuning MA-HMR with SYN4D improves human pose metrics across 3DPW, Hi4D, and CHI3D.

## Results

Co-training the 4RC 4D reconstruction model with SYN4D yields substantial gains in 3D tracking: APD improves from 58.35 to 74.46 on Soviet and from 79.07 to 88.79 on Warehouse, while EPE drops from 3.8458 to 1.8934 (Soviet) and 0.3302 to 0.1915 (Warehouse). Camera pose estimation on Sintel sees ATE halved (0.144→0.076), and video depth Rel on Sintel drops from 0.311 to 0.211 under scale alignment. For human pose estimation, adding SYN4D to MA-HMR training reduces MPJPE from 58.8 to 57.7 mm on Hi4D and from 63.2 to 62.5 mm on 3DPW. The geometry-aware NVS model trained on SYN4D outperforms the Kubric-trained variant on all visual and geometric metrics (CLIP-V 0.740 vs 0.643, FVD 452 vs 631).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two baselines: 4RC (a feed-forward 4D reconstruction model) and MA-HMR (a multi-person human mesh recovery model). Against 4RC, co-training with SYN4D consistently wins on every evaluated task without any architectural change: 3D tracking APD improves by 16.1 points on Soviet and 9.7 on Warehouse; camera pose ATE on Sintel drops by 47% (0.144→0.076); multi-view reconstruction NC on 7-Scenes rises from 0.912 to 0.924; and depth Rel on Sintel improves by 32% under scale alignment. The gains are most dramatic on dense 3D tracking, where EPE is nearly halved, suggesting that the dense multiview tracking annotations in SYN4D are the primary driver. Against MA-HMR, adding SYN4D (MA-HMR+SYN4D) beats both the original MA-HMR and the continued-training control (MA-HMR+Cont) on all three metrics (MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, PVE) across all three benchmarks (Hi4D, CHI3D, 3DPW), with the largest gain on Hi4D PVE (73.6→72.2 mm). The continued-training control confirms that the improvement is due to SYN4D's content rather than additional training epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SYN4D extends the procedural synthetic dataset paradigm established by Kubric (Blender-based, rigid objects) to a more realistic Unreal Engine 5 pipeline, combining 1,674 animated assets from Objaverse with 585 human characters from BEDLAM2. Its key technical departure is the barycentric-coordinate-plus-mesh representation for dense multiview 3D tracking, which overcomes the storage infeasibility that limited prior datasets like PointOdyssey to sparse points and Kubric to rigid-body tracking. For the generative component, the paper extends ReCamMaster's camera-controlled video diffusion to jointly synthesize novel-view geometry (point maps), a capability no prior camera-retargeting method provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SYN4D addresses what the authors identify as a critical bottleneck: the absence of large-scale, publicly available datasets with dense, complete geometric annotations for dynamic multiview scenes. At 4.7K clips and 1.4M frames with full 3D tracking, depth, camera poses, and SMPL-X annotations, it is substantially larger than any comparable existing resource and is likely to become a standard co-training set for 4D reconstruction and tracking research. Open questions include the synthetic-to-real generalization gap (all experiments on real data show only moderate gains), the limited environmental diversity (30 purchased scenes), and whether the geometry-aware NVS benchmark (280 unseen pairs) is sufficiently challenging to drive progress.
