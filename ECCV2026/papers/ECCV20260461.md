## Problem

Scoring-based end-to-end planners rely on static trajectory vocabularies, but prior work limited these to a few thousand anchors due to computational constraints, leaving it unclear whether this coarse discretization is an intrinsic limitation or merely a scaling bottleneck. The paper asks whether dynamic trajectory generation (regression, diffusion) is fundamentally necessary, or whether a sufficiently dense static vocabulary with efficient scoring can match or exceed dynamic methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260461/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three camera views (left, front, right) at 256×512 resolution along with ego vehicle status (position, velocity, etc.), encoded by a ResNet-34 backbone without explicit BEV construction. The output is a single ego trajectory of 8 waypoints over a 4-second horizon (0.5 s intervals), selected from a combinatorial vocabulary of 262,144 candidates (1024 geometric paths × 256 velocity profiles) via a two-stage scoring pipeline that ultimately scores only 400 composed trajectories.

## Method

SparseDriveV2 factorizes each spatiotemporal trajectory into a geometric path (resampled at 1 m intervals up to 50 m) and a velocity profile (scalar speeds at 0.5 s intervals over 4 s), then constructs a super-dense vocabulary by composing every path anchor with every velocity anchor (32× denser than the 8192 anchors in prior methods). To score this combinatorial set efficiently, a two-stage strategy is used: first, coarse factorized scoring independently scores all 1024 paths (via deformable aggregation along path geometry) and all 256 velocities (via cross-attention) to select top-128 paths and top-64 velocities; second, fine-grained scoring is applied to the 400 composed trajectories, with a trajectory re-conditioning step (deformable aggregation with scene features) to capture spatiotemporal dependencies between path and velocity. Training uses soft classification losses (cross-entropy against distance-weighted targets) at each scoring stage plus binary cross-entropy metric supervision distilled from a rule-based teacher for safety, progress, comfort, and rule compliance.

## Results

On NAVSIM v1 (navtest), SparseDriveV2 achieves a PDMS of 92.0 with a lightweight ResNet-34 backbone (21.8M parameters), surpassing all prior scoring-based and dynamic-generation methods. On NAVSIM v2, it reaches an EPDMS of 90.1, outperforming DiffusionDriveV2 by 2.6 points. On the closed-loop Bench2Drive benchmark, SparseDriveV2 attains a Driving Score of 89.15 and a Success Rate of 70.00%, setting a new record among scoring-based planners. The method scales the trajectory vocabulary to 262,144 anchors (1024 paths × 256 velocity profiles), 32× denser than the 8192 anchors used by prior scoring-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseDriveV2 (92.0 PDMS) outperforms all ResNet-34 baselines on NAVSIM v1, beating the strongest dynamic-generation method ipad (91.7) by 0.3 and DiffusionDriveV2 (91.2) by 0.8. Notably, it also surpasses Hydra-MDP and GoalFlow equipped with the much larger V2-99 backbone (96.9M params, 90.3 PDMS each) by 1.7 points, despite using only 21.8M parameters. On NAVSIM v2, SparseDriveV2 achieves 90.1 EPDMS, exceeding DiffusionDriveV2 (87.5) by 2.6 and DriveSuprim with V2-99 (77.9) by a wide margin, with the largest gain in the Ego Progress metric (91.1 vs. 90.6). On Bench2Drive closed-loop evaluation, SparseDriveV2 leads with a Driving Score of 89.15 and Success Rate of 70.00%, surpassing HiP-AD (86.77 / 69.09%) and SimLingo (86.02 / 67.27%), while also achieving the best multi-ability mean score of 67.67. The method does not lose to any listed baseline on any primary metric across all three benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scoring-based planning lineage (VADv2, Hydra-MDP, DriveSuprim) by identifying vocabulary density as the key bottleneck and proposing a factorized representation (geometric paths × velocity profiles) to achieve 32× denser trajectory coverage without dynamic generation. It explicitly departs from the growing trend toward dynamic trajectory generation (DiffusionDrive, GoalFlow, ipad) by demonstrating that the perceived limitations of static vocabularies stem from insufficient density rather than an inherent architectural deficiency. The two-stage scoring strategy (coarse factorized scoring followed by fine-grained trajectory scoring) is a novel computational mechanism that makes super-dense vocabulary scoring tractable, building on the deformable attention and cross-attention interaction patterns established in prior end-to-end driving work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a purely static, scoring-based planner with a lightweight ResNet-34 backbone can outperform dynamic generation methods (DiffusionDriveV2, ipad) on both NAVSIM and Bench2Drive challenges the field's working assumption that dynamic trajectory generation is necessary for fine-grained action-space coverage. The factorized vocabulary construction and progressive filtering strategy offer a practical, computationally efficient recipe that other scoring-based methods can adopt. Open questions include whether the factorization into paths and velocities is the optimal decomposition, whether further scaling beyond 262K trajectories yields diminishing returns, and whether the approach generalizes to reactive or multi-agent driving settings where the static vocabulary may be less adequate.
