## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting degrades severely under sparse views (3–9 images), producing floaters, splat collapse, and view-dependent artifacts, yet existing remedies (external depth priors, heuristic regularization, stochastic Gaussian dropout) treat symptoms without explaining why sparse supervision causes failure. This paper reveals through optimization manifold analysis that sparse training produces directionally anisotropic loss surfaces with drastically different sensitivities along three geometric fragility axes—depth-ordering swaps, projection ill-conditioning, and alpha-blending instability—explaining why standard optimization converges to view-specific sharp minima that fail on novel viewpoints.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse RGB images (3/6/9 views on LLFF at 378×504, 3 views on DTU at 4× downsampled resolution, 12/24 views on Mip-NeRF360 at 8× downsampled resolution) along with their camera poses, initialized via SfM. It outputs a standard 3DGS representation (N anisotropic Gaussians with position, scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and SH coefficients) that renders novel views at real-time speed with zero additional inference cost—the probing losses are training-only and the final model is indistinguishable from vanilla 3DGS at render time.

## Method

StableGS steers optimization from anisotropic to isotropic loss surfaces via two complementary geometric probes. Attribute-Space Probing (ASP) estimates per-attribute Hessian curvature using Hutchinson's trace estimator (B=10 random probes, updated every 100 iterations), then perturbs all N Gaussians simultaneously along Hessian-informed directions—depth-biased position shifts (γ=0.7 weight on the viewing-ray component), SO(3) tangent-space rotations scaled by projection condition number, log-space scale perturbations, logit-space opacity flips, and frequency-weighted SH probes—and minimizes the rendering loss at the single probed configuration. Viewpoint-Space Probing (VSP) constructs one composite novel pose per training camera by combining occlusion-boundary rotation (from rendered depth gradients), Slerp interpolation/extrapolation beyond the training convex hull, and parallax translation, then enforces L1+SSIM consistency between base and probed renderings without ground truth. The total loss L_recon + 1.0·L_ASP + 0.5·L_VSP reshapes the manifold, reducing the anisotropy index by 74% and yielding +0.33/+0.64/+0.25 dB PSNR over SOTA on LLFF/DTU/Mip-NeRF360 at 1.3× training time.

## Results

StableGS achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all three benchmarks: 21.19 dB on LLFF 3-view (vs. 20.76 for DropGaussian), 20.86 dB on DTU 3-view (vs. 20.22 for DropoutGS), and 24.38 dB on Mip-NeRF360 24-view (vs. 24.13 for DropGaussian). Headline improvements over the strongest baseline are +0.33 dB (LLFF), +0.64 dB (DTU), and +0.25 dB (Mip-NeRF360). The method also reduces the optimization manifold anisotropy index by 74% compared to vanilla 3DGS, and at the same 9.7-min wall-clock budget as vanilla 3DGS, StableGS already reaches 21.16 dB—exceeding every baseline's fully converged result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableGS outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset and view setting. Against the strongest 3DGS-based competitors, it beats DropGaussian by +0.43 dB (LLFF 3-view) and +0.25 dB (Mip-NeRF360 24-view), and DropoutGS by +0.64 dB on DTU. Depth-prior methods (DNGaussian, FSGS) are surpassed by +2.07 dB on LLFF 3-view and +1.95 dB on DTU without any external monocular depth supervision. Co-regularization (CoR-GS) and dropout-based (DropoutGS) approaches trail by 0.74 dB and 0.43 dB respectively on LLFF 3-view. On Mip-NeRF360, StableGS leads DropGaussian by 0.24 dB (12-view) and 0.25 dB (24-view), and CoR-GS by 0.46 dB (12-view). All NeRF-based baselines (Mip-NeRF, DietNeRF, RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, SparseNeRF) are outperformed by 1.5–6.2 dB on LLFF, confirming the advantage of the Gaussian splatting representation combined with manifold-guided probing.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableGS builds directly on the 3DGS representation and rasterizer pipeline, but departs from the dominant sparse-view 3DGS paradigm of injecting external supervision (monocular depth priors in DNGaussian/FSGS, semantic encoders in NeRF-based methods) or stochastic structural regularization (DropoutGS, DropGaussian). Conceptually, it draws on Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and optimization landscape analysis, but explicitly rejects SAM's uniform parameter-space smoothing in favor of domain-specific geometric probes that target the splatting operator's three identified fragility axes (depth-sorting, projection conditioning, alpha-blending). The Hessian trace estimation via Hutchinson's estimator and Power Iteration are borrowed from numerical linear algebra and second-order optimization literature, yet the paper stresses it never forms or inverts the Hessian—using these only as cheap diagnostics to place probes, making it orthogonal to second-order solvers like 3DGS².

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic diagnosis of *why* sparse-view 3DGS fails, identifying three geometric fragility axes with measurable curvature signatures, rather than treating degradation as a data-scarcity problem to be patched with external priors. The zero-inference-cost design (training-only probes, standard 3DGS output) makes it immediately deployable and composable with renderer-side advances like Mip-Splatting or 2D-GS. Open questions include whether the Hessian-based diagnostics scale efficiently to very large scenes (tens of millions of Gaussians), whether the fixed probe directions (depth-biased γ=0.7, condition-number-scaled rotation) are truly optimal or dataset-dependent, and whether the isotropy objective generalizes to other differentiable rendering operators beyond 3DGS.
