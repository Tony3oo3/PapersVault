## Problem

Fixed-frequency action chunking in robotic imitation learning forces a trade-off: low-frequency chunks capture long-horizon dependencies but lack temporal resolution for fine-grained closed-loop control, while high-frequency chunks enable precise adjustments but cannot model long-horizon temporal structure. No existing method jointly addresses both requirements within a single policy architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a hierarchical observation history consisting of M frequency-sampled sequences of visual observations (2D RGB images or 3D point clouds) and proprioceptive states (joint positions, velocities, gripper status), each of length L_h. It outputs a single hierarchical action chunk containing action sequences at all M frequencies simultaneously (total length M·L_c), from which an entropy-guided mechanism selects one frequency's sub-chunk for actual execution at each decision step.

## Method

HiPolicy is a diffusion-based policy (1D-CNN U-Net) that jointly denoises concatenated noisy action chunks at M different frequencies within a single forward pass. Hierarchical FiLM conditioning maps each frequency's observation features to the corresponding frequency's action features, while a cross-attention module with a CLS token fuses global information across frequencies. At inference, the policy samples N action chunks in parallel, computes the Shannon entropy of the predicted Gaussian action distributions, and adaptively selects the execution frequency via preset entropy thresholds—low entropy triggers high-frequency execution for precise closed-loop control, while high entropy triggers low-frequency execution for faster, high-level planning. This design is plug-and-play, integrating directly into existing 2D (Diffusion Policy) and 3D (DP3) generative policies without modifying their core diffusion training procedure.

## Results

On the RoboTwin 1.0 and 2.0 simulation benchmarks (21 tasks total), DP+HiPolicy achieves a 60% average success rate versus 37% for DP (62% relative improvement), while DP3+HiPolicy reaches 59% versus 41% for DP3 (44% relative improvement). The gains are most dramatic on precise tasks (e.g., Block hammer beat: 0→67% for DP, 57→94% for DP3) and super-long-horizon tasks (e.g., Put bottles dustbin: 38→81% for DP). On 8 real-world Franka Panda tasks, DP+HiPolicy improves average success rate from 60% to 85% (42% relative) while reducing execution steps by 14%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiPolicy is evaluated as a plug-in module on two generative policy backbones: 2D Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3). Against DP, HiPolicy wins on 20 of 21 simulation tasks, with the largest gains on precise tasks (e.g., Block hammer beat: 0→67%, Stamp seal: 5→19%) and long-horizon tasks (Put bottles dustbin: 38→81%), yielding a 62% relative improvement in total average success rate (37%→60%). Against DP3, HiPolicy wins on 18 of 21 tasks, with standout improvements on Block hammer beat (57→94%) and Stamp seal (15→74%), for a 44% relative improvement (41%→59%). The one notable exception is Pick apple messy, where DP3+HiPolicy (3%) underperforms both DP+HiPolicy (81%) and DP3 alone (10%), suggesting the 3D representation may introduce noise on cluttered scenes. On real-world Franka Panda tasks, DP+HiPolicy improves average success rate from 60% to 85% and reduces execution steps from 129 to 111, with the most dramatic gain on Close microwave door (0%→100%), where the non-Markovian pause before latching is captured by the low-frequency component.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiPolicy builds directly on Diffusion Policy and 3D Diffusion Policy as its generative backbone, extending them from single-frequency action chunking to a hierarchical multi-frequency formulation where both observation histories and action sequences are jointly encoded at multiple temporal resolutions. It departs from prior hierarchical manipulation policies (H³DP, Reactive Diffusion Policy, CARP, Dense Policy) that introduce hierarchy only in the perceptual or autoregressive decoding stages; HiPolicy instead applies temporal hierarchy symmetrically to both input and output, using FiLM conditioning and cross-attention to fuse features across frequencies within a single U-Net. The entropy-guided adaptive execution mechanism is a novel inference-time strategy that has no direct analogue in prior action-chunking literature, drawing loosely on the motor-control observation that humans switch between slow planning and fast reactive control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and widely felt trade-off in action-chunking policies—choosing between long-horizon coherence and fine-grained closed-loop reactivity—and shows that a single unified architecture can serve both, with the added practical benefit of 25% faster execution via entropy-guided frequency selection. Its plug-and-play integration into existing 2D (DP) and 3D (DP3) policies makes it immediately applicable to a broad range of manipulation stacks. However, evaluation is limited to relatively small models and curated benchmark subsets; the authors explicitly note that integration with large vision-language-action frameworks remains unexplored, and whether the multi-frequency structure retains its advantage at VLA scale is an open question.
