## Problem

End-to-end video prediction models that forecast future frames directly in VAE latent space entangle semantic structure and fine-grained appearance within a single representation, forcing the model to simultaneously infer scene dynamics and render photorealistic details. This entanglement causes temporal semantic inconsistencies (object identity drift, structural degradation, flickering) and slows convergence. The paper asks whether semantic forecasting and visual synthesis can be explicitly separated into distinct stages while still producing coherent, photorealistic predictions.

## Input / Output

The method takes M=13 context frames at 432×768 resolution from a driving video sequence and predicts K−M=14 future RGB frames. Internally, context frames are compressed by a WAN2.1 causal 3D VAE (8×8×4 spatial-temporal downsampling) into 7 latent frames of 54×96, while DINOv2 ViT-B/14 features are extracted per frame and projected to 1152 channels via PCA. The semantic prediction stage operates at the VAE's temporal subsampling rate, and the final output is decoded back to pixel space by the VAE decoder.

## Method

Re2Pix decomposes video prediction into two stages: (1) a lightweight masked transformer autoregressively predicts future DINOv2 semantic features frame-by-frame in a frozen VFM space, and (2) a ~800M-parameter DiT-based latent diffusion model (following Cosmos-Predict with AdaLN-LoRA and 3D RoPE, but without cross-attention) generates future VAE latents conditioned on both context and predicted semantic features. Semantic features are fused with VAE latents via early channel-wise summation at the input level after independent patchification, avoiding extra tokens or parameters. To bridge the train-test mismatch between ground-truth features (training) and autoregressively predicted features (inference), the authors introduce nested dropout—stochastically retaining only the first c∈{8,…,1152} PCA channels during training—and mixed supervision, which feeds 10% of batches predicted features and 90% ground-truth features. This hierarchical design yields up to 7× faster convergence on FID/FVD and 14× on segmentation mIoU compared to a matched diffusion baseline.

## Results

On Cityscapes (single-dataset training), Re2Pix achieves mIoU(A) 63.53, IoU(M) 62.29, FID 9.90, and FVD 52.66, improving over the strongest baseline (Baseline-Large, 1.5B params) by +1.84 mIoU(A), +2.64 IoU(M), −2.09 FID, and −4.15 FVD. When trained on extended data (Cityscapes + nuScenes + CoVLA), Re2Pix reaches mIoU(A) 64.63 and FID 9.29 on Cityscapes, and demonstrates zero-shot transfer to KITTI (FID 15.94, FVD 61.73). Training convergence is accelerated by 7× for FID/FVD and 14× for segmentation mIoU relative to the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Re2Pix outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against REPA [41] (representation alignment via distillation), Re2Pix gains +2.08 mIoU(A), +2.66 IoU(M), −2.44 FID, and −2.49 FVD, showing that explicit hierarchical forecasting surpasses implicit feature alignment. Against VideoREPA [86] (relational alignment for video), the margin is even larger: +2.55 mIoU(A), +3.68 IoU(M), −3.05 FID, and −6.37 FVD. Against the larger Baseline-Large (1.5B params vs. Re2Pix's 1.1B), Re2Pix still wins by +1.84 mIoU(A) and −2.09 FID, confirming gains come from architecture rather than scale. In the extended-data setting (Table 3), Re2Pix beats the fine-tuned Vista [18] on Cityscapes (FID 9.29 vs. 12.17; FVD 49.03 vs. 84.14) and nuScenes (FID 18.96 vs. 21.06), but Vista retains an advantage on zero-shot KITTI (FID 7.95 vs. 15.94). Re2Pix is competitive with the much larger internet-pretrained Cosmos-Predict-2 [48], trailing by only 1.06 FID on Cityscapes (9.29 vs. 7.74) and 0.78 FVD (49.03 vs. 46.22), while Cosmos-Predict-2 was pretrained on orders of magnitude more data and compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

Re2Pix builds on two prior works: the Cosmos-Predict diffusion transformer architecture for latent video generation, and the DINO-Foresight masked transformer for autoregressive VFM feature prediction. Its key conceptual departure is from alignment-based methods (REPA, VideoREPA) that inject semantic structure into diffusion via auxiliary distillation losses within a single latent space; instead, Re2Pix treats forecasted VFM features as an explicit intermediate generative variable, creating a true two-stage hierarchy. It also extends the semantic future prediction line (DINO-WM, DINO-Foresight) by using predicted features not merely as evaluation targets but as conditioning signals for pixel-space synthesis, and from image-domain hierarchical synthesis (Würstchen) by evolving the semantic intermediate through time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7× convergence speedup for FID/FVD and 14× for segmentation, achieved with a 1.1B model that outperforms a 1.5B baseline, suggests the hierarchical decomposition is doing substantive work beyond parameter scaling. The identification of the train-test mismatch (teacher-forced GT features vs. autoregressive predicted features) as the critical bottleneck, and the relatively simple fixes (nested dropout, 90/10 mixed supervision), provide actionable guidance for anyone building multi-stage generative pipelines. Limitations include evaluation primarily on driving datasets, a frozen VFM that cannot adapt to domain-specific dynamics, and the added pipeline complexity of two sequential models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer prediction horizons, whether end-to-end joint training of the VFM and generator would further close the gap, and whether the hierarchical decomposition transfers to non-driving or non-rigid-body video prediction.
