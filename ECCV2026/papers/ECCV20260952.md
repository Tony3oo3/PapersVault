## Problem

Indoor 3D semantic occupancy prediction is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale annotated 3D data, as indoor scenes are privacy-sensitive, geometrically complex, and expensive to scan and label. Existing benchmarks like NYUv2 contain only ~1,449 images, and manual annotation is unscalable. The paper asks whether 3D indoor semantic occupancy can be learned from abundant, unlabeled, and uncalibrated in-the-wild internet videos without extensive manual annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260952/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The occupancy prediction model takes a single RGB image and outputs a 60×60×36 voxel grid at 8 cm resolution with 13 classes (11 semantic + free + unknown). The upstream data pipeline consumes uncalibrated YouTube video clips (real estate footage) and produces pseudo-labels including estimated camera intrinsics, 6-DoF poses, depth maps, 2D semantic segmentation maps, and 3D semantic occupancy grids. A key constraint is that the source videos lack any camera calibration, so the pipeline must jointly estimate geometry and semantics from raw RGB alone.

## Method

The paper contributes an automated data pipeline and a model-agnostic pre-training framework. The pipeline harvests YouTube real estate videos, uses Dust3R to jointly estimate camera parameters and reconstruct point clouds (with a 2.8 m wall-height prior to resolve metric scale), and applies SAN for open-vocabulary semantic segmentation to generate dense pseudo-labels. Because direct supervision with these pseudo-labels fails due to geometric scale and semantic taxonomy misalignment, the pre-training framework instead distills 2D foundation model knowledge via a dual-alignment strategy: (1) an intra-frame region contrastive loss that projects 3D voxel features onto the image plane through a Voxel-Anchored Gaussianization module (parameterizing each voxel as a fixed-scale Gaussian primitive for differentiable rendering) and aligns the rendered 2D features with DINOv2 teacher features via masked average pooling; and (2) a cross-scene prototype contrastive loss that maintains a dynamic class-prototype memory bank updated by EMA and pulls 3D region embeddings toward their corresponding global prototypes. The framework requires only a feature-dimension change to the occupancy network, making it plug-and-play across architectures (MonoScene, Symphonies, EmbodiedOcc).

## Results

On NYUv2, Symphonies with the proposed pre-training achieves 31.65% mIoU and 52.15% IoU, surpassing the previous best (ISO at 31.25% mIoU) and the Symphonies baseline (29.70% mIoU) by +1.95%. On Occ-ScanNet, the same configuration reaches 49.38% mIoU and 61.25% IoU, outperforming RoboOcc (47.67% mIoU) and the Symphonies baseline (47.71% mIoU) by +1.67%. The gains are consistent across all three tested architectures (MonoScene, EmbodiedOcc, Symphonies), with improvements of +0.93% to +1.95% mIoU on NYUv2 and +1.48% to +1.67% on Occ-ScanNet. In few-shot settings (5% labels on NYUv2), pre-training on YouTube-Occ yields +3.29% mIoU over random initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed pre-training framework consistently outperforms all compared baselines. On NYUv2, Symphonies+Pre-training (31.65% mIoU) surpasses ISO (31.25%), NDC-Scene (29.03%), MonoScene (26.94%), and all earlier methods (3DSketch 22.91%, AICNet 18.15%, LMSCNet 15.88%). On Occ-ScanNet, it beats RoboOcc (47.67%), EmbodiedOcc++ (46.20%), EmbodiedOcc (45.48%), and ISO (28.71%). Against alternative training strategies in Table 4, the pre-training approach (31.65% mIoU on NYUv2) outperforms auxiliary-loss methods HASSC (30.70%) and GaussRender (30.37%), and notably surpasses the 3D pre-training method Pri3D (28.40%), which actually underperforms the vanilla Symphonies baseline (29.70%). The gains are model-agnostic: EmbodiedOcc improves from 19.14% to 20.18% mIoU on NYUv2 and from 44.69% to 46.17% on Occ-ScanNet, while MonoScene improves from 26.09% to 27.02% on NYUv2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of 3D self-supervised learning and web-scale data curation, but deliberately departs from both. Unlike outdoor self-supervised occupancy methods (GaussTR, SelfOcc, S4C) that assume calibrated multi-view sensor data, YouTube-Occ targets uncalibrated single-view internet video, requiring Dust3R for joint geometry estimation and a 2.8 m wall-height prior to resolve scale ambiguity. The voxel-anchored Gaussianization module repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting's differentiable rendering as a 3D-to-2D feature projection bridge, while the dual-alignment distillation (intra-frame region contrast + cross-scene prototype contrast) draws on image-to-LiDAR distillation paradigms but adapts them to the noisy, semantically misaligned pseudo-labels inherent to open-vocabulary 2D foundation models applied to indoor scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that uncalibrated web video is a viable, scalable data source for indoor 3D occupancy pre-training, directly addressing the annotation bottleneck that limits indoor scene understanding. The key open question is whether the 2.8 m wall-height assumption and single-view formulation can be relaxed for more general indoor environments, and whether the framework extends to multi-view or temporal occupancy networks. The finding that naive pseudo-label supervision actively degrades performance (worse than no pre-training) underscores that the distillation framework is not optional but essential for extracting signal from foundation-model-generated labels, a lesson likely transferable to other pseudo-label-driven 3D tasks.
