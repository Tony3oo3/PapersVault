## Problem

Existing GUI navigation agents struggle to process dense, continuous visual interaction histories: aggressive compression discards the visual evidence needed to diagnose failures, while full retention causes multimodal token explosion. This bottleneck simultaneously hinders immediate error correction within a single episode and experience distillation across multiple attempts, because agents cannot distinguish which steps require visual grounding versus which can be abstracted into text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261152/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives an RGB GUI screenshot, a natural language goal, and an asymmetric interaction history where mismatched steps retain their original screenshot while matched steps are reduced to text-only records. At each step, the policy outputs a spatial action (click, swipe, type, or terminate with 2D coordinates) paired with a textual expected-transition description; the evaluator then produces a Cognitive State Anchor comprising the observed transition and a binary alignment verdict. Across trials, the distiller outputs a concise set of natural-language strategic rules that are injected into the policy prompt for subsequent attempts.

## Method

AnchorGUI introduces the Cognitive State Anchor (CSA), a per-step primitive in which a single VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) with role-specific prompts jointly predicts an action and its anticipated visual effect, then compares that expectation against the post-action screenshot to emit a binary verdict. This verdict drives an asymmetric memory: within a sliding window (k=2), steps with V=0 retain their screenshot as causal evidence while V=1 steps are compressed to text, reducing intra-trial context from O(T) to O(|mismatches|). For cross-trial distillation, the same asymmetric retention is applied over the full episode, bounding the credit-assignment search space to detected mismatches so the distiller can extract reusable strategic rules at a fraction of the token cost of full-trajectory reflection. The entire pipeline is zero-shot, requiring no task-specific fine-tuning, and the evaluator's verdict achieves 99.2% precision on manual annotation.

## Results

On AndroidWorld, AnchorGUI achieves a 57.3% single-trial success rate (SR@1) with Qwen3-VL-8B, outperforming all zero-shot and fine-tuned baselines while consuming only 13.7k tokens per step (a 2.4× reduction over ReAct's 32.7k). In cross-trial evaluation, asymmetric distillation lifts performance to 69.2% SR@3 (+11.9% absolute gain) at a distillation cost of just 24.4k tokens per trial, compared to ~60k for Reflexion-style baselines. On offline benchmarks, AnchorGUI leads all zero-shot methods on AITZ (75.2% TM, 59.1% EM) and Android-Control (74.6% TM, 73.0% EM), and remains competitive on GUI-Odyssey (80.6% TM, 60.6% EM) without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnchorGUI (57.3% SR@1) outperforms the strongest zero-shot baseline Mobile-Agent-v3 (55.2%) by +2.1% and the best fine-tuned system Agent-S2 (54.3%) by +3.0% on AndroidWorld, while using a smaller 8B model versus Agent-S2's 72B backbone. Against same-backbone baselines (Qwen3-VL-8B), AnchorGUI gains +13.6% over ReAct, +11.7% over ReSum, and +9.6% over Self-Reflection, demonstrating that the CSA-driven asymmetric memory is more effective than simple truncation, text summarization, or self-reflection for in-trial correction. On offline benchmarks, AnchorGUI leads all zero-shot methods on AITZ (+3.8% TM over ReAct) and Android-Control (+2.3% TM over ReSum), though the fine-tuned OdysseyAgent still dominates GUI-Odyssey (90.8% TM) due to domain-specific training. In cross-trial learning, AnchorGUI's asymmetric distillation reaches 69.2% SR@3, surpassing ReAct+Reflexion (55.2%) by +14.0% and Self-Reflection+Reflexion (56.8%) by +12.4%, while consuming only 24.4k cross-trial tokens versus ~60k for Reflexion-style baselines. The method also achieves this with sub-linear token scaling (13.7k per step at step 30 vs. 105.3k for ReAct), confirming that the 2.4× intra-trial token reduction does not come at the cost of accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnchorGUI carves new ground at the intersection of three established lines of work: (i) history management in GUI agents (truncation, summarization, hierarchical planning), (ii) step-level verification (GUI-Critic, MobileUse), and (iii) cross-trial learning via verbal reflection (Reflexion, Episodic Memory). Rather than building on any single prior method, it identifies a gap shared by all three: they treat visual history uniformly, either retaining or discarding frames without distinguishing expectation-consistent from expectation-violating transitions. The Cognitive State Anchor repurposes the prediction-error concept from cognitive science as a per-step gating signal, unifying intra-trial correction and cross-trial distillation under one asymmetric retention principle that no prior GUI agent framework explicitly formalizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.4× per-step token reduction combined with a 57.3%→69.2% success-rate trajectory on AndroidWorld addresses two practical bottlenecks simultaneously: multimodal context explosion in long-horizon navigation and the combinatorial cost of credit assignment across trials. The sub-linear scaling property (context bounded by O(k) rather than O(T)) is a meaningful prerequisite for deploying VLM-based agents in open-ended, multi-minute tasks. Open questions include whether the informational asymmetry principle generalizes beyond mobile GUIs to web or desktop interfaces, whether the binary verdict is sufficient for more nuanced failure modes (e.g., partially correct transitions), and whether the approach extends to settings where the VLM backbone is significantly weaker than Qwen3-VL-8B.
