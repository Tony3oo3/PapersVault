## Problem

Causal-attention streaming visual geometry models like StreamVGGT enable single-pass 3D reconstruction but accumulate a linearly growing KV cache that exhausts GPU memory within hundreds of frames, precluding the long-horizon deployment that motivates streaming inference. Existing cache-eviction approaches either break FlashAttention compatibility or degrade reconstruction quality over extended sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260583/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

OVGGT consumes streaming RGB frames one at a time, encoding each via a frozen DINOv2 backbone into 1,041 patch tokens (at 518×392 resolution) plus one camera token and four register tokens. For each frame it outputs a 9-dimensional camera parameter vector, a dense H×W depth map, and an H×W×3 3D pointmap, all produced in a single forward pass with a fixed 200K-token KV cache (~10 GB VRAM) that supports arbitrarily long sequences on a 32 GB GPU.

## Method

OVGGT is a training-free plug-in for StreamVGGT's 24-layer causal-attention decoder that bounds the KV cache to a fixed budget via two complementary mechanisms. Self-Selective Caching scores each token's geometric salience by its FFN residual magnitude (already computed in the forward pass, zero overhead, FlashAttention-compatible), applies 2D Gaussian smoothing for spatial coherence, and uses a hybrid scoring scheme that combines current-frame activation scores with historical key-vector diversity to evict the lowest-priority tokens. Dynamic Anchor Protection permanently shields all first-frame tokens (Global Initial Anchor) to preserve coordinate-system consistency and adaptively registers Historical Anchors at frames where view-overlap with the most recent anchor drops below a threshold, protecting the top-confidence patch tokens from eviction to suppress long-range geometric drift. The entire pipeline requires no architectural modifications or retraining and achieves O(1) per-step compute and storage regardless of sequence length.

## Results

On 7-Scenes at 500 frames, OVGGT achieves the best Accuracy (0.031 m), Completeness (0.020 m), and Normal Consistency (0.561) among all methods that complete inference, while StreamVGGT and Evict3R (original) hit OOM. At 1000 frames, OVGGT maintains Acc 0.039 m and Comp 0.020 m, outperforming the next-best InfiniteVGGT (Acc 0.061 m, Comp 0.035 m) by roughly 36% in accuracy. On the ultra-long Long3D benchmark (up to 10,000 frames), OVGGT with a 200K token budget achieves Acc 2.453 m and Comp 0.390 m, halving the accuracy error of InfiniteVGGT (4.344 m) and Evict3R† (4.928 m). On KITTI depth estimation at 500 frames, OVGGT reaches δ<1.25 = 0.839, surpassing the full-cache StreamVGGT (0.740 at 100 frames) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVGGT outperforms all seven baselines on Accuracy and Completeness across every sequence length tested. Against the full-cache reference StreamVGGT, OVGGT achieves 37% lower Accuracy (0.024 vs. 0.038 m) and 28% lower Completeness (0.021 vs. 0.029 m) at 200 frames on 7-Scenes, and is the only method that completes 500- and 1000-frame sequences without OOM. The closest competitor, InfiniteVGGT, is beaten by OVGGT on Accuracy at all lengths (e.g., 0.031 vs. 0.040 m at 500 frames; 0.039 vs. 0.061 m at 1000 frames) and on Completeness (0.020 vs. 0.024 m at 500 frames), while Normal Consistency is essentially tied (0.561 vs. 0.561 at 500 frames). The budget-matched Evict3R† trails OVGGT on Accuracy at 500 frames (0.042 vs. 0.031 m) and 1000 frames (0.134 vs. 0.039 m), with the gap widening sharply as sequences grow. Earlier streaming methods (Spann3R, CUT3R, Point3R, TTT3R) are substantially worse on Accuracy and Completeness at all lengths, with Spann3R degrading most severely (Acc 0.340 m at 1000 frames vs. OVGGT's 0.039 m). On KITTI depth at 500 frames, OVGGT achieves δ<1.25 = 0.839, exceeding StreamVGGT's 0.740 (at only 100 frames) and InfiniteVGGT's 0.577, confirming the advantage extends to per-frame geometric accuracy in outdoor driving scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OVGGT builds directly on StreamVGGT's causal-attention streaming architecture (itself derived from VGGT's all-to-all geometric transformer), which processes each frame once with a growing KV cache. The key departure is in cache management: rather than retaining all prior tokens (StreamVGGT) or applying generic eviction heuristics (Evict3R, InfiniteVGGT), OVGGT exploits the spatial and metric structure unique to geometric patch tokens—using FFN-residual magnitudes as a FlashAttention-compatible saliency proxy and shielding coordinate-critical anchor tokens—to compress the cache to a fixed budget. The approach is entirely training-free and plug-in, requiring no architectural modification to the underlying causal transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bounding both memory and per-step compute to O(1), OVGGT makes arbitrarily long streaming 3D reconstruction feasible on a single 32 GB consumer GPU, removing the primary deployment barrier for real-time applications like autonomous navigation and digital twins. The finding that a compressed, selectively curated cache can outperform the full cache challenges the implicit assumption that more context is always better in geometric streaming. The main open limitation is the inherent error accumulation of single-pass causal inference; the authors note that OVGGT is complementary to backend global optimization (loop closure, bundle adjustment) and that staged inference combining mini-batch prediction with periodic refinement is a promising direction.
