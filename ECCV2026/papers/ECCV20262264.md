## Problem

Existing 3DGS generation methods that adapt pretrained image diffusion models (e.g., DiffSplat) require training a fragile cascade of three interdependent components—a reconstructor, a VAE, and a latent diffusion model—making them computationally expensive and scalability-limited. More critically, the compressed latent space inherently caps the representational capacity of generated 3D assets, producing decoding artifacts and accumulated errors that degrade fine-grained detail. No prior single-stage approach directly denoises 3D Gaussian attributes in pixel space while retaining the powerful 2D generative priors from web-scale image corpora.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a text prompt, a reference RGB image, or both, along with camera poses for V_in viewpoints (encoded as Plücker coordinates). It produces a 3D Gaussian Splat parameterized as a tensor G ∈ R^{V_in × g × H × W}, where each pixel in the H×W grid encodes 12 attributes (color, scale, rotation, opacity, depth) of a single Gaussian primitive, yielding V_in × H × W total Gaussians. Inference completes in approximately 1 second on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

PixGS is a single-stage Flow Matching diffusion model that operates directly on Gaussian attribute tensors in pixel space, bypassing any VAE latent bottleneck. It adapts the PixNerd architecture—where a transformer predicts per-patch MLP weights that decode pixel-level velocities via coordinate-conditioned neural fields—to the 3D domain, inheriting 2D priors from ~45M pretraining images. Cross-view geometric consistency is enforced through a Multi-view Attention block and a Multi-view RoPE2d positional encoding that treats all viewpoints as spatially aligned segments of a composite image. Training uses a multi-task objective combining Flow Matching loss, super-resolved appearance supervision (MSE + LPIPS + silhouette mask at 2H×2W), depth/normal geometry loss, a multi-scale Laplacian-of-Gaussian loss for high-frequency detail, and an explicit rotation-norm regularizer. A three-phase curriculum (pseudo-label FM → FM + GT rendering → GT-only) decouples final quality from the reconstructor's capacity, with pseudo-labels from GSRecon used only for early stabilization.

## Results

On T3Bench (text-conditioned), PixGS achieves the best CLIP similarity (31.98% single object, 31.32% with surroundings, 30.45% multi-object), CLIP R-Precision (88.75%, 87.25%, 77.25%), and ImageReward (-0.171, -0.341, -0.740) across all three prompt complexity levels, while maintaining ~1s inference on a single A100. On GSO (image-conditioned), PixGS attains PSNR 21.21, SSIM 0.842, and LPIPS 0.123, outperforming the next-best method (DiffSplat SD-3.5) by ~1.6 dB in PSNR and 0.031 in LPIPS. Inference latency is 1s, matching the fastest baselines and far exceeding DreamGaussian's 47s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixGS outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric in both text-conditioned (T3Bench) and image-conditioned (GSO) evaluations. Against DiffSplat [21], the closest competitor, PixGS gains ~1 CLIP similarity point and ~4 R-Precision points on single-object prompts, and ~1.6 dB PSNR / 0.031 LPIPS on GSO, while also being 3× faster (1s vs 3s for SD-3.5). TRELLIS [48] and GaussianCube [57] (3D-native models) trail by 2–5 CLIP similarity points and 10–30 R-Precision points, reflecting their limited training data scale. LGM† [40] (reconstruction-based) is competitive on CLIP similarity but lags by 10+ R-Precision points and 1.6 dB PSNR, and requires 6s inference. DreamGaussian [41] (optimization-based) is the weakest on quality (CLIP Sim 24.78%) and 47× slower. DiffusionGS [2] (single-stage, trained from scratch) achieves only 16.53 PSNR on GSO, well below PixGS's 21.21, confirming the benefit of leveraging pre-trained 2D pixel-space priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixGS sits at the intersection of two recent threads: pixel-space diffusion models (PixNerd, PixelDiT) that bypass VAE latent compression, and structured 3DGS generation (DiffSplat, Zero-1-to-G) that represents Gaussians as 2D attribute tensors. The key departure from DiffSplat is eliminating the three-stage cascade (reconstructor → VAE → latent diffusion) in favor of a single pixel-space denoiser operating directly on Gaussian attributes, thereby inheriting 2D priors from ~45M pre-trained images without the representational bottleneck of a compressed latent. Unlike DiffusionGS, which trains a dedicated architecture from scratch on synthetic 3D views, PixGS repurposes an existing pixel-diffusion backbone and adds targeted 3D supervision (depth, normals, multi-scale LoG) plus a three-phase curriculum that decouples final quality from pseudo-label fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is a single-stage, ~1-second pipeline that matches or exceeds multi-stage baselines on both text-to-3D and image-to-3D benchmarks while being substantially simpler to train and scale. The finding that pseudo-label source (GSRecon vs. LGM) has negligible impact on final quality (21.21 vs. 21.15 PSNR) is practically important: it means the generative model can overcome imperfect reconstruction priors, reducing the engineering burden of maintaining a high-quality reconstructor. Open questions include whether the pixel-space advantage persists at higher spatial resolutions or for scene-level (rather than object-level) generation, and whether the multi-view attention mechanism scales gracefully beyond the 38-view training protocol.
