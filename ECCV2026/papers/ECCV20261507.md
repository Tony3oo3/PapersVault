## Problem

DP-SGD adds isotropic Gaussian noise that corrupts every singular direction of the gradient equally, yet vision-model gradients concentrate signal in a low-rank subspace, so most noise energy falls in directions carrying little signal. Spectral orthogonalization (polar decomposition) is established in non-private optimization but its utility under differential privacy is unexplored, with no principled criterion for when it recovers directional signal versus amplifying noise. This paper identifies and characterizes the SNR-governed phase transition that separates the harmful and beneficial regimes.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-time post-processing step on 2D weight-matrix gradients; it does not alter the model's input (RGB images at 32×32, 64×64, or 224×224) or output (class label) format. For 1D parameters (biases, layer norms), standard DP-SGD with momentum is used unchanged. The orthogonalization is applied per-layer to the privatized gradient tensor after noise addition, so the privacy mechanism and data pipeline remain identical to vanilla DP-SGD.

## Method

After standard DP-SGD clipping and Gaussian noise addition, the privatized gradient is passed through Nesterov momentum and then orthogonalized via five iterations of a quintic Newton-Schulz scheme, producing the nearest orthogonal matrix UVᵀ (polar decomposition) at zero additional privacy cost by the post-processing theorem. A dimension-scaling factor √(max(1,m/n)) corrects for rectangular gradients, and a magnitude-preserving variant rescales by the spectral norm σ₁ for moderate-to-high SNR. The key theoretical contribution is a recovery condition (Proposition 1) derived from the Wedin sin θ bound: orthogonalization recovers the signal subspace only when the batch size exceeds σC(√m+√n)/(σ₁(G)−σ₂(G)), i.e., when the spectral gap dominates the noise operator norm. This yields a predictable phase transition—harmful at small B (low SNR, noise directions normalized to unit norm) and beneficial at large B (high SNR, signal directions recovered)—validated across four architectures and three datasets, with crossover at B≈1024 on CIFAR-10 and B≈4096–8192 on CIFAR-100.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ε=4, B=4096), DP-Muon achieves 48.7% top-1 accuracy, outperforming DP-SGD (39.3%) by +9.4%. The gain scales with model capacity: on WRN-28-10 (B=4096, ε=4), DP-Muon reaches 54.9% versus DP-SGD's 34.0% (+20.9%), and on ResNet-18 the gap is +14.9%. Combining spectral orthogonalization with temporal denoising (DOPPLER-Muon) yields 50.3% on CIFAR-10 (ε=4, B=4096), the highest accuracy in any tested configuration. On full ImageNet-1k (NF-ResNet-50, B=32768), DP-Muon improves over DP-SGD by +2.50% at ε=4 (20.97% vs 18.47%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DP-SGD [1], DP-Muon wins decisively at large batch sizes: +9.4% on CIFAR-10 (B=4096), +20.9% on WRN-28-10, +14.9% on ResNet-18, and +14.9% on SVHN, while the two are within 1.3% at B=256 where SNR is too low for spectral recovery. Against DP-Adam [20], DP-Muon outperforms at large batch sizes (48.7% vs 38.8% on CIFAR-10, B=4096) and matches it within 0.4% in the ViT-Small fine-tuning regime, while using a stateless first-order memory footprint versus Adam's second-moment buffer. Against DiSK [40], the comparison is regime-dependent: DiSK-SGD dominates at small batches (49.0% at B=256) but degrades to 39.9% at B=4096 as fewer gradient steps shrink the Kalman filter's averaging window, whereas DP-Muon improves monotonically with batch size. Against DOPPLER [41], standalone DOPPLER-SGD underperforms DP-SGD at large batches (32.3% at B=4096), but combining DOPPLER's low-pass filter with spectral orthogonalization (DOPPLER-Muon) achieves the paper's best result of 50.3%, confirming the two denoising mechanisms are complementary. On ImageNet-1k, DP-Muon (20.97%) substantially outperforms DP-AdamW [24] (10.37%) at ε=4, a +10.6% gap with seven-fold lower across-seed variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Muon optimizer's Newton-Schulz polar decomposition [18] as a deterministic post-processing step on DP-SGD gradients, applying it in a setting (differential privacy) where its interaction with isotropic noise had not been studied. It explicitly positions itself against temporal denoising methods (DOPPLER, DiSK) that filter across training steps, and against frequency-domain or anisotropic-noise approaches that modify the privacy mechanism itself; spectral orthogonalization leaves the standard isotropic Gaussian mechanism untouched and operates purely on the already-privatized gradient's matrix structure. The theoretical contribution—a recovery condition derived from the Wedin sin θ bound linking batch size, noise multiplier, and spectral gap—provides a principled criterion absent from prior DP optimization work, which typically treats gradient noise as a uniform utility cost without exploiting the low-rank structure of vision-model gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a zero-cost, drop-in post-processing step that yields large accuracy gains (+20.9% on WRN-28-10, +14.9% on ResNet-18) in the large-batch, high-capacity regime where DP-SGD stagnates or degrades, and the SNR-based phase transition gives a clear decision rule for when to apply it. The demonstrated complementarity with temporal denoising (50.3% on CIFAR-10 at ε=4) suggests a path toward combining orthogonalization with existing noise-reduction pipelines. Key limitations include the regime restriction (harmful below the SNR threshold, bimodal convergence of the magnitude-preserving variant on SVHN) and the fact that the benefit vanishes in parameter-efficient fine-tuning where low-rank constraints remove the spectral mismatch. Open questions include layer-wise selective orthogonalization, optimal singular value shrinkage, and whether the recovery condition generalizes to non-convex or multi-task settings.
