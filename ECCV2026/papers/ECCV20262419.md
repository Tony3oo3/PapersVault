## Problem

Deterministic speech-to-face models produce over-smoothed, low-variance motions due to the many-to-many audio-to-expression mapping, while diffusion-based alternatives capture expressive distributions but require tens to hundreds of iterative denoising steps, precluding real-time deployment. The paper tackles the open question of how to achieve one-step generative facial animation that simultaneously preserves lip-sync precision, geometric validity on the FLAME manifold, and stylistic expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The model consumes speech audio (encoded as acoustic features), a reference video from which a static FLAME shape coefficient β is extracted for identity, and a reference motion sequence from which a frozen style encoder produces a temporal style code S. The output is a sequence of FLAME parameters (expression and pose) that drive a 5023-vertex mesh; during training, each sample spans two consecutive 100-frame clips at 25 fps with a 10-frame historical motion window for temporal continuity. At inference, a single forward pass (NFE=1) yields the final motion, achieving an RTF of 0.004 on an RTX 3090.

## Method

MeanTalker replaces iterative diffusion with a Mean Flow formulation that learns an average-velocity field u(z_t, r, t) enabling exact one-step generation via x₁ = z_t + (1−t)·u_θ(z_t, 1, t). The Dual-Time Denoising Transformer (DTDT) introduces an interleaved dual-time encoding where the reference time r is set equal to t in Phase I (instantaneous velocity) and sampled independently in Phase II (average velocity), establishing a progressive curriculum that stabilizes training. The Geometric-Aware Trajectory Learning (GATL) module rectifies the generation path through two synergistic mechanisms: (A) implicit manifold projection that analytically recovers the endpoint x_θ and applies vertex-level geometric losses (lip sync, temporal smoothness, coefficient continuity), and (B) JVP-rectified mean-flow supervision combined with a Semantic-aware Manifold-GVC loss that projects latent velocity errors onto the FLAME surface via a Jacobian-aware finite-difference approximation, imposing stronger constraints on articulation-critical lip regions than on structural style regions. Training proceeds in two stages—standard CFM with geometric supervision, then mean-flow rectification with a 50/50 curriculum between instantaneous and average velocity sampling—yielding state-of-the-art LVE (7.88 mm) at 200× faster inference than diffusion baselines.

## Results

On the TFHP benchmark, MeanTalker achieves the best score across all five metrics: LVE 7.88 mm, FDD 5.19×10⁻⁵ m, MOD 2.83 mm, MVE 0.79 mm, and RTF 0.004. The lip-sync improvement over the strongest diffusion baseline (MSMD, LVE 8.39 mm) is modest (~0.5 mm), but the speedup is dramatic: MeanTalker's RTF of 0.004 is roughly 230× faster than DiffPoseTalk (0.919) and 254× faster than MSMD (1.015). In the user study, MeanTalker also leads on all three MOS dimensions (Lip Sync 4.32, Style Sim 4.05, Natural 4.24), with MSMD as the clear second-best.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeanTalker outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest diffusion competitor MSMD, it improves LVE from 8.39 to 7.88 mm, FDD from 5.76 to 5.19, MOD from 2.98 to 2.83, and MVE from 0.83 to 0.79, while reducing RTF from 1.015 to 0.004 (~254× speedup). Compared to DiffPoseTalk (the other diffusion baseline), the LVE gap is larger (9.12 → 7.88 mm) and the speedup is ~230×. The autoregressive baselines MultiTalk and ARTalk trail substantially on lip-sync (LVE 11.10 and 11.78 mm respectively) and on upper-face dynamics (FDD 8.35 and 12.05), though ARTalk is relatively fast (RTF 0.020) yet still 5× slower than MeanTalker. The regression baseline ScanTalk is the weakest overall, with the highest LVE (13.15 mm) and FDD (12.55), confirming the over-smoothing limitation of deterministic mapping. In the user study, MeanTalker leads MSMD by 0.63 on Lip Sync (4.32 vs 3.69) and 0.38 on Naturalness (4.24 vs 3.86), while DiffPoseTalk is a distant third on all three MOS dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeanTalker adapts the Mean Flow framework [Geng et al., NeurIPS 2026]—which models average velocity for one-step generation—to the speech-driven 3D facial animation domain, where it must jointly preserve framewise audio-lip alignment and FLAME manifold validity. Its Stage I initialization draws on standard Conditional Flow Matching [Lipman et al., ICLR 2023] with optimal-transport linear trajectories, while the Dual-Time Denoising Transformer introduces a progressive curriculum (instantaneous → average velocity) that departs from prior mean-flow work by addressing training instability specific to cross-modal generation. The Geometric-Aware Trajectory Learning module is a novel departure from purely latent-space flow matching, projecting velocity errors onto the physical vertex manifold via Jacobian-aware linearization—a constraint absent from general-purpose mean-flow or consistency-model formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving SOTA lip-sync accuracy (LVE 7.88 mm) while accelerating inference by over 200× relative to iterative diffusion baselines (RTF ≈ 0.004), MeanTalker demonstrates that one-step flow-based generation can simultaneously improve quality and enable real-time deployment for interactive avatars, VR, and gaming. The geometric-aware trajectory rectification offers a transferable recipe for any flow-matching task where the latent space must map to a constrained physical manifold. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (TFHP) and a single topology (FLAME), no explicit emotion or full-body control, and reliance on a pre-trained style encoder from DiffPoseTalk; extending to multi-topology, emotion-conditioned, and full-body settings remains open.
