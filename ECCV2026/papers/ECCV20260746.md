## Problem

Existing few-shot Chinese handwriting generation methods encode content with recognition-trained networks (capturing 'what' a character is) and represent style via global image-level features, ignoring the compositional hierarchy of Chinese characters (radicals, components, strokes) and local stylistic details. This mismatch between global style modeling and the intrinsic multi-scale structure of handwriting leads to inaccurate stylization and poor generalization to unseen characters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260746/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes k reference glyph images (cropped and resized to 64×64) from a target writer, along with the target character's UTF-8 identity and the reference characters' identities, which are used to look up hierarchical decompositions (radical, component, stroke sequences) from a fixed table. Output is either an online handwriting trajectory (a sequence of GMM parameters for pen-tip displacement plus three pen-state probabilities) or an offline 64×64 handwritten character image, depending on the chosen decoder. The same aggregated style representation Z drives both decoders without architectural redesign.

## Method

HiSAC combines two core modules. FASE (Frequency-Aware Style Encoder) splits patch embeddings into three channel groups fed into coarse (self-attention, acting as a low-pass filter), medium (depthwise convolution), and fine (max-pooling + 1×1 conv) branches to learn multi-band style representations across spectral ranges, trained with a triplet contrastive loss. HiSA (Hierarchical Style Aggregation) decomposes both target and reference characters into radical, component, and stroke sequences; at the radical/component levels it performs cross-attention between target structural embeddings and reference style features, while at the stroke level it computes cosine-similarity-weighted aggregation over reference glyphs. The three-level stylized representations are concatenated into a single condition vector Z, which is injected into either a Transformer decoder (producing GMM-parameterized trajectories) or a DDPM U-Net (via cross-attention for image denoising), making the framework generator-agnostic.

## Results

On the ICDAR 2013 online handwriting benchmark, HiSAC achieves a DTW of 0.8224, Style Score of 97.01, and Content Score of 96.68, outperforming the strongest baseline SDT (DTW 0.8780, Style 94.56, Content 96.47) by 0.0556 in DTW and 2.45 in Style Score. On the offline benchmark, HiSAC (Diffusion) attains FID 34.88, LPIPS 0.1627, Style 68.21, and Content 89.48, surpassing the best prior method DiffBrush (FID 36.41, LPIPS 0.1704, Style 66.18, Content 87.11). The VAR-based variant further reduces FID to 34.17 and RMSE to 0.1485, confirming generator-agnostic performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiSAC outperforms all five online baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor SDT [9], it reduces DTW from 0.8780 to 0.8224 and raises Style Score from 94.56 to 97.01; against DNA [22], it improves DTW by 0.0817 and Style by 3.58. In the offline setting, HiSAC dominates GAN-based methods (HWT [4], VATr [39]) by a wide margin (FID 34.88 vs. 162.43 and 136.78), and edges out diffusion-based competitors: it beats DiffBrush [8] on FID (34.88 vs. 36.41), LPIPS (0.1627 vs. 0.1704), Style (68.21 vs. 66.18), and Content (89.48 vs. 87.11), and surpasses One-DM [7] on FID, LPIPS, Style, and Content, though One-DM retains a marginal RMSE advantage (0.1520 vs. 0.1531). For artistic font generation, HiSAC achieves the best SSIM (0.4684) and L1 (0.1655) but trails FontDiffuser [55] on FID (16.23 vs. 14.84) and LPIPS (0.1395 vs. 0.1486), indicating that the frequency-aware encoder is less effective for highly exaggerated decorative styles. Overall, HiSAC wins or ties on the vast majority of metrics across all three task settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiSAC departs from the dominant paradigm in few-shot Chinese handwriting generation, where content is encoded by recognition-pretrained networks (focusing on 'what' a character is) and style is captured via global image-level features. Instead, it repurposes the hierarchical decomposition (radicals → components → strokes) previously explored in font generation [26,37,38,47] as the central mechanism for cross-scale style transfer, and introduces a multi-band frequency encoder (FASE) that learns spectral style representations end-to-end rather than treating frequency analysis as an external input [7] or post-processing step [5,32,53]. The framework is architecturally agnostic, plugging the aggregated style representation into either a Transformer decoder (online) or a DDPM/VAR decoder (offline), unifying two previously separate task formulations under one style model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling style modeling from task-specific generation, HiSAC offers a reusable style representation that transfers across online trajectories, offline images, artistic fonts, and even cross-lingual scripts (Japanese Kanji, English), suggesting the hierarchical structural prior is a general inductive bias for character-level generation. The ablation showing Content Score collapsing from 96.68 to 67.35 when HiSA is removed underscores that structural guidance is not merely helpful but essential for compositional correctness. Open questions remain for highly deformed scripts (running/cursive), where the fixed decomposition table may not capture the fluid stroke merging, and for scaling beyond single-character generation to full text lines with inter-character dynamics.
