surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that parallel decoding not only maintains but slightly *improves* captioning quality (F1 61.5→61.8, Sim 38.4→38.8), which is counterintuitive since one would expect some quality loss from relaxing cross-event dependencies. However, the overall contribution is well-motivated and the gains, while consistent across benchmarks, follow logically from the structural observation, so the surprise is moderate rather than paradigm-shifting.

## Problem

Autoregressive Video-LLMs for dense video captioning decode strictly token-by-token, causing inference latency to scale linearly with the total number of caption tokens across all events. As video length and event density grow, this sequential bottleneck becomes prohibitive, and existing parallel alternatives (e.g., diffusion LLMs) fail to match both the efficiency and generation quality of strong autoregressive models in multimodal video settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262586/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video sampled at 0.5 FPS (up to 256 frames) with synchronized audio, encoded by Qwen2.5-VL and Whisper-Large-v3 respectively, with a window-level Q-Former aligning audio tokens to visual segments. Output is structured in two phases: first, K adaptively inferred latent global event tokens (each temporally grounded via similarity matching to the multimodal prefix), followed by event-specific caption tokens generated in parallel across K branches. The number of events K is not fixed but learned per video, and each branch terminates independently upon generating an EOS token.

## Method

PadCaptioner restructures the autoregressive dependency graph into two stages. In the latent global planning stage, the model autoregressively generates K compact event tokens, each trained with a BCE grounding loss to align with its ground-truth temporal segment and enriched via attention-guided aggregation of audio-visual prefix semantics into the token embedding. In the event-factorized parallel decoding stage, caption generation is decomposed into K weakly coupled subchains decoded simultaneously: within each subchain, tokens attend causally to their own local history and the event anchor token, while across subchains, local tokens are masked from one another but all branches retain full visibility of the shared global event tokens. This factorized attention preserves intra-event sequential coherence while eliminating redundant cross-event coupling, yielding a 3.8× wall-time speedup with improved grounding and captioning accuracy over the sequential baseline.

## Results

On the LongVALE dense video captioning benchmark, PadCaptioner (3B) achieves F1=56.4 and Sim=58.5, surpassing the previous SOTA ChronusOmni (7B) by +6.7 F1 and +6.1 Sim. On ChronusAV, it reaches F1=63.2 and Sim=40.0, again outperforming ChronusOmni (60.1/36.8). In terms of efficiency, PadCaptioner delivers a 3.7× wall-clock speedup (4,284 ms vs. 16,162 ms per video on LongVALE) and a 3× per-token speedup (13.4 ms vs. 41.3 ms) over ChronusOmni, while also outperforming the 30B-parameter Qwen3-Omni on all captioning metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PadCaptioner (3B) outperforms the previous SOTA ChronusOmni (7B) [8] on every metric across both LongVALE (F1 56.4 vs. 49.7; Sim 58.5 vs. 52.4) and ChronusAV (F1 63.2 vs. 60.1; Sim 40.0 vs. 36.8), while being 3.7× faster in wall-clock decoding time. It also surpasses the much larger Qwen3-Omni (30B-A3B) [62] by substantial margins (e.g., F1 56.4 vs. 34.2 on LongVALE; F1 63.2 vs. 51.8 on ChronusAV), demonstrating that the parallelized decoding framework compensates for the smaller model size. Against the 3B base model Video-SALMONN 2+ [50], PadCaptioner improves F1 from 35.5 to 56.4 and Sim from 26.6 to 58.5 on LongVALE, confirming that the gains come from the proposed architecture rather than additional training data. On the visually-centric YouCook2 benchmark, PadCaptioner achieves F1=27.1 and CIDEr=31.7 in zero-shot, beating ChronusOmni (24.2/27.3) and D2VLM (26.4/10.6). On the six ChronusAV cross-modal temporal grounding tasks (Table 5), PadCaptioner leads on all tasks, e.g., V2T R@0.5 of 68.4 vs. 63.2 for ChronusOmni and A2T R@0.5 of 91.3 vs. 90.5. The only baseline it does not directly compare against in a head-to-head efficiency sense is the 3B Video-SALMONN 2+ [50], which is faster in absolute wall-clock time (2,707 ms vs. 4,284 ms per video) but generates substantially shorter, less informative captions, as reflected in its much lower F1 and Sim scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Video-SALMONN 2+ omni-LLM architecture and departs from its strictly sequential autoregressive decoding by restructuring the token dependency graph through a learned latent event-level planning stage. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: parallel decoding in visual autoregressive generation (which relies on fixed spatial token layouts) and latent reasoning/planning in MLLMs (which uses intermediate tokens for structured reasoning), adapting both to the variable-length, dynamically structured nature of dense video captioning. It explicitly contrasts with diffusion-based LLMs that achieve parallelism through iterative refinement but struggle to match autoregressive quality, and with query-based DVC methods (e.g., PDVC) that support parallel event-level generation but cannot inherit the rich world knowledge of pretrained MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous 3.8× wall-clock speedup and SOTA accuracy improvement on omni-modal DVC benchmarks addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying video-LLM captioning in long-form, high-density settings (egocentric video, video search, multi-event generation). The core insight—that cross-event token dependencies are weak enough to parallelize while preserving intra-event autoregression—generalizes beyond DVC to any temporally structured generation task. Open questions include whether finer-grained parallelization units (sub-events, spatiotemporal instances) can be learned without human annotations, how to handle coarse captioning of very long events, and whether the modified attention pattern can be optimized at the kernel level for further speedups.
