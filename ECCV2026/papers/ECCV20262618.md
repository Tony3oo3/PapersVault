## Problem

Existing LLM-based CAD frameworks operate over procedural intermediates (command sequences, sketch operations, or code) rather than native B-rep entities, preventing direct geometric and topological reasoning. Moreover, construction-history data is extremely scarce compared to the abundant native B-rep models available in industry, creating a fundamental data bottleneck for scaling CAD foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262618/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw B-rep models (parametric faces, edges, and their adjacency topology) and a natural language prompt. Each B-rep is discretized via adaptive UV sampling into a face-edge graph with 10-dimensional face node features and 8-dimensional edge features, where per-face and per-edge sample counts are adaptively scaled to [32, 64] based on area and length respectively. The model produces free-form text outputs including detailed construction descriptions, object category labels, and answers to B-rep-specific questions.

## Method

BrepLLM employs a two-stage training pipeline. In cross-modal alignment pre-training, a hierarchical BrepEncoder extracts three disentangled features per face node—fine-grained face geometry via PointTransformerV3 (32-d), edge-conditioned boundary features via NNConv (32-d), and global topology via multi-hop EGATConv (64-d)—concatenated into 128-d node tokens, while a global token is aligned to a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder via symmetric InfoNCE loss. In two-stage LLM fine-tuning with Qwen3-8B, Stage I trains only a two-layer MLP to project B-rep node tokens into the BLIP-2 Q-Former's 1408-d embedding space using 32 learnable queries and autoregressive cross-entropy, establishing a geometry-to-vision bridge; Stage II applies LoRA to jointly fine-tune the Q-Former, projection head, and selected LLM layers for full 3D-language generation. The approach is trained on the newly constructed Brep2Text dataset of 269,444 B-rep/text QA pairs derived from the Text2CAD corpus.

## Results

On the Brep2Text captioning task, BrepLLM (8.4B) achieves a Qwen3-30B judge score of 61.36, Sentence-BERT similarity of 75.42, and SimCSE similarity of 77.23, outperforming the strongest baseline MiniGPT-3D (2.7B) by +4.78, +3.78, and +4.10 respectively. In human evaluation, BrepLLM attains 83.62% precision with the lowest hallucination rate (0.83) among all methods. On generative 3D object classification, BrepLLM reaches 60.0% average accuracy (60.5% instructional / 59.5% completion), exceeding the best baseline by +3.75 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrepLLM is compared against five families of baselines: image-based VLMs (LLaVA-13B, Qwen3-VL-8B) and point-cloud-based 3D LMMs (PointLLM-7B/13B, ShapeLLM-7B/13B, MiniGPT-3D). BrepLLM (8.4B) wins on every metric in both captioning and classification, surpassing the strongest point-cloud baseline MiniGPT-3D by +4.78 on the LLM judge score and +3.75 percentage points on classification accuracy. Notably, BrepLLM outperforms all 13B-parameter baselines (LLaVA-13B, PointLLM-13B, ShapeLLM-13B) despite having fewer parameters, suggesting that native B-rep encoding provides a more information-dense input than rendered images or 8192-point clouds. The image-based baselines (LLaVA-13B, Qwen3-VL-8B) lag substantially, with LLaVA-13B scoring only 36.73 on the LLM judge, confirming that 2D renderings lose critical parametric and topological detail. Among point-cloud methods, MiniGPT-3D is the closest competitor, but BrepLLM's advantage is consistent across automatic metrics, human-evaluated precision, and hallucination rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant procedural-sequence paradigm in CAD-LLM research (CAD-MLLM, CadVLM, CAD-Coder), which converts B-reps into command or sketch sequences, and instead directly encodes native B-rep geometry and topology via a hierarchical graph encoder (PointTransformerV3 + NNConv + EGATConv). For cross-modal alignment it borrows the CLIP-style contrastive pre-training recipe (frozen ViT-L/14 text tower) and the BLIP-2 Q-Former bridging architecture, adapting both to a graph-structured geometric input rather than an image. Among 3D LMMs, it extends the point-cloud-to-language pipeline (PointLLM, ShapeLLM, MiniGPT-3D) to a fundamentally different input modality—parametric B-rep with explicit face-edge topology—while retaining the Q-Former + LLM generation backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that native B-rep encoding yields consistent gains over point-cloud and multi-view baselines on both captioning and classification, the paper provides evidence that parametric surface and topological information is not redundant for CAD understanding, opening a path toward LLMs that reason directly over industrial CAD files without lossy discretization. The Brep2Text dataset (269k QA pairs) is the first large-scale benchmark for B-rep-centric language tasks and will likely become a standard evaluation resource. Key limitations include reliance on a single 8B backbone, evaluation confined to one dataset, and the absence of generation or editing capabilities; scaling to freeform NURBS surfaces, multi-body assemblies, and industrial design workflows remains open.
