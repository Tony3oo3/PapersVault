## Problem

Supervised 3D reconstruction of articulated objects from single images requires large paired 2D-3D training datasets with diverse, realistic articulations, which are prohibitively expensive to create manually. While a single rigged canonical mesh per category is readily available, generating the wide range of natural poses needed for effective training remains a major bottleneck, particularly for non-human articulated categories like quadrupeds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262789/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and a foreground segmentation mask (obtainable via off-the-shelf tools like SAM) and produces layered dual point maps: K posed 3D intersection points per foreground pixel in the camera frame plus corresponding canonical points in the rest-pose frame, capturing both visible and occluded geometry. At training time, the only 3D asset required is a single rigged and skinned canonical mesh per category; no ground-truth articulations, camera poses, or additional 3D instances are needed. The iterative fitting process also recovers camera poses as a by-product, which are then used to render self-annotated training pairs.

## Method

BAT3R is an iterative bootstrapping loop built on the DualPM point-map architecture. Starting from a weak predictor trained solely on canonical-pose renders, each iteration (1) runs inference on the 2D image collection to obtain posed and canonical point clouds, (2) fits the canonical mesh to these predictions via gradient-based optimization that jointly solves for joint rotations, per-bone scales, and a global rigid transform (initialized with Kabsch alignment), using a Huber data term plus geometric regularizations (angle, volume, edge-length, scale-symmetry, and self-repulsion losses) to prevent overfitting to noisy predictions, and (3) renders the fitted articulated meshes from multiple novel viewpoints to create new self-annotated training pairs with exact point-map supervision. The predictor is then retrained on this augmented dataset, and the cycle repeats (typically 4 iterations), progressively closing the gap to fully supervised DualPM while requiring only a single canonical mesh and unannotated 2D images.

## Results

On the Animodel-Points benchmark, BAT3R trained on synthetic images ('Ours (synt)') achieves RMS Chamfer Distance of 5.65±1.57 cm (horse), 4.17±1.15 cm (cow), and 4.35±1.26 cm (sheep), approaching the fully supervised DualPM upper bound of 4.30/3.18/3.30 cm respectively. On the larger Horse-Robust split (15,000 samples), the real-image variant ('Ours (real)') achieves 6.01 cm RMS CD, outperforming zero-shot foundation models SAM-3D (6.42 cm) and Trellis.2 (8.92 cm) while remaining within ~2 cm of DualPM (3.84 cm). The canonical-only initialization already reaches 7.88 cm on horse, and the iterative refinement closes most of the gap to full supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAT3R (synt) outperforms all prior category-specific methods (A-CSM, MagicPony, Farm3D, 3D-Fauna) by a wide margin on all three categories, e.g., 5.65 vs. 11.19–11.86 cm RMS CD on horse. It also beats the general-purpose Trellis model (6.93 cm) on horse, though the gap narrows on sheep (4.35 vs. 5.91 cm). The primary gap is to the fully supervised DualPM: BAT3R trails by ~1.35 cm on horse, ~1.0 cm on cow, and ~1.05 cm on sheep, representing roughly a 30% relative increase in error. On the Horse-Robust split, the real-image variant (6.01 cm) surpasses both zero-shot foundation models SAM-3D (6.42 cm) and Trellis.2 (8.92 cm) despite using orders of magnitude less 3D supervision, but still lags DualPM (3.84 cm) by ~2.2 cm. Under model-view alignment (which penalizes camera pose errors), BAT3R's advantage over 3D-Fauna is most pronounced (7.73 vs. 15.70 cm on horse), while the gap to DualPM widens slightly (7.73 vs. 5.49 cm), indicating that camera pose estimation remains the weakest link relative to full supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DualPM's dual posed-canonical point map architecture as its reconstruction backbone, but fundamentally rethinks the training data pipeline. Where prior supervised methods (DualPM, Farm3D, 3D-Fauna) require large collections of manually articulated 3D meshes in diverse poses, and unsupervised methods (LASSIE, MagicPony, SAOR) forgo 3D supervision entirely, BAT3R occupies a middle ground: it bootstraps from a single rigged canonical mesh plus unannotated 2D images, iteratively fitting the template to predicted point clouds to synthesize self-consistent 3D supervision. The mesh-fitting stage draws on classical techniques (Kabsch alignment, Huber loss, geodesic-aware sampling) applied in a novel iterative data-generation loop rather than a single test-time optimization as in LASSIE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: it provides a scalable, low-cost pathway to train articulated 3D reconstruction models for categories where diverse 3D assets are scarce or prohibitively expensive to create, reducing the bottleneck from 'hundreds of articulated meshes' to 'one rigged mesh plus a 2D image collection.' However, a performance gap persists relative to fully supervised DualPM, and the method still requires a rigged canonical mesh (though auto-rigging tools can mitigate this). Open questions include whether the approach extends to categories with high intra-class shape variation (beyond quadrupeds), whether it can operate without any 3D template, and whether the iterative process can be accelerated or made more sample-efficient.
