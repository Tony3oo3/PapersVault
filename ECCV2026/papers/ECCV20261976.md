## Problem

Pixel-based feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods are efficient but degrade severely under the minimal cross-view overlap (<15%) typical of ego-centric autonomous driving cameras. Voxel-based dual-branch approaches like Omni-Scene resolve occlusion but uniformly instantiate millions of Gaussians across the entire 3D volume, incurring massive computational redundancy since most driving scenes consist of geometrically deterministic surfaces (flat roads, clear skies) that need no volumetric completion. The paper asks whether structural completion can be decoupled from deterministic regions and concentrated exclusively on the small subset of the scene exhibiting geometric ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse surround-view RGB images (e.g., 6 cameras with ~15% adjacent-view overlap on nuScenes) and produces a combined 3D Gaussian scene representation consisting of pixel-aligned base Gaussians plus a compact set of targeted compensatory Gaussians. Image features are extracted at 4× downsampling via a DINO-pretrained ResNet-50, and predicted per-pixel depth serves as an intermediate signal for both Gaussian placement and ambiguity localization. The final representation supports real-time novel-view rendering (0.058 s latency on nuScenes) and depth estimation, with the targeted Gaussians contributing only ~26% of the total Gaussian count compared to Omni-Scene.

## Method

FocusGS introduces a Targeted Structure Completion paradigm that spatially decouples deterministic surfaces from geometrically uncertain regions. First, a 2D geometric ambiguity manifold is derived by computing central-difference depth gradients, thresholding to isolate occlusion boundaries, and applying morphological dilation to form continuous uncertainty bands; these 2D regions are then lifted into a sparse 3D uncertainty subspace by projecting ambiguous pixels along camera rays with depth-adaptive longitudinal thickness. A lightweight completion module samples a fixed budget of N_q queries uniformly within this subspace and processes them through stacked blocks combining 3D sparse convolution (self-context aggregation) and deformable attention over multi-view features (cross-view aggregation), followed by an MLP that decodes residual position offsets and Gaussian attributes. Training uses a composite objective: global L1 and LPIPS losses on the full scene plus masked photometric and depth losses applied only to pixels within the ambiguity manifold, ensuring gradients are dedicated exclusively to ambiguous regions. This design reduces total Gaussians by ~74% and rendering time by ~34% relative to Omni-Scene while improving PSNR from 24.27 to 24.65 on nuScenes.

## Results

On the ego-centric nuScenes benchmark, FocusGS achieves PSNR 24.65, SSIM 0.754, LPIPS 0.220, and PCC 0.837, outperforming the closest competitor Omni-Scene (PSNR 24.27, PCC 0.800) while reducing rendering latency from 0.088 s to 0.058 s (~34% faster) and cutting the total Gaussian count by ~74%. On the scene-centric RealEstate10K benchmark, MVSplat+FocusGS attains the best scores across all metrics (PSNR 27.02, SSIM 0.892, LPIPS 0.118, PCC 0.374), demonstrating that the targeted structure completion module generalizes beyond the driving domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary competitor Omni-Scene [49], FocusGS wins on every reported metric (PSNR +0.38 dB, SSIM +0.018, LPIPS −0.017, PCC +0.037) while running ~34% faster (0.058 s vs. 0.088 s) and using ~74% fewer Gaussians, confirming that targeted completion on the geometric ambiguity manifold is both more accurate and more efficient than uniform volumetric densification. Compared to STORM [56], the strongest non-Gaussian baseline, FocusGS leads on PSNR (+0.09), SSIM (+0.002), and PCC (+0.049), though STORM retains a marginal LPIPS advantage (0.217 vs. 0.220). FocusGS substantially outperforms the feed-forward pixel-based methods MVSplat [8] (+3.04 dB PSNR, +0.656 PCC) and pixelSplat [5] (+3.14 dB PSNR, +0.836 PCC), as well as the NeRF-based MuRF [53] and light-field AttnRend [11], which suffer severe geometric degradation under the <15% cross-view overlap typical of ego-centric driving. On RealEstate10K, standalone FocusGS is competitive with MVSplat (PSNR 26.32 vs. 26.39) and slightly edges Omni-Scene on PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, while the combined MVSplat+FocusGS configuration achieves the best overall scores, validating the plug-and-play nature of the targeted structure completion module.

## Relation to Prior Work

FocusGS positions itself at the intersection of two existing paradigms for feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting: pixel-based methods (pixelSplat, MVSplat) that are efficient but fail under minimal cross-view overlap, and voxel-based dual-branch methods (Omni-Scene) that achieve structural completeness at the cost of uniform volumetric densification. The key departure is the 'targeted structure completion' paradigm, which decouples completion from deterministic regions by localizing a geometric ambiguity manifold via depth-gradient analysis and restricting Gaussian instantiation to that sparse 3D subspace. Technically, it repurposes sparse 3D convolution and deformable attention—components borrowed from occupancy prediction and object detection—within a tightly constrained spatial budget, rather than operating over the full scene volume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving systems where camera overlap is inherently minimal (<15%) and computational budgets are tight: a 74% reduction in Gaussian count and 34% reduction in rendering time while improving PSNR over the strongest baseline (Omni-Scene) makes high-fidelity 3D reconstruction more deployable. However, the method's reliance on accurate depth estimation for ambiguity localization introduces a vulnerability under adverse conditions (rain, lens artifacts), and the current spatial-only completion strategy does not handle dynamic objects or temporal consistency. Open questions include whether the depth-gradient-based ambiguity signal generalizes to highly dynamic scenes, whether spatiotemporal completion can further reduce redundancy, and whether the fixed query budget (N_q) can be made adaptive per-scene complexity.
