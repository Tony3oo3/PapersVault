## Problem

Continual fine-tuning of vision-language models like CLIP progressively erodes zero-shot generalization and causes catastrophic forgetting, yet existing methods largely ignore the optimization geometry that governs this degradation. While flatness-aware optimization (e.g., SAM) helps in single-task settings, the paper empirically shows that seeking flatness on only the current task distribution is insufficient under sequential distribution shifts, because perturbations derived from a single task fail to capture directions of instability introduced by the gap between broad pre-training knowledge and narrow task-specific objectives.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images paired with text prompts in a class-incremental learning protocol where tasks have non-overlapping class spaces and no task identifiers are available at inference. During training, it additionally consumes predictions from a frozen pre-trained CLIP model as a zero-shot reference and synthesizes noisy visual variants of training images by perturbing latent features with Gaussian noise. The output is a classification model evaluated on cumulative class spaces, reporting average accuracy, last accuracy, forward/backward transfer, and zero-shot accuracy on held-out datasets (Food-101, ImageNet-100, ImageNet-1K).

## Method

DGM is a plug-and-play optimizer that extends SAM's minimax framework by redefining the inner perturbation to be computed from a composite loss rather than the current task loss alone. The composite perturbation loss combines two terms: a zero-shot knowledge loss (KL divergence between the evolving model's predictions and the frozen pre-trained model's predictions on the same batch) and a task-specific robustness loss (training loss evaluated on noisy visual inputs synthesized by adding Gaussian noise to latent feature dimensions). The adversarial perturbation ε is then computed as the normalized gradient of this combined objective, and the outer minimization step updates parameters at the perturbed location. This dual-source perturbation steers optimization toward solutions that are simultaneously flat with respect to pre-trained knowledge erosion and task-specific visual variation, requiring only 2 forward and 2 backward passes per iteration. DGM integrates seamlessly into full-parameter, adapter-based, and LoRA-based continual learning pipelines without additional parameters or architectural changes.

## Results

DGM is a plug-and-play optimizer that consistently improves all six integrated baselines across six benchmarks. On CIFAR-100 (B0_Inc10, Last accuracy), DGM lifts Finetune from 79.30% to 81.12% (+1.82%) and ZSCL from 78.33% to 80.45% (+2.12%). On the largest benchmark, ImageNet-1K (B0_Inc100, Last), Finetune improves from 71.83% to 73.40% (+1.57%). For zero-shot generalization on unseen datasets (Food-101, ImageNet-100, ImageNet-1K), DGM recovers or exceeds the original CLIP zero-shot performance, with ZSCL gaining +5.13% average return over its baseline (75.90% vs. 70.77%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGM is not a standalone method but a plug-and-play optimizer integrated into five representative continual fine-tuning baselines (RAPF, ZSCL, MoE4CL, MG-CLIP, DMNSP) plus a plain Finetune baseline. Across all 36 dataset-configuration combinations in Table 1, DGM improves every baseline without exception, with gains ranging from +0.06% (DMNSP on ImageNet-1K B0_Inc200) to +2.68% (ZSCL on ImageNet-100 B0_Inc10). The largest absolute improvements appear on ZSCL (full-parameter tuning), where DGM adds +2.12% on CIFAR-100 and +2.28% on ImageNet-100, suggesting that unconstrained parameter updates benefit most from the dual-perturbation regularization. On adapter-based methods (RAPF, DMNSP, MoE4CL), gains are more modest (+0.2% to +1.7%) because the architectural constraints already partially limit distributional drift. For zero-shot generalization (Table 2), DGM is most impactful on ZSCL (+5.13% average return, recovering to 75.90% vs. CLIP's 75.35%) and RAPF (+2.46%), while DMNSP sees a smaller +1.26% boost, indicating that methods with weaker built-in zero-shot preservation gain the most from DGM's knowledge-preservation perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) [Foret et al., ICLR 2021] to the setting of continual fine-tuning of vision-language models, where the key departure is redefining the perturbation objective to account for two complementary distributions: the frozen pre-trained model's zero-shot predictions and the current task's visual variations. Prior flatness-aware methods (SAM, GAM, C-Flat) assume a stationary single-task loss landscape, and the paper's empirical analysis (loss landscape visualizations, Hessian eigenvalue tracking) shows this assumption breaks down under sequential distribution shifts in VLMs. The resulting DGM optimizer is positioned as a plug-and-play module orthogonal to existing CIL architectures (adapters, LoRA, MoE), rather than a competing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is that DGM can be dropped into any existing CLIP-based continual learning pipeline with no architectural changes or extra parameters, consistently yielding 1–2% accuracy gains and notably preserving (sometimes even slightly improving) zero-shot generalization after sequential fine-tuning. However, the improvements are modest, the evaluation is limited to CLIP ViT-B/16 on image classification CIL, and the 2× forward/backward overhead may be non-trivial at scale. Open questions include whether the dual-distribution perturbation idea transfers to other foundation model families (LLMs, diffusion models), whether it helps in task-incremental or domain-incremental settings, and whether the specific Gaussian visual perturbation in L_NV is optimal compared to other noise models.
