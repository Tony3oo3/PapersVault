## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for sparse-view CT reconstruction are tied to a fixed acquisition sampling rate and image resolution, requiring separate models for each configuration and failing to generalize across the varying protocols encountered in clinical practice. This discretization binding limits scalability and flexibility, as no single model can handle the range of view counts and detector resolutions used across organs and diagnostic tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260466/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a subsampled CT sinogram p(θ,r) in polar coordinates (view angle θ, detector position r) at any of several sampling rates (9, 18, 36, or 72 views) and produces a reconstructed 2D CT image. A single trained model handles all sampling rates and image resolutions simultaneously; at inference, the sinogram-space operator processes the input at its native angular resolution while the image-space operator adapts to the target output resolution (e.g., 256×256 or 512×512) without retraining or architectural changes.

## Method

CTO is an unrolled network with three cascades, each combining an image-space neural operator NO_i (a U-shaped DISCO block in 2D Cartesian coordinates) with a data-consistency update that enforces Radon-transform fidelity. The key architectural innovation is a sinogram-space operator NO_s defined in polar coordinates (r,θ) with a dual-branch design: a spatial UDNO capturing local geometry-aware correlations and a frequency UDNO operating on the 1D Fourier transform along the detector axis, inspired by the ramp filter in FBP. Both operators use DISCO convolutions, which parametrize kernels as continuous functions in function space and discretize them at the input resolution, ensuring consistent receptive fields across resolutions and convergence to local integral operators. Rotation equivariance is enforced via a modified circular padding along θ that flips boundary data along r at 180°, aligning the network's inductive biases with the sinogram's π-periodicity-with-flip symmetry.

## Results

On the AAPM Low-Dose CT dataset, CTO achieves PSNR of 31.51 / 35.06 / 37.88 dB at 18 / 36 / 72 views, outperforming the closest single-pass baseline GloReDi by 2.0–4.9 dB and the unrolled CNN baseline by 2.4–4.5 dB across all sampling rates. Compared to diffusion-based methods (DPS, ALD), CTO gains 5.7–7.3 dB PSNR while being >500× faster at inference (0.065 s vs. 32.7–51.6 s per image). On the KiTS19 Kidney dataset, CTO averages 4 dB PSNR over the unrolled CNN, 3 dB over DPS, and 5 dB over DuDoTrans across view counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTO outperforms all 10 baselines across every sampling rate on both AAPM and KiTS19. Against the closest competitor GloReDi [28] (single-pass dual-domain), CTO gains 2.0 dB (18-view) to 4.9 dB (72-view) in PSNR. Versus the unrolled CNN baseline [3] (same architecture with standard convolutions replacing DISCO), CTO achieves a consistent 2.4–4.5 dB PSNR improvement, confirming the benefit of function-space kernels over fixed-resolution convolutions. Against diffusion methods DPS [16] and ALD [24], CTO leads by 5.7–7.3 dB PSNR while running >500× faster (0.065 s vs. 32.7–51.6 s inference). Among unrolled-network baselines (Learned PD [2], LEARN [11], RegFormer [56]), CTO gains 6.0–8.0 dB PSNR, with the gap widening at higher view counts. The largest absolute gains are over learning-free methods (FBP: +18–24 dB; SART: +6.5–11.2 dB), as expected for a learned model versus classical reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTO extends the DISCO convolution and UDNO block (developed for compressed-sensing MRI) to the CT domain, adapting them from Cartesian to polar coordinates to exploit the sinogram's angular structure. It follows the unrolled-network paradigm of MODL, replacing the discrete CNN prior with a function-space neural operator, and draws on the Fourier slice theorem to motivate a dual spatial-frequency sinogram branch analogous to the ramp filter in FBP. The key departure from prior dual-domain CT methods (DuDoTrans, GloReDi, etc.) is that all components operate in continuous function space rather than fixed grids, making the architecture inherently resolution-agnostic rather than requiring separate models per sampling rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The resolution-agnostic property directly addresses a practical clinical bottleneck: acquisition settings (view count, detector count, output resolution) vary across organs and protocols, and current methods require retraining or maintaining separate models for each configuration. The 500× inference speedup over diffusion-based SOTA while simultaneously achieving 3 dB higher PSNR makes CTO a compelling practical alternative for deployment. Open questions include extension to 3D cone-beam CT, validation on clinical scanners with non-ideal geometries, uncertainty quantification, and whether the function-space formulation generalizes to other inverse problems beyond tomography.
