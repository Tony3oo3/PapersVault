## Problem

Physically plausible shadows are absent when animating 3DGS avatars composited into 3DGS scenes, causing inserted humans to appear visually detached from their environment. No existing method supports shadow casting at composition time in the fully volumetric setting where both the shadow caster (avatar) and receiver (scene) are 3D Gaussians; prior approaches rely on mesh-based casters, binary ray-Gaussian hit tests, or per-scene baked occlusion that cannot handle dynamic avatars.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3DGS scene (typically ~500K Gaussians), one or more animated 3DGS avatars with per-frame body pose, and a user-specified point or directional light position. It produces per-scene-Gaussian shadow transmittance values T_k ∈ [0,1] that multiplicatively modulate each Gaussian's color before standard α-blending rasterization. The shadow tracer alone runs at ~50 FPS on an NVIDIA H100, with the full pipeline (including 3DGS rasterization) achieving near real-time performance even for avatars with up to 400K Gaussians.

## Method

RAGA casts shadow rays from each scene Gaussian toward the light source and, for every occluding avatar Gaussian, computes exact entry/exit points via a quadratic intersection test against the Gaussian's χ²-confidence ellipsoid (3σ cutoff), then integrates the unnormalized Gaussian opacity profile between those bounds in closed form using the error function. The resulting line integral is normalized by the theoretical maximum integral (a center pass along the longest axis), yielding a dimensionless thickness factor η ∈ [0,1] that captures how much of the Gaussian the ray actually traverses rather than merely whether an intersection occurred. This η modulates each Gaussian's opacity in an exponentiated transmittance update T ← T(1−α)^η, accumulated multiplicatively over all occluders found via BVH traversal over conservative AABBs using NVIDIA OptiX hardware ray tracing. To suppress temporal flickering from pose-dependent Gaussian topology and anisotropic orientation changes, an avatar proxy freezes the generative topology at a canonical pose, replaces anisotropic scales with isotropic radii, and articulates positions via Linear Blend Skinning, guaranteeing a continuous shadow signal with respect to pose.

## Results

On ScanNet++ (5 scenes), RAGA achieves SAE 0.031, SM-IoU 0.847, and BF 0.812 against mesh-based pseudo-GT, substantially outperforming both Gaussian-based baselines (3DGRT: SAE 0.058, SM-IoU 0.741, BF 0.693; RaySplat: SAE 0.062, SM-IoU 0.728, BF 0.679). For temporal stability, the avatar proxy reduces TSC to 0.0018, a 3.4× improvement over the no-proxy variant (0.0061) and a 6.3× improvement over 3DGRT (0.0114). The shadow tracer runs at ~50 FPS on an NVIDIA H100 with ~500K scene Gaussians, and a 2AFC perceptual study shows raters prefer RAGA over no-shadow 73% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAGA outperforms both Gaussian-based baselines on all three shadow-quality metrics. Against 3DGRT (modified), RAGA reduces SAE by 47% (0.031 vs. 0.058), improves SM-IoU by 10.6 points (0.847 vs. 0.741), and raises BF by 11.9 points (0.812 vs. 0.693); the gap is attributed to 3DGRT's icosahedron proxy producing hard, artifact-laden shadow boundaries without penumbrae. Against RaySplat (modified), the improvements are similar in magnitude (SAE 0.031 vs. 0.062, SM-IoU 0.847 vs. 0.728, BF 0.812 vs. 0.679), with RaySplat's binary hit-test reducing to a max-response evaluation that lacks proper volumetric attenuation. On temporal stability (TSC), RAGA with the avatar proxy (0.0018) is 6.3× more stable than 3DGRT (0.0114) and 6.7× more stable than RaySplat (0.0121), confirming that the isotropic proxy and topology locking are critical for eliminating shadow flicker during animation.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAGA builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and the ray-tracing infrastructure established by 3DGRT and RaySplat, but departs fundamentally from their binary hit-test and icosahedron-proxy intersection strategies by formulating shadow attenuation as a normalized ray-Gaussian line integral with a closed-form error-function solution. It also extends the line of work on animatable 3DGS avatars (HUGS, ASH, Avatar-ReX) by addressing the previously unaddressed problem of casting those avatars' shadows onto 3DGS scene receivers at composition time, a setting where classical shadow mapping, per-scene inverse rendering, and existing ray-splatting methods all fail. The avatar proxy (isotropic regularization + canonical topology locking + LBS) is a new stabilization mechanism not present in prior Gaussian shadow or avatar work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a concrete gap in 3DGS-based content creation pipelines (virtual production, CG compositing, robotics simulators) where inserted avatars currently appear visually detached due to missing shadows, and it does so without the lossy mesh-extraction step that would be required by classical approaches. The 50 FPS shadow tracer on an H100 makes the technique practical for interactive use. Key limitations remain: only point and directional lights are supported (no area lights), the avatar and scene lighting stays baked into the 3DGS representation (no relighting), and light positions must be specified manually rather than estimated from the scene. Open questions include whether the normalized line-integral formulation extends naturally to area lights and whether the avatar proxy can be made differentiable to support learned shadow-aware avatar optimization.
