## Problem

ViT-based weather forecasters apply uniform O(L²D) self-attention computation to every spatial token, yet atmospheric fields are highly autocorrelated and much of the intermediate per-token computation is redundant. Existing token-efficiency methods (pruning, merging, mask-embedding fusion) are incompatible with weather forecasting's dense prediction requirement: every grid cell must retain a physically meaningful representation, especially under autoregressive rollout, so tokens cannot be dropped, fused, or replaced by synthetic placeholders.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 40 gridded atmospheric variables (six at seven pressure levels plus four surface fields) from ERA5 at 1.40625°, 1.0°, or 0.25° resolution, patchified into L = 8,192–16,200 tokens per sample. It produces spatially dense 120-hour forecasts for Z500, T850, T2m, and U10 on the original latitude-longitude grid; the generative EDM variant additionally takes a noise level σ and outputs an ensemble of denoised future states. The critical constraint is that the output must be a complete, physically coherent field at every grid position—no missing or placeholder cells—making it fundamentally different from image generation where skipped tokens can be reconstructed from learned masks.

## Method

Sparse-Reslim is a parameter-free plug-in that partitions N transformer blocks into dense early (n₁), sparse middle (n₂), and dense late (n₃) stages, routing only K = ⌊rL⌋ randomly selected tokens (default r = 0.25) through the expensive middle blocks. The key mechanism is a residual delta formulation: after the sparse blocks produce updated representations h, the module computes Δ = h − h₀ and scatters only this delta back to the original token positions, so unselected tokens receive zero delta and retain their pre-routing representations exactly—forming an implicit identity skip with no mask embeddings, fusion layers, or learnable parameters. For the generative EDM variant, an asymmetric attention design sparsifies self-attention over K target tokens while retaining the full conditioning sequence as cross-attention keys/values, preserving meteorological context needed for denoising. Random token selection (rather than attention-score or learned-head selection) acts as a stochastic regularizer on the token dimension, and ablations show it outperforms deterministic selection strategies while adding zero overhead.

## Results

On the deterministic Res-Slim-ViT at 1.0° (16,200 tokens), Sparse-Reslim (r=0.25, split 2-8-2) reduces Z500 RMSE from 868.7 to 768.3 (−11.6%) and raises ACC from 0.551 to 0.651, while T2m RMSE drops from 3.19 to 2.68 and ACC rises from 0.797 to 0.859. At the operational 0.25° resolution, Z500 RMSE improves from 1042 to 892 and ACC from 0.548 to 0.670. Training is ~2.5× faster in wall-clock time (reaching 3.18× at 0.25°) with over 2.2× lower peak VRAM, and the generative EDM variant also improves all four variables (e.g., Z500 RMSE 951.3→889.7, ACC 0.517→0.571) at 1.82× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the dense Reslim (identical architecture with sparse routing disabled, r=1), which isolates the effect of sparse token routing. Sparse-Reslim outperforms the dense baseline on every metric (RMSE, ACC, SSIM) for all four target variables (Z500, T850, T2m, U10) at all three resolutions (1.40625°, 1.0°, 0.25°), with the largest gains at 1.0° and 0.25° where token counts are highest. The generative EDM variant follows the same pattern: Sparse-Reslim EDM beats Dense EDM on all four variables (e.g., Z500 RMSE 889.7 vs. 951.3; T2m ACC 0.802 vs. 0.771), though the margin is smaller due to fewer sparse middle blocks (4 vs. 8). In 10-day autoregressive rollout, Sparse-Reslim maintains a consistent ACC advantage over the dense baseline (0.69 vs. 0.53 at day 10 for T2m), confirming that the residual identity path prevents compounding error. Ablations further show that random selection outperforms both attention-score-based top-K selection (RMSE 784.1) and a learned difficulty head (RMSE 779.8), validating the zero-parameter design.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sparse-Reslim extends the dense-sparse-dense block scheduling idea from Sprint [20] (originally for diffusion-based image generation) to spatiotemporal weather forecasting, but departs fundamentally in how unselected tokens are handled: instead of replacing them with learned mask embeddings and fusing via a linear projection, it computes a residual delta on the routed subset and scatters only that delta back, leaving unselected tokens at their exact pre-routing representations. The backbone architectures are drawn from the ORBIT-2 exascale system [27] (Res-Slim-ViT) and the EDM diffusion framework [11], and the method is positioned against the broader ViT-efficiency literature on token pruning, merging, and windowed attention, which the authors argue is incompatible with the fixed-grid dense-prediction requirement of weather forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that sparse token routing can be applied to efficient training of ViT-based weather models in both deterministic and generative settings, yielding ~2.5× training speedup and >2.2× peak-memory reduction while simultaneously improving forecast accuracy across all variables and resolutions. The finding that random token selection outperforms both attention-score-based and learned-selection strategies is practically useful (zero parameters, zero overhead) but also raises open questions about whether adaptive, dynamics-aware routing (e.g., concentrating compute on storm fronts) could push accuracy further. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (ERA5), a single lead time (120 h) for most experiments, and the generative model's smaller sparse-block count (4 vs. 8) yielding a more modest 1.82× speedup; whether the accuracy gains persist under longer autoregressive horizons or at sub-0.25° resolutions remains to be tested.
