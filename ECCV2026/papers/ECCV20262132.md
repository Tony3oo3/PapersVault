## Problem

Egocentric human motion forecasting is inherently ill-posed because the camera captures little of the body and sparse wearable cues are consistent with many plausible futures. Existing methods either omit explicit 3D scene representations whose spatial and semantic context resolves motion ambiguity, or treat pose and language as separate inference streams, forgoing the cross-modal consistency that arises from holistic joint prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262132/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three-point tracking (6D head and hand poses), 3D scene features (semantic features from Mask2Former and EVA-ViT-G lifted onto a voxelized point cloud, compressed to 32 Q-Former query embeddings), and per-frame CLIP video embeddings. All four outputs—past pose, future pose, past narration, and future description—are generated in a single autoregressive pass over 5-second segments at 10 FPS, with pose sequences tokenized via a PQ-VAE into indices over a 4096-entry codebook. The output follows a structured order: spatial scene description → past & future motion tokens → past & future motion text, so that spatial reasoning propagates into both pose and language generation.

## Method

Ego3DLM is a GPT-2 medium LM extended with pose tokens and 3D scene conditioning, trained in three stages. Stage I pretrains the LM on ~535K spatial QA pairs (directional clearance, obstruction, navigable direction) and ~115K semantic QA pairs (object identity, place relations, attributes) to instill 3D scene awareness before any motion reasoning. Stage II instruction-tunes the model to generate all four outputs in one autoregressive pass, prepending an explicit spatial scene reasoning step so that obstacle and free-space analysis informs subsequent pose and text generation. Stage III applies GRPO with a composite reward combining intra-modal signals (JPE for motion, BLEU-4 for text) and an inter-modal matching reward that penalizes embedding-space distance between ground-truth descriptions and predicted motions, predicted descriptions and ground-truth motions, and the simultaneously predicted motion-description pair—directly optimizing cross-modal coherence that likelihood-based training cannot enforce.

## Results

On the Nymeria benchmark, Ego3DLM achieves state-of-the-art performance across all motion prediction and tracking metrics. For future motion prediction, it reduces JPE to 364.5 mm (vs. 424.3 for UniEgoMotion, the strongest baseline) and FID to 0.0160 (vs. 0.1530), while also attaining the highest diversity (1.0624). For motion tracking, Ego3DLM reduces APE to 96.4 mm (vs. 152.2 for UniEgoMotion, a 36.7% improvement) and Root error to 19.57 mm. On language metrics, it achieves Bleu-4 of 0.1039 for future motion description and 0.1107 for past narration, with a motion–description alignment distance (d_pp) of 4.2571, a 56.8% improvement over EgoLM's 9.8686.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ego3DLM outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest motion-prediction baseline UniEgoMotion [52], it reduces JPE by 14.1% (364.5 vs. 424.3 mm), FDE₂ₛ by 13.3% (312.6 vs. 360.4 mm), and FID by 89.6% (0.0160 vs. 0.1530), while also achieving higher diversity (1.0624 vs. 0.9022). For motion tracking, the gap widens dramatically: APE drops from 152.2 to 96.4 mm (−36.7%), upper-body APE from 79.4 to 53.1 mm (−33.1%), and lower-body APE from 233.3 to 152.7 mm (−34.5%). Against FIction [1], which also uses 3D scene geometry, Ego3DLM improves JPE by 35.4% (364.5 vs. 564.7) and tracking APE by 46.8% (96.4 vs. 181.1). On language metrics, Ego3DLM surpasses EgoLM [30] (instruction-tuning variant) by 60.0% in future-description Bleu-4 (0.1039 vs. 0.0649) and 70.4% in past-narration Bleu-4 (0.1107 vs. 0.0649), and reduces the motion–description alignment distance d_pp from 9.8686 to 4.2571 (−56.8%). Compared to the general-purpose LLM baseline (Qwen 2.5 7B), Ego3DLM achieves 5.1× higher future-description Bleu-4 (0.1039 vs. 0.0202) and 4.8× higher past-narration Bleu-4 (0.1107 vs. 0.0232), demonstrating that motion-grounded generation substantially outperforms text-only generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ego3DLM extends the egocentric motion-language modeling line initiated by EgoLM (which tokenizes SMPL poses and jointly models motion and text) and the 3D-scene-grounded forecasting approaches of FIction and HMD² (which inject scene geometry directly into prediction networks). The key departure is twofold: rather than treating 3D scene features as a passive conditioning signal, the model is explicitly pretrained to reason about spatial layout and semantic content before any motion task is introduced; and rather than decoding pose and language through separate streams, all four outputs (past pose, future pose, past narration, future description) are generated in a single autoregressive pass, with a spatial scene reasoning step prepended to propagate constraints downstream. The GRPO stage with an inter-modal matching reward (measuring embedding-space consistency between predicted motion and description) is a novel application of group-relative RL to cross-modal coherence in motion generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D scene reasoning and holistic cross-modal decoding together yield substantially more physically plausible and semantically coherent egocentric motion forecasts than either modality-specific prediction or separate-stream generation, with the 3D scene input identified as the single most critical factor in ablations. A key limitation is the assumption that a precomputed 3D scene is available, which is reasonable for familiar XR environments but restricts deployment to novel or unstructured settings; the authors flag incremental on-the-fly scene registration as a natural next step. The inter-modal GRPO reward, which simultaneously improves both motion accuracy and description quality, suggests a general recipe for enforcing cross-modal consistency in multi-output generative models beyond motion prediction.
