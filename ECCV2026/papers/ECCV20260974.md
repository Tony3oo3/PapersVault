## Problem

In realistic long-tailed semi-supervised learning, noisy pseudo-labels drift toward majority classes, causing confirmation bias and semantic collapse for tail categories. Existing debiasing strategies operate at the logit or output level and lack an explicit mechanism to align intermediate feature representations between uncertain unlabeled samples and reliable class prototypes, leaving the feature-level geometry of the decision boundary unregularized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260974/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 32×32 or 64×64 for ImageNet-127/1K) split into a small labeled set and a larger unlabeled set under long-tailed class distributions. Beyond standard class probability outputs, the framework maintains a dynamic Prototype Atlas storing per-class anchor features (2–8% of dataset size) that are queried at inference time to construct the bridge path. The bridge is injected at the penultimate feature map (Stage 4 for ResNet-50, final token for ViT) before the classification head, so the output distribution is produced from a bridged residual feature rather than the raw student representation.

## Method

GBC constructs a class-conditional Gaussian Feature Bridge in latent space by linearly interpolating between an unlabeled sample's feature and a same-class anchor retrieved from a dynamically updated Prototype Atlas, with a noise schedule σ(t)=ν√(t(1−t)) and a Beta(2,2) sampling of t. The bridged point is softly merged into the student's hidden representation via a residual-style update gated by ω(t)=4t(1−t), which emphasizes mid-bridge states of highest semantic uncertainty. A bridge consistency loss then aligns the student's prediction at the bridged state with a geometrically interpolated target distribution (softmax of log-interpolated endpoint predictions), weighted by a class-frequency reweighting factor to emphasize tail categories. BridgeMix extends this by interpolating both sample features and their anchors using a confidence-guided coefficient λᵢ=oᵢ/(oᵢ+oⱼ), so high-confidence pseudo-labels steer lower-confidence ones along the bridge manifold. The full objective combines standard supervised cross-entropy, consistency-based unsupervised loss, and the bridge consistency term with a linearly warmed-up weight β=0.75, adding only ~1.7% runtime overhead and 0.5M parameters over the base SSL pipeline.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (γ_ℓ=100, consistent), GBC achieves 92.30% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing SimPro (85.66%) by 6.64% and SimPro+Manifold MixUp (89.14%) by 3.16%. On ImageNet-127 (γ_t≈286, 64×64), GBC reaches 68.6%, outperforming Meta-Expert (67.4%) and SimPro (67.0%). On full ImageNet-1K (γ_t=1, 64×64), GBC attains 27.5% Top-1, a 2.1% gain over Meta-Expert and 2.5% over SimPro. Computational overhead is minimal: only +1.7% wall-clock time and +0.5M parameters over FixMatch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GBC consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across every distribution setting. Against the strongest ReaLTSSL baseline SimPro [10], GBC gains 6.64% (consistent), 0.75% (uniform), 0.94% (reversed), 0.66% (middle), and 1.52% (head-tail) on CIFAR-10-LT at γ=100, with the largest margins under imbalanced and reversed distributions where pseudo-label noise is most severe. Against SimPro+Manifold MixUp [38], GBC wins on all four imbalanced settings (e.g., +3.16% consistent, +3.13% head-tail) but is marginally outperformed on the uniform setting (94.51 vs 94.19), confirming that class-conditional bridging is most beneficial under skew. On ImageNet-127 (64×64, γ_t≈286), GBC surpasses Meta-Expert [16] by 1.2% and SimPro by 1.6%; on ImageNet-1K (64×64), it leads Meta-Expert by 2.1% and SimPro by 2.5%. Logit-level debiasing methods (ACR [42], DyTrim [5]) and earlier feature-level approaches (SAW [21], Adsh [14], DePL [39], BaCon [12], CPE [23]) are all dominated by GBC on both CIFAR and ImageNet benchmarks, with gaps of 4–10% on ImageNet-127.

## Relation to Prior Work

GBC is built directly on the SimPro ReaLTSSL pipeline (ICML 2024), which established the realistic long-tailed SSL protocol with distribution mismatch between labeled and unlabeled data, and on MixUp/Manifold MixUp for feature-level interpolation. Its conceptual departure is importing the Schrödinger Bridge / optimal-transport framing into SSL: rather than debiasing at the logit level (as ACR, DASO, or DyTrim do) or applying class-agnostic feature mixing, GBC constructs class-conditional Gaussian paths in latent space between unlabeled samples and reliable anchors stored in a dynamic Prototype Atlas. BridgeMix further extends MixUp by making the interpolation coefficient confidence-guided and by interpolating both sample features and their corresponding anchors simultaneously, a combination not present in prior interpolation-based SSL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-overhead (+1.7% runtime) regularization module that can be plugged into existing SSL pipelines, and its geometric interpretation (Lipschitz continuity, decision-boundary flattening) offers a clearer mechanistic account of why consistency regularization helps under imbalance. However, the Schrödinger Bridge connection is largely metaphorical—the actual path is linear interpolation with a simple noise schedule—so the theoretical contribution, while formally stated, does not unlock new algorithmic possibilities beyond what a well-motivated linear bridge already provides. Open questions include whether the Prototype Atlas mechanism generalizes to non-vision modalities or streaming settings, whether the class-adaptive threshold strategy (flagged but not fully explored) is necessary for very high-imbalance regimes, and whether the approach's gains persist when the teacher-student gap is already small.
