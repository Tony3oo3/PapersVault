## Problem

Radar semantic segmentation is hampered by sparse, noisy, and weakly semantic measurements, and existing methods rely on local convolutions or pairwise interactions that cannot capture the higher-order relational structure formed by multiple radar returns from the same physical object. Additionally, multi-view radar projections exhibit unequal measurement densities and partial overlap, making conventional balanced alignment strategies brittle under realistic sensing inconsistencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262644/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D projections (Range-Doppler 256×64, Range-Angle 256×256, Angle-Doppler 256×64) extracted from a 3D RAD tensor, processed as temporal sequences of 5 consecutive frames. It produces dense per-pixel semantic segmentation masks for the RD and RA views (4 classes on CARRADA, 2 on RADIal), and on RADIal additionally outputs detection boxes with range and angle regression errors. On RADIal, where only a single-view input is available, the multi-view UOT and consistency losses are disabled, isolating the single-view hypergraph backbone.

## Method

HyperRadar refines view-specific encoder features using a learnable hypergraph where a soft incidence matrix (softmax over M learnable hyperedges) enables higher-order message passing among spatially and spectrally related radar tokens, capturing object-level structure that local convolutions miss. Cross-view alignment is performed via entropically regularised Unbalanced Optimal Transport solved with a differentiable generalised Sinkhorn solver (30 iterations), which relaxes strict mass preservation to accommodate the inherent density mismatch between RD and RA projections. An adaptive channel-wise attention module then selectively emphasises informative responses before shallow view-specific decoders produce segmentation logits. Training is end-to-end with a combined loss of cross-entropy plus soft Dice for segmentation, plus two cross-view consistency regularisers (L2 coherence and Huber loss) that enforce agreement between RD and RA predictions along their shared range dimension.

## Results

On CARRADA, HyperRadar achieves 63.8% mIoU and 75.2% mDice in the RD view, and 44.4% mIoU and 54.3% mDice in the RA view, improving over the strongest prior method TransRadar by +1.7 and +1.5 mIoU respectively. On RADIal, HyperRadar reaches 83.4% mIoU for segmentation and 98.5% AP / 98.9% AR for detection, outperforming TransRadar by +2.3 mIoU and +1.2 AP. The method also matches the best range error (0.10 m) while reducing angle error to 0.08°, and requires slightly fewer GFLOPs (1132.13 vs. 1138.57) and less GPU memory (3.18 vs. 3.25 GB) than TransRadar while running faster (10.55 vs. 9.6 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperRadar outperforms all 11 baselines on both CARRADA and RADIal. On CARRADA RD, it beats the closest competitor TransRadar [6] by +1.7 mIoU (63.8 vs. 62.1) and +1.8 mDice, with the largest per-class gains on pedestrian (+2.5 IoU) and cyclist (+2.2 IoU). On CARRADA RA, the margin over TransRadar narrows to +1.5 mIoU (44.4 vs. 42.9), but HyperRadar still leads on every foreground class. Earlier radar-specific methods (TMVA-Net, RAMP-CNN, PeakConv) trail by 2–7 mIoU in RD and 1–18 mIoU in RA, while generic architectures (FCN-8s, U-Net, DeepLabv3+) and weakly-supervised RSS-Net/MVNet are substantially weaker, particularly on sparse classes like cyclist. On RADIal, HyperRadar surpasses TransRadar by +2.3 mIoU (83.4 vs. 81.1) and +1.2 AP, and outperforms C-M DNN [13] by +3.0 mIoU and FFTRadNet [24] by +9.4 mIoU, confirming that the hypergraph + UOT backbone generalises to single-view settings. Pixor [34] is the weakest detection baseline on RADIal (96.6% AP, 81.7% AR), further highlighting the gap between point-cloud-oriented and radar-native representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established but previously unconnected ideas for radar perception: learnable hypergraph message passing (building on HGNN/HyperGCN-style higher-order relational modelling) and Unbalanced Optimal Transport (building on entropically regularised UOT solvers) for correspondence-free distribution alignment. It departs from the dominant radar segmentation lineage—TMVA-Net, TransRadar, PeakConv, RAMP-CNN—which relies on convolutional or pairwise attention over grid-based radar projections, by replacing local/pairwise interactions with explicit hyperedge-based group reasoning and by replacing balanced transport or simple concatenation with UOT that tolerates the mass imbalance inherent to different radar view integrations. The cross-view consistency regularisation along the shared range axis extends the multi-view fusion strategy of TMVA-Net with a geometrically grounded penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that higher-order relational structure (multiple returns from one object spanning non-adjacent bins) and view-dependent mass imbalance are first-order challenges in radar segmentation, and that addressing them with hypergraphs and UOT yields consistent gains across two benchmarks without increasing compute. However, the method is limited to 2D RA/RD/AD projections rather than the full 3D RAD tensor, the cross-view constraint only exploits the shared range axis (no explicit temporal or full 3D geometric consistency), and evaluation is confined to two datasets. Open questions include whether the hypergraph-UOT combination transfers to richer 3D radar tensors, longer temporal windows, or multi-modal radar-camera fusion settings, and whether the modest gains (+1.7/+2.3 mIoU) scale to more challenging or diverse sensor configurations.
