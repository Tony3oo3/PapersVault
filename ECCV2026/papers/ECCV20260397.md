## Problem

Sign language translation models are trained on datasets captured under highly constrained conditions—near-frontal cameras, studio backgrounds, and limited signer pools—making them brittle to real-world shifts in viewpoint, background, and signer identity. Conventional pixel-level augmentations cannot synthesize the structured, 3D-consistent variations that characterize these shifts, leaving a critical coverage blind spot in both training data and evaluation benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260397/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes sign language video clips from seven source corpora (Phoenix14T, How2Sign, OpenASL, ASL50K, YouTube-ASL, CSL-Daily, CSL-News) along with spherical camera presets (azimuth, elevation, radius), target background scenes, and SMPL-X/FLAME identity parameters for cross-reenactment. It outputs ~1M augmented clips (7.41s average length) each tagged with metadata specifying the augmentation factor axis (viewpoint, background, identity, post-rendering) and a 10-level severity score, organized into 8:1:1 train/val/test splits at the source-clip level to prevent leakage.

## Method

The augmentation pipeline operates in three stages that factorize visual variation into orthogonal axes. Stage 1 applies diffusion-based background replacement (FlowPortal + IC-Light) confined to the background region via a video matting alpha matte, producing illumination-shifted scene variants. Stage 2 uses EHM-Tracker to fit SMPL-X + FLAME per frame, factorizing clip-level identity (body shape, face shape, joint offsets) from time-varying motion (body/hand/jaw poses, facial expression); GUAVA then reconstructs a 3D Gaussian avatar and renders novel views under 10 spherical camera presets (static or dynamically interpolated), while cross-identity reenactment swaps the identity block from one subject with the motion block from another. Stage 3 applies lightweight post-rendering augmentations (geometric transforms, photometric jitter, mild temporal resampling, compression) to emulate in-the-wild capture artifacts. The key design principle is that all stages preserve sign motion and linguistic annotations, enabling severity-stratified robustness evaluation across a four-setting protocol (source-train/source-test, source-train/augmented-test, augmented-train/augmented-test, augmented-train/source-test).

## Results

Under the four-setting protocol, models trained on source benchmarks suffer large BLEU-4 drops when evaluated on SignNet-1M's augmented test split (Setting B vs. A): SpaMo on Phoenix14T drops from 22.49 to 7.81 (Gap = −14.68), and UniSign on OpenASL drops from 22.67 to 8.12 (Gap = −14.55). Training on SignNet-1M (Setting C) recovers most of this loss, yielding Gains of +11.17 (SpaMo/Phoenix14T), +14.71 (UniSign/OpenASL), and +13.69 (GASLT/Phoenix14T) over Setting B. Crucially, SignNet-1M-trained models also improve on the original source test sets (Setting D vs. A): SpaMo gains +5.29 BLEU-4 on Phoenix14T and +8.37 on How2Sign, confirming that augmentation helps under natural distributions as well.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five model architectures across three model families (RGB gloss-free SLT, RGB+Pose gloss-based SLT, and pose-based SLR) to demonstrate that SignNet-1M's benefits are architecture-agnostic. SpaMo [9] (gloss-free, RGB) shows the largest absolute Gap (−14.68 BLEU-4 on Phoenix14T) but recovers +11.17 with SignNet-1M training, and also improves +5.29 on the source test (Setting D). GASLT [27] (gloss-free, RGB) exhibits the most extreme collapse under augmentation (15.74 → 1.26, Gap −14.48) yet gains the most from SignNet-1M training (+13.69), suggesting weaker baselines benefit most from augmented diversity. FPT4SLT [4] (gloss-free, RGB) shows a moderate Gap (−11.93) and Gain (+11.47) on Phoenix14T. UniSign [13] (RGB+Pose, gloss-based) achieves the largest Gain on OpenASL (+14.71) and a strong +8.19 on CSL-Daily, while its Gap on How2Sign (−8.03) is smaller than SpaMo's (−3.86), indicating pose streams provide some inherent viewpoint robustness. Online-CSLR [36] (RGB+Pose, SLR) shows the most dramatic WER% degradation (22.21% → 51.45%, Gap −29.24%) and the largest absolute recovery (+23.26%) on Phoenix14T, confirming that recognition tasks are particularly sensitive to visual shifts that SignNet-1M addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper assembles three existing generative components—3DGS-based novel-view rendering (GUAVA), diffusion-based scene/identity editing (FlowPortal + IC-Light), and standard post-rendering transforms—into a sign-language-specific augmentation pipeline that prior SLT work has not attempted at scale. It departs from the conventional pixel-level augmentation paradigm (RandAugment, AugMix, CutMix) by arguing that sign meaning depends on 3D-consistent hand/face motion, so structured, geometry-aware shifts along viewpoint, background, and identity axes are necessary. On the evaluation side, it extends the standard i.i.d. train/test protocol used by Phoenix14T, How2Sign, and CSL-Daily with a four-setting (A–D) framework and severity-stratified analysis that explicitly isolates factor-specific robustness, a diagnostic dimension absent from prior SLT benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SignNet-1M provides the first large-scale (~1 M clips, 3 languages) augmented sign-language resource with controlled, factorized visual shifts, giving the community a practical tool to stress-test and improve SLT/SLR models beyond studio-conditioned benchmarks. The four-setting protocol and severity stratification offer a reusable diagnostic template for quantifying where models break under realistic deployment shifts. Key limitations include reliance on single-image 3DGS reconstruction (degrading fine hand detail at large viewpoint changes), coverage of only DGS/ASL/CSL, and evaluation on a limited set of model families; whether the synthetic shifts truly proxy in-the-wild recordings remains an open empirical question.
