## Problem

Mixed-supervised 3D tumor segmentation combines a small set of pixel-wise masks with abundant bounding-box labels, but existing methods largely ignore the distribution gaps between the datasets supplying these heterogeneous supervision signals. CT reconstruction kernels introduce frequency-domain shifts that degrade cross-domain generalization, and naively bolting domain-adaptation modules onto a mixed-supervision pipeline is non-trivial because quality assessment and precise boundary classification are fundamentally different objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260008/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 96×96×96 3D CT patch centered on a pre-localized nodule candidate (first-stage detection is assumed complete). During training, the same patch is additionally fed through KernelWave to produce smooth and sharp frequency-shifted variants, and the box-annotated branch receives bounding-box coordinates as auxiliary supervision. At inference, only the segmentation branch is active, producing a single voxel-wise foreground probability map per patch; the detection branch is discarded.

## Method

WCC4MS decomposes each input volume via a two-level 3D Haar wavelet transform into one low-frequency approximation and seven high-frequency detail subbands, then scales the HF coefficients by δ=4 (sharp) or 1/δ (smooth) before inverse reconstruction to synthesize kernel-shifted variants without requiring raw acquisition metadata. A dual-branch architecture with shared backbones (3D UNet or 3D UNetr) routes mask-annotated patches to a Dice-loss segmentation decoder and box-annotated patches to a detection decoder (classification + IoU regression), while a cross-branch KL-divergence loss enforces output-distribution consistency between the two branches. A lightweight domain-classification head per branch acts as a domain-aware regularizer—retaining quality-sensitive cues rather than forcing invariance—so the encoder learns features that are both task-relevant and robust to reconstruction-kernel variation. The four loss terms (segmentation, detection, KL consistency, domain classification) are jointly optimized with weights (1, 1, 0.5, 0.1), and the box branch is used exclusively during training to exploit weak annotations.

## Results

On the primary CT evaluation (3D UNet backbone, 1:2 mask-to-box ratio), WCC4MS achieves DSC of 80.32% / HD95 of 1.52 mm on LIDC-IDRI, 57.93% / 7.06 mm on MSD-Lung, and 65.84% / 7.98 mm on LNDb, setting new bests on all six metric-dataset pairs. These gains exceed the fully supervised 3D UNet baseline by up to 3.1% DSC (LIDC-IDRI) and 2.6% DSC (LNDb), and surpass the strongest mixed-supervised competitor (WSL4MIS) by 2.7% DSC on LIDC-IDRI. Under the leave-one-domain-out protocol, WCC4MS reaches an average DSC of 59.88% and HD95 of 9.68 mm, outperforming the next-best DG method (GTVSeg) by 1.95% DSC and 1.01 mm HD95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WCC4MS outperforms all 10 baselines (4 weakly supervised, 5 mixed-supervised, 1 fully supervised) on every dataset and both metrics in the primary 3D UNet comparison. The closest competitor is WSL4MIS [20], which WCC4MS beats by 2.70% DSC on LIDC-IDRI (80.32 vs 77.62), 0.92% on MSD-Lung, and 1.85% on LNDb; the gap widens to over 11% DSC against the best weakly supervised method (ReliableMD [8]). Against the fully supervised 3D UNet [28], WCC4MS gains 3.06% DSC on LIDC-IDRI while using only one-third of the mask annotations, demonstrating that the box-branch supervision and wavelet consistency effectively compensate for reduced dense labels. In the domain-generalization setting (Table 3), WCC4MS leads GTVSeg [35] by 1.95% average DSC and 1.01 mm average HD95, and outperforms M2CAN [43], XNet [45], and MKCNet [4] by 2–4% DSC. In the SAM comparison (Table 4), WCC4MS exceeds SAM-Med3D [34] by 1.95% DSC on LIDC-IDRI and 6.70% on LNDb, confirming that task-specific mixed supervision with wavelet consistency surpasses general-purpose foundation models for small-lesion segmentation. The only marginal loss is HD95 on MSD-Lung with the 3D UNetr backbone (8.21 vs MIST's 8.13), a 0.08 mm difference that is within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

WCC4MS synthesizes three previously separate research threads—mixed-supervised segmentation (MIST, ConfKD, MixSegNet, MSDN), domain generalization for medical imaging (MKCNet, XNet, M2CAN, GTVSeg), and wavelet-based frequency manipulation (Wt-BCP, multi-kernel CT analysis)—into a single dual-branch framework. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, the paper repurposes 3D discrete wavelet decomposition as a metadata-free domain augmentation mechanism (KernelWave) that approximates reconstruction-kernel frequency shifts, a role previously unexplored in the mixed-supervision literature. The authors explicitly distinguish their approach from foundation models and from semi-supervised teacher-student methods, positioning the contribution as a practical, compute-efficient alternative for clinical deployment where annotation budgets and domain heterogeneity coexist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically important gap: mixed-supervised methods that ignore inter-dataset distribution shift can degrade significantly in multi-site clinical settings, and the wavelet-based frequency proxy offers a lightweight, metadata-free way to bridge this gap without requiring raw CT acquisition parameters. Cross-modality validation on MRI (BraTS, MSD-BT, FeTS) suggests the frequency-domain consistency principle may generalize beyond CT, though the authors acknowledge this remains an open question. Limitations include the assumption that first-stage nodule detection is already completed, the focus on lung tumors, and the fact that KernelWave is an approximation rather than an exact reconstruction-kernel simulation—extending the framework to weaker annotation regimes (e.g., scribbles, points) and other anatomical sites is left for future work.
