## Problem

Existing monocular 3D detection methods predict objects as compact 3D entities (oriented cuboids, 6D poses) in camera or world coordinates, making image-plane geometry—projected 3D box corners with depths—accessible only when camera intrinsics are known. This is a critical limitation for in-the-wild applications (controllable generation, diffusion-based editing, visual servoing) that consume geometry directly in the image plane and often lack reliable intrinsics. The paper asks whether image-plane 3D geometry can be predicted directly from a 2D box without intrinsics, and how to evaluate such pixel-aligned outputs.

## Input / Output

MoCA3D takes a single RGB image (resized to 512×512 with aspect-ratio-preserving letterboxing) and a tight oracle 2D bounding box as input, and produces eight projected 3D box corner locations (u, v in pixel coordinates) paired with per-corner virtual depths. Crucially, no camera intrinsics are required at inference; the output is natively in image-plane space. When intrinsics are available at evaluation, the corner-depth pairs can be unprojected into a shared 3D representation for comparison with standard 3D detection baselines.

## Method

MoCA3D uses a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone to extract geometry-aware features, which are conditioned by a learned box prior map encoding per-pixel center-normalized offsets and box-normalized coordinates. A box-conditioned 3D Geometry Transformer then refines these tokens: unlike DETR-style detectors where object queries attend to image features, MoCA3D reverses the conditioning direction so that dense image tokens serve as queries and a small set of box embeddings (N_q=9) provide keys/values in cross-attention, letting every spatial token incorporate the instance specification. The decoder output is fused with backbone features, upsampled 4×, and fed to two lightweight convolutional heads that predict eight corner heatmaps (sigmoid-bounded) and eight per-corner depth maps (softplus-bounded). Corners are extracted via differentiable soft-argmax with inverse-temperature β, and depths are sampled at predicted corner locations via bilinear interpolation. Training uses a peak-weighted SmoothL1 heatmap loss (coarse), a coordinate-level refinement loss on soft-argmax outputs (fine), and a confidence-weighted depth loss where predicted heatmaps act as per-pixel reliability weights, with a warm-up schedule that first optimizes only the coarse term before enabling refinement and depth losses. The model has 19M trainable parameters (57× fewer than DetAny3D) and trains in ~60 GPU-hours on 3× RTX 3090s.

## Results

Under the oracle-2D box protocol on Omni3D, MoCA3D achieves the lowest PAG_uv (image-plane corner error) across all six sub-datasets, improving the aggregate PAG_uv by 22.8% over the strongest prior method (DetAny3D). On PAG_d (relative depth error), MoCA3D ranks second overall, achieving the best result on ARKitScenes (8.78%) and second-best on KITTI (5.04%), nuScenes (7.17%), SUN RGB-D (10.08%), and Hypersim (19.57%). On 3D-space metrics, MoCA3D attains the best NHD on KITTI (0.1902) and ARKitScenes (0.2529), and the second-best IoU_3D on KITTI (0.4735), while using only 19.0M trainable parameters (57× fewer than DetAny3D) and running at 0.14 s/example.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoCA3D decisively outperforms all three baselines on PAG_uv (image-plane corner localization) across every dataset: it reduces KITTI corner error from 4.90 px (DetAny3D) to 3.12 px, and Objectron from 23.55 px (OVMono3D-Lift*) to 18.60 px. Against DetAny3D, the closest competitor, MoCA3D wins on PAG_uv by 1.78 px on KITTI, 1.12 px on nuScenes, 8.81 px on Objectron, 2.25 px on ARKitScenes, 4.07 px on SUN RGB-D, and 1.15 px on Hypersim. However, DetAny3D retains an edge on PAG_d (depth accuracy) for most datasets (e.g., 4.82% vs. 5.04% on KITTI, 6.07% vs. 7.17% on nuScenes) and on 3D-space metrics (NHD and IoU_3D), where it achieves the best NHD on nuScenes (0.2428) and IoU_3D on five of six datasets. Cube R-CNN and its class-agnostic variant (Cube R-CNN*) are consistently the weakest baselines, with PAG_uv values 3–10× higher than MoCA3D, confirming that dense pixel-aligned prediction substantially outperforms parametric 3D box regression for image-plane geometry. MoCA3D-Cube, the lightweight 3D box adapter, narrows the IoU_3D gap to DetAny3D (e.g., 0.4549 vs. 0.4834 on KITTI) while retaining MoCA3D's parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoCA3D departs from the dominant RoI-to-3D-box-lifting paradigm (Cube R-CNN/Omni3D, DetAny3D, OVMono3D) by predicting image-plane geometry—projected corner locations and per-corner depths—directly, rather than regressing 3D box parameters and projecting them back. It inherits the dense heatmap formulation and adaptive Gaussian target design from Yu et al. [52] (BoxDreamer) and the virtual-depth and 3D box parameterization conventions from Cube R-CNN [5], but restructures the pipeline around a box-conditioned transformer with a frozen DINOv3 backbone and a reversed cross-attention direction (image tokens as queries attending to box embeddings). The paper also introduces Pixel-Aligned Geometry (PAG) as a complementary evaluation suite, filling a gap left by 3D-space metrics (NHD, IoU₃D) that obscure reprojection errors relevant to image-plane downstream tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: diffusion-based editing, controllable generation, and AR pipelines consume projected 3D corners and depth ordering, which standard 3D detectors cannot produce without known intrinsics—yet intrinsics are often unavailable or inconsistent in the wild. MoCA3D demonstrates that a 19M-parameter model can outperform 57×-larger detection-based lifting methods on image-plane geometry, making annotation-free geometric control feasible from raw images. Key limitations remain: the model assumes all eight corners are visible (truncated objects degrade performance), symmetric objects retain orientation ambiguity, and the oracle-2D-box protocol means a fully end-to-end detection pipeline is still open. The PAG metric itself is a useful addition that the community may adopt for evaluating any method whose outputs are consumed in pixel space.
