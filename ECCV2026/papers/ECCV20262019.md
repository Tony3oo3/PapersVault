## Problem

Subject-driven image generation suffers from an 'Identity-Diversity Paradox': models that strongly preserve subject identity collapse to near-duplicate outputs with similar poses and viewpoints (structural mimicry), while methods that encourage diversity drift away from the subject's identity. This tension arises because identity-defining attributes are entangled with spatial structure in the reference image, so preserving one inadvertently constrains the other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262019/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (1024×1024) and a text prompt describing the desired context, pose, or style, and produces generated images at 1024×1024 that preserve the subject's visual identity while varying in structural configuration. Training uses a 10k subset of SynCD where each identity is paired with 10 text prompts; evaluation is conducted on DreamBench++ (150 identities × 9 prompts) spanning animals, humans, objects, and style transfer. The backbone is Flux-Kontext, a flow-matching diffusion transformer that natively accepts in-context image conditioning.

## Method

DivRL introduces two reward signals computed on MTG's disentangled visual features (48×48 spatial grids): a negative Self-Similarity Measure (nSSM) that quantifies structural diversity via the negative Pearson correlation between self-similarity matrices of reference and generated features, and a Visual Semantic Matching (VSM) score that measures identity consistency over semantically corresponding regions. These are combined through a two-stage 'Explore-and-Suppress' strategy on Flow-GRPO: the first stage (3200 steps) maximizes nSSM alone to encourage free structural exploration, while the second stage (3200 steps) applies VSM as a gated constraint, penalizing only samples that fall below an identity threshold via a quadratic hinge loss. This converts identity preservation from a competing objective into a feasibility constraint, allowing both metrics to improve jointly. A 2×2 average pooling on the feature grids (reducing resolution to 24×24) suppresses high-frequency textural artifacts that would otherwise be exploited as reward hacking.

## Results

On DreamBench++ (150 identities × 9 prompts), DivRL (VSM + nSSM) achieves a VSM-0.7 identity consistency score of 0.614 and an MTG-nSSM structural diversity score of 0.689, improving diversity over the Flux-Kontext baseline (0.659) while maintaining comparable consistency (0.605). The out-of-domain DINO-nSSM also improves from 0.433 (Flux-Kontext) to 0.453 (DivRL), and DINO identity similarity rises from 0.594 to 0.600. The nSSM-only ablation reaches the highest diversity (MTG-nSSM 0.704, DINO-nSSM 0.489) but drops VSM to 0.562, while the VSM-only ablation achieves the highest consistency (VSM 0.688, DINO 0.670) at the cost of the lowest diversity (MTG-nSSM 0.608).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Flux-Kontext base model, DivRL improves MTG-nSSM from 0.659 to 0.689 (+4.6%) and DINO-nSSM from 0.433 to 0.453 (+4.6%) while slightly increasing VSM from 0.605 to 0.614 and DINO from 0.594 to 0.600, confirming the paper's claim of gaining diversity without sacrificing identity. Against PaCo-RL (the closest RL-based competitor), DivRL wins on both identity (VSM 0.614 vs 0.576, DINO 0.600 vs 0.570) and diversity (MTG-nSSM 0.689 vs 0.668, DINO-nSSM 0.453 vs 0.445). Against non-Flux-Kontext models (OmniGen, OmniGen2, UNO, Flux-IP-Adapter), DivRL achieves substantially higher identity consistency (VSM 0.614 vs 0.454–0.481; DINO 0.600 vs 0.387–0.568) but lower raw diversity scores (MTG-nSSM 0.689 vs 0.727–0.816), reflecting the trade-off that these models sacrifice identity for structural variation. The paper notes that even without explicit text-alignment optimization, the nSSM-only variant achieves the highest CLIP-T score (0.287, tied with PaCo-RL), suggesting that relaxing structural rigidity indirectly improves prompt following. Aesthetic quality (HPS) is comparable across all methods (0.287–0.311), with OmniGen2 scoring highest at 0.311.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MTG (Mind-the-Glitch) for disentangled visual features and the VSM identity metric, and on Flow-GRPO for the RL optimization backbone. It departs from prior RL-based subject-driven methods (PaCo-RL, Identity-GRPO) that use pairwise consistency rewards by introducing a novel structural diversity signal (nSSM) computed from self-similarity correlations of disentangled features. The key conceptual departure is the 'Explore-and-Suppress' two-stage gated formulation, which converts identity preservation from a competing objective into a feasibility constraint, avoiding the gradient competition that plagues linear reward combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for balancing identity and diversity in subject-driven generation, and the gated reward formulation is a transferable idea for any multi-objective RL setting where one objective should act as a constraint rather than a competing target. An interesting side finding is that relaxing structural rigidity through nSSM inadvertently improves prompt following, suggesting that identity-preservation pretraining induces a geometric rigidity that hinders contextual adaptation. Open questions include whether the nSSM metric captures higher-level semantic or compositional diversity beyond pose/viewpoint changes, whether the approach generalizes beyond single-reference conditioning, and whether the RL overhead can be reduced for practical deployment.
