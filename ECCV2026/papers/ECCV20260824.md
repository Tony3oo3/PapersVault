## Problem

Existing acoustic-based human pose estimation methods are limited to single-person settings, where signal variations are uniquely attributable to one individual's motion. In multi-person scenarios, the observed acoustic signal is a superposition of concurrent motion features further complicated by inter-person reflections, making it fundamentally difficult to disentangle which signal changes correspond to which person's pose. This paper presents the first approach to estimating 3D poses of multiple individuals simultaneously from acoustic signals alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260824/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 4-channel ambisonics microphone recording (W, X, Y, Z) of a time-stretched pulse (TSP) signal emitted by a pair of speakers, segmented into frames of length L. It takes N_in = 24 consecutive acoustic frames (N_out + N_prev = 8 + 16) to predict N_out = 8 consecutive 3D pose frames for up to M = 15 people, each represented by 21 joint positions, along with a per-person confidence score. In the cross-modal experiment, the same decoder architecture operates on WiFi CSI features instead of acoustic spectrograms.

## Method

SoundMHPE is a DETR-inspired encoder-decoder with two key innovations. The Acoustic Multi-scale Encoder applies STFT at three window sizes (L, 2L, 4L) to produce complementary high-temporal and high-frequency spectrograms, then processes them through stacked blocks that decouple attention into Temporal Self-Attention (within each resolution) and Frequency Self-Attention (across resolutions for the same acoustic segment), preventing the model from conflating time and frequency dependencies. The Temporal Pose Decoder assigns N_out + 1 learnable queries per person (one per predicted frame plus one instance query for confidence), and splits self-attention into Motion Self-Attention (within a person's queries to capture intra-person temporal dynamics) and Interaction Self-Attention (across different persons' queries to model inter-person acoustic coupling). Training uses Hungarian matching to assign predicted poses to ground-truth persons, with MSE loss on matched joints and binary cross-entropy on confidence scores.

## Results

On the AMP dataset (cross-subject, 3-fold group evaluation), SoundMHPE achieves 106.5 mm MPJPE, 65.0 mm PA-MPJPE, and 0.43 PCKh@0.5, outperforming both baselines on all three metrics. In the single-person setting, the method reaches 95.0 mm MPJPE and 0.47 PCKh@0.5, while in the triple-person setting it maintains 111.2 mm MPJPE and 0.44 PCKh@0.5. On the cross-modal Person-in-WiFi 3D (PiW) benchmark, SoundMHPE achieves 122.6 mm MPJPE and 0.31 PCKh@0.5, surpassing the original single-frame PiW model (127.4 mm, 0.13) and a naively extended multi-frame variant (161.0 mm, 0.06).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoundMHPE outperforms both baselines across all metrics on the AMP dataset. Against the adapted single-person acoustic method of Shibata et al. [24], it reduces MPJPE by 15.2 mm (121.7 → 106.5) and PCKh@0.5 by 7 percentage points (0.36 → 0.43). Against the adapted multi-person WiFi method of Yan et al. [30], the improvement is 13.4 mm in MPJPE (119.9 → 106.5) and the same 7-point PCKh gain. The gap is most pronounced in the triple-person setting, where SoundMHPE achieves 111.2 mm MPJPE versus 124.5 mm (Shibata) and 122.4 mm (Yan), indicating that the multi-scale encoder and temporal decoder are particularly effective when acoustic signals from multiple subjects overlap. On the cross-modal PiW benchmark, SoundMHPE also beats both the original single-frame PiW model and a naively extended multi-frame variant, confirming that the temporal query design generalizes beyond the acoustic modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoundMHPE extends the DETR-style query-based decoder (Carion et al., ECCV 2020) from object detection to multi-person pose estimation, but departs from the standard one-query-per-instance design by assigning N_out temporal queries per person to capture frame-specific acoustic-to-pose correspondences. The encoder draws on the multi-scale STFT idea from prior signal-processing work and the temporal windowing strategy (N_out + N_prev frames) from the authors' own single-person acoustic pose estimator (Oumi et al., BMVC 2024). The instance-query mechanism for per-person confidence scores follows Liu et al. (CVPR 2023). Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously separate lines: single-person active acoustic sensing (chirp/TSP signals, inaudible tones) and multi-person DETR-based pose estimation from RGB or WiFi, unifying them under a single encoder-decoder with decomposed self-attention (temporal/frequency in the encoder, motion/interaction in the decoder).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that multi-person 3D poses can be recovered from acoustic signals alone, opening a modality that is immune to lighting, occlusion by metal, and privacy concerns inherent to RGB. The 6-hour AMP dataset (432K synchronized frames, 1–3 people) is a necessary foundation that did not previously exist and will likely anchor future work in this space. However, practical deployment remains constrained: the method requires a soundproof room, handles at most three subjects, and generalization to unseen reflective environments yields only coarse poses. The cross-modal transfer to WiFi (PiW) is encouraging but the PCKh@0.5 of 0.31 is still far below what dedicated WiFi methods achieve, suggesting the acoustic-specific inductive biases do not fully transfer. Open questions include scaling beyond three people, reducing the soundproof-room requirement, and whether the multi-scale STFT design generalizes to other time-frequency representations.
