## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) produce confident but fabricated answers when confronted with long-tail or specialized-domain entities beyond their parametric knowledge, rather than acknowledging their ignorance. While 'I don't know' alignment has been explored for text-only LLMs, the multimodal setting—where refusal decisions depend on the interplay between visual perception and internal knowledge mastery—remains largely unaddressed. This paper targets the more insidious case of epistemic uncertainty, where the visual input is perfectly clear but the model simply lacks the required world knowledge.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a concise text question (~10 words on average) and produces either a short factual answer (3–4 words) or a standardized refusal phrase. Evaluation is conducted under two protocols: zero-shot (no abstention instruction, testing internalized self-awareness) and few-shot (explicit refusal instruction plus two in-context demonstrations). The test set is deliberately balanced at 60% known / 40% unknown samples to measure both knowledge retention and honest abstention simultaneously.

## Method

The framework constructs a model-specific Visual-Idk dataset through a three-stage pipeline: (1) a high-capability VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) filters InfoSeek samples for visual clarity and knowledge intensity; (2) multi-sample consistency probing (N=10 high-temperature samples, mastery threshold τ=0.7) categorizes each image-question pair as Known or Unknown for the target model; (3) preference pairs are generated by contrasting correct answers against refusals for Known facts and refusals against the model's own hallucinated responses for Unknown facts. Training proceeds in two stages—SFT to instill the refusal format, followed by preference-aware optimization (DPO, CPO, or ORPO) to calibrate the answer-refusal boundary without the over-conservatism of SFT alone. All training uses LoRA (r=64, α=128) on LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B. Internal logprob and perplexity probing confirms the model genuinely recalibrates its epistemic confidence rather than memorizing a refusal template.

## Results

On the Visual-Idk test set (60% Known / 40% Unknown), ORPO-aligned LLaVA-1.5-13B achieves the highest TRUTHFUL rate of 76.6% in the few-shot setting (vs. 69.4% base), while CPO-aligned LLaVA-1.5-7B reaches 67.3% (vs. 57.9% base). In the zero-shot setting, ORPO on 7B attains 60.0% TRUTHFUL, surpassing the base model's few-shot score of 57.9%, indicating internalized knowledge-boundary awareness. Cross-dataset evaluation on ScienceQA confirms generalization, with ORPO improving TRUTHFUL from 56.1% to 63.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five strategies: the unaligned base model, Idk-Prompting (zero-shot instruction), SFT, DPO, CPO, and ORPO. SFT dramatically increases refusal (IK-IDK) but incurs a severe alignment tax, dropping IK-IK by 11.4–22.1% and even reducing overall TRUTHFUL below base on 13B few-shot (67.3 vs. 69.4). DPO preserves knowledge accuracy well (IK-IK stays near base) but provides only modest refusal gains (TRUTHFUL +1.7 to +5.2). CPO and ORPO strike the best balance: CPO achieves the top 7B few-shot TRUTHFUL (67.3, +9.4 over base) while ORPO leads on 13B few-shot (76.6, +7.2) and 7B zero-shot (60.0, +12.4). On ScienceQA, ORPO again leads (63.8 vs. 56.1 base), and on OOD medical VQA (PMC-VQA), CPO achieves the highest TRUTHFUL at 80.0%, confirming that preference-aware methods generalize better than SFT's blanket refusal prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'I don't know' (Idk) alignment framework of Cheng et al. (ICML 2024) from text-only LLMs to the multimodal VLM setting, where refusal decisions must account for the interplay between visual perception and parametric knowledge mastery. It departs from prior VLM hallucination work that targets object-level misidentifications or perceptual uncertainty (e.g., blurred images), instead focusing on epistemic uncertainty where the visual input is clear but the model lacks internal world knowledge. The preference-optimization toolkit (DPO, ORPO, CPO) is well-established in both NLP and multimodal alignment, but here it is repurposed to calibrate the answer-refusal boundary rather than answer quality, with a novel multi-sample consistency probing pipeline for constructing model-specific knowledge-boundary datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in deploying VLMs in high-stakes domains (medical, legal) where confident hallucinations are costly, and the domain-agnostic generalization to OOD scenarios (blurred VizWiz, medical PMC-VQA) suggests the learned honesty is not overfitted to specific entities. However, the training set is modest (20K samples), experiments are limited to LLaVA-1.5 at two scales, and the absolute improvement (~10 points in TRUTHFUL rate) leaves substantial room for further gains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger or more architecturally diverse VLMs, how it interacts with multi-step reasoning or compositional queries, and whether the 60/40 known-unknown ratio in the training data is optimal across domains.
