## Problem

Text-to-video generation models achieve high visual fidelity but frequently produce physically implausible results (e.g., distorted bodies, incorrect collisions, unrealistic fluid dynamics). Existing remedies—graphics-based simulation and LLM prompt extension—either cannot generalize beyond simple rigid-body scenarios or outsource physics reasoning to LLMs that are themselves unreliable, leaving the T2V model without an internal physics prior. The paper tackles this by formulating a principled preference-optimization framework that teaches the T2V model implicit physics reasoning directly, without any LLM guidance at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short text prompt (480×832 spatial resolution) and produces a generated video via the Wan2.1-T2V-14B flow-matching transformer. During training, each optimization step consumes a group of m+1 videos: one real-world video (winning case, guaranteeing correct physics) and m model-generated videos (losing cases) conditioned on the same prompt. The training dataset PhyVidGen-135K provides 17K such text-video pairs after physics-aware filtering, action clustering, and difficulty-weighted resampling from an initial pool of one million pairs.

## Method

PhyGDPO replaces the pairwise Bradley-Terry model of vanilla DPO with a groupwise Plackett-Luce probabilistic model that captures holistic preference over a set of candidate videos, using a real-world video as the guaranteed-correct winning case. The loss is derived under rectified flow matching and reduced to a single-timestep objective via Jensen's inequality plus an additional product inequality that decomposes the groupwise term into per-sample logistic losses. A Physics-Guided Rewarding (PGR) scheme parameterizes the per-sample weights γ_j and α_j from VideoCon-Physics VLM scores, amplifying the gradient contribution of physics-violating losing samples. A LoRA-Switch Reference (LoRA-SR) scheme freezes the base transformer as the reference model and attaches LoRA adapters with a binary environment manager to toggle between reference and action modes, eliminating the need to store a full duplicate model and reducing GPU memory by 44%.

## Results

On VideoPhy2, PhyGDPO achieves an overall score of 0.1627, surpassing the strongest closed-source models Sora2 (0.1508) and Veo3.1 (0.1525), with particularly large gains on the Hard action track (0.0500 vs. 0.0389 for Sora2 and 0.0444 for Veo3.1). On PhyGenBench, PhyGDPO reaches an average score of 0.55, outperforming VideoDPO (0.54) and PhyT2V (0.50), with the largest margin on the Thermal track (0.58 vs. 0.47 and 0.43). In the user study with 104 participants, PhyGDPO is preferred over Sora2 (67.3%), Veo3.1 (64.4%), and VideoDPO (89.4%) in pairwise physical-realism comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyGDPO outperforms all 12 baselines on the VideoPhy2 Overall score. Against the two strongest closed-source models, it beats Sora2 by 8.6% on Overall (0.1627 vs. 0.1508) and by 28.5% on the Hard track (0.0500 vs. 0.0389), and beats Veo3.1 by 6.8% on Overall (0.1627 vs. 0.1525) and by 12.6% on Hard (0.0500 vs. 0.0444), though Veo3.1 retains a lead on the Interaction track (0.1887 vs. 0.1761). Against the SOTA DPO method VideoDPO, PhyGDPO improves the Hard score by 200% (0.0500 vs. 0.0167) and Overall by 18.2% (0.1627 vs. 0.1373). Against the SOTA physics-consistent generation method PhyT2V, PhyGDPO gains 29% on Hard (0.0500 vs. 0.0389) and 9.0% on Overall (0.1627 vs. 0.1492). On PhyGenBench, PhyGDPO leads on Mechanics (0.55 vs. 0.48 for VideoDPO, 0.45 for PhyT2V) and Thermal (0.58 vs. 0.47, 0.43) but trails VideoDPO on Material (0.47 vs. 0.58) and ties on Optics (0.60). In the user study, PhyGDPO is preferred over all baselines, with the largest margins over open-source methods (89.4% over VideoDPO, 88.5% over PhyT2V) and a more modest but still significant advantage over closed-source models (67.3% over Sora2, 64.4% over Veo3.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) from pairwise Bradley-Terry comparisons to a groupwise Plackett-Luce model, and departs from prior physics-aware T2V approaches that either rely on simulation engines or outsource physics reasoning to an LLM via prompt extension (PhyT2V, DiffPhy). The key conceptual departure is using real-world video as the winning case in preference optimization—guaranteeing physically correct supervision—rather than generated video, and introducing a VLM-guided data pipeline (PhyAugPipe) to curate physics-rich training pairs. The LoRA-Switch Reference scheme is a practical engineering contribution that avoids the full-model duplication required by standard DPO, building on the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Physically consistent T2V generation is a prerequisite for applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and interactive media, yet even frontier closed-source models (Sora2, Veo3.1) still fail on complex human motions and multi-object interactions. The groupwise PL formulation and physics-guided rewarding offer a principled path toward learning implicit physics without LLM scaffolding at inference time, and the LoRA-SR scheme makes DPO training substantially more memory-efficient (44% reduction) while improving stability. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in complex momentum modeling (e.g., multi-ball collisions), and absolute scores on VideoPhy2 remain low (~0.16 overall), suggesting substantial headroom. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos, more diverse physics domains, and whether the VLM-based reward signals can be replaced by learned differentiable physics verifiers.
