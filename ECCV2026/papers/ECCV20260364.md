## Problem

Large-scale video diffusion models produce visually impressive but geometrically inconsistent videos, exhibiting camera jitter, 3D drift, and cross-view structural artifacts. Existing geometry-aware alignment methods either modify the generator architecture (compromising pretrained generalization) or compute rewards in RGB space via repeated VAE decoding, which is computationally expensive and limited to static scenes. No prior approach efficiently performs geometry-guided post-training in latent space while supporting dynamic 4D scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260364/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a temporally coherent video as output, with the key distinction that all geometry-aware reward signals are computed directly from VAE latents rather than decoded RGB frames. During training, the latent geometry model predicts per-frame camera poses, depth maps, 3D point maps, and (for dynamic scenes) scene flow from the denoised latent sequence, enabling reward evaluation without any pixel-space decoding. The framework is applied as a post-training stage on pretrained rectified-flow video diffusion backbones (Wan2.1-1B, Wan2.2-5B) via LoRA fine-tuning.

## Method

VGGRPO introduces a Latent Geometry Model (LGM) that stitches video diffusion VAE latents to a pretrained geometry foundation model (Any4D or VGGT) by replacing the model's first ℓ transformer layers with a lightweight 3D convolutional connector trained to match intermediate features, enabling direct geometry prediction from latents. Building on this, VGGRPO performs latent-space Group Relative Policy Optimization with two complementary rewards: a camera motion smoothness reward that penalizes scale-normalized translational and rotational accelerations, and a geometry reprojection consistency reward that reprojects the predicted 3D point cloud into each view and measures depth discrepancy against predicted depth maps (using scene flow to isolate static geometry in dynamic scenes). The group-relative advantage is the average of the two separately normalized rewards, and the policy is updated via the standard clipped GRPO surrogate with KL regularization toward a reference policy. This design eliminates the VAE decoding bottleneck of prior RGB-based rewards and naturally extends to dynamic 4D scenes through the 4D-capable geometry backbone.

## Results

On the Wan2.2-5B backbone, VGGRPO achieves 62.63% Visual Quality and 68.42% Motion Quality win rates on static scenes, and 56.50% VQ / 66.00% MQ on dynamic scenes, with a Sampson epipolar error of 0.093. On Wan2.1-1B, the corresponding numbers are 59.47% VQ / 66.84% MQ (static) and 57.00% VQ / 63.00% MQ (dynamic), with epipolar error 0.102. VGGRPO also improves VBench metrics, reaching 0.9753 motion smoothness and 0.8255 subject consistency on Wan2.1-1B, and 0.9619 motion smoothness and 0.8672 subject consistency on Wan2.2-5B, while reducing peak GPU memory for reward computation by 24.5% compared to RGB-based rewarding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGGRPO consistently outperforms both DPO-based baselines (Epipolar-DPO and VideoGPA) on Visual and Motion Quality win rates across all four scene/model combinations. The gap is most pronounced on dynamic scenes: on Wan2.1-1B, VGGRPO reaches 57.00% VQ versus 45.50% (Epipolar-DPO) and 42.50% (VideoGPA), and on Wan2.2-5B it reaches 56.50% versus 38.00% and 40.00%, reflecting the 4D-aware LGM's advantage under non-rigid motion. On static-scene epipolar error, Epipolar-DPO achieves the lowest value on Wan2.1-1B (0.098 vs. VGGRPO's 0.102), while VGGRPO achieves the lowest on Wan2.2-5B (0.093 vs. 0.101 and 0.098). Against the unaligned base model, VGGRPO improves epipolar error by roughly 23–35% (e.g., 0.142→0.093 on Wan2.2-5B) and lifts motion quality win rates from 0% to 66–68%. On VBench general-quality metrics, VGGRPO leads on subject consistency, background consistency, aesthetic quality, imaging quality, and motion smoothness for both backbones, with a slight trade-off on Dynamic Degree attributed to the motion-smoothness reward reducing optical-flow magnitude.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGGRPO sits at the intersection of two lines of work: geometry-aware video alignment (Epipolar-DPO, VideoGPA) and flow-based GRPO training (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO). It departs from prior alignment methods in two key ways: (1) it moves reward computation from RGB pixel space into the VAE latent space via a lightweight 'stitching' connector that maps latents into a pretrained geometry foundation model's intermediate feature space, eliminating repeated VAE decoding; and (2) by instantiating the latent geometry model with a 4D-capable backbone (Any4D), it extends geometry-consistency rewards from the static-scene assumption of prior work to dynamic videos with non-rigid motion. The GRPO training loop itself follows the on-policy group-relative formulation from DeepSeekMath and its flow-matching adaptations, but the novelty lies in what is being optimized (latent geometry) rather than the optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The latent geometry model is a practical and model-agnostic building block: any future geometry foundation model with 4D capability can be stitched in without redesigning the reward pipeline, and the differentiable LGM enables training-free test-time guidance as a bonus. The 24.5% reward-computation speedup and ~10 GB memory reduction are meaningful for group-based RL at scale, where K=64 samples per prompt make repeated VAE decoding a real bottleneck. Open questions include whether the stitching approach degrades as the gap between VAE latent structure and geometry-model input features widens (e.g., for higher-resolution or longer videos), whether the two-reward formulation generalizes to other consistency objectives (e.g., physical plausibility), and whether the modest Dynamic Degree drop on VBench signals a fundamental tension between camera smoothness and perceived motion richness that will need explicit resolution.
