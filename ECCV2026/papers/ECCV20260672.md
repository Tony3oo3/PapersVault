## Problem

Personalized face restoration methods that fine-tune diffusion models per identity suffer from two practical limitations: they require expensive full-model optimization with large regularization datasets (inflating training and storage costs), and their performance degrades sharply when reference images are low-quality. This paper tackles both issues by asking whether a lightweight, regularization-free personalization mechanism can remain robust when only a subset of references are high-quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260672/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded low-quality face image (affected by blur, noise, compression, or low resolution) and a small set of reference portraits of the same identity that may be mixed quality (e.g., 1 HQ + 4 LQ out of 5). Personalization is a one-time per-identity procedure that produces a compact LoRA adapter (~13 MB in FP16); at inference, any number of LQ inputs are restored by loading the frozen SDXL backbone with the cached adapter, optionally applying ID-preserving sampling on the last few denoising steps. The output is a pixel-aligned high-quality face image preserving the subject's identity.

## Method

ID-PreFeR personalizes a frozen SDXL backbone by fine-tuning only rank-16 LoRA weights on the decoder cross-attention layers (6.41 M parameters, ~0.25% of the backbone), eliminating the need for external regularization images via a latent prior loss that penalizes deviation from the frozen backbone's noise prediction under a class prompt lacking the identity token. To handle mixed-quality references, the method introduces two learnable quality tokens (initialized from 'sharp'/'blurry' embeddings) assigned by an MLLM-based classifier, which absorb per-reference degradation residuals while the identity token captures shared identity cues; this is combined with Min-SNR-γ weighting to suppress overfitting to high-frequency artifacts at high-SNR timesteps. At inference, an ID-preserving sampling step performs one gradient-ascent iteration on the cosine similarity between the VAE-decoded intermediate prediction and a frozen ArcFace embedding of the closest reference, applied only to the last few denoising steps to correct residual identity drift without altering the base restoration model.

## Results

On CelebRef with mixed LQ&HQ references, ID-PreFeR achieves the best LPIPS (0.242), MANIQA (0.649), and CLIPIQA (0.701), along with a highly competitive ID score of 0.767 (second only to InstantRestore's 0.769, which requires HQ-only references). It is the only method ranking first or second on every reported metric simultaneously. On the real-world mobile dataset, ID-PreFeR attains the best LIQE (4.8927), MANIQA (0.6528), and VLM-as-a-judge identity score (8.92), while maintaining low identity variance (std 0.0192) across diverse in-the-wild captures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against reference-free methods (GFPGAN, CodeFormer, DiffBIR, AuthFace), ID-PreFeR dominates on perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.242 vs. 0.294–0.401) and identity (ID 0.767 vs. 0.562–0.649), confirming that reference-guided personalization is essential for identity preservation under strong degradation. Among personalized baselines using mixed LQ&HQ references (FaceMe, Gen2Res), ID-PreFeR substantially outperforms on MANIQA (0.649 vs. 0.504/0.331), CLIPIQA (0.701 vs. 0.646/0.491), and ID (0.767 vs. 0.704/0.446), though FaceMe achieves a marginally higher PSNR (25.82 vs. 25.51). The closest competitor is InstantRestore (ID 0.769), which edges out ID-PreFeR by 0.002 on identity but requires HQ-only references and trails significantly on MANIQA (0.488 vs. 0.649) and CLIPIQA (0.561 vs. 0.701). RestorerID leads on CLIPIQA (0.717) but sacrifices identity (ID 0.522) and structural fidelity (SSIM 0.636), while ReF-LDM achieves competitive LPIPS (0.244) yet falls short on MANIQA (0.562) and ID (0.695). Critically, ID-PreFeR is the only method that uses mixed-quality references while remaining first or second on every metric, whereas all baselines that match or exceed it on a single axis (e.g., InstantRestore on ID, RestorerID on CLIPIQA) rely exclusively on HQ references or collapse on other dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ID-PreFeR sits at the intersection of personalized text-to-image diffusion (DreamBooth-style fine-tuning on subject references) and blind face restoration via ControlNet-conditioned latent diffusion. It departs from DreamBooth by replacing expensive regularization image sets with a latent prior loss that anchors the personalized LoRA injector to the frozen SDXL backbone's noise predictions, and from prior personalized face restoration methods (DMDNet, FaceMe, Gen2Res, ReF-LDM) by explicitly handling mixed-quality references through learnable quality tokens and Min-SNR-γ weighting rather than assuming all references are high-quality. The ID-preserving sampling step, which performs a single gradient ascent on ArcFace cosine similarity during late denoising, is a lightweight inference-time correction not seen in prior personalized restoration pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mixed-quality reference setting is the realistic scenario for consumer face restoration (e.g., a phone user's photo album contains a mix of sharp and blurry portraits), and demonstrating near-constant identity fidelity as HQ references drop from 5 to 1 addresses a gap that prior methods leave open. The practical efficiency (6.41 M trainable parameters ≈ 0.25% of SDXL, ~4.2 min per-identity training, ~13 MB storage) makes per-user personalization feasible on mobile hardware, though the ID-preserving sampling step (29 s, 34.8 GB VRAM) remains a barrier to on-device deployment. Open questions include how the method handles accessory/makeup variation across references (acknowledged limitation), whether the VLM-as-a-judge evaluation protocol generalizes, and whether the single-step identity correction can be distilled into a cheaper surrogate for real-time use.
