## Problem

Aerial-Ground Re-Identification (AG-ReID) is hindered by the viewpoint-domain gap, where drastic elevation changes occlude or distort discriminative features. Existing methods require paired cross-view annotations or target-domain data, making them infeasible for cold-start deployments like search-and-rescue where only a single viewpoint (e.g., ground-level) is available. This paper formalizes the Single-View AG-ReID (SV AG-ReID) setting and addresses the challenge of generalizing from one real viewpoint to an unseen one without any paired cross-view supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image (resized to 256×128) from one viewpoint domain, segments the foreground with YOLOv11, and lifts it into a textured 3D mesh via Hunyuan3D. Novel views are rendered by perturbing the calibrated camera pose within constrained elevation (±30°) and azimuth (±30°) ranges, then composited onto inpainted real backgrounds and harmonized with StyleID style transfer. At inference, the ViT backbone produces an identity feature embedding used for query-to-gallery retrieval ranking via mAP and Rank-1 metrics.

## Method

3D-LENS combines geometrically-consistent novel view synthesis with a robust representation learning scheme. For synthesis, a single image is lifted into a textured mesh by Hunyuan3D, the virtual camera pose is calibrated by maximizing IoU between the rendered silhouette and the segmentation mask, and novel views are rendered by controlled elevation/azimuth perturbations—ensuring all views share the same geometry and texture, unlike 2D generative models that hallucinate independently per view. For training, a curriculum scheduler progressively introduces harder elevation shifts (rejection probability decays linearly over 20 warmup epochs), a balanced sampling strategy enforces a fixed 1:1 real-to-synthetic ratio per mini-batch to prevent synthetic overfitting, and a domain classification loss with a dedicated domain token in the ViT backbone encourages the model to separate domain-specific rendering artifacts from identity features. The full objective combines cross-entropy, triplet, and domain losses, and the approach is class-agnostic, requiring no category-specific 3D templates.

## Results

On AG-ReID, 3D-LENS achieves 49.8% mAP (G→A, aerial training) and 55.1% mAP (A→G, ground training), outperforming the strongest prior method DCAC by +6.4 and +2.2 pp mAP respectively. On the more complex AG-ReID.v2 dataset, 3D-LENS reaches 37.8% mAP (C→A, aerial training), surpassing the best baseline PASS by +21.2 pp mAP. On the MOO cattle dataset, 3D-LENS achieves 51.4% mAP (T→S, top training), exceeding RotTrans by +10.0 pp mAP, demonstrating cross-class generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-LENS outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every cross-view scenario on all three datasets. The closest competitor is DCAC [16], a diffusion-based generative method, which 3D-LENS surpasses by +6.4 pp mAP on AG-ReID G→A (aerial training) and by a larger +14.3 pp on A→G, where DCAC's 2D generation struggles with geometric consistency. On AG-ReID.v2, large-scale pretraining models like PASS [52] and RotTrans [4] are the strongest baselines, yet 3D-LENS still leads by +21.2 pp mAP (C→A) and +15.7 pp (W→A) over PASS. On MOO, where rotation invariance is critical, RotTrans [4] is the best prior method at 41.4% mAP (T→S), but 3D-LENS achieves 51.4%, a +10.0 pp margin. The ViT [6] baseline is consistently outperformed by 14–23 pp mAP depending on the scenario, confirming that explicit 3D geometric consistency provides a substantial advantage over purely data-driven appearance learning in the single-view setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard AG-ReID paradigm, which assumes paired aerial-ground training data, by formalizing the Single-View AG-ReID (SV AG-ReID) setting where only one viewpoint is available during training. Methodologically, it moves beyond 2D generative view synthesis (diffusion models, GANs) that suffer from cross-view geometric inconsistencies, and beyond category-specific 3D templates (SMPL, CAD) that cannot generalize across object classes, by leveraging class-agnostic large-scale 3D reconstruction (Hunyuan3D) to produce a textured mesh from which all novel views are rendered consistently. The representation learning component builds on the BoT/ViT ReID baseline, augmented with a curriculum scheduler, balanced real-synthetic sampling, and a domain classification loss to mitigate synthetic-to-real bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SV AG-ReID formulation addresses a practical cold-start problem in search-and-rescue and wildlife monitoring where paired cross-view data is unavailable, making the contribution directly relevant to deployment scenarios. The large margins over 2D generative baselines (+14.3 pp mAP on AG-ReID, +21.2 pp on AG-ReID.v2) suggest that explicit 3D geometric consistency is a substantially more effective inductive bias than learned appearance mapping for extreme viewpoint shifts. However, the authors acknowledge that synthesized view quality is bounded by source image quality (low resolution, occlusion, multi-person overlap), and automated quality-control mechanisms for filtering or weighting synthetic views during training remain an open challenge that limits the approach's reliability at scale.
