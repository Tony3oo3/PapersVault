## Problem

Existing ViT feature codecs minimize feature-space MSE, implicitly assuming that preserving Euclidean similarity preserves downstream task performance. However, under fixed-group product quantization, task sensitivity is highly uneven across channel subspaces, so near-uniform per-group bit budgets waste capacity on insensitive directions while underrepresenting critical ones, causing rapid accuracy collapse at the low bitrates required for split inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262669/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec consumes an intermediate ViT feature tensor F ∈ R^{T×D} extracted at a chosen split layer (e.g., 257×1024 for ViT-L/14 classification, 2×1370×1024 for segmentation), where T is the token count and D the channel dimension. It produces a compact bitstream of grouped PQ indices entropy-coded with learned per-group categorical priors; the cloud side decodes these indices via table lookup, applies the inverse orthogonal transform, and feeds the reconstructed features into the frozen tail network and task heads. Bitrate is measured in bits per feature point (BPFP), and the operating range of interest is below 0.1 BPFP.

## Method

ORFC applies a learnable orthogonal transform R (parameterized via the Cayley transform of a skew-symmetric matrix to guarantee R^T R = I by construction) to rotate the feature representation before grouped product quantization, redistributing task-sensitive directions across the G fixed-size groups so that per-group task influence becomes more balanced. Each group is quantized with an independent codebook, and a learnable categorical prior per group provides both entropy coding and a differentiable rate estimate. Training uses an entropy-constrained RD objective L = R_bits + λ·L_ref, where L_ref is the squared deviation of the frozen tail's outputs between original and reconstructed features, replacing the conventional feature-space MSE. Gradients propagate through the discrete index selection via a straight-through estimator with temperature-annealed soft assignment, enabling joint end-to-end optimization of the rotation, codebooks, and entropy priors. The key insight is that this representation-level reparameterization aligns the implicit uniform per-group budgets with task importance (satisfying the equal-slope condition) without modifying codebook sizes or introducing heterogeneous rate allocation.

## Results

On DINOv2 ViT-L/14 classification at the 10th block, ORFC achieves 96.0% Acc@1 at 0.06 BPFP, while OPQ reaches only 34.2% and VTM 19.8% at 0.11 BPFP. For object detection on VitDet ViT-B/16 (3rd block), ORFC attains 91.6% mAP at 0.05 BPFP versus 49.5% (VTM) and 36.7% (OPQ) at comparable bitrates. In segmentation on DINOv2 ViT-G/14 (20th block), ORFC reaches 83.1% mIoU at ~0.06 BPFP (original: 83.3%), while VTM drops to 65.5% at 0.09 BPFP. BD-rate relative to VTM ranges from −77.5% to −88.6% on classification and −41.4% to −65.8% on segmentation across split points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORFC consistently outperforms all three baselines across every task, model, and split point evaluated, with the largest margins in the low-bitrate regime (<0.1 BPFP). Against VTM (VVC-based generic compression), ORFC achieves BD-rate reductions of 77–89% on classification and 41–66% on segmentation; VTM exhibits an abrupt accuracy collapse below a bitrate threshold that ORFC largely avoids. Against OPQ (MSE-driven orthogonal PQ), ORFC closes a substantial gap at low bitrate: on DINOv2 ViT-L/14 at the 10th block, ORFC reaches 96.0% Acc@1 at 0.06 BPFP while OPQ only reaches 34.2%, and on VitDet detection ORFC achieves 91.6% mAP versus OPQ's 36.7% at comparable rates. Against LaMoFC (learned entropy model with feature-space MSE distortion), ORFC dominates at most operating points, with LaMoFC remaining below both VTM and OPQ in several settings. The gains are most pronounced for dense prediction tasks (segmentation, depth) where structured spatial coherence matters, and diminish slightly at deeper split points where features are more semantically compressed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the product quantization lineage (Jegou et al. 2010; OPQ, Ge et al. 2013) and the split-inference feature coding literature (Matsubara et al. 2022; Gao et al. 2025), but departs from the standard practice of minimizing feature-space MSE as the distortion signal. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that, under ViT's global attention mixing, Euclidean reconstruction error is misaligned with downstream task degradation at low bitrate, and that fixed grouped PQ budgets interact poorly with anisotropic task sensitivity across channel subspaces. The orthogonal reparameterization via Cayley transform is a lightweight representation-side fix that repurposes the OPQ rotation idea not for MSE minimization but for balancing per-group task influence, coupled with a frozen-tail output-deviation distortion inspired by rate-distortion theory for coding for machines (Harell et al. 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As ViT-based foundation models are increasingly deployed in edge-cloud split settings, efficient intermediate feature transmission becomes a practical bottleneck, and this paper provides a principled, low-overhead solution that substantially delays accuracy collapse below 0.1 BPFP. The diagnostic finding that MSE-driven coding wastes bits on task-insensitive directions while underrepresenting critical ones is actionable for any grouped-quantization pipeline. Open questions include whether the learned rotation generalizes across split points without retraining, how the approach interacts with adaptive or learned split-point selection, and whether similar sensitivity-balancing transforms help for non-ViT backbones (e.g., CNNs with local receptive fields where the global-mixing argument is weaker).
