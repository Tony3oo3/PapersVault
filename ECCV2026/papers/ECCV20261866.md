## Problem

Videos contain very low task-relevant information density: most consecutive frames are nearly identical and large portions of each frame are uninformative background. Existing training-free methods address temporal frame selection or spatial token compression separately, and no prior work explicitly guides intra-frame spatial focusing by query relevance while simultaneously preserving global temporal structure. This leaves Video LLMs processing vast amounts of redundant visual input that dilutes meaningful signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261866/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (sampled at 1 FPS to form a frame pool of N frames) and a natural-language text query as input. It produces a compact, optimized visual context: a subset of k frames (from hierarchical temporal selection) plus k_add additional frames (from temporal reallocation), where each frame retains only the top-τ fraction of spatial patch tokens. The total token budget is fixed, so the output is a budget-constrained set of focused frame-token pairs ready for joint decoding with text tokens in the LLM.

## Method

STVFocus is a training-free, model-agnostic pipeline with three stages. First, Hierarchical Temporal Focus (HTF) performs coarse-to-fine frame selection inspired by Farthest Point Sampling: a coarse phase (β=1) selects diverse frames capturing global video structure, then a fine phase (β=0.5) adds query-relevant frames under a diversity regularizer, using CLIP cosine similarity as the relevance signal. Second, Query-informed Spatial Focus (QSF) fuses an activation-based attention map (sum of absolute patch-token features) with a gradient-based spatial saliency map (from CLIP, Gaussian-smoothed) via a weighted combination (γ=0.5), then retains only the top-τ spatial tokens per frame. Third, Temporal Reallocation reinvests the token capacity freed by spatial suppression into additional high-value frames drawn sequentially from the HTF-ordered list, enriching temporal coverage without exceeding the fixed budget. The entire framework requires no gradient updates to the base Video LLM and uses a single set of hyperparameters across all models and benchmarks.

## Results

STVFocus achieves consistent gains across three Video LLMs and three benchmarks. On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, it improves VideoMME from 58.5 to 61.1 (+2.6), LongVideoBench from 56.4 to 61.2 (+4.8), and MLVU from 63.2 to 68.7 (+5.5). On InternVL3-8B, it reaches 67.0 on VideoMME (+2.5), 61.5 on LVB (+3.0), and 74.8 on MLVU (+7.0). On VILA1.5-8B, it achieves 52.6 on VideoMME (+5.1), 52.5 on LVB (+5.4), and 50.9 on MLVU (+4.6). All improvements are training-free and use a single set of hyperparameters across all models and datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, STVFocus outperforms both training-free frame-selection methods: AKS (59.6) and BOLT (59.9) on VideoMME, with margins of +1.5 and +1.2 respectively, and also leads on LVB (61.2 vs. 59.3/59.6) and MLVU (68.7 vs. 66.8 for BOLT). On VILA1.5-8B, STVFocus surpasses the training-based Frame-Voyager by +2.1 on VideoMME (52.6 vs. 50.5) and the training-free Q-Frame by +1.9 on VideoMME (52.6 vs. 50.7) and +0.9 on LVB (52.5 vs. 51.6), though Q-Frame retains a lead on MLVU (54.4 vs. 50.9). On InternVL3-8B, STVFocus delivers the largest absolute gain on MLVU (+7.0, reaching 74.8) and consistent improvements on VideoMME (+2.5) and LVB (+3.0) over the unmodified baseline. Across all three base models, STVFocus is the only method that simultaneously improves all three benchmarks, and its gains are largest on longer/more complex video benchmarks (LVB, MLVU), consistent with the hierarchical coarse-to-fine design targeting global temporal structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

STVFocus extends the training-free temporal frame selection line (BOLT, AKS) by replacing their single-stage, query-biased selection with a hierarchical coarse-to-fine strategy inspired by Farthest Point Sampling, and by explicitly introducing a spatial focusing stage that prior temporal methods entirely ignore. It departs from token-compression approaches (which prioritize efficiency over semantic quality) and from Q-Frame (which adjusts frame resolution but remains at the frame level) by operating at the intra-frame token level with query-informed spatial saliency. The novel Temporal Reallocation mechanism—reinvesting spatially saved token budget into additional frames—has no direct precedent in prior video LLM context-adaptation work, which typically treats spatial and temporal budgets independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, plug-and-play way to improve Video LLM accuracy without any fine-tuning, validated across three architecturally different base models and three benchmarks, making it broadly applicable to practitioners who cannot retrain. The consistent outperformance of training-based methods (e.g., +2.1 over Frame-Voyager on VILA1.5) suggests that careful context curation can rival learned temporal adaptation. Open questions include whether CLIP-based saliency generalizes to domains with weak text-visual alignment (e.g., abstract or symbolic content), whether the fixed hyperparameters (α, β, γ, τ) transfer to substantially different video lengths or aspect ratios, and whether the approach extends to streaming or real-time settings where the full frame pool is unavailable.
