## Problem

Contrastive VLMs like CLIP and SigLIP exhibit a persistent modality gap between vision and language embeddings, which degrades performance under distribution shift. Existing unsupervised adaptation methods either fine-tune the backbone (eroding pretrained multimodal knowledge) or rely on iterative pseudo-labeling that accumulates confirmation bias. The paper asks whether VL synchronization can be improved in a fully label-free manner while keeping the original VLM completely frozen.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261975/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (any resolution compatible with the backbone) and a set of text templates (m templates per class, randomly sampled from a large pool). For each image, k augmented views are generated to form a training group; for each class, m template embeddings form the corresponding text group. At inference, only the encoder sub-networks of the two synchronizers project image and text embeddings into a shared aligned latent subspace, from which standard zero-shot classification, retrieval ranking, detection, or segmentation heads operate without any further modification to the frozen VLM.

## Method

SyncVL introduces two lightweight transformer-based encoder-decoder synchronizers—Vision-to-Text (V2T) and Text-to-Vision (T2V)—that progressively map each modality's embeddings toward the other's class centers without touching the frozen VLM backbone. Both are trained with a GRPO-inspired group-relative objective that combines a consistency reward (KL divergence between each group member's softmax distribution and the group mean) and an alignment reward (binary contrastive loss pulling toward the majority-vote pseudo-positive class center and away from the hard negative). The V2T synchronizer additionally serves as a teacher, providing a knowledge-distillation reward that aligns the T2V encoder's latent space with its own, enforcing bidirectional coherence. Training proceeds iteratively: V2T is first trained on augmentation groups, then T2V on template groups under V2T supervision, and in subsequent iterations both are refined alternately using updated pseudo-class centers and re-grouped images, with the clipped PPO-style policy update ensuring stable relative optimization within each group.

## Results

Using the CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone, SyncVL achieves an average accuracy of 76.48% across 12 unsupervised adaptation datasets, surpassing the previous best method DPA (72.29%) by 4.19%. The largest gains appear on fine-grained and texture datasets: DTD (+7.59% over DPA), SUN397 (+6.71%), and UCF101 (+6.01%). On OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-Sketch/Rendition/Adversarial), SyncVL in the transductive setting reaches 47.24/74.18/36.98, outperforming all few-shot and transductive baselines. For cross-modal retrieval, SyncVL improves mAP by 10.62% on SVT and 20.71% on TTR over CYN-Ret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncVL outperforms all 22 compared baselines across every task. Against the closest unsupervised adaptation competitor DPA [3], SyncVL gains +4.19% average accuracy (76.48 vs. 72.29), with the largest margins on DTD (+7.59%), SUN397 (+6.71%), and UCF101 (+6.01%). Compared to ReCLIP [30] (+6.40%) and LafTer [50] (+6.58%), the advantage is even larger, while over the raw CLIP baseline [57] the gain reaches +11.44%. In the OOD setting (Table 3), SyncVL (transductive) beats CoOp-16 [100] by +6.80/+9.73/+6.36 on ImageNet-Sketch/Rendition/Adversarial, and outperforms CLIP-Adapter [22] by a wide margin (+15.20/+16.51/+15.02). For few-shot comparison (Table 4), SyncVL (UA, no labels) surpasses CoOp-8 [69] by +4.02% average and MaPLe-16 [34] by +6.71% average across six datasets. In cross-modal retrieval (Table 7), SyncVL improves mAP over CYN-Ret [56] by +10.62 on SVT and +20.71 on TTR, and over the TULIP backbone [70] by +5.20/+11.81/+11.34/+4.19 on SVT/TTR/PSTR/INQUIRE respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncVL extends the unsupervised VL adaptation line (DPA, ReCLIP, LaFTER) by keeping the pre-trained dual-encoder backbone frozen and instead training two lightweight transformer-based synchronizers (V2T and T2V) that iteratively refine each other through mutual knowledge distillation. Its key conceptual departure is repurposing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), originally designed for LLM fine-tuning, to contrastive VLMs by redefining embedding generation as a latent policy whose reward derives from intra-group consistency and cross-modal alignment rather than explicit labels. Unlike prior unidirectional adaptation strategies, the bidirectional design with a shared latent subspace at inference is a structural novelty, and the gradient-conflict analysis (Table 10) provides mechanistic evidence that GRPO's relative-reward normalization is what decouples the otherwise competing consistency and alignment objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, label-free recipe for closing the modality gap in any dual-encoder VLM without risking catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge, which is a persistent concern with fine-tuning-based adaptation. The consistent gains across seven backbones, 24 datasets, and diverse tasks (classification, detection, segmentation, retrieval, clustering) suggest the bidirectional synchronizer + GRPO formulation is broadly applicable rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or generative VLMs, how sensitive performance is to the number of augmentation views and text templates, and whether the iterative pseudo-label refinement can still accumulate confirmation bias over many iterations despite the relative-reward framing.
