## Problem

Existing 3DGS pruning methods face an irreconcilable trade-off: parameter-space heuristics (e.g., LightGaussian) are fast but ignore the non-linear rendering pipeline, causing erroneous removal of critical high-frequency primitives, while render-aware methods (e.g., PUP 3D-GS) preserve perceptual accuracy but require expensive forward rendering and backpropagation loops, incurring ~10,000 GFLOPs per scene. No prior method achieves render-aware importance scoring without the computational cost of actual rasterization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260721/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

REFINE consumes a pre-trained 3DGS model (typically ~2.1M Gaussian primitives, each with 59 parameters including position, scale, rotation, SH color coefficients, and opacity) along with COLMAP camera poses for the training views. It outputs a globally ranked importance score per primitive and a pruned scene with the bottom β·N primitives removed, where β is a user-specified ratio (10%–70%). The pipeline is strictly zero-shot post-processing: no fine-tuning, re-optimization, or access to original multi-view images is required after pruning.

## Method

REFINE formulates primitive importance as the expected L2 image-space error induced by removal, approximated via a Gauss-Newton/Fisher Information Hessian field, then analytically decomposes the rendering Jacobian into two rendering-free components: view-dependent visibility (approximated by standalone opacity under a zero-occlusion assumption) and geometric projection (approximated by inverse-square depth scaling from a local affine projection Jacobian). A content-adaptive hyperparameter mechanism dynamically calibrates the relative sensitivity of geometry, color, and opacity attributes using scene-level statistics (color variance, opacity ambiguity, scale anisotropy), normalized by their expectations. The final per-primitive importance score is a weighted sum of these Hessian weights multiplied by the corresponding parameter magnitudes, computed in O(N) algebraic operations across sampled viewpoints. This yields a ~3,000× GFLOPs reduction over rendering-based baselines while matching their PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS at pruning ratios up to 50%, with a ~20× wall-clock speedup on GPU.

## Results

On MipNeRF 360 at 50% pruning, REFINE achieves 26.61 PSNR / 0.800 SSIM / 0.233 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest rendering-based baseline PUP (26.07 PSNR) while requiring only 3.14 GFLOPs versus PUP's 9,582.81 GFLOPs — a >3,000× reduction in computational cost. At 10% pruning, REFINE matches PUP exactly (27.34 PSNR, 0.814 SSIM). In terms of wall-clock time, REFINE completes pruning in 3.85 s on MipNeRF 360 (Ratio=0.5) versus 37.02 s for PUP, yielding a practical ~10–20× latency speedup on an RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PUP 3D-GS (the strongest rendering-based baseline), REFINE wins on MipNeRF 360 at 50% pruning (26.61 vs. 26.07 PSNR) and ties at 10% (27.34 vs. 27.34), but loses at 70% pruning on Deep Blending (27.93 vs. 29.14 PSNR) where dense occlusion makes the omitted cross-primitive coupling non-negligible. Against LightGaussian, REFINE gains a substantial 4.4 dB PSNR advantage at 50% pruning on MipNeRF 360 (26.61 vs. 22.21), confirming that parameter-space heuristics erroneously remove critical high-frequency primitives. Against MesonGS, REFINE leads by ~0.9 dB PSNR at 50% on MipNeRF 360 (26.61 vs. 25.68) and by ~1.4 dB at 70% (24.43 vs. 24.35 on MipNeRF 360; 20.98 vs. 21.41 on Tanks & Temples where MesonGS actually edges ahead). Against GHAP, REFINE dominates by 6+ dB PSNR across all datasets at 50% pruning, as GHAP's optimal-transport formulation is designed for iterative cluster reconstruction rather than one-shot pruning. In computational cost, REFINE requires only 3.14 GFLOPs on MipNeRF 360 versus 9,582.81 (PUP) and 9,539.19 (MesonGS), a >3,000× reduction, while completing in 3.85 s versus 37.02 s (PUP) and 41.43 s (MesonGS).

## Relation to Prior Work

REFINE positions itself as a bridge between two established categories of 3DGS pruning: parameter-space heuristics (LightGaussian, GHAP) that are fast but ignore rendering physics, and rendering-aware methods (PUP 3D-GS, MesonGS) that are accurate but require expensive forward/backward rasterization passes. Its core departure is to analytically approximate the Gauss-Newton Hessian (equivalently, the Fisher Information Matrix) that PUP computes via actual differentiable rendering, by decomposing the rendering Jacobian into a view-dependent visibility term (alpha-blending transmittance) and a geometric projection term (EWA-style Jacobian energy), then replacing both with closed-form expressions. The content-adaptive hyperparameter mechanism, which dynamically calibrates attribute sensitivities from scene-level statistics (color variance, opacity ambiguity, scale anisotropy), is a novel addition not present in prior pruning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3000× reduction in pruning-related GFLOPs makes 3DGS compression practically feasible on resource-constrained devices (VR/AR headsets, mobile phones) where rendering-based pruning is simply too slow, and the plug-and-play post-processing design means it can be applied to any pre-trained 3DGS model without retraining. The key limitation is the primitive-independence assumption: at extreme pruning ratios (≥70%) in densely occluded scenes, cross-primitive alpha-blending coupling becomes non-negligible and quality degrades below rendering-aware baselines. Open questions include whether lightweight localized re-rendering or low-rank off-diagonal Hessian approximations can close this gap while preserving the speed advantage, and whether the Hessian field formulation can be repurposed as a rate-distortion guide or training regularizer beyond post-hoc pruning.
