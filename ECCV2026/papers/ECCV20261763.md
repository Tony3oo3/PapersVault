## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion-based real-world SR methods face two intertwined gaps: a single static LoRA adapter cannot specialize across heterogeneous real-world degradations (blur, noise, JPEG, resize), and dual-branch designs that freeze the pixel module during semantic training still allow the semantic branch to redundantly re-learn features already encoded in the pixel subspace, preventing genuine perceptual gains. No prior method explicitly controls the geometry of semantic updates relative to the fidelity subspace.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261763/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB low-resolution image (×4 SR task) and produces a high-resolution RGB image in one forward pass through a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet. The LR image is encoded by a pre-trained VAE into latent space, and the SR output is decoded back to pixel space. An optional adjustable inference mode introduces pixel-level and semantic-level guidance scales (λ_pix, λ_sem) that require two forward passes but enable continuous fidelity-perception trade-off without retraining.

## Method

FreqOrtho-SR replaces the monolithic pixel-level LoRA with a Frequency-guided Mixture of LoRA Experts (FreqMoE): N parallel LoRA expert pairs per layer are routed via top-k gating that combines local spatial features with a non-parametric FFT-based degradation descriptor (radial band energies plus blur/JPEG/noise scalar scores) computed once per image. A second contribution, Orthogonal Gradient Projection (OGP), performs SVD on the concatenated expert weight deltas after Phase 1 to extract the pixel-fidelity subspace, then projects all semantic LoRA gradients onto its null space during Phase 2, guaranteeing zero overlap between the two objectives. Training is two-phase: Phase 1 optimizes FreqMoE with L2 and load-balancing losses; Phase 2 trains the semantic LoRA with L2, LPIPS, and classifier score distillation losses under the OGP constraint, while both the base SD weights and FreqMoE remain frozen.

## Results

On DRealSR, FreqOrtho-SR achieves the best PSNR (29.08 dB), SSIM (0.7911), LPIPS (0.2799), DISTS (0.2110), and MANIQA (0.6182) among all one-step diffusion methods. On RealSR, it attains the best LPIPS (0.2537), DISTS (0.1951), FID (108.91), NIQE (5.32), and MANIQA (0.6586), with PSNR of 26.27 dB. On the synthetic DIV2K benchmark, it achieves the best PSNR (24.35 dB), LPIPS (0.2748), and FID (23.54). The method also achieves the best no-reference scores on the OOD RealLR200 benchmark (NIQE 3.90, CLIPIQA 0.6912, MUSIQ 70.60, MANIQA 0.6321).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AddSR and SinSR, FreqOrtho-SR dominates across all metrics and all three datasets, with margins of 2–3 dB in PSNR and 0.03–0.10 in LPIPS. Compared to OSEDiff, FreqOrtho-SR wins on PSNR, LPIPS, DISTS, FID, and MANIQA on every dataset, though OSEDiff edges ahead on NIQE for RealSR (5.65 vs. 5.32 is actually FreqOrtho-SR winning; OSEDiff wins on nothing clearly). Against MoR-DASR, FreqOrtho-SR achieves better PSNR, LPIPS, and MANIQA, though MoR-DASR reports higher CLIPIQA on RealSR (0.6910 vs. 0.6630) and DRealSR (0.7170 vs. 0.6879). The closest competitor is PiSA-SR: FreqOrtho-SR wins on PSNR (+0.77 dB on DRealSR), LPIPS, DISTS, and FID (RealSR), while PiSA-SR retains slight advantages on MUSIQ (70.15 vs. 69.39 on RealSR), NIQE (6.20 vs. 6.29 on DRealSR), and CLIPIQA (0.6970 vs. 0.6879 on DRealSR). TVT is the strongest baseline on SSIM (0.7596 vs. 0.7481 on RealSR; 0.6292 vs. 0.6189 on DIV2K) and CLIPIQA (0.7220 vs. 0.6879 on DRealSR), but FreqOrtho-SR surpasses it on PSNR, LPIPS, DISTS, FID, and MANIQA across all datasets, with a 0.81 dB PSNR gap on DRealSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-LoRA paradigm of PiSA-SR [51] and the one-step diffusion formulation of OSEDiff [66] in two directions: replacing the single pixel-level LoRA with a frequency-guided Mixture of LoRA Experts (FreqMoE) that routes via non-parametric FFT-based degradation signatures, and augmenting the simple pixel-branch freezing with Orthogonal Gradient Projection (OGP) borrowed from Gradient Projection Memory in continual learning [48]. Unlike MoR-DASR [19], which routes experts via a frozen CLIP encoder with predefined prompt pairs, FreqMoE uses explicit frequency-domain features (radial band energies plus blur/JPEG/noise scores) for interpretable, parameter-free routing. The key conceptual departure from PiSA-SR is the argument that freezing pixel weights is insufficient because the semantic branch can still receive gradient components along the pixel-fidelity subspace, leading to redundant capacity usage; OGP addresses this by projecting semantic gradients into the null space of the pixel subspace extracted via SVD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, mathematically grounded mechanism (OGP) for enforcing subspace independence between fidelity and perceptual objectives in diffusion-based SR, moving beyond the ad-hoc parameter freezing used by prior dual-branch methods. The quantification of subspace overlap (up to 87% in the output layer without OGP) offers concrete evidence for why naive decoupling fails, which could inform future multi-objective SR designs. Limitations include the MoE inference overhead (0.30 s vs. 0.09 s for PiSA-SR on 128×128 inputs), evaluation only on SD 2.1-base at ×4 scale, and the fact that FreqMoE experts are not pre-assigned to degradation types, making the specialization emergent rather than guaranteed. Open questions include whether OGP generalizes to non-LoRA parameterizations, whether expert pruning or structured sparsity can close the inference gap, and whether the frequency-based routing signal remains discriminative for degradations outside the RealESRGAN pipeline.
