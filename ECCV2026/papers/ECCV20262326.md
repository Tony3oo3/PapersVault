## Problem

Large 3D spatial graphs of vessel-like tubular networks (airways, cerebral and pulmonary vasculature) contain tens of thousands of nodes and complex non-tree topologies, making them computationally intractable for existing graph or shape tokenizers. Prior vessel-specific methods are limited to small tree-only structures or operate at native graph resolution, while generic surface-based shape tokenizers waste representational capacity on redundant tubular surface samples. The paper addresses the gap of learning compact, high-fidelity latent tokens for large, topologically complex vascular graphs that support reconstruction, generation, and inverse problems.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a 3D centerline point cloud (not surface points) with a fixed pseudo-radius r=0.016 assigned globally, producing a fixed-length sequence of 512 continuous tokens of dimension 4 regardless of input graph size (which ranges from ~2,500 to >100,000 nodes). The decoder reconstructs a continuous occupancy field evaluated on a 512³ voxel grid, which is thresholded and skeletonized back into a discrete centerline graph with neighborhood-based edge connectivity. For downstream tasks, an incomplete vessel graph (~40% missing links) serves as conditioning for link prediction, and anatomical class labels condition the generative diffusion model.

## Method

VesselTok represents each 3D graph as a continuous occupancy field by dilating centerline edges with a fixed pseudo-radius, then learns a VAE-style tokenizer over this field using transformer encoder-decoder blocks. The key architectural choice is operating on centerline points rather than surface samples: the encoder initializes query points via farthest point sampling from the centerline cloud, applies Fourier positional encoding, and uses cross-attention between point embeddings and queries followed by self-attention to produce the latent; the decoder uses self-attention on the latent and cross-attention with grid-sampled query points to predict the occupancy field. Training uses binary cross-entropy on the occupancy field plus KL divergence, with an imbalance-aware query selection strategy emphasizing boundary points. At inference, the predicted field is thresholded and skeletonized to recover a discrete graph. For generation, an EDM diffusion model is trained in the 512-token latent space; for link prediction, a Diffusion Transformer with conditional flow matching denoises toward complete graphs given incomplete ones as conditioning.

## Results

On the ATM airway tree dataset, VesselTok achieves a clDice of 96.61 and Chamfer Distance of 0.005, outperforming 3DShape2VecSet (clDice 90.11, CD 0.007) and Hunyuan3D 2.0 (clDice 87.31, CD 0.007). On unseen anatomies, VesselTok generalizes strongly: 99.42 clDice on the small Circle of Willis (TopCoW) and 88.86 clDice on large-scale renal vasculature (>100K nodes), compared to 79.66 and 73.81 for 3DShape2VecSet respectively. For generative modeling, VesselTok's latent tokens yield lower FID and MMD-CD/EMD in both unconditional and conditional settings, with reduced Betti errors indicating better topological alignment with the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DShape2VecSet, VesselTok wins on every metric on ATM (clDice 96.61 vs. 90.11, CD 0.005 vs. 0.007, |Δβ₀| 0.07 vs. 0.09, |Δβ₁| 8.97 vs. 9.48), with the largest gap in clDice (~6.5 points). Against Hunyuan3D 2.0, VesselTok similarly dominates across all four metrics (clDice 96.61 vs. 87.31). Against the vessel-specific VesselGPT, VesselTok achieves substantially higher clDice (96.61 vs. 77.09) and lower CD and |Δβ₁|, though VesselGPT edges out on |Δβ₀| (0.05 vs. 0.07), likely benefiting from its tree-only preprocessing that removes spurious cycles. On the link-prediction (infill) task, VesselTok outperforms the autodecoder baseline [1] on all metrics (clDice 88.13 vs. 83.49, CD 0.043 vs. 0.066, |Δβ₀| 3.19 vs. 4.32). On out-of-distribution renal vasculature, VesselTok's advantage over 3DShape2VecSet widens to ~9 clDice points (88.86 vs. 79.66), suggesting the centerline-based design scales better to very large, complex graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

VesselTok extends the 3D shape tokenization paradigm (3DShape2VecSet, Hunyuan3D 2.0, Michelangelo) from surface-centric representations to a centerline-based occupancy field, motivated by the extreme surface-to-volume ratio of tubular networks where surface sampling wastes query budget on redundant geometry. Architecturally, it adopts the transformer encoder-decoder with cross-attention to query points from Hunyuan3D 2.0 and Michelangelo, and the imbalance-aware boundary query selection from 3DShape2VecSet. It departs from prior vessel-specific methods (VesselGPT, VesselVAE, Batten et al.) that are restricted to small tree-like structures by handling arbitrary topologies including loops and non-tree connectivity at scale (up to 100K+ nodes), and from generic graph tokenizers (VQGraph, OpenGraph) that retain native node count and thus cannot compress large graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VesselTok provides a single compact token space (512×4) that simultaneously supports high-fidelity reconstruction, unconditional/conditional generative modeling, and inverse problems (link prediction) for large 3D spatial graphs, removing the computational bottleneck that has limited prior vessel-generation work to small or tree-only structures. The demonstrated generalization to unseen anatomies (renal vasculature at 10× training scale, Circle of Willis) and to topologically perturbed synthetic data suggests the learned prior captures structural regularities beyond dataset-specific geometry. A key open question is whether the fixed pseudo-radius and fixed token budget remain sufficient as topological complexity grows (the paper notes a sharp clDice drop on large COSTA graphs), and whether the approach extends to other high-surface-to-volume structures beyond vasculature and airways.
