## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models suffer from 'visual fading' in long-context scenarios: as the text sequence lengthens, attention to visual tokens decays sharply, causing generated text to detach from visual constraints. The paper identifies the root cause as MRoPE's inherited long-term decay property, which imposes a distance penalty on inter-modal attention that grows monotonically during autoregressive generation, structurally forcing the model to look away from the image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260426/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in position-encoding modification within an existing MLLM (Qwen2.5-3B backbone with SigLIP2-SO400M vision encoder using NaFlex for dynamic resolution). The image is projected via a two-layer MLP with GELU, and the model takes an image plus a text query to produce a text response. No changes to input format, output structure, or model parameters are required; DIPE only alters how positional indices are assigned to query vectors depending on whether the target key belongs to the same or a different modality.

## Method

DIPE orthogonally decouples position encoding based on modality interactions: intra-modal attention (text-to-text, image-to-image) retains standard MRoPE to preserve linguistic locality and 2D spatial geometry, while inter-modal attention applies an Anchored Position Encoding to queries that broadcasts the first token's (t,h,w) coordinates across the entire modality segment, fixing the perceptual distance between text and visual tokens to a constant regardless of generation length. At implementation, this requires a split-kernel strategy: two modality-masked attention passes (one with sequential queries, one with anchored queries) are executed and merged via a LogSumExp fusion formula, ensuring mathematically exact normalization. The approach introduces zero additional parameters, is compatible with FlexAttention kernels and standard KV cache infrastructure (since it only modifies the query-side rotation), and generalizes to interleaved multi-image contexts by assigning a unique anchor per modality segment.

## Results

Under the Long-Context VQA protocol (8K distractor tokens), MRoPE + DIPE achieves 48.79% overall accuracy, a +4.10% absolute gain over the MRoPE baseline (44.69%). Vanilla RoPE + DIPE and MRoPE-I + DIPE yield +2.00% and +2.01% gains respectively. On the Short-Context VQA protocol, DIPE-enhanced models maintain strict parity with baselines (e.g., MRoPE-I + DIPE: 51.4% vs. 51.5% baseline), confirming it is a non-destructive enhancement. On the smaller Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone, the overall gain expands to +8.81%, and on Qwen3-1.7B it reaches +4.25%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIPE is evaluated as a plug-in on top of three position-encoding baselines: Vanilla RoPE, MRoPE (Qwen2-VL), and MRoPE-I (Qwen3-VL). MRoPE + DIPE achieves the largest overall gain of +4.10% (44.69→48.79%), with standout improvements on POPE (+11.08%), CountBench (+9.37%), and MMBench-EN (+8.75%), because MRoPE's suboptimal frequency allocation across spatial dimensions most severely exacerbates inter-modal distance decay. Vanilla RoPE + DIPE and MRoPE-I + DIPE yield more modest but consistent gains of +2.00% and +2.01% respectively. A few isolated regressions appear (e.g., V* drops 3.15% under MRoPE-I + DIPE; InfoVQA drops 1.76% under MRoPE + DIPE), but these are outweighed by the broad improvements across 17 of 19 benchmarks. On the Short-Context protocol, all three DIPE variants match their baselines within 0.1%, confirming no degradation in standard settings. The gains are further amplified on smaller backbones: Qwen2.5-0.5B sees +8.81% and Qwen3-1.7B sees +4.25%, indicating DIPE is most beneficial where long-context visual grounding is weakest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MRoPE framework from Qwen2-VL [57], which decomposes position encoding into temporal, height, and width components for multimodal sequences, and on the foundational RoPE rotation mechanism [47] whose long-term decay property is the root cause of the identified problem. Rather than proposing a new position encoding scheme, DIPE orthogonally decomposes the existing MRoPE into intra-modal (sequential) and inter-modal (anchored) attention, making it a complementary plug-in to any MRoPE variant (including MRoPE-I [20], VideoRoPE [60], and Circle-RoPE [55]). The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the distance penalty—designed to enforce linguistic locality in text—becomes structurally harmful when applied to the fixed visual-to-growing-text relationship during autoregressive generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIPE addresses a practical and increasingly critical limitation of MLLMs as they are pushed toward long-context multimodal reasoning, offering a parameter-free, infrastructure-compatible fix (works with KV cache and FlexAttention) that practitioners can adopt without retraining from scratch. The residual attention decay even with DIPE, and the sharp accuracy drop at 64K tokens on MM-NIAH, indicate that position encoding is not the sole bottleneck—attention dilution from softmax normalization and the underlying LLM's context capacity remain open challenges. Whether the anchored-distance principle generalizes to video (where temporal dynamics are more complex) or to settings with many interleaved images at extreme resolutions is not yet explored.
