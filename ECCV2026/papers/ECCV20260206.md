## Problem

Existing video object removal methods excel at inpainting behind objects and correcting photometric artifacts like shadows and reflections, but they fail when the removed object has physical interactions with the scene—such as collisions, support, or blocking—producing implausible results (e.g., dominoes continuing to fall after middle tiles are removed). The core challenge is that correct removal requires counterfactual causal reasoning: the model must simulate how the scene would evolve in the object's absence, not merely fill a spatial hole.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip and a user-provided binary object mask (generated from sparse clicks via Segment Anything 2). At inference, a VLM pipeline expands this binary mask into a four-region quadmask (black = object, dark grey = object/affected overlap, light grey = affected region, white = unchanged) by identifying affected objects, segmenting them with SAM 3, and predicting their counterfactual positions via a coarse spatial grid. The output is a counterfactual video in which the target object and all downstream physical interactions it induced are removed, with unaffected regions preserved.

## Method

VOID is a two-pass video diffusion model built on the CogVideoX transformer backbone, initialized from Generative Omnimatte weights and fine-tuned on ~6,400 counterfactual video pairs generated from Kubric rigid-body simulations and HUMOTO human-object interaction mocap. The key training innovation is quadmask conditioning, which extends the prior trimask with a fourth color to resolve ambiguity where the removed object overlaps with dynamically affected regions, providing tighter spatial guidance than the looser trimask. At inference, a VLM (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro) performs scene analysis to identify affected objects and predict their counterfactual trajectories, producing the quadmask without requiring ground-truth masks. The first pass generates the counterfactual trajectory; an optional second pass re-runs the model with flow-warped noise (following Go-with-the-Flow) derived from the first pass's optical flow to correct structural deformation in newly moving objects, triggered only when the VLM detects significant dynamic reconfiguration.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (40 videos with ground-truth counterfactuals), VOID achieves the best PSNR (31.49 dB), DreamSim (0.07), DINOv2 similarity (0.92), FVD (260.31 vs. 437.88 for the next-best Gen-Omnimatte), and VLM-judge score (25.10/30), while being competitive on LPIPS (0.12). In the human preference study on 75 real-world videos, VOID is selected 64.8% of the time versus 18.4% for Runway and 11.2% for Generative Omnimatte. Across three VLM judges on real-world edits, VOID ranks first in total score (e.g., 26.13/30 under Gemini 3 Pro), with the largest margin in the 'Interaction & Physics' criterion (3.66 vs. 2.61 for Runway).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VOID outperforms all six baselines on the majority of metrics. Against the strongest inpainting baselines (ProPainter, DiffuEraser, MiniMax-Remover, ROSE), VOID leads by 1–2 dB in PSNR, 0.02–0.06 in DreamSim, and 40–240 points in FVD, while matching or slightly trailing on frame-wise LPIPS (0.12 vs. 0.10–0.11), which the authors attribute to LPIPS's sensitivity to small spatial shifts in counterfactual motion. Against Generative Omnimatte (the closest prior method using trimask conditioning), VOID improves FVD from 437.88 to 260.31 and VLM-judge from 20.40 to 25.10, confirming that quadmask conditioning plus counterfactual training data substantially improves interaction-aware synthesis. Against Runway (a text-guided commercial editor given explicit prompts about expected scene evolution), VOID wins on every metric, with the largest gap in FVD (260 vs. 443) and VLM-judge (25.10 vs. 21.67), and in human preference (64.8% vs. 18.4%). The paper notes that traditional inpainting methods (ProPainter, DiffuEraser) receive 0–4% human preference, indicating they cannot model downstream physical interactions at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

VOID extends the video object removal line of work (Omnimatte, Generative Omnimatte, ROSE) from photometric effect correction (shadows, reflections) to physically-causal counterfactual rewriting, where removing an object must alter downstream kinetic interactions. It is initialized from Generative Omnimatte's weights and extends its trimask conditioning to a four-region quadmask that disambiguates overlap between the removed object and affected regions. The second-pass stabilization borrows the flow-warped noise mechanism from Go-with-the-Flow, and the inference-time pipeline repurposes VLMs (Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.2) for counterfactual scene reasoning—a use of VLM world knowledge in video editing that prior work (Video-Repair, Veggie) has only applied to simpler grounding or segmentation tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VOID reframes video object removal as a causal simulation problem rather than a spatial inpainting problem, and the VLM-guided quadmask pipeline offers a practical recipe for injecting high-level world knowledge into diffusion-based video editing. The qualitative generalization to unseen physical interactions (balloons floating, blenders not activating) suggests the approach will improve as underlying VLMs and video diffusion models grow more capable, making it a useful template for future work. Open questions include scaling to longer videos, handling multi-object simultaneous removal, and closing the domain gap for unusual camera geometries that the authors acknowledge as limitations.
