## Problem

Existing interactive benchmarks evaluate either production/skill execution (e.g., Minecraft, Atari) or social reasoning (e.g., Avalon, Smallville) in isolation, but none assess agents within the coupled, constraint-bound structure of human life where productive outputs enable social participation and social relationships unlock production opportunities. This paper introduces StarDojo to systematically benchmark agentic MLLMs on open-ended production-living tasks that require simultaneous mastery of farming, crafting, exploration, combat, and socially situated decision-making under realistic time, energy, and seasonal constraints.

## Input / Output

Each observation combines a 720P (1280×720) gameplay screenshot with structured textual state including a 7×7 agent-centered tile grid, character health/energy, and global information (time, weather, NPC positions, budget), plus the previous timestep's history for temporal consistency. The agent outputs at most two abstracted skills per step from a vocabulary of ten fundamental actions (move, use, interact, choose_item, attach_item, detach_item, craft, choose_option, menu), which are executed sequentially before the environment pauses for the next inference. Task completion is determined automatically by an evaluator that compares incremental game-state changes via StarDojoMod rather than relying on fixed action sequences.

## Method

StarDojo's core technical contribution is StarDojoMod, a C# extension built on SMAPI that communicates with the Stardew Valley engine through a socket server, exposing rendered frames, internal game states, and callable action functions directly to agents—eliminating the need for keyboard/mouse simulation and foreground window constraints. A Python Wrapper encapsulates this interface for observation retrieval, action execution, and task customization, while a configurable pause-and-resume mechanism freezes the game during model inference to decouple reasoning latency from in-game time. The environment supports parallel headless execution via unique ports per instance and Xvfb, with shared-memory communication reducing observation retrieval to ~30 ms. The benchmark comprises 1,000 curated tasks across five domains and three difficulty levels (with a 100-task StarDojo-Lite subset), each initialized from saved game files and task-specific commands, and evaluated through a general incremental-state-comparison evaluator that tracks progress without hardcoding per-task logic.

## Results

On the StarDojo-Lite benchmark (100 tasks across Farming, Crafting, Exploration, Combat, and Social), the best-performing model GPT-4.1 achieves only 12.7% overall success rate, with Gemini 2.5 Pro (10.7%) and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (10.3%) close behind. All open-source models (Llama 4 Maverick, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Gemma 3 27B) remain below 8%, with Gemma 3 27B at just 4.0%. A striking finding is that every model achieves near-zero success on Hard tasks (0% across the board) and very low success on Medium tasks (≤5.3%), indicating that long-horizon multi-step tasks remain far from solved even by frontier MLLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4.1 leads all baselines at 12.7% overall, outperforming Gemini 2.5 Pro (10.7%) by 2.0 points and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (10.3%) by 2.4 points, with its advantage concentrated in Crafting (26.2% vs. 23.8% and 14.3%) and Exploration (10.7% vs. 6.0% and 9.5%). Among open-source models, Llama 4 Maverick (7.7%) edges out GPT-4.1 mini (7.0%), Qwen2.5-VL-72B (6.7%), and Gemma 3 27B (4.0%), but the gap to the best closed-source model exceeds 5 points. Notably, all models collapse to 0% on Hard tasks and achieve at most 5.3% on Medium tasks, meaning no baseline can reliably complete multi-day or multi-subtask objectives. Combat is the weakest category overall, with only GPT-4.1 and GPT-4.1 mini scoring above 0% (2.8%), while Social tasks show the most variance across models (5.3%–8.0%). The ablation further reveals that removing textual input drops GPT-4.1's performance substantially, and disabling game pausing (real-time mode) severely degrades results on time-sensitive tasks like combat and long-horizon chopping.

## Relation to Prior Work

StarDojo builds technically on SMAPI (the Stardew Modding API) to create a structured agent-game interface, but conceptually it carves out a new niche at the intersection of two previously separate benchmark lineages: production-focused environments (MineDojo, Crafter) and social-interaction environments (Smallville, AvalonBench). The authors explicitly position StarDojo as the first benchmark where production activities and social interactions are coupled through shared resource, time, and economic constraints, rather than evaluated in isolation. It also departs from prior interactive benchmarks by providing a unified Python API that eliminates raw keyboard/mouse control and supports parallel headless execution, addressing practical barriers that limited prior game-based agent evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark capability gap: even GPT-4.1 achieves only 12.7% overall success, with 0% on all hard tasks and near-zero on medium tasks across every model tested. The error analysis (42% visual understanding, 21% multimodal reasoning, 21% long-term planning, 16% low-level control) and the real-time ablation—showing that API inference latency alone can render tasks infeasible—provide concrete, actionable diagnostics for the agent community. Key open questions include whether the 1,000-task full suite and the extended Playthrough task (1 million in-game currency) can be tackled by future models, whether the narrow social evaluation (focused on early-game NPC interactions) generalizes to richer social intelligence, and whether the benchmark's reliance on a commercial game copy limits reproducibility.
