## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models face a fundamental dilemma: mitigating error accumulation over long sequences requires extensive historical context, yet the KV cache grows linearly with video length, making minute-scale generation intractable on a single GPU (a 2-minute 832×480 video requires ~138 GB of KV storage). Existing workarounds like history truncation or sliding windows irreversibly discard intermediate memory, causing progressive semantic drift and quality collapse beyond the training horizon.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262603/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (refined via Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and generates block-by-block (4 latent frames per block, 4 distilled denoising steps) a video up to 120 seconds at 832×480 resolution and 16 FPS on a single H200 GPU. Despite being trained exclusively on 5-second clips (20 latent frames), it achieves 24× temporal extrapolation to 2-minute outputs, with the attention context strictly bounded at ~27,872 tokens regardless of total video length.

## Method

PackForcing partitions the autoregressive KV cache into three functional regions: sink tokens (first 8 frames at full resolution as permanent semantic anchors), compressed mid tokens (the bulk of history reduced ~32× via a dual-branch module fusing progressive 3D convolutions with low-resolution VAE re-encoding, further pruned by a query-key affinity-based top-k selection), and recent/current tokens (kept at full resolution for local temporal coherence). A dual-resolution shifting pipeline concurrently computes full-resolution and compressed KV pairs to hide compression latency, while an incremental temporal-only RoPE rotation corrects positional discontinuities caused by evicted tokens at <0.1% overhead. This hierarchical design enforces O(1) attention complexity with a constant ~27,872-token context, enabling the model to attend to a comprehensive compressed history without ever exceeding a fixed memory budget.

## Results

On VBench-Long, PackForcing achieves the highest Dynamic Degree (56.25 at 60 s, 54.12 at 120 s) and best Overall Consistency (26.07 at 60 s, 26.05 at 120 s) among all compared autoregressive video generation methods. On CLIP-score stability over 60 s, PackForcing shows the smallest decline (34.04 → 32.90, only 1.14 points) versus Self-Forcing's severe 6.77-point drop (33.89 → 27.12). At 120 s, PackForcing also leads in Subject Consistency (92.84) and Aesthetic Quality (61.98), while maintaining competitive Image Quality (69.67) despite a ~32× token reduction in the mid-buffer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CausVid, PackForcing wins on Dynamic Degree by +7.82 (60 s) and +4.12 (120 s), Overall Consistency by +2.71 and +2.92, and shows far superior CLIP stability (1.14-point vs 1.86-point decline over 60 s). Against Self-Forcing, the gap is largest: PackForcing leads Dynamic Degree by +20.32 (60 s) and +23.66 (120 s), and avoids Self-Forcing's catastrophic CLIP collapse (32.90 vs 27.12 at 60 s). Against Deep Forcing, PackForcing gains +2.58 Dynamic Degree and +4.32 Overall Consistency at 60 s, though Deep Forcing edges ahead on Background Consistency (93.80 vs 93.46) and Subject Consistency (92.55 vs 90.49). Against LongLive, PackForcing wins Dynamic Degree by +11.72 (60 s) and +9.59 (120 s) but concedes Subject Consistency (90.49 vs 92.00 at 60 s), reflecting a motion-vs-rigidity trade-off. Against Rolling Forcing, PackForcing leads Dynamic Degree by +22.66 (60 s) and +18.97 (120 s), with a small Overall Consistency edge (+0.34 at 60 s), while Rolling Forcing retains a slight Image Quality advantage (71.06 vs 69.36 at 60 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

PackForcing extends the autoregressive video diffusion lineage established by CausVid (ODE-initialized causal generation) and Self-Forcing (self-generated frame conditioning), and directly builds on DeepForcing's attention-sink and participative compression mechanisms. The key departure is replacing DeepForcing's irreversible token eviction with a learned dual-branch spatiotemporal compression (~32× token reduction) that preserves intermediate history in a bounded buffer, complemented by an incremental RoPE adjustment to handle positional gaps from dynamic top-k selection. This positions the work as an architectural refinement of the KV-cache management problem rather than a new generation paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: generating coherent 2-minute 832×480 videos on a single H200 GPU with strictly bounded memory (~27,872 tokens per layer) removes a major hardware barrier for long-video synthesis. The 24× temporal extrapolation (5s training → 120s generation) demonstrates that context-size invariance, not longer training sequences, is the critical ingredient for long-horizon coherence. However, the paper acknowledges a residual trade-off: PackForcing's Dynamic Degree (56.25) comes at a small cost in Subject Consistency (90.49 vs. LongLive's 92.00), and all results are validated on a single 1.3B backbone at one resolution, leaving open whether the compression strategy generalizes to larger models or higher resolutions.
