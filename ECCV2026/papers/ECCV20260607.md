## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving frameworks model temporal perception and expert specialization as separate, loosely coupled components: temporal models treat all historical frames uniformly without identifying decision-critical moments, while Mixture-of-Experts gating relies on shallow networks over short-term features, producing unstable and uninterpretable expert routing. This decoupling leads to inconsistent decisions under long-horizon or dynamic driving conditions where both temporal reasoning and experience-guided specialization must operate in concert.

## Input / Output

The system consumes six-camera-view images and ego-state measurements (speed, heading, steering angle) at each time step, maintaining a FIFO buffer of K1=20 historical frames sampled every 5 frames along the same route. A driving goal position is additionally provided as a conditioning signal. The output is a sequence of T=4 discrete trajectory waypoints predicted at 2 Hz, generated autoregressively by the activated expert and subsequently refined by a trajectory head before being passed to a PID controller for low-level actuation.

## Method

TEX-Drive integrates two tightly coupled modules: a Key-Frame Guided Temporal Perception (KTP) unit that computes explicit cross-temporal relevance scores between the current frame and each historical candidate, combines them with frame-quality and temporal-proximity bias terms to deterministically select top-K2 key frames, and hierarchically fuses them into a temporally coherent representation; and an Experience-Driven MoE (E-MoE) planner that applies FiLM-based goal conditioning to the fused feature, routes to top-K3 experts by cosine similarity against each expert's long-term memory vector, and generates trajectories via scalar-gated memory fusion followed by autoregressive decoding. Expert memories are updated through momentum-based rehearsal during inference (not backpropagation), with only the top-ranked expert receiving updates to prevent homogenization. The closed-loop design means KTP supplies phase-aware temporal semantics that sharpen E-MoE routing, while E-MoE's experience-driven feedback stabilizes KTP's temporal focus, yielding a synergistic effect that substantially outperforms either component alone.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, TEX-Drive achieves a Driving Score of 68.22 and a Success Rate of 40.62%, surpassing the strongest baseline GEMINUS (65.39 / 37.73%) by 2.83 and 2.89 points respectively, while using only camera input. In open-loop evaluation, TEX-Drive attains the lowest average L2 error of 0.60 m among all compared methods. On the nuScenes real-world dataset, TEX-Drive achieves the lowest average collision rate (0.20%) and a competitive average L2 error of 0.65 m at 3.3 FPS, with the smallest performance degradation as the prediction horizon extends from 1 s to 3 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEX-Drive outperforms all 11 Bench2Drive baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor GEMINUS (the only other MoE-based E2E method), TEX-Drive gains +2.83 Driving Score and +2.89 Success Rate while also achieving a substantially lower L2 error (0.60 m vs. 1.60 m). Compared with the strongest camera-only baselines DriveAdapter (64.22 DS / 33.08% SR) and DriveTrans (63.46 DS / 35.01% SR), TEX-Drive improves Driving Score by 4.0 and 4.8 points respectively. Even against multimodal (C+L) methods such as ThinkTwice (58.79 DS) and LeTFuser (52.53 DS), TEX-Drive leads by 9.4 and 15.7 Driving Score points using only monocular input. In the multi-ability evaluation, TEX-Drive achieves the highest mean score (45.09%) and leads in Merging (32.22% vs. 28.82% for DriveAdapter), while matching the best in Overtaking, Give Way, and Traffic Sign. On nuScenes, TEX-Drive attains the lowest average collision rate (0.20% vs. 0.22% for VAD) and the smallest L2 growth from 1 s to 3 s (0.45→0.91 m), indicating superior long-horizon stability compared to ST-P3, UniAD, GenAD, VAD, and DWM.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEX-Drive carves new ground by unifying two research threads in E2E-AD that have been developed in isolation: temporal perception (RNN, Transformer, and Diffusion-based frame aggregation) and Mixture-of-Experts functional specialization (GEMINUS, MoSE). Prior temporal models treat all historical frames uniformly or rely on implicit attention weighting, while existing MoE gating networks operate on shallow, short-term features without temporal grounding. The paper's conceptual departure is making temporal perception an explicit, interpretable routing signal that feeds into expert selection via long-term memory, rather than treating temporal fusion and expert assignment as independent pipeline stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified temporal-experience framework addresses a practical gap in E2E-AD: unstable expert switching and inconsistent long-horizon decisions that plague both pure temporal models and standard MoE planners. The interpretability of key-frame selection and phase-coherent expert activation offers a diagnostic tool for understanding failure modes in closed-loop driving. However, closed-loop evaluation remains limited to CARLA simulation, and the momentum-based memory update (bypassing backpropagation) raises open questions about long-term adaptation in truly open-ended real-world deployment. Whether the super-additive synergy between KTP and E-MoE generalizes beyond Bench2Drive's 44 scenarios to more diverse driving distributions remains to be tested.
