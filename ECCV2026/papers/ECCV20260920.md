## Problem

Existing test-time scaling methods for text-to-image generation rely on global exploration (noise resampling, prompt rewriting) that becomes sample-inefficient for compositional prompts, where valid structural solutions are sparse and additional sampling yields diminishing returns. No prior training-free TTS approach leverages region-level decomposition as a scaling axis, leaving a gap between global conditioning and the localized, object-centric constraints required by multi-object and spatially relational prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260920/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt (e.g., 'a cat below a backpack') and produces one selected image from N generated candidates. The prompt is first decomposed via chain-of-thought planning into R semantic regions, each assigned a bounding-box mask and a region-level sub-prompt, plus an enriched global prompt. Regional guidance is injected only during the first ~10% of denoising steps (3 of 30 on FLUX.1-dev, 4 of 20 on SANA-1.0-1.6B), after which standard global refinement resumes, keeping per-sample overhead to roughly 10% regardless of region count.

## Method

RAS introduces a training-free Region-Aware Generation (RAG) primitive that, during early denoising, crops local latents from a shared global latent, denoises each under its region-specific prompt in parallel, and fuses them back via normalized mask-weighted blending (z_t = (1−α_t)·z_t^(g) + α_t·Σ M̃_i ⊙ z_t^(i)). After T_rag steps, sampling reverts to a single global trajectory, avoiding boundary artifacts and region-linear cost. RAS then treats the full configuration ξ = (z_0, p̃, {(M_i, p_i′)}, T_rag) as the unit of Best-of-N scaling, allowing independent resampling of noise seeds, global prompts, region prompts, and region layouts. A balanced allocation across noise, global-prompt, and region-prompt dimensions (e.g., 2/4/4) outperforms any single-axis extreme, and the entire pipeline requires no training or architectural modification to the underlying diffusion/flow-matching backbone.

## Results

On GenEval (FLUX.1-dev backbone), RAS achieves an overall score of 0.85 with only 2 samples, matching the 32-sample noise-scaling baseline (0.85) at a 16× reduction in sampling cost, while dramatically improving the Position sub-task from 0.52 to 0.77. With 4 samples, RAS reaches 0.88 overall, surpassing the 32-sample prompt-scaling baseline (0.87) and achieving 0.88 on Position. At the high-budget regime (N=32), RAS attains a state-of-the-art 0.92 overall. On T2I-CompBench, RAS improves spatial relationship scoring from 0.5442 (vanilla FLUX) to 0.7310 and outperforms RPG on all compositional categories while maintaining comparable HPSv3 human-preference scores (9.20 vs. 9.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAS outperforms all single-sample text-to-image baselines (SANA-1.5 at 0.72, Lumina-Image 2.0 at 0.73, SD3 at 0.74, Playground v3 at 0.76) with just N=1 (0.77 overall), and surpasses Janus-Pro-7B (0.80) at N=2 (0.85). Against noise scaling, RAS matches the 32-sample overall score (0.85) with only 2 samples but gains a decisive 0.25-point advantage on Position (0.77 vs. 0.52), revealing that global resampling fails to fix spatial relations. RAS also beats 32-sample prompt scaling (0.88 vs. 0.87 overall) with 8× fewer samples and improves Position from 0.80 to 0.88. On the SANA-1.0-1.6B backbone, RAS at N=8 (0.81) matches 20-sample noise scaling (0.80) and Reflect-DiT (0.81) at 2.5× lower cost. On T2I-CompBench, RAS dominates RPG across all compositional metrics (spatial 0.7310 vs. 0.7042, shape 0.7790 vs. 0.7534, texture 0.9107 vs. 0.8952) while maintaining HPSv3 quality on par with vanilla FLUX (9.20 vs. 9.22), confirming that compositional gains come at negligible aesthetic cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: region-aware generation (RPG [45], hard/soft binding [5]) and test-time scaling via Best-of-N resampling (noise scaling [24], prompt rewriting [50]). It departs from prior region-aware methods by restricting regional conditioning to only the early denoising steps (~10% of the trajectory) rather than the full sampling process, and from prior TTS methods by treating region layouts and region-level prompts as additional resampling dimensions alongside noise and global prompt. The conceptual contribution is reframing regional decomposition not merely as a generation technique but as a new scaling axis that densifies the distribution of valid compositional candidates, making Best-of-N selection far more sample-efficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: matching 32-sample noise scaling with only 2 samples (16× reduction) and achieving a 7.5× end-to-end speedup over global scaling at budget-matched accuracy makes compositional T2I generation substantially cheaper. However, the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to planner quality—GPT-4o achieves 90% layout accuracy while Qwen2-VL-7B drops to 52%—and failure cases persist for highly complex prompts with many objects and multiple pairwise relations. Open questions include developing robust training-free planners from open-source models, exploring sequential (reflection-based) regional refinement without training, and whether the early-injection design generalizes to video or 3D generation where spatial coherence constraints are even stricter.
