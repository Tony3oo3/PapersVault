## Problem

Existing training-free panorama generation methods (DynamicScaler, SphereDiff) are constrained by MultiDiffusion's direct pixel-sampling assumption, which leaves many panorama pixels uncovered when projecting perspective views onto an equirectangular grid. To compensate, they require 44–89 heavily overlapping camera views, each demanding a full diffusion model evaluation per denoising step, resulting in 7–32 minute runtimes per panorama. The paper asks whether denser, non-bijective mappings between panorama and perspective spaces can reduce the view count while improving generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261781/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 2048×4096 equirectangular-projection (ERP) panoramic image. Internally, the panorama is represented as a VAE latent tensor, and 14 perspective views of 512×512 pixels (90° FOV) are rendered from it at each denoising step. The pre-trained FLUX image generator is used in a fully frozen, zero-shot manner with classifier-free guidance (scale 3.5) and 28 total denoising steps.

## Method

LF-MultiDiffusion replaces MultiDiffusion's direct pixel-sampling operators with arbitrary linear projection matrices S_{t,i} that map between the ERP panorama latent and perspective-view latents via bilinear interpolation over 3D sphere correspondences. Because the resulting aggregation no longer admits a closed-form weighted average, the paper reformulates it as a regularized least-squares problem (with a discrete Laplacian regularizer) and solves it inside the denoising loop using a matrix-free Krylov solver (LSMR), adding only ~20% overhead per step. The view directions sweep horizontally across timesteps following DynamicScaler's strategy, and a view-wise post-refinement stage continues denoising each perspective view independently in its native domain before aggregating back to ERP via distortion-aware weighted averaging. This formulation enables coherent panorama generation with only 14 views (vs. 44–89 in baselines), yielding a 15.36× speedup over SphereDiff while improving all quality metrics.

## Results

LF-MultiDiffusion generates 2048×4096 ERP panoramas in 2 min 6 s on a single A100 GPU, achieving a 15.36× speedup over SphereDiff (32 min 15 s) and a 3.58× speedup over DynamicScaler (7 min 31 s). Among training-free methods, it attains the best scores on every metric: Aesthetic 0.616, Imaging (MUSIQ) 0.568, QAlign 3.34, CLIP similarity 29.19, Distortion 4.64, and Continuity 4.86. In a user study (5-point Likert scale), it also receives the highest mean ratings for image quality (4.07), distortion (3.75), and continuity (4.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest training-free baseline SphereDiff, LF-MultiDiffusion improves Aesthetic (0.616 vs 0.597), Imaging (0.568 vs 0.533), QAlign (3.34 vs 3.23), CLIP (29.19 vs 27.65), and Continuity (4.86 vs 4.82), while matching its Distortion score (4.64), all while running 15.36× faster (2 min 6 s vs 32 min 15 s). Against DynamicScaler, it wins on every metric (e.g., CLIP 29.19 vs 26.32, Continuity 4.86 vs 3.83) with a 3.58× speedup. Both training-based baselines (Text2Light, PanFusion) are substantially weaker on text alignment and geometric consistency, and PanFusion is limited to 512×1024 resolution. The user study confirms the quantitative advantage: LF-MultiDiffusion scores 4.07 on image quality versus 3.64 for SphereDiff and 2.39 for DynamicScaler, with the largest gap in perceived continuity (4.07 vs 3.96 vs 2.25). The authors attribute the gains over SphereDiff to denser bilinear linear projections that stabilize the least-squares fusion on the curved ERP domain, allowing only 14 views instead of 89.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MultiDiffusion (Bar-Tal et al., ICML 2023), which fuses diffusion paths for arbitrary-shape generation, by replacing its direct pixel-sampling constraint with arbitrary linear projections between target and reference image spaces. It builds on the panoramic-specific formulations of DynamicScaler (ERP-based view sweeping) and SphereDiff (spherical latent representation and Distortion-Aware Weighted Averaging), but departs from both by casting latent aggregation as a regularized least-squares problem solvable with a matrix-free Krylov method (LSMR/PCG). This removes the requirement for dense, overlapping camera views that prior training-free panorama methods needed to cover all ERP pixels, reducing the view count from 44–89 to 14.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15.36× speedup over SphereDiff while simultaneously improving all quality metrics (aesthetic, imaging, text alignment, distortion, continuity) makes training-free panorama generation substantially more practical for real-world applications like HMD content and virtual tours. The formulation is general—any linear projection between target and reference spaces is admissible—suggesting applicability to other non-planar domains (torus-like walk-through representations, mesh surface textures) as the authors note. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to video panorama generation (briefly explored in the supplement with LTX-Video), how it behaves with different base models beyond FLUX, and whether the view-wise post-refinement stage can be eliminated without quality loss.
