## Problem

Current computational pathology foundation models are slide-centric: they encode each whole-slide image independently and merge multi-slide cases via naive heuristics (concatenation or averaging), discarding cross-slide interactions that carry diagnostic signal. Additionally, top-performing models often depend on proprietary data, expensive paired-report supervision, or billion-parameter tile encoders, limiting reproducibility and scalability. MOOZY addresses this by making the patient case, not the individual slide, the fundamental unit of representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261807/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes precomputed 224×224 patch feature grids (extracted by a frozen 21.67M-parameter ViT-S patch encoder) at 20× and 40× magnification, arranged as 2-D spatial grids with tissue validity masks; multiple WSIs from the same patient are grouped into a single case. The output is a unified case-level embedding produced by the case transformer, which is routed to task-specific linear or MLP heads for classification (weighted cross-entropy) or survival prediction (discrete-hazard NLL over adaptive time bins). At inference, task heads are discarded and the case embedding serves as the final representation regardless of slide count.

## Method

MOOZY uses a two-stage framework that decouples representation learning from semantic alignment. Stage 1 pretrains a 6-layer ViT slide encoder (d=768, 12 heads, 4 register tokens) on 77,134 public slide feature grids via DINOv2-style CLS self-distillation with an EMA teacher, combined with DINOv3-style contiguous block masking on global crops and 2-D ALiBi positional encoding for spatial structure. Stage 2 fine-tunes the encoder with multi-task supervision across 333 tasks (205 classification, 128 survival) from 56 public datasets, where a lightweight 3-layer case transformer with a learnable [CASE] token explicitly models inter-slide dependencies within each patient case rather than collapsing slides into a single bag or averaging independent predictions. The total model is 85.77M parameters (64.10M trainable slide+case encoder plus 21.67M frozen patch encoder), making it 4–14× smaller than competing slide encoders while achieving the strongest macro weighted F1 and balanced accuracy across sixteen held-out tasks.

## Results

Across sixteen held-out tasks spanning 7 datasets, MOOZY achieves the strongest macro weighted F1 (0.769) and balanced accuracy (0.702) among all slide encoders, while TITAN edges it on weighted ROC-AUC (0.768 vs. 0.763). In the MIL setting, MOOZY outperforms the best patch-encoder baseline (CONCH v1.5) by +0.029 weighted F1, +0.043 weighted ROC-AUC, and +0.041 balanced accuracy. MOOZY totals only 85.77M parameters, making it 14× smaller than GigaPath (1.22B) and 4× smaller than PRISM (742M), while improving over PRISM by +4.19% F1, +7.93% balanced accuracy, and +6.95% ROC-AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among slide encoders, MOOZY achieves the best macro weighted F1 (0.769) and balanced accuracy (0.702), while TITAN (355M params) narrowly leads on weighted ROC-AUC (0.768 vs. 0.763). MOOZY substantially outperforms PRISM (742M) by +4.19% F1, +7.93% balanced accuracy, and +6.95% AUC, and also surpasses GigaPath (1.22B), Madeleine (400M), and CHIEF on all three macro metrics. In the MIL comparison, MOOZY beats the strongest patch-encoder baseline CONCH v1.5 by +0.029 F1, +0.043 AUC, and +0.041 balanced accuracy, and also outperforms UNI v2, Phikon v2, and MUSK across all metrics. Notably, MOOZY achieves these gains with only 85.77M parameters—4–14× smaller than the competing slide encoders—by concentrating capacity at the slide level and reusing a lightweight 21.67M-parameter public patch encoder (Phikon). The case aggregator contributes +0.020 F1, +0.026 AUC, and +0.020 balanced accuracy over mean slide pooling, confirming that explicit inter-slide modeling provides measurable benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOOZY builds directly on the DINOv2 self-distillation recipe (EMA teacher, CLS-level distillation, weight-normalized prototypes) and adapts it to precomputed patch-feature grids rather than raw pixels, following the slide-encoder line of work (TITAN, PRISM, GigaPath). Its key departure is architectural and conceptual: rather than concentrating capacity in a heavy tile encoder and using a lightweight or absent slide aggregator, MOOZY uses a frozen 21.67M-parameter patch encoder and places a 6-layer transformer at the slide level plus a 3-layer case transformer that explicitly models inter-slide dependencies within a patient. This contrasts with prior multi-slide handling, which relies on naive early fusion (concatenating patch bags) or late fusion (averaging slide embeddings), and with the field's trend toward scaling tile encoders to ViT-Giant backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The patient-first framing addresses a genuine clinical gap: pathologists interpret multiple slides from the same patient jointly, yet prior foundation models treat slides as independent units. The 14× parameter reduction relative to GigaPath while remaining competitive suggests the field's scaling axis may be misallocated, pointing toward slide/case-level modeling as the more productive direction. Limitations include the restriction to global case-level predictions (no dense or segmentation outputs), the modest absolute gains over the strongest baselines, and the unquantified benefit of cross-slide aggregation on tasks specifically designed to require multi-slide reasoning. Open extensions the authors identify include case-level retrieval, slide-report co-training, and genomic fusion.
