## Problem

Deploying 3D Gaussian Splatting on mobile devices is hindered by two bottlenecks: third-order Spherical Harmonics require 48 float parameters per Gaussian (prohibitive storage and memory bandwidth at millions of primitives), and single-view gradient-based densification produces excessive, overfitting Gaussians. Existing lightweight variants either rely on costly teacher-student distillation to compress SH or introduce neural networks into the rendering loop, sacrificing the real-time throughput that mobile hardware demands.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and produces a compressed 3D Gaussian scene representation using only first-order SH (4 coefficients per channel vs. 16 for third-order), reducing per-Gaussian memory by 61%. The final representation is rendered via a WebGL-based pipeline that offloads depth-sorting to an asynchronous WebWorker, enabling cross-platform browser rendering that breaks the 120 FPS display limit. Training runs for 30k iterations without any pre-training or distillation stage, and the SH compression step is executed exactly once after the first 3k iterations.

## Method

Flux-GS first trains full third-order SH for 3k iterations, then applies a Monte Carlo Specular Energy Aggregator: it uniformly samples K directions on the sphere, computes the positive residual between third-order and second-order SH radiance, and aggregates these into compact energy-magnitude and energy-direction descriptors per Gaussian. A lightweight MLP maps these descriptors (conditioned on normalized Gaussian position) to first-order SH coefficients, preserving specular directionality without distillation. An Attribute-Conditioned SH Enhancement module (a second MLP with zero-initialized output) predicts view-independent residual offsets from intrinsic Gaussian attributes (position, scale, rotation, opacity, first-order SH), which are statically baked into the parameters before inference, adding zero rendering cost. For compactness, a Multi-view Alpha-based Densification and Pruning strategy uses stratified camera sampling to compute alpha-weighted photometric error scores across multiple views; densification requires both a positional gradient threshold and a multi-view importance quantile, while pruning requires low opacity plus a high multi-view redundancy score, jointly controlling the Gaussian budget.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset evaluated on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 mobile GPU, Flux-GS achieves 30.22 PSNR (indoor) and 24.45 PSNR (outdoor) while using only 0.22M and 0.48M Gaussians respectively, with 2.1 MB and 4.6 MB storage. It delivers 147 FPS (indoor) and 132 FPS (outdoor) on mobile, the highest among all compared methods, and trains in just 11 minutes—roughly 8× faster than Mobile-GS (86 min) and 2.5× faster than vanilla 3DGS (27 min). On Tanks & Temples and Deep Blending, Flux-GS similarly achieves the best FPS (137 and 158) with the lowest storage (2.4 MB and 1.9 MB) while maintaining competitive PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS, Flux-GS trades a small PSNR drop (30.22 vs. 30.41 indoor) for a 228× storage reduction (2.1 MB vs. 478 MB) and 6.6× fewer Gaussians, making mobile rendering feasible at 147 FPS where 3DGS cannot run. Compared to Mobile-GS, Flux-GS achieves slightly lower PSNR (30.22 vs. 30.37) but uses 42% fewer Gaussians (0.22M vs. 0.38M), 40% less storage (2.1 vs. 3.5 MB), higher FPS (147 vs. 131), and trains 7.8× faster (11 vs. 86 min). Against Mobile-GS without its inference MLP (Mobile-GS*), Flux-GS wins on every metric: higher PSNR (30.22 vs. 29.58), fewer Gaussians, less storage, higher FPS, and faster training. Versus Speedy-Splat, Flux-GS achieves higher PSNR (30.22 vs. 30.11), 7× fewer Gaussians, 30× less storage, and 7× higher FPS (147 vs. 21). Against C3DGS and LocoGS-S, Flux-GS leads in PSNR, storage, and FPS by wide margins (e.g., 147 vs. 18 FPS vs. C3DGS; 147 vs. 13 FPS vs. LocoGS-S), confirming that its multi-view densification and SH compression strategy yields the best quality–efficiency trade-off among all lightweight GS variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flux-GS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) but departs from the Mobile-GS line of work (Du et al., ICLR 2026) in a key conceptual way: rather than distilling third-order SH into first-order via a teacher-student pipeline, it uses Monte Carlo directional moment extraction to aggregate specular energy into a compact latent, then maps it back to first-order SH with a lightweight MLP. It also diverges from prior compression methods (C3DGS, LocoGS, HAC) that rely on per-view neural field decoding at inference time, instead baking all view-dependent corrections into explicit Gaussian parameters for zero inference overhead. The multi-view alpha-based densification strategy extends the single-view gradient-based approach inherited from 3DGS and Mobile-GS by introducing stratified camera sampling and alpha-weighted error accumulation for multi-view consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practically deployable pipeline for real-time 3DGS on commodity mobile hardware (147 FPS, 2.1 MB storage, 11 min training on Snapdragon 8 Gen 3), which is a significant step toward making high-fidelity 3D scene rendering accessible outside desktop workstations. The zero-inference-overhead design for SH enhancement and the WebGL-based cross-platform renderer lower the barrier for consumer-facing 3D applications. Open questions include whether the Monte Carlo aggregation generalizes to scenes with extremely complex multi-specular lighting, whether the first-order SH ceiling limits quality on highly reflective materials, and how the method scales to unbounded or dynamic scenes beyond the bounded Mip-NeRF 360 / Tanks & Temples / Deep Blending benchmarks.
