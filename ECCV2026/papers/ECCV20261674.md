## Problem

Existing jailbreak defenses for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) follow a detect-then-detoxify paradigm that inherently trades safety for utility: strengthening refusal mechanisms suppresses benign visual-grounded queries and degrades general reasoning performance. This paper argues the tradeoff is not inherent but stems from a shared structural cause—modality-induced bias arising from imperfect coupling between the vision encoder and LLM backbone—that simultaneously undermines both safety alignment and visual grounding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text query paired with an RGB image as input to an LVLM (e.g., LLaVA-7B/13B, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5, InstructBLIP). At inference time, it intercepts the activation at the final decoder layer at the last input token position—a single compact feature vector—and applies an orthogonal projection before the model proceeds to generate its text response. An offline anchor set (20% of MM-SafetyBench mixed with 10% of MME) is used once to compute the SVD basis; no per-query computation beyond the single forward pass is required.

## Method

TBOP (Two Birds, One Projection) identifies a low-dimensional nuisance subspace spanned by modality-induced shift vectors, computed as the difference between cross-modal features with a blank dummy image and text-only features. SVD on the stacked shift matrix yields top-k right singular vectors (default k=32) forming an approximate orthonormal basis for this subspace. At inference, the cross-modal feature is projected onto the orthogonal complement via (I − V_k V_kᵀ)h, removing the bias-aligned component while preserving the ideal multimodal representation under an orthogonality assumption. Crucially, this projection is invariant to the bias intensity α, unlike steering-based methods (e.g., CMRM) that must estimate a per-sample coefficient, making TBOP robust across datasets and attack distributions. The entire correction requires a single forward pass with no auxiliary models, no retraining, and no architectural modifications.

## Results

On LLaVA-7B, TBOP achieves the lowest Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 5.09% on MM-SafetyBench and 5.48% on HADES, while simultaneously attaining the highest utility scores of 43.98 on MM-Vet, 63.10 on ScienceQA, and 1870.17 on MME — all surpassing the vanilla model. On LLaVA-13B, TBOP further reduces ASR to 2.56% (MMSB) and 1.74% (HADES) while improving utility to 49.98 (MM-Vet), 65.20 (ScienceQA), and 1798.85 (MME). The method requires only a single forward pass and outperforms all five baselines on both safety and utility metrics simultaneously, breaking the conventional safety-utility tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TBOP outperforms all five baselines on both safety and utility simultaneously. Against MLLM-Protector (train-based), TBOP achieves comparable or better ASR (5.09 vs. 7.48 on MMSB-7B) while avoiding the catastrophic utility collapse that Protector suffers (MME drops from 1715 to 725). Against Immune, TBOP is substantially better on both dimensions: lower ASR (5.09 vs. 9.39 on MMSB-7B) and far higher utility (MM-Vet 43.98 vs. 21.18; MME 1870 vs. 1395). Against ECSO and ETA, TBOP achieves lower ASR on MMSB (5.09 vs. 8.48/8.34) and higher utility scores across all three benchmarks, whereas ECSO and ETA still trail the vanilla model on utility. Against CMRM (the closest methodological competitor using feature steering), TBOP reduces MMSB ASR from 25.02 to 5.09 and HADES ASR from 32.30 to 5.48, while also improving MM-Vet (43.98 vs. 41.11) and MME (1870 vs. 1815), demonstrating that coefficient-free orthogonal projection is more effective and generalizable than coefficient-dependent steering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CMRM (Liu et al., ACL Findings 2025), which introduced the decomposition of cross-modal representations into an ideal component plus a modality-induced shift, and proposed steering (subtracting α·Δh) to counteract the shift. TBOP departs from CMRM by replacing coefficient-dependent steering with a coefficient-free orthogonal projection onto the null space of the bias direction, eliminating the need to estimate the shift intensity α. More broadly, the method sits within the feature-manipulation lineage (ReFAT, TSV, InferAligner, VTI, Nullu) but is distinguished by its geometric, subspace-based formulation rather than additive vector steering, and by its explicit targeting of a shared degradation axis across safety and utility rather than a single objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a single geometric direction that simultaneously degrades both safety and utility challenges the field's working assumption that these objectives are inherently antagonistic, and the projection-based remedy is simple, training-free, and requires only a single forward pass—making it practically deployable. Generalization across four distinct LVLM architectures (LLaVA, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5, InstructBLIP) and the demonstrated synergy with existing defenses (MLLM-Protector, ECSO, Immune, ETA) strengthen its practical relevance. Open questions include whether the orthogonality assumption (Assumption 1) holds under distribution shift or adversarial perturbations beyond modality activation, whether the bias direction remains stable as LVLM architectures evolve (e.g., natively multimodal pretraining), and whether the fixed-rank approximation (k=32) suffices for more complex or multi-image inputs.
