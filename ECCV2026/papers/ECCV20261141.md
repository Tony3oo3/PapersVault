## Problem

Current head avatar methods model hair implicitly as part of the head representation, producing static or over-smoothed hair that ignores physically plausible dynamics such as gravity-driven settling, inertial lag, and follow-through. While physics-based simulators can generate realistic hair motion, they require hand-tuned parameters and strand-level reconstruction that is difficult to recover from occluded video. The paper addresses the gap of learning personalized, physically plausible hair dynamics directly from multi-view video without relying on a differentiable physics simulator.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video from 4–15 synchronized cameras (2048×2048 to 4K at 72 FPS) and a BFM parametric face model tracking the head pose. It produces a fully animatable head avatar where the hair is represented as ~11,000 explicit polyline strands (40 points each) with strand-aligned 3D Gaussians, and the upper body as unstructured 3D Gaussians conditioned on facial expression and pose. The hair deformation is predicted per-point relative to a canonical hairstyle, making the output directly manipulable (e.g., trimming, brightness editing) while preserving learned dynamics.

## Method

The canonical hairstyle is initialized from the Im2Haircut PCA-based strand prior and refined with geometric consistency losses. At each timestep, angular velocity, acceleration, and relative gravity are extracted from the BFM head pose, encoded through a sliding-window LSTM to produce a temporal motion embedding, which modulates per-point strand features via FiLM conditioning before a strand-wise MLP predicts non-rigid displacements scaled by a learnable root-attenuation factor. The hair is rendered as 3D Gaussians constrained to lie on strand polylines (mean at segment midpoint, primary axis aligned via parallel transport, fixed cross-section width), and is jointly optimized with the upper-body Gaussians through differentiable splatting. Training combines photometric losses (L1, SSIM, LPIPS), hair-geometric supervision (asymmetric silhouette, wrap-aware orientation, head-hair penetration, elastic stretch preservation), and spatially/temporally coherent color regularization, all in an end-to-end supervised framework.

## Results

On self-reenactment across 3 test subjects, DynHair achieves the best FID (30.06 full image, 37.51 hair region) and the closest-to-zero temporal excess LPIPS (tLPIPS_ex = 0.0045), indicating the most perceptually realistic and temporally coherent hair dynamics. It also attains the highest hair velocity (2.41 ×10⁻³) and best hair silhouette IoU (0.878), confirming more expressive and geometrically faithful strand motion. Pixel-aligned metrics (PSNR 21.60, SSIM 0.7638) are slightly below GHA (22.33, 0.7925), a trade-off the authors attribute to enforcing strand-level geometric consistency rather than arbitrary high-frequency smoothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GaussianAvatars (GA), DynHair wins on every reported metric except SSIM (0.7638 vs 0.7787), with large margins in FID (30.06 vs 45.73), hair-region LPIPS (0.1101 vs 0.1362), and hair velocity (2.41 vs 0.40 ×10⁻³), indicating far more expressive and realistic strand motion. Against Gaussian Head Avatar (GHA), DynHair achieves lower FID (30.06 vs 36.25), better tLPIPS_ex (0.0045 vs −0.0127, i.e. less over-smoothing), higher hair velocity (2.41 vs 2.17), and a substantially better hair IoU (0.878 vs not reported), while GHA retains a small edge in PSNR and SSIM due to its unconstrained Gaussian optimization. Against the Maya physics simulator, DynHair produces markedly better perceptual quality (FID 30.06 vs 62.64) and silhouette coverage (IoU 0.878 vs 0.776), but Maya achieves lower acceleration (0.11 vs 0.19), reflecting the simulator's inherent temporal smoothness at the cost of matching the true hair motion. Overall, DynHair trades a small amount of pixel-level alignment for superior temporal coherence, geometric fidelity, and perceptual realism in hair dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynHair builds on two key prior works: Im2Haircut [50] provides the PCA-based strand prior for initializing canonical hairstyle geometry, and Gaussian Head Avatar [62] supplies the unstructured 3D Gaussian representation for the upper body region. The paper departs from the dominant head-avatar paradigm (GHA, GaussianAvatars, FlashAvatar) that treats hair implicitly within the head representation, instead disentangling hair into explicit polyline strands with strand-aligned Gaussians. It also diverges from capture-based hair dynamics methods (HHAvatar, NeuWigs, HVH) that predict deformations from past hair states or use unstructured Gaussians, by predicting non-rigid displacements relative to a canonical hairstyle conditioned on head kinematics (angular velocity, acceleration, gravity) through an LSTM–FiLM–MLP pipeline, and from physics simulators (Maya) by learning dynamics end-to-end from video rather than requiring hand-tuned mass-spring parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to learn strand-based hair dynamics directly from multi-view video with physics-based supervision, producing animatable avatars whose hair exhibits inertia, gravity response, and settling behavior without manual parameter tuning. The new 15-camera 4K/72 FPS dataset with predefined diverse hair motions is a valuable resource for the community. Key limitations include the absence of explicit hair-body collision handling, dependence on pre-trained segmentation for hair silhouettes, and rendering artifacts at the hair-head boundary. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to unseen hairstyles without per-subject optimization, how it scales to full-body avatars with hair-body interactions, and whether the learned dynamics transfer across subjects.
