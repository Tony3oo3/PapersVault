## Problem

Spacetime Autoregressive (STAR) video generation suffers from a 'token explosion' at high resolutions, where the final 4 of the total resolution scales consume 81% of inference latency due to quadratic attention complexity O(T²H²W²). Existing token reduction methods designed for image synthesis or diffusion models are structurally mismatched with STAR's hierarchical, parallel next-scale refinement, leading to inaccurate token identification and error propagation across scales.

## Input / Output

FastSTAR is a training-free acceleration layer applied on top of the InfinityStar backbone, accepting text prompts (T2V), a reference image plus text prompt (I2V), or a source video plus text prompt (V2V) and producing 720p, 5-second (81-frame) videos. It operates exclusively on the discrete token maps within the spatiotemporal pyramid, pruning tokens at the final four resolution scales (scales 26–29) while leaving earlier structural scales untouched. The method requires no additional fine-tuning or architectural modification to the underlying model.

## Method

FastSTAR introduces Spatiotemporal Token Pruning (STTP), which computes two cosine-similarity metrics—spatial similarity between consecutive scale feature maps and temporal similarity between consecutive clip feature maps—and fuses them via an ℓp-norm dissimilarity score to produce a per-clip binary pruning mask. A key design choice is pruning-over-merging: because STAR accumulates discrete quantized tokens, averaging similar tokens distorts the latent distribution and triggers compounding errors, whereas pruning preserves spatial integrity and maintains tighter error bounds. The Partial Update (PU) mechanism then zero-pads pruned positions before adding the residual to the cumulative feature map, preventing uncomputed noise from polluting converged regions. Active tokens retain their original RoPE position indices to keep spatial alignment intact, and the pruning mask is downsampled via trilinear interpolation with a strict >0.5 threshold at each scale to ensure binary compatibility with the hierarchical VAR structure.

## Results

On 720p, 5s (81-frame) Text-to-Video generation with InfinityStar as backbone, FastSTAR achieves a 2.01× end-to-end speedup (40.6s vs. 81.7s baseline) on a single H100 GPU while maintaining a PSNR of 28.29, SSIM of 0.828, and LPIPS of 0.185. The VBench Total Score drops only 0.85% from the unaccelerated baseline (83.18 vs. 83.89). On Image-to-Video, FastSTAR similarly achieves 2.01× speedup with PSNR 25.65 and VBench Quality Score of 80.01 (vs. 80.97 baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastSTAR dominates all three baselines on both speed and quality simultaneously. In terms of speed, FastSTAR (2.01×) outperforms ToMe (1.76×), FastVAR (1.61×), and SparseVAR (1.44×). On reconstruction fidelity, FastSTAR achieves PSNR 28.29 vs. ToMe 26.47, SparseVAR 26.59, and FastVAR 25.00 — a 1.7–3.3 dB margin over all baselines. On VBench Total Score, FastSTAR (83.18) exceeds ToMe (81.60), SparseVAR (82.13), and FastVAR (81.19), and is within 0.85% of the unaccelerated InfinityStar baseline (83.89). The baselines suffer 1.75–2.70% VBench Total Score degradation, whereas FastSTAR's degradation is less than 1%, confirming that image-centric token merging and cached-token restoration strategies are structurally mismatched with STAR's hierarchical spatiotemporal refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastSTAR builds directly on the Spacetime Autoregressive (STAR) paradigm introduced by InfinityStar, which extends Visual Autoregressive (VAR) modeling from 2D image pyramids to 3D spatiotemporal video pyramids. It departs from existing token-reduction methods (SparseVAR, FastVAR, ToMe) that were designed for image synthesis or sequential AR decoding by adopting a pruning-over-merging strategy specifically suited to STAR's discrete, cumulative residual feature accumulation. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that token merging distorts quantized latent representations in a compounding manner across scales, whereas pruning with a Partial Update mechanism preserves structural integrity while eliminating redundant computation in converged regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 2.01× training-free speedup with less than 1% VBench degradation is practically significant for deploying STAR-based video synthesis, where the final refinement scales account for 81% of inference latency. The pruning-over-merging insight generalizes to any discrete-token autoregressive framework, offering a design principle beyond STAR. However, evaluation is limited to a single backbone (InfinityStar) at 480p/720p, and open questions remain about scalability to longer videos, higher resolutions, or other STAR variants. The content-adaptive pruning mechanism (up to 74.8% in simple videos) suggests further gains are possible but requires per-video complexity estimation.
