## Problem

Error-diffusion-based binary defocusing fringe projection achieves high-speed 3D measurement but introduces systematic phase errors whose origin and structure have not been theoretically characterized. Existing optimization methods tune diffusion kernel parameters without recognizing that the diffusion direction fundamentally determines the sign and magnitude of the induced phase error, leaving a consistent bias uncorrected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260870/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard 8-bit sinusoidal fringe patterns (four-step phase-shifting, 16-pixel period) and produces two sets of binary (0/255) fringe patterns for projection via a DLP LightCrafter 4500 (912×1140, 4225 Hz binary mode). A Basler acA640-750um camera (640×480, 751 fps) captures the defocused binary fringes, from which wrapped phase and ultimately a 3D point cloud are recovered. The entire binarization is a preprocessing step that adds zero runtime overhead to the reconstruction pipeline.

## Method

The paper first derives a closed-form phase error model Δφ = (β/α)·tan(2π/P), proving the error is independent of the number of phase steps, fringe period (for P > 24 px), and projector defocus amount, and is governed solely by the asymmetric forward/backward error propagation coefficients of the diffusion kernel. The key insight is that reversing the Floyd–Steinberg diffusion kernel (i.e., using its inverse) flips the sign of β, producing an equal-magnitude but opposite-sign phase error. The proposed Error Diffusion Fusion (EDF) strategy splits the four-step phase-shifting sequence into two groups of two patterns: the first group is binarized with standard FS dithering and the second with inverse FS dithering. When the conventional four-step phase retrieval formula φ = arctan((I₄−I₂)/(I₁−I₃)) is applied, the opposing error terms cancel algebraically, reducing the effective unknowns from five to four and eliminating the systematic bias without any post-processing correction or additional projections.

## Results

On non-smooth objects (Table 2), the proposed EDF method achieves the lowest AE and RMSE across all six test objects, reducing AE by 13.85% and RMSE by 23.77% relative to the second-best method (FOC). On smooth objects (Table 1), EDF reduces AE by 72.22% and RMSE by 58.30% compared to the standard Floyd–Steinberg baseline. EDF introduces zero additional computational overhead (0 s in Table 3), matching the runtime of other pre-correction methods like FS, OK, and HTF, while post-correction methods FOC and EF add 0.007 s and 0.085 s respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDF outperforms all five baselines on both smooth and non-smooth objects. Against the standard Floyd–Steinberg (FS) dithering [30], EDF reduces AE by 72.22% and RMSE by 58.30% on smooth objects, eliminating the directional offset errors inherent in unidirectional error diffusion. The Optimized Kernel (OK) [26] and Fixed Offset Compensation (FOC) [41] methods achieve moderate improvements over FS but remain 13–24% worse than EDF in RMSE on non-smooth objects; FOC's fixed 0.19-pixel phase compensation over-corrects for short-period fringes. The Half-Truncated Fringe (HTF) [40] method achieves competitive AE but suffers from large local distortions near object boundaries, yielding RMSE values up to 1.825 mm (Caster) versus EDF's 0.220 mm. The Error Fitting (EF) [12] method, evaluated only on smooth objects, is outperformed by EDF in both AE and RMSE and additionally incurs 0.085 s of post-processing overhead, whereas EDF operates entirely at the pattern-generation stage with zero runtime cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binary-defocusing fringe projection profilometry line of work, which has evolved from basic dithering (FS, Sierra Lite) through kernel optimization (Shan et al., OK) and post-hoc phase compensation (You et al., FOC/HTF; Hu et al., EF). The key departure is a theoretical reframing: rather than tuning kernel parameters or applying fixed offsets, the authors derive that the phase error introduced by error diffusion is invariant to the number of phase steps, fringe period, and defocus amount, and is governed solely by the diffusion direction. The proposed EDF method exploits this by binarizing alternating fringe groups with the FS kernel and its exact inverse, converting the directional error into a self-cancelling pair within a four-step sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The invariance result (phase error independent of N, period, and defocus) is a clean theoretical contribution that explains why prior parameter-tuning and fixed-offset approaches can only partially correct the error, and it provides a principled basis for the EDF strategy. Practically, the 23.77% RMSE reduction at zero additional runtime makes EDF attractive for real-time high-speed 3D measurement systems where post-processing budgets are tight. The main limitation is that the method is tailored to four-step phase shifting; the three-step extension is only sketched, and the approach does not address motion-induced errors beyond what binary projection speed already mitigates. Open questions include generalization to odd-step frameworks and whether the self-compensation principle extends to other dithering families beyond FS.
