## Problem

CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection suffers from a geometric dilemma: object-centric pre-training causes normal and anomalous text prototypes to exhibit high semantic overlap, yet enforcing strict orthogonality between them to improve discriminability disrupts the continuous metric structure of the pre-trained hyperspherical space, degrading generalization to unseen categories. The paper addresses this by learning a visually-calibrated semantic margin that preserves structural continuity while still enabling fine-grained anomaly discrimination.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 518×518 as input and produces two outputs: a binary image-level anomaly score (via cosine similarity of a pooled anomaly token against calibrated text prototypes) and a dense pixel-level anomaly map (via multi-scale patch-to-prototype alignment). Training follows a cross-dataset zero-shot protocol where the model is trained on one dataset (e.g., VisA) and evaluated on entirely unseen categories (MVTec-AD, BTAD, MPDD, and three medical datasets), requiring no category-specific normal or abnormal training samples at test time.

## Method

Proximity-CLIP is a two-stage parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework built on OpenCLIP ViT-L/14. In Stage 1, norm-preserving residual adapters are inserted into the frozen text encoder, and a bounded dynamic proximity regularization (a learnable margin λ mapped via sigmoid to [0.3, 0.95]) drives the cosine similarity between normal and anomalous text prototypes toward a value calibrated by actual visual variance, rather than forcing orthogonality; the text branch is optimized jointly with a segmentation alignment loss (Focal + Dice) against ground-truth masks. In Stage 2, the frozen calibrated anomalous prototype serves as a semantic query in a cross-attention Anomaly Query Module (AQM) deployed across multiple visual transformer layers, where adapted visual patch features act as keys and values; the resulting anomaly-aware tokens are cross-layer mean-pooled into a global representation, and the visual adapters are trained with a joint objective combining pixel-level segmentation loss and image-level cross-entropy classification loss.

## Results

Proximity-CLIP achieves an average Pixel AUROC of 95.7% across seven industrial and medical datasets, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art AA-CLIP (95.5%) and AnomalyCLIP (94.1%). On image-level classification, it attains an average Image AUROC of 81.0%, surpassing AA-CLIP (80.6%) and AdaCLIP (80.6%). The method shows particular strength on subtle medical defects, reaching 96.2% Pixel AUROC on Brain MRI and 95.9% on Retina OCT, while maintaining competitive industrial performance (92.0% on MVTec-AD, 95.7% on VisA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor AA-CLIP (the previous SOTA), Proximity-CLIP wins on 5 of 7 datasets in Pixel AUROC (MVTec-AD +0.2, VisA +1.0, Brain MRI +0.9, Retina OCT +0.4) and ties on BTAD (97.0), while losing narrowly on MPDD (−0.3) and Liver CT (−0.8). On Image AUROC, Proximity-CLIP edges out AA-CLIP by 0.4% on average (81.0 vs. 80.6), winning on MVTec-AD (+2.2), VisA (+4.6), and Retina OCT (+1.6), but trailing on BTAD (−2.5), MPDD (+0.3), Brain MRI (−0.4), and Liver CT (−2.8). Compared to prompt-based methods (AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP), Proximity-CLIP gains 1.6–2.9% in average Pixel AUROC and 0.4–2.1% in Image AUROC. Against earlier multi-level aggregation methods (VAND, MVFA-AD), the advantage is more substantial: +3.4% average Pixel AUROC and +3.9–4.6% Image AUROC. The raw CLIP baseline is far behind (51.0% Pixel, 68.3% Image), confirming that both the semantic calibration and active querying stages are essential for competitive zero-shot anomaly detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection lineage (WinCLIP, VAND, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP) by departing from the adapter-based orthogonality strategy popularized by AA-CLIP. While prior adapter methods enforce strict orthogonality between normal and anomalous text prototypes to maximize classification margins, Proximity-CLIP argues this creates a geometric conflict with the inherently proximate visual representations of normal and defective instances. The key conceptual departure is replacing rigid angular constraints with a visually-calibrated, dynamically learned semantic margin, paired with an Anomaly Query Module that shifts from passive global matching to active cross-attention-based defect retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine tension in CLIP-based ZSAD—maximizing text-prototype separation improves discriminability but corrupts the pre-trained hyperspherical metric structure, limiting open-world generalization. The visually-calibrated margin offers a principled middle ground, though its practical impact is modest (marginal gains over AA-CLIP on most datasets). Open questions include whether the learned margin transfers robustly across vastly different anomaly taxonomies, whether the approach scales beyond ViT-L/14, and whether the cross-dataset training protocol (VisA→others) masks domain-specific limitations. The AQM's active retrieval paradigm could inform broader dense-prediction tasks beyond anomaly detection.
