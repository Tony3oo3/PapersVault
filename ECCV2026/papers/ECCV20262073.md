## Problem

Automating injection mold design for complex parts with undercuts and side holes remains a significant bottleneck in manufacturing workflows, as it requires expert knowledge to determine demolding orientations, design auxiliary components, and construct parting surfaces. Prior learning-based approaches target isolated subproblems (e.g., orientation or undercut detection) rather than holistic assembly generation, and the absence of any public dataset pairing single-body CAD parts with professionally designed mold assemblies has made it nearly impossible to frame this as a viable learning problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-body CAD model (watertight mesh) as input, voxelized into a binary occupancy grid V(P) ∈ {0,1}^{N×N×N} in a canonical coordinate frame. The output is a structured assembly A = {M_u, M_l, S_part, C} where the number of auxiliary components K is not fixed but implicitly determined by the part's geometric features. Final outputs are exported as NURBS surface patches in STEP/STP format compatible with standard CAD software, with the upper and lower molds generated as two non-overlapping yet fully occupied volumetric partitions of the space.

## Method

AIMold decomposes mold assembly generation into three stages: (1) orientation estimation via plane detection to discretize the continuous pose space into candidates, followed by a binary classifier on voxel-encoded features; (2) a two-stage component generation pipeline where a voxel encoder-decoder (3DVAE transformer) first predicts coarse occupancy voxels—binary for auxiliary components, ternary (upper/lower/empty) for molds—and a Rectified Flow model then refines these into detailed meshes by generating structure latents decoded via FlexiCubes; (3) a geometry-processing post-processing stage that regularizes raw meshes through Poisson reconstruction, RANSAC primitive fitting, boolean composition, ShapeUp optimization, and quad-remeshing before NURBS conversion. The key architectural choice is a voxel-based representation over implicit VecSet methods, which the authors show aligns better with the limited dataset scale (~5K samples), and the two-stage design separates global layout prediction from fine geometric detail recovery.

## Results

On the MoldCAD dataset, AIMold achieves 57.29% COV, 0.0147 MMD, and 0.050 JSD for auxiliary component generation, and 49.01% COV, 0.0091 MMD, and 0.061 JSD for upper/lower mold generation, substantially outperforming the VecSet-based implicit baseline (21.24% / 9.21% COV respectively). For demolding orientation estimation, the plane-detection-based binary classifier reaches 91.17% accuracy and 0.9129 F1-score, compared to only 12.66% accuracy and 0.2267 F1 for a naive regression approach. The two-stage pipeline (voxel generation + geometry flow refinement) contributes a consistent 1.6–2.5% COV gain over the first stage alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the VecSet-based implicit representation architecture (as used in Cube [68] and Hunyuan3D 2.0 [88]), which has demonstrated strong performance on large-scale 3D datasets. AIMold wins decisively on both generation tasks: auxiliary component COV improves from 21.24% to 57.29% (a 2.7× gain), and upper/lower mold COV improves from 9.21% to 49.01% (a 5.3× gain), with corresponding reductions in MMD and JSD. The paper attributes the VecSet-based methods' poor performance to the limited dataset scale (~5k samples), which is insufficient for proper latent-space alignment that these architectures require. For orientation estimation, the internal comparison against a direct regression baseline shows a dramatic gap (91.17% vs. 12.66% accuracy), confirming that discretizing the continuous pose space via plane detection is essential. No published end-to-end mold assembly generation method exists for direct comparison, making the VecSet-based shape generation models the closest available reference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the structured 3D latent framework of Xiang et al. [73] (CVPR 2025), adapting its 3D VAE encoder, Rectified Flow latent generation, and FlexiCubes mesh decoder to the mold design domain. It departs from prior mold design literature, which has addressed isolated subproblems (demolding direction search, undercut detection, parting surface construction) using geometric heuristics, by formulating the full assembly generation as a single conditional generation task. The authors also deliberately choose voxel-based occupancy prediction over the VecSet/implicit-representation approaches that dominate large-scale 3D generation, arguing that at the constrained scale of MoldCAD (~5K samples), voxel paradigms align more closely with the input geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MoldCAD dataset is the first public resource pairing single-body CAD parts with professionally designed mold assemblies, filling a gap that made learning-based mold design essentially intractable. The pipeline demonstrates that a data-driven approach can produce manufacturing-ready STEP files for complex parts with undercuts and side holes, a task previously reliant entirely on expert engineers. Key limitations remain: the dataset scale (~5K samples) is far below general 3D benchmarks, thin structures and watertightness are not reliably handled, and the moderate COV scores (~50–57%) suggest the generated geometry still requires significant human refinement before production use. Scaling the dataset and guaranteeing manufacturability constraints during generation are the most pressing open questions.
