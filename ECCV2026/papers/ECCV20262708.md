## Problem

Existing robotic assembly planners face a fundamental dependency dilemma: classical geometric planners (e.g., ASAP) require precise CAD models as primary inputs, while recent VLM-based systems (e.g., Manual2Skill) depend on human-authored assembly manuals. Neither approach supports truly autonomous, zero-shot assembly in unstructured environments where neither clean 3D priors nor pre-written instructions are available.

## Input / Output

The system takes three calibrated orthogonal RGB-D views (front, side, top) of a preparation region containing K spatially separated unassembled parts, plus a language-level assembly goal. After automatic instance segmentation and part-wise cropping, the VLM-Parser consumes part-centric RGB crops (depth maps are retained only for downstream grasp-pose estimation, not VLM planning) and produces Structured Part Dossiers in JSON format encoding functional roles, axis-aligned bounding volumes, and six-directional interface features. The VLM-Planner then outputs a sequence of K−1 transitions, each specifying which mobile part to integrate into the growing subassembly, validated by a MuJoCo-based digital-twin collision and joint-alignment checker.

## Method

ZAP reformulates assembly as a perception-to-logic mapping with two frozen-VLM modules and no task-specific training. The VLM-Parser applies an orthogonal-projection prior and sparse structural encoding to map 2D part crops onto six axial directions, producing Structured Part Dossiers that combine VLM-inferred semantic affordances, deterministic geometric primitives from cross-view consistency, and topological interface manifolds—effectively synthesizing an 'implicit manual' from raw perception. The VLM-Planner performs heuristic state-space search via an Iterative Decision Aggregator (IDA): at each step, Chain-of-Thought reasoning scores candidate parts on structural stability, functional role, and assembly precedence, then a digital-twin feasibility verifier (G_contact) performs discrete collision and joint-alignment checks using object meshes only as simulation environment (never as VLM input). A systematic backtracking mechanism reverts to the last stable subassembly when no remaining part passes verification, enabling recovery from perceptual ambiguities. The entire pipeline operates with a frozen VLM backbone (Gemini/GPT, temperature 0.1) and a fixed prompt template, ensuring zero-shot evaluation without any fine-tuning on the ASAP-G benchmark.

## Results

On the ASAP-G benchmark (150 assemblies), ZAP achieves a mean Planning Success Rate (PSR) of 88.67%, outperforming the ASAP geometric planner (86.00%) and Random Permutation baseline (84.67%). The advantage is most pronounced on Hard-tier assemblies (11–30 parts), where ZAP reaches 86.67% PSR versus 76.67% for both baselines—a 10.00-point margin. In the Toy category, ZAP attains 95.24% PSR, a 19.05-point improvement over ASAP. Sim-to-real execution on a UR5e arm yields 100%, 90%, and 80% success rates on a desk lamp, stool, and toy car respectively over 10 trials each.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZAP is compared against two baselines: the ASAP geometric planner (heuristic and learning variants, which rely on full CAD mesh priors) and a Random Permutation baseline with the same digital-twin verification. ZAP ties ASAP on Simple-tier assemblies (90.00% each) but pulls ahead on Medium (+2.00 pts) and Hard (+10.00 pts), where combinatorial explosion degrades the geometry-only solver. Against Random Permutation, ZAP gains +4.00 pts on Medium and +10.00 pts on Hard, confirming that VLM-guided semantic pruning narrows the search space beyond what random ordering plus verification can achieve. By category, ZAP dominates Factory (+9.68 over ASAP) and Toy (+19.05 over ASAP), but trails ASAP on Furniture by 2.85 pts, suggesting the geometric prior still helps for standardized furniture joints. The authors note that ASAP enjoys an in-distribution advantage since ASAP-G is a subset of the original ASAP corpus, making ZAP's zero-shot performance under these conditions particularly notable.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZAP carves out a middle ground between two established but limited paradigms: geometry-dependent classical planners (ASAP, decomposition-based solvers) that require precise CAD models as primary inputs, and VLM-based manual-following systems (Manual2Skill) that depend on pre-authored assembly instructions. Rather than building directly on either, the paper repurposes VLM semantic reasoning (in the spirit of Manual2Skill) and digital-twin physics verification (in the spirit of ASAP) into a unified zero-shot pipeline where the VLM infers an 'implicit manual' from raw multi-view perception and a deterministic verifier gates each proposed transition. The Chain-of-Thought planning loop with backtracking (IDA) draws on LLM reasoning literature, while the structured part-dossier representation is a novel intermediate schema that bridges visual perception and combinatorial assembly search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen VLM, given only segmented multi-view RGB-D crops and no task-specific training, can outperform geometry-based planners that enjoy full CAD access—suggesting that semantic-physical common sense encoded in foundation models can substitute for explicit geometric priors in assembly planning. However, the physical validation is a pilot study on only three objects with ten trials each, and the authors themselves note that the benchmark lacks high-fidelity mechanical constraints (friction, precise fastening), limiting confidence in real-world transfer. Open questions include whether the approach scales to tighter-tolerance industrial assembly, whether VLMs can reason about fine-grained contact geometries beyond axial interfaces, and whether the 12.5% perceptual hallucination rate on symmetric parts can be reduced without task-specific fine-tuning.
