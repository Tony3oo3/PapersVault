## Problem

3D Vision Foundation Models with view synthesis heads (3D-VS-VFMs) like DA3 and WorldMirror can jointly estimate geometry and render novel views, but they suffer from two fundamental training-time pathologies: a pose-geometry discrepancy where target poses are estimated from both context and target views while scene geometry is conditioned only on context views (causing coordinate misalignment and corrupted gradients), and local multi-view inconsistencies that produce floaters and spatial artifacts. These issues prevent 3D-VS-VFMs from achieving high-fidelity pose-free novel view synthesis comparable to pose-required pipelines.

## Input / Output

The model consumes V uncalibrated RGB context views (252×252 on RE10K, 252×448 on DL3DV) and outputs N pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian primitives (each with 3D mean, covariance, opacity, and color) along with estimated camera intrinsics and extrinsics for all views. During training, 24 context views are randomly sampled to build scene geometry while 8 spatially distinct target views provide rendering supervision. At inference, the feed-forward model produces novel view renderings in a single forward pass without any per-scene optimization or ground-truth poses.

## Method

AirSplat fine-tunes only the Gaussian prediction head of DA3-GIANT (freezing the encoder and depth/pose heads) using two complementary training modules. Self-Consistent Pose Alignment (SCPA) renders initial target views from predicted Gaussians, feeds the rendered images back through the frozen model to obtain a second pose estimate, computes the SE(3) log-transform between the two predictions, and back-extrapolates along the manifold to produce an aligned pose that cancels the systematic drift; a minimum-error supervision then selects the better of aligned vs. initial renderings for photometric loss. Rating-based Opacity Matching (ROM) uses a lightweight sparse-view NVS teacher to compute per-primitive geometric ratings by measuring scale-normalized multi-view projection error, then applies a one-sided margin loss that penalizes the student's predicted opacity only when it exceeds the teacher's rating, driving inconsistent floaters to zero opacity while leaving valid primitives free to optimize under the photometric loss. A clamped spatial regularization term (weighted by detached opacity) complements ROM by pulling mildly deviant coordinates toward local consensus before opacity pruning takes over.

## Results

On RE10K (12 views), AirSplat achieves 23.08 PSNR / 0.799 SSIM / 0.190 LPIPS, outperforming the best pose-free baseline YoNoSplat (21.62 PSNR) by +1.46 dB and the best pose-required method DepthSplat (22.56 PSNR) by +0.52 dB. On DL3DV (12 views), AirSplat reaches 22.50 PSNR, surpassing DepthSplat (22.14) and the DA3 baseline (20.74) by +1.76 dB. On the zero-shot ACID cross-dataset benchmark (16 views), AirSplat attains 25.96 PSNR / 0.796 SSIM / 0.188 LPIPS, leading SPFSplat (24.58 PSNR) by +1.38 dB and all other pose-free methods by >3 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AirSplat wins against all nine baselines across every dataset and view-count setting. On RE10K (12 views), it surpasses the strongest pose-free competitor YoNoSplat by +1.46 dB PSNR and the strongest pose-required method DepthSplat by +0.52 dB, while also improving LPIPS from 0.229 (YoNoSplat) to 0.190. On DL3DV (12 views), AirSplat (22.50 PSNR) edges out DepthSplat (22.14) and leads the DA3 baseline by +1.76 dB, confirming gains on more complex outdoor scenes. On the zero-shot ACID benchmark (16 views), the margin widens dramatically: AirSplat (25.96 PSNR) beats SPFSplat (24.58) by +1.38 dB and YoNoSplat (22.49) by +3.47 dB, indicating strong generalization beyond the training distribution. The DA3 baseline, which AirSplat fine-tunes, improves from 20.78 to 23.08 PSNR on RE10K-12V, a +2.30 dB gain attributable to the SCPA and ROM training modules. Notably, MVSplat runs out of memory at 36 views on RE10K, while AirSplat scales gracefully to all tested view counts without degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

AirSplat positions itself as a training framework for 3D-VS-VFMs (specifically DA3), departing from prior pose-free NVS methods (SPFSplat, AnySplat, YoNoSplat) that either restrict supervision to context views or suffer from pose-geometry misalignment in context-target training. The SCPA module introduces a novel SE(3)-based pose correction loop that has no direct analogue in prior feed-forward NVS work, while ROM repurposes the rating-based reinforcement learning paradigm (learning from scalar feedback) into a 3D geometry consistency filter using a sparse-view NVS model as a teacher oracle. Conceptually, the paper treats the 3D-VS-VFM as a frozen geometric prior and addresses NVS quality purely through training-time alignment and pruning strategies, rather than architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The large, consistent margins over both pose-free and pose-required baselines across RE10K, DL3DV, and ACID suggest that the identified pose-geometry discrepancy is a genuine bottleneck in 3D-VS-VFM fine-tuning, and that the SCPA+ROM recipe is a broadly applicable training strategy for any 3D-VS-VFM with a Gaussian head. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to static scenes, scales poorly to thousands of input views due to pixel-aligned primitives and self-attention cost, and cannot hallucinate occluded geometry. Open questions include whether the rating-based feedback paradigm generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether test-time training or causal attention can extend the view-count ceiling, and whether generative inpainting priors can fill the deterministic blind spots.
