## Problem

Traditional UV-mapping for texture representation suffers from persistent artifacts (seams, distortions, wasted space, vertex duplication) and incurs fixed per-vertex storage overhead. While 3D Gaussian Splatting has revolutionised novel view synthesis, its Euclidean primitives cannot intrinsically conform to surface manifolds, and existing surface-aligned variants still rely on UV parameterisations or ambient-space regularisation. The paper asks whether a truly intrinsic, UV-free texture representation can be built from geodesic primitives that natively respect the underlying mesh geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a manifold triangular mesh (≤60k vertices, single connected component, eigendecomposable LBO) as the geometric substrate, paired with either a ground-truth UV albedo texture or a set of multi-view rendered images with known camera poses. It produces a set of S optimisable heat kernels—each carrying a surface position (face + barycentric coords), anisotropy angle and strength, a scale threshold, a sharpness parameter, and an RGB colour—plus a global base colour. Kernels are not splatted into a raster; they are evaluated continuously at arbitrary ray–mesh intersection points during differentiable PBR ray tracing, yielding a physically based render as the final output.

## Method

HKTex replaces Euclidean Gaussians with anisotropic heat kernels defined via the spectral decomposition of the Anisotropic Laplace-Beltrami Operator (ALBO), evaluated continuously on the surface through barycentric interpolation of precomputed eigenfunctions. To avoid per-kernel eigendecomposition, a half-polar grid of (angle, log-anisotropy) pairs is precomputed and queried via bilinear interpolation, with an alignment procedure (Hungarian matching + Procrustes + sign correction) resolving eigenvector ambiguities across grid nodes. A biharmonic-distance Gaussian weighting suppresses spectral ringing from the finite eigen-truncation and enforces local kernel support, while a rescaled soft-step filter gives explicit control over kernel scale and border sharpness. Kernel positions are optimised with Riemannian gradient descent: gradients are projected to the tangent plane and integrated via the exponential map (straightest geodesics), with momentum parallel-transported between steps. Density is managed by manifold-aware pruning (hit-count and energy thresholds) and densification (cloning small kernels, splitting large kernels along their principal axis via the exponential map). The entire pipeline is integrated into Mitsuba3/Dr.Jit for differentiable PBR ray tracing, where colour at each hit point is computed as an alpha-weighted partition-of-unity over the top-K contributing kernels plus a learnable base colour.

## Results

On 313 Objaverse meshes for UV-texture fitting, HKTex (S≈4.8k kernels) achieves the best SSIM (98.9), MS-SSIM (99.5), and MSE (0.08×10⁻³) among all methods except HR GT VTex, while using only 96.6 KB of storage—roughly 5–10× less than neural baselines (473–928 KB). In the multi-view inverse rendering setting (162 meshes), HKTex (S≈3.8k) leads all baselines on every quality metric (PSNR 37.61, LPIPS 2.1×10⁻², SSIM 98.3, MSE 0.30×10⁻³) at 78.7 KB storage, compared to 516–603 KB for neural methods. The main trade-off is rendering speed: HKTex takes 784.7 ms per frame in the UV-fitting setting versus 25–91 ms for baselines, though this gap narrows in the multi-view setting where ray tracing dominates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three neural field baselines (MLP with Fourier encoding, InstantNGP hash-grid, and Intrinsic Neural Fields), HKTex wins on SSIM, MS-SSIM, MSE, and LPIPS while using 5–10× less storage (96.6 KB vs. 473–928 KB); the neural methods achieve comparable PSNR but at substantially higher memory cost and with more variance. Against ImageGS (2D Gaussian Splatting in UV space), HKTex dominates on every quality metric (e.g., SSIM 98.9 vs. 97.3, MSE 0.08 vs. 3.65×10⁻³) at similar storage (~97 vs. ~115 KB), and ImageGS suffers from severe artifacts under tight storage budgets due to UV-coordinate overhead. Compared to vertex-colour baselines, HKTex matches HR GT VTex (N≈210k) on MSE and SSIM while using only 96.6 KB versus 179.6 KB, and substantially outperforms the native-resolution GT VTex (N≈7.7k) on all quality metrics. In the multi-view inverse rendering setting, HKTex beats both the MLP and NVDiffRec* baselines on all five quality metrics (e.g., LPIPS 2.1 vs. 3.4–3.7×10⁻², MSE 0.30 vs. 0.79–1.55×10⁻³) at roughly 1/6 the storage, and also surpasses HR VTex on every metric. The consistent weakness is rendering throughput: HKTex's 784.7 ms per frame in the UV-fitting setting is 8–30× slower than baselines, though the gap narrows to ~2× in the multi-view setting where ray tracing dominates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions the 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm from ambient Euclidean space to the intrinsic geometry of a mesh, replacing Euclidean Gaussians with anisotropic heat kernels whose positions, shapes, and density controls all operate via Riemannian geodesic operations. It draws its mathematical foundation from spectral geometry (Laplace-Beltrami eigendecomposition, heat diffusion) and its optimisation machinery from the differentiable straightest-geodesics framework of Verninas et al. [38], while its adaptive densification/pruning logic is a direct manifold-aware reworking of the 3DGS strategy [18]. Conceptually, it departs from both neural texture fields (INFs, MLP-based encodings) and prior GS-on-surface attempts (UVGS, Texture-GS, SUGAR, MeshGS), all of which remain anchored in UV parameterisations or ambient 3D coordinates, by making the texture primitive itself a continuous function on the manifold with no external parametrisation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HKTex eliminates the entire class of UV-mapping artifacts (seams, distortion, wasted space, vertex duplication) by construction and achieves competitive reconstruction quality at roughly half the storage of low-resolution UV textures, which is practically relevant for games, AR/VR, and VFX pipelines where texture memory is a first-order constraint. The authors note that the representation is currently limited to albedo on single-connected manifold meshes, and the rendering cost (~785 ms per frame in the UV-fitting setting) is substantially higher than rasterised UV lookups, limiting near-term real-time adoption. The paper frames HKTex as a foundational step toward manifold vector graphics (diffusion curves on surfaces), and extending kernels to full spatially-varying BSDF parameters is identified as the natural next step.
