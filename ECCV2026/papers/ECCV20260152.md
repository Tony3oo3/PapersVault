## Problem

Real-world MRI datasets often contain incomplete modality sets due to acquisition time and protocol constraints, yet existing medical image translation methods are limited to fixed source-target pairs or require retraining per combination. Unified approaches that handle arbitrary missing modalities (e.g., MM-GAN, CCR, MMHVAE) rely on complex architectures, explicit contrast-content disentanglement, or hierarchical latent structures that introduce optimization difficulty and risk losing clinically critical correlations between modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260152/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-channel 3D MRI volume (e.g., 4 channels for BraTS: T1w, T2w, T1ce, FLAIR; 3 channels for IXI: T1w, T2w, PD) where missing modality channels are zeroed out, along with a binary channel mask indicating which modalities are observed. It produces the synthesized missing modality as a single-channel 3D volume at the same spatial resolution (1 mm isotropic for BraTS, 0.9375×0.9375×1.2 mm³ for IXI). A many-to-one sampling strategy generates one target modality at a time, so for multiple missing modalities the procedure is repeated per target, each time conditioning only on the real observed channels.

## Method

The paper formulates missing-modality generation as a linear inverse problem under a joint distribution and solves it via posterior sampling with a conditional flow matching model. A UNet-based flow matching network is trained to learn the joint prior over all modality channels simultaneously, using stochastic masking (up to two channels randomly zeroed) and a partial loss computed only on observed channels. At inference, the sampling trajectory is guided by two interventions: (1) the vector field in observed channels is replaced with the measurement-induced velocity (y − x₀), anchoring those channels to ground truth, and (2) a PSLD-style hybrid gradient-and-projection guidance term minimizes ‖y − A(x̂₁)‖² to enforce measurement consistency. A many-to-one sampling strategy (zeroing all non-observed, non-target channels via a scenario mask) prevents inter-modality error propagation that occurs when multiple targets are synthesized jointly. Finally, expectation-approximation sampling averages N_Ex=10 generated samples to suppress stochastic noise, analogous to signal averaging in physical MRI acquisition.

## Results

On BraTS (4 modalities: T1, T2, T1ce, FLAIR), the proposed method achieves the best PSNR across nearly all 28 missing-modality scenarios, with representative gains of ~1.2 dB over the strongest baseline (e.g., 25.56 dB vs. 24.36 dB for T1 synthesis from T2 alone; 27.84 dB vs. 26.23 dB for T1 from three sources). On IXI (3 modalities: T1, T2, PD), the method similarly leads, reaching 25.93 dB PSNR for T1 synthesis from two sources versus 24.79 dB for the best baseline (ResViT). In downstream tumor segmentation using synthesized T1ce, the method achieves 78.43% average Dice, outperforming all baselines and approaching the 91.53% ground-truth upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four unified baselines (MMGAN, ResViT, CCR, MMHVAE) on PSNR in virtually every scenario on both BraTS and IXI. The margin over MMGAN is typically 1–2 dB PSNR (e.g., 25.56 vs. 24.36 for T1←T2 on BraTS), while the gap over MMHVAE is the largest, reaching 2–4 dB in single-source settings (e.g., 23.56 vs. 20.53 for T2←T1). ResViT is the closest competitor on SSIM in some single-source cases (e.g., 88.57 vs. 88.47 for T2←T1), but the proposed method pulls ahead by 1–3 SSIM points once two or more source modalities are available. CCR consistently underperforms, particularly on PSNR, due to its explicit contrast–content disentanglement that the authors argue discards clinically relevant correlations. In the downstream tumor segmentation task (synthesized T1ce fed to nnUNet), the method achieves 78.43% average Dice versus 76.69% (MMGAN), 73.21% (ResViT), 59.10% (CCR), and 57.35% (MMHVAE), with the largest gains in enhancing-tumor (ET) and tumor-core (TC) sub-regions where baselines produce overly dark or distorted tumor contrasts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes missing-modality MRI generation as a linear inverse problem solved via posterior sampling with a flow-matching model, building directly on PSLD's hybrid gradient-and-projection guidance and RePaint-style vector field replacement for observed channels. It departs from prior unified methods (MM-GAN, CCR, MMHVAE) that rely on complex hierarchical architectures or explicit contrast/content disentanglement, instead showing that a single joint prior over all modalities with a standard UNet backbone suffices. The many-to-one sampling strategy is a novel practical contribution that addresses inter-modality error propagation, a problem not explicitly tackled by prior unified generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the inverse-problem formulation with posterior sampling provides a clean, unified mechanism for handling arbitrary observed-missing combinations without per-pair retraining, which is practically important for clinical deployment where acquisition protocols vary. The downstream tumor segmentation validation (nnUNet on synthesized T1ce) strengthens the clinical relevance claim beyond pixel-level metrics. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with 3–4 modalities, a single UNet architecture, and a computationally expensive inference pipeline (40 NFE × 10 ExpA samples); whether the approach scales to larger modality sets, 3D volumetric data, or other imaging domains remains open.
