## Problem

Federated learning for robot perception suffers severe performance degradation under domain shifts caused by heterogeneity across robot platforms, environments, and depth distributions. Existing clustered FL methods assume clearly separable client domains, but in real robotic deployments data distributions overlap across platforms and sensing conditions, breaking the hard-clustering assumption. The paper introduces two realistic non-IID scenarios (heterogeneous platform-environment and bi-modal depth range) and a soft clustering framework to handle these overlapping client distributions.

## Input / Output

Each client provides local RGB images (from datasets like M3ED, NYUv2, or KITTI) and generates a client-level descriptor by extracting features with a pre-trained encoder, applying GeM pooling, and computing the mean and variance of the resulting feature vectors. At inference, an unseen test sequence is assigned to one or more cluster models based on descriptor similarity, and the selected model produces a per-pixel depth map. The framework is model-agnostic, supporting depth estimation architectures with different output representations (adaptive bins in AdaBins, CRF-based regression in NeWCRFs, frequency-domain in DCDepth).

## Method

FEDEPTH is a soft clustering-based clustered FL framework with three stages. In the Soft Clustering stage, each client's data distribution is summarized as a Gaussian (mean, variance) over GeM-pooled encoder features; pairwise Jeffreys divergence between these Gaussians builds a similarity matrix, which is thresholded to produce a binary cluster map allowing each client to belong to multiple clusters, followed by an iterative subset-merging step to eliminate redundant partitions. In the Cluster Model Update stage, each cluster performs FedAvg-style weighted aggregation over its member clients, and each client initializes the next round by averaging the models of all clusters it belongs to. At inference, a test sequence's descriptor is matched against cluster centroids using the same Jeffreys divergence and threshold, and the prediction is obtained by averaging the selected cluster models. The key distinction from prior CFL is the soft (multi-cluster) assignment, which captures the continuous, overlapping domain transitions inherent in heterogeneous robotic deployments.

## Results

On the HPE scenario (M3ED, 41 clients), FEDEPTH achieves the best Abs Rel across all three depth estimation architectures: 0.318 (AdaBins), 0.249 (NeWCRFs), and 0.293 (DCDepth), consistently outperforming the strongest FL baseline PACFL† (0.602, 0.318, 0.321 respectively). On the BMR scenario (NYUv2 + KITTI, 32 clients), FEDEPTH matches PACFL† closely (e.g., 0.082 vs. 0.082 Abs Rel for DCDepth) while both dramatically outperform FedAvg (0.158). The δ<1.25 threshold accuracy for NeWCRFs on HPE reaches 0.703 with FEDEPTH versus 0.540 for PACFL† and 0.504 for FedAvg, confirming substantial robustness gains under robot-driven heterogeneity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDEPTH consistently outperforms all six FL baselines on the HPE scenario across all three depth estimation architectures. Against the strongest FL baseline PACFL†, FEDEPTH reduces Abs Rel by 47% for AdaBins (0.318 vs. 0.602), 22% for NeWCRFs (0.249 vs. 0.318), and 9% for DCDepth (0.293 vs. 0.321). Standard FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedDyn, SCAFFOLD) all suffer severe degradation relative to centralized training (e.g., NeWCRFs Abs Rel jumps from 0.166 CL to 0.356–0.366 for FL methods), and FedClust performs worst among CFL methods on AdaBins (0.444) due to inaccurate hard cluster assignments. On the BMR scenario where domains are clearly separable, FEDEPTH ties PACFL† on DCDepth (0.082 vs. 0.082) and NeWCRFs (0.089 vs. 0.087), with a marginal gap on AdaBins (0.126 vs. 0.124), confirming that soft clustering gracefully degenerates to near-hard clustering when distributions are well-separated. Both FEDEPTH and PACFL† dramatically outperform FedAvg on BMR (e.g., DCDepth: 0.082 vs. 0.158 Abs Rel), validating that clustered approaches are essential even for simple two-domain splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDEPTH extends the clustered federated learning (CFL) paradigm [Sattler et al., 2021] and the FedAvg aggregation scheme [McMahan et al., 2017] by replacing hard cluster assignments with a soft, descriptor-based clustering strategy. It departs from prior CFL methods (FedClust, PACFL) that assume clearly separable client domains, instead modeling the overlapping, multi-attribute client relationships characteristic of heterogeneous robot fleets. The descriptor extraction pipeline draws on visual place recognition techniques (GeM pooling, feature distribution matching), repurposing them for client-level domain characterization rather than image retrieval. In the robot perception FL literature, prior works (FedEMA, FedVision, FedSCDepth) address single-platform or single-environment settings; FEDEPTH is the first to systematically tackle multi-platform, multi-environment heterogeneity in federated depth estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two proposed scenarios (HPE and BMR) fill a notable gap by providing realistic, multi-source non-IID benchmarks for federated depth estimation that go beyond the single-dataset evaluations common in prior robot FL work. The finding that ground-truth label-based grouping (by platform or environment) does not outperform distribution-aware soft clustering is practically important: it suggests that in real deployments, data-driven clustering will be necessary rather than relying on known metadata. Limitations include evaluation on only three depth architectures and a relatively small client count (32–41), and the 2–4 day training cost per experiment limits scalability studies. Open questions include whether the soft clustering approach generalizes to other perception tasks (segmentation, detection), how it behaves with hundreds of clients, and whether the descriptor-based clustering can be made adaptive across training rounds rather than fixed after round zero.
