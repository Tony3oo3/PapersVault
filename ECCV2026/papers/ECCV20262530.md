## Problem

Existing benchmarks evaluate unified models' text-to-image (T2I) and image-to-text (I2T) capabilities in isolation, yet the core promise of unification is cross-consistency: what a model understands, it should be able to generate. This paper shows that models scoring competitively on single-pass metrics (FID, GenEval, MME) can fail severely when their own I2T and T2I outputs are iteratively composed, progressively losing entities, attributes, spatial relations, and counts in a process the authors term semantic drift.

## Input / Output

The protocol starts from 400 image-text pairs (200 from NoCaps for novel objects, 200 from DOCCI for fine-grained descriptions) and constructs two chain types: Text-First (T→I→T→I…) and Image-First (I→T→I→T…), each run for up to 20 generations. Outputs include per-generation similarity scores across four modality mappings (text→text via MPNet, image→image via DINO, and cross-modal via CLIP), aggregated into a single MCD scalar; object-level GenEval compliance scores per generation (using OWLv2 detection) averaged into MGG; and a human cross-consistency ranking over 200 sampled chains.

## Method

The Semantic Drift Protocol (SDP) alternates a unified model's own T2I and I2T steps without user intervention, so that small understanding-generation mismatches accumulate into measurable drift. Two complementary metrics quantify this: MCD (Mean Cumulative Drift) computes embedding similarity between the original input and each subsequent generation across three representation spaces (MPNet, DINO, CLIP), then averages over generations and modality mappings; MGG (Multi-Generation GenEval) extends GenEval's object-level compliance scoring—single object, two-object, counting, colors, positions, attribute binding—across all generations using the OWLv2 detector. The benchmark deliberately samples from NoCaps and DOCCI to stress-test beyond COCO-style categories, and diagnostic splits (common vs. novel objects, simple vs. compositional prompts) isolate where drift originates. A key empirical finding is that degradation is typically catastrophic rather than gradual: once a critical semantic error (e.g., a color swap or object misidentification) occurs, subsequent generations compound it and scores saturate, a failure mode invisible to single-pass evaluation.

## Results

On the Nocaps+Docci400 benchmark (400 image-text pairs from NoCaps and DOCCI), BAGEL achieves the highest semantic retention with MCD_avg = 0.4414 and MGG = 78.2, while VILA-U scores the lowest at MCD_avg = 0.2829 and MGG = 19.7, a 4.0× gap in object-level fidelity. The diagnostic split (Table 2) reveals that all models degrade sharply on compositional prompts: BAGEL drops from 92.4 (simple) to 75.9 (complex) MGG, whereas VILA-U collapses from 49.5 to 8.6. MCD_avg correlates strongly with human rankings (Pearson r = −0.818 for T2I, r = −0.833 for I2T), and MGG outperforms single-pass GenEval in aligning with human generation judgments (r = −0.821 vs. r = −0.753).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAGEL [8] leads all seven systems on both MCD_avg (0.4414) and MGG (78.2), maintaining semantic fidelity over 20 alternating I2T↔T2I generations where competitors collapse. BLIP3-o [5] ranks second (MCD_avg 0.3984, MGG 62.2), while Show-o [42] and Janus-7B [40] occupy the middle tier with MGG scores of 57.8 and 52.5 respectively. The decoupled LLaVA+SDXL [18,25] baseline is notable for scoring 32.3 on MGG (better than Janus-1.3B and VILA-U) yet only 0.3298 on MCD_avg, indicating it can render individual objects but loses holistic scene semantics under cycling. Janus-1.3B [40] (MGG 27.0) and VILA-U [41] (MGG 19.7) degrade catastrophically, with VILA-U reaching near-zero MGG by generation 19. Human evaluation (Table 3) confirms the automated ranking: BAGEL receives the best T2I rank (1.63) and competitive I2T rank (2.65), while VILA-U is last on both (5.65 and 4.70), and the cross-consistency analysis shows most models exhibit Poor-Generation/Good-Understanding asymmetry, with BAGEL showing the fewest such mismatches (7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cycle-consistency idea from Bahng et al. [3] (which used a single I2T→T2I round to build preference data) into a multi-generation protocol that tracks semantic degradation over up to 20 alternating steps. It builds directly on GenEval [10] by extending its object-level compliance scoring across generations (MGG), and positions itself against the fragmented single-pass evaluation landscape (FID, GenEval for T2I; MME, MMBench for I2T) that never tests whether a model's understanding and generation are mutually consistent. The closest prior work, Uni-MMMU [46], designs bidirectional tasks but does not measure how core scene semantics erode under repeated self-composition, which is the specific gap SDP targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that single-pass benchmark scores do not predict multi-generation stability has direct practical implications for any pipeline that chains understanding and generation (e.g., iterative editing, agentic visual workflows). The observation that drift is typically catastrophic rather than gradual—once a critical entity or relation is lost, subsequent generations compound the error—suggests that robustness requires preventing the first failure rather than recovering from it. Open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond the seven open-weight models tested, whether training objectives (e.g., explicit cycle-consistency losses) can reduce drift, and whether the 400-pair Nocaps+Docci400 benchmark is sufficiently diverse to capture all failure modes at scale.
