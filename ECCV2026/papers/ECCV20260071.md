## Problem

Existing label smoothing and margin-based label smoothing methods apply a fixed, uniform smoothing strength to all samples and only correct overconfidence, ignoring underconfident predictions. They also rely on softmax outputs (which are themselves overconfident) and do not account for the non-stationary evolution of the model during training, leaving a gap in per-sample, bidirectional calibration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260071/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a training-time plug-in that consumes the intermediate feature vector (d-dimensional backbone output for ResNets, CLS token for ViTs) and the detached class logits from a standard classifier. It produces a per-sample scalar smoothing coefficient α^(i) ∈ (0, 2α) that modulates the base LS or MbLS loss during backpropagation; at inference the model outputs the usual class label and softmax confidence, now better calibrated. No auxiliary validation set is required at training time.

## Method

FeDLaS introduces an Adaptive Smoothing Module (ASM) that computes a per-sample coefficient α^(i) = α · 2·σ(β · ψ(sg[s]) · γ(sg[z])) via a sigmoid of two detached signals. The Norm-based Confidence Indicator (NCI) γ(·) normalizes the L1 norm of backbone features against an Exponentially Moving Average of batch statistics, yielding a class-agnostic confidence proxy that avoids softmax saturation and tracks non-stationarity. The Bidirectional Calibration Gating (BCG) module ψ(·) is a lightweight MLP on detached logits with a fixed [1,−1] readout and a Straight-Through Estimator, outputting +1 (overconfident → increase smoothing) or −1 (underconfident → decrease smoothing). This coefficient is then plugged into either the vanilla LS loss as a convex combination of CE and entropy terms, or the MbLS loss as a unified balancing parameter between CE and the margin-based logit-gap penalty, replacing the fixed hyperparameter with a sample-adaptive one.

## Results

On standard classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet with ResNet-50/101), FeDLaS-MbLS achieves the best mean ECE rank (1.83) and AECE rank (3.83) across all 14 compared methods. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-50), FeDLaS-MbLS reduces ECE to 1.12% versus 1.21% for MbLS and 2.32% for ACLS, while maintaining 79.58% average Top-1 accuracy (vs. 79.66% for MbLS). On fine-grained CUB-2011 (ViT-B/16), FeDLaS-LS achieves ECE of 2.85%, dramatically outperforming LS (5.36%) and ACLS (11.82%). The decomposed ECE analysis confirms FeDLaS corrects both overconfidence (O-ECE) and underconfidence (U-ECE), whereas base LS/MbLS reduce O-ECE at the cost of inflating U-ECE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeDLaS-MbLS outperforms the strongest SOTA baseline ACLS on CIFAR-10 (ECE 1.12% vs. 2.32%) and CIFAR-100 (4.42% vs. 7.05%), and on Tiny-ImageNet (1.22% vs. 1.37%), while also beating its own base MbLS on CIFAR-100 (4.42% vs. 5.00%) and Tiny-ImageNet (1.22% vs. 1.45%). FeDLaS-LS similarly dominates LS on CIFAR-10 (1.29% vs. 3.59%) and CIFAR-100 (4.29% vs. 5.42%), and beats DFL on CIFAR-10 (1.29% vs. 2.46%). On fine-grained CUB-2011 with ViT-B/16, FeDLaS-LS achieves ECE 2.85% versus 11.82% for ACLS and 5.36% for LS — a 4–7× improvement. The one notable trade-off: FeDLaS-MbLS slightly underperforms MbLS on CIFAR-10 ResNet-101 AECE (3.58% vs. 3.25%), and on FGVC-Aircraft ViT-B/16, FeDLaS-MbLS (ECE 23.99%) lags behind FeDLaS-LS (12.38%), leading the authors to recommend FeDLaS-LS for fine-grained tasks. Top-1 accuracy is preserved: FeDLaS variants (79.58–79.62% average) match or slightly trail their base losses (79.66–79.69%) by <0.1%, while substantially outperforming Focal Loss variants (78.26–78.89%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the label smoothing calibration lineage (Müller et al. 2019; Liu et al. 2022) by replacing the fixed smoothing hyperparameter with a per-sample adaptive coefficient, and grounds its confidence signal in the feature-norm theory of Park et al. (ICCV 2023), which shows L1 norms of hidden activations converge to an implicit hidden-classifier logit. It departs from the dominant unidirectional framing (overconfidence-only correction) adopted by ACLS, Focal Loss variants, and ECP, and from softmax-based adaptive methods identified by Park et al. (ACLS, ICCV 2023) as structurally limited, by introducing a bidirectional gate that corrects both over- and under-confidence using detached internal features rather than the model's own (overconfident) outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposed ECE analysis reveals that standard LS and MbLS substantially inflate under-confidence (U-ECE) while reducing over-confidence (O-ECE), a trade-off the bidirectional gating explicitly addresses without requiring a validation set. The plug-and-play design (two hyperparameters: β and θ) makes adoption straightforward for practitioners already using LS or MbLS. Open questions include whether the L1-norm confidence proxy generalizes to architectures without unit-wise rectifier activations, whether the approach transfers to unsupervised or noisy-label calibration, and whether the OOD-detection degradation observed in base LS/MbLS is fully recoverable at scale.
