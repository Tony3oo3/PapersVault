## Problem

Existing person ReID research treats modality discrepancy (visible-infrared) and clothing variation as separate, mutually exclusive challenges, yet in long-term surveillance both coexist simultaneously. No prior benchmark or method addresses the combined setting where a pedestrian must be matched across both a modality switch and an outfit change, and the paper shows that naively applying VI-ReID alignment on top of clothing-varying features can actually degrade performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260937/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes visible (RGB) and infrared pedestrian images resized to 384×192, where each identity is captured under two distinct outfits across the two modalities (3 cameras, 214 identities, 18,375 images in SYSU-CMCC). During training, a balanced sampling strategy feeds 8 identities per mini-batch with 4 visible and 4 infrared instances each. The network outputs a single identity embedding per image, which is used for cross-modality retrieval ranking under both V2I and I2V protocols, evaluated via CMC (Rank-1/10/20) and mAP.

## Method

PIA employs a two-stage progressive learning paradigm on a ResNet-50 backbone: Stage I optimizes a Dual-Branch Disentanglement Learning (DBDL) module that uses a CBAM-style spatial attention to produce a clothing mask, then applies a smooth suppression (m_id = 1 − λ·m_c) to derive an identity mask, with an orthogonality loss enforcing decorrelation between the two feature subspaces. Stage II introduces a Bi-Directional Prototype Learning (BPL) module that maintains modality-specific identity prototypes updated via momentum (α=0.9), and jointly optimizes intra-modality ProtoNCE losses (pulling samples toward their own prototype, pushing away from others) and inter-modality prototype losses (aligning each sample with its counterpart prototype in the other modality). The critical design choice is that cross-modality alignment is deferred until clothing-irrelevant representations are already established, preventing the model from aligning clothing cues instead of identity semantics. Stage II training begins at epoch 55 of a 90-epoch schedule, with λ₁=0.5 and λ₂=1.5 balancing the orthogonality and inter-modality terms respectively.

## Results

On the SYSU-CMCC dataset, PIA achieves 57.0% Rank-1 and 46.5% mAP under the Visible-to-Infrared (V2I) protocol, and 50.4% Rank-1 and 50.3% mAP under the Infrared-to-Visible (I2V) protocol. These results substantially exceed the strongest VI-ReID baseline (DEEN: 28.3% R-1 / 24.8% mAP V2I) and the strongest CC-ReID baseline (CaAug: 42.6% R-1 / 36.6% mAP V2I), demonstrating that jointly handling modality discrepancy and clothing variation requires a dedicated approach rather than a simple combination of existing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIA outperforms all 15 baselines across every metric in both V2I and I2V settings. Against the strongest VI-ReID method (DEEN, 28.3% R-1 / 24.8% mAP V2I), PIA gains +28.7% R-1 and +21.7% mAP, confirming that modality alignment alone is insufficient when clothing changes are present. Against the strongest CC-ReID method (CaAug, 42.6% R-1 / 36.6% mAP V2I), PIA still leads by +14.4% R-1 and +9.9% mAP, showing that clothing-agnostic features learned under visible-only conditions degrade significantly under infrared. The gap is even more pronounced in I2V, where PIA (50.4% R-1 / 50.3% mAP) surpasses the best CC-ReID baseline (CaAug, 38.2% R-1 / 38.2% mAP) by +12.2% R-1 and +12.1% mAP. Notably, VI-ReID methods as a group perform substantially worse than CC-ReID methods on this benchmark (e.g., the best VI-ReID mAP is 24.8% vs. 36.6% for the best CC-ReID), empirically validating the paper's claim that naive cross-modality alignment without clothing suppression is counterproductive under CMCC-ReID conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied ReID subfields—Visible-Infrared ReID (VI-ReID) and Clothing-Change ReID (CC-ReID)—and argues that their core challenges (modality gap and clothing variation) coexist in real long-term surveillance rather than being mutually exclusive. Methodologically, it builds on ProtoNCE-style prototype contrastive losses for the BPL module and on adversarial/attention-based clothing suppression (CAL) for the DBDL module, but departs from both lines by introducing a progressive training paradigm: disentangle clothing first, then align modalities. The key conceptual departure from prior VI-ReID work is the demonstration that naive cross-modality alignment (e.g., SAAI) can be counterproductive when clothing varies, motivating the staged optimization strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SYSU-CMCC benchmark is the first to jointly stress-test modality discrepancy and clothing variation, filling a gap that limits the practical deployment of either VI-ReID or CC-ReID systems in long-term surveillance. The finding that modality alignment without prior clothing suppression degrades performance below a plain ResNet-50 baseline is a useful cautionary result for the field, suggesting that the order of operations in multi-factor ReID matters. Limitations include the relatively small scale (214 identities, 3 cameras), the dataset being derived from two existing benchmarks rather than newly collected, and evaluation on a single dataset, leaving open whether the progressive disentangle-then-align principle generalizes to additional modalities, larger identity pools, or unconstrained wild settings.
