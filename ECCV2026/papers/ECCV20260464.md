## Problem

3D assets in the wild arrive with arbitrary global rotations, and existing canonical datasets (COD 32K, Objaverse-OA 14.8K, OmniObject3D 6K) are too small to make directional semantics like 'front' or 'up' statistically learnable. The bottleneck is that canonicalization workflows rely on manual 3D alignment taking minutes per object, making it prohibitively expensive to scale beyond tens of thousands of instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260464/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a 3D mesh (from Objaverse/Objaverse-XL), samples a colored point cloud normalized to a unit sphere, and uses a category label to retrieve a per-category template and symmetry parameters. Rather than outputting a continuous rotation, the system produces a discrete set of five candidate rotation matrices (combinations of vertical and horizontal criteria), from which a human annotator selects one via a 2D GUI in under 5 seconds. The final output is a canonicalized 3D dataset of 320K objects across 1,156 categories with consistent global orientation, scale, and position.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage canonicalization engine that reformulates alignment as hypothesis generation plus lightweight human discrimination. Stage I decouples orientation into vertical and horizontal components: vertical alignment uses either supporting-surface enumeration (center-of-mass within supporting polygon, verified by a VLM) or PCA eigendecomposition with semantic-part Chamfer distance to resolve sign ambiguity; horizontal alignment uses either pure geometric Chamfer distance to a category template or a joint energy function combining semantic part alignment with geometric fit near extrema. Stage II presents the five resulting candidates (horizontal semantic, horizontal geometric, 180°-flipped geometric for symmetric objects, vertical-support + horizontal semantic, vertical-PCA + horizontal semantic) as 2D renderings for one-click selection. This converts the continuous SO(3) search into a discrete 5-way 2D selection, cutting per-object annotation from ~101 s to ~3 s while maintaining alignment precision within ~6°.

## Results

On in-distribution orientation estimation, VI-Net trained on CanoVerse achieves 55.31% Acc@10° and 64.14% Acc@30° (Abs error 51.47°), substantially outperforming the same model trained on Objaverse-OA (21.77% / 25.86%) and all other baselines. On the out-of-distribution OmniObject3D benchmark, VI-Net(CanoVerse) reaches 20.18% Acc@10°, roughly 3× the next-best method (Orient Anything at 4.77%). For 3D generation, fine-tuning on canonical data reduces pose IQR from 48.5° to 7.7° (Trellis) and from 75.6° to 8.1° (Hunyuan3D 2.1), while also improving Chamfer Distance and CLIP score. The annotation pipeline achieves 2.6 s/object for high-skill annotators (36× faster than manual Blender alignment at 94.5 s) with 5.9° precision, and on DREDS canonicalization it attains IC 0.032 / GEC 0.123, beating COD (0.194 / 0.208) by 83.5% and 40.9% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VI-Net trained on CanoVerse dominates all in-distribution baselines: it beats SecondPose (49.15%) by 6.2 points and CleanPose (35.02%) by 20.3 points on Acc@10°, while the same architecture trained on the smaller Objaverse-OA dataset collapses to 21.77%, confirming that dataset scale and quality drive the gain. On the OOD OmniObject3D test, VI-Net(CanoVerse) at 20.18% Acc@10° outperforms Orient Anything (4.77%) and GenPose++ (4.33%) by roughly 4×, and also surpasses VI-Net trained on NOCS (3.92%) or Objaverse-OA (6.56%). For 3D generation, canonical fine-tuning of Trellis and Hunyuan3D 2.1 consistently lowers Chamfer Distance (e.g., 0.095 vs 0.227 on Toys4K for Trellis) and reduces pose IQR by 84–89%, with no baseline matching this stability. In cross-modal retrieval, Uni3D with canonical data improves Recall@10 from 51.29 to 53.88 (text) and 79.07 to 81.82 (image) on CanoVerse, and from 61.34 to 64.86 (image) on OmniObject3D. On DREDS canonicalization, the proposed pipeline achieves IC 0.032 and GEC 0.123, beating COD (0.194 / 0.208) by large margins, though it trails COD slightly on the Camera category (GEC 0.584 vs 1.177) due to 2D projection ambiguity; against self-supervised methods (CaCa, ConDor, ShapeMatcher), it improves average IC by 83.5% and GEC by 40.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the COD (Jin et al., CVPR 2025) one-shot canonicalization framework by replacing its fully automatic alignment with a hybrid candidate-generation-plus-human-selection pipeline, trading a few seconds of human judgment for substantially higher alignment quality. It also builds on the broader canonical-dataset lineage (ShapeNet, OmniObject3D, Objaverse-OA) and the geometric/semantic tooling of NOCS, Find3D, and VLM-based selection, but repositions canonicalization as a scalable data-production problem rather than a per-object optimization. For downstream evaluation, it reuses established backbones (VI-Net for pose, Trellis/Hunyuan3D for generation, ULIP/Uni3D for retrieval) under strictly controlled single-variable comparisons where canonicalization is the sole factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By delivering a 10× scale increase over prior canonical datasets (320K objects, 1,156 categories), CanoVerse makes directional semantics statistically learnable at a coverage that was previously infeasible, enabling zero-shot point-cloud orientation estimation and dramatically more pose-stable 3D generation. The canonicalization pipeline (≈3 s candidate generation + ≈3 s one-click selection) is a practical recipe that other groups can adopt to canonicalize their own asset collections. Open questions include whether the five-candidate set covers all pathological geometries, whether further scale (beyond 320K) yields diminishing returns, and whether the human-in-the-loop can be eliminated entirely without quality loss.
