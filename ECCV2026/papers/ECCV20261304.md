## Problem

Real-world single-photon LiDAR perception is fundamentally challenging due to Poisson photon statistics, low signal-to-background ratios, and complex multi-return transient phenomena that jointly complicate geometric reconstruction and semantic understanding. Despite growing interest in SPAD-based sensing, existing studies rely on simulated data or small-scale controlled captures, leaving systematic multi-task evaluation of real-world single photon perception across depth estimation, multi-view reconstruction, and 3D semantic segmentation underexplored.

## Input / Output

Each view provides a raw per-pixel time-of-flight histogram of size 256×192×672 (672 temporal bins at 750 ps resolution), preserving full multi-return transient structure rather than collapsed depth estimates, along with calibrated camera poses derived from auxiliary Livox Avia LiDAR SLAM. Outputs span three tracks: metric depth maps (evaluated via Chamfer Distance and Recall at 1/3/5-bin tolerances), novel-view intensity/depth/histogram renderings (SSIM, LPIPS, L1, PSNR), and 13-class 3D semantic labels encoded as a histogram-domain semantic bin tensor S ∈ {0,...,C}^{N×B} that assigns multiple disjoint labels along a single pixel ray. The full dataset occupies approximately 168.7 GB across 10,316 real-captured views.

## Method

The core contribution is a real-captured multi-task SPL benchmark built on a dual-sensor platform (Adaps ADS6311 Hawk solid-state SPL at 256×192, 128°×96° FOV, 10–20 Hz, plus Livox Avia auxiliary LiDAR for pose and depth reference). For semantic annotation, the authors develop a sequential peak peeling strategy: iteratively identifying the dominant peak in each pixel histogram, assigning a semantic label to the FWHM-defined peak-support interval, and suppressing the labeled signal neighborhood to expose subsequent weaker returns until all valid returns are annotated, enabling a single pixel ray to encode multiple disjoint semantic entities. Three standardized task tracks—depth estimation (10 samples), multi-view reconstruction (9 scenes with 20–40 views), and 3D semantic segmentation (27 sequences, 10,297 samples)—are provided with unified splits, per-track release contents, and evaluation protocols. The paper further includes a simulation-to-real gap study demonstrating that simulation pretraining aids performance under limited real data but cannot fully capture real SPL noise statistics and multi-return effects.

## Results

On the depth estimation track, SSPINET achieves the best Chamfer Distance (1.4978 m) and highest Recall at all temporal tolerances (44.27% at 1 bin, 83.72% at 3 bins, 93.68% at 5 bins), outperforming model-based methods Shin (CD 1.6908 m), Rapp (CD 1.7486 m), and Li (CD 1.5506 m). For multi-view reconstruction, TransientNeRF leads Transientangelo on all four metrics (SSIM 0.7717 vs. 0.7597, LPIPS 0.2315 vs. 0.2501, depth L1 0.7836 vs. 0.8710, histogram PSNR 45.43 vs. 41.57). In 3D semantic segmentation, the best combination (SSPINET preprocessing + PointNet++) reaches 90.73% OA and 51.31% mIoU, while the highest mIoU (52.22%) comes from simple thresholding with PointMLP, indicating that histogram-domain preprocessing contributes more to performance than backbone architecture choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In depth estimation, SSPINET [44] clearly dominates all three model-based baselines: it reduces CD by 0.05–0.25 m and improves 1-bin Recall by 9.7–18.3 percentage points over Shin [37], Rapp [30], and Li [18], with the gap narrowing at 5-bin tolerance (93.68% vs. 83.88–89.14%). For multi-view reconstruction, TransientNeRF [22] outperforms Transientangelo [20] across all metrics, with a notable 3.86 dB advantage in histogram PSNR (45.43 vs. 41.57) and a 0.088 lower depth L1 error, suggesting better geometric fidelity from the NeRF-based formulation. In 3D semantic segmentation, the choice of histogram preprocessing has a larger impact than the backbone: SSPINET preprocessing yields the best OA (90.73% with PointNet++ [28]), while simple thresholding with PointMLP [21] achieves the highest mIoU (52.22%). Matched filtering [42] and PPC [10] provide only marginal gains over no preprocessing, and PointNeXt [29] consistently underperforms the other three backbones (PointNet++, PointMLP, Point Transformer [47]) by 1–8 mIoU points regardless of preprocessing. Overall, the benchmark reveals that learning-based transient processing (SSPINET) is the most effective front-end, while backbone architecture differences are relatively modest (≤3 mIoU) once adequate preprocessing is applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not extend a specific algorithmic lineage but instead carves new ground in the evaluation infrastructure for single-photon LiDAR (SPL) perception. Prior SPAD datasets (O'Toole et al., SPADNet, TransientNeRF) are either simulation-based, single-task, or lack calibrated multi-view geometry and semantic annotations, leaving no unified real-captured benchmark for systematic cross-method comparison. The paper repurposes established depth estimation methods (Shin, Rapp, SSPINET), transient rendering frameworks (TransientNeRF, Transientangelo), and point-cloud segmentation backbones (PointNet++, PointMLP, Point Transformer, PointNeXt) as baselines, while introducing a novel histogram-domain annotation framework (sequential peak peeling) that has no direct predecessor in the SPL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STB fills a critical gap by providing the first real-captured, multi-task SPL benchmark with full time-of-flight histograms, calibrated poses, and 3D semantic labels under unified splits and protocols, enabling fair comparison across signal-processing, physics-based, and learning-based approaches. The simulation-to-real gap experiment quantifies that simulated transients provide useful initialization but cannot substitute for real captures, reinforcing the need for real-world data. Key limitations include modest dataset scale (10 depth samples, 9 reconstruction scenes), reliance on auxiliary LiDAR for poses and depth references (introducing SLAM drift and cross-sensor calibration errors), and the observation that mIoU plateaus near 50% despite ~90% OA, reflecting inherent photon sparsity. Open questions include scaling to more diverse scenes and SPL hardware, illumination-aware reconstruction, and whether the histogram-domain annotation paradigm generalizes to higher-resolution SPAD arrays.
