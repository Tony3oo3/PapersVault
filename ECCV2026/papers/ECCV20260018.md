## Problem

Monocular depth estimation collapses layered geometry—where a single camera ray can intersect multiple visible surfaces, as through transparent glass—into one scalar depth per pixel, turning the supervised target into a dataset-shaped convention rather than a scene-intrinsic truth. No prior work has systematically measured which valid layer a frozen depth model reports under this ambiguity, nor tested whether that layer choice can be modulated without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260018/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a Laplacian-transformed version of the same image (LVP: per-channel 3×3 Laplacian convolution followed by min-max rescaling to uint8 [0,255]) as two alternative inputs to a frozen depth model, producing one single-channel depth map per input. Evaluation is sparse and ordinal: MD-3k provides one annotated point pair per image with sign labels for both the transparent foreground and visible background layers, so outputs are scored by whether the predicted depth ordering between the two points matches each layer's ground-truth ordering (SRA), rather than by dense metric depth error.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part diagnostic framework. First, MD-3k (3,161 images from GDD) encodes two-layer ambiguity as sparse ordinal constraints—each image has one point pair with independent foreground and background ordering labels—enabling measurement of a model's depth-layer preference α = SRA(background) − SRA(foreground) without dense metric ground truth. Second, Laplacian Visual Prompting (LVP) applies a fixed 3×3 Laplacian kernel channel-wise in floating point, min-max normalizes the signed residual back to the model's input range, and feeds the result through the same frozen weights, yielding a second depth hypothesis from the same model. The RGB and LVP outputs form an unordered candidate pair evaluated jointly via Multi-Layer Spatial Relationship Accuracy (ML-SRA), where a single dataset-level permutation assigns each output to a layer. The key finding is that LVP is a training-free spectral probe: it substantially shifts the reported layer for spectrally receptive models (e.g., DAv2-L goes from 65.3% foreground to 84.1% background SRA on the Reverse subset) while leaving others nearly unchanged, and the DAv2-L RGB/LVP pair reaches 75.5% ML-SRA, exceeding the 56.4% ceiling of any duplicated single-map pair.

## Results

On MD-3k, the RGB/LVP candidate pair with DAv2-L achieves 75.5% Multi-Layer Spatial Relationship Accuracy (ML-SRA) overall, with 52.2% on the Reverse subset (where the two layers impose conflicting ordinal constraints) and 93.6% on the Same subset. This exceeds the strict 56.4% Ideal Collapsed Baseline (a duplicated single-map ceiling) by +19.1 points, demonstrating that the same frozen model queried under RGB and LVP inputs can jointly satisfy contradictory spatial relations. On the non-ambiguous DA-2K reference, DAv2-L's RGB SRA is 96.9%, confirming that the benchmark's challenge is specifically the layered ambiguity rather than general ordinal difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The RGB/LVP pair with DAv2-L (75.5% overall, 52.2% Reverse) outperforms all other evaluated models, with DAv2-B (73.3%) and DAv2-I-L (71.1%) as the next strongest. DPT (70.2%) and ZoeDepth (68.8%) show moderate LVP modulation, while Depth Pro (66.3%) and the DAv2-S variant (67.2%) fall in the mid-range. The DAv1 family (56.6–57.9%) and diffusion-based estimators Marigold (57.4%) and GeoWizard (59.5%) respond weakly to LVP, staying near the 56.4% collapsed baseline, indicating their spectral architecture does not support layer switching. On the critical Reverse subset, DAv2-L's 52.2% represents a 52.2-point gain over the 0% collapsed baseline, whereas DAv1-L achieves only 10.9% and Marigold 15.3%. Against a semantics-assisted mask-interpolation pipeline (Table 3), LVP with DAv2-L (75.5%) is competitive with the predicted-mask variant (75.8%) without requiring an auxiliary segmentation model, though the GT-mask oracle (82.5%) remains higher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inherits the sparse ordinal evaluation framework from DIW (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2016) and DA-2K (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2024), but repurposes it to diagnose a previously unmeasured property of frozen depth models: their depth-layer preference under geometric ambiguity. It deliberately departs from the multi-layer depth estimation line (Wen et al., ICCV 2025), which retrains models to output separate layers, by instead probing what a single-output model already encodes and whether that encoding can be modulated without retraining. The Laplacian Visual Prompting technique extends the input-space prompting paradigm (Tsai et al., ICML 2020) from classification and domain adaptation into 3D geometric reasoning, applying a non-learnable spectral transform to a frozen depth estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a frozen depth model's output is not a fixed scene property but a representation-dependent convention challenges the implicit assumption in depth evaluation that a single scalar target is the ground truth. The 75.5% ML-SRA (vs. the 56.4% single-hypothesis ceiling) for DAv2-L demonstrates that complementary valid hypotheses are already latent in current models, suggesting that future depth systems should represent and evaluate multiple plausible depths rather than collapsing ambiguity into one label. Key limitations include the sparse annotation (one point pair per image), reliance on a single source dataset (GDD), and the model-dependent nature of LVP modulation, leaving open questions about automatic layer selection, causal mechanisms behind spectral receptivity, and generalization beyond transparent scenes.
