## Problem

Vision-Language Models provide rich open-world semantics but remain weakly grounded in 3D geometry and temporal continuity, while geometric perception captures structure and motion yet is semantically sparse. No existing framework unifies these into a persistent, queryable 4D representation that VLMs can reason over without task-specific training or architectural modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261324/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SNOW consumes temporally synchronized RGB images and 3D point clouds (from LiDAR sensors or MapAnything feed-forward reconstruction) along with a natural-language query. All sensors must be geometrically calibrated; the pipeline runs at ~1.2 fps on a single H100 GPU. The output is a persistent 4D Scene Graph (4DSG) where each object node carries STEP tokens—visual patch tokens from masked image regions plus explicit parameter tokens for 3D centroid, 3D Gaussian covariance/extent, and temporal appearance/disappearance timestamps—serialized directly in the VLM's native token language so the model can reason over spatial structure and temporal dynamics without any alignment training.

## Method

SNOW is a training-free, backbone-agnostic pipeline that first clusters each point cloud via HDBSCAN to form object-level proposals, then uses uniformly sampled cluster points as prompts for SAM2 segmentation. Each resulting mask is encoded through Spatio-Temporal Tokenized Patch Encoding (STEP): the masked image is partitioned into 16×16 patches, cells with IoU ≥ 0.5 are retained as visual tokens, and three attribute tokens are appended—a 3D centroid, a 3D Gaussian shape descriptor (3×3 covariance matrix plus axis-aligned min/max bounds), and a temporal interval (t_start, t_end). These STEP tokens accumulate across a sliding window of T=10 frames into a 4D Scene Graph, with a SLAM backend (MapAnything for image-only, KISS-SLAM for LiDAR) providing global coordinate alignment and an H_hop reasoning step filtering implausible geometries. The VLM (e.g., Gemma3-4B-IT) then performs inference directly on the serialized 4DSG tokens, requiring no fine-tuning, feature pooling, or architectural changes, and the same interface generalizes across VLM backbones and sensing modalities.

## Results

SNOW achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across four major benchmarks without any training or fine-tuning. On NuScenes-QA, it reaches 60.1% overall accuracy (vs. 58.5% for OpenDriveVLA-3B), with the largest gain in the Status category (+23.5% over prior best). On RoboSpatial-Home, SNOW attains 72.3% average spatial reasoning accuracy in a fully zero-shot setting, surpassing the best finetuned baseline (RoboPoint+RS, 63.4%) by +8.9%. On VLM4D, SNOW scores 72.6% overall, outperforming all 7 compared models by an average margin of +10.6%, including a +16.4% lead in directional reasoning. On ERQA, the 4B-parameter SNOW achieves 61.5%, exceeding Gemini 2.0 Pro (48.3%) and Qwen3 VL 235B (52.5%) while trailing only o3 (64.0%) and GPT-5 (65.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNOW outperforms all 12 compared baselines on at least one primary metric, with the most dramatic margins on VLM4D where it leads every competitor by 10+ points on the overall score. On NuScenes-QA, SNOW beats OpenDriveVLA-7B (58.2%), BEVDet+BUTD (57.0%), and OccLLaMA3.1 (54.5%) on overall accuracy, with its largest single-category win being Status (80.5% vs. 57.0% for OpenDriveVLA-0.5B); however, it trails OpenDriveVLA-7B slightly on the Comparison sub-category (61.0% vs. 68.8%), which the authors attribute to a retrieval bottleneck rather than a representational limitation. On RoboSpatial-Home, SNOW (72.3% zero-shot) surpasses all finetuned baselines including RoboPoint+RS (63.4%), LLaVA-NeXT+RS (59.6%), and SpaceLLaVA+RS (52.4%), with the largest gap in Spatial Context (+23.8% over RoboPoint+RS). On ERQA, SNOW (61.5%) clearly beats Gemini 2.0 Pro (48.3%), GPT-4o (47.0%), and Qwen3 VL 235B (52.5%), but is outperformed by the two largest reasoning models o3 (64.0%) and GPT-5 (65.7%), which benefit from substantially more parameters and test-time compute. The backbone-only Gemma3-4B-IT baseline (40.7% on ERQA, 43.1% on RoboSpatial-Home, 39.9% on VLM4D ablation) confirms that the 4D STEP token injection—not the VLM itself—drives the gains, yielding absolute improvements of +20.8%, +29.2%, and +32.7% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

SNOW carves new ground by repurposing off-the-shelf components (HDBSCAN clustering, SAM2 segmentation, SLAM backends) into a unified, training-free pipeline that was not previously assembled in this configuration. It departs from prior spatio-temporal grounding work (3DGraphLLM, PSG-4D, DSM) by eliminating the need for modality-alignment training and fixed relation vocabularies, instead expressing all geometric and temporal attributes directly in the VLM's native token language via the proposed STEP encoding. The conceptual lineage draws from both the scene-graph tradition (structured, queryable scene representations) and the VLM prompting literature (injecting structured context without fine-tuning), but the specific combination of density-based 3D clustering, point-prompted segmentation, and Gaussian-shape tokenization into a persistent 4D graph is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, backbone-agnostic design means SNOW can be dropped into any VLM pipeline without retraining, which is practically significant for robotics teams that cannot afford task-specific fine-tuning across diverse sensor suites. The ablation cleanly isolates that metric 3D consistency (not merely temporal tracking) is the primary driver of 4D reasoning gains, a finding that should inform future spatio-temporal model design. Key open questions include scalability (1.2 fps on H100, growing token sequences in large scenes), whether the 4DSG can capture fine-grained object deformation, and whether learned 4D encoders can eventually replace the hand-crafted STEP statistics while retaining the training-free interface.
