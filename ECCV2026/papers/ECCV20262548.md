## Problem

Transformer-based object trackers suffer from quadratic attention cost over long token sequences, and existing token-pruning approaches for tracking face two systematic limitations: early-layer attention scores are too diffuse to reliably guide pruning (forcing methods to defer sparsification to intermediate layers), and the prediction head remains a dense bottleneck that requires padding sparse tokens back to a full 2D grid, partially negating the computational savings. The paper asks whether early-layer pruning is inherently harmful or merely lacks the right spatial guidance, and whether an end-to-end sparse pipeline from tokens to boxes is feasible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a template image (128×128 or 192×192) and a search image (256×256 or 384×384) as RGB patches, plus the previous frame's predicted bounding box (center, width, height) which is used to construct a 2D Gaussian motion window over the search region. Unlike penalty-window trackers that apply temporal priors only as post-processing on already-computed score maps, MaST uses the previous prediction to actively determine which tokens are computed in all subsequent encoder layers. The output is a single bounding box decoded from the highest-scoring retained token's stored 2D coordinate, with no dense score map or feature grid materialized.

## Method

MaST performs one-shot token sparsification at the first encoder layer by fusing cross-attention importance scores (simplified to interaction with only the center template token) with a 2D Gaussian motion window centered at the previous prediction, retaining the top-K tokens (30% retention) so all subsequent Transformer blocks operate on the reduced set. The key insight is that the motion prior compensates for the diffuseness of early-layer attention, making layer-1 pruning nearly as accurate as later-layer pruning while halving compute. A natively sparse prediction head then decodes the target via a score-first, regress-once design: an MLP score branch assigns a scalar confidence to each retained token, the argmax token is selected, and a regression branch predicts box offsets only for that single token using its stored patch-grid coordinate as anchor, eliminating all dense padding, reshaping, and redundant per-position regression.

## Results

MaST-tiny (0.84G MACs) achieves 63.8 AUC on LaSOT, 80.1 SUC on TrackingNet, and 66.6 AO on GOT-10k, setting new state-of-the-art among ~1G-MACs lightweight trackers while running at 152 FPS on Jetson Nano and 22.6 FPS on Raspberry Pi 5. MaST-small (1.82G MACs) further reaches 65.8 AUC on LaSOT and 82.3 SUC on TrackingNet, surpassing all ~2G-MACs competitors. MaST-nano (0.59G MACs) delivers 58.6 AUC on LaSOT at a leading 230 FPS on Jetson Nano, making it suitable for the most resource-constrained edge devices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaST-tiny outperforms the prior best lightweight tracker AsymTrack-S by +1.0 AUC on LaSOT, +2.2 SUC on TrackingNet, and +1.1 AO on GOT-10k, while running nearly 2× faster on Jetson Nano (152 vs. 88 FPS). Against HiT-Small, MaST-tiny gains +3.3 AUC on LaSOT at comparable MACs. In the ~2G tier, MaST-small surpasses FERMT by +0.7 AUC on LaSOT and FARTrack_tiny by +2.6 AUC, while also achieving the highest GOT-10k SR0.75 (67.0). On the VastTrack generalization benchmark, MaST-small leads with 43.2 PNorm, exceeding the next-best method by +6.7 points. On UAV123, MaST-tiny achieves the highest AUC (66.6) among all compared trackers; on NFS, it is second only to FARTrack_tiny (66.2 vs. 66.9 AUC) but runs substantially faster on edge hardware. MaST-nano trades 2.2 AUC on LaSOT relative to AsymTrack-T for a 2.3× speedup on Jetson Nano (230 vs. 99 FPS), positioning it for the most latency-sensitive deployments.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaST builds directly on OSTrack's one-stream ViT tracker and its cross-attention-based token scoring, but departs from the field's conservative practice of deferring pruning to intermediate layers by injecting a lightweight Gaussian motion prior (derived from the previous frame's prediction) into the selection process. This repurposes the classical penalty-window idea from SiamFC/SiamRPN++—previously applied only as post-hoc score re-weighting—into a computation-reducing mechanism that guides *which* tokens are processed in subsequent layers. On the output side, the natively sparse MLP head extends LoRAT's MLP-based decoding to operate on unstructured, non-padded token sets, filling a gap left by prior sparse-backbone trackers that still required dense reshaping before a convolutional or grid-aligned head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that end-to-end sparsity (from tokens to boxes) is practically achievable for real-time tracking on edge hardware, with MaST-tiny running at 152 FPS on Jetson Nano while surpassing the prior best lightweight tracker by +1.0 AUC on LaSOT. The diagnostic experiment showing that early-layer pruning is not inherently harmful—only mis-guided—reframes the design space for future sparse ViT trackers beyond tracking. A stated limitation is that high-resolution inputs still incur full attention cost before the first sparsification layer, motivating input-adaptive or multi-resolution pruning as a natural next step.
