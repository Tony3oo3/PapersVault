## Problem

Existing RGBT single-object tracking methods assume perfectly calibrated, spatially aligned, and temporally synchronized sensors, an idealization that breaks down in real-world anti-UAV deployments where heterogeneous cameras exhibit parallax, asynchronous frame rates, and uncalibrated offsets. No prior work formally defines or provides a solution for tracking under such spatio-temporal misalignment without offline pre-calibration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261709/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB and thermal video frames (uniformly resized to 540×960) that are temporally synchronized but spatially unaligned, along with an initial bounding box for tracker initialization. It outputs per-modality corrected bounding boxes and confidence scores, operating as a training-free plug-and-play wrapper around an off-the-shelf base tracker (DIMP) without requiring any retraining. A lightweight camera-motion estimator (normalized cross-correlation) is applied to compensate for rapid panning before the correction pipeline runs.

## Method

OCTA-SOT formulates the uncalibrated observation process as a unified state-space model and implements it through three cascaded sub-functions: (1) a History Update module that maintains per-modality trajectory records and reliability weights; (2) a Cross-Modal Calibration module that estimates time-varying inter-modality mapping parameters via a Kalman filter whose state variables model geometric offsets (not target kinematics), with F=I and heavily regularized process noise to enforce slow-varying assumptions; and (3) an Adaptive Adjustment module that uses confidence gating to either preserve high-confidence raw outputs or project reliable cross-modal states into degraded modalities. The Kalman filter is triggered only when a direction-aware reliability score indicates the current alignment is unstable, otherwise historical mappings are preserved. The entire framework is training-free and requires no architectural modification to the base tracker, with two deployment schemes: handcrafted empirical thresholds or Bayesian-optimized hyperparameters (GP surrogate with RBF kernel and EI acquisition).

## Results

On the Anti-UAV300 dataset, OCTA-SOT (Handcrafted) achieves a Mean AUC of 72.23%, Mean Precision of 94.96%, and Mean Normalized Precision of 91.68%, outperforming all single-modal and multi-modal baselines. Compared to its base tracker DIMP (62.95% AUC), OCTA-SOT delivers absolute improvements of +9.3% in AUC, +12.9% in Precision, and +11.9% in Normalized Precision. The PBO-optimized variant achieves nearly identical performance (72.21% Mean AUC), confirming the framework's robustness to hyperparameter choices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCTA-SOT outperforms all eight baselines on Mean AUC, Precision, and Normalized Precision. Against the strongest single-modal baseline SiamDT (71.45% AUC), OCTA-SOT gains +0.78% AUC and +0.33% Precision, a modest but consistent edge. The gap widens substantially against RGBT multi-modal trackers: TBSI (68.51% AUC), BAT (68.19%), and STTrack (68.40%) all suffer severe thermal degradation (64–65% AUC on Thermal vs. 71–73% on RGB), confirming their rigid alignment assumption breaks under uncalibrated conditions, whereas OCTA-SOT maintains balanced performance across both modalities (73.01% RGB vs. 71.44% Thermal AUC). Compared to the base DIMP tracker, the improvement is dramatic: +9.3% AUC, +12.9% Precision, and +11.9% PNorm, validating the cross-modal trajectory adjustment mechanism. The handcrafted and PBO-optimized variants perform nearly identically (72.23% vs. 72.21% Mean AUC), indicating the framework is not sensitive to threshold tuning and is ready for zero-cost deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DIMP as a base tracker and departs fundamentally from the entire line of RGBT multi-modal tracking work (TBSI, BAT, STTrack, etc.), which universally assumes perfectly calibrated, spatially aligned, and temporally synchronized sensors. Rather than designing a new deep fusion architecture, it repurposes the classical Kalman filter as an online cross-modal mapping estimator, wrapping around any off-the-shelf tracker as a training-free plug-and-play module. The theoretical contribution formalizes the uncalibrated observation process as a unified state-space model, bridging the gap between the spatio-temporal misalignment modeling and the tracker's internal state transition into a single joint correction framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world anti-UAV sensor arrays are rarely perfectly calibrated, yet all existing RGBT trackers silently assume they are. The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes the approach immediately deployable without retraining pipelines, and the near-identical performance of handcrafted vs. Bayesian-optimized hyperparameters (72.23% vs. 72.21% AUC) suggests robustness for zero-cost deployment. Key limitations include reliance on explicit confidence scores (incompatible with pure regression Transformers like OSTrack), a Kalman warm-up period causing transient early-frame errors, and evaluation confined to a single dataset (Anti-UAV300) with RGBT baselines evaluated in a non-standard dual-run protocol. Open questions include whether non-linear mappings (e.g., GNNs) can eliminate the warm-up delay and whether the state-space formulation generalizes to multi-view sensor arrays.
