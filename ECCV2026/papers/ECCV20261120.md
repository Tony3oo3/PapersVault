## Problem

Deep generative models like text-to-image diffusion systems operate as opaque black boxes, and while sparse autoencoders (SAEs) offer empirical interpretability, they lack theoretical guarantees that recovered features correspond to true latent concepts. A fundamental gap exists for hierarchical selection models—where higher-level concepts act as colliders (effects) of lower-level constituents rather than confounders (causes)—for which existing identifiability results are inapplicable, leaving interpretations potentially subjective and untrustworthy for safety-critical applications.

## Input / Output

The method consumes intermediate feature tensors (64×64×5120) extracted from U-Net or DiT layers of a pretrained diffusion model at three specific denoising timesteps (899, 500, 100), paired with text prompts that serve as auxiliary conditioning variables. It produces a hierarchical concept graph whose nodes are organized into abstraction levels corresponding to those timesteps, with directed edges encoding causal/selection relationships. Downstream, individual graph nodes can be steered (positive or negative) at their corresponding timestep to yield edited images, unlearned models, or multi-level compositions that single-layer methods cannot achieve.

## Method

The paper establishes the first component-wise identifiability theorem for continuous hierarchical selection models, where higher-level latent variables are effects (colliders) of lower-level ones via a selection function, in contrast to traditional top-down hierarchical models. The key theoretical insight is that the causal minimality principle—favoring the simplest causal explanation—manifests as a sparsity constraint on the latent concept graph (Condition 1-iv: each parent has a unique subset of children sharing no other common parent), which, combined with informativeness, smooth density, and sufficient variability conditions, guarantees that learned representations are equivalent to true latent variables up to invertible per-component transforms. Practically, this is instantiated in two steps: (1) training K-sparse SAEs on diffusion features at each timestep to approximate the sparsity condition, and (2) applying the PC causal discovery algorithm across the sparse features to recover the hierarchical graph structure. The resulting graph enables level-specific concept steering by injecting or suppressing individual SAE features back into the diffusion process only at their corresponding timestep, reducing artifacts compared to global steering.

## Results

On model unlearning across four benchmarks (IP2P, RING-A-BELL, P4D, UnlearnDiffATK), the proposed method achieves the lowest nudity-detector accuracy of 0.26% on IP2P, 1.17% average on RING-A-BELL, and 0.66% on P4D, outperforming the strongest baselines TRASCE (0.45%, 1.75%, 3.97%) and ConceptSteer (0.36%, 7.02%, 1.99%). On general generation quality (COCO 10K), it attains the best CLIP score of 31.3 (matching the unmodified SD 1.4) with a competitive FID of 17.02, confirming that concept removal does not degrade unrelated generation. For controllable image generation (Table 3), the method achieves the best CLIP-I fidelity on all three editing tasks (e.g., 0.93 for adding tabby patterns vs. 0.91 for SD 1.4 and 0.83 for SAE without hierarchy) while maintaining comparable text-alignment (CLIP-T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary unlearning metrics: it achieves the lowest IP2P score (0.26% vs. 0.36% for ConceptSteer and 0.45% for TRASCE), the lowest RING-A-BELL average (1.17% vs. 1.75% for TRASCE and 7.02% for ConceptSteer), and the lowest P4D score (0.66% vs. 1.99% for ConceptSteer and 3.97% for TRASCE). On UATK, it ties with ConceptSteer at 2.11% and is competitive with RECE (0.72%) and TRASCE (0.70%), which achieve slightly lower values. For general generation preservation, the method attains the highest COCO CLIP score (31.3, matching the unmodified SD 1.4 base) and a FID of 17.02, slightly behind MACE (16.80) but well ahead of most other unlearning methods (e.g., SA at 25.80, SLD-MAX at 28.75). In controllable generation (Table 3), the hierarchical SAE approach achieves the best CLIP-I image fidelity on all three editing tasks compared to both the standard text-guided SD 1.4 and a non-hierarchical SAE baseline, confirming that the hierarchical structure enables more precise, artifact-free edits. MACE remains the strongest competitor on RING-A-BELL (0.70% avg) and FID (16.80), but the proposed method dominates on the other three unlearning benchmarks while better preserving overall generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability literature on latent hierarchical models (Kong et al., NeurIPS 2023) by shifting from traditional top-down causal hierarchies to a selection-based formulation where higher-level concepts are *effects* of lower-level configurations, making latent variables colliders rather than confounders. It also provides the first theoretical grounding for the empirical practice of training sparse autoencoders on diffusion model features (Surkov et al., 2024), showing that the sparsity constraint is not merely a heuristic but an instantiation of causal minimality that guarantees component-wise identifiability. The key departure from prior work is that existing identification techniques for hierarchical models (linear, discrete, or subspace-level) are inapplicable to this selection structure, necessitating the new theoretical development presented here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By proving that sparsity constraints derived from causal minimality yield component-wise identifiable representations in hierarchical selection models, this work provides the missing theoretical justification for why SAE-based interpretability tools produce meaningful, individually controllable concepts in diffusion models. The practical implication is that practitioners can now perform fine-grained, level-specific interventions (unlearning, editing, steering) with theoretical guarantees rather than relying on empirically validated but theoretically ungrounded heuristics. Open questions include whether the regularity and minimality assumptions hold under broader model misspecification, and whether the framework extends to architectures beyond U-Net/DiT-based diffusion (e.g., autoregressive or flow-matching models).
