## Problem

Correspondence-based planar trackers achieve high precision but fail to recover after occlusion, motion blur, or appearance changes, while segmentation-based trackers like SAM 2 produce robust long-term masks that lack the 8-DoF geometric structure needed for planar tracking. The paper asks whether reliable homographies can be extracted from segmentation mask contours, and how to combine the complementary strengths of both paradigms.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video sequence and an initial quadrilateral specified by four 2D control points in the first frame, which are used to prompt SAM 2 with a quadrilateral mask. Per frame, it outputs a 3×3 homography matrix mapping the initial target pose to the current frame, from which the four corner positions are derived. The pipeline is entirely training-free, relying on pre-trained SAM 2, RAFT optical flow, and DINOv2 features.

## Method

SAM-H is a training-free geometric pipeline that converts SAM 2 segmentation masks into homographies: it fits four lines to the mask boundary via Hough transform, identifies corners as line intersections, and resolves the 4-fold cyclic symmetry ambiguity using a motion model for frame-to-frame ordering and DINOv2 appearance similarity (requiring Θ_r=5 consecutive frames of agreement) for re-detection after tracking loss. WOFTSAM wraps this in a three-level cascade: it first attempts correspondence-based estimation via the Weighted Flow Homography (WFH) module with the previous-frame homography as pre-warp; if the inlier ratio drops below 20%, it re-detects by pre-warping with the SAM-H homography and re-running WFH; if that also fails, it falls back to the SAM-H homography directly. This design exploits the observation that correspondences provide pixel-level precision when texture is visible, while segmentation masks provide robust re-detection under blur, occlusion, and appearance changes.

## Results

On PlanarTrack, SAM-H sets a new state-of-the-art with 62.0 p@5 and 80.0 p@15, outperforming the prior best (WOFT) by +18.4pp on p@5 and +15.2pp on p@15. On POT-210, WOFTSAM achieves 91.9 p@5 and 97.5 p@15, a new state-of-the-art that nearly halves the 15px alignment error rate compared to WOFT (95.4 p@15). On MPOT-3K, WOFTSAM reaches 95.1 p@50, outperforming the dataset-specific PRTrack (92.85 p@50). The paper also provides re-annotated PlanarTrack initial poses, showing that original GT errors account for more than half of the p@5 gap between WOFTSAM and SAM-H.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM-H decisively outperforms all prior planar trackers on PlanarTrack, beating WOFT by +18.4pp (p@5) and +15.2pp (p@15), and HVC-Net by +20.0pp (p@5). On POT-210, WOFTSAM achieves the best p@5 (91.9) and p@15 (97.5), surpassing WOFT by +1.9pp and +2.1pp respectively, and HVC-Net by +0.5pp and +1.7pp. SAM-H alone is substantially weaker on POT-210 (64.4 p@5 vs. 90.0 for WOFT), confirming that mask-boundary precision is insufficient for the high-precision regime where dense optical-flow correspondences excel. On MPOT-3K, WOFTSAM (95.1 p@50) outperforms PRTrack (92.85 p@50), the method specifically designed for that multi-target dataset. The per-sequence oracle analysis reveals that SAM-H and WOFTSAM are complementary: roughly half of PlanarTrack sequences are better tracked by each method, and an oracle selector reaches 86.9 p@15 (+6.9pp over SAM-H alone), indicating room for tighter integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: correspondence-based planar trackers (WOFT, HVC-Net, HDN) that estimate 8-DoF homographies from dense optical flow or keypoint matches, and segmentation-based video trackers (SAM 2, DAM4SAM) that produce robust long-term masks but no explicit geometric pose. The key conceptual departure is repurposing segmentation mask boundaries as input to a training-free classical geometry pipeline (Hough line fitting, corner intersection, DINOv2-based symmetry disambiguation) to recover homographies — a task segmentation trackers were never designed for. WOFTSAM then combines both paradigms in a three-level cascade, using SAM-H's robust re-detection to rescue correspondence-based estimation after tracking loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The +18.4pp gap on PlanarTrack reveals that the primary bottleneck in planar tracking was robustness to appearance changes and recovery after tracking loss, not homography estimation precision — a finding that reframes where the field should invest. The per-sequence oracle experiment (86.9 p@15 vs. 77.2 for WOFTSAM) quantifies a substantial but tractable gap in integrating the two paradigms, pointing to reliable per-frame quality estimation as the key open problem. The re-annotation finding that initial-frame GT errors account for over half the p@5 score difference between methods has immediate practical implications for benchmarking high-precision trackers. Limitations include the quadrilateral assumption, vulnerability to SAM 2 tracking the wrong 3D surface, and failure during partial occlusions with linear boundaries.
