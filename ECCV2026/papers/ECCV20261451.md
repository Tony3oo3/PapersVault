## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based LiDAR simulators for autonomous driving are trained exclusively on single-traversal sensor scans, causing severe overfitting to the recorded ego-vehicle trajectory and producing artifacts, missing geometry, and unrealistic intensity on extrapolated views. Because LiDAR point clouds are sparse and lack the dense texture supervision available to RGB, generalization to novel driving paths remains particularly challenging, yet is essential for closed-loop simulation where the vehicle deviates from the recorded path.

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-traversal LiDAR point clouds (32-beam or 64-beam), synchronized camera images, and ego-vehicle poses, requiring no additional multi-pass data collection. It additionally leverages N=10 temporally adjacent LiDAR frames and SAM2 segmentation masks to separate static from dynamic geometry during pseudo-supervision generation. Outputs are LiDAR range maps, intensity maps, ray-drop probability maps, and RGB images rendered at both interpolated (original trajectory) and extrapolated (laterally shifted by ±3–4 m lane-width) viewpoints.

## Method

LiDAR-EVS introduces two plug-and-play components on top of an existing 3DGS renderer (SplatAD). First, a pseudo-LiDAR curation pipeline generates explicit supervision for extrapolated views by: (a) fusing static LiDAR points from multiple temporally adjacent frames after SAM2-based dynamic-object removal, (b) warping the fused cloud to novel poses via world-coordinate geometric invariance, (c) applying occlusion curling that ray-casts from the extrapolated pose to discard self-occluded points, and (d) adjusting intensity using surface-normal incidence-angle ratios between original and target viewing directions. Second, a spatially-constrained dropout regularization selectively drops Gaussians within a sensor-specific region of interest (bounded by elevation angle and maximum sensing distance) during training, preventing trajectory overfitting while preserving distant background stability; at inference, opacities are rescaled by the expected retention probability to compensate. Training alternates random left/right lateral shifts at each iteration, optimizing a combined RGB (L1+SSIM) and LiDAR (range L2, intensity L2, ray-drop BCE, line-of-sight, MCMC) loss.

## Results

On the Para-Lane dataset with real physically-captured extrapolated trajectories, LiDAR-EVS reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.35 m (SplatAD) to 0.18 m (48% reduction) and Depth Error from 0.003 m² to 0.001 m² (67% reduction) on right-shifted views, while improving ray-drop accuracy from 75.8% to 84.3%. On nuScenes with 4 m lateral shifts, the Depth Error drops from 1.399 m² to 0.072 m² (95% reduction) and CD from 2.07 m to 0.55 m (73% reduction). On PandaSet (64-beam LiDAR), CD decreases from 0.94 m to 0.36 m and ray-drop accuracy jumps from 41.3% to 79.1%. Camera rendering quality is preserved or slightly improved (PSNR 26.11 vs. 25.97 on nuScenes, 27.27 vs. 27.12 on PandaSet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline SplatAD [9], LiDAR-EVS achieves dramatic gains on extrapolated LiDAR: CD drops from 0.35 m to 0.18 m and Depth from 0.003 to 0.001 m² on Para-Lane, and from 1.399 to 0.072 m² on nuScenes, while interpolated LiDAR quality is maintained (CD 0.15 vs. 0.19 m). Against NeuRAD [28], the method wins on all extrapolated LiDAR metrics (e.g., CD 0.18 vs. 0.48 m on Para-Lane right-shift), and the plug-and-play 'NeuRAD+Ours' variant further confirms sensor-agnostic applicability. Against LiDAR-GS [3] and GS-LiDAR [11], LiDAR-EVS substantially outperforms on extrapolated views (CD 0.18 vs. 0.45 and 0.56 m respectively), though LiDAR-GS retains a higher interpolated ray-drop accuracy (92.8% vs. 87.7%). Against LiDAR-RT [36], the improvement is the most pronounced (CD 0.18 vs. 0.49 m, Depth 0.001 vs. 0.054 m²). Against OmniRE [5], LiDAR-EVS dominates on all LiDAR metrics (e.g., CD 0.55 vs. 2.42 m on nuScenes extrapolated), while matching or exceeding its camera PSNR (26.11 vs. 22.77 on nuScenes). The only minor trade-off is a slight LPIPS increase on Para-Lane interpolated images (0.295 vs. 0.192 for SplatAD), while PSNR and SSIM remain essentially unchanged.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SplatAD's 3DGS-based joint LiDAR+camera rendering pipeline for autonomous driving, extending it to handle extrapolated views beyond the training trajectory. It departs from the diffusion-prior approach of LiDAR-GS++ for generalization, instead proposing a lightweight pseudo-LiDAR curation pipeline (multi-frame fusion, occlusion curling, intensity adjustment) that generates explicit supervision for novel viewpoints without external generative models. The spatially-constrained dropout regularization adapts DropGaussian's structural dropout to LiDAR-specific sensor geometry (elevation bounds, range limits), and the framework is validated as plug-and-play across NeuRAD, LiDAR-RT, and GS-LiDAR baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiDAR-EVS addresses a critical gap for closed-loop autonomous driving simulation: generating accurate LiDAR along unseen ego-vehicle paths without requiring expensive multi-pass data collection. The plug-and-play design and sensor-agnostic curation pipeline make it broadly applicable across LiDAR configurations (32-beam, 64-beam) and rendering backends. Key limitations include the reliance on static-scene assumptions (dynamic objects are removed via SAM2), the fixed lane-width shift as the extrapolation model, and the absence of temporal consistency for dynamic actors, which the authors flag as future work. Open questions include whether the pseudo-LiDAR approach generalizes to larger lateral/longitudinal deviations or more complex urban topologies.
