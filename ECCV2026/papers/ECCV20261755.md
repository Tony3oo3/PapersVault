## Problem

Existing segmentation systems are fragmented across tasks, vocabularies, and domains, requiring separate architectures and training pipelines for each setting. Prior attempts to repurpose diffusion models for segmentation rely on extracting noisy, low-resolution attention maps that demand heavy post-processing and do not generalize across multiple tasks. The paper asks whether a single pretrained diffusion backbone can be directly fine-tuned into a unified, text-controllable segmentation engine that spans closed-vocabulary, open-vocabulary, and cross-domain scenarios without architectural customization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261755/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 512×512 RGB image and an arbitrary text prompt (class name or free-form description) as input. The single-channel segmentation map is replicated across three channels to match the Stable Diffusion VAE's 3-channel interface before encoding. Output is a continuous-valued mask in [0,1] decoded from the latent space, binarized per class with a fixed threshold τ=0.7; by default, 8 stochastic denoising passes are averaged in latent space before decoding to improve boundary quality.

## Method

DiGSeg repurposes Stable Diffusion v2 by encoding both the input image and ground-truth segmentation map into the VAE latent space, then fine-tuning only the denoising U-Net to predict noise on the segmentation latent conditioned on the image latent and a CLIP text embedding. A frozen CLIP text encoder injects language features via cross-attention at multiple U-Net scales, enabling open-vocabulary grounding without task-specific heads. Training uses the standard diffusion MSE objective with an annealed multi-resolution noise schedule that blends high-frequency perturbations early and low-frequency components later to improve spatial coherence. At inference, DDIM-trailing sampling (often just 1 step) plus optional test-time ensembling of 8 noise seeds produces the final mask, achieving SOTA mIoU on COCO (50.8) and ADE20K (58.6) while generalizing zero-shot to medical, agricultural, and remote-sensing domains.

## Results

On open-vocabulary segmentation (CLIP ViT-L/14), DiGSeg achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all five benchmarks: 19.9 on A-847, 29.2 on PC-459, 43.2 on A-150, 68.4 on PC-59, and 38.5 on Cityscapes, surpassing the previous best (ESC-Net) by +1.8, +2.2, +1.4, +2.8, and +0.6 respectively. On standard semantic segmentation, DiGSeg reaches 50.8 mIoU on COCO and 58.6 mIoU on ADE20K, exceeding the second-best methods (EoMT at 48.7/57.1) by +2.1 and +1.3. On the DeepGlobe road segmentation benchmark, DiGSeg attains 65.78 IoU and 78.79 F1, outperforming the best remote-sensing-specific method (CGC-Net at 68.80 IoU) on F1 while remaining competitive on IoU without any domain-specific architectural changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiGSeg outperforms all 14 open-vocabulary baselines on every reported benchmark with CLIP ViT-L/14, with the largest margins on PC-459 (+2.2 over ESC-Net) and PC-59 (+2.8 over ESC-Net). Among diffusion-based methods specifically, DiGSeg surpasses ODISE [90] by +8.9 mIoU on A-847 and +13.1 on PC-59, and Seg4Diff [39] by +8.0 on A-150 and +17.2 on PC-59, demonstrating that explicit fine-tuning of the denoising U-Net substantially outperforms attention-map extraction approaches. On standard semantic segmentation (Table 2), DiGSeg beats EoMT [38] (48.7/57.1) by +2.1/+1.3 mIoU on COCO/ADE20K, and outperforms transformer-based methods like Mask2Former-Swin-L [12] (57.3 on ADE20K) and OneFormer [34] (57.0) by +1.3 mIoU. On the DeepGlobe road segmentation task (Table 3), DiGSeg achieves 65.78 IoU, trailing only CGC-Net [103] (68.80) by 3.0 points but surpassing it on F1 (78.79 vs. 81.51 is actually CGC-Net higher; DiGSeg leads on Precision at 79.93 vs. 82.67 is also CGC-Net higher). Correcting: CGC-Net leads on all four metrics (IoU 68.80, P 82.67, R 80.39, F1 81.51), while DiGSeg is second-best at 65.78/79.93/78.92/78.79, still substantially ahead of general-purpose segmenters like SegMAN (48.12 IoU) and EoMT (52.52 IoU) that were not designed for remote sensing. The cross-domain ablation (Table 5) further shows that training on ADE20K alone (43.58 Cityscapes mIoU with 8×2 ensemble) outperforms training on COCO+ADE20K combined (39.04), indicating dataset relevance matters more than data volume for transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiGSeg extends the Marigold paradigm of fine-tuning pretrained diffusion U-Nets for dense prediction (originally depth estimation) to the segmentation domain, but departs from prior diffusion-based segmentation methods (DiffSeg, DiffCut, DiffuMask, Seg4Diff) that extract attention maps post-hoc and rely on heuristic mask construction. Instead of treating the diffusion backbone as a feature extractor, DiGSeg directly trains the denoising network to produce segmentation-consistent latents, with a CLIP-aligned text pathway injected at multiple U-Net scales to enable open-vocabulary conditioning. This positions the work as a unification of the latent-diffusion fine-tuning line (Marigold, LDMSeg) and the CLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation line (OpenSeg, ODISE, Mask-Adapter) into a single generative framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single diffusion backbone, without any domain-specific architectural modification, transfers to medical, agricultural, and remote-sensing segmentation is practically significant for teams maintaining multiple segmentation pipelines. However, the inference speed (3.15 FPS at the best-quality setting) remains a substantial barrier to real-time deployment, and the medical-domain underperformance highlights CLIP's limited grounding in specialized vocabularies. Open questions include whether diffusion acceleration techniques (distillation, consistency models) can close the speed gap without sacrificing the cross-domain generality, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video segmentation settings.
