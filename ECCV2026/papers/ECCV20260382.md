## Problem

Inference-time scaling for diffusion and flow-matching models is far less developed than for autoregressive LLMs, and existing reward-guided methods (importance sampling, tree search) enforce a constant number of parallel samples throughout the denoising trajectory. This constant-memory constraint is an unnecessary restriction that prevents front-loading exploration over a larger pool of initial noise seeds, where intermediate reward signals are already informative for prompt-alignment tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260382/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PSP takes a text prompt and a black-box scalar reward model (e.g., ImageReward) as input, and produces a single best-scoring generated image (or a small set of k₀ final survivors). All randomness is confined to the initial noise seed; the denoising solver is deterministic (DDIM η=0 or Euler Discrete), so the method is fully reproducible given the seed pool and pruning schedule. Intermediate scoring reuses the denoised estimate x̂₀ already computed inside the sampler, adding no extra generator forward passes.

## Method

Progressive Seed Pruning (PSP) begins with a large pool of k_T i.i.d. noise seeds (2N̄ for the default schedule), advances all survivors through each denoising step, scores their intermediate denoised estimates x̂₀ with the black-box reward, and prunes to the top-k_{t-1} survivors at predetermined steps before continuing. The total compute is fixed at C = Σk_t generator updates, giving an effective multiplier N̄ = C/T equivalent to N̄ full BoN trajectories. The key insight is that for prompt-alignment, salient features are coarse and largely fixed early in the trajectory, so spending more compute on evaluating a larger seed pool early and eliminating poor candidates before they consume the full denoising budget is more effective than maintaining constant parallelism or stochastic resampling. The default schedule (prune to N̄ at 25% progress, then to N̄/2 at 50%) is off-the-shelf, but because sampling is deterministic, alternative schedules can be searched offline from cached intermediate rewards without rerunning the generator.

## Results

Under a matched compute budget of N̄=4, PSP achieves the best prompt-alignment scores across all three backbones: GenEval 0.574 (SD v1.5), 0.645 (SDXL), and 0.747 (SD 3.5), and the best human-evaluation scores of 0.624, 0.713, and 0.841 respectively. PSP also attains the highest ImageReward guidance scores (0.827, 1.224, 1.380) and HPSv2 scores (0.278, 0.294, 0.306) among all compared methods. The method scales with compute: at N̄=16, PSP continues to improve and its regret relative to the 2N̄-seed upper bound decreases, indicating pruning becomes increasingly reliable at larger budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSP outperforms Best-of-N on GenEval (e.g., 0.574 vs 0.542 on SD v1.5; 0.645 vs 0.629 on SDXL) and human evaluation (0.624 vs 0.594; 0.713 vs 0.682) across all backbones, while also matching or exceeding BoN on ImageReward. Against FK-Steering (importance sampling), PSP wins on GenEval by 0.043 (SD v1.5), 0.018 (SDXL), and 0.005 (SD 3.5), and on human evaluation by 0.055, 0.037, and 0.004 respectively; FK-Steering's reliance on stochastic resampling particularly hurts it on the flow-matching SD 3.5 backbone where BoN actually beats FK-Steering. Against DSearch (tree search), PSP leads on GenEval by 0.068 (SD v1.5), 0.056 (SDXL), and 0.043 (SD 3.5), with the largest human-evaluation gap on SD v1.5 (0.624 vs 0.514). The one notable exception is BFS, which edges out PSP on ImageReward for SDXL (1.247 vs 1.224), yet PSP still surpasses BFS on GenEval (0.645 vs 0.636) and human evaluation, suggesting PSP is less susceptible to reward hacking. PSP also dominates NTS, RBF, and SVDD on every reported metric across all three backbones. In the DPO-finetuning comparison (Table 3), PSP on a non-finetuned SD v1.5 (IR 0.827, GenEval 0.574) exceeds standard sampling on a DPO-finetuned model (IR −0.022, GenEval 0.454), and combining PSP with DPO yields further gains (IR 0.907, GenEval 0.593).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of gradient-free, reward-guided inference-time scaling for diffusion models, including importance-sampling methods (FK-Steering, TDS, SVDD) and tree-search methods (DSearch, BFS), all of which maintain a constant number of parallel samples throughout denoising. It departs from this design by drawing on classical particle-filtering ideas (adaptive particle counts, KLD-sampling) and the Successive Halving principle from multi-armed bandit literature, applying them to progressively shrink the candidate pool of initial noise seeds. The key conceptual move is treating the constant-memory constraint as unnecessary and showing that front-loading exploration with a larger seed pool, then pruning on a fixed schedule, is more compute-efficient than maintaining a fixed population with stochastic resampling or branching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSP offers a simple, training-free, deterministic inference-time scaling strategy that is immediately deployable on multi-GPU servers with elastic memory, and it complements (rather than competes with) reward-based finetuning such as DPO. The finding that a fixed geometric-halving schedule captures nearly all the benefit of tuned schedules, and that intermediate reward rankings become reliable enough for safe pruning at modest compute multipliers, lowers the barrier to practical adoption. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-objective or spatially-constrained generation (where a single scalar reward is insufficient), and whether the advantage persists for objectives dominated by fine-grained late-stage detail (e.g., aesthetic quality) where early seed selection matters less.
