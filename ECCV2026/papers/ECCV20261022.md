## Problem

Unifying visual comprehension and image generation in a single model is non-trivial because the two tasks demand mismatched decoding regimes (autoregressive vs. diffusion) and conflicting visual representations (semantic-rich features for understanding vs. detail-preserving latents for generation). Prior unified multimodal models either separate these representations, fuse heterogeneous features with potential interference, or jointly optimize a shared tokenizer with multiple objectives, but the intrinsic optimization conflict between comprehension and generation remains insufficiently addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261022/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For understanding tasks, the model accepts a 512×512 RGB image plus a text instruction and produces a text response (caption, answer, etc.). For generation, it takes a text prompt and produces a 512×512 image via iterative flow-matching sampling in VAE latent space. A key architectural detail is that the LLM backbone operates on 4× spatially compressed tokens (h/2 × w/2) produced by a pixel-unshuffle operation, reducing the sequence length fed to the transformer while the full-resolution detail is recovered only at the generation head.

## Method

CHEERS decouples patch-level details from semantic representations through three components. First, a unified vision tokenizer passes VAE latents through a VAE decoder to reconstruct pixel space, then encodes the reconstruction with SigLIP2-ViT (16×16 patches) to extract semantic tokens, followed by pixel-unshuffle for 4× token compression before LLM conditioning. Second, a Qwen2.5-1.5B backbone applies bidirectional attention to visual tokens and causal attention to text, routing outputs to either an AR LM head (text) or a cascaded flow matching head (image). Third, the CFM head uses two stages (7+3 DiT blocks): the first generates low-resolution global semantics, then a pixel-shuffle upsamples to full resolution where a gating network adaptively injects high-frequency residuals from the vision tokenizer, with injection intensity dynamically coupled to the denoising timestep. Training follows a four-stage progressive pipeline (alignment, general pre-training, refined pre-training, SFT) using 83M total samples with a combined cross-entropy and flow-matching loss.

## Results

On GenEval, CHEERS (1.5B, 83M training samples) achieves an overall score of 0.78, surpassing Tar (0.76, 403M samples) and Harmon (0.76, 113M samples) while using only ~20% of Tar's training data. On DPG-Bench, CHEERS scores 83.48, outperforming Tar (82.96) and Janus-Pro (82.63) but trailing Show-o2 (85.02). On multimodal understanding, CHEERS leads among 1.5B UMMs on SEEDBench (71.7), MMStar (50.9), ChartQA (75.7), OCRBench (58.4), RealWorldQA (60.9), AI2D (74.4), and MathVista (50.5), though Janus-Pro retains an edge on MMBench (75.5 vs. 70.4). The model achieves a 4× token compression rate via pixel-unshuffle, enabling efficient high-resolution processing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHEERS outperforms Tar (0.76) and Harmon (0.76) on GenEval overall (0.78) while using only 83M training samples versus 403M and 113M respectively, demonstrating a ~5× data efficiency advantage. On DPG-Bench, CHEERS (83.48) beats Tar (82.96) and Janus-Pro (82.63) but falls short of Show-o2 (85.02), which used 177M samples. Among 1.5B unified models, CHEERS leads on most understanding benchmarks—SEEDBench (71.7 vs. 70.4 for Tar), MMStar (50.9 vs. 43.1 for Janus-Pro), ChartQA (75.7 vs. 23.4 for Janus-Pro), and AI2D (74.4 vs. 64.5 for Janus-Pro)—while Janus-Pro retains a 5.1-point edge on MMBench (75.5 vs. 70.4) and Tar leads on POPE (88.4 vs. 87.9). Against larger generation-only models, CHEERS surpasses SDXL (0.55) and DALL-E 3 (0.67) on GenEval but remains below SD3-Medium (0.74), which is a dedicated 2B diffusion model without an understanding component. The ablation in Table 5 confirms that the high-frequency injection (HFI) mechanism is critical for generation quality (GenEval 0.30 vs. 0.17 without HFI) while having negligible impact on understanding scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHEERS positions itself against three existing paradigms for unified multimodal models: separated visual spaces (Janus, Janus-Pro), single semantic-centric representations (TUNA), and fused heterogeneous features (Show-o2, Harmon). It departs from all three by repurposing pre-trained components—SigLIP2 for semantics and a VAE decoder for detail reconstruction—into a unified vision tokenizer, then injecting high-frequency patch details through a lightweight gating mechanism in a cascaded flow matching head. Rather than jointly training a new tokenizer from scratch (as TokenFlow or VQRAE do), CHEERS leverages existing encoders and adds only a pixel-unshuffle compression and a scalar gating network, framing the core contribution as decoupling patch-level details from semantic representations to resolve the optimization conflict between comprehension and generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the data efficiency: CHEERS matches or exceeds Tar (403M samples) on GenEval and MMBench using only 83M samples (~20% training cost), suggesting the decoupled representation design enables more effective knowledge transfer between understanding and generation. The 4× token compression via pixel-unshuffle also offers a concrete path toward efficient high-resolution multimodal processing. However, the 1.5B parameter scale, lack of initialization from large pre-trained VLMs, and single-image-only training limit generalization; open questions include whether the decoupling strategy scales to video, larger backbones, and more diverse multimodal data, and whether the emergent zero-shot editing capability generalizes beyond the tested scenarios.
