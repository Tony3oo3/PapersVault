## Problem

Existing Joint-Embedding Architectures for self-supervised learning prevent representation collapse through batch-level interactions (in-batch negatives or batch statistics), making them unreliable when batch sizes shrink to 1–2 as required by memory-constrained high-dimensional data such as 3D medical volumes. This creates a paradox: high-dimensional scientific data demands robust anti-collapse regularization, yet its memory footprint forces the small batches where batch-dependent regularization fails.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D medical images (224×224) or 3D medical volumes (64³ voxels) from the MedMNIST+ benchmark, generating V augmented views per instance during training. It outputs backbone feature representations directly on the unit hypersphere without a projector head, eliminating the ambiguity of choosing between projected and backbone features at evaluation. At inference, the encoder operates as a standard feature extractor requiring no instance identifiers; the auxiliary embedding table is purely a training-time construct.

## Method

IConE decouples collapse prevention from the training batch by introducing a persistent, learnable embedding table E ∈ R^{N×d} where each row serves as a geometric anchor for its corresponding instance. The total loss has three terms: a view-view consistency term (L_vv) that pulls augmented views of the same instance together, a view-instance alignment term (L_vi) that anchors encoder outputs to their corresponding table entries via cosine similarity, and an instance diversity term (L_div) that penalizes positive pairwise correlations across the full N×N Gram matrix of normalized embeddings using a squared hinge loss. Critically, the encoder receives gradients only from attractive forces (L_vv and L_vi), while all repulsive pressure is handled by the embedding table update, making the anti-collapse mechanism mathematically independent of batch composition. This eliminates the need for in-batch negatives, temperature scaling, momentum encoders, or batch statistics, enabling stable training from batch size 1 to 64.

## Results

On 3D biomedical datasets, IConE achieves 63.6% k-NN-1 balanced accuracy at B=4, outperforming the strongest baseline (BYOL at 54.6%) by ~9 points, and maintains 62.5% even at B=64. In the linear-probe evaluation (Fig. 2), IConE exhibits a flat performance curve from B=1 to B=64, while all baselines degrade characteristically as batch size shrinks. In the supervised setting, IConE-Class reaches 82.31% top-1 balanced accuracy at B=64 on 2D datasets, surpassing standard Cross-Entropy (80.32%) and SupCon (79.73%). Geometric analysis confirms IConE preserves higher effective rank and intrinsic dimensionality (RankMe, LiDAR) than all baselines across the entire batch-size range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IConE outperforms all seven SSL baselines across the full small-batch range (B=1 to 64) on both 2D and 3D biomedical datasets. The largest margins appear at small batch sizes: at B=4 on 3D data, IConE leads VICReg (the strongest non-contrastive baseline) by 12.8 points in kNN-1 accuracy (63.6% vs 50.8%) and SimCLR by 13.7 points. VICReg is the closest competitor at B=64 (59.1% vs 62.5%), but it degrades sharply below B=16, while IConE remains stable. DIET, which shares IConE's parametric instance-discrimination philosophy, consistently trails by 5–8 points, confirming that the explicit geometric regularizer and view-view consistency term provide meaningful gains over DIET's implicit softmax competition. In the supervised extension, IConE-Class surpasses both SupCon and standard Cross-Entropy at every batch size, with the gap over SupCon widening at small batches (e.g., 78.71% vs 72.20% at B=1 on 2D), demonstrating that the parametric anchoring mechanism eliminates SupCon's inherited batch dependency.

## Relation to Prior Work

IConE builds on Parametric Instance Discrimination (PID) and its recent revival in DIET, but departs in two key ways: it adds an explicit view-view consistency loss (rather than relying on implicit alignment through shared instance labels) and replaces the softmax-based separation with a direct geometric orthogonality regularizer on a persistent embedding table. Conceptually, the paper's central move is to decouple the anti-collapse mechanism from the transient training batch entirely, transferring it to a dataset-level parameter space — a structural departure from both contrastive methods (SimCLR, MoCo) that require in-batch negatives and non-contrastive methods (VICReg, BYOL, DINO) that depend on batch statistics. The modular design also positions IConE as complementary to, rather than competitive with, existing statistical regularizers like VICReg or SIGReg, which can substitute the O(N²) diversity term for larger-scale settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: high-dimensional scientific data (3D CT/MRI volumes, video sequences) forces batch sizes of 1–2 due to GPU memory, a regime where virtually all existing SSL methods degrade or fail. The clean conceptual separation of alignment (encoder space) from uniformity (persistent embedding space) provides a principled, minimal framework that practitioners in biomedical and scientific imaging can adopt without architectural changes. The O(N²) diversity loss remains a scalability ceiling for very large datasets, though the authors note the modularity allows substitution with O(N·d²) statistical regularizers. Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly massive datasets (N > 100k), whether the instance-level anchors generalize to streaming or online settings, and whether the supervised extension (IConE-Class beating Cross-Entropy) holds at scale beyond the small-data regimes tested here.
