## Problem

Aerial-ground person re-identification suffers from a drastic cross-view feature gap caused by severe viewpoint discrepancies between UAV and ground cameras. Existing AG-ReID methods attempt to bridge this gap by learning direct global semantic correspondences through auxiliary components (attributes, view tokens, or prompts), which overlooks the underlying spatial feature-space misalignment, limits training effectiveness, and increases inference-time computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261629/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB person images from aerial (UAV, 15–75 m altitude) and ground (CCTV) cameras, resized to 256×128 pixels and processed by a ViT-Base backbone with 16×16 patches, producing a class-token embedding F ∈ ℝ^C for identity matching. A key design choice is that the DHT module, CVL losses, and VDCL memory bank are used exclusively during training; at inference, only the standard ViT backbone runs, yielding the same FLOPs and latency (3.7 ms/image) as the unmodified ViT baseline.

## Method

MCVL bridges the aerial-ground gap through multi-space cross-view learning across both spatial and global feature spaces. The Deformable Homography Transformation (DHT) module estimates a 3×3 homography matrix via a lightweight regression head and augments it with a learnable deformable offset field (P=4 candidate offsets weighted by softmax attention), enabling both coarse planar alignment and fine-grained local correction when projecting features between aerial and ground planes. The Cross-View Learning (CVL) strategy then enforces identity consistency via SmoothL1 between original and projected features (CICL) and perspective invariance via a margin-based contrastive loss on projected features (CVCL). In the global feature space, the View Decorrelation Contrastive Loss (VDCL) maintains a momentum-updated memory bank of view-specific prototypes, pushing each sample's global feature away from its own view prototype while pulling same-identity features together via InfoNCE, thereby suppressing view-specific bias. The overall objective combines standard Identity and Triplet losses with CVL and VDCL terms, and all cross-view components are discarded at test time.

## Results

On the CARGO dataset (108K images, 5K identities), MCVL achieves 70.51% Rank-1 and 62.92% mAP under the ALL protocol, and 60.62% Rank-1 / 53.74% mAP under the most challenging A↔G cross-view protocol, outperforming the previous best (SeCap) by +1.92% and +2.49% Rank-1 respectively. On the largest aerial-ground benchmark G2APS-ReID (200K images), MCVL reaches 78.87% Rank-1 / 62.95% mAP (A→G), surpassing SeCap by +3.56% Rank-1. On AG-ReIDv1, MCVL attains 87.29% Rank-1 / 78.97% mAP (G→A), setting a new state of the art across all five evaluated datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCVL outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the CARGO A↔G protocol (the hardest cross-view setting), achieving 60.62% Rank-1 vs. SeCap (58.13%, +2.49%), VIF (51.25%, +9.37%), and VDT (48.12%, +12.50%). On the G2APS-ReID A→G protocol, MCVL leads SeCap by +3.56% Rank-1 (78.87% vs. 75.31%) and VDT by +5.82% (78.87% vs. 73.05%). On AG-ReIDv1 G→A, MCVL edges out SeCap by +0.28% Rank-1 (87.29% vs. 87.01%) and VDT by +0.70% (87.29% vs. 86.59%), though SeCap retains a marginal 0.26% mAP advantage on A→G (76.16% vs. 75.90%). General Re-ID baselines (SBS, PCB, BoT, MGN, ViT) underperform substantially in cross-view protocols, with ViT achieving only 43.13% Rank-1 on CARGO A↔G versus MCVL's 60.62%, confirming that standard person Re-ID methods cannot handle the aerial-ground viewpoint gap. MCVL also matches or exceeds SeCap on LAGPeR G→A+G (25.18% vs. 24.39% Rank-1) while using 1.89× fewer FLOPs and 2.02× faster inference (3.7 ms vs. 7.5 ms per image).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior AG-ReID methods (VDT, SeCap, VIF, Explain) that bridge the aerial-ground gap via auxiliary components—view tokens, recalibrated prompts, or attribute streams—by instead constructing an explicit geometric cross-view projection. The Deformable Homography Transformation module repurposes classical homography estimation (previously applied in multi-view detection and pedestrian detection) and augments it with learnable deformable offsets, creating an intermediate spatial feature space that prior AG-ReID work had not exploited. The View Decorrelation Contrastive Loss combines a view-prototype repulsion term with standard InfoNCE contrastive learning, a combination not previously applied to cross-view Re-ID. All components are training-only, eliminating the inference overhead that plagues prompt- and decoder-based competitors like SeCap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AG-ReID is a practically important task as UAV surveillance becomes integrated with fixed ground-camera networks, and this paper demonstrates that geometric cross-view alignment can outperform semantic-auxiliary approaches while remaining inference-efficient (same FLOPs and speed as the ViT backbone). The cross-dataset transfer results (e.g., +13.5% Rank-1 over SeCap on LAGPeR→AG-ReIDv1) suggest the learned view-invariant representations generalize beyond the training domain. Open questions include whether the DHT module scales to highly non-planar or occluded scenes, whether the approach extends to multi-aerial or multi-ground camera configurations, and how performance degrades under extreme altitude or lighting variations not well represented in current benchmarks.
