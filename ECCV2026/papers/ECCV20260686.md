## Problem

Existing action quality assessment models achieve strong score prediction but function as opaque black boxes that cannot reveal the step-by-step visual reasoning behind their judgments. This lack of interpretability is a critical barrier in high-stakes domains like surgery and elite sports, where understanding *why* a particular assessment is made is as important as the assessment itself. The paper tackles the problem of simultaneously producing accurate skill scores and exposing the latent visual reasoning trajectory that leads to those scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260686/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video of T frames, per-frame 3D body keypoints (or surgical instrument keypoints), and expert commentary text that is encoded by a frozen text encoder to define the target semantic distribution. It produces a continuous performance score, a per-timestamp keypoint attention pattern (color-coded by selection frequency in MCTS), and a 2D-projected latent reasoning trajectory showing progressive denoising from a noisy initial state to the target semantic state. The MCTS tree is domain-specific: four joint nodes (elbow, shoulder, hip, knee) for sports and three instrument-part nodes (tip, body, tail) for surgery, with finite depth K=4 or K=3 respectively.

## Method

LVDR formulates visual reasoning as a DDIM-based diffusion process in a latent space: a Reasoning State Learner maps each frame and its 3D keypoints to an initial embedding P_i, and a shared-weight DiT denoiser (12 layers, 16 heads, 512-dim) autoregressively refines P̂_i = θ_D(P̂_{i-1}, P_i) over temporal steps, treating the first frame as a noisy initialization that is progressively denoised toward a target distribution derived from expert commentary. The score is regressed from the final denoised embedding via a linear head, trained jointly with the diffusion noise-prediction loss (L = L_DM + L_score). For interpretability, a keypoint-guided MCTS with UCT selection (C=√2) is run per frame over a finite-depth tree whose nodes encode joint angle, speed, and position; the reward is cosine similarity between a path's keypoint embedding and a pre-computed target embedding, and the highest-reward path yields the explicit visual grounding. The visual and text backbones are frozen InternVideo2.5 encoders, and all experiments run on a single NVIDIA L40S GPU.

## Results

On EgoExo4D, LVDR achieves a Spearman rank correlation (ρ) of 0.88 and R-ℓ₂ of 1.69, outperforming the strongest baseline MAGR (ρ=0.73, R-ℓ₂=10.14) and the best VLM Gemini 2.5 (ρ=0.61, R-ℓ₂=5.57). On JIGSAWS, LVDR reaches ρ=0.69 and R-ℓ₂=9.93, surpassing MAGR++ (ρ=0.55). On FitnessAQA, LVDR attains F1 scores of 0.747 (OHP Knee), 0.818 (OHP Elbow), 0.813 (Squat Forward), and 0.920 (Squat Inward), all leading the competition. On Cataract-101, LVDR achieves 71% accuracy versus 62% for the best baseline (MAGR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LVDR outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset. Against the strongest domain-specific method MAGR [33], LVDR improves ρ from 0.73 to 0.88 on EgoExo4D and from 0.48 to 0.69 on JIGSAWS, while also reducing R-ℓ₂ from 10.14 to 1.69 and 10.60 to 9.93 respectively. The closest VLM competitor, Gemini 2.5 [8], achieves ρ=0.61 on EgoExo4D and ρ=0.64 on Soccer, both substantially below LVDR's 0.88 and 0.94. GPT-4o [15] and Qwen-2.5-VL-7B [3] show particularly weak performance on surgical tasks (JIGSAWS ρ=0.09 and 0.03), highlighting the difficulty of fine-grained skill assessment for general-purpose VLMs. LLaVA-Video [29] and FineParser [27] trail significantly on all benchmarks, with LLaVA-Video achieving only ρ=0.21 on EgoExo4D. Notably, even LVDR without the diffusion module (Ours ΔDiffusion) already surpasses all baselines, indicating that the keypoint-guided MCTS and latent reasoning space provide a strong foundation, while the diffusion component adds a further 0.05 ρ improvement on EgoExo4D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDIM-based diffusion modeling and repurposes it for a fundamentally different purpose: rather than generating images or text, the diffusion process models the progressive refinement of a latent visual reasoning state across video frames. It departs from prior interpretable-AQA work (NS-AQA, IRIS, Interpretability-AQA) that relies on human-defined rules, scoring rubrics, or attention-weight explanations, by instead learning a data-driven sequential reasoning trajectory and extracting explicit keypoint-level groundings via Monte Carlo Tree Search. The MCTS component draws on the standard UCT formulation but is instantiated over a domain-specific keypoint action space (joints for sports, instrument parts for surgery), making the search structure task-aware rather than generic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in AQA: existing high-performing models are opaque, and prior interpretability attempts are rule-based or segment-level rather than step-by-step. The keypoint masking validation (substantial performance drop when masking MCTS-selected keypoints vs. negligible drop for unselected ones) provides concrete evidence that the extracted reasoning is causally relevant, not merely post-hoc. Limitations include reliance on expert commentary text to define the target semantic distribution, single-person scenarios, and added inference latency from MCTS iterations. Open questions include whether the learned reasoning trajectories generalize to multi-person interactions, whether the diffusion formulation is truly necessary versus simpler sequential models, and whether the interpretability holds under distribution shift.
