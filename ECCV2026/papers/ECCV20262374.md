## Problem

State-of-the-art monocular RGB normal estimators systematically fail on reflective, textureless, and low-albedo surfaces where photometric cues are ambiguous, yet existing shape-from-polarization methods require either multi-view capture or expensive paired polarization-normal training data, limiting generalization. No prior approach leverages the strong geometric priors already embedded in modern RGB backbones while incorporating physics-based polarization constraints at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262374/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-shot 4-angle linear polarization capture (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) as its sole physical input; the RGB image fed to the backbone is derived directly from the unpolarized intensity S₀ of the Stokes vector, so no separate RGB camera is needed. At 768×768 resolution, the pipeline outputs a refined per-pixel surface normal map along with a learned specular radiance map L_s and the corresponding diffuse radiance L_d = S₀ − L_s, enabling a physics-based appearance decomposition. Polarization data is required only at test time—the backbone itself is trained exclusively on RGB-normal pairs.

## Method

Poppy is a training-free, plug-and-play guidance framework that keeps any differentiable RGB normal backbone (diffusion, flow-matching, or feed-forward) fully frozen and introduces three lightweight learnable parameters optimized at test time: a per-pixel image offset O_x added to the input, a per-pixel normal offset O_n added to the output, and a per-pixel specular radiance map L_s. A differentiable rendering layer converts the refined normal n̂ = f(x+O_x)+O_n and L_s into a predicted Stokes vector via the Fresnel equations, and an L1 polarization-consistency loss (masked to valid, non-saturated pixels) drives joint optimization over 100 Adam steps. A staged schedule first optimizes L_s and O_x for 50 steps to achieve global geometric plausibility, then activates O_n to recover high-frequency surface detail the backbone over-smooths. The image offset exploits the observation that small input perturbations propagate globally through the backbone (verified via Jacobian magnitude maps), making it an efficient mechanism for scene-wide steering, while the normal offset handles local corrections. The learned L_s simultaneously resolves the diffuse-specular (π/2) ambiguity inherent in single-view polarization, and the RGB backbone's orientation prior resolves the azimuthal (π) ambiguity.

## Results

Across seven benchmarks (SfPUEL, NeRSP, NeISF, DeepPol, PISR) and three backbone architectures, Poppy reduces mean angular error (MAE) by 23–26% on synthetic data and 6–16% on real data. The best configuration (MoGe-2 + Poppy) achieves MAE of 12.26° (real) and 10.89° (synthetic), outperforming all RGB-only and polarization-only baselines. Accuracy at the 11.25° threshold (Acc11.25) improves by 37–87% on synthetic data, indicating substantial recovery of fine geometric detail. RMSE reductions of 6–18% confirm gains in both global orientation and local surface detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Poppy + MoGe-2 (MAE 12.26° real, 10.89° synthetic) outperforms all seven baselines on both splits. Against the strongest RGB-only baseline MoGe-2 alone, Poppy reduces MAE by 6.4% (real) and 22.9% (synthetic), with Acc11.25 jumping from 0.51 to 0.70 on synthetic data. Against the polarization-only baseline SfPUEL, Poppy + MoGe-2 improves MAE by 40.7% (real) and 36.1% (synthetic), demonstrating that test-time polarization guidance on a strong RGB backbone far exceeds dedicated polarization training. Against diffusion-based baselines (Marigold, Lotus, StableNormal), Poppy + MoGe-2 leads by 2–11° in MAE on real data and 4–10° on synthetic. The only exception noted is MoGe-2 + Poppy on the PISR real dataset, where a large perspective distortion not modeled by the orthographic assumption causes a slight regression (13.54° vs. 12.82° backbone-only).

## Relation to Prior Work

Poppy sits at the intersection of monocular normal estimation (Marigold, Lotus-v2, MoGe-2, DSINE) and shape from polarization (SfPUEL, DeepSfP, NeRSP, PISR), but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than training a dedicated polarization-to-normal model or requiring multi-view capture to resolve SfP ambiguities, it treats polarization as a test-time physics constraint that steers a frozen RGB backbone. Conceptually, it extends the test-time guidance paradigm established for depth completion (Marigold-DC) and diffusion posterior sampling to the normal-estimation setting, replacing sparse depth measurements with dense polarization signals governed by Fresnel equations. The plug-and-play design—learnable per-pixel input and output offsets plus a specular radiance map, all external to the backbone—distinguishes it from prior polarization methods (SfPUEL, PPFT) that require polarimetric training data or weight modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Poppy addresses a concrete and recurring failure mode of RGB-based normal estimators (reflective, textureless, low-albedo surfaces) with a training-free mechanism that can be attached to any future backbone without retraining, making it a practical tool for robotics and AR pipelines where polarization cameras are increasingly available. The learned specular radiance map also yields a physics-based diffuse/specular decomposition as a byproduct, enabling appearance editing and downstream mesh reconstruction improvements (~6% Chamfer distance gain). Key limitations include the 57–173 s per-image optimization cost, the orthographic projection assumption that fails under strong perspective distortion, and the modest real-data gains (6–16% MAE) where sensor noise partially offsets the normal-offset refinement. Open questions include whether faster optimization or neural adapters can reduce the compute overhead, and whether the framework extends to transparent or translucent materials with complex refractive indices.
