## Problem

Scaling Vision Transformers in width (hidden dimension) frequently triggers severe training instability and optimization collapse, even when depth scaling remains stable. The paper identifies the root cause as 'high-activation domination': in high-dimensional feature vectors, a small number of outlier elements disproportionately determine the global normalization statistic (RMS or variance), suppressing the remaining dimensions toward zero and causing gradient vanishing, especially under low-precision (bfloat16) training.

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in replacement for the normalization layer inside existing ViT and DiT blocks, operating on token embeddings of shape (B, T, D) where D is the hidden dimension. For classification, inputs are 192×192 or 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K producing class labels; for segmentation, 512×512 images from ADE20K produce pixel-wise masks via UperNet; for generation, 256×256 class-conditional images are produced by DiT. The key structural constraint is that D must be divisible by the chosen number of heads H, with a default per-head chunk size of d=256 (e.g., width 1024 → 4 heads, width 4096 → 16 heads).

## Method

MH-RMSNorm partitions a D-dimensional token embedding into H equal-sized sub-vectors (aligned with attention heads, each of dimension d = D/H) and computes the RMS statistic independently within each sub-vector before normalizing, rather than computing a single global RMS over all D dimensions. The theoretical analysis shows that in D dimensions the maximum activation scales as σ√(2 log D) while the RMS converges to σ, so the domination ratio grows with D; by restricting normalization to d-dimensional chunks, this ratio is reduced to √(2 log d), localizing the influence of any single outlier. Each head also receives its own learnable affine scale γ^(h), allowing the model to adapt to heterogeneous activation scales across subspaces. The method introduces no additional parameters beyond the per-head scales and no extra FLOPs, making it a zero-cost structural modification. Ablations confirm that the benefit is not merely from having more normalization groups but specifically from localizing the RMS statistic, and that applying MH-RMSNorm throughout the network (with deeper layers being most critical) yields the strongest gains.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, MH-RMSNorm achieves 83.9%, 84.7%, and 85.5% top-1 accuracy for ViT-B (768-d), ViT-L (1024-d), and ViT-H (1280-d) respectively, consistently outperforming both LayerNorm and RMSNorm with gains that grow with width (e.g., +0.3% over RMSNorm at ViT-H). On ADE20K segmentation, MH-RMSNorm reaches 51.0 mIoU on ViT-H, a +0.4 point improvement over RMSNorm. For ImageNet-256 generation with DiT-XL/2 (7M steps), MH-RMSNorm reduces FID from 2.20 (RMSNorm) to 1.95 while improving recall from 0.57 to 0.59. In the width-scaling stress test, MH-RMSNorm maintains 84.4% top-1 at width 4096 where both LayerNorm and RMSNorm collapse to ~61%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MH-RMSNorm against two global normalization baselines: LayerNorm [Ba et al., 2016] and RMSNorm [Zhang & Sennrich, 2019]. MH-RMSNorm wins on every configuration tested. On ImageNet-1K, the top-1 gap over RMSNorm grows from +0.1% (ViT-B) to +0.2% (ViT-L) to +0.3% (ViT-H), and the mIoU gap on ADE20K widens similarly from +0.2 to +0.4 points, confirming that the benefit scales with feature dimensionality. On ImageNet-256 generation (DiT-XL/2, 7M steps), MH-RMSNorm achieves FID 1.95 versus 2.20 for RMSNorm and 2.27 for LayerNorm, a 0.25 FID improvement over the stronger baseline. In the extreme width-scaling regime (width 4096, depth 12), the advantage becomes dramatic: MH-RMSNorm reaches 84.4% top-1 while both LayerNorm (60.5%) and RMSNorm (61.1%) suffer near-complete training collapse, and FID drops from ~82–88 (global norms) to 35.5 (MH-RMSNorm). The consistent pattern across discriminative and generative tasks indicates the improvement is rooted in normalization dynamics rather than task-specific effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the normalization design space in Vision Transformers by proposing Multi-Head RMSNorm (MH-RMSNorm), which partitions the feature dimension into head-aligned chunks and computes RMS statistics independently per chunk. It builds directly on RMSNorm [41] and LayerNorm [1], and is conceptually related to GroupNorm's channel-group decoupling and NormFormer's extra normalization, but targets the specific failure mode of global-statistic domination in high-dimensional spaces. Unlike prior deep-ViT stabilization work (CaiT's LayerScale, DeepNet's DeepNorm, ReZero) that modifies residual connections or attention, this paper isolates the normalization statistic itself as the bottleneck, drawing on extreme-value-theory arguments and observations of massive activations in LLMs [6, 25].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear mechanistic diagnosis—few outlier activations dominate global normalization in high-dimensional ViTs, suppressing most dimensions and attenuating gradients—backed by both theoretical lemmas and visual evidence. The practical implication is that width scaling, long considered a harder axis than depth scaling, becomes viable up to 4096 dimensions with a zero-cost drop-in replacement, which could reshape how future wide ViTs and diffusion backbones are designed. Open questions include whether the optimal chunk size (d=256 here) generalizes across architectures and tasks, how MH-RMSNorm interacts with other stabilization techniques (LayerScale, DropPath), and whether the same diagnosis applies to other high-dimensional normalizers in non-ViT settings such as large language models.
