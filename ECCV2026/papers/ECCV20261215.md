## Problem

When text-conditioned diffusion models perform super-resolution via latent tiling at high resolutions, a single global caption is reused for all tiles, causing prompt misguidance: the prompt omits tile-specific details (errors of omission) and injects irrelevant semantics from other regions (errors of commission). This semantic misalignment degrades reconstruction quality, particularly at extreme resolutions where hundreds of tiles are processed, and is further exacerbated in video by the additional temporal axis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution image (512×512, producing 2048×2048 after 4× magnification) or a low-resolution video sequence, splits it into latent tiles (64×64 spatial tiles with 16-pixel overlap for images; 32×720×1280 spatio-temporal blocks for video), and produces a corresponding high-resolution output. No retraining of the underlying SR model is required; the framework operates entirely at inference time as a drop-in replacement for the global prompt, adding only ~2–6% overhead from VLM prompt extraction.

## Method

Tiled Prompts replaces the single global text condition with a tile-specific prompt c_local^(i) generated by a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B for images, Qwen3-VL-8B for video) conditioned on each local LR tile, so that each tile's super-resolution is guided by locally relevant semantics. For video, the VLM additionally receives the full LR sequence as global context to capture temporal dynamics (e.g., motion descriptions) that a small spatial crop alone cannot convey. Theoretically, the paper formalizes prompt misguidance δ_i(c) as the deviation of the CFG guidance direction from the ideal local-prompt direction, proves it is expressible via a score-function difference (Lemma 1), and establishes an information-theoretic bound (Proposition 1) showing that the mutual information gap between the ideal and actual prompt lower-bounds the accumulated misguidance integral. Proposition 2 then shows that tile-specific prompts provably reduce this gap relative to a global prompt, thereby lowering the theoretical floor of misguidance and allowing the generative trajectory to converge to accurate high-frequency details.

## Results

On image SR (4× upscaling to 2048×2048 with 25 latent tiles), tiled (local) prompts consistently outperform the global-prompt baseline across all three datasets: on LSDIR1K, local prompts reduce NIQE from 2.94 to 2.90 and boost CLIP Score from 25.33 to 27.23; on URBAN100, NIQE drops from 3.62 to 3.50 and HPSv2 rises from 0.178 to 0.209. For video SR, gains are even more pronounced in text-alignment: VQAScore jumps from 0.399 to 0.554 on VideoLQ and from 0.557 to 0.746 on MVSR4x, while DOVER improves from 53.14 to 58.35 on MVSR. Reference-based metrics confirm fidelity gains (e.g., FID drops from 17.09 to 16.42 on LSDIR2048), and the added VLM prompt-extraction overhead is negligible (~2% for DiT4SR, ~6% for STAR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other SR architectures; instead, it ablates the prompt strategy (Null, Global, Global+Local, Local) on the same DiT4SR and STAR backbones. Local (tiled) prompts win on nearly every metric: on LSDIR1K they improve CLIP Score by +1.89, ImageReward by +0.91, and HPSv2 by +0.042 over the global baseline; on MVSR4x video, DOVER improves by +5.21 and VQAScore by +0.189. Notably, Local alone outperforms Global+Local on most quality and alignment metrics (e.g., NIQE 2.90 vs 2.94 on LSDIR1K; VQAScore 0.554 vs 0.444 on VideoLQ), indicating that irrelevant global tokens actively harm reconstruction rather than merely being neutral. The only metrics where Global+Local edges out Local are MANIQA and CLIPIQA on some datasets, suggesting a small perceptual trade-off from removing global context. Overall, the results confirm that prompt relevance, not prompt length, drives SR quality under latent tiling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of text-conditioned diffusion-based super-resolution (DiT4SR, SUPIR, SeeSR) and video super-resolution (STAR, SeedVR), all of which rely on a single global text prompt during latent tiling. While prior work has begun incorporating VLMs for prompt extraction (SUPIR and DiT4SR use LLaVA), they do so only at the global level; this paper repurposes VLMs to generate tile-specific prompts, introducing the concept of 'prompt misguidance' as a formal failure mode. The theoretical contribution—an information-theoretic bound linking mutual information gaps to score-function-level misguidance (Propositions 1–2)—is new and provides a principled justification for why localized conditioning reduces the KL divergence between the ideal and actual posterior. The method is entirely inference-time and plug-and-play, requiring no retraining of the SR backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~2–6% increase in inference time yields consistent gains in perceptual quality, fidelity, and image/video-text alignment across both SISR and VSR, making it an easy drop-in improvement for existing tiled SR pipelines. The finding that local-only prompts outperform global+local concatenation challenges the assumption that more text tokens always help, suggesting that semantic relevance and specificity matter more than coverage. Key limitations include heavy dependence on VLM quality (hallucinated or misinterpreted local descriptions could introduce new errors) and the fact that the theoretical bound, while elegant, is not directly optimizable in practice. Open questions include whether the VLM can be distilled into a lighter prompt extractor, whether the approach extends to other tiled generative tasks beyond SR, and whether the gains persist at even higher resolutions (e.g., 8K) where tile counts grow substantially.
