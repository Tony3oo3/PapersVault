## Problem

Existing training-free visual token pruning methods (Visual Redundancy, Visual Saliency, Query Relevance) are designed for VideoQA and fail on Video Temporal Grounding because VTG requires boundary-sensitive evidence and cross-frame reasoning chains that these objectives destroy. The paper identifies two missing VTG-specific pruning principles—Evidence Retention (preserving query-critical patches near event boundaries) and Connectivity Strength (maintaining cross-frame relay tokens for multi-hop attention propagation)—and shows that naively transferring VideoQA pruning causes drastic mIoU degradation.

## Input / Output

SemVID takes a video tokenized into T frames × P patch tokens (V_patch ∈ R^{T×P×D}) plus a text query (Q ∈ R^{N×D}) and produces a pruned visual token subset V′ of size r·(TP) at a user-specified retention ratio r (e.g., 12.5% or 25%). The pruned tokens are then fed to a frozen VLM (Qwen3-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, or LLaVA-OneVision) which outputs start/end timestamps. The method is entirely training-free and plug-and-play, requiring only lightweight feature-similarity and temporal-difference computations—no cross-attention materialization—making it practical for long videos where full attention maps cause OOM.

## Method

SemVID operates in two stages. First, frame-level budget allocation assigns per-frame token quotas by blending a query-relevance score (cosine similarity between frame-mean and query features) with an inter-frame variation score (L2 distance between consecutive frame means), with a per-frame floor k_ctx to prevent token-empty gaps. Second, within each frame, tokens are selected into three semantic roles: (i) object tokens (α·k^(t) slots) chosen via Maximal Marginal Relevance to retain diverse query-aligned evidence; (ii) motion tokens ((1−α)·k^(t)−k_ctx slots) scored by a query-aware temporal difference (fusing patch-level L2 change with max query similarity) to capture meaningful transitions as cross-frame relays; and (iii) context tokens (k_ctx slots) consisting of a proto token most similar to the frame mean plus top-k saliency anchors for scene continuity. The key insight is that VTG accuracy depends not just on retaining evidence but on keeping it temporally connected—motion tokens serve as relay nodes that preserve multi-hop attention paths across frames, while MMR on object tokens prevents redundant selection of the same region.

## Results

On ActivityNet-Grounding with Qwen3-VL-4B at 12.5% token retention, SemVID retains 95.4% of the original mIoU (38.49 vs. 40.33) and 89.0% on Charades-STA (49.89 vs. 56.07), while delivering a 5.8× prefill speedup (1263.4 ms → 217.7 ms). At 25% retention, SemVID approaches near-lossless compression, retaining up to 96.9% mIoU on ActivityNet and 93.3% on Charades-STA. The same trend holds for Qwen3-VL-8B (92.6% / 88.1% at 12.5%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (77.4% / 64.3% at 12.5%), where SemVID consistently outperforms all baselines under identical budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemVID consistently outperforms all seven baselines on both ActivityNet-Grounding and Charades-STA across all model sizes and retention ratios. Against the strongest baseline FastVID (saliency-driven), SemVID gains +13.2 percentage points in mIoU retention on ActivityNet at 12.5% (95.4% vs. 82.2%) and +24.8 points on Charades-STA (89.0% vs. 64.2%), with the gap widening for the 8B model. VisionZip (redundancy-driven) suffers the largest degradation, retaining only 49.3% mIoU on ActivityNet at 12.5% for the 4B model and a mere 20.8% for the 8B model, because its query-agnostic merging suppresses boundary-critical evidence and removes intermediate relay tokens. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Table 2), SemVID leads the next-best method (VisionZip, 73.3% mIoU retention) by 4.1 points on ActivityNet and 5.4 points on Charades-STA, while FastV and VScan are entirely non-deployable due to out-of-memory errors from materializing full attention matrices. The paper's attention-graph diagnostics (ER and CS) explain the gaps: SemVID achieves the highest ER and CS simultaneously, whereas FastVID preserves CS but sacrifices ER by concentrating tokens on few anchor frames, and VisionZip degrades both metrics by over-merging temporally adjacent states.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the general training-free token pruning toolkit for VLMs (VisionZip, FastVID, ToMe, TokenSculpt, PruneVid) by identifying that their objectives—visual redundancy, saliency, and query relevance—were designed for VideoQA and fail on Video Temporal Grounding. Rather than building on a single prior method, it introduces a task-specific diagnostic lens (Evidence Retention and Connectivity Strength) grounded in attention-graph message-passing, and repurposes Maximal Marginal Relevance from information retrieval to enforce diversity in object-token selection. The role-aware decomposition into object, motion, and context tokens is a novel synthesis that no prior pruning method for VLMs has attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.8× prefill speedup at 95.4% mIoU retention makes long-video VTG with VLMs practically deployable, and the ER/CS metrics offer a reusable diagnostic for evaluating any pruning strategy's impact on temporal reasoning. A key practical finding is that RoPE-based models (Qwen2.5-VL) are more sensitive to irregular token removal than models with explicit timestamp tokens (Qwen3-VL), which has implications for how practitioners choose architectures for pruning-friendly deployment. Open questions include more robust motion identification (current proxies are biased by camera jitter) and whether the evidence-chain framing generalizes to other temporal reasoning tasks beyond VTG.
