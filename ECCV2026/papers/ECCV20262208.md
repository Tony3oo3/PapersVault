## Problem

State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models achieve near-saturated success rates on standard robotic manipulation benchmarks, but it remains unclear whether these scores reflect genuine embodied reasoning or merely the exploitation of shortcut correlations memorized from static training distributions. Existing robustness benchmarks (e.g., LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-Pro) apply surface-level perturbations but cannot systematically manipulate task semantics, causal logic, or interaction structure to isolate high-level cognitive failures from low-level execution errors.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds standard RGB images and text instructions to VLA policies, but the critical distinction is in the evaluation protocol: at test time, controlled causal interventions (spatial layout shifts, primitive recompositions, adversarial object swaps, temporal extrapolations) are applied while enforcing kinematic isolation so that all required motions remain within the policy's learned reachable space. Beyond binary success rates, the system logs privileged per-timestep states (depth maps, 2D/3D bounding boxes, segmentation masks) and computes structured diagnostic metrics including failure-mode classification (lexical-kinematic shortcut, semantic feature collapse, behavioral inertia, causal state-tracking failure) and Distractor Grasp Rate (DGR) to quantify invalid object interactions.

## Method

BeTTER is a template-driven diagnostic benchmark that generates 60 controlled task variations from 10 base manipulation tasks by combining VLM-instantiated task templates, open-vocabulary 3D asset retrieval from Objaverse, and procedural trajectory amplification via MimicGen. The core mechanism is a set of four targeted causal interventions—spatial layout shift, primitive recomposition, adversarial object perturbation, and temporal extrapolation—applied under strict kinematic isolation to decouple reasoning failures from execution errors. The paper further provides a mechanistic diagnosis through ablation studies on the InternVL-3.5 backbone, showing that capacity compression (8B→2B) and single low-resolution (224×224) perception collapse semantic grounding and planning scores, while behavioral cloning under static distributions amplifies non-causal correlations into shortcut policies. Real-world validation on a SO101 robot confirms that the identified reasoning failures (behavioral inertia, phase conflict, causal state-tracking breakdown) persist in physical deployment and are not simulation artifacts.

## Results

On the standard LIBERO benchmark, all pre-training recipes (no pre-training, VLA-only, VLA+VLM) saturate at 96.3–97.4% average success, masking reasoning differences. Under CALVIN ABC→D out-of-distribution evaluation, VLA+VLM pre-training achieves 4.09 average completed task length versus 3.67 for no pre-training, a gap invisible on LIBERO. Under BeTTER's causal interventions, all three evaluated VLAs (π0.5, GR00T-N1.6, Being-H0.5) exhibit catastrophic drops: subgoal composition generalization (B→C) falls to 0–15% success (ΔSR of −42.5 to −52.5 relative to seen sequences), and GR00T-N1.6 drops from 100% to 5% on semantic instruction following. Capacity compression from 8B to 2B in the InternVL-3.5 backbone degrades fine-grained grounding (RefSp.) from 27.50 to 6.00 and sequential planning (EgoPlan) from 40.80 to 33.38.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new VLA but diagnoses three state-of-the-art VLAs under BeTTER's causal interventions. GR00T-N1.6 [26] exhibits the most extreme lexical-kinematic shortcut: 100% success on spatial instructions (top/bottom) but only 5% on semantic instructions (red/blue), and 15% on novel subgoal composition (B→C). Being-H0.5 [22] shows a strong visual-layout bias (100% on 'top', 30% on 'bottom') and the worst composition generalization (0% on B→C, ΔSR = −52.5). π0.5 [13] is less polarized but still drops to 5% on novel composition and frequently stalls on counterfactual instruction-scene pairings. As reference VLMs in the architectural ablation (Table 3), Mimo-Embodied (7B) [12] and RoboBrain 2.0 (32B) [32] achieve RefSp. scores of 48.00 and 54.00 respectively, far exceeding the 6.00–29.50 range of the compressed VLA configurations, confirming that semantic grounding is lost during VLM-to-VLA deployment rather than absent from the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard VLA evaluation lineage (LIBERO, CALVIN, RLBench) and recent robustness benchmarks (LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-PRO) by shifting from surface-level perturbations to targeted causal interventions that manipulate task semantics, spatial logic, and temporal structure under strict kinematic isolation. Conceptually, it repurposes the diagnostic-intervention methodology from cognitive science and causal inference, applying it to robotic policy evaluation for the first time in a systematic, template-driven benchmark. The mechanistic analysis builds on the VLM-to-VLA deployment literature (capacity compression, flow-matching heads) and the behavioral cloning paradigm, but reframes their interaction as a coupled explanation for shortcut amplification rather than treating them as independent limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a diagnostic lens that the VLA community urgently needs: high success rates on static benchmarks (e.g., 97%+ on LIBERO) are shown to be unreliable proxies for genuine embodied reasoning, and the specific failure modes identified (lexical-kinematic shortcuts, behavioral inertia, causal state-tracking failures) point toward concrete design requirements for future architectures. The real-world validation on SO101 confirms these are not simulation artifacts, strengthening the claim that current VLA paradigms need to preserve semantic and causal state representations while maintaining efficient continuous control. Open questions include whether alternative training objectives (beyond behavioral cloning) can recover causal structure, whether higher-resolution or multi-view perception can mitigate the architectural bottleneck, and whether the diagnostic framework generalizes beyond manipulation to locomotion or dexterous tasks.
