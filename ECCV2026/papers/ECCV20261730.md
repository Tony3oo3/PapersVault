## Problem

Existing GRPO-based video reasoning methods suffer from two coupled limitations: (1) all tokens in a reasoning chain receive uniform credit, causing the model to waste capacity on generic scaffolding (e.g., 'Let's think...') rather than critical spatiotemporal cues, and (2) binary rewards from single-choice QA provide no signal for partially correct answers, producing high reward variance and unstable policy updates. Neither issue has been addressed jointly in the video QA setting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames (128×28×28 patches during training, 256×28×28 at inference) alongside a text question and a set of candidate answer options. It generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by one or more selected options. A key structural difference from standard video QA is the multi-answer formulation: after Question-Answer Inversion augmentation, a question may have multiple correct options, and the reward is a continuous value in [0,1] reflecting the fraction of ground-truth options correctly identified (with zero penalty for any false positive).

## Method

TW-GRPO modifies the GRPO objective along two axes. First, a token-level importance weight w_t is computed as the min-max-normalized sum of KL divergences between each candidate output's token distribution and the group-averaged distribution at position t (with uniform-distribution padding for variable lengths); this weight multiplies the per-token policy gradient, concentrating learning on high-information positions while retaining a (1+α) floor so no token is fully ignored. Second, the reward is replaced by a multi-level soft reward R_soft = |P|/|G| when the predicted set P is a subset of ground truth G, and 0 otherwise, which grants partial credit for correct-but-incomplete answers while strictly penalizing false positives to prevent reward hacking. To supply multi-choice training data without new annotations, Question-Answer Inversion negates the question stem (e.g., 'did' → 'didn't') and inverts the correct/incorrect option labels, with random removal of some correct options to maintain difficulty. The full objective (Eq. 6) normalizes by total token count across the group and applies the standard PPO-style clipping, requiring no auxiliary critic model and adding only O(L) overhead per step.

## Results

TW-GRPO achieves 50.4% accuracy on CLEVRER, 76.3% on NExT-GQA, 65.8% on MMVU (multi-choice), 64.2% on MVBench, 73.3% on TempCompass, and 56.1% on VideoMME (wo_sub) using only 4K RL samples. Under the 1K RL setting, it reaches 50.4% on CLEVRER and 76.1% on NExT-GQA, already surpassing VideoChat-R1 (18K RL) on CLEVRER (50.4% vs. 29.2%) and matching or exceeding it on NExT-GQA (76.1% vs. 76.0%). The headline claim is an average improvement of 2.6% over Video-R1 across four comparable benchmarks, with +3.8% on MVBench, +2.4% on TempCompass, and +2.3% on VideoMME.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Video-R1-Zero (4K RL, same data budget), TW-GRPO† wins on all three comparable understanding benchmarks: MVBench 64.2 vs. 60.4 (+3.8%), TempCompass 73.3 vs. 70.9 (+2.4%), and VideoMME 56.1 vs. 53.8 (+2.3%). Against the fully pretrained Video-R1 (165K SFT + 4K RL), TW-GRPO† surpasses it on NExT-GQA (76.3 vs. 74.3, +2.0%) and MVBench (64.2 vs. 62.7, +1.5%), though it trails on VideoMME (56.1 vs. 57.4). Against VideoChat-R1 (18K RL, 18× more data), TW-GRPO with only 1K RL samples achieves a higher CLEVRER score (50.4 vs. 29.2) and a marginally better NExT-GQA (76.1 vs. 76.0), while VideoChat-R1 edges ahead on TempCompass (72.9 vs. 73.3 is actually TW-GRPO winning) and VideoMME (52.4 vs. 55.1, TW-GRPO wins). Against the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, TW-GRPO† improves CLEVRER by +1.5 points (32.0 vs. 30.5) and NExT-GQA by +0.4 points (76.3 vs. 75.9), while the 1K RL variant gains +19.9 on CLEVRER (50.4 vs. 30.5). The ablation GRPO (1K RL, no token weighting, no soft reward) is consistently outperformed by TW-GRPO on CLEVRER (50.4 vs. 41.1) and NExT-GQA (76.1 vs. 75.2), confirming the contribution of both proposed mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (originally from DeepSeekMath) to video reasoning by introducing two targeted modifications: a token-level importance weighting mechanism based on intra-group KL divergence, and a multi-level soft reward derived from reformulating single-choice QA as multi-choice QA. It departs from Video-R1, VideoRFT, and VideoChat-R1, which all rely on uniform token credit assignment and binary rewards, by treating token informativeness as an explicit training signal rather than a post-hoc diagnostic. The soft reward design draws conceptual inspiration from IoU-based rewards in VideoChat-R1's grounding task, but the authors are the first to adapt such continuous feedback to video QA through the multi-choice reformulation and Question-Answer Inversion augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18× data efficiency claim (1K RL samples matching or exceeding methods using 18K) is practically significant for groups with limited compute, and the O(L) token-weighting overhead (<0.01 s/step) makes the approach nearly drop-in for existing GRPO pipelines. However, the absolute gains over Video-R1 are modest (≈2.6% average), the QAI augmentation retains a 14% invalidity rate, and all experiments are confined to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, leaving open whether the token-weighting and soft-reward mechanisms transfer to other model families or to non-QA video tasks like grounding or captioning. A key open question is whether the multi-choice reformulation is truly necessary or whether alternative reward-shaping strategies (e.g., per-option confidence scoring) could achieve similar gradient density without altering the task format.
