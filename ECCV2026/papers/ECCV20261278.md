## Problem

Classifying images encoded as implicit neural representation (INR) weights remains challenging because INR parameters are high-dimensional, exhibit permutation symmetries, and carry discriminative information in a structured but poorly understood way across layers and frequency components. Standard Transformer classifiers treat all weight tokens uniformly, ignoring the fact that INR layers encode distinct functional roles. Additionally, no explainability tools existed for weight-space classifiers, leaving it unclear which INR parameters drive classification decisions.

## Input / Output

The classifier consumes the difference between the adapted INR weights φ and the meta-learned initialization θ (scaled by λ=500), tokenized by INR layer: each hidden layer's weight matrix W∈R^{n×n} and bias b∈R^n are folded into W'∈R^{(n+1)×n}, yielding n tokens of dimension D=n+1 per layer, for a total of M=nL tokens. The primary output is a class label from a linear head over average-pooled token representations. A secondary output is a per-weight importance score (Grad-CAG) that can be converted into spatial attribution maps by pruning low-importance weights and computing reconstruction differences.

## Method

The HMoE Transformer replaces each standard FFN block with a two-stage sparse Mixture-of-Experts module. Stage 1 (Layer-wise MoE) pools all tokens within each INR layer into a summary vector, routes the entire layer to a top-k subset of experts, and applies the selected experts to all tokens in that layer. Stage 2 (Token-wise MoE) performs fine-grained per-token routing, with routing scores conditioned on Stage 1's layer-level gate vector via a learned cross-projection, enabling content-adaptive specialization. The full system is trained end-to-end via a MAML/MetaSGD meta-learning loop: the inner loop fits each INR to its image for k steps, while the outer loop updates the shared initialization θ and per-parameter learning rates α using both reconstruction and classification losses, so the classifier directly shapes the INR weight landscape. A load-balancing loss across all MoE modules prevents expert collapse, and sparse TopK routing (k=1, 4 experts per stage) keeps per-token compute comparable to a dense model while increasing total capacity.

## Results

On low-resolution benchmarks, HMoE-MWT achieves 99.06% on MNIST, 90.72% on Fashion-MNIST, and 65.01% on CIFAR-10, setting new state-of-the-art among weight-space classifiers. On the high-resolution Imagenette dataset, HMoE-MWT-L reaches 62.96% top-1 accuracy with 23.01 dB reconstruction PSNR, surpassing the prior best MWT-L (60.62%). On ImageNet-1K, HMoE-MWT-L achieves 26.73% accuracy with 22.06 dB PSNR, outperforming MWT-L (24.11%). A parameter-matched comparison on Imagenette confirms the architectural advantage: at 1.1M classifier parameters, HMoE-MWT gains +4.7% over MWT, and at 11M parameters it gains +2.6% over a scaled-up dense MWT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest prior work, MWT/MWT-L [14], HMoE-MWT improves CIFAR-10 accuracy by +8.1% over MWT (65.01% vs. 56.90%) and +5.4% over MWT-L (65.01% vs. 59.57%), while also leading on MNIST (+0.76%) and Fashion-MNIST (+0.29%). On Imagenette, HMoE-MWT-L surpasses MWT-L by +2.34% (62.96% vs. 60.62%), and on ImageNet-1K it gains +2.62% (26.73% vs. 24.11%). Against equivariant and graph-based baselines, the gap is substantial: HMoE-MWT outperforms ScaleGMN-B [21] by +26.2% on CIFAR-10, DWS [30] by +30.6%, NG-GNN [23] by +29.0%, and NFN variants [45] by over 30%. ProbeGen [20], the strongest probe-based method, is surpassed on MNIST (+0.66%) and Fashion-MNIST (+3.02%), though it does not report CIFAR-10 results. Inr2Vec [5] is far behind at 23.69% on MNIST, confirming that compact embedding approaches struggle with the full weight-space structure that HMoE exploits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Meta Weight Transformer (MWT) framework of Gielisse & van Gemert (CVPR 2025), which jointly trains a meta-learned SIREN INR and a Transformer classifier in weight-space, by replacing the shared feed-forward blocks with a hierarchical Mixture-of-Experts (HMoE) module that routes tokens based on their originating INR layer and then performs fine-grained token-level expert selection. The meta-learning formulation follows MAML/MetaSGD (Finn et al., 2017), and the broader INR classification landscape (DWS, NFN, ScaleGMN, ProbeGen) is addressed as competing baselines. The paper also introduces the first weight-space explainability tool (Grad-CAG) and structured pruning analysis for INR classifiers, filling a gap that pixel-space attribution methods cannot address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that conditional expert specialization is particularly well-suited to INR weight-space, where layers encode distinct frequency components and discriminative information is distributed across coordinated parameter subcircuits rather than isolated weights. The parameter-matched comparison showing HMoE outperforming a dense Transformer at equal parameter budget, and the finding that classifier architecture matters more than INR width, offer practical guidance for weight-space model design. However, performance still lags substantially behind pixel-based classifiers on ImageNet-1K (26.73% vs. >80%), per-image INR fitting remains a computational bottleneck, and the explainability analysis is preliminary. Open questions include generalization to 3D NeRFs, other INR architectures beyond SIREN, and deeper circuit-level interpretability.
