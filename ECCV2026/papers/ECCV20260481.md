## Problem

Online video instance segmentation methods rely on per-frame predicted queries for cross-frame association and contrastive supervision, but under occlusion, motion blur, and clutter these queries become noisy—producing false positives, missed instances, and inaccurate masks. This noisy query contamination destabilizes similarity-based matching, corrupts positive/negative pair construction in contrastive objectives, and propagates drift through momentum-based memory updates. No prior online VIS method explicitly identifies or mitigates this source of instability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming video frames in a strictly causal manner (no access to future frames), with training clips of 8 frames on YouTube-VIS and 10 frames on OVIS, and inference at 480p resolution. It outputs per-frame instance segmentation masks, class labels, and persistent track identities maintained across the video. A key operational detail is that the query-level gating module filters unreliable predictions before they enter either the contrastive training pipeline or the online association module, ensuring consistent query selection throughout both training and inference.

## Method

MiNQVIS builds on Mask2Former and introduces three complementary noise-mitigation components. First, query-level gating suppresses unreliable predictions via a classification-guided confidence threshold (foreground probability > γ) combined with ℓ2-normalized embeddings for cosine similarity, reinforced by a log-domain hinge loss that enforces a confidence margin between matched and unmatched queries. Second, video-level prototypical contrastive learning (VPCL) aggregates ℓ2-normalized embeddings matched to each instance across a training clip into stable prototypes, which serve as positives in a contrastive objective with negatives drawn from previous-frame embeddings and other instance prototypes. Third, dual-memory fusion for online association combines a momentum embedding (exponential moving average) with a history-best retrieval from a per-tracklet cache of historical embeddings, where both similarity matrices are calibrated via bi-directional softmax before being fused with a fixed weight (w=0.5) to produce the final matching score for one-to-one query-tracklet assignment.

## Results

With ResNet-50, MiNQVIS achieves 55.6 AP on YTVIS19, 51.7 AP on YTVIS21, and 38.2 AP on OVIS, matching or exceeding the strongest baselines (CAVIS: 55.7/50.5/37.6). With Swin-L, it reaches 66.2 AP on YTVIS19, 61.6 AP on YTVIS21, and 49.1 AP on OVIS, setting new state-of-the-art on YTVIS21 and OVIS. The method also improves stricter localization metrics, e.g., AP75 of 56.6 vs. 54.9 on YTVIS21 (ResNet-50), indicating more precise masks and stable identity assignment. Gains are most pronounced on OVIS, where heavy occlusion causes the largest improvements over prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiNQVIS is compared against 15 baselines spanning early (MaskTrack R-CNN, SipMask, CrossVIS, IFC) and recent (IDOL, GENVIS, DVIS, TCOVIS, CTVIS, VISAGE, CAVIS) online VIS methods. On YTVIS21 with ResNet-50, it outperforms CAVIS by 1.2 AP (51.7 vs. 50.5) and VISAGE by 0.1 AP (51.7 vs. 51.6), while on YTVIS19 it is marginally behind CAVIS (55.6 vs. 55.7). On OVIS, the most challenging benchmark, MiNQVIS achieves the best AP under both backbones: 38.2 vs. 37.6 (CAVIS, ResNet-50) and 49.1 vs. 48.6 (CAVIS, Swin-L), with the gap widening to +2.4 AP over TCOVIS on OVIS (Swin-L). With Swin-L on YTVIS21, it surpasses TCOVIS (61.6 vs. 61.3) and CAVIS (61.6 vs. 61.1). The largest absolute gains over the strongest prior method (CAVIS) appear on OVIS, consistent with the paper's focus on occlusion-induced query noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

MiNQVIS extends the query-based online VIS pipeline established by Mask2Former and the momentum-embedding contrastive framework of CTVIS, adding three noise-aware mechanisms (query-level gating, video-level prototypical contrastive learning, and dual-memory fusion) that prior methods like IDOL, VISAGE, and CAVIS all implicitly assume away. The key conceptual departure is treating per-frame query reliability as a first-class problem rather than an architectural afterthought: prior work improves temporal consistency through better matching or richer embeddings, but none explicitly filters unreliable queries before they contaminate contrastive supervision or memory states. The dual-memory design also departs from the single momentum buffer used in CTVIS and IDOL by introducing a history-best retrieval cache as a drift countermeasure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying noisy queries as a root cause of instability in online VIS, the paper provides a diagnostic lens that could inform future work beyond this specific framework. However, the practical gains are modest (0.1–0.5 AP over CAVIS/CTVIS on YTVIS21 and OVIS), and the authors acknowledge failure under extreme motion and pose ambiguity, suggesting the approach addresses mid-level noise but not fundamental appearance discontinuities. Open questions include whether the dataset-specific gating thresholds (γ=0.10 for YTVIS vs. 0.05 for OVIS) can be learned adaptively, whether the dual-memory cache scales to very long videos, and whether the noise-aware philosophy transfers to other streaming video tasks like online action detection or video re-identification.
