## Problem

Existing video-to-audio (V2A) models generate an entire audio track in a single pass, offering no mechanism for incremental refinement: if specific sound events are missing from the output, users must regenerate the whole track. This non-interactive design is impractical for Foley-style workflows where artists layer complementary sounds step-by-step, and it is compounded by the scarcity of multi-reference video-audio datasets needed to train a model that produces distinct, non-overlapping tracks per video.

## Input / Output

At each generation step k, the model takes an 8-second video clip, a text prompt C_k specifying the target sound event, and the loudness-normalized mixture of all previously generated tracks (x̃_{:k}) as a negative audio condition. It outputs a single complementary 16 kHz audio track that semantically and temporally aligns with the video while avoiding duplication of sounds already present in prior tracks. After all steps, the individual tracks are mixed via loudness normalization (target −20 LUFS) into a final composite audio.

## Method

The core contribution is Negative Audio Guidance (NAG), a three-term guided flow for flow-matching V2A models: an unconditional term, a standard classifier-free guidance term (video + text), and a new repulsive term that subtracts an audio-conditioned flow estimate from the video+text flow, pushing the sampling trajectory away from previously generated sounds. The audio-conditioned branch is implemented as a ControlNet adapter on the MMAudio MM-DiT backbone, with stacked single-modal transformer blocks extracting audio features that are injected into the main branch's intermediate representations. Crucially, the branch is trained on non-overlapping 4-second segments from the same video clip (using standard single-reference datasets like VGGSound, Clotho, AudioCaps, and WavCaps), which encourages it to capture shared high-level acoustic context (environment, timbre, recording conditions) rather than copying the conditioning waveform—making it a suitable repulsive prior when applied with a negative sign at inference. The pre-trained MMAudio weights are frozen; only the ControlNet parameters ψ are updated, with guidance coefficients α=4.5 and β=1.5 set empirically.

## Results

On the Multi-Caps VGGSound benchmark (15,221 clips, 5 captions each), the proposed NAG method achieves the best composite-audio quality among step-by-step generation approaches: FD_PANNs = 6.47, FD_VGG = 0.98, KL_PaSST = 1.76, IB-score = 28.65, and DeSync = 0.42. For individual tracks, NAG reduces CLAP audio-audio similarity (separability) from 79.75 (vanilla MMAudio) to 71.38 while preserving audio quality (IS = 12.01 vs. 12.47) and audio-video alignment (IB-score = 26.67 vs. 27.76). Subjective evaluation confirms the advantage: NAG is preferred over vanilla MMAudio with 71.4% win rate on audio quality, 76.0% on semantic alignment, and 61.1% on temporal alignment for composite audio, and receives a significantly higher separability rating (3.35 vs. 2.24 on a 5-point scale) for individual tracks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla MMAudio-S-16k with independent generation, NAG improves composite-audio FD_PANNs from 7.76 to 6.47 and FD_VGG from 1.35 to 0.98, while matching DeSync (0.42) and slightly improving IB-score (28.65 vs. 28.13) and IS (10.58 vs. 10.42). Compared to MMAudio with text-based negative prompting, NAG substantially outperforms on all composite-audio metrics (e.g., FD_PANNs 6.47 vs. 9.21, IS 10.58 vs. 9.08, IB-score 28.65 vs. 25.89), showing that audio-level negation is far more effective than caption-level negation for suppressing already-generated sounds. Against the stronger external baselines FoleyCrafter and Seeing-and-Hearing, NAG dominates across every metric (e.g., FD_PANNs 6.47 vs. 20.04 and 31.81; DeSync 0.42 vs. 1.18 and 1.19), reflecting both the superior base model (MMAudio) and the added step-by-step control. On individual-track separability (Table 2), NAG achieves the lowest CLAP A-A similarity (71.38) among all MMAudio variants, confirming that it most effectively prevents sound duplication across tracks, whereas negative prompting only marginally improves separability (75.57) at the cost of degrading audio quality (IS 11.19) and A-V alignment (IB-score 24.53).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MMAudio (a flow-matching-based video-to-audio model) and extends its multi-condition guidance framework from VinTAGe by introducing a fourth, audio-conditioned term applied negatively. The concept-negation formulation draws on Du et al.'s energy-based compositional generation, but the key departure is repurposing audio conditioning as a repulsive prior rather than an attractive one (as in MultiFoley, Sketch2Sound, or Action2Sound). The training strategy—using non-overlapping segments from the same video as positive pairs—avoids the need for multi-reference or source-separated datasets, distinguishing it from generative-add methods in text-to-audio and text-to-music that require explicit triplets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in V2A workflows: current models generate a single monolithic track, forcing full regeneration when specific events are missing, which is inefficient for collaborative Foley-style authoring. The NAG mechanism is lightweight (a frozen base model plus a small ControlNet adapter) and trainable on standard single-reference datasets, making it broadly applicable to other V2A backbones. Open questions include scalability beyond 8-second clips and 5 sequential tracks, sensitivity to the hand-tuned guidance coefficients (α, β), and whether the non-overlapping-segment training signal degrades when videos contain highly heterogeneous acoustic environments.
