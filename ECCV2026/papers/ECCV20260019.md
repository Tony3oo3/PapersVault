## Problem

Existing mask refinement methods generate training noise through morphological operations (dilation, erosion, random boundary shifts) that are spatially random and semantically agnostic, failing to capture the structured, context-dependent error patterns produced by real segmentation models. This mismatch limits refiners' ability to correct the complex boundary, structural, and semantic-confusion errors that persist even in state-of-the-art segmentation architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260019/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a binary noisy segmentation mask of the same spatial dimensions (H×W) as input. The noisy mask is additionally decomposed into point and box prompts that are encoded as visual prompt embeddings fed to the decoder alongside image embeddings from the frozen SAM ViT-H encoder. The output is a refined binary mask at the same resolution, produced by the lightweight 4M-parameter SAM decoder.

## Method

Phoenix introduces two key components built on a frozen SAM encoder with a fine-tuned lightweight decoder. First, Adversarial Mask Perturbation (AMP) generates semantically meaningful training noise by injecting learnable perturbation embeddings into the frozen decoder and updating them via FGSM-style gradient steps in embedding space, guided by a guidance mask (expansion, contraction, or inversion) and an IoU threshold that controls noise magnitude; this concentrates perturbations in regions of high model uncertainty, mimicking real segmentation errors. Second, Contrastive Mask Refinement Learning (CMRL) categorizes pixels into six regions (true/success/failure × foreground/background) based on target, noisy, and refined masks, then applies a tri-directional contrastive loss in feature space—combining intra-class consistency, inter-class separation, and self-improvement regularization—that explicitly models the transformation from incorrect to correct predictions. The contrastive loss is combined with standard Dice and Focal losses, and the entire decoder is trained for 10K iterations with only 4M parameters updated.

## Results

On COCO validation, Phoenix consistently improves SOTA segmentation models: Mask R-CNN (RN50) gains +7.1 AP^mask (46.9 vs. 39.8 base), Mask2Former gains +3.8 (50.6 vs. 46.8), and MaskDINO gains +1.2 (58.0 vs. 56.8). On the challenging DIS fine-grained task, Phoenix achieves the largest absolute gains, improving U-Net by +21.0 IoU (75.7 vs. 54.8) and ISNet by +11.0 IoU (76.2 vs. 66.1). In label-efficient settings, Phoenix refines 1%-supervised PointWSSIS masks to 21.8 AP^mask, a +16.1 absolute gain over the unrefined model (5.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Phoenix outperforms all four baselines (SegRefiner, SAMRefiner, SegFix, BPR) across every evaluated setting without exception. Against the strongest prior refiner SAMRefiner, Phoenix gains +1.6 AP^mask on Mask R-CNN (RN50) (46.9 vs. 45.3), +1.6 on Mask2Former (50.6 vs. 49.0), and a substantial +11.4 IoU on the DIS task (75.7 vs. 64.3 for U-Net). Against SegRefiner, the margins are even larger: +5.0 AP^mask on Mask R-CNN and +17.0 IoU on DIS-U-Net, reflecting SegRefiner's reliance on morphological noise that fails to capture semantic error patterns. Against the older methods SegFix and BPR, Phoenix leads by +6.3 and +5.9 AP^mask respectively on Mask R-CNN (RN50). The gains are most dramatic on low-quality inputs (semi/weakly-supervised masks, +16.1 AP^mask over PointWSSIS) and fine-grained structures (DIS task), while shrinking to +1.2–1.7 AP^mask on already-strong SOTA models like ViTDet and MaskDINO, indicating diminishing returns as the base model improves.

## Relation to Prior Work

Phoenix builds on SAM's encoder-decoder architecture (freezing the 637M-parameter ViT-H encoder and fine-tuning only the 4M-parameter decoder) and repurposes FGSM-style adversarial perturbation from a destructive robustness-testing tool into a constructive data-augmentation mechanism operating in embedding space. It departs from prior mask refiners (SegFix, BPR, SegRefiner, SAMRefiner) that generate training noise via morphological operations (dilation, erosion, random boundary shifts) by instead optimizing learnable perturbation embeddings against the decoder's own uncertainty landscape, producing semantically structured noise. The contrastive learning component extends InfoNCE-style objectives from general representation learning into a tri-directional framework that explicitly models the ground-truth → noisy → refined transformation, a relationship structure not present in prior pixel-wise or pairwise contrastive segmentation losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for label-efficient settings: Phoenix achieves up to +16.1% AP^mask when refining extremely noisy pseudo-labels from 1%-supervision semi-supervised models, directly addressing the bottleneck where low-quality pseudo-labels limit downstream learning. The finding that adversarial noise generation is the critical bottleneck (swapping morphological for adversarial noise in SegRefiner yields +13 IoU on DIS) suggests the field has been under-investing in training-data realism for refinement. Open questions include whether the approach extends to self-supervised refinement without any ground-truth masks (hinted at but not fully explored), whether the model-learned 'semantics' generalize to domains where the base model's uncertainty landscape differs substantially, and whether the SAM-specific architecture is truly necessary or whether the adversarial-noise principle transfers to lighter refiners.
