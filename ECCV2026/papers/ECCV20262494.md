## Problem

Existing training-free visual token pruning methods for Video LLMs are primarily validated on MCQA benchmarks where coarse-grained cues and language priors often suffice, masking a sharp performance collapse on fine-grained tasks like hallucination evaluation and compositional reasoning. The paper identifies sink tokens—semantically uninformative tokens that attract disproportionate attention—as the key obstacle: when these tokens survive pruning, they consume the limited token budget and distort the visual evidence grounding the model's generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262494/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a 32-frame video (yielding 32×196 visual tokens after SigLIP encoding and MLP projection) paired with a text question, and produces a reduced set of visual tokens (at 10%, 15%, or 20% retention) fed to the LLM for autoregressive text generation. SToP is a plug-and-play module that modifies the attention scores used for token selection without altering the input format, output structure, or requiring any retraining; it can be grafted onto existing spatial-only (VisionZip, FastVid) or temporal+spatial (HoliTom) pruning pipelines.

## Method

SToP introduces a sink score computed by summing each token's attention score across all T frames at the same spatial position, raising the result to a power w (default 1.1) to sharpen the distribution, and applying min-max normalization to [0,1]. The STSP module then subtracts a weighted sink score (μ_s·s_i) from each token's per-frame attention score before selection, so tokens that appear salient under standard attention criteria but are actually sinks are deprioritized. The STTP module adds a weighted sink score (μ_t·s_i) to the inter-frame similarity in the temporal pruning threshold, explicitly pushing sink tokens above the pruning threshold so they are removed along the temporal axis. The approach is training-free and architecture-agnostic, requiring only the vision encoder's attention matrix, and is validated as a drop-in enhancement for VisionZip, FastVid, and HoliTom, reducing performance drop rates by up to ~10 percentage points at 10% retention.

## Results

On fine-grained video understanding tasks, SToP reduces the performance drop rate relative to the vanilla model by up to 9.92 percentage points (VisionZip at 10% retention: 16.79% → 6.87%) and 9.37 points (FastVid at 10%: 15.69% → 6.32%). On the EventHallusion Binary hallucination metric at 10% retention, VisionZip+SToP achieves 60.39 vs. 50.37 for VisionZip alone, and FastVid+SToP reaches 60.15 vs. 49.63 for FastVid. On MCQA benchmarks (Table 2), SToP also yields consistent gains, e.g., reducing the drop rate from 7.36% to 3.34% for VisionZip at 10% retention across MVBench, VideoMME, NextQA, LongVideoBench, and MLVU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SToP is applied as a plug-and-play module to three baselines (VisionZip, FastVid, HoliTom) and compared against the state-of-the-art PruneVid. Against VisionZip (spatial-only), SToP delivers the largest gains: at 10% retention, EventHallusion Binary improves from 50.37 to 60.39 (+10.02) and the overall drop rate shrinks from 16.79% to 6.87%. Against FastVid (spatial-only with density merging), the improvement is similarly dramatic: Binary goes from 49.63 to 60.15 (+10.52) and drop rate from 15.69% to 6.32% at 10%. Against HoliTom (temporal+spatial), gains are smaller but consistent (e.g., Binary 60.88→62.59, drop rate 6.22%→4.80% at 10%), confirming that even when temporal pruning already suppresses some sink tokens, explicit sink-aware pruning still helps. Against PruneVid, HoliTom+SToP achieves a lower drop rate at every retention ratio (4.80% vs. 9.22% at 10%; 2.78% vs. 5.68% at 15%), and FastVid+SToP also surpasses PruneVid on fine-grained tasks. On MCQA benchmarks, all SToP variants outperform their respective baselines and PruneVid, with the largest margin again for VisionZip+SToP (drop rate 3.34% vs. 7.36% for VisionZip at 10%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free visual token pruning line for Video LLMs (VisionZip, FastVid, HoliTom, PruneVid) by identifying a specific failure mode—sink tokens, a phenomenon previously documented in LLMs and ViTs but not connected to pruning—rather than proposing a new pruning architecture. It departs from prior work in two ways: (1) it shifts the evaluation lens from MCQA benchmarks, where coarse cues mask degradation, to fine-grained tasks (hallucination, compositional reasoning, open-ended generation), and (2) it repurposes the attention-sink literature into a practical pruning criterion (the sink score) that plugs into existing spatial and temporal pruning pipelines without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard pruning methods appear acceptable on MCQA but collapse sharply on fine-grained tasks challenges the field's reliance on MCQA as the primary stress-test for token compression, and the identification of sink tokens as the mechanism provides a concrete, actionable target. The plug-and-play design (STSP/STTP modules) means the insight is immediately adoptable by any attention-based pruning pipeline. Open questions include whether the sink score generalizes to non-attention-based pruning (e.g., feature-based DPC-KNN), whether it holds for larger or differently-architected Video LLMs, and whether the phenomenon persists in autoregressive decoding stages where attention patterns shift.
