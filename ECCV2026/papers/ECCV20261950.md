## Problem

Single-shot HDR imaging for dynamic robotic scenes is limited by temporal exposure bracketing, where sequential captures introduce motion artifacts and photometric alignment between drastically different exposures is fundamentally unreliable. No public benchmark exists for evaluating spatially distributed exposure bracketing—distributing different exposures across synchronized multi-view cameras with depth—as an alternative to temporal bracketing or expensive specialized HDR sensors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261950/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N synchronized 12-bit LDR images from multi-view cameras, each set to a geometrically spaced target mean intensity (0.05 to 0.8), along with a sparse depth map from LiDAR or active stereo and the previous frame's images for adaptive exposure control. It produces a linear-intensity HDR image in the middle camera's view (t = N/2) and a fused dense depth map. The sparse depth is densified via a pretrained monocular depth prior (Depth Anything V2) with metric-scale anchoring before fusion, and the final HDR is recovered from a three-level μ-law tone-mapped representation via a transformer.

## Method

DMEB replaces temporal bracketing with spatial bracketing: it assigns drastically different exposures to synchronized cameras and uses metric depth (not optical flow) to warp differently exposed views into a reference view, enabling exposure gaps far beyond what photometric matching tolerates. The pipeline has three stages—(1) adaptive multi-view exposure control with geometrically spaced target intensities, (2) depth-guided confidence-aware fusion where per-pixel confidence combines a trapezoid intensity function with a CNN, and a soft visibility mask enforces depth consistency during warping, and (3) HDR refinement via a transformer that merges three μ-law tone-mapped images (μ = 10³, 5×10⁴, 10⁶) back into the linear domain. The confidence network and refinement transformer are trained in two phases: 50 epochs on 40k synthetic CARLA scenes (L1, SSIM, gradient loss in μ-law domain; L1 + SILog for depth), then 10 epochs of fine-tuning on 10k real scenes with HDR losses only, using Adam at lr = 10⁻⁴.

## Results

On the single-shot multi-camera setting (DMEB's primary configuration), DMEB achieves 39.40 dB PSNR-μ and 0.916 SSIM-μ on the Real (Modest DR) dataset, and 39.17 dB PSNR-μ and 0.868 SSIM-μ on the Real (Ultra DR) dataset, outperforming all four baselines on both PSNR and SSIM. On the synthetic benchmark with >150 dB effective dynamic range, DMEB reaches 34.72 dB PSNR-μ and 0.885 SSIM-μ at 9.76 FPS. On the iPhone dataset, DMEB achieves 26.12 dB PSNR-μ, and in a zero-shot transfer to the external Choi et al. two-view dataset it reaches 33.00 dB without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMEB consistently outperforms all four baselines on PSNR-μ and SSIM-μ in the single-shot multi-camera setting: it leads HDR Transformer [26] by 5.79 dB (39.40 vs 33.61) and AFUNet [23] by 7.15 dB (39.40 vs 32.25) on Modest DR, and by 0.43 dB over HDR Transformer (39.17 vs 38.74) on Ultra DR. On HDR-VDP, DMEB edges out HDR Transformer on Modest DR (9.69 vs 9.64) but trails both HDR Transformer (9.40) and AFUNet (9.39) on Ultra DR, suggesting the perceptual metric is less sensitive to the large exposure gaps that DMEB exploits. In terms of speed, DMEB (13.07 FPS) is substantially faster than HDR Transformer (1.19 FPS) and AFUNet (0.80 FPS) but slower than SAFNet (53.94 FPS) and HDRFlow (77.52 FPS). On the synthetic >150 dB benchmark, DMEB achieves the best PSNR-μ (34.72) and SSIM-μ (0.885), surpassing AFUNet (33.52) and HDR Transformer (32.65). In the zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on Choi et al.'s two-view dataset, DMEB reaches 33.00 dB PSNR-μ without retraining, outperforming HDR Transformer (26.33) and AFUNet (25.39) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant temporal exposure bracketing paradigm (Debevec & Malik lineage) and from prior multi-camera HDR work that restricted inter-camera exposure differences to preserve photometric consistency. Rather than building on a single prior method, DMEB repurposes depth-guided warping (borrowed from 3D reconstruction and LiDAR-assisted perception) and confidence-aware fusion (from classical HDR merging) into a spatial exposure bracketing framework where drastically different exposures across synchronized views are aligned geometrically rather than photometrically. The monocular depth prior (Depth Anything V2) and transformer-based refinement are standard components recombined for this new configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary contribution is the first public benchmark for spatially distributed exposure bracketing with aligned depth, filling a gap that made this alternative to temporal bracketing and specialized HDR sensors unevaluable. The practical demonstration on an iPhone 13 Pro and the zero-shot transfer to an external two-view dataset suggest that standard multi-camera-plus-depth configurations already present on robots and phones can substantially expand recoverable dynamic range without hardware changes. Key limitations include the requirement for overlapping fields of view and a depth sensor, and the authors identify the transition from 2D HDR image reconstruction to a full 3D HDR scene representation as the natural next step.
