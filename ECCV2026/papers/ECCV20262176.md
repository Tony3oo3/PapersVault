## Problem

Existing multimodal document retrieval methods encode each page independently, implicitly assuming that per-page representations suffice. This overlooks cross-page dependencies—coreference, multi-hop reasoning, and structured content spanning page boundaries—that are essential for resolving many real-world queries. The paper introduces CMDR, a new retrieval task and benchmark (CMDR-Bench) that explicitly requires modeling document context to identify relevant pages not directly mentioned in the query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262176/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text query and a multi-page document rendered as PNG images (average 183.5 pages per document) and produces a ranked list of top-k relevant pages. Pages are consumed in overlapping sliding-window chunks (stride s=2, window w=4) rather than individually, and the output is a set of contextualized multi-vector page embeddings (one vector per image patch) scored against the query via ColBERT-style late interaction. Hierarchical token pooling can optionally reduce the stored vectors by up to 80% with minimal accuracy loss.

## Method

CMDR-Embed adopts a chunk-then-split strategy: consecutive pages are jointly encoded through an LVLM backbone (ColPali or ColQwen, fine-tuned with LoRA) using a sliding window, and the resulting token-level representations are split back into per-page multi-vector embeddings, so each page's representation carries cross-page context. Training uses a new objective, Contextual Multimodal Contrastive Learning (CMCL), which augments standard InfoNCE with two types of context-aware hard negatives—In-Chunk Negatives (other pages in the same window) and In-Document Negatives (pages from other chunks of the same document)—to prevent information leakage from collapsing page-level discriminability. The total loss is a weighted combination of a context-aware contrastive term and a standard in-batch contrastive term (λ=0.5). Scoring uses Late Interaction (max-pool dot product summed over query tokens), preserving fine-grained token-level matching between query and contextualized page vectors.

## Results

On CMDR-Bench (nDCG@5), CMDR-Embed_Qwen achieves an overall score of 56.6, outperforming the strongest non-contextual baseline (ColQwen+Finetune, 40.4) by 16.2 points. CMDR-Embed_Pali reaches 49.8, a 13.5-point gain over its finetuned ColPali counterpart (36.3). The largest per-category improvements are on Structured Understanding (+26.0 for Qwen, +20.9 for Pali) and Text Completion (+18.8 for Qwen, +12.8 for Pali), confirming that cross-page context is most critical for these reasoning types. On the non-contextual ViDoRe benchmark, CMDR-Embed with multitask learning recovers most of the performance gap (89.2 vs. 88.9 for ColQwen finetuned on ViDoRe) while retaining strong contextual retrieval (56.1 vs. 56.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMDR-Embed decisively outperforms all 16 non-contextual baselines on CMDR-Bench. Against the strongest text retriever (BGE, 29.8) and the best general multimodal model (Qwen3-VL Embedding, 34.4), CMDR-Embed_Qwen leads by 26.8 and 22.2 points respectively, demonstrating that neither pure text nor generic multimodal embeddings suffice for cross-page contextual retrieval. Among document-specific retrievers, the gap is most telling: even after finetuning ColQwen on the same CMDR-Synth training data (40.4), it trails CMDR-Embed_Qwen (56.6) by 16.2 points, isolating the architectural benefit of joint multi-page encoding over per-page encoding. The improvement is consistent across all four query categories, with the largest margins on Structured Understanding (+26.0) and Text Completion (+18.8), where cross-page dependencies are most pronounced. On the non-contextual ViDoRe benchmark, CMDR-Embed without multitask learning is slightly weaker (85.5 vs. 88.9 for ColQwen+ViDoRe), but adding multitask training on both ViDoRe and CMDR-Synth closes this gap (89.2 vs. 88.9) while preserving contextual retrieval performance (56.1 vs. 56.6), confirming that the contextual capability does not come at the cost of standard retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ColPali/ColQwen line of vision-based document retrieval by replacing independent per-page encoding with a chunk-then-split strategy that jointly encodes multiple pages before splitting into page-level embeddings, thereby capturing cross-page context. The late-interaction scoring mechanism is inherited from ColBERT, and the training objective builds on InfoNCE contrastive learning, augmented with a novel CMCL loss that introduces context-aware hard negatives (in-chunk and in-document) to preserve page-level discriminability. Conceptually, the work bridges two previously separate threads: contextual passage retrieval (DAPR, ConTEB, which are text-only and single-page) and multimodal document retrieval (ColPali, VisRAG, which are non-contextual), unifying them for the first time in a long-document, visually-rich setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13–16 point nDCG@5 gap over the same finetuned backbone (ColPali/ColQwen) on the same training data provides strong evidence that the prevailing assumption of independent page encoding is a significant bottleneck, not a minor one, in multimodal document retrieval. CMDR-Bench fills a clear gap as the first benchmark jointly requiring contextual reasoning and multimodal retrieval over long documents (avg. 183.5 pages), and is likely to become a standard evaluation for context-aware document RAG systems. Key open questions include whether bidirectional attention (currently blocked by causal-attention backbones) could further improve long-range context modeling, how the approach scales to documents exceeding the jointly-encodable window, and whether the gains transfer to downstream RAG generation tasks rather than retrieval-only metrics.
