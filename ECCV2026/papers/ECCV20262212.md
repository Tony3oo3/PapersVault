## Problem

When 3D Gaussian Splatting is trained on only a handful of input views, it overfits to the observed images and produces blurry, geometrically inconsistent renderings at novel viewpoints. This paper reframes the overfitting as convergence to sharp minima in the Gaussian parameter space and addresses it through flat minima optimization, a perspective previously unexplored for 3DGS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262212/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of RGB images (3/6/9 views on LLFF, 12/24 on Mip-NeRF360, all downsampled 8×) together with their camera poses, and initializes Gaussians from an SfM point cloud. It outputs photorealistic novel-view renderings via standard 3DGS differentiable rasterization. No external depth maps, diffusion models, or additional geometric supervision are required; the only modification is to the optimization loop itself.

## Method

The core mechanism is Scale-Adaptive Perturbation (SAP): at each iteration, Gaussian positions are stochastically displaced with noise drawn from N(0, γ²Σᵢ), where Σᵢ is each Gaussian's anisotropic covariance, so larger or elongated primitives receive proportionally larger perturbations while compact ones are barely affected. Each Gaussian is perturbed independently with probability p (0.3), and the perturbation magnitude is linearly annealed from 0 to its target over training to avoid destabilizing early optimization. Gradients are computed at the perturbed parameters but applied to the clean parameters, locally smoothing the loss landscape without accumulating stochasticity along the trajectory. A complementary periodic reinitialization step resets scale, rotation, and higher-order SH coefficients to their SfM-based initial values for W=100 iterations every 1,000 steps, reducing effective degrees of freedom and further regularizing against overfitting. Ablations confirm that position perturbation outperforms perturbing scale, rotation, or opacity, and that the stochastic per-Gaussian scheme preserves fine details better than always-on loss-mixing alternatives.

## Results

On LLFF under the most challenging 3-view setting, the method achieves 20.88 PSNR, 0.731 SSIM, and 0.184 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline DropGaussian (20.76 / 0.713 / 0.200) and vanilla 3DGS (19.22 / 0.649 / 0.229). Under 6-view and 9-view settings, gains narrow but remain consistent (e.g., 24.76 PSNR at 6-view vs. 24.74 for DropGaussian; 26.23 PSNR at 9-view vs. 26.21). On Mip-NeRF360, the method leads on SSIM and LPIPS across both 12-view and 24-view settings, though it trails DropGaussian by 0.24 dB in PSNR at 12-view (19.50 vs. 19.74) while still winning on perceptual quality (0.348 vs. 0.364 LPIPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against seven baselines: vanilla 3DGS, DNGaussian (depth-normalization), FSGS (depth-prior), CoR-GS (co-regularization), DropGaussian (structural dropout), Difix3D+ (diffusion-based correction), and AnySplat (feed-forward). On LLFF, the method wins on all three metrics across all view counts, with the closest competitor being DropGaussian (0.12 dB PSNR gap at 3-view, 0.02 dB at 9-view). On Mip-NeRF360 12-view, the method loses PSNR to DropGaussian by 0.24 dB (19.50 vs. 19.74) but leads on SSIM (0.584 vs. 0.577) and LPIPS (0.348 vs. 0.364), indicating better perceptual quality despite slightly lower photometric accuracy. At 24-view, the method wins all metrics over every baseline. In the plug-in compatibility study (Table 4), adding the method to FSGS, DropGaussian, Difix3D+, and AnySplat consistently improves PSNR and SSIM, confirming that the flat-minima regularization is complementary to depth priors, structural dropout, diffusion correction, and feed-forward initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets 3D Gaussian Splatting as a supervised learning system where Gaussian parameters play the role of learnable weights, then adapts flat minima optimization principles—specifically stochastic weight perturbation from the RWP literature [17,18]—to the geometric structure of 3DGS. It departs from the dominant sparse-view 3DGS line of work (DropGaussian, CoR-GS, FSGS, DNGaussian) that regularizes the representation structurally (dropping primitives, adding depth priors, co-regularizing densification) by instead regularizing the optimization landscape itself through scale-adaptive positional perturbations. The stochastic, per-Gaussian perturbation design is inspired by the perturbed/unperturbed loss mixing in [17] but avoids the double forward pass, and the periodic reinitialization complements the standard opacity reset in 3DGS [12] with a return-to-initialization of scale, rotation, and higher-order SH coefficients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in compatibility demonstrated across optimization-based (FSGS, DropGaussian), diffusion-enhanced (Difix3D+), and feed-forward (AnySplat) pipelines makes this a broadly applicable regularization technique that requires no architectural changes or external priors, lowering the barrier to adoption. The perturbation robustness analysis (Fig. 4) provides a clean, direct validation that the method produces genuinely flatter minima rather than merely better-fitting solutions. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other 3D representations (NeRF, mesh-based), whether perturbing non-positional parameters with different schedules could yield further gains (the position+scale combination surprisingly degrades performance), and whether the benefits persist at higher view counts where overfitting is less severe.
