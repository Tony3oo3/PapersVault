## Problem

Prior satellite image synthesis methods convert native geospatial vector primitives into vision-compatible formats (pixel maps, bounding boxes, or point prompts), which degrades precise geometric properties—such as layered overpass connectivity and multi-instance overlaps—and forces practitioners to deploy separate architectures for different annotation budgets. No existing framework directly consumes the full spectrum of geospatial primitives (polygons, polylines, bounding boxes, points) within a single model while remaining compatible with end-to-end GeoAI workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261370/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model directly ingests native geospatial primitives without rasterization: polygons and polylines as coordinate sequences (up to V_p and V_ℓ vertices respectively), bounding boxes as axis-aligned corner pairs, and points as single coordinates, each optionally accompanied by an instance caption. Global text and geolocation serve as additional contextual conditions. The output is a 256×256 satellite image tile synthesized via a flow-matching ODE integrated from pure noise to data over 50 sampling steps.

## Method

TerraDiT-Ω builds on a Latent Diffusion Transformer (flow-matching backbone) and introduces two key components for primitive conditioning. A Unified Primitive Encoder maps all four primitive types into a shared token via Fourier feature mapping concatenated with a frozen LongCLIP text embedding, passed through format-specific MLPs (with learned null embeddings for absent primitives). Geometry-Aware Local Attention (GALA) then grounds these tokens in two stages: a MetaRBF+ module (multi-head attention + MLP) predicts rotated anisotropic Gaussian kernel parameters (σ_x, σ_y, θ) for soft point-level localization, and a Spatial Geometry Field provides dense multiplicative modulation—signed distance fields with sigmoid falloff for polygons/boxes, and exponential distance-to-segment kernels for polylines. The final normalized prior multiplicatively modulates cross-attention logits between primitive and visual tokens, yielding format-adaptive grounding where points induce soft focus while polygons and polylines sharpen attention to respect full instance geometry. The model is initialized from TerraDiT-α and fine-tuned for 200k steps with REPA alignment via DINOv3 and geolocation conditioning via RANGE.

## Results

On Git-Rand-15k, TerraDiT-Ω with full primitive conditioning (T+Ω+L) achieves FID 9.25, sFID 4.38, and LPIPS 0.3438, outperforming the point-conditioned TerraDiT-Σ (FID 12.01) and text-only TerraDiT-α (FID 14.21). On the dense Git-Dense-3.5k split, the gains are most pronounced: FID drops from 29.76 (TerraDiT-α) to 18.20, and LPIPS from 0.4853 to 0.3455. For grounding accuracy (CAS Top-1), TerraDiT-Ω reaches 79.36% versus 70.23% for TerraDiT-Σ and 67.83% for AeroGen. In data augmentation, synthetic imagery boosts AID scene classification Top-1 from 72.67% to 86.53% and DIOR mAP@50 to 78.87%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TerraDiT-Ω outperforms all 15 baselines across every conditioning format and all three evaluation splits. Against general image models (SDXL, SD 3, PixArt-α/σ), the gap is dramatic: FID of 9.25 vs. 79–150 on Git-Rand-15k, confirming that generalist models do not transfer to satellite imagery. Among spatially controlled natural-image methods, GLIGEN (FID 54.00) and InstanceDiffusion (FID 94.18) are far outperformed, with TerraDiT-Ω achieving roughly 6× lower FID. Against geospatial-specific baselines, TerraDiT-Ω beats GeoSynth-OSM (FID 53.88), VectorSynth (FID 95.15), AeroGen (FID 85.96), and Text2Earth (FID 25.93) by wide margins. The closest competitor is the prior TerraDiT-Σ (point-conditioned, FID 12.01), which TerraDiT-Ω surpasses by 2.76 FID points on Git-Rand-15k and by 9.29 FID points on the dense Git-Dense-3.5k split, where the advantage of richer primitives is most impactful. For grounding accuracy (CAS), TerraDiT-Ω with full primitives (Top-1 79.36%) exceeds TerraDiT-Σ (70.23%), AeroGen (67.83%), and GLIGEN (65.21%), approaching the 84.23% upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TerraDiT lineage (TerraDiT-α for text conditioning, TerraDiT-Σ for point-prompt conditioning via Adaptive Local Attention) by replacing the axis-aligned Gaussian prior with a rotated anisotropic Gaussian (MetaRBF+) and adding a Spatial Geometry Field for complex primitives. It departs from prior geospatial generation methods (GeoSynth-OSM, VectorSynth) that convert native vector primitives into raster pixel maps or single-format sparse prompts, instead directly consuming polygons, polylines, bounding boxes, and points through a Unified Primitive Encoder. The flow-matching formulation follows the broader diffusion-transformer framework (Lipman et al., Peebles & Xie), while the conditioning mechanism is a deliberate departure from generic learnable-token approaches (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion) that the authors argue cannot handle the dense, oriented, overlapping geometry of satellite scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-model-for-all-primitives design eliminates the need for separate architectures per annotation budget, which is practically significant for GeoAI pipelines where annotation cost varies from cheap point clicks to expensive polygon tracing. The data augmentation results—particularly the +13.86 Top-1 jump on AID scene classification and consistent gains across four heterogeneous downstream tasks without any post-processing—demonstrate that a single generative model can serve as a versatile synthetic data engine. Open questions include whether the framework extends to change detection, whether primitive-level visual control (color, texture) becomes feasible as richer instance captions become available, and the acknowledged risk of generating deceptive geospatial data.
