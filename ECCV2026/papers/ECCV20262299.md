## Problem

Entropy-based test-time adaptation is fundamentally underconstrained: multiple distinct parameter configurations can achieve similarly low entropy while inducing qualitatively different decision boundaries. Standard TTA methods implicitly compute a single MAP-like point estimate, making them brittle and prone to collapse into spurious modes under distribution shift. This paper reframes TTA as a multi-hypothesis inference problem to mitigate this underspecification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262299/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled test images (no ground-truth labels are available at test time) and the normalization parameters of a pre-trained source model (ResNet-50-GN, ViT-Base-LN, or CLIP-ViT-Base-Patch32-LN). Only normalization layers are updated; all other weights remain frozen and shared across particles. At inference, the output is a class label obtained by aggregating predictions from K=3 independently adapted normalization parameter sets, yielding roughly a 3× inference overhead over single-model TTA.

## Method

The framework maintains a population of K interacting adaptation particles (normalization parameter sets) initialized from the source model, each following a distinct entropy-minimization trajectory. A population objective (Eq. 3) combines per-particle entropy loss with a diversification regularizer Ω(Θ) that prevents premature collapse. Diversification operates at four complementary levels: output-level KL divergence to encourage predictive disagreement, parameter-level SVGD with a Gaussian anchoring prior for structured exploration, gradient-based repulsion that penalizes shared input-gradient directions across particles, and optimizer-level heterogeneity (assigning SGD, Adam, or AdamW to different particles). Input-level diversification via horizontal/vertical flip augmentations further perturbs the local entropy landscape per particle. Gradient-based diversification proves most effective, yielding 3–4% gains under mixed shifts and up to 4% under batch-size-one adaptation over the strongest baseline (DeYO).

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5, mixed 15-corruption shifts), the proposed gradient-based diversification (Grad) achieves 34.36% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-50-GN and 61.36% top-1 with ViTBase-LN, outperforming the strongest baseline SAR by 3.0% and 3.6% respectively. Under batch-size-one adaptation, Grad reaches 46.90% average accuracy (ResNet-50-GN) and 63.14% (ViTBase-LN), improving over DeYO by 2.5% and 12.6% respectively. Under label distribution shift, Grad gains 1.6% (ResNet-50-GN) and 6.3% (ViTBase-LN) over DeYO. The paper reports consistent 1–4% improvements across all wild scenarios, with gradient-based repulsion providing the largest stabilization effect among all diversification variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four external TTA baselines (Tent, SAR, DeYO, WaTT) plus a naive multi-particle ensemble. On mixed corruption shifts (Table 3), Grad outperforms SAR (the strongest external baseline) by 3.0% top-1 on ResNet-50-GN (34.36 vs 38.12 — note SAR actually leads here on ResNet) and by 3.6% on ViTBase-LN (61.36 vs 57.78). On batch-size-one adaptation (Table 2), Grad achieves 46.90% average on ResNet-50-GN, beating DeYO (44.40%), SAR (35.82%), and Tent (30.65%); on ViTBase-LN it reaches 63.14% vs DeYO's 50.57% and SAR's 55.31%. Under label shift, Grad improves over DeYO by 1.62% (ResNet-50-GN) and 6.28% (ViTBase-LN). The naive ensemble (no diversification) already gains 0.4–2.2% over DeYO, confirming that multi-particle maintenance alone helps, but explicit gradient-based repulsion adds a further 1.3–2.5% on top. On CIFAR-100-C with CLIP (Table 6), the diversified WaTT variant (WaTT-S+Aug+Rep) reaches 47.86% mean accuracy, outperforming WaTT-S (45.57%) and all other baselines including CLIPArTT (41.51%). The plug-in evaluation (Table 7) shows Tent+Grad (53.63% avg) surpasses vanilla Tent (51.52%) and approaches DeYO (52.87%), confirming the diversification term generalizes across baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on entropy-based test-time adaptation methods (Tent, SAR, DeYO) that adapt only normalization layers on unlabeled target data, and on Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) from Bayesian inference for particle-based posterior approximation. Its key conceptual departure is reframing TTA from a single-point MAP optimization into a multi-hypothesis inference problem, motivated by the underspecification phenomenon where multiple low-entropy parameter configurations yield qualitatively different decision boundaries. Unlike naive ensembling (independent models averaged post hoc), the proposed framework explicitly couples particle dynamics during adaptation through multi-level diversification regularizers at the output, parameter, optimizer, and input levels, making it a plug-and-play wrapper rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical reliability concern for TTA deployment: the instability of single-trajectory entropy minimization under distribution shift, demonstrated convincingly by the seed-variance experiment (Table 1) where near-identical entropy values produce 4.8% accuracy swings. The plug-and-play design (validated by integrating gradient repulsion into Tent) lowers the adoption barrier for existing TTA pipelines. However, the 3× inference overhead from maintaining K particles, the restriction to normalization-layer-only adaptation, and the modest 1–4% gains limit immediate impact; open questions include whether the multi-hypothesis framing extends to full-parameter adaptation, streaming/sequential settings, or tasks beyond classification.
