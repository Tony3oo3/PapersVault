## Problem

Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has advanced LLM reasoning but its extension to Large Vision-Language Models is constrained by a structural representational bottleneck: existing uniform-reward methods (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) broadcast sequence-level advantages across all tokens without explicitly coupling visual spatial information to the optimization signal. The authors show that visual perception errors account for 48.9% of reasoning failures, indicating that dense, homogeneous visual token encoding fails to isolate the sparse, task-relevant visual evidence needed for correct multimodal reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (e.g., geometric diagrams, charts) paired with a text question, producing a chain-of-thought text response. Unusually, the image is pre-processed by a geometry-aware token filter (SGUF) that converts it to CIELab space, computes per-patch structure tensors, and retains only tokens from structurally salient regions while sparsely sampling background. The response is segmented by paragraph delimiters (\n\n), and KAWHI outputs paragraph-level scalar weights in [w_min, w_max] that modulate the RL advantage before the policy gradient update.

## Method

KAWHI is a plug-and-play reward reweighting module with three coupled components. First, Structure-Guided Union-Find (SGUF) identifies key visual regions by computing second-order structure tensors on CIELab luminance, performing eigendecomposition for rotation-invariant anisotropy descriptors, and aggregating patches via a dual-threshold Union-Find rule that enforces both structural and luminance coherence. Second, vision-critical attention heads are identified through global ablation on the MME benchmark (heads whose removal drops MME score by >100). Third, spatial credit assignment is computed as the mean cosine similarity between response-token query vectors and SGUF-selected visual-token key vectors, restricted to those critical heads and aggregated at paragraph granularity via temperature-scaled softmax with uniform mixing. The resulting paragraph weights multiply the group-standardized advantage (Eq. 17), amplifying gradient signals for reasoning steps that attend to visually salient regions while preserving stable credit allocation across semantically coherent blocks. The entire mechanism adds only ~3.6% per-step latency and requires no architectural modification to the base LVLM.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, GSPO+KAWHI achieves the best average score of 52.94 across MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, and WeMath, outperforming the strongest fine-grained reward baseline VPPO (50.29) by 2.65 points. On Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, GSPO+KAWHI reaches 55.54 average, a 1.40-point gain over GSPO alone (54.14). Across all three uniform-reward backbones (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO), KAWHI consistently adds +0.81 to +1.54 points in average accuracy. On chart benchmarks, KAWHI under GRPO yields gains of 0.8–2.3% across five chart datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAWHI is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on top of three uniform-reward RL backbones (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) and compared against three fine-grained reward methods (Step-GRPO, FT-RL, VPPO). GSPO+KAWHI achieves the highest average (52.94 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 55.54 on Qwen3-VL-4B), surpassing the best fine-grained baseline VPPO by 2.65 and 5.25 points respectively. Against its own backbone, KAWHI adds +0.82 (GRPO), +1.06 (DAPO), and +1.54 (GSPO) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and +0.81, +1.26, and +1.40 on Qwen3-VL-4B, confirming consistent gains regardless of the underlying optimizer. The largest single-benchmark improvement is on MathVision, where GSPO+KAWHI gains +9.21 over base and +5.27 over GSPO on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. On chart benchmarks, KAWHI under GRPO improves all five datasets (ChartXivDesc +2.1%, ChartXivRea +2.1%, ChartQA +0.8%, ChartQA-Pro +1.6%, ChartMimic +2.3%), demonstrating generalizability beyond mathematical reasoning. The method incurs only 3.6% additional per-step training latency (19.6 s over a 540.6 s baseline), making the performance gains computationally inexpensive.

## Relation to Prior Work

KAWHI builds directly on GRPO's group-relative advantage mechanism, extending it from a uniform sequence-level reward broadcast to a paragraph-level spatially-weighted credit assignment. It departs from prior fine-grained reward approaches (VPPO, AT-RL) by avoiding indirect statistical surrogates and cross-modal attention extraction that conflicts with flashattention, instead using classical image-processing tools (structure tensor, union-find) to identify salient regions and vision-critical attention heads to compute Q-K alignment scores. The method is positioned as a plug-and-play module compatible with any uniform-reward optimizer (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO), distinguishing it from approaches that require architectural modifications or specific training pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying visual perception errors as the dominant failure mode (48.9% of errors) and showing that explicitly grounding reward signals in geometric structure yields consistent 1–1.5 point gains across three RL frameworks at only 3.6% overhead, the paper provides a practical, low-cost recipe for improving multimodal RLVR. However, the evaluation is confined to structured visual content (math diagrams, charts) and two Qwen model families, leaving open whether the SGUF-based region selection generalizes to natural-image understanding or to architectures without grouped query attention. A key open question is whether the paragraph-level granularity assumption holds for non-CoT or very short reasoning outputs, and whether the vision-critical head identification transfers across model scales without re-running the MME ablation.
