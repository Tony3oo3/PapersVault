## Problem

Existing methods for reconstructing articulated digital twins either require controlled multi-view captures in discrete static states or implicitly depend on an initial static scan of the object, making them impractical for in-the-wild monocular videos that begin with immediate motion. More fundamentally, these approaches optimize per-frame motion parameters independently, failing to capture the continuous physical trajectory that governs articulated motion and producing reconstructions that lack temporal coherence during transition phases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260360/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single casually captured monocular video as input, with no requirement for a static pre-scan, controlled camera trajectory, or known camera intrinsics/extrinsics. Preprocessing uses SAM3 for object segmentation, Qwen-Image inpainting to remove occluding hands, and TAPIP3D to extract sparse 3D point trajectories with visibility. The output is a 3D Gaussian Splatting digital twin with explicit, physically parameterized articulation (per-part joint axis on S², pivot point in ℝ³, and per-frame motion scalars) along with part-level segmentation, making the reconstruction directly controllable in physics simulators.

## Method

Articulat3D employs a two-stage differentiable optimization over 3D Gaussian Splatting. Stage 1 (Motion Prior-Driven Initialization) classifies TAPIP3D trajectories into static, prismatic, or revolute types via geometric fitting, clusters them into B part-level groups, and models scene dynamics as a weighted combination of B shared SE(3) motion bases initialized by weighted Procrustes alignment; per-Gaussian coefficients are regularized to remain sparse and near-discrete, yielding a coherent part-aware motion representation. Stage 2 (Geometric and Motion Constraints Refinement) replaces the unconstrained bases with explicit kinematic primitives—Rodrigues' formula for revolute joints and axis-aligned translation for prismatic joints—and jointly optimizes canonical Gaussian parameters, latent part assignments (refined via a Straight-Through Estimator for differentiable re-skinning), and articulation parameters under a combined loss of photometric rendering (L1 + D-SSIM + scale-invariant depth), acceleration smoothness, and assignment consistency.

## Results

On the synthetic Articulat3D-Sim benchmark (multi-part kinematics, 2–7 parts), Articulat3D achieves 0.53° joint-axis error, 0.65 cm pivot-position error, 0.76 cm whole-object Chamfer distance, 0.04 cm tracking EPE, and 37.80 dB PSNR / 0.98 SSIM / 0.03 LPIPS for view synthesis. On the simpler Video2Articulation-S dataset, it reaches 1.60° axis error and 35.91 dB PSNR. On the real-world Articulat3D-Real dataset (casual iPhone captures), it attains 26.73 dB PSNR and 0.93 SSIM, outperforming the best dynamic-rendering baseline (Shape of Motion) by 2.6 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Articulat3D dominates all four baselines on every metric where they overlap. Against RSRD [8], the gap is dramatic: on Articulat3D-Sim, axis error drops from 55.75° to 0.53° and position error from 91.52 cm to 0.65 cm, while PSNR improves from 28.19 to 37.80 dB. Against Articulate Anything [9], Articulat3D reduces axis error from 44.65° to 0.53° on Articulat3D-Sim and additionally provides view-synthesis metrics that Articulate Anything cannot produce. Against iTACO [18], Articulat3D cuts axis error from 48.50° to 0.53° and tracking EPE from 0.14 cm to 0.04 cm on Articulat3D-Sim. Against the closest dynamic-rendering competitor, Shape of Motion [22], Articulat3D improves PSNR by 4.03 dB (37.80 vs 33.37) on Articulat3D-Sim, 4.03 dB (35.91 vs 31.88) on Video2Articulation-S, and 2.60 dB (26.73 vs 24.13) on Articulat3D-Real, while also reporting joint-estimation and reconstruction metrics that Shape of Motion does not provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

Articulat3D builds directly on Shape of Motion [22], which introduced shared SE(3) motion bases for 4D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction from a single video, but repurposes this mechanism from generic free motion to part-aware, articulated motion with sparse, near-discrete assignments. It departs from the dominant articulated-object reconstruction paradigm (RSRD, iTACO, ArtGS, ArticulatedGS) that requires multi-view captures, controlled static scans, or template libraries, by formulating the problem as a two-stage differentiable optimization over explicit kinematic primitives (revolute/prismatic joints) initialized from low-dimensional motion bases. The pipeline also integrates recent foundation-model components (SAM3 for segmentation, Qwen-Image for inpainting, TAPIP3D for 3D tracking) as preprocessing, positioning itself at the intersection of 4D-GS dynamic rendering and classical kinematic modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for static scans, controlled multi-view setups, or template libraries, Articulat3D makes articulated digital twin creation feasible from the kind of casual monocular footage that robotic agents or internet users actually produce, directly addressing the sim-to-real gap in manipulation. The two new benchmarks (Articulat3D-Sim with multi-part kinematics, Articulat3D-Real with in-the-wild captures) fill a gap in evaluation for complex, uncontrolled articulation. Key limitations remain: the method assumes independent (non-hierarchical) joints, degrades on texture-less surfaces where sparse 3D tracks fail, and does not estimate dynamic physical parameters (mass, friction), leaving nested assemblies and full physics simulation as open problems.
