## Problem

Existing instruction-guided image editing models optimize for semantic alignment and visual consistency but routinely produce physically implausible results, such as objects floating after their support is removed or reflections persisting after an object is deleted. This failure stems from two compounding bottlenecks: training data consists of static input-output pairs that provide only sparse, discontinuous physical supervision, and the prevailing single-step architectures bypass the continuous causal chain that should underpin realistic visual transformations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a source RGB image and a free-form text editing instruction as input and produces a final edited image as output. Internally, however, PhysEdit generates a trajectory of N intermediate visual states (N=4 by default) that progressively transition from the source to the edited image, with each state conditioned autoregressively on all preceding states. The training data (PhysEdit-50K) provides these trajectories as N uniformly sampled frames from videos synthesized by Wan2.2, giving dense temporal supervision rather than a single input-output pair.

## Method

PhysEdit reformulates single-step image editing as a progressive, causal generation process using a Causal Diffusion Transformer (cDiT) that autoregressively synthesizes N intermediate images, each denoised via rectified flow and conditioned on self-generated historical context through KV caching and a self-forcing training strategy. A consistency regularization term distills from a frozen single-step teacher model to anchor unedited background regions, counteracting viewpoint drift and camera jitter inherited from video-synthesized training data. A second-stage Physics-Aware Reinforcement Learning (PARL) phase applies GRPO with a fine-grained reward computed by an MLLM that evaluates the edited image against K sample-specific physical constraints decomposed from the instruction, steering the policy toward stricter adherence to real-world causality. The entire pipeline is trained on PhysEdit-50K, a 51K-sample dataset of image-video pairs synthesized from Wan2.2 and filtered by an adaptive MLLM-based verification mechanism that checks each sample against instruction-specific fine-grained criteria.

## Results

On PICABench-Superficial, PhysEdit_Qwen achieves a new state-of-the-art Overall Accuracy of 60.70% and Overall Consistency of 28.35 dB, surpassing the strongest baseline ChronoEdit (58.83% / 27.91 dB) by 1.87% and 0.44 dB respectively. The OmniGen2-based PhysEdit variant reaches 56.72% Accuracy and 27.23 dB Consistency. On ImgEdit-Bench, PhysEdit_Qwen attains an Overall score of 4.29, outperforming its backbone Qwen-Image-Edit (4.16) and ChronoEdit (4.16). On RISEBench, PhysEdit_Qwen achieves 9.1% Overall accuracy, edging out Qwen-Image-Edit (8.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysEdit_Qwen outperforms all ten baselines on PICABench Overall Accuracy (60.70%), beating the closest competitor ChronoEdit (58.83%) by 1.87% and its own backbone Qwen-Image-Edit (57.21%) by 3.49%. On PICABench Overall Consistency, PhysEdit_Qwen (28.35 dB) also leads all baselines, with a 0.44 dB margin over ChronoEdit (27.91 dB). The OmniGen2-based PhysEdit variant (56.72% Acc) trails ChronoEdit (58.83%) on accuracy but still surpasses FLUX.1 Kontext (48.08%) by 8.64% and OmniGen2 (45.36%) by 11.36%. On ImgEdit-Bench, PhysEdit_Qwen (4.29) exceeds Qwen-Image-Edit and ChronoEdit (both 4.16) and FLUX.1 Kontext (3.52) by a relative 6.5%. On RISEBench, PhysEdit_Qwen (9.1%) marginally beats Qwen-Image-Edit (8.9%) and substantially outperforms FLUX.1 Kontext (5.8%) and BAGEL (6.1%). The largest single-dimension gains appear in Refraction (+9.71% over Qwen-Image-Edit) and Light Source Effects, where the causal trajectory modeling provides the most benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-guided image editing line (OmniGen2, Qwen-Image-Edit, FLUX.1 Kontext) by reformulating the single-step input→output mapping as a progressive, autoregressive causal generation process over intermediate visual states. It departs from prior physically-consistent editing efforts (EditWorld, ChronoEdit, F2F) that also leverage video model priors, by introducing a dedicated Causal DiT with self-forcing training, a consistency regularization term to counter viewpoint drift in video-synthesized data, and a physics-aware RL stage (GRPO with MLLM-based fine-grained rewards). The rectified-flow formulation and KV-caching mechanism are inherited from standard diffusion-transformer practice, while the data pipeline repurposes Wan2.2 video generation as a dense physical supervision source rather than a mere two-frame sampler as in PICABench/UniReal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Physical implausibility (floating objects, missing shadows, broken reflections) remains a visible failure mode in production editing pipelines, and this paper provides a principled recipe—causal multi-step generation plus targeted RL—to close that gap with measurable gains on dedicated benchmarks. The ablation cleanly isolating the contribution of dense temporal supervision (48.94% with static pairs vs. 56.72% with full trajectories from the same videos) validates the core thesis that sparse endpoint supervision is insufficient for physical reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer causal chains (N=4 saturates quickly), how it scales to multi-object or multi-step compositional edits, and whether the dependence on a large video model for data synthesis limits accessibility. The consistency loss is a practical concern for any future work training on video-synthesized editing data.
