## Problem

DETR-based video temporal grounding methods predict moment spans at the instance level by aggregating global semantic evidence through decoder queries, but unlike spatial objects with clear physical edges, video moments exhibit ambiguous temporal boundaries due to semantic gradient. This reliance on global semantics alone causes severe boundary misalignment, yielding sub-optimal localization. The paper validates this gap with diagnostic experiments showing that injecting hard boundary signals as priors dramatically closes the performance gap between synthetic and real videos.

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video (represented as L_v clips with features extracted at 1–2 second intervals via frozen SlowFast/CLIP or I3D backbones) and a natural language query (encoded by CLIP text encoder), both projected into a shared d-dimensional space. It outputs K moment spans as (start, end) timestamp pairs predicted by N=10 learnable queries, along with clip-level saliency scores over all L_v clips for highlight detection. The prediction is end-to-end via Hungarian matching, requiring no proposal generation or NMS.

## Method

DE²TR introduces a dual-branch decoder (DEFormer) that channel-wise decouples each moment query into a semantic sub-query (d/2 dims) and start/end boundary sub-queries (d/4 dims each), enabling independent evidence aggregation without feature conflict. The semantic branch uses a saliency-guided query refiner (attention pooling with the semantic prior map P_s) followed by cross-attention with anchor-based positional encoding, supervised by a differentiable-IoU-inspired alignment loss (L_sea). The boundary branch modulates the video memory with a boundary prior map P_b (start/end probabilities trained with adaptive Gaussian soft labels whose bandwidth scales with local semantic ambiguity) and uses local positional encoding to focus on boundary neighborhoods, supervised by a boundary alignment loss (L_bea). A prior-guided refinement mechanism injects semantic priors into queries and boundary priors into memory in a branch-specific manner, while a Boundary Augmentation Strategy (BAS) synthesizes training samples via temporal splicing to teach the model ideal boundary responses. The total loss combines standard MR/HD losses with weighted L_bnd, L_sea, and L_bea terms under Hungarian matching.

## Results

On the QVHighlights test set, DE²TR achieves 48.74% Avg. mAP and 51.39% R1@0.7 for moment retrieval, outperforming the previous best TD-DETR (46.69% Avg. mAP, 50.37% R1@0.7) by +2.05% and +1.02% respectively. With the Boundary Augmentation Strategy (BAS), these gains widen to +3.18% Avg. mAP and +2.09% R1@0.7. On Charades-STA, DE²TR reaches 39.87% R1@0.7 and 51.58% mIoU, surpassing the prior best (MS-DETR) by +3.39% and +0.99%. On TVSum, DE²TR attains 89.6% average Top-5 mAP, exceeding the previous SOTA (MS-DETR, 88.4%) by 1.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DE²TR outperforms all 18 compared baselines across all four benchmarks. On QVHighlights (test), the closest competitor is TD-DETR‡ (46.69% Avg. mAP), which DE²TR surpasses by +2.05%; KDA (45.67%) and MS-DETR (44.89%) trail by even larger margins. The advantage is most pronounced at strict IoU thresholds: DE²TR gains +2.51% mAP@0.75 over TD-DETR and +3.52% over KDA, confirming superior boundary precision. On Charades-STA, DE²TR leads MS-DETR by +3.39% R1@0.7 (39.87 vs. 36.48) and +0.99% mIoU; on TACoS, it beats FlashVTG by +2.40% R1@0.7 (27.14 vs. 24.74). For highlight detection on TVSum, DE²TR achieves 89.6% average Top-5 mAP, exceeding MS-DETR (88.4%) and TR-DETR (88.1%), and wins on 7 of 10 video categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR-based VTG paradigm initiated by Moment-DETR [24] and refined by QD-DETR [40] and TR-DETR [51], which all rely on a single set of decoder queries to aggregate global semantic evidence for moment localization. The key conceptual departure is the argument that video moments, unlike spatial objects, exhibit ambiguous temporal boundaries due to semantic gradients, and that existing query-based methods suffer from boundary misalignment as a result. To address this, the paper introduces a dual-branch decoder (DEFormer) that channel-wise decouples moment queries into semantic and boundary sub-queries, drawing on DAB-DETR's [32] position-content decoupling for the cross-attention mechanism, and adds a boundary augmentation strategy that synthesizes clear boundary signals via temporal splicing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic experiment showing that a simple splicing operation to create hard boundaries dramatically closes the performance gap between synthetic and real videos provides compelling evidence that boundary ambiguity is a genuine bottleneck in DETR-based VTG, not merely a data artifact. The boundary augmentation strategy is practical and low-cost, making it potentially adoptable by other VTG methods. However, the improvements over strong baselines like TD-DETR are moderate (2–3% in mAP), and the dual-branch architecture adds complexity; it remains open whether the boundary-aware formulation generalizes beyond VTG to related temporal tasks such as action detection or temporal action localization, and whether the Gaussian soft-labeling strategy scales to longer videos with many overlapping moments.
