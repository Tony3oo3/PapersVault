## Problem

Estimating fetal head pose from rapid, low-resolution 3D MRI navigator volumes is particularly challenging due to low SNR, spin history artifacts that obscure critical anatomy, and approximate left-right brain symmetry that creates pose ambiguities. Existing pose estimation methods—whether landmark-based, template-registration, or standard CNN regression—struggle to generalize from high-quality research data to noisy clinical volumes, and none jointly enforce rotation equivariance and object symmetry in the network architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261377/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution 3D MRI navigator volume (4–6 mm isotropic voxels, potentially containing a dark spin-history artifact plane) and a binary brain segmentation mask. Translation is computed as the center-of-mass of the mask; the cropped volume is then fed to the E(3)-CNN, which outputs three unit-length basis directions: one pseudovector along the left–right axis and two vectors along the posterior–anterior and inferior–superior axes. At inference, SVD enforces orthonormality and the pseudovector sign is flipped if needed to guarantee a proper rotation (det = 1).

## Method

E(3)-Pose replaces standard translation-equivariant convolutions with E(3)-equivariant convolutions (built from irreducible tensor fields and spherical-harmonic kernels) so that the rotation regressor is equivariant under all rigid transforms by construction. The key architectural insight is a novel rotation parametrization: the left–right axis is represented as a pseudovector (order l=1, even parity), which is invariant under left–right reflection while remaining continuous over SO(3), while the two orthogonal axes are standard vectors (odd parity). Training uses a weighted angular loss that accounts for the pseudovector's sign ambiguity (|sin θx| vs. |sin θk/2| for vectors), and data augmentation includes a Gaussian model of the spin-history artifact to bridge the research-to-navigator domain gap. Theorem 1 proves the parametrization is invariant under the object symmetry group, and the E(3)-CNN guarantees SE(3) equivariance of the full mapping.

## Results

On the clinically representative Navigators dataset (4–6 mm voxels with spin-history artifacts), E(3)-Pose achieves 9.4 ± 7.5° rotation error and 6.3 ± 4.0 mm AAD when trained on Research-Fetal, and 13.9 ± 13.2° / 7.9 ± 5.6 mm when trained on dHCP. On Clinical-Young (younger fetuses, GA 18–23 weeks), E(3)-Pose reaches 9.1 ± 4.7° / 3.0 ± 1.4 mm (Research-Fetal training) and 12.1 ± 6.8° / 3.9 ± 1.9 mm (dHCP training). These are the best results across all six baselines on both out-of-distribution clinical datasets, with the largest margins appearing when the training-to-test domain gap is greatest (dHCP → Navigators).

## Baselines & Comparisons

E(3)-Pose outperforms all six baselines on both out-of-distribution clinical datasets (Clinical-Young and Navigators) under both training settings. The margin is most dramatic on Navigators when trained on dHCP: E(3)-Pose achieves 13.9° rotation error versus 77.2° for RbR, 84.1° for 6DRep, and 93.9° for 3DPose-Net, indicating that standard CNNs fail catastrophically under the large domain gap. On Clinical-Young (trained on Research-Fetal), E(3)-Pose (9.1°) edges out the best CNN baseline RbR (11.2°) and substantially outperforms 6DRep (14.1°) and 3DPose-Net (39.2°). Template-based methods (FireANTs, EquiTrack) and the landmark-based Fetal-Align degrade severely on Navigators due to spin-history artifacts and low resolution, with rotation errors exceeding 44° in all cases. On in-distribution research data (Research-Fetal test), E(3)-Pose (5.1°) ties with Fetal-Align (5.0°) but is outperformed by template methods on dHCP test where subject-specific templates provide an unfair information advantage. The consistent superiority on clinical data, especially under the dHCP→Navigators domain shift, demonstrates that enforcing E(3) equivariance and pseudovector-based symmetry in the architecture provides critical regularization that standard CNNs lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the E(3)-equivariant CNN architecture (e3nn [25], 3D steerable CNNs [76]) and repurposes the mathematical concept of pseudovectors [33]—previously used only to model rotation axes in pose estimation—to encode object reflectional symmetry directly in the output parametrization. It departs from prior equivariant pose estimation methods, which either predict relative transforms between image pairs or discretize rotations into a classification problem, by regressing a continuous, overcomplete orthonormal basis over SO(3) while maintaining invariance under the object's symmetry group. The symmetry-aware loss and SVD-based inference step distinguish it from earlier approaches that handle symmetries via probabilistic outputs or heuristic pose selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evaluation of pose estimation on clinically representative fetal MRI navigator volumes, demonstrating that architectural constraints (equivariance + symmetry) yield substantially better out-of-distribution generalization than data augmentation alone—a finding with implications beyond fetal MRI for any pose estimation task with limited data, noisy sensors, and inherent object symmetries. Key limitations include the assumption of approximate left-right symmetry (which breaks under large lateralized pathologies) and the absence of explicit motion-history modeling or pose uncertainty quantification. Future work on equivariant probabilistic reasoning over SO(3) conditioned on motion trajectories is identified as a natural next step toward full clinical deployment.
