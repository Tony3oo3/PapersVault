## Problem

Current 6D pose estimation benchmarks have reached near-saturation on household objects but fail to capture the stochastic and optical complexities of industrial bin-picking, where parts are texture-less, metallic, geometrically symmetric, and highly reflective. Existing industrial datasets lack both the high-density stochastic stacking and severe multi-instance ambiguity of real factory scenes and the sub-millimeter annotation precision required for reliable industrial-grade evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260290/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes synchronized RGB-D streams from three co-mounted sensors (Intel RealSense D415 at 1920×1080, XYZ Robotic DLP structured-light at 1440×1080 with 0.08 mm depth resolution, and Photoneo PhoXi M laser scanner at up to 2064×1544 with 0.1 mm voxel precision) along with CAD models of 15 industrial parts, at sensor-to-object distances of 600–1000 mm. Outputs are 2D binary instance masks with confidence scores and 6D poses (rotation + translation), evaluated via symmetry-aware MSSD and MSPD metrics aggregated into AP scores. Scenes contain 22 instances on average (up to 60), introducing dense multi-instance ambiguity absent from prior benchmarks.

## Method

The core contribution is a metrology-grade data acquisition and annotation pipeline rather than a new estimation architecture. A dual-phase capture strategy applies anti-reflection coating (Acksys SP-102) to suppress specularity for ground-truth depth, then repeats the identical capture without coating for native optical properties, with pixel-wise correspondence maintained by the FANUC arm's ±0.06 mm repeatability. Annotations are derived from multi-view TSDF fusion of spray-enhanced depth, refined via constrained GUI alignment and multi-scale ICP, then propagated across 50 viewpoints using pre-calibrated hand-eye transformations. Annotation error is quantified by replicating the full pipeline in a BlenderProc simulation with matched Gaussian noise (σ = 0.26 mm), yielding a mean positional error of 0.999 mm and angular error of 0.432°. The benchmark evaluates SOTA methods under both seen-object (trained on 45 k synthetic BlenderProc frames) and unseen-object (zero-shot inference) settings following BOP 2024–2025 protocols.

## Results

On XYZ-IBD, all SOTA methods suffer severe performance degradation compared to standard BOP core datasets. For 6D detection, seen-object methods GDRNet and SurfEmb drop from 0.827/0.758 (BOP-Core 5 average) to 0.266/0.247 AP on XYZ-IBD, while unseen-object methods SAM-6D and FoundationPose achieve 0.578 and 0.564 AP respectively, still well below their BOP-Core 5 scores of 0.704 and 0.734. For 2D detection, the unseen method SAM-6D degrades by over 33% in AP (0.449 → 0.296), and NIDS-Net drops from 0.494 to 0.258. The annotation precision of XYZ-IBD is validated at 0.99 mm mean positional error and 0.43° angular error via simulation-based quantification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 8 SOTA methods across 2D and 6D detection under both seen and unseen object settings. For 6D detection, seen-object methods GDRNet (0.266) and SurfEmb (0.247) collapse on XYZ-IBD despite strong BOP-Core 5 scores (0.827, 0.758), indicating that synthetic-to-real transfer fails under industrial clutter and reflectivity. Among unseen-object 6D methods, SAM-6D achieves the best XYZ-IBD score (0.578 AP), narrowly edging out FoundationPose (0.564) and MatchU (0.529), but all three still lose 13–18 AP points relative to their BOP-Core 5 averages. For 2D detection, the seen-object YOLOX retains a relatively high score (0.774) due to its overfitting setup, while unseen methods degrade sharply: SAM-6D drops 33% (0.449→0.296) and NIDS-Net drops 48% (0.494→0.258), confirming that heavy occlusion, multi-instance ambiguity, and specular reflections are the primary failure modes. Overall, no method maintains its BOP-level performance on XYZ-IBD, with the largest gaps appearing for seen-object 6D estimators, establishing XYZ-IBD as a significantly harder benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the 6D pose estimation benchmarking landscape by addressing a specific gap: no existing industrial dataset simultaneously provides high-density stochastic stacking, severe specular reflections, multi-instance ambiguity, and sub-millimeter annotation precision. It departs from prior industrial datasets (T-LESS, ITODD, DIMO, ROBI, IPD) which either lack clutter density, have annotation errors of 1.8–11.3 mm, or omit reflective/symmetric object challenges. The annotation pipeline repurposes multi-view depth fusion (TSDF) and ICP refinement from prior work but introduces an anti-reflection coating strategy and a simulation-based error quantification protocol to validate metrology-grade accuracy. For benchmarking, it adopts the BOP evaluation protocol and tests both seen-object (YOLOX, GDRNet, SurfEmb) and unseen-object (SAM-6D, FoundationPose, MatchU, CNOS, NIDS-Net) methods, positioning itself as the missing industrial stress-test in the BOP ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark domain gap: methods achieving near-saturation on household benchmarks (e.g., GDRNet at 0.906 AP on YCB-V) collapse to 0.266 on XYZ-IBD, and even the best unseen-object method (SAM-6D) plateaus at 0.578, far from the sub-millimeter tolerance industrial manipulation demands. This quantifies how far current SOTA is from deployment-ready industrial perception and provides a concrete, publicly available testbed (integrated into BOP 2025) to drive progress. Open questions include whether generalizable foundation-model-based methods can close the gap without object-specific training, how to handle extreme specular surfaces without physical coatings, and whether the 15-object, 75-scene scale is sufficient to capture the full diversity of real factory environments. The paper's own future work acknowledges the need to expand object categories and working conditions.
