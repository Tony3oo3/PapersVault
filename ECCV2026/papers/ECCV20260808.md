## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves high-quality real-time rendering but struggles to maintain interactive frame rates when scaling to ultra-dense scenes (millions of splats) or ultra-high resolutions (4K), limiting its use in VR, AR, and mobile applications. Existing acceleration efforts focus on modifying the splatting pipeline itself (compact representations, Gaussian pruning, rasterization optimization), yet a fundamental computational bottleneck persists. This paper asks whether a post-rendering paradigm—joint super sampling and frame interpolation on low-resolution outputs—can close the gap without altering the underlying splatting pipeline.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes low-resolution 3DGS rendered frames (e.g., 1024×540) together with analytically computed image-space gradients derived from the continuous Gaussian signal, depth and normal maps (via RaDe-GS), camera poses for motion-vector estimation, and a warped historical feature from the previous frame. It produces a 4× super-sampled high-resolution image and an interpolated intermediate frame at the midpoint between two consecutive frames. Both modules are deployed via TensorRT and executed in parallel, so the effective per-frame cost is the maximum rather than the sum of GASS and LTFI latencies.

## Method

The framework comprises two jointly trained modules. GASS performs gradient-aware interpolation inspired by Hermite polynomials: a 3×3 block of pixel values and their x/y gradients is mapped to an S×S high-resolution block via a 3×3 convolution followed by an S×S transposed convolution, where the intermediate feature is interpreted as a polynomial coefficient vector. A GRU-based Geometry-Temporal Recurrent Refinement network then updates this coefficient vector using depth, normal-view inner product, and temporally warped historical features to suppress aliasing and enforce temporal consistency. LTFI warps the preceding high-resolution frame and the subsequent low-resolution frame to the intermediate timestep using camera-pose-derived motion vectors, fuses them through a compact U-Net-like Feature Fusion Network with spatial attention blocks and pixel-shuffle upscaling, and applies a Temporal Recurrent Unit (modified GRU) to accumulate spatio-temporal context. The key differentiator is exploiting the analytical differentiability of Gaussian splats to obtain continuous image gradients as free geometric cues, eliminating the need for scene-specific retraining or rasterized G-buffers.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360 at 4096×2160, 3DGS³ achieves 49.22 PSNR / 0.995 SSIM at 96.47 FPS, outperforming all learning-based super sampling and frame interpolation baselines in both quality and speed. On Deep Blending (2560×1440), it reaches 40.85 PSNR / 0.983 SSIM at 226.50 FPS, and on Tanks and Temples (2560×1440) it attains 37.64 PSNR / 0.978 SSIM at 246.35 FPS. The method also scales to scenes with >10M Gaussians (UrbanScene3D, Mill 19), maintaining real-time rendering at both 2K and 4K resolutions (e.g., 83.83 FPS at 2K and 61.33 FPS at 4K on Sci-Art) with negligible quality loss (<0.1 dB PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest 3DGS-specific baseline, UpscaleGS [44], 3DGS³ wins decisively on both quality and speed: 49.22 vs. 41.45 PSNR (+7.77 dB) and 96.47 vs. 77.50 FPS on Mip-NeRF 360, with similar margins on the other two datasets. Among general-purpose super sampling methods, 3DGS³ outperforms ECBSR (44.97 PSNR, 47.30 FPS), RT4KSR (43.82 PSNR, 12.00 FPS), and NinaSR-B1 (40.49 PSNR, 4.47 FPS) on Mip-NeRF 360, achieving the best PSNR while running 2–22× faster. For frame interpolation, 3DGS³'s LTFI module delivers 226.50 FPS on Deep Blending versus 0.67 FPS for FLAVR and 4.83 FPS for GIMM-VFI, a 47–338× speedup, though InterpAny achieves slightly higher PSNR (48.43 vs. 40.85) by using high-resolution ground-truth inputs that 3DGS³ does not have access to. When integrated with 3DGS acceleration methods (Table 2), 3DGS³ consistently boosts FPS by 3–4× (e.g., Mini-Splatting: 181.83→463.90 FPS at 2K; AdR-Gaussian: 127.10→260.60 FPS) with <0.1 dB PSNR degradation, confirming its plug-and-play compatibility. Overall, 3DGS³ is the only method that simultaneously achieves the highest FPS and top-tier quality across all three benchmark datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3DGS acceleration paradigm (compact representations, Gaussian pruning, rasterization optimization) by adopting a post-rendering strategy inspired by commercial graphics pipelines (DLSS, XeSS, FSR). It builds directly on the analytical image-space gradient formulation from UpscaleGS [44], but replaces its per-scene bicubic spline fitting with a scene-agnostic, learnable Hermite-inspired interpolation plus a GRU-based temporal refinement network. The temporal frame interpolation component draws on forward-backward warping and recurrent architectures from the video frame interpolation literature, adapted to exploit 3DGS-specific differentiable cues (gradients, depth, normals) rather than rasterized G-buffers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, scene-agnostic design makes this framework composable with existing 3DGS acceleration methods (Mini-Splatting, Speedy-Splat, AdR-Gaussian), offering a practical path to real-time 4K rendering of 10M+ Gaussian scenes at 60+ FPS with negligible quality loss. Key limitations include restriction to static scenes, training on a single 300-frame sequence, and reliance on TensorRT deployment for peak performance. Open questions include extension to dynamic 3DGS representations, mobile/on-device deployment with compact model variants, and whether the differentiable gradient cues generalize to other radiance field representations beyond 3DGS.
