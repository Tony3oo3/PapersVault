## Problem

Visible-infrared person re-identification is hindered by the absence of color in infrared images, and existing text-based compensation methods rely on global textual guidance that cannot accurately associate local infrared structures with color distributions in text. Furthermore, the semantic priors produced by existing prototype-based methods are too coarse-grained, causing textual color cues to be incorrectly propagated to irrelevant infrared regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260530/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes visible and infrared pedestrian images (resized to 3×384×144) along with manually annotated text descriptions that are used in both training and testing. It produces a fused identity embedding per image, from which cross-modal retrieval rankings (Rank-k, mAP) are computed. The text descriptions serve as a dynamic semantic filter rather than a fixed lookup, and the fusion weights are channel-wise vectors that modulate how much textual color information each infrared channel absorbs.

## Method

GSC builds on a ResNet-50 backbone and introduces three coupled components. The Hierarchical Refinement Module (HRM) maintains k learnable semantic prototypes that are iteratively refined across s stages: at each stage, pixel-wise features are assigned to prototypes via softmax similarity, aggregated into part-level features, refined by a two-layer MLP, and fed back to update pixel-wise features, progressively evolving coarse semantics into fine-grained part-level representations. The Semantics-guided Adjustment Module (SAM) then uses the final-stage prototypes to generate channel-wise fusion weights via a gating network, which control how textual color features are blended into the infrared embedding, while a text-guided spatial modulation and a visual-aware text refinement step align the two modalities before fusion. A novel fusion-weighted identity contrastive loss (L_FWIC) operates on ℓ2-normalized fusion weight vectors, enforcing that same-identity samples share similar channel-preference patterns while penalizing similar fusion behaviors across different identities, thereby reducing semantic ambiguity. Training combines identity classification, triplet, prototype diversity, center separation, and the FWIC loss with a ResNet-50 backbone optimized by SGD.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (all-search), GSC achieves 89.6% Rank-1 and 90.8% mAP, surpassing the previous best TVI-LFM by 4.7% in Rank-1 and 9.3% in mAP. Under the indoor-search protocol, GSC reaches 90.1% Rank-1 and 91.4% mAP. On RegDB (IR→VIS), GSC attains 91.2% Rank-1 and 87.0% mAP, and on the more challenging LLCM dataset it achieves 58.8% Rank-1 and 64.9% mAP, setting new state-of-the-art results across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSC outperforms all 12 compared baselines on SYSU-MM01 all-search, with the largest margin over TVI-LFM [6] (+4.7% Rank-1, +9.3% mAP) and ICRE [33] (+3.1% Rank-1, +18.1% mAP). On SYSU-MM01 indoor-search, GSC leads TVI-LFM by 1.0% Rank-1 and 0.6% mAP, while ICRE [33] actually achieves a slightly higher Rank-1 (91.6% vs. 90.1%) but GSC substantially outperforms it in mAP (91.4% vs. 82.9%). On RegDB, GSC achieves the best mAP (87.0%) but trails STAR-ReID [24] (93.3%) and DSFAD [2] (94.3%) in Rank-1, indicating that GSC's advantage on this dataset is primarily in retrieval precision rather than top-1 recall. On LLCM, GSC achieves the best Rank-1 (58.8%) and mAP (64.9%), edging out TVI-LFM [6] by 0.6% Rank-1 and ICRE [33] by 0.4% Rank-1, confirming consistent gains on the most challenging low-light multi-camera benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two complementary lines of work: (1) prototype-based part discovery for ReID (Fang et al., ICCV 2023; Li et al., CVPR 2021), which learns latent semantic parts via softmax assignment of pixel features to learnable prototypes, and (2) text-compensated VI-ReID (CSDN, TVI-LFM), which injects textual color descriptions to bridge the visible-infrared modality gap. The key departure is that prior prototype methods produce coarse-grained semantics unsuitable for region-specific text fusion, and prior text-based methods apply global, static fusion that ignores local semantic relevance. GSC bridges these by hierarchically refining prototypes across multiple stages to yield fine-grained part semantics, then using those semantics to generate channel-wise fusion weights that adaptively gate textual color information into infrared features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the quality of semantic priors is a critical bottleneck in text-assisted cross-modal ReID: coarse semantics cause color cues to be misallocated to irrelevant regions, and the hierarchical refinement mechanism directly addresses this. The fusion-weighted identity contrastive loss is a useful idea—operating on fusion weight vectors rather than feature embeddings to enforce identity-aware fusion behavior—but its contribution is modest (~1.9% mAP). A notable limitation is the fixed number of semantic prototypes, which the authors acknowledge as a constraint; dynamic prototype learning that adapts granularity per sample is left as future work. The approach also depends on manually annotated text descriptions, limiting scalability to settings where such annotations are unavailable.
