## Problem

Video generation models produce visually realistic outputs but fail to generate physically plausible deformation dynamics (melting, squeezing, fracturing) because they lack the ability to reason about where in the frame physical laws should apply. Existing VLM-guided approaches are restricted to rigid-body trajectories, and the semantic-spatial gap—where abstract physical keywords in text cannot be autonomously associated with the correct pixels—causes attention to dilute across irrelevant regions, producing physically inconsistent artifacts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single initial RGB image and a free-form text prompt describing a deformation action (e.g., 'the wax melts') and produces a multi-frame video of the resulting deformation dynamics. The base generator is Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, a flow-matching video model operating in latent space; the VLM reasoning agent (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and open-vocabulary detector (LLMDet) consume the image and prompt to produce a binary spatial-temporal mask downsampled to latent dimensions (T'×H'×W'), which is then injected into the generation pipeline.

## Method

DeforM introduces a VLM-guided physical reasoning module (DeforM-Reason) that identifies the target object from the prompt and image, grounds it with an open-vocabulary detector, and constructs a binary spatial-temporal mask aligned to video latents. Two alternative injection strategies then steer generation: DeforM-Free is a training-free method that scales cross-attention queries within masked regions by a focus factor α, empirically demonstrating that refocusing dispersed attention is sufficient for physical consistency; DeforM-Injection is a training-based method that uses a Dense Mask Modulator (3D convolutions with zero-initialized outputs) to generate learned offsets injected into both self-attention keys/values and cross-attention queries, trained with LoRA and a composite loss that up-weights reconstruction error within physics-critical regions via a region-sensitive term.

## Results

On the WISA-80K deformation test set, DeforM-Injection achieves the highest overall visual quality score (73.17) and physical consistency score (4.40/5) among all compared models. The most notable advantage is in Dynamic Degree (54.74 vs. 48.32 for the next-best CogVideoX1.5-I2V) and Local Deformation Fidelity (4.16 vs. 3.65 for Wan2.2-I2V-14B), confirming that the method generates more vivid and physically accurate deformation dynamics. DeforM-Injection also leads in Physical Commonsense (4.59) and Semantic Adherence (4.46), while slightly trailing Hunyuan-I2V in Temporal Flickering (97.24 vs. 99.30) and Motion Smoothness (98.40 vs. 99.54).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeforM-Injection outperforms all four baselines on the aggregate Physical Score (4.40 vs. 3.95 for Wan2.2-I2V-14B, 3.64 for CogVideoX1.5-I2V, 3.44 for MAGI-1, and 3.40 for Hunyuan-I2V) and on the aggregate Visual Quality Score (73.17 vs. 71.40 for CogVideoX1.5-I2V). The largest margin is in Dynamic Degree, where DeforM-Injection scores 54.74 compared to 48.32 (CogVideoX1.5-I2V) and 46.07 (Wan2.2-I2V-14B), while Hunyuan-I2V and MAGI-1 produce nearly static scenes (27.74 and 28.03 respectively). On physical sub-metrics, DeforM-Injection leads in all three dimensions—PC, SA, and LDF—with the most pronounced gap in LDF (4.16 vs. 3.65 for the next-best Wan2.2-I2V-14B). The only metrics where DeforM-Injection trails are Temporal Flickering and Motion Smoothness, where Hunyuan-I2V achieves slightly higher scores (99.30/99.54 vs. 97.24/98.40), likely because its near-static outputs trivially minimize flicker and motion artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeforM extends the VLM-guided physics-aware video generation line (VLIPP, PhyRPR) from rigid-body trajectory planning to deformation dynamics, where explicit trajectories or simple formulas are insufficient. It builds on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B as a flow-matching backbone and departs from simulator-based (PhysGen, PhysMotion) and reward-based (PhysRVG, PhysHPO) approaches by using a VLM reasoning module to produce spatial-temporal masks rather than relying on scene reconstruction or reward engineering. The core methodological contribution is the spatial-temporal mask injection into attention mechanisms, which is distinct from prior VLM-guided methods that plan bounding-box trajectories for rigid objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies attention dilution across irrelevant regions as the primary bottleneck for deformation generation, offering a concrete and actionable diagnosis that shifts focus from 'teach the model physics' to 'direct the model's attention to where physics matters.' The training-free DeforM-Free variant provides a lightweight, plug-and-play tool for mechanism analysis, while DeforM-Injection offers a stronger generator. Key limitations include dependence on the VLM's localization accuracy in complex multi-object scenes and inheritance of the base model's representational ceiling; whether the spatial-temporal masking strategy generalizes to multi-object simultaneous deformation or non-rigid interactions remains open.
