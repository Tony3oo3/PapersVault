## Problem

Existing plug-in defenses for adversarial robustness either hard-suppress non-robust feature channels (risking loss of useful information) or recalibrate them with additional MLPs whose contributions are hard to interpret. No prior method provides a single lightweight, differentiable mechanism that can both amplify and attenuate adversarial signals through the same learnable parameters, nor exploits the amplified signals as structured negative references during training.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) and outputs class labels via the backbone's final classifier head. The A3 plug-in is inserted after a specific residual block (block 4 in ResNet-18, block 3 in WRN-34-10) and processes intermediate activations of shape C×H×W. At inference, predictions are computed solely from the attenuated activations; the amplification branch is active only during training to supply negative references for the auxiliary losses.

## Method

A3 computes a channel-wise binary mask via a Gumbel-Softmax trick applied to a learned linear projection of globally-pooled activations, then derives two statistics (total and masked activation magnitudes) to form a logarithmic scaling factor bounded in [0,1]. Flipping the sign of this factor yields either attenuated activations z·[1−Scale] (used at inference) or amplified activations z·[1+Scale] (used only during training). Two novel losses exploit this duality: a hinge-based ranking loss that penalizes the CE gap between amplified and attenuated adversarial predictions, and a contrastive logit loss that pulls clean-attenuated and adversarial-attenuated logits together while pushing them apart from amplified logits. The entire module adds only ~0.27 M parameters and 0.002 G FLOPs to ResNet-18, and the logarithmic formulation was chosen over linear, quadratic, or exponential alternatives for its stable gradient behavior under adversarial perturbation-induced activation spikes.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 trained via AT, A3 achieves 58.66% PGD-20 robust accuracy and 47.28% AutoAttack accuracy, improving over the vanilla baseline by +9.54% and +3.00% respectively. Under TRADES, A3 reaches 55.10% PGD-20 and 49.27% AA on CIFAR-10, and under MART it reaches 59.05% PGD-20 and 47.29% AA. On CIFAR-100 (AT), A3 achieves 32.34% PGD-20 and 24.36% AA, outperforming all compared plug-in defenses. The module adds only 0.27M parameters and 0.0021G FLOPs to ResNet-18, far less than FSR (+1.26M) or FTA2C (+10.71M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A3 achieves the highest robust accuracy across all three training strategies (AT, TRADES, MART) on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, outperforming every compared plug-in defense. The largest gains appear under AT training on CIFAR-10, where A3 improves PGD-20 accuracy by +9.54% over the vanilla baseline and by +6.63% over the strongest baseline (FTA2C at 52.03%). Under TRADES, A3 leads by +2.11% PGD-20 over the best baseline (FTA2C at 52.99%) and by +0.59% AA over PFCM (48.67%). Under MART, A3 gains +6.41% PGD-20 over the baseline and +6.41% over the next-best method (RiFT at 52.87%). On CIFAR-100, A3 consistently leads by 2–6% PGD-20 over all baselines across all training strategies. In terms of efficiency, A3 adds only 0.27M parameters and 0.0021G FLOPs to ResNet-18, compared to +1.26M/+0.0201G for FSR and +10.71M/+0.1711G for FTA2C, while delivering substantially higher robust accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

A3 repurposes the activation-scaling intuition from OOD detection (ASH, SCALE) for adversarial robustness, replacing the non-differentiable top-k selection with a learnable Gumbel-Softmax mask and a logarithmic scaling factor to ensure differentiability and numerical stability. Conceptually, it departs from prior plug-in feature-manipulation modules (CAS, CIFS, FSR, FTA2C, EMFF) that suppress or recalibrate non-robust features via additional MLPs; instead, A3 uses a single bounded scaling operation that can both amplify and attenuate activations, with the amplified signal serving as a training-time negative reference for novel contrastive and ranking losses. The module is compatible with standard adversarial training strategies (AT, TRADES, MART) without modifying the backbone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A3 achieves substantial robust accuracy gains (e.g., +6.6% PGD-20 on CIFAR-10/AT over the best baseline) while adding only 0.27M parameters and 0.002G FLOPs to ResNet-18, far less than competing masking-based modules like FTA2C (+10.71M params). The dual-mode training paradigm—using deliberately degraded (amplified) predictions as negative references to sharpen the attenuated (inference) path—offers a lightweight alternative to heavier recalibration blocks. However, all experiments are confined to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet with ResNet-18 and WRN-34-10; whether the approach scales to ImageNet, deeper transformers, or stronger attack budgets (ε=16/255) remains open. The modest clean-accuracy trade-off and the sensitivity of the contrastive loss hyperparameters (λ_cl, τ_cl) also warrant further investigation for practical deployment.
