## Problem

Existing natural-language-controllable driving scene editors face a fundamental trade-off: agentic pipelines (e.g., ChatSim) rely on unimodal text reasoning, naive compositing, and lack feedback loops, resulting in poor photorealism and inaccurate trajectories; while implicit world models (e.g., Cosmos) achieve visual quality but sacrifice object-level controllability and unintentionally alter scene structure. No prior method simultaneously achieves photorealism, instruction alignment, structure preservation, and traffic realism for fine-grained, multi-object driving video editing.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an 8-second, 10 FPS front-camera driving video, a free-form natural language instruction (e.g., 'insert a blue sedan 3 meters to the left of ego vehicle, 7 meters ahead, and make it change to the right lane'), and a scene map with original object trajectories. It produces an edited 8-second driving video and an updated scene graph in which the static background (3DGS primitives) is preserved while dynamic object nodes (vehicles, pedestrians) are removed, inserted, replaced, or re-trajectored. A one-time 3D reconstruction preprocessing step (~2 hours per scene) is required before editing.

## Method

LangDriveCTRL represents each video as an explicit 3D scene graph (static 3DGS background + dynamic object nodes with trajectories) and orchestrates a feedback-driven pipeline of specialized agents and tools. An LLM-based Orchestrator decomposes instructions into object-level sub-tasks and generates executable Python scripts to invoke downstream modules. A VLM-powered Object Grounding Agent localizes target nodes by jointly reasoning over SAM-segmented appearance, heuristic trajectory descriptions, and lane-based spatial relations. For behavior editing, a Behavior Editing Agent generates counterfactual behavior combinations (replace/remove/keep/add on each original behavior), filters them against the map, and feeds the selected combination as text condition to LangTraj, a diffusion-based multi-object trajectory simulator; a Behavior Reviewer Agent then iteratively validates trajectories and adjusts classifier-free guidance weights or adds collision/off-road avoidance guidance until alignment is achieved. Finally, a fine-tuned CogVideoX video diffusion tool harmonizes the coarse render, and a Video Reviewer Agent iteratively tunes denoising strength and L2 latent-space guidance to balance photorealism against appearance preservation of inserted vehicles.

## Results

On 30 curated Waymo scenes, LangDriveCTRL achieves FID 32.85 and FVD 467.20 for photorealism, outperforming Cosmos (FID 33.42, FVD 797.51) and ChatSim (FID 47.70, FVD 605.69). For instruction alignment, it reaches 82.19% appearance alignment and 71.67% behavior alignment, nearly 2× higher than Cosmos (46.25%/32.86%) and ChatSim (42.33%/26.64%). Structure preservation (DINO distance 34.62) and traffic realism (0.58% collision rate, 1.73% off-road rate) are also best-in-class, while editing time (17.1 min/scene) is comparable to both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LangDriveCTRL is compared against two state-of-the-art baselines: Cosmos (a diffusion-based world model that edits videos in pixel space) and ChatSim (an LLM-agent-based pipeline using explicit 3D representations). Against Cosmos, LangDriveCTRL wins on every metric: instruction alignment more than doubles (82.19% vs. 46.25% appearance; 71.67% vs. 32.86% behavior), structure preservation improves dramatically (34.62 vs. 74.07 DINO distance), and FVD drops from 797.51 to 467.20, while FID is marginally better (32.85 vs. 33.42). Against ChatSim, the advantage is even more pronounced: photorealism improves (FID 32.85 vs. 47.70), instruction alignment roughly doubles, and traffic realism is far superior (0.58% collision vs. 27.62%; 1.73% off-road vs. 24.71%). In user studies, LangDriveCTRL is preferred by 54.2% of participants for photorealism, 60.4% for instruction alignment, 65.0% for structure preservation, and 48.3% for traffic realism, versus single-digit or low-double-digit percentages for both baselines. Editing latency is comparable across all three methods (16.9–19.3 min per scene on a single A6000 GPU).

## Relation to Prior Work

LangDriveCTRL sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: explicit 3D scene editing via 3D Gaussian Splatting (building on Omnire's scene decomposition) and diffusion-based video generation (using CogVideoX and LangTraj). It departs from prior agentic pipelines (ChatSim, AutoVFX, ChatDyn) in three key ways: replacing unimodal LLM reasoning with multi-modal VLM agents for object grounding, replacing naive compositing with a fine-tuned video diffusion tool for harmonization, and introducing iterative feedback loops (Behavior Reviewer and Video Reviewer agents) absent from all prior feed-forward pipelines. It also contrasts with implicit world models like Cosmos, which achieve photorealism but sacrifice object-level controllability and scene structure preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the controllability–photorealism tradeoff in driving scene editing can be substantially narrowed by combining explicit 3D scene graphs with diffusion-based refinement in a closed-loop agentic pipeline, which is practically important for generating diverse safety-critical training data for autonomous driving stacks. The ablation revealing that video diffusion actively degrades appearance alignment (87.7% → 65.5%) without the reviewer agent, yet is necessary for photorealism, highlights a general tension in diffusion-based editing that other domains may face. Limitations include the ~2-hour 3D reconstruction preprocessing per scene, restriction to front-camera 8-second clips at 10 FPS, and reliance on GPT-4/GPT-4o for agent reasoning, which constrains scalability and deployment. Open questions include extending to multi-camera rigs, longer temporal horizons, pedestrian-centric editing, and reducing the per-scene reconstruction cost.
