## Problem

4D Gaussian representations for dynamic scene novel-view synthesis suffer from aliasing artifacts when rendering from viewpoints that diverge from training cameras (zoom-in/zoom-out). Existing static anti-aliasing filters like Mip-Splatting fail in this setting because they assign a single, time-invariant sampling rate per primitive, ignoring the fact that motion causes the focal-to-depth ratio (f/d) to vary substantially over time for dynamic regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262304/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB video (15–20 synchronized cameras on Plenoptic Video; 8 synthetic scenes on D-NeRF) along with novel camera poses at arbitrary focal lengths and resolutions. It is trained at a single downsampled scale (e.g., 1× = 676×507 or 200×200) and produces alias-free renderings at multiple scales up to 4× (zoom-in) or down to 1/4× (zoom-out). Beyond the rendered image, the method outputs a per-primitive, time-varying sampling-rate curve ν̂(t) that drives the anti-aliasing filter at inference.

## Method

The key mechanism is a motion-aware 3D smoothing filter that replaces Mip-Splatting's constant per-primitive sampling rate with a time-varying one estimated via non-parametric joint kernel density estimation. For each Gaussian primitive, the paper accumulates (t, f/d) pairs across all training views and timestamps, normalizes f/d to [0,1] per primitive for scale invariance, and fits a 2D Parzen-window density using a Weibull kernel along the f/d axis (chosen because k>1 biases toward maxima, matching the max operation in the Nyquist-based sampling-rate definition) and a Gaussian kernel along the time axis. At inference, for a query timestamp t, the method extracts the conditional density slice P(X|T=t) and takes its mode to obtain the appropriate sampling rate, which is then rescaled back to f/d units and plugged into the standard Gaussian low-pass convolution (Eq. 5–6) applied to the projected 3D Gaussian. Because the filter operates on projected 3D Gaussians and merely substitutes ν̂(t) for the constant ν_k, it is representation-agnostic and can be attached to any 4D Gaussian pipeline (demonstrated with both SARO-GS and SpeeDe3DGS).

## Results

On the Plenoptic Video dataset (single-scale training, multi-scale evaluation), the method achieves an average PSNR of 31.01 dB, SSIM of 0.926, and LPIPS of 0.155, outperforming the strongest baseline SARO-GS (29.85 dB) by 1.16 dB on average and by 2.11 dB at 4× resolution. On the D-NeRF synthetic dataset, the method reaches 34.50 dB average PSNR, surpassing Grid4D (32.97 dB) by 1.53 dB and SARO-GS (31.12 dB) by 3.38 dB. Temporal consistency is also improved, with the lowest warped temporal error (0.608) and highest warped SSIM (0.9946) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is built on SARO-GS and outperforms it by 2.11 dB PSNR at 4× resolution on Plenoptic Video and 5.21 dB on D-NeRF, confirming that the motion-aware filter adds substantial value over the base representation. Against 4DRotorGS (the strongest baseline at 4× on Plenoptic with 29.10 dB), the method gains 0.69 dB; against Grid4D, the gap widens to 3.15 dB at 4×. On the D-NeRF synthetic dataset, the method leads all baselines at 2× and 4×, with the largest margins over 4DRotorGS (+7.53 dB avg) and 4DGS (+6.78 dB avg), while Grid4D (the closest competitor) still trails by 1.53 dB. At the training resolution (1×), the method is comparable to or slightly below baselines (e.g., 32.52 vs. 32.75 PSNR vs. SARO-GS on Plenoptic), which is expected since the band-limiting filter suppresses high-frequency overfitting. When integrated with the architecturally different SpeeDe3DGS, the filter still yields a 4.45 dB PSNR gain at 4×, demonstrating representation-agnostic effectiveness. The method also achieves the best temporal consistency metrics (lowest warped error 0.608, highest warped SSIM 0.9946), outperforming 4DGS (0.780) and 4DRotorGS (0.769) in frame-to-frame coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mip-Splatting's static 3D Gaussian low-pass filter to the dynamic setting by replacing the per-primitive constant sampling rate with a time-varying one estimated via Parzen window kernel density estimation (Weibull kernel for f/d, Gaussian kernel for time). It builds on SARO-GS as the base 4D representation but is explicitly representation-agnostic, demonstrated by integration with the architecturally different SpeeDe3DGS. The key conceptual departure from prior anti-aliasing work (Mip-NeRF, Mip-Splatting, EWA splatting) is recognizing that in dynamic scenes, a primitive's depth—and hence its required sampling interval—changes over time, making a single static filter fundamentally inadequate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Aliasing artifacts in dynamic 4D representations are a practical barrier for AR/VR novel-view synthesis, and this paper provides a lightweight, plug-and-play fix that works across multiple 4D architectures without modifying their core training. The main open limitation is the O(NDT) memory footprint of storing per-primitive joint density matrices, which the authors acknowledge but leave for future work (compression, shared networks, motion clustering). Broader questions remain about whether this KDE-based approach generalizes to scenes with very rapid or topologically complex motion, and whether the technique transfers to feed-forward 4D reconstruction methods.
