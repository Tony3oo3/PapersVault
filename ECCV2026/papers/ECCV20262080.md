## Problem

Category-level 6D pose estimation of articulated objects from a single view is severely degraded by occlusion-induced ambiguity and entangled pose-shape representations. Existing generative methods model only the pose distribution and ignore shape uncertainty during hypothesis scoring, so high-scoring poses can correspond to geometrically implausible shapes. Real-world depth noise further amplifies this failure mode.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262080/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view point cloud (from a depth sensor, potentially noisy and partial) together with the known kinematic topology of the object category (tree of P rigid parts with revolute or prismatic joints). It outputs a ranked set of pose-shape hypothesis pairs: per-part SE(3) poses in the observation space and a canonical-shape SDF, with K=1024 hypotheses generated and filtered to the top δK by a two-stage consistency score. For real-world (HOI4D) inputs, a denoising step precedes verification; for clean synthetic (PM-Videos) inputs it is omitted.

## Method

The framework has three stages. First, a Pose-Shape Hypothesis Generator learns a VAE shape prior (PointNet++ encoder → Triplane-MLP SDF decoder with Eikonal regularization) and then a Glow normalizing flow conditioned on Mamba3D global features to model the joint distribution p(t, z | O), producing K intrinsically coupled pose-shape pairs. Second, a Part-Level Point Cloud Denoising module (coarse-to-fine completion built on PointAttN) recovers clean, part-wise dense point clouds from noisy depth. Third, a Cross-Space Consistency Verification module performs two-stage pruning: (i) joint consistency transforms observed joint parameters into canonical space and scores angular/distance agreement with the hypothesized joints, aggregating the top δ₁K base-part poses; (ii) shape consistency transforms the denoised child-part points into canonical space and computes the mean absolute SDF value at the zero level set, yielding a continuous, density-robust score that replaces Chamfer Distance. The highest-scoring hypothesis pair is returned as the final prediction.

## Results

On the real-world HOI4D benchmark (severe occlusion, sensor noise), the method achieves 61.0% mAP at 5°/5 cm, a 16.3-percentage-point improvement over the strongest baseline PPF-Tracker (44.7%), with mean rotation error of 4.51° and translation error of 0.032 m—both best among all compared methods. On the synthetic PM-Videos dataset, the method attains the lowest mean rotation error in every category (e.g., 2.3° on Drawer vs. 5.2° for PPF-Tracker) while remaining competitive on translation error despite operating as a single-view estimator rather than a sequence-based tracker. For canonical shape reconstruction, the method reduces CD-L₂ to 0.016 overall versus 0.054 for CARTO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest single-view baseline PPF-Tracker (AAAI 2026), the method improves mAP by 16.3 pp (61.0% vs. 44.7%), reduces R_err by 2.3° (4.51° vs. 6.81°), and lowers T_err by 6 mm (0.032 m vs. 0.038 m) on HOI4D; on PM-Videos it wins R_err in all five categories (e.g., 2.3° vs. 5.2° on Drawer) but trails slightly on T_err, which is expected since PPF-Tracker exploits inter-frame smoothness. Against regression-based baselines ANCSH (CVPR 2020) and RPF (NeurIPS 2025), the gains are dramatic—mAP more than doubles (61.0% vs. 31.0% and 31.2%) and R_err drops from 15.54°/9.48° to 4.51°. ContactArt (3DV 2024) is competitive on Laptop mAP (62.5%) but degrades sharply on multi-part categories (24.0% on StorageFurniture), where the proposed method reaches 52.9%. Sequence-based trackers CAPTRA (ICCV 2021) and GAPS (ICASSP 2025) are only evaluated on PM-Videos; the method outperforms both on R_err across all categories. For shape reconstruction, the method reduces CD-L₂ from 0.054 (CARTO, CVPR 2023) to 0.016, a 3.4× improvement, confirming that the joint pose-shape generation yields more accurate canonical geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the category-level articulated object pose estimation line (ANCSH, ContactArt, RPF) by replacing single-point regression with a generative-verification paradigm, and departs from prior generative pose methods (Diff-Art, Jain et al.) that model only the pose distribution by jointly modeling pose and shape in a shared latent space via a Glow normalizing flow. The cross-space consistency verification draws on the analysis-by-synthesis tradition (OP-Align, SE(3)-equivariant self-supervision) but repurposes it as an explicit inference-time pruning criterion rather than a training-time loss, and substitutes the commonly used Chamfer Distance with an SDF-based continuous score for smoother evaluation. The part-level denoising module is adapted from the rigid-object completion framework PointAttN, extended to handle per-part segmentation and articulated geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16.3-point mAP gain over the strongest baseline on HOI4D demonstrates that explicitly coupling pose and shape uncertainty and verifying geometric consistency at inference time is a practical recipe for robust articulated-object perception under severe occlusion, which is critical for safe robot manipulation. Key limitations remain: the framework assumes the base part is largely visible (a strong assumption in cluttered scenes), requires known kinematic topology and joint types, and relies on complete-shape priors, limiting open-world generalization. Open questions include whether the SDF-based verification can be extended to handle unknown or variable topologies, and whether the generation-verification paradigm scales to objects with more than a handful of parts without combinatorial explosion in hypothesis space.
