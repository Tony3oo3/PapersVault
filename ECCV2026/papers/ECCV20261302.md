## Problem

VLA models trained in a source domain degrade substantially when deployed under environmental shifts such as camera viewpoint changes or embodiment transfers, yet existing adaptation methods require task-wise expert demonstrations that are costly to collect. One-shot fine-tuning on a single target-domain demonstration fails to generalize to held-out tasks because the resulting weight update is dominated by task-specific rather than domain-specific directions, leaving no practical method to adapt a multi-task VLA policy with only one demonstration of one task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261302/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a base VLA policy (π0.5 or π0-FAST) trained on multi-task source-domain data, a single target-domain demonstration of one adaptation task (camera images at the shifted viewpoint plus the corresponding action sequence), and the matching source-domain demonstration of that same task. It produces a single set of adapted model weights that, when deployed, accept the same standard VLA inputs (third-person and wrist RGB images plus a language task instruction) and output an action distribution across all tasks in the target domain, not just the one used for adaptation.

## Method

DART exploits the empirically validated property that one-shot fine-tuned update-vectors decompose additively into orthogonal task-specific and domain-specific directions in weight space. It extracts a domain vector by subtracting a source-domain update-vector from a target-domain update-vector (both obtained by 1,000-step BC fine-tuning on the same task), which cancels shared task directions via an analogy operation. To suppress fine-tuning noise and source-domain artifacts, DART applies SVD-based subspace filtering that retains only source basis vectors whose overlap energy with the target subspace exceeds a dynamic threshold derived from the subspace alignment score, followed by subspace scaling that multiplies the resulting domain vector by the alignment score to down-weight misaligned, noise-dominant components. The adapted policy is then θ* = θ0 + α·δ̃_tgt with a single scalar α, requiring no architectural modification and generalizing across flow-matching and autoregressive VLA backbones.

## Results

On LIBERO with π0.5 under novel camera viewpoints, DART achieves a 79.1% average success rate across 40 tasks, outperforming the next-best baseline FLA (74.3%) by 4.8 pp and RETAIN (69.6%) by 9.5 pp. Under combined visual perturbations (viewpoint + noise + illumination), DART maintains a 75.0% average, again leading all baselines. In the real-world UR10e setup, DART reaches 81.7% average success across five tasks from a single Stack Cube demonstration, compared to 55.0% for FLA and 48.3% for RETAIN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DART outperforms all six compared baselines. Against the two primary VLA adaptation baselines, DART leads FLA [26] by 4.8 pp (79.1% vs 74.3%) and RETAIN [53] by 9.5 pp (79.1% vs 69.6%) on LIBERO novel viewpoints with π0.5; the same ordering holds on π0-FAST (79.4% vs 76.6% vs 76.5%) and in the real-world setup (81.7% vs 55.0% vs 48.3%). For the model-merging comparison in Table 7, where three domain vectors are consolidated into one, DART's merged vector achieves 79.1% average success, beating TSV [15] (75.7%), Iso-C [35] (74.8%), TA [18] (74.5%), and TIES [52] (70.9%), confirming that the extracted domain vectors are more composable than raw task update-vectors merged by standard methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DART directly extends Task Arithmetic's analogy operation (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023) from language-model capability composition to VLA domain adaptation, repurposing the 'queen = king + woman − man' subtraction to isolate a domain-specific weight direction. The subspace filtering and scaling mechanisms adapt SVD-based alignment techniques from model merging (Marczak et al., ICML 2025), but invert their purpose: rather than preserving each model's unique subspaces, DART identifies and retains only the *shared* singular components between source and target updates to cleanly cancel task-specific directions. This departs from prior VLA adaptation work (RETAIN, FLA) that either fine-tunes specific modules or merges full models, by operating purely in weight space with a single demonstration and no architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-shot VLA adaptation addresses a critical deployment bottleneck: collecting task-wise expert demonstrations in every new environment is prohibitively expensive, especially in household or industrial settings. The method's architecture-agnosticism (validated on both flow-matching π₀.₅ and autoregressive π₀-FAST) and compositional domain vectors (mergeable across multiple target domains) make it practically deployable. Key limitations remain: performance degrades under severe shifts (Large viewpoint), the scalar α requires a small per-environment search, and the ablation reveals that subspace filtering/scaling contribute only ~1 pp over raw subtraction, suggesting the core analogy operation does most of the work. Open questions include hyperparameter-free per-layer scaling and whether the additive task-domain decomposition holds under more extreme embodiment changes.
