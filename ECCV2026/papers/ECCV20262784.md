## Problem

Conventional Mixture-of-Experts routing for embodied agents lacks an explicit notion of scale, making it impossible to target test-time updates to the relevant knowledge granularity (mixture challenge), and a single uniform update policy cannot respect the fact that low-level physical dynamics change rapidly while high-level abstract rules remain stable (evolution challenge). These two limitations prevent MoE-based world models from adapting selectively and at appropriate rates in non-stationary environments.

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives an egocentric RGB(-D) observation, a natural-language instruction, the current action, and the accumulated observation sequence within the episode. The frozen CLIP-ViT-Large encoder maps observations to 384-d embeddings used to compute experiential distance; the 3D scale space is integrated via 16 Monte Carlo samples. Output is a top-4 mixture of 15 world models (across 3 groups) that jointly predict the next observation and produce an executable action plan, with per-group knowledge states updated at test time via prediction-error-driven increments.

## Method

MuSix decomposes routing into two stages: a continuous meta-router maps experiential distance (Mahalanobis distance between the current observation embedding and an exponentially-weighted Gaussian fit over past observations) to a softmax weight function over a 3D scale space, and per-scale base routers then select world models within the identified scale. A scale-aware loss aligns the expected scale magnitude with normalized experiential distance, concentrates the scale distribution, and regularizes meta-router output magnitude. For adaptation, prediction-error knowledge increments are distributed across groups via meta-router weights and integrated with scale-dependent forgetting rates (exponentially decaying from low to high scale), while learned gated bidirectional transfer between neighboring groups maintains hierarchical coherence. The entire framework is trained with a three-phase curriculum (router-only, then world models, then diversity regularization) on top of a Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct backbone with LoRA adapters, and the knowledge states are updated at test time without retraining the backbone.

## Results

On EmbodiedBench, MuSix achieves 40.44% average success rate on EB-Habitat and 57.92% on EB-Navigation, outperforming the strongest baseline SayCanPay (34.39% and 47.20%) by 6.05 and 10.72 percentage points respectively. On HAZARD, MuSix attains the highest rescue value in the fire scenario (43.71 vs. 42.22 for FLARE) and the lowest damage ratio in the flood scenario (4.65 vs. 6.60–7.49 for baselines). On real-world Franka Research 3 manipulation, MuSix reaches 56.2% average success rate, comparable to SayCanPay (58.4%) while achieving the best worst-case task performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuSix outperforms all three external baselines on both benchmarks. On EmbodiedBench (EB-Habitat), MuSix (40.44%) beats SayCanPay (34.39%) by 6.05 pp, FLARE (27.11%) by 13.33 pp, and LLM-Planner (23.11%) by 17.33 pp; the gap is even larger on EB-Navigation where MuSix (57.92%) leads SayCanPay (47.20%) by 10.72 pp. On HAZARD Fire, MuSix (43.71) surpasses FLARE (42.22) by 1.49 pp and SayCanPay (41.07) by 2.64 pp in rescue value, while also achieving the lowest damage ratio (1.95 vs. 2.53–2.89). On HAZARD Flood, rescue values are statistically tied across methods (~49.8), but MuSix reduces damage ratio to 4.65 versus 6.60–7.49 for all baselines, indicating more efficient rescue behavior. Against the internal Conventional MoE ablation (same backbone, standard top-k routing without scale awareness), MuSix gains 14.88 pp on EB-Habitat and 28.64 pp on EB-Navigation, confirming that the scale-aware two-stage routing is the primary source of improvement rather than the multi-expert architecture alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mixture-of-Experts architectures for embodied agents by introducing an explicit scale axis grounded in Construal Level Theory, departing from conventional MoE where routing operates over latent representations without any notion of knowledge granularity. It builds on hierarchical world model decompositions (e.g., Gumbsch et al., Zadem et al.) but adds the missing test-time adaptation mechanism: scale-dependent forgetting rates and gated inter-scale transfer allow each level to evolve at its own rate. The experiential distance metric (Mahalanobis distance in a frozen encoder space) operationalizes CLT's psychological-distance-to-abstraction mapping as a continuous routing signal, a connection not previously exploited in MoE-based systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in embodied AI: agents deployed in non-stationary environments need to update low-level reactive knowledge quickly while preserving stable high-level abstractions, and conventional MoE cannot target updates at a specific scale. However, the real-world validation is limited to 8 tasks on a single Franka arm where MuSix actually trails SayCanPay in average success rate, and the 39.7% latency overhead from two-stage routing and Monte Carlo integration is a non-trivial cost. Open questions include whether the number and composition of world model groups can be discovered automatically, whether the CLT-inspired scale axis generalizes beyond the 3D space tested, and whether the approach scales to longer-horizon or multi-robot settings.
