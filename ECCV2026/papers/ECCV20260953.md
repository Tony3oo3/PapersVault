## Problem

Depth foundation models (DFMs) achieve strong monocular depth estimation from learned priors but remain fundamentally scale-ambiguous because a single RGB image lacks physical depth cues. Existing solutions to resolve this ambiguity rely on active sensors (LiDAR) or computationally expensive inference-time optimization, deviating from a strict passive monocular setting. This paper asks whether a compact passive optical element can supply the missing metric depth cues directly to a DFM within a single monocular capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260953/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system captures two polarization-resolved images (I_x, I_y) simultaneously in a single exposure through a 3-mm-diameter birefringent TiO₂ metalens operating at 590 nm on a 20-MP monochrome CMOS sensor, with a depth range of 20–120 cm. These two channels are converted to a three-channel pseudo-RGB input (I_x, I_y, (I_x+I_y)/2) that matches the standard RGB interface of monocular depth models without any architectural modification. The output is a dense per-pixel metric depth map in meters, predicted by a fine-tuned DPT-based ViT backbone.

## Method

A custom birefringent metalens composed of 700-nm-tall TiO₂ nanopillars independently modulates the optical phase for x- and y-polarized light, engineering a depth-dependent rotating PSF in each channel via concentric pupil rings with increasing topological charge; the two PSFs are set 180° apart so their positional shifts encode a monotonic disparity vector proportional to 1/z. A simple input adaptation strategy maps the two polarization channels to three channels, preserving scene semantics while injecting physical depth cues, enabling direct fine-tuning of a pretrained Depth Anything V2 (DPT/ViT) without adding auxiliary branches or fusion modules. To generate large-scale training pairs, a disocclusion-aware optical forward model simulates the metalens response from RGB-D data using FFT-based Kirchhoff diffraction, with explicit handling of occlusion edges, disocclusion gaps, and aliasing fringes at depth discontinuities. Polarization-aware data augmentation (global scaling, local brightness perturbation, Poisson/Gaussian noise, Gaussian blur) plus a few manually annotated real samples bridge the remaining sim-to-real gap. The model is fine-tuned for 80k steps on Hypersim with L1 + gradient loss, and ablations confirm that both the physical optical encoding and the pretrained DFM priors are necessary for the observed gains.

## Results

On NYU Depth V2 (simulated), Ours-Base achieves MAE 0.022, RMSE 0.039, AbsRel 0.036, and δ₀.₅ 0.957, outperforming all LiDAR-free monocular metric depth baselines and matching the LiDAR-assisted PromptDA (MAE 0.021, RMSE 0.042). On the zero-shot MIT-CGH-4k benchmark, Ours-Base (MAE 0.068, δ₀.₅ 0.772) substantially outperforms the next-best LiDAR-free method UniDepth v2 (MAE 0.127, δ₀.₅ 0.360), confirming robust decoding of physical depth cues without semantic priors. In physical experiments, Ours-Base achieves MAE 0.032 and δ₀.₅ 0.895, approaching PromptDA's MAE 0.030 and δ₀.₅ 0.951. Against depth-from-defocus baselines on FlyingThings3D, Ours-Small (MAE 0.022, δ₁ 0.995) outperforms DeepDfD (MAE 0.089) and Split-Aperture (MAE 0.086) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 LiDAR-free monocular metric depth baselines (Depth Anything v2/v3, DepthPro, Lotus, Marigold, Metric3D v2, MoGe v2, UniDepth v2, ZoeDepth) on both NYU Depth V2 and MIT-CGH-4k, with the largest margins on the zero-shot MIT-CGH-4k benchmark where semantic priors are absent (e.g., δ₀.₅ 0.772 vs. 0.360 for UniDepth v2). Against the LiDAR-assisted PromptDA, the method is highly competitive on NYU Depth V2 (MAE 0.022 vs. 0.021, δ₀.₅ 0.957 vs. 0.955) but slightly trails on MIT-CGH-4k (MAE 0.068 vs. 0.058) and physical experiments (MAE 0.032 vs. 0.030, δ₀.₅ 0.895 vs. 0.951). In the depth-from-defocus comparison on FlyingThings3D, Ours-Small achieves MAE 0.022 and δ₁ 0.995, substantially beating DeepDfD (MAE 0.089, δ₁ 0.941) and Split-Aperture (MAE 0.086, δ₁ 0.993). Fine-tuning Depth Anything v2 without the physical depth encoding does not improve over zero-shot performance, confirming that gains come from the optical cues rather than dataset-specific adaptation. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that the pretrained DFM prior is the primary driver of the DfD advantage, while the metalens design provides additional physical grounding beyond DeepDfD's optics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: (1) depth foundation models (Depth Anything V2, Marigold, Metric3D) that learn rich geometric priors from large-scale data but remain scale-ambiguous without physical cues, and (2) metasurface/metalens optics that encode depth in engineered PSFs (rotating, double-helix, defocus) but have been limited to task-specific networks trained from scratch. The key departure is bridging these: prior metalens depth systems (Shen et al. 2023, Ghanekar et al. 2022) and DfD methods (Ikoma et al. 2021, Shi et al. 2024) never leveraged pretrained DFM priors, while LiDAR-assisted DFMs (PromptDA, Park et al. 2024) require active multi-sensor hardware. This paper is the first to combine a passive birefringent metalens with a pretrained DFM via a minimal input adaptation, and introduces a disocclusion-aware optical forward model to close the sim-to-real gap for asymmetric PSFs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 3-mm passive metalens can bring a monocular DFM to performance comparable to a LiDAR-assisted dual-sensor system (PromptDA) is a strong proof of concept for physically grounded depth without active illumination, with direct implications for compact depth sensing in VR/AR, miniature robotics, and medical endoscopy. However, the current prototype is severely constrained (f/11.3 aperture, 10-nm bandpass, 20–120 cm range, limited FOV), and the ablation reveals that the pretrained prior—not the optical design—is the dominant performance driver, raising the question of how much the metalens contributes beyond what a well-pretrained DFM already captures. Open questions include end-to-end metalens-DFM co-design, scaling to larger apertures and wider depth ranges, and robustness to real-world polarization imbalance from specular surfaces.
