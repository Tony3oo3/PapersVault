## Problem

Existing cross-view geo-localization methods are trapped between coarse image-level retrieval (which cannot provide position or orientation within the matched aerial image) and expensive fine-grained alternatives (pose estimation requiring pairwise alignment and continuous regression, or object-level localization requiring laborious instance annotations). This paper argues that the geometric inclusion relationship between a limited-FoV ground view and an omnidirectional aerial scene naturally defines a wedge-shaped sector, enabling fine-grained localization and orientation inference without either continuous pose supervision or manual instance labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a limited-FoV ground query (dynamically cropped from North-aligned panoramas resized to 140×768, with FoV ∈ {70°, 90°, 135°, 180°} and unknown orientation) and a gallery of geo-referenced aerial images resized to 384×384. It outputs a joint index pair (j*, p*) identifying the best-matched aerial image and its optimal azimuthal sector, from which the discrete camera heading θ̂ is directly read off. The sector candidates are generated by rotating a sector-shaped mask of angular span φ around the aerial image center in steps of Δθ (default 50°, yielding P=7 sectors), so the output orientation is inherently quantized to the sector grid.

## Method

SectorGeo uses a shared ConvNeXt-Base backbone in a dual-branch design: the ground branch produces a single L2-normalized global descriptor, while the aerial branch retains a dense spatial feature map that is decomposed via Azimuthal Feature Decoupling—rotating sector-shaped binary masks around the image center and applying masked average pooling to yield P orientation-aware descriptors. Matching proceeds through two coupled streams: a Relational Retrieval Stream that first reconstructs direction-specific ground context via Azimuthal Context Aggregation (attention-weighted modulation of the ground descriptor by sector affinities), then refines sector-wise similarity embeddings through T iterative Cross-Sector Relational Reasoning blocks (self-attention over the P sector nodes) to produce a robust image-level retrieval score; and a lightweight Azimuthal Alignment Stream that computes raw cosine similarity between the ground descriptor and each sector descriptor to directly localize the best-matching sector. Training jointly optimizes an InfoNCE contrastive loss on the retrieval score and a cross-entropy loss on the alignment logits with Ground-Truth Soft Labels (linear interpolation weights assigned to the two nearest sector indices based on angular distance to the true heading), avoiding the instability of continuous pose regression while preserving sub-sector geometric information.

## Results

On the repurposed CVACT benchmark at the most constrained 70° FoV, SectorGeo achieves R@1 = 41.50% and S-R@1 = 39.81%, a 30.17% absolute improvement in R@1 over the second-best method (PLGeo, 11.33%). On CVUSA at the same setting, it reaches R@1 = 63.79% and S-R@1 = 59.92%. At the wider 180° FoV, SectorGeo attains R@1 = 87.87% / S-R@1 = 83.67% on CVUSA and R@1 = 57.38% / S-R@1 = 46.11% on CVACT, maintaining a consistent gap between image-level and sector-level metrics that other methods fail to preserve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SectorGeo outperforms all six baselines (FRGeo, GeoDTR, GeoDTR+, Sample4Geo, ConGeo, PLGeo) across every FoV setting on both CVUSA and CVACT. The margin is largest under tight FoV constraints: on CVACT at 70°, SectorGeo's R@1 of 41.50% exceeds PLGeo (11.33%) by 30.17 points and ConGeo (8.31%) by 33.19 points. On CVUSA at 70°, it surpasses the next-best PLGeo (44.56%) by 19.23 points in R@1. A critical distinction is sector-level consistency: PLGeo's S-R@1 drops sharply relative to its R@1 (e.g., 53.94→40.42 on CVUSA at 70°, a 13.5-point gap), whereas SectorGeo maintains a much smaller gap (63.79→59.92, only 3.87 points), confirming that its azimuthal scanning genuinely localizes the correct sub-region rather than merely retrieving a visually similar image. GeoDTR and GeoDTR+ are consistently the weakest baselines, trailing SectorGeo by over 50 points in R@1 at 70° FoV on CVUSA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground by reframing cross-view orientation estimation as a discrete sector-retrieval problem rather than the continuous 3-DoF pose regression used by methods like SliceMatch or the pairwise geometric offset approaches of Shi et al. Architecturally it is a standard ConvNeXt dual-branch encodertractor with InfoNCE contrastive training, but the key departure is the azimuthal scanning mechanism that decomposes the aerial feature map into overlapping directional sectors, a structural idea not present in prior image-level retrieval methods (Sample4Geo, ConGeo, PLGeo) which treat the aerial image holistically. It also repurposes existing panoramic benchmarks (CVUSA, CVACT) into a new limited-EoV sector-level protocol with soft labeling, rather than introducing new data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a lightweight discrete sector-matching stream maintains R@1/S-R@1 consistency (e.g., 72.44%/66.41% at 90° on CVUSA) while baselines like PLGeo drop sharply (E3.94%→40.42%) suggests that the optimization instability plaguing continuous pose regression can be sidestepped by discretization. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets under the authors' own repurposed protocol, and the 'implicit orientation' is inherently quantized to sector granularity (~50° steps) rather than continuous. Open questions include whether this sector-level formulation composes with a subsequent continuous refinement stage, and whether the approach scales to continent-level databases where the number of candidate sectors per image multiplies the search space.
