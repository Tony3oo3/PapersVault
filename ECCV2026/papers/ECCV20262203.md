## Problem

VLMs' visual reasoning degrades in complex visual environments because cluttered textures and colors disperse attention away from task-relevant regions, yet existing enhancement methods require additional training, depend on external segmentation tools, or operate only at coarse-grained levels. The paper asks whether VLMs' own attention patterns can be exploited in a training-free manner to isolate semantic signal from visual noise at the pixel level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262203/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed at 448×448 for Qwen2.5-VL or 336×336 for LLaVA-1.5) and a text question, and produces a text answer generated from a refined image. The refinement pipeline masks, crops, and resizes the input to retain only task-relevant regions, requiring three autoregressive forward passes (one for general-instruction attention, one for question-specific attention, and one final inference on the enhanced image). No external OCR or segmentation tools are used; the VLM's internal attention maps serve as the sole source of spatial grounding.

## Method

CARVE rests on a multiplicative (Hadamard) decomposition of attention into image-inherent visual noise and task-specific semantic signal, justified by the observation that general-instruction attention approximates a uniform distribution over visual tokens. The core operation contrasts task-specific attention A^(Q) against general-instruction attention A^(G) via element-wise division with a small regularization term (Â_i = A_i^(Q) / (A_i^(G) + λ)), which under the decomposition approximates the pure semantic signal when visual noise dominates. Contrasted attention maps are fused across layers [20,25] and generation time steps with recency-weighted aggregation, then thresholded at the top-p percentile and processed via connected-component analysis to select up to K coherent regions. The selected regions define a mask that physically removes background noise through cropping and resizing before a final inference pass. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring no parameter updates or external models, and the multiplicative contrast is shown empirically to outperform an additive alternative by nearly doubling the relative gain on TextVQA.

## Results

CARVE achieves consistent gains across all seven benchmarks and four VLMs without any training. On DocVQA, the largest relative improvements reach +24.14% EM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) and +16.02% EM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B); on ChartQA, gains of +4.67% EM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) and +4.56% EM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) are observed; and on ScienceQA, smaller but positive gains of +0.60% to +5.93% confirm no reasoning degradation. On the perceptually demanding V* benchmark, CARVE yields the most dramatic absolute gains: +71.83 points for LLaVA-1.5-7B (38.7→66.5) and +75.24 points for LLaVA-1.5-13B (42.4→74.3), with statistically significant improvements (p < 0.001, non-overlapping 95% bootstrap CIs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARVE outperforms ViCrop [34] on every dataset and model in Table 3, with the largest margins on DocVQA (+5.9 to +10.7 EM) and ChartQA (+1.9 to +11.1 EM), confirming that pixel-level contrastive masking provides finer-grained noise suppression than ViCrop's strategic cropping. Against SD-RPN [26], which requires training, CARVE wins on DocVQA for all four models (e.g., 63.00 vs. 55.90 EM for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and on ChartQA for three of four models, but SD-RPN edges out CARVE on ScienceQA for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (83.84 vs. 82.50 MC Acc), suggesting that knowledge-heavy reasoning benefits less from visual noise removal. On TextVQA with LLaVA-1.5-7B (Table 5), CARVE achieves 58.2% accuracy, surpassing ViCrop's best variant (grad-att, 56.06%) and all external-tool baselines—SAM (49.42%), YOLO (48.84%), and CLIP (48.55%)—while maintaining a 1.34 s per-sample latency that is competitive with ViCrop (0.89–1.16 s) and far faster than SAM (3.33 s). The consistent pattern across comparisons indicates that CARVE's training-free contrastive attention mechanism matches or exceeds methods that rely on learned segmentation or external detectors, particularly on tasks with high perceptual demands.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARVE extends the observation from ViCrop [34] that VLMs' attention maps encode spatial information about task-relevant regions, but departs from it by moving from coarse cropping to pixel-level contrastive masking. Conceptually, it repurposes the contrastive learning paradigm (previously applied to embeddings, token probabilities, or hidden representations in LLMs) to the attention-map domain, using a general-instruction query as a 'noise baseline' against which task-specific attention is normalized. The multiplicative decomposition of attention into visual-noise and semantic-signal factors (Eq. 4) is a new theoretical framing not present in prior attention-analysis or token-pruning work, which typically treat attention as a monolithic relevance score rather than a product of image-inherent and query-specific components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARVE offers a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism that practitioners can apply to any VLM without retraining, and the attention-entropy diagnostic provides a useful lens for understanding *why* a given image is hard for a model. The method's limitation to local fine-grained perception (text recognition, small-target localization) and its 3× inference overhead mean it is not a universal fix; tasks requiring global spatial context may degrade. Open questions include whether the multiplicative decomposition assumption holds for video or multi-image inputs, whether the overhead can be reduced via attention caching or early termination, and whether the contrastive principle generalizes to other modalities or to internal representation editing rather than pixel-level masking.
