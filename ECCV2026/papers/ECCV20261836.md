## Problem

Kernel-based surface reconstruction methods (Neural Splines, Matérn kernels) rely on low-rank Nyström approximations that impose a global smoothness assumption, causing them to degrade on dense point clouds with high geometric complexity or large-scale inputs with millions of points. Existing work falls back to subdivision schemes that are sensitive to chunk count and prone to artifacts, making kernel methods impractical for real-world dense or large-scale scans.

## Input / Output

VKSR takes an oriented point cloud (m₀ points with per-point normals) and augments it by offsetting each point ±ε along its normal to create off-surface constraints, yielding 3m₀ total points. The SDF is evaluated on a sparse voxel grid built via iterative conditional subdivision (not a dense grid), and the final surface is extracted as a triangular mesh via Marching Cubes. The method requires no training or parameter fitting beyond choosing the number of nearest neighbors k (typically 16–128).

## Method

VKSR replaces the global low-rank Nyström approximation with Vecchia's approximation from the Gaussian Process literature, which conditions each observation only on its k nearest neighbors rather than all previous ones. This transforms the single m×m KRR linear solve into many independent k×k local solves (one per query point), each computing a localized SDF from a small neighborhood, with complexity O(k³) per query independent of total point count. The approach is theoretically grounded in the equivalence between KRR and GP posterior predictive means, and the local smoothness assumption aligns better with real-world surfaces than the global assumption of Nyström methods. Implementation leverages FAISS for GPU-accelerated nearest neighbor search, sparse voxel grids to limit query count, and batched single-precision Cholesky factorization of the small well-conditioned local systems, achieving 14M+ point reconstruction in minutes with under 1 GB VRAM.

## Results

On ShapeNet (dense, 100K points), VKSR achieves 99.8% F-Score, 2.19×10⁻³ Chamfer Distance, and 96.8% Normal Consistency in 0.77 s with 342 MB VRAM, outperforming all kernel-based baselines and matching SPSR's runtime while using far less memory. On the Stanford 3D Scanning Repository, VKSR reconstructs a 14M-point cloud (Lucy) in ~80 s with 762 MB VRAM, compared to >40 min and ~7 GB for the best low-rank kernel method, yielding speedups of 11×–78×. On the Surface Reconstruction Benchmark (incomplete/noisy scans), VKSR matches NS and Matérn ν=3/2 in accuracy while being 20× and 5× faster respectively. In the sparse ShapeNet setting (1K points), VKSR (3.95 CD) slightly trails the best kernel methods (3.89–3.91 CD) but clearly outperforms SPSR (4.36 CD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPSR [26], VKSR is marginally faster in the dense ShapeNet setting (0.77 s vs. 0.82 s) and uses 342 MB VRAM vs. SPSR's CPU-only execution, but SPSR retains a slight accuracy edge (99.9% vs. 99.8% FS). In the sparse setting, VKSR clearly outperforms SPSR on Chamfer Distance (3.95 vs. 4.36) and F-Score (93.6% vs. 91.5%), confirming that SPSR degrades on incomplete inputs. Against the low-rank kernel baselines NS [51] and Matérn [48], VKSR achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy in dense settings while being 60×–180× faster (0.77 s vs. 47–132 s) and using 5× less VRAM; in sparse settings, the kernel methods retain a small CD advantage (3.89–3.91 vs. 3.95) due to their global smoothness assumption. On the Stanford 3D Scanning Repository, VKSR matches or beats SPSR on all six objects in CD while being 2×–30× faster, and outperforms the best low-rank kernel method by 11×–78× in runtime; the chunked variants of NS and Matérn that are needed to compete in accuracy still take 17 min to 3.5 h. Against RIMLS [53] and SAP [37], VKSR is substantially more accurate in sparse and incomplete settings (e.g., 3.95 vs. 9.27 CD on ShapeNet sparse; 0.16 vs. 0.17 CD on DC in the Surface Reconstruction Benchmark) and faster by 20×–50×. The learnable NKSR [20] baseline, included for reference, achieves the best CD on ShapeNet dense (1.96) but requires training on large datasets and is not directly comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

VKSR extends the kernel-based surface reconstruction line initiated by Neural Splines [51] and Matérn kernels [48], which formulate implicit surface reconstruction as Kernel Ridge Regression solved via a global linear system. The key departure is replacing the Nyström low-rank approximation (which imposes a global smoothness assumption) with Vecchia's approximation [47] from the Gaussian Process literature, specifically following Finley et al. [17] in applying it to the noisy response process. This shifts computation from a single large n×n solve to many small k×k local solves, implicitly imposing a local smoothness assumption that better matches real-world surface geometry. The paper also positions VKSR as the natural bridge between SPSR (strong for dense inputs, weak for sparse) and global kernel methods (strong for sparse, impractical for dense/large-scale).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VKSR is the first kernel-based surface reconstruction method to handle 14M+ point clouds in under two minutes with <1 GB VRAM, making kernel methods' favorable inductive bias (especially for sparse and incomplete inputs) practical at scale for the first time. The 180× speedup over existing kernel methods is well-evidenced across four diverse datasets, and the method's simplicity (a local KRR per query point) makes it easy to integrate into learnable frameworks like Neural Kernel Fields. Open questions include whether hierarchical multi-scale neighbor selection can close the small gap to global kernel methods in very sparse settings, and whether the probabilistic GP interpretation can yield useful uncertainty estimates for downstream tasks like robotic grasping.
