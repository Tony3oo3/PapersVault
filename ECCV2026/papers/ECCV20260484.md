## Problem

Chain-of-Thought fine-tuning of Large Vision-Language Models paradoxically exacerbates hallucinations, revealing a reasoning-faithfulness trade-off. The paper identifies the root cause as a 'Confidence Trap': under visual ambiguity or degradation, CoT-tuned models produce low-entropy, high-confidence errors driven by linguistic priors rather than expressing appropriate uncertainty. This failure mode renders conventional uncertainty-based defenses (e.g., Visual Contrastive Decoding) ineffective, since confident hallucinations remain stable under visual perturbations and do not exhibit the high-entropy signal those methods rely on.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images paired with text prompts, producing text responses that include explicit chain-of-thought reasoning steps. For reward computation, ground-truth annotations are decomposed into atomic visual propositions (object existence and attribute statements), and a DeBERTa-v3 NLI model scores each generated response against these propositions to yield a hierarchical factual-correctness signal. Training uses LLaVA-CoT-100k for the initial SFT stage and the Visual Attributes in the Wild (VAW) dataset for the RL alignment stage, with all inference performed under greedy decoding (temperature = 0).

## Method

HERO is an on-policy reinforcement learning framework built on GRPO that introduces three mechanisms to target confident hallucinations. First, an entropy-aware weight w(H(x)) = 1 + σ(−k·(H(x) − H₀)) dynamically amplifies the optimization penalty on low-entropy erroneous outputs while gracefully decaying to standard weighting for high-entropy (genuinely uncertain) responses. Second, variance-gated sample selection computes the reward variance across G sampled responses per prompt and filters out plateaued groups, focusing updates on informative hard negatives that expose recoverable decision boundaries. Third, a dynamic offensive/defensive balancer uses the group mean reward relative to an SFT baseline threshold to shift between aggressive policy exploration (offense) and KL-anchored regularization (defense), mitigating reward hacking and catastrophic forgetting. The composite reward combines NLI-based object existence and attribute scores with a format-compliance term, providing fine-grained factual supervision without requiring human feedback or LLM-as-judge scoring.

## Results

On the 7B Qwen2.5-VL model, HERO achieves 82.9% THRONE accuracy, 90.8% POPE accuracy (90.4% F1), 61.2% AMBER-G coverage, and 81.0% AMBER-D accuracy, outperforming all baselines on THRONE and POPE. On the 3B model, HERO reaches 82.8% THRONE accuracy and 86.5% POPE accuracy. Most notably, in the critical low-entropy zone (entropy ≤ 0.3), HERO recovers truthfulness from ~50% (CoT-SFT) to over 92%, an 84% relative improvement, directly addressing the Confidence Trap. HERO also reduces wall-clock training time from ~22h (vanilla GRPO) to ~16h via variance-gated sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERO consistently outperforms all four baselines on THRONE and POPE across both 3B and 7B models. Against the inference-time method VCD, HERO gains +4.4 points on THRONE Acc (82.9 vs 78.5) and +3.8 on POPE Acc (90.8 vs 87.0) for the 7B model, demonstrating that training-time entropy-aware penalties address confident hallucinations that contrastive decoding cannot resolve. Against the offline alignment method HA-DPO, HERO improves THRONE Acc by +3.4 and POPE Acc by +3.6, attributed to on-policy exploration discovering model-specific blind spots. Against vanilla GRPO (the direct ablation baseline), HERO gains +1.0 on THRONE Acc, +1.6 on POPE Acc, and +2.2 on AMBER-G Coverage, while incurring a minor trade-off on AMBER-G CHAIR (6.3 vs 6.0) and Hal/sample (37.0 vs 36.8), which the authors attribute to a coverage-precision trade-off under detailed CoT-style generation. CoT-SFT, the starting point for all methods, actually degrades THRONE and POPE relative to the base model, confirming the reasoning-faithfulness paradox that HERO is designed to resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

HERO extends GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) by adding an entropy-aware weighting term that amplifies penalties on low-entropy erroneous outputs, plus a variance-gated sample selection mechanism for training efficiency. It departs from inference-time contrastive decoding methods (VCD, CDAR) by arguing that the 'Confidence Trap'—where hallucinations are low-entropy and thus invisible to contrastive signals—renders such approaches ineffective. It also moves beyond offline preference optimization (DPO, HA-DPO, V-DPO) by operating on-policy and explicitly distinguishing confident hallucinations from uncertain mistakes, rather than applying uniform penalties across all error types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the Confidence Trap challenges a foundational assumption in hallucination mitigation—that errors correlate with high uncertainty—and suggests that a meaningful class of the most harmful hallucinations is structurally invisible to existing uncertainty-based defenses. The 84% relative truthfulness recovery in the ultra-low-entropy zone (from ~50% to >92%) is a concrete demonstration that targeted entropy-aware training can realign confidence with visual evidence. Open questions include whether the static entropy baseline H0 generalizes across tasks and input conditions, whether the NLI-based reward adequately captures spatial and relational errors, and whether the AMBER coverage-hallucination trade-off can be resolved with more fine-grained reward signals.
