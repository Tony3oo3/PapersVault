## Problem

Feedforward geometric foundation models achieve strong short-window 3D reconstruction but cannot scale to minutes-long videos due to quadratic attention complexity and training on only short-context data (≤128 frames). Existing sequential alternatives either compress all temporal context into a single lossy recurrent state (sacrificing the high-precision dense geometry needed for adjacent-frame alignment) or preserve local detail without long-range memory (causing unbounded drift). The paper tackles both the architectural 'context wall' and the training 'data wall' simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular RGB video frames processed sequentially in chunks (e.g., 64 frames per chunk with a single overlapping frame), and produces per-frame dense 3D pointmaps in local camera coordinates alongside per-frame camera poses in a shared global world coordinate frame. Although trained on sequences of only 128 frames, the architecture generalizes at inference to sequences of up to 19,000 frames (≈11.5 km trajectory) via periodic TTT state resets and optional feedforward SE(3) pose alignment at chunk boundaries.

## Method

LoGeR processes video in chunks using a bidirectional geometry backbone (π³) for high-fidelity intra-chunk reasoning, then propagates information across chunks through a dual-component hybrid memory module. The first component is a non-parametric Sliding Window Attention (SWA) layer, sparsely inserted at only 4 depths, that attends losslessly over the token sequences of the previous and current chunks to preserve high-precision geometric alignment at boundaries. The second component is a parametric chunk-wise Test-Time Training (TTT) layer using fast weights (SwiGLU MLP updated with the Muon optimizer) that compresses global context into a fixed-size state, anchoring the coordinate frame and preventing scale drift over thousands of chunks. Training employs a three-stage progressive curriculum (48→128 frames, increasing chunk count from 4 to 20) to stabilize TTT optimization, and the model is trained on a mixture of 13 real and synthetic datasets heavily weighted toward large-scale navigation scenes. An optional LoGeR* variant adds a purely feedforward SE(3) alignment step at each chunk boundary using overlapping-frame poses to further suppress accumulated error.

## Results

On KITTI, LoGeR* achieves an average ATE of 18.65 m, reducing the error by over 74% compared to the prior best feedforward method TTT3R (72.86 m) and surpassing the strongest optimization-based method VGGT-Long (27.64 m) by 32.5%. On the VBR benchmark with sequences spanning 8,815–18,846 frames (up to 11.5 km), LoGeR achieves a 55.2% relative improvement over prior feedforward methods. On shorter-sequence benchmarks, LoGeR outperforms prior work by 69.2% on 7-Scenes, 80.0% on ScanNet, and 66.1% on TUM-Dynamics in terms of ATE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoGeR* decisively outperforms all feedforward baselines on KITTI: it reduces average ATE from 72.86 m (TTT3R) to 18.65 m (−74%), from 91.62 m (CUT3R) to 18.65 m (−80%), and from 206.78 m (InfiniteVGGT) to 18.65 m (−91%). It also beats the strongest optimization-based method VGGT-Long (27.64 m avg) by 32.5%, and the proposed Pi3-Chunk baseline (52.07 m) by 64%. FastVGGT, while efficient, suffers OOM on four of eleven KITTI sequences and produces extremely high ATE (201–639 m) on the remaining ones, confirming the 'data wall' of short-context-trained models. On the VBR benchmark (8.8k–18.8k frames), LoGeR's advantage over Pi3-Chunk grows with sequence length because Pi3-Chunk's SIM(3) stitching accumulates exponential scale drift, whereas LoGeR's TTT module anchors global scale. On short-sequence benchmarks (7-Scenes, ScanNet, TUM-Dynamics), LoGeR outperforms TTT3R, CUT3R, and StreamVGGT by 69–80% in ATE, with qualitative results showing LoGeR recovers structural details that baselines distort.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoGeR builds directly on the bidirectional geometry backbone of π³ (and VGGT) for intra-chunk dense reasoning, and repurposes Test-Time Training (TTT) as a parametric global memory that compresses long-range geometric context into fast weights. Its key architectural departure from prior long-sequence methods (CUT3R's single recurrent state, TTT3R's frame-wise linear updates, FastVGGT's sparse attention) is the hybrid memory design: a non-parametric Sliding Window Attention pathway preserves lossless local features across adjacent chunks, while the TTT pathway handles compressed global propagation—decoupling two tasks that prior single-mechanism approaches conflate. The paper also introduces a repurposed VBR benchmark (up to 19k frames, 11.5 km) to evaluate at scales no prior feedforward method has been tested on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a model trained on 128 frames can generalize to 19k-frame sequences with only periodic state resets, LoGeR provides a practical recipe for scaling feedforward 3D reconstruction to minutes-long video without per-sequence optimization—a capability previously exclusive to SLAM pipelines with expensive backends. The hybrid memory pattern (lossless local + compressed global) is a reusable architectural template likely applicable to other long-horizon dense prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the periodic resets and optional feedforward pose alignment (LoGeR*) are fundamental requirements or artifacts of the current training regime, and whether the approach extends to multi-sensor or dynamic-scene settings beyond the monocular, static-scene evaluation.
