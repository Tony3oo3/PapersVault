## Problem

In neighboring-slice self-supervised denoising for volumetric medical imaging, inter-slice misalignment breaks same-coordinate anatomical correspondence, causing ghosting and blurred margins. Existing remedies either mask discrepant pixels (discarding ~20% of neighboring supervision concentrated at high-frequency boundaries) or estimate dense deformation fields for warping (unreliable under severe noise and introducing resampling artifacts). The paper asks how to recover displaced anatomical supervision locally without dense registration or excluding structurally critical regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy volumetric CT or MRI volume (no clean reference required) and produces a denoised volume of the same geometry. A 2D slice denoiser (NAFNet backbone) processes each slice independently, while supervision is constructed from adjacent slices within the volume. The key input detail is that the method consumes both the raw noisy adjacent slices (for target retrieval) and a bilateral-plus-median filtered guide version (for correspondence search), operating at slice spacings from 1.0 mm to 2.5 mm.

## Method

NGPS reframes inter-slice misalignment as a training-time supervision construction problem rather than an inference-time denoising prior. For each pixel flagged as misaligned by a direction-aware threshold on the filtered guide disagreement, NGPS searches a local 15×15 window in the adjacent slice for Top-K structurally similar patches using guide-based SSD, then retrieves the raw center-pixel values at those matched coordinates as pseudo-targets. This decouples structure matching (performed on the noise-attenuated guide) from signal retrieval (taken from the unfiltered noisy neighbor), avoiding dense deformation estimation and spatial resampling. Training uses a hybrid loss that applies standard Noise2Noise same-coordinate supervision on unmasked static regions and NGPS-retrieved targets on masked misaligned regions, plus a regional consistency regularizer on static areas to reduce inter-slice flicker. The entire pipeline requires no learnable alignment module and adds only ~0.72 s of preprocessing per 100-slice volume.

## Results

On the AAPM-Mayo quarter-dose CT benchmark, NGPS achieves 36.68 dB PSNR, 0.8986 SSIM, 0.9470 FSIM, 0.2056 HFEN, and 0.0362 GMSD, leading all five metrics over every baseline. On the simulated ultra-low-dose LIDC-IDRI CT, NGPS reaches 31.03 dB PSNR and 0.9168 FSIM, improving over the closest competitor NS-N2N by 0.41 dB and 0.0224 respectively, while also achieving the lowest HFEN (0.4161). On the IXI MRI dataset under synthetic Rician noise (5–9%), NGPS maintains the highest PSNR and SSIM across all three corruption levels. The method also shows the smallest PSNR drop when slice thickness doubles from 1.25 mm to 2.5 mm (0.16 dB vs. 0.63 dB for NS-N2N and 1.88 dB for Deformed2Self).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NGPS outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric for the AAPM-Mayo quarter-dose CT task, with the closest competitor NS-N2N trailing by 0.77 dB PSNR and 0.0402 SSIM. On the more challenging LIDC-IDRI ultra-low-dose CT, NGPS leads in PSNR (+0.41 dB), FSIM (+0.0224), and HFEN (+0.0245) over NS-N2N, though NS-N2N retains a marginal GMSD advantage (0.0777 vs. 0.0788) and the SSIM gap is only 0.0022. Among registration-based methods, Deformed2Self degrades substantially at thicker 2.5 mm slices (1.88 dB PSNR drop vs. 0.16 dB for NGPS), while masking-based methods (Noise2Sim, NS-N2N) produce visibly softer anatomical boundaries in qualitative comparisons. On the IXI MRI benchmark, NGPS maintains the highest PSNR and SSIM across all three Rician noise levels (5%, 7%, 9%), with Deformed2Self ranking second overall. Single-image baselines (BM3D, Noise2Void, Pixel2Pixel, ZS-N2N) consistently underperform volumetric methods, confirming the value of inter-slice context, but they also exhibit more residual noise or over-smoothing in high-frequency regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

NGPS sits at the intersection of Noise2Noise-style self-supervised denoising and neighboring-slice volumetric SSL, directly addressing the inter-slice misalignment problem that prior methods handle either by masking discrepant pixels (NS-N2N, Noise2Sim) or by estimating dense deformation fields and warping (Deformed2Self). Its conceptual departure is to reframe misalignment as local displacement rather than absence, borrowing the patch-matching intuition from Pixel2Pixel's PixelBank but restricting search to a small local window in the adjacent slice and decoupling guide-based structural matching from raw-value target retrieval. The NAFNet backbone and Noise2Noise training paradigm are inherited unchanged; the novelty is entirely in the supervision-construction pipeline (bilateral+median guide, direction-aware masking, Top-K local patch search, raw center-pixel retrieval).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a concrete cost of the standard masking remedy—approximately 20% of neighboring supervision is discarded, concentrated at high-gradient anatomical boundaries—making the case for recovering that evidence rather than excluding it. The practical impact is a lightweight (0.72 s per 100 slices) drop-in supervision module that improves structure-sensitive metrics (HFEN, GMSD, FSIM) over both masking- and warping-based baselines without requiring a learnable alignment network. Key limitations include the assumption that a corresponding structure exists within the fixed 15×15 search window (which can fail under large through-plane gaps or abrupt anatomical changes), fixed hyperparameters across datasets, and MRI evaluation limited to synthetic Rician noise rather than acquisition-realistic corruption. Open questions include whether confidence-based rejection or adaptive window sizing can extend the approach to larger inter-slice gaps, and whether the findings transfer to 3D volumetric denoisers or non-medical volumetric data.
