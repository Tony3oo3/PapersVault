## Problem

Dual-pixel (DP) imaging provides metric depth from a single camera, but its extremely small effective baseline causes disparity signals to weaken toward the noise floor in textureless, low-contrast, or downsampled regions, leading to structural degradation and depth failure. Existing DP-based methods rely primarily on local sub-pixel correspondence and cannot recover reliable geometry when disparity observability is insufficient, while monocular depth foundation models offer strong global structural priors but lack physical metric scale. The paper addresses how to combine physically grounded DP metric cues with foundation-model structural reasoning while handling ViT representation degradation induced by DP defocus blur.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dual-pixel left-right image pair (I_L, I_R) at 512×768 resolution and produces a dense metric depth map with physical units, where all valid depths are linearly mapped to a 1–10 m range. For datasets providing disparity only, depth is recovered via D = 1/(a + bd) with calibrated constants. The framework additionally consumes a pretrained monocular depth foundation model (Depth Anything V2) as a frozen structural prior, and the final output is a guided depth map that preserves DP metric scale while restoring structural coherence in weak-disparity regions.

## Method

FoundDP is a three-stage cascaded framework: (1) a DP Depth Estimation Module (DDE) with a shared-weight encoder and symmetric 3D cost volume regressed via Softmin to produce initial metric depth; (2) a Structure Refinement Module (SR) that predicts a residual correction ΔD via a hierarchical encoder-decoder conditioned on both images and the initial DP depth, preserving metric scale while repairing local discontinuities; and (3) a Depth Guidance Module (DG) that decodes DAV2 ViT features in a DPT-style architecture, injecting the refined metric depth as a spatial condition through an adaptive gating mechanism that selectively trusts DP cues in high-confidence areas and defers to foundation priors in weak-disparity regions. A key technical contribution is a ViT feature alignment strategy that enforces L2 consistency between features of clear images and their DP-defocus-degraded counterparts, mitigating the representation bias that otherwise destabilizes depth guidance. Training proceeds in four sequential stages (DDE → SR → ViT encoder fine-tuning → DPT decoder) with Smooth L1 loss in log-depth space, preserving absolute metric scale rather than using scale-invariant objectives.

## Results

On the four standard DP benchmarks, FoundDP achieves the lowest affine-invariant error AI(1) across all datasets: 0.1475 on NYUData, 0.0110 on DP2020, 0.2579 on DP5K, and 0.1168 on DP2019. Threshold accuracy Acc-1 reaches 0.9661 (NYUData), 0.9998 (DP2020), 0.7976 (DP5K), and 0.8571 (DP2019), with Acc-2 at or near 1.0 on DP2020. On the self-constructed downsampling dataset DPDown70, FoundDP reduces AI(1) to 0.2482 versus 0.4003 for the next-best method (CADSNet), demonstrating substantially improved robustness under reduced disparity observability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoundDP outperforms all four DP baselines on AI(1) across every dataset, with the largest margins over the earliest method DPNet (e.g., 0.1475 vs. 0.9271 on NYUData, a 6.3× reduction). Against the strongest recent competitor CADSNet, FoundDP improves AI(1) by 27% on NYUData (0.1475 vs. 0.2018), 32% on DP2020 (0.0110 vs. 0.0162), 4% on DP5K (0.2579 vs. 0.2680), and 11% on DP2019 (0.1168 vs. 0.1313). On threshold accuracy, FoundDP achieves near-perfect Acc-1 (0.9998) and Acc-2 (1.0000) on DP2020, surpassing SFBDNet (0.9963/0.9987) and CADSNet (0.8153/0.9997). The one metric where FoundDP does not lead is rank consistency (1−|ρs|) on DP5K, where CADSNet edges ahead slightly (0.0977 vs. 0.0989), though the gap is negligible. On the downsampling robustness test (DPDown70), FoundDP's AI(1) of 0.2482 is 38% lower than CADSNet (0.4003) and 61% lower than SFBDNet (0.6458), confirming that foundation-model structural priors provide the largest gains precisely when disparity observability is most degraded.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: DP-based metric depth estimation (Garg et al. 2019, Pan et al. 2021, Kim et al. 2023, Ghanekar et al. 2024), which relies on local sub-aperture disparity cues, and monocular depth foundation models (Depth Anything V2, DPT), which provide global structural priors but lack physical scale. The key departure is identifying that directly applying ViT-based foundation features to DP images introduces systematic representation degradation due to defocus blur, and proposing an explicit feature alignment strategy to correct this before metric-guided fusion. Unlike prior DP methods that improve local correspondence modeling or optical design, FoundDP treats the weak-disparity problem as fundamentally requiring external structural reasoning, while unlike pure foundation-model approaches, it preserves metric grounding through DP disparity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of ViT feature degradation under DP defocus as a concrete barrier to foundation-model-guided depth estimation is a useful diagnostic that likely generalizes to other settings where pretrained encoders encounter distribution-shifted inputs. The controlled observability analysis (progressive downsampling as a disparity-attenuation proxy) provides a principled way to study when local correspondence methods fail, which is valuable for the DP imaging community. However, the framework's dependence on a single foundation model (DAV2), the additional ViT computational overhead limiting real-time deployment, and the acknowledged imperfection of adapting foundation priors to DP-specific imaging characteristics leave open questions about scalability and robustness under extreme noise or optical degradation.
