limitations: The finite realizability assumption (Assumption 1) requires that a good target approximator exists within the hypothesis space, and the proxy distribution is restricted to Wasserstein-1 balls around class prototypes, which may be insufficient for highly structured tasks such as segmentation or detection. ## Problem

Existing Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) methods rely on heuristic objectives—source-like data generation or pseudo-labeling—whose connection to target error generalization remains unclear. Classical UDA theory, which provides principled error bounds, assumes access to both source and target data and depends on a joint-error term that becomes unbounded under large domain shifts, rendering it inapplicable in the source-free setting. This paper asks how to derive a tractable target-error bound for SFDA and translate it into a practical optimization objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained source model (decomposed into encoder G* and classifier f_S*, e.g., ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50/101 or ViT-B/32) and a set of unlabeled target images; no source data is accessed during adaptation. For the multimodal variant APP^M, a second source model (CLIP with learnable text prompts 'a photo of a [CLS]') is additionally provided. The output is a class label per target image, with the target hypothesis f_T' initialized from f_S* and refined through adversarial and self-supervised losses. Class prototypes μ^y are estimated online via EMA over soft assignments from f_S*, requiring no ground-truth target labels.

## Method

The paper first establishes a target-error upper bound (Theorem 1) under a finite realizability assumption that decouples target realizability from the classical joint-error term, making the bound robust under large domain shifts. To operationalize this bound, Adversarial Proxy Perturbation (APP) introduces a non-parametric distributional proxy set Q defined as Wasserstein-1 balls of radius ξ centered at class prototypes μ^y in feature space; the DRO/Kantorovich dual of the predictive-consistency loss is simplified to a constrained perturbation optimization where the worst-case proxy collapses to a Dirac measure at μ^y + r_adv^y, with r_adv^y computed via a first-order projected gradient step. Training proceeds as a min-max game: the encoder G minimizes L_dis + L_ce + γL_ssl + λL_adv while the target hypothesis f_T' minimizes L_ce + γL_ssl − λL_adv, where L_ssl combines weighted pseudo-labeling and regularized entropy minimization. For multi-source settings, Theorem 2 extends the bound to N source models, and mutual distillation replaces single-model pseudo-labeling with cross-model CED between each model and the average of the others, enabling co-training with VLMs like CLIP via prompt learning.

## Results

On Office-Home, APP (unimodal) achieves 74.6% average accuracy, outperforming the best prior unimodal methods RGV (74.1%) and DVD (73.7%). In the multimodal setting, APP^M reaches 86.2% average on Office-Home and 91.7% on VisDA-C, surpassing DUET (84.7% / 91.4%) and DIFO (83.1% / 90.3%). On the large-scale DomainNet benchmark, APP^M achieves 84.5% average, a 1.6% improvement over DUET (82.9%). Gains are most pronounced on large-shift targets such as Clipart (e.g., APP^M scores 92.1% on Cl→Pr vs. 90.7% for DUET).

## Baselines & Comparisons

APP outperforms all unimodal SFDA baselines on Office-Home average accuracy (74.6% vs. 74.1% for RGV, 73.7% for DVD, 73.4% for I-SFDA), with the largest margins on large-shift transfers like Cl→Ar (+2.1% over RGV) and Pr→Ar (+2.0% over RGV). In the multimodal setting, APP^M surpasses DUET by 1.5% on Office-Home average (86.2% vs. 84.7%) and by 1.6% on DomainNet (84.5% vs. 82.9%), with particularly strong gains on Clipart targets (e.g., 92.1% on Cl→Pr vs. 90.7% for DUET). APP^M also exceeds DIFO by 3.1% on Office-Home average, demonstrating that the mutual distillation co-training framework effectively leverages VLM priors beyond DIFO's alternating optimization. On VisDA-C, where the domain shift is smaller due to ImageNet pre-training overlap, margins are tighter (APP 89.5% vs. RGV 89.1%; APP^M 91.7% vs. DUET 91.4%), consistent with the paper's analysis that gains scale with domain discrepancy. On partial-set and open-set tasks, APP^M achieves 88.3% and 85.2% respectively, exceeding DIFO (85.6% / 75.9%) by 2.7% and 9.3%, confirming robustness to asymmetric label spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) line initiated by SHOT [31], which established that a frozen source classifier can guide target adaptation, by providing the first theoretical upper bound on target error that avoids the classical joint-error term from Ben-David et al. [1]—a term that becomes unbounded under large domain shifts. The adversarial proxy perturbation mechanism draws on the discriminator formulation of Ganin & Lempitsky [12] and the perturbation-based simplification of virtual adversarial training [42], repurposing them within a distributionally robust optimization framework over Wasserstein balls to approximate missing source prototypes. The multi-source extension with VLMs generalizes DIFO [52]'s CLIP integration paradigm from alternating updates to a co-training scheme with mutual distillation, grounded in a multi-source generalization bound (Theorem 2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finite realizability assumption provides a theoretically grounded optimization objective for SFDA that remains valid under large domain shifts, addressing a long-standing gap where classical UDA theory was inapplicable without source data. The practical implication is that APP's adversarial proxy perturbation offers a stable training signal that avoids the pseudo-label degradation observed in prior methods (e.g., SHOT's performance decay), as confirmed by the consistent upper-bound validity throughout training. Open questions include whether the proxy distribution assumption (Wasserstein-1 ball around class prototypes) remains adequate for highly structured tasks like segmentation or detection, and whether the mutual distillation framework scales beyond two models without introducing conflicting supervision signals.
