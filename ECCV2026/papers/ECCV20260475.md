## Problem

Multiresolution hash encodings in implicit neural representations use data-agnostic geometric resolution schedules that allocate identical hash-table capacity to every level, regardless of the image's actual frequency content. This causes some levels to suffer severe hash collisions while others underutilize parameters, and the interaction between resolution assignment and collision-induced information loss is treated as independent, leading to suboptimal parameter utilization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260475/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (ranging from 768×512 Kodak photos to gigapixel natural scenes and ultra-high-resolution whole-slide pathology images) and produces a trained multiresolution hash encoding plus a lightweight MLP decoder that reconstructs the image at full resolution. The input is consumed as normalized pixel-center coordinates in [0,1]²; an optional invertible pixel-shuffle with factor s reduces the spatial domain by s along each axis while expanding the output channel dimension by s², so the INR predicts a cs²- dimensional vector per coordinate and the full-resolution image is recovered via the inverse shuffle.

## Method

CARA formulates per-level resolution selection as an information-allocation problem: it measures band information density via entropy increments between adjacent low-pass cutoffs (scaled by the number of spatial samples at that resolution), then solves a min-max optimization over ordered band partitions to equalize the effective information carried by each hash-grid level, solved exactly by dynamic programming in O(LK²). Crucially, the per-band information is weighted by a collision-efficiency factor w^hash = (1−e^{−α})/α derived from the load factor α = N²/T, so levels with high collision rates are allocated less information budget. An invertible pixel-shuffle transform further reduces the effective load factor by s² without enlarging the hash table, mitigating collision-induced information loss at fixed parameter count. The resulting adaptive schedule replaces the fixed geometric progression of Instant-NGP with image-specific cutoffs that concentrate capacity where the signal actually demands it.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset, CARA achieves 40.03 dB PSNR and 0.9714 SSIM at 205K parameters, outperforming all baselines at comparable budgets (~207K). On the gigapixel Girl image, CARA matches MetricGrid's best fidelity (37.06 dB) using only 114.6M parameters—27.76% of MetricGrid's 412.8M setting—and reaches 43.37 dB at a comparable large-model budget, a 6.11 dB improvement over the strongest baseline. On uncompressed whole-slide images, CARA attains 44.02 dB PSNR at 233.57M parameters, surpassing MetricGrid (43.03 dB at 390.01M) and all other INR baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARA consistently outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and parameter budget. On Kodak, it surpasses I-NGP by 1.33 dB (40.03 vs. 38.70) and MetricGrid by 0.30 dB at matched ~205–207K parameters, while also beating Gaussian Splatting (48.00 vs. 44.08 dB) and GaussianImage (48.00 vs. 44.51 dB) at comparable budgets in Table 2. On the gigapixel Girl image, CARA matches MetricGrid's peak fidelity (37.07 vs. 37.06 dB) with only 27.76% of the parameters (114.6M vs. 412.8M) and extends the lead to 6.11 dB at the large-model scale (43.37 vs. 37.06 dB). On whole-slide images, CARA leads MetricGrid by 0.99 dB at a lower parameter count (44.02 at 233.57M vs. 43.03 at 390.01M) and outperforms CINR by 3.33 dB. No baseline is competitive in the high-fidelity regime, where the collision-aware adaptive scheduling and pixel-shuffle transform yield the largest margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Instant-NGP's multiresolution hash encoding by replacing its fixed, data-agnostic geometric resolution schedule with an entropy-driven, per-image adaptive allocation solved via dynamic programming. It also draws on the pixel-shuffle operation from super-resolution literature, repurposing it here not for upsampling but to reduce the effective hash load factor and mitigate collision-induced information loss. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of information theory (Shannon entropy as a band-level information proxy) and practical neural representation design, and it is most directly comparable to MetricGrid, which also adapts grid structure to the input but without explicit collision modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 27.76% parameter-efficiency gain and the 6+ dB PSNR improvement at matched budgets on gigapixel images make CARA a practically attractive drop-in for any hash-grid-based INR pipeline, and the first public uncompressed WSI dataset fills a notable gap for high-resolution pathology research. However, the method is currently validated only on 2D images; extending the entropy-based allocation and pixel-shuffle collision mitigation to 3D volumetric or 4D spatiotemporal settings (e.g., NeRF, video) remains open. The reliance on a discrete candidate set and a min-max objective also raises questions about how the approach behaves when the information spectrum is highly multimodal or when the hash table is already very large (low load factor), where the collision term becomes negligible.
