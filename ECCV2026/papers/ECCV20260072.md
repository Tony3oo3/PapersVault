## Problem

Existing multispectral object detectors treat spectral bands as independent discrete channels, ignore the scale-dependent shift in relative reliability between spectral and spatial cues across feature pyramid levels, and lack explicit rotation-equivariant geometric priors for arbitrarily oriented objects. No prior method has unified continuous spectral modeling, reliability-aware multi-scale fusion, and full rotation equivariance into a single detection framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260072/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multispectral images with C spectral bands (e.g., 4 bands on MODA) at standard aerial/drone resolutions, processed through a YOLO-based backbone with the first convolution expanded to match the band count. It outputs oriented bounding boxes (center, width, height, angle) with class labels and confidence scores, using a C4 rotation group (90° discretization) for orientation indexing. Notably, the model is trained from scratch without any rotation-based data augmentation, relying entirely on built-in equivariance for geometric robustness.

## Method

FressDet integrates three components within a fully rotation-equivariant pipeline. Spectral Implicit Warp (SpeIW) parameterizes spectral resampling as a coordinate-conditioned implicit neural field: Fourier-encoded spectral coordinates are mapped through a shared MLP to basis vectors, which are inner-producted with a spatial latent code to yield per-channel warp offsets; softplus activation plus cumulative summation enforces strict monotonic ordering, and differentiable linear interpolation performs the actual resampling along the spectral axis. Rotation-Equivariant Consistency Weighting (ReCoW) fuses a spectral branch (soft cosine-based prototype assignment preserving spectral diversity) with a spatial branch (hard Gaussian-affinity routing preserving geometric coherence) via a learned sigmoid gate that computes a position-wise convex combination. The oriented-aware head exploits group-indexed features with shared parameters across orientations, achieving classification invariance through group mean pooling, equivariant box prediction via cyclic-tied DFL, and angle prediction via soft circular aggregation with π-periodic normalization. Equivariant convolutions are decomposed into depthwise rotated spatial filtering and pointwise group mixing, reducing cost from O(c²Ns²) to O(cNs² + c²N).

## Results

On MODA, FressDet achieves 73.1% mAP50, 60.2% mAP75, and 54.3% mAP, outperforming the previous best multispectral detector OSSDet by 4.1% mAP50, 14.3% mAP75, and 11.6% mAP while using only 6.3% of its parameters (2.3M vs. 36.5M) and 12.0% of its FLOPs (31.7G vs. 263.1G). On HOD3K, FressDet reaches 93.8% mAP50 and 61.1% mAP, surpassing OSSDet by 0.2% mAP with 26.8% of its FLOPs. On DroneVehicle, FressDet attains 80.3% mAP50 and 61.6% mAP, exceeding the best prior method (WaveMamba) by 0.5% mAP50 and 1.1% mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FressDet outperforms all 51 compared baselines across all three main benchmarks. On MODA, the most significant margin is against the previous multispectral SOTA OSSDet [15]: FressDet gains +4.1% mAP50 (73.1 vs. 69.0), +14.3% mAP75 (60.2 vs. 45.9), and +11.6% mAP (54.3 vs. 42.7), while using 93.7% fewer parameters and 88% fewer FLOPs. Against strong general-purpose detectors, FressDet leads YOLO11m [22] by +5.9% mAP50 and YOLOv8m [20] by +7.5% mAP50, and surpasses the rotation-equivariant MessDet [53] by +5.0% mAP50 and +14.2% mAP. On HOD3K, FressDet edges out OSSDet by +0.4% mAP50 and +0.2% mAP while reducing FLOPs from 131.2G to 35.2G (73% reduction). On DroneVehicle, FressDet beats WaveMamba [77] (the prior best) by +0.5% mAP50 and +1.1% mAP, and leads IV-YOLO [46] by +5.7% mAP50. No baseline is matched or exceeded by FressDet on any reported metric across the five benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

FressDet sits at the intersection of three research threads: group-equivariant CNNs (Cohen et al., 2016; FRED, Lee et al. 2024), implicit neural representations for continuous signal modeling (Tancik et al., 2020; NeRF), and multispectral object detection (OSSDet, S2ADet). It departs from prior multispectral detectors that treat spectral bands as independent discrete channels by instead parameterizing the spectral dimension as a continuous coordinate-conditioned field, and from prior equivariant detectors (ReDet, FRED) that operate purely on spatial geometry by integrating spectral structure into the equivariant pipeline. The depthwise-pointwise decomposition of group convolutions addresses the computational bottleneck identified in FRED, while the order-preserving softplus-cumsum construction for spectral indices is a novel constraint not present in prior INR or deformable-convolution work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 93% parameter reduction relative to the previous best multispectral detector (OSSDet) while simultaneously improving accuracy suggests that much of the prior model capacity was spent compensating for the lack of geometric and spectral inductive biases. The INR-based spectral warp offers a generalizable mechanism for any task requiring continuous, order-preserving resampling along a structured dimension, potentially extending to hyperspectral imaging with hundreds of bands. Open questions include whether the C4 group suffices for scenes with fine angular variation, how the implicit field scales to very high band counts, and whether the approach transfers to other spectral modalities (e.g., LiDAR, radar) where the 'spectral' axis has different physical structure.
