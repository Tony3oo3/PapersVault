## Problem

Sparse-view novel view synthesis suffers from poor generalization to unseen viewpoints, and while flat-minima optimization (e.g., SAM) is widely known to improve generalization in deep learning, the paper shows it does not straightforwardly transfer to this domain. The key gap is that loss sharpness and generalization are not strictly correlated in novel view synthesis: high-detail regions inherently require sharp minima for accurate reconstruction, while low-detail regions benefit from flat minima. This structure-dependent relationship between loss landscape and generalization had not been previously investigated.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse multi-view RGB images (3 views for LLFF, 12 views for MipNeRF-360, all 8× downsampled) with known camera parameters and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation (per-Gaussian means, rotations, scales, opacities, and spherical harmonic coefficients) that renders novel views in real time. The optimization strategy is plug-and-play: it modifies only the training objective and perturbation schedule without altering the scene representation or adding external priors, and it is demonstrated to work across 3DGS variants, implicit NeRF representations, and online dynamic 3D Gaussian pipelines.

## Method

The paper proposes Structure-Aware Sharpness Regularization, which adapts both the perturbation magnitude and the regularization weight in SAM based on local image structure. A geometric tolerance map is first computed for each training image, encoding the distance from every pixel to the nearest high-detail (large-gradient) region. During optimization, each Gaussian's perturbation radius in the SAM worst-case loss is scaled by its projected geometric tolerance converted to 3D via perspective geometry (γ_i · d_i/f · ρ_θ), so high-detail regions receive smaller perturbations that preserve necessary loss sharpness while low-detail regions receive larger perturbations that encourage flatness. Additionally, the sharpness regularization weight is modulated by the same tolerance: regions near high-detail structures get a near-zero weight (reducing to plain empirical loss), while low-detail regions get a high weight emphasizing the flatness penalty. This dual adaptation—structure-aware sharpness (SAS) and structure-aware regularization weighting (SAR)—is shown to be complementary, with ablations confirming that removing either component degrades performance.

## Results

On LLFF (3 views), the full method applied to 3DGS achieves PSNR 20.783, SSIM 0.7197, and LPIPS 0.1965, improving over the plain 3DGS baseline (PSNR 19.810, SSIM 0.6790, LPIPS 0.2145) by approximately +0.97 dB in PSNR. On MipNeRF-360 (12 views), the method consistently improves all five 3DGS-based baselines, with the best absolute PSNR reaching 19.931 (SE-GS+Ours). The method also generalizes to RWP (PSNR 20.650 vs. 20.079 on LLFF), dynamic 3DGS (mTV reduced from 0.1109 to 0.0989), and NeRFs (FreeNeRF PSNR 19.584 vs. 19.523).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is a plug-and-play optimization strategy, so every comparison is a direct A/B test of a baseline with and without the proposed structure-aware sharpness regularization. On 3DGS (Kerbl et al.), the full method (SAM+SAS+SAR) improves PSNR from 19.810 to 20.783 on LLFF (+0.97 dB) and reduces LPIPS from 0.2145 to 0.1965. CoR-GS, DropGaussian, NexusGS, and SE-GS all show consistent gains across both datasets when the method is added, with NexusGS reaching the highest LLFF PSNR of 21.048. Against the alternative flat-minima optimizer RWP (Li et al.), the method lifts LLFF PSNR from 20.079 to 20.650 and MipNeRF-360 LPIPS from 0.3767 to 0.3567. In the dynamic setting (Yun et al., 3DGStream backbone), mTV drops from 0.1109 to 0.0989 while PSNR and SSIM also improve slightly. For the NeRF baseline FreeNeRF, the method yields a smaller but consistent gain (PSNR 19.523→19.584, LPIPS 0.3103→0.2983), confirming the insight transfers across representations. The method wins on every comparison across all eight baselines and all reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and 3D Gaussian Splatting, modifying SAM's uniform perturbation and regularization into a structure-aware variant that adapts both the neighborhood radius and the regularization weight per-Gaussian based on local image detail. It departs from the standard flat-minima literature by showing that in novel view synthesis, the optimal loss sharpness is not uniform: high-detail regions inherently require sharp minima for accurate reconstruction, while low-detail regions benefit from flat minima for generalization. The approach is positioned as complementary to existing sparse-view methods that add geometric priors, depth constraints, or correspondence models, and it contrasts with Sparfels, which approximates worst-case loss via a color-variance proxy rather than directly optimizing over all Gaussian parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption that flatter minima universally improve generalization by demonstrating, with loss landscape visualizations and ablations, that SAM's uniform flattening actually degrades reconstruction quality in high-detail regions. The plug-and-play nature of the strategy—improving 3DGS, NeRF, dynamic scenes, and multiple flat-minima optimizers without architectural changes—makes it broadly applicable to current and future reconstruction pipelines. Open questions include whether the geometric tolerance map (based on image gradients) is the optimal proxy for local structure, whether the approach extends to other under-constrained inverse problems beyond view synthesis, and whether the modest per-metric gains (typically 0.2–0.5 dB PSNR) scale to more challenging real-world sparse-view scenarios.
