## Problem

Dynamic hand gesture recognition for continuous local deployment on consumer-grade hardware faces a fundamental conflict: Video Transformers achieve strong global temporal modeling but incur prohibitive Memory Access Cost and dynamic KV-cache expansion, while lightweight CNNs lack the temporal capacity to model complex kinematics. Additionally, standard State Space Models (SSMs) are inherently unidirectional, failing to capture the non-causal global context essential for distinguishing gesture phases (e.g., preparatory vs. execution).

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sliding window of T=40 consecutive frames at 192×256 spatial resolution, supporting both RGB and Depth modalities. RGB variants leverage Jester pre-training for semantic initialization while Depth variants are trained entirely from scratch. The output is a K-dimensional probability vector over gesture classes (25 for NVGesture, 83 for EgoGesture, 12 for Briareo), with the full S2Gest family spanning three scales (Small 2.59M, Tiny 1.17M, Nano 0.68M parameters) to accommodate varying hardware budgets.

## Method

S2Gest employs a Context-Aware Dual-Stream architecture: a lightweight Context Stream extracts global spatiotemporal priors via aggressive downsampling, which condition the high-resolution Focal Stream through Adaptive Feature Modulation (AFM) that generates channel-wise affine scaling and shifting parameters (FiLM-style). The core temporal module, the S2-Block, first serializes features via a Patch-Interleaved Tokenizer (PIT) that enforces tube-major scanning order to preserve local motion continuity, then applies a Split-Scan mechanism that partitions the embedding dimension into two complementary subgroups (D_emb/2 each) routed through a dual-stream SSM processing forward and backward temporal directions simultaneously. This structural channel partitioning achieves global bidirectional modeling with linear-time complexity while halving the internal SSM transformation cost (0.5 D_emb² vs. 1.0 D_emb² for full-channel unidirectional), making it strictly parameter-neutral. The continuous-time SSM formulation uses Zero-Order Hold discretization with an input-dependent timescale Δ(x_t) that acts as a dynamic gate, maintaining state memory during static poses and driving fine-grained transitions during rapid motion.

## Results

S2Gest-Small (2.59M params, 14.90 GFLOPs) achieves 90.66% top-1 accuracy on NVGesture Depth, matching the heavyweight MotionRGBD (38.14M params) with a ~15× parameter reduction. On EgoGesture, S2Gest-Small sets new SOTA at 96.47% (RGB) and 97.35% (Depth), surpassing NAS (93.31%/94.13%) by over 3%. On Briareo, S2Gest-Tiny reaches a peak 98.61% Depth accuracy, exceeding ConvMixFormer (97.22%), while the ultra-lightweight Nano variant (0.68M params, 4.02 GFLOPs) maintains >97% on both EgoGesture and Briareo Depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2Gest-Small (2.59M) matches MotionRGBD (38.14M) on NVGesture Depth (90.66% vs 90.62%) with a ~15× parameter reduction, and outperforms it on NVGesture RGB (86.10% vs 89.58% is actually a loss here — S2Gest-Small RGB 86.10% trails MotionRGBD RGB 89.58%). On EgoGesture, S2Gest-Small decisively beats NAS (96.47% vs 93.31% RGB), MDRA (96.47% vs 93.96%), and DSTCNet (96.47% vs 93.70%), establishing clear SOTA. On Briareo Depth, S2Gest-Tiny (98.61%) surpasses ConvMixFormer (97.22%), DSTCNet (98.10%), and MVTN (97.92%), while S2Gest-Nano (97.69%) still exceeds MDRA (96.92%) and GestFormer (96.18%). Against the lightweight MobileNetV2 reference, S2Gest-Nano outperforms it by 7.26% on NVGesture Depth (82.99% vs 75.73%) using only 29% of the parameters, and by 5.89% on Briareo Depth (97.69% vs 91.20%). The only notable losses are on NVGesture RGB, where S2Gest-Small (86.10%) trails MotionRGBD (89.58%) and MDRA (83.87% is actually lower, so S2Gest wins there) — the RGB gap is attributed to the dataset's dimly lit vehicle interiors where depth modality provides illumination-invariant cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2Gest builds directly on Mamba's selective state-space formulation for linear-time temporal modeling, but departs from the standard unidirectional SSM by introducing a channel-partitioned Split-Scan mechanism that achieves bidirectional context capture without the 2× parameter and memory penalty incurred by prior bidirectional SSM extensions (Bi-Mamba, VideoMamba, Vision Mamba). The spatial front-end draws on Inception-style multi-scale topologies and FiLM/SE-Net-style modulation, while the Patch-Interleaved Tokenizer introduces a tube-major serialization strategy that contrasts with the frame-major or flattened 3D scanning used in prior video SSMs. The paper positions itself explicitly against the efficiency-accuracy trade-off between Video Transformers (quadratic complexity, dynamic KV-cache) and lightweight CNNs (insufficient temporal capacity), framing SSMs as the structural middle ground for memory-constrained local deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-3M parameter footprint matching or exceeding 48M-parameter baselines on three benchmarks makes S2Gest a practical reference for always-on, privacy-preserving gesture interfaces on consumer hardware, and the Split-Scan mechanism is presented as a general building block for efficient bidirectional video understanding beyond gestures. Key limitations remain: aggressive early downsampling degrades fine-grained finger articulation under motion blur, and the ZOH discretization assumption breaks down for highly erratic or rapid cyclical motions. Open directions include adaptive token preservation, dynamic ODE solvers to replace fixed-step ZOH, and multi-modal fusion, all of which would extend the architecture's robustness without sacrificing its efficiency profile.
