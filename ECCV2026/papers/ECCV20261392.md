## Problem

Prior machine unlearning methods are designed and evaluated for single-shot requests, yet real-world deployments must honor multiple sequential unlearning requests over a model's operational lifetime. This paper identifies and characterizes 'plasticity collapse'—a progressive, fundamental breakdown in a model's ability to forget as unlearning requests accumulate—which had not been previously recognized or theoretically analyzed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261392/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pre-trained image classifier and a sequence of T forget sets (each comprising 1% random samples or an entire class from Tiny-ImageNet 64×64 or CIFAR-100 32×32), with optional retain sets, and produces updated model parameters after each unlearning step. Beyond the standard forgetting accuracy metric, the paper introduces two diagnostic quantities—Energy Ratio (fraction of update energy projected onto the shared subspace W) and Projection Coefficient (signed projection onto W's principal directions)—that serve as lightweight online monitors for detecting impending collapse in real time.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical analysis of continual unlearning dynamics, instantiated on linear models with squared loss and extended to neural networks via the NTK regime. The key mechanism is that successive unlearning operators A_t = I + M_t^F − λM_t^R become expansive on a shared subspace W where forget curvature dominates retain curvature, causing the time-ordered product A_{t:s} to grow exponentially as (1+ρ)^{t−s+1}; to keep parameter iterates bounded, the optimization must either suppress updates along W (yielding forward failure: diminishing forgetting quality) or inject cancelling updates that partially undo prior forgetting (yielding backward failure: spontaneous re-memorization). The paper validates this mechanism empirically across five unlearning methods (FT, NegGrad+, RandomLabeling, SalUn, MUNBa) on two datasets and two architectures, confirming both failure modes are pervasive rather than implementation-specific. The Energy Ratio and Projection Coefficient are derived as direct empirical probes of the theoretical predictions, with ER_t decaying toward zero under forward failure and CO_t exhibiting sign oscillations around re-memorization events.

## Results

Under random data forgetting on Tiny-ImageNet with VGG-16-BN, all five unlearning methods exhibit progressive degradation in forgetting quality, with the last-task forgetting accuracy averaging approximately 33.2% higher than the single-task baseline. NegGrad+ shows the most severe forward failure, while even the most recent method MUNBa is not immune. Under class-wise forgetting, FT, NegGrad+, and MUNBa display forward failure, whereas RL and SalUn exhibit backward failure (spontaneous re-memorization of previously forgotten classes, e.g., tasks 35–60 in SalUn). MUNBa uniquely suffers from both failure modes simultaneously under class-wise forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four compared unlearning methods exhibit plasticity collapse, confirming it is a pervasive phenomenon rather than an artifact of any single implementation. NegGrad+ [17] suffers the most severe forward failure under random data forgetting, with the largest gap between its continual and single-task forgetting accuracy. RandomLabeling [12] and SalUn [8] avoid pronounced forward failure under class-wise forgetting but instead exhibit clear backward failure: previously well-forgotten classes (near-0% accuracy at their unlearning stage) spontaneously re-emerge to high accuracy in later stages, as shown in the heatmap off-diagonals (Fig. 5c–d). MUNBa [28], the most recent method (ICCV 2025), is the only one to display both forward and backward failure simultaneously under class-wise forgetting, indicating that even state-of-the-art unlearning approaches cannot escape the geometric constraints identified theoretically. No method outperforms the single-task baseline in the continual setting, and the paper's diagnostic quantities (Energy Ratio decaying toward 0, Projection Coefficient oscillating) provide consistent mechanistic evidence across all four methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the concept of plasticity loss—well-studied in continual learning and deep RL (dormant neurons, implicit rank collapse, gradient interference)—and transplants it into the machine unlearning context, where the optimization objectives and parameter-space constraints differ fundamentally. It positions itself against prior continual/incremental unlearning work (e.g., FG-OrIU, ACU) that proposes new methods for sequential forgetting, instead identifying a shared structural failure mode (subspace saturation) that underlies all existing gradient-based unlearning approaches. The theoretical toolkit draws on standard NTK linearization and operator-product analysis, but the specific application to the unlearning objective (forget-loss minus retain-loss) and the resulting two-mode failure taxonomy are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that plasticity collapse is universal across five diverse unlearning methods and two forgetting scenarios raises serious concerns about the long-term privacy guarantees of any deployed unlearning system: if forgetting quality degrades or earlier forgetting is silently reversed, regulatory compliance becomes unsustainable. The diagnostic quantities (Energy Ratio, Projection Coefficient) offer lightweight online monitors that could be integrated into existing pipelines, but the paper stops at diagnosis and does not propose a plasticity-preserving remedy. Key open questions include whether the phenomenon persists in LLMs and generative models, whether the NTK-regime analysis captures the behavior of modern architectures, and what architectural or optimization modifications could prevent subspace saturation without sacrificing single-shot unlearning quality.
