## Problem

RGB-only 6D pose refinement methods rely on photometric or flow-based consistency that degrades under textureless regions, occlusions, and illumination changes, while monocular depth estimators remain scale-ambiguous and locally noisy, preventing their direct use for metric pose correction. No prior method closes the loop between depth correction and pose refinement, treating monocular depth as a static prior rather than an iteratively calibrated geometric signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262597/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (cropped to the object and resized to 256×256), a coarse initial 6D pose, camera intrinsics, and the object's 3D CAD mesh. It produces a refined 6D pose (rotation and translation) and a geometrically consistent monocular depth map after three recurrent refinement iterations. No real depth sensor input is required at inference; a frozen pre-trained monocular depth estimator (MoGe-2) supplies the initial relative depth, which the network converts to a metric-consistent proxy.

## Method

MDC-Net is a recurrent pose-depth co-optimization loop built around two complementary modules. The Scale Refinement Module (SRM) regresses global affine parameters (α, β) and a pixel-wise adaptive scale field S(x,y) plus a local residual ΔD(x,y) from image features, converting relative monocular depth into a metric depth proxy via D_abs = S·(α·D_rel + β) + ΔD, trained with normal-consistency and reprojection losses. The Inlier-Guided Depth Refinement (IGDR) module computes dense optical flow (RAFT) between rendered and observed views, lifts correspondences to 3D using the current depth, evaluates pairwise geometric inconsistency (inspired by TurboClique), constructs a compatibility graph, and extracts inliers via greedy clique sampling with flow-confidence reweighting; the resulting weighted fusion updates the depth map for the next iteration. A GRU-based recurrent pose regressor consumes the 4D cost volume matching features, corrected depth features, and its hidden state to predict a residual pose update ΔP_k applied to the previous pose. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on ~3M synthetic renderings from ShapeNet, Google-Scanned-Objects, and Objaverse with exponentially weighted multi-iteration losses, and operates plug-and-play: it can replace the refinement stage of any initialization method (SCFlow, FoundPose, GenFlow, Co-op) without retraining the initializer.

## Results

On the BOP benchmark (unseen track, single hypothesis, RGB-only), MDC-Net achieves an average Recall of 66.9% across seven datasets, outperforming the previous best RGB-only method Co-op (5 hypo) at 65.7%. The largest gains appear on YCB-Video (74.1% vs. 68.9% for Co-op) and LM-O (66.8% vs. 65.5%), while MDC-Net trails MegaPose on TUD-L (75.2% vs. 84.6%) and Co-op on ITODD (48.2% vs. 49.0%) and HB (84.2% vs. 84.9%). Ablation confirms both SRM and IGDR contribute: removing both drops AR to 59.9% on LM-O, while the full model reaches 66.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDC-Net (66.9% avg AR) outperforms all six single-hypothesis RGB-only baselines: MegaPose (62.3%), SCFlow (60.5%), GigaPose (60.5%), FoundPose (59.6%), and GenFlow (57.0%), with the largest margin over GenFlow (+9.9%). The closest competitor is Co-op with 5 hypotheses (65.7%); MDC-Net wins on LM-O (+1.3), T-LESS (+0.4), IC-BIN (+0.6), and YCB-V (+5.2) but trails Co-op on ITODD (−0.8) and HB (−0.7). Against SCFlow2's RGB variant (63.8% avg in Table 5), MDC-Net leads by 3.1 points, and its RGB-only result (66.9%) approaches SCFlow2's RGB-D result (68.6%) without any real depth input. When used as a plug-and-play refiner on top of other methods' initializations (Table 4), MDC-Net consistently improves AR by 20–40 points over the coarse initial poses and even surpasses the original methods' own depth-assisted final refinement stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDC-Net extends the render-matching-iterative-refinement paradigm established by SCFlow, GenFlow, and Co-op by injecting monocular depth as an explicit geometric signal rather than relying solely on photometric or flow-based consistency. It repurposes TurboClique's pairwise geometric consistency scoring for depth outlier rejection within a pose refinement loop, and builds on recent monocular depth estimators (MoGe-2, Depth Anything V2) as frozen feature providers. The key conceptual departure from prior RGB-only refiners is the closed-loop mutual update: the current pose induces geometric constraints that correct depth, and the corrected depth in turn refines the next pose, rather than treating monocular depth as a static auxiliary prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of MDC-Net—consistently improving the refinement stage of SCFlow, FoundPose, GenFlow, and Co-op by 20–40% AR without modifying their initialization—makes it a practical drop-in component for existing pose estimation pipelines. The remaining gap to RGB-D oracle performance (75.3% vs. 85.5%/79.1% on YCB-V/LM-O) highlights that monocular depth, even after iterative refinement, still carries irreducible ambiguity for textureless or highly symmetric objects. Open questions include whether the 1.92 s runtime can be reduced for real-time deployment, whether integration with generative 3D reconstruction can close the RGB-D gap further, and how the method generalizes to dynamic or deformable objects beyond the rigid-body BOP setting.
