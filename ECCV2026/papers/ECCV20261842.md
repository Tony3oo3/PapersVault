## Problem

Audio-visual language models (AVLMs) suffer from a pervasive form of cross-modal hallucination where strong visual priors cause models to fabricate sounds based on what is seen rather than what is heard. Existing mitigations—training-free contrastive decoding and noise-based preference optimization—either do not penalize cross-modal leakage directly or leave the weak audio representations unimproved, leaving video-driven audio hallucination largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261842/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a video clip, a synchronized audio track, and a text prompt (e.g., an audio-focused question or a joint captioning instruction) and generates a free-form text response. For training, the pipeline additionally constructs audio-swapped inputs by pairing the original video with a mismatched audio track from a different clip, and decomposes joint audio-visual captions into modality-specific targets (visual-only and audio-only captions) via an LLM, which serve as the preferred/dispreferred responses in the preference pairs.

## Method

ACPO builds on DPO but constructs preference pairs along two orthogonal axes to force true modality attribution. First, output-contrastive (audio-attribution) pairs present the model with an audio-swapped input (v_A, a_B) under an audio-focused prompt, preferring the audio caption of the actual track over the visual caption of the video, thereby penalizing visually-driven descriptions masquerading as audio facts. Second, input-contrastive (audio-sensitivity) pairs evaluate the identical joint caption under both the aligned input (v_A, a_A) and the swapped input (v_A, a_B), penalizing predictions that remain invariant when the supporting auditory evidence is removed. The key insight is that naively applying DPO to static, co-occurring audio-visual inputs fails because the model can still exploit visual shortcuts; audio-swapped inputs break this correlation. Training fine-tunes only the audio projection layer (plus LoRA on the LLM for some backbones) over 5,000 preference pairs for one epoch, strengthening audio grounding without disrupting established vision-language capabilities.

## Results

On AVHBench audio hallucination, ACPO achieves the best F1 (80.4) and accuracy (79.9), outperforming OmniDPO (F1 79.0, Acc 78.4) and the base Video-LLaMA2 (F1 77.3, Acc 74.0). On CMM, ACPO reaches 82.0 hallucination resistance on the Overrely Vision subtask (vs. 64.5 for the base model and 77.5 for OmniDPO) and the highest overall accuracy of 83.4. On the newly introduced unimodal captioning evaluation, ACPO improves audio CIDEr from 27.9 to 43.6 on original clips and from 13.1 to 30.6 on swapped clips, while remaining competitive on video captioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACPO outperforms all training-based baselines (SFT, DPO, OmniDPO) on the primary AVHBench audio hallucination task, achieving F1 80.4 vs. 79.0 (OmniDPO), 78.7 (DPO), and 77.8 (SFT), with the largest gain in precision (78.2 vs. 76.6 for OmniDPO), indicating reduced over-prediction of plausible sounds. On CMM's Overrely Vision subtask—directly measuring resistance to visual priors overriding audio—ACPO achieves 82.0 HR, a 4.5-point improvement over OmniDPO (77.5) and a 17.5-point improvement over the base model (64.5). ACPO also attains the best overall accuracy on both benchmarks (78.8 on AVHBench, 83.4 on CMM), surpassing DPO (76.7, 82.9) and OmniDPO (78.6, 82.4). Among zero-shot reference models, ACPO substantially exceeds Gemini-Flash-1.5 (F1 71.9, Acc 63.0) and MiniCPM-o (F1 74.4, Acc 72.8) on audio hallucination, and marginally surpasses Qwen2.5-Omni (F1 75.3, Acc 67.6). The only sub-metric where ACPO trails a baseline is CMM Aud-Lang PA (84.5 vs. 87.0 for DPO), a minor trade-off for the substantial gains in hallucination resistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACPO builds directly on DPO as its optimization backbone but departs from prior preference-optimization work for multimodal hallucination (notably OmniDPO) by replacing noise-based input corruption with audio-swapped inputs that create genuine cross-modal conflict. The key conceptual departure is the dual-axis pair construction: output-contrastive pairs penalize visually-sourced descriptions of audio, while input-contrastive pairs penalize audio-invariant predictions—neither of which is addressed by single-axis methods like V-DPO or OmniDPO. The paper positions itself against the growing body of cross-modal hallucination analysis (AVHBench, CMM, AVCD) that has documented the problem but offered only inference-time decoding fixes or insufficient training signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACPO provides a lightweight, architecture-agnostic recipe (fine-tuning only the audio projection layer) that meaningfully rebalances modality reliance in AVLMs, which is practically important as these models are deployed in safety-critical settings like autonomous systems and assistive technologies. The finding that standard DPO on aligned audio-visual inputs fails to break co-occurrence shortcuts—requiring explicit cross-modal conflict—offers a general design principle for preference learning in multimodal settings. Remaining limitations include persistent failure on brief, subtle audio events and a tendency to over-prioritize auditory detail at the expense of visual specificity in joint captioning, suggesting that fully balanced multimodal grounding remains an open challenge.
