## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D generative methods produce high-fidelity geometry as implicit fields that, when extracted via iso-surfacing, yield over-tessellated meshes unsuitable for downstream workflows like real-time rendering or editing. Existing autoregressive mesh generation approaches that directly predict discrete triangle sequences suffer from slow token-by-token inference and error accumulation, particularly on out-of-distribution inputs. A gap remains between neural geometry synthesis and the production of compact, artist-like mesh topology with structured edge flows and adaptive polygon allocation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a signed distance field voxelized on a 512³ grid (sparsified to voxels within 1/128 of the bounding-box extent), along with user-specified topology parameters: a target face count and a quad-face ratio controlling output density and regularity. The SDF is encoded into a 64³ sparse latent (16-dim) via a fine-tuned Direct3D-S2 VAE. The output is a compact triangle mesh with consistent face orientation, structured edge flows aligned to geometric features, and vertex positions snapped to the input geometry via constrained QEM optimization.

## Method

The key insight is representing mesh topology as a nearest-vertex vector field (NVF), a piecewise-continuous field over the surface where each point encodes the vector to its nearest triangle vertex in the local barycentric frame, creating a bijective mapping to discrete connectivity. A latent flow-matching network (built on the TRELLIS architecture) is trained to generate the NVF latent (64³, 16-dim) conditioned on the SDF latent and topology parameters, using a linear interpolation path between data and Gaussian noise with an ℓ₁ velocity-matching objective. For mesh extraction, a watershed algorithm clusters surface regions by propagating labels from low-displacement roots using a priority queue ordered by predicted target-position distance, then a constrained QEM simplification rejects edge collapses across watershed groups and augments geometric quadrics with positional penalties (Q_t) that bias contraction points toward the NVF-predicted vertex positions. Random 3D surface distortion augmentation during training improves robustness to noisy or irregular input geometry.

## Results

On Objaverse, TriFlow achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.12×10⁻³) and FID (5.0), outperforming QEM (CD 0.14, FID 8.0) and all learning-based baselines. On out-of-distribution TRELLIS-generated shapes, TriFlow ties QEM on CD (0.20) while achieving the best FID (16.2 vs. 22.1 for QEM) and the highest perceptual scores (VLM topology 4.0/5, human topology 4.6/5). Inference takes only 31 seconds per sample, an 8× speedup over TreeMeshGPT (4.3 min) and a ~260× speedup over MeshMosaic (2.2 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriFlow outperforms all four baselines on FID and perceptual scores across both datasets. Against the classical QEM baseline, TriFlow achieves a lower CD on Objaverse (0.12 vs. 0.14) and a substantially lower FID on TRELLIS (16.2 vs. 22.1), while matching QEM's CD on TRELLIS (0.20). Against QuadriFlow, TriFlow is dramatically better on all metrics, with CD 40× lower on Objaverse (0.12 vs. 4.81) and FID 6× lower on TRELLIS (16.2 vs. 95.6). The two autoregressive learning-based baselines degrade severely out-of-distribution: TreeMeshGPT's CD explodes from 0.98 (Objaverse) to 30.00 (TRELLIS), and MeshMosaic's CD jumps from 1.62 to 9.02, while TriFlow remains stable at 0.20. In human preference studies, TriFlow is preferred over TreeMeshGPT 97.9% of the time overall and over MeshMosaic 90.8% of the time, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceived topology quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

TriFlow departs from the dominant autoregressive sequence-modeling paradigm for mesh generation (PolyGen, MeshGPT, TreeMeshGPT, MeshMosaic) by reframing topology as a piecewise-continuous nearest-vertex vector field (NVF) amenable to latent flow matching. Architecturally, it builds directly on TRELLIS for the conditional flow-matching backbone and on Direct3D-S2's VAE for SDF encoding, while its extraction stage repurposes classical QEM simplification with topology-aware constraints derived from a watershed segmentation of the predicted field. This positions the work at the intersection of generative 3D modeling, classical geometry processing, and field-based surface segmentation, rather than extending any single prior line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D content pipelines: converting high-fidelity implicit or over-tessellated generative outputs into compact, artist-like meshes suitable for real-time rendering and editing. The 8× speedup and substantially lower Chamfer Distance over autoregressive baselines make this a viable production alternative, and the out-of-distribution evaluation on TRELLIS-generated shapes demonstrates robustness that prior sequence-based methods lack. Key open questions include scaling beyond the 512³ voxel grid to large scenes, extending the NVF to native n-gon (quad-dominant) topology, and whether the learned topology prior transfers across drastically different geometric domains without retraining.
