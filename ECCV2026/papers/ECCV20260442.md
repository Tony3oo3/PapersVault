## Problem

Existing IL-based visuomotor policies imitate the execution speed of expert demonstrations and operate with a fixed temporal prediction horizon, limiting task throughput and flexibility. Different manipulation stages exhibit varying difficulty, suggesting that motion speed and prediction horizon should be stage-dependent, yet no prior method can implicitly infer such stage-adaptive speeds during end-to-end training without external speed or stage annotations.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes multi-view RGB images (two wrist cameras plus one fixed camera), robot proprioceptive state, and a text task instruction, and outputs a fixed-length action chunk of joint states and gripper state. The key structural detail is that the output chunk is retimed in the frequency domain via DCT so that the same H-step tensor encodes a variable effective temporal horizon h = H·r, where r is the predicted speed ratio; a lightweight Ratio Head outputs r from latent observation features, and at inference a Nonlinear Temporal Aggregation module blends overlapping variable-speed chunks with exponential-decay weighting to produce smooth per-step actions.

## Method

AutoSpeed formulates training as cost-aware multi-target selective optimization: at each timestep it constructs M candidate action chunks by applying DCT-based frequency-domain retiming at a discrete set of speed ratios (e.g., 0.8–2.2 in steps of 0.2), evaluates each candidate with a composite cost J = E/(w + log h) that penalizes short horizons, and back-propagates only on the minimum-cost target, so the policy implicitly learns which speed is optimal per stage. The DCT transform enables non-integer speed scaling while preserving high-frequency action details critical for fine-grained manipulation. A lightweight Ratio Head is co-trained to predict the speed ratio from latent features, and for generative models (diffusion or flow-matching heads) a three-stage recipe first minimizes the average loss over all candidates, then switches to selective optimization, and finally freezes the Ratio Head to converge the generator toward the predicted speed mode. At inference, Nonlinear Temporal Aggregation replaces the standard uniform-interval temporal ensemble, collecting overlapping predictions across variable-speed chunks within a time window and combining them with exponential-decay weights plus a frequency-domain correction for slight temporal misalignment.

## Results

On the ALOHA single-task benchmark, AutoSpeed reduces Transfer Cube episode length from 272 to 160 steps (1.7× speedup) and, with a high-gain controller, improves success rate from 72% to 78%. On the multi-task Meta-World benchmark, BAKU-Flow(AutoSpeed) achieves 65% success rate (vs. 57% vanilla) with episode length reduced from 82 to 71 steps, while BAKU-DiT(AutoSpeed) jumps from 43% to 62% SR. On real-world tasks, AutoSpeed+NTA delivers an average ~1.78× speedup (e.g., 50.11s → 28.52s on Stacking Two Cubes) while simultaneously improving success rates by 3–10 percentage points across all four tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla ACT baseline [40] on ALOHA, AutoSpeed substantially reduces execution time (Transfer Cube: 272→160 steps; Insertion: 353→296 steps) and, when paired with a high-gain controller, improves Transfer Cube success rate from 72% to 78% while matching or exceeding the Insertion success rate (24% vs. 22%). Against the vanilla BAKU baselines [13] on multi-task benchmarks, AutoSpeed consistently wins: on Meta-World, BAKU-DiT(AutoSpeed) gains +19.0% SR (43%→62%) and BAKU-Flow(AutoSpeed) gains +8.0% SR (57%→65%), both with shorter episode lengths; on LIBERO-10, BAKU-Flow(AutoSpeed) improves SR from 47% to 52% and cuts episode length by ~40% (287→171). On real-world tasks, BAKU-Flow(AutoSpeed)+NTA outperforms BAKU-Flow+TA on all four tasks in both success rate and execution time, with the largest gain on Place the Toy (+10% SR, 18.13s→10.13s). The one case where AutoSpeed slightly underperforms is LIBERO-10 with BAKU-DiT (49% vs. 50% SR), though episode length is marginally shorter, suggesting the DiT head benefits less from speed modulation on this particular suite.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoSpeed extends the action chunking paradigm (ACT, BAKU) by replacing the fixed temporal prediction horizon with a stage-adaptive one, and departs from prior motion-speed modulation work (DemoSpeedUp, ESPADA, SpeedAug) by eliminating the need for pre-training annotations or test-time inference. The key conceptual departure is formulating speed selection as a cost-aware multi-target optimization problem solved implicitly during end-to-end training, rather than relying on external proxy policies or VLM-based stage labels. Technically, the DCT-based frequency-domain retiming generalizes prior integer-rate downsampling to arbitrary non-integer speed ratios while preserving high-frequency action details, and the Nonlinear Temporal Aggregation module adapts the uniform-interval temporal ensemble from ACT to handle variable-speed chunk overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for industrial deployment where task throughput is critical: AutoSpeed delivers ~1.7× real-world speedups while simultaneously improving success rates, and its model-agnostic design means it can be dropped into existing ACT, diffusion, or flow-matching pipelines without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the implicitly inferred speed ratios transfer to unseen tasks or novel embodiments, how the composite cost parameter w should be set for safety-critical applications, and whether the approach scales to very long-horizon or highly multi-modal manipulation sequences. The paper does not explore failure modes when the speed range is mis-specified for a given task, nor does it analyze the computational overhead of multi-candidate denoising at scale.
