## Problem

Vision-language models are fundamentally 'flat' in spatial reasoning: they are trained to interpret 2D projections but must recover latent 3D geometry and temporal continuity to reason about the physical world. Existing approaches—scaling spatial SFT data or adding spatial tokens—fail to produce a coherent latent world model, while directly optimizing a unified 4D objective over space and time is computationally and algorithmically intractable. The paper tackles this dimensional mismatch by asking whether spatial reasoning can be decomposed into verifiable, orthogonal sub-objectives that are individually optimizable via reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images or video frame sequences as visual tokens, paired with a text spatial-temporal query; for the XY and Z reward computations, ground-truth depth maps and camera intrinsics/poses are used as supervision signals during training but are not required at inference. Outputs are structured text sequences following an 'Anchor-Transfer-Verify' chain-of-thought format, which may include 3D bounding boxes, point correspondences, camera-motion descriptions, or route plans, all gated by a format reward that enforces structural validity before any geometric reward is applied.

## Method

FactoSR is a two-stage pipeline on Qwen3-VL: Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning with a short-to-long curriculum (general multimodal data followed by long-form 'Anchor-Transfer-Verify' CoT trajectories) to build spatial grounding; Stage 2 applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a factorized reward that decomposes 4D consistency into three orthogonal signals. The XY reward enforces reprojection-consistent point correspondence across views, gated by a depth-overlap visibility mask to reject occluded matches; the Z reward uses Kendall-τ rank correlation on matched 3D bounding-box centers to supervise relative depth ordering rather than metric localization; the T reward requires that forward and inverse camera-motion predictions are both correct, enforcing temporal cycle consistency. The total reward is a weighted sum of accuracy, XY, Z, and T terms, all multiplied by a format gate, and is optimized via group-relative advantage estimation without a value model.

## Results

FactoSR-8B-RL achieves 62.0 average accuracy across 9 3D/4D spatial benchmarks, outperforming the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct base model by +2.9 points. On the two core 4D reasoning benchmarks, it reaches 61.5 on VSI-Bench (+5.9 over base) and 55.4 on All-Angles-Bench (+5.9 over base), setting new state-of-the-art among open-source VLMs. The model also maintains competitive general multimodal performance, scoring 85.7 on MMBench-EN and 69.1 on MMStar, with only a −1.0 drop on MMStar relative to the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open-source spatial VLM, VST-7B-Thinking (60.5 3/4D-Avg), FactoSR-8B-RL leads by +1.5 points, with particularly large margins on VSI-Bench (+9.3) and All-Angles-Bench (+6.4). Compared to the base Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (59.1), the full pipeline (SFT + RL) yields +2.9 on the 3/4D average, with the largest single-benchmark gain on RealWorldQA (+7.2) and VSI-Bench (+5.9). FactoSR also surpasses Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (59.9) by +2.1 and MiMo-VL-7B (58.2) by +3.8 on the 3/4D average. However, it still trails the proprietary Gemini-2.5-Pro (64.4) by 2.4 points on the 3/4D average, though it narrows the gap on VSI-Bench (61.5 vs. 48.4) and All-Angles-Bench (55.4 vs. 61.3). On general benchmarks, FactoSR-8B-RL matches or slightly exceeds the base model on MMBench-EN (+1.5) but drops 1.0 on MMStar, indicating the spatial RL training preserves but does not enhance general multimodal capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR-for-spatial-reasoning line (SVQA-R1, SpatialThinker, SATORI-R1, SpaceR) by replacing their single-view, final-answer-only rewards with a factorized multi-dimensional reward that explicitly supervises planar correspondence (XY), depth ordering (Z), and temporal reversibility (T). It builds on the GRPO/DAPO policy optimization framework and the Qwen3-VL backbone, but departs from prior spatial RL work that treats the 4D world as a flat 2D pattern-matching problem. The two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline with an 'Anchor-Transfer-Verify' CoT structure follows the progressive-training philosophy of SpatialLadder and VST, but the key conceptual departure is the geometric decomposition of the reward space into orthogonal physical dimensions rather than a monolithic correctness signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factorized reward design offers a principled recipe for injecting physically grounded spatial reasoning into VLMs without requiring 3D reconstruction modules or external perception tools, making it broadly applicable to any VLM backbone. However, the reward computation for XY and Z requires ground-truth depth maps and camera poses, which limits direct deployment in open-world settings where such annotations are unavailable. The 8B model still trails Gemini 2.5-Pro by ~2.4 points on the 3D/4D average, and all experiments use a single backbone, leaving open whether the factorization generalizes across architectures. A key open question is whether the T reward's cycle-consistency principle can be extended beyond discrete camera-motion sequences to continuous egocentric video understanding.
