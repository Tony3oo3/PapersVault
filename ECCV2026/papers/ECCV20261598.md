## Problem

Existing pose-free view synthesis methods are restricted to sparse inputs (typically fewer than 10–20 views) and cannot scale to the long sequences needed for wide scene coverage, while geometry-based pose estimators like VGGT and DUSt3R rely on expensive dense 3D supervision (point maps, depth) that is far less scalable than image-level labels. No prior work jointly achieves long-sequence (hundreds of views) pose-free novel view synthesis and accurate camera pose estimation without dense geometric ground truth.

## Input / Output

The model takes N uncalibrated RGB images (up to 256 views at 512×288) as input, where each image is patch-tokenized and augmented with a learnable camera token; the first view is fixed as the canonical reference frame with identity rotation and zero translation, and translation scale is normalized to unit length. Target views are specified by camera pose and intrinsics, encoded as Plücker-ray patch tokens. The model outputs 9-dimensional camera parameters (4-dim quaternion, 3-dim translation, 2-dim normalized focal lengths) for every input view, plus rendered RGB images for arbitrary target camera configurations, with novel views rendered independently at 58.8 FPS regardless of target count.

## Method

LVSPM stacks 24 LaCT blocks (312M params, d=768, 12 heads), each combining per-view windowed self-attention, a large-chunk test-time training (TTT) layer with SwiGLU-MLP, and a feed-forward MLP. A key architectural choice is asymmetric K/V routing: only input-view tokens generate keys/values and drive TTT weight updates, while target-view tokens are read-only queries, enabling each novel view to be synthesized independently in parallel. Input views are encoded without Plücker rays (unlike prior pose-required methods), relying instead on learnable camera tokens inspired by VGGT for pose regression via a lightweight two-layer MLP head. Training uses only RGB photometric loss (L2 + LPIPS), pose L2 loss, and intrinsic L2 loss—no dense 3D labels—across a three-stage curriculum on synthetic (ASE) and real (DL3DV, ScanNet++, Hypersim, Co3Dv2) data. The ablation confirms that NVS supervision alone provides sufficient geometric signal for pose estimation: removing it drops AUC3 from 71.9 to 50.9, while the full model surpasses geometry-based methods trained with point-map supervision.

## Results

On DL3DV-10K (the most challenging large-scale benchmark), LVSPM achieves 90.1 AUC3 at 256 input views, surpassing VGGT (83.6) and DA3 (83.8) while baselines like Fast3R collapse to 8.0 AUC3. On RealEstate10k at 128 views, LVSPM reaches 61.3 AUC3 versus VGGT's 20.6—a nearly 3× margin at the strictest threshold. For novel view synthesis on DL3DV-10K, LVSPM achieves 25.77 PSNR at 16 views (beating pose-dependent DepthSplat at 24.80) and maintains 22.09 PSNR at 256 views while AnySplat drops to 18.55. The model processes 256 input views in under 2 seconds on a single H100 GPU and renders novel views at 58.8 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LVSPM outperforms all six pose-estimation baselines (Cut3R, Fast3R, Flare, VGGT, TTT3R, DA3) on DL3DV-10K across every view count and metric, with the largest margins at 256 views where Fast3R and Cut3R collapse (8.0 and 19.2 AUC3 vs. LVSPM's 90.1). Against VGGT specifically, LVSPM gains +13.7 AUC3 at 16 views and +6.5 AUC3 at 256 views on DL3DV, and +40.7 AUC3 at 128 views on RE10k (61.3 vs. 20.6). DA3 is the closest competitor, leading LVSPM by 1.2 AUC3 at 16 views on Co3Dv2 (47.2 vs. 39.2), but LVSPM surpasses DA3 on AUC3 at 64/128 views and AUC5 at 128 views on Co3Dv2, and leads on all DL3DV metrics. For view synthesis, LVSPM beats pose-free AnySplat by +2.62 dB PSNR at 16 views and +3.54 dB at 256 views on DL3DV, and also surpasses pose-dependent DepthSplat (25.77 vs. 24.80 PSNR at 16 views) despite requiring no ground-truth poses; DepthSplat fails entirely beyond 64 views. On RE10K sparse-view NVS (Table 4), LVSPM zero-shot (22.50 avg PSNR) outperforms all pose-free baselines including NoPoSplat (23.42), CocaSplat (23.20), and Flare (22.40), and its fine-tuned variant (25.15 avg PSNR) exceeds pose-required methods like MVSplat (23.98) and pixelSplat (23.85).

## Relation to Prior Work

LVSPM unifies two previously separate research threads: long-sequence view synthesis via the LaCT test-time-training backbone [86] and the minimal-3D-bias sequence-to-sequence formulation of LVSM [27], with the camera-token mechanism borrowed from VGGT [63] for direct pose regression. Prior pose-free NVS methods (AnySplat, DUSt3R-based pipelines) are restricted to fewer than ~30 views and rely on dense 3D geometric supervision, while long-sequence models (LaCT, Long-LRM) assume known poses. LVSPM departs from both by jointly predicting poses and novel views from unposed inputs at 256 views, trained with only RGB photometric and pose losses—no point maps or depth labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is enabling pose-free, long-sequence view synthesis without expensive SfM preprocessing or dense 3D annotation, which is a significant barrier to deploying 3D reconstruction on real-world image collections. The ablation showing NVS supervision alone nearly matches dense-geometry supervision for pose accuracy (AUC3 71.9 vs. 50.9 without it) opens the door to cheaper training pipelines for geometry tasks more broadly. Limitations include the multi-stage training recipe, potential degradation in weakly textured or extreme-lighting scenes, and the fact that specialized geometry models (DA3) still lead on pure pose estimation in sparse-view settings. Open questions include whether fully self-supervised training can eliminate even the pose labels, and how far the TTT-based scaling can be pushed beyond 256 views.
