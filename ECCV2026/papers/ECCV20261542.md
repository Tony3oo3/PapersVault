## Problem

Current Vision-Language Models can recognize objects and localize them in 2D pixel space but remain deficient in 3D spatial perception (depth, multi-view consistency, spatiotemporal relationships) and spatial reasoning (mentally manipulating scene layouts). Prior data-driven efforts address only isolated aspects—single-image geometry, a single scenario, or only the SFT stage—leaving no unified framework that holistically cultivates both perception and reasoning across diverse visual modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261542/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes single RGB images, multi-view image sets, or video clips alongside a text question, with a FoV unification module warping metric-sensitive inputs into a canonical virtual camera to stabilize cross-dataset geometry. Outputs are task-dependent: 9-DoF 3D bounding boxes in camera coordinates (x, y, z, x_l, y_l, z_l, p, y, r), spatial relationship answers, depth/distance estimates, or discretized 256-bin action tokens for VLA. For spatial reasoning, the output follows the RT-CoT structure of three explicit segments—[RECON] (structured textual layout), [REASON] (grounded reasoning trace), and [ANSWER]—where the reconstruction is renderable as an SVG.

## Method

VST employs a progressive three-stage pipeline on a standard ViT-MLP-LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL): (1) SFT on VST-P (4.1M samples, 19 tasks across single-image, multi-image, and video) to build foundational spatial perception, with a FoV unification module normalizing camera intrinsics; (2) cold-start CoT training on VST-R (135K samples) using RT-CoT, where the model first reconstructs the scene layout as text before reasoning, with a BEV annotation strategy that renders ground-truth 3D boxes onto a top-down plane to guide the teacher model in generating coherent reasoning traces; (3) GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a 3D-aware layout reward that combines 3D IoU and F1 score via bipartite matching between predicted and ground-truth boxes, explicitly optimizing layout accuracy beyond what SFT alone achieves. The key insight is that textual scene reconstruction serves as a more general intermediate representation than numerical 3D box outputs, and that BEV prompting compensates for the teacher model's own spatial limitations during data synthesis.

## Results

VST-7B-RL achieves state-of-the-art spatial performance among open VLMs: 86.5 on CV-Bench, 60.1 on 3DSRBench, 34.8 on MMSI-Bench, and 61.2 on VSI-Bench, while retaining competitive general scores (e.g., 83.0 MMBench, 86.1 OCRBench). On SUN RGB-D 3D object detection, VST-7B-RL reaches 44.2 AP₁₅, surpassing all general VLMs (Seed1.5-VL: 33.5, Gemini-2.0-Pro: 32.5) and expert 3D methods (Implicit3D: 24.1, Total3DU: 14.3). On the LIBERO robotic benchmark, the VST-tuned 3B model improves average success rate by 8.6% over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline at 2.5K training steps (61.7 vs. 53.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o, VST-7B-RL wins decisively on all spatial benchmarks (CV-Bench 86.5 vs. 76.0, MMSI 34.8 vs. 30.3, VSI 61.2 vs. 34.0) but trails on general tasks like MMMU (49.4 vs. 70.7) and RWQA (68.5 vs. 76.2). Versus the proprietary Gemini-2.5-Pro, VST-7B-RL narrows the MMSI gap to 2.1 points (34.8 vs. 36.9) and substantially outperforms on VSI-Bench (61.2 vs. not reported), yet remains behind on BLINK (62.6 vs. 70.6) and most general benchmarks. Compared to Seed1.5-VL, VST-7B-SFT edges ahead on CV-Bench (85.5 vs. 85.2) and VST-7B-RL leads on MMSI (34.8 vs. 29.7) and VSI (61.2 vs. 41.5), though Seed1.5-VL retains advantages on BLINK (72.1 vs. 62.6) and 3DSRBench (61.6 vs. 60.1). Against spatial-specialized baselines (SpaceR-7B, SPAR-8B), VST-7B-RL dominates across the board, e.g., VSI 61.2 vs. 43.5 (SpaceR) and 41.1 (SPAR), and MMSI 34.8 vs. 20.1 (SpaceR). Relative to its own base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VST-7B-RL gains +5.9 on CV-Bench, +9.9 on MMSI, and +22.3 on VSI-Bench while incurring only minor drops on general tasks (e.g., MMMU 49.4 vs. 51.2, MMB 83.0 vs. 83.5). On 3D object detection (SUN RGB-D), VST-7B-RL (44.2 AP₁₅) outperforms all general VLMs and expert 3D methods except the specialized Gemini Robotics-ER (48.3), confirming that spatial tuning alone can rival dedicated 3D encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of spatial-tuning work for VLMs (SpatialVLM, SPAR, SAT, Space-llava) by unifying previously isolated aspects—single-image geometry, multi-view consistency, and spatiotemporal understanding—into a single progressive SFT→RL pipeline. It departs from approaches that attach expert 3D encoders (e.g., VLM-3R) by achieving competitive 3D object detection with a standard ViT backbone, and from prior spatial-reasoning methods that rely on cognitive maps or hint tokens by proposing RT-CoT, a purely textual scene reconstruction grounded via BEV prompting. The VLA adaptation follows OpenVLA's action-discretization formulation, but the key departure is that no robotic pretraining data is used; spatial priors alone are shown to transfer to manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.1M-sample VST-P dataset and the RT-CoT + BEV prompting recipe provide a practical, reproducible recipe for injecting 3D spatial competence into any ViT-MLP-LLM VLM without architectural changes, which is directly relevant to the growing physical-AI and robotics community. The 8.6% LIBERO improvement from spatial tuning alone (no robotic data) suggests that spatial perception is a key bottleneck for VLA models, opening a path to cheaper robotic pretraining. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor/egocentric settings, whether the RL stage can be made more sample-efficient, and whether the textual reconstruction in RT-CoT can be replaced by a learned latent representation for higher-fidelity 3D reasoning.
