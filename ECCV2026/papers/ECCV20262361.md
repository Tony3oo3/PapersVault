## Problem

Inferring intrinsic physical properties (density, elasticity, yield stress) from a single static image is fundamentally under-constrained, as multiple motion trajectories can appear visually plausible while corresponding to very different material parameters. Existing physics-aware methods either embed physical priors into generative architectures (limiting generalization to novel materials) or rely on supervised property estimation (suffering from data scarcity and domain shift), and neither provides a mechanism to actively gather physical evidence beyond the single frame.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'a wolf-like sand object collapses under gravity') as input. It produces a physically consistent dynamic 3D video rendered from a differentiable MPM simulation (128–200 steps, single RTX 4090) initialized from a Trellis 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction, along with a structured Hybrid Physical Parameters specification containing SI-unit material constants and simulator-native descriptors (boundary conditions, force directions, contact modes). The intermediate I2V probe videos are generated at 720p with 50 frames using CogVideoX-5B.

## Method

PhyMAGIC is a training-free, three-stage pipeline that reframes single-image physical inference as an active evidence-acquisition problem. First, a pretrained CogVideoX-I2V diffusion model synthesizes motion probe videos from the input image, with motion-aware optical-flow subsampling to retain informative frames. Second, GPT-4o performs iterative physical reasoning: it estimates object-level parameters with self-consistency confidence scores (agreement across up to three independent samples), and any parameter below a threshold γ triggers a confidence-driven prompt refinement loop that generates targeted new probe motions to expose complementary cues for the uncertain attributes. Third, the converged material properties are combined with VLM-selected simulator-native descriptors (canonical motion templates defining boundary type, initial velocity, force magnitude, and contact configuration) into a Hybrid Physical Parameters specification, which is executed in a differentiable MPM solver where each 3DGS Gaussian kernel from Trellis reconstruction serves as a material-carrying particle. The entire loop requires no task-specific training, manual annotation, or fine-tuning.

## Results

On the physics-aware comparison (Table 2), PhyMAGIC achieves an average CLIP similarity of 0.252 across six scenes, outperforming Physics3D (0.217), PhysDreamer (0.213), and OMNIPHYSGS (0.197) by 16.1%, 18.3%, and 27.4% respectively. On Image-Motion-FID (Table 3), PhyMAGIC scores 94.69, the lowest among all physics-aware baselines (Physics3D: 98.91, OMNIPHYSGS: 106.60, PhysDreamer: 107.47). In the 61-participant user study, PhyMAGIC attains the highest mean ratings of 3.00/4 for physical plausibility and 3.07/4 for text consistency, surpassing CogVideoX* (2.97/3.00), CogVideoX (2.58/2.49), and OpenSora2.0 (2.02/2.18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three physics-aware 3D dynamic generation baselines, PhyMAGIC wins on CLIP similarity in every scene where all methods report results: it leads Physics3D by 0.069 on swing ficus (0.294 vs. 0.225), by 0.053 on sand wolf (0.200 vs. 0.147), and by 0.041 on lifting hat (0.270 vs. 0.229). On Image-Motion-FID, PhyMAGIC (94.69) is the best, beating Physics3D (98.91) by 4.2 points and OMNIPHYSGS (106.60) by 11.9 points. Against the two general I2V video generators, PhyMAGIC achieves a higher average CLIP similarity (0.251) than CogVideoX (0.239) and OpenSora2.0 (0.233), and also outperforms CogVideoX on aesthetic score in four of eight scenarios (e.g., 39.67 vs. 21.51 on sway tree). In the human study, PhyMAGIC edges out CogVideoX* (the ablation with prompt refinement but no MPM simulation) by 0.03 on plausibility and 0.07 on text consistency, confirming that the full pipeline with differentiable simulation provides a measurable advantage over prompt refinement alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyMAGIC sits at the intersection of three established lines: 3D Gaussian Splatting for single-image 3D reconstruction (Trellis), image-to-video diffusion models for motion synthesis (CogVideoX), and VLM-based physical reasoning (PhysGen, PhysFlow). It departs from prior physics-aware generators (PhysGaussian, PhysDreamer, Physics3D, OMNIPHYSGS) by rejecting both paradigms—embedding physical priors into architectures and learning property estimators from annotated data—in favor of a training-free, test-time active probing loop where the I2V model generates targeted motion probes and the VLM iteratively refines parameter estimates via confidence scores. The differentiable MPM simulator, initialized from 3DGS particles augmented with VLM-inferred material properties, follows the simulation substrate of PhysGaussian but replaces its manually specified parameters with inferred ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, modular design (off-the-shelf I2V + VLM + MPM) lowers the barrier to physically consistent dynamic generation, making it accessible without task-specific fine-tuning or large annotated datasets. However, the VLM's reasoning remains qualitative, limiting precision for high-frequency phenomena (exact friction coefficients, elastic vibrations), and simulation fidelity is bottlenecked by the quality of the initial 3DGS reconstruction, particularly for thin-shell or complex-topology objects. Open questions include whether differentiable rendering gradients between probe videos and simulated outputs could close the remaining gap, and whether the framework extends to articulated or dense multi-object interactions.
