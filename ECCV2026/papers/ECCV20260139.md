## Problem

Existing egocentric reconstruction methods treat scene geometry and human motion as separate problems, often requiring auxiliary inputs such as pre-computed SLAM trajectories, 3D point clouds, or dedicated hand-tracking modules, and suffer from slow optimization-based inference. No unified framework exists that jointly reconstructs the viewer's full-body kinematics, hand dynamics, and gaze alongside the scene's camera trajectory and depth from a single monocular egocentric video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260139/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular egocentric RGB video, processed as 2-second clips downsampled to 8 FPS at 256×256 resolution for video modalities (RGB, depth), while non-video modalities (camera, gaze, body, hand) retain the original 30 FPS. It simultaneously outputs camera trajectory, 2D gaze locations, full-body SMPL(-X) motion, hand joint trajectories, and temporally consistent depth maps, all integrated into a shared 4D coordinate system via a lightweight floor-plane fitting alignment step that maps affine-invariant depth to metric scale.

## Method

ReViV discretizes all six modalities (RGB, depth, camera, gaze, hand, body) into a unified token sequence using modality-specific VQ-VAEs, with body and hand tokenizers employing spherical quantization and EMA codebook updates to prevent collapse across heterogeneous datasets. A Masked Generative Egocentric Transformer (MGET), a T5-based encoder-decoder with 12 blocks and 768 hidden dimension, learns the joint distribution p(X,Y) by predicting randomly masked tokens conditioned on visible ones, with a Dirichlet-sampled masking strategy that produces an ensemble of arbitrary conditional distributions over all modality subsets. An additional trainable ViT encodes raw video into continuous spatially detailed features to compensate for quantization loss. At inference, RGB tokens serve as visible context while all viewer and scene modalities are decoded via iterative parallel sampling, yielding a single feed-forward pass that is 100× faster than diffusion-based baselines. The model is pretrained on a 7B-token multimodal corpus spanning eight datasets, with geometric pseudo-labels generated by Video Depth Anything for datasets lacking dense depth.

## Results

On the Aria Digital Twin benchmark, ReViV achieves GA-MPJPE of 111.5 mm and PA-MPJPE of 88.6 mm for full-body motion, with a motion FID of 0.442 and cosine similarity of 0.751, all from pure monocular RGB without any camera trajectory input. For hand reconstruction across four benchmarks (HoloAssist, HOT3D, ARCTIC, TACO), ReViV achieves PA-MPJPE of 10.5, 13.6, 13.7, and 9.4 mm respectively, outperforming both HaMeR and Dyn-HaMR by large margins. Inference takes only 0.7 s per clip, making it 100× faster than EgoAllo and over 400× faster than Dyn-HaMR. On camera tracking, ReViV achieves the lowest RRE (1.279°) and competitive ATE (0.015 m), while on depth estimation it reports AbsRel of 0.265 and δ₁.₂₅ of 56.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EgoAllo [57] and UniEgoMotion [36] on body motion (Table 2), ReViV wins on PA-MPJPE, Similarity, and FID even when those baselines are given ground-truth camera trajectories, while UniEgoMotion retains a slight edge on GA-MPJPE (105.8 vs. 111.5 mm) thanks to its access to perfect external tracking. For hand reconstruction (Table 3), ReViV dominates both HaMeR [38] and Dyn-HaMR [59] across all four benchmarks by 2–4× in PA-MPJPE (e.g., 10.5 vs. 32.9 mm on HoloAssist; 9.4 vs. 29.1 mm on TACO) while running 100× and 400× faster respectively. On camera tracking (Table 4), ReViV achieves the best RRE (1.279°) and RTE (0.009 m) among all methods, trailing only VIPE [17] on ATE (0.015 vs. 0.005 m), which benefits from dense bundle adjustment. For gaze estimation, ReViV reduces MSE from 0.0311 (EgoM2P [22]) to 0.0211. On depth estimation, ReViV (AbsRel 0.265, δ₁.₂₅ 56.5%) significantly outperforms EgoM2P (0.458, 30.3%) but falls short of EgoMono4D [60] (0.150, 83.9%), which leverages UniDepth pretrained weights; however, ReViV is over 20× faster than EgoMono4D.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReViV builds directly on EgoM2P's scene-centric egocentric pretraining foundation, extending it from 4B to 7B tokens by adding dense hand and full-body kinematics. It departs from prior egocentric pose estimation methods (EgoAllo, UniEgoMotion, HMD²) that require auxiliary inputs like SLAM trajectories or external hand trackers, and from 4D reconstruction methods (Shape of Motion, feed-forward Gaussian approaches) that target third-person or multi-view settings. The key conceptual departure is formulating the joint viewer-view reconstruction as masked generative modeling over a unified discrete token sequence, borrowing the MaskGIT/MAGVIT paradigm and VQ-VAE tokenization (Cosmos Tokenizers) from the broader multimodal generation literature, rather than treating body, hand, gaze, camera, and depth as separate regression or diffusion tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for pre-computed SLAM trajectories, 3D point clouds, or dedicated hand-tracking modules, ReViV makes holistic egocentric 4D reconstruction accessible from casual monocular video, which is critical for real-world AR/VR and assistive robotics deployments. The 100× speedup over diffusion-based baselines and the single feed-forward architecture position this as a practical foundation model for embodied AI. The acknowledged limitation—VQ-VAE quantization degrading fine-grained depth estimation relative to continuous regression methods like EgoMono4D—points to an open question about integrating continuous generative priors (e.g., conditional diffusion) into the decoding stage while preserving the unified multimodal reasoning. Whether the cross-modal supervision benefits generalize beyond the specific datasets used, and whether the approach scales to longer temporal horizons, remain open.
