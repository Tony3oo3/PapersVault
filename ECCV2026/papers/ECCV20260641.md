## Problem

Current NeRF- and GS-based SLAM systems rely on pixel-wise color and depth supervision, lacking the higher-level scene understanding needed for complex environments. While vision foundation models like DINOv2 extract rich semantic features, they inherently ignore 3D scene structure, so naively embedding them into SLAM pipelines yields only marginal gains. The paper asks whether enriching DINO features with explicit geometry awareness can unlock substantially larger improvements in both reconstruction quality and tracking accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260641/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized RGB-D frames; the RGB channel is processed by a frozen DINOv2 ViT-small to extract per-pixel DINO features, while the depth map is projected through a lightweight MLP into geometric features. Outputs include per-frame camera poses, a persistent 3D scene representation (tri-planes for the NeRF variant, 2D Gaussian surfels for the GS variant), rendered novel-view images, extracted meshes, and rendered depth maps. The SGE is optimized online alongside the SLAM backbone, so no offline pre-training on scene data is required.

## Method

The core component is a Scene Geometry Encoder (SGE) that projects DINO features into appearance embeddings f_a and depth into geometric embeddings f_g, then applies two rounds of cross-attention (f_g as query attending over f_D and f_a) to produce geometry-aware geoDINO features that encode both contextual semantics and local 3D structure. For the NeRF-based pipeline, geoDINO features supervise dedicated tri-plane encodings via an L1 feature loss, while vanilla DINO features supervise a rendered DINO feature map through standard ray-casting; for the GS-based pipeline, geoDINO features are appended as extra channels to each Gaussian's parameter vector and vanilla DINO features supervise the rasterized feature map. Both pipelines additionally use standard color and depth photometric losses, and the SGE is jointly optimized online with the scene representation and camera poses during mapping. Ablations confirm that geoDINO features roughly double the improvement over vanilla DINO alone, and that simple depth concatenation cannot replicate the cross-attention mechanism's effect.

## Results

On Replica, DINO-SLAM (NeRF) achieves the best reconstruction quality among NeRF-based methods with 1.88 cm accuracy, 1.60 cm completion, 97.22% completion rate, 0.63 cm depth L1, and 0.49 cm ATE RMSE. DINO-SLAM (GS) achieves state-of-the-art rendering with 41.42 dB PSNR, 0.994 SSIM, 0.021 LPIPS, and the best tracking at 0.05 cm RMSE. On ScanNet, the NeRF variant achieves 6.9 cm average ATE RMSE and the GS variant 7.3 cm, outperforming most baselines. On TUM, DINO-SLAM (GS) achieves 1.42 cm average RMSE, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINO-SLAM (NeRF) outperforms ESLAM (its direct baseline) by 22% in RMSE (0.49 vs 0.63 cm) and achieves the best completion rate (97.22%) and accuracy (1.88 cm) among all NeRF-based methods, though SNI-SLAM retains a slight edge on RMSE (0.46 vs 0.49 cm). DINO-SLAM (GS) decisively beats GauS-SLAM (its direct baseline) across all metrics: +1.17 dB PSNR (41.42 vs 40.25), 0.994 vs 0.991 SSIM, and 0.05 vs 0.06 cm RMSE, while also surpassing all other GS methods by large margins (e.g., +2.48 dB PSNR over MonoGS). On ScanNet, DINO-SLAM (NeRF) achieves 6.9 cm average RMSE, beating ESLAM (7.4) and Co-SLAM (8.7), though PLGSLAM (6.8) edges it slightly due to its large-scene-specific design. On TUM, DINO-SLAM (GS) achieves the best average RMSE (1.42 cm), narrowly beating MonoGS (1.47) and VSS-SLAM (1.49), while DINO-SLAM (NeRF) (1.93 cm) outperforms ESLAM (2.00) and Co-SLAM (2.17). The ablation confirms that geoDINO features (via SGE) roughly double the improvement over vanilla DINO features alone, validating the core claim that geometry-aware lifting is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense RGB-D SLAM literature by injecting foundation-model features into scene representations, building directly on ESLAM (tri-plane NeRF) and GauS-SLAM (2D Gaussian Surfels) as base systems. Prior work such as SNI-SLAM already embedded vanilla DINO features for semantic segmentation, but the authors argue these features lack 3D geometric understanding. The key departure is the Scene Geometry Encoder (SGE), which uses cross-attention between depth-derived geometric features and DINO features to produce 'geoDINO' features that encode both semantics and spatial structure — a mechanism absent from prior DINO-informed SLAM methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SGE module is a lightweight, general-purpose component that can be plugged into diverse scene encodings (hash grids, tri-planes, neural points, 3D Gaussians, 2D surfels), making it a practical building block for future radiance-field SLAM systems. The ablation showing that geoDINO roughly doubles the improvement of vanilla DINO, and that simple depth concatenation cannot replicate this, provides a clear design principle for integrating foundation-model features with geometric cues. Limitations include sensitivity to depth noise (though more robust than the baseline), a notable FPS reduction in the NeRF variant, and the absence of loop closure or sub-mapping, which the authors acknowledge as future work.
