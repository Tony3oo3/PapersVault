## Problem

The embodiment gap between human hands and robotic hands of varying morphologies (2-, 3-, and 5-fingered) makes it difficult to learn effective manipulation policies from human demonstrations. Existing imitation-centric methods merely reproduce human motions without adapting to the robot's physical characteristics, while goal-centric RL methods lose the crucial human prior and fall into local optima. No unified framework exists that can discover morphology-aligned manipulation policies across diverse robotic hand configurations from human demonstrations.

## Input / Output

The policy network consumes a fixed-dimensional observation vector constructed by concatenating a 13-dim wrist state (position, orientation, velocities), a zero-padded joint state (angles and velocities trigonometrically encoded as cos/sin to avoid 2π wrap-around, padded to D_max), and a 3-dim static morphological descriptor [N_finger, D_h, N_body] extracted from the hand's URDF. During training, the executed action is a weighted blend of the policy's predicted joint-action delta and the corresponding expert demonstration action; at inference, only the policy's predicted action is executed. The output is a vector of joint-space action deltas (Δa_t) of dimension D_a matching the hand's degrees of freedom.

## Method

UniBYD introduces three key components. First, a Unified Morphological Representation (UMR) standardizes the observation space across hand morphologies via zero-padding and static morphological embeddings, enabling a single policy network to handle 2-, 3-, and 5-fingered hands. Second, a dynamic PPO with a three-stage reward annealing curriculum transitions from dense multi-component imitation rewards (wrist pose, fingertip contact, joint positions, object pose) to a sparse goal reward, with the transition governed by a sliding-window success rate and a competence threshold to prevent premature decay. Third, a Hybrid Markov-based Shadow Engine blends the policy action with the expert action using a linearly decaying weight β_t (from 1 to 0 over T_decay epochs), effectively converting early training into pointwise imitation before the policy must handle the full Markov process; a PD controller simultaneously applies a decaying support force to the object to prevent catastrophic drops. The PPO objective further incorporates a decaying entropy bonus for sustained exploration and a differentiable soft boundary loss to keep the action mean within physically feasible limits without disrupting gradient flow.

## Results

On the UniManip benchmark, UniBYD achieves a 44.08% average success rate improvement over the SOTA baseline (ManipTrans). Specifically, UniBYD reaches 85.67% SR on 5-finger unimanual tasks (vs. 26.44% for ManipTrans), 57.67% on 5-finger bimanual tasks (vs. 28.75% ManipTrans, 25.33% DexMachina*), and 78.13% / 71.81% on 2-finger / 3-finger tasks where no prior SOTA method applies. Manipulation precision also improves substantially: PE drops to 0.72 cm and OE to 13.44° on bimanual tasks, and the Adaptation Score (AS) reaches 8.16–9.29, far exceeding the best baseline (5.88). On the GRAB dataset, UniBYD achieves 98.00% SR and 9.43 AS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniBYD decisively outperforms ManipTrans [13] on every metric where both methods are evaluated: on 5-finger unimanual tasks, UniBYD achieves 85.67% SR versus ManipTrans's 26.44% (a 59.23-point margin), and on 5-finger bimanual tasks, 57.67% vs. 28.75% (28.92 points). Manipulation precision gaps are equally large—PE is reduced by 60.10% and OE by 51.08% relative to ManipTrans on bimanual tasks. Against DexMachina* [22] (reimplemented for fair comparison), UniBYD wins on all four bimanual metrics: SR 57.67% vs. 25.33%, PE 0.72 vs. 1.66 cm, OE 13.44° vs. 14.13°, and AS 8.16 vs. 4.81. The classical retargeting baseline is far behind on every hand type (SR ≤ 12.27%), confirming that kinematic-only mapping cannot close the embodiment gap. Critically, UniBYD is the only method that supports all four hand morphologies (2-, 3-, and 5-finger, unimanual and bimanual), whereas ManipTrans and DexMachina* are limited to 5-fingered hands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PPO-based reinforcement learning framework for dexterous manipulation by introducing a systematic transition from imitation to autonomous exploration. It departs from imitation-centric methods (ManipTrans, DexMV) that rigidly reproduce human joint trajectories, and from goal-centric methods (DexMachina) that discard human demonstrations entirely, by proposing a reward-annealing curriculum that starts from expert-guided imitation and gradually shifts to morphology-adaptive exploration. The Unified Morphological Representation (UMR) is a novel contribution that standardizes the state-action space across 2-, 3-, and 5-fingered hands, addressing a gap the authors identify as largely unexplored in prior cross-embodiment work. The hybrid Markov-based shadow engine, which blends policy and expert actions with a decaying weight, is a practical departure from standard MDP formulations to combat early-training state drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniBYD provides the first unified framework and benchmark (UniManip) for evaluating cross-embodiment manipulation from human demonstrations across diverse hand morphologies, filling a clear gap where prior SOTA methods only supported 5-fingered anthropomorphic hands. The demonstrated ability to discover morphology-specific strategies (e.g., using two fingers instead of three for a mug handle) is conceptually significant, suggesting that RL can genuinely transcend human priors rather than merely retarget them. However, all quantitative results are from Isaac Gym simulation, real-world transfer shows a notable success-rate drop (52–70%), and the novel Adaptation Score metric relying on LLMs plus human raters has not yet been validated as a reliable evaluation standard. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-horizon or multi-object tasks, and whether the 4096-parallel-environment training requirement limits practical adoption.
