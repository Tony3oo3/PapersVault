## Problem

Determining which image pairs share overlapping visible surfaces (co-visibility) is critical for robust SfM and SLAM, yet existing learned matchers and VLMs degrade sharply under minimal spatial overlap. While geometry-grounded foundation models like VGGT exhibit emergent 3D reasoning, it remains unclear whether their internal representations encode a directly usable co-visibility signal and how to extract it without modifying the backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262747/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of RGB views from the same scene (no depth, intrinsics, or pose required) and outputs a pairwise co-visibility probability in [0,1] for every view pair, forming a symmetric adjacency matrix that defines a scene-level visibility graph. It operates in two unified modes: pairwise (one image pair per sample) and multiview (an entire scene with S>2 views and multiple labeled pairs), where pairwise is a special case with S=2. The pairwise probabilities are well-calibrated (ECE=0.030), enabling direct use as continuous edge weights for downstream graph filtering without post-hoc threshold correction.

## Method

Co-VGGT freezes the VGGT backbone and trains only a lightweight (~7.5M parameter) layer-wise Mixture-of-Experts head. Layer-wise patch-token features are projected, summarized via cross-attention with learned queries into per-view embeddings, then combined into symmetric pair features [e_i, e_j, |e_i−e_j|, e_i⊙e_j]. Each VGGT layer acts as an expert producing a logit, while a gating network adaptively weights experts per input pair, and the final probability is a softmax-weighted sum. The key empirical finding is that co-visibility reasoning is concentrated in late VGGT layers: L17 serves as a consistent 'negative anchor' that confidently rejects non-co-visible pairs, while positive pairs route to a broader set of late layers (L15 in multiview, L18–L23 in pairwise), revealing a hierarchical structure analogous to LLM layer specialization. Training uses binary cross-entropy on the aggregated logit, and the zero-shot variant (cosine similarity on frozen features, no trainable parameters) already achieves competitive performance, confirming the signal is latent in VGGT's representations.

## Results

On the Co-VisiON benchmark, Co-VGGT achieves 0.85/0.78 Graph IoU*/AUC on Gibson and 0.84/0.78 on HM3D in the pairwise setting, and 0.74/0.73 on Gibson and 0.76/0.74 on HM3D in the multiview setting. In the multiview Gibson setting, Co-VGGT surpasses the human annotation baseline (0.72/0.72). Pairwise predictions are well-calibrated with ECE=0.030, enabling direct use as edge weights in visibility graphs without post-hoc correction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Co-VGGT outperforms all 14 baselines on both Gibson and HM3D across both pairwise and multiview settings. The closest competitor is GPT-4o (0.58/0.58 IoU*/AUC on Gibson pairwise), which Co-VGGT surpasses by 27 percentage points in IoU*; the dedicated learned baseline Covis (0.56/0.54) trails by 29 points. Reconstruction-based methods (DUSt3R at 0.54/0.54, MV-DUSt3R+ at 0.56/0.56 multiview) and feature matchers (SuperGlue at 0.47/0.11, SIFT+RANSAC at 0.35/0.05) are substantially weaker, with SuperGlue and SIFT+RANSAC showing near-random AUC scores indicating they cannot reliably distinguish co-visible from non-co-visible pairs. VLM baselines (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Qwen2.5-VL 72B, SpatialRGPT) cluster between 0.37–0.58 IoU* and degrade sharply under low-overlap conditions (e.g., GPT-4o drops to 0.34 on hard edge-level pairs vs. Co-VGGT's 0.84). Notably, even the zero-shot variant of Co-VGGT (0.50/0.31) already matches or exceeds several supervised baselines, confirming that VGGT's frozen representations carry a strong intrinsic co-visibility signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VGGT, a geometry-grounded transformer trained for 3D reconstruction without explicit co-visibility supervision, and repurposes its frozen internal representations as a feature extractor for a new downstream task. It departs from prior co-visibility predictors (Covis, DUSt3R-based pipelines) that either train dedicated architectures or infer overlap implicitly through reconstruction, and from VLM prompting approaches (GPT-4o, Gemini) that treat co-visibility as a language-grounded reasoning problem. The methodological lineage draws on LLM interpretability work on layer specialization and probing, transplanted here to a geometric foundation model, with the MoE head treating each VGGT layer as a specialized expert whose contribution is adaptively weighted per image pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of L17 as a consistent 'negative anchor' that rejects non-co-visible pairs across all evaluation settings provides the first task-grounded evidence of hierarchical layer specialization in a geometry-grounded model, mirroring patterns known from LLMs. The well-calibrated pairwise outputs (ECE=0.030) enable direct integration as edge weights in SfM and SLAM visibility graphs without post-hoc correction, offering a practical auditing signal for detecting reconstruction failures. Key open questions include whether the binary formulation can be extended to continuous overlap estimation, whether the multiview calibration gap (ECE=0.074) can be closed with global graph-consistency mechanisms, and whether the indoor-only evaluation (Gibson, HM3D) generalizes to outdoor or real-world embodied settings. The layer-level interpretation remains correlational, leaving the mechanistic 'why' of geometric abstraction at specific depths unresolved.
