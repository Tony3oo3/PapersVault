## Problem

Existing perceptual video codecs lack native support for variable bitrate and progressive delivery, and their generative modules (particularly diffusion-based denoisers) are decoupled from entropy coding, so their spatio-temporal priors do not directly reduce transmitted bits. Additionally, diffusion-based decoding requires iterative denoising steps that increase latency and hinder real-time streaming. The paper asks whether a visual autoregressive (VAR) model can simultaneously serve as an entropy context model and a detail generator within a single progressive video codec.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video clip (720p, 81 frames) and produces a progressive bitstream composed of hierarchical multi-scale token maps encoded via arithmetic coding. The encoder transmits all intra-frame scales plus the first k inter-frame scales (k ≤ K), where choosing different k values yields different bitrates without retraining. At the decoder, transmitted scales are losslessly recovered and the remaining K−k high-frequency inter scales are synthesized by selecting the most probable tokens from the autoregressive model, after which multi-scale summation and a VAE decoder reconstruct the video.

## Method

ProGVC encodes video through a causal VAE into continuous latents, then discretizes them into K hierarchical token maps via multi-scale residual quantization with Binary Spherical Quantization (BSQ). A Transformer-based multi-scale autoregressive context model predicts each scale conditioned on all coarser scales (and, for inter scales, on intra scales), using a sparse attention mask that lets each scale attend only to itself and its immediately preceding scale plus the largest intra scale, balancing compression efficiency against computational cost. The predicted token distributions drive arithmetic coding for the transmitted scales (lossless entropy coding) and simultaneously serve as the generative distribution from which discarded high-frequency inter scales are sampled at the decoder. This dual use of a single autoregressive model unifies progressive scalability, efficient entropy coding, and perceptual detail synthesis in one pipeline, eliminating the need for separate diffusion denoisers or multiple trained models at different rates.

## Results

On the Xiph dataset, ProGVC achieves the best DISTS BD-rate of −61.5% and NIQE BD-rate of −62.2% relative to the VTM-17.0 anchor, substantially outperforming all fidelity-oriented neural codecs (DCVC-FM, SEVC, DCVC-B) and the perceptual GAN-based PLVC. Similar gains are observed on HEVC Class B (DISTS −47.7%, NIQE −52.5%) and MCL-JCV (DISTS −46.2%, NIQE −65.5%). On LPIPS, ProGVC is competitive (3.5% BD-rate on Xiph) but trails PLVC (−83.7%), while on PSNR it is expectedly the weakest, reflecting its perceptual-optimization objective. For temporal consistency (t-LPIPS on Xiph), ProGVC achieves the best BD-rate of −20.76%, outperforming all baselines including PLVC (+709.87%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional VVC anchor (VTM-17.0), ProGVC delivers large perceptual gains: −61.5% BD-rate on DISTS and −62.2% on NIQE (Xiph), while sacrificing PSNR fidelity as expected for a perceptual codec. Among fidelity-oriented neural codecs (DCVC-FM, SEVC, DCVC-B), ProGVC consistently wins on DISTS and NIQE by 20–130 percentage points in BD-rate, and is competitive on LPIPS (within ~20 BD-rate points). Against the perceptual GAN-based PLVC, ProGVC achieves better DISTS (−61.5% vs −40.3% on Xiph) and NIQE (−62.2% vs +169.9% on Xiph), but PLVC retains an advantage on LPIPS (−83.7% vs +3.5% on Xiph; −45.1% vs +3.3% on MCL-JCV), suggesting PLVC preserves local feature-level correspondence more faithfully. On temporal consistency (t-LPIPS), ProGVC is the clear winner at −20.76% BD-rate, while PLVC degrades severely (+709.87%). In terms of complexity, ProGVC has the lowest encoding time (0.56 s/frame) and decoding time (1.53 s/frame) among all compared neural codecs, well below the diffusion-based DiffVC (0.82 s enc / 4.54 s dec).

## Relation to Prior Work

ProGVC repurposes the Visual Auto-Regressive (VAR) paradigm—originally developed for image generation via next-scale prediction [32] and extended to video by InfinityStar [15]—as the backbone of a perceptual video codec, a direction the authors identify as previously unexplored. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based perceptual codecs (e.g., DiffVC [19]) by replacing iterative denoising with a single-pass autoregressive context model that simultaneously serves as the entropy coder and the generative sampler for discarded high-frequency scales. The multi-scale residual quantization with Binary Spherical Quantization and the causal video VAE are inherited directly from InfinityStar, while the sparse attention mask is a novel design that balances the full-history attention of VAR against the restricted receptive field of InfinityStar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProGVC is the first perceptual video codec to natively support progressive scalability and variable-bitrate control through hierarchical scale truncation, addressing a practical gap that has limited the deployment of generative codecs in streaming scenarios. Its lower decoding latency relative to diffusion-based alternatives makes it more suitable for real-time or low-delay applications. However, the current system is constrained to 720p and 81-frame clips, and rate adaptation remains coarse-grained (entire scales are dropped rather than individual tokens), leaving open questions about scaling to higher resolutions, longer sequences, and finer-grained bitrate control as AR video backbones mature.
