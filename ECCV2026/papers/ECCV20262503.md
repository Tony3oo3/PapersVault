## Problem

Existing object-concealment attacks on vision-language models rely on suppressing or masking region-specific visual features, which creates representational gaps that trigger compensatory hallucination in the language decoder. This paper identifies the root cause as semantic discontinuity between the concealed region and its surrounding context, rather than object absence itself, and asks whether concealment can be achieved without introducing such discontinuities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262503/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a region-of-interest bounding box (from object detectors, annotations, or user specification) and produces a single adversarial image under an ℓ∞ pixel perturbation budget (ε=0.2). Optimization is performed in pixel space but targets internal vision-encoder representations at selected transformer layers, so the output image remains visually close to the input (high SSIM, low LPIPS) while the target object is semantically concealed from the VLM's captioning and VQA outputs.

## Method

Background-Consistent Re-encoding (BCR) re-encodes ROI visual tokens so they become statistically and semantically indistinguishable from surrounding background tokens, rather than suppressing or zeroing them. The objective jointly enforces three per-layer losses: (i) statistical alignment matching first- and second-order moments of ROI and background features, (ii) a dictionary projection loss that reconstructs each ROI token as a soft convex combination of background tokens via scaled dot-product attention, and (iii) a background preservation loss that penalizes deviation of non-ROI features from their clean counterparts. These losses are summed across multiple late vision-transformer layers, with a total-variation regularizer encouraging spatially smooth pixel perturbations. The key distinction from suppression-based attacks is that BCR preserves token structure and attention flow, eliminating the representational void that would otherwise prompt the language decoder to hallucinate missing content.

## Results

On ImageNet with Instruct-BLIP, BCR achieves 92% concealment success while reducing grounded hallucination to 0.19 (vs. 0.43 for VIP and 0.62 for PRM) and raising global preservation to 0.81 (vs. 0.57 for VIP). On the more complex COCO scenes, BCR reaches 89% concealment with a grounded hallucination rate of 0.23, compared to 0.58 for VIP. Across all three VLMs (Instruct-BLIP, BLIP2-T5, LLaVA), BCR consistently achieves the lowest grounded hallucination (0.14–0.23) and highest global preservation (0.77–0.86) while maintaining concealment success above 89%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VIP [11] (attention/value suppression), BCR matches concealment success (92% vs. 93% on ImageNet/Instruct-BLIP) while cutting grounded hallucination by more than half (0.19 vs. 0.43) and nearly doubling global preservation (0.81 vs. 0.57). On COCO, the gap widens: BCR achieves 89% concealment with 0.23 hallucination versus VIP's 80% concealment and 0.58 hallucination. Against PRM [5] (patch representation misalignment), BCR dominates on every metric: higher concealment (0.92 vs. 0.66), substantially higher preservation (0.81 vs. 0.38), and lower hallucination (0.19 vs. 0.62) on ImageNet/Instruct-BLIP. The paper also reports that BCR achieves superior perceptual fidelity (SSIM 0.96, LPIPS 0.02) compared to VIP (SSIM 0.71, LPIPS 0.34), indicating that the pixel-level perturbations remain visually unobtrusive. Overall, BCR wins on all four evaluation metrics across all three VLMs and both datasets, with the largest relative gains in grounded hallucination reduction (up to 3× lower than VIP) and global semantic preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in VLM object concealment—suppression-based methods like VIP (attention/value blocking) and PRM (patch representation misalignment)—by reframing concealment as a feature re-encoding problem rather than an information-removal problem. Conceptually, it repurposes statistical moment matching and dictionary-based manifold projection (ideas familiar from domain adaptation and feature alignment literature) into an adversarial pixel-space optimization objective. The paper does not build on a single prior method but carves new ground by arguing that the representational 'gap' created by suppression, not the object's absence itself, is the proximate cause of hallucination, and then designing a loss that explicitly closes that gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuity-hallucination link, if it generalizes, provides a diagnostic principle for designing future concealment or privacy-preserving attacks on multimodal models: preserve feature-space smoothness rather than zeroing activations. The grounded hallucination evaluation protocol (caption object extraction + GLIP verification) is a methodological contribution that addresses a real weakness in prior VLM attack evaluations. However, the study is limited to three VLMs, a white-box setting, single-object ROIs, and two datasets; whether the continuity principle holds for multi-object concealment, black-box transfer, or newer architectures (e.g., GPT-4V-class models) remains open. The abstract's '3× reduction' claim is also slightly overstated relative to the reported numbers (~2.3× on ImageNet/Instruct-BLIP).
