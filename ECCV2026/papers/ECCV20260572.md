## Problem

Existing Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT) methods rely on single-agent imitation learning from expert demonstrations, which limits generalization under distribution shift and active interference, and the field lacks both a systematic RL training paradigm and benchmarks that evaluate tracking under adversarial conditions. CoMaTrack addresses this by reframing EVT training as a competitive multi-agent game inspired by Red Queen co-evolution, where adaptive opponents continuously escalate task difficulty to produce an automatic curriculum that static data or single-agent exploration cannot replicate.

## Input / Output

The model consumes four egocentric RGB views (front, rear, left, right) at the current timestep plus a sliding-window sequence of historical front-view frames, fused with a natural-language instruction describing the target's visual characteristics. Visual tokens are processed at two granularities—fine-grained for the current observation (precise target grounding) and coarse-grained for temporal context—then a <Nav> special token routes the LLM output to either autoregressive text decoding (recognition) or a flow-matching head that regresses a 5-step waypoint sequence (x, y, θ) in the agent's local frame. An episode succeeds only if the agent maintains 1–3 m following distance, keeps the target in view, and avoids collisions throughout.

## Method

CoMaTrack builds an end-to-end VLA on Qwen2.5VL-3B with a flow-matching action head, trained in two phases: one epoch of multi-task SFT (tracking, navigation, VQA) followed by one epoch of GRPO-based RL with LoRA adapters on the LLM. The core novelty is the multi-agent RL stage, where a tracker and an opponent—both using the identical VLA architecture—co-evolve under asymmetric rewards: the opponent is incentivized to track at a closer distance (d_opt = 1.25 m) to create direct contention, while the tracker retains the standard safe-following objective (d_opt = 2.25 m) plus an opponent-aware collision-avoidance term. GRPO optimizes each agent's policy with group-relative advantages, and a KL anchor to the frozen SFT reference bounds per-update drift to mitigate non-stationarity from the co-evolving opponent. The competitive interaction acts as an automatically escalating curriculum: any improvement by one agent becomes a harder challenge for the other, driving the emergence of anticipatory, interference-resilient tracking behaviors that static demonstrations or single-agent exploration fail to produce.

## Results

On EVT-Bench, CoMaTrack (a 3B VLM) achieves 92.1% SR / 90.3% TR / 0.9% CR on STT, 74.2% SR / 80.5% TR / 2.1% CR on DT, and 57.5% SR / 73.4% TR / 12.0% CR on AT, surpassing the strongest prior 7B baseline TrackVLA++ on every metric. On the newly introduced CoMaTrack-Bench (competitive adversarial tracking), CoMaTrack reaches 85.0% SR and 5.5% CR, more than doubling the 42.4% SR of the only available baseline (Uni-Navid). The ablation confirms that multi-agent RL contributes +2.6 SR over SFT-only and +2.6 SR over single-agent RL on STT, with the largest gain in collision reduction (3.1% → 0.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMaTrack outperforms all eight baselines on EVT-Bench across every reported metric. Against the strongest prior method TrackVLA++ (7B), it gains +1.2 SR on STT (92.1 vs 90.9), +0.2 SR on DT (74.2 vs 74.0), and +1.6 SR on AT (57.5 vs 55.9), while also improving TR by 7.6, 6.8, and 9.6 points respectively and reducing CR on all three tasks. The gap over TrackVLA (7B) is larger: +7.0 SR on STT and +7.3 SR on AT. On the competitive CoMaTrack-Bench, CoMaTrack achieves 85.0% SR versus 42.4% for Uni-Navid (the only baseline with public weights), a 42.6-point margin, with CR dropping from 23.8% to 5.5%. Notably, CoMaTrack is built on a 3B backbone (Qwen2.5VL-3B) while all competing VLA baselines (TrackVLA, TrackVLA++, Uni-NaVid, VLingNav, NavFoM) use 7B models, making the performance gains attributable to the multi-agent competitive RL training paradigm rather than model scale. The older non-VLA baselines (IBVS, PoliFormer, EVT) remain far behind, with SR below 50% on STT, confirming that the VLA + RL paradigm is essential for competitive tracking.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMaTrack adapts the asymmetric dueling principle from AD-VAT (Zhong et al., ICLR 2019)—originally built on CNN/A3C backbones with discrete actions and no language conditioning—to modern VLA architectures (Qwen2.5VL-3B with flow-matching action heads and continuous waypoint prediction). For optimization, it repurposes GRPO from the LLM-reasoning literature (DeepSeekMath) as the policy-update mechanism, combined with LoRA adapters to keep the VLM backbone stable during RL. The paper departs from the dominant single-agent imitation-learning paradigm (TrackVLA, TrackVLA++, Uni-NaVid) by replacing static expert trajectories with a co-evolving adversarial training loop, and introduces CoMaTrack-Bench as the first competitive EVT evaluation protocol, filling a gap left by EVT-Bench and TPT-Bench which only test passive or stochastic disturbances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The competitive multi-agent RL paradigm offers a principled alternative to ever-larger demonstration datasets for EVT, and the finding that a 3B VLM trained this way matches or exceeds 7B single-agent IL baselines suggests that training dynamics, not parameter scale, may be the key lever for robust tracking. The CoMaTrack-Bench benchmark is practically useful for evaluating interference-resilient tracking policies under active adversarial conditions. However, the validation is confined to EVT; the paper acknowledges that opponent strategies are bounded by simulated priors, multi-agent training is computationally costly and non-stationary, and extension to broader VLN tasks (instruction following, object navigation) remains untested. Open questions include whether the competitive-curriculum idea transfers to other embodied tasks, how to generate more diverse and realistic opponents, and whether the gains hold under truly out-of-distribution real-world conditions beyond the Unitree GO2 demo.
