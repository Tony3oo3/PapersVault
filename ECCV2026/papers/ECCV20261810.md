## Problem

GUI agents powered by vision-language models frequently fail in long-horizon tasks because they lack step-level verification of whether an executed action produced its intended visual effect. Existing process reward models verify actions through textual reasoning, tool calls, or structured checklists, which is fundamentally misaligned with the visual nature of GUI state changes where outcomes manifest as pixel-level transformations such as button highlights, dialog appearances, and page scrolls.

## Input / Output

The critic receives a pair of RGB screenshots (pre-action and post-action), the executed action string (e.g., CLICK(x,y)), and the task instruction. It produces three structured predictions with distinct activation functions: a success probability in [0,1] (sigmoid), a task progress score in [-1,1] (tanh), and a 5-class error type (success, no-op, wrong-target, page-error, timeout) via softmax, alongside an interpretable change-region attention map over the ViT patch grid. When the success score falls below a threshold γ=0.5, the signal can trigger downstream recovery hooks such as retry or backtracking.

## Method

VisCritic employs a shared-weight Siamese ViT (InternViT-6B with LoRA adapters) to encode both screenshots into patch-level features, computes per-patch difference vectors, and applies a learnable change-magnitude-weighted softmax attention to produce an attended difference vector that focuses on semantically meaningful UI changes while suppressing rendering noise and animation artifacts. An Action-Aware Critic Head fuses this visual difference with action and task text (encoded by Qwen2.5-7B) through cross-attention and spatial self-attention, feeding into three parallel MLP heads for the multi-task outputs. Training proceeds in two phases: contrastive pre-training with InfoNCE loss (plus an auxiliary KL localization loss when action-region masks are available) to learn discriminative difference representations, followed by end-to-end multi-task fine-tuning with weighted BCE, MSE, and CE losses. Critic-training data is constructed without human labels by drawing positive samples from successful trajectories and generating negatives through four perturbation strategies: action mismatch, state mismatch, no-op pairing (SSIM>0.98), and failed-trajectory sampling after estimated divergence points.

## Results

Across five benchmarks (Mind2Web, AndroidWorld, OSWorld, WebArena, AITW), VisCritic improves the interactive average success rate of every base agent tested: SeeClick from 16.8% to 20.7%, ShowUI from 22.8% to 27.4%, CogAgent from 20.7% to 23.6%, and Qwen2.5-VL Agent from 27.2% to 32.6%. On the strongest base agent (Qwen2.5-VL), VisCritic achieves 54.1% step SR on Mind2Web, 29.8% task SR on AndroidWorld, 17.1% on OSWorld, and 29.4% on WebArena. In intrinsic critic quality (action verification F1 on AndroidWorld), VisCritic reaches 85.2% F1, substantially exceeding the best text-based baseline (GUI-PRA at 70.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisCritic outperforms all four text-based baselines on the majority of benchmark-agent combinations. Against GUI-PRA (text-based PRM), VisCritic gains +3.4 to +4.5 points on interactive benchmarks with Qwen2.5-VL (e.g., 54.1% vs 50.1% on Mind2Web, 29.4% vs 24.9% on WebArena) and +2.0 to +2.4 points with SeeClick. Against GuidNav (text PRM with search), VisCritic leads by +2.9 to +3.3 points on Qwen2.5-VL across Mind2Web, AndroidWorld, and WebArena. Against GUI-Critic-R1 (pre-execution critic), VisCritic wins on Mind2Web (+2.3), AndroidWorld (+1.6), and WebArena (+2.9) with Qwen2.5-VL, but narrowly loses on OSWorld (17.1% vs 17.4%), which the authors attribute to text-heavy desktop environments where textual verification has a natural advantage. In the controlled critic-quality comparison (Table 2), VisCritic achieves 85.2% F1 for action success prediction versus 70.4% for GUI-PRA and 69.5% for BacktrackAgent, a gap of 14.8 and 15.7 F1 points respectively, confirming that visual state comparison provides a substantially more reliable verification signal than text-mediated reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisCritic repurposes the Siamese change-detection architecture (well-studied in remote sensing and video surveillance) for a novel context: verifying GUI agent actions by comparing pre- and post-action screenshots in learned semantic feature space. It departs from the dominant line of GUI process reward models (GUI-PRA, Web-Shepherd, GUI-Shepherd, GuidNav) and error-detection methods (BacktrackAgent, GUI-CriticR1), which all mediate verification through textual reasoning, tool calls, or structured checklists. The paper positions visual state comparison as a complementary signal to these textual PRMs rather than a replacement, and contrasts itself with GUI world models (MobileDreamer, CUWM) that predict future states rather than verify actual outcomes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (no agent retraining, 66 ms per-step overhead) makes visual step-level verification practically deployable across heterogeneous GUI agents, addressing a widely acknowledged fragility in long-horizon task execution. The annotation-free data construction pipeline lowers the barrier to training domain-specific critics. Key open questions remain: recovery policies are architecturally compatible but not quantitatively evaluated in isolation; task-irrelevant visual dynamics (animations, ads) and fine-grained text changes (e.g., counter increments) still degrade accuracy; and the heuristic weak-label pipeline (divergence-point estimation) may inject label noise that limits ceiling performance.
