## Problem

Unified multimodal models face a fundamental granularity gap: visual understanding requires high-level semantic abstractions while image generation demands fine-grained pixel-level fidelity. Existing unified tokenizers either entangle both objectives on the same features (causing optimization conflict) or fully decouple them into separate encoders or codebooks (losing intrinsic consistency between visual structure and semantics). The paper asks whether a single latent space can simultaneously support both tasks without the interference of entanglement or the isolation of rigid decoupling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260712/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes 256×256 or 384×384 RGB images and encodes them into a 16×16×16 (or 27×27×16) grid of discrete residual codes, each drawn from a 32,768-entry codebook per depth level. For understanding, the cumulative semantic feature (sum of all 16 residual stages) is decoded and projected into the Qwen-2.5-7B LLM's embedding space alongside text tokens. For generation, the LLM autoregressively predicts only the first 4 pixel-level residual codes per spatial position via a depth RQ-Transformer head, which are then decoded into an image. Text prompts condition both understanding and generation tasks.

## Method

EvoTok represents an image as a residual evolution trajectory within a single shared latent space using cascaded residual vector quantization (16 stages, 32,768 entries each) on top of a SigLIP2-large-patch16-256 encoder. The key design is a pixel-to-semantic progression: the first L_pix=4 residual stages capture spatial structure and perceptual details for reconstruction, while the full L_sem=16-stage cumulative sum converges to high-level semantics aligned with SigLIP2 embeddings. Training jointly optimizes a pixel decoder (reconstruction + perceptual + adversarial losses), a semantic decoder (cosine similarity to SigLIP2 features), and standard VQ codebook losses, enforcing both task-specific fidelity and cross-task consistency within one trajectory. For understanding, semantic features pass through an MLP projector into the LLM; for generation, a depth RQ-Transformer head autoregressively predicts pixel residual codes with classifier-free guidance at inference. Despite training on only 13M images, EvoTok achieves rFID 0.43/0.25 at 256/384 resolution and leads on 8 of 9 understanding benchmarks plus GenEval, GenAI-Bench, and DPG generation benchmarks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K reconstruction, EvoTok achieves rFID of 0.43 at 256×256 and 0.25 at 384×384, trained on only 13M images versus billion-scale data used by prior unified tokenizers. For visual understanding, EvoTok (256 res.) secures the best score on 7 of 9 benchmarks among discrete unified methods, including SEEDBench (71.8), AI2D (76.2), MMMU (45.9), and MME (1895.1). For image generation, EvoTok achieves the highest DPG score (86.36), GenEval overall (0.75), and GenAI-Bench Basic (0.87), surpassing both unified autoregressive baselines and specialized diffusion models like DALL-E 3 (DPG 83.50) and SDXL (DPG 74.65).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoTok outperforms all discrete unified baselines on the majority of understanding benchmarks: it leads TokenFlow-B (MME 1895.1 vs. 1660.4), UniTok (MME 1895.1 vs. 1448.0), VILA-U (MME 1895.1 vs. 1336.2), and DualToken (MME 1895.1 vs. 1502.7) by large margins. On generation, EvoTok surpasses DALL-E 3 (DPG 86.36 vs. 83.50; GenEval 0.75 vs. 0.67) and SDXL (DPG 86.36 vs. 74.65; GenEval 0.75 vs. 0.55), as well as unified AR models like EMU3 (DPG 86.36 vs. 80.60) and TokenFlow-XL (DPG 86.36 vs. 73.38). EvoTok also beats SemHiTok (DPG 86.36 vs. 83.59) and UniTok (DPG 86.36 vs. 83.45) on generation. The only notable exception is AToken-So/D, which reports higher AI2D (81.2 vs. 76.2) and RealWorldQA (68.8 vs. 62.0) at 1536 resolution, though it does not report MME or generation scores for a full comparison. At 384 resolution, EvoTok further extends its lead, reaching AI2D 81.4 and MM-Vet 56.8, establishing new state-of-the-art among discrete unified methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoTok builds directly on RQ-VAE's cascaded residual quantization as its core tokenization mechanism, adopts VQGAN's adversarial and perceptual training losses for pixel reconstruction, and follows the LLaVA paradigm (encoder → projector → LLM) for the understanding branch. It departs from two established lines of unified tokenizers: entangled designs (ViLA-U, UniTok) that optimize both semantic alignment and pixel reconstruction on the same features, and decoupled designs (TokenFlow, DualToken, SemHiTok) that separate pixel and semantic representations into different branches or layers. The key conceptual departure is the 'pixel-to-semantic evolution' principle: rather than entangling or rigidly separating the two representations, EvoTok organizes them along a single residual trajectory where early stages capture pixel structure and deeper stages progressively converge to SigLIP2-aligned semantics, maintaining both decoupling and cross-task consistency within one shared latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The residual evolution paradigm offers a principled, architecturally simple solution to the long-standing tension between semantic abstraction and pixel fidelity in unified tokenizers, and the ablation cleanly demonstrates that the direction of evolution (pixel→semantic vs. semantic→pixel) materially affects downstream performance. Achieving competitive rFID (0.43 at 256, 0.25 at 384) and strong generation scores (GenEval 0.75, DPG 86.36) with only 13M training images—versus billion-scale corpora used by prior unified tokenizers—suggests the evolution structure is a more data-efficient inductive bias. Open questions include whether the fixed depth allocation (L_pix=4, L_sem=16) generalizes across resolutions and domains, whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, and whether the SigLIP2 distillation target can be replaced by other semantic anchors without degrading the trajectory.
