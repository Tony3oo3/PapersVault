## Problem

When fusing image and tabular features for multimodal classification, existing methods treat the concatenated feature vector as an unordered set, yet sequence-sensitive operators like self-attention are sensitive to column permutation. This paper identifies feature sequencing as a neglected inductive bias, showing that arbitrary column ordering increases inter-feature dispersion and destabilizes training, and frames the problem as a Column Permutation Problem (CPP) that can be solved to produce a coherent cross-modal feature order.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (encoded to 2048-d ResNet-50 features) and tabular attributes (encoded by a Transformer encoder) per sample, concatenating them into a fused multimodal feature vector of dimension m = d_tab + d_vis. The NS-PFS module reorders these m features into a global permutation before the OMT backbone processes them as L = n_tab + n_vis tokens. The output is a single class label predicted from the mean of learnable memory-token states, with an auxiliary head predicting the learned positional order of each token.

## Method

iSyncTab introduces two coupled components. First, NS-PFS clusters features within each modality, computes a synchrony score between every visual-tabular cluster pair as the product of energy coherence and centroid cosine similarity, and solves a bipartite matching via the Hungarian algorithm to pair clusters; within each matched joint cluster, a local CPP is solved to minimize a regime-weighted dispersion objective, and the local orders are concatenated into a global permutation. Second, the OMT backbone is a Linformer-based Transformer with learnable memory tokens prepended to the NS-PFS-ordered token sequence, reducing attention complexity from O(L²) to O(L). Training uses a joint loss combining cross-entropy classification with an order-consistency term that penalizes deviation of predicted token positions from the NS-PFS-induced target ordering, enforcing the learned sequence as a structural constraint during optimization.

## Results

iSyncTab achieves the best test accuracy on all six image-tabular benchmarks: 99.60% (DVM), 86.82% (HAM10000), 85.63% (Deep Lesion), 42.44% (Pokémon), 88.60% (CheXpert), and 88.02% (PetFinder), yielding an average rank of 1.08 ± 0.19 and zero average regret. Under label noise on HAM10000, iSyncTab attains the best mean accuracy (78.41%) across 10–60% noise rates, edging out CUFIT (78.3%). On the RAVDESS audio-video extension, the Linformer-ResNet50 variant reaches 97.56% best test accuracy (95.25 ± 1.34% five-fold mean). In the efficiency-centric protocol on DVM, iSyncTab achieves the highest accuracy while using FLOPs close to lightweight baselines (DAFT) and the lowest peak GPU memory among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iSyncTab outperforms all 16 baselines on every dataset, achieving zero average regret. Against the strongest multimodal baselines, it improves over STiL by +0.33 (DVM), +8.34 (HAM), +4.28 (DLes), +15.12 (Pok), and +0.34 (Pet), while tying on CheXpert (88.60%); over TIP, gains range from +0.33 (DVM) to +16.43 (HAM). Tabular-only methods (LGBM, CatBoost, TabM, SCARF, SAINT, TabSeq) and image-only methods (ResNet-50, ViT, SimCLR, BYOL) trail by 10–50+ points on most datasets, with ViT narrowing the gap only on image-dominant DVM (88.00% vs. 99.60%). Under label noise on HAM10000, iSyncTab edges out CUFIT (78.41% vs. 78.3% mean) and substantially outperforms Co-teaching (75.5%), JoCor (75.4%), CoDis (75.5%), and Rein (60.8%). In the efficiency-centric DVM protocol, iSyncTab matches or exceeds the accuracy of heavier models (STiL, TIP, MIITA) while using FLOPs comparable to lightweight DAFT and the lowest peak GPU memory, confirming that gains stem from structured sequencing rather than model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

iSyncTab extends the authors' prior iStructTab [41], which used graph-enhanced descriptor sequencing to reduce image-tabular feature dispersion, by replacing the graph-based ordering with a neuroscience-inspired energy-phase synchrony criterion and a Hungarian bipartite matching between visual and tabular feature clusters. The combinatorial formulation draws on the Column Permutation Problem literature [8, 63, 64] and the broader tabular-sequencing line (TabSeq [44], Mambular [84]) that demonstrates column order affects optimization. Unlike concurrent multimodal image-tabular methods (STiL, TIP, MMCL, MIITA, CHARMS) that treat fused embeddings as unordered vectors and focus on cross-modal attention or contrastive pretraining, iSyncTab injects an explicit permutation inductive bias before sequence-sensitive modeling, and pairs it with a Linformer-based memory-augmented Transformer (OMT) for linear-complexity fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides evidence that feature sequencing is a meaningful inductive bias for multimodal image-tabular classification, with ablations showing that the *specific* synchrony-derived ordering substantially outperforms both no sequencing and random shuffling. The neuro-inspired framing (Communication Through Coherence, regime-aware scaling) offers biological interpretability that could guide future cross-modal alignment design beyond ad-hoc attention. Open questions include whether the one-shot Hungarian matching can be made end-to-end differentiable, whether task-adaptive clustering improves over fixed k, and whether the approach generalizes to structured prediction or higher-dimensional modality combinations beyond the preliminary RAVDESS audio-video experiment.
