## Problem

Physics-based human-scene interaction (HSI) imitation learning has been largely restricted to simplified scenes with single objects and a handful of predefined tasks, leaving complex 3D environments unexplored. The paper identifies a fundamental feasibility-faithfulness trade-off: relaxing the tracking constraint improves collision avoidance but degrades motion fidelity, while strict tracking causes premature failures in cluttered scenes. This is the first work to systematically address HSI imitation under dense, realistic scene geometry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 121-frame MoCap reference sequences, full 3D scene meshes for collision, and a 20×20 height-map grid (8 cm spacing, centered on the character and aligned with its heading) as scene perception. The student policy is a 4-layer Transformer encoder (4 heads, hidden 512) that outputs whole-body joint actions at 30 Hz within a 60 Hz Isaac Gym simulation, using a mean SMPL body shape. The final output is a single unified policy that produces physically simulated HSI motion sequences (joint rotations and positions) matching the reference while respecting scene constraints.

## Method

ComplexMimic is a two-stage framework. In Stage I (Dual Flow Strategy), two PPO-trained expert policies are learned: an imitation expert in a scene-free setting with a strict early-termination threshold (τ ≤ 0.25) for motion fidelity, and an interaction expert in the full scene simulator with a relaxed threshold (τ ≥ 0.5) and height-map conditioning for collision-aware adaptation. In Stage II (Difficulty-Aware Distillation), both frozen experts are distilled into a single Transformer student via regime-specialized teacher routing (motions are partitioned into tracking-friendly and interaction-critical groups, each supervised by its best expert) and a joint PPO + KL-divergence distillation loss. Difficulty-Aware Sampling reallocates the training budget using online failure statistics: inter-group softmax sampling favors harder regimes, intra-group prioritization upweights frequently-failing motions, and a learnability filter (EMA of return improvement) suppresses stagnant, effectively infeasible clips to keep mining focused on hard-yet-learnable trajectories.

## Results

On the primary in-domain TRUMANS benchmark, ComplexMimic achieves a success rate of 0.906 and a global MPJPE of 77.84 mm, outperforming the strongest baseline MaskedMimic (Succ 0.872, E_g-mpjpe 120.26 mm) by 3.9% in success rate and 35.3% in tracking error. On the out-of-domain LINGO dataset with unseen scene configurations, the method attains the highest success rate (0.746) while maintaining competitive tracking accuracy. On the real-world GIMO dataset with scanned scene meshes and reconstruction noise, ComplexMimic achieves the best performance across all five metrics, reaching a success rate of 0.579 and E_g-mpjpe of 161.97 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComplexMimic outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three datasets. On TRUMANS, the largest gap is against MaskedMimic, which achieves a high success rate (0.872) but suffers severe tracking degradation (E_g-mpjpe 120.26 mm vs. 77.84 mm for ComplexMimic), indicating that its unified controller cannot simultaneously handle scene collisions and motion fidelity. Against PHC, the closest competitor on tracking accuracy, ComplexMimic improves success rate from 0.853 to 0.906 while reducing E_g-mpjpe from 85.79 to 77.84 mm, demonstrating that the dual-expert decomposition resolves the feasibility-fidelity trade-off that PHC's single MoE policy cannot. On the out-of-domain LINGO dataset, ComplexMimic leads in success rate (0.746 vs. 0.719 for MaskedMimic) and physical realism metrics (E_acc 20.64 vs. 23.65 for PHC), though PHC retains a slight edge in raw tracking error (E_g-mpjpe 120.47 vs. 124.67 mm), suggesting that cross-scene generalization of motion fidelity remains challenging. On the real-world GIMO dataset with reconstruction artifacts, all baselines degrade substantially (Succ drops to 0.365–0.446), while ComplexMimic maintains the highest success rate (0.579) and lowest tracking error, highlighting the robustness of the difficulty-aware distillation to noisy scene geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-based humanoid imitation learning lineage (DeepMimic → AMP → PHC → MaskedMimic) into the human-scene interaction domain, where prior work (MimicBench, TokenHSI, UNIHSI) has largely restricted scene geometry to single objects or a handful of predefined tasks. The key methodological departure is the Dual Flow Strategy, which decouples motion-faithful tracking (strict termination threshold, scene-free) from collision-aware interaction (relaxed threshold, height-map conditioning) into two specialized PPO-trained experts, then unifies them via a difficulty-aware multi-teacher distillation objective. This builds on the MoE paradigm from PHC and the knowledge distillation framework of Hinton et al., but introduces regime-specialized routing and online failure-statistics-driven sampling that are specific to the HSI setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By systematically addressing HSI imitation in dense, complex 3D scenes (TRUMANS, LINGO, GIMO), the paper fills a gap between simplified benchmark settings and realistic deployment scenarios for embodied intelligence and character animation. The empirically revealed feasibility-faithfulness trade-off (controlled by the early-termination threshold τ) provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners tuning imitation policies. Open questions include whether the dual-expert + distillation paradigm scales to multi-human interactions in complex scenes, whether the height-map scene representation suffices for highly occluded or dynamic environments, and how the approach transfers to real hardware with sim-to-real gaps beyond reconstruction noise.
