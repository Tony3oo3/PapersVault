## Problem

Existing cross-view geo-localization methods estimate only the camera center (image-level), which is insufficient for applications requiring the geographic location of individual scene elements. Homography-based registration approaches assume planar geometry and degrade under the large viewpoint changes, parallax, and occlusions inherent to drone-to-satellite pairs. The field also lacks standardized training data and dense per-pixel evaluation protocols for cross-view geo-registration.

## Input / Output

The method takes an uncalibrated perspective drone query image (no intrinsics, no pose, no metadata) and a georeferenced satellite reference image that carries known intrinsics, a 6-DoF pose in the ECEF world frame, and either a DEM (orthorectified case) or metric depth with camera parameters (perspective case). It produces a dense per-pixel latitude-longitude map for the query, a query-to-reference warping function, and optionally a warped RGB overlay composited onto the reference. The query is consumed as raw pixel intensities only; all geometric information must be inferred by the model.

## Method

SkyReg instantiates the MASt3R feed-forward 3D reconstruction backbone (initialized from AerialMegaDepth pretraining) to predict dense point maps for both the query and reference views in two forward passes. From these point maps, it recovers the query intrinsics by solving a focal-length optimization (Eq. 2) and the relative query-to-reference pose via Procrustes alignment (Eq. 3). The warping function is then simply the back-projection of the query point map (already expressed in the reference camera frame) through the reference intrinsics (Eq. 5), and per-pixel GPS coordinates are obtained by interpolating the reference's geodetic lat/lon map at the warped locations. Training uses the DUSt3R confidence-weighted regression loss on ground-truth point maps constructed from LiDAR-derived depth (Urban) or COLMAP SfM/MVS depth (Landmarks/Suburban), with each epoch mixing 20k pairs each from Urban, Landmarks, and standard 3D reconstruction datasets (ARKitScenes, ScanNet++, CO3D) to preserve geometric priors while adapting to the cross-view setting. The key distinction from prior work is that no planar homography is ever estimated; 3D geometry is modeled explicitly, making the warp robust to parallax from tall structures and to occlusions that break 2D correspondence.

## Results

On the pixel-wise geo-registration benchmark (SkyReg-Bench), SkyReg achieves a mean geodetic error of 44.88 m on Urban and 21.34 m on Suburban, reducing error by 88.18 m and 90.76 m respectively over the strongest baseline (RoMa). Recall at 20 m threshold reaches 78.78% (Urban) and 74.94% (Suburban), compared to 25.69% and 60.00% for RoMa. On the median-GPS image-level protocol, SkyReg reports 36.40 m (Urban) and 21.45 m (Suburban) mean geodetic error, outperforming all retrieval and homography baselines by 60+ m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkyReg outperforms all six baselines by large margins on both pixel-wise and image-level metrics. The strongest competitor, RoMa (dense feature matching), achieves 133.06 m pixel-wise GE on Urban and 112.10 m on Suburban, while SkyReg reaches 44.88 m and 21.34 m respectively — a 3× improvement. RoMa Dense performs similarly to RoMa (131.45 m / 111.25 m), confirming that dense correspondence alone without explicit 3D geometry is insufficient. SP+SG (sparse SuperPoint+SuperGlue matching) is the weakest pixel-wise baseline at 209.40 m (Urban) and 223.69 m (Suburban), with recall below 4% at 20 m on Urban. Among retrieval/homography baselines evaluated on the median-GPS protocol, University-1652 (121.17 m Urban) is the best, followed by EarthMatch (163.60 m), TransGeo (160.98 m), and SP+SG (134.09 m); SkyReg's 36.40 m Urban GE exceeds all of them by 85+ m. The gap is especially pronounced on Suburban, where retrieval methods degrade severely (TransGeo: 272.53 m, EarthMatch: 302.34 m) while SkyReg maintains 21.45 m, highlighting the robustness of 3D-aware warping under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkyReg builds directly on the feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm of MASt3R and DUSt3R, repurposing their dense point-map and camera-parameter predictions for a geo-registration objective rather than reconstruction or matching. It departs from the dominant geo-registration literature in remote sensing, which relies on planar homography estimation and 2D feature matching (SuperPoint/SuperGlue, RoMa), by explicitly lifting pixels into 3D and composing geometric mappings through a shared world frame. The paper also extends the geo-localization literature (TransGeo, University-1652, VIGOR) by shifting the target from a single camera-center coordinate to a dense per-pixel lat/lon assignment, a formulation the authors argue is necessary for applications like infrastructure inspection and disaster assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of SkyReg-Bench as the first dense, pixel-wise geo-registration benchmark for drone-satellite pairs fills a clear gap: prior evaluation was limited to sparse correspondences or image-level GPS error, making it impossible to assess whether a method truly aligns scene content. The large performance gap over 2D baselines (roughly 3× on Urban, 5× on Suburban) underscores that planar assumptions break down under the parallax and viewpoint changes inherent to drone-satellite pairs. Key limitations temper the impact: the satellite references are rendered in Google Earth rather than real orthorectified imagery, the 3D backbone (MASt3R) was not originally trained for the extreme satellite-drone modality gap, and the benchmark is relatively small (13k Urban + 950 Suburban queries). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real multi-sensor satellite data with different radiometry and whether the 3D reconstruction backbone can be made more robust without multi-view inputs.
