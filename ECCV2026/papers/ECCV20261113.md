## Problem

Text-to-image models are trained on highly descriptive captions, yet real-world user prompts are often short and underspecified, creating a distributional gap that degrades image-text alignment, aesthetics, and visual quality. Existing prompt-refinement approaches either rely on costly model-specific supervised fine-tuning data, human-in-the-loop pipelines, or jointly update the T2I backbone, limiting automation and cross-model transferability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261113/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short user text prompt as input and produces a single rewritten, more detailed text prompt as output, which is then fed to a frozen T2I model (e.g., FLUX.1-dev, SD-3.5-medium, JanusPro) to synthesize the final image. During training, the LLM rewriter generates n candidate rewrites per prompt, and the T2I model renders one image per candidate; the rewriter itself is an LLM (Llama-3-70B-Instruct, Qwen-3-32B, or DeepSeek-70B) fine-tuned with LoRA, while the T2I backbone remains entirely frozen with no parameter updates or architectural access.

## Method

POET trains an LLM-based prompt rewriter exclusively through iterative Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), bypassing supervised fine-tuning entirely. At each round, the rewriter proposes n candidate rewrites for a user prompt; a frozen T2I model synthesizes images from all candidates, and a multimodal LLM judge (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) performs pairwise image comparisons across four reward dimensions—image quality, general text-image alignment, physical text-image alignment, and aesthetics. The candidate with the highest aggregated pairwise score becomes the chosen prompt and the lowest becomes the rejected prompt, forming a DPO training triplet. The DPO loss updates the rewriter relative to a reference policy, and the process repeats over multiple rounds to progressively refine the rewriter's understanding of the T2I model's implicit prompt preferences. Two reward-weighted variants are trained: a general rewriter prioritizing faithfulness and quality, and an aesthetics rewriter that additionally optimizes visual appeal.

## Results

On GenEval with FLUX.1-dev, POET achieves the highest overall score of 0.79, surpassing RePrompt (0.76), ICL (0.74), and the unmodified baseline (0.70). On Pick-a-Pic v2 (GPT-4o-judged win rates vs. DALL-E 3), the general rewriter lifts FLUX.1-schnell's text-image alignment from 0.419 to 0.561, while the aesthetics rewriter reaches a striking 0.818 aesthetics win rate. On PartiPrompts, POET's rewritten prompts exceed 0.5 win rate across all evaluation dimensions for every tested T2I backbone (FLUX.1-schnell, FLUX.1-dev, SD-3.5-medium, JanusPro), with the highest average of 0.659 on JanusPro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POET outperforms RePrompt (Wu et al., 2025) by 0.03 on GenEval overall (0.79 vs. 0.76) and by a wider margin on individual sub-tasks such as two-object (0.95 vs. 0.87) and color attribution (0.56 vs. 0.49), while RePrompt leads only on position (0.62 vs. 0.58). Against PARM/PARM++ (Guo et al., 2025), which operate on the Show-o backbone, POET on FLUX.1-dev achieves 0.79 vs. 0.70 overall, though a direct same-backbone comparison is not provided. On Pick-a-Pic v2, POET's general rewriter substantially exceeds the DALL-E 3 reference (Betker et al., 2023) in text-image alignment (0.561 vs. 0.500 win rate on FLUX.1-schnell), while the aesthetics rewriter achieves 0.818 aesthetics win rate at the cost of alignment (0.424). Human evaluations confirm that GPT-4o-judged preferences closely track human annotator judgments (e.g., 0.658 vs. 0.618 alignment win rate), validating the automated evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

POET builds directly on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the MLLM-as-a-judge paradigm, applying them to the T2I prompt-rewriting task. It departs from prior RL-based rewriters (Hao et al.'s PPO, Wu et al.'s SFT+PPO, RePrompt) by eliminating SFT entirely and using pairwise image comparisons rather than scalar rewards, and from SFT-based approaches (DALL-E 3, Prompt Expansion) by avoiding costly model-specific annotation. The key conceptual departure is treating the T2I backbone as a completely frozen environment and optimizing only the input text, positioning the work as an input-side, model-agnostic strategy rather than a joint model+prompt optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POET offers a practical, low-cost strategy for improving T2I systems without touching model weights, which is attractive for practitioners who cannot or do not want to retrain large diffusion or autoregressive backbones. The demonstrated cross-model transferability (a rewriter trained on FLUX generalizes to SD-3.5 and JanusPro) suggests that 'good prompt structure' is partially model-agnostic, a useful insight for multi-model deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video generation or more diverse architectures, whether the MLLM judge's biases propagate into the learned rewriter, and whether the aesthetics-alignment trade-off can be resolved with more sophisticated reward shaping.
