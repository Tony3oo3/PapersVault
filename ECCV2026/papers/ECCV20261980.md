## Problem

Extending pretrained 2D diffusion models to stereo image editing is highly challenging because these models operate in an image-centric latent space with no awareness of the rigid epipolar geometry that governs cross-view correspondences. Naively applying diffusion independently to each view produces severe texture mismatches and geometric distortions, while video editing methods emphasize temporal continuity rather than the spatial epipolar constraints unique to stereo pairs. Text-guided, stereo-consistent editing has not been explored in prior work, and existing 3D editing approaches (NeRF, 3DGS) are fragile for small-baseline stereo inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a stereo image pair (left and right views, resized to 512×512) and a text prompt as input, with an optional binary mask to restrict editing to specific regions. Both views are jointly inverted into a shared latent via 50-step DDIM inversion and denoised together under text guidance. The output is an edited stereo pair that preserves cross-view geometric and textural consistency, directly displayable as anaglyph or side-by-side stereo content. Disparity maps are estimated on-the-fly using MASt3R, and segmentation uses SAM, so no additional user-provided depth or geometry is required.

## Method

StereoEdit is a zero-shot, training-free framework built on Stable Diffusion v1.5 that enforces stereo consistency through two complementary geometry-aware modules operating during the denoising process. The Geometrically-Gated Attention Reference (GGAR) module warps cross-view key/value features using predicted disparity maps, applies a soft Gaussian epipolar mask to suppress attention across non-corresponding rows, and introduces a reliability gate derived from forward-backward disparity consistency to suppress occluded or ambiguous regions—ensuring each token attends only to geometrically valid counterparts. The Geometry-Aware Latent Alignment (GALA) module operates at the latent level after each denoising step, computing bidirectional warping errors to identify the less consistent view, then adaptively fusing it with the warped counterpart using confidence-weighted corrections that accumulate over iterations to restore global parallax alignment. Together, GGAR enforces local feature-level coherence within the U-Net while GALA corrects residual global structural drift across sampling steps, requiring no retraining or fine-tuning of the base diffusion model.

## Results

On a 120-pair stereo benchmark (NBU-SIRQA, Flickr1024, Middlebury 2014), StereoEdit achieves the best scores on all three automatic metrics: CLIP-T 33.82, CLIP-F 97.36, and Warp-Err 3.92. The Warp-Err improvement of 29.11% over the best prior method (TokenFlow, 5.53) is the most striking gain, reflecting substantially tighter cross-view geometric alignment. In the human study (25 raters, 50 pairs), StereoEdit leads on all three dimensions—Edit Accuracy 7.65, Stereo Consistency 8.01, and Overall Quality 8.09—outscoring the next-best method (VideoGrain, Overall 7.18) by nearly a full point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StereoEdit outperforms all five video-editing baselines on every reported metric. Against TokenFlow (the strongest prior on Warp-Err at 5.53), StereoEdit reduces geometric error to 3.92 (−29.1%) while also improving CLIP-T by 1.95 points and CLIP-F by 1.22 points. VideoGrain is the closest competitor on CLIP-T (33.77 vs. 33.82) but lags substantially on CLIP-F (94.62 vs. 97.36) and Warp-Err (6.51 vs. 3.92), indicating its temporal-alignment strategy fails to enforce epipolar geometry. VideoDirector shows the worst Warp-Err (8.07) and lowest Stereo Consistency in the human study (6.96), confirming that spatial-temporal decoupled guidance is ill-suited to fixed-baseline stereo pairs. VidToMe and ReAtCo fall in the middle: VidToMe achieves the second-best CLIP-F (96.38) but still trails StereoEdit by 0.98 points, while ReAtCo's re-attentional mechanism yields the weakest stereo consistency score (6.76) among all methods. In the human evaluation, StereoEdit's Overall score of 8.09 exceeds the runner-up VideoGrain (7.18) by 0.91 points, a margin the authors attribute to the combined effect of GGAR's local attention gating and GALA's global latent realignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

StereoEdit positions itself at the intersection of latent diffusion editing and explicit stereo geometry, deliberately departing from both video editing frameworks (TokenFlow, VidToMe, VideoGrain, etc.) and 3D-representation-based editors (NeRF, 3DGS). The key conceptual departure is recognizing that stereo pairs are governed by epipolar geometry—simultaneous projections along a fixed baseline—rather than the temporal dynamics that video methods optimize for, and that 3D reconstruction methods are too fragile for the weak geometric constraints of small-baseline stereo. The method repurposes Stable Diffusion's pretrained editing capability and Repaint's mask-guided inpainting, but injects geometry at two levels: disparity-warped, reliability-gated cross-view attention (GGAR) and confidence-weighted bidirectional latent correction (GALA), both operating training-free during DDIM denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free framework for free-form, text-guided stereo-consistent editing, filling a gap where prior stereo editing was limited to depth retargeting or style transfer. The practical implication is that existing stereo content (films, AR/VR assets) can be semantically edited without re-shooting or re-rendering. However, several limitations temper the impact: evaluation is on only 120 pairs at 512×512, no purpose-built stereo editing baselines exist for comparison (all baselines are repurposed video methods), and the approach depends on external disparity estimation (MASt3R) whose errors propagate directly into the geometric gating. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions and larger disparity ranges, robustness to noisy disparity in textureless regions, and whether the geometry-aware modules can be integrated into native stereo diffusion models trained end-to-end.
