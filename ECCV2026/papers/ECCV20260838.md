## Problem

Existing two-stage 3D-OVD pipelines suffer from a discovery bottleneck: 2D-Detection-based methods produce noisy 3D bounding boxes via back-projection, while 3D-Proposal-based methods assign incorrect semantic labels due to occlusion. Furthermore, the training stage treats these noisy pseudo-labels with equal supervision as ground-truth annotations, propagating localization and classification errors into the final model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260838/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The discovery stage consumes multi-view RGB-D images (with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics) and point clouds, using a 2D open-vocabulary detector (Detic) and a class-agnostic 3D detector (CuTR) in parallel to produce pseudo-labeled 3D objects. The training stage operates solely on point cloud input (no RGB images), producing 3D bounding boxes parameterized as (x, y, z, l, w, h, θ) with open-vocabulary class labels for both base and novel categories. Text category labels are encoded via CLIP for semantic alignment, and an LLM generates two additional super-category hierarchy levels for robust classification.

## Method

Co-3DGT proposes a unified two-stage framework. In the Co-Distillation Discovery stage, back-projected 2D OVD results and class-agnostic 3D proposals are jointly matched via a Hungarian algorithm over a composite cost combining 3D IoU (geometric consistency), 3D objectness score, and 2D class confidence, with an IoU threshold of 0.1 filtering geometrically infeasible pairs; the matched 3D geometry is paired with the 2D semantic label to form reliable pseudo-labels. In the Dual Guidance Robust Training stage, (i) scene-awareness-guided uncertainty regularization uses the stop-gradient log-uncertainty of base objects as a scene-level anchor to softly constrain novel-object regression uncertainty, preventing both under- and over-confidence in noisy bboxes, and (ii) LLM-guided hierarchical alignment uses an LLM to derive top and mid super-categories from fine-grained labels, then aligns 3D object features with CLIP text embeddings via binary cross-entropy at all three semantic levels, making classification robust to label noise.

## Results

On SUN RGB-D, Co-3DGT achieves 14.37% AP25_novel, 49.85% AP25_base, and 22.63% AP25_mean, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art OV-Uni3DETR by +4.71% on novel-class AP25. On ScanNetV2, the method reaches 21.91% AP25_novel, 32.83% AP25_base, and 23.75% AP25_mean, a +9.82% gain in novel-class AP25 over OV-Uni3DETR. In the annotation-free setting (no ground-truth base objects) on ScanNetV2, Co-3DGT still achieves 36.15% AP25_mean across 20 unseen categories, outperforming OV-Uni3DETR by +10.82%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Co-3DGT outperforms all nine baselines on both datasets. The most direct comparison is against OV-Uni3DETR [39] (previous SOTA), where Co-3DGT gains +4.71% AP25_novel on SUN RGB-D (14.37% vs 9.66%) and +9.82% on ScanNetV2 (21.91% vs 12.09%), while also improving AP25_mean by +4.57% and +8.60% respectively. Against CoDAv2 [4] and INHA [17], the gains are even larger in absolute terms (+5.20% and +5.46% AP25_novel on SUN RGB-D; +12.79% and +14.12% on ScanNetV2). The earlier 2D-Detection-based methods (OV-3DET [24], Det-PointCLIP [43], Det-CLIP2 [46]) and 3D-Proposal-based methods (CoDA [3]) show substantially lower novel-class performance, confirming that the co-distillation discovery and dual-guidance training jointly address the localization and semantic noise that limit these pipelines. In the annotation-free setting on ScanNetV2 (Table 2), Co-3DGT achieves 36.15% AP25_mean versus 25.33% for OV-Uni3DETR and 22.72% for CoDAv2, with particularly large per-category gains on objects like chair (81.27% vs 28.11%) and bed (70.42% vs 50.49%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established two-stage 3D-OVD pipeline (discovery then training) by addressing a specific weakness: the noise in discovered pseudo-labels that degrades downstream training. It builds on Uni3DETR as the 3D detection backbone, the uncertainty-weighted regression loss of Seitzer et al., and the back-projection strategy from OV-3DET, but departs from prior work by unifying the 2D-Detection-based and 3D-Proposal-based discovery paradigms into a single Hungarian-matching co-distillation formulation. The LLM-guided hierarchical alignment is a novel training-time contribution that repurposes LLMs for structured label augmentation rather than direct feature extraction, distinguishing it from prior CLIP-based classification heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck in the 3D-OVD pipeline—noisy pseudo-labels from discovery stages—and provides a principled solution that yields substantial gains (+9.82% AP25 on ScanNetV2), suggesting the field has been underperforming due to suboptimal discovery-training coupling. The finding that the LLM choice has minimal impact (Table 5) implies the hierarchical structure itself, not the specific model, drives the benefit, making the technique broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the framework scales to outdoor/autonomous-driving 3D-OVD, whether the two-stage pipeline can be replaced by end-to-end learning, and whether the annotation-free setting (where SGUR is inapplicable) can be further improved. The authors note future work toward real-time perception for robotic navigation.
