## Problem

Existing cervical cytology classifiers either transfer generic backbones or build dataset-specific pipelines, both of which lack interpretability and fail to align with the clinical diagnostic workflow (screening → reading → reporting). The paper asks whether a compact model can be made structurally transparent—where spatial selection, feature extraction, and decision are all analytically solvable and clinically grounded—without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260759/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB cervical cytology image (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224 at inference) and produces class logits for the target taxonomy (3, 5, or 7 classes depending on dataset). Beyond the logit, the forward pass intrinsically yields four pixelwise concept maps (nuclear-envelope roughness, orientation disorder, local N/C ratio, chromatin heterogeneity), a nonnegative diagnostic potential field, and a closed-form spatial attention computed on a 1/32-resolution downsampled lattice. The final logit is fully decomposable as a sum over spatial locations and channels, enabling per-concept, per-location, and per-channel attribution without any post-hoc explainer.

## Method

GeoCEAN encodes the clinical workflow in three analytically constrained stages. First, four fixed geometric operators (boundary Laplacian flux, circular gradient-orientation variance, local area ratio, log-intensity variance) produce concept maps that are monotonically calibrated and fused via nonnegative simplex weights into a diagnostic potential V; the spatial attention is then the unique minimizer of a free-energy functional ⟨A,E⟩ − τH(A), yielding the closed-form softmax(V/τ). Second, a geometry-driven backbone applies fixed gradient-magnitude and Laplacian responses per channel, mixes them through a nonnegative near-diagonal 1×1 matrix (bandwidth-limited coupling), and updates via a convex residual step X + γ(X̂ − X) with γ ∈ (0,1), ensuring stable, auditable feature evolution. Third, an attention-weighted probabilistic readout aggregates the terminal feature map into a compact descriptor, and a linear head maps it to logits. The entire pipeline contains only 2.2M parameters, is trained end-to-end with standard cross-entropy loss (SGD, 300 epochs), and requires no learned attention parameters—the attention is solved in closed form at every forward pass.

## Results

On DSCC, GeoCEAN achieves 93.2% ACC, 92.6% F1, and 98.7% AUC with only 2.2M parameters, 0.4 GFLOPs, and 5.4 ms latency, outperforming all 17 baselines. On SIPaKMeD it reaches 98.5% ACC and 99.9% AUC, and on Herlev 76.8% ACC, both state-of-the-art among the compared methods. The model is roughly 20–45× smaller than the strongest general-purpose and cervix-specific baselines (e.g., Swin-GMRS at 100.8M params, Hybrid Models at 57.5M) while delivering 3.7–10.3 percentage points higher accuracy on DSCC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoCEAN outperforms all 17 baselines on all three datasets. On DSCC, it leads the strongest cervix-specific baseline Swin-GMRS (89.5% ACC, 100.8M params) by 3.7 points and the next-best general-purpose model SHViT-S1 (88.5%) by 4.7 points, while using 46× fewer parameters than Swin-GMRS. On Herlev, GeoCEAN (76.8%) surpasses the best baseline vHeat-B (73.9%) by 2.9 points and cervix-specific Swin-GMRS (73.5%) by 3.3 points. On SIPaKMeD, it achieves 98.5% ACC versus MedViT's 97.9% and MobileMamba/SHViT-S1/MSCCNet at 97.3%. Among structurally transparent baselines, CRATE (84.6% on DSCC) and BDD Net (81.5%) trail GeoCEAN by 8.6 and 11.7 points respectively, confirming that domain-native geometric constraints yield substantially better accuracy than generic white-box designs. In cross-dataset transfer (DSCC→Herlev, binary), GeoCEAN also leads Swin-GMRS (74.0% vs. 70.2% ACC) and RES_DCGAN (59.8%), suggesting the explicit concept-to-potential pipeline generalizes better than learned attention under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoCEAN carves new ground at the intersection of white-box/structurally transparent architectures (CRATE, BDD Net, Dendrite Net) and domain-specific medical backbones (MedViT, MedMamba), but does not directly extend any single prior architecture. Its conceptual lineage draws from statistical mechanics (free-energy minimization yielding a Boltzmann/softmax distribution) and classical image processing (fixed gradient and Laplacian operators), repurposing both into a constrained deep-learning pipeline where attention is a closed-form solution rather than a learned parameter. The key departure from prior cervical cytology systems (MSCCNet, CerviNet, Swin-GMRS) is that clinical semantics—nuclear envelope roughness, orientation disorder, N/C ratio, chromatin heterogeneity—are encoded as explicit input features driving a thermodynamic attention, rather than being recovered post hoc from a generic backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: a 2.2M-parameter model matching or exceeding 100M+ parameter baselines on three cervical cytology benchmarks is attractive for resource-constrained screening settings, and the intrinsic evidence-to-logit decomposition (concept → potential → attention → logit) offers an audit trail that post-hoc explainers like Grad-CAM cannot provide. However, systematic cytopathologist validation of the attention maps and concept attributions remains absent, and the free-energy formulation, while elegant, reduces computationally to a softmax over a hand-crafted energy, raising the question of whether the thermodynamic framing adds genuine inductive bias beyond a well-tuned weighted sum. Extending GeoCEAN to whole-slide imaging and validating its attributions against expert reading order would be the natural next steps to establish clinical utility beyond benchmark accuracy.
