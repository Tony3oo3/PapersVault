## Problem

L0-regularized structured channel pruning with dual-ascent constraint enforcement suffers a long-horizon instability: the Lagrange multiplier accumulates over training, amplifies pruning-specific gradients, and induces late-stage oscillations in both gradients and loss. This instability caps the pruning-phase token budget at a few billion tokens, preventing the monotonic accuracy gains that larger data budgets should provide for training-based, surrogate-differentiable pruning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261925/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM or VLM backbone (e.g., Qwen2.5VL-3B, Gemma, LLaMA2) initialized from an SFT-adapted checkpoint, along with a mixed text-and-multimodal pruning-phase dataset. It produces a physically pruned sparse model at a target sparsity (e.g., 30% or 60%), where structured units (attention heads, FFN channels) are removed via hard-concrete gate thresholding. In the VLM setting, only the LLM backbone is pruned while the vision encoder and multimodal projector remain frozen; the pruning rate emerges adaptively from the gradient-norm constraint rather than being prescribed by a fixed schedule.

## Method

DIGS replaces dual-ascent Lagrange multiplier updates with a per-step L2-norm bound on the composite gate gradient. At each step, it solves a closed-form quadratic for Δλ such that ‖g_t + Δλ·g_p‖ ≤ c_g, using a feasible-root branch (saturating the bound) or a least-squares branch (zeroing the pruning-direction projection) when the bound is infeasible; an adaptive target c_g^eff scales with ‖g_p‖ to prevent coefficient inflation as gate Jacobians shrink late in training. The GNCL-Opt realization achieves this in a single backward pass by duplicating pruning parameters into distillation and pruning branches in the forward pass, then computing λ and rescaling only the pruning-branch gradient before merging in the backward pass. The framework retains global parameter-weighted sparsity budgets, hard-concrete relaxation for differentiability, and incremental sparsity ramping (0%→60%), enabling stable pruning beyond 100B tokens where L0-Dual degrades.

## Results

At 60% sparsity on Qwen2.5VL-3B, DIGS-FR24.5B achieves an average score of 51.93 (ΔAcc = −13.08 vs. dense 65.01), substantially outperforming L0-Dual-FR24.5B at 38.97 (ΔAcc = −26.04). At 30% sparsity, DIGS-FR24.5B reaches 58.75 versus L0-Dual's 56.85. Scaling the pruning-phase budget to ~114B tokens at 33% sparsity yields an average of 64.32 after distillation and SFT recovery, within 0.69 of the dense baseline. On LLMs (Gemma-2B/7B, LLaMA2-7B/13B), DIGS at 30% sparsity achieves 49.81/70.12/58.27/66.15 respectively, surpassing all prior pruning methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against L0-Dual (ShearedLlama configuration), DIGS wins decisively at larger pruning budgets: at 24.5B tokens and 60% sparsity, DIGS scores 51.93 vs. L0-Dual's 38.97 (a 12.96-point gap), while at the smaller 5.7B budget L0-Dual edges ahead at 30% sparsity (58.26 vs. 56.04) but DIGS leads at 60% (47.92 vs. 44.51). The critical finding is that L0-Dual degrades monotonically beyond ~5.7B tokens due to Lagrange-multiplier-driven oscillations, whereas DIGS improves monotonically with budget. On LLMs (Table 3), DIGS outperforms LLM-Pruner by 9.76 points on Gemma-2B at 30% sparsity (49.81 vs. 40.05) and SliceGPT by 9.92 points (49.81 vs. 39.89). Against PAT, DIGS leads on Gemma-2B (49.81 vs. 45.33) and Gemma-7B (70.12 vs. 64.58) at 30% sparsity, while PAT retains a slight edge on LLaMA2-7B (57.81 vs. 58.27 is actually DIGS winning by 0.46) — DIGS wins or ties on all four LLM backbones at 30% sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIGS sits within the L0-regularized structured channel pruning lineage initiated by the hard-concrete relaxation [20] and extended by the augmented-Lagrangian (dual-ascent) formulations of ShearedLlama [37], Wang et al. [34], and Xia et al. [38]. Its key departure is replacing the accumulating Lagrange multiplier with a per-step L2-norm bound on gate gradients, solved in closed form each update, which eliminates the long-horizon instability that caps the pruning-phase token budget in L0-Dual. The GNCL-Opt backward-path realization further distinguishes it by avoiding an extra backward pass, and the framework is validated against post-hoc methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) and training-based baselines (PAT, LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT) under a unified SFT-then-prune protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: by stabilizing long-horizon pruning, DIGS enables scaling the pruning-phase token budget from the ~0.4–1.6B range explored by ShearedLlama to over 100B tokens, closing most of the accuracy gap to the dense model at 33% sparsity (64.32 vs. 65.01 Avg). However, the headline scaling results are demonstrated on a single model (Qwen2.5VL-3B), the 100B-token run targets only 33% sparsity rather than the 60% in the main comparison tables, and the vision encoder is left unpruned. Open questions include whether the gradient-norm control generalizes to other structured units (MoE experts, context windows), to vision-encoder pruning, and to settings where the distillation loss is replaced by other objectives.
