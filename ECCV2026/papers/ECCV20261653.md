## Problem

In 3D masked autoencoders, the decoder over-relies on positional embeddings that encode explicit spatial coordinates, creating a positional shortcut that bypasses encoder-derived semantic features. Gradient analysis reveals that positional embedding gradients consistently dominate encoder output gradients throughout training, causing the model to minimize reconstruction loss through coordinate cues rather than learning discriminative geometric representations. This positional leakage weakens the quality of pre-trained features and limits downstream generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud downsampled to 1024 points, partitioned into 64 local patches of 32 points each via Farthest Point Sampling and KNN grouping. During pre-training, a masking ratio is applied to patches; the encoder processes only visible patches to produce latent semantic tokens, while the decoder reconstructs the masked point coordinates using those tokens plus recalibrated positional embeddings. The final output is a set of learned patch-level representations transferred to downstream tasks (classification, segmentation, registration, few-shot learning), with reconstruction of masked coordinates serving as the self-supervised training signal.

## Method

MPL-MAE introduces two complementary modules to rebalance positional and semantic contributions. The Recalibrated Positional Embedding (RPE) module replaces continuous patch-center coordinates with quantized ranking indices along each axis (order-isomorphism encoding), preserving relative ordering while discarding absolute metric information, followed by sinusoidal encoding and a topology preservation regularizer that maintains local neighborhood adjacency. The Gated Positional Interface (GPI) module replaces unconditional additive positional injection at each decoder layer with a Gumbel-Softmax gated mechanism that stochastically selects whether to inject positional cues at each token location, creating a sparse information bottleneck. An adversarial leakage regularization term further penalizes the positional encoding for being sufficient to reconstruct masked coordinates without encoder features. The total training objective combines Chamfer Distance reconstruction loss, topology preservation, and leakage suppression, jointly discouraging positional shortcuts while retaining geometric structure.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN (FULL fine-tuning), MPL-MAE achieves 95.52% (OBJ-BG), 94.18% (OBJ-ONLY), and 90.35% (PB-T50), matching or exceeding the prior SOTA PCP-MAE. Under the more diagnostic MLP-Linear protocol, MPL-MAE leads PCP-MAE by 1.7% (OBJ-BG), 1.2% (OBJ-ONLY), and 1.5% (PB-T50), and by 2.2%, 1.1%, and 2.4% under MLP-3. On ModelNet40, MPL-MAE reaches 94.1% (w/o vote) and 94.4% (w/ vote) under full fine-tuning, and 92.9%/93.2% under MLP-Linear. In few-shot learning (5-way 10-shot), MPL-MAE attains 97.7±1.9%, outperforming PCP-MAE (97.4±2.3%) and Point-MAE (96.3±2.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Point-MAE [22], MPL-MAE gains 1.77% on ScanObjectNN PB-T50 (90.35 vs 88.58) and 0.9% on ModelNet40 (94.1 vs 93.2) under full fine-tuning, with even larger margins under linear probing (e.g., +6.0% on PB-T50). Against the prior SOTA PCP-MAE [44], MPL-MAE ties on OBJ-BG (95.52) and PB-T50 (90.35) under full fine-tuning but clearly wins on OBJ-ONLY (+0.2%) and under MLP-Linear/MLP-3 protocols (up to +2.4% on PB-T50). MPL-MAE outperforms Point-FEMAE [41] on all ScanObjectNN splits under every protocol (e.g., +0.9% OBJ-BG, +2.4% PB-T50 under MLP-3) despite using fewer parameters (22.1M vs 27.4M). It surpasses Point-M2AE [42] by 4.3% on OBJ-BG and 3.9% on PB-T50 (MLP-3), and Point-PQAE [45] by 2.0% on OBJ-BG and 2.6% on PB-T50 (MLP-3). Among supervised baselines (PointNet [25], PointNet++ [26], DGCNN [24]), MPL-MAE leads by 12–23% on ScanObjectNN, confirming the advantage of self-supervised pre-training. In few-shot learning, MPL-MAE outperforms all self-supervised baselines including OcCo [30] (97.7 vs 90.6 on 5-way 10-shot) and Point-BERT [40] (97.7 vs 94.6), with lower variance than PCP-MAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D masked autoencoder lineage initiated by Point-MAE [22], which established direct coordinate reconstruction as a viable self-supervised objective. While subsequent works refined architectural design (PointM2AE [42]), masking strategies (Point-FEM-MAE [41]), or auxiliary supervision (PCP-MAE [44]), none examined the interaction between positional embeddings and encoder semantic features during decoding. MPL-MAE departs by treating positional leakage as a first-order optimization problem, introducing the Recalibrated Positional Embed (order-isomorphic encoding + topology regularization) and Gated Positional Interface (Gumbel-Softmax gating) as targeted modifications to the decoding stage, alongside a novel min-max leakage loss that has no direct precedent in prior 3D MAE formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that 3D MAE decoders systematically over-rely on positional embeddings (RRS ≈ 0.87) provides a unifying explanation for why architectural improvements alone have yielded diminishing returns in representation quality. The robustness results—where the performance gap over baselines widens from +0.9% to +5.2% as input noise increases—suggest that semantically grounded representations are not merely more accurate but fundamentally more stable. Open questions include whether the Gumbel-Softmax gating and adversarial leakage loss are necessary at scale, whether the approach transfers to multimodal or large-scale LiDAR pre-training, and whether the topology-preservation regularizer generalizes beyond the k-NN neighborhood structure assumed here.
