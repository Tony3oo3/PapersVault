## Problem

Discrete diffusion VLA (dVLA) models unify perception and control in a single discrete space, preserving VLM prior knowledge better than flow-matching VLAs, but their bidirectional attention prevents KV cache reuse, making inference far too slow for real-time robotic control (~30 Hz). Existing acceleration strategies either degrade performance (Fast-dLLM) or preclude inter-block parallelism (Block Diffusion), leaving a gap between dVLA quality and practical deployment speed.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images from an overhead camera and wrist-mounted cameras along with a natural-language instruction, and produces a length-L discrete action token sequence where each token maps to a low-level robot action via uniform bins or a quantized tokenizer. For the unified UD-VLA variant, future visual observations are additionally encoded into discrete tokens via a VQ-VAE encoder and jointly denoised with action tokens in a single sequence. The action sequence is partitioned into blocks whose size equals the per-step action dimensionality (7 tokens) or its multiples, which the ablation shows preserves intrinsic temporal dependencies better than arbitrary block sizes.

## Method

Fast-dVLA exploits the observation that bidirectional dVLAs still decode in an implicit left-to-right block-wise order, and converts this into an explicit block-wise causal attention pattern so that KV states of completed blocks remain fixed and reusable across denoising iterations. On top of this, a diffusion-forcing schedule assigns monotonically increasing noise levels to successive blocks, allowing earlier (less noisy) blocks to finish denoising while later blocks are still being refined, thereby enabling inter-block parallelism without sacrificing temporal causality. Training uses an asymmetric distillation loss where a student with block-wise causal attention is KL-divergence-matched to a teacher with full bidirectional attention, converging in roughly 1/10 of the steps needed for from-scratch training. At inference, a pipelined parallel decoding algorithm introduces new blocks as semi-activated once the predecessor exceeds a completion threshold τ_add, promotes them to fully-activated at τ_act, and applies confidence-aware token selection, achieving 2.8×–4.1× speedup while maintaining or slightly improving success rates.

## Results

On LIBERO, Fast-dVLA achieves 3.2× speedup on Dream-VLA (313.1 vs 98.8 tokens/s) and 4.1× speedup on DD-VLA (402.7 vs 152.1 tokens/s) while maintaining or slightly improving average success rates (0.870 vs 0.856; 0.966 vs 0.963). On SimplerEnv, Fast-dVLA applied to Dream-VLA reaches 59.3% average task success rate at 366.4 tokens/s, the highest among all discrete-output VLAs tested. On CALVIN, UD-VLA+Fast-dVLA achieves a 2.8× inference speedup (186.7 vs 67.3 tokens/s) with 4.54 average tasks completed in a row, surpassing UP-VLA (4.42) and MDT (4.52). Real-world experiments confirm a consistent 30 Hz execution frequency across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fast-dVLA outperforms all acceleration baselines on both speed and accuracy: on Dream-VLA/LIBERO it reaches 313.1 tokens/s (3.2×) vs Fast-dLLM's 183.2 (1.9×) and Block Diffusion's 181.7 (1.8×), while also achieving the highest success rate (0.870 vs 0.828 and 0.858). On SimplerEnv, Fast-dVLA (Dream-VLA) achieves 59.3% average success at 366.4 tokens/s, surpassing the base Dream-VLA (51.0%, 100.1 tokens/s), LLaDA-VLA (55.5%, 160.0), and all flow-matching VLAs including GR00T-N1 (36.5%) and π₀ (27.1%). Against the SOTA AR VLA π₀-FAST, Fast-dVLA more than doubles both success rate (59.3% vs 32.1%) and speed (366.4 vs 107.5 tokens/s). On CALVIN, UD-VLA+Fast-dVLA achieves 4.54 average tasks-in-a-row, edging out UP-VLA (4.42) and MDT (4.52) while running 2.8× faster than the unaccelerated UD-VLA. The only comparison where Fast-dVLA does not lead is on individual SimplerEnv sub-tasks where Block Diffusion slightly edges it on Carrot-on-Plate success (45.8% vs 54.1% — actually Fast-dVLA wins here too), confirming consistent superiority across all tested baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three key threads: block diffusion (Arriola et al., 2025) for interpolating between autoregressive and parallel decoding, diffusion forcing (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024) for assigning progressively decaying noise levels across a sequence, and Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025) for confidence-aware parallel decoding in diffusion LLMs. The critical departure is the observation that fully bidirectional dVLAs exhibit an implicit left-to-right decoding tendency, which the authors exploit to combine block-wise attention (enabling KV cache reuse) with diffusion forcing (enabling inter-block parallelism) in a single framework. Unlike prior VLA acceleration work focused on pruning, caching, or early-exit strategies, this is the first method specifically designed for the discrete diffusion VLA architecture, and it introduces an asymmetric distillation loss to efficiently convert a pretrained bidirectional dVLA into the block-wise variant without retraining from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 30 Hz execution frequency on real-world bimanual tasks, this work removes the primary practical barrier to deploying dVLAs in physical robotics, making them competitive with flow-matching VLAs like π₀.₅ while retaining dVLAs' advantages in unified multimodal alignment and preservation of VLM prior knowledge. The asymmetric distillation converging in ~1/10 the training steps of from-scratch training offers a cost-efficient pathway for the community to accelerate existing dVLA checkpoints. Open questions include whether the block-size heuristic (multiples of action dimensionality) generalizes to higher-dimensional or variable-length action spaces, whether the implicit AR tendency persists in dVLAs trained natively with block-wise attention rather than distilled from bidirectional models, and whether the pipelined decoding thresholds transfer across task complexity without re-tuning.
