## Problem

While vision-language models have advanced high-level reasoning in autonomous driving, no existing benchmark verifies whether their predictions are physically consistent with the ego-vehicle's own kinematic state. Current evaluation paradigms assess external environments, spatial relations, or abstract actions, but leave the self-referential axis of ego-motion understanding entirely unevaluated, making it impossible to distinguish genuine kinematic grounding from spurious visual correlations or language priors.

## Input / Output

The model receives 10 uniformly sampled frames from a 3-second driving clip (~3.3 FPS) paired with a natural-language query about a specific motion property (e.g., turning direction, braking intensity). The physical state sequence (speed, acceleration, jerk, yaw rate, heading) is strictly withheld during inference; ground-truth labels are derived by a deterministic oracle applying calibrated thresholds to these signals. Optionally, explicit ego-motion text in four encoding formats (scalar summary, per-channel kinematic timeseries, spatial coordinates, or full combination) can be appended to probe modality reliance. Output is a discrete semantic label from a predefined binary or multiple-choice space, parsed deterministically for scoring.

## Method

EgoDyn-Bench formulates ego-motion understanding as a semantic video-QA task where a deterministic oracle maps continuous kinematics to 14 discrete motion-concept categories via calibrated thresholds, decoupling physical logic from visual perception. The dataset (1,000 balanced 3-second clips, 14,000 QA pairs) combines nuScenes real-world sequences with CARLA-simulated scenarios generated from CommonRoad test cases and a Frenetix motion planner, balanced through a four-stage curation pipeline and aligned via Cosmos Transfer 2 style transfer. Evaluation employs Balanced Accuracy, Macro-F1, Temporal Accuracy, and a novel Weighted Physics Consistency Rate (WPCR) that checks 10 Boolean kinematic implication rules per clip to diagnose internal physical coherence independent of correctness. The large-scale audit of 20+ models (closed-source MLLMs, open-source VLMs at multiple scales, and domain-specific VLAs) reveals a structural Perception Bottleneck: models possess physically consistent reasoning in their language modality but fail to ground it in visual observations, with classical geometric baselines (visual odometry, optical flow) outperforming even the largest MLLMs on the overlapping question subset.

## Results

On EgoDyn-Bench (14,000 QA pairs, 1,000 balanced 3-second clips), vision-only VLMs substantially underperform classical geometric baselines: the best VLM (Gemini 3 Pro) achieves 47.0% Balanced Accuracy vs. 63.8% for the Visual Odometry baseline and 65.1% raw accuracy. Adding explicit kinematic text recovers performance across all models, with Gemini 3 Pro reaching 68.3% BAcc and GPT-5.1 reaching 66.1%. Critically, for Qwen3-VL-8B, replacing visual frames entirely with trajectory text yields 59.6% BAcc (+20.7 pp over vision-only 38.9%), while reintroducing visual frames adds only +2.6 pp, revealing that ego-motion reasoning is routed almost exclusively through the language modality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark reveals a consistent Perception Bottleneck: all 20+ evaluated VLMs and VLAs lose to classical non-learned geometric baselines on the overlapping 6/14 geometrically answerable questions (Visual Odometry BAcc 63.8% vs. best VLM Gemini 3 Pro 47.0%, a 16.8 pp gap). Among VLMs, scaling yields only marginal gains—Gemini 3 Pro (47.0%) beats the best open-source 8B model (Cosmos Reason 2-8B, 39.9%) by just 7.1 pp despite orders-of-magnitude scale difference. Domain-specific VLAs (RoboTron-Drive 38.6%, ImpromptuVLA 37.8%) perform at or below general open-source models of equivalent size, indicating in-domain training does not resolve the deficit. With explicit kinematic text, the ranking shifts: Gemini 3 Pro (68.3%) and GPT-5.1 (66.1%) lead, while smaller models like Qwen3-VL-8B with Timeseries encoding (62.2% BAcc, 92.6% WPCR) approach closed-source performance, showing representation quality matters more than parameter scale. The most striking comparison is Qwen3-VL-8B text-only (59.6% BAcc) vs. vision-only (38.9%), a 20.7 pp gap that demonstrates visual observations contribute negligible temporal signal to ego-motion reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of classical geometric ego-motion estimation (optical flow, visual odometry) and VLM-based driving benchmarks, which have evaluated external scene understanding or high-level reasoning but never the agent's own kinematic state. It repurposes the video-QA paradigm but grounds labels in a deterministic oracle over continuous vehicle kinematics rather than subjective annotation, and introduces WPCR as a consistency diagnostic orthogonal to accuracy. The geometric baselines (Horn-Schunck flow, KLT-based VO, RAFT, TartanVO) are not methods the paper builds on but rather serve as non-learned reference points that expose the VLM deficit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Perception Bottleneck finding—that 20+ VLMs and VLAs across scales consistently underperform simple non-learned geometric baselines on ego-motion, and that explicit trajectory text recovers performance while visual frames contribute negligible temporal signal—challenges the core assumption that vision-centric foundation models can ground physical reasoning in pixels. This has direct implications for the VLA paradigm in autonomous driving: if ego-motion logic is routed exclusively through the language modality, scaling visual encoders or adding domain-specific training will not close the gap without native vision-kinematics alignment during pre-training. Limitations include the 3-second single-agent horizon and the diagnostic (open-loop) nature of the evaluation, leaving long-horizon multi-agent reasoning and closed-loop driving performance as open questions.
