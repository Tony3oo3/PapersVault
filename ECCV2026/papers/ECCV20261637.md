## Problem

Large VLMs process hundreds to thousands of visual tokens per image or video frame, incurring quadratic attention costs and propagating query-irrelevant patches that dilute reasoning. Existing token-reduction methods are either text-agnostic (removing semantically critical regions) or rely on deep attention maps that become unstable under aggressive pruning, making it difficult to achieve both query-awareness and robustness at high compression ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261637/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FlashVLM consumes a frozen vision encoder's patch features (e.g., 576 tokens for a 24×24 grid, or 2048 tokens for 8 video frames at 224×224) together with the LLM's text token embeddings for the user query. It outputs a compact subset of visual tokens (configurable budgets of 128, 64, or 32 for images; 455, 227, or 114 for video) that replaces the full sequence before entering the LLM, after which the LLM produces its standard text answer. The entire selection is a single-shot operation at the encoder-decoder boundary, requiring no per-layer sparsification or architectural changes.

## Method

FlashVLM fuses two complementary relevance signals in a log-domain weighted geometric mean: an intrinsic visual saliency score derived from the vision encoder's averaged self-attention (min-max normalized), and an extrinsic query-relevance score computed as a dot-product similarity between ℓ₂-normalized projected image tokens and context-gated, ℓ₂-normalized text embeddings in the LLM space, with temperature-controlled aggregation (τ=0.05) and adaptive z-score filtering. The context gate is extracted from the LLM's early-layer text-to-text self-attention, weighting each text token by its aggregated attention sink score to reflect syntactic and semantic importance. A diversity-preserving partitioning step then splits the token budget equally between top-scoring 'important' tokens and a 'diverse background' set obtained via iterative bipartite pruning: candidates are deterministically split into even/odd spatial subsets, and at each step the most similar cross-subset pair is identified and the lower-scoring token is removed until the target count is reached. This attention-light, single-shot design avoids modifying transformer internals, remains fully compatible with FlashAttention, and achieves beyond-lossless compression (100.6% relative accuracy at 77.8% pruning on LLaVA-1.5) while retaining 92.8% performance at 94.4% compression.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 with 10 image benchmarks, FlashVLM achieves 100.6% relative accuracy while pruning 77.8% of visual tokens (128 kept), slightly surpassing the unpruned upper bound (100.0%). Under aggressive 94.4% compression (32 tokens), it retains 92.8% relative accuracy, outperforming the next-best method VisPruner (91.5%) by 1.3 points. On video QA (Video-LLaVA, 3 benchmarks), FlashVLM reaches 48.7% average accuracy at 77.8% pruning, marginally exceeding the unpruned score (3.33 vs. 3.32), and maintains 44.7% at 94.4% compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashVLM outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark and every token budget in Table 1. At the moderate 128-token setting, it leads VisPruner (the strongest text-agnostic baseline) by 0.9 points in relative accuracy (100.6% vs. 99.7%) and VisionZip by 2.2 points; the gap widens dramatically at 32 tokens, where FlashVLM (92.8%) beats VisPruner (91.5%) by 1.3 points and VisionZip (80.0%) by 12.8 points. Among text-guided methods, FlashVLM surpasses SparseVLM by 6.2 points at 128 tokens and by 14.9 points at 32 tokens, while FastV and ToMe degrade severely under aggressive pruning (64.1% and 61.4% at 32 tokens vs. FlashVLM's 92.8%). On video QA (Table 2), FlashVLM consistently edges out both FastV and VisPruner at all three compression levels, achieving the highest average accuracy (48.7%, 47.1%, 44.7%) and slightly exceeding the unpruned upper-bound score at 77.8% pruning. In the efficiency comparison (Table 4), FlashVLM attains the lowest FLOPs (11.3T at 640 tokens; 3.6T at 160 tokens) and latency (115 ms; 74 ms) among all methods, confirming that its single-shot encoder-decoder design avoids the repeated attention recomputation overhead of layer-wise sparsification approaches like FastV and SparseVLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashVLM carves out a distinct niche within the visual token reduction literature by departing from both text-agnostic pruning (ToMe, VisionZip, VisPruner) and attention-map-based text-guided pruning (FastV, SparseVLM). Rather than extracting relevance from deep self- or cross-attention weights—which the authors argue are unstable under aggressive pruning—it computes an explicit dot-product similarity between projected visual tokens and context-gated text embeddings directly in the LLM space, operating as a single-shot selection at the encoder-decoder boundary. The log-domain geometric fusion of intrinsic saliency with extrinsic query relevance, combined with a diversity-preserving bipartite pruning step, distinguishes it from prior methods that either collapse focus onto anchor objects or require per-layer architectural modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in a plug-and-play, FlashAttention-compatible token selector that reduces FLOPs and latency without modifying transformer internals, making it immediately deployable across mainstream VLMs (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, InternVL, CogVLM) for both image and video tasks. The 'beyond-lossless' result—slightly exceeding unpruned accuracy while discarding up to 77.8% of tokens—suggests that query-irrelevant visual tokens actively harm reasoning, a finding with broader implications for how VLMs should handle redundant inputs. Open questions remain: the method's single-shot design lacks multi-step refinement or temporal feedback, performance depends on projected embedding quality for fine-grained tasks, and pure logical negation in queries remains challenging. Whether the modest margins over VisPruner persist at higher resolutions or longer video contexts is an important direction for future validation.
