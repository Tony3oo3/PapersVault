## Problem

LLM agents fail in long-horizon interactive tasks because their internal world models diverge from true environment dynamics, causing compounding simulation errors that invalidate multi-step plans. Additionally, standard multi-turn RL for LLMs suffers from high-variance value estimation due to the prohibitively slow autoregressive sample generation, leading to training instability. The paper addresses both gaps: grounding the agent's lookahead reasoning in real environmental feedback and stabilizing policy optimization with a low-variance critic.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261485/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decision step, the agent receives the current environment state (e.g., a 4×4 tile grid for 2048 or a 2D map with walls/boxes for Sokoban) and produces a structured reasoning chain followed by a discrete action. The reasoning chain follows a strict causal format—Observation → Analysis → Conclusion—and must explicitly simulate multiple future trajectories, compare their outcomes, and justify why rejected actions were suboptimal (counterfactual reasoning). A special <BACKTRACK> token allows the agent to revert to a previous state during the data-generation phase, enabling self-correction within the deliberation loop.

## Method

ProAct is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (GLAD) performs environment-driven MCTS from each state to collect N trajectories of depth T, feeds these raw futures into the LLM for analysis and decision-making, then compresses the verbose search traces into concise natural-language reasoning chains via a teacher model following four principles (format simplification, explicit causal logic, future-trend estimation, and diversity preservation). SFT on these compressed chains teaches the model to hallucinate correct future trends without inference-time search. Stage 2 (MC-Critic) introduces a parameter-free value estimator that replaces the LLM policy with a random policy for Monte-Carlo rollouts, computing state values as the average discounted return over M trajectories in under 3 seconds (vs. 3–6 s per LLM step). This low-variance value signal is plugged into PPO (as a weighted blend with a learned turn-level critic) or GRPO (replacing immediate step-rewards with action-value estimates), stabilizing multi-turn policy-gradient training and incentivizing long-term lookahead over myopic single-step gains.

## Results

On the standard 2048 (4×4) benchmark, the 4B ProAct model (Base+GLAD+MC-Critic) achieves a cumulative tile merge score of 4503.8, surpassing GPT-5 (4040.0) and all open-source baselines (best: Qwen3-30B-A3B at 838.7). On the 3072 variant, ProAct scores 6013.7, outperforming Doubao-Seed-1.8 (4210.0) but trailing GPT-5 (6962.0). On Sokoban (Unseen levels), ProAct achieves 0.94 average successful box pushes, a 2.4× improvement over the base Qwen3-4B (0.39) but below GPT-5 (1.89) and Doubao-Seed-1.8 (1.80). The GLAD SFT stage alone lifts 2048 4×4 from 721.3 to 3335.3, and adding MC-Critic RL further improves it to 4503.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProAct (4B) decisively outperforms all open-source baselines: on 2048 4×4 it scores 4503.8 versus 838.7 (Qwen3-30B-A3B) and 721.3 (Qwen3-4B base), a 5.4× improvement over the same-size base model. Against closed-source models, ProAct beats GPT-5 (4040.0) on standard 2048 4×4 and Doubao-Seed-1.8 (4210.0) on the 3072 variant (6013.7), but trails GPT-5 on 2048 3×3 (464.4 vs. 1184.0), 2048 3072 (6013.7 vs. 6962.0), and all Sokoban variants (e.g., 0.94 vs. 1.89 on Unseen). Doubao-Seed-1.8 edges out ProAct on 2048 4×4 (4662.7 vs. 4503.8) and 3×3 (545.3 vs. 464.4). On Sokoban, ProAct's 0.94 (Unseen) roughly doubles the base model's 0.39 but remains well below GPT-5 (1.89) and Doubao-Seed-1.8 (1.80), indicating that the 4B model's planning depth is still limited on deterministic, sparse-reward tasks. Overall, the 4B ProAct model is competitive with the strongest closed-source systems on 2048 while remaining the best-performing open-source model across all six evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProAct sits at the intersection of three active research threads: multi-turn agentic RL (AgentGym-RL, RAGEN, Turn-PPO), reasoning distillation from System 2 search to System 1 policies (Tree of Thoughts, STaR, VAGEN), and value estimation for sparse-reward LLM agents (ArCHer, SWEET-RL). Its key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) GLAD compresses MCTS search trees into concise natural-language reasoning chains for SFT, rather than cloning verbose search traces or training explicit world models; and (2) MC-Critic replaces the standard parametric critic network with a parameter-free Monte-Carlo estimator that uses a *random* policy as a rollout surrogate, sidestepping the sample-efficiency bottleneck of LLM-based value estimation. The framework is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play augmentation to existing PPO/GRPO pipelines rather than a new optimization algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, parameter-free nature of MC-Critic makes it immediately applicable to any multi-turn agentic RL setup that requires state-value estimation, potentially reducing the engineering burden of training and stabilizing separate critic networks. However, evaluation is confined to two relatively simple games (2048, Sokoban) with small action spaces, leaving open whether the random-policy surrogate and the GLAD compression strategy transfer to richer, higher-dimensional environments (e.g., web navigation, robotics, or open-ended dialogue). The finding that a 4B model can rival much larger closed-source systems on these tasks is encouraging but may reflect the narrowness of the benchmark rather than a general scaling property. A key open question is whether the MC-Critic's advantage over parametric critics persists as environment complexity and trajectory length grow substantially beyond the tested settings.
