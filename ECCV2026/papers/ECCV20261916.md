## Problem

Existing adversarial concept erasure methods for diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off between erasure robustness and computational cost. The paper identifies the root cause: adversarial optimization that uses a small number of random samples to approximate the adversarial objective produces low-quality adversarial embeddings that fail to accurately capture the target concept space, forcing practitioners to either increase sample count (and cost) or accept incomplete erasure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261916/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target concept prompt, a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (SDv1.4 or SDXL), and a set of anchor prompts generated by GPT-4 (keywords that co-occur with the target concept but are unrelated to it). It outputs a modified diffusion model in which only the CLIP text encoder has been fine-tuned, completing erasure in approximately 4 minutes. Because only the text encoder is updated, the erased model can be transferred to other diffusion architectures sharing the same CLIP encoder without retraining.

## Method

S-GRACE consists of two components operating in an iterative adversarial erasure loop (4 iterations). First, Semantic-Guided Adversarial Optimization uses a single noisy latent sample per optimization step (rather than resampling) but augments the adversarial objective with a CLIP-based semantic similarity prior that constrains the adversarial embedding to remain close to the target concept embedding, compensating for the information loss from using only one sample. Second, Semantic-Guided Concept Erasure fine-tunes the CLIP text encoder with a three-term loss: it pushes the encoder's output on adversarial embeddings away from the original target concept representation, pulls them toward the frozen CLIP representation of the adversarial embedding itself (automatically selecting a semantically similar surrogate without manual curation), and preserves anchor prompt representations to maintain non-target generation utility. This design avoids backpropagation through the noise predictor, drastically reducing the computational graph and enabling the 4-minute erasure time.

## Results

On NSFW concept erasure (Nudity), S-GRACE achieves the lowest average ASR of 8.80% across four attack types (Prompt, P4D, RAB, UD), compared to 17.78% for the next-best method CPE and 48.77% for ESD, while maintaining a CLIP-Score of 29.44 and FID of 15.01. On artistic style erasure (Van Gogh), S-GRACE achieves 0.00% average ASR with the best CLIP-Score (31.22) and lowest FID (13.87) among all methods achieving complete erasure. On object erasure (Garbage Truck), S-GRACE achieves 0.00% average ASR with CLIP-Score 30.65 and FID 15.84, outperforming all baselines on robustness while preserving utility. S-GRACE completes erasure in approximately 4 minutes per concept.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-GRACE achieves the lowest average ASR across all three erasure scenarios (NSFW, artistic styles, objects), outperforming every adversarial concept erasure baseline. Against ESD (non-adversarial), S-GRACE reduces average ASR from 48.77% to 8.80% on Nudity, a 5.5× improvement, at a small CLIP-Score cost (29.44 vs. 30.12). Among adversarial methods, S-GRACE halves the average ASR of the next-best baselines CPE (17.78%) and STEREO (17.96%) on Nudity, and achieves 0.00% vs. 0.50% for Receler on Garbage Truck. On Van Gogh erasure, S-GRACE ties Receler and STEREO at 0.00% average ASR but delivers the best utility (CLIP 31.22, FID 13.87 vs. 30.87/15.07 and 30.76/16.19 respectively). On SDXL, S-GRACE reduces Nudity prompt ASR from 11.33% (SDXL) to 5.42%, outperforming ESD (10.27%) while maintaining comparable CLIP-Score (35.67 vs. 35.95). The main trade-off is a modest CLIP-Score reduction (~1–2 points) relative to the strongest-utility baselines like CPE and Receler, reflecting the cost of more thorough erasure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial concept erasure framework established by AdvUnlearn (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2024) and R.A.C.E (Kim et al., ECCV 2024), which iteratively optimize adversarial embeddings to uncover residual concept representations and then erase them. The key departure is identifying the Monte Carlo sampling approximation in adversarial optimization as the root cause of the robustness-cost trade-off, and proposing to substitute expensive multi-sample estimation with a semantic-guided prior derived from the CLIP text encoder's image-text knowledge. The adversarial objective itself is inherited from the zero-shot classifier property of diffusion models (Li et al., ICCV 2023), and the erasure stage follows the CLIP-text-encoder fine-tuning paradigm from AdvUnlearn, but augments it with automatic surrogate discovery and anchor-based utility preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: S-GRACE erases a concept in approximately 4 minutes while achieving the lowest average attack success rate across nine concepts spanning NSFW content, artistic styles, and objects, making robust concept erasure feasible at scale. The insight that CLIP's semantic space can serve as a free regularizer for adversarial optimization—replacing costly Monte Carlo sampling—is a reusable technique for other adversarial training settings in diffusion models. However, the method remains heuristic, inherits CLIP's biases, is vulnerable to the CCE embedding-level attack, and the precise boundary of surrogate mapping is uncharacterized, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to non-CLIP-based text encoders or more complex compositional concepts.
