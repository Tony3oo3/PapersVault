## Problem

Model-free 6D pose estimation of unseen objects remains limited to pairwise single-anchor matching, which fails under occlusion and large viewpoint changes where query-reference overlap is minimal. Model-based and reconstruction-first alternatives require CAD assets or dense posed captures, making them impractical for open-world deployment. The paper formalizes and addresses the underexplored regime of estimating pose from arbitrary sparse, unordered, and pose-free reference views without any onboarding stage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query image plus K reference views (one posed anchor and M unposed assist views, M≥0) as RGB or RGB-D inputs, pre-processed to 518×518. At inference, the system outputs the 6D object pose in a canonical anchor-centric coordinate frame, computed as T^Q = T^A · T_{Q→A}^{-1}. Intermediate outputs include per-view dense point maps, depth maps, and cross-view 3D correspondences; the assist views require no camera pose annotation and can be any number (the paper defaults to 8), with performance saturating beyond 4.

## Method

PANY builds on the VGGT multi-view transformer backbone (frozen, adapted via LoRA rank-8) to predict object-centric dense point maps and depth from all input views simultaneously, replacing scene-level view-dependent representations with canonical object-centric geometry. A geometry-aware matching head fuses per-view visual embeddings (from DINOv2) with geometric features extracted from predicted point maps via a rotation-invariant geometric transformer, producing 3D-aligned descriptors supervised by a local contrastive objective (bidirectional cross-entropy on cross-view attention matrices with nearest-neighbor correspondence labels). At inference, pose-free assist views are integrated through a pose-graph construction where edges connect views with sufficient valid correspondences (≥500 pairs), and local similarity transforms estimated via RANSAC-Umeyama are composed along graph paths to register all point clouds into the anchor-centric canonical frame before final Kabsch-Umeyama or PnP pose recovery. Training jointly optimizes L1 depth/point-map losses and the contrastive matching loss (weighted λ_d=1.0, λ_p=1.0, λ_m=0.05) on the Omni6DPose synthetic dataset (5000+ objects, 2M+ RGB-D images) for 15 epochs on two A100 GPUs.

## Results

On Real275 (single-reference), PANY achieves AR 59.3 / ADD-0.1d 27.8 in RGB mode and AR 81.8 / ADD-0.1d 89.3 in RGB-D mode, substantially outperforming the best prior model-free baselines (SingRef6D: AR 28.7; One2Any: AR 54.9). On YCB-Video, PANY with one anchor plus eight unposed assist views reaches ADD AUC 94.6, surpassing One2Any (84.4) by over 10 points. On the challenging LM-O benchmark, PANY (1+8 unposed) attains AR 42.3, exceeding model-based methods that use 57–520 posed CAD templates (FoundPose: 39.7, RayPose: 42.1) while remaining fully model-free.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANY decisively outperforms all single-reference model-free baselines on Real275: in RGB mode it nearly doubles the AR of SingRef6D (59.3 vs 28.7) and more than triples One2Any (59.3 vs 26.6); in RGB-D mode it leads One2Any by 26.9 AR points (81.8 vs 54.9) and Any6D by 30.8 points. On Toyota-Light RGB, PANY wins on AR (37.5 vs 31.7 for SingRef6D) but trails SingRef6D on ADD-0.1d (10.8 vs 13.1), the one metric where a baseline edges ahead. On YCB-Video, PANY with one anchor alone (ADD AUC 92.5) already surpasses One2Any (84.4) and FoundationPose (76.1), and adding eight unposed assist views pushes it to 94.6. On LM-O, PANY (1+8 unposed, AR 42.3) matches or exceeds model-based methods that require dozens to hundreds of posed CAD templates—RayPose (42.1 with 8 posed refs) and FoundPose (39.7 with 57 posed refs)—while being fully model-free and faster (0.75 s vs 1.7 s for FoundPose). Against the closest model-free competitor One2Any on LM-O, PANY nearly doubles the AR (42.3 vs 18.2 with single reference; 36.9 vs 18.2 even in single-ref mode).

## Relation to Prior Work

PANY builds directly on VGGT as a frozen geometry backbone (fine-tuned via LoRA), adapting a scene-level multi-view geometry foundation model to object-centric canonical alignment. It departs from the dominant pairwise model-free paradigm (One2Any, Any6D, Oryon) by replacing single-anchor matching with multi-view reasoning over sparse, unordered references, and from reconstruction-first pipelines (SfM/NeRF/3DGS) by inferring pose directly without explicit 3D model recovery. The cross-view contrastive matching head and pose-graph canonical registration for unposed assist views are the key architectural additions that distinguish PANY from both the geometry backbone it inherits and the prior model-free baselines it outperforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PANY addresses a practical gap in open-world robotics: estimating 6D pose of unseen objects without CAD models, onboarding stages, or dense posed captures, while remaining robust to occlusion and large viewpoint changes. The ability to leverage pose-free assist views as intermediates via pose-graph registration is a practical recipe for real deployment where only a handful of uncalibrated snapshots are available. Limitations remain for extreme occlusion and strongly symmetric objects where geometric cues alone cannot resolve orientation ambiguity, and the method's reliance on a frozen VGGT backbone with LoRA may constrain further architectural improvements. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large numbers of assist views, whether it can operate in real-time on embedded hardware, and how it handles objects with minimal texture or repetitive geometry.
