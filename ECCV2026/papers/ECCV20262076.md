## Problem

Autoregressive KV cache in MLLMs grows linearly with streaming video length, making long-horizon video understanding infeasible on a single GPU. Existing training-free compression methods apply a uniform eviction or retrieval strategy to all attention heads within a layer, ignoring that heads naturally specialize into distinct temporal roles (instantaneous, short-term, episodic) with very different receptive fields. This paper reveals this head-level temporal heterogeneity and exploits it for head-aware, heterogeneous cache management.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames in a strictly streaming, frame-by-frame fashion: each incoming frame is encoded and prefilled into the model sequentially, with no access to future frames. A text query (e.g., a question about the video) is appended after the visual stream to trigger autoregressive decoding. The output is a standard text response, while the internal KV cache is maintained under a fixed per-head budget (default: 64 global + 16 streaming + 4 ultra-short frames), ensuring constant memory regardless of total video duration.

## Method

H²SVC is a training-free KV cache compression framework built on two components. First, an offline Spatio-temporal Attention Profiling (SAP) computes Vision-to-Vision and Text-to-Vision Temporal Attention Distance (TAD) scores for every KV head, which are combined into a unified Temporal Receptive Score to classify heads into instantaneous (bottom 10%), short-term (middle 40%), and episodic (top 50%) categories; this classification is input-agnostic and frozen after a one-time profiling pass. Second, during inference each head's KV states are statically routed to a dedicated memory bank: an ultra-short FIFO window for instantaneous heads, a standard sliding-window cache for short-term heads, and a bounded global cache for episodic heads. The global cache employs Semantic Trajectory Curvature Eviction (STCE), which models the sequence of Value states as a geometric trajectory and scores each frame by angular curvature, magnitude gating, and temporal span, evicting predictable (low-curvature) frames while preserving sharp semantic inflection points. The entire pipeline operates on raw Key and Value vectors without materializing the attention matrix, maintaining full compatibility with Flash Attention kernels.

## Results

On the primary online benchmarks, H²SVC achieves 77.5 on StreamingBench and 62.6 on OVO-Bench (average) with LLaVA-Video-7B, outperforming the full-context offline baseline (75.4 / 57.8) and all training-free offline-to-online methods. With Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it reaches 77.0 / 60.3, surpassing InfiniPot-V (76.4 / 56.8) and LiveVLM (73.2 / 56.3). On offline benchmarks, H²SVC matches or slightly exceeds full-cache performance (e.g., 65.1 vs. 64.7 on MLVU-dev with LLaVA-OV-7B), while reducing KV cache memory by up to 75% and maintaining stable ~20.5 FPS throughput regardless of video length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H²SVC consistently outperforms all training-free offline-to-online baselines: on LLaVA-OV-7B it beats LiveVLM by +1.0 on StreamingBench and +7.5 on OVO-Bench average (61.0 vs. 53.5), and beats StreamKV by +5.1 on StreamingBench. Against the strongest dedicated streaming model StreamForest, H²SVC gains +4.4 on OVO-Bench average (61.0 vs. 56.6) and +2.5 on ODV-Bench. Notably, H²SVC surpasses the full-context offline baseline on OVO-Bench Backward (56.3 vs. 43.4 for LLaVA-OV; 58.0 vs. 52.7 for LLaVA-Video), indicating that semantic curvature eviction selectively preserves long-range episodic events better than retaining all frames. On offline benchmarks, H²SVC matches or slightly exceeds full-cache performance (e.g., 65.1 vs. 64.7 MLVU-dev, 59.0 vs. 58.4 Video-MME with LLaVA-OV), confirming minimal degradation from compression. On RVS, H²SVC leads all training-free methods (58.9 Acc / 4.0 Score on RVS-Ego; 54.2 / 3.6 on RVS-Movie), though it trails the upper-bound ReKV with offline retrieval (63.7 / 4.0) which uses query-aware retrieval not available in streaming.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in training-free streaming video KV cache compression (ReKV, LiveVLM, StreamKV, Flash-VStream), which applies a uniform eviction or retrieval strategy to all attention heads within a layer. It draws conceptually from sparse-attention work in LLMs (StreamingLLM's attention sinks, H2O's heavy-hitter eviction) but re-frames the problem around a newly identified empirical regularity: heads in MLLMs exhibit input-agnostic temporal specialization into instantaneous, short-term, and episodic roles. The Spatio-temporal Attention Profiling (SAP) methodology and the resulting head-aware heterogeneous routing represent a new design axis that prior video-specific compression methods did not exploit, while the STCE eviction strategy repurposes geometric trajectory analysis (curvature-based importance) from a video-specific context rather than relying on attention-weight matrices incompatible with Flash Attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: H²SVC enables effectively unbounded streaming video on a single GPU with ~4× VRAM reduction and stable throughput, making real-time surveillance or broadcast applications feasible without architectural retraining. The input-agnostic head specialization finding is a reusable empirical insight that could inform future model design or other compression settings beyond video. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B backbones, a somewhat arbitrary 10/40/50 head split, and the need for an offline profiling pass on ~50 videos before deployment. Open questions include whether the head taxonomy generalizes to larger or differently-architected MLLMs (e.g., MoE, different GQA groupings), and whether the STCE geometric heuristic remains optimal under very different frame rates or domain shifts.
