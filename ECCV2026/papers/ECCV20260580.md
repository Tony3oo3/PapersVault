## Problem

Latent tokenizers in modern generative pipelines systematically under-reconstruct high-frequency details (textures, edges, fine structures) while excelling at low-frequency structure, because joint optimization over all spatial frequencies creates an implicit bias toward coarse content. This frequency optimization coupling leads to overly smoothed reconstructions and degraded perceptual realism in textured regions, a limitation that persists across both spatial and spectral tokenizer architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260580/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (256×256 for ImageNet-1K, 1024×1024 for FFHQ) and first applies a Haar wavelet DWT to split it into low- and high-frequency components before encoding. The latent embedding is a 16×16×32 tensor structured as a channel-wise concatenation of a low-frequency code z_L and a high-frequency code z_H. At generation time, a class label conditions LightningDiT to sample from the unified latent, which is then split, decoded by separate decoders, and fused via inverse wavelet transform to produce the final image.

## Method

DeBaT explicitly decouples frequency-band learning by applying a Haar wavelet DWT to the input and routing the low- and high-frequency components through two independent encoder-decoder pairs (E_L, D_L) and (E_H, D_H), each learning its own latent embedding. The low-frequency branch is trained with the full VA-VAE objective (reconstruction, KL, DINOv2 foundation-model alignment, adversarial, and LPIPS losses), while the high-frequency branch uses a deliberately simpler objective (reconstruction, KL, and adversarial only) to avoid inheriting biases from pretrained models trained on full-spectrum images. At inference, both decoders reconstruct their respective bands and an inverse wavelet transform fuses them into the final image. For generation, the two latent codes are concatenated channel-wise into a unified representation on which LightningDiT performs diffusion, then the sampled latent is split and decoded independently before wavelet recombination. This decoupling eliminates the spectral optimization coupling that causes low frequencies to dominate, yielding substantially lower high-frequency reconstruction error (0.0020 vs. 0.0074 for VA-VAE) and better generative FID scores.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K class-conditional generation with LightningDiT (64 epochs, 675M params), DeBaT achieves a gFID of 1.52 with CFG (3.24 without), substantially outperforming the VA-VAE baseline (2.11 / 5.14) and all other latent diffusion and autoregressive methods. In reconstruction, DeBaT achieves a high-frequency loss of 0.0020 and overall reconstruction loss of 0.0044, compared to 0.0074 and 0.0105 for VA-VAE, with a PSNR of 30.03 dB. On FFHQ at 1024² resolution, DeBaT improves PSNR from 33.84 to 34.27 dB and gFID from 6.92 to 6.86 over VA-VAE, confirming scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeBaT outperforms its direct baseline VA-VAE across all generation metrics when paired with LightningDiT: gFID improves from 2.11 to 1.52, IS from 252.3 to 317.4, precision from 0.81 to 0.83, and recall from 0.58 to 0.65 (with CFG). In reconstruction (Table 1), DeBaT achieves the best high-frequency loss (0.0020) among all 18 compared tokenizers, more than 3× lower than VA-VAE (0.0074) and 2.5× lower than the previous wavelet-based LiteVAE (0.0050), while also achieving the lowest overall reconstruction loss (0.0044) and rFID (0.2156). Among autoregressive models, DeBaT's gFID of 1.52 beats MAR (1.55), MagViT-v2 (1.78), and VAR (1.80), though VAR achieves a higher IS (365.4) and MDTv2 a slightly lower gFID (1.58) at the cost of lower precision (0.79). Against latent diffusion baselines using SD-VAE, DeBaT achieves the best gFID (1.52 vs. 1.42 for REPA, 1.58 for MDTv2) while also leading in precision (0.83) and recall (0.65). In the efficiency comparison (Table 5), DeBaT achieves 30.03 dB PSNR with 137.91M parameters, outperforming WF-VAE (29.00 dB, 317.08M params) in both quality and efficiency, while using less GPU memory (5892 MB vs. 9571 MB) and faster inference (19.97 ms/img vs. 36.39 ms/img).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeBaT builds directly on the VA-VAE/LightningDiT framework [51], which aligns latent tokenizers with DINOv2 features for improved reconstruction and generation. It departs from prior wavelet-based tokenizers (WF-VAE, LiteVAE) that use wavelet transforms for compression but still optimize low- and high-frequency components jointly within a shared latent space. The key conceptual shift is allocating separate encoder-decoder pairs to each frequency band with independently optimized objectives, rather than treating wavelet decomposition as merely a preprocessing step. The paper also positions itself against the broader latent diffusion pipeline (LDM, DiT, SiT, etc.) by arguing that the tokenizer stage, not the generative model, is the primary bottleneck for high-frequency fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that high-frequency reconstruction error remains nearly constant across diverse tokenizer architectures—despite large gaps in overall reconstruction quality—provides a useful diagnostic that the field has lacked, suggesting frequency coupling (not model capacity) is the core bottleneck. Practically, DeBaT offers a drop-in tokenizer replacement that improves generative quality without modifying the diffusion backbone, making it immediately applicable to existing latent diffusion pipelines. Open questions include whether multi-stage wavelet decomposition (beyond the single Haar stage used here) yields further gains, whether the decoupling strategy transfers to discrete tokenizers or video generation, and whether the simpler high-frequency objective (lacking foundation-model alignment) is optimal or merely sufficient.
