## Problem

Existing 3D inpainting methods face a fundamental trade-off: 3D-first pipelines (e.g., 3DGIC, GScream) require pixel-accurate multi-view segmentation masks and are restricted to object removal, while 2D-first pipelines (e.g., MVInpainter) leverage flexible generative models but produce cross-view inconsistencies that corrupt downstream 3D reconstruction. No prior framework simultaneously supports arbitrary-shaped masks, diverse editing tasks (removal and insertion), and strict multi-view geometric consistency within an efficient explicit 3D representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images (e.g., 100 views per scene) with corresponding binary masks (segmentation or bounding-box) and, for insertion tasks, a text prompt. Internally, Stable Diffusion 2.0 processes 512×512 mask-centered crops, while DINO/FeatUp features are extracted at 256×256 for memory efficiency during guidance. The output is a refined Scaffold-GS-based 3D scene (approximately 2.5 hours per scene on a single RTX 4090) that renders photorealistic novel views, alongside the consistency-guided inpainted images used as training targets.

## Method

CoIn is a three-stage pipeline that alternates between 2D and 3D domains. First, Reference Adaptive GS with Feature Attention (Ref-GS) builds a coarse 3DGS scene by applying per-view sigmoid weights that up-weight a selected reference view and down-weight views whose photometric loss is increasing, combined with unidirectional anchor-feature attention that propagates texture from surrounding anchors into the inpainting region. Second, Consistency Loss Guidance (CLG) warps DINO high-frequency features and RGB from the reference view onto a GS-derived point cloud and reprojects them into an intermediate viewpoint (via linear translation interpolation and Slerp rotation), forming an energy function that steers the frozen latent diffusion inpainter at each denoising step. Third, a Texture-Enhancing Discriminator (TE-D) performs adversarial patch-level training (64×64 patches sampled near the inpainted region) to recover high-frequency details lost during guidance, while the GS generator is simultaneously supervised by the CLG-inpainted images for photometric agreement.

## Results

On SPIn-NeRF with segmentation masks, CoIn achieves m-LPIPS 0.032, LPIPS 0.23, m-FID 80.4, and FID 28.9, outperforming the best GS baseline GScream (m-FID 86.1, FID 31.2) and 3DGIC (m-LPIPS 0.028, FID 36.4). With bounding-box masks, CoIn maintains strong performance (m-LPIPS 0.033, FID 26.6) while 3DGIC degrades severely (FID 104.1), demonstrating robustness to mask shape. On the IMFine dataset, CoIn achieves LPIPS 0.1685, PSNR 23.88, and FID 53.37, surpassing IMFine (FID 57.53) and GScream (FID 112.91). For object insertion, CoIn attains CLIP_dir 0.1628, slightly exceeding Infusion (0.1589) and substantially outperforming Gaussian Editor (0.0222).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoIn outperforms all NeRF-based baselines (SPIn-NeRF, MVIP-NeRF, MALD-NeRF) by a wide margin on FID (28.9 vs. 44.7–50.5) and LPIPS (0.23 vs. 0.30–0.31) on SPIn-NeRF. Among GS-based methods, CoIn beats 3DGIC on LPIPS (0.23 vs. 0.26), m-FID (80.4 vs. 96.3), and FID (28.9 vs. 36.4) with segmentation masks, and dramatically outperforms it with bounding-box masks (FID 26.6 vs. 104.1). CoIn surpasses GScream on LPIPS (0.23 vs. 0.26), m-FID (80.4 vs. 86.1), and FID (28.9 vs. 31.2) under segmentation masks, and on all four metrics under bounding-box masks. On the IMFine dataset, CoIn edges out IMFine (LPIPS 0.1685 vs. 0.1747, FID 53.37 vs. 57.53) and substantially outperforms GScream (FID 53.37 vs. 112.91) and 3DGIC (FID 53.37 vs. 200.99). For object insertion, CoIn achieves the highest CLIP_dir (0.1628) and wins the user study on text-prompt alignment (59.1%) and artifact-free rendering (45.4%), surpassing both Infusion and Gaussian Editor.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoIn positions itself at the intersection of two established but conflicting paradigms in 3D scene inpainting: 3D-first pipelines (3DGIC, GScream, IMFine) that reconstruct a 3DGS scene before editing but require precise multi-view segmentation masks and are limited to object removal, and 2D-first pipelines (MVInpainter, Instant3dit) that leverage the generative flexibility of diffusion models but struggle with cross-view consistency. The method builds directly on Scaffold-GS's anchor-based structure to enable 3D feature attention, adapts FreeDoM's training-free energy guidance to incorporate GS-derived geometry, and uses Stable Diffusion 2.0 as the frozen 2D inpainting backbone. The key conceptual departure is the bidirectional 2D→3D→2D→3D information flow: 2D inpainting results seed a coarse 3DGS, which then guides subsequent 2D denoising via GS-based Reference Feature Warping, and finally an adversarial discriminator refines the 3D scene for texture fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoIn offers a practical, unified framework for 3D scene inpainting that handles both object removal and insertion under arbitrary-shaped masks, addressing a real limitation of prior 3D-first methods that were architecturally restricted to removal. The demonstrated robustness to mask shape (small performance gap between segmentation and bounding-box masks, unlike baselines) has direct practical value since obtaining pixel-accurate multi-view segmentations is often infeasible. However, the evaluation is limited to two relatively small datasets (10 and 20 scenes), the pipeline still requires ~2.5 hours per scene on a single GPU, and the reliance on a single reference view may limit scalability to very large or highly dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether the framework extends to 4D (temporal) inpainting, whether the Scaffold-GS anchor structure is strictly necessary versus vanilla 3DGS, and how the method behaves under extreme viewpoint changes beyond the 180° coverage tested.
