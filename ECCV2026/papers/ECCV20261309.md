## Problem

Most end-to-end autonomous driving policies are short-horizon and reactive, relying exclusively on a few seconds of instantaneous sensor observations. This limits them to purely reactive behavior without the anticipatory visual-spatial foresight that human drivers draw from prior experience, and renders them highly vulnerable to sensor corruption such as occlusion or weather degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261309/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The module is model-agnostic and operates on the intermediate state of any existing E2E planner (BEV features, image features, or scene tokens depending on the base model), augmented with N=20 retrieved geospatial visual priors—SigLIP2-encoded street-level images anchored to relative (x,y) coordinates along the intended route, spanning ~100 m of look-ahead. The output is an enhanced driving state that replaces the original intermediate representation before the downstream trajectory decoder, with no change to the final trajectory format.

## Method

PriorEye introduces a memory augmentation module built around a dual-memory architecture inspired by Titans. A pre-built memory bank stores SigLIP2 embeddings of street-level images sampled at 5 m intervals along lane centerlines; at inference, intention-guided retrieval (DFS over the lane connectivity graph conditioned on the navigational command) selects relevant visual-spatial pairs. These form a contextual memory C = φ(V) + ψ(X) combining projected visual embeddings with 2D sinusoidal positional encodings, while a set of K learnable persistent tokens P serves as a fallback reference. Cross-attention with the current driving state as queries and [P; C] as keys/values produces a memory-attended state, which is then blended via an adaptive gate G = σ(f_g([S; S̃; d; c])) that modulates memory influence based on Euclidean distance and cosine similarity between the original and attended states. The final gated residual update S' = S + G ⊙ S̃ is fed to the downstream decoder, and the entire module (713K parameters) is jointly trained with the base model's original objective without additional loss terms.

## Results

On the NAVSIM-v2 navhard-two-stage benchmark, PriorEye consistently improves all four E2E baselines in EPDMS: LTF from 24.7 to 32.4 (+31.2%), GTRS-DP from 26.3 to 30.1 (+14.4%), GTRS-Dense from 44.9 to 48.6 (+8.2%), and DrivoR from 48.9 to 49.6 (+1.4%). On the navtest split, gains are 2.5, 0.3, 3.4, and 2.7 EPDMS points respectively. Under heavy sensor corruption (mud), the method reduces performance degradation from 53.2% to 32.5%, and under corrupted geospatial priors, the dual-memory design limits degradation to 6.8% versus 19.6% for a context-only variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriorEye is evaluated as a plug-in module on four E2E baselines spanning regression (LTF/TransFuser), diffusion (GTRS-DP), and trajectory-scoring (GTRS-Dense, DrivoR) paradigms. The largest relative gain is on LTF (+31.2% EPDMS on navhard-two-stage), where the lightweight regression baseline benefits most from the additional visual-spatial context. GTRS-DP improves by 14.4%, GTRS-Dense by 8.2%, and DrivoR by 1.4%, with the diminishing returns reflecting that stronger baselines already extract more from instantaneous observations. On the navtest split, all four baselines improve in absolute EPDMS (by 0.3–3.4 points), with GTRS-Dense showing the largest absolute gain (85.4→88.8). The module adds only 713K parameters (≤1.7% overhead) and 9.3 ms inference latency, confirming its model-agnostic, lightweight integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the dual-memory decomposition from Titans (contextual + persistent memory) and the persistent-memory tokens of Sukhbaatar et al. into the autonomous driving domain, repurposing them as a retrieval-augmented state-editing module for E2E planners. It departs from prior driving-prior work (spatial priors for HD map construction, visual priors for scene context) by coupling street-level visual embeddings with route-level spatial anchors into a single 'geospatial visual prior' representation and injecting it directly into the planning pipeline rather than an upstream perception task. The model-agnostic cross-attention + adaptive-gate design is inspired by local inference enhancement techniques in NLP, but its application to driving state tokens is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a lightweight (~713K params, <2% overhead) plug-in that consistently improves four architecturally diverse E2E baselines and, more importantly, provides a sensor-independent information channel that substantially reduces performance degradation under camera corruption (e.g., 53%→33% drop under heavy mud). The dual-memory fallback mechanism offers a principled safety net when retrieved priors are stale or misaligned, addressing a real deployment concern. Open questions include whether the static memory bank can be updated online with driving logs, whether appearance-based retrieval (beyond ego-location) would improve robustness in unfamiliar or rapidly changing environments, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the NAVSIM-v2/nuPlan geographic coverage.
