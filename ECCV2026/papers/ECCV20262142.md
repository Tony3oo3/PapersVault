## Problem

Driving video generation via flow matching or diffusion models requires dozens to hundreds of sampling steps because the source distribution is standard Gaussian noise, forcing the model to regenerate deterministic scene structures (roads, buildings, vegetation) from scratch for every frame. This ignores the strong spatiotemporal and geometric correlations inherent in driving videos, leading to high inference latency and temporal inconsistency (texture flickering, geometric drift) especially in few-step regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference frame (256×448) along with camera extrinsics/intrinsics (SE(3) transforms), 3D bounding boxes for dynamic agents, and a BEV road map as control conditions, and produces a 16-frame, six-camera multi-view driving video. Generation proceeds autoregressively in chunks of L=6 (1 reference + 5 new frames), with the reference frame's metric depth and latent features explicitly warped to future poses to construct the source distribution rather than sampling from pure noise.

## Method

GeoFlow replaces the standard Gaussian source distribution with a Geometry-Aligned Prior (GAP) constructed in VAE latent space: the reference frame is depth-estimated, encoded, unprojected into a 3D feature point cloud, transformed to the target pose via ego-motion, and rendered back to 2D via Z-buffer feature splatting. A Spatially-Adaptive Noise Injection strategy then blends this warped prior with Gaussian noise using a pixel-wise reliability mask that aggregates occlusion (M_occ), dynamic-object (M_dyn), and depth-uncertainty (M_unc) signals, preserving reliable geometric regions while allowing the model to freely regenerate unreliable areas. The flow matching backbone is fine-tuned (≈30 H100 GPU-hours, lr 5e-5) to learn the vector field between this new source and the target data, yielding a significantly shorter and straighter transport path that achieves SOTA FVD in 8 steps versus 40 for the baseline.

## Results

On the NuScenes validation set, GeoFlow achieves FVD 32.5 and FID 6.8 using only 15 sampling steps, outperforming the OpenDWM baseline (FVD 38.8) which requires 40 steps. Notably, GeoFlow at just 8 steps (FVD 38.6) already matches the 40-step baseline, representing a 5× reduction in inference steps. In the few-step regime (5 steps), GeoFlow reduces FVD from 123.5 (OpenDWM) to 49.2, a 60% improvement. The method also demonstrates generality, improving 5-step FVD by 38–63% across different base architectures (OpenDWM-tvae, OpenDWM-vae, UniMLVG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoFlow is compared against six baselines on the NuScenes validation set. Against its direct baseline OpenDWM (same architecture, Gaussian initialization), GeoFlow achieves a lower FVD (32.5 vs. 38.8) while using only 15 steps instead of 40, and matches its FID (6.8). Against UniMLVG, which uses 3 reference frames and 50 steps, GeoFlow achieves a better FVD (32.5 vs. 36.1) with fewer steps and a single reference frame, though UniMLVG has a slightly lower FID (5.8 vs. 6.8). GeoFlow substantially outperforms DriveDreamer-2 (FVD 55.7), MagicDrive-V2 (FVD 94.8), DreamForge (FVD 103.6), and Drive-WM (FVD 122.7) on FVD. The most striking comparison is in the few-step regime: GeoFlow at 8 steps (FVD 38.6) surpasses OpenDWM at 40 steps (FVD 38.8), and at 5 steps GeoFlow reduces FVD by 60% over the baseline (49.2 vs. 123.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoFlow sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) driving video generation via flow matching (MagicDrive, DriveDreamer, OpenDWM, UniMLVG), (2) non-Gaussian source distributions in generative modeling (Diffusion Bridges, Video Bi-flow), and (3) geometry-conditioned video synthesis (Gen3C, ViewCrafter, GeoDrive). The key conceptual departure is from thread (3): prior geometry-constrained works use 3D information as a *conditioning signal* while still traversing the full noise-to-data trajectory, whereas GeoFlow uses multi-view geometry to *construct the source distribution itself*, fundamentally shortening the transport path. It also diverges from Video Bi-flow, which simply adds noise to the previous frame, by explicitly warping latent features through metric depth and ego-motion to produce a geometrically aligned prior with spatially-adaptive noise injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving data pipelines: a 5× reduction in inference steps (40→8) with comparable FVD, achievable with only ~30 H100 GPU hours of fine-tuning and no architectural changes, makes high-throughput synthetic data generation and interactive closed-loop simulation far more tractable. The plug-and-play nature (validated across 2D-VAE, 3D-VAE, and UniMLVG backbones) suggests broad applicability to existing driving video generators. Open questions include how the method scales to longer horizons beyond the L=6 chunk constraint, its behavior in highly dynamic or adversarial scenes where geometric warping degrades, and whether the geometry-aligned prior idea transfers to non-driving video domains with weaker spatiotemporal structure.
