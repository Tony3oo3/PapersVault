## Problem

Capturing high-speed polarization video is fundamentally challenging: DoFP polarization cameras are limited to low frame rates causing motion blur, while snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) only recovers intensity. Directly combining SCI with DoFP sensing creates a tightly coupled inverse problem where temporal multiplexing is entangled with spatial polarization mosaicing, and naive two-stage pipelines (reconstruct then demosaic) propagate and amplify errors through nonlinear Stokes-to-AoP/DoP transformations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2048×2048 DoFP polarized snapshot (decomposed into four 1024×1024 sub-measurements at 0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) and the known DMD mask sequence as input, and produces a full-resolution polarization video of T frames (e.g., 8 frames at compression ratio 8) with per-frame intensities at all four polarization angles, from which S0, DoP, and AoP are derived. The physical system operates at 10 fps camera rate, yielding an effective temporal sampling rate of 10×Cr fps (80 fps at Cr=8), with each 4×4 DMD micromirror block aligned to four 2×2 DoFP macro-pixels on the sensor.

## Method

Pol-CACTI is a hardware-software co-designed framework: the hardware integrates a DMD-based CACTI encoder with a DoFP polarization camera (Sony IMX250MZR), while the software employs a two-stage coarse-to-fine CNN. Stage 1 uses EfficientSCI as a backbone to reconstruct the raw DoFP mosaic sequence from the snapshot; Stage 2 applies a refinement module with five residual blocks for high-frequency polarization structure recovery, followed by the key Polarization-Aware Learnable Back-Projection (PA-LBP) module, which re-projects the predicted demosaiced estimate back to the polarization component space via a learnable strided convolution, computes the discrepancy with the raw mosaic, and back-projects this error to enforce measurement consistency. Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule (backbone pre-training, demosaicing branch training, end-to-end fine-tuning) with a physics-constrained multi-term loss combining Charbonnier losses on raw channels, S0, and DoP, plus a DoP-weighted cosine difference loss for AoP that respects π-periodicity and suppresses gradients in unpolarized regions.

## Results

On the simulated Pol-CACTI test set at compression ratio Cr=8, Pol-CACTI achieves 42.122 dB S0 PSNR, 0.987 S0 SSIM, 31.327 dB DoP PSNR, 0.874 DoP SSIM, and 7.279° AoP MAE, outperforming all baselines on every metric. As compression increases to Cr=48, performance degrades gracefully (S0 PSNR drops to 33.507 dB, AoP MAE rises to 10.213°) while still maintaining the best scores across all competing methods. On real captured scenes (Panda, Ball), the method produces sharper DoP/AoP boundaries and reduced temporal flickering compared to alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pol-CACTI wins decisively over all four baselines on every metric at every compression ratio. The largest gaps appear in polarization-specific metrics: Polanalyser (a single-image demosaicing tool) lags by 5.9 dB in DoP PSNR and 9.7° in AoP MAE, while PUGDiff (a diffusion-based demosaicer) trails by 5.1 dB in DoP PSNR and 9.7° in AoP MAE. SwinCPD is the closest competitor on S0 PSNR (35.923 vs. 42.122 dB, a 6.2 dB gap) and AoP MAE (9.537° vs. 7.279°), but still loses on all five metrics. PIDSR sits in the middle, losing 4.7 dB in S0 PSNR and 7.0° in AoP MAE. Notably, the simple SCI Interpolation baseline is competitive on DoP PSNR (31.103 vs. 31.327 dB) but falls behind on S0 PSNR (5.8 dB gap) and AoP MAE, highlighting that the joint reconstruction–demosaicing design is what drives the polarization gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established lines: video snapshot compressive imaging (SCI/CACTI) for recovering high-speed video from a single coded measurement, and DoFP polarization demosaicing for recovering full-resolution Stokes parameters from a micro-polarizer mosaic. It explicitly builds on EfficientSCI as the temporal reconstruction backbone and on CACTI's coded-aperture hardware encoding, but departs from prior work by arguing that the two stages—temporal inversion and polarization demosaicing—cannot be treated sequentially without severe error propagation. The PA-LBP module and the DoP-weighted cosine AoP loss are novel architectural and loss-function contributions that enforce measurement consistency and physical validity simultaneously, rather than treating demosaicing as a post-hoc image restoration step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first working hardware prototype for high-speed polarized video SCI and a large-scale dynamic polarization video dataset (8,889 frames at 2048×2048) that fills a clear gap, since existing polarization datasets are predominantly static. The ablation showing the serial cascade collapsing to ~16 dB versus ~40 dB for the joint model provides strong empirical evidence that decoupled pipelines are fundamentally inadequate for this coupled inverse problem. Limitations include the prototype's modest 10 fps base rate, the resolution mismatch between the 1920×1080 DMD and 2048×2048 sensor, and evaluation only on a single self-collected dataset; open questions include scalability to higher frame rates, real-time inference, and generalization to other polarimetric configurations (e.g., circular polarization, multi-spectral).
