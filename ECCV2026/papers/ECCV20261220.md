## Problem

Existing Next-Best-View (NBV) methods for robotic grasping assume that a single observation at an arbitrary camera-object distance suffices for accurate grasp prediction, overlooking that perception quality is highly sensitive to viewing distance and that the optimal distance varies across object categories and scales. Additionally, VLM-based semantic scoring incurs second-level inference latency, making it impractical to force a single heavy model to handle both global occlusion resolution and high-frequency local decision-making during manipulator approach.

## Input / Output

The system consumes accumulated RGB-D observations for scene reconstruction, a text query specifying the target object, and during approach, per-frame RGB-D observations with measured camera-object distance. The distance-aware scorer operates on a 1035-dimensional feature vector combining 512-dim RGB features, 10-dim depth statistics, a normalized scalar distance, and a 512-dim CLIP text embedding of the object category (enabling generalization to unseen categories). Outputs include the selected next-best-view SE(3) camera pose, a 6-DOF grasp pose predicted from the most informative observation, and a calibrated grasp success probability in [0,1] used for online stopping decisions.

## Method

DiNBV-Grasp decouples NBV planning into two stages with distinct computational budgets. Stage I distills Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct into a DINOv3-Base student (28 ms/frame vs. 693 ms for the teacher) to score candidate rendered views via a visibility-adaptive utility combining geometric information gain, semantic relevance, and execution feasibility, iteratively selecting the next-best-view until target visibility exceeds a threshold. Stage II introduces a lightweight distance-aware discriminator trained with a composite objective—binary cross-entropy for per-observation calibration, a scene-level margin ranking loss ordering successful vs. failed distances, and a pairwise contrastive loss for discriminability—that predicts grasp success probability conditioned on visual features, depth statistics, normalized distance, and category embedding. During manipulator approach, the system continuously scores intermediate observations and selects the one with highest predicted success, terminating early when the probability exceeds a task-dependent threshold or a minimum safe distance (15 cm) is reached, thereby explicitly optimizing the observation distance rather than treating it as incidental.

## Results

In simulation (Test Seen), the distance-aware second-stage plug-in improves Average Success Rate (ASR) by 6–10 percentage points across all five object-centric grasping baselines, e.g., ManipLLM rises from 56.00% to 65.62% and CrayonRobo from 74.00% to 83.01%. On the more challenging Test Unseen split, gains of 6–8 points are maintained. In real-world cluttered scenes, the full two-stage system achieves the highest Success Rate in all five scenarios (e.g., 6/10 on the mouse, 5/10 on the USB drive) while requiring only 2.15–3.10 exploration views versus ≥3.82 for the geometric NBV baseline, and completing tasks in 10–15 s versus 22–30 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates its distance-aware second stage as a plug-in on five object-centric grasping methods in simulation and the full two-stage system against three baselines in real-world experiments. In simulation, Ours consistently outperforms every baseline's default (Baseline) setting: Flowbot3D improves from 37.00% to 45.16% ASR (+8.2 pp), Implicit3D from 46.00% to 52.62% (+6.6 pp), ManipLLM from 56.00% to 65.62% (+9.6 pp), CrayonRobo from 74.00% to 83.01% (+9.0 pp), and RobMRAG from 49.25% to 57.69% (+8.4 pp) on Test Seen, with similar 6–8 pp gains on Test Unseen. The Heuristic baseline (Gaussian distance prior + depth-quality score) is the strongest non-learned alternative but still trails Ours by 3–5 pp, confirming that learned distance-aware scoring captures nuances that handcrafted rules miss. In real-world experiments, the full system (Ours Full) beats Breyer et al.'s geometric NBV [4] in all five cluttered scenes (e.g., 6/10 vs 4/10 on the mouse; 5/10 vs 0/10 on the USB drive) while using fewer views (≤3.10 vs ≥3.82) and roughly half the execution time (~12 s vs ~25 s). The initial-view and fixed-trajectory baselines perform substantially worse, with SR dropping to 0–3/10 in most scenes, underscoring the necessity of active view selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NBV-for-grasping pipeline established by GraspView [41] (scene reconstruction, multi-view rendering, and VLM-based view scoring) and Contact-GraspNet [37] (6-DOF grasp prediction) by inserting a distance-aware local refinement stage during the manipulator approach phase. Where prior NBV methods (Breyer et al. [4], GraspView [41], Neural Graspness Field [29]) treat camera-object distance as incidental and select a single global viewpoint, DiNBV-Grasp explicitly models distance as an optimization variable via a lightweight scorer trained with ranking and contrastive objectives. The VLM distillation strategy (Qwen3-VL-32B → DINOv3-based student) follows the broader trend of compressing foundation models for real-time robotics (BitVLA, Drive-KD, Shallow-π), but applies it specifically to the semantic scoring term within a visibility-adaptive NBV utility function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design—requiring no architectural changes to existing object-centric grasping pipelines—makes the distance-aware refinement broadly applicable, and the consistent 6–9% gains across geometry-based, learning-based, and VLM-based baselines suggest the distance dimension is genuinely underexploited. However, the real-world evaluation is limited to five scenes with a single gripper type (suction in simulation, parallel-jaw in hardware), and the distance-aware scorer is trained on a fixed set of approach trajectories, raising questions about transfer across sensor modalities, object scales beyond the tested range, and dexterous manipulation. An open question is whether the two-stage decomposition (global semantic exploration + local distance refinement) is optimal compared to a unified end-to-end formulation that jointly optimizes viewpoint and distance, and whether the CLIP-based category encoding generalizes reliably to truly novel object geometries.
