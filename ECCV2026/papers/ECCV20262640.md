## Problem

Existing interactive 3DGS segmentation methods require a per-scene setup stage—typically tens of seconds to minutes—involving multi-view mask preparation, mask lifting, and feature distillation before any interactive segmentation can begin. This setup bottleneck severely limits real-time and online applications. The paper aims to completely eliminate this setup stage while keeping per-interaction segmentation latency under 1 second.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262640/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw pretrained 3DGS scene (no per-scene preprocessing) and a single user prompt (point click, scribble, bounding box, or text) on one 2D view as input; text prompts are converted to bounding boxes via Grounding-DINO. Each virtual drone viewpoint is rendered at 512×512 to match SAM2's input resolution. The output is a hard partition of all 3D Gaussians into a foreground set (target object) and background set, produced in 0.4–0.9 seconds, which can be directly used for 3D asset extraction and online scene editing (relocation, removal, combination, copying).

## Method

SAGO reframes 3D segmentation as an online Next-Best-View (NBV) planning problem for two virtual drones launched from the interaction viewpoint, each exploring a 180° yaw arc in opposite directions within a Markov process. At each step, the drone's state comprises the current foreground Gaussian set, its spherical-coordinate pose (target center, fixed radius, yaw, pitch), a SAM2 memory bank for cross-view tracking, and a coverage indicator. The NBV is selected via an Exploration-Evaluation strategy that tries yaw increments of 90°/60°/30° and pitch angles {θ₀, 0°, 30°, 60°}, choosing the candidate that maximizes the number of background Gaussians pruned by Mask-shaped Frustum Filtering (MFF) while enforcing an mIoU consistency constraint against the initial mask to prevent cascading over-segmentation. MFF projects each Gaussian's center onto the 2D SAM mask at the new view and reclassifies it as background if the center falls outside the mask. The target centroid is iteratively refined from depth back-projection of the initial mask to the mean position of the current foreground set, and the process terminates when full 360° coverage is achieved—typically in two exploration steps per drone.

## Results

On NVOS, SAGO achieves the best mIoU of 92.7% and mAcc of 98.7%, outperforming all offline and online baselines. On SPIn-NeRF, it reaches 92.5% mIoU (second to SAGA's 93.4%) and the highest mAcc of 99.3%. On 3D-OVS, SAGO attains a mean mIoU of 96.2%, surpassing the previous best (SAGA, 96.0%) by 0.2%. Critically, SAGO requires zero setup time and completes each interaction in 0.4–0.9 s, a >50× speedup over the prior setup-free method GaussianCut (40–60 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest offline method SAGA (~1 h setup), SAGO matches or slightly exceeds its quality: on NVOS it gains +0.1 mIoU and +0.1 mAcc, while on SPIn-NeRF it trails by 0.9 mIoU but leads by +0.1 mAcc—all without any setup. Versus the prior setup-free SOTA GaussianCut (40–60 s per interaction), SAGO improves NVOS mIoU by +0.2 and mAcc by +0.3, is within 0.4 mIoU on SPIn-NeRF, and is >50× faster. Compared to iSegMan (4–6 s), SAGO wins on both datasets in mIoU (+0.1/+0.7) and mAcc (+0.2/+0.3) while being roughly 5–10× faster. On LERF-Mask, SAGO surpasses ClickGaussian by +1.9% mean mIoU (91.0 vs 89.1), with a notable +5.4% gain on the Teatime scene. On 3D-OVS, SAGO edges out SAGA by +0.2% mean mIoU (96.2 vs 96.0) and N2F2 by +2.3%, confirming that the virtual-drone NBV strategy generalizes across diverse scene types.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGO builds on the 3DGS representation (Kerbl et al.) and SAM2's video-tracking capability (Ravi et al.), but departs conceptually from both optimization-based 3DGS segmentation methods (SAGA, Click-Gaussian, LangSplat) that require per-scene feature distillation, and from prior online methods (GaussianCut, iSegMan) that still incur 40–90 s of per-interaction computation. The key lineage shift is borrowing Next-Best-View planning from active robotic vision and recasting 3D segmentation as an iterative viewpoint-exploration problem solved by virtual drones, rather than as a graph-partitioning or feature-aggregation problem. This reframing lets a frozen 2D tracker (SAM2) plus simple geometric frustum filtering replace the learned 3D features that prior methods distill during setup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the setup stage entirely and achieving sub-second (0.4–0.9 s) interaction latency, SAGO makes interactive 3DGS segmentation practical for real-time applications such as live 3D asset extraction and scene editing, where even a 30-second wait is prohibitive. The 50× speedup over the prior best setup-free method (GaussianCut) while matching or slightly exceeding its quality suggests that the field's assumption that complex 3D feature learning is necessary for high-quality segmentation may be overstated. Open questions include handling jagged boundaries (the center-based MFF limitation), robustness to poor initial views, and whether the NBV strategy generalizes to dynamic or multi-object scenes without per-object re-planning.
