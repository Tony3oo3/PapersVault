## Problem

Training-free sampling acceleration methods for text-to-image diffusion models (fast ODE solvers, optimized time schedules, feature caching) have been developed in isolation, with no systematic study of their design components or relative contributions to overall performance. This paper identifies that the outer time schedule is the dominant factor, and that the default uniform schedule is suboptimal, causing slow structural convergence in generated images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260775/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (or a reference image plus text prompt for editing) and produces a generated or edited RGB image using flow-based text-to-image models such as Flux.1-Dev (12B) or Stable Diffusion 3.5 medium (8B). TORS is a pre-computed outer time schedule derived from 100 profiling trajectories of 100 steps each (~3 A6000-hours), which can then be instantiated for any target number of sampling steps (e.g., 10, 20, 50) with negligible overhead. The schedule is model-specific but generalizes to LoRA-fine-tuned variants, different architectures (e.g., 20B Qwen-Image), and downstream tasks like image editing with Flux.1-Kontext.

## Method

TORS (Total Rotation Schedule) leverages the Frenet-Serret geometric framework to construct an outer time schedule that maintains constant total rotation along the sampling trajectory. The authors first project 100 sampling trajectories into a 3D PCA subspace (explaining >99% variance), then compute arc-length-parameterized curvature κ(s) and torsion τ(s) for each trajectory. The total rotation rate |ω| = √(κ²+τ²) (the Darboux vector magnitude) is integrated to define total rotation Θ over the trajectory, which is then partitioned into N equal segments. Each segment boundary's arc length is mapped back to its corresponding timestamp, yielding a non-uniform schedule that allocates denser steps to high-curvature/torsion regions (early sampling) and sparser steps elsewhere. This geometric justification is grounded in the local truncation error decomposition of Euler steps, where the O(h²) normal bending error (κ) and O(h³) binormal twisting error (κτ) are both captured by the total rotation rate, making it a principled single scalar for adaptive discretization.

## Results

On Flux.1-Dev with 10 sampling steps, TORS achieves an Image Reward of 0.97 and HPSv2 of 29.30, nearly matching the 50-step baseline (IR 0.96, HPSv2 30.15) while substantially outperforming the 10-step baseline (IR 0.71, HPSv2 27.70). On Stable Diffusion 3.5 medium, TORS reaches IR 0.86 and HPSv2 26.90 versus the 10-step baseline's IR 0.55 and HPSv2 25.31. On GenEval, TORS with 10 steps achieves an overall score of 0.67, surpassing even the 50-step baseline (0.65) and the 10-step baseline (0.57).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TORS outperforms all six baselines on both Flux.1-Dev and SD 3.5 medium across every reported metric. On Flux.1-Dev, TORS leads the closest competitor GITS by +0.07 IR (0.97 vs 0.90) and +0.17 HPSv2 (29.30 vs 29.13), and surpasses the next-best solver UniPC by +0.19 IR and +1.14 HPSv2. Feature-caching methods (FORA, TaylorSeers) and solvers (DPM-Solver, UniPC) provide only marginal gains over the 10-step baseline (≤0.07 IR), whereas TORS closes most of the gap to the 50-step baseline. On SD 3.5 medium, TORS achieves IR 0.86 vs GITS's 0.75 and UniPC's 0.69, a gap of +0.11 and +0.17 respectively. Notably, TORS is the only method that exceeds the 50-step baseline on GenEval overall score (0.67 vs 0.65), indicating superior compositional generation quality under the 10-step budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric regularity analysis of diffusion sampling trajectories (Chen et al., 2023, 2024) from variance-exploding SDEs to the flow-matching formulation used by state-of-the-art text-to-image models (Flux, SD 3.5). While prior training-free acceleration methods—fast ODE solvers (DPM-Solver, UniPC), optimized schedules (GITS), and feature caching (FORA, TaylorSeers)—were developed in isolation, this paper unifies them into a single design space and identifies the outer time schedule as the dominant performance factor. The proposed TORS schedule departs from GITS's dynamic-programming approach to truncation error by explicitly leveraging Frenet-Serret curvature and torsion to enforce constant total rotation along the projected sampling trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the outer schedule dominates over solvers and feature caching redirects practitioner attention toward a simpler, more impactful lever for few-step generation, and TORS achieves near-50-step quality with only 10 steps on Flux.1-Dev and SD 3.5 with negligible per-inference overhead. The architecture-agnostic transferability (LoRA variants, Qwen-Image, image editing via Flux.1-Kontext) suggests the geometric statistics are a reusable asset across model families. Open questions include whether the geometric scheduling principle extends to video or 3D generation, whether optimal multi-component combinations (schedule + solver + caching) can be jointly optimized, and whether the one-time profiling cost (100 trajectories × 100 steps) can be further reduced.
