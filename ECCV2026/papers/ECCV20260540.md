## Problem

Cross-view localization (CVL) must bridge the extreme viewpoint gap between ground and satellite images, but existing pipelines either project ground features into a Bird's-Eye-View (requiring unavailable 3D priors and introducing geometric distortion) or rely on external depth foundation models for 3D lifting (adding latency and sensitivity to noisy depth predictions). This paper asks whether a purely 2D geometric formulation—inspired by the traditional navigation technique of resection—can achieve competitive localization without any BEV transformation or depth dependency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260540/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground panorama (resized to 720×1440), a geo-referenced satellite patch (656×656), and a heading/orientation prior (from a consumer-grade IMU or orientation prediction network, with up to ±10° noise tolerated). It outputs a single 2D camera position (x, y) in the satellite map coordinate frame. The orientation is assumed known, so the task is reduced to position-only estimation; the heading prior is consumed to compute the azimuthal direction of each matched ground keypoint, and under noisy orientation the method searches over a bounded set of heading candidates via parallelized RANSAC.

## Method

ARC-Loc reformulates ground-to-satellite correspondences as 2D line-to-point constraints: each matched ground keypoint defines an azimuthal ray drawn from the corresponding satellite keypoint along the ground-view bearing, and the camera position is the convergence point of these rays. Feature matching uses a frozen RADIOv3-L backbone with separate descriptor and keypoint heads, combined via a dual-softmax (with learnable dustbin) weighted by keypoint confidence to select the top-512 correspondences. The ARC solver is a closed-form weighted least-squares solution (Eq. 5) that estimates position from a minimal set of two line-point pairs, and is wrapped in RANSAC for robustness at inference. Training uses a composite ARC loss combining a direct position L2 term and a ray-distance term that penalizes azimuthal rays failing to converge at the predicted location, enabling end-to-end supervision without dense correspondence labels or depth priors. The entire pipeline operates in the native 2D image domain, eliminating BEV warping and external depth modules, yielding ~59 ms matching latency and ~1.1 GB memory versus 138–188 ms and 2.1–2.6 GB for depth-dependent baselines.

## Results

On VIGOR (known orientation), ARC-Loc achieves 2.52 m mean / 1.20 m median position error on Same-Area and 3.11 m / 1.59 m on Cross-Area, the best among all Non-BEV methods and second-best overall (behind only FG² at 1.95 m). On KITTI (±10° orientation noise), ARC-Loc reaches 0.79 m mean / 0.50 m median on Same-Area, competitive with FG² (0.75 m / 0.51 m), and 7.45 m / 4.32 m on Cross-Area. Inference latency is 59.67 ms (matching) + 20.03 ms (solver) with 1134 MB memory, substantially lower than depth-dependent methods like Loc² (137.52 ms + 28.32 ms, 2810 MB) and BevSplat (139.90 ms end-to-end, 2577 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARC-Loc outperforms all Non-BEV baselines on VIGOR: it reduces mean Same-Area error by 17.6% over Loc² (2.52 m vs. 3.06 m) and by 30% over CCVPE (2.52 m vs. 3.60 m), while eliminating Loc²'s dependency on an external depth foundation model (UniK3D). Against the strongest BEV method FG², ARC-Loc trails by 0.57 m on VIGOR Same-Area (2.52 m vs. 1.95 m) but narrows the gap to 0.70 m on Cross-Area (3.11 m vs. 2.41 m). On KITTI Same-Area (±10° noise), ARC-Loc (0.79 m) is nearly on par with FG² (0.75 m) and clearly beats Loc² (1.13 m) and CCVPE (1.22 m). On KITTI Cross-Area, ARC-Loc (7.45 m) is slightly worse than FG² (7.31 m) and notably worse than Loc² (5.60 m), which the authors attribute to limited angular diversity of azimuthal rays under the narrow front-facing FoV. In terms of efficiency, ARC-Loc's matching latency (59.67 ms) is roughly 2.3× faster than Loc² (137.52 ms) and 3.2× faster than FG² (187.34 ms), with memory usage (1134 MB) well below BevSplat (2577 MB) and Loc² (2810 MB).

## Relation to Prior Work

ARC-Loc departs from the dominant CVL paradigm of bridging the ground-to-aerial viewpoint gap via 3D lifting or BEV warping by repurposing the traditional navigation technique of resection: converting 2D ground-to-satellite correspondences into azimuthal ray (line-to-point) constraints whose intersection yields the camera position. Conceptually, it sits closest to the direct-matching line of work (Loc2, CCVPE) but eliminates the external depth foundation model that Loc2 requires for 3D lifting, and avoids the geometric distortions inherent to BEV-based methods (FG2, BevSplat, HC-Net). The feature extraction and dual-softmax matching pipeline follows established practice from vision foundation models (RADIOv3-L) and prior matching literature, while the closed-form 2-point ARC solver and the ray-convergence loss are the paper's novel geometric contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a purely 2D line-intersection formulation can achieve competitive CVL accuracy without depth models or BEV warping, ARC-Loc offers a practical, interpretable, and memory-efficient alternative for resource-constrained deployment (3× faster matching latency, ~900 MB memory vs. 2.5 GB for depth-dependent methods). However, two limitations remain open: accuracy degrades under heading uncertainties beyond ±10°, and the solver suffers geometric degeneracy when matched keypoints lack angular diversity (e.g., KITTI's narrow front-facing FoV). Whether the azimuthal-ray formulation can be extended to full 6-DoF pose estimation or to scenarios without reliable orientation priors is an important direction the authors leave for future work.
