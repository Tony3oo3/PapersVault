## Problem

Conformal prediction for gaze and head pose estimation typically computes nonconformity scores in flat coordinate charts (yaw-pitch or Euler angles), but these charts distort intrinsic distances near coordinate singularities (sphere poles, gimbal lock). This causes slice-conditional coverage to collapse by 30–50 percentage points in safety-critical output regions while marginal coverage remains nominally at target, a failure mode that is invisible under standard global evaluation metrics. No prior work had identified this geometric mechanism or shown that standard adaptive conformal remedies are structurally unable to correct it.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB face images and a held-out calibration set of (prediction, label) pairs. The critical design choice is the nonconformity score: the same predictions and labels can be scored as Euclidean norms of chart-coordinate residuals (yaw-pitch Δψ,Δθ or Euler Δα,Δβ,Δγ) or as coordinate-free geodesic distances on S²/SO(3). The output is a conformal prediction set whose local shape (axis ratios of the acceptance region) is determined entirely by the scoring geometry, not by the underlying model. Slice-conditional coverage in specified output regimes (e.g., |pitch|>70°, |β|>60°) is the diagnostic metric that reveals the hidden undercoverage.

## Method

The paper formalises how the Riemannian metric tensor G(ξ) of a coordinate chart distorts chart-coordinate residuals, proving (Proposition 1) that a fixed conformal threshold carves out vanishing intrinsic volume near singularities where λ_min(G)→0, redistributing coverage from well-conditioned to singular regions. It then proves (Proposition 2) that any scalar threshold-modulating method (normalised CP, CQR) can only rescale the acceptance-set radius but cannot alter its local axis ratios, which are fixed by the metric eigenvalues—making chart distortion a shape problem, not a scale problem. The remedy is to replace chart residuals with coordinate-free geodesic scores (arc distance on S², Frobenius log-norm on SO(3), or monotone-equivalent surrogates like chord distance or quaternion distance), which produce locally isotropic acceptance sets and require no retraining. A three-layer decomposition separates marginal validity (Layer 1), heteroscedastic model error (Layer 2, addressable by normalisation), and chart-induced shape distortion (Layer 3, addressable only by intrinsic scoring), and the Riemannian volume density ρ(ξ)=√det G(ξ) serves as a closed-form diagnostic for where chart-based systems are likely to undercover.

## Results

Across four datasets, chart-coordinate conformal scores (yaw-pitch L2, Euler L2) suffer 30–50 pp drops in slice-conditional coverage near coordinate singularities while maintaining ~90% marginal coverage. On ETH-XGaze, near-pole (|pitch|>70°) coverage collapses to 38.9% under yaw-pitch L2 scoring versus 74.6% for geodesic and 89.3% for normalised geodesic. On Gaze360 the same region drops to 42.0% (YP L2) vs. 77.2% (geodesic) vs. 86.5% (normalised geodesic). On SO(3), Euler L2 coverage at |β|>60° falls to 57.5% (BIWI controlled) and 55.2% (AFLW2000-3D real model), while geodesic scoring maintains 89.5% and 75.1% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five conformal scoring strategies rather than external gaze/pose models: yaw-pitch L2 (chart baseline), metric-corrected yaw-pitch (first-order shape fix), normalised yaw-pitch (scalar scale fix), geodesic (exact shape fix), and normalised geodesic (shape + scale fix). Yaw-pitch L2 is the worst performer in every near-singularity region, dropping to 38.9% on ETH-XGaze and 42.0% on Gaze360. Normalised yaw-pitch (representing the entire class of scalar adaptive methods including CQR) improves only to 50.4% on ETH-XGaze, confirming the paper's Proposition 2 that scalar rescaling cannot correct chart-induced anisotropy. Geodesic scoring recovers most of the lost coverage (74.6% on ETH-XGaze, 77.2% on Gaze360, 89.5% on BIWI) by eliminating the geometric distortion entirely. Normalised geodesic achieves the best near-pole coverage (89.3% ETH-XGaze, 86.5% Gaze360, 89.0% BIWI, 80.3% AFLW2000) by additionally addressing heteroscedastic model error. Mondrian conformal prediction with a geodesic base score restores 90.1% on AFLW2000-3D, showing that group-wise calibration can close the remaining model-regime gap once the base score geometry is intrinsic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the split conformal prediction framework of Vovk et al. [37] and the recent marginal-validity proof for geodesic scores on Riemannian manifolds by Cholaquidis et al. [4], but departs from both by focusing on the *failure* of the chart-coordinate scores that practitioners actually deploy in gaze and head-pose pipelines. While Cholaquidis et al. establish that geodesic scores are valid, this paper shows that the yaw-pitch and Euler-angle scores commonly used in intermediate prediction heads, residuals, and calibration produce systematic 30–50 pp slice-conditional undercoverage near coordinate singularities. The impossibility result (Proposition 2) further distinguishes this work from the adaptive conformal literature (normalised CP [30], CQR [33]), proving that scalar threshold rescaling cannot correct the geometric shape distortion, a limitation not previously formalised.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical applications such as driver monitoring, a conformal system that reports 90% marginal coverage can silently miss the true gaze direction in over 60% of extreme-pitch samples, with no warning from standard evaluation metrics. The practical remedy—replacing chart-coordinate residuals with coordinate-free geodesic or monotone-equivalent scores—requires no retraining and adds negligible computation, making it immediately adoptable. Open questions include extending the analysis to SE(3) camera pose, articulated human pose, and manifolds without closed-form geodesics, as well as whether the slice-conditional guarantees can be strengthened toward pointwise conditional coverage. The impossibility result also cautions the broader conformal prediction community that adaptive methods address scale, not shape, a distinction that may apply beyond geometric outputs.
