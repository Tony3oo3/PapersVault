## Problem

Token compression and structural pruning for ViT-based semantic segmentation have never been systematically compared under a unified, FLOPs-matched protocol, and their behavior under aggressive compression and corrupted inputs remains unclear. Additionally, it is unknown whether stacking moderate token compression on top of a pruned backbone yields a stronger accuracy-robustness trade-off than either technique alone at comparable compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262587/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images (512×512 crops for ADE20K, 768×768 for Cityscapes) and produces per-pixel semantic segmentation masks over the original spatial grid. For token-compression methods, the encoder token sequence is reduced and the compressed tokens are passed to an unchanged transformer decoder, which reconstructs full-resolution predictions; for pruning methods, the full spatial token grid is preserved end-to-end. Robustness is measured by aggregating mIoU across 16 corruption types at 5 severity levels (80 variants per image) on ADE20K-C and Cityscapes-C.

## Method

The paper benchmarks three token-compression methods (ToMe, ALGM, CTS) and structural pruning (NViT) within a controlled flat-token Segmenter pipeline (DeiT-B encoder + transformer decoder) under matched FLOPs across mild, moderate, and aggressive compression regimes. The key empirical finding is that token compression preserves near-baseline accuracy at mild reduction but degrades sharply at 3.4×–3.8× FLOPs reduction (e.g., ToMe drops from 47.22 to 23.94 mIoU on ADE20K), while structural pruning exhibits a smooth degradation curve. Effective-rank analysis reveals that aggressive token merging concentrates encoder features into fewer dominant directions, explaining the loss of spatial coherence around fine structures. Motivated by these findings, the paper proposes a prune-then-merge (PtM) pipeline: first apply moderate NViT structural pruning to obtain a compact backbone, then apply moderate ToMe token merging on top, achieving 45.71/34.33 mIoU at 3.7× FLOPs reduction on ADE20K—outperforming both single-axis strategies at comparable compute.

## Results

On ADE20K at aggressive compression (~3.7× FLOPs reduction), the proposed prune-then-merge (PtM) pipeline achieves 45.71/34.33 mIoU_clean/mIoU_noise at 34.84 GFLOPs, outperforming all single-axis baselines. ToMe collapses to 23.94/16.86 under the same budget, while NViT-S reaches 45.30/32.86 and ALGM 42.85/30.20. On Cityscapes at ~3.6× reduction, PtM achieves 77.52/60.71 mIoU, ahead of NViT-S (76.76/58.75), CTS (73.81/60.99), and ToMe (64.50/50.56). At mild compression (~1.4–1.9×), all token-compression methods preserve near-baseline performance, with CTS on Cityscapes retaining 78.15/63.07 vs. the 78.25/63.26 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMe (general-purpose token merging), PtM wins decisively at aggressive compression: on ADE20K, PtM achieves 45.71/34.33 mIoU vs. ToMe's 23.94/16.86 at comparable ~3.7× FLOPs reduction, a gap of over 21 points in clean mIoU. Against segmentation-aware token compression (ALGM, CTS), PtM also leads at aggressive compression on ADE20K (45.71 vs. 42.85 for ALGM, 45.71 vs. 42.16 for CTS in clean mIoU) and on Cityscapes (77.52 vs. 73.15 for ALGM, 77.52 vs. 73.81 for CTS). Against structural pruning alone (NViT-S), PtM edges ahead on both datasets at aggressive compression (45.71 vs. 45.30 on ADE20K; 77.52 vs. 76.76 on Cityscapes), with a more notable advantage in corruption robustness (34.33 vs. 32.86 on ADE20K). At mild compression, all methods are competitive and NViT actually slightly exceeds the baseline on ADE20K (47.93 vs. 47.22), indicating that the advantage of PtM is specifically a high-compression phenomenon.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate compression literatures for ViTs—token compression (ToMe, ALGM, CTS) and structural pruning (NViT, SAViT, Isomorphic Pruning)—under a single matched-FLOPs benchmark for semantic segmentation. It departs from prior token-compression evaluations, which largely focus on low-to-moderate ratios and classification, by probing the high-compression regime and adding corruption-robustness metrics (ADE20K-C, Cityscapes-C). The proposed prune-then-merge pipeline extends the multi-axis compression idea from joint-optimization approaches [14, 34] into a simpler sequential recipe: prune first, then merge, which is evaluated under matched compute rather than a joint objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark gives deployment practitioners a clear decision rule: token compression is safe at mild reductions but fragile at aggressive ones, while structural pruning degrades more smoothly, and a stacked prune-then-merge pipeline recovers much of the lost accuracy-robustness trade-off at high compression. Limitations include evaluation on a single flat-token backbone (DeiT-B), a single pruning method (NViT), and two datasets, so generalizability to hierarchical transformers, other pruning criteria, and additional tasks (detection, classification) remains open. The noted FLOPs-to-FPS mismatch for pruning-only models also highlights that compute accounting alone is insufficient for deployment decisions.
