## Problem

Existing learning-based point cloud registration methods approximate rotation invariance through fragile local reference frames or extensive rotation augmentation, providing only empirical invariance that degrades under unseen rotational transformations. This paper addresses the gap by enforcing strict SO(3) equivariance by construction, so that feature representations transform predictably under arbitrary rotations without relying on pose normalization or data-driven invariance.

## Input / Output

The method takes two partially overlapped 3D point clouds (source P with N points and target Q with M points) and outputs a single rigid transformation T = (R, t) with R ∈ SO(3) and t ∈ R³. In practice, 5000 keypoints are sampled per fragment to establish correspondences. The pipeline produces intermediate rotation-invariant descriptors for matching and rotation-equivariant features (as direct sums of SO(3) irrep blocks) that are consumed by the hypothesis proposer to generate SE(3) candidates in closed form from individual correspondences.

## Method

SHReg encodes point-wise features as direct sums of irreducible representations of SO(3) using spherical harmonics, with message passing defined by tensor-product convolutions followed by Clebsch-Gordan projections and scalar-gated nonlinearities that jointly preserve strict equivariance across layers. Rotation-invariant descriptors for correspondence matching are derived by taking the ℓ₂ norm within each irrep block, exploiting the orthogonality of Wigner-D matrices. A coarse-to-fine matching strategy uses a Geometric Transformer for superpoint-level context and a LightGlue-style matchability/saliency head for point-level assignment. The key architectural insight is a single-correspondence hypothesis proposer: ℓ=2 features yield unsigned principal axes via symmetric trace-free tensors, ℓ=1 features resolve sign ambiguity, and together they construct local orthonormal frames whose alignment gives a closed-form SE(3) transformation per correspondence, eliminating the need for RANSAC triplet sampling.

## Results

On 3DMatch, SHReg achieves 98.5% FMR, 78.6% IR, and 95.4% RR in 0.28 s, outperforming the closest competitor PARE-Net (76.9% IR, 95.0% RR). On the more challenging 3DLoMatch (10–30% overlap), SHReg reaches 88.6% FMR, 48.8% IR, and 82.4% RR, surpassing PARE-Net (47.5% IR, 80.5% RR) and RoITr (54.6% IR, 74.1% RR). Under arbitrary full-range rotations on Rotated 3DLoMatch, SHReg maintains 83.3% TR (a +0.3 gain over the non-rotated setting), while rotation-sensitive methods like FCGF drop by 15.1 points. On KITTI Odometry, SHReg achieves 0.23° RE, 4.7 cm TE, and 99.8% TR in 0.24 s, matching the best rotation-robust baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHReg outperforms the strongest rotation-robust baseline PARE-Net [36] on both datasets: +1.7 pp IR and +0.4 pp RR on 3DMatch, and +1.3 pp IR and +1.9 pp RR on 3DLoMatch, while running in comparable time (0.28 s vs 0.17 s). Against rotation-sensitive scene-level methods, the gap is larger: SHReg improves RR by 3.0 pp over GeoTransformer [27] (95.4 vs 92.4) and by 8.1 pp over Predator [17] (95.4 vs 89.0) on 3DMatch, with even wider margins on 3DLoMatch (82.4 vs 74.3 and 82.4 vs 64.4). Among patch-wise descriptors, SHReg surpasses SpinNet [2] by 6.8 pp RR on 3DMatch and 22.6 pp on 3DLoMatch, while being ~35× faster (0.28 s vs 9.85 s). On the Rotated 3DLoMatch benchmark, SHReg is the only method that maintains or slightly improves TR under arbitrary rotations (83.3% vs 83.0%), whereas FCGF [7] drops 15.1 pp and GeoTransformer [27] drops 2.8 pp; among equivariant methods, SHReg leads PARE-Net by 1.5 pp TR (83.3 vs 81.8). On KITTI Odometry, SHReg ties PARE-Net [36] and GeoTransformer [27] at 99.8% TR but achieves the lowest translation error (4.7 cm vs 4.9 cm and 6.2 cm respectively) in 0.24 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHReg extends the line of rotation-equivariant point cloud registration methods (YOHO, RoReg, RoITr, PARE-Net) by replacing group-convolution or vector-neuron backbones with a spherical-harmonics-based architecture that encodes features as irreducible representations of SO(3), guaranteeing strict equivariance by construction rather than through rotation discretization or augmentation. It also departs from the standard RANSAC/LGR triplet-sampling paradigm for transformation estimation by exploiting the orientation information preserved in equivariant features to generate one closed-form SE(3) hypothesis per correspondence. The coarse-to-fine matching pipeline (superpoint + point level with matchability/saliency heads) follows the structure established by Predator and PARE-Net, while the Geometric Transformer for superpoint context is borrowed from GeoTransformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded alternative to the dominant augmentation-based strategy for rotation robustness, showing that strict equivariance by design generalizes better to unseen rotations (confirmed on Rotated 3DLoMatch). The single-correspondence hypothesis proposer is a clean efficiency gain that reduces the search space from cubic (triplet sampling) to linear, which could be attractive for real-time applications. However, the practical margins over PARE-Net (the closest competitor) are modest (e.g., 82.4% vs 80.5% RR on 3DLoMatch), the model is larger (9.52 MB vs 3.84 MB), and KITTI results show performance saturation. Open questions include whether the spherical-harmonics approach scales to higher-order irreps without prohibitive cost, whether it extends to non-rigid or partial-symmetry settings, and whether the single-correspondence estimator remains reliable under extreme noise or very low overlap.
