## Problem

Reconstructing a full hyperspectral image from a single grayscale snapshot captured through one flat optic lens on a filterless panchromatic sensor is severely ill-posed: the measurement has the same spatial resolution as the output, no spectral filtering, and the wavelength-dependent PSF scatters signal across patch boundaries. Existing snapshot HSI systems rely on complex multi-stage optics, color filter arrays, or oversized sensors to better condition the problem, and no prior patch-based reconstruction or diffusion approach has been shown to succeed when the PSF support is comparable to the patch size.

## Input / Output

The method takes an H×W grayscale snapshot measurement (rendered or captured through a single diffractive/metalens element) and the known wavelength-dependent PSF as input, and produces an H×W×31 hyperspectral image spanning 400–700 nm along with a per-pixel spectral uncertainty map. Because the model operates shift-invariantly on 64×64 (or 32×32) patches, it generalizes to arbitrary image sizes at inference without retraining; in experiments it reconstructs scenes from 256×256 up to 1280×1280 pixels. The measurement is a non-invertible linear encoding formed by convolving each spectral channel with its wavelength-specific PSF and summing, so the input carries no explicit spectral separation.

## Method

The core contribution is a conditional denoising diffusion model (UNet backbone) trained on small HSI–measurement patch pairs, combined with a PSF-guided sampling procedure that resolves the ambiguity inherent in patch-based reconstruction. During inference, the full measurement is split into non-overlapping patches, each denoised in parallel; intermediate clean estimates are stitched into a full-field HSI, passed through the forward measurement operator, and a gradient-based correction (Eq. 5) is applied to all patch states to enforce optical consistency with the observed measurement. Per-patch scale factors are recovered via a closed-form least-squares solve to handle the max-normalization ambiguity introduced during training, and multiple guidance iterations are repeated before each DDIM denoising step. Drawing multiple samples with different initial noise yields per-pixel spectral variance that correlates strongly (Pearson r ≈ 0.80 in simulation, 0.69 in experiment) with true reconstruction error, providing calibrated uncertainty without additional model components.

## Results

On the ARAD1K test set (256×256, 31 channels, R1 PSF), the proposed guided patch diffusion model achieves a PSNR of 34.63 dB, SSIM of 0.94, and SAM of 0.11, outperforming the next-best baseline (SST) by 2.86 dB in PSNR. In the experimental prototype with a fabricated TiO₂ metalens, the method achieves PSNR 31.20 dB, SSIM 0.894, and SAM 0.157 (8.9°) on 8 held-out tabletop scenes. Per-pixel uncertainty estimates derived from multiple diffusion samples correlate strongly with true reconstruction error (Pearson r = 0.80 in simulation, r = 0.69 in experiment).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, SAM) in the filterless single-optic setting. The closest competitor is SST [28] (PSNR 31.8, SSIM 0.90, SAM 0.15), which the method surpasses by 2.86 dB in PSNR and 0.04 in SSIM. Among the remaining baselines, DGSMP [23] (PSNR 30.0) and In2Set [42] (PSNR 30.1) are the next closest, followed by MST [5] (29.8), DAUHST [6] (29.7), SPECAT [44] (29.6), HDNet [22] (29.3), and TSANet [31] (29.2). A UNet backbone trained with standard MSE regression on the full HSI (29.12 dB) underperforms all baselines, confirming that the gains come from the patch-based diffusion formulation and PSF guidance rather than increased model capacity. All baselines were retrained from scratch on the same rendered filterless measurements to ensure a fair comparison, as no prior models were designed for this single-optic, filterless scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends patch-based diffusion models for inverse problems (Hu et al., 2024) to a regime where the optical PSF support is comparable to the patch size, a setting the authors note no prior patch-based method has addressed. The guidance mechanism draws on diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., 2023) but applies it globally across stitched patches to enforce physical consistency with the full-field measurement, rather than per-patch. In the broader snapshot hyperspectral imaging literature, the work departs from designs relying on complex multi-stage optics (CASSI, coded masks) or spectral filter arrays (Bayer, optimized CFA) by using a single flat diffractive lens with a bare panchromatic sensor, shifting complexity from the optical front-end to the computational decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a viable path toward ultra-compact snapshot hyperspectral cameras that trade optical complexity for computational complexity, with a real metalens prototype validating the approach end-to-end. The patch-based formulation offers a practical advantage over full-field methods by scaling to arbitrary image resolutions without retraining, and the per-pixel uncertainty estimates (Pearson r = 0.80 with true error) provide actionable reliability signals for downstream applications. Key limitations remain: reconstruction requires scene texture at the PSF scale (textureless regions are fundamentally unrecoverable), global context cannot propagate across patches, and inference is slow (22 s to 52 min per scene), though the authors note distillation (DMD) could reduce this to seconds. Open questions include whether encoder-based global conditioning can resolve the patch-boundary quality unevenness and whether the approach generalizes to more extreme PSF designs or lower-light conditions.
