## Problem

Vision-language models (VLMs) pre-trained on ground-level, foreground-dominant imagery suffer a severe domain gap when applied to UAV scenes characterized by bird's-eye views, small objects, and background dominance. Standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as LoRA and Adapter fail to bridge this gap because their static low-rank structures lack the representational capacity for complex aerial spatial details and cannot actively suppress the overwhelming background noise that dilutes foreground signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a drone RGB image and an open-vocabulary text prompt (free-form object category descriptions) and outputs bounding boxes with corresponding class labels, evaluated via mAP and mAP50. Internally, a pre-computed 20-word background vocabulary (covering streetscapes, natural terrains, and atmospheric conditions) is fed into the gating module to compute background similarity scores alongside the foreground text features. The base detector is GroundingDINO with a Swin Transformer visual backbone, and all adaptation is restricted to modality-specific modules while the text and visual backbones remain frozen.

## Method

DroneFINE introduces two complementary domain-aware modules on top of GroundingDINO. The first, HyperAdapter, replaces static low-rank adapters with a foreground-aware dynamic convolution generator: learnable foreground queries aggregate global foreground semantics via cross-attention, and a shared hypernetwork maps these features to data-dependent depthwise separable convolution kernels across parallel 1×1, 3×3, and 5×5 branches, providing multi-scale spatial inductive bias that adapts per-image. The second, SemanticGate, actively suppresses background interference by computing similarity between image features and a curated background vocabulary, then applying a three-gate mechanism (Protection Gate, Inhibition Gate, and Candidate Adjustment) to re-rank detection queries—protecting high-confidence foreground candidates while suppressing those dominated by background responses. An InfoNCE contrastive loss is applied on the combined foreground and background text embeddings to enforce semantic separability, ensuring the gating operates on well-discriminated features. Together, these modules train only 1.54M parameters (5.6% of full fine-tuning) while matching full fine-tuning performance on VisDrone and surpassing it on UAVDT.

## Results

On VisDrone, DroneFINE-T achieves 38.4% mAP and 61.2% mAP50, matching full fine-tuning (38.4%/61.3%) while training only 1.54M parameters (5.6% of the 27.5M in full FT). On UAVDT, DroneFINE-T reaches 26.5% mAP and 43.6% mAP50, surpassing full fine-tuning (22.4%/38.6%) by 4.1% mAP. The larger DroneFINE-L variant attains 42.3% mAP and 65.8% mAP50 on VisDrone, setting a new SOTA among UAV detectors. On COCO (anti-forgetting evaluation), DroneFINE preserves 44.9% mAP versus 42.5% for full fine-tuning, confirming superior retention of pre-trained knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DroneFINE-T outperforms all visual-side PEFT baselines on both datasets: it beats LoRA by 1.7% mAP / 2.0% mAP50 on VisDrone and by 1.5% mAP / 2.8% mAP50 on UAVDT, and surpasses Mona by 0.5% mAP / 0.7% mAP50 on VisDrone. Against text-side methods (CoOp, Shine), DroneFINE gains 1.1% mAP / 1.2% mAP50 on VisDrone and 1.6% mAP / 2.2% mAP50 on UAVDT, confirming that visual-side dynamic adaptation is essential for dense small-object detection. On VisDrone, DroneFINE-T matches full fine-tuning exactly (38.4% mAP) with only 5.6% of the trainable parameters, while on UAVDT it exceeds full fine-tuning by 4.1% mAP, indicating that full FT overfits on the highly redundant video sequences. In the broader UAV detector comparison (Table 2), DroneFINE-L (42.3% mAP) surpasses the previous SOTA RemDet-X (40.0%) by 2.3% mAP on VisDrone, and DroneFINE-T (26.5% mAP) leads ESOD (23.6%) by 2.9% mAP on UAVDT. For anti-forgetting, DroneFINE retains 44.9% COCO mAP versus 42.5% for full fine-tuning and 41.9% for Mona, demonstrating that the domain-aware PEFT strategy preserves the VLM's generalization far better than updating all parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) paradigm—rooted in adapter-tuning [13] and low-rank adaptation [14]—to the specific challenge of adapting VLM-based detectors (GroundingDINO) to aerial imagery. It departs from generic PEFT by incorporating hypernetwork-based dynamic convolution generation [11] and Mona's scaled-input architecture [43] into a foreground-aware module (HyperAdapter), and introduces a text-conditioned background suppression mechanism (SemanticGate) that has no direct precedent in the PEFT literature. Conceptually, it positions itself against both closed-set traditional UAV detectors (which lack open-vocabulary flexibility) and full fine-tuning/retraining approaches (which are expensive and prone to catastrophic forgetting), arguing that the domain gap between ground-level pre-training data and bird's-eye-view aerial imagery demands domain-specific adaptation structures rather than generic low-rank updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pathway for deploying open-vocabulary VLM detectors on UAV platforms without the computational cost of full retraining, which is significant given the resource constraints of edge deployment. The SVD-based diagnosis of why standard low-rank PEFT fails on aerial imagery (high-rank requirements in deeper FFN layers) offers a useful analytical tool for understanding domain-gap adaptation failures more broadly. However, the approach is tightly coupled to GroundingDINO's query-selection architecture, the 20-word background vocabulary is manually curated and domain-specific, and evaluation is limited to two UAV datasets—generalization to other VLM backbones, sensor modalities, or geographic domains remains untested. An open question is whether the background-suppression strategy can be automated (e.g., learned vocabulary) rather than relying on manual curation and LLM-assisted generation.
