## Problem

Multi-tool agents for instruction-guided image editing remain brittle under open-loop pipelines: early routing errors propagate unchecked, and existing systems either treat editing as a single forward pass or commit to a fixed tool chain before observing execution failures. The paper tackles the absence of a closed-loop mechanism that can verify intermediate outputs, calibrate tool confidence, and selectively repair only the failing branch without restarting the entire pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262786/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image (3×H×W) and a free-form natural-language editing instruction that may be abstract or multi-step (e.g., 'make this photo more cinematic'). It produces a single edited image that satisfies the instruction while preserving unedited regions. The pipeline is entirely training-free and orchestrates 26 heterogeneous generation and editing tools; a GPT-4V-based agent handles decomposition and ambiguity resolution, while the verifier combines CLIP ViT-B/32, ImageReward, LAION Aesthetic Predictor, NIQE, and repeated VLM judgments.

## Method

VLTR reformulates editing as closed-loop tool reasoning over a directed acyclic graph of atomic primitives. A GPT-4V decomposer converts the instruction into a DAG of typed primitives; a Bayes-UCB router then adaptively selects tools per primitive by maintaining Beta posteriors updated online with a clamped quality-uncertainty reward, invoking LLM semantic scores only when the bandit is statistically ambiguous. After each execution, a heteroscedastic verifier fuses three complementary assessment tiers (CLIP alignment, expert ensemble, repeated VLM judgments) via inverse-variance weighting—interpretable as a BLUE-style estimator under a heteroscedastic Gaussian model—yielding a calibrated (q, σ²) pair. A Bayesian-risk-guided scheduler maps this joint signal to ACCEPT, RETRY, REROUTE, or REPLAN decisions, and because primitives form a DAG, only the failed node and its descendants are re-executed while validated ancestors are cached, turning full-pipeline restarts into efficient local repair.

## Results

On PIE-Bench++, VLTR achieves the best overall VLM Rank (1.8) and leads on five of seven metrics: Structure Distance 28.5 (vs. 64.4–154.0 for baselines), BG-PSNR 25.20, BG-SSIM 0.855, CLIP-T 0.672, and ImageReward 0.758. On MagicBrush, VLTR attains the highest CLIP-T (0.669) and ImageReward (0.752) while requiring only 5.7 tool switches, delivering a 4.8× runtime speedup (97.4 s vs. 466.9 s) over the strongest multi-tool competitor RPG. Out-of-domain pilots on GEdit-Bench and RISEBench show consistent gains of +9.4% (G_SC) and +3.5 pp (Causal%) over their respective baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against end-to-end editors, VLTR dominates on preservation: its Structure Distance of 28.5 is 3.6× lower than FLUX.1-Kontext-Dev (101.5), 2.3× lower than InstructPix2Pix (64.4), and 2.8× lower than SDXL-Inpainting (78.8), while simultaneously improving CLIP-T from 0.656–0.663 to 0.672. Among multi-tool systems, VLTR matches GenArtist's structural preservation (SD 28.5 vs. 29.0) but nearly doubles its ImageReward (0.758 vs. 0.374) and improves VLM Rank from 4.5 to 1.8. Versus RPG, VLTR achieves 94% of RPG's aesthetic score (0.758 vs. 0.805) at only 19% of its Structure Distance (28.5 vs. 154.0), and on MagicBrush uses 47% fewer tool switches (5.7 vs. 10.8) with a 4.8× wall-clock speedup. SmartEdit is the closest competitor on preservation (BG-PSNR 24.99 vs. 25.20) but trails VLTR on every other metric, including VLM Rank (2.9 vs. 1.8). On out-of-domain benchmarks, VLTR outperforms Step1X-Edit on GEdit-Bench (G_SC 7.69 vs. 7.03) and FLUX.1-Kontext-Dev on RISEBench (Causal 8.8% vs. 5.3%, Spatial 19.0% vs. 13.0%), confirming that the closed-loop loop transfers beyond the primary evaluation distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLTR extends the multi-tool vision agent line (VisProg, HuggingGPT, GenArtist) by replacing their open-loop, plan-once-then-execute paradigm with a closed-loop reasoning architecture where tool selection is continuously revised based on online verification feedback. The adaptive routing mechanism is grounded in the Bayes-UCB bandit literature [10], repurposed here as a contextual tool-selection policy over a DAG of editing primitives rather than a generic decision problem. The heteroscedastic verifier draws on inverse-variance (BLUE-style) estimation from classical statistics, and the deliberative-search framing connects to recent multi-step reasoning work (Graph of Thoughts, LATS), though the paper explicitly positions those as background rather than direct predecessors. The underlying editing tools (FLUX.1, SDXL, InstructPix2Pix, ControlNet, etc.) are treated as interchangeable primitives rather than the contribution itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-loop formulation with uncertainty as an active control signal (driving retry, reroute, or replan decisions) offers a principled alternative to the brittle fixed-pipeline approach that dominates current multi-tool editing systems, and the 4.8× runtime speedup over RPG at matched quality makes the approach practically deployable. However, the framework still depends on a strong LLM (GPT-4V) for decomposition, and the paper acknowledges that long-horizon multi-turn planning remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the DAG-based local repair generalizes to video or 3D editing, whether the calibration properties hold under distribution shift beyond the tested benchmarks, and whether the training-free design can be matched by a learned end-to-end policy that internalizes the routing and verification logic.
