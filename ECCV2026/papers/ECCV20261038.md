## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models suffer from exposure bias: they train on ground-truth histories but must condition on their own imperfect predictions at inference, causing error accumulation and eventual video collapse over long horizons. Existing remedies like Self Forcing and adversarial post-training require a pretrained bidirectional teacher or online discriminator, preventing scalable end-to-end training from scratch and risking future-information leakage into the causal student.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt as conditioning signal and generates video autoregressively in chunks of 3 latent frames (the autoregressive unit), producing 5-second or 15-second (249-frame) videos. Each chunk is synthesized by solving a reverse-time ODE (32-step Euler at inference) conditioned on all previously generated frames via causal attention, with classifier-free guidance at scale 5.0. The backbone is Wan2.1-1.3B with per-frame noise-level timestep conditioning and no additional parameters beyond the base architecture.

## Method

Resampling Forcing is a teacher-free, end-to-end training framework whose core mechanism is autoregressive self-resampling: during training, each ground-truth frame is corrupted to a sampled timestep t_s (drawn from a shifted logit-normal distribution), then the model's own online weights complete the remaining denoising steps autoregressively to produce degraded history frames that simulate inference-time errors. The model is then trained in parallel with a per-frame flow-matching diffusion loss under a sparse causal mask, conditioning on these degraded histories while targeting clean ground-truth frames, with gradients detached from the resampling to prevent shortcut learning. A teacher-forcing warmup phase precedes the resampling phase to ensure the model has basic autoregressive capability. For efficient long-horizon attention, a parameter-free history routing mechanism (inspired by MoBA) dynamically selects the top-k most relevant history frames per query token via mean-pooled frame descriptors, reducing per-token attention complexity from O(L) to O(k) and achieving 75% sparsity with negligible quality loss.

## Results

On VBench evaluation of 15-second generated videos (split into three 5-second segments), Resampling Forcing achieves the highest temporal consistency scores across all segments (91.20 / 90.44 / 89.74), surpassing all baselines including distillation-based methods like Self Forcing (90.03 / 84.27 / 84.26) and LongLive (81.84 / 81.72 / 84.57). Visual quality scores are competitive (64.72 / 64.03 / 63.99), slightly below Self Forcing and LongLive in later segments but without requiring a 14B-parameter bidirectional teacher. With 75% attention sparsity via history routing, the model retains 90.18 / 89.80 / 87.03 temporal scores, demonstrating negligible quality loss from sparse context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Resampling Forcing achieves the best temporal consistency across all three video segments, outperforming Self Forcing by up to 5.5 points (90.44 vs. 84.27 at 5-10s) and LongLive by up to 8.7 points (90.44 vs. 81.72 at 5-10s), while requiring no auxiliary teacher model. On visual quality, the method is competitive but trails Self Forcing (67.12 vs. 64.72 at 0-5s) and LongLive (67.17 vs. 63.99 at 10-15s), which benefit from distillation from a 14B bidirectional teacher. Against non-distilled baselines, the method clearly leads: it surpasses MAGI-1 (4.5B) in temporal consistency (91.20 vs. 87.09) and Pyramid Flow (2.0B) by ~9 points, while also exceeding SkyReels-V2 and NOVA substantially. The 75%-sparsity variant (top-5 history routing) incurs only ~1 point temporal degradation relative to dense attention, confirming the routing mechanism's efficiency. Qualitatively, the paper demonstrates that distillation-based baselines (especially LongLive) violate strict causality—e.g., liquid levels that rise then fall during continuous pouring—whereas Resampling Forcing maintains monotonic physical behavior due to its teacher-free causal training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the autoregressive video diffusion paradigm (Wan2.1, DiT-based) and the exposure-bias problem identified in Self Forcing [32] and AAPT [42], which rely on a bidirectional teacher or online discriminator for post-training. The key conceptual departure is replacing these auxiliary models with a self-resampling mechanism that uses the model's own online weights to simulate inference-time errors on history frames, drawing an analogy to Scheduled Sampling in LLMs. The history routing mechanism is adapted from MoBA's [46] and VMoBA's [69] top-k block attention, but repurposed for the autoregressive setting where the number of history frames grows unboundedly, reducing per-token attention from O(L) to O(k).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for a 14B-parameter bidirectional teacher, Resampling Forcing makes training-from-scratch feasible for autoregressive video models, substantially lowering the compute barrier for world-model research. The demonstration that distillation from a bidirectional teacher can violate strict causality (e.g., liquid level non-monotonicity) raises important questions about the fidelity of current distillation pipelines. The history routing mechanism offers a practical, parameter-free path to near-constant attention complexity for long-horizon generation, though its behavior at extreme sparsity (top-1) and its interaction with very long sequences (>15s) remain to be fully explored. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to interactive/controllable settings and whether the logit-normal timestep distribution is optimal across model scales.
