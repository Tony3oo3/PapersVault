## Problem

Existing weakly supervised affordance grounding methods struggle with two fundamental issues: they cannot isolate action-specific visual evidence in exocentric images where multiple actions co-occur (31.7% of images are reused across classes), and they fail to distinguish semantically similar actions (e.g., 'push' vs. 'sit on' on the same motorcycle) because they rely on brief action phrases lacking rich semantic detail. While large vision-language models encode rich action semantics and their attention maps implicitly contain spatial cues, they do not directly output action-specific spatial localization, making it non-trivial to extract precise affordance regions from them.

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric RGB image (uniformly resized to 1000×1000 pixels) and a plain text prompt of the form 'When people perform [action] with [object], which part of the [object] is used for [action]? Answer in one sentence.' It produces a pixel-level affordance heatmap at the original image resolution, obtained by upsampling the selected token's masked attention map via bilinear interpolation. A CLIPSeg binary object mask is used as an auxiliary spatial constraint but is not part of the core input specification.

## Method

TokAG is a zero-shot framework that extracts affordance heatmaps from the cross-modal attention maps of a frozen Qwen3-VL backbone without any task-specific training. For each generated output token, it aggregates token-to-image attention weights across all transformer layers and all attention heads (rather than selecting specific layers or heads), then applies a CLIPSeg-derived binary object mask via element-wise multiplication to suppress background noise. A spatial-aware token-selection mechanism computes an overlap score for each token by summing the masked attention values and selects the single token whose attention is most concentrated on the target object region. The final heatmap is the upsampled masked attention map of this selected token. The key insight is that spatial activations vary substantially across output tokens, and the token most focused on the functional part of the object (often decoded to meaningful terms like 'blade', 'seat', or 'handle') yields the most accurate affordance localization.

## Results

On the AGD20K Unseen split (Set 2), TokAG (2B) achieves NSS 1.514, SIM 0.455, and KLD 1.060, improving NSS by 10.7% over the prior best (Moon et al., 1.368). On HICO-IIF, the 32B variant reaches NSS 1.655, a 29.7% gain over the best weakly supervised baseline (1.234). On the Seen split (Set 1), TokAG (2B) also leads with NSS 1.406 and KLD 1.020, outperforming all training-based methods despite using no training data. On EPIC-Aff, TokAG achieves NSS 0.927 vs. WSMA's 0.413, confirming generalization to a different egocentric benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokAG outperforms all six weakly supervised affordance grounding baselines (Cross-View-AG, LOCATE, INTRA, WSMA, LoopTrans, Moon et al.) on every metric across both AGD20K splits and HICO-IIF, with the largest margin over Moon et al. (NSS 1.514 vs. 1.368, +10.7% on AGD20K Unseen) and LoopTrans (NSS 1.514 vs. 1.315). Against foundation-model-based zero-shot baselines, TokAG substantially exceeds direct Qwen3-VL bounding-box prompting (NSS 1.514 vs. 1.085), LVLM+SAM3 pipelines (NSS 1.514 vs. 1.189), and GroundingDINO+SAM3 (NSS 1.514 vs. 0.657), confirming that extracting cross-modal attention maps is far more effective than explicit coordinate prediction or whole-object segmentation. On HICO-IIF, the 32B model achieves NSS 1.655 versus 1.234 for the best prior method (Moon et al.), a 29.7% relative improvement. On EPIC-Aff, TokAG nearly doubles WSMA's NSS (0.927 vs. 0.413) while matching its KLD, indicating robustness to less object-centric ground-truth regions. The only scenario where TokAG does not dominate is when CLIPSeg segmentation fails (NSS drops to 1.168), though even then it remains competitive with the best prior method.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokAG departs fundamentally from the dominant weakly supervised paradigm for affordance grounding (Cross-View-AG, LOCATE, INTRA, WSMA, LoopTrans), which all require training on exocentric images with action-level labels to transfer knowledge to egocentric views. Instead, it repurposes the cross-modal attention mechanisms of pretrained LVLMs (Qwen3-VL) as a spatial signal source, building on the observation from Kang et al. and Zhang et al. that LVLM attention heads encode useful localization cues. The key conceptual departure is the spatial-aware token-selection mechanism: rather than selecting specific layers or heads (which prior visual grounding work requires supervision to identify), TokAG aggregates across all layers and heads and selects the single output token whose attention is most concentrated on the target object region, guided by a CLIPSeg mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Demonstrating that zero-shot affordance grounding can substantially outperform weakly supervised methods (10.7% NSS on AGD20K unseen, 29.7% on HICO-IIF) shifts the field's assumptions about the necessity of task-specific training data for spatial localization. The interpretability finding—that selected tokens often decode to meaningful functional parts like 'blade' or 'mattress'—provides a mechanistic explanation for why the approach works and suggests LVLMs implicitly encode functional object knowledge. Key limitations remain: the single-token assumption fails for compositional affordances (e.g., 'pour from' requiring both handle and spout) and bimanual interactions, and 37.7% of failures stem from CLIPSeg segmentation errors, indicating sensitivity to the quality of the external mask. Future work on multi-region token aggregation and reducing dependency on segmentation quality would address these gaps.
