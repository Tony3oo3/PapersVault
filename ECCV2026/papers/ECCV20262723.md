## Problem

Event cameras offer high temporal resolution and low latency ideal for motion estimation, yet no real high-resolution event camera dataset provides scene flow ground truth with independent moving objects (IMOs). Existing datasets either assume static scenes (introducing ground truth errors where IMOs are present), use low-resolution sensors, or are entirely synthetic, leaving a critical gap for training and benchmarking event-based scene flow methods in dynamic real-world conditions.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides stereo event streams from two Prophesee EVK4 cameras (IMX636 sensor, 1280×720, ~20 cm baseline, ~41° FOV with 90% overlap at 3 m) stored in HDF5 files, synchronized in hardware with a 33-camera Optitrack system. Ground truth is delivered at 200 Hz as dense per-pixel maps for depth, optical flow (both rectified and raw/distorted), and scene flow (3D velocity vectors [Ẋ Ẏ Ẑ]ᵀ), plus binary segmentation masks for each IMO and full 6DoF poses for cameras and all seven moving objects. Sequences span 43 recordings (2.2 TB total, ~42 min) across three categories (static scene, drones, YCB objects) with controlled variations in camera motion type, velocity, and number of IMOs.

## Method

Ground truth is generated by projecting high-fidelity 3D models onto the camera image plane using optitrack-measured poses. The scene model comes from a FARO Focus3D laser scan (millimeter accuracy), while IMO models are obtained via laser scanning and Meshroom photogrammetry (drones) or updated YCB scans. Depth maps are produced by first projecting the background environment, densifying gaps with nearest-neighbor assignment, then overlaying foreground objects whose masks are computed via Delaunay triangulation (edges < 4 px) and filled; occlusions are resolved by taking the minimum depth. Scene flow is computed as the 3D relative velocity between each surface point and the camera, and optical flow is derived analytically by differentiating the pinhole projection function with respect to time. Calibration combines Kalibr-based intrinsic/extrinsic estimation with an iterative hand-eye refinement (quasi-Newton minimization of re-projection error over AprilGrid corners) to align the optitrack rigid-body frame to the actual sensor frame, achieving sub-pixel alignment between ground truth and events.

## Results

E-MOTION provides 43 sequences (42 minutes, 2.2 TB) recorded with a stereo pair of Prophesee EVK4 (IMX636, 1280×720) event cameras, with dense ground truth for depth, optical flow, scene flow, segmentation, and 6DoF poses at 200 Hz. The dataset features 7 independent moving objects (quadrotors and YCB items) tracked with an Optitrack motion capture system, with an average alignment error of 1.1 cm between the scenario model and the optitrack frame. In the demonstration (Section 6.1), MCEMVS depth estimation performs well at slow and normal camera velocities but degrades significantly at fast velocities and completely fails on IMOs, while contrast-maximization optical flow also produces inaccurate measurements at object boundaries—validating the dataset's utility as a challenging benchmark for dynamic scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-MOTION is positioned against a range of existing event camera datasets, and its key differentiator is being the first to combine real high-resolution (1280×720) stereo event data, dense scene flow ground truth, and independent moving objects in a single dataset. All real-data datasets for depth/motion estimation (MVSEC, DSEC, CoSEC, M3ED, VECtor, SHEF) assume static scenes and therefore produce inaccurate ground truth where moving objects appear. EVIMO2 is the only other real dataset with IMOs and dense ground truth, but it uses VGA-resolution cameras and does not provide scene flow. The only datasets with scene flow ground truth and IMOs (EKubric, BlinkVision) are entirely synthetic, leaving a sim-to-real gap that E-MOTION addresses. The paper's demonstration with SOTA methods (MCEMVS for depth, contrast maximization for optical flow) shows that both fail on IMOs and at high velocities, confirming that existing methods trained or evaluated on static-scene datasets are insufficient for dynamic scenarios and highlighting the practical value of E-MOTION as a benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground as the first real, high-resolution stereo event camera dataset providing scene flow ground truth alongside Independent Moving Objects (IMOs). Prior event camera datasets for depth and motion estimation (MVSEC, DSEC, CoSEC, M3ED, VECtor) all assume static scenes, while the only datasets with scene flow and IMOs (EKubric, BlinkVision) are entirely synthetic. The recording methodology draws on established motion-capture-plus-3D-reconstruction pipelines (Optitrack, FARO scanning, hand-eye calibration via Kalibr) previously used in datasets like EVIMO2 and SHEF, but extends them to a stereo high-resolution setup with 200 Hz dense ground truth for multiple simultaneous tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E-MOTION fills a critical gap that has limited progress in event-based scene flow estimation: the absence of real data with dynamic objects and dense 3D motion ground truth. The qualitative evaluation showing that state-of-the-art methods (MCEMVS, contrast maximization) fail on IMOs and fast motions underscores the practical difficulty of dynamic event-based perception and motivates new algorithmic approaches. Limitations include the indoor-only setting, the fishing-line actuation constraining YCB object motion, the absence of a physical IMU, and residual noise from the line itself. Open questions include whether stereo event information can be effectively exploited for scene flow, how methods generalize from this controlled indoor setting to outdoor dynamic scenes, and whether the 200 Hz ground truth rate is sufficient for the highest-velocity sequences.
