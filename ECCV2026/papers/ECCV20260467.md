## Problem

Text-guided 3D motion editing has been well-studied for single-person scenarios, but extending it to multi-person (dyadic) interactions remains underexplored due to the scarcity of paired source-target-instruction triplets and the complexity of preserving spatio-temporal coupling (synchrony, phase alignment, contact timing) during edits. No dedicated benchmark or method existed for multi-human 3D motion editing, where a small modification to one person can disrupt the interaction semantics of both.

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-person source motion sequence in a non-canonical representation (global joint positions, velocities, 6D local rotations, and binary foot-ground contact per person, concatenated along the feature dimension) and a free-form text editing instruction. The source motion is encoded by a lightweight learnable Transformer encoder (with a CLS query token), while the instruction is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder. The output is an edited two-person 3D motion sequence of the same length, generated via 50-step DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance (γ=3.5), achieving 2.842 samples/s at inference.

## Method

InterEdit is a conditional diffusion model (START_X parameterization) with a 5-block transformer denoiser that uses symmetric interleaved token aggregation to model role-switching and temporal-order effects between two persons. Two key alignment mechanisms distinguish it: (1) Semantic-Aware Plan Token Alignment appends 16 learnable plan tokens to the denoiser sequence and aligns them via an InfoNCE loss against a frozen contrastively-trained motion teacher embedding of the ground-truth target, providing high-level editing intent; (2) Interaction-Aware Frequency Token Alignment computes DCT band-energy descriptors (low/mid/high) from the average and difference of the two persons' motion signals, projects them into 6 frequency control tokens, and supervises them to regress the target's band-energy profiles, regularizing interaction rhythm and synchrony. Synchronized Classifier-Free Guidance drops both text and source conditions simultaneously to avoid leakage, and the total loss combines diffusion reconstruction, per-person geometric losses (velocity, foot-contact, bone-length), inter-person interaction losses (masked distance-map, relative-orientation), and the two auxiliary alignment terms.

## Results

On the InterEdit3D benchmark, InterEdit achieves the lowest FID (0.3707) and the highest retrieval scores across all metrics. For generated-to-target retrieval (instruction adherence), InterEdit reaches R@1/2/3 of 30.82/40.84/47.65%, and for generated-to-source retrieval (source preservation), 17.08/24.04/29.32%. On the InterHuman two-person generation benchmark, InterEdit also attains the best R-Precision (0.523/0.665/0.753 at ranks 1/2/3), confirming that the token-alignment design generalizes beyond editing. In a human preference study against TIMotion, InterEdit is preferred 75.5% overall, 78.5% on instruction adherence, and 81.0% on interaction realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterEdit outperforms all four baselines on every metric in Table 2. Against the strongest baseline TIMotion [46], InterEdit improves generated-to-target R@1 by +5.85 points (30.82 vs. 24.97), R@2 by +7.07, and R@3 by +6.97, while also improving source-preservation R@1 by +4.54 and reducing FID by 16.7% (0.3707 vs. 0.4451). The adapted single-person editors (MotionFix, MotionLab) lag substantially in instruction adherence (g2t R@1 of 11.60% and 18.50% respectively), confirming that interaction-aware modeling is essential for dual-person editing. The two-person generation baselines (InterGen, TIMotion) achieve better g2t than the single-person editors but remain limited in g2s, since they are not designed to balance instruction following with source preservation. On the InterHuman generation benchmark (Table 3), InterEdit also leads TIMotion by +3.2 points in R-Precision@1 (0.523 vs. 0.491), indicating the token-alignment architecture generalizes to pure text-to-motion generation as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: single-person text-guided motion editing (MotionFix, MotionLab) and multi-person motion generation (InterGen, TIMotion). It departs from both by introducing the editing constraint—source preservation plus instruction-following—into the multi-person setting, a combination absent from prior work. Architecturally, it inherits TIMotion's symmetric interleaved token scheme and Localized Pattern Amplification, InterGen's non-canonical motion representation and geometric/interaction losses, and standard classifier-free diffusion, while adding two novel alignment mechanisms (semantic plan tokens via InfoNCE and DCT-based frequency tokens) that have no direct precedent in either editing or generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first multi-person motion editing benchmark (TMME) and dataset (InterEdit3D), this paper provides a concrete evaluation target that was previously missing, enabling systematic comparison of interaction-aware editing methods. The 'change what is requested, preserve the rest' constraint is substantially harder in interactive settings due to spatio-temporal coupling, and the paper demonstrates that frequency-domain regularization of interaction signals (DCT band-energy) is a useful inductive bias for maintaining synchrony and coordination. Open questions include scaling beyond two participants, handling longer-horizon or multi-stage edits, and whether the retrieval-based evaluation metrics adequately capture interaction quality. The dataset's reliance on InterHuman and its semi-automatic construction pipeline also raise questions about annotation quality and coverage of rare interaction types.
