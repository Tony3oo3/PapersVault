## Problem

Existing 3D geometric foundation models (Depth Anything, DUSt3R, VGGT) are constrained to moderate resolutions (~960×480) because dense inference at 2K-level resolution incurs prohibitive computational and memory costs, making large-scale deployment impractical. Prior high-resolution approaches either require full retraining at 2K, introduce boundary artifacts through patch-wise processing, or rely on simple upsampling that fails to recover fine geometric structures. This paper addresses the gap by enabling existing frozen foundation models to produce accurate 2K geometric predictions without any backbone modification or retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261054/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts high-resolution (2K, e.g., 1440×1920) RGB images—either a single frame for monocular depth or a set of multi-view images for pointmap estimation. The input is first downsampled to a tractable resolution (~256 px on the longer side) for the frozen backbone, then the output is upsampled back to full 2K resolution. The final output is a dense 2K depth map (C=1) or 2K 3D point map (C=3), where only a sparse subset (~10%) of high-uncertainty pixels undergoes full-resolution refinement while the rest retain the upsampled coarse prediction.

## Method

2K Retrofit is a two-stage pipeline: (i) a frozen geometric backbone (e.g., Depth Anything v2 or VGGT) produces a coarse low-resolution prediction that is nearest-neighbor upsampled to 2K; (ii) an entropy-based selector computes per-pixel uncertainty from the backbone's pre-regression head features and selects the top ~10% highest-entropy pixels for targeted refinement. A modified MinkowskiUNet sparse convolutional network processes only the selected irregular pixel locations to predict local residual corrections, avoiding the inefficiency of dense CNNs on sparse, non-uniform point sets. A gated ensembler then adaptively fuses the coarse estimate with the sparse corrections via a two-layer MLP conditioned on both predictions and their entropies, producing a smooth sigmoid weight that balances global consistency with local detail. The entire refinement module is trained end-to-end on 50K synthetic frames rendered in NVIDIA Omniverse for 13 epochs, while the backbone remains frozen throughout.

## Results

On ETH3D monocular depth estimation, 2K Retrofit achieves AbsRel 0.0192, RMSE 0.0877, and δ₀.₅ 0.9700 at 8.1 FPS, outperforming the strongest baseline PRO (AbsRel 0.0217, 6.2 FPS) by over 12% in AbsRel while running 30% faster. On ETH3D point map estimation, 2K Retrofit achieves overall error 0.839 (Acc 0.935, Comp 0.602) at 5.5 FPS, reducing overall error by ~15% versus VGGT (0.903) and ~30% versus DUSt3R (1.195). On ARKitScenes and ScanNet++ (zero-shot), the method achieves AbsRel 0.0118 and 0.0146 respectively, surpassing all baselines including PromptDA and MSPF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For monocular depth estimation on ETH3D, 2K Retrofit (AbsRel 0.0192) outperforms all baselines, with the largest margins over BPNet (0.1494) and Marigold (0.0711), and a 12% improvement over the closest competitor PRO (0.0217) while also being 30% faster (8.1 vs. 6.2 FPS). For point map estimation, 2K Retrofit (Overall 0.839) beats VGGT (0.903) by ~7%, WinT3R (0.891) by ~6%, and DUSt3R (1.195) by ~30%, while running at 5.5 FPS versus 2.6 FPS for VGGT and 1.1 FPS for DUSt3R. In the MVS comparison on ETH3D, 2K Retrofit (F1 84.88, 0.3 s) surpasses CascadeMVS (80.84, 0.4 s), RAFT-Stereo (81.42, 0.8 s), and MVSFormer++ (83.75, 1.5 s), and is competitive with fine-tuned MVSFormer++† (85.13) while being 5× faster. On ARKitScenes and ScanNet++ (zero-shot), 2K Retrofit (AbsRel 0.0118 / 0.0146) beats PromptDA (0.0131 / 0.0175) and MSPF (0.0149 / 0.0226), confirming consistent gains across datasets. The method also demonstrates plug-and-play generalization to Pi3 and Depth Anything v3 without retraining, though it remains slightly behind retrained VGGT at 2K in absolute accuracy (0.935 vs. 0.911 Acc) while using 57% less VRAM and running 17× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the sparse refinement paradigm from 2D segmentation (PointRend, SparseRefine) and applies it to high-resolution 3D geometry prediction, a domain where selective refinement had been largely unexplored. It departs from patch-based high-resolution depth methods (PatchFusion, PatchRefiner, PRO) that require multiple low-resolution inferences and complex stitching, instead using a single coarse inference followed by entropy-guided correction on a sparse pixel subset. The sparse feature extractor borrows MinkowskiUNet from sparse 3D convolution, and the overall design is explicitly model-agnostic, functioning as a plug-and-play 'retrofit' on frozen backbones (Depth Anything, VGGT, DUSt3R) without architectural modification or retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 17× speedup over retraining VGGT at 2K (8.5 vs. 0.5 FPS) with over 50% reduction in memory and FLOPs, while matching or exceeding accuracy, makes 2K-level geometric prediction feasible for real-time deployment in robotics and AR. The plug-and-play compatibility across multiple foundation model backbones (demonstrated with Depth Anything v2, VGGT, Pi3, and Depth Anything v3) suggests broad adoptability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 2K to 4K+ resolutions, whether stronger geometric priors can address the acknowledged failure modes on reflective/transparent/textureless surfaces, and whether the entropy signal remains discriminative as backbone architectures evolve.
