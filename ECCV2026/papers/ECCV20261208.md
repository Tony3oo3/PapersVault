## Problem

Existing lineart colorization methods are designed for a single additional control signal (text, sparse color hints, or a reference image), making them inflexible for production workflows that require simultaneous, potentially conflicting constraints. No prior framework supports arbitrary combinations of heterogeneous guidance signals while maintaining structural fidelity and temporal consistency across animation sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261208/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a lineart as the mandatory structural input and accepts any subset of five optional conditions: text prompts, sparse color hints (uniform pixel blocks ≥10×10), an identity reference image, a recent history frame (immediate preceding frame in the same shot), and long-term history frames (from adjacent shots). Output is a colorized RGB image at 1376×768 resolution. Conditions are internally partitioned into spatially-aligned (pixel-level: lineart, color hints, recent history) and semantic-reference (high-level: text, ID maps, long-term history), which determines their encoding pathway.

## Method

OmniColor builds on an MMDiT backbone trained with flow matching, where spatially-aligned conditions are processed through a dual-encoder (VAE for high-frequency boundaries + VLM for localized semantics) and semantic-reference conditions through a VLM-only encoder. A Dense Feature Alignment loss using DINOv3 features is applied selectively during late denoising (t > 0.7) to enforce structural correspondence beyond what the latent-space flow matching objective provides. A Temporal Redundancy Elimination module computes color-histogram similarity between adjacent-frame patches, flags redundant regions, and extracts only delta-content bounding boxes to shorten the token sequence by ~25%. An Adaptive Spatial-Semantic Gating module, integrated into the joint attention blocks, derives a global spatial descriptor via a low-rank bottleneck and uses it to compute per-token gating scores that modulate the semantic branch (range [0.5, 1.5]), with LoRA zero-initialization ensuring identity mapping at training start.

## Results

In the L+T (lineart + text) setting, OmniColor achieves Image-Align 0.9242, SSIM 0.7572, PSNR 16.19, FID 84.86, and LPIPS 0.2817, substantially outperforming the best unified multi-modal baseline (Qwen-Image-Edit: FID 148.49, LPIPS 0.4682) and text-based methods (Tag2pix: FID 179.84). With sequential history and first-frame reference (L+T+H+G), OmniColor reaches FID 27.76, LPIPS 0.0770, PSNR 34.65, and ΔFC 0.14, surpassing the best reference-based method LVCD (FID 36.08, ΔFC 4.65) on every metric. The user study confirms a dominant 81.0% preference rate in Structural Fidelity and 61.0% overall preference over three SoTA competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the L+T setting, OmniColor (FID 84.86, LPIPS 0.2817) outperforms all 14 baselines: it beats the best text-based method Tag2pix (FID 179.84) by 53% in FID, and the strongest unified multi-modal model Qwen-Image-Edit (FID 148.49) by 43% in FID and 40% in LPIPS. Against reference-based methods (which use L+G), OmniColor with L+T+H+G dominates across all six metrics, achieving FID 27.76 vs. LVCD's 36.08 and LPIPS 0.0770 vs. 0.2699, while also reducing temporal inconsistency (ΔFC 0.14 vs. 4.65). The closest competitor on temporal consistency is AniDoc (ΔFC 0.44), but OmniColor still leads by 3×. OmniColor's advantage is most pronounced in structural fidelity (Image-Align 0.9769 vs. 0.9025 for Manganinja) and perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.0770 vs. 0.2699 for LVCD), confirming that the dual-encoder + DFA loss design provides substantially tighter spatial alignment than prior single-condition approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MMDiT backbone (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as its generative foundation, initializing from Qwen-Image-Edit. It departs from prior lineart colorization work—which typically supports a single auxiliary signal (text, scribbles, or a reference image)—by introducing a systematic two-way categorization of conditions (spatially-aligned vs. semantic-reference) with tailored encoding paths for each. Unlike general unified multi-modal generation models (Janus, Emu, Flux-Kontext) that rely on prompt engineering or generic adapters to balance heterogeneous inputs, OmniColor designs task-specific modules (dual-encoder + DFA loss for spatial inputs, VLM-only + TRE for semantic inputs, and AS-Gate for conflict resolution), positioning itself as a purpose-built framework rather than a general-purpose editor repurposed for colorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine production bottleneck in animation pipelines where artists need to combine structural lineart, identity references, color hints, and temporal history simultaneously without re-running the entire generation. The modular design (spatial vs. semantic branches, adaptive gating) offers a reusable template for other conditional generation tasks with heterogeneous constraints. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond anime-style lineart to more abstract or photorealistic sketches, whether the 150-second inference time is acceptable for interactive workflows, and whether the AS-Gate's learned balancing transfers to unseen condition combinations not seen during training.
