## Problem

Existing VFI methods operate at a single granularity: pixel-level approaches face infinite correspondence possibilities that undermine global motion consistency, while object-level approaches capture global object motion but produce discontinuous local motion within objects. No prior deep-learning VFI method explicitly leverages open-world object priors to bridge this gap between pixel-level and object-level motion estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262125/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive video frames (I_0, I_1) and SAM-generated object masks (preprocessed so each pixel belongs to exactly one mask, with uncovered pixels assigned to a background mask) as input, and produces a single intermediate frame I_t at a user-specified temporal position t. Training uses 224×224 patches from Vimeo90K; evaluation spans 448×256 (Vimeo90K), up to 1280×720 (SNU-FILM), and 2K/4K (Xiph). The efficient variant (HML+HFL only) introduces zero additional inference cost, while the full variant adds HCE and SAM segmentation overhead at inference time.

## Method

The framework builds on AMT/IFRNet pixel-level VFI baselines and introduces three SAM-prior-driven adaptations. (1) A Hybrid Contextual Feature Extraction (HCE) module indexes object-level global features from encoder outputs using SAM masks, then fuses them with pixel-level features via channel attention to produce hybrid discriminative representations for motion estimation. (2) A Hierarchical Motion Loss (HML) adaptively distills pseudo optical flows from LiteFlow by computing per-pixel and per-object end-point errors, then modulating Charbonnier loss parameters (ε, α) so that regions with larger deviations from pseudo labels receive weaker supervision, suppressing harmful knowledge at both granularities. (3) A Hierarchical Feature Loss (HFL) extracts VGG19 features from predicted and ground-truth intermediate frames, applies masked self-attention (with negative-infinite penalties across object boundaries) to compute intra-object global correlations, and penalizes correlation differences to enforce motion continuity within each object. HML and HFL are training-only losses; HCE is the sole inference-time addition in the full configuration.

## Results

On the SNU-FILM Extreme subset (most challenging), the full model (Ours AMT-G1*) achieves 30.97 PSNR / 0.9384 SSIM / 0.0583 LPIPS, surpassing the strongest pixel-level baselines by 0.77 dB over RIFE, 0.35 dB over IFRNet-L1*, and 0.18 dB over AMT-G1*. On Xiph-4K, the method reaches 34.87 PSNR / 0.9490 SSIM / 0.1295 LPIPS, outperforming EMA-VFI by 0.30 dB. On Vimeo90K, the full model scores 36.62 PSNR / 0.9820 SSIM / 0.0168 LPIPS, while the efficient variant (Ours IFRNet-L1*) achieves 36.28 PSNR with zero additional inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The full model (Ours AMT-G1*) outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark. On the most challenging SNU-FILM Extreme subset, it surpasses RIFE by 0.77 dB (30.97 vs. 30.10 PSNR), IFRNet-L1* by 0.35 dB (30.97 vs. 30.55), and AMT-G1* by 0.18 dB (30.97 vs. 30.75). On Xiph-4K, it leads EMA-VFI by 0.30 dB (34.87 vs. 34.57 PSNR) and AMT-G1* by 0.14 dB. The efficient variant (Ours IFRNet-L1*), which adds no inference overhead, still beats IFRNet-L1* by 0.22 dB on Xiph-4K (34.31 vs. 34.16) and matches or exceeds RIFE on Vimeo90K. VFIFormer achieves competitive PSNR on SNU-FILM but fails (OOM) on Xiph-4K, while VFI-Mamba and AdaCoF trail by more than 1 dB on the Extreme subset. The gains are most pronounced in large-motion and occlusion-heavy scenarios, confirming that object-level priors address the correspondence ambiguity inherent in pixel-only methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AMT (CVPR 2023) as its pixel-level VFI backbone and extends IFRNet's (CVPR 2022) task-oriented flow distillation loss into a hierarchical, object-aware variant. It departs from the dominant pixel-level motion estimation paradigm by explicitly incorporating open-world object priors from SAM, bridging a gap between pixel-level methods (which suffer from correspondence ambiguity) and earlier object-level methods (which relied on fixed semantic categories). The three proposed adaptations—HCE for hybrid feature extraction, HML for adaptive distillation, and HFL for intra-object continuity—represent a systematic integration of segmentation priors into both the forward pass and the training objective, a direction not previously explored in VFI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering two deployment configurations: a full-accuracy variant (HCE+HML+HFL) and a zero-overhead efficiency variant (HML+HFL only, applied at training time), making the approach accessible to real-time applications. However, performance gains are modest (0.18–0.77 dB over strong baselines on challenging subsets), and the method's effectiveness is contingent on SAM mask quality, which degrades under severe occlusion or ambiguous boundaries. Open questions include whether stronger segmentation models or active object-level motion control (e.g., user-specified trajectories) can push the framework beyond its current limitations on extreme non-rigid motions.
