## Problem

Existing parametric body models (SMPL family) are trained on expensive, demographically narrow 3D scan datasets (e.g., CAESAR: <5000 adults aged 18–65 from three countries), making them unsuitable for representing the full diversity of human bodies—especially children, elderly individuals, and uncommon morphologies. No single open, scan-free model covers the entire human lifespan with interpretable shape control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260286/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Anny body model takes continuous phenotype parameters (age, gender, height, weight, muscle, proportions, each in [0,1]) and joint rotation pose parameters, producing a posed mesh of 13,718 vertices and 13,710 quadrilateral faces via differentiable forward kinematics and blend skinning over 163 bones. For HMR, the network consumes full RGB images (1280×1280 in Anny-One; uncropped for multi-person, human-centric crops for single-person) and regresses Anny's shape and pose parameters. The model also supports alternative topologies (SMPL-X: 10,475 verts; coarse: 1,229 verts) for interoperability with existing pipelines.

## Method

Anny replaces learned PCA shape bases with 1,136 artist-designed prototypical blendshapes from MakeHuman, extended to a continuous shape space via piecewise multilinear interpolation of vertex positions, where each phenotype parameter in [0,1] linearly blends between adjacent prototypical shapes. The shape distribution is calibrated to WHO growth standards by fitting age- and gender-conditioned Beta distributions to match population height and BMI statistics. For HMR, the authors adapt two ViT-based architectures (HMR2.0 with ViT-H for single-person; Multi-HMR with ViT-L for multi-person) to predict Anny parameters instead of SMPL-X, training on the 780k-image Anny-One synthetic dataset (rendered in Blender Cycles with Infinigen Indoors scenes, AMASS body poses, GRAB hand poses, and HumGen3D clothing) combined with standard HMR data (BEDLAM, MS-COCO, MPII). A self-collision check and bounding-volume-hierarchy intersection test ensure physical plausibility during data generation, and sparse linear regressors enable bidirectional vertex mapping between Anny and SMPL-X/HumGen3D topologies (1.3 mm cyclic consistency error).

## Results

On the CMU-Toddler benchmark (which includes children), Multi-HMR+Anny achieves 102.1 MPJPE and 263.8 Pair-PA-MPJPE, dramatically outperforming the original Multi-HMR (153.6 / 638.9) and all other baselines. On 3DPW, the method achieves 41.8 PA-MPJPE and 83.2 PVE, beating AiOS (45.0 / 90.9) and SAT-HMR (52.7 / 94.5) on shape accuracy while being competitive on MPJPE (71.5 vs. 68.8 for AiOS). On EMDB, Multi-HMR+Anny reaches 48.5 PA-MPJPE and 83.4 PVE, substantially outperforming AiOS (63.3 / 108.1) and SAT-HMR (71.0 / 126.7). The scan-fitting evaluation on 3DBodyTex adults yields a 2.5 mm mean point-to-mesh error, competitive with scan-based models (1.8 mm for ATLAS, 1.9 mm for SMPL-X with 300 components).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original Multi-HMR (SMPL-X), swapping in Anny yields consistent gains: PA-MPJPE improves from 46.9 to 41.8 on 3DPW, PVE drops from 88.8 to 83.2, and the most dramatic improvement appears on CMU-Toddler where MPJPE falls from 153.6 to 102.1 and Pair-PA-MPJPE from 638.9 to 263.8, confirming Anny's advantage in modeling children. Compared to AiOS, Multi-HMR+Anny wins on PA-MPJPE (41.8 vs. 45.0) and PVE (83.2 vs. 90.9) on 3DPW, and dominates on EMDB (48.5 vs. 63.3 PA-MPJPE) and CMU-Toddler (102.1 vs. 162.4 MPJPE), though AiOS retains a slight MPJPE edge on 3DPW (68.8 vs. 71.5). Against SAT-HMR, the gap is even wider: Multi-HMR+Anny outperforms on every reported metric across all four benchmarks, with the largest margins on EMDB (48.5 vs. 71.0 PA-MPJPE) and CMU-Toddler (102.1 vs. 153.9 MPJPE). On the AGORA benchmark (Table 2), Anny with Anny-One pretraining + fine-tuning achieves 72.8 PVE and 69.3 PVE on AGORA-Kids, beating SMPL-X+A (76.6 / 77.6) and SMPL-X (78.2 / 96.5) in the full-data regime. The scan-fitting comparison shows Anny at 2.5 mm on 3DBodyTex adults, slightly behind ATLAS (1.8 mm) and SMPL-X-300 (1.9 mm), but outperforming SMPL-X on all three child scans (2.0 mm vs. 3.2 mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

Anny departs from the dominant scan-based parametric body model lineage (SMPL, SMPL-X, ATLAS, MHR) by replacing learned shape bases from 3D scans with artist-designed blendshapes from the MakeHuman community, connected via piecewise multilinear interpolation over interpretable phenotype parameters (age, gender, height, weight, etc.). For the HMR evaluation, it builds directly on the ViT-based architectures of HMR2.0 and Multi-HMR, adapting them to regress Anny parameters instead of SMPL-X, and leverages HumGen3D and Infinigen Indoors for synthetic scene generation. The paper also positions itself against the fragmented approach of separate demographic models (SMIL for infants, SMPL-X+A interpolation) by offering a single unified model spanning the full lifespan, calibrated to WHO growth standards rather than proprietary scan distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Anny provides the first fully open, scan-free, interpretable body model that covers infants through elders in a single topology, removing the privacy, cost, and demographic-bias constraints of scan-based models. The Anny-One dataset (780k photorealistic images) is a substantial resource that demonstrates synthetic data from a simple body model can match or exceed scan-based training data for HMR, particularly for underrepresented populations like children. Open questions remain: the phenotype parameters encode artist stereotypes rather than measured anthropometry, the model does not capture fine skin-fold deformations of high-BMI bodies, and the paper acknowledges that the precise mechanism by which Anny's parameterization aids or hinders learned HMR is unclear. Whether the community will adopt Anny as a SMPL-X replacement at scale, and whether its interpretability translates to better generalization on truly out-of-distribution morphologies, will require broader empirical validation.
