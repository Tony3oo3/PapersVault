## Problem

Existing omnidirectional stereo matching methods apply spatially uniform update policies across the entire equirectangular domain, yet fisheye radial distortion and view-transition ambiguity concentrate severe geometric uncertainty in seam regions (azimuths θ=±π/2) where front and back cameras observe the scene through their most distorted peripheral fields. No prior method explicitly models this spatially varying uncertainty to selectively expand the receptive field where matching cues are sparse, leaving depth errors consistently peaked at seam boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261738/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes four calibrated fisheye RGB images (768×800, 220° FOV) from front, right, back, and left cameras and produces an omnidirectional inverse depth map on the equirectangular domain. Depth is parameterized as an index over N=192 concentric spherical sweeping hypotheses, and evaluation is restricted to the elevation band −π/4 ≤ φ ≤ π/4 at 160×640 resolution. The PDD distortion field is precomputed once per spatial resolution and reused across all T=12 refinement iterations, so the geometric prior adds no per-iteration overhead.

## Method

PDF-Omni builds on RomniStereo's adaptive-weighting iterative refinement pipeline and introduces three geometry-aware components. First, a Poincaré Dual Disk distortion field instantiates two unit Poincaré disks at the front and back camera optical axes, combining a radial term (tanh of the minimum geodesic distance) with a view-transition term (similarity of the two distances) into a single scalar field D that peaks at seam regions. Second, a Distortion-Aware Attention module (CBAM-based) conditions a spatial attention map on D, which drives a Distortion-informed GRU that blends a 1×1 small-kernel branch with a separable 1×7+7×1 large-kernel branch, allocating wider receptive fields only where geometric uncertainty is high. Third, a Local Entropy Cumulative loss adapts the supervision neighborhood radius to per-pixel cost-volume entropy (with stop-gradient on the radius), relaxing supervision for ambiguous high-entropy predictions. The full model is pre-trained on OmniThings for 30 epochs and fine-tuned for 15 epochs on target datasets, using a sequence loss with exponentially increasing weights over T=12 iterations.

## Results

After fine-tuning on OmniHouse and Sunny, PDF-Omni-ft achieves the best results across all five datasets on every metric. On Sunny, it reaches >1=3.54%, MAE=0.25, RMS=1.26, outperforming the closest competitor MDP-Omni-ft (>1=4.51%, MAE=0.33, RMS=1.43). On OmniThings, PDF-Omni-ft reduces >1 error to 17.75% versus 29.53% for MDP-Omni-ft, and on OmniHouse it achieves >1=2.86% and MAE=0.23. In seam regions specifically, PDF-Omni-ft narrows the seam-to-non-seam >1 gap to 1.04 percentage points, compared to 1.83 for MDP-Omni-ft and 1.90 for RomniStereo-ft.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDF-Omni-ft outperforms all six learned baselines across all five datasets and all five metrics. The closest competitor is MDP-Omni-ft [17], which PDF-Omni-ft surpasses by 11.78 points on OmniThings >1 (17.75 vs. 29.53), 2.75 points on OmniHouse >1 (2.86 vs. 5.61), and 0.97 points on Sunny >1 (3.54 vs. 4.51). RomniStereo-ft [10], the prior RAFT-based iterative method, is beaten by 16.57 points on OmniThings >1 and 1.65 points on Sunny >1. OmniMVS+₃₂-ft [27] and OmniMVS-ft [25] (3D CNN paradigms) lag substantially, with OmniMVS-ft exceeding 50% >1 error on OmniThings. S-OmniMVS-ft [5] reports results only on OmniHouse and Sunny, where PDF-Omni-ft still leads by 4.13 and 3.12 points on >1 respectively. In the drop-in replacement experiment (Table 6), DAA+DiGRU inserted into RomniStereo reduces OmniThings >1 by 15.4% and OmniHouse MAE by 21.3% relative to the original ConvGRU, confirming the modules' generality beyond the full PDF-Omni pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDF-Omni extends the RAFT-based iterative refinement paradigm (RAFT-Stereo, RomniStereo) to omnidirectional stereo matching by replacing the uniform GRU update with a geometry-driven selective mechanism. The selective two-branch blending is adapted from Selective-Stereo, but the paper's key departure is substituting frequency-based spatial attention with a Poincaré Dual Disk distortion field that encodes fisheye radial distortion and view-transition ambiguity in a unified hyperbolic-geometric formulation. This extends the single-disk Poincaré modeling of DCPB and FisheyeHDK from single-camera recognition to the multi-camera OSM setting, where distortion varies jointly with azimuth and camera-pair geometry. The Distortion-Aware Attention module is a direct extension of CBAM with the PDD field injected as an explicit geometric input, and the LEC loss introduces entropy-adaptive supervision not present in prior OSM or stereo matching losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Seam regions have been a persistent, unaddressed weakness in OSM where all prior methods show concentrated error peaks; PDF-Omni provides the first explicit geometric model of this spatially varying uncertainty and demonstrates that it can be exploited for adaptive receptive field allocation. The drop-in replacement experiments (Table 6) showing 15–21% relative improvements when DAA+DiGRU is inserted into RomniStereo suggest the modules are architecture-agnostic upgrades for any RAFT-based omnidirectional framework. Limitations include evaluation exclusively on synthetic datasets, a fixed 4-camera front/right/back/left configuration, and a precomputed (non-learned) distortion field that assumes the analytic Poincaré model accurately captures real fisheye optics. Open questions include whether the PDD formulation generalizes to arbitrary camera counts or non-opposite pairings, and whether a learned distortion field could outperform the closed-form Poincaré geodesic distance.
