## Problem

Modern dense predictors rely on pyramidal pixel decoding that progressively lifts low-resolution features to dense outputs, tightly coupling computation to output resolution. This creates two walls: an efficiency wall where memory and latency escalate sharply with resolution, and a capability wall where additional pixels fail to improve—and can even degrade—local geometric detail. The paper asks whether high-capacity dense prediction can be achieved by decoupling reasoning from rendering, concentrating modeling capacity in a compact latent space and delegating resolution to a lightweight pixel readout.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at arbitrary resolution as input; it is trained at a fixed 768×768 crop but extrapolates to ultra-high-resolution photographs up to 100 megapixels at inference. It produces pixel-aligned dense fields: a relative depth map (K=1) for depth estimation or a per-pixel class assignment (K semantic classes) for segmentation. The key constraint is that the decoder operates on a compact latent grid aligned with the ViT patch layout (N = H/P × W/P tokens), so the spatial resolution of the output is determined entirely by the readout stage rather than by the decoding computation.

## Method

L-DPT decomposes dense prediction into two stages: a high-capacity latent decoder that performs attention-based reasoning on a single compact token grid, and a lightweight pixel readout (linear projection + pixel shuffle) that renders the latent predictions to the output resolution. For feature integration, all intermediate encoder features are concatenated into a shared pool, and each decoding stage applies its own linear projection to adaptively select and compress encoder information, which is then additively injected into the latent state—replacing the fixed stage-to-stage binding of pyramidal decoders. The latent decoder is a stack of self-attention blocks with 2D RoPE using continuous normalized coordinates (following DINOv3), augmented during training with random coordinate jitter and rescaling to encourage resolution extrapolation. Training uses a fixed 768×768 crop with strong bicubic scale augmentation (×0.5 to ×2.0) and RoPE coordinate rescaling (×1/8 to ×8), so resolution shifts become part of the training distribution. The backbone is a frozen DINOv3 ViT-7B encoder, and the decoder family (S/B/L: 23M/98M/269M parameters) traces a clear accuracy-efficiency frontier across depth estimation and semantic segmentation.

## Results

Under a controlled setting with a frozen DINOv3 ViT-7B encoder (only the decoder is trained), L-DPT L (269M decoder params) achieves ARel 4.3 / δ1 98.2 on NYUv2, 7.4 / 96.5 on KITTI, 5.2 / 97.6 on ETH3D, and 22.0 / 84.5 on DIODE in zero-shot relative depth estimation. The smallest variant, L-DPT S (23M), already surpasses the much heavier DPT(DINOv3) decoder (230M) on DIODE (δ1 84.4 vs. 82.2) with 10× fewer trainable parameters. On semantic segmentation, L-DPT L reaches 54.2 mIoU (TTA) on COCO-Stuff 164k and 94.1 mIoU (TTA) on PASCAL VOC 2012, outperforming the DINOv3 Mask2Former baseline (54.0 / 90.4) with a substantially lighter decoder. Inference on 100MP images requires only 15.3 GB peak memory and 5.3 s latency for L-DPT, versus 59.4 GB and 394 s for a DPT decoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the controlled DPT(DINOv3) baseline (same frozen ViT-7B encoder, 230M DPT decoder), L-DPT S (23M) wins on DIODE (δ1 84.4 vs. 82.2) and NYUv2 (δ1 98.4 vs. 98.0) with 10× fewer decoder parameters, while L-DPT L matches or beats DPT on NYUv2 (ARel 4.3 vs. 4.3), ETH3D (ARel 5.2 vs. 5.4), and DIODE (δ1 84.5 vs. 82.2), at a slight cost on KITTI (ARel 7.4 vs. 7.3). Compared to the original DPT(origin) with mixed-domain supervision, L-DPT L improves δ1 on every benchmark (e.g., 98.9 vs. 90.3 on NYUv2, 87.0 vs. 75.8 on DIODE). Against the DINOv3 Mask2Former segmenter (927M decoder), L-DPT L surpasses it on both COCO-Stuff (54.2 vs. 54.0 TTA mIoU) and VOC 2012 (94.1 vs. 90.4 TTA mIoU) with roughly 3.4× fewer decoder parameters; even L-DPT S (23M) already beats the heavy baseline on VOC (93.6 vs. 90.4). Versus InfiniDepth (the closest arbitrary-resolution depth method), L-DPT-L with DINOv3-7B reduces inference memory from 10.2 GB to 7.6 GB and latency from 3.38 s to 1.99 s while matching or exceeding δ1 on NYUv2 (98.9 vs. 97.8), KITTI (96.8 vs. 95.8), and DIODE (87.0 vs. 85.5), though InfiniDepth retains a small edge on ETH3D (98.6 vs. 98.1). Classical baselines (MiDaS, LeReS, Omnidata) are substantially outperformed across all four depth benchmarks, confirming that the gains come from the latent decoding paradigm rather than backbone scale alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

L-DPT departs from the dominant pyramidal pixel-decoding paradigm (DPT, FPN-style cascades) that underlies most modern dense predictors, arguing that coupling high-capacity computation to the pixel grid creates both an efficiency wall and a capability wall. Conceptually, it repurposes the latent-space philosophy from generative modeling (VAE, latent diffusion, DiT) — where heavy computation lives in a compact latent and pixels are a lightweight readout — and applies it to deterministic dense prediction. On the resolution-extrapolation axis, it builds on RoPE-based positional modeling and coordinate augmentation from DINOv3, and is complementary to implicit-field approaches like InfiniDepth, which query pixels from a fixed encoder grid rather than restructuring the decoder itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 23M-parameter latent decoder matches or beats a 230M DPT decoder under a frozen 7B backbone, and inference at 100MP becomes feasible (15.3 GB / 5.3 s vs. 59.4 GB / 394 s for DPT), opening dense prediction to professional ultra-high-resolution imaging without tiling or resizing. The 'capability wall' finding — that more pixels can make fine structures blurrier under pyramidal decoding — challenges a core assumption in the field and motivates rethinking how resolution scaling interacts with decoder design. Open questions include whether the principle extends to tasks with heterogeneous output resolutions, whether native ultra-high-resolution training (rather than extrapolation from 768px) further improves quality, and how latent decoding interacts with iterative or diffusion-style refinement for uncertainty-aware prediction.
