## Problem

Existing AI-generated image quality assessment methods compress complex generation results into a single coarse-grained score, making it difficult to identify the specific sources of visual errors and providing limited interpretable guidance for targeted generative model optimization. No prior dataset or benchmark systematically evaluates fine-grained quality along the two most critical dimensions for photorealistic content—naturalness (perceptible AI artifacts) and distortion (structural/visual degradation)—at the entity level with spatial grounding and reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single AI-generated RGB image (photorealistic, from models like FLUX, SD3.5, Kwai-Kolors, Dreamina 3.0) and produces a multi-level output: binary Yes/No answers to six structured judgment questions per entity (2 for naturalness, 4 for distortion), bounding boxes localizing problematic entities, free-text descriptions attributing specific quality issues, and a final discrete quality rating (Bad/Poor/Fair/Good/Excellent) whose logits are converted to a continuous score via weighted softmax. In the scoring stage, the input is augmented with a structured CoT context listing detected issues and their coordinates, generated dynamically by the Stage-2 model to maintain training-inference consistency.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-stage progressive fine-tuning pipeline (LoRA, rank 8) for MLLMs such as Qwen3-VL-8B. Stage 1 trains on object-level judgment QA pairs to establish basic naturalness/distortion discrimination. Stage 2 trains on image-level grounding instances where the model must output bounding boxes and reasoning descriptions exclusively for entities exhibiting quality issues, building localization and analysis capability. Stage 3 incorporates the Stage-2 model's own detected-issue outputs as structured CoT context into a scoring prompt, using a rating-token mapping (MOS discretized into five adjective tokens) and a hybrid loss combining cross-entropy for QA format and MSE for score accuracy. The supporting Q-Real dataset (10K images, 32,815 annotated entities) is built via an automated pipeline—GPT-4o for entity extraction and QA generation, Grounding DINO for localization, Q-Eval-Score for conditioning—followed by multi-annotator manual verification and refinement.

## Results

On the quality score prediction task, Q-Real-Score (Qwen3-VL-8B fine-tuned with the three-stage pipeline) achieves SRCC 0.710 / PLCC 0.720 on Q-Real, SRCC 0.608 / PLCC 0.611 on AGIQA-3K, and 60.7% accuracy on ImageReward, outperforming the previous best Q-Eval-Score (SRCC 0.690/0.693, 0.587/0.582, 60.1%) across all three benchmarks. On the fine-grained ObjectQA task, fine-tuned models push most accuracy and F1 scores above 0.7, surpassing closed-source GPT-5.4 and Gemini 3.1 Pro. On ImageQA, fine-tuning dramatically improves detection rate from 0.06–0.31 (zero-shot) to 0.49–0.60 and LLM-Score from 0.23–0.44 to 0.63–0.74, demonstrating that the model learns to localize and describe specific quality issues rather than indiscriminately flagging all entities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-Real-Score outperforms all five quality-assessment baselines in Table 3: it beats Q-Eval-Score by +0.020 SRCC on Q-Real and +0.021 SRCC on AGIQA-3K, and edges out Q-Align by +0.059 SRCC on Q-Real. On the fine-grained ObjectQA task (Table 1), fine-tuned open-source MLLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B* reaching 0.777 Acc on Distortion Q1) surpass both GPT-5.4 (0.640 Acc) and Gemini 3.1-Pro (0.691 Acc), with the largest gains in F1 score (up to +57.3% over zero-shot). On ImageQA (Table 2), fine-tuned models dramatically outperform all baselines in detection rate: Qwen2.5-VL-7B* reaches 0.596 (naturalness) vs. 0.069 for GPT-5.4 and 0.165 for Q-Eval-Score, while LLM-Score jumps to 0.713 vs. 0.439 (GPT-5.4) and 0.353 (Q-Eval-Score). Cross-dataset validation on RichHF-18K (Table 4) confirms generalizability: InternVL3-8B* achieves 0.542 recall and 0.635 precision on distortion localization, far exceeding its zero-shot baseline (0.172/0.175). The ablation in Table 6 shows that training-inference consistency (TIC) is the most critical design choice in Stage 3, with models lacking it dropping to 0.692 SRCC vs. 0.710 with TIC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fine-grained image quality assessment line (Q-Instruct, Grounding-IQA) from user-generated content to photorealistic AI-generated images, and unifies two complementary quality dimensions—naturalness and distortion—under a single entity-level framework with spatial grounding and descriptive reasoning. For the scoring component, it adopts the discrete rating token mapping strategy from Q-Align and Q-Boost, and leverages the Q-Eval-100K dataset as both a data source and a baseline. It departs from prior AI-generated image quality work (RichHF-18K, FakeXplain, X-AIGD) by providing structured judgment questions, bounding-box localization, and free-text attribution descriptions in a unified annotation schema rather than heatmaps or region-level classification alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing interpretable, entity-level quality signals rather than a single scalar score, Q-Real offers actionable feedback for targeted optimization of generative models—a gap the authors identify as critical for iterative model improvement. The cross-dataset validation on RichHF-18K suggests the learned representations generalize beyond the training distribution, though the dataset is limited to photorealistic images and relies on GPT-4o for initial annotation, introducing potential model-specific biases. Open questions include whether this framework extends to non-photorealistic styles, video generation, or other modalities, and whether the 10K scale is sufficient for robust generalization across the rapidly evolving landscape of generative models.
