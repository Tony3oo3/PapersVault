## Problem

Existing video-driven mesh animation methods rely exclusively on 2D supervision (rendering-based or projection-based losses), which provides no signal for occluded regions and only indirect, ambiguous cues for visible areas. This fundamental limitation causes severe shape collapse and motion artifacts even in clearly visible regions, as optimizing a 2D loss does not guarantee a plausible 3D deformation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261955/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static textured mesh (or generates one from the first video frame via Trellis) and a monocular video from a fixed viewpoint as input. It produces a topology-consistent animated mesh sequence where all frames share identical vertex/face sets and texture, parameterized by per-frame skeletal motion (root joint rigid transform plus non-root joint local rotations) and a pose-dependent MLP post-skinning correction field. The video is preprocessed by foreground segmentation, and camera parameters are estimated to align the rendered static mesh with the initial frame.

## Method

The core contribution is Shape Flow Guidance (SFG), a training-free procedure that elicits a temporally coherent 3D shape sequence from a pretrained rectified-flow mesh generator by constructing a 2D flow field over both sampling time and frame index. All latent trajectories are anchored to originate from the static mesh's latent, and an advection-diffusion PDE couples adjacent-frame trajectories to enforce temporal smoothness, yielding guidance shapes that inherit the static mesh's pose while capturing per-frame local deformations. The animation model decouples motion into local deformation (supervised in 3D by bidirectional point-mesh distance to SFG in root space) and global transformation (supervised by 2D rendering losses), preventing 2D supervision from corrupting local geometry. A post-skinning correction MLP compensates for LBS limitations and rigging errors, while consistent barycentric surface sampling stabilizes gradient computation during optimization.

## Results

On a 40-video benchmark (Consistent4D, STAG4D, V2M4, Sketchfab), SFG achieves the best scores across all four metrics: LPIPS 0.0791, CLIP 0.9442, FVD 544.16, and Uni3D 0.3486. The method also runs in 5 minutes per 32-frame sequence—2× faster than Puppeteer (10 min), 4× faster than V2M4 (20 min), and 12× faster than DreamMesh4D (60 min). In a user study with 20 participants, 84.8% preferred SFG's output over all baselines combined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFG outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest optimization-based competitor V2M4 [4], SFG reduces LPIPS from 0.1025 to 0.0791 (23% lower), improves CLIP from 0.8791 to 0.9442, cuts FVD from 763.89 to 544.16 (29% lower), and raises Uni3D from 0.2963 to 0.3486. Against Puppeteer [41], which uses projection-based 2D tracking, SFG achieves a 31% FVD reduction (544 vs. 805) and a 16% Uni3D gain, while also running 2× faster. DreamMesh4D [18] trails most significantly (FVD 931.50, Uni3D 0.2761) due to its reliance on Gaussian splatting and SDS-based optimization. Among feedforward methods, ActionMesh [37] is the strongest baseline (LPIPS 0.0971, Uni3D 0.3335) but still falls short of SFG on all four metrics, despite being 3 min faster; Motion324 [3] is fastest (40 s) but weakest in quality. The consistent margin across both 2D rendering metrics (LPIPS, CLIP, FVD) and the 3D geometric metric (Uni3D) confirms that the 3D shape-flow supervision addresses a fundamental limitation of 2D-only supervision rather than merely improving rendering fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established paradigm of video-driven mesh animation (DreamMesh4D, Puppeteer, V2M4), where all supervision is 2D—either rendering-based reconstruction losses or projection-based keypoint alignment. Its key conceptual shift is repurposing a pretrained rectified-flow mesh generator (Trellis) not as a direct output producer but as a training-free source of temporally consistent 3D shape supervision, elicited by intervening in the generator's sampling trajectories with an advection-diffusion PDE over the frame dimension. The animation model itself builds on standard skeletal animation (LBS, automatic rigging via UniReg) but introduces a post-skinning correction MLP and a clean decoupling of root-space local deformation from global transformation, so that 3D and 2D losses each supervise the component they handle best.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 2D supervision alone is fundamentally insufficient for mesh animation—the ablation without SFG degrades below all baselines—validating the broader argument that explicit 3D guidance is necessary for geometrically faithful deformation. The 12× speedup over DreamMesh4D and the model-agnostic design (works with both Trellis and Hunyuan3D) make the approach practically deployable in production 3D pipelines. Open questions remain around topology-changing animation, moving-camera inputs, and whether the advection-diffusion temporal coupling generalizes to longer sequences or more complex multi-object scenes.
