## Problem

State-of-the-art LiDAR scene flow methods are trained and evaluated independently on single datasets, and prior work in LiDAR 3D detection and segmentation shows that naively combining multiple datasets often degrades performance. This paper challenges that conventional wisdom for motion estimation, asking whether low-level, class-agnostic tasks like scene flow can benefit from cross-dataset training without bespoke architectural modifications, and whether such unified models can generalize zero-shot to unseen sensors and driving scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260738/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pairs of consecutive LiDAR sweeps (standardized to 10 Hz across all datasets) as input and produces a per-point 3D displacement vector as output. Point clouds are motion-compensated using dataset-provided ego-motion, ground points are removed via LineFit, and only near-range points (≤35 m from the ego-vehicle) are used during training. The input point clouds vary substantially in density and sensor type across datasets (32-beam, 64-beam, and FMCW LiDARs), and the model must handle this diversity without any sensor-specific conditioning.

## Method

UniFlow is a 'frustratingly simple' training strategy that retrains existing feedforward scene flow architectures (SSF, Flow4D, and ΔFlow) without any architectural modification on a unified mixture of Argoverse 2, Waymo, and nuScenes. The key insight is that scene flow is inherently class-agnostic, avoiding the label-taxonomy inconsistencies that plague multi-dataset training for semantic tasks; the authors further show that motion prediction accuracy correlates strongly with object velocity, so training on a diverse velocity distribution (urban sedans + highway trucks) is critical for generalization. Training uses minimal augmentation—height randomization (z ± 0.5–2 m, p=0.8) to simulate different sensor mounts and LiDAR ray dropping (alternating odd/even rays, p=0.35) to simulate sparser sensors—without re-weighting dataset frequencies or applying dataset-specific transforms. The authors also introduce Flow4D-XL, a scaled-up variant of Flow4D with additional parameters to address underfitting. This unified paradigm yields state-of-the-art in-domain results (5.1% and 35.2% improvements on Waymo and nuScenes) and strong zero-shot transfer to unseen sensors (TruckScenes, AEVAScenes) and extended spatial ranges.

## Results

On nuScenes, ΔFlow (UniFlow) achieves 0.140 Dynamic Mean EPE, a 35.2% improvement over the single-dataset ΔFlow baseline (0.216), while SSF (UniFlow) reaches 0.144, outperforming even Flow4D-XL (UniFlow) (0.196) due to SSF's suitability for sparse point clouds. On Waymo, ΔFlow (UniFlow) improves Dynamic Mean EPE from 0.198 to 0.188 (5.1% gain). Zero-shot on TruckScenes, Flow4D-XL (UniFlow) achieves 0.281 Dynamic Mean EPE versus 0.402 for the dataset-specific ΔFlow (30.1% improvement), and on AEVAScenes, ΔFlow (UniFlow) reaches 0.303 versus 0.391 for the best single-dataset model (22.5% improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniFlow consistently outperforms its single-dataset counterparts across all architectures and benchmarks. On nuScenes, ΔFlow (UniFlow) beats ΔFlow by 35.2% (0.140 vs. 0.216 Dynamic Mean EPE), SSF (UniFlow) beats SSF by 34.5% (0.144 vs. 0.220), and Flow4D-XL (UniFlow) beats Flow4D by 15.2% (0.196 vs. 0.230). On Waymo, gains are more modest: ΔFlow (UniFlow) improves over ΔFlow by 5.1% (0.188 vs. 0.198), while Flow4D-XL (UniFlow) edges out Flow4D by 11.2% (0.191 vs. 0.215). The most striking results are zero-shot: on TruckScenes, Flow4D-XL (UniFlow) (0.281) outperforms the dataset-specific Flow4D (0.456) by 38.4% and ΔFlow (0.402) by 29.6%, despite never seeing highway truck data. On AEVAScenes (FMCW LiDAR), ΔFlow (UniFlow) (0.303) beats the best single-dataset model ΔFlow(Waymo) (0.391) by 22.5%. Notably, SSF (UniFlow) outperforms Flow4D-XL (UniFlow) on nuScenes (0.144 vs. 0.196), suggesting SSF's architecture is inherently better suited for sparse 32-beam point clouds, while Flow4D-XL (UniFlow) dominates on denser sensors like Waymo and TruckScenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniFlow builds directly on three existing feedforward LiDAR scene flow architectures—SSF, Flow4D, and ΔFlow—retraining them without architectural modification on a unified mixture of Argoverse 2, Waymo, and nuScenes. It deliberately departs from the established finding in LiDAR 3D object detection and semantic segmentation that naive multi-dataset training degrades performance, drawing instead an analogy to RGB low-level vision (optical flow, monocular depth) where cross-dataset training yields strong zero-shot transfer. The only architectural addition is Flow4D-XL, a parameter-scaled variant of Flow4D, and the training recipe relies on minimal augmentations (height randomization, LiDAR ray dropping) rather than bespoke cross-domain alignment modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LiDAR scene flow is inherently class-agnostic, making it far more amenable to cross-domain training than semantic tasks, and provides a practical velocity-distribution analysis explaining *why* certain dataset combinations transfer well. This opens a path toward a single general-purpose scene flow model that handles diverse sensors (rotating, FMCW), ego-vehicle platforms (sedan, truck), and driving scenarios (urban, highway) without per-dataset fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other low-level LiDAR tasks (e.g., odometry, tracking), whether the velocity-distribution insight generalizes beyond the tested datasets, and whether the gains persist as sensor technology diverges further from the training distribution.
