## Problem

Direct zero-shot quantization from full-precision to ultra-low-bit (W4A4, W3A3) regimes suffers from severe optimization instability and activation collapse in deep layers, because the large numerical gap injects excessive quantization noise that biases gradients without real data to anchor learning. Existing data-free methods perform calibration and distillation in a single step, which is insufficient to bridge this representation gap and leads to catastrophic accuracy degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261554/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained full-precision CNN (ResNet-18, ResNet-50, or MobileNetV2) along with its pre-stored Batch Normalization statistics as the sole source of information—no training or validation images are accessed. It produces an ultra-low-bit quantized model (W4A4 or W3A3) where all layers except the first are quantized using LSQ, while the first layer retains 8-bit precision to preserve boundary sensitivity. A GAN-based generator synthesizes calibration images conditioned on uniformly sampled labels, and the entire pipeline operates in a data-free regime.

## Method

ZEST introduces a hierarchical two-stage distillation pipeline centered on an intermediate-precision Teacher Assistant (TA) at W8A8 that acts as a numerical stepping stone between the full-precision teacher and the ultra-low-bit student. In stage one, synthetic images are generated by a GAN trained to match the teacher's BN statistics, then the TA is initialized by quantizing the teacher to W8A8 and fine-tuned via sequential block-wise reconstruction to preserve semantics at intermediate precision. In stage two, the synthetic images are refined through gradient descent on the TA's BN statistics (quantization-aware recalibration) to align them with quantized-domain constraints such as clipping boundaries, and the student is then quantized to the target bit-width and fine-tuned block-wise under TA supervision. The block-wise reconstruction (matching per-block activations rather than end-to-end logits) prevents error accumulation in deeper layers, while the TA's proximity to the student's numerical regime filters high-variance quantization noise and yields a +68.3 percentage point improvement in loss-landscape smoothness over direct distillation.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K at W4A4 precision, ZEST achieves 70.50% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-18 (within 0.51 pp of the 71.01% full-precision baseline), 76.32% on ResNet-50, and 70.71% on MobileNetV2, outperforming all nine compared baselines. At the more extreme W3A3 regime, ZEST reaches 68.45% on ResNet-18, 75.28% on ResNet-50, and 67.74% on MobileNetV2, again leading all baselines with the largest margin on MobileNetV2 (+8.59 pp over GenQ). Across five independent runs, the W4A4 ResNet-18 result is stable at 70.42% ± 0.08%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZEST outperforms all nine baselines on every architecture at both W4A4 and W3A3. At W4A4, the closest competitors are GenQ (69.77% on ResNet-18, 75.50% on ResNet-50, 68.96% on MobileNetV2) and Genie (69.66%, 75.59%, 68.38%), with ZEST leading by 0.73 pp on ResNet-18, 0.73 pp on ResNet-50, and 1.75 pp on MobileNetV2. The gap widens substantially at W3A3: ZEST beats GenQ by 0.27 pp on ResNet-18 (68.45% vs 68.18%), 1.29 pp on ResNet-50 (75.28% vs 73.99%), and a decisive 8.59 pp on MobileNetV2 (67.74% vs 59.15%). Earlier methods such as GDFQ, AdaDFQ, TexQ, and SynQ suffer severe accuracy collapse at W3A3 (e.g., GDFQ drops to 20.23% on ResNet-18), while ZEST retains over 68% on ResNet-18, confirming that the hierarchical TA bridge is critical in the ultra-low-bit regime where direct distillation from a full-precision teacher fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Teacher Assistant (TA) distillation paradigm from Mirzadeh et al. (AAAI 2020), which was originally designed to bridge architectural capacity gaps (e.g., ResNet-101 → ResNet-18), to the numerical precision domain, where the gap is between full-precision and ultra-low-bit quantized models. It also builds on the standard zero-shot quantization (ZSQ) pipeline of generating synthetic calibration data via BatchNorm statistics matching (ZeroQ, GDFQ, Genie), but departs from the single-step full-precision-to-low-bit distillation by inserting an intermediate-precision TA as a 'numerical stepping stone.' The key conceptual shift is treating quantization noise as an optimization-landscape problem solvable by hierarchical supervision rather than purely a data-synthesis problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework achieves only 0.51% top-1 accuracy loss at W4A4 on ResNet-18 (70.50% vs. 71.01% full precision) and maintains 68.45% at W3A3, making ultra-low-bit deployment on edge hardware practically viable without access to training data. The non-monotonic TA precision finding (W8A8 outperforms both W16A16 and W6A6) provides actionable guidance for practitioners choosing intermediate anchors. However, the evaluation is limited to CNNs (ResNet, MobileNetV2) on ImageNet; the authors themselves flag generalizability to Vision Transformers and LLMs—where outlier activations and attention mechanisms introduce fundamentally different quantization challenges—as open work, and no transformer experiments are provided.
