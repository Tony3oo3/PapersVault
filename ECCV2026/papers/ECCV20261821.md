## Problem

Inference-time alignment of diffusion and flow models via noise optimization is typically restricted to differentiable or cheap reward models, or is memory/compute inefficient due to backpropagation through the full sampling trajectory. Black-box source-noise search methods, which are the most versatile, remain underexplored and existing approaches (random search, zero-order search) struggle to balance global exploration with local exploitation, tending toward one extreme or the other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261821/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source noise vector drawn from the model's prior (e.g., 4096-dim Gaussian for SD1.5, 65536-dim for SDXL-Lightning, 291-dim for EquiFM molecules) and treats the entire generative model plus reward function as a single black-box mapping to a scalar reward. It outputs a single optimized noise sample that, when passed through the frozen generative model, produces a data sample (image, molecule, or protein backbone) with maximized reward. No gradients through the generator or reward are required, and the reward can be arbitrarily expensive or non-differentiable.

## Method

TRS adapts the TuRBO trust-region framework from Bayesian optimization to source-noise search, replacing the surrogate model with structured sampling (Sobol or Gaussian perturbations) since the highly non-linear noise space makes surrogates ineffective under constrained budgets. The algorithm maintains k hypercubic trust regions, each defined by a center and side length; candidates are proposed by adding masked perturbations (Bernoulli coordinate mask with random probability) to the center, then evaluated in parallel batches. Trust-region side lengths adapt via success/failure counters (expand on improvement, contract on stagnation), and after each batch all regions are re-centered to the global top-k observed noises, naturally shifting computation from exploration toward exploitation. This global re-centering is a key departure from vanilla TuRBO, where regions are treated independently, and is critical for performance as only a subset of regions typically remains promising.

## Results

On DrawBench (200 prompts), TRS achieves mean best ImageReward of 1.62 and HPSv2 of 0.322 on SD1.5, and 1.66 / 0.340 on SDXL-Lightning, outperforming the strongest noise-sequence baseline DTS* (1.59 / 0.303 and 1.62 / 0.327) while using fewer reward calls (400 vs. 503) and 4× less wall-clock time (226.5 s vs. 924.9 s per prompt). On multi-property molecule generation, TRS reduces the mean distance to 3-property targets (L₃) from 1.21 (base) to 0.39, versus 0.83 for gradient-based OC-Flow, while preserving molecule stability (85.7% vs. 68.0% for OC-Flow). On protein design at 50 residues, TRS raises designability from 0.05 (base) to 0.65, the highest among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRS outperforms all gradient-based and noise-sequence baselines across every setting. Against OC-Flow [36], TRS gains +1.20 IR on SD1.5 (1.62 vs. 0.42) and reduces molecule L₃ by 53% (0.39 vs. 0.83), while OC-Flow degrades molecule stability from 88.3% to 68.0%. Against the strongest noise-sequence method DTS* [16], TRS improves HPSv2 by 0.019 on SD1.5 (0.322 vs. 0.303) and 0.013 on SDXL-L (0.340 vs. 0.327), using 21% fewer reward calls and 4× less wall-clock time. SMC-based FKS [30] and DAS [19] require 6× more reward evaluations (2400 vs. 400) and still trail TRS on both reward metrics. Among black-box baselines, TRS consistently beats random search [26] and zero-order search [26] by 0.08–0.18 IR on DrawBench and by 0.04–0.08 on molecule L₃/L₆. Diagonal CMA-ES [12] is the closest competitor: it marginally edges TRS on SDXL-Lightning (1.67 vs. 1.66 IR; 0.344 vs. 0.340 HPS) but TRS wins on SD1.5, all molecule metrics, and both protein designability settings (0.65 vs. 0.62 at 50 residues; 0.45 vs. 0.44 at 100 residues), and does so with the same hyperparameters across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRS adapts the trust-region framework of TuRBO (Eriksson et al., NeurIPS 2019) from surrogate-based Bayesian optimization to source-noise search for generative models, dropping the Gaussian-process surrogate entirely and replacing independent region management with a global top-k re-centering scheme. It positions itself within the black-box noise-optimization line initiated by Ma et al. (CVPR 2025) with random and zero-order search, and by concurrent evolutionary-algorithm work (Jajal et al., 2025), but departs from both by introducing structured, adaptive exploration via hypercubic trust regions with stochastic coordinate masking. Conceptually, it also contrasts with gradient-based guidance (OC-Flow, DNO) and noise-sequence search (FKS, DAS, DTS, Fast Direct) by requiring no differentiability, no SDE sampling, and no intermediate-step intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in TRS's model- and reward-agnostic design: it works identically for diffusion and flow-matching generators, for differentiable or expensive black-box rewards, and across image, molecule, and protein modalities with minimal hyperparameter tuning. A notable open question is whether the current axis-aligned hypercube perturbations adequately capture the anisotropic geometry of the source-noise manifold, which the authors flag as future work. All methods remain bounded by reward-model accuracy, so the approach's ceiling will rise as reward models improve (e.g., via reward scaling). The finding that gradient-based optimization can underperform simple random search in multi-property molecule design suggests that conflicting classifier gradients may be a broader obstacle for backpropagation-based alignment in multi-objective settings.
