## Problem

Existing pseudo-anomaly synthesis methods for unsupervised 3D anomaly detection produce a narrow range of geometric defects (1–3 types), lack anisotropic and tangential displacement support, and are tightly coupled to specific detection architectures. This limits the diversity of abnormal cues a detector can learn, reducing generalization to real-world defect distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261846/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The synthesis module takes a normal 3D point cloud (M points) with precomputed surface normals, partitions it into K=64 patches via random center assignment, and outputs a deformed point cloud together with preset-specific anomaly masks. When paired with the offset-prediction detector (MinkUNet34C backbone + multi-head MLP), the system produces per-point anomaly scores (L1 norm of predicted 3D offset) and an object-level score (sum of point scores); the reconstruction-based instantiation instead yields reconstruction residuals as the anomaly signal.

## Method

AF3AD defines a center-conditioned parametric deformation in a local PCA orthonormal frame at a randomly selected patch center, displacing each point as p′ = p + s·B·K(t)·g·d, where s is sign, B is magnitude, K(t) is a smooth spatial-falloff kernel (cosine, Gaussian, or polynomial) evaluated on an ellipsoidal anisotropic distance, g is an optional directional or normal-alignment gate, and d is a unit direction drawn from the principal axes. Stochasticity is injected by sampling B and the nominal radius R from bounded Beta distributions per preset. Eleven geometric presets spanning four groups (isotropic bulges/dents, anisotropic ridges/trenches/craters, multi-frequency ripples/dimple fields, and tangential shear/drag) are organized under this single formulation, and the module is fully standalone—producing pseudo-anomalous point clouds and GT masks that can supervise any detector paradigm. The paper validates modularity by plugging AF3AD into both an offset-prediction detector (replacing PO3AD's Norm-AS) and a diffusion-based reconstruction detector (replacing R3D-AD's Patch-Gen), with the offset-prediction instantiation achieving 91.5% O-AUROC on AnomalyShapeNet and 85.2% on Real3D-AD.

## Results

On AnomalyShapeNet, PO3AD+AF3AD achieves 91.5% O-AUROC and 92.5% P-AUROC (mean ± std over three seeds), outperforming the previous best (Reg2Inv at 86.0% O-AUROC) by 5.5 points and PO3AD (89.9% P-AUROC) by 2.6 points. On Real3D-AD, the method reaches 85.2% O-AUROC and 86.1% P-AUROC, surpassing the second-best O-AUROC (Reg2Inv at 78.1%) by 7.1 points, though it trails Reg2Inv by 1.8 points on P-AUROC (87.9%). The R3D-AD reconstruction instantiation also shows gains: replacing Patch-Gen with AF3AD improves O-AUROC from 62.7% to 67.2% on 15 AnomalyShapeNet categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AF3AD (integrated with PO3AD's offset-prediction detector) achieves the best O-AUROC on both benchmarks: 91.5% on AnomalyShapeNet (+5.5 over Reg2Inv's 86.0%, +7.6 over PO3AD's 83.9%) and 85.2% on Real3D-AD (+7.1 over Reg2Inv's 78.1%, +8.6 over PO3AD's 76.6%). On AnomalyShapeNet P-AUROC, AF3AD leads all methods at 92.5%, ahead of PO3AD (89.9%) and Reg2Inv (88.2%). On Real3D-AD P-AUROC, AF3AD (86.1%) is second to Reg2Inv (87.9%) but ahead of ISMP (83.6%) and all other baselines. Against reconstruction-based methods, AF3AD substantially outperforms R3D-AD (74.9% O-AUROC on AnomalyShapeNet) and IMRNet (65.9%), while the R3D-AD+AF3AD instantiation (67.2% O-AUROC on 15 categories) still improves over R3D-AD+Patch-Gen (62.7%). Classical and memory-bank baselines (BTF, M3DM, PatchCore, CPMF) trail by 25–40 O-AUROC points, confirming that pseudo-anomaly synthesis with diverse geometric deformations is the primary driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

AF3AD builds directly on the pseudo-anomaly synthesis line of work in unsupervised 3D anomaly detection, specifically replacing the Norm-AS module in PO3AD (offset-prediction) and the Patch-Gen module in R3D-AD (diffusion-based reconstruction). Prior synthesis methods (CLIP3D-AD, GLFM, Norm-AS, Patch-Gen) were tightly coupled to a single detection paradigm and offered only 1–3 deformation types; AF3AD decouples synthesis from detection architecture and unifies 11 geometric presets under a single center-conditioned parametric model with PCA-based local frames, anisotropic ellipsoidal support, directional gating, and Beta-distributed stochastic parameters. Conceptually, it extends the simple normal-direction displacement idea (bulge/dent) into a richer design space covering tangential shear, elongated ridges, multi-frequency ripples, and direction-gated craters, while remaining a deterministic geometric transform rather than a learned generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, standalone design makes AF3AD a drop-in replacement for the synthesis step in any pseudo-anomaly-based 3D detector, lowering the barrier to adopting diverse synthetic supervision. The ablation showing that AF3AD underperforms Norm-AS at low head capacity but dramatically outperforms it at h=128 (97.0 vs. 91.6 O-AUROC) reveals a non-trivial capacity–diversity interaction that practitioners should account for. Key limitations remain: smooth kernel-based displacement cannot faithfully reproduce sharp defects (cracks, fine scratches) or non-geometric anomalies (material/appearance flaws), and the fixed preset set is not dataset-adaptive, so generalization may degrade if real defects diverge from the synthetic distribution. Open directions include generative modeling for richer anomaly representations, curriculum learning over preset complexity, and principled domain-guided preset selection.
