## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors either provide no explanation beyond a binary label or generate post-hoc rationalizations that are not grounded in verifiable, low-level forensic evidence. This lack of structured, evidence-grounded reasoning leads to poor cross-domain generalization and unreliable forensic claims, as current MLLM-based approaches treat detection as a general-purpose visual classification task rather than a systematic evidence-collection and synthesis process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261894/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a binary Real/Fake label accompanied by a structured chain-of-evidence (CoE) reasoning trace formatted as <think>...<answer>...</answer>. The reasoning trace must explicitly reference multi-view forensic cues (spectral representations, high-pass filtered responses, texture anomalies, geometric/shadow inconsistencies) and build a step-by-step logical argument from low-level artifacts to the final verdict. Notably, the eight lightweight expert models that generate the forensic evidence are used only offline during dataset construction and do not participate at inference time; the MLLM must synthesize the reasoning from the raw image alone.

## Method

REVEAL employs a two-stage progressive training paradigm on a multimodal LLM backbone. Stage 1 (CoE Tuning) performs supervised fine-tuning on REVEAL-Bench using a joint reasoning-decision factorization p(y,z|x) = p(z|x)·p(y|x,z), which structurally enforces a think-then-answer mechanism distinct from post-hoc rationalization, with a weighted loss balancing reasoning generation (α=0.1) against answer correctness plus KL regularization. Stage 2 (R-GRPO) extends Group Relative Policy Optimization with a task-specific composite reward combining three components: a binary answer reward (λ=0.8), a think reward measuring semantic alignment with reference traces and logical coherence under structural perturbation (λ=0.1), and a multi-view alignment reward ensuring the reasoning is grounded across spectral, high-pass, and other forensic views (λ=0.1). Reward scoring is performed by a pretrained VLM agent (Qwen-3-VL-8B) rather than simple embedding similarity, capturing contextual logic and factual consistency. The REVEAL-Bench dataset itself is constructed by running eight specialized expert models (covering local artifacts, spectral clues, pixel noise, spatial consistency, geometry, shadows, texture fusion, and high-pass fusion) to extract structured evidence, then consolidating multi-perspective outputs into unified CoE annotations via Qwen-2.5VL-72B, yielding 60K high-quality samples from an initial 6M pool.

## Results

On the in-domain REVEAL-Bench, REVEAL achieves 95.31% accuracy, comparable to the best lightweight classifier NPR (95.40%). On the challenging out-of-domain REVEAL-Bench++ (unseen generators: FLUX, FLUX2, Qwen-Image, Z-Image, SD3.5), REVEAL reaches 92.35% mean accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline Ivy-xDetector (88.21%) by 4.14%. On the GenImage cross-domain benchmark, REVEAL achieves 94.96% mean accuracy, surpassing the best baseline AIDE (90.61%) by 4.35%. REVEAL also demonstrates superior robustness to Gaussian blur and JPEG compression perturbations compared to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVEAL outperforms all nine baselines on the out-of-domain REVEAL-Bench++ benchmark. The closest competitor, Ivy-xDetector (88.21% mean), is surpassed by 4.14 points, while Veritas (86.43%) trails by 5.92 points. Among conventional detectors, NPR achieves the highest in-domain accuracy (95.40%) but collapses to 59.37% on unseen generators, whereas REVEAL maintains 92.35%, highlighting the generalization advantage of evidence-grounded reasoning over low-level statistical features. On GenImage, REVEAL (94.96%) beats AIDE (90.61%) by 4.35% and NPR (87.69%) by 7.27%. Among MLLM-based explainable detectors, AIGI-Holmes (79.32% on Bench++) is outperformed by 13.03 points, and the paper reports a 26% human preference rate for REVEAL's explanations over AIGI-Holmes in a qualitative study.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based explainable detection line (AIGI-Holmes, FakeBench, RAIDX, Veritas) by replacing post-hoc rationalization with a causally grounded evidence-then-reasoning paradigm, where structured low-level forensic evidence from eight lightweight expert models is explicitly synthesized into a chain-of-evidence trace before the final prediction. It also builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath, adapting it with a forensic-specific composite reward (R-GRPO) that jointly optimizes detection accuracy, reasoning stability, and multi-view evidence alignment. Conceptually, the work bridges two previously separate threads—specialized artifact-detection experts and large multimodal reasoning—by using the former as an offline annotation engine rather than an inference-time component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: current detectors either achieve high accuracy without forensic explainability or produce fluent but ungrounded post-hoc explanations that fail under distribution shift. The REVEAL-Bench dataset and R-GRPO training recipe provide a reusable recipe for building evidence-grounded multimodal reasoners, and the large generalization gap (NPR drops from 95% to ~53% on unseen generators while REVEAL holds ~91–95%) is well-evidenced across five distinct unseen generators. Open questions include whether the eight-expert annotation pipeline scales to video or 3D content, whether the agent-based reward (Qwen-3-VL-8B) introduces systematic bias that limits reward reliability, and whether the 60K training set is sufficient for the claimed robustness or whether performance degrades with more diverse artifact types.
