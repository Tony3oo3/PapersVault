## Problem

Pose-free feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from cumulative camera pose drift in long image sequences, where small misalignments amplify over time and severely degrade rendering fidelity. Existing approaches either rely on SfM pseudo-ground-truth poses (introducing sensor noise) or pure rendering-based supervision (causing optimization instability and local minima due to entangled geometry-pose optimization), and no prior method explicitly couples dense geometric cues with appearance supervision to break this drift cycle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261795/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of N unposed, uncalibrated RGB images at 224×224 resolution and produces a complete 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (per-pixel opacity, color, anisotropic scale, and rotation quaternion), predicted camera poses, per-pixel raymaps (6-channel: 3D ray direction + 3D ray origin), and scalar depth maps. All experiments are conducted at 224×224 for fair comparison, and the model operates in a single forward pass without per-scene optimization or any camera calibration at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is a Raymap-Guided Coupling Module (RGC) that lifts pixels to 3D via p_j = o_j + D_j · r_j, anchoring every Gaussian center to predicted raymap geometry rather than camera-head poses alone. This creates a bidirectional optimization loop: RGB rendering loss backpropagates through the raymap-derived positions to refine geometry, while raymap consistency loss regularizes Gaussian spatial distribution to improve rendering. Training uses a unified objective combining MSE+LPIPS rendering loss, Huber camera regularization, and raymap absolute loss, all sharing the same raymap-derived Gaussian positions. A Dual-Frequency Viewpoint Scheduling strategy further stabilizes learning by using DINOv2 cosine-similarity overlap scores for an easy-to-hard curriculum (threshold annealed from 1.0 to 0.75, interval clipped at g_max=15) combined with stochastic replay of small-interval samples (g∈{6,…,10} at 50% probability in the second half of training). The backbone is a vanilla DINOv2 ViT (Giant: 1536-dim, 40 blocks; Large: 1024-dim, 24 blocks) with a Dual-DPT head for raymap/depth prediction, initialized from Depth Anything v3 pretrained weights.

## Results

On the DL3DV dataset, NoDrift3R achieves 24.24 dB PSNR / 0.794 SSIM / 0.142 LPIPS with 24 input views, outperforming the strongest pose-free baseline YoNoSplat by 2.07 dB PSNR and 0.074 SSIM. The advantage grows with sequence length: at 6 views the gap over YoNoSplat is 0.39 dB, while at 24 views it widens to 2.07 dB, confirming drift-robustness. For camera pose estimation, NoDrift3R reaches AUC@5° of 0.949 at 24 views versus 0.778 for YoNoSplat and 0.389 for E-RayZer. On cross-dataset generalization to ScanNet++ (128 views), NoDrift3R scores 19.71 dB PSNR, surpassing YoNoSplat (17.64 dB) by over 2 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoDrift3R outperforms all nine baselines across every view count and dataset. Against the closest pose-free competitor YoNoSplat [42], the gap is modest at 6 views (24.92 vs 24.53 dB PSNR) but widens to 2.07 dB at 24 views, directly reflecting YoNoSplat's susceptibility to cumulative pose drift. E-RayZer [49] is competitive at 6 views (24.81 dB) but collapses at 12 and 24 views (20.45 and 18.75 dB), confirming the paper's critique of its linear overlap-extension strategy. On pose estimation (Table 2), NoDrift3R achieves AUC@5° of 0.967/0.961/0.949 at 6/12/24 views, beating YoNoSplat (0.833/0.804/0.778) by 0.13–0.17 AUC and E-RayZer (0.846/0.421/0.389) by even larger margins at longer sequences. On RE10K (Table 4), NoDrift3R reaches 25.74 dB PSNR without any ground-truth poses, surpassing YoNoSplat's pose-free result (24.57 dB) by 1.17 dB and even its pose-assisted result (25.40 dB). Cross-dataset transfer to ScanNet++ (Table 5) shows NoDrift3R leading YoNoSplat by 0.68 dB at 32 views and 2.07 dB at 128 views, with the margin growing as sequence length increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pose-free feed-forward 3DGS line (NoPoSplat, AnySplat, YoNoSplat, Uni3R) by introducing a Raymap-Guided Coupling Module that lifts Gaussian centers from per-pixel ray directions and depths, rather than relying on low-dimensional camera parameters or pixel-aligned predictions without explicit geometric anchoring. It builds on multi-view geometry foundation models (Depth Anything v3, VGGT, Pi3) as backbones and pseudo-label sources, and departs from E-RayZer's linear visual-overlap curriculum by adding a stochastic replay mechanism to preserve small-interval consistency. The key conceptual departure is framing the geometry-appearance interaction as a bidirectional optimization loop ('rendering-to-geometry gain') rather than treating rendering and geometric losses as loosely coupled independent signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies cumulative pose drift as the primary bottleneck for long-sequence pose-free 3DGS and provides a principled architectural solution (raymap-guided coupling) plus a practical training schedule (Dual-Frequency Viewpoint Scheduling) that together yield consistent gains in both rendering and pose accuracy, especially at 24+ views. The bidirectional coupling insight—that appearance supervision can refine geometry and vice versa through a shared representation—is a design principle potentially transferable to other coupled optimization problems in 3D vision. Limitations include reliance on pose and raymap supervision during training (not fully self-supervised), fixed hyperparameters across datasets, and evaluation at 224×224 resolution; future work on adaptive cross-dataset scheduling and unified posed/unposed training is identified as important open directions.
