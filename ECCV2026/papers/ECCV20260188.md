## Problem

Existing feed-forward 4D reconstruction methods either predict per-frame static point clouds without modeling temporal motion, or estimate point trajectories but are restricted to small camera motions. This limits their ability to aggregate observations over time and reconstruct complete dynamic scenes under large viewpoint changes, because static geometry matching and dynamic foreground correspondence learning are entangled and distract from each other.

## Input / Output

The method accepts arbitrary collections of videos (monocular sequences with complex camera motion, temporally disjoint video pairs, or hybrid image-video sets) at 280×504 resolution, with 16-frame sequences during training but generalizing to 32+ frames at inference. It outputs a per-pixel 4D trajectory for every pixel in every input image, represented as a 6D rotation plus 3D translation applied to the recovered 3D point, along with depth maps, camera ray maps, camera poses, and per-pixel dynamic score maps that gate the trajectory displacement.

## Method

OmniX builds on the DepthAnything3 transformer backbone and adds a trajectory module with two key components. First, Sparse Spatiotemporal Attention (SSA) exploits the sparse, low-rank structure of 3D motion by selecting only the top-ρ% dynamic tokens via a learned probability, expanding them along the temporal axis with modulated sinusoidal embeddings, and using stacked cross-attention blocks (without self-attention) to predict trajectory transformation bases for all timestamps. Second, a Deformable Trajectory Sampling Head (DTSH) upsamples the resulting sparse trajectory field to pixel-level transformations via multi-scale deformable sampling, jointly sampling trajectory bases and dynamic scores so that the trajectory loss backpropagates through the differentiable token selection. The final per-pixel trajectory is computed as P + θ·(P_traj − P), where the dynamic score θ encourages background points to remain static. Training uses confidence-aware L1 losses on depth, rays, and trajectories, plus gradient and Huber losses, supervised on a UE5-generated dataset of 80K scenes and 1.28M multi-view videos with full geometric annotations, combined with eight public datasets.

## Results

On the primary dense 3D point trajectory prediction task (Table 1), OmniX achieves APD3D of 0.284 (FG) and 0.391 (ALL) with EPE of 0.133 (FG) and 0.116 (ALL) on monocular video (first-frame evaluation), substantially outperforming VDPM (APD3D 0.187/0.120, EPE 0.338/0.287) and TraceAnything (APD3D 0.062/0.043, EPE 0.354/0.316). On the TAPVid-3D sparse tracking benchmark (Table 2), OmniX achieves the best EPE on ADT (0.134) and DriveTrack (1.490), and the best APD3D on all three sub-datasets (0.367, 0.256, 0.141). On video depth (KITTI), OmniX attains Abs Rel of 0.024 and δ<1.25 of 0.990, and on camera pose (Sintel) achieves ATE of 0.108, both competitive with or better than dedicated methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniX decisively outperforms both TraceAnything [18] and VDPM [32] on the primary dense trajectory prediction task across all three input settings (monocular, disjoint pairs, hybrid), with EPE improvements of roughly 2.5× over VDPM (e.g., 0.133 vs 0.338 FG on monocular) and APD3D gains of ~50% (0.284 vs 0.187 FG). On the TAPVid-3D sparse tracking benchmark, OmniX achieves the best EPE on ADT (0.134 vs 0.162 VDPM) and DriveTrack (1.490 vs 1.576 VDPM), and the best APD3D on all three sub-datasets, though VDPM edges out slightly on PStudio EPE (0.141 vs 0.156). Against iterative trackers SpatialTrackerV2 [41] and St4RTrack [5], OmniX wins on EPE across all TAPVid-3D sub-datasets while also being ~10× faster at inference (2.15 s vs 20.59 s for St4RTrack). On video depth (KITTI), OmniX (Abs Rel 0.024) beats all compared methods including the dedicated DepthAnythingV3 [16] (0.039) and π³ [40] (0.032). On camera pose (Sintel), OmniX achieves the best ATE (0.108) and RPE_trans (0.152), outperforming π³ [40] (0.157/0.174) and DepthAnythingV3 [16] (0.252/0.299), though its RPE_rot (0.722) is slightly worse than π³ (0.627). The paper attributes its large-camera-motion advantage to explicit training on UE5-rendered multi-view videos with diverse camera trajectories, a data regime absent from all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniX builds directly on the feed-forward 3D reconstruction backbone of DepthAnything3, WorldMirror, and VGGT, augmenting their per-frame point-cloud prediction with a trajectory module for 4D geometry. It departs from prior dense trajectory prediction methods (TraceAnything, VDPM, St4RTrack) that are limited to small camera motions by explicitly disentangling dynamic foreground motion from static scene structure via a Sparse Spatiotemporal Attention mechanism that parameterizes motion with a small set of dynamic tokens. The trajectory field formulation draws on the Shape-of-Motion representation, while the Deformable Trajectory Sampling Head adapts the DPT architecture for sparse-to-dense upsampling. The UE5-based data engine addresses a critical gap: the absence of large-scale 4D datasets with full geometric annotations and diverse multi-view camera configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniX provides the first feed-forward framework that handles large camera motion (up to 180°) and temporally disjoint multi-view inputs for dense 4D reconstruction, opening the door to applications in embodied AI, autonomous driving, and AR/VR that require consistent dynamic scene understanding across arbitrary viewpoints. The 80K-scene, 1.28M-video synthetic dataset with full trajectory annotations is a significant resource that the community can leverage. Open questions include whether the sparse-motion assumption (top-ρ% dynamic tokens) generalizes to scenes with pervasive non-rigid deformation, how well the model transfers to real-world dynamic scenes beyond the synthetic training distribution, and whether the 16-frame training window limits scalability to longer temporal horizons despite observed inference-time generalization.
