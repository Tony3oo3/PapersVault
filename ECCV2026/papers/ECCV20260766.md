## Problem

Existing temporal action localization methods either require full segment-level annotations (online TAL) or full-video access (point-supervised TAL), but no prior work combines the label efficiency of point supervision with the strict causality constraints of streaming inference. This paper introduces POTAL, a new task that simultaneously enforces online inference (no future frames, no revision) and point-level supervision (one timestamp per instance), and proposes the first framework to solve it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260766/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The online student consumes pre-extracted video features (TSN, I3D, or CLIP-ViT depending on dataset) in sliding windows of W frames (e.g., 64 for THUMOS, 24 for EGTEA), processing only the current and preceding frames at each time step. At inference, the model emits action proposals (start, end, class, confidence) immediately upon detecting an action end, with no access to future frames or opportunity for revision; Online Non-Maximum Suppression (ONMS) filters redundant proposals and discards any whose predicted end exceeds the current timestamp. Training requires only a single annotated timestamp and class label per action instance, reducing annotation cost by up to 6× compared to full boundary annotation.

## Method

OnPoint is an offline-to-online multi-level distillation framework where a frozen point-supervised offline teacher (HR-Pro or TSASPC) generates structured supervision for a strictly online Transformer-based student. Three complementary distillation signals are used: (i) instance-level pseudo ground truth from the teacher supervises the anchor-based classification and regression heads, (ii) frame-level Class-Activation Sub-Sequence (CASS) alignment via L2 loss strengthens temporal representations, and (iii) window-level anticipatory distillation transfers the teacher's next-window action likelihoods (binarized CAS) to guide the student toward imminent boundaries without future access. The anchor decoder incorporates an actionness-calibrated attention mechanism that adds a bias term (r + log(r), derived from CASS background scores) to cross-attention logits, emphasizing action-relevant frames and suppressing background. An auxiliary anchor-level point prediction head (classification + regression) directly uses the original point annotations to stabilize training against teacher noise, and the total loss is a weighted sum of instance, CASS, anticipation, and point losses with only the anticipation weight (γ) requiring tuning.

## Results

On THUMOS'14, OnPoint achieves 61.9% average mAP@tIoU[0.1:0.5] and 42.7% mAP@tIoU[0.3:0.7], outperforming the strongest distillation baseline (HR-Pro+OAT-ONMS, 55.7% / 38.7%) by 6.2% and 4.0% respectively. On EGTEA and HOI4D-Office, OnPoint gains 3.4% and 2.3% average mAP@tIoU[0.1:0.5] over the best baselines (TSASPC+OAT-ONMS). The method also surpasses several fully-supervised online methods (e.g., OAT-OSN at 44.6% and HAT at 44.8% on mAP@tIoU[0.3:0.7]) while using only point-level supervision, and is competitive with the best offline point-supervised model HR-Pro (51.1% mAP@tIoU[0.3:0.7]).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four distillation-based Online+Point baselines (HR-Pro+OAT-OSN, HR-Pro+HAT, HR-Pro+MATR-ONMS, HR-Pro+OAT-ONMS), OnPoint wins across all tIoU thresholds, with the largest gap over HR-Pro+OAT-ONMS (6.2% on AVG[0.1:0.5], 4.0% on AVG[0.3:0.7]). The distillation-free baseline (trained solely with anchor-level point supervision) is far weaker at 33.3% AVG[0.1:0.5], confirming that multi-level distillation is essential. OnPoint also outperforms all fully-supervised online methods except MATR-ONMS and OAT-ONMS on the stricter AVG[0.3:0.7] metric (42.7% vs. 49.5%), while surpassing OAT-OSN (44.6%) and HAT (44.8%) on the same metric. Against offline point-supervised references, OnPoint trails HR-Pro (51.1% vs. 42.7% on AVG[0.3:0.7]) and QROT (52.1%), which is expected given the online causality constraint, but narrows the gap to within 8–9 points. On EGTEA, OnPoint (23.1%) beats TSASPC+OAT-ONMS (19.7%) by 3.4%; on HOI4D-O, it leads TSASPC+OAT-ONMS (42.3%) by 2.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

OnPoint sits at the intersection of two previously disjoint research threads: point-supervised offline TAL (HR-Pro, TSASPC, LACP) and fully-supervised online TAL (OAT, HAT, MATR). While offline-to-online distillation has been explored in video instance segmentation and spatio-temporal action detection, this is the first framework to apply it to temporal action localization, using a point-supervised offline teacher to generate multi-level supervision (pseudo segments, class-activation sequences, future-window cues) for a strictly causal online student. The online student's anchor-based transformer decoder directly inherits from OAT and HAT, while the offline teacher leverages HR-Pro or TSASPC depending on dataset density, and the actionness-calibrated attention mechanism is a novel addition that repurposes the CASS signal to modulate cross-attention logits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POTAL addresses a practically critical gap: streaming video understanding with minimal annotation cost, relevant to AR-assisted surgery, live sports analytics, and robotics where dense boundary labeling is infeasible. The finding that a point-supervised online model (61.9 avg mAP on THUMOS) outperforms fully-supervised online baselines (49.5) suggests that offline teacher knowledge transfer can more effectively compensate for missing future context than simply providing more training labels in the online regime. Open questions include whether the two-stage teacher-student pipeline can be collapsed into end-to-end training, how the approach scales to very long or highly overlapping action sequences, and whether the distillation gap to offline point-supervised methods (~10 mAP) can be further closed with richer anticipation signals.
