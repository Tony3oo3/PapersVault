## Problem

Existing gaze target estimation methods formulate the task as direct pixel-level regression from global image features to spatial heatmaps, ignoring the object-oriented nature of human visual attention. This leads to spatially scattered and semantically unstable predictions in complex scenes with multiple competing targets, because no explicit mechanism models the two-stage process of selecting which object is attended and then localizing within it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260668/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image containing an observer, along with offline-generated object masks (from YOLO11x + SAM2-hiera-large) and 2D eye position coordinates. It produces a 64×64 gaze probability heatmap, a binary in/out-of-frame prediction, and a normalized 2D gaze direction vector. The DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone is entirely frozen, leaving only 7.1M learnable parameters across the object-fusion, geometry-prior, and multi-scale decoding modules.

## Method

The framework reformulates gaze estimation as hierarchical reasoning with three mechanisms operating on a frozen DINOv3 backbone. First, object-level semantic tokens (from masked region pooling of detected objects) are concatenated with image patch tokens and processed by a 3-layer Transformer encoder, producing an object-aware enhanced representation that establishes discrete candidate gaze hypotheses. Second, a lightweight MLP predicts a normalized gaze direction from head appearance features and eye coordinates, which is converted into a 120° field-of-view cone map (Gaussian-smoothed, learnable embedding) encoding geometrically reachable regions. Third, multi-scale features from the backbone are fused with deep features as the primary representation and mid/shallow features as bounded residuals (α_mid=0.05, α_shallow=0.10), then the FOV prior is injected before a lightweight decoder recovers spatial resolution. Training jointly optimizes heatmap binary cross-entropy, a cosine direction loss (λ_dir=0.5), and an optional in/out classification loss.

## Results

The proposed method achieves AUC of 0.961, 0.948, 0.987, and 0.977 on GazeFollow, VideoAttentionTarget, ChildPlay, and GOO-Real respectively, with corresponding L2 errors of 0.094 (Avg), 0.095, 0.084, and 0.092. On GazeFollow, it attains the best Min L2 of 0.038, outperforming the previous best (FGI-Gaze, 0.044) by 14%. All results are achieved with only 7.1M trainable parameters, compared to 35M–2B for competing methods, while using a fully frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GazeFollow, the proposed method (AUC 0.961, Avg L2 0.094, Min L2 0.038) outperforms all 13 compared baselines, with the closest competitors being Gaze-LLE (AUC 0.956, 2.8M params) and FGI-Gaze (AUC 0.950, 35M params), yielding gains of +0.005 AUC and −0.006 Avg L2 over Gaze-LLE. On VideoAttentionTarget, FGI-Gaze achieves a marginally higher AUC (0.958 vs. 0.948) and lower L2 (0.091 vs. 0.095), but the proposed method wins on in/out-of-frame AP (0.923 vs. 0.911), indicating better binary classification of gaze presence. On ChildPlay, the method achieves a substantial AUC improvement of +0.036 over Gaze-LLE (0.987 vs. 0.951) and L2 reduction of 0.017 (0.084 vs. 0.101), though Gaze-LLE edges ahead on AP (0.994 vs. 0.990). On the cross-domain GOO-Real benchmark, the method attains the best AUC (0.977 vs. 0.957 for Wang et al.) but a slightly higher L2 (0.092 vs. 0.081), suggesting strong semantic discrimination with modestly less precise pixel-level localization. Overall, the 7.1M-parameter model matches or exceeds methods with 5–280× more parameters across all four benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in gaze target estimation: multi-branch architectures that fuse auxiliary modalities (depth, pose, 3D head geometry) and foundation-model-based methods that regress heatmaps from single-layer features. Rather than adding modalities or deeper decoders, it repurposes a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone and injects object-aware tokens (from YOLO11x/SAM2 segmentation) plus a learned field-of-view geometric prior into the feature pipeline. The conceptual departure is framing the task as hierarchical reasoning—object selection followed by spatial localization—rather than the pixel-level regression that both prior lines share, though the individual components (cross-attention token fusion, multi-scale residual aggregation, cosine-similarity FOV masking) are standard building blocks recombined in a new configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.1M-parameter model matching or exceeding 68M–2B-parameter competitors across four benchmarks is practically significant for deployment-constrained settings (edge devices, real-time driving systems). The hierarchical reasoning formulation offers an interpretable decomposition that could guide future work on multi-person scenes, temporal gaze tracking, and uncertainty-aware prediction—areas the authors identify as open. However, the paper acknowledges failure modes with semantically similar co-located objects and extended-region gaze targets in human-centered scenes, and the gains over the strongest compact baseline (Gaze-LLE, 2.8M) are modest (~0.5–3.6 AUC points), leaving room for whether the added object-detection/segmentation pipeline is worth its inference overhead relative to simpler alternatives.
