## Problem

Existing medical vision-language pretrained models for chest radiology suffer from two underexplored limitations: (1) contrastive training preserves in-batch false negatives, where semantically related image-concept pairs are incorrectly treated as negatives simply due to batch co-occurrence, undermining the structured semantics extracted from clinical reports; and (2) cross-modal fusion modules either produce localization maps only as a byproduct of global alignment or require additional architectural complexity (e.g., transformer decoders) to achieve fine-grained spatial grounding.

## Input / Output

The model takes a chest X-ray image (224×224 or 518×518) and a text query in a structured medical concept template encoding disease type, polarity, and anatomical location (e.g., 'There is [finding] in [location]'). At inference, the same representation yields three types of output: a scalar classification score per concept (for zero-shot classification), a spatial attention map over image patches (for grounding/phrase grounding via Pointing Game), and a dense pixel-level mask (for segmentation via thresholding). The concept vocabulary comprises ~215K unique clinical concepts extracted by Qwen3-30B-A3B from MIMIC-CXR training reports, and each image is scored against a sampled subset of 512 concepts per batch.

## Method

ALPHARAD introduces two coupled contributions. First, it reformulates training as a per-image medical concept discrimination task: an LLM parses each radiology report into atomic clinical concepts, and the model is trained with an α-Corrected Binary Cross Entropy (α-BCE) objective that treats only report-absent concepts as negatives, eliminating in-batch false negatives and removing pairwise matching heuristics; a Monte Carlo negative-sampling estimator (α=1) makes the 215K-concept space computationally tractable. Second, it proposes Factorized Latent Supervision (FLaS), which partitions the 768-dim latent space into F=8 disjoint coordinate subspaces of 96 dims each; every subspace independently computes Scaled Cosine Similarity Attention over image patches and is supervised by its own MP-NCE contrastive loss, introducing nonlinear representational diversity with zero additional parameters. The final training objective sums the FLaS loss (independent per-subspace MP-NCE) with α-BCE applied to the average logit across subspaces, jointly optimizing fine-grained spatial alignment and coarse-grained concept discrimination.

## Results

ALPHARAD achieves a state-of-the-art average AUROC of 83.3% (224-res) and 83.6% (518-res) across 16 zero-shot chest X-ray classification benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baseline RadZero-518 (81.1%) by over 2 points. On grounding (Pointing Game accuracy), ALPHARAD-518 reaches 62.3% average, outperforming RadZero-518 (53.4%) by nearly 9 points; on phrase grounding it scores 79.5% vs. 75.3%, and on segmentation (Dice) 41.9% vs. 38.8%. Notably, ALPHARAD-224 already exceeds the average performance of all baselines tested at higher resolutions, including BioViL-T (448-res, 72.8%) and RadZero-518 (81.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALPHARAD outperforms all nine baselines on average AUROC across 16 classification datasets, with the largest margin over RadZero-518 (+2.5 pts at 224-res, +2.5 pts at 518-res) and CARZero (+6.3 pts). On individual datasets, ALPHARAD-224 sets new records on VinDr-CXR (92.9 vs. 87.8 for CARZero), Montgomery (94.1 vs. 88.8 for RadZero-518), and all three CheXchoNet tasks (73.0/78.6/75.7 vs. 70.8/76.4/74.3 for RadZero-518), while CARZero retains a narrow edge on CheXpert (90.1 vs. 89.9) and KAD on CheXpert5x200 (78.2 vs. 77.1). For grounding (Pointing Game), ALPHARAD-224 surpasses RadZero-518 on CheXlocalize (66.9 vs. 65.4) and both MS-CXR versions despite operating at lower resolution, and ALPHARAD-518 achieves 62.3% average vs. RadZero-518's 53.4%. On segmentation (Dice), ALPHARAD-224 exceeds RadZero-518 on CheXlocalize (54.2 vs. 50.5) and QaTa-COV19 (54.7 vs. 45.6), while ALPHARAD-518 shows a near-twofold gain on the challenging SIIM-ACR task (21.7 vs. 12.1). The MPNet backbone variant of ALPHARAD (matching RadZero's exact pretrained encoder) still outperforms RadZero on all three task averages (82.7 vs. 80.2 AUROC, 51.6 vs. 48.4 PG, 38.4 vs. 36.1 Dice), isolating the contribution of α-BCE and FLaS from backbone differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALPHARAD sits within the medical VLPM lineage that began with CLIP-style contrastive pretraining (BiomedCLIP, MedKLIP) and evolved through LLM-parsed clinical concept alignment (KAD, CARZero, RadZero). It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) it replaces in-batch contrastive learning with a multi-label discrimination objective (α-BCE) over a large-scale LLM-extracted concept pool, eliminating false-negative pairs that plague prior contrastive formulations; and (2) it introduces FLaS, a parameter-neutral factorized-subspace fusion module that replaces both SCSA (single-projection grounding) and MHCA (parametric transformer decoder) with disjoint latent subspaces each receiving independent contrastive supervision. The LLM concept extraction pipeline itself follows CARZero and RadZero, and the XrayDINOv2 backbone is shared with RadZero, so the contribution is squarely in the training objective and fusion architecture rather than in data curation or encoder design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple multi-label BCE loss can replace contrastive learning for zero-shot medical VLPM training without sacrificing open-vocabulary generalization, which challenges the field's default assumption that contrastive objectives are necessary for zero-shot transfer. FLaS's parameter-neutral design makes it a low-cost drop-in replacement for SCSA or MHCA in existing medical VLPM pipelines, and the consistent SOTA across 26 benchmarks (16 classification, 7 grounding, 3 segmentation) at both 224 and 518 resolutions strengthens the practical case. Open questions include whether the disjoint-subspace design (which forgoes cross-subspace interaction) can be extended to capture inter-concept relationships, whether the approach generalizes beyond chest radiography to other modalities, and how sensitive performance is to LLM parsing quality at scale. The observation that 224-resolution outperforms 518 on segmentation tasks hints at a fundamental tension between peak-localization and dense-pixel-precision that remains unaddressed.
