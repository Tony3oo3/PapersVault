## Problem

Existing text-guided open-world semi-supervised learning methods suffer from two critical issues: (1) model bias toward known classes during textual prompt tuning, which suppresses the learning of effective textual representations for unknown classes, and (2) limited cross-modal connections that produce sparse graph structures, hindering the propagation of visual and semantic information. These issues collectively degrade unknown class discovery performance, particularly in scenarios with a large number of classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (both labeled with known-class labels and unlabeled), class-specific textual descriptions retrieved from a description database via image-text similarity matching, and randomly initialized learnable prompt vectors (one per class, dimension d). The frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP encoders process images and text in a single forward pass; only the prompt vectors and the pseudo-label confidence matrix are optimized. The final output is a confidence matrix of shape (|D_u| + |C_u|) × |C_u|, from which argmax over columns yields the predicted class label for each unlabeled sample.

## Method

TPOBGE comprises two sequentially applied components. First, Textual Prompt Optimization reformulates the prompt tuning loss as an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem solved via the Sinkhorn algorithm, imposing marginal constraints that enforce a uniform assignment distribution across classes—mechanically preventing the model from over-allocating probability mass to known classes and thereby generating less biased textual representations for unknown classes. High-confidence samples are converted to one-hot pseudo-labels to further sharpen supervision. Second, Bimodal Graph Enhancement constructs a proximity graph whose nodes are the union of all unlabeled image features and all class prompt vectors, with edges derived from top-k image-image and image-prompt similarities (text-text edges are deliberately omitted to avoid the known modality gap). A label propagation algorithm then iteratively updates the pseudo-label confidence matrix over this denser graph, facilitating the flow of visual and semantic information across both modalities and improving class discrimination for unknown categories.

## Results

TPOBGE achieves an average clustering accuracy of 68.41% (All), 68.47% (Known), and 68.65% (Unknown) across four datasets (Hybrid, ImageNet-500, ImageNet-1K, WebVision), outperforming all baselines on every metric. On unknown classes specifically, the method reaches 65.27% (Hybrid), 73.40% (ImageNet-500), 68.98% (ImageNet-1K), and 66.96% (WebVision), representing a 20.7% relative improvement over the prior state-of-the-art. The method also requires dramatically less training time (3 min on Hybrid, 10 min on ImageNet-1K) and GPU memory (~1 GB) compared to CSC-OWSSL (543 min, ~19 GB and 7303 min, ~19 GB respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPOBGE outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and every metric (All, Known, Unknown). The largest gains appear on ImageNet-1K and WebVision, where TPOBGE achieves 68.98% and 66.96% unknown-class accuracy versus 58.08% (SSR2-GCD) and 53.92% (SSR2-GCD) respectively — absolute improvements of ~11 and ~13 points. On ImageNet-500, TPOBGE (73.40% U) surpasses the previous best TP-OWSSL (67.69% U) by 5.7 points. On Hybrid, the margin is tighter: TPOBGE (65.27% U) edges out SpectralGCD (64.15% U) by 1.1 points, while also matching its overall accuracy (63.81 vs 63.79). Notably, TPOBGE also reduces unknown-to-known misclassifications from 15k to 1.6k on ImageNet-1K (Table 3) and increases graph edge count from 50k to 375k, confirming both the debiasing and graph-enhancement mechanisms are active. In terms of efficiency, TPOBGE trains in 3 minutes on Hybrid and 10 minutes on ImageNet-1K using ~1 GB GPU memory, versus 543 minutes / 7303 minutes and ~19 GB for CSC-OWSSL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-guided OWSSL line initiated by TextGCD and refined by TP-OWSSL (learnable textual prompts) and CSC-OWSSL (class semantic correlations), both from the same group. It departs from these predecessors by identifying two overlooked failure modes: (1) model bias toward known classes during prompt tuning, addressed via an entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) constraint that enforces uniform class marginals, and (2) severe graph sparsity in cross-modal connections, addressed by constructing a bimodal proximity graph over image-image and image-prompt pairs followed by label propagation. The method also draws on the broader graph-based semi-supervised learning literature (Zhou et al.) and the modality-gap observations in CLIP-based models to justify design choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency gain (3 min vs. 543 min on Hybrid; 10 min vs. 7,303 min on ImageNet-1K) alongside a 20.7% relative accuracy improvement on unknown classes makes this a practically attractive option for resource-constrained open-world deployments. However, the authors acknowledge that performance relies on sufficient training samples and the generalization of pre-trained VLMs, limiting applicability in few-shot or vertical-domain settings. The visual-textual alignment is only superficially addressed, and the deep semantic gap between modalities remains unresolved. Open questions include robustness under long-tailed distributions, out-of-distribution samples, and extension to detection or video tasks.
