## Problem

Recovering full-body mesh from only the head pose of a wearer is an ill-posed problem since most body parts are unobservable in egocentric input. Existing head-to-motion generation methods rely on diffusion models whose iterative denoising process is computationally expensive and produces unstable poses when the number of steps is reduced, limiting real-time deployment on head-mounted devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261966/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T head poses, each a 16-dimensional vector encoding relative motion, canonicalized 6D orientation, and absolute height in the world coordinate (canonicalized to remove global rotation/translation following [31]). It outputs per-timestep SMPL-H parameters: 21×6 local joint rotations, 16 shape parameters, and 21 per-joint contact probabilities; the global root pose is recovered post-hoc via alignment with the input head trajectory. Inference uses a sliding window of 128 frames, with the last predicted pose adopted as the final output for online operation.

## Method

EgoGVAE employs two transformer-based variational autoencoders: a motion-to-motion network that encodes full-body poses into a Gaussian latent distribution (serving as a motion prior), and a head-to-motion network that encodes the head trajectory augmented with learnable tokens representing unobserved body parts into its own Gaussian latent distribution. The core mechanism is a symmetric KL divergence loss that aligns the two latent distributions during training, so that the head-to-motion network learns to produce latent features within the manifold of realistic full-body poses. At inference, a single latent sample is drawn from the guided distribution and decoded by a shared transformer decoder in one step, yielding full-body poses without iterative denoising. This one-step sampling achieves over 50× faster inference than diffusion-based baselines while improving MPJPE, grounding, and temporal smoothness metrics on AMASS, RICH, and EgoBody benchmarks.

## Results

On the AMASS benchmark (128-frame sequences), EgoGVAE achieves MPJPE of 106.7, PA-MPJPE of 89.9, Ground of 21.6, and T_head of 5.5, outperforming the best baseline EgoAllo by 10.9%, 11.1%, 17.9%, and 11.3% respectively. It also attains the lowest Jitter (3.08) and Foot sliding (8.2) among all methods. In terms of efficiency, EgoGVAE requires only 0.026 s and 3.45G FLOPs for 128 frames, making it more than 50× faster than EgoAllo (1.510 s, 378.16G FLOPs) and EgoEgo (7.222 s, 3025.13G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoGVAE outperforms all four baselines on every metric in the main AMASS comparison. Against the strongest baseline EgoAllo [31] (a diffusion-based method), it reduces MPJPE from 119.7 to 106.7 (−10.9%), PA-MPJPE from 101.1 to 89.9 (−11.1%), Ground from 26.3 to 21.6 (−17.9%), and T_head from 6.2 to 5.5 (−11.3%), while also improving Jitter (4.06→3.08) and Foot sliding (10.2→8.2). The diffusion-based EgoEgo [18] is substantially worse on pose accuracy (MPJPE 167.4 vs. 106.7) and head tracking (T_head 54.9 vs. 5.5), reflecting the instability of iterative denoising at reduced step counts. The non-diffusion baselines AvatarPoser† [13] and EgoPoser† [12], retrained with head-only inputs, trail EgoGVAE by roughly 35–37 MPJPE points, indicating that explicit hand observations are not strictly necessary when a proper variational prior is available. In cross-validation on RICH (trained on AMASS), EgoGVAE again leads all methods (MPJPE 179.9 vs. 193.1 for EgoAllo), and on the real-world EgoBody dataset it achieves the best MPJPE (162.9) and PA-MPJPE (140.4) among diffusion-based competitors. The efficiency gap is dramatic: EgoGVAE's one-step VAE sampling (0.026 s) is ~58× faster than EgoAllo (1.510 s) and ~278× faster than EgoEgo (7.222 s) for 128 frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoGVAE builds on the diffusion-based head-to-motion generation line (EgoEgo, EgoAllo) that conditions a generative model on 3D head trajectories to produce full-body poses, but replaces the iterative denoising process with a one-step variational autoencoder. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'guidance network'—a motion-to-motion VAE trained on full-body poses—whose latent distribution is aligned with that of the head-to-motion VAE via symmetric KL divergence, borrowing the variational framework from TEMOS-style motion generation. Learnable tokens appended to head embeddings bridge the input modality gap between head-only and full-body inputs, a design choice not present in prior diffusion-based or regression-based ego-body methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× inference speedup (0.026 s vs. 1.510 s for 128 frames) while simultaneously improving accuracy over all baselines makes ego-body mesh reconstruction viable for real-time head-mounted device applications (smart glasses, AR/VR), where iterative diffusion is prohibitively expensive. The method's reliance on a shared decoder and one-step sampling also suggests the latent-space guidance paradigm could transfer to other sparse-to-full motion generation settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SMPL-H to other body models, how it performs with even shorter or noisier head trajectories, and whether the guidance benefit persists at substantially larger sequence lengths beyond the 128-frame training window.
