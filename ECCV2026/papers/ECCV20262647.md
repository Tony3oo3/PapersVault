## Problem

Current LMMs degrade on high-resolution visual tasks because image tokens scale quadratically with resolution, and existing grounding-based reasoning methods either require costly bounding-box annotations or suffer from reward misspecification in RL-based approaches—where a correct final answer can be produced from an incorrect localization, leading to negative optimization of grounding. The paper asks how to directly optimize visual grounding capabilities without any external visual supervision beyond final answers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262647/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a high-resolution RGB image and a textual question. In the first turn, it processes a downsampled version of the image and outputs bounding-box coordinates for key regions of interest. In the second turn, the original full image is deliberately withheld; the model only sees the cropped sub-regions extracted at original resolution plus the question, and must produce the final answer. This two-turn closed-loop design means the model self-verifies whether its localization captured sufficient visual information to answer correctly.

## Method

HART is a two-stage post-training framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Stage 1 introduces AP-GRPO, a modified GRPO objective that assigns dynamic weights to each candidate response: correct answers receive a larger policy-gradient coefficient (μ₁) and a smaller KL penalty (μ₂), controlled by a single hyperparameter k, so the model preferentially reinforces trajectories where the cropped ROIs actually contain the needed information. The key insight is that withholding the full image in the second turn makes answer correctness a much tighter proxy for grounding correctness (reducing the 36.5% reward-misspecification rate observed in baselines), which is formalized via Proposition 1 (increased mutual information between localization and response correctness) and Proposition 2 (AP-GRPO subtracts the negative gradient contribution from incorrect-grounding/correct-answer cases). Stage 2 applies standard SFT on a separate clean dataset with the full image visible, recovering answer accuracy lost during the RL phase while preserving the learned grounding behavior.

## Results

On the in-distribution MME-RealWorld-Lite benchmark, HART-7B achieves 62.4% overall accuracy, surpassing the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (42.3%) by +20.1 points and outperforming all visual grounded reasoning baselines (DeepEyes-7B: 53.2%, Pixel-Reasoner-7B: 49.7%) and private models (GPT-4o: 45.2%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet: 51.6%). On the out-of-distribution TreeBench, HART-7B reaches 43.7% accuracy, exceeding MGPO+SFT (36.2%) and the base model (33.2%). On high-resolution benchmarks, HART-7B scores 80.6% on V* Bench, 71.9% on HR-Bench-8K (+10.9 over base), and 71.1% on HR-Bench-4K, while also improving on low-resolution tasks (MMStar: 62.8%, MathVista: 71.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HART-7B (62.4%) outperforms all open-source baselines on MME-RealWorld-Lite, beating the strongest post-training baseline MGPO+SFT (60.5%) by 1.9 points and the visual grounded reasoning models DeepEyes-7B (53.2%) and Pixel-Reasoner-7B (49.7%) by 9.2 and 12.7 points respectively. It also surpasses all private models tested (GPT-4o: 45.2%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet: 51.6%, Gemini-1.5-Pro: 38.2%) and larger open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL-32B: 45.6%, InternVL3-38B: 51.0%), indicating that the closed-loop grounding strategy is more effective than simply scaling parameters. On TreeBench (out-of-distribution), HART-7B (43.7%) leads all open-source methods, exceeding MGPO+SFT and GRPO+SFT, though it trails the strongest private model o3 (22.4% on a different sub-metric scale). On grounding accuracy (Table 6), AP-GRPO achieves 75.4% correct localization on TreeBench and 77.7% on Visual CoT, substantially outperforming GRPO (51.0%/66.1%) and MGPO (51.3%/66.3%), confirming that the reward-misspecification issue in standard RL baselines limits their grounding improvements. On high-resolution benchmarks (Table 4), HART-7B leads on V* Bench (80.6% vs. 78.5% Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and HR-Bench-8K (71.9% vs. 61.0%), while also matching or exceeding baselines on low-resolution tasks (MMStar: 62.8%, MathVista: 71.8%), demonstrating that the method does not sacrifice general visual understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

HART extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (from DeepSeekMath) by introducing dynamic, advantage-based weighting (AP-GRPO) that prioritizes samples with correct grounding, and adapts the reasoning-decomposition idea from self-rewarding VLMs to the visual grounding setting. It departs from prior annotation-free grounding methods (MGPO, DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner) that rely solely on final-answer correctness as reward, which the paper shows leads to reward misspecification where incorrect localizations still receive positive signal. The closed-loop self-verification mechanism—cropping ROIs and withholding the full image before answering—is the key conceptual departure, enabling the model to implicitly validate its own grounding without bounding-box annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a previously unmeasured failure mode (36–64% of correct answers involve incorrect grounding) and offers a practical, annotation-free fix that substantially improves both grounding accuracy (+25% on TreeBench) and downstream VQA on high-resolution benchmarks. The approach is limited to grounding-based reasoning pipelines and requires a subsequent SFT stage to recover answer accuracy lost from withholding the full image, suggesting the closed-loop mechanism trades some information for better localization. Open questions include whether the technique generalizes to tasks where spatial grounding is not the primary bottleneck, whether it scales effectively to 32B+ models, and whether the single hyperparameter k remains stable across diverse visual domains.
