## Problem

Deterministic video prediction models operate in discrete pixel space and are typically optimized with pixel-wise MSE, which encourages regression toward the expected value across all possible futures and produces over-smoothed outputs lacking sharp motion boundaries and fine structural detail. While 2D Gaussian Splatting has shown promise for continuous image representation, its potential for video prediction—particularly with arbitrary channel dimensions required by multi-variable spatiotemporal forecasting—remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261498/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of observed frames X_{1:T} (multi-channel spatiotemporal sequences ranging from RGB video to multi-variable weather/traffic fields, at resolutions from 32×32 to 256×256) and produces predicted future frames X̂_{T+1:T+τ}. Internally, each predicted frame is parameterized as a set of N anisotropic 2D Gaussians, each defined by 5+C learnable parameters (2D position, 2D scale, rotation angle, and C channel amplitudes), which are rendered back to the pixel grid via a differentiable renderer before being fused with the coarse pixel-space prediction.

## Method

PDR reformulates video prediction as a dual-domain problem: a pixel-space backbone (e.g., TAU) produces a coarse forecast, while a lightweight plug-and-play PredGS adapter maps spatiotemporal features into 2D Gaussian parameter space via four dedicated MLPs (for coordinates, scales, rotation, and channel-aware amplitudes). A custom CUDA-accelerated differentiable renderer (predgsplat) rasterizes the Gaussians with additive accumulation, supporting arbitrary channel dimensions and achieving up to 10× speedup over prior 2D Gaussian renderers. The rendered continuous-space output is fused with the pixel-space prediction via channel-wise concatenation and a 1×1 convolution. Training uses a hybrid L1+SSIM loss (λ=0.5) instead of MSE, which the ablations show is essential: PDR with L2+regularization actually underperforms the TAU baseline, demonstrating that continuous Gaussian representation and perceptual supervision must be combined to realize the full benefit of sharper, detail-preserving predictions.

## Results

On TaxiBJ (4→4 frames), PDR achieves MSE 0.2807 and MAE 14.29, reducing MSE by 9.7% over TAU (0.3108) and outperforming all 15 deterministic baselines. On WeatherBench T2m (12→12 frames), PDR lowers MSE to 1.071 (7.8% below TAU's 1.162) and RMSE to 1.035. On KTH (10→20 frames), PDR attains SSIM 0.9145 and reduces LPIPS by 29.0% versus TAU (0.16237 vs 0.22856), while on Human3.6M it improves SSIM to 0.9857 and cuts LPIPS by 21.2%. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor ARFree on KTH, PDR achieves higher PSNR (26.69 vs 25.74) and SSIM (0.825 vs 0.813) at 33× the inference speed (146 FPS vs 4.41 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDR outperforms all 15 deterministic baselines on both TaxiBJ and WeatherBench, with the largest margins over TAU (its pixel-space backbone): 9.7% MSE reduction on TaxiBJ and 7.8% on WeatherBench. On Human3.6M, PDR achieves the best SSIM (0.9857) and LPIPS (0.02192), surpassing USTEP (SSIM 0.9838) and PredFormer (LPIPS 0.03069). On KTH, PDR leads in SSIM (0.9145) and LPIPS (0.16237) over all deterministic methods, though its PSNR (26.98) trails USTEP (28.47) by 1.5 dB. Against diffusion-based methods on KTH, PDR wins on PSNR (26.69 vs ARFree's 25.74) and SSIM (0.825 vs 0.813) but concedes on LPIPS (0.130 vs 0.068), reflecting the expected trade-off of single-pass deterministic prediction versus iterative diffusion sampling; however, PDR runs 33× faster (146 FPS vs 4.41 FPS). The plug-and-play ablation (Table 4) confirms PDR improves every tested MetaFormer backbone, with the largest MSE gain (8.1%) on MLP-Mixer and the largest MAE gain (6.7%) on ConvNeXt.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDR draws on the 2D Gaussian Splatting line of work (GaussianImage, GaussianSR) that models images as continuous parametric fields, but repurposes this representation for the first time in end-to-end video prediction. It also inherits the continuous-representation philosophy from 3D Gaussian Splatting, while deliberately simplifying to 2D Gaussians with arbitrary channel support to handle multi-variable spatiotemporal data (weather, traffic). On the prediction side, PDR is positioned as a plug-and-play adapter (PredGS) that attaches to existing deterministic pixel-space predictors (TAU, SimVP, various MetaFormers) rather than proposing a new backbone, and it replaces the standard MSE objective with a hybrid L1+SSIM loss to counteract the well-known over-smoothing artifact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and real-time inference (>1000 FPS at 256×256) make PDR immediately applicable to practical forecasting pipelines (weather, traffic, motion) where latency and integration cost matter. The ablation showing that PDR underperforms TAU under L2+regularization but substantially outperforms it under L1+SSIM highlights an important co-design principle: continuous representations and perceptual losses must be paired to realize their full benefit. Open questions include whether the approach extends to probabilistic or diffusion-based predictors, whether per-frame Gaussian parameterization introduces subtle temporal artifacts at longer horizons, and how the method scales to higher resolutions or 3D volumetric prediction tasks.
