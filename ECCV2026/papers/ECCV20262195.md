## Problem

INR-based image codecs achieve orders-of-magnitude faster decoding than end-to-end neural codecs, but their encoding requires per-image training that takes minutes to hours, making them impractical for real-world deployment. No existing learning-based codec simultaneously delivers fast (feedforward) encoding, sub-millisecond decoding, and rate-distortion performance competitive with JPEG.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262195/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes an RGB image (minimum 256×256 due to the 8-level latent pyramid; trained on 512×512 crops) and produces a compressed bitstream in a single forward pass. The bitstream contains a header, an image-specific Huffman code table, DCT-quantized latent symbols, and the per-image MLP^i parameters in FP32. The decoder reconstructs the image by evaluating the generated INR network at pixel coordinates, achieving ~0.42 ms decoding on an RTX 4090D.

## Method

PIC is a feedforward INR codec where a latent encoder g_a produces L=8 pyramid-like multiresolution latents that are patchified into 8×8 blocks, DCT-transformed, quantized, and entropy-coded with a 64-channel piecewise-linear model. The key architectural insight is a hypernetwork-style channel-wise normalization and modulation: the synthesis network g_s is split into a shared MLP^s and an instance-dependent MLP^i, and per-image behavior is achieved by normalizing intermediate features and modulating them with per-channel γ and β parameters generated by a modulation net g_m, which can be fused into MLP^i weights at inference. A ZpR module (zigzag reorder + partial run-length encoding) further compresses the quantized DCT symbols losslessly. The decoder is hardware-optimized with 16-wide MLP layers matching Tensor Core TF32 warp matrix dimensions, and training uses Slang's differentiable shading language to ensure FP32/TF32 consistency between training and Tensor Core-accelerated inference.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset, PIC achieves a BD-Rate of −12.78% relative to JPEG, surpassing JPEG in rate-distortion performance while maintaining a practical feedforward encoding speed of 28.6 ms (≈20 FPS). The optimized decoder reaches 0.418 ms per image (≈2000 FPS), which is faster than nvJPEG and comparable to GaussianImage (0.439 ms), while requiring only 1.074K FLOPs/pixel—roughly 40× less than end-to-end codecs like Factorized (42.175K) and Hyperprior (45.546K). On the CLIC dataset, PIC maintains comparable trends, outperforming JPEG in PSNR at high bit-rates and matching MS-SSIM/LPIPS, while retaining the same speed advantages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two end-to-end baselines, PIC trades RD performance for speed: Factorized achieves −47.34% BD-Rate and Hyperprior −57.58%, both substantially better than PIC's −12.78%, but PIC decodes 85× faster than Factorized (0.418 vs 35.7 ms) and 404× faster than Hyperprior (0.418 vs 169 ms), with 40× lower FLOPs. Against representation-based methods, PIC wins decisively on encoding speed: it encodes in 28.6 ms versus 1.40×10⁶ ms for COIN, 1.77×10⁵ ms for Cool-Chic v4.2, and 6.99×10⁵ ms for GaussianImage—over three orders of magnitude faster. In RD performance, PIC (−12.78%) outperforms COIN (+25.53%) and GaussianImage (+36.55%) but falls well short of Cool-Chic v4.2 (−64.86%). On decoding speed, PIC (0.418 ms) is marginally faster than GaussianImage (0.439 ms) and far faster than Cool-Chic (216 ms) and COIN (3.18 ms), making it the fastest decoder among all methods except GaussianImage. Overall, PIC is the only method that simultaneously beats JPEG in both RD performance and decoding speed while keeping encoding in the feedforward regime, though it does not match the best RD performance of any single baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIC bridges two previously separate paradigms in learned image compression: end-to-end autoencoder codecs (Factorized, Hyperprior) that encode in a single forward pass but decode slowly, and representation-based codecs (COIN, Cool-Chic, GaussianImage) that decode extremely fast but require per-image training. The key architectural departure is a hypernetwork-style mechanism—channel-wise instance normalization followed by per-image modulation (γ, β)—that generates the weights of a small MLP synthesis network in one forward pass, eliminating the per-image training bottleneck. For entropy modeling, PIC adopts the DCT-coefficient distribution estimation approach from Luo et al. and the parallel GPU Huffman decoding from GPUHD, combining JPEG-inspired lossless techniques (zigzag, partial RLE) with learned neural components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIC establishes the first learning-based image codec that simultaneously matches or exceeds JPEG in both RD performance and decoding speed while retaining practical (feedforward) encoding, a combination no prior method achieved. This opens a practical deployment path for INR-based compression that was previously blocked by encoding times of minutes to hours. However, the RD gap versus the SOTA representation-based method Cool-Chic (−12.78% vs. −64.86% BD-Rate) remains substantial, and the decoder's speed advantage depends on NVIDIA Tensor Core hardware. Open questions include whether the RD gap can be closed without sacrificing the sub-millisecond decoding, whether the approach extends to video or higher resolutions, and whether the Tensor Core dependency limits real-world portability.
