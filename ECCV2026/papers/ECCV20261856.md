## Problem

Current text-to-image models generate entire scenes in a single forward pass, making them brittle on spatial relations, object counts, and fine-grained attributes. While chain-of-thought reasoning has been extended to multimodal settings, existing approaches either decouple textual reasoning from visual generation or treat images as static endpoints rather than intermediate states to be inspected and corrected. The paper asks whether a unified multimodal model can genuinely interleave textual planning with visual drafting in a closed loop, where each modality grounds and constrains the other at every step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261856/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (and optionally an input image for editing) and autoregressively emits an interleaved trajectory of tagged textual segments (<ins> for step instructions, <des> for scene descriptions, <refine> for corrections) and visual states wrapped in / tokens, ultimately producing a final image. The trajectory length is complexity-adaptive: the model autonomously determines how many Plan-Sketch-Inspect-Refine cycles are needed, averaging 2.62 reasoning steps (131 total sampling steps) per image. Training uses packed sequences of 33,000 tokens on the BAGEL-7B backbone.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-stage iterative cycle (Plan → Sketch → Inspect → Refine) executed by a single unified multimodal model (BAGEL-7B) that autoregressively generates text tokens (CE loss) and images via rectified flow (MSE loss) within one sequence. Training data is constructed from three complementary subsets totaling 62K samples: (1) Multi-turn Generation trajectories built via scene-graph subsampling to yield contradiction-free incremental prompts, synthesized with Flux-Kontext; (2) Instruction-Intermediate Conflict samples from self-sampled model outputs where GPT judges textual consistency with the original prompt; and (3) Image-Instruction Alignment pairs (from Gen-Ref) supervising visual-side error detection and refinement. A key design choice is semantic partitioning—supervising on concrete, human-interpretable visual states rather than blurry diffusion latents (as in PARM)—combined with self-sampled critiques that align supervision to the model's own failure distribution, yielding +4% on GenEval and +6% on WISE over the base model with 8× lower inference cost.

## Results

On GenEval, STIR achieves 0.83 overall, a +4% absolute improvement over the BAGEL-7B base (0.79) and +3% over Janus-Pro-7B (0.80), with the largest gains in Position (0.51→0.72) and Color Attribute (0.60→0.69). On WISE, STIR reaches 0.76 overall (+6% over BAGEL's 0.70), with particularly large improvements in Time (0.69→0.82) and Chemistry (0.58→0.78). Compared to the process-based baseline PARM (RL+TTS), STIR achieves higher GenEval accuracy (0.83 vs. 0.77) while using 11× less training data (62K vs. 688K samples) and 8× fewer inference steps (131 vs. 1000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STIR outperforms its base model BAGEL-7B by +4% on GenEval (0.83 vs. 0.79) and +6% on WISE (0.76 vs. 0.70), with the most dramatic gains in spatial and attribute-sensitive tasks (Position: +21%, Color Attr.: +9% on GenEval; Time: +13%, Chemistry: +20% on WISE). Among unified multimodal models, STIR surpasses Janus-Pro-7B (0.80) and Show-o2 (0.76) on GenEval, and leads all unified models on WISE by a wide margin over Show-o2 (0.61) and MetaQuery (0.55). Against the strongest generation-only model FLUX.1-dev (12B, 0.82), STIR (7B) achieves a marginal +1% edge on GenEval while also matching or exceeding it on WISE (0.76 vs. 0.50). Compared to the process-based PARM baseline, STIR delivers +6% GenEval accuracy (0.83 vs. 0.77) with 11× less training data and 8× lower inference cost (131 vs. 1000 sampling steps). Training-free approaches (BAGEL + GPT-4o as external planner/inspector) collapse to 0.60–0.80, confirming that internalizing process-level supervision via SFT is essential rather than relying on external verbal guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BAGEL, a unified multimodal model that jointly performs autoregressive text generation and rectified-flow image synthesis, and fine-tunes it end-to-end for a new interleaved reasoning objective. Conceptually, it departs from three prior lines: (1) single-pass text-to-image generation, which the authors argue forces all spatial and compositional decisions into one forward pass; (2) text-only chain-of-thought for image generation, which remains 'visually blind' to intermediate rendering errors; and (3) PARM, a process-level verification approach that supervises blurry diffusion-latent intermediates rather than semantically grounded visual states. The key methodological departure is the use of scene-graph subsampling to construct logically ordered intermediate visual states and self-sampled critique traces to supervise the model's own error detection, rather than relying on external verifiers or latent-space scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B unified model, with only 62K training samples and ~131 inference steps, can match or exceed 12B generation-only models (FLUX.1-dev) on compositional benchmarks, suggesting that explicit process-level supervision is a more data- and compute-efficient path to compositional fidelity than scaling generation-only architectures. The finding that self-sampled critiques (the model's own failure modes) outperform externally derived symbolic corrections by ~6% points toward a broader principle: internalizing a model's distribution-specific errors may be more effective than imposing generic fixes. Open questions include whether the four-stage Plan-Sketch-Inspect-Refine loop generalizes to video or 3D generation, whether the approach scales to more complex multi-object scenes beyond GenEval's scope, and whether the complexity-adaptive trajectory length (averaging 2.62 steps) can be further reduced without quality loss.
