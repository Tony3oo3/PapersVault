## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods rely on a pixel-aligned paradigm where each 2D pixel maps to one 3D Gaussian, creating two fundamental limitations: multi-view feature fusion is forced through error-prone 2D matching, and Gaussian density is rigidly fixed at H×W×N regardless of scene complexity. This produces floaters, view-biased density distributions, and alignment failures under occlusions or low texture.

## Input / Output

The method takes N multi-view RGB images at 256×256 resolution along with their known camera intrinsics and extrinsics as input. Unlike pixel-aligned baselines that always emit exactly H×W×N Gaussians, VolSplat outputs a variable number of 3D Gaussians (position, covariance, opacity, spherical-harmonic color) determined by the occupancy of the predicted 3D voxel grid, enabling adaptive density. These Gaussians are then rendered via standard 3DGS rasterization to synthesize novel views at arbitrary camera poses.

## Method

VolSplat replaces pixel alignment with voxel alignment by lifting 2D image features into a shared 3D voxel grid before predicting Gaussians. Specifically, a weight-sharing ResNet backbone extracts per-view features refined by local-window cross-view attention; a plane-sweep cost volume combined with a Depth Anything V2 backbone produces per-pixel depth maps; features are unprojected into world space and aggregated into voxels via average pooling. A sparse 3D U-Net then refines the voxel features through a residual update, capturing multi-scale geometric context. Finally, per-voxel Gaussian parameters (center offset, opacity, covariance, SH color) are regressed from the refined features, with the center offset constrained to a localized neighborhood around the voxel centroid via a sigmoid-scaled residual. Training is end-to-end supervised with MSE + LPIPS photometric loss on rendered novel views, following the DepthSplat protocol.

## Results

On RealEstate10K with 6 input views, VolSplat achieves 31.30 PSNR, 0.941 SSIM, and 0.075 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest pixel-aligned baseline DepthSplat (30.52 PSNR) by 0.78 dB. On ScanNet (6 views), VolSplat reaches 28.41 PSNR and 0.906 SSIM, surpassing FreeSplat (27.45 PSNR) by nearly 1 dB. In a zero-shot cross-dataset transfer to outdoor ACID scenes, VolSplat achieves 32.65 PSNR and 0.092 LPIPS, exceeding the best baseline DepthSplat (28.37 PSNR) by over 4 dB, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VolSplat outperforms all nine baselines on the primary RealEstate10K benchmark across all view counts. Against the strongest pixel-aligned method DepthSplat, VolSplat gains +0.78 dB PSNR at 6 views (31.30 vs 30.52) and +0.86 dB at 12 views (29.40 vs 28.54). The enhanced pixel-aligned methods GGN, AnySplat, and WorldMirror all underperform VolSplat substantially; AnySplat trails by over 12 dB PSNR (19.05 vs 31.30 at 6v), and WorldMirror, while competitive on LPIPS at 24 views (0.071 vs 0.111), lags by 0.74 dB PSNR. On ScanNet, VolSplat beats FreeSplat/FreeSplat++ by ~1 dB PSNR (28.41 vs 27.45) and WorldMirror by 2.58 dB. In the zero-shot ACID generalization test, VolSplat leads DepthSplat by 4.28 dB PSNR (32.65 vs 28.37), indicating the voxel-aligned formulation is significantly more robust to domain shift than pixel-aligned alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pixel-aligned paradigm in feed-forward 3DGS (pixelSplat, MVSplat, DepthSplat, TranSplat) by shifting Gaussian prediction from per-pixel to per-voxel, decoupling the 3D representation from the 2D image grid. It reuses MVSplat's plane-sweep cost volume construction and DepthSplat's training protocol, but introduces a sparse 3D U-Net refinement stage and voxel-based feature aggregation as the core architectural change. The work is positioned against both pixel-aligned baselines and enhanced variants (GGN, AnySplat, WorldMirror) that attempt to fix pixel alignment's limitations through post-hoc fusion, arguing that those patches do not address the fundamental coupling to the pixel grid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The voxel-aligned formulation offers a principled solution to two persistent problems in feed-forward 3DGS: view-dependent density (one Gaussian per pixel regardless of scene complexity) and multi-view alignment errors from 2D feature matching. The adaptive density property—concentrating Gaussians on geometrically complex regions while sparsifying flat areas—has practical value for downstream tasks like robotics and world models where representation efficiency matters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or unbounded scenes, whether it can operate without ground-truth camera poses (a setting where AnySplat and WorldMirror target), and whether the reliance on predicted depth for the initial lifting step remains a bottleneck for geometric accuracy in textureless regions.
