## Problem

Existing training-free hallucination editing methods for LVLMs (e.g., Nullu) derive editing directions from teacher-forcing contrasts between hallucinated and truthful responses, but the paper shows this is insufficient: truthful tokens remain highly ranked under TF even in hallucination cases, TF-based editing barely shifts the logit margin between truthful and hallucinated tokens during actual autoregressive decoding, and text-only editing directions inadvertently reduce attention to visual tokens across layers. The core gap is that TF-based analysis does not reflect the autoregressive generation dynamics or multimodal interactions that actually produce hallucinated outputs.

## Input / Output

At inference time, DARE takes a standard RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail') and produces a free-form text caption, identical to the base LVLM pipeline. The one-time weight editing requires paired auxiliary data: the LURE dataset for collecting AR decoding trajectories and the BEAF dataset for controlled visual counterfactual pairs (object-present vs. object-removed images from MSCOCO). No additional decoding steps or inference-time overhead are introduced; the edited model is decoded with the original greedy or beam-search pipeline.

## Method

DARE constructs three complementary hallucination direction sets and applies module-specific null-space projections to LVLM weights in a training-free manner. First, TF-based textual contrasts (hallucinated vs. truthful pooled hidden representations under teacher-forcing) yield a TF HalluSpace via SVD. Second, AR-aware HalluSpace is built from sequential hidden-state differences (Δh_ℓ,j = h_ℓ,j − h_ℓ,j−1) collected in a short temporal window (w=3) around each hallucination token position during autoregressive decoding on the LURE dataset. The TF and AR directions are merged into an Augmented HalluSpace by residualizing AR vectors against the TF subspace and applying QR decomposition, then null-space projecting the FFN down-projection weights. Third, Visual-Difference Editing uses BEAF paired images (object removed vs. present) under aligned teacher-forcing to isolate purely visual representation contrasts, whose SVD directions are used to null-space project the attention output projection weights, thereby suppressing hallucination-related visual pathways while preserving grounded multimodal interactions.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 (the primary backbone), DARE reduces CHAIR_S from 24.32 (greedy baseline) to 14.93 and CHAIR_I from 8.91 to 6.07, while maintaining BLEU at 15.61 (vs. 15.16 baseline). On POPE (random sampling), DARE achieves 89.74% accuracy, 91.40% precision, and 90.04% F1, the best among all methods. The gains are consistent across MiniGPT-4 (CHAIR_S 22.64 vs. 35.91 base) and mPLUG-Owl2 (CHAIR_S 16.81 vs. 24.96 base), and DARE also outperforms Nullu on recent models (Qwen3-VL, Gemma-3) at 4B–12B scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARE outperforms all eight baselines on CHAIR_S across all three LVLMs. The closest competitor is CMI-VLD (decoding-based), which achieves CHAIR_S of 16.42 on LLaVA-1.5 vs. DARE's 14.93 — a 1.49-point gap. The most directly comparable method is Nullu (TF-based null-space editing), which DARE beats by 5.60 points on LLaVA-1.5 CHAIR_S (14.93 vs. 20.53) and by 0.96 on CHAIR_I (6.07 vs. 7.03), confirming that AR-aware and visual-difference signals substantially improve over text-only TF contrasts. Among decoding-based methods, OPERA (17.61) and SID (17.95) are the strongest but still trail DARE by 2.7–3.0 points on CHAIR_S. On POPE, DARE leads CMI-VLD by 1.73 points in accuracy (89.74 vs. 88.01) and 0.87 in F1 (90.04 vs. 89.17) on LLaVA-1.5. DARE also maintains or improves BLEU relative to the base model (15.61 vs. 15.16), indicating that hallucination reduction does not come at the cost of general generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DARE extends the null-space projection paradigm introduced by Nullu (Yang et al., CVPR 2025), which constructs a hallucination subspace from teacher-forced textual contrasts and removes it via weight editing. The key departure is identifying a concrete mismatch: prior TF-based methods derive editing directions from static teacher-forced representations, yet hallucinations manifest during autoregressive decoding where token selection depends on local logit competitions. DARE addresses this by adding an AR-aware HalluSpace built from sequential hidden-state transitions around hallucination positions, and a Visual-Difference Editing pathway that uses controlled image pairs (BEAF) to isolate visual representation changes, thereby complementing the text-only contrast with grounded visual evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that TF-based editing produces near-zero changes in the truthful-vs-hallucinated logit margin under AR decoding is a useful diagnostic that questions whether the entire TF-contrast paradigm is targeting the right signal for hallucination mitigation. DARE's training-free, one-time weight editing with no inference-time overhead makes it practically attractive for deployment, and the consistent gains across LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, mPLUG-Owl2, Qwen3-VL, and Gemma-3 at multiple scales suggest broad applicability. Open questions include whether the AR-aware component remains necessary as models improve, whether the approach extends to video or multi-image settings, and whether the visual attention preservation benefit translates to downstream tasks beyond captioning and POPE-style yes/no questions.
