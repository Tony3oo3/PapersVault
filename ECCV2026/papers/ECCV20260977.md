## Problem

Prior work on VLM hallucinations focuses almost entirely on detecting or suppressing them at generation time, leaving the post-hallucination reasoning (PHR) stage—where hallucinated semantics enter the model's inference context and influence downstream predictions—largely unexplored. It remains unclear whether speculative cues help, hinder, or merely perturb subsequent reasoning, and under what conditions they do so.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260977/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HIVE takes a raw input (text passage, RGB image, or structured record) paired with a task instruction and produces a task prediction under three controlled conditions: raw input alone, input concatenated with a faithful caption, and input concatenated with a hallucinated caption. The primary output metric is Δ(H−F), the expected accuracy gap between the hallucinated and faithful augmentation paths, computed over a matched evaluation set. Captions are generated from a single unified source with identical prompts, temperature, and token budget so that hallucination is the only varying factor across conditions.

## Method

HIVE is a three-module evaluation infrastructure rather than a new model: a Caption Generator produces N diverse captions from a unified LLM source; a Caption Discriminator uses an ensemble of independent detectors with majority voting to label each caption as faithful or hallucinatory, retaining only contrasted ⟨C_F, C_H⟩ pairs; and a Task Solver concatenates the task instruction, serialized input, and selected caption into a unified prompt to elicit predictions under matched decoding settings. The paper then characterizes PHR at three levels: input-level embedding distribution shifts (hallucinated captions show wider variance and heavier tails in SBERT space), process-level reasoning-entropy modulation (hallucinations reduce trajectory entropy on reasoning-heavy tasks but increase it on open-ended tasks), and output-level caption-entropy correlation with correctness. Ablations over sampling temperature (peak at T=0.6), token budget (256 tokens optimal), and hallucination intensity (inverted-U pattern) identify the conditions under which speculative cues provide the strongest downstream benefit.

## Results

On vision-language tasks, hallucinated captions yield strong and consistent accuracy gains across all four VLMs: GPT-4o improves by +11.76 pp on ISIC and +14.68 pp on PlantVillage; Qwen-VL-Max gains +16.91 pp on ISIC; Claude-3-Sonnet gains +17.22 pp on PlantVillage. On text-only tasks, effects are small and inconsistent, with Δ(H−F) ranging from −13.80 pp (O3 on BBBP) to +6.66 pp (GPT-4o on BBBP), indicating hallucinated semantics are hard to exploit in symbolically constrained settings. The hallucination discriminator achieves 81.76% accuracy on TruthfulQA and 83.72% on a human-annotated cross-domain set, validating the paired-comparison protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between hallucinated (H) and faithful (F) captions under identical model and decoding settings, with raw input (no caption) as a third reference condition. On vision-language tasks, H consistently outperforms F: 12 out of 16 model–dataset pairs show positive Δ, with the largest gains on perception-heavy benchmarks (ISIC, PlantVillage) where partial observability is most acute. GPT-4o and Qwen-VL-Max show the most reliable positive effects across all four VLM tasks, while Gemini-2.0-Flash gains are smaller and Claude-3-Sonnet drops slightly on GQA (−0.99 pp). On text-only tasks (Table 1), the comparison is far less favorable: only 10 of 40 model–dataset pairs show positive Δ, and several strong models (O3, DeepSeek-R1) actually degrade under hallucinated inputs, suggesting that hallucinated semantics are exploitable primarily when visual grounding is incomplete. A random-checker ablation confirms that the observed gains are not artifacts of arbitrary filtering (all p > 0.14), and a token-level ablation shows that masking the specific hallucinated evidence tokens drops accuracy from near-perfect to 0.24–0.70, confirming the hallucinated content is causally responsible for the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant hallucination literature in VLMs, which focuses almost exclusively on detecting or suppressing hallucinations at generation time, by instead studying the downstream reasoning effects once hallucinated semantics enter the model's inference context. It draws a conceptual parallel to LLM chain-of-thought research showing that unfaithful intermediate steps do not necessarily break reasoning, but repurposes this insight for multimodal settings where hallucinated captions serve as input-level semantic anchors rather than intermediate reasoning artifacts. The HIVE framework itself is not a new model but a controlled evaluation infrastructure that organizes existing caption generation and hallucination detection practices into a paired-comparison protocol, carving out a previously unexamined analytical stage (Post-Hallucination Reasoning) between hallucination occurrence and final prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that hallucinated captions systematically improve vision-language task accuracy (up to ~17 percentage points) challenges the field's default assumption that hallucinations are purely detrimental, reframing them as potential speculative anchors under partial observability. This opens practical questions about how to control hallucination-induced semantic expansion—temperature, token budget, and interpolation strength emerge as tunable levers—without deliberately injecting errors into production systems. Key limitations include the caption-level intervention scope (hallucinations within intermediate reasoning steps remain unaddressed), the fixed benchmark set, and the fact that benefits are task-dependent and absent in text-only settings, leaving open whether the phenomenon generalizes to more complex multi-step multimodal reasoning pipelines.
