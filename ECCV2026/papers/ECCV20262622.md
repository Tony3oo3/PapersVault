## Problem

Current text-to-image models produce scientific figures that are visually plausible but factually incorrect, violating physical laws, topological constraints, and causal logic. This stems from three compounding gaps: the scarcity of training data with explicit scientific reasoning annotations, the absence of evaluation frameworks that decompose scientific correctness into verifiable atomic checks, and the lack of models that can internalize domain-specific constraints beyond perceptual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262622/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt as input, which comes in two forms: a concise abstract prompt (for Intrinsic Reasoning evaluation) or a detailed Scientific Reasoning Chain-of-Thought (Sci-RCoT) (for Instruction Following evaluation). The output is a 1024×1024 RGB scientific illustration. The SciIR-82k training corpus consists of 80,000+ image-text pairs sourced from CC BY 4.0-licensed Nature and Nature Communications articles, each annotated with a structured Sci-RCoT reasoning chain and a distilled prompt. Evaluation on SciIR-Bench (800 instances) uses binary atomic checklist questions generated per scientific term, with a strict veto mechanism where a single failed check invalidates the entire sample for that track.

## Method

The paper introduces a semiotic-triad-grounded framework (Icon→Entity Structure, Index→Scientific Process, Symbol→Scientific Law) that organizes both training data and evaluation. For data construction, a multi-stage pipeline uses YOLO11 for sub-figure decomposition, InternVL3.5 for schematic filtering, Qwen3-VL for semiotic stratification, and a reverse-engineering annotation process (Qwen3-VL for visual grounding + Qwen3-Max for semantic abstraction) to derive Sci-RCoT traces and compact prompts from ground-truth images. For evaluation, SciIR-Bench organizes 800 samples into four combinatorial groups (all-three, Law-Entity, Law-Process, Entity-Process) with adaptive difficulty, and employs Gemini-3-Pro to generate term-driven atomic checklists and adjudicate via a Visual Evidence Retrieval protocol. The proposed Qwen-Image-SciIR model decouples reasoning from rendering: a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct reasoning planner (LoRA r=64, α=16) generates Sci-RCoT from the prompt, which is then fed to a Qwen-Image-2512 diffusion generator (LoRA r=32) to synthesize the final image, raising the SciIR-Bench score from 35% to 43%.

## Results

On SciIR-Bench (800 test instances), Qwen-Image-SciIR achieves a Final Accuracy Score of 43%, up from 35% for its backbone Qwen-Image-2512, with the largest gains in Scientific Process (+16%, from 37% to 53%) and Entity Structure (+9%, from 50% to 59%). Among open-source models, Qwen-Image-SciIR is the strongest, outperforming the best autoregressive model Show-o2-7B (21%) by 22 points and the best open-source diffusion model Flux-Dev (9%) by 34 points. The top closed-source model Nano-Banana-Pro reaches 95%, indicating the task is solvable but leaving a 52-point gap for open-source systems. All models show severe weakness on the Text track, with even the best open-source model scoring only 15%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen-Image-SciIR (43% Final) substantially outperforms all open-source baselines: it beats Show-o2-7B (21%) by 22 points, HiDream-L1-Full (13%) by 30 points, Flux-Dev (9%) by 34 points, SD 3.5 Large (5%) by 38 points, BAGEL-7B-MoT (2%) by 41 points, and Janus-Pro-7B (1%) by 42 points. It also surpasses the closed-source FLUX.1-Kontext-Max (22%) by 21 points, though it trails the top closed-source systems: Nano-Banana-Pro (95%), GPT-Image-1 (62%), and Seedream 4.5 (55%). Compared to its own backbone Qwen-Image-2512 (35%), the fine-tuned model gains 8 points overall, with the most pronounced improvement in Scientific Process (37%→53%, +16%) and Entity Structure (50%→59%, +9%), while Scientific Law and Text scores remain nearly unchanged (40%→43% and 15%→15% respectively), suggesting the reasoning-chain training primarily strengthens structural and causal reasoning rather than law adherence or text rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-image generation literature by grounding scientific figure synthesis in Peirce's Semiotic Triad (Icon/Index/Symbol), mapping it to Entity Structure, Scientific Process, and Scientific Law. It departs from prior scientific figure generation work (AutoFigure, PaperBanana, ImgCoder), which emphasizes layout fidelity and procedural diagrams, by targeting schematics that encode physical laws and causal mechanisms. The Sci-RCoT annotation scheme builds on chain-of-thought reasoning in multimodal generation (ImageGen-CoT, self-reflective RL) but inverts the pipeline—reverse-engineering reasoning from ground-truth images rather than forward-generating from prompts. For evaluation, it extends the atomic-checklist paradigm from VQA-based T2I benchmarks (GenEval, TIFA) into a domain-specific, term-driven protocol with adversarial probes, filling the gap left by SridBench and FigureBench, which lack fine-grained multi-dimensional scientific correctness assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SciIR-82k and SciIR-Bench provide the first large-scale, reasoning-annotated resource and the first benchmark covering all three semiotic dimensions simultaneously, giving the community a principled diagnostic tool for scientific image generation. The finding that even the strongest open-source model (Qwen-Image-2512) scores only 35% versus 95% for Nano-Banana-Pro, and that text rendering fails catastrophically across all architectures (≤15%), highlights a fundamental gap between perceptual quality and scientific validity that current training paradigms do not address. Limitations include bias toward published, standardized Nature/Nature Communications figures and an emphasis on correctness over visual aesthetics; future work should broaden domain and style coverage, incorporate cross-lingual and multimodal annotations, and explore hybrid symbolic-constraint or adversarial evaluation approaches.
