## Problem

Modern text-to-image models exhibit severe diversity collapse: multiple samples from the same prompt converge on a single high-level semantic interpretation, differing only in visually insignificant details. Existing diversity methods (conditioning perturbation, latent repulsion, candidate-pool filtering) increase sample variety but produce stochastic, uninterpretable variations rather than meaningful design choices. No prior approach provides structured, user-controllable diversity where each variation corresponds to a specific, navigable semantic decision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt and produces a structured gallery of 27 images (branching factor 3, depth 3) organized as a rooted tree of fully specified JSON scene representations. Unlike flat image sets, the tree encodes semantic relationships: sibling nodes differ by exactly one atomic semantic constraint while preserving all ancestor constraints, and a 'preserve' branch at each level propagates the parent state unchanged to the leaf level. The final output is both a set of renderable images and a navigable hierarchy where topological proximity reflects semantic similarity.

## Method

The core insight is that recent prompt-adherent T2I models (e.g., FIBO) are trained on elaborated captions, enabling diversity to be induced at the text level rather than the pixel/latent level. A multi-agent workflow (Context Analyst, Brainstormer, Decision Maker, Critic) iteratively expands a tree of JSON scene representations, where each edge encodes one atomic semantic constraint. The Context Analyst identifies mutable scene details, the Brainstormer groups them into high-level semantic aspects, the Decision Maker selects one aspect and formulates maximally divergent sibling constraints, and the Critic validates plausibility against the prompt and accumulated ancestor constraints. Three interdependent requirements—Semantic Structuring (siblings vary along one shared aspect), Heterogeneity (each sibling is conceptually distinct), and Plausibility (constraints remain logically consistent with the prompt and branch history)—govern every branching decision. The entire pipeline is training-free, leveraging the VLM for semantic reasoning and the T2I model solely as a faithful renderer of the specified JSON scenes.

## Results

On 50 MS-COCO prompts, Semantic Browsing achieves the highest Vendi Score (3.34) and lowest pairwise DINO similarity (0.61) among all methods, indicating the greatest semantic diversity in the generated gallery. Image quality is preserved with an Aesthetic Score of 6.52, comparable to the best baselines (6.53–6.56), while prompt adherence (VQAScore 0.90) remains competitive, trailing the strongest baselines by only 0.02–0.03. In a user study with 25 participants, Semantic Browsing wins the majority vote for overall preference and achieves the highest diversity win rate across all four baseline comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CADS, the closest generator-level competitor, Semantic Browsing wins on both diversity metrics (Vendi 3.34 vs. 3.29; DINO Sim. 0.61 vs. 0.67) and on Aesthetic Score (6.52 vs. 6.30), while matching VQAScore (0.90 vs. 0.89). Guidance Interval is outperformed more clearly on diversity (Vendi 3.34 vs. 2.96; DINO Sim. 0.61 vs. 0.71) and Aesthetic (6.52 vs. 6.42), though it edges ahead on VQAScore (0.92 vs. 0.90). Power-Law CFG shows the weakest diversity among the generator-level baselines (Vendi 2.75, DINO Sim. 0.74) and the lowest Aesthetic Score (6.28), with Semantic Browsing leading on all four metrics. The VLM-stochasticity baselines (Stochastic Seeding, Post-Hoc Optimization, High-Temp. Post-Hoc) trail the proposed method by 0.49–0.74 in Vendi Score and 0.05–0.15 in DINO similarity, confirming that simply varying VLM seeds or selecting diverse subsets from a large pool cannot match the structured, aspect-level diversity achieved by the agentic tree expansion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of diversity-in-T2I work (CADS, Guidance Interval, Particle Guidance, Contextual Repulsion, SGI) that perturbs the generation process in latent or guidance space, arguing instead that diversity should be induced at the text/semantic level. It repurposes the strict prompt adherence of modern T2I models (exemplified by FIBO's structured-caption training) not as a limitation on variety but as the mechanism that makes explicit semantic edits reliably translate into visual changes. The multi-agent workflow draws on recent agentic T2I systems (Maestro, PromptSculptor, TwinCo) but inverts their goal: rather than converging on a single optimal interpretation, the agents are organized to explore and maintain multiple plausible readings of an under-specified prompt, structured as a hierarchical tree of scene interpretations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a navigable, interpretable alternative to stochastic diversity, which is practically useful for creative workflows where users need to compare and select among meaningfully different design choices rather than visually similar variants. Key limitations include dependence on the VLM's ability to propose genuinely diverse semantic alternatives (which remains narrower than the full space of possible interpretations) and on the T2I model's fidelity to fine-grained prompt edits. The authors note the paradigm could extend to video, 3D, and multimodal generation, but the current evaluation is confined to a single generator (FIBO) and a 50-prompt MS-COCO subset, leaving open questions about generalizability across architectures and prompt complexity.
