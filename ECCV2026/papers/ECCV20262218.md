## Problem

State-of-the-art vision models process entire images uniformly and require resizing to fixed input dimensions, causing small task-relevant details in high-resolution scenes to be lost. For instance detection specifically, task-specific information (e.g., a search target) is only incorporated after a costly full-scene feature extraction stage, rather than being used early to steer computation toward promising regions. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a task-driven front-end that selectively directs limited-view glimpses to relevant regions before passing them to a downstream detector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262218/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CF-GAP takes a high-resolution scene image (up to 6144×8192 pixels) and multiple visual examples of the search target from different viewpoints. It produces a sequence of 30 targeted regions of interest (each a fixed-size crop around a fine glimpse location), the fine glimpse coordinates themselves (serving as spatial prompts or bounding-box filters for the downstream detector), and the search target examples for final matching. The log-polar sensor has a 4096-pixel diameter and outputs 245×245 glimpses, and the entire front-end is agnostic to the choice of downstream architecture (SAM, GroundingDINO, MobileSAM, STT, or NIDS-Net).

## Method

CF-GAP operates in two nested stages. Coarse glimpsing uses lightweight MobileNet-V3 encoders on a downscaled scene to produce a search map via feature correlation with the search target, then iteratively selects locations via winner-takes-all with inhibition-of-return. Fine glimpsing is a closed loop where a fovea-inspired log-polar sensor extracts high-resolution glimpses, and Perceiver-based encoders (cross-attention compression to N latent embeddings, self-attention, then cross-attention decoding back to spatial dimensions) generate fine search maps whose 2D centroids steer the sensor to progressively refine the location. The log-polar representation provides non-uniform resolution—high detail near the center, compressed periphery—making fine search map extraction robust to peripheral distractors without requiring crop-size tuning. The resulting RoIs and glimpse locations are passed to any existing instance detector, which performs final recognition via DINOv2 feature matching, enabling lightweight detectors to outperform larger counterparts by receiving targeted, high-quality input stripped of irrelevant context.

## Results

CF-GAP improves Average Precision (AP) by up to 20% across all tested instance detectors on the HR-InsDet and Robotools benchmarks. On the most challenging HR-InsDet subsets (small objects and hard/cluttered scenes), CF-GAP+NIDS-Net achieves 52.1 and 63.2 AP respectively, compared to 32.4 and 39.9 for standalone NIDS-Net. The lightweight OTS-FM_MobileSAM backbone benefits most, jumping from 12.4 to 29.3 AP on small objects and from 22.0 to 41.7 AP on hard scenes, surpassing both OTS-FM_SAM and OTS-FM_GroundingDINO on these difficult subsets. On Robotools (novel objects unseen during training), CF-GAP consistently improves AP for all baselines, with the lightweight variant achieving AP_50 comparable to heavier models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CF-GAP consistently outperforms all standalone baselines across both benchmarks. Against OTS-FM (Shen et al., NeurIPS 2023), the CF-GAP extension with the same SAM backbone improves AP from 14.6 to 32.4 on small objects and from 28.0 to 47.1 on hard scenes; notably, even the lightweight MobileSAM variant with CF-GAP (29.3/41.7) surpasses the full OTS-FM_SAM (14.6/28.0) and OTS-FM_GroundingDINO (28.8/37.2) on these difficult subsets. Against NIDS-Net (Lu et al., IROS 2025), CF-GAP+NIDS-Net achieves 52.1 vs. 32.4 AP on small objects and 63.2 vs. 39.9 on hard scenes, a gain of roughly 20 AP points. The patched-baseline comparison (splitting scenes into 1024×1024 patches) shows that exhaustive high-resolution processing recovers some detail (e.g., Patched NIDS-Net reaches 50.1 on small) but still trails CF-GAP+NIDS-Net (52.1) while processing over 5× more RoIs. For MobileSAM (Zhang et al., 2023) and STT (Schmidt et al., CVPR 2025) as efficient SAM backbones, CF-GAP enables these lightweight models to match or exceed the performance of the full SAM-based OTS-FM at a fraction of the FLOPs (80–130 GFLOPs vs. 5800 GFLOPs for full SAM), demonstrating that targeted high-resolution input compensates for a weaker detector backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

CF-GAP directly extends the Glimpse-based Active Perception (GAP) framework by replacing its saliency-driven fixation with a task-conditioned, coarse-to-fine search strategy guided by a specific search target. The search-map extraction mechanism (scene encoder vs. target encoder compared via convolution) is inherited from Transporter Networks and Finding Any Waldo, while the Perceiver-based cross-attention encoders are a novel architectural choice motivated by the non-uniform resolution of log-polar glimpses, which breaks the uniform-resolution assumption of standard CNNs. Unlike prior saccadic or foveated vision approaches that use fovea-inspired representations to encode a fixed view, CF-GAP uses the log-polar sensor as a navigational tool in a closed-loop process, and unlike prior instance detectors (OTS-FM, NIDS-Net, IDOW) that exhaustively process the entire scene before matching, CF-GAP conditions exploration on the task from the very first glimpse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a useful architectural principle—decomposing perception into a lightweight 'looking' front-end and a 'seeing' back-end—that enables distilled models (MobileSAM) to match or surpass full-size counterparts (SAM) on challenging subsets, with practical implications for edge deployment. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond texture-based matching to semantic or text-driven queries, how to adapt the fixed-size RoI to variable object scales, and whether an early-termination mechanism could reduce the linear cost of repeated downstream invocations. The finding that targeted processing of 30 RoIs outperforms exhaustive patching of 165 RoIs suggests that selective, task-driven computation may be a broadly applicable strategy for high-resolution vision, though the current evidence is confined to instance detection.
