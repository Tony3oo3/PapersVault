## Problem

In masked autoencoders, the high spatial redundancy and correlation among neighboring image patches make random masking produce tasks that are too easy (masked patches sit too close to visible ones), while raw-pixel reconstruction forces the model to recover high-frequency components irrelevant to intrinsic image content. Existing remedies—content-based masking or semantic reconstruction targets—add substantial computational overhead, undermining the simplicity and scalability that make MAE attractive for large-scale self-supervised pre-training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-10) divided into 16×16 patches, and outputs a pre-trained ViT encoder whose features are evaluated via linear probing or fine-tuning. Two unusual processing steps modify the standard MAE pipeline: the random-value map used to select unmasked patches is first passed through a k×k avg- or max-pooling filter, and the reconstruction target is a Gaussian-smoothed version of the original patches rather than raw pixels. No architectural changes to the ViT encoder or decoder are required.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary filtering operations that control MAE task difficulty at negligible extra cost. First, pooling-based masking: a uniform random-value map R ∈ [0,1]^{H×W} is filtered by k×k avg- or max-pooling before the top n(1−p) values are selected as unmasked patches; this introduces spatial correlation that localizes unmasked patches and increases the mean nearest-neighbor distance (formalized as a difficulty score c), with 2×2 max-pooling (c≈3.83) found optimal. Second, target image smoothing: a Gaussian filter (scale σ) is applied to the masked-patch targets before computing the L2 reconstruction loss, softly suppressing high-frequency components that the DCT-based frequency analysis shows are barely recovered during training and are irrelevant to intrinsic image semantics. The two filters work in opposite directions—masking filtering raises difficulty to push the model beyond superficial patch correlation, while reconstruction smoothing lowers difficulty to prevent the model from overfitting to noise-like textures—jointly promoting richer, more uniform feature representations without altering the MAE architecture.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ViT-B (800 epochs), the proposed method (σ=1) achieves 70.07% linear-probe accuracy and 83.75% fine-tuning accuracy, outperforming original MAE (65.14% / 83.44%) and ColorMAE (68.67% / 83.6%). At 1600 epochs, the gap widens: Ours (σ=1) reaches 72.26% linear-probe and 84.03% fine-tuning versus MAE's 66.71% / 83.65%. On CIFAR-10, 2×2 max-pool masking boosts accuracy from 79.18% (random) to 82.16%, and on ImageNet-100 the combined max-pool + σ=4 smoothing yields 73.29% versus 68.99% for vanilla MAE. On ADE-20K semantic segmentation, the pre-trained ViT-B achieves 45.33% mIOU (σ=4, 1600 epochs) versus 43.46% for original MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms original MAE [22] across all datasets: +4.93% linear-probe on ImageNet-1K (800 ep), +5.55% on ImageNet-100 (73.29% vs 68.99%), and +2.98% on CIFAR-10 (82.16% vs 79.18%). Against ColorMAE [23], the closest data-agnostic masking competitor, the method gains +1.40% linear-probe on ImageNet-1K (800 ep) and +1.41% on ImageNet-100 (73.29% vs 71.88% with σ=4). On structured masking baselines, BEiT [1] (56.7% lin-probe) and block masking [25,51] (71.05% on ImageNet-100) are both clearly surpassed. The method also improves upon U-MAE [52] (73.31% vs 69.36% on ImageNet-100) and D-MAE [49] (62.27% vs 57.20% certified accuracy at r=0), demonstrating compatibility with other MAE variants. On frequency-based losses, the simple Gaussian smoothing (σ=4) outperforms Focal Frequency Loss [26] (73.29% vs 70.94%) and Freq. L1 loss [50] (73.29% vs 66.03%) on ImageNet-100. The only baseline that slightly edges out the method is HPM [46] on fine-tuning (84.2% vs 83.75%), though HPM uses data-adaptive masking at additional computational cost; CAE [6] at 1600 epochs is comparable on fine-tuning (83.9% vs 84.03%) but inferior on linear-probe (71.4% vs 72.26%). On ADE-20K segmentation, the method improves mIOU by +1.87 over MAE [22] (45.33% vs 43.46% at 1600 epochs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) by introducing a quantitative task-difficulty measure—mean nearest neighbor distance between masked and unmasked patches—and using it to guide two simple filtering operations: pooling-based masking (to increase difficulty) and Gaussian smoothing of reconstruction targets (to decrease difficulty). It is conceptually closest to ColorMAE (Hinojosa et al., ECCV 2024), which also applies band-pass filtering to the random mask map, but departs by providing a principled difficulty score rather than a heuristic filter design. Unlike content-based masking (SemMAE, HPM) or semantic reconstruction targets (HOG, visual tokenizers), the method remains entirely data-agnostic and architecture-preserving, requiring no additional networks or training stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mean nearest neighbor distance provides a lightweight, interpretable diagnostic for MIM task difficulty that practitioners can use to tune mask ratio and masking strategy without expensive ablations. The frequency analysis revealing that MAE reconstruction is heavily biased toward low-frequency components (with roughly the top half of DCT frequencies essentially unreconstructed) offers a useful explanation for why raw-pixel MAE works as a representation learner rather than a pixel-perfect reconstructor. Open questions include whether the optimal difficulty range (c ≈ 3–4) generalizes across architectures beyond ViT, whether combining the data-agnostic difficulty control with content-based masking yields further gains, and whether the smoothing approach transfers to video or 3D reconstruction settings.
