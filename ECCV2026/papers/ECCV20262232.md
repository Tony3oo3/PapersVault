## Problem

Reconstructing the surface mass density of galaxy clusters from gravitational lensing is highly ill-posed, especially for the common 10^13.5–10^14 M☉ range where lensing signals are sparse. Existing fully automatic methods either impose a rigid light-traces-mass 1:1 assumption or ignore photometry entirely, while state-of-the-art tools like Lenstool and WSLAP+ require cluster-specific expert tuning that does not scale to the hundreds of thousands of clusters expected from upcoming wide-field surveys. No large benchmark dataset existed to train or evaluate scalable mass-mapping methods across redshifts and simulation frameworks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three 512×512 HST photometry images (F125W, F606W, F814W bands), strong-lensing multiple-image angular positions (5–20 sources per cluster), and sparse weak-lensing shear measurements (~420 points per 225×225 arcsec field of view at SNR 0.01–0.1). It outputs a 512×512 convergence (κ) map along with 20 posterior samples whose per-pixel standard deviation provides calibrated uncertainty estimates. Photometry enters only as a conditioning signal for the generative prior, while lensing observables are enforced through physics-based forward models in the likelihood, so the two information channels are decoupled by design.

## Method

The core architecture is a photometry-conditioned DDPM++ diffusion prior trained with the EDM loss on DARKCLUSTERS-15K (15,000 mock clusters from IllustrisTNG and SIMBA at 512×512 resolution), which learns the joint mass–light distribution without assuming a fixed mass-to-light ratio. At inference, the Decoupled Annealing Posterior Sampling (DAPS) algorithm alternates between drawing a clean sample from a conditional posterior that fuses the diffusion prior with a physics-based likelihood, and re-noising that sample, iterating to progressively lower noise levels. The likelihood is a weighted sum of three losses: a strong-lensing geometric loss (squared deviation of back-projected source positions from their mean), a strong-lensing photometric loss (Sersic-profile image matching at each multiple-image position), and a weak-lensing shear loss (RBF-interpolated sparse-to-dense shear comparison scaled to a reference distance). This design lets the generative prior capture flexible, non-parametric mass–light correlations while the lensing operators enforce physical consistency, yielding 512×512 free-parameter reconstructions in minutes with well-calibrated per-pixel uncertainties.

## Results

On the DARKCLUSTERS-15K test set at z_L=0.5, the method achieves PSNR 33.3 (TNG) and 30.9 (SIMBA), SSIM 0.927/0.884, and PCC 0.975/0.930, outperforming all baselines on nearly every metric. Held-out convergence RMSE is 0.0058 (TNG) and 0.0067 (SIMBA), the lowest among all methods. On the real MACS 1206 cluster, the method recovers an enclosed mass of (7.61±0.04)×10¹³ M⊙ within 5.03% of the expert-tuned Lenstool estimate, with a strong-lensing RMS scatter of 0.56 arcsec versus 0.44 arcsec for the reference reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Kaiser-Squires inversion [25], the method dominates across all metrics: PSNR improves from 20.9 to 33.3 (TNG) and PCC from 0.189 to 0.975, reflecting that weak-lensing-only inversion is severely under-constrained. Against Napier et al. [39], which uses a parametric PIEMD model with a rigid 1:1 mass-to-light ratio, the method wins on every metric (PSNR 33.3 vs 26.0, PCC 0.975 vs 0.832 on TNG), with the gap widening on SIMBA where Napier's assumptions break down (PCC 0.930 vs 0.616). Against the UNet photometry-to-mass baseline, the method wins on PSNR, SSIM, PCC, κ-RMSE, and γ-RMSE on both test sets, but the UNet achieves a slightly lower α-RMSE on TNG (0.411 vs 0.530), suggesting the diffusion prior's photometry conditioning alone can match direct regression on deflection angles in-distribution. On the real MACS 1206 cluster, the method's enclosed mass estimate differs from the expert-tuned Lenstool reconstruction of Caminha et al. [8] by only 5.03% (well within the ~10% spread typical among independent expert models), and the strong-lensing RMS scatter of 0.56 arcsec is close to the 0.44 arcsec of the reference, all without any cluster-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) galaxy-cluster mass reconstruction, where prior work ranges from parametric tools requiring expert tuning (Lenstool, WSLAP+) to free-form methods with simple regularizers (MARS, Kaiser-Squires inversion), and (2) diffusion-based posterior sampling for inverse problems (DAPS, diffusion posterior sampling). The key departure is combining a photometry-conditioned diffusion prior—trained on the new DARKCLUSTERS-15K dataset—with physics-based forward models for both strong and weak lensing in a single automated pipeline, rather than imposing a rigid light-traces-mass (1:1) assumption or relying on a single lensing regime. The dataset itself, derived from IllustrisTNG and SIMBA with simulated lensing observables, is the largest of its kind and fills a gap left by smaller benchmarks like the Frontier Fields clusters or the coarser CAMELS Multifield Dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Upcoming wide-field surveys (Euclid, Rubin/LSST) are expected to discover hundreds of thousands of clusters above 10^13.5 M_sun, making fully automated, scalable mass reconstruction essential; this paper demonstrates that a diffusion-based pipeline can match expert-tuned Lenstool results on MACS 1206 in minutes rather than hours, with calibrated per-pixel uncertainties. The DARKCLUSTERS-15K dataset provides a much-needed large-scale benchmark for the community. Open questions include how well the method generalizes to real observational noise beyond the single MACS 1206 validation, whether the simulation-trained prior captures all relevant baryonic physics, and whether the approach scales to the even lower-mass clusters (10^13–10^13.5 M_sun) where lensing signals are extremely sparse.
