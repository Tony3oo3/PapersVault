## Problem

Reliable detection of sharp features (loci of surface-normal discontinuity) in point clouds degrades under the joint effect of increased local k-NN spacing variability from subsampling and additive measurement noise, because neighborhood-based differential cues become inconsistent across irregular densities. Additionally, sharp features constitute a small minority class (1–10%), so standard training objectives bias predictions toward the dominant non-sharp class, yielding poor recall for these critical points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262499/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unordered 3D point cloud (10K points from ABC, further subsampled to 500 points per forward pass to stress-test sparse-input recovery) and produces a per-point binary sharp/non-sharp label. The per-point input to the network is a 6-channel vector formed by concatenating the 3D coordinates with a 3-channel Local Geometric Descriptor (LGD) computed on the CPU as a one-time preprocessing step, making the descriptor architecture-agnostic and attachable to any backbone that accepts per-point feature channels.

## Method

SFD-Net is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 computes a compact Local Geometric Descriptor (LGD) per point: PCA normals are estimated at three nested k-NN scales (k ∈ {20, 40, 80}), pairwise normal differences are aggregated into a weighted second-moment tensor per scale, and the per-scale isotropy index (1 − λ_max/trace) is concatenated into a 3D descriptor that is rigid-motion-, scale-, and flip-invariant. Stage 2 concatenates LGD with raw coordinates and feeds the 6-channel input into a PointNet++ encoder-decoder augmented with two lightweight Transformer encoder blocks (one after the first Set Abstraction stage for cross-patch aggregation, one after the first Feature Propagation stage for cross-scale consistency). Training uses a composite Focal Tversky + Dice loss to explicitly balance false positives and false negatives under severe class imbalance. LGD is architecture-agnostic: prepending it to PointNet++, RepSurf, or EDWG improves F1 and FPR without any backbone modification, and the full model trained on synthetic ABC CAD data generalizes zero-shot to real S3DIS indoor scans.

## Results

On the ABC benchmark, SFD-Net achieves state-of-the-art Average F1 across all four noise conditions: 61.33% (no noise), 60.42% (0.12%), 58.62% (0.6%), and 57.05% (1.2%), with FPR ranging from 36.93% to 41.94%. The largest margin over the second-best method (MSL-Net) is 4.31 pp at the highest noise level. In zero-shot transfer to S3DIS Area 5, SFD-Net attains 53.20% F1 and 41.63% FPR, outperforming the best baseline (EDWG: 50.88% F1, 45.40% FPR) without any retraining on real scans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFD-Net outperforms all ten baselines on Average F1 across all four ABC noise conditions. The closest competitor, MSL-Net, is surpassed by 1.92 pp (no noise) to 4.31 pp (1.2% noise), with the gap widening as noise increases. Generic backbones (PointNet++, DGCNN, PointMLP) achieve near-random FPR (~50%), indicating severe majority-class bias under the 1–10% sharp-feature ratio. RepSurf is competitive at low noise (57.16% F1 at 0.12%) but degrades to 48.17% at 1.2% noise, while EdgeFormer collapses dramatically (F1 drops from 52.04% to 28.13%) under moderate-to-large noise. PIE-Net maintains the lowest FPR among task-specific methods (37–40%) but sacrifices recall, yielding only 47–52% F1. On S3DIS zero-shot evaluation, SFD-Net leads all baselines by 2.32 pp F1 over EDWG, confirming that the geometric cues learned on 10K-point CAD instances transfer to 363K-point real scans.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFD-Net sits at the intersection of classical geometric feature extraction (PCA normals, surface variation, curvature-based descriptors) and learning-based point cloud detection (PointNet++, MSL-Net, EdgeFormer, EDWG). Its core departure is the Local Geometric Descriptor (LGD), which aggregates second-moment statistics of multi-scale PCA normal differences into a 3-channel isotropy index—a design that is explicitly invariant to rigid motion, global scale, and normal sign flip, addressing a joint density-variation-and-noise robustness gap that prior neighborhood-based methods (including MSL-Net and EdgeFormer) do not systematically handle. The enhanced PointNet++ backbone with dual Transformer encoder blocks extends the standard hierarchical architecture with non-local attention, while the composite Focal Tversky–Dice loss targets the severe class imbalance inherent to sparse sharp-feature labels. Crucially, LGD is architecture-agnostic and can be prepended to any per-point-feature backbone, positioning it as a drop-in geometric prior rather than a monolithic new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reliable sharp-feature localization is a prerequisite for wireframe generation, point cloud registration, boundary-aware semantic segmentation, and mesh reconstruction, yet degrades sharply under the joint perturbations (noise + spacing variability) that co-occur in real scanning pipelines. The plug-and-play nature of LGD—improving F1 by 4–7 pp and FPR by 2.6–9 pp across three independent backbones without architectural changes—makes it a practically adoptable component for existing detectors. Key limitations include reliance on PCA-based normals (fragile under heavy noise), binary sharp/non-sharp output that does not distinguish creases from corners, and S3DIS evaluation based on pseudo-labels that understate true detection quality. Open directions include learned normal estimation, finer geometric category discrimination, semi-supervised transfer from synthetic CAD to real scans, and fully end-to-end differentiable descriptor learning.
