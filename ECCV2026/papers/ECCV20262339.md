## Problem

Existing 3D scene representations such as NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting operate on low-level geometric or radiometric primitives, so object-level decomposition requires additional segmentation, grouping, or semantic overlays. The paper asks whether objects or object parts can instead serve as the fundamental functional units of the representation, with object-level structure emerging naturally from the representation's inductive bias rather than being imposed externally.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes posed RGB images and a COLMAP point cloud (used for octree-based initialization of primitive centers). At inference, the scene is queried by camera rays to produce rendered RGB images; with optional semantic embeddings, it also yields per-pixel semantic masks from text prompts (via LSeg-CLIP) or point/box prompts (via SAM). Each primitive receives a 6D geometric input—ray direction concatenated with the local offset from the soft contact point to the primitive center—positionally encoded to 72 dimensions, rather than a raw 3D coordinate.

## Method

MLP-Splatting represents a scene as a collection of independent compact MLPs (72-32-32-4), each with an anisotropic Gaussian spatial support defined by a center, scale vector, and rotation quaternion. For each ray-primitive pair, a closed-form soft contact point is computed by minimizing a Gaussian-weighted distance, yielding a 6D input that couples viewing direction with local geometric context; multi-resolution positional encoding enhances high-frequency expressivity. Rendering uses tile-based sparse volumetric compositing: primitives are sorted by depth along a virtual ray through each tile center, then alpha-blended front-to-back, avoiding dense ray marching. Under RGB supervision alone, primitives naturally specialize to coherent object or object-part regions, enabling interactive editing without segmentation; optional feature distillation from LSeg-CLIP adds open-vocabulary semantic capability. The paper further derives an Equal-Quality Memory Law showing that achieving pixel error ε requires Ω(ε^{-2}) parameters for Gaussian splatting but only Θ(ε^{-3/s}) for MLP-Splatting, where s is the local smoothness order.

## Results

On Replica, MLP-Splatting achieves 36.25 PSNR / 0.971 SSIM (vs. 36.18 / 0.964 for Feature-3DGS) and 77.7% mIoU / 93.9% accuracy in semantic segmentation, while using only 32 MB of memory and rendering at ~8 FPS (vs. 629.7 MB and ~3 FPS for Feature-3DGS). On ScanNet, the method reaches 25.35 PSNR / 0.830 SSIM (vs. 23.32 / 0.817) and 64.3% mIoU / 89.4% accuracy (vs. 60.0% / 87.0%), with 206 MB memory and ~12 FPS (vs. 1120 MB and <1 FPS). Overall, the method achieves a ~7× memory reduction and ~5× speedup over Feature-3DGS while maintaining competitive photometric and semantic quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Feature-3DGS [43], MLP-Splatting trades a small semantic gap on Replica (77.7% vs. 78.9% mIoU; 93.9% vs. 94.3% accuracy) for a dramatic efficiency win: 32 MB vs. 629.7 MB memory (≈1/20×) and ~8 FPS vs. ~3 FPS. On ScanNet, the method actually surpasses Feature-3DGS on both semantic metrics (64.3% vs. 60.0% mIoU; 89.4% vs. 87.0% accuracy) while using 5.4× less memory (206 vs. 1120 MB) and rendering >12× faster (~12 vs. <1 FPS). For photometric quality, MLP-Splatting slightly edges out Feature-3DGS on Replica (36.25 vs. 36.18 PSNR) and ScanNet (25.35 vs. 23.32 PSNR), though LPIPS is marginally worse on both datasets. Against NeRF-DFF [16], the method dominates across all semantic metrics (77.7% vs. 63.6% mIoU; 93.9% vs. 86.4% accuracy), uses less memory (32 vs. 86.5 MB), and renders faster (~8 vs. <3 FPS). The paper also reports that a single MLP primitive has fewer parameters than ten semantic Gaussian ellipsoids, underscoring the order-of-magnitude compression advantage of the neural-primitive formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MLP-Splatting sits at the intersection of neural radiance fields (NeRF's volumetric rendering integral) and splatting-style pipelines (3DGS's tile-based rasterization and alpha compositing), but departs fundamentally from both by replacing geometric primitives (Gaussian ellipsoids) or a single global implicit field with independent compact MLPs that serve as the scene's atomic units. For semantic understanding, it follows the feature-distillation paradigm of Feature-3DGS and NeRF-DFF (distilling LSeg-CLIP embeddings into per-primitive features), but attaches semantics to the representation's native granularity rather than to low-level geometric elements. The paper also connects to the object-centric scene representation line (iLabel, Panoptic Neural Fields, Object-centric Neural Scene Rendering) yet distinguishes itself by making object-level structure emerge from the inductive bias of independent neural primitives rather than from external pose estimation, tracking, or panoptic supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The emergent object-level specialization under RGB-only supervision is the paper's most conceptually significant finding: it suggests that the right inductive bias (independent, spatially-localized neural predictors) can make semantic grouping a first-class property of the representation rather than an afterthought. The Equal-Quality Memory Law provides a principled theoretical justification for the observed 1/7× memory and 5× speed advantages over Feature-3DGS, grounding the practical gains in approximation-theoretic rates. Key open questions include the absence of a principled density control mechanism (the authors note positional-gradient densification from 3DGS does not transfer), whether the object-level structure generalizes to large-scale outdoor or dynamic scenes, and how to handle the parameter oscillation observed in appearance-complex regions.
