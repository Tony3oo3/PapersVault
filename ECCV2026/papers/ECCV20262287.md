## Problem

Existing X-ray security systems and multimodal models are fundamentally object-centric, inferring threat from the presence of individual categories. In operational settings, however, improvised explosive devices are often transported in dismantled form with spatially separated components (batteries, detonators, charges) that are individually benign but collectively hazardous through functional compatibility. No prior benchmark or model explicitly formalizes this compositional, relationally-grounded safety reasoning under heavy X-ray superposition.

## Input / Output

Falcon takes a 448×448 dual-energy X-ray image and an optional text query or referring expression as input. Beyond standard segmentation outputs (masks, bounding boxes, panoptic maps), the system produces an explicit structured safety state: a 3-dimensional component presence vector, a 3×3 pairwise functional link matrix, and a scalar scene-level risk score in [0,1]. The risk label is expert-annotated to incorporate perceptual uncertainty (e.g., a missing component may still yield high risk due to occlusion), preventing trivial derivation from presence alone. The structured state is tokenized and injected into the LLM as conditioning tokens before language decoding.

## Method

Falcon introduces a Structured Safety Adapter (SSA) as an explicit semantic bottleneck between segmentation-aware perception and LLM decoding. A DINOv2 backbone produces patch tokens, and a class-agnostic RF-DETR head extracts instance proposals with masks; each region is encoded by fusing ROIAlign features with mask-pooled features into a language-aligned embedding. The SSA maintains three learned component queries that attend to the variable-sized region set via scaled dot-product attention, yielding fixed-size component slot embeddings from which presence probabilities, pairwise functional links (via MLP on concatenated slots), and scene risk (via MLP on all slots plus links) are predicted. These seven scalar predictions are converted into safety tokens and concatenated with image, region, and text tokens before Vicuna-7B (frozen, LoRA-adapted) generates the response. Training proceeds in three stages: RF-DETR pretraining, structured multimodal alignment with frozen LLM, and LoRA instruction fine-tuning, jointly supervising language generation and structured safety prediction.

## Results

On the Falcon-X benchmark, Falcon achieves 50.45 cIoU and 69.58 mIoU on Referring Functional Grounding (RFG), surpassing the next-best fine-tuned baseline (Groma: 20.07 cIoU, 29.55 mIoU) by +30.38 and +40.03 points respectively. On standard perception tasks, Falcon also leads in Referring Segmentation (25.86 cIoU) and Referring Panoptic Segmentation (21.74 cIoU, 35.30 mIoU). For compositional safety reasoning (Table 4), Falcon achieves the lowest MAE on Functional Completeness (0.017), Scene Risk Level (0.02), and Component Link Risk (0.005), while component presence saturates at ~98% across all fine-tuned models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Falcon's most dramatic advantage is on Referring Functional Grounding (RFG), where it outperforms the strongest fine-tuned baseline Groma by +30.38 cIoU (50.45 vs. 20.07) and +40.03 mIoU (69.58 vs. 29.55), and the domain-adapted Sting-Bee by +37.73 cIoU. On standard appearance-driven tasks (RS, PS, RPS), Falcon is competitive but does not uniformly dominate: Groma fine-tuned leads on PS cIoU (36.73 vs. 14.37), and LISA fine-tuned leads on RPS cIoU (30.12 vs. 21.74), though Falcon wins on mIoU for both. For compositional safety reasoning (Table 4), Falcon achieves the best CLR MAE (0.005 vs. 0.20 for LISA fine-tuned) and best FC MAE (0.017 vs. 0.019 for Groma), but slightly trails Groma on MCI accuracy (94.75 vs. 97.1). Zero-shot models (Sa2Va, Kosmos-2, GLaMM, Llava-1.5) perform poorly across all compositional tasks, with MCI accuracy below 15% and FC MAE above 0.49, confirming that functional reasoning does not transfer from natural-image pretraining. The domain-adapted Sting-Bee, despite X-ray-specific training, still lags Falcon by large margins on RFG and CLR, indicating that domain adaptation alone is insufficient for relational safety inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the object-centric paradigm that dominates X-ray security detection (SIXray, OPIXray, HiXray, PIDray) by reframing threat assessment as a relational property of spatially dispersed components rather than an independent detection outcome. Architecturally, it builds on Groma's token aggregation strategy for visual sequence compression and on the broader MLLM lineage (LISA, GLaMM, Kosmos-2) for region-level grounding, but introduces a Structured Safety Adapter (SSA) that inserts an explicit intermediate safety state—component presence, pairwise functional links, and scene risk—between perception and LLM decoding. The closest prior work in the X-ray domain, Sting-Bee, provides image-text pairs for captioning and VQA but does not model inter-component functional compatibility or structured risk inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Falcon-X is the first benchmark to jointly evaluate dense grounding, compositional functional reasoning, and calibrated risk assessment in X-ray imagery, establishing a new evaluation paradigm that goes beyond object detection. The finding that component presence saturates near 98% across fine-tuned models while relational metrics (functional completeness, link risk) remain substantially harder suggests that the field's focus on individual object recognition is insufficient for safety-critical compositional threats. Limitations include the narrow three-component taxonomy (battery, detonator, main charge), single-view X-ray setting, and a relatively small base dataset (~7K images), leaving open whether the structured-state approach generalizes to richer component vocabularies, multi-view scans, or operational deployment with adversarial concealment.
