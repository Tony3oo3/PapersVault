## Problem

Existing VLM spatial reasoning benchmarks predominantly evaluate single-hop, elementary relations (e.g., 'Is X to the right of Y?') via standard multiple-choice questions that do not require spatial localization. This design masks a critical blind spot: models can select correct answers through linguistic shortcuts without genuinely grounding the target in the image, a failure mode that is fatal for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) deployment where precise object localization is essential. Moreover, no prior benchmark systematically evaluates multi-hop compositional spatial reasoning (chaining attribute, position, and relation constraints) across both ego-centric and exo-centric perspectives.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark sample pairs a spatially complex RGB image (curated from COCO and PACO-Ego4D) with a 1- to 3-hop compositional spatial question in four-option MCQ format, spanning ego-centric and exo-centric viewpoints. The model must produce two outputs in a fixed format: a letter choice ('Answer: (X)') and a normalized bounding box ('Bounding Box: [x1, y1, x2, y2]' on a [0,1000] scale) for the target object. The 4,500 evaluation samples are perfectly balanced (1,500 per hop level; 750 ego/exo per hop), and the 6,791-sample training set follows the same grounded VQA format.

## Method

MultihopSpatial introduces a compositional question structure where ATTRIBUTE cues narrow the candidate set, POSITION further restricts the spatially valid subset, and RELATION selects the final target—each hop constraining the next step's search space rather than acting as a parallel filter. To expose genuine spatial grounding, the paper proposes Acc@50IoU, which scores a prediction correct only if the MCQ answer matches AND the predicted bounding box achieves IoU ≥ 0.5 with ground truth, complementing standard MCQ accuracy and average IoU (computed over MCQ-correct samples only). For training, the authors post-train Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct via GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with LoRA on the LLM backbone, using a composite reward R = R_format + R_mcq + R_bbox where R_bbox is a normalized GIoU score in [0,1] providing dense localization signal alongside the discrete answer-correctness signal. All 4,500 QA pairs and bounding boxes were manually annotated by ten experts with three rounds of independent cross-checking (Krippendorff's α = 0.90), and the training corpus of 6,791 samples is designed to support RLVR post-training for spatial intelligence.

## Results

On MultihopSpatial (4,500 QA pairs), Gemini-3-Pro achieves the highest overall MCQ accuracy (64.7%) and Acc@50IoU (40.6%), while Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking leads open-weight models (46.8% Acc, 37.4% Acc@50IoU). Performance degrades sharply with hop depth: on 3-hop ego-centric queries, even GPT-5.2-Thinking drops to 36.1% MCQ and only 8.5% Acc@50IoU. RL post-training (GRPO) on MultihopSpatial-Train boosts Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct from 37.8% to 62.9% MCQ and 31.0% to 53.8% Acc@50IoU in-domain, with consistent gains on five out-of-domain spatial benchmarks. Downstream VLA evaluation shows the trained model improves CALVIN ABC→D from 3.75 to 3.98 average task completions and Libero from 35.8% to 40.0% success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-3-Pro dominates both MCQ (64.7%) and Acc@50IoU (40.6%), while GPT-5.2-Thinking achieves 57.9% MCQ but only 11.5% Acc@50IoU, and Claude-Opus-4.5-Thinking reaches 47.0% MCQ with just 4.7% Acc@50IoU—revealing that high answer-selection accuracy does not imply spatial grounding. Open-weight models (Qwen3-VL, GLM-4.6V) consistently outperform proprietary counterparts on Acc@50IoU (35–37% vs. 2–11% for GPT/Claude), with Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking (37.4%) nearly matching Gemini-3-Pro. Specialized spatial reasoning models (SenseNova, Cosmos-Reason2, VST-7B-RL) are outperformed by generalist VLMs on MCQ at every hop count (e.g., SenseNova 42.3% vs. Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking 46.8%), yet they surpass generalists on Acc@50IoU at deeper hops (e.g., Cosmos-Reason2 27.9% vs. Qwen3-VL-4B 31.0% overall, but the gap narrows to +3.4 pp at 3-hop), indicating their advantage is confined to localization rather than compositional reasoning. The RL-trained Qwen3-VL-4B (62.9% Acc, 53.8% Acc@50IoU) surpasses all 37 evaluated baselines on MultihopSpatial, exceeding even Gemini-3-Pro on Acc@50IoU by 13.2 pp. On downstream VLA tasks, the trained model outperforms the untrained Qwen3-VL-4B baseline on all five CALVIN tasks (gaps widening from +0.6% to +7.0%) and Libero (+4.2 pp), confirming transfer of spatial reasoning to embodied manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial reasoning benchmark lineage (SpatialVLM, BLINK, 3DSRBench, OmniSpatial, SpatialMQA, etc.) along two axes: from single-hop to 1–3-hop compositional queries, and from pure MCQ evaluation to a grounded metric (Acc@50IoU) that jointly requires correct answer selection and precise bounding-box localization. It departs from prior work that provides training sets alongside benchmarks (e.g., OmniSpatial, SpatialScore) by explicitly validating the training corpus's utility for downstream VLA execution via RL post-training (GRPO) and embodied manipulation benchmarks (CALVIN, Libero). The ego/exo-centric perspective split and the compositional ATT+POS+REL structure are novel design choices not present in earlier single-relation benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical evaluation blind spot: the average ungrounded ratio of 59% across 37 models means most 'correct' MCQ answers lack any spatial localization, a gap invisible to standard accuracy metrics. This has direct implications for VLA deployment, where an agent that cannot localize a target cannot act on it. Open questions include whether the 4.5K-sample benchmark is sufficiently diverse for long-tail spatial scenarios, whether the RL post-training gains generalize beyond the Qwen3-VL-4B base model and the two VLA benchmarks tested, and whether closing the reasoning–grounding gap requires architectural changes (e.g., larger vision encoders) rather than additional training data. The finding that LLM scaling alone plateaus while vision-encoder scaling drives grounding improvements points toward a concrete research direction.
