## Problem

Existing Transformer-based STVG methods rely on low-rate temporal downsampling for global semantic alignment, which physically discards the high-frequency boundary cues needed for precise temporal localization. Additionally, these methods lack explicit inter-frame dependency modeling, relying instead on implicitly encoded motion features that are insufficient for fine-grained boundary delineation. An oracle experiment on HC-STVGv1 confirms this bottleneck: replacing predicted timestamps with ground-truth ones boosts vIoU@0.5 from 42.2% to 86.9%, showing temporal ambiguity is the primary constraint.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (resized to 384×384) and a natural language query as input. The video is consumed in two passes: 64 frames uniformly sampled at a low rate for the global scan, and 8 densely sampled frames (at 2× the coarse rate) in local windows centered on the predicted start and end boundaries for refinement. The output is a spatio-temporal tube consisting of a per-frame 2D bounding box sequence and precise start/end timestamps, produced by dual DETR-style decoders in each stage.

## Method

ScanFocus decouples STVG into a coarse Global Spatio-Temporal Scan and a fine Local Boundary Focus. The coarse stage uses a unified BEiT-3 vision-language fusion encoder (pre-aligned on massive image-text pairs) and a frozen VideoMAE for motion, combined via a lightweight Deformable Semantic-Motion Fusion module (multi-scale deformable attention with linear complexity) to replace the heavy MDETR encoder, followed by dual DETR-style decoders with semantic-injected queries for coarse tubes and intervals. The refine stage performs dense boundary sampling around the coarse predictions, then applies the Semantic-Guided Temporal Aggregator (SGTA), which uses RoI Pooling guided by interpolated spatial priors to isolate object-aligned appearance, modulates motion features via Hadamard product with appearance and text features to filter background dynamics, and applies stacked temporal self-attention over the flattened local window to capture explicit inter-frame dependencies. Training is decoupled into two stages: the coarse stage is optimized with L1, GIoU, and KL-divergence losses, while the refine stage (with frozen coarse parameters) uses binary cross-entropy for framewise boundary and action classification.

## Results

On HC-STVGv1, ScanFocus achieves m_tIoU 55.5, m_vIoU 41.8, vIoU@0.3 67.5, and vIoU@0.5 42.2, surpassing the previous best method TA-STVG by 4.4% and 5.4% on the strict vIoU@0.3 and vIoU@0.5 metrics respectively. On HC-STVGv2, it reaches m_tIoU 62.4 and vIoU@0.5 39.3, improving over TA-STVG by 2.0% and 2.6%. On VidSTG, ScanFocus leads all baselines on both declarative (vIoU@0.5: 36.0 vs. 33.5) and interrogative (vIoU@0.5: 29.4 vs. 28.0) sentence types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScanFocus outperforms all 16 compared baselines across all three benchmarks and all four metrics. The most direct competitor, TA-STVG [20] (the previous SOTA), is beaten by 4.4% on vIoU@0.3 and 5.4% on vIoU@0.5 on HC-STVGv1, and by 2.0% on m_tIoU and 2.6% on vIoU@0.5 on HC-STVGv2. Among Transformer-based one-stage methods, CG-STVG [19] and STVGFormer [33] trail ScanFocus by 2.7–5.4% on vIoU@0.5 depending on the dataset. The MLLM-based approaches (SpaceVLLM-7B [45] and ASTG [60]) lag substantially behind, with SpaceVLLM-7B scoring only 26.2 on vIoU@0.5 (declarative) versus ScanFocus's 36.0 on VidSTG, indicating that current LLM-based grounding has not yet closed the gap with dedicated spatio-temporal architectures. On VidSTG, ScanFocus also leads the strongest traditional baseline TA-STVG by 2.5% on declarative vIoU@0.5 and 1.4% on interrogative vIoU@0.5, confirming consistent gains across linguistic diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScanFocus departs from the dominant MDETR-based STVG paradigm (TubeDETR, STCAT, CG-STVG, TA-STVG), where a single heavy Transformer encoder simultaneously fuses appearance, motion, and language features. Instead, it decouples this into a unified vision-language encoder (BEiT-3) for pre-aligned semantic features and a lightweight Deformable Semantic-Motion Fusion module (adapted from Deformable DETR) for efficient cross-modal interaction. The coarse-to-fine structure—sparse global scan followed by dense boundary refinement with the Semantic-Guided Temporal Aggregator—repurposes the well-known coarse-to-fine principle from object detection and temporal grounding into the spatio-temporal tube prediction setting, where it had not been explicitly applied to recover high-frequency boundary cues lost during downsampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle experiment (vIoU@0.5 jumping from 42.2% to 86.9% with GT timestamps) provides a clear, quantitative diagnosis that temporal boundary localization—not spatial grounding—is the primary bottleneck in current STVG, a finding that should guide future research priorities. The 50% GFLOPs reduction from deformable fusion while improving strict-metric performance offers a practical efficiency-accuracy tradeoff for deploying STVG in real-time or resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the two-stage decoupled training is strictly necessary versus end-to-end optimization, how the approach scales to significantly longer videos or denser boundary windows, and whether the SGTA's explicit inter-frame modeling can be generalized to other spatio-temporal tasks beyond grounding.
