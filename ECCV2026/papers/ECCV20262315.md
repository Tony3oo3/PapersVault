## Problem

Prior sparse-view novel view synthesis methods rely on image diffusion backbones that lack inherent multi-view knowledge and require large pose-annotated multi-view datasets (e.g., 80K+ scenes) to induce 3D awareness. Video diffusion models naturally encode cross-view consistency and viewpoint transitions from web-scale pretraining, but their temporal ordering assumptions make them ill-suited for unordered sparse-view inputs. This paper asks whether video diffusion models can be repurposed for sparse-view NVS with minimal architectural changes and far less multi-view supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262315/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K≈5 posed RGB images (treated as an unordered set) with known SE(3) camera extrinsics and intrinsics, plus a target camera pose, and produces a single photorealistic novel view image at that pose. Input views are encoded independently through a frozen video VAE (no temporal compression), and camera geometry is injected as per-pixel Plücker ray maps downsampled via pixel unshuffle to match latent resolution. The model is trained at 192×336 and 480×832 resolutions, and only the target frame's latent is decoded and supervised during both training and inference.

## Method

FrameCrafter adapts a pretrained video diffusion transformer (Wan2.1-I2V-14B or CogVideoX-5B) into a permutation-invariant multi-view generator through three key modifications: (1) per-view independent VAE encoding, where each input image is encoded as a standalone single-frame video to eliminate temporal compression and causal ordering bias; (2) query-centered Plücker ray map conditioning, where camera poses are defined relative to the target view (not the first input) and concatenated channel-wise with the noisy latent; and (3) removal of the temporal component of 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings so the transformer cannot rely on frame indices. Training uses LoRA (rank 32) on attention and feed-forward layers plus a re-initialized patch embedding, updating only ~1% of parameters on just 1K scenes from DL3DV-10K with a flow-matching loss masked to the target frame only. This lightweight specialization preserves the video model's geometric priors while enforcing permutation invariance, achieving state-of-the-art sparse-view NVS without large curated multi-view datasets.

## Results

On the 6-view NVS setting, FrameCrafter with the Wan2.1-14B backbone (trained on only 1K DL3DV scenes) achieves 17.18 PSNR, 0.223 LPIPS, and 0.066 DreamSim on DL3DV-Benchmark, and 15.64 PSNR, 0.365 LPIPS, and 0.111 DreamSim on Mip-NeRF 360. With the smaller CogVideoX-5B backbone, it still reaches 13.65 PSNR and 0.113 DreamSim on DL3DV. The method outperforms the prior SOTA image-diffusion method SEVA (trained on 80K scenes) on PSNR, LPIPS, and DreamSim on DL3DV, while using 80× less training data. A clear backbone scaling trend is observed: upgrading from CogVideoX-5B to Wan2.1-14B improves PSNR by ~3.5 dB on DL3DV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SEVA (the prior SOTA image-diffusion method trained on 80K scenes), FrameCrafter with Wan2.1-14B wins on PSNR (17.18 vs 16.15), LPIPS (0.223 vs 0.253), and DreamSim (0.066 vs 0.088) on DL3DV, and on PSNR (15.64 vs 14.59) and DreamSim (0.111 vs 0.137) on Mip-NeRF 360, though SEVA retains a slight SSIM edge on Mip-NeRF 360 (0.294 vs 0.279). Against Aether (same CogVideoX-5B backbone), FrameCrafter improves PSNR by ~1 dB and DreamSim by ~0.03 on DL3DV, attributed to per-view independent encoding and unordered-set formulation. Against EscherNet (image diffusion, 10K scenes), even the smaller CogVideoX-5B variant of FrameCrafter outperforms it on all four metrics on both benchmarks. Against the regression baselines LVSM and E-RayZer, FrameCrafter achieves comparable or higher PSNR/SSIM but substantially better LPIPS and DreamSim, indicating sharper and more perceptually realistic outputs. The consistent improvement from CogVideoX-5B to Wan2.1-14B (~3.5 dB PSNR gain) demonstrates a clear backbone scaling trend absent in the image-diffusion baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of adapting image diffusion models (SEVA, EscherNet) for sparse-view NVS by instead repurposing pretrained video diffusion backbones (Wan2.1, CogVideoX) as multi-view generators. It also distinguishes itself from prior camera-conditioned video diffusion work (Aether, Gen3C) that treats generation as a temporally ordered sequence; FrameCrafter removes temporal compression, eliminates temporal RoPE, and encodes each view independently to achieve permutation invariance over an unordered input set. The method builds directly on the latent diffusion + DiT architecture of modern video models, adding only Plücker ray conditioning, per-view VAE encoding, and lightweight LoRA fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that video diffusion models already encode strong multi-view geometric priors from web-scale pretraining, requiring only ~1K scenes and ~1% trainable parameters to achieve competitive sparse-view NVS—challenges the assumption that large curated multi-view datasets are necessary. This opens a scalable pathway where advances in video foundation models transfer directly to NVS without additional multi-view supervision. Open questions include whether the permutation-invariant formulation extends to dynamic/4D scenes, whether the approach generalizes to object-level synthesis, and whether the surprising ease of 'unlearning' temporal structure reveals something fundamental about what video models actually represent.
