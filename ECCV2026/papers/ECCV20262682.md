## Problem

Existing UAV vision-language navigation methods suffer from a 'double crutches' dependency: they inject dense ground-truth directional hints into prompts (degrading the agent into a passive instruction follower) and rely on external object detectors like Grounding DINO to trigger landing. This creates a disjointed perception-control loop that limits genuine autonomy and robustness in open-world scenarios where localization is imperfect and detectors may fail.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a vertically concatenated 224×224 composite of front and down camera views (aligned to the ViT's 14×14 patch grid to prevent intra-patch corruption at the seam), a coarse 4-bucket fuzzy directional hint derived from onboard IMU relative bearing, and a free-text target description. It autoregressively outputs three integer tokens (each in {0,…,98}) that dequantize to continuous 3-DoF spatial offsets—forward displacement Δx∈[0,5] m, vertical Δz∈[−5,5] m, and yaw Δψ∈[−1.1,1.1] rad—plus an intrinsic landing signal triggered by either a LAND text token or a near-zero displacement prediction, eliminating the need for any external detector.

## Method

AeroVLA builds on the OpenVLA-7B backbone (Llama-2 with a hybrid SigLIP+DINOv2 ViT encoder) and introduces three key mechanisms: (1) a minimalist dual-view perception interface that discards redundant side/rear cameras while preserving forward navigation and ground-alignment cues; (2) fuzzy directional prompting that replaces dense oracle guidance with coarse semantic buckets (e.g., 'forward-right', 'to your left rear') from IMU, forcing active visual grounding; and (3) numerical tokenization that maps 99-bin discretized actions directly to existing LLM digit tokens rather than novel special tokens, leveraging pre-trained magnitude awareness to avoid a cold-start problem. Training is behavior cloning over 420k frames from 7,922 expert trajectories using LoRA (r=64) on the language backbone with frozen visual encoders and a fully fine-tuned projector. A geometry-consistent filtering step removes ~4% of ambiguous training frames where a lateral target bearing conflicts with a straight-flight label unless a nearby obstacle (depth ≤ 20 m) justifies the maneuver, ensuring the policy learns valid evasion rather than contradictory supervision.

## Results

On the TravelUAV Seen test set, AeroVLA achieves a state-of-the-art 47.96% Success Rate (SR) and 38.54% SPL, surpassing the strongest baseline LongFly by +11.57% SR and +7.47% SPL. On the Unseen Map split, the advantage is most dramatic: AeroVLA reaches 37.58% SR and 28.22% SPL, approximately three times the 11.27% SR and 9.32% SPL of LongFly. The method also demonstrates superior OSR-to-SR conversion (57.69% → 47.96%) compared to LongFly (65.87% → 36.39%), indicating that the intrinsic landing mechanism effectively closes the gap between reaching the target vicinity and actually stopping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AeroVLA outperforms all four baselines on SR and SPL across every test split (Seen, Unseen Object, Unseen Map). Against the strongest baseline LongFly [14], AeroVLA gains +11.57% SR on Seen, +12.73% SR on Unseen Object, and +26.31% SR on Unseen Map, with the gap widening substantially as environments become more novel. LongFly achieves a lower NE (60.02 vs. 65.88 m) and higher OSR (65.87% vs. 57.69%) on the Seen split, but this is attributed to its reliance on dense oracle directional hints; its SR drops sharply from OSR, exposing a failure in the terminal landing phase that AeroVLA's intrinsic stopping mechanism avoids. TravelUAV [36] and NavFoM [41] trail significantly (17.45% and 29.17% SR on Seen), with TravelUAV additionally depending on an external Grounding DINO detector for landing triggers. The CMA [1] baseline, a traditional RNN approach, achieves only 8.37% SR, confirming the difficulty of the task without modern foundation-model backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

AeroVLA builds directly on the OpenVLA-7B backbone (Kim et al., CoRL 2025), adapting its hybrid SigLIP+DINOv2 visual encoder and Llama-2 language model for aerial navigation via LoRA fine-tuning. It deliberately departs from the dominant modular UAV-VLN paradigm (TravelUAV, NavFoM, LongFly) that couples a language model with hierarchical decoders, dense oracle directional hints, and external object detectors like Grounding DINO for landing. The key conceptual shift is replacing these 'double crutches' with fuzzy IMU-derived directional prompts and an intrinsic landing signal, while the numerical tokenization strategy (mapping 99-bin actions to existing LLM digit tokens) extends the RT-2 action-tokenization idea by avoiding the cold-start problem of training novel action embeddings from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that stripping away auxiliary modules and dense supervision can paradoxically improve generalization—achieving ~3× the success rate of the strongest baseline on unseen maps—suggesting that minimalist reactive policies capture more transferable visual-motor representations than memory-heavy modular pipelines. However, absolute performance remains modest (37.58% SR on unseen maps), evaluation is confined to a single simulation benchmark (TravelUAV/AirSim), and the reactive design without explicit memory limits global backtracking in repetitive urban layouts. Open questions include whether the sim-to-real gap (motion blur, sensor noise) erodes these gains, whether reinforcement learning fine-tuning can push beyond the conservatism of behavior cloning, and whether the dual-view minimalist perception generalizes to more complex multi-object or multi-agent aerial scenarios.
