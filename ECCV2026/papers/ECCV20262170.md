## Problem

Existing 2D/3D Gaussian Splatting methods degrade significantly on indoor scenes due to large textureless surfaces, complex occlusions, and intricate geometry, producing noisy and incomplete reconstructions. Three root causes are identified: SfM-based initialization fails in textureless regions, default densification and global opacity resets are too indiscriminate, and photometric loss alone cannot disambiguate geometry from appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262170/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (typically ~200 per scene) along with monocular depth maps from DepthPro and monocular normal maps from StableNormal, both generated offline. MASt3R-SfM provides the initial point cloud and camera poses. The output is a set of optimized 2D Gaussian Splatting primitives whose depth maps are fused via TSDF and extracted into a 3D surface mesh using Marching Cubes. Training runs for 30k iterations on a single RTX 3090 (~40 min, ~11 GB VRAM), with geometric prior losses activated from iteration 7k onward.

## Method

IndoorSplat extends 2DGS with three key components. First, a dense initialization strategy uses MASt3R-SfM for the base point cloud, then identifies sparse image patches by projecting points and counting per-patch occupancy; sparse patches are filled by locally aligning monocular depth (solving a least-squares scale-shift) and back-projecting new 3D points, with PCA-derived normals initializing Gaussian orientations. Second, an iterative adaptive densification (ADC) strategy tracks 'active' Gaussians (newly split or cloned) and applies a more aggressive 4-way proliferation policy to them, exploiting temporal locality to concentrate capacity in geometrically intricate regions; a soft L1 opacity regularization replaces the disruptive global opacity reset for non-destructive pruning. Third, geometric supervision is added via a PCC-based depth loss (invariant to scale/shift) with a gradient smoothness term, plus a cosine-similarity normal loss, all weighted into the standard 2DGS photometric objective.

## Results

IndoorSplat achieves state-of-the-art surface reconstruction on both ScanNet and ScanNet++, attaining the best F1 score of 0.7587 (ScanNet) and 0.8967 (ScanNet++), along with the highest Precision (0.7844 / 0.8735) and Recall (0.7352 / 0.9216). On ScanNet, it outperforms the strongest baseline (2DGS+MASt3R, F1=0.6368) by 12.2 percentage points in F1. On ScanNet++, it surpasses the best RGBD-based method (DN-Splatter, F1=0.8357) by 6.1 points in F1 while also achieving the lowest Accuracy (0.0319) and Completion (0.0227) errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IndoorSplat outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets. On ScanNet, the largest gap is against 2DGS+MASt3R (F1 0.6368 vs. 0.7587, +12.2 pts), while the closest competitor is PGSR+MASt3R (F1 0.6140). On ScanNet++, the two RGBD-based indoor-specific methods DN-Splatter (F1 0.8357) and AGS-Mesh (F1 0.8346) are the strongest baselines, yet IndoorSplat still leads by 6.1 and 6.2 F1 points respectively, despite not requiring any depth sensor input. Replacing COLMAP initialization with MASt3R consistently boosts all general-purpose baselines (e.g., 2DGS F1 rises from 0.4514 to 0.6368 on ScanNet), confirming that initialization quality is a dominant factor, but IndoorSplat's additional dense initialization, adaptive densification, and geometric priors close the remaining gap. DN-Splatter and AGS-Mesh report no ScanNet results, likely due to insufficient depth quality in that dataset, whereas IndoorSplat handles both datasets uniformly without depth input.

## Relation to Prior Work

IndoorSplat extends 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) for indoor surface reconstruction, departing from prior Gaussian Splatting-based methods (3DGS, GOF, SuGaR, PGSR) that rely on sparse COLMAP point clouds for initialization. Its key conceptual departure is treating initialization quality as the primary bottleneck: rather than improving the optimization loop alone, it combines a feed-forward SfM model (MASt3R-SfM) with monocular depth-guided local densification to produce a geometrically complete starting point. This positions it against indoor-specific methods (DN-Splatter, IndoorGS, GaussianRoom, AGS-Mesh) that also incorporate geometric priors but still inherit the sparse-SfM initialization weakness. The iterative adaptive densification and soft L1 opacity regularization are incremental refinements over the standard ADC and hard-reset pruning inherited from 3DGS/2DGS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ablation reveals that the MASt3R-SfM initialization swap is by far the largest single contributor to performance, while the paper's headline adaptive densification strategy yields only marginal metric gains—suggesting that for indoor scenes, getting the starting geometry right matters more than cleverly refining it during training. This reframing is practically useful: it directs future effort toward better structural priors rather than more complex optimization schedules. The method's limitation (degradation under motion-blurred monocular depth) and the acknowledged potential of semantic cues point to open questions about robustness in dynamic or partially observed environments. With indoor reconstruction being critical for robotics, VR, and digital heritage, a reliable pipeline that avoids the catastrophic failures of RGBD-dependent methods on noisy depth is a meaningful practical advance.
