## Problem

Existing text-to-video retrieval methods, including the recent diffusion-inspired DITS, treat cross-modal alignment as deterministic point-to-point matching, collapsing the inherent one-to-many ambiguity of multimodal data into single embedding vectors. This ignores modality-specific uncertainty—where a single query can validly describe diverse scenes and a single video can correspond to multiple captions—limiting representational flexibility and ranking coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261956/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text query and a corpus of video clips (12 uniformly sampled frames per clip, resized to 224×224), encoded by a frozen or fine-tuned CLIP ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16 backbone. It produces a ranked video list for the query, where ranking scores are negative directional KL divergences between the bridged text distribution and each candidate video distribution. Additionally, the bridge-induced KL margin between the top-1 and top-2 candidates serves as a calibrated confidence signal for selective prediction, enabling the system to flag low-confidence queries without post-processing.

## Method

DAB encodes both text and video embeddings as diagonal Gaussian distributions N(μ, diag(σ²)) via a shared probabilistic encoder with separate mean and log-variance heads; video variance is further refined by a token-based uncertainty branch that applies self-attention over frame tokens to capture spatio-temporal variability. The core Distribution Bridge then deterministically transforms the text distribution toward the target video distribution over T′=32 truncated refinement steps: at each step, a drift MLP (conditioned on sinusoidal time embeddings and both distributions) predicts offsets (Δμ, Δlogσ²), which are scaled by a precomputed step-size coefficient χ_t ∈ [0.2, 0.6] and subtracted from the current parameters—no stochastic noise is injected. Training uses a bidirectional Probabilistic Alignment Contrastive (PAC) loss over directional KL divergence costs, where the asymmetric D_KL(Q_v ‖ P̂_t) measures how well the refined text distribution approximates the target video distribution, and the bidirectional objective ranks both videos-for-text and texts-for-video within the batch. The drift heads are zero-initialized and log-variance outputs are clipped to [−6, 2], ensuring the refinement trajectory remains bounded and stable throughout training.

## Results

On MSR-VTT (ViT-B/32), DAB achieves R@1=56.2, R@5=85.4, R@10=92.4, and MnR=4.1, surpassing the strongest prior method DITS (R@1=51.9, MnR=11.6) by +4.3 in R@1 and reducing MnR by 64%. On MSVD, DAB reaches R@1=54.5, R@5=87.8, R@10=93.9 with MnR=3.4. On VATEX with ViT-B/16, DAB attains the best overall result with R@1=70.7, R@5=95.7, R@10=98.3, and MnR=1.9, outperforming NarVid (68.4) and Video-ColBERT (66.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAB outperforms all 12 compared baselines across MSR-VTT, MSVD, and VATEX. Against the closest diffusion-inspired competitor DITS (NeurIPS'24), DAB gains +4.3 R@1 on MSR-VTT (56.2 vs 51.9) and reduces MnR from 11.6 to 4.1, a 64% relative improvement indicating a globally more coherent embedding space. Versus probabilistic methods, DAB surpasses UATVR (ICCV'23) by +8.7 R@1 and DiffusionRet (ICCV'23) by +7.2 R@1 on MSR-VTT, while also achieving substantially lower MnR (4.1 vs 12.9 and 12.1 respectively). Against recent CVPR'25 methods, DAB leads NarVid by +5.4 R@1 and Video-ColBERT by +8.1 R@1 on MSR-VTT, and on VATEX (ViT-B/16) DAB (70.7) exceeds NarVid (68.4) and Video-ColBERT (66.8). The largest absolute gap is over CLIP4Clip (+11.7 R@1 on MSR-VTT), confirming that distribution-level refinement with KL-based contrastive loss provides substantial gains over standard CLIP-based projection. Notably, DAB's MnR advantage (4.1 vs 10–15 for most baselines) is consistent across all three datasets, suggesting the benefit extends beyond top-1 to the full ranking distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAB extends the diffusion-inspired truncated refinement strategy of DITS [28] from deterministic point-wise vector alignment to distribution-to-distribution transformation, where both text and video are modeled as Gaussians (μ, log σ²) and a drift network iteratively transports the text distribution toward the video target. It also builds on X-Pool's [7] text-conditioned cross-attention frame pooling as its foundational feature extractor. Conceptually, DAB departs from probabilistic retrieval methods like UATVR [6] and T-MASS [27], which rely on stochastic sampling of prototypes to compute similarity, by introducing a fully deterministic, sampling-free bridge that refines both mean and variance simultaneously under a directional KL-divergence contrastive objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 64% relative reduction in Mean Rank (11.6 → 4.1 on MSR-VTT) far exceeds the R@1 gain, indicating that distributional alignment reorganizes the entire ranking space rather than merely fixing top-1 matches — a property that could benefit downstream applications requiring reliable mid-rank ordering. The bridge-induced KL margin provides a calibrated confidence signal (85.6% R@1 at 25% selective coverage vs. 56.2% random), opening a path toward abstention-aware retrieval. Open questions include whether the Gaussian assumption suffices for highly ambiguous or compositional queries, whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H) or longer videos, and whether the deterministic bridge can be extended to non-diagonal covariance structures to capture inter-dimensional uncertainty correlations.
