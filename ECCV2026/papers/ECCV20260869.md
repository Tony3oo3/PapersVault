## Problem

Existing temporal grounding approaches target only explicit temporal relations (e.g., localizing a visual event in time) and largely neglect the audio modality, overlooking implicit cross-modal temporal relations such as identifying what is visually present when a character speaks or what is said when a visual event occurs. These cross-modal temporal dependencies are prevalent in real-world scenarios but remain unaddressed by prior work, which typically processes audio and video in isolation without modeling their temporal synchronization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 64 uniformly sampled video frames, a full audio track (split into speech via Whisper-Large-V3 and sound/music via BEATs, concatenated and downsampled 10x), and a text prompt specifying the subtask. Outputs are subtask-dependent: for moment retrieval (V2T, A2T) the model produces a time interval in the explicit format 'second{start}-second{end}'; for captioning subtasks (T2V, T2A, V2A, A2V) it generates a natural-language description of the corresponding video or audio segment. The key structural constraint is that all three modalities are tokenized at matching time units, so the LLM receives a single interleaved sequence rather than separate modality blocks.

## Method

ChronusOmni introduces Temporal Interleaved Tokenization (TIT), which encodes absolute timestamps as text tokens (e.g., 'second[2.0]') and interleaves them with visual and audio tokens at each time step, producing a unified sequence [T1, V1, A1, T2, V2, A2, ...] that explicitly aligns all three modalities along the timeline. Training follows a two-stage progressive refinement: first, supervised fine-tuning on a dense video-and-audio captioning task teaches the model to localize events and produce modality-separated captions; second, GRPO reinforcement learning with task-specific rewards (IoU for interval prediction, METEOR for caption quality, plus a binary format reward) enforces precise boundary localization and correct temporal ordering without the discretization and exposure-bias limitations of SFT. The architecture builds on the Ola omni-modal LLM (Qwen-2.5-7B backbone, OryxViT vision encoder, Whisper-Large-V3 + BEATs audio encoders), with only the LLM backbone fine-tuned while all encoders and projection adapters remain frozen.

## Results

On the newly introduced ChronusAV benchmark, ChronusOmni achieves 63.15% R@0.5 on Video-to-Time and 90.50% R@0.5 on Audio-to-Time, outperforming the second-best fine-tuned baselines (ARC-Hunyuan-Video-FT at 55.25% and 77.85% respectively) by an average of 17.3% across all six subtasks. On LongVALE (zero-shot), it reaches 34.5% mIoU on Omni-TVG, surpassing the second-best zero-shot model by 28.8% on average and even exceeding LongVALE-LLM (trained on LongVALE) in most metrics. On visual-only benchmarks, ChronusOmni achieves 85.1/75.0/54.2 (R@0.3/0.5/0.7) on Charades-STA and 22.1 R@0.7 on ActivityNet (zero-shot), setting new state-of-the-art results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChronusOmni decisively outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every subtask on ChronusAV. The strongest zero-shot baselines—Qwen3-Omni (30B) and ARC-Hunyuan-Video—score only 46.70% and 36.85% R@0.5 on A2T, while ChronusOmni reaches 90.50%, a gap of over 40 points. Even after fine-tuning Qwen2.5-Omni and ARC-Hunyuan-Video on the ChronusAV training data (which substantially boosts their scores, validating dataset quality), ChronusOmni still leads by 17.3% on average. On LongVALE in a zero-shot setting, ChronusOmni surpasses all zero-shot competitors by 28.8% on average and notably exceeds LongVALE-LLM (which was explicitly trained on LongVALE) in most metrics, demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization. On visual-only Charades-STA, ChronusOmni (1-epoch GRPO) beats Time-R1 by +2.2/+2.8/+4.0 at R@0.3/0.5/0.7, and on ActivityNet (zero-shot) it achieves the best R@0.7 (22.1 vs. Time-R1's 21.4) while ranking second at R@0.3 and R@0.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Ola omni-modal LLM backbone and extends the temporal grounding literature from explicit, single-modality formulations (video-to-time, time-to-video) to a unified audiovisual setting that includes implicit cross-modal grounding (video-to-audio, audio-to-video). It departs from prior approaches that use learnable temporal positional embeddings (Qwen2.5-Omni, TriSense) or watermark-based time encoding (ARC-Hunyuan-Video) by proposing Temporal Interleaved Tokenization (TIT), which encodes time as interpretable text tokens interleaved with visual and audio tokens at each time step. The progressive training strategy—SFT followed by GRPO with task-specific rewards—follows the broader RL-for-LLM paradigm but applies it specifically to temporal boundary localization, where SFT's categorical treatment of continuous time is known to be limiting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of six audiovisual temporal grounding subtasks and the release of ChronusAV (47K videos, 677K segments with modality-separated captions and precise timestamps) provide the field with a much-needed benchmark that prior datasets lacked in both audio completeness and cross-modal alignment. The substantial margins over strong baselines (17.3% on ChronusAV, 28.8% on LongVALE) and the zero-shot transfer where a 7B model surpasses a 30B model on temporal grounding suggest that architectural design for temporal representation matters more than raw scale for this task. Open questions include whether TIT scales to hour-long videos, whether the approach generalizes to streaming/interactive settings, and whether the GRPO reward design can be extended to more complex multi-event temporal reasoning.
