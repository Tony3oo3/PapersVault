## Problem

MLLM vision encoders, trained via contrastive pretraining and text-centric instruction tuning, achieve strong VQA performance but exhibit systematic deficiencies in dense feature representations, as evidenced by poor linear-probing results on semantic segmentation and depth estimation. The paper asks whether these encoders can be rebalanced into genuinely versatile vision foundation models that handle both language-mediated reasoning and pixel-level perception without full-scale retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260614/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The shared vision encoder (Qwen2-VL-ViT, H/14) accepts RGB images at dynamic resolutions and extracts multi-layer patch features. Three task heads consume these features differently: the VQA/captioning head projects final-layer features into an LLM (Qwen3-1.7B) for autoregressive text generation; the depth head (DPT) fuses three uniformly sampled intermediate layers to produce an H×W depth map supervised by pseudo-labels from Depth Anything V2; the segmentation head (SAM-style mask decoder) takes final-layer features plus a text prompt embedding (from Qwen3-Embedding-8B) to output per-pixel binary masks. The backbone itself outputs transferable visual features evaluated via frozen linear probing.

## Method

VersaViT post-trains an MLLM vision backbone with a multi-task collaborative framework that attaches lightweight, task-specific heads to a shared encoder, jointly optimizing three complementary supervision signals: VQA/captioning (autoregressive cross-entropy via an LLM), monocular depth estimation (scale-invariant + multi-scale gradient-matching loss with pseudo-labels), and referring segmentation (BCE + DICE loss with SAM-style decoding). Training proceeds in two stages: a captioning alignment warm-up that trains only the vision projector with frozen backbone and LLM, followed by full multi-task collaborative training where all parameters are unfrozen and batches alternate across the three tasks with gradient accumulation. The key design insight is that task-specific heads absorb inter-task gradient conflicts while the shared backbone receives multi-granularity supervision (high-level semantics, mid-level 3D geometry, pixel-level localization), producing a representation that is simultaneously instruction-competent and dense-prediction-friendly. The framework is modular and drop-in compatible with standard MLLM training pipelines, requiring no architectural changes to the backbone.

## Results

On the OpenCompass VQA benchmark (8 tasks), VersaViT achieves an average score of 66.4, a +1.6 gain over the Qwen2-VL-ViT baseline (64.8). On dense prediction via linear probing, VersaViT delivers dramatic improvements: mIoU rises from 33.6 to 49.6 on ADE20k (+16.0), from 67.5 to 86.6 on Pascal VOC (+19.1), and from 57.6 to 74.5 on Cityscapes (+16.9); depth RMSE drops from 3.735 to 3.136 on KITTI (−0.599) and from 0.541 to 0.473 on NYUv2 (−0.068). These dense-feature gains surpass SigLIP2 across all five benchmarks, though DINOv3 remains stronger (e.g., 55.9 vs. 49.6 mIoU on ADE20k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VersaViT substantially outperforms the Qwen2-VL-ViT baseline on every dense-prediction metric (e.g., +16.0 mIoU on ADE20k, −0.599 RMSE on KITTI) while also improving VQA by +1.6 average points on OpenCompass. Against SigLIP2, VersaViT wins on all five linear-probing benchmarks (e.g., 49.6 vs. 42.7 mIoU on ADE20k; 3.136 vs. 3.273 RMSE on KITTI), confirming that multi-task post-training of an MLLM encoder can match or exceed dedicated weakly-supervised vision encoders on dense tasks. However, DINOv3 remains the strongest self-supervised baseline, leading VersaViT by 6.3 mIoU on ADE20k and 0.79 RMSE on KITTI. On VQA, VersaViT (66.4 avg.) beats MiniCPM-V-2.6 (65.2) and ties DeepSeek-VL2 (66.4), but trails the full Qwen2.5-VL-7B model (70.9) due to the latter's larger training data scale. For referring segmentation after fine-tuning on RefCOCO, VersaViT achieves 78.8/81.2/76.2 (val/testA/testB), surpassing all specialist baselines including LISA-7B (74.1/76.5/71.1) and VideoLISA-3.8B (73.8/76.6/68.8). On zero-shot depth estimation, VersaViT (9.4 AbsRel on KITTI) is competitive with specialist models like DPT (10.0) and Marigold (9.9), and after depth-specific fine-tuning reaches 7.1 AbsRel, approaching Depth Anything V2 (7.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Qwen2-VL-ViT vision encoder and positions its contribution within the broader vision foundation model landscape, which spans self-supervised methods (DINO, MAE) and weakly-supervised contrastive/autoregressive methods (CLIP, SigLIP, CapPa). It departs from recent encoder-enhancement approaches that rely on teacher distillation (DUNE, UNIC), decoupled backbones (Perception Encoder), or RL-based optimization, instead proposing a multi-task collaborative post-training paradigm with lightweight task-specific heads. The method draws on multi-task learning principles from NLP (sequence-to-sequence unification) and applies them to the vision backbone, using complementary supervision from VQA/captioning, depth estimation, and referring segmentation to reshape shared representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, modular recipe for converting MLLM vision encoders into more versatile backbones without architectural changes or full retraining, which is directly applicable to existing MLLM training pipelines. The empirical finding that VQA-only instruction tuning actively degrades dense feature quality (Table 4) offers a useful diagnostic for the field, suggesting that current MLLM training curricula have a representational blind spot. Open questions include whether multi-granularity supervision should be integrated earlier in the MLLM training pipeline (pre-training rather than post-training), whether the approach scales to larger encoder/LLM combinations, and whether the gains persist when the backbone is further fine-tuned for downstream tasks.
