## Problem

Existing text-driven referring video object segmentation methods are pretrained on static 2D image datasets with naive segmentation losses, which overlooks cross-frame geometric consistency and 3D structural relationships. This results in weak spatial understanding, causing unstable tracking and inconsistent segmentation under viewpoint changes and complex camera motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261090/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a video clip (sequence of RGB frames) and a natural-language referring sentence, and outputs a temporally coherent sequence of per-frame binary segmentation masks. During training, the input is augmented: single COCO images are expanded into 5-frame geometry-consistent synthetic videos via monocular depth estimation and virtual camera re-projection, and real video sequences are paired with dual-teacher features (3D geometry from π³ and semantics from DINOv3) for distillation supervision.

## Method

GeoLaV is a two-stage framework built on the SAMWISE/SAM2 architecture with a trainable cross-modal adapter at each encoder level. Stage I (Monocular Geometry Pretraining) synthesizes novel views from single images using feed-forward 3D geometry models (π³, VGGT) to estimate depth, lift pixels into 3D, and re-project under continuous camera trajectories, forming geometry-consistent pseudo-videos; a frozen VFM encoder (DINOv3) then supervises the memory-attention features via cosine similarity loss, teaching cross-view structural consistency without real video annotations. Stage II (Geometry-Aware Distillation) fine-tunes on real video data with dual-teacher distillation: both a 3D-aware encoder (π³) and the VFM encoder (DINOv3) provide feature-level supervision through separate projection heads and cosine similarity losses, injecting depth-aware structural priors and semantic cues simultaneously. The key insight is that depth-based 3D warping for view synthesis yields substantially larger gains than planar homography augmentation, and combining geometry-consistent pretraining with 3D distillation produces synergistic improvements in spatiotemporal coherence and language grounding.

## Results

GeoLaV (202M params) achieves state-of-the-art J&F scores of 70.5 on Ref-YouTube-VOS, 72.5 on Ref-DAVIS17, and 50.0 on MeViS, surpassing the previous best non-large VLM methods (SAMWISE 202M: 69.2/70.6/49.5) by 1.3, 1.9, and 0.5 points respectively. In zero-shot evaluation (trained only on RefCOCO/+/g images, tested on video), GeoLaV reaches 47.0 J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS and 31.6 on MeViS, improving over SAMWISE's zero-shot scores by +15.1 and +5.2 points. The ablation shows MGP alone lifts J&F from 65.1 to 67.2, GAD alone to 67.5, and the full two-stage pipeline to 70.5 on Ref-YouTube-VOS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among non-large VLM methods, GeoLaV (202M) outperforms all competitors on all three benchmarks: it beats SAMWISE (202M, the base architecture) by +1.3 J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS (70.5 vs 69.2), +1.9 on Ref-DAVIS17 (72.5 vs 70.6), and +0.5 on MeViS (50.0 vs 49.5). Against ReferDINO (230M), GeoLaV gains +1.2 on Ref-YouTube-VOS, +3.6 on Ref-DAVIS17, and +0.7 on MeViS. Compared to MUTR (250M), the improvement is +3.0 on Ref-YouTube-VOS and +6.1 on Ref-DAVIS17. Against the large VLM GLUS^A (7B), GeoLaV wins on Ref-YouTube-VOS (70.5 vs 67.3) but trails by 1.3 J&F on MeViS (50.0 vs 51.3), indicating that very large language models still hold an edge on motion-centric expressions. In the zero-shot setting (image-only pretraining, no video fine-tuning), GeoLaV achieves 47.0 J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS versus SAMWISE's 31.9, a +15.1 gap that validates the geometry-consistent novel-view synthesis as a strong video-domain bridge.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoLaV builds directly on the SAMWISE architecture (CVPR 2025), which itself extends SAM2 with temporal adapters for text-driven video segmentation. The key departure is a two-stage training paradigm that injects 3D geometric priors: Stage I synthesizes geometry-consistent novel views from single images (inspired by L2M's lifting approach) and distills from a VFM encoder (DINOv3), while Stage II fine-tunes on real video with dual-teacher distillation from a 3D-aware model (π³) and the VFM. This contrasts with prior RVOS methods (MTTR, SOC, DsHmp, SAMWISE) that rely on naive 2D segmentation losses and implicit temporal modeling via memory banks, without explicit cross-view geometric consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that geometry-consistent data expansion from images alone yields substantial zero-shot video generalization (+15.1 J&F over SAMWISE on Ref-Youtube-VOS), suggesting that the bottleneck in image-to-video transfer is not merely data volume but the absence of geometric structure. The combined MGP+GAD framework achieves SOTA across all three major RVOS benchmarks with a compact 202M-parameter model, competitive with 7B-parameter LLM-based methods. Open questions include whether the geometric distillation transfers to other video understanding tasks (action recognition, video retrieval), how the approach scales to longer videos or more complex camera motions, and whether the 3D teacher models (π³, VGGT) introduce their own biases that limit generalization. The authors note future work toward open-vocabulary and large-scale 3D-aware multimodal segmentation.
