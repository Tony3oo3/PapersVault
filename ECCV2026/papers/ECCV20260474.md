## Problem

Low-cost GNSS introduces meter-level 3-DoF pose offsets in retrieved SD maps, degrading downstream planning and control in autonomous driving. Prior visual re-localization methods correct these offsets by rasterizing both camera observations and SD maps into dense BEV grids, which are computationally expensive and inject nuisance features from scene content irrelevant to road geometry (buildings, vegetation, sidewalks). No prior work has addressed SD map re-localization directly in vectorized map representations, nor has the true distribution of real GNSS offsets been characterized.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8 surround-view images (1024×768) processed by a frozen online visual map construction network (MapTR, MapQR, or LaneSegNet) into a vectorized visual map of lane centerlines, boundaries, and crossings, alongside a GNSS-retrieved SD map augmented to lane-level vectors with a fixed 3 m lane width, both within a 200×100 m ego-centric window. It outputs a 3-DoF pose offset (Δx, Δy, Δθ) via a lightweight MLP, plus per-dimension reliability scores in [0,1]³. At inference, the GNSS SD map is warped by the predicted SE(2) offset and re-encoded to produce reliability scores, whereas at training the reliability module sees RTK and GNSS SD map features separately with pseudo-labels derived from ground-truth offset magnitudes.

## Method

VectorReLoc encodes the vectorized visual map and GNSS SD map with modality-specific 10-layer Transformer encoders (d=256, 8 heads), where the two SD map variants (RTK and GNSS) share a weight-tied encoder so their features are directly comparable; a learnable global query aggregates tokens via cross-attention into fixed-size map features. The key architectural insight is a bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive objective augmented with a per-sample margin push loss that explicitly structures the embedding space: visual-map features are pulled toward RTK SD-map features while GNSS SD-map features are repelled from both, and this alignment loss is jointly optimized with the SL1 regression loss (rather than in a two-stage pipeline, which destroys the learned geometry). A per-dimension reliability predictor, trained with pseudo-labels from normalized GT offset magnitudes via binary cross-entropy, enables downstream modules to selectively accept or reject each component of the predicted correction. The entire re-localization head is only 45.42 M parameters and 1197.71 GFLOPs, running in 2–5 ms on edge chips (Horizon J6M), roughly 17–27% lighter than the dense-BEV baseline HOLO.

## Results

On nuScenes (±30 m/±30° simulated offsets), VectorReLoc achieves 65.79% 1 m position recall and 87.64% 1° orientation recall, with mean position error of 1.36 m and orientation error of 0.62°, substantially outperforming all prior methods. On Argoverse2 under the same protocol, it reaches 58.05% 1 m position recall and 75.08% 1° orientation recall, reducing E_pos from 8.66 m (SegLocNet) to 1.42 m (−83.6%) and E_ori from 34.03° to 1.06° (−96.9%). The model is also the lightest (45.42 M params, 1197.71 GFLOPs) and fastest (65.45 ms on A100) among compared methods, and the re-localization head runs in 2–5 ms on the Horizon J6M edge chip.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VectorReLoc decisively outperforms all five baselines on both nuScenes and Argoverse2. On nuScenes, it surpasses the strongest prior method SegLocNet (which additionally uses LiDAR) by +30.16 pp in 1 m position recall (65.79% vs. 35.63%) and reduces E_pos from 8.15 m to 1.36 m; against HOLO, the closest camera-only competitor, it gains +35.39 pp at 1 m and cuts E_pos from 3.76 m to 1.36 m. On Argoverse2, the margin over SegLocNet is even wider: +17.33 pp at 1 m position recall and E_pos drops from 8.66 m to 1.42 m (−83.6%), while E_ori falls from 34.03° to 1.06° (−96.9%). The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is the 5° orientation recall on Argoverse2 (MapLocNet 96.24% vs. 95.93%), a threshold of limited practical relevance since 99% of real frames have |Δθ| < 1.40°. OrienterNet and U-BEV, the earliest BEV-matching approaches, trail by 30–50 pp on 1 m recall, underscoring the gap between rasterized and vectorized representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant dense-BEV paradigm in SD map visual re-localization (OrienterNet, MapLocNet, SegLocNet, HOLO) by operating entirely on sparse vectorized map tokens produced by off-the-shelf online mapping networks (MapTR, MapQR, LaneSegNet). It repurposes the bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive objective from representation learning to structure a shared embedding space between visual-map and SD-map features, a use the authors note is unexplored for vectorized map alignment. The reliability prediction module draws on selective classification and OOD-detection literature but adapts it to continuous pose-offset regression via pseudo-labels derived from ground-truth offset magnitude, since no explicit failure annotations exist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 170K-frame VectorReLoc dataset with paired real RTK/GNSS-retrieved SD maps is a major resource that fills a critical gap: all prior benchmarks rely on synthetically perturbed poses, and the authors show real offsets follow a heavy-tailed Student-t distribution rather than the assumed Gaussian, with 99% of 2D offsets under 14 m versus the 30 m simulation range used in open-source benchmarks. The sparse vectorized architecture achieves 2–5 ms inference on edge chips (Horizon J6M), making real-time deployment on resource-constrained platforms feasible. Open questions include whether the Student-t offset hypothesis generalizes across GNSS hardware and geographies, whether the approach extends to 3-DoF+ (e.g., altitude) or multi-map-fusion settings, and how the reliability score should be consumed in closed-loop planning under the heavy-tailed failure regime.
