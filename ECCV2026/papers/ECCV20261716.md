## Problem

Current Vision-Language-Action models emit final actions without explicit step-by-step reasoning over affordance constraints, geometric relations, or container selection, leading to failures under color similarity, duplicate instances, or multiple candidate receptacles. Additionally, their post-training pipelines rely almost exclusively on supervised fine-tuning with weak or single-objective reward design, failing to systematically reinforce reasoning quality alongside execution accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261716/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and a natural language instruction as input. It produces a structured two-part output: an explicit chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in <think> tags, followed by geometric action specifications in <output> tags consisting of a 2D affordance bounding box and an ordered sequence of 2D waypoints (normalized to [0,1000]) representing the execution trajectory. At deployment, a deterministic control stack performs depth back-projection with calibrated camera parameters and applies robot kinematics to convert these geometric predictions into joint-level commands.

## Method

VLA-R1 is built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B with a redesigned window-attention ViT vision encoder and an attached action decoder, and is trained in two stages. Stage 1 performs SFT on VLA-CoT-13K, a 13K-sample dataset where a Qwen2.5-VL-72B teacher generates task-aware chain-of-thought traces (task parsing → scene understanding → localization → feasibility check) explicitly aligned with affordance and trajectory annotations from ShareRobot. Stage 2 applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning with three verifiable rewards: a GIoU reward for affordance box alignment (providing informative gradients even for non-overlapping boxes), an Angle-Length Augmented Fréchet distance (ALAF) reward that penalizes positional, tangential-angle, and segment-length mismatches along the trajectory, and a binary format reward enforcing the required <think>/<output> structure. The key design insight is that CoT supervision provides structured reasoning priors during SFT while multi-objective RLVR refines the policy using task-success signals, jointly optimizing interpretability and execution accuracy.

## Results

On the in-domain ShareRobot benchmark, VLA-R1-3B achieves an affordance IoU of 36.51% and an average trajectory error (mean of DFD, HD, RMSE) of 91.74, improving over the strongest RL baseline ManipLVM-R1-3B by 17.78% in IoU and 17.25% in trajectory error. On out-of-domain evaluation (UMD Part Affordance + VAIT), VLA-R1-3B attains IoU 33.96% and Avg 93.90, surpassing all baselines despite substantial distribution shift. In real-world deployment across four tabletop scenarios, VLA-R1 reaches 62.5% success rate for affordance perception and 75% for trajectory execution, outperforming Pi0.5 (62.5%/70%) and all earlier VLA baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLA-R1-3B outperforms all 12 baselines on every in-domain and out-of-domain metric. Against the closest RL competitor ManipLVM-R1-3B, VLA-R1 gains +5.51 IoU points (36.51 vs 31.00) and reduces average trajectory error from 110.87 to 91.74 (−17.25%). Against Embodied-R1-3B, the trajectory advantage is even larger: Avg drops from 110.75 to 91.74 in-domain and from 126.13 to 93.90 out-of-domain. SFT-only baselines (NORA-3B, RoboBrain-7B, LLaVA-1.6-7B, InternVL2-2B) trail by 13–33 IoU points, confirming that supervised fine-tuning alone is insufficient for precise embodied grounding. Open-source instruction-tuned models (Phi-4, Gemma-3, Qwen2.5-VL) perform worst, with IoU below 8 and trajectory errors exceeding 100, underscoring the gap between generic MLLMs and task-specific VLA models. In real-world and simulation settings (Table 3), VLA-R1 matches Pi0.5 on affordance (62.5% vs 62.5%) but leads on trajectory (75% vs 70% real-world; 70% vs 65% simulation), while older baselines like OpenVLA and RT-2-X remain below 40% on both tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLA-R1 sits at the intersection of two recent threads: (1) VLA models for robotic manipulation (OpenVLA, RoboBrain, ManipLVM-R1) that ground language instructions into affordance regions and trajectories, and (2) RLVR-based post-training for multimodal LLMs (Vision-R1, LMM-R1, VLM-R1) that uses verifiable rewards to sharpen reasoning. The closest prior work, ManipLVM-R1, already applies RLVR to embodied manipulation but relies on a single-objective reward. VLA-R1 departs by jointly optimizing three verifiable rewards (GIoU for region alignment, an angle-length-augmented Fréchet distance for trajectory shape, and a binary format reward) under GRPO, and by pairing this optimization with a dedicated CoT data engine (VLA-CoT-13K) that provides step-by-step reasoning supervision during SFT—something prior VLA pipelines largely omit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit chain-of-thought supervision and multi-objective RLVR can be combined to close the gap between 'reasoning about what to do' and 'executing it correctly' in embodied manipulation, yielding consistent gains across in-domain, out-of-domain, simulation, and real-robot settings. A key open question is whether this paradigm transfers to more complex embodiments (bimanual arms, quadrupeds) and longer-horizon tasks, which the authors acknowledge as untested. The VLA-CoT data engine and the ALAF trajectory metric are practical tools that other VLA groups could adopt, but the overall recipe (SFT with CoT + GRPO with hand-crafted rewards) is an incremental extension of the R1-style RLVR recipe already validated in non-embodied vision-language settings.
