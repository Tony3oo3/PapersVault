## Problem

Unified multimodal models that integrate understanding and generation in a single architecture struggle with complex compositional image generation—multi-object co-occurrence, attribute binding, and spatial constraints—exhibiting concept confusion, misaligned attributes, and structural defects. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) functional conflicts arising when image understanding and generation share a single visual Transformer branch with incompatible optimization objectives, and (2) the absence of a reasoning-guided generation process that can detect and correct residual defects after an initial generation attempt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260165/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a simple text prompt as input and produces a multi-stage output sequence: a dense caption expanding the prompt, per-object layout bounding boxes in normalized coordinates, a first-pass generated image, an artifact heatmap highlighting defective regions, and a final corrected image. Images are encoded/decoded via SD3's VAE in latent space with 16×16 pixel patches compressed into single tokens, and text is tokenized with Qwen2's 151,936-token vocabulary expanded by 6 functional special tokens. The full Paint-CoT pipeline thus requires two image generation passes (initial + inpainting correction) rather than a single forward diffusion.

## Method

MANGO combines two key contributions. First, Functionality-Oriented Transformers (FoT) reassigns Transformer branches by function rather than modality: a Linguistic Branch handles text understanding and generation (which share the same next-token objective), a Semantic Visual Branch handles image understanding (next-token prediction over visual features), and a Generative Visual Branch handles image generation (Rectified Flow denoising loss). Branches share multimodal routing and global attention but maintain independent weights for attention projections, FFNs, and layer norms, eliminating the cross-functional interference that plagues modality-oriented designs like MoT and LlamaFusion. Second, Paint-CoT structures generation as a four-step artistic chain of thought—Planning (dense caption + layout boxes), Acting (diffusion-guided image synthesis), Reflection (artifact heatmap via image understanding), and Correction (targeted inpainting of masked regions)—mimicking a human artist's sketch-reflect-revise workflow. To avoid the infeasible cost of collecting fully aligned multi-step CoT annotations, a multi-task joint training paradigm decomposes the pipeline into independently trainable subtasks (Planning+Acting, Reflection, Correction), each supervised with readily available or synthetically constructed data.

## Results

On GenEval, MANGO (1.3B) achieves an overall score of 0.77, surpassing the much larger SD3 (12.7B, 0.68) by 9% and DALL-E 3 (0.67) by 10%, while also outperforming CoT-guided models GoT (0.64) and GoT-R1 (0.75). MANGO-4B (4.2B) reaches 0.82 overall, matching BAGEL (7B, 0.82) and exceeding Janus-Pro (7B, 0.80). On T2I-CompBench, MANGO (1.3B) attains state-of-the-art scores in Spatial (35.71), Non-Spatial (34.19), and Complex (42.78), and on MS-COCO achieves FID 7.24 and CIDEr 126.5, outperforming DALL-E 2 (FID 10.39) and NExT-GPT (FID 10.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MANGO (1.3B) decisively outperforms same-scale unified models: it beats JanusFlow (0.63) by +0.14, Show-o (0.53) by +0.24, and Janus (0.61) by +0.16 on GenEval overall. Against larger diffusion models, MANGO (1.3B) surpasses SD3 (12.7B) by 9% and DALL-E 3 by 10% overall, with particularly large gains on Attribute Binding (+21% over SD3, +19% over DALL-E 3). Among CoT-guided generation models, MANGO (1.3B) outperforms GoT (0.64) by +0.13 and GoT-R1 (7B) by +0.02, despite using a smaller base model. MANGO-4B (4.2B) ties BAGEL (7B) at 0.82 overall and edges out Janus-Pro (7B, 0.80), while on T2I-CompBench it surpasses both T2I-R1 (7B) and GoT-R1 (7B) across all six sub-metrics. On WISE, MANGO-4B achieves 0.56 overall, exceeding T2I-R1 (0.54) and BAGEL (0.52), and on MS-COCO MANGO (1.3B) attains the best FID (7.24) and CIDEr (126.5) among all compared unified models.

## Relation to Prior Work

MANGO builds directly on the hybrid autoregressive-diffusion paradigm of Transfusion and the multi-branch architecture of MoT/LlamaFusion, but departs from their modality-oriented branch assignment (one branch per modality) by introducing Functionality-Oriented Transformers (FoT), which reassigns branches by function—linguistic, semantic visual (understanding), and generative visual (generation)—to eliminate cross-functional interference that modality-oriented designs leave unresolved. On the reasoning side, Paint-CoT extends the CoT-for-generation line (GoT, GoT-R1, T2I-R1) by adding a reflection-correction loop that performs targeted inpainting, moving beyond the single-pass planning that prior CoT-based generators rely on. The multi-task joint training paradigm further distinguishes MANGO by decomposing the full reasoning-generation pipeline into independently trainable subtasks, sidestepping the need for expensive fully-aligned multi-step CoT annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and frequently encountered failure mode of unified multimodal models—degraded performance on complex compositional prompts (multi-object, attribute binding, spatial constraints)—and shows that a clean architectural reassignment plus a human-like iterative painting workflow can close much of the gap to dedicated diffusion models at a fraction of the parameter count. The FoT design principle (disentangle by function, not just by modality) is a reusable architectural insight likely to inform future unified model designs. Open questions include whether the reflection-correction loop scales to video or 3D generation, whether the multi-task training paradigm generalizes to other reasoning-guided generation settings, and whether the gains persist at much larger scales where the functional-conflict hypothesis may interact differently with model capacity.
