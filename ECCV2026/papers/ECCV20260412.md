## Problem

Native 3D generative models like Trellis produce high-fidelity assets from text or image prompts but cannot prescribe precise structural articulations such as specific joint angles or atypical topologies. Existing skeleton-guided 3D methods (e.g., SKDream) project 3D skeletons into 2D to condition multi-view diffusion, introducing spatial ambiguity from depth flattening and self-occlusion, followed by lossy multi-stage reconstruction that degrades geometry and texture. No prior work achieves direct, precise skeletal control within the native 3D generation space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a 3D skeleton defined as N joint coordinates in R^{N×3} plus a topology graph (parent mapping or adjacency matrix) as input, and produces a textured 3D mesh via Trellis's structured 3D latents (SLaT). The skeleton is consumed natively as sparse spatial tokens rather than being projected to 2D, and it is kept dropout-free during training (unlike the 10% text dropout for classifier-free guidance), reflecting its role as a hard structural constraint. For the editing extension, the input additionally includes a masked bounding box on an existing asset and a modified skeleton, with output restricted to the masked region.

## Method

SK-Adapter is a lightweight structural adapter that freezes the entire Trellis flow-transformer backbone and trains only a skeleton encoder plus injected cross-attention layers. The skeleton is encoded via Graph Relative Positional Encoding (GRPE), which learns embeddings for topological distance and joint relations to produce topology-aware skeletal tokens in R^{J×F}. In each transformer block, a cross-attention layer uses the frozen backbone's voxel features as queries and the skeletal tokens as keys/values, allowing each spatial voxel to attend to relevant joints; the output passes through a zero-initialized linear layer and is added residually, so the adapter contributes a null signal at initialization and gradually learns structural modulation without destabilizing the pretrained distribution. Training uses Latent Flow Matching in Trellis's VAE latent space for 200 epochs (batch 16, lr 1e-5) on the 24k-sample Objaverse-TMS dataset, optimizing only the GRPE encoder, cross-attention projections, and zero-init layers.

## Results

On TMS-eval (140 test instances), SK-Adapter achieves a ReRigging Score of 0.2228 (lower is better; oracle reference 0.2073), substantially outperforming SKDream (0.2818) and SpaceControl (0.2740) in structural alignment. It also attains the highest CLIP Score (26.16) and PickScore (21.01), while reducing KD-DINO to 0.7778 (vs. 1.3809 for SKDream and 1.7821 for SpaceControl), confirming that structural control is achieved without sacrificing visual fidelity. Generation runs in under 15 seconds per asset, roughly 2.7× faster than SKDream (~40 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SK-Adapter is compared against two baselines: SKDream [56], a multi-view diffusion approach that projects 3D skeletons into 2D maps before 3D reconstruction, and SpaceControl [8], a training-free spatial guidance method adapted to skeletal conditioning by converting joints to spheres and bones to cylinders. SK-Adapter wins decisively on structural alignment, reducing the ReRigging Score by 21% over SKDream (0.2228 vs. 0.2818) and 19% over SpaceControl (0.2228 vs. 0.2740), with the largest gains on the 'Other' category (0.2861 vs. 0.4075 and 0.3386). On visual fidelity, SK-Adapter also leads: PickScore 21.01 vs. 20.46 (SKDream) and 20.55 (SpaceControl), and KD-DINO 0.7778 vs. 1.3809 and 1.7821 respectively, indicating that the frozen-backbone adapter preserves generative quality far better than multi-stage reconstruction or over-constrained spatial guidance. SKDream's 2D projection introduces spatial ambiguity and multi-view inconsistencies, while SpaceControl over-constrains the skeleton, producing fragmented topologies. SK-Adapter matches SpaceControl on the 'Other' CLIP Score (23.43) but leads on all other sub-categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

SK-Adapter extends the adapter/PEFT paradigm (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, IP-Adapter) from 2D image generation into the native 3D domain, using Trellis's structured 3D latents and flow-matching transformer as a frozen backbone. It departs conceptually from SKDream's '2D lifting' strategy—projecting 3D skeletons into 2D maps to condition multi-view diffusion—by arguing that skeletal control signals must be dimensionally congruent with the generation space, and instead injects topology-aware joint tokens directly via cross-attention. The topology encoding borrows GRPE's graph-relative positional mechanism, and the editing capability adapts Repaint's inpainting logic to skeleton-conditioned latent regeneration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that precise skeletal structural control can be achieved within a native 3D generative model without sacrificing the foundation model's generative priors, which is critical for producing animation-ready, articulable assets in production pipelines. The Objaverse-TMS dataset (24k expert-annotated text-mesh-skeleton triplets) addresses a genuine data bottleneck that has limited prior work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Trellis to other 3D backbones, how it scales to very high joint counts or non-articulated objects, and whether the editing capability can be extended beyond addition/re-posing to more complex topological modifications.
