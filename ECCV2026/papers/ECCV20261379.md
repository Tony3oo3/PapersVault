## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation and completion methods are trained exclusively on complete, synthetic 3D datasets, creating a fundamental domain gap with real-world scans that are inherently incomplete and noisy due to occlusions and limited sensor coverage. This limits their ability to capture the variability, complexity, and off-axis alignments of real environments, and prevents them from learning meaningful geometry distributions from partial observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261379/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 256³ TSDF voxel chunks (1.1 cm voxel size, 3× truncation) reconstructed from raw depth sequences via volumetric fusion, where unknown regions are explicitly marked with a sentinel value of −3× voxel size. Conditioning is provided by axis-aligned 3D bounding boxes with CLIP-encoded semantic labels, or alternatively by text prompts parsed into layouts via an LLM. Output is a complete TSDF volume decoded into surface and empty voxel categories, which can be tiled via MultiDiffusion (0.2 overlap ratio) to produce arbitrarily large multi-room scenes.

## Method

Seen2Scene introduces visibility-guided flow matching that trains a sparse transformer (28-block SparseDiT with RoPE) to generate geometry latents from Gaussian noise, where the flow matching loss is computed only over observed regions identified by the TSDF visibility mask. A masked sparse VAE (fVDB residual convolutions, 8× downsampling, 8 latent channels) compresses partial TSDF patches into compact latents, with reconstruction losses applied exclusively to known voxels to prevent the model from learning ambiguity from unobserved space. For scan completion, a ControlNet branch initialized from the pretrained generator injects a more-incomplete scan variant (created by removing depth frames) as a per-layer conditioning signal while keeping the base flow matching weights frozen. The layout condition is rendered as a 3D spatial map of CLIP text features aligned to the latent grid, and classifier-free guidance (scale 3.0) is used at inference with 50-step Euler sampling.

## Results

On 3D scan completion (Table 1), Seen2Scene achieves a Chamfer Distance of 2.05×10⁻² on 3D-FRONT, substantially outperforming SG-NN (10.77×10⁻²) and NKSR (5.22×10⁻²), while also achieving the best L2 (0.51×10⁻⁴), TMD (3.33×10⁻²), and U3D-FPD (7.79×10⁻²) across ScanNet++, ARKitScenes, and 3D-FRONT. On layout-conditioned 3D scene generation (Table 2), Seen2Scene attains the lowest DINOv2-FID (2.73×10²), lowest U3D-FPD (11.55×10²), and highest VLM quality score (5.61/10) among all compared methods on 3D-FRONT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seen2Scene outperforms both scan-completion baselines by large margins: CD drops from 10.77 (SG-NN) and 5.22 (NKSR) to 2.05, and U3D-FPD improves from 15.41/21.78 to 7.79. Because SG-NN and NKSR are deterministic regressors, their TMD is zero, whereas Seen2Scene's generative formulation yields a TMD of 3.33, indicating diverse yet plausible completions. In the scene-generation comparison, Seen2Scene (synthetic-only variant) beats BlockFusion by a wide margin on DINOv2-FID (2.73 vs. 12.92) and U3D-FPD (11.55 vs. 28.30), and also surpasses LT3SD (3.51 FID, 28.55 FPD) and WorldGrow (4.44 FID, 50.83 FPD) on all three metrics. The VLM quality score confirms the gap: Seen2Scene scores 5.61/10 versus 5.20 (LT3SD), 4.69 (WorldGrow), and 3.51 (BlockFusion), reflecting more realistic surface detail and semantic coherence learned from real-world partial scans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow matching generative paradigm (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) to 3D scene geometry by introducing visibility-guided masking that allows training directly on incomplete real-world TSDF scans, rather than requiring complete synthetic ground truth as prior scene generation methods (BlockFusion, LT3SD, WorldGrow) do. It repurposes the masked training strategy from SG-NN and the ControlNet conditioning mechanism from image diffusion to frame scan completion as a generative task rather than a deterministic regression, and adopts MultiDiffusion-style tiled generation for large-scale scenes. The sparse VAE backbone draws on fVDB's residual sparse convolutional blocks, and the overall architecture (sparse transformer over TSDF latents) follows the recent trend of structured sparse latent representations for 3D generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By training directly on incomplete real scans with visibility-guided masking, this work addresses a fundamental domain gap that has limited prior 3D scene generators to synthetic data, making the approach more applicable to real-world AR/VR and robotics pipelines where scans are inherently partial. The flexibility to condition on text prompts, 3D layouts, or partial scans via a single backbone is practically useful. Open questions include whether the axis-aligned bounding box layout assumption generalizes to non-axis-aligned or outdoor scenes, whether the VLM-based evaluation metric is sufficiently reliable, and whether the approach scales to very large multi-building environments beyond the indoor settings tested here.
