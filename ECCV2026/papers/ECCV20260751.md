## Problem

Federated Latent Diffusion Models (FedLDMs) share a global model among all participating clients, creating a critical ownership vulnerability: malicious clients can leak or resell the model without authorization. Existing watermarking approaches for FL either support only ownership verification without per-client traceability, or (when applied to LDMs via VAE-based methods like Stable Signature) are trivially defeated by a zero-cost VAE decoder replacement that removes the watermark without any image quality degradation.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v2.1 model (VAE + U-Net), K client datasets (LAION-10K, 256×256 images), and a 48-bit watermark split into a 16-bit ownership prefix and 32-bit per-client tracing suffix. It outputs K watermarked model replicas for federated fine-tuning, and at verification time consumes images generated by a potentially leaked model to produce a binary ownership decision (threshold τ=0.69, FPR 0.1%) and, upon confirmation, the index of the specific leaking client. LVT training uses 512×512 COCO2017 images, while the watermark is embedded in the VAE decoder and extracted from generated images via a Stable Signature-style extractor.

## Method

FedOT combines a chunked watermark with Latent Vector Transformation (LVT) to achieve both ownership verification and per-client leakage tracing in federated LDMs. The chunked watermark is embedded once per client into the VAE decoder using a Stable Signature extractor guided by BCE loss plus Watson-VGG perceptual loss, so the first r bits confirm group ownership and the remaining n−r bits uniquely identify the leaking client. To prevent the VAE replacement attack, LVT fine-tunes the VAE in two stages before FL training: Stage I trains the encoder to map inputs to a transformed latent space z′=T(z), and Stage II freezes the encoder and adapts the decoder to reconstruct from the new latent distribution z*. Three deterministic transformations are explored—translation (z+c), mirror (−z), and pixel-negative (1−x)—and the negative transformation is selected as optimal because it preserves high-frequency structural details while still forcing the U-Net to depend on the modified latent space, so any unauthorized VAE swap causes severe FID degradation (e.g., +20.17 for FedOT_neg vs. −0.13 for untransformed Stable Signature).

## Results

On LAION-10K (256×256, 48-bit watermark), FedOT_neg achieves the best generation quality among LVT variants (FID 20.367, CLIP-Score 0.295) with ownership detection rate 0.954 and bit accuracy 0.922. Under the VAE replacement attack, FedOT_neg's FID degrades from 20.367 to 40.537 (+20.170), rendering the stolen model unusable, while Stable Signature* and FedOT w/o LVT retain near-original image quality (FID ≈ 16–17) but lose all watermark detection (Detection drops to 0.000). For ownership verification and client tracing, FedOT_neg achieves Detection 0.960 / Bit Acc 0.947 (ownership) and Detection 0.932 / Bit Acc 0.910 (tracing), both above 0.91 across all LVT variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stable Signature* [9] (adapted to FL), FedOT wins decisively on two axes: (1) traceability — Stable Signature* can only verify group-level ownership and provides no per-client tracing, whereas FedOT's chunked watermark identifies the specific leaking client (Detection 0.932, Bit Acc 0.910 for FedOT_neg); (2) robustness to VAE replacement — after the attack, Stable Signature* retains near-identical image quality (FID 16.282 vs. 16.412) but its watermark is completely erased (Detection 0.000), meaning the attack succeeds at zero cost, while FedOT_neg's FID jumps to 40.537, destroying the model's utility and making the attack impractical. Against the Original SD [36] pre-trained baseline (FID 22.99), all FedOT variants with FL fine-tuning improve generation quality (FedOT_neg reaches FID 20.367), confirming that the federated fine-tuning on LAION-10K is beneficial. Among the three LVT strategies, FedOT_neg offers the best quality–robustness trade-off: it has the lowest original FID (20.367) and the smallest post-attack FID increase (+20.170) compared to FedOT_tran (+70.070) and FedOT_mir (+49.147), while still exceeding the 22.99 FID threshold that defines attack failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Signature (Fernandez et al., ICCV 2023), adopting its VAE-decoder watermark embedding and extraction pipeline for LDMs, but repurposes it within a federated learning setting where the VAE is frozen and only the U-Net is updated. It extends the FL watermarking line initiated by WAFFLE and FedTracker, which were restricted to classification models, by addressing the fundamentally different architecture of generative LDMs. The key conceptual departure is the Latent Vector Transformation (LVT), which proactively modifies the VAE's latent distribution before federated training to create a hard dependency between VAE and U-Net — a mechanism absent from prior LDM watermarking work that assumed the VAE could be freely swapped.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedOT fills a concrete gap: federated LDMs currently have no mechanism to identify which client leaked a model, and the most natural watermarking approach (VAE-based) is trivially defeated by a zero-cost decoder swap. The LVT defense reframes the problem from 'prevent watermark removal' to 'make the model worthless if the watermark is removed,' which is a practical and elegant shift. However, the evaluation is limited to Stable Diffusion v2.1 on LAION-10K with at most 20 clients and 48-bit watermarks, leaving open whether LVT generalizes to SDXL, video diffusion, or larger-scale deployments. A key open question is whether the binding strength of LVT degrades over many FL rounds as the U-Net continues to adapt, and whether more sophisticated attacks (e.g., partial VAE retraining) can recover utility without full replacement.
