## Problem

Existing 1D video tokenizers like LARP entangle spatial and temporal modeling within a single transformer backbone, resulting in slow convergence (over 2× the training steps of MAGVIT-v2) and difficulty simultaneously preserving fine-grained motion dynamics and spatial details. The paper asks whether explicitly decoupling spatial-temporal representation learning with dedicated supervision from specialized foundation models can resolve both the efficiency bottleneck and the quality ceiling of 1D video tokenization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260809/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes 16-frame video clips at 128×128 resolution, patchified with temporal patch size t=4 and spatial patch size p=8. The first frame is routed to the appearance stream while the full clip feeds the motion stream, producing 256 appearance tokens and 768 motion tokens (1024 total) that are vector-quantized into a codebook of 8192 entries. For downstream generation, a LLaMA-style AR transformer takes a class label (UCF-101) or 5 context frames (K600) as conditioning and autoregressively samples the 1024-token sequence, which the decoder reshapes back into a 16-frame 128×128 video.

## Method

DeRA factorizes video encoding into an appearance stream (processing only the first frame) and a motion stream (processing the full clip), both passing through a shared transformer encoder with distinct learnable query sets. The key supervision is Decoupled Representation Alignment: a lightweight MLP projects encoder latents to match frozen DINOv3 features (appearance) and InternVideo2 features (motion) via negative cosine similarity, explicitly anchoring each stream to its specialized semantic subspace. To resolve gradient conflicts between these heterogeneous alignment objectives, the SACP module computes the dot product of the two alignment gradients at each step; when negative, it subtracts the opposing projection component from each gradient (with stopgrad coefficients) to yield a consensus update direction without introducing new hyperparameters. The concatenated appearance and motion tokens are vector-quantized and decoded, while all alignment modules are discarded at inference, leaving a standard encoder-quantizer-decoder pipeline.

## Results

On UCF-101, DeRA achieves a reconstruction rFVD of 15 and a generation gFVD of 50 with 1024 tokens, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art LARP (rFVD 20, gFVD 57) by 25% in rFVD. On Kinetics-600 frame prediction, DeRA attains a gFVD of 4.1, surpassing LARP (5.1) and AdapTok (11). DeRA also outperforms MAGVIT-v2-AR (gFVD 109) and OmniTokenizer (gFVD 191) on UCF-101 class-conditional generation while using fewer tokens (1024 vs. 1280) and fewer generator parameters (632M vs. 840M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeRA outperforms LARP [29], the closest 1D video tokenizer baseline, on all three metrics: rFVD 15 vs. 20 (25% improvement), K600 gFVD 4.1 vs. 5.1, and UCF-101 gFVD 50 vs. 57, while using the same 1024-token budget and comparable parameter counts (174M/632M vs. 173M/632M). Against grid-based AR tokenizers, DeRA achieves substantially lower generation FVD than MAGVIT-v2-AR (50 vs. 109) and OmniTokenizer (50 vs. 191) on UCF-101, though MAGVIT-v2-AR retains a lower reconstruction rFVD (8.6 vs. 15) due to its larger 1280-token budget. DeRA also beats AdapTok [13] on both rFVD (15 vs. 28) and gFVD (50 vs. 67) despite using half the tokens (1024 vs. 2048). On K600 frame prediction, DeRA's gFVD of 4.1 is the best reported, ahead of LARP (5.1) and AdapTok (11). The paper also reports that DeRA converges in ~73 epochs versus LARP's 150 epochs to reach the same rFVD of 20, and reaches rFVD 15 within the same 150-epoch budget, demonstrating a ~2× training speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeRA builds directly on the 1D query-based video tokenizer architecture introduced by LARP and TiTok, which compresses video into a fixed-length sequence of latent queries rather than a spatial-temporal grid. Its key departure is the explicit spatial-temporal decoupling: rather than a single entangled transformer backbone, DeRA factorizes encoding into an appearance stream (first frame) and a motion stream (full clip), each aligned to a specialized frozen foundation model (DINOv3 for spatial semantics, InternVideo2 for temporal dynamics), extending the single-model alignment paradigm of REPA to a dual-model, dual-stream setting. The SACP module is a novel contribution that addresses the gradient conflicts this dual-alignment introduces, a problem not present in prior single-objective alignment work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-2× training speedup over LARP (73 vs. 150 epochs for equivalent rFVD) is practically significant for practitioners who find 1D video tokenizer training prohibitively slow, and the decoupled latent space enables zero-shot token swapping for video editing—a capability not available in entangled tokenizers. However, the paper acknowledges that video foundation models currently provide weaker supervisory signals than image FMs, leaving open the question of whether a dedicated high-fidelity motion representation model could further unlock the approach. Experiments are limited to 128×128, 16-frame clips, so scalability to higher resolutions and longer sequences remains untested.
