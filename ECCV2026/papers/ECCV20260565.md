## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models suffer from progressive quality degradation (oversaturation, motion repetition, semantic drift) over long horizons because they condition each new block on a fully denoised context, which propagates accumulated prediction errors with maximal confidence. The paper argues that a highly clean context is not a prerequisite for temporal continuity and that matching the context noise level to the current denoising step can substantially reduce inter-block error transmission.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260565/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates a 20-second video autoregressively in chunks of 3 latent frames using a 4-step flow-matching denoising schedule on the Wan2.1-1.3B backbone. At inference, a sliding-window KV cache with a constant 5-second attention window bounds memory, and the hierarchical structure enables pipelined parallelism across denoising steps, yielding 30 fps throughput and 0.30 s per-chunk latency.

## Method

HiAR reverses the conventional block-first denoising order into a step-first (hierarchical) paradigm: at each denoising step j, causal generation is performed across all N blocks simultaneously, so every block conditions on its predecessors at the matched output noise level t_{j+1} rather than on fully clean frames. A bias–information trade-off analysis shows t_{j+1} is the noisiest context level that still satisfies the temporal-causality SNR constraint, minimising propagated error while retaining all information the denoiser needs. The hierarchical grid structure makes blocks on the same anti-diagonal independent, enabling pipelined parallel inference with a fused KV-cache-write-and-denoise forward pass for ~1.8× wall-clock speedup. To stabilise self-rollout distillation training, a forward-KL regulariser is computed in bidirectional-attention mode and restricted to the first denoising step, counteracting the low-motion shortcut of the reverse-KL (DMD) objective without interfering with the causal distillation loss.

## Results

On VBench 20-second generation, HiAR achieves the best overall Total score (0.821) and Quality score (0.846) among all compared methods, surpassing the strongest distilled AR baseline Causal Forcing (0.810/0.837) and the bidirectional Wan2.1-1.3B teacher (0.802/0.813). HiAR also attains the lowest drift score (0.257) among all distilled AR models, a 27.6% reduction over Self-Forcing (0.355) and a 58.1% reduction over Causal Forcing (0.615). Inference efficiency is improved via pipelined parallelism, yielding 30 fps throughput and 0.30 s per-chunk latency — approximately 1.8× faster than other 4-step distilled AR methods (17 fps, 0.69 s) sharing the same Wan2.1-1.3B backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the three distilled AR baselines sharing the Wan2.1-1.3B backbone and 4-step schedule, HiAR outperforms all on Total, Quality, and Drift: it beats Causal Forcing (0.810→0.821 Total, 0.615→0.257 Drift) and Self-Forcing (0.805→0.821 Total, 0.355→0.257 Drift) while also surpassing CausVid (0.764 Total, 0.842 Drift) by a wide margin. On the Dynamic dimension, HiAR (0.686) closely matches the bidirectional Wan2.1-1.3B teacher (0.690) and substantially exceeds Self-Forcing (0.542) and Causal Forcing (0.672), validating the forward-KL regulariser's role in preventing motion collapse. HiAR trails Wan2.1-1.3B on Semantic (0.723 vs. 0.766) and CausVid on Semantic (0.723 vs. 0.740), reflecting a modest trade-off in prompt alignment for improved visual quality and temporal stability. Against non-distilled AR baselines (SkyReels-V2, MAGI-1, NOVA, Pyramid Flow), HiAR leads on Total and Quality while being orders of magnitude faster in latency (0.30 s vs. 2.5–282 s). In throughput, HiAR's 30 fps is 1.8× faster than the other distilled AR methods (17 fps) and 38× faster than full-step bidirectional Wan2.1-1.3B (0.78 fps), while simultaneously achieving higher Total and Quality scores than the latter.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiAR builds directly on the Self-Forcing/DMD-style distilled autoregressive video generation pipeline, departing from its core assumption that each block must be conditioned on a fully denoised (t_c=0) context. The key conceptual departure is inspired by bidirectional diffusion models, which denoise all frames at a shared noise level yet remain temporally coherent; HiAR formalises this as a bias-information trade-off and derives the optimal context noise level t_c* = t_{j+1} analytically. The training side extends Self-Forcing's reverse-KL DMD objective with a forward-KL regulariser computed in bidirectional-attention mode, a design choice motivated by the strong empirical correlation (r=0.968) between dynamics under the two attention modes. The hierarchical reordering also enables pipelined parallel inference across denoising steps, a systems-level contribution absent from prior AR video methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-horizon autoregressive video generation is a critical building block for interactive world models and real-time agents, and distribution drift is the central obstacle to scaling beyond a few seconds. HiAR's matched-noise principle provides a clean, theoretically grounded fix that practitioners can adopt with minimal architectural change, and the 1.8× pipelined speedup makes few-step AR generation more practical for streaming applications. The forward-KL regulariser addresses a known and under-appreciated failure mode (motion collapse under reverse-KL distillation) that likely affects other DMD-based video distillation pipelines. Open questions include whether the matched-noise schedule generalises to longer horizons (beyond 20 s), to larger backbones, or to interactive settings where the context window is dynamically truncated, and whether the low-motion shortcut can be further mitigated without the bidirectional-attention proxy.
