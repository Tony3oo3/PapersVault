## Problem

Prior automatic paper-to-slide generation methods lack effective structural modeling of the source paper's narrative: raw-text summarization produces incoherent surface-level condensation, section-level organization fractures the storyline into temporally disconnected slides, and direct outline extraction struggles with fine-grained coherence in dense content. No existing approach explicitly models the rhetorical dependencies between text units to orchestrate a unified, compelling presentation arc.

## Input / Output

The system ingests a raw academic paper PDF along with user-specified target audience and presentation duration. The PDF is parsed via Docling and Marker into clean Markdown (excluding bibliography and appendix), with all figures and tables extracted into an asset dictionary carrying captions and size metadata for downstream layout decisions. The final output is an editable .pptx deck at 13.33×7.5 inches using 14 layout templates, where text is toggled between bulleted hierarchies and paragraph narration based on per-slide information density, and short-form citations are rendered as footnotes.

## Method

ArcDeck formulates slide generation as structured narrative reconstruction through three mechanisms. First, a Discourse Parser builds a hierarchical RST tree per section, treating paragraphs as elementary discourse units and assigning nucleus-satellite or multinuclear relations to recursively encode both local rhetorical dependencies and global narrative organization. Second, a Commitment Builder produces a Global Commitment (snapshot, core content, talk contract, narrative spine, section plan) that acts as persistent high-level conditioning to prevent context loss across the generation pipeline. Third, a Narrative Refinement Loop iteratively refines the slide outline via a closed-loop critique-judge-revise cycle (Slide Planner → Narrative Critic → Narrative Judge → Reviser), evaluating alignment with the commitment, narrative flow, section balance, slide-level coherence, and redundancy over up to three cycles. The finalized outline then drives a two-agent Slide Generation stage: a Slide Deck Constructor maps assets to 14 layout templates and generates slide text guided by the RST-derived outline, followed by an Aesthetics Refiner that performs figure matching, content density adjustment, theme-color extraction, and targeted bold formatting before rendering via python-pptx.

## Results

On ArcBench (100 paper-slide pairs), ArcDeck achieves the highest VLM-as-Judge Narrative Flow score of 91.39 (open judge) and 63.83 (closed judge) under GPT-5, outperforming SlideGen (88.18/56.67), P2Poster (70.55/41.67), PPTAgent (55.94/37.67), and HTML (89.06/61.50). In pairwise narrative-flow A/B tests, ArcDeck wins 100% against PPTAgent, 62–97% against P2Poster, and 76–92% against HTML, while maintaining a narrower 50–81% edge over SlideGen. Against author-prepared (AP) reference slides, ArcDeck achieves the highest selection rate (35.7–48.1%) among all automated methods, though AP slides remain preferred overall. Under Qwen3-VL-32B, ArcDeck leads on Hard (83.22) and Depth (71.39) Q/A quiz categories, exceeding the next-best baseline by 6.65 and 3.43 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArcDeck is compared against four baselines spanning three paradigms: HTML (single-LLM prompt-based), P2Poster (visual-in-the-loop multi-agent), PPTAgent (two-stage edit-based), and SlideGen (six-agent VLM with template-driven layout). Against PPTAgent, ArcDeck achieves a decisive 95–100% pairwise win rate in narrative flow across all judge models, with the largest NF gap (e.g., 91.39 vs. 55.94 under GPT-5/open judge). Against P2Poster, ArcDeck wins 62–97% in pairwise comparisons, with NF scores of 91.39 vs. 70.55 (GPT-5/open). Against HTML, ArcDeck wins 76–92%, though HTML's NF score (89.06) is closer, reflecting that single-prompt generation can produce reasonable surface-level flow. The narrowest margin is against SlideGen (50–81% win rate; NF 91.39 vs. 88.18 under GPT-5/open), where both methods use multi-agent pipelines but ArcDeck's discourse-aware outline and refinement loop provide a consistent but smaller edge. In the holistic comparison against author-prepared slides (Table 4b), ArcDeck achieves the highest selection rate (up to 48.1% with GPT-5 judge) among all automated methods, yet all methods remain below 50%, indicating a persistent quality gap to human-level presentation design.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArcDeck carves new ground by repurposing Rhetorical Structure Theory (RST) discourse parsing—previously applied to summarization, QA, and sentiment analysis—as a structural prior for slide generation, a task where no prior method had explicitly modeled rhetorical dependencies between text spans. It departs from the three existing paradigms (direct LLM summarization, section-level organization, and flat multi-agent outlining) by introducing a hierarchical discourse tree and a persistent 'Global Commitment' document that conditions all downstream agents, rather than relying on section headings or one-shot outline generation. The multi-agent critique-judge-revise loop extends recent agentic pipelines (SlideGen, PPTAgent, Paper2Poster) by adding an iterative narrative-coherence check that prior frameworks lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ArcBench provides the first curated, author-prepared reference benchmark for academic paper-to-slide generation, filling a gap where prior datasets lacked quality curation or were not publicly released. However, the benchmark is limited to CV/ML venues and 100 pairs, and the generated slides still trail author-prepared presentations by a wide margin (roughly 40–48% win rate in A/B tests), indicating the task remains far from solved. Open questions include whether the discourse-parsing approach generalizes beyond AI papers to other scientific disciplines, whether the heavy multi-agent token overhead is justified at scale, and whether VLM-based evaluation (which dominates the protocol) can be trusted as a proxy for human judgment of narrative quality.
