## Problem

MLLMs underperform on fine-grained perception tasks—detecting small objects in high-resolution images, locating key moments in long videos, and pinpointing precise event timestamps—because uniform processing of vast visual inputs causes models to miss sparse but critical details. Existing remedies rely on task-specific fine-tuning, custom datasets, or external specialized models, limiting generalizability. This paper asks whether an MLLM's own intrinsic uncertainty can serve as a unified, training-free signal to direct attention toward the most informative visual content.

## Input / Output

For visual search, a high-resolution image (up to 8K) and a text query are partitioned into a grid of overlapping crops via a sliding window; for video tasks, the input is a sequence of uniformly sampled frames (up to 256 candidates from videos up to 41 minutes) paired with a query. The framework operates in two stages: a scoring pass evaluates each candidate crop or frame, and a final answering pass generates the output. Outputs are free-form text answers for QA tasks or start/end timestamp pairs for temporal grounding, with no additional structured prediction required.

## Method

The UG framework reformulates diverse localization tasks as a search for the visual input that minimizes the MLLM's output uncertainty. Two scoring metrics are used: Average Entropy (Shannon entropy averaged over all generated tokens) for general relevance, and Binary Response Confidence (BRC = P('yes') − P('no') from the first token distribution) for binary presence decisions. In UG-Search, the lowest-entropy crop is selected for final inference; in UG-Sample, the top-k lowest-entropy frames are concatenated in temporal order; in UG-Ground, a sliding window produces a BRC score sequence from which Kadane's algorithm extracts the maximum-sum subarray as the predicted event segment. The entire pipeline is training-free and architecture-agnostic, requiring only multiple forward passes of an off-the-shelf MLLM, with KV-state caching to reduce redundant computation across scoring passes.

## Results

On Visual Search, UG-Search yields large gains: InternVL2.5-8B improves from 71.7 to 91.1 on V* Bench (+19.4) and from 58.0 to 73.6 on HR8K (+15.6). On Long Video QA, UG-Sample boosts Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +6.1 on Video-MME (53.7→59.8) and +7.8 on LongVideoBench (52.7→60.5). On Temporal Grounding, UG-Ground produces the most dramatic improvements: LLaVA-OV-7B's mIoU on Charades-STA jumps from 10.4 to 46.8 (+36.4), and on ActivityNet Captions from 11.9 to 37.2 (+25.3), surpassing instruction-tuned models like VTimeLLM-13B (34.6 mIoU on Charades-STA) and TimeMarker-8B (48.4 mIoU) without any training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Visual Search (Table 2), UG-Search outperforms all four specialized baselines across V* Bench, HR4K, and HR8K: it beats the training-free ZoomEye (86.9 vs. 85.0 on V* Bench with LLaVA-OV) and substantially surpasses TextCoT (81.7 vs. 67.0), ViCrop (81.7 vs. 69.6), and the fine-tuned Thyme (81.7 vs. 68.1) with Qwen2.5-VL. On Long Video QA (Table 3), UG-Sample alone outperforms KFC, BOLT, AKS, and FRAG on Video-MME (58.6 vs. 55.4–56.3) and LongVideoBench (59.5 vs. 55.6–57.3), but underperforms on MLVU (62.0 vs. 65.0 for KFC/AKS); the hybrid UG-Sample+AKS recovers this gap to match the best (65.0). On Temporal Grounding (Table 5), UG-Ground with InternVideo2.5-8B achieves 51.0 mIoU on Charades-STA, exceeding the best instruction-tuned method TimeMarker-8B (48.4) and all training-free baselines (TFVTG 44.5, TAG 45.7), while with LLaVA-OV-7B it jumps from a 10.4 mIoU baseline to 46.8, surpassing VTimeLLM-13B (34.6) by 12.2 points without any fine-tuning. Overall, the training-free UG framework matches or exceeds both instruction-tuned and specialized training-free methods on all three tasks, with the largest margins on temporal grounding where baseline MLLMs are weakest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a concept well-studied in the LLM reliability literature—output entropy as a signal of model confidence—from its traditional role as a post-hoc hallucination detector into a proactive, training-free localization mechanism for visual tasks. It departs from the dominant paradigm of task-specific fine-tuning or external model integration (e.g., YOLO/SAM for cropping, CLIP similarity for frame selection, time-aware tokens for grounding) by showing that the MLLM's own uncertainty, computed via Shannon entropy or a binary yes/no confidence score, is sufficient to identify the most informative visual sub-input. The unification of three structurally different tasks (spatial cropping, temporal frame selection, and event boundary detection) under a single 'minimize uncertainty' principle, with Kadane's Algorithm handling the temporal case, is the paper's key conceptual contribution over prior fragmented approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's training-free, model-agnostic nature makes it immediately applicable to any off-the-shelf MLLM without architectural changes, which is practically significant given the rapid turnover of open-source multimodal models. The main open limitation is the multi-pass scoring overhead, though the paper provides concrete mitigations (decoupled scorer, external pre-filters, dynamic stride) and notes the inherent parallelizability of the scoring stage. An unresolved question is whether the uncertainty signal remains well-calibrated as models scale further or as context windows grow, and whether the global-salience bias (exposed by the MLVU Action Count gap) can be addressed without sacrificing the framework's simplicity. More broadly, the work suggests that intrinsic model signals may underpin a wider class of test-time compute strategies beyond localization.
