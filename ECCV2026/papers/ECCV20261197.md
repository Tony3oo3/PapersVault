## Problem

Existing image editing models, despite excelling at object manipulation and style transfer, frequently produce blurry or hallucinated characters when tasked with modifying, translating, or rearranging text embedded in images. The authors attribute these failures to three compounding gaps: the absence of specialized training paradigms for text-centric editing, the lack of large-scale multilingual datasets covering diverse text editing operations, and the absence of a standardized benchmark for systematic evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261197/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Beyond the source image and a natural-language editing instruction, the model consumes a glyph image—a rendered layout of target text strings (white on black) positioned within bounding boxes on a canvas matching the source image dimensions. This glyph image is generated at inference time by a VLM (Qwen3-VL-235B) that first detects existing text regions and then plans the target text content and spatial placement. The output is a full-resolution edited image where only the designated text regions are modified while all non-target pixels are preserved.

## Method

WeEdit employs a two-stage post-training strategy on top of the flow-based MM-DiT (Qwen-Image-Edit). In the SFT stage, LoRA adapters (rank 256) are trained on the multi-modal attention layers so the model learns to condition on the glyph image, which is VAE-encoded and concatenated with the source image latent along the token dimension, injecting explicit spatial and content priors for precise text placement. In the RL stage, DiffusionNFT performs online policy optimization on the forward diffusion process, where K candidate images per conditioning input are scored by four separate VLM-based reward models (instruction adherence, text clarity, background preservation, and relative quality against a reference image). Each reward uses logit-weighted continuous scoring over a 0–9 token set to avoid sparse integer signals, and the composite reward is converted to an optimality probability via intra-group normalization, which weights the positive and negative velocity branches in the contrastive RL objective.

## Results

On the Bilingual Benchmark, WeEdit-RL achieves overall scores of 7.47 (Instruction Adherence), 8.19 (Text Clarity), and 9.21 (Background Preservation), improving over the base model (Qwen-Image-Edit-2509) by +3.98/+2.35/+2.21. On the Multilingual Benchmark (15 languages), WeEdit-RL reaches 6.70/7.10/8.49 in IA/TC/MS, a gain of +3.07/+1.28/+1.82 over the base model. The gains are most pronounced on the hardest operations: on Rearrange and Translate, all open-source baselines score below 1.8 IA, while WeEdit-RL achieves 7.50 and 6.36 IA respectively. WeEdit-RL is surpassed only by the proprietary Gemini-3-Pro-Image (8.58/9.10/8.85 overall on Bilingual).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeEdit-RL outperforms all 11 open-source baselines by a wide margin on both benchmarks. On the Bilingual Benchmark, the strongest open-source competitors (HY-image-Instruct, FireRed-Media-Instruct, PXLK-Image-Edit) achieve only 4.16 overall IA, whereas WeEdit-RL reaches 7.47—a gap of over 3 points. The advantage is most dramatic on Rearrange (7.50 vs. ≤1.73 IA for all open-source models) and Translate (6.36 vs. ≤1.47 IA), where existing models essentially fail. Among proprietary systems, WeEdit-RL trails only Gemini-3-Pro-Image (8.58 IA) and surpasses GPT-Image-1.5 (3.92 IA), Gemini-2-Flash-Image (3.92 IA), and Seedream4.5 (6.29 IA) on overall instruction adherence. On the Multilingual Benchmark, WeEdit-RL (6.70 IA) again leads all open-source models and remains competitive with Gemini-3-Pro-Image (8.05 IA), while several baselines degrade sharply on non-Latin scripts. The ablation confirms that the RL stage adds +0.48 IA and +0.86 TC over SFT-only, and that glyph guidance is essential—applying it at inference without training yields negligible gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow-matching MM-DiT architecture of Qwen-Image-Edit as its base model and adopts DiffusionNFT for the RL policy optimization stage. It departs from prior text-aware image generation work (which uses glyph images, character bounding boxes, or character-level features for *generation*) by repurposing rendered glyph images as explicit spatial priors specifically for the *editing* task, conditioned via LoRA on the multi-modal attention layers. The HTML-based data construction pipeline is a novel departure from prior image editing datasets, which rely on manual annotation or generative-model-produced pairs, by leveraging deterministic browser rendering to guarantee pixel-perfect non-target preservation. The multi-objective RL reward design with separate VLM-based reward models per dimension extends prior single-reward RL approaches for diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of text-centric image editing as a standalone task, providing a 330K-sample multilingual dataset, a standardized bilingual/multilingual benchmark across 8 operations and 15 languages, and a training recipe that closes the gap between open-source and proprietary models. The HTML-based pipeline offers a reusable, scalable recipe for constructing pixel-perfect editing pairs that naturally extends to new languages, which could accelerate data construction for related tasks. Open questions include whether the glyph-guided approach generalizes to video editing with dynamic text, how well it handles highly stylized or artistic typography that resists clean glyph rendering, and whether the VLM-based reward models introduce systematic biases that limit further RL gains.
