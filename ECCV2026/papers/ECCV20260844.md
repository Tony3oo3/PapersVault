## Problem

Low-level vision tasks (restoration, enhancement, dense prediction, stylization) remain fragmented across task-specific models, in-domain-only all-in-one restorers, and visual-prompt methods that require carefully crafted exemplar pairs. No existing framework unifies over 100 sub-tasks across these four domains with flexible text-and-visual prompting at arbitrary resolutions, and the interaction between high-level generative capabilities and fidelity-critical low-level tasks is poorly understood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260844/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a degraded or source RGB image, a natural-language instruction (e.g., 'estimate the Canny edge'), and optionally one or more visual prompt pairs (exemplar input-output images) for tasks hard to specify linguistically such as stylization. It supports arbitrary input resolutions with optimal performance at 1K (1024×1024), and produces task-appropriate outputs ranging from pixel-aligned restored/enhanced images to dense prediction maps (depth, normals, edges) and stylized images. When text and visual prompts conflict, the model prioritizes the text instruction.

## Method

OmniLV initializes from Lumina-Next, a flow-matching DiT, and introduces a co-trained condition adapter that injects encoded condition features into the first half of the network while jointly fine-tuning the base model (unlike ControlNet-style frozen-base designs). A key architectural finding is that text instructions must be encoded separately via Gemma-2B LLM while visual exemplars are encoded independently via the VAE; fusing both in a single MLLM causes visual tokens to dominate the shared feature space and leads to task misinterpretation. In-context visual prompts are integrated via projection-addition (lightweight linear projectors summing into the image latent) rather than token concatenation, which better preserves task-relevant information. Training proceeds in three stages—512² single-task, 512² with in-context learning, then 1024² all tasks—on a 40-million-instance dataset spanning 100+ sub-tasks. A notable empirical finding is that co-training with high-semantic generative/editing tasks consistently degrades pixel-fidelity metrics on restoration and enhancement, motivating a dedicated low-level vision architecture.

## Results

On restoration tasks (Table 5), OmniLV achieves the highest PSNR among all diffusion-based and generalist baselines across all benchmarks, e.g., 22.57 PSNR / 68.95 MUSIQ on Deblur (OLV-T) and 23.53 / 69.23 on Denoise (OLV-T), while remaining competitive with specialized models on BIR (22.36/69.55 vs. DiffBIR 22.77/67.01) and Face restoration (25.04/70.70 vs. CodeFormer 25.15/75.55). On enhancement tasks (Table 6), OmniLV leads all baselines on every reported metric, reaching 22.58/70.54 on low-light (OLV-T) and 21.80/70.91 on saturation adjustment. On dense prediction (Table 7b), OmniLV outperforms generalist baselines (e.g., HED MAE 20.75 vs. PixWizard 25.95; DA-2K accuracy 90.86 vs. 85.49) but trails the specialized Depth Anything model (RMSE 0.525 vs. 0.291). In human-preference Elo ratings, OmniLV scores 1475, substantially above PixWizard (1310) and PromptFix (1000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniLV outperforms all generalist and prompt-based baselines (GenLV, PromptGIP, Painter, PixWizard, PromptFix, AutoDIR, DA-CLIP) on PSNR across every restoration benchmark, with the largest margins on Deblur OLV-T (+0.42 PSNR over GenLV) and Denoise OLV-T (+0.02 PSNR over GenLV but +11 MUSIQ). Against specialized models, OmniLV is competitive but not always superior: MAXIM leads on RealBlur-J (29.99 vs. 28.24 PSNR) and SIDD (39.68 vs. 32.96), while DiffBIR edges out OmniLV on BIR PSNR (22.77 vs. 22.36) and CodeFormer leads on Face MUSIQ (75.55 vs. 70.70). On enhancement tasks, OmniLV dominates all baselines by 5–10 PSNR on low-light and 5–8 MUSIQ on contrast/saturation adjustment. On dense prediction, OmniLV beats PixWizard on every metric (e.g., HED MAE 20.75 vs. 25.95, depth RMSE 0.525 vs. 0.941) but remains behind the specialized Depth Anything model (RMSE 0.291). In instruction-following success rates, OmniLV achieves 100% on all three dense prediction tasks, matching PixWizard on Canny and exceeding it on Depth (100% vs. 93%) and HED (100% vs. 97%), while OmniGen lags at 80%/75%. In human-preference Elo, OmniLV (1475) leads PixWizard (1310) by 165 points and PromptFix (1000) by 475 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniLV builds on the Lumina-Next flow-based diffusion transformer as its generative backbone and adopts the projection-addition mechanism from in-context learning for diffusion models to integrate visual exemplars. Conceptually, it unifies three previously separate lines of work: diffusion-based restoration (StableSR, DiffBIR, SUPIR), all-in-one restoration models (AirNet, PromptIR, OneRestore), and visual/text-prompt-based generalists (PromptGIP, GenLV, PixWizard, PromptFix). The key architectural departure is the separate encoding of text instructions (via Gemma-2B LLM) and visual prompts (via VAE), motivated by the finding that unified MLLM encoding causes visual tokens to dominate the shared feature space and obscure textual task specifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to handle over 100 sub-tasks across four distinct low-level vision domains (restoration, enhancement, dense prediction, stylization) with both text and visual prompts at arbitrary resolutions up to 1K, backed by a 40M-instance training dataset. The negative finding that co-training with high-semantic generative/editing tasks consistently degrades fidelity-critical restoration performance challenges the common assumption that broader task coverage improves generalization, suggesting that low-level vision may require dedicated architectures rather than being subsumed into general-purpose generative models. Open questions include whether the fidelity gap versus specialized models on dense prediction and stylization can be closed through continued fine-tuning, and how the framework scales to video or 3D low-level tasks.
