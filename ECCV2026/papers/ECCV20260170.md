## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving research overwhelmingly centers on dynamic agents and dense geometric cues, leaving the causal role of traffic elements (lights, signs) in planning decisions largely unquantified. This gap persists due to two practical barriers: most public driving datasets lack structured traffic-element annotations, and the architectural heterogeneity of modern E2E planners makes conclusions from any single method difficult to generalize.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260170/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view camera images, LiDAR point clouds, and ego-vehicle state, and produces an ego-vehicle trajectory alongside auxiliary outputs: 3D traffic-element center coordinates (x, y, z) and lane topology adjacency matrices (LCLC and LCTE). The 3D TE coordinates are not directly annotated in most benchmarks; they are extracted at training time by fusing 2D detection boxes, monocular depth estimates (UniDepthv2), and LiDAR geometry, while at inference the planner uses its own predicted TE representations rather than ground-truth annotations. Topology is converted into a short structured language string (e.g., 'a green traffic_light ahead controlling the current ego-lane, which connects 1 straight centerline') and encoded by a frozen BERT-base into compact topology queries.

## Method

The core contribution is a minimal, plug-and-play integration mechanism that requires negligible architectural modification to existing E2E planners. First, an auxiliary 3D traffic-element detection head is added to the multi-task training objective, supervising TE center locations with L1 loss and categories with focal loss, using an independent prediction head and adaptive max-pooling to prevent gradient domination by the dense background. Second, when lane topology is available, an ego-centric filtering step retrieves only the centerlines and traffic elements governing the ego lane, converts them into a structured language sequence, and encodes it with a frozen BERT encoder; the resulting topology queries are concatenated with the TE-enhanced BEV queries and fed into the planning decoder. This 'dual spatial stream' design—spatially aligned TE features plus language-encoded topology—outperforms cross-attention routing and GNN-based graph encoding. The approach is validated across six representative planner families (regression, diffusion, scoring, perception-planning, VLM/VLA) on four benchmarks (nuScenes, NAVSIM-v1, NAVSIM-v2, Bench2Drive), with consistent gains in trajectory accuracy, safety, and rule-compliance metrics at only ~0.3 FPS overhead.

## Results

On NAVSIM-v2 (navhard), the method establishes a new state of the art: DrivoR+SimScale+Ours achieves 57.9 EPDMS, surpassing the previous best (GuideFlow, 46.7) by 11.2 points, while LTF+SimScale+Ours improves from 33.6 to 36.9 (+47% relative). On NAVSIM-v1, consistent gains of +1.1 to +3.5 PDMS are observed across regression (LTF), diffusion (DiffusionDrive), and scoring (DrivoR) paradigms, with DrivoR+SimScale+Ours reaching 95.1 PDMS—above the human-driver reference (94.8). On nuScenes, VAD+Ours reduces average L2 error from 0.72 m to 0.60 m and collision rate from 0.22% to 0.17%, while Orion+Ours improves L2 from 0.34 m to 0.26 m. On closed-loop Bench2Drive, VAD+Ours boosts Driving Score from 42.3 to 56.4 and DriveTransformer-Large+Ours from 63.46 to 68.29, with only ~4 ms additional latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is applied as a plug-and-play add-on to six representative planners (VAD, Orion, LTF, DiffusionDrive, DrivoR, DriveTransformer) across four benchmarks, and consistently improves every baseline it is attached to. On NAVSIM-v2, the strongest configuration (DrivoR+SimScale+Ours, 57.9 EPDMS) surpasses all prior scoring/generative methods including ZTRS (45.5), DiffVLA (45.0), GTRS-Dense (41.9), and GuideFlow (46.7) by 11–16 points. On NAVSIM-v1, DrivoR+SimScale+Ours (95.1 PDMS) exceeds both the rule-based PDM-Closed (89.1) and the human-driver reference (94.8), while LTF+SimScale+Ours (87.6) and DiffusionDrive+SimScale+Ours (89.1) also outperform their respective baselines by 3.5 and 3.1 points. On nuScenes, VAD+Ours (L2 0.60 m, CR 0.17%) beats the original VAD-Base (0.72 m, 0.22%) and approaches the stronger PARA-Drive (0.48 m, 0.25%) in trajectory accuracy while maintaining comparable collision rate; Orion+Ours (L2 0.26 m, CR 0.23%) improves over Orion (0.34 m, 0.37%) and matches or beats VLM-based methods like OmniDrive (0.33 m) at 10× higher throughput. On Bench2Drive, VAD+Ours (Driving Score 56.4) outperforms the original VAD (42.3) by 14.1 points and surpasses UniAD-Base (45.81), while DriveTransformer-Large+Ours (68.29) exceeds the baseline (63.46) and ThinkTwice (62.44). The only metric where performance slightly decreases is Efficiency on Bench2Drive, which the authors attribute to TE awareness correcting over-aggressive behaviors in favor of safer, rule-compliant maneuvers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by systematically introducing traffic element (TE) awareness into end-to-end driving, a dimension that prior E2E work has largely ignored in favor of dynamic agents and dense geometric cues. It repurposes existing components—OpenLane-V2 topology annotations, BERT language encoding, YOLO-based detection, and UniDepthv2 depth estimation—into a unified 3D TE representation and a minimal plug-and-play auxiliary supervision mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from prior topology-prediction work (e.g., TopoMLP, GCN-based methods) that optimized benchmark scores without validating downstream planning impact, and from VLM-based scene understanding that captures TE semantics at high runtime cost, by showing that a lightweight explicit TE head plus language-encoded ego-centric topology suffices across diverse planner families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a minimal TE auxiliary loss plus topology conditioning yields consistent gains across regression, diffusion, scoring, and VLA paradigms on four benchmarks suggests traffic-element reasoning is a broadly underutilized signal that any E2E driving system should incorporate. The new SOTA on NAVSIM-v2 (e.g., +47% EPDMS for LTF+SimScale+Ours) demonstrates scalability with data engines. Open questions include whether the pseudo-labeling pipeline for NAVSIM (trained on OpenLane-V2) generalizes to unseen TE categories or degraded weather, whether the language-based topology encoding scales to highly complex multi-lane highway topologies, and whether the gains persist in fully reactive closed-loop settings beyond Bench2Drive's scenario-isolated routes.
