## Problem

Camera-controlled interactive video generation systems roll out growing sequences of latent video frames, entangling stochastic state transition with high-frequency appearance synthesis in a single process. This entanglement makes long-horizon pose consistency and revisitation self-consistency increasingly difficult to maintain. The paper asks whether replacing the rollout variable with a compact, renderable implicit scene state can decouple these two processes and improve consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260167/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each interaction step the system receives the current posed view (256×256 RGB image with SE(3) camera pose), a short future camera trajectory (sequence of poses), and a small set of retrieved history frames from a memory bank. The NIS encoder maps all of these into a unified token modality: sparse future poses become partial NIS tokens (with zeroed RGB), the reference image becomes a pose+reference partial NIS, and history frames become memory NIS tokens. The output is a fixed-length NIS latent (1024 tokens × 64 channels) that is rendered by a frozen decoder into future observations at any queried pose, rather than producing a fixed-length video clip.

## Method

NeuWorld factorizes each rollout step into stochastic NIS state sampling and deterministic pose-conditioned rendering. A transformer VAE (NIS-VAE) encodes sparse posed views into NIS tokens via Plücker ray embeddings and decodes target views, while a set-based diffusion transformer (NIS-DiT) with a U-shaped backbone and flow-matching objective samples the next local NIS state. The key architectural choice is reusing the frozen NIS encoder as a unified conditioner: camera-only cues, reference images, and retrieved history are all mapped into partial or memory NIS tokens, injected via channel-wise and token-wise concatenation respectively, avoiding separate image, camera, or reconstruction encoders. A weak-to-strong training curriculum (pose-only → pose+reference → memory) stabilizes from-scratch learning, and anti-drift augmentation (stochastic image degradation plus latent condition noise ramped over interaction steps) bridges the train-test gap for generated history. Geometry-aware retrieval combines pose distance, field-of-view overlap, and recency to select relevant history frames for loop-closure consistency.

## Results

On Re10K forward trajectory generation (50th frame), NeuWorld achieves the best score across all six metrics: LPIPS 0.431, PSNR 15.11, SSIM 0.476, FID 34.55, R_dist 0.026°, and T_dist 0.098. On the cycle-revisitation protocol (Re10K), NeuWorld attains the best revisit self-consistency (LPIPS 0.208, SSIM 0.692, R_dist 0.128°) and the lowest return-path translation error (T_dist 0.382), while running at 3.24 min per forward-and-return trajectory—approximately 14× faster than VMem and Gen3C (47.62 min each). On DL3DV, NeuWorld achieves the best long-horizon translation consistency (T_dist 0.274 at the 80th frame) and the best return-path pose errors (R_dist 0.410°, T_dist 0.507) in the cycle setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuWorld outperforms all five baselines on the majority of pose-consistency metrics (R_dist, T_dist) across both datasets and both protocols, with particularly large margins on long-horizon translation drift (e.g., T_dist 0.141 vs. 0.295–0.609 at the 200th frame on Re10K). On revisit self-consistency (Re10K), NeuWorld achieves the best LPIPS (0.208 vs. 0.242 for VMem, the next best) and SSIM (0.692 vs. 0.674), while Gen3C and VMem lead on revisit PSNR but at 14× the runtime. Matrix-Game 2.0 is the only faster baseline (1.33 min) but degrades severely in image quality (PSNR 6.72, SSIM 0.092 on return path) and pose consistency (R_dist 0.594°). On DL3DV, NeuWorld trades some image-quality metrics (LPIPS 0.638 vs. Gen3C's 0.479 on return path) for substantially better pose consistency (R_dist 0.410° vs. 0.938° for Gen3C) and competitive runtime (1.14 min). Overall, NeuWorld's advantage is most pronounced in geometric/pose consistency and inference efficiency, while reconstruction-based baselines (Gen3C, VMem) retain an edge on pixel-level fidelity at the cost of much slower inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuWorld builds directly on the LVSM-style latent scene representation (fixed-length token sets encoding posed views into a renderable latent) and the DiT architecture for conditional generation, but repurposes these components in a fundamentally different role: rather than using latent scene tokens solely as an NVS condition, the paper promotes them to the *state variable* of interactive rollout. This departs from the dominant paradigm in interactive video generation (SEVA, ViewCrafter, Gen3C, VMem, Matrix-Game 2.0), which rolls out growing sequences of latent video frames and entangles state transition with high-frequency appearance synthesis. The unified conditioning design—reusing the NIS encoder to map camera, reference-image, and history cues into the same NIS token modality—also distinguishes NeuWorld from prior systems that rely on heterogeneous image, camera, or reconstruction encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factorization of interactive generation into stochastic NIS-state sampling and deterministic pose-conditioned rendering offers a clean conceptual alternative to frame-latent rollout, and the 14× inference speedup over reconstruction-based baselines (VMem, Gen3C) makes long-horizon interactive exploration more practical. Training entirely from scratch on public posed-view data without pretrained video backbones or auxiliary 3D reconstructors demonstrates that the compact implicit state carries sufficient geometric structure for consistent exploration. Key limitations include the restriction to static scenes at 256×256 resolution, the local (non-global) nature of the NIS state that must be re-anchored at each step, and evaluation on only two datasets with 100 trajectories each. Extending the formulation to dynamic environments, richer action spaces, and persistent global scene composition are the natural next steps the authors identify.
