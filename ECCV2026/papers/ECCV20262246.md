## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from severe performance degradation under low-bit quantization due to non-stationary activation distributions and prominent outliers that shift across denoising timesteps. Existing QAT methods like EfficientDM lack mechanisms to suppress these activation outliers, and the Double Hadamard Transformation used in prior work is incompatible with integer convolution and amplifies weight outliers, further degrading quantized performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262246/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The quantized diffusion model takes Gaussian noise in a compressed latent space (plus a class label for conditional generation) and iteratively denoises it over 20–100 DDIM steps to produce 256×256 RGB images. During each denoising step, activations are transformed by a block-diagonal Hadamard matrix before symmetric integer quantization, and all subsequent matrix multiplications and convolutions are executed entirely in integer arithmetic, with the Hadamard normalization factor absorbed into floating-point scale parameters.

## Method

HQ-DM introduces a Single Hadamard Transformation that applies the Hadamard matrix only to activations (not weights), avoiding the weight-outlier amplification and convolution incompatibility of the conventional Double Hadamard scheme. A block-diagonal Hadamard matrix handles non-power-of-2 channel dimensions, and the 2^(−k/2) normalization is folded into the quantization scale so that the core computation (XH)_int · H · W_int is pure integer arithmetic, enabling hardware-efficient inference including integer convolutions (Hadamard applied along the width dimension). The framework combines this transformation with LoRA-based distillation (freezing pretrained weights and training low-rank adapters) and timestep-aware learnable quantization scales (LSQ), optimizing a MSE distillation loss between a full-precision teacher and the quantized student. Symmetric activation quantization is used throughout for hardware compatibility, and the entire pipeline is trained data-free via the distillation objective.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with LDM-4 (20 DDIM steps), HQ-DM achieves IS 248.30 / FID 6.58 / sFID 9.60 / Precision 84.44% under W4A4 quantization, surpassing the prior SOTA EfficientDM (IS 220.20) by 12.76% in IS. Under the more aggressive W4A3 setting, HQ-DM reaches IS 49.62 and FID 33.47, a 467.73% IS improvement over EfficientDM (IS 8.74, FID 99.78). At W8A8, HQ-DM matches or slightly exceeds EfficientDM on IS (363.22 vs 362.34) and sFID (7.76 vs 8.04) while using hardware-friendly symmetric quantization. On DiT-XL (ImageNet 256), HQ-DM achieves IS 355.74 vs EfficientDM's 221.07 under W4A4, a 60.92% relative IS gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HQ-DM is compared against five baselines: two PTQ methods (Q-Diffusion, PTQD), one PTQ method with temporal modeling (TFMQ-DM), and two QAT methods (EfficientDM, QuEST). Against the primary SOTA baseline EfficientDM, HQ-DM wins on IS, FID, and Precision at W4A4 (248.30 vs 220.20 IS; 6.58 vs 6.63 FID; 84.44% vs 80.97% Precision) and dominates overwhelmingly at W4A3 (49.62 vs 8.74 IS; 33.47 vs 99.78 FID). Against QuEST at W4A4, HQ-DM achieves higher IS (248.30 vs 202.45) and comparable FID (6.58 vs 5.98). PTQ-based methods (Q-Diffusion, PTQD, TFMQ-DM) collapse at 4-bit activation quantization (IS < 3 on ImageNet; FID > 100 on LSUN-Bedrooms), while HQ-DM maintains usable generation quality. On LSUN-Bedrooms, HQ-DM reduces FID by 5.56 (W8A4), 3.66 (W4A4), and 4.92 (W4A3) over EfficientDM, and on LSUN-Churches it cuts FID by 3.19 (W4A4) and 6.30 (W4A3). On DiT-XL, HQ-DM outperforms EfficientDM by 60.92% relative IS and 13.79% absolute Precision at W4A4, confirming generality beyond U-Net architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LoRA-based distillation framework of EfficientDM [9] and the timestep-aware LSQ quantization scales, while adapting the Hadamard transformation idea from QuaRot [1] and Xi et al. [39] to diffusion model quantization. The key departure is replacing the conventional Double Hadamard Transformation (which applies Hadamard to both activations and weights) with a Single Hadamard Transformation applied only to activations, motivated by the observation that transforming already low-outlier weight matrices actually amplifies their quantization error and breaks integer convolution compatibility. This positions HQ-DM as a targeted fix to a specific failure mode of prior Hadamard-based quantization methods when applied to the convolution-heavy U-Net architectures common in diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hardware-friendly design (symmetric quantization, integer-only matrix multiplication and convolution, no dequantization step) makes HQ-DM a practical candidate for on-device diffusion model deployment, and the 467.73% relative IS gain at W4A3 demonstrates that activation outlier suppression via a single orthogonal transform can be the dominant factor at ultra-low activation bit-widths. However, absolute quality at W4A3 (IS ≈ 49.6 vs. FP 365.35) remains far from full precision, and the method has only been validated on 256×256 ImageNet/LSUN with U-Net and DiT-XL backbones; its behavior on larger text-to-image models (e.g., SDXL-scale) or on video diffusion remains open. A further open question is whether the timestep-aware LSQ scales can be further optimized jointly with the Hadamard block size to close the remaining gap at 3-bit activations.
