## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods rely on context-independent semantic representations, treating objects as isolated entities and leaving structured spatial relationships underexplored for contextual refinement. This limitation makes it difficult to distinguish semantically similar categories (e.g., 'curtain' vs. 'shower curtain') that are only resolvable through awareness of surrounding object relationships. Additionally, introducing scene graphs into this setting faces two challenges: the absence of large-scale relationship annotations in open-vocabulary settings, and a feature granularity dilemma where naively mixing dense geometric features with CLIP instance embeddings causes severe cross-modal interference during graph aggregation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB-D sequences and 3D point clouds (obtained from SLAM or dense reconstruction) along with free-form natural-language queries, and produces point-level semantic labels for open-vocabulary segmentation. A key intermediate artifact is a relationship-aware 3D scene graph whose nodes carry decoupled dense LSeg features and CLIP instance embeddings, and whose edges are encoded as concatenated CLIP text embeddings of SVO triplets inferred by a VLM. The graph construction is fully automated via multi-view VLM chain-of-thought reasoning, requiring no manual relationship annotations.

## Method

RelGraphOV constructs a 3D scene graph by extracting 3D object proposals from RGB-D sequences, selecting representative keyframes via a composite scoring function, and using a VLM with Set-of-Mark prompting and chain-of-thought reasoning to infer and validate inter-object relationships, pruning geometrically implausible edges and encoding valid ones as SVO-triplet CLIP embeddings. The core architecture is an Adaptive Gated Dual-Stream Contextual GAT that decouples dense LSeg features (main stream) from CLIP instance embeddings (auxiliary stream), performs edge-feature-guided cross-attention message passing independently in each stream, and uses a learnable gating mechanism to selectively inject auxiliary semantics into the main stream while preserving the auxiliary stream's pure CLIP priors. A virtual super-node provides global context injection via cross-attention to expand the receptive field. Training employs a hierarchical contrastive loss enforcing self-consistency, intra-class consistency, and inter-class separation, combined with dual-alignment losses that anchor each stream to its original modality-specific prior to prevent catastrophic forgetting during contextual refinement.

## Results

On ScanNetV2 (20-class), RelGraphOV achieves 58.4% mIoU and 73.4% mAcc, outperforming the strongest prior open-vocabulary method OV3D (57.3% mIoU) and OpenScene-2D (50.0% mIoU) by +1.1 and +8.4 mIoU respectively. On the more challenging ScanNet200 (200-class) benchmark, RelGraphOV obtains 14.5% mIoU and 26.1% mAcc, surpassing Mosaic3D (12.4/25.1) and MaskClustering (11.8/24.7). Zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on ScanNet++ (100-class) yields 20.9% mIoU, and on the synthetic Replica dataset (51-class) it reaches 22.7% mIoU, both substantially ahead of prior open-vocabulary baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanNetV2, RelGraphOV (58.4 mIoU) beats the closest competitor OV3D (57.3) by +1.1 mIoU and the distillation-based OpenScene-2D (50.0) by +8.4 mIoU; it also surpasses CUA-O3D (55.3) by +3.1 mIoU. On ScanNet200, the advantage widens: RelGraphOV (14.5 mIoU) outperforms Mosaic3D (12.4) by +2.1, MaskClustering (11.8) by +2.7, and OpenScene-2D (8.8) by +5.7, with the largest gains on common and tail categories where relational context resolves ambiguity. In zero-shot transfer to ScanNet++, RelGraphOV (20.9 mIoU) exceeds OpenScene-2D (13.3) by +7.6 and CUA-O3D (9.3) by +11.6. On Replica, it achieves 22.7 mIoU versus OpenScene-2D's 12.9 (+9.8) and even surpasses the non-zero-shot OpenNeRF (20.4) by +2.3, though OpenNeRF retains a slight edge on tail-category mAcc (17.6 vs. 9.2). The per-category analysis on ScanNetV2 highlights a concrete win: RelGraphOV correctly segments 'shower curtain' at 64.2% IoU where both OpenScene and CUA-O3D score 0.0%, demonstrating the value of VLM-reasoned spatial relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation (OpenScene, CUA-O3D, OV3D, Mosaic3D), which transfers 2D vision-language features into 3D but treats objects as context-independent entities, and 3D scene graph methods (SceneGraphFusion, Lang3DSG, Open3DSG), which model object relationships but target graph prediction or grounding rather than dense segmentation. RelGraphOV departs from both by using VLM-reasoned scene graphs purely as a *contextual refinement structure* for open-vocabulary features, rather than as a prediction target. Architecturally, it builds on MaskClustering for proposal generation and on LSeg/CLIP as feature backbones, but introduces a dual-stream GAT that decouples dense geometric and instance-level semantic propagation to avoid the feature interference that arises from naively mixing them in a single GNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation—semantic ambiguity that is resolvable only through spatial context (e.g., curtain vs. shower curtain)—and provides a practical, annotation-free pipeline for constructing relational priors via VLM reasoning. The zero-shot generalization to ScanNet++ and Replica suggests the learned relational refinement transfers beyond the training distribution. However, the method is limited to static reconstructed scenes, depends on the quality of the VLM for both edge reasoning and node annotation, and the dual-stream ablation gap (0.5 mIoU) is modest, leaving open whether the feature-interference hypothesis is the dominant source of improvement. Extending to dynamic scenes with temporally consistent graph updates and integrating newer dense vision-language backbones are natural next steps.
