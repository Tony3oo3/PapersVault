## Problem

Modern video diffusion models produce unintelligible sign language video because the fine-grained hand and face structures that carry linguistic meaning are aggressively compressed in the patchified latent space, while existing sign-language-specific models overfit to narrow training distributions and fail to generalize across signers, scales, and environments. No prior approach simultaneously achieves high visual fidelity, broad generalizability, and actual comprehensibility to Deaf viewers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261827/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single reference image (defining signer appearance) and a full-body 2D keypoint sequence (specifying sign motion) as input, and generates an 81-frame sign language video at 832×480 resolution. The keypoint sequence is additionally rendered as three separate 256×256 regional crops (face, left hand, right hand) that feed the local adapters at higher spatial resolution than the backbone's latent grid, providing the structural detail that the compressed latent space loses.

## Method

SignRefine freezes a Wan2.1-1.3B-Fun-Control video DiT backbone and injects two trainable local cross-attention adapters (one for face, one for hands) at staggered block positions to avoid simultaneous multi-adapter residuals. Each adapter receives regional keypoint crops processed by a ResNet-style encoder with CoordConv coordinate channels for spatial grounding, compressed temporally via causal 1D convolutions to match the backbone's T/4 latent resolution. A binary spatial mask derived from bounding boxes routes foreground latent patches to attend on the regional motion tokens while background patches are directed to a learned sink token, preventing feature bleed into unrelated areas. Training uses a spatially non-uniform MSE loss that weights hand and face regions 10× relative to background, compensating for their small area in latent space while preserving global coherence.

## Results

On the NVSign test set, SignRefine achieves hand MPJPE of 6.602 (right) and 6.638 (left), a ~30% reduction over the strongest baseline Wan-FC (9.565 and 9.341), and face MPJPE of 1.770 vs. 1.835. On Phoenix-2014T, hand MPJPE drops to 4.582/5.101 (vs. 5.754/5.719 for Wan-FC) and face MPJPE to 0.916 (vs. 1.151). In the user study with 15 BSL signers, SignRefine attains a mean rank of 1.22 (vs. 1.89 for Wan-FC) and is preferred in over 83% of head-to-head comparisons; in the comprehension study, keyword recall reaches 0.54 vs. 0.30 for Wan-FC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SignRefine outperforms all four baselines across every region and dataset. Against the strongest baseline Wan-FC (a general-purpose DiT video model with full-body skeleton conditioning), the gains are most pronounced on hands: ~30% MPJPE reduction on NVSign (6.602 vs. 9.565 RH) and ~20% on Phoenix-2014T (4.582 vs. 5.754 RH), while face improvements are more modest (~5–20%). Wan-VACE, another general DiT model, consistently underperforms Wan-FC by a wide margin (e.g., 15.904 vs. 9.565 RH MPJPE on NVSign), suggesting its VACE conditioning is less effective for fine-grained articulation. Sign-specific models SignViP and SignGAN perform worst overall; SignViP suffers a severe domain shift collapse on out-of-distribution data (hand MPJPE roughly doubles from Phoenix-2014T to NVSign), while SignGAN, trained on a single fixed appearance in a studio, shows the weakest generalization. In the perceptual user study, SignRefine is preferred over every baseline in >83% of head-to-head comparisons, with a clear hierarchy: Ours > Wan-FC > Wan-VACE > SignGAN ≈ SignViP. The comprehension study further confirms that the visual improvements translate to linguistic intelligibility, with SignRefine achieving 0.54 keyword recall vs. 0.30 for Wan-FC and a lower failure rate (0.17 vs. 0.27).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two underperforming lines of work: sign-language-specific generators (SignGAN, SignViP) that overfit to narrow training distributions, and general-purpose human video DiTs (Wan, Animate Anyone) that produce plausible motion but fail on fine-grained hand and face articulation. Rather than training a sign language model from scratch or fine-tuning the full backbone, SignRefine freezes a pretrained Wan-Fun-Control DiT and injects high-resolution regional conditions (face, left hand, right hand) through lightweight cross-attention adapters with CoordConv spatial grounding and masked attention with sink tokens. This adapter-on-frozen-backbone strategy is conceptually related to ControlNet-style conditioning but is specifically designed for DiT architectures and introduces the spatial masking mechanism to prevent feature bleed into background regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NVSign dataset (78.1 hours, 2,184 signers, ~40% multi-party conversation) fills a critical gap in sign language research by providing natively produced, visually diverse content that prior corpora lack, opening research directions in multi-signer dynamics and turn-taking. The local-adapter-with-spatial-grounding pattern is a reusable recipe for any task where a strong pretrained video model needs targeted refinement of small, linguistically critical regions without degrading global quality. Key limitations remain: generation is still minutes-per-clip (no real-time use), motion blur from 25 fps training data smears fast hand movements, and the pipeline depends on off-the-shelf pose estimators whose errors propagate. Whether this adapter approach transfers to other fine-grained articulation tasks (e.g., lip-sync, finger-spelling at higher resolution) or scales to multi-signer generation from a single reference remains open.
