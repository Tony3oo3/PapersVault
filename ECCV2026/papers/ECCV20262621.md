## Problem

Active satellite sensors like EarthCARE retrieve accurate ice cloud vertical geometry but observe only a narrow nadir track covering ~1.6% of pixels, while passive imagers like VIIRS cover the full globe daily but lack direct sensitivity to cloud vertical structure. This paper asks whether dense per-pixel cloud geometry can be learned from such extremely sparse spatial supervision, and how to provide calibrated, distribution-free uncertainty for the resulting dense predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262621/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 64×64 pixel patches (~48 km²) of 10 VIIRS channels (five thermal-infrared brightness temperatures, four shortwave reflectance bands, and solar zenith angle at 750 m nadir resolution) concatenated with a 104-dimensional ERA5 atmospheric profile (temperature, specific humidity, relative humidity, geopotential height on 26 pressure levels). It outputs eight cloud geometry targets per pixel—centroid/top/base height, peak ice concentration level, geometric thickness, core IWC, column-mean IWC, and log IWP—each with a 90% calibrated prediction interval. Training labels exist only along the ~64-pixel-wide EarthCARE track within each patch, yet the network predicts at all 4,096 pixels.

## Method

A 14M-parameter ConvNextUNet with three encoder stages (96/192/384 channels, 7×7 depthwise separable convolutions, 4× inverted bottlenecks) and U-Net skip connections predicts three conditional quantiles (τ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9}) per target via pinball loss, computed only at supervised pixels. ERA5 profiles are injected additively at the bottleneck through a 2-layer MLP, and a linear-attention module captures global dependencies at the coarsest resolution. The best configuration (G-QE) averages quantile predictions from five independently trained models with D4 test-time augmentation, then applies conformalized quantile regression (CQR) on a held-out calibration set to produce distribution-free 90% coverage guarantees. A key empirical finding is that R², coverage, and interval width remain statistically constant across pixel distance from the supervision track, confirming the model performs per-pixel spectral retrieval rather than spatial interpolation from nearby labels. Physics ordering constraints (base ≤ centroid ≤ top) are enforced via zero-cost post-hoc projection at inference.

## Results

The best configuration (G-QE, a five-member quantile ensemble) achieves R²=0.742 with MAE=6.08 across eight cloud geometry targets, with calibrated 90% prediction intervals (PICP=0.908) and the sharpest mean interval width (MPIW=25.1) among all methods. Prediction quality is invariant to distance from the EarthCARE supervision track (R²=0.730 at 0–1 px vs. 0.731 at 5–8 px), confirming per-pixel spectral retrieval rather than spatial interpolation. Per-target, geometric heights (centroid, top, base) reach R²>0.82, while cloud thickness is the hardest target at R²=0.407. Globally, the system produces 609M dense predictions from one day of VIIRS data in 2.4 hours on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-QE outperforms all seven alternative uncertainty methods on R² (0.742 vs. 0.732 for G-DE and 0.731 for G-Q) and produces the sharpest calibrated intervals (MPIW=25.1 vs. 27.9 for G-DE and 26.8 for G-Q). G-DE matches G-QE in point accuracy but is severely miscalibrated before CQR (raw PICP=0.371 vs. 0.796 for G-QE), resulting in wider post-calibration intervals and a much weaker error–uncertainty correlation (ρ=0.242 vs. 0.415). The ConvNextUNet backbone (G-B, R²=0.716) improves over the best MLP baseline (0.641) by +7.5 percentage points, confirming that convolutional spatial processing captures patterns beyond local per-pixel statistics. Against the physics-informed BT regression baseline (R²=0.590, matching operational MODIS cloud-top retrieval inputs), G-QE achieves a +15.2% R² gain, quantifying the benefit of learned multi-target joint estimation. On the hardest target (cloud thickness), ensembling provides the largest relative improvement: R² rises from 0.339 (G-B) to 0.370 (G-Q) to 0.407 (G-QE), though all methods remain well below the geometric height targets (R²>0.82).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: physics-based cloud retrieval from passive imagery (lookup-table methods, IceCloudNet), deep uncertainty quantification (deep ensembles, MC-Dropout, evidential regression, quantile regression), and conformal prediction (CQR). It adapts the deep-ensemble averaging strategy to quantile outputs rather than Gaussian parameters, and applies CQR post-hoc to obtain distribution-free coverage guarantees—neither of which had been combined for satellite cloud retrieval. The sparse-to-dense framing draws an explicit parallel to LiDAR depth completion and GEDI canopy-height estimation, but departs by explicitly studying the cross-track generalization mechanism and providing calibrated (not just point) predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The operational deployment (609M dense predictions in 2.4 h on one GPU) makes calibrated 3D cloud geometry available at planetary scale for the first time, directly addressing the cloud-feedback uncertainty that dominates climate projections. The cross-track invariance result—if it holds beyond the tested ~6 km range—implies that any sparse active-sensor pattern covering diverse atmospheric conditions suffices, potentially reducing the required active-sensor observation time. Key open questions include whether the 3.8% deep-convective under-coverage can be fixed via subgroup-conditional CQR, whether the thickness ceiling (R²≈0.4) is truly information-theoretic or can be broken by multi-angle temporal compositing, and whether the finding generalizes to liquid/mixed-phase clouds requiring additional radar supervision.
