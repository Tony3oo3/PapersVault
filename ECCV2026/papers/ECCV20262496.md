## Problem

As modern generative models achieve near-perfect semantic alignment with real images, the decisive forensic cues for AIGI detection shift into subtle non-semantic residuals, yet no prior work has identified a universal, architecture-agnostic structural signature in this residual space. Existing detectors either exploit fragile low-level pixel artifacts or operate at the data-alignment level without revealing the underlying geometric mechanism that distinguishes synthetic from natural images across diverse generator paradigms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262496/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (randomly cropped to 224×224 during training, center-cropped with padding at inference) and outputs a real/fake binary label via a linear classifier. The image is first passed through a frozen DINOv3 ViT-H+/16 encoder to extract N patch tokens and a global [CLS] token; the [CLS] token is retained as a global context anchor and concatenated with the pooled low-rank residual feature before the final classification head.

## Method

LoRC decouples detection from fragile spatial artifacts by explicitly modeling a geometric signature: a systematic low-rank collapse in the semantic-residual orthogonal subspace induced by the final decoding bottleneck of generative models. The pipeline has three stages: (1) Semantic Decomposition projects frozen DINOv3 patch tokens onto the orthogonal complement of the normalized [CLS] token, isolating the residual subspace where real/fake discrepancy concentrates; (2) a Low-Rank Attention Block performs self-attention in a rank-r bottleneck (r=32 ≪ D), forcing the network to attend only to the dominant collapsed directions while suppressing high-dimensional noise; (3) an auxiliary Subspace Separation Loss minimizes the Frobenius inner product between normalized covariance descriptors of real and fake residual patches, explicitly widening the geometric gap between the high-rank natural manifold and the collapsed synthetic subspace. The backbone is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 16) on semantically aligned pairs (MS-COCO images and their SD2.1 VAE reconstructions), ensuring the model learns the low-rank collapse signal rather than content-level differences.

## Results

LoRC achieves a 97.2% average balanced accuracy across 7 benchmarks, surpassing the second-best method (DDA, 90.2%) by 7.0 percentage points. On the zero-shot T2I-CoReBench benchmark covering 39 unseen generators, LoRC reaches 97.0% accuracy versus DDA's 91.1%. On the in-the-wild WildRF dataset, LoRC attains 97.6% balanced accuracy, outperforming DDA by 7.3%. The method also achieves near-perfect scores on Synthbuster (99.9%) and DRCT-2M (99.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRC outperforms all 9 baselines on every benchmark. The closest competitor, DDA (NeurIPS'25), achieves 90.2% average balanced accuracy; LoRC exceeds it by 7.0 points on average, with the largest gaps on Synthbuster (+9.8), AIGCDetectionBenchmark (+8.5), and WildRF (+7.3). Among reconstruction-based methods, DRCT (ICML'24, 71.0% avg) and AlignedForensics (ICLR'25, 73.2% avg) are the strongest, yet both fall 24–26 points short of LoRC, particularly on in-the-wild and zero-shot settings where their scores drop to 50–58% and 42–80% respectively. Frequency-domain and upsampling-artifact methods (NPR, SAFE) and frozen-feature classifiers (UnivFD, FatFormer, AIDE) all cluster around 46–55% average, with several collapsing below 15% on T2I-CoReBench, indicating severe cross-architecture fragility. C2P-CLIP (AAAI'25, 66.4% avg) is the strongest prompt-based baseline but still trails LoRC by 30.8 points on average and by 26.7 points specifically on T2I-CoReBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes insights from three distinct research threads: (1) the semantic-decoupling line (NS-Net, VIB-Net, UnivFD) that motivates projecting features away from dominant semantic directions; (2) the observation by Liu et al. [32] that the final decoding component of diverse generators imprints transferable traces; and (3) low-rank parameterization ideas (LoRA) repurposed here as an inductive bias for the detector. Rather than building on a single prior method, LoRC carves new ground by providing a geometric explanation—low-rank collapse in the orthogonal complement of the [CLS] token—for why the final decoding stage is a shared bottleneck, and by introducing a Subspace Separation Loss that directly shapes the covariance geometry of real versus fake residual subspaces, a formulation absent from prior AIGI detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of low-rank collapse as an architecture-agnostic, JPEG-robust forensic signature offers a principled alternative to fragile pixel-level or frequency-domain cues, potentially shifting the field toward manifold-geometry-based detection. The 7% average improvement and 97% zero-shot accuracy across 39 unseen generators (spanning diffusion, autoregressive, unified, and closed-source models) suggest this geometric signal may persist even as generator architectures evolve. Open questions include whether the signature survives for generators without a clear terminal decoding stage (e.g., end-to-end pixel-space models), whether adversarial training can specifically mask the low-rank structure, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D content where the 'final decoding' analogy is less clear.
