## Problem

Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting faces a fundamental tension: deformation-based approaches preserve temporal correspondence but over-smooth high-frequency dynamics and misinterpret appearance changes as geometric motion, while 4D-primitive methods capture fine visual detail but suffer from severe temporal over-parameterization, breaking object identity and inflating storage. No existing framework simultaneously maintains long-term motion consistency, captures fine dynamic appearance, and produces a compact representation suitable for downstream tasks like 4D segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260083/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (e.g., Technicolor at 2048×1088, Neu3D at 1352×1014) or monocular video (NeRF-DS) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses; for the segmentation task it additionally requires 2D SAM masks as supervision. Outputs include real-time novel view renderings (161–217 FPS), a decomposed 4D Gaussian scene representation with only ~165k dynamic primitives (vs. 4.2M in 4DGS, reducing storage from 2.6 GB to 215 MB), 4D segmentation masks, and 3D motion trajectories. Notably, segmentation operates exclusively on the persistent Gaussian subset, enabling 32-dimensional feature rendering at 204 FPS—nearly 10× faster than prior tracking-based methods.

## Method

Multi4D decomposes the scene into three functionally specialized Gaussian subsets—static (G_s), persistent dynamic (G_d) governed by a HexPlane-based geometric deformation field, and transient (G_t) as 4D spatiotemporal Gaussians with 4D spherical harmonics—that jointly render in a single depth-sorted pass where shared transmittance couples gradients and induces residual-driven competition. A bottom-up training strategy initializes subsets inversely proportional to expressive capacity (dense COLMAP points for G_s, sparse random points for G_d, empty G_t), then progressively activates velocity-aware periodic lifting to promote active persistent Gaussians into the transient set with momentum inheritance, and mask-aware utility-based pruning to suppress cross-set redundancy. Self-supervised dynamic-static decomposition via a learned mask logit on G_d enforces spatial separation without ground-truth tracking labels, while an SSIM-based diversity loss discourages overlapping modeling. Training proceeds in two phases (subset formation, then rendering refinement), and for 4D segmentation the persistent subset is frozen and augmented with 32-dim learnable features supervised by contrastive distillation against SAM masks.

## Results

On the Technicolor multi-view benchmark, Multi4D achieves a mean PSNR of 34.30 dB and DSSIM of 0.037, outperforming the strongest Gaussian baseline STG (33.35 dB) by 0.95 dB while rendering at 161 FPS (vs. 86 FPS for STG). On Neu3D, it reaches 32.30 dB mean PSNR at 217 FPS, surpassing STG (32.04 dB, 140 FPS) and 4DGS (31.57 dB, 114 FPS). For monocular reconstruction on NeRF-DS, Multi4D attains 24.74 dB mean PSNR, exceeding the best deformation-based method Def-3DGS (23.96 dB) and substantially outperforming 4D-primitive methods (4DGS: 21.51 dB, STG: 22.54 dB). On 4D segmentation (Neu3D-Mask), Multi4D achieves 0.9142 mIoU with 204 FPS feature rendering, beating TRASE (0.8932 mIoU, 21 FPS) by 2.1 mIoU with a ~10× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi4D outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated task. Among deformation-based methods, it surpasses STG (the strongest baseline) by 0.95 dB on Technicolor and 0.26 dB on Neu3D, while also exceeding E-D3DGS (32.89 dB), 4DGaussian (30.86 dB), Def-3DGS (30.95 dB), and DeGauss (31.52 dB on Neu3D) by 1.4–3.4 dB. Against 4D-primitive methods, Multi4D beats 4DGS by 2.23 dB on Technicolor and 0.73 dB on Neu3D, and STG by 0.95 dB and 0.26 dB respectively, while using 25× fewer dynamic Gaussians (165k vs. 4.2M for 4DGS). On the monocular NeRF-DS benchmark, Multi4D (24.74 dB) clearly dominates 4D-primitive baselines that degrade under sparse supervision (4DGS: 21.51 dB, STG: 22.54 dB) and also edges out deformation-based Def-3DGS (23.96 dB) and 4DGaussian (22.79 dB). For 4D segmentation, Multi4D (0.9142 mIoU) beats TRASE (0.8932 mIoU), SA4D (0.8832 mIoU), and OpenGaussian (0.8178 mIoU), while rendering semantic features at 204 FPS versus TRASE's 21 FPS. NeRF-based baselines (HyperReel, DyNeRF, NeRFPlayer, HyperNeRF) are consistently outperformed by 1.5–5.7 dB in PSNR and are orders of magnitude slower (≤4 FPS vs. 161–217 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

Multi4D sits at the intersection of two opposing families of dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting: deformation-based methods (Def-3DGS, 4DGaussian, DeGauss) that warp canonical Gaussians via neural or grid-based deformation fields, and 4D-primitive methods (4DGS, STG) that model dynamics as spatiotemporal Gaussian hyper-cylinders. Rather than committing to one formulation, it borrows the HexPlane-based deformation field from the former for persistent geometric tracking and the 4D slicing + 4DSH machinery from the latter for transient appearance, then unifies them under a single differentiable rasterizer where three subsets (static, persistent dynamic, transient) compete via residual-driven allocation. The dynamic-static decomposition strategy follows DeGauss, the unified rendering builds on Hybrid 3D-4D GS, and the downstream 4D segmentation adapts TRASE's contrastive feature distillation — but the competitive multi-level allocation with velocity-aware lifting and mask-aware pruning is the paper's own architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25× reduction in dynamic primitives (4.2M → 165k) while simultaneously improving PSNR over 4DGS, and the 10× segmentation speedup (21 → 204 FPS) with only 13k vs. 624k dynamic Gaussians, make this a practically significant efficiency result for real-time dynamic scene applications. The conceptual framing — that rigid motion and transient appearance should be modeled by structurally different primitives that compete rather than a single monolithic representation — offers a clean inductive-bias argument that could inform future hybrid representations. However, the paper acknowledges the lack of explicit attribute compression (quantization, distillation), and the quality gain over the strongest baselines (~1 dB PSNR) is modest, leaving open whether the competitive allocation framework scales to longer sequences or more complex multi-object interactions without further regularization.
