## Problem

REPA demonstrates that aligning DiT features with external DINO embeddings dramatically accelerates training, but this introduces a dependency on a separately pretrained vision encoder. Existing self-contained alternatives (SRA, LayerSync, Dispersive Loss) avoid this dependency yet still lag behind REPA in both convergence speed and generation quality. The paper asks whether this gap is fundamental or simply a consequence of the immature quality of internally generated guidance signals in early training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260185/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within the standard latent diffusion pipeline: a noisy VAE latent (from SD-VAE or VAVAE) conditioned on a class label (ImageNet 256×256 / 512×512) or a text prompt (MS-COCO) is denoised by a DiT/SiT/LightningDiT backbone to produce a clean latent, which is decoded to an RGB image. The guiding model shares the exact same architecture as the student, so no additional input modality or external feature extractor is required at inference time.

## Method

Self-Transcendence is a two-stage, fully self-contained training pipeline. In Stage 1 (VAE Structure Guidance), a guiding model is trained for ~40 epochs with an auxiliary L2 loss that aligns an intermediate feature (at layer n/2, projected through a lightweight MLP) to the ground-truth VAE latent, instilling structurally clean representations that help shallow blocks separate noise from signal. In Stage 2 (Self-guided Representation), the guiding model is frozen; features from a deeper layer (2n/3) are extracted under both conditional and unconditional inputs and combined via classifier-free guidance in feature space (f_g = f_u + ω(f_c − f_u), with ω=30 for SiT), producing semantically enriched supervision signals. These CFG-enhanced internal features then supervise a new DiT trained from scratch via an L2 alignment loss on its intermediate layers, with the guidance loss removed after an early-stop window (20 epochs for base, 10 for larger models) to avoid destabilizing deeper layers. The key insight is that effective guidance requires both structural cleanliness (supplied by VAE latents) and semantic discriminability (amplified by feature-space CFG), and that the DiT's own partially trained representations can satisfy both criteria without any external encoder.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, Self-Transcendence achieves FID 7.51 with SiT-XL/2 at 80 epochs, surpassing REPA (7.90) and LayerSync (8.80 at 200 epochs) without any external features. On LightningDiT-XL/1, it reaches FID 3.55 at 64 epochs, outperforming REPA (4.09). With classifier-free guidance, Self-Transcendence matches REPA's FID (1.44 vs. 1.42) at half the training epochs (400 vs. 800) on SiT-XL/2, and achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.25 on LightningDiT-XL/1. On text-to-image (MS-COCO), it beats REPA with FID 4.56 vs. 4.90 and IS 33.08 vs. 32.55 at 150K iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-Transcendence outperforms REPA (the strongest external-guidance baseline using DINO features) across all tested configurations: FID 20.49 vs. 24.40 (SiT-B/2), 8.74 vs. 9.70 (SiT-L/2), 7.51 vs. 7.90 (SiT-XL/2), and 3.55 vs. 4.09 (LightningDiT-XL/1), all at the same number of epochs. Against self-contained baselines, the gains are even larger: on SiT-XL/2, Self-Transcendence (7.51 FID at 80 epochs) beats LayerSync (8.80 at 200 epochs) and Disperse Loss (10.64 at 200 epochs) by a wide margin. With CFG enabled (Table 3), Self-Transcendence at 400 epochs achieves FID 1.44 and IS 311.3 on SiT-XL/2, nearly matching REPA at 800 epochs (FID 1.42, IS 305.7) while using half the training budget, and achieves a new SOTA FID of 1.25 on LightningDiT-XL/1. On text-to-image generation (MS-COCO), Self-Transcendence surpasses REPA with FID 4.56 vs. 4.90 and IS 33.08 vs. 32.55 at 150K iterations. The only comparison where Self-Transcendence does not clearly win is linear probing on ImageNet classification (Table 7), where REPA's DINO-pretrained teacher yields slightly higher top-1 accuracy at intermediate layers, though the authors argue this does not translate to generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation-alignment line of work initiated by REPA, which showed that aligning DiT intermediate features with external DINO embeddings accelerates training. It also builds on self-contained alternatives (SRA, LayerSync) that use deeper-layer features to guide shallower layers, but departs from them by introducing a two-stage pipeline: first aligning with VAE latents for structural stability, then applying classifier-free guidance (originally designed for output-space conditioning) to the feature space to boost semantic discriminability. The key conceptual departure is treating the partially trained DiT itself as a sufficient teacher, eliminating the need for any external pretrained encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a fully self-contained method can match or exceed REPA (which depends on DINOv2) removes a significant practical dependency for DiT training, making acceleration accessible without large external models or additional pretraining data. The approach generalizes across backbones (SiT, LightningDiT), scales (B through XL), and tasks (class-to-image, text-to-image), suggesting broad applicability. However, the quality of internal guidance is upper-bounded by the model's own capacity, additional hyperparameters (guidance scale, layer selection, loss weight) require tuning, and the method has only been validated on image generation—its transfer to video, 3D, or other modalities remains an open question.
