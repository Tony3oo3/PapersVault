## Problem

DiT-based video generation models incur prohibitive inference costs from the combination of quadratic 3D spatiotemporal attention and dozens of sequential denoising steps. Existing training-free acceleration methods (sparse attention, KV-caching, quantization) reduce per-step computation but rigidly enforce a uniform, dense denoising schedule on every frame across all timesteps. The paper identifies this uniform allocation as inherently redundant for natural video, where temporal coherence means non-keyframe trajectories are highly predictable once critical semantic-transition frames are anchored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces an 81-frame, 720p video by accelerating the inference of pre-trained DiT-based T2V models (Wan 2.1 with 21 latent frames × 3600 tokens, CogVideoX-v1.5 with 11 frames × 4080 tokens, HunyuanVideo). It is entirely training-free and operates in the latent space of the 3D-VAE, requiring no architectural modification or retraining of the base model. The only additional hyperparameters are the warm-up step count, keyframe budget, and skip strides.

## Method

RhymeFlow decouples denoising trajectories across frames via three mechanisms. First, a sequential keyframe selection pass identifies a sparse set of frames (M=4) that dominate latent semantic evolution by traversing predicted clean latents and selecting frames whose cosine similarity to the nearest preceding keyframe falls below a threshold. Second, a progressive asynchronous schedule assigns keyframes full step-by-step denoising while non-keyframes skip steps with an increasing stride (n_small=2 at high noise, n_large=3 at low noise), with periodic 'Rhythmic Points' where all frames re-synchronize via full 3D attention. Third, a latent trajectory projection module linearly interpolates the skipped intermediate states of non-keyframes in the latent space (exploiting the near-linear ODE trajectories of Rectified Flow), enabling keyframes to attend to a temporally complete sequence at every step without executing full network evaluations. A per-layer rolling KV-cache stores only the two most recent post-attention hidden states per non-keyframe, bounding peak memory while supporting on-the-fly projection.

## Results

On Wan 2.1, RhymeFlow achieves PSNR 26.291, SSIM 0.783, and LPIPS 0.168 with a 1.53× speedup (650.4 s vs. 993.5 s dense baseline), outperforming all intra-step sparse-attention baselines on quality. On CogVideoX-v1.5, it reaches PSNR 26.890, SSIM 0.852, and SubConsistency 0.986 at 1.78× speedup (351.1 s), leading both quality and latency among all compared methods. When combined with SAP's token-level sparsity (Ours+SAP), the framework achieves the highest speedups of 1.66× (Wan 2.1) and 1.93× (CogVideoX-v1.5) while maintaining competitive fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RhymeFlow consistently outperforms all eight baselines on quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across both Wan 2.1 and CogVideoX-v1.5. On Wan 2.1, it surpasses SAP (the strongest intra-step baseline) by +1.84 PSNR and +0.053 SSIM, though SAP is slightly faster (1.63× vs. 1.53×); SVG and SpargeAttn trail by 3.9 and 5.9 PSNR respectively. On CogVideoX-v1.5, RhymeFlow dominates on both axes: it achieves the best quality (PSNR 26.890 vs. SVG's 24.130) *and* the lowest latency (351.1 s vs. SVG's 385.8 s), yielding a 1.78× speedup that exceeds SVG's 1.62×. On HunyuanVideo (Table 3), RhymeFlow leads all cache-based methods (EasyCache, DiCache) and SVG on PSNR and SSIM, while SAP achieves a higher 2.52× speedup at the cost of lower SSIM (0.904 vs. 0.918). The Ours+SAP hybrid achieves the best overall speedups (1.66× and 1.93×) by exploiting the orthogonality between inter-step frame skipping and intra-step token sparsity, confirming that the two acceleration dimensions compound rather than conflict.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by shifting the acceleration axis from intra-step (making each denoising step cheaper via sparse attention, KV-caching, or quantization) to inter-step (skipping entire denoising steps for selected frames). It repurposes the well-known temporal coherence of video sequences and the linearity of Rectified Flow ODE trajectories into a lightweight latent projection mechanism, but the core idea of assigning heterogeneous, frame-specific denoising schedules is absent from prior video diffusion acceleration literature. The keyframe selection draws on content-aware frame sampling from video compression, and the KV-cache management borrows from LLM inference, but the asynchronous scheduling paradigm that unifies these elements is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic nature of RhymeFlow makes it immediately applicable to any DiT-based video generator without retraining, and its demonstrated orthogonality to intra-step sparse attention (e.g., SAP) opens a path toward compounded speedups that neither approach achieves alone. However, the 1.5–1.9× speedup is moderate, and the linear-projection assumption for skipped latents may degrade on highly dynamic or semantically abrupt content where trajectories are less linear. Open questions include whether learned, scene-adaptive scheduling functions can push the quality-speed frontier further, and how the approach scales to longer videos or higher resolutions where the keyframe budget and projection error may grow.
