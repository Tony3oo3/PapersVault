## Problem

Vision-Language Models perform coarse perception and reasoning on fine-grained motion questions because fixed-rate frame sampling misses rapid inter-frame motion transitions, and VLMs lack structured constraints that separate what motion a subject performs from when and where it occurs. Existing training-based temporal grounding methods cannot precisely capture sub-second motion boundaries, while training-free retrieval-augmented approaches rely on semantic relevance rather than motion-specific physical cues like abrupt optical-flow changes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip and a motion-centric text question as input. A three-VLM consensus router (Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B, Molmo2-8B, each with 32 uniform frames) first filters easy samples; only disputed cases enter the full MotionChain pipeline. The output is the final answer to the motion question, produced after the VLM verifies candidate options against a chain of visually grounded claims derived from structured (time range, subject, motion, environment) tuples. The adaptive frame budget averages 26.24 frames on MotionBench and 23.8 on FAVOR-Bench, with each frame annotated with a [t=τs] temporal marker.

## Method

MotionChain is a training-free framework that decomposes continuous video motion into an ordered sequence of (time range, subject, motion, environment) tuples via a ReAct-inspired VLM-LLM coarse-to-fine grounding loop, where the VLM proposes question-aware segments and the LLM (QwenPlus) evaluates coverage, granularity, and accuracy. Farneback dense optical flow at 4 Hz produces a temporal motion energy curve whose changepoints (|ΔE| > μ + 1.5σ) are merged with VLM-predicted boundaries to form a complete boundary set. A boundary-aware non-uniform sampler then adds evidence frames at non-grid timestamps (distance ≥ τ from the 1 fps grid, deduplicated at 0.3 s spacing), and SAM3 text-driven segmentation highlights the motion subject region in each evidence frame to suppress background distraction. For answering, each tuple is converted into element-wise visually grounded claims (subject presence, motion performance, environment context) that the VLM verifies segment-by-segment against temporally annotated frames, converting holistic video description into a structured, traceable reasoning chain.

## Results

On MotionBench, MotionChain with Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 63.59% overall accuracy using only 26.24 frames on average, surpassing the 106B-parameter GLM-4.5V (61.42%) and GLM-4V-Plus (62.80%) while using fewer frames than the 32-frame baselines. On FAVOR-Bench, MotionChain with Qwen3-VL-8B reaches 58.03% overall accuracy with 23.8 frames (just 3.32 beyond the 1 fps baseline), outperforming all proprietary and open-source VLMs tested. The largest per-category gains appear on tasks demanding precise temporal localization: Action Order (+2.12% over Qwen3 baseline), Camera Motion (+5.72% for InternVL3.5), and Non-Subject Motion (+3.13% on FAVOR-Bench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MotionBench, MotionChain + Qwen3-VL-8B (63.59%) outperforms the 106B GLM-4.5V (61.42%) by 2.17 points and GLM-4V-Plus (62.80%) by 0.79 points, while using fewer frames (26.24 vs. 2 fps). It also edges out MotionSight with InternVL3-78B (63.00%) by 0.59 points and the Qwen3-VL-8B 32-frame baseline (62.87%) by 0.72 points. On FAVOR-Bench, MotionChain + Qwen3-VL-8B (58.03%) surpasses the strongest proprietary model Gemini-1.5-Pro (49.87%) by over 8 points and the best open-source baseline Qwen3-VL-8B at 1 fps (56.68%) by 1.35 points. Among enhancement methods, MotionChain beats MotionSight (InternVL3-78B) by 4.23 points on FAVOR-Bench and Open-o3-Video (7B) by 7.09 points on MotionBench. The gains are most pronounced on categories requiring precise temporal reasoning (Action Order, Camera Motion, Non-Subject Motion), confirming that the structured tuple decomposition and optical-flow boundary calibration target the specific failure modes of fixed-rate sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionChain repurposes several established components—Farneback dense optical flow, ReAct-style VLM-LLM iterative refinement, and SAM3 text-driven segmentation—into a unified training-free pipeline specifically targeting motion-centric video QA. It departs from training-based temporal reasoning methods (ThinkingWithVideos, Open-o3 Video, VideoMind) by requiring no parameter updates, and from retrieval-augmented long-video methods (DrVideo, VideoLucy) by using physical motion signals (optical-flow changepoints) rather than semantic relevance as the keyframe selection criterion. The structured tuple decomposition (<time, subject, motion, environment>) as visually verifiable claims is the paper's distinctive conceptual contribution, distinguishing it from MotionSight's unstructured visual prompting approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design makes MotionChain immediately applicable to any frozen VLM without retraining, and the modest frame overhead (~3 extra frames on FAVOR-Bench) suggests practical deployability. However, the end-to-end latency of ~27 s median (vs. ~4 s for direct 32-frame answering) is a significant practical limitation, and the MotionBench results rely on a three-VLM consensus router that bypasses the full pipeline for 52.8% of samples, partially inflating the apparent gain. Open questions include whether the structured tuple approach generalizes to non-motion video QA tasks, how it scales to very long videos where optical-flow computation becomes prohibitive, and whether the modest 0.7–1.35% improvements justify the added pipeline complexity in production settings.
