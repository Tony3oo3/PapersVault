## Problem

Pathology foundation models exceed 600M–1.1B parameters and produce high-dimensional embeddings, making them impractical for resource-limited clinics and research centers. Existing compression approaches are limited: single-teacher distillation (e.g., H0-mini) inherits the teacher's biases and retains large embedding sizes, while multi-teacher distillation (GPFM) does not reduce model capacity. No prior work combines multi-teacher distillation with Matryoshka-style nested embeddings to jointly compress both model size and embedding dimensionality in pathology.

## Input / Output

The student model takes 224×224 px H&E-stained image tiles (resized from 448×448 crops at 10×, 20×, or 40× magnification) and produces a 768-d CLS token plus a set of 768-d patch token embeddings. The key structural property is that any prefix of these embeddings (384, 192, 96, or 48 dimensions) remains a semantically valid representation, enabling dynamic truncation at inference time. During training, each model receives two augmented views per tile: an aligned crop shared across all models (for both CLS and patch losses) and an individual random crop (for CLS loss only), simulating magnification variation.

## Method

Pathryoshka distills three frozen pathology FMs (Virchow2, UNI2-h, H-optimus-1, each >600M params) into a compact ViT student (86M or 22M params) using a fully unsupervised, token-level objective. For each teacher and each nesting level m ∈ {768, 384, 192, 96, 48}, a dedicated 3-layer MLP projection head maps the first m dimensions of the student's CLS or patch tokens into the teacher's embedding space; the CLS loss uses cosine similarity while the patch loss uses MSE after channel-wise batch standardization of teacher patch embeddings. A novel robustness-enhancing cropping strategy feeds each model both a shared aligned crop (25–100% of tile area, aspect ratio 0.9–1.1) and an individual random crop, with patch-level loss computed only on the aligned crop to enforce spatial correspondence. Training uses 243M tiles from 158,233 internal H&E WSIs (no public data) with H&E-specific stain perturbations, random flips, and Gaussian blur, ensuring the student generalizes rather than memorizes teacher embeddings.

## Results

On ten public pathology benchmarks (8 patch-level, 2 slide-level), Pathryoshka-B (86M params) achieves a median accuracy of 85.6%, exceeding the best teacher UNI2-h (84.3%) by 1.3 points, Virchow2 (84.1%) by 1.5 points, and H-optimus-1 (78.3%) by 7.3 points, while using 86–92% fewer parameters. It outperforms the single-teacher distilled baseline H0-mini (78.6%) by 7.0 points and the multi-teacher baseline GPFM (80.3%) by 5.3 points in median accuracy. Pathryoshka-S (22M params) achieves a median of 80.5%, still exceeding H0-mini, H-optimus-1, and GPFM. In terms of efficiency, Pathryoshka-B achieves 842 img/s throughput on an RTX 3090, roughly 11× faster than H-optimus-1 (82 img/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three teacher models, Pathryoshka-B (86M params) achieves a higher median accuracy than UNI2-h (681M, 84.3%) by 1.3 points, Virchow2 (631M, 84.1%) by 1.5 points, and H-optimus-1 (1,135M, 78.3%) by 7.3 points, while using 86–92% fewer parameters; Wilcoxon Signed-Rank tests confirm the teachers do not significantly outperform Pathryoshka-B (p = 0.967, 0.935, 0.995 respectively). Against the single-teacher distillation baseline H0-mini (86M, median 78.6%), Pathryoshka-B wins by 7.0 median points (p = 0.010), and against the multi-teacher distillation baseline GPFM (303M, median 80.3%), it wins by 5.3 points (p = 0.019). Pathryoshka-B is best or tied-best on 9 out of 10 benchmarks, losing only on Patch Camelyon (94.1 vs. UNI2-h's 95.1) and CAMELYON16 where GPFM's 94.9† (trained on that data) is marginally higher. The smaller Pathryoshka-S (22M, median 80.5%) still exceeds H0-mini, H-optimus-1, and GPFM in median accuracy despite a 4× parameter reduction over Pathryoshka-B.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pathryoshka combines two previously separate ideas—agglomerative multi-teacher distillation (AM-RADIO, Ranzinger et al. 2024) and Matryoshka nested embeddings (Kusupati et al. 2022)—within a single pathology foundation model framework. Prior pathology distillation was limited to single-teacher setups (H0-mini, Virchow2G-Mini) relying on the DINOv2 framework that requires non-public teacher heads, or to multi-teacher objectives without model compression (GPFM). The key departure is a distillation protocol that needs only CLS and patch tokens from teachers (no DINO/iBOT heads), a robustness-enhancing cropping strategy for cross-teacher alignment, and nested embedding constraints applied at multiple halving levels during distillation—none of which had been jointly realized in the pathology domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 86–92% parameter reduction with on-par or superior benchmark performance directly addresses the deployment barrier for pathology FMs in resource-limited clinics, and the nested embeddings add a practical accuracy-vs-cost dial for downstream tasks like clustering or retrieval on gigapixel slides. The paper's broader claim—that current large-scale pathology FMs underutilize their capacity and that multi-teacher agglomeration can exceed individual teachers—challenges the prevailing scaling paradigm, though this is validated only on established classification and representation benchmarks rather than prospective clinical utility. Open questions include whether the nested structure retains its advantage in more complex slide-level or multi-modal settings, and whether the single-institution training data limits generalizability across scanner and staining protocols.
