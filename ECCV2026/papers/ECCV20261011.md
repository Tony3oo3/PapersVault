## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods for billion-scale video diffusion models still require millions of trainable parameters, making them impractical on resource-constrained hardware. Additionally, reward-based optimization for condition-guided generation suffers from noise-induced instability at high-noise denoising stages, where pixel-space rewards become unreliable and timestep-sensitive. The paper addresses both gaps simultaneously with an ultra-lightweight prompt tuning framework and a noise-robust dual-space reward mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained Wan2.1 (1.3B, 14B) and Wan2.2 (5B) video diffusion models, consuming text prompts, reference RGB images, or control signals (canny edges, depth maps) as conditioning. Only 64 soft prompt tokens per layer are learned, with visual prompts concatenated into self-attention key-value sequences and textual prompts prepended to text embeddings in cross-attention. The output is a temporally coherent video generated in latent space and decoded via VAE, with reward signals computed on decoded key frames at low-noise timesteps (t ≥ 3T/4).

## Method

MagicPrompt introduces Attention-Embedded Prompt Tuning, which injects trainable visual prompts into the Key and Value projections of each DiT self-attention block and textual prompts into cross-attention text embeddings, while keeping all Query projections and backbone weights frozen. A learnable shift bias (γ·X + β) per layer calibrates latent distribution shifts caused by prompt concatenation, preventing frame inconsistency. For training, a Dual-Space Reward Feedback Optimization combines pixel-space perceptual rewards (HPS for text-video alignment, MPS for optical-flow temporal coherence) with a latent-space reward defined as 1 − MSE between CFG-guided and unconditional denoising trajectories at the same timestep. The latent reward is timestep-agnostic and noise-robust, providing stable supervision across all denoising stages without external reward models, while the pixel reward anchors perceptual quality at low-noise steps.

## Results

On Text-to-Video (1.3B), MagicPrompt achieves FID 86.56 and FVD 637.46, substantially outperforming LoRA (FID 101.76, FVD 853.56) and VACE (FID 99.64, FVD 760.33) while matching LoRA on CLIP (0.68) and LPIPS (0.66). On Image-to-Video (1.3B), it attains the best CLIP (0.92) and FID (29.23) across all methods. On Control-to-Video (14B), MagicPrompt achieves the best FVD (412.82) and CLIP (0.87), though LoRA retains a FID advantage (26.09 vs. 34.43). All results are obtained with fewer than 1% trainable parameters (64 soft prompt tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, MagicPrompt wins decisively on T2V distribution metrics (FID 86.56 vs. 101.76; FVD 637.46 vs. 853.56) while tying on CLIP and LPIPS. On I2V, MagicPrompt surpasses LoRA in CLIP (0.92 vs. 0.91) and FID (29.23 vs. 43.19), though LoRA edges ahead on LPIPS (0.35 vs. 0.37) and FVD (269.02 vs. 274.88). On Control2V (14B), MagicPrompt achieves the best FVD (412.82 vs. 419.82) and CLIP (0.87 vs. 0.86), but LoRA maintains a clear FID advantage (26.09 vs. 34.43). Against VACE, MagicPrompt dominates on T2V (FID 86.56 vs. 99.64; FVD 637.46 vs. 760.33) and I2V (FID 29.23 vs. 43.04; FVD 274.88 vs. 354.67), and also leads on Control2V FVD (412.82 vs. 464.45) while VACE trails on FID (39.29 vs. 34.43). Overall, MagicPrompt achieves the best or near-best results on 10 of 12 metric-task combinations while using orders of magnitude fewer trainable parameters than either baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicPrompt extends the prompt-tuning paradigm (established in ViT and NLP via Visual Prompt Tuning and Prefix Tuning) into the attention mechanisms of video diffusion transformers, specifically injecting trainable soft prompts into self-attention key-value sequences and cross-attention text embeddings. It departs from the dominant PEFT approaches for video diffusion—LoRA (low-rank weight decomposition) and adapter-based methods (SimDA, VACE)—by avoiding any modification of backbone weights, instead steering generation through attention redistribution with orders of magnitude fewer parameters. The Dual-Space Reward Feedback Optimization builds on DRaFT-style pixel-space reward backpropagation but introduces a novel latent-space reward that compares CFG-guided versus unconditional denoising trajectories, addressing the high-noise incompatibility that plagues pixel-only reward training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: adapting billion-parameter video diffusion models with fewer than 1% trainable parameters on consumer hardware opens the door to personalized and domain-specific video generation that was previously infeasible. The latent-space reward design (CFG trajectory similarity) is a reusable idea that could benefit other reward-guided diffusion training beyond video. However, open questions remain: the evaluation is confined to the Wan model family and a limited set of datasets, the performance gains over LoRA are most pronounced on distribution metrics (FID/FVD) rather than alignment metrics (CLIP), and it is unclear whether the approach generalizes to non-DiT architectures or to tasks requiring more complex structural changes than attention redistribution can provide.
