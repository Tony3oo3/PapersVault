## Problem

Despite the success of foundation models in vision, language, and audio, no domain-specific foundation model exists for sheet music—a highly structured visual encoding of symbolic musical language. General-purpose vision encoders, regardless of scale or pre-training diversity, produce representations that are systematically misaligned with musical notation content, as evidenced by negative embedding-transcription correlations. This paper asks whether music-specific self-supervised pre-training can close this gap and yield a reusable backbone for diverse sheet music analysis tasks.

## Input / Output

MuSViT takes an RGB sheet music page image and produces a sequence of patch-level embedding vectors (d=768 for MuSViT, d=384 for MuSViT_Light). During pre-training, Stage 1 operates on random 512×512 crops while Stage 2 processes full pages resized to 1024×1024, both with a fine-grained patch size of P=16 so that each patch captures only elementary notational content (a single notehead, stem, or accidental). The encoder uses 2D sinusoidal positional encodings rather than the standard 1D variant, explicitly encoding vertical staff position into each token's representation.

## Method

MuSViT is a 12-layer ViT encoder (~85M parameters) pre-trained via Masked Autoencoders on 9.7 million IMSLP pages using a two-stage curriculum: a synthetic warm-up on DeepScoresV2 at 50% masking (512×512 crops) followed by large-scale training on real-world scores at 70% masking (1024×1024 full pages). The high masking ratio is central to the design: occluding entire measures forces the encoder to infer which symbols should appear, their vertical staff position (pitch), and their sequential context, effectively learning the visual grammar of music notation without supervision. The asymmetric MAE decoder is discarded after pre-training, leaving only the encoder for downstream use. A lightweight variant (MuSViT_Light, ~25M parameters) shares the same architecture with a reduced embedding dimension for resource-constrained settings. The synthetic warm-up is shown to be necessary—without it, the encoder suffers dimensional collapse and predicts average pixel values rather than structured notation.

## Results

Under linear probing (frozen encoder), MuSViT achieves 16.4% SER on full-page recognition (2.5× lower than the best general-purpose baseline), 18.4% SER on staff-level recognition, 79.7% mAP on symbol detection, and 47.4% Acc₀ on difficulty classification—best on all four tasks. Under fine-tuning, MuSViT reaches 10.9% SER on full-page recognition (vs. 20.0% prior SOTA), 97.0% mAP₅₀ on symbol detection (vs. 90.5% SOTA), and 54.2% Acc₀ on difficulty classification (vs. 38.4% SOTA), while matching the staff-level SOTA within 0.6 points (8.6% vs. 8.0% SER). The lightweight MuSViT_Light variant (25M params) stays within 0.2–4.5 points of the full model across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuSViT outperforms all four general-purpose vision encoders (PaliGemma 2, Kosmos-2.5, Qwen3-VL, DINOv3-7B) on every task under linear probing, with the largest gap on full-page recognition (16.4% vs. 48.6% SER for PaliGemma 2, a 2.5× improvement) and symbol detection (79.7% vs. 70.4% mAP for DINOv3-7B). DINOv3-7B is the strongest general-purpose baseline overall but still trails MuSViT by 9–40 points depending on the task; vision-language models (PaliGemma 2, Qwen3-VL) perform notably worse on spatially dense tasks like symbol detection, suggesting language-aligned training degrades fine-grained localization. Under fine-tuning, MuSViT surpasses task-specific state-of-the-art on three of four tasks: full-page recognition (10.9% vs. 20.0% SER), symbol detection (97.0% vs. 90.5% mAP₅₀), and difficulty classification (54.2% vs. 38.4% Acc₀), while matching the staff-level SOTA within 0.6 points (8.6% vs. 8.0% SER). The embedding-transcription consistency analysis further shows that all general-purpose encoders yield negative Pearson correlations (−0.04 to −0.13) between embedding distance and transcription distance, whereas MuSViT achieves +0.61 to +0.71, confirming that only music-specific pre-training captures symbolic musical structure in the representation space.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuSViT directly builds on the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) framework of He et al. (CVPR 2022), adopting its asymmetric encoder-decoder design, high masking ratio, and pixel-level MSE reconstruction loss as the pre-training algorithm. It departs from general-purpose vision foundation models (DINO, CLIP/SigLIP, PaliGemma) by restricting pre-training to a single symbolic visual domain—sheet music—and introduces a two-stage curriculum (synthetic warm-up on DeepScoresV2 followed by large-scale real-world training on IMSLP) that the authors show is necessary to avoid dimensional collapse. The 2D sinusoidal positional encoding is inherited from prior music document recognition work, and the overall positioning mirrors the audio-domain precedent where music-specific foundation models (MERT, MuLan) outperform general-purpose alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MuSViT fills a clear gap: no foundation model previously existed for sheet music, a domain where millions of digitized pages remain untranscribed and inaccessible. The embedding-transcription consistency analysis provides a diagnostic tool for the broader question of whether general-purpose vision encoders can represent any symbolic visual domain, and the finding of systematic anti-correlation suggests a fundamental limitation rather than a mere performance shortfall. Open questions include whether the two-stage curriculum generalizes to other symbolic domains (e.g., mathematical notation, chemical structures), whether the model handles non-Latin or non-CWMN notation systems adequately, and whether the 9.7M-page corpus is sufficient to cover the full historical range of engraving conventions. For the OMR community, the released backbone and code lower the barrier to building new task-specific systems.
