## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting requires millions of primitives, each storing 3D position, opacity, full SH coefficients, 3D scale, and rotation, leading to file sizes of hundreds of megabytes to several gigabytes that hinder scalable deployment. While prior work reduces Gaussian count, quantizes attributes, or uses neural fields for implicit estimation, no method simultaneously minimizes per-primitive attribute dimensionality to achieve the most compact storage footprint without sacrificing rendering quality or speed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a trained 3DGS scene and produces a compressed file where each Gaussian stores only 9–17 explicit dimensions (3D center, 1D scale magnitude, 1D luma DC, and a configurable 4–12D local feature) instead of the conventional ~60+ dimensions. The compression pipeline encodes positions via G-PCC, applies VQ to scale magnitude and luma, SVQ to local features, then Huffman coding and LZMA packing. At inference, the compressed file is decompressed and six lightweight MLPs reconstruct all remaining attributes (opacity, rotation, non-DC SH, chrominance, scale factors) to render novel views at real-time speeds (up to 389 FPS).

## Method

EGGS introduces two attribute factorization techniques that each compress a 3D attribute to a single explicit dimension. Luma-based DC Representation converts the 3D RGB DC SH component to YUV space, explicitly stores only the luminance channel, and predicts the 2D chrominance via an MLP fed by a hybrid feature (positional encoding + local feature). Volume-Preserving Scale Factorization decomposes the 3D scale into a magnitude s_m and a 3D factor s_f, but reconstructs each axis as s_m · exp(s_f,i − mean(s_f)), guaranteeing the product of all three axes equals s_m³ and preventing the unbounded divergence that causes training failures in naive 1D scale approaches. Six single-hidden-layer MLPs (64 neurons) estimate all implicit attributes from the hybrid feature. Training proceeds in stages: 8K iterations of standard Mini-Splatting2 densification, introduction of the EGGS representation, importance-based simplification at 13K, and VQ/SVQ codebook fine-tuning from 19K, with the local feature dimension D_L serving as a tunable rate-control knob.

## Results

EGGS-XS achieves the smallest file size across all three datasets: 2.67 MB (Mip-NeRF360), 1.33 MB (Tanks&Temples), and 2.53 MB (Deep Blending), while maintaining competitive quality (e.g., PSNR 26.47 dB, SSIM 0.793 on Mip-NeRF360). The larger EGGS-L configuration (6.21 MB on Mip-NeRF360) reaches SSIM 0.817, PSNR 27.45 dB, and LPIPS 0.221, ranking second-best in SSIM and LPIPS. On Deep Blending, EGGS-L achieves the best SSIM (0.911) and PSNR (30.21 dB) among all compression methods. Rendering speed is also strong: EGGS-XS reaches 189 FPS on Mip-NeRF360 and 389 FPS on Tanks&Temples, the fastest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGGS-XS achieves the smallest file size on all three datasets (2.67/1.33/2.53 MB), beating the next-smallest OMG-XS (4.04/2.44/3.29 MB) by roughly 34–45% in storage while maintaining comparable or slightly lower quality metrics. On Deep Blending, EGGS-L (SSIM 0.911, PSNR 30.21 dB) outperforms all baselines including OMG-XL (0.909, 29.87) and HAC++ high-rate (0.906, 30.13) in both SSIM and PSNR. On Mip-NeRF360, EGGS-L ranks second in SSIM (0.817 vs. OMG-XL's 0.818) and LPIPS (0.221 vs. 0.218), while HAC++ high-rate leads in PSNR (27.79 vs. 27.45). On Tanks&Temples, HAC++ dominates quality (SSIM 0.852, PSNR 24.24) at a larger file size (10.07 MB), whereas EGGS-L (0.842, 23.74 at 3.14 MB) trades ~0.5 dB PSNR for a 3× smaller file. In rendering speed, EGGS-XS is the fastest method on Mip-NeRF360 (189 FPS) and Tanks&Temples (389 FPS), surpassing OMG-XS (170/321 FPS) and Mini-Splatting2 (162/301 FPS). Overall, EGGS wins on storage efficiency and speed, matches or slightly trails the best methods on quality at comparable file sizes, and clearly leads in the extreme-compression regime below 5 MB.

## Relation to Prior Work

EGGS sits within the neural-field-based 3DGS compression lineage (Scaffold-GS, HAC++, LocoGS, OMG), which separates Gaussian attributes into explicitly stored and MLP-estimated components. It departs from this line by aggressively minimizing the explicit attribute set to just 9 dimensions (center, scale magnitude, luma, and a 4-dim local feature) through two targeted factorizations: a YUV-based luma/chroma split for the SH DC term and a volume-constrained exponential decomposition for scale. The volume-preserving constraint is a direct response to the training instability observed in LocoGS's naive 1D scale factorization, and the luma representation borrows a well-known perceptual coding principle from video compression (HEVC/VVC) that had not been applied to 3DGS attribute storage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EGGS achieves the smallest storage footprint among all compared methods (e.g., 2.67 MB vs. 4.04 MB for OMG-XS on Mip-NeRF360) while maintaining competitive rendering speed, making it a practical option for deploying 3DGS on bandwidth- or storage-constrained XR devices. The configurable local feature dimension (D_L = 4–12) offers a simple rate-control knob that the ablation shows is often more efficient than aggressive primitive pruning in high-quality regimes. Limitations include the requirement for a full training pipeline (not post-hoc compression), a modest quality gap at the smallest configuration, and evaluation restricted to static scenes; open questions include whether the volume-preserving factorization and luma split generalize to dynamic 4DGS settings or to scenes with extreme color variation where chroma carries more structural information.
