## Problem

MLLM inference is bottlenecked by quadratic self-attention over thousands of visual tokens, and naive token pruning irreversibly discards non-subject information (background text, peripheral objects) needed by roughly 19–27% of real-world visual questions. Existing solutions either rely on similarity heuristics that destroy original token representations or introduce extra compression modules requiring additional training, leaving a gap for a training-free mechanism that both reduces computation and preserves non-subject semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261104/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard MLLM input sequence—system prompt tokens, visual tokens (from a CLIP/ViT encoder at resolutions yielding 576 to 2880 tokens depending on the backbone), and question tokens—and produces a free-form text answer. The key operational detail is that visual tokens are split into subject and non-subject subsets via [CLS]-attention ranking in the vision encoder; the non-subject subset is active only during the first n transformer layers and is then permanently dropped, so the decoding phase operates on a much shorter sequence. The framework is training-free and requires no architectural modification, remaining compatible with Flash-Attention and standard KV-cache reuse.

## Method

ParVTS partitions visual tokens into subject (top-k by [CLS] attention) and non-subject groups, then routes them through two parallel LLM pathways in a single forward pass by concatenating along the batch dimension with a modified attention mask that makes the two visual groups mutually invisible while both attend to their respective copies of the question tokens. After n early transformer layers, the question-token hidden states from both paths are fused via weighted averaging (α=β=0.5), and the non-subject visual branch is discarded entirely. This exploits the visual information migration phenomenon—where self-attention in shallow layers transfers visual semantics into question tokens—so that by layer n the merged question representation already encodes both subject and non-subject context. Subsequent layers and the full decoding phase then run on only the subject tokens plus merged question tokens, reducing attention complexity from O(L²) to O(L_s²) with L_s ≪ L, achieving up to 88.9% token pruning, 1.77× speedup, and 70% FLOPs reduction without any training or auxiliary modules.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B with 88.89% visual token reduction (576→64 tokens), ParVTS achieves 93.01% average score across 8 benchmarks, outperforming the next-best method PruneSID (92.75%) and substantially exceeding FastV (78.64%) and SparseVLM (78.64%). On LLaVA-Next-7B (2880→320 tokens, also 88.89% reduction), ParVTS reaches 89.07% average, again ranking first. In efficiency comparisons at matched MME scores (~1680), ParVTS retains only 46 tokens, achieving 1.77× speedup and 30.31% FLOPs relative to the full model, versus 135 tokens and 42.92% FLOPs for FastV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParVTS outperforms all six baselines on the primary LLaVA-1.5-7B comparison (88.89% token reduction), achieving 93.01% average vs. PruneSID (92.75%), SAINT (85.69%), HiRED (83.13%), FastV (78.64%), and SparseVLM (78.64%). On LLaVA-Next-7B, ParVTS leads with 89.07% average, narrowly beating HiRED (88.91%) and PruneSID (88.28%), while maintaining a clear margin over FastV (85.87%) and SparseVLM (78.03%). The advantage is most pronounced on OCRBench (282 vs. 172 for FastV on LLaVA-1.5-7B; 329 vs. 374 for FastV on LLaVA-Next-7B), reflecting ParVTS's ability to preserve non-subject contextual information. On Video-LLaVA-7B (49.8% reduction), ParVTS (57.8% avg accuracy) is marginally below FastV (58.1%), the only comparison where it does not lead. In efficiency-matched settings (Table 5), ParVTS consistently uses fewer tokens and lower FLOPs than FastV and SparseVLM at comparable MME scores, e.g., 46 tokens at 30.31% FLOPs vs. 135 tokens at 42.92% FLOPs for FastV at ~1680 MME.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParVTS sits at the intersection of two research threads: visual token compression in MLLMs (FastV, PruMerge, SparseVLM, HiRED) and the recently characterized phenomenon of visual information migration in early transformer layers (VTW, HiMAP, Cross-modal Information Flow). Rather than pruning or merging tokens based on similarity heuristics or adding learned compression modules, ParVTS repurposes the [CLS]-attention saliency signal from ViTs to partition tokens into subject and non-subject groups, then exploits the natural early-layer migration to distill non-subject semantics into question tokens before discarding them. The key conceptual departure from prior scheduling work is the parallel batch-wise execution of both token groups through early layers, which avoids the representational degradation and semantic mismatch inherent in sequential (subject-first or non-subject-first) strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a training-free, module-free method that achieves 1.77× speedup and 70% FLOPs reduction while retaining 93% of vanilla performance across five MLLM backbones, and—critically—remains compatible with Flash-Attention and KV-cache reuse, unlike attention-matrix-based pruners. Open questions include the reliability of [CLS]-based partitioning on images with multiple salient regions or subtle foregrounds, the empirical (non-adaptive) setting of migration depth n and fusion weights, and whether the approach generalizes to video or audio modalities beyond the limited Video-LLaVA evaluation. The finding that three layers suffice for non-subject information migration, while consistent with prior observations, provides a concrete and actionable design principle for future efficient MLLM architectures.
