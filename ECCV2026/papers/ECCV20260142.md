## Problem

Existing audio-visual personalization methods rely on cascaded pipelines that generate video and audio independently, preventing a text prompt from jointly controlling scene content, environmental acoustics, and speaking style. Voice-cloning stages condition only on a reference recording, so they cannot redirect acoustic environment or delivery style to match a new prompt, and same-video editing approaches cannot generalize a subject to entirely novel contexts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a short reference audio clip of the target speaker, a single first-frame RGB image providing a visual identity anchor, and a free-text prompt describing the target scene, environment sounds, and desired speaking style. It produces a 1024×1024, 121-frame (25 fps) synchronized audio-video clip in a single generative pass. The reference audio is encoded via an Audio VAE into mel-spectrogram latents and concatenated with the noisy target audio latents along the sequence dimension, while the video stream uses standard first-frame conditioning with 32× spatial and 8× temporal VAE compression.

## Method

ID-LoRA adapts the LTX-2 joint audio-video DiT (48-layer asymmetric dual-stream transformer with bidirectional cross-modal attention) via rank-128 In-Context LoRA, using an audio-only in-context strategy where reference and target audio latents share the sequence dimension while the video stream remains free to generate from the first frame and text prompt. Two key architectural components address cross-modal identity transfer: (1) negative temporal positions assign reference audio tokens RoPE positions in [−T_ref, 0) and target tokens in [0, T_target], cleanly separating them in positional space without disrupting the pretrained target positions; and (2) identity guidance, a classifier-free-guidance variant applied to the audio stream that extrapolates between reference-conditioned and unconditional predictions to amplify speaker-specific timbre, rhythm, and pronunciation at inference. Training uses the standard diffusion denoising loss for 6,000 steps on a single H100 with ~3K pairs, and inference employs 30 denoising steps with separate guidance scales for video CFG (3.0), audio CFG (7.0), identity guidance (4.0), and AV-bimodal CFG (3.0).

## Results

On the CelebV-HQ hard (cross-video) split, ID-LoRA achieves speaker similarity of 0.477 (vs. 0.385 for Kling 2.6 Pro and 0.391 for the best cascaded baseline), LSE-D of 8.49 (vs. 9.49 for Kling), and CLAP of 0.363 (vs. 0.316 for Kling), with WER of 0.113. The speaker-similarity gap over the best cascaded baseline widens from +0.063 on the easy (same-video) split to +0.086 on the hard split, indicating stronger generalization to novel acoustic settings. In human A/B preference studies, ID-LoRA is preferred over Kling 2.6 Pro by 73.1% of annotators for voice similarity and 65.2% for speech manners (both p<0.001). Cross-dataset transfer (CelebV-HQ checkpoint → TalkVid) retains speaker similarity of 0.595, only 0.004 below the in-domain checkpoint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ID-LoRA outperforms all three cascaded baselines (CosyVoice 3.0, VoiceCraft, ElevenLabs paired with WAN2.2) on speaker similarity, lip synchronization (LSE-D and LSE-C), audio prompt adherence (CLAP), and intelligibility (WER) across every split. Against the strongest unified commercial baseline, Kling 2.6 Pro, ID-LoRA leads on speaker similarity by +0.092 (hard split), LSE-D by −1.00, CLAP by +0.047, and WER by −0.008, while Kling retains a slight edge on face similarity (0.854 vs. 0.874) — a gap the authors attribute to ArcFace bias favoring less lip motion. The advantage over cascaded pipelines grows with condition divergence: on the easy (same-video) split the speaker-similarity gap is +0.063, widening to +0.086 on the hard (cross-video) split, confirming that unified generation generalizes better when reference and target acoustics differ. In the MOS study on physically grounded sound interaction, ID-LoRA scores 3.05 vs. Kling's 2.90 overall, winning 8 of 10 scenarios with fewer 'Bad' ratings (15.3% vs. 23.3%). The CelebV-HQ checkpoint transfers to TalkVid without fine-tuning, maintaining speaker similarity of 0.595 (vs. 0.599 in-domain) while improving CLAP to 0.412, suggesting the learned identity representations are dataset-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

ID-LoRA generalizes the In-Context LoRA paradigm (originally a visual-only identity transfer technique) to the joint audio-video setting by concatenating reference audio latents with target latents in a shared DiT backbone (LTX-2). It departs from cascaded pipelines (voice cloning + video generation) that cannot let a text prompt jointly control acoustics and visuals, and from same-video editing methods (Just-Dub-It, EditYourself) that inherit the source video's environment and cannot synthesize a subject in a novel context. Two new architectural components—negative temporal positions in RoPE space and identity guidance—address challenges specific to cross-modal in-context conditioning that have no analogue in the visual-only IC-LoRA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that parameter-efficient adaptation (~3K pairs, single GPU) of a joint audio-video backbone can match or exceed a large-scale commercial system (Kling 2.6 Pro) on speaker similarity and prompt adherence, suggesting that unified generation is a strong inductive bias for physically grounded audio-visual correspondence. The cross-dataset transfer result (CelebV-HQ checkpoint on TalkVid losing only 0.004 speaker similarity) hints at learnable, dataset-agnostic identity representations. Open questions include multi-speaker scenes, cross-lingual transfer, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or more diverse acoustic environments; the current evaluation is limited to talking-head clips of ~5 seconds.
