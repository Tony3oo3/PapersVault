## Problem

Existing T2I evaluators rely on static prompt sets, detector-based checks, or proprietary LLMs at inference time, making them unable to capture subtle failure modes like partial prompt misalignment, compositional errors, and visually plausible but semantically incorrect generations. Moreover, prior fine-tuned evaluators (e.g., EvalMuse) depend on 40–50K human-annotated ratings, raising scalability concerns. The paper asks whether a compact VLM-as-a-judge can be trained entirely without human annotations while jointly assessing text-image alignment and image quality in a truly dynamic, open-set manner.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and a generated RGB image, DynEval first produces a set of atomic binary verification questions grounded in the prompt (T2IA) and a scene graph with object-level quality questions grounded in the image (IQA). It then answers each question via VQA-style scoring on a 1–5 scale, averaging per-question scores into separate T2IA and IQA scores that are combined via a 0.5/0.5 weighted sum into a single overall evaluation score. The structured intermediate outputs (questions, scene graph, per-question scores) are retained as part of the training supervision, not just the final scalar.

## Method

DynEval full-fine-tunes Qwen3-VL-2B/4B on a 250K instruction dataset (DynEvalInstruct) distilled from the Qwen3-VL-235B teacher, using a curriculum that first teaches the model to generate structured questions and then to answer them, triggered by three task-specific tokens (<T2IA>, <IQA>, <EVALUATION>). The T2IA branch generates prompt-grounded atomic yes/no questions covering objects, attributes, spatial relations, and distortion checks; the IQA branch constructs a scene graph from the image (using the prompt as contextual reference to reduce hallucination) and decomposes each node into attribute-level quality questions targeting shape, texture, completeness, and 3D spatial cues. Training data is sourced from GenDB, a 500K prompt-image dataset built with a tier-matched generation strategy that pairs prompts and T2I models by complexity/capability tiers to maximize informative failure cases. The distillation avoids black-box score regression by transferring the teacher's full structured reasoning (questions, answers, scene graphs, per-question scores) rather than a single scalar, which the authors argue prevents the redundancy and score inflation that arise when VLMs are asked to generate and score questions in a single pass.

## Results

DynEval-4B achieves state-of-the-art SRCC on 9 out of 11 benchmarks, with headline scores of 0.8022 on TIFA, 0.7944 on EvalMI, 0.7932 on EvalMuse, and 0.7508 on GenEval2. Averaged across all 11 benchmarks, DynEval-4B surpasses the previous best evaluators by a relative margin of +4.77% in SRCC, with the largest gains on EvalMI (+9.65%) and UniGenBench++ (+20.87%). Scaling from DynEval-2B to DynEval-4B yields a consistent average relative SRCC improvement of +4.62% across all benchmarks. Notably, DynEval-4B uses only 4B parameters, half the size of the strongest 8B baselines (GenEval2, T2I-CoReB), yet matches or exceeds them on most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynEval-4B outperforms all 13 compared baselines on 9 of 11 benchmarks in SRCC. Against the strongest 8B competitors, GenEval2 and T2I-CoReB, DynEval-4B wins on 9 benchmarks but trails on RichHF (0.5457 vs. 0.6311 for GenEval2, a 9.41% relative gap) and GenEval (0.5894 vs. 0.6044 for GenEval2, a 2.48% relative gap), while using only half the parameters. Against the fine-tuned EvalMuse evaluator (FGA-BLIP2-OS), DynEval-4B achieves higher SRCC on all 7 shared benchmarks (e.g., 0.7932 vs. 0.7742 on EvalMuse, 0.8022 vs. 0.7604 on TIFA), and crucially does so without any human annotations during training. Compared to VQA-based methods like VQAScore and TIFA, DynEval-4B shows substantial gains (e.g., 0.8022 vs. 0.6951 on TIFA, 0.7944 vs. 0.6062 on EvalMI), demonstrating that jointly evaluating alignment and image quality via scene-graph-grounded questions captures failure modes that prompt-only VQA misses. Against traditional metrics (CLIPScore, ImageReward, HPSv2, PickScore), DynEval-4B dominates by wide margins across all benchmarks, confirming that distribution- or preference-based scores are insufficient for fine-grained T2I evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynEval sits at the intersection of VQA-based T2I evaluation (TIFA, DPG-Bench, TIIF-Bench) and knowledge distillation, but departs from both in key ways. Unlike prior VQA-based methods that rely on external proprietary LLMs at inference time to decompose prompts into questions, DynEval trains a compact VLM end-to-end to generate its own dynamic, image-specific questions (via scene graphs for IQA) without any external model. It also breaks from the human-annotation-dependent line (EvalMuse-40K, EvalMI-50K) by constructing its 250K training set entirely through teacher-VLM distillation, and from alignment-only evaluators by jointly scoring text-image alignment and intrinsic image quality within a single unified model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first compact, annotation-free evaluator that jointly handles T2IA and IQA, making large-scale T2I model comparison practical without expensive human labeling or proprietary API calls. The tier-matched data construction strategy and the finding that performance saturates around 250K distilled samples offer a practical recipe for building evaluators in other generative domains. Open questions remain: whether the scene-graph-based IQA captures all perceptual quality dimensions (e.g., global aesthetics, style coherence), whether the distillation approach transfers to video or 3D generation, and whether the 4B model's remaining gap to 8B evaluators on RichHF and GenEval reflects a fundamental capacity limit or a data-coverage issue.
