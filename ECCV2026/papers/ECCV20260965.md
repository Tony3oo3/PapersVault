## Problem

Existing VLM-based driving systems process multi-view images independently, preventing spatially consistent 3D reasoning and causing computational cost to scale with frame count. Meanwhile, BEV representations offer geometric coherence but lack the semantic richness of foundation vision encoders because they cannot be pre-trained on large-scale image-text data. This paper addresses both gaps by demonstrating BEV's superiority for LLM spatial reasoning and proposing a mechanism to inject LLM semantic knowledge into BEV encoders.

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-view camera images (6 cameras, nuScenes format) and natural-language driving queries. The BEV encoder produces a spatially consistent grid that is max-pooled to 50×50 (2,500 tokens), which replaces the original vision encoder tokens in the VLM. Object locations in VQA questions are converted from image-plane pixel coordinates to ego-centric metric descriptions (e.g., '3 m in front, 1.5 m to the left') to match the BEV spatial frame. Downstream, the distilled BEV encoder feeds UniAD or VAD pipelines that output driving trajectories evaluated via open-loop L2 error and closed-loop NeuroNCAP safety scores.

## Method

BEVLM connects a BEV encoder (from BEVFormer/UniAD) to a frozen LLM (InternVL3 or DeepSeek-VL) through a lightweight MLP projector, following the LLaVA-style token projection pattern. The core contribution is representation-level semantic distillation: the frozen LLM acts as a fixed semantic teacher, and the BEV encoder (student) is trained to reshape its feature grid so that projected tokens satisfy the LLM's VQA reasoning requirements, using the LLM's cross-entropy loss as a differentiable proxy for an inaccessible ideal semantic embedding. To prevent catastrophic forgetting of spatial structure, distillation is jointly trained with the original object-detection task for one epoch with equal loss weights. The distilled BEV encoder is then frozen and plugged into UniAD or VAD end-to-end pipelines, where only task-specific decoders are retrained. Ablations confirm that the gains originate from the pretrained LLM's semantic latent space rather than from the auxiliary VQA head itself, and that Behavior/Planning questions reduce impact velocity more than Perception/Prediction questions.

## Results

On the Ego3D cross-view spatial reasoning benchmark, BEV tokens (B_UniAD) achieve 61.34% MCQ accuracy, a 46.0% improvement over perspective-view ViT tokens (28.57%), while also reducing L1 distance error by 21.8% (7.05 m vs. 14.15 m). On the NeuroNCAP closed-loop safety benchmark, distilling with an 8B LLM raises the UniAD safety score from 2.38 to 3.05 (+28.2%) and lowers the collision rate from 0.56 to 0.47; transferring the same distilled encoder to VAD improves the score from 3.24 to 3.42 and reduces collision rate from 0.37 to 0.34. On open-loop nuScenes, the distilled BEV reduces average L2 trajectory error from 1.05 m to 0.97 m (UniAD) and from 0.82 m to 0.74 m (VAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the UniAD baseline (no distillation), BEVLM with an 8B teacher LLM improves the NeuroNCAP safety score by 28.2% (2.38 → 3.05) and reduces collision rate from 0.56 to 0.47, while the 1B teacher yields a more modest 23.1% score gain (2.38 → 2.93) with nearly unchanged collision rate. Against VLM-AD-style distillation (both the CLIP-embedding variant at 2.07 and the random-transformer variant at 2.02), BEVLM's 8B result (3.05) substantially outperforms both, which in fact fall below the undistilled baseline (2.38), indicating that CLIP-space supervision transfers weaker driving semantics than token-level LLM distillation. A randomly initialized auxiliary VQA head (1.75 score, 0.73 CR) performs worse than the baseline, confirming that the gains originate from the pretrained LLM's semantic latent space rather than from the auxiliary task structure itself. On the VAD pipeline, the same 8B-distilled BEV encoder (frozen, with only the VAD decoder retrained) improves the NeuroNCAP score from 3.24 to 3.42 and lowers CR from 0.37 to 0.34, demonstrating cross-architecture generalizability. On the Ego3D cross-view reasoning task, BEV tokens (B_UniAD, 44M encoder, 2,500 tokens) match or exceed a 10× larger fine-tuned ViT encoder (400M, 4,608 tokens) in MCQ accuracy (61.34% vs. 62.19%) while using far fewer parameters, and surpass the unfine-tuned ViT by 46.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BEV-based end-to-end driving pipelines of UniAD and VAD, which themselves rely on the BEVFormer encoder, and adopts the LLaVA-style MLP projector pattern to bridge visual features into an LLM's token space. It departs from the dominant VLM-for-driving paradigm (DriveLM, DriveVLM, Senna, etc.) that feeds independently extracted multi-view image tokens to the LLM, arguing instead that a spatially consistent BEV grid is a superior visual input for spatial reasoning. The semantic distillation step is novel in framing the frozen LLM as a fixed teacher that reshapes the BEV encoder's feature space via VQA supervision, rather than performing output-level distillation or multi-task learning with a trainable head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work bridges a fundamental gap: BEV encoders provide geometric structure but lack the semantic richness of foundation vision encoders, while LLMs offer commonsense reasoning but struggle with 3D spatial consistency when fed independent view tokens. The demonstrated 28.2% closed-loop safety improvement on NeuroNCAP is practically significant for safety-critical autonomous driving, and the finding that a 44M BEV encoder matches a 400M ViT for LLM spatial reasoning suggests representation quality can substitute for model scale. Key limitations include reliance on a single VQA dataset (DriveLM-nuScenes), evaluation on only two E2E pipelines, and the absence of LLM-based (VLA) control, which the authors explicitly defer to future work. Scaling the distillation to more diverse and semantically rich VQA data, and validating on additional driving benchmarks, remain open questions.
