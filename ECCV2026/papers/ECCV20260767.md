## Problem

Existing online novel-view synthesis methods for dynamic scenes require many input viewpoints, rely on iterative per-frame optimization, and depend on external pose estimation pipelines (e.g., COLMAP) that introduce additional latency. These limitations prevent true real-time streaming at standard 30 FPS frame rates from sparse-view inputs, blocking practical deployment in live broadcasting, VR streaming, and interactive media.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260767/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step, the method receives two unposed RGB video frames (sparse-view, no precomputed camera poses) and a user-specified target camera pose expressed in the physical 3D space of a canonical reference frame. It outputs a single photorealistic novel-view image at the target pose, processed in a fully feedforward pass at 32 FPS on a single A100 GPU. Evaluation uses 300-frame sequences at 256 resolution, and the method requires no offline preprocessing such as Gaussian initialization or COLMAP pose estimation.

## Method

REON-NVS comprises a feedforward pose estimator that predicts 9-dimensional latent camera poses (3D translation + 6D rotation) via self-supervised RGB reconstruction loss rather than GT pose supervision, and a pose mapper (MLP) that translates physical target poses into the latent space using a reference pose pair to resolve scale ambiguity. The scene reconstructor is built on Mamba2-based bidirectional SSM blocks: a history fusion module propagates SSM hidden states across time steps as temporal memory to aggregate past-frame information into a coarse target-view prediction, while a view refinement module (hybrid Bi-SSM + Transformer blocks) refines the coarse output using only current multi-view inputs to recover fine details lost in SSM compression. Training is two-phase: first, the pose estimator and reconstructor are jointly trained self-supervised on RealEstate10K static video clips; second, the model is fine-tuned on dynamic data (the authors' new 150-scene dataset plus SelfCap) and the pose mapper is trained supervised. A VGG-feature-based flicker loss enforces temporal consistency between consecutive frames.

## Results

On the Neural 3D Video benchmark (fixed cameras), REON-NVS achieves 23.63 PSNR, 0.715 SSIM, 0.199 LPIPS, and 8.97 flicker at 32 FPS, outperforming all baselines on every quality metric while being the fastest method. On the authors' moving-camera dataset, it reaches 23.26 PSNR and 0.617 SSIM at 32 FPS, again leading all competitors. The method surpasses the closest feedforward baseline (RayZer) by +1.6 dB PSNR on Neural 3D Video and +1.7 dB on the moving-camera dataset, while reducing flicker by more than 2×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REON-NVS outperforms all six baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across both evaluation settings. Against the closest feedforward geometry-free baseline RayZer [12], it gains +1.6 dB PSNR (23.63 vs 21.97) and reduces flicker from 24.16 to 8.97 on Neural 3D Video, attributed to SSM-based temporal memory that RayZer lacks. Against the streamable 3DGS-based methods (3DGStream, HiCoM, IGS), REON-NVS leads by 3.3–4.0 dB PSNR while running 6–53× faster (32 FPS vs 0.6–5.5 FPS), and requires no offline Gaussian initialization or COLMAP pose estimation. DepthSplat [36], a feedforward method that still relies on ground-truth poses and explicit 3D Gaussians, trails REON-NVS by 3.8 dB PSNR, confirming the advantage of the geometry-free formulation. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is flicker on Neural 3D Video, where IGS achieves 6.40 vs REON-NVS's 8.97, but IGS runs at only 5.5 FPS and requires per-scene initialization, making it impractical for real-time streaming.

## Relation to Prior Work

REON-NVS sits at the intersection of two recent threads: geometry-free feedforward NVS (RayZer, LVSM) that avoids explicit 3D representations, and state-space models (Mamba2, Vision Mamba) that offer efficient long-range sequence modeling. It departs from prior streamable methods (3DGStream, HiCoM, IGS) by eliminating iterative 3D Gaussian optimization and external pose pipelines (COLMAP), replacing them with a self-supervised latent pose estimator and an SSM-based scene reconstructor. The key architectural departure is using SSM hidden states as a compressed temporal memory to maintain cross-frame consistency, rather than accumulating explicit 3D scene representations that require re-optimization as new frames arrive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to close the full loop—pose estimation, reconstruction, and rendering—in a single feedforward pass at 32 FPS from just two unposed views, making real-time free-viewpoint streaming from commodity multi-camera rigs practically feasible. The 150-scene moving-camera dataset addresses a real gap: existing benchmarks (Neural 3D Video, SelfCap) use fixed cameras and few scenes, limiting evaluation of in-the-wild streaming. Open questions include whether the SSM temporal memory degrades over very long sequences (hundreds of frames), how the method scales beyond two input views or to higher resolutions, and whether the non-physically-interpretable latent pose space can support more complex camera control (e.g., arbitrary orbiting) without the pose mapper's scale-ambiguity workaround.
