## Problem

Single-view strand-level 3D hair reconstruction is inherently ill-posed: visible regions provide only partial geometric cues, and existing methods rely on small-scale, style-restricted synthetic datasets whose priors are insufficient to produce stylistically consistent hair in occluded regions. Reconstructed hair often appears plausible from the frontal view but suffers from severe artifacts or style inconsistency under unseen viewpoints, limiting its use in digital avatars and AR/VR applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260756/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single frontal-view RGB portrait as input, which is first aligned to a template body mesh via landmark fitting. It produces strand-level 3D hair geometry (approximately 100k strands) as the final output, along with intermediate calibrated multi-view images synthesized along a 360° orbital path (upscaled via Flux.1-dev for fine hair texture), per-view orientation maps, and per-view depth maps derived from a 2D Gaussian Splatting fit. The multi-view images are generated at a lower resolution by the video diffusion model and then super-resolved to ensure sufficient detail for orientation extraction.

## Method

HairOrbit reformulates single-view hair reconstruction as a calibrated multi-view problem by fine-tuning the WAN video diffusion model with LoRA adapters on a small set of synthetic multi-view hair renderings, enabling generation of temporally consistent 360° orbital views from a single input image. A U-Net orientation extractor, trained on frontal-view annotations from HiSa but predicting undirectional (rather than directional) orientation maps, generalizes to full-view orientation estimation with significantly lower angular error than Gabor filters or HairStep. A hybrid implicit field unifies orientation and occupancy into a single network that predicts unit-length orientation vectors inside the hair volume and zero vectors outside, making strand growth fully parallelizable for a fixed number of steps without boundary checks. A combined scalp-root and segment-based growing strategy fills gaps left by root-only growth, while a re-projection step recovers collapsed buzz-cut strands near the scalp.

## Results

On single-view 3D hair strand reconstruction evaluated over 196 hairstyles, HairOrbit achieves a HairSale angle error of 12.83°, HairRida of 80.52%, and IoU of 0.847, outperforming all baselines on every metric. For full-view orientation extraction on 395 annotated images, the neural orientation extractor reduces mean angular error to 5.13° versus 10.88° for HairStep and 16.05° for Gabor filters. The multi-view generation module produces novel views with L1 error 0.049, PSNR 20.897, LPIPS 0.162, and CLIP similarity 0.834 to the input view.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HairOrbit outperforms all three single-view baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor Im2Haircut [30], it reduces HairSale angle error from 16.24° to 12.83° (−3.41°), improves HairRida from 79.38% to 80.52%, and raises IoU from 0.757 to 0.847. Against HairStep [42], the gains are larger: HairSale drops from 17.38° to 12.83° and IoU jumps from 0.639 to 0.847, reflecting HairStep's inability to reconstruct invisible side/back regions. DiffLocks [25] is the weakest baseline, with HairSale of 26.50° and IoU of 0.593, as it generates scalp texture latents without sufficient spatial correspondence to the input image. For orientation extraction, the proposed neural extractor (5.13° mean angular error) more than halves the error of HairStep's strand-map-based approach (10.88°) and triples the accuracy of Gabor filters (16.05°), particularly on curly and back-view samples where Gabor degrades to 24.56°.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of single-view 3D hair reconstruction (HairStep, Im2Haircut, Difflocks) and multi-view hair capture (MonoHair, DeepMVSHair, GroomCap), but departs from both by using a video diffusion model (WAN) fine-tuned with LoRA to synthesize calibrated multi-view images from a single portrait, thereby converting the ill-posed single-view problem into a multi-view one. For orientation estimation, it builds on HairStep's neural extractor but redefines the learning target from directional strand maps to undirectional orientation maps, enabling generalization beyond frontal views. The hybrid implicit field unifies the separate occupancy and orientation fields used in prior work (DeepSketch2Hair, HairStep) into a single representation that enables fully parallel strand growth, and the strand-growing strategy combines scalp-rooted growth with segment-based filling from MonoHair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-view bridging strategy offers a practical path to high-fidelity single-view hair reconstruction without requiring expensive multi-view capture rigs, which is important for consumer-facing avatar and AR/VR applications. The ablation showing a 142% degradation when multi-view synthesis is removed underscores that the generative prior is not merely helpful but essential for plausible unseen-region geometry. Limitations include continued reliance on synthetic 3D hair datasets (USC-HairSalon, Difflocks) for training the field and LoRA, sensitivity to the quality of synthesized views for downstream orientation extraction, and persistent challenges in extremely thin structures like buzz cuts. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to dynamic hair (wind, motion) and whether the video-diffusion multi-view prior can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives to reduce inference cost.
