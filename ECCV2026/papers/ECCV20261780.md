## Problem

Long video understanding with MLLMs is hampered by severe temporal redundancy: uniform sampling misses semantically critical moments, while purely query-relevance-based selection overlooks content diversity and produces redundant frames. Existing approaches lack a principled mechanism that jointly models query relevance and content structure to allocate a limited frame budget across temporally heterogeneous video segments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261780/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video uniformly sampled at 1 fps (yielding N frames) and a natural-language query as input, and produces a compact keyframe subset of size N′ (default 64, where N′ ≪ N). The selected frames are then passed to an off-the-shelf Video-LLM (e.g., LLaVA-Video, Qwen3-VL, InternVL) together with the query to generate the final answer. All scoring relies on a frozen BLIP-2 image-text matching function and BLIP-2 visual features; no gradients flow through the selection pipeline.

## Method

QCA operates in two stages. First, the video is split into S=12 uniform temporal segments, and each segment receives a frame budget proportional to a weighted combination of its average query-relevance score (mean ITM) and its content deviation (L2 distance of segment mean from global mean plus trace of segment covariance), both softmax-normalized before fusion. Second, within each segment, selection is anchored on the single most query-relevant frame; a candidate pool is then restricted to frames whose ITM score exceeds γ (0.7) times the anchor score, and remaining frames are greedily added by maximizing the sum of Euclidean distances to the already-selected keyframes (a farthest-point-sampling criterion). The entire pipeline is training-free, model-agnostic, and adds only ~0.005 s of selection overhead on top of the ITM encoding cost.

## Results

With Qwen3-VL-8B under a 64-frame budget, QCA achieves 66.9% on LongVideoBench, 69.5% on Video-MME, 75.7% on MLVU, and 51.8% on LVBench, yielding average gains of 3.8–8.0% over uniform sampling. The method is training-free and model-agnostic, delivering consistent improvements across LLaVA-Video-7B (avg. +4.00%), InternVL-3.5-8B (avg. +3.20%), and Qwen3-VL-8B (avg. +4.78%). With Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B, QCA reaches 69.9% on LongVideoBench and 77.1% on MLVU, surpassing GPT-4o (66.7%, 64.6%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (64.0%, 33.1% on LVBench) using only 64 frames versus 256 frames for GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QCA consistently outperforms all keyframe selection baselines across all four benchmarks and three MLLM backbones. Against the strongest recent methods, QCA beats AKS [27] by 2.1% on LVBench and 2.2% on Video-MME (InternVL-3.5-8B), and surpasses Q-Frame [38] by 2.2% on Video-MME and 1.0% on MLVU (Qwen3-VL-8B). Notably, QCA also outperforms the trained method OneClip-RAG [8] by 2.9% on MLVU (LLaVA-Video-7B) without any fine-tuning, and exceeds FRAG [14] and E-VRAG [34] by 2.4% and 3.9% respectively on Video-MME and MLVU. At the SOTA Video-LLM level (Table 2), QCA with Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B surpasses GPT-4o by 12.5% on MLVU (77.1 vs. 64.6) and Gemini-1.5-Pro by 19.5% on LVBench (52.6 vs. 33.1), while using only 64 frames versus 256 for GPT-4o. Compared to the token-pruning approach ForestPrune [16] under an identical 64-frame effective budget, QCA gains 1.9% on Video-MME and 3.6% on MLVU (LLaVA-Video-7B), confirming that proactive frame selection before encoding is more effective than post-encoding token pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a structured two-level framework for keyframe selection that repurposes well-known primitives—image-text matching scores (via BLIP-2/CLIP), farthest-point-style diversity maximization, and softmax-based budget allocation—into a unified query- and content-aware pipeline. It departs from prior relevance-only methods (Top-k, Q-Frame) by explicitly modeling content deviation at the segment level, and from diversity-only or clustering-based approaches by anchoring selection to the most query-relevant frame. Unlike trained selection methods such as OneClip-RAG or BOLT, QCA is entirely training-free and model-agnostic, positioning itself as a plug-and-play preprocessing step for any existing Video-LLM rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in its training-free, backbone-agnostic design: a single selection module improves LLaVA-Video, InternVL, and Qwen3-VL simultaneously without any fine-tuning, making it immediately deployable in production Video-LLM pipelines. The paper acknowledges that uniform temporal segmentation is a simplification and identifies content-aware segmentation (e.g., shot boundary detection) as a natural next step. An open question is whether the BLIP-2-based ITM scores remain the bottleneck for very long or domain-specific videos, and whether the fixed segment count S=12 generalizes to videos of vastly different lengths without the naive linear scaling the authors tested and rejected.
