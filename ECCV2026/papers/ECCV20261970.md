## Problem

VLM-driven embodied agents excel at task completion but fail to proactively handle dynamically emerging physical hazards during closed-loop household interactions. Existing safety approaches rely on runtime guardrails that block or abort unsafe actions, creating a fundamental safety-progress trade-off where agents either stall task execution or miss latent risks. The paper tackles the missing capability for proactive, progress-preserving hazard mitigation under partial observability, where risks emerge from state-intent couplings rather than explicit malicious instructions.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes RGB visual observations from the OmniGibson simulator and a natural-language household task instruction at each timestep, producing a parameterized skill action alongside a predictive one-step intent used as a proactive lookahead probe. The RSG belief state is maintained over a finite vocabulary of functional/safety tags and topological predicates (e.g., SAFETY_FLAMMABLE, NEXTTO) rather than dense scene descriptions, keeping the representation risk-sufficient and instance-decoupled. At episode completion, the system outputs evolved procedural Meta-Skills structured along PURPOSE, WHEN, HOW, GROUNDING, TIMING, and CONSTRAINTS dimensions, refined through the Test-Verify-Write loop.

## Method

The Just-In-Time Memory framework coordinates three specialized modules: (1) a Risk-Sufficient Topological Belief Graph (RSG) updated via action-conditioned patches over a k-hop local focus region, preserving occluded hazardous states under POMDP without full-scene re-parsing; (2) an Agency-Grounded Factual Memory that compiles abstract safety norms into deterministic triggerable rules over the RSG vocabulary, activating only when a predictive intent couples with a local state (situational risk) or a persistent unsafe state exists (temporal risk); and (3) an Experience Memory storing decontextualized procedural Meta-Skills that inject executable mitigation guidance with precise grounding and timing into the planner prompt. A key differentiator is the Test-Verify-Write evolution loop: after each episode, execution traces are programmatically verified against triggered factual rules using historical RSG snapshots, mining minimal causal case patches that are routed to success/failure buffers, which then drive the agent to contrast and refine its Meta-Skills at test time without manual annotation.

## Results

On IS-Bench, the Just-In-Time Memory framework achieves a Safe Success (SS) rate of 48.7% on Qwen3-VL-8B (+30.3% over Vanilla) while simultaneously improving Task Success (TS) from 69.3% to 71.3%, breaking the safety–progress trade-off. On GPT-4o, SS reaches 66.2% with TS of 81.3%, and the Post (temporal risk) metric surges from 72.0% to 96.8%. Across all three VLM backbones with full results (Qwen3-VL-8B, 32B, GPT-4o), the method consistently outperforms both Vanilla and Safe-CoT on every metric, with the largest absolute gains in Post (up to +43.2 pp on Qwen3-VL-8B) and Pre (up to +55.5 pp on Qwen3-VL-32B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Safe-CoT (the primary baseline from IS-Bench [9]), the proposed method wins on every metric across all three fully-evaluated backbones: on Qwen3-VL-8B it achieves SS 48.7% vs. 30.2% while also recovering TS to 71.3% (Safe-CoT drops TS to 57.6%), and on GPT-4o it reaches SS 66.2% vs. 31.2% with TS 81.3% vs. 71.7%. This demonstrates that step-wise safety reasoning induces excessive caution that stalls task progress, whereas just-in-time triggering preserves both safety and efficiency. Against the proprietary upper-bound references, Qwen3-VL-8B with the proposed framework (SS 48.7%) surpasses both GPT-5.2 with Safe-CoT (SS 45.3%) and Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview with Safe-CoT (SS 46.0%), suggesting that structured memory and procedural Meta-Skills can be more effective than raw model scale for interactive safety. The Post metric gap is particularly striking: the method reaches 89.4–96.8% temporal risk mitigation versus 61.1–77.2% for Safe-CoT, confirming that persistent RSG-based state tracking is the key differentiator over per-step re-reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of work: memory-augmented embodied planning (KARMA, RoboMemory, Voyager), which optimizes for task completion and dense scene reconstruction but lacks safety awareness, and reactive safety enforcement (Agentspec, Plug in the Safety Chip, LTL/DSL-based guardrails), which treats safety as task refusal rather than hazard resolution. Rather than building on a single prior method, it synthesizes scene-graph memory and skill-library ideas into a unified Just-In-Time Memory framework, introducing a state-agency conditioned formalization that reframes interactive hazards as bipartite functions of environmental state and agent intent. The Test-Verify-Write loop for test-time Meta-Skill evolution draws on the broader test-time adaptation literature but is novel in its RSG-grounded, annotation-free verification of execution traces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in embodied AI—safety mechanisms that block execution stall task progress—by demonstrating that structured, just-in-time memory can achieve both high safe-success and high task-success simultaneously. The state-agency formalization provides a computable, instance-decoupled foundation for proactive safety that could generalize beyond household tasks, and the test-time evolution loop offers a practical path to self-improving agents without manual annotation. Key limitations include evaluation on a single simulation benchmark (IS-Bench, 161 tasks in OmniGibson), a finite RSG vocabulary tied to the evaluated rule set, and reliance on a separate auxiliary VLM for graph updates. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex, multi-agent, or real-world robotic settings and whether the finite tag vocabulary can be automatically extended to novel domains.
