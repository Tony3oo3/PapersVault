## Problem

Existing cross-view geo-localization methods rely on explicit geometric warping (polar or BEV projections) to reduce viewpoint discrepancies, but these transformations depend on restrictive spatial assumptions (e.g., planar-ground, center-alignment) and inevitably introduce severe visual distortions that corrupt co-visible cues and yield noisy supervision. The paper argues the core challenge is not reducing geometric discrepancy but reliably discovering the shared scene evidence (consensus) underlying both observations, which warping-based approaches actively undermine.

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired street-view image (140×768 or 384×768) and a satellite image (384×384) as input and produces compact L2-normalized descriptors (dimension D = K×C, with K=64 probes by default) in a shared metric space for retrieval. During training, a third joint-view descriptor is also produced and used as a semantic anchor, but at inference the joint-view branch is entirely discarded, so retrieval uses only the street and satellite descriptors with zero additional test-time overhead.

## Method

The framework has three components operating on a shared ConvNeXt-B backbone. First, Unified Joint-view Encoding concatenates street and aerial token sequences and applies global multi-head self-attention, allowing each view's tokens to dynamically borrow corroborative evidence from the other and produce a joint-view representation that encodes cross-view consensus. Second, Global Pattern Probes (GPP) — a set of K learnable prototypes refined via self-attention — serve as a shared semantic dictionary: the same probes query all three streams (street, satellite, joint) through cross-attention, projecting heterogeneous tokens into a strictly aligned metric space and filtering out view-specific noise. Third, a consensus-mediated contrastive objective extends symmetric InfoNCE with two auxiliary branches (street↔joint and satellite↔joint) that explicitly pull single-view embeddings toward the joint-view anchor, distilling the consensus-mining capability into the single-view encoders so the joint branch can be removed at inference.

## Results

The method achieves state-of-the-art Recall@1 across all four benchmarks: 99.29% on CVUSA, 92.23% on CVACT-Val, 74.20% on CVACT-Test, and 83.15% on VIGOR (same-area). On the more challenging VIGOR cross-area split, it reaches 73.60% R@1 and 83.01% Hit Rate, outperforming the strongest baseline (Co-Retrieval) by 1.41% and 2.37% in R@1 and R@5 respectively. On University-1652, it attains 97.42% R@1 and 97.84% AP for Drone→Satellite, and 97.72% R@1 and 96.40% AP for Satellite→Drone, surpassing all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all ten baselines on every benchmark. On CVUSA and CVACT, the closest competitor is Co-Retrieval [38] (BEV-based), which the method surpasses by 0.54–0.52% in R@1; Sample4Geo [8] (symmetric InfoNCE) trails by 0.58–0.69% in R@1. On VIGOR, the advantage over Co-Retrieval widens to 1.41% R@1 (same-area) and 1.41% R@1 / 2.37% R@5 (cross-area), while polar-warping methods (SAFA [23], GeoDTR [40]) degrade severely under the non-center-aligned VIGOR protocol (33.93% and 56.51% R@1 same-area, respectively). On University-1652, the method leads QDFL [14] by 2.42% R@1 (Drone→Sat) and 0.57% R@1 (Sat→Drone), and outperforms the earlier Sample4Geo [8] by 4.77% R@1 on Drone→Sat. Notably, the method does not use any geometric warping at inference, yet consistently beats warping-based approaches (SAFA, GeoDTR, Co-Retrieval), supporting the paper's claim that feature-space consensus mining is more robust than pixel-level geometric alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant geometry-based paradigm (polar warping in SAFA/LPN, BEV projection in GeoDTR/Co-Retrieval) that forces one view to geometrically mimic the other, arguing instead that such warping introduces distortions that corrupt co-visible cues. It builds directly on Sample4Geo's symmetric InfoNCE objective and shared-backbone design, but augments them with a training-time joint-view pathway that enables direct cross-view token interaction via global self-attention, and introduces globally shared pattern probes (conceptually related to Deformable DETR queries and BoQ) applied across heterogeneous streams to enforce a unified semantic basis. The key conceptual shift is replacing pixel-level geometric alignment with feature-space consensus mining, where a joint-view representation acts as a semantic anchor that distills co-visible evidence into the single-view encoders during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit geometric warping is not a prerequisite for strong cross-view geo-localization, offering a cleaner and more generalizable alternative that avoids the restrictive planar-ground and center-alignment assumptions underlying polar and BEV methods. The zero-overhead inference (joint-view branch discarded at test time) makes the approach practically attractive for deployment. The notably larger gains under VIGOR's cross-area split (1.41% R@1 over Co-Retrieval) suggest the consensus-mining formulation transfers better under domain shift than branch-based geometric compensation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even more extreme viewpoint regimes (e.g., lunar rover-to-orbital matching) and whether the consensus mechanism can be adapted to other cross-domain retrieval tasks beyond geo-localization.
