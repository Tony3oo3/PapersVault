## Problem

Existing radiance field splatting methods rely on convex or fixed-shape primitives (Gaussian disks, rigid triangles, smooth convex hulls), which cannot represent non-convex or curved local geometry without excessive primitive proliferation. This forces a trade-off between geometric fidelity and rendering efficiency, as curved surfaces and concave structures demand many small primitives to approximate what a single flexible shape could capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260588/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse SfM point cloud (one triangle per 3D point, with vertices randomly oriented and scaled by nearest-neighbor distance) and RGB training images with known camera poses. It produces a soup of deformable triangle primitives—each carrying three 3D vertices, K=3 learnable scalar displacements per edge, a corner-smoothness parameter δ, a base opacity o, a falloff exponent σ, and SH color coefficients—rendered in real-time via front-to-back α-blending. All per-pixel deformation and distance computations are performed in a canonical barycentric frame, making the learned shape strictly view-independent.

## Method

Each triangle primitive is augmented with K learnable control points per edge, each parameterized by a single scalar displacement along the edge normal in barycentric coordinate space, producing a piecewise-linear non-convex boundary while preserving the three base vertices that define the 3D plane. A ray-casting winding number test classifies pixels as inside or outside the deformed polygon, handling concavities and self-intersections robustly. A polynomial smooth minimum (C¹-continuous, no transcendental functions) over point-to-segment distances yields a distance field whose corner rounding is governed by a learnable parameter δ, and a power-law window function with normalization radius computed via the shoelace formula converts this distance to per-pixel opacity. Training combines photometric L1, D-SSIM, opacity, distortion, normal-consistency, size, and curvature losses, with a 3000-iteration warm-up that delays control-point learning until vertex positions settle, followed by adaptive midpoint-subdivision densification that interpolates displacement profiles onto child edges.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, DETRIS achieves the best LPIPS among non-volumetric methods across all splits: 0.206 (outdoor), 0.155 (indoor), and 0.183 (average), while also attaining the best indoor PSNR (31.13 dB) and SSIM (0.930). On Tanks & Temples, it records the best LPIPS (0.133) and PSNR (23.31 dB) among non-volumetric baselines. Rendering speed is 43 FPS on Mip-NeRF 360 and 79 FPS on T&T, with a model size of 946 MB, roughly 2× the training time of Triangle Splatting due to the additional per-pixel distance computations over M boundary segments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DETRIS outperforms Triangle Splatting [14] on every average metric across all three dataset splits (LPIPS 0.183 vs 0.191, outdoor PSNR 24.28 vs 24.27, indoor PSNR 31.13 vs 30.80, T&T LPIPS 0.133 vs 0.143), with the gap driven by better boundary fidelity at fine structures. Against 3DGS [21], DETRIS wins decisively on indoor metrics (LPIPS 0.155 vs 0.189, PSNR 31.13 vs 30.41) and average LPIPS (0.183 vs 0.214), but 3DGS retains an edge on outdoor PSNR (24.64 vs 24.28) and SSIM (0.731 vs 0.725) due to its volumetric soft blending. Versus 3DCS [15], DETRIS improves average LPIPS (0.183 vs 0.207) and outdoor SSIM (0.725 vs 0.700) while 3DCS edges ahead on indoor PSNR (31.33 vs 31.13). Among volumetric methods, DBS [28] achieves the highest indoor PSNR (32.29) and SSIM (0.936), but DETRIS surpasses it on LPIPS (0.183 vs 0.234) and outdoor PSNR (24.28 vs 25.10 is actually DBS winning; correction: DETRIS wins on LPIPS and T&T LPIPS 0.133 vs 0.140). DETRIS clearly dominates 2DGS [17] and BBSplat [46] on all reported metrics, with LPIPS improvements of 0.069 and 0.053 respectively on the Mip-NeRF 360 average. The main trade-off is rendering speed: at 43 FPS, DETRIS is slower than TS (97 FPS) and 3DGS (134 FPS) due to the M-segment distance field, though it remains comparable to 3DCS (25 FPS) and BBSplat (25 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Triangle Splatting (TS) [14] by augmenting each rigid triangle with K learnable control points per edge that displace the boundary along edge normals in a canonical barycentric coordinate frame, enabling non-convex shape representation while preserving the three base vertices that define the 3D plane. The overall splatting pipeline (tile-based rasterization, front-to-back α-blending, photometric + D-SSIM loss) follows 3DGS [21], and distortion/normal-consistency losses are adopted from 2DGS [17]. The key conceptual departure from prior non-volumetric methods (2DGS disks, 3DCS convex hulls, TS rigid triangles) is the unified deformable boundary formulation, and the key technical departure is performing all per-pixel deformation and distance computations in barycentric space rather than image space, which the authors show eliminates view-dependent artifacts that affect even TS's base window function under perspective foreshortening.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to represent non-convex shapes (arrows, crescents, concavities) with a single primitive rather than over-tessellating with many convex ones addresses a practical limitation of all prior non-volumetric splatting methods, potentially reducing primitive counts for complex geometry. The view-independent barycentric formulation is a clean, generalizable design principle that could inform future deformable-primitive methods. However, the method still lags TS in rendering speed (43 vs. 97 FPS) and training time (~2×), and it does not yet handle semi-transparent or thin structures, which the authors flag as future work. The modest but consistent quality gains (best LPIPS among non-volumetric methods on all splits) suggest the approach is a practical step forward rather than a paradigm shift.
