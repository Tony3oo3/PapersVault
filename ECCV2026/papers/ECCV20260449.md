## Problem

Diffusion models excel at standalone image synthesis but fail on structured multi-page documents: they overwrite or alter foreground text and figures, produce visually disjointed backgrounds across pages, and ignore readability constraints. Existing document-editing systems either rely on heavy human prompting, assume clean separable layers, or treat the document as a flat image rather than a layered composition, leaving no principled mechanism to protect dense text while regenerating backgrounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a structured document page (A4 PDF or 16:9 slide) with fixed foreground content—text lines, bounding boxes, and embedded figures extracted via PyMuPDF and OpenCV—plus an optional natural-language style prompt. It produces a generated background image composited behind the untouched foreground, along with per-text-box semi-transparent rounded-rectangle RGBA overlays whose opacity is computed to meet WCAG 2.2 contrast (target ratio 7.0, 98% pixel coverage). The foreground layout, text, and figures are never re-rendered or modified; only the background layer and the readability overlay are synthesized.

## Method

The framework is training-free and built on BAGEL as the diffusion backbone, contributing three mechanisms. First, Latent Masking applies a soft, time-gated attenuation in latent space: a centered window covering fraction ρ of the token lattice is scaled by λ∈(0,1), and the effective velocity is computed as v′ = m⊙v_raw + (1−m)⊙stopgrad(v_raw), which suppresses diffusion updates in foreground regions without introducing hard binary boundaries. Second, Automated Readability Optimization (ARO) converts sRGB to linear light, computes per-pixel blended luminance L_blend(α) = αL_o + (1−α)L_bg, and searches for the minimal opacity α* such that at least 98% of pixels in each text box achieve a WCAG contrast ratio ≥ 7.0 against the text color, then draws a rounded-rectangle overlay at that opacity. Third, a Recursive Narrative Bank maintains multi-page consistency: a GPT-4o Summarization Model distills each page's text into a ≤5-word semantic label, and an Instruction Generation Model converts that label into a concise background-design prompt conditioned on a sliding window of prior page instructions, so stylistic motifs (palette, texture, tone) evolve coherently across the document.

## Results

On the three-page academic document benchmark, the proposed method (Prompt+Text mode) achieves 99.75% WCAG contrast coverage and 0.9665 OCR accuracy, substantially outperforming BAGEL (66.98%, 0.5536) and GPT-5 (55.02%, 0.5225). For multi-page consistency, CLIP MP Consistency reaches 0.6955 (vs. 0.5571 for BAGEL and 0.6870 for GPT-5), and LLM-judged design quality scores lead across all four dimensions (Layout 4.20, Color 4.43, Graphic Style 4.25, Compliance 4.40 on a 1–5 scale). In a 30-participant user study, 83.57% of votes favored the proposed method over GPT-5 (15.24%) and BAGEL (1.43%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: BAGEL (a unified multimodal editing system) and GPT-5 (a general-purpose multimodal model). Against BAGEL, the proposed method wins decisively on every metric, with the largest gaps in WCAG contrast coverage (99.75% vs. 66.98%, a 32.8-point improvement) and OCR accuracy (0.9665 vs. 0.5536), indicating that BAGEL's background synthesis frequently corrupts foreground text. CLIP MP Consistency also improves markedly (0.6955 vs. 0.5571), reflecting BAGEL's weaker cross-page stylistic coherence. Against GPT-5, the proposed method leads on all LLM-judged design dimensions (e.g., Layout 4.20 vs. 3.88, Compliance 4.40 vs. 4.12) and on readability metrics (WCAG 99.75% vs. 55.02%, OCR 0.9665 vs. 0.5225), while GPT-5's only relative strength is a marginally higher CLIP MP Consistency in the Prompt-only ablation (0.6870 vs. 0.6259); in the primary Prompt+Text mode, the proposed method edges ahead (0.6955 vs. 0.6870). The user study confirms these quantitative findings: 83.57% of participants preferred the proposed output, with BAGEL receiving only 1.43% of votes, underscoring that GPT-5's tendency to alter layout and hallucinate text makes it unsuitable for foreground-preserving document editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BAGEL, a unified multimodal editing framework, as its diffusion backbone, and positions itself as a training-free add-on that addresses two gaps BAGEL leaves open: foreground preservation during background synthesis and cross-page stylistic coherence. Conceptually, the latent masking mechanism draws an analogy to smooth barrier functions from control theory and numerical optimization, repurposing the idea of soft constraint enforcement for diffusion update suppression in text regions. The multi-page consistency module adapts the Recursive Narrative Bank from dialogue generation [23] to document design, and the ARO module imports WCAG 2.2 contrast mathematics into a generative pipeline—neither of which had been combined in prior document-editing systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, training-free path to accessible document design: 99.75% WCAG contrast coverage without manual tuning is a concrete accessibility win that general-purpose models (GPT-5 at 55%) fail to deliver. However, the evaluation is limited to synthetic three-page academic documents, and the reliance on GPT-4o for summarization and instruction generation (though swappable) introduces a quality ceiling and cost dependency. Open questions include scalability to long documents (50+ pages), handling of highly irregular or scanned layouts, and whether the soft-attenuation masking generalizes to non-text foreground elements like data tables or chemical structures without per-class tuning.
