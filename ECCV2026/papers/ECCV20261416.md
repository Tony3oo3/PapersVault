## Problem

Existing 3D understanding models are tightly coupled to a single geometric representation (point cloud, voxel, mesh, or 3D Gaussian) and degrade substantially when the encoding format changes, even though the underlying 3D semantics remain identical. This 'representation shift' is distinct from domain generalization (which assumes a fixed input structure) and is not addressed by existing multi-representation fusion methods, which either use separate encoders per format or couple inference to a fixed training-time representation set. The paper formalizes this gap as Representation Generalization (RG), requiring shared learning across heterogeneous representations, decoupled single-representation inference, and efficient extension to unseen representations.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any subset of four aligned 3D representations per object—point clouds (1,024 points), voxel grids, meshes (1,024 faces for classification/segmentation, 4,096 for reconstruction), and 3D Gaussian primitives—all normalized to a unit sphere and partitioned into 64 patches of 16 primitives. At test time, only a single representation may be available, and the model must still produce valid outputs without architectural modification. Outputs include reconstructed geometry and attributes (measured by Chamfer Distance and MSE), class labels (40-class accuracy on ModelNet40), and part-level segmentation masks (mIoU on ShapeNet-Part). For unseen representations, a lightweight LoRA adapter aligns new inputs to the learned token space while keeping the shared backbone frozen.

## Method

ReGen3D unifies heterogeneous 3D inputs through three modules. Unified Representation Tokenization (URT) maps each representation's primitives into a shared token space by selecting FPS anchors in R³, gathering KNN neighbors, applying a single shared linear projection to zero-padded attribute vectors, and aggregating with a permutation-invariant operator plus a learnable representation embedding—preserving native structural cues without explicit format conversion. Shared Memory Interaction (SMI) performs cross-attention where each representation's tokens attend to a shared context formed by concatenating all available representation tokens with a learnable memory bank, enabling cross-representation knowledge transfer during training while reducing to self-attention over the memory bank when only one representation is present at test time. Efficient Representation Adapter (ERA) extends the model to unseen representations via LoRA low-rank branches on the URT embedding layers and Transformer weights, keeping the shared backbone and memory frozen. Training proceeds in two stages: self-supervised reconstruction pretraining (masked autoencoder with 0.6 mask ratio, 300 epochs) across all available representations, followed by supervised finetuning for classification and segmentation, with no explicit pairwise alignment loss—cross-representation consistency is induced entirely through shared supervision on identical semantics and the SMI latent interaction.

## Results

Under the leave-one-representation-out (LORO) protocol on RepShift-3D, ReGen3D achieves 85.1% classification accuracy on unseen 3D Gaussian primitives, surpassing the strongest baseline ShapeSplat (45.2%) by 39.9 points, and 77.6% on unseen voxels vs. DGCNN's 48.3%. For segmentation on held-out representations, ReGen3D reaches 75.2/76.6/62.0/56.6 mIoU on Point/Voxel/Mesh/Gaussian, outperforming the best baselines by 28.5–10.5 points. On reconstruction of unseen representations, ReGen3D reduces Chamfer Distance to 32.1×10⁻³ on Mesh and 32.2×10⁻³ on Gaussian, compared to 52.4 and 48.8 from the best baselines (ShapeSplat and MeshMAE respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGen3D outperforms all eight representation-specific baselines across every held-out representation and both downstream tasks. The strongest single-representation baseline is ShapeSplat [34] (Gaussian), which reaches 61.2% on unseen Point classification and 46.7% mIoU on unseen Point segmentation, yet ReGen3D exceeds it by 17.2 and 28.5 points respectively. Among point-based methods, PCT [60] is the best on unseen Voxel classification (61.2%) but still trails ReGen3D (77.6%) by 16.4 points; Point-MAE [36] and DGCNN [37] are consistently weaker. Mesh-based methods (MeshNet [6], MeshMAE [27]) and VoxelNet [62] degrade most severely on non-native representations, often falling below 30% classification accuracy. The naive multi-representation cross-attention baseline also underperforms ReGen3D by 10–40 points, confirming that simple post-hoc fusion is insufficient. The largest absolute gains appear on unseen Gaussian (85.1% vs. 45.2% best baseline) and unseen Voxel segmentation (76.6% vs. 47.4%), where no single-representation method was trained on that format.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from three existing lines of work: representation-specific 3D models (PointNet, VoxelNet, MeshNet, ShapeSplat) that are architecturally locked to one geometric format; multi-representation fusion methods (Voxel-Mesh Network, Hunyuan3D) that use separate encoders with post-hoc interaction and assume a fixed representation set at inference; and domain generalization, which studies statistical distribution shift under a fixed input structure rather than structural variation from heterogeneous geometric encodings. ReGen3D builds on transformer-based 3D tokenization, shared-memory cross-attention mechanisms, and LoRA-style parameter-efficient adaptation, but recombines them under a new problem formulation (Representation Generalization) that requires decoupled single-representation inference and open-ended extension to unseen formats—properties none of the prior lines explicitly address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3D representations continue to proliferate (Gaussian splatting, neural fields, hybrid encodings), the inability of models to transfer across formats is a practical bottleneck that this paper formalizes and addresses with a clean LORO evaluation protocol and the RepShift-3D benchmark. The RepShift-3D dataset, with aligned point/voxel/mesh/Gaussian counterparts on ModelNet40 and ShapeNet-Part, provides a reusable resource for the community. Open questions remain: whether the FPS+KNN tokenization preserves fine-grained topology (e.g., mesh connectivity), whether the memory bank scales beyond four representations, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or scene-level 3D data where representation heterogeneity is even more pronounced.
