## Problem

Existing depth benchmarks for VLMs do not isolate individual pictorial depth cues, so it is impossible to determine which cues models actually exploit. Moreover, the bimodal nature of VQA evaluation conflates vision and language contributions, leaving it unclear whether VLMs' depth-related performance reflects genuine visual understanding or linguistic shortcuts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260160/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each input is a 1024×1024 (synthetic) or 1024×683 (real) RGB image showing five same-class objects where one target sits at a different depth plane, paired with a text prompt drawn from 1,026 unique templates that vary query phrasing, target referring clarity (from vague 'salient object' to explicit red-circle markers), distractor descriptions, and answer-option order. The model must output a forced binary choice (closer/farther); evaluation produces depth-ordering accuracy, a novel SDGM language-consistency score, and a near-far bias value per model.

## Method

O3-D is constructed by building 3D odd-one-out scenes in Kubric where a target differs from four distractors only in size and depth plane, then rendering 2D views with scale ambiguity so depth information is available exclusively through controlled pictorial cues. Nine cues (Height-in-Plane, Occlusion, Relative Size, Familiar Size, Light-and-Shadow, Texture Gradient, Saturation, Focusness, Linear Perspective) are introduced or removed by manipulating camera translation, directional lighting, object textures, aperture, and simulated haze, yielding 37K images with exactly 0, 1, or 2 cues present. A novel SDGM metric quantifies vision-versus-language sensitivity by computing the standard deviation of within-group mean accuracy across 36 cue groups and 42 question-variation groups, where higher SDGM indicates greater sensitivity to that modality. Twelve VLMs are evaluated under this protocol, and ICL/CoT prompting is tested on five of them to probe whether additional demonstrations improve depth perception.

## Results

All 12 evaluated VLMs (open-source and commercial) achieve depth-ordering accuracies between 47% and 56%, with no model significantly above the 50% chance level. The DepthAnythingV2 baseline substantially outperforms all VLMs: e.g., 96.60% vs. 53.92% on real 1-cue/2-cue images, and 74.46% vs. 52.82% on synthetic 2-cue images. The best individual pictorial cues for VLMs are Height-in-Plane (0.54) and Relative Size (0.54), with a maximum gap of 0.45 to the baseline. The novel SDGM metric reveals that language influence on VQA responses is uniformly larger than vision influence across all models, with InternVL2.5 showing the smallest vision–language gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DepthAnythingV2 [54] is the primary baseline and decisively outperforms every VLM on all image subsets, with gaps ranging from 0.03 (0-cue) to 0.43 (real 1-cue/2-cue). Among the 12 VLMs, none—including the commercial GPT-4.1-mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, or the vision-centric Cambrian [49]—achieves accuracy significantly above chance. ICL and CoT prompting yields modest gains only for commercial models (GPT-4.1-mini δ=+0.068, Gemini 2.5 δ=+0.042) while slightly hurting open-source models (DeepSeek-VL δ=−0.022, Qwen2-VL δ=−0.012, Phi-3 δ=−0.006). InternVL2.5 [11] stands out as the most language-consistent VLM (lowest SDGM), while BLIP2 [27], Phi-3 [1], and GPT-4.1-mini exhibit the strongest near-bias (bias_NF < −0.5). The human near-far bias reference from [10] (−0.428) is comparable to several VLMs, but unlike DepthAnythingV2, VLM biases do not reduce as cue strength increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two established psychophysical paradigms—odd-one-out detection and depth ordering—into a controlled VQA benchmark, extending the DepthCues [15] line of work by isolating 9 individual pictorial cues through synthetic scene construction rather than relying on uncontrolled real-world images. The referring-expression dimension draws on visual grounding and REC literature, while the SDGM metric is a novel formulation for quantifying vision-versus-language sensitivity in VQA. Unlike prior depth benchmarks that test models on natural images where cues co-occur, O3-D generates views from a single 3D scene with cues added or removed one at a time, enabling cue-level attribution that was previously infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all 12 evaluated VLMs—including commercial models—perform at or near chance on controlled depth ordering, while a dedicated depth estimator (DepthAnythingV2) substantially outperforms them, raises serious questions about whether current VLMs genuinely perceive depth or merely exploit language priors. The uniform dominance of language over vision in response variation suggests that VQA accuracy on spatial tasks may be misleadingly inflated by textual shortcuts. Open questions include whether motion-based or binocular cues would unlock depth understanding, whether the task is fundamentally too hard for static-image training, and how these findings translate to downstream applications like robotic manipulation where depth perception is critical.
