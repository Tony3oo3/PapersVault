## Problem

Existing 3DGS watermarking methods suffer from extremely low payload capacity (e.g., 48 bits), fragility under geometric perturbations such as rotation and cropping, and the infeasible requirement for per-scene fine-tuning or pipeline modifications. No prior work supports simultaneous multi-modal watermark embedding (binary messages, images, and 3D objects) in a single 3DGS scene with a generalizable, feed-forward approach that avoids altering the original 3DGS parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained 3DGS scene (generated by MVSplat from multi-view images) and one or more watermarks: a 1D binary sequence (48-bit × 1024 patches), a 2D logo image (processed through a feature-sparse DCAE into 16×2048 feature patches), and/or a 3D GS object (10,000 points from Objaverse/Gamba). It outputs a watermarked 3DGS scene where only the high-order spherical harmonic coefficients (48 per selected Gaussian) are modified, and at extraction time it recovers the original 1D bits, 2D image, and 3D object. The entire injection and extraction pipeline is feed-forward, completing in ~3 seconds without access to the original training images or any fine-tuning of the 3DGS model.

## Method

X-SG²S is a feed-forward watermarking framework built from four modules: a self-adaptive gate, an XD-injection head, a learnable selection gate, and an XD-extraction head. The self-adaptive gate combines a self-score (via Induced Set Attention with a single inducing token, reducing complexity to O(n)) and a cross-score (via Efficient Attention between message patches and Gaussian embeddings) to produce a top-k mask selecting optimal injection positions. Watermarks are first split into modality-specific patches with redundancy (duplication for 1D/3D, feature-sparse DCAE with position encoding and nearest-neighbor reconstruction for 2D), then embedded into high-order SH parameters by a PointNet-style multi-head MLP injector. At extraction, a 4-layer MLP learnable selection gate (trained with gradient truncation) identifies watermarked Gaussians under 3D distortions, and a corresponding XD-extraction head decodes the patches. Training uses a composite loss combining modality-specific reconstruction losses (BCE for 1D, MSE for 2D/3D), mask BCE for the selection gate, SH-parameter MSE, and optional LPIPS/2D rendering losses to preserve visual fidelity.

## Results

On 48-bit binary watermark extraction, X-SG²S achieves 100% accuracy under no perturbation and maintains 94.12–99.07% accuracy across Gaussian noise (σ=0.05), translation, rotation (±π/6), and 10% cropout attacks, outperforming all five baselines on every perturbation condition. It is also the only method supporting simultaneous multi-modal (1D+2D+3D) watermark embedding, completing injection in ~3 seconds versus 3–5 minutes for all fine-tuning-based alternatives. On the ACID/Mip-NeRF360 robustness evaluation (Table 1), scene fidelity is preserved with PSNR(org) dropping from 27.826 to 22.539 under 10% cropout while extraction accuracy remains at 100%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-SG²S outperforms all five baselines on every perturbation condition. Against the strongest baseline, GaussianMarker 3D decoder (100% clean, 92.70% cropout), X-SG²S matches clean accuracy but leads by 2.31 points on cropout and by 5.78 points on rotation. The gap is most dramatic under Gaussian noise: X-SG²S retains 94.12% accuracy while GaussianMarker 2D decoder collapses to 57.05% and GuardSplat to 67.10%. 3D-GSW is the second-best under noise (84.54%) but still trails X-SG²S by 9.58 points. All baselines require 3–5 minutes of per-scene fine-tuning and support only single-modality 48-bit watermarks, whereas X-SG²S operates in ~3 seconds as a feed-forward pass and simultaneously embeds 1D binary, 2D image, and 3D object watermarks without modifying the original 3DGS pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation [18] and repurposes the Deep Compression Autoencoder [4] into a feature-sparse variant for robust image watermark recovery. It departs from prior 3DGS watermarking methods (GS-Hider, GaussianMarker, GuardSplat, 3D-GSW, GaussianStego) in two fundamental ways: (1) it is a feed-forward framework requiring no per-scene fine-tuning or access to original training views, and (2) it unifies 1D binary, 2D image, and 3D object watermarks into a single injection/extraction pipeline via modality-specific patching and an adaptive position-selection gate. The self-adaptive gate combines Induced Set Attention for scene-level scoring with Efficient Attention for message-scene alignment, a design not present in prior 3DGS steganography work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS assets proliferate on commercial platforms, the ability to embed rich, multi-modal ownership signals without retraining the underlying model addresses a practical gap that per-scene fine-tuning methods cannot fill. The feed-forward design (3-second embedding vs. minutes of fine-tuning) makes watermarking feasible at upload time for large asset libraries. Open questions remain regarding robustness against more aggressive attacks (re-rendering at different resolutions, format conversion, adversarial pruning targeted at watermarked Gaussians) and scalability to scenes with tens of millions of Gaussians. The paper does not explore whether the watermark survives downstream generative editing pipelines (e.g., diffusion-based 3D editing), which is likely a critical real-world threat.
