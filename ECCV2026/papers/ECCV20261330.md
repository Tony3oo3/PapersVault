## Problem

Existing 3D foundation models are either object-level and promptable (e.g., SAM-style) but cannot resolve semantically meaningful internal parts, or part-aware but restricted to fixed closed-set taxonomies that fail at open-vocabulary generalization. This gap prevents a robot from taking a free-form language instruction and localizing the corresponding functional part on a previously unseen object in zero-shot.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261330/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UniPart takes a 3D point cloud (either a single-view partial observation or a complete object mesh) and a free-form text phrase (e.g., 'spout of a kettle' or simply 'spout') as input. The point cloud is tokenized via Farthest Point Sampling and kNN neighborhoods, while the text is encoded by a frozen CLIP model into a single global embedding. The output is a per-point binary mask over the input points, indicating which points belong to the queried part; no multi-class label set is required, enabling open-vocabulary queries at inference.

## Method

UniPart is a feed-forward cross-modal 3D Transformer that injects the frozen CLIP text embedding into every point token at every layer via simple element-wise addition, a choice the ablation study shows outperforms cross-attention, concatenation, and multiplication. The point encoder uses FPS + kNN + a lightweight PointNet-style local feature extractor to produce 3D tokens, and a PointNet decoder upsamples the [CLS] token back to per-point logits. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) cross-modal pretraining that aligns point features to CLIP image-patch features via a margin-based loss, (2) large-scale supervised pretraining on LangPart-1M (160K+ Objaverse objects, 8M text-part pairs generated by a multi-view pipeline combining SAM segmentation, Set-of-Mark prompting, and GPT-5.2-Pro text-guided mask merging), and (3) fine-tuning on the manually verified LangPart-4K subset to correct automatic annotation noise. The segmentation objective is binary cross-entropy per part, and the entire model runs as a single forward pass without multi-view rendering or back-projection at inference.

## Results

On the primary open-world benchmark Objaverse-General, UniPart achieves 46.43 mIoU (seen, compositional prompt) and 41.46 mIoU (unseen, compositional prompt), outperforming the closest competitor FIND3D by 12.65 and 15.25 mIoU respectively. On ShapeNetPart, UniPart reaches 55.63 mIoU on the V2 split (compositional prompt), a 13.48-point gain over FIND3D. On the authors' own LangPart-1K benchmark, UniPart scores 33.56 mIoU on single-view point clouds with short prompts, more than 3× the FIND3D score of 8.97. The controlled study (Table 6) shows UniPart on LangPart-1M reaches 47.25 mIoU vs. 30.75 for FIND3D on its own data, attributing ~14.7 mIoU to architecture and ~24.9 mIoU to the dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPart outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark and prompt format. Against the closest concurrent method FIND3D [38], UniPart gains 12.7–15.3 mIoU on Objaverse-General (seen/unseen), 12.2–25.6 mIoU on ShapeNetPart, 6.3–7.7 mIoU on PartNet-E, and 20.2–24.6 mIoU on LangPart-1K single-view. Against the 2D-to-3D lifting baselines, the margins are much larger: UniPart exceeds OpenMask3D [61] by ~30–38 mIoU and PointCLIPV2 [88] by ~35–38 mIoU on Objaverse-General, reflecting the advantage of a feed-forward 3D model over multi-view querying at inference. PartSLIP++ [35] is the weakest baseline, scoring below 15 mIoU in most settings, likely due to its reliance on category-specific superpoint generation that does not transfer well to open-vocabulary queries. The controlled study confirms both data and architecture contribute: UniPart on FIND3D's data still beats FIND3D by 4.5 mIoU, while FIND3D on LangPart-1M beats FIND3D on its own data by 7.7 mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out the intersection of two underdeveloped areas: open-vocabulary 3D segmentation (which handles instances/scenes but not internal parts) and fine-grained 3D part segmentation (which achieves part-level detail but only under closed-set taxonomies like ShapeNetPart or PartNet). The closest concurrent work is FIND3D, which also formulates text-prompted 3D part segmentation; UniPart differentiates through a 4× larger training set (160K+ vs 36K objects), a multi-view consistency mechanism using text-guided mask merging, and a feed-forward point-based architecture with layer-wise additive CLIP text injection. The data pipeline repurposes SAM, Set-of-Mark prompting, and GPT-5.2-Pro in a novel composition for scalable 3D part annotation, while the model architecture draws on PointNet-style local encoding and standard Transformer blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a language-to-part-mask interface that generalizes zero-shot to unseen objects and part types, UniPart offers a practical perception primitive for dexterous manipulation, validated with 85% grasp success on real sensor point clouds. The controlled study cleanly quantifying that data scale contributes ~25 mIoU and architecture ~15 mIoU independently gives the community a useful reference for where to invest effort in this task. Open questions remain: the evaluation covers only 20 real objects with a single camera, the pipeline depends on GPT-5.2-Pro for both filtering and mask merging (raising reproducibility and cost concerns), and extending part understanding to articulated or multi-object scenes is explicitly left as future work.
