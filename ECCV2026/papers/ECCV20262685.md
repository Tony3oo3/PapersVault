## Problem

When general-purpose VLMs compress an information-dense 360° panorama into a single global embedding, fine-grained local details are submerged by overwhelming background context—a phenomenon the paper terms semantic dilution. This causes a stark granularity asymmetry: zero-shot VLMs achieve >60% R@1 on global captions but drop to ~6% on local captions targeting the same panoramas, making fine-grained panoramic retrieval fundamentally infeasible with single-vector representations.

## Input / Output

The panorama tower ingests six distortion-free cubemap faces (each 280×280, yielding 100 visual tokens) and a downsampled global ERP panorama (560×280), each followed by a learnable spatial anchor token (<front>, <right>, …, <global>) to form a single interleaved sequence. The query tower encodes either a global/local text caption or a narrow-FoV image crop into a dense embedding. The output is a multi-vector panoramic representation—six L2-normalized view-specific local embeddings (E_local ∈ R^{6×D}) plus one holistic global embedding (E_global ∈ R^D)—extracted from the hidden states at anchor-token positions in a single forward pass, enabling both scene-level and view-specific retrieval ranking.

## Method

PanoRec serializes the six cubemap faces and a downsampled global panorama into a unified spatially-structured sequence with learnable spatial anchor tokens, leveraging the native interleaved-sequence capability of a frozen-visual-encoder VLM (Qwen3-VL-Embedding) to extract decoupled multi-granularity embeddings in one forward pass without architectural modifications. For local matching, a MaxSim routing strategy computes similarity by aligning the query with the single most relevant cubemap face, suppressing background noise at inference and—critically—introducing hard spatial negative mining during training, where the query is contrasted against the highest-scoring face from every non-target panorama in the batch. The overall training objective is a joint spatial InfoNCE loss (L = λ_global·L_global + λ_local·L_local) that simultaneously supervises global scene alignment and fine-grained view-specific discrimination, with the visual encoder kept frozen and only the language model (via LoRA or full fine-tuning) plus the seven anchor tokens updated.

## Results

On ZInD (indoor) with LoRA fine-tuning, PanoRec-8B achieves 79.68% R@1 for Global Caption retrieval and a striking 76.55% R@1 for Local Caption retrieval, compared to 71.49% and 17.33% for the ERP baseline-8B. On CVACT (outdoor) with full fine-tuning, PanoRec-8B reaches 61.91% Global R@1 and 71.47% Local Caption R@1 versus 57.79% and 16.37% for the baseline. Zero-shot evaluation on the unseen Matterport3D dataset confirms generalization: PanoRec-8B attains 82.78% Local Caption R@1, far exceeding the Direct Train-8B baseline (17.13%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the ERP single-vector approach (same Qwen3-VL backbone, same training paradigm, but processing the full equirectangular image into one embedding). PanoRec wins decisively on all local-granularity tasks: Local Caption R@1 jumps from 17.33% to 76.55% on ZInD and from 16.37% to 71.47% on CVACT (8B, Full-FT), a gap of over 55 percentage points. On Global Caption retrieval, PanoRec also leads by 4–8 points (e.g., 79.68% vs. 71.49% on ZInD), indicating that decoupled local views provide complementary cues for holistic matching. Against zero-shot general-purpose VLMs (Qwen3-VL-2B/8B) on Matterport3D, PanoRec-8B improves Local Caption R@1 from 6.96% to 82.78% and Local Image R@1 from 12.50% to 91.95%. The Direct Train baseline (fine-tuning the VLM on ERP without spatial structure) also trails PanoRec substantially on local tasks (17.13% vs. 82.78% Local Caption R@1), confirming that the spatially-structured sequence design—not merely fine-tuning—is the critical factor. Across seven diverse VLM backbones (SmolVLM-256M through InternVL3.5-8B, Gemma-3-4B, Kimi-VL-A3B), PanoRec consistently outperforms the ERP baseline on local tasks by 40–55 R@1 points, demonstrating broad applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanoRec builds directly on the Qwen3-VL-Embedding backbone but departs fundamentally from prior panoramic retrieval approaches in two ways. Earlier methods either modified VLM architectures with spherical operators (PanoSwin, Spherenet) or projected panoramas into cubemaps/tangent patches but treated them as unordered bag-of-views collapsed into a single global embedding. PanoRec instead introduces a spatially-structured sequence paradigm where distortion-free cubemap faces and a downsampled global panorama are serialized with learnable spatial anchor tokens, extracting decoupled multi-vector representations in a single forward pass without any architectural modification. The MaxSim routing strategy for local matching draws conceptual inspiration from multi-vector retrieval (Poly-encoders, ColPali) but is specifically adapted to the geometrically-organized six-face structure of panoramas, and the joint spatial InfoNCE objective repurposes in-batch contrastive learning to perform hard spatial negative mining across views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration of semantic dilution—showing a ~10x performance gap between global and local retrieval on the same panoramas—provides a clear diagnostic for why naively applying VLMs to dense visual inputs fails, a finding broadly relevant beyond panoramas to any task compressing information-rich scenes into single embeddings. The practical simplicity of the solution (serialize images with anchor tokens, extract hidden states, apply MaxSim) makes it easy to adopt across VLM families, as confirmed by consistent gains from 256M to 8B parameters. Open questions include whether the fixed six-face cubemap decomposition is optimal for all scene types, how the approach extends to higher-resolution or non-cubemap projections, and whether the spatial anchor tokens can be made adaptive to query content rather than fixed to geometric positions.
