## Problem

Existing music-to-dance generation methods model motion as a continuous signal in an end-to-end sequence-to-sequence fashion, capturing only local beat alignment while neglecting the compositional structure of real choreography. This results in generated dances that are visually smooth but lack segment-level coherence, are difficult to interpret, and offer no mechanism for controllable editing at the level of individual movement units.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262443/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-length music audio signal (not short clips) as input and produces two outputs: a symbolic atomic movement plan specifying the category, temporal position, and duration of each atomic movement across the entire sequence, and a continuous 3D dance motion sequence at a fixed frame rate R_F. The atomic movement vocabulary consists of 100 base prototypes further subdivided into ~7.3 semantically labeled sub-prototypes each, discovered from the AIST++ dataset. The planned atomic movements are explicitly editable before the completion stage, enabling user-controlled reassembly of dance sections.

## Method

The framework decouples generation into two stages mirroring human choreography. Stage 1 (Atomic Movement Planning) uses a Discrete Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (D3PM) conditioned on full-music features to predict a per-frame symbolic label sequence over K+1 categories (K atomic movements + transition), followed by sliding-window majority-vote smoothing and minimum-duration merging to correct frame-level errors. Stage 2 (Dance Completion) retrieves duration-matched motion instances from the atomic movement database, applies masked noise to allow re-creation with variation, and refines the coarse sequence with a transition-aware DDPM (based on the EDGE denoiser) that synthesizes smooth inter-movement transitions while preserving atomic semantics. The atomic movement vocabulary itself is constructed via a three-stage discovery pipeline: temporal-event-proposal segmentation using I3D visual similarity clustering, K-Means clustering in TMR motion-text embedding space, and LLM-assisted in-group re-clustering (Gemini for fine-grained descriptions, a reasoning LLM for sub-prototype identification) to yield interpretable, semantically labeled categories.

## Results

On AIST++, the proposed method achieves the best scores across all five metrics: FID_k = 25.26, FID_g = 9.03, Div_k = 8.01, Div_g = 6.69, BAS = 0.2470, and R-precision = 26.6. The FID scores are closest to the ground truth (17.1 / 10.6), indicating superior kinematic and geometric fidelity. The R-precision of 26.6 substantially exceeds the next-best baseline (Lodge at 18.2), demonstrating markedly better structural consistency between music phrasing and choreographic organization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor Lodge [13], it reduces FID_k from 37.09 to 25.26 (−32%) and FID_g from 18.79 to 9.03 (−52%), while improving R-precision from 18.2 to 26.6 (+46%). Against the VQ-VAE-based Bailando [21], which is the closest in FID_g (9.62 vs. 9.03), the gap in R-precision is even more pronounced (17.9 vs. 26.6), highlighting the advantage of explicit atomic-movement planning over quantized codebook representations. EDGE [24] and the older sequence-to-sequence baselines DanceNet [33] and DanceRevolution [10] are substantially outperformed on all metrics, with FID_k gaps exceeding 17 and 48 points respectively. The diversity scores (Div_k, Div_g) of the proposed method (8.01, 6.69) approach ground-truth levels (8.19, 7.45), whereas all baselines fall well short, confirming that the two-stage design preserves expressive variation without sacrificing structural coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end sequence-to-sequence paradigm in music-to-dance generation (EDGE, Bailando, DanceNet) and the coarse-to-fine keyframe approach of LODGE by introducing an explicit intermediate representation: a vocabulary of semantically interpretable atomic movements discovered from data. It builds on EDGE's diffusion denoiser for the motion completion stage and on TMR's contrastive motion-text encoder for clustering, but the core novelty is the three-stage atomic movement discovery pipeline (temporal-event segmentation, K-Means clustering, LLM-assisted in-group re-clustering) combined with a discrete diffusion planner (D3PM) that produces a symbolic choreographic score before continuous motion synthesis. The two-stage design explicitly mirrors the human choreography workflow of planning structure first and then performing with variation, a framing absent from prior diffusion-based dance generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit atomic movement representation offers a level of interpretability and segment-level controllability (editing individual movements, adjusting durations, reassembling sections) that end-to-end diffusion models cannot provide, making the framework practical for interactive choreography tools. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (AIST++), the key structural metric (R-precision) is self-proposed and thus hard to compare across papers, and the 100-prototype vocabulary's adequacy for underrepresented genres or novel choreographic styles remains untested. Open questions include whether the atomic movement discovery pipeline generalizes to unseen dance styles without re-clustering, how the approach scales to multi-dancer or full-body-with-object scenarios, and whether the discrete planning stage can be made more efficient for real-time applications.
