## Problem

Existing 3D world generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: view-centric approaches (image/video diffusion + reconstruction) produce geometrically inconsistent and incomplete scenes, while world-centric approaches trained on 3D datasets are restricted to narrow domains (e.g., medieval villages, driving scenes) due to the scarcity of diverse world-level data. No prior method simultaneously achieves complete, layout-controllable, and domain-generalizable 3D world generation with flexible spatial conditioning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D segment map with K arbitrarily-shaped labeled regions and one text prompt per region as input; the 2D map is extruded uniformly along the height axis to form 3D masks. The output is a single coherent 3D world represented as 3D Gaussian Splatting, with user-defined spatial dimensions that can exceed the 64³ cube limit of the underlying TRELLIS asset generator. Unlike grid-based baselines such as SynCity, the segment regions need not be square or tile the space uniformly, enabling free-form spatial layouts.

## Method

Map2World extends the pretrained TRELLIS rectified-flow pipeline (sparse-structure Transformer G_S and latent-feature Transformer G_L) to world scale via three mechanisms. First, it applies a 3D MultiDiffusion strategy: space is partitioned into overlapping 64³ windows, each denoised independently, and velocity predictions are fused with a 3D Gaussian kernel; per-position velocities are further weighted by segment masks with a time-varying Gaussian σ_t to ensure smooth inter-region transitions. Second, a scale-aware initial-noise optimization steers the starting latent onto a scale-dependent manifold using a linearized trajectory approximation and 3D FFT spectral parameterization for stable, fast convergence. Third, a detail enhancer—comprising a channel-concatenation MLP that injects the truncated large-cube latent and adjacent-cube latents into the frozen flow Transformer—auto-regressively predicts fine-grained structured latents for eight sub-cubes, while a fine-tuned SLAT decoder handles the distribution shift from complete objects to partial scene fragments.

## Results

Map2World achieves a World Quality (WQ) score of 7.76, outperforming the closest baselines CityDreamer (7.36) and SynCity (7.25), with the largest gap over InfiniCube (4.05) and GaussianCube (5.08). On the GPTScore sub-metrics, Map2World leads in world completeness (7.8), coherence (7.9), and realism (7.6), while SynCity edges it slightly on sharpness (8.2 vs 8.0). In pairwise human evaluation (19 participants, 21 pairs), Map2World is preferred over every baseline in 67–98% of comparisons across realism, completeness, coherence, and overall quality. On distribution-based FID/KID, Map2World (192.4 / 0.1480) trails SynCity (143.4 / 0.1060) and GaussianCube (169.1 / 0.0906), though the authors argue these metrics are unreliable given the small 35-scene reference set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is with SynCity [11], which also builds on TRELLIS for map-conditioned generation; Map2World surpasses it on WQ (7.76 vs 7.25), world completeness (7.8 vs 6.8), coherence (7.9 vs 7.6), and realism (7.6 vs 7.3), while SynCity retains a slight edge on sharpness (8.2 vs 8.0) and FID/KID. Against CityDreamer [49], a domain-specific urban scene generator, Map2World leads on WQ (7.76 vs 7.36) and all GPTScore sub-metrics except world completeness where CityDreamer scores higher (8.5 vs 7.8), reflecting its specialized training on city data. GaussianCube [55] and InfiniCube [32] are substantially weaker, scoring 5.08 and 4.05 on WQ respectively, with GaussianCube producing incomplete structures and InfiniCube limited to driving scenes. In human evaluation, Map2World is preferred over SynCity in 89–95% of pairwise comparisons and over CityDreamer in 81–91%, confirming the WQ trends. The paper explicitly cautions that FID/KID are unreliable here because the 35-scene reference set is too small to represent the 3D scene manifold, and these metrics do not capture 3D-aware properties like geometric completeness or map alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

Map2World extends the MultiDiffusion paradigm (overlapping-window latent fusion for spatial expansion) from 2D image generation to volumetric 3D latent space, and repurposes TRELLIS's pretrained rectified-flow Transformers as a general 3D prior rather than training a world-specific generator from scratch. It departs from domain-specific world generators (BlockFusion, NuiScene, CityDreamer) that are constrained to their training distribution, and from SynCity's rigid grid-tile approach by supporting arbitrary-shaped segment maps with per-region text prompts. The detail enhancer is a novel latent-space super-resolution module that conditions on both the coarse scene latent and adjacent-cube latents, distinguishing it from autoregressive cube-prediction pipelines that lack global context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the core data-scarcity bottleneck in world-scale 3D generation by leveraging a general asset generator's prior, making the approach potentially applicable to any domain TRELLIS has seen rather than a single trained scene type. However, the detail enhancer is trained on only 35 scenes, and the FID/KID results are actually worse than SynCity and GaussianCube (which the authors attribute to the small reference set and the inability of image-distribution metrics to capture 3D structural properties). Open questions include whether the 64-voxel-per-window resolution and the single-scale-level noise optimization strategy scale to truly massive or multi-scale worlds, and whether the approach generalizes to domains underrepresented in TRELLIS's training data.
