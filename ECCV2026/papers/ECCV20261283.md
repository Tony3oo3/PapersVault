## Problem

Sparse-view 3D reconstruction faces a fundamental trade-off: generative methods (e.g., MVSplat360, latentSplat) produce visually plausible novel views but yield inaccurate, fragmented depth, while geometry-focused methods (e.g., DepthSplat) produce clean depth estimates but cannot synthesize appearance or geometry in unobserved regions. No existing feed-forward model simultaneously achieves photorealistic novel view synthesis and geometrically coherent depth in wide-baseline extrapolation settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261283/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse context RGB images (N=2 for RE10K, N=4 for DL3DV-10K) with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, obtained via VGGT preprocessing. It produces M novel-view images and their corresponding relative depth maps for arbitrary target viewpoints, which can be unprojected into dense 3D point clouds. Notably, depth is generated jointly with appearance as a by-product of the unified generative process rather than predicted separately, and the architecture naturally supports variable numbers of context views beyond the training configuration.

## Method

ReconSplat uses a two-stage design: a feed-forward MVSplat backbone predicts pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians augmented with per-Gaussian variational latent distributions (4-dim mean and diagonal covariance) for both appearance and geometry, which are rasterized into 2D latent distributions via a 'rasterize-then-sample' formulation that enables KL regularization in 2D space. A multi-view latent diffusion model (MV-LDM), built on the SD2.1 U-Net with zero-initialized residual branches for cross-view 3D self-attention (camera-conditioned via PRoPE) and optional 3D convolutions, then refines and inpaints these preliminary latents using a ControlNet-style injection of the rasterized 3DGS features. The diffusion process jointly samples appearance and geometry latent pairs, enforcing cross-modality consistency and multi-view coherence. Training involves LoRA fine-tuning of separate SD-VAE decoders for RGB and depth reconstruction, followed by diffusion training with v-prediction, annealed multi-resolution noise, and classifier-free guidance, all supervised with pixel-space reconstruction, perceptual, adversarial, and gradient-matching losses.

## Results

On RE10K extrapolation (the paper's headline setting), ReconSplat achieves FID 4.89, LPIPS 0.222, DISTS 0.120, and PSNR 21.92 dB, outperforming all generative baselines (MVSplat360: FID 5.49; latentSplat: FID 6.93) and regression-based methods (DepthSplat: FID 11.81; pixelSplat: FID 11.18). On DL3DV-10K (two-round, n=300), ReconSplat attains FID 43.87 and DISTS 0.137, beating MVSplat360 (FID 52.86) and latentSplat (FID 73.08), though DepthSplat (trained on ~5× more data) achieves a lower LPIPS of 0.297. For depth estimation on ScanNet++, ReconSplat yields AbsRel 0.206 and RMSE_log 0.238, surpassing both MVSplat360 (AbsRel 0.410) and DepthSplat (AbsRel 0.255) on all metrics except δ₁.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the RE10K extrapolation setting, ReconSplat clearly wins on FID (4.89 vs. 5.49 for MVSplat360 and 6.93 for latentSplat), LPIPS (0.222 vs. 0.245 and 0.234), DISTS (0.120 vs. 0.148 and 0.127), and PSNR (21.92 vs. 20.46 and 21.60), establishing a new state of the art. However, it trails DepthSplat on SSIM (0.735 vs. 0.776) in extrapolation, and DepthSplat dominates the interpolation setting across all metrics (FID 2.70, PSNR 27.65, SSIM 0.894), reflecting its strength in narrow-baseline reconstruction. On DL3DV-10K, ReconSplat outperforms MVSplat360 and latentSplat on FID and DISTS in both trajectory spans, but DepthSplat (trained on ~5× more data) achieves lower LPIPS (0.297 vs. 0.351) and higher PSNR (19.41 vs. 17.58) in the two-round setting. For depth estimation on ScanNet++, ReconSplat beats both MVSplat360 and DepthSplat on AbsRel, RMSE, and RMSE_log, though DepthSplat retains a slight edge on δ₁ (70.58 vs. 67.32). In the cross-dataset generalization test (RE10K→DL3DV-10K without fine-tuning), ReconSplat achieves FID 27.24, substantially outperforming MVSplat360 (38.75) and latentSplat (43.03).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReconSplat builds directly on the MVSplat multi-view 3DGS encoder [15] and the variational Gaussian formulation of latentSplat [84], but departs from latentSplat's sample-then-rasterize scheme by adopting a rasterize-then-sample formulation that allows KL regularization in 2D latent space. The multi-view latent diffusion stage is conditioned on the rasterized 3DGS latents via a ControlNet-style [97] injection into a U-Net backbone adapted from Stable Diffusion 2.1, with cross-view 3D self-attention and PRoPE camera encoding. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate lines: generative NVS methods (MVSplat360, latentSplat) that produce plausible appearance but weak geometry, and geometric methods (DepthSplat) that produce clean depth but cannot extrapolate beyond observed views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReconSplat is the first feed-forward model to jointly produce geometrically coherent novel views and accurate depth maps in wide-baseline extrapolation settings, addressing a trade-off that has persisted across the 3DGS-based reconstruction literature. The depth results on ScanNet++ (beating DepthSplat on AbsRel and RMSE despite DepthSplat using ~5× more training data) suggest the joint appearance-geometry diffusion prior provides genuine geometric grounding beyond what monocular depth features alone can achieve. Key limitations include reliance on the accuracy of the feed-forward 3DGS prior for unobserved regions and the inheritance of a single-image latent space (SD-VAE) rather than a natively multi-view one, pointing toward future work on stronger 3D reconstruction priors and native multi-view latent representations.
