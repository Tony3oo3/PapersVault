## Problem

Existing video object segmentation paradigms each suffer from a critical weakness: unsupervised methods yield coarse masks, few-shot methods struggle with referential ambiguity among similar objects, and referring methods lack visual priors to ground vague language. The Few-Shot Referring VOS (FSRVOS) task attempts to combine both, but the original formulation entangles query-level intent with support-side text descriptions that introduce cross-video semantic misalignment, and naive multi-modal fusion fails to resolve visual ambiguities effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261630/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query video of F frames, a single natural-language referring expression describing the target, and N=5 masked support images drawn from external videos of the same (novel) object class. Notably, no textual descriptions are provided for the support images—only the query-level text encodes semantic intent, while the support masks supply instance-level visual evidence. The output is a set of per-frame binary segmentation masks (H×W) for the referred object across the entire query video, evaluated with J&F (IoU and contour accuracy) metrics.

## Method

QST-SAM builds on SAM2 and introduces three coupled modules. The Support Instruction Compressor distills dense support feature maps into K=32 compact instruction vectors via dual-branch MLPs producing attention maps (masked by support masks to suppress background) and value features, then aggregates them into a noise-resilient representation. The Q-S-T Transformer performs two-stage bidirectional fusion: first, Text-Guided Semantic Refinement uses T2S/S2T cross-attention to condition support instructions on the referring expression, filtering irrelevant intra-class variation; second, Spatio-Contextual Enhancement uses S2Q/Q2S cross-attention to inject refined structural priors into query tokens. The Multi-Priors Prompting module computes a percentile-thresholded cosine-similarity affinity between query and masked support features to propagate support masks as a spatial mask prior, while projecting text tokens into a complementary semantic prior; both priors condition SAM2's mask decoder alongside memory-enhanced query features. The model is initialized from SAMWISE weights and fine-tuned for 4 epochs with a frozen text encoder and SAM2 backbone at learning rate 1e-5.

## Results

On Ref-DAVIS17, QST-SAM achieves 70.3% J&F (J=67.5, F=73.1) in the 5-shot setting, surpassing the strongest baseline SAMWISE (67.5%) by 2.8 points and showing a +4.8 gain over its own zero-shot counterpart. On the larger Ref-YouTube-VOS benchmark, QST-SAM reaches 62.4% J&F (J=60.9, F=63.9), outperforming SAMWISE (60.4%) by 2.0 points with a +3.1 few-shot improvement. Under the legacy FSRVOS protocol on Mini-Ref-YouTube-VOS, QST-SAM attains 59.2% J&F without any support-side text, exceeding CMA-ISM (54.0%) by 5.2 points under strictly weaker supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QST-SAM outperforms all seven baselines on both primary benchmarks. Against the strongest zero-shot RVOS methods, ReferDINO (67.8 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17, 60.6 on Ref-YouTube-VOS) and SAMWISE (67.5 / 60.4), QST-SAM leads by 2.5 and 2.0 J&F points respectively, with the gap widening on the J metric (67.5 vs. 65.5 on Ref-DAVIS17). The earlier baselines DsHmp (64.5 / 57.7) and LoSh (64.9 / 58.1) trail QST-SAM by 5.8 and 5.4 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17, and by 4.7 and 4.3 on Ref-YouTube-VOS. Notably, the few-shot gains over each method's own zero-shot score are largest for QST-SAM (+4.8 / +3.1), whereas ReferDINO and SAMWISE show only +1.9/+0.8 and +2.0/+1.1, indicating that naive cross-attention fusion of support features is insufficient. Under the legacy FSRVOS protocol (Table 2), QST-SAM (59.2 J&F, no support text) surpasses CMA-ISM (54.0, with support text) by 5.2 points, and also beats HPAN (50.9) and DANet (42.3), demonstrating that the decoupled formulation with visual-only support cues is more effective than adding cross-video textual annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM2 as its segmentation backbone and adopts SAMWISE's cross-modal temporal adapter and training protocol as its starting point. Conceptually, it revisits the Few-Shot Referring VOS (FSRVOS) task introduced by Liu et al. (CMA-ISM, IJCV 2025), but deliberately removes support-side textual descriptions, retaining only query-level language and masked support images. This decoupling positions the work at the intersection of few-shot VOS (visual exemplar guidance) and referring VOS (linguistic intent), arguing that the two modalities are complementary rather than redundant when properly separated. The Q-S-T Transformer's structured two-stage fusion—text first refines support, then support refines query—departs from the flat cross-attention fusion used in prior RVOS methods like ReferFormer and MTTR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that support-side text descriptions are not merely redundant but actively harmful (QST-SAM without support text outperforms CMA-ISM with support text by +5.2 J&F) challenges the common assumption that adding modalities always improves grounding. This has practical implications for annotation pipelines: removing support text reduces labeling cost without sacrificing, and in fact improving, segmentation quality. Open questions include whether the decoupled formulation generalizes to multi-object or long-video settings, whether the K=32 instruction budget scales gracefully with more support shots, and whether the percentile-based affinity sparsification in the Q-S Matching module remains robust under extreme appearance variation or domain shift.
