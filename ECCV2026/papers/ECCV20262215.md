## Problem

Diffusion-based video super-resolution achieves strong perceptual quality but is restricted to fixed integer scales, while arbitrary-scale VSR methods using implicit neural representations suffer from severe over-smoothing at large factors due to deterministic regression objectives. Combining diffusion priors with continuous coordinate decoding in the video domain is further hindered by severe temporal flickering, as the stochasticity of per-frame diffusion sampling is amplified by the sensitivity of continuous decoders to feature-level drift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262215/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video sequence of N frames (3×H×W) and a target continuous scaling factor s > 1, and outputs a high-resolution video at the arbitrary target resolution (3×sH×sW). Crucially, the diffusion denoising operates entirely in a fixed LR latent space whose spatial dimensions match the input, making the generative sampling cost independent of the target scale. During training, the continuous scale s is uniformly sampled from [1.1, 4.0], and the final pixel rendering is performed by querying a coordinate-based INR at any sub-pixel location, enabling truly continuous (not just integer) resolution output.

## Method

AVSR-Diff is a decoupled two-stage framework built on a frozen Stable Diffusion ×4 Upscaler. In the first stage, a trainable ControlNet with a Temporally-Gated Feature Recurrence (TGFR) module guides the frozen denoising U-Net to perform scale-agnostic latent denoising; TGFR recurrently aligns ControlNet residual features across frames using flow-based warping, deformable convolutions for sub-pixel refinement, and a learned spatial gate that suppresses unreliable temporal information, with a sparsity penalty on the gate to prevent long-term error accumulation. In the second stage, a continuous video decoder extends the pre-trained VAE decoder by (i) repurposing TGFR for bidirectional temporal feature propagation, (ii) a Scale-Aware Fourier Refinement (SAFR) module that applies a scale-conditioned spectral gate in the Fourier domain to dynamically modulate high-frequency components according to the target scale, and (iii) a LIIF-style coordinate-based INR for continuous spatial querying. The ControlNet is trained with a denoising loss plus gate sparsity regularization, while the decoder is trained in two phases (L1 + perceptual, then + PatchGAN adversarial loss) on randomly sampled continuous scales.

## Results

On the standard 4× VSR benchmark (REDS4), AVSR-Diff achieves LPIPS 9.54, DISTS 4.42, PSNR 28.75 dB, and tLPIPS 4.20, outperforming the best fixed-scale generative baseline StableVSR (LPIPS 9.74, tLPIPS 5.40) on every metric. On Vid4, it reaches LPIPS 16.79 and tOF 5.78, again leading all generative methods. At the challenging 8× scale on REDS4, AVSR-Diff attains LPIPS 29.43 and tOF 39.13, substantially surpassing the only other arbitrary-scale generative method VEnhancer (LPIPS 43.87, tOF 129.42) and all regression-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fixed-scale regression baselines (BasicVSR++, RVRT), AVSR-Diff trades ~3–4 dB PSNR for substantially better perceptual quality (LPIPS 9.54 vs. 13.32–13.49) and temporal consistency (tLPIPS 4.20 vs. 8.98–9.19 on REDS4). Among arbitrary-scale regression methods (SAVSR, ST-AVSR, VideoINR, MoTIF, BF-STVSR, V³VSR), AVSR-Diff dominates on perceptual metrics at all scales; at 2× it achieves PSNR 35.47 dB, comparable to the best regression baseline V³VSR (36.13 dB), while its LPIPS of 3.84 far exceeds V³VSR's 6.01. Against fixed-scale generative methods (StableVSR, RealBasicVSR, Upscale-A-Video, MGLD-VSR, STAR), AVSR-Diff wins on every metric at their native 4× resolution, with the closest competitor StableVSR trailing by 0.20 LPIPS and 1.20 tLPIPS on REDS4. The only other arbitrary-scale generative method, VEnhancer, is decisively outperformed: at 8× on REDS4, AVSR-Diff achieves LPIPS 29.43 vs. VEnhancer's 43.87 and tOF 39.13 vs. 129.42, reflecting VEnhancer's severe temporal instability from resolution-specific 3D U-Net denoising.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVSR-Diff sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: diffusion-based VSR (StableVSR, MGLD-VSR, Upscale-A-Video), which achieves strong perceptual quality but is locked to a fixed 4× scale, and arbitrary-scale VSR via implicit neural representations (LIIF, VideoINR, MoTIF, SAVSR), which offers continuous scale flexibility but suffers from over-smoothing due to regression objectives. The paper's key architectural departure is a decoupled design that performs all diffusion denoising in a fixed LR latent space (inheriting the temporal conditioning strategy of StableVSR and the deformable alignment philosophy of BasicVSR++) and then renders to arbitrary scales through a continuous video decoder built on LIIF. The TGFR module repurposes ControlNet's residual branch as a recurrent temporal alignment mechanism, and the SAFR module introduces Fourier-domain scale conditioning—neither of which has a direct precedent in prior VSR or arbitrary-scale SR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled architecture delivers a practically important property: GPU memory remains constant regardless of target scale, making long-video inference at large factors (6×, 8×) feasible where prior generative approaches (e.g., VEnhancer's 3D U-Net) become intractable. The finding that continuous coordinate decoding amplifies latent stochasticity into severe flickering—and that a gated recurrent alignment mechanism is necessary to suppress it—provides a useful conceptual framework for future work combining generative priors with implicit representations in any video task. Open questions include whether the 50-step DDPM sampling can be accelerated (e.g., via few-step distillation) without degrading temporal consistency, how the approach scales to very long sequences beyond 100 frames, and whether the decoupled paradigm generalizes to other generative video enhancement tasks such as deblurring or frame interpolation.
