## Problem

Existing road surface reconstruction methods either require multi-view inputs with lengthy per-scene optimization (NeRF/GS-based approaches) or reconstruct only geometry without texture (RoadBEV). Feed-forward Gaussian Splatting methods that do handle single-frame input rely on per-pixel prediction, which is ill-suited for road scenes where the perspective effect causes severe near-far density imbalance. The paper addresses how to achieve real-time, single-frame reconstruction of both road geometry and texture that generalizes to unseen scenes without iterative optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (528×960) captured from a forward-facing camera and produces a structured BEV elevation map over a predefined 1.9 m × 5.0 m region discretized into 3 cm grid cells (64×164 grids), along with novel-view RGB renderings via differentiable Gaussian splatting. Unlike unstructured point-cloud outputs from depth-estimation baselines, the geometry is delivered as a regular grid of elevation values, and texture is encoded as per-grid spherical harmonic coefficients (order L=1) on a 4× finer texture grid (256×656). The feed-forward prediction runs at ~26 FPS and rendering at ~2061 FPS on an A100 GPU.

## Method

BEV-GS decouples road reconstruction into two independent CNN branches operating in BEV: a geometry branch (simplified EfficientNet) that projects PV features into 3D road voxels via direct 3D-to-2D projection, fuses height anchors with a softmax-weighted sum, and predicts elevation as a global reference plane plus a per-grid offset (classified over 40 bins then regressed); and a texture branch (simplified MobileNetV3) that queries BEV features guided by the predicted elevation to avoid multi-elevation overlap, then decodes spherical harmonic color coefficients. The key representation is a grid Gaussian: 3D Gaussians are placed at fixed uniform grid positions (centers from grid coordinates + predicted elevation) with shared isotropic scale and identity rotation, eliminating the need for density optimization or geometric regularization. Rendering uses standard differentiable 3D Gaussian splatting with alpha-blending, and the texture branch is supervised end-to-end by L1+SSIM loss on synthesized stereo views. The entire pipeline is feed-forward—no per-scene optimization is required—enabling real-time inference and generalization to unseen road conditions.

## Results

On the RSRD dataset, BEV-GS achieves state-of-the-art road geometry reconstruction with an average absolute elevation error (AAE) of 1.73 cm and RMSE of 1.94 cm, reducing errors by 10.4% and 10.2% over the closest BEV baseline RoadBEV (1.93 cm / 2.16 cm). For novel view synthesis, BEV-GS reaches 28.36 dB PSNR, 0.77 SSIM, and 0.14 LPIPS, substantially outperforming the best feed-forward GS baselines (Flash3D: 23.27 dB; Splatter Image: 22.32 dB). The method runs at 26.3 FPS for prediction and 2061 FPS for rendering with only 8M parameters, enabling real-time deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEV-GS outperforms all six perspective-view depth estimation baselines (iDisc, LapDepth, PixelFormer, AdaBins, ZoeDepth, Depth Anything V2) by a wide margin on geometry, with the best PV method (Depth Anything V2) still trailing by 0.57 cm in AAE. Against the closest BEV baseline RoadBEV, BEV-GS reduces AAE from 1.93 to 1.73 cm and RMSE from 2.16 to 1.94 cm, while also lowering the >5 mm error rate from 83.4% to 80.2%. For novel view synthesis, BEV-GS surpasses Flash3D by 5.09 dB in PSNR (28.36 vs. 23.27) and Splatter Image by 6.04 dB (28.36 vs. 22.32), with LPIPS dropping from 0.41 to 0.14. In terms of efficiency, BEV-GS matches RoadBEV's inference speed (26.3 vs. 26.8 FPS) while adding rendering at 2061 FPS, and uses only 8M parameters versus 27M for RoadBEV, 56M for Splatter Image, and 400M for Flash3D. The paper also reports that test-time optimization (60 iterations, ~0.7 s) further improves synthesis quality beyond the feed-forward prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEV-GS sits at the intersection of three research threads: BEV-based road geometry estimation (RoadBEV), feed-forward per-pixel Gaussian Splatting (Splatter Image, Flash3D), and the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline (Kerbl et al.). It departs from the per-pixel GS paradigm by replacing dense pixel-wise Gaussian prediction with structured BEV grid Gaussians, motivated by the observation that perspective-view prediction suffers from uneven near-far density on open roads. The decoupled geometry-and-texture branch design, where the geometry branch's elevation output guides texture feature query, is a novel architectural choice not present in prior feed-forward GS methods that typically share a single backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of 26 FPS prediction, 2061 FPS rendering, and only 8M parameters makes single-frame road reconstruction genuinely deployable in real-time autonomous driving stacks, a regime where prior GS-based methods (requiring per-scene optimization or 400M+ parameters) were impractical. However, validation is confined to a single dataset (RSRD) collected exclusively under sunny, well-lit conditions, leaving open whether the method generalizes to adverse weather, nighttime, or diverse geographic road types. The grid-Gaussian representation also raises the question of whether this structured approach can be extended beyond the relatively planar road surface to more complex 3D scene geometry.
