## Problem

Existing optical flow methods face two critical bottlenecks: they rely on task-specific feature extractors and per-domain fine-tuning, severely limiting zero-shot generalization, and their iterative local search architectures suffer from severe ambiguities when resolving large displacements across massive spatial gaps. No prior method simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on extreme motions and strong out-of-distribution generalization without dataset-specific adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260208/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a variable-length sequence of T video frames (default T=4 at 432×960 training resolution) and produces dense optical flow fields of shape H×W×2 between consecutive frames. For point tracking, it accepts a query frame with a set of 2D pixel locations and outputs trajectories across all target frames, using a sliding window of 8 frames to handle sequences up to 600 frames. The architecture is agnostic to the number of input frames, requiring no structural modification for different temporal spans.

## Method

MegaFlow formulates flow estimation as a global matching problem by leveraging frozen DINOv2 patch tokens processed through a 24-layer VGGT-style Transformer with alternating frame-wise and global self-attention, fused with trainable ResNet CNN features via a DPT-style head. An all-pairs correlation with softmax normalization over the fused features (at 1/7 resolution) yields initial flow estimates that naturally capture large displacements. A lightweight recurrent refinement module (two ConvNeXt blocks + two temporal attention blocks, 4 training / 8 inference iterations) then uses local correlation volumes to improve sub-pixel accuracy while preserving fine-grained details. The model is trained in three progressive stages (FlyingChairs → TartanAirV1 → FlyingThings) followed by a mixed-dataset stage, with the DINOv2 encoder kept frozen throughout. For point tracking, the same architecture computes correspondences from the first frame to each target frame without any architectural changes, propagating tracks through continuous displacement fields rather than explicit visibility heuristics.

## Results

On the Sintel (Final) zero-shot benchmark, MegaFlow achieves an EPE of 1.83 with 4 input frames, substantially outperforming the previous best multi-frame method MemFlow-T (2.06) and UFM (2.01). On KITTI (train), it attains the best Fl-all of 10.7% and a competitive Fl-epe of 3.00. For large displacements (s40+), MegaFlow reduces EPE to 4.729 on Sintel (Clean) and 11.175 on Sintel (Final), a significant margin over all baselines. On zero-shot point tracking (TAP-Vid), MegaFlow achieves 73.6% mean accuracy, surpassing dedicated trackers like CoTracker2 (70.0%) and PIPs++ (65.7%), and reaches 79.6% after fine-tuning on Kubric, setting a new state of the art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MegaFlow decisively outperforms all two-frame baselines on Sintel (Final): it beats UFM (2.01) by 0.18 EPE, SAMFlow (2.11) by 0.28, and FlowDiffuser (2.19) by 0.36. Among multi-frame methods, it surpasses MemFlow-T (2.06) by 0.23 EPE and StreamFlow (2.11) by 0.28 on Sintel (Final), while tying MemFlow-T on Sintel (Clean) at 0.85. On large displacements (s40+), the advantage widens dramatically: MegaFlow achieves 4.729 EPE on Sintel (Clean) versus 5.117 (SAMFlow), 5.239 (MemFlow-T), and 6.836 (UFM), confirming that global matching is critical for extreme motion. On KITTI, MegaFlow achieves the best Fl-all (10.7%) among all methods, beating UFM (11.0%) and MemFlow-T (12.8%). For zero-shot point tracking, MegaFlow (73.6% mean) outperforms all flow-based baselines (MemFlow-T: 70.6%, SEA-RAFT: 66.2%) and dedicated trackers (CoTracker2: 70.0%, PIPs++: 65.7%), and after fine-tuning on Kubric reaches 79.6%, surpassing CoTracker3 (77.7%) and AllTracker (79.5%). On the Spring benchmark, MegaFlow's zero-shot EPE of 0.349 (4 frames) beats all other zero-shot methods and is competitive with fine-tuned WAFT (0.325) without any dataset-specific adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MegaFlow synthesizes three established paradigms—VGGT's alternating frame-wise/global attention backbone, GMFlow's softmax global matching for correspondence, and RAFT's recurrent local-correlation refinement—into a deliberately simple pipeline, but its key departure is the use of frozen DINOv2 features and VGGT pre-trained weights as a static geometric prior adapted to dynamic motion, rather than training task-specific feature extractors from scratch. This contrasts with the dominant RAFT-family lineage (SEA-RAFT, FlowFormer, etc.) that relies on iterative local search and per-domain fine-tuning, and with recent foundation-model flow methods (UFM, WAFT) that either regress flow directly or confine pre-trained features to local cost-volume processing. The sliding-window tracking strategy follows AllTracker, but MegaFlow's global matching initialization replaces the warping-based local search that prior trackers depend on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single unified architecture achieves SOTA zero-shot optical flow and competitive point tracking without task-specific architectural changes, MegaFlow points toward a generalizable motion-estimation foundation model that reduces the need for per-domain fine-tuning. The practical limitation is computational: dense multi-frame modeling with 936M parameters and 24 Transformer layers imposes significant memory and latency costs (328 ms, 6 GB on RTX 3090), and the fixed-patch tokenization of the backbone degrades performance on non-standard resolutions like KITTI. Open questions include whether sequence-level efficiency can be improved without sacrificing the global context that drives the large-displacement advantage, and whether unified pre-training on flow and tracking jointly would further close the gap to dedicated trackers.
