## Problem

Deep multi-modal prompt learning for CLIP (e.g., MaPLe, CoPrompt) substantially improves few-shot and base-to-novel accuracy but requires millions of trainable parameters, abandoning the parameter efficiency that makes prompt tuning attractive. The paper asks whether deep multi-modal prompting can be made as parameter-efficient as early text-only methods like CoOp (~8K params) while maintaining competitive accuracy and generalization to novel classes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of class labels (wrapped in text templates) as input, processing them through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone. At inference, the prompted image feature is matched against drift-corrected text features (where the uniform component of the prompt-induced shift is subtracted and features are re-normalized) via cosine similarity to produce a class label. Only 11.5K low-rank prompt parameters are trained; the entire CLIP backbone and the zero-shot text features used for consistency anchoring remain frozen.

## Method

MMLoP parameterizes deep prompts at each of the first 9 transformer layers via a rank-1 factorization P = UV, where the up-projection U ∈ R^{4×1} is shared between vision and text modalities (enforcing cross-modal alignment at zero extra parameter cost) and the down-projections V_v, V_t are modality-specific. Three complementary regularizers close the accuracy gap left by the low-rank constraint: (i) a Self-Regulating Consistency Loss that penalizes L1 deviation of prompted features from frozen zero-shot CLIP features and applies a symmetric KL divergence on the logit distributions; (ii) a Uniform Drift Correction that subtracts the mean residual shift across all C class embeddings (computed as the difference between prompted and zero-shot text features) to remove base-class bias while preserving class-discriminative structure; and (iii) the shared up-projection itself, which forces gradient updates to simultaneously benefit both modalities. Training uses cross-entropy plus the consistency loss with SGD (lr=0.0025, 30 epochs), and all hyperparameters are fixed across 11 datasets and three benchmarks.

## Results

On the primary base-to-novel generalization benchmark (16-shot, 11 datasets), MMLoP achieves a harmonic mean of 79.70% with only 11.5K trainable parameters, outperforming 16 of 19 compared methods including MaPLe (78.55%, 3.55M params), PromptSRC (79.62%, 46K params), and CLIP-LoRA (77.28%, 184K params). On domain generalization (ImageNet → 4 OOD variants), MMLoP attains 60.46% average target accuracy, the best on ImageNet-R (77.63%). In all-to-all few-shot classification, MMLoP achieves 81.5% mean accuracy at 16 shots and the highest mean of 77.5% at 4 shots across 11 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMLoP (79.70% HM, 11.5K params) outperforms all text-only prompt methods: CoOp (71.66%), CoCoOp (75.83%), and KgCoOp (77.00%), with the largest gap over CoOp at +8.04% HM. Against multi-modal deep prompting baselines, MMLoP beats MaPLe (78.55%, 3.55M params) by +1.15% HM while using ~300× fewer parameters, and surpasses PromptSRC (79.62%, 46K), TCP (79.51%, 332K), MetaPrompt (79.09%, 31K), LoGoPrompt (79.03%), LASP (78.61%), RPO (77.78%), and CLIP-LoRA (77.28%, 184K). MMLoP trails only three methods on HM: MMA (79.87%, 581K params, −0.17%), 2SFS (80.20%, 170K params, −0.50%), and CoPrompt (80.48%, 3.82M params, −0.78%), all of which require 15×–332× more trainable parameters. On domain generalization, MMLoP (60.46% avg) edges out MaPLe (60.28%), CoPrompt (60.42%), and RPO (60.27%), and achieves the best ImageNet-R score (77.63%). In the 4-shot all-to-all setting, MMLoP attains the highest mean accuracy (77.5%), narrowly beating CLIP-LoRA (77.4%) and LP++ (75.6%), confirming its strength in extremely low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMLoP extends the deep multi-modal prompting framework of IVLP (Rasheed et al., CVPR 2023), which learns separate prompt tokens at every transformer layer of both CLIP encoders, by replacing full-rank prompt matrices with rank-1 low-rank factorizations inspired by LoRA. Unlike weight-space LoRA variants (CLIP-LoRA, Comp-LoRA, Block-LoRA) that modify frozen backbone parameters, MMLoP operates entirely in prompt space, keeping the CLIP backbone untouched. The self-regulating consistency loss builds on PromptSRC's (Khattak et al., ICCV 2023) idea of anchoring prompted features to zero-shot CLIP representations, but extends it with a symmetric KL divergence and L1 feature-level terms. The shared up-projection coupling vision and text prompts through a common low-rank factor is a novel structural constraint not present in prior multi-modal prompt methods, which treat the two modalities' prompts as fully independent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that MMLoP demonstrates deep multi-modal prompting can be made as parameter-efficient as early text-only methods (11.5K vs. 3.5M+ for MaPLe) while remaining competitive on accuracy, which matters for deployment scenarios where per-task parameter storage and update bandwidth are constrained. The uniform drift correction—a simple O(Cd) mean subtraction—offers a lightweight, interpretable mechanism for removing base-class bias that could be adopted by other prompt-tuning methods. Limitations include a persistent gap on fine-grained datasets (FGVCAircraft) where the rank-1 constraint limits discriminative capacity, and the approach has only been validated on CLIP ViT-B/16 and ViT-B/32, leaving open whether it transfers to larger or non-CLIP VLMs. An open question is whether the rank-1 sweet spot persists with more training data or larger backbones, where higher-rank subspaces might become beneficial.
