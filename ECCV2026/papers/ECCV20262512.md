## Problem

Looped (weight-shared) transformers offer extreme parameter efficiency by reusing a compact set of layers across multiple iterations, but standard training only optimizes the final loop output, causing severe quality degradation at any other loop count. This makes them impractical for flexible deployment where compute budgets vary. The paper asks: can a single looped transformer be trained to produce high-fidelity generation at *any* intermediate loop count, enabling elastic inference without retraining?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262512/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model takes a class label and either a noised 32×32×4 latent (diffusion path, via Stable Diffusion v1.4 VAE) or a 16×16 masked discrete token sequence (masked generative path, codebook size 1024), and outputs a 256×256 image. For video, it consumes a 4×16×16 masked token sequence and produces a 16×128×128 video. Critically, the loop count L at inference is a free parameter: the same trained model can be run with any L from 1 up to (and slightly beyond) its training L_max, trading compute for quality without architectural changes.

## Method

ELT replaces a deep stack of unique transformer layers with a composite block g_Θ of N unique layers applied L times (weight-shared), yielding effective depth N×L with only N layers of parameters. The key training innovation is Intra-Loop Self Distillation (ILSD): at each step, a teacher path executes the full L_max loops while a student path exits at a randomly sampled L_int < L_max, and the student is supervised by both ground-truth labels and the teacher's output (with stop-gradient). Because the student trajectory is a strict prefix of the teacher's forward pass, ILSD adds zero extra computation. The loss blends cross-entropy (masked generative) or sigmoid-weighted MSE (diffusion) for both paths, with a curriculum that linearly decays the ground-truth weight λ from 1 to 0 so the student transitions from label-anchored learning to teacher-mimicking. This forces the shared parameters to compress complex transformations into earlier loops, producing a continuous family of performant models from a single training run.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 (masked generative), ELT-XL (7N×4L) achieves FID 2.0 with only 111M parameters, matching MaskGIT-XL (446M) at 4× fewer parameters under iso-inference-compute (3.9k GFLOPs). In the diffusion setting, ELT (16N×2L) achieves FID 2.83 with 1.1B parameters, outperforming the 32-layer DiT baseline (FID 3.43, 2.1B params) by 0.6 FID with 2× fewer parameters. On class-conditional UCF-101 video generation, the compact 76M ELT model achieves FVD 72.8, beating MAGVIT-L (FVD 76, 306M params) by 3.2 FVD with 4× fewer parameters, and further reaches FVD 60.8 with increased loop count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The headline comparison is ELT-XL (7N×4L, 111M params) vs. MaskGIT-XL (446M params): both achieve FID 2.0 at the same inference compute (3.9k GFLOPs, 24 steps), representing a 4× parameter reduction. ELT-L (12N×2L, 152M) matches MaskGIT-Lg (303M) at FID 2.1 with half the parameters. In the diffusion setting (Table 2), ELT (16N×2L, 1.1B) beats DiT-32 (2.1B) by 0.6 FID (2.83 vs. 3.43), and ELT (8N×4L, 539M) beats it by 0.27 FID (3.16 vs. 3.43) with 4× fewer parameters. For video generation on UCF-101, ELT (6N×4L, 76M) outperforms MAGVIT-L (306M) by 3.2 FVD (72.8 vs. 76) at iso-inference-compute (~4.3k GFLOPs), and surpasses MaGNets (306M, FVD 96.42) by 23.6 FVD. ELT also outperforms PAR-14 (792M, FVD 99.5) and PAR-16 (792M, FVD 103.4) in video generation. The paper notes that ELT's elastic capability (varying L at inference) is unique among these baselines, which are fixed-depth and cannot trade quality for speed without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELT extends the weight-tied looping paradigm introduced by Universal Transformers [9] into the visual generation domain, applying it to both masked generative transformers (MaskGIT [7], MAGVIT [72]) and diffusion transformers (DiT [49]). The key departure from prior looped architectures is the Intra-Loop Self Distillation (ILSD) training objective, which treats the full-loop model as an internal teacher and distills it into intermediate-loop students within the same forward pass—eliminating the 'black box' problem where vanilla looping only produces coherent outputs at its exact training depth. Unlike Deep Equilibrium Models that require fixed-point solvers, or elastic generation methods (EDiT, LoopViT) that rely on architectural skipping or external search, ELT directly regularizes the recursive process so that any prefix of the loop trajectory yields a valid prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× parameter reduction at iso-inference-compute (FID 2.0 with 111M vs. 446M params) combined with up to 3.5× throughput gains from on-chip parameter residency makes ELT practically significant for deploying high-fidelity generative models on resource-constrained hardware. The any-time inference capability—traversing the quality-compute Pareto front by simply adjusting loop count at test time without retraining—opens a deployment axis (latency-critical on-device vs. high-fidelity cloud) that was previously unavailable in looped architectures. Open questions include whether the modest extrapolation beyond training depth (L > L_max) generalizes across scales, whether ELT can dynamically allocate compute across denoising steps in diffusion, and how it composes with emerging one-step generative paradigms like consistency models where loop count becomes the sole quality lever.
