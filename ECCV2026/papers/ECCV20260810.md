## Problem

Industrial anomaly inspection is bottlenecked by the scarcity of real defect samples, and existing synthesis methods fail to simultaneously achieve high visual fidelity and pixel-accurate spatial grounding: inpainting-based approaches produce poorly integrated anomalies with misaligned masks, while joint image-generation methods lack precise mask control. The paper asks whether a diffusion framework can learn anomaly appearance and position from only a few exemplars while generating coherent anomalous images with exact per-pixel spatial alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260810/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pixel-wise integer semantic map (encoding anomaly class identity per pixel, encoded to an 8×8 spatial feature grid via ConvNeXt-Tiny), a text prompt containing learned product and anomaly token embeddings, and optionally a real normal image whose latent is partially noised to initialize the reverse diffusion (NDI). It outputs a full anomalous RGB image with the defect spatially grounded to the semantic map, along with the corresponding mask; 1,000 image-mask pairs per anomaly type are generated to train downstream detectors. Multi-class defects are supported by summing separate semantic maps and extending the prompt template.

## Method

GroundingAnomaly builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.4 U-Net and introduces two trainable modules. The Spatial Conditioning Module (SCM) encodes the semantic map with ConvNeXt-Tiny, learns disentangled product and anomaly token embeddings via a prompt template, projects them to the CLIP embedding dimension, spatially broadcasts them according to the semantic labels, concatenates with the spatial features, and flattens into conditioning tokens. The Gated Self-Attention Module (GSM) concatenates these conditioning tokens with visual tokens in each U-Net transformer block, applies self-attention over the joint sequence, extracts only the visual portion, and scales the residual by tanh(γ) (γ initialized to 0) so pretrained priors are preserved at the start of training; LoRA provides low-rank updates to the self-attention layers. Training uses mixed normal-anomalous batches (MNT) to regularize against domain collapse, and inference optionally starts from a partially-noised normal image latent (NDI) to anchor the background and reduce denoising steps.

## Results

On MVTec AD, GroundingAnomaly achieves 99.3% pixel-level AUROC, 85.9% image-level AP, and 81.8% F1-max for anomaly segmentation using a simple U-Net trained on generated data, outperforming all synthesis-based and unsupervised baselines. On VisA, it reaches 98.2% AUROC-P and 67.2% AP-I, surpassing the second-best synthesis method (DualAnoDiff) by 6.3% AP. For generation quality, it attains the highest IS (1.99 on MVTec AD, 1.29 on VisA) and IC-LPIPS (0.41, 0.31) among all compared methods. For instance-level detection, average mAP improves by 1.52% on MVTec AD and 2.34% on VisA over the best prior synthesis method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among synthesis-based baselines, GroundingAnomaly outperforms SeaS (the closest competitor) by 3.7% AP-I on MVTec AD (85.9% vs. 82.2%) and 1.5% AP-I on VisA (67.2% vs. 65.7%), while also achieving higher generation quality (IS 1.99 vs. 1.95). Against DualAnoDiff, it gains 4.8% AP-I on VisA (67.2% vs. 62.4%) and 3.2% AP-I on MVTec AD (85.9% vs. 82.7%). Compared to unsupervised inspection methods, a simple U-Net trained on GroundingAnomaly-generated data surpasses the best unsupervised method (Dinomaly2) by 11.8% AP-I on MVTec AD (85.9% vs. 74.1%) and 12.5% AP-I on VisA (67.2% vs. 54.7%), though Dinomaly2 retains a slight edge on image-level AUROC (100.0% vs. 99.2% on MVTec). Against the synthesis-based inspection method GLASS, GroundingAnomaly matches its pixel-level AUROC (99.3%) but substantially outperforms it on AP-I (85.9% vs. 74.1% on MVTec; 67.2% vs. 45.6% on VisA) and F1-max (81.8% vs. 70.4%). For instance-level detection, GroundingAnomaly achieves the highest average mAP across all three detectors (Faster R-CNN, DETR, YOLOv5) on both datasets, with gains of 1.52% (MVTec) and 2.34% (VisA) over SeaS.

## Relation to Prior Work

GroundingAnomaly extends the Anomaly Image Generation (AIG) line of work (DFMGAN, DualAnoDiff, SeaS) by introducing explicit pixel-wise spatial grounding, drawing on GLIGEN's gated attention mechanism for layout-conditioned generation and Textual Inversion for compact token learning. It departs from Anomaly Generation (AG) methods like AnomalyDiffusion that inpaint defects onto fixed backgrounds, and from prior AIG methods that derive masks post-hoc from low-resolution attention maps. The core architectural contribution is a Spatial Conditioning Module that fuses a ConvNeXt-encoded semantic map with disentangled product/anomaly tokens, injected into a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net via a Gated Self-Attention Module with LoRA, preserving pretrained priors while enabling few-shot adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in industrial quality control: the scarcity of real anomalous samples and the need for pixel-accurate masks to train downstream detectors. By generating spatially grounded anomalies with precise masks from as few as 1–2 exemplars, GroundingAnomaly offers a viable data-augmentation pipeline that outperforms both unsupervised detectors and prior synthesis-based approaches on MVTec AD and VisA. However, the 1-shot setting shows substantial degradation (AP drops from 85.9% to 72.3% on MVTec AD), and the method still requires at least one real anomalous exemplar per class, leaving zero-shot anomaly synthesis as an open problem the authors acknowledge for future work.
