## Problem

Foundation models like SAM, pretrained on natural imagery, struggle to segment human figures in bas-relief photographs because the extreme material homogeneity, subtle 2.5D depth variations, and indistinct soft edges create a severe lack of chromatic and geometric contrast. For reburied or inaccessible heritage (e.g., Borobudur's Karmawibhangga panels), only archival monocular photographs exist, ruling out external 3D sensing, yet prompt-free single-image segmentation under these conditions remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB photograph as the sole external input; depth and edge priors are derived from that same image via a monocular estimation framework, so no depth sensor or multi-view data is required. The dataset images have a native resolution of 3200×1024 pixels, well exceeding the model's 1024×1024 patch size, so inference uses overlapped sliding windows (patch 1024, stride 64) with Gaussian-weighted merging to produce a seamless full-resolution binary mask. The output is a single foreground probability map thresholded at 0.5, with multimask output explicitly disabled for binary human-figure segmentation.

## Method

ReliefSAM freezes the SAM ViT-B image and prompt encoders and introduces two trainable components: a Multi-Prior Feature Encoder (MFE) that processes depth and edge maps through parallel 5-stage CNN branches, fuses them via channel concatenation and a Squeeze-and-Excitation recalibration module, and tokenizes the result into a geometric token sequence; and a series of relief-specific geometric adapters inserted at transformer blocks {2,4,6,8,10} plus a final post-encoder injection. Each adapter uses an extract-then-inject bidirectional cross-attention mechanism: geometric tokens first attend to image tokens to absorb instance-aware cues, then the filtered geometry is injected back into the image stream, preventing spurious structures (e.g., weathering cracks) from contaminating SAM's visual features. Training uses a Dice-Focal loss with only the MFE, adapters, and mask decoder updated (parameter-efficient fine-tuning), while inference is fully prompt-free using SAM's learned no-mask embeddings. A key empirical finding is that depth and edge priors exhibit opposite biases—depth is conservative (high precision, low recall) while edge is aggressive (high recall, low precision)—and their joint fusion achieves a geometric consensus that outperforms either prior alone or the RGB-only adapter baseline.

## Results

On the 156-panel Borobudur Karmawibhangga (Hidden Relief) benchmark, ReliefSAM achieves 91.78% IoU and 95.65% Dice for binary human figure segmentation under a single monocular RGB input. This represents a +7.96 IoU gain over decoder-only SAM fine-tuning (83.82% IoU) and a +0.53 IoU gain over the strong pure-RGB adapter baseline (91.25% IoU). The method also surpasses conventional segmentation networks: +3.92 IoU over SegFormer (87.86%) and +2.46 IoU over DeepLabV3+ (89.32%).

| Method | Backbone | IoU (%) | Dice (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SegFormer [28] | MiT-B3 | 87.86 | 93.45 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two conventional RGB segmentation baselines, ReliefSAM wins decisively: it outperforms SegFormer (MiT-B3) by +3.92 IoU / +2.20 Dice and DeepLabV3+ (ResNet-50) by +2.46 IoU / +1.38 Dice, confirming that task-specific geometry-aware adaptation of a foundation model surpasses generic segmentation architectures on this domain. Against the SAM-based baselines, the picture is more nuanced. SAM-DecoderFT (frozen image encoder, fine-tuned decoder only) is the weakest SAM variant at 83.82% IoU, showing that decoder-only adaptation is insufficient for relief geometry. The SAM-Adapter baseline (same adapter architecture but RGB-only, no geometric priors) is a very strong competitor at 91.25% IoU, and ReliefSAM's advantage over it is modest (+0.53 IoU, +0.28 Dice). Ablation further reveals that injecting a single geometric prior actually degrades performance below the RGB-only adapter (Depth-only: 89.97% IoU; Edge-only: 90.52% IoU), indicating modality interference; only the joint depth+edge fusion recovers and exceeds the RGB-only ceiling, with depth providing conservative structural cues (higher Precision, 95.37%) and edge providing aggressive boundary sensitivity (higher Recall, 97.43%) that together balance the Precision–Recall trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM (Segment Anything Model) backbone by injecting image-derived geometric priors through lightweight cross-attention adapters, building on the PEFT/adapter paradigm (Houlsby et al., Hu et al.) and the monocular relief depth/edge estimation framework of Pan et al. [23]. It departs from prior cultural-heritage segmentation work that either relies on explicit 3D acquisition (LiDAR, structured light) or on prompt-based SAM pipelines (Galanakis et al., Réby et al.) by operating under a strict monocular, prompt-free constraint where geometry must be recovered from the same RGB photograph. The Multi-Prior Feature Encoder with its extract-then-inject bidirectional cross-attention design is a novel architectural contribution that avoids the modality contamination problem of naive feature addition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: many heritage sites (e.g., Borobudur's reburied Karmawibhangga panels) are permanently inaccessible to modern 3D scanning, leaving archival monocular photographs as the sole visual record. The finding that a single geometric prior can degrade performance through modality interference, while joint depth-edge fusion achieves a complementary Precision-Recall balance, offers a useful design principle for multi-prior integration in low-contrast domains. Limitations include evaluation on a single small dataset (20 test images), binary-only segmentation, and dependence on a specific prior-extraction model; open questions include generalization to multi-class heritage semantics, other relief traditions, and robustness when prior quality degrades further.
