## Problem

Existing multi-view anomaly detection methods assume all views are fully available and model each view independently, so they degrade sharply when real-world occlusions or sensor faults cause partial information loss. No prior work defines a benchmark or architecture specifically designed to handle incomplete views by borrowing complementary information across viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260852/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 5 incomplete RGB views per sample (each with a binary mask marking missing pixels) at 392×392 resolution, alongside the corresponding complete views as reconstruction targets during training. The RIMAD dataset simulates incompleteness by masking 50% of the SAM-segmented product area with random 8–32 px² rectangles. At inference, the model outputs a pixel-level anomaly map derived from the cosine distance between reconstructed and ground-truth features, and an image-level anomaly score aggregated from that map.

## Method

IMMoE is a reconstruction-based framework built on a DINOv2-R ViT-Base/14 encoder with three synergistic components. The Local Anomaly Enhancement Encoder (LAEE) applies stochastic dropout (p=0.1) to per-view features followed by linear attention, regularizing the model against overfitting to masked patterns while recovering spatial dependencies disrupted by discarding. The Multi-view Expert Fusion (MVEF) module treats each view as a dedicated expert in a Mixture-of-Experts architecture: a gating network computes per-view importance scores, view-specific two-layer experts encode each perspective, and a weighted sum yields a global fused representation that is injected back into each view via cross-attention. The Area Adaptive Loss modulates per-patch gradients by the ratio of local cosine loss to batch mean (raised to θ=3) plus a region-specific prior (β=0.4 for masked regions, 0.2 for product foreground, 0 for background), steering optimization toward information-deficient and task-relevant areas.

## Results

On the newly introduced RIMAD dataset (50% view masking), IMMoE achieves 84.7% I-AUROC and 33.1% P-AP, outperforming the strongest single-view baseline INP-Former (82.4% / 29.6%) by 2.3% and 3.5% respectively, and the dedicated multi-view method MVAD (79.3% / 27.4%) by 5.4% and 5.7%. On the complete-view Real-IAD benchmark, IMMoE reaches 89.7% I-AUROC and 44.3% P-AP, surpassing the previous best (Dinomaly, 89.3% / 42.8%) and INP-Former (89.1% / 43.7%). The paper reports an 11.8% relative gain in pixel-level AP and 2.8% in image-level AUROC over the prior SOTA on RIMAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMMoE outperforms all nine baselines on both datasets and both metrics. On RIMAD (the incomplete-view setting), the largest margins are against MVAD (−5.4% I-AUROC, −5.7% P-AP) and Dinomaly (−7.0% I-AUROC, −6.0% P-AP), indicating that existing multi-view and single-view methods degrade severely under masking. On Real-IAD (complete views), IMMoE edges out the two strongest competitors—Dinomaly (89.3/42.8) and INP-Former (89.1/43.7)—by 0.4% I-AUROC and 1.5% P-AP, showing the MVEF module provides consistent gains even without missing data. The gap over MVAD widens to 3.1% I-AUROC and 14.0% P-AP on Real-IAD, suggesting that MVAD's window-based attention is less effective at capturing cross-view correlations than the MoE-based gating. Older baselines (UniAD, SimpleNet, DiAD) trail by 6–19% in I-AUROC, confirming that recent reconstruction-based and SSM-based architectures are substantially stronger but still fall short of IMMoE's cross-view fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-reconstruction paradigm established by INP-Former and Dinomaly (both CVPR 2025), adopting their linear-attention decoder and cosine-distance loss, but reframes the problem around incomplete multi-view inputs rather than complete single-view or complete multi-view settings. It departs from existing multi-view anomaly detection methods (MVAD, IDIF) that assume all views are fully available, and repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts routing mechanism—previously applied to class-conditional expert selection (MoEAD) or prompt aggregation (PromptMoE)—as a view-level fusion operator that adaptively weights cross-view semantic contributions. The Area Adaptive Loss generalizes the region-weighted gradient modulation from INP-Former by adding explicit priors for masked versus foreground regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing IMVAD and releasing RIMAD, the paper provides the first controlled benchmark for evaluating robustness under realistic view incompleteness, a failure mode that is common in industrial inspection but previously unaddressed. The non-monotonic mask-ratio ablation (performance dips at 25% masking but recovers at 75%) suggests that moderate corruption is harder than severe corruption because it leaves ambiguous local cues that mislead single-view reasoning—a finding that could inform data augmentation and robustness strategies more broadly. Open questions include whether the random-rectangle masking protocol captures the spatial structure of real occlusions, how the approach scales beyond five views or to 3D/multi-modal settings, and whether the MoE fusion generalizes to heterogeneous view types (e.g., RGB + thermal).
