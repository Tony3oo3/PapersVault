## Problem

Training-free feature caching accelerates DiT-based video world models by reusing intermediate activations across denoising steps, but existing methods treat cache reuse as a zero-order hold—copying stale features verbatim when global drift is small. This produces ghosting, semantic smearing, and motion incoherence in dynamic scenes, where errors compound across autoregressive rollouts. Three specific blindspots worsen the problem: global drift metrics mask foreground changes, all spatial locations are weighted equally regardless of perceptual importance, and a single static threshold ignores the distinct structure-formation versus detail-refinement phases of denoising.

## Input / Output

WorldCache operates as a training-free, drop-in inference accelerator on DiT-based video world models (Cosmos-Predict2.5 at 2B/14B, WAN2.1 at 1.3B/14B, HunyuanVideo). It accepts the same inputs as the base model—text prompts for Text2World or a conditioning image plus text prompt for Image2World—and produces 93-frame video rollouts (~5.8 s at 16 FPS, 24 latent frames) using 35 denoising steps with Euler scheduling. No architectural changes or retraining are required; the method only modifies which transformer blocks execute at each denoising step and how cached residuals are combined.

## Method

WorldCache inherits the probe-then-cache skeleton from DiCache (evaluate shallow probe blocks, then decide whether to skip deep blocks) but redesigns both the skip criterion and the reuse mechanism. Four composable, training-free modules work together: (1) Causal Feature Caching (CFC) computes a two-step latent velocity and tightens the skip threshold during fast motion; (2) Saliency-Weighted Drift (SWD) reweights the probe drift signal using channel-variance saliency so that foreground changes trigger recomputation while background fluctuations do not; (3) Optimal Feature Approximation (OFA) replaces verbatim copying with a least-squares vector-projection blend of cached residuals plus optional motion-compensated warping estimated via multiscale latent correlation; (4) Adaptive Threshold Scheduling (ATS) progressively relaxes the threshold across the denoising trajectory, keeping it tight during structure-formation steps and aggressive during late refinement. Together these modules convert caching from a brittle static shortcut into a perception-constrained dynamical approximation, achieving 2.3× speedup with 99.4% quality retention on Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B.

## Results

On PAI-Bench (Image2World, Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B), WorldCache achieves a 2.3× speedup (55.04 s → 24.48 s) while retaining 99.4% of baseline overall quality (0.7977 vs. 0.8030). On Text2World at the same scale, it reaches 2.1× speedup with near-identical average score (0.7485 vs. 0.7480). At the 14B scale, WorldCache delivers 2.2× (T2W) and 2.1× (I2W) speedups with <0.5% quality loss. On VBench (HunyuanVideo), WorldCache attains the best accelerated score (82.23) at 2.95× speedup, and on Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B it even exceeds the unaccelerated VBench score (76.47 vs. 76.35) at 2.25×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldCache dominates the speed–quality frontier across all six baselines. Against DiCache (1.39×, Overall 0.7941), WorldCache is 65% faster (2.30×) while matching or exceeding quality (0.7977 vs. 0.7941). Against FasterCache (1.7×, Overall 0.665 on T2W / 0.75 on I2W), WorldCache is slower but recovers 4–5 points of overall score, eliminating the severe ghosting and hallucination artifacts. Against EasyCache (1.37×, 0.7975) and TeaCache-Slow (1.10×, 0.7979), WorldCache achieves comparable quality at 1.7–2.1× the speed. Against MagCache (2.05×, 0.7546) and TaylorSeer (1.86×, 0.7031), WorldCache is both faster and substantially higher in quality (+4.3 and +9.5 Overall points respectively), confirming that motion-aware and saliency-weighted decisions are critical for dynamic world-model scenes where magnitude-only or Taylor-extrapolation caching degrades sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldCache inherits its architectural skeleton from the probe-then-cache paradigm of DiCache and FasterCache, which skip deep transformer blocks when a lightweight drift probe falls below a threshold. It departs from these methods by replacing the zero-order-hold reuse (verbatim copying of stale cached features) with a perception-constrained dynamical approximation: motion-adaptive thresholds (CFC), saliency-weighted drift (SWD), least-squares optimal blending with optional motion-compensated warping (OFA), and phase-aware threshold relaxation (ATS). The motion-compensation component draws on classical optical-flow ideas (Lucas-Kanade, RAFT) but operates entirely in latent space via multiscale correlation, and the saliency signal is derived from channel-variance of probe features rather than an external detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.3× speedup with <1% quality loss on Cosmos-Predict2.5 is practically significant for interactive world-model rollouts and closed-loop deployment, where latency is the primary bottleneck. The clean ablation narrative (CFC for safety, SWD for hit-rate, OFA for quality margin, ATS for speed) gives practitioners a modular toolkit they can adopt incrementally. Open questions include whether the fixed hyperparameters (τ₀=0.08, α=0.2, βs=0.12, βd=4.0) truly generalize to very long autoregressive rollouts where approximation errors compound, whether latent-space correlation-based warping remains reliable under extreme or non-rigid motion, and whether the approach extends to action-conditioned world models where the conditioning signal changes per-step.
