## Problem

In instance-dependent partial label learning (IDPLL), noisy candidate labels are semantically similar to the ground-truth label, making disambiguation far harder than in the instance-independent setting. Existing methods fail to enforce the structural prior that candidate labels should dominate non-candidate labels in the prediction distribution, and the recent distillation-based approach DIRK relies on a heuristic rectification that does not guarantee minimal perturbation to the teacher's original predictions, potentially destroying their relative structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260650/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (28×28 for MNIST variants, 32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for fine-grained datasets) paired with a candidate label set S_i ⊂ Y that is guaranteed to contain the true label but whose members are visually and semantically similar to it. The output is a single class label from the full label space Y, with the true label hidden within S_i. The candidate set size varies per sample, and the method must disambiguate among candidates that are specifically chosen to be confusable with the ground truth.

## Method

DKDP performs dual knowledge distillation at both the logit and embedding levels. At the logit level, a constrained projection maps the teacher's softmax output to a feasible distribution where the minimum candidate prediction exceeds the maximum non-candidate prediction by a margin ρ; this is solved in closed form via a one-parameter rescaling family q_i(r) that preserves within-group relative structure, with the minimal scaling factor r* = max(1, r_min) derived analytically to guarantee interpretability. The student is then trained to match the corrected teacher distribution via cross-entropy. At the embedding level, class prototypes are maintained in the student and updated by moving average over pseudo-labeled samples; a KL divergence loss aligns the prototype-similarity distribution with the corrected teacher's candidate-restricted confidence, transferring reliable supervision into the feature space to enforce inter-class separability. The total loss is L_kd + β·L_fd, combining both distillation objectives.

## Results

DKDP achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all seven datasets. On benchmark datasets, it reaches 91.81% on Fashion-MNIST, 98.49% on Kuzushiji-MNIST, 93.64% on CIFAR-10, and 70.72% on CIFAR-100. On fine-grained datasets, it attains 48.92% on Flower-102, 70.50% on Oxford-IIITPet, and 54.04% on CUB-200, consistently outperforming the strongest IDPLL baseline (DIRK) by 0.3–5.6 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DKDP outperforms all 12 baselines (7 IIPLL + 5 IDPLL) on every dataset. Against the closest IDPLL competitor DIRK, DKDP gains +0.33% on FMNIST, +1.69% on KMNIST, +2.77% on CIFAR-10, and +1.95% on CIFAR-100. The advantage is most pronounced on fine-grained datasets: +4.89% over DIRK on Flower-102, +5.55% on Oxford-IIITPet, and +4.75% on CUB-200, indicating the dual distillation strategy is particularly effective when inter-class semantic overlap is high. Among IIPLL methods, the strongest (RC) trails DKDP by 6.94% on FMNIST and 23.85% on Flower-102, confirming that instance-independent assumptions are insufficient for IDPLL. The ablation further shows that removing the fine-grained semantic distillation (L_fd) costs 0.8–4.6% depending on dataset complexity, while removing both distillation components causes a dramatic 13–11% drop, underscoring the synergistic contribution of the two modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DIRK [27], a distillation-based approach for instance-dependent partial label learning (IDPLL), by replacing its heuristic teacher-output rectification with a constrained projection that admits a closed-form solution and provably minimizes KL divergence from the original teacher distribution. It also builds on the broader PLL disambiguation literature (Feng et al. [7], Lv et al. [15], Wang et al. [23]) but departs from those methods by explicitly encoding the candidate/non-candidate label prior into the distillation signal rather than relying on feature-space similarity or consistency regularization. The dual distillation design—combining logit-level correction with prototype-based embedding-space alignment—repurposes the class-prototype mechanism from PICO [23] and Yang et al. [34] but couples it to the corrected teacher distribution, creating a unified output-to-representation knowledge transfer pathway.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constrained projection offers a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc teacher-output adjustments in weakly supervised distillation, and the finding that fine-grained semantic distillation yields increasingly large gains as dataset complexity grows (0.94% on KMNIST to 4.56% on Flower-102) suggests the embedding-space component is particularly valuable in fine-grained recognition where inter-class similarity is high. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification with ResNet backbones, moderate computational overhead from the prototype module, and reliance on a momentum-based teacher that may lag in highly non-stationary training. Open questions include whether the constrained projection generalizes to multi-label or hierarchical label structures, and whether the dual distillation strategy transfers to other weakly supervised paradigms such as noisy-label or semi-supervised learning.
