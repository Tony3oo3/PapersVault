## Problem

Transformer-based video diffusion models rely on 3D attention with quadratic complexity, making end-to-end training at ultra-high resolutions (e.g., 4K) prohibitively expensive, so most models are capped at 480P. While fine-tuning with high-resolution images is a natural workaround, naively doing so introduces noticeable noise and artifacts due to the image-video modality gap, leaving no principled way to upgrade a low-resolution video DiT to synthesize 4K content using only image data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260438/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and produces an ultra-high-resolution video (1920×1088 or 3840×2176) via a coarse-to-fine pipeline: a video is first generated at the model's native resolution to establish global layout and motion, then a second pass recovers high-frequency details at the target resolution. Training requires no video data at all—only 2.3K high-resolution images (2752×1536, generated by FLUX 1.1 Pro Ultra) are used to fine-tune LoRA adapters on a single A100 GPU in roughly one day.

## Method

The core contribution is Relay LoRA, a two-stage LoRA adaptation that decouples modality alignment from spatial extrapolation: Stage 1 trains LoRA₁ on low-resolution images to bridge the image-video gap, which is then merged into the base weights; Stage 2 trains LoRA₂ on high-resolution images for spatial extrapolation, and only LoRA₂ is loaded at inference to preserve video generation fidelity. To handle the increased token count at high resolution, Global Coarse Local Fine Attention (GCLFA) replaces full 3D attention with an inward sliding-window local branch (ensuring uniform receptive fields at boundaries) combined with a pooled coarse global branch that preserves long-range semantic coherence. A High Frequency Awareness Training Objective (HFATO) further improves detail by first degrading latents via a downsample-upsample operator, adding noise, and supervising the model to reconstruct the clean latent, explicitly encouraging high-frequency recovery. The entire framework operates within a flow-matching formulation on top of Wan2.2's DiT backbone.

## Results

On VBench, ViBe achieves an Overall Score of 74.3% at 1080P (1920×1088) and 74.4% at 4K (3840×2176), outperforming all seven baselines at both resolutions. At 4K, the method surpasses the strongest training-based baseline CineScale by 0.8 points (74.4% vs. 73.6%) and the base Wan2.2 model by 8.9 points (74.4% vs. 65.5%). ViBe also leads on Aesthetic Quality (61.4% at both resolutions), Imaging Quality (65.7% at 1080P, 66.1% at 4K), and Overall Consistency (26.7% at 1080P, 27.1% at 4K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViBe outperforms all seven baselines on Overall Score at both 1080P and 4K. At 1080P, the margin over the closest competitor (ESRGAN, 72.6%) is 1.7 points, while T3-Video lags far behind at 63.2% due to severe degradation in Aesthetic Quality (37.8%) and Imaging Quality (35.8%). At 4K, ViBe edges out CineScale (73.6%) by 0.8 points and UAV (73.5%) by 0.9 points, confirming the paper's claim of surpassing training-based methods that use real high-resolution video data. The training-free methods I-Max and HiFlow preserve global structure but produce lower-fidelity details, reflected in their lower Aesthetic Quality scores (57.0% and 56.6% at 4K). Super-resolution methods (ESRGAN, UAV) improve clarity over the base Wan2.2 model but cannot synthesize new fine-grained content, resulting in lower Aesthetic and Overall Consistency scores. ViBe's advantage is most pronounced on Aesthetic Quality and Overall Consistency, where it leads by 1.3–4.4 points over the nearest baseline at each resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the coarse-to-fine generation pipeline from SD-Edit [34] and the DiT-based video generation architecture of Wan2.2 [46], but departs fundamentally from prior high-resolution video generation work by eliminating the need for any video training data. Where training-based methods like CineScale and T3-Video require expensive high-resolution video corpora, and training-free methods like I-Max and HiFlow sacrifice fine-grained detail, this work introduces a two-stage Relay LoRA strategy that decouples modality alignment from spatial extrapolation—drawing a conceptual parallel to prerequisite learning in NLP [31]. The GCLFA attention mechanism extends the sliding-window attention idea from FreeSwim [49] with an inward-shift design to handle boundary tokens, and the HFATO loss repurposes standard flow-matching training by inserting a degradation step before noise injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single A100 GPU and roughly one day of training suffices to upgrade a 480P/720P video model to 4K generation, making ultra-high-resolution video synthesis accessible without large-scale video data or multi-GPU clusters. The finding that image-only training can outperform video-trained baselines challenges the implicit assumption that video models must see video data to generate video, suggesting that spatial extrapolation and temporal modeling can be decoupled more cleanly than previously thought. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to substantially longer videos, to architectures beyond Wan2.2, and whether the reliance on FLUX-generated synthetic images (2.3K samples) introduces a quality ceiling that real high-resolution image data could break.
