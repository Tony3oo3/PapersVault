## Problem

Offline-trained local feature detectors and matchers degrade significantly when deployed on previously unseen targets, since offline training cannot anticipate the appearance and structural variations of unknown objects. Existing event-camera pipelines typically treat events as an auxiliary input modality or rely on heavy multi-GPU offline pre-training, leaving little room for fast, label-free test-time adaptation to new targets in robotics applications such as 6-DoF pose tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261002/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EVEE consumes synchronized RGB frames and a calibrated event stream (456×360 resolution, 60 FPS windows) from a beam-splitter RGB-event rig. Events are not concatenated as features but are converted into a single normalized event-count/magnitude confidence map that serves as proxy supervision for online updates and as voting evidence for the Event Voting Bank. The system outputs high-confidence keypoint correspondences (after dual-softmax mutual filtering with dustbin) and a camera pose estimate evaluated via axis-angle rotation error.

## Method

EVEE constructs event-derived proxy supervision to drive label-free online adaptation of a lightweight RGB keypoint head (Haar-wavelet contour extractor → 3×3×3 Conv3D → 3×3 conv → 1×1 conv → Sigmoid) on top of a frozen backbone. Three components enable this: (i) Training Warehouse (TraW) runs 23 parallel training threads with best-weight broadcasting and a loss threshold (τ=0.0001) to achieve ~250 updates in 20 seconds; (ii) Event Voting Bank (EVB) maintains temporally decayed (ρ=0.6) confidence-weighted foreground and background prototypes at feature resolution, suppressing background noise while retaining persistent target structure; (iii) ReWeight is a fixed 2-layer MLP that outputs a bounded attenuation weight w_ij ∈ (0,1] from the base match logit, a soft geometric cue, and EVB priors, applied as a non-positive logit penalty before dual-softmax mutual selection. Offline pre-training on the Event-Camera Dataset learns an RGB-to-event-corner mapping via MagicPoint-associated corners, so the network can predict physically grounded proxy event maps from RGB alone at inference.

## Results

On NAVI-MultiView and TUD-L, EVEE achieves an average pose ACC/AUC of 68.7, outperforming the strongest RGB baseline SP+LightGlue (66.8) by ~1.9% and running at 51.3 FPS versus 6.9 FPS for SP+LG. On the real Event-Camera Dataset (Table 4), EVEE reaches an average ACC/AUC of 66.5, a 4.4% gain over the second-best method (DISK+LG at 62.1), with a ~9.6% relative gain on the two unseen sequences (boxes-6DoF and poster-6DoF). EVEE also substantially outperforms the event-input baseline SuperEvent+SuperGlue, whose ACC@10° on NAVI is 10 percentage points lower than EVEE's.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVEE outperforms all seven RGB-only baselines on the combined NAVI+TUD-L average: it leads SP+LightGlue (66.8) by 1.9 points, SP+SuperGlue (66.3) by 2.4, SIFT+LG and DoGHardNet+LG (65.7 each) by 3.0, OmniGlue (64.9) by 3.8, ALIKED+LG (64.8) by 3.9, and DISK+LG (62.8) by 5.9. On TUD-L specifically, EVEE's ACC@5° of 77.0 exceeds the next best (OmniGlue at 74.5) by 2.5 points, reflecting its advantage under severe illumination changes. Against the event-input baseline SuperEvent+SuperGlue (SE+SG), EVEE's ACC@10° on NAVI is 81.4 versus 71.4—a 10-point gap—attributed to SE+SG's training bias toward high-overlap consecutive frames. In speed, EVEE runs at 51.3 FPS for matching, roughly 7× faster than SP+LG (6.9 FPS) and 5.6× faster than OmniGlue (9.1 FPS), while also being faster than SP+SuperGlue (42.5 FPS). On the real Event-Camera Dataset, EVEE leads the second-best method (DISK+LG) by 4.4% in average ACC/AUC and by ~9.6% on the two unseen sequences, confirming generalization to previously unseen targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVEE sits at the intersection of learned local feature matching (SuperPoint, LightGlue, SuperGlue, OmniGlue) and event-based vision, but departs from both lines in a specific way: rather than treating events as an auxiliary input modality to be fused with RGB features (as in SuperEvent or multimodal depth networks), it repurposes short-window event activity as online proxy supervision that drives label-free adaptation of a frozen RGB keypoint head at deployment. The offline pre-training stage borrows the MagicPoint-style corner-detection formulation to teach the network to predict event-like corner responses from RGB, while the online components (Training Warehouse, Event Voting Bank, ReWeight) are novel architectural contributions with no direct predecessor in the feature-matching literature. The approach also connects to the broader 'events-as-supervision' trend (dense event pre-training, V2E, EvINR) but applies it to a fundamentally different regime: test-time, target-specific adaptation rather than large-scale offline training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in robotics deployment where targets change frequently and cannot be covered by offline training data, offering a deployable solution at 51.3 FPS matching speed on a single GPU. A notable finding is that naively appending event features to a strong RGB matcher (SP+LG) actually degrades accuracy, suggesting that the structured use of events through temporal voting and proxy supervision is essential rather than the raw signal itself. The main limitation acknowledged is that EVEE remains weaker than LightGlue-family matchers under large viewpoint changes, and the evaluation relies partly on simulated events (Prophesee simulator) for NAVI and TUD-L, which may not fully capture real sensor noise characteristics. Open questions include whether the multi-threaded Training Warehouse scales to more complex or textured targets, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the 6-DoF pose estimation setting to broader open-world matching.
