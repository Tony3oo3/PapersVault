## Problem

Small MLLMs (≤3B) face a zero-sum trade-off when incorporating a new tactile modality: the limited parameter budget means that updating weights for touch alignment overwrites pretrained vision-language representations, causing catastrophic forgetting even with small tactile datasets. Prior VTL alignment methods either rely on large 7B+ LLM backbones or use multi-stage PEFT pipelines, leaving the question of how to non-destructively expand sensory capabilities in compact, edge-deployable models largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image, a 224×224 tactile contact image from a DIGIT sensor, and a text prompt, which are concatenated into a single autoregressive token sequence. The tactile image is processed by a 5.8M-parameter ViT-Tiny (16×16 patches, ImageNet-initialized) followed by a two-layer MLP projector into the language embedding space. The output is a short natural-language tactile description (2–3 single-word descriptors in comma-separated form), generated autoregressively by the LLM backbone.

## Method

SPLASH partitions the LLM's linear-layer parameters into a frozen critical subspace and a trainable dormant subspace using a Wanda-inspired visual-relative importance score (|W_{i,j}| · ‖x_i‖₂) computed on 128 CC3M calibration samples, with a 60% sparsity ratio and the first/last transformer blocks always frozen. During a single unified training stage, the tactile front-end is fully trainable while LLM gradients are masked via Hadamard product so that only dormant parameters receive updates, keeping critical VL weights as stable structural anchors. This strict structural isolation (as opposed to additive PEFT like LoRA's θ+Δθ) confines all tactile adaptation to underutilized weights, preventing catastrophic forgetting with zero additional inference overhead. The approach is validated on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL2.5-1B, trained on SSVTP (4.5K pairs) and TVL (44K pairs) with AdamW, cosine LR decay, and a 2×10⁻⁵ initial learning rate.

## Results

On the three VTL benchmarks (SSVTP, TVL, TacQuad), SPLASH-3B (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) achieves an average LLM-judge score of 4.91, outperforming the strongest same-backbone baseline TVL+LoRA (4.50) and the 7B-scale UniTouch (3.74). SPLASH-1B (InternVL2.5-1B) reaches an even higher average of 5.01, with the best single-benchmark score of 5.69 on SSVTP. On VL preservation benchmarks, SPLASH-3B improves MMMU-val from 50.0 to 55.3 and MathVista from 52.8 to 65.3 over TVL+LoRA, while remaining close to the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (53.1 / 62.3), confirming that tactile adaptation does not degrade pretrained visual reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TVL+LoRA on the same Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone, SPLASH-3B wins on all three VTL benchmarks (SSVTP 5.48 vs 4.98, TVL 4.39 vs 4.29, TacQuad 4.86 vs 4.22; avg 4.91 vs 4.50) and also preserves VL capability better (MMMU 55.3 vs 50.0, MathVista 65.3 vs 52.8). Against TVL-(IA)³, SPLASH-3B achieves a large margin on TacQuad (4.86 vs 3.84), where (IA)³'s minimal weight perturbation causes severe under-fitting of the tactile modality; however, (IA)³ retains slightly higher scores on MME (2216 vs 2155) and MMBench (78.4/78.9 vs 78.0/76.9), reflecting its more conservative update strategy. Against UniTouch (LLaMA1-7B), SPLASH-3B surpasses the larger 7B model on every VTL benchmark (avg 4.91 vs 3.74) and on all VL benchmarks (e.g., MMMU 55.3 vs 26.6, MathVista 65.3 vs 9.0), demonstrating that mask-isolated adaptation in a modern compact VLM outperforms LoRA-based adaptation in a language-only 7B backbone. Overall, SPLASH uniquely balances tactile acquisition and VL preservation, whereas (IA)³ trades tactile generalization for VL retention and TVL+LoRA degrades both.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPLASH extends the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis and Wanda-style importance scoring from post-hoc pruning to a training-time mechanism: rather than removing unimportant weights, it repurposes them as trainable capacity for a new modality while freezing the critical subset. This departs from the dominant PEFT paradigm (LoRA, IA3) used in prior VTL alignment work (TVL, UniTouch), which adds low-rank or scaling parameters on top of a frozen backbone; SPLASH instead performs selective in-place updates within the original parameter space, requiring no external modules. The paper also generalizes pruning-based multi-task learning, which allocates disjoint parameter subsets to different tasks, to the modality-level setting where the 'new task' is an entirely new sensory channel with a large representational gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical recipe for expanding the sensory repertoire of compact MLLMs (≤3B) without the catastrophic forgetting that plagues naive fine-tuning, which is directly relevant to resource-constrained edge robotics where 7B+ backbones are infeasible. Key open questions include whether the static dormant mask remains valid under dynamic interaction states (the authors acknowledge this limitation), whether the approach generalizes across heterogeneous tactile sensors beyond the single DIGIT setup tested, and whether the mask-isolation principle transfers to other new-modality integration problems (e.g., proprioception, audio). The finding that even unpaired noise calibration yields competitive results suggests the method captures intrinsic architectural redundancy, opening the door to calibration-free deployment.
