## Problem

Existing one-shot structured pruning methods for visual foundation models suffer from two key limitations: (1) rigid point-to-point token alignment between native and pruned models encourages memorization of specific numerical features rather than preserving the global semantic topology, leading to robustness degradation; and (2) a single task-agnostic subnetwork optimized on a generic dataset (e.g., ImageNet) is deployed across all downstream tasks, ignoring that different tasks require different information granularities and data distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained DINOv3 (ViT-B/16) encoder and N=1000 unlabeled calibration images from the target downstream dataset. It produces a binary weight mask yielding pruned subnetworks at user-specified sparsity levels (10%, 20%, 30%) in approximately 61 seconds on a single A100 GPU with 10.7 GB memory. Crucially, the pruning is performed separately per task on that task's own dataset, so each downstream application receives a tailored subnetwork rather than a shared universal one.

## Method

Cut-ViT replaces rigid token-level MSE alignment with a gram anchoring subspace consistency framework. It constructs spatial (F·F^T) and channel (F^T·F) gram matrices for both the native and pruned models, applies truncated SVD (K=192 components) to extract orthonormal basis vectors, and enforces alignment via a basis-agnostic loss (1 − ‖U_p^T U_t‖_F²) that is provably invariant to arbitrary orthogonal rotations of the SVD bases, plus a residual loss that minimizes the energy of pruned features orthogonal to the teacher subspace. To adapt to task-specific information density, spectral entropy of the normalized singular value distributions along spatial and channel axes dynamically weights the two basis consistency losses—classification tasks (low spatial entropy) emphasize channel alignment, while dense prediction tasks (high spatial entropy) emphasize spatial alignment. The entire pipeline is training-free: a single forward pass through the pruned model, backpropagation to compute per-parameter gradients, and ranking by gradient magnitude to select the subnetwork.

## Results

Cut-ViT achieves SOTA training-free pruning performance across six tasks at 10–30% sparsity. At 30% sparsity, it improves (J&F)m by 3.4% on DAVIS-2017 video object segmentation, PCK@0.05 by 5.1% on FG3DCar semantic matching, mAP by 2.8% on COCO object detection, mIoU by 1.2% on ADE20K segmentation, and ARel by 3.1 (lower is better) on NYUv2 depth estimation over the strongest prior training-free method (SnapViT). In terms of efficiency, Cut-ViT requires only 61 seconds and 10.7 GB GPU memory on a single A100, compared to 292 s / 23.5 GB for SnapViT and 13,920 s / 37.9 GB for the training-based EA-ViT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free baselines, Cut-ViT consistently outperforms all five competitors (LAMP, NVIT, SNIP, SNOWS, SnapViT) across every task and sparsity level, with the largest margins over the older methods (LAMP, NVIT, SNIP) and the smallest but still clear margins over the two most recent methods (SNOWS, SnapViT). Against SnapViT [50], the closest training-free competitor, Cut-ViT gains +2.8 mAP on COCO, +3.4 (J&F)m on DAVIS-2017, +5.1 PCK@0.05 on FG3DCar, +1.2 mIoU on ADE20K, and −3.1 ARel on NYUv2 at 30% sparsity. Against training-based HydraViT [16], Cut-ViT surpasses it on all six tasks at 30% sparsity (e.g., 48.6 vs. 47.6 mAP on COCO, 59.4 vs. 58.6 (J&F)m on DAVIS). Against training-based EA-ViT [77], Cut-ViT trails by a small margin on some tasks (e.g., −0.4 mAP on COCO, −0.3 (J&F)m on DAVIS at 30%) but does so while using only 0.44% of EA-ViT's pruning time and 28.2% of its GPU memory. The paper further shows that even when SNOWS and SnapViT are re-run on the target dataset rather than ImageNet, their gains are marginal (≤0.5%), confirming that Cut-ViT's advantage stems from the subspace-consistency mechanism rather than dataset choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot structured pruning (OSP) line for ViTs, building directly on SNOWS [35] and SnapViT [50], which both rely on rigid point-to-point token alignment between teacher and student features. The key conceptual departure is replacing element-wise feature matching with a subspace-level consistency objective: gram anchoring matrices (spatial and channel) are decomposed via SVD, and the pruned model is constrained to align with the teacher's dominant subspace rather than its exact token values. A further departure from prior SVD-based compression work [8, 56] is the introduction of a rotation-invariant (basis-agnostic) loss that makes the alignment well-posed despite the non-uniqueness of singular vectors, combined with a spectral entropy mechanism that dynamically reweights spatial vs. channel constraints per downstream task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Cut-ViT prunes DINOv3 in ~61 seconds on a single A100 (vs. 292 s for SnapViT and 13,920 s for EA-ViT) while matching or exceeding the best training-free baselines across six tasks and nine datasets, making task-specific subnetwork generation feasible in edge-deployment pipelines where retraining is prohibitive. The shift from a single universal subnetwork to per-task pruning on the target dataset (without labels) addresses a real deployment concern, though it multiplies the number of subnetworks one must maintain. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger ViT variants (tested only on ViT-B/16), whether the spectral entropy weighting generalizes beyond the six evaluated tasks, and whether the modest gap to training-based methods (e.g., EA-ViT) can be closed without sacrificing the training-free property.
