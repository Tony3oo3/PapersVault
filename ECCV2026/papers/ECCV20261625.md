## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods produce robust reconstructions in a single pass but consistently lag behind dense-view optimization in recovering fine-grained texture and appearance, yielding blurry text, smeared logos, and distorted facial features. The paper investigates whether substantially expanding the transformer context window—by increasing active object and image tokens—can close this fidelity gap, and how to do so efficiently given the quadratic (or cubic) memory growth that naive resolution scaling incurs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 12–16 posed RGB images at 768×768 resolution with explicit camera parameters encoded as Plücker rays; images are split into 8×8 patches and fused with frozen DINOv3 features. The output is an explicit textured mesh plus a sparse 384³ feature volume (combined with a 64³ dense volume during ray marching via VolSDF), from which novel views, SDF, and BRDF maps (albedo, roughness, metallic) are decoded by lightweight MLPs. Inference on a single H100 takes ~5 seconds with 40 GB peak memory at the highest resolution.

## Method

LSRM adapts Native Sparse Attention (NSA) to 3D reconstruction through a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline: a dense 24-block transformer (16³ volume, 32² image tokens) provides geometric initialization, and a sparse transformer (96³ volume, 96² image tokens) predicts high-resolution residuals over the dense prediction, scaling to 20× more object tokens and >2× more image tokens than prior SOTA. Two key architectural adaptations maximize fidelity and hardware efficiency: (1) a 3D-aware spatial routing mechanism that selects attention KV-blocks using explicit geometric distances from Stage 1 geometry and known camera parameters rather than data-driven attention scores, which dramatically improves early-layer 2D–3D correspondence and texture sharpness; and (2) a custom block-aware sequence-parallelism scheme that shards tokens by spatial block to keep each block on a single GPU, combined with an All-gather-KV protocol exploiting GQA's high KV-head compression (32 query heads, 2 KV heads) to broadcast a lightweight global KV cache with minimal communication. The sparse transformer is initialized from the dense model's weights and uses gated cross-attention branches with independent block-selection counts for image and volume tokens, trained on 600K curated 3D models across three progressive resolution stages on 128 H100 GPUs.

## Results

On the GSO novel-view synthesis benchmark, LSRM (full model, S^img=S^vol=96 with 3D-aware routing) achieves 33.08 dB PSNR, 0.971 SSIM, and 0.028 LPIPS, improving over the strongest feed-forward baseline LIRM by >2.4 dB PSNR and >40% LPIPS reduction. On inverse rendering, LSRM attains the lowest LPIPS (0.022) and Chamfer Distance (0.29) on StanfordORB, matching the SOTA optimization-based method NeuralPBIR on LPIPS (0.023) while substantially outperforming it on geometry (CD 0.29 vs. 0.43). With only 8 input views, LSRM still achieves 32.46 dB PSNR, exceeding LIRM's 16-view result (30.56 dB) by nearly 2 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSRM substantially outperforms all three feed-forward NVS baselines on GSO: over Mesh-LRM [58] by +4.95 dB PSNR and −70% LPIPS, over GS-LRM [73] by +2.56 dB PSNR and −44% LPIPS, and over LIRM [38] by +2.43 dB PSNR and −48% LPIPS. In inverse rendering on StanfordORB, LSRM achieves the best LPIPS (0.022) and Chamfer Distance (0.29) among all methods, beating the optimization-based NVDiffrecMc [48] by 39% in LPIPS and 43% in CD, and matching NeuralPBIR [52] on LPIPS (0.022 vs. 0.023) while halving its CD (0.29 vs. 0.43), though NeuralPBIR retains a slight PSNR-H edge (26.01 vs. 25.47). Against the feed-forward LIRM [38] on inverse rendering, LSRM improves LPIPS from 0.025 to 0.022 and CD from 0.38 to 0.29 on StanfordORB. With only 6 input views, LSRM also outperforms RelitLRM [78] across all StanfordORB metrics (PSNR-H 25.57 vs. 24.67, LPIPS 0.023 vs. 0.032) and DTC/OWL benchmarks, despite RelitLRM being a dedicated generative relighting model.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSRM extends the object-centric feed-forward reconstruction line (LRM, MeshLRM, GS-LRM, LIRM) by identifying context-window size as the key bottleneck for texture fidelity and addressing it through Native Sparse Attention (NSA). It departs from prior attempts that closed the gap via alternative 3D representations or post-hoc refinement by instead scaling active object and image tokens by 20× and 2×, respectively, supported by a coarse-to-fine pipeline that predicts sparse high-resolution residuals over a dense initialization. The 3D-aware spatial routing and block-aware sequence-parallel training are novel adaptations of NSA specifically for posed multi-view 3D reconstruction, distinguishing LSRM from generative sparse-voxel methods like Direct3D-S2 that target single-view synthesis rather than deterministic reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching or exceeding dense-view optimization methods on perceptual metrics (LPIPS) for inverse rendering while running in ~5 seconds per object, LSRM makes high-fidelity feed-forward 3D reconstruction practically viable for industrial digital-twin pipelines. The 3D-aware routing and All-gather-KV sequence-parallelism techniques are reusable components for any sparse-attention 3D model. Open questions remain: the method still struggles with ill-posed material estimation (roughness, metallic) and extremely fine text, and the authors note that further scaling via Ulysses or ring attention could push fidelity higher. Extending the coarse-to-fine NSA framework to large-scale scene reconstruction and video generation is identified as a natural next step.
