## Problem

Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models for multi-arm robotics treat language as a single global instruction mapped directly to control, lacking an explicit mechanism for decomposing tasks and assigning sub-goals to individual arms. This design causes collaboration to be brittle and over-specialized to the specific coordination patterns seen during training, with no principled route to recombining learned skills into unseen multi-arm coordination structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261062/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a high-level natural-language instruction, multi-view RGB observations (global and per-arm wrist cameras), and concatenated proprioceptive states (joint angles, gripper states) for all N arms. A VLM planner (GPT-4.1) first decomposes the instruction into a temporally ordered sequence of per-arm atomic prompts drawn from a finite curated template set, which are then concatenated into a unified instruction string. The VLA executor consumes this unified prompt alongside visual and proprioceptive inputs to produce a single continuous multi-arm action vector via flow-matching, with a multi-head projection layer separating the shared latent into arm-specific action heads.

## Method

MA-VLA is a two-stage architecture: a VLM-based planner decomposes a high-level instruction into stage-wise per-arm atomic prompts, and a Pi0-based flow-matching executor grounds these prompts into smooth continuous actions for all arms in a single forward pass. The key training-time innovation is Arm Shuffle, which with probability p_shuffle randomly permutes the correspondence between each arm's state, wrist view, atomic prompt, and action, forcing the model to interpret instructions semantically rather than binding behaviors to fixed arm indices or spatial positions. A complementary View Dropout randomly masks camera views to encourage redundant multi-view reasoning. Both augmentations are applied under a standard behavioral cloning objective without modifying the loss, and the unified single-model design (as opposed to per-arm independent policies) preserves inter-arm coordination while scaling to arbitrary numbers of arms.

## Results

On RoboFactory (in-domain), MA-VLA achieves 83.5% average success on two-arm tasks and 83.3% on three/four-arm tasks, outperforming the Pi0 backbone (80.3% and 76.5% respectively) and diffusion-based baselines (DP: 27.5% and 15.3%). On RoboTwin 2.0 (Hard), MA-VLA reaches 49.0% average success, a 7.9-point gain over Pi0 (41.1%) and 20.4 points over DP3 (28.6%). Most strikingly, on out-of-domain compositional generalization tasks where all baselines score 0%, MA-VLA achieves 13.0% average success, and on real-world SO101 it obtains non-zero success (e.g., 10/20 on Stack Bowls OOD) where Pi0 fails completely (0/20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MA-VLA consistently outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest baseline Pi0 [3], MA-VLA gains 3.2 points on RoboFactory two-arm (83.5% vs 80.3%), 6.8 points on three/four-arm (83.3% vs 76.5%), and 7.9 points on RoboTwin 2.0 (49.0% vs 41.1%), with the largest per-task margin on RoboTwin Handover (46% vs 27%). Pi0-FAST [51] is substantially weaker, trailing MA-VLA by 26 points on RoboFactory two-arm and 22 points on RoboTwin 2.0. Diffusion Policy [10] and DP3 [73] are the weakest baselines, scoring 27.5% and 15.3% on RoboFactory respectively, and 18.9% and 28.6% on RoboTwin 2.0. ACT [77] is only evaluated on RoboTwin 2.0 and achieves just 12.3% average. The most dramatic separation appears in out-of-domain compositional generalization (Table 3) and real-world OOD (Table 4), where all five baselines score 0% while MA-VLA achieves 13.0% and non-zero real-robot success, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

MA-VLA extends the Pi0 flow-matching VLA backbone by inserting a VLM-based planner (GPT-4.1) that decomposes a single high-level instruction into per-arm atomic action prompts, making division of labor explicit rather than implicit in the learned policy. This departs from prior multi-arm VLA systems (e.g., GR-3, Gemini Robotics) that treat language as a single global command mapped directly to joint control, and from prior multi-arm imitation work (Diffusion Policy, ACT) that lacks any language interface. The Arm Shuffle training-time permutation is a novel regularization that has no direct analogue in prior single-arm or multi-arm policy learning, drawing instead on the broader idea of data augmentation to break spurious identity correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evaluation of multi-arm compositional generalization—recombining known atomic actions into unseen coordination patterns—and shows that a simple training-time permutation (Arm Shuffle) is sufficient to move from 0% to non-zero success where all end-to-end baselines completely fail. However, absolute OOD success rates remain modest (13% simulation average; 2–10/20 real-world episodes), and the planner's reliance on a closed-source LLM (GPT-4.1) raises questions about deployment cost and latency. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 4 arms, whether a learned (rather than prompted) planner can replace the external VLM, and whether the finite atomic-action vocabulary can be expanded to cover genuinely novel sub-skills rather than only recombination.
