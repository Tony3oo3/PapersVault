## Problem

General-purpose MLLMs applied to gastrointestinal endoscopy suffer from two critical failures: their reasoning diverges from the standardized hierarchical cognitive pathway that expert endoscopists follow (skipping anatomical localization, morphological evaluation, or micro-detail analysis), and they ground diagnoses in spurious background correlations rather than causal lesion features, making them brittle under environmental artifacts like bubbles or instrument shadows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261472/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB endoscopic image and a free-form diagnostic instruction as input, and produces a structured response that sequentially concatenates a three-stage clinical reasoning chain (Location & Imaging Environment → Mucosal Morphology & Focal Lesions → Surface Texture & Microvascular Architecture) with a final diagnostic label. For multi-label cases (e.g., The SEE-AI Project's 235 co-occurring pathology combinations), the output must enumerate all concurrent pathologies, and accuracy is scored under a strict exact-match criterion on the full label set.

## Method

CogAlign is a two-stage training pipeline built on Qwen3-VL with LoRA (rank 16, α=32) and a frozen vision encoder. Stage 1 performs SFT on a newly curated 24,515-sample hierarchical clinical cognition dataset (assembled from five public endoscopy repositories and annotated via a Gemini-3-Pro teacher model with human expert verification) to internalize the expert diagnostic trajectory from anatomical localization through micro-detail analysis. Stage 2 applies counterfactual-driven GRPO: lesion regions are identified via expert-refined bounding boxes and obliterated with high-intensity Gaussian smoothing to synthesize counterfactual normal samples, and the policy is optimized against three additive rewards (format compliance, keyword-based clinical cognition, and diagnostic consistency) with a KL penalty against the SFT reference. A theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–2) formalizes why standard SFT converges to spurious background shortcuts under simplicity bias and proves that the counterfactual penalty drives sensitivity to non-causal factors to zero as the penalty weight grows unbounded.

## Results

CogAlign-8B achieves 67.67% average accuracy across five gastrointestinal endoscopy benchmarks (CrohnIPI, GastroVision, HyperKvasir, Kvasir-Capsule, SEE-AI Project), surpassing the strongest general MLLM baseline Qwen3-VL-Plus (41.16%) by 26.5 percentage points. On the most challenging multi-label benchmark (SEE-AI Project), CogAlign-8B reaches 60.18% versus 33.63% for Qwen3-VL-Plus. The SFT-only variant (Qwen3-VL-8B SFT) already reaches 66.31%, and the full CogAlign pipeline adds a further 1.36% gain, with the largest improvement on multi-label cases (13.62% vs. 10.64%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gemini 3 Pro (24.82% avg), CogAlign-8B (67.67%) wins by 42.9 points on every individual benchmark, with the largest gap on Kvasir-Capsule (74.01% vs. 21.83%). Against the Qwen3-VL family, CogAlign-8B outperforms Qwen3-VL-Plus (41.16%) by 26.5 points and the same-size Qwen3-VL-8B (35.30%) by 32.4 points; notably, the SFT-only variant already narrows the gap to Qwen3-VL-Plus to just 24.8 points, showing that the hierarchical dataset is the dominant contributor. Against the medical-specific Hulu-Med-7B (8.58%), CogAlign-8B achieves a 59.1-point margin, indicating that general medical VLMs trained on broad radiology tasks are poorly suited to fine-grained endoscopic diagnosis. The counterfactual GRPO stage contributes a modest but consistent 1.36-point average gain over SFT alone, with the largest relative improvement on multi-label cases (13.62% vs. 10.64%), confirming its role in enforcing causal grounding over background shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical MLLM line (LLaVA-Med, Hulu-Med, Med-Flamingo) by addressing two specific failure modes in GI endoscopy: unstructured reasoning and spurious background reliance. It builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as the RL backbone but repurposes it with a counterfactual intervention—lesion masking to synthesize 'normal' counterfactuals—that has no direct precedent in medical VLM training. The theoretical shortcut-convergence analysis (Theorems 1–2) connects to the broader simplicity-bias literature [33] but instantiates it specifically for the medical image domain, providing a principled justification for why SFT alone is insufficient. The hierarchical clinical cognition dataset departs from existing GI benchmarks (HyperKvasir, GastroVision, etc.), which are image-label pairs, by adding expert-verified multi-step reasoning chains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a reusable recipe—hierarchical SFT followed by counterfactual-driven RL—that could transfer to other endoscopic or medical imaging tasks where structured reasoning and causal grounding are needed. However, the RL stage's marginal accuracy gain over SFT alone (~1.4% average) suggests the primary benefit is robustness to visual noise and multi-label recall rather than raw diagnostic accuracy, which remains modest (13.6% on multi-label). Open questions include whether the counterfactual masking strategy generalizes to lesions without clear boundaries, whether the theoretical shortcut-convergence result holds under the specific LoRA + frozen-encoder setup used here, and whether the approach scales to video endoscopy or other organ systems.
