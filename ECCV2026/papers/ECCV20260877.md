## Problem

State-of-the-art monocular metric depth estimation models, trained predominantly on ground-level street-view and indoor datasets, exhibit a severe domain gap when deployed on aerial UAV imagery, producing near-zero threshold accuracy (δ₁ ≈ 0%) on oblique and nadir views. This failure is compounded by the absence of a large-scale, variable-controlled aerial dataset with reliable metric depth ground truth and a systematic benchmark for evaluating how viewpoint, altitude, and camera parameters affect depth prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (resized to 1K resolution and cropped to dimensions divisible by 14) and outputs a per-pixel metric depth map in absolute meters, clipped to 0.001–400 m for evaluation. The AerialMetric dataset provides training pairs at resolutions up to 8192×5460 (real) and 3840×2160 (synthetic), while the Decoupled subset captures images at two altitudes (80 m, 120 m), four pitch angles (−90° to −45°), and two FOVs (63°, 83°) to enable factorized robustness analysis.

## Method

The paper introduces AerialMetric, a four-subset benchmark (52K real + 16K synthetic image-depth pairs) constructed via photogrammetry (DJI Terra mesh rasterization), controlled RTK-UAV acquisition, Unreal Engine/AirSim rendering of Google Earth 3D Tiles, and a human-in-the-loop scale calibration pipeline for in-the-wild videos. For model adaptation, the authors fine-tune MoGe2 using LoRA (rank 96) with adapters injected into all linear layers (qkv, proj, fc1, fc2), trained with AdamW (lr 1×10⁻⁶ encoder, 5×10⁻⁵ decoder, polynomial decay) over 1,800 steps at batch size 32. Training data mixes AerialMetric-Oblique, AerialMetric-Synthetic, and ground-domain sources (Hypersim, MVS-Synth, TartanAir) at an 80/15/5 sampling ratio to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Ablations show LoRA (r=96) outperforms full fine-tuning on ground-domain generalization while matching aerial accuracy, and that including even 5% ground data limits ETH3D δ₁ degradation to 5.1 points versus 17+ points without it.

## Results

On AerialMetric-Oblique (without GT intrinsics), MoGe2-Aerial achieves δ₁ of 89.3% on City, 81.2% on Rural, and 73.6% on Natural, compared to 5.1%, 0.3%, and 23.0% for zero-shot MoGe2 respectively. On AerialMetric-Decoupled, MoGe2-Aerial maintains δ₁ above 87% across Building, Factory, and Lawn scenes, while zero-shot baselines drop to 0–61%. On the in-the-wild AerialMetric-Wild set, fine-tuning reduces AbsRel from 55.26 to 21.34 and improves δ₁ from 9.5 to 53.7. On ground-domain benchmarks, MoGe2-Aerial preserves competitive performance (e.g., NYUv2 δ₁ 96.2% vs. 96.7% for MoGe2; KITTI δ₁ 68.7% vs. 64.7%), confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven zero-shot metric depth methods against the proposed MoGe2-Aerial (MoGe2 fine-tuned with LoRA on AerialMetric). ZoeDepth, DepthPro, UniDepthV1, Depth Anything 3, and Metric3Dv2 all produce near-zero δ₁ (≤0.1%) across every aerial scene, indicating complete failure to transfer from ground-level pretraining to aerial viewpoints. UniDepthV2 is the strongest zero-shot baseline, reaching δ₁ of 73.0% on Oblique-Natural and 57.9% on Decoupled-Farm, yet still trails MoGe2-Aerial by 15–56 percentage points on most scenes. MoGe2 zero-shot achieves moderate δ₁ (0–61%) but is dramatically outperformed by its fine-tuned variant MoGe2-Aerial, which gains 84+ points on City (5.1→89.3) and 75+ points on Lawn (19.5→94.0). The one exception is the Decoupled-Farm scene, where UniDepthV2 (57.9%) slightly edges out MoGe2-Aerial (54.2%), likely due to agricultural textures in UniDepthV2's pretraining corpus. On ground-domain benchmarks, MoGe2-Aerial matches or slightly exceeds zero-shot MoGe2 (e.g., KITTI δ₁ 68.7 vs. 64.7; DIODE δ₁ 77.0 vs. 67.9), confirming that aerial adaptation does not sacrifice terrestrial generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the monocular metric depth estimation lineage (ZoeDepth, Metric3Dv2, MoGe2, UniDepth, Depth Anything) from ground-level to aerial UAV viewpoints, where prior models were never trained or evaluated. It aggregates and standardizes six existing photogrammetry datasets (UrbanBIS, GauU-SceneV2, UAVScenes, UrbanScene3D, ODM, ESRI) into a unified aerial depth benchmark, and introduces a variable-decoupled acquisition protocol (pitch, altitude, FOV) that no prior UAV dataset provides. The adaptation strategy repurposes LoRA fine-tuning—originally developed for LLMs—on the MoGe2 vision-transformer backbone, combined with a human-in-the-loop scale calibration pipeline for in-the-wild internet drone videos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a severe and previously unquantified domain gap: SOTA metric depth models (ZoeDepth, DepthPro, Metric3Dv2) produce near-zero accuracy on aerial imagery, making them effectively unusable for UAV 3D perception tasks. The variable-decoupled design enables systematic attribution of performance degradation to specific camera parameters, which is critical for UAV system design. Limitations include persistent difficulty on repetitive-texture scenes (Farm), pseudo-metric ground truth for the Wild subset, and no coverage of adverse weather conditions. The authors identify multi-view extension and extreme-weather robustness as key open directions.
