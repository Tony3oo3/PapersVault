## Problem

Physical property inference methods (density, material, center-of-mass estimation) lack real-world volumetric ground truth for rigorous evaluation, as existing datasets are built from synthetic 3D assets with assumed rather than measured physical properties. This gap prevents quantitative benchmarking of density estimation and limits the development of physically grounded embodied AI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260815/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The reconstruction pipeline takes a part-segmented watertight 3D mesh (with K disjoint homogeneous-material regions) and paired biplanar X-ray images (captured at 100 kV, 450 mA, 280 ms exposure, 126 mAs) as input, and produces a piecewise-constant volumetric density field where each part region has a single linear attenuation coefficient converted to density via a fixed MAC of ~0.17 cm²/g at 100 keV. The X-Field segmentation model additionally consumes a point cloud and biplanar X-ray images, outputting a dense per-voxel density field that is clustered into material-aware volumetric segments. The dataset provides 1,000 objects across 137 categories with meshes, X-ray scans, and reconstructed density fields per object.

## Method

The core reconstruction framework models the attenuation field as piecewise-constant over manually annotated part regions, reducing the ill-posed 3D inverse problem to a low-dimensional optimization over K per-part LAC values. Path lengths through each region are precomputed geometrically from the known segmentation and biplanar projection geometry, and a differentiable forward-projection operator (based on the Beer-Lambert law) renders synthetic X-ray intensities that are matched to real measurements via L2 loss, optimized with Adam. For the X-Field segmentation application, the paper extends PartField's triplane representation by encoding biplanar X-ray images with an image encoder, applying FiLM modulation for scale awareness, and fusing the resulting features into the triplane via cross-attention; a density MLP head regresses per-query-point densities supervised by an ℓ₂ loss, with an auxiliary X-ray reconstruction loss to mitigate missing-view ambiguity.

## Results

On the XDen-1K physical reasoning benchmark (Table 4), NeRF2Physics achieves the best density MAE (1.157) and CoM distance (0.523 cm), while Pixie obtains the lowest mass MAPE (0.713). For X-ray-conditioned volumetric segmentation (Table 5), X-Field improves IoU from 53.19 to 72.65 on synthetic data and from 38.41 to 49.25 on real data over PartField. In CoM-aware manipulation (Table 6), using the reconstructed density-field CoM with AnyGrasp raises grasp success from 27.50% (vanilla) to 77.75%, while reducing wrist (Joint-7) torque from 0.521 to 0.435 N·m.

| GaussianProperty | 1.295 | 0.632 | 1.247 |
| Pixie | 1.228 | 0.547 | **0.713** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three physical-reasoning methods on XDen-1K: NeRF2Physics (LLM-based) leads on density MAE (1.157) and CoM distance (0.523 cm), while Pixie (learning-based) achieves the best mass MAPE (0.713), and GaussianProperty trails on all three metrics. For volumetric segmentation, X-Field (the paper's X-ray-conditioned extension of PartField) outperforms PartField by ~19.5 IoU points on synthetic data (72.65 vs. 53.19) and ~10.8 points on real data (49.25 vs. 38.41), demonstrating the value of X-ray conditioning. In the manipulation experiment, the reconstructed density-field CoM guides AnyGrasp to a 77.75% success rate, substantially exceeding both vanilla AnyGrasp (27.50%) and the NeRF2Physics-CoM-guided variant (55.00%), while also yielding the lowest wrist torque (0.435 N·m vs. 0.521 and 0.443 N·m respectively). Overall, the paper's contributions win or tie on the primary metrics in each of the three application settings, with the largest gains appearing in the manipulation task where accurate CoM estimation is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PartField's triplane-based volumetric feature representation by adding biplanar X-ray conditioning via FiLM and cross-attention, creating X-Field for material-aware segmentation. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant CT-based density reconstruction paradigm (which requires dozens to hundreds of projections) by formulating density recovery as a low-dimensional optimization over per-part linear attenuation coefficients constrained by the Beer-Lambert law and only two orthogonal X-ray views. It also diverges from generative or regression-based biplanar-to-CT methods (X2CT-GAN, perspective projection networks) that require large paired CT–X-ray training sets, instead leveraging explicit geometric part priors and physics-based forward projection to avoid learned surrogates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

XDen-1K fills a critical gap: prior physical-reasoning methods (Pixie, GaussianProperty, NeRF2Physics) were evaluated only on synthetic or assumed-material assets, leaving their real-world accuracy unverifiable. By providing 1,000 objects with measured X-ray scans and CT-validated density fields, the dataset enables the first quantitative benchmarking of density estimation against physical ground truth. Key limitations include the reliance on manual part annotation (which caps scalability) and the fact that X-Field's predicted priors still yield higher reprojection error than human labels, so full automation of the pipeline remains open. The 3× grasp-success improvement from CoM guidance (27.5%→77.75%) on a single Franka arm is promising but needs validation across object categories, gripper types, and dynamic scenarios before it can be considered a general principle.
