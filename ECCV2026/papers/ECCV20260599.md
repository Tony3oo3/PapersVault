## Problem

Existing text-to-video generation methods encode camera trajectories using relative or ambiguous representations (e.g., Plücker rays relative to the first frame), making it impossible to specify absolute camera orientation with respect to gravity. This limits precise geometric control and causes models to fail on extreme camera rotations such as top-down shots, barrel rolls, or full 360° pans, which lie outside the training distribution of conventional forward-facing video data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and an absolute camera trajectory (pitch, roll, yaw, and translation per frame) expressed in a gravity-aligned global coordinate system, where pitch and roll are well-defined but yaw is unconstrained. Training uses 49-frame sequences at 720×480 resolution sampled on-the-fly from 360° panoramic videos, with camera conditioning injected via either UCPE self-attention layers or per-pixel Plücker ray maps. A key detail is that captions are generated from a separate null-pitch (forward-facing, 0° pitch/roll) crop of the same panorama, not from the actual rotated viewpoint used for the diffusion loss.

## Method

GimbalDiffusion grounds camera poses in a gravity-aligned absolute coordinate system by geometrically calibrating 360° panoramic videos from PanoVid: SfM estimates relative poses, Perspective Fields provides the gravity up-vector at frame 0, and a rotation aligns all frames to a shared world frame with null yaw. Random camera rotation paths are then sampled across the full sphere (uniform pitch/roll/yaw initialization plus 0–3 additional random-axis rotations with cubic-spline interpolation), producing exhaustive coverage of extreme viewpoints absent from natural video. The core training innovation is null-pitch conditioning: captions are generated from upright forward-facing crops rather than the actual rotated view, preventing the model from entangling prompt semantics with camera angle (e.g., generating grass when the camera should face the sky). The approach is architecture-agnostic, applied as full fine-tuning on WAN 2.1 1.3B, WAN 2.2 5B, and CogVideoX-2B backbones with either UCPE or Plücker ray camera encoders, and is complemented by a 'look-down' negative prompt strategy to suppress artifacts like tripods visible in the nadir of real panoramas.

## Results

On the SpatialVID-extreme benchmark, GimbalDiffusion (WAN 2.2 5B + Plücker) achieves the best absolute camera orientation metrics: PitchErr of 8.36° and GravityErr of 10.54°, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: UCPE at 16.25°/18.88°). On relative rotation error (RotErr), the WAN 2.1 1.3B + UCPE variant achieves the best score of 12.12°, while the Plücker variants range from 15.05° to 25.84°. Visual quality (FID/FVD) is competitive but slightly behind PreciseCam-based baselines, which trade orientation accuracy for better FVD (776.3 vs. 1041.6). Ablating null-pitch conditioning increases PitchErr by 35–72%, and removing absolute rotations increases it by 3.3–3.6×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GimbalDiffusion decisively outperforms AC3D on absolute orientation: PitchErr drops from 39.09° to 21.19° and GravityErr from 44.13° to 24.84° on the CogVideoX-2B backbone, with RotErr also improving (29.27° → 18.48°). Against PreciseCam+WAN-I2V-CC, GimbalDiffusion (WAN 2.1 1.3B) cuts PitchErr from 29.16° to 10.50° and GravityErr from 33.48° to 13.05°, but at the cost of higher FVD (1041.6 vs. 776.3) and slightly worse FID, since PreciseCam's image-first pipeline produces sharper initial frames. Compared to the concurrent UCPE method on the same WAN 2.1 1.3B backbone, GimbalDiffusion improves PitchErr (13.98° vs. 16.25°) and GravityErr (17.16° vs. 18.88°) while also achieving lower RotErr (12.12° vs. 18.38°), though UCPE has a marginal edge on TransErr (0.44 vs. 0.46) and FVD (957.9 vs. 978.1). The best absolute-orientation configuration (WAN 2.2 5B + Plücker) achieves 8.36° PitchErr and 10.54° GravityErr, roughly 2× better than UCPE. PreciseCam+GEN3C, while competitive on RotErr (10.99°), cannot generate dynamic content and is included for reference only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the camera-controlled video generation line (AC3D, CameraCtrl, UCPE) by replacing relative-to-first-frame camera encodings with a gravity-aligned absolute coordinate system, resolving the rotational ambiguity that limits prior methods to small, forward-biased rotations. It builds on UCPE's gravity-aligned framing and AC3D's Plücker-ray/ControlNet architecture, but departs fundamentally in data generation: instead of relying on natural videos (which are heavily biased toward null pitch/roll) or complex multi-dataset matching (as UCPE requires), it samples arbitrary camera trajectories from 360° panoramic videos to cover the full sphere of viewpoints. The null-pitch conditioning strategy is a novel data-augmentation insight not present in prior camera-control work, addressing the text–camera entanglement problem that causes models to ignore extreme-angle conditioning when prompt semantics conflict.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes extreme camera angles (barrel rolls, top-down, 180° turnarounds) practically achievable in text-to-video generation, opening creative workflows that were previously out-of-distribution for all existing models. The SpatialVID-extreme benchmark and the prompt–camera entanglement evaluation fill a gap in how the field measures camera fidelity, and the panoramic-video data pipeline is a reusable recipe for any camera-conditioned video model. Key limitations remain: on-the-fly sampling covers rotations only (translations come from source videos), and visual artifacts persist at extreme angles. Extending the augmentation to translations (e.g., via 3DGS) and scaling to larger backbones are natural next steps.
