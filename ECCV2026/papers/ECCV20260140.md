## Problem

Modern 3D object reconstruction captures geometry and appearance but ignores the acoustic properties revealed through physical interaction, such as impact sounds that encode material, stiffness, and structural information. Existing impact sound modeling approaches either rely on computationally expensive finite-element physics simulation or require large-scale training data to generalize, leaving no efficient path to reconstruct an object's full spatial sound field from just a handful of recordings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260140/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes calibrated multi-view RGB images of a single object plus a small set of impact sound recordings (typically 20% of available contacts, selected via farthest-point sampling), each paired with a 3D contact location on the object surface. It produces a parametric modal sound field — global vibration frequencies and dampings, a learnable residual noise spectrum, and an implicit spatial gain field — from which impact waveforms can be rendered at any novel surface point, 3D contact positions can be inferred from unseen recordings, and material-level sound edits can be applied via text prompts.

## Method

AV-MSF builds a geometry-aware visual prior by reconstructing a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, lifting pre-trained DINOv2 features into 3D, and enforcing geometric consistency through symmetry-aware feature alignment (orbit/reflection pooling). Modal frequencies and dampings are extracted from the few-shot recordings via STFT decomposition, Hilbert-transform envelope estimation, and log-linear regression, then jointly optimized with a neural gain field that predicts position-dependent modal excitation gains by conditioning on local DINOv2 features, sin-cos position-encoded offsets to spatial cluster centers, and dot-product attention over cluster-level visual descriptors. A learnable per-band residual noise component captures non-modal environmental effects. Training uses a two-stage strategy: a warm-up phase anchoring the gain field to extracted per-contact gains, followed by end-to-end optimization with a multi-scale STFT reconstruction loss applied to both full and early-time waveform segments.

## Results

On ObjectFolder Real (20% training data), AV-MSF achieves the best L1 (0.013), ENV (0.014), and CDPAM (1.35e-4), with L1 Log of 0.951. On RealImpact, it achieves the best scores across all four metrics: L1 0.021, L1 Log 0.996, ENV 0.017, and CDPAM 2.16e-4. The paper reports roughly a 2× improvement in CDPAM over both the physics-based DiffSound and the data-driven SonicGauss baselines in the few-shot (20%) setting. On the non-symmetric object subset, AV-MSF further outperforms the strong KNN baseline (L1 0.0110 vs. 0.0135, CDPAM 1.53e-4 vs. 2.15e-4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-MSF outperforms the physics-based DiffSound on all four metrics for both datasets: on ObjectFolder Real, CDPAM improves from 2.53e-4 to 1.35e-4 (~1.9×) and L1 from 0.031 to 0.013 (~2.4×); on RealImpact, L1 improves from 0.029 to 0.021 and CDPAM from 2.39e-4 to 2.16e-4. Against the data-driven SonicGauss, AV-MSF similarly wins across the board, with the largest gap on ObjectFolder Real where L1 drops from 0.033 to 0.013 and CDPAM from 2.01e-4 to 1.35e-4. The nearest competitor is the KNN baseline, which is competitive on symmetric objects (slightly better L1 Log on ObjectFolder Real: 0.930 vs. 0.951) but is clearly surpassed by AV-MSF on the non-symmetric subset (L1 0.0110 vs. 0.0135, CDPAM 1.53e-4 vs. 2.15e-4) and on RealImpact where AV-MSF leads on every metric. For the downstream contact-localization task, AV-MSF achieves 34.61% RMED versus 41.78% for DiffSound, and for sound editing it attains the lowest UMAP distance (2.753) compared to Audio-SDS (4.234) and a text-to-audio generation baseline (3.077).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the gap between physics-based impact sound synthesis (DiffSound, DiffImpact) and data-driven generative approaches (SonicGauss) by combining a compact modal parameterization with geometry-aware visual priors. It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for dense 3D representation and lifts DINOv2 features into that space, departing from prior physics-based methods that require expensive FEM-based forward simulation over volumetric meshes and from data-driven methods that demand large-scale training corpora. The residual noise modeling follows DiffImpact's per-band magnitude approach, while the text-driven editing pipeline adapts Audio-SDS with a novel hierarchical frequency parameterization to handle sparse modal spectra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AV-MSF demonstrates that a few-shot, physics-grounded representation can outperform both expensive simulation pipelines and data-hungry generative models for impact sound synthesis, suggesting a practical path toward scalable multisensory object digitization without requiring full material parameter estimation or large annotated audio datasets. The downstream applications (contact localization, physically grounded sound editing) show the representation's utility beyond mere synthesis. However, the evaluation is limited to uniform-material objects on two relatively small datasets, and generalization to non-uniform materials, complex geometries, or real-time interactive settings remains open. The reliance on symmetry detection and the assumption of position-invariant modal frequencies/dampings may limit applicability to more diverse real-world objects.
