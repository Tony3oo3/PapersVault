## Problem

Existing brain-driven visual manipulation methods rely on diffusion-based inversion-generation paradigms (DDIM, Null-Text Inversion) whose stochastic noise-reversal cycles introduce cumulative errors, preventing precise local editing and strict background preservation. Additionally, heterogeneous brain signals (fMRI, EEG, fNIRS) are difficult to encode robustly, and in multimodal synthesis, implicit neural intents are often overshadowed by explicit visual or textual anchors. The paper asks whether Rectified Flow's deterministic ODE trajectories can enable a unified, inversion-free paradigm for both brain-driven synthesis and precise local editing.

## Input / Output

The method consumes fMRI voxel-wise ROI activations, EEG/fNIRS time-series (C channels × T timepoints), a reference image compressed to a VAE latent, and optional T5-XXL text embeddings, all processed within the FLUX.1-dev MM-DiT latent space. It produces a single generated or edited image per forward pass using only 5 Euler solver steps (NFE), compared to 50–100 steps typical of diffusion samplers. Three hierarchical tasks are defined: Neuro-Context (fMRI + reference image, no text), Neuro-Creation (fMRI + reference image + text), and Neuro-Editing (EEG/fNIRS ± text + reference image), evaluated on the NSD and L-Mind datasets.

## Method

MinD-Flow is an inversion-free Rectified Flow framework with three components. The Neuro-aware Hybrid Encoder processes fMRI via a Masked Volumetric Autoencoder + ViT (spatial stream) and EEG/fNIRS via a 1D-CNN with Rotary Position Embeddings (temporal stream), disentangling topological semantics from dynamic fluctuations. The Neuro-Perceiver Bridge compresses both streams through K=64 learnable queries with cross-attention, then combines them via an Adaptive Gated Fusion mechanism that dynamically weights spatial vs. temporal contributions. The Inversion-Free Neuro-Flow Solver introduces Decoupled Flow Guidance (DFG), which computes delta velocity fields (brain and text) relative to a reference flow rather than a single joint prediction, and Neuro-Adaptive Soft Masking, which thresholds cross-attention maps to localize edits spatially. Crucially, the editing ODE is initialized at the source image latent (z_{t=0} = z_src) rather than noise, so the reference flow predicts near-zero velocity and the masked delta flow drives only the target region, achieving precise local modification in a single forward pass without any inversion loop. Training uses a flow-matching MSE loss plus a CLIP semantic alignment regularizer, with stochastic condition dropout to enable decoupled guidance at inference.

## Results

On the Neuro-Editing task (the most challenging setting), MinD-Flow with full multimodal input (EEG+fNIRS+Text) achieves L1=0.1356, FID=29.13, and CLIP-I=0.7638, reducing pixel-level error by 47.7% and FID by 50.1% compared to LoongX (Neural+Speech). In the pure neural signal setting (EEG+fNIRS, no text), MinD-Flow reduces L1 by 24.5% and LPIPS by 19.4% over LoongX (All Neural). On Neuro-Context, MinD-Flow achieves L1=0.1427 and FID=32.18, outperforming the strongest baseline MindCustomer by 9.9% (L1) and 12.0% (FID). The inversion-free design achieves these results with only 5 NFE, outperforming Null-Text Inversion at 50 steps (CLIP-I: 0.7638 vs. 0.7453).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MinD-Flow consistently outperforms all baselines across every input modality. Against LoongX (the strongest brain-editing baseline), MinD-Flow (EEG+fNIRS+Text) achieves a 47.7% reduction in L1, 50.1% reduction in FID, and 19.8% gain in CLIP-I, while MinD-Flow (EEG+fNIRS) alone reduces L1 by 24.5% and LPIPS by 19.4% over LoongX (All Neural). Against Uni-Neur2Img (the strongest flow-based baseline), MinD-Flow wins on all metrics in every setting: 4.4% lower L1 and 0.9% higher CLIP-I in the EEG+Text condition, and 6.4% lower L1 and 1.8% higher CLIP-I in the pure EEG condition. On Neuro-Context, MinD-Flow surpasses MindCustomer (the strongest direct baseline) by 9.9% in L1 and 5.0% in CLIP-I, and outperforms FLUX.1-fused decoding methods (MindEye, NeuroPictor, UMBRAE, MindTuner) by 12–20% in FID. In the inversion ablation (Table 5), MinD-Flow at 5 steps beats Null-Text Inversion at 50 steps on CLIP-I (0.7638 vs. 0.7453) and L1 (0.1356 vs. 0.1394), confirming that the inversion-free trajectory mixing is both faster and more accurate than iterative noise-reversal approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the inversion-free editing paradigm of FlowEdit (Kulikov et al., 2025) from text-driven image editing to the brain-signal-driven domain, replacing the stochastic DDIM/Null-Text Inversion loops that plague prior brain-decoding methods (MindEye, NeuroPictor, UMBRAE, LoongX) with a deterministic Rectified Flow ODE. It builds directly on the flow matching training objective (Lipman et al., 2022) and the straight-trajectory property of Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2022), but introduces a Neuro-aware Hybrid Encoder and Decoupled Flow Guidance specifically to handle the heterogeneity and noise of fMRI/EEG/fNIRS signals—problems that text-conditioned flow editors never face. The Neuro-Perceiver Bridge with learnable queries and gated fusion is a Perceiver-style compression adapted to the 'mind space → latent space' mapping, departing from the CLIP-embedding alignment strategy used by most fMRI decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5-NFE inference (vs. 50–100 for diffusion) is the key practical unlock for real-time BCI, and the Brain-Gen Benchmark provides the first standardized evaluation protocol separating context synthesis, creation, and editing—tasks that prior brain-decoding papers conflated under 'reconstruction.' However, the evidence base is narrow: only 4 subjects, two datasets (NSD, LMind), and the largest reported margins (47.7% L1 reduction) compare against LoongX(Neural+Speech), a different modality combination, making the true gap harder to isolate. Open questions include whether the deterministic ODE formulation adequately captures the inherent stochasticity of neural signals, how the framework scales to unseen subjects without per-subject fine-tuning, and whether the 60th-percentile attention thresholding generalizes beyond the tested editing scenarios.
