## Problem

Vision systems lack a general, structured representation for motion and behavior: pixel-space forecasting entangles appearance, lighting, and camera motion with object dynamics, while parameterized 3D models require strong category-specific priors and apply only to a handful of modeled species. This paper asks whether dense point trajectories can serve as a category-agnostic mid-level token that captures motion structure while remaining general enough to scale across diverse non-rigid agents in the wild.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image cropped to the animal's bounding box (expanded 50% on each side, resized to 256×256), a short motion history of 4 conditioning frames of point tracks with occlusion indicators, and an optional 2D displacement vector describing average track motion. It outputs an unordered set of N future point trajectories over 28 timesteps at 15 FPS, along with per-point occlusion states; coordinates are normalized to the bounding box and the model internally operates in velocity space (first-order differences) rather than absolute positions, converting back via cumulative summation at inference.

## Method

The core architecture is a DiT-based diffusion transformer where each point trajectory is a single token, making the model permutation-invariant over the track set. A key reparameterization converts absolute track coordinates into per-point velocities (first-order differences) with occluded gaps interpolated, decoupling initial position variance from motion dynamics and improving training stability. Each token concatenates DINOv3 image features at the track's initial location, sinusoidal embeddings of the velocity history, occlusion history, and the noisy diffusion target, with a sinusoidal position encoding derived from the 2D initial location rather than a sequence index. The model explicitly reasons about occlusion by including visibility indicators in both conditioning and diffusion targets, and uses DDIM deterministic sampling (100 steps vs. 1000 training steps) for efficient inference. Training is supervised with L1 loss on the velocity-space diffusion target, and the pipeline includes camera stabilization via RANSAC homography estimation on background tracks to isolate animal motion from camera motion in the 300-hour MammalMotion dataset.

## Results

On the full MammalMotion dataset (combined motion levels), the proposed method with displacement conditioning (Ours(cond)) achieves ADE 0.028, FDE 0.042, PWT 63.48%, and FVMD 14.38, substantially outperforming Track2Act (ADE 0.053, FDE 0.110, PWT 56.59%, FVMD 26.17) and WHN (ADE 0.105, FDE 0.200, PWT 29.92%, FVMD 94.7). On high-motion examples, the advantage is even more pronounced: Ours(cond) reaches FVMD 40.24 vs. Track2Act's 76.28 and WHN's 280.77. On the Panthera subset, Ours(cond) achieves ADE 0.028, FDE 0.039, PWT 61.67%, and FVMD 24.90, again leading all baselines. The model also generalizes out-of-distribution to humans, non-mammals, and rare species (e.g., polar bears at 0.31% of training data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all learned baselines on every metric across both the full MammalMotion dataset and the Panthera subset. Against Track2Act (the closest architectural competitor, also using a diffusion transformer with point-track tokens), Ours(cond) nearly halves FDE (0.042 vs. 0.110) and improves PWT by ~7 points (63.48% vs. 56.59%) on the combined dataset, with an even larger FVMD gap (14.38 vs. 26.17) indicating better motion-distribution fidelity. Against WHN (applied zero-shot due to grid constraints), the gap is dramatic: Ours(cond) achieves 3.7× lower FDE and 2.1× higher PWT. Against ATM (regression-based, Panthera only), Ours(cond) improves ADE from 0.057 to 0.028 and PWT from 48.39% to 61.67%. Notably, simple non-learned baselines (no-motion, constant velocity) remain competitive on low-motion examples but collapse on high-motion data, where Ours(cond) achieves FVMD 40.24 vs. 210.47 for constant velocity. The paper also shows that training on the full multi-species dataset transfers to the Panthera subset, improving FVMD from 84.8 to 49.3 on high-motion examples compared to a model trained only on Panthera.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions dense point trajectories as a mid-level 'visual token' for behavior, occupying a principled middle ground between pixel-space video prediction (which entangles appearance, lighting, and camera motion with dynamics) and category-specific 3D parameterized models (which require strong object priors). Methodologically, it builds directly on the DiT diffusion transformer architecture and DDIM sampling, and draws on the robotics trajectory-forecasting line (ATM, Track2Act) that treats point tracks as tokens, but extends this from rigid manipulation scenes to non-rigid, in-the-wild animal motion. The closest conceptual predecessor is the 'What Happens Next' work showing point tracks outperform pixel generation for rigid Kubric objects; this paper validates and extends that finding to the far more challenging non-rigid, multi-species domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MammalMotion dataset (300 hours of camera-stabilized, annotated animal motion) and the point-trajectory-as-token formulation provide a reusable foundation for studying predictive visual intelligence across diverse animate agents without category-specific models. The demonstrated generalization to species comprising as little as 0.025% of training data suggests the representation captures motion structure rather than memorizing species-specific patterns. Open questions include whether this single-agent formulation extends to multi-agent interaction forecasting, whether the 2D trajectory representation suffices for 3D-aware prediction, and whether the approach transfers to human or robotic motion at comparable data efficiency.
