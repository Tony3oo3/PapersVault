## Problem

Existing generative, text-guided mesh deformation methods optimize vertex displacements while keeping triangulation fixed, which limits them to modest edits and produces poor triangle quality under large-scale structural changes like growths and appendages. Remeshing could resolve this, but it is a discrete operation that is notoriously difficult to couple with the noisy supervision signals (score distillation) used in text-based generative settings, so it has been largely absent from this line of work.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source triangle mesh (normalized to a [-1,1]³ bounding box), a text prompt, and an optional binary per-vertex selection mask that restricts where deformation and remeshing occur. For local growths, selected vertices receive an initial inflation displacement proportional to the cube root of the selection's enclosed volume. The output is a deformed and remeshed mesh with isotropic, near-equilateral triangles; outside the selection region, all vertex attributes, correspondence, and corner UVs are preserved exactly, allowing straightforward re-texturing of newly grown geometry.

## Method

RADmesh optimizes a per-vertex 6-component deformation quantity (3 direction + 3 scale) that extends Geometry in Style's normal-based representation by adding optimizable local scaling, enabling growth beyond the original selection's spatial extent. Each epoch, a local step computes per-vertex rotations (via a weighted Procrustes solve) and diagonal scales, and a global step solves a differentiable ARAP system to obtain deformed positions; the deformed mesh is rendered with nvdiffrast and supervised by Cascaded Score Distillation from DeepFloyd IF. Every 100 epochs, a Botsch & Kobbelt isotropic remesher (2 iterations) replaces the base mesh with a new triangulation at a coarsely-to-finely scheduled target edge length, and the Adam optimizer's gradient-accumulator state is interpolated onto the new vertices via barycentric coordinates, preserving optimization momentum across the discrete connectivity change. This vertex-based formulation is critical: it makes state interpolation straightforward during remeshing, unlike face-based quantities such as per-face Jacobians, and the coarse-to-fine schedule (validated by ablation) allows the optimization to explore large geometric changes before committing to fine resolution.

## Results

On 64 shape-prompt pairs, RADmesh achieves the best CLIP score (31.42) and VQA score (72.93), indicating superior perceptual quality and prompt adherence. It also attains the highest average triangle face quality (FQ = 96.52) while using fewer faces (16,964) than the other remeshing baselines (MagicClay: 17,204; Instant3dit: 40,035; MeshUp+remesh: 29,461). The non-remeshing baselines (Geometry in Style, MeshUp) report lower FQ (94.15 and 84.83) and lower CLIP/VQA scores, reflecting their inability to adapt triangulation to the generated geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADmesh outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the proxy-representation methods MagicClay (implicit SDF) and Instant3dit (multiview inpainting), RADmesh gains +1.60 CLIP and +15.78 VQA over MagicClay, and +3.02 CLIP and +20.82 VQA over Instant3dit, while also producing far fewer faces (16,964 vs. 40,035 for Instant3dit). Against the direct mesh-deformation baselines Geometry in Style and MeshUp (which do not remesh), RADmesh improves CLIP by +2.14 and +0.69 respectively, and VQA by +20.81 and +6.84, with substantially better face quality (96.52 vs. 94.15 and 84.83). Notably, adding RADmesh's coarse-to-fine remesh schedule to MeshUp alone boosts its VQA from 66.09 to 69.36 and FQ from 84.83 to 96.31, confirming the benefit of remeshing in explicit mesh deformation, yet RADmesh still leads on all metrics with fewer faces (16,964 vs. 29,461).

## Relation to Prior Work

RADmesh extends the per-vertex normal deformation quantity of Geometry in Style [16] by appending optimizable per-axis scale, and couples it with the isotropic Botsch & Kobbelt remesher [10] at regular intervals during SDS-based optimization. This departs from prior generative deformation methods (TextDeformer, MeshUp, Geometry in Style) that fix connectivity throughout, and from prior remeshing optimizers (Palfinger, Barda et al.) that optimize raw vertex positions rather than differential deformation quantities. The key conceptual bridge is that a vertex-based quantity admits barycentric interpolation of optimizer state across discrete connectivity changes, something face-based Jacobians cannot support cleanly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that discrete remeshing can be stably interleaved with noisy score-distillation supervision, enabling large structural growths (wings, antlers, garments) that are infeasible under fixed connectivity without degenerating triangle quality. The coarse-to-fine target-length schedule and optimizer-state interpolation are shown via ablation to be individually necessary, and the resulting triangulations adapt density to generated geometry rather than uniformly upsample. Limitations include the manifold-mesh requirement (Poisson solve), 70–90 minute runtimes on a single GPU, and reliance on SDS supervision; future directions noted include non-manifold Laplacians, quad meshing, and topological tearing/merging operations.
