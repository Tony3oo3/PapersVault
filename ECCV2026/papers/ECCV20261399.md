## Problem

Existing audio description (AD) systems treat each video clip independently or propagate context in shallow, static forms (e.g., concatenating prior descriptions or reusing fixed identity features), failing to preserve character identities, causal relationships, and evolving story salience across a full film. Training-free pipelines that avoid task-specific fine-tuning still depend on pre-prepared resources such as character banks, aligned subtitles, or AD transcripts, limiting scalability to new movies. The paper asks whether a system can maintain coherent, narrative-grounded AD across long-form video using only raw video and a movie title, without any curated movie-specific resources.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequence of raw video clips {v_1, …, v_T} in chronological order plus a movie title, which is used solely to retrieve optional public IMDb metadata (plot summaries, character lists, short character descriptions). No subtitles, scripts, AD transcripts, character banks, or precomputed face identities are required. The output is a sequence of audio description narration segments, one per clip, generated to complement the original movie audio. Internally, the system also maintains a persistent narrative state comprising an identity graph (character embeddings) and a salience-weighted fact memory that evolves across clips.

## Method

StoryTeller is a fully training-free, memory-augmented pipeline that processes clips sequentially while updating a persistent narrative state through three modules. (1) Identity Graph Update: ArcFace face embeddings are extracted per tracklet and matched to existing character nodes via cosine similarity (threshold τ=0.58); characters begin anonymous and are named only after VLM-verified visual and narrative evidence supports the association, allowing identities to emerge dynamically without a precomputed character bank. (2) Grounded Fact Induction: a VLM (Qwen3-VL-2B) proposes structured facts (subject, action, object, context) conditioned on the identity graph, optional IMDb metadata, and prior narrative memory; candidates then pass a lightweight semantic filter (cosine similarity σ=0.20 against the scene summary and stored facts) followed by a dedicated VLM verifier (Qwen3-VL-8B) that outputs ACCEPT/REJECT, ensuring only video-supported facts enter memory. (3) Salience-Based Narrative Memory: accepted facts are stored with salience weights updated by reinforcement-decay dynamics w_m^(t) = λw_m^(t−1) + α·r_m^(t), where r_m^(t) is the cosine similarity between the current scene embedding and the stored fact; this causes persistently relevant facts (central characters, ongoing conflicts) to accumulate weight while transient details fade, approximating how human viewers track evolving story salience.

## Results

On MAD-Eval, StoryTeller (Qwen3-VL) achieves CIDEr 21.4, SPICE 6.7, and ROUGE-L 15.3, while the VideoLLaMA2 variant reaches CIDEr 19.1, SPICE 9.0, and ROUGE-L 16.0. On the proposed StoryAD-QA narrative-comprehension benchmark, StoryTeller substantially outperforms AutoAD-Zero across all segment lengths: e.g., 0.972 vs. 0.917 accuracy on 240 s Track A and 0.788 vs. 0.673 on the 90+30 s Track B setting. StoryTeller requires no task-specific training, subtitles, character banks, or precomputed face identities, using only raw video and an optional public movie title for metadata retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On standard MAD-Eval captioning metrics, StoryTeller is competitive but not uniformly dominant: it trails AutoAD-Zero on CIDEr (21.4 vs. 22.4) and SPICE (6.7 vs. 7.3) with Qwen3-VL, yet leads on ROUGE-L (15.3 vs. 14.4); the VideoLLaMA2 variant reverses the SPICE gap (9.0 vs. 7.3) and ROUGE-L (16.0 vs. 14.4) at the cost of CIDEr. Critically, StoryTeller achieves this without any curated resources (subtitles, character banks, precomputed face identities), whereas AutoAD-Zero, MM-Narrator, and MM-Vid all require such resources. On the novel StoryAD-QA narrative-comprehension benchmark, StoryTeller wins decisively over AutoAD-Zero in every setting: +5.2 to +5.5 points on Track A (segment-only QA) and +9.2 to +11.5 points on Track B (context-conditioned QA), with the largest margin at 90+30 s (0.788 vs. 0.673). Among the trained baselines (AutoAD-I/II/III), StoryTeller matches or exceeds their CIDEr scores (21.4 vs. 14.3/19.2/24.0) while requiring no training data or task-specific fine-tuning. The ablation (A5) confirms that even without IMDb metadata, StoryTeller remains competitive with or above AutoAD-Zero on StoryAD-QA, indicating the gains stem from the narrative memory and identity graph rather than external text alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

StoryTeller carves new ground at the intersection of training-free AD pipelines (AutoAD-Zero, MM-Narrator) and long-horizon video memory systems (VideoAgent, Optimus-1, M3-Agent). It departs from supervised AD systems (AutoAD I/II/III, MICAP) by eliminating task-specific training, character banks, and curated movie annotations, and from prior training-free methods by removing reliance on pre-prepared resources like aligned subtitles or fixed identity features. The identity graph repurposes ArcFace face tracking in a dynamic, evidence-accumulating manner rather than a static lookup, and the salience-weighted narrative memory adapts reinforcement-decay dynamics from long-video reasoning to the accessibility-specific constraint that descriptions must complement (not replace) the original audio. The StoryAD-QA benchmark extends the ADQA evaluation paradigm from segment-level visual appreciation to multi-scene narrative comprehension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The StoryAD-QA benchmark shifts AD evaluation from lexical overlap (CIDEr, SPICE) to whether a listener can actually reconstruct story context from the narration alone, which is a more faithful measure of accessibility utility. The finding that the identity graph provides substantial gains even when public IMDb metadata is available (A4 ablation) suggests that persistent visual character tracking captures information that text-based retrieval cannot, pointing to a complementary role for visual and textual evidence in narrative grounding. Limitations include evaluation on only 10 movies, a 3–4 hour runtime per feature-length film, and dependence on VLM quality for both fact extraction and verification; open questions include how the salience-decay dynamics behave on very long films with large ensemble casts and whether the fixed thresholds generalize across genres and visual styles.
