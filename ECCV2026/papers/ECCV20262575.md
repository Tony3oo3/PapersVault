## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models suffer from error accumulation because the joint distribution of generated conditioning frames drifts from the training distribution, eventually reaching 'terminal points' where no valid continuation exists within the learned real manifold. Existing approaches focus on keeping individual frames on the manifold (via attention sinks, temporal unrolling, or timestep repetition) but assume that if all individual frames are realistic, the trajectory will remain valid — ignoring that the joint distribution can still be out-of-distribution.

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts, single images, or short video clips as conditioning and generates arbitrarily long videos at 832×480 resolution and 16 FPS, operating in the latent space of a 3D VAE with ×8 spatial and ×4 temporal compression (latent dimensions 60×104×16). A rolling KV cache retains 21 latent frames (~5 s of video), enabling unbounded generation. The test-time optimization introduces LoRA adapters (rank 8) into the self-attention layers of the causal diffusion transformer, requiring only a single Nvidia RTX Pro 6000 GPU (96 GB) at inference.

## Method

TANGO exploits the hypothesis that for well-modeled conditioning sequences, the denoiser's predicted noise should match the isotropic Gaussian distribution of the forward noising process; violations signal a terminal point. At each autoregressive step, the model predicts one step forward using a frozen copy of itself as a critic, and the noise residual is evaluated via a four-component consistency objective: first/second moment penalties, higher-order moment penalties (skewness and excess kurtosis), spectral flatness of the power spectrum, and low-frequency moment penalties. A constrained optimization then updates LoRA parameters (rank 8, learning rate 2×10⁻⁶) to minimize this noise consistency loss subject to an L2 regularization anchoring the output near the original prediction, using B=10 noise vectors per step. This test-time adaptation requires no additional training data and is applied uniformly across text-to-video, image-to-video, and video-to-video generation.

## Results

TANGO achieves a VBench total score of 0.863, a 3.1% absolute improvement over the previous state-of-the-art (LongLive at 0.832), with gains of 4.8% in semantic score (0.860 vs. 0.814) and 2.6% in quality score (0.864 vs. 0.838). On Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) against real videos from LV-Bench, TANGO reduces FVD by 28.3% on average across 15-second videos and by 38.7% at the 15-second mark compared to the SelfForcing baseline. The method operates on a 1.3B-parameter model with 8.6 min runtime and 37.6 GiB memory, outperforming 13–14B parameter models (HunyuanVideo, SVI-1.0/2.0) on VBench while using a fraction of the compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TANGO outperforms all five autoregressive baselines (RollingForcing, SelfForcing, CausVid, RewardForcing, LongLive) on the VBench total score, with the largest margin over RollingForcing (+5.9%) and the smallest over LongLive (+3.1%). The method leads on quality score (0.864) and semantic score (0.860) across all baselines, but trails CausVid on dynamic degree (0.769 vs. 0.872), which CausVid achieves at the cost of lower semantic coherence (0.794). Against larger-model approaches, TANGO (1.3B params) surpasses HunyuanVideo (13B, 0.832) and SVI-2.0 (14B, 0.849) on VBench total score while running 4–10× faster and using less memory. TANGO approaches SVI-2.0-Pro (27B, 0.869) within 0.6% but at 5× lower runtime (8.6 min vs. 43.2 min) and half the memory (37.6 vs. 68.4 GiB). On FVD, TANGO reduces the distance to real videos by 28.3% on average over 15 s compared to the SelfForcing baseline, with the gap widening over time (38.7% reduction at the 15-s mark), indicating that terminal-point avoidance becomes increasingly critical as generation length grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the autoregressive video diffusion framework of Self-Forcing [19] and the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B base model [47], applying a test-time LoRA-based adaptation on top of their causal-attention, KV-cache architecture. It departs conceptually from the dominant line of work (attention sinks, temporal unrolling, noise rescheduling) that treats error accumulation as a per-frame manifold-exit problem, arguing instead that the joint trajectory distribution can reach 'terminal points' where no valid continuation exists even if every individual frame is on the real manifold. The noise consistency hypothesis — that the denoiser's predicted noise should be isotropic Gaussian for well-modeled conditioning — repurposes the diffusion model as its own critic, a formulation not present in prior autoregressive video generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The terminal-point framing provides a more precise diagnosis of why autoregressive video generation degrades over time, shifting the focus from individual frame fidelity to trajectory-level distribution matching. The test-time-only nature (no retraining, LoRA rank 8) makes the approach broadly applicable to any autoregressive diffusion video model, and the 28.3% average FVD reduction over 15 s is practically meaningful. However, the method is fundamentally bounded by the learned manifold: for underrepresented visual topics (exotic physics, nanobiology), noise guidance produces visually pleasant but semantically wrong outputs, confirming that test-time adaptation is complementary to, not a substitute for, data and model scaling. An open question the authors raise is whether architectures could learn to quantify their own epistemic uncertainty natively, eliminating the need for gradient-descent-based test-time optimization.
