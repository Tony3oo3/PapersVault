## Problem

Previous work concluded that Vision Transformers are inherently more vulnerable to backdoor attacks than CNNs and that existing defenses fail on ViTs. This paper shows those conclusions are overly optimistic, stemming from improper adaptation of CNN-based defenses (e.g., using SGD instead of AdamW for fine-tuning, or pruning norm-layer channels instead of linear-projection channels in ViTs). With correct adaptations, standard defenses effectively neutralize existing attacks on ViTs, motivating the question of whether a fundamentally stronger attack exists.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32 resized to 224×224, or ImageNet at 224×224) and produces a single class label. The trigger is applied via a binary mask and pattern (Equation 1), and the crafted perturbation is l2-bounded (budget 16/255) with random patch-level masking (2×2 patches, 5–10% dropped). Crucially, the perturbation is added only during the poisoning/training phase; at inference time the trigger remains the original unmodified pattern, so the victim model sees no additional noise.

## Method

CAT (Channel Activation attack in ViTs) exploits the observation that backdoor defenses detect the large per-channel activation gap between triggered and benign inputs in intermediate ViT layers. The attack crafts adversarial perturbations on the trigger pattern via l2-PGD (10 steps, step size 4/255) to minimize this activation difference while preserving correct classification, using a composite loss that combines backdoor discriminators (BD) and target classifiers (TC) trained on the last n=2 layers of a surrogate ViT-B. A Random Masking of Perturbation (RMP) step randomly drops a fraction of 2×2 perturbation patches at each PGD iteration, creating multi-scale features that improve transferability across ViT variants with different patch sizes (ViT-B, DeiT-S, Swin-B, CaiT-S, XciT-S). The enhanced poisoned dataset is then released for training the victim model, requiring no architectural or training-time modifications on the defender's side.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ViT-B, properly adapted defenses (AdamW-based FT, channel-level pruning) reduce ASR of all six baseline attacks to 0.5–5.7%, confirming that ViTs are not inherently more vulnerable than CNNs. The proposed CAT attack dramatically reverses this: e.g., BadNets+CAT achieves 65.01% ASR after FT (vs. 0.72% for vanilla BadNets) and 92.23% after ANP (vs. 23.70% for Blend). On ImageNet, CAT-enhanced BadNets reaches 81.98% ASR against AWM on ViT-B, while the strongest ViT-specific baselines (TrojViT, DBIA, BadViT) remain below 10% ASR under the same defenses. CAT also achieves superior perceptual stealthiness (PSNR 58.86, SSIM 0.9999) compared to all baseline triggers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the six standard backdoor attacks (BadNets, Blend, CLB, SIG, IAD, SSBA), CAT consistently and substantially increases post-defense ASR across all five ViT architectures and all eight defenses; the largest gains appear against FT-SAM (e.g., BadNets 2.57%→98.44% on ViT-B) and ANP (e.g., Blend 23.70%→92.23% on ViT-B). Against ViT-specific attacks on ImageNet (Table 5), CAT-enhanced BadNets/Blend outperform TrojViT (max ASR 91.08% before defense, ≤0.46% after), DBIA (≤0.18% after), and BadViT (≤8.97% after) by a wide margin under all model-agnostic defenses. CAT also improves ASR against ViT-specific defenses: e.g., against PPD on ImageNet, Blend+CAT reaches 92.76% ASR on ViT-B versus 86.54% for vanilla Blend. The only defense where CAT shows a modest gain is AWM on ViT-B (BadNets 0.71%→6.78%), suggesting AWM's element-wise masking is partially effective even against perturbed triggers. Overall, CAT wins on every comparison against both standard and ViT-specific baselines, with the exception that AWM remains the strongest single defense, keeping most CAT-enhanced ASRs below 50%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the backdoor attack/defense arms race in the context of Vision Transformers, building on the extensive CNN backdoor literature (BadNets, Blend, ANP, AWM, etc.) and the emerging ViT-specific attack line (TrojViT, DBIA, BadViT). Its key departure is methodological: it identifies that prior reports of ViT vulnerability were inflated by inheriting CNN defense protocols (SGD-based fine-tuning, norm-layer pruning) without adapting them to ViT training conventions (AdamW, channel-level pruning in attention/MLP blocks). The proposed CAT attack repurposes the adversarial perturbation framework from adversarial robustness (PGD) and the channel-activation observation from ANP/AWM, but applies it to trigger design rather than model purification, combined with a novel random masking strategy for cross-architecture transferability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By correcting the evaluation methodology, this paper resets the baseline for ViT backdoor robustness: with proper adaptation, existing defenses reduce ASR to near-zero, undermining the narrative that ViTs are inherently more vulnerable than CNNs. The CAT attack then reopens the question by showing that trigger-level adversarial perturbations can restore high ASR (e.g., 0.72%→65.01% under FT), suggesting current defenses remain insufficient against a more informed adversary. Open questions include whether defenses can be further adapted to handle channel-activation-minimized triggers, whether CAT's transferability holds for unseen architectures beyond the five tested, and whether the findings extend to other modalities (video, point clouds) where transformer backdoors are increasingly relevant.
