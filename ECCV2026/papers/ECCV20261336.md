## Problem

Current text-to-motion diffusion models suffer from two coupled limitations: pre-trained text encoders (e.g., CLIP, DistilBERT) lack motion-specific temporal and kinematic cues, forcing the model to bridge a large representational gap between abstract language and high-dimensional motion dynamics; and iterative denoising propagates early error patterns across subsequent steps, degrading sample quality. Existing two-stream approaches that implicitly learn a joint language-motion space sacrifice motion fidelity, while no prior method explicitly exploits the diffusion model's self-correction ability to counteract error accumulation during sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261336/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text description as input, which is tokenized and embedded by DistilBERT into a sequence of token-level vectors (L × d_f). These are compressed by a 6-layer transformer text encoder into a single 256-dim (HumanML3D) or 192-dim (KIT-ML) latent token. The output is a 3D human motion sequence in the standard redundant representation (joint velocities, positions, and rotations) of shape T × d, generated over 20 denoising steps at inference. The single-token text representation is acknowledged as a limitation for long-horizon, multi-action descriptions.

## Method

RAM employs a two-stream architecture sharing an 8-layer MDM diffusion decoder: a motion reconstruction branch (motion encoder E_m → latent z_m → denoiser) and a text-to-motion branch (text encoder E_t → latent z_t → denoiser). Two novel training objectives shape the latent space: self-regularization, a cross-entropy loss over batch-normalized motion latents that enforces separability and semantic resolution, and motion-centric latent alignment, which pulls text latents toward motion latents using a stop-gradient with a small β=0.01 so the motion space remains dominant while still permitting minimal gradient flow for convergence. At inference, Reconstructive Error Guidance (REG) reconstructs the previous step's prediction via the motion encoder to capture embedded error patterns, then amplifies the residual between the current text-driven prediction and this reconstruction (weighted by w₁=5.0) to push sampling away from error-prone regions. REG is combined with standard classifier-free guidance (w₂=1.5) to simultaneously improve motion realism (FID) and semantic alignment (R-Precision).

## Results

On HumanML3D, RAM achieves an FID of 0.032 with only 20 inference steps, matching the best VQ-VAE method (LaMP, 0.032) and surpassing all prior diffusion-based methods (previous best: COME at 0.041, Salad at 0.076). RAM also attains R-Precision@1 of 56.1%, R-Precision@3 of 83.9%, and MM Dist of 2.716, ranking second only to Salad (58.1%) in semantic accuracy among diffusion models. On the smaller KIT-ML dataset, RAM achieves FID 0.172 and R-Precision@1 of 46.4%, remaining highly competitive with the leading diffusion method Salad (FID 0.296, R-Precision@1 47.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAM's headline result is that a diffusion-based model now matches the best VQ-VAE FID: RAM (0.032) ties LaMP (0.032) and beats MoGenTS (0.033), MoMask (0.045), and KinMo (0.039) on FID, while simultaneously surpassing all of them in R-Precision@1 (56.1% vs. 55.7%, 52.9%, 52.1%, 53.2% respectively). Among diffusion-based methods, RAM achieves the lowest FID by a wide margin—0.032 vs. COME (0.041), MotionLCM v2 (0.056), Salad (0.076), and ReMoDiffuse (0.103)—but trails Salad (58.1%) by 2 points on R-Precision@1. On KIT-ML, RAM (FID 0.172, R-Prec@1 46.4%) is the most balanced diffusion model: it beats Salad (FID 0.296) by 0.124 in FID while losing only 1.3 points in R-Precision@1, and it outperforms ReMoDiffuse (FID 0.155, R-Prec@1 42.7%) on semantic alignment by 3.7 points. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that REG alone drops FID from 0.132 to 0.032 (a 76% reduction), while the motion-centric latent alignment (L_latent) contributes the largest single training-time gain, reducing FID from 0.624 to 0.187.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAM adopts the two-stream architecture from Language2Pose [2]—a motion reconstruction branch and a text-to-motion generation branch sharing a diffusion decoder (MDM [44])—and reuses the encoder designs from TEMOS [31]. Its key conceptual departure is rejecting the joint language-motion embedding space pursued by TEMOS, TMR, and KinMo in favor of a motion-centric latent space shaped by self-regularization, with text latents aligning *to* motion rather than both converging to a shared space. At inference, the proposed Reconstructive Error Guidance (REG) draws on the 'guiding with a weakened score' lineage (perturbed-attention guidance [1], bad-version-of-itself guidance [19]) but instantiates the weakened score by reconstructing the previous denoising estimate through the motion encoder, turning the model's own error patterns into a contrastive reference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most notable practical implication is that a diffusion-based pipeline now matches the best VQ-VAE method (LaMP) on FID (0.032) on HumanML3D, closing a gap that has persisted across the literature and suggesting diffusion models need not sacrifice motion realism for flexibility. REG is a lightweight, training-free inference technique (one extra forward pass per step) that could be adopted by other motion diffusion models. However, the single-token text representation limits applicability to long-horizon or multi-action sequences, and the gains on the smaller KIT-ML dataset are more modest, leaving open whether the approach scales to richer, longer motion descriptions without finer-grained conditioning.
