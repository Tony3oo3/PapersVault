## Problem

Medical MLLMs can answer clinical VQA questions but provide no pixel-level evidence for their diagnoses, while segmentation models localize well but lack the multi-step diagnostic reasoning needed for question-driven grounding. Existing reasoning-segmentation approaches (e.g., LISA) rely on a single holistic [SEG] token, which cannot capture the distributed, multi-step clinical logic that justifies a specific lesion boundary, and promptable foundation models (e.g., MedSAM3) depend on explicit category labels that fail in real diagnostic workflows requiring natural-language reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D medical image (ultrasound, X-ray, dermoscopy, or endoscopy) and a natural-language clinical question as input. The MLLM autoregressively generates a textual answer that includes an explicit [SEG] token marking where spatial grounding is needed; simultaneously, the framework decodes a binary evidence segmentation mask that must be spatially consistent with the predicted diagnosis. Unlike standard segmentation that accepts geometric prompts (points, boxes), MedREAL's mask is conditioned entirely on the model's internal reasoning trace, with no oracle category labels or predefined referring phrases at inference time.

## Method

MedREAL embeds a [SEG] token in the MLLM's generation sequence and uses its hidden state as an attention anchor in the SARP module, which applies scaled dot-product attention over the full terminal-layer hidden states to distill a compact reasoning-relevant vector, suppressing irrelevant linguistic noise. This vector is then fused with a global visual context vector (obtained by average-pooling and projecting SAM's image-encoder features) through a two-layer MLP in the R2V Fusion module, producing a semantically-informed conditioning vector that replaces geometric prompts in the SAM mask decoder. A bounding-box guidance signal provides an additional coarse spatial prior. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a weighted combination of autoregressive cross-entropy (text) and BCE + Dice (mask) losses, using Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct as the MLLM backbone and SAM as the segmentor, on the newly constructed MedRAVS-13K dataset of 13,824 image–QA–mask triplets across four imaging modalities.

## Results

On the MedRAVS-13K benchmark spanning four imaging modalities (ultrasound, X-ray, dermoscopy, endoscopy), MedREAL achieves an overall gIoU of 68.49% and cIoU of 70.47%, substantially outperforming the primary reasoning-segmentation baseline LISA (55.70% gIoU, 56.05% cIoU) by over 12 points in gIoU. The advantage is most pronounced on the low-contrast BUSI ultrasound dataset, where MedREAL reaches 69.15% gIoU versus LISA's 41.76%. For text generation quality, MedREAL achieves a BLEU score of 89.12 and ROUGE-L of 96.69, far exceeding LISA's 24.04 BLEU and 48.11 ROUGE-L, confirming that the segmentation mechanism does not degrade linguistic output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LISA (the primary end-to-end reasoning-segmentation baseline), MedREAL wins decisively on every dataset, improving overall gIoU from 55.70% to 68.49% (+12.79 pts) and cIoU from 56.05% to 70.47% (+14.42 pts), with the largest gap on BUSI ultrasound (69.15% vs. 41.76% gIoU). Against MedSAM3, which reports a higher overall gIoU of 70.99%, the comparison is not apples-to-apples: MedSAM3 receives oracle ground-truth category labels as prompts (e.g., 'malignant breast tumor'), whereas MedREAL operates solely on natural-language clinical questions without any predefined category hints. When MedSAM3 is forced to generate its own prompts via an agent loop (MedSAM3-Agent), its gIoU collapses to 55.71%, well below MedREAL's 68.49%. Against the general-domain referring baselines OVSeg and SAM3, MedREAL's advantage is overwhelming (68.49% vs. 13.48% and 5.43% gIoU respectively), reflecting the difficulty of medical reasoning-driven segmentation for models without domain-specific training. In text generation, MedREAL achieves BLEU-4 of 96.26 versus LISA's 13.15, confirming that the SARP/R2V mechanism preserves the MLLM's linguistic fidelity while adding spatial grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedREAL extends the reasoning-segmentation paradigm introduced by LISA, which uses a single [SEG] token's hidden state as a holistic prompt for SAM's mask decoder. The key architectural departure is the SARP module, which repurposes the [SEG] token not as a feature source but as an attention anchor to pool distributed, multi-step diagnostic reasoning from the full MLLM sequence, followed by R2V fusion that projects these distilled semantics into SAM's visual feature space as a pseudoprompt. The paper also positions itself against decoupled agent-based systems (SAM3-Agent, MedSAM3-Agent) and purely structural referring models (OVSeg, MedSAM3), arguing that end-to-end coupling of reasoning and segmentation within a shared latent space is necessary for clinically faithful grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in clinical AI: MLLMs produce diagnostically fluent answers but cannot point to the pixels that justify them, undermining trust in high-stakes settings. MedRAVS-13K is a useful new benchmark that pairs reasoning-oriented QA with verified masks across four modalities, filling a void where prior Med-VQA datasets lack pixel supervision and segmentation datasets lack reasoning queries. Limitations include 2D-only operation, autoregressive inference cost, and evaluation restricted to four source datasets; extending to 3D volumetric imaging (CT/MRI) and real-time deployment remain open. The finding that injecting unrefined linguistic features without SARP actively degrades segmentation (23.22% gIoU) suggests that naive text-to-segmentation fusion is counterproductive, a caution for future multimodal grounding designs.
