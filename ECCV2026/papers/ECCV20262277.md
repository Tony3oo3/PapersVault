## Problem

Flow Matching models suffer from exposure bias: during training the model sees ideal linear-path perturbations, but during multi-step inference it must rely on its own accumulated predictions, causing progressive drift from the target distribution. Existing mitigations (input perturbation, scheduled sampling, inference-time regularization) enforce static alignment or external heuristics, lacking a mechanism that adapts correction strength to the actual severity of drift. The paper asks whether the exposure bias signal itself carries enough directional and frequency information to drive its own rectification.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise (and a class label for conditional generation) and produces synthetic RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 64×64 (CelebA-64), or 256×256/512×512 (ImageNet, in VAE latent space). During training, the key unusual input is a *biased* state  x̂_{t₀,t₁}  constructed by a single Euler step from an ideal perturbed state using the model's own predicted velocity; this biased state, rather than the ground-truth interpolation, is fed to the network to compute both the ADR direction loss and the FC reweighting mask. Inference uses standard ODE or SDE solvers with 30–500 NFE.

## Method

DEFAR augments the standard Flow Matching objective with two terms computed from a single-step inference simulation performed inside the training loop. (1) Anti-Drift Rectification (ADR) defines a restorative target velocity v_ADR = a'_{t₁}x_* + b'_{t₁}x̂_{t₀,t₁} that points from the biased state back toward the true data endpoint, then penalizes the *directional* (L2-normalized) mismatch between the model's prediction on the biased state and v_ADR; because the penalty scales with angular deviation, the correction automatically intensifies as drift worsens. (2) Frequency Compensation (FC) computes the exposure-bias vector δ = v_θ(x̂_{t₀,t₁},t₁) − v_θ(x_{t₁},t₁) and uses its per-pixel magnitude as a spatial reweighting mask W = 1 + α·‖δ‖²/Σ‖δ‖² applied to the original FM loss at the earlier timestep t₀; empirical Fourier analysis shows δ concentrates on low-frequency structural regions during high-noise timesteps, so this negative-feedback weighting adaptively reinforces the missing low-frequency content. The total training loss is β₁·L_ADR + β₂·L_FC (β₁=10, β₂=1, α=1), and the framework is plug-and-play across SiT, REPA, and DDT backbones with linear, SBDM-VP, or GVP path interpolants.

## Results

On conditional ImageNet-256 (50 NFE), DEFAR (500k iters) achieves FID 56.39/28.34 (without/with CFG) and sFID 9.50/7.84, outperforming the SiT baseline (61.64/31.64) by 5.25/3.30 in FID. On conditional CIFAR-10, DEFAR (180k iters) reaches FID 11.22/7.56 and sFID 5.32/5.21, improving over SiT (14.09/9.36) by 2.87/1.80. Under a comparable training budget (250k/144h vs. SiT's 500k/166h on ImageNet-256), DEFAR still reduces FID by 1.24/1.83, demonstrating efficiency. On unconditional CIFAR-10 and CelebA-64, DEFAR (130k iters) achieves FID 15.93 and 16.03 respectively, beating SiT by 1.48 and 1.13.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEFAR outperforms all four exposure-bias baselines (IP, SDSS, MDSS) on FID for both ImageNet-256 and CIFAR-10; notably, MDSS actually degrades FID relative to SiT on ImageNet-256 (63.66 vs. 61.64), while DEFAR reduces it to 56.39. Against the SiT baseline, DEFAR (500k) improves FID by 5.25 (w/o CFG) and 3.30 (w/ CFG) on ImageNet-256, and by 2.87/1.80 on CIFAR-10. DEFAR also achieves the best sFID across all baselines (9.50/7.84 on ImageNet-256, 5.32/5.21 on CIFAR-10), indicating superior sample realism. In complementary experiments, combining DEFAR with Discriminator Guidance (DG) reduces ImageNet-256 FID by an additional 1.89 over DG alone, and pairing DEFAR with mini-batch OT coupling yields the best Avg. FID of 3.960±0.034 on unconditional CIFAR-10, outperforming OT-CFM alone by 0.135. DEFAR also integrates cleanly into REPA-XL/2 and DDT-XL/2 on ImageNet-512, improving FID by 0.53 and 0.08 respectively under comparable budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exposure-bias mitigation line for generative models (IP, SDSS, MDSS) from the DDPM setting into Flow Matching, but departs fundamentally in philosophy: rather than passively hardening the model against input perturbations via static alignment or fixed noise injection, DEFAR treats the bias signal itself as an endogenous corrective resource. It builds directly on the standard FM training objective (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and the inference-simulation-during-training paradigm of MDSS/SDSS (Ren et al., AAAI 2024), replacing their static ground-truth target with a dynamic anti-drift direction (ADR) and adding a frequency-domain reweighting mechanism (FC) inspired by wavelet-based regularization in diffusion models (Yu & Zhan, 2025) and saliency-aware loss weighting in video generation (MotiF, Proteus-ID).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of exposure bias from a nuisance to be suppressed into a signal that can drive its own correction offers a cleaner conceptual foundation for future work on training-inference mismatch in iterative generative models. Practically, DEFAR is a drop-in training modification (no architectural changes, no inference-time overhead) that yields consistent 1–2 FID gains across scales and datasets, and its demonstrated compatibility with REPA, DDT, DG, and OT-CFM suggests broad applicability. Open questions include whether the single-step inference simulation adequately captures the compounding dynamics of many-step sampling, whether the frequency compensation mechanism transfers to video or audio generation where temporal low-frequency structure differs, and whether the added training cost (extra forward passes, FFT computation) remains justified at very large scale.
