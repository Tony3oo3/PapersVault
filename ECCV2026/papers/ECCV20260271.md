## Problem

Video-action models face a fundamental trade-off: multi-step video generation yields high-fidelity future foresight but is too slow for real-time closed-loop control, while one-step denoising features are fast yet produce noisy, entangled representations that lack the geometric structure and semantic distinctiveness needed for precise manipulation. No existing method simultaneously achieves real-time inference latency and coherent, decoupled future representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260271/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single monocular RGB observation frame and a text task instruction as input, producing an 8-step robot action chunk per forward pass (achieving 25 Hz effective control frequency on a single RTX 3090). Internally, the pipeline generates two intermediate foresight representations—dynamic geometric features in DPAv3's representation space and patch-level semantic features in DINOv2's space—alongside the raw one-step diffusion features, all of which are fused before action decoding. The total inference latency is 307.6 ms (231 ms for the SVD backbone, 40 ms for decouplers, 37 ms for the action expert).

## Method

S-VAM introduces a self-distillation shortcut: during training, VFM representations (DPAv3 for geometry, DINOv2 for semantics) extracted from the diffusion model's own multi-step generated videos serve as teacher targets, while lightweight spatio-temporal transformer decouplers (students) learn to map noisy one-step denoising features directly to these targets in a single forward pass. Each decoupler is conditioned on the current observation's VFM representation as a spatial anchor to stabilize the mapping. A QFormer-style Uni-Perceiver then condenses the concatenated geometric foresight, semantic foresight, and original diffusion features into a compact token set, which conditions a DiT-based diffusion policy for action prediction. Training proceeds in three stages: fine-tuning the SVD backbone, self-distilling the decouplers (50k steps, single GPU), and training the action expert with frozen upstream components. The critical design choice is distilling from the model's own predictions rather than ground-truth future frames, preserving trajectory alignment between the one-step features and the teacher targets.

## Results

On CALVIN (ABC→D), S-VAM achieves a state-of-the-art average sequence length of 4.16, surpassing the closest predictive baseline HiF-VLA (4.08) and the direct baseline VPP (3.58) by 0.08 and 0.58 respectively. On MetaWorld (50 tasks, 50 demos each), S-VAM reaches 72.8% average success rate, outperforming VPP (68.2%) by 4.6 points, with the largest gap on hard tasks (68.4% vs. 52.6%). In real-world dual-arm experiments, S-VAM outperforms VPP on all four tasks while maintaining a 25 Hz effective control frequency at 307.6 ms per forward pass (only 15.8% overhead over VPP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-VAM outperforms all 11 baselines on both simulated benchmarks. Against the closest predictive competitor HiF-VLA [26], S-VAM gains 0.08 in CALVIN average length (4.16 vs. 4.08) and 15.1 points on MetaWorld (72.8% vs. 57.7%), attributed to the video diffusion backbone's richer spatiotemporal dynamics versus HiF-VLA's VLM-based approach. The primary direct baseline VPP [14] is surpassed by 0.58 in CALVIN length and 4.6 points on MetaWorld, with the gap widening to 15.8 points on hard MetaWorld tasks where geometric precision is critical. Among direct action learning methods, S-VAM leads Spatial Forcing [19] by 0.22 in CALVIN length and 11.9 points on MetaWorld, π₀ [3] by 0.51 in CALVIN length, and OpenVLA [18] by 0.89 in CALVIN length. On MetaWorld, S-VAM also dominates RT-1 [6] (72.8% vs. 34.6%), Diffusion Policy [9] (72.8% vs. 27.9%), GR-1 [42] (72.8% vs. 57.4%), and SuSIE [4] (72.8% vs. 41.0%), underscoring the advantage of video-generation priors in data-scarce (50-demo) settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Video-Action Model (VAM) paradigm, building directly on VPP's [14] one-step denoising feature extraction and Stable Video Diffusion [5] as the video generation backbone. The key departure is introducing a self-distillation shortcut: rather than accepting the noisy, entangled one-step features at face value (as VPP does) or paying the latency cost of full multi-step generation, S-VAM trains lightweight spatio-temporal transformer decouplers to map those features into coherent DPAv3 geometric and DINOv2 semantic representations. The teacher targets are extracted from the diffusion model's own multi-step generated videos, a self-supervised choice that avoids the trajectory misalignment inherent in using ground-truth future frames. The action expert then fuses these decoupled foresight representations with the original diffusion features via a QFormer-style Uni-Perceiver and a DiT-based diffusion policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S-VAM addresses a fundamental tension in VAMs—real-time inference versus high-fidelity visual foresight—by condensing multi-step generative priors into a single forward pass, achieving 25 Hz control on a single RTX 3090 with only 15.8% overhead over VPP. The self-distillation mechanism (using the model's own predictions as teacher) is a clean and generalizable principle that could transfer to other video generation backbones beyond SVD. However, the performance gains, while consistent, are moderate (e.g., 4.16 vs. 4.08 average length on CALVIN), and the approach remains tied to the SVD architecture and relatively constrained manipulation benchmarks. Open questions include whether the shortcut generalizes to more complex, long-horizon tasks, whether alternative video backbones (e.g., autoregressive or flow-matching models) benefit similarly, and whether the decoupled geometric/semantic foresight can be further exploited for explicit planning or error recovery.
