## Problem

Conventional autoregressive visual generation models use a fixed raster-scan order, which limits them to unidirectional prediction and prevents native image inpainting. Random permutation approaches restore sequence-order flexibility for editing but degrade sampling performance and require complex training strategies like order annealing. The paper asks whether a structured randomization strategy can simultaneously preserve generation quality and enable region-level modification without architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261109/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a class label and produces a 256×256 RGB image by sequentially predicting 256 VQGAN tokens (16×16 patch grid, 1024-codebook, 16× downsampling). For inpainting, it additionally receives a connected set of observed patch tokens as a prefix and completes the remaining masked tokens as a postfix, with the mask constrained to be connected and not covering all four corners. The model is a ViT decoder-only transformer with causal attention, QK LayerNorm, adaLN class conditioning, and learnable positional embeddings that condition each step on the next-token's 2D position.

## Method

STAR replaces the fixed or randomly permuted sequence order with the BFS traversal order of a uniform spanning tree sampled over the 2D patch lattice (a regular taxicab graph), rooted at a randomly chosen corner. The spanning tree is sampled in O(N log N) time via Wilson's algorithm, and the BFS traversal structurally encodes two image priors: locality (adjacent patches are predicted together) and center bias (prediction proceeds from non-salient corners toward the center). For native inpainting, the model performs rejection sampling of spanning trees in the non-masked region such that maximum-depth nodes lie on the mask boundary, then extends the tree into the masked region, guaranteeing the partial observation forms a valid prefix. Training uses standard maximum-likelihood cross-entropy with no annealing schedule, and the only architectural addition beyond a standard decoder-only transformer is a learnable positional embedding for the next-token position.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k at 256×256, STAR-XXL (1.5B) achieves FID 1.55, IS 338.8, Precision 0.81, and Recall 0.62, matching the FID of MAR-H (1.55) while substantially outperforming RandAR-XXL (FID 2.15) and LlamaGen-3B (FID 3.05). For image inpainting, STAR-XL achieves FID 2.07 and IS 111.3, significantly outperforming RandAR-XL (FID 2.58, IS 60.3), RAR-XL (FID 3.40, IS 88.2), and DiT-XL/2 (FID 4.58, IS 50.4). The spanning tree traversal strategy incurs negligible overhead (<0.0004% of inference time) and requires fewer than 5 rejection sampling trials on average for postfix completion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR outperforms RandAR (random permutation AR) by a large margin at every scale: STAR-XXL achieves FID 1.55 vs. RandAR-XXL's 2.15, and IS 338.8 vs. 322.0, confirming that structured spanning-tree traversal is more effective than unstructured random permutation for sequence order randomization. Against raster-scan AR (RAR), STAR-XXL is slightly behind on FID (1.55 vs. 1.48) but ahead on IS (338.8 vs. 326.0), and critically, STAR natively supports image inpainting while RAR does not. In the inpainting comparison (Table 2), STAR-XL (FID 2.07, IS 111.3) substantially outperforms RandAR-XL (FID 2.58, IS 60.3), RAR-XL (FID 3.40, IS 88.2), and DiT-XL/2 (FID 4.58, IS 50.4), demonstrating that bidirectional dependency exposed only during training (RAR) or via noisy partial observations (DiT) is insufficient for native region-level modification. Against block-wise AR (VAR-d30, FID 1.92) and LlamaGen-3B (FID 3.05), STAR-XXL achieves better FID without requiring specialized tokenizers or multi-scale architectures. STAR-XXL ties MAR-H on FID (1.55) but leads on IS (338.8 vs. 303.7), suggesting the discrete VQ + spanning tree approach is competitive with continuous VAE + diffusion-loss bidirectional AR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work in AR visual generation: fixed-order models (raster-scan, as in LlamaGen and RAR) that achieve strong sampling performance but lack flexibility for region-level editing, and random-permutation models (RandAR, MAR) that support native inpainting but sacrifice generation quality. STAR departs from both by replacing the unstructured N! permutation space with the much smaller space of uniform spanning tree traversals (BFS), which structurally encodes image priors (locality, center bias) while retaining the sequence-order diversity needed for postfix completion. The approach requires only the addition of next-token position conditioning to a standard Decoder-only Transformer, avoiding the architectural modifications (block-wise attention, dedicated spatial transformers) or complex training schedules (annealing from permutation to raster-scan) used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAR resolves a practical tension in AR visual generation: the ability to natively perform image inpainting without degrading generation quality or requiring architectural changes, making it a drop-in building block for early-fusion multimodal models that share the Decoder-only Transformer across modalities. The paper's ablations (random permutation as pretext task does not help; BFS vastly outperforms DFS for rejection sampling) provide clear design guidance for future sequence-order strategies. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous VAE tokenizers with diffusion losses, higher resolutions, and whether the representation-learning benefits observed on CIFAR-100 transfer to larger-scale self-supervised settings. The evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet-1k at 256×256, so generalization to open-ended text-to-image or video generation remains untested.
