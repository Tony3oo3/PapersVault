## Problem

Density-based zero-shot object counting methods treat the predicted density map as an unconstrained continuous field, providing no explicit regulation over how density mass is distributed across individual object instances. This leads to spatial ambiguity where mass leaks into background regions or concentrates on discriminative object parts rather than stable instance centers, producing fragmented representations and inflated counting error in cluttered or geometrically complex scenes.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image at arbitrary resolution and a free-form text query naming the target category, with no visual exemplars required at inference. It outputs a full-resolution density map (H×W), a variable-size set of instance center proposals extracted via NMS on a sigmoid confidence map with a fixed threshold τ, and a scalar count estimate obtained by integrating the density map. The number of predicted points M varies per image and is not fixed a priori.

## Method

DualCount employs a dual-decoder head over shared cross-modal features (enhanced via bidirectional cross-attention and feature-wise text modulation) that simultaneously predicts a density map and a point confidence map. The key mechanism is a soft instance-wise decomposition of the density map using Gaussian-kernel assignment weights centered on predicted points, which transforms density estimation into a structured mass allocation problem. Two geometric constraints are then enforced on the decomposed components: per-instance mass conservation (each instance receives approximately one unit of density mass) and center-of-mass alignment (each component's centroid is pulled toward its predicted center). Training combines an optimal-transport density loss, focal loss for point detection, an InfoNCE contrastive loss on multi-scale point features, a count consistency loss penalizing disagreement between density-integrated count and point count, and the two geometric losses above.

## Results

On FSC-147 (zero-shot), DualCount with SwinT+BERT achieves 11.21 MAE / 42.77 RMSE on validation and 11.32 MAE / 97.16 RMSE on test, setting new state-of-the-art. On PUCPR+, it reaches 24.27 MAE / 28.91 RMSE, surpassing the previous best (CountGD: 24.31 / 32.16). On CARPK, CountGD retains the lead (3.83 MAE vs. DualCount's 7.95), but DualCount still outperforms all other zero-shot baselines (CLIP-Count, CountTX, T2ICount).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FSC-147 (zero-shot test), DualCount (SwinT+BERT) achieves 11.32 MAE, outperforming the previous best T2ICount (11.76) by 0.44 MAE and CountGD (12.98) by 1.66 MAE; on validation it leads CountGD by 0.93 MAE (11.21 vs. 12.14) and T2ICount by 2.57 MAE. On PUCPR+, DualCount edges out CountGD with 24.27 MAE / 28.91 RMSE versus 24.31 / 32.16, and substantially beats VLCounter (48.94 MAE) and CLIP-Count (55.82 MAE). On CARPK, CountGD remains the strongest method (3.83 MAE / 5.41 RMSE), with DualCount second at 7.95 / 9.78, still ahead of T2ICount (8.61 / 13.47), CountTX (8.13 / 10.87), and CLIP-Count (11.96 / 16.61). The CLIP-backbone variant of DualCount (12.33 MAE) is competitive with CountGD but trails the SwinT+BERT variant by 1.01 MAE, confirming the benefit of the stronger encoder. Overall, DualCount wins on 2 of 3 datasets and is competitive on the third, establishing a clear advantage in the zero-shot setting across diverse object categories and camera viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualCount builds directly on the author's prior work in optimal-transport-based density supervision (ICTC 2025) and contrastive point-feature matching for open-world counting (BMVC 2025), combining both into a unified dual-decoder framework. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant zero-shot counting paradigm (CLIP-Count, VLCounter, CountGD, T2ICount) that treats density estimation as unconstrained pixel-wise regression conditioned on text embeddings. The key departure is reformulating density prediction as a structured mass-allocation problem over a latent set of instances, enforced through per-instance mass conservation and center-of-mass alignment constraints that couple the density and point branches geometrically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The instance-level geometric constraints offer a principled way to reduce background leakage and part-biased density drift that plagues density-based counting in cluttered scenes, and the formulation could transfer to other density-map prediction tasks (pose estimation, repetition counting) as the authors note. However, the method still trails CountGD by a wide margin on CARPK (7.95 vs. 3.83 MAE), suggesting the structural constraints may not fully address the challenges of aerial vehicle counting with regular spatial layouts. An open question is whether the Gaussian-kernel soft decomposition scales gracefully to very high instance counts (thousands per image) where the M-way assignment becomes computationally and geometrically ill-conditioned.
