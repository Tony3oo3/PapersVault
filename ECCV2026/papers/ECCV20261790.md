## Problem

Existing multi-view human mesh recovery methods either rely on cumbersome camera calibration (geometry-based triangulation/volumetric fusion) or generalize poorly to unseen camera configurations because learning-based fusion modules memorize dataset-specific camera layouts from limited multi-view training data. The paper asks whether pretrained single-view HMR models, trained on massive-scale data with diverse viewpoints, can serve as strong enough priors to enable calibration-free multi-view reconstruction without any multi-view training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261790/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view RGB images (typically 4–9 cameras) along with 2D detections from a pretrained DensePose-based anatomical landmark detector that provides 44 joint keypoints and 35 surface landmarks per view, each with a confidence score (only detections above 0.9 are retained). The output is a unified 3D human mesh parameterized by SMPL pose (θ ∈ R^72) and shape (β ∈ R^10) from a single 'virtual view' representation, plus 3D joint positions. The framework operates in both calibrated and calibration-free modes; in the latter, global orientation is excluded from cross-view averaging since per-view orientations lack a shared coordinate frame.

## Method

The framework is a training-free test-time adaptation (TTA) pipeline that leverages frozen pretrained single-view HMR backbones (HMR2.0, CameraHMR, or TokenHMR) as strong priors. It introduces a 'virtual view'—an optimizable SMPL parameter set initialized via a 1-δ reliability-filtered weighted average across per-view predictions—and jointly optimizes this virtual view alongside the learned SMPL tokens within each single-view backbone (gradients flow only through the final MLP, not the full transformer). Refinement is driven by three losses: a 2D reprojection loss against anatomical landmarks and keypoints, a cross-view consistency loss enforcing pairwise agreement in pose, shape, and mesh geometry, and a regularization loss anchoring updates to the initial single-view predictions. The key design choice is optimizing compact learned SMPL tokens rather than full model weights (too high-dimensional) or raw SMPL parameters (too low-dimensional and prone to local minima), which ablations confirm yields the best accuracy and stability across all three backbones.

## Results

On Human3.6M with camera calibration, the CameraHMR backbone achieves 26.9 MPJPE and 20.6 PA-MPJPE, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art HeatFormer (29.5/22.4) and all other multi-view baselines. In the calibration-free setting, the same backbone reaches 32.7 MPJPE / 22.0 PA-MPJPE, substantially surpassing HeatFormer (42.5/25.8) and PaFF (44.8/28.2). On MPI-INF-3DHP, CameraHMR achieves 39.0 MPJPE, 99.9 PCK, and 83.8 AUC, leading all multi-view methods. Under cross-camera evaluation (unseen camera set), the method attains 43.71 MPJPE and 80.62 AUC versus HeatFormer's 45.74 MPJPE and 68.93 AUC, demonstrating strong generalization to novel camera configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With camera calibration on Human3.6M, Ours (CameraHMR) achieves 26.9 MPJPE / 20.6 PA-MPJPE, beating HeatFormer (29.5/22.4) by 2.6/1.8 mm, PaFF (33.0/26.9) by 6.1/6.3 mm, and the volumetric methods MV-SPIN (49.8/35.4) and MuVS (58.2/47.1) by large margins. In the calibration-free setting, the method (32.7/22.0) outperforms HeatFormer (42.5/25.8) by 9.8/3.8 mm and PaFF (44.8/28.2) by 12.1/6.2 mm, though U-HMR (31.0/22.8) edges it slightly on MPJPE while the method wins on PA-MPJPE. On MPI-INF-3DHP, CameraHMR (39.0 MPJPE, 99.9 PCK, 83.8 AUC) surpasses HeatFormer (39.8/99.5/72.8), U-HMR (39.7/99.4/74.0), and PaFF (48.4/98.6/67.3) across all three metrics. Under cross-camera generalization (unseen camera set 1,4,5,6 vs. training set 0,2,7,8), the method (43.71 MPJPE, 80.62 AUC) outperforms HeatFormer (45.74 MPJPE, 68.93 AUC) by 2.03 mm and 11.69 AUC points, confirming that the geometry-driven TTA paradigm generalizes to novel camera configurations without multi-view training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of learning-based multi-view HMR (e.g., UHMR, HeatFormer, PaFF), which fuses multi-view features via trained networks and consequently memorizes dataset-specific camera configurations. Instead, it repurposes pretrained single-view transformer backbones (HMR2.0, CameraHMR, TokenHMR) as frozen priors and introduces a training-free test-time adaptation loop that optimizes a lightweight 'virtual view' representation and learned SMPL tokens under 2D anatomical landmark supervision and cross-view consistency. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between geometry-based multi-view methods (which require calibration and suffer from quantization) and purely data-driven fusion (which fails on unseen cameras) by performing explicit 3D geometric reasoning at inference time without any multi-view training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: deploying multi-view HMR in the wild no longer requires collecting and training on multi-view data for each camera layout, nor does it require precise camera calibration. The calibration-free result (32.7 MPJPE vs. HeatFormer's 42.5) and the cross-camera generalization (Table 3) demonstrate that the approach addresses a real deployment bottleneck. Open questions include scalability to larger numbers of views and multi-person scenes, the inference cost of 200 TTA steps per frame, and whether the fine-tuned 2D detector (Synthpose) introduces a hidden dependency on dataset-specific pseudo-GT annotations that could limit true zero-shot transfer.
