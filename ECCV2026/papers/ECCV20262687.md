## Problem

3D Morphable Models (3DMMs) inherit morphological biases from their finite training data, yet standard evaluation metrics based on Euclidean distances (NMSE, RMSE) are insensitive to localized geometric failures such as lost ridges or unnatural creases. No prior work has systematically discovered, quantified, or visualized demographic biases in 3D face reconstruction, leaving a critical gap in a field increasingly regulated as high-risk under frameworks like the EU AI Act.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes pairs of rigidly ICP-aligned triangle meshes (ground truth scan and 3DMM-based reconstruction) along with demographic labels (age, gender, ethnicity) and anatomical region masks (nose, mouth, cheek, forehead) from the REALY benchmark. It produces per-vertex curvature error maps, a scalar Curvature Reconstruction Error (CRE) aggregated via a mass-weighted L1 norm, K-means clusters of spectral error coefficients representing distinct failure modes, and Pearson correlation coefficients linking reconstruction error to demographic attributes per anatomical region.

## Method

The core mechanism estimates mean curvature at each vertex by locally fitting a quadratic bivariate surface over an R-ring neighborhood via least squares, then computing the Laplace-Beltrami operator as a sparse matrix from the resulting polynomial coefficients; the curvature reconstruction error is the per-vertex difference in mean curvature between ground truth and reconstruction. For bias discovery, error maps are projected into a low-dimensional spectral space spanned by the first K eigenfunctions of the cotangent-weighted Laplace-Beltrami operator on the 3DMM template, reducing the problem from |V| dimensions to K ≪ |V|, after which K-means clustering identifies recurring geometric failure modes. A user study with 19 annotators validates that CRE achieves 73.63% pairwise perceptual accuracy versus ~50% for NMSE variants. The framework is applied to BFM and FLAME bases and five 2D-to-3D reconstruction algorithms (Deep3D, 3DDFA-v3, MGCNet, DECA, MICA), revealing strong age-related bias (Pearson r up to 0.88) and preliminary evidence of ethnicity- and gender-related systematic errors.

## Results

The proposed Curvature Reconstruction Error (CRE) metric achieves 73.63% pairwise accuracy and 82.66% ROC-AUC in a user study with 19 annotators, substantially outperforming all NMSE-based baselines which hover near random chance (42–54% pairwise accuracy). In the bias analysis, strong positive Pearson correlations between subject age and CRE are found across nearly all anatomical regions for both BFM (r = 0.77 on cheek) and FLAME (r = 0.82 on cheek), confirming systematic age-related representation gaps. The same age bias propagates to all five evaluated 2D-to-3D reconstruction methods (Deep3D, 3DDFA-v3, MGCNet, DECA, MICA), with cheek correlations ranging from 0.69 to 0.80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRE decisively outperforms all three NMSE variants in the perceptual user study: it surpasses E_NMSE^REALY (53.92%) by ~20 points, E_NMSE^pts (49.32%) by ~24 points, and E_NMSE^vtx (42.03%) by ~32 points in pairwise accuracy, with ROC-AUC gaps of 26–40 points. The NMSE baselines perform at or below random-chance level for binary choices, confirming that Euclidean distances are poorly aligned with human geometric perception. In the bias analysis (Table 2), both 3DMMs (BFM, FLAME) and all five 2D-to-3D reconstruction methods (Deep3D, 3DDFA-v3, MGCNet, DECA, MICA) exhibit strong age-related CRE correlations (r ≥ 0.5) on the cheek region, indicating the bias is inherited from the 3DMM prior rather than introduced by the fitting algorithm. Notably, the age bias is not exacerbated by the predictive 2D-to-3D models, and gender-stratified analysis reveals the effect is intensified in males under BFM (r = 0.88 on cheek). For ethnicity, clustering of spectral error maps shows African and Asian subjects are disproportionately concentrated in specific failure-mode clusters for both BFM and FLAME, whereas Caucasian subjects are distributed more evenly across clusters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Laplace-Beltrami operator and spectral decomposition—tools well-established in differential geometry and shape analysis—into a fairness evaluation framework for 3D face reconstruction. It departs from the standard evaluation paradigm (NMSE/RMSE via ICP alignment, as used in REALY and M3DFB) by introducing a curvature-based error metric that captures local surface character invisible to Euclidean distances. The bias-detection task extends prior 2D facial recognition fairness studies and a limited 3D attempt [10] by grounding the analysis in geometric frequencies rather than global point-to-surface distances, and by using spectral clustering to discover previously unknown failure modes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard Euclidean metrics show no significant demographic bias while curvature-based metrics reveal strong age-related disparities (r up to 0.88) is practically critical: it means the field's current evaluation protocols are systematically masking representation failures that the EU AI Act now requires to be declared. The bias is shown to be inherent to the 3DMM linear basis rather than the 2D-to-3D fitting algorithm, implying that fixing reconstruction networks alone will not resolve the problem. Open questions include whether the curvature error can serve as a differentiable refinement loss, how to expand the evaluation beyond 100 faces to gain statistical power on ethnicity (only ~10–20 samples per group), and whether non-linear or hierarchical shape priors can close the identified morphological gaps.
