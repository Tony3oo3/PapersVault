## Problem

Existing location encoders project coordinates onto fixed positional bases (spherical harmonics, Fourier features) that allocate representational capacity uniformly across the sphere, devoting equal resources to open ocean and dense urban areas. Because the spatial structure is determined before training, these bases cannot adapt to the geographic complexity of the underlying signal, creating a practical bottleneck for fine-grained spatial discrimination.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single latitude-longitude coordinate, projects it onto the unit sphere S², and produces a 512-dimensional location embedding with no imagery required. During contrastive pretraining, the coordinate is paired with a co-located Sentinel-2 satellite image encoded by a frozen MoCo-pretrained ViT; the image embedding enters the contrastive objective directly and supervises the semantic tokens. The final embedding is a LayerNorm-fused combination of the Voronoi soft-assignment output (projected to 512 dims) and the token-attended representation, with a fixed mixing weight α=0.5.

## Method

TTE replaces fixed positional bases with 4,096 learnable Spherical Voronoi sites on S², each carrying a 384-dimensional embedding and a per-site temperature that controls partition sharpness; sites are initialized on a Fibonacci lattice filtered to land and migrate during training to concentrate capacity on discriminative regions like coastlines and ecological boundaries. To bridge local spatial structure with global semantic understanding, the model introduces 64 shared learnable concept tokens (512-dim) over which both the location path and the frozen image encoder compute attention; the image path uses a fixed low temperature (0.05) producing peaked, nearly one-hot attention, while the location path learns to predict this distribution from coordinates alone. Training jointly optimizes a symmetric contrastive loss between location and image embeddings, a reconstruction loss requiring tokens to span the image feature space, and a KL-divergence alignment loss that pulls location token attention toward the image-side target. The ablation confirms that learnable site migration is the single most critical component (−12.9% classification when fixed), and that the peaked image-side attention provides a sharper alignment target than symmetric soft attention.

## Results

TTE achieves the highest average classification accuracy (80.0%) and average regression R² (0.777) across the geospatial benchmark suite, improving over the previous best (TaxaBind: 77.2% cls, GeoCLIP: 0.732 reg) by 2.8% and 0.045 respectively. The largest individual gains are on Biome (77.8% vs. 72.3% for TaxaBind), Country (94.8% vs. 88.3% for SINR), and Elevation (R²=0.839 vs. 0.666 for SatCLIP). On iNaturalist-2018 fine-grained species classification, TTE as a geographic prior lifts Top-1 accuracy from 66.1% (image-only) to 76.2%, outperforming SatCLIP (75.1%) and TaxaBind (75.1%) across all Top-k metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTE outperforms all baselines on average classification (80.0% vs. 77.2% TaxaBind, 74.4% GeoCLIP) and average regression R² (0.777 vs. 0.732 GeoCLIP, 0.719 TaxaBind). The largest margins are on Country classification (+6.5 over SINR's 88.3%), Biome (+5.5 over TaxaBind's 72.3%), and Elevation (+0.173 over SatCLIP's 0.666). TTE loses on two tasks: EcoRegion (67.4% vs. TaxaBind's 72.9%, where TaxaBind's encoder is directly aligned to iNaturalist species data) and California Housing (R²=0.532 vs. GeoCLIP's 0.708, where GeoCLIP's ground-level imagery better captures street-level cues in Western countries). On iNaturalist-2018, TTE as a geographic prior beats TaxaBind (76.2% vs. 75.1% Top-1) and SatCLIP (76.2% vs. 75.1%), despite TaxaBind's encoder being explicitly trained on species data. The ablation confirms the learnable Voronoi sites are critical: fixing sites at initialization drops classification by 12.9% and regression by 0.125, falling below SatCLIP on several tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TTE adapts the differentiable Spherical Voronoi partition of Di Sario et al. [10]—originally designed for view-dependent appearance modeling—to geographic representation learning, replacing the fixed positional bases (spherical harmonics, multi-scale Fourier features, random Fourier features) used by SatCLIP, GeoCLIP, CSP, and Sphere2Vec with a fully learnable spatial decomposition. The global semantic tokens repurpose the learnable-query mechanism from ViT, Set Transformer, and register-token work, but redirect it from intra-encoder aggregation to cross-modal semantic anchoring between a frozen image encoder and the location encoder. The contrastive pretraining protocol follows SatCLIP's setup with Sentinel-2 imagery and a MoCo-pretrained ViT, keeping the comparison controlled while the architectural novelty lies entirely in the spatial partitioning and token mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ablation showing that freezing Voronoi sites at their initial Fibonacci-lattice positions causes the single largest performance drop (−12.9% classification) provides concrete evidence that uniform spatial allocation is a practical bottleneck for location encoders, not merely a theoretical limitation of spherical harmonics. The emergent behavior of sites migrating offshore to dedicate receptive fields to narrow coastal segments, and tokens spontaneously specializing in coherent environmental concepts (snow, desert, rocky terrain) without explicit supervision, suggests the architecture captures genuine geographic structure. Open questions include whether multi-temporal conditioning would close the gap on temporally sensitive tasks like housing prediction, whether the Voronoi-plus-tokens pattern generalizes to other spherical domains (planetary science, molecular surfaces), and how the approach scales if site counts must grow for finer-grained applications.
