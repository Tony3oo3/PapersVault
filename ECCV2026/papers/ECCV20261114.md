## Problem

Training-free, prompt-guided image editing remains largely unexplored for visual autoregressive (VAR) models, despite their competitive generation quality and substantially faster inference compared to diffusion models. The core obstacle is that VAR models sample discrete tokens via the Gumbel-max trick, whose argmax operator is non-invertible, so standard continuous noise-inversion strategies cannot be directly applied to recover a controllable editing representation from a source image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261114/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a source RGB image, a source text prompt describing it, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit, VARIN encodes the source image into K multi-scale token maps via the VAR encoder, inverts Gumbel noise at each scale, then regenerates token scales from a chosen starting scale s (default 5) under the target prompt and decodes the result back to an RGB image. The entire pipeline is training-free and completes in approximately 0.65 seconds per image on a single A100 GPU, with no iterative latent optimization.

## Method

VARIN introduces Location-aware Argmax Inversion (LAI), a pseudo-inverse of the argmax operator that recovers the Gumbel noise used during discrete autoregressive sampling. For each token position, LAI samples the winning class's logit from a Gumbel distribution centered at the model's predicted log probability, then samples all other classes' logits from a truncated Gumbel with threshold q_max − τ, where τ controls how much source-image bias is retained in the recovered noise. During editing, the recovered inverse noise n_t is interpolated with fresh Gumbel noise g via a coefficient λ that starts at 1 at the editing scale and decays logarithmically to 0 at the final scale, allowing the model to progressively follow the target prompt while preserving unedited background structure. This design satisfies two key properties: the recovered noise remains close to standard Gumbel (ensuring generative diversity) while retaining controllable source-image bias (ensuring background preservation), outperforming the simpler one-hot argmax inversion used in DICE.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, VARIN achieves a Structure Distance of 11.54×10³, the best among all autoregressive and discrete editing methods, and competitive with the strongest diffusion inversion baselines (PnP Inversion: 11.65, Null-text Inversion: 13.44). For prompt alignment, VARIN attains the highest Whole CLIP score (25.59) among all discrete/autoregressive methods and the second-highest overall (behind only DDPM-Inversion at 26.22). Background preservation is strong with SSIM of 85.27×10² and LPIPS of 54.62×10³, while the entire editing pipeline runs in 0.65 s per image on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the autoregressive baselines, VARIN dominates: it reduces Structure Distance from 25.56 (Regeneration/AREdit) to 11.54 and improves Edited CLIP from 21.13 to 22.35, while also substantially outperforming EditAR (39.43 Structure Distance, 21.87 Edited CLIP). Among discrete methods, VARIN trades a small background-preservation gap with DICE (PSNR 26.47 vs 27.29, SSIM 85.27 vs 89.79) for a clear prompt-alignment advantage (Whole CLIP 25.59 vs 23.79, Edited CLIP 22.35 vs 21.23). Versus the strongest diffusion inversion baselines, VARIN matches or beats PnP Inversion on Structure Distance (11.54 vs 11.65) and CLIP scores, and outperforms Null-text Inversion and InfEdit on Structure Distance, though it trails DDPM-Inversion on CLIP alignment (25.59 vs 26.22 Whole, 22.35 vs 23.02 Edited). VARIN also substantially outperforms attention-manipulation methods (Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl) and instruction-tuned methods (InstructPix2Pix, MGIE) on every structural and background metric. The key practical advantage is speed: VARIN completes editing in 0.65 s per image, orders of magnitude faster than iterative diffusion inversion methods that require dozens of denoising steps plus optional optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noise-inversion-for-editing paradigm established in diffusion models (SDEdit, Null-text Inversion, P2P) to the visual autoregressive (VAR) setting, where the non-invertible argmax operator in Gumbel-max sampling prevents direct inversion. It builds directly on DICE, which introduced a one-hot argmax inversion for discrete MaskGiT models, but identifies that this approach produces uncontrollable noise for VAR's multi-scale generation. The key methodological departure is Location-aware Argmax Inversion (LAI), which uses the model's predicted logits as the Gumbel location parameter to produce inverse noises that are both distributionally faithful and carry source-image bias, enabling controllable editing in the next-scale autoregressive framework of HART.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VARIN provides the first practical, training-free editing pipeline for VAR-based models, achieving competitive quality with diffusion inversion methods (Null-text Inversion, P2P) at 0.65 seconds per image, which is orders of magnitude faster than iterative diffusion editing. This makes real-time text-guided editing feasible for the growing family of next-scale autoregressive generators (HART, Switti, VAR). Limitations include difficulty with substantial pose or structural modifications, and the method's reliance on a source caption that must align with the source image. Open questions include whether LAI generalizes to other discrete autoregressive architectures and whether combining VARIN with optimization-based or attention-manipulation techniques (EditAR, P2P-style) can push editing quality further.
