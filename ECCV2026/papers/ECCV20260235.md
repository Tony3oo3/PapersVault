## Problem

Open-vocabulary scene understanding from sparse satellite views remains unsolved: per-scene optimization methods (e.g., LangSplatV2, Feature-3DGS) are computationally prohibitive and require dense observations, while generalizable 3DGS methods (e.g., LSM, Uni3R) assume standard pinhole cameras and fail under the pushbroom imaging mode, limited geometric constraints, and extreme scale variations inherent to satellite imagery. No prior work integrates the RPC camera model into a feed-forward language 3DGS pipeline for satellite data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260235/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse-view satellite image crops (256×256 for DFC19/SpaceNet4, 768×768 for MVS3D) along with their corresponding RPC camera models (refined via bundle adjustment) and a free-form text prompt. It produces a language-embedded 3D Gaussian field in a single feed-forward pass, from which it renders novel views, extracts DSM-level height maps, and generates open-vocabulary segmentation masks at both 2D (per-view) and 3D (object-space, via RPC projection with per-grid argmax) levels. The entire pipeline is self-supervised, requiring no ground-truth geometry or semantic annotations.

## Method

SkySplat-OV constructs RPC-based cost volumes by warping multi-view CNN/Transformer features across candidate heights using the RPC model, then regresses per-pixel height maps from the resulting probability volumes. A Geometry-Radiance-Semantics Fusion (GRS-Fusion) module concatenates geometric features (cost volume, predicted height, confidence), radiometric features (multi-view images), and semantic features (frozen SAM3 features plus CNN/Transformer features) and processes them through a lightweight 2D U-Net to produce unified fusion features that drive all Gaussian parameter heads (center via RPC unprojection, covariance, color, opacity, and language-aligned semantic embedding). SAM3 features are distilled into the 3DGS field in a single stage, and the rendered language features are fed back into SAM3's FPN and cross-modal decoder for open-vocabulary segmentation. Training is fully self-supervised with three complementary losses: RGB reconstruction (MSE + LPIPS), language feature alignment (MSE + cosine similarity against SAM3), and relative height alignment (Pearson correlation against DA3 depth estimates), eliminating the need for any ground-truth supervision.

## Results

On DFC19, SkySplat-OV achieves mIoU 48.12%, mF1 62.42%, PSNR 17.63 dB, SSIM 0.531, and LPIPS 0.443, outperforming the best generalizable 3DGS baseline Uni3R (mIoU 22.20%) by 25.92 percentage points. On SpaceNet4, it reaches mIoU 51.02% and mF1 67.40%, surpassing the strongest 2D OV baseline SegEarth-OV (mIoU 38.11%) by 12.91 points. In cross-dataset evaluation on MVS3D, SkySplat-OV reduces geometric MAE from 18.01 m (HiSplat) to 4.28 m while achieving mIoU 43.22%. Against per-scene optimization methods, SkySplat-OV completes scene understanding in 9.82 s versus 2.92 h for LangSplatV2 (~1070× speedup) while improving mean mIoU from 22.71% to 42.15% across eight AOIs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkySplat-OV wins on every metric against all 11 baselines across both datasets. Against generalizable 3DGS methods (LSM-2V/3V, Uni3R), it gains 25.92 and 12.91 mIoU points on DFC19 and SpaceNet4 respectively, with Uni3R being the closest competitor at mIoU 22.20% vs. 48.12%. Against 2D open-vocabulary baselines (ProxyCLIP, Trident, SegEarth-OV), which rely on HiSplat-rendered novel views, SkySplat-OV improves mIoU by 25.75–28.04 points on DFC19 and 12.91–16.94 points on SpaceNet4, while also delivering superior photometric quality (PSNR 17.63 vs. 16.88 dB). Against per-scene optimization methods (S-NeRF, Sat-NeRF, EOGS, Feature-3DGS, LangSplatV2), SkySplat-OV achieves lower MAE (2.77 m mean vs. 4.68 m best baseline) and higher mIoU (42.15% vs. 22.71%) while running ~1070× faster than LangSplatV2 (9.82 s vs. 2.92 h). HiSplat, used as the rendering backbone for 2D baselines, is also outperformed on all photometric metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), confirming that the RPC-based geometry reconstruction contributes to better novel-view synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkySplat-OV sits at the intersection of three research threads: generalizable 3DGS (MVSplat, LSM, Uni3R, HiSplat), open-vocabulary scene understanding via feature distillation (Feature-3DGS, LangSplatV2, SemanticSplat), and satellite photogrammetry (S-NeRF, Sat-NeRF, EOGS, SkySplat). Its key departure is the explicit integration of the RPC camera model into a feed-forward language-3DGS pipeline, which none of the prior generalizable methods support, combined with a GRS-Fusion module and single-stage SAM3 distillation to handle the unique challenges of pushbroom satellite imaging (limited geometric constraints, extreme scale variation). The authors' prior SkySplat work provides the geometric backbone, while this paper adds the semantic and open-vocabulary capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1070× speedup over per-scene optimization methods (9.82 s vs. hours) makes open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding from satellite imagery practically deployable at scale, which was previously infeasible. The strong cross-dataset generalization (US training → Argentina testing, with MAE dropping from ~18 m to ~4.3 m) suggests the RPC-based formulation captures transferable geometric priors rather than dataset-specific artifacts. Open questions remain: SAM3-based segmentation struggles with visually ambiguous satellite categories, height estimation degrades in highly reflective regions, and it is unclear how the approach performs with even fewer than 2–3 input views or at coarser GSDs.
