## Problem

State-of-the-art end-to-end driving models trained on existing benchmarks (nuScenes, nuPlan, etc.) suffer severe performance degradation under diverse real-world atmospheric conditions because those datasets are dominated by clear-weather, uniform-road, limited-maneuver scenarios. This homogeneity causes models to overfit to over-represented conditions and fail on the long tail of fog, rain, snow, glare, dust, and darkness encountered in deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261501/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes six synchronized RGB camera views (two forward at 30°/120° FoV plus four side/rear at 120° FoV), the ego-vehicle's historical trajectory over the preceding 2 seconds, and high-level navigation commands. The BEV planning space is quantized into a discrete grid that forms a token vocabulary; the VLM autoregressively emits a sequence of K coordinate tokens representing future waypoints over the next 3 seconds, supervised by cross-entropy. A secondary output is natural-language VQA answers over scene-graph-derived questions, enabling vision-language-action research.

## Method

The framework is built on a VLM backbone (Qwen) and reformulates trajectory prediction as token generation over a quantized BEV grid. The core contribution is an Atmospheric-Invariant Feature Learning mechanism with two auxiliary losses that anchor visual feature learning to condition-invariant cues. First, Driving-Intent Anchored Learning computes pairwise trajectory similarity from normalized direction vectors (isolating turning/accelerating patterns from absolute position) and minimizes a soft-weighted KL divergence between these intent weights and the cosine-similarity distribution of visual features. Second, Scene-Structure Anchored Learning uses a frozen DINOv3 expert to extract atmosphere-agnostic structural features, converts pairwise expert similarities into Gaussian-kernel weights, and distills this similarity structure into the primary visual encoder via another weighted KL term. The total objective is L_traj + λ_int·L_int + λ_str·L_str, and at inference the model predicts trajectories directly from observations with no additional overhead.

## Results

On DiverseAD (all atmospheric conditions), the proposed method achieves an average L2 displacement error of 1.24 m (0.53 m at 1 s, 1.21 m at 2 s, 1.99 m at 3 s), outperforming the best fine-tuned baseline FSDrive* (1.44 m) by 14%. It also attains 72.68% VQA accuracy, surpassing FSDrive* (65.33%) and OmniDrive* (64.51%). On nuScenes, the model trained on DiverseAD achieves 0.39 m average L2 error and 0.16% average collision rate, matching World4Drive on collision rate while beating it on L2 (0.50 m) and OmniDrive (0.40 m L2, 0.35% collision).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DiverseAD, the proposed method outperforms all five fine-tuned VLM-based baselines (UniAD*, VAD*, OpenEMMA*, OmniDrive*, FSDrive*) on average L2 error, with the largest margin over UniAD* (1.24 m vs. 2.11 m, a 41% reduction) and a consistent 0.10–0.20 m advantage over the closest competitor FSDrive*. In VQA, the method leads by 7.35 points over FSDrive* and 8.17 points over OmniDrive*, indicating stronger scene comprehension under diverse weather. On nuScenes, the model achieves the best average L2 (0.39 m) among all 12 baselines, edging out OmniDrive (0.40 m) and World4Drive (0.50 m), while tying World4Drive on average collision rate (0.16%) and substantially outperforming OmniDrive (0.35%) and FSDrive (0.23%). Notably, without fine-tuning on DiverseAD, all baselines suffer catastrophic degradation (e.g., UniAD jumps from 1.03 m on nuScenes to 31.17 m on DiverseAD), whereas the proposed method maintains sub-1.3 m errors across all nine atmospheric conditions, demonstrating the core robustness claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based end-to-end driving paradigm established by EMMA, OmniDrive, and FSDrive, using Qwen as the backbone to predict trajectories as discrete coordinate tokens. Its key departure is the introduction of an Atmospheric-Invariant Feature Learning mechanism that explicitly disentangles task-relevant invariants (driving intent, scene structure) from atmospheric variants via two soft-weighted KL-divergence losses, rather than relying on data augmentation or image restoration. The scene-structure component borrows the knowledge-distillation idea of aligning features to a frozen vision foundation model (DINOv3), while the driving-intent component is novel in using normalized trajectory direction vectors as soft similarity labels. The accompanying DiverseAD dataset positions itself against existing benchmarks (nuScenes, nuPlan, ACDC, K-Radar) by offering substantially greater atmospheric and road-type diversity at a comparable or larger scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 150K-scene DiverseAD dataset with nine atmospheric conditions, fourteen road types, and VQA annotations fills a clear gap in the driving benchmark landscape, where most planning-capable datasets (nuScenes, nuPlan) are collected under near-ideal conditions. The striking performance collapse of prior SOTA methods under adverse conditions (e.g., UniAD L2 error jumping from ~1 m to ~50 m in darkness) validates the dataset's motivation and suggests that current models' robustness is far weaker than benchmark numbers imply. Open questions include whether the atmospheric-invariant mechanism generalizes to truly unseen conditions (e.g., extreme blizzards, volcanic ash), whether the single-vehicle, single-region collection limits geographic diversity, and whether the VQA annotation pipeline scales reliably to new scenes without manual template redesign.
