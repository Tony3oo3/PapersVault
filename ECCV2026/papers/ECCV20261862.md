## Problem

Multi-perturbation adversarial training (MAT) suffers from robustness trade-offs because different ℓ_p threats induce conflicting optimization objectives within a single model pathway. While mixture-of-experts (MoE) could route threats through distinct pathways, naive MoE application fails: experts redundantly capture threat-common features, and gating networks learn nearly identical routing patterns across threats (threat-agnostic routing), preventing the construction of truly threat-specific model pathways.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261862/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100/1K) and outputs class labels. During training, each image is perturbed under multiple ℓ_p norms (ℓ_1, ℓ_2, ℓ_∞) with specified budgets, and the model is trained to be robust across all threats simultaneously. At inference, the gating network automatically predicts the optimal expert combination per token without requiring knowledge of the input threat type, enabling generalization to unseen threats including non-ℓ_p attacks.

## Method

RoME implements each expert as a low-rank (LoRA-style) additive update to a shared backbone, so the backbone captures threat-common features while experts specialize in threat-specific information. To resolve threat-agnostic routing, RoME introduces dual-scale gating that combines local patch-level features (capturing sparse ℓ_1 perturbations) and global image-level features (capturing uniform ℓ_∞ perturbations), with a layer-adaptive coefficient that emphasizes global gating in early Transformer layers and local gating in deeper layers. A threat-guided gating diversification loss then maximizes pairwise Euclidean distances between threat-specific average gating patterns at both local and global scales, explicitly supervising the gating network to assign distinct expert combinations to each threat. The full training objective combines the base MAT loss (MAX or RANDOM aggregation over per-threat adversarial losses) with the diversification regularization, and the framework is modular enough to plug into existing MAT methods and non-ℓ_p adversarial training approaches.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under PGD, RoME+RANDOM achieves the best natural accuracy (88.1%) and average robustness (56.1%), while RoME+MAX achieves the best union robustness (42.3%), improving over baseline MAX by 3.8%p. On ImageNet-100 under APGD, RoME+MAX reaches 42.8% union robustness, a 3.0%p gain over MAX (39.8%). On ImageNet-1K, RoME improves union robustness by 1.7%p over both RANDOM and MAX under PGD. The method also achieves the highest natural accuracy across all settings, e.g., 88.1% on CIFAR-10 (PGD) vs. 86.7% for RANDOM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoME consistently outperforms all eight baselines on union robustness across both datasets and both attack types (PGD and APGD). Against RANDOM [42], RoME+RANDOM gains +2.5%p union robustness on CIFAR-10 (PGD) and +3.5%p on ImageNet-100 (PGD), while also achieving the highest natural accuracy (88.1% vs. 86.7%). Against MAX [59], RoME+MAX improves union robustness by +3.8%p on CIFAR-10 (PGD) and +3.0%p on ImageNet-100 (APGD). Against RAMP [28], the strongest APGD baseline, RoME+MAX achieves +1.2%p union robustness on CIFAR-10 (43.7% vs. 42.5%) and +1.0%p on ImageNet-100 (42.8% vs. 41.8%). Against MSD [44] and AVG [59], RoME gains +4.0%p and +4.4%p union robustness respectively on CIFAR-10 (PGD). Against MORE [5], the only other MoE-based MAT method, RoME achieves substantially higher robustness (e.g., 42.3% vs. 31.1% union on CIFAR-10 PGD) with far fewer parameters (88.85M vs. 340.60M). Against E-AT [9], RoME+MAX outperforms on union robustness by +4.0%p on CIFAR-10 (APGD). For unseen threats (Table 2), RoME+PAT+VR outperforms PAT [33] and VR [13] by up to +6.8%p on StAdv and +4.7%p on LPA, confirming broader generalization beyond ℓₚ threats.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoME repurposes LoRA-style low-rank additive updates (originally for parameter-efficient fine-tuning) as the expert mechanism in a mixture-of-experts framework, departing from conventional MoE that uses independent FFN experts. It builds on the sparsely-gated MoE routing architecture but identifies a specific failure mode—threat-agnostic routing—where the gating network cannot distinguish between adversarial threats sharing the same underlying image content. This positions RoME as a targeted fix for MoE in adversarial training, complementing prior MAT methods (MAX, RANDOM, E-AT, RAMP) that force all threats through a single fixed pathway, and differentiating from MORE which also uses MoE for MAT but lacks explicit threat-aware routing supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of threat-agnostic routing as a distinct failure mode of MoE in adversarial training provides a clear diagnostic for why naive MoE application to MAT underperforms, and the dual-scale gating insight (ℓ₁ threats are locally distinguishable, ℓ∞ threats are globally distinguishable) offers a principled design rationale. Practically, RoME achieves state-of-the-art union robustness with only 1.04× more parameters and 1.17× more training time compared to the RANDOM baseline, versus 4× parameters for MORE, making it a lightweight drop-in improvement for existing MAT pipelines. Open questions include whether the fixed layer-adaptive prior generalizes to non-Transformer architectures without manual tuning, whether the approach scales to more than three threat types, and whether the threat-guided diversification loss remains effective when threat budgets vary significantly in magnitude.
