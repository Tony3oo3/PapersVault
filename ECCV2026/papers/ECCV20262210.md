## Problem

Existing joint video super-resolution and deblurring (VSRDB) methods assume a fixed exposure time, making them fragile to the dynamically varying motion blur caused by camera auto-exposure mechanisms that change exposure duration frame-by-frame. Additionally, prevailing temporal modeling strategies face inherent trade-offs: sliding-window designs limit the temporal receptive field, while recurrent architectures suffer from sequential bottlenecks that prevent parallelization and cause gradient vanishing over long sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262210/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a blurry low-resolution RGB video sequence of T frames at resolution H×W×3 and produces a sharp high-resolution sequence at sH×sW×3 (×4 upscaling in experiments). The per-frame exposure time is unknown at test time and must be inferred implicitly from the input; no auxiliary event streams or explicit exposure metadata are required. The predicted degradation kernel is spatio-temporally variant and position-dependent, with shape 3×H×W×k_d² per frame, capturing the coupled effect of motion and exposure at each spatial location.

## Method

FMA-Net++ is built from stacked HRBA (Hierarchical Refinement with Bidirectional Aggregation) blocks that process all frames in parallel while hierarchically expanding the temporal receptive field through bidirectional occlusion-aware warping and multi-flow-mask aggregation, avoiding both sliding-window limitations and recurrent sequential bottlenecks. An Exposure Time-aware Modulation (ETM) layer, driven by contrastive-pretrained ResNet-18 exposure embeddings, injects per-frame exposure conditioning into every HRBA block via lightweight SFT affine modulation. The architecture decouples degradation learning from restoration: Net^D predicts motion- and exposure-aware degradation kernels through an exposure-aware Flow-Guided Dynamic Filtering module, while Net^R exploits these priors via Degradation-Aware cross-attention for targeted high-frequency restoration. Training follows a three-stage protocol: contrastive pretraining of the ETE, supervised training of Net^D with reconstruction/warping/flow losses, and joint end-to-end fine-tuning of both networks.

## Results

On REDS4-ME-5:5 (severe motion blur), FMA-Net++ achieves 29.24 dB PSNR, 0.8453 SSIM, and 1.956 tOF, outperforming the second-best method FMA-Net* by 0.73 dB in PSNR and 0.313 in tOF. On the more challenging REDS-RE benchmark with dynamic exposure transitions, FMA-Net++ reaches 30.13 dB PSNR and 1.360 tOF, widening its lead over FMA-Net* (29.29 dB, 1.614 tOF) by 0.84 dB. On the unseen GoPro dataset, FMA-Net++ attains 30.49 dB PSNR and 0.9018 SSIM, surpassing the next-best FMA-Net* (28.83 dB) by 1.66 dB. The model uses only 12.8M parameters and runs at 0.074 s/frame, achieving a >5.2× speedup over RVRT* while delivering superior quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMA-Net++ outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric on both REDS4-ME exposure levels. Against the closest competitor FMA-Net* (the prior joint VSRDB method), it gains 0.62 dB PSNR on 5:4 and 0.73 dB on 5:5, while also improving tOF by 0.203 and 0.313 respectively, and running 4.3× faster (0.074 s vs. 0.318 s). Sequential pipelines (SwinIR+Restormer, HAT+FFTformer, BasicVSR+++RVRT, IART+BSSTNet) all trail by 2–4 dB PSNR, confirming that joint modeling is essential. Among retrained single-task baselines, BSSTNet* (52.0M params, 0.548 s) is the strongest non-joint method at 28.75 dB on 5:4, still 0.91 dB below FMA-Net++. The event-based Ev-DeblurVSR* lags by 2.26 dB on 5:4, and the blind VSR method DBVSR* falls 2.89 dB behind. On the dynamic-exposure REDS-RE benchmark, FMA-Net++ extends its lead to 0.84 dB over FMA-Net*, and on the out-of-distribution GoPro dataset it achieves a 1.66 dB margin, demonstrating that the exposure-aware ETM mechanism provides robustness beyond what fixed-exposure or capacity-matched alternatives can match.

## Relation to Prior Work

FMA-Net++ is a direct architectural successor to FMA-Net [49], retaining its Flow-Guided Dynamic Filtering (FGDF) module for degradation kernel estimation but replacing the sliding-window temporal backbone with the new Hierarchical Refinement with Bidirectional Aggregation (HRBA) block, which achieves sequence-level parallelism while hierarchically expanding the temporal receptive field. The Exposure Time-aware Modulation (ETM) layer and its pretrained ETE encoder draw on exposure-aware restoration work in video deblurring and frame interpolation [20,37,46], but are the first to explicitly model the coupled motion–exposure degradation in the joint VSRDB setting. The decoupling of a Degradation Learning Network from a Restoration Network is a key structural departure from prior joint VSRDB methods, which typically entangle degradation estimation and pixel restoration in a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing REDS-ME and REDS-RE, the paper fills a notable evaluation gap: prior VSRDB benchmarks assumed fixed exposure, making it impossible to measure robustness to the auto-exposure dynamics that real cameras exhibit. The 5.2× speedup over RVRT and the 3.2× memory reduction versus VRT make sequence-level temporal modeling practical for longer clips, which is important for deployment. Open questions remain: the ETE requires exposure pseudo-labels during pretraining (derived from the synthetic pipeline), and it is unclear how well the approach generalizes to truly unknown, continuous exposure ranges beyond the five discrete levels used in training. The reliance on optical flow for degradation kernel estimation also remains a potential fragility under extreme motion or textureless regions.
