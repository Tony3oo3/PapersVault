## Problem

Existing image personalization methods require dense per-user interaction histories (10–100+ samples) or per-user fine-tuning, making them infeasible in cold-start settings where no user-specific data exists. Moreover, these approaches collapse each user's context-dependent, pluralistic preferences into a single static representation, producing the same style regardless of prompt context. The paper asks whether a compact natural-language persona—requiring no user-specific training data or weight updates—can serve as a sufficient conditioning signal for personalized image generation.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a base text prompt (e.g., 'a cozy wooden table with an open book') and a natural-language persona string (e.g., 'Film photography enthusiast favoring high-grain black-and-white aesthetics with natural lighting') as the sole user-side input. The LLM rewrites the prompt in first-person roleplay conditioned on the persona, and the rewritten prompt is fed to a downstream diffusion model (Flux, DALL-E, etc.) to produce a personalized RGB image. No per-user adapters, preference pairs, or interaction histories are required at any stage; the persona is the only user-specific artifact, making the pipeline fully transferable across diffusion model families without retraining.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a two-layer GATv2 (4 heads each) is trained inductively on a 23M-user × 32K-subreddit Reddit bipartite graph using dual contrastive objectives: an InfoNCE link-prediction loss aligning user and subreddit embeddings, and a CLIP-based user-image contrastive loss grounding representations in actual visual content users post. The learned attention weights α_us rank each user's subreddits by behavioral salience; importance-sampled posts and comments from top-ranked subreddits are then summarized by an MLLM into a coherent natural-language persona capturing demographics, interests, and aesthetic tendencies. Second, the persona is prepended as a first-person roleplay instruction to GPT-4o, which rewrites the base prompt to reflect the persona's stylistic and semantic preferences; this rewritten prompt conditions the diffusion model. The key design choice is roleplay prompting over direct concatenation, which avoids treating persona attributes as literal scene content and preserves intra-user preference diversity (CMMD 0.16 vs. 0.55 for fine-tuned baselines).

## Results

ZIPPY (5-shot persona conditioning) achieves the highest CLIPScore and PIGReward across all four benchmarks: 68.5/82.8 on ZIP-Bench, 66.3/72.1 on PIP, 80.9/77.9 on MovieLens, and 78.3/74.0 on RapidData. It also attains the lowest CMMD (0.16), indicating the best preservation of intra-user preference diversity, compared to 0.25 (TV), 0.31 (DrUM), and 0.55 (ViPer). In the zero-shot setting, ZIPPY already surpasses the fine-tuned DrUM baseline on three of four benchmarks (e.g., 61.8 vs. 65.6 CS on ZIP-Bench is a loss, but 76.4 vs. 72.8 on MovieLens and 73.9 vs. 70.3 on RapidData are wins). A human evaluation confirms a 79% win rate over generic generation and 58–65% win rates over all fine-tuned baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZIPPY (5-shot) outperforms the fine-tuned DrUM baseline on three of four benchmarks in CLIPScore (ZIP-Bench 68.5 vs. 65.6, MovieLens 80.9 vs. 72.8, RapidData 78.3 vs. 70.3), with DrUM retaining a narrow edge only on PIP (66.9 vs. 66.3), which the authors attribute to DrUM's adapter being trained on the same dense prompt histories that define PIP users. Against ViPer (fine-tuned VLM), ZIPPY wins on both MovieLens (80.9 vs. 76.1 CS) and RapidData (78.3 vs. 74.1 CS), and achieves a substantially lower CMMD (0.16 vs. 0.55), indicating ViPer collapses intra-user diversity by applying the same style universally. In the few-shot regime, ZIPPY (3-shot) consistently beats TV (3-shot) on all benchmarks (e.g., 66.7 vs. 62.4 CS on ZIP-Bench, 77.9 vs. 77.4 on RapidData), confirming that persona priors provide a stronger conditioning signal than retrieval over raw prompt histories. ZIPPY (5-shot) also surpasses FABRIC (1-shot) on PIP (66.3 vs. 63.7 CS) and MovieLens (80.9 vs. 75.1 CS). Notably, even the zero-shot ZIPPY (no fine-tuning, no historical prompts) already exceeds DrUM on MovieLens and RapidData, demonstrating that a compact natural-language persona captures preference signals as effectively as per-user adapter training on 100+ examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of image personalization (ViPer, Tailored Vision, DrUM, FABRIC) and LLM-based persona modeling (Persona Hub, PersonaCraft, Behavior-LLaVA), but departs from both lines by requiring no per-user interaction data or fine-tuning. The GATv2-based inductive encoder draws on graph recommendation literature (PinSAGE-style edge weighting, GATv2 attention) but applies it to a 23M-user Reddit bipartite graph with a novel dual contrastive objective that jointly aligns graph structure and visual content. The core personalization mechanism—LLM roleplay prompt rewriting conditioned on a natural-language persona—repurposes the roleplay prompting paradigm from dialogue and recommendation into a zero-shot image generation setting, eliminating the need for per-user adapters or retrieval pipelines that prior methods depend on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZIPP provides a scalable, interpretable, and model-agnostic alternative to fine-tuning-based personalization, directly addressing the cold-start problem that limits all existing methods. The introduction of ZIP-Bench (1.5K users, 40K images, graph-mined personas) fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark paired explicit natural-language personas with generation histories, making systematic zero-shot personalization evaluation impossible before. The IPF-normalized demographic analysis and CMMD pluralism metric expose biases and diversity collapse in existing benchmarks and methods that were previously invisible. Open questions include whether static personas can capture evolving preferences, how the approach generalizes beyond Reddit/Civitai cross-linked users, and whether the GAT mining pipeline transfers to other social graph structures without retraining.
