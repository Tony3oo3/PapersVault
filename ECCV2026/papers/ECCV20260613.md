## Problem

Existing video VAEs designed for natural scenes produce latents that are either too large (2D image VAEs encoding frame-by-frame) or lose fine-grained robotic motion semantics (video VAEs with temporal compression), making them unsuitable for embodied manipulation world models that require both compact representations for efficient LDM training and explicit motion controllability for precise action-conditioned generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260613/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 16-frame, 256×256 robotic manipulation video clips (8 fps for training, 16 fps for evaluation) and produces two disentangled latents: an arm latent at 4×(16×16) temporal×spatial compression and an environment latent at 8×(8×8) compression, both with 4 channels, yielding a combined 0.39% compression rate. Robotic arm masks (generated offline via SAM2 + Grounding DINO) serve as auxiliary supervision only during training; at inference the model disentangles arm and background without any mask input. In the downstream setting, the VAE latents are consumed by an IRASim DiT world model conditioned on history frames and a future action trajectory to generate action-conditioned future video.

## Method

EmbodiedVAE employs a dual-encoder, single-decoder architecture with asymmetric compression: the arm encoder applies stronger spatial downsampling (16×16) to match the manipulator's small spatial footprint, while the environment encoder applies stronger temporal downsampling (8×) to exploit first-person-view redundancy. A two-stage training strategy first learns the two encoders separately (with arm masks as auxiliary supervision and an OT-based motion consistency loss on the arm branch), then freezes the encoders and trains a unified decoder that fuses both latents via channel concatenation at matched resolution. The OT-based motion consistency module computes an entropy-regularized optimal transport plan (via Sinkhorn-Knopp) between consecutive latent frames, minimizing a transport-guided alignment loss plus a permutation regularizer to enforce temporal coherence of arm motion. The encoder-decoder blocks use hybrid 2D spatial and causal 1D temporal downsampling fused by an AlphaBlender operator, with a 3D attention block in the penultimate layer for fine-grained spatio-temporal dependency capture.

## Results

On the action-conditioned robotic manipulation video generation task (Table 2), EmbodiedVAE achieves 24.71 PSNR on RT-1 and 24.25 PSNR on Bridge, outperforming the strongest video VAE baseline Wan-VAE by 2.02 PSNR on RT-1 and 2.08 PSNR on Bridge, while also surpassing the frame-by-frame SDXL image VAE (23.20/22.36 PSNR). On the video VAE reconstruction task (Table 1), EmbodiedVAE achieves the best overall quality with 31.67 PSNR on Agibot-2025 and 30.70 PSNR on Bridge at a 0.39% compression rate, beating the next-best CMD (31.40/31.38 PSNR) which requires a 6.85% compression rate (~17× larger latents). The 0.39% compression rate is competitive with VidTwin (0.20%) while delivering substantially better reconstruction and manipulation fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmbodiedVAE wins decisively on the downstream manipulation task against all 11 baselines. Against the strongest video VAE baselines, it beats Wan-VAE by 2.02 PSNR (RT-1) and 2.08 PSNR (Bridge), and Cog-VAE by 3.49/2.55 PSNR respectively, while using roughly 6× fewer latent tokens (0.39% vs. 2.45%/2.08% compression). It also surpasses the SDXL image VAE (which has no temporal compression and 8.32% compression rate) by 1.51/1.89 PSNR, demonstrating that compact temporal compression with disentangled latents can outperform frame-by-frame encoding for action control. On the reconstruction task, EmbodiedVAE achieves the best PSNR on Agibot-2025 (31.67) and the best LPIPS/SSIM/FVD on both datasets, while CMD achieves marginally higher PSNR on Bridge (31.38 vs. 30.70) but at a 17× larger compression rate (6.85% vs. 0.39%). Among high-compression baselines, VidTwin (0.20%) achieves the smallest latents but trails EmbodiedVAE by 0.85 PSNR on Agibot-2025 reconstruction and 3.61 PSNR on RT-1 manipulation, indicating that extreme compression without explicit motion modeling degrades controllability. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing the OT-based motion consistency loss costs ~0.65–0.89 PSNR on manipulation, and removing training masks costs ~0.90–0.52 PSNR, validating both design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

EmbodiedVAE builds directly on the hybrid 2D/1D downsampling architecture and causal 3D convolution design from VidTok [38], extending it into a dual-encoder, single-decoder framework specifically for embodied manipulation. It departs from the broader line of high-compression video VAEs (VidTwin, CMD, Hi-VAE) that decouple structure and dynamics for general video, by instead disentangling robot-arm motion from environmental context with asymmetric spatio-temporal compression rates tailored to each component's characteristics. The OT-based motion consistency loss draws on optimal transport formulations from semantic correspondence and visual place recognition, repurposing them to enforce temporal coherence in a VAE latent space rather than for feature alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in embodied manipulation world models: the VAE component has been an afterthought, borrowed from natural-scene video generation, leading to either oversized latents (image VAEs) or lost motion semantics (generic video VAEs). The demonstrated 2 dB PSNR gain over Wan-VAE at a 6× lower compression rate, plus the ability to outperform the uncompressed SDXL image VAE on action control, suggests that task-specific VAE design is a meaningful lever for downstream manipulation performance. Open questions include generalization to bimanual or multi-robot settings, longer-horizon generation, and whether the SAM2/Grounding DINO mask supervision during training can be replaced by self-supervised alternatives to reduce pipeline complexity.
