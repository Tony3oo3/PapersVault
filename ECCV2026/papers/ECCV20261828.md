## Problem

Diffusion transformers compute rich perceptual representations across dozens of denoising timesteps, yet all intermediate features are discarded once the final image is rendered, forcing downstream tasks to re-extract semantic and geometric information from pixels using separate models. Prior multi-modal diffusion methods (DatasetDM, DiffuMask) extract features only from the final denoised latent of U-Net-based generators, but in transformer-based DiTs, perceptual information is distributed across timesteps rather than concentrated at specific spatial scales, making single-timestep extraction fundamentally insufficient for dense prediction.

## Input / Output

For synthetic generation, the method takes a text prompt and simultaneously produces a generated image plus any combination of dense annotations (segmentation masks, saliency maps, depth maps) in a single pass. For real-image analysis, an RGB image is noised via the forward diffusion process at multiple timesteps (t ∈ {0.78, 0.52, 0.26, 0.001}) and features are extracted from a frozen FLUX.1-dev backbone at layers {4, 15, 26, 37}, yielding |L|×|T| = 16 feature maps per input that are fused into a single representation before decoding.

## Method

The core mechanism is multi-timestep feature fusion with spatially varying aggregation: features from four timesteps and four layers are linearly projected and layer-normalized, then a 3-layer transformer (8 heads, 768-dim) predicts per-pixel weights α_t over timesteps, enabling each spatial location to adaptively combine coarse early-timestep semantics with fine late-timestep details. A CBAM module refines the weighted sum, and a residual from the cleanest timestep (t=0) preserves fine structure. Concept-driven attention extraction injects task-specific concept tokens (e.g., 'object', 'near') via one-directional attention to produce interpretable spatial guidance maps without altering generation. Optionally, DINOv3-ViT-B/16 discriminative features are concatenated with the fused DiT features and concept maps. Lightweight decoders (DeepLabV3+ for segmentation, DPT for depth and saliency; ~36M trainable parameters) are trained on real annotations while the 12B FLUX backbone remains entirely frozen, with multi-timestep fusion alone contributing +28.7% mIoU over single-timestep extraction.

## Results

On real-image feature quality validation (Table 4), MMDiff with a fully frozen FLUX backbone achieves 78.90% mIoU on PASCAL VOC 2012, 0.918 S-measure on DUTS, and 0.1175 AbsRel on NYU Depth v2. Adding DINO-v3 features (still frozen) lifts these to 84.95% mIoU, 0.934 S-measure, and 0.1164 AbsRel, outperforming both VPD+DINO-v3 (83.77% mIoU) and DINO-v3 alone (83.09% mIoU) which require encoder fine-tuning. Multi-timestep fusion is the dominant factor, contributing +28.7% mIoU over single-timestep extraction. In the multi-modal consistency evaluation (Table 1), MMDiff achieves 72.25% mIoU for segmentation on its own generated images, surpassing DatasetDM (62.56%), DiffuMask (57.35%), and Dataset Diffusion (57.50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMDiff (frozen) substantially outperforms both U-Net-based diffusion extractors: it beats DatasetDM by +37.71% mIoU (78.90 vs 41.19) and Diffusion Hyperfeatures by +11.33% mIoU (78.90 vs 67.57), despite both being frozen. Against VPD, which requires fine-tuning the U-Net, MMDiff (frozen) trails on segmentation mIoU (78.90 vs 82.36) but wins on saliency (0.918 vs 0.912 Sm) and depth (0.1175 vs 0.1244 AbsRel, 0.951 vs 0.867 δ₁). MMDiff alone is below DINO-v3 on segmentation (78.90 vs 83.09 mIoU), but the combined MMDiff+DINO-v3 model (84.95% mIoU, both frozen) surpasses DINO-v3 alone, VPD+DINO-v3 (83.77%), and VPD (82.36%) across all three tasks. In the multi-modal consistency setting (Table 1), MMDiff leads all U-Net-based generators by 9.69–14.90% mIoU on segmentation and by 0.022/0.010 on Sm/Fm for saliency, confirming that DiT-based multi-timestep features capture generated content more faithfully than single-timestep U-Net extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on extracting dense perceptual annotations from diffusion models (DatasetDM, DiffuMask, Dataset Diffusion, VPD, Diffusion Hyperfeatures) by shifting the architectural substrate from U-Net to Diffusion Transformer (FLUX) and introducing multi-timestep feature fusion as the core mechanism. It builds on Concept Attention (Helbling et al., 2025) for interpretable spatial guidance and on the Lyra/Wonderland video-to-3DGS pipeline for a preliminary video extension. The key conceptual departure is the observation that DiTs distribute semantic information uniformly across layers and timesteps (lacking U-Net's spatial hierarchy), making single-timestep extraction—used by all prior works—fundamentally insufficient for dense prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical path to scalable synthetic dataset creation: a single frozen 12B-parameter model simultaneously generates images and extracts segmentation, depth, and saliency annotations without task-specific fine-tuning of the backbone. The finding that all prior U-Net-based synthetic data methods fall below a simple DINOv3 standalone baseline on generated images is a useful negative result that reframes the value proposition of diffusion-based annotation. Open questions include whether the multi-timestep fusion strategy generalizes to other DiT architectures beyond FLUX, how it scales to additional modalities (e.g., optical flow, 3D point maps), and whether the lightweight decoders trained on real annotations transfer robustly to out-of-distribution generated content.
