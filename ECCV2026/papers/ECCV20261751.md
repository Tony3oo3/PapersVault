## Problem

Existing video motion captioning datasets focus on global scene descriptions, which are inherently difficult to evaluate reliably—assessing completeness and faithfulness of free-form global captions is intractable, leading to hallucination-prone annotation pipelines that cannot scale. Current Video-MLLMs also struggle with fine-grained, context-aware motion dynamics over localized regions, particularly when precise spatiotemporal reasoning is required.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261751/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip (1 s to 1 min, 16 frames uniformly sampled) together with a spatiotemporal region mask specifying the target entity. Two grounding settings are supported: Single-Frame (mask provided only at the entity's first visible frame, requiring autonomous tracking) and Full-Sequence (per-frame masks overlaid on every frame, removing tracking ambiguity). The output is a dense, region-level motion caption averaging 23 action verbs and 212 words, structured across six motion aspects (kinematics, parts, spatial, interaction, state, camera). For evaluation, the benchmark converts captions into 2,073 multiple-choice questions organized in an event-motion-fact hierarchy, scored via a judge model.

## Method

The core pipeline is a four-stage segment–caption–bootstrap–summarize process using Qwen3-VL-235B as the unified VLM backbone. The key mechanism is self-bootstrap refinement: two independent caption rollouts are generated for the same merged focal crop (union of bounding boxes across the event), conflicting claims are extracted, and each conflict is resolved via 3-way blind VLM adjudication, replacing the human fact-verification step that limits scalability. A full-video global caption is then used as a temporal anchor to remove boundary speculation and inter-event redundancy during multi-source narrative synthesis. For training, a tailored SFT mixture combines 159K MotionAtlas region captions with 417K Tarsier2-Recap general captions, 130K MotionSight motion QA, and 320K LLaVA-OneVision-1.5 general QA, sampled proportionally for one epoch of full-parameter fine-tuning on Qwen3-VL and Molmo2 base models.

## Results

On the proposed MotionAtlas-Bench, fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B with MotionAtlas-Data yields 31.6% accuracy under Single-Frame Grounding and 34.5% under Full-Sequence Grounding, surpassing the 235B-parameter Qwen3-VL-235B (30.5% / 30.0%) and all proprietary models (GPT-5.2: 36.9% / 24.0%; Gemini 3 Pro: 36.4% / 22.0%). On seven external benchmarks, Qwen3-VL-4B + MotionAtlas-Data gains +6.0 on MotionBench, +7.8 on TOMATO, +8.1 on FAVOR-Bench, and +4.6 on TempCompass over its base model, while Qwen3-VL-8B + MotionAtlas-Data reaches 75.1 on TempCompass, exceeding GPT-5.2 (73.0) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (73.7). Molmo2-8B + MotionAtlas-Data achieves 84.3 on NExT-QA, outperforming all closed-source models (79.6–79.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-5.2, the fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B matches it on MotionBench (62.6 vs. 65.4, a 2.8-point gap) and surpasses it on TempCompass (75.1 vs. 73.0) and NExT-QA (77.2 vs. 79.9, a 2.7-point gap), while Molmo2-8B + MotionAtlas-Data clearly beats GPT-5.2 on NExT-QA (84.3 vs. 79.9). Against Gemini 2.5 Pro, Qwen3-VL-8B + MotionAtlas-Data wins on TempCompass (75.1 vs. 73.7) and MotionBench (62.6 vs. 62.0), but trails on DREAM-1K (39.6 vs. 42.7) and TVBench (52.9 vs. 59.9). Against the Qwen3-VL base models, MotionAtlas-Data delivers the largest relative gains on the 4B variant (+6.0 MotionBench, +7.8 TOMATO, +8.1 FAVOR) and consistent 3–4 point improvements on the 8B variant, with no degradation on any benchmark. Against Molmo2, the 4B model gains +7.0 on DREAM-1K and +4.9 on TempCompass, while the 8B model gains +4.4 on DREAM-1K and +3.6 on TempCompass; both surpass their respective base models on all seven external benchmarks without any regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of global video motion captioning (MotionBench, Tarsier, FAVOR-Bench) by restricting descriptions to a spatiotemporal region mask, thereby decoupling motion entanglement and enabling reliable evaluation. It also extends the region-level understanding line (VideoRefer, PixelRefer, Shikra), which has focused on static visual attributes, into the dynamic motion domain. The self-bootstrap refinement mechanism adapts the dual-rollout comparison idea from SelfCheckGPT [23] and self-consistency [36] to the video captioning setting, replacing human fact-verification with automated hallucination filtering at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making fine-grained motion evaluation quantifiable through checklist-based MCQs with explicit identity references and temporal anchors, MotionAtlas-Bench addresses a long-standing intractability in scoring free-form motion descriptions. The 159K-sample region-level dataset and its demonstrated transfer to general (non-region) motion benchmarks suggest that region-grounded supervision is a scalable path to improving fine-grained video understanding broadly. Key limitations include the single-target setting (no multi-object interactions or identity correspondence) and reliance on a 235B VLM for offline data construction, leaving open whether the pipeline's quality holds with smaller or open-weight backbones.
