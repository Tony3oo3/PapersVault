## Problem

While video world models produce photorealistic future frames, no existing benchmark quantifies whether the generated motion actually constitutes a valid navigation plan toward a semantic target in unstructured real-world environments. Prior evaluation frameworks focus on visual fidelity, spatio-temporal consistency, or rely on subjective VLM-based judges, leaving the critical question of mapless path-planning capability unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262043/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each scenario provides a single initial RGB frame (720p/1080p) and a natural-language prompt specifying a semantic target (either explicit, e.g., 'approach the car', or implicit, e.g., 'go to the place where you can park'). The world model generates 5–10 seconds of future video, from which the world-decoder extracts a 3D camera trajectory. Evaluation compares this trajectory against SLAM-derived ground-truth robot paths (collected at 25 Hz on a quadruped robot) and reports six metrics: ADE, FDE, MR, SE, AC, and a weighted overall score in [0,1].

## Method

Target-Bench introduces a two-stage evaluation pipeline. The world-decoder reconstructs camera poses from generated video using one of three interchangeable 3D reconstruction backends (VGGT, SpaTracker, or ViPE); for monocular methods, a lightweight scale-factor recovery anchors predictions to metric units using the first two frames' known displacement. The path-evaluation module scores the decoded trajectory with five complementary metrics, most notably Approach Consistency (AC), which uses a progress-dependent Gaussian corridor around the ground-truth path so that multiple valid trajectories with similar motion tendency receive comparable scores, and Soft Endpoint (SE), a Gaussian proximity penalty on the final position. The weighted overall score assigns 67.5% weight to SE·AC to prioritize task success over point-wise trajectory overlap. The paper further demonstrates that LoRA fine-tuning of the open-source Wan2.2-TI2V-5B model on only 325 real robot scenarios (with optional frame-shifting data augmentation) lifts the overall score from 0.068 to 0.428, surpassing all off-the-shelf proprietary models.

## Results

On Target-Bench (125 benchmark scenarios, VGGT reconstruction), the best off-the-shelf video world model, Wan2.2-Flash, achieves a Weighted Overall (WO) score of only 0.368, with FDE of 1.364 m, ADE of 0.753 m, Miss Rate of 24.49%, Soft Endpoint of 0.292, and Approach Consistency of 0.745. The ground-truth video upper bound scores 0.882 WO, revealing a large gap between visual generation quality and semantic path planning. Fine-tuning the open-source Wan2.2-TI2V-5B model on 325 real-world robot scenarios with data augmentation (Wan2.2-5B-FT-DA) raises the WO score to 0.428, surpassing all off-the-shelf models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among off-the-shelf video world models, Wan2.2-Flash leads with a WO score of 0.368, outperforming Sora 2 (0.232), Veo 3.1 (0.250), and Veo 3.1-fast (0.212) by a substantial margin. Sora 2 achieves the best Approach Consistency (0.794) among proprietary models but suffers from a high FDE of 1.915 m, while Veo 3.1 has the worst AC (0.448) and FDE (3.129 m). The fine-tuned open-source Wan2.2-5B-FT-DA (0.428 WO) surpasses the best off-the-shelf model (Wan2.2-Flash, 0.368) by 0.060, demonstrating that data-efficient adaptation on just 325 real-world robot scenarios can close much of the gap. Performance on implicit semantic targets closely matches explicit targets (e.g., Wan2.2-Flash: 0.362 vs. 0.372 WO), indicating current models can handle indirect goal descriptions. All models remain far below the ground-truth upper bound of 0.882 WO, confirming a significant gap between photorealistic video generation and reliable semantic path planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video world models and embodied navigation. It repurposes state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction methods (VGGT, SpaTracker, ViPE) as a 'world-decoder' that bridges generated video and quantitative path evaluation, a pipeline not previously applied to benchmark video WMs for planning. It departs from existing navigation datasets (R2R, RxR, MuSoHu) by providing metric-scale robot trajectories paired with explicit/implicit semantic targets, and from prior world-model benchmarks (VBench, WorldScore, WorldModelBench) by replacing subjective VLM-based scoring with invariant, geometry-based metrics focused on approach tendency rather than trajectory overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a significant gap: the best off-the-shelf video WM (Wan2.2-Flash) scores only 0.368, showing that photorealistic generation does not translate to reliable spatial planning. The finding that fine-tuning a 5B open-source model on just 325 real-world scenarios surpasses all proprietary models is practically encouraging for data-efficient robot deployment. Open questions include whether the evaluation is robust to reconstruction-tool errors, whether closed-loop re-planning is feasible, and how latent memory in interactive WMs could support receding-horizon navigation in truly unstructured environments.
