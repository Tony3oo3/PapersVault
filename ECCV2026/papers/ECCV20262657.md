## Problem

Current state-of-the-art trajectory forecasting models tightly couple their training objectives to specific benchmark metrics (e.g., minFDE for Argoverse 2, soft mAP for Waymo), which encode conflicting goals—distance-based metrics reward concentrated predictions while window-based metrics reward broad coverage. This coupling makes it difficult to assess whether improvements reflect genuinely better forecasting or merely better metric-specific tuning, and prevents a single model from performing well across heterogeneous evaluation criteria.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a vectorized road graph (lanes, crosswalks) and 1.1 s of agent state histories sampled at 10 Hz, and outputs a full predictive distribution over an 8 s horizon parameterized as a K-component mixture of 1D distributions (Laplace or generalized Gaussian for longitudinal/lateral positions, von Mises for headings) with learned mixture weights. At evaluation time, TraDiE policies convert this distribution into the K=6 trajectories with confidences required by benchmarks, using Monte Carlo samples from the endpoint marginal—no retraining is needed to switch between metrics.

## Method

The paper introduces DONUT-NLL, which replaces DONUT's winner-takes-all loss with two negative log-likelihood objectives (Traj-NLL with shared mixture weights across timesteps, and Step-NLL with time-dependent weights) that directly optimize the predictive distribution rather than a metric-specific surrogate. Position distributions are aligned to each agent's predicted local longitudinal/lateral axes rather than global x/y axes. For evaluation, TraDiE policies map the predictive distribution to K trajectories and confidences in a metric-specific way: the minFDE policy minimizes expected endpoint distance via gradient descent over Monte Carlo samples, while the (soft) mAP and miss-rate policies use a greedy maximum-coverage heuristic that iteratively selects the sample covered by the most yet-unhit evaluation windows. The framework is validated as model-agnostic by applying the same policies to MTR and QCNet, and a systematic comparison shows generalized Gaussian position distributions outperform Laplace and Gaussian scale mixtures under policy-optimized evaluation.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Motion Dataset test set, DONUT-NLL with the window policy achieves state-of-the-art soft mAP of 0.5018, mAP of 0.4987, and miss rate of 0.0900, surpassing all prior single-model and ensemble methods. With the distance policy applied to the same model weights, DONUT-NLL achieves a minFDE of 1.0304, outperforming the best prior method (IMPACT e2e at 1.0497). Notably, DONUT-NLL beats ensemble methods (e.g., IMPACT ensemble at 0.4801 soft mAP) using only a single model, and uses roughly 5× fewer parameters than IMPACT e2e while achieving better minFDE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DONUT-NLL outperforms all eight compared baselines on their respective best metrics. On window-based metrics, DONUT-NLL (window policy) beats the strongest single-model baselines—IMPACT (0.4721 soft mAP), TrajFlow (0.4710), and RMP-YOLO (0.4673)—by 2.5–3.3 points in soft mAP, and also surpasses the best ensemble (IMPACT E at 0.4801) by 2.2 points. On minFDE, the distance policy yields 1.0304, improving over IMPACT e2e (1.0497), DriveGPT (1.0609), and RMP-YOLO e2e (1.0932) by 0.02–0.06 m. MTR, when retrained with NLL objectives and evaluated with TraDiE policies, improves from 0.4288 to 0.4837 soft mAP and from 1.2357 to 1.0615 minFDE, but still trails DONUT-NLL. QCNet shows a stark distribution mismatch: its minFDE degrades from 1.2254 to 2.2805 under the distance policy, revealing that its heading-aligned Laplacian parameterization produces miscalibrated endpoint distributions on curved trajectories, while its window-based metrics improve modestly (soft mAP 0.3236→0.4045).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DONUT, a decoder-only transformer for trajectory prediction, by replacing its winner-takes-all (WTA) training objective with negative log-likelihood (NLL) objectives that optimize the predictive distribution rather than a metric-specific surrogate. It also generalizes the per-metric post-processing idea from HOME, which was tightly coupled to a discrete heatmap representation, into model-agnostic sampling-based policies (TraDiE) that operate on any probabilistic forecaster's output distribution. The paper departs from the dominant paradigm in trajectory prediction—where models are trained with losses that mirror benchmark metrics (WTA for minFDE, NMS for soft mAP)—by arguing that metric optimization should be a downstream evaluation step rather than a training signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single NLL-trained model, combined with simple Monte Carlo sampling policies, achieves state-of-the-art results on both distance-based and window-based Waymo metrics simultaneously challenges the field's assumption that these conflicting objectives require separate training regimes. The policies also serve as a diagnostic tool: the QCNet failure mode (severe minFDE degradation under the distance policy) reveals distribution miscalibration that naive metric computation would obscure. Key limitations include restriction to the Waymo benchmark (Argoverse 2 shows Step-NLL overfitting), additional inference-time computational cost, and the greedy heuristic for mAP being a per-agent approximation of a globally coupled ranking problem. Open questions include extending policies to multi-agent forecasting, continuity-aware objectives, and practical deployment efficiency trade-offs.
