## Problem

Text-to-image and text-to-video diffusion models produce photorealistic local detail but fundamentally lack persistent 3D structure, making them unable to maintain coherent appearance across diverse viewpoints around objects or across multi-room environments. Existing 3D scene generation methods that rely on these 2D/video priors accumulate geometric drift over long trajectories and struggle with close-up or tightly rotating views, rendering them intractable for arbitrarily large, multi-room scene synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural language text prompt (e.g., "a cozy Scandinavian apartment") and produces a navigable 3D scene represented as 3D Gaussian splats, along with an intermediate mesh scaffold encoding the scene's geometric layout. The text prompt is first converted by an LLM (Claude Opus 4.6) into a JSON floor plan specifying wall thickness, ceiling height, room polygons, and openings. Approximately 24 images per room at 1376×768 resolution are synthesized and used to optimize the final 3DGS scene, with the pipeline scaling linearly in the number of rooms and running on a single 24 GB GPU.

## Method

WorldMesh decouples large-scale 3D scene synthesis into a geometry-first mesh scaffold construction stage and a mesh-anchored appearance synthesis stage. The structural mesh is built from the LLM-generated floor plan (extruded wall cross-sections, boolean-subtracted openings, floor/ceiling planes), then populated with 3D objects reconstructed via SAM3 segmentation and SAM-3D-Objects from a depth-conditioned image synthesis pass. Appearance is generated progressively: two bootstrap cameras per room establish global style, and subsequent cameras are selected by greedy nearest-neighbor quaternion similarity to maximize style reference overlap; each view is synthesized by an image diffusion model conditioned on a composite rendering of the mesh (grayscale depth, alpha-blended accumulated wall textures, textured objects) plus a style reference image. Wall textures are accumulated on-the-fly via projective texturing from each generated view, and structural fidelity is enforced through a Canny edge-recall validation loop that regenerates images whose estimated depth edges deviate from the mesh. The final scene is optimized as geometry-regularized 3DGS with mesh-depth point-cloud initialization and an L1 depth loss term (λ_d=0.7) that prevents geometric drift while preserving architectural structure.

## Results

WorldMesh achieves the highest scores on all automatic and perceptual metrics: CLIP-IQA+ of 0.5114 and CLIP Aesthetic of 5.5799 on rendered trajectory frames, and user-study ratings of 4.48 (Quality), 4.40 (3D Objects), and 4.35 (3D Structure) on a 1–5 scale across 31 participants. In pairwise preference, users choose WorldMesh over every baseline in 93.5–100% of comparisons (96.2% average). The largest margins appear in 3D-consistency dimensions, where the closest baseline (DreamScene360) scores 3.10 on 3D Objects and 3.37 on 3D Structure versus WorldMesh's 4.40 and 4.35.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldMesh outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor on 3D-consistency user ratings is DreamScene360 (Quality 3.19, 3D Objects 3.10, 3D Structure 3.37), still trailing WorldMesh by roughly 1.3 points on each dimension; SpatialGen is the nearest on 3D Objects (3.00) and 3D Structure (3.00) but lags by 1.4 points. On automatic image-quality metrics, WorldExplorer is the closest on CLIP Aesthetic (5.5476 vs. 5.5799) and WonderWorld on CLIP-IQA+ (0.4872 vs. 0.5114), but both remain below WorldMesh. In pairwise user preference, WorldMesh is chosen over SpatialGen and WonderWorld in 100% of trials, over WorldExplorer in 96.8%, and over FlexWorld, LayerPano3D, and DreamScene360 in 93.5% each. Qualitatively, baselines without explicit 3D structure (WorldExplorer, FlexWorld, WonderWorld) exhibit object-level inconsistencies in close-up views, panorama-based methods (DreamScene360, LayerPano3D) distort beyond their central perspective, and SpatialGen's box-based layout conditioning constrains geometry but sacrifices local photorealistic detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldMesh departs from the dominant line of text-to-3D scene generation that operates in pixel space (iterative T2I lifting like WonderWorld, video diffusion like FlexWorld and WorldExplorer, panorama-to-3D like DreamScene360), which the authors argue fundamentally cannot maintain multi-room consistency without explicit 3D structure. It also extends layout-guided methods (SpatialGen, SceneCraft, ControlRoom3D) that use coarse bounding boxes by replacing them with a full geometric mesh scaffold that encodes walls, floors, openings, and reconstructed object geometry. The pipeline composes 3D Gaussian Splatting for the output representation with SAM-3D-Objects for per-object 3D reconstruction, but the core architectural contribution is the mesh scaffold as a persistent geometric anchor that conditions image diffusion for appearance synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first approach to scale text-driven 3D scene generation to arbitrarily large, multi-room environments with linear cost in the number of rooms, addressing a gap where prior methods are limited to single rooms or single objects. The clean decoupling of structure (mesh scaffold) from appearance (conditioned image diffusion) provides a principled framework that could be extended to outdoor scenes, multi-level buildings, or interactive world generation. Key limitations include single-story restriction, dependence on SAM-3D-Objects quality for object geometry (incomplete back faces, occluded regions), and reliance on a specific image synthesis model (Nano Banana Pro) for the most faithful mesh-conditioned generation, leaving open whether the approach generalizes across backbones without quality loss.
