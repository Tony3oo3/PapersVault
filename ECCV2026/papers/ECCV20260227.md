## Problem

Existing zero-shot composed image retrieval methods rely on textual inversion to compress a reference image into a few pseudo-text tokens and compose them with the instruction via concatenation in text space. This paradigm is lossy for fine-grained visual semantics and requires expensive multi-GPU contrastive training on webscale data. The paper asks whether cross-modal composition can be reformulated as a more principled and training-efficient continuous transport in the embedding space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260227/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image and a relative text instruction, both encoded by a frozen CLIP backbone (ViT-B/32 or ViT-L/14) into l2-normalized embeddings in a shared VLM space. It produces a single target-oriented query embedding in the text embedding space, which is matched against pre-extracted gallery image embeddings via nearest-neighbor search. At inference, a one-step transport (velocity prediction at t=0 followed by a single Euler step to t=1) is used for efficiency, and for negation-heavy queries an additional Multi-Negative Steering module adjusts the instruction embedding before transport using a lightweight LLM (TinyLlama-1.1B) to decompose affirmative and negated components.

## Method

FlowCIR formulates ZS-CIR as conditional semantic transport: a lightweight velocity field (a deep residual MLP with timestep conditioning, following the MAR architecture) learns to map the relative-instruction embedding toward a target-aligned text embedding, conditioned on the reference image embedding, via conditional flow matching with a linear interpolation path. Training combines an FM velocity-regression loss with a top-K hard-negative InfoNCE retrieval loss on synthesized compositions from HQ-Edit-200k, requiring only ~0.5 hours on a single RTX 3090. A theoretical analysis shows that conditioning on the reference image resolves the multi-modal ambiguity of the instruction-to-target mapping (preventing mean collapse) and yields exact point-to-point transport under the conditional path assumption. An inference-only Multi-Negative Steering strategy, inspired by SpaceVLM's hyper-spherical cap geometry, decomposes negation-containing instructions into an affirmative intent and a set of negated concepts, then computes a closed-form steered embedding that remains aligned with the affirmative direction while being repelled from all negative directions, improving robustness on negation-heavy queries without any additional training.

## Results

On CIRR (ViT-L/14), FlowCIR achieves R@1=26.2, R@5=56.1, R@10=68.6; on CIRCO it reaches mAP@5=14.9, mAP@25=17.3. Under ViT-B/32, it obtains CIRR R@5=56.5 and CIRCO mAP@5=13.1. All results are obtained with only 0.5 hours of training on a single RTX 3090 GPU, compared to 4–24 hours on 8 A100s for prior textual-inversion methods and 231 hours on 128 A100s for Compo-Diff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among public-data textual-inversion and generative ZS-CIR baselines, FlowCIR achieves the best or near-best results: under ViT-B/32 it outperforms iSEARLE by +0.8 on CIRR R@5 (56.5 vs 55.7) and by +2.5 on CIRCO mAP@5 (13.1 vs 10.6); under ViT-L/14 it beats LinCIR by +1.2 on CIRR R@1 (26.2 vs 25.0) and by +2.3 on CIRCO mAP@5 (14.9 vs 12.6), and surpasses Compo-Diff by +8.0 on CIRR R@1 (26.2 vs 18.2) and +2.3 on CIRCO mAP@5. FlowCIR also outperforms SEARLE, Pic2Word, and Context-I2W across all reported metrics. It trails MagicLens (trained on 36.7M private triplets with 128 TPUs) and MCL (which uses an external LLaMA2-7B LLM) on CIRCO mAP@5, but these methods rely on substantially stronger external resources. The key differentiator is training efficiency: FlowCIR matches or exceeds all public-data baselines with 0.5 hours on a single 24 GB GPU, whereas prior methods require 4–24 hours on 8 A100s or 231 hours on 128 A100s.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowCIR departs from the dominant textual-inversion paradigm in zero-shot CIR (Pic2Word, SEARLE, LinCIR), which compresses a reference image into a few pseudo-text tokens and composes via concatenation, by instead formulating composition as conditional semantic transport in the VLM embedding space. The method is built directly on the rectified-flow formulation of Lipman et al. and Liu et al., adopting their linear-path velocity regression, and borrows the lightweight residual-MLP architecture from MAR for the velocity network. The Multi-Negative Steering component extends the hyper-spherical-cap negation geometry from SpaceVLM into an inference-only correction for composed retrieval, rather than a training-time objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.5-hour single-GPU training cost (vs. multi-A100 hours or 128-GPU days for generative baselines) makes flow-based CIR practically accessible, and the theoretical propositions clarifying why reference conditioning prevents mean-collapse give the design a principled foundation. However, FlowCIR remains behind MagicLens on all benchmarks, which relies on 36.7M private triplets and large-scale TPU training, so the zero-shot gap to fully-supervised methods persists. Open questions include whether multi-step ODE integration at inference (rather than the current one-step approximation) would close the remaining accuracy gap, and whether the text-space transport target generalizes to tasks requiring finer visual discrimination than text embeddings can express.
