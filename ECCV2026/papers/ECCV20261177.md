## Problem

Existing unified multimodal understanding and generation models rely on Transformer-based LLMs with quadratic computational complexity, resulting in slow inference for long sequences, and require over 100M image-text pairs for training, creating a substantial barrier to broader research participation. The paper asks whether a single model can achieve both training efficiency (minimal data) and inference efficiency (linear-time computation) for unified multimodal generation.

## Input / Output

For multimodal understanding, the model takes a 384-resolution RGB image (encoded via DINOv2+SigLIP into continuous features) and a text prompt, producing a free-form text response. For text-to-image generation, it takes a text prompt and autoregressively generates discrete VQVAE tokens (from the LlamaGen tokenizer) that are decoded into a 256-resolution image. Both tasks share a unified next-token prediction interface structured by special tokens ([MMU]/[T2I] for task selection, [SOT]/[EOT] and [SOI]/[EOI] for modality boundaries), with separate text and image vocabularies so the model never needs to learn which modality to output.

## Method

OmniMamba builds on Mamba-2-1.3B (48 layers) as the backbone, exploiting the linear-time recurrence of state space models for both understanding and generation via a single next-token prediction objective. Three key design choices enable data-efficient training: (1) decoupled vocabularies that explicitly separate text and image token spaces, removing the need for the model to learn modality selection; (2) task-specific LoRA adapters (rank 8, only 0.65% extra parameters) inserted into each Mamba-2 block's input projection, where the MMU or T2I branch is activated depending on the task; and (3) a two-stage decoupled training strategy—Stage 1 trains the linear projection + MMU LoRA (with T2I path frozen) and the T2I LoRA + image head (with MMU path frozen) independently on 665K and 83K samples respectively, while Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes all trainable modules with both LoRA branches active. The model is trained on only 2M image-text pairs total, achieving competitive performance with JanusFlow (65M pairs) and surpassing Show-o (2B pairs), while delivering up to 119.2× inference speedup and 63% GPU memory reduction over Transformer-based counterparts at long sequence lengths.

## Results

On multimodal understanding benchmarks, OmniMamba (Mamba-2-1.3B, 384px) achieves POPE 86.3, MME-P 1290.6, VQAv2 77.7, GQA 60.8, and MMMU 30.6, surpassing Show-o (80.0/1097.2/69.4/58.0/26.7) across all five metrics while using 1,000× less training data (2M vs. 2B image-text pairs). For text-to-image generation on MS-COCO, OmniMamba achieves a FID-30K of 5.50 using only 83K COCO training images, outperforming Show-o (9.24, trained on 35M images) and even the generation-only U-ViT (5.95, same 83K COCO data). In inference efficiency, OmniMamba delivers a 119.2× speedup at 16k sequence length and 63% GPU memory reduction at 23k versus Show-o, and a 7.0× faster image generation speed (5.68 img/s vs. 0.81 img/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Show-o (the closest unified model in parameter count), OmniMamba wins on every understanding metric—POPE by 6.3 points, MME-P by 193.4, VQAv2 by 8.3, GQA by 2.8, and MMMU by 3.9—while using roughly 1,000× fewer training pairs (2M vs. 2B). Against JanusFlow (DeepSeek-LLM-1.3B, 65M training pairs), OmniMamba trails slightly on POPE (86.3 vs. 88.0), MME-P (1290.6 vs. 1333.1), and VQAv2 (77.7 vs. 79.8) but edges ahead on GQA (60.8 vs. 60.3) and MMMU (30.6 vs. 29.3), making the two models broadly competitive. On visual generation, OmniMamba's FID-30K of 5.50 is substantially better than Show-o (9.24) and DreamLLM (8.76), and even surpasses the generation-only U-ViT (5.95) trained on the identical 83K COCO subset. In inference speed, OmniMamba is 7.0× faster than Show-o and 5.6× faster than JanusFlow for image generation (5.68 vs. 0.81 vs. 1.02 images/s), and achieves a 119.2× token-generation speedup at 16k sequence length over Show-o. The paper also benchmarks against SmolLM2-1.7B (a Transformer of comparable size) on understanding, where OmniMamba sustains ~248 tokens/s regardless of sequence length (1k–16k) versus ~57 tokens/s for the Transformer, confirming the linear-complexity advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniMamba builds directly on Mamba-2 as its backbone LLM, extending the state space model paradigm from pure text generation to unified multimodal understanding and generation. Conceptually, it departs from the Transformer-based unified models (Show-o, JanusFlow, Emu3) by adopting a linear-complexity architecture, while following the next-token prediction paradigm for both modalities as advocated by Emu3. The paper's central design philosophy of 'decoupling'—separate encoders for understanding vs. generation, modality-specific vocabularies, and task-specific LoRA adapters—builds on Janus/JanusFlow's finding that unified encoders are suboptimal, but pushes decoupling further into the vocabulary and parameter spaces. The two-stage decoupled training strategy addresses data imbalance in a way that prior unified models (which use fixed data ratios) do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1000× data efficiency (2M vs. 2B image-text pairs) is the most practically significant contribution, potentially lowering the barrier for smaller research groups to develop unified multimodal models. The 119.2× inference speedup and 63% memory reduction for long sequences make real-time multimodal generation on consumer hardware more feasible. However, the paper acknowledges that Mamba-2's foundational capabilities remain weaker than Transformers, its training is limited to 2048-token sequences, and generated image quality (especially for faces) is coarse due to relying solely on MS-COCO. Open questions include whether Mamba-2's base model can be strengthened to close the remaining performance gap, whether the decoupled design generalizes to video or higher-resolution generation, and whether the linear-complexity advantage persists as model scale increases.
