## Problem

Existing object detection calibration methods assign a single confidence score to the entire bounding box, treating localization accuracy as a monolithic box-level quantity (e.g., IoU or precision). This fails to capture the fact that individual coordinates can exhibit very different degrees of misalignment and directional shifts even when box-level IoU is identical, limiting the usefulness of calibrated confidence in safety-critical downstream decisions such as autonomous driving.

## Input / Output

ReDC is a post-hoc module that consumes the output logits and bounding-box feature vectors (the first two elements of the box regression output) already produced by any pre-trained detector, requiring no architectural changes or retraining of the detector itself. It outputs four per-coordinate confidence scores (one each for x¹, y¹, x², y²), four binary directional displacement predictions (+1 or −1 per coordinate indicating whether the ground-truth coordinate is larger or smaller), and an approximated box-level IoU that is further calibrated via isotonic regression or Platt scaling.

## Method

ReDC introduces three coupled components. First, a Coordinate-wise Alignment Ratio (CAR) defines per-coordinate empirical accuracy as the ratio of axis-wise intersection length to the sum of coordinate-wise absolute difference and intersection length, yielding a value in [0,1] for each of the four box coordinates. Second, a Confidence Re-encoder (CR), a lightweight MLP, re-scales the detector's class logit per coordinate using the bounding-box feature vector, producing coordinate-wise confidence scores trained with negative log-likelihood to match CAR. Third, a Directional Displacement Estimator (DDE), another MLP, takes the predicted logit vector together with geometric attributes (center, width, height, area, aspect ratio) and predicts a binary direction for each coordinate's deviation, trained with binary cross-entropy. The two modules are trained sequentially (CR first, then DDE), and box-level IoU is reconstructed from the calibrated coordinate-wise confidences combined with directional information, then aligned to ground-truth IoU via isotonic regression or Platt scaling, yielding complementary calibration at both coordinate and box levels.

## Results

On COCO (Deformable-DETR backbone), ReDC with isotonic regression (IR) achieves an average coordinate-wise ECE (C-ECE) of 10.05 across the four bounding-box coordinates, substantially outperforming the best prior method (IR for LaECE₀ [12] at 11.9) while matching its box-level LaECE₀ of 7.9 (vs. 7.8). On Cityscapes, ReDC (IR) records an average C-ECE of 9.9 and LaECE₀ of 6.5, again leading all baselines on coordinate-wise calibration. Under domain shift (COCO-C, Foggy Cityscapes), ReDC maintains average C-ECE of 14.1 and 12.0 respectively, while prior methods degrade to 16.1–18.5, confirming robustness to distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDC decisively outperforms all box-level calibration baselines on the proposed coordinate-wise C-ECE metric: on COCO, IR for Ours achieves an average C-ECE of 10.05 versus 11.9 for the strongest prior method (IR for LaECE₀ [12]), 13.8 for Cal-DETR [20], 15.3 for BPC [18], and 17.9 for TCD [19]. On box-level LaECE₀, ReDC (7.9) essentially ties the best prior method IR for LaECE₀ [12] (7.8) and substantially beats Cal-DETR (11.7), BPC (12.8), and TCD (13.1). The only metric where a baseline leads is AP, where Cal-DETR [20] reaches 52.5 versus ReDC's 50.4, reflecting that Cal-DETR is a train-time method that can modify the detector itself. Against the probabilistic GP-Normal [14], ReDC achieves a higher mean correlation with coordinate-wise error (0.1771 vs. 0.0263), indicating superior capture of per-coordinate localization uncertainty without requiring a probabilistic detector. Under domain shift (COCO-C, Foggy Cityscapes), ReDC's C-ECE advantage over IR for LaECE₀ [12] widens (14.1 vs. 16.1 on COCO-C; 12.0 vs. 14.1 on Foggy Cityscapes), while box-level LaECE₀ remains competitive (11.0 vs. 11.2; 8.4 vs. 7.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the box-level calibration formulation of Kuzucu et al. (ECCV 2024), which aligned detection confidence with IoU, by decomposing localization accuracy into per-coordinate alignment ratios (CAR) and adding a directional displacement component. It departs from prior post-hoc methods (TCD, BPC, Cal-DETR) that treat the bounding box as an indivisible unit, and from probabilistic detectors (GP-Normal) that model coordinate-wise uncertainty but collapse it back to a single box-level score. The framework is explicitly detector-agnostic, operating on logits and box features that any pre-trained detector already produces, positioning it as a lightweight add-on rather than a retraining-based approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical deployment (autonomous driving, medical imaging), knowing which specific coordinate is mislocalized and in which direction is actionable information that a single box-level confidence score cannot provide; the paper demonstrates that well-calibrated box-level scores can coexist with poorly calibrated coordinate-level scores, masking dangerous localization errors. Limitations include the binary (+1/−1) directional prediction, which is coarse for continuous displacement, and the reliance on ground-truth CAR for training the calibrator, limiting applicability in fully unsupervised settings. Open questions include whether coordinate-wise calibration generalizes to 3D detection or multi-object scenes with occlusion, and whether the directional signal can be made continuous to capture magnitude of displacement.
