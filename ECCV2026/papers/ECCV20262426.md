## Problem

Few-shot semantic segmentation degrades when support and query objects exhibit large pose or scale differences, because conventional prototype matching operates in geometry-entangled feature spaces where semantic identity and instance-level variation are conflated. Additionally, standard multi-support fusion strategies (uniform averaging or global scene-level weighting) assume all supports are equally informative, which fails when supports vary in pose, scale, and occlusion relative to the query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262426/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes K support images each paired with a binary segmentation mask, plus one unlabeled query image, all processed through a shared frozen CNN backbone (VGG-16 or ResNet-50) to extract feature maps. Object-centric features are obtained by masking support features with their ground-truth masks and computing pixel-wise cosine similarity between query features and the support prototype. The output is a binary segmentation mask for the query, produced by thresholding a per-pixel probability map; during the refinement stage, the query prediction is also used as a pseudo-support to reverse-predict support masks, and the final fused probability is a pixel-wise weighted combination across all K supports.

## Method

DMNet disentangles object features into a geometry-invariant Semantic Code and a low-dimensional Modulation Code via a shared encoder-decoder (DAE). The modulation code is projected onto a 2D geometric pointer, and an equivariance consistency loss forces the decoder to produce spatially transformed reconstructions when the pointer undergoes a similarity transformation, thereby encoding pose-scale variation in the modulation pathway while keeping the semantic branch invariant. Training combines reconstruction, semantic consistency, equivariance, and geo-prediction losses under an episodic protocol. A Cyclic Ensemble Refinement stage generates augmented support views, uses the current query prediction as a pseudo-support to reverse-predict support masks, scores augmentations by IoU compatibility, and iteratively refines the query prediction over up to three iterations. Finally, a Spatially Adaptive Fusion module computes per-pixel weights from each support's influence (marginal contribution to the fused probability) and confidence (1 − binary entropy), producing a reliability-weighted aggregation that up-weights reliable object regions and suppresses misleading background cues.

## Results

On PASCAL-5^i with ResNet-50, DMNet achieves a mean mIoU of 75.6% (1-shot) and 77.5% (5-shot), outperforming the previous SOTA DeFSS (72.2% and 75.1%) by 3.4 and 2.4 points respectively. On the more challenging COCO-20^i, DMNet reaches 57.5% (1-shot) and 63.1% (5-shot), improving over DeFSS by 3.8 and 2.2 points. With a VGG-16 backbone, DMNet still leads all baselines, achieving 70.7% (PASCAL 1-shot) and 52.3% (COCO 1-shot). The method uses only 5.3M parameters with 19.7 ms inference time, comparable to lightweight baselines like BAM (4.9M, 7.4 ms) while substantially outperforming them in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMNet consistently outperforms all 16 compared baselines across every setting (both backbones, both shot counts, both datasets). Against the strongest prior method DeFSS [19], DMNet gains +3.4 mIoU on PASCAL-5^i 1-shot (75.6 vs 72.2) and +3.8 on COCO-20^i 1-shot (57.5 vs 53.7), with the largest relative improvement on COCO where pose-scale variation is more severe. Compared to attention-based methods like AENet [30] and HMNet [31], DMNet leads by 3.6–5.4 mIoU on COCO-20^i 1-shot. Against multi-support fusion baselines (BAM [8], DCAMA [24], PANet [29]), DMNet's spatially adaptive fusion yields 10–14 mIoU gains on COCO-20^i, confirming that pixel-level reliability weighting substantially outperforms global scene-difference or uniform averaging strategies. In the foundation-model comparison (Table 2), DMNet-SAM (70.8 avg mIoU) surpasses GF-SAM (70.1) and FCP (64.4) while using a frozen SAM encoder without prompting, and even the ResNet-50 variant (68.4) is competitive with GF-SAM at a fraction of the parameter count (5.3M vs 180M) and inference time (19.7 ms vs 1800 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

DMNet carves new ground in FSS by repurposing the equivariance-constraint idea from geometric deep learning (Cohen & Welling; Lenc & Vedaldi) into a lightweight autoencoder that explicitly separates class-identity semantics from instance-level pose-scale variation, rather than relying on the similarity re-calibration strategies used by PMMs, SSFSS, or attention-based fusion methods. For multi-support aggregation, it departs from scene-level adjustment (BAM), prototype alignment (PANet), and cross-attention weighting (DCAMA) by introducing pixel-level influence-and-confidence weighting plus a cyclic pseudo-support refinement loop that is conceptually distinct from CyCTR's cycle-consistency objective. The framework does not build on a single prior architecture but synthesizes disentanglement, equivariant reconstruction, and iterative refinement into a unified pipeline tailored to the pose-scale mismatch problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, lightweight (5.3 M parameters, ~20 ms inference) alternative to foundation-model-based FSS methods that achieve comparable accuracy at a fraction of the compute, making it practically relevant for deployment-constrained settings. The identification of pose-scale discrepancy bias as a first-class failure mode and the demonstration that a 2-D geometric pointer with equivariance constraints can absorb most of the geometric variation (DAE alone accounts for ~13 mIoU of the total gain) offer a reusable design pattern for other correspondence-sensitive tasks. Open questions include whether the 2-D similarity-transform parameterization generalizes to perspective, non-rigid, or 3-D deformations, and whether the cyclic refinement's fixed 3-iteration budget is sufficient under extreme multi-instance or heavily occluded scenes.
