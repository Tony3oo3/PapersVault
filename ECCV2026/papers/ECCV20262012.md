## Problem

Pre-trained Vision Foundation Models produce inherently low-resolution features due to patchification and aggressive pooling, limiting their use in pixel-level dense prediction tasks. Existing learned upsampling methods either require VFM-specific retraining, rely on heavy architectures that sacrifice inference speed, or degrade semantic fidelity at boundaries. The paper addresses the gap of achieving universal, encoder-agnostic feature upsampling at arbitrary resolutions while maintaining both geometric consistency and ultra-lightweight efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262012/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full-resolution RGB image (used only for guidance encoding, downsampled to 224×224 during training) and a low-resolution VFM feature map at patch resolution (e.g., 16×16 or 32×32) with arbitrary feature dimensionality D_f. It produces a high-resolution feature map at any target spatial resolution (H_any × W_any) while preserving the original feature dimension D_f, enabling seamless plug-and-play integration with any frozen VFM backbone without architectural modification.

## Method

RaysUp lifts feature reconstruction into a geometry-aware ray domain by combining four components: (1) a Spatially Decoupled Guidance Encoder that decomposes a standard 3×3 convolution into four directional branches (1×1, 1×3, 3×1, and 2×2 dilated) to capture anisotropic spatial semantics with ~69% fewer parameters; (2) an Any-Resolution Cross-Attention mechanism that uses adaptive average pooling to generate queries at the target resolution and keys at the VFM resolution, decoupling upsampling from fixed ratios; (3) Ray Positional Encoding (RayPE), which maps each pixel to a 6D Plücker ray coordinate, applies multi-band log-spaced harmonic phase encoding, and performs RoPE-style rotational modulation to inject implicit 3D geometric priors into attention; and (4) a Geometry-Aware Neighborhood Attention module that restricts cross-attention to dilated local neighborhoods, reducing complexity from O(H_any·W_any·H_lr·W_lr) to O(H_any·W_any·k²). The entire 0.14M-parameter network is trained for ~1 hour on ImageNet with a cosine + L2 reconstruction loss against frozen VFM features, achieving ~7× faster inference than AnyUp with only 16% of its parameters.

## Results

On semantic segmentation with DINOv2-S, RaysUp achieves 62.32 mIoU on COCO-Stuff, 84.64 mIoU on Pascal-VOC, 42.34 mIoU on ADE20K, and 61.88 mIoU on Cityscapes, matching or exceeding all baselines. On dense geometric prediction (NYUv2), RaysUp attains the best surface normal RMSE of 27.69° and the best absolute depth RMSE of 0.4658 with δ₁ = 0.8103. In terms of efficiency, RaysUp uses only 0.14M parameters (vs. 0.87M for AnyUp) and achieves 55 FPS at 224×224 (vs. 11 FPS for AnyUp), corresponding to roughly 7× faster inference and 16% of the parameter count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaysUp outperforms the strongest encoder-agnostic baseline AnyUp on nearly all metrics: +0.18 mIoU on COCO-Stuff, +0.46 mIoU on Pascal-VOC, +0.19 mIoU on ADE20K, and a 0.14° lower normal RMSE (27.69 vs. 27.83), while using only 16% of AnyUp's parameters (0.14M vs. 0.87M) and running ~7× faster (55 vs. 11 FPS at 224×224). Against JAFAR, RaysUp improves depth estimation (0.4658 vs. 0.4693 abs RMSE) and normal estimation (27.69 vs. 27.80 RMSE) but trails slightly on COCO-Stuff mIoU (62.32 vs. 61.79 is actually a win; JAFAR scores 61.79). Compared to LoftUp, RaysUp wins on depth/normal metrics and COCO-Stuff, but LoftUp edges ahead on Cityscapes mIoU (62.09 vs. 61.88) and open-vocabulary segmentation, which the authors attribute to LoftUp's use of SAM masks as auxiliary supervision during training. Against FeatUp, RaysUp dominates across all geometric prediction metrics (e.g., 27.69 vs. 28.94 normal RMSE) and matches or exceeds it on segmentation. Overall, RaysUp achieves the best or second-best result on virtually every metric while being the most parameter-efficient and fastest method tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaysUp reformulates the classical Joint Bilateral Upsampling (JBU) paradigm by replacing its RGB-difference range kernel with a learned Spatially Decoupled Guidance Encoder and its fixed 2D neighborhood with a ray-aware Neighborhood Cross-Attention. It extends the attention-based upsampling line (JAFAR, AnyUp, FeatUp, LoftUp) by injecting 3D geometric structure via 6D Plücker ray coordinates, generalizing RoPE from 1D sequential positions to 3D ray-manifold positions inspired by NeRF. The key conceptual departure from AnyUp is the shift from purely 2D pixel-plane proximity to projective ray geometry, while retaining the encoder-agnostic, arbitrary-resolution design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7× inference speedup and 16% parameter footprint relative to AnyUp make geometry-aware feature upsampling practical for high-resolution and real-time dense prediction pipelines, where prior methods OOM or run at single-digit FPS. An open question is how much of the geometric benefit comes from the ray-direction encoding versus the neighborhood-attention structure itself, since the identity-pose configuration (no actual camera extrinsics) performs nearly as well as DA3-estimated poses, suggesting the directional prior may suffice without true 3D scene geometry. The framework's reliance on camera intrinsics (even if trivially set) also limits direct applicability to non-pinhole or panoramic inputs without further adaptation.
