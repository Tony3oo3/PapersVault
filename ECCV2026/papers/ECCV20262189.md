## Problem

Current T2I safety alignment methods enforce a debilitating trade-off between safety and generation quality: enforcing safety constraints degrades aesthetic appeal and instruction-following fidelity. The paper identifies three systemic root causes—flawed data paradigms that transmit biased optimization signals, myopic safety-only reward functions that ignore quality, and binary evaluation protocols that cannot capture the safety-quality balance—arguing that resolving the trade-off requires addressing all three simultaneously.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a natural-language text prompt and produces a safety-aligned RGB image from a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion (v1.5 or SDXL) model. During training, the model consumes preference triplets (T, I⁺, I⁻) where each image carries a dual-dimension annotation: a continuous safety cost from the trained Safety Cost Model and a quality score from PickScore. The safety-aware inpainting process used to construct training pairs preserves background, composition, and artistic style from the harmful image, isolating only the safety-relevant visual changes in the safe counterpart.

## Method

T2I-SPO extends Diffusion-DPO by replacing the single safety reward with a composite reward R_λ(T,I) = R(T,I) − λ·C(I) that jointly models quality preference and safety cost, then re-labels preference pairs using this composite score before applying the standard DPO loss in the diffusion denoising framework. A Safety Cost Model (SCM) based on Open-CLIP ViT-H/14 is trained on 90K pairs with a contrastive ranking loss plus a cost-anchoring term that regularizes safe-image scores toward a common anchor to prevent over-penalization of imperceptible risks. A Dynamic Focusing Mechanism monitors per-sample loss descent rates, identifies stagnant hard examples, and re-injects them with the augmentation (from a pool of sharpening, color jittering, latent noise, and frequency compensation) that maximizes gradient direction change. The training dataset LibraAlign-HF (10,760 pairs) is constructed via safety-aware inpainting that uses the harmful image as initialization for an SDXL image-to-image pipeline guided by a semantically-aligned safe prompt, yielding high-fidelity pairs that avoid the quality-degradation bias of simple prompt editing.

## Results

On I2P-Sexual, T2I-SPO achieves the lowest Inappropriate Probability (IP) of 4.40% (vs. 31.90% for unaligned SD-v1.5) and the highest Unified Alignment Score (UAScore) of 0.757, while maintaining a PickScore of 19.38. On the full I2P benchmark spanning 7 NSFW categories, T2I-SPO attains the best Safety Cost (SC = −7.73) and UAScore (0.784) among all methods, with a PickScore of 19.61 that exceeds the unaligned baseline. On NSFW-56K-Sexual, T2I-SPO reduces IP to 13.65% (from 57.05% unaligned) with a UAScore of 0.725. On benign-prompt benchmarks (Pick-a-Pic, HPDv2, DrawBench), T2I-SPO's preference scores (e.g., PickScore 20.4–21.0, HPSv2 26.0–28.0) match or slightly exceed the unaligned SD-v1.5, confirming no quality degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2I-SPO outperforms all seven baselines on the UAScore across all three NSFW benchmarks, confirming its superior safety–quality balance. Against the strongest RL-based competitor AlignGuard, T2I-SPO achieves substantially lower IP (4.40% vs. 10.53% on I2P-Sexual; 13.65% vs. 19.80% on NSFW-56K) and higher PickScore (19.38 vs. 18.82), though AlignGuard retains a lower IP on the full 7-category I2P set (13.18% vs. 17.07%); however, T2I-SPO still leads on the finer-grained Safety Cost (−7.73 vs. −6.14) and UAScore (0.784 vs. 0.690) there. Among concept-erasure methods, T2I-SPO beats RECE (the closest competitor) by 0.30 IP points on I2P-Sexual and by a wide margin on SC (−8.14 vs. −6.49) and UAS (0.757 vs. 0.672). Parameter-editing baselines (UCE, SLD-STRONG, ESD-u) all show 1–7 IP points higher than T2I-SPO on I2P-Sexual and consistently lower PickScores, indicating greater quality degradation. The unaligned SD-v1.5 baseline and its safety-filter variant are dominated on every metric, with the filter reducing IP only to 9.99% while T2I-SPO reaches 4.40% without any external checker. On benign-prompt evaluation (Pick-a-Pic, HPDv2, DrawBench), T2I-SPO matches or slightly exceeds the unaligned SD-v1.5 on PickScore, HPSv2, and ImageReward, whereas AlignGuard and RECE show measurable drops (e.g., PickScore 20.1 vs. 20.5 on Pick-a-Pic), reinforcing that T2I-SPO preserves general generation capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffusion-DPO lineage (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024; Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) by replacing the safety-only reward signal used in prior T2I safety alignment methods (AlignGuard, SafetyDPO) with a composite reward that jointly models generation quality and safety cost. It departs from the concept-erasure and safety-fine-tuning paradigms by arguing that their parameter-level interventions cause catastrophic forgetting of benign concepts, and from the RL-based safety alignment line by identifying the 'Sleeping Venus Dilemma'—a failure mode where single-objective safety rewards force models to censor legitimate artistic content. The Safety Cost Model (an Open-CLIP-based scorer trained with a contrastive ranking loss) and the safety-aware inpainting pipeline for constructing high-fidelity preference pairs are novel data-side contributions that have no direct precedent in the T2I safety literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UAScore metric and LibraAlign-100K dataset address a genuine gap in the field: prior evaluations relied on binary harm classifiers that cannot quantify the safety-quality trade-off, and prior datasets lacked quality annotations, implicitly teaching models that safety requires quality sacrifice. If the composite-reward framing generalizes beyond SD-v1.5/SDXL to video generation or multimodal LLMs, it could become a standard recipe for safety alignment. Open questions include whether the GPT-4o-annotated severity rubric introduces systematic biases across cultural contexts, whether the method scales to the 634-concept long tail without per-category tuning, and whether the UAScore's sigmoid-centering choices (PS₀=19.31, SC₀=−2.62) remain stable across model architectures.
