## Problem

Group-relative online RL methods for text-to-image alignment (e.g., DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO) require generating multiple trajectories per prompt to estimate a local advantage baseline, introducing synchronization overhead that scales with the slowest sample and causing the learning signal to collapse as intra-group rewards become homogeneous. This paper asks whether stable variance reduction can be achieved without concurrent group sampling, enabling each generated trajectory to contribute directly to a policy update.

## Input / Output

MPO takes a text prompt and a fixed initial latent z0 (drawn once per prompt to isolate policy-driven reward changes from noise resampling) and produces a single generated image via a 12-step Euler-Maruyama SDE rollout through a flow-matching or diffusion model. The reward is a non-differentiable black-box score (GenEval, OCR edit distance, PickScore, or a scalarized combination) evaluated on the decoded image. Unlike group-based baselines that emit G images per prompt per iteration, MPO emits exactly one image and one gradient step per prompt, reducing peak GPU memory from 68 GB to 60 GB at batch size 64.

## Method

MPO replaces the group-relative baseline with a persistent per-prompt Bayesian value tracker that maintains a Gaussian posterior N(μ_c, σ_c²) over expected reward, updated via a Kalman-style rule after each single-trajectory observation. The process noise Q_k is set proportional to a Girsanov-inspired drift proxy measuring the L2 change in the drift field between consecutive policies, so large policy updates inflate posterior uncertainty and prevent the baseline from going stale. Exploration is provided by SDE sampling along the trajectory (rather than multiple initial latents), and the raw advantage r − μ_c is normalized globally across recent batches via exponential moving averages to prevent signal vanishing. An uncertainty-guided UCB-style curriculum prioritizes prompts with high posterior uncertainty or low visit counts. The policy update uses an advantage-weighted regression objective with a reward-surprise weight w_c = 1 + γ|r−μ_c|/σ_c, emphasizing informative samples while suppressing low-signal updates, all within a single-stream loop of one prompt, one trajectory, one update.

## Results

On GenEval (compositional generation), MPO applied to FLUX.1 Dev achieves an overall score of 0.98, surpassing DanceGRPO (0.91), Flow-GRPO (0.95), and the base FLUX.1 Dev (0.66). On visual text rendering, MPO reaches 0.97 OCR accuracy versus 0.91 for DanceGRPO, and on human-preference alignment it scores 23.89 PickScore versus 23.28. Under matched H800 hardware, MPO delivers a 26× wall-clock speedup (9 vs. 235 GPU hours to reach GenEval 0.91) and a 5× sample-efficiency gain over DanceGRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPO consistently outperforms all group-relative baselines. Against DanceGRPO (the primary controlled baseline on FLUX.1 Dev), MPO improves GenEval from 0.91 to 0.98, OCR from 0.91 to 0.97, and PickScore from 23.28 to 23.89, while also winning a blind human-preference study (58% vs. 15% win rate). Against Flow-GRPO on SD3.5-M, MPO gains 0.03 on GenEval (0.98 vs. 0.95), 0.05 on OCR (0.97 vs. 0.92), and 0.60 on PickScore (23.91 vs. 23.31). In the broader GenEval leaderboard, MPO (0.98) surpasses GPT-4o (0.84), SANA-1.5 (0.81), Janus-Pro-7B (0.80), and DALLE-3 (0.67). Against DiffusionNFT, MPO maintains 0.98 GenEval at 2k training iterations where DiffusionNFT degrades to 0.53, and under multi-objective (PickScore+OCR) stress MPO holds 0.98 versus DiffusionNFT's 0.94. Out-of-domain quality metrics on DrawBench (Aesthetic, DeQA, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) are preserved or improved, indicating no reward over-optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPO sits at the intersection of three research threads: SDE-based exploration for diffusion/flow-matching policies (Black et al., 2023), advantage-weighted regression as a low-variance policy update (Peng et al., 2019), and Kalman-style temporal filtering for non-stationary value tracking (Geist & Pietquin, 2010). Its primary departure is from the GRPO family (DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO, MixGRPO), which reduce gradient variance by sampling G trajectories per prompt and computing a local group baseline; MPO eliminates this group abstraction entirely, replacing it with a persistent per-prompt Bayesian posterior whose uncertainty is adapted via a Girsanov-inspired drift proxy. Unlike DiffusionNFT, which also removes groups but shifts optimization to the forward process with deterministic ODE solvers, MPO preserves the reverse-time SDE trajectory, keeping the optimization route aligned with standard online RL for generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 26× wall-clock speedup and 5× sample-efficiency gain are practically significant for teams training large flow-matching or diffusion T2I models with RL, where group synchronization and redundant sampling are major cost drivers. The conceptual contribution—that a history-aware Bayesian tracker with adaptive forgetting can substitute for concurrent group baselines without sacrificing alignment quality—challenges a widely adopted design assumption in the GRPO literature and may generalize to other online RL settings for generative models. Key limitations include the fixed initial latent during training (which reduces exploratory diversity and is acknowledged as a future direction for dynamic noise scheduling) and the reliance on a black-box reward model whose imperfections could still lead to over-optimization, though out-of-domain metrics suggest this is mitigated in practice.
