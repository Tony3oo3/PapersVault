## Problem

Existing driving world models forecast external environment evolution but treat the driver as a static, post-hoc risk source, while in-cabin monitoring systems (DMS, LLM assistants) perform only instantaneous recognition without multi-step rollout. This leaves a critical gap for L2/L3 shared-control safety: no model predicts how external traffic events causally drive the driver's physical and cognitive state over a prediction horizon, making anticipatory takeover-readiness reasoning impossible.

## Input / Output

The model consumes temporally synchronized in-cabin and out-cabin (multi-view: front/left/right) video frames segmented into 3-second clips of 10 frames (~3.3 Hz), with per-frame features pre-extracted by a frozen Qwen3-VL-2B encoder (D=2048) and cached outside the training graph. Under a 5→5 causal protocol, it rolls out 5 future steps and decodes the primary output as HALPE-136 2D skeleton keypoints in normalized [0,1] image-plane coordinates, alongside four auxiliary clip-level classification heads (7-class driver behavior, 5-class emotion, 3-class traffic context, 5-class vehicle condition) that serve purely as semantic regularizers and are excluded from conditioning the temporal transition.

## Method

Driver-WM adopts a dual-stream latent architecture that separately encodes internal driver state and external traffic context, coupling them through a directed Gated Causal Injection module: a cross-attention context summary m_t is computed from truncated causal histories, and a learned vector gate g_t ∈ (0,1)^D (derived from the current external latent via an MLP) modulates how much external perturbation enters the internal transition, enforcing strict temporal causality (update at t+1 accesses only histories ≤ t). The internal latent is rolled out autoregressively via a lightweight transition predictor, then decoded into skeleton trajectories by an ST-GCN head regularized with physical priors (bone-length preservation, first/second-order temporal smoothness, seat feasibility). Frozen Qwen3-VL features with learnable view embeddings serve as the perceptual interface, keeping the VLM out of the training graph so optimization focuses on the world-model core. The key architectural insight is directional (external→internal, not symmetric) coupling with a time-varying learned gate, which controlled interventions confirm is necessary: disabling the gate causes severe rollout degradation, while unmodulated injection destabilizes predictions.

## Results

On the AIDE benchmark under a 5→5 causal rollout protocol, Driver-WM (main) achieves 71.47 px MPJPE (3.24% d-nMPJPE) on all clips and 138.03 px (6.27%) on the safety-critical High-Motion (HM) subset, with PCK@0.05 of 71.66. On auxiliary semantic heads, it reaches 68.07 F1 (Driver Behavior), 72.61 F1 (Emotion), 90.15 F1 (Traffic Context), and 68.34 F1 (Vehicle Condition). On the HM tail at the final horizon step (h=5), Driver-WM reduces MPJPE to 155.82 px versus 178.62 px for the zero-velocity baseline, demonstrating substantially better long-horizon reactive forecasting. Across three random seeds, the configuration is stable at 3.25±0.01% d-nMPJPE and 89.26±1.33 TCR F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Driver-WM outperforms all motion-only baselines (Zero-Velocity, ST-GCN, SiMLPe, MotionBERT, Offline Enc-Dec) on the High-Motion subset, where safety-critical reactive maneuvers occur: it achieves 138.03 px HM MPJPE versus 141.53 (MotionBERT), 144.05 (Offline Enc-Dec), 156.29 (SiMLPe), and 158.10 (ST-GCN). On the All-clip average, the Zero-Velocity baseline appears stronger (52.89 vs. 71.47 px MPJPE) due to an inertia effect under ℓ₂ metrics on predominantly low-motion clips, but Driver-WM substantially narrows the gap on the HM tail and at longer horizons (h=5: 155.82 vs. 178.62 px). Among contextual baselines sharing the same frozen Qwen3-VL interface, Driver-WM beats Late Fusion (82.59→71.47 px All MPJPE) and Cross-Attn only (80.14→71.47) on geometric metrics, while the Static pooling baseline achieves slightly lower averaged MPJPE (68.50) via mean-regression but cannot produce autoregressive trajectories or support mechanism probes. On all four auxiliary semantic heads, Driver-WM achieves the highest Macro-F1 scores (DBR 68.07, DER 72.61, TCR 90.15, VCR 68.34), surpassing the next-best contextual baseline (Cross-Attn only: 62.52/68.75/87.94/69.09) and all motion-only methods by wide margins. The non-causal bidirectional variant does not yield consistent gains, supporting the causal inductive bias as a deployment-consistent design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

Driver-WM carves new ground at the intersection of three established threads: environment-centric driving world models (GAIA-1, DriveDreamer, Vista), recognition-oriented driver monitoring (DMS, Drive&Act, AIDE), and unconditional human motion prediction (SiMLPe, MotionBERT, ST-GCN). Rather than extending any single line, it repurposes the latent world-model paradigm—autoregressive rollout in a compressed state space—by redirecting the predicted dynamics from the external scene to the internal driver, while conditioning on synchronized out-cabin traffic via a novel gated causal injection module. The frozen Qwen3-VL encoder is repurposed from a language-generation backbone into a continuous perceptual interface, following the fixed-encoder adaptation paradigm of BLIP-2 but applied to temporal dynamics rather than static understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For L2/L3 shared-control driving, the ability to anticipate driver reactions seconds ahead under hypothetical traffic interventions is a safety-critical capability that neither current DMS nor environment world models provide. The paper's exposure of the 'inertia effect'—where a copy-last-frame baseline minimizes averaged l2 error on naturalistic driving clips—offers a practical caution for evaluation design in this domain. Key limitations include the short 5→5 rollout horizon, 2D-only skeleton output, single-dataset validation (AIDE), and absence of real-time deployment; open questions include whether the gated coupling generalizes to longer horizons, 3D reconstruction, multi-driver personalization, and closed-loop integration with actual takeover policies.
