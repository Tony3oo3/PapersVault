## Problem

Current VLMs suffer from a critical deficiency in fine-grained visual perception, which the authors attribute to a text-dominant optimization bias during pre-training that treats visual signals as passive conditioning inputs, causing the model to discard fine-grained visual details. Prior attempts to inject visual supervision during post-training yield only marginal gains because visual representations are already entrenched by large-scale text-centric pre-training, making the pre-training phase the critical window for fundamentally optimizing visual representation learning.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (processed through a SigLIP2-so400m-patch16-naflex encoder into patch-level features) and text queries, producing a generated text response. During pre-training, the input sequence is augmented with discrete visual tokens from a 150K-vocabulary codebook (768-dim embeddings) derived by a dense-structural-semantic tokenizer, which are interleaved with text tokens in a unified autoregressive stream. The visual supervision is active only during pre-training stages and deactivated during post-training (SFT/RL), adding only 3.8% inference latency from the vocabulary expansion.

## Method

VIVAS introduces a unified token space paradigm that expands the textual vocabulary with a 150K visual vocabulary, enabling stable autoregressive training over both modalities under a single cross-entropy objective (weighted λ=0.5 for vision). The visual vocabulary is constructed by a dense-structural-semantic tokenizer that fuses DINOv3 structural features (as cross-attention queries) with SigLIP2 semantic features (as keys and values), concatenates the result with structural features, and discretizes via Index Backpropagation Quantization (IBQ) trained with perceptual and adversarial losses but deliberately excluding L1 loss to avoid texture bias. Training follows a four-stage curriculum: pure-text language pre-training (~10T tokens), multimodal pre-training with joint visual-textual autoregressive supervision (~2.4T tokens), task adaptation, and post-training with SFT and RL where visual supervision is deactivated. The key empirical finding is that visual supervision must be applied during pre-training rather than post-training, and that the supervision signal must be both structurally dense and semantically aligned to the language modality to avoid the modality disconnect of text-decoupled vocabularies or the redundancy of purely semantic vocabularies.

## Results

VIVAS (4B) achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA results across 39 multimodal benchmarks, with particularly strong gains on fine-grained perception tasks: 25.7 on VisuLogic (+6.7 over Qwen3-VL 4B), 38.8 on OSWorld (+12.6 over Qwen3-VL 4B), 93.6 on RefCOCOval (+2.9 over Qwen3-VL 4B), and 74.6 on RealWorldQA (+3.7 over Qwen3-VL 4B). On general understanding, VIVAS scores 83.9 on MMBenchEN, 71.1 on MMStar, and 2384 on MME, matching or exceeding all 4B baselines. The vocabulary expansion incurs only 3.8% additional inference latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3-VL (4B), VIVAS wins on 11 of 12 selected benchmarks, with the largest margins on OSWorld (+12.6), VisuLogic (+6.7), and RealWorldQA (+3.7), while losing narrowly on DocVQA (94.4 vs. 95.3) and tying on MMBenchEN (83.9). Against LLaVA-OV1.5 (4B), VIVAS dominates on reasoning and perception tasks (MMStar +6.2, MathVista +8.6, RealWorldQA +6.8) but trails slightly on MMBenchEN (83.9 vs. 84.2). Against InternVL-3.5 (4B), VIVAS leads on MME (+112), RefCOCOval (+1.1), and OSWorld (38.8 vs. not reported), but falls behind on CRPE-relation (72.2 vs. 75.0) and MathVista (76.5 vs. 77.1). The ablation baseline (VIVAS w/o visual supervision) confirms that the visual supervision contributes +2.5 on VisuLogic, +5.5 on RefCOCOval, and +1.7 on HallusionBench, validating the core mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard VLM architecture (vision encoder + projector + LLM) by introducing visual supervision directly into pre-training via a unified token space, departing from both the conventional text-only autoregressive objective and the post-training visual alignment approaches (e.g., Vista, spatial forcing) that the authors argue are too late to reshape entrenched representations. The dense-structural-semantic tokenizer is a novel fusion of DINOv3's self-supervised structural features and SigLIP2's contrastive semantic features via cross-attention, discretized with IBQ, which is distinct from prior pixel-level (VQ-VAE) or purely semantic (SigLIP2-based) visual tokenizers. The unified token space paradigm itself builds on the autoregressive LLM backbone (Youtu-LLM) but expands the vocabulary to jointly predict visual and textual tokens, contrasting with the architectural separation approach that appends an auxiliary DiT head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable evidence that pre-training is the critical window for shaping visual representations in VLMs, and that the *type* of visual supervision matters as much as its presence: text-decoupled vocabularies actively degrade understanding, while purely semantic ones offer little beyond what the vision encoder already provides. The 3.8% inference overhead for SOTA across 39 benchmarks makes this a practically adoptable recipe for VLM developers. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or ultra-high-resolution inputs, whether the specific DINOv3+SigLIP2 pairing is optimal or whether other structural/semantic encoder combinations work equally well, and whether the unified token space paradigm scales cleanly to much larger model sizes or longer contexts without the vocabulary expansion becoming a bottleneck.
