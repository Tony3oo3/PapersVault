## Problem

Slot-based video object-centric learning produces two spatially misaligned maps: the encoder's attention map (sharp but noisy) and the decoder's object map (coherent but blurry). Existing dense alignment strategies (e.g., SRL) enforce agreement across all spatio-temporal patches, which propagates each branch's inherent weaknesses and incurs quadratic memory cost, limiting scalability to long or high-resolution videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260186/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames tokenized into an H×W patch grid over T timesteps, processed by a slot attention encoder and a lightweight MLP decoder. It produces object-centric slot representations alongside refined encoder attention maps and decoder object maps. The selective alignment operates on spatio-temporal neighborhoods (8-neighborhood spatial plus adjacent temporal frames), and the pseudo-labeling is generated on-the-fly from the model's own intermediate predictions, requiring no external supervision or additional architectural components.

## Method

SSync implements a selective mutual distillation principle: the encoder's boundary-sensitive cues supervise the decoder only at detected boundary patches, while the decoder's spatial coherence supervises the encoder only at interior patches. Boundary and interior regions are identified via local slot-consistency counts over spatio-temporal neighborhoods, yielding a linear-complexity pseudo-labeling scheme that replaces quadratic dense comparisons. To prevent over-fragmentation from corrupting pseudo-labels, a transitive merging step constructs a redundancy graph over slots using frame-averaged IoU of above-mean activation masks, then consolidates all slots within each connected component to a single dominant identity. The final training objective combines the base reconstruction and temporal contrastive losses with asymmetric MSE cross-distillation losses (encoder→decoder at boundaries, decoder→encoder at interiors), activated after a 30% warm-up phase.

## Results

On MOVi-C, SSync achieves 79.4 FG-ARI and 39.5 mBO, outperforming the previous best (SlotCurri: 77.6 / 32.8) by +1.8 FG-ARI and +6.7 mBO. On MOVi-E, SSync reaches 84.0 FG-ARI and 34.8 mBO, surpassing SlotContrast (82.9 / 29.2) and SRL (81.9 / 29.3). On YouTube-VIS, SSync attains the highest mBO (38.7) while remaining competitive on FG-ARI (42.6 vs. SlotCurri's 44.8). On image benchmarks, SSync achieves 86.0 FG-ARI on MOVi-E and 47.9 FG-ARI / 33.1 mBO on COCO2017, substantially outperforming SRL (42.8 / 29.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSync outperforms the direct predecessor SRL [14] on all three video benchmarks: +5.1 FG-ARI and +5.0 mBO on MOVi-C, +2.1 FG-ARI and +5.5 mBO on MOVi-E, and +3.1 mBO on YTVIS (with a marginal −0.3 FG-ARI gap). Against SlotContrast [21], SSync gains +10.1 FG-ARI and +6.8 mBO on MOVi-C, +1.1 FG-ARI and +5.6 mBO on MOVi-E, and +4.6 FG-ARI and +5.0 mBO on YTVIS. Compared to the strongest recent competitor SlotCurri [15], SSync leads by +1.8 FG-ARI and +6.7 mBO on MOVi-C, +0.3 FG-ARI and +5.9 mBO on MOVi-E, and +3.2 mBO on YTVIS (though SlotCurri retains a +2.2 FG-ARI edge on YTVIS). On image-level benchmarks, SSync achieves 86.0 FG-ARI on MOVi-E (vs. 84.9 for SlotCurri) and 47.9 FG-ARI / 33.1 mBO on COCO2017, surpassing SRL by +5.1 FG-ARI and +3.7 mBO. SSync also functions as a plug-and-play module, improving both RandSF.Q variants (VideoSAUR-based and SlotContrast-based) by 2–4 FG-ARI points under the RandSF.Q protocol. The largest absolute gains appear on mBO across all datasets, reflecting SSync's effectiveness at consolidating fragmented slot assignments into coherent object representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the slot attention encoder-decoder architecture (Locatello et al., 2020) and its video extensions, but its most immediate predecessor is SRL (Seong et al., ICLR 2026), which proposed dense contrastive alignment across all spatio-temporal patches to reconcile encoder attention maps with decoder object maps. SSync departs from SRL's core assumption that every patch provides equally reliable supervision, arguing instead that the encoder and decoder have complementary but region-specific expertise (sharp boundaries vs. coherent interiors). The method repurposes pseudo-labeling ideas from semi-supervised learning (FixMatch, Noisy Student) into the slot-learning context, augmented with a transitive merging mechanism to handle over-fragmentation that is unique to slot-based architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SSync reduces memory from quadratic to linear in patch count (60% VRAM savings), making dense alignment feasible for longer/higher-resolution videos, and its plug-and-play nature means it can be dropped into existing slot pipelines (demonstrated on VideoSAUR and SlotContrast variants). The robustness to slot count (S=7 to 15) alleviates a persistent practical pain point in VOCL where prior methods require careful per-dataset tuning. Open questions include whether the selective alignment principle generalizes to non-slot architectures (e.g., query-based or graph-based object discovery), whether the pseudo-labeling approach can address other encoder-decoder mismatches in generative models, and whether the transitive merging criterion can be made fully adaptive rather than relying on a per-dataset IoU threshold.
