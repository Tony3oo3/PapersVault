## Problem

Existing egocentric video datasets capture benign, everyday activities in controlled or semi-scripted settings, leaving a critical gap for evaluating video understanding models on real, high-stakes police body-worn camera (BWC) footage characterized by rapid camera motion, severe occlusions, and rare critical events. Commercial vendors have entered the BWC analysis space without published benchmarks, so the research community lacks instruments to characterize model limitations or stress-test them against footage that is often difficult to parse even for trained human observers.

## Input / Output

For the classification task, frozen feature extractors (CLIP, DINOv2, VideoMAE V2, X-CLIP, Hiera) consume video clips sampled at 16 fps and produce per-second action class predictions that are aggregated to window-level (1s, 10s, or 1min) by taking the maximum score across seconds. For the zero-shot MCQ task, image-based VLMs receive frames sampled at 1 fps (minimum 8 frames) while video-based VLMs use author-recommended sampling strategies; each question presents one correct action label plus four distractors (including 'None of the above') drawn from actions that never occurred in the clip. The dataset contains 9 action classes across three roles (BWC Wearer, Other Officer, Civilian) annotated at second-by-second granularity over 180+ hours of footage from 7 police departments and 2 news networks.

## Method

EgoPolice is constructed via a two-stage annotation pipeline: three independent annotators first flag interaction timestamps (union with 10-second buffers), then per-second labels are assigned across all action classes using objective, intent-free definitions to minimize inter-annotator disagreement (Krippendorff's α = 79.4%). A custom web-based annotation tool displays a grid of looping 1-second clips rather than a single video player, enabling faster labeling with less psychological discomfort. Evaluation uses 6-fold case-level cross-validation on Chicago COPA footage with two OOD splits (temporal: 2024 videos; location: Pasadena PD), plus auxiliary training data from other departments. The benchmarking protocol combines linear probing on frozen video/image representations with zero-shot multiple-choice QA across 12,000 questions, revealing that video-pretrained models (VideoMAE V2, X-CLIP) outperform image-only models, yet even Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves only 76.9% on 1-minute clips and struggles most on high-risk actions like 'Weapon Out' and 'Handcuffing'.

## Results

In the linear probing classification setting (Table 4), VideoMAE V2 achieves the best mean F1 of 58.4% on the 1-minute in-distribution (ID) split, outperforming DINOv2 (54.2%), X-CLIP (55.4%), CLIP (53.5%), and Hiera (48.8%). On the 1-second ID split, DINOv2 leads at 40.2% F1, with CLIP close behind at 39.2%. In the zero-shot VLM setting (Table 5), Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the highest accuracy at 76.9% on 1-minute clips, while GPT-4.1 reaches 74.4%; the best open-source model, InternVL3, scores only 60.6% on the same setting. Even the strongest model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) remains well below the reliability threshold for autonomous deployment in high-stakes policing contexts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five frozen feature extractors evaluated via linear probing, VideoMAE V2 (video-pretrained) consistently outperforms image-pretrained models (CLIP, DINOv2) on longer windows (10s and 1min), achieving 51.8% vs. 49.4% (DINOv2) and 48.5% (CLIP) at 10s ID, and 58.4% vs. 54.2% and 53.5% at 1min ID. However, DINOv2 leads on the 1-second ID split (40.2% vs. 37.9%), suggesting image-based appearance cues help for very short clips. X-CLIP shows the strongest out-of-distribution location generalization (45.3% OL at 10s vs. 35.3% for DINOv2), indicating its video-text contrastive pretraining aids cross-department transfer. In the zero-shot VLM setting, proprietary models (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 76.9%, GPT-4.1 at 74.4% on 1-min clips) substantially outperform all open-source models, with the best open-source model InternVL3 reaching only 60.6%. Notably, LLaVA-Mini (15.1%) and GPT-4.1-Nano (18.6%) perform at or below the 20% random baseline, failing to follow the multiple-choice format. The paper concludes that no current model—proprietary or open-source—achieves the accuracy needed for reliable autonomous deployment on BWC footage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of egocentric video understanding and law-enforcement analytics. Prior BWC research has focused almost exclusively on audio transcription and NLP-based analysis of officer-civilian dialogue, while the video stream itself remained largely unexplored. On the egocentric video side, datasets like Ego4D and EPIC-Kitchens capture routine, low-stakes activities with relatively stable cameras; EgoPolice departs from this by featuring severe, action-correlated camera motion, dense multi-person interactions, and rare high-stakes events. Methodologically, the evaluation reuses well-established paradigms—linear probing over frozen video/image encoders and zero-shot multiple-choice VLM evaluation—rather than proposing new architectures, positioning the contribution squarely as a benchmark and empirical study.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoPolice is the first benchmark that systematically quantifies why BWC footage is harder than standard video datasets: optical-flow analysis shows heavy-tailed motion specifically during annotated actions, and CLIP embedding analysis reveals near-zero inter-class separability (TVD 0.150 vs. 0.57 for Kinetics), meaning models cannot rely on scene shortcuts. The finding that even Gemini 2.5 Pro plateaus at ~77% on 1-minute clips underscores a significant gap between current model capabilities and the reliability needed for autonomous deployment in a domain where errors carry legal and safety consequences. Open questions include whether domain-specific pretraining or motion-robust architectures can close this gap, how to handle the inherent overrepresentation of high-stakes events in publicly released footage, and whether the human-in-the-loop deployment workflow generalizes beyond the single action class tested so far.
