## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods are locked into mutually exclusive paradigms: offline diffusion models achieve high quality via bidirectional attention but require the full sequence, while streaming models enforce strict causality and suffer from temporal myopia, missing semantics from complex multi-action prompts. No prior framework simultaneously supports future-aware streaming, high-quality offline generation, and bidirectional tasks like editing and in-betweening within a single model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence-level text prompt (embedded via CLIP) and produces a 272D SMPL-based motion sequence at 30 FPS, where each frame encodes root linear/angular velocities and per-joint positions, velocities, and 6D rotations for 22 joints—directly drivable by SMPL without inverse kinematics. For in-betweening, boundary poses are provided as fully denoised anchors; for editing, a subset of existing motion latents is fixed while the remainder is regenerated under a new prompt. The intermediate output is a frame-level action plan: 16D text latents temporally aligned to each motion frame, obtained by CLIP-encoding per-frame descriptions and compressing through a pretrained MLP autoencoder.

## Method

ActionPlan introduces a hierarchical two-stage diffusion framework where a Transformer denoiser (16 layers, 1024 hidden dim) jointly predicts frame-level action plan latents and motion latents in a Causal TAE continuous latent space. The key mechanism is latent-specific noise scheduling: each motion latent receives an independent timestep sampled via the t̄ algorithm, while all action latents share a single global timestep, enabling heterogeneous denoising states and flexible inference-time sampling orders. Training uses Rectified Flow with velocity parameterization and a masked loss that applies frame-level text supervision only where BABEL annotations exist, allowing the full HumanML3D-272 dataset to be used. At inference, Stage 1 denoises the complete action plan while all motion latents remain at pure noise; Stage 2 progressively denoises motion latents in overlapping windows—random order for offline mode, sequential raster order for streaming—conditioned on the generated action plan to preserve global semantic consistency even under causal constraints.

## Results

On HumanML3D-272, ActionPlan-Offline achieves FID 5.522, R-Precision@3 0.892, and MatchS 15.09, outperforming the best offline baseline MARDM (FID 7.044) by 22% in FID. In streaming mode, ActionPlan-Streaming reaches FID 5.735, surpassing the best streaming method MotionStreamer (FID 11.790) by 51% and even the best offline method MARDM by 18%. Runtime per motion token drops to 40 ms after the first token (vs. 210 ms for MARDM and 360 ms for MotionStreamer), yielding a 5.25×–9× speedup during continuous streaming.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActionPlan outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric in both offline and streaming modes. Against the strongest offline competitor MARDM [40] (FID 7.044), ActionPlan-Offline improves FID by 22% (5.522) and R@3 by 3.2 points (0.892 vs 0.860). Against the best streaming method MotionStreamer [61] (FID 11.790), ActionPlan-Streaming achieves a 51% FID reduction (5.735) while also being 5.25×–9× faster per token (40 ms vs 360 ms). Earlier diffusion baselines MDM [55] and MLD [11] trail by 4.2× and 3.3× in FID respectively, while VQ-based methods T2M-GPT [67], MotionGPT [27], and MoMask [20] all exceed FID 12. AttT2M [78] (FID 15.428) is the weakest among the non-diffusion baselines. Notably, ActionPlan-Streaming (FID 5.735) surpasses even the best offline baseline MARDM, demonstrating that the action-plan mechanism compensates for the lack of future context inherent in causal streaming.

## Relation to Prior Work

ActionPlan sits at the intersection of two previously incompatible paradigms in text-to-motion generation: offline bidirectional diffusion (e.g., MARDM) and causal streaming diffusion (e.g., MotionStreamer). It inherits the Causal TAE latent space and 272D pose representation from MotionStreamer [61], the frame-specific noise-level scheduling from Diffusion Forcing [7], and the t̄ timestep sampling plus per-latent variance regularization from Spatial Reasoning with Denoising Models [60]. The key conceptual departure is introducing a hierarchical frame-level action plan—generated first via a dedicated denoising stage—that serves as dense semantic anchors, effectively substituting for the bidirectional motion attention that streaming models lack. This makes ActionPlan the first method to generate textual action plans as a guiding blueprint before structured real-time motion synthesis, unifying offline, streaming, editing, and in-betweening in a single checkpoint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single model handles both high-quality offline generation and 5.25× faster streaming with no retraining, while also supporting zero-shot editing and in-betweening—capabilities that previously required separate architectures. The finding that a streaming model can outperform the best offline baseline by 18% in FID challenges the assumed quality-speed tradeoff in causal generation. However, the method is limited to whole-body motion without finger articulation, facial expression, or scene/object interaction, and all results are on a single dataset (HumanML3D-272). Open questions include whether the action-plan mechanism generalizes to multi-person interaction, object manipulation, or longer-horizon generation beyond the ~10-second sequences typical of the benchmark.
