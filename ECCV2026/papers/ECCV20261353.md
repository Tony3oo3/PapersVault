## Problem

Existing diffusion decoders for image tokenization suffer from two critical limitations: slow multi-step iterative sampling that bottlenecks downstream generative pipelines, and a reliance on GAN losses to achieve competitive reconstruction quality. Meanwhile, the dominant KL-VAE tokenizers use deterministic decoders that cannot model the distribution over reconstructions, limiting their ability to reduce distribution shift under high compression. No prior method achieves state-of-the-art continuous tokenization with a single-step, GAN-free diffusion decoder.

## Input / Output

The decoder receives a spatially downsampled latent grid z (e.g., 4 channels at H/8 × W/8 for the f8c4 configuration) and a Gaussian noise vector ε, and outputs a full-resolution RGB image. The latent is upsampled to pixel resolution and concatenated with the noised image along the channel dimension, while also modulating the network via adaptive normalization layers. By varying ε, the model produces diverse decodings of the same latent, enabling generative reconstruction rather than a single deterministic output. The architecture is tested at spatial downsampling factors f=8 and f=16 with c=4 or c=16 latent channels, operating on images from 128×128 up to 1024×1024.

## Method

SSDD introduces a hybrid U-Net/transformer decoder (based on U-ViT) with four levels of convolutional ResNet blocks and a central transformer operating on 8×8 pixel patches, using GEGLU activations, relative positional embeddings with a 17×17 local attention window, and dual conditioning via channel concatenation plus AdaGN/AdaLN. Training is entirely GAN-free, combining an optimal-transport flow-matching loss (predicting velocity x−ε with L2), an LPIPS perceptual loss on the single-step prediction, and a REPA feature-alignment loss that matches DINOv2-B reference features to the 4th transformer layer tokens through a 2-layer MLP. To eliminate multi-step inference, a lightweight distillation procedure fine-tunes the decoder at t=1 against a frozen 8-step teacher, preserving both the flow-matching and LPIPS objectives against teacher outputs rather than using a simple L2 regression. The model uses a shared KL-VAE encoder trained once with multi-scale augmentation, and a two-stage training schedule (128×128 pre-training followed by target-resolution fine-tuning) to reduce compute while maintaining multi-resolution generalization.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with the standard f8c4 encoder, SSDD-S (13.4M params, 1 step) achieves rFID 0.46, LPIPS 0.060, and DreamSim 0.039 at 1027 imgs/sec, outperforming KL-VAE (rFID 0.87, 705 imgs/sec) by 47% in rFID and 1.4× in throughput. Scaling to SSDD-XL (153.8M) further improves rFID to 0.35. Under the higher-compression f16c4 setting, SSDD-XL reduces rFID from 2.93 (KL-VAE) to 0.89, and when used with a DiT-XL/2 generator, the f16c4 configuration yields 3.8× faster end-to-end sampling (25.95 vs 7.05 imgs/sec) while matching or improving gFID (16.27 vs 19.71 without CFG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSDD dominates all baselines on perceptual reconstruction metrics (rFID, LPIPS, DreamSim) across every encoder configuration. Against KL-VAE (47.2M), SSDD-M (48.0M, matched parameter count) improves rFID from 0.87 to 0.39 and DreamSim from 0.046 to 0.034, while SSDD-S (13.4M) already beats KL-VAE on rFID (0.46 vs 0.87) at 1.4× higher throughput. Against the strongest diffusion decoder baseline ε-VAE-H (355.6M, 3 steps, rFID 0.38), SSDD-XL (153.8M, 1 step) achieves rFID 0.35 at 6.5× higher throughput. DiTo-XL-LPIPS (592.2M, 50 steps) is outperformed by even the smallest SSDD-S on rFID (0.46 vs 1.06) and is 1000× slower. The Consistency decoder (625.1M, 2 steps) is beaten by SSDD-S on rFID (0.46 vs 0.80) and throughput (1027 vs 23 imgs/s). The one area where SSDD trails is low-level distortion (PSNR/SSIM): ε-VAE-H achieves PSNR 29.49 vs SSDD-XL's 24.60, though the authors argue these metrics are less representative of perceived quality for generative decoders. For downstream generation with DiT-XL/2, SSDD-S (f8c4) improves gFID from 19.71 (KL-VAE) to 17.77 without CFG, and the f16c4 configuration with SSDD-L achieves 3.8× faster end-to-end sampling at comparable quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSDD builds on the U-ViT hybrid U-Net/transformer architecture (Hoogeboom et al., 2023) and the optimal-transport flow-matching objective (Lipman et al., 2023), combining them with the diffusion autoencoder conditioning scheme from DiTo (Chen et al., 2025) and ε-VAE (Zhao et al., 2025). The key departure is twofold: (i) it eliminates the GAN discriminator that prior diffusion decoders (DiTo, ε-VAE) still required for state-of-the-art reconstruction, replacing it with LPIPS perceptual loss and REPA feature alignment (Yu et al., 2025); and (ii) it introduces a single-step distillation procedure that preserves the full multi-step training objective rather than relying on L2 regression alone, enabling one-step decoding without the quality collapse typical of aggressive distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSDD provides the first GAN-free, single-step diffusion decoder that matches or exceeds KL-VAE on both reconstruction quality (rFID 0.39 vs. 0.87 at matched parameters) and throughput, making it a practical drop-in replacement for the KL-VAE backbone in existing DiT and flow-matching pipelines. The 3.8× speedup in end-to-end DiT sampling via the f16c4 encoder, with no measurable loss in generation FID, suggests that higher-compression tokenizers can now be adopted without the quality penalty that previously made them impractical. Open questions include whether the GAN-free recipe and single-step distillation transfer cleanly to video tokenization, whether the modest rFID cost of distillation (+0.07) can be eliminated, and whether the approach maintains its advantage at resolutions beyond 1024×1024 where the current evaluation is limited.
