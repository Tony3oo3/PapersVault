## Problem

Existing contact-centric grasp synthesis methods treat sparse geometric contact cues as hard constraints without explicit semantic grounding, leading to weak contact-intent coupling, poor constraint sensitivity, and pose drift. The core gap is that a single thumb-contact region is inherently ambiguous—it can correspond to multiple valid grasp organizations—yet prior work lacks a mechanism to infer the user's intended grasp mode from such underdetermined cues and propagate that intent consistently through generation and refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261744/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object mesh whose surface is partitioned into N regions via Capacity-Constrained Centroidal Voronoi Tessellation (target region diameter ~2 cm, so N scales with surface area) and a user-specified thumb-contact region expressed as a discrete region index. The output is a full MANO hand pose (joint parameters) that places the thumb on the designated region, is geometrically executable on the object (no interpenetration), and matches the inferred grasp organization. The object is represented as point features per region, and the thumb-contact region is derived from a predefined thumb anchor on the MANO mesh projected into object coordinates.

## Method

TaxoGrasp threads the Feix grasp taxonomy as a persistent semantic bottleneck through a three-stage pipeline. Stage I (Cognition) encodes each surface region with a shared Transformer, propagates context over a region adjacency graph via edge-conditioned Graph Transformer layers, and predicts a discrete Feix taxonomy label plus a unified thumb-aware condition embedding via masked aggregation and attention pooling. Stage II (Instantiation) samples an initial MANO pose from a Conditional Diffusion Transformer (DDPM, 1000 steps) in MANO parameter space, where the denoiser cross-attends to pose, object, and taxonomy tokens; the taxonomy feature is a medoid prototype per Feix class providing a structured organization prior beyond a discrete label. Stage III (Grounding) refines the pose via differentiable optimization using taxonomy-specific contact templates that partition hand anchors into contact and non-contact groups, applying soft-min distance minimization to attract contact anchors (thumb anchors restricted to the specified region) and SDF-based clearance penalties to repel non-contact anchors, alongside vertex interpenetration and swing-twist joint constraints.

## Results

On HOGraspNet, TaxoGrasp achieves the best score across all seven reported metrics: Contact Area (CA) of 120.1 mm², Maximum Penetration Depth (PD_max) of 0.340 mm, Mean Penetration Depth (PD_mean) of 0.131 mm, Penetration Volume (PV) of 1.265 mm³, Simulation Displacement (SIM) of 10.72 mm, cFID of 1.714, and Taxonomy Distribution Divergence (TDD) of 0.223. The taxonomy classifier in the Cognition stage reaches 72.8% Top-1 and 99.8% Top-3 accuracy on the test split. A user study with 20 participants confirms perceptual superiority over RegionGrasp and ClickDiff on interaction quality, pose quality, and intent consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaxoGrasp outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor RegionGrasp [37] (the only other method using a thumb-contact region as input), TaxoGrasp improves CA by 9.3 mm² (120.1 vs. 110.8), reduces PV by 70.6% (1.265 vs. 4.309), lowers cFID by 42.2% (1.714 vs. 2.968), and halves TDD (0.223 vs. 0.435), indicating substantially better intent consistency and distributional fidelity. Against ClickDiff [24], which achieves the lowest penetration among baselines (PD_max 0.392), TaxoGrasp further reduces PD_max to 0.340 and PD_mean to 0.131 while also achieving the largest contact area (120.1 vs. 102.5), showing that better contact is not obtained at the cost of more interpenetration. Against GraspTTA [20] and ContactGen [28], the gaps are even larger: PV is reduced by 84.4% and 76.7% respectively, and cFID drops from 11.75/6.754 to 1.714. The paper also reports that TaxoGrasp achieves the highest Hit Rate for thumb-contact constraint adherence (Fig. 4), a metric not directly comparable to the other baselines since only RegionGrasp shares the same sparse thumb-region input format.

## Relation to Prior Work

TaxoGrasp extends the contact-centric grasp synthesis paradigm (RegionGrasp, ContactGen, ClickDiff, GraspTTA) by introducing the Feix grasp taxonomy as a persistent semantic bottleneck threaded through all three pipeline stages, rather than treating sparse contact cues as hard geometric constraints to enforce. It builds on CPF's differentiable hand anchors for the Grounding stage and on conditional diffusion transformers for pose synthesis, but departs from prior taxonomy-conditioned work (Dexonomy, OmniDexVLG) by supervising taxonomy inference from object geometry and thumb-contact region rather than treating taxonomy as a fixed user input. The key conceptual shift is reframing sparse-contact synthesis as a supervised intent-inference problem (classification) followed by taxonomy-anchored generation, rather than a pure constraint-satisfaction problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in interactive grasp synthesis: sparse contact cues are underdetermined and prior contact-centric methods produce semantically inconsistent grasps that violate user intent. The taxonomy-as-bottleneck formulation is a clean and reusable architectural idea that could transfer to other underdetermined generation tasks. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (HOGraspNet) with post-hoc thumb-contact annotations, the taxonomy classifier's 72.8% Top-1 accuracy limits reliability, and the per-instance single-label supervision prevents multi-modal taxonomy inference—key open questions for scaling to truly interactive, multi-modal grasp synthesis.
