## Problem

Existing driving NVS benchmarks repurpose single-trajectory datasets where train and test frames are sampled from the same vehicle path, testing only interpolation rather than true generalization to unseen viewpoints. Recording identical dynamic scenes from multiple viewpoints with a single vehicle is infeasible due to traffic constraints, and concurrent efforts rely on synthetic data or assume static scenes. This leaves a critical gap in evaluating NVS under the large viewpoint variations that real driving simulation demands.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 1080×1920 RGB frames at 60 FPS from GoPro 10 sensors mounted on a car (left and center cameras), a scooter, and a drone, all synchronized via wall-clock. For dynamic NVS methods, monocular depth from DaV3 is aligned to COLMAP SfM depth via a per-scene affine scale-and-shift, and dynamic object masks are obtained by intersecting DroneSplat motion masks with SAM-segmented vehicle and pedestrian regions. The benchmark produces rendered novel views scored by PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, and additionally evaluates camera pose estimation accuracy via epipolar error computed on manually annotated pixel correspondences.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-vehicle data collection and pose-verification pipeline rather than a new rendering architecture. Camera poses are estimated in two steps—COLMAP registers training images first, then localizes test images against the 15 nearest training neighbors—avoiding the pose degradation that joint registration causes under large viewpoint diversity. Poses are verified using epipolar geometry: human annotators mark bounding boxes of shared 3D regions, RoMA produces dense pixel correspondences within those boxes, and sequences are retained only if maximum epipolar error stays below 30 pixels. Two evaluation protocols (Eval-Car-Train and Eval-Drone-Train) test cross-vehicle generalization by training on one platform's camera stream and rendering views from another, with additional same-trajectory test sets for comparison. The benchmark covers optimization-based methods (NeRF, 3DGS, PVG, DesireGS, DroneSplat) and feed-forward 3DGS methods (DepthSplat, MonoSplat, MVSplat) at 2, 6, and 12 context views, plus feed-forward pose estimators (Reloc3R, MASt3R-SfM, MapAnything, VGGT) against COLMAP.

## Results

On the standard same-trajectory test set T_{C→C} (Eval-Car-Train), PVG achieves the best PSNR of 27.01 (SSIM 0.72, LPIPS 0.20), followed by DesireGS (25.96) and 3DGS (24.22). Performance degrades sharply under cross-vehicle evaluation: on T_{C→S} (car-to-scooter), PVG drops to 20.23 PSNR, and on the aerial-to-ground T_{D→C} split, even the best method (PVG) reaches only 12.65 PSNR. Feed-forward 3DGS methods (DepthSplat, MonoSplat, MVSplat) consistently trail optimization-based methods by 5–10 dB PSNR across all test sets, with the gap widening under larger viewpoint changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVG (optimization-based, dynamic) dominates the Eval-Car-Train setup, achieving the highest PSNR on all three test sets (27.01 / 25.44 / 20.23), outperforming DesireGS by ~1–1.5 dB and 3DGS by ~3–6 dB. In Eval-Drone-Train, 3DGS edges out PVG on the same-trajectory T_{D→D} (29.13 vs. 28.12 PSNR), but PVG recovers the lead on cross-vehicle T_{D→C} and T_{D→S} (12.65/13.82 vs. 10.36/11.42). The aerial-specific DroneSplat is notably outperformed by general-purpose 3DGS and PVG on all cross-vehicle splits, suggesting aerial-specific priors do not transfer well to ground-level synthesis. All three feed-forward 3DGS methods (DepthSplat, MonoSplat, MVSplat) trail the best optimization-based methods by 5–10 dB PSNR, with MVSplat performing worst overall (12.88 PSNR on T_{C→S}). For camera pose estimation, COLMAP (optimization-based SfM) produces mean epipolar errors below 30 px, while all four feed-forward pose estimators (Reloc3R, MASt3R-SfM, MapAnything, VGGT) yield errors exceeding 50 px, with the gap most pronounced on the wide-baseline T_{D→C} aerial-to-ground split.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first real-world, multi-vehicle synchronized dataset (car, scooter, drone) for novel view synthesis, departing from prior driving NVS benchmarks (KITTI, Waymo, nuScenes, Argoverse) that only capture a single vehicle trajectory and thus test interpolation rather than true cross-trajectory generalization. Concurrent efforts address parts of this gap—XLD uses synthetic CARLA data, MTGS and Para-lane collect multi-traversal scans but assume static scenes—while MV² captures dynamic scenes from genuinely independent vehicle trajectories. On the method side, the paper repurposes existing per-scene optimization (NeRF, 3DGS, PVG, DesireGS) and feed-forward 3DGS (DepthSplat, MonoSplat, MVSplat) approaches as benchmarks, and introduces a COLMAP-based two-step pose estimation pipeline with epipolar-geometry verification as the dataset's quality-control backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that current NVS methods, which perform well under standard same-trajectory interpolation, degrade substantially under cross-vehicle viewpoint changes, and that aerial-to-ground synthesis is dramatically harder (e.g., 3DGS drops from 29.1 to 10.4 PSNR), suggesting driving simulation via NVS remains far from practical for lane-change or multi-agent scenarios. The finding that the aerial-specific droneSplat is outperformed by general-purpose 3DGS and PVG on aerial-to-ground tasks challenges the assumption that domain-specific architectures transfer across the aerial-ground gap. Open questions include whether larger-scale multi-vehicle captures, better depth priors, or explicit cross-trajectory training can close the extrapolation gap, and whether the 50-scene scale is sufficient to draw robust conclusions about method rankings.
