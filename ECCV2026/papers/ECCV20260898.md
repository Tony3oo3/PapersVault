## Problem

VLM-based embodied reasoning systems rely on fixed, pre-captured viewpoints from episodic RGB-D memories, which inherently miss query-relevant evidence due to occlusion, object truncation, and suboptimal view composition. Language-embedded 3DGS methods overcome the fixed-view limitation but depend on static embedding similarity that fails on compositional or spatially-referential queries. The paper asks whether integrating novel view synthesis directly into the VLM reasoning loop can recover the missing visual evidence that fixed-view memories cannot provide.

## Input / Output

The method takes N posed RGB images (with 4×4 extrinsic matrices) and a natural-language query (a question for EQA or a referring expression for grounding) as input. It produces a text answer for embodied question answering, or a set of activated 3D Gaussians representing the grounded region for 3D object localization and referring segmentation. The pipeline is entirely training-free: it uses a pre-trained 3DGS reconstruction, a closed-set detector (YOLOv8) or open-set CLIP+SAM features for semantic registration, and off-the-shelf VLMs (GPT-4o or Qwen3-VL-8B) for reasoning, with no fine-tuning of any component.

## Method

SplatReasoner performs a two-stage, occlusion-aware view selection over a language-embedded 3DGS scene. In the initial stage, an LLM extracts evidence categories from the query, matching Gaussians are activated (by category label or CLIP similarity), clustered via HDBSCAN into instance-level candidates, and the best pre-recorded view per cluster is chosen by a visibility score that accounts for occluding Gaussians through per-pixel maximum rendering-weight attribution. In the final stage, four novel views (left, right, forward, backward perturbations) are synthesized around each initial view via 3DGS rendering; a VLM-as-Judge independently evaluates each candidate view's rendered image against the query, distills per-view text descriptions, and an LLM selects the most informative answer. A verification step retains a novel view only if it supplies evidence absent from the initial view. For 3D grounding, the activated Gaussians are further restricted to the union of final-view frustums before the VLM reasoning output determines the grounded region.

## Results

On the EM-EQA benchmark (OpenEQA), SplatReasoner achieves 57.8 LLM-Match with GPT-4o and 51.6 with Qwen3-VL-8B, outperforming the strongest baseline 3D-Mem (54.6 / 50.8) while using fewer average frames (2.6 vs. 2.7). On 3D grounding, SplatReasoner attains 11.12 3D mIoU on ScanRefer and 6.32 on Multi3DRefer, surpassing Dr. Splat (9.44 / 4.14) and ReferSplat (3.14 / 1.25) by substantial margins. For 3D referring segmentation, the method reaches 12.46 3D mIoU, exceeding Dr. Splat (10.56) and ReferSplat (2.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatReasoner outperforms all six baselines across both task families. On EM-EQA, it beats 3D-Mem by +3.2 LLM-Match (57.8 vs. 54.6) with GPT-4o and by +0.8 with Qwen3-VL-8B, while also reducing average frames from 2.7 to 2.6. The text-only baselines (BlindLLM at 34.8, CG Captions at 36.5, SVM Captions at 38.9) and the naive Multi-Frame approach (49.1) are all substantially below SplatReasoner, confirming that query-conditioned view selection over 3DGS is critical. On 3D grounding, SplatReasoner surpasses Dr. Splat (sentence) by +1.68 3D mIoU on ScanRefer (11.12 vs. 9.44) and by +2.18 on Multi3DRefer (6.32 vs. 4.14), with the gap widening on Acc@8 (29.59 vs. 15.40 on Multi3DRefer). ReferSplat, despite being designed for fine-grained textual descriptions, lags far behind (3.14 3D mIoU on ScanRefer) because its rendering-based optimization is incompatible with direct 3D search. The verification stage contributes an additional +3.3 LLM-Match (57.8 vs. 54.5) on the full 1,623-question set, showing that novel-view adjustment recovers evidence missed by initial views.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatReasoner sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: (1) episodic-memory embodied reasoning systems like 3D-Mem and OpenEQA, which reason over fixed pre-captured RGB-D views, and (2) language-embedded 3D Gaussian Splatting methods like Dr. Splat and ReferSplat, which attach CLIP or category features to Gaussians for static similarity-based retrieval. The key departure is treating 3DGS not as a static queryable representation but as a dynamic novel-view synthesis engine within a VLM reasoning loop: an LLM extracts evidence categories, a visibility score selects initial views, and a VLM-as-Judge mechanism evaluates synthesized perturbed views to pick the most informative perspectives. This bridges the gap between the rich 3D geometry of 3DGS and the compositional, context-sensitive reasoning that 2D VLMs provide, without requiring any fine-tuning of the VLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a concrete and common failure mode of fixed-view embodied agents—occlusion, truncation, and suboptimal view composition—by actively synthesizing viewpoints that were never captured, all while keeping the number of frames fed to the VLM compact (~2.6). The finding that a text-only VLM-as-Judge outperforms image+text variants (Table 5) suggests that per-view textual distillation is a more robust aggregation strategy than multi-image prompting, which has practical implications for any pipeline that must reason over many candidate views. However, the gap to human performance remains large (57.8 vs. 86.8 LLM-Match), the system is limited to passive episodic-memory scenarios rather than active navigation, and the 3D grounding gains, while consistent, are moderate in absolute terms. Extending the framework to active, long-horizon exploration with sequential view selection is the most natural and impactful next step.
