## Problem

Current video generation models achieve high visual fidelity but lack explicit physical controllability and plausibility, relying on data-driven imitation rather than causal understanding of physical rules. Existing physics-based approaches either impose coarse single-object property predictions that fail to capture real-world multi-material scenes, or use simulators that are too rigid to support fine-grained temporal control, resulting in short and monotonous dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262006/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image plus a global text prompt and optional part-level text prompts (e.g., 'metallic blade', 'rubber handle') as input. The image is segmented into instances via Grounded-SAM, reconstructed as dense meshes via InstantMesh, and uniformly sampled into point clouds. The output is a per-point material field consisting of a discrete material model class (6 categories) and continuous parameters (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, density), which drives a physics simulator whose trajectories condition a pre-trained video model (Wan2.2 Fun-5B-Control) to produce the final video.

## Method

PhysChoreo is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (part-aware physics reconstruction) injects part-level semantics into point features via soft assignment—projecting points and part-prompt embeddings into a shared alignment space and computing a per-point softmax distribution over prompts—followed by hierarchical cross-attention that first attends to a global text token for scene-level consistency, then to part tokens for local refinement. A Set Transformer encoder aggregates non-local context, and point-wise heads decode material class and continuous parameters. Training uses four coupled losses: per-point regression/classification, within-part wave-speed smoothness (penalizing spatial discontinuities in longitudinal and shear wave speeds), inter-part contrastive separation in log-domain shear/bulk modulus space, and cross-entropy on the soft-assignment logits. Stage 2 (physics-editable simulation) introduces temporal control into MPM and rigid-body solvers, allowing per-object modulation of constitutive parameters, force fields, and momentum at each simulation step with exponential smoothing and wave-speed-bounded stiffness scaling, so that counterfactual interventions (liquefying on collision, collapsing on landing) remain physically stable without restarting the simulation.

## Results

On physical property prediction, PhysChoreo achieves 78.9% material accuracy and a log-E error of 0.661, substantially outperforming NeRF2Physics (62.8%, 2.033), PUGS (28.3%, 2.778), and Pixie (34.9%, 4.129). For physics-controllable video generation, PhysChoreo scores 4.70/5 on Semantic Alignment, 4.55/5 on Physical Commonsense, and 4.75/5 on Visual Quality (VLM-evaluated), yielding the best average of 4.67 versus Veo 3.1's 4.40. In a 31-participant user study, PhysChoreo is preferred 50.1% of the time overall, with 67.4% preference on semantic alignment, far exceeding the next-best method (Veo 3.1 at 16.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For physical property prediction, PhysChoreo dominates all three baselines: it improves material accuracy by 16.1 points over NeRF2Physics (78.9% vs. 62.8%) and by 44.0 points over Pixie (78.9% vs. 34.9%), while reducing log-E error from 2.033 (NeRF2Physics) to 0.661. For video generation, PhysChoreo wins on Semantic Alignment (4.70) and Physical Commonsense (4.55) against every baseline, including the closed-source Veo 3.1 (4.10 and 4.20 respectively), thanks to its explicit physics simulation driving motion. However, Veo 3.1 retains a slight edge on Visual Quality (4.90 vs. 4.75), reflecting the rendering limitations of the underlying Wan2.2 video model. Despite this, PhysChoreo achieves the highest average score (4.67 vs. 4.40) and a decisive 50.1% preference rate in the user study, compared to 16.1% for Veo 3.1 and 14.1% for PhysCtrl. The physics-based baselines (PhysGen3D, PhysCtrl) trail significantly on both alignment and realism, confirming that simulation-guided generation with part-level property control is critical for instruction-following physical dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysChoreo extends the physics-based video generation line (PhysGaussian, PhysGen, PhysCtrl, PhysMotion) by addressing two gaps those methods leave open: coarse object-level physics modeling and the absence of temporal control over material properties during simulation. For physical property estimation, it departs from multi-view VLM-based approaches (NeRF2Physics, PUGS) and voxel-based direct estimation (Pixie) by operating on single-image-reconstructed point clouds with part-level text conditioning via soft assignment and hierarchical cross-attention. The physics-editable simulation mechanism, which smoothly interpolates constitutive parameters and force fields over time within an MPM/rigid-body solver, is a novel control layer not present in prior physics-guided generation pipelines that rely solely on initial conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that part-level semantic grounding combined with temporally continuous physics editing can produce counterfactual yet physically consistent dynamics (liquefying on collision, material transformations mid-sequence), a capability absent from both pure generative video models and prior simulation-based methods. The released text-part-physics dataset (9,580 samples, 24 categories, with counterfactual labels) provides a reusable benchmark for per-part physics estimation. Key limitations remain: the method targets independent objects rather than large-scale interactive scenes, and internal volumetric physical states are approximated via surface-to-interior propagation rather than directly predicted, which constrains accuracy for thick or heterogeneous objects.
