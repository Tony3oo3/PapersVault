## Problem

Despite Vision Transformers' strong generalization, their internal representation of world knowledge—what concepts they encode and how those concepts interact across layers—remains opaque. Existing interpretability methods (saliency maps, feature-importance scores) treat the network as a black box, and SAE-based circuit discovery primarily captures input-dependent behavior, making it difficult to recover an input-invariant, global view of concept-to-concept structure without expensive dataset-scale attribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained ViT backbone (CLIP-ViT-B/32, DINO, or ImageNet-supervised ViT-B/16) and standard RGB images as input. It produces two complementary directed graphs: a global concept circuit (input-invariant, read directly from learned CLT encoder/decoder weights, with edges reweighted by feature co-activation statistics) and an instance concept circuit (input-dependent, a pruned subgraph of active concepts and attribution-patched edges for a specific prediction). Downstream, these circuits yield automatic spurious-correlation labels (spurious, causal, or non-discriminative) and steered class predictions after mean-ablating identified shortcut concepts.

## Method

The core contribution is adapting Cross-Layer Transcoders (CLTs)—sparse cross-layer dictionaries originally designed for language models—to ViTs, where the CLT encodes each layer's MLP input into sparse hidden activations and reconstructs downstream MLP outputs using all prior-layer activations, trained jointly with a sparsity penalty (L0=35, dictionary size 6144) on image tokens plus CLS tokens. Because CLT encoder/decoder weights are input-invariant, the global concept circuit is read directly from these parameters: edge weights between features s and t are computed as inner products of s's decoder vectors with t's encoder vector across intermediate layers, then reweighted by co-activation frequency to suppress spurious connections from the residual stream. For instance circuits, the method computes attribution-patching weights between sparse feature activations, aggregates edges across the ~196 token positions into a single node per feature, and prunes by cumulative logit contribution to yield a compact, interpretable subgraph. This two-level factorization (input-invariant weights for global structure, input-dependent sparse codes for instance behavior) is what distinguishes CLTs from SAEs, which lack a principled input-invariant interaction structure and require costly averaging to approximate a global view.

## Results

On the Waterbird dataset, the CLT-based instance concept circuit achieves 0.71 steering accuracy on the worst group (waterbirds on land), outperforming the original model (0.48) by 23% and the best baseline SpLiCE (0.60) by 11%. On the ImageNet validation set, mean-ablating the top-10 concepts identified by the global concept circuit drops CLIP accuracy from 0.60 to 0.22, while random ablation only reduces it to 0.66, confirming the circuits capture functionally important structure. For instance circuit faithfulness on CLIP, the CLT-based circuit surpasses the SAE-based circuit by 24.4% on faithfulness and 22.1% on completeness in average across 50 ImageNet classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three named baselines for spurious correlation removal on Waterbird: SpLiCE, SAE, and BatchTopKSAE. The CLT-based method achieves 0.71 accuracy on the worst group (waterbirds on land), beating SpLiCE (0.60) by 11 percentage points, SAE (0.53) by 18 points, and BatchTopKSAE (0.56) by 15 points. SpLiCE requires manual identification of spurious concepts (e.g., 'bamboo', 'forest'), whereas the CLT method uses an automated set-difference strategy over instance circuits, yet still achieves superior performance. On the faithfulness evaluation (Fig. 5), the CLT-based instance circuit consistently outperforms the SAE-based circuit across all three models (CLIP, DINO, supervised ViT) and all graph sparsity levels, with the largest gap on CLIP (24.4% faithfulness, 22.1% completeness). The random ablation baseline (0.22) confirms that the gains come from correctly identifying spurious concepts rather than simply ablating more features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Cross-Layer Transcoders (CLTs), originally developed for language model circuit tracing (Ameisen et al., 2025), to Vision Transformers, leveraging their input-invariant encoder/decoder weights to extract global concept circuits directly from learned parameters. It departs from the dominant SAE-based pipeline (Bricken et al., 2023; Huben et al., 2023), which captures primarily input-dependent feature interactions and requires expensive dataset-scale attribution to approximate a global view. The paper also builds on attribution patching (Nanda, 2022) for instance-level circuit extraction but argues that CLT-based circuits are more faithful than SAE-based ones. Positioning-wise, it moves beyond saliency maps and feature-importance scores that treat model internals as a black box, instead recovering structured, interpretable concept-to-concept graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to read input-invariant global concept circuits directly from CLT weights—without dataset-scale attribution—opens a path to scalable, automated auditing of vision foundation models, particularly for detecting spurious correlations without domain-specific human annotation. The demonstrated 11% steering improvement on Waterbird and the model-comparison insights (CLIP's dense deep-layer routing vs. DINO's shallow/middle-layer focus) provide a mechanistic lens for understanding how supervision paradigms shape behavior. Key limitations remain: CLTs capture only MLP pathways and only indirectly reflect attention structure, concept interpretation still requires human inspection, and the global graphs do not fully encode causal relations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger ViT variants, whether attention-mediated interactions can be incorporated, and whether the co-activation reweighting strategy generalizes beyond ImageNet-scale data.
