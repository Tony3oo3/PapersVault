## Problem

Deploying video anomaly detection in the real world is constrained by the scarcity, privacy, and cost of collecting real abnormal footage. Prior pseudo-anomaly synthesis works only supplement rather than replace real anomalies, and rely on rule-based edits or narrow generative priors. This paper asks whether a high-accuracy VAD detector can be trained entirely without any real abnormal videos, using only diffusion-synthesized pseudo-anomalies generated from a small set of normal images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261537/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For training, the method consumes real normal surveillance videos, a small pool of real normal images (used as I2V seeds), and textual class names of abnormal categories; no real abnormal videos are used. Pseudo-anomaly synthesis runs the Wan 2.2 I2V model at 832×480 resolution with 81-frame clips, conditioned on CLIP-selected initial images and VLM-refined prompts. At inference, the detector takes a surveillance video clip, extracts segment features via a Qwen2.5-VL 7B-Instruct backbone (3,584-D embeddings), and outputs per-frame anomaly scores through a MIL scoring head with dual memory banks.

## Method

PA-VAD comprises two components. The Class-Aware Pseudo-Anomaly Generator (CA-PAG) synthesizes pseudo-abnormal videos by (i) selecting class-relevant initial images from the normal pool via CLIP Top-K scoring with scene-balanced ranking, and (ii) refining raw class labels into scene-consistent prompts using a Qwen3 30B-A3B VLM before driving the Wan 2.2 I2V diffusion model. The Domain-Aligned Regularized Module (DARM) addresses a key empirical discovery that pseudo anomalies exhibit a spatiotemporal magnitude bias—feature norms inflate up to 20× over real anomalies in the detector space—causing MIL Top-k to latch onto magnitude shortcuts. DARM combines a DANN-based adversarial domain-alignment loss between real and pseudo normal streams with a usage-aware memory update that reweights slot updates by inverse usage, pulling under-utilized abnormal prototypes toward their responsibility-weighted centers. The full objective augments the UR-DMU MIL discrimination loss with these two regularizers, enabling stable training on pseudo-only supervision while preserving prototype diversity across anomaly modes.

## Results

PA-VAD with DARM achieves 98.2% frame-level AUC on ShanghaiTech, 82.5% on UCF-Crime, and 95.1% on XD-Violence, all trained exclusively on real normal videos and diffusion-synthesized pseudo-abnormal clips (no real abnormal footage). On ShanghaiTech it surpasses the strongest real-abnormal WVAD baseline CMRL (97.6%) by +0.6 points, and on XD-Violence it exceeds UR-DMU (94.2%) by +0.9 points. On UCF-Crime, where minute-long context-heavy anomalies are harder to synthesize, PA-VAD (82.5%) remains below the best Real/Real WVAD methods (UR-DMU 87.0%, CMRL 86.1%) but still outperforms the UVAD state-of-the-art MGSTRL (80.6%) by +1.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UVAD methods, PA-VAD with DARM dominates across all three datasets: it outperforms MGSTRL (the strongest UVAD) by +10.7 points on SHT (98.2 vs. 87.5) and +1.9 points on Crime (82.5 vs. 80.6), while also exceeding HSC, LERF, and SeeKer by wide margins on SHT. Against WVAD baselines that use real abnormal videos, PA-VAD wins on SHT (98.2 vs. CMRL's 97.6, +0.6) and XD-Violence (95.1 vs. UR-DMU's 94.2, +0.9; vs. VERA's 88.3, +6.8), but loses on UCF-Crime to UR-DMU (82.5 vs. 87.0, −4.5) and CMRL (82.5 vs. 86.1, −3.6), which the authors attribute to the difficulty of synthesizing long, context-heavy anomalies. Compared to prior pseudo-data generation work (Cai et al. [3], which still mixes pseudo with real anomalies), PA-VAD achieves competitive SHT performance (98.2 vs. 89.3 with LAP) while using no real abnormal clips at all. Against SF-VAD/FPL [5], which uses single-frame-level supervision plus real abnormal videos, PA-VAD is within 0.2 points on SHT (98.2 vs. 98.4) but trails on Crime (82.5 vs. 89.9). In open-set evaluations, PA-VAD outperforms OpenVAD by up to +16.6 points (XD, 1 seen class: 89.08 vs. 72.50) and consistently leads on Crime across all seen-class settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PA-VAD builds on the UR-DMU dual-memory-bank classifier [36] and the MIL ranking formulation of Sultani et al. [18], but departs fundamentally from both UVAD (normal-only) and WVAD (real-abnormal) training regimes by introducing a pseudo-only middle ground where all abnormal supervision comes from diffusion-synthesized video. Unlike prior pseudo-anomaly works (Cai et al. [3], Rai et al. [16]) that augment real abnormal clips with generated ones, PA-VAD eliminates real abnormal data entirely, using CLIP-guided image selection and VLM-driven prompt refinement to anchor generation to class-relevant normal scenes. The DARM module repurposes DANN-style adversarial domain alignment [9] not for cross-domain transfer but for suppressing a newly identified magnitude bias between real and pseudo normal streams, combined with a usage-aware memory update that has no direct analogue in prior VAD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: high-accuracy VAD becomes deployable without collecting real abnormal footage, sidestepping privacy, safety, and rarity constraints that have limited real-world adoption. The discovery that pseudo anomalies inflate detector-space feature norms by over 20× relative to real anomalies (and that this bias specifically corrupts MIL Top-k selection) is a cautionary finding for any pipeline that substitutes synthetic for real data in weakly supervised settings. The main open limitation is UCF-Crime, where minute-long, context-heavy anomalies remain difficult for current video generators to synthesize faithfully, suggesting the approach's ceiling is tied to generative-model quality. Future work on improving motion priors and extending to longer temporal dependencies will determine whether pseudo-only training can close the remaining gap on harder benchmarks.
