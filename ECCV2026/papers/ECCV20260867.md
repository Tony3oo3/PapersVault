## Problem

LM-based VLN-CE agents assume clean visual inputs, yet real-world navigation exposes them to diverse corruptions (noise, blur, weather, sensor artifacts) that can halve navigation success rates. This is the first work to explicitly formulate and address image decorruption for VLN in continuous environments, where existing restoration methods fail to enforce temporal consistency, action-level coherence, and real-time latency simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260867/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FlowDec takes a corrupted RGB image (encoded to latent via a pretrained VAE) and an atomic action label (e.g., forward, turn left, turn right) predicted by the downstream LM-based VLN agent, and outputs a decorrupted RGB image. At inference, the flow-matching integration starts from a partially corrupted latent z_0' = t_θ·z_0 + (1−t_θ)·z_cor rather than pure noise, reducing randomness and accelerating generation. The method is model-agnostic: it operates as a plug-in visual module independent of the navigation backbone.

## Method

FlowDec performs latent conditional flow matching with a U-Net velocity field, trained via a hybrid temporal conditioning strategy that dynamically schedules three condition types: single-frame (c₁), previous-frame difference against ground truth (c₂), and previous-frame difference against the model's own denoised output (c₃), the latter bridging the synthetic-to-real domain gap. At inference, an action-centroid guided filtering mechanism computes the Mahalanobis distance between inter-frame latent differences and per-action centroid distributions (precomputed from 2.4M+ image pairs across 70+ scenes); if the distance exceeds a threshold, the model re-generates using the temporal condition c₃ and blends outputs via inverse-distance weighting. This selective temporal invocation prevents error accumulation while preserving single-frame stability. By leveraging flow matching's single-step integration instead of iterative diffusion sampling, FlowDec achieves 3×–8× faster decorruption than diffusion-based TTA baselines while maintaining superior navigation success.

## Results

On R2R-CE, FlowDec achieves an average SR of 27.04% and SPL of 23.62% across six corruption types, yielding a 25.33% relative SR improvement over the unmodified NaVid baseline (21.58% SR). On RxR-CE, FlowDec reaches 33.63% average SR and 26.31% SPL, a 9.37% relative SR gain over NaVid (30.75%). Inference latency is 173 ms per frame for FlowDec versus 1,411 ms for Dec-DPM and 588 ms for Dec-CM, a 3×–8× speedup. FlowDec also achieves the lowest average warp error (0.11 on R2R-CE, 0.13 on RxR-CE), confirming superior temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowDec outperforms all four baselines on every corruption type and both datasets. Against the unmodified NaVid backbone, FlowDec lifts average SR by 5.46 points on R2R-CE (27.04 vs. 21.58) and by 2.88 points on RxR-CE (33.63 vs. 30.75), with the largest gains under fog (+9.58 SR on R2R-CE) and contrast (+10.35 SR on R2R-CE). Compared to the diffusion-based TTA baselines Dec-DPM and Dec-CM (both from [34]), FlowDec achieves higher SR and SPL across all six corruptions; the gap is especially pronounced on RxR-CE where Dec-DPM drops to 25.01% average SR versus FlowDec's 33.63%. SCUNet, the blind denoising baseline, collapses catastrophically under structured corruptions (e.g., 0.05% SR on R2R-CE contrast, 6.73% on RxR-CE contrast), confirming that pixel-level denoising alone is insufficient for navigation. In terms of speed, FlowDec's 173 ms per-frame overhead is 3× faster than Dec-CM (588 ms) and 8× faster than Dec-DPM (1,411 ms), making it the only method practical for real-time deployment over ~70-step episodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowDec builds directly on latent conditional flow matching (Dao et al., 2023) as its generative backbone and adopts the corruption modeling and augmentation pipeline (PIXMIX + SimSiam) from Decorruptor (Oh et al., ECCV 2024). It departs from prior image restoration work in two key ways: (1) it replaces iterative diffusion sampling with single-step flow matching to meet real-time latency constraints, and (2) it introduces navigation-specific conditioning—hybrid temporal conditions and action-centroid guided filtering—that explicitly optimize for inter-frame consistency and action coherence rather than perceptual quality. Unlike blind denoising (SCUNet) or frame-independent diffusion TTA (Dec-DPM/Dec-CM), FlowDec is designed as a plug-in module that operates in the latent space of a frozen VLN agent without retraining the navigation backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to systematically study and address visual corruption robustness for LM-based VLN in continuous environments, a gap that becomes critical as these agents move toward real-world deployment. The 3–8× speed advantage over diffusion-based TTA and the model-agnostic design make FlowDec practically deployable on resource-constrained robots, validated on a Unitree GO2 platform. Open questions remain: whether the action-centroid statistics generalize to unseen action spaces or continuous (non-atomic) control, how the method handles dynamically varying or compound corruptions, and whether the domain gap between synthetic training corruptions and real sensor degradation can be further closed without real-world data.
