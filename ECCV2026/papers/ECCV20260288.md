limitations: All experiments are restricted to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 generation, and the method requires a frozen DINOv2 encoder during training (though not at inference), which adds training compute. surprise_factor: The finding that a fully dual-stream architecture with zero shared blocks outperforms all single-stream fusion variants (including widened shared backbones) is mildly counterintuitive, as one might expect parameter sharing to help. ## Problem

Pixel-space diffusion models lack explicit high-level semantic supervision, and while representation-alignment methods (e.g., REPA) and visual co-denoising have shown promise, existing co-denoising systems entangle architecture, guidance, loss, and calibration choices, making it unclear which ingredients are truly essential. This paper asks: what specific design decisions make visual co-denoising effective for representation alignment in pixel-space generation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260288/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the model jointly denoises a noised 256×256 RGB image and noised patch-level DINOv2 semantic features (extracted by a frozen encoder) under a shared flow-matching timestep, conditioned on a class label. At inference time, only the class label and random noise are required—the DINOv2 encoder is not needed—producing a 256×256 RGB image via iterative denoising with classifier-free guidance.

## Method

V-Co builds a unified pixel-space testbed on JiT and systematically ablates four design axes. Architecturally, it adopts a fully dual-stream Transformer where pixel and semantic streams maintain separate normalization, MLPs, and Q/K/V projections while interacting through joint self-attention, preserving feature-specific computation. For classifier-free guidance, it defines the unconditional branch via structural semantic-to-pixel attention masking (blocking semantic→pixel information flow) combined with joint dropout of class labels and semantic features during training. For auxiliary supervision, it proposes a perceptual-drifting hybrid loss that combines instance-level DINOv2 perceptual alignment (pulling generated features toward paired ground-truth features) with distribution-level drifting repulsion (pushing away from same-class generated neighbors), gated by a similarity-based mechanism. Finally, it calibrates cross-stream denoising difficulty via RMS-based feature rescaling, shown to be SNR-equivalent to shifting the semantic-stream noise schedule.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, V-Co-B/16 (260M params, 600 epochs) achieves FID 2.35, matching the larger JiT-L/16 (459M params, FID 2.36). V-Co-L/16 (918M, 500 epochs) and V-Co-H/16 (1.9B, 300 epochs) reach FID 1.72 and 1.71 respectively, outperforming JiT-G/16 (2B params, 600 epochs, FID 1.82) with substantially fewer training epochs. V-Co-H/16 also surpasses PixelGen-XL/16 (FID 1.83 at 160 epochs) and PixelFlow XL/4 (FID 1.98 at 320 epochs) while using fewer total training GFLOPs than JiT-G (218 vs. 384).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-Co-B/16 (260M params) matches JiT-L/16 (459M params) at FID 2.35 vs. 2.36 after 600 epochs, demonstrating that co-denoising nearly doubles parameter efficiency over the plain JiT backbone. V-Co-L/16 (918M, 500 epochs) and V-Co-H/16 (1.9B, 300 epochs) both outperform JiT-G/16 (2B, 600 epochs) by roughly 0.1 FID (1.71–1.72 vs. 1.82), while requiring fewer total training GFLOPs (218 vs. 384 for V-Co-L). Against other pixel-space methods, V-Co-H/16 beats PixelGen-XL/16 (1.71 vs. 1.83) and PixelFlow XL/4 (1.71 vs. 1.98), and V-Co-L/16 beats PixelFlow (1.72 vs. 1.98). However, V-Co-H/16 trails DeCo-XL/16 (1.71 vs. 1.69) and ReDi at 800 epochs (1.71 vs. 1.61), and V-Co-B/16 at 200 epochs is slightly behind Latent Forcing (2.52 vs. 2.48). Overall, V-Co is competitive with or superior to all pixel-space diffusion baselines at comparable or smaller model sizes, though it does not yet match the best latent-space methods (e.g., RAE at FID 1.13) that rely on a separate autoencoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on JiT's pixel-space flow-matching formulation and extends it with a joint semantic-feature stream from a frozen DINOv2 encoder, positioning itself within the broader representation-alignment literature (REPA, iREPA, REPA-E) and the co-denoising/joint-generation line (Latent Forcing, ReDi). Unlike prior co-denoising systems that bundle architecture, guidance, loss, and calibration choices together, V-Co deliberately decouples these into a controlled ablation study on a unified JiT backbone, distilling which ingredients are actually essential. The perceptual-drifting hybrid loss repurposes the drifting objective from single-step generation (Deng et al.) into a multi-step diffusion setting by replacing its positive real-distribution term with a paired perceptual field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, controlled recipe for visual co-denoising, giving practitioners clear guidance on architecture (fully dual-stream), CFG design (structural semantic-to-pixel masking), auxiliary loss (perceptual-drifting hybrid), and feature calibration (RMS rescaling) rather than a monolithic end-to-end design. The efficiency story is notable: a 260M V-Co-B/16 matches a 459M JiT-L/16, and a 1.9B V-Co-H/16 beats a 2B JiT-G/16 with fewer epochs, suggesting co-denoising improves the quality-compute trade-off for pixel-space diffusion. Open questions include whether the recipe transfers to higher resolutions, text-to-image settings, or video, and whether the dual-stream architecture remains optimal at much larger scales where parameter sharing might become more efficient.
