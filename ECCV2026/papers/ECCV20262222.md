## Problem

Existing Referring Multi-Object Tracking (RMOT) methods rely solely on RGB observations, causing severe performance degradation in low-visibility conditions such as nighttime, smoke, and tunnels where target contours and appearance details are lost. No prior work or dataset addresses the fusion of language, RGB, and thermal modalities for language-guided multi-object tracking, leaving a gap for all-day robust referring tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262222/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language description (e.g., 'people walking near a crosswalk at night') together with a pixel-level aligned RGB–thermal image pair from a continuous video sequence, and produces structured text output containing bounding-box coordinates [x1,y1,x2,y2] for all referred targets within <answer> tags, preceded by a <think> reasoning block. The MLLM processes 10% temporally uniform-sampled frames during training, and the full pipeline assigns persistent identity IDs via Hungarian matching to form multi-frame tracking trajectories rather than per-frame detections.

## Method

RTrack builds on Qwen2.5-VL-3B as a perception backbone that jointly encodes language, RGB, and thermal inputs through visual encoding, text encoding, modality fusion, and cross-modal attention to localize referred targets. A Kalman-filter-based Trajectory Prediction Module provides motion priors, and an Identity Association Module uses IoU-cost Hungarian matching to maintain consistent target IDs across frames. For training, the authors apply Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) at the sequence level with LoRA fine-tuning, introducing a Clipped Advantage Scaling (CAS) strategy that caps the normalization factor to suppress gradient explosion when group rewards are concentrated. The reward function combines a Structured Output Reward (format compliance plus sine-window length regularization) with a Comprehensive Detection Reward that balances recall (Output Encouragement Reward rewarding matched ground-truth count) and precision (Precision Detection Reward penalizing excessive predictions via an N_det^γ denominator), guiding the model toward both complete and spatially accurate multi-target detection.

## Results

On the RefRT test set, RTrack achieves a HOTA of 15.53, DetA of 12.39, DetRe of 20.15, and DetPr of 22.78 under the L-RGB-T modality, outperforming all 11 baselines across these four metrics. Compared to the strongest L-RGB baseline (CRTracker, HOTA 9.30), RTrack gains +6.23 HOTA points; compared to the best RGBT tracker baseline (PFTrack+iKUN, HOTA 8.55), it gains +6.98 HOTA points. The framework also improves over the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (HOTA 4.98) by +10.55 HOTA points after RL fine-tuning, and over its own no-CoT variant by +5.65 HOTA points, confirming the value of both multimodal input and structured reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTrack decisively outperforms all 11 baselines on HOTA, DetA, DetRe, and DetPr. Against the strongest L-RGB end-to-end RMOT methods, it beats CRTracker (HOTA 9.30) by 6.23 points and TransRMOT (8.69) by 6.84 points, showing that RGB-only language-guided tracking is fundamentally limited in low-visibility conditions. Among L-RGB-T baselines, RTrack surpasses the best RGBT tracker pipeline PFTrack+iKUN (HOTA 8.55) by 6.98 points and the general MLLM Qwen2.5-VL-3B (4.98) by 10.55 points, demonstrating that simply coupling an existing RGBT tracker with a language module (iKUN) is insufficient for the RT-RMOT task. Notably, RTrack also leads on AssRe (22.02 vs. 49.15 for PFTrack+iKUN in AssA but 22.02 in AssRe), though PFTrack+iKUN retains an advantage on AssA (45.92 vs. 20.79) and AssPr (73.96 vs. 81.99), suggesting room for improvement in long-term identity consistency. The ablation further confirms that removing the CAS strategy drops HOTA from 15.53 to 5.27, and removing either reward component costs 3–4 HOTA points, validating each design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Referring Multi-Object Tracking (RMOT), where prior work (TransRMOT, TempRMOT, CRTrack, iKUN, CPAny) is confined to RGB-only inputs; (2) RGB-Thermal (RGBT) tracking, where methods like DeformCAT, PFTrack, and MCTrack handle modality fusion but lack language-guided target specification; and (3) MLLM-based perception, where models like Qwen2.5-VL provide strong cross-modal alignment but have not been adapted to temporally consistent tracking. RTrack departs from prior RMOT by replacing task-specific Transformer detectors with an MLLM backbone and from prior RGBT trackers by introducing language as a third modality, while its GSPO-based RL fine-tuning with CAS and structured rewards represents a novel training strategy not previously applied to tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RefRT dataset is the first benchmark for language-guided RGBT multi-object tracking, filling a clear gap for all-day (nighttime, smoke, low-visibility) tracking research. The MLLM-as-perception-backbone paradigm, combined with RL fine-tuning, suggests a path toward more flexible, instruction-driven tracking systems that could generalize to new object categories without retraining. However, the absolute HOTA scores remain low (~15.5), the dataset is small (72 scenes, 388 descriptions), and evaluation is limited to a single dataset, leaving open questions about scalability, cross-dataset generalization, and whether the RL training strategy transfers to other MLLM perception tasks.
