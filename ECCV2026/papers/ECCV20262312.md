## Problem

CLIP's Vision Transformer backbone scales quadratically with image resolution, is susceptible to spurious correlations under distribution shift, and is limited to a fixed 77-token text context. While Mamba-based state-space models offer linear complexity and spatial inductive biases that benefit vision tasks, their integration into contrastive vision-language pre-training remained unexplored, with no fully SSM-based dual-encoder CLIP model existing prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262312/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CLIMP takes RGB images at arbitrary resolutions (trained at 224×224, evaluated up to 896×896 without positional encoding interpolation or fine-tuning) and text prompts of arbitrary length (no 77-token cap, with dense captions averaging 134–142 tokens). It produces 768-dimensional shared embedding vectors for both modalities, from which image-text similarity scores, zero-shot class labels, and retrieval rankings are derived. The text representation is extracted via last-token pooling from the autoregressive Mamba encoder, which naturally aggregates full-context information at the final position.

## Method

CLIMP replaces both CLIP encoders with state-space architectures: VMamba (with its SS2D cross-scan mechanism that unfolds patches along four traversal paths for global receptive fields) serves as the vision encoder, while pretrained Mamba-1 (1.4B) or Mamba-2 (1.3B) LLMs serve as the text encoder. The model is trained with standard contrastive learning on CC12M (10 epochs, batch 2048, AdamW with cosine schedule, peak LR 5×10⁻⁵), producing sub-quadratic O(L) complexity in both modalities. The key architectural insight is that VMamba's scanning-based spatial inductive bias provides native variable-resolution support and reduces reliance on spurious correlations, while the matched SSM architectures in both towers learn more compatible cross-modal representations than mixed Transformer-SSM pairings. This yields tighter embedding alignment, lower text hubness, and superior OOD robustness—surpassing CLIP-ViT-B/16 trained on a 167× larger dataset on ImageNet-O.

## Results

On CLIP-Benchmark (31 datasets), CLIMP with Mamba-1 achieves the best zero-shot classification Acc@1 of 29.6% (+2.3% over the best transformer baseline RoPE-ViT at 27.3%) and the best retrieval scores of 65.5% IR@5 and 77.0% TR@5. On out-of-distribution robustness, CLIMP-Mamba-1 leads with 35.2% average top-1 accuracy across five ImageNet variants, with a striking +9.7% gain on ImageNet-O (49.8% for Mamba-2) that surpasses CLIP-ViT-B/16 trained on LAION-2B—a dataset 167× larger. At 896×896 resolution, CLIMP achieves up to 6.6% higher retrieval accuracy while using 5× less memory and 1.8× fewer FLOPs than ViT-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIMP outperforms all four transformer baselines (FlexViT, NaFlex, ViT, RoPE-ViT) on retrieval: Mamba-1 leads with 65.5% IR@5 and 77.0% TR@5, beating the best transformer (RoPE-ViT) by +2.1% and +4.1% respectively. On OOD robustness, CLIMP achieves the top two average scores (35.2%/34.8% vs. 33.2% for RoPE-ViT), with the largest gap on ImageNet-O where both CLIMP variants exceed the best transformer by 8–10%. Against MetaCLIP-ViT, which benefits from a vision encoder pretrained on 400× more data, CLIMP still achieves higher classification accuracy (29.6% vs. 25.7%) while matching retrieval, suggesting architectural inductive bias outweighs data scale. RoPE-ViT retains a slight edge on ImageNet-R and ImageNet-A (strong visual-style shifts), consistent with complementary failure modes between SSMs and transformers. On dense captioning retrieval (DOCCI, avg. 142 tokens), CLIMP-Mamba-1 achieves the best image retrieval (67.1% R@5, +11.8% over NaFlex), though NaFlex leads on text retrieval, likely due to its sequence-packing design.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLIMP extends the CLIP contrastive pretraining framework by replacing both the Vision Transformer and the text encoder with state-space models, specifically pairing VMamba's SS2D cross-scan vision encoder with Mamba-1 or Mamba-2 language models. This departs from prior hybrid efforts like CLIP-Mamba, which combined Mamba and Transformer modules within individual towers, by constructing the first fully SSM-based dual-encoder architecture. The spatial inductive bias inherited from VMamba's multi-directional scanning is the key architectural departure from ViT's pairwise self-attention, and the paper positions this as the source of improved robustness and sample efficiency rather than a mere efficiency trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLIMP establishes that SSMs are a viable architectural alternative to Transformers for vision-language pretraining, with practical advantages in memory (5× less), compute (1.8× fewer FLOPs), native variable-resolution inference, and long-text handling that directly address known CLIP limitations. The finding that architectural inductive bias can outweigh a 167× data scale advantage on OOD robustness challenges the prevailing assumption that more data always dominates. Open questions include whether these advantages persist at much larger model and dataset scales (the paper's largest model is only 87M parameters trained on 12M pairs), whether the Mamba-1-over-Mamba-2 preference generalizes, and how the approach extends to generative vision-language tasks.
