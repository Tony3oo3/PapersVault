## Problem

Multimodal LLMs ground whole objects well from free-form queries but systematically fail on part-level queries, returning the entire parent object instead of the named sub-region. The paper traces this to a missing object-part hierarchy: current grounding pipelines process every query through the same single-step localization with no mechanism to exploit the natural containment relationship between an object and its parts, and vision-language pretraining is dominated by object-level descriptions with part annotations being comparatively rare.

## Input / Output

The system receives an RGB image and a free-form natural language query that may name either a whole object (e.g., 'cat') or a semantic part (e.g., 'cat's tail'). The MLLM emits a structured, multi-stage output with tagged fields (reasoning trace, object/part decision, parent-object boxes, initial part boxes and points, critique, refined answer), from which bounding boxes and representative points are extracted and passed to a frozen SAM2-Large decoder to produce the final binary segmentation mask. The structured format is essential: it exposes intermediate reasoning states that enable fine-grained, verifiable reward signals during RL training.

## Method

OP-HRG is a structured prompting paradigm that enforces coarse-to-fine hierarchical localization: the model first classifies the query as object or part, then (for parts) localizes the parent object as an anchor, generates an initial part localization constrained to lie within the object box, and finally performs a self-reflective critique that either retains or refines the prediction. An active visual perception extension pauses generation after the initial answer, crops the predicted regions, re-encodes them with the vision encoder, and injects the crops as fresh visual tokens so the critique is grounded in actual image content rather than just coordinates. Training uses GRPO with a composite part-aware reward decomposed into base rewards (format, target decision, IoU, L1, point accuracy, compactness, non-repetition), hierarchical grounding rewards (object hint IoU, part-in-object containment with anti-triviality checks), and reflective refinement rewards (improvement over initial answer and an external SAM3 baseline, adjustment consistency). The 4B Qwen3-VL-Instruct backbone is trained from pretrained weights without a supervised cold-start, and the frozen SAM2 decoder isolates all gains to the MLLM's reasoning-guided localization.

## Results

On cross-dataset zero-shot part grounding, the 4B OP-HRG model achieves 38.59 gIoU on PascalPart and 56.87 gIoU on PartImageNet, surpassing the strongest baseline SAM3 (33.05 and 53.89) by +5.54 and +2.98 gIoU respectively. On the in-domain InstructPart test split, it reaches 75.56 gIoU, outperforming SAM3 (71.06) and the 7B VisionReasoner (59.38) by +4.50 and +16.18 gIoU. On object grounding (Pascal-Obj), the method attains 87.50 gIoU, the best among all baselines. On RefCOCO referring grounding, the model shows a modest trade-off (86.5 avg Acc@0.5 vs. 89.7 for the untrained base), while on ReasonSeg reasoning segmentation it leads all baselines at 69.6 gIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method wins on all four part/object gIoU benchmarks in Table 1, beating the strongest text-promptable segmentation baseline SAM3 by +4.50 to +5.54 gIoU on parts and +0.82 on objects. Against the closest decoupled MLLM-to-SAM competitor VisionReasoner-7B, the 4B OP-HRG model gains +16.18, +11.15, and +11.28 gIoU on InstructPart, PascalPart, and PartImageNet parts respectively, despite using a smaller backbone. Token-based grounding MLLMs (LISA, Sa2VA, PixelLM, UniPixel) all trail by 7–16 gIoU on parts, confirming that special-token segmentation does not capture part-level structure. On RefCOCO referring grounding (Table 4), the trained model drops from 89.7 to 86.5 average Acc@0.5 relative to its untrained Qwen3-VL-Instruct-4B base, a modest ~3-point trade-off that the authors attribute to part-centric reward emphasis. On ReasonSeg reasoning segmentation (Table 5), the 4B model leads all baselines at 69.6 gIoU, surpassing the 7B VisionReasoner (63.6) and GenSeg-R1-4B (68.4), indicating that hierarchical part reasoning transfers to broader reasoning-driven segmentation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled MLLM-to-SAM + GRPO paradigm established by VisionReasoner and Seg-Zero, which pair a reasoning LLM with a frozen mask decoder and optimize via GRPO. The key departure is the introduction of OP-HRG, a structured coarse-to-fine prompting paradigm that explicitly models the object-part hierarchy (localize parent object first, then part within it, then self-reflect), combined with a part-aware GRPO reward framework providing stage-wise verifiable signals for parent-object accuracy, part containment, critique consistency, and answer improvement. Prior RL-based grounding methods optimize a single localization reward uniformly across all queries; this work argues that the missing object-part hierarchy is the root cause of MLLMs' part-grounding weakness and that structured prompting plus targeted rewards can activate dormant hierarchical reasoning capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 4B model with structured reasoning and part-aware rewards outperforms 7B grounding LLMs and SAM3 on cross-dataset zero-shot part grounding suggests that targeted reward design and prompting structure can substitute for model scale in fine-grained spatial tasks. The finding that the self-reflective step acts primarily as a train-time regularizer (nearly all benefit retained when the reflective prompt is removed at inference) is a useful insight for practitioners: the value is in shaping the policy during RL, not in the inference-time critique loop itself. Open questions include whether the part containment reward generalizes to multi-instance scenes, whether the reflective mechanism can remain active as base localization quality improves, and whether the approach extends to 3D or video part grounding.
