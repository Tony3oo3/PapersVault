## Problem

Textual image classification is challenged by the wide diversity of document layouts, yet curriculum learning has rarely been applied to this domain. Existing curriculum methods rely on model-dependent difficulty signals (loss, gradients, predictions) that must be recomputed for each backbone architecture, and fixed scheduling rules ignore the learner's real-time state. This paper asks whether a text-independent difficulty measurer combined with an RL-based scheduler can improve both accuracy and data efficiency without modifying the base classifier.

## Input / Output

The method takes grayscale document images resized to 224×224 pixels and produces one of 16 document type class labels. Difficulty scores are pre-computed once per sample using hand-crafted features and a frozen ResNet18 forward pass, then reused across all backbone architectures (CNN, DiT, UDOP, LayoutLMv3) without recomputation. The RL agent's action is a continuous scalar in [-0.01, 0.01] that adjusts a difficulty threshold, which in turn determines which subset of training samples is selected for the next training episode.

## Method

TiCRL comprises two decoupled components: a text-independent difficulty measurer and a DDPG-based training scheduler. The measurer combines five visual-textual features (Canny edge count, HOG gradient magnitude, GLCM contrast, GLCM homogeneity, and cosine similarity to a class-prototype ResNet50 embedding) with a one-time cross-entropy loss from a frozen ResNet18, aggregated via percentile-rank averaging into a single [0,1] score per sample. The scheduler is a DDPG agent whose 8-dimensional state encodes the current threshold, its change, episode index, training/validation losses and accuracies, and a clipping flag; it outputs a 1D threshold adjustment that controls which fraction of the difficulty-sorted training set is exposed to the classifier. Training proceeds in two stages: an agent-training phase over multiple cycles (classifier reinitialized each cycle) where the agent learns from relative loss-improvement rewards, followed by a curriculum-application phase where the frozen agent guides a fresh classifier without further policy updates. This design makes the learned curriculum policy architecture-agnostic and transferable to unseen datasets without retraining.

## Results

On RVL-CDIP with a lightweight CNN (1.35M params) trained from scratch, TiCRL achieves 83.67% accuracy using only 81.92% of training data, outperforming the no-curriculum CNN baseline (80.43% on 100% data) and all curriculum baselines by 6.7–13.8 percentage points. Applied to the pretrained DiT transformer, TiCRL reaches 93.02% accuracy using just 57.66% of training data, surpassing DiT (91.07%), UDOP (74.00%), and LayoutLMv3 (91.53%) all trained on 80% of data. Cross-dataset transfer to Tobacco-3482 (10 classes, 3,482 images) yields 86.88% accuracy without retraining, versus 85.30% for the standard baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TiCRL decisively outperforms both automated curriculum baselines: Self-Paced Learning (SPL, Kumar et al. 2010) achieves only 69.89% accuracy, a 13.78-point gap, while Adaptive Curriculum Learning (ACL, Kong et al. 2021) reaches 75.84%, still 7.83 points below TiCRL. Against pre-defined curriculum schedules (linear, step, root), TiCRL leads by 6.7–9.5 points, confirming that a fixed schedule cannot match the adaptive threshold control of the DDPG agent. On the transformer side, DiT+TiCRL (93.02% on 57.66% data) surpasses the DiT baseline (91.07% on 80% data) by 1.95 points while using 22% less training data, and also beats LayoutLMv3 (91.53% on 80% data) by 1.49 points. UDOP (74.00%) is the weakest transformer baseline and is not directly paired with TiCRL in the reported results. All TiCRL improvements over baselines are statistically significant (paired t-test, p < 0.01, Cohen's d: 2.17–7.62) across 5 independent runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

TiCRL sits at the intersection of curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009) and RL-based scheduling (El-Bouri et al., 2020; Fan et al., 2018), but departs from both in a key way: its difficulty measurer relies on text-independent visual-textual features (Canny, HOG, GLCM, ResNet50 cosine similarity) rather than model-dependent signals like loss or gradients, making the curriculum architecture-agnostic. Unlike static coreset selection (CRAIG, EL2N) or fixed-schedule CL (linear, step, root), the DDPG scheduler adapts the difficulty threshold in real time based on the learner's state, and unlike prior RL-CL work that recomputes difficulty per backbone, TiCRL's scores are computed once and reused across CNNs and transformers. The paper explicitly positions itself as filling a gap: textual image classification had rarely been addressed with curriculum learning, with prior efforts focused on model architecture or multi-modal fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a lightweight RL scheduler plus a hand-crafted difficulty score can simultaneously improve accuracy and reduce data consumption for document image classification, with the learned policy transferring to unseen datasets (Tobacco-3482) and architectures (DiT, UDOP, LayoutLMv3) without retraining. Limitations include the narrow task scope (document images only), the difficulty measurer's dependence on document-specific features that may not generalize to natural images, and the modest transfer margin (1.58%p on Tobacco-3482). Open questions raised by the authors include extending TiCRL to other vision tasks, exploring broader domain transfer, and developing confidence-based agent selection using policy-level diagnostics such as actor/critic stability and Q-value variance.
