## Problem

Existing long-context egocentric video understanding benchmarks rely on template-based, generic questions that ignore user attention, use unnatural phrasing, and restrict questioning to before or after a clip ends. This creates a mismatch with real AR-assistant usage, where users ask context-specific, attention-driven questions during ongoing interactions. EgoEverything addresses this by grounding question generation in measured gaze traces and natural, human-like inquiry patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261387/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides egocentric video clips at 1408×1408 (AEA) or 2016×2208 (Nymeria) resolution with synchronized per-frame gaze coordinates, paired with a 5-option multiple-choice question whose target object was sampled via a gaze-centered 2D Gaussian prior (θ=400 px). The questioning timestamp is randomized to at least 3 minutes after the target frame, simulating mid-interaction queries over partially observed streams. The model outputs a single selected answer option; evaluation also includes ablations with gaze-cropped, gaze-masked, downsampled, and full-resolution frame inputs.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) Video Stream Summary and Clustering extracts ResNet-50 frame features, applies k-means (K=12) with ±5-frame temporal smoothing to segment video into action summaries; (2) Gaze-Oriented Target Sampling detects objects per frame via a VLM, deduplicates them using CLIP-embedding ReID, then selects a target object from a randomly chosen frame using a 2D Gaussian perception sampler S_θ(o) ∝ exp(−‖o−f‖²/2θ²) centered on the measured gaze position f; (3) Question Generation and Manual Curation deploys a Synthesizer Agent (a fine-tuned VLM with GetFrame/GetSegment tool access) to draft natural-language MCQs about the target object, iteratively validated by a separate Validator Agent that does not see the target. Generated questions then pass through rule-based filtering (removing walls, body parts, timestamp references), 400+ hours of human review by 12 annotators, and LLM-based blind filtering that discards any MCQ solvable from text alone.

## Results

On EgoEverything, Gemini 1.5 Pro achieves the highest VLM accuracy of 63.1% under full-resolution (FR) input, while VideoLLaMA3-7B reaches 49.1%, LLaVA-Video 42.6%, and LongVA 34.6%. Human annotators (12 participants) achieve 83.5% average accuracy, revealing a substantial 20+ point gap over the best VLM. Structured-text LEU methods (VMP and AMEGO) perform worst, dropping to 19.5–33.2%, and a text-only (no-video) evaluation further degrades Gemini to 35.9% and VideoLLaMA3-7B to 22.9%, confirming that questions cannot be solved from linguistic cues alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four VLMs evaluated, Gemini 1.5 Pro consistently outperforms the others across all preprocessing settings (e.g., 63.1% vs. 49.1% for VideoLLaMA3-7B under FR), but all models remain 20+ points below the 83.5% human baseline. The two structured-text LEU methods, AMEGO and VideoMindPalace (VMP), perform worst (19.5–33.2%), because they convert video into object-detection text and discard activity-irrelevant objects that EgoEverything's gaze-aware sampling deliberately includes. Among input-preprocessing baselines, Gaze Crop (GC) beats Gaze Mask (GM) by 7–15 points, confirming that gaze-centered content is most relevant, while Average Downsampling (AD) slightly outperforms GC by retaining useful peripheral information. A saliency-map gaze-prompting baseline lifts VideoLLaMA3-7B from 49.1% to 51.19%, a modest gain that underscores the benchmark's difficulty. Ablation analyses further show accuracy drops from 54.8% to 31.4% as object-gaze distance increases (100–200 px to 600–700 px) and from 49.0% to 33.3% as recall interval grows, validating the benchmark's attention-aware and long-recall design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-context egocentric video understanding (LEU) benchmarking line (EgoSchema, NExT-QA, HD-EPIC, HourVideo, X-LeBench) by introducing three dimensions absent from prior work: gaze-grounded target sampling via a 2D Gaussian prior, multi-agent natural-language question synthesis, and mid-interaction questioning times. It draws on cognitive-science models of spatial attention (spotlight/zoom-lens literature) to justify the Gaussian sampling, and on concurrent gaze-aware benchmarks (EgoGazeVQA, GazeVQA, StreamGaze) while deliberately using gaze only offline during target selection rather than as an explicit query input. The data pipeline repurposes existing egocentric datasets (Aria Everyday Activities, Nymeria) and standard tools (ResNet-50 clustering, CLIP-based ReID) within a novel multi-agent VQA generation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20-point gap between the best VLM (Gemini 1.5 Pro at 63.1%) and human annotators (83.5%) reveals that current models remain far from reliable AR-assistant performance under attention-aware, long-recall conditions. The finding that structured scene-graph methods (AMEGO, VMP) perform worst (18–33%)—worse than raw full-resolution video—challenges the assumption that intermediate structured representations improve long-context egocentric reasoning, at least when they discard activity-irrelevant objects. Open questions include how to make VLMs genuinely attention-aware without explicit gaze input, how to close the peripheral-attention and long-recall-interval gaps, and whether the benchmark's gaze-sampling prior (θ=400 px) is the right granularity for diverse AR use cases. On-device deployment constraints (shared RAM, thermal budgets) further limit which models can be evaluated in realistic AR settings.
