## Problem

Standard federated VLN (e.g., FedAvg) produces a compromised global policy that fits no individual client well under the severe non-IID heterogeneity of distinct indoor environments. Existing personalized FL methods apply static, per-client personalization that cannot adapt to the time-varying decision difficulty within a single navigation episode, where some steps require strong local grounding while others benefit from shared knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260332/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each navigation step, the agent consumes a natural-language instruction (single or multi-turn dialog), a panoramic visual observation of the current viewpoint, and the accumulated decoder hidden state encoding trajectory history. The SPM fuses the history state and current observation into a compact step vector to predict per-block scaling factors in (0,1); the adapters then produce additive ΔQKV projection deltas for selected attention layers. The final output is a per-step navigation action (view selection, movement, or stop), and over a full episode this yields a complete action sequence terminating at a goal viewpoint.

## Method

FedNASP introduces two coupled mechanisms for step-wise personalized federated VLN. First, a lightweight Step-wise Personalization Modulator (SPM) — a two-branch MLP over history and observation features — predicts block-wise scaling factors α ∈ (0,1) at every navigation step, dynamically controlling how much client-specific adaptation to apply. Second, Structure-aware Personalized Prefix Injection (SPI) uses per-client adapters to generate additive ΔQKV terms injected into selected attention modules (cross-modal fusion, visual encoder, critic), with injection strength modulated by the SPM's α. A module-sensitivity study guides which components to personalize: cross-modal fusion is the primary target, the visual encoder is jointly personalized, and the language encoder remains globally shared. During federated training, only backbone updates are uploaded for FedAvg aggregation while SPM and adapter parameters stay local, adding ~9.2% client-side storage with negligible communication overhead.

## Results

On REVERIE, FedNASP achieves 43.23% SR, 36.62% SPL, and 35.22% RGS, surpassing the centralized baseline (41.78% SR, 32.87% RGS) while operating in a fully federated setting. On CVDN, FedNASP reaches 39.02% SR and 23.14% SPL, improving over the federated baseline FedVLN by +84% in SR and +180% in SPL. The paper reports a 13.0% absolute gain in Remote Grounding Success on REVERIE and a 22.6% absolute gain in Success Rate on CVDN over the strongest non-centralized baselines. Client-side storage increases only ~9.2% over FedVLN (256.86M vs. 235.18M params) with negligible per-round runtime overhead (+1.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedNASP outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both REVERIE and CVDN. Against FedVLN (the first federated VLN method), FedNASP improves SR by +45% on REVERIE (43.23 vs. 29.83) and by +84% on CVDN (39.02 vs. 21.19), with even larger relative gains in SPL and RGS. Against the closest pFL competitor FedPerfix on REVERIE, FedNASP leads by 6.01 points in SR (43.23 vs. 37.22) and 4.04 points in RGS (35.22 vs. 31.18), while using comparable client storage. On CVDN, FedNASP beats FedCP (the strongest pFL baseline there) by 7.20 points in SR (39.02 vs. 31.82) and 8.65 points in SPL (23.14 vs. 14.49). Per-FedAvg, a classic personalization-layers method, performs poorly in both settings (12.51% SR on REVERIE, 22.16% on CVDN), confirming that naive per-client fine-tuning is insufficient under VLN's coupled heterogeneity. pFedNavi reports no REVERIE numbers but trails FedNASP substantially on CVDN (19.61% SR vs. 39.02%), and its 199.9% client storage overhead highlights the cost of parameter-wise personalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedNASP builds directly on FedVLN (Zhou & Wang, ECCV 2022), which established the federated VLN training paradigm with FedAvg aggregation of backbone updates, and on pFedNavi, the first personalized FL method for VLN that used layer selection and parameter-wise personalization. The key conceptual departure is from static, episode-level personalization (as in pFedNavi and most prior pFL methods like Per-FedAvg, FedPerfix, FedCP) to dynamic step-wise modulation of personalization strength, motivated by the non-uniform uncertainty along long-horizon navigation trajectories. The structure-aware adapter-based prefix injection draws on the adapter/prefix personalization lineage (FedPerfix) but is specifically designed around VLN's multimodal architecture, with a module sensitivity study guiding which functional components (cross-modal fusion, visual encoder, critic) should be personalized versus kept globally shared.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Privacy-preserving VLN is a practical deployment necessity since real navigation data (house layouts, trajectories, user instructions) are inherently sensitive, and this paper provides a concrete, well-ablated framework that substantially narrows the gap between federated and centralized performance (e.g., 43.23 vs. 41.78 SR on REVERIE). The step-wise personalization insight—that a single fixed personalization level is suboptimal for sequential decision-making with time-varying ambiguity—could generalize to other long-horizon embodied tasks beyond VLN. Open questions include whether the approach scales to continuous-environment VLN (VLN-CE, Robo-VLN), whether the SPM's two-branch MLP design is optimal versus more expressive modulators, and how performance holds under even more extreme client heterogeneity or larger client pools. The evaluation is limited to three datasets and two backbone architectures (ViLBERT-style and seq2seq-LSTM), leaving generalizability to LLM/VLM-based VLN agents untested.
