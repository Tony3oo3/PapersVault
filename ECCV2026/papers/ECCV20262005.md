## Problem

Cycle Self-Training (CST) effectively breaks the shared-classifier assumption in self-training for domain adaptation, but extending it to object detection faces three unresolved challenges: pseudo-label unreliability manifests in three distinct forms (classification error, localization error, and their combination), direct application to dense detectors causes training instability, and the wide numerical range of bounding-box regression coordinates leads to exploding losses and model collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262005/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain RGB images with per-object class labels and bounding-box coordinates alongside unlabeled target-domain RGB images. The frozen Teacher receives only weakly augmented target images (resizing, horizontal flip) to generate pseudo-labels, while the trainable Student processes strongly augmented images (color jittering, Gaussian blur, random erasing) from both domains. Output is pixel-level classification scores and four-value bounding-box offsets per spatial location, produced by an anchor-free FCOS detector with a VGG-16 backbone.

## Method

DSBCO constructs CST upon the Mean Teacher paradigm, where the Teacher is updated via exponential moving average of Student parameters to stabilize dense-prediction training. On top of this, a Feature Bilevel-Cycle Optimization module operates in feature space with two parallel streams: a Classification Cycle Stream that solves a ridge-regression problem to find a linear projection from target features to pseudo-labels (inner loop) and then enforces that the same projection applied to source features recovers source ground-truth labels (outer loop), and a Regression Cycle Stream that first normalizes target regression offsets into a standard distribution before solving an analogous ridge-regression projection, preventing gradient divergence from large coordinate values. An Instance Consistency Distillation loss with dual-threshold masking (classification probability and centerness) provides the baseline self-training signal, and the total objective combines supervised source loss, distillation loss, and both cycle losses with tunable weighting.

## Results

On Adverse Weather Adaptation (Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes), DSBCO achieves 64.7% mAP, surpassing the best FCOS-based method HT (50.4%) by 14.3% and the overall best non-FCOS method MGCAMT (55.9%) by 8.8%. On Diverse Context Adaptation (Cityscapes → BDD100K), DSBCO reaches 41.9% mAP, the best among FCOS-based methods, improving ~2% over HT (40.2%). On Distinct Camera Adaptation (KITTI → Cityscapes), DSBCO attains 62.2% AP (Car), matching MGCAMT and exceeding HT (60.3%) by ~2%. On Synthetic Source Adaptation (Sim10K → Cityscapes), DSBCO achieves 68.6% AP (Car), outperforming MGCAMT (67.5%) and HT (65.5%) by 3.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSBCO outperforms all FCOS-based baselines across all four scenarios, with the largest margin over HT (CVPR'23): +14.3% mAP on Adverse Weather, +1.7% on Diverse Context, +1.9% AP on Distinct Camera, and +3.1% AP on Synthetic Source. Against the strongest non-FCOS competitor MGCAMT (TIP'25, RetinaNet), DSBCO wins by 8.8% on Adverse Weather and 1.1% on Synthetic Source, ties at 62.2% on Distinct Camera, but trails by 2.9% on Diverse Context (41.9% vs. 44.8%), where MGCAMT's multi-granularity confidence alignment provides an advantage. Among Faster R-CNN-based methods, DSBCO exceeds the best (AT+REACT, 53.1% mAP) by 11.6% on Adverse Weather. Among Def DETR-based methods, DSBCO surpasses the best (MRT, 51.2% mAP) by 13.5% on Adverse Weather. The ablation confirms that the dual-stream cycle optimization (classification + regression) contributes the largest single gain (+13.8% over instance consistency alone), while regression normalization is critical for preventing the loss explosion that causes model collapse in naive bilevel approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Cycle Self-Training (CST), originally formulated for image classification domain adaptation (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2021), to the object detection setting by decomposing the bilevel optimization into separate classification and regression streams. It builds on the Mean Teacher paradigm (Tarvainen & Valpola, NeurIPS 2017) to stabilize the dense-prediction training that CST alone cannot handle in anchor-free detectors like FCOS. Within the broader DAOD landscape spanning adversarial alignment, graph matching, and transformer-based methods, DSBCO positions itself firmly in the self-training/teacher-student sub-line, departing from prior work by introducing regression normalization to prevent the exploding losses that plague naive cycle-consistency on unbounded bounding-box coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent improvements across four distinct domain-shift scenarios (weather, context, camera, synthetic-to-real) demonstrate that the dual-stream bilevel formulation is not an artifact of a single benchmark, and the 14.3% mAP gain over the strongest FCOS-based baseline (HT) on Adverse Weather is a meaningful margin. However, the reliance on a VGG-16 backbone and the fixed thresholding mechanism limit immediate practical adoption, and the authors themselves note that extending the framework to Transformer-based detectors (DETR variants) remains open. The theoretical regression bound, while useful for grounding the method, is stated without full proof in the main text, leaving its tightness and practical relevance somewhat unverified. A key open question is whether the regression normalization trick generalizes to other regression-heavy tasks beyond bounding-box prediction.
