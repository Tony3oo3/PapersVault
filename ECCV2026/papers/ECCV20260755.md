## Problem

Novel view synthesis from only two input images under large viewpoint changes remains difficult: regression-based feedforward methods lack the generative capacity to reconstruct unseen regions, while camera-guided video diffusion models frequently deviate from prescribed camera trajectories due to noisy projected point clouds and insufficient pose conditioning. The paper tackles the specific challenge of making diffusion-based video generation both geometrically faithful to target camera poses and capable of hallucinating occluded content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260755/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images (first and last frames) at 256×256 resolution, a sequence of target camera poses, and a projected point cloud with per-point confidence weights estimated by a 3D foundation model (VGGT by default). It outputs a 33-frame video of intermediate novel views generated via 50-step rectified flow denoising (~10 s per scene on a single GPU). The confidence map is projected into each target camera space to produce frame-wise reliability weights that modulate how much the model trusts the geometric prior at each pixel.

## Method

ConfCtrl builds on the pretrained Wan2.1-Interpolation model and introduces two mechanisms. First, it replaces pure Gaussian noise initialization with a confidence-weighted projected point cloud latent (z₀ = w ⊙ z_pc + ε), giving the rectified flow a geometry-informed starting distribution that bridges interpolation priors toward novel view synthesis. Second, it inserts 10 Kalman DiT blocks, each containing a prediction submodule (cross-attention between camera Plücker embeddings and the point-cloud projection latent, added via a zero-initialized linear layer) and an update submodule (additional DiT blocks that compute a learned residual correction from the discrepancy between the predicted latent and the noisy point-cloud measurement, also through a zero-initialized linear layer). This predict-update architecture explicitly down-weights unreliable geometric observations while preserving pose-driven predictions. Training uses a standard rectified flow loss plus a latent gradient regularization term (λ_grad = 0.05) to enforce spatial consistency under rapid viewpoint changes, and the entire 1.3B-parameter model is fine-tuned for 20k steps at batch size 1 on a single GPU.

## Results

On CO3D-Hydrant, ConfCtrl achieves the best LPIPS (0.339), PSNR (15.54), SSIM (0.328), translation error (0.143 m), and rotation error (0.103 rad) among all 11 baselines. On CO3D-Teddybear it reaches PSNR 17.27 and LPIPS 0.354, and on DL3DV it attains PSNR 16.49, LPIPS 0.287, and the lowest camera errors (Et=0.195, Er=0.149). The closest diffusion-based competitor, Gen3R, trails by 0.002 LPIPS and 0.22 dB PSNR on Hydrant, while the best regression method AnySplat lags by 0.029 LPIPS and 0.18 dB PSNR on the same dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConfCtrl outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all three in-distribution datasets. Among regression-based methods, AnySplat [25] is the strongest (LPIPS 0.368, PSNR 15.36 on Hydrant) but still trails ConfCtrl by 0.029 LPIPS and 0.18 dB PSNR, while MvSplat [8] and NopoSplat [69] show substantially worse camera errors (Et > 0.2 m). Among diffusion-based methods, Gen3R [21] is the closest competitor (LPIPS 0.341, PSNR 15.32, Et 0.189 on Hydrant), yet ConfCtrl improves LPIPS by 0.002, PSNR by 0.22 dB, and reduces translation error by 0.046 m. Uni3C [4] and ViewCrafter [71] both use VGGT-projected point clouds as input but lack the confidence-aware initialization and predict-update mechanism, resulting in 0.011–0.074 higher LPIPS and 0.13–0.93 dB lower PSNR. CameraCtrl [18] and SeVA [78], which do not use explicit 3D point-cloud priors, show the largest camera-following errors (Et 0.26–0.30 m on Hydrant) compared to ConfCtrl's 0.143 m. In cross-dataset zero-shot evaluation (Table 3), ConfCtrl again leads all methods, with the gap over Uni3C [4] widening on DL3DV→GraspNet (LPIPS 0.229 vs. 0.238, Et 0.099 vs. 0.128), confirming that the confidence-aware design generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConfCtrl builds on the Wan2.1 video interpolation model as its generative backbone, departing from the more common single-image-to-video paradigm by exploiting the head-tail interpolation prior for stronger geometric consistency. It extends the camera-guided diffusion line (CameraCtrl, ViewCrafter, Uni3C) by replacing simple concatenation of Plücker embeddings and projected point clouds with a Kalman-inspired predict-update mechanism that explicitly models uncertainty in 3D geometric priors. The confidence-weighted noise initialization (Eq. 3) is a novel departure from standard rectified-flow training, which always starts from pure Gaussian noise, and the use of VGGT confidence maps as reliability weights for point cloud conditioning is a new application of uncertainty signals from 3D foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: enabling diffusion-based novel view synthesis to strictly follow prescribed camera trajectories from only two input images under large viewpoint changes, a setting where regression-based methods fail due to limited generative capacity. The Kalman-inspired predict-update conditioning offers a general architectural pattern for fusing noisy geometric observations with control signals in diffusion models, potentially transferable beyond camera control. However, the authors acknowledge a fundamental limitation: the VAE in current video diffusion models is designed for temporally smooth content and struggles with abrupt camera motion, capping the method's fidelity for large viewpoint jumps. Open questions include whether VAE-free or motion-aware latent spaces could unlock further gains, and whether the confidence-aware conditioning paradigm extends to other noisy conditioning signals (e.g., depth, segmentation).
