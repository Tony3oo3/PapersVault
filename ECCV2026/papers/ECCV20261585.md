## Problem

Region understanding (captioning) and referring localization (segmentation/grounding) in MLLMs are typically optimized as separate tasks, each requiring large-scale human-annotated text-region pairs. Existing RL approaches for captioning rely on external LLM judges vulnerable to reward hacking, while localization demands ground-truth masks, creating a data bottleneck that limits scalability. The paper asks whether the inherent duality between these two tasks can be exploited to form a self-evaluating loop that requires only region inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261585/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the captioning phase, the model receives an RGB image with a target region specified as a binary mask (or bounding box) and produces a free-form text description. For the localization phase, it receives the same image with a text query and autoregressively generates a segmentation mask as a sequence of discrete mask tokens (via SAMTok's tokenizer) or bounding box coordinates. Training data consists solely of (image, mask) pairs from DenseWorld (20k images) plus 1k no-target expressions from GRES, with no textual ground truths at any point.

## Method

CycleGRPO implements an 'Actor as Its Own Critic' paradigm where a single MLLM (SAMTok on Qwen3-VL-4B) alternates between two roles within one training step. In Phase 1, the model acts as Actor, sampling G candidate captions for a given region; in Phase 2, it transitions to Critic, performing K independent localization rollouts per caption to reconstruct the mask. The cycle-consistency reward is the IoU between the original mask and each reconstruction: the captioning reward is the mean IoU across K rollouts, and the localization reward is this mean multiplied by each individual rollout's IoU, amplifying gradients from high-quality captions. Both rewards are standardized within the group and fed into the GRPO objective with KL regularization. The key insight is that a genuinely discriminative caption must contain sufficient unique detail to guide the model back to the exact initial region, so spatial reconstruction accuracy serves as an intrinsic, physically grounded quality signal for text generation—eliminating the need for external judges, textual ground truths, or task-specific heads.

## Results

On GroundingSuite, CycleGRPO (4B) achieves 67.6% overall gIoU, surpassing the SAMTok baseline (57.5%) by 10.1 points and outperforming EVF-SAM (62.6%) and LISA (57.6%). On GRES, the method delivers a 7.0% average gIoU gain over SAMTok (78.2 vs. 71.2) and a dramatic 33.4-point jump in target-rejection accuracy (92.1% vs. 58.7%). On DLC-Bench for region captioning, CycleGRPO reaches 67.7 average score, matching DAM (67.3) which was trained on 1.5M in-domain pairs, while surpassing GPT-4o (61.5) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (55.8). On GAR-Bench-VQA, the model achieves 65.1% overall accuracy, exceeding Gemini-2.5-Pro (64.2%) and o3 (61.3%) without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CycleGRPO (4B) consistently outperforms its SAMTok base model across all five benchmarks, with the largest gains on GRES (+7.0 gIoU, +33.4 N-acc) and GroundingSuite (+10.1 overall gIoU). Against closed-source frontiers, it surpasses GPT-4o (61.5) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (55.8) on DLC-Bench, and exceeds Gemini-2.5-Pro (64.2%) and o3 (61.3%) on GAR-Bench-VQA, all without task-specific fine-tuning. On GroundingSuite, it beats larger dedicated segmentation models including LISA (7B, 57.6), GLaMM (7B, 57.2), and EVF-SAM (1B, 62.6) by 5.0–15.1 points in overall gIoU. On GRES, it outperforms SAM4MLLM (70.5 gIoU), MLLMSeg (73.9), and ARGenSeg (73.6) while also achieving far superior target-rejection accuracy (92.1% vs. 63.3–70.4%). Compared to standard GRPO (which uses the same IoU reward but without the cycle loop), CycleGRPO yields an additional +4.9 gIoU on GroundingSuite and +4.3 on DLC-Bench, confirming that the self-evaluating cycle structure provides a more effective training signal than one-directional RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAMTok's discrete mask tokenizer, which reframes segmentation as autoregressive token prediction and makes RL-based fine-tuning of spatial outputs feasible, and on GRPO's group-relative advantage formulation, which removes the need for a separate value network. It departs from prior RL-for-MLLMs work (e.g., CapRL, grounding RL methods) that treats region captioning and localization as isolated optimization problems requiring external reward models, text ground truths, or LLM-as-a-judge. The key conceptual departure is the 'Actor as Its Own Critic' cycle: the same model generates captions and then grounds them back into masks, using the reconstructive IoU as an intrinsic, self-evaluating reward that obviates both textual supervision and external judges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for human-annotated text-region pairs and external LLM judges, CycleGRPO addresses a major scalability bottleneck in fine-grained MLLM training, making it feasible to bootstrap pixel-level capabilities from region inputs alone. The framework's generalizability across grounding formats (mask tokens vs. bounding boxes) and model scales (3B–8B) suggests it could become a standard post-training recipe for region-aware MLLMs. Open questions include whether the cycle reward adequately captures spatial-relational understanding (evidenced by slight drops on Position/Relation splits in GAR-Bench-VQA) and whether the approach degrades gracefully when the base model's grounding capability is weak, since the critic's quality is bounded by the actor's own localization accuracy.
