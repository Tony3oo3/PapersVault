## Problem

Existing HDR novel view synthesis methods are optimization-based, requiring calibrated poses, dense SfM point clouds, and per-scene densification that takes ~25 minutes, while recent feed-forward 3D reconstruction models ignore HDR entirely by assuming exposure-invariant appearance. No prior work provides a fast, generalizable feed-forward pipeline that handles multi-exposure LDR inputs with large brightness variations and produces controllable-exposure HDR renders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260526/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V uncalibrated multi-exposure LDR images (448×448, gamma-encoded) with known exposure times Δt_v and produces, in a single forward pass, a set of HDR 3D Gaussians parameterized by log-radiance spherical-harmonic colors, per-view camera poses (9 parameters each), a mid-exposure anchor, and scene-specific tonemapper weights. Rendering at any target exposure ℓ is achieved by shifting log-irradiance before passing through the predicted two-layer MLP tonemapper, enabling controllable-exposure LDR output without re-optimization.

## Method

InstantHDR builds on AnySplat's frozen alternating-attention transformer geometry backbone and adds a trainable Geo-guided Appearance Modeling branch with three stages: (1) FiLM-based exposure normalization that coarsely aligns all views to a shared mid-exposure level, (2) cross-view irradiance fusion that reuses the Q/K attention maps from the 14th frozen geometry encoder layer to match patches across views under extreme exposure gaps, and (3) Difference-of-Gaussians guided upsampling that injects high-frequency texture from a shallow CNN into the patch-level fused features. A MetaNet then predicts scene-specific tonemapper parameters (a 2-layer MLP) from concatenated LDR features, exposure embeddings, and the predicted Gaussians, enabling generalizable HDR-to-LDR mapping without per-scene optimization. The entire system is trained end-to-end with photometric MSE and perceptual losses on LDR images only—no HDR ground truth is used—on the newly constructed HDR-Pretrain dataset of 168 Blender-rendered indoor scenes with diverse tone-mapping operators.

## Results

On the HDR-NeRF benchmark (18-view standard protocol, real scenes), InstantHDR_1K achieves 29.19 dB PSNR / 0.931 SSIM in ~39 s, matching GaussHDR (29.36 dB / 0.929 SSIM) while being ~50× faster (1891 s). In the challenging 4-view sparse setting on real scenes, InstantHDR_1K reaches 22.16 dB PSNR, surpassing GaussHDR by +2.90 dB and HDR-GS by +6.76 dB. Zero-shot (no post-optimization), InstantHDR already outperforms AnySplat by +5.65 dB PSNR on real 4-view scenes, demonstrating strong exposure-robust generalization. Overall, InstantHDR_1K is ~20× faster than HDR-GS and ~50× faster than GaussHDR while reaching comparable or superior quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnySplat (the feed-forward baseline without HDR handling), InstantHDR wins decisively in zero-shot mode: +5.65 dB PSNR on real 4-view and +8.07 dB on synthetic 8-view, confirming that naive multi-exposure fusion causes severe ghosting. Against GaussHDR (optimization-based, CVPR 2025), InstantHDR_1K wins on sparse real scenes (4-view: +2.90 dB; 8-view: +0.36 dB) but trails slightly on dense 18-view real (−0.17 dB) and synthetic scenes (−2.64 dB on 18-view), where GaussHDR's dedicated 3D+2D dual-branch tone mapper has an advantage. Against HDR-GS (NeurIPS 2024), InstantHDR_1K is consistently ahead on real scenes across all view counts (e.g., +6.76 dB at 4 views, +1.77 dB at 18 views) and also leads on synthetic 4-view (+3.37 dB), though it falls behind on synthetic 18-view (−2.64 dB). In terms of speed, InstantHDR_1K completes reconstruction in ~30–40 s per scene, roughly 20× faster than HDR-GS (~700–800 s) and ~50× faster than GaussHDR (~1800–1900 s), while the zero-shot feed-forward pass alone takes under 3 s (~700× faster than optimization-based methods).

## Relation to Prior Work

InstantHDR builds directly on the AnySplat feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting pipeline and the VGGT alternating-attention transformer as a frozen geometry backbone, extending the 3DGS representation with log-radiance spherical-harmonic colors for HDR. It departs from the dominant optimization-based HDR NVS paradigm (HDR-GS, GaussHDR) by replacing per-scene densification and iterative tone-mapper fitting with a single forward pass, and from naive feed-forward extensions (e.g., AnySplat applied to multi-exposure inputs) by introducing a geometry-guided appearance module that reuses the frozen encoder's cross-view attention maps to fuse exposure-inconsistent features. The MetaNet for scene-specific tone mapping and the HDR-Pretrain dataset (168 Blender scenes) are new contributions not present in prior HDR or feed-forward 3D reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 700× speedup for initialization and ~20× speedup even after post-optimization make HDR NVS practical for real-time or near-real-time applications (autonomous driving, digital humans, immersive editing) that were previously infeasible with per-scene optimization. The HDR-Pretrain dataset addresses a genuine data bottleneck for feed-forward HDR models, and the insight that frozen geometry-encoder attention maps remain reliable under extreme exposure variation (0.125 s–32 s) opens a path for other appearance-invariant tasks. Key open questions include the acknowledged over-brightness of zero-shot HDR outputs, the synthetic-to-real gap requiring one-time finetuning, and the reliance on feed-forward pose estimation that lags COLMAP on dense noise-free scenes.
