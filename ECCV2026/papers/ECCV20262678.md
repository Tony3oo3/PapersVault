## Problem

Existing ViT-based continuous VAEs suffer from three practical limitations as visual tokenizers: weak resolution extrapolation (absolute positional embeddings break at unseen resolutions), slow convergence with poor fine-grained color and texture fidelity, and inconsistent scaling gains when increasing model size. Pure CNN-VAEs, while efficient at local details, model long-range dependencies inefficiently, leaving an architectural gap where neither paradigm alone yields a complete representation for high-fidelity reconstruction.

## Input / Output

TransVAE takes RGB images at 256×256 as input and produces a continuous Gaussian latent representation with spatial compression ratios of f8d16, f16d32, or f32d32, which is then decoded back to a reconstructed image. A key property is that models trained exclusively at 256×256 can perform robust inference at 512×512 and 1024×1024 without retraining, enabled by the RoPE-only positional encoding. The latent space is regularized with KL divergence and optionally aligned via VF loss for generation-friendly semantics.

## Method

TransVAE unifies a shallow CNN front-end (two stages of ResBlocks) for hierarchical local feature extraction with a deep Transformer backbone for global context modeling, connected via sequential overlapping convolutions (SeqConv) for downsampling/upsampling. Three principled modifications target the identified limitations: (i) RoPE-only positional encoding replaces absolute embeddings, enabling resolution extrapolation without interpolation; (ii) a multi-stage CNN-Transformer design lets the network build local features before applying global attention; and (iii) a convolutional feed-forward network (FullConv with residual connection) re-introduces local spatial mixing inside each Transformer block. Additional stability measures include RMSNorm before QKV projections and parallel information-preserving shortcuts at every spatial resolution change. The architecture scales from 44M to 2.3B parameters with predictable improvements in reconstruction PSNR, linear probing accuracy, and downstream generation FID.

## Results

At f8d16 compression, TransVAE-L (719M) achieves 33.30 PSNR / 0.041 LPIPS on ImageNet-1k 256×256, surpassing FLUX-VAE (84M) at 32.80 / 0.044, and extends to 40.67 PSNR at 1024×1024 vs. FLUX-VAE's 39.84. At f16d32, TransVAE-T (44M) reaches 29.08 PSNR at 25 epochs—already exceeding VA-VAE's (70M) 28.57 PSNR at 100 epochs—and TransVAE-L (545M) achieves 38.25 PSNR at 1024×1024 vs. VA-VAE's 36.15. For downstream generation with LightningDiT-XL, TransVAE-VF-L achieves gFID(w/CFG) of 1.85 vs. VA-VAE-VF's 1.96 at 6480 epochs, demonstrating a 2× faster convergence in the f8d16 setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransVAE outperforms all nine baselines on reconstruction quality. Against the strongest CNN-VAE baselines, TransVAE-L (719M) beats FLUX-VAE (84M) by +0.50 PSNR at 256×256 and +0.83 PSNR at 1024×1024, while TransVAE-T (60M) already matches FLUX-VAE at 256×256 (32.83 vs. 32.80) and surpasses it at 1024×1024 (40.15 vs. 39.84). Against VA-VAE (70M, f16d32), TransVAE-T (44M) achieves 29.08 PSNR vs. 28.57 at 256×256, and TransVAE-L (545M) extends the gap to 38.25 vs. 36.15 at 1024×1024. All ViT-based tokenizers (MAETok, SoftVQ, ViTok, DeTok) are substantially outperformed: DeTok fails to extrapolate beyond 256×256 due to absolute position embeddings, while ViTok's RoPE design allows extrapolation but its reconstruction remains inferior (24.36 PSNR vs. TransVAE-T's 29.08). DC-AE (f32d32) is outperformed by TransVAE-B (f32d32) by +0.43 PSNR at 256×256 and +0.57 PSNR at 1024×1024. For downstream generation, TransVAE-VF-L achieves gFID(w/CFG) of 1.85 vs. VA-VAE-VF's 1.96, and in the f8d16 LightningDiT-B setting, TransVAE-VF-L matches FLUX-VAE's FID at 100k steps vs. 200k steps, indicating 2× faster convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-studied lines: CNN-based VAE tokenizers (SD-VAE, FLUX-VAE) that dominate latent diffusion models but struggle with long-range dependencies, and ViT-based tokenizers (ViTok, DeTok, MAGI-VAE, MAETok) that offer global context but suffer from slow convergence, poor resolution extrapolation, and inconsistent scaling. It draws on hybrid CNN-ViT architectures from visual understanding (DHVT, CvT, PVT) and adapts them to the continuous VAE setting for the first time in a principled, component-by-component manner. The two direct technical dependencies are RoPE for position encoding (replacing APE to enable resolution extrapolation) and DC-AE's information-preserving shortcuts for high-compression fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAE tokenizers are a critical bottleneck in latent diffusion pipelines, and this paper provides the first systematic evidence that a hybrid CNN-ViT design can simultaneously achieve fast convergence, robust resolution extrapolation (256→1024), and predictable scaling up to 2.3B parameters—properties that neither pure CNN nor pure ViT VAEs deliver. However, several open questions remain: VF alignment training degrades extrapolation performance, suggesting that resolution-generalizable semantic alignment requires new objectives; inference latency is notably slower than FLUX-VAE and VA-VAE due to unoptimized kernels; and the absolute reconstruction gains over strong CNN baselines (e.g., FLUX-VAE) are modest, so the practical adoption case rests more on scalability headroom than on immediate quality wins.
