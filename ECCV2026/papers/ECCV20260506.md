## Problem

Existing universal virtual IHC staining models rely on external text prompts to guide biomarker-specific generation, but the semantic gap between text embeddings and fine-grained pathological morphology causes 'manifold drift,' pulling generation away from real tissue structure. Additionally, these models use homogeneous shared parameters to fit heterogeneous tissue components (nuclei, stroma, cytoplasm), compromising marker specificity, and no large-scale 1(H&E):N(IHC) paired training matrices exist to support universal model training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single H&E image (256×256 for Orion-CRC, 512×512 for MIST) and a discrete biomarker index as input. The H&E image is channel-concatenated with the noisy IHC latent (forming a 6-channel tensor) at every denoising step, and the biomarker index is mapped through a class embedder into learnable tokens injected into transformer layers. The output is a pixel-level virtual IHC image for the requested marker, generated by integrating a predicted velocity field via an ODE solver (Euler method) from pure noise.

## Method

HUSE is built on the JiT pixel-space flow-matching backbone with x-prediction, avoiding latent-space compression loss. Its core mechanism, Joint Manifold Anchoring (JMA), concatenates the H&E image with the noisy IHC state at the input stage, treating H&E as a persistent structural scaffold that anchors the entire generation trajectory to the intrinsic tissue manifold. For local refinement, a Histocomponent-driven Mixture-of-Experts (Hi-MoE) routes each token to one of three specialized experts (nucleus, stroma, cytoplasm/background) via cosine similarity against CLIP-initialized prototypes, while a Representation Conflict Gating (RCG) module monitors feature displacement through the shared expert and dynamically scales the residual contribution of the assigned specialist. Training data is constructed via a pioneering mIF-driven paradigm that converts in-situ aligned multiplex immunofluorescence channels into clinically realistic virtual IHC images through color deconvolution and physics-driven DAB synthesis, yielding large-scale 1(H&E):16(IHC) matrices validated by senior pathologists.

## Results

On the Orion-CRC dataset (16 biomarkers), HUSE achieves SOTA average performance with SSIM 0.829, PSNR 22.81 dB, and KID 19.93, surpassing all universal models and matching or exceeding the best single-marker baselines (e.g., ASP: SSIM 0.806, PSNR 22.72). On the MIST dataset (4 biomarkers), HUSE delivers SSIM 0.361, PSNR 18.35 dB, and KID 54.78, outperforming all universal models by a wide margin (e.g., PD-UniST: SSIM 0.250, PSNR 15.49, KID 87.35). HUSE also maintains a stable computational footprint (~142 GFLOPs) regardless of input resolution, unlike baselines whose FLOPs quadruple when scaling from 256×256 to 512×512.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUSE outperforms all three universal baselines on both datasets across all three metrics. On Orion-CRC, HUSE leads PD-UniST by +0.019 SSIM, +0.53 dB PSNR, and −20.44 KID, and leads Multi-IHC by +0.016 SSIM, +0.44 dB PSNR, and −15.94 KID; VIMs is dramatically inferior (SSIM 0.522 vs. 0.829, KID 181.15 vs. 19.93), confirming that text-prompt-driven generation suffers severe manifold drift. On MIST, the gap widens: HUSE exceeds PD-UniST by +0.111 SSIM and +2.86 dB PSNR, and reduces KID from 87.35 to 54.78. Against single-marker baselines, HUSE matches or slightly exceeds ASP (the strongest single-marker model) on Orion-CRC (SSIM 0.829 vs. 0.806, PSNR 22.81 vs. 22.72) while achieving comparable KID (19.93 vs. 24.75), demonstrating that a single universal network can rival dedicated per-marker models. On MIST, HUSE substantially outperforms all single-marker baselines (e.g., +0.192 SSIM over USIGAN, +4.92 dB PSNR over ASP), indicating that the structural anchoring via JMA is particularly beneficial when training data is limited and alignment quality is lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUSE builds directly on the JiT pixel-space diffusion backbone (Li & He, CVPR 2026), adopting its x-prediction and flow-matching formulation to avoid the latent-space compression losses that degrade microscopic histological detail in LDM-based approaches. It departs from prior universal IHC staining models (VIMS, PD-Unist, Multi-IHC) that condition generation on externally designed text prompts, replacing them with learnable class-embedder tokens and a Joint Manifold Anchoring mechanism that couples the H&E image as a persistent structural scaffold at the input level. The Hi-MoE module with CLIP-initialized histocomponent prototypes and the RCG gating mechanism are novel architectural contributions not present in prior virtual staining work, while the mIF-driven cross-modal data synthesis paradigm is a new data pipeline that repurposes multiplex immunofluorescence signals to construct 1(H&E):N(IHC) training matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mIF-driven data synthesis paradigm addresses a genuine bottleneck in the field—existing public H&E-IHC datasets lack the co-localized multiplexed labels needed to train and evaluate universal models—and the pathologist-validated realism (4.91/5) suggests this pipeline could become a standard data source for the community. The JMA concept of anchoring generation to the H&E manifold is a clean, generalizable solution to manifold drift that could transfer to other paired-image translation tasks in pathology. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (Orion-CRC and MIST), the mIF-to-IHC synthesis is a physics-driven approximation rather than real IHC, and it remains open whether the pixel-space JiT backbone scales efficiently to full whole-slide-image resolution or to tissue types beyond CRC and breast cancer.
