## Problem

Vision-language models for video reasoning suffer substantial accuracy and reasoning degradation (up to 35%) under realistic visual disturbances such as adverse weather, dynamic occlusion, abrupt illumination changes, and camera motion, yet existing benchmarks evaluate models only under clean conditions and prior robustness methods treat corruption as generic noise rather than structured, semantically grounded perturbations. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether video reasoning models can be trained to maintain coherent reasoning under the specific, spatially-aware, and temporally coherent disturbances encountered in real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (T frames of H×W×C) paired with a text query and produces a structured output consisting of an embodied reasoning process enclosed in  tags followed by a final answer in <answer></answer> tags. During training, each video is processed in a dual-branch configuration: the clean video serves as a fixed reference anchor (gradients detached) while a corrupted counterpart—generated by jointly applying a style-specific spatial mask (weather, lighting, camera, or occlusion) and a random temporal permutation—is optimized to produce consistent outputs. The corrupted video is regenerated on demand from stored mask metadata rather than stored as full video data.

## Method

ROVA combines three mechanisms: (1) structured spatio-temporal corruption that generates perturbed videos using depth-aware, style-specific continuous masks fused with binary layout maps, combined with random frame permutation to disrupt temporal order—unlike generic pixel-level augmentations, these preserve spatial grounding and cross-frame coherence; (2) a self-reflective difficulty-aware curriculum where the model itself evaluates clean-vs-corrupted response divergence to classify each sample as easy (discarded), informative (prioritized for training), or difficult (stored in a temporal memory buffer with periodic re-evaluation and eviction after a maximum retry threshold), forming an adaptive online curriculum that tracks the model's evolving capability; (3) dual-branch alignment optimization via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where the perturbed branch is trained to match the clean branch's outputs using a composite reward combining format compliance, answer accuracy, and a robustness-aware alignment reward that scores semantic consistency in both the reasoning trace and final answer between paired clean and perturbed inputs.

## Results

On PVRBench, ROVA achieves 0.50 average accuracy under perturbations (7B, Embodied-R base) vs. 0.42 for the base model, a 17% relative gain, and 0.56 (72B, Qwen2.5-VL base) vs. 0.45 for the base, a 24% relative gain. ROVA 72B (0.56) exceeds Gemini-3-Pro (0.55) and GPT-4o (0.51), while ROVA 7B (0.50) matches GPT-4o. Open-source baselines suffer 21–35% accuracy drops and 16–28% reasoning quality drops under perturbations, whereas ROVA reduces these drops to 5–15% and 10–17%, respectively. On clean (unperturbed) data, ROVA also improves: e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B goes from 0.51 to 0.53, and Qwen3-VL-13B from 0.53 to 0.56.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROVA (7B, Embodied-R base) achieves 0.50 average perturbed accuracy, surpassing Embodied-R (0.42) by 17% relative and matching GPT-4o (0.51) while using a 7B open-source backbone. ROVA 72B (Qwen2.5-VL base) reaches 0.56, exceeding both Gemini-3-Pro (0.55) and GPT-4o (0.51) by 1–5 points. Against open-source video LLMs, ROVA consistently wins: Qwen2.5-VL-7B improves from 0.33 to 0.47 (+42% relative), InternVL2.5-8B from 0.31 to 0.40 (+29%), and Qwen3-VL-13B from 0.40 to 0.52 (+30%). In the dedicated robustness comparison (Table 4), ROVA (0.47 acc / 2.99 reasoning) outperforms PanoDPO (0.43/2.88), Ro-Bench (0.42/2.83), Dbl. Visual Def. (0.40/2.74), and MVTamperBench (0.38/2.68) while using fewer GPU-hours than PanoDPO (134.4 vs. 146.0). On out-of-distribution perturbations (MSRVTT-P, YouCook2-P), ROVA gains +21–23% accuracy and +19–20% reasoning over the base model, confirming that the learned representations generalize beyond the training perturbation distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROVA builds directly on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) from DeepSeekMath as its optimization backbone, but departs from prior robustness work (generic data augmentation, adversarial training, test-time adaptation) by introducing structured, semantically grounded spatio-temporal corruptions rather than treating robustness as a single generic objective. The self-reflective difficulty-aware curriculum distinguishes itself from fixed-schedule curriculum learning by dynamically re-estimating sample informativeness via the model's own evolving outputs. The dual-branch consistency alignment with reasoning-aware rewards is a novel combination that has no direct precedent in the video reasoning literature, and PVRBench extends the ImageNet-C corruption-evaluation paradigm from static images to temporally coherent video reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 21–35% accuracy drops observed in open-source models under realistic perturbations expose a significant deployment gap that clean-benchmark evaluations mask, making PVRBench a practically important diagnostic tool for the embodied AI and autonomous driving communities. The finding that structured corruption masks trained on only two perturbation styles transfer to unseen types suggests a path toward efficient robustness training without exhaustive perturbation coverage. Open questions remain: whether the synthetic perturbations adequately capture the full diversity of real-world degradation, whether the LLM-as-judge evaluation introduces systematic biases in reasoning quality scores, and whether the approach scales to longer-horizon embodied tasks with more complex action spaces.
