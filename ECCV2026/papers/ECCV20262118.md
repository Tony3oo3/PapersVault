## Problem

Fusing LiDAR and 4D radar for 3D object detection is hindered by three interrelated gaps: existing methods apply only rudimentary transformations (magnitude, squaring) to raw radial velocities rather than modeling true object motion states; the large density and semantic disparity between dense LiDAR and sparse, noisy 4D radar prevents effective single-space fusion; and current BEV-level fusion relies on simple concatenation without dynamic alignment, making it fragile under adverse weather where one modality degrades.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two synchronized point clouds per frame: a LiDAR cloud with 4D features (x, y, z, intensity) and a 4D radar cloud with 7D features (x, y, z, RCS, radial velocity, ego-compensated absolute radial velocity, timestamp). Both are encoded into pillar features before fusion. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes parameterized by position, dimensions, heading, and class label, produced by a detection head on the fused BEV feature map. The framework is built atop the L4DR pillar-based backbone and operates at 148 ms inference on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU.

## Method

HVA-Fusion is a two-stage hierarchical fusion architecture. In Stage I (pillar space), a Motion Velocity Estimation and Encoding (MVEE) module uses PointNet++ to predict point-wise heading angles, recovering true velocities from radial components via geometric correction, and a foreground-probability filter removes radar clutter; a Cross-Modal Progressive Adaptation (CPA) module then performs bidirectional feature propagation within co-located pillars followed by a density-aware k-NN cross-modal neighborhood attention that adaptively calibrates sampling radius based on local radar density. In Stage II (BEV space), an RCS-Guided Gaussian Generation (RGG) module densifies sparse radar features by constructing per-pillar Gaussian kernels whose scale is conditioned on RCS (via a physically-constrained Sigmoid baseline plus a learnable MLP refinement) and modulated by normalized range; a Multi-Scale Bi-Directional Deformable Attention Gate (MS-BDDG) then performs three-layer hierarchical bidirectional deformable cross-attention with learnable spatial offsets to dynamically align misregistered features, combined with a modal adaptive gating network that suppresses degraded-modality noise. Training uses Focal Loss, Smooth L1, a cosine direction-prediction loss, and Random Modal Dropout (20% probability) to simulate single-sensor failure.

## Results

On the VoD dataset, HVA-Fusion achieves 75.51% mAP on the Entire Area and 88.48% mAP on the Driving Area, setting a new SOTA among LiDAR-4D radar fusion methods. It particularly excels in pedestrian (72.46% AP) and cyclist (83.96% AP) detection. On the K-Radar adverse-weather dataset, HVA-Fusion reaches 65.7% AP_3D (vs. 53.5% for L4DR), and on TJ4DRadSet it achieves 70.09% AP_3D mAP (vs. 65.74% for L4DR). Under simulated fog (VoD-Fog), HVA-Fusion leads at fog levels 0–2 (e.g., 81.2% vs. 78.9% mAP at level 0) but is slightly surpassed by L4DR at extreme fog level 4 (25.8% vs. 28.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVA-Fusion outperforms all prior LiDAR-4D radar fusion baselines on VoD Entire Area mAP: 75.51% vs. ELMAR (74.89%), SVEFusion (74.55%), CM-FA (73.83%), MoRAL (73.30%), L4DR (72.70%), and MutualForce (71.76%). The gains are driven primarily by pedestrian (+3.1% over ELMAR) and cyclist (+0.9% over SVEFusion) detection, where the MVEE velocity estimation and RGG densification modules contribute most. On K-Radar, HVA-Fusion leads L4DR by 12.2 points in AP_3D (65.7% vs. 53.5%) and 3D-LRF by 20.5 points, with the largest margins in heavy-snow (57.7% vs. 37.0%) and sleet (60.7% vs. 46.2%) conditions. On TJ4DRadSet, HVA-Fusion surpasses L4DR by 4.35 points in AP_3D mAP (70.09% vs. 65.74%) and 3.74 in AP_BEV mAP. The one notable loss is at VoD-Fog level 4 (extreme fog), where L4DR achieves 28.0% mAP vs. HVA-Fusion's 25.8%, attributed to unreliable local cross-modal associations in CPA under severe degradation; at fog levels 0–2, HVA-Fusion consistently leads by 1.7–2.3 mAP points.

## Relation to Prior Work

HVA-Fusion builds directly on L4DR as its backbone, extending its pillar-level Multi-Modal Encoder with a two-stage hierarchical fusion strategy that prior LiDAR-4D radar methods (InterFusion, M²Fusion, 3D-LRF, MutualForce, MoRAL) lacked. The key conceptual departure is the MVEE module, which recovers true object motion velocity by predicting point-wise heading direction rather than relying on the raw radial velocity that prior methods (RadarPillars, MutualForce) use as a simple auxiliary feature. The RGG module introduces RCS-conditioned Gaussian densification as an alternative to temporal accumulation, and the MS-BDDG module replaces the simple BEV-space concatenation common in prior fusion work with multi-scale bidirectional deformable cross-attention and adaptive gating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a well-motivated, modular architecture for weather-robust LiDAR-4D radar fusion that achieves SOTA on three datasets, with particularly strong gains for small dynamic objects (pedestrians, cyclists) where velocity-aware features are most discriminative. However, the authors acknowledge that under extreme fog (level 4), the local cross-modal interaction in CPA becomes unreliable and the method is surpassed by L4DR on cyclist detection, suggesting that adaptive interaction strength (e.g., SNR-aware gating) is needed for the most degraded conditions. The isotropic Gaussian in RGG also blurs small nearby objects like pedestrians, pointing toward shape-aware anisotropic distributions as a clear next step. These limitations frame open questions about when and how much cross-modal interaction is beneficial versus harmful under severe sensor degradation.
