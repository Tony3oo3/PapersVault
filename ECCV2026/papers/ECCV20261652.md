## Problem

Existing SR evaluation metrics (FR and NR) fail to capture semantic-level human preferences: FR metrics penalize plausible but spatially misaligned details, while NR metrics reward sharp but unfaithful artifacts. Moreover, generative SR optimization relies exclusively on GT distribution matching, which does not necessarily correspond to human perceptual judgments and cannot capture higher-level semantic consistency. The paper addresses both gaps by formulating SR evaluation as an LR-conditioned preference modeling problem and using the learned reward to drive preference-aligned generation.

## Input / Output

The evaluator (RefReward-SR) takes a paired LR-HR image and an evaluation prompt, producing a structured output with a reasoning chain in <thinking> tags followed by a numerical score in <answer> tags; during inference it additionally extracts local crops via RAM and Grounding DINO for area-weighted multi-scale scoring. The SR generator takes a single LR image (512×512 training resolution, Real-ESRGAN degradation) and produces an HR reconstruction. The RefSR-18K dataset provides 4,699 annotation groups (18,796 HR reconstructions) where each group contains one LR reference and four HR candidates from eight diverse SR models plus GT, annotated with pairwise rankings on semantic consistency and plausibility.

## Method

RefReward-SR fine-tunes Qwen3-VL 8B via GRPO using a composite reward of a format penalty and an LR-conditioned rank reward computed through a Thurstone-model-based comparative probability over K stochastic rollouts per candidate, averaged across the G−1 other candidates in each group. A global-local crop scoring strategy extracts salient regions via RAM tags and Grounding DINO boxes, then fuses global and local MLLM scores via area-weighted averaging to capture fine-grained semantic violations. For preference-aligned SR generation, the C-FLUX diffusion model is fine-tuned with GRPO using a three-component reward: RefReward-SR (global-only score) for semantic alignment, negative LPIPS for perceptual fidelity to GT, and DEQA for low-level quality, with hyperparameters balancing these signals. The key design choice is treating the LR image as a semantic anchor throughout, enabling the MLLM to reason about whether generated details are consistent with the input rather than merely assessing standalone image quality.

## Results

On the in-domain human preference alignment test, RefReward-SR achieves 85.0% agreement with human annotators (84.7% for humans themselves), 84.5% Recall@1, and 78.0% Filter@1, substantially outperforming the best FR metric (LPIPS at 62.5% agreement) and the best MLLM baseline (Gemini 3 Pro at 58.8%). On the out-of-domain set, RefReward-SR maintains 80.2% agreement and 77.0% Recall@1, again far exceeding all baselines. For preference-aligned SR generation on RealSR, the full method achieves PSNR 24.68, SSIM 0.7101, and Q-Align 4.1596, outperforming the C-FLUX base model and the DP²O-FLUX DPO variant on most metrics, with a 72.7% human win rate in the user study.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefReward-SR achieves near-human agreement (85.0% vs. 84.7% in-domain; 80.2% vs. 81.8% out-of-domain), surpassing the strongest FR metric LPIPS by ~22 points and the best MLLM baseline Gemini 3 Pro by ~26 points in agreement. Notably, zero-shot Qwen3-VL 8B drops below 50% agreement (31.8% in-domain) because it assigns near-identical scores to structurally similar SR candidates, while RefReward-SR's GRPO fine-tuning on RefSR-18K resolves this. For SR generation on RealSR, the full method outperforms C-FLUX (base) on PSNR (24.68 vs. 24.42), SSIM (0.7101 vs. 0.6742), and Q-Align (4.1596 vs. 3.4943), and beats DP²O-FLUX on SSIM (0.7101 vs. 0.6774) and Q-Align (4.1596 vs. 3.9757), though DP²O-FLUX edges ahead on MUSIQ (72.42 vs. 71.70) and CLIPIQA (0.7156 vs. 0.7007). On DIV2K-Val, the method leads on SSIM (0.5786), Q-Align (4.4605), and VQ-R1 (4.4396) but trails TSD-SR and LucidFlux on MUSIQ and NIMA, indicating that NR sharpness metrics still favor some aggressive generative models. In the user study, the method achieves a 72.7% win rate over C-FLUX (60.2%) and DP²O-FLUX (67.1%), confirming that the RefReward-SR-guided optimization produces reconstructions humans prefer despite not always winning on every automated metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the C-FLUX diffusion-based SR model from DP²O-SR and the GRPO reinforcement learning algorithm from the DeepSeek lineage, but departs from both in key ways. Rather than using DPO or existing FR/NR metrics as reward signals (as DP²O-SR and prior RL-for-SR works do), it introduces a purpose-built LR-conditioned MLLM reward model (RefReward-SR) trained on a new preference dataset (RefSR-18K), shifting the optimization objective from GT distribution matching to explicit human semantic preference alignment. The conceptual departure is treating the LR input as a 'semantic anchor' for evaluation—mimicking how humans assess SR—rather than relying on pixel-level correspondence (FR) or reference-free sharpness (NR), which prior metrics handle only passively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: generative SR has outgrown its evaluation and optimization tools, which still assume pixel-level fidelity or reference-free sharpness as proxies for quality. RefSR-18K and the LR-conditioned evaluation paradigm provide a foundation for human-centric assessment in restoration tasks more broadly. However, the authors acknowledge a circularity risk—using RefReward-SR as both training reward and evaluation metric invites reward hacking—and the approach's generalization beyond SR (e.g., to denoising, inpainting) remains untested. An open question is whether the MLLM-based reward signal can be made more efficient for iterative optimization, since even the global-only scoring still requires a full MLLM forward pass per candidate.
