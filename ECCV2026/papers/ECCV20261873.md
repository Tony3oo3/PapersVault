## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods adopt rigid pixel-to-Gaussian or voxel-to-Gaussian pipelines that uniformly allocate Gaussians, producing redundant primitives in simple regions and across overlapping views while lacking any mechanism to control the total Gaussian count without retraining. This results in suboptimal, non-compact representations under a limited Gaussian budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261873/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N_ctx uncalibrated RGB context images (no camera poses or intrinsics provided) and a scalar target Gaussian budget N_G as input, and outputs a set of 3D Gaussian primitives (center, opacity, quaternion rotation, scale, and spherical-harmonic coefficients) whose count does not exceed N_G, along with predicted camera intrinsics and camera-to-world poses for each context view. The budget is a single scalar that, at inference time, determines a threshold via a budget-matching algorithm, allowing the same trained model to produce a family of representations at different compactness levels without retraining.

## Method

F⁴Splat uses a VGGT-based geometry backbone (DINOv2 encoder + alternating frame-wise/global self-attention) to encode multi-view geometry, followed by a DPT-based decoder with two parallel heads that predict multi-scale Gaussian parameter maps and densification score maps at L=3 resolution levels. The key insight is a feed-forward predictive densification: during training, the densification score for each spatial region is supervised to match the log-scaled L2 norm of the homodirectional view-space positional gradient (borrowed from AbsGS's adaptive density control), computed by backpropagating the rendering loss through a novel target view. At inference, thresholding these predicted scores at each scale level yields non-overlapping binary allocation masks that concentrate Gaussians in geometrically complex or under-represented regions while suppressing duplicates in simple or multi-view-overlapping areas. Training further employs novel-view rendering supervision (with Sim(3) camera alignment to resolve coordinate-frame mismatch) and a scene-scale regularization loss to stabilize optimization in the uncalibrated setting.

## Results

On RE10K with 8 input views, F⁴Splat_τ⁻ (447K Gaussians) achieves LPIPS 0.128, SSIM 0.863, and PSNR 25.73, outperforming AnySplat (LPIPS 0.167, PSNR 24.07) by a large margin at the same Gaussian budget. Remarkably, F⁴Splat_τ⁺ uses only 105K Gaussians (~24% of the budget) yet still achieves LPIPS 0.140 and PSNR 25.35, surpassing AnySplat's 447K-Gaussian result. On the unseen ACID dataset, F⁴Splat_τ⁺ with just 52K Gaussians (11% of AnySplat's 481K) achieves PSNR 24.55 vs. AnySplat's 23.30. Under the challenging 2-view setting on ACID, F⁴Splat_τ⁻ (131K GS) reaches PSNR 26.26, competitive with pose-free SPFSplat (26.80) while operating in the uncalibrated setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary uncalibrated baseline AnySplat, F⁴Splat wins decisively at every view count: at 8 views, F⁴Splat_τ⁻ improves PSNR from 24.07 to 25.73 (+1.66 dB) and LPIPS from 0.167 to 0.128 at the same 447K Gaussian budget, while F⁴Splat_τ⁺ matches or exceeds AnySplat's quality using only 105K Gaussians (76% fewer). Against VicaSplat (uncalibrated), the gap is even larger (e.g., PSNR 25.73 vs. 20.77 at 8 views). In the pose-free setting, F⁴Splat_τ⁻ at 16 views (PSNR 26.28, 820K GS) surpasses SPFSplat (PSNR 25.88, 1049K GS) despite using fewer Gaussians, and F⁴Splat_τ⁺ at 16 views (PSNR 25.91, 210K GS) already beats SPFSplat while using only 20% of its primitives. On the 2-view ACID benchmark, F⁴Splat_τ⁻ (PSNR 26.26) is competitive with pose-free SPFSplat (26.80) and NoPoSplat (25.96) while requiring no camera intrinsics, and F⁴Splat_τ⁺ (PSNR 25.75, 26K GS) outperforms NoPoSplat (25.96, 131K GS) with 5× fewer Gaussians. On the DTU generalization test (trained on RE10K+DL3DV), F⁴Splat achieves PSNR 19.76 with 26K Gaussians vs. NoPoSplat's 17.51 with 131K, confirming the advantage scales with training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

F⁴Splat builds on the VGGT backbone for multi-view geometric encoding and the 3DGS representation with differentiable rasterization, but its core conceptual departure is from prior feed-forward 3DGS methods (pixelSplat, MVSplat, NoPoSplat, VicaSplat, SPFSplat) that fix Gaussian count via uniform pixel-to-Gaussian allocation, and from AnySplat's uniform voxel-to-Gaussian scheme that requires retraining to change the budget. The key idea is to repurpose the iterative adaptive density control (ADC) signal from optimization-based 3DGS—specifically AbsGS's homodirectional view-space positional gradient—as a learnable feed-forward prediction target, converting an iterative densification decision into a single-pass densification score that enables spatially adaptive, budget-controllable allocation without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to explicitly control the final Gaussian count at inference time without retraining is practically important for deployment scenarios where memory or rendering speed constraints demand a specific primitive budget. Achieving competitive or superior novel-view synthesis quality with only 10–28% of the Gaussians used by uncalibrated baselines suggests that most prior feed-forward 3DGS representations are substantially over-allocated in simple or redundant regions. Open questions include how the multi-scale thresholding strategy (limited to 3 levels here) scales to much larger scenes or very tight budgets, whether the VGGT backbone dependency limits applicability to settings where its geometric priors are less reliable, and whether the learned densification score transfers well to domains outside indoor/outdoor real-estate scenes.
