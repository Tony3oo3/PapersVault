## Problem

Adversarial detection methods have been well-studied for unimodal vision or language models, but their transferability to multimodal VLPs remains unexplored because contrastive alignment and fusion reshape the geometry of clean neighborhoods in embedding space. The paper identifies that VLP embedding spaces exhibit structured anisotropy distinct from unimodal models, and that adversarial perturbations systematically push representations into off-manifold regions—a geometric signature that has not been formally characterized or exploited for detection in this setting.

## Input / Output

GeoDetect takes a query image-text pair and a randomly sampled clean reference batch of n embeddings (n=128 for CLIP, n=64 for ALBEF/TCL) as input, and outputs a binary adversarial/clean decision. For LID, scores are computed layer-wise across multiple encoder layers (producing a vector of l features fed to logistic regression), while k-NN, Mahalanobis, and KDE produce a single scalar score compared against a threshold calibrated on a held-out split. The method is task-agnostic: it requires no class labels, making it applicable to both zero-shot classification and image-text retrieval.

## Method

GeoDetect is grounded in a theoretical analysis showing that under anisotropic covariance (verified via isotropy scores I₁, I₂, and effective rank), the optimal adversarial embedding derived from KL-divergence maximization takes the form z'*(Σ+λI)⁻¹λz, which suppresses tangent components and amplifies normal components, pushing embeddings off the clean manifold. This implies adversarial examples have strictly greater expected distance to random clean points than their clean counterparts (Theorem 1). Practically, the method extracts deep representations from VLP encoders and computes four geometric scores—LID (layer-wise, capturing local dimensionality shifts), k-NN distance, Mahalanobis distance (using clean-data covariance), and KDE—against a clean reference batch. LID's layer-wise operation across both unimodal and multimodal encoder layers makes it particularly sensitive to perturbations in fused VLPs, while the other three metrics operate on the image encoder output. Detection is performed via a threshold rule (scalar metrics) or logistic regression (multi-layer LID), requiring no fine-tuning of the VLP itself.

## Results

On CLIP_CNN zero-shot classification, GeoDetect-k-NN achieves near-perfect detection with AUC of 99.65–100% and FPR95 of 0–1.62% across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1k, STL-10, and Food-101, while the MCM baseline achieves only 41–96% AUC and 17–94% FPR95 under the same attacks. GeoDetect-LID is comparable, reaching 99.31–100% AUC. On ALBEF, GeoDetect-k-NN and Mahalanobis maintain strong performance on unimodal attacks (AUC 99–100%) but degrade on multimodal Sep_multi attacks (AUC 78–99%), where the multimodal-aware LID variant partially recovers (AUC 78–99%). Under adaptive attacks on retrieval tasks, GeoDetect-LID retains AUC of 70–95% versus 51–74% for k-NN, confirming robustness when the attacker knows the detector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is MCM [Ming et al., NeurIPS 2022], a score-based zero-shot OOD detector that uses softmax-normalized similarity scores. On CLIP_CNN, GeoDetect-k-NN and GeoDetect-LID overwhelmingly outperform MCM: for example, on CIFAR-100 under Sep_uni, k-NN achieves 100% AUC / 0% FPR95 versus MCM's 41.13% AUC / 94.15% FPR95 — a gap of roughly 59 AUC points. On ImageNet-1k under Co-Attack, k-NN reaches 99.67% AUC / 0.89% FPR95 while MCM drops to 80.83% AUC / 68.38% FPR95. On ALBEF, the comparison is more nuanced: MCM matches or slightly exceeds GeoDetect on Sep_uni (e.g., 100% AUC on CIFAR-10), but GeoDetect-k-NN and Mahalanobis substantially outperform MCM on Sep_multi (e.g., 100% vs. 82.80% AUC on CIFAR-100), indicating that geometric scores better capture multimodal perturbations that shift the fused representation. Overall, GeoDetect wins decisively on aligned VLPs (CLIP) and on multimodal attacks for fused VLPs (ALBEF), while MCM remains competitive only on unimodal attacks for fused models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends geometric adversarial detection methods (LID, k-NN, Mahalanobis, KDE) from unimodal classification settings—where they were established by Ma et al. (ICLR 2018) and others—to multimodal vision-language pre-trained models. The key conceptual departure is providing a theoretical bridge: the anisotropic structure of contrastive-learned VLP embedding spaces (previously observed empirically in CLIP) is shown to guarantee that adversarial perturbations push embeddings off-manifold, creating a measurable expected distance gap. Unlike prior VLP-specific detectors such as PIP (VQA-only, attention-based) or MCM (classification-only, score-based), GeoDetect is task-agnostic and architecture-agnostic, covering both aligned (CLIP) and fused (ALBEF, TCL) VLPs across classification and retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoDetect offers a lightweight, training-free detection layer that can be bolted onto any VLP without fine-tuning, making it practically attractive for safety-critical deployments where adversarial training is too expensive. The theoretical grounding (anisotropy → off-manifold deviation → distance gap) provides a principled explanation for *why* simple geometric metrics work, rather than relying on empirical heuristics. Open questions include whether the anisotropy assumption and resulting detection gap persist in newer, larger VLPs (e.g., LLaVA, Flamingo) or in video/audio-language settings, and whether the significant performance degradation under k-NN adaptive attacks (AUC dropping from ~100 to ~60–75) limits real-world deployability against informed adversaries.
