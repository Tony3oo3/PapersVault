## Problem

Vision-only relative camera pose estimation degrades significantly under common real-world conditions such as motion blur, occlusions, and poor lighting, yet no prior work has exploited passive, incidental scene audio as a complementary spatial signal for pose estimation in in-the-wild video. Existing audio-based pose methods rely on actively emitted echolocation signals in simulated environments, leaving the rich geometric information in naturally occurring multichannel sound entirely untapped for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261435/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of RGB frames together with their temporally synchronized multi-channel audio segments (≥2 channels, 1000 ms windows centered on each frame), captured by microphones rigidly co-located with the camera. It outputs a 6-DoF relative camera pose as a 3×4 rotation-translation matrix, predicted bidirectionally (source→target and target→source) in a single forward pass. No assumptions are made about audio content beyond the presence of at least two co-located microphones, and the method operates on passive, uncontrolled scene sound rather than emitted probes.

## Method

The core contribution is a Spatial Audio Encoder that fuses two complementary representations: an analytical 360-dimensional direction-of-arrival spectrum computed via a MUSIC++ algorithm (capturing angular sound-energy distribution) and a learned binaural embedding extracted from a model trained on a Novel Viewpoint Acoustic Synthesis task (predicting inter-channel spectrogram differences, which forces the encoder to encode spatial geometry). These audio features are concatenated and late-fused with the visual features of the Reloc3r transformer backbone at the pose prediction head, preserving the visual encoder's pre-trained cues while allowing audio to augment them. The binaural encoder is trained self-supervised with an L1 loss on inter-channel difference spectrograms, while the full pose predictor is trained supervised with combined rotation and translation losses. This late-fusion design ensures that uninformative or corrupted audio cannot override strong visual signals, providing graceful degradation and robustness under visual corruption.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D validation split, the proposed audio-visual method achieves Total AUC@5/10/20 of 0.81/2.99/8.82, yielding relative gains of +66.7%/+44.0%/+31.2% over the vision-only Reloc3r baseline (0.57/2.33/7.33). Under corrupted visual inputs, the method maintains Total AUC@5 of 0.35 versus 0.21 for vision-only (+66.7%). On the HM3D-SS dataset, the method achieves a mean absolute rotation error of 0.06°, outperforming the state-of-the-art SLfM (0.77°) by 12.8× and the vision-only Reloc3r (0.09°) by 33.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vision-only Reloc3r [16] baseline, the proposed method wins on every metric across both datasets. On Ego-Exo4D, Total AUC@5 improves from 0.57 to 0.81 (+66.7%), with rotation AUC@5 rising from 37.35 to 38.54 and translation AUC@5 from 0.73 to 1.03. Under corrupted vision, the gap widens: Total AUC@5 goes from 0.21 to 0.35 (+66.7%), confirming audio's robustness when visual cues degrade. On HM3D-SS, the method achieves a mean absolute rotation error of 0.06°, dramatically outperforming the prior audio-visual state-of-the-art SLfM [11] (0.77°, a 12.8× reduction) and the vision-only Reloc3r (0.09°, a 33.3% improvement). The audio-only baselines (NA-CP, DOA-CP) perform near chance level, underscoring that the gains come from the audio-visual fusion rather than audio alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Reloc3r vision-transformer backbone for relative camera pose estimation by adding a spatial audio encoder that fuses two complementary cues: an analytical MUSIC++ direction-of-arrival spectrum and a learned binaural embedding trained via a novel-view acoustic synthesis (NVAS) objective borrowed from prior audio-visual self-supervised work. It departs sharply from the only prior audio-for-pose work (active echolocation in simulation [70]) by using entirely passive, incidental scene audio captured by consumer-grade multichannel microphones in unconstrained in-the-wild video. The late-fusion design with Reloc3r's pose head positions the audio as an augmenting signal rather than a replacement, preserving the visual backbone's dominance while allowing audio to fill in where vision degrades.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that everyday, uncontrolled ambient audio carries usable geometric signal for 6-DoF relative camera pose in real-world video, opening a practical path for AR, robotics, and embodied AI in visually degraded conditions (darkness, blur, occlusion) where vision-only methods fail. The negative-control experiments (cross-scene audio degrades below vision-only) convincingly rule out dataset-prior explanations, and the robustness analysis under Gaussian noise, blur, and color jitter shows consistent gains across corruption levels. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across devices and datasets, how to explicitly gate audio influence under non-stationary or silent conditions, and whether translation estimation can be further improved in acoustically complex scenes.
