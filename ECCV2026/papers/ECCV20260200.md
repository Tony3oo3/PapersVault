## Problem

Inserting foreground vehicles into autonomous driving simulation scenes requires both visual realism (matching background illumination and style) and strict geometric consistency (rigid-body dynamics across frames). Existing approaches either compose pre-reconstructed 3D assets that suffer from persistent lighting and style gaps, or optimize physics-based inverse rendering per scene using costly CAD meshes, making both pipelines difficult to scale to large, diverse real-world scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a background driving video, per-instance vehicle point clouds (LiDAR or otherwise), and predefined trajectories, which are rendered into dense pixel-level condition maps via fixed-radius point rasterization (radius 0.005) and projected 3D bounding-box masks. It produces a 480×720 video sequence with the vehicle seamlessly inserted (49 frames per clip, extendable to 193 frames via the hierarchical strategy), and optionally a distilled 3D Gaussian Splatting representation of the inserted vehicle for real-time rendering. The pixel-level point cloud conditioning is source-agnostic, enabling cross-dataset and non-LiDAR point cloud inputs at inference without retraining.

## Method

DriveWeaver builds on the frozen Wan2.1 VACE-14B flow-matching video diffusion backbone and introduces a lightweight 3B-parameter PointAdapter—a shallow DiT branch inserted every 5th backbone block—that injects rendered point cloud maps and inpainting masks via residual modulation, requiring only the adapter to be trained (20K iterations, 8×H200 GPUs). For long-term generation, a global-to-local hierarchical strategy first synthesizes a sparse 49-frame anchor sequence in a single forward pass to lock vehicle identity and lighting, then uses those anchors as dense guidance for local high-frame-rate interpolation, eliminating the error accumulation of autoregressive methods. At inference, classifier-free guidance (scale 2.5, 30 steps) with randomly dropped point conditions (p=0.15 during training) improves fidelity. Finally, the temporally consistent output is distilled into an explicit 3D Gaussian representation via OmniRe, decoupling the inserted vehicle from the background for real-time closed-loop simulation.

## Results

On the Waymo dataset (long-term, 193 frames), DriveWeaver achieves FID 17.50, FVD 22.46, and LPIPS 0.159 for visual realism, substantially outperforming all baselines. For geometric consistency, it reaches PSNR 32.23 and SSIM 0.931, ranking second only to HUGSIM (PSNR 33.96, SSIM 0.966) which relies on explicit 3D meshes. On PandaSet, DriveWeaver similarly leads in visual realism (FID 21.54, FVD 146.15, LPIPS 0.182) while HUGSIM retains the geometric consistency edge (PSNR 34.89 vs. 31.34). The distilled 3D Gaussian representation (DriveWeaver-D) incurs only marginal degradation (e.g., FID 24.28 vs. 17.50 on Waymo long-term) while enabling real-time rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveWeaver decisively outperforms all three baselines on visual realism metrics across both Waymo and PandaSet. On Waymo long-term, it reduces FVD from 150.95 (HUGSIM), 105.70 (StreetCrafter), and 63.90 (Wan2.1 VACE-14B) to 22.46, and FID from 56.99/28.49/35.12 to 17.50. HUGSIM, which composes pre-reconstructed 3D assets with shadow synthesis, retains the best geometric consistency (PSNR 33.96, SSIM 0.966 on Waymo long-term) due to its explicit mesh rigidity, but suffers a severe domain gap in lighting and style that inflates FID to 56.99. StreetCrafter, which generates the entire scene autoregressively, shows the worst long-term geometric consistency (PSNR 28.81, SSIM 0.871) due to cumulative semantic drift and shape deformation over extended trajectories. Wan2.1 VACE-14B in MV2V mode, lacking explicit point cloud conditioning, produces suboptimal rigid-body dynamics and ranks last in FID (35.12) among the generative baselines on Waymo long-term.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveWeaver builds directly on the Wan2.1 VACE-14B video diffusion backbone, introducing a lightweight PointAdapter that injects rendered point cloud maps as pixel-level geometric conditions into the frozen DiT via residual connections. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of vehicle insertion in driving simulation—which relies on pre-reconstructed 3D assets (HUGSIM, R3D2, G²Editor) or physics-based inverse rendering with CAD meshes (UrbanCAD, S-NeRF++)—by replacing these costly 3D representations with readily available LiDAR point clouds as inpainting conditions. The global-to-local hierarchical generation strategy also distinguishes itself from autoregressive long-video approaches (StreetCrafter, Vista) by decoupling identity anchoring from local interpolation to prevent temporal drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving simulation: the high cost and limited scalability of 3D asset pipelines for constructing corner-case scenarios. By showing that cheap point cloud renderings suffice as geometric conditions and that the resulting video can be distilled into 3DGS for real-time rendering, the paper offers a scalable path toward large-scale scene augmentation without per-scene optimization. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to multi-vehicle interactions, extreme weather beyond training distribution, and whether the 10-minute per-instance 3DGS distillation cost remains acceptable at fleet-scale deployment. The reliance on synchronized LiDAR for point cloud acquisition, while more accessible than 3D meshes, still constrains applicability to sensor-equipped datasets.
