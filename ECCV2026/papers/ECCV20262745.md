## Problem

High-resolution MLLMs generate thousands of visual tokens per input, and while subset-optimization pruning methods (DPP, MWIS, greedy diversity maximization) effectively model both importance and redundancy, their iterative sequential selection introduces latency that scales linearly with the number of retained tokens. This sequential overhead prevents theoretical FLOP reductions from translating into actual wall-clock speedups, especially as token counts grow in dense single-sequence or multi-frame video settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262745/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes L2-normalized visual token embeddings (N×D matrix) from a vision encoder, a projected text embedding for instruction relevance, and vision encoder self-attention weights for visual saliency. It outputs a pruned subset of K tokens selected via a single Top-K operation, with the critical property that selection latency is retention-invariant (e.g., 2.5 ms whether retaining 512 or 256 tokens in Qwen2.5-VL). The Woodbury matrix identity is exploited to perform the covariance inversion on min(N, D) dimension, avoiding the O(N³) cost in high-resolution regimes where N ≫ D.

## Method

SFPruner embeds redundancy control directly into the scoring space, eliminating iterative subset construction. First, a Semantics-Guided Ridge Leverage Score (SG-RLS) computes ridge leverage scores ℓ_i = v_i(C+λI)^{-1}v_i^T from the feature covariance matrix, which suppresses dominant eigendirections; these are multiplicatively fused with a semantic guidance score (linear combination of temperature-scaled cosine similarity to the text query and CLS-attention saliency) to form a soft-AND gate requiring both structural uniqueness and task relevance. Second, ranking-based directional masking constructs an asymmetric indicator matrix M_ij = I(S_j > S_i) over the pairwise cosine similarity matrix, so that each token's penalty P_i = 1 − max_j(M_ij · C_sim,ij) reflects only suppression from strictly higher-scoring competitors. All operations—matrix inversion, elementwise comparison, row-wise max reduction, and Top-K—are fully parallel tensor operations with no sequential dependency, reducing selection latency from 112.4 ms (CDPruner) to 2.5 ms at 512 tokens while retaining 99.0% relative performance.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 40% token retention (512 tokens), SFPruner achieves 99.0% relative performance retention with a selection latency of 2.5 ms, outperforming CDPruner (98.8%, 112.4 ms) and D²Pruner (98.7%, 31.2 ms). At 20% retention (256 tokens), SFPruner retains 96.5% performance while maintaining the same 2.5 ms latency, compared to CDPruner's 96.2% at 56.7 ms. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B at 640-token retention, SFPruner matches the unpruned baseline at 100.0% relative performance with 3.5 ms selection time. Under extreme scaling (9,216 tokens on Qwen2.5-VL), SFPruner completes pruning in 28 ms versus 458–576 ms for DivPrune/CDPruner, reducing total inference time from 6,101 s to 3,601 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFPruner achieves the highest relative performance at both 40% (99.0%) and 20% (96.5%) retention on Qwen2.5-VL, beating the strongest optimization-based baseline CDPruner (98.8% / 96.2%) and D²Pruner (98.7% / 95.3%) while being 45× and 12× faster in selection time (2.5 ms vs. 112.4 ms / 56.7 ms). Against the learned VisionSelector, SFPruner wins by 0.9% at 40% and 0.8% at 20% retention without requiring any additional training. Compared to heuristic methods (VisionZip, PruMerge, VisPruner), SFPruner gains 1.0–2.0% relative performance at 20% retention (96.5% vs. 93.7–95.1%) at only a 1.4 ms latency cost (2.5 ms vs. 1.1 ms). On LLaVA-NeXT-7B, SFPruner matches the unpruned baseline at 100.0% relative performance at 640-token retention and retains 96.4% at 160 tokens, outperforming DivPrune (92.9%) and VisionZip (90.0%) under aggressive compression. On video (LLaVA-Video-7B), SFPruner achieves the best 94.9% relative performance at 512 tokens/frame, surpassing DivPrune (94.5%) and CDPruner (93.5%) while completing selection in 3.1 ms versus 131.0 ms and 92.0 ms respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFPruner repurposes the Ridge Leverage Score (RLS) from randomized numerical linear algebra—a tool previously applied to transformer compression (MoDeGPT) and quantum attention approximation—as a non-iterative redundancy metric for visual token pruning. It departs from the dominant subset-optimization paradigm (DPP, MMDP, graph-based MWIS) that relies on sequential greedy selection, instead embedding both covariance-level and pairwise redundancy control directly into a single scoring pass. The directional masking mechanism is a novel parallel replacement for the iterative pairwise exclusion step in methods like CDPruner and DivPrune, while the Woodbury matrix identity trick (inverting the smaller of N×N or D×D) is a practical linear-algebraic enabler rather than a conceptual departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underappreciated bottleneck: at high resolution (thousands of tokens), the iterative selection overhead of optimization-based pruners (up to 576 ms) can consume a large fraction of the attention savings they enable, undermining the practical value of token reduction. By reducing selection to ~2.5–28 ms while retaining 96–99% relative accuracy, SFPruner makes the theoretical FLOP reduction actually translate into wall-clock speedup, which is critical for real-time deployment of high-resolution MLLMs. Open questions include whether the single-pass approximation degrades relative to truly optimal subset selection at extreme compression (<10% retention), how the method behaves when the feature dimension D is comparable to or exceeds N (invalidating the Woodbury shortcut), and whether the hyperparameters (α, λ, τ) transfer robustly across architectures without per-model tuning.
