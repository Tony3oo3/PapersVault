## Problem

Integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting into online large-scale LiDAR SLAM faces two critical challenges: unbounded map growth that inflates GPU memory and rendering/registration cost, and error persistence where corrupted primitives bias subsequent registrations over long horizons. LiDAR-only dense Gaussian mapping is especially difficult because sparse range observations provide no photometric cues, making it hard to initialize and optimize Gaussians with meaningful geometric structure.

## Input / Output

The system consumes deskewed LiDAR point cloud scans (deskewing via interpolated ego-motion) and produces a persistent 2D Gaussian Splatting map in spherical coordinates, per-frame SE(3) poses, and a reconstructed 3D mesh. Each Gaussian primitive stores a 3D mean, orientation quaternion, 2D in-plane anisotropic scale, opacity, plus per-Gaussian LiDAR confidence and control-score signals. The map is rasterized into a spherical range-image domain for supervision, and the full tracking-plus-mapping pipeline runs at >20 FPS on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

The core mechanism is a bidirectional covariance coupling between G-ICP tracking and spherical-rasterized 2D Gaussian mapping. During tracking, per-point G-ICP covariances are cached and reused as low-cost priors to initialize new Gaussians' rotation, range-adaptive anisotropic scale, and physics-based opacity (from range and incidence angle), eliminating the need for separate geometric estimation. A control score computed from covariance eigenvalues (linearity, curvature) and registration residuals drives geometry-aware map management: multi-pass planar cover-and-prune compresses redundant flat regions while structure-aware splitting densifies edges and corners. Conversely, mapping-refined Gaussian orientations and scales construct O(1) surfel-like target covariances for covariance-weighted G-ICP, upweighting planar structures via a curvature-derived matching weight, so that optimized Gaussians serve as progressively better registration targets. The mapping loss combines masked L1 depth, positive-only BCE opacity, dual normal supervision (G-ICP normals + rendered surface normals with scheduled activation), and scale regularization, all optimized online per keyframe.

## Results

On the Newer College dataset, the method achieves an F-score of 86.78% (Quad) and 70.35% (Math Institute) using purely online estimated poses at 21.24 FPS average, outperforming PIN-SLAM (50.61% F-score, 8.87 FPS) by +18.90 F-score with a 2.39× speedup, and Splat-LOAM (46.46% F-score, 3.44 FPS) by +23.05 F-score with a 6.18× speedup. The persistent map is compact at 7.23 MB on average, 61.6% smaller than PIN-SLAM (18.83 MB). On KITTI sequence 00, the map management strategy reduces Gaussian count from 2.48M to 1.51M and persistent storage from 61.3 MB to 42.1 MB, confirming scalability in large-scale driving scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest LiDAR-only Gaussian Splatting baseline, Splat-LOAM [7], the proposed method achieves substantially higher F-scores (e.g., 86.78% vs. 72.06% on Newer College Quad; 64.02% vs. 33.97% on Oxford Keb) while running 5–10× faster (21.24 vs. 3.44 FPS average); Splat-LOAM also fails on all KITTI sequences. Compared to the neural-field-based PIN-SLAM [23], the method improves average F-score by +18.90 points (69.51 vs. 50.61) at 2.39× the speed, and produces maps 61.6% smaller (7.23 vs. 18.83 MB). Against the surfel-based SuMa [1], the method achieves lower ATE RMSE on 7 of 9 sequences (e.g., 0.080 m vs. 0.249 m on Newer College Quad) and avoids the tracking failures SuMa exhibits on KITTI 01. The point-based KISS-SLAM [8] shares the same loop-closure backend, so differences reflect the front-end and map representation; the proposed method achieves lower ATE on 6 of 9 sequences. The GT-pose baselines (Voxblox, VDBFusion, N³-Mapping) serve as quality upper bounds; notably, the method with estimated poses matches or exceeds VDBFusion's F-score on Newer College Quad (86.78% vs. 76.27%) while operating fully online, though it trails N³-Mapping on Oxford sequences where dense handheld observations favor neural fields.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three lines of work: G-ICP-based LiDAR tracking (Segal et al.), 2D anisotropic Gaussian Splatting as a surface representation (Huang et al., SIGGRAPH 2024), and the KISS-SLAM pose-graph/loop-closure backend. Its key architectural departure from prior Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems (Splat-LOAM, GS-ICP, G2S-ICP, PINGS) is a LiDAR-only, geometry-centered design that tightly couples tracking and mapping through covariance reuse: G-ICP local covariances initialize Gaussian orientation, scale, and confidence without any separate geometry estimator, while mapping-optimized Gaussian parameters feed back as O(1) target covariances for the next registration. Unlike RGB-D or multi-sensor GS-SLAM approaches, the system relies entirely on range observations and spherical rasterization, making the covariance coupling the sole source of geometric supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time dense LiDAR mapping at >20 FPS with compact persistent storage (42 MB on KITTI seq 00) is practically important for autonomous-driving simulation and HD-map production, where sparse point maps are insufficient for visibility-aware reasoning. The covariance-coupling design is elegant in that it eliminates a separate geometry-estimation module, but its reliance on G-ICP local statistics means performance degrades in vegetation, dynamic-object-heavy, or geometrically degenerate scenes. Open questions include whether the piecewise-rigid loop-closure correction (keyframe-wise delta applied to associated Gaussians) remains adequate after large global corrections, and whether the sensor-specific parameters (voxel size, scale bounds, keyframe thresholds) transfer cleanly across LiDAR models with different angular resolutions.
