## Problem

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) train visual understanding and generation with fundamentally divergent supervisory signals—sparse text labels versus dense pixel reconstruction—leaving the two capabilities in misaligned representation spaces that fail to mutually reinforce. While recent work has attempted to bridge this gap via pixel-space reconstruction, it remains unclear whether low-level visual fidelity is the optimal proxy for coupling understanding and generation, or whether higher-level semantic objectives would be more effective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260642/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the SGT generative tuning phase, the model consumes an RGB image alongside a concise natural-language instruction (e.g., 'segment the objects in the image') and produces a segmentation mask as the generative target, with all visual data sourced exclusively from the SAM dataset to avoid evaluation overlap. In the full post-training regime, the model additionally processes 500k VQA samples from LLaVA-OneVision at a 1:2 VQA-to-segmentation intra-batch ratio. The dual-encoder architecture extracts semantic ViT tokens (L×D) for reasoning and VAE latents (H×W×C) for structural detail, with the generative process initialized from Gaussian noise z_noise.

## Method

SGT formulates image segmentation as a conditional generative task y = f_θ(x, [z_vit, z_noise]) within the UMM's existing architecture, using the segmentation mask as the training target rather than raw pixels. The key empirical finding, established through a systematic hierarchical taxonomy of visual proxy tasks (low-level: edge detection; mid-level: depth estimation, inpainting; high-level: segmentation, object detection), is that high-level semantic tasks—particularly segmentation—yield substantially greater gains in vision-centric perception and spatial reasoning than low-level reconstruction, because they align with the structural semantics that understanding requires rather than distracting the model with texture memorization. Mechanistic analyses via t-SNE visualization and cross-attention probing reveal that SGT improves intra-class compactness and inter-class separability of visual embeddings, shifts attention toward visual tokens in deeper transformer layers (mitigating linguistic over-reliance), and amplifies attention on critical attribute tokens (position, color, object identity) during generation. The paradigm is validated across two architecturally distinct UMMs—BAGEL (Mixture-of-Transformers with interleaved training) and OmniGen2 (frozen VLM + diffusion module)—achieving a 6.02% improvement over BAGEL on CV-Bench and 90.0% on GenEval.

## Results

SGT-BAGEL (7B+7B) achieves 90.0% on GenEval and 79.23 on CV-Bench, representing a 6.02% absolute improvement over the BAGEL baseline (73.21) and surpassing all other large-scale UMMs on most understanding benchmarks. On MMVP, VSR, HallusionBench, MMStar, RWQA, and MathVista, SGT-BAGEL scores 83.33, 81.54, 70.24, 68.33, 72.42, and 73.90 respectively, consistently outperforming UniWorld-v1, Janus-Pro, and Chameleon. SGT-Gen2 (3B+4B) improves over OmniGen2 on every metric, reaching 78.9 on GenEval and 68.33 on MMVP. In the ablation (Table 3), SGT yields the largest CV-Bench gain (+6.02 for BAGEL, +0.97 for OmniGen2) compared to pixel reconstruction (+2.02, +0.77) and edge detection (+1.35, +0.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGT-BAGEL outperforms the BAGEL baseline on every reported metric, with the largest gains on HallusionBench (+1.90), VSR (+1.09), and GenEval (+2.0 points to 90.0). Against UniWorld-v1, SGT-BAGEL wins on MMVP (+5.66), HallusionBench (+1.89), MMStar (+4.43), RWQA (+4.84), MathVista (+5.70), GenEval (+6.0), and GEdit-Bench-En (+2.09), but trails on VSR (81.54 vs. 83.34). SGT-BAGEL dominates Janus-Pro and Chameleon across all shared benchmarks by wide margins (e.g., +20.33 on MMVP over Chameleon). In the small-scale regime, SGT-Gen2 improves over OmniGen2 on all eight metrics (e.g., +3.33 on MMVP, +2.3 on GenEval), while ReCA-Harmon [80]—the closest pixel-reconstruction competitor—achieves 90.0 on GenEval but scores only 47.00 on MMVP and 36.70 on HallusionBench, confirming that pixel-level reconstruction sacrifices understanding for generation. The ablation in Table 3 further shows SGT outperforms both pixel reconstruction and edge detection proxies on CV-Bench (79.23 vs. 75.23 vs. 74.56 for BAGEL), validating segmentation as the optimal generative proxy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on using generative objectives to improve unified multimodal models (UMMs), building on architectures like BAGEL and OmniGen2 that already unify understanding and generation in a single framework. It departs fundamentally from prior generative-tuning approaches (ReCA, GenHancer, DIVA) that rely on pixel-space reconstruction as the auxiliary visual objective, arguing that low-level reconstruction introduces representational noise irrelevant to semantic understanding. By drawing on the broader literature on generative representation learning (SODA, DDAE, I-JEPA's semantic abstraction philosophy), the paper carves new ground as the first systematic investigation into which *level* of visual task—low, mid, or high—serves as the optimal generative proxy for coupling understanding and generation within UMMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that image segmentation outperforms pixel reconstruction as a generative alignment proxy provides a clear, actionable recipe for UMM practitioners: swap pixel-level reconstruction for segmentation masks in the training loop. The mechanistic analyses (improved feature linear separability, reduced linguistic over-reliance in attention) offer a principled explanation for *why* semantic proxies work, strengthening confidence beyond empirical correlation. However, the paper acknowledges that SGT functions best as a foundational alignment strategy rather than a standalone solution—it does not improve knowledge-intensive or mathematical reasoning tasks, and the optimal tripartite mixing of understanding, generation, and SGT data remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the semantic-proxy principle generalizes to video, 3D, or other modalities, and how SGT interacts with reinforcement learning-based post-training.
