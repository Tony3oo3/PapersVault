## Problem

Optimization-based visual jailbreaking attacks on multimodal large language models (MLLMs) achieve near-perfect success on the source model but exhibit extremely limited cross-model transferability, failing to reliably exploit vulnerabilities in closed-source commercial MLLMs. The paper identifies the root cause: these attacks reside in high-sharpness loss landscape regions due to over-reliance on model-specific early-layer features and semantically poor high-frequency spectral components, making them highly sensitive to even minor parameter shifts during transfer.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (initialized as a blank grey image of RGB(128,128,128) or a panda image) paired with a malicious text prompt, and produces an adversarial perturbation δ bounded by 32/255 that, when added to the image, elicits harmful responses from the target MLLM. The perturbation is optimized on a source model (LLaVA-v1.5-7B) using a target affirmative string ("Sure, here is") and then transferred zero-shot or via multi-query (100 samples) to black-box target models. The perturbation budget and step size (2/255) follow standard PGD constraints, and the output is evaluated by combining substring matching with LLM-based harm judgment (HarmBench).

## Method

FORCE augments standard PGD with two corrective mechanisms that eliminate non-generalizable feature reliance. First, a layer-aware regularization samples N reference points in the perturbation's neighborhood, computes per-softmax L2 distances at each intermediate layer, and maximizes these distances weighted by a quadratically decreasing schedule (stronger penalties on early layers) to encourage broader feasible regions in shallow feature spaces. Second, a spectral rescaling step applies FFT to the perturbation, divides the magnitude spectrum into M equal-width frequency bands, measures each band's influence via masking, and downscales high-frequency components whenever their loss contribution exceeds a β-scaled threshold relative to the adjacent low-frequency band, restoring a natural frequency distribution. These two operations are integrated into each PGD iteration—frequency rescaling is applied first, followed by the layer-space exploration—guiding the attack toward flatter loss landscapes without requiring access to target model parameters.

## Results

On adapter-based MLLMs (LLaVA-v1.6, InstructBLIP, Idefics3), FORCE improves average ASR by 12.3% over PGD while reducing average query cost by over 15% (e.g., 69.0% vs 61.0% ASR on LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B with MaliciousInstruct). On early-fusion MLLMs (Llama-3.2-Vision, Qwen2.5-VL), where PGD achieves near-zero ASR (1–5%), FORCE nearly doubles or triples the attack success rate (e.g., 11.0% vs 5.0% on Qwen2.5-VL with MaliciousInstruct). Against commercial MLLMs (Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5), FORCE delivers an average 70% relative ASR improvement, with the largest gain being 200% on GPT-5 (HADES: 3.0% vs 1.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard PGD (step size 2/255, budget 32/255, target 'Sure, here is'), and FORCE outperforms it across every target model and dataset combination. On adapter-based MLLMs, FORCE consistently raises ASR (e.g., 69.0% vs 61.0% on LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B/MaliciousInstruct; 92.0% vs 84.0% on InstructBLIP) while reducing the number of queries needed for a successful jailbreak (e.g., 39.73 vs 44.95 on LLaVA; 12.80 vs 20.75 on InstructBLIP). On early-fusion MLLMs, where PGD is nearly ineffective (ASR ≤ 5%), FORCE more than doubles the success rate (e.g., 11.0% vs 5.0% on Qwen2.5-VL; 10.26% vs 6.27% on Llama-3.2-Vision for HADES). Against commercial MLLMs, FORCE achieves the largest relative gains—200% on GPT-5 (HADES) and 100% on both Claude-Sonnet-4 and GPT-5 (MaliciousInstruct)—though absolute ASRs remain low due to the strength of commercial safety alignment. The ablation (Table 3) confirms both components contribute: layer-feature regularisation alone adds 3.8% ASR, spectral rescaling alone adds 11.3%, and their combination yields 20.6% on Idefics3-8B-Llama3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PGD optimization framework (Madry et al., ICLR 2018) as the base attack loop, extending the line of optimisation-based visual jailbreaking attacks (Qi et al. 2024, Niu et al. 2024, Zhao et al. 2023) that demonstrated reliable manipulation of open-source MLLMs but poor cross-model transfer. The key departure is a diagnostic analysis inspired by loss-landscape and sharpness-aware minimization literature (Wei et al. 2023), which identifies two specific sources of non-generalizable reliance—early-layer feature over-dependence and high-frequency spectral over-emphasis—that prior visual attack work had not isolated. The proposed FORCE method introduces a layer-aware L2-distance regularisation and a frequency-band rescaling step, both of which are novel additions to the visual jailbreaking optimisation objective, positioning the work between the empirical transferability failures documented by Schaeffer et al. (2025) and the textual jailbreaking transferability literature (Lin et al. 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanistic diagnosis—that gradient-based visual attacks progressively shift reliance from semantically rich low-frequency content to model-specific high-frequency patterns, and that early layers impose the tightest feasible regions—provides actionable insight for both attack design and potential defence (e.g., spectral filtering or early-layer robustness training). However, the practical impact remains limited: absolute ASR on commercial MLLMs (GPT-5, Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini-2.5-Pro) stays below 11%, meaning the attacks still pose a marginal rather than systemic threat. Open questions include whether the spectral and layer corrections generalise to next-generation early-fusion architectures with fundamentally different tokenisation, whether the identified frequency-band vulnerabilities can be exploited by defenders to build targeted filters, and whether the modest computational overhead (26% more time per iteration) scales acceptably for large-scale red-teaming campaigns.
