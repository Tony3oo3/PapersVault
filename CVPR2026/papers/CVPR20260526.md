## Problem

Unified multimodal models require a single visual tokenizer that simultaneously produces continuous semantic features for understanding and discrete fine-grained tokens for generation and reconstruction. Existing approaches rely on dual-encoder architectures or contrastive-loss balancing, which increase model complexity, hinder deeper representation interaction, and demand impractically large batch sizes. No prior work has demonstrated a single convolution-free tokenizer that yields both representation types with competitive performance across all three tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260526/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

VQRAE takes a single RGB image (e.g., 256×256 or 512×512) and produces two distinct outputs from the same encoder: continuous latent features Z_I ∈ R^{(hw/p²)×d} used directly for multimodal understanding without quantization, and discrete VQ tokens Z_q ∈ R^{(hw/p²)×e} (e=1536, codebook size 4k–16k) used for autoregressive generation and pixel-space reconstruction via a symmetric ViT decoder. The reconstruction preserves input resolution (patch size p is matched in encoder and decoder), and the discrete tokens are compatible with standard next-token-prediction LLM backbones such as Qwen3.

## Method

VQRAE replaces the VAE in RAE with a vector-quantized semantic codebook while retaining pretrained VFMs (SigLIP2, InternViT) as a unified ViT encoder and a symmetric ViT decoder, eliminating all convolutional blocks. A key empirical finding is that quantizing ViT-based semantic features requires a high-dimensional codebook (1536-d) to avoid collapse and achieve 100% utilization, in direct contrast to the low-dimensional (8–256) codebooks used in CNN-based VQGAN/VQVAE. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 freezes the VFM encoder and jointly optimizes the SimVQ codebook and ViT decoder with L2 + LPIPS + adversarial reconstruction loss plus VQ commitment loss; Stage 2 unfreezes the encoder and adds a self-distillation loss (L2 distance to a frozen teacher copy) on the continuous features to preserve semantic understanding while the encoder adapts for finer reconstruction. This design allows the tokenizer to be dropped into existing MLLMs without additional alignment or instruction-tuning, since the continuous features remain semantically aligned with the original VFM.

## Results

On multimodal understanding (Table 3), VQRAE with InternViT-300M and Qwen2.5-7B achieves MME-P 1746.8, SEED 77.0, TQA 80.6, and MMB 85.1, matching or exceeding the InternVL3 baseline (MME-P 1748.4, SEED 77.1) without any tokenizer-specific fine-tuning. On reconstruction (Table 2), VQRAE-InternViT achieves rFID 1.39, PSNR 22.88, and SSIM 0.784 at 14× downsampling, surpassing dual-encoder methods like TokenFlow (rFID 1.37, PSNR 21.41) and MUSE-VL (rFID 2.26). On visual generation (Table 4), the 0.6B VQRAE model achieves GenEval Overall 0.76 and DPG-Bench Overall 86.67, outperforming Janus-Pro (1B; 0.73 / 82.63) and TokenFlow (13B; 0.55 / 73.38).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQRAE with InternViT-300M nearly matches the InternVL3 baseline (MME-P 1746.8 vs. 1748.4; SEED 77.0 vs. 77.1) while adding reconstruction and generation capability, confirming that the two-stage training with self-distillation preserves semantic understanding. Against dual-encoder unified tokenizers, VQRAE (SigLIP2, Vicuna-13B, 512px) outperforms TokenFlow-L on every reported metric (MME-P 1543.3 vs. 1365.4; TQA 61.7 vs. 54.1; SEED 69.9 vs. 62.6) and also surpasses TokenFlow-XL on MME-P (1543.3 vs. 1545.9 is a near-tie) while using a simpler single-encoder architecture. On reconstruction, VQRAE-InternViT (rFID 1.39, PSNR 22.88, SSIM 0.784) beats TokenFlow (rFID 1.37, PSNR 21.41, SSIM 0.690) and MUSE-VL (rFID 2.26, PSNR 20.14, SSIM 0.646) on PSNR and SSIM, and is competitive on rFID. For visual generation, the 0.6B VQRAE model achieves GenEval Overall 0.76 and DPG-Bench 86.67, surpassing Janus-Pro (1B; 0.73 / 82.63), SimpleAR (1.5B; 0.63 / 81.97), and TokenFlow (13B; 0.55 / 73.38), demonstrating that the high-dimensional semantic codebook benefits autoregressive generation even at small model scale. VQRAE also outperforms Tar (which distills directly onto discrete tokens) on MME-P under the Qwen2.5-7B setting (1746.8 vs. 1571.0), attributing the gap to quantization errors in Tar's approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

VQRAE extends the Representation Autoencoder (RAE) framework by introducing vector quantization, enabling discrete token output alongside continuous semantic features from a single pretrained VFM encoder. It departs from the dominant dual-encoder paradigm (Janus, TokenFlow, MUSE-VL) that maintains separate semantic and pixel encoders, and from traditional VQ tokenizers (VQGAN, VQ-VAE) that operate on low-dimensional CNN features. The key conceptual departure is the finding that quantizing ViT-based semantic features requires high-dimensional codebooks (≥1536) for stable training and 100% utilization, directly contradicting the established practice of low-dimensional codebooks (8–256) in CNN-based VQ reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for separate pixel encoders and convolutional blocks, VQRAE simplifies unified multimodal architectures and enables plug-and-play integration into existing MLLMs without additional alignment training. The high-dimensional semantic codebook finding opens a new design space for VQ tokenizers built on foundation models, potentially benefiting any autoregressive generation pipeline that relies on discrete visual tokens. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or higher-resolution generation, whether the discrete tokens match continuous representations in all generative settings, and how the method performs when the VFM encoder is substantially smaller or from a different architecture family.
