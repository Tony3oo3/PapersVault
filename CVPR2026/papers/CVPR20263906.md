## Problem

Existing human-scene reconstruction methods from monocular video either model humans and scenes independently or rely on environment maps that assume infinitely distant, uniform lighting. This overlooks the critical interplay of spatially-varying illumination and dynamic cast shadows between a moving human and its surrounding scene, resulting in inconsistent human appearance and degraded scene realism. The paper addresses this by jointly inferring geometry, material, and spatially-varying illumination within a unified photometrically consistent framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263906/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video as input and produces two separate 3D Gaussian Splatting representations: an animatable human (Gaussians anchored to a refined SMPL mesh in canonical space, warped to posed space via LBS) and a static scene (Gaussians in world space), plus a light volume grid encoding localized illumination. Beyond standard novel-view and novel-pose rendering, the outputs support human relighting under arbitrary environment maps and compositing the human into a different scene with contextually appropriate lighting, as well as depth map rendering.

## Method

The framework has three core components. First, a light volume—a 3D grid of probes each storing spherical harmonic coefficients for radiance and a learnable latent feature—replaces the conventional environment map to capture spatially-varying illumination; incoming radiance for each human Gaussian is obtained by interpolating over k-nearest probes via Fibonacci-sampled Monte Carlo integration within a Disney BRDF PBR pipeline. Second, a two-stage human reconstruction strategy first optimizes SMPL mesh geometry (vertex offsets and colors via hash encoders) and then anchors Gaussians to the refined surface to estimate PBR material attributes (albedo, roughness, metallic) along with a pose-aware visibility estimator to resolve self-occlusion ambiguity. Third, an implicit shadow estimation module queries lighting features from the light volume for scene Gaussians within the human's bounding box, concatenates them with spatial descriptors (distance and orientation relative to the human center), and passes them through a small decoder to predict a per-Gaussian occlusion factor that modulates scene spherical harmonics, avoiding costly full-scene ray tracing.

## Results

On the NeuMan dataset (6 sequences), the method achieves scene-level PSNR of 26.1–29.9 dB, SSIM of 0.862–0.934, and LPIPS of 0.046–0.120, outperforming the strongest baseline HUGS by approximately 2–3 dB in PSNR across all sequences. For human-region reconstruction, PSNR reaches 20.5–22.9 dB, again surpassing HUGS (17.5–19.5 dB) by a clear margin. On ZJU-MoCap (single-view novel view synthesis), the method attains PSNR 30.73, SSIM 0.9705, and LPIPS 0.0284, edging out HUGS (30.56 / 0.9703 / 0.0309) and HumanNeRF (30.53 / 0.9695 / 0.0313).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against HUGS (the strongest 3DGS-based baseline), the gains are most pronounced on the Bike sequence (+3.57 dB PSNR scene-level, +3.11 dB human-region) and Seattle (+3.63 dB scene-level), while on ZJU-MoCap the margin is smaller (+0.17 dB PSNR) due to the controlled indoor setting. NeuMan (NeRF-based) trails by 2–4 dB in PSNR and shows notably higher LPIPS (0.15–0.27 vs. 0.05–0.12), reflecting its inability to model spatially-varying illumination. Vid2Avatar and 4DGS are substantially weaker, with Vid2Avatar's human-centered coordinate system causing global 3D inconsistency and 4DGS lacking explicit human modeling. On ZJU-MoCap, the method also surpasses HumanNeRF (+0.20 dB) and IntrinsicAvatar (+2.30 dB), with the latter's over-smoothed geometry and albedo inconsistencies being the primary sources of its lower scores. For human relighting (qualitative, Fig. 5), the method produces more faithful albedo and geometry than R4D, IntrinsicAvatar, and IRAGA, which suffer from limited material disentanglement, geometric over-smoothing, and regional albedo artifacts respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based human-scene reconstruction line (HUGS, NeuMan, Vid2Avatar) by introducing a physically-based rendering pipeline with a novel 'light volume' representation that replaces the standard environment-map assumption with spatially-varying spherical-harmonic probes. It builds directly on iHuman's mesh-anchored Gaussian placement, GauHuman's KL-divergence densification, and Relightable 3D Gaussians' Disney BRDF formulation, but departs from prior relightable-avatar work by rejecting the infinitely-distant-light assumption in favor of localized illumination. The implicit shadow decoder, which modulates scene Gaussian SH coefficients using learned lighting features and spatial descriptors, is a new mechanism that avoids the computationally prohibitive full-scene ray tracing used in static inverse-rendering methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is framed as the first exploratory study of illumination-consistent joint human-scene reconstruction from in-the-wild monocular video, filling a gap where prior methods either ignored lighting/shadow interplay or relied on environment maps that cannot capture spatially-varying illumination. The light volume and implicit shadow estimation are practical, lightweight alternatives to ray tracing that could be adopted in broader 3DGS pipelines. However, the method remains per-scene optimization (no feedforward generalization), assumes a single static scene with one dynamic human, and the quantitative gains over HUGS, while consistent, are moderate (~2–4 dB PSNR), leaving open questions about scalability to multi-person dynamics, non-static backgrounds, and real-time deployment.
