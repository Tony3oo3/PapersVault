## Problem

Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), a foundational weight-regularization method for continual learning, consistently underperforms due to a fundamental flaw in its importance estimation: when the model is confident in correct predictions, the Fisher Information Matrix values vanish, causing critical weights to be under-protected. The paper further shows that the popular variant MAS, while avoiding gradient vanishing, introduces 'redundant protection' by over-constraining parameters irrelevant to prior tasks. No prior work had identified or addressed these specific misalignments in gradient-based importance estimation.

## Input / Output

For exemplar-free class-incremental learning, the method consumes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 224×224 ImageNet-Subset, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet) sequentially across non-overlapping class sets and outputs class labels, with no exemplar storage required. For multimodal continual instruction tuning, it takes image–text instruction pairs and produces text answers, using a pretrained ViLT backbone. In both settings, the importance matrix is computed once per task from the trained model on that task's training data, then frozen as a diagonal regularization term for all subsequent tasks.

## Method

The core contribution is a Logits Reversal (LR) operation applied during FIM computation: each logit z_k is negated (z̃_k = −z_k) before computing the softmax and cross-entropy gradient, so that high-confidence correct predictions (where p_c ≈ 1 originally) now yield large gradients (1 − p̃_c is large because p̃_c is small), directly preventing the gradient vanishing that causes EWC to underestimate important weights. The regularization loss itself (Eq. 1) remains unchanged; only the importance scores Ω are computed with the reversed logits. This single modification simultaneously eliminates EWC's under-protection and avoids MAS's redundant protection, because the reversed softmax keeps gradients concentrated on the ground-truth class while suppressing contributions from irrelevant classes. The resulting method, EWC-DR, is a drop-in replacement for vanilla EWC's importance computation and is evaluated across EFCIL (ResNet-18, PyCIL framework) and MCIT (ViLT, CLiMB benchmark) settings.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (big-start, T=5), EWC-DR achieves 50.23% A_last and 63.75% A_avg, outperforming the strongest baseline MAS (35.37% / 48.32%) by 14.86 and 15.43 points respectively. On ImageNet-Subset (big-start, T=5), EWC-DR reaches 66.18% A_last and 76.00% A_avg, a 53.18% relative improvement over vanilla EWC (11.44% A_last). In the MCIT setting, EWC-DR reduces the forgetting transfer on NLVR2 from 90.66% (EWC) to 27.48% after the final task, while maintaining nearly identical new-task accuracy (59.38% vs 59.30% on VCR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EWC-DR consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and protocol tested. Against vanilla EWC, the improvement is the most dramatic: up to 53.18% relative gain in A_last and 55.47% in A_avg on CIFAR-100, attributed to EWC's gradient vanishing problem that underestimates importance for correctly-predicted classes. Online EWC (which accumulates importance with a decay factor) is the second-weakest baseline in the equally-split setting, still trailing EWC-DR by 10–20 points in A_last. Synaptic Intelligence (SI) performs comparably to or slightly worse than vanilla EWC, confirming that its online loss-reduction tracking does not resolve the fundamental importance misalignment. MAS is the strongest baseline (avoiding gradient vanishing by using the ℓ₂-norm of logits), but EWC-DR still surpasses it by 10–15 points in A_last on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-Subset, because MAS's redundant protection over-constrains parameters associated with large negative logits that have negligible impact on output probabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC, Kirkpatrick et al. 2017), proposing a modification to its Fisher Information Matrix computation. It also provides a unified gradient-based analysis of EWC variants—Online EWC, Synaptic Intelligence, and Memory Aware Synapses—identifying two distinct failure modes: gradient vanishing in EWC (where the most important weights for correct predictions receive the lowest importance scores) and redundant protection in MAS (where irrelevant parameters with large negative logits are over-constrained). The Logits Reversal operation is positioned as a minimal, targeted fix to EWC's FIM estimation rather than a new regularization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EWC is a foundational baseline in continual learning research, and the finding that its FIM systematically underestimates the importance of the very weights most critical for correct classification is a significant diagnostic insight that could affect how EWC is used as a comparison point in many CL studies. The fix (negating logits during FIM computation) is trivially simple and broadly adoptable. However, the evaluation is confined to the weight-regularization family; comparisons against the strongest exemplar-free CIL methods (e.g., FE-CAM, FETRIL) are deferred to supplementary material, leaving open whether EWC-DR can compete with state-of-the-art EFCIL approaches. The analysis also focuses on FC-layer weights, and whether the gradient vanishing diagnosis and LR fix generalize to convolutional or transformer layers remains unaddressed.
