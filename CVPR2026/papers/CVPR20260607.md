## Problem

Dynamic 3D black-hole emission tomography is severely ill-posed: only sparse radio visibilities from a single viewpoint are available, the source evolves over time, and the forward model depends on unknown fluid dynamics. The prior method BH-NeRF imposed strict Keplerian velocity as a hard constraint and could only model initial emissivity, failing near the black hole where infall, turbulence, and new emission events dominate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260607/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes complex EHT visibilities (sparse 2D Fourier samples from VLBI baselines) across a time window, along with assumed AART velocity-model parameters (sub-Keplerianity ξ, radial/angular infall fractions β_r, β_φ, black-hole spin a, and orbit direction). It produces two coordinate-based neural fields: a 4D emissivity e(t, x) parameterized by an MLP over [t, x] with positional encoding, and a 3D velocity ũ^i(x) in the normal observer frame parameterized by a separate MLP over x. The forward model applies general-relativistic radiative transfer (geodesic ray-tracing with a redshift factor g² derived from the estimated four-velocity) to convert the 3D emissivity into image-plane intensities, which are then compared to the measured visibilities.

## Method

PI-DEF jointly optimizes two coordinate-based neural networks (emissivity and velocity) under a three-term loss: a data-fit term that ray-traces the emissivity through the GRRT forward model and matches EHT visibilities, a dynamics term that propagates the emissivity forward by Δt via an ODE solver using the velocity network and enforces agreement with the emissivity network at t+Δt (a soft velocity constraint), and a velocity regularization term that pulls the estimated velocity toward an AART analytical prior. The key distinction from BH-NeRF is that velocity is a soft constraint whose regularization weight decays exponentially to near zero during training, allowing the network to deviate from the assumed model where the data demand it. The dynamics loss uses a Gaussian-blurred L1 comparison to prevent the network from producing blurry reconstructions that trivially satisfy the propagation constraint. Geodesics are precomputed with kgeo, and the emissivity is evaluated on those geodesic points for the data loss while the dynamics loss is evaluated on a 64³ spatial grid.

## Results

On a test set of five simulated 4D emissivity fields, PI-DEF achieves 37.3±2.3 dB PSNR and 2.3±0.2×10⁻⁵ MSE, outperforming the physics-agnostic 4D-MLP baseline (35.4±0.5 dB, 3.8±0.4×10⁻⁵) and the previous BH-NeRF method (34.0±1.9 dB, 4.9±0.8×10⁻⁵). Notably, these gains are achieved even when PI-DEF uses a deliberately mismatched velocity prior (fully sub-Keplerian, ξ=1, β=1) versus the true AART model (ξ=0.7, β=0.9). In the region of high emissivity density (above the 65th percentile), the recovered radial and azimuthal velocities closely match ground truth despite the incorrect prior, and a proof-of-concept experiment shows the data-fit loss is sensitive to the assumed black hole spin (correct spin a=0.2 yields the best fit).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PI-DEF outperforms both baselines on every reported metric. Against BH-NeRF [27], the prior state-of-the-art for black-hole emission tomography, PI-DEF improves PSNR by 3.3 dB and reduces MSE by more than half (2.3 vs. 4.9 ×10⁻⁵), a gap that persists even though PI-DEF is given a deliberately wrong velocity prior (pure sub-Keplerian) while BH-NeRF enforces its Keplerian assumption as a hard constraint. Against the physics-agnostic 4D-MLP baseline, which simply fits a 4D neural field to the data without any dynamics or velocity modeling, PI-DEF gains 1.9 dB in PSNR and reduces MSE by 40% (2.3 vs. 3.8 ×10⁻⁵), demonstrating that the soft physics-informed dynamics loss provides meaningful regularization beyond what the data alone can constrain. The paper also shows that PI-DEF recovers the true velocity field in regions of sufficient emissivity signal, something neither baseline can do since BH-NeRF has no learned velocity and 4D-MLP has no velocity network at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends BH-NeRF (Levis et al., CVPR 2022), which represented the initial 3D emissivity as a coordinate-based neural field propagated under strict Keplerian dynamics. PI-DEF departs from BH-NeRF in two key ways: it replaces the static initial emissivity with a fully time-dependent 4D emissivity field (capturing new emission events), and it replaces the hard Keplerian constraint with a jointly-learned 3D velocity field regularized by a soft physics prior (the AART model). The broader methodological lineage draws on dynamic neural radiance fields (NeRFies, D-NeRF) for temporal modeling and physics-informed neural networks for incorporating domain constraints as soft losses rather than hard constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to perform dynamic 3D black-hole emission tomography without assuming Keplerian dynamics, which is critical because Keplerian motion breaks down near the event horizon where infall, turbulence, and sub-Keplerian speeds dominate. The joint recovery of velocity and emissivity opens a path to inferring physical parameters like black hole spin directly from EHT data, as demonstrated in a proof-of-concept experiment. Key limitations include validation only on simulated data, unreliable velocity recovery in low-emissivity regions (near the horizon where redshift suppresses signal), and dependence on an assumed velocity model even if softened. Open questions include whether the approach scales to real EHT data with atmospheric noise, whether GRMHD-simulated ground truth (rather than simple hotspot models) would stress-test the method, and whether the spin-inference capability can be made statistically rigorous.
