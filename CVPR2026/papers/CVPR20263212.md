## Problem

Existing VLN agents face two intertwined challenges: long-horizon memory construction (uniform sampling or fixed heuristics dilute contextual information density, impairing reasoning over extended trajectories) and compounding errors (minor early deviations accumulate, and most methods lack effective closed-loop correction once the agent goes off-course). Prior approaches either pause to reason (causing perceptual blindness) or stream all frames indiscriminately (causing context pollution), leaving a gap in robust, continuous perception with proactive error recovery.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a continuous stream of egocentric RGB frames and a single natural-language instruction. At inference, the model consumes the instruction, a refined memory bank of K=8 selected historical frames, and the most recent 4 visual frames, then autoregressively generates an action chunk of 4 consecutive discrete actions (MOVE_FORWARD, TURN_LEFT, TURN_RIGHT, STOP). No depth, odometry, or global map inputs are used; the entire pipeline operates on monocular RGB and text.

## Method

DecoVLN decouples observation, reasoning, and correction into parallel processes. The Adaptive Memory Refinement (AMR) module formulates long-term memory construction as an iterative selection problem: at each timestep, it picks the next frame from a candidate pool by maximizing a unified score that jointly rewards semantic relevance to the instruction (cosine similarity of VLM embeddings), penalizes visual redundancy (max cosine similarity to already-selected memory frames), and penalizes temporal clustering (inverse minimum time gap to stored frames), with a scalar λ_R trading off relevance against redundancy. For error correction, the agent computes geodesic distance from its current state to the nearest point on the expert trajectory; only states within a trusted region (τ=3 m) yield corrective state-action pairs from an expert policy (Habitat shortest-path follower), filtering out heavily deviated samples that would contaminate training. The corrective dataset (~180K pairs) is combined with LLaVA-Video-178K to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the VLM's general multimodal reasoning during fine-tuning. The base model is LLaVA-Video-7B (SigLIP vision tower + Qwen2-7B language model), trained with imitation learning followed by this targeted corrective fine-tuning stage.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, DecoVLN achieves 56.3% SR, 50.5% SPL, 63.5% OS, and 5.01m NE using only egocentric RGB input. On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, it reaches 54.2% SR, 46.3% SPL, and 63.5% nDTW. These results surpass the closest RGB-only competitor StreamVLN by +3.5% SR and +3.3% SPL on R2R, and by +5.6% SR and +3.8% SPL on RxR, while also outperforming multi-sensor waypoint models (e.g., ETPNav, HAMT+ScaleVLN) that rely on panoramic images, depth, and odometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecoVLN outperforms all 19 baselines on both R2R-CE and RxR-CE Val-Unseen using only egocentric RGB, a strict subset of the multi-sensor inputs (panoramic, depth, odometry) used by waypoint models like ETPNav (SR 57.0, SPL 49.0) and HAMT+ScaleVLN (SR 55.0, SPL 51.0). Against the closest RGB-only competitor StreamVLN, DecoVLN gains +3.5% SR and +3.3% SPL on R2R, and +5.6% SR and +3.8% SPL on RxR. Compared to NaVILA (which uses additional ScaleVLN pretraining data), DecoVLN improves SR by +2.3% on R2R and +4.9% on RxR without any large-scale pretraining. Against Uni-NaVid, the gains are even larger: +9.3% SR and +7.8% SPL on R2R. Notably, DecoVLN also surpasses ETPNav on SPL (50.5 vs 49.0) despite ETPNav having a lower NE (4.71 vs 5.01), indicating that DecoVLN's paths are more instruction-aligned even if slightly longer in absolute distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DecoVLN extends the VLM-based VLN paradigm established by NaVid, NaVILA, and StreamVLN, which fine-tune video-language models for end-to-end action prediction from egocentric RGB. It departs from prior work in two conceptual directions: (1) replacing uniform or heuristic frame sampling with an optimization-based adaptive memory refinement that jointly scores semantic relevance, visual diversity, and temporal coverage, and (2) shifting error correction from episode-level DAgger to step-level state-action pair collection gated by geodesic distance to a trusted region. The framework is built on LLaVA-Video-7B (SigLIP + Qwen2-7B), aligning with the current VLM backbone trend, but the decoupling of continuous observation from discrete reasoning is a structural departure from the stop-and-think and full-history-streaming paradigms it critiques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent bottlenecks in VLN—context dilution over long horizons and compounding errors—through complementary mechanisms, and validates the approach with a real-world quadruped deployment (Unitree GO2) that required no real-world fine-tuning. The finding that a carefully curated 8-frame memory outperforms longer uniform histories, and that step-level geodesic-gated correction beats DAgger at equal data budget, offers practical design guidance for embodied agents. Open questions include whether the geodesic-distance requirement for corrective fine-tuning limits applicability to environments lacking ground-truth expert trajectories, whether the approach scales to substantially longer or multi-goal navigation, and whether the memory refinement objective can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on hand-tuned hyperparameters (λ_R, w_V, w_T).
