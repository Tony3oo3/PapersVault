## Problem

Early fusion in collaborative perception transmits raw point clouds, preserving full scene information and remaining robust to model heterogeneity, but its high communication cost has pushed most prior work toward intermediate or late fusion. The paper tackles how to retain early fusion's information-preservation and model-agnostic advantages while substantially reducing bandwidth, by selectively transmitting sparse points and reconstructing the missing scene structure at the receiver.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260805/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The ego agent receives its own full LiDAR point cloud plus sparse point clouds from each neighbor, where foreground points are subsampled via Farthest Point Sampling (ratio R^fg) and background points via Random Point Sampling (ratio R^bg), yielding a transmitted message far smaller than the full cloud. All point clouds are pillarized into a 0.4 m × 0.4 m BEV grid with 64 channels; the completion module reconstructs dense pillars from the sparse neighbor pillars before fusion. The final output is standard 3D object detections (classification, 3D box regression, and heading direction) evaluated at IoU thresholds of 0.3, 0.5, and 0.7.

## Method

CoLC combines three components. (1) Foreground-Aware Point Sampling (FAPS) uses a pre-trained MLP saliency selector to partition each neighbor's point cloud into foreground and background sets, then applies FPS to foreground (preserving object structure) and RPS to background (efficient contextual sampling), producing a compact transmitted message. (2) Completion-Enhanced Early Fusion (CEEF) pillarizes the sparse neighbor points, passes them through a VQ-based completion module (Swin Transformer encoder → learnable codebook quantization → convolutional dense decoder with occupancy prediction), and adaptively fuses the completed pillars into the sparse early-fusion result only at empty pillar locations via a learned spatial correlation weight. (3) Dense-Guided Dual Alignment (DGDA) adds two auxiliary losses during training—KL divergence on channel-wise softmax distributions for semantic alignment and marginal cosine similarity for geometric direction alignment—between the enhanced early-fusion pillars and their dense counterparts. Training is two-stage: the completion module is first pre-trained with reconstruction and VQ losses, then frozen while the full detection pipeline is trained end-to-end with the standard detection loss plus the two alignment terms.

## Results

On V2XSim, OPV2V, and V2XSet, CoLC (100% LiDAR) achieves the best AP@0.7 among all methods: 87.89, 92.93, and 89.81 respectively, surpassing the Early Fusion baseline (83.61, 90.69, 88.00) and all intermediate/late fusion methods. On DAIR-V2X, CoLC attains 62.17 AP@0.7 (slightly below Early Fusion's 63.83) but achieves higher AP@0.5 (76.71 vs. 76.51), indicating stronger robustness under relaxed IoU thresholds in real-world settings. With only 50% of the LiDAR transmitted (CoLC*), performance remains competitive, approaching or even surpassing the full early-fusion baseline on most datasets. Inference latency is 75.86 ms, comparable to Where2comm (69.7 ms) and CoSDH (70.9 ms), and significantly faster than ERMVP (100.5 ms) and V2X-ViT (197.7 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLC outperforms the Early Fusion baseline on AP@0.7 across V2XSim (+4.28), OPV2V (+2.24), and V2XSet (+1.81), while slightly trailing on DAIR-V2X (−1.66) but leading on AP@0.5 (+0.20). Among intermediate fusion methods, CoLC surpasses the strongest competitor ERMVP by +3.13 AP@0.7 on V2XSim and +7.90 on V2XSet, and outperforms MRCNet by +23.40 on V2XSim AP@0.7. Where2comm, the best-performing intermediate method on OPV2V AP@0.7 (88.48), is exceeded by CoLC by +4.45. Under 50% bandwidth (CoLC*), performance drops by only 1.5–2.6 AP@0.7 yet still exceeds or matches Early Fusion on OPV2V and V2XSet. In heterogeneous model settings, CoLC remains robust while intermediate fusion methods (e.g., CoBEVT, CoSDH) degrade significantly, sometimes falling below the no-fusion baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLC positions itself against the dominant intermediate/late fusion paradigm in collaborative perception (Where2comm, CoBEVT, ERMVP, CoSDH), arguing that early fusion's information preservation and model-agnostic robustness have been underexplored solely due to communication cost. It repurposes vector quantization (VQ) from discrete representation learning [27] into a pillar-level LiDAR completion module, departing from generative completion approaches (VAEs, GANs, diffusion models) that are too heavy for real-time deployment, and from STAR's MAE-based masking which is designed for intermediate rather than early fusion. The Foreground-Aware Point Sampling strategy draws on Farthest Point Sampling for structural preservation but introduces a novel hybrid of FPS for foreground and random sampling for background, motivated by the empirical finding that both point types are essential in early fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoLC demonstrates that early fusion—long abandoned for its bandwidth cost—can be made practical through sparse transmission plus lightweight completion, offering a model-agnostic alternative that naturally handles heterogeneous agent backbones without domain adaptation. The finding that background points provide critical contextual cues (outperforming foreground-only transmission) challenges the foreground-centric bias in intermediate fusion literature. Open questions include whether the frozen completion module generalizes to unseen scene types, how the approach scales to larger agent counts or higher-resolution LiDAR, and whether the ICP-based pose alignment remains feasible under severe latency in production V2X deployments.
