## Problem

Existing late-interaction document retrievers suffer from three coupled limitations: single fixed views fragment spatially dispersed information, unidirectional contrastive learning overlooks textually similar but visually mismatched negatives, and static hard-negative curricula become uninformative as the model converges, capping peak discriminative performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261504/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The retriever takes a natural-language text query (augmented with five <unused0> special tokens following ColPali) and document page screenshots as input, with up to 1,024 visual tokens per image. During training, each image is additionally rendered as a horizontally stitched composite of its original, downsampled, and rotated versions, but this multi-view augmentation adds no inference cost. Output is a ranked list of document pages scored via MaxSim late-interaction over L2-normalized token embeddings projected to 128 dimensions.

## Method

Evo-Retriever combines three synergistic components on a Qwen2.5-VL backbone with ColBERT-style multi-vector late interaction. First, Multi-View Alignment (MVA) enforces scale and rotation invariance by jointly contrasting the original and a stitched augmented view against the same query under a softplus margin loss. Second, Bidirectional Contrastive Learning (BCL) adds a document-to-query path where a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) synthesizes hard negative queries that are textually similar but semantically inconsistent with the positive image, compelling the model beyond surface-level text matching. Third, an LLM-Guided Evolutionary Curriculum (LLM-EC) replaces static thresholds with a closed-loop controller (Qwen3-235B) that periodically reads structured training-state summaries and selects from 16 discrete difficulty intervals via a Three-Phase Decision Protocol (Exploration → Transition → Lock-in), enabling non-monotonic adjustments such as rolling back to easier negatives when instability is detected.

## Results

On ViDoRe V2, Evo-Retriever-7B achieves a new SOTA nDCG@5 of 65.2%, surpassing the previous best (llama-nemoretriever-3b-v1 at 63.5%) by +1.7 points, while the smaller Evo-Retriever-3B reaches 63.3%. On MMEB (VisDoc), Evo-Retriever-7B attains 77.12% nDCG@linear 5, outperforming the prior best gme-Qwen2-VL-7B (75.18%) by +1.94 points, and Evo-Retriever-3B scores 75.91%. The 3B model notably outperforms the architecturally similar colqwen2.5-v0.2 by +4.0 points on ViDoRe V2, demonstrating that the training strategy contributes substantially beyond backbone capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ViDoRe V2, Evo-Retriever-7B (65.2%) beats the previous SOTA llama-nemoretriever-3b-v1 (63.5%) by +1.7 points and colnomic-embed-multimodal-7b (60.8%) by +4.4 points; the 3B variant (63.3%) edges out llama-nemoretriever-1b-v1 (62.1%) by +1.2 points and surpasses jina-embeddings-v4 (57.6%) by +5.7 points. On MMEB (VisDoc), Evo-Retriever-7B (77.12%) outperforms gme-Qwen2-VL-7B (75.18%) by +1.94 points and Seed-1.6-embedding (73.44%) by +3.68 points, while Evo-Retriever-3B (75.91%) exceeds colpali-v1.3 (70.97%) by +4.94 points and VLM2Vec-V2.0-Qwen2VL-2B (65.36%) by +10.55 points. Notably, Evo-Retriever-3B also surpasses gme-Qwen2-VL-2B (72.71%) by +3.2 points on MMEB, indicating that the LLM-guided curriculum and bidirectional contrastive learning yield gains that are not simply a function of model scale. The paper attributes the consistent advantage over all static-curriculum baselines to the adaptive difficulty scheduling that keeps hard negatives challenging as the model evolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

Evo-Retriever extends the ColPali/ColBERT multi-vector late-interaction paradigm for visual document retrieval by introducing three training-time enhancements: multi-view image alignment for spatial robustness, bidirectional contrastive learning with VLM-synthesized hard negative queries (inspired by DocReRank), and an LLM-guided evolutionary curriculum that replaces the static hard-negative mining strategies used in NV-Retriever and Llama-Nemoretriever. The key conceptual departure is the 'Viewpoint-Pathway Collaboration' framing, which couples representation-level augmentation (MVA) with a new retrieval direction (D→Q) under a shared optimization objective, and then feeds the resulting training dynamics into an external LLM meta-controller for adaptive difficulty scheduling. Unlike prior work that treats curriculum design as a fixed hyperparameter schedule, Evo-Retriever positions curriculum as a co-evolving component of the model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LLM-guided evolutionary curriculum offers a generalizable training paradigm that could extend beyond document retrieval to other contrastive-learning settings where static hard-negative mining plateaus. The finding that a 32B controller matches a 235B controller suggests the protocol structure, not raw model scale, drives effective curriculum decisions, lowering the barrier to adoption. However, the method's complexity (multi-view augmentation, bidirectional loss, offline candidate pool construction, and periodic LLM queries) adds substantial engineering overhead, and the gains over strong static-curriculum baselines (e.g., +0.24% over a rule-based oracle in ablation) raise questions about cost-benefit in practice. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Qwen backbones, whether the LLM-EC can operate without a pre-computed candidate pool, and whether the bidirectional path remains beneficial at larger corpus scales.
