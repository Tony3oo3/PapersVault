## Problem

Existing cross-modal event-frame asymmetric stereo methods either unify both modalities into a common representation or derive shared embeddings from separate extractors, but both strategies marginalize discriminative domain-specific cues (e.g., color in frames, temporal dynamics in events) that are essential for robust stereo matching. The key challenge is learning expressive, high-fidelity cross-modal representations without information-lossy alignment that suppresses modality-specific features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263239/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a calibrated asymmetric stereo pair: one view provides an event stream (encoded as event concentration or voxel grid) and the other provides a synchronized RGB frame, at 640×480 resolution on DSEC. The frame is warped to the event camera's coordinate frame using provided calibration. The output is a dense disparity map at full resolution, produced through cascaded coarse-to-fine refinement from 1/16 to 1/4 scale with convex upsampling to full resolution.

## Method

Bi-CMPStereo alternately designates events and frames as the target (canonical) domain, instantiating two CMPStereo variants: evCMPStereo (events as target) and imgCMPStereo (frames as target). Each variant uses a Cross-Domain Embedding Adapter (CDEA) to map the source modality into the target's embedding space, and a Stereo Canonicalization Constraint (SCC) that enforces reconstruction of both modalities from a shared canonical latent space, preventing the encoders from collapsing into overly similar representations. The two pre-trained, frozen CMPStereo networks produce complementary multi-scale cost volumes that are concatenated at 1/16 and 1/8 scales and fused via a 3D hourglass at 1/4 scale, followed by cascaded ConvGRU iterative disparity refinement. Hierarchical Visual Transformation (HVT) is applied to the context network to learn shortcut-invariant features, improving cross-dataset generalization.

## Results

On the DSEC dataset, Bi-CMPStereo achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.532, 1PE of 10.613%, 2PE of 2.415%, and RMSE of 1.210 across all test sequences. This represents a 30% MAE reduction over the retrained ZEST† baseline (0.763) and a 13% improvement over the best symmetric event stereo method SE-CFF (0.612). On the DSEC online benchmark, Bi-CMPStereo achieves MAE 0.475 and 2PE 2.253%, outperforming all compared methods. Cross-dataset generalization to MVSEC yields MAE 1.858 and 2PE 32.121%, substantially surpassing ZEST (MAE 5.220) and symmetric baselines (MAE 2.297–2.614).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi-CMPStereo outperforms all five baselines across every metric on DSEC. Against the primary asymmetric baseline ZEST† (retrained under identical conditions), it reduces MAE from 0.763 to 0.532 (−30%) and 2PE from 4.646% to 2.415% (−48%). Compared to SEVFI [18], the improvement is MAE 0.711→0.532 and RMSE 1.509→1.210. Notably, Bi-CMPStereo also surpasses both symmetric event stereo methods—SE-CFF (MAE 0.612, 2PE 3.288%) and DTC (MAE 0.621, 2PE 3.018%)—despite using only one event camera, demonstrating that the bidirectional cross-modal prompting effectively recovers the information typically lost in asymmetric setups. On the DSEC online benchmark, it beats Zhuang et al. [89] (MAE 0.488, 2PE 2.335%) by a smaller margin (MAE 0.475, 2PE 2.253%), while maintaining a clear advantage over symmetric baselines. For cross-dataset generalization on MVSEC, Bi-CMPStereo (MAE 1.858) dramatically outperforms ZEST (MAE 5.220) and even the best symmetric method DTC (MAE 2.297), confirming that the HVT-based context learning and canonical-space alignment transfer effectively to unseen scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the iterative refinement paradigm of RAFT-Stereo (cascaded ConvGRUs with convex upsampling) and the group-wise correlation cost volume construction, repurposing them for the cross-modal setting. It departs from prior cross-modal asymmetric stereo methods (ZEST, SEVFI) that rely on domain-level or feature-level alignment to unify events and frames, arguing that such alignment marginalizes discriminative domain-specific cues. The key conceptual departure is the bidirectional target-canonical-space formulation: rather than forcing both modalities into a single shared space, each modality alternately serves as the canonical reference, with the Stereo Canonicalization Constraint and Cross-Domain Embedding Adapter preserving modality-specific features while still enabling cross-view matching. Hierarchical Visual Transformation is borrowed from domain-generalization literature to prevent shortcut learning in context features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a well-designed asymmetric stereo system (one event + one frame camera) can outperform symmetric event stereo (two event cameras) challenges the implicit assumption that homogeneous binocular inputs are inherently superior for disparity estimation. This has practical implications for sensor deployment, as frame cameras are far cheaper and more widely available than event cameras. Open questions include whether the bidirectional prompting strategy generalizes to other heterogeneous modality pairs (e.g., LiDAR + RGB, thermal + visible), whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or real-time constraints on embedded hardware, and whether the training-time-only SCC and HVT regularizations can be replaced by lighter alternatives for faster convergence.
