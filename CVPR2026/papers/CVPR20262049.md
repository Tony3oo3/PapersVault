## Problem

Foundational ViT-based vision models are powerful but opaque, and the existing B-cosification recipe for converting them into inherently interpretable B-cos variants was designed for CNNs and performs poorly on ViTs, sometimes failing to match training from scratch. This limits the practical adoption of inherently interpretable architectures at foundation scale, where post-hoc attribution methods remain noisy and unfaithful.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (typically 224×224) that are preprocessed to 6 channels (r, g, b, 1−r, 1−g, 1−b) to enable color visualization of explanations, and optionally text prompts for zero-shot classification. Outputs include class labels, model-inherent explanation maps given by the exact dynamic-linear decomposition W(x)·x, frozen feature embeddings for linear probing and k-NN, and depth maps for dense prediction. The explanations are not post-hoc approximations but constitute an exact summary of the model's computation by architectural design.

## Method

ALOE performs a one-time, label-free feature alignment that converts a frozen ViT teacher into a B-cos student in two steps. First, the teacher is converted to a B-cos architecture by replacing linear/MLP layers with bias-free B-cos transforms (B=2), removing all biases, while preserving self-attention, positional embeddings, and special tokens ([CLS], registers) to maintain one-to-one token routing. Second, the B-cos student is aligned to the frozen teacher on unlabeled images using a multi-layer cosine-similarity objective that combines a global embedding term with token-level supervision at three evenly spaced depths (⌊L/3⌋, ⌊2L/3⌋, L), plus a Frobenius-norm regularization coupling student and teacher weight magnitudes. Cosine similarity is chosen over MSE or contrastive losses for its scale invariance and robustness across pre-training paradigms. The aligned backbone is then frozen and deployed as a drop-in replacement across downstream tasks, requiring only ~3M unlabeled images (≈1000× fewer than billion-scale pre-training) to recover most of the teacher's generalization.

## Results

On linear probing (ViT-B/16), ALOE achieves 80.23% average accuracy across 10 datasets for the supervised teacher, 88.48% for SigLIP2, and 89.50% for DINOv3—closely matching the respective teachers (79.13%, 89.63%, 90.25%) while substantially outperforming B-cosification by +13.24, +7.62, and +15.82 p.p. respectively. On zero-shot ImageNet-1k, ALOE reaches 77.20% vs. 61.01% for B-cosification and 78.07% for the SigLIP2 teacher. For dense prediction (NYUv2 depth), ALOE improves relative δ₁ from 0.83 to 0.94 and reduces RMSE from 0.46 to 0.30 over B-cosification, approaching the DINOv3 teacher (0.97/0.24). On GridPG localization, ALOE's model-inherent B-cos explanations score 84.2% vs. 54.4% for AttnLRP on the SigLIP2 model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALOE decisively outperforms B-cosification [4] across all three pre-training paradigms: +13.24 p.p. average (supervised), +7.62 p.p. (SigLIP2), and +15.82 p.p. (DINOv3) on linear probing, with the largest per-dataset gains on fine-grained and texture datasets (e.g., +39.27 p.p. on Stanford Cars for DINOv3). Against the original foundation-model teachers, ALOE closes the gap to within ~1 p.p. on average (e.g., 88.48% vs. 89.63% for SigLIP2; 89.50% vs. 90.25% for DINOv3), confirming that the one-time alignment preserves general-purpose feature quality. On zero-shot transfer, ALOE reaches 77.20% on ImageNet-1k versus 61.01% for B-cosification and 78.07% for the SigLIP2 teacher, a 16.19 p.p. improvement over the prior B-cosification recipe. For k-NN evaluation, ALOE achieves 82.63% average accuracy, matching the DINOv3 teacher (82.95%) and surpassing B-cosification (50.77%) by over 31 p.p. On interpretability (GridPG), ALOE's model-inherent W(x) explanations score 84.2% versus 54.4% for the post-hoc AttnLRP method on the same SigLIP2 model, demonstrating that inherent interpretability via B-cos transforms yields substantially better localization than gradient-based attribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the B-cosification recipe [Arya et al., NeurIPS 2024], which converts pre-trained models into inherently interpretable B-cos variants via supervised fine-tuning, by replacing that supervised step with a label-free, cosine-similarity feature-alignment objective between a frozen teacher and a B-cos student. The underlying architecture (bias-free dynamic-linear B-cos transforms) comes directly from Bohle et al. [TPAMI 2024], and the teacher-student alignment borrows conceptual machinery from knowledge distillation (feature-level matching, multi-layer supervision), though the goal is interpretability rather than compression. The key departure from B-cosification is that the original recipe was designed for supervised CNNs and underperformed on ViTs; ALOE addresses this gap with ViT-specific design choices (preserving [CLS]/register tokens, attention-pooled global features for SigLIP2, depth-wise token alignment) and shows that a single alignment pass suffices across all three pre-training paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALOE provides a practical, low-cost path to inherently interpretable foundation-model backbones: a one-time alignment on ~3M unlabeled images (100–1000× less data than pre-training) yields a drop-in B-cos encoder that retains competitive linear-probing, k-NN, zero-shot, and dense-prediction performance while producing faithful, well-localized explanations by design. This is significant for safety-critical and regulated domains where post-hoc attributions are insufficient. Open questions include whether the approach extends to end-to-end interpretable multimodal LLMs (the current MLLM demo still relies on AttnLRP through the language model), whether interpretability fidelity degrades under distribution shift, and whether the recipe scales to even larger ViT variants (e.g., ViT-H or beyond) without additional architectural modifications.
