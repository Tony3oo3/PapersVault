## Problem

MeanFlow (MF) enables one-step generative modeling but suffers from two unresolved issues: its training target depends on the network's own prediction (making it a non-standard regression problem with high-variance, non-decreasing loss), and it fixes the classifier-free guidance (CFG) scale at training time, sacrificing inference-time flexibility. These limitations hinder MF's practicality as a stand-alone fastforward generative paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260697/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a random Gaussian latent vector (32×32×4, from a pretrained VAE tokenizer), a class label, and a continuous CFG scale ω (optionally with CFG interval bounds t_min, t_max) as conditioning. It produces a 256×256 RGB image in a single network evaluation (1-NFE) by computing z_0 = z_1 − u_θ(z_1). Unlike original MF, the CFG scale is a free inference-time variable rather than a frozen hyperparameter, and the model can also mimic no-CFG behavior by simply setting ω=1.

## Method

The paper reformulates MF's u-loss as a v-loss re-parameterized by the average-velocity network u_θ, where the JVP tangent vector uses the network's own marginal velocity prediction v_θ (obtained via the boundary condition u_θ(z_t, t, t) or an auxiliary v-head) instead of the conditional velocity e−x. This yields a network-independent regression target and a legitimate prediction function V_θ(z_t) that depends only on the noisy sample. Separately, the CFG scale ω and interval bounds are treated as continuous conditioning variables (embedded like time steps), enabling flexible guidance at inference. Architecturally, all heterogeneous conditions (r, t, class, Ω) are processed via multi-token in-context conditioning (8 tokens for class, 4 per other condition) concatenated with image latent tokens, replacing adaLN-zero and reducing model size by ~1/3 while improving FID.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, iMF-XL/2 achieves a 1-NFE FID of 1.72 (IS 282.0) trained entirely from scratch, representing a 50% relative improvement over the original MeanFlow-XL/2 (FID 3.43). With 2 NFEs, iMF-XL/2 further improves to FID 1.54. The smaller iMF-B/2 model (89M params, ~1/3 the size of MF-B/2 due to removing adaLN-zero) achieves FID 3.39 at 1-NFE, matching the original MF-XL/2 (676M params) with a fraction of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iMF-XL/2 (FID 1.72, 1-NFE, from scratch) decisively outperforms all prior from-scratch fastforward methods: it beats α-Flow-XL/2+ (2.58) by 0.86 FID, TiM-XL/2 (3.26) by 1.54, original MeanFlow-XL/2 (3.43) by 1.71, Shortcut-XL/2 (10.60) by 8.88, and iCT-XL/2 (34.24) by 32.52. It also surpasses all distillation-based 1-NFE methods, including FACM-XL/2 (1.76), DMF-XL/2+ (2.16), and π-Flow-XL/2 (2.85), despite using no pre-trained teacher. At 2 NFEs, iMF-XL/2 (1.54) improves over α-Flow-XL/2+ (1.95) and MeanFlow-XL/2+ (2.20). Compared to multi-step diffusion/flow models, iMF-XL/2 at 1-NFE (1.72) is competitive with SiT-XL/2+REPA (1.42, 250×2 NFEs) and SiD2 (1.38, 512×2 NFEs), closing the gap to within ~0.2–0.4 FID while requiring 250–512× fewer function evaluations. Among GANs and autoregressive models, iMF-XL/2 beats StyleGAN-XL (2.30) and VAR-d30 (1.92) but trails the strongest autoregressive methods (MAR-H 1.55, RAR-XXL 1.48, xAR-H 1.24).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MeanFlow (MF) [12], the most recent fastforward generative framework, by identifying and fixing two fundamental issues in its training objective and guidance mechanism. Conceptually, it re-derives the MF identity to show that the original u-loss is equivalent to a v-loss re-parameterized by the u-prediction network, exposing a hidden network-dependence in the regression target that the original formulation masked. The flexible CFG conditioning and multi-token in-context conditioning draw on ideas from multi-step diffusion literature [31, 6, 46] and DiT [32], respectively, but adapt them to the one-step setting where they were previously inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 1.72 FID at 1-NFE on ImageNet 256×256 entirely from scratch (no distillation, no pre-trained alignment) substantially narrows the gap with multi-step diffusion/flow methods and demonstrates that fastforward generation can be a viable stand-alone paradigm. The v-loss reformulation provides a cleaner theoretical lens that may benefit other fastforward methods (Consistency Models, Shortcut Models, IMM) with similar network-dependent targets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, whether the VAE tokenizer's inference cost becomes a bottleneck at 1-NFE, and whether the in-context conditioning strategy generalizes beyond DiT-style architectures.
