## Problem

RAW images from different camera sensors exhibit substantial domain shifts in spectral response, noise, and tone behavior, requiring camera-specific downstream models. Prior RAW-to-RAW translation methods are limited to one-to-one mappings between two cameras, making them impractical for real-world scenarios with many commercial camera types. No unified framework existed to translate across arbitrary multi-domain pairs with a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260992/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 four-channel Bayer RAW patch from any source camera domain and a target-domain reference RAW image (used only to extract a style embedding via the style encoder). At inference, the generator requires only the source RAW image and the precomputed domain embedding, eliminating the need for a per-image reference. Output is a translated RAW patch in the target sensor's linear-light domain, preserving content while matching the target's color response and signal-dependent noise profile.

## Method

MERIT uses a single generator conditioned on domain-specific style embeddings extracted by a transformer-based style encoder, enabling one-to-many and many-to-many translation. Two key innovations distinguish it: (1) a Sensor-Aware Noise Modeling (SANM) loss that computes a differentiable histogram of per-patch variance binned by intensity (using Sobel-filtered flat regions to isolate sensor noise from texture) and aligns it with a precomputed target-domain noise profile grounded in the Poisson-Gaussian model; and (2) a Multi-Scale Large Kernel Attention (MS-LKA) module on the upsampling path that fuses three dilated depthwise convolution branches and applies style-conditioned channel-wise attention weights to adaptively emphasize domain-relevant features. Training is fully unsupervised, combining patch-based adversarial loss, style reconstruction, cycle-consistency L1 and SSIM losses, and the noise alignment term, with a patch-based discriminator using majority voting for image-level decisions.

## Results

On the RAW-to-RAW mapping dataset, MERIT achieves 35.29 dB PSNR and 0.015 MAE for Samsung-S9→iPhone-X, and 31.90 dB PSNR and 0.021 MAE for iPhone-X→Samsung-S9, outperforming the best prior unsupervised method (Xie et al.) by +5.56 dB PSNR and 0.008 MAE reduction in the former direction. On the proposed MDRAW dataset (5 cameras, 20 domain pairs), MERIT attains the highest SSIM in all 20/20 pairs, the lowest MAE in 17/20 pairs, and the highest PSNR in 14/20 pairs compared to UVCGAN and Xie et al. The method also demonstrates superior scalability, maintaining ~58.7M parameters and 180K training iterations regardless of the number of domains, whereas UVCGAN scales linearly (186M→620M parameters from 3 to 5 domains).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERIT decisively outperforms all unsupervised baselines on the RAW-to-RAW mapping dataset. Against the strongest prior methods, Xie et al. (29.73/28.09 dB PSNR) and Rawformer (29.32/28.45 dB PSNR), MERIT gains +5.56 dB and +3.45 dB PSNR respectively in the two translation directions, while reducing MAE from 0.025 to 0.015 (S→i) and from 0.037 to 0.021 (i→S). Even the semi-supervised Afifi et al. (29.65/28.58 dB), which requires paired training data, is surpassed by MERIT's fully unsupervised results. On the broader MDRAW benchmark (5 cameras, 20 domain pairs), MERIT achieves the best SSIM in all 20 pairs and the best MAE in 17/20 pairs versus UVCGAN and Xie et al., confirming consistent cross-domain superiority. In terms of scalability, MERIT uses only 58.7M parameters and 180K iterations for 5 domains, compared to UVCGAN's 620M parameters and proportionally longer training, while still matching or exceeding UVCGAN's quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERIT extends the one-to-one RAW-to-RAW translation paradigm established by Afifi et al. [1], Rawformer [27], and Xie et al. [33] into a unified many-to-many setting by adapting StarGAN v2's [6] style-encoder-and-multi-domain-generator architecture to the RAW domain. Architecturally, it builds on the large-kernel attention concept from the Visual Attention Network [12], extending it into a multi-scale, style-conditioned module (MS-LKA) tailored for RAW signal characteristics. The key conceptual departure is the explicit sensor-aware noise modeling loss (SANM), which grounds the translation objective in the Poisson-Gaussian physics of RAW sensor noise rather than relying solely on implicit adversarial learning of domain statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As commercial cameras proliferate with heterogeneous RAW formats (Apple ProRAW, Samsung Expert RAW, DNG, etc.), the inability to share downstream vision models across sensors is a practical bottleneck; MERIT's constant-parameter, single-model approach directly addresses this scalability gap, and the released MDRAW benchmark (5 cameras, paired + unpaired) fills a previously empty evaluation niche. Limitations include the 256×256 patch resolution, the assumption that Poisson-Gaussian noise fully characterizes sensor differences (ignoring, e.g., demosaicing artifacts or ISP-specific tone curves), and evaluation on only five camera models. Open questions include whether the style encoder can disentangle sensor identity from scene-dependent variables (brightness, color temperature) at scale, and how the framework behaves with 10+ domains or fundamentally different sensor architectures (e.g., stacked vs. back-illuminated).
