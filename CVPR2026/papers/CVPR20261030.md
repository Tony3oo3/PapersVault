## Problem

Existing vector glyph generation pipelines either require users to prepare curated exemplar sheets of raster glyphs or produce raster outputs needing separate vectorization, creating a major bottleneck for non-experts and rapid ideation. General-purpose LLMs and off-the-shelf vector-graphics LLMs fail on typography because glyphs impose strict geometric constraints—long precise coordinate sequences, watertight topology, and coherent stroke logic—that generic SVG training data does not capture. No prior work unifies text- and image-referenced conditioning within a single model that directly emits editable vector glyph code.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a bag of style tags (randomly permuted during training to reduce positional bias) or 1–8 reference glyph images rendered at 192×192 with centering and uniform padding, always paired with a target character from the closed set '0-9a-zA-Z'. The output is a single SVG <path> d-string using only MoveTo, LineTo, Quadratic Bézier, and ClosePath commands, with all coordinates quantized to one decimal and normalized to UPM=1000 on a shared baseline. No raster intermediates, denoisers, or post-optimizers are applied; the detokenized string is directly renderable and editable.

## Method

VecGlypher is a multimodal LLM (Gemma3-4B/27B or Llama3.3-70B) trained via two-stage supervised fine-tuning to autoregressively emit SVG path tokens. Stage 1 performs large-scale continuation on 39K noisy Envato fonts using text-referenced samples only, teaching SVG syntax and long-horizon coordinate prediction; Stage 2 post-trains on 2.5K expert-annotated Google Fonts with a mixture of text- and image-referenced samples to align geometry with appearance instructions. A typography-aware preprocessing pipeline de-duplicates near-identical fonts, normalizes all glyphs to UPM=1000, canonicalizes paths to a single <path> element, and quantizes coordinates to one decimal to stabilize long-sequence decoding. Ablations show that absolute-coordinate serialization consistently outperforms relative coordinates at 27B scale, and that the two-stage recipe (E→G) is critical for out-of-distribution generalization, yielding R-ACC 101.0 and FID 3.47 on cross-family OOD evaluation.

## Results

On cross-family OOD evaluation over Google Fonts, VecGlypher-70B (text-referenced) achieves R-ACC 100.4, Chamfer Distance 1.68, DINO 94.28, and FID 3.34, substantially outperforming the strongest general LLM baseline (Claude Sonnet 4.5: R-ACC 46.65, CD 5.28, FID 19.59). For image-referenced generation, VecGlypher-27B reaches R-ACC 99.12, CD 1.18, and FID 2.32, improving over the best specialized vector-font baseline (DualVector: R-ACC 49.20, CD 16.45, FID 105.5) by roughly 2× in recognizability, 92% lower geometric error, and 97.8% lower distributional error. Budget-tier LLMs (GPT-5 Mini, Gemini-2.5 Flash) rarely emit valid SVG, scoring R-ACC below 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VecGlypher wins decisively on every metric against all five baseline groups. Against general-purpose LLMs (Table 7), budget models GPT-5 Mini (R-ACC 4.17) and Gemini-2.5 Flash (R-ACC 4.89) almost never produce valid closed SVG paths; flagship models GPT-5 (R-ACC 43.98) and Claude Sonnet 4.5 (R-ACC 46.65) improve but still fail to close contours or match the requested character, while VecGlypher-70B achieves R-ACC 100.4 with 68% lower CD and 83% lower FID than Claude Sonnet 4.5. Gemini 2.5 Pro (R-ACC 24.04) and Claude Haiku 4.5 (R-ACC 32.38) fall well behind even the mid-tier GPT-5. For image-referenced generation (Table 8), the specialized vector-font methods DeepVecFont-v2 (R-ACC 37.86, FID 115.5) and DualVector (R-ACC 49.20, FID 105.5) struggle with thin strokes and contour closure on unseen families, whereas VecGlypher-27B reaches R-ACC 99.12 and FID 2.32—roughly a 2× recognizability gain and >97% reduction in distributional error over the best baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

VecGlypher departs from the dominant image-referenced vector-font paradigm (DeepVecFont, DualVector, VecFusion), which relies on separate encoder-decoder architectures or diffusion cascades and requires carefully prepared exemplar sheets. It also diverges from vector-graphics LLMs (StarVector, IconShop, OmniSVG) that target generic icons and simple SVG drawings but fail on the strict geometric and topological constraints of typography. The paper's key conceptual move is recasting glyph synthesis as autoregressive language modeling over SVG path tokens within a single multimodal LLM, unifying text- and image-referenced conditioning under one architecture and training objective, supported by a two-stage recipe (large-scale noisy Envato continuation followed by expert-annotated Google Fonts alignment) that no prior vector-font or SVG-LLM work has employed at this data scale (39K + 2.5K fonts).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a sharp domain gap exists between general-purpose LLMs' ability to write SVG code and their ability to produce typographically valid, watertight glyphs, positioning unified vector glyph generation as primarily a data and training-recipe problem rather than an architecture problem. This reframing suggests that next-generation LLMs ingesting large corpora of annotated vector glyph programs could acquire the capability through instruction tuning alone, potentially eliminating the need for specialized vector-font architectures. Open questions include extending beyond Latin alphanumerics to diacritics, cursive scripts, and pictographs (which require compositional encodings and part-sharing), reducing the ~30B parameter floor through improved tokenization or constrained decoding, and whether the two-stage recipe generalizes to other structured-geometry generation tasks.
