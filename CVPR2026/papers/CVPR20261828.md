## Problem

Jointly optimizing camera poses and object geometry from unposed images suffers from an underexplored 'easy-view bias': uniform view optimization lets highly overlapping, texture-rich views dominate gradient updates while marginalizing weakly-textured or sparsely-overlapping views that are critical for geometric completeness. This bias causes pose drift and fragmented reconstructions, particularly in SDF-based neural implicit methods where minor misalignments propagate to severe surface distortion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of unposed RGB images (49 or 64 per scene on DTU) along with known camera intrinsics, and monocular depth/normal supervision from MonoSDF and OmniData. Camera poses are initialized as identity matrices with no pose priors. The output consists of estimated camera poses (rotation in SO(3) and translation in R³) and a 3D surface mesh extracted via marching cubes at 512 resolution from the learned SDF field.

## Method

ManifoldNeuS introduces the Manifold-aware View Optimizability Score (MaVOS), which unifies local constraint strength (normalized feature co-visibility from a covisibility matrix) and global topological coverage (minimum distance in a spectral embedding of the view graph) into a single per-view score. This score drives three synergistic components within the NeuS SDF framework: (1) dynamic view scheduling that initializes with top-K MaVOS anchor views and greedily expands with dynamic reordering of successors; (2) MaVOS-gated positional encoding that delays high-frequency band activation for high-scoring views to prevent premature detail fitting from destabilizing pose estimation; and (3) anti-score loss weighting (w_i = 1 − S_i) that rebalances gradient contributions by up-weighting low-optimizability views. The spectral embedding is computed via the symmetric normalized Laplacian of the co-visibility graph, projecting pairwise feature correspondences onto a low-dimensional manifold where geodesic distances capture topological relationships invisible to Euclidean metrics.

## Results

On the DTU benchmark (9 scenes), ManifoldNeuS achieves a mean rotation error of 0.602° and mean translation error of 0.022, substantially outperforming VGGT (2.300° / 0.106) and SG-NeRF (4.505° / 0.235), while NeuS-BARF and HT-3DGS fail catastrophically (118.5° / 4.38 and 101.3° / 4.22 respectively). In terms of reconstruction quality (Chamfer distance), ManifoldNeuS achieves 0.404 on average, competitive with VGGT (0.409, which fails on 3 of 9 scenes) and close to NeuS trained with COLMAP poses (0.368), while SG-NeRF lags at 0.590.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ManifoldNeuS decisively outperforms all four baselines on pose estimation. Against NeuS-BARF (118.5° avg rotation error) and HT-3DGS (101.3°), the improvement is orders of magnitude, as both methods suffer catastrophic pose divergence. Compared to SG-NeRF (4.51° / 0.235), which is initialized with noisy poses perturbed around ground truth, ManifoldNeuS starting from identity initialization achieves 7.5× lower rotation error and 10.7× lower translation error. Against VGGT (2.30° / 0.106), the closest competitor, ManifoldNeuS reduces rotation error by 3.8× and translation error by 4.8×, while VGGT completely fails on 3 of 9 scenes (Scan 63, 83, 97). On reconstruction quality (Chamfer distance), ManifoldNeuS (0.404) is competitive with VGGT (0.409, with 3 missing scenes) and slightly behind NeuS with COLMAP poses (0.368), but substantially outperforms SG-NeRF (0.590), confirming that accurate pose recovery translates to high-fidelity geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeuS (SDF-based neural implicit surface reconstruction) and BARF (coarse-to-fine positional encoding for joint pose-geometry optimization), extending them with a per-view optimizability signal (MaVOS) that combines local feature-matching strength with global topological coverage via spectral embedding on a view graph. It departs from prior pose-free surface reconstruction methods (NoPose-NeuS, ParaSurRe, PoRF, SC-NeuS) that either rely on external priors (monocular depth, optical flow, SLAM) or treat all views uniformly, by explicitly diagnosing and addressing 'easy-view bias' as the root cause of pose drift. The conceptual lineage is closest to BARF's joint optimization framework, but the key departure is replacing BARF's global progress-based frequency scheduling with a view-adaptive, manifold-aware control signal that governs scheduling, encoding, and loss weighting simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnostic (easy-view bias) and a unified solution for a failure mode that has plagued pose-free neural surface reconstruction, achieving near-ground-truth pose accuracy from identity initialization without any pose priors. However, evaluation is limited to 9 DTU scenes with known intrinsics and external depth/normal supervision (MonoSDF, OmniData), leaving open questions about scalability to larger or unbounded scenes, robustness to unknown intrinsics, and whether the spectral embedding approach remains tractable for very large view collections. The method's reliance on feature matching for the local constraint term also raises questions about performance on textureless or highly repetitive scenes where correspondences are unreliable.
