## Problem

Existing streaming 3D reconstruction models (e.g., Stream3R, StreamVGGT) anchor all poses to the first frame and regress absolute coordinates, causing attention decay, scale drift, and catastrophic extrapolation failure that collapses trajectories within tens of meters. No prior method achieves stable, metric-scale reconstruction over kilometer-scale sequences under a strictly online, future-invisible setting at real-time speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260352/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a streaming RGB image sequence at a maximum long side of 518 pixels in a strictly causal, future-invisible manner, processing frames one at a time with a keyframe interval of 10. For each frame it outputs a keyframe-relative camera pose (3D translation, unit quaternion, 2D focal offset), a per-pixel depth map, a world-coordinate 3D pointmap with confidence scores, and a single global metric scale factor. At inference the system sustains 18 FPS on a single GPU with stable memory usage across thousands of frames.

## Method

LongStream introduces a gauge-decoupled formulation that eliminates the first-frame anchor by predicting keyframe-relative poses (T_{i←k} = T_i · T_k^{-1}), reformulating long-range extrapolation as a constant-difficulty local estimation task invariant to global coordinate choice. An orthogonal scale learning mechanism separates geometry optimization in a scale-invariant normalized space from a dedicated scale head that predicts the global metric factor, ensuring ∂L_geom/∂s = 0. To address attention-sink reliance and KV-cache contamination identified as primary causes of temporal degradation, the paper proposes Cache-Consistent Training (CCT), which explicitly passes and trims the KV cache between chunks during training to mirror inference visibility, combined with periodic cache refresh that hard-marginalizes stale context every N keyframes. The architecture builds on VGGT's 24-layer DINOv2-tokenizer + causal Transformer backbone (~1.3B parameters) with a RAFT-style AdaLN-modulated iterative pose head, trained in two stages (batch-independent then KV-cache-consistent) on a multi-domain dataset collection.

## Results

On the KITTI benchmark (sequences up to 5.1 km), LongStream achieves a mean ATE of 51.90 m, outperforming the next-best streaming method TTT3R (177.73 m) by 3.4× and the best optimization-based baseline MASt3R-SLAM (177.93 m) by 3.4×. On vKITTI, LongStream reduces mean ATE to 1.610 m versus 23.667 m for VGGT-SLAM and 28.099 m for TTT3R. Across TUM, Oxford Spires, and Waymo (Table 2), LongStream achieves ATE of 0.076 m, 19.815 m, and 0.737 m respectively, beating all streaming and optimization-based baselines on every dataset. The model operates at 18 FPS on a single GPU with stable memory usage over kilometer-scale sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongStream decisively outperforms all six baselines on ATE across every evaluated dataset. Against the strongest streaming competitor TTT3R, LongStream reduces KITTI mean ATE from 177.73 m to 51.90 m (3.4×) and vKITTI mean ATE from 28.099 m to 1.610 m (17.5×). Against optimization-based SLAM systems, LongStream beats VGGT-SLAM on KITTI (51.90 vs 263.37 m) and vKITTI (1.610 vs 23.667 m), and surpasses MASt3R-SLAM on Waymo (0.737 vs 7.625 m) and Oxford Spires (19.815 vs 37.728 m). The two causal-Transformer streaming baselines (STream3R and StreamVGGT) show the largest degradation on long sequences, with Waymo ATE exceeding 42 m versus LongStream's 0.737 m. On 3D reconstruction (Chamfer Distance), LongStream achieves the best CD on both 7Scenes (2.260) and TUM (0.225), though it trails MASt3R-SLAM on TUM F1@0.25 (0.673 vs 0.954) and FastVGGT on 7Scenes F1@0.25 (0.641 vs 0.710), suggesting slightly less precise per-point alignment despite superior global trajectory accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LongStream builds directly on the VGGT backbone (24-layer causal Transformer with DINOv2 tokenizer) and the streaming paradigm established by Stream3R and StreamVGGT (causal attention + KV cache for incremental reconstruction). Its key conceptual departure is the 'gauge-decoupled' reformulation: replacing first-frame-anchored absolute pose regression with keyframe-relative pose estimation (drawing on gauge-invariance ideas from bundle adjustment and VIO literature) and orthogonal scale learning (from the SI-Log philosophy in Depth Anything). The iterative pose refinement head follows RAFT's AdaLN-modulated Transformer design, while the cache-consistent training and periodic cache refresh mechanisms are novel contributions addressing attention-sink reliance and KV-cache contamination identified in streaming Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies a fundamental architectural flaw in existing streaming 3D reconstruction models—the gauge-coupled first-frame anchoring—as the root cause of long-sequence extrapolation failure, rather than attributing it to insufficient training data or model capacity. The practical impact is substantial: stable, metric-scale, real-time (18 FPS) reconstruction over kilometer-scale sequences opens the door to deployment in autonomous driving, AR/VR, and embodied robotics where offline reprocessing is infeasible. Open questions remain around dynamic scenes (the model assumes a largely static world), the heuristic nature of keyframe scheduling, and whether the gauge-decoupled formulation generalizes to multi-sensor or 4D settings. The cache-consistent training insight may also transfer to other long-context streaming Transformer applications beyond 3D geometry.
