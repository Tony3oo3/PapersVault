## Problem

End-to-end RL driving policies remain brittle under distribution shift because perception-centric encoders discard causal interaction structure and per-entity confidence, rewards are sparse or discontinuous in safety-critical regimes, and uncertainty is estimated but never propagated to regulate exploration or cross-domain transfer. The paper asks how safety-aware E2E driving policies can be reliably transferred across towns, traffic densities, and weather by aligning causality and uncertainty at the control layer rather than the perception layer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes structured ego-centric observations from CARLA at 20 Hz — per-agent relative position and velocity, semantic class codes, local lane curvature and heading offset, and per-edge aleatoric variance — rather than raw camera or LiDAR images. These edge features are aggregated through uncertainty-weighted attention into a single compact state vector that also concatenates ego speed, the previous action, route arc-length progress, and aggregated aleatoric uncertainty. The policy outputs a 3-dimensional continuous action (throttle, brake, steering) per timestep; at inference only a single actor pass with top-K relational aggregation is needed (≤3 ms overhead), while the critic ensemble and MAML loop are training-only.

## Method

The framework is built around a single normalized uncertainty signal σ̄ that simultaneously (i) weights edges in an ego-centric relational graph via softmax attention over distance-squared divided by per-edge heteroscedastic variance, (ii) gates policy entropy as β(σ̄) = β₀(1−σ̄) so low confidence suppresses stochasticity and high confidence restores exploration, and (iii) regularizes cross-domain transfer by matching uncertainty moments alongside KL divergence on action distributions and MMD on attention vectors. A differentiable multi-objective reward replaces sparse collision and lane-departure penalties with smooth tanh and exponential surrogates for safety, progress, comfort, and a calibrated uncertainty term, all sharing the same σ̄. Epistemic uncertainty is obtained from a K-critic ensemble variance, while aleatoric uncertainty is per-edge variance injected directly into the attention weights. Transfer is initialized via a MAML inner/outer loop across source domains, after which a few target-domain SAC updates refine the policy while preserving aligned attention and uncertainty profiles.

## Results

On Town10HD under adverse conditions (heavy rain, dense fog, night), the proposed framework achieves a CTE of 0.65 m (28.6% below ST-P3, 20.7% below ThinkTwice) and heading error of 0.31 rad (47.5% below ST-P3), with an average episode reward of 265.3 (69.6% above ST-P3's 156.4). Collision rate drops to 0.006/km (45.5% below ST-P3) and driving stability reaches 0.91, while zero-shot transfer to Town05 yields 100% success rate with CTE 0.192 m. Cross-town transfer to Town02 improves Driving Score from 188.6 (target-only) to 214.3 (full causal-uncertainty transfer) with Infraction Score rising from 0.88 to 0.94.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against ST-P3 [10] (spatiotemporal tensor state), it reduces CTE by 28.6%, heading error by 47.5%, collision rate by 45.5%, and improves reward by 69.6%. Against ThinkTwice [14] (scalable decoder), gains are 20.7% in CTE, 32.6% in heading error, and 61.7% in reward. Against TransFuser [5] (BEV + camera fusion), the method achieves 15.6% lower CTE, 26.2% lower heading error, and 52.3% higher reward. Against RaSc [8] (risk-aware imitation), the closest competitor, it still leads by 11.0% in CTE, 18.4% in heading error, 45.1% in reward, and 14.3% in collision reduction. The ablation confirms that removing uncertainty-weighted attention raises CTE by 0.11 m and removing the differentiable reward drops Driving Score by 18.4 points, indicating the gains are not attributable to a single component but to the unified reliability interface.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the E2E driving RL line (ST-P3, ThinkTwice, TransFuser, RaSc) by replacing global tensor or BEV encoders with an ego-centric relational graph whose attention is weighted by per-edge aleatoric variance, and by coupling that same uncertainty signal to policy entropy gating and a causal-semantic transfer objective (KL + MMD + uncertainty moment matching with MAML initialization). It departs from prior uncertainty-aware driving work, which typically treats aleatoric and epistemic components separately or uses them only diagnostically, by routing both through a single normalized confidence scalar that simultaneously modulates attention, exploration, and cross-domain alignment. The MAML-based few-shot transfer builds on the meta-learning initialization idea from Think2Drive [21] but applies it at the control layer rather than the perception layer, aligning action distributions and attention statistics rather than pixel or BEV features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified control-layer reliability interface offers a clean architectural principle—using one calibrated uncertainty signal to coordinate attention, exploration, and transfer—that could generalize beyond driving to other safety-critical sequential decision problems. However, all validation is in CARLA 0.9.15 with a single ego vehicle across three towns and 20-episode transfer evaluations, leaving Sim2Real transfer, multi-agent interactions, and standardized leaderboard benchmarks (CARLA Leaderboard, Bench2Drive) as open questions. The paper also notes that reducing critic-ensemble overhead and moving toward interventional causal modeling are necessary next steps before the framework can be considered deployment-ready.
