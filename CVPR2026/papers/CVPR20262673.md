## Problem

Continuous-time neural networks such as Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) encoders rely on a single hidden state to simultaneously track fast input fluctuations and accumulate longer-term context, creating a representation bottleneck where rapidly varying signals overwrite slower contextual information. No prior architecture has unified continuous-time adaptive encoding with high-capacity associative memory while preserving explicit bounded-stability guarantees, leaving a gap between the biological separation of online processing and episodic recall and existing computational models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes univariate or multivariate time-series sequences (including irregularly sampled event streams and wearable sensor data) at arbitrary time stamps, with the LTC encoder maintaining a 32-dimensional hidden state that evolves via closed-form continuous-time dynamics. At each time step the hidden state is projected to a 32-dimensional query, and the MHN retrieves a 32-dimensional memory vector from N=16 stored patterns; the concatenated 64-dimensional representation feeds a linear readout that produces either class logits (11-class Gesture, 6-class Activity, 10-class Sequential MNIST, binary Occupancy/Ozone) or a scalar MSE-regressed value (Power).

## Method

The architecture is a three-stage sequential pipeline: (1) an LTC encoder with input-dependent time constants produces a bounded hidden state x(t); (2) a learned projection W_q maps x(t) to a query q(t), which drives a Modern Hopfield retrieval h_β(q) that returns a softmax-weighted sum of N stored memory patterns; (3) x(t) and the retrieved vector r(t) are concatenated and passed through a single linear readout. The key theoretical contribution is a boundedness theorem showing ‖z(t)‖ ≤ √(‖A‖² + R²) for all t, and a gradient-contraction lemma proving that the L-Lipschitz retrieval map (L ≤ 1) contracts upstream gradients, yielding a Hessian-trace reduction of roughly one order of magnitude relative to the standalone LTC. Empirically, the Hopfield layer acts as a denoising prior that pulls latent states toward stored prototypes, producing tighter within-class clusters, wider loss-landscape basins, and a 2.3% mean accuracy gain over recurrent and continuous-time baselines on five of six benchmarks.

## Results

On six time-series benchmarks, LTC-MHN achieves the best performance on five of six datasets, with a mean accuracy improvement of 2.3% over the strongest competing baselines. Gains are most pronounced on classification tasks: 71.23% on Gesture (+2.78% over LTC), 95.77% on Occupancy (+2.11%), 96.86% on Activity Recognition (+2.35%), and 97.30% on Sequential MNIST (+1.94%). On the regression Power dataset, LTC-MHN (MSE 0.629) remains competitive but trails CT-GRU (0.579). The estimated Hessian trace drops by roughly one order of magnitude (from 1.6×10⁻² for LTC to 1.55×10⁻³ for LTC-MHN), indicating a substantially flatter loss landscape.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standalone LTC encoder (the closest baseline), LTC-MHN wins on all five classification/detection tasks with margins ranging from +1.94% (Sequential MNIST) to +2.78% (Gesture), and also improves F1 on Ozone from 0.304 to 0.321; on the Power regression task, LTC-MHN (MSE 0.629) is slightly worse than LTC (0.592). Compared to LSTM, LTC-MHN outperforms it on every dataset, with the largest gap on Gesture (+8.02% accuracy) and Ozone (+0.043 F1). Against Neural ODE, the advantage is even more pronounced, with LTC-MHN exceeding it by 25.24% on Gesture accuracy and 0.157 F1 on Ozone, while Neural ODE also suffers the worst regression error (MSE 1.281 on Power). The only baseline that beats LTC-MHN is CT-GRU on the Power regression task (0.579 vs. 0.629 MSE), where the discrete-memory bias of Hopfield retrieval appears to introduce overshooting corrections unsuitable for continuous targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper couples two established components—Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) encoders for adaptive continuous-time dynamics and Modern Hopfield Networks (MHNs) for content-addressable memory—into a single architecture, motivated by the observation that the LTC hidden state suffers from a representation bottleneck where fast inputs overwrite slower contextual signals. It departs from prior memory-augmented architectures (Neural Turing Machines, Memory Networks, memory-augmented transformers) by preserving the closed-form stability guarantees of liquid dynamics while adding an explicit associative recall step, and from standard attention-based models by grounding retrieval in the Hopfield energy formulation rather than learned key-value projections. The theoretical contribution (boundedness of the coupled state, gradient contraction through the retrieval map, Hessian trace reduction) extends the stability analysis of standalone LTCs to the compositional setting, which prior work had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean, minimal recipe for decoupling real-time temporal encoding from long-term associative memory in continuous-time networks, with explicit stability guarantees that make the coupling safe to deploy. However, the discrete-memory bias limits gains on regression tasks, the fixed inverse temperature β is acknowledged as suboptimal, and the evaluation is confined to six moderate-length benchmarks without comparison to recent SSM or foundation-model baselines. Open questions include task-dependent retrieval scheduling, memory refresh under non-stationarity, and whether the gradient-contraction benefit persists at larger sequence lengths or with more memory slots.
