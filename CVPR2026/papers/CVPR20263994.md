## Problem

Single-view 6-DoF grasp pose estimation loses critical geometric information in corner views due to inherent occlusion, degrading grasp robustness. Existing multi-view approaches that reconstruct a complete scene before grasping are time-consuming and task-agnostic, making them impractical for real-time robotic pipelines. The paper addresses how to efficiently incorporate an auxiliary view to improve corner-view robustness without the overhead of full scene reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263994/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB-D images (a primary view and an auxiliary view from a different robot joint configuration), back-projected into 3D point clouds of 15,000 points each with 512-dim features. The auxiliary view's relative transformation is obtained from robot kinematics rather than camera calibration. Output is a set of 6-DoF grasp poses parameterized as approaching direction (evaluated over 300 predefined candidates on a unit sphere), in-plane rotation (12 candidates), approaching depth (4 candidates), and gripper width, each accompanied by a quality score predicted by MLPs rather than regressed directly.

## Method

The framework adopts a post-fusion strategy: a shared ResUNet14 sparse 3D convolution backbone encodes each single-view point cloud independently, and cross-view fusion occurs only at the grasp-relevant cylindrical region around each seed. A self-supervised contrastive learning objective defines cross-view matches (points at the same 3D location) and non-matches (same object, different grasp approach directions) to regularize features for spatial consistency and direction discriminability, preventing corner-view features from deviating from the training distribution. The cross-view-aligned cylinder integration module groups K=8 neighbors in a grasp-oriented cylinder, aligns cross-view points via a weighted coordinate-distance and feature-dot-product similarity, registers all points into cylindrical coordinates (θ, r, d) that directly correspond to in-plane rotation, width, and depth, and then alternates local self-attention layers (within each view) with seed cross-attention layers (across views) inspired by VGGT's multi-frame transformer design to integrate cross-view context into the seed feature while preserving fine-grained geometry.

## Results

On the GraspNet-1Billion benchmark, the proposed cross-view fusion framework (2 views) achieves AP of 74.08 (Seen), 62.38 (Similar), and 27.27 (Novel) on RealSense data, outperforming the previous SOTA ZeroGrasp by 3.55, 0.23, and 0.81 AP points respectively. On Kinect data, it surpasses EconomicGrasp (the prior SOTA) by 1.61, 1.68, and 1.84 AP on Seen, Similar, and Novel splits. In real-world clutter removal experiments, the method achieves a 96% success rate, exceeding GSNet (82%) and GraspNet (77%) by 14% and 19% respectively, while reducing total pipeline time from 8.5s (6-view reconstruction) to 4.6s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 10 single-view methods on GraspNet-1Billion and one multi-view reconstruction pipeline. Against the strongest single-view baselines, the method wins on all splits: it beats ZeroGrasp (70.53 AP Seen, RealSense) by 3.55 AP and EconomicGrasp (68.21 AP Seen, RealSense) by 5.87 AP on the Seen split, while also leading on Similar (+0.23 over ZeroGrasp) and Novel (+0.81 over ZeroGrasp). On Kinect data, it surpasses EconomicGrasp (62.59 AP Seen) by 1.61 AP and GSNet (61.19 AP Seen) by 3.01 AP. Against older baselines (GraspNet, TransGrasp, TSB, GSNet), the gains are substantial—e.g., 46.52 AP over GraspNet on Seen RealSense. The multi-view GeneGN pipeline [14] (6 calibrated external cameras) requires 5.4s of reconstruction overhead versus 1.2s for the proposed method, and the proposed approach achieves higher real-world success rate (96% vs. not directly reported but implied lower) with only one auxiliary view rather than six. The method consistently wins across all evaluated splits and both camera types, with the largest relative gains on the Novel split where occlusion is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-view 6-DoF grasp estimation line (GraspNet, GSNet, EconomicGrasp, ZeroGrasp) by incorporating an auxiliary view through a post-fusion strategy, deliberately departing from prior multi-view pipelines (VGN, GeneGN, RVT) that require full scene reconstruction before grasping. The feature backbone follows the sparse 3D convolution paradigm (ResUNet14) established in recent grasp detectors, while the cross-view fusion module draws on point cloud registration principles and the alternating attention design from VGGT. The self-supervised contrastive learning for cross-view feature regularization is a novel addition not present in prior grasp estimation work, which typically relies solely on supervised losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in robotic grasping—occlusion in corner views—without the 5+ second reconstruction overhead of full multi-view scene completion, making it suitable for real-time manipulation loops. The finding that naive pre-fusion of cross-view point clouds yields negligible gains (0.32 AP) while structured post-fusion with cylindrical alignment and alternating attention yields 6.41 AP improvement provides useful guidance for future multi-view grasp systems. Limitations include the requirement for known inter-view transforms (robot kinematics or calibrated cameras), evaluation on only 12 real-world objects, and reliance on the GraspNet-1Billion benchmark's synthetic-to-real gap; open questions include scalability to more than two views, dynamic scenes, and integration with learned scene representations.
