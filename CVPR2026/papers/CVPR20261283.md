## Problem

Existing cultural understanding benchmarks for LVLMs depict scenes rooted in a single cultural context, leaving unexplored how models behave when cultural elements from multiple regions coexist within a single visual scene. This paper identifies culture mixing as a critical challenge: models must preserve individual cultural identities while distinguishing and integrating multiple conflicting cultural cues, a capability that single-culture training data does not equip them with.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261283/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a culturally mixed food image (synthetically generated at varying compositions: single food, multiple foods, food with cultural background, or all three) along with a VQA prompt asking for the target food's name and country of origin. In multi-food settings, the target is explicitly designated as the 'left food' to disambiguate from the distractor. Outputs are evaluated via similarity matching for food names and exact string matching (with country-name variation handling) for cultural origin, and prediction entropy is tracked as a consistency metric across contexts.

## Method

CULTUREMIX is constructed via a diffusion-based pipeline (FLUX.1-Kontext and Qwen-Image-Edit) that composes 247 seed food images from 30 countries with 50 background images from 5 continents, producing 23k human-verified synthetic images across four subtasks that systematically vary distractor type (none, food, background, both) and cultural distance (same country, same continent, different continent). The paper then evaluates 10 LVLMs and analyzes prediction shifts, entropy, and label-shift ratios to quantify how distractors pull predictions toward their cultural source. For mitigation, the authors explore three strategies: direct prompting (instructing the model to focus on the target), Chain-of-Thought prompting, and supervised fine-tuning on 5K culturally mixed images presented in a progressive difficulty order (SF→MF→SFB→MFB) to encourage consistent predictions across contexts. SFT yields the most substantial gains, reducing entropy by up to 25% in SFB/MFB settings while improving accuracy, whereas CoT prompting can paradoxically amplify background reliance and degrade consistency.

## Results

Across all 10 evaluated LVLMs, performance degrades consistently as cultural distractors are introduced: background distractors (SFB) cause a 13% larger drop in country accuracy and a 7% larger drop in food name accuracy compared to food-only distractors (MF). On average, 15% of predictions shift directly to the distractor's country and an additional 12% shift to the same continent. In the mitigation study (Table 2), supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on 5K culturally mixed images yields the largest gains: for Ovis2.5-9B, SFB entropy drops from 3.34 to 2.52 and SFB accuracy rises from 5.65% to 8.59%; for InternVL3-8B, SFB entropy drops from 3.77 to 2.76 and SFB accuracy rises from 2.14% to 4.16%. Proprietary models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) substantially outperform all open-source models across every subtask.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 10 LVLMs on CULTUREMIX. Proprietary models GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro substantially outperform all open-source models across every subtask, with Gemini achieving the highest accuracy but also exhibiting the largest label shifts toward distractor countries. Among open models, Ovis2.5-9B is the most competitive despite its small parameter size, followed by InternVL3-78B, QwenVL3-32B, and QwenVL3-8B; Molmo-72B trails the other open models. In the mitigation study, SFT provides statistically significant improvements (paired T-test, p < .01) over the base model for both Ovis2.5-9B and InternVL3-8B, particularly on SFB and MFB where entropy drops by 0.8–1.0 nats and accuracy roughly doubles. Direct prompting yields modest gains on MF but is less effective on background-heavy subtasks, while CoT prompting occasionally degrades consistency by amplifying reliance on background cues. The real-world CULTUREMIX-Real evaluation confirms the same degradation pattern (MF-same > SF > MF-diff), validating that synthetic findings generalize to natural images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of cultural-awareness VQA benchmarks (WorldCuisines, WorldWideDishes, CVQA) by introducing a fundamentally different evaluation condition: scenes where multiple cultural cues coexist and conflict, rather than single-culture depictions. It repurposes diffusion-based image editing (FLUX.1-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) not for model improvement but for constructing controlled culturally mixed evaluation scenes, drawing on the image composition literature. A concurrent work [46] explores a related 'confused tourist' setting from an adversarial-robustness angle, and Kim et al. [45] examines isolated cultural artifacts, but neither systematically varies distractor type and cultural distance as CULTUREMIX does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a consistent and quantified failure mode—background cues dominate over target-object cues, causing 14% accuracy drops and directional label shifts toward the distractor's culture—that is likely to affect any LVLM deployed in culturally diverse real-world settings. The finding that CoT prompting can worsen consistency in mixed-culture contexts challenges the default assumption that chain-of-thought reasoning helps in multi-cue visual scenarios. Key open questions include whether SFT on culture mixing data scales to broader artifact types (clothing, rituals), what the optimal training objective is for distractor resistance, and whether the observed background reliance is an artifact of training data composition or a fundamental architectural limitation.
