## Problem

Fully Few-shot Class-incremental Audio Classification (FFCAC) requires recognizing new audio classes from only a handful of samples per class across multiple sessions while retaining old knowledge, yet existing few-shot incremental methods assume abundant base-session data. Pre-trained Audio-Language Models (ALMs) like CLAP cannot be directly applied because text-audio misalignment (e.g., speaker IDs bear no semantic relation to waveforms) and fine-tuning-induced catastrophic forgetting and overfitting degrade performance sharply across sessions. This paper is the first to adapt ALMs to FFCAC.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw audio clips (instrument sounds, environmental events, or speech) that are encoded into fixed-dimensional feature vectors by a frozen CLAP audio encoder. Training uses N-way K-shot sets with only 5 labeled samples per class per session across 5 sequential sessions (1 base + 4 incremental). The output is a single class label obtained by argmax over a softmax of cosine distances between the transformed query feature and all accumulated class prototypes.

## Method

TAPE decouples the frozen CLAP audio encoder from a lightweight Task-Adapter that computes a linear projection matrix W = P⁺R, where P is the matrix of class prototypes and R is a set of orthogonally-constrained reference vectors learned per session; this projects all features into a task-adaptive metric space where old and new class subspaces are geometrically isolated, directly mitigating catastrophic forgetting with minimal learnable parameters. During inference, a Prototype Evolution module maintains a per-class priority queue of the top-M lowest-entropy query features (assigned by pseudo-labels) and continuously blends the stored features into the class prototype via a momentum factor α, pulling prototypes toward the true class center and reducing overfitting caused by the small training set. The training loss combines a standard cross-entropy classification term with an orthogonality regularizer ‖RᵀR − I‖²_F on the reference points, and the entire pipeline requires no model expansion across sessions.

## Results

TAPE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three FFCAC benchmarks: 94.78% AA and 4.08% PD on Nsynth-100 (instrument recognition), 68.97% AA and 20.53% PD on FSC-89 (event detection), and 84.53% AA and 12.78% PD on LBS-100 (voice recognition). Averaged across datasets, TAPE improves AA from 54.93% (second-best) to 82.76% and reduces PD from 28.74% to 12.56%. The method maintains near-flat accuracy across all five incremental sessions (e.g., 97.05% → 92.97% on Nsynth-100), whereas all baselines exhibit steep accuracy drops after session 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAPE outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and both metrics (AA and PD). Among FFCAC-specific methods, EDE (Interspeech'24) and AISP (TASLP'25) are the closest competitors: on Nsynth-100, TAPE's 94.78% AA exceeds EDE's 65.40% by 29.4 points, while its 4.08% PD beats AISP's 10.94% by 6.9 points. On LBS-100 (voice recognition), the gap is even starker—TAPE achieves 84.53% AA versus EDE's 61.85%, and 12.78% PD versus AISP's 46.05%. Fine-tuned CLAP variants (PALM, COOP, COCOOP) suffer severe catastrophic forgetting, with PD values of 62.77%, 60.45%, and 52.94% on Nsynth-100 respectively, confirming the text-audio misalignment problem the paper identifies. Zero-shot CLAP performs worst overall (21.42% AA on Nsynth-100, 8.41% on LBS-100), validating that audio-language alignment is insufficient for fine-grained audio classification without task-specific adaptation. Classical incremental learning baselines (iCaRL, PODNET, DER, CEC, FACT, PAN) all show PD > 35%, indicating they are ill-suited to the fully few-shot setting where even the base session has only 5 samples per class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to apply Audio-Language Models (CLAP) to the Fully Few-shot Class-incremental Audio Classification (FFCAC) task, which was previously addressed only by EDE and AISP using expandable dual-embedding extractors that grow in size with each session. It departs from fine-tuned ALM approaches (COOP, COCOOP, PALM) that the authors show suffer severe catastrophic forgetting in incremental settings, and from zero-shot CLAP which fails due to text-audio misalignment (e.g., speaker IDs in LBS-100 have no semantic relation to audio). The Task-Adapter's orthogonal reference-point construction draws on metric-learning and prototype-based classification ideas, while the Prototype Evolution mechanism repurposes test-time adaptation concepts (priority queues with entropy-based selection) from the few-shot learning literature into an incremental audio setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that ALMs can be made practical for FFCAC without the model-size growth problem of prior expandable-encoder methods, offering a fixed-size alternative that scales to more sessions. The finding that text-audio misalignment in CLAP is a fundamental barrier (not just a tuning issue) for audio classification tasks with non-semantic labels is a useful caution for the broader ALM community. However, the evaluation is limited to three small datasets with only 5 sessions and 25 classes per run, and the encoder mismatch between baselines (AST) and the proposed method (CLAP) makes direct comparison somewhat confounded. Open questions include whether the orthogonal reference-point strategy and entropy-based prototype evolution generalize to longer incremental sequences, larger class counts, or other modalities.
