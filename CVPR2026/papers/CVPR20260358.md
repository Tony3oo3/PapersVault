## Problem

Existing multi-identity image customization methods rely on a one-to-one mapping paradigm that learns a direct correspondence between each reference identity and its generated counterpart. This approach fails to simultaneously address intra-ID variability (pose, expression changes) and inter-ID distinction (suppressing cross-identity feature leakage), causing identity confusion and degraded similarity to scale poorly as the number of identities grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the desired scene and M reference face images (one or more per identity) as input, and produces a single customized image in which all referenced identities appear with high fidelity and mutual distinction. UMO is a plug-and-play optimization layer applied on top of existing diffusion-based customization models (UNO, OmniGen2), so the input/output format is inherited from the base model. Training data pairs each target image with multiple reference images per identity, sourced from real multi-person video frames and filtered synthetic compositions.

## Method

UMO reformulates multi-identity generation as a global assignment optimization via a multi-to-multi matching paradigm. During training, ReReFL (Reference Reward Feedback Learning) extends ReFL to the customization setting by directly backpropagating a face-recognition-based reward through a partial denoising trajectory, rather than relying on SFT or GRPO-style rollouts. The key reward, MIMR (Multi-Identity Matching Reward), constructs a bipartite graph between M reference face embeddings and N detected face embeddings in the generated image, computes the optimal one-to-one assignment via the Hungarian algorithm, and then defines a composite score that positively weights matched pairs (lambda_1 > 0) and negatively weights all unmatched pairs (lambda_2 < 0), thereby simultaneously maximizing identity fidelity and inter-identity separation. The framework is model-agnostic and is applied via LoRA fine-tuning (rank 512) on top of pretrained customization models, with the reward computed using a lightweight face-recognition network (PuLID-style) that requires no additional preference data annotation.

## Results

On the Multi-Subject task of XVerseBench, UMO applied to OmniGen2 achieves the best ID-Sim (71.59) and ID-Conf (77.74), substantially outperforming the OmniGen2 baseline (40.81 / 62.02) and the strongest prior method XVerse (66.59 / 72.44). On the Single-Subject task, UMO on OmniGen2 reaches an ID-Sim of 91.57, surpassing XVerse (79.48) and OmniGen (76.51). On OmniContext, UMO on OmniGen2 attains the highest ID-Sim (7.07) and ID-Conf (7.60) among all compared methods, with an overall average of 7.28 versus 5.68 for the OmniGen2 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMO consistently outperforms all seven baselines on the multi-subject XVerseBench. The most dramatic gains are over the two pretrained baselines: UMO lifts UNO's ID-Sim from 31.82 to 69.09 (+37.27) and OmniGen2's from 40.81 to 71.59 (+30.78), while also improving ID-Conf by roughly 16–17 points in both cases. Against the strongest dedicated multi-identity method XVerse (ID-Sim 66.59, ID-Conf 72.44), UMO on OmniGen2 leads by 5.0 and 5.3 points respectively. On the single-subject XVerseBench, UMO on OmniGen2 achieves an ID-Sim of 91.57, beating XVerse (79.48) by 12.09 points and OmniGen (76.51) by 15.06 points, while retaining competitive IP-Sim (79.74). On OmniContext, UMO on OmniGen2 dominates in identity metrics (ID-Sim 7.07 vs. 3.51 for OmniGen2; ID-Conf 7.60 vs. 6.35) while maintaining a comparable overall score (7.16 vs. 7.18), confirming that the identity gains come without sacrificing prompt-following or subject consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-feedback learning line (ReFL/ImageReward) to the image customization setting, proposing ReReFL which directly backpropagates reference-based reward signals rather than using GRPO-style weighted SFT. Its core conceptual departure is the 'multi-to-multi matching paradigm,' which reformulates multi-identity generation as a bipartite assignment problem (inspired by DETR and multi-object tracking) solved via the Hungarian algorithm, in contrast to the prevailing 'one-to-one mapping' paradigm used by methods like MSDiffusion, DreamO, and OmniGen. The framework is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both UNO (in-context DiT) and OmniGen2 (unified multimodal model), and leverages PuLID-style face recognition embeddings as the reward signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-to-multi matching paradigm offers a general, plug-and-play recipe for improving identity consistency in any customization model, and the ID-Conf metric fills a gap in evaluating multi-identity confusion that prior benchmarks lacked. However, the approach is validated on only two base architectures and two benchmarks, and its reliance on face detection quality and a specific face-embedding model (PuLID) limits immediate generalization to non-face subjects or very large identity counts. Open questions include whether the matching-based reward degrades gracefully beyond 4–5 identities, whether it transfers to video generation, and whether the ReReFL training signal remains stable as the number of reference images grows.
