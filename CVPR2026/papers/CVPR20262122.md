## Problem

Existing audio-driven talking avatar methods suffer from three coupled limitations: they depend on intermediate representations (3D landmarks, pose trajectories) that restrict motion flexibility, they regulate expression via discrete emotion labels that cannot capture the continuous temporal dynamics of natural speech, and they treat text prompts as passive style descriptors rather than actionable motion-control signals. As a result, generated avatars exhibit identity drift, flat or temporally misaligned expressions, and no meaningful response to user-specified behavioral instructions such as gestures or gaze shifts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (portrait or full-body), a speech audio clip (which may include singing, humming, or non-verbal sounds), and a natural-language text prompt describing desired body behavior. The text prompt is first augmented by a vision-language model that extracts visual attributes (attire, posture, objects) from the reference image before being encoded by T5. The output is a temporally coherent talking avatar video (5–30 s) with synchronized lip motion, expressive facial dynamics, and full-body or upper-body gestures, generated without any pose or landmark supervision.

## Method

SyncDreamer is built on the Wan diffusion-transformer backbone (42 transformer blocks) and introduces three specialized conditioning modules. A Visual Adapter uses a Q-Former-style query encoder to distill identity embeddings from the reference image, injected via a cross-attention block in the DiT; an Attention Localization Loss then regularizes mid-level cross-attention maps using segmentation-derived region masks so that spatial queries attend to semantically corresponding reference tokens (e.g., mouth-to-mouth), preventing identity drift under large pose changes. An Audio Dynamics Encoder extends Wav2Vec2 with a sliding-window temporal MLP (window k=3) that produces per-frame expressiveness scores in [0,1], reweighting acoustic features to amplify rhythm accents and energy peaks while suppressing flat segments. A Cross-Modal Prompt Enhancer, built on Qwen2-VL, extracts structured visual attributes from the reference image and rewrites the user prompt into a motion-aware instruction; it is fine-tuned with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using task-specific rewards for visual grounding, gesture clarity, and temporal coherence. Training proceeds in two stages—first on HumanVID text-video pairs for motion priors, then with audio conditioning on Hallo3, HDTF, and AVSpeech—using classifier-free guidance (0.1 dropout) and 100 denoising steps.

## Results

On the HDTF portrait benchmark, SyncDreamer achieves the best scores across all five metrics: FID 52.8, FVD 508.1, Sync-C 8.04, Sync-D 6.15, and IQA 3.72, outperforming the closest competitor OmniAvatar (FID 53.7, Sync-C 7.92). On the in-the-wild AVSpeech benchmark, it similarly leads with FID 67.9, FVD 72.6, and Sync-C 6.31. On the full-body EMTD benchmark, SyncDreamer achieves FID 41.77, FVD 483.59, and a notably higher CSIM of 0.598 versus 0.393 for the best pose-driven baseline (EchoMimicV2), demonstrating strong identity preservation in full-body generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncDreamer outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated benchmark and metric. On HDTF, the closest competitor is OmniAvatar (FID 53.7, Sync-C 7.92), with SyncDreamer improving FID by 0.9 and Sync-C by 0.12; the gap widens substantially against older methods like V-Express (FID 62.8, Sync-C 4.13) and FantasyTalk (FID 62.7, Sync-C 5.48). On AVSpeech, SyncDreamer leads OmniAvatar by 0.5 FID and 0.07 Sync-C, while maintaining the best Sync-D (7.05 vs. 6.73 for OmniAvatar, though OmniAvatar edges ahead on this single metric). On the full-body EMTD benchmark, SyncDreamer surpasses EchoMimicV2 (the strongest pose-driven baseline) by 0.92 FID, 44.5 FVD, and a large 0.205 CSIM margin, indicating markedly better identity consistency in full-body generation. The pose-driven baselines (AnimateAnyone, MimicMotion, EchoMimicV2) all trail SyncDreamer on every EMTD metric, confirming that end-to-end audio+text conditioning without explicit pose representations yields superior full-body motion quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncDreamer builds on the Wan diffusion Transformer backbone for video generation, extends Wav2Vec2 with a temporal reweighting mechanism for expressive audio encoding, and adapts the Q-Former-style query encoder (from BLIP-2) for identity-preserving visual conditioning. It departs from prior audio-driven avatar methods (Hallo, EchoMimic, OmniAvatar) in two key ways: (1) it treats text as an active, RL-optimized motion-control signal rather than a passive style descriptor, and (2) it replaces discrete emotion labels and 3D pose/landmark intermediates with continuous audio-dynamics modeling and end-to-end full-body synthesis. The Attention Localization Loss is a new spatial regularization not present in prior DiT-based avatar systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying identity preservation, expressive audio dynamics, and text-driven behavioral control in a single DiT framework, SyncDreamer addresses a practical gap: prior systems either produce flat portrait-only motion or require handcrafted pose trajectories for full-body animation. The GRPO-based prompt enhancer is a promising pattern for grounding user intent in visual context, potentially transferable to other controllable generation tasks. Open questions include real-time inference (100 denoising steps on two A100s is far from interactive), scalability to multi-character scenes, and whether the Attention Localization Loss generalizes beyond facial regions to arbitrary body parts without additional segmentation supervision.
