## Problem

Text-to-image models struggle to accurately depict complex spatial relationships among multiple objects, and applying online RL to fix this is blocked by the absence of a reliable spatial reward model. Existing human-preference reward models, text-image alignment scorers, and even proprietary VLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5 Pro) exhibit hallucinations or limited accuracy when evaluating multi-object spatial correctness, making them unsuitable as reward signals for RL training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262713/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The reward model SpatialScore takes a text prompt encoding complex multi-object spatial relationships (significantly longer than GenEval's single-relation templates) together with a generated RGB image, and outputs a scalar spatial-accuracy score sampled from a learned Gaussian distribution. The RL pipeline takes the same spatial prompts and produces generated images via FLUX.1-dev, with the reward model scoring each image in a group of 24 SDE-sampled candidates to compute advantage signals for policy updates.

## Method

The paper introduces SpatialReward-Dataset, 80K adversarial preference pairs where GPT-5 perturbs one or more spatial relations in a prompt while preserving the rest, and images from the original vs. perturbed prompts form winner/loser pairs (human-verified). SpatialScore fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B with LoRA, replacing the language-modeling head with an MLP reward head that maps a special <reward> token embedding to Gaussian (μ, σ) parameters, trained via Bradley-Terry preference loss. For online RL, the authors adopt GRPO with Flow-GRPO's ODE-to-SDE conversion for stochastic sampling, and propose a top-k filtering strategy that selects only the top-k and bottom-k scored images per group to compute advantages, mitigating the bias where easy prompts' high group means assign negative advantages to genuinely good samples. This reduces NFE from 24×6 to 12×6 per prompt while maintaining or improving final performance.

## Results

On the in-domain SpatialScore metric, the proposed method improves the base Flux.1-dev from 2.18 to 7.81, far exceeding the Flow-GRPO* variant trained on GenEval (3.01). On DPG-Bench Relation-Spatial, the method achieves 0.932 vs. 0.871 (base) and 0.742 (Flow-GRPO*). On long-prompt TIIF-Bench, the method scores 0.845/0.715/0.675 (BR/AR/RR) versus 0.758/0.677/0.645 for the base model, while Flow-GRPO* degrades to 0.577/0.510/0.482. On UniGenBench++ long-prompt Layout-3D, the method reaches 0.801 vs. 0.742 (base) and 0.405 (Flow-GRPO*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the reward-model evaluation benchmark (Table 1), SpatialScore (7B) achieves 95.8% pairwise accuracy, surpassing all baselines: proprietary GPT-5 (89.0%) and Gemini-2.5 Pro (95.1%), the largest open-source VLM Qwen2.5-VL-72B (76.4%), and all image-reward models (HPSv3 65.2%, UnifiedReward 60.5%, VQAScore 60.3%, PickScore 50.9%, ImageReward 47.9%, HPSv2.1 46.3%). For end-to-end image generation (Table 2), the proposed method outperforms the Flux.1-dev base model on every spatial sub-dimension across DPG-Bench, TIIF-Bench (short and long), and UniGenBench++ (short and long), with the largest gains on long-prompt settings (e.g., TIIF-Bench-long BR: 0.845 vs. 0.758). The Flow-GRPO* variant trained on the rule-based GenEval reward degrades substantially on long prompts (e.g., UniGenBench++ long Layout-3D drops from 0.742 to 0.405), confirming that GenEval-based rewards do not generalize to complex multi-object spatial compositions. On the full DPG-Bench (Table 3), the RL-enhanced model reaches 85.03 overall, approaching the proprietary GPT-Image-1 (85.15), while Flow-GRPO* collapses to 57.02.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: (1) online RL for flow-matching/diffusion models (Flow-GRPO, Dance-GRPO, MixGRPO), which established the GRPO-based training pipeline but relied on rule-based or generic reward signals, and (2) reward modeling for T2I (HPSv2/v3, PickScore, ImageReward, UnifiedReward), which optimized for aesthetic quality or coarse text-image alignment. The key departure is the argument that neither existing reward models nor general-purpose VLMs (even Qwen2.5-VL-72B) provide reliable spatial-reasoning signals, motivating a purpose-built 80K adversarial preference dataset and a 7B VLM-based reward model (SpatialScore) trained with Bradley-Terry preference loss. The GRPO training loop itself follows Flow-GRPO's ODE-to-SDE conversion, with the addition of a top-k filtering strategy to correct advantage bias across prompts of varying difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small (7B) specialized reward model can outperform much larger proprietary VLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5 Pro) on spatial evaluation, suggesting that task-specific preference data can substitute for raw model scale in narrow evaluation settings. The negative result that GenEval-based RL actively degrades long-prompt spatial performance (Table 2) is practically important: it warns against using simple rule-based benchmarks as RL reward signals for complex compositional generation. Open questions include whether the SpatialScore reward model generalizes beyond spatial relationships to other compositional dimensions (attribute binding, counting, temporal ordering), and whether the top-k filtering strategy transfers to other reward types or group-based RL settings.
