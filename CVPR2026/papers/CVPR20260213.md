## Problem

YOLO-series detectors rely on convolutional operations with inherently local receptive fields, limiting their ability to model long-range dependencies and rich contextual information in complex scenes. While Transformers address this gap, their quadratic attention complexity is incompatible with real-time inference. State Space Models offer linear-time global modeling, but prior work (e.g., Mamba YOLO) only integrates SSM into the backbone, leaving the neck's multi-scale fusion still convolution-based and underexploiting the SSM's sequential modeling capacity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes fixed-resolution 640×640 RGB images and outputs per-object bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated via standard detection metrics (mAP, AP50, AP75, AP50:95). Training uses Mosaic and HSV augmentation with a 500-epoch SGD schedule; inference is measured on an NVIDIA V100 GPU reporting FPS alongside accuracy. The architecture is designed for resource-constrained deployment, targeting real-time throughput on safety-critical tasks such as railway pedestrian and power-tower foreign-object detection.

## Method

AKCMamba-YOLO replaces all C2f blocks in both the backbone and neck of YOLOv8 with two specialized modules: 3CAKCMamba (backbone) and 4CAKCMamba (neck), each composing three sub-components. AKCBlock performs adaptive kernel convolution with learnable sampling offsets (AKConv) to capture irregular structures with linear parameter growth. AKSS2D unfolds feature maps into four directional 1-D sequences, processes each through a selective S6 state-space block with input-dependent B, C, and Δ parameters, then merges the outputs to model long-range spatial dependencies at linear complexity. AKCAttention applies squeeze-and-exitation recalibration on top of AKConv to adaptively weight channels. The 3CAKC/4CAKC hierarchical structures (3- and 4-layer conv stacks with residual shortcuts) provide multi-scale local feature extraction, while the full 3CAKCMamba/4CAKCMamba pipeline chains local extraction → SSM long-range modeling → attention recalibration, achieving a paradigm shift from static convolution to dynamic sequential modeling across both backbone and neck.

## Results

On COCO2017 val, AKCMamba-YOLO achieves 46.3% mAP, 63.1% AP50, and 51.4% AP75 with 9.1M parameters and 14.9G FLOPs, outperforming YOLOv8-S (44.9% mAP, 28.6G FLOPs) by +1.4% mAP while reducing FLOPs by 47.9%. On the Power Tower Foreign Object Detection Dataset, it reaches 92.8% precision, 86.9% AP50, and 72.5% AP50:95. On the Railway Pedestrian Detection Dataset, it achieves 95.1% precision, 97.4% AP50, and 75.5% AP50:95, consistently leading all baselines across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AKCMamba-YOLO outperforms all ten baselines across all three datasets. On COCO2017, it surpasses the closest competitor Mamba YOLO-T by +0.9% mAP (46.3 vs 45.4) and +2.3% AP75 (51.4 vs 49.1), while also beating DAMO YOLO-S by +0.3% mAP with 60.6% fewer FLOPs (14.9G vs 37.8G). Against YOLOv8-S, it gains +1.4% mAP and +2.8% AP75 while halving FLOPs. On the Power Tower dataset, it leads YOLOv11 (92.3% precision, 40G FLOPs) by +0.5% precision with 62.5% fewer FLOPs, and edges out Mamba YOLO-T by +0.7% precision and +1.2% AP50:95. On the Railway Pedestrian dataset, the margins are tighter: it beats YOLOv11 and Mamba YOLO-T (both 94.8% precision, 75.1% AP50:95) by +0.3% precision and +0.4% AP50:95, while using only 14.9G FLOPs versus YOLOv11's 40G. The consistent pattern is that AKCMamba-YOLO achieves the best accuracy at a mid-range computational cost, never losing to any baseline on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the YOLOv8 detection framework by replacing its C2f blocks in both the backbone and neck with modules that fuse adaptive kernel convolution (derived from LDConv) and selective state space modeling (from Mamba). The most direct predecessor is Mamba YOLO (Wang et al., AAAI 2025), which integrates SSM into YOLO's backbone only; AKCMamba-YOLO departs by also replacing neck components and threading AKConv through every sub-module (AKCBlock, AKSS2D, AKCAttention), creating a more uniform adaptive-convolution-plus-SSM design. The broader lineage traces from convolutional YOLO variants toward global-context architectures, positioning SSM as a linear-complexity alternative to the quadratic-cost attention that prior YOLO-attention hybrids (e.g., GoldYOLO) relied on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a practical recipe for injecting global receptive fields into a lightweight YOLO detector without the latency penalty of self-attention, which is relevant for edge-deployed safety monitoring (railway, power infrastructure). The newly released Railway Pedestrian Detection Dataset (2,975 images) fills a gap in safety-critical pedestrian benchmarks. However, the performance gains over the closest SSM-based baseline (Mamba YOLO-T) are modest (+0.9% mAP on COCO, +0.3–0.7% precision on domain datasets), and the ablation reveals that the 3CAKC component alone actually degrades precision relative to the C2f baseline, suggesting the SSM and attention components carry most of the benefit. Open questions include whether the gains persist at larger model scales, whether the four-directional scan in AKSS2D is necessary versus a single or dual scan, and how the architecture behaves under video or multi-modal settings.
