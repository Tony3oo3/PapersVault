## Problem

Existing GRPO-based alignment for visual generation assigns a single scalar advantage to each entire image or video, treating all pixels as contributing uniformly to perceptual quality. This coarse, undifferentiated credit assignment ignores the rich spatial and temporal structure of visual content, making it impossible to correct localized artifacts or model fine-grained perceptual cues, and can amplify misleading gradients in irrelevant regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262139/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts as input and produces generated images (trained at 512×512, evaluated at 1024×1024 with FLUX.1-dev) or videos (trained at 240×416×53, evaluated at 480×832×53 with Wan2.1-T2V-14B-480P). During training, each prompt yields a group of G samples (G=12 for images, G=8 for videos) that are scored by a reward model (HPSv2.1 or VideoAlign) and simultaneously processed by the Perceptual Structuring Module to produce per-pixel allocation maps at latent resolution. The allocation maps are the key intermediate output that transforms the scalar reward signal into a spatially structured advantage field.

## Method

ViPO introduces a Perceptual Structuring Module (PSM) that decomposes the scalar GRPO advantage into pixel-level, preference-aware advantages. The PSM consists of a Visual Preference Extractor (VPE) that uses a pretrained vision backbone (DINOv2, SAM, or ResNet) to produce feature embeddings, followed by PCA to retain K=3 dominant components, and a Visual Preference Allocator (VPA) that aggregates these components via variance-weighted summation into a spatial allocation map M. The core reformulation multiplies the allocation map with the scalar group advantage (A_i^p = M(p)·A_i), so that gradient updates are concentrated on perceptually salient regions while preserving the sign and relative ordering of the original GRPO advantage. For video, allocation maps are computed per frame and temporally aligned into a spatio-temporal volume. The approach is plug-and-play with existing GRPO pipelines, requires no dense annotations or region labels, and is validated on both diffusion (FLUX) and flow-matching (Wan2.1) generators under SDE sampling.

## Results

On the HPD image benchmark, ViPO(DINO) achieves the best in-domain HPSv2.1 score of 0.3321 and out-of-domain ImageReward of 1.1883, surpassing both the base FLUX model (0.3121 / 1.1495) and DanceGRPO (0.3203 / 1.0392). The ResNet-based variant attains the highest out-of-domain PickScore (22.8492). On the VidProM video benchmark, ViPO reaches a VBench Total score of 81.70 with VQ of 3.5501 and MQ of 1.1515, outperforming both Wan2.1 (80.92) and DanceGRPO (80.84) across all in-domain and out-of-domain metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base FLUX model, ViPO(DINO) improves HPSv2.1 by +0.020, PickScore by +0.127, and ImageReward by +0.039, confirming that structured advantage redistribution yields net gains over the unaligned generator. Compared to DanceGRPO, ViPO(DINO) wins on all three metrics, with the largest margin on ImageReward (+0.149) and PickScore (+0.234); notably, DanceGRPO actually degrades PickScore and ImageReward relative to base FLUX, whereas ViPO improves both. On the video side (Wan2.1), ViPO outperforms DanceGRPO by +0.457 in VQ, +0.288 in MQ, and +0.86 in VBench Total, while also surpassing the unaligned Wan2.1 baseline on every metric. The consistent advantage across in-domain and out-of-domain evaluations indicates that perceptual structuring generalizes beyond the specific reward model used during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViPO extends the GRPO lineage from DeepSeek-R1 and its visual-generation adaptations (DanceGRPO, FlowGRPO) by replacing the single scalar advantage per sample with a spatially and temporally resolved advantage map. The key conceptual departure is the Perceptual Structuring Module, which borrows from the visual perception literature (saliency models, meaning maps, perceptual loss) to decompose frozen backbone features via PCA into region-wise weighting factors, thereby introducing a spatial credit-assignment mechanism absent from prior GRPO-based visual alignment work. Unlike earlier RL-for-diffusion methods (DPOK, ReFL) that optimize the score function directly, ViPO preserves the group-relative formulation and SDE sampling scaffold of DanceGRPO/FlowGRPO while only modifying how the advantage is distributed across pixels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and widely felt limitation—scalar rewards collapse all spatial evidence into one number—offering a lightweight, architecture-agnostic fix that drops into existing GRPO training loops without additional annotation or reward-model changes. The consistent (if modest) gains across image and video backbones, in-domain and out-of-domain metrics, and the redness-reward robustness experiment suggest the approach is not an artifact of a particular reward model. Open questions include whether richer perceptual models (e.g., attention-based or learned allocators) could yield substantially larger gains, whether the PCA-based decomposition is truly capturing human perceptual relevance or merely providing a reasonable spatial prior, and how the method behaves under 3D or multi-view generation where spatial credit assignment becomes even more complex.
