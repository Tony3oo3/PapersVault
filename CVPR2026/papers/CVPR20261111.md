## Problem

Existing multimodal sentiment analysis methods rely on uniform attention that treats all modalities as equally trustworthy, causing significant performance degradation when signals conflict, contain noise, or are missing. No prior framework provides a principled, sample-specific mechanism to assess per-modality reliability before deciding how to fuse them, leaving models vulnerable to cross-modal inconsistencies such as sarcasm or sensor degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261111/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes aligned text utterances, video clips, and audio signals from each sample, processed through Transformer-based unimodal encoders with a MixDomainAdapter that disentangles task-specific features from domain-related features at different layers. Beyond the standard sentiment intensity score (regression) and class label (classification), each encoder additionally emits a scalar confidence score s_m ∈ [0,1] and a bounded absolute-error estimate u_m ∈ [0,1] per sample, which serve as continuous control signals to the downstream fusion module rather than being used for direct prediction.

## Method

CICA implements a two-phase 'perceive-and-decide' pipeline. In Phase 1 (CAP), each unimodal encoder is pretrained with three auxiliary heads under a joint objective: a task head for unimodal sentiment prediction, a confidence head trained with an Adaptive CA loss that uses learnable boundary parameters (α, β) and piecewise penalty weights to calibrate confidence scores, and an uncertainty head that regresses the detached absolute prediction error. In Phase 2 (CIF), the frozen encoders feed features and reliability signals into a fusion module where a Confidence-Informed Attention mechanism first computes an Intrinsic Structure Modulator (capturing token-level saliency and local contextual dependencies) added to standard QK attention, then applies a gating function r_m = ReLU(1 + s_m − u_m) to continuously scale each modality's contribution. A Mutual-information Contrastive Preservation (MCP) loss, inspired by Noise-Contrastive Estimation, regularizes the fused representation to retain unique information from all modalities and prevent modality collapse.

## Results

CICA achieves state-of-the-art results on all four benchmarks. On MOSI, it reaches MAE 0.630 and Corr 0.855 (beating the prior best 3WD-DRT by 0.071 MAE and 0.060 Corr); on MOSEI, MAE 0.489 and Corr 0.856 (improving Corr by 0.077 over the best baseline). The gains are most pronounced on the Chinese datasets: CH-SIMS Corr jumps from 0.627 to 0.754 (+0.127) and CH-SIMSv2 Acc-3 rises from 76.21% to 80.56% (+4.35 pp). Classification metrics (Acc-7, Acc-2, F1) also improve consistently across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CICA outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric across all four datasets, with no losses or ties. The strongest prior competitor is 3WD-DRT [9], which CICA surpasses by 0.071 MAE and 0.060 Corr on MOSI, 0.029 MAE and 0.077 Corr on MOSEI, and 0.030 MAE and 0.127 Corr on CH-SIMS. On CH-SIMSv2, the best baseline is KuDA [3] (MAE 0.271, Corr 0.759), which CICA improves by 0.026 MAE and 0.078 Corr. Among Transformer-based fusion models, MulT [14] and MISA [7] are the closest in the mid-range (Corr ~0.70–0.78 on MOSI), but CICA extends their Corr by 0.06–0.15 points. The largest relative gains appear on the Chinese benchmarks (CH-SIMS Corr +0.127, Acc-3 +9.73 pp), suggesting the confidence-gating mechanism is particularly effective when modality misalignment is more prevalent.

## Relation to Prior Work

CICA sits at the intersection of three prior lines: (1) standard transformer-based multimodal fusion (MulT, MISA, SelfMM), which treats all modalities as equally reliable; (2) guided/asymmetric fusion (ALMT, KuDA, 3WD-DRT), which introduces modality-specific weighting but typically relies on a single confidence score mapped to discrete decision regions; and (3) contrastive representation learning (Contrastive Predictive Coding / NCE), repurposed here as a modality-collapse regularizer. The key conceptual departure is the tight coupling of a perception stage (CAP, where each encoder self-assesses dual reliability signals: adaptive confidence and predicted error) with a decision stage (CIF, where those signals continuously modulate attention outputs), replacing the discrete accept/defer/reject logic of 3WD-DRT with a smoother, sample-specific gating mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'perceive-and-decide' design principle—making fusion weights a direct function of encoder self-assessed reliability—offers a clean, interpretable recipe that could transfer to other multimodal tasks beyond sentiment (e.g., video understanding, medical imaging). The robustness results under synthetic noise and modality masking are practically important for deployment in real-world sensor-degraded settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than three modalities, whether the frozen-encoder two-phase training is necessary versus end-to-end joint training, and whether the text-dominance observed on MOSI (where T-only slightly beats T+V+A) indicates a ceiling that the MCP loss cannot fully overcome on highly text-driven datasets.
