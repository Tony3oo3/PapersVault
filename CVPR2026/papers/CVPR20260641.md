## Problem

Pure vision foundation models (VFMs) such as DINOv2 have consistently underperformed vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP in zero-shot anomaly detection, despite arguably being better suited to the fundamentally visual nature of the task. The authors attribute this gap to two practical limitations: existing auxiliary anomaly datasets lack the diversity needed to train VFMs on broadly applicable cues, and prior VFM adaptation strategies only fine-tune a small output head, leaving internal visual representations unchanged.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260641/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, a single RGB image is resized to 768×768 and passed through the adapted VFM backbone, producing two outputs simultaneously: a pixel-level anomaly segmentation mask (same spatial resolution as input) and a scalar image-level anomaly score derived from the [CLS] token. Training uses 10,000 synthetic 1024×1024 image triplets (anomaly-free image, anomalous image, binary mask) generated entirely by FLUX with no real-world samples. The full model has 345.8M parameters with only 35.4M trainable, and inference runs in ~20.5 ms on an A100 GPU.

## Method

AnomalyVFM combines a three-stage synthetic data generator with a parameter-efficient VFM adaptation scheme. The data pipeline uses FLUX to generate diverse anomaly-free object images (100 object classes × 50 backgrounds), inpaints realistic local defects via the RePaint approach at randomly sampled foreground locations (extracted by IS-Net), and filters failed generations by comparing DINOv2 feature cosine distances between the normal and anomalous images. For adaptation, LoRA modules (rank 64) are injected into the query, value, and output projection layers of every transformer block in the backbone, coupled with a two-stage convolutional decoder that upsamples features to produce both the anomaly mask and a confidence map. A confidence-weighted segmentation loss (L1 + Focal, weighted by the predicted confidence with a log-regulariser) downweights noisy gradients from imperfect synthetic labels, while a Focal loss handles the image-level classification. The framework is model-agnostic and demonstrated across DINOv2, DINOv3, and RADIO backbones, achieving 94.1% average image-level AUROC across 9 industrial datasets.

## Results

On 9 industrial anomaly detection datasets, AnomalyVFM (with RADIO backbone) achieves an average image-level AUROC of 94.1% and F1-Max of 87.6%, surpassing the previous best method (Bayes-PFL) by 3.3 percentage points in image-level AUROC. For anomaly localisation, it reaches 96.9% pixel-level AUROC and 44.3% pixel-level F1-Max, improving over prior methods by 0.9 p.p. in pixel-level AUROC. On 9 medical datasets (without any medical-specific fine-tuning), AnomalyVFM achieves 94.0% average image-level AUROC and 90.0% pixel-level AUROC, improving localisation by 1.2 p.p. over the best prior method. In the few-shot regime (1–4 normal samples), it reaches 98.2% image-level AUROC on MVTec AD (4-shot), matching or exceeding dedicated few-shot methods like INP-Former.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnomalyVFM outperforms all seven zero-shot VLM-based baselines (SAA, WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP, AACLIP, Bayes-PFL, FAPrompt) on average image-level AUROC across 9 industrial datasets, with the largest margin over Bayes-PFL (94.1% vs. 90.8%, +3.3 p.p.) and SAA (94.1% vs. 69.5%, +24.6 p.p.). On pixel-level AUROC, AnomalyVFM leads all baselines (96.9% vs. 96.0% for Bayes-PFL), though AdaCLIP achieves a higher pixel-level F1-Max (51.0 vs. 44.3) at the cost of lower pixel-level AUROC. In the medical domain, AnomalyVFM (94.0% image-level AUROC) is competitive with Bayes-PFL (94.9%) and FAPrompt (95.0%) but leads in pixel-level AUROC (90.0% vs. 88.8% for AdaCLIP, which was additionally trained on medical data). In the few-shot setting, AnomalyVFM surpasses INP-Former on MVTec AD in all shot settings (e.g., 97.2% vs. 96.1% at 1-shot) and on VisA at 1-shot (93.8% vs. 91.4%), but trails INP-Former on VisA at 2-shot (94.2% vs. 94.6%) and 4-shot (94.5% vs. 96.4%). It also outperforms PatchCore and PromptAD across all few-shot settings on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VLM-based paradigm for zero-shot anomaly detection (AdaCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, WinCLIP, Bayes-PFL, FAPrompt), which relies on CLIP's image-text pretraining to encode generic normality/abnormality concepts. It also extends prior VFM-based attempts (SAA, early DINOv2 fine-tuning) that underperformed by addressing two specific gaps: insufficient data diversity and overly shallow adaptation (training only a small output head). The method combines FLUX-based synthetic data generation with RePaint inpainting, LoRA adapters injected throughout the transformer backbone, and a confidence-weighted pixel loss, positioning itself as a model-agnostic framework applicable to any transformer-based VFM (demonstrated on DINOv2, DINOv3, and RADIO).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result challenges the working assumption that vision-language models' high-level concept knowledge is necessary for competitive zero-shot anomaly detection, showing that sufficiently diverse synthetic data plus deeper parameter-efficient adaptation of pure visual backbones is sufficient. The 4× inference speed advantage over AdaCLIP (20.5 ms vs 82.4 ms) and the model-agnostic design make this practically attractive for deployment. Key limitations include the ~1-day generation bottleneck on a single A100, pixel-level F1-Max still trailing AdaCLIP, and reliance on an external salient object segmentation model for anomaly placement. Open directions noted by the authors include improving defect realism, extending to RGBD via monodepth models, and using AnomalyVFM as a backbone for full-shot methods.
