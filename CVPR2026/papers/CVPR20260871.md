## Problem

Creating simulation-ready articulated 3D assets requires expert modeling of part hierarchies and kinematic structures, yet existing generative methods either produce fused shapes lacking kinematic consistency, depend on multi-state images or templates for reliable part segmentation, or require explicit kinematic parameters as input. No prior method reconstructs both kinematic part-level geometry and full URDF parameters from a single RGB image in an end-to-end framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260871/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a set of K part-level 3D meshes, a composed complete articulated mesh, full hierarchical URDF parameters (joint type, axis, origin, and motion limits), and textured meshes aligned via ICP. Internally, a VLM (GPT-4o) generates per-part reference images, a predicted open-state image, and a coarse URDF template that condition the downstream generation and optimization stages. The final output is directly loadable into physics simulators such as Isaac Sim for robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

SPARK is a three-stage pipeline combining VLM priors with a diffusion transformer and differentiable optimization. First, a VLM extracts coarse URDF parameters (discrete attributes like joint type and axis are selected from predefined dictionaries; continuous attributes like joint origin are coarsely estimated) and generates per-part reference images plus an open-state image. Second, a DiT (inspired by TripoSG) trained with Rectified Flow matching synthesizes part-level geometry latents using a multi-level attention scheme: local cross-attention to per-part DINOv2 embeddings, global cross-attention to the whole-object embedding, and a hierarchical parent-child attention that exchanges information along the VLM-inferred link tree, all stabilized by dual position embeddings (relative index + absolute part identity). Third, a differentiable forward-kinematics-and-rendering module refines continuous joint parameters (origin offset and rotation angle) by minimizing a combined region-overlap and gradient-edge silhouette loss against the VLM-predicted open-state image, while a VLM re-prediction step corrects discrete attributes (axis, type) using the coarse URDF as additional context.

## Results

On the GAPartNet test set (100 images, 25 categories), SPARK achieves the best shape reconstruction with a Chamfer Distance of 0.3915 and F-Score@0.1 of 0.4151, outperforming PartCrafter (CD 0.4342, F@0.1 0.3600) and OmniPart (CD 0.4971, F@0.1 0.1928). For URDF parameter estimation, SPARK achieves AxisErr of 0.1577, PivotErr of 0.1653, and TypeErr of 0.0500, substantially outperforming Articulate-Anything (AxisErr 0.5491, PivotErr 0.3529, TypeErr 0.2500) and Articulate AnyMesh (AxisErr 1.1834, PivotErr 0.9162, TypeErr 0.7000). The advantage is most pronounced at the strict F-Score@0.1 threshold and on continuous URDF parameters (axis and pivot), where the differentiable joint optimization module provides the largest gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARK outperforms all quantitative baselines on every reported metric. In shape reconstruction (Table 1), PartCrafter is the closest competitor, trailing SPARK by 0.0437 in CD and 0.0551 in F-Score@0.1, while OmniPart lags further behind (F@0.1 of 0.1928 vs. 0.4151) due to its sensitivity to segmentation noise and bounding-box misalignment. URDFormer performs worst overall (CD 1.0556, F@0.1 0.0438) because its template-retrieval strategy frequently misidentifies part types. In URDF estimation (Table 2), SPARK reduces AxisErr by 71% relative to Articulate-Anything (0.1577 vs. 0.5491) and by 87% relative to Articulate AnyMesh (0.1577 vs. 1.1834), with TypeErr dropping from 0.2500/0.7000 to 0.0500. ArtFormer and CAGE are compared only qualitatively (Figure 4); the paper reports that these text-conditioned methods produce less physically consistent articulation motions compared to SPARK's optimization-refined URDF parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPARK sits at the intersection of part-level 3D generation (PartCrafter, OmniPart, DualPacker), articulated object synthesis (MeshArt, ArtFormer, CAGE), and URDF estimation (Articulate-Anything, URDFformer). It departs from prior part-generation methods by conditioning a DiT (architecturally inspired by TripoSG) on VLM-derived part images and a structural graph rather than bounding boxes or 2D segmentations, and from prior URDF estimators by using a single closed-state image plus a VLM-predicted open-state image rather than multi-state videos or 3D meshes. The differentiable forward-kinematics refinement module extends the VLM-reprediction idea from Articulate-Anything and DreamArt into a continuous optimization loop with differentiable rendering, closing the gap between coarse VLM estimates and physically consistent joint parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Simulation-ready articulated assets with accurate URDF parameters are a bottleneck for embodied AI and robotic manipulation, and SPARK's single-image pipeline substantially lowers the creation barrier compared to multi-state or mesh-retrieval approaches. The demonstrated downstream application (training a robot to open a drawer in Isaac Sim) validates the practical utility of the generated assets. Key limitations include support only for single-DOF revolute/prismatic joints (no multi-DOF, compound mechanisms, or closed chains), dependence on VLM quality for structural reasoning, and evaluation on a modest 100-image test set. Open questions include whether the VLM-guided approach generalizes to novel or highly articulated objects beyond the 46 PartNet-Mobility categories, and whether the differentiable optimization can scale to more complex kinematic chains.
