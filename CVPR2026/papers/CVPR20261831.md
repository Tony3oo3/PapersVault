## Problem

Existing cross-subject fMRI pretraining methods either impose arbitrary spatial patchification on brain volumes or project whole-brain activity into unstructured shared latent spaces via subject-specific adapters. Both paradigms ignore the brain's intrinsic functional architecture organized around regions of interest (ROIs), overlooking voxel-level redundancy and inter-regional functional correlations, which leads to suboptimal few-shot transfer and poor interpretability of which brain regions drive decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261831/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input consists of fMRI scans parcellated into M anatomically defined ROIs (e.g., V1–V3, hV4, FFA, PPA, OPA, RSC), each containing several hundred voxels; a subject-specific linear embedder maps each ROI to a shared-dimension (D=400) token. Output includes reconstructed images at 4×64×64 latent resolution refined to full resolution via image-to-image diffusion (strength 0.75), 300/1000-way retrieval matches, and a sequence of MRL-ordered ROI embeddings where earlier tokens carry progressively more informative visual content than later ones.

## Method

ROITok tokenizes fMRI at the ROI level and processes the resulting token sequence through an 8-block Residual MLP encoder under a Sparse ROI Context Fusion objective: 50% of ROI tokens are randomly masked and replaced with a learnable [MASK] token, forcing the encoder to reconstruct visual information from the remaining ROIs and thereby learn inter-regional functional synergy. An MRL-inspired compression function F(Z,m) randomly truncates the embedding sequence at a uniform m ∈ {1,…,M′}, zeroing later positions with a learnable empty token, which trains earlier components to capture the most salient visual features and induces a hierarchy of information density across tokens. The joint training objective combines SoftCLIP contrastive loss, MSE semantic reconstruction loss, and L1 loss against Stable Diffusion VAE latents. For downstream generation, the pretrained ROI embeddings are decoupled from the encoder and fed into either an IP-Adapter (cross-attention injection into a frozen SD U-Net) or a diffusion-prior module (predicting CLIP vision tokens to condition a frozen generative model). A two-stage finetuning protocol—first training only new ROI embedders for 5,000 steps with all pretrained modules frozen, then jointly fine-tuning all parameters for 5,000 steps—enables strong cross-subject transfer from as little as 1 hour of new-subject data.

## Results

On NSD (40h, 4 subjects), ROITok achieves the highest low-level reconstruction fidelity: PixCorr 0.475 (IP-Adapter) and 0.470 (Diffusion Prior), substantially outperforming the next best NeuroSwift (0.335) and MindTuner (0.322). For high-level features, ROITok (Diffusion Prior) leads with Alex(5) 99.0%, Inception 95.7%, and CLIP 95.2%, while ROITok (IP-Adapter) achieves the best SSIM (0.426). On 1-hour transfer, ROITok (IP-Adapter) reaches PixCorr 0.341 and 86.9% image retrieval, surpassing MindTuner (0.224, 83.1%) and NeuroSwift (0.253, n/a). On the cross-dataset GOD benchmark, ROITok (IP-Adapter) attains the best PixCorr (0.321) versus STTM (0.253) and CMVDM (0.279).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROITok decisively outperforms all baselines on low-level pixel reconstruction: its PixCorr of 0.475 (IP-Adapter) exceeds NeuroSwift (0.335) by 42% and MindTuner (0.322) by 47% on NSD 40h, and the 1-hour transfer gap is even larger (0.341 vs. 0.253 for NeuroSwift). On high-level semantic features, ROITok (Diffusion Prior) leads in Alex(5) (99.0% vs. 98.8% MindTuner) and CLIP (95.2% vs. 97.1% NeuroSwift, though NeuroSwift lacks retrieval numbers), while ROITok (IP-Adapter) trails slightly on CLIP (92.9%) but matches or exceeds all methods on SSIM (0.426). For 300-way retrieval, ROITok (98.7%/98.7%) is essentially tied with MindTuner (98.9%/98.3%), the strongest retrieval baseline. On the 1-hour few-shot setting, ROITok's advantage is most pronounced: it achieves 86.9% image retrieval versus 83.1% (MindTuner) and 79.0% (MindEye2), and its PixCorr of 0.341 is 35% higher than NeuroSwift's 0.253. On the cross-dataset GOD benchmark, ROITok (IP-Adapter) leads in PixCorr (0.321 vs. 0.279 CMVDM, 0.253 STTM), while ROITok (Diffusion Prior) achieves the best Alex(5) (96.8% vs. 95.6% STTM), confirming cross-dataset generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant cross-subject fMRI pretraining paradigm that uses subject-specific adapters (MindEye2, STTM, MindTuner) or arbitrary spatial patchification (fMRI-PTE, NeuroPictor) to align neural signals into a shared latent space. Instead, ROITok reorients pretraining around anatomically defined Regions of Interest as computational tokens, arguing that both prior strategies ignore the brain's functional topology and inter-ROI redundancy. The MRL-inspired embedding compression strategy is borrowed from Kusupati et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and repurposed to impose a saliency-ordered hierarchy on fMRI component tokens, while the Residual MLP backbone is chosen for memory efficiency over attention-based encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROITok offers a level of neuroscientific interpretability absent in prior fMRI decoding work: it enables quantitative per-ROI contribution analysis, reveals that the model automatically recovers the brain's visual hierarchy via RSA, and supports controllable decoding from synthetic ROI patterns. The practical efficiency gain (single H800 GPU, ~30 hours vs. 8×A100 for MindEye2) lowers the barrier for the community. Open questions include whether the learned shared space captures non-linear cross-subject correspondences, whether the synthetic noise robustness translates to physiological artifacts (cardiac, motion), and whether the ROI-based formulation generalizes beyond visual cortex to other sensory or cognitive domains.
