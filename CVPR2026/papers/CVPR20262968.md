## Problem

Existing monocular 3D detectors regress the seven degrees-of-freedom of a 3D bounding box through separate heads, ignoring the geometric collaborative constraints that link center, depth, dimensions, and rotation. This decoupled paradigm produces predictions that violate physical spatial relationships and 3D-to-2D projection consistency, limiting localization accuracy. No prior framework explicitly enforces unified geometric coherence across all box attributes during end-to-end training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image and outputs per-object 3D bounding boxes parameterized as (x, y, z, h, w, l, r_y) in camera coordinates, alongside 2D detection boxes and class labels. SPAN operates as a plug-and-play training-time module: it derives eight 3D corner points from the predicted 7-DoF parameters, uses them for spatial and projection alignment losses, and introduces no additional inference-time computation or architectural changes to the underlying detector.

## Method

SPAN introduces two geometric collaborative losses applied to the eight corners computed from the primary 7-DoF prediction. Spatial Point Alignment projects each corner onto three face-normal axes and computes a marginalized GIoU (MGIoU) between predicted and ground-truth corner sets, yielding a differentiable 3D overlap score that regularizes all box parameters jointly. 3D-2D Projection Alignment projects the same corners onto the image plane via the pinhole camera model, forms the horizontally-aligned minimal enclosing rectangle of the projection, and penalizes its 2D GIoU discrepancy with the corresponding 2D detection box. To prevent early-training instability from noisy 3D predictions invalidating these high-order constraints, a Hierarchical Task Learning schedule organizes training into four sequential stages (2D detection → 3D dimensions/rotation → depth → spatial-projection alignment), dynamically up-weighting each geometric loss only after its prerequisite tasks have converged.

## Results

On the KITTI test set (Car category), MonoDGP (+SPAN) achieves 19.30% AP_3D (Moderate) and 16.49% AP_3D (Hard), improving over the MonoDGP baseline by +0.58% and +0.52% respectively. On the validation set, the gains are larger: +0.92% (Moderate) and +1.15% (Hard) AP_3D over the baseline. The method also outperforms detectors trained with extra LiDAR or depth data, surpassing OccupancyM3D by 2.28% in moderate AP_3D on the test set. When applied to MonoDETR and MoVis, SPAN yields consistent gains of +0.61–0.82% (Moderate AP_3D) on the validation set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAN is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on three baselines (MonoDETR, MoVis, MonoDGP) and consistently improves all of them. Against the strongest no-extra-data baseline MonoDGP, SPAN gains +0.58% (test) and +0.92% (val) in moderate AP_3D for Car, with even larger gains on Hard (+0.52% test, +1.15% val). It also surpasses detectors that rely on additional LiDAR (OccupancyM3D, +2.28% test Mod. AP_3D) or depth (OPA-3D, +2.25% test Mod. AP_3D) supervision. On Pedestrian and Cyclist categories (KITTI test), MonoDGP (+SPAN) achieves the best AP_3D across all difficulty levels, beating the second-best by up to +1.58% (Cyclist Easy) and +0.87% (Cyclist Mod.). When applied to MonoDETR and MoVis, SPAN yields +0.61–0.82% moderate AP_3D gains on the val set, confirming generality across different detector architectures. The ablation (Table 4) shows that neither Spatial Point Alignment nor 3D-2D Projection Alignment alone improves performance without Hierarchical Task Learning, underscoring that the training schedule is essential for the geometric constraints to be beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric-constraint lineage in monocular 3D detection (Deep3DBox, Shift R-CNN, RTM3D) by replacing brittle analytical solvers with differentiable, gradient-based losses applied directly to the primary 7-DoF regression parameters. It departs from prior corner-regression auxiliary tasks (ROI-10D, MonoDIS) by using MGIoU [10] to enforce spatial alignment on the main box parameters rather than treating corners as a separate prediction head, and from global homography or uncertainty-propagation approaches (Homography Loss, GUPNet++) by imposing explicit per-object 3D-2D projection consistency. The Hierarchical Task Learning scheduling is adapted from GUPNet [21] to gate when these high-order geometric constraints become active during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPAN offers a plug-and-play geometric regularization that improves three architecturally distinct monocular 3D detectors without any inference-time overhead, making it immediately adoptable by practitioners working on KITTI-style benchmarks. The ablation revealing that geometric constraints without staged scheduling actively degrade performance underscores a practical training-dynamics lesson for anyone adding auxiliary geometric losses. Limitations include evaluation primarily on KITTI (with Waymo only in the appendix), sharp performance collapse under >15px 2D noise, and the approach's reliance on accurate camera intrinsics. The authors note future extension to multi-view 3D perception as an open direction.
