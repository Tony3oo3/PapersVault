## Problem

Existing two-stage HDR video reconstruction methods suffer from two coupled failures in dynamic scenes: the first stage relies on simple alignment strategies (generic optical flow or global motion estimation) that produce inaccurate flow for occluded and saturated regions across alternating exposures, and the second stage lacks targeted guidance to suppress ghosting and noise in motion-dominated areas. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a flow adapter that adapts pretrained optical flow for cross-exposure alignment and a physical motion modeling module that extracts continuous, physically meaningful motion cues to guide refinement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three consecutive LDR frames (L_{t-1}, L_t, L_{t+1}) with alternating long/short exposures and their scalar exposure values, mapping each to the linear HDR domain via gamma correction (γ=2.2) and exposure normalization. During training, frames are cropped to 256×256 patches; at inference the full frame is processed on a single GPU. The output is a single reconstructed HDR frame H_final in the linear HDR domain, evaluated after μ-law tonemapping (μ=5000) for perceptual metrics and directly for linear-domain metrics.

## Method

F²HDR is a two-stage CNN pipeline. In Stage I (coarse fusion), a lightweight Flow Adapter—a shallow residual CNN with dilated convolutions—takes the output of a frozen SEA-RAFT pretrained flow network as a coarse prior and predicts residual flow offsets (scaled by λ=20) to correct misalignment in over/under-exposed and occluded regions; the refined flow warps neighboring HDR-domain frames, which are then fused with the reference and original neighbors via a U-Net that outputs five learned weight maps. Simultaneously, a Physical Motion Modeling module decomposes the flow field into translation magnitude, divergence, curl, and shear via Sobel derivatives, combines them with learned adaptive weights into a motion energy, applies multiscale center-surround contrast, and thresholds with an adaptive Otsu-style rule to produce a differentiable soft motion mask. In Stage II (refinement), features from both LDR and HDR inputs are extracted, flow-aligned, and modulated element-wise by motion features (sigmoid of convolved flow magnitude and mask), then enhanced through three dilated branches and fused with coarse-HDR features before a decoder produces the final HDR frame. The entire network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss on μ-law tonemapped outputs, so the motion mask and flow adapter are jointly optimized for reconstruction quality.

## Results

On the DeepHDRVideo benchmark, F²HDR achieves the best PSNR_T of 43.87 dB and the best HDR-VDP-2 score of 78.88, surpassing the second-best method NECHDR by 0.43 dB in PSNR_T. On the Real-HDRV real-world benchmark, F²HDR obtains the highest PSNR_T (41.01 dB), SSIM_T (0.9538), HDR-VDP-2 (76.93), and PSNR_L (50.51 dB) among all compared methods. Inference time is 0.290 s per frame at 1920×1080, substantially faster than Chen21 (0.570 s) and LAN-HDR (0.905 s) while remaining competitive with NECHDR (0.160 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

F²HDR outperforms all 10 baselines on PSNR_T across both benchmarks. Against the closest two-stage competitor NECHDR [6], F²HDR gains +0.43 dB PSNR_T and +1.60 HDR-VDP-2 on DeepHDRVideo, and +0.13 dB PSNR_T on Real-HDRV. Compared to the other two-stage method Chen21 [2], the improvement is +0.55 dB PSNR_T and +0.51 HDR-VDP-2 on DeepHDRVideo. Against single-stage video methods, F²HDR leads HDRFlow [35] by +0.84 dB PSNR_T and LAN-HDR [5] by +2.04 dB PSNR_T on DeepHDRVideo. The diffusion-based HDR-V-Diff [8] achieves the highest SSIM_T (0.9604) but trails F²HDR by 1.80 dB in PSNR_T, and its HDR-VDP-2 score is unavailable. All four image-based methods (Kalantari13, AHDRNet, Prabhakar, HDR-Transformer) are outperformed by 2.4–3.6 dB in PSNR_T, confirming the advantage of video-specific temporal modeling over frame-level fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage coarse-to-fine HDR video reconstruction paradigm established by Chen et al. (ICCV 2021) and Cui et al. (ACM MM 2024), but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, rather than retraining a task-specific flow network or using simple global motion estimation for alignment, it introduces a lightweight residual flow adapter on top of the pretrained SEA-RAFT model, preserving fine edge details while adapting to cross-exposure and occlusion challenges. Second, instead of learned binary masks or exposure-based masks to guide refinement, it derives a continuous motion mask from the physical decomposition of the optical flow field (translation, divergence, curl, shear), providing interpretable and differentiable guidance for artifact suppression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The flow adapter offers a practical, low-cost strategy for adapting general-purpose optical flow to domain-specific alignment tasks, a pattern that could transfer to other multi-exposure or multi-modality registration problems. The physical motion modeling provides an interpretable alternative to purely learned masks, though its advantage over a standard CNN-learned mask is modest (0.11 dB on DeepHDRVideo). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two alternating exposures, whether the physical decomposition remains informative under very large or non-rigid motions, and whether the method can be made real-time for on-device deployment given its 0.29 s inference at 1080p.
