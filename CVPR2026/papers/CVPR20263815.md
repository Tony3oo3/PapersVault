## Problem

Existing MLLM-based referring video segmentation methods rely on offline pipelines that require full video access, carefully designed frame sampling, and a separate mask propagation stage, making them incompatible with real-time streaming applications. The paper addresses how to extend image-level MLLM segmentation to video-level in a streaming fashion without introducing extra parameters or preprocessing steps, while maintaining temporal semantic consistency across frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263815/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video as a sequential stream of RGB frames (no pre-sampling) together with a single referring text expression, and produces a per-frame binary segmentation mask in real time (~7 FPS on 1×A800). Starting from the second frame, the MLLM is not invoked on every frame; instead, SAM2 propagates the mask using the recycled [INFO] token, and the MLLM is re-invoked only when the OMCP strategy detects a drop in mask confidence or inter-frame consistency below predefined thresholds (τ₁, τ₂).

## Method

StreamingRVOS builds on an InternVL2.5 MLLM (1B or 4B) coupled with SAM2 as the segmentation assistant, introducing two key mechanisms without adding parameters. First, Semantic Embedding Recycling (SER) repurposes the [SEG] token generated by the MLLM for the previous frame as an [INFO] token fed back into the MLLM input, so the model retains a condensed representation of the foreground target across frames. Second, Online Mask Consistency Perception (OMCP) computes the predicted IoU of the current mask (P_i) and the IoU between consecutive masks (P_c); if both exceed thresholds, the MLLM is skipped and SAM2 propagates the mask, otherwise the MLLM is re-invoked with an explicit contextual instruction to regenerate the [SEG] token. Training follows a two-stage streaming pipeline: joint optimization on mixed image/video data, followed by video semantic fine-tuning under OMCP conditions to align training with the adaptive inference behavior. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a combined text cross-entropy and mask (BCE + DICE) loss, using LoRA on the LLM while freezing the vision encoder.

## Results

StreamingRVOS-4B achieves state-of-the-art J&F of 76.6 on Ref-DAVIS17, 70.5 on Ref-YouTube-VOS, 50.9 on MeViS, and 63.0 on ReVOS(Referring), surpassing the previous best MLLM-based method VRS-HQ-7B by 0.6, 0.1, 0.3, and 0.9 points respectively. The 1B variant improves upon the same-MLLM baseline Sa2VA-1B by 8.0 J&F points on MeViS (49.7 vs. 41.7, a 19.2% relative gain) and 20.7 points on ReVOS (59.7 vs. 39.0). On the RES benchmarks, StreamingRVOS-4B reaches 82.5/77.9/79.9 on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg, outperforming UniPixel-3B (80.5/74.3/76.3). The method operates at an average of 7 FPS under streaming inference on a single A800 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest MLLM-based competitor VRS-HQ-7B, StreamingRVOS-4B wins on all four RVOS benchmarks (J&F: 76.6 vs 76.0 on Ref-DAVIS, 70.5 vs 70.4 on Ref-YouTube-VOS, 50.9 vs 50.6 on MeViS, 63.0 vs 62.1 on ReVOS), with the largest margin on ReVOS (+0.9). Compared to the same-MLLM baseline Sa2VA, the gains are substantial: the 1B variant improves MeViS J&F from 41.7 to 49.7 (+8.0) and ReVOS from 39.0 to 59.7 (+20.7), while the 4B variant lifts MeViS from 46.2 to 50.9 (+4.7) and ReVOS from 59.8 to 63.0 (+3.2). Against larger-parameter MLLM methods (GLUS-7B, ViLLa-6B, VISA-13B), StreamingRVOS-4B achieves higher J&F on Ref-DAVIS (76.6 vs 74.3 for ViLLa-6B) and Ref-YouTube-VOS (70.5 vs 67.5 for ViLLa-6B), though GLUS-7B edges out on MeViS (51.3 vs 50.9). On SAM-based baselines (Table 2), StreamingRVOS-1B surpasses AL-Ref-SAM2 by 1.2 J&F on Ref-DAVIS and MPG-SAM2 by 5.6 on Ref-YouTube-VOS. On RES benchmarks, StreamingRVOS-4B outperforms UniPixel-3B (NeurIPS'25) by 2.0/3.6/3.6 on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg, and exceeds VRS-HQ-7B by 9.0/16.2/13.2 on the same datasets, confirming that the streaming video training does not degrade image-level segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-plus-SAM2 architecture popularized by LISA and Sa2VA from offline, frame-sampled video processing to a true streaming pipeline. Rather than the standard three-stage recipe (sample keyframes → MLLM segments → SAM2 propagates), it reuses the [SEG] token as a temporal [INFO] prompt (Semantic Embedding Recycling) and introduces an adaptive re-invocation mechanism (OMCP) that skips the expensive MLLM call when the current mask is consistent with the previous frame. The approach deliberately avoids adding any new modules or parameters, positioning itself as a minimal architectural modification to the existing LISA/Sa2VA stack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Streaming referring video segmentation is a practically important setting for real-time human-computer interaction, yet prior MLLM-based methods were fundamentally offline and required full video access. The paper demonstrates that a 1B-parameter model can outperform the same-backbone Sa2VA baseline by ~19 points on MeViS while running at 7 FPS on a single A800, making streaming MLLM-based segmentation viable in practice. A key open limitation is Video Reasoning Segmentation with complex global queries, which inherently requires offline access to the full video; the authors acknowledge this and leave online reasoning as future work. Whether the SER+OMCP recipe transfers to other streaming MLLM tasks (e.g., video QA, action recognition) remains to be explored.
