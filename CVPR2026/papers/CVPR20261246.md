## Problem

In vision-language pretraining, selecting hard negatives (semantically similar to the anchor) inevitably increases the risk of including false negatives—true positives mistakenly treated as negatives—which corrupts the learned embedding space. Existing mitigations rely on fixed similarity thresholds or pretrained-model-based filtering, but the optimal hardness level is anchor-specific and evolves throughout training, making static heuristics insufficient. FALCON addresses this by learning a per-instance, per-step scheduling policy that adaptively balances informative hard negatives against misleading false negatives during mini-batch construction.

## Input / Output

The scheduler consumes a compact similarity distribution matrix of size |M|×m (where m ≪ |M|), constructed by selecting m evenly spaced quantiles from the combined I2T+T2I cosine similarity rows of cached [CLS] embeddings, then applying row-wise softmax normalization. It outputs Beta distribution parameters (α, β) per anchor, from which a hardness quantile q ∈ [0,1] is sampled to determine how similar the next mini-batch member should be. The resulting scheduled mini-batch of image-text pairs is then used to update the VLP model, which produces aligned cross-modal embeddings for downstream tasks.

## Method

FALCON introduces a lightweight 4-layer residual MLP scheduler π_φ that maps the normalized similarity distribution matrix (rows sorted for permutation equivariance) to Beta distribution parameters governing the hardness quantile for each anchor. Mini-batch construction proceeds iteratively: starting from a random anchor, each subsequent sample is drawn at the scheduler-predicted quantile level within a local search space, forming a batch of size B. The scheduler is trained via the log-derivative (policy gradient) trick to maximize the reduction in masked language modeling loss (or image-grounded text generation loss for BLIP-2) measured before and after the VLP model update on the constructed batch, using identical masking for both evaluations. This generative-loss proxy avoids the scheduler exploiting trivial easy negatives that would result from optimizing contrastive objectives directly, and the instance-level scheduling ensures each anchor receives a hardness level matched to its current alignment difficulty.

## Results

On MSCOCO (pretrained on MSCOCO), FALCON achieves Text Retrieval R@1 of 62.28 and Image Retrieval R@1 of 46.18, outperforming the strongest baseline MAFA (60.96 / 44.77) by +1.32 and +1.41 respectively. On NLVR2, FALCON reaches 75.17 dev / 75.47 test-P, and on VQA it scores 71.24 test-dev / 71.36 test-standard, both best among all compared methods. On the 4M web-crawled dataset (Table 2), FALCON attains zero-shot Text R@1 of 74.1 and fine-tuned Text R@1 of 78.7, again leading all baselines. Under the BLIP-2 framework, FALCON improves OK-VQA test from 17.94 to 20.96 and COCO Text Retrieval R@1 from 75.22 to 75.56.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FALCON outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the primary MSCOCO evaluation (Table 1). Against the closest competitor MAFA [8], FALCON gains +1.32 on Text R@1 (62.28 vs 60.96) and +1.41 on Image R@1 (46.18 vs 44.77), with consistent margins across R@5, R@10, NLVR2, and VQA. GRIT-VLP [7] (q=1.0 hard-negative sampling) trails FALCON by +1.68 on Text R@1 and +1.57 on Image R@1, confirming that aggressive hard-negative mining without false-negative awareness degrades performance. DiHT [40] and SRCL [23], which use pretrained-model-based filtering, are substantially weaker (Text R@1 54.72 and 54.52 respectively), suggesting fixed ITM thresholds are insufficient. On the 4M web-crawled dataset (Table 2), FALCON leads TCL [51] by +2.7 on zero-shot Text R@1 (74.1 vs 71.4) and MAFA [8] by +1.5 (74.1 vs 72.6), though the gap narrows relative to MSCOCO due to caption noise. Under BLIP-2 (Table 3), FALCON improves OK-VQA test by +3.02 over the base model (20.96 vs 17.94) and +2.42 over MAFA (20.96 vs 18.54). For SigLIP-2 (Table 4), FALCON gains +3.0 on Text Retrieval R@1 (72.96 vs 69.96) but shows a marginal −0.06 on Image Retrieval R@1 (54.15 vs 54.21), which the authors attribute to the proxy signal being computed only through the vision encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

FALCON extends the GRIT-VLP line of grouped mini-batch sampling for hard negative mining by replacing the fixed 'always pick the most similar sample' rule with a learned scheduler that predicts a per-anchor hardness quantile. It departs from prior false-negative mitigation strategies (MAFA, DiHT, SRCL) that rely on a frozen pretrained model's ITM score as a static filter, arguing instead that the optimal similarity threshold is anchor-specific and evolves over training. The scheduler is trained via a policy-gradient objective using the reduction in a generative loss (MLM or ITG) as a proxy for cross-modal alignment improvement, connecting the mini-batch construction problem to the broader learning-to-optimize literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The false-negative problem is fundamental to any contrastive VLP pipeline, and FALCON's plug-and-play scheduler offers a principled, architecture-agnostic solution validated across ALBEF, BLIP-2, and SigLIP-2. A key open limitation is that the proxy signal must integrate both vision and text encoders; the SigLIP-2 asymmetry (gains only in image-to-text retrieval) highlights that unimodal proxy signals leave the text encoder unscheduled. Whether the learned scheduling generalizes to extremely noisy web-scale corpora (where the paper observes smaller gains) and to non-contrastive pretraining paradigms remains to be explored.
