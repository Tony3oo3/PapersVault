## Problem

Existing 3DGS understanding, generation, and editing methods remain limited to low-level perception, low-quality outputs, and iterative optimization workflows, lagging far behind the capabilities of 2D MLLMs. The paper asks whether 2D MLLMs can be adapted to 3DGS with minimal architectural modifications and training cost, preserving pretrained generative priors while achieving 3D consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263230/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For understanding, the method renders 16 images at 640×480 from the input 3DGS, extracts per-pixel features via the MLLM's visual tokenizer, and aggregates them into per-Gaussian tokens (filtered by a contribution threshold τ=0.1, downsampled via FPS if needed, and serialized with a Z-order curve) that are fed alongside text questions to the language model. For generation and editing, the model takes a text prompt (and optionally reference latents from rendered views of the source 3DGS for editing) and produces a full 3DGS scene via 50 Rectified Flow sampling steps with a self-refinement loop activated at step 30. The editing pipeline requires only the original 3DGS plus four rendered images and a text instruction—no auxiliary masks.

## Method

The framework introduces three components: (1) a training-free 3DGS tokenizer that back-projects 2D visual features onto Gaussians using alpha-blending weights, filters negligible Gaussians, and serializes the result with a Z-order space-filling curve for the language model; (2) a 3DGS de-tokenizer comprising a latent generator that replaces the unit-level RoPE in the DiT's inter-view attention with Geometry-aware Positional Encoding (GaPE), which encodes camera projection matrices directly into the attention score as qᵀPᵢPⱼ⁻¹k, and a dual-stream 3DGS decoder that pairs a ViT branch (cross-view self-attention with the same GaPE+RoPE encoding) with a frozen U-Net branch (per-view multi-scale features), integrated via cross-attention and a DPT head predicting splatter images; (3) a surrogate task of novel-view extrapolation that finetunes the generator to condition on clean reference latents, transferring 2D image-editing capability to 3DGS editing in a single forward pass. Joint training uses a weighted combination of latent MSE and rendering loss (MSE+LPIPS), with the rendering loss decayed by (1−t)² to prioritize low-noise timesteps.

## Results

On 3DGS generation, MLLMSplat achieves FID 50.07 and CLIPScore 25.79 on RealEstate10K, and FID 53.67 and CLIPScore 27.68 on DL3DV-10K, outperforming the best baseline Prometheus (FID 63.40/64.18, CLIP 23.36/24.51) by a wide margin. For 3DGS understanding, the training-free Gaussian tokenizer boosts LLaVA-Video-7B from BLEU-1 39.60 to 42.92 and CIDEr 87.05 to 93.71 on ScanQA, and from EM 50.01 to 53.49 on SQA3D. For 3DGS editing, MLLMSplat achieves CLIP-Dir 22.40 (vs. DGE's 15.38) while running 4× faster (25s vs. 100s per edit).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 3DGS generation, MLLMSplat decisively outperforms all three feed-forward baselines: it reduces FID by 13.33 points over Prometheus (50.07 vs. 63.40 on RealEstate10K) and by 32.84 over SplatFlow (50.07 vs. 82.91), while improving CLIPScore by 2.43 and 5.88 points respectively. The gap is even larger on DL3DV-10K, where MLLMSplat achieves FID 53.67 versus Prometheus's 64.18 and SplatFlow's 84.74. For 3DGS understanding, the Gaussian tokenizer provides consistent gains over both Qwen2.5-VL-3B (BLEU-1: 28.02 vs. 23.26) and LLaVA-Video-7B (BLEU-1: 42.92 vs. 39.60) without any additional training, confirming its model-agnostic, training-free nature. In 3DGS editing, MLLMSplat surpasses DGE on both CLIP-Similarity (26.91 vs. 24.38) and CLIP-Directional similarity (22.40 vs. 15.38) while completing edits in 25 seconds versus DGE's 100 seconds, a 4× speedup. The ablation further confirms that the DRoPE positional encoding contributes the largest single gain (FID drops from 67.34 to 50.07 when added), validating that naive camera concatenation disrupts the pretrained generative prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OmniGen2 (a 2D MLLM combining Qwen2.5-VL-3B with a FLUX-like diffusion transformer) and adapts it to the 3DGS domain with minimal architectural changes. It departs from prior 3DGS generation methods (Director3D, SplatFlow, Prometheus) that inject camera geometry via channel-wise concatenation of Plücker coordinates, instead proposing DRoPE—a geometry-aware positional encoding that modifies attention scores without altering input distributions. For understanding, it moves beyond the open-vocabulary segmentation paradigm (LangSplat, Feature 3DGS) to full MLLM-based VQA, and for editing, it replaces iterative multi-view optimization (DGE, GaussianEditor) with a single forward pass that transfers 2D editing priors to 3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic 3DGS tokenizer is immediately applicable to any future MLLM without retraining, and the DRoPE technique offers a general recipe for injecting 3D geometry into pretrained 2D diffusion models without destroying their generative prior. However, the editing evaluation is notably thin (10 scenes, one baseline, sparse-view setup), and the surrogate task for editing (novel view extrapolation) is a proxy whose fidelity to true instruction-guided editing remains unverified at scale. Open questions include whether the framework scales to scenes with tens of millions of Gaussians, whether it generalizes to other 3D representations (meshes, NeRFs), and whether the single-forward-pass editing approach can match the controllability of iterative methods for complex multi-object edits.
