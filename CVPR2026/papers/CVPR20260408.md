## Problem

The strongest video-language models remain proprietary with closed weights, data, and training recipes, while open-weight models often rely on synthetic data distilled from those same proprietary systems. Critically, even proprietary models largely lack video grounding—the ability to emit spatio-temporal points or object tracks in response to queries—leaving the open-source community without foundations to advance video understanding with pixel-level localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260408/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Molmo2 accepts single images (up to 24 overlapping crops at inference), multi-image sets (2–5 images), and video clips sampled at 2 fps (up to 128 frames, or 384 in long-context mode), always including the final frame. Outputs include free-form text, spatio-temporal points encoded as a compressed plain-text format containing normalized (x, y) coordinates, a timestamp or image index, and a unique integer object ID, and continuous object tracks. Multi-crop images carry column tokens encoding aspect ratio, and subtitles are appended as timestamped text after the visual tokens.

## Method

Molmo2 follows a standard ViT–connector–LLM architecture (Qwen3 or OLMo backbones) with 2×2 patch pooling for images and 3×3 for video frames, and introduces bi-directional attention among visual tokens. Training proceeds in three stages—image pre-training, joint multimodal SFT, and short long-context SFT—over a mixture of nine newly collected datasets (dense video captions averaging 924 words, 650k video pointing queries, 15k tracking queries, multi-image QA and pointing) plus curated academic data, all constructed without distilling from proprietary VLMs. Two key training innovations drive efficiency and quality: a message-tree encoding that shares visual tokens across multiple QA branches for the same input while masking cross-branch attention (achieving ~15× throughput via sequence packing), and a token-weighting scheme (0.1 for captions, 0.2 for pointing, 4/√n otherwise) that prevents long-output examples from dominating the loss.

## Results

Molmo2-8B achieves a 63.1 average across 15 video understanding benchmarks (NextQA, MVBench, Video-MME, EgoSchema, etc.), ranking 5th overall and outperforming all open-weight models (best open-weight: Qwen3-VL-8B at 59.5). On video grounding, Molmo2-8B scores 35.5% accuracy on video counting (vs. 29.6% for Qwen3-VL-8B) and 38.4 F1 on video pointing (vs. 20.0 for Gemini 3 Pro). On video object tracking, Molmo2-8B reaches 56.2 J&F on Molmo2-Track (vs. 44.6 for Gemini 3 Pro and 28.7 for Qwen3-VL-8B), establishing new state-of-the-art among all models. The 4B variant (62.8 avg) and fully-open OLMo-based 7B variant (59.7 avg) also surpass prior fully-open models by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Molmo2-8B outperforms the strongest open-weight baseline Qwen3-VL-8B by 3.6 points on average (63.1 vs. 59.5), with particularly large gains on MVBench (75.9 vs. 68.7), Perception Test (82.1 vs. 72.7), and video counting (35.5 vs. 29.6 accuracy). On video pointing, Molmo2-8B achieves 38.4 F1, nearly double Gemini 3 Pro's 20.0 and vastly exceeding Qwen3-VL-8B's 1.5, while on video tracking it reaches 56.2 J&F on Molmo2-Track versus 44.6 for Gemini 3 Pro and 28.7 for Qwen3-VL-8B. Molmo2-8B trails the top proprietary model Gemini 2.5 Pro (71.2 avg) by 8.1 points, primarily on long-video benchmarks (Long VideoBench: 67.5 vs. 76.8) and Video-MME (69.9 vs. 87.8), which the authors attribute to limited long-context training data. Among fully-open models (open weights + open data + no distillation), Molmo2-8B leads all prior work by a wide margin, with the 4B variant (62.8) even surpassing the 8B open-weight Qwen3-VL-8B. On human preference (Elo), Molmo2-8B ranks 5th overall (1057), ahead of GPT-5 (1031) and Claude Sonnet 4.5 (1008), and ranks 1st among fully-open models.

## Relation to Prior Work

Molmo2 is a direct successor to Molmo/PixMo [29], retaining its ViT-connector-LLM architecture, multi-crop strategy, and point-based grounding paradigm, while extending the 2D image pointing formulation into the spatio-temporal (video) and multi-image domains. The key methodological departures are the message-tree encoding with custom attention masks, on-the-fly sequence packing, bi-directional attention over vision tokens, and a token-weighting scheme to balance long-caption and short-answer training. A central positioning choice is that all nine new datasets are collected without distilling from proprietary VLMs, in contrast to the dominant trend in open video-language training data (e.g., LLaVA-Video, ShareGPT4Video) that relies on GPT-generated captions or QA pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Molmo2 closes a significant gap in the open-source ecosystem by providing the first fully open (weights, data, code, no distillation) VLM family with video grounding capabilities—pointing and tracking in spatio-temporal space—that even proprietary systems like Gemini 3 Pro and GPT-5 lack in any robust form. The nine new datasets (especially 650k video pointing instances and 15k tracking queries) become foundational resources for the community to build on. A key limitation is that Molmo2 still lags behind the strongest open-weight models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B) on long-video benchmarks (10+ minutes), attributed to the absence of open long-video training data and compute constraints on ultra-long-context training; whether the grounding capability transfers to novel domains or scales to longer videos remains an open question.
