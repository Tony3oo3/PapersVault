## Problem

Generative video models produce visually realistic content but frequently violate basic physical laws, and existing physics-injection methods operate at either the frame level (domain-specific, short-horizon) or the global prompt level (coarse, temporally misaligned). The core gap is that global cross-attention applies identical text guidance to every frame, failing to capture fine-grained physical dynamics that evolve over short temporal subintervals within a video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262245/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the user supplies a single global text prompt; an LLM then auto-generates temporally coherent local physics prompts and their counterfactual counterparts for each of 7 chunks (~0.7 s each) without any visual input. The model outputs a 5-second video at 832×480 resolution and 16 fps (81 frames), generated via iterative flow-matching denoising from 4D Gaussian noise. During training, each 5-second clip is segmented into 7 contiguous chunks, and a VLM (VideoLLama3-7B) annotates each chunk with structured physics descriptions covering dynamics, shape, and optics.

## Method

PhysVid inserts chunk-aware cross-attention layers into every transformer block of a pretrained Wan2.1 DiT (1.3B base, 1.7B total), where Rotary Positional Embeddings are applied to both video query tokens (3D spatiotemporal grid) and text key projections (with an additional chunk axis), making cross-attention logits explicitly cross-modal position-aware so each video token attends differently to text from its own chunk versus other chunks. A parallel global cross-attention path preserves long-range conditioning. Training uses flow matching in two stages: 1000 steps with the base frozen to stabilize new modules, then 2000 steps with full fine-tuning. At inference, an LLM generates both positive local physics prompts and counterfactual prompts (deliberate violations of locally observable physics) from the global caption alone, and classifier-free guidance is applied at both the global and chunk levels, using the counterfactual prompts as negative guidance to steer generation away from physically implausible trajectories.

## Results

On VideoPhy, PhysVid (1.7B) achieves a Physical Commonsense (PC) score of 0.32, a ~33% relative improvement over both Wan-1.3B (0.24) and Wan-14B (0.24), while Semantic Alignment (SA) is 0.43. On VideoPhy2, PhysVid reaches PC 0.64, outperforming Wan-1.3B (0.61) by ~5% and Wan-14B (0.59) by ~8%. Notably, PhysVid achieves these gains with only 1.7B parameters, substantially smaller than the 14B baseline it outperforms on physical plausibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Wan2.1 baselines, PhysVid (1.7B) clearly wins on Physical Commonsense: 0.32 vs. 0.24 on VideoPhy (~33% relative gain) and 0.64 vs. 0.59 on VideoPhy2 (~8% gain over Wan-14B), despite being 8× smaller than the 14B model. However, PhysVid trades some Semantic Alignment, scoring 0.43 vs. 0.52 for Wan-14B on VideoPhy. In the broader comparison (Table 2), PhysVid's PC of 0.32 is competitive but trails the strongest physics-aware methods: PhyT2V reaches 0.42, VideoREPA-5B and Hao et al. both reach 0.40, and WISA reaches 0.38 on VideoPhy. Among general (non-physics-aware) video generators, PhysVid's PC of 0.32 is on par with CogVideoX-2B (0.34) and Wan2.1-14B (0.35), and well above most other general models. The paper notes that VideoPhy's auto-evaluator is inherently noisy and subjective, so cross-method comparisons should be interpreted with caution.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysVid extends the physics-aware T2V generation line (WISA, VideoREPA, PhyT2V, Hao et al.) by shifting the granularity of physics conditioning from the global video level or individual frames to temporally contiguous chunks. It repurposes the RoPE mechanism from self-attention to create a cross-modal positional coupling between video tokens and chunk-aligned text keys, a design not present in standard T2V cross-attention where text keys carry only 1-D sequential positions. The counterfactual guidance at inference builds directly on Hao et al.'s global counterfactual prompting but localizes it per chunk, and the VLM-based annotation pipeline (using VideoLLama3-7B) replaces the need for pre-annotated physics labels that WISA and similar methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 1.7B model with chunk-level physics conditioning outperforms a 14B model on physical commonsense (~33% relative gain on VideoPhy) suggests that targeted local guidance can be more effective than brute-force scaling for physical fidelity, a useful signal for resource-constrained deployments. However, the semantic alignment score drops (0.43 vs. 0.46–0.52 for baselines), indicating a trade-off between physical plausibility and prompt adherence that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos (beyond 5-second clips), more complex multi-body interactions, and whether the VLM annotation quality bottleneck limits ceiling performance. The method's reliance on a VLM at both training-annotation and inference time also introduces latency and cost considerations for practical deployment.
