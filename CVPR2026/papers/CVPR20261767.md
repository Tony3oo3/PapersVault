## Problem

Existing token compression methods for diffusion models (token merging, KV downsampling) are evaluated and optimized solely for generation fidelity under reduced compute, yet they can dramatically degrade the same backbone's discriminative/classification performance. This paper identifies that naive compression preferentially removes high-frequency structures (edges, textures, small-object boundaries) that recognition relies on, creating a gap between what 'looks good' and what 'classifies well,' and reframes token compression as a joint bi-objective problem.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the latent feature maps (H×W×C) inside the denoiser's self-attention blocks of pre-trained diffusion backbones (Stable Diffusion 2.0 U-Net or DiT-XL/2). For classification, an input image is noised at Monte Carlo-sampled timesteps and scored per class by the denoiser's prediction error under each class/text conditioning, with a staged-pruning schedule over the class set to reduce cost. For generation, text prompts or class indices drive the standard denoising loop. All compression is applied per-timestep and per-block at inference time with no cross-timestep caching, so the same compressed denoiser serves both tasks without retraining.

## Method

BiGain introduces two training-free, plug-and-play frequency-aware operators guided by the principle of balanced spectral retention. (1) Laplacian-Gated Token Merging (L-GTM) reshapes the token sequence into a spatial grid, applies a 2-D Laplacian kernel to compute per-location frequency magnitudes, then uses the lowest-scoring (spectrally smooth) tokens as merge destinations and greedily pairs them with the most similar source tokens via bipartite matching, collapsing redundant flat regions while leaving high-contrast edge/texture tokens intact. (2) Interpolate-Extrapolate KV-Downsampling (IE-KVD) reduces keys and values to a coarser grid using a per-site convex combination of nearest-neighbor and average pooling controlled by a single parameter α, while keeping queries at full resolution to preserve fine-grained attention localization. Both operators are architecture-agnostic, timestep-local, and avoid cross-timestep caching, making them directly compatible with the diffusion-classifier's paired-difference Monte Carlo scoring rule.

## Results

On Oxford-IIIT Pets under ~10% FLOPs reduction, BiGain-TM (Laplacian-Gated Token Merging) achieves 78.38% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming ToMe (72.96%), DiP-GO (76.53%), SiTo (68.84%), and MosaicDiff (77.38%). Under 14.2% FLOPs reduction via KV downsampling, BiGain-TD reaches 79.90% accuracy versus ToDo's 79.15%. On ImageNet-1K with 70% token merging on Stable Diffusion 2.0, BiGain-TM improves classification accuracy by 7.15% over ToMe (44.50% vs. 37.35%) while simultaneously improving generation FID by 0.34 (18.08 vs. 18.42). On DiT-XL/2 with 3× token downsampling on ImageNet-100, BiGain-TD achieves 61.58% accuracy versus ToDo's 8.46%, a 53-point gap, while also reducing FID from 190.18 to 32.95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiGain-TM decisively outperforms ToMe [3] across all merge ratios and datasets: at 70% merging on ImageNet-1K, BiGain gains +7.15% accuracy (44.50% vs. 37.35%) and +0.34 FID improvement, while on Pets at 50% merging it gains +5.42% (78.38% vs. 72.96%). Against ToDo [22], BiGain-TD shows a dramatic advantage on DiT-XL/2, where ToDo collapses to 8.46% at 3× downsampling on ImageNet-100 while BiGain maintains 61.58%; on SD-2.0 Pets at 8× downsampling, BiGain leads by +9.87% (66.03% vs. 56.16%). Among pruning baselines, BiGain-TM surpasses DiP-GO [28] by 1.85% and MosaicDiff [9] by 1.00% on Pets at matched ~10% FLOPs reduction, and outperforms SiTo [27] by a large margin of 9.54%. Notably, BiGain also improves generation FID over both ToMe and ToDo at comparable compression levels, confirming that frequency-aware compression benefits both tasks simultaneously rather than trading one for the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiGain directly extends two lines of diffusion acceleration work: ToMe's greedy token merging and ToDo's KV downsampling, both of which were designed solely to preserve generation fidelity under reduced compute. The key conceptual departure is reframing token compression as a bi-objective problem that must simultaneously safeguard generative quality and discriminative utility, guided by a frequency-separation principle (Laplacian-gated merging for smooth regions, interpolate-extrapolate pooling for KV). Unlike prior training-based pruning methods (DiP-GO, MosaicDiff), BiGain is entirely training-free and plug-and-play, and unlike step-reduction solvers (DDIM, DPM-Solver), it targets intra-step attention cost rather than the number of denoising iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to explicitly study and optimize both generation and classification under accelerated diffusion, filling a gap where prior accelerations were shown to silently degrade discriminative performance while appearing harmless on FID. The frequency-aware design rule (preserve high-frequency edges for classification, retain low/mid-frequency semantics for generation) offers a practical, architecture-agnostic heuristic for future compression of dual-purpose generative systems. Limitations include reliance on the expensive Monte-Carlo diffusion-classifier protocol for evaluation, testing only on 2D image backbones (U-Net, DiT), and the fact that the speedup is modest (10–14% FLOPs in the main comparisons), leaving open whether the principle scales to video diffusion, 3D generation, or much higher compression ratios.
