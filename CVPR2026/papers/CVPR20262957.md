## Problem

Existing radar perception methods rely on coarse-to-fine cross-modal supervision that forces networks to reconcile conflicting feature-to-label mappings from sparse measurements, producing geometrically implausible averaged solutions. Additionally, heavy dependence on amplitude cues leaves these methods vulnerable to multipath-induced spurious reflections. No prior work explicitly models the physically grounded anisotropic uncertainty inherent in radar's limited angular resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262957/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a 4D radar measurement tensor of shape R×A×E×2, where R, A, E are range, azimuth, and elevation bins and the two channels encode intensity and Doppler velocity. The network outputs a dense radar point cloud with per-point anisotropic spatial uncertainty (a full 3×3 Cartesian covariance derived from independently predicted radial, azimuth, and elevation variances via first-order error propagation) and a per-detection Doppler velocity estimate. Ground truth occupancy is constructed via frustum-based voxelization of LiDAR points, extending rays along each radar bin to capture the physical spread of reflections.

## Method

RaUF formulates radar perception as a Bayesian inference problem with two jointly learned networks: a localization model f_θ and an uncertainty model g_φ, trained by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of a heteroscedastic Gaussian whose covariance is anisotropic in polar coordinates (separate σ_r, σ_α, σ_β) and mapped to Cartesian space through a Jacobian (Theorem 2). Spatial and Doppler features are extracted by separate 3D CNN backbones that jointly model range-azimuth-elevation correlations. A Bidirectional Domain Attention Fusion (BDAF) module then performs two rounds of cross-attention: spatial tokens query Doppler tokens to inject velocity-consistency cues, and Doppler-projected tokens query spatial tokens to refine occupancy semantics, exploiting the kinematic constraint that positive reflections' Doppler must match ego-velocity (Theorem 1) to suppress spurious returns. The final spatial features are decoded with an NLL loss using the anisotropic covariance, while an auxiliary MSE loss supervises Doppler velocity regression.

## Results

On the Coloradar dataset, RaUF outperforms traditional OS-CFAR by 70.1% in average Chambers Distance and 5× in F-score across both single-chip and cascade radar configurations. On the cascade radar, RaUF achieves CD of 0.45–3.79 m and F-score of 0.34–0.54 across six scenes, compared to SDDiff's 0.69–9.00 m and 0.08–0.44. In downstream case studies, RaUF's uncertainty-aware point clouds yield the best transformation estimation (0.42 m / 0.39°), improving over GICP by 22% in both translational and rotational accuracy, while matching RadarEVE on ego-velocity estimation (10th-percentile error of 0.09 m/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaUF decisively outperforms the traditional OS-CFAR [3] and RPDNet [4] baselines across all scenes and radar types, reducing CD by over 70% and improving F-score by roughly 5×. Against the learning-based RadarHD [19], RaUF achieves lower CD (e.g., 0.50 vs. 1.08 m in Armyroom) and higher F-score (0.47 vs. 0.23) on the cascade radar, with similar margins on single-chip. The closest competitor is SDDiff [24], which matches or slightly beats RaUF on a few individual scenes (e.g., Armyroom CD 0.42 vs. 0.50 m for single-chip) but degrades sharply in long-range scenes (Longboard CD 9.00 vs. 3.79 m for cascade). For transformation estimation, RaUF (0.42 m / 0.39°) surpasses GICP [22] (0.54 m / 0.50°) by 22% and ICP [2] (0.99 m / 0.63°) by ~58% in translation. On ego-velocity estimation, RaUF ties RadarEVE [18] at the 10th percentile (0.09 m/s) and slightly trails at the 20th (0.17 vs. 0.15 m/s), while clearly outperforming RANSAC [7] (0.11 m/s at 10th percentile) and ICP [2] (0.15 m/s). Overall, RaUF wins or ties on the majority of metrics, with SDDiff being the only method that occasionally edges it out on short-range single-chip scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaUF departs from the dominant paradigm in radar point cloud generation—coarse-to-fine cross-modal supervision from LiDAR or cameras (RPDNet, RadarHD, SDDiff, RaLD)—by reframing the task as joint deterministic prediction and anisotropic uncertainty estimation rather than pure geometric refinement. It draws on Bayesian heteroscedastic regression (Mahalanobis-distance NLL loss) and first-order error propagation in polar-to-Cartesian coordinates, but applies these to radar for the first time, exploiting the physically inherent azimuth-range resolution disparity that prior uncertainty work (GICP, S3E, UTR) treats as isotropic. The Bidirectional Domain Attention Fusion module is a novel architectural contribution that repurposes cross-attention between spatial and Doppler token sequences, going beyond the intensity-only or single-directional fusion strategies in prior radar perception networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling the anisotropic 'crescent-shaped' uncertainty that is physically intrinsic to radar, RaUF provides calibrated confidence estimates that downstream tasks (registration, ego-velocity estimation, tracking) can exploit to down-weight unreliable measurements, addressing a gap that deterministic PCL generators leave open. The Doppler-consistency prior via BDAF offers a physically interpretable mechanism for suppressing multipath-induced ghost points without requiring additional hardware. Open questions include whether the frustum-based voxelization supervision generalizes to automotive MIMO radars with denser angular sampling, whether the 3D CNN backbone scales efficiently to long-range cascade arrays, and whether the learned uncertainty can be leveraged for active sensing or sensor scheduling in real-time autonomous driving stacks.
