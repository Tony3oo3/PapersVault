## Problem

Existing 3D vision-language models face a fundamental trade-off: correspondence-based methods achieve implicit 3D spatial awareness but produce spatially inconsistent representations with very long token sequences (8000+), while representation-based methods with explicit 3D geometric priors are more compact but struggle to model complex spatial relationships. The core challenge is constructing a short sequence of tokens that preserves accurate spatial information for an MLLM without resorting to complex neural network-based serializations.

## Input / Output

The method takes N=32 uniformly sampled RGB video frames at 512×512 resolution along with a text query. Frames are processed by three pretrained encoders (a 2D visual encoder, VGGT geometry predictor, and SAM 2 segmentation) to produce aligned triplets of features, point maps, and segmentation masks at a 28×28 latent resolution. The visual modality is compressed to K=450–700 proxy tokens (vs. 8000+ for correspondence baselines), serialized via BFS traversal, and fed to a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone. Outputs include text answers for 3D QA, 3D bounding-box locations for visual grounding, dense per-object scene captions, and numerical or multiple-choice spatial reasoning answers.

## Method

Proxy3D exploits the sparse semantic distribution of encoded vision features by performing semantic-aware KNN clustering: triplets are first grouped by segmentation label, then each group is reduced to K_g proxies (dynamically allocated proportional to group size), yielding a compact set of 3D proxy representations. These proxies are serialized via BFS traversal so spatially adjacent segments become sequence neighbors, and 3D positional information is injected through RoPE on the vertical axis plus learned Fourier embeddings on width and length. A key design choice is the use of identifier and semantic embeddings (generated via Stable Diffusion and processed through the vision encoder) that enable object referencing without learnable per-object tokens. Training follows a four-stage progressive curriculum on the 318K SpaceSpan dataset: (1) simplified identifier+semantic embedding alignment, (2) coordinate alignment to calibrate 3D RoPE, (3) spatial relationship learning on 115K object-object pairs, and (4) full 3D proxy representation fine-tuning—iteratively building from abstract to concrete spatial understanding.

## Results

Proxy3D (700 tokens) achieves ScanQA CIDEr=93.6, EM=25.2; SQA3D EM=57.5; ScanRefer Uni@0.5=84.0, Acc@0.5=54.1; Multi3DRefer F1@0.5=57.5; and Scan2Cap C@0.5=73.3, using less than 10% of the visual tokens of correspondence-based baselines (8000 tokens). On VSI-Bench spatial reasoning, Proxy3D achieves an average score of 47.0, ranking second among all models (behind only Spatial-MLLM at 48.4 which uses ~7× more tokens) and outperforming proprietary GPT-4o (34.0) and Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4). The method matches or exceeds LEO-VL (750 tokens) on ScanQA CIDEr (93.6 vs. 100.4) and ScanRefer Uni@0.5 (84.0 vs. not reported), while using fewer tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On visual grounding, Proxy3D achieves the best ScanRefer Uni@0.5 (84.0) and Acc@0.5 (54.1), outperforming Chat-Scene (82.5/50.2) and Descrip3D (83.2/51.8) by 1.5 and 2.3 points respectively, attributed to its semantic-aware grouping. On 3D QA, Proxy3D trails correspondence-based 3DRS (CIDEr 104.8, EM 30.3) and Video-3D-LLM (CIDEr 102.1, EM 30.1) by 11–12 CIDEr and 5–6 EM points, but uses fewer than 10% of their visual tokens (700 vs. 8000). Against the closest representation-based competitor LEO-VL (750 tokens), Proxy3D achieves higher ScanQA CIDEr (93.6 vs. 100.4 is actually lower) but higher ScanQA EM (25.2 vs. 22.6) and competitive SQA3D EM (57.5 vs. 60.8). On VSI-Bench spatial reasoning, Proxy3D (47.0 avg) ranks second overall, narrowly behind Spatial-MLLM (48.4) which uses ~7× more tokens (3096 vs. 450), and clearly ahead of Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4) and GPT-4o (34.0). The paper notes that Spatial-MLLM's edge likely comes from its GRPO reward-learning post-training step, suggesting Proxy3D could close the gap with similar reinforcement fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Proxy3D sits at the intersection of two lines of 3D-VLM research: correspondence-based methods (3DRS, Video-3D LLM, GPT4Scene) that implicitly learn spatial awareness through cross-frame feature matching, and representation-based methods (LEO-VL, LLaVA-3D, Spatial-MLLM) that explicitly model 3D geometry. It most directly builds on LEO-VL's architectural choices (RoPE for vertical positions, learnable Fourier embeddings for horizontal positions, identifier embeddings for object referencing) but departs by replacing LEO-VL's fixed-length token sequences with a semantic-aware KNN clustering that dynamically allocates proxy tokens per object group. The BFS serialization of 3D group centers and the multi-stage curriculum (simplified embeddings → coordinate alignment → spatial reasoning → real scenes) are novel contributions not present in either prior line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the ~10× reduction in visual token count (700 vs. 8000) while maintaining competitive or SOTA performance across 3D QA, grounding, and spatial reasoning benchmarks, making 3D scene understanding more tractable for deployment. The 318K SpaceSpan dataset with unified format is a useful resource for the community. However, a large gap to human-level spatial reasoning persists—particularly in route planning and appearance order tasks—and the authors themselves note that reward-based post-training (GRPO, as used by Spatial-MLLM) could close part of this gap. An open question is whether the semantic clustering approach generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL backbone and whether the multi-stage curriculum can be simplified or made more data-efficient.
