## Problem

Existing single-image human mesh recovery methods lack robustness on in-the-wild images with challenging poses, severe occlusion, or uncommon viewpoints, and cannot reliably estimate both overall body pose and fine hand/foot details within a unified framework. The paper attributes these failures to two root causes: training data that is either low in pose diversity (lab-captured) or low in mesh quality (pseudo-labeled), and architectures that do not separate the distinct optimization needs of body versus hand estimation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a human-cropped RGB image resized to 512×512, optionally augmented with 2D keypoint prompts, segmentation masks, and hand crop images, plus camera intrinsics (FOV) estimated by MoGe-2. It outputs full-body MHR parameters (pose, shape, camera, skeleton) along with 2D/3D keypoints and, when the hand decoder is active, refined hand pose parameters that are merged into the body output via the kinematic tree. The promptable design allows operation in fully automatic mode or user-guided mode where prompts steer the prediction.

## Method

3DB employs a promptable encoder-decoder architecture with a shared ViT image encoder and two separate decoders—one for the body and one for hands—connected via cross-attention over four types of query tokens (MHR+camera, 2D keypoint prompt, auxiliary 2D/3D keypoint, and hand position). The key architectural insight is that the dual-decoder design alleviates optimization conflicts between body and hand pose estimation arising from differences in input resolution, camera estimation, and supervision objectives. The model is built on the Momentum Human Rig (MHR) parametric mesh, which decouples skeletal structure from surface shape, providing richer control than SMPL. Training uses a multi-task loss with warm-up schedules and prompt-aware sampling (randomly injecting prompts over multiple rounds per sample), while a VLM-driven data engine mines challenging in-the-wild images and a multi-stage annotation pipeline (manual keypoint correction, 595-point dense keypoint detection, gradient-based single-view fitting, and multi-view optimization) produces high-quality 3D mesh supervision across 7 million images.

## Results

On the five standard benchmarks (Table 2), 3DB-H achieves PA-MPJPE of 33.2 mm on 3DPW, 38.5 mm on EMDB, and 31.9 mm on RICH, along with PCK@0.05 of 86.8% on COCO and 68.9% on LSPET, outperforming all single-image baselines. On five new out-of-domain datasets (Table 3), the full-data 3DB-H model reaches PVE of 37.0 mm on EE4D-Phys, 34.5 mm on Goliath, and 76.6% Avg-PCK on SA1B-Hard, substantially exceeding the best prior method (NLF) by 10–20 mm in PVE. For hand pose on FreiHand (Table 4), 3DB achieves PA-MPJPE of 5.5 mm and F@5 of 0.735 without training on FreiHand, matching hand-specialized methods like WiLoR and MaskHand. In a 7,800-participant human preference study, 3DB achieves an 83.8% win rate against the strongest baseline (NLF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DB outperforms all single-image baselines on 3DPW, EMDB, and COCO/LSPET: it beats CameraHMR by 1.9 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW (33.2 vs 35.1), PromptHMR by 2.9 mm (33.2 vs 36.1), and SMPLerX-H by 13.4 mm (33.2 vs 46.6). Against the strongest single-image baseline NLF, 3DB wins on 3DPW (33.2 vs 33.6), EMDB (38.5 vs 40.9), COCO PCK (86.8 vs 74.9), and LSPET PCK (68.9 vs 54.9), but NLF retains a 3.2 mm PA-MPJPE edge on RICH (28.7 vs 31.9) because NLF was trained on RICH while 3DB was not. On the five new out-of-domain datasets, 3DB's leave-one-out variant already surpasses NLF by 10–20 mm in PVE across all five, and the full-data variant extends the gap to 20–40 mm. For hand pose on FreiHand, 3DB (PA-MPJPE 5.5 mm, F@5 0.735) ties WiLoR and MaskHand on PA-MPJPE and trails them slightly on F@5 (0.735 vs 0.825/0.801), while substantially outperforming LookMa (8.6 mm) and METRO (6.5 mm), all without training on FreiHand. In the human preference study, 3DB achieves an 83.8% win rate against NLF and a 5:1 overall win rate across all six baselines (7,800 participants, 20,000+ responses).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the promptable encoder-decoder paradigm from the SAM family of segmentation models to 3D human mesh recovery, adapting the concept of user-guided inference (2D keypoints, masks) to the HMR setting. It departs from the dominant SMPL/SMPL-X parametric body models by adopting the Momentum Human Rig (MHR), which explicitly decouples skeletal pose from body shape, and from prior full-body HMR methods by introducing a two-decoder architecture (shared image encoder with separate body and hand decoders) to resolve optimization conflicts between body and hand pose estimation. The data pipeline draws on multi-view geometry, dense keypoint detection, and a VLM-driven mining strategy, building on the observation that pseudo-ground-truth annotations from monocular fitting introduce systematic errors that limit generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By delivering a single open-source model that simultaneously matches body-specialized and hand-specialized methods while supporting interactive prompting, 3DB provides a practical foundation for embodied AI, robotics, and biomechanics applications that require reliable full-body understanding from in-the-wild images. The VLM-driven data engine and multi-stage annotation pipeline offer a reusable recipe for generating high-quality 3D supervision at scale, addressing a persistent bottleneck in the field. Open questions include whether the MHR representation's decoupling advantage translates to downstream tasks like motion synthesis or physics simulation, whether the promptable design enables genuinely interactive correction workflows in deployment, and how the approach scales to multi-person or video settings without the temporal cues that current video-based methods exploit.
