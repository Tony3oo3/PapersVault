## Problem

Lightweight ViT-based UAV trackers achieve efficiency through structural simplification (layer pruning, token dropping), but this weakens target feature representation and degrades tracking in complex aerial scenarios with occlusion, fast motion, and appearance variation. Existing distillation-based trackers (e.g., ORTrack) apply only global feature-level supervision, overlooking spatial priors and prediction-level localization cues that are critical for maintaining target focus under structural constraints.

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a 128×128 RGB template image and a 256×256 RGB search image, each independently patch-embedded into token sequences. It outputs a center-based bounding box prediction and a spatial confidence map over the search region. During inference, the template is encoded only at initialization and at periodic update intervals (gated by a confidence threshold), while the search frame is processed every frame, yielding an asymmetric computational profile that keeps per-frame cost low.

## Method

EATrack employs an asymmetric two-stage ViT backbone (Stage 1: 6 layers for independent template/search encoding; Stage 2: 2 layers for joint feature modeling) with a 12-layer full ViT teacher used exclusively during training. A dual-branch distillation strategy transfers knowledge: (1) a spatially weighted MSE loss aligns student and teacher backbone features only within a target-region mask derived from the ground-truth box, and (2) a masked KL-divergence loss aligns temperature-scaled confidence distributions over the same target region. A Target Store accumulates high-confidence target embeddings over time, and periodic template updates integrate these into the original template representation for temporal adaptation at inference. Both distillation branches are discarded at test time, so the student runs independently at 241.9 FPS on GPU with 6.20M parameters and 1.87G FLOPs.

## Results

EATrack-DeiT achieves an average precision of 85.5% and average success rate of 65.6% across five UAV tracking benchmarks (DTB70, UAVDT, VisDrone2018, UAV123, UAV123@10fps), improving over the previous SOTA (AVTrack-DeiT) by 1.4% in precision and 1.2% in success rate. The largest gains appear on UAV123 (89.0% precision, 68.1% success) and UAV123@10fps (85.4% precision, 67.4% success), surpassing the prior best by 2.6% and 1.9% in precision respectively. The model runs at 241.9 FPS on an NVIDIA A100 GPU and 33.6 FPS on a Jetson TX2, with 6.20M parameters and 1.87G FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EATrack-DeiT outperforms all 21 baselines in average precision (85.5%) and average success rate (65.6%) across the five UAV benchmarks. Against the closest ViT-based competitors, it leads SGLATrack-DeiT [43] by 2.7% in average precision and 1.9% in average success, and surpasses AVTrack-DeiT [29] by 1.4% in precision and 1.2% in success. The most notable margin is on UAV123, where EATrack-DeiT achieves 89.0% precision versus 84.9% for SGLATrack-DeiT and 84.8% for AVTrack-DeiT. On VisDrone2018, AVTrack-DeiT retains a slight precision edge (86.0 vs. 85.3), but EATrack-DeiT wins on success rate (65.6 vs. 65.3). Among CNN-based trackers, the gap is larger: EATrack-DeiT exceeds the best CNN method (TCTrack++ [4]) by 8.2% in average precision and 6.8% in average success, confirming the advantage of the Transformer backbone combined with dual-branch distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the one-stream ViT tracking architecture of OSTrack as its teacher model and extends the line of lightweight ViT trackers (Aba-ViTrack, AVTrack, SGLATrack) that reduce computational cost through structural simplification such as token dropping, adaptive computation, or layer pruning. The key conceptual departure is that rather than accepting the representational degradation inherent in pruning, EATrack introduces a dual-branch, target-aware distillation strategy—combining spatially weighted feature-level MSE with masked prediction-level KL divergence—that explicitly compensates for the lost capacity. This distinguishes it from ORTrack, which performs only feature-level distillation, and from prior KD work in tracking that operates at a global level without leveraging spatial priors from the ground-truth bounding box.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and persistent tension in UAV tracking: structural simplification for real-time deployment inevitably weakens feature representation, and this work shows that targeted teacher supervision during training can recover much of that loss without any inference overhead. The target-aware masking (concentrating distillation within the bounding box) is a simple but effective design choice that could transfer to other lightweight tracking or detection settings. Limitations include the modest teacher-student gap (12 vs. 8 layers), evaluation only at ViT-tiny/DeiT-tiny scale, and the fact that temporal cues yield zero gain on UAV123, suggesting the mechanism may be dataset-dependent. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger architectural gaps or to multi-object tracking, and whether the target-aware masking remains beneficial when the bounding box is uncertain or the target is heavily occluded.
