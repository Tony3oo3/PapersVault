## Problem

Existing latent action tokenizers for video-based robot pre-training suffer from two fundamental limitations: the unique pairing of successive frames permits future-frame reconstruction without genuine understanding of transition dynamics, yielding semantically inconsistent latent actions; and distinct latent action subspaces are allocated per embodiment to handle heterogeneous morphologies, fragmenting the representation and constraining cross-embodiment transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260271/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes pairs of RGB video frames (current o_t and future o_{t+H}) and produces l_z quantized latent action tokens drawn from a codebook of size K, optimized via VQ-VAE. The downstream policy model (Prismatic-7B VLM with SigLip+DINOv2 visual encoder and LLaMA-2 LLM) takes a single observation, a language instruction, and a prefix of latent action tokens, and autoregressively predicts N=4 next latent action tokens. An action decoder then maps the hidden states of appended action query tokens to continuous delta end-effector movements via an MLP.

## Method

CycleMimic introduces Action-Centric Cycle Consistency (AC3): rather than relying on fixed successive-frame pairs, it samples a latent action z_s^q from a buffer accumulated over B preceding batches and decodes it with an arbitrary video frame o_c to generate a future frame ô_g, then trains the encoder to recover z_s^q from both o_c and ô_g via cross-entropy over codebook indices. A local-global discriminator (12-layer spatial transformer with patch-level and global-pooled logits) aligns the distribution of generated frames with dataset frames, preventing the decoder from leaking latent action information to the encoder. Cross-embodiment unification is achieved by sampling actions from embodiment E_i and applying them to frames from E_j, forcing the cycle-consistency loss to align representations across morphologies. The policy is pre-trained to predict latent action tokens (augmented as LACT_1…LACT_K vocabulary entries) on unlabeled video, then fine-tuned with LoRA on the VLM and full training of the action decoder on action-labeled demonstrations, jointly optimizing latent action prediction loss and L1 action regression.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, CycleMimic (pre-trained on the full dataset) achieves 96.6% average success rate across four suites, a 20.1% absolute improvement over OpenVLA (76.5%) and a 1.4% gain over UniVLA (95.2%). On the CALVIN ABC→D generalization benchmark, the method increases the average number of consecutively completed tasks from 3.27 (OpenVLA) to 3.93. In real-world Franka Emika experiments with only 30 trajectories per task, CycleMimic reaches 88% overall success rate, a 44% improvement over OpenVLA (44%) and an 11% gain over UniVLA (77%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CycleMimic outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against OpenVLA [50], the largest gap appears on LIBERO (96.6% vs. 76.5%, +20.1%) and real-world tasks (88% vs. 44%, +44%), while on CALVIN the average chain length improves from 3.27 to 3.93. Against the closest competitor UniVLA [21], CycleMimic leads by 1.4% on LIBERO (96.6% vs. 95.2%), 0.13 on CALVIN average length (3.93 vs. 3.80), and 11% on real-world success (88% vs. 77%). The Genie-based ablation (same architecture, Genie latent actions) trails the full method by 8.0% on LIBERO (88.6% vs. 96.6%), 0.47 on CALVIN (3.46 vs. 3.93), and 20% on real-world (68% vs. 88%), isolating the contribution of action-centric cycle consistency. On CALVIN, CycleMimic also surpasses Moto [20] (3.93 vs. 3.10), GR-1 [51] (3.93 vs. 3.06), Dita [52] (3.93 vs. 3.61), and CLOVER [53] (3.93 vs. 3.53). Notably, CycleMimic pre-trained only on the Bridge dataset (95.4%) already exceeds OpenVLA and Octo, which are pre-trained on the substantially larger OXE dataset, highlighting the data efficiency of the cycle-consistency objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent action learning paradigm established by Genie, LAPA, and Moto, which extract discrete action tokens from successive video frames via an encoder-decoder with VQ-VAE quantization. The key departure is the introduction of action-centric cycle consistency (AC3), which breaks the fixed frame-pairing constraint by sampling latent actions from a buffer and applying them to arbitrary (potentially cross-embodiment) frames, then enforcing that the encoder can recover the sampled action from the generated pair. The policy backbone follows the Prismatic-7B VLM architecture and adopts UniVLA's LACT token vocabulary augmentation, while the local-global discriminator draws on GAN-based distribution alignment techniques from image translation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental weakness in video-based robot pre-training: the unique pairing of successive frames allows reconstruction without genuine understanding of transition dynamics, and per-embodiment latent action fragmentation limits knowledge transfer. The 20.1% improvement over OpenVLA on LIBERO (despite using only the smaller Bridge dataset for pre-training) and 44% real-world improvement suggest that semantic consistency in latent actions is a more critical bottleneck than previously appreciated. Open questions include whether the cycle consistency mechanism scales to much larger and more diverse video corpora, whether the discriminator overhead becomes prohibitive at scale, and whether the unified latent action space generalizes beyond manipulation to locomotion or dexterous tasks.
