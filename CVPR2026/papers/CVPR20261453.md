## Problem

Existing embodied visual reasoning methods face a fundamental trade-off: LLM-based pipelines rely on static, instruction-agnostic video captions that omit fine-grained visual details, while end-to-end VLMs lack the high-level logical planning needed for stepwise compositional reasoning over complex instructions. Neither paradigm can simultaneously handle the diversity of free-form instructions and the intricate spatiotemporal dynamics in long-term egocentric videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261453/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes egocentric video segmented into fixed 30-second clips (shorter clips left unsegmented) along with a free-form language instruction. The VLM processes individual video segments guided by LLM-generated sub-instructions, and the system iterates up to 10 dialogue rounds before producing a final text response. Evaluation covers both multiple-choice (EgoSchema) and open-ended (OpenEQA, EgoTempo) question formats, with open-ended answers scored via a 5-point Likert scale using Qwen2.5-Max.

## Method

CLiViS is a training-free framework that orchestrates an LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-Max) as a high-level planner and a VLM (e.g., InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL, VideoLLaMA3) as a perceptual executor through iterative linguistic-visual synergy. The core mechanism is a dynamic Cognitive Map composed of a Navigation Graph (temporal segments recording entities, actions, and captions) and a Relation Graph (fine-grained spatial, agent-object, and functional relationships between entities), which is initialized from coarse VLM scene descriptions and then refined each iteration as the LLM decomposes the instruction into targeted sub-instructions for the VLM. An Evidence Memory module accumulates instruction-relevant rationales extracted at each reasoning step. The LLM decides when sufficient evidence has been gathered to produce the final answer, otherwise it generates the next sub-instruction, creating a hypothesis-verification loop that bridges fine-grained visual perception with structured semantic reasoning.

## Results

CLiViS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three egocentric video QA benchmarks with a training-free framework. Using InternVL3 as the VLM backbone, CLiViS reaches 55.4% on OpenEQA, 23.0% on EgoTempo, and 69.4% on EgoSchema, yielding an overall average of 49.3%. The framework is model-agnostic, consistently improving each VLM backbone: +6.2% on OpenEQA with Qwen2.5-VL, +6.0% on EgoTempo with InternVL3, and +3.6% on EgoTempo with VideoLLaMA3. The advantage grows with video length, with a 6.5% gain over the Qwen2.5-VL baseline on videos ≥30s versus 3.5% on shorter clips.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLiViS outperforms the strongest baseline in every paradigm: a 20.2% average gain over the best Socratic-based model (Qwen2.5-VL + Qwen2.5-Max, 29.1%), a 2.9% gain over the best end-to-end VLM (VideoLLaMA3, 46.4%), and a 18.9% gain over the best video reasoning method (VideoTree, 30.4%). Against its own VLM backbones, CLiViS consistently improves: InternVL3 goes from 45.7% to 49.3% average (+3.6%), Qwen2.5-VL from 40.6% to 44.9% (+4.3%), and VideoLLaMA3 from 46.4% to 48.4% (+2.0%). The largest single-benchmark gains appear on EgoTempo (temporal reasoning), where CLiViS(InternVL3) reaches 23.0% versus 19.8% for VideoLLaMA3 and 17.0% for InternVL3 alone. On EgoSchema, CLiViS(InternVL3) achieves 69.4%, surpassing the previous best of 66.6% (InternVL3) and 62.2% (VideoLLaMA3). Notably, CLiViS(VideoLLaMA3) edges out VideoLLaMA3 alone on OpenEQA (57.3% vs 57.1%), confirming that even a strong end-to-end VLM benefits from the LLM-driven iterative planning loop.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLiViS carves new ground by synthesizing two previously separate paradigms—Socratic-style LLM reasoning over captions and end-to-end VLM perception—into a single training-free loop, rather than building on either lineage alone. The dynamic cognitive map (a navigation graph for temporal structure plus a relation graph for entity-level semantics) and the evidence memory module are novel structural contributions that have no direct predecessor in the EQA or video-reasoning literature. The iterative LLM-as-planner / VLM-as-perceiver interaction draws conceptually on agent-style episodic memory work (MemGPT, ReMEmbR) and video-reasoning methods (VideoTree, VideoAgent), but departs from them by making the LLM an active task decomposer rather than a passive frame selector, and by maintaining an evolving graph representation rather than a flat context window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, training-free recipe for embodied QA that is model-agnostic across VLM backbones, which is valuable given the rapid turnover of open-source VLMs. However, absolute accuracy on EgoTempo (23%) remains low, and the 195-second inference latency on EgoSchema limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the cognitive map can scale to videos far longer than the 30-second segmentation used here, whether the reliance on a frontier LLM (Qwen2.5-Max) can be relaxed without the 12.4% drop observed in ablation, and whether the graph-based representation generalizes to active (interactive) EQA settings rather than the offline episodic-memory setting studied here.
