## Problem

Subject-driven image customization methods excel at identity preservation but lack fine-grained, instance-level spatial control (placement, scale, occlusion, relative layout) needed for real-world applications like e-commerce and interior design. This gap stems from two coupled bottlenecks: the absence of large-scale position-annotated multi-subject datasets, and the entanglement of identity and layout by global attention mechanisms in diffusion transformers, which prevents independent control of where each subject appears.

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or more reference images (encoded via VAE into latent tokens), a global text prompt, and per-subject bounding boxes that specify where each reference object should appear in the output canvas. The bounding boxes define both the spatial region and the occlusion ordering (depth) among overlapping subjects. The output is a single high-resolution image in which all referenced subjects are rendered at their designated positions with preserved identity, while the surrounding scene follows the text prompt. Training uses FLUX.1 dev as the base DiT with LoRA (rank 512) on 8 A100 GPUs, first on 44k single-subject pairs then 54k multi-subject pairs.

## Method

PositionIC introduces two tightly coupled components. First, BMPDS is a bidirectional data-synthesis pipeline that expands Subject200K into PIC-98K (44k single + 54k multi-subject pairs) with explicit position masks, using a hierarchical schedule (weak model → forward multi-subject generation → reverse LLM-guided generation) and a three-level filter combining CLIP-I/DINO scores, MLLM similarity, and DAM-based textual description comparison. Second, the Visibility-Aware Attention (VAA) mechanism embeds a NeRF-inspired Volumetric Weight Regulation (VWR) directly into the DiT's attention computation: given per-object binary masks and a semantic density σ, VWR computes a depth-ordered volumetric weight for each pixel that encodes occlusion likelihood, which is then used as a log-space additive mask in the attention formula. This restricts each reference image's attention to only its designated bounding-box region in the noise latent and blocks cross-reference attention, effectively decoupling spatial placement from identity features without adding any trainable parameters or inference overhead.

## Results

On the proposed PositionIC-Bench, PositionIC achieves an IoU of 0.828 (single-subject) and mIoU of 0.860 (multi-subject), with AP50 scores of 0.904 and 0.939 respectively, outperforming all baselines including GLIGEN (0.808/0.825) and Instance-Diffusion (0.789/0.799). On DreamBench for identity consistency, PositionIC attains the highest CLIP-I (0.846 single, 0.819 multi) and DINO (0.823 single, 0.771 multi) scores among all compared methods, surpassing the previous best UNO (0.840/0.781 CLIP-I) and DreamO (0.835/0.779 CLIP-I). The method also achieves competitive CLIP-T text-fidelity scores (0.269 single, 0.279 multi), matching or exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PositionIC wins across all evaluated baselines on both identity consistency (DreamBench) and spatial precision (PositionIC-Bench). On DreamBench single-subject, it surpasses UNO by +0.006 CLIP-I and +0.009 DINO, and DreamO by +0.011 CLIP-I and +0.021 DINO. On multi-subject DreamBench, the margin over UNO widens to +0.038 CLIP-I and +0.064 DINO, indicating stronger gains in the harder multi-entity setting. On PositionIC-Bench, PositionIC outperforms the strongest spatial baselines GLIGEN and Instance-Diffusion by +0.020 IoU (single) and +0.035 mIoU (multi), with a particularly large AP50 gap of +0.079 in the multi-subject case (0.939 vs. 0.860). The weaker position-only methods (RPF, MS-Diffusion) are far behind, with IoU scores below 0.51, confirming that prior layout-control approaches struggle to maintain subject identity simultaneously. No baseline is beaten by PositionIC on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

PositionIC sits at the intersection of two established lines: subject-driven image customization (DreamBooth, UNO, DreamO) that preserves identity but offers no spatial control, and position-controllable generation (GLIGEN, MS-Diffusion, Instance-Diffusion, Regional Prompting Flux) that places objects but often degrades identity fidelity. The paper's key conceptual departure is repurposing NeRF's volume rendering equations to construct attention masks (Volumetric Weight Regulation), thereby decoupling spatial layout from semantic identity within a diffusion transformer without adding parameters. It also introduces BMPDS, an automatic data-synthesis pipeline that addresses the previously unaddressed scarcity of position-annotated multi-subject training pairs, building on Subject200K and FLUX-based outpainting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free, zero-inference-overhead design of VWR makes spatially controllable customization practically deployable in real-world pipelines (e-commerce, storybooks, interior design) where prior methods' trade-off between layout precision and identity consistency was a blocking limitation. The released PositionIC-Bench and PIC-98K dataset fill a gap in evaluation infrastructure for this sub-task. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two or three subjects, whether it extends to video or 3D-consistent generation, and whether the FLUX.1-dev + LoRA backbone is essential or the VWR mechanism transfers to other DiT architectures.
