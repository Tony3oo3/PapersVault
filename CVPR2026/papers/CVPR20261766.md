## Problem

Traditional Planar Block Adjustment (PBA) for satellite images relies on sparse tie points matched via a 'match-then-adjust' cascade, which suffers from large parallax errors near high buildings, fragile hard outlier rejection via RANSAC, and irreversible error accumulation from front-end matching failures. This paper asks whether PBA can be reformulated to bypass tie points entirely, instead optimizing adjustment parameters directly from dense feature consistency across overlapping views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261766/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiview satellite images (0.5–0.7 m resolution from SuperView-2 or WorldView-2), their RPC sensor models, and pixel-aligned elevation maps (FABDEM 30 m DTM or USGS 3DEP 1/3 arc-second). Within each grid cell, images are resampled to a fixed 1024×1024 resolution before feature extraction. The output is a set of six-parameter affine correction matrices (one per image) that are compounded onto the original RPC model, along with per-pixel dense feature maps and confidence maps produced by the feature extractor.

## Method

A frozen DINOv3-pretrained ViT-L/16 backbone is augmented with a lightweight trainable adapter (feature fusion module, self-attention layer, and confidence head) that is trained via a geographic coordinate regression proxy task—regressing UTM coordinates per feature patch—using a composite loss of coordinate regression, cross-view coordinate consistency, triplet feature consistency, and binary cross-entropy confidence. At inference, the adjustment is formulated as a confidence-weighted nonlinear least-squares problem minimizing L2 feature distance between homologous pixels across all image pairs. A gridded coarse-to-fine strategy makes this tractable: overlap regions are partitioned into a quadtree, top-K high-confidence cells are selected at each level, and residual affine corrections are solved iteratively (Adam + OneCycle, up to 1000 iterations per level) before compounding into the RPC baseline for the next finer level.

## Results

On the three multiview satellite datasets (Beijing, Guangzhou, San Jose), the proposed method reduces mean object-space error by up to 75.43% compared to SIFT-based PBA (e.g., 1.42 m vs. 5.78 m on Guangzhou-a). Under large initial errors (~10 m), the method still achieves mean errors of 1.84 m (Guangzhou-b) and 2.41 m (Beijing-b), whereas SIFT-based PBA degrades to 6.41 m and 6.26 m respectively. On the easy San Jose dataset, the method reaches 0.50 m mean error with 97.14% of homologous pairs within 1 m (small initial error). The method slightly underperforms LoFTR+confidence-filtering on San Jose-b (0.75 m vs. 0.56 m), attributed to overly smooth features weakening the supervisory signal at coarse pyramid levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: PBA-SI (SIFT-based tie-point PBA), PBA-Lo (LoFTR-based tie-point PBA), and PBA-Lo† (LoFTR PBA augmented with the proposed confidence map for hard outlier filtering). The proposed method outperforms both PBA-SI and PBA-Lo on all six dataset variants in mean error, with the largest gains on the difficult urban scenes: 63.8% reduction on Beijing-a (2.24 m vs. 6.19 m) and 75.4% reduction on Guangzhou-a (1.42 m vs. 5.78 m). Against the strongest baseline PBA-Lo†, the proposed method still wins on 5 of 6 variants, achieving 1.42 m vs. 1.97 m on Guangzhou-a and 2.24 m vs. 4.03 m on Beijing-a. The sole exception is San Jose-b, where PBA-Lo† (0.56 m) slightly edges out the proposed method (0.75 m), which the authors attribute to the region's overly homogeneous features producing weak supervisory signals at coarse pyramid levels. The confidence-weighted dense feature consistency approach thus provides a consistent and substantial improvement over sparse tie-point pipelines, particularly in high-parallax urban environments where traditional methods degrade most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the long-standing 'match-then-adjust' paradigm in satellite Planar Block Adjustment, which has relied on sparse tie points (SIFT, SuperGlue, LoFTR) as geometric constraints. Conceptually, it draws from direct SLAM methods (DSO, DTAM) and feature-metric SfM approaches (Lindenberger et al., Duisterhof et al., VGGT) that optimize dense image alignment rather than sparse correspondences, but repurposes these ideas for the unique challenges of satellite imagery (RPC models, extreme parallax, anisotropy). The geographic coordinate regression proxy task for training the feature adapter is borrowed from the scene coordinate regression literature (SCRegNet, DSAC), and the DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone provides a strong frozen initialization. No single prior method is directly extended; instead, the paper synthesizes ideas from multiple subfields into a new paradigm for satellite PBA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from sparse tie points to confidence-weighted dense feature consistency addresses a fundamental fragility in satellite image adjustment—irreversible error accumulation through the cascaded matching pipeline—and the 75% error reduction over traditional PBA on difficult urban datasets is practically significant for large-scale geospatial applications. However, the method is confined to 2D planar adjustment (requiring elevation data), relies on an iterative solver that precludes end-to-end training, and has been validated on only three regional datasets. Open questions include whether the dense feature consistency approach can be extended to full 3D bundle adjustment without elevation constraints, whether end-to-end parameter prediction is feasible, and how the method scales to continental or global blocks.
