## Problem

Pixel-level annotations for medical image segmentation are expensive, labor-intensive, and inherently ambiguous due to unclear lesion boundaries. Bounding box labels offer a cheaper alternative but introduce strong box-shaped structural bias and under-constrained optimization, causing existing box-supervised methods to produce overly rectangular or unstable predictions. No general framework existed that simultaneously removes box-induced bias and resolves the supervision ambiguity inherent in coarse geometric labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image resized to 352×352 pixels and, during training, two independently resized versions (s₁×s₁ and s₂×s₂) fed into the same segmentation backbone. Supervision uses only bounding box annotations (derived from ground-truth masks but masks are never accessed during training); at inference, a single forward pass produces a binary segmentation mask via 0.5-thresholding of the probability map. Both proposed components are training-only and impose zero additional inference cost, making the framework drop-in compatible with any existing segmentation architecture.

## Method

WeakMed introduces two lightweight, plug-and-play training modules. The Mask-to-Box (M2B) transformation projects each predicted mask into a box-aligned representation by max-pooling along horizontal and vertical axes within the ground-truth box (compressing the 2D mask into two 1D occupancy descriptors), then back-projecting via outer-product expansion and element-wise min to recover a box-shaped support region; this fully differentiable pipeline replaces non-differentiable tight-box extraction and is supervised with BCE + Dice loss against the box mask. The Scale Consistency (SC) loss addresses the many-to-one ambiguity of M2B by enforcing symmetric KL-divergence consistency between the two scale predictions within the bounding box region, providing complementary pixel-level regularization. The total loss is simply L_M2B + L_SC with equal weights, and the entire framework is model-agnostic, requiring no architectural modification to the underlying segmentation network.

## Results

On SUN-SEG (the primary benchmark), WeakMed with PVTv2-B2 achieves 85.9% Dice and 79.0% IoU on the test set, surpassing the best weakly supervised baseline (CDSP, 79.1% Dice) by 6.8 points and the box-only baseline (BoxSup, 77.7% Dice) by 8.2 points. Across eight additional datasets spanning six imaging modalities, WeakMed averages 90.9% Dice and 84.7% IoU, outperforming the next-best weakly supervised method (DiscoBox, 77.6% Dice) by 13.3 points. The method also matches or exceeds fully supervised baselines (U-Net, PraNet, SANet, PNS+) on SUN-SEG despite using only bounding box annotations during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeakMed outperforms all ten weakly supervised baselines on SUN-SEG by a wide margin. Among box-based methods, it beats BoxInst [35] by 8.6 Dice points (85.9 vs 77.3), DiscoBox [26] by 13.1 points, and BoxLevelSet [29] by 13.4 points; BoxTeacher [10] and SIM [28] are the closest box-based competitors at 78.1 and 76.9 Dice respectively. Against non-box weak supervision, WeakMed surpasses CDSP [45] (scribble-based, 79.1 Dice) by 6.8 points, GazeMedSeg [48] (gaze-based, 78.4) by 7.5 points, PointSup [9] (point-based, 76.8) by 9.1 points, and P2Object [7] (single-point, 73.9) by 12.0 points. On the eight-dataset evaluation (Table 3), WeakMed's 90.9% average Dice exceeds DiscoBox (77.6%), BoxSup (76.9%), BoxLevelSet (67.0%), and BoxInst (64.5%) by 13.3, 14.0, 23.9, and 26.4 points respectively. Against fully supervised methods (U-Net, UNet++, PraNet, SANet, PNS+ in Figure 4a), WeakMed achieves comparable or slightly superior Dice on SUN-SEG, suggesting that box-level supervision can be more robust than pixel-level masks when annotations are noisy or ambiguous.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeakMed extends the box-supervised segmentation line (BoxInst, DiscoBox, BoxLevelSet) and medical-specific variants (BoxPolyp, WeakPolyp) by reframing box supervision as a differentiable projection problem rather than relying on non-differentiable tight-box extraction or iterative pseudo-mask refinement. The Mask-to-Box transformation is a novel reformulation that projects predicted masks into a box-aligned space before applying loss, decoupling spatial extent from shape learning. The Scale Consistency loss draws on multi-scale self-supervision ideas but is specifically designed to compensate for the information loss inherent in the M2B many-to-one mapping, a gap not addressed by prior box-supervised methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving performance competitive with fully supervised baselines using only bounding boxes, WeakMed offers a practical path to scaling medical segmentation to large datasets where pixel-level annotation is prohibitively expensive. The plug-and-play design (no architecture changes, no inference overhead) makes it immediately applicable to existing clinical pipelines. Open questions remain around temporal modeling for video/longitudinal imaging, handling of very large or extremely thin structures where box supervision provides weak geometric constraints, and robustness to noisy or loosely annotated boxes. The finding that coarse supervision can outperform dense supervision in noisy-annotation regimes also raises broader questions about the optimal annotation granularity for medical tasks.
