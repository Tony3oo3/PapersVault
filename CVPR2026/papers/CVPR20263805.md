## Problem

Existing training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods rely on CLIP's encoder-only, discriminative architecture, which requires explicit background class definitions and can falsely assign similar but non-existent classes to image patches. This paper investigates whether the decoder-only generative architecture of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), specifically LLaVA, can perform dense segmentation in a non-discriminative manner without any task-specific fine-tuning, by exploiting the spatial awareness already embedded in their LLM layers.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized directly to the target input size without padding) and a predefined set of foreground class names as text queries; notably, no background class names are required. For each queried class, LLaVA is prompted with a fixed two-sentence template to confirm presence and generate a description. The final output is a per-class binary segmentation mask produced by SAM (ViT-H), with point prompts derived from morphologically denoised connected components and box prompts from the minimal bounding rectangle of the refined mask.

## Method

FSeg-LLaVA comprises three modules operating entirely on frozen LLaVA weights. (1) A Question-Answer Pipeline queries LLaVA per class with a structured prompt to obtain a yes/no answer plus a descriptive sentence, filtering out absent classes. (2) A Text-Visual Response Module computes visual-class activation maps by averaging cosine distances between the class text token and all visual tokens across middle LLM layers (7–13), applies a dynamic threshold to retain high-confidence regions, and multiplies by a binary mask derived from top-K cross-attention maps (selected by spatial entropy) to suppress noise. (3) A Visual Generation Module uses the vision encoder's feature map to build per-class foreground and background prototypes guided by the activation map, produces a refined binary mask via prototype cosine-similarity comparison, and feeds the resulting point and box prompts to SAM for final mask prediction. The key empirical finding is that middle LLM layers (7–13) yield the most precise class-specific spatial activation, while shallow layers are diffuse and deep layers produce spurious or global responses.

## Results

FSeg-LLaVA1.5 (Vicuna-7B) achieves state-of-the-art mIoU of 68.0% on PASCAL VOC 2012 and 42.0% on COCO Object, outperforming the next best training-free methods (FreeCP at 65.8% and ProxyCLIP at 37.5%) by 2.2 and 4.5 points respectively. FSeg-LLaVA1.6 (Vicuna-7B) attains the best average mIoU of 36.8% across all five benchmarks. On 'Stuff'-heavy datasets (COCO Stuff, ADE20K), the method remains competitive but trails the strongest CLIP+DINO baselines, as large-area stuff categories are harder to capture with point and box prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSeg-LLaVA1.5 decisively outperforms all training-free baselines on the two 'Thing'-category benchmarks: it leads FreeCP [4] by 2.2 mIoU on VOC21 (68.0 vs. 65.8) and ProxyCLIP [19] by 4.5 mIoU on COCO Object (42.0 vs. 37.5). On the 'Stuff'-heavy datasets, the gap narrows or reverses: ProxyCLIP [19] and LPOSS [35] achieve higher mIoU on COCO Stuff (26.5 vs. 21.2) and ADE20K (22.2 vs. 16.9), which the authors attribute to the difficulty of covering large-area stuff regions with point/box prompts. FSeg-LLaVA1.6 (Vicuna-7B) attains the best overall average (36.8 mIoU), edging out FreeCP (36.3) and ProxyCLIP/LPOSS (36.2). Compared to supervised methods like DINOiser [44] (34.8 avg) and CoDe [42] (32.4 avg), FSeg-LLaVA1.6 (Vicuna-7B) surpasses them without any task-specific training. A notable practical advantage is that FSeg-LLaVA requires no explicit background subclass definitions, unlike all CLIP-based baselines, making it more suitable for open-world deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CLIP-based paradigm for training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, which relies on encoder-only discriminative matching between image patches and text embeddings. Instead, it repurposes the decoder-only generative architecture of LLaVA, discovering that middle LLM layers (7–13) produce spatially-localized visual-class activation maps via cosine distance between class text tokens and visual tokens. The framework also draws on prototype-based segmentation (foreground/background prototypes from the vision encoder) and SAM for final mask refinement, but the core novelty is treating an MLLM's internal representations as a dense-prediction signal rather than a classification one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical advantage is that FSeg-LLaVA requires no explicit background class definitions, addressing a fundamental ambiguity in CLIP-based methods where background subclasses are hard to specify in open-world settings. However, performance on 'Stuff' categories (large-area, texture-dominated regions) remains notably weaker than on 'Thing' categories, suggesting the point/box prompt mechanism and the LLM's object-centric prior limit coverage of amorphous regions. An open question is whether the middle-layer spatial activation phenomenon generalizes to other MLLMs (e.g., Qwen-VL, InternVL) or is specific to LLaVA's architecture and training. The finding that scaling from 7B to 13B does not improve localization also raises questions about what drives spatial precision in MLLMs.
