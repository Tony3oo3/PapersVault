## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs and multi-agent video systems lack the ability to integrate external professional knowledge and perform rigorous step-wise reasoning required for scientific video comprehension. Moreover, they lack systematic mechanisms to self-evolve and optimize their workflows based on previous execution failures, making them unreliable for tasks demanding domain expertise and iterative refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263292/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query and a scientific video (sampled at 1 fps, with all subtitles and audio narrations stripped so answers must be derived purely from visual content) as input. It produces a structured text answer to the query and, optionally, a multimodal educational e-booklet containing textual instructions, step-by-step diagrams, equipment images with purchase hyperlinks, synthesized audio narrations for safety warnings, and a principle summary, all formatted in a child-friendly progressive layout.

## Method

SciEducator is a 16-component multi-agent system (10 agents, 6 tools) organized around an iterative Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) loop inspired by the Deming Cycle. In the Plan stage, a captioner produces a temporally grounded video description, a retrieval agent extracts entities and fetches domain knowledge from an internal corpus of 84 physics/chemistry documents, and a planner composes a diverse candidate plan pool. The Do stage uses an evaluator that scores each plan via a composite of objective metrics (empirical latency/token priors from 20 probe calls per tool, IDF-weighted keyword relevance) and LLM-based perceptual criteria, then executes the selected plan and estimates a confidence score. If confidence falls below a threshold, the Study stage diagnoses failure causes and aggregates newly acquired knowledge, while the Act stage replans the next pool by adjusting granularity, query specificity, or invoking additional tools (e.g., video super-resolution, higher-fps captioning). The loop repeats until confidence exceeds a threshold or a maximum of 5 cycles is reached; the same PDSA structure is reused in the education stage with a different toolset to assemble the multimodal e-booklet.

## Results

On SciVBench, SciEducator achieves 81.88 relevance and 65.31 accuracy on Physics, 73.97/64.86 on Chemistry, and 64.58/62.24 on Daily Life, substantially outperforming all baselines. The best commercial MLLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash) reaches only 52.81/38.75 on Physics, and the best multi-agent system (VideoAgent) reaches 49.06/36.56, leaving gaps of roughly 29 and 27 points in relevance and accuracy respectively. On the Education Subset, SciEducator attains win rates of 77.50% (Relevance), 87.50% (Instructional Quality), 97.50% (Attractiveness), and 82.50% (Educational Value), while the strongest MLLM baseline (Gemini 2.0 Flash) peaks at 10.00%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SciEducator wins decisively over all five baselines on every domain and metric in Table 1. Among commercial MLLMs, Gemini 2.0 Flash is the strongest (52.81 Rel / 38.75 Acc on Physics), yet SciEducator still leads by +29.07 Rel and +26.56 Acc; GPT-4o and Claude 3.7 Sonnet trail further behind at 47.50/34.69 and 44.06/31.88 respectively. Among multi-agent systems, VideoAgent [52] (49.06/36.56 Physics) and videoagent [8] (46.62/37.16 Chemistry) are the closest competitors, but SciEducator exceeds them by 32.82 Rel and 28.75 Acc on Physics, and by 27.35 Rel and 27.70 Acc on Chemistry. On the Education Subset (Table 2), the gap is even more extreme: SciEducator's win rates range from 77.50% to 97.50%, whereas the best MLLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash) never exceeds 10.00% on any metric, and GPT-4o and Claude 3.7 Sonnet peak at 7.50%. No baseline matches or surpasses SciEducator on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-agent video understanding line (VideoAgent, VideoAgent2, memory-augmented MAS) by introducing an iterative self-evolving mechanism inspired by the Deming (PDSA) Cycle from management science, addressing the identified gap that prior agents lack systematic self-optimization based on execution feedback. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes a well-known quality-management loop as the control structure for a 16-component agent system, combining retrieval-augmented knowledge, confidence-gated stopping, and failure-driven replanning. The educational e-booklet generation stage is a novel extension that goes beyond the question-answering scope of all prior video agents, and the SciVBench benchmark carves out a previously unaddressed domain (scientific-phenomenon video QA with literature-grounded answers).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides the first dedicated benchmark and system for scientific video comprehension paired with multimodal educational content generation, a combination with clear practical value for science education. The PDSA framing offers a principled, interpretable template for iterative agent self-improvement that could transfer to other domains requiring external knowledge and multi-step reasoning. Key open questions include whether the large performance margins over single-pass MLLMs persist under human (rather than LLM-as-judge) evaluation, how the system scales to longer or more diverse scientific videos, and whether the 16-component architecture can be simplified without losing the iterative gains. The reliance on a 500-QA benchmark and a single LLM evaluator (Qwen3-Max) limits the generalizability of the reported numbers.
