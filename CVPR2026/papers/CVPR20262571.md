## Problem

Unified conditional image generation tasks depend on fundamentally different internal representations: some require conceptual understanding for semantic synthesis, while others rely on localization cues for spatial precision. Forcing these heterogeneous tasks to share a single static representation causes concept-localization representational conflict, leading existing unified frameworks to excel at a subset of tasks while underperforming on others.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262571/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

CoLoGen accepts heterogeneous conditional inputs—source image plus text instruction (editing), reference image plus text prompt (customized generation), structural condition images such as depth maps, segmentation masks, Canny edges, or lineart plus text prompt (controllable generation), and source image plus mask plus text prompt (inpainting)—and produces a single generated or edited RGB image. All conditions are consumed as latents within a multi-modal attention mechanism alongside the noisy latent and text latent, with the source latent's Key-Value projections dynamically adapted per task via the PRW module before cross-modal attention fusion.

## Method

CoLoGen is built on the FLUX.1 MMDiT architecture and introduces two key components. First, the Progressive Representation Weaving (PRW) module inserts a pool of lightweight LoRA-based KV-projection experts into each MMDiT block, with a Noisy Router performing top-1 sparse selection to route the source latent through the most relevant expert, producing an adaptive residual added to a base projection. Second, a progressive staged training curriculum organizes tasks from easy to hard—endogenous pre-training (mask inpainting, then image grounding), conditional injection (controllable generation), and instruction-image alignment (customized generation, then instruction editing)—progressively adding one new expert per stage while freezing prior ones. A Veteran Gate Routing Supervision loss penalizes deviations from a target routing density ρ for the newest expert, encouraging balanced utilization and preventing catastrophic forgetting of earlier-stage knowledge.

## Results

On instruction editing, CoLoGen achieves the best CLIPi (0.866 on Emu Edit, 0.931 on MagicBrush) and DINO (0.843, 0.932) scores among all specialist and generalist models, surpassing the strongest generalist baseline UniReal by 0.015 and 0.028 on CLIPi respectively. On controllable generation, CoLoGen attains the highest CLIP-S (33.31) for Canny-to-Image and SSIM (77.96) for LineArt-to-Image, though OmniGen leads on Depth RMSE (28.54 vs. 31.79) and COCO-Stuff mIoU (44.23 vs. 29.43). On customized generation (DreamBench), CoLoGen achieves the best CLIP-I (0.825) and DINO (0.714), while UniReal slightly edges ahead on CLIP-T (0.326 vs. 0.315).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On instruction editing, CoLoGen outperforms all specialist baselines (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, Emu Edit, PnP, Null-Text Inv.) and generalist baselines (UltraEdit, OmniGen, PixWizard, Explanatory Instructions, UniReal) on CLIPi and DINO across both Emu Edit and MagicBrush benchmarks, with the largest margin over UniReal (+0.028 CLIPi, +0.095 DINO on MagicBrush). On controllable generation, CoLoGen leads on Canny CLIP-S (33.31 vs. ControlNet's 32.15) and LineArt SSIM (77.96 vs. UniControl's 70.54), but OmniGen surpasses it on Depth RMSE (28.54 vs. 31.79) and COCO-Stuff mIoU (44.23 vs. 29.43), indicating a trade-off between structural fidelity and semantic alignment. On customized generation (DreamBench), CoLoGen achieves the highest CLIP-I (0.825) and DINO (0.714), beating UniReal (0.806, 0.702) and OmniGen (0.810, 0.693), while UniReal retains a slight CLIP-T advantage (0.326 vs. 0.315). The ablation in Table 5 further confirms that the progressive staged training (CoLoGen) outperforms naive multi-task co-training on both instruction editing and customized generation, validating the concept-localization duality hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLoGen builds on the FLUX.1 MMDiT diffusion architecture and positions itself against a growing family of unified image generation models (OmniGen, PixWizard, UniReal, UltraEdit) that typically unify tasks through in-context concatenation of reference images, control signals, and noisy latents. Its key conceptual departure is the explicit 'Concept-Localization Duality' framing: rather than treating all conditional tasks as a single representational problem, it hypothesizes that conceptual and localization cues occupy competing subspaces and that naive joint optimization causes interference. Architecturally, the Progressive Representation Weaving module introduces a lightweight MoE-style routing over KV-projection experts (implemented via LoRA) with a noise-augmented router, which is distinct from the shared-encoder or in-context approaches used by prior generalist models. The progressive staged training strategy draws on curriculum and lifelong learning principles to sequence tasks from simple (inpainting, grounding) to complex (instruction editing, customized generation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled diagnostic for why existing unified image generation models underperform on subsets of tasks—representational conflict between concept and localization—and provides a concrete, modular remedy (staged curriculum + expert routing) that practitioners can adopt. The ablation confirming that naive multi-task co-training actively degrades fidelity metrics (CLIP-I, DINO) on customized generation relative to a single-task baseline is a useful cautionary result for the field. However, the authors acknowledge a scalability limitation: PRW's memory footprint grows with the number of experts, and it remains open whether the duality framing generalizes to tasks beyond the five evaluated (e.g., video generation, 3D synthesis) or whether the specific expert count and routing density transfer across architectures. Broader impact hinges on whether the concept-localization decomposition proves as useful for other multi-modal generation settings.
