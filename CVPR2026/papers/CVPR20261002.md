## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods rely on learned text-embedding spaces (CLIP, BERT) and require labor-intensive per-scene human annotations of description-mask pairs, limiting them to short word queries and making them impractical for complex referring expressions. No prior approach handles arbitrary natural-language queries for 3D segmentation in a fully training-free manner without embedding alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261002/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained Sparse Voxel Rasterization (SVR) model (N sparse voxels with opacity and color attributes), per-view SAM2 segmentation masks, and a free-form natural language query (ranging from a single category word to a multi-attribute referring sentence), optionally accompanied by a query image. It produces a structured scene map S — a text document storing each group's ID, 3D centroid, and a canonical caption in a fixed template — and, at query time, a binary segmentation mask rendered in the target view by rasterizing only the matched voxel group. The entire pipeline runs in ~3 minutes on a single GPU with no gradient descent.

## Method

OpenVoxel comprises three training-free stages. First, Training-Free Sparse Voxel Grouping encodes each voxel's group membership as a 3D spatial coordinate (the instance centroid) and progressively updates it in a single pass over all K views by lifting SAM2 masks to 3D via masked point-map averaging, then matching and merging instance IDs across views using IoU and SAM2 re-prompting — inspired by deep Hough voting but requiring no gradient descent. Second, Canonical Scene Map Construction renders each group's binary mask across all views, feeds them to the Describe Anything Model (DAM) for detailed captioning, and then uses Qwen3-VL to canonicalize each caption into a fixed template (<category noun>, <appearance details>, <function/affordance>, <placement/relation>), storing the result alongside the group's 3D position. Third, Referring Query Inference canonicalizes the user's query into the same template and performs direct text-to-text retrieval over the entire scene map using the MLLM, which can also verify spatial relations using stored group centers — completely bypassing any learned embedding space.

## Results

On the Ref-LeRF referring expression segmentation (RES) benchmark, OpenVoxel achieves 42.4% mIoU on average across four scenes, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art ReferSplat (29.2% mIoU) by 13.2 percentage points without requiring any ground-truth description-mask annotations. On the LeRF-OVS open-vocabulary segmentation benchmark, OpenVoxel reaches 66.2% mIoU, surpassing CCL-LGS (65.1%) and 3DVLGS (64.3%). On the LeRF-Mask subset, OpenVoxel attains 89.7% mIoU and 86.8% mBIoU, exceeding ObjectGS (88.3% / 84.4%). The entire pipeline (grouping + scene map construction) runs in approximately 3 minutes on a single RTX 5090 GPU, roughly 10× faster than training-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenVoxel decisively outperforms all training-free baselines on the Ref-LeRF RES task: it leads GOI (20.5%) by 21.9 points, GS-Grouping (14.4%) by 28.0 points, LangSplat (13.9%) by 28.5 points, Grounded SAM (15.8%) by 26.6 points, and SPIn-NeRF (9.8%) by 32.6 points in average mIoU. Against the only annotation-requiring baseline, ReferSplat, OpenVoxel gains 13.2 points over the originally reported 29.2% and 17.9 points over the authors' own reproduction (24.5%), while requiring no description-mask pairs at all. On LeRF-OVS, OpenVoxel (66.2%) edges out CCL-LGS (65.1%) and 3DVLGS (64.3%) by narrow margins, and surpasses ReferSplat (57.6%) by 8.6 points. On LeRF-Mask, OpenVoxel (89.7% mIoU) beats ObjectGS (88.3%) by 1.4 points and Gaga (78.5%) by 11.2 points. The paper notes that ReferSplat's learned sentence-level embeddings tend to overfit seen descriptions, causing unstable performance, whereas OpenVoxel's text-to-text retrieval via MLLM remains robust across phrasing variations.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenVoxel builds on the Sparse Voxel Rasterization (SVR) representation and borrows its feature-accumulation update equation from Dr.Splat, but departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of aligning 3D primitives with learned text-embedding spaces (CLIP, BERT, or sentence-level encoders as in LangSplat, ReferSplat, and GS-Grouping). Instead of training 3D language fields, it lifts SAM2 2D masks into 3D via spatial voting (inspired by deep Hough voting) and then uses an MLLM (Qwen3-VL) to generate canonical captions and perform direct text-to-text retrieval, effectively replacing the embedding-alignment bottleneck with an LLM reasoning step. This positions the work at the intersection of training-free 3D grouping and the emerging trend of leveraging MLLMs as general-purpose reasoning engines rather than fixed feature extractors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design (~3 min per scene vs. >1 hr for ReferSplat) and the 10× speedup make open-vocabulary 3D understanding far more accessible for new scenes without labor-intensive annotation pipelines. The finding that a simple text-to-text MLLM retrieval over canonical captions outperforms learned sentence-level embeddings on complex referring queries challenges the assumption that 3D representations must be explicitly aligned to a language embedding manifold. However, the evaluation is confined to the small LeRF dataset (3–4 scenes), and the method's quality is tightly coupled to the underlying MLLM's captioning and reasoning ability, leaving open questions about scalability to large-scale scenes, robustness to MLLM hallucinations, and generalization beyond the LeRF evaluation protocol.
