## Problem

First-order delta caching methods for video diffusion acceleration reuse residuals between adjacent timesteps, but approximation errors accumulate over extended cache intervals, capping achievable speedups and degrading quality in dynamic scenes. Despite recent refinements in error modeling (e.g., TeaCache's timestep-embedding-driven estimation), these first-order approaches are approaching their theoretical limits, and the smoothness properties of second-order deltas remain unexplored as a mechanism for error correction.

## Input / Output

D2Cache is a training-free plug-in that operates on top of existing video diffusion models (Latte, Open-Sora 1.2, LTX-video, Wan2.1) without altering their architecture or compute/cache scheduling policies. It consumes the same text prompt and noisy latent as the base model and produces a generated video at the same resolution and frame count (ranging from 16 frames at 512×512 to 161 frames at 768×512), with negligible additional latency (<0.3 s) compared to the underlying first-order caching strategy it augments.

## Method

D2Cache introduces a second-order delta correction term δ₂(t) = δ₁(t) − δ₁(t+1) added to the standard first-order prediction, which the paper proves reduces local truncation error from O((Δt)²) to O((Δt)³) under a smoothness assumption on δ₂. For dynamic caching with irregularly spaced compute steps, the method extends this correction to arbitrary skip intervals by summing second-order deltas over the known interval and scaling the result by a factor s. This scaling factor is computed as the ratio of accumulated error proxies e_t (derived from polynomial-fitted relative L1 distances of timestep-embedding-modulated inputs, following TeaCache) over the target and source intervals, compensating for interval-induced fluctuations in δ₂ magnitude. The approach is fully plug-and-play, requiring no retraining and integrating directly into existing caching pipelines such as TeaCache or EasyCache.

## Results

D2Cache achieves identical speedup to TeaCache under the same caching strategy (e.g., 3.61× vs 3.62× on Latte, 3.52× vs 3.54× on LTX-video) with negligible latency overhead (<0.3 s), while improving VBench scores by 0.4%–2.5% across four video diffusion models. The largest gains appear at the highest acceleration (superfast): +0.42% on Latte (76.03% vs 75.61%), +0.4% on Open-Sora 1.2 (77.47% vs 77.07%), +2.5% on LTX-video (73.42% vs 70.92%), and +0.42% on Wan2.1 (80.12% vs 79.70%). Sub-metric improvements are concentrated in Image sharpness (+1.7%–7.04%) and Quality (+0.16%–2.35%), confirming reduced cumulative error under aggressive caching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2Cache is compared against four caching baselines: TeaCache (the primary baseline, sharing identical compute/cache scheduling), PAB (pyramid attention broadcast), Δ-DiT (stage-dependent delta caching), and T-GATE (cross-attention gating), plus EasyCache on Wan2.1. Against TeaCache, D2Cache wins on VBench at every model and every acceleration level, with the gap widening at superfast: +0.42% (Latte), +0.4% (Open-Sora 1.2), +2.5% (LTX-video), and +0.42% (Wan2.1), while maintaining the same speedup (e.g., 3.61× vs 3.62×). Against PAB-fast, D2Cache achieves substantially higher VBench (e.g., 76.03% vs 73.13% on Latte superfast) at a higher speedup (3.61× vs 1.34×). Against Δ-DiT, D2Cache improves VBench by 24+ points on Latte (76.03% vs 52.00%) and 25+ points on Open-Sora 1.2 (77.47% vs 78.21% at slow, but Δ-DiT only reaches 1.03× speedup). Against T-GATE, D2Cache gains 0.6–2.5 VBench points at 2–3× higher speedup. On Wan2.1, D2Cache-slow (80.74%) slightly edges out EasyCache (80.54%) at comparable speedup (2.03× vs 2.54×), though EasyCache achieves a higher speedup at the cost of a small quality drop.

## Relation to Prior Work

D2Cache extends the delta-caching paradigm for video diffusion acceleration, building directly on TeaCache's timestep-embedding-based error estimation for first-order residual reuse. While prior caching methods (DeepCache, PAB, Delta-DiT, EasyCache, FasterCache) all operate on first-order deltas—reusing the difference between adjacent model outputs—D2Cache introduces a second-order correction term that exploits the observed smoothness of second-order differences. The method is positioned as a training-free plug-in that layers on top of existing caching strategies without altering their compute/cache scheduling, rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical analysis of delta caching error via Taylor expansion, establishing that second-order correction reduces local truncation error from O(Δt²) to O(Δt³), which explains the consistent 0.4%–2.5% VBench gains across four models at up to 3.63× speedup. The ablation showing the embedding-derived scaling factor s is critical (1.72% VBench drop without it) highlights that naive second-order summation over non-uniform intervals is insufficient. Open questions include whether third- or higher-order deltas yield further gains, whether the approach transfers to image diffusion or non-DiT architectures, and whether the modest VBench improvements translate to perceptual quality in production settings beyond the 10 complex prompts tested.
