## Problem

Distilling knowledge from large vision-language models to compact students remains challenging because the substantial parameter gap causes the student to fail at reproducing the teacher's high-dimensional representations, leading to unstable training and degraded performance. Existing distillation methods modify training objectives or align intermediate features but rarely address the fundamental size gap itself, and conventional online RL for refinement is computationally prohibitive due to the 'think-answer' paradigm requiring repeated long-form generation at every step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261393/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student VLM receives an RGB image paired with a text question and generates a free-form text response. During training, the method consumes pre-generated sets of 8 responses per question (produced by both the masked teacher and the student at temperature 1.0, top-p 0.9, top-k 50) over a 1.5M-sample dataset, rather than relying on single-answer SFT labels. The distillation loss operates on logit-softmax distributions over the vocabulary, and the RL stage evaluates each generated response against ground-truth answer labels via LLM-as-a-Judge for the accuracy reward.

## Method

Masters introduces a mask-progressive RL distillation framework with three coupled mechanisms. First, it applies magnitude-based weight masking to the teacher (masking the smallest-magnitude weights per layer at a ratio r, e.g., 0.20), then progressively restores the teacher from mask over training (r: 0.20→0.15→0.10→0.05→0) so the student learns from increasing representational complexity in a capacity-aligned manner. Second, it replaces single-answer SFT with multiple pre-generated responses from both the masked teacher and the student (1:1 ratio), using Jensen-Shannon Divergence as the distillation objective. Third, it performs offline RL (GRPO-based) with two complementary rewards: an accuracy reward from LLM-as-a-Judge measuring semantic correctness, and a distillation reward computed as reverse min-max normalized JSD between teacher and student logits, which together guide the student toward responses that are both factually correct and easily transferable from the teacher.

## Results

Across 13 multimodal benchmarks, Masters-applied 8B models achieve substantial gains over their base counterparts: Qwen3-VL-8B-Masters reaches 95.9 on ChartQA, 81.8 on MathVista, and 72.9 on MMMU (vs. 88.4, 77.2, 69.6 for the base model), while InternVL3-8B-Masters attains 83.8 on MM-Vet and 74.0 on MMMU (vs. 78.5 and 62.7). Even at 2B scale, InternVL3.5-2B-Masters jumps from 71.7 to 81.4 on MM-Vet and from 59.0 to 64.6 on MMMU. The method consistently narrows the gap to 38B–78B teachers, with the 8B Masters models matching or exceeding several larger open-source VLMs on most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Masters consistently outperforms its own base models across all benchmarks: Qwen3-VL-8B-Masters gains +7.5 on ChartQA (95.9 vs. 88.4), +4.6 on MathVista (81.8 vs. 77.2), and +3.3 on MMMU (72.9 vs. 69.6); InternVL3-8B-Masters gains +5.3 on MM-Vet (83.8 vs. 78.5) and +11.3 on MMMU (74.0 vs. 62.7). Against external 8B-class baselines, Qwen3-VL-8B-Masters surpasses MiMo-VL-8B on MMMU (72.9 vs. 66.7) and BLINK (72.3 vs. 62.4), and InternVL3-8B-Masters exceeds GLM-4.1V-9B on MMMU (74.0 vs. 68.0) and MMStar (82.0 vs. 72.9). At 4B scale, Qwen3-VL-4B-Masters (79.6 MathVista, 70.3 MMMU) outperforms the 8B MiMo-VL-8B on MathVista and matches or exceeds several 8B models on MMMU. At 2B scale, InternVL3.5-2B-Masters (81.4 MM-Vet, 64.6 MMMU) approaches the performance of 4B base models, demonstrating that the distillation gap narrows substantially. The method does not uniformly dominate every benchmark—e.g., Keye-VL-8B leads on MMB (92.0) and Keye-VL-1.5-8B on AI2D (89.5)—but Masters-applied models achieve the strongest overall breadth across the 13-benchmark suite.

## Relation to Prior Work

Masters builds on three distinct threads: magnitude-based weight pruning (Han et al., 2015) repurposed as a *temporary* teacher-simplification mechanism rather than a compression tool; JSD-based logit distillation (Agarwal et al., 2024) as the core alignment loss; and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the RL training objective. The key departure from prior VLM distillation work (DistiLLM, LLaVA-KD, VLsI, RIL) is a direct attack on the teacher-student parameter gap itself—most prior methods modify objectives or align intermediate features while leaving the full-capacity teacher intact. Additionally, the paper replaces the computationally expensive online 'think-answer' RL paradigm with an offline setup using pre-generated multi-response data, a practical shift motivated by the need for on-device deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a concrete, scalable pathway to deployable compact VLMs: a 2B student distilled with Masters reaches 75.1 average across 13 benchmarks, approaching 38B-teacher-level performance while remaining inference-efficient (no think-answer overhead). The finding that progressive teacher-size scaling (14B→38B→78B) outperforms one-shot distillation from the largest teacher suggests a general principle for curriculum-based distillation that could transfer beyond VLMs. Key limitations remain: the offline training paradigm precludes real-time adaptation, and the authors note that online distillation would require 30+ days on 256 A100s versus Masters' 2-day training, leaving a significant gap for truly adaptive deployment. Open questions include data-driven (rather than magnitude-based) masking criteria and RL replay buffers for hard-example handling.
