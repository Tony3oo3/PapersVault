## Problem

Prior MLLM-based visual search methods operate on static 2D images with computational zoom/crop tools, making them non-interactive (cannot change perspective) and disembodied (cannot couple reasoning with physical action). No existing benchmark or method studies active, embodied visual search in visually crowded real-world environments, leaving a fundamental gap between passive image understanding and the head-and-eye coordination humans use to locate objects or paths in 360°.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263568/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a narrow-FOV perspective view sampled from a 7680×3840 equirectangular panorama at a known (φ, γ) orientation, plus a natural-language instruction. At each step it outputs a rotation action (Δφ, Δγ) or a submission action committing the final direction; success is judged by whether the submitted direction falls within a bbox-centered tolerance (τ_φ=30°, τ_γ=20° for object search; τ_φ=10° for path search). Each episode allows up to 10 rotation turns before a mandatory submission, and the agent also emits a textual chain-of-thought at every step.

## Method

The core mechanism treats a single 360° panorama as a lightweight simulator: each head rotation simply re-samples a new perspective crop, closing the perception-action loop without hardware. The agent is a tool-augmented MLLM whose policy π_θ(y_t, a_t | o_t, x, H_t) generates a chain-of-thought and a rotate-or-submit action conditioned on the current view, instruction, and full interaction history. Post-training proceeds in two stages: (1) SFT on 2,000 multi-turn trajectories with GPT-4o-generated, human-verified CoT rationales to instill structured action planning, then (2) GRPO-based multi-turn RL to develop strategic exploration policies (e.g., large-angle turns to cover unseen regions, acting on directional signs). The H*Bench benchmark provides ~3,000 annotated task instances across 12 in-the-wild scene categories (airports, malls, libraries, streets) in 12 countries, with four starting orientations per instance yielding 12,000 episodes.

## Results

On H*Bench, the best zero-shot proprietary model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieves only 31.96% on Humanoid Object Search (HOS) and 33.00% on Humanoid Path Search (HPS), while the strongest open-weight zero-shot model (Qwen2.5-VL-4B) manages just 14.83% (HOS) and 6.44% (HPS). After two-stage post-training (SFT + GRPO), the 3B model (HVS-3B) reaches 47.38% on HOS and 24.94% on HPS, and the 8B model (HVS-8B) reaches 60.29% on HOS and 32.87% on HPS. Notably, HVS-8B surpasses Gemini 2.5 Pro on HOS by ~28 points, yet no fine-tuned model exceeds the proprietary ceiling on HPS, revealing path search as a substantially harder problem requiring physical and socio-spatial commonsense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest proprietary baseline Gemini 2.5 Pro (HOS 31.96%, HPS 33.00%), the fine-tuned HVS-8B wins decisively on HOS (60.29% vs 31.96%) but falls marginally short on HPS (32.87% vs 33.00%), indicating path search remains at the frontier of current capability. HVS-3B (SFT+RL) outperforms GPT-4o on both tasks (47.38% vs 19.75% HOS; 24.94% vs 23.69% HPS), demonstrating that targeted post-training on a 3B model can surpass a much larger proprietary model. Among open-weight baselines, all zero-shot models (Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, Gemma-3, InternVL3.5, Kimi-VL) are substantially outperformed by their fine-tuned counterparts, with the largest gap on HOS (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-4B: 14.83% → 47.38%). On HPS specifically, MiMo-Embodied-7B + SFT (31.56%) is the closest open-weight competitor to HVS-8B (32.87%), suggesting embodied pre-training data helps path search. Disembodied 2D search methods (Mini-o3 at 2.5%, Chain-of-Focus at 11.6% on H*Bench) collapse dramatically compared to their near-saturation scores on V*Bench (88.2% and 88.0%), confirming that passive image-level search skills do not transfer to active 3D embodied search. The paper's own HVS-3B achieves only 38.4% on H*Bench while maintaining 65.5% on V*Bench, showing the embodied setting remains far from solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based visual search line (V*, Chain-of-Focus, Mini-o3) from static 2D image cropping/zooming to an active, embodied 3D setting where the agent physically rotates its head within a 360° panorama. It departs from prior embodied AI benchmarks (Habitat, ProcTHOR, Gibson) that are confined to household scenes and require full 3D simulators, instead using real-world panoramas as lightweight simulators to close the perception-action loop without hardware. The training pipeline builds on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) for multi-turn RL and the tool-augmented MLLM paradigm, but couples tool calls to physical head-rotation actions rather than computational image manipulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

H*Bench is the first systematic benchmark for embodied visual search in visually crowded, in-the-wild environments, and its results expose a fundamental gap: methods that achieve near-saturation on 2D visual search (88% on V*Bench) collapse to 2–12% on the embodied 3D version, revealing that passive Internet pretraining does not transfer to active spatial interaction. The finding that RL can degrade performance on hard path-search tasks (where visual cues misalign with the true path) highlights an open problem in reward design for spatially grounded reasoning. More broadly, the paper quantifies how far current MLLMs are from being deployable in everyday human environments, pointing to the need for pre-training that instills physical, spatial, and social commonsense rather than merely improving visual grounding.
