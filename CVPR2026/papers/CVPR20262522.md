## Problem

Existing industrial anomaly detection benchmarks like MVTec AD have reached performance saturation, with state-of-the-art methods exceeding 99% AUROC and making it impossible to meaningfully differentiate algorithms. Additionally, no benchmark exists that evaluates multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) on anomaly detection in a way aligned with real-world inspection workflows, as prior efforts like MMAD rely on multiple-choice VQA that does not reflect practical defect inspection demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262522/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For the unsupervised protocol, models receive 256×256 RGB images and produce image-level anomaly scores plus pixel-level anomaly maps. For the MLLM protocol, inputs are RGB images paired with semantically rich text questions (optionally with a highlighted bounding box region or a normal reference image in the 1-shot setting), and outputs are constrained to binary Yes/No for discrimination, a selected defect type from candidate options for classification, or a strict JSON object containing [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] coordinates and a defect label for localization. Invalid JSON or out-of-option responses are scored as failures.

## Method

Omni-AD collects 34,886 pixel-annotated images from 150 product categories across 16 real industrial production lines, annotated via a cyclic two-stage pipeline: professional annotators (qualified via a 500-image control set) draw polygonal masks under inspector review, then a supervised U-Net is trained on the manual labels to flag discrepancies that are iteratively corrected until the error rate falls below threshold. For MLLM evaluation, a hierarchical GPT-4o-based pipeline first enriches each instance's structured metadata with visual prompts (edge-highlighted defect contours plus a normal reference image) to produce instance-level prior knowledge, then consolidates all instances of a product into product-level global knowledge, and finally generates 12K VQA pairs and 3K grounding pairs with rich defect semantics. Three subtasks of ascending difficulty—discrimination, classification, and localization—are evaluated under both 0-shot and 1-shot settings, with the 1-shot setting providing the most similar normal sample as additional visual context.

## Results

On the proposed Omni-AD benchmark, unsupervised IAD methods show substantial performance degradation compared to existing datasets: the best I-AUROC drops from 99.9% (GLASS on MVTec AD) to 87.8% (PatchCore on Omni-AD), and the best P-AUPRO drops from 96.8% to 81.6%. The performance spread across methods widens markedly on Omni-AD (I-AUROC: 82.1%–87.8%; P-AUPRO: 71.4%–81.6%) versus MVTec AD (I-AUROC: 94.9%–99.9%; P-AUPRO: 91.0%–96.8%), confirming the dataset's greater discriminative power. For MLLM-based evaluation, the best model (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Thinking, 1-shot) achieves 73.32% box-level discrimination accuracy, 69.31% box-level classification accuracy, but only 26.20 F1 on localization, far below the human expert score of 79.32 F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six unsupervised IAD baselines, PatchCore [28] achieves the highest I-AUROC (87.8%) on Omni-AD, while GLASS [6] leads on MVTec AD (99.9%) but drops to 83.1% on Omni-AD, illustrating that methods excelling on saturated benchmarks do not transfer well. Dinomaly [12], which claims parity with single-class methods on MVTec AD (99.6% I-AUROC), suffers the largest relative drop on Omni-AD (84.5%), and its P-AUPRO of 71.4% is the worst among all methods, exposing its vulnerability to the dataset's defect diversity. PaDiM [7] and SimpleNet [23] consistently rank at the bottom on Omni-AD (82.5% and 82.1% I-AUROC respectively), confirming that simpler modeling assumptions degrade under real-world variability. For MLLM baselines, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Thinking [2] outperforms all other models across every subtask, achieving 73.32% box-level discrimination and 69.31% box-level classification (1-shot), and the best localization F1 of 26.20. Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct [2] trails its Thinking counterpart by 7.08 F1 points on localization, highlighting the benefit of chain-of-thought reasoning for visual grounding. InternVL-3.5 [33] (38B) and LLaVA-NeXT [20] (34B) lag further behind, with LLaVA-NeXT entirely unable to produce valid JSON outputs for localization (N/A). All MLLMs substantially underperform the human expert baseline (79.32 F1 on localization), indicating a large gap between current general-purpose MLLMs and practical industrial inspection requirements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IAD benchmark lineage (MVTec AD → VisA → Real-IAD) by collecting data directly from production lines across 16 industries and 150 product categories, addressing the performance saturation observed on prior datasets. For MLLM evaluation, it builds on the MMAD pipeline [18] but departs from its multiple-choice VQA format, instead defining three progressively harder subtasks (discrimination, classification, localization) that more closely mirror real inspection workflows, and introduces a hierarchical GPT-4o-based enrichment process that consolidates instance-level and product-level priors before QA generation. The unsupervised IAD evaluation follows standard protocols from MVTec AD and VisA, while the MLLM protocol is a novel contribution that reframes IAD as VQA plus visual grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Omni-AD addresses a critical practical gap: existing benchmarks are saturated (99%+ AUROC on MVTec AD) and lack the category diversity and real-world variability of actual production lines, making it impossible to differentiate state-of-the-art methods or validate industrial readiness. The MLLM evaluation reveals a substantial and quantified gap—best MLLM achieves only 26.20 F1 on localization versus 79.32 for human experts—highlighting that general-purpose MLLMs are far from replacing specialized IAD pipelines. Open questions include whether thinking-mode MLLMs can close the localization gap, whether the 1:1 normal-to-abnormal ratio and 150-category scale will become the new standard for IAD evaluation, and whether domain-specific fine-tuning can bridge the remaining performance deficit.
