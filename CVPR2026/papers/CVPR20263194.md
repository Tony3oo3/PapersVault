## Problem

Existing indoor 3D object detectors are trained per-scene, and naive cross-scene joint training with a shared backbone intertwines irrelevant heterogeneous signals, degrading detection quality. The paper identifies that fundamental geometric structures (lines, planes, corners) evoke consistent spatial responses across scenes (homogeneous features), while scene-specific layout and sensor characteristics produce heterogeneous responses, yet no prior method explicitly disentangles and jointly exploits both properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263194/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes color point clouds (RGB + XYZ) processed through a depth-aligned sparse voxel pipeline, with the MinkResNet backbone operating on active sites in a sparse tensor representation. It produces anchor-free predictions of class labels, centerness scores, and Möbius-parameterized oriented 3D bounding boxes for 18 categories (ScanNet), 10 categories (SUN RGB-D), or 5 categories (S3DIS), evaluated at IoU thresholds of 0.25 and 0.5.

## Method

H²A² introduces a Structural-Feature-Aware Kernel Selection (SF-KS) module that adaptively decides, per convolution offset, whether to use a cross-scene shared weight or a scene-exclusive weight. SF-KS combines three mechanisms: (i) task-aware linear modulation that maps learnable scene embeddings to channel-wise affine parameters to amplify structural features, (ii) a kernel weight selection strategy fusing a data-independent offset validity prior (sigmoid-gated learnable scores) with a structural consistency posterior (cosine similarity between learned prototype vectors and ℓ₂-normalized scene features), binarized at a threshold to produce a binary mask over offsets, and (iii) task-aware channel gating that re-calibrates output channels via a scene-conditioned sigmoid gate. To stabilize multi-source optimization, a Norm-Based Gradient Homogenization (NGH) algorithm monitors pairwise rank-wise gradient norm similarity; when imbalance exceeds a threshold, it rescales per-rank gradients to a common target norm (preserving directions) before standard cross-rank averaging, preventing large-norm scenes from dominating shared-parameter updates.

## Results

On ScanNet v2, H²A² achieves 77.5 mAP@0.25 and 63.8 mAP@0.5, outperforming the TR3D baseline by +4.6 and +4.5 points respectively. On S3DIS, the gains are even more pronounced at +4.2 mAP@0.25 (78.7) and +7.6 mAP@0.5 (59.3) over TR3D. On SUN RGB-D, H²A² reaches 68.0 mAP@0.25 and 51.4 mAP@0.5, a +0.9 and +1.0 improvement over TR3D. Inference throughput ranges from 14.3 FPS (S3DIS) to 21.8 FPS (SUN RGB-D) on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, slightly below TR3D's 21.0–27.5 FPS range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H²A² outperforms all 13 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the direct baseline TR3D, H²A² gains +4.6 mAP@0.25 and +4.5 mAP@0.5 on ScanNet v2, +0.9 and +1.0 on SUN RGB-D, and +4.2 and +7.6 on S3DIS, with the S3DIS mAP@0.5 improvement being the largest single-metric gain in the table. Compared to the next-best methods on ScanNet v2 (SPGroup3D at 74.3 mAP@0.25 and DLLA at 73.8), H²A² leads by +3.2 and +3.7 points respectively. On S3DIS, H²A² surpasses Point-GCC (75.1 mAP@0.25, 56.7 mAP@0.5) by +3.6 and +2.6 points, and on SUN RGB-D it exceeds Point-GCC (67.7, 51.0) by +0.3 and +0.4. The FPS trade-off is modest: H²A² runs at 18.2 FPS on ScanNet v2 versus TR3D's 23.7, and 14.3 FPS on S3DIS versus TR3D's 21.0, while SUN RGB-D throughput (21.8 FPS) is closer to TR3D's 27.5 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FCAF3D → TR3D lineage of fully convolutional, anchor-free sparse-voxel indoor detectors by introducing a multi-scene joint training paradigm that explicitly disentangles cross-scene homogeneous geometric features from scene-specific heterogeneous ones. Conceptually, it departs from the standard shared-backbone-plus-scene-specific-heads approach (used in UniDet3D and multi-data learning literature) by operating at the kernel level: SF-KS adaptively selects which convolutional offsets are shared versus exclusive per scene, while NGH addresses the gradient-norm imbalance that arises when multiple datasets co-optimize shared parameters. The observation that fundamental geometric structures (lines, planes, corners) produce consistent sparse-convolution offset patterns across scenes provides the mechanistic motivation for this kernel-level sharing strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-scene joint training formulation is practically relevant for deploying indoor detectors across diverse environments without per-scene retraining, and the zero-shot transfer to 3RScan (+3.1 mAP@0.25 over TR3D) suggests the learned homogeneous representations carry genuine structural information. However, the approach incurs a notable FPS cost (18.2 vs. 23.7 on ScanNet v2), and the kernel-selection mechanism is tightly coupled to the sparse-convolutional architecture, limiting immediate transfer to transformer-based or voting-based detectors. Open questions include whether the homogeneous/heterogeneous decomposition generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the binarization threshold τ in SF-KS requires per-dataset tuning, and whether the NGH gradient homogenization remains effective as the number of jointly trained datasets grows beyond three.
