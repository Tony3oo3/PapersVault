## Problem

Existing equivariant diffusion policies for robot manipulation suffer from three coupled limitations: they are computationally intensive (hundreds of denoising steps), rely on single-modality visual inputs that miss fine-grained scene details, and become unstable when naively combined with fast-sampling methods like flow matching. No prior work has successfully unified the data efficiency of continuous SE(3) equivariance with the speed of few-step flow-based generation while leveraging hybrid visual modalities.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes three input streams: hand-eye RGB images cropped to 84×84, single-view point clouds downsampled to 1024 colored points via farthest point sampling (workspace-filtered), and a proprioceptive state comprising 3D end-effector position, 6D rotation, and 1D gripper state. The 3D position and 6D rotation are treated as equivariant vectors embedded onto the sphere, while the gripper state is a trivial (scalar) representation. The output is a sequence of robot actions (action chunks) generated by integrating the learned velocity field over 10 ODE steps, yielding a full manipulation trajectory in approximately 0.51 seconds.

## Method

E3Flow constructs an SO(3)-equivariant observation encoder by processing point clouds through EquiformerV2 (yielding scalar Type-0 and higher-order spherical harmonic features) and images through a ResNet (yielding invariant features). A Feature Enhancement Module (FEM) then injects the invariant image features into the scalar component of the point cloud representation via cross-modal attention followed by a learned gating mechanism, while leaving higher-order equivariant components untouched, producing a fused spherical representation that is both semantically rich and rotationally consistent. For action generation, the paper adapts rectified flow to the equivariant setting: the velocity field network is equivariant by construction, and because the training target (a − x₀) is a linear transformation of equivariant actions, the loss is invariant under group actions, guaranteeing an equivariant optimal solution. This end-to-end equivariant flow matching is trained via behavior cloning with an L2 velocity-matching loss (AdamW, lr 1e-4, EMA 0.95, 500 epochs), and at inference requires only 10 ODE integration steps, achieving a 7× speedup over the strongest equivariant diffusion baseline (SDP) while improving average success rate by 3.12%.

## Results

On 8 MimicGen manipulation tasks (100 expert demonstrations each), E3Flow achieves a 79.00% average success rate, outperforming the strongest equivariant baseline SDP (DDPM) at 75.88% by 3.12 percentage points. In inference efficiency, E3Flow runs at 0.51 s per step on average, delivering a 7× speedup over SDP (DDPM) at 3.73 s and a 5× speedup over EquiDiff (img) at 2.51 s. Under a 10° SE(3) tilt perturbation, E3Flow retains the highest success rates among all methods (e.g., 52% on Nut_Assembly vs. 39% for SDP), confirming its superior generalization to unseen scene transformations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E3Flow outperforms all seven baselines on average success rate. Against the strongest equivariant baseline SDP (DDPM, 75.88%), E3Flow gains 3.12 points (79.00%), with the largest per-task margin on Hammer_Cleanup_D1 (+10) and Square_D2 (+6). Compared to the strongest non-equivariant 3D baseline DP3 (25.50%), E3Flow achieves a 53.5-point advantage, underscoring the value of equivariant representations. E3Flow also surpasses EquiDiff (voxel, 68.50%) by 10.5 points, demonstrating that continuous SO(3) spherical-harmonic equivariance outperforms discrete SO(2) voxel equivariance. EquiBot (5.38%) fails catastrophically because its SIM(3) scaling disrupts point-cloud structure in multi-object scenes. On inference speed, E3Flow (0.51 s) is 7× faster than SDP (DDPM, 3.73 s), 5× faster than EquiDiff (img, 2.51 s), and 2× faster than EquiDiff (voxel, 1.10 s), while DP3 is faster (0.109 s) but achieves far lower success rates due to its simple MLP architecture lacking directional information.

## Relation to Prior Work

E3Flow sits at the intersection of two research threads: equivariant policy learning (EquiBot, EquiDiff, SDP) and fast generative sampling via flow matching (Lipman et al., Klein et al.). It departs from the diffusion-based equivariant policies by replacing iterative denoising with rectified flow, and from prior equivariant methods by introducing hybrid visual inputs (point clouds + hand-eye images) fused through a novel Feature Enhancement Module that injects invariant semantic cues into the scalar component of spherical harmonic features. The spherical harmonic representation itself is inherited from EquiformerV2 and SDP, but the paper is the first to combine it with a flow-matching action generator, closing a gap the authors identify between equivariant data efficiency and fast-sampling practicality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7× inference speedup over SDP (0.51 s vs. 3.73 s) while matching or exceeding success rates makes equivariant policies substantially more viable for real-time robotic deployment, and the real-robot experiments confirm this is not purely a simulation artifact. The ablation showing that one-step flow variants (MeanFlow, AlphaFlow) degrade significantly on complex equivariant tasks is a useful negative result that clarifies why a moderate number of integration steps (10) remains necessary. Open questions the authors flag include the relative contribution of invariant vs. equivariant visual features to action quality, and whether strict local equivariance (beyond global SE(3)) can further improve robustness in cluttered real-world scenes.
