## Problem

Current world models for autonomous driving keep video generation and motion planning as decoupled or merely parallel processes, even within ostensibly unified architectures. The rich spatiotemporal latent representations learned by video generators from large-scale driving video—encoding scene semantics, agent dynamics, and physical regularities—remain untapped as the planning state, leaving a gap between visual imagination and action selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263151/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes four past camera frames (8 Hz for video pretraining on nuScenes/NuPlan, 2 Hz for planning on NAVSIM), ego-vehicle kinematics, and a high-level navigation command rendered as a natural-language text prompt. It produces a future driving video at up to 1280×704×25 frames and a continuous ego trajectory sampled at 2 Hz over the next 4 seconds. The spatiotemporal VAE compresses each clip to a 32×32×8 latent with 128 channels (1:192 pixel-to-token ratio), and the planner is conditioned on cached intermediate features from the first denoising step of the Video DiT rather than on decoded pixels.

## Method

DriveLaW chains a 2B-parameter Video DiT (initialized from LTX-Video, trained with flow matching) and a 133M-parameter Action DiT in a shared latent space: the video generator's mid-denoising latents are cached and fed directly as conditioning to the diffusion planner, which predicts trajectories via a flow-matching objective. A Noise Reinjection mechanism selectively re-injects Gaussian noise into high-frequency regions (identified by a Laplacian mask computed in pixel space) before each denoising step, restoring sharpness in fast-motion scenes without a super-resolution module. A hybrid decoding scheme performs the final refinement in pixel space to recover fine textures. Training follows a three-stage progressive curriculum—low-resolution long clips for temporal coherence, high-resolution short clips for spatial fidelity, then joint video-planner fine-tuning—avoiding gradient interference between the two components while ensuring the planner inherits physically grounded driving representations.

## Results

On nuScenes video generation, DriveLaW achieves state-of-the-art FID of 4.6 and FVD of 81.3, surpassing the previous best (Vista: FID 6.9, FVD 89.4) by 33.3% in FID and 1.8% in FVD. On the NAVSIM planning benchmark, DriveLaW attains a PDMS of 89.1 without any reinforcement learning or learned scorers, setting a new record among all world-model and end-to-end methods. On the additional nuScenes open-loop planning evaluation, DriveLaW reduces average L2 error to 1.15 m and average collision rate to 0.24%, both lower than Epona (1.25 m, 0.36%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveLaW outperforms all 17 baselines on both tasks. In video generation (nuScenes), it beats the closest competitor Vista by 2.3 FID (6.9→4.6) and 8.1 FVD (89.4→81.3), and surpasses Epona by 2.9 FID (7.5→4.6). In closed-loop planning (NAVSIM), DriveLaW's PDMS of 89.1 exceeds the strongest end-to-end method DiffusionDrive (88.1) by 1.0, the best world-model baselines WoTE (88.3) and PWM (88.1) by 0.8 and 1.0 respectively, and the parallel-generation world model Epona (86.2) by 2.9. The representation ablation (Table 5) further shows that video-generator latents yield +5.0 PDMS over BEV features and +2.6 over VLM hidden states under the same diffusion planner, confirming that the chained video-to-action design is the key differentiator. On the nuScenes open-loop planning test, DriveLaW reduces average L2 error from 1.25 m to 1.15 m and average collision rate from 0.36% to 0.24% relative to Epona.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveLaW builds on the LTX-Video spatiotemporal VAE and Video DiT backbone, the PixArt-α transformer design, and flow-matching as the generative objective, while adapting the iterative-refinement principle from DiffuseSlide into a targeted Noise Reinjection mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from prior 'unified' world models (Epona, DriveVLA-W0, VaViM) that treat video generation and trajectory planning as parallel output heads, instead adopting a chained design inspired by Genie Envisioner where mid-level video-generator latents are injected directly into a diffusion planner. This positions the video generator not merely as a renderer but as a feature extractor whose internal representations encode driving cognition priors superior to purpose-built BEV features or VLM hidden states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that large-scale video pretraining transfers to planning with a clear scaling law (+3.2 PDMS from 0 to 7.6M pretraining samples), suggesting that investing in video generation capacity is a viable path to better driving policies without explicit planning supervision. The representation ablation—video latents outperforming BEV features by 5.0 PDMS and VLM hidden states by 2.6 PDMS under an identical planner—challenges the field's default assumption that task-specific geometric or language features are the best planning conditioning. Open questions include whether the chained design generalizes to multi-view settings, whether the 30-step video generation at inference is compatible with real-time closed-loop driving, and whether the noise-reinjection overhead scales gracefully to longer horizons or higher resolutions.
