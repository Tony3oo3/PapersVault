## Problem

State-of-the-art AVSR models trained on clean offline datasets suffer catastrophic performance degradation (e.g., WER from 0.93% to 33.09%) when deployed in real-world video conferencing, yet no prior work has systematically evaluated this gap or identified its underlying causes. The paper addresses two missing pieces: a dataset that captures the unique signal distortions and spontaneous speaker hyper-expression of VC, and a mechanistic explanation for why models fail under these conditions.

## Input / Output

The AVSR models consume 16 kHz mono audio and lip-cropped video frames (from 1920×1080 @ 25 FPS recordings) to produce transcribed text in English or Chinese. The MLD-VC dataset provides paired offline and VC-transmitted recordings across four platforms with lip landmark annotations, and the evaluation protocol transmits test sets through live VC sessions (Zoom, Lark, Tencent Meeting, DingTalk) rather than simulating distortions offline, capturing the full transmission pipeline including codec compression and platform-specific speech enhancement.

## Method

The paper combines systematic evaluation, dataset construction, and mechanistic analysis. It transmits test sets of three SOTA AVSR models (Auto-AVSR, mWhisper-Flamingo, LiPS-AVSR) through real VC platforms to quantify degradation, then constructs MLD-VC (31 speakers, 22.79 hours, 4 platforms, English+Chinese) that explicitly elicits the Lombard effect via controlled 40/60/80 dB background noise to model hyper-expression. Using openSMILE acoustic features (F0, F1, F2, loudness, AlphaRatio) and lip landmark geometry, the authors decompose the VC processing pipeline and identify speech enhancement algorithms (Sepformer, DeepFilterNet, NoiseReduce) as the primary source of F1/F2 formant shift, while showing OPUS codec compression has minimal spectral impact. They further demonstrate that the Lombard-induced distribution shift closely mirrors the speech-enhancement-induced shift, explaining why Lombard-trained models are inherently more robust. Fine-tuning LiPS-AVSR on MLD-VC achieves a 17.5% average CER reduction, with ablation confirming both online distortion and hyper-expression data are indispensable.

## Results

All three SOTA AVSR models suffer severe performance degradation under video conferencing (VC) conditions. Auto-AVSR's WER on LRS3 (audio-visual) jumps from 0.93% offline to 33.09% on Zoom (Δ=32.16%), while mWhisper-Flamingo's audio-only WER on LRS3 increases from 0.68% to 10.38% on Zoom. Fine-tuning LiPS-AVSR on the proposed MLD-VC dataset achieves an average 17.5% relative CER reduction across three VC platforms (e.g., Lark CER drops from 18.53% to 13.64%), and a 67.2% reduction on the MLD-VC test set itself (42.37% → 13.91%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three SOTA AVSR models—Auto-AVSR, mWhisper-Flamingo, and LiPS-AVSR—across Zoom, Lark, and Tencent Meeting. All three models degrade severely in VC, but the extent varies: Auto-AVSR suffers the largest collapse on LRS3 (WER 0.93%→33.09% on Zoom), while mWhisper-Flamingo shows a more moderate increase (WER 0.68%→10.38% audio-only on Zoom). A critical finding is that Auto-AVSR trained on Lombard-Grid data is substantially more robust in VC than the same model on LRS3: its WER on Zoom is only 12.36% (Lombard-Grid) versus 33.09% (LRS3), a 2.7× difference, confirming that Lombard-trained models inherently resist VC distortions. The visual-only modality is the most vulnerable across all models (Auto-AVSR WER reaches 92.08% on Lark), while audio-visual fusion provides the best cross-modal robustness. After fine-tuning LiPS-AVSR on MLD-VC, the model outperforms its own offline-trained version on all three platforms (e.g., Lark CER 18.53%→13.64%, a 26.4% relative reduction), demonstrating that the proposed dataset effectively bridges the offline-to-VC gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AVSR robustness literature, which has primarily addressed noise and modality loss, by identifying video conferencing as a distinct and previously unexamined degradation regime. It draws on the Lombard effect literature (Marxer et al., Ma et al.) and the hyper-expression findings of Russell et al. (2023) to frame VC-specific challenges, but departs from prior work by isolating speech enhancement algorithms—not codec compression—as the primary driver of acoustic formant shift. The dataset construction repurposes the Grid-style corpus design from Lombard-Grid and DB-MMLC but is the first to capture recordings through actual VC platforms with controlled Lombard induction, bridging the gap between offline robustness benchmarks and real deployment conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MLD-VC dataset (31 speakers, 22.79 h, 4 platforms, bilingual) fills a clear gap for evaluating and training AVSR under realistic VC conditions, and the 17.5% average CER reduction from fine-tuning demonstrates practical utility. The mechanistic finding that speech enhancement—not compression—causes the dominant F1/F2 shift redirects attention toward how VC platform signal processing interacts with downstream recognition, and the observation that lip-landmark geometry remains stable while image-level features degrade suggests a concrete direction for more robust visual encoders. Limitations include the modest speaker count, fine-tuning evaluated only on LiPS-AVSR, and the reliance on simulated Lombard noise as a proxy for spontaneous VC hyper-expression; whether geometry-based visual encoders actually close the gap, and whether findings generalize beyond the four tested platforms, remain open.
