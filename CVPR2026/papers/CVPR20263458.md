## Problem

Existing video editing methods face a fundamental trade-off: expert models achieve precise, localized edits but require user-provided masks and per-task overhead, while unified temporal in-context learning models are mask-free yet lack explicit spatial cues, resulting in weak instruction-to-region mapping and imprecise localization—especially for multi-instance or spatially complex edits. The paper asks whether a single model can maintain expert-level precision and unified mask-free operation simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes a source video clip (33 frames at resolutions up to 400×944) and a free-form text editing instruction as input, and produces an edited video of the same length. Internally, the model generates a 4-frame reasoning clip (progressive gray-highlighted grounding region) as intermediate tokens before the edited frames. Despite being trained on 33-frame clips, the RoPE alignment design enables inference on sequences up to 513 frames (16× training length), and DMD-LoRA distillation reduces inference to 4 denoising steps (~10 s on a single H100).

## Method

VideoCoF introduces a Chain-of-Frames (CoF) mechanism that reformulates video editing as a 'see → reason → edit' process: the video diffusion model (WAN-14B VideoDiT) is forced to first predict reasoning tokens—progressive gray-highlighted grounding latents indicating the edit region—before generating the target video tokens, all within a single temporally concatenated sequence processed by self-attention. A key RoPE alignment strategy assigns temporal indices [1, F] to both source and target frames while fixing the reasoning frame at index 0, which prevents index-collision artifacts and enables length extrapolation beyond the training duration. Training uses flow-matching velocity prediction with loss computed only on the reasoning and target frames, requiring just 50k curated video triplets (source, reasoning, edited) generated via a pipeline combining Qwen-VL 72B multi-instance detection, Grounding-SAM2 segmentation, and task-specific editing tools.

## Results

On VideoCoF-Bench, VideoCoF achieves the highest GPT-4o Instruct Follow score (8.97) and Success Ratio (76.36%), outperforming the strongest baseline ICVE (7.79 and 57.76%) by +15.1% and +18.6% respectively. VideoCoF also leads on Preservation (8.20 vs. 8.06) and CLIP-T (28.00 vs. 27.49), while ICVE retains a slight edge on Quality (8.14 vs. 7.77). Notably, VideoCoF is fine-tuned on only 50k video pairs, whereas ICVE uses ~1M pretraining videos plus 150k for fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoCoF decisively outperforms the closest competitor ICVE [17] on the two most critical editing metrics: Instruct Follow (8.97 vs. 7.79, +15.1%) and Success Ratio (76.36% vs. 57.76%, +18.6%), while using roughly 20× less training data (50k pairs vs. ~1.15M total). Against the other in-context method Lucy Edit [30], VideoCoF leads by a wide margin on Instruct Follow (8.97 vs. 5.24) and Success Ratio (76.36% vs. 29.64%). The training-free baselines TokenFlow [6], InsV2V [5], and Señorita [52] are far behind, with Success Ratios below 11%, confirming that instruction-guided training is essential for instance-level editing. VACE [9] achieves a competitive Quality score (7.61) but lags significantly on instruction following (7.47) and success ratio (26.60%), indicating it struggles with precise region localization. The only metric where VideoCoF trails is GPT-4o Quality (7.77 vs. ICVE's 8.14), suggesting a minor trade-off in raw visual fidelity for the gain in editing precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoCoF sits at the intersection of two established video-editing paradigms: mask-based expert models (VideoPainter, DiffuEraser, VideoSwap) that achieve precision but require per-task inputs, and unified temporal in-context learning models (UNIC, ICVE, EditVerse) that are mask-free but suffer from weak instruction-to-region mapping. The paper's core departure is borrowing the Chain-of-Thought principle from LLMs and instantiating it as a 'Chain of Frames' in a video diffusion transformer (WAN-14B), where the model must first generate reasoning tokens (a gray-highlighted edit region) before producing the edited video. The RoPE alignment strategy for length extrapolation extends prior 3D factorized RoPE designs by explicitly accounting for the inserted reasoning latent, and the data curation pipeline repurposes Grounding-SAM2, Qwen-VL, and GPT-4o to synthesize instance-level triplets at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving state-of-the-art instruction-following and success ratio with only 50k training pairs (versus ~1M pretraining + 150k finetuning for ICVE) suggests that explicit spatial reasoning is a more data-efficient inductive bias than brute-force temporal concatenation. The 16× length extrapolation capability makes the approach viable for real-world video lengths without retraining. Open questions include whether the gray-highlight reasoning format generalizes to more complex multi-step or compositional edits, whether the approach transfers to other video diffusion backbones beyond WAN-14B, and whether the instance-level reasoning can be extended to part-level or attribute-level edits without additional supervision.
