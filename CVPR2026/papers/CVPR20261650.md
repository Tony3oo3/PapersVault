## Problem

Existing VLM red-teaming and jailbreak methods are confined to a single, manually predefined attack strategy, enabling only refinement within a fixed paradigm rather than autonomous discovery of novel vulnerabilities. This linear exploration paradigm prevents systematic branching into new attack directions, leaving large portions of the VLM vulnerability landscape unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261650/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework is bootstrapped with a single one-shot seed example and 500 malicious queries from SafeBench (10 categories). It produces adversarial image-text pairs generated via Qwen-Image and Qwen-Image-Edit, a hierarchical strategy tree (abstract parent categories → concrete leaf strategies, each tracking ASR, exploitation budget, and dominant failure mode), and per-model attack success rates. A Strategy Consistency Checker (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) filters out samples that drift from the intended strategy before ASR is recorded.

## Method

TreeTeaming replaces static strategy testing with a dynamically evolving Strategy Tree organized hierarchically: a root objective, parent nodes representing abstract strategy categories (e.g., 'Cognitive bias exploitation'), and leaf nodes containing concrete executable strategies. An LLM-based Orchestrator (Qwen2.5-72B) governs exploration via a dynamic threshold that linearly decays as the tree grows, deciding each round whether to exploit a promising leaf (refine its description using its Dominant Failure Mode) or explore a new direction (generate a novel leaf and place it under an existing or new parent). A Multimodal Actuator equipped with 11 tools across four categories (geometric, color, composition, generative) translates each leaf strategy into an image-text test case through multi-step tool invocation. A dual-loop feedback mechanism operates at sample level (failure classification triggers retries up to 5 attempts) and strategy level (aggregated failure logs produce a Dominant Failure Mode that informs the Orchestrator's next decision), closing the evolutionary loop.

## Results

TreeTeaming achieves state-of-the-art Attack Success Rate (ASR) on 11 out of 12 evaluated VLMs, with an average ASR of 89.48% across all models. Headline results include 100.00% on LLaVA-1.5, 99.00% on Qwen-VL, 98.00% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 87.60% on GPT-4o, and 61.60% on Claude-3.5 Sonnet. The method also achieves the highest visual diversity (0.641) and lowest toxicity (6.63% textual, 9.86% visual) among all compared methods, and its discovered strategy set surpasses the union of 15 prior public jailbreak strategies in both kNN-Distance (0.282 vs 0.263 at n=15) and kNN-Entropy (2.723 vs 2.694).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TreeTeaming outperforms all seven jailbreak baselines (FigStep, MML, SI-Attack, MM-safety, Hades, CS-DJ, JOOD) on 11 of 12 target models; the sole exception is GPT-4o, where MML achieves 97.80% ASR versus TreeTeaming's 87.60%, though MML completely fails (0.00%) on LLaVA-1.5 and DeepSeek-VL, revealing severe brittleness. Against the prior red-teaming method Trust-VLM, TreeTeaming wins on every comparable model, including a 5.56-point margin on GPT-4o (87.60% vs 82.04%) and 5.00 points on LLaVA-1.5 (100.00% vs 95.00%). Arondight is only reported on Qwen-VL (58.36%), where TreeTeaming achieves 99.00%. In terms of sample quality, TreeTeaming achieves the highest visual diversity (0.641 vs Trust-VLM's 0.500) and the lowest toxicity (6.63% textual, 9.86% visual vs Trust-VLM's 11.67% and 51.00%). For strategy diversity, TreeTeaming's autonomously discovered strategies surpass the union of 15 prior public methods on both kNN-Distance (0.282 vs 0.263) and kNN-Entropy (2.723 vs 2.694) at n=15.

## Relation to Prior Work

TreeTeaming departs from two established lines of VLM attack research: (1) single-heuristic jailbreak methods (FigStep, MML, SI-Attack, JOOD, etc.) that each instantiate one manually-designed trick, and (2) automated red-teaming frameworks (Arondight, TRUST-VLM) that generate diverse test cases but remain confined to a pre-specified strategic paradigm. Conceptually, it draws on LLM-agent-based red-teaming for text-only models (AutoDAN-Turbo, AutoRedTeamer) but introduces a hierarchical tree structure to organize strategy exploration, replacing the flat strategy pools used in those prior works. The multimodal Actuator with a 11-tool toolkit and the dual-loop failure analysis mechanism are novel engineering contributions that have no direct predecessor in the VLM jailbreak literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that autonomous strategy discovery can outperform the collective diversity of 15 hand-crafted attack methods, suggesting that human-designed attack taxonomies may be systematically incomplete. The 'Attention Diversion' paradigm extracted from the strategy tree, when injected into FigStep, boosts GPT-4o ASR from 3.4% to 87.5%, indicating that discovered meta-strategies can serve as universal plug-ins for existing methods. Open questions include whether the discovered strategies generalize to unseen model architectures, how the approach scales as VLM safety training improves, and whether the reliance on a 72B-parameter LLM for orchestration limits practical deployment. The low toxicity of generated attacks (6.63% textual) raises the question of whether such stealthy adversarial samples are harder to detect and defend against in production settings.
