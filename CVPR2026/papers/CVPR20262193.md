## Problem

Generating explicit, dynamic 3D scenes from a single image remains unsolved: multi-stage pipelines (video generation followed by per-scene 3D reconstruction) are slow—requiring 18–45 minutes per scene—and error-prone, while existing feed-forward methods produce only 2D video frames or static 3D representations without explicit geometry or motion. The paper identifies the absence of a unified framework that can directly synthesize a controllable, explicit 4D scene representation in a single forward pass as the critical gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, a user-specified camera trajectory encoded as per-pixel Plücker coordinate embeddings (T×H×W×6), and an optional text prompt. It outputs a deformable 3D Gaussian field—a set of M Gaussian primitives each with position, scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color features, plus per-frame deformation offsets (Δμ∈ℝ³, Δq∈ℝ⁴, Δs∈ℝ³)—that can be rendered into dynamic video, novel views, depth maps, or interacted with in real time. Inference completes in approximately 30 seconds on a single GPU, compared to 18–45 minutes for optimization-based baselines.

## Method

DIFF4SPLAT repurposes a pretrained CogVideoX video diffusion transformer (32 DiT blocks, 4096-dim hidden) by fine-tuning it with a Flow Matching loss on 130K metric-scale 4D-annotated scenes, so its latent space encodes 3D-consistent geometry. A novel Latent Dynamic Reconstruction Model (LDRM) then lifts these latents into explicit 3D: latent and camera-pose tokens are concatenated, processed by 16 transformer blocks with cross-attention to T5 text embeddings, and decoded via a 3D deconvolutional layer into per-pixel Gaussian attributes and a 10-dim deformation map. The deformable Gaussian field represents dynamics as per-frame offsets to a static Gaussian set, rendered through differentiable rasterization. Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule—static geometry pretraining at 256×256, high-resolution refinement at 512×512, then dynamic fine-tuning with a motion loss from CoTracker point tracks—using a composite objective of photometric (MSE+LPIPS), geometric (depth correlation + total variation), and motion (tracked-point displacement) losses.

## Results

DIFF4SPLAT achieves the best FVD (210.15) and CLIP-Score (23.12) among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest optimization-based baseline AC3D+Mosca (FVD 235.96, CLIP-Score 20.21) while running 90× faster (30 s vs. 45 min). On geometric integrity, it records the highest average MASt3R matches (5114.22) and subject consistency (88.32), and reduces Relative Pose Error to 0.012 (translation) and 0.008 (rotation) versus 3.001 and 0.810 for AC3D. The method slightly trails AC3D+Mosca on KVD (2.32 vs. 2.01) and QA-Quality (2.81 vs. 2.84), but leads on all other reported metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest two-stage baseline AC3D+Mosca, DIFF4SPLAT wins on FVD (210.15 vs. 235.96), CLIP-Score (23.12 vs. 20.21), CLIP-Aesthetic (5.23 vs. 5.00), average MASt3R matches (5114 vs. 4501), and subject consistency (88.32 vs. 86.23), while narrowly losing on KVD (2.32 vs. 2.01), QA-Quality (2.81 vs. 2.84), and background consistency (89.89 vs. 90.43). Compared to the other optimization-based baselines (AC3D+SaV, AC3D+Shape of Motion), DIFF4SPLAT dominates across nearly all metrics with large margins (e.g., FVD 210 vs. 327/373; CLIP-Score 23.1 vs. 19.0/16.2). Against the pure video-generation baselines CameraCtrl and AC3D, DIFF4SPLAT achieves substantially better geometric integrity (5114 vs. 2016/2489 matches) and reduces RPE by two orders of magnitude (0.012 vs. 3.001 translation), while also unlocking depth rasterization and real-time interaction that those 2D-only methods cannot provide. The 90× speedup over AC3D+Mosca (30 s vs. 45 min) is the most practically significant advantage, making dynamic 3D scene generation feasible in interactive settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFF4SPLAT builds on CogVideoX as its video diffusion backbone and extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with a deformable inter-frame module, but its core departure is from the dominant two-stage paradigm (generate video, then optimize 3D reconstruction) toward a single feed-forward pass that directly predicts a deformable 3D Gaussian field. The Video Latent Transformer (LDRM) is a novel architectural bridge that reinterprets 2D spatio-temporal diffusion latents as a dynamic point cloud conditioned on Plücker camera embeddings, a design not present in prior video diffusion or 3D generation work. The paper also extends the static single-image 3D generation line (Wonderland, DiffSplat) into the dynamic 4D regime, and introduces a large-scale metric-annotated 4D dataset to enable supervised training—addressing a data bottleneck that prior dynamic 3D methods sidestepped via per-scene optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By collapsing a multi-stage, per-scene optimization pipeline into a ~30-second feed-forward inference, DIFF4SPLAT makes dynamic 3D scene generation practical for interactive applications (real-time rendering, novel view synthesis, depth extraction) that were previously infeasible at scale. The explicit 3DGS representation also unlocks capabilities absent in implicit video models—precise camera pose control (RPE reduced by ~250×), depth rasterization, and real-time interaction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer temporal sequences, more complex multi-object dynamics, and whether the 32-A100/7-day training cost can be reduced; the paper also does not address physical plausibility of generated motions beyond point-tracking consistency.
