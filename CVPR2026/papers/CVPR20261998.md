## Problem

Fixed-resolution MLLMs suffer from distracted attention (irrelevant regions receiving high attention) and blurry vision (inability to zoom into fine details at 336² input resolution), degrading performance on detail-sensitive tasks. Existing training-free visual search methods rely on unstable raw attention maps or multi-step iterative cropping, lacking a robust single-pass mechanism that mimics the human Scan-Locate-Focus perceptual process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261998/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image (default 336² pixels for LLaVA-v1.5, or higher-resolution images split into tiles for the high-res variant) and a text question, and produces a free-form text answer. Unusually, the actual inference input is augmented to a triple (I, I_sub, X) where I_sub is a self-identified cropped sub-image added as extra visual tokens (~576 additional tokens), and the original image tokens are progressively pruned during forward pass, so the effective token count shrinks layer by layer.

## Method

SLoFo is a training-free, self-guided inference framework with two stages. In the Scan-Locate stage, a Semantic branch back-propagates the loss of the model's first generated token (planning anchor) through the attention scores to visual tokens at layer 14, producing a gradient-weighted semantic relevance map; a Structure branch computes PCA reconstruction error on early-layer (layer 7) visual hidden states to score token uniqueness, filtering out attention-sink regions. The two maps are fused into a Semantic-Structural Importance Map (SSIM) via a weighted sum (β_s=0.7), and a sliding-window search selects the sub-region with maximum internal-to-adjacent contrast for cropping. In the Focus stage, the LLM's transformer layers are divided into K=4 phases, and at the end of each phase the bottom 50% of remaining original-image tokens (ranked by attention to the planning anchor) are pruned, progressively increasing the signal-to-noise ratio of attention while reducing computational overhead from the added sub-image tokens.

## Results

On detail-sensitive tasks, SLoFo (built on LLaVA-v1.5-7B) achieves 63.02% on TextVQA (+4.79% over baseline), 27.69% on DocVQA (+12.01%), 57.59% on V* (+15.18%), and 44.25% on HR-Bench-4K (+9.125%). On general-purpose benchmarks, SLoFo gains +4.20% on VQAv2 (79.44%), +1.46% on GQA (61.78%), and +0.72% on SEED-Bench (66.89%). The high-resolution variant (SLoFo high-res) further improves to 64.67% on TextVQA, 61.78% on V*, and 46.125% on HR-Bench-4K, outperforming all training-free baselines including DyFo-LLaVA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLaVA-v1.5 baseline, SLoFo wins on every benchmark, with the largest gains on DocVQA (+12.01%) and V* (+15.18%). Among training-free visual-search methods, SLoFo outperforms ViCrop-LLaVA on all six detail-sensitive tasks (e.g., 63.02 vs 61.56 on TextVQA, 27.69 vs 19.84 on DocVQA) and also beats DyFo-LLaVA on POPE (88.19 vs 87.71), though DyFo retains a narrow edge on V* (59.16 vs 57.59) at default resolution; under the high-res setting SLoFo reverses this to 61.78 vs 59.16. SEAL achieves a much higher V* score (75.30) but requires additional trained localization modules and underperforms SLoFo on TextVQA (36.30 vs 63.02), DocVQA (5.31 vs 27.69), and POPE (82.40 vs 88.19). Compared to other MLLM backbones (InstructBLIP, MiniGPT4, DeepSeek-VL, QwenVL-chat), SLoFo-LLaVA leads on TextVQA, POPE, V*, and both HR-Bench tasks, though QwenVL-chat scores higher on DocVQA (37.25 vs 27.69) due to its 448² input resolution. On general-purpose tasks (Table 2), SLoFo consistently improves over the LLaVA-v1.5 baseline across GQA, SEED-Bench, VQAv2, ScienceQA, and MME, with the most notable gain being +4.20% on VQAv2.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLoFo extends the training-free visual search line (ViCrop-LLaVA, DyFO-LLaVA, SEAL) by replacing their reliance on raw attention maps or multi-step search with a single-pass, self-guided pipeline that fuses gradient-weighted semantic attention with a PCA-based structural uniqueness score. It draws on the emergent response-planning observation in LLMs [7] to use the first generated token as a planning anchor, and explicitly addresses the attention-sink problem [1,6,14] that undermines pure attention-based region selection. The progressive token-pruning strategy during inference is inspired by PyramidDrop's finding that visual tokens matter most in early layers, but is applied here under a multi-image input setting to simultaneously improve focus and reduce overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because SLoFo is entirely training-free and self-guided, it can be dropped onto any fixed-resolution MLLM (LLaVA-v1.5, InstructBLIP, etc.) without retraining or external detectors, making it a practical plug-in for improving detail perception in deployed systems. The key open questions the authors identify include: whether adaptive-resolution strategies can eliminate the sub-image overhead, whether multi-sub-image or dynamic selection can handle relational/counting tasks, and whether the approach generalizes beyond VQA-style benchmarks to open-ended dialogue and multi-turn reasoning. The single-sub-image design and VQA-only evaluation remain clear limitations for broader adoption.
