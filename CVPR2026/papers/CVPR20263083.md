## Problem

Active stereo matching lacks large-scale ground-truth depth datasets, forcing existing methods to rely on unstable self-supervised reprojection losses or synthetic data with significant domain gaps. This results in predictions that lack clarity and accuracy compared to supervised models, particularly in real-world scenes with textureless regions or non-Lambertian materials.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a pair of 848×480 infrared stereo images (left and right) captured by a RealSense D435 sensor, along with a binary IR projection pattern extracted by comparing seven IR frames at different emitter intensities and applying local window normalization with binarization. The output is a dense disparity map (converted to depth via the pinhole model with COLMAP-estimated focal length and camera baseline), evaluated at 256×512 random crops during training.

## Method

The core idea is to use 2D Gaussian Splatting trained on zero-emitter IR images to reconstruct scene geometry and render depth maps, from which disparity proxy labels (d = b·f/z) are derived via TSDF fusion—providing supervised training signals without any ground-truth depth. The same 2DGS model synthesizes novel-view stereo pairs with projected IR patterns (back-projected through the pinhole model using rendered depth) for data augmentation. During training, a hybrid supervision regularization strategy adaptively reweights the proxy-supervised smooth-L1 disparity loss and the self-supervised reprojection loss on binary patterns at each iteration, using inverse-proportional updates to loss-change ratios with softmax normalization and clamping. The full pipeline is trained in a mixed-domain setting on both real-world proxy-labeled data and Blender-rendered synthetic data (via DREDS), with PSMNet, RAFT, or StereoNet as the stereo backbone.

## Results

On the simulated test set, GS-ASM achieves the lowest EPE across all three backbone networks: 0.2613 px (PSMNet), 0.1279 px (RAFT), and 0.4124 px (StereoNet), compared to 0.4300/0.4715/0.5551 px for the ActiveZero baseline and 0.5488/0.5428/0.7190 px for the D435 sensor. The best configuration (RAFT + GS-ASM) reaches 97.44% of pixels within 1 px error and 99.55% within 5 px, substantially exceeding both baselines. Qualitative results on real-world scenes confirm that GS-ASM produces sharper disparity details and fewer boundary artifacts than both ActiveZero and the D435 sensor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-ASM is compared against two baselines: ActiveZero [Liu et al., CVPR 2022], a mixed-domain self-supervised + synthetic-supervised active stereo method, and the Intel RealSense D435 commercial depth sensor. GS-ASM outperforms ActiveZero on every metric and every backbone: with RAFT, EPE drops from 0.4715 to 0.1279 px (a 73% reduction) and 1-px accuracy rises from 93.06% to 97.44%. Against the D435 sensor, the gains are even larger—RAFT + GS-ASM achieves 0.1279 px EPE versus 0.5428 px for D435, and 1-px accuracy of 97.44% versus 95.13%. The advantage is most pronounced on the RAFT backbone and least on StereoNet, where the absolute EPE gap narrows (0.4124 vs. 0.5551 for ActiveZero, 0.7190 for D435), though GS-ASM still leads on all four metrics. On real-world scenes, the paper reports that ActiveZero generalizes poorly and produces worse results than the D435 sensor, whereas GS-ASM remains robust even in regions where its own proxy labels contain inaccuracies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the active stereo matching line initiated by ActiveStereoNet and ActiveZero, which established self-supervised and mixed-domain (synthetic + real) training paradigms for IR pattern-based depth estimation. The key conceptual departure is repurposing 2D Gaussian Splatting — originally a novel-view synthesis tool — as a proxy-label generator directly from real-world multi-view captures, thereby eliminating the synthetic-to-real domain gap that plagues prior mixed-domain approaches. The hybrid supervision regularization (adaptive loss weighting between proxy-supervised and self-supervised terms) is a new training strategy not present in ActiveZero or other stereo matching frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in active stereo research: the absence of large-scale, accurately-labeled real-world IR stereo datasets. By demonstrating that 2DGS-rendered proxy labels from real scenes can drive supervised training that outperforms both pure self-supervision and synthetic-data supervision on real data, it offers a practical recipe for the field. However, the method remains tightly coupled to RealSense hardware and controlled indoor environments; outdoor generalization under ambient IR interference, scalability to other active sensor designs, and the reliability of proxy labels in highly reflective or transparent regions remain open. The released 100K+ image dataset and active 2DGS model will likely become a reference resource for subsequent active stereo work.
