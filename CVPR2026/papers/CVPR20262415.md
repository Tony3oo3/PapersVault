## Problem

Most perceptual video quality assessment (VQA) systems are trained on SDR content and fail to capture HDR-specific distortions such as near-black crushing, highlight clipping, banding, and exposure flicker that are amplified in user-generated HDR video. Existing HDR subjective datasets are small, limited to synthetic distortions or professionally produced content, and do not reflect the heterogeneous capture conditions and compression artifacts of real-world HDR-UGC. Additionally, multimodal large language models applied to VQA suffer from modality neglect, where policies rely on textual priors rather than exploiting HDR visual cues.

## Input / Output

The model ingests 10-bit PQ HDR video clips (BT.2020 gamut) at native precision without linear SDR downscaling, alongside a deterministic tone-mapped SDR counterpart and a text prompt. Each clip is uniformly sampled into T=8 frames, and visual tokens from both the HDR-aware encoder and a standard SDR encoder feed the language decoder via learned projections. The output is a continuous MOS score on a 0–100 Likert scale accompanied by a concise, HDR-grounded chain-of-thought rationale identifying specific distortions (e.g., banding, chroma shifts, jitter) and linking them to the perceptual judgment.

## Method

HDR-Q couples two novel components: (i) an HDR-aware vision encoder that adapts SigLIP-2 on HDR frame-caption pairs using dual-domain contrastive supervision, enforcing that HDR embeddings remain closer to their captions than SDR embeddings to prevent modality collapse; and (ii) HDR-Aware Policy Optimization (HAPO), an RL fine-tuning framework extending GRPO with three mechanisms: an HDR-SDR contrastive KL term that maximizes the divergence between rollouts with and without HDR tokens to provably increase conditional mutual information between HDR inputs and outputs, dual-entropy regularization on both pathways to prevent entropy inflation, and High-Entropy Weighting (HEW) that rescales token-level advantages by per-token entropy to concentrate gradients on informative reasoning steps. A Gaussian-weighted regression reward enables fine-grained MOS calibration, while group-level self-rewarding improves reasoning consistency. The full model is built on Ovis2.5 with rank-4 LoRA adapters and trained in two stages (modality alignment, then full RL fine-tuning) on the Beyond8Bits dataset of ~44K HDR-UGC videos with >1.5M crowd ratings.

## Results

On the Beyond8Bits dataset, HDR-Q (full) achieves SRCC 0.9206, PLCC 0.9118, RMSE 5.16, and KRCC 0.7218, outperforming the best deep-learning baseline HIDRO-VQA (SRCC 0.8508, PLCC 0.8784, RMSE 6.09) and all MLLM-based VQA methods by a wide margin. Zero-shot transfer to LIVE-HDR yields SRCC 0.9081 / PLCC 0.8978 / RMSE 7.60, and to SFV+HDR yields SRCC 0.7251 / PLCC 0.7502 / RMSE 0.25, again surpassing all baselines without retraining. The ablation confirms that the HDR-aware encoder contributes the largest single gain (SRCC 0.81→0.83 with GRPO alone), while HAPO's HDR-SDR KL, dual-entropy regularization, and HEW collectively push SRCC from 0.83 to 0.92.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDR-Q dominates all 21 baselines across every metric on Beyond8Bits. Against the strongest deep-learning VQA method, HIDRO-VQA [49], HDR-Q improves SRCC by 0.07 (0.9206 vs 0.8508) and reduces RMSE by 15% (5.16 vs 6.09). Against the best MLLM-based VQA model, Q-Insight [27], the gap is dramatic: SRCC 0.9206 vs 0.5170 and RMSE 5.16 vs 20.78, indicating that standard MLLM VQA pipelines are fundamentally ill-suited to HDR-UGC. Base MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Ovis2.5, GLM-4.1V, OmniLong) all score below SRCC 0.35, confirming that off-the-shelf multimodal models lack HDR perceptual sensitivity. On the cross-dataset LIVE-HDR benchmark, HDR-Q (SRCC 0.9081) still outperforms HIDRO-VQA (0.8793) and HDR-ChipQA (0.8250) zero-shot; on SFV+HDR it leads with SRCC 0.7251 vs HIDRO-VQA's 0.7003. The SDR-only variant of HDR-Q (SRCC 0.8914) already surpasses all prior methods, but the full HDR-aware pipeline adds a further 0.03 SRCC gain, validating the HDR-SDR contrastive KL and HDR-aware encoder as complementary contributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024) with three HDR-specific modifications—contrastive KL grounding, dual-entropy regularization, and entropy-weighted advantage shaping—to address modality neglect in perception-heavy MLLM tasks. It also builds on the MLLM-based VQA line (Q-Align, Q-Instruct, Q-Insight, DeQA, VisualQuality-R1) but departs from them by introducing an HDR-aware SigLIP-2 vision encoder trained with dual-domain contrastive supervision, and by operating natively on 10-bit PQ signals rather than tone-mapped SDR. On the data side, it extends prior HDR VQA benchmarks (LIVE-HDR, SFV+HDR) by shifting from professionally produced content to heterogeneous real-world UGC, filling a gap the authors identify as a major obstacle to generalizable HDR quality models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Beyond8Bits (44K videos, 1.5M ratings) is the first large-scale crowdsourced HDR-UGC quality dataset and will likely become a standard benchmark for HDR-aware VQA research. The finding that the SDR-only variant of HDR-Q already reaches 0.8914 SRCC—close to the full model's 0.9206—suggests that much of an MLLM's VQA capability derives from general video understanding, with HDR-specific processing contributing a meaningful but bounded increment; this raises open questions about whether the added complexity of HDR-aware encoders and contrastive KL is justified across all HDR content types. The zero-shot transfer to LIVE-HDR and SFV+HDR without retraining is encouraging but leaves open whether the approach generalizes to other extended-range formats (e.g., HDR10+, Dolby Vision) or to non-UGC professional HDR pipelines with different distortion profiles.
