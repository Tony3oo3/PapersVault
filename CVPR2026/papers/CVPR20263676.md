## Problem

Existing exemplar-free class-incremental learning (EFCIL) methods leverage pre-trained models with parameter-efficient tuning but overlook a fundamental cause of catastrophic forgetting: the simultaneous collapse of both intra-class structure (the shape of individual class distributions) and inter-class structure (the geometric relationships among class prototypes) as the feature space migrates during fine-tuning on new tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263676/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes standard RGB images (224×224 for ViT-B/16) and outputs a class label from the cumulative label space across all tasks seen so far. A key operational detail is that the cosine classifier is retrained after each task using synthetic features sampled from Gaussian prototype distributions (mean and covariance per class), since no old-task data is available for direct replay. The classifier weight matrix expands incrementally by appending new class weights while old weights are frozen.

## Method

The framework builds on a pre-trained ViT-B/16-IN21K backbone with LoRA (rank 64) applied to Query and Value projections, combined with a cosine classifier. Two complementary mechanisms preserve the class-discriminative structure: (1) Adaptive Prototype Rectification (APR) preserves intra-class structure by inferring old-class displacement from new-class displacements via cosine-similarity-weighted attention, then updating both prototype centers and covariance matrices (adding a rank-one perturbation scaled by a hyperparameter ρ); (2) Structural Consistency Constraint (SCC) preserves inter-class structure by penalizing changes in the cosine-similarity vectors between new-task samples and all old prototypes, acting as a lightweight geometric regularization during training. After each task, APR rectifies all old-class prototypes and synthetic features are drawn from the updated Gaussian distributions to retrain the full classifier, ensuring balanced representation of old and new classes without any stored exemplars.

## Results

On ImageNet-A (5-task), the proposed EFCIL-PCDS achieves 66.65±0.20% Last-Acc and 74.09±4.09% Inc-Acc, surpassing the strongest baseline SSIAT by 2.44% and 2.46% respectively. On the fine-grained Cars-196 dataset (10-task), it reaches 80.23±0.68% Last-Acc and 85.53±0.35% Inc-Acc, improving over SSIAT by 11.24% and 9.66%. On ObjectNet (10-task), the method attains 63.39±0.71% Last-Acc, outperforming SLCA by 3.89 points, and on OmniBenchmark it achieves 79.19±0.16% Last-Acc, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the majority of dataset-task settings. The closest competitor is SSIAT [28], which the method surpasses by 2.44% Last-Acc on ImageNet-A (5-task) and by a substantial 11.24% on Cars-196 (10-task), indicating the advantage is most pronounced on fine-grained data. SLCA [36] is the second-strongest baseline, beaten by 5.62% on ImageNet-A (5-task) and 13.74% on Cars-196 (10-task). Prompt-based methods (L2P [32], DualPrompt [31]) and adapter-based methods (EASE [41], infLoRA [22], LAE [11]) trail by 10–25% on most settings, confirming that parameter-efficient tuning alone is insufficient without explicit structure preservation. On ImageNet-R (10-task), the method's 79.17% Last-Acc is marginally below SSIAT's 79.28%, but it achieves the best Inc-Acc (83.84% vs. 83.63%). In the ablation comparisons, APR outperforms SDC [34] by 3.97% on Cars-196 (80.23% vs. 76.26%), and SCC outperforms both logit distillation [21] (67.76%) and feature distillation [8] (75.37%) on Cars-196, demonstrating that geometric structure preservation is more effective than output-level consistency constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the EFCIL-on-pre-trained-models paradigm, building on parameter-efficient tuning (LoRA, adapters, prompts) as the backbone adaptation strategy. Its key departure is reframing catastrophic forgetting not as a parameter-update problem but as a geometric one: the simultaneous collapse of intra-class shape (prototype center + covariance) and inter-class angular relationships. While prototype correction (SDC, SSIAT) and distillation-based constraints (logit, feature) have been explored individually, this paper unifies them into a single structural-preservation framework—APR for intra-class and SCC for inter-class—treating the two as complementary rather than competing remedies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition of forgetting into intra-class and inter-class components provides a useful diagnostic lens, and the ablation showing their complementary dominance (SCC on coarse-grained, APR on fine-grained) gives practitioners guidance on which mechanism matters most for their setting. The finding that standard logit/feature distillation actively degrades fine-grained performance (Cars-196 drops ~9 points) challenges the default assumption that output alignment is always beneficial. Open questions include whether the Gaussian prototype assumption holds for highly non-convex class manifolds, whether the framework extends to non-ViT backbones or very long task sequences (beyond 20 tasks), and whether the angular-consistency constraint scales gracefully as the number of old prototypes grows.
