## Problem

Existing vision-language-action models treat force as a passive perceptual cue rather than an active control signal, lacking mechanisms to reason about stage-specific contact dynamics and to perform closed-loop hybrid force-position regulation. This limits stability, precision, and robustness in contact-rich manipulation tasks where force feedback is essential for safe and accurate interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261515/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three RGB camera streams (resized to 480×640), a global task prompt, a discrete force prompt encoding the current subtask state (functioning as a finite-state machine), the 7D end-effector pose, and a 6D force-torque signal sampled at 300 Hz. It outputs a 13-dimensional hybrid action (7D EE pose delta + 6D contact force) plus a continuous subtask progress scalar s_t ∈ [0,1] that triggers subtask transitions when exceeding a threshold. Notably, force enters the network at two distinct scales: as a textual prompt fused with vision-language tokens in the VLM, and as a raw signal that bypasses cross-modal fusion to feed the MoE directly.

## Method

ForceVLA2 employs a dual-level architecture inspired by human sensorimotor hierarchy. At the long-horizon level, force prompts (textual cues indicating the current subtask) are concatenated with task instructions and encoded alongside multi-view images by a SigLIP-based VLM (PaliGemma) to construct force-aware task concepts. At the short-horizon level, a Cross-Scale Mixture-of-Experts with three modality-specific MLP experts (visual, state, force) dynamically routes tokens to emphasize the most informative modality per manipulation phase, while a gradient bypass path delivers raw force directly to the MoE for reactive compliance. A flow-matching policy head then denoises from Gaussian noise to produce the hybrid force-position action, conditioned on the MoE-fused representation. Subtask transitions are governed by a probabilistic model combining Beta (orientation alignment), Exponential (remaining distance), and Uniform (contact force) distributions, trained end-to-end via imitation learning on 1,000 GELLO-teleoperated trajectories.

## Results

On the ForceVLA2-Dataset (5 contact-rich tasks, 20 trials each), ForceVLA2 achieves a 66.0% average success rate, outperforming π0 (18.0%) by 48.0 percentage points and π0.5 (31.0%) by 35.0 percentage points. The largest single-task gain is on Assemble Gears, where ForceVLA2 reaches 70.0% versus 10.0% for ForceVLA (the second-best method), a 50-point margin. ForceVLA2 also substantially reduces arm-overload failures on force-intensive tasks such as Clean Board (70.0% vs. 55.0% for ForceVLA) and Press Bottle (80.0% vs. 70.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForceVLA2 wins on all five tasks against every baseline. Against π0 (18.0% avg), the improvement is 48.0 points, with the most dramatic gap on Retrieve Plate (35.0% vs. 0.0%) and Assemble Gears (70.0% vs. 0.0%), where π0 completely fails. Against π0.5 (31.0% avg), ForceVLA2 leads by 35.0 points, again with the largest margin on Assemble Gears (70.0% vs. 20.0%). Against ForceVLA (35.0% avg), the closest competitor, ForceVLA2 gains 31.0 points on average; the gap is most pronounced on Clean Vase (75.0% vs. 25.0%) and Assemble Gears (70.0% vs. 10.0%). ACP (16.0% avg) and π0 with naive force input (17.0% avg) both underperform even π0 without force, confirming that simply concatenating force signals as an auxiliary input degrades rather than helps VLA performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ForceVLA2 builds directly on the π₀ flow-matching VLA architecture and extends ForceVLA's use of force as a perceptual input by elevating force to an active control signal within a closed-loop hybrid force-position framework. The key architectural departure is the Cross-Scale Mixture-of-Experts module, which dynamically routes between visual, proprioceptive, and force modalities at each token, and the force-prompt mechanism that encodes subtask-level physical context into the VLM's reasoning. This positions the work at the intersection of dual-system VLA designs (π₀.₅, GR00T-N1) and classical impedance/admittance control, but unifies them end-to-end rather than treating force as a post-hoc correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first VLA that outputs both position and force commands in a single end-to-end policy, which is a practically important step toward deploying VLAs in tasks where contact force regulation is non-negotiable (assembly, insertion, compliant wiping). However, the evaluation is limited to 5 tasks with 1,000 trajectories and 20 trials each, and the force-prompt subtask segmentation relies on manually predefined task graphs, raising open questions about scalability to open-ended or novel contact-rich scenarios. Whether the Cross-Scale MoE routing generalizes beyond the five demonstrated tasks, and whether the probabilistic subtask-transition model (Eq. 10) is the right inductive bias or an over-parameterized heuristic, remain unresolved.
