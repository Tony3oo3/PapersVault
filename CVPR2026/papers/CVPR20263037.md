## Problem

Existing ViT compression methods for edge devices treat all classes equally, retaining redundant and even class-detrimental knowledge that degrades performance on the specific classes an edge device actually needs. Moreover, the diversity of target classes and resource constraints across edge devices demands many customized models, but current pruning methods are too slow and computationally expensive to scale to such large-scale deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263037/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NuWa takes a frozen pre-trained base ViT (e.g., DeiT-Base), a target class subset S, class-specific calibration data D_S (128 images for OFP, batch size 1 for SKP), and a target pruning rate α or resource constraint (measured in GFLOPs). It produces a structured-pruned edge ViT with reduced MLP intermediate dimensions and MHA QK/VO dimensions, requiring no post-pruning retraining. The SVD-based MHA pruning results are data-free and reusable across all sub-tasks, while MLP pruning requires one forward pass on D_S to compute activation features.

## Method

NuWa operates in two stages. First, Self-Knowledge Purification (SKP) freezes the base ViT and embeds learnable binary mask vectors and control factors into each MLP module, using a straight-through estimator to handle the non-differentiable binarization. Training only these masks (≤0.05% of total parameters) with class-specific data under the original task loss autonomously identifies and prunes class-detrimental weights, yielding an anchor model that is both smaller and more accurate on target classes than the base ViT. Second, Optimization-based Fast Pruning (OFP) further compresses the anchor model without retraining: MHA pruning is formulated as a low-rank matrix approximation of W_QK and W_VO, solved via SVD (Eckart-Young theorem) with an adaptive energy-ratio hyperparameter ρ, while MLP pruning is formulated as a least-squares problem over activation features, solved in closed form via the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse. This eliminates the need for iterative retraining, enabling 33.69× speedup over the best training-dependent baseline.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with DeiT-Base, NuWa achieves average class-specific accuracy improvements of +15.37% (pruning rate 0.40) and +10.04% (pruning rate 0.60) over the unpruned base model, without any retraining. At pruning rate 0.60, NuWa outperforms the best training-free method (Numerical Pruning) by up to 29.00% in accuracy. In terms of derivation efficiency, NuWa achieves a 33.69× speedup over X-Pruner (the best training-dependent baseline) with only 0.61% average accuracy loss, and reduces large-scale deployment cost (N=50 sub-tasks, M=10 devices) from $23,074 to $59.12 (99.83% reduction). On Jetson Orin NX, derived models achieve 1.31×–2.07× inference speedup with 26.47%–58.82% memory reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-free baselines (Magnitude Pruning, Wanda-sp, Numerical Pruning), NuWa achieves up to 29.00% higher class-specific accuracy at pruning rate 0.60, because these methods retain class-detrimental weights that degrade target-class performance. Among training-dependent baselines, NuWa (without fine-tuning) reaches 99.35%, 100.03%, and 100.23% of the average accuracy of X-Pruner, RECAP, and MDP respectively at pruning rate 0.40; with 10-epoch fine-tuning, NuWa surpasses them by 2.00%, 2.64%, and 2.82% on average. DC-ViT performs worst, dropping below the base model at pruning rate 0.60 (e.g., 73.73% vs. 78.60% on 10-class sub-tasks). In derivation efficiency, NuWa requires only 0.07–0.09 GPU hours per model versus 0.89–11.27 GPU hours for baselines, yielding a 33.69× speedup over X-Pruner and reducing AWS deployment cost by up to 99.83% for large-scale (N=50, M=10) scenarios. The key differentiator is that NuWa's SKP step identifies and removes class-detrimental weights, enabling the pruned model to *exceed* the base model's accuracy on target classes—a property no baseline achieves without full fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard class-agnostic structured pruning paradigm for ViTs by introducing a class-specific derivation setting, where the goal is to retain only knowledge relevant to a target subset of classes. It builds on SVD-based low-rank approximation (Eckart-Young theorem) for MHA compression and straight-through estimators for binary mask learning, but repurposes them within a two-stage pipeline: Self-Knowledge Purification (SKP) to identify class-detrimental weights, followed by closed-form optimization for further compression. Unlike training-dependent methods (X-Pruner, RECAP, MDP) that rely on expensive retraining, NuWa eliminates retraining entirely by solving pruning as analytical optimization problems, and unlike training-free methods (Magnitude, Wanda, Numerical Pruning), it explicitly targets the removal of weights that harm target-class performance rather than merely preserving the base model's behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 33.69× derivation speedup and 99.83% cost reduction make large-scale, heterogeneous edge deployment of class-specific ViTs practically feasible for the first time, addressing a real bottleneck in deploying customized models across diverse devices. The discovery of class-detrimental weights challenges the implicit assumption in pruning literature that all removed weights represent lost knowledge, suggesting that base ViTs contain redundant or actively harmful representations for specific class subsets. Open questions include whether the class-detrimental phenomenon generalizes to non-ImageNet pre-training regimes, whether SKP can be extended to MHA modules (currently restricted to MLPs based on empirical observation), and whether the approach transfers to other transformer architectures beyond ViTs and Swin.
