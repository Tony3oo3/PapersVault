## Problem

Existing digital human systems rely on fragmented, modality-specific expert models that suffer from distribution mismatches, redundant capacity, and brittleness when coordinated as a unified pipeline. Meanwhile, contemporary unified multimodal models either restrict outputs to text or omit critical digital-human modalities such as speech audio, 3DMM animation, and temporally consistent identity. No prior work provides a single pretrained model capable of any-to-any generation and editing across the full set of digital human modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260078/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any arbitrary subset of seven modalities—text description, script, speech audio (16 kHz), 3DMM shape/expression/pose parameters, semantic video (21-class face segmentation at 128×128), reference image (256×256), and RGB video—as structured natural-language prompts, and autoregressively generates any combination of the remaining modalities. The final RGB video is not produced directly by the language model but is decoded at 512×512 resolution by a separate diffusion model conditioned on the generated semantic video, reference image, and text description. A 5-second 30 fps clip is represented as roughly 2,250 semantic tokens (vs. ~9,000 for raw RGB), fitting within the 8K context window.

## Method

Archon unifies seven modalities through modality-specific tokenizers (MAGVITv2 for images, SoundStream RVQ for speech, three VQVAEs for 3DMM shape/expression/pose, and a finetuned MAGVITv2 variant for semantic video) whose discrete tokens are merged into a single 550K-vocabulary space consumed by a 1B-parameter PaLM2 autoregressive backbone. A key efficiency contribution is the semantic video reparameterization: replacing RGB frames with 21-class discrete semantic labels reduces token count by 4× while preserving structural dynamics, with a WALT-based latent diffusion decoder (v-prediction, MSE loss) upsampling to 512×512 video conditioned on semantic masks, reference image, and text. Training spans 72 multimodal tasks over 6,000 hours of synchronized monologue video, using a perplexity-weighted, modality-balanced sampling strategy to avoid bias toward easier or more frequent tasks. At inference, a 'Thinking in Modality' strategy decomposes high-uncertainty cross-modal transitions (e.g., speech→video) into stepwise chains through intermediate modalities (3DMM, description, semantic video), progressively reducing ambiguity without any retraining.

## Results

On speech-driven video generation, Archon achieves FID 6.82 and FVD 93.8 on CelebV-HQ, and FID 5.78 and FVD 81.6 on HDTF, substantially outperforming all baselines on both perceptual quality metrics. Lip synchronization (Sync-C 5.21/6.20, Sync-D 9.00/8.82) and image quality (IQA 3.79/3.94) are competitive with or exceed task-specific models trained directly on the benchmark data. On image-conditioned text-to-speech, Archon achieves C-SIM 0.912/0.900 and Identity Accuracy 0.622/0.591 on CelebV-HQ/HDTF, surpassing FaceTTS on identity coherence while trailing slightly on MCD-DTW (8.92/8.98 vs. 7.94/7.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Archon decisively outperforms all three video generation baselines on FID and FVD across both benchmarks, with FID improvements of 2.3× over Hallo3 (6.82 vs. 15.67 on CelebV-HQ) and 7.3× over AniPortrait (6.82 vs. 39.73). On lip synchronization, Archon matches or exceeds Hallo3 on Sync-D (9.00 vs. 9.16) and Sync-C on HDTF (6.20 vs. 6.38, a narrow loss), while substantially surpassing AniPortrait and EchoMimic on both Sync metrics. For image-conditioned text-to-speech, Archon beats FaceTTS on voice identity coherence (C-SIM 0.912 vs. 0.905) and identity accuracy (0.622 vs. 0.603) on CelebV-HQ, but trails on MCD-DTW (8.92 vs. 7.94), which the authors attribute to using a lightweight general-purpose detokenizer rather than a task-specific audio diffusion model. Notably, Archon is evaluated zero-shot on both benchmarks without any fine-tuning, whereas EchoMimic and Hallo3 (marked with *) were trained directly on the evaluation data, making the FID/FVD margins particularly striking.

## Relation to Prior Work

Archon extends the unified multimodal generation paradigm (VideoPoet, Gemini, AudioLM) into the digital human domain, where prior unified models either omit speech/animation modalities or restrict outputs to text. It repurposes MAGVITv2 for a novel semantic video tokenizer and adapts WALT as a semantic-conditioned video diffusion decoder, but the core architectural contribution is the autoregressive PaLM2 backbone trained across 72 cross-modal tasks with a structured prompt design that avoids combinatorial task-token explosion. The 'Thinking in Modality' inference strategy departs from prior any-to-any generation by decomposing ambiguous transitions into intermediate modality chains, a technique not present in prior unified models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model to treat all seven digital-human modalities (description, script, speech, 3DMM animation, semantic video, image, video) as a single joint distribution, enabling any-modality editing without retraining. The 4× token reduction via semantic video reparameterization addresses a practical bottleneck that has limited prior unified models from handling high-fidelity talking videos within standard context windows. Open questions include scalability beyond 5-second monologue clips at 256×256, multi-person or full-body generation, and whether the Thinking-in-Modality chain generalizes to longer or more ambiguous cross-modal gaps. The 1B parameter scale and reliance on Gemini 2.5 Pro for dataset capping also raise questions about how much of the performance is attributable to the unified architecture versus data quality.
