## Problem

Existing infrared and visible image fusion (IVIF) methods are locked into a single fusion objective and cannot adaptively satisfy heterogeneous demands from human perception, vision-language model instructions, or downstream task models (detection, segmentation). Building separate models for each preference is inflexible and does not scale, leaving a critical gap for a unified framework that aligns fusion output with diverse, user-specified criteria.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262605/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired infrared-visible image (cropped to 256×256 patches), a preference text prompt (generated by human annotators, Qwen3-Omni VLM, or task models YOLOv11/SegFormer), and optionally a binary region mask indicating where the preference should apply. It outputs a single fused infrared-visible image that is globally consistent with the source pair but locally adjusted to match the specified preference within the masked region. The preference signal can be global (full-image mask) or instance-level (localized mask), enabling both holistic quality alignment and fine-grained regional adaptation.

## Method

DPOFusion is a two-stage latent diffusion framework. The first stage, PALDM, is a latent diffusion model conditioned on concatenated source latents, a CLIP-encoded text prompt, and timestep; it is trained with a joint conditional loss that simultaneously denoises the standard fused latent and property-interpolated latents (linear blends between source and prior-fused latents at N discrete levels), producing a diverse candidate pool. The second stage, PCLDM, is initialized from a copy of the frozen PALDM and connected via a zero-initialized 1×1 convolution (ControlNet-style), then fine-tuned with the proposed Instance Direct Preference Optimization (IDPO) loss. IDPO applies the DPO preference objective exclusively within a masked region of interest while enforcing strict pixel-level consistency with the frozen reference outside the mask, achieving localized preference alignment without degrading global fusion quality. This decoupling of candidate generation from preference alignment, combined with region-restricted DPO, is the core architectural and training contribution.

## Results

On the LLVIP dataset, RLHF achieves the best overall fusion quality with EN 7.725, SD 61.911, AG 5.977, MUS 57.295, and CNN 0.655, while RLVF leads on EN (7.554), SD (53.299), and AG (5.439). On MSRS, RLVF attains the highest EN (7.203), SD (56.614), and AG (5.782), surpassing the best baseline SHIP by 11.9% in SD and 46.8% in AG. On RoadScene, RLVF achieves the highest AG (8.622) and EN (7.574), while RLHF leads in MUS (43.785) and CNN (0.539). For downstream tasks, RLDF-Seg improves mIoU to 55.96 on MSRS (+0.5% over the best baseline), and RLDF-OD achieves mAP@.5:95 of 46.03 on M3FD (+4.2% over the best baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPOFusion (both RLVF and RLHF variants) outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of metrics across all three datasets. RLVF dominates on information-theoretic and texture metrics (EN, SD, AG), achieving the highest SD on LLVIP (53.299 vs. 50.961 for U2Fusion) and MSRS (56.614 vs. 44.591 for EMMA), while RLHF leads on perceptual quality metrics (MUS, CNN), e.g., MUS 57.295 on LLVIP vs. 56.683 for EMMA. The closest competitor is EMMA (CVPR24), which achieves the highest SD on RoadScene (56.300) but is surpassed by RLVF on EN (7.574 vs. 7.499) and AG (8.622 vs. 6.163) on the same dataset. SHIP (CVPR24) is the strongest baseline on MSRS (EN 6.429, SD 41.152) yet trails RLVF by 11.9% in SD and 46.8% in AG. Among the 2025 methods, DCEvo and GIFNet are competitive on individual metrics (e.g., GIFNet achieves CNN 0.605 on RoadScene) but are consistently outperformed by DPOFusion on the aggregate of all five metrics. On downstream tasks, RLDF-OD improves mAP@.5:95 by 4.2% over the best baseline (46.03 vs. 42.93 for DCEvo on M3FD), and RLDF-Seg gains 0.5% mIoU over the best baseline (55.96 vs. 55.66 for DCEvo on MSRS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the DPO alignment paradigm—originally developed for LLMs (Rafailov et al., 2023) and adapted to text-to-image diffusion (Wallace et al., 2024)—into the infrared-visible image fusion domain, where prior methods require separate models and training pipelines for each preference type. Architecturally, it builds on latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022) for the generative backbone and adopts ControlNet's zero-convolution connection (Zhang et al., 2023) to link a frozen reference fusion model with a trainable preference branch. The key departure from prior DPO-for-diffusion work is the introduction of IDPO, which restricts preference optimization to a masked region of interest while enforcing pixel-level consistency elsewhere, addressing the local-adaptability-versus-global-coherence tension specific to fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPOFusion demonstrates that a single latent diffusion framework can be fine-tuned with minimal overhead to satisfy four fundamentally different evaluators (human annotators, a VLM, a detector, and a segmenter), which prior fusion methods could not achieve without retraining separate networks. The IDPO mechanism is a reusable technique for any diffusion-based generation task requiring region-specific preference alignment. Open questions include whether the preference collection pipeline scales to open-ended user instructions, whether the approach generalizes beyond two-source fusion (e.g., multi-spectral or video), and whether the 20-epoch fine-tuning per preference type remains practical as the number of target tasks grows.
