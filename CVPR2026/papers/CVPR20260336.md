## Problem

Existing multi-agent video understanding frameworks rely on static, non-learnable tool invocation policies that follow pre-defined rules for tool selection, preventing adaptive discovery of diverse temporal and spatial clues in complex videos. No prior work has jointly trained multiple heterogeneous agents via reinforcement learning to collaboratively plan and refine their tool-use strategies for video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260336/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a video clip and a natural-language query as input, but rather than processing all frames, agents use tools to selectively extract relevant video clues, consuming only ~70 frames per video on average (12–18% of baselines). Output format depends on query type: multiple-choice questions are resolved by majority voting across agents, open-ended questions are consolidated by the strongest agent (Qwen3-8B), and temporal grounding queries produce start/end timestamp segments evaluated by m-IOU.

## Method

VideoChat-M1 introduces a Collaborative Policy Planning (CPP) paradigm where a group of four heterogeneous agents (Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B; 37B total) iteratively generate, execute, and communicate tool-invocation policies. Each agent first generates its own policy plan specifying a sequence of tools, then executes steps sequentially while sharing intermediate results through a shared memory buffer; after each step, agents decide whether to revise their remaining policy based on peers' findings. The entire agent group is trained with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning using GRPO and a hybrid reward (result accuracy + format validity + LLM-judged collaboration quality), with agent dropout (random DAG sampling of the communication topology) to prevent co-adaptation. A supervised fine-tuning stage precedes MARL, using policy plans auto-annotated by GPT-4o/DeepSeek-R1 that yield correct answers without modification.

## Results

VideoChat-M1 (37B total parameters) achieves SOTA across all eight benchmarks: 82.3% on LongVideoBench, 83.2% on Video-MME (M-avg), 83.4% on MLVU (G-avg), 60.5% on Video-Holmes, 80.0% on VideoMMMU, 60.4% on MMR-V (CoT), 71.9% on VSIBench (Avg), and 67.7% m-IOU on Charades-STA. Notably, it uses only 69.9 frames per video (12.3%–18.2% of baselines) and 19.8 s average inference time (8.7%–21.9% of baselines), achieving a superior efficiency–performance trade-off. On LongVideoBench it surpasses Gemini 2.5 Pro by 3.6% and GPT-4o by 15.6%, while its 37B agent group matches Qwen3-VL-235B on Video-MME and MLVU using only ~15% of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoChat-M1 outperforms all closed-source MLLMs on every benchmark where they report scores: it beats Gemini 2.5 Pro by 3.6% on LongVideoBench (82.3 vs 78.7), GPT-4o by 15.6% on LongVideoBench (82.3 vs 66.7), 14.8% on Video-Holmes (60.5 vs 42.0), and 14.3% on MMR-V CoT (60.4 vs 46.1). Against the strongest open-source 235B model (Qwen3-VL-235B), VideoChat-M1 is competitive on Video-MME (83.2 vs 79.2) and VideoMMMU (80.0 vs 74.7) while using only ~15% of the parameters, though it trails slightly on MLVU G-avg (83.4 vs 84.3). On spatial intelligence (VSIBench), it exceeds Gemini 1.5 Pro by 26.5% (71.9 vs 45.4) and InternVL-3.5-241B by 2.4% (71.9 vs 69.5). For temporal grounding, it leads Seed 1.5VL by 3.0% m-IOU (67.7 vs 64.7). Among agent-based baselines, VideoChat-M1 surpasses the strongest (VideoRAG-72B) by 16.9% on LongVideoBench and 7.5% on Video-MME, and in Table 5 it outperforms untrained GPT-4o + DeepSeek-R1 agent groups by at least 4.3 points on Video-Holmes and 6.4 points on LongVideoBench, confirming that MARL training injects task-specific coordination beyond zero-shot reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing agent-based video understanding frameworks (e.g., VideoChat-A1, VideoRAG, ReAgent-V) that rely on fixed, hand-designed tool invocation policies, introducing instead a learnable Collaborative Policy Planning (CPP) paradigm where multiple agents dynamically generate, execute, and refine tool-use strategies through inter-agent communication. Methodologically, it adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) from DeepSeek-R1 to a multi-agent setting, extending multi-agent RL—previously confined to text-only domains (CAMEL, MetaGPT, MALT)—to the video understanding setting with a hybrid reward that evaluates both final answers and intermediate collaboration quality. The shared-memory communication topology with stochastic agent dropout draws inspiration from multi-agent GUI pipelines, but the application to temporal and spatial video reasoning is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VideoChat-M1 demonstrates that a 37B multi-agent group can match or exceed 235B single-model MLLMs (Qwen3-VL-235B, Gemini 2.5 Pro) on long-video QA and reasoning while using only ~70 frames per video versus 384–568 for baselines, suggesting that structured multi-agent tool use is a viable path to parameter-efficient video understanding. Open questions include whether the CPP paradigm generalizes beyond the four-agent, Qwen-family setup, how the reliance on GPT-4o as a collaboration reward model affects deployment cost and circularity, and whether the iterative communication overhead scales gracefully to even longer or higher-resolution videos. The finding that architectural diversity among agents matters more than raw parameter count per agent could reshape how future multi-agent video systems are designed.
