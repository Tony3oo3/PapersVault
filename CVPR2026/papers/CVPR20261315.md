## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on incremental densification—iteratively detecting under-reconstructed regions via gradient norms and splitting Gaussians—which produces a long optimization path and struggles to capture fine details in high-frequency regions. The gradient-based criterion also misaligns with perceptual quality, and each densification step requires many iterations before new splats are deemed necessary. The paper asks whether densification can be eliminated entirely by providing a dense, well-informed initialization from the start.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261315/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with camera poses from COLMAP SfM and dense pixel-wise correspondences (with confidence scores) computed by a pretrained matching network (RoMa by default) between each reference image and its 2 nearest neighbors. It produces a dense set of 3D Gaussian splats (position, covariance, RGB color, opacity, and up to degree-3 spherical harmonics) that are then refined via standard photometric loss optimization without any densification. Preprocessing takes ~120 s per scene on an A100 (76 s matching, 11 s triangulation, 15 s SH estimation) with 15 GB peak GPU memory, using up to 180 reference views and 20 k sampled correspondences per view.

## Method

EDGS replaces 3DGS's sparse SfM initialization plus iterative densification with a one-step dense initialization: it triangulates dense 2D correspondences from a pretrained matcher (RoMa) across multiple view pairs to recover 3D Gaussian positions, then assigns each splat its color from the reference image and estimates spherical harmonics coefficients by solving a least-squares system over multi-view RGB observations. A sampling distribution combining correspondence confidence (uniform over thresholded matches) and reprojection error (uniform over geometrically consistent matches) selects which correspondences to triangulate, ensuring spatially uniform coverage while suppressing outliers. Because every Gaussian is placed near its final surface position from the outset, the subsequent photometric-loss optimization requires no densification and converges dramatically faster—reducing coordinate displacement by 50× and trajectory length by 30× compared to standard 3DGS. The approach is fully compatible with existing densification strategies, serving as a plug-and-play initialization that improves AbsGS, 3DGS-MCMC, and Taming 3DGS without increasing training time or final Gaussian count.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, EDGS+3DGS achieves PSNR 28.02, SSIM 0.839, and LPIPS 0.141 in 27 minutes with only 1.9M Gaussians and no densification, outperforming 3DGS (PSNR 27.49, LPIPS 0.215) and matching or exceeding 3DGS-MCMC (PSNR 28.15, LPIPS 0.176) on LPIPS. On Tanks&Temples, EDGS achieves the best PSNR (24.28) and LPIPS (0.132) across all methods. On Deep Blending, EDGS attains the lowest LPIPS (0.223) while using fewer Gaussians (1.6M) than ScaffoldGS (4.0M) or Mip-Splatting (3.6M). Under early stopping at 10K steps, EDGS reaches LPIPS 0.154 in 12 minutes, outperforming all efficiency-focused baselines (best competitor: Taming 3DGS at LPIPS 0.207 in 14 minutes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDGS+3DGS achieves the best LPIPS on all three datasets (0.132 on Tanks&Temples, 0.141 on Mip-NeRF360, 0.223 on Deep Blending), substantially outperforming 3DGS (0.169/0.215/0.242) and 3DGS-MCMC (0.158/0.176/0.244). On PSNR, EDGS leads on Tanks&Temples (24.28 vs. 24.22 for MCMC) but trails 3DGS-MCMC on Mip-NeRF360 (28.02 vs. 28.15) and ScaffoldGS on Deep Blending (29.81 vs. 30.25), though it uses far fewer Gaussians (1.9M vs. 3.2M and 6.0M respectively). Against efficiency-focused methods at 10K steps, EDGS achieves LPIPS 0.154 in 12 minutes, beating Taming 3DGS (0.207, 14 min), MiniSplatting (0.217, 12 min), and EAGLES (0.232, 16 min). When used as an initialization for existing densification methods (Table 3), EDGS consistently improves all baselines—e.g., boosting 3DGS-MCMC LPIPS from 0.176 to 0.159 (−10%) and Taming 3DGS from 0.207 to 0.179 (−14%)—without increasing training time or final Gaussian count. Among matching algorithms, RoMa (PSNR 28.02, LPIPS 0.141) outperforms LoFTR (27.79, 0.179), DKM (27.81, 0.192), and RAFT (26.90, 0.201), with RAFT underperforming due to its optical-flow design for small viewpoint changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDGS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), retaining its Gaussian representation, splatting rasterizer, and photometric loss optimization while replacing the incremental densification loop with a one-shot dense initialization from triangulated multi-view correspondences. This departs from the large body of work that improves densification itself (AbsGS, 3DGS-MCMC, Scaffold-GS, RevDev, MiniSplatting) by arguing the process is unnecessary rather than suboptimal. For the initialization signal, EDGS leverages pretrained dense feature matchers (RoMa, LoFTR, DKM) rather than the sparse SfM points (COLMAP) or random sampling (RAIN-GS) used by prior 3DGS variants, and it is explicitly designed as a plug-in compatible with existing adaptive density control methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing densification, EDGS shortens the optimization path substantially (50× less coordinate displacement, 30× shorter trajectories) and achieves competitive or superior LPIPS/SSIM without the iterative split-and-adjust cycle that dominates 3DGS training time. Its plug-and-play nature—improving AbsGS, 3DGS-MCMC, and Taming 3DGS when used as their initialization without increasing Gaussian count or training time—makes it a broadly applicable component for 3DGS pipelines. Open questions include performance in sparse-view settings (only briefly addressed in supplementary material), scalability to very large scenes where dense matching cost grows, and whether the dependency on a high-quality pretrained matcher (RoMa) limits applicability in domains where such models are unavailable or unreliable.
