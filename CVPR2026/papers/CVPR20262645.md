## Problem

Existing multimodal graph reasoning pipelines treat nodes as isolated image-text pairs and feed them to LLMs with a single fixed modality template, ignoring two critical realities: (1) text and image attributes of the same node are often only weakly consistent, and (2) different nodes prefer different modality configurations (text-salient, image-salient, or complementary). This paper asks whether graph structure can be exploited to enforce reliable cross-modal alignment and to drive a dynamic, node-specific prompting policy for LLM-based reasoning over multimodal graphs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multimodal graph where each node carries a raw text sequence and an RGB image, plus the adjacency matrix. Text and images are first encoded by frozen CLIP encoders into token/patch sequences; a learnable projector then maps the resulting [CLS] embeddings into the LLM's token space as special tokens ⟨GT_v⟩ and ⟨GI_v⟩. For each anchor node, top-K 1-hop and 2-hop neighbors are selected by cosine similarity of concatenated text+image embeddings and injected as in-context exemplars with their labels. The LLM (LLaMA3.1-8B with LoRA) outputs a node class label or a link-prediction decision, with only one of three modality-specific prompt templates fed at inference time.

## Method

Mario is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (GVLM) uses dual L-layer Transformers for text and image, each augmented with a topology-aware multimodal mixer that performs multi-head attention over all nodes' [CLS] tokens with a learnable position bias indexed by shortest-path-distance buckets; the resulting structure-aware [CLS] is reinjected into the token stream each layer, and bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive loss aligns the final text and image embeddings. Stage 2 (Modality-Adaptive Graph Instruction Tuning) constructs three prompt templates per node (text-only, image-only, multimodal) incorporating the anchor's and top-K neighbors' special tokens, and trains a lightweight MLP router (MAPR) whose input is the concatenated Stage-1 embeddings, mean-pooled 1-/2-hop context, and log degree. The router's softmax probabilities are trained via a composite loss that weights each template's LLM cross-entropy by a performance posterior (softmax of negated losses) plus a KL term matching router output to that posterior, effectively shifting gradient toward the most informative modality per node. At inference the router switches to hard argmax selection, incurring no extra compute over a single-template pipeline.

## Results

On the Single-Focus setting (train/test per dataset), Mario-8B achieves node classification accuracy of 53.63% (Movies), 95.30% (Reddit), 63.43% (CDs), and 92.13% (Arts), and link prediction accuracy of 93.90%, 91.30%, 92.70%, and 89.96% respectively, outperforming all baselines across every dataset and task. The largest gains over the best multimodal baseline (Graph4MM/MLaGA) are +6.98% on NC-CDs and +9.49% on LP-CDs. In zero-shot transfer (Table 3), Mario achieves 1.64× higher NC accuracy than the best baseline on Toys→Movies (41.00% vs. 24.95%) and 1.25× higher LP accuracy (86.60% vs. 79.87%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mario outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every dataset and task in the Single-Focus setting. Against the strongest multimodal baselines, Mario beats Graph4MM by +2.56% NC (Movies), +2.41% NC (Reddit), +7.90% NC (CDs), +2.81% NC (Arts), and by +3.66%, +0.50%, +9.49%, +8.36% on LP respectively; it beats MLaGA by +4.21%, +5.51%, +6.98%, +2.31% on NC and +3.94%, +0.95%, +19.83%, +6.99% on LP. Among text-centric GraphLLMs, LLaGA-A is the closest competitor (50.61/92.94/56.29/88.83 NC), yet Mario still leads by 3.02–7.14 points; GraphPrompter-A and GraphGPT-A trail more substantially (up to 33.87% behind on NC-CDs). GNN baselines (GCN, GATv2, SAGE) are consistently 6–16 points below Mario on NC and 19–25 points below on LP. LVLM baselines (LLaVA1.5-13B, Qwen2.5-VL) underperform Mario by 34–40 points on NC for most datasets, confirming that direct VLM prompting without graph-aware alignment is insufficient. In zero-shot transfer, Mario's advantage widens dramatically: it achieves 41.00% NC on Toys→Movies vs. 24.95% (MLaGA, best baseline) and 54.32% vs. 36.66% (GraphPrompter) on Toys+Movies→CDs, demonstrating that the modality-adaptive router and structure-aware alignment confer transferable inductive bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mario sits at the intersection of two active research threads: LLM-based graph reasoning (GraphGPT, LLaGA, GraphPrompter), which operates on text-attributed graphs with a single fixed instruction template, and multimodal graph learning (MLaGA, Graph4MM, UniGraph2), which typically plugs frozen or lightly tuned VLM embeddings into GNN backbones. The paper departs from both by identifying two specific failure modes—weak cross-modal consistency and heterogeneous per-node modality preference—that prior methods implicitly assume away. Its Stage 1 repurposes the dual-tower contrastive alignment paradigm (CLIP-style InfoNCE) but injects graph topology via a topology-aware multimodal mixer, while Stage 2 introduces a learnable modality router that breaks the fixed-template assumption inherited from GraphLLM instruction tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a concrete, well-motivated recipe for making LLM-based graph reasoning genuinely multimodal rather than text-centric with image captions bolted on, and the modality-adaptive routing mechanism is a reusable component for any setting where per-instance modality informativeness varies. The strong zero-shot transfer results (up to 1.6× over the best baseline) suggest the structure-aware alignment captures graph-invariant semantics that generalize across domains. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs (the mixer attends over all sampled nodes), whether the approach extends beyond text+image to additional modalities, and how sensitive performance is to the quality of the initial CLIP embeddings. The evaluation is limited to six medium-scale e-commerce and social-network graphs, so robustness on larger or more heterogeneous real-world MMGs remains to be established.
