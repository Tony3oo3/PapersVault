## Problem

Existing text-to-motion diffusion models in continuous latent space lack a principled way to make the latent space semantically structured for diffusion, and they typically condition on text via a single token that is insufficiently expressive. MoLingo asks two targeted questions: how to build a latent space where similar text semantics are spatially close (making diffusion more effective), and how to inject text conditioning most effectively so generated motion faithfully follows the description.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'The person is sweeping the floor') encoded by a frozen T5-Large text encoder and produces a sequence of 3D human pose frames up to 10 seconds long at 20 FPS (or 30 FPS for the 272D representation). Motion is parameterized as a sequence of D-dimensional pose configurations, and the latent space uses 4× temporal downsampling with 16-dimensional latents, meaning N frames are compressed into N/4 latent vectors. At inference, all latents start as learned mask tokens and are progressively denoised in an auto-regressive order.

## Method

MoLingo combines three key design choices. First, a Semantically Aligned Autoencoder (SAE) is trained with a cosine-similarity loss that pulls motion latents closer to class tokens derived from BABEL frame-level text labels, creating a latent space where semantically similar motions are spatially proximate; a filtering step discards consecutive latents with near-identical labels to avoid over-constraining. Second, text conditioning uses multi-token cross-attention (T5 embeddings passed through a text adapter) rather than the common single-token AdaLN modulation, giving the model access to the full semantic content of the prompt. Third, generation proceeds via masked auto-regressive rectified flow: a decoder-only transformer with self-attention over motion latents and cross-attention to text tokens produces a conditioning vector for each latent position, which an MLP uses to denoise via a reverse ODE trajectory, with masked positions revealed one at a time until the full latent sequence is clean and decoded to motion.

## Results

On the MARDM-67 evaluator (HumanML3D test set, 20 runs), MoLingo(VAE) achieves the best FID of 0.049, outperforming the previous best ACMDM-XL-PS2 (0.058) and DisCoRD (0.053). MoLingo(SAE) achieves the best text-motion alignment with R-Precision Top-1 of 0.544 and CLIP-Score of 0.686, surpassing ACMDM-XL-PS2 (0.522 / 0.652). In a user study, participants preferred MoLingo over DisCoRD (83.75%), MoMask (77.70%), and MotionStreamer (84.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoLingo(VAE) achieves the lowest FID (0.049) among all compared methods, beating the closest competitors ACMDM-XL-PS2 (0.058) and DisCoRD (0.053) by a clear margin. MoLingo(SAE) dominates on text-motion alignment: R-Precision Top-1 of 0.544 exceeds ACMDM-XL-PS2 (0.522) by 2.2 points, and CLIP-Score of 0.686 surpasses ACMDM-XL-PS2 (0.652) by 3.4 points. Against the VQ-based baselines (MoMask, MMM, BAMM, DisCoRD, MoMask++), MoLingo wins on both FID and alignment metrics across the board. The one metric where MoLingo trails is MModality (diversity): MDM-50Step (3.604) and MLD (3.506) generate more diverse motions from the same prompt, while MoLingo scores 1.414 (VAE) and 1.226 (SAE), suggesting the stronger text conditioning slightly reduces output diversity. In the user study, MoLingo is preferred over all three baselines (DisCoRD, MoMask, MotionStreamer) by 77–85% of participants, confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoLingo sits at the intersection of two recent trends in text-to-motion generation: continuous-valued auto-regressive latent denoising (following MARDM and MotionStreamer) and rectified-flow-based sampling (Liu et al., 2022; Fluid, Fan et al., 2024). It departs from prior work in two specific ways: (1) introducing a semantically aligned autoencoder (SAE) that uses frame-level text labels from BABEL to pull motion latents with similar textual meaning closer in latent space, and (2) replacing the common single-token text conditioning with multi-token cross-attention, drawing an analogy to how cross-attention conditioning works in image diffusion transformers. The causal 1D-convolutional encoder-decoder architecture follows MotionStreamer, while the masked auto-regressive rectified-flow generation loop is adapted from the continuous-token image generation paradigm of Li et al. (NeurIPS 2024) and Fluid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful empirical study of what makes a latent space 'diffusion-friendly' for motion generation, with the key finding that soft semantic alignment (cosine similarity to text class tokens) improves text-motion faithfulness without sacrificing motion quality, whereas a harder contrastive (InfoNCE) constraint hurts performance. The cross-attention vs. single-token ablation offers practical guidance for future T2M architectures. A notable limitation is the absence of hand motion generation, and the SAE variant trades a small FID increase for substantially better R-Precision and CLIP-Score, leaving open the question of whether the alignment can be made lossless. The multi-protocol evaluation (MARDM-67, MS-272, TMR-263) is a welcome step toward more robust benchmarking in this field.
