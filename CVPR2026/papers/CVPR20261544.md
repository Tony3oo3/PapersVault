## Problem

Learning physics-based character controllers from video data is hindered by the ill-posed 2D-to-3D reconstruction step: off-the-shelf 3D pose estimators produce physically implausible motions that over-constrain and destabilize physics-based imitation, and no unified framework exists for non-human characters or complex human-object interactions where 3D training data is scarce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261544/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracking policy consumes a future L-frame clip of 2D reference keypoints, the character's current 2D projection in simulation (computed from its 3D state under a fixed camera), proprioceptive state, and external object state, then outputs PD control targets as a diagonal Gaussian over joint torques. Notably, no explicit camera parameters are provided to the policy; it infers the viewpoint solely from the character's own 2D projection. For multi-view tracking, the policy accepts K view-specific 2D observation sets and aggregates them by averaging their feature embeddings, requiring no fine-tuning. The hierarchical generator outputs discrete 2D motion tokens that are detokenized into global 2D keypoint sequences to drive the low-level tracker.

## Method

Mimic2DM formulates motion imitation as direct 2D reprojection minimization under physics constraints, eliminating the 3D reconstruction step entirely. A view-agnostic tracking policy is trained via RL on 2D motions from diverse camera viewpoints, implicitly acquiring 3D motion structure without any 3D supervision; an adaptive state initialization strategy uses critic-network scores to maintain a prioritized buffer of feasible initial poses, replacing the 3D-dependent Reference State Initialization. Multi-view 3D tracking is achieved zero-shot by averaging the feature embeddings of K single-view 2D observations, matching 3D-trained baselines in tracking accuracy. For generative tasks, a VQ-VAE with causal convolutions tokenizes a canonical 2D motion representation (local pose, relative root translation, and log-scale change) into discrete codes, which an autoregressive causal transformer generates frame-by-frame, outperforming diffusion-based baselines in both FID and downstream tracking success rate.

## Results

On the Soccer Dribble dataset, Mimic2DM achieves a 91.3% success rate with 17.1 px 2D tracking error and 1.69 jitters, substantially outperforming the two-stage Sfv* baseline (47.8% success, 19.9 px error, 2.62 jitters with SLAHMR; 37.1% success, 25.1 px error, 2.54 jitters with SMPLify). On the Animal dataset, the method reaches 83.3% success with 26.8 px error and 3.36 jitters versus 50.0% success, 68.9 px error, and 9.20 jitters for Sfv*/SMPLify. On AIST++, the 3-view 2D tracking policy achieves 90.4% success and 144.3 mm 3D tracking error, approaching the 3D-trained baseline (92.1% success, 114.5 mm error) while using only 2D supervision. The autoregressive 2D generator also outperforms the diffusion baseline, achieving FID 2.44 vs 5.92 and 92% vs 44% success rate on AIST++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two-stage Sfv* pipeline (which first reconstructs 3D poses from video then performs physics-based imitation), Mimic2DM wins decisively on all metrics for both Soccer Dribble and Animal. On Soccer Dribble, the success rate nearly doubles (91.3% vs 47.8% with SLAHMR, 37.1% with SMPLify), while the 2D object tracking error is halved (17.5 vs 38.2/42.0 px), indicating far more accurate ball interaction. On Animal, the 2D tracking error drops from 68.9 to 26.8 px and jitters decrease from 9.20 to 3.36, reflecting much more physically plausible quadruped locomotion. On AIST++, the 3-view 2D policy (90.4% success, 144.3 mm 3D error) approaches but does not fully match the 3D-trained baseline (92.1% success, 114.5 mm error), though it achieves lower jitters (1.58 vs 2.81). For the generative component, the autoregressive 2D generator outperforms the CLOSD diffusion baseline [40] on both FID (2.44 vs 5.92 on AIST++; 5.16 vs 6.29 on Soccer Dribble) and downstream tracking success rate (92% vs 44%; 71% vs 13%), demonstrating that the AR architecture produces more physically trackable 2D motion references.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (reconstruct 3D poses from video, then imitate in physics simulation) exemplified by Sfv [30] and its successors, instead formulating motion imitation as a direct 2D reprojection minimization problem. It builds on DeepMimic's [29] RL tracking framework and proprioceptive observation design, but replaces 3D reference tracking with a view-agnostic 2D tracking objective that implicitly learns 3D structure from diverse viewpoints. The hierarchical control structure (tracking policy + generative model) follows the lineage of [14, 39, 49], but substitutes 2D motion as the unified interface between high-level generation and low-level physics control, and introduces an autoregressive VQ-VAE-based 2D generator in place of diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for 3D motion capture or 3D pose reconstruction, this work significantly lowers the barrier to training physics-based character controllers for domains where 3D data is scarce or unavailable (complex HOI, non-human locomotion). The view-agnostic design and simple averaging-based view aggregation provide an elegant, zero-shot path from 2D to 3D tracking without fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-agent or scene-interaction settings, how sensitive performance is to 2D pose estimation quality in cluttered real-world videos, and whether the autoregressive generator can match diffusion-based methods on longer-horizon or more diverse motion distributions.
