## Problem

Training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation suffers from two structural weaknesses: (i) contrastive pretraining optimizes global CLS-level alignment, leaving patch-level attention geometry misaligned and spatially inconsistent for dense prediction, and (ii) text is used only as a classifier via cosine similarity rather than as a structural prior that governs how semantic evidence propagates across neighboring pixels. Existing remedies introduce auxiliary vision backbones (DINO, SAM), multi-model pipelines, or heavy post-processing, increasing complexity and latency while sacrificing design simplicity.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of natural-language class labels as input. The image is processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder using sliding-window inference with 224×224 crops at stride 112 (shorter side resized to 336 px, or 560 px for Cityscapes), and the label set is encoded into unit-norm text prototypes. The output is a full-resolution per-pixel segmentation mask obtained by argmax over refined class logits, with no auxiliary backbones, additional training, or external supervision required.

## Method

PEARL follows an align-then-propagate recipe with two closed-form, parameter-free operators. First, Procrustes Alignment (PA) is inserted inside the last self-attention block: for each head, queries and keys are weighted-centered (weights ∝ token norms, CLS weight optionally zeroed), and a single input-dependent orthogonal rotation R* = UVᵀ (from the SVD of the cross-covariance K_cᵀQ_c, or via Newton-Schulz polar iterations) rotates keys toward the query subspace, preserving magnitudes while removing background-biased drift; attention is then recomputed with the rotated keys to yield geometry-corrected patch features. Second, Text-aware Laplacian Propagation (TLP) refines the resulting patch-text cosine logits on a compact grid (80×80 or 224×224) by solving a convex quadratic: a class graph G built from text-prototype similarities (row-softmax of TTᵀ/τ_s + βI) provides both a confidence signal (peak probability × text-prior agreement) for the data term and a semantic gate on neighbor edges, while image gradients (exp(−κ|∇I|)) protect boundaries; the system (D_ρ + τL)F_g = D_ρ Z_g is solved with a fixed number of conjugate-gradient iterations and bilinearly upsampled to full resolution.

## Results

PEARL achieves 43.2% average mIoU across seven benchmarks (V21, PC60, Object, V20, PC59, Stuff, City), the best result among all training-free methods without auxiliary backbones, surpassing SFP (39.6%), NACLIP (39.4%), and SCLIP (38.2%). It leads individually on V21 (64.1%), PC59 (38.6%), and City (37.6%), and is competitive on V20 (86.9%). In pixel accuracy (Table 2), PEARL reaches 67.2% average, also the best training-free no-extra-backbone result, edging out CASS+DINOv3 (67.0%) which relies on an auxiliary DINO backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEARL wins on all seven mIoU benchmarks against every training-free, no-extra-backbone competitor: it outperforms SFP by +3.6 avg (64.1 vs 56.8 on V21, 37.6 vs 34.1 on City), NACLIP by +3.8 avg (86.9 vs 79.7 on V20), and SCLIP by +5.0 avg. Against methods that do use auxiliary DINO backbones, PEARL still leads: it beats CASS+DINOv3 (42.2) by 1.0 and CASS+DINOv2 (41.1) by 2.1 in average mIoU, while using only a single frozen CLIP ViT-B/16. The one notable exception is the Object benchmark, where CLIPtrase reports 43.6% (vs. PEARL's 37.3%), likely due to CLIPtrase's explicit background-cleaning heuristic that PEARL does not employ. In pixel accuracy (Table 2), PEARL (67.2%) also surpasses SFP (65.0%), NACLIP (64.9%), and CASS+DINOv3 (67.0%), confirming consistent gains across both evaluation metrics. The plug-and-play TLP component alone lifts SCLIP from 38.2→42.2, NACLIP from 39.4→42.3, and SFP from 39.6→41.5 in average mIoU, demonstrating that the propagation step is broadly beneficial independent of the upstream attention modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEARL sits within the training-free OVSS lineage that began with MaskCLIP's cosine-similarity baseline and evolved through feature purification (ClearCLIP, SFP), neighbor-aware attention (NACLIP, SCLIP), and auxiliary-backbone fusion (LaVG, CASS, ProxyCLIP). It departs from this lineage by addressing the two identified weaknesses at their origin: rather than post-hoc feature cleaning or spatial smoothing, it inserts a closed-form orthogonal Procrustes rotation inside the last self-attention block to correct key-query geometry, and it repurposes text prototypes from mere classifiers into structural priors that gate a graph Laplacian propagation. The 'align-then-propagate' framing unifies these two operations under a single principle, and the TLP component is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play module that can be grafted onto other training-free methods (SCLIP, NACLIP, SFP) without modifying their backbones or prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEARL sets a new state-of-the-art among training-free, no-extra-backbone OVSS methods (43.2 avg mIoU) while remaining fully parameter-free and adding only a small per-head SVD and a few conjugate-gradient steps, making it attractive for deployment where retraining or auxiliary models are infeasible. The plug-and-play TLP component, which adds 3–4 mIoU points to existing methods, suggests a broadly applicable refinement operator for any CLIP-based dense prediction pipeline. Open questions remain around prompt sensitivity (rare 'stuff' categories on ADE20K), low-contrast boundary handling, the grid-size/detail trade-off, and the absence of instance awareness, all of which the authors acknowledge as common constraints in the training-free setting.
