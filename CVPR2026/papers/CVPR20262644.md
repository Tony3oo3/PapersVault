## Problem

Existing depth estimation methods for autonomous driving cannot simultaneously achieve metric-scale accuracy, temporal consistency, robustness to sparse/intermittent LiDAR prompts, and zero-shot cross-domain generalization. Monocular foundation models suffer from scale ambiguity and temporal flickering, MVS methods degrade under low parallax and repetitive textures, and prompt-guided approaches become fragile when LiDAR inputs are partial or absent. The paper addresses this by asking how to anchor multi-view stereo in absolute metric scale while preserving the generalization and temporal stability needed for real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video sequence of T frames, each providing a reference image, N source images, full camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and sparse LiDAR metric prompts (e.g., 16 scan lines from KITTI/DDAD, 8 from Waymo). Features are processed at H/4 × W/4 resolution with 64 depth hypotheses sampled uniformly in log space; the final output is a per-pixel absolute metric depth map recovered by rescaling a sigmoid output to the cost volume's metric bounds [d_min, d_max]. Sparse prompts are projected into logit space via min-max log normalization to match the network's output scale, and the model is trained at 640×480 resolution.

## Method

DriveMVS integrates sparse LiDAR prompts through two complementary pathways within a three-stage architecture. First, the Prompt-Anchored Cost Volume (PACV) disentangles relative consistency (learned feature-matching costs) from absolute scale anchoring (explicit LiDAR depth differences at each hypothesis plane) via separate MLPs, preventing cost-volume collapse in low-parallax or textureless regions. Second, the Triple-Cues Combiner (TCC) is a 12-layer Mask Transformer that fuses three heterogeneous streams—geometrically anchored CV cues, DINOv2/DepthAnything-V2 monocular priors, and sparsity-aware metric prompt features—using masked self-attention for independent refinement and cross-attention restricted to valid prompt locations for metric injection. Third, a DPT-based Spatio-Temporal Decoder embeds motion-aware temporal self-attention with a Relative Pose Encoder to propagate smooth, metrically consistent depth across frames. Training is supervised on four synthetic datasets with 50% random modality dropout to ensure robustness to prompt absence, and the loss combines per-frame depth/gradient/normals terms with a temporal consistency penalty.

## Results

DriveMVS achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot metric depth estimation across all three autonomous driving benchmarks. On KITTI, it reaches 0.49 m MAE, 2.56% AbsRel, and 98.78% τ<1.25; on DDAD, 2.64 m MAE, 5.45% AbsRel, and 95.25% τ; and on Waymo, 1.24 m MAE, 4.46% AbsRel, and 95.95% τ. For temporal consistency on KITTI, DriveMVS reports a TAE of 0.296, outperforming MVSAnywhere (0.338) and VideoDA-B (0.767) while also achieving the best per-frame accuracy (2.56% AbsRel vs. 10.37% and 16.64% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveMVS outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the closest prompt-guided monocular method PriorDA [68], DriveMVS reduces KITTI MAE from 0.61 m to 0.49 m (−20%) and Waymo AbsRel from 6.04% to 4.46% (−26%), while also improving Waymo τ from 85.55% to 95.95%. Versus the strongest MVS baseline MVSAnywhere [28], DriveMVS cuts KITTI AbsRel from 10.48% to 2.56% (−76%) and DDAD MAE from 4.18 m to 2.64 m (−37%), demonstrating that explicit LiDAR prompt anchoring is critical when multi-view geometry alone is insufficient. Feed-forward reconstruction methods (VGGT, MapAnything) lag substantially in metric accuracy—VGGT reports 77.58% AbsRel on KITTI—confirming that single-pass architectures struggle with absolute scale in driving scenes. Among monocular methods without prompts (MoGe-2, DepthPro), DriveMVS achieves 3–6× lower AbsRel, highlighting the benefit of combining multi-view geometry with sparse metric cues. For temporal consistency, DriveMVS (TAE 0.296) beats MVSAnywhere (0.338) and VideoDA-B (0.767) by 12% and 61% respectively, while simultaneously achieving the best per-frame accuracy, showing that its spatio-temporal decoder does not sacrifice spatial precision for temporal smoothness.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveMVS builds directly on the view-count-agnostic cost volume mechanism from MVSAnywhere [28] and SimpleRecon [50], the DPT decoder architecture [48], and the temporal loss formulation from Video Depth Anything [8]. Its key conceptual departure is the explicit disentanglement of relative-consistency learning from absolute-scale anchoring within the cost volume (PACV), which prior MVS methods treat as a single undifferentiated matching signal. The Triple-Cue Combiner extends the prompt-guided depth estimation line (PromptDA, PriorDA) by fusing sparse LiDAR cues not as a post-hoc correction but as a first-class token stream alongside MVS and monocular features within a Mask Transformer, while the spatio-temporal decoder addresses the temporal flickering that plagues per-frame MVS approaches like MVSAnywhere.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For autonomous driving deployment under minimalist LiDAR configurations, this paper demonstrates that metric-scale accuracy, temporal stability, and zero-shot cross-domain generalization can be achieved simultaneously rather than traded off, which prior methods could not. The robustness to prompt dropout, occlusion, and complete absence (validated via random modality dropping during training) makes the system practically deployable with heterogeneous sensor availability. The main open limitation is inference cost: multi-view and temporal dependencies make it slower than monocular alternatives, and the authors explicitly flag computational efficiency as future work. Whether the synthetic-to-real gap fully closes under truly unseen sensor configurations (e.g., different LiDAR line counts or mounting geometries) remains to be tested at scale.
