## Problem

Pixelwise uncertainty scores from segmentation UQ methods must be aggregated into image-level scalars for downstream tasks like OoD detection and failure detection, yet the properties, limitations, and relative performance of common aggregation strategies have never been systematically studied. Global Average is the default choice but ignores spatial structure, and existing alternatives lack formal analysis or cross-dataset comparison, leaving practitioners without principled guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260753/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a normalized 2D uncertainty map U ∈ [0,1]^{m×n} (produced by Monte Carlo Dropout or other UQ methods on segmentation networks) and, for prediction-based aggregators, the corresponding segmentation mask M ∈ {1,…,K}^{m×n}. Spatial aggregators apply sliding windows over U to compute local Moran's I, Edge Density, or Entropy scores, then weight the uncertainty map by these spatial scores to yield a Spatial Mass Ratio in [0,1]. The meta-aggregator (GMM-All) takes a d-dimensional feature vector of individual aggregator scores and outputs a single scalar negative log-likelihood under a fitted GMM.

## Method

The paper introduces three spatially-aware aggregators that compute a Spatial Mass Ratio (SMR): the fraction of total uncertainty mass concentrated in regions with high local spatial structure, using Moran's I (clustering), Edge Density Score (boundaries), or Shannon Entropy (heterogeneity) as the spatial measure. These address documented pitfalls of intensity-only aggregators—insensitivity to spatial structure, lack of proportion invariance, and non-monotonicity. The key meta-aggregation strategy (GMM-All) represents each uncertainty map as a feature vector of d individual aggregator scores, fits a Gaussian Mixture Model on in-distribution feature vectors (with BIC-selected component count), and defines the final score as the negative log-likelihood of the feature vector. This unifies intensity-based and spatial aggregators into a single robust score that adapts to dataset-specific uncertainty patterns without requiring task-specific tuning.

## Results

For OoD detection (AUROC), prediction-based BCA and ICA along with the proposed GMM-All form a statistically dominant top tier (p<0.05, Wilcoxon test), while the default AVG performs near-random in 6 of 10 scenarios. For failure detection (E-AURC), QFR achieves the best mean rank (3.4), followed by GMM-All (4.2), GMM-Int (5.4), and GMM-Spa (6.0), with AVG ranking worst (11.9). QFR shows statistically significant improvement over all other AggSs (p<0.001), while BCA and GMM-based methods form the next tier (p<0.05). GMM-All outperforms its ablated variants (GMM-Spa, GMM-Int) in both tasks, confirming that combining intensity and spatial features enhances downstream performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QFR [Kahl et al., 2024], the proposed GMM-All is consistently second-best for failure detection (mean rank 4.2 vs. 3.4) and competitive for OoD detection, where both methods fall in the statistically dominant tier. QFR's advantage stems from its object-size-independent foreground-ratio thresholding, which targets the most uncertain regions (typically object boundaries) without being biased by class proportions. Against BCA and ICA [Camarasa et al., 2021], GMM-All outperforms both for failure detection (mean rank 4.2 vs. 6.7 for BCA and 10.7 for ICA) and matches or exceeds them for OoD detection, where BCA and ICA are top performers. ICA's poor FD performance is attributed to segmentation errors frequently arising in small objects, which its class-proportion weighting fails to capture. The default AVG baseline is outperformed by all prediction-based and GMM-based methods in both tasks, with AVG ranking last or near-last in most scenarios, confirming it should not be the default aggregation choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes three established image-analysis measures—Moran's I, Shannon Entropy, and Edge Density Score—as spatial mass ratio aggregators for pixelwise segmentation uncertainty, a use not previously explored in the UQ literature. It builds on the GMM framework (via the EM algorithm) to define a meta-aggregator (GMM-All) that treats multiple aggregation scores as features in a joint distribution, departing from prior work that proposed narrow, task-specific aggregation heuristics without formal property analysis. The paper positions itself as the first systematic comparison of aggregation strategies, formalizing properties (spatial sensitivity, proportion invariance, monotonicity) that prior methods like AVG, AQA, and ATA were shown to violate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the universally adopted global average (AVG) is suboptimal in 6 of 10 OoD scenarios and ranks near the bottom for failure detection provides strong evidence that the field's default practice is leaving performance on the table. GMM-All offers a practical, dataset-agnostic default that requires only a representative iD set, though its reliance on a GMM distributional assumption and sufficient iD data limits applicability in low-data regimes. Open questions include whether the spatial aggregators generalize to other UQ paradigms (e.g., evidential or reconstruction-based), whether the GMM can be replaced with more flexible density estimators, and how these strategies interact with active learning or test-time adaptation pipelines where the iD set shifts over time.
