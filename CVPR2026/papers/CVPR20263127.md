## Problem

Existing On-the-Fly Category Discovery methods freeze both the feature encoder and class prototypes during the online phase, relying on heuristic hash-based quantization into binary codes. This static inference ignores the learning potential of incoming data, while binary quantization introduces information loss and amplifies intra-class variance, causing category explosion where a single class fragments into multiple pseudo-classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263127/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled support set (half of all categories) during offline training, then processes an unlabeled sequential image stream where each instance arrives one at a time. Each incoming sample receives an instant class label (known or novel) via cosine similarity against a growing prototype memory, while encoder parameters and prototypes are updated periodically in small batches (size 64). The prototype memory expands dynamically as new categories are discovered, and the encoder is adapted via lightweight gradient steps on the final ViT-B-16 transformer block.

## Method

TALON replaces hash-based binary prototypes with a hash-free continuous feature space framework built on three components. During offline training, a margin-aware logit calibration (MLC) applies an angular margin to cosine similarities between ℓ₂-normalized features and class weight vectors, enlarging inter-class separation and tightening intra-class compactness to reserve embedding space for future novel categories. At test time, a semantic-aware prototype update refines each class prototype via a confidence-controlled exponential moving average, where the adaptive step size scales with both assignment confidence and effective sample count (n/(n+κ)), preventing noisy drift from early outliers. Simultaneously, a stable encoder adaptation minimizes predictive entropy augmented with prototype-level alignment and inter-class separation losses, backpropagating only through encoder parameters while keeping prototypes fixed per update step. This joint adaptation of both the parameter space and the prototype memory enables the model to learn through discovery rather than perform static inference.

## Results

On the Greedy-Hungarian protocol, TALON-DINO achieves 86.2% overall and 79.3% novel-class accuracy on CIFAR-10, and 84.1%/63.4% on ImageNet-100, surpassing the previous best (SMILE) by 8.0 and 47.2 points on novel classes respectively. On fine-grained benchmarks, TALON-CLIP reaches 60.4% overall and 45.8% novel accuracy on Stanford Cars, and 61.2%/47.3% on Food-101, outperforming SMILE by 27.0 and 28.0 points on novel classes. Under the stricter Strict-Hungarian protocol, TALON-DINO still leads with 65.0% overall on CIFAR-10 and 69.2% on Oxford Pets, while TALON-CLIP achieves 53.5% on Stanford Cars. The method also reduces the estimated number of discovered categories from 2910 (SMILE-64bit) to 153 on CUB, effectively mitigating category explosion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TALON substantially outperforms the primary OCD baselines SMILE [11] and PHE [59] across all seven datasets; for example, on ImageNet-100 novel-class accuracy, TALON-DINO reaches 63.4% versus SMILE's 16.2% (a 47.2-point gap), and on Stanford Cars, TALON-CLIP achieves 45.8% novel accuracy versus SMILE's 21.3%. Against the diffusion-based DiffGRE [32] (both +S and +P variants), TALON-CLIP leads by 16.4 and 18.9 points on CUB novel accuracy (44.7% vs 28.3%/23.8%) and by 28.9 and 29.3 points on Stanford Cars novel accuracy (45.8% vs 16.9%/16.5%). The older NCD/GCD baselines (SLC, MLDG, RankStat, WTA) are outperformed by even larger margins, confirming that the hash-free TTA framework is far more effective than static clustering or meta-learning approaches. In the TTA comparison (Table 4), conventional entropy-minimization methods TENT [48] and OSTTA [9] actually degrade performance when applied to the OCD baseline (e.g., TENT drops Stanford Cars All from 49.0% to 48.1%), whereas TALON's joint prototype-encoder adaptation improves it to 53.5%, demonstrating that standard domain-shift TTA is ill-suited for the label-space shifts inherent in category discovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

TALON departs from the dominant hash-based paradigm in On-the-Fly Category Discovery (OCD) established by SMILE and PHE, which freeze the encoder and quantize features into binary codes for prototype matching. It repurposes test-time adaptation (TTA) techniques—originally designed for domain shift (e.g., TENT, OSTTA)—and redirects them toward the more challenging label-space shift inherent in streaming category discovery. The margin-aware logit calibration draws on angular-margin losses (à la ArcFace) but applies them specifically to reserve embedding space for future novel classes, a use not previously explored in the OCD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a hash-free, continuously adapting framework can dramatically reduce category explosion (153 vs. 2910 pseudo-classes on CUB) while substantially boosting novel-class accuracy, the paper challenges the assumption that frozen encoders and binary quantization are necessary for stable online discovery. Open questions remain around scalability to very long or highly non-stationary streams, the optimal frequency of encoder updates, and whether the approach extends to multimodal or video settings. The finding that standard TTA methods (TENT, OSTTA) actually degrade OCD performance highlights that label-space adaptation requires fundamentally different objectives than domain-shift adaptation.
