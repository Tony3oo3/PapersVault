## Problem

Failure detection (FD) in multimodal systems remains largely unexplored, despite being critical for safety-sensitive deployments. Existing OOD detection methods (Energy, MaxLogit, Entropy) underperform even a simple MSP baseline on multimodal FD tasks, and unimodal FD approaches fail to exploit complementary cross-modal signals or handle failure modes unique to multimodal data such as signal conflict. The paper identifies and formalizes 'confidence degradation'—where fused multimodal confidence drops below at least one unimodal branch—as a strong failure indicator that prior methods do not explicitly address.

## Input / Output

The model ingests M modalities per sample (e.g., video clips encoded by SlowFast, optical flow via a slow-pathway SlowFast, audio via ResNet-18, or image + LiDAR point clouds for SemanticKITTI), each passed through a modality-specific encoder to produce an embedding. A fusion classifier concatenates all embeddings to yield the multimodal prediction, while parallel unimodal classifiers produce per-modality predictions used only during training for the confidence penalty. At inference, the output is a class label over the original C classes with an MSP confidence score; a threshold on this score yields the binary failure-detection decision (correct vs. misclassified).

## Method

ACR introduces two complementary training-time mechanisms. First, the Adaptive Confidence Loss (ACL) penalizes confidence degradation by computing ½·Σₖ max(0, confₖ − conf), forcing the fused prediction's confidence to never fall below any individual modality's confidence; this simultaneously regularizes unimodal branches against overconfidence when they conflict. Second, Multimodal Feature Swapping (MFS) synthesizes failure-aware outliers in feature space by randomly swapping n_swap ~ U(n_min, n_max) continuous dimensions between cross-modal embeddings, then supervising the resulting outlier prediction with a soft label that interpolates between the ground-truth one-hot and a dedicated outlier class, weighted by the swap ratio λ = n_swap/n_max. The total training objective is L_cls + L_outlier + λ_acl·L_acl, and at test time the model simply uses MSP on the fused prediction for the confidence score, requiring no architectural changes at inference.

## Results

On the four action recognition datasets (HMDB51, EPIC-Kitchens, HAC, Kinetics-600) with video + optical flow modalities, ACR achieves AURC of 19.97, 103.25, 27.41, and 41.85 (×10⁻³), AUROC of 92.02%, 79.27%, 91.48%, and 88.99%, and FPR95 of 41.96%, 71.58%, 39.39%, and 55.89%, respectively. ACR also improves classification accuracy on all four datasets (e.g., 87.23% on HMDB51 vs. 86.20% for MSP). The largest gains appear on HAC, where ACR reduces AURC by 15.22 points and FPR95 by 15.45 points relative to the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACR outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and every FD metric (AURC, AUROC, FPR95). On HMDB51, ACR reduces AURC to 19.97 vs. the best baseline OpenMix (24.15) and CRL (26.44), and improves AUROC to 92.02% vs. CRL's 90.39% (+1.63 pp). On HAC, the gains are most dramatic: ACR achieves AURC 27.41 vs. Entropy's 42.63 (−15.22) and FPR95 39.39% vs. OpenMix's 54.84% (−15.45 pp). On Kinetics-600, ACR lowers AURC to 41.85 vs. MSP's 46.29 and FPR95 to 55.89% vs. OpenMix's 60.27%. On EPIC-Kitchens, the margins are smaller but consistent (AURC 103.25 vs. RegMixup's 105.25; FPR95 71.58% vs. OpenMix's 73.68%). Notably, ACR also improves classification accuracy over MSP on all four datasets (e.g., 87.23% vs. 86.20% on HMDB51), indicating that the confidence regularization does not sacrifice predictive performance. Among the training-strategy baselines, OpenMix and CRL are the closest competitors, but both are unimodal methods that cannot capture cross-modal failure modes, which ACR explicitly targets through its Adaptive Confidence Loss and Multimodal Feature Swapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established unimodal failure detection (FD) literature—spanning confidence-based scoring (MSP, DOCTOR), outlier synthesis (OpenMix, Mixup), and confidence regularization (CRL)—into the multimodal setting, which the authors argue has been largely unexplored. It departs from the OOD detection line of work (Energy, MaxLogit, Entropy, A2D/MultiOOD) by demonstrating that these methods, while effective for OOD, are poorly suited to detecting misclassified in-distribution samples in multimodal systems. The two proposed components—Adaptive Confidence Loss and Multimodal Feature Swapping—are novel in that they target a newly identified phenomenon (confidence degradation) and a cross-modal failure mode (inconsistent fused vs. unimodal confidence) that neither unimodal FD nor multimodal OOD methods address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multimodal models are increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains (autonomous driving, medical diagnostics), the inability to reliably flag their own misclassifications is a critical gap; this paper provides the first dedicated framework for that purpose and identifies a concrete, measurable failure signature (confidence degradation) that practitioners can monitor. The finding that simple MSP outperforms sophisticated OOD scores for FD challenges the default assumption that more complex scoring functions are always preferable, and the MFS technique offers a data-free way to synthesize realistic multimodal failures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to generative or foundation-model multimodal systems, to more than two modalities with asymmetric reliability, and to streaming/online settings where unimodal auxiliary classifiers may not be available.
