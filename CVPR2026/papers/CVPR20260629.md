## Problem

Existing robust CIR methods rely on the small-loss hypothesis to identify noisy triplets, but NTC noise consists of semantically ambiguous 'partial matches' (e.g., shirt vs. T-shirt) that violate this assumption, causing the model to misclassify hard negatives as clean. This triggers a self-dependent vicious cycle where the learner simultaneously acts as its own arbiter, leading to catastrophic representation pollution. The paper addresses how to decouple a reliable arbiter from the learner while avoiding the prohibitive online cost of MLLMs.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a reference RGB image and a natural-language modification text as a composed query, encodes them via BLIP-2's Q-Former (32 learnable queries, 256-dim embeddings), and retrieves the best-matching target image from a gallery using cosine similarity. During training, the EKI module additionally consumes a Geometric Deconstruction Vector (concatenation of query feature, target feature, their difference, and their element-wise product) to output a scalar confidence score in [0,1] that dynamically gates which stream each sample enters. The anchor dataset used to train EKI is a small subset (randomly sampled) of the full training set, labeled offline by GPT-4o.

## Method

Air-Know is a three-phase decoupled paradigm. Phase 1 (EPA) uses GPT-4o offline with a three-step cross-validation protocol (deconstruct inputs, compare inferred vs. given instruction, judge and diagnose) to produce a high-precision small anchor dataset. Phase 2 (EKI) trains a lightweight ReLU-MLP proxy on this anchor set using variational inference with MC Dropout to model the posterior over network weights, learning a non-linear geometric boundary in GDV space that separates clean from noisy triplets; at inference it averages T stochastic forward passes to yield a calibrated confidence. Phase 3 (DSR) uses this confidence as a dynamic gate: high-confidence samples enter a clean alignment stream (robust contrastive loss weighted by confidence), while low-confidence but high-similarity samples enter a feedback reconciliation stream that pushes their representations apart via a margin-based penalty, thereby preventing representation pollution without requiring the MLLM online.

## Results

On FashionIQ under 20% noise, Air-Know achieves 55.18% R@10 and 75.71% R@50 (average across Dress, Shirt, TopTee), outperforming the next-best HABIT (54.48/74.28) and TME (54.03/73.91). On CIRR under the same 20% noise, Air-Know reaches 80.40 on Avg(R@5, Rsub@1), surpassing TME (79.74) and INTENT (79.66). The performance gap widens substantially at 80% noise: Air-Know leads by +1.43 over INTENT on FashionIQ R@10 (49.23 vs. 48.51) and by +1.93 over INTENT on CIRR Avg (76.90 vs. 75.97), while the non-robust SPRC baseline collapses to 33.51% R@10 on FashionIQ and 60.74 on CIRR. On clean (0%) data, Air-Know remains competitive (65.73 avg on FashionIQ, 80.73 on CIRR) though slightly behind TME on CIRR (82.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Air-Know outperforms all four robust baselines (TME, HABIT, INTENT) and the non-robust SPRC across every noise level (20%, 50%, 80%) on both FashionIQ and CIRR. Against TME (CVPR'25), the closest robust competitor, Air-Know gains +1.15 on FashionIQ R@10 at 20% noise (55.18 vs. 54.03) and +2.56 at 80% noise (49.23 vs. 46.67), with a similar widening trend on CIRR (+0.66 at 20%, +2.32 at 80%). Against HABIT and INTENT (both AAAI'26), the margins are smaller at moderate noise (+0.70 and +0.96 on FashionIQ R@10 at 20%) but grow to +0.87/+0.72 at 80% noise. The non-robust SPRC baseline degrades catastrophically under noise (e.g., 54.92→33.51 R@10 on FashionIQ from 0% to 80%), confirming the necessity of the decoupled arbiter design. On clean (0%) data, Air-Know is competitive but not always best: it leads on FashionIQ (65.73 AVG) but trails TME on CIRR (80.73 vs. 82.01 Avg), which the authors attribute to the robustness-oriented design sacrificing some clean-data performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper addresses the Noisy Triplet Correspondence (NTC) problem in Composed Image Retrieval, a formulation introduced by Li et al. (TME, CVPR 2025). It departs from the dominant 'small-loss hypothesis' paradigm inherited from Noisy Correspondence Learning, arguing that NTC's unique 'partial match' noise (e.g., a shirt vs. a T-shirt) violates the assumption that noisy samples produce high losses. Rather than relying on the learner's own loss dynamics for noise identification, Air-Know repurposes MLLMs (GPT-4o) as an offline expert arbiter and distills their judgment into a lightweight Bayesian proxy network, thereby decoupling the arbiter from the learner. The dual-stream reconciliation mechanism draws on robust contrastive learning (RCL) and margin-based rejection ideas, but applies them through an externally calibrated confidence signal rather than self-estimated GMM partitioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of noise arbitration from representation learning offers a principled escape from the self-reinforcing 'representation pollution' cycle that plagues small-loss-based methods under semantically ambiguous noise. However, the approach introduces practical dependencies: the anchor dataset requires GPT-4o annotation (cost and availability concerns), and the three-phase training pipeline (EPA → EKI → DSR) adds significant engineering complexity. Notably, Air-Know underperforms several baselines in the clean (0% noise) setting, suggesting the robustness-oriented design trades off peak accuracy. Open questions include whether the anchor dataset size and MLLM choice significantly affect downstream performance, whether the Bayesian proxy generalizes to other noisy multimodal tasks beyond CIR, and whether the dual-stream mechanism can be simplified without sacrificing the decoupling benefit.
