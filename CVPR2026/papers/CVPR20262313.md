## Problem

Adapting Vision-Language Models to few-shot action recognition creates a tension: unconstrained low-rank updates overwrite the dominant semantic directions of the pre-trained backbone, causing catastrophic forgetting and reduced cross-domain transfer, while few-shot episodes expose each query to only one positive and a handful of negatives, yielding small inter-class margins and weak decision boundaries. Prior PEFT methods for FSAR do not explicitly protect the pre-trained cross-modal structure, and existing negative-learning work for VLMs targets image classification rather than video action recognition with continual-learning protocols.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 vision backbone (with rank-2 LoRA adapters on selected attention projections) and text prompts consisting of one positive class description plus M=3 LLM-generated negative prompts of a given difficulty level, all encoded by the frozen text encoder. In the N-way K-shot episodic protocol (5-way 1/5-shot), each episode provides K support videos per class and Q query videos; the model outputs a class label per query via cosine similarity between the visual embedding and the positive/negative text embeddings in the shared CLIP space, scored with a composite softmax cross-entropy plus curricular contrastive loss.

## Method

P2A combines two complementary modules operating at the parameter and data levels. Orthogonal Subspace Control (OSC) performs a truncated SVD on selected pre-trained weight matrices, identifies the top-k left singular vectors (k set to the entropy-based effective rank) as a protected principal subspace, and projects the LoRA update B onto its orthogonal complement via P_k^⊥ = I − U_k U_k^⊤, guaranteeing that pre-activation responses along the dominant directions remain exactly unchanged; OSC is applied to upper transformer layers (6–11) on the W_q, W_v, W_k projections. Ranked Negative-prompt Curriculum (RNC) uses an LLM (DeepSeek-R1) in a generator-verifier loop to produce class-specific negative prompts at three difficulty levels (coarse substitutions, semantic inversions, fine-grained near-miss counterfactuals), which are cached after verification and scheduled across a three-phase training curriculum (easy → medium → hard). The training objective combines an alignment classification loss (softmax CE over query logits) with a curricular contrastive loss that pulls visual features toward the correct class prototype while pushing them away from the active difficulty-level negatives, using only 3.1 M trainable parameters (≈2% of the backbone).

## Results

P2A achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on 8 of 10 evaluation settings across five FSAR benchmarks (HMDB51, UCF101, Kinetics-100, SSv2-Small, SSv2-Full) using only 3.1M trainable parameters (2.0% of the CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone). Headline numbers include 82.3% (1-shot) and 89.0% (5-shot) on HMDB51, 93.7% and 96.7% on Kinetics-100, and 96.9% and 99.1% on UCF101. In the cross-dataset continual-learning setting (S→K→H sequence), P2A attains the highest AvgAcc (78.0%) and the least negative Forward Transfer (−7.7) among all compared methods, while maintaining competitive Backward Transfer (−5.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2A outperforms all 14 baselines on every setting where a comparison is available. Against the strongest prior PEFT method EMP-Net [39] (same CLIP-ViT-B backbone, 7.7M trainable params), P2A gains +5.5% on HMDB51 1-shot (82.3 vs 76.8), +4.6% on Kinetics-100 1-shot (93.7 vs 89.1), and +3.9% on SSv2-Small 1-shot (58.5 vs 57.1), while using fewer trainable parameters (3.1M vs 7.7M). Compared to the full-fine-tuning CLIP-FSAR [35] baseline (89M params), P2A improves HMDB51 1-shot by 6.5% (82.3 vs 75.8) and Kinetics-100 1-shot by 4.0% (93.7 vs 89.7) with only 2% of the parameters. Against the LoRA variant of CLIP-FSAR (same 3.1M budget), P2A adds +3.7% on HMDB51 1-shot (82.3 vs 78.6) and +3.0% on Kinetics-100 1-shot (93.7 vs 90.7), confirming that the orthogonal subspace constraint and negative-prompt curriculum are the sources of the gain rather than additional parameters. In the continual-learning protocol (S→K→H), P2A achieves AvgAcc 78.0% vs 74.1% (CLIP-FSAR) and 70.5% (EMP-Net), with the least negative FWT (−7.7 vs −13.0 and −13.8), demonstrating that OSC's subspace protection directly mitigates catastrophic forgetting across sequential video datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

P2A directly extends LoRA by projecting its low-rank update onto the orthogonal complement of a principal subspace identified via SVD, adding a spectral-structure-aware constraint absent from vanilla LoRA and prior PEFT methods like ST-Adapter, AIM, and EMP-Net. On the data side, it departs from standard contrastive learning in VLM-based FSAR (which typically uses only positive prompts or random negatives) by introducing an LLM-generated, verifier-filtered curriculum of class-specific hard negatives with graded difficulty. The paper also extends the evaluation scope beyond within-dataset FSAR to a cross-dataset continual-learning protocol (AvgAcc, BWT, FWT) that prior VLM-based FSAR work has not systematically addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that constraining *where* adaptation happens (orthogonal subspace) and *what* the model sees (curriculum-ranked hard negatives) can outperform tuning 30× more parameters, offering a principled recipe for stable VLM adaptation in data-limited video settings. The cross-dataset continual-learning evaluation is a useful addition, though it covers only 2–3 task sequences and a single backbone (CLIP ViT-B/16), leaving open whether the entropy-based effective rank and the upper-layer WqWvWk projection strategy generalize to larger or architecturally different VLMs. The LLM-based negative generation adds a non-trivial preprocessing dependency (DeepSeek-R1 or equivalent), and the curriculum schedule (fixed phase boundaries) has not been explored for adaptive or per-class variants.
