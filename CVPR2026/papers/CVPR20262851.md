## Problem

Discrete visual generation methods are confined to low-dimensional latent tokens (8–32 dims), sacrificing the semantic richness that high-dimensional pretrained representations (768–1024 dims) provide for understanding. Directly generating from such high-dimensional discrete tokens is intractable: autoregressive models require O(hwd) sequential steps, and standard discrete diffusion masks entire spatial positions, failing to capture dependencies across dimensions within each location.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label as conditioning and generates 256×256 RGB images. Internally, it operates on a 16×16×768 tensor of discrete tokens (196,608 total per image) produced by dimension-wise quantization of frozen DINOv2-B or SigLIP2-B features. Generation proceeds in T parallel iterations (typically 256–512 steps) regardless of the 196,608 token count, with a decoder from RAE converting the final discrete tensor back to pixels.

## Method

CubiD discretizes high-dimensional encoder features via dimension-wise quantization (each of the 768 dimensions independently mapped to L discrete levels), then models the resulting h×w×d tensor with a masked diffusion approach that treats the entire 3D tensor as a unified space. During training, individual elements are randomly masked across both spatial and dimensional axes (sampled from a truncated Gaussian ratio), and a bidirectional Transformer predicts the masked values via cross-entropy loss, where each spatial position's d tokens form a single sequence token. At inference, the model starts from a fully masked tensor and iteratively unmasks subsets following a cosine schedule, requiring only O(T) steps independent of dimensionality. The key architectural choice is that the Transformer's sequence length remains h×w (256) regardless of d, with an MLP head producing d×L logits per position, decoupling computational cost from feature dimensionality.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, CubiD-XXL (3.7B params, 768-dim tokens) achieves a gFID of 1.88 with classifier-free guidance and 2.02 without guidance, establishing state-of-the-art among discrete generation methods. The method shows consistent scaling: gFID improves from 2.37 (CubiD-L, 946M) to 2.04 (CubiD-XL, 1.4B) to 1.88 (CubiD-XXL, 3.7B) with guidance. Notably, even without guidance, CubiD-XXL (2.02) outperforms most low-dimensional baselines that require guidance or rejection sampling to reach comparable quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CubiD-XXL (1.88 gFID with cfg) outperforms the strongest low-dimensional autoregressive baseline VAR (1.97) and TiTok-S-128 (1.97) by ~0.09 gFID, despite operating in a 768-dim token space versus their 32-dim and 16-dim spaces. Against VFM Tok-XXL (1.95 w/o guidance, 2.19 with cfg), CubiD-XXL wins decisively with guidance (1.88 vs 2.19) but trails slightly without guidance (2.02 vs 1.95); however, VFM Tok reorganizes features into 12-dim VQ tokens, fundamentally altering the representation space. CubiD-XXL substantially outperforms LlamaGen-XXL (2.34 with cfg) and MaskGIT (4.02 with rejection sampling), the two most prominent prior discrete diffusion/autoregressive methods. The paper also highlights that CubiD's representation tokens show reduced dependency on classifier-free guidance—achieving 2.02 without guidance versus VAR's 2.16—suggesting the semantic richness of 768-dim tokens provides stronger intrinsic conditioning. On understanding benchmarks (Table 3), dimension-wise quantized SigLIP2 features match continuous performance (63.1 vs 63.2 on GQA), while vector quantization degrades significantly (54.9 on GQA), validating the discretization choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked discrete diffusion paradigm of MaskGIT from spatial-level masking to element-wise masking across the full h×w×d tensor, and pairs it with dimension-wise quantization to discretize frozen high-dimensional representation features (DINOv2, SigLIP2) without retraining. It departs from the dominant line of discrete visual generation (VQGAN, VAR, LlamaGen, VFM Tok) that operates on compressed low-dimensional tokens (8–32 dims) by generating directly in the native 768-dim representation space. The continuous-diffusion approach of RAE, which also uses high-dimensional features, is replaced here with a fully discrete token-prediction objective, aligning visual generation with the autoregressive token paradigm used in language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that dimension-wise quantized 768-dim tokens retain near-identical understanding performance (Table 3) while supporting competitive generation (FID 1.88), the paper provides a concrete path toward unified multimodal architectures where a single discrete token space serves both comprehension and synthesis. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, text-conditioned generation, and video, and whether the cubic masking strategy transfers to other structured token spaces (e.g., multi-scale or hierarchical representations). The current evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, so broader applicability remains to be demonstrated.
