## Problem

Existing controllable diffusion frameworks like ControlNet and OminiControl either assume spatial alignment between conditions and latents (breaking down for subject-driven generation) or converge slowly on linear-attention backbones like SANA. This limits the deployment of high-quality controllable generation on edge devices, where privacy-sensitive users need local inference without uploading personal data to cloud servers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261811/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt plus a flexible image condition (Canny edges, depth maps, HED sketches, blurred/colorless images, or subject reference photos) and produces a generated image at 512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution. Both the noisy latent and the image condition are encoded through the same VAE into a shared parameter space, so no separate condition encoder (e.g., CLIP) is required. The framework handles both spatially aligned conditions (edges, depth) and spatially unaligned conditions (subject identity) within a single unified pipeline.

## Method

GateControl introduces a unified gated conditioning module built on a shared-module dual-path design: noisy latents and image conditions are concatenated as a single token sequence [X; C_T; C_I] and processed through identical SANA blocks with LoRA (rank 16) fine-tuning. The key mechanism is a minimal token-wise gate applied after each linear-attention layer, where gating scores are computed as σ(X·W_g1) ⊙ h_X for the latent tokens and σ(C_I·W_g2) ⊙ h_C_I for condition tokens, then fused by element-wise addition. This gate adaptively preserves or suppresses per-token information that would otherwise be lost in linear-attention's kernel decomposition, effectively compensating for information compression without requiring full softmax attention. The entire gated module adds only 0.09M parameters (0.006% of SANA), yet achieves over 10× faster convergence on spatially aligned tasks and stable performance on subject-driven generation, outperforming ControlNet and OminiControl across FID, SSIM, MUSIQ, and CLIP metrics.

## Results

On five spatially aligned tasks (Canny, Depth, Deblurring, Colorization, HED) evaluated on COCO 2017, the proposed GateControl method consistently outperforms both ControlNet and OminiControl on the SANA backbone. Headline improvements include reducing FID from 24.95 to 10.28 on colorization, cutting HED MSE from 2320 to 1168 (>50% reduction), and dropping deblurring FID from 69.24 (ControlNet) to 7.45. The method also achieves over 10× faster convergence on spatial tasks, reaching competitive quality within 1k training steps versus >10k for the OminiControl baseline, while adding only 0.09M parameters (0.006% of SANA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet on SANA, GateControl delivers large gains across all tasks: FID drops from 24.95 to 10.28 on colorization, MSE halves from 2320 to 1168 on HED, and deblurring FID plummets from 69.24 to 7.45, while also improving SSIM, MUSIQ, and CLIP scores on every task. Compared to OminiControl (the closest same-backbone baseline), GateControl wins on controllability (F1 0.26 vs 0.23 on Canny; MSE 626 vs 803 on Depth; MSE 14 vs 120 on Deblurring) and on image quality (FID 7.45 vs 10.65 on Deblurring; CLIP-Image 0.934 vs 0.896), with only marginal trade-offs on MUSIQ for Deblurring. Against SD1.5-based methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter), GateControl is competitive on FID and alignment but slightly lower on Canny F1 (0.26 vs 0.35), reflecting the different base model and resolution. The paper also reports that GateControl converges >10× faster on spatial tasks (1k vs >10k steps to reach comparable quality) and adds only 0.09M parameters versus 590M for ControlNet on SANA, making it far more suitable for on-device deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SANA's linear-attention diffusion backbone and OminiControl's token-concatenation conditioning strategy, adapting both to a shared-module architecture that avoids the heavy parameter overhead of ControlNet's trainable copy. It departs from the multimodal-attention paradigm (OminiControl, EasyControl) by replacing full attention-based condition injection with a minimal token-wise gated fusion mechanism, drawing conceptual inspiration from attention-sink analysis in LLMs and classical gating in recurrent networks. The work positions itself as the first controllable generation framework specifically designed for linear-attention diffusion transformers, filling a gap where neither feature-addition (ControlNet) nor attention-based (OminiControl) control works well under the information compression inherent to linear attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling privacy-preserving, on-device controllable image generation with negligible parameter overhead (0.09M, ~0.006% of SANA), which is critical for edge deployment where cloud-based diffusion models raise data-privacy concerns. The 10× convergence speedup on spatially aligned tasks makes training substantially cheaper, and the unified design handles both spatially aligned (edges, depth) and unaligned (subject-driven) conditions without architectural changes. Limitations include evaluation on a single model family (SANA-1.0-1.6B), a relatively small training set (10K images for spatial tasks), and no comparison against full-fine-tuning baselines; open questions include whether the gated mechanism transfers to other linear-attention architectures (e.g., Mamba-based) and whether it scales to video or higher-resolution generation.
