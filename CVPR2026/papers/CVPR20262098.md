## Problem

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting models rely on pixel-aligned or voxel-aligned primitive placement on rigid regular grids, which forces a fixed number of primitives per pixel or voxel regardless of scene content. This limits both rendering quality (redundant primitives cause blurriness, grid artifacts are visible) and efficiency (one primitive per pixel at 256×256 resolution), and prevents the adaptive, content-aware distribution that optimization-based 3DGS achieves through densification and pruning.

## Input / Output

The model takes N (2–12) unposed, uncalibrated RGB images of a static scene, resized so the longest side is 518 pixels, and produces a complete 3DGS model with sub-pixel-positioned primitives, predicted camera extrinsics and intrinsics, and depth maps—all in a single forward pass. The output model uses only ~0.14 primitives per input pixel (an 86% reduction after confidence-based pruning), and the system is fully pose-free, requiring no precomputed SfM or camera annotations.

## Method

The architecture combines a fine-tuned VGGT multi-view transformer backbone (1B parameters) with a custom U-Net convolutional decoder that detects 3D Gaussian primitives at sub-pixel resolution. The decoder uses DSNT (soft-argmax) on per-primitive heatmaps within 14×14 image patches to extract continuous 2D center coordinates, then unprojects them with predicted depth to obtain 3D positions. An adaptive density mechanism assigns 16, 32, or 64 primitives per patch based on Shannon entropy of grayscale intensities, allocating more capacity to detailed regions. Multi-view aggregation uses learned per-Gaussian confidence scores to prune redundant primitives across views. Training is fully self-supervised with photometric losses (L1, SSIM, LPIPS), geometry consistency losses (rendered vs. predicted depth and normals), teacher geometry losses anchoring to VGGT's original predictions, and an opacity regularization term, all optimized end-to-end without any 3D annotation.

## Results

On the average of 6 held-out datasets (3/6/9/12 views), Off-The-Grid achieves 21.21 PSNR, 0.6470 SSIM, and 0.3532 LPIPS, outperforming AnySplat (17.71 PSNR) by +3.5 dB and DA3-GS (18.83 PSNR) by +2.4 dB. The method uses only 0.1431 Gaussians per input pixel (86% fewer than pixel-aligned), compared to 0.8141 for AnySplat and 1.0 for DA3-GS, achieving a 7× compression ratio. On geometry evaluation, the fine-tuned encoder improves over VGGT (depth AbsRel 0.1433 vs 0.1491; camera pose AUC@30 0.9278 vs 0.8957) and achieves the best intrinsics accuracy (FoV angular error 0.96° vs 3.66° for DA3-Giant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnySplat (the closest prior work, also fine-tuning VGGT), Off-The-Grid wins decisively on all NVS metrics: +3.5 dB PSNR, +0.14 SSIM, and −0.04 LPIPS on average, while using 5.7× fewer primitives (0.1431 vs 0.8141 #G/pix). AnySplat's voxel-aligned decoder produces visible grid artifacts and noisy geometry under extrapolated views, whereas Off-The-Grid's sub-pixel detection yields cleaner renderings. Against DA3-GS (Depth Anything 3 with a pixel-aligned decoder), Off-The-Grid gains +2.4 dB PSNR and −0.03 LPIPS while using 7× fewer primitives; DA3-GS produces detailed but blurry and half-transparent outputs due to its dense pixel-aligned representation. On geometry, Off-TheGrid's fine-tuned VGGT encoder beats AnySplat on all three metrics (depth AbsRel 0.1433 vs 0.159, AUC@30 0.9278 vs 0.8333, FoV error 0.96° vs 3.23°) and outperforms the unmodified VGGT backbone, confirming that photometric fine-tuning improves multi-view consistency. DA3-Giant remains slightly better on depth (0.1339 vs 0.1433 AbsRel) and camera pose (0.9339 vs 0.9278 AUC@30), but its focal length estimation is substantially worse (3.66° vs 0.96° FoV error), explaining the blur observed in its renderings. The SplatterImage-style 3D offset baseline (pixel-aligned + offset) performs comparably to plain pixel-aligned (18.33 vs 18.33 avg PSNR) and degrades LPIPS, confirming that simple offsets are insufficient for accurate primitive placement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feed-forward 3DGS line initiated by PixelSplat (pixel-aligned) and AnySplat (voxel-aligned, also fine-tuning VGGT) by replacing the regular grid with a learned sub-pixel detection mechanism. It borrows the DSNT/soft-argmax operation from keypoint detection literature (Nibali et al.) to extract continuous 2D primitive centers from convolutional heatmaps, and adapts Shannon-entropy-based patch sizing from APT to create an adaptive density decoder. The conceptual departure is framing primitive placement as a detection problem rather than a per-pixel or per-voxel regression, while retaining VGGT as the geometry backbone and training end-to-end with photometric supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 86% primitive reduction (7× fewer than pixel-aligned) while achieving state-of-the-art NVS quality makes feed-forward 3DGS substantially more practical for high-resolution inputs, where pixel-aligned methods become intractable. The confidence-based pruning that implicitly learns multi-view redundancy is a clean mechanism that could transfer to other feed-forward 3D representations. Open questions include whether the method can handle occluded regions (currently only visible geometry is reconstructed), view-dependent appearance, and whether the adaptive density thresholds generalize beyond the training distribution. The paper also notes that humans are absent from training data, limiting applicability to dynamic subjects.
