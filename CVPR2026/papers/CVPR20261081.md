## Problem

Existing MLLMs excel at object-level grounding but remain inaccurate at part-level point grounding (e.g., localizing a knife's handle rather than the whole knife), a gap that limits their utility in fine-grained robotic manipulation. Prior frozen-attention approaches extract native text-to-image attention maps but produce suboptimal spatial precision for part-level targets, while fine-tuning-based methods risk degrading the model's original reasoning and conversational abilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261081/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text query (e.g., 'Point to the handle of the knife') as input, processed through a frozen MLLM backbone. It outputs a high-resolution 2D point-centric heatmap from which the final point coordinate is extracted as the argmax location. The heatmap is upsampled from the low-resolution patch grid (typically image dimensions divided by 14) to full image resolution, and the method operates on the top-k grounding-relevant attention heads identified from the LLM's intermediate layers.

## Method

The framework has two trainable components attached to a fully frozen MLLM. The Query Synthesis (Q-Synth) Module replaces the conventional last-token query with a single grounding-aware query synthesized via stacked cross-attention layers: N learnable latent vectors iteratively attend to all text features as keys/values, then a lightweight MLP aggregates the refined latents into one query that drives a targeted text-to-image attention map. The Attention-to-Point (A2P) Decoder modulates image features with this synthesized attention, fuses across k localization heads via learned weighting, and applies progressive convolutional upsampling to produce a high-resolution heatmap. Training uses a per-patch BCE loss to shape the synthesized attention toward near-binary target-region activations, combined with a modified SDF-based penalty field that asymmetrically penalizes predictions outside the ground-truth mask more heavily than those inside, encouraging concentration at the innermost point. All original MLLM parameters remain frozen throughout training, preserving pre-trained reasoning capabilities.

## Results

On the PACO direct pointing task, the method achieves 0.510 accuracy with Molmo-7B and 0.479 with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, outperforming the respective text-pointing baselines (0.487 and 0.407). On the InstructPart reasoning pointing task, gains are even larger: Molmo-7B reaches 0.868 accuracy (vs. 0.710 for text pointing) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B reaches 0.818 (vs. 0.708). Remarkably, even a First-Gen-MLLM without any point-grounding training achieves 0.463 accuracy on PACO and 0.783 on InstructPart with the proposed modules, far exceeding its native text-pointing performance of 0.068 and 0.033.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all baselines across both datasets and all three MLLM backbones. Against the strongest pointing-capable baseline (Molmo-7B text pointing [5]), the method gains +2.3 points on PACO accuracy (0.510 vs. 0.487) and a substantial +15.8 points on InstructPart accuracy (0.868 vs. 0.710), indicating the Q-Synth Module is particularly effective for longer, reasoning-heavy queries. Compared to the attention-pointing baseline [9], improvements are even more dramatic on InstructPart (e.g., 0.868 vs. 0.378 for Molmo-7B), confirming that native attention maps are insufficient for part-level grounding. Segmentation-based baselines (VLPart [20], X-Decoder [29]) are far weaker, with VLPart scoring only 0.008 on InstructPart due to its lack of reasoning ability. For the First-Gen-MLLM without any point-grounding training, the method lifts accuracy from 0.068 (text pointing) to 0.463 on PACO and from 0.033 to 0.783 on InstructPart, demonstrating that the framework can equip even untrained models with competitive part-level grounding while keeping all backbone parameters frozen.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the emergent attention property in MLLMs identified by Kang et al. [9] and Wu et al. (F-LMM) [24], which showed that a small subset of attention heads naturally encodes text-to-image grounding signals. Rather than using those native attention maps directly (which the authors show is suboptimal for part-level targets) or fine-tuning the full model with special grounding tokens (which degrades reasoning), the paper introduces a frozen-backbone refinement pipeline: a cross-attention-based Query Synthesis Module replaces the single last-token query with a learned, sentence-level grounding query, and an Attention-to-Point Decoder upsamples the resulting map into a high-resolution heatmap supervised by an SDF-based penalty field. This positions the work as a lightweight, plug-and-play complement to existing pointing-capable MLLMs (Molmo, Qwen2.5-VL) and a principled alternative to segmentation-token fine-tuning approaches (LISA, GLaMM, X-Decoder).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Part-level point grounding is a critical perception primitive for robotic manipulation (e.g., grasping a knife handle or folding a garment at the cuff), yet current MLLMs show a large object-level-to-part-level accuracy gap. The frozen-backbone design is practically important because it lets practitioners bolt precise pointing onto any open-source MLLM without retraining, preserving all pre-trained reasoning and conversational abilities. Open questions include whether the 2D SDF penalty field and patch-based attention formulation extend naturally to 3D grounding or video, whether the method degrades on very long or compositional instructions beyond the tested benchmarks, and whether the top-k head selection strategy remains stable as MLLM architectures evolve.
