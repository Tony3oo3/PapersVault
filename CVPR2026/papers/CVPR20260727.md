## Problem

Despite sharing a similar problem structure, geometric keypoint matching across image-to-image (2D-2D), image-to-point cloud (2D-3D), and point cloud-to-point cloud (3D-3D) has been solved exclusively with task-specific models and architectures. No unified correspondence model with shared weights exists that can handle all three modality combinations, preventing joint learning of geometric priors across 2D and 3D domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260727/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source-target pair (image-image, image-point cloud, or point cloud-point cloud) along with a set of source keypoints of interest (N×m, where m∈{2,3}), which can be detector outputs or uniformly grid-sampled points. It produces target correspondence keypoints (N×l, where l matches the target modality dimension) plus per-keypoint confidence scores that quantify uncertainty in challenging regions such as occlusions, sky, and translucent objects. The query-based interface means the model decodes correspondences only for the specified source keypoints rather than producing dense full-resolution matches.

## Method

UniCorrn employs modality-specific backbones (ViT for images, Point Transformer v3 for point clouds) feeding into a shared feature fusion encoder with alternating self- and cross-attention layers, followed by the key contribution: a dual-stream matching decoder. In each decoder layer, a single attention matrix is computed via a Gaussian kernel over pairwise L2 distances between position-augmented (RoPE) query and key features, then used to independently update two residual streams—an appearance stream (F_k) and a positional stream (P_k) initialized to zeros. The positional stream accumulates attention-weighted learned absolute positional encodings of target coordinates, from which final correspondence locations are regressed via the Moore-Penrose inverse of the encoding matrix. The model is trained jointly on all three tasks using a confidence-aware L1 loss, InfoNCE contrastive loss on feature descriptors, and auxiliary coordinate supervision at every decoder layer, with pseudo point clouds from depth maps supplementing scarce 3D correspondence annotations.

## Results

On 2D-3D matching, UniCorrn achieves 91.0% Registration Recall (RR) on 7Scenes and 92.5% on RGB-D Scenes V2, surpassing the previous best (Diff-Reg at 83.8% and 87.4% respectively) by 8% and 5%. On 3D-3D matching, it reaches 86.7% RR on the challenging low-overlap 3DLoMatch benchmark (vs. 79.0% for PEAL-3D) and 97.5% on 3DMatch. On 2D-2D matching, UniCorrn is competitive but not SOTA: it achieves 54.2% AUC@5° on MegaDepth-1500 (vs. 62.6% for RoMa) and 71.3% AUC@20° on ScanNet-1500 (vs. 74.3% for MASt3R).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCorrn decisively outperforms all 2D-3D baselines: it beats Diff-Reg (the previous SOTA) by 8% RR on 7Scenes (91.0 vs. 83.8) and 5% on RGB-D Scenes V2 (92.5 vs. 87.4), while also achieving substantially higher Inlier Ratio (82.4 vs. 59.1 on 7Scenes). On 3D-3D, UniCorrn surpasses PEAL-3D by ~8% RR on 3DLoMatch (86.7 vs. 79.0) and Diff-Reg by 2.5% on 3DMatch (97.5 vs. 95.0), with the largest IR gap on 3DLoMatch (77.3 vs. 49.0). On 2D-2D, UniCorrn trails the dedicated SOTA methods: RoMa achieves 62.6% AUC@5° on MegaDepth vs. UniCorrn's 54.2%, and MASt3R leads on ScanNet-1500 (74.3 vs. 71.3 AUC@20°). On the InLoc visual localization benchmark, UniCorrn (56.1/79.3/89.4 on DUC1) is competitive with MASt3R (56.1/79.3/90.9) but behind RoMa (60.1/79.3/89.9). The paper notes that 2D-2D SOTA methods like RoMa and DKM exploit high-resolution feature warping for subpixel accuracy, a technique inapplicable to 2D-3D correspondence, which explains the performance gap on 2D-2D but not on cross-modal tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCorrn extends the line of unified correspondence work (UCN, Glu-Net, RGM, MatchAnything, MATCHA, UFM) that has so far been confined to the 2D image domain, and the separate task-specific 2D-3D (2D3D-MATR, B2-3Dnet, DiffReg) and 3D-3D (GeoT, RegTR, PEAL-3D) registration methods, into a single shared-weight Transformer model spanning all three modality combinations. Architecturally, it builds on MASt3R's confidence-aware loss and CroCo v2's pretrained image features, while introducing a novel dual-stream decoder that decouples appearance and positional feature streams to enable stackable, end-to-end correspondence refinement. The key departure from prior unified 2D approaches is the rejection of cost-volume, nearest-neighbor, and direct-regression matching paradigms in favor of attention-based query matching that generalizes to the sparse, irregular geometry of 3D point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single model with shared weights can handle geometric correspondence across 2D and 3D modalities, reducing engineering complexity and enabling cross-modal reconstruction pipelines. The 8% and 10% registration-recall gains on 7Scenes and 3DLoMatch over task-specific SOTA validate the approach, but the joint-training analysis reveals that normalization layers suffer gradient conflicts across modalities, and 2D-2D performance remains slightly below dedicated SOTA (RoMa, DKM). Open questions include whether better cross-modality normalization or alignment strategies can resolve these conflicts, and whether the unified formulation scales to additional modalities (e.g., LiDAR, multi-spectral) or longer sequences.
