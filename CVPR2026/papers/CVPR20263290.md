## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for LVLMs rely on a decoupled two-stage heuristic: first selecting top-k tokens by attention scores, then applying post-hoc diversity compensation (clustering or merging). This strategy is fundamentally sub-optimal because the initial top-k selection systematically ignores secondary yet critical semantic clusters, and the retained high-salience tokens exhibit high semantic redundancy, leading to sampling collapse where tokens concentrate on the main subject while neglecting the rest of the image.

## Input / Output

SCoRe operates as a training-free, plug-and-play module between the Vision Encoder and the Modality Projector. It consumes two tensors from the Vision Encoder's second-to-last layer: L D-dimensional visual token features and their associated [CLS] attention scores (used as salience weights), along with a target subset size k. It outputs k representative token indices, which are used to extract a compact token subset that is then projected into the LLM's semantic space and concatenated with text tokens for downstream generation. The method handles variable token counts across architectures (576 for LLaVA-1.5, 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT, 256 for Qwen-VL, 2048 for Video-LLaVA).

## Method

SCoRe formalizes visual token pruning as a unified Representativeness Optimization Problem, proving it is formally equivalent to the classic Weighted k-Center Problem (NP-hard but admitting efficient greedy approximation). The representativeness loss is defined as the maximum over all tokens of (salience weight × minimum distance to the selected set), encoding the principle that every token must either be semantically close to the retained subset or intrinsically unimportant. The greedy algorithm iteratively selects the token maximizing a single composite score: the product of its minimum cosine distance to the already-selected set and its [CLS] attention score raised to a balance hyperparameter α. This unified score intrinsically suppresses redundancy (selecting a point from a high-salience cluster immediately decreases the distance score of nearby tokens) while incentivizing coverage of previously overlooked semantic clusters, thereby preventing the sampling collapse inherent in decoupled heuristics.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B with 10 image benchmarks, SCoRe retains 95.0% of full-model performance at a 94.4% token reduction (32 tokens), compared to 91.5% for VisPruner, 89.0% for VisionZip, and 77.9% for SparseVLM. At a milder 77.8% reduction (128 tokens), SCoRe actually exceeds the full-model baseline (100.6% relative performance). On LLaVA-NeXT-7B (2880 tokens), SCoRe preserves 93.4% of performance at 94.4% reduction, outperforming the next-best method VisPruner (86.7%) by 6.7 percentage points. In video understanding with Video-LLaVA, SCoRe achieves 45.2% average accuracy at 94.4% reduction versus 44.5% for VisPruner and 42.4% for FastV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCoRe outperforms all five baselines across every reduction ratio and every benchmark tested. Against the strongest cross-attention baseline SparseVLM, SCoRe leads by 17.1 percentage points in relative performance at 94.4% pruning (95.0% vs. 77.9%) and by 11.4 points at 88.9% pruning (97.8% vs. 86.4%). Among vision-feature-based methods, SCoRe surpasses the closest competitor VisPruner by 3.5 points at 94.4% pruning (95.0% vs. 91.5%) and by 1.2 points at 88.9% (97.8% vs. 96.6%), with the gap widening on high-resolution LLaVA-NeXT (93.4% vs. 86.7% at 94.4%). VisionZip, which relies on token merging, trails SCoRe by 6.0 points at 94.4% pruning (89.0% vs. 95.0%), confirming that unified salience-coverage selection is more effective than post-hoc merging. The older baselines FastV and ToMe are substantially weaker, retaining only 64.1% and 61.4% of performance respectively at 94.4% pruning, underscoring the advantage of vision-encoder-based signals over LLM cross-attention or naive similarity merging.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCoRe departs from the dominant decoupled heuristic paradigm in visual token pruning (select top-k by salience, then compensate for diversity via clustering or merging, as in VisionZip and VisPruner) by unifying both objectives into a single greedy optimization step. Theoretically, it grounds the pruning problem in the classic Weighted k-Center Problem (Hochbaum & Shmoys, 1986), providing a principled combinatorial-optimization formulation rather than ad-hoc heuristics. Practically, it inherits the vision-encoder feature source (CLS attention for salience, cosine distance for coverage) shared with recent methods like VisPruner and VisionZip, but eliminates their two-stage pipeline in favor of a single iterative selection rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 94.4% token reduction while retaining 95% of full-model performance across LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen-VL, and Video-LLaVA makes SCoRe a practically attractive plug-and-play module for deploying VLMs on resource-constrained hardware, and its pre-LLM placement is compatible with FlashAttention. A key open question is that SCoRe is strictly query-agnostic: it never conditions on the user's text prompt, so it cannot adaptively retain tokens relevant to a specific question, which may limit gains on highly selective tasks. Additionally, the greedy k-Center approximation's quality relative to the true optimum is not characterized, and the single hyperparameter α, while robust in [0.7, 0.9], still requires a small search per deployment.
