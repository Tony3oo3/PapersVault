## Problem

Multi-modal continual AQA methods assume all modalities remain complete and stable across tasks, but real-world deployments suffer from non-stationary modality imbalance where sensors fail or annotations are intermittently missing. Existing multi-modal AQA models degrade sharply when modalities go missing, while continual AQA approaches that mitigate task-level forgetting still rely on fully observed inputs, leaving the combined challenge of missing-modality reconstruction and cross-task stability unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263945/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each continual session, the method receives a subset of observed modalities (video, audio, textual commentary) along with a binary mask indicating which modalities are missing, plus a memory buffer of feature embeddings from prior tasks. Missing modalities are reconstructed at the feature-embedding level (not raw data) for efficiency and privacy. The output is a single continuous action quality score per sample, evaluated via SRCC, MSE, and RL2 across sequential tasks with missing rates of 10%, 25%, and 50%.

## Method

BriMA combines two modules: (1) Memory-Guided Bridging Imputation (MBI) retrieves K structurally aligned exemplar features from the memory buffer via cosine similarity, forms a weighted-average initial estimate, and then predicts only a minimal residual correction through a bridging network conditioned on observed features and a task-specific modality mask embedding—avoiding the noise of full generative synthesis; (2) Modality-Aware Replay Optimization (MRO) maintains a quantile-stratified buffer of modality-complete samples and prioritizes replay by a composite score of modality distortion and score drift, complemented by a score-consistency loss that penalizes prediction shifts relative to the previous model snapshot. The joint optimization minimizes score prediction error, memory regularization, and feature-level reconstruction loss simultaneously, with the residual-bridging design ensuring score-faithful completion under evolving modality availability.

## Results

On the RG dataset (β=10%), BriMA achieves an average SRCC of 0.746, MSE of 6.26, and RL2 of 2.231, outperforming the best baseline MER (SRCC 0.722) by 3.8%. On Fis-V (β=10%), BriMA reaches SRCC 0.653 and MSE 19.69, surpassing the second-best NC-FSCIL (SRCC 0.628) by 4.5%. On FS1000 (β=10%), BriMA attains SRCC 0.756, MSE 33.35, and RL2 1.441, beating the best baseline DER++ (SRCC 0.755) by 5.2% in relative terms. As the missing rate increases to 50%, BriMA's advantage expands, maintaining SRCC of 0.659 (RG), 0.634 (Fis-V), and 0.698 (FS1000) while most baselines degrade sharply (e.g., ST-MLAVL drops to 0.422 on RG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BriMA outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and missing rate on SRCC, MSE, and RL2. The closest competitors are DER++ (rehearsal-based, SRCC 0.755 at β=10% on FS1000) and MER (SRCC 0.739), both of which degrade more sharply as β increases (DER++ drops to 0.668 at β=50% vs. BriMA's 0.698). Among AQA-specific continual methods, ASAL (TVCG'25) is the strongest baseline, achieving SRCC 0.739 at β=10% on FS1000, but BriMA still leads by 1.7% and pulls further ahead at higher missing rates (0.698 vs. 0.666 at β=50%). Rehearsal-free methods (SI, EWC, LwF) show the largest degradation under modality imbalance, with LwF dropping to SRCC 0.583 at β=50% on FS1000. On RG at β=50%, BriMA's SRCC of 0.659 exceeds the next-best MER (0.594) by 11%, while ST-MLAVL collapses to 0.422, confirming that both modality completion and replay are essential under severe missingness.

## Relation to Prior Work

BriMA sits at the intersection of three previously separate lines of work: multi-modal AQA (MLAVL, Skating-Mixer), continual AQA (Fs-Aug, MAGR, ASAL), and incomplete multi-modal learning (imputation, retrieval, generative completion). It departs from prior continual AQA by explicitly modeling non-stationary modality imbalance rather than assuming complete inputs, and from prior incomplete multi-modal methods by operating in a continual setting where modality availability evolves across tasks. The method repurposes retrieval-augmented exemplar selection and replay-based CL, but adapts them to AQA's score-sensitive geometry through residual bridging (predicting a correction rather than full synthesis) and modality-distortion-aware replay prioritization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical deployment gap: real-world AQA systems face sensor failures and annotation gaps that existing methods silently ignore, and the paper demonstrates that standard imputation, retrieval, and generative strategies all distort the scoring manifold in ways that degrade rank correlation. Limitations include moderate training overhead from retrieval and replay, pattern-level conditioning that may not scale to many modalities, and the assumption that missing-modality sets are known at training time. Open questions include handling weak (not just missing) modalities, unseen missing patterns at scale, and whether the residual-bridging design generalizes beyond the three sports domains tested.
