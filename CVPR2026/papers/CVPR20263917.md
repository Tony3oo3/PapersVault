## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving methods directly predict raw future trajectories from sensor data, but the inherent spatiotemporal imbalance of trajectory data—where variance grows with horizon—causes two critical failures: models latch onto spurious correlations instead of robust driving logic, and large long-horizon errors dominate the optimization signal at the expense of safety-critical near-term precision.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view camera images and LiDAR point clouds, fused via a Transfuser-style encoder with a ResNet-34 or V2-99 backbone. It outputs 8 future waypoints at 0.5 s intervals, but internally it predicts only the normalized residual from a constant-velocity inertial reference rather than absolute coordinates. At inference, 200 multimodal trajectory candidates are generated via 2-step DDIM denoising and a Transformer-based ranker selects the final trajectory.

## Method

ResAD reframes trajectory prediction as residual prediction: a constant-velocity inertial reference is computed from the ego-vehicle's current state, and a 2-layer diffusion decoder learns to denoise the point-wise residual (ground-truth minus reference) conditioned on encoder features and positional encodings of the reference. Point-wise Residual Normalization (PRNorm) applies a per-component min-max scaling—precomputed across all timesteps and trajectories in the training set—mapping residuals into a symmetric [−γ, γ] interval, which equalizes the optimization weight across near and far waypoints. Inertial Reference Perturbation (IRP) samples K Gaussian perturbations on the initial velocity to generate diverse inertial references, enabling context-aware multimodal generation without a fixed trajectory vocabulary. A Transformer-based ranker, trained with a Gaussian-weighted distillation loss over PDMS/EPDMS sub-metrics, selects the optimal candidate from the generated set.

## Results

On the NAVSIM v1 NAVTEST benchmark, ResAD achieves a state-of-the-art PDMS of 88.8 with a ResNet-34 backbone, surpassing the previous best (WoTE, 88.3) and DiffusionDrive (88.1). With a stronger V2-99 backbone, ResAD further improves to PDMS 90.6, outperforming Hydra-MDP and GoalFlow (both 90.3). On the more challenging NAVSIM v2 benchmark, ResAD reaches EPDMS 85.5, leading all methods by 1.0 over DiffusionDrive (84.5) and 2.4 over DriveSuprim/ARTEMIS (83.1), with notable gains in EP (88.2 vs. 87.5) and DAC (97.2 vs. 95.9) over DiffusionDrive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResAD outperforms all 13 compared baselines on NAVSIM v1 PDMS. Against the closest competitors, ResAD (88.8) beats WoTE (88.3) by 0.5 and DiffusionDrive (88.1) by 0.7, while achieving the best DAC (97.5) and EP (83.3) among all ResNet-34 methods. The gap widens substantially over earlier methods: +1.8 over ARTEMIS (87.0), +2.3 over Hydra-MDP (86.5), and +7.9 over VADv2 (80.9). With the V2-99 backbone, ResAD (90.6) edges out both Hydra-MDP and GoalFlow (90.3) by 0.3, while leading in NC (98.9) and EP (87.0). On NAVSIM v2, ResAD (EPDMS 85.5) leads DiffusionDrive (84.5) by 1.0, DriveSuprim and ARTEMIS (83.1) by 2.4, and Hydra-MDP++ (81.4) by 4.1, with particularly strong improvements in EP (+0.7) and DAC (+1.3) over DiffusionDrive, indicating better route completion and drivable-area adherence under the extended evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResAD builds on the diffusion-based trajectory generation paradigm established by DiffusionDrive and the Transfuser-style sensor fusion encoder, but departs fundamentally from the standard E2EAD formulation of directly predicting raw future waypoints. Its core conceptual shift—reframing the task as predicting a normalized residual from a deterministic inertial (constant-velocity) reference—draws on the reparameterized pushforward idea from R2P2 [42] but applies it to the ego-vehicle's own trajectory rather than other agents' motion. For multimodal planning, ResAD replaces the fixed trajectory vocabulary used by Hydra-MDP, VADv2, and DiffusionDrive with stochastic perturbation of the inertial reference, generating context-aware candidates without a discrete set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play demonstration (Table 4) showing that TRM+PRNorm lifts both MLP-based and diffusion-based planners by ~1.5 PDMS suggests the residual formulation is a broadly applicable inductive bias for any E2EAD system, not just ResAD's specific architecture. The test-time scaling property—where ResAD's PDMS continues to grow with more inference samples while DiffusionDrive plateaus—offers a practical compute-adaptive deployment advantage. However, all evaluation is open-loop on NAVSIM (NuPlan-based, 2 Hz), leaving open whether the inertial reference prior remains valid in closed-loop, reactive, or higher-frequency settings, and whether the constant-velocity baseline is sufficient for aggressive maneuvers like hard braking or sharp turns.
