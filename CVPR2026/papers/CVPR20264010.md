## Problem

Weakly supervised temporal forgery localization (WS-TFL) trains on binary clip-level labels but must localize forged segments at inference, creating a fundamental training-inference objective mismatch. Existing WS-TFL methods suffer from limited semantic supervision from a single binary label, gradient blockage caused by non-differentiable top-k aggregation, and fragmented proposals due to the absence of global inter-proposal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264010/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed talking-face videos with temporally aligned audio and visual streams, extracting frame-level features via pre-trained ResNet-50 and Wav2Vec 2.0 encoders that are aligned to a unified 512-dimensional representation. Training requires only a single binary clip-level label (real vs. forged) per video, with no frame-level or segment-level annotations. At inference, the model outputs a set of forged segments, each specified by a start timestamp, end timestamp, and a confidence score, refined by soft-NMS.

## Method

GEM-TFL is a two-phase classification-regression framework. In the classification phase, a MIL-based branch produces frame-level attention and attribute predictions; an EM-guided Latent Attribute Decomposition (LAD) module iteratively decomposes the binary label into m+1 latent attributes (E-step estimates posteriors, M-step updates parameters), enriching supervision without additional labels. A training-free Temporal Consistency Refinement (TCR) module then solves a KL-based Bregman projection via Iterative Proportional Scaling to realign frame-level attribute predictions with clip-level priors, and a Graph-based Proposal Refinement (GPR) module constructs a proposal relation graph using DIoU and semantic similarity to diffuse confidence across neighbors for globally coherent pseudo proposals. In the localization phase, a regression branch (UMMAFormer) is trained on these pseudo proposals with a gradually increasing regression loss weight and an auxiliary binary classification head to suppress noise from imperfect pseudo labels, bridging the supervision gap between weakly and fully supervised settings.

## Results

On AV-Deepfake1M, GEM-TFL achieves 42.7% average mAP and 59.8% average mAR under weak supervision, surpassing the strongest prior WS-TFL method WMMT by 8.4% in mAP and 7.8% in mAR. On LAV-DF, it reaches 77.6% average mAP and 86.5% average mAR, outperforming WMMT by 4.3% in mAP. Notably, GEM-TFL maintains 57.1% mAP at IoU 0.7 on LAV-DF, substantially narrowing the gap with the best fully supervised method (MFMS at 95.8% mAP@0.7). The ablation confirms each module contributes: LAD adds +18.7% mAP, TCR +3.5%, GPR +4.6%, and the Localization Phase +10.8% on AV-Deepfake1M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM-TFL outperforms all weakly supervised baselines on both datasets. On AV-Deepfake1M, it surpasses WMMT (the strongest prior WS-TFL method) by 8.4% in average mAP (42.7% vs. 34.3%) and 7.8% in average mAR (59.8% vs. 52.0%), and beats PseudoFormer by 19.1% in mAP (42.7% vs. 23.6%). On LAV-DF, it leads WMMT by 4.3% mAP (77.6% vs. 73.3%) and PseudoFormer by 12.9% mAP (77.6% vs. 64.7%). The gap to fully supervised methods narrows substantially: on LAV-DF, GEM-TFL reaches 77.6% mAP versus MFMS's 97.3% (a 19.7-point gap), while on AV-Deepfake1M the gap is 51.4 points (42.7% vs. 94.1%), reflecting the greater difficulty of the latter benchmark. In cross-dataset generalization (trained on AV-Deepfake1M, tested on LAV-DF), GEM-TFL achieves 17.1% average mAP, outperforming PseudoFormer (13.7%) and WMMT (13.0%), confirming that the latent attribute decomposition provides more transferable semantic supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM-TFL inherits its two-phase classification-regression architecture from PseudoFormer [22], which first bridged weak and full supervision in temporal action localization, and adapts it to the audio-visual deepfake setting. Its supervision-enrichment strategy is conceptually aligned with WMMT [39], which augments binary labels with modality-specific forgery types, but GEM-TFL replaces the (partially label-leaking) quadruple labels with an EM-optimized latent attribute decomposition that stays within the strict weak-supervision assumption. Proposal confidence estimation follows AutoLoc's OIC formulation [32], but the paper departs from direct OIC-weighted fusion by introducing graph-based diffusion over a proposal relation graph, and adds a training-free IPS-based temporal consistency refinement absent from all prior WS-TFL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that enriching a single binary clip-level label into multiple latent attributes via EM can substantially narrow the weak-to-full supervision gap in temporal forgery localization (+8% mAP on AV-Deepfake1M), offering a principled alternative to methods that relax the weak-supervision assumption. However, a large absolute gap to fully supervised methods persists (42.7% vs. 94.1% mAP on AV-Deepfake1M), and the approach is tied to a specific regression backbone (UMMAFormer) and audio-visual deepfake domain. Open questions include whether the EM decomposition generalizes to other weakly supervised localization tasks, whether the optimal number of latent attributes (m=3) is dataset-dependent, and whether multimodal foundation models or self-distillation can close the remaining gap as the authors suggest.
