## Problem

Standard MLE-based prompt learning for vision-language models overfits on limited training data and generalizes poorly to out-of-distribution samples. Existing Bayesian prompt learning methods rely on unimodal Gaussian variational approximations or deterministic particle-based methods (e.g., SVGD) that limit the diversity of the posterior, failing to capture the complex multimodal landscape of effective prompts. The paper asks how to efficiently sample from this multimodal posterior in a modular way that can extend any existing prompt learning method.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a 16-shot few-shot setting, taking RGB images and their class labels as input, with learnable prompt tokens injected at multiple depths in both the vision and text transformer encoders of a frozen CLIP model. At inference, predictions are produced by uniform-weighted ensembling over C×K posterior prompt samples (where C is the number of cycles and K the number of MCMC particles), yielding a class label whose implicit uncertainty is reflected in the ensemble spread. The repulsive force is computed on mini-batch-level representation distributions (e.g., 32 samples), keeping the additional computational cost negligible.

## Method

ReBaPL introduces a repulsive cyclical SGHMC (rcSGHMC) sampler that alternates between exploration phases (high cosine-scheduled learning rate, no noise injection) and exploitation/sampling phases (noise injected to draw from the posterior) within each cycle. The key distinguishing mechanism is an inter-cycle repulsive force: each sample in the current cycle is pushed away from the terminal samples of the previous cycle via a potential V(θ,θ′)=1/(d_Θ(θ,θ′)²+ε), where d_Θ is a probability metric (MMD or Wasserstein distance) computed between the distributions of network activations induced by the two prompt sets. This representation-space repulsion captures functional similarity between prompts rather than raw parameter distance, encouraging discovery of functionally diverse modes. The method is fully plug-and-play: it wraps any MLE-based prompt learning objective (demonstrated on MaPLe and MMRL) by replacing the single-point optimization with the rcSGHMC sampling procedure, requiring no architectural changes to the underlying prompt learning method.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization protocol (16-shot, 11 datasets), MMRL + ReBaPL achieves the best average harmonic mean of 81.38% (Base 85.74%, Novel 77.44%), outperforming all baselines. MaPLe + ReBaPL reaches 79.52% HM (Base 83.28%, Novel 76.08%), improving over MaPLe alone by +1.15 in HM. The largest individual gains appear on EuroSAT (+4.21 HM for MMRL+ReBaPL) and DTD (+3.17 HM for MaPLe+ReBaPL) novel-class accuracy, while cross-dataset transfer (ImageNet→10 targets) yields a 67.62% average for MMRL+ReBaPL, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMRL + ReBaPL (81.38% avg HM) outperforms all seven baselines, with the largest margins over CoOp (+10.55), APP (+8.77), and CLIP (+9.68). Against the closest competitor PromptSRC (78.61% HM), MMRL+ReBaPL leads by 2.77 points, and against CoCoOp (75.83%) by 5.55 points. Compared to its own base method MMRL (80.59% HM), ReBaPL adds +0.79 HM with the gain concentrated on novel classes (+0.92), confirming the repulsive exploration mechanism improves generalization rather than in-distribution fit. MaPLe + ReBaPL (79.52% HM) similarly beats MaPLe (78.37%) by +1.15, with notable novel-class gains on DTD (+4.10) and EuroSAT (+2.17). The method does not uniformly win every per-dataset cell (e.g., MaPLe+ReBaPL loses 0.36 on OxfordPets novel), but consistently improves the harmonic mean, indicating a better base/novel balance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian prompt learning line (APP, VaMP) by replacing deterministic variational approximations (SVGD particles, unimodal Gaussians) with a stochastic MCMC sampler—specifically a repulsive cyclical SGHMC algorithm. It departs from prior Bayesian prompt methods in two key ways: (1) the repulsive force operates in representation space via probability metrics (MMD, Wasserstein) rather than in parameter space, capturing functional similarity between prompts; and (2) it is explicitly modular, acting as a plug-and-play Bayesian wrapper over any existing MLE-based prompt learner (demonstrated on MaPLe and MMRL). The cyclical exploration/sampling schedule is inherited from Zhang et al.'s Cyclical SGMCMC, while the Hamiltonian dynamics follow Chen et al.'s SGHMC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design lowers the barrier to adopting Bayesian prompt learning: practitioners can wrap their existing MLE prompt method without architectural changes, gaining posterior sampling and diversity for free. The representation-space repulsion is a conceptually clean idea—comparing what prompts *do* (their induced distributions) rather than what they *are* (their weights)—but the empirical gains are modest (~1% harmonic mean), and inference requires running C×K forward passes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger prompt spaces, whether adaptive cycle counts or alternative metrics (Sinkhorn divergence, information-theoretic measures) yield larger gains, and whether the benefits persist beyond the 16-shot CLIP setting to other foundation model architectures.
