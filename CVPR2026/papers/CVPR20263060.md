## Problem

Existing Gaussian Splatting-based dynamic surface reconstruction methods are limited to single objects or small deformations, producing surface jitter and temporally inconsistent geometry when scenes undergo large deformations over long sequences. No prior-free, generic method exists that can handle multiple (non-)rigid shapes with large deformations while maintaining temporal surface consistency from sparse-view inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263060/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse-view RGB video (fewer than 10 views per timestep) as input, with depth available from RGB-D cameras on CMU Panoptic (10 cameras, 1920×1080, 24 timesteps) and RGB-only on Hi4D (8 cameras, 940×1280, ~118 timesteps). It produces a temporally consistent 3D mesh sequence extracted via TSDF volume fusion from all training views, with the virtual-timestep mesh discarded during extraction to avoid duplication. The initial point cloud is obtained from a visual hull constructed from all-view foreground masks at the first timestep.

## Method

4DSurf builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting and introduces a Gaussian Velocity Field (GVF) that predicts per-Gaussian linear velocity, angular velocity, and expansion velocity from a canonical space to any timestep, enabling differentiable rendering at arbitrary times. The key regularization matches two estimates of SDF flow: one derived analytically from the Gaussian motion (negative projection of scene flow onto the surface normal) and another computed from temporal changes in rendered depth at Gaussian centers, enforcing that the surface evolves consistently with the underlying motion. To handle large deformations over long sequences, an Overlapping Segment Partitioning strategy divides the video into segments of K+1 timesteps sharing a virtual timestep, passing geometric initialization forward while allowing new Gaussians in previously unseen regions. An Incremental Motion Tuning (IMT) variant uses LoRA to fine-tune the GVF from the previous segment rather than training from scratch, reducing storage while maintaining competitive accuracy. The total loss combines photometric (L1 + D-SSIM), normal alignment, depth distortion, SDF flow, and mask terms.

## Results

On the Hi4D dataset (6 multi-human scenes, ~118 timesteps each), 4DSurf without IMT achieves an average Chamfer Distance Overall of 0.65 cm, outperforming the second-best method (Dynamic-2DGS, 1.72 cm) by 49%. On CMU Panoptic (4 scenes, 24 timesteps), 4DSurf achieves an average Overall of 11.6 mm versus 14.6 mm for the best baseline (Dynamic-2DGS), a 19% improvement. In terms of temporal stability, 4DSurf reduces the standard deviation of Chamfer Distance Overall to 0.18 cm on Hi4D, compared to 0.68 cm for Sparse2DGS and 1.19 cm for Dynamic-2DGS, demonstrating substantially fewer surface jitters over time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4DSurf outperforms all 10 baselines on both datasets across every scene. The closest competitor is Dynamic-2DGS [55], which 4DSurf beats by 49% (Hi4D Overall) and 19% (CMU Panoptic Overall); Dynamic-2DGS also shows the worst temporal stability (STD 1.19) due to accumulated geometric errors over long sequences. Among novel-view-synthesis methods, FreeTimeGS [43] and SC-GS [10] produce visually plausible renders but yield significantly coarser and jittering surfaces (Hi4D Overall 2.71 and 2.41 on average vs. 0.65 for 4DSurf). Sparse2DGS [46], a strong static surface method applied per-timestep, achieves the best temporal stability among baselines (STD 0.68) but still lags 4DSurf by 3.8× in Overall CD on Hi4D. GauSTAR [56] relies on dense-view optical flow and depth priors and shows high Completeness variance (STD 4.39), while Neural SDF-Flow [26] and NDR [2] (NeRF-based) are substantially worse on CMU Panoptic (Overall 15.6 and 19.8 average vs. 11.6 for 4DSurf). Space-Time-2DGS [42] and Tensor4D [35] are competitive on CMU Panoptic but do not scale to the longer, larger-deformation Hi4D sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) as its geometric primitive and on the SDF flow formulation from Neural SDF-Flow to characterize temporal surface evolution. It departs from prior GS-based dynamic surface reconstruction methods (Dynamic-2DGS, GauSTAR, ST-2DGS) that are confined to single objects or small deformations and often rely on external priors (SMPL-X, optical flow, depth), by introducing a prior-free Gaussian Velocity Field that predicts per-Gaussian velocity, angular velocity, and expansion velocity rather than absolute deformation. The Overlapping Segment Partitioning strategy and LoRA-based Incremental Motion Tuning are novel engineering contributions that address the scalability bottleneck of long-sequence reconstruction, a problem that prior incremental GS methods handle only by reusing Gaussians without explicit geometric consistency constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing GS-based dynamic surface reconstruction methods degrade significantly under large deformations and multi-object scenes, which are common in real-world applications like digital avatars and VR. The SDF flow regularization provides a principled, differentiable mechanism to enforce temporal consistency without requiring dense views or object-specific priors, and the segment partitioning with LoRA makes the approach scalable to sequences of ~100+ timesteps. Open questions include whether the method extends to truly single-view or monocular settings, how it behaves on scenes with topological changes (e.g., objects entering/leaving the frame), and whether the multi-component pipeline (visual hull initialization, segment training, TSDF extraction) can be simplified or accelerated for near-real-time use.
