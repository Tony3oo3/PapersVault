## Problem

Masked visual modeling for video representation learning typically relies on a single fixed mask ratio, which may fail to capture the full spectrum of visual semantics from low-level details (edges, textures) to high-level patterns (object interactions, actions). Additionally, semantically difficult regions receive insufficient learning focus, and shallow encoder layers lack guidance from deeper semantic features, leading to inconsistent representations across network depth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263109/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16-frame video clips where each frame is resized to a 256-pixel shorter side and center-cropped to 224×224, then tokenized with cube masking. Pre-training uses two frozen teachers (an image teacher from ImageNet-1K and a video teacher from K400) alongside progressive student models at mask ratios of 75%→80%→85%→90%. The final output is a video representation vector that, after appending a single FC classifier, yields action class predictions for downstream recognition on SSv2, K400, UCF-101, and HMDB-51.

## Method

PMD introduces three complementary modules on top of a masked video distillation base. (1) Progressive Student Distillation trains four sequential student models with increasing mask ratios (75%→90%, 100 epochs each); each late-phase student is initialized from the prior phase and additionally distilled from it as an auxiliary teacher, enabling easy-to-hard semantic transfer. (2) Difficulty-Aware Region Enhancement tracks per-patch reconstruction losses across epochs via exponential moving average, groups 32 epochs into stages, and learns softmax-normalized region weights that reweight the loss to prioritize high-loss (difficult) regions. (3) Cross-Layer Feature Alignment divides the ViT encoder into shallow/middle/deep layer groups and aligns student shallow features with teacher middle features and student middle features with teacher deep features via per-layer FC projections, bridging the semantic gap across depths. The total training loss combines the base distillation loss, progressive distillation loss, difficulty-aware region loss, and cross-layer alignment loss with fixed hyperparameters (λ_base=1, λ_pg=0.05, λ_df=1, λ_al=1).

## Results

On Something-Something V2 (SSv2), PMD achieves 73.7% Top-1 with ViT-B (400 epochs), outperforming the closest baselines MVD (72.5%) and AMD (73.3%) by 1.2% and 0.4% respectively. On Kinetics-400 (K400), PMD reaches 83.3% Top-1 with ViT-B, surpassing SMILE (83.1%) and MVD (82.7%). For feature transferability, PMD attains 97.6% Top-1 on UCF101 and 78.6% on HMDB51, exceeding MVD (97.0% and 76.4%) and SMILE (96.4% and 69.5%). With ViT-L, PMD achieves 76.4% on SSv2 and 86.3% on K400, setting new state-of-the-art in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMD consistently outperforms all compared baselines across backbone sizes on both SSv2 and K400. On SSv2 with ViT-B, PMD (73.7%) beats the strongest distillation-based competitor AMD (73.3%) by 0.4% and the base masked distillation method MVD (72.5%) by 1.2%, while matching the FLOPs of both. On K400 with ViT-B, PMD (83.3%) surpasses SMILE (83.1%, which uses 200 GFLOPs and 94M params) and MVD (82.7%) with fewer parameters (87M) and lower FLOPs (180 vs. 200 GFLOPs). For transfer to UCF101 and HMDB51, PMD with dual teachers (ImageNet-1K + K400) achieves 97.6% and 78.6% respectively, exceeding MVD by 0.6% and 2.2%, and substantially outperforming SMILE (96.4% and 69.5%) on HMDB51. Notably, PMD achieves these gains with only 400 pre-training epochs, whereas several baselines (e.g., VideoMAE V1, MGMAE) require 1600–2400 epochs, and the FLOPs overhead over MVD is only ~14% (133,200G vs. 116,480G) per Table 9.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Masked Video Distillation (MVD) [34], which pairs an image teacher (pretrained on ImageNet-1K) and a video teacher (pretrained on K400) to guide a single masked student at a fixed high mask ratio. It departs from this single-ratio paradigm by introducing a progressive student distillation schedule where earlier-phase students (lower mask ratios, e.g., 75%) serve as auxiliary teachers for later-phase students (higher mask ratios, e.g., 90%), echoing the 'easy-to-hard' curriculum idea from progressive distillation in NLP but applied to the mask-ratio axis of masked video modeling. The difficulty-aware region enhancement and cross-layer feature alignment modules draw on standard reweighting and dense-distillation techniques, respectively, but are specifically tailored to the multi-phase, multi-teacher setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive mask-ratio strategy offers a principled way to address the well-known tension in masked autoencoding between low-level detail recovery (favored by low mask ratios) and high-level semantic abstraction (favored by high mask ratios), and the consistent gains across SSv2, K400, UCF-101, and HMDB-51 suggest the learned representations transfer well. However, the absolute improvements over the strong MVD baseline are modest (0.2–0.6% Top-1), and the full model incurs roughly 14% additional FLOPs and ~11% more GPU hours, raising the question of whether the complexity is justified at scale. Open questions include whether the progressive schedule generalizes beyond four phases, whether the region-enhancement mechanism transfers to non-video or non-masked settings, and whether the approach remains beneficial when the base model is already very large (e.g., ViT-H).
