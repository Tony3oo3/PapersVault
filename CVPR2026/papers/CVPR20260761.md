## Problem

Existing rotation-invariant (RI) point cloud representations suffer from two structural ambiguities: local point-pair descriptors (e.g., PPF) produce identical features for geometrically distinct but symmetric arrangements, and local reference frames discard global pose information, making it impossible to distinguish locally similar but globally distinct structures. No prior method simultaneously resolves both the local encoding ambiguity and the lack of global context in a principled, learnable framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260761/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point cloud coordinates (1,024 points for classification, 2,048 for segmentation) with optional surface normals, and produces class labels, per-point part labels, or dense rotation-invariant point features. A key design detail is that the input is first lifted into a 4D rotation-equivariant space via a learnable steerable transform before any pairwise feature computation, and a Bingham-distribution-sampled global rotation is applied to generate anchor points that augment each local descriptor, yielding an 8-dimensional Ga4DPF feature per point pair. Evaluation follows three rotation protocols (z/z, z/SO(3), SO(3)/SO(3)) to stress-test invariance.

## Method

Ga4DPF bridges rotation equivariance and invariance through two coupled mechanisms. First, a learnable steerable transform built on 3D spherical neurons lifts each 3D point into a 4D rotation-equivariant representation; 4D Point Pair Features (4DPPF) computed in this lifted space provably disambiguate symmetric point pairs that are indistinguishable in 3D (Theorem 1). Second, a Bingham distribution over the unit quaternion hypersphere models uncertainty in a global rotation R_g, whose geometric-mean estimate produces anchor points that inject global pose context into each local neighborhood, breaking the local equivariance that causes symmetric confusion. The full Ga4DPF descriptor concatenates the local 4DPPF with a global-aware 4DPPF (computed against the anchor), and the Bingham parameters are jointly optimized with the task loss via a regularized objective. The framework is instantiated within PaRI-Conv using DGCNN (classification) or AdaptConv (segmentation) backbones, trained end-to-end with SGD and cosine annealing.

## Results

On ModelNet40, Ga4DPF achieves 91.9% classification accuracy using only point coordinates (pc) and 92.8% with normals (pc+n), maintaining identical accuracy across all three rotation protocols (z/z, z/SO(3), SO(3)/SO(3)). On the real-world ScanObjectNN OBJ_BG benchmark, it reaches 87.4% (z/SO(3)) and 87.3% (SO(3)/SO(3)), outperforming the previous best by 2.4–2.8%. For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, it attains 82.2% class mIoU and 85.1% instance mIoU (pc+n, z/SO(3)), while using only 2.25M parameters and 3841M FLOPs—roughly half the FLOPs of LocoTrans and a third of RISurConv.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ModelNet40 with coordinates only, Ga4DPF (91.9%) surpasses ELGANet (90.3%), PaRot (90.9%), and TetraSphere (90.5%) by 1.0–1.6%, and also beats the normal-dependent RI-GCN (91.0%) and OrientedMP (88.4%). On ScanObjectNN OBJ_BG, it outperforms LocoTrans by 2.4% (87.4% vs. 85.0%) and PaRI-Conv by 4.1% (87.4% vs. 83.3%), demonstrating stronger robustness to real-world noise and occlusion. For ShapeNetPart segmentation (pc only, z/SO(3)), it edges out LocoTrans by 1.2% in class mIoU (81.3% vs. 80.1%) and CRIN by 0.8% (81.3% vs. 80.5%); with normals, it surpasses RISurConv by 0.7% (82.2% vs. 81.5%). In terms of efficiency, Ga4DPF uses 2.25M parameters and 3841M FLOPs, roughly half the FLOPs of LocoTrans (7998M) and a third of RISurConv (12120M), while matching or exceeding their accuracy. Rotation-sensitive baselines (PointNet, PointNet++, DGCNN) collapse under arbitrary rotations (e.g., PointNet++ drops from 89.3% to 28.6% on z/SO(3)), underscoring the necessity of the RI formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PaRI-Conv (Chen & Cong, CVPR 2022), whose pose-aware convolution framework it extends by replacing handcrafted local reference frame descriptors with a learnable 4D lifting via steerable 3D spherical neurons (Melnyk et al., ICML 2022). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant RI paradigm of rigid LRF-based point-pair encoding by adopting an equivariance-to-invariance pipeline: first constructing rotation-equivariant features in a lifted 4D space, then deriving invariant descriptors from them. The introduction of a Bingham-distribution-based dynamic global rotation as a per-point anchor is a novel mechanism not present in prior RI or equivariant methods, which either discard global pose entirely or rely on fixed canonical alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized but persistent limitation in RI point cloud analysis—structural ambiguity in symmetric and repetitive geometries—by providing both a theoretical disambiguation guarantee (Theorem 1) and a practical global pose mechanism. The combination of 4D local lifting with Bingham-distribution global anchoring offers a principled, compact (2.25M params, 3.8 GFLOPs) alternative to heavier transformer-based RI methods. Open questions include whether the Bingham-based global rotation generalizes to multi-object scenes or partial observations, whether the 4D lifting can be extended beyond the tetrahedral basis, and how the approach scales to large-scale outdoor point clouds where the k-NN graph construction becomes a bottleneck.
