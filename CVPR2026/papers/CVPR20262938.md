## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on 3D reconstruction backpropagate gradients through the entire pipeline as a monolith, failing to identify which specific module is the true geometric bottleneck. This paper argues that the Structure-from-Motion (SfM) initialization stage—responsible for keypoint detection, feature matching, and pose estimation—is the intrinsic vulnerability whose corruption naturally propagates to all downstream reconstruction systems (MVS, NeRF, 3DGS), enabling highly transferable poisoning without overfitting to any particular architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N multi-view RGB images and an ℓ∞ perturbation budget ε (16/255 in experiments), poisoning only a subset of views (ratio r=0.6) while leaving the rest as clean references. The output is a set of visually imperceptible poisoned images (PSNR 27.8 dB, SSIM 0.86 vs. clean) that, when fed into any off-the-shelf SfM pipeline such as COLMAP, cause registered cameras to drop by over 80% and sparse 3D points to collapse by ~85%, ultimately producing severely degraded novel-view renderings across all tested reconstruction backends.

## Method

PoInit-of-View introduces a Cross-View Gradient Inconsistency (CVI) loss that maximizes the L1 difference between Sobel gradients at projections of corresponding 3D points across a reference view and a poisoned view, deliberately violating the local gradient consistency that SIFT descriptors and epipolar constraint verification rely upon. Because COLMAP is non-differentiable, a differentiable 3DGS proxy is trained to approximate cross-view rendering behavior and provide tractable gradients; the proxy is refreshed every K=10 iterations to track evolving visibility. Perturbations are optimized via projected gradient ascent (PGD) with SSIM and total-variation regularizers for imperceptibility, applied only to the poisoned-view subset. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) proves that once CVI exceeds a threshold L_th = τ_g + τ_d/β_r, the inlier matching probability decays exponentially, causing critical pose-graph edges to fail and the global SfM optimization to become underconstrained.

## Results

On the Tanks and Temples dataset with Mip-Splatting, PoInit-of-View degrades PSNR from 23.93 (clean) to 17.63 and SSIM from 0.833 to 0.684 under a perturbation budget of ρ=16/255, surpassing the single-view baseline by 25.1% in PSNR and 16.5% in SSIM. Across three reconstruction pipelines (COLMAP/MVS, Instant NGP/NeRF, Mip-Splatting/3DGS), the attack causes an average PSNR drop of ~25%, SSIM drop of 15–18%, and LPIPS nearly doubling. SfM stability metrics show registered views collapsing from ~93–99% to ~24–29%, triangulated keypoints dropping by ~80%, and sparse 3D points falling by over 85% across all three datasets (NeRF Synthetic, Mip-NeRF360, T&T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PoInit-of-View against two baselines: Gaussian noise injection and single-view adversarial methods (NeRFool [23] and IL2-NeRF [28]). PoInit-of-View substantially outperforms both: it reduces PSNR to 17.63 versus 23.53 for the single-view baseline (a 25.1% relative drop) and 23.43 for Gaussian noise (a 24.6% relative drop). In SSIM, the cross-view method achieves 0.684 versus 0.819 (single-view) and 0.821 (noise), representing a 16.5% and 16.7% relative degradation respectively. The single-view baselines, which backpropagate through the full reconstruction pipeline without targeting cross-view geometric consistency, fail to disrupt SfM initialization and thus produce only marginal quality loss. The key advantage of PoInit-of-View is its transferability: perturbations optimized with a 3DGS proxy transfer effectively to NeRF (Instant NGP) and MVS (COLMAP) pipelines, whereas single-view attacks are inherently tied to a specific reconstruction architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior adversarial attacks on 3D reconstruction (NeRFool, IL2-NeRF, Poison-Splat, GaussTrap) that backpropagate through the full rendering/optimization pipeline or target single-view photometric losses. Instead, it identifies the Structure-from-Motion (SfM) initialization as the geometric bottleneck and introduces a cross-view gradient inconsistency loss that exploits the multi-view correspondence assumptions underlying keypoint matching and pose estimation. The method repurposes differentiable 3DGS rendering as a gradient proxy for the non-differentiable COLMAP pipeline, and provides a theoretical analysis (Lipschitz bounds on descriptors, sub-exponential matching probability) that prior single-view attacks lack. Conceptually, it shifts the adversarial target from the neural rendering stage to the classical multi-view geometry stage that precedes it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that imperceptible cross-view perturbations can collapse SfM initialization—and thereby transfer across fundamentally different downstream systems (MVS, NeRF, 3DGS)—exposes a shared geometric vulnerability that no single-system defense can address. This has direct implications for safety-critical deployments (autonomous driving, surgical robotics) where SfM-based initialization is ubiquitous. Open questions include: whether robust feature matching or adversarial regularization in pose optimization can mitigate the attack, how transferability holds across heterogeneous SfM implementations (e.g., SuperGlue vs. SIFT), and whether the theoretical threshold L_th can be turned into a practical detection mechanism. A limitation is that the attack still requires a differentiable proxy for gradient computation, placing it in a grey-box regime rather than fully black-box.
