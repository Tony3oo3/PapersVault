## Problem

Existing MLLM-based VLN frameworks process sequences of RGB frames as flat patch tokens, producing t×H_p×W_p visual tokens with no explicit 3D spatial structure. This causes substantial computational overhead and weak multi-view geometric consistency, limiting spatial reasoning and navigation efficiency in continuous environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263385/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 60°-FOV front-view RGB-D image at each step plus up to 8 historical frames, a natural language instruction, and its current pose (position and rotation). Depth maps are bicubic-interpolated to patch resolution before back-projection. The MLLM outputs four discrete actions (turn left, turn right, move forward, stop) per dialogue round in a two-round format, with the BEV representation refreshed only once every eight actions, compressing ~4003 visual tokens down to ~514.

## Method

GA-VLN constructs a Geometry-Aware BEV (GA-BEV) representation by fusing two complementary geometry sources: (1) explicit depth-guided projection, where SigLIP patch features are back-projected into 3D via the pinhole camera model using per-patch depth values and transformed into the agent-centric frame, and (2) implicit 3D geometry priors from a frozen VGGT-1B foundation model whose penultimate-layer features are projected into the same 3D space through a 2-layer MLP. All 3D-grounded features are then aggregated via grid-based BEV pooling over a 0.25 m × 0.25 m grid spanning [−10 m, 10 m], retaining only non-empty cells with sinusoidal position embeddings. The resulting compact BEV tokens, together with the current front-view image and instruction, are fed into LLaVA-Video-7B in a two-round dialogue that predicts four actions per round. Training uses supervised fine-tuning on curated navigation data (R2R-CE, RxR-CE, EnvDrop, ScaleVLN, SRDF) for two epochs with VGGT frozen, requiring no DAgger augmentation or VQA co-training.

## Results

On R2R-CE val_unseen, GA-VLN achieves SR=61.0%, SPL=55.2%, OSR=67.6%, and NE=4.80 m, setting new state-of-the-art among all compared methods. On RxR-CE, it reaches SR=55.4% and SPL=45.2%, surpassing the previous best image-based MLLM (InternVLA-N1: SR=53.5%, SPL=46.1%). On NavRAG-CE, GA-VLN attains the best NE (7.88 m) and OSR (46.4%), though Dynam3D slightly edges it on SR (24.7% vs 22.2%) and SPL (18.8% vs 18.2%). Notably, GA-VLN achieves these results without DAgger augmentation or VQA co-training, while most competitors rely on both.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GA-VLN outperforms all image-based MLLM baselines on R2R-CE, improving SR by 2.8 points over InternVLA-N1 (61.0% vs 58.2%) and by 4.1 points over StreamVLN (61.0% vs 56.9%), while also achieving the best SPL (55.2 vs 54.0). On RxR-CE, GA-VLN surpasses InternVLA-N1 in SR (55.4% vs 53.5%) and NE (5.88 vs 5.91 m), though InternVLA-N1 retains a slight SPL edge (46.1 vs 45.2). Against 3D end-to-end methods, GA-VLN dominates on R2R-CE (SR 61.0% vs Dynam3D's 52.9%) and matches or exceeds them on NavRAG-CE in NE and OSR, though Dynam3D leads by ~2.5 points in SR (24.7% vs 22.2%). A critical distinction is that GA-VLN achieves these results without DAgger augmentation (marked ✗), whereas nearly all competing MLLM and 3D E2E methods rely on it (marked ✓), underscoring the data efficiency of the GA-BEV representation. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that the BEV projection alone reduces MLLM inference TFLOPs from 32.19 to 5.15 and latency from 342.9 ms to 212.9 ms, with the full GA-BEV configuration adding only ~3.6 ms of overhead from VGGT while delivering the best navigation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) MLLM-based VLN frameworks (StreamVLN, NaVILA, NaVid) that process dense RGB video as flat patch tokens, and (2) BEV-map-based VLN methods (BEVBERT, GridMM) that construct top-down spatial representations but were limited to discrete environments or served only as auxiliary structures. The key departure is the first successful integration of an MLLM policy backbone with a spatially-grounded BEV representation as the primary visual input, further enriched by implicit geometry priors from a pretrained 3D foundation model (VGGT). The two-round dialogue format is directly adopted from StreamVLN, while the depth-guided projection and grid aggregation extend earlier BEV ideas from discrete VLN into the continuous setting with MLLM compatibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: an 87% reduction in visual tokens (4003→514) while simultaneously achieving SOTA across R2R-CE, RxR-CE, and NavRAG-CE without DAgger augmentation or VQA co-training, making the approach substantially more data- and compute-efficient for practitioners. However, the ablation reveals that the 3D foundation model component (VGGT) contributes only ~1.75% SR over depth-only BEV projection while adding non-trivial overhead (1.97 TFLOPs, 120 extra tokens), raising the question of whether the added complexity is justified. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger outdoor environments, whether the frozen VGGT features remain beneficial when the MLLM is fine-tuned for longer, and how the method performs under more aggressive sensor degradation than the mild noise tested here.
