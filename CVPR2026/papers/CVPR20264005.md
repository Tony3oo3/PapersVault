## Problem

Existing egocentric video QA benchmarks are overwhelmingly visual-centric, treating audio as secondary or ignoring it entirely, despite sound providing indispensable cues about spatial layout, off-screen events, and causal interactions in first-person settings. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates how well MLLMs perceive and reason about sound from an egocentric perspective, leaving a critical gap in multisensory egocentric intelligence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264005/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes egocentric video clips (5 s to 5 min, average 59 s) with synchronized audio tracks and a natural-language question as input, and requires the model to produce a free-form descriptive answer (open-ended, not multiple-choice). The 7,315 QA pairs are drawn from 900 videos sourced from Ego4D (260 clips) and EgoBlind (640 clips), spanning seven task categories. Evaluation uses GPT-5 as an automatic judge scoring factual consistency on two metrics: binary Accuracy (0–100%) and a 0–5 semantic Score, with a 350-pair human verification subset confirming 92.1% pipeline accuracy.

## Method

EgoSound is constructed via a three-stage auto-generative pipeline: (1) Qwen2.5-VL annotates temporally-grounded human-object and human-human interaction events in each clip; (2) Gemini-2.5 generates sound-centric audio-visual captions conditioned on those interaction labels, linking each sound to its source, acoustic traits, spatial position, duration, and causal context while transcribing all speech; (3) GPT-4o constructs open-ended QA pairs across seven tasks (Sound Characteristics, Counting, Temporal Attribute, Spatial Location, Sound Source Identification, Inferential Causality, Cross-Modal Reasoning), with every answer cross-verified against video frames to suppress hallucination. The resulting benchmark is then used to evaluate nine state-of-the-art MLLMs (ranging from 3B to 72B parameters), and an audio-only ablation on the best model (Qwen3-Omni-Thinking-30B) isolates the contribution of visual input, showing that sound-dependent tasks remain stable while joint audio-visual tasks degrade by over 20 points without vision.

## Results

On EgoSound, the best-performing model Qwen3-Omni-Thinking-30B achieves 56.7% average accuracy (score 3.0/5), while human evaluators reach 83.9% (score 3.9/5), leaving a gap of over 27 percentage points. The weakest model, VideoLLaMA2.1-AV-7B, scores only 20.5% accuracy. Performance varies substantially across task categories: Causality is the easiest for models (up to 73.3% for Qwen3-Omni-Thinking-30B), while Sound Characteristics and Cross-Modal Reasoning remain the hardest (55.0% and 54.5% respectively for the best model). An audio-only ablation on Qwen3-Omni-Thinking-30B shows sound-dependent tasks (Characteristics, Counting, Temporal) drop only ~6 points, whereas audio-visual tasks (Location, Identification, Causality, Cross-Modal) degrade by 20–28 points without visual input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen3-Omni-Thinking-30B leads all baselines with 56.7% average accuracy, outperforming Qwen3-Omni-Instruct-30B (51.9%) by 4.8 points and video-SALMONN2+-72B (46.6%) by 10.1 points. Scaling within a model family consistently helps: video-SALMONN2+-72B gains 10.6 points over its 7B version, and Qwen2.5-Omni-7B gains 5.9 points over the 3B variant. However, scale alone is insufficient—video-SALMONN2+-72B (46.6%) still trails the smaller Qwen3-Omni-30B models (51.9–56.7%), and VideoLLaMA2.1-AV-7B (20.5%) lags far behind all other 7B models. Notably, EgoGPT-7B, despite being specifically pretrained on egocentric data, achieves only 34.3% accuracy, below even Qwen2.5-Omni-3B (33.9%) and far below the best models, suggesting egocentric pretraining alone does not address sound-understanding limitations. The largest single-task gap is in Causality, where Qwen3-Omni-Thinking-30B reaches 73.3% versus 16.5% for VideoLLaMA2.1-AV-7B, a 56.8-point difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established but previously disjoint lines of work: egocentric VideoQA (EgoVQA, EgoSchema, EgoThink, AMEGO, EgoTempo, EgoCross) and audio(-visual) QA (SpatialSoundQA, SAVVY, Magnet). Prior egocentric benchmarks are explicitly vision-centric with no sound questions, while prior audio QA work operates in third-person or non-egocentric settings. EgoSound departs from both by constructing the first benchmark that requires sound-centric reasoning from a first-person perspective, sourcing data from both sighted (Ego4D) and blind (EgoBlind) egocentric recordings. The data curation pipeline repurposes existing generative models (Qwen2.5-VL for interaction annotation, Gemini-2.5 for audio-visual captioning, GPT-4o for QA construction) in a multi-stage pipeline rather than introducing new model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoSound fills a genuine gap in the MLLM evaluation landscape by providing the first systematic testbed for egocentric sound understanding, a dimension that prior benchmarks entirely overlooked. The 27-point gap between human accuracy (83.9%) and the best model (56.7%) quantifies how far current omni-models are from multisensory parity, and the finding that egocentric pretraining (EgoGPT) does not improve sound understanding challenges the assumption that domain-specific adaptation alone suffices. Open questions include whether larger or better-trained omni-models will close the gap, how to design modality-balanced training that treats audio as a first-class signal rather than an afterthought, and whether the benchmark's reliance on GPT-5 for automatic evaluation introduces systematic biases. A limitation is that only one egocentric-specialized model (EgoGPT) was available for comparison, weakening the conclusion about egocentric pretraining.
