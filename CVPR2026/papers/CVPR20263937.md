## Problem

Image clustering methods are fragmented across scenarios: approaches tuned for general object categorization fail at fine-grained distinctions, balanced-distribution methods struggle with long-tail data, and single-criteria techniques cannot handle composite guidelines. No existing framework unifies these diverse clustering requirements under a single, training-free system driven by natural language specifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263937/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts a set of RGB images plus a free-form textual guideline specifying one or more grouping attributes (e.g., 'group by brand and material'). When no explicit guideline is available, heuristic prompts elicit one from the LLM. Output is a partition of images into clusters; when the cluster count is unknown, the system auto-discovers it via HDBSCAN followed by LLM-guided merging, and the ABO-LC benchmark contains 10,756 products across 4,952 ground-truth clusters with 78.7% being singletons or pairs.

## Method

The method is a training-free two-stage hybrid. Stage 1 (Generative Concept Proxy Modeling) uses a 7B MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) to generate guideline-aware concept proxy captions that explicitly surface the attributes named in the guideline, then encodes those captions with an instruction-aware embedder (INSTRUCTOR, E5-Mistral, or GME-Qwen2-VL) to produce embeddings suitable for standard clustering. Stage 2 (MST-based LLM Traversal) addresses the limitation that HDBSCAN produces many small, unmerged clusters: it computes Ward distances between all HDBSCAN clusters, builds a Minimum Spanning Tree, and traverses edges in ascending-distance order, querying the LLM only for the most promising merge candidates. This reduces LLM invocations from O(M²) to O(M log M) while caching prior decisions to avoid redundant queries, and the entire pipeline requires no task-specific training.

## Results

On general clustering, GCPM-G with K-Means achieves 98.8% ACC on ImageNet-10 and 98.8% on STL-10, surpassing the best training-based baseline IDCTCL (97.2% and 92.7% respectively). On fine-grained clustering, GCPM-G reaches 72.9% ACC on CUB Birds and 86.0% on Oxford Flowers, dramatically outperforming the previous best DiFiC (31.7% and 72.9%). On the proposed long-tail ABO-LC dataset, GCPM-E with MST Traversal achieves 51.5% ARI versus 5.3% for the IC|TC baseline. The framework is entirely training-free, using only Qwen2.5-VL-7B for captioning and LLM traversal with instruction-aware embedders for clustering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 39 baselines spanning general, multiple, fine-grained, and long-tail clustering. On general clustering, GCPM-G (K-Means) outperforms all training-based methods: it beats IDCTCL by 1.6% ACC on ImageNet-10 (98.8 vs. 97.2) and by 6.1% on STL-10 (98.8 vs. 92.7), and surpasses SeCu by 1.1% on CIFAR-10 (94.1 vs. 93.0). On multiple clustering, GCPM-G achieves 99.9% NMI on Fruit (vs. Multi-Sub 98.5%) and 56.3% average NMI on Cards (vs. Multi-Sub 35.1%), a 21-point margin. On fine-grained clustering, GCPM-G dominates: 72.9% ACC on CUB Birds vs. DiFiC's 31.7%, 75.1% on Stanford Dogs vs. DiFiC's 17.2%, and 86.0% on Oxford Flowers vs. DiFiC's 72.9%. On the long-tail ABO-LC task, GCPM-E with MST Traversal achieves 51.5% ARI versus IC|TC's 5.3%, a nearly 10× improvement. The only notable exception is the Card dataset where GCPM-E (91.1% NMI for number) outperforms GCPM-G (82.0%), attributed to visual entanglement of card numbers and suits that the text-only proxy approach handles better.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing existing components—multimodal LLMs for captioning, instruction-aware text/multimodal embedders, HDBSCAN, and minimum spanning trees—into a unified training-free pipeline that no prior work has assembled. It departs from deep clustering methods (IIC, SCAN, SeCu, DiFiC) that require dataset-specific training, from text-guided approaches (IC|TC, Multi-Sub, Multi-MaP) limited to single concrete criteria, and from LLM-based clustering (ClusterLLM) that incurs O(M²) inference cost. The key conceptual departure is the Generative Concept Proxy Modeling step, which inserts an MLLM captioning stage between the image and the embedding model to achieve attribute disentanglement, and the MST-based traversal that converts an exhaustive pairwise LLM comparison problem into an O(M log M) selective one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single training-free pipeline replaces the need for task-specific trained models across general, fine-grained, multiple, and long-tail clustering, with guidelines specified in natural language. The introduced ABO-LC benchmark (10,756 products, 4,952 clusters, 78.7% singletons) addresses a realistic e-commerce scenario previously lacking a standard evaluation set. Open questions include scalability to very large collections (the MST traversal still requires ~1 LLM call per sample), the modest gains in fine-grained tasks where conservative LLM merging limits improvement, and whether the framework degrades gracefully when guidelines are ambiguous or contradictory. The reliance on a 7B MLLM for both captioning and merging also raises cost considerations for production deployment.
