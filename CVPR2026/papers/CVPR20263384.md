## Problem

Reasoning LMMs generate Chain-of-Thought sequences whose length is unknown a priori, causing memory fragmentation in serving systems and sub-optimal accuracy from over- or under-thinking. No prior method can estimate how long a CoT will be before generation completes, making it impossible to intervene in advance. This paper identifies a hidden 'fuel level' signal within LMM hidden states that monotonically decreases during reasoning, enabling the first runtime CoT length prediction.

## Input / Output

At each CoT generation step, the method consumes 8 consecutive hidden states (an 8×2560 matrix for Qwen3-4B) from a single mid-layer (layer 18 of 36) of the LMM, requiring no access to the full model or logits. It produces a scalar fuel level reading r_i and, via linear extrapolation to the zero-crossing, a running estimate of total CoT length N̂_i that is updated at every token. Downstream, this estimate drives one-shot KV cache pre-allocation to reduce fragmentation, and through normalized gradient updates on the current hidden state, modulates CoT length to target accuracy levels.

## Method

The Fuel Gauge is a compact 82k-parameter network operating in two stages. Stage 1 extracts a hidden signal from the 8-step hidden state window using a 1D depth-wise convolution followed by a 1D point-wise convolution (f_sig), then maps it to a scalar fuel level r_i via a 2-layer MLP (f_fuel). Stage 2 fits a linear model r_i ≈ k·[0…i]ᵀ + 1 over all accumulated fuel readings and extrapolates to the zero-crossing point N̂_i = −1/k as the CoT length estimate. The network is trained with only 200 CoT traces (MMLU for text, MMMU for multimodal) using smooth L1 loss against the normalized token index (1−i/N). The key insight is that the fuel level decreases approximately linearly with generation step, making simple linear extrapolation sufficient for length prediction without needing the full autoregressive sampling process.

## Results

On CoT length prediction (rMAE, lower is better), Fuel Gauge achieves 0.2732 on GPQA-Diamond with Qwen3-8B, less than half the error of the Direct baseline (0.5795) which uses the same network architecture. For fuel level estimation, Fuel Gauge reaches 0.1322 rMAE on GPQA-Diamond (Qwen3-8B) versus 0.4999 for the EoC Prob baseline. In the predictive KV cache allocation application, Fuel Gauge reduces memory allocation frequency by up to 13.37× (39.87 vs. 533.0 allocations on GPQA-Diamond, Qwen3-8B). For CoT length modulation, the Pearson correlation between the modulation factor η and CoT length is 0.9936 (text→text), and between η and accuracy is 0.9722, demonstrating near-linear controllability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fuel Gauge outperforms all baselines across every benchmark and model configuration. Against the Direct baseline (same 82k-parameter network trained to predict CoT length directly), Fuel Gauge achieves less than half the rMAE on GPQA-Diamond (0.2732 vs. 0.5795 for Qwen3-8B) and a similar margin on MathVision-m (0.3139 vs. 0.4965). The static Mean/Median baselines perform poorly due to high variance in CoT lengths, and the EoC Prob baseline (using the end-of-CoT token probability as a fuel proxy) is essentially useless with a near-constant rMAE of 0.4999. For the KV cache allocation application, Fuel Gauge reduces allocation count by 9.97×–13.37× on text/image benchmarks and 1.57×–2.25× on video benchmarks compared to the HuggingFace (HF) default 16-token block allocation strategy. Notably, the Direct baseline fails catastrophically on cross-modality transfer (rMAE > 9 on LongVideoBench), while Fuel Gauge maintains rMAE < 0.51, demonstrating that the two-stage fuel-level-then-extrapolation design generalizes far better than end-to-end length prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying and exploiting an internal 'fuel level' signal in reasoning LMMs to predict CoT length before generation begins — a task the authors explicitly frame as previously infeasible. Conceptually, it repurposes ideas from steering vectors and classifier guidance (originally developed for controlled text generation and diffusion models) into a novel test-time CoT length modulation mechanism, and connects to the KV cache management literature (PagedAttention, Jenga, VTensor) by providing the length estimate those systems lack. The method itself is deliberately minimal — an 82k-parameter depth-wise separable CNN plus a two-layer MLP — trained with supervised regression on 200 CoT traces, departing from the heavier probing or fine-tuning approaches common in interpretability work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a 13.37× reduction in KV cache allocation frequency directly addresses a known bottleneck in LMM serving, and the CoT modulation mechanism offers the first principled way to counter over- and under-thinking at inference time without retraining. However, the evaluation is confined to the Qwen3/Qwen3VL family with only 200 training samples, and baselines (mean, median, a single 'Direct' regression) are relatively weak, leaving open whether the linear fuel-consumption assumption and the extracted signal generalize to other architectures (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, GPT-OSS) or to models without explicit CoT delimiters. A key open question is whether the fuel-level signal persists in non-reasoning or non-RLHF-trained models, and whether the linear extrapolation degrades on tasks with highly variable or multi-modal CoT length distributions.
