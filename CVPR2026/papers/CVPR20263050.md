## Problem

Exemplar-free lifelong person re-identification suffers from semantic drift and catastrophic forgetting because existing methods rely solely on visual-only distillation or parameter regularization, which cannot maintain stable identity semantics as domain distributions shift. No prior approach exploits the domain-invariant nature of textual semantics (e.g., 'a person wearing a red jacket') as a persistent anchor to guide visual representation evolution across sequentially arriving domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263050/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB person images resized to 256×128 (with random flip/erase augmentation) and class-specific textual prompt tokens fed into a frozen CLIP text encoder. At inference, only the partially trained image encoder is retained, producing a single L2-normalized visual embedding per person for gallery retrieval; the text encoder and all prompts are discarded, keeping deployment cost identical to a standard ReID model. The lifelong protocol processes T domains sequentially with no access to prior-domain data, and evaluation covers both seen and unseen test sets.

## Method

PAD introduces an asymmetric vision-text distillation framework built on CLIP ViT-B/16. On the textual side, a frozen text encoder provides a stable semantic coordinate system, while learnable TA-Prompts are optimized via symmetric image-text SupCon loss and a deliberately weak temperature-scaled KL distillation (TEXKD) from a fixed text teacher to prevent long-term semantic drift. On the visual side, a DualPrompt-inspired VA-Prompt pool (6 general + 6 expert tokens per layer, Top-K=4 routing) allocates fresh expert slots per new domain while freezing all previous ones, and an EMA-based teacher (momentum 0.997) enforces strong visual distillation through multi-level feature MSE and logit-level KL divergence. Only the last few transformer blocks and the classification head are unfrozen, yielding a peak of ~13.7M trainable prompt parameters. The overall loss combines SupCon, identity cross-entropy, triplet loss, and weighted text/visual KD terms, with the text branch acting as a global semantic reference and the visual branch providing domain-wise plasticity.

## Results

On AKA-order1, PAD achieves 70.7 mAP / 81.0 R1 on seen domains and 78.6 mAP / 71.4 R1 on unseen domains, outperforming the strongest baseline PAEMA by +8.9 mAP (seen) and +8.3 mAP (unseen). On AKA-order2, PAD reaches 69.3 mAP / 80.0 R1 (seen) and 76.2 mAP / 68.6 R1 (unseen), again leading all methods. Over 7 random seeds, PAD's seen-domain average is 70.30 ± 0.49 mAP and 80.98 ± 0.31 R1, indicating stable performance across runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAD outperforms all 12 baselines on both seen and unseen domain averages under both AKA-order1 and AKA-order2. Against the strongest exemplar-free baseline PAEMA (IJCV'24), PAD gains +8.9 mAP on seen domains (70.7 vs 61.8) and +8.3 mAP on unseen domains (78.6 vs 70.3) on AKA-order1, with similar margins on AKA-order2 (+8.9 mAP seen, +6.8 mAP unseen). The most recent seen-only method DAFC (arXiv'25) reports 65.6 mAP seen but does not evaluate on unseen domains; PAD exceeds it by +5.1 mAP on seen while also reporting 78.6 mAP unseen. Among distribution-aware methods, PAD surpasses DKP++ (PAMI'25) by +5.3 mAP seen and +12.9 mAP unseen, and DASK (AAAI'25) by +5.3 mAP seen and +13.3 mAP unseen. Generic continual-learning baselines (LwF, DualPrompt, FCS) trail PAD by 25–30 mAP on seen domains, highlighting the gap between task-agnostic and ReID-specific lifelong approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAD extends CLIP-ReID's learnable prompt tokens into a lifelong setting and borrows the G-Prompt/E-Prompt architecture from DualPrompt for visual adaptation, but departs from prior exemplar-free LReID methods (PAEMA, DKP, DAFC) that rely exclusively on visual distillation or distribution statistics. The key conceptual departure is treating the frozen CLIP text encoder as a persistent, domain-invariant semantic anchor rather than a one-shot alignment tool, and coupling it with an asymmetric distillation scheme where the text side receives only weak regularization while the visual side receives strong EMA-guided distillation. The authors explicitly position PAD as a vision-text formulation of the stability-plasticity trade-off rather than a modular combination of existing prompting and distillation components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a frozen text encoder can serve as a stable cross-domain semantic reference without any replay, PAD offers a privacy-friendly and computationally efficient path for deploying lifelong ReID in real surveillance systems where storing old images is infeasible. The ablation revealing that weak textual distillation outperforms strong textual distillation (Table 4) provides a useful design principle: implicit contrastive anchoring already does most of the work, and over-constraining the text side actively harms plasticity. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the AKA protocol to truly open-world identity streams, whether adaptive distillation weighting could close the remaining gap on harder domains (e.g., MSMT17), and whether the frozen-text-anchor idea transfers to other fine-grained recognition tasks such as vehicle or object ReID.
