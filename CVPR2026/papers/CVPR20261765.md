## Problem

MCQA is the dominant format for evaluating and reinforcement-fine-tuning vision-language models, but its predefined options leak exploitable signals that inflate accuracy by up to 20 percentage points and encourage answer-guessing shortcuts during training. The paper quantifies this fragility—showing that RFT on MCQA data widens the gap between MCQA and open-ended performance—and asks how to systematically convert MCQA into open-ended QA that remains deterministically verifiable for reliable reward signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261765/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an MCQA question (stem, K options, ground-truth label) and an optional image, then produces a rewritten open-ended question paired with a structured, verifiable answer. The output format is category-dependent: numeric answers become value-unit pairs, keyword answers enumerate acceptable synonyms, per-option questions become comma-separated True/False judgment lists, and only genuinely open answers remain free-form. This structured output enables 70–96% of items to be graded by deterministic rules without invoking an LLM judge.

## Method

ReVeL is a three-phase pipeline: (1) a rule-based filter extracts numeric-answer questions, and a lightweight LLM classifier routes the remainder into keyword, open-answer, or per-option-verification categories; (2) category-specific rewriting prompts rephrase each question to preserve semantics while enabling deterministic checking (e.g., converting MCQA options into declarative True/False statements the model must judge); (3) a hybrid verifier applies pattern matching, synonym matching, or structured list comparison for the rule-verifiable majority, reserving an LLM judge only for the small open-answer fraction. For RFT, 20k MCQA examples are rewritten through this pipeline and used as training data for GRPO-based reinforcement learning on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B, where the verifiable OpenQA reward replaces the brittle MCQA exact-match signal. The key design principle is maximizing deterministic verification to reduce cost and variance relative to a pure LLM-judge approach, while the per-option-verification category preserves the discriminative intent of the original MCQA without exposing the option set to the model.

## Results

Training Qwen2.5-VL-7B with ReVeL-rewritten OpenQA data (20K samples via GRPO) achieves 40.4 overall accuracy, outperforming MCQA-based training (36.3) by +4.1 points while improving OpenQA accuracy from 24.7 to 34.0 (+9.3 points) with MCQA accuracy remaining competitive (46.8 vs 47.8). The 3B model shows a similar trend: 34.3 overall with OpenQA(ReVeL) vs 30.1 with MCQA. Across four benchmarks (EMMA, MMMU, MME-RealWorld, MMLU-Pro), rewriting from MCQA to OpenQA reveals up to 20 percentage points of score inflation (e.g., GPT-5 drops 19.8 points on MMMU, from 79.2% to 59.5%), and ReVeL's hybrid judge achieves 98.5% accuracy vs 97.3% for a pure LLM judge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The ReVeL-OpenQA-trained Qwen2.5-VL-7B (40.4 overall) substantially outperforms R1-OneVision-7B (31.3, +9.1) and VL-Rethinker-7B (37.5, +2.9), and moderately beats Mixed-R1-7B (37.2, +3.2), all of which were trained on open-source data recipes with significant MCQA proportions. Compared to the MCQA-trained variant of the same base model, ReVeL-OpenQA training improves OpenQA accuracy by 9.3 points (34.0 vs 24.7) while sacrificing only 1.0 point on MCQA (46.8 vs 47.8), confirming that the OpenQA reward signal is more transferable. Against proprietary models in Table 7, the 7B model's OpenQA scores (e.g., 37.0 on MMMU) remain well below GPT-5 (59.5) and Gemini-2.5 Flash (57.7), but the MCQA-to-OpenQA gap for the ReVeL-trained model (12.1–19.5 points) is consistently smaller than for the base model (18.7–20.0 points), indicating reduced format overfitting. The 3B model follows the same pattern: OpenQA(ReVeL) yields 34.3 overall vs 30.1 for MCQA, with OpenQA accuracy jumping from 19.7 to 28.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on a growing body of work identifying MCQA fragility (option-order sensitivity, NOTA failures, option exploitation by reasoning models) but departs by proposing a systematic rewriting-and-verification framework rather than merely diagnosing the problem. It sits at the intersection of two threads: (1) the shift from MCQA to open-ended evaluation for LLMs/VLMs, which prior work has noted faces LLM-judge cost and variance issues, and (2) GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning for multimodal reasoning, where datasets like Mixed-R1, R1-OneVision, and VL-Rethinker rely heavily on MCQA-format rewards. ReVeL's hybrid rule-based + LLM-judge design is a practical middle ground between the two extremes of pure deterministic matching and pure LLM-as-judge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MCQA benchmarks inflate scores by up to 20 percentage points relative to OpenQA — even for frontier models like GPT-5 — has immediate implications for how the field benchmarks and compares VLMs, and the demonstration that MCQA-based RFT actively degrades open-ended generalization changes how practitioners should design training data for multimodal RL. The ReVeL framework is practically impactful because 70–96% of rewritten items become rule-verifiable, substantially reducing evaluation cost and variance compared to pure LLM judging. Open questions include whether the rewriting generalizes beyond QA to long-form generation or other task formats, whether the approach scales to even larger and more diverse benchmarks, and how to handle the residual 4–30% of items that still require LLM judgment without inheriting known judge biases.
