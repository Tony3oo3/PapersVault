## Problem

Existing earth observation foundation models either rely on sensor-specific encoders that limit generalization to new modalities, or handle only one axis of resolution heterogeneity (spectral, spatial, or temporal) in isolation. No prior model simultaneously provides a modality-agnostic, multi-temporal, and resolution-adjustable framework that lets users control the spatial detail of encoded features at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260918/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RAMEN accepts geospatially aligned multimodal tensors of arbitrary shape (T_m × C_m × H_m × W_m) spanning multispectral optical bands, SAR polarizations, and elevation products, each with its own native GSD and temporal sampling. A user-specified target GSD parameter controls the spatial resolution of the output feature map, enabling explicit trade-offs between detail and compute cost. The model produces a unified D-dimensional token sequence in a shared latent space, which a lightweight UPerNet decoder converts into per-pixel semantic segmentation masks.

## Method

RAMEN is a single shared ViT-Base encoder pretrained via masked autoencoding on a heterogeneous multimodal corpus, where three resolution-aware modules project each modality into a common latent space: (i) a channel-conditioned projector that sinusoidally encodes each band's central wavelength (or learned embeddings for SAR/elevation channels) to produce a per-modality projection matrix; (ii) an adjustable spatial resampler that bilinearly interpolates features to the target GSD and refines them with a mixture of 1×1 convolutions whose weights are conditioned on the log-scaled interpolation ratio σ_m = log(GSD_m / GSD_target); (iii) a Lightweight Temporal Attention Encoder with day-of-acquisition positional encoding to aggregate time-series. During pretraining, a random multimodal sampling strategy draws a dataset, a subset of its modalities, and a target GSD from a dataset-specific range at every iteration, forcing the encoder to learn resolution-consistent, modality-agnostic representations. At inference, the frozen encoder plus a fine-tuned UPerNet decoder yields segmentation masks, with the target GSD freely adjustable to match task requirements.

## Results

On the PANGAEA benchmark (8 semantic segmentation tasks spanning unimodal, multimodal, and multi-temporal EO data), RAMEN (ViT-Base) achieves the best average mIoU of 60.03 and best average rank of 2.63, outperforming the larger TerraMindv1-L (59.10 mIoU, rank 3.75) by ~1 mIoU while using a smaller encoder. RAMEN ranks in the top 2 on 6 out of 8 tasks, with particularly strong gains on AI4SmallFarms (38.78 mIoU vs. ≤30 for all other foundation models) and DynamicEarthNet (39.85 vs. 37.89 for TerraMind-L). The resolution-adjustable design also enables a compute/performance trade-off: on Pastis, a coarser GSD yields 33.26 mIoU at 359 GFLOPs (~80% of peak) with ~7.4× faster inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAMEN (ViT-Base, 60.03 avg mIoU) outperforms the larger TerraMindv1-L (ViT-Large, 59.10) by ~1 mIoU on average and by a wider margin on detail-critical tasks: AI4SmallFarms (38.78 vs. 27.47, +11.31) and DynamicEarthNet (39.85 vs. 37.89, +1.96). However, TerraMind-L retains the edge on MADOS (75.57 vs. 69.72), PASTIS (43.13 vs. 42.29), and CTM-SS (55.04 vs. 53.27), suggesting its larger capacity helps on certain multi-temporal and fine-grained segmentation tasks. Against other Base-size foundation models, RAMEN leads by a clear margin: +5.14 mIoU over CROMA-L (55.72), +5.14 over DOFA (54.89), +5.86 over SpectralGPT (54.17), and +8.29 over SatlasNet (51.74). Among the small S.-S12 SSL baselines (MoCo, DINO, MAE, Data2Vec), RAMEN exceeds the best (MoCo, 53.02) by 7.01 mIoU. The U-Net baseline (57.22) and ViT baseline (53.48) trained from scratch are both surpassed, confirming the benefit of the multimodal self-supervised pretraining. Notably, RAMEN achieves its best average rank (2.63) with a Base-size encoder, whereas TerraMind requires a Large encoder to reach rank 3.75, highlighting the efficiency of the resolution-adjustable design.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAMEN synthesizes and extends several threads in EO foundation model research: the MAE pretraining objective (He et al., 2022), wavelength-conditioned channel encoding from DOFA (Xiong et al., 2024), GSD-based positional encodings from Scale-MAE (Reed et al., 2023), and the Lightweight Temporal Attention Encoder (Sainte Fare Garnot & Landrieu, 2020). The key departure is that prior models address spectral, spatial, or temporal resolution heterogeneity in isolation (e.g., DOFA for spectral, Scale-MAE/FlexiMo for spatial, AnySat/Galileo for temporal), whereas RAMEN is the first to jointly encode all three axes within a single modality-agnostic transformer. Its novel adjustable spatial resampler—conditioning a mixture of 1×1 convolutions on the log-scaled interpolation ratio—goes beyond the fixed patch-size or GSD-encoding strategies of prior work by giving users explicit, continuous control over output resolution at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The resolution-adjustable inference mechanism is practically significant: practitioners can trade spatial precision against computational cost per task (e.g., coarse GSD for rapid wildfire detection, fine GSD for smallholder farm delineation) without retraining, and the paper demonstrates that ~80% of peak performance is achievable at ~7.4× lower compute. RAMEN's ability to outperform the larger TerraMind-L with a ViT-Base encoder suggests that architectural awareness of resolution heterogeneity can substitute for raw model scale. Open questions include whether the quadratic transformer complexity becomes prohibitive at very fine resolutions for large scenes, whether the approach generalizes to truly novel sensor types absent from pretraining, and whether the random multimodal sampling strategy during pretraining can be further optimized for compute efficiency.
