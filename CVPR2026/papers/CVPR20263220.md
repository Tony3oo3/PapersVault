## Problem

Monocular BEV semantic segmentation struggles with two coupled issues: foreground objects are poorly segmented due to occlusion, and layout categories in occluded regions are inaccurately estimated. Existing Cartesian-based methods lack geometric priors along viewing rays, while polar-coordinate methods introduce computational overhead and over-sample near points at the expense of far-range accuracy. No prior approach handles both cross-view transformation and occlusion distortion without incurring inference-time cost from coordinate transformations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes surround-view RGB images (six cameras on nuScenes, multiple cameras on Argoverse) together with camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters, and produces a 200×198 BEV semantic segmentation map at 25 cm/pixel resolution covering 50 m forward and 25 m laterally on each side. The context-aware rasterization mechanism operates exclusively during training; at inference the model outputs a standard Cartesian BEV grid with no additional latency, making it drop-in compatible with downstream planning modules.

## Method

BEV-CAR employs a bifurcation encoder that extracts depth BEV features via the LSS lift-splat pipeline and multi-scale global BEV features via densetransformer (high-resolution maps for distant regions, low-resolution for nearby areas), then fuses them through spatially-aligned concatenation. The key novelty is a context-aware rasterization mechanism that reinterprets the Cartesian BEV grid as a set of discrete rays emanating from the ego-vehicle, rasterized gap-free using the Bresenham line algorithm so that each ray becomes a depth-indexed 1D sequence. During training, a dropout-like random de-duplication step (p_drop = 0.5) removes redundant near-point samples, and a TOP-K raster loss performs online hard-sample mining along each ray to focus on occluded or high-error regions. This raster loss is combined with a standard weighted cross-entropy global loss (λ = 10), and the entire rasterization branch is discarded at inference, contributing zero extra FLOPs or parameters to the forward pass.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, BEV-CAR achieves a mean IoU of 31.5%, surpassing the previous best (OEBEV at 29.3% and TADE at 29.2%) by over 2 points. It excels particularly on layout categories: 70.9% IoU for drivable area, 48.3% for pedestrian crossings, and 49.2% for walkways. On Argoverse, BEV-CAR sets a new SOTA with 29.9% mean IoU, outperforming HFT (29.7%) and FTVR (25.9%), with strong gains in vehicle segmentation (43.1%) and drivable area (81.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEV-CAR outperforms all 11 compared methods on mean IoU for nuScenes, beating the closest competitors OEBEV (29.3%) and TADE (29.2%) by 2.2 and 2.3 points respectively. The largest margins are over earlier Cartesian-based methods: VPN (17.5%), PON (19.1%), and HFT (21.3%), reflecting the advantage of the depth-aware raster mechanism. On layout categories, BEV-CAR leads all baselines across the board (e.g., 48.3% vs. 41.7% for crossing over OEBEV; 49.2% vs. 42.3% for walkway over TADE). For object categories, BEV-CAR wins on trailer (29.1% vs. 28.3% OEBEV), truck (29.7% vs. 28.9% MoBEV), and bus (38.9% vs. 38.5% TADE), but trails MoBEV on car (42.5% vs. 49.3%) and OEBEV on construction vehicles (17.1% vs. 18.4%). On Argoverse, BEV-CAR edges out HFT (29.9% vs. 29.7%) and FTVR (25.9%), with particularly strong gains in bike (12.9% vs. 7.7% HFT) and vehicle (43.1% vs. 38.8% HFT) segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEV-CAR builds on the lift-splat (LSS) framework for implicit depth-based BEV feature extraction and extends it with a multi-scale global branch using DenseTransformer, addressing the known limitation of fixed-depth projection in producing imprecise object boundaries. It departs from polar-coordinate BEV methods (GitNet, TIM, TADE) that suffer from near-point over-sampling and computational overhead by instead sampling polar rays onto a standard Cartesian grid via the Bresenham line algorithm, retaining a deployment-friendly Cartesian output at inference. The context-aware rasterization mechanism with a dedicated ray-wise loss is positioned as a training-only regularizer, distinguishing it from prior approaches that bake coordinate transformations into the architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead design makes the occlusion-aware training signal practically deployable, which is a meaningful advantage for real-time autonomous driving systems where polar-coordinate methods add latency. The plug-and-play demonstration on PYVA and PON suggests the raster loss could be adopted as a general training regularizer across BEV segmentation architectures. Open questions include whether the ray-wise formulation generalizes to multi-camera fusion settings, whether the Bresenham-based ray sampling is optimal compared to learned ray parameterizations, and whether the approach extends to 3D occupancy prediction or end-to-end planning tasks the authors mention as future directions.
