## Problem

High-fidelity animatable head avatar synthesis from casual monocular videos remains challenging because existing methods rely on either a 3D data prior (which struggles to generalize to in-the-wild identities and expressions) or a 2D generative prior (which is computationally expensive and prone to severe identity hallucination). No prior work has effectively coupled both priors to exploit their complementary strengths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263536/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a casual monocular video as input, from which offline FLAME mesh tracking extracts canonical UV texture/geometry maps and per-frame driving signals (expression code, jaw/eye/neck poses, head rotation, translation). By default only 3 sampled frames are used as supervision. The output is an animatable 2D Gaussian Splatting head avatar structured as UV-aligned 2D Gaussian primitives (position offset, color, rotation, scale, opacity per UV coordinate), drivable by arbitrary FLAME signals and including torso/shoulder regions.

## Method

ELITE introduces a feed-forward Mesh2Gaussian Prior Model (MGPM), a U-Net that maps FLAME UV maps and FiLM-conditioned driving signals to UV-aligned 2D Gaussian parameters, trained on NerSembleV2 multi-view captures with photometric, perceptual, and geometry regularization losses. At test time, a two-stage adaptation first fine-tunes MGPM on 3 real video frames (Stage 1), then renders the avatar from novel viewpoints/expressions and passes degraded renderings through a single-step diffusion enhancer (fine-tuned SD-Turbo, inspired by DIFIX) conditioned on clean reference frames to produce identity-preserving synthetic images (Stage 2). The key insight is that 3D avatar renderings ground the diffusion model—avoiding the hallucinations of full denoising from noise—while the generated images provide diverse supervision that helps the 3D prior generalize beyond the limited training set. This rendering-guided single-step enhancement achieves 60× faster image generation than full diffusion denoising while maintaining high identity consistency (CSIM 0.97 vs. 0.50 for CAP4D).

## Results

On the INSTA dataset (self re-enactment, 3 input frames), ELITE achieves the best PSNR (25.22 dB), LPIPS (0.0732), and CSIM (0.7396) among all compared methods, while being slightly behind SplattingAvatar on SSIM (0.8771 vs. 0.8831). ELITE synthesizes avatars in ~20 minutes per identity, compared to 400 minutes for CAP4D (20× faster) and 15 minutes for SplattingAvatar. For the single-step diffusion enhancer, ELITE generates identity-preserving images (CSIM = 0.9725) in 0.3 s/image, versus CAP4D's full-diffusion denoising (CSIM = 0.5037) at 18 s/image—a 60× speedup with substantially better identity fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELITE outperforms all four baselines on PSNR and LPIPS, and achieves the highest CSIM (0.7396), indicating superior identity preservation. Against the overfitting baselines FlashAvatar (PSNR 20.88, CSIM 0.58) and SplattingAvatar (PSNR 24.84, CSIM 0.64), ELITE gains +0.38 dB PSNR and +0.099 CSIM over SplattingAvatar, the strongest overfitting method, while SplattingAvatar retains a marginal SSIM edge (0.8831 vs. 0.8771). Against the 2D generative prior method CAP4D, ELITE improves PSNR by +5.74 dB and CSIM by +0.033, while reducing synthesis time from 400 min to 20 min (20× faster); CAP4D's full-diffusion images also suffer severe identity hallucination (CSIM 0.5037 vs. ELITE's 0.9725). The 3D data prior method SynShot is compared qualitatively (Fig. 8) and found to lack torso modeling and fine-grained expression generalization, though no quantitative numbers are reported for it in the main table.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELITE sits at the intersection of two established lines of avatar synthesis research: 3D data prior methods (Cao et al., HeadGAP, SynShot) that learn generalizable initialization from multi-view capture but struggle with in-the-wild generalization, and 2D generative prior methods (GAF, CAP4D) that use full diffusion denoising from pure noise to synthesize supervision but suffer from slow sampling and severe identity hallucination. The paper's conceptual departure is identifying and exploiting the complementary synergy between these two priors: 3D avatar renderings serve as grounded initialization for a single-step diffusion enhancer (replacing pure noise), while the generated images in turn improve the 3D prior's generalization during test-time adaptation. Technically, the avatar representation builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting (Huang et al.) with UV-aligned primitives, and the enhancer is fine-tuned from SD-Turbo (Sauer et al.), drawing on the DIFIX paradigm of rendering-guided single-step enhancement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60× speedup over 2D generative prior methods (20 min vs. 400 min per identity) combined with substantially better identity preservation (CSIM 0.9725 vs. 0.5037) makes high-fidelity animatable avatars practical for real-world deployment, a significant step toward democratizing avatar creation from casual phone videos. The paper acknowledges limitations in unusual lighting conditions and the absence of accessory modeling (e.g., glasses), pointing to lighting priors and material/texture modeling as natural next steps. The broader implication is that grounding generative models on 3D renderings rather than pure noise may be a generalizable recipe for efficient, identity-preserving synthesis beyond head avatars.
