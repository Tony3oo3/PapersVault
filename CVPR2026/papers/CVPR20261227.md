## Problem

Existing talking-head avatar models generate one-way, audio-synchronized lip movements but fail to produce truly interactive, bidirectional communication where the avatar reacts to the user's verbal and non-verbal cues in real time. Two specific gaps remain: (1) current interactive models require full future context (e.g., >3 s), making real-time causal generation with low latency infeasible, and (2) expressive listening and reactive behaviors are inherently ambiguous and under-represented in training data, so models learn stiff, passive motions without additional human annotations.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single 512×512 reference portrait, the user's audio (16 kHz) and head motion (encoded to a 512-d motion latent via a shared autoencoder), and the avatar's own audio (16 kHz, encoded with Wav2Vec2.0 multi-scale features). It produces a 25 fps interactive head avatar video generated block-by-block (10 frames per block) in a learned motion latent space, decoded to pixels by a latent-to-frame decoder. Inference uses a rolling KV cache with a maximum window, yielding an end-to-end latency of roughly 500 ms per block.

## Method

Avatar Forcing generates avatar motion latents autoregressively via a causal diffusion-forcing transformer (DFoT) with a blockwise causal attention mask augmented by a look-ahead window (l=2 frames) to smooth inter-block transitions. A Dual Motion Encoder first cross-attends user motion and user audio to form a holistic user representation, then cross-attends with avatar audio to produce a unified user-avatar condition that conditions the motion generator. For real-time inference, a rolling KV cache over past blocks avoids recomputation, enabling stepwise generation under strict causal constraints. To boost expressiveness without human labels, the authors fine-tune with Direct Preference Optimization: preferred samples are ground-truth motion latents, while less-preferred samples are synthesized by a separate talking-head model conditioned only on avatar audio (i.e., user signals dropped), so the DPO loss pushes the model toward reactive, expressive behavior while preserving lip-sync quality.

## Results

On RealTalk, Avatar Forcing achieves a latency of 0.5 s (vs. 3.4 s for INFP*), a 6.8× speedup, while dramatically improving reactiveness (rPCC-Exp 0.003 vs. 0.035; rPCC-Pose 0.036 vs. 0.064) and motion richness (SID 2.442 vs. 2.343; Var 1.734 vs. 1.638) over INFP*. Visual quality (FID 24.33, FVD 170.87) and lip sync (LSE-D 8.06, LSE-C 6.72) remain comparable to INFP*. In a 42-participant human study, Avatar Forcing is preferred over 80% of the time overall, with 73% preference on reactiveness and 76% on motion richness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against INFP* (the primary interactive baseline), Avatar Forcing wins decisively on latency (0.5 s vs. 3.4 s), reactiveness (rPCC-Exp 0.003 vs. 0.035, rPCC-Pose 0.036 vs. 0.064), and motion richness (SID 2.442 vs. 2.343, Var 1.734 vs. 1.638), while matching or slightly exceeding INFP* on FID (24.33 vs. 24.55) and LSE-C (6.72 vs. 6.54); INFP* retains a small edge on FVD (159.0 vs. 170.9) and CSIM (0.867 vs. 0.833). Against FLOAT (non-interactive talking head), Avatar Forcing is far superior on FID (24.33 vs. 81.30) and FVD (170.9 vs. 438.8), confirming that the interactive framework does not sacrifice visual quality. On HDTF (Table 3), Avatar Forcing achieves the best FID (20.33) and FVD (149.80) among SadTalker, Hallo3, FLOAT, and INFP*, validating its talking-head capability. On ViCo (Table 4), Avatar Forcing outperforms RLHG, L2L, DIM, and INFP* on FD-Exp (16.64), rPCC-Exp (0.01), rPCC-Pose (0.01), SID-Exp (3.12), and Var-Exp (2.80), establishing state-of-the-art listening behavior. In the human preference study, Avatar Forcing is preferred over INFP* by 73% (reactiveness), 76% (motion richness), and >80% (overall), confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the diffusion forcing framework (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024) and the DFoT architecture (Song et al., ICML 2025) from general video generation into the motion latent space of talking-head models (Ki et al., ICCV 2025), replacing the bidirectional DiT used by INFP (Zhu et al., CVPR 2025) with a blockwise causal structure that supports KV-cached autoregressive inference. It further departs from standard DPO (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023; Wallace et al., CVPR 2024) by constructing 'losing' preference samples synthetically—simply dropping user motion and audio conditions—rather than relying on human-annotated preference pairs, which is particularly natural in the interactive-avatar setting where the 'correct' reaction is inherently ambiguous.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving ~500 ms latency (vs. 3.4 s for INFP) through causal generation with rolling KV caches, the work makes genuinely real-time, bidirectional avatar interaction feasible on a single GPU, opening the door to live virtual-presence applications. The label-free DPO strategy for boosting expressiveness is a practical recipe that could transfer to other one-to-many motion generation tasks where ground-truth reactions are ambiguous. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 512×512 head crops to full-body or multi-person scenes, how performance degrades over very long conversations with the fixed-size KV cache, and whether the synthetic-preference signal remains effective when the user and avatar have very different visual identities.
