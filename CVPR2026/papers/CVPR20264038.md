## Problem

Diffusion-based video super-resolution remains impractical for real-world deployment due to three coupled obstacles: chunk-wise processing introduces large lookahead latency (≈80 frames), dense 3D attention scales quadratically with resolution making high-res inference prohibitively expensive, and a train-test resolution gap causes severe degradation (repeated patterns, blurring) when models trained at medium resolution are applied to ultra-high resolutions like 1440p. No prior diffusion VSR model achieves near real-time streaming inference at high resolutions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes low-resolution video frames (projected into latent space via a lightweight LR Proj-In layer rather than full VAE encoding) and Gaussian noise, producing high-resolution video frames in a streaming fashion with only 8 frames of lookahead latency. It operates at 768×1408 resolution on a single A100 GPU at ~17 FPS, and scales to 1440p without spatial tiling. Because VSR is strongly conditioned on LR frames, the model does not require clean historical latents for temporal continuity—temporal consistency is instead propagated through the KV-cache in later layers.

## Method

FlashVSR is built on WAN2.1-1.3B (fine-tuned with LoRA rank 384) and trained via a three-stage distillation pipeline: (1) joint video-image SR training with a block-diagonal segment mask to establish a full-attention teacher, (2) adaptation to block-sparse causal attention (top-k block selection over (2,8,8) blocks) for streaming efficiency, and (3) distribution-matching distillation (DMD) into a one-step student trained in parallel using LR frames and noise as the sole inputs, eliminating the train-infer gap. Two key architectural innovations address the remaining bottlenecks: locality-constrained sparse attention restricts each query to a local spatial window (boundary-preserved or boundary-truncated), aligning RoPE positional encoding ranges between training and inference to eliminate the resolution gap without spatial tiling; and a Tiny Conditional Decoder conditions reconstruction on both LR frames and latents, reducing VAE decoding time by ~7× while maintaining comparable quality. The full pipeline achieves 17 FPS at 768×1408 with 11.1 GB peak memory, a ~12× speedup over the fastest prior one-step diffusion VSR model.

## Results

On YouHQ40, FlashVSR-Tiny achieves the best perceptual scores: MUSIQ 66.63, CLIPIQA 0.5221, DOVER 12.66, and LPIPS 0.3866, while DOVE leads on PSNR (24.39) and SSIM (0.6651). On REDS and SPMCS, FlashVSR-Full attains the highest MUSIQ (68.97 and 71.05) and CLIPIQA (0.4661 and 0.5792), with DOVE retaining the edge on PSNR/SSIM. On the no-reference datasets VideoLQ and AIGC30, FlashVSR-Full dominates all four metrics (e.g., DOVER 8.149 on VideoLQ vs. 6.786 for DOVE; DOVER 12.65 on AIGC30 vs. 12.48 for SeedVR2-3B). In terms of efficiency, FlashVSR-Tiny runs at 16.92 FPS (5.97 s) on 101×768×1408 video with only 11.13 GB peak memory, versus 1.43 FPS and 52.88 GB for SeedVR2-3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashVSR consistently outperforms all five baselines on perceptual metrics (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA, DOVER) across every dataset. Against the strongest one-step diffusion baseline SeedVR2-3B, FlashVSR-Full gains +0.28 DOVER on YouHQ40 (12.71 vs. 12.43), +0.08 CLIPIQA on SPMCS (0.5792 vs. 0.5307), and +0.17 DOVER on VideoLQ (8.149 vs. 6.040), while running ~12× faster (16.92 FPS vs. 1.43 FPS) with 4.8× less peak memory (11.13 GB vs. 52.88 GB). Against DOVE (the best multi-step one-step diffusion model), FlashVSR wins on all perceptual metrics but trails on PSNR/SSIM by ~1 dB, which the authors attribute to PSNR/SSIM favoring smoother outputs. Against the multi-step diffusion baselines STAR and Upscale-A-Video, FlashVSR achieves substantially higher perceptual quality (e.g., DOVER 12.71 vs. 7.363 for STAR on YouHQ40) while being 114× and 136× faster respectively. On the no-reference real-world (VideoLQ) and AIGC (AIGC30) benchmarks, FlashVSR-Full leads all methods on every metric, with the largest margin over DOVE on VideoLQ DOVER (+1.36) and AIGC30 CLIPIQA (+0.088). The Tiny conditional decoder variant (Ours-Tiny) retains most of the quality gains while further reducing peak memory to 11.13 GB and runtime to 5.97 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashVSR builds on the WAN2.1 video diffusion backbone and the DMD (Distribution Matching Distillation) framework for one-step generation, while adopting block-sparse attention mechanisms from DraftAttention and VSA. It departs from prior diffusion-based VSR methods (STAR, DOVE, SeedVR2) that rely on chunk-wise inference with substantial lookahead latency, by introducing a streaming causal design with only 8 frames of latency. Unlike autoregressive video diffusion models (Self Forcing, Diffusion Forcing) that require sequential frame generation during training, FlashVSR exploits the strong LR-frame conditioning inherent to VSR to enable fully parallel training, eliminating the train-infer gap without student forcing. The locality-constrained sparse attention is a novel contribution that addresses a previously underexplored RoPE periodicity issue at ultra-high resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlashVSR makes diffusion-based VSR practically deployable for the first time, achieving 17 FPS at 768×1408 on a single A100 with 12× speedup over the fastest prior one-step method, which is a significant milestone for real-world and AIGC video applications. The VSR-120K dataset (120K videos, 180K images) addresses a clear gap in training data scale and quality for the field. Open questions include whether the locality-constrained attention and tiny conditional decoder generalize to other restoration tasks (deblurring, denoising), whether the 8-frame lookahead is sufficient for all streaming applications, and how the method performs on hardware beyond A100-class GPUs for broader deployment.
