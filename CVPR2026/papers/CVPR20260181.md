## Problem

Existing fingerspelling recognition methods suffer from three interrelated limitations: explicit signing-hand detection fails when the non-signing hand exhibits larger motion, the CTC loss induces peaky behavior that provides sparse supervision to the encoder, and the out-of-vocabulary problem is largely unaddressed because manually collecting data for new words is prohibitively expensive. No prior work simultaneously resolves all three issues while supporting multi-hand inputs.

## Input / Output

The recognizer consumes normalized 2D hand pose sequences (single- or multi-hand) extracted by MediaPipe, with each frame embedding both hand identity (left/right and person) and temporal index via a dual-level sinusoidal encoding; it outputs a decoded letter sequence with start/end tokens. The generator takes a frame-wise letter sequence (each letter repeated 3–10 frames to simulate temporal duration) and produces a 3D hand pose sequence via iterative diffusion denoising (50 steps), enabling synthesis of pose data for arbitrary OOV words without human annotation.

## Method

The core recognizer is a Transformer encoder-decoder that replaces explicit signing-hand detection with implicit detection through two auxiliary losses: a signing-hand focus (SF) loss that minimizes the entropy of the hand-wise attention distribution in cross-attention (forcing attention onto the dominant signing hand), and a monotonic alignment (MA) loss that penalizes positive deviations in cumulative cross-attention differences between consecutive letter tokens (enforcing temporal order). A dual-level positional encoding separately encodes hand identity and temporal position, enabling the model to distinguish hands while preserving frame alignment. For OOV data synthesis, a diffusion-based frame-wise letter-conditioned (FWLC) generator conditions each denoising step on the per-frame letter token, producing pose-accurate articulations; frame-wise letter annotations are obtained via a coarse-to-fine pipeline that first extracts coarse labels from the recognizer's cross-attention and then refines them with a lightweight supervised refiner. The generator is used to construct FSNeo (8,175 samples across 1,635 neologism words) and to augment training data, improving both the proposed recognizer and PoseNet.

## Results

On ChicagoFSWild (CFSW), the proposed multi-hand-capable recognizer achieves 75.4% letter accuracy, outperforming the strongest prior method PoseNet (61.6%) by 13.8 points. On ChicagoFSWildPlus (CFSWP), it reaches 70.5% versus PoseNet's 61.0%. On the newly introduced FSNeo benchmark for out-of-vocabulary neologisms, the method scores 80.5% compared to PoseNet's 61.2%. With additional synthetic training data, the recognizer further improves to 77.7% (CFSW), 74.6% (CFSWP), and 97.6% (FSNeo), while also boosting PoseNet's FSNeo accuracy from 61.2% to 94.9%. Inference runs at 962 FPS, approximately 100× faster than PoseNet's 6 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: three RGB-based methods (Shi et al. 2018, Shi et al. 2019, FSS-Net) and one pose-based Transformer method (PoseNet). The proposed method outperforms all baselines on every dataset where they overlap. Against the RGB-based Shi et al. [51] (57.5% CFSW) and Shi et al. [52] (61.2% CFSW), the gains are 17.9 and 14.2 points respectively, though these RGB methods cannot be evaluated on FSNeo. FSS-Net [54] is the weakest baseline at 52.5% on CFSW, though it does reach 64.4% on CFSWP. The most direct comparison is with PoseNet [13], the only prior pose-based method: the proposed method wins by 13.8 points on CFSW (75.4 vs. 61.6), 9.5 points on CFSWP (70.5 vs. 61.0), and 19.3 points on FSNeo (80.5 vs. 61.2). Additionally, the implicit signing-hand detection achieves 99.9% accuracy versus PoseNet's explicit detector at 90.4%, and inference is ~100× faster (962 vs. 6 FPS) due to eliminating the re-ranking post-processing step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper rethinks the fingerspelling recognition pipeline by replacing two established components: explicit signing-hand detection (used by Shi et al. and PoseNet) and the CTC loss (universal in prior work) with implicit detection via dual-level positional encoding plus a signing-hand focus loss, and a monotonic alignment loss, respectively. For the generation side, it departs from word-level CLIP conditioning used in text-to-motion models by introducing frame-wise letter conditioning within a diffusion framework, motivated by the observation that fingerspelling requires letter-level temporal precision rather than global semantic guidance. The work also repurposes the trained recognizer's cross-attention as a weak annotation signal to bootstrap frame-wise letter labels, a coarse-to-fine strategy not previously applied in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OOV problem in fingerspelling has been largely ignored, yet new words and neologisms emerge continuously; FSNeo provides the first dedicated benchmark for this setting, and the frame-wise letter-conditioned generator offers a scalable, labor-free path to creating training and evaluation data for arbitrary words. The 100× inference speedup (962 FPS vs. 6 FPS) makes real-time fingerspelling recognition practically viable without post-processing, which is significant for deployment in assistive communication systems. Open questions include whether the implicit detection approach generalizes to multi-person signing scenarios, whether synthetic data quality degrades for highly atypical letter sequences, and whether the monotonic alignment loss transfers to other sequential recognition tasks beyond fingerspelling.
