## Problem

Visual tokenizers for diffusion models are confined to low-dimensional latent spaces (typically 32 channels) because increasing dimensionality degrades generation quality. Prior methods that distill vision foundation model (VFM) semantics into the clean latent x_0 fail to account for the fact that diffusion transformers are actually trained on the forward flow trajectory {x_t}, where semantic discriminability deteriorates sharply. This paper asks whether enhancing semantics along the entire forward flow, rather than only at x_0, can unlock the benefits of high-dimensional latent spaces for both reconstruction and generation.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer encodes 256x256 RGB images into continuous latent tensors of shape h x w x c (with c in {16, 32, 64, 128}), and the pixel decoder reconstructs the image from the latent. The diffusion model (DiT^dH-XL) consumes these latents conditioned on ImageNet class labels and generates 256x256 images via 150-step Euler sampling with a dimension-dependent timestep shift. A lightweight 1.5M-parameter semantic decoder is used only during training to supervise flow-level features and is discarded at inference, so the deployed system requires no VFM or auxiliary decoder.

## Method

RecTok introduces two training objectives that enrich semantics along the rectified-flow trajectory x_t = (1-t)*x_0 + t*eps. First, Flow Semantic Distillation (FSD) decodes each x_t through a lightweight transformer and aligns the output with frozen VFM features (DINOv2 or DINOv3) via a cosine-similarity loss, forcing every point on the flow to remain semantically discriminative. Second, Reconstruction and Alignment Distillation (RAD) applies random spatial masks (ratio 0 to 0.4) to the input image and requires the same semantic decoder to reconstruct full-image VFM features from the visible noisy latent x_t^vis, combining masked reconstruction with alignment. The encoder-decoder is a ViT-B with ROPE, SwiGLU, and RMSNorm; a dimension-dependent timestep shift (s = sqrt(4096/(r^2*d))) is used to sample t. After joint training, the encoder is frozen and only the pixel decoder is finetuned to recover reconstruction fidelity, yielding a tokenizer that simultaneously improves rFID, gFID, and linear-probing accuracy as latent dimension increases from 16 to 128.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256 class-conditional generation, RecTok with DiT^dH-XL achieves a gFID of 1.34 and IS of 254.6 without classifier-free guidance (600 epochs), and a gFID of 1.13 with IS of 289.2 using AutoGuidance. At only 80 epochs, RecTok already reaches gFID 2.09 (w/o guidance), surpassing RAE's 80-epoch result (2.16) and substantially outperforming REPA-E (3.46) and DDT (6.62) at the same budget. At the tokenizer level, RecTok achieves rFID 0.48, PSNR 26.16, and gFID 1.34, combining the best generation quality with strong reconstruction fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Without classifier-free guidance, RecTok (gFID 1.34) outperforms all baselines: it beats RAE (1.51) by 0.17, REPA-E (1.83) by 0.49, l-DeTok (1.86) by 0.52, and VA-VAE (2.17) by 0.83. With AutoGuidance, RecTok ties RAE on gFID (both 1.13) but achieves a substantially higher IS (289.2 vs. 262.6), while also surpassing REPA-E (1.26) and DDT (1.26) on gFID. In terms of convergence speed, RecTok at 80 epochs (gFID 2.09 w/o guidance) already exceeds RAE at 80 epochs (2.16) and dramatically outperforms REPA-E (3.46) and DDT (6.62) at the same budget, indicating roughly 7.75× faster convergence. At the tokenizer level (Table 3), RecTok achieves the best gFID (1.34) among all ViT-based tokenizers while maintaining strong reconstruction (PSNR 26.16), whereas RAE suffers from poor reconstruction (PSNR 18.98) despite competitive generation. The paper also demonstrates that increasing latent dimension from 16 to 128 consistently improves reconstruction (rFID 0.74→0.65), generation (gFID 2.75→2.27), and linear probing accuracy (24.1%→55.4%), a trend not observed in prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

RecTok extends the VFM-semantic-distillation line of work (VA-VAE, REPA, AFM) by shifting the distillation target from the clean latent x₀ to the entire forward flow trajectory {x_t | t∈[0,1]}, which is the actual training space of diffusion transformers. Architecturally it follows l-DeTok's ViT-based encoder-decoder and adopts RAE's DiT^dH-XL backbone and dimension-dependent timestep shift, while introducing two new training objectives—Flow Semantic Distillation (FSD) and Reconstruction-and-Alignment Distillation (RAD)—inspired by masked image modeling. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods treat the latent space as the semantic bottleneck, whereas RecTok argues the flow path itself must remain discriminative for DiT to train efficiently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper breaks a long-standing assumption that increasing latent dimensionality degrades generation quality, showing for the first time that reconstruction, generation, and linear-probing accuracy all improve monotonically from 16 to 128 dimensions. This opens the door to richer latent representations for downstream tasks (editing, personalization) that were previously sacrificed for generative stability. Limitations include evaluation only on class-conditional ImageNet-1K at 256×256, no text-to-image or video experiments, and a somewhat ad-hoc decoder finetuning step. Open questions include whether the flow-level distillation insight transfers to autoregressive tokenizers, higher resolutions, or video generation, and whether the dimensionality gains persist beyond 128 channels.
