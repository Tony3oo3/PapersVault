## Problem

Existing automated detectors for copyrighted character infringement in AI-generated images either rely on pixel-level similarity matching (poor scalability, misses stylistic imitation) or use fixed manually-crafted prompts for LVLMs (highly sensitive to phrasing, weak alignment with human judgment). General-purpose LLM-based prompt optimizers (IPO, LLM-OPT, OPRO, APE) fail in this task-specific setting due to sparse supervision signals and the discrete, vast prompt space, leaving a gap in scalable, human-aligned detection.

## Input / Output

The detector takes a single AI-generated RGB image (512×512 from PixArt-XL or 1024×1024 from SDXL/Playground) paired with a textual detection prompt and outputs a binary label (infringing/non-infringing) aligned with majority-vote human annotations. The optimizer loop additionally consumes the Cohen's Kappa score computed over 150 training images plus the current prompt and prompt history, and produces a refined detection prompt. The final optimized prompt is a free-form natural-language instruction fed to the LVLM at inference time, requiring no model weight updates.

## Method

COPYLENS couples an LLM optimizer (Qwen2.5-14B) with an LVLM detector (Qwen2-VL-7B) in a closed loop where the Kappa score on 150 training images serves as the scalar feedback signal. The key innovation is a Token Learning Rate (TLR) mechanism embedded in the meta-prompt that constrains how much textual modification the optimizer may make per iteration, combined with TLR Decay (progressive narrowing from broad exploration to fine-grained refinement) and Local Optimum Detection (automatic TLR increase when repeated prompts are detected). On the exemplar side, a top-k Prompt History buffer provides short-term memory of the best-scoring prompts, a GPT-4o-distilled Prompt Guidance encodes long-term linguistic patterns across runs, and a Masking Mechanism strips extraneous text so only the core detection prompt is evaluated. The full optimization converges in ~15 steps (≈40 min on one A800), improving Kappa from 0.58 (baseline) to 0.62 on seen characters and 0.63 on unseen characters.

## Results

On COPYCHARS-TEST (average across PixArt, SDXL, and Playground generators), COPYLENS achieves a Cohen's Kappa of 0.62±0.03, outperforming the CopyCat baseline (0.58±0.02) by 4 percentage points and the strongest LLM-optimizer baseline LLM-OPT (0.27±0.11) by 35 percentage points. In terms of accuracy, COPYLENS reaches 88% (vs. 86% baseline, 83% fine-tuning), with a notably lower false positive rate of 9% (vs. 13% baseline). On the unseen-IP generalization set (COPYCHARS-UNSEEN), COPYLENS achieves 63.3% Kappa, exceeding the baseline's 55.9% and showing no degradation, whereas Beam Search drops from 50.6% to 42.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COPYLENS outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the CopyCat baseline (the closest competitor), it gains +0.04 Kappa (0.62 vs. 0.58), +2% accuracy, and reduces FPR from 13% to 9%. The fine-tuning (LoRA) and Beam Search baselines are further behind at 0.54 and 0.50 Kappa respectively. Most strikingly, all four general-purpose LLM-based prompt optimizers (OPRO, APE, IPO, LLM-OPT) perform substantially worse than the simple CopyCat baseline, with Kappa scores ranging from 0.19 to 0.27, confirming that off-the-shelf prompt optimizers are ineffective for this task-specific detection scenario. On the unseen-IP generalization set, COPYLENS (63.3%) not only beats the baseline (55.9%) but also avoids the degradation seen in Beam Search (42.9%) and Fine-tune (47.1%), indicating that the optimized prompt captures transferable detection principles rather than overfitting to specific characters.

## Relation to Prior Work

COPYLENS builds on the LLM-as-implicit-optimizer paradigm (IPO, LLM-OPT) where a language model iteratively refines a prompt based on downstream task feedback, and adopts the instruction-vs-exemplar (IO/EO) decomposition from Wan et al. (NeurIPS 2024) as its structural backbone. It departs from CopyCat (ICLR 2025), the first LVLM-based copyright detector that relies on a single manually crafted prompt, by replacing that fixed prompt with a closed-loop optimization process. The paper also explicitly contrasts with traditional pixel/embedding-similarity methods and shows that general-purpose prompt optimizers (OPRO, APE, IPO, LLM-OPT) fail in this domain, motivating the task-specific TLR and exemplar-memory mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COPYCHARS is the first large-scale, human-annotated benchmark (7,050 images, 100 IPs, 5 annotators) for copyright character detection, filling a critical gap that has limited prior work. The finding that off-the-shelf LLM prompt optimizers collapse to near-chance Kappa scores (0.19–0.27) while a tailored framework reaches 0.62 highlights that copyright detection is not a drop-in application of general prompt engineering. Open questions remain: the absolute Kappa (~0.62) is still far from reliable legal-grade detection, artistic-style variation and complex backgrounds remain failure modes, and it is unclear whether the framework scales to the thousands of IPs in commercial use or to video/multimodal content.
