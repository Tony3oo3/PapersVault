## Problem

Planar CNNs assume pinhole geometry and 2D grid adjacency, which misrepresents physical proximity for wide-FoV cameras (fisheye, panoramic) and makes models sensitive to global rotations. Existing spherical CNNs achieve rotation-equivariance via spherical harmonic transforms, but these impose costly spectral computations that constrain resolution and efficiency. The paper addresses the gap of a distortion-free, lens-agnostic spherical processing pipeline that operates entirely in the spatial domain while retaining built-in rotation-equivariance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a planar RGB image from any calibrated camera (pinhole, fisheye, or panoramic) along with its intrinsics, from which a dense lens normal map (per-pixel unit-norm R³ ray directions) is derived. After projection onto the unit sphere and resampling to a near-uniform point distribution, the pipeline produces task-specific outputs: class labels (Spherical MNIST), oriented bounding boxes in Rotated Bounding FoV format (PANDORA, 960×480 panoramas), or per-pixel semantic masks (Stanford 2D-3D-S, 4096×2048 equirectangular). All geometric mappings are cacheable per camera, so subsequent inference reuses them with negligible overhead.

## Method

USF is a modular pipeline with four decoupled stages: spherical projection, resampling (location sampling + value interpolation), spherical convolution, and spherical pooling. Spherical convolution aggregates input features within a geodesic circular cap around each output point, weighting neighbors by a factorized function of relative geodesic distance and (optionally) local tangent-plane bearing angle; using distance-only weighting yields a zonal filter that is trivially rotation-equivariant by construction. Location sampling supports multiple near-uniform schemes (Goldberg polyhedron, HEALPix, Fibonacci lattice, quasi-random), and value interpolation uses radial basis functions or bandlimited spherical harmonic fitting over unordered point neighborhoods. The entire pipeline avoids spherical harmonic transforms, operating purely in the spatial domain, and is designed as a plug-and-play replacement for planar convolution and pooling layers in existing backbones (YOLOv11, DeepLab v3, UNet), enabling rotation-equivariant processing without architectural redesign or rotational data augmentation.

## Results

On Spherical MNIST, the radial-only spherical PWC kernel (DisPWC×3) achieves 87.18% accuracy under non-rotated (NR) testing and 85.43% under random rotation (RR), compared to 98.45% and 41.08% for the planar CNN, demonstrating a 44-point improvement in rotation robustness at a ~11-point NR accuracy cost. On PANDORA object detection, the spherical YOLOv11 maintains 29.59% mAP10 under random rotation versus 12.71% for the planar YOLOv11 (a 2.3× improvement), while the planar model achieves 39.65% mAP10 in the non-rotated setting. On Stanford 2D-3D-S segmentation, spherical DeepLab v3 retains 28.09% mIoU under random rotation versus 12.11% for the planar counterpart, again at a modest NR accuracy reduction (28.78% vs. 35.01%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against spectral spherical CNNs (S²CNN and SO(3)CNN), the proposed spatial-domain USF with a 6-level Fourier-embedded radial MLP (DisMLP L=6) achieves 92.13%/91.50% NR/RR accuracy, trailing SO(3)CNN (98.7%/98.1%) by ~6 points but avoiding the costly spherical harmonic transforms that limit those methods to low-resolution inputs. The simpler 3-segment PWC kernel (87.18%/85.43%) is less accurate than both spectral baselines but is the only variant used in the high-resolution detection and segmentation experiments where spectral methods are infeasible. Against the planar YOLOv11 on PANDORA, the spherical variant sacrifices ~10 points of mAP10 in the non-rotated setting (29.54% vs. 39.65%) but more than doubles performance under random rotation (29.59% vs. 12.71% mAP10), and also outperforms R-CenterNet (35.73% mAP10, NR only) in the rotated regime. On Stanford 2D-3D-S segmentation, spherical DeepLab v3 (28.78% mIoU NR) underperforms planar DeepLab v3 (35.01%) by ~6 points, but under random rotation the gap reverses: spherical retains 28.09% mIoU while planar collapses to 12.11%. The distance×direction kernel variant matches planar accuracy on upright MNIST digits (98.28% vs. 98.45%) but loses equivariance entirely (43.54% RR), confirming the fundamental trade-off between directional expressivity and rotation invariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

USF departs from spectral spherical CNNs (S2CNN, SO(3)CNN) by performing all operations in the spatial domain, avoiding costly spherical harmonic transforms that constrain resolution. It also diverges from structured spatial-domain methods (SphereNet, SpherePHD, DISCO) by treating spherical data as an unordered point set with no mesh, grid, or predefined connectivity, and by decoupling location sampling from value interpolation into independently configurable stages. The lens-agnostic projection step (via dense ray-direction maps) is a practical repurposing of standard camera calibration, but the key conceptual move is making the entire convolution/pooling pipeline agnostic to the source lens, so that standard planar backbones (YOLOv11, DeepLab v3, UNet) can be swapped to spherical variants with minimal architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

USF offers a practical, plug-and-play path to rotation-equivariant and lens-agnostic perception for robotics, AR/VR, and omnidirectional systems, where harmonic-transform-based methods are computationally prohibitive at high resolution. The open question of how to recover directional sensitivity (e.g., bounding-box orientation) while preserving equivariance remains unresolved; the paper acknowledges that gauge-dependent prediction targets cannot be handled by radial-only kernels alone. The zero-shot lens generalization claim, while compelling in principle, is supported only by a single-batch overfitting experiment, leaving open whether the effect holds under standard train/test splits and at scale.
