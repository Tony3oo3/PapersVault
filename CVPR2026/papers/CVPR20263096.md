## Problem

Existing diffusion-based material reconstruction methods face three key limitations: they struggle to faithfully preserve fine details (text, logos) from input images, their cross-view attention mechanisms scale as O(N²) in memory preventing high-resolution or many-view inputs, and they often depend on multiple pre-trained models or complex multi-stage pipelines that reduce stability. MatMart addresses all three by unifying prediction and generation in a single end-to-end trainable diffusion model with a scalable progressive inference strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more RGB images (arbitrary count) and a UV-mapped 3D mesh as input; for meshes lacking UV maps, Blender's Smart UV Project is used. Inference runs at 1024×1024 resolution on a single V100 GPU, taking 9–23 minutes per object depending on the number of generated views. The output consists of three PBR material maps (albedo, roughness, metallic) baked into UV space, with the geometry's normals and point positions rendered as auxiliary priors during generation.

## Method

MatMart operates a two-stage pipeline within a single Stable Diffusion-based model. Stage 1 performs progressive material estimation: input images are fed sequentially, and a proposed view-material cross-attention (VMCA) module uses the previous round's prediction as a reference view, achieving O(1) space complexity instead of O(N²) for standard cross-view attention. Stage 2 performs prior-guided generation for occluded/unobserved regions: views are adaptively selected via greedy UV texel-coverage maximization, then the network alternates between generating view-space materials (conditioned on projected material priors, geometric priors, and generation masks) and baking them into UV space with cosine-similarity-weighted blending. Both stages share one diffusion model trained end-to-end with alternating prediction/generation objectives, where zero-padding in feature space distinguishes the two tasks, and v-prediction with AdamW is used for optimization.

## Results

On the Objaverse test set (100 objects, 9 rendered views), MatMart at 1024×1024 resolution achieves the best scores across all metrics in both single-view and multi-view settings. In the multi-view setting, it reaches albedo SSIM 0.945, PSNR 32.10, metallic MSE 0.015, roughness MSE 0.008, rendering FID 26.20, and LPIPS 0.052. In the single-view setting, it achieves albedo SSIM 0.931, PSNR 29.89, metallic MSE 0.017, roughness MSE 0.007, FID 31.49, and LPIPS 0.066. The method also generalizes to real-world data on Stanford-ORB, where it produces the most visually faithful reconstructions among compared approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatMart outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in both single-view and multi-view settings. Against the closest competitor MaterialMVP [13], MatMart (1024×1024) improves multi-view albedo SSIM from 0.902 to 0.945 (+0.043), PSNR from 27.61 to 32.10 (+4.49 dB), and rendering FID from 38.00 to 26.20 (−11.80), while also reducing metallic and roughness MSE by roughly 40–42%. Material Anything [14] trails by a wider margin (multi-view FID 47.27 vs. 26.20), which the authors attribute to its reliance on a separate pre-trained diffusion model for RGB generation followed by material prediction, introducing domain mismatch and instability. NvDiffRec [33], an inverse-rendering method, produces the noisiest results (FID 60.97 multi-view) due to difficulty disentangling material and illumination from sparse views. Paint3D [47] is evaluated only in single-view and yields the lowest PSNR (24.66) and highest LPIPS (0.166), as it bakes shadows and reflections into the albedo rather than predicting true material properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatMart sits at the intersection of diffusion-based material prediction (IDArb, MaterialMVP) and prior-guided generation (Material Anything, TexGEN), but departs from both by unifying prediction and generation within a single jointly-trained diffusion model rather than chaining separate pretrained components. Its progressive inference strategy with view-material cross-attention (VMCA) addresses the O(N²) memory bottleneck of cross-view attention used in prior multi-view methods, enabling arbitrary input counts. The texture baking mechanism is adapted from Hunyuan3D 2.1 with progressive weight updates, and the overall two-stage design (estimate-then-generate) contrasts with methods that either predict all views at once or generate RGB first and then decompose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MatMart offers a practically deployable framework for PBR material reconstruction that scales from single-view to arbitrary multi-view inputs at high resolution using a single model, reducing the training and deployment complexity of multi-stage pipelines. The ablation showing that stage-1 baking is critical (FID drops from 26.2 to 42.4 without it) underscores that generation alone cannot yet match prediction for fidelity, a useful signal for the field. Open questions include the inherent ambiguity of material decomposition (scaled albedo colors), the need for more views on heavily self-occluded objects, and whether the unified single-model approach generalizes to other material properties or lighting conditions beyond the tested PBR set.
