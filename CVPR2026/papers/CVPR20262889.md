## Problem

In GRPO-based reinforcement learning for flow-matching models, the importance-ratio clipping mechanism fails to constrain overconfident positive updates because the ratio distribution is systematically left-shifted (mean < 1) with inconsistent variance across denoising timesteps. This causes the policy to enter an implicit over-optimization regime where proxy reward keeps rising while image quality, text-prompt alignment, and diversity collapse, rendering the learned policy impractical.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image via iterative SDE denoising over T timesteps. During RL training, a group of G diverse images is sampled from the same prompt using stochastic SDE transitions, each scored by a proxy reward model (GenEval, PickScore, or TextRender) to compute group-relative advantages. A composite gold score combining HPSv2, ImageReward, and UnifiedReward is used to measure true generation quality independent of the proxy task.

## Method

GRPO-Guard introduces two modifications to the standard GRPO policy objective for flow-matching models. First, RatioNorm standardizes the log-importance ratio at each denoising step by multiplying by σ_t√dt and adding back the quadratic bias term ‖Δμ_θ‖²/(2σ_t²dt), which removes the timestep-dependent negative bias inherent in Gaussian state-transition probabilities (unlike discrete token probabilities in LLMs), restoring a zero-mean ratio distribution so that PPO clipping symmetrically constrains both positive and negative updates. Second, a gradient reweighting factor δ=1/dt (or δ=β/dt for DanceGRPO's constant-noise schedule) is applied to the policy loss to equalize gradient magnitudes across timesteps, preventing low-noise steps from dominating optimization. Together these form a regulated clipping mechanism that stabilizes training without requiring heavy KL regularization, and the approach is model-agnostic, applying to both FlowGRPO and DanceGRPO across SD3.5-M and Flux.1-dev backbones.

## Results

GRPO-Guard consistently achieves higher composite gold scores (average of HPSv2, ImageReward, UnifiedReward, normalized to base model = 1.00) than baselines under comparable proxy scores. On SD3.5-M, GRPO-Guard improves the average gold score over FlowGRPO by +0.05 (GenEval), +0.04 (PickScore), and +0.11 (TextRender), while TempFlowGRPO collapses to 0.52 due to severe over-optimization. On Flux.1-dev, GRPO-Guard lifts the average gold score from 0.88 to 1.02 (+0.14) on GenEval and from 0.96 to 1.02 (+0.06) on PickScore relative to DanceGRPO, demonstrating that the method prevents quality degradation while maintaining proxy reward gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlowGRPO on SD3.5-M, GRPO-Guard wins on all three proxy tasks in average gold score: +0.05 on GenEval (0.89 vs. 0.84), +0.04 on PickScore (1.20 vs. 1.16), and +0.11 on TextRender (0.99 vs. 0.88), while matching or slightly exceeding the proxy scores. Against DanceGRPO on Flux.1-dev, GRPO-Guard achieves a substantially larger gain of +0.14 on GenEval (1.02 vs. 0.88) and +0.06 on PickScore (1.02 vs. 0.96), indicating that the regulated clipping mechanism is especially effective when the baseline suffers more severe reward hacking. Against TempFlowGRPO, GRPO-Guard dramatically outperforms on the TextRender task (0.99 vs. 0.52 average gold score), as TempFlowGRPO's aggressive reweighting accelerates optimization but triggers early and severe over-optimization. In all comparisons, GRPO-Guard maintains proxy scores within 0.01–0.02 of the baselines, confirming that the quality gains come from preventing degradation rather than from sacrificing proxy reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FlowGRPO and DanceGRPO, which transplant GRPO-style policy optimization into flow-matching models by converting deterministic ODE sampling into stochastic SDE trajectories. The key departure is a diagnostic: the authors show that the PPO/GRPO importance-ratio clipping mechanism, inherited wholesale from LLM alignment, is fundamentally mismatched to diffusion models because Gaussian state-transition probabilities introduce a timestep-dependent negative bias in the log-ratio (mean < 1), causing positive-advantage samples to never enter the clipped region. The proposed RatioNorm and gradient reweighting are lightweight corrections to the existing GRPO objective rather than a new RL framework, and the gradient-balancing component is conceptually related to but distinct from TempFlowGRPO's noise-aware reweighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Over-optimization (reward hacking) is the primary practical barrier to deploying RL-aligned diffusion models, and this paper identifies a concrete, previously overlooked mechanism—clipping asymmetry due to ratio distribution shift—as a root cause, offering a simple drop-in fix that works across multiple backbones and tasks without heavy KL regularization. The limitation is that GRPO-Guard cannot close the intrinsic proxy-to-gold score gap; it only prevents the policy from exploiting that gap destructively. An open direction the authors highlight is developing reward models that more faithfully approximate human perceptual quality, which would reduce the over-optimization pressure at its source.
