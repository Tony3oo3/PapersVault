## Problem

Adapting CLIP to vertical domains typically requires continual pre-training on massive domain-specific datasets, but existing influence-based data selection methods (TracIn, TRAK) were designed for single-tower supervised classification and systematically mis-rank CLIP examples due to cross-modal curvature, non-local InfoNCE gradients, and endpoint dominance at projection heads. The paper asks whether principled, CLIP-aware data selection can substitute for raw dataset scale in CPT.

## Input / Output

CHIPS consumes a large image-text training pool (e.g., BIOMEDICA at 24M pairs) and a small target evaluation set (default 200 samples per task across 17 medical benchmarks) to compute a single scalar utility score per candidate pair. The scoring operates entirely in CLIP's end-point subspace (two projection heads plus temperature), so it requires no full-parameter backpropagation and the resulting scores can be cached and reused across different backbone architectures (B32/B16/L14/H14) and pre-training scales (400M vs. 2.5B pairs) without recomputation.

## Method

CHIPS assigns each image-text pair a utility score that is the product of three factors: (1) a curvature-aware proxy Newton alignment A(z) = g_ϑ(z)ᵀ M⁻¹ u_ϑ computed in the end-point subspace, where the curvature surrogate M blends positive-pair self-moments with negative-pair cross-moments from symmetric InfoNCE (mixing weight α) and is compressed via Johnson-Lindenstrauss sketching for near-linear cost; (2) a learnability weight w_L that downweights already-correct pairs and upweights small-margin decision-boundary examples; and (3) a target-domain relevance weight w_R that softly biases selection toward samples whose embeddings align with evaluation-set centroids. The paper proves a lower bound on the Pearson correlation between the end-point proxy and full-parameter alignment (Theorem 1) and an error decomposition separating JL projection variance from curvature-mixing bias (Theorem 2). The top-n scored samples are then used for standard CPT with AdamW and a cosine LR schedule.

## Results

On 17 medical benchmarks, CHIPS achieves Medical Avg scores of 27.03, 28.20, and 29.96 at 10%, 20%, and 30% data retention, exceeding the second-best selector (TracIn) by +0.57, +1.57, and +4.28 points respectively. With only 30% of BIOMEDICA, CHIPS reaches 95.1% of full-dataset CPT performance (29.96 vs. 31.51), and at 10% it outperforms a 50% random subset (27.03 vs. 26.26). On 31 general-domain benchmarks, CHIPS retains 90.1%, 89.3%, and 87.2% of the vanilla model's classification accuracy at r = 10%, 20%, 30%, compared to 88.9%, 88.0%, 87.0% for TracIn, indicating superior retention of general capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHIPS outperforms all seven selection baselines on Medical Avg across every retention ratio. Against the strongest influence-based competitor TracIn, CHIPS leads by +0.57 (r=10%), +1.57 (r=20%), and +4.28 (r=30%) points, with the gap widening as more data is available. TRAK and Dot trail CHIPS by 1.84–6.42 and 1.71–5.99 points respectively, while heuristic methods (CLIPScore, Concept-Balance, Concept-Filter) are consistently 1.5–11 points behind. On general-domain retention, CHIPS slightly lags Random and Concept-Balance on CLS/RET (by ≤1.5 points) but substantially outperforms TracIn, TRAK, and Dot, which suffer 2–5 point drops in general classification. Among specialized medical CLIP models, CHIPS at r=30% (29.96) marginally surpasses BMCLIP (29.86) and clearly outperforms PubMedCLIP (26.21) and BioMedCLIP (21.45), despite using only 30% of BIOMEDICA rather than full-scale pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHIPS extends the data attribution/influence function lineage (Koh & Liang 2017; TracIn; TRAK) from single-tower supervised classification to CLIP's dual-encoder contrastive setting, where three structural properties—cross-modal curvature, non-local InfoNCE gradients, and endpoint dominance at projection heads—systematically invalidate prior assumptions. Rather than computing full-parameter influence, the paper restricts alignment to CLIP's end-point subspace (projection heads and temperature), justified by a lower-bound correlation theorem, and makes the curvature estimator scalable via Johnson-Lindenstrauss sketching. The learnability and domain-relevance weighting mechanism departs from pure influence scoring by explicitly balancing target adaptation against general-domain retention, a concern absent from classical TracIn/TRAK formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that 30% of a 24M-pair medical pool matches full-dataset CPT and 10% outperforms 50%—offers a concrete data-efficiency recipe for vertical-domain CLIP adaptation where collecting and curating large datasets is the dominant cost. The theoretical guarantees (proxy-full correlation lower bound, bias-variance decomposition of the curvature estimator) provide principled justification rather than purely empirical tuning. Key limitations include reliance on a labeled target evaluation set for the alignment direction and the method's specificity to CLIP's architecture; the authors flag unlabeled or shift-robust target signals and extension beyond CLIP as open directions. The finding that cached scores transfer across backbone sizes and pre-training scales (400M vs. 2.5B) suggests the scoring step is a one-time investment, which is practically significant for multi-model pipelines.
