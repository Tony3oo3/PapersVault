## Problem

While MLLMs generate tokens autoregressively, it remains unclear which specific visual regions drive each token's generation and whether the model relies on perceptual evidence or language priors. Existing attribution methods face inherent limitations: gradient-based approaches are memory-intensive and confounded by textual priors, activation-based methods lack causal grounding, and prior subset-selection methods like VPS cannot be directly transferred to autoregressive generation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260339/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input, with the image sparsified into N sub-regions via SLICO superpixel segmentation (typically 36–64 regions). It produces an ordered ranking of perceptual sub-regions (a saliency map) that attributes which regions promote generation of user-selected tokens, plus a per-token influence score quantifying the relative contribution of perceptual evidence versus language priors. The framework is fully black-box, requiring only forward-pass probability queries without access to internal activations or gradients.

## Method

EAGLE casts attribution as an ordered subset-selection problem over sparsified image regions, optimized via greedy search. The core objective unifies two complementary scores: an insight score measuring whether a region set is sufficient to maximize target-token probabilities, and a necessity score measuring whether removing those regions significantly impairs generation. The greedy strategy evaluates marginal increments of this objective along an ordered sequence, yielding a ranked saliency map with diminishing-returns behavior. For modality analysis, EAGLE tracks how each token's probability evolves as salient regions are progressively introduced, defining an influence score where large variation indicates perceptual grounding while stable probabilities indicate reliance on language priors. The approach is token-agnostic (no pre-selection of visually relevant tokens needed) and requires only O(|V|²) forward inferences with O(|V|) space, making it substantially more memory-efficient than gradient-based baselines.

## Results

On MS COCO image captioning, EAGLE achieves sentence-level insertion AUC of 0.7006 (Qwen2.5-VL 7B) and 0.7665 (InternVL3.5 4B), outperforming the best baselines by 20.0% (insertion) and 13.4% (deletion) on average. On MMVP VQA, EAGLE reaches 0.8052 insertion (Qwen2.5-VL 3B) and 0.8012 (InternVL3.5 4B), improving over baselines by 20.6% and 8.1% respectively. At the word level, EAGLE scores 0.8052 on the Pointing Game (box-level) and 0.7792 (mask-level) on LLaVA-1.5, surpassing TAM by 36.42% and 42.63%. EAGLE also requires substantially less GPU memory (e.g., 17.68 GB vs. 96.90 GB for IGOS++ on Qwen2.5-VL 7B) while being the only method that consistently produces faithful, sparse attributions across all tested MLLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAGLE consistently outperforms LLaVA-CAM [38] on both insertion and deletion across all four MLLMs and both datasets; for example, on Qwen2.5-VL 7B for MS COCO captioning, EAGLE achieves 0.7006 insertion vs. 0.5605 for LLaVA-CAM (+25.0%) and 0.4597 deletion vs. 0.5464 (−15.9%). Against IGOS++ (w/GNC) [34], EAGLE wins on faithfulness (e.g., 0.7006 vs. 0.5603 insertion on Qwen2.5-VL 7B) while using dramatically less GPU memory (17.68 GB vs. 96.90 GB, a 5.5× reduction). On word-level localization (Pointing Game), EAGLE surpasses TAM [17] by 36.42% (box-level: 0.8052 vs. 0.1818 on LLaVA-1.5) and 42.63% (mask-level: 0.7792 vs. 0.1428), with TAM performing well only on stronger models like Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3.5. On the RePOPE hallucination benchmark, EAGLE improves insertion over the best baseline by 65.4% (LLaVA-1.5), 164.7% (Qwen2.5-VL 7B), and 123.4% (InternVL3.5 4B), and achieves Correction Success Rate under 10% budget of 77.50–80.41% versus 18.75–42.21% for baselines. The main trade-off is speed: EAGLE takes ~250–800 s per sample versus ~15–55 s for LLaVA-CAM/IGOS++, though it remains the only method producing sparse, human-consistent attributions across all tested architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAGLE directly extends VPS (Visual Precision Search, CVPR 2025), a submodular subset-selection attribution method originally designed for object-level tasks like grounding and detection, to the autoregressive token generation setting of MLLMs. The key conceptual departure is the design of a dual-component objective (insight score for sufficiency, necessity score for indispensability) that accounts for sequential token context, which VPS's single-output formulation cannot handle. The paper positions itself against gradient-based methods (LLaVA-CAM, IGOS++) and activation-based methods (TAM) by being fully black-box, requiring no access to internal activations or gradients, and by supporting arbitrary multi-token attribution rather than single-token or classification-level explanations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLMs are deployed in safety-critical domains (healthcare, autonomous driving), the ability to diagnose *why* a model hallucinated and to identify the minimal visual evidence responsible is practically urgent; EAGLE's finding that removing only 8–14% of image regions corrects hallucinations in 73–80% of cases provides a concrete, actionable diagnostic tool. The modality-aware analysis (language prior vs. perceptual evidence per token) opens a path toward targeted interventions that reduce reliance on spurious language priors. Key limitations include the O(|V|²) inference cost (~10× slower than gradient baselines), dependence on superpixel segmentation quality, and evaluation restricted to 2D single-image inputs; scaling to video, multi-image, or higher-resolution settings remains open.
