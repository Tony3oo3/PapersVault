## Problem

State-Space Models (SSMs) such as Mamba and Vim have not been adapted for continual learning while mitigating catastrophic forgetting. Existing CL methods treat SSM parameters as generic weights, ignoring the underlying state-space geometry and the P-equivalence invariance that makes standard Frobenius-norm regularization unfaithful to the system's true behavior. This paper introduces the first CL method that explicitly exploits the geometric structure of SSMs in an exemplar-free setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261382/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (patchified into token sequences for the Vim-Small backbone) and produces class labels from an incrementally growing label set across 5 or 10 sequential tasks. Critically, during training on task T the model has access only to the current task's data distribution—no stored exemplars, feature embeddings, or logits from previous tasks are permitted (exemplar-free CIL protocol). The regularization signal is computed entirely from the model's own state-space parameters (A, C) at the current and previous task, requiring no external data.

## Method

Inf-SSM constrains state evolution by regularizing the extended observability subspace O_∞(A,C) = [C; CA; CA²; …] on the infinite-dimensional Grassmannian, whose chordal distance captures how much the SSM's infinite-horizon output behavior has shifted between tasks. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) shows this subspace is invariant to P-equivalence, making the loss faithful to the SSM's geometry in a way that parameter-level penalties are not. Computing the Gram matrix for the chordal distance requires solving a Sylvester equation (AᵀGA′ − G = −CᵀC′), which is O(n³) in general; however, for the diagonal-A parameterization used by Mamba/Vim, the equation reduces to element-wise division, yielding an O(n²) solution with up to 100× FLOPS reduction. For time-varying S6/Mamba dynamics, the method averages state transitions along the outer dimension and applies soft-normalization to enforce Schur stability before computing per-position observability subspaces. The final loss L_ISM = E_{D_T}{d_chord²(O_{T−1}, O_T)} is added to the classification loss with a single hyperparameter λ, making it plug-and-play with any existing CL baseline.

## Results

Integrating Inf-SSM into existing CL baselines (ER, LUCIR, X-DER, L2P-R, CLFD) yields an average AA improvement of 8.31% and FM reduction of 9.36% across 5-task and 10-task settings on ImageNet-R, CIFAR-100, and Caltech-256. When compared directly against EFCIL baselines (EWC, SI, MAS, LwF) with (A,C) regularization, Inf-SSM achieves an average FM reduction of 40.18% and AA improvement of 21.73%. Even under the deliberately disadvantaged setting where baselines regularize all of (A,B,C) while Inf-SSM regularizes only (A,C), Inf-SSM still reduces FM by 14.56% and improves AA by 6.79% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Inf-SSM consistently outperforms all four EFCIL baselines (EWC, SI, MAS, LwF) on both AA and FM across all three datasets and task settings, with the largest margins on ImageNet-R where the 10-task FM gap reaches over 20 points versus SI. Against the replay/hybrid baselines in Table 1, Inf-SSM provides the largest absolute gains for the strongest method X-DER (AA +19.61%, FM −23.16%), confirming it is complementary rather than redundant. The advantage grows with the number of tasks: 10-task improvements in AA and FM are substantially larger than 5-task ones, indicating Inf-SSM better captures long-horizon state evolution. Notably, Inf-SSM wins even when baselines are extended to regularize the full (A,B,C) parameter set while Inf-SSM constrains only (A,C), empirically validating Theorem 3.3 that the extended observability subspace is sufficient to control SSM behavior. The only marginal exception is a slight AIA decrease for LUCIR on 5-task ImageNet-R, but all other metric instances improve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two mature fields: continual learning (CL) and state-space models (SSMs). Existing CL methods (EWC, SI, MAS, LwF, ER, LUCIR, X-DER, L2P, CLFD) treat SSM parameters as generic weights, ignoring the state-space geometry and the P-equivalence invariance that makes standard Frobenius regularization fundamentally misaligned with SSM behavior. Inf-SSM repurposes classical system-identification concepts—extended observability subspaces, the infinite Grassmannian, and the Sylvester equation—to construct a geometry-faithful, exemplar-free regularization loss that is invariant to P-equivalence. The computational shortcut (O(n²) via diagonal-A structure) is borrowed from the SSM architecture design itself rather than from prior CL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Inf-SSM is the first CL method that explicitly exploits SSM geometry, and its plug-and-play, exemplar-free design means it can be layered onto any existing CL pipeline without architectural changes or stored data—addressing a practical gap as SSMs (Mamba, Vim) gain traction in vision and NLP. The theoretical result that regularizing only (A,C) suffices (Theorem 3.3) is a clean, actionable insight that simplifies implementation. Limitations include reliance on diagonal-A parameterizations for the fast path (general A still works via the full Sylvester solver but at O(n³)), evaluation limited to Vim-Small on three mid-scale datasets, and the open question of whether the geometric regularization transfers to non-diagonal or fully time-varying SSM variants. Future directions the authors outline—Schubert varieties for varying dimensionality and RKHS kernel methods—suggest a broader program of geometry-aware model compression and distillation.
