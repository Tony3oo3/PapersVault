## Problem

Existing event-based low-light image enhancement methods largely overlook the severe background-activity (BA) noise that contaminates event streams in low-light conditions, and they treat event denoising as a static pre-processing step decoupled from the enhancement objective. This creates a fundamental dilemma: aggressive denoising removes structural details needed for enhancement, while conservative denoising lets residual noise propagate into the fusion stage and degrade reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263464/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB image and its temporally paired noisy event stream (four-tuples of spatial position, timestamp, and polarity) at 346×260 resolution as input. It produces an enhanced high-light RGB image via Retinex decomposition (enhanced illumination × enhanced reflectance) and a denoised three-channel event map (positive, negative, no-event) that serves as an intermediate structural prior fed exclusively into the reflectance enhancement branch.

## Method

BiEvLight comprises two encoder-decoder (SegNet) sub-networks: an event denoising module and a dual-branch Retinex-based enhancement module, jointly optimized via a bilevel formulation where the lower-level enhancement task dynamically calibrates the upper-level denoiser through gradient feedback. A Spatially-adaptive Gradient-guided Denoising strategy uses the reflectance gradient to construct a local sliding-window mask that preserves events aligned with strong gradients while suppressing BA noise in smooth regions, providing implicit supervision for the denoising network. The bilevel optimization is solved with a one-step truncated iterative differentiation scheme, approximating the Hessian-vector product via finite differences to avoid explicit Hessian inversion. The enhancement loss combines L1 terms on the full image, illumination, and reflectance components, while the denoising loss uses cross-entropy over three event categories.

## Results

On the SDE dataset, BiEvLight achieves 22.87 dB PSNR and 26.00 dB PSNR* (SDE-in) and 24.36 dB PSNR and 26.16 dB PSNR* (SDE-out), outperforming the strongest baseline EvLight by 0.68–1.92 dB in PSNR and 1.74–2.31 dB in PSNR*. On the SDSD dataset, it reaches 30.76 dB PSNR and 31.00 dB PSNR* (SDSD-in) and 27.41 dB PSNR and 30.46 dB PSNR* (SDSD-out). Across all four evaluation splits, BiEvLight attains the best PSNR, PSNR*, and SSIM among all compared methods, with average improvements of 1.30 dB (PSNR), 2.03 dB (PSNR*), and 0.047 (SSIM) over the prior SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiEvLight outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all four dataset splits. Against the closest Image+Event competitor EvLight (CVPR'24), it gains +0.68 dB PSNR and +2.31 dB PSNR* on SDE-in, +1.92 dB PSNR and +1.74 dB PSNR* on SDE-out, +1.40 dB PSNR on SDSD-in, and +0.67 dB PSNR on SDSD-out. Among RGB-only methods, URWKV (CVPR'25) is the strongest single-modality baseline (21.58 dB PSNR on SDE-in, 28.70 dB on SDSD-in), yet BiEvLight still leads by 1.29 dB and 2.06 dB respectively, confirming the benefit of event-guided denoising. ELIE (TMM'23) and eSLNet (ECCV'20), the other event-assisted methods, trail BiEvLight by 0.83–3.74 dB in PSNR depending on the split. Notably, the PSNR* gap (which measures structural fidelity beyond illumination fitting) is substantially larger than the PSNR gap—up to 2.31 dB on SDE-in—indicating that BiEvLight's gradient-guided denoising recovers fine structural details that prior fusion-only approaches miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based low-light image enhancement (LLIE) line of work (ELIE, EvLight, eSLNet), which has focused primarily on designing cross-modal fusion strategies, by identifying and addressing a previously underexplored bottleneck: background-activity (BA) noise in event streams that degrades the quality of the event modality before fusion. It departs from prior event denoising methods (nearest-neighbor filtering, time-surface approaches) that operate on a single modality, introducing a gradient-guided denoising prior that exploits the spatial correlation between image reflectance gradients and event locations. The key conceptual departure is recasting event denoising from a static preprocessing step into a bilevel optimization problem constrained by the enhancement objective, borrowing the bilevel learning paradigm from architecture search and hyperparameter optimization and applying it to a denoising-enhancement coupling that prior event-based LLIE work has treated as sequential and independent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bilevel formulation offers a principled mechanism for resolving the over-denoising vs. under-denoising dilemma that plagues all current event-based LLIE pipelines, and the gradient-guided denoising prior provides a practical, physically motivated cross-modal signal that could be adapted to other event-camera tasks beyond LLIE. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (SDE, SDSD) at a single resolution, and the bilevel optimization introduces additional computational overhead (Hessian-vector products via finite differences) that may limit real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the task-aware bilevel framework generalizes to other multi-task event-camera settings (e.g., depth estimation, pose tracking), whether the gradient-guided prior remains effective when the image modality is severely degraded, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or longer-duration event streams.
