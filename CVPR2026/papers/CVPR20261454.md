## Problem

Existing VLMs for medical imaging lack verifiable reasoning chains aligned with imaging evidence, limiting clinical trust and auditability. Compounding this, standard RL-based post-training (e.g., GRPO) is dominated by majority classes in both sampling and value estimation, causing severe degradation in recognition and reasoning for rare disease categories under the long-tail distributions inherent to medical data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261454/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single LGE-CMR image (resized to 256×256) paired with a fixed diagnostic prompt asking the model to reason about and classify the cardiac condition. Output is a structured sequence with three components: a free-text reasoning chain inside <think> tags, a lesion bounding box [x1,y1,x2,y2] inside <location> tags, and a disease label (one of six categories) inside <answer> tags. The model is required to name the actual disease category rather than selecting from lettered options, as the authors found the semantic content of labels aids training.

## Method

CMR-RD is built on Gemma-4B and trained in two stages. Stage 1 performs medical alignment by fine-tuning only the vision-to-language projector on PMC-VQA and CMR image-text pairs (vision backbone and LLM frozen), followed by cold-start supervised fine-tuning on ~400 physician-style chain-of-thought reasoning templates to establish basic diagnostic reasoning. Stage 2 introduces Group-Phased Policy Optimization (GPPO), which decomposes GRPO-style RL into five online sub-stages, each with two epochs and decaying learning rate. At each stage, Thompson Sampling over per-class Beta posteriors (updated from validation accuracy) generates difficulty weights that dynamically allocate batch quotas, oversampling underperforming and rare classes; a bucket-refill strategy handles exhausted minority pools. The RL objective combines three rewards—formatting compliance, answer correctness, and a thresholded IoU reward for lesion bounding-box accuracy—optimized via GRPO's group-relative advantage with KL regularization and an entropy bonus, jointly updating the vision encoder, projector, and LLM.

## Results

On the CMR-VQA test set (30 cases per class, 6 categories), CMR-RD achieves the best per-class ACC, AUC, and F1 across all six cardiac conditions. Headline numbers include ACC of 0.641 (HCM), 0.622 (DCM), 0.574 (CAM), 0.582 (LVNC), 0.611 (MI), and 0.633 (NOR), with corresponding AUC values ranging from 0.654 to 0.739. The gains are most pronounced on the rare classes: for cardiac amyloidosis (CAM, 146 training samples) and LVNC (465 samples), most baselines score 0% ACC, while CMR-RD reaches 0.574 and 0.582 respectively. Ablation confirms that combining Stage 1 (medical alignment + cold-start) with Stage 2 (GPPO) yields ACC 0.610 / F1 0.639 / AUC 0.703, versus 0.212 / 0.244 / 0.494 with neither stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMR-RD outperforms all six baselines on every per-class metric (ACC, AUC, F1) across all six cardiac conditions. The margin is largest on the rare classes: for LVNC, four of six baselines (Qwen2.5-3B, MedGemma-4B, HuatuoGPT-7B, Lingshu-7B, Seed1.5-vl) score 0% ACC, while CMR-RD reaches 0.582; for CAM, CMR-RD (ACC 0.574, AUC 0.688) beats the next-best Seed1.5-vl (ACC 0.800 on 30 test cases but AUC only 0.607) and HuatuoGPT-7B (ACC 0.555, AUC 0.529). On the most common class HCM (6,645 training samples), CMR-RD (ACC 0.641) still leads the strongest baseline HuatuoGPT-7B (ACC 0.300) by 34 percentage points. On MI, CMR-RD (ACC 0.611, AUC 0.699) surpasses Qwen2.5-3B (ACC 0.586, AUC 0.539) and InternVL3-2B (ACC 0.550, AUC 0.481). In qualitative reasoning evaluation by GPT-4o and a radiology expert, CMR-RD's diagnostic chains were rated superior in accuracy, coherence, and clinical reliability compared to all baselines, with particular strength in correctly identifying cardiac amyloidosis via diffuse LGE patterns that other models misattributed to MI or DCM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active research threads: medical VLMs (Med-Flamingo, LLaVA-Med, Lingshu, HuatuoGPT, MedGemma), RL-based post-training for reasoning (GRPO, MedVLM-R1, PathVLM-R1), and long-tail learning strategies (balanced/weighted sampling, loss reweighting). It departs from prior medical VLMs by targeting CMR specifically and by coupling a two-stage pipeline (medical alignment + cold-start CoT, then RL) with a novel Group-Phased Policy Optimization (GPPO) algorithm that embeds Thompson Sampling into the RL loop for adaptive per-class quota allocation. Unlike static rebalancing methods that are misaligned with iterative RL updates, GPPO treats sampling as a Bayesian decision process that co-evolves with the policy, and it adds an IoU-based grounding reward to anchor textual descriptions to lesion locations—something prior medical VLMs largely lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CMR interpretation is a genuine clinical bottleneck (subspecialty reader scarcity, rare-disease under-recognition), and this paper is the first to frame CMR diagnosis as an explainable VLM task with explicit reasoning chains under realistic long-tail distributions. However, the validation is limited to a single small dataset (30 test cases per class, ~13k training images total, with rare classes having only 146–465 samples), a single 2D LGE sequence, and six categories, making it unclear whether the GPPO+TS advantage generalizes to 3D volumetric CMR, additional sequences (cine, T1/T2 mapping), or larger multi-center cohorts. The paper itself acknowledges that lesion bounding boxes remain oversized and that future work should extend toward tool-augmented medical agents, leaving open whether the adaptive-sampling RL recipe transfers to other long-tailed medical imaging tasks beyond CMR.
