## Problem

Existing Video Moment Retrieval models are trained on detailed, visually-informed captions that map to a single ground-truth moment, but real users issue under-specified search queries that are shorter, more abstract, and often correspond to multiple moments. This paper quantifies the resulting performance degradation (up to 77% mAPm drop) and identifies two root causes: a language gap from the distribution shift in query specificity, and a multi-moment gap triggered by an active decoder-query collapse in DETR architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262300/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video and a text query as input, where the query is an under-specified search phrase (e.g., 'Pick up something' rather than a 16-word caption) that may correspond to multiple temporal segments. The model outputs start-end times for all matching moments with per-moment confidence scores. Evaluation uses novel multi-moment metrics (Rm and mAPm) that score each ground-truth moment independently, avoiding the masking effect that standard mAP has when a query maps to several moments.

## Method

The paper introduces three search-query benchmarks (HD-EPIC-S1/2/3, YC2-S, ANC-S) built via a two-stage LLM pipeline (Gemma-12B rewriter + validator) that progressively under-specifies captions and groups semantically similar queries into multi-moment instances. To mitigate the multi-moment gap without re-annotating data, the authors modify DETR-based VMR decoders in two ways: (1) removing the self-attention module from each decoder layer to prevent coordination collapse, where queries suppress one another, and applying NMS at inference to filter redundant predictions; and (2) applying a Bernoulli query-dropout regularizer during training that randomly zeroes a fraction of learnable decoder queries to prevent index collapse, where a fixed small subset of query indices monopolizes activation. Together these changes nearly double the number of active decoder queries and recover up to 70% of the oracle gap, improving mAPm by up to 14.82% on search queries and 21.83% on multi-moment search queries.

## Results

The proposed -SA+QD architectural modifications (removing self-attention and adding decoder-query dropout) improve mAPm by up to 14.82% and up to 21.83% on multi-moment search queries across three benchmarks. On YC2-S, CG-DETR's mAPm@0.3 rises from 26.96 to 29.40 and LD-DETR's from 30.04 to 33.09. On ANC-S, CG-DETR's mAPm@0.3 improves from 54.92 to 56.42. The method recovers approximately 70% of the performance gap to an oracle model trained directly on under-specified queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two DETR-based VMR baselines—CG-DETR and LD-DETR—against their modified -SA+QD variants across three search-query benchmarks. The -SA+QD variant consistently outperforms both baselines on every metric (Rm and mAPm at IoU 0.1/0.3/0.5) across all datasets. Gains are largest on YC2-S, where 43% of queries are multi-moment: LD-DETR improves mAPm@0.3 by +3.05 (30.04→33.09) and mAPm@0.1 by +3.97 (41.69→45.66). On HD-EPIC-S2, CG-DETR gains +3.23 mAPm@0.1 (32.15→35.38). On ANC-S, where the multi-moment gap is smaller (14.9% multi queries), improvements are more modest (e.g., CG-DETR mAPm@0.3: 54.92→56.42, LD-DETR: 56.17→56.31), confirming that the architectural fix specifically targets the multi-moment collapse. The paper also reports that -SA+QD recovers ~70% of the gap to an oracle model trained directly on under-specified queries, validating the approach without requiring re-annotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DETR-based Video Moment Retrieval (building on CG-DETR and LD-DETR) by identifying that the single-moment prior in existing caption-based benchmarks causes an 'active decoder-query collapse' when models face under-specified search queries that map to multiple moments. It departs from prior generalization studies in VMR, which have focused on vision-centric factors (action duration, temporal shifts) or surface-level language robustness, by isolating the multi-moment gap as a distinct architectural bottleneck. The proposed fix—removing self-attention and adding decoder-query dropout—draws on the query-collapse literature from object detection and 3D detection but applies it to a new cause (single-moment training prior rather than sparse one-to-one matching alone), while retaining the 1-to-1 matcher to preserve prediction diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental misalignment between how VMR models are trained (on visually-informed captions) and how users actually query (with under-specified, visually-uninformed search terms), with degradation reaching up to 77% in Rm@0.3. The three new search-query benchmarks (HD-EPIC-S, YC2-S, ANC-S) and the multi-moment metrics (Rm, mAPm) give the community tools to evaluate this gap without costly re-annotation. The language gap remains unaddressed and is deferred to future work with more advanced vision-language models, and the approach's transferability to non-DETR VMR architectures or to truly user-generated search queries (rather than LLM-rewritten captions) is an open question.
