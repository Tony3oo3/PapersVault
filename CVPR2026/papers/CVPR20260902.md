## Problem

Existing differentiable renderers such as DiffVG are constrained to vector primitives, yet the vast majority of real-world 2D assets are bitmap images. Because bitmaps are discrete, high-dimensional pixel arrays, they impose a substantial memory and computational burden that has prevented the development of a general, scalable engine for differentiably optimizing the transforms of thousands of raster primitives. This gap has made it impossible to automatically create compelling compositions (e.g., image assemblage, video synthesis) using first-order optimization on arbitrary bitmap images.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of bitmap images as primitives (each with learnable position, rotation, scale, RGB color, and opacity) along with a target image, video sequence, or text prompt (via CLIP). It produces optimized per-primitive parameters, a rendered composition, and a native layered PSD file exported at up to 4× the optimization resolution via a dedicated CUDA kernel. Spatially constrained variants accept a target alpha channel and enforce foreground-only rendering, while video mode processes frames sequentially with anti-flicker heuristics.

## Method

DiffBMP implements a custom CUDA kernel for both forward and backward passes of differentiable bitmap rendering. The forward pass applies per-primitive coordinate transformation (translation, rotation, scale), samples the bitmap via bilinear interpolation (following Spatial Transformer Networks), and composites layers using Porter-Duff alpha blending; the backward pass computes exact gradients through the chain rule with FP16 half-precision accumulation via atomicAdd for throughput. A tile-and-bin parallelization scheme (adapted from gsplat) assigns one CUDA thread block per spatial tile to achieve full pixel-level parallelism, reducing per-iteration runtime to ~2–16 ms and VRAM to 1–6 GB depending on resolution. Three optimization aids address gradient sparsity and convergence: Gaussian-blur soft rasterization enriches spatial gradients, structure-aware initialization places primitives proportionally to local image variance, and a uniform-noise canvas prevents uncovered regions. For video, sequential frame-to-frame optimization with stuck-primitive removal and unchanged-region freezing balances per-frame fidelity against temporal flicker.

## Results

The custom CUDA-16bit renderer achieves 4.3 ms forward / 5.5 ms backward per iteration at 1024×1024 (tile 32) on an RTX 3090, compared to 1393 ms / 2477 ms for the PyTorch baseline — a ~320× speedup in the forward pass and ~450× in the backward pass, while reducing VRAM from 5.0 GB to 1.6 GB. At 2048×2048, the speedup grows to ~600× (9.0 ms vs. 5405 ms forward) with VRAM dropping from 9.0 GB to 3.8 GB. In the ablation study (Table 3), enabling both soft rasterization and structure-aware initialization yields the best PSNR across all three test scenarios (25.7, 21.7, 27.4 dB), outperforming the no-helper baseline by 1.3–1.5 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffBMP is compared against two prior differentiable rendering methods. Against DiffVG [23] (the dominant vector-graphics differentiable renderer), DiffBMP is the first method capable of handling arbitrary bitmap primitives; DiffVG works well on simple SVG paths but degrades significantly on complex SVG curves (lower PSNR, drastically increased runtime) and cannot process raster images at all (Fig. 5). Against Reddy et al. [39] (the only prior bitmap differentiable renderer), DiffBMP adds opacity support, full parallelism via custom CUDA kernels, and general-purpose applicability beyond repetitive opaque pattern composition. The paper's primary quantitative comparison is internal: the custom CUDA-16bit kernel outperforms the PyTorch reference implementation by 2–3 orders of magnitude in runtime and reduces peak VRAM by 2–4×, demonstrating that the CUDA implementation is essential for scaling to thousands of primitives at high resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffBMP extends the differentiable rendering lineage from vector primitives (DiffVG and its extensions) to bitmap primitives, filling what the authors identify as a critical gap: the vast majority of real-world 2D assets are raster images, yet no scalable differentiable renderer existed for them. The differentiable sampling mechanism is inherited from Spatial Transformer Networks [14] and the limited bitmap composition work of Reddy et al. [39], while the tile-and-bin CUDA parallelization architecture is adapted from gsplat [52] for 3D Gaussian splatting. The sequential frame optimization for video follows Dynamic 3D Gaussians [28], and the noisy canvas trick is modified from Tian & Ha [48]. The key departure is the custom half-precision CUDA kernel that makes thousands of bitmap primitives tractable, something neither the PyTorch-based STN mechanism nor Reddy et al.'s non-parallelized implementation could achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making bitmap differentiable rendering practical at scale (thousands of primitives in under a minute on a consumer GPU), DiffBMP opens a new class of creative and inverse-graphics problems that were previously infeasible with first-order optimization, including intrinsic-preserving assemblage, spatially constrained video composition, and CLIP-guided text-to-composition. The PSD export and Python interface position it as a practical creative tool rather than a purely academic contribution. Key limitations include mandatory GPU usage, sensitivity to hyperparameters that can lead to local minima, and the absence of automatic hyperparameter selection. Future directions the authors highlight include extending to autoregressive and RL-based drawing, where gradient-based optimization could overcome the inherent limitations of current non-differentiable approaches.
