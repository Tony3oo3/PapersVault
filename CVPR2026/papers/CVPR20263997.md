## Problem

State-of-the-art AI weather forecasters (GraphCast, FuXi, Pangu-Weather) predict only aggregated moisture variables and cannot separate cloud microphysical species (ice, liquid water, rain, snow) that determine aviation icing risk. Traditional NWP systems can simulate these species but are computationally prohibitive at aviation-relevant resolution and suffer from systematic premature liquid-to-ice conversion that misses icing hazards. The paper addresses the fundamental challenge of forecasting sparse, discontinuous, multi-phase cloud fields with a data-driven model that respects the underlying microphysical constraints.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two consecutive 6-hourly ERA5 states at 1°×1° global resolution (181×360 grid), each comprising 117 channels formed by stacking 9 variables (Z, T, Q, U, V, CIWC, CLWC, CRWC, CSWC) across 13 pressure levels (1000–50 hPa). It produces the next 6-hour state with the same 117-channel structure, rolled out autoregressively up to 28 steps (7 days). Unusually, the cloud prediction path is conditioned on an intermediate binary cloud mask (Mask_{t+1}) that the model learns to predict, effectively decomposing the regression into a spatial localization sub-task and a concentration estimation sub-task.

## Method

AviaSafe employs a hierarchical two-branch architecture built on Swin Transformer V2. The Forecasting Backbone encodes the two input states and routes features to two decoupled decoders: a direct path for smooth background variables and a guided path for cloud species. The Physics-Informed Guidance Head constructs a 65-channel hybrid input by concatenating a 52-channel diagnostic cloud mask (thresholded from the four cloud variables) with a 13-channel Icing Condition (IC) index computed deterministically from T and Q via the August-Roche-Magnus saturation formula and a quadratic temperature factor—introducing no learnable parameters. This hybrid, together with detached multi-scale backbone features, passes through an MLP, a Swin Block, and a deconvolutional decoder to produce the future cloud mask, which is then element-wise added to the cloud-path features before final decoding. Training uses a composite objective: latitude-weighted Charbonnier L1 loss for the full forecast plus Focal Loss (γ=1.5, α=0.25) for the mask, with equal weighting (λ=1), addressing the extreme class imbalance of sparse cloud pixels.

## Results

AviaSafe outperforms the FuXi baseline in 93.7% of all variable/lead-time combinations over a 7-day forecast, with particularly large gains on cloud microphysics: CIWC50 RMSE drops from 1.059 to 0.956 (×10⁻⁸ g/kg), CLWC100 from 1.318 to 0.875, and CRWC250 from 1.010 to 0.863 (5-day averages). Against the operational ECMWF HRES system, AviaSafe achieves comparable or lower RMSE on all five 500 hPa background variables (Z, T, Q, U, V) across the full 7-day horizon, with the clearest advantages on specific humidity (Q500) and temperature (T500). For CIWC specifically, the NRMSE advantage over the baseline grows from near-zero at 1–2 days to a sustained improvement through 15 days, peaking in the 3–7 day medium-range window critical for flight planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the FuXi baseline (reimplemented with 20 Swin Transformer V2 blocks), AviaSafe achieves lower RMSE in 93.7% of variable/lead-time pairs; the largest relative gains appear on the sparse cloud fields (e.g., CLWC100 RMSE reduced by 33.6%, CRWC250 by 14.5%) while background variables also improve by 1–3%. Against the operational ECMWF HRES system, AviaSafe matches or beats RMSE on all five 500 hPa variables over 7 days, with the most pronounced advantage on Q500 and T500, and shows consistently higher ACC; however, a direct cloud-species comparison with HRES was not possible because the required HRES cloud products were unavailable during the 2024 test period. The ablation analysis further shows that the physics-informed IC prior contributes an additional 5.5% RMSE reduction on CIWC50 beyond the mask-prediction branch alone, confirming that the aviation-validated empirical formula provides meaningful spatial guidance that a data-only mask cannot replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

AviaSafe extends the FuXi/Swin Transformer V2 architecture for global weather forecasting by introducing a hierarchical, cloud-centric design that separates spatial cloud detection (via a mask predictor) from species-level concentration regression. It departs from prior AI forecasters (GraphCast, Pangu-Weather, FuXi) that predict only aggregated moisture variables by targeting four individual hydrometeor species, and from traditional NWP systems that simulate microphysics explicitly but at prohibitive computational cost. The physics-informed component repurposes the aviation-validated Icing Condition (IC) index as a deterministic, parameter-free spatial prior, a novel integration of an empirical meteorological formula into a deep learning pipeline. The CNOP-based interpretability analysis adapts a tool from meteorological predictability theory to probe the learned model's sensitivity to initial conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first data-driven model to produce global, phase-resolved cloud microphysics forecasts (CIWC, CLWC, CRWC, CSWC) at aviation-relevant temporal resolution, directly addressing a gap where current AI forecasters and operational NWP products (e.g., ECMWF HRES) cannot distinguish ice from liquid water for icing-risk assessment. Key limitations include the coarse 1° spatial resolution, the 7-day lead time ceiling, reliance on ERA5 reanalysis rather than operational analyses, and the inability to directly benchmark cloud variables against ECMWF HRES (products unavailable during the test period). Open questions include whether the hierarchical mask-then-quantify design generalizes to higher resolutions, whether the IC prior remains effective under different microphysics regimes, and how the model performs when trained on or assimilated with satellite/radar observations.
