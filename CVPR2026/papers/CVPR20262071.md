## Problem

Existing mono-to-stereo video conversion methods either rely on explicit depth estimation and warping (introducing disocclusion artifacts and binocular rivalry from VAE compression) or, in the case of the recent warping-free Eye2Eye, lack any mechanism to control the 3D disparity strength and are prohibitively slow due to multi-step pixel-space refinement. No prior method simultaneously achieves warping-free direct synthesis, intuitive scalar control over disparity, and high-fidelity detail transfer that minimizes binocular rivalry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular left-eye video (N=16 frames at 512×512×3) and a single scalar disparity factor δ (in pixels, representing the median left-to-right disparity) as input, and produces the corresponding right-eye video in a single feed-forward pass. The scalar δ acts as an intuitive '3D strength knob' at inference time, allowing the user to generate stereo content for any disparity distribution without retraining or camera calibration. The guided decoder additionally consumes the original left video as a high-resolution guidance signal during decoding.

## Method

Elastic3D builds on Stable Video Diffusion with 1-step denoising to directly synthesize the right-view latent in a single feed-forward step, conditioned on the left-view latent and a disparity token τ(δ) injected into spatial attention layers. The key architectural innovation is a guided VAE decoder that reformulates decoding as a guided latent upsampling task: an extractor network (initialized from the VAE encoder) produces multi-scale guidance feature pyramids from the left video, and a lightweight epipolar attention module at each upsampling block restricts cross-attention to the corresponding horizontal epipolar line, reducing complexity from O(H²W²) to O(HW²) and bypassing the VAE's 1:48 compression bottleneck. The decoder is trained separately as a plug-and-play module with L1+LPIPS reconstruction loss, making it interchangeable with third-party synthesis cores. Training employs a composite loss (L2-latent, L1, SSIM, LPIPS) with a data augmentation strategy that randomly scales ground-truth disparity maps to generate synthetic right views, enabling the model to generalize across disparity ranges.

## Results

On the Stereo4D test set, Elastic3D achieves 26.1 dB PSNR, 0.913 SSIM, and 0.176 LPIPS, outperforming the second-best method M2SVid by +1.5 dB PSNR and 15% lower LPIPS. It also attains the lowest disparity error (1.24 px) and matchability error (27.8), indicating superior geometric correctness and stereoscopic fidelity. On the out-of-distribution iPhone Spatial Video set (19.2 mm baseline vs. 63 mm training), Elastic3D still leads in SSIM (0.890), LPIPS (0.193), and matchability error (26.5), while M2SVid edges it out by 0.4 dB in PSNR (22.9 vs. 22.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Elastic3D wins on all seven metrics against every baseline on all three evaluation sets. Against M2SVid (the strongest warp-based competitor), Elastic3D gains +1.5 dB PSNR, reduces LPIPS from 0.206 to 0.176, and cuts matchability error by 30% (39.6 → 27.8), demonstrating that the guided VAE decoder effectively eliminates the binocular rivalry caused by VAE compression. Against Eye2Eye (the only other warping-free method), Elastic3D achieves +5.0 dB PSNR and halves the disparity error (2.45 → 1.24), largely because Eye2Eye's fixed implicit baseline produces spatially misaligned views and incorrect depth ordering. Against StereoCrafter and SVG, both of which rely on multi-step latent diffusion without task-specific guidance, Elastic3D improves PSNR by +2.5 and +3.2 dB respectively, with substantially lower LPIPS (0.176 vs. 0.258). Against ReStereo, which combines video generation with a restoration stage, Elastic3D avoids texture hallucination (LPIPS 0.176 vs. 0.307) while maintaining better geometric accuracy (Disp. err 1.24 vs. 1.95). The only metric where Elastic3D does not lead is PSNR on the iPhone set, where M2SVid's inpainting-based approach retains a 0.4 dB advantage, though Elastic3D still dominates in perceptual and geometric metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

Elastic3D builds on Stable Video Diffusion's latent diffusion backbone and the 1-step denoising trick to create a feed-forward stereo synthesis model, positioning itself between two prior paradigms: warp-based pipelines (M2SVid, StereoCrafter) that rely on explicit monocular depth estimation and Eye2Eye, which is warping-free but lacks 3D-strength control and is prohibitively slow. Its two key departures are a scalar disparity conditioning mechanism (analogous to depth-map scaling in warp methods but learned end-to-end) and a guided VAE decoder with epipolar attention that re-injects high-frequency source details, drawing on guided super-resolution and epipolar-transformer literature. The paper also contributes a standardized black-box evaluation protocol, filling a gap where prior methods either leaked target-view information or relied on hard-to-scale user studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The guided VAE decoder is a modular, plug-and-play component that addresses a fundamental limitation of all latent diffusion stereo methods (the VAE information bottleneck causing binocular rivalry), and its demonstrated transfer to M2SVid without retraining suggests broad applicability beyond this specific pipeline. The disparity conditioning enables practical 3D-strength control on uncalibrated in-the-wild data, which is essential for content creators and end-users. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to non-rectified or multi-view settings, whether the 1-step feed-forward formulation can be extended to longer sequences or higher resolutions without quality degradation, and whether the guided decoder's epipolar constraint remains effective when the rectification assumption is relaxed.
