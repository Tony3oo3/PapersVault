## Problem

Video dataset condensation is uniquely challenging because spatial appearance and temporal motion are inseparably coupled in real-world actions, yet the only prior method (Wang et al., CVPR 2024) relies on a static/dynamic disentanglement paradigm that decomposes video into separate components optimized in distinct stages. This decomposition misrepresents intrinsic spatiotemporal coupling and, combined with a fixed frame budget, either wastes capacity on redundant frames or fails to capture complex non-linear dynamics where they actually occur.

## Input / Output

The method consumes large-scale video datasets (miniUCF and HMDB51 sampled at 16 frames, 112×112; Kinetics-400 and SSv2 at 8 frames, 64×64) and produces a condensed synthetic set where each video is represented by a variable number of trainable key-frames (starting at 2, growing adaptively) plus their temporal indices, with all intermediate frames reconstructed via linear interpolation. Unlike prior methods that store a fixed 16-frame sequence per video, PRISM's output size is data-dependent and typically far smaller (e.g., ~20 MB vs. 94 MB at 1 VPC on miniUCF), with the exact frame count determined by the gradient-guided insertion process during training.

## Method

PRISM treats each video as a unified spatiotemporal structure, initializing with only two key-frames (first and last) and reconstructing all intermediate frames via linear interpolation. During training, it monitors the cosine similarity between the task-loss gradient of each interpolated frame and the gradients of its two adjacent key-frames; when both similarities fall below a threshold (ε=0), the frame is promoted to a trainable key-frame, resolving a theoretical loss-descent conflict (Lemma 1) where no first-order endpoint update can reduce the intermediate loss. This insertion is gated by warm-up and cool-down phases (20% of iterations each) to avoid premature or late-stage insertions, and all candidates within an iteration are evaluated against a fixed key-frame set before simultaneous insertion. The optimization objective is a distribution-matching loss over a miniC3D backbone, with gradients backpropagated exclusively to the sparse key-frame set while interpolated frames are updated indirectly, and synthetic videos are initialized from Gaussian noise rather than real frames to enforce holistic synthesis from scratch.

## Results

On MiniUCF (16 frames, 112×112), PRISM achieves 17.9% top-1 accuracy at 1 VPC, outperforming the prior video condensation method Wang et al. (17.5%) and the image-based DM baseline (15.3%), while requiring only 20 MB of storage (0.2% of the full dataset) versus 94 MB for Wang et al. On HMDB51, PRISM reaches 7.5% at 1 VPC, surpassing DM (6.1%) and Wang et al. (6.0%) by a clear margin. At 5 VPC on MiniUCF, PRISM scores 28.0% with 133 MB storage (1.4%), compared to 586 MB (6.0%) for all dense-frame baselines. On the large-scale Kinetics-400 (8 frames, 64×64), PRISM achieves 7.1% top-5 at 1 VPC, beating Wang et al. (6.3%) and DM (6.3%), though DM leads at 5 VPC (9.1% vs. 8.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM outperforms the only prior video-specific method, Wang et al. (CVPR 2024, static/dynamic disentanglement), on nearly all settings: 17.9% vs. 17.5% on MiniUCF 1 VPC, 10.5% vs. 8.2% on HMDB51 5 VPC, and 7.1% vs. 6.3% on Kinetics-400 1 VPC, while using roughly 5× less storage (20 MB vs. 94 MB at MiniUCF 1 VPC). Against the image-based distribution matching baseline DM (WACV 2023), PRISM wins on all HMDB51 settings and most MiniUCF settings, but DM edges ahead on Kinetics-400 at 5 VPC (9.1% vs. 8.1%), likely due to the stability of image-based initialization in the extremely low-frame regime. Among coreset selection baselines, Herding (ICML 2009) is the strongest, beating PRISM on MiniUCF at 10 VPC (33.7% vs. 31.0%), but PRISM surpasses Herding on every HMDB51 setting and on MiniUCF at 5 and 1 VPC. K-Center (1985) is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing PRISM by 4–15 points across all configurations. Overall, PRISM establishes state-of-the-art storage efficiency (0.2–5.8% of full dataset) while matching or exceeding the accuracy of all dense-frame and disentangled baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM departs from the sole prior video dataset condensation method (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), which decomposes video into static content and auxiliary motion signals in a two-stage disentanglement pipeline. Rather than inheriting the image condensation literature's gradient-matching or distribution-matching objectives as-is, PRISM repurposes gradient information for a fundamentally different purpose: identifying *where* in the temporal axis to allocate representational capacity via a loss-descent conflict lemma. The linear interpolation between sparse key-frames draws on video interpolation literature, but the adaptive insertion criterion driven by gradient misalignment is a new mechanism not present in either image or prior video condensation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× storage reduction (20 MB vs. 94 MB at 1 VPC on miniUCF) while matching or exceeding prior accuracy makes video dataset condensation practically viable for resource-constrained settings, and the non-linear storage growth with VPC offers a scaling advantage absent in fixed-frame methods. However, the approach is currently validated only on short clips (8–16 frames) and small-to-medium benchmarks; the authors acknowledge instability for sequences exceeding 16 frames and unexplored applicability to generative or fine-grained semantic tasks. Open questions include whether the gradient-misalignment criterion generalizes to longer, more complex temporal structures and whether quantitative motion-fidelity metrics can be incorporated to evaluate synthesized dynamics beyond classification accuracy.
