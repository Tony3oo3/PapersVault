## Problem

Photorealistic 3D scene representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting produce millions of unstructured primitives that are difficult to edit semantically. Existing semantic decomposition methods on point-based representations suffer from unlabeled primitives in object interiors, overlapping primitives causing semantic ambiguity, and reliance on post-hoc heuristics like convex-hull construction that restrict extraction to convex shapes. The paper addresses how to achieve clean, spatially consistent semantic decomposition that enables practical editing (extraction, removal, insertion) without these artifacts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images, automatically generated DEVA segmentation masks (produced in 'everything mode' for all training views with densified novel views for cross-view consistency), and a COLMAP sparse point cloud for initialization. The output is a structured 3D Voronoi mesh where each cell carries a 16-dimensional piecewise-constant, view-independent identity encoding alongside standard density and spherical-harmonic appearance parameters. Because the representation is a non-overlapping volumetric partition with explicit face adjacency, editing operations (extraction, removal, insertion) operate directly on cell subsets and require no post-processing heuristics.

## Method

Semantic Foam extends Radiant Foam's differentiable Voronoi mesh by augmenting each cell with a learnable 16-dimensional identity encoding vector, rendered along rays via the standard volume-rendering formulation and supervised against 2D DEVA masks through a cross-entropy loss. The key technical contribution is a total-variation loss defined on the Voronoi adjacency graph: a face-area-weighted L1 penalty between each cell's identity feature and those of its face-sharing neighbors, which enforces spatial smoothness and suppresses noisy features in under-supervised regions like object interiors. This explicit mesh connectivity is what distinguishes the approach from Gaussian-based methods, where neighborhood regularization is computationally expensive and boundaries are inherently diffuse. Training follows Radiant Foam's 20k-iteration protocol with identical densification/pruning (cloning identity features to new cells), and the TV loss weight is exponentially decayed (×0.99 per 1000 iterations from iteration 2000) to balance smoothness against boundary sharpness.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, Semantic Foam achieves 0.82 mIoU and 0.93 mAcc, outperforming SAGA (0.81 mIoU) and Gaussian Grouping (0.76 mIoU). On LERF-Mask, it reaches 0.85 mIoU, a substantial margin over Gaussian Grouping (0.76) and SAGA (0.72). On LLFF, Semantic Foam ties Gaussian Grouping at 0.84 mIoU while surpassing SAGA (0.72) and LabelGS (0.69). The paper notes that Gaussian-based baselines often inflate mAcc through over-segmentation, whereas Semantic Foam maintains sharper boundaries reflected in consistently higher mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semantic Foam outperforms all three Gaussian-based baselines on mIoU across the three datasets. Against SAGA, it gains +1% mIoU on Mip-NeRF 360 (0.82 vs 0.81), +13% on LERF-Mask (0.85 vs 0.72), and +12% on LLFF (0.84 vs 0.72). Against Gaussian Grouping, the margins are +6% on Mip-NeRF 360 (0.82 vs 0.76), +9% on LERF-Mask (0.85 vs 0.76), and a tie on LLFF (0.84 vs 0.84). Against LabelGS, the improvements are the largest: +12% on Mip-NeRF 360, +31% on LERF-Mask, and +15% on LLFF. On mAcc, Semantic Foam is competitive but not uniformly best — SAGA achieves higher mAcc on Mip-NeRF 360 (0.97) and LERF-Mask (0.98) — which the authors attribute to over-segmentation in Gaussian methods that inflates pixel accuracy while degrading boundary precision (mIoU). Qualitatively, the paper shows that Gaussian Grouping's convex-hull extraction fails on non-convex objects, whereas Semantic Foam's Voronoi adjacency enables clean extraction of arbitrary shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Radiant Foam, a Voronoi-based volumetric representation for real-time differentiable ray tracing, by adding per-cell identity encoding features supervised with 2D DEVA masks. It departs from the dominant 3DGS-based semantic segmentation line (Gaussian Grouping, SAGA, LabelGS) by exploiting the explicit Voronoi adjacency graph to impose a total-variation spatial regularization, addressing the unstructured-primitive artifacts (convex-hull extraction, floating Gaussians, semantic ambiguity) that plague Gaussian-based methods. Conceptually, it also connects to the NeRF-based semantic segmentation tradition (SemanticNeRF, LaTeRF, Distilled Feature Fields) but replaces the continuous neural field with a piecewise-constant volumetric mesh, enabling direct spatial decomposition without post-processing heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in interactive 3D graphics: photorealistic reconstructions from 3DGS or NeRF are difficult to edit because they lack semantic structure. By showing that a structured Voronoi mesh with per-cell identity features enables clean object extraction, removal, and insertion without convex-hull heuristics or floating artifacts, it offers a viable path toward editable scene representations. Key limitations remain: supervision is still 2D-derived (DEVA), which can produce inconsistent cross-view signals, and the per-primitive evaluation of identity features is misaligned with the volume-rendered supervision during training. Open questions include whether 3D-aware foundation models could provide more consistent supervision, and whether the Voronoi-based approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes.
