## Problem

Diffusion-based SR models built on Stable Diffusion (e.g., SD2.1-base) are natively limited to ~512² resolution, producing severely degraded outputs when the target upsampling ratio (e.g., ×8) or target resolution (e.g., 2048²) exceeds this native capacity. Scaling to larger generative models (SD3.5, FLUX.1-dev) to handle higher resolutions incurs prohibitive computational and GPU memory costs, making high-resolution SR infeasible on resource-constrained hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263128/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (e.g., 128² or 256²) and a text prompt as input, and produces a high-resolution super-resolved image (e.g., 512², 1024², or 2048²). The total upsampling ratio M×N is decomposed into two sequential stages: the input is first ×M upsampled and processed by the first-stage LoRA, then the intermediate output is ×N bilinearly upsampled and processed by the second-stage LoRA. For inputs exceeding 512 pixels, tiled diffusion is applied during inference to handle arbitrary spatial dimensions.

## Method

TUDSR decomposes a single high-ratio upsampling into two lower-ratio stages, each operating within the native resolution of the SD2.1-base backbone. Stage 1 trains a LoRA adapter at R-resolution (e.g., 512²) using a one-step GAN formulation where the generator is the LoRA-finetuned diffusion model performing single-timestep denoising, and the discriminator is a frozen DINOv3-ViT-B backbone with trainable multi-level heads at layers 3, 6, and 9. Stage 2 freezes the first-stage LoRA, feeds its output through ×N bilinear upsampling, and trains a second LoRA on R-resolution chunks of the NR-resolution image using a for-loop chunked strategy that backpropagates gradients per chunk to limit GPU memory. The training objective combines an edge-aware dists perceptual loss (replacing LPIPS to reduce artifacts), a multi-level BCE adversarial loss with soft labels, and an MAE term. At inference, only the two LoRA adapters are swapped within a single backbone instance, avoiding duplicate model loading.

## Results

On the ×4 SR task, TUDSR-S (M2N2) achieves state-of-the-art perceptual quality across all four real-world benchmarks, e.g., CLIPIQA 0.7315 and MUSIQ 71.93 on RealLQ250, and MANIQA 0.6786 on RealSR. On the more challenging ×8 SR task, TUDSR-S (M4N2) substantially outperforms all one-step baselines, reaching CLIPIQA 0.7222, MUSIQ 63.21, and MANIQA 0.5847 on RealLQ250 (256²→2048²), where competing methods drop sharply (e.g., SinSR falls to MUSIQ 40.10). TUDSR-S also achieves the fastest inference among the compared one-step models on low-resolution tasks (0.0596 s for 128²→512²) and competitive speed on high-resolution tasks (2.28 s for 256²→2048²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TUDSR-S achieves the best or near-best results on the majority of perceptual metrics (CLIPIQA, CLIPIQA+, LIQE, MUSIQ, MANIQA, NIQE) across all four ×4 benchmarks, with the most consistent wins over PiSA-SR, OSEDiff, and InvSR. On RealSR, TUDSR-S leads in MUSIQ (70.24 vs. 70.15 for PiSA-SR) and MANIQA (0.6786 vs. 0.6628 for InvSR), while trailing slightly on LPIPS (0.3217 vs. 0.2604 for StableSR), indicating a fidelity-perceptual trade-off. On the ×8 task, the gap widens dramatically: TUDSR-S outperforms the next-best one-step model (OSEDiff) by +0.043 CLIPIQA and +7.4 MUSIQ on RealLQ250, while SinSR and ResShift collapse (MUSIQ drops to ~40–53). Multi-step models (StableSR, SeeSR, DiffBIR, ResShift) are only compared on ×4 due to prohibitive inference cost (>10 min per ×8 image). Overall, TUDSR-S is the only method that maintains strong perceptual quality at both ×4 and ×8 scales without requiring a larger backbone model.

## Relation to Prior Work

TUDSR extends the one-step diffusion-based SR line (SinSR, OSEDiff, PiSA-SR, InvSR) that fine-tunes Stable Diffusion via LoRA, but departs from the single-stage paradigm by decomposing high-ratio upsampling into two sequential LoRA adapters within the same backbone. It also diverges from the pure diffusion-distillation training objective by adopting a GAN formulation with a frozen DINOv3-ViT-B feature extractor and multi-level discriminator heads, drawing on the stability benefits of pre-trained priors on both generator and discriminator sides. The chunked for-loop training strategy for the second stage is a practical departure from standard tiled-diffusion inference, addressing GPU memory constraints during training rather than inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TUDSR provides a practical pathway to 2048² super-resolution from a 512²-native model without the prohibitive compute and memory costs of training on larger architectures (e.g., FLUX.1-dev), making high-resolution SR accessible on resource-constrained hardware. The framework is explicitly designed to generalize to larger backbones and higher target resolutions (4096²), positioning it as a scalable strategy rather than a one-off trick. Open questions include whether the chunked training introduces subtle seam artifacts at chunk boundaries in complex scenes, whether the two-stage decomposition degrades gracefully when the first stage is under-trained, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to non-SD backbones with different latent-space structures.
