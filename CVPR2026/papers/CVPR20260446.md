## Problem

Unsupervised rPPG methods rely on large unlabeled video corpora, but no existing framework assesses whether a video actually contains an extractable physiological signal before it is used for training. General-purpose VQA metrics target human perceptual quality, and rPPG-specific metrics like SNR are easily fooled by non-physiological periodic artifacts, leaving a critical gap: a task-specific, pre-emptive quality assessment that verifies both signal integrity and scene-level suitability for rPPG.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes raw facial video clips (resampled to 30 fps) from large unlabeled pools such as CAS(ME)² and Celeb-DF (v2). It outputs a per-video scalar quality score Q(v_i) in [0,1] that fuses a signal-level consensus SNR with an MLLM-based scene interference score, and then uses those scores to produce a curated target training set of a specified size (e.g., 140 videos) via two-stage adaptive sampling. The MLLM branch additionally requires text prompts describing each noise dimension, making the pipeline jointly video-and-text conditioned.

## Method

rPPG-VQA is a dual-branch, training-free assessment framework. The signal-level branch extracts rPPG signals with seven diverse conventional algorithms (GREEN, ICA, CHROM, LGI, PBV, POS, OMIT), computes per-method SNR from power spectral density, and fuses them via a consensus mechanism that weights each method by (a) frequency consistency—deviation from a RANSAC-estimated consensus heart rate mapped through a Gaussian kernel—and (b) spectral correlation—mean squared Pearson correlation of the method's PSD with all others. The scene-level branch queries Qwen3-VL with four task-specific prompts (head movement, illumination, skin optics, camera artifacts) to obtain ordinal scores, with a frame-rate compensation factor applied to the camera dimension. The two branch scores are IQR-truncated, min-max normalized, and combined as a weighted average (α = 0.8 favoring signal quality). A two-stage adaptive sampling (TAS) strategy then converts scores into a training set: first, ranking-based filtering retains the top η·|D_tgt| videos; second, duration-aware probabilistic sampling draws clips with a softmax probability modulated by a logarithmic duration factor, followed by stochastic rounding to yield integer clip counts.

## Results

On the PURE test set, the proposed rPPG-VQA quality criterion combined with TAS sampling achieves MAE 0.47, RMSE 0.74, and R 1.00 for Contrast-Phys, substantially outperforming random sampling (MAE 7.86, RMSE 15.02, R 0.39) and the best baseline combination WRS+SNR (MAE 0.88, RMSE 1.76, R 0.99). On BUAA-MIHR, the same configuration yields MAE 2.19, RMSE 3.99, and R 0.96 for Contrast-Phys, compared to MAE 7.69 and R 0.41 for random sampling. Across all three unsupervised rPPG methods (Gideon21, Contrast-Phys, SiNC) and both test sets, rPPG-VQA + TAS consistently achieves the lowest MAE and RMSE and highest Pearson correlation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three quality criteria (COVER, SNR, IPR) and three sampling strategies (Top/Bottom K, WRS) as baselines. On the PURE dataset with Contrast-Phys, rPPG-VQA + TAS (MAE 0.47) outperforms the strongest baseline WRS + SNR (MAE 0.88) by 47% in MAE, and outperforms WRS + COVER (MAE 1.64) by 71%. The general-purpose VQA metric COVER is consistently the weakest quality criterion across all sampling strategies, confirming that perceptual quality does not align with physiological signal integrity. The rPPG-specific IPR metric frequently underperforms (e.g., MAE 4.89 with WRS vs. 0.71 for rPPG-VQA), because it cannot distinguish signal harmonics from noise harmonics. For sampling, TAS with rPPG-VQA beats WRS + rPPG-VQA (MAE 0.47 vs. 0.71) and Top/Bottom K + rPPG-VQA (MAE 0.47 vs. 1.36), demonstrating that the two-stage duration-aware process is critical. On BUAA-MIHR, the same pattern holds: TAS + rPPG-VQA (MAE 2.19) beats WRS + SNR (MAE 2.37) and WRS + rPPG-VQA (MAE 2.50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of general-purpose video quality assessment (VQA) and rPPG signal processing, arguing that neither lineage is adequate for its purpose. It departs from perceptual VQA (PSNR, SSIM, COVER) by reframing quality as physiological signal integrity rather than human-visual fidelity, and departs from post-hoc rPPG signal metrics (SNR, IPR) by introducing a pre-emptive, multi-method consensus mechanism and scene-level MLLM reasoning. Conceptually, it repurposes seven conventional rPPG algorithms (GREEN, CHROM, POS, etc.) as quality estimators rather than final signal extractors, and applies a multimodal LLM (Qwen3-VL) to scene interference detection—a use case not previously explored in the rPPG literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that naive data augmentation in unsupervised rPPG can be catastrophically harmful (MAE 31+ on Contrast-Phys) while curated 'in-the-wild' data yields dramatic gains, reframing data curation as a first-class research problem rather than an implementation detail. The MLLM-based scene assessment is a promising but underexplored direction; its modest ablation contribution (α=0.8 optimal vs. α=1.0) raises open questions about whether more capable or fine-tuned MLLMs would close the gap. Limitations include evaluation on only three unsupervised methods, two source datasets, and two test benchmarks, leaving scalability to truly large-scale video corpora and non-facial rPPG scenarios unaddressed.
