## Problem

Large video diffusion models can generate vivid, realistic talking portrait videos with rich dynamics (lip motion, expressions, head/torso movement, hair, lighting), but their high computational cost—several minutes for a 5-second clip—makes them impractical for real-time interactive applications. No existing method simultaneously achieves real-time streamable generation, high visual fidelity across a wide torso region, and precise audio-visual synchronization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263282/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a speech audio signal and one to three reference portrait images as input, producing a 512×512 talking portrait video at 42 FPS on a single H100 GPU. The reference images serve a dual role: they condition the latent generator and are injected into the VAE decoder to guide reconstruction. Generation is streamable and blockwise autoregressive, supporting arbitrary-length video with KV caching across windows of 128 frames (32 latent frames per block of 4).

## Method

The framework combines a reference-guided causal video VAE with a blockwise autoregressive Rectified Flow Transformer. The VAE achieves a 768× compression ratio (64× spatial, 4× temporal, 64 channels) by injecting reference image features via cross-attention in the decoder, allowing the encoder to focus on dynamic motion rather than static appearance; it further extends DC-AE's residual auto-encoding to video with separate temporal and spatial residual steps (CR-VA) while preserving causality by handling the first frame independently. The generator is a 24-block Transformer with 3D RoPE that predicts video latents block-by-block using blockwise causal attention (full attention within each 4-frame block, restricted to preceding blocks across blocks), trained with teacher forcing and flow-matching loss. At inference, KV caching and window-to-window context reuse enable seamless streaming, and classifier-free guidance (scale 2) is applied to both audio and reference conditions over 12 denoising steps.

## Results

On HDTF, the proposed method (M=1) achieves the best lip-sync confidence (S_C = 8.943) and lowest lip-sync distance (S_D = 6.286), as well as the best head-pose alignment (CAPP = 0.699). On PortraitOneMin, it similarly leads in S_C (8.537), S_D (6.619), and CAPP (0.648). With three reference images (M=3), FVD drops to 43.27 on HDTF and 73.69 on PortraitOneMin, surpassing the strongest baseline Sonic (43.92 / 95.05). The method generates 512×512 video at 42.3 FPS on a single H100 GPU, over 25× faster than all compared diffusion-based baselines (0.27–13.3 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest baseline Sonic, the proposed method (M=1) wins on all lip-sync and head-pose metrics on both benchmarks (e.g., S_C 8.943 vs 8.799, S_D 6.286 vs 6.602, CAPP 0.699 vs 0.689 on HDTF), while Sonic retains a slight FVD edge on HDTF (43.92 vs 62.30); however, with three references (M=3) the gap reverses (43.27 vs 43.92). On PortraitOneMin, the proposed method dominates Sonic across every metric including FVD (91.96 vs 95.05). Against the Hallo series (Hallo, Hallo2, Hallo3), the proposed method achieves substantially better lip-sync (S_C 8.94 vs 7.26–7.55), head-pose alignment (CAPP 0.70 vs 0.24–0.34), and FVD on HDTF. EchoMIMIC and FantasyTalking are clearly outperformed on all quality metrics. The most striking advantage is speed: 42.3 FPS versus 0.27–13.3 FPS for all baselines, a 3–157× speedup, enabling real-time streaming that none of the compared diffusion-based methods support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the residual auto-encoding paradigm of DC-AE to the video domain with causal constraints, and builds its generator on the Rectified Flow Transformer architecture. It departs from prior efficient video generation work (which typically relies on distillation or discrete tokenization) by instead achieving real-time speed through a reference-guided deep-compression VAE (768× compression vs. ~48× in standard video diffusion) combined with blockwise autoregressive generation and KV caching. The key conceptual departure from generic video diffusion is the observation that talking portraits have a fixed dominant subject, allowing reference images to be injected into the VAE decoder so the latent space encodes dynamics rather than appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 42 FPS at 512×512 on a single GPU opens the door to interactive, streaming talking-portrait applications (virtual companions, real-time avatars, interactive education) that were previously restricted to offline generation taking minutes per 5-second clip. The reference-guided compression insight is domain-specific but potentially transferable to other fixed-subject video tasks. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes beyond single-subject portraits to multi-person scenes, whether the 330-hour training corpus is sufficient for robust identity preservation across diverse demographics, and whether the quality gap versus much larger foundation models narrows or widens at higher resolutions or longer durations.
