## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models suffer from two critical limitations: incomplete suppression (the model still occasionally generates the target concept) and poor semantic selectivity (erasure degrades generation quality for unrelated concepts). Plug-in adapter approaches further require high-quality paired training data and experience parameter conflicts when erasing multiple concepts simultaneously, necessitating complex isolation mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261558/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a set of target concept–surrogate concept pairs as input. At inference, the prompt is encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, then routed through the top-k most relevant Mapper modules (selected by cosine similarity between the prompt embedding and all target concept embeddings) before being fed to the frozen U-Net. The output is a generated image in which the target concept has been replaced by the surrogate, while all other semantic content is preserved with near-zero distortion.

## Method

MapRoute introduces lightweight Mapper modules (three linear+GELU layers plus LayerNorm) inserted after the frozen text encoder, each trained via a two-stage scheme: a self-supervised stage that learns an identity mapping over a 15,000-concept dictionary (achieving ~1e-6 MSE), followed by a concept-specific stage that maps the target embedding to an arbitrary surrogate embedding while preserving all other dictionary and person-name embeddings via auxiliary losses. A key insight is that the surrogate concept need not be semantically related to the target (e.g., 'automobile' can map to 'Van Gogh'), eliminating the need for carefully curated paired data. To handle multi-concept erasure without catastrophic forgetting, an input-driven top-k routing mechanism dynamically selects and serially applies only the Mappers most relevant to the current prompt, avoiding the parameter conflicts that arise from chaining all adapters.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 object erasure (average across 10 classes), MapRoute achieves near-perfect erasure with ACC_e = 0.50% and ACC_g = 0.92%, while preserving non-target concepts at ACC_s = 99.53%, yielding an overall harmonic score H_o = 99.37. For celebrity erasure (50 celebrities), MapRoute achieves GCD_e = 0.00% and GCD_s = 90.16% (H_c = 94.83), and for artistic style erasure it attains the highest H_a = 12.21 on Brent Heighton. Training a single Mapper takes only 13.3 minutes on one A100 GPU, with 6-second inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MapRoute decisively outperforms all seven baselines on the primary H_o metric. The closest competitor, MACE (CVPR 2024), achieves H_o = 92.61 with ACC_e = 8.49%, still leaving residual target-concept generation, whereas MapRoute reduces ACC_e to 0.50% and H_o to 99.37. UCE (WACV 2024) and ESD-u (ICCV 2023) achieve moderate erasure (ACC_e ≈ 13–18%) but sacrifice generalization (ACC_g ≈ 16–23%) and preservation (ACC_s drops to 86.76% for ESD-u). SPM (CVPR 2024) and FMN (CVPR 2024) fail to erase effectively (ACC_e > 95%), indicating their adapter-based approaches cannot fully suppress target concepts. RECE (ECCV 2024) and GLoCE (CVPR 2025) show intermediate performance but still exhibit non-negligible residual erasure (ACC_e ≈ 21.55% and GCD_e > 0, respectively). In celebrity erasure, MapRoute achieves GCD_e = 0.00% across all tested celebrities, matching or exceeding MACE (GCD_e = 0.00–9.50%) while preserving unrelated celebrities at 90.16% versus MACE's 81.83%.

## Relation to Prior Work

MapRoute departs from the three dominant concept-erasure paradigms (loss-based fine-tuning, closed-form projection, and plug-in adapters) by operating exclusively in the text encoder's embedding space with a lightweight feedforward module, leaving the diffusion backbone entirely untouched. Its two-stage training (identity mapping followed by target-to-surrogate mapping) draws on the concept-dictionary idea from Splice, while the top-k cosine-similarity routing mechanism for multi-concept erasure is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior adapter-based methods like SPM or Receler. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the demonstration that the surrogate concept can be arbitrary (even semantically unrelated), breaking the dependency on carefully curated target-surrogate pairs that methods like SPM require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, routing-based design offers a practical path to scalable, on-demand concept erasure: new sensitive concepts can be added by training a single lightweight Mapper (13.3 min on one A100) without retraining or re-deploying the full model, which is attractive for dynamic safety-policy updates. However, all experiments are conducted on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with relatively small evaluation sets (CIFAR-10 objects, 200 celebrities/artists), leaving open whether the linear-mapping assumption and routing mechanism generalize to larger architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models) or to more semantically entangled concepts. A key open question is robustness: whether adversarially crafted prompts can bypass the top-k routing or whether the identity-mapping guarantee degrades under distribution shift.
