## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods under adverse weather either target a single degradation type (haze, rain, or snow) or rely on two-stage restoration-then-reconstruction pipelines that break multi-view consistency. Real-world outdoor scenes frequently exhibit hybrid weather where continuous media (haze) and discrete particles (rain, snow) co-occur, creating entangled degradations that no prior unified framework can jointly model while preserving geometric fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261575/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N degraded multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and requires no paired clean references or external restoration models at test time. It outputs a clean 3D Gaussian scene representation (centers, covariances, opacities, colors) plus a per-view particulate residual layer, from which clean novel views can be rendered. Weather degradations in experiments are synthesized via OneRestore for hazy/snowy/hybrid settings and taken from the RainyScape synthetic rain dataset.

## Method

NimbusGS decomposes weather degradation into a view-consistent Continuous Scattering Field (a voxelized extinction field β(x) integrated along rays to yield transmission T and airlight P via discrete radiative transfer) and a view-dependent Particulate Residual Layer (per-view residuals R extracted by subtracting the continuous-medium rendering from the input during a geometry-initialization warm-up stage). A Geometry-Guided Gradient Scaling mechanism adaptively rescales per-Gaussian gradients using normalized depth, projected radius, and median-normalized reconstruction error to counteract the under-densification of distant geometry caused by visibility attenuation. Training proceeds in two stages: a brief warm-up with reduced scale learning rate to stabilize geometry and initialize the particulate residual, followed by joint optimization of Gaussians, the extinction field, and periodically refreshed residuals under a photometric loss with total-variation regularization on the extinction field.

## Results

NimbusGS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four weather settings. On hazy scenes, it reaches 20.79 dB PSNR / 0.776 SSIM / 0.190 LPIPS, surpassing the best baseline (WaterSplatting) by 4.64 dB in PSNR. On rainy scenes it attains 32.41 dB PSNR / 0.904 SSIM, a 2.54 dB gain over RainyScape. On the most challenging hybrid-weather setting (haze + rain + snow), NimbusGS scores 22.25 dB PSNR / 0.742 SSIM / 0.202 LPIPS, outperforming the restoration-then-reconstruction pipeline (OneRestore+3DGS) by 2.16 dB in PSNR and 0.081 in SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NimbusGS outperforms all eight baselines across every weather setting it is evaluated on. On hazy scenes, it beats WaterSplatting (16.15 dB) and SeaSplat (15.57 dB) by 4.64 and 5.22 dB in PSNR respectively, while plain 3DGS scores only 14.57 dB. On rainy scenes, NimbusGS (32.41 dB) surpasses the strongest rainy-specific method RainyScape (29.87 dB) by 2.54 dB, and outperforms WeatherGS (25.57 dB) and RobustSplat (26.88 dB) by 6.84 and 5.53 dB. On snowy scenes, it leads RainyScape (22.34 dB) by 0.93 dB and RobustSplat (21.29 dB) by 1.98 dB. On the hybrid-weather benchmark, NimbusGS (22.25 dB) exceeds the restoration-then-reconstruction pipeline OneRestore+3DGS (20.09 dB) by 2.16 dB, and dominates all 3D-native baselines (3DGS 15.29 dB, RobustSplat 15.57 dB, WeatherGS 15.56 dB) by margins exceeding 6 dB, demonstrating that joint 3D modeling of continuous scattering and particulate effects is substantially more effective than either 2D restoration or single-mechanism 3D approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

NimbusGS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its geometric backbone and on the Radiative Transfer Equation as the physical model for atmospheric attenuation, following the lineage of DehazeNeRF, SeaThru-NeRF, and ScatterNeRF for scattering-aware reconstruction. It departs from prior weather-specific methods (DeRainGS, WeatherGS, RainyScape, DehazeGS) that each target a single degradation type by introducing a unified decomposition into a volumetric extinction field (for continuous media like haze) and per-view particulate residuals (for discrete particles like rain and snow). The two-stage restoration-then-reconstruction pipeline (e.g., OneRestore + 3DGS) is explicitly rejected in favor of joint self-supervised optimization, and the Geometry-Guided Gradient Scaling mechanism is a novel optimization contribution not present in any prior 3DGS variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly handle continuous and discrete weather degradations in a single differentiable 3D reconstruction pipeline, and it introduces a hybrid-weather benchmark (simultaneous haze + rain + snow) that no prior method addresses. The self-supervised design (no paired clean/degraded data, no large-scale pretraining) makes it practical for real-world deployment where such supervision is unavailable. Open questions include whether the single-scattering assumption holds under very dense fog, whether the synthetic weather generation via OneRestore faithfully captures real-world particle dynamics, and whether the approach extends to indoor or semi-structured scenes where the dark-channel prior and volumetric extinction model may be less appropriate.
