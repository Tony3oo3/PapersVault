## Problem

Existing benchmarks for unified multimodal models evaluate understanding and generation in isolation or merely co-locate both capabilities without testing whether task success genuinely depends on their interaction. This leaves the fundamental question unanswered: does architectural unification actually enable synergetic enhancement between understanding and generation, or do the two capabilities remain functionally independent?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262530/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For UEG tasks, the model receives a text prompt requiring reasoning (e.g., mathematical computation, logical constraints) and must produce a generated image, which is then verified via a polling-based question list judged by Gemini 2.5 Pro. For GEU tasks, the model receives a disrupted or complex image (shuffled patches, transformed digits, mazes, maps) plus a question with multiple-choice options and must select the correct answer. In stepwise evaluation, UEG tasks insert an intermediate text reasoning step before generation, while GEU tasks insert an intermediate image reconstruction step before answering, making the dependency on each capability explicit.

## Method

RealUnify introduces a dual-evaluation protocol that combines direct end-to-end assessment with a diagnostic stepwise decomposition into separate understanding and generation phases, enabling precise attribution of performance bottlenecks to either weak individual capabilities or failed integration. The benchmark comprises 1,000 human-annotated instances across 10 categories and 32 subtasks organized along two axes: Understanding Enhances Generation (6 categories spanning world knowledge, commonsense, mathematical, logical, scientific reasoning, and code-to-image) and Generation Enhances Understanding (4 categories covering mental reconstruction, mental tracking, attentional focusing, and cognitive navigation). Each task is designed so that success requires an intricate interplay of both capabilities rather than either alone. The evaluation covers 12 unified models (11 open-source, 1 proprietary) and 6 specialized baselines, with an oracle upper bound constructed by chaining Gemini 2.5 Pro (understanding) and GPT-Image-1 (generation) in a stepwise pipeline.

## Results

On RealUnify's 1,000 instances, the best open-source unified model (UniPic2/OneCAT) achieves only 37.5% on UEG tasks, while the proprietary Nano Banana reaches 63.0%. On GEU tasks, the best open-source model (BAGEL) scores 39.3%, outperforming Nano Banana (31.8%). Under stepwise evaluation, UEG performance improves substantially (e.g., BAGEL: 32.7% → 47.7%, +15 points), while GEU performance degrades across all models, revealing that unified models possess the individual capabilities but fail to integrate them end-to-end. An oracle combining Gemini 2.5 Pro (understanding) and GPT-Image-1 (generation) achieves 72.7% on UEG, far exceeding any current unified model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On UEG tasks, the proprietary Nano Banana (63.0%) narrowly surpasses the best specialized generation model GPT-Image-1 (62.2%), while open-source unified models (UniPic2/OneCAT at 37.5%) trail both by ~25 points, indicating a large gap in leveraging understanding for generation. On GEU tasks, open-source unified models (BAGEL 39.3%, Ovis-U1 39.0%) outperform GPT-4.1 (38.5%) and Qwen2.5-VL (34.5%) but fall well short of Gemini 2.5 Pro (54.8%), showing that understanding in unified models is competitive but not yet at the frontier. The stepwise evaluation reveals a critical dissociation: UEG scores improve for all models when decomposed (BAGEL +15, Ovis-U1 +8.1), confirming models retain the knowledge but cannot spontaneously integrate it; conversely, GEU scores degrade under stepwise decomposition (e.g., BAGEL 39.3→35.8, Ovis-U1 39.0→29.5), suggesting models default to understanding shortcuts rather than leveraging generation. The oracle combination of Gemini 2.5 Pro + GPT-Image-1 achieves 72.7% on UEG (vs. 63.0% best unified), establishing a 10-point upper-bound gap, while on GEU the oracle (31.8%) actually underperforms BAGEL (39.3%), confirming that current generative models are inadequate for real-world problem-solving. Specialized generation models (FLUX.1 Kontext at 40.0%, Qwen-Image at 52.2%) further illustrate that open-source unified models have not yet closed the generation quality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the evaluation of unified multimodal models by introducing the explicit notion of bidirectional capability synergy as the evaluation target, departing from prior benchmarks (MME-Unify, UniEval) that assess understanding and generation in isolation or merely co-locate tasks. It builds conceptually on the 'thinking with images' literature and T2I reasoning benchmarks (T2I-CoReBench, WISE) that preliminarily probe one direction of cross-capability influence, but extends this to a symmetric, two-axis framework (UEG and GEU) with a novel dual-evaluation protocol combining direct end-to-end scoring with stepwise decomposition. The stepwise protocol draws on the 'first understanding, then generation' paradigm in T2I models and the 'first generation, then understanding' strategy in visual reasoning, but repurposes them as a diagnostic tool to isolate whether failures stem from weak individual capabilities or from failed integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the field with the first systematic diagnostic for whether architectural unification yields genuine synergy, and the consistent dissociation pattern (UEG improves under stepwise decomposition while GEU degrades) offers actionable guidance: current models possess the requisite knowledge but lack integration mechanisms, and generation does not yet serve as a useful reasoning tool for understanding. The large oracle gap (72.7% vs. ~37.5% for best open-source on UEG) quantifies substantial headroom, motivating new training strategies and inductive biases beyond mere co-training. Open questions include whether the GEU degradation reflects a fundamental limitation of current generative architectures or a training-data imbalance, and whether scaling or new architectural designs (e.g., latent visual reasoning) can close the synergy gap.
