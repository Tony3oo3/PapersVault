## Problem

Existing articulated object reconstruction methods treat articulation as a geometric interpolation problem, lacking physical feasibility constraints and semantic part understanding, which leads to floating components, interpenetration, and implausible joint behavior. They also depend on external structural priors such as predefined part libraries, kinematic graphs, or category-specific templates, limiting generalization to unseen objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263558/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images captured at exactly two joint states (e.g., fully closed and fully open) and produces a complete articulated digital twin: a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene decomposed into a static base and K movable parts, each with a learned SE(3) rigid transformation and per-Gaussian part-identity embedding. The resulting representation supports continuous rendering at any intermediate joint configuration between the two observed states, with repel-point fields ensuring collision-free trajectories throughout the articulation range.

## Method

Part²GS augments each 3D Gaussian with a learnable part-identity embedding ψ_i regularized by a neighborhood-consistency KL loss, enabling unsupervised part discovery that aligns geometric and kinematic structure. A motion-aware canonicalization step fuses two single-state reconstructions via Hungarian matching with adaptive interpolation weights derived from per-state motion richness scores, biasing the canonical space toward the more informative configuration. For articulation, each movable part undergoes an SE(3) transformation refined iteratively under a repel-point field that applies localized inverse-cube repulsive forces at part interfaces to prevent interpenetration. Three physics-informed losses—contact loss penalizing obtuse inter-part angles, velocity consistency penalizing intra-part displacement variance, and vector-field alignment enforcing SE(3) transformation consistency with observed motion—constrain the optimization to physically plausible trajectories. The total objective jointly optimizes rendering fidelity, part regularization, articulation alignment, and physical consistency in a single differentiable pipeline.

## Results

On the PARIS benchmark, Part²GS achieves angular errors below 0.01° on nearly all simulated objects, over two orders of magnitude lower than Ditto (~89°) and PARIS (~19°). For geometry, Part²GS reduces CD_movable by up to 10× compared to ArtGS and 4–10× compared to DTA, while also achieving the lowest CD_whole across all categories. On the multi-part ARTGS-MULTI and DTA-MULTI benchmarks, Part²GS attains the lowest Chamfer Distance for static, movable, and whole-object regions in almost all categories, with CD_movable improvements of up to 10× over DTA and 3× over ArtGS on objects with 3–7 movable parts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Part²GS decisively outperforms Ditto and PARIS across all metrics on the PARIS benchmark: angular error drops from ~89° (Ditto) and ~19° (PARIS) to ~0.01°, and CD_movable on Foldchair improves from 141.11 (Ditto) and 24.23 (PARIS) to 0.12. Against DTA, Part²GS achieves lower CD_movable on most objects (e.g., 0.51 vs. 10.11 on Scissor, 0.12 vs. 0.15 on Foldchair) and lower CD_whole (0.19 vs. 0.27 on Foldchair), though DTA remains competitive on some real-world objects. The closest competitor is ArtGS, which also uses 3DGS-based reconstruction; Part²GS surpasses ArtGS on CD_movable by up to 10× (e.g., 0.12 vs. 0.54 on Foldchair, 0.51 vs. 0.64 on Scissor) and on CD_whole by 1.7–2×, while matching or slightly exceeding ArtGS on angular and positional errors. On the multi-part benchmarks (Table 2), Part²GS extends these gains to objects with 3–7 movable parts, reducing CD_movable by up to 10× over DTA (e.g., 1.83 vs. 476.91 on Storage-7parts) and 3× over ArtGS (1.83 vs. 3.70). Statistical t-tests confirm Part²GS is significantly better than ArtGS (p<0.05) in 83 of 111 object-metric pairs, with no significant difference in 25 and only 3 cases where ArtGS is preferred.

## Relation to Prior Work

Part²GS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting into the articulated-object domain by augmenting each Gaussian with a learnable part-identity embedding, departing from prior methods (Ditto, PARIS, DTA, ArtGS) that rely on predefined part libraries, kinematic graphs, or heuristic Gaussian clustering. It introduces a motion-aware canonical representation that adaptively weights interpolation between two joint states based on per-state motion richness, replacing the simple averaging used by ArtGS. The physics-informed regularization suite (contact loss, velocity consistency, vector-field alignment) and the repel-point repulsion field are novel contributions not present in prior articulated 3DGS or NeRF-based reconstruction work, which largely treated articulation as a pure geometric interpolation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Articulated object reconstruction is a bottleneck for robotics, embodied AI, and interactive 3D content creation, and Part²GS demonstrates that part structure and physically consistent motion can emerge jointly from raw multi-view observations without category-specific priors or manual part annotations. However, the evaluation is limited to small synthetic benchmarks (17 objects total) with only two joint states per object, and real-world results (two objects) show substantially larger errors, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to continuous multi-state articulation, complex real-world scenes, or objects with more than ~7 parts. The repel-point mechanism, while effective in controlled settings, has not been validated against true physics simulators, raising questions about its robustness for downstream manipulation tasks.
