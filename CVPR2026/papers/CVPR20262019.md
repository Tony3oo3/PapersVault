## Problem

Existing LLM-based VFM evaluation protocols suffer from two blind spots: (i) instruction-tuning data may not align with VQA test distributions, so errors may reflect data mismatch rather than genuine visual limitations, and (ii) typical VQA questions require multiple visual abilities simultaneously, making it impossible to determine whether a failure stems from one missing ability or several. This paper introduces AVA-BENCH, the first benchmark that explicitly disentangles 14 Atomic Visual Abilities (AVAs) to pinpoint exactly where a VFM excels or falters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262019/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample consists of an RGB image paired with a text question designed to test exactly one AVA in isolation; bounding box annotations are provided where needed to decouple the target ability from confounding ones (e.g., a bounding box is supplied to test depth estimation without requiring localization). The LLM head produces a free-form text answer, which is scored using AVA-specific metrics: GIoU for localization, CIEDE2000 for color, ANLS for OCR, normalized MAE for absolute depth and counting, and standard accuracy for all other AVAs. The final output is a per-AVA performance score and a cross-AVA ranking that constitutes each VFM's 'ability fingerprint.'

## Method

AVA-BENCH curates 218K image-question pairs from 26 diverse datasets, each pair explicitly aligned to a single AVA, with an 80/20 train/test split that mirrors object classes and answer bins to eliminate distribution mismatch. Evaluation follows the LLaVA-style two-stage protocol: a connector between the frozen VFM and an LLM is first pre-trained for feature alignment, then the connector and LoRA adapters on the LLM are fine-tuned separately for each AVA while the VFM remains frozen. A key efficiency finding is that a 0.5B Qwen2 LLM preserves the same relative VFM rankings as a 7B Vicuna-1.5 while reducing GPU hours by approximately 8×, making large-scale comparative analysis substantially more practical. Bounding boxes serve as a critical disentanglement tool: providing ground-truth boxes isolates a single AVA (e.g., spatial reasoning), whereas removing them transforms the task into a composite one that also tests localization, revealing which specific ability drives performance gaps.

## Results

AVA-BENCH evaluates 9 leading VFMs across 14 Atomic Visual Abilities using 218K curated image-question pairs from 26 source datasets. SigLIP-1/2 and AIMv2 achieve the highest average rank across all AVAs, confirming the critical role of language supervision in general visual capability. DINOv2 (self-supervised) performs comparably or better than language-supervised models on vision-centric AVAs such as localization, absolute depth estimation, and orientation, while non-language-aligned VFMs (SAM, MiDaS) show significant weaknesses on language-centric tasks like OCR. A lightweight 0.5B LLM (Qwen2) preserves reliable VFM rankings comparable to a 7B Vicuna-1.5 while reducing evaluation GPU hours by approximately 8×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 9 VFMs spanning five training paradigms. SigLIP-1/2 (Zhai 2023; Tschannen 2025) and AIMv2 (Fini 2024) dominate the overall ranking, achieving the best average rank across all 14 AVAs, with SigLIP-2 edging out on language-centric tasks like OCR. DINOv2 (Oquab 2023), despite lacking any language supervision, matches or surpasses language-supervised models on vision-centric AVAs including orientation, localization, and absolute depth estimation, but falls sharply behind on OCR and scene recognition. SAM (Kirillov 2023) achieves exceptional color recognition but is weakest on localization for small objects and language-centric tasks. MiDaS (Ranftl 2020) is strongest on relative depth but underperforms broadly, particularly on small-object localization. RADIO (Ranzinger 2024) shows the most balanced profile with no extreme weaknesses, suggesting the value of agglomerative multi-teacher distillation. CLIP (Radford 2021) and InternVL-2.5 (Chen 2024) occupy the middle tier, with CLIP retaining an edge on OCR due to its contrastive image-text alignment. All VFMs, regardless of training objective, perform well on low-to-mid-level AVAs (texture, relative depth, object recognition), indicating that complex VQA failures stem from specific critical AVA deficiencies rather than broad visual incompetence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-style two-stage training protocol (Liu et al., 2023) and the LLM-as-general-purpose-head evaluation paradigm popularized by Cambrian-1 (Tong et al., 2024), which attach a frozen VFM to a fine-tuned LLM and evaluate on broad VQA benchmarks. It departs from this lineage by identifying two structural blind spots—train/test distribution mismatch and multi-ability confounding—and proposing AVA-BENCH, which isolates 14 atomic visual abilities with distribution-matched splits so that each ability is probed in isolation. The evaluation pipeline itself (frozen VFM + LoRA-tuned connector/LLM) is standard; the novelty lies entirely in the benchmark design and the diagnostic framing of results as per-ability 'fingerprints.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVA-BENCH converts VFM selection from heuristic guesswork into a principled, diagnostic process: practitioners can now match a VFM's ability profile to the specific perceptual demands of a downstream task rather than relying on a single aggregate VQA score. The finding that a 0.5B LLM preserves reliable relative VFM rankings while cutting GPU hours by 8× makes large-scale VFM comparison substantially more accessible. Open questions remain: how to align non-language-aligned VFMs (e.g., DINOv2, SAM) to language modalities without eroding their visual strengths (the paper reports a 66%→26% linear-probing drop through the connector), whether the 14 AVAs capture the full space of relevant perceptual skills, and whether the partial support for the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (convergence on low/mid-level AVAs but divergence on high-level ones) generalizes to new architectures and training regimes.
