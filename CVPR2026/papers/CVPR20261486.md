## Problem

Existing video stylization methods are largely confined to a single style condition (text, style image, or stylized first frame), limiting their scope and generalization to novel styles. Additionally, the absence of high-quality paired video training data leads to style inconsistency and temporal flicker, and extended applications such as multi-style fusion and long-video stylization remain under-explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261486/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an input video (480P, up to 81 frames) and one of three style conditions—a text prompt, a style reference image, or a stylized first frame—DreamStyle produces a temporally consistent stylized video that preserves the main content and motion of the input. The raw video is injected as an additional channel condition (not as in-context frames), keeping computational overhead minimal relative to the base I2V model. Multiple style conditions can be combined in a single forward pass, enabling extended use cases like multi-style fusion.

## Method

DreamStyle extends the Wan14B-I2V DiT-based model to a V2V stylization framework by injecting the raw video through additional image condition channels (mask=0.0) while reusing the original cross-attention for text and image channels for the first frame (mask=1.0). The style image is appended as an extra frame with mask=1.0 and its CLIP features fed through the native CLIP branch for global semantic cues. To prevent inter-token confusion among the three condition types (first-frame, style-image, raw-video), a modified LoRA with a shared down matrix and token-specific up matrices is applied in full-attention and FFN layers, analogous to a manually-routed LoRA MoE. Training follows a two-stage flow-matching objective: a large-scale CT stage (40K pairs) for broad style coverage, followed by a high-quality SFT stage (5K pairs) for visual fidelity, with conditions sampled at a 1:2:1 ratio across the three tasks.

## Results

DreamStyle achieves the highest style-consistency scores across all three video stylization tasks: CLIP-T 0.167 (text-guided), CSD 0.515 (style-image-guided V2V), and CSD 0.851 (first-frame-guided), outperforming all baselines on these primary metrics. On structure preservation (DINO score), DreamStyle leads in text-guided (0.584) and style-image-guided (0.526) settings but trails VACE (0.716) and VideoX-Fun (0.702) in first-frame-guided mode. On VBench quality metrics, DreamStyle attains the best aesthetic quality (0.656 text, 0.635 style-image, 0.630 first-frame) and dynamic degree (0.894 text, 0.867 style-image) among all compared methods, while image quality is marginally lower than Pixverse (0.738 vs. 0.746) in the text-guided task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In text-guided stylization, DreamStyle outperforms all three commercial baselines (Luma, Pixverse, Runway) on style following (CLIP-T 0.167 vs. 0.132–0.155), structure preservation (DINO 0.584 vs. 0.406–0.504), and dynamic degree (0.894 vs. 0.766–0.809), while trailing Pixverse by only 0.008 on image quality. For style-image-guided stylization, DreamStyle (V2V) dramatically surpasses StyleMaster (T2V) on CSD (0.515 vs. 0.198) and dynamic degree (0.867 vs. 0.289), and is the only method that preserves structure (DINO 0.526) since StyleMaster lacks a video condition. In first-frame-guided stylization, DreamStyle achieves the best CSD (0.851 vs. 0.766 for VideoX-Fun and 0.689 for VACE) and best aesthetic quality (0.630), but sacrifices structure preservation (DINO 0.640 vs. 0.716 for VACE) and dynamic degree (0.856 vs. 0.889 for VACE), which the authors attribute to occasional conflicts between geometrically deformed stylized first frames and the input video's structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamStyle extends the Wan14B-I2V DiT-based image-to-video model into a video-to-video stylization framework by injecting the raw video through additional image-condition channels, a design that avoids the in-context frame concatenation used by UNIC. Prior video stylization methods (StyleCrafter, StyleMaster, UniVST, TokenFlow) each handle a single style modality—text, style image, or first frame—and rely on image-domain stylization datasets or DDIM inversion to generalize to video. The token-specific LoRA module draws on HydraLoRA's asymmetric up-matrix idea, repurposed here to disentangle three semantically distinct token types (first-frame, style-image, raw-video) within a shared attention/FFN backbone. The data curation pipeline is novel in combining SOTA image stylization (InstantStyle/SDXL, Seedream 4.0) with ControlNet-guided I2V generation to produce motion-aligned stylized-raw video pairs, sidestepping the quality ceiling of T2V-based synthesis used by UNIC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying three style-condition modalities in a single LoRA-trained model, DreamStyle offers practitioners a practical, flexible tool that eliminates the need to maintain separate pipelines for text-, image-, and frame-guided stylization, and naturally supports extended scenarios like multi-style fusion and long-video stylization. The two-stage CT+SFT training strategy and the token-specific LoRA ablation provide actionable guidance for multi-condition fine-tuning of large video diffusion models. Limitations include the 480P/81-frame resolution, reliance on in-house models (Seedream 4.0) for the SFT dataset, and a DINO structure-preservation gap versus VACE/VideoX-Fun in the first-frame-guided setting when geometric deformation is present. Open questions include whether the unified framework scales to higher resolutions and longer sequences, and whether the token-specific LoRA generalizes beyond three condition types.
