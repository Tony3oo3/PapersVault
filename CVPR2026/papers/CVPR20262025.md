## Problem

Prior model stitching work demonstrated that small models trained on the same dataset remain stitchable, but it is unclear whether this extends to large, heterogeneous Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) trained with different data, objectives, and modalities (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP2). Conventional stitching training strategies—layer feature matching and end-to-end task loss optimization—fail for VFMs, particularly at shallow stitch positions, leaving open whether complementary VFM strengths can be practically fused.

## Input / Output

The stitched model takes a standard RGB image, processes it through the early layers of a source VFM, passes the intermediate token features through a lightweight trainable stitch layer (e.g., a two-layer MLP), and then feeds them into the frozen later layers of a target VFM. All VFM parameters remain frozen; only the stitch layer is trained. Depending on the downstream head, the output is a class label (classification), a per-pixel segmentation mask (ADE20K), or a text response (multimodal LLM via VST). The stitch layer can also be pre-trained in a task-agnostic manner on general image data (e.g., LLaVA-1.5's 1.1M images) and reused across tasks without retraining.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a two-stage stitch-layer training recipe: (1) Final Feature Matching (FFM), which pre-trains the stitch layer to minimize the ℓ₂ distance between the stitched model's final output features and the target model's final features, and (2) Task Loss Training, which fine-tunes the stitch layer with the downstream objective. FFM addresses the core failure mode of conventional approaches: layer feature matching at the stitch position does not prevent error accumulation through frozen target layers, and naive task-loss optimization suffers from poorly conditioned gradients that must traverse many frozen layers to update only the randomly initialized stitch layer. The paper further introduces the VFM Stitch Tree (VST), an architecture that shares early Transformer layers across multiple VFMs while retaining model-specific deep layers connected via stitch layers, yielding a controllable accuracy-latency trade-off for multimodal LLMs that deploy several VFMs (e.g., recovering 45% of the two-VFM gain with only 4.3% extra compute).

## Results

Using the two-stage FFM+TLT training, stitched VFM models consistently outperform their self-stitch baselines across all four evaluation datasets. On fMoW (layer 22), DINOv2→SigLIP2 reaches 71.8% accuracy vs. 69.9% (DINOv2 self-stitch) and 68.9% (SigLIP2 self-stitch); on iNaturalist, SigLIP2→DINOv2 reaches 91.9% vs. 91.2% and 87.3%. On ADE20K segmentation, SigLIP2→DINOv2 achieves 51.4% mIoU vs. 50.9% and 50.5% self-stitch baselines. In the VST application, VST-22 (4.3% extra resources) recovers 45% of the full two-VFM performance gain, while VST-14 (39% extra) recovers 84%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINOv2 (self-stitch), both cross-VFM stitched models win on every dataset: e.g., on fMoW at layer 22, DINOv2→SigLIP2 scores 71.8% vs. DINOv2 self-stitch 69.9%, and SigLIP2→DINOv2 scores 72.2% vs. 69.9%. Against SigLIP2 (self-stitch), the gains are even larger in some cases: on iNaturalist at layer 6, SigLIP2→DINOv2 reaches 86.3% vs. SigLIP2 self-stitch 71.2% (+15.1%). Against CLIP, the paper notes CLIP is a weaker VFM in their setting; when CLIP is the source, the stitched model improves over CLIP itself but still falls short of the stronger target, whereas CLIP as target yields strong stitched performance. Against DINOv3, stitched models generally outperform self-stitch baselines across stitch positions (Figure 6), confirming the findings extend beyond the DINOv2/SigLIP2 pair. Overall, the consistent +0.7% to +5.5% classification gains and +0.5 to +0.7 mIoU segmentation gains over self-stitch controls demonstrate that the improvements come from genuine complementary knowledge fusion rather than added stitch-layer capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model stitching framework introduced by Bansal et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and Csiszárik et al. (NeurIPS 2021), which demonstrated stitchability for small models (e.g., ResNet-18) trained on the same dataset. The key departure is moving from same-dataset, same-architecture comparisons to stitching large, heterogeneous Vision Foundation Models (CLIP, DINOv2, DINOv3, SigLIP2) that differ in pretraining data, objectives (contrastive vs. self-supervised), and modality. The paper introduces Final Feature Matching (FFM) as a new stitch-layer training objective and proposes the VFM Stitch Tree (VST) as a practical multi-VFM architecture, shifting stitching from a passive representational probe to an active knowledge-fusion tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that heterogeneous VFMs are reliably stitchable with a simple two-stage recipe (FFM pre-training + task fine-tuning) challenges the assumption that different training objectives and data create fundamentally incompatible representation spaces, and provides a practical path for fusing complementary VFM strengths without retraining. VST offers a concrete compute-aware knob for multi-VFM MLLM systems, though the current evaluation is limited to a single MoF-LLaVA configuration with a small set of benchmarks. Open questions include whether task-agnostic stitch pre-training generalizes to substantially different downstream domains, how VST scales beyond two VFMs in production MLLMs, and whether the observed knowledge-fusion gains persist under stronger fine-tuning regimes where individual VFMs are already near-saturated.
