## Problem

Standard CNN kernels and window-based attention in learned image compression impose fixed receptive fields and static connectivity patterns, which couple non-redundant pixels merely due to Euclidean proximity and prevent the model from adaptively capturing spatially varying redundancy—particularly long-range correlations. This rigidity limits rate-distortion performance because natural images exhibit highly non-uniform redundancy (smooth vs. textured regions) that a fixed connectivity pattern cannot accommodate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260216/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image as input and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image via a VAE-based LIC pipeline with hyper-prior entropy coding (SCCTX, 5 slices). Training is on the Flickr2W dataset; evaluation spans three resolutions: Kodak (768×512), Tecnick (1200×1200), and CLIC2020Val (2K). The graph structure is content-adaptive and re-computed per image at inference time, with the bisection-based graph construction adding less than 0.02 s to encoding/decoding latency on 2K images.

## Method

GLIC introduces a Graph-based Feature Aggregation (GFA) block deployed in the two lower-resolution stages of the nonlinear transform, combining two complementary mechanisms. First, dual-scale candidate sampling constructs a dense local window (8×8) plus a sparse per-node mesh grid (16×16) to achieve a near-full receptive field at near-linear cost O(Nn), avoiding the quadratic complexity of global attention. Second, a complexity-aware degree budgeting mechanism computes per-pixel Sobel-gradient magnitudes pooled via RMS across channels, normalizes them into weights, and allocates a global edge budget proportionally so that structurally complex regions receive more neighbors while smooth regions receive fewer. Given these per-node quotas, each node selects its actual neighbors by per-node bisection on cosine-similarity thresholds, yielding a directed adjacency matrix. Feature updates then proceed via edge-conditioned aggregation with learnable linear projections and softmax-normalized attention weights over the constructed directed graph.

## Results

GLIC achieves BD-rate reductions of −19.29%, −21.69%, and −18.71% relative to VTM-9.1 on Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC2020Val respectively, setting a new state of the art among learned image compression methods. In terms of efficiency, GLIC uses 67.20M parameters and 2.48T FLOPs with a decoding latency of 0.395 s on 2K images, reducing peak memory by 73.1% and decoding latency by 41.0% compared to the closest SOTA competitor MambaIC. In BD-PSNR terms, GLIC gains 0.39–0.56 dB over TCM-L and 0.26–0.38 dB over FTIC across the three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLIC outperforms all 11 baselines on BD-rate across all three datasets. The closest competitors are DCAE (−17.18/−20.07/−16.91%) and HPCM (−16.13/−17.26/−15.02%), which GLIC surpasses by 2.11/1.62/1.80 and 3.16/4.43/3.69 percentage points respectively. Against the transformer-based FTIC, GLIC gains 4.46/7.30/8.01 points in BD-rate while also achieving substantially lower decoding latency (0.395 s vs >10 s). Compared to the Mamba-based MambaIC, GLIC improves BD-rate by 4.16/5.91/2.98 points while using 57.2% fewer parameters and 73.1% less peak memory. Against the earlier CNN/Transformer hybrids TCM-L and MLIC++, GLIC leads by 5.87/10.76/9.61 and 4.15/4.46/4.30 points respectively. The traditional VTM-9.1 anchor is beaten by a wide margin of 19.29/21.69/18.71 points, confirming the advantage of learned, content-adaptive graph-based transforms over fixed-block coding.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLIC builds on the standard VAE-based learned image compression backbone (Ballé et al.) and the SCCTX entropy model from ELIC, but replaces the rigid CNN/Transformer nonlinear transforms with a Graph-based Feature Aggregation block. The graph aggregation mechanism follows edge-conditioned message passing (Simonovsky & Komodakis), while the dual-scale sampling strategy explicitly departs from the fixed global anchors used in IP-GNN (Tian et al., CVPR 2024) by generating per-node mesh-grids. Prior GNN-based LIC methods (GABIC, fully-connected GAT, global kNN GNN) either suffer from quadratic complexity or remain confined to local windows; GLIC addresses both by combining a dense local graph with a sparse global graph and introducing a complexity-aware per-node degree budget, achieving near-global receptive fields at linear cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The content-adaptive connectivity demonstrated through ERF visualizations—where background pixels attend to grass and foreground pixels attend to parrot feathers despite Euclidean proximity—provides concrete evidence that GNN-based LIC can model spatially varying redundancy in a way CNNs and window attention fundamentally cannot. The practical significance is reinforced by the efficiency profile: GLIC matches or beats SOTA RD performance (−19 to −22% BD-rate over VTM-9.1) while using fewer parameters and FLOPs than MambaIC and WeConvene. Open questions include whether the Sobel-based complexity scoring generalizes to video or 3D data, whether the bisection-based graph construction can be replaced by differentiable alternatives for end-to-end optimization, and whether the approach extends naturally to the entropy model or remains confined to the nonlinear transform.
