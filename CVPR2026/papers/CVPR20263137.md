## Problem

Existing training-free token pruning methods for VLMs either rely on importance scores (retaining redundant clustered tokens around the same object) or on redundancy reduction (producing dispersed, scattered selections that miss fine-grained local details). Neither strategy explicitly accounts for spatial relationships among visual tokens, leading to incomplete coverage of target object regions under aggressive pruning budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263137/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full set of N visual tokens (hidden states H ∈ R^{N×d} and keys K ∈ R^{N×d_k}) from a specified LLM decoder layer, along with their 2D grid coordinates (or 3D coordinates for video frames). It outputs a pruned index set S of size R (e.g., 64 out of 576 tokens for LLaVA-1.5) and updated hidden states for the retained tokens, which are then fed into subsequent decoder layers. The token grid dimensions H×W are model-specific (24×24 for LLaVA-1.5, dynamic for LLaVA-Next and Qwen2-VL), and D_max is computed accordingly.

## Method

VLM-Pruner is a training-free, three-stage centrifugal pruning paradigm. Stage 1 initializes κ diverse pivot tokens via a max-min strategy in the low-dimensional key space to coarsely cover distinct semantic regions. Stage 2 performs parallel greedy selection governed by the Buffering for Spatial Sparsity (BSS) criterion, which modulates pairwise cosine similarity by a factor (1 + λ·δ̄_i(S)) where δ̄_i is the normalized minimum spatial distance from candidate i to the selected set; this makes spatially distant candidates appear more redundant and defers them, enforcing an orderly near-to-far expansion that densifies local neighborhoods before expanding outward. A strict-to-loose threshold schedule (τ^(t) = τ^(0) + t·Δτ) further prevents premature admission of isolated far tokens. Stage 3 recovers information from all discarded tokens via Similarity-Weighted Aggregation (SWA), assigning each discarded token to its most similar retained token and blending 70% of the similarity-weighted aggregate into the retained token's hidden state.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B across 9 image benchmarks, VLM-Pruner retains 98.85%, 98.07%, and 95.61% of the full-model average at 66.7%, 77.8%, and 88.9% pruning ratios, respectively. At the most aggressive 88.9% pruning (64 tokens), VLM-Pruner ranks first on 7 of 9 benchmarks, with the largest absolute gains over the strongest baseline (DivPrune) on GQA (+1.41%), SEEDBench (+2.05%), and OK-VQA (+1.06%). On Qwen2-VL-7B at 88.9% pruning, VLM-Pruner achieves a 92.58% average and an OCRBench score of 581 versus 481 for DART (+12.56% absolute). On LLaVA-Video-7B at 88.9% pruning, VLM-Pruner attains a 90.55% average, surpassing DART (89.84%) and DivPrune (88.52%).

## Relation to Prior Work

VLM-Pruner sits at the intersection of two established token-pruning lines for VLMs: importance-driven selection (FastV, SparseVLM, PDrop, MustDrop) and redundancy-reduction greedy selection (DART, DivPrune, BTP, SAINT). Rather than building on a single predecessor, it repurposes the max-min diversity problem for pivot initialization and submodular greedy optimization for token selection, but introduces a fundamentally new constraint—spatial sparsity via the BSS criterion—that neither family addresses. The centrifugal near-to-far selection paradigm and the Similarity-Weighted Aggregation recovery step are novel contributions that explicitly target the failure modes identified in both prior camps: over-clustering around salient regions and scattered, detail-incomplete coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, training-free, plug-and-play pruning method that consistently outperforms strong baselines across five VLMs and thirteen benchmarks, with gains widening at higher pruning ratios—making it directly useful for deploying VLMs on resource-constrained devices. The key conceptual contribution is identifying spatial ordering as a missing ingredient: both importance-driven and pure-diversity methods fail because they lack an explicit near-to-far expansion mechanism, and the dramatic ablation (DivPrune as Stage-2 selector collapsing to 46.47% vs. 95.30%) underscores how critical this ordering is. Open questions include whether the hand-tuned BSS strength λ and threshold schedule generalize to unseen architectures or ultra-high-resolution inputs with thousands of tokens, and whether the greedy selection can be replaced by a globally optimal solver without prohibitive cost.
