## Problem

3D Gaussian splatting cannot be relit or composited into complex scenes because conventional path tracing is not directly applicable to its rasterized representation. Existing relighting methods for Gaussian splatting either rely on approximate formulations (split-sum, ambient occlusion) that sacrifice realism, or perform expensive Gaussian ray tracing that precludes real-time use. The paper addresses how to achieve physically-based global illumination—soft shadows, interreflections, color bleeding—on Gaussian splatting at interactive frame rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260109/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images of a scene as input and produces a relightable Gaussian splatting model with per-Gaussian albedo and roughness parameters, plus a learned environment map. At inference, the model is rendered at 1920×1080 using only 2 spp with temporal accumulation (history length 20) and spatial denoising, achieving ~113 fps on an RTX 4080. The trained GS model can be inserted into arbitrary MIP-NeRF 360 scenes (GARDEN, KITCHEN, ROOM) for real-time composition under user-selected environment maps.

## Method

GHPT is a three-stage inverse rendering pipeline that decouples Gaussian appearance from mesh-based lighting. Stage 1 uses PGSR (planar-based Gaussian splatting) with a StableNormal monocular prior to render multi-view consistent depth and reconstruct a surface mesh. Stage 2 performs physically-based differentiable rendering on that mesh via SlangPy (hardware-accelerated ray tracing with NEE and MIS) to recover UV-mapped albedo/roughness textures and an environment map. Stage 3 applies Factorized Inverse Path Tracing (FIPT) on the PGSR-rendered G-buffer: diffuse and specular shadings are pre-baked at 256 spp by ray-tracing the textured mesh for visibility and indirect illumination, then per-Gaussian albedo and roughness are optimized against the pre-baked shadings using a simple L1 + edge-aware smoothness loss. The key insight is that hybrid path tracing—shooting rays from the rasterized G-buffer rather than from cameras—allows the expensive visibility and GI computation to be delegated to the mesh via GPU hardware ray tracing, while the Gaussian primitives retain their fast alpha-blended rendering for appearance.

## Results

On SYNTHETIC4RELIGHT, GHPT achieves the best relighting PSNR of 35.87 (vs. 34.76 for IRGS, the next best), best novel view synthesis PSNR of 37.11 (vs. 36.06 for R3DG), and best LPIPS for relighting (0.045). On TENSOIR SYNTHETIC, GHPT achieves the best relighting PSNR of 32.46 (vs. 31.10 for SVG-IR) and best albedo PSNR of 33.67 (vs. 33.40 for IRGS), though it trails GI-GS on novel view synthesis PSNR (35.57 vs. 36.75). The method also achieves real-time relighting at 113 fps at 1920×1080 on an RTX 4080, with a total frame time of 7.3–9.8 ms across tested scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GHPT outperforms all seven baselines on relighting PSNR on SYNTHETIC4RELIGHT (35.87 vs. 34.76 for IRGS, 32.89 for R3DG, 30.58 for TensorIR) and on TENSOIR SYNTHETIC (32.46 vs. 31.10 for SVG-IR, 30.63 for IRGS, 28.83 for TensorIR), confirming its primary contribution in physically-based relighting. On novel view synthesis, GHPT leads on SYNTHETIC4RELIGHT (37.11 PSNR vs. 36.06 for R3DG) but trails GI-GS on TENSOIR SYNTHETIC (35.57 vs. 36.75 PSNR), suggesting the hybrid path tracing pipeline slightly sacrifices view synthesis fidelity on more complex scenes. For albedo recovery, IRGS edges out GHPT on SYNTHETIC4RELIGHT (33.07 vs. 32.20 PSNR), while GHPT wins on TENSOIR SYNTHETIC (33.67 vs. 33.40). Among NeRF-based baselines (NeRFactor, InvRender, TensorIR), GHPT consistently leads by 5–14 dB in relighting PSNR, highlighting the advantage of the explicit Gaussian + mesh representation over implicit radiance fields. The Gaussian-based baselines that rely on split-sum approximation (GS-IR, GI-GS) or Gaussian ray tracing (R3DG, IRGS) all underperform GHPT on relighting, validating the paper's claim that hybrid path tracing on the G-buffer with hardware-accelerated mesh ray tracing captures visibility and indirect illumination more faithfully than approximate formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three established techniques into a unified three-stage pipeline: PGSR for multi-view consistent surface reconstruction, physically-based differentiable rendering (in the SlangPy/NVDiffRec lineage) for material and environment map recovery on the mesh, and FIPT for efficient material decomposition on the Gaussian representation. The key conceptual departure from prior GS relighting work is the 'hybrid' rendering model: rather than performing path tracing directly on Gaussian primitives (as in R3DG, IRGS, or 3DGRT-based methods) or relying on split-sum approximations (as in GS-IR, GI-GS), the authors rasterize a G-buffer from PGSR and evaluate visibility and indirect illumination via hardware-accelerated ray tracing on the underlying textured mesh. This deferred-rendering formulation borrows the hybrid path tracing paradigm from real-time game engines and applies it to the inverse rendering setting for the first time in the GS context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is enabling real-time (113 fps at 1080p) relighting and scene composition of Gaussian splatting assets, which opens the door to using 3DGS in AR/VR and interactive applications where inter-object effects (shadows, interreflections, color bleeding) are essential. The three-stage separation (geometry → materials → per-Gaussian decomposition) is a clean architectural choice that allows each stage to leverage the most appropriate tool. Limitations include dependence on PGSR's mesh quality (self-intersection issues require thresholding), the heuristic contrast enhancement of the environment map (power of 1.35), and evaluation limited to synthetic datasets plus a small set of MIP-NeRF 360 scenes. Open questions include whether the mesh-based visibility evaluation remains accurate for highly detailed or thin-geometry scenes, and whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or unbounded scenes.
