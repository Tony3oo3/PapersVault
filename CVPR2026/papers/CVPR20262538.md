## Problem

Conventional closed-set remote sensing object detectors face a persistent accuracy bottleneck due to limited prior knowledge, while existing VLM-based detectors are designed for open-vocabulary tasks and incur substantial inference and deployment overhead. The paper addresses how to transfer the rich semantic knowledge of a VLM into a standard closed-set detector without any additional cost at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262538/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB remote sensing images (satellite, UAV, or aerial) as input and produces either horizontal or oriented bounding boxes with class labels. During training, the FPN features are resized to 32×32 with 1024 channels before feeding the VLM branch, and the VLM outputs are structured as category-grouped token sequences (e.g., <x_1-1-1><y_1-1-1>...) separated by <sep> tokens, with a maximum generation length of 2048. At inference, only the standard detection head outputs are used, so the deployed model is architecturally identical to the baseline detector.

## Method

VLM4RSDet jointly trains a conventional detector (e.g., DetectoRS, Faster R-CNN) and a VLM (Florence-2-Base) that share a ResNet-50 backbone and FPN. The FPN multi-scale features P_i feed both the detection head (producing L_cls and L_reg) and the VLM branch (producing L_llm via a projector and LLM), with total loss L = L_cls + L_reg + 0.05·L_llm. Two auxiliary modules enhance the VLM branch: a Global-Local Cross Attention (GLCA) module that uses the highest-level FPN feature as a global context to cross-attend with lower-level local features, and a Learnable Hierarchical Prediction Strategy (LHPS) that partitions objects into size-based groups via learnable proportions β_i and assigns each group to a different FPN level for prediction. Critically, at inference the VLM is discarded entirely, so the deployed model incurs zero additional parameters, FLOPs, or latency compared to the baseline detector.

## Results

On VisDrone2019, DN-FPN w/ VLM4RSDet achieves 45.3 mAP_0.5:0.95, surpassing the previous SOTA DN-FPN (37.8) by 7.5 percentage points without any additional inference overhead. On AI-TOD, DetectoRS w/ VLM4RSDet reaches 28.5 mAP_0.5:0.95, a 13.7% improvement over the base DetectoRS (14.8). On DOTA-v1.0, LEGNet-S w/ VLM4RSDet attains a new SOTA of 84.07 mAP_0.5, and on DOTA-v1.5 it achieves 78.42 mAP_0.5. On MS COCO 2017, the method improves Faster R-CNN from 37.4 to 42.0 mAP_0.5:0.95, confirming generalizability beyond remote sensing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLM4RSDet consistently outperforms all conventional detector baselines: on VisDrone2019, DetectoRS improves from 25.7 to 31.4 mAP_0.5:0.95 (+5.7), and on AI-TOD the same backbone gains +13.7 (14.8→28.5). Against VLM-based open-vocabulary detectors (Florence-2, YOLO-World, Grounding DINO, LLMDet), the collaborative approach achieves substantially higher accuracy while retaining zero additional inference cost — e.g., DetectoRS w/ VLM4RSDet (31.4) beats LLMDet (20.4) by 11.0 points on VisDrone2019. The headline result is the 7.5-point mAP_0.5:0.95 gain over the previous SOTA DN-FPN (37.8→45.3) on VisDrone2019, with identical inference parameters, FLOPs, and FPS. On OBB tasks, LEGNet-S w/ VLM4RSDet surpasses the prior best LEGNet-S by 4.04 points on DOTA-v1.0 (80.03→84.07) and 5.53 points on DOTA-v1.5 (72.89→78.42). Crucially, Table 8 confirms that inference parameters and FLOPs remain unchanged relative to the base detector, while training FPS drops only modestly (e.g., 55.3→55.3 for RetinaNet inference, 20.1→15.2 for training), and the total training time is actually shorter than fine-tuning Florence-2-Base (12 vs. 50 epochs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of VLM-based detection (LLMDet, YOLO-World, Grounding DINO, CoseDet), which retains the language model at inference for open-vocabulary tasks, by instead using a VLM (Florence-2-Base) exclusively as a training-time collaborator whose knowledge is absorbed into a conventional detector. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: conventional closed-set remote sensing detectors (DetectoRS, RetinaNet, FCOS) and vision-language models, through a shared visual backbone and joint loss backpropagation. The GLCA module extends standard cross-attention by fusing the highest-level FPN feature as global context into lower-level local features, while LHPS adapts the single-layer prediction of Florence-2 into a size-stratified multi-level output scheme inspired by FPN-based detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that VLM prior knowledge can be distilled into a standard detector at zero inference cost, resolving the long-standing tension between VLM accuracy potential and deployment overhead in remote sensing. The approach is architecture-agnostic (validated on RetinaNet, FCOS, Faster R-CNN, DetectoRS, LEGNet) and task-agnostic (HBB and OBB), suggesting broad applicability. However, the method remains confined to closed-set detection since the VLM is discarded at inference; the authors explicitly identify extending this to efficient open-vocabulary detection as future work. An open question is whether the collaborative training signal degrades when the base detector is already very strong, or whether the gains saturate with larger VLMs.
