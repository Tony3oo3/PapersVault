## Problem

Indoor scene understanding with a single static mmWave radar remains unsolved: specularity limits direct visibility to a small angular region, and existing wireless approaches either require mobile platforms or produce only partial, wall-only reconstructions. No prior system achieves both complete layout reconstruction and object-level detection from a fixed, single radar without optical sensors.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a ~30-second sequence of mmWave frames (20 Hz, ~600 frames) from a single static TI MMWCAS-RF-EVM Cascade radar mounted at 1.2 m, processed as range-angle heatmaps aggregated over the full human trajectory rather than per-frame. Outputs are 2D top-down wall layout polygons and axis-aligned bounding boxes for furniture, both in a shared 2D coordinate frame calibrated against a synchronized Intel Realsense depth camera for ground-truth alignment.

## Method

RISE exploits human-mobility-induced multipath reflections as geometric cues through a three-stage pipeline. First, a Bi-Angular Multipath Enhancement (BAME) module decouples Angle-of-Arrival and Angle-of-Departure beamforming (Eq. 4) to recover off-diagonal ghost reflections that standard AOA=AOD processing suppresses, using 3D CFAR detection in the Range-AOA-AOD cube. Second, a multipath inversion step identifies four ghost targets (two- and three-bounce) via CFAR + RANSAC clustering and solves for reflector point geometry, aggregating estimates across the trajectory into a coarse layout hypothesis. Third, a Sim2Real Hierarchical Diffusion (SRHD) model—trained on a synthetic corpus of 35,000 floor plans with random missing/rotation/scaling augmentations—operates in two stages: a first diffusion model predicts an object segmentation map, and a second, conditioned on the first, reconstructs the full wall layout; at inference, an overlap-based reverse optimization iteratively adjusts latent noise to eliminate physically impossible wall–trajectory intersections.

## Results

On the RISE-Indoor Benchmark (100 trajectories, 50,000 frames), RISE achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 16.03 cm and F1-score of 83.63 for wall layout reconstruction, compared to 39.06 cm and 63.43 for the EMT baseline. For object detection, RISE attains 57.78% IoU and 69.34% Dice across 11 scenes, substantially outperforming EMT+SRHD (7.70% IoU, 12.66% Dice) and BRL (1.17% IoU, 1.82% Dice). The ablation shows progressive gains: Ghost Signal Enhancement alone reduces Chamfer from 39.06 to 32.31 cm, adding the diffusion model brings it to 19.82 cm, and reverse optimization achieves the final 16.32 cm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RISE is compared against two baselines: EMT [9] (a multi-frame mmWave layout reconstruction method) and BRL [22] (a self-supervised single-radar object detector). On layout reconstruction, RISE reduces the Chamfer Distance by 59% (16.03 cm vs. 39.06 cm) and improves F1-score by 20 points (83.63 vs. 63.43) over EMT, demonstrating that the Bi-Angular Multipath Enhancement and Sim2Real Hierarchical Diffusion pipeline recovers substantially more complete wall geometry. On object detection, RISE achieves 57.78% IoU, which is 7.5× higher than EMT+SRHD (7.70%) and 49× higher than BRL (1.17%); the Dice gap is even more pronounced (69.34% vs. 12.66% and 1.82%). BRL, designed for autonomous driving radar, essentially fails in the indoor static-radar setting, producing near-zero detections in 10 of 11 scenes. EMT, while capable of wall reconstruction, cannot recover reflectors from small objects, making it unable to produce meaningful object detections without the SRHD post-processing augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multipath-inversion geometry from Wu et al. [68] (NLOS positioning via two- and three-bounce reflections) into a full indoor scene understanding pipeline, departing from prior static-mmWave work (EMT [9]) that only reconstructed walls and suffered from blind spots and inconsistent ghost visibility. The key conceptual departure is the Bi-Angular Multipath Enhancement (BAME), which identifies that standard MIMO beamforming implicitly assumes AOA ≈ AOD and thereby suppresses off-diagonal ghost reflections—a failure mode not addressed in prior radar processing. On the reconstruction side, RISE introduces a simulation-to-reality hierarchical diffusion model trained on a purpose-built layout simulator, borrowing the generative-reconstruction paradigm from vision and 3D perception but applying it for the first time to fragmented mmWave radar observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RISE establishes the first large-scale benchmark (50K frames, 100 trajectories, 11 environments) for single-static-radar indoor scene understanding, filling a gap that makes systematic comparison possible in a modality previously lacking standardized evaluation. The privacy-preserving angle is practically significant for smart-home and corporate deployments where cameras are unacceptable, and the 60% Chamfer-distance reduction plus first-ever mmWave object detection (58% IoU) demonstrate that a single fixed sensor can approach useful scene understanding. Open questions include generalization to multi-person scenarios, shorter trajectories, 3D reconstruction, and real-time deployment; the current 2D, single-person, 30-second-trajectory setting limits immediate practical adoption.
