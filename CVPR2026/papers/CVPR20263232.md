## Problem

Existing active 3D reconstruction methods either rely on handcrafted geometric heuristics that cannot identify under-reconstructed or occluded regions, or depend on costly per-scene neural-field optimization (NeRF/3DGS) that degrades under sparse views and becomes prohibitively expensive at scale. No prior approach efficiently unifies data-driven geometric confidence with high-level semantic reasoning to guide viewpoint selection under tight view budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263232/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives an initial set of posed RGB observations (optionally with depth) and a total view budget T, then iteratively selects up to T−|O₀| additional viewpoints. Object-level experiments use |O₀|=4 and T=25; scene-level uses |O₀|=15 and T=40. The final output is a 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction (via PGSR) evaluated on held-out test views, with the intermediate dual-field uncertainty map on a shared voxel grid driving all selection decisions.

## Method

AREA3D builds a dual-field uncertainty representation on a shared voxel grid: (1) a geometric field from VGGT's per-pixel depth confidence (trained with a heteroscedastic objective), back-projected via camera intrinsics and poses and aggregated across frames; and (2) a semantic field from InternVL3, prompted with a structured grid-based template to output region tuples (occlusion, geometric, lighting, boundary, texture) with priority scores, converted to soft spatial masks and modulated by vision-feature variability. Both fields are fused into a single visibility-aware utility map. Viewpoint selection proceeds greedily via a priority queue over voxelized candidate seeds, where each seed's utility is scored using precomputed Monte Carlo ray-sampling FOV masks (first-hit termination) rather than online optimization. After each committed view, a frustum-based multiplicative decay reduces uncertainty in the observed region, coupling selection with evidence accumulation and preventing redundant revisits.

## Results

On the scene-level Replica benchmark (40 total views, 15 initial), AREA3D achieves mean PSNR of 30.68 dB, SSIM 0.861, and LPIPS 0.116 across four rooms, outperforming FisherRF (mean PSNR 28.06 dB) by ~2.6 dB on average. On the object-level OmniObject3D benchmark (25 total views, 4 initial), AREA3D reaches mean PSNR 32.39 dB and LPIPS 0.089 across single-object and multi-object scenes, surpassing AIR-Embodied (mean PSNR 29.73 dB) by ~2.7 dB. The advantage is most pronounced in complex multi-object settings (7 objects), where AREA3D gains over 5 dB PSNR over AIR-Embodied. Ablations confirm both the feed-forward geometric field and the VLM semantic field contribute: removing either degrades PSNR by 0.5–3 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FisherRF (scene-level, Replica), AREA3D wins on all four rooms in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the largest gap on office0 (+5.85 dB PSNR) and office4 (+4.00 dB), while the smallest margin is on room0 (+0.12 dB). Against AIR-Embodied (object-level, OmniObject3D), AREA3D dominates across all four scene complexities, gaining +1.24 dB (single-object), +1.51 dB (5 objects), +5.09 dB (7 objects, scenario 1), and +2.84 dB (7 objects, scenario 2) in PSNR; the gap widens sharply with scene complexity, suggesting the dual-field design excels at resolving occlusions in cluttered scenes. Both the naive VLM-based planner and random/uniform sampling are substantially outperformed (by 4–9 dB PSNR in the worst cases), confirming that neither pure semantic reasoning nor passive sampling suffices. The ablation (Ours w/o VLM) shows the feed-forward geometric field alone already beats FisherRF and AIR-Embodied, but adding the VLM semantic field provides a further 0.5–1.1 dB PSNR gain, validating the complementary role of high-level reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

AREA3D departs from the dominant paradigm of active reconstruction that couples uncertainty estimation to per-scene optimized representations (NeRF, 3DGS), where information gain requires costly gradient-based updates. Instead, it repurposes the heteroscedastic confidence output of a feed-forward 3D model (VGGT) as a geometric uncertainty field computable in a single forward pass, and pairs it with a VLM-derived semantic uncertainty field for complementary high-level guidance. This dual-field design is conceptually distinct from FisherRF's information-theoretic approach (which remains tied to a differentiable radiance field) and from VLM-only planners (which lack metric geometry), positioning the work at the intersection of feed-forward 3D perception and embodied language reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is decoupling uncertainty estimation from online optimization, making active view selection feasible under tight budgets without the convergence problems that plague NeRF/3DGS-based methods in sparse-view regimes. However, the VLM's marginal gain over geometry-only selection (~0.5–1 dB PSNR in ablations) raises the question of whether the added inference cost of a large VLM is justified in real-time deployment. Evaluation is confined to simulated environments (Replica, OmniObject3D) with a fixed downstream reconstructor (PGSR), leaving open how the framework generalizes to real-world sensor noise, dynamic scenes, and alternative reconstruction backends. The unified object-to-scene benchmark is a useful contribution for consistent cross-scale evaluation.
