## Problem

State-of-the-art multimodal retrieval models are confined to text-vision pairs and cannot handle composed queries spanning three or more modalities, with audio being critically underserved. Even when LLM-based composers are used, two practical barriers remain: feeding >500 media-encoder tokens per input into an LLM is computationally prohibitive for contrastive training, and collapsing rich multimodal representations into a single embedding via mean pooling or [EOS] discards fine-grained token-level detail needed for high-fidelity retrieval.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any combination of text, RGB images, video clips, and audio clips as either a query or a candidate. Queries are prepended with a natural-language instruction specifying the retrieval task and domain (e.g., 'Find the target audio that matches this modification'), while candidates remain in raw form. Media inputs are first encoded by frozen SigLIP (vision) or QwenAudio (audio) encoders, then resampled to a fixed number of tokens before interleaving into the LLM's token sequence. The output is a single fixed-dimensional embedding vector (L=4096 dimensions by default) compatible with standard approximate nearest-neighbor indexes, produced by the same model for both query and candidate sides.

## Method

OmniRet uses a frozen GTE-Qwen2-1.5B LLM as a universal composer, with only ~84M trainable parameters (LoRA rank-16 on the LLM plus novel modules). A Shared Media Resampler, built on the Perceiver architecture, condenses large media-token sequences into a compact set of latent vectors; a single Perceiver is shared across all modalities while modality-specific latent queries are added to preserve modality sensitivity, and a diversity loss (smooth-L1 on a dropout-sampled pairwise similarity matrix) enforces orthogonality among the resampled tokens. For the final embedding, Attention Sliced Wasserstein Pooling (ASWP) first resamples LLM output hidden states into S=128 latent vectors, then computes 1D Monge couplings between these and S learnable reference points across L=4096 random projections, yielding a histogram-like descriptor; a straight-through maximum (STM) estimator performs hard column-wise selection to produce the final vector, preserving fine-grained distributional structure while remaining a single-vector output. Training uses a combined loss of hard-negative InfoNCE (with adaptive weighting, τ=0.07, β=0.5), hinge triplet loss (margin 0.1), and the diversity loss (weight 0.1), executed in two stages: a warm-up on uni-modality and textual-binding tasks with frozen LLM, followed by full multi-task fine-tuning on ~6.2M pairs from 30 datasets with LoRA enabled and batch size 3072.

## Results

On the extended M-BEIR benchmark (13 retrieval tasks, Recall@5), OmniRet (1.5B) achieves leading performance on 12 out of 13 tasks, including V→T (43.8), T→V (43.2), A→T (66.8), T→A (62.4), and V,T→V (86.2), while trailing PE-Core only on I→I (24.4 vs. 32.0). On the MMEBv2 subset (Recall@1), OmniRet sets new state-of-the-art on all three video tasks: Video CLS (48.6), Video RET (36.5), and Video MRET (43.3). On the novel ACM benchmark (Recall@5), OmniRet achieves 23.0 on composed audio retrieval (A,T→A), outperforming all single-stage baselines, and matches ImageBind on A→V (35.5) while remaining competitive on other audio-visual tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest text-binding baseline PE-Core [3], OmniRet trails only on I→I (24.4 vs. 32.0) but surpasses it on every other shared task, most dramatically on video (V→T: 43.8 vs. 32.3; T→V: 43.2 vs. 29.5). On audio retrieval, OmniRet outperforms the dedicated audio model CLAP [74] by 2.9 points on A→T (66.8 vs. 63.9) and 5.8 points on T→A (62.4 vs. 56.6), and dominates MMT [54] by 17 and 15.6 points respectively. Versus the closest same-size multi-task baseline VLM2VecV2 [50], OmniRet gains 26.2 points on V→T (43.8 vs. 17.6), 24.8 on T→V (43.2 vs. 18.4), and 9.8 on V,T→V (86.2 vs. 76.4), while also leading on all composed image-text tasks. On the ACM benchmark, OmniRet (23.0) substantially outperforms ImageBind [16] (7.32), ViT-Lens [34] (10.1), and OmniBind [70] (15.6) on composed audio retrieval, though it trails the multi-stage QwenOmni+Gemma pipeline [76,68] (44.6) which incurs significant additional inference cost. On audio-visual retrieval, OmniRet matches ImageBind on A→V (35.5) but falls slightly behind on V→A (34.4 vs. 36.3), A→I (24.5 vs. 30.1), and I→A (26.0 vs. 29.7), indicating that dedicated omni-modal alignment models still hold an edge on pure cross-modal matching without compositional text.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniRet extends the LLM-as-composer paradigm established by VLM2Vec and MMEmbed (which handle text and vision) to include audio, while addressing two bottlenecks those works leave open: the computational cost of feeding 500+ media tokens into an LLM, and the information loss from naive single-vector pooling. The Shared Media Resampler adapts the Perceiver architecture for cross-modal token condensation, and the ASWP pooling mechanism is a direct adaptation of PSWE's sliced Wasserstein distance to the embedding-aggregation setting. Unlike ImageBind, which aligns six modalities in a shared space via separate encoders without compositional query understanding, OmniRet uses an LLM to fuse modalities and handle natural-language instructions, enabling composed queries that prior omni-modal models cannot process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ACM benchmark fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark evaluates composed audio retrieval or audio-visual retrieval in a unified framework, and the paper's human-validated construction (87% human accuracy, 96% Gemini text-only accuracy) provides a credible evaluation target for future omni-modal systems. The ASWP pooling technique is potentially broadly applicable to any LLM-based embedding model that needs to preserve fine-grained token structure within a single-vector constraint compatible with ANN indexes. Key limitations include the 1.5B LLM scale, restriction to three modalities (no speech, 3D, or depth), and the fact that on the headline composed-audio task, a multi-stage QwenOmni+Gemma pipeline still outperforms OmniRet (44.6 vs. 23.0), suggesting that true compositional audio understanding remains an open challenge.
