## Problem

Standard stochastic decoding in LLMs and VLMs produces token-level diversity but fails to generate high-level strategic diversity, causing the model to revisit closely related reasoning trajectories with only superficial differences. This mismatch between the variability needed to discover distinct reasoning strategies and the low-level variability of token sampling limits both pass@k inference performance and the efficiency of RL training, where the policy repeatedly explores near-duplicate chains of thought rather than probing qualitatively different reasoning modes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (and optionally an RGB image for VLM tasks) plus a sampled latent vector z ~ N(0, I) from a VAE-learned prior. The latent is decoded into L continuous prefix embeddings (L tunable, typically 1 for SFT and 4–8 for inference/RL) that are prepended to the prompt's token embeddings before autoregressive generation. For VLM tasks, the prefix is injected alongside image tokens encoded by a ViT, and the model outputs a bounding box; for LLM tasks, it outputs a full reasoning trace with a final answer.

## Method

Reasoning Palette constructs a VAE over the model's frozen token embedding layer: the encoder maps mean-pooled embeddings of question-answer pairs to a Gaussian latent, and the decoder reconstructs the embedding, learning a latent space where distinct regions correspond to different reasoning strategies (math, code, QA). At inference or RL rollout time, a sampled latent z is decoded into L prefix embeddings that are prepended to the prompt, steering the model's internal planning before the first output token is produced. Training proceeds in two stages: a brief SFT warm-up (10 iterations, L=1) sensitizes the model to arbitrary prefix embeddings without overfitting, followed by RL (GRPO or RLOO) where the latent acts as a per-episode control variable with a scheduled fraction of rollouts receiving latent conditioning (two-phase or linear-decay schedule) to balance strategic exploration against exploitation. The key distinction from entropy regularization or temperature sampling is that diversity is injected at the level of reasoning architecture—different latents induce qualitatively different organizational and explanatory styles—rather than at the level of individual token probabilities.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B-Base with RLOO, Reasoning Palette (Linear Decay) achieves the best average score of 63.00 across five math benchmarks, improving +3.09 over the RLOO baseline (59.91), with the largest gains on AMC23 (+4.38) and MinervaMath (+4.29). On Qwen3-1.7B-Base with GRPO, the Linear Decay variant reaches 24.05 average (+2.87 over GRPO's 21.18). For VLM referring expression comprehension (pass@32), latent-guided + sampling achieves 87.53 on RefCOCO, 86.03 on RefCOCO+, and 85.7 on RefCOCOg, substantially outperforming both greedy baseline (2.0–4.67) and sampling-only baseline (62.57–72.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two RL baselines: GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and RLOO (Guo et al., 2024), both applied to Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B-Base models trained on DeepMath. Reasoning Palette consistently outperforms both baselines across all six model–algorithm combinations, with average gains ranging from +0.52 (8B + GRPO, Two-Phase) to +3.09 (8B + RLOO, Linear Decay). The Linear Decay schedule generally edges out Two-Phase by ~0.5–1.0 points on average, suggesting a smoother exploration-to-exploitation transition is more effective. Gains are most pronounced on harder benchmarks (AMC23, MinervaMath, OlympiadBench) and smaller models (1.7B), where exploration diversity matters most; on the 4B + GRPO setting, Linear Decay actually drops 3.44 points on AMC23, showing the method is not uniformly beneficial per-benchmark. For VLM tasks, the comparison is against greedy decoding and temperature sampling without latent control: latent-guided + sampling achieves 87.53/86.03/85.7 on RefCOCO/+/g (pass@32), exceeding sampling-only by 22–24 points and greedy-only by 85+ points, confirming that structured latent diversity is complementary to and more effective than token-level stochasticity alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of variational autoencoders, soft prompt/prefix tuning, and RL-based post-training for LLMs. It departs from the standard exploration paradigm in RLVR (token-level stochasticity via temperature or nucleus sampling) by introducing a VAE-learned latent space over mean-pooled question-answer embeddings, whose decoded prefixes act as a 'reasoning palette' conditioning the policy before generation. Conceptually, it extends the prefix-tuning literature [32, 35] by making the prefix a stochastic sample from a structured latent manifold rather than a fixed learned parameter, and it extends GRPO/RLOO [49, 15] by treating the latent as an auxiliary control variable with a scheduled exploration-to-exploitation curriculum. The motivating observation—that a single Gaussian noise embedding before the prompt dramatically boosts pass@k under greedy decoding—connects to activation-steering and internal-representation work [55] but is formalized here through a trained VAE rather than hand-crafted perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, interpretable mechanism for structured exploration in RL training of reasoning models, shifting the diversity knob from token-level randomness to strategy-level latent sampling. The disentangled latent space (math vs. code vs. QA clusters) enables targeted intervention at inference, which is a capability absent in standard RLVR pipelines. However, the RL gains are modest (+1 to +3 points average), the VLM experiments rely on a randomly-initialized decoder rather than the trained VAE (weakening the methodological claim), and the approach has only been validated on mathematical reasoning for the RL component. Open questions include whether the latent space scales to longer-horizon agentic tasks, whether the VAE can be jointly optimized with the policy, and whether the scheduling heuristics generalize beyond the two tested strategies.
