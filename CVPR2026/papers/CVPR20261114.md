## Problem

Few-shot anomaly detection (FSAD) suffers from progressive defect cue fading: subtle anomaly signals are progressively lost as they pass through multi-stage deep feature extraction pipelines, leading to mislocalized regions and imprecise boundaries. Existing FSAD methods treat detection as a static one-shot feature-matching problem and lack any mechanism to counteract this progressive signal degradation across the pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261114/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single query image and K reference normal images (K ∈ {1, 2, 4}) as input, processing them through frozen CLIP ViT-L/14@336px and DINOv2 backbones to extract multi-level patch features (from CLIP layers 6, 12, 18, 24) and fine-grained DINOv2 features. It produces two outputs: a pixel-level anomaly segmentation mask with refined boundaries, and an image-level anomaly score obtained by fusing a global score (from class-token reconstruction residual) with a local score (mean of top-p% segmentation pixels). The model is evaluated on industrial images ranging from 700² to 1024² (MVTec AD) and ~1500×1000 (VisA).

## Method

DCP-SFR is a three-stage pipeline that progressively preserves and refines defect cues. First, a Conditional Anomaly Cue Amplification (CACA) module computes an initial anomaly score map via cosine similarity between learnable prompt embeddings (normal/anomaly) and multi-level CLIP patch features, then amplifies it through a learned non-linear transformation to increase contrast between anomalous and normal regions. Second, an Anomaly Cue-guided Localization (ACL) module reconstructs anomaly-free features using stacked cross-attention layers where the amplified cue map spatially modulates the query at each layer (multiplying by 1−s_amp to suppress anomalous regions and force reliance on normal reference features); the L1 residual between original and reconstructed DINOv2 features yields a precise localization map. Third, a Structure-aware Segmentation Refinement (SSR) module fuses the reconstruction residual with a cosine-similarity regularization map (computed against a memory bank of all reference patch features) and imposes an edge-alignment loss to sharpen boundaries. The image-level classification combines a global score (class-token reconstruction residual passed through a lightweight adapter) with a local score (mean of top-p% segmentation pixels), trained jointly with focal loss, Dice loss, cross-entropy, and the edge consistency loss while keeping both backbones frozen.

## Results

On MVTec AD (1-shot), DCP-SFR achieves 97.3% i-AUROC and 96.9% p-AUROC, with a pixel-level AP of 61.2%, surpassing the closest competitor AnomalyDINO (96.6% i-AUROC, 56.5% p-AP) by 0.7 and 4.7 points respectively. On VisA (1-shot), it reaches 92.0% i-AUROC and 98.2% p-AUROC, outperforming AnomalyDINO by 4.6 points in classification. Performance improves steadily with more references: at 4-shot, MVTec AD i-AUROC rises to 98.0% and VisA p-AUROC to 98.6%, maintaining state-of-the-art results across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCP-SFR outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across both datasets and all shot settings. The closest competitor is AnomalyDINO (WACV 2025), which DCP-SFR surpasses by 0.7 points in MVTec AD i-AUROC (97.3 vs 96.6, 1-shot) but by a much larger 4.7 points in MVTec AD p-AP (61.2 vs 56.5), indicating substantially better pixel-level localization. On VisA, the classification gap is even wider: DCP-SFR leads AnomalyDINO by 4.6 points in 1-shot i-AUROC (92.0 vs 87.4). Against PromptAD (CVPR 2024), the MVTec AD p-AP advantage is 7.3 points (61.2 vs 53.9, 1-shot), and against WinCLIP+ (CVPR 2023) it reaches 13.9 points (61.2 vs 47.3). AnomalyGPT (AAAI 2024) and APRIL-GAN (CVPR 2023) trail by 3–5 points in i-AUROC and 9–14 points in p-AP. InCTRL (CVPR 2024) reports only classification metrics, where DCP-SFR still leads by 3.2 points in 1-shot i-AUROC (97.3 vs 94.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

DCP-SFR sits at the intersection of three established FSAD families—reconstruction-based (FastRecon), feature-comparison (WinCLIP, AnomalyDINO, PatchCore), and prompt-learning (PromptAD, InCTRL)—but reframes the problem around a specific failure mode it calls 'defect cue fading': the progressive attenuation of subtle anomaly signals through multi-layer feature extraction. Rather than treating detection as a static one-shot similarity matching problem (as most prior feature-comparison methods do), it introduces a multi-stage pipeline that amplifies early cues via learnable prompts (CACA), uses those cues to guide cross-attention reconstruction for localization (ACL), and refines boundaries with structural regularization (SSR). The architectural ingredients—CLIP/DINOv2 backbones, cross-attention, cosine similarity matching, focal/dice losses—are individually well-known; the contribution is their orchestrated sequencing to preserve spatial and structural information that prior methods discard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'defect cue fading' diagnosis is a useful and concrete way to explain why few-shot anomaly detection struggles with small or low-contrast defects, and the multi-stage preservation strategy offers a principled remedy that practitioners can adopt. However, the gains over the strongest baseline (AnomalyDINO) are modest—typically 0.5–1.5 points on AUROC and 1–5 points on AP/F1—raising the question of whether the added pipeline complexity (three modules, multiple loss terms, two frozen backbones) is justified over simpler feature-matching approaches. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond the two industrial benchmarks tested, how it behaves under domain shift to entirely unseen product categories, and whether the learnable prompt mechanism can be replaced by more scalable alternatives as defect taxonomies grow.
