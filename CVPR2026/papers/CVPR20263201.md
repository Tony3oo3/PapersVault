## Problem

Existing world models for driving either decouple spatial and temporal reasoning (binding them to fixed camera configurations and ego-motion patterns) or impose explicit 3D geometric priors like point clouds and BEV features (hindering generalization beyond training distributions). No prior approach provides a unified, geometry-free framework that jointly enforces multi-view spatial consistency and frame-to-frame temporal coherence while remaining adaptable to arbitrary sensor setups and open-world conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263201/cropped_page1_idx50.webp)

## Input / Output

RAYNOVA accepts optional control signals—text descriptions (via T5), 3D object bounding boxes (projected to image space), and HD map elements (sampled as 3D points and projected)—along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics for each view and frame. It outputs multi-view video at 384×672 resolution, where each frame is represented as K multiscale token maps generated autoregressively from coarse to fine. The same model can synthesize novel-view images by simply specifying shifted camera poses, without any explicit 3D scene representation.

## Method

RAYNOVA employs a dual-causal autoregressive architecture that factors the joint distribution over multi-view, multi-frame images along both scale (coarse-to-fine within a frame) and time (frame-by-frame conditioned on all past views) topological orders. The key mechanism is an isotropic 4D relative position embedding: each visual token is associated with a 7D Plücker ray (3D moment, 3D direction, and timestep), and attention logits are computed via a 7D rotary position encoding that encodes only the relative displacement between token pairs in ray space, eliminating dependence on absolute camera positions. The transformer alternates image-wise self-attention (inherited from Infinity for per-image realism), a global causal self-attention module (randomly initialized, operating across all views/frames/scales with the ray embedding for spatio-temporal consistency), and image-wise cross-attention for condition alignment. A recurrent training paradigm with per-frame backpropagation, latent-feature KV caching, and random bitwise error injection on scale-wise inputs mitigates distribution drift during long-horizon autoregressive generation.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, RAYNOVA achieves FID 10.5 and FVD 91 at 384×672 resolution, substantially outperforming all baselines on both image quality and temporal coherence. It also delivers the highest throughput at 1.96 images/s, exceeding MagicDrive (1.76) and far surpassing diffusion-based methods like DriveDreamer (0.37). For novel view synthesis with 1m/2m/4m camera shifts, RAYNOVA achieves FID 14.11/15.58/17.48 and FVD 117.20/122.60/143.48, roughly 2× better than the next-best baseline (OmniRe) on FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAYNOVA outperforms all six video-generation baselines on FID (10.5 vs. best baseline 14.9 for DriveDreamer) and FVD (91 vs. best 139 for Panacea), while also achieving the highest throughput (1.96 img/s vs. 1.76 for MagicDrive). Against diffusion-based methods (DriveDreamer, DrivingDiffusion, BEVWorld), the advantage is most pronounced on FVD—RAYNOVA's 91 is 2.7×–3.7× lower—reflecting the temporal coherence benefit of its dual-causal autoregressive design. For object-condition fidelity, RAYNOVA reaches 89% of oracle NDS via StreamPETR (41.9 vs. Panacea's 32.1) and 99% via SparseFusion (72.0 vs. X-Drive's 69.6). On map-condition fidelity, RAYNOVA improves mIoU over MagicDrive by 10 points in the camera-only setting (34.9 vs. 28.9) and by 3 points in the multi-sensor setting (49.9 vs. 47.0). In novel view synthesis, RAYNOVA dominates all three geometry-based baselines (StreetGaussian, OmniRe, FreeVS) by a wide margin—e.g., at 4m shift its FID of 17.48 is less than half that of StreetGaussian (67.44)—despite using no explicit 3D representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAYNOVA builds directly on the 'next-scale prediction' visual autoregressive paradigm (Infinity, VARM) and extends it from single-image to multi-view, multi-frame video generation via a dual-causal (scale × time) autoregressive formulation. It departs from prior driving world models (GAIA, MagicDrive, Panacea, X-Drive, BEVWorld) that impose explicit 3D geometric priors (point clouds, BEV features, Gaussian splatting) or fixed camera-adjacency assumptions, instead adopting a geometry-free relative Plücker-ray positional encoding inspired by camera-ray-space representations (LVM, Rayzer) but reformulated in relative rather than absolute coordinates. The rotary position embedding (RoPE) is extended from 1D/2D to a 7D space (3D moment + 3D direction + time), unifying spatial and temporal reasoning in a single attention module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a geometry-free, relative ray-space representation can outperform explicit 3D-prior methods on both video generation and novel view synthesis challenges the prevailing assumption that driving world models require structured 3D scene representations for spatial consistency. The zero-shot generalization to unseen camera configurations has direct practical value for simulation and data augmentation in autonomous driving, where sensor suites vary across fleets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer horizons and more diverse driving conditions beyond the nuScenes/nuPlan distribution, and whether the isotropic ray-space formulation transfers to non-driving domains (e.g., robotics, indoor navigation) where camera motion is less constrained.
