## Problem

Pixel-wise MLLMs require complex region-level encoders, specialized segmentation decoders, and task-specific losses that are incompatible with standard next-token prediction training and make reinforcement learning infeasible. Existing mask representations (bounding boxes, polygons, RLE, or mask images) either sacrifice precision or incur enormous token costs, preventing MLLMs from learning mask understanding and generation as simply as they learn language.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263979/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image, a text instruction, and optionally a region mask as input, and produces segmentation masks, text descriptions, or discrete mask tokens as output. The key design constraint is that any 2D mask is compressed into exactly two discrete tokens drawn from a shared codebook, which are appended to the MLLM vocabulary as special words. This means a mask occupies the same token budget as two ordinary words, and the SAMTok decoder reconstructs the full-resolution mask from the original image plus these two tokens at inference time.

## Method

SAMTok is a mask VAE with residual vector quantization, where both the encoder and decoder are instantiated as SAM models. The encoder (a SAM model with the final mask prediction head removed) encodes a 2D mask into a single continuous embedding z via dense prompt embeddings added to image features; a two-stage residual vector quantizer then discretizes z into two codebook entries [e1, e2] through successive nearest-neighbor lookups. The decoder (a full SAM model) treats [e1, e2] as a sparse prompt embedding and reconstructs the mask via cross-attention with image features. Trained on 209M masks with cross-entropy + Dice reconstruction loss and a commitment loss, SAMTok is then plugged into QwenVL by adding codebook-sized special tokens to the vocabulary, so all mask understanding and generation tasks reduce to standard next-token prediction. For reinforcement learning, a purely textual answer-matching reward checks whether predicted mask tokens appear in the ground-truth string, enabling GRPO without any detokenization or external segmentation model.

## Results

On the GRES text-to-mask benchmark, Qwen25VL-SAMTok (3B, RL) achieves 76.7 gIoU, 73.7 cIoU, and 77.1 N-acc on average across three splits, surpassing the previous best (MLLMSeg, 8B) by +2.8 gIoU and +6.7 N-acc. On the GCG interleaved text-mask generation benchmark, Qwen25VL-SAMTok (7B) attains 38.2 AP50, 72.6 mIoU, and 48.9 Recall on validation, exceeding Sa2VA (8B) by +5.0 AP50 and +4.8 Recall. On multi-round interactive segmentation (MR-RefCOCO/+/g), Qwen3VL-SAMTok (4B) reaches 86.7 cIoU at Round #2, outperforming SegLLM (7B) by +7.0. On zero-shot GroundingSuite, the 3B model achieves 67.8 gIoU, beating the previous best EVF-SAM (1B) by +5.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GRES, Qwen25VL-SAMTok (3B, RL) outperforms all baselines by a wide margin: +2.8 gIoU over MLLMSeg (8B, 73.9), +6.2 over SAM4MLLM (8B, 70.5), +4.3 over HiMTok (8B, 72.4), and +14.5 over LISA (7B, 62.2), while using fewer parameters than every 8B competitor. On GCG, the 7B model surpasses Sa2VA (8B) by +5.0 AP50 and +4.8 Recall, GLaMM (7B) by +7.4 AP50, and LISA (7B) by +13.0 AP50, establishing new SOTA on both captioning and segmentation metrics. On multi-round interactive segmentation, Qwen3VL-SAMTok (4B) beats SegLLM (7B) by +7.0 cIoU at Round #2 on MR-RefCOCO and by +8.3 on MR-PACO, while the 3B variant already exceeds SegLLM by +3.6. On RefCOCO referring segmentation, the 4B model achieves 83.4 val cIoU, edging out Sa2VA (4B, 82.4), PaDT Pro (3B, 81.3), and UniPixel (3B, 81.9). On zero-shot GroundingSuite, the 3B model leads all baselines by +5.2 gIoU over EVF-SAM (1B) and +10.2 over LISA (7B). The only near-tie is on DLC-Bench region captioning, where Qwen3VL-SAMTok (4B, 65.6) trails the expert model DAM (3B, 67.3) by 1.7 points, though it substantially outperforms general MLLMs like Qwen2.5VL-7B (41.2) and GPT-4o (61.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMTok builds on SAM2 as its segmentation backbone and on residual vector quantization (Lee et al., CVPR 2022) to discretize mask embeddings, but departs fundamentally from prior pixel-wise MLLMs (LISA, GLaMM, Sa2VA, OMG-LLaVA) that rely on continuous segmentation embeddings, specialized decoders, and task-specific losses. The key conceptual shift is treating region masks as a two-token 'language' rather than as pixel-level outputs requiring dedicated architectures, making mask understanding and generation reducible to standard next-token prediction. For reinforcement learning, the paper also departs from prior pixel-wise RL methods (Seg-Zero, VisionReasoner, LENS) that require external mask decoders for reward computation, instead using purely textual answer-matching over discrete mask tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm of compressing arbitrary masks into two discrete tokens that plug into any MLLM's vocabulary via standard NTP training is a significant simplification that removes architectural modifications, specialized segmentation losses, and co-training complexity from pixel-wise MLLM development. The demonstration that purely textual rewards (no mask IoU, no external decoder) can drive substantial RL gains in mask generation opens a path to applying the full RL toolchain from language modeling to pixel-level tasks. Open questions include whether the two-token budget suffices for extremely complex or multi-object masks, how the approach extends to video segmentation, and whether the SAM2 backbone dependency limits generalization to non-natural-image domains.
