## Problem

Existing semi-dense feature matching methods rely on visual-only learning and expensive 3D supervision, causing overfitting and poor generalization under domain shifts and illumination changes. Purely visual features fail in textureless or repetitive regions where geometric cues are ambiguous, and frozen VFM backbones still struggle under large appearance variations. The integration of domain-invariant semantic information from vision-language models into feature matching pipelines remained entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (resized to a maximum edge of 800 px during training and 1200 px during evaluation) and produces semi-dense correspondences with sub-pixel coordinates. Beyond the image pair, the pipeline consumes frozen CLIP text embeddings for 150 ADE20K class labels (with learnable prompts) as instance-level semantic queries, and derives four illumination-invariant physical priors (H, C, W, O) from each input image via Kubelka-Munk theory before feeding them into the FPN backbone.

## Method

TextFM is a detector-free coarse-to-fine matching pipeline built on the LoFTR/EfficientLoFTR architecture, with three key innovations. First, a language-guided coarse feature transformation module generates textual queries from a frozen CLIP encoder (with learnable prompts), refines coarse pixel features via cross-attention against these textual prototypes (text-aware refinement), and then aligns pixel features with refined textual queries through a transformer decoder to produce a contextual correlation feature map for dual-softmax + MNN coarse matching. Second, the visual backbone fuses LoRA-finetuned DINOv2-L features (only ~3M trainable parameters via low-rank token-MLP adaptation) with multi-scale features extracted from illumination-invariant physical priors in an FPN, combining semantic robustness with spatial fidelity. Third, coarse matches are refined to sub-pixel accuracy using fine-level features following the EfficientLoFTR strategy, with the entire pipeline trained end-to-end on MegaDepth using negative log-likelihood losses at both coarse and fine levels.

## Results

On the in-domain MegaDepth benchmark, TextFM achieves AUC@5°/10°/20° of 58.0/73.6/84.6, outperforming all sparse and semi-dense baselines (best semi-dense: EfficientLoFTR at 55.3/71.4/83.1) while remaining competitive with dense methods (ROMA: 62.4/76.4/86.2). On out-of-domain ScanNet, TextFM reaches 22.7/43.2/59.9, a substantial +4.3/+7.4/+7.0 AUC@5°/10°/20° improvement over EfficientLoFTR. On the newly introduced MegaDepth-Sync day-night benchmark (N2N), TextFM achieves 45.1/62.7/76.1, surpassing the next-best semi-dense method (EfficientLoFTR: 42.6/60.1/74.0) and all sparse methods. For visual localization, TextFM attains 73.85 mean accuracy on InLoc and 92.70 on Aachen v1.1, both the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextFM outperforms all sparse baselines (LightGlue, OmniGlue) by a wide margin across every dataset, e.g., +10.4 AUC@5° on ScanNet over LightGlue. Among semi-dense methods, TextFM consistently beats LoFTR (+5.5 AUC@5° on MegaDepth), MatchFormer, TopicFM, and DRC-Net, with the closest competitor being EfficientLoFTR, which TextFM surpasses by +2.7 AUC@5° on MegaDepth and +4.3 on ScanNet. On the challenging day-night MegaDepth-Sync benchmark, TextFM gains +2.5 AUC@5° over EfficientLoFTR (N2N) and +1.9 over TopicFM (N2D), confirming the benefit of language guidance and physical priors under illumination shifts. Against dense methods (DKM, ROMA), TextFM trails by ~2–4 AUC@5° on in-domain MegaDepth but narrows the gap to ~2.7 on ScanNet and ~2.7 on N2N, while running at roughly 2.7–3.9× lower inference time (183.5 ms vs. 492.6/707.3 ms). On visual localization, TextFM achieves the best mean scores on both InLoc (73.85 vs. 73.63 for EfficientLoFTR) and Aachen v1.1 (92.70 vs. 92.28 for LightGlue), with the largest night-time gain on Aachen (+2.8 over EfficientLoFTR at the 0.25 m/2° threshold).

## Relation to Prior Work

TextFM extends the detector-free coarse-to-fine matching paradigm established by LoFTR and EfficientLoFTR by introducing a language-guided module that uses VLM text embeddings as instance-level queries to enhance coarse feature discriminability. It departs from prior VFM integration approaches (e.g., OmniGlue, ROMA) that rely on frozen backbones by adopting LoRA-based fine-tuning, which the ablations show outperforms both frozen and fully fine-tuned variants. The method also incorporates illumination-invariant physical priors from Kubelka-Munk theory, a technique previously applied to day-night domain adaptation but not yet integrated into feature matching pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that domain-invariant textual semantics from VLMs can meaningfully improve feature matching under domain shift, opening a direction for cross-modal matching that goes beyond purely visual cues. The MegaDepth-Sync benchmark addresses a real gap in evaluating illumination robustness, though its synthetic nature (generated by an image translation model) may not fully capture the complexity of real day-night transitions. Open questions include whether language guidance scales to open-vocabulary or novel-category matching, whether the physical priors approach generalizes beyond the tested lighting extremes, and whether the LoRA-VFM recipe transfers to other geometric perception tasks beyond matching.
