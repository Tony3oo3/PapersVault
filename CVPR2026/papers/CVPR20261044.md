## Problem

Existing video generation paradigms face a fundamental trade-off: full-sequence models denoise all frames jointly with bidirectional attention but incur quadratic attention cost, enforce fixed generation length, and cannot stream; autoregressive models enable streaming but suffer from exposure bias (error accumulation across frames) and are constrained to causal attention for KV caching. No prior method simultaneously avoids error accumulation, supports variable-length generation, and reduces computational cost relative to full-sequence denoising.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts, one or more reference frames (for image-to-video), or an ordered set of context frames (for interpolation/scene completion), with each context frame designated as either 'active' (allowing insertions to its right) or 'passive' (blocking insertions). The output is a variable-length video sequence whose length is learned jointly with content — the model is never told the target number of frames or the gap sizes between context frames. Experiments use 256-resolution frames, up to 145 frames at 16 FPS, with a 32× spatial and 8× temporal downsampling autoencoder.

## Method

Flowception couples continuous flow-matching denoising with discrete frame insertions in a single unified model, producing a coupled ODE-jump process over variable-length sequences. At each sampling step, the DiT-based architecture (2.1B params, 38 attention blocks) predicts two outputs per frame position: a velocity field for denoising and a Poisson insertion rate for placing new noise frames. Per-frame time values (rather than a global timestep) track each frame's denoising progress, with a linear scheduler κ(t)=t controlling the fraction of visible frames over a global extended time τ_g ∈ [0,2]. Training uses extended time values τ_i ∈ [-1,2] to sample the correct distribution of visible/missing frames, with a Poisson NLL loss on insertion rates and a standard flow-matching velocity loss. Because early in sampling only a small subset of frames is active, the expected quadratic attention cost averages to 1/3 of full-sequence Flow Matching during training and 2/3 during sampling, while the learned insertion mechanism avoids the sequential commitment that causes AR error accumulation.

## Results

On the Image-to-Video task (145 frames, 256 resolution), Flowception achieves the best FVD across all three datasets: 164.73 on Kinetics-600 (vs. 204.65 Full-Seq, 201.34 Autoreg), 25.21 on Tai-Chi-HD (vs. 27.30, 25.30), and 21.80 on RealEstate10K (vs. 26.17, 47.48). Flowception also leads on most VBench quality metrics, e.g., imaging quality of 51.18 and dynamic degree of 78.59 on RealEstate10K. For Text-to-Video (LTX-2B, CFG=7.0, 50 NFEs), Flowception improves imaging quality to 51.37 and aesthetic quality to 49.56 over the original LTX (49.22, 48.25), while sampling ~30% faster in wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Full-Sequence baseline, Flowception wins on FVD across all three datasets (e.g., 21.80 vs. 26.17 on RealEstate10K, a 17% reduction) and on most VBench sub-metrics including imaging quality, background consistency, and aesthetic quality. Against the causal Autoregressive baseline, the FVD gap is even larger on RealEstate10K (21.80 vs. 47.48) and Kinetics-600 (164.73 vs. 201.34), while on Tai-Chi-HD the two are nearly tied (25.21 vs. 25.30). For the Text-to-Video comparison with LTX-Video [13], Flowception (finetuned from LTX-2B) improves imaging quality from 49.22 to 51.37 and aesthetic quality from 48.25 to 49.56 over the original LTX, while slightly trailing on dynamic degree (52.43 vs. 53.90) and subject consistency (91.40 vs. 92.30). Flowception also samples ~30% faster in wall-clock time than the full-sequence LTX baseline on a single H200 GPU. The paper additionally shows that Flowception is more robust to local attention windows than the full-sequence model, suggesting better scalability for long videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flowception unifies two existing generative modeling frameworks — continuous Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) for denoising and discrete Edit Flows (Havasi et al., 2025) for sequence-length changes — into a single coupled ODE-jump process over variable-length video sequences. The insertion-rate prediction with Poisson NLL and the time-agnostic rate design directly follow OneFlow (Nguyen et al., 2025), while the backbone is a DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) with per-frame AdaLN modulation and VideoROPE positional embeddings. Conceptually, Flowception departs from both full-sequence flow matching (which denoises all frames jointly at a shared timestep) and autoregressive generation (which commits frames sequentially), by allowing frames to be inserted at learned positions while all visible frames are simultaneously denoised at heterogeneous per-frame timesteps — a formulation not present in prior AR-with-overlap methods that maintain strict left-to-right insertion order.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3× training FLOPs reduction (from the linear insertion schedule) and ~30% sampling speedup make long-video generation substantially more tractable, while the variable-length, non-autoregressive design eliminates the error-accumulation drift that plagues AR methods without sacrificing bidirectional attention. The framework's ability to natively support I2V, T2V, frame interpolation, and scene completion by simply toggling active/passive context frames is a practical unification that avoids task-specific architectural changes. Key limitations include the doubled number of flow iterations needed for complete denoising (τ_g ∈ [0,2] vs [0,1]), and the observation that the emergent coarse-to-fine insertion order, while effective, is not explicitly controlled — exploring learned or scheduled insertion policies for further efficiency gains is identified as future work.
