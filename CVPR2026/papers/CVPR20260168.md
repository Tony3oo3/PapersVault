## Problem

Existing 3D occupancy prediction methods rely on in-domain annotations and precise sensor-rig calibration, limiting scalability and out-of-domain generalization. While visual geometry foundation models (e.g., Dust3R, MUSt3R) generalize well, they lack the metric-scale accuracy, cluttered geometry completion, and urban-scene adaptation simultaneously required for occupancy prediction. This paper addresses the gap by presenting the first unconstrained urban 3D occupancy model that operates zero-shot on out-of-domain, uncalibrated scenes.

## Input / Output

OccAny accepts unposed, uncalibrated RGB images in three configurations: a sequence of 5 frames from a single camera, a single monocular frame, or all surround-view cameras at one timestep. Input frames are resized to 512-pixel width with varying aspect ratios. The model outputs metric-scale 3D occupancy voxels (obtained by aggregating all predicted pointmaps and voxelizing via trilinear interpolation), SAM2-like segmentation features that support promptable instance and semantic segmentation through the Grounded SAM2 pipeline, per-pixel metric pointmaps in both global and local camera coordinates, and registered camera poses.

## Method

OccAny extends MUSt3R's multi-view transformer with a scene memory by adding a SAM2 feature prediction head and a separate Novel View Rendering (NVR) stage. Segmentation Forcing distills SAM2 features into the geometry network via a confidence-weighted MSE loss, regularizing pointmap predictions with spatially and temporally consistent segmentation cues—particularly effective where LiDAR supervision is sparse. The NVR stage uses a smaller 6-block encoder (distilled from the 24-block reconstruction encoder) and a decoder initialized from the reconstruction decoder to render pointmaps and segmentation features for arbitrary novel views conditioned on the frozen scene memory. At inference, Test-time View Augmentation (TTVA) samples novel camera poses along the predicted trajectory with forward, lateral, and angular offsets to densify occluded geometry before final voxelization. The model is trained in two stages on five urban datasets (Waymo, DDAD, PandaSet, VKITTI2, ONCE) using projected LiDAR pointmaps as metric supervision, with the reconstruction stage initialized from MUSt3R and the rendering stage initialized from the trained reconstruction decoder.

## Results

In the Sequence setting, OccAny achieves 25.91% IoU on SemanticKITTI and 23.55% IoU on Occ3D-NuScenes, surpassing the best zero-shot baselines (CUT3R* at 15.93% and DA3 at 19.30%) by roughly 10 and 4 points respectively. In the more challenging Monocular setting, OccAny reaches 24.03% IoU on SemanticKITTI, outperforming all zero-shot baselines by over 11 points and even exceeding in-domain self-supervised methods SelfOcc (21.97%) and OccNeRF (22.81%). In the Surround-view setting on Occ3D-NuScenes, OccAny achieves 34.15% IoU, beating all zero-shot methods and in-domain approaches like DistillNeRF (29.11%) and SimpleOcc (33.92%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OccAny decisively outperforms all five zero-shot visual geometry baselines (MUSt3R, CUT3R, VGGT, AnySplat, DA3) across all three evaluation settings. On SemanticKITTI sequence, OccAny (25.91% IoU) leads the nearest baseline CUT3R* (15.93%) by ~10 points; on Occ3D-NuScenes sequence, it beats DA3 (19.30%) by 4.25 points. In the Monocular setting, OccAny (24.03%) surpasses not only all zero-shot methods (best: CUT3R* at 13.03%) but also in-domain self-supervised SOTAs SelfOcc (21.97%) and OccNeRF (22.81%) despite never being trained on SemanticKITTI. In the Surround-view setting, OccAny (34.15%) exceeds in-domain methods DistillNeRF (29.11%) and SimpleOcc (33.92%) but remains behind the most recent in-domain methods GaussTR (45.19%) and SelfOcc (45.01%). The OccAny_base ablation (fine-tuned MUSt3R without Segmentation Forcing or NVR) only marginally improves over baselines (16.09% vs. 15.93% on SemKITTI), confirming that the two proposed strategies are the primary source of the large gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

OccAny extends the MUSt3R/Dust3R family of visual geometry foundation models—originally designed for general-purpose 3D reconstruction—by specializing them for urban occupancy prediction through two novel components: Segmentation Forcing (distilling SAM2 features to regularize geometry) and a Novel View Rendering pipeline for test-time geometry completion. It departs from both supervised occupancy methods (which require dense voxel annotations and precise sensor calibration) and self-supervised occupancy methods (which still need in-domain training data and known camera parameters) by training once on five heterogeneous urban datasets and operating zero-shot on arbitrary out-of-domain scenes. The paper positions itself as the first model to unify sequential, monocular, and surround-view occupancy prediction in a single unconstrained framework, repurposing the pointmap-prediction paradigm of geometry foundation models for a task they were not originally designed for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a single model trained on diverse urban data can generalize zero-shot to unseen sensor rigs and environments, addressing a critical scalability bottleneck for autonomous driving fleets with heterogeneous hardware. The SAM2-like feature output enables promptable segmentation without a separate model, simplifying the pipeline. However, the authors acknowledge a substantial gap remains to fully-supervised in-domain methods, and the approach still relies on LiDAR-projected pointmaps during training, limiting its applicability to LiDAR-free scenarios. Open questions include whether the gap to supervised SOTA can be closed with more training data or architectural improvements, and whether the NVR/TTVA strategy scales efficiently to longer sequences or higher-resolution inputs.
