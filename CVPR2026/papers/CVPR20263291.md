## Problem

Existing customized video generation methods lack object-level guidance for both subject structure and motion trajectories, resulting in generalized subject appearances and incorrect motion patterns (e.g., missing hands, wrong human-object interactions). Furthermore, DiT-based backbones lack the spatial-temporal layer separation found in U-Net architectures, so naively combining subject and motion LoRAs during inference causes feature entanglement, artifacts, and degraded video quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263291/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of reference subject images (resized to 512×512), a reference motion video (49 frames at 576×320), and a text prompt containing special tokens V* and S* to designate subject and motion. It produces a 49-frame customized video at 15 fps and 832×480 resolution via a 50-step DDIM sampler with classifier-free guidance. SAM-generated masks are applied during subject training to restrict the model's attention to the subject region and prevent background overfitting.

## Method

SMRABooth is a stage-by-stage LoRA fine-tuning framework with three components. First, the Subject Representation Alignment (SuRA) module aligns intermediate DiT features with DINOv2 ViT patch embeddings via a cosine similarity loss, training a subject LoRA (rank 32) under a SAM-masked velocity prediction loss to preserve global spatial structure and semantics. Second, the Motion Representation Alignment (MoRA) module decodes denoised latents back to pixel space, computes optical flow with SEA-RAFT on both reference and generated frames, and enforces an L1 flow-matching loss to train a motion LoRA (rank 64) that captures appearance-independent motion trajectories. Third, a subject-motion association decoupling strategy exploits LoRA sparsity: subject LoRA is injected only into Q, K, and FFN.0 layers while motion LoRA targets V, O, FFN.0, and FFN.2 layers, and a timestep threshold (T_point=15) modulates subject LoRA weight—lower before the threshold to prioritize motion recovery, higher after to refine appearance—preventing the spatial-temporal entanglement inherent to DiT architectures.

## Results

On the DiT-based WAN2.1 backbone, SMRABooth achieves the best scores across all nine evaluation metrics. The headline gains are in Motion Fidelity (62.89 vs. 45.75 for DualReal and 60.08 for WAN2.1+LoRAs) and semantic alignment (CLIP-T 0.363, CLIP-I 0.700, DINO-I 0.519), all surpassing the strongest baseline DualReal. Perceptual quality metrics also improve: PickScore 21.14, Aesthetic Quality 62.18, and Imaging Quality 67.46. In user studies (100 raters, 8,400 ratings), SMRABooth leads on all four criteria, with Appearance Similarity at 4.178/5 and Video Quality at 4.244/5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMRABooth outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest DiT-based competitor DualReal, the most striking margin is Motion Fidelity (62.89 vs. 45.75, a +17.14 gap), showing that the optical-flow alignment module (MoRA) captures object-level motion far more faithfully than DualReal's joint-training approach. On subject fidelity, SMRABooth edges out DualReal on CLIP-I (0.700 vs. 0.692) and DINO-I (0.519 vs. 0.509), with a larger gap over WAN2.1+LoRAs (0.700 vs. 0.681). The base WAN2.1 model without customization is far behind on all metrics (e.g., DINO-I 0.165, Motion Fidelity 35.18), confirming that the LoRA-based customization is essential. On the U-Net backbone (ZeroScope), qualitative comparisons show SMRABooth preserves subject identity and motion where DreamVideo, MotionBooth, and MotionDirector each fail on at least one of the two (e.g., MotionDirector produces nearly static videos, MotionBooth loses subject consistency). In the user study, SMRABooth leads DualReal by 0.31 on Prompt Alignment, 0.45 on Motion Similarity, 0.65 on Appearance Similarity, and 0.28 on Video Quality (all on a 5-point scale), with statistically significant 95% confidence intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMRABooth extends the subject-and-motion customized video generation paradigm established by U-Net-based methods (DreamVideo, MotionBooth, MotionDirector) to DiT-based backbones like WAN2.1, where the absence of a clear spatial-temporal layer separation creates a new interference problem when combining subject and motion LoRAs. The core methodological departure is the use of external representation alignment—DINOv2 patch embeddings for subject structure and SEA-RAFT optical flow for motion trajectories—as auxiliary supervision during LoRA fine-tuning, rather than relying solely on the diffusion velocity loss. The subject-motion association decoupling strategy, which exploits empirically observed sparsity in LoRA injection locations and denoising timing, is a novel practical contribution that has no direct analogue in prior U-Net-based customization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the video generation community shifts from U-Net to DiT architectures, the entanglement of spatial and temporal processing in DiT blocks is a practical bottleneck for any method that separately fine-tunes appearance and motion; SMRABooth provides a concrete, well-ablated solution that could be adopted by subsequent DiT-based customization work. The sparse-layer finding (only Q, K, FFN.0 for subject; V, O, FFN.0, FFN.2 for motion) offers a reusable heuristic for parameter-efficient video customization. Limitations include reliance on two external encoders (DINOv2, SEA-RAFT) that add inference/training overhead, an empirically chosen T_point that may not transfer across architectures, and evaluation on a modest set of 30 subjects and 21 motion types. Open questions include whether the sparse layer selection generalizes to other DiT variants and whether the approach extends to multi-subject or long-video settings.
