## Problem

Instance-level object segmentation across egocentric and exocentric views is severely challenged by large scale, perspective, and occlusion differences that destabilize direct pixel-level matching. While geometry-aware transformers like VGGT provide reliable object-level feature alignment, their pixel-level point projections exhibit systematic drift in ego-exo scenarios, causing dense segmentation to fail. This paper identifies and bridges the gap between VGGT's consistent internal attention and its unreliable dense projections.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image with a binary object mask (the query) and a target image from a different viewpoint, both processed at 518×518 resolution through a frozen VGGT encoder. Five representative points are sampled from the source mask via K-Means and projected to the target view by VGGT's track head to serve as geometric anchors. The output is a per-pixel sigmoid segmentation mask in the target view, refined over two iterative passes to sharpen boundaries and fill occluded regions.

## Method

VGGT-S augments a frozen VGGT encoder with a lightweight, trainable Union Segmentation Head comprising three coordinated stages. Mask Prompt Fusion encodes the source mask into an embedding added to source features, then a Bottleneck Fusion module (downsample → joint self-attention across both views → upsample) couples geometric and semantic cues at 37×37 resolution. Point-Guided Prediction uses VGGT's track head to project K-Means-sampled source points into the target frame, then applies two decoder blocks with bidirectional point-to-image and image-to-point cross-attention to generate an initial mask via per-pixel dot products. Mask Refinement iteratively re-invokes a lightweight decoder (2 iterations by default) to correct boundary misalignments and fill occlusions. Training employs a Single-Image Self-Supervised strategy that generates pseudo-pairs via VGGT-adaptive and VGGT-non-adaptive augmentations on a 1/20 subset of SA-1B, eliminating the need for paired ego-exo annotations while enabling strong zero-shot generalization.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D benchmark, VGGT-S achieves state-of-the-art average IoU of 67.7% (Ego→Exo) and 68.0% (Exo→Ego) in the supervised setting, surpassing the previous best method DOMR by 18.0% and 12.8% respectively. In the zero-shot (correspondence-free pretrained) setting, VGGT-S still reaches 54.1% and 58.4% IoU, exceeding the fully-supervised DOMR baseline by 4.4% and 3.2%. On the MvMHAT dataset, the correspondence-free pretrained model fine-tuned for 1 epoch achieves 80.7% AP, outperforming DOMR (71.1%) by 9.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGGT-S decisively outperforms all 8 baselines on both Ego→Exo and Exo→Ego tasks. Against the previous SOTA DOMR [31] (49.7/55.2 IoU), VGGT-S gains +18.0/+12.8 points, and against the LLM-based ObjectRelator [17] (45.4/50.9), the margin widens to +22.3/+17.1. The zero-shot VGGT-S variant (54.1/58.4) surpasses the fully-supervised DOMR by +4.4/+3.2, demonstrating that the geometry-enhanced design generalizes without paired annotations. On MvMHAT, the correspondence-free pretrained model fine-tuned for just 1 epoch reaches 80.7% AP, beating DOMR (71.1%) by 9.6% and the original MvMHAT method [20] (63.8%) by 16.9%. Notably, VGGT-S relies solely on spatial (single-frame) features yet outperforms the spatio-temporal XView-XMem [21] by a large margin (67.7 vs. 36.9 on Ego→Exo), indicating that geometric cues from VGGT are more informative than temporal context for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGGT-S builds on VGGT as a frozen geometry-consistent encoder and departs from prior cross-view segmentation methods (DOMR, ObjectRelator, PSALM) that rely primarily on semantic or linguistic cues for matching. Its key conceptual shift is injecting explicit 3D point correspondences—tracked via VGGT's track head—as sparse geometric anchors into a transformer-based segmentation head, rather than treating cross-view transfer as a purely appearance-matching problem. The single-image self-supervised training strategy is directly inspired by MASA's augmentation-based pseudo-correspondence generation, extendeding that idea from single-view instance tracking to cross-view mask prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful result is that a correspondence-free pretrained variant (trained on 1/20 of SA-1B with self-supervised augmentations) surpasses the previous fully-supervised SOTA by 3–4% IoU, suggesting that paired ego-exo annotations may not be strictly necessary for strong cross-view segmentation. A useful diagnostic contribution is the finding that VGGT's pixel-level point projections drift under large viewpoint changes while its internal feature alignment remains consistent, which clarifies where geometry-based methods break down. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond egogo-Exo4D to other large-baseline settings, whether unfreezing VGGT or improving its point tracker yields further gains, and whether temporal cues (which the method deliberately omits) could close the remaining gap.
