## Problem

Implicit-knowledge KVQA (IK-KVQA) requires MLLMs to answer knowledge-intensive visual questions using only parametric knowledge, yet existing approaches train with answer-only supervision, leaving intermediate reasoning implicit, justifications weak or inconsistent, and post-SFT generalization brittle. The paper addresses the absence of explicit, structured supervision on intermediate reasoning steps in a fully self-contained (no-retrieval) setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263053/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the model takes a single RGB image and a free-form text question as input and produces, in one autoregressive pass, a structured output sequence: a text relation path (semantic hops from the question), a vision relation path (attribute/relation hops grounded in the image), a path-grounded natural-language explanation, and the final answer. No external knowledge base, retriever, or auxiliary module is invoked; all reasoning is emitted as a single token sequence by the fine-tuned MLLM.

## Method

StaR-KVQA builds an offline trace-enriched dataset using a single open-source MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) in three roles: a dual-path planner generates K candidate pairs of symbolic relation paths (text path over linguistic priors, vision path over image-grounded attributes); a reasoning composer converts each path pair into a natural-language explanation with coverage-based filtering to enforce path grounding; and a best-triplet selector (LLM-as-a-judge) ranks triplets by answer-oriented consistency, coherence, and explicit path citation. The selected triplets are concatenated with ground-truth answers to form the training target, and the model is fine-tuned via standard token-level cross-entropy (LoRA, rank 32) on this augmented data, performing structure-aware self-distillation. The key design insight is that relation paths—being more stable and reusable than surface entities—serve as low-dimensional, modality-aware planning scaffolds that provide a stronger inductive bias than generic chain-of-thought or answer-only supervision, while the single-model pipeline avoids supervision-generation mismatch and keeps inference to one pass.

## Results

On OK-VQA, StaR-KVQA achieves 91.51% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), 90.01% (Llama-3.2-11B-Vision), and 91.90% (Gemma-3-12B) VQA accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline (SDFT at 82.56%) by up to +9.3% and closed-source Gemini 2.5 Pro (80.53%) by +11.4%. On FVQA, the corresponding accuracies are 82.82%, 80.19%, and 81.20%, outperforming the best prior method (LLaVA-CoT at 78.45%) by +4.4%. Cross-domain generalization shows StaR-KVQA retains +10–14% gains over frozen backbones on unseen datasets, whereas standard SFT degrades by 2.6–8.2% below the frozen baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StaR-KVQA outperforms all 24 baselines on both OK-VQA and FVQA. Against retrieval/KG-based methods (BAN 25.17%, KRISP 38.90%, MAVEx 41.37%, MCAN 44.65%), the gains are large (+46 to +67 points) but expected given the fundamentally different paradigm. Against LLM-based KVQA systems (PICA 48.00%, KAT 54.41%, REVIVE 53.83%, MAIL 56.69%), StaR-KVQA leads by 34–45 points. The most competitive comparisons are within the IK-KVQA+MLLM group: StaR-KVQA (Qwen) beats the strongest fine-tuned baseline SDFT (82.56%) by +8.95 points, and surpasses the strongest frozen open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-72B (80.75%) by +10.76 points. It also exceeds closed-source Gemini 2.5 Pro (80.53%) by +10.98 points and GPT-4o (77.86%) by +13.65 points. On FVQA, the margin over the best prior method LLaVA-CoT (78.45%) narrows to +4.37 points, and over SDFT (75.54%) to +7.28 points, indicating the advantage is somewhat dataset-dependent but consistent across all three backbone families.

## Relation to Prior Work

StaR-KVQA extends the self-distillation paradigm of SDFT [57] from answer-only rewriting to structured intermediate reasoning, applying it in the multimodal IK-KVQA setting where no external retrieval is permitted. It departs from the dominant KVQA pipeline (KG/retrieval-augmented systems like MAVEx, KRISP, VLC-BERT) by treating the MLLM's parametric knowledge as the sole knowledge source, and from generic chain-of-thought or VQA-NLE rationales by replacing free-form explanations with dual symbolic relation paths (text and vision) that serve as low-dimensional planning scaffolds. The LLM-as-a-judge selector and coverage-based filtering draw on recent work in explanation-answer agreement (DCLUB, MCLE) but instantiate them within a single-model, dependency-free pipeline rather than multi-stage architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a 7B open-source MLLM with LoRA fine-tuning and single-pass inference can surpass closed-source systems (Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4o) on knowledge-intensive VQA without any external retrieval, which matters for privacy-constrained and latency-sensitive deployments. The cross-domain generalization finding—that standard SFT degrades sharply on unseen datasets while structured-trace self-distillation maintains or improves performance—suggests that supervising intermediate reasoning is a more robust fine-tuning signal than answer-only targets. Open questions include whether the faithfulness gap between generated explanations and actual model computation can be formally bounded, whether the approach transfers to multi-hop or compositional reasoning beyond single-step KVQA, and whether the LLM-as-a-judge selector introduces systematic biases that compound at scale.
