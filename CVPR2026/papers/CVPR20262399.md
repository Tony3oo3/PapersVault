## Problem

Specular highlight removal from a single RGB image is fundamentally ill-posed because the specular component is saturated, spatially sparse, and entangled with geometry, material, and illumination. Existing learning-based methods rely on scarce paired diffuse-specular supervision or specialized hardware (polarization cameras), and they struggle to generalize across domains—particularly to endoscopic imagery where moist tissue and non-uniform lighting produce severe, structured reflections. No prior work generates physically grounded synthetic specular supervision from monocular geometry to enable RGB-only training across both natural and surgical scenes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image of arbitrary resolution and jointly produces a reflection-free diffuse RGB image and a soft, pixel-level highlight probability map in [0,1]. The highlight map is not binary but encodes continuous specular likelihood, and it is downsampled to the DINOv3 patch grid to form the binary patch mask that drives token inpainting. Because the encoder is a frozen DINOv3-Large ViT, the input is consumed as 16×16 patch tokens at four hierarchical depths, and the final diffuse image is decoded from the inpainted multi-scale token set via a DPT head.

## Method

UnReflectAnything couples a Virtual Highlight Synthesis pipeline with token-space inpainting. For training supervision, monocular geometry (depth, normals, intrinsics from MoGe-2) is used to backproject a 3D point cloud; a point light is sampled in camera space, and a Blinn-Phong lobe modulated by Schlick-Fresnel reflectance is rendered and alpha-composited onto the input, producing physically plausible synthetic highlights with randomized intensity, shininess, and light position. At inference, a frozen DINOv3-Large encoder extracts multi-scale patch features; a DPT-based head predicts the highlight map, which is downsampled to a patch mask. A lightweight ViT token inpainter replaces masked tokens with a blend of a learnable mask token and a local mean prior (from depthwise convolution over visible neighbors), adds 2D positional encodings, and refines them through six self-attention layers before merging back with unmasked tokens. A DPT decoder, pre-trained as an autoencoder on frozen DINOv3 features, reconstructs the diffuse RGB image. Training uses a hybrid loss combining soft Dice + L1 + TV for highlight prediction, cosine-similarity + L1 in token space (restricted to patches with reliable ground truth), and pixel-space L1 + SSIM + a seam-continuity term + a Charbonnier specular-suppression penalty on the decoded output.

## Results

On the three diffuse-referenced benchmarks (PSD, SHIQ, SSHR), UnReflectAnything achieves the lowest highlight-region MSE (0.004, 0.003, 0.002 respectively) and the highest SSIM on PSD (0.911) and SHIQ (0.988), indicating superior structural fidelity in highlight-affected regions. However, it reports the lowest PSNR (17.23 on PSD, 17.61 on SHIQ), suggesting more aggressive modification of non-highlight areas. On non-referenced datasets, the method attains the best Luminance Suppression Ratio across all six benchmarks (e.g., 0.012 on CroMo, 0.002 on SCRREAM, 0.002 on Cholec80), confirming effective highlight attenuation without global dimming.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnReflectAnything dominates on highlight-region error (MSE_m) and structural similarity (SSIM) across all three paired benchmarks, outperforming DHAN-SHR (SSIM 0.911 vs. 0.868 on PSD), Fu et al. (SSIM 0.988 vs. 0.978 on SHIQ), and StableDelight (SSIM 0.911 vs. 0.838 on PSD). The trade-off is a substantially lower PSNR (17.23 on PSD vs. 24.86 for DHAN-SHR), indicating the method modifies more of the image to achieve cleaner highlight suppression. Against the polarization-based PA+PF pipeline, the gap is decisive: UnReflectAnything achieves MSE_m of 0.004 vs. 0.050 and SSIM of 0.911 vs. 0.645 on PSD, validating the RGB-only approach. On non-referenced datasets (Table 2), the method achieves the best LSR on all six benchmarks (e.g., 0.002 on SCRREAM vs. 0.028 for EndoSTTN; 0.002 on Cholec80 vs. 0.021 for EndoSTTN), while NIQE scores remain competitive. In downstream pose estimation (Table 3), UnReflectAnything delivers the lowest epipolar error on StereoMIS-Tracking (0.167 vs. 0.213 for SpecularityNet) and the best inlier ratio on CroMo (0.997), though results are mixed on other datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of geometry-guided rendering and transformer-based feature inpainting for specular highlight removal. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes a frozen DINOv3 encoder and DPT decoders in a novel token-space inpainting pipeline, while its Virtual Highlight Synthesis module (monocular depth via MoGe-2, Schlick-Fresnel modulation, randomized Blinn-Phong lighting) provides a self-contained alternative to the paired-data or polarization-hardware requirements of prior methods like HighlightNet, SpecularityNet, and PolarAnything. The closest conceptual neighbors are diffusion-based approaches (StableDelight) and endoscopic inpainting (Endo-STTN), but UnReflectAnything departs from both by operating entirely in feature space with a feedforward architecture and by generating its own training supervision from arbitrary RGB images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physically grounded synthetic supervision can replace the need for paired diffuse-specular data or polarization sensors, enabling a single RGB-only model to generalize across natural and surgical domains—a practical advantage for clinical deployment where specialized hardware is unavailable. Key limitations remain: the method underperforms on PSNR in non-highlight regions, struggles with transparent/refractive surfaces, and its token inpainter depends heavily on a binary highlight mask derived from a hard luminance threshold, limiting robustness to gradual or low-contrast specularities. Open questions include whether soft masking or semantic cues could replace the luminance-based detection, and whether the frozen-encoder paradigm can be extended to video or multi-view settings without sacrificing the cross-domain generality that makes the approach attractive.
