## Problem

Pretrained text-to-video diffusion models produce silent videos, and training audio-video generative models from scratch is suboptimal because high-quality music-aligned dance datasets are far scarcer than general video data. The core challenge is adapting a pretrained video diffusion model to musical conditioning while preserving its rich prior knowledge of human motion physics and choreographic patterns, without the cost of full fine-tuning or the representational limitations of skeletal motion intermediates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a music track (encoded via Wav2Vec 2.0 and projected through a shallow MLP with temporal downsampling) and a free-form text prompt specifying dancer attributes, style, setting, and number of performers. It produces a dance video (up to 9 seconds, trained on ~2.5-second clips) where choreography is synchronized to the music's beat and tempo while visual content follows the prompt. The text prompt retains full control over aesthetics and scene context independently of the musical conditioning.

## Method

MusicInfuser injects Zero-Initialized Cross-Attention (ZICA) blocks and rank-64 LoRA adapters into selected DiT layers of the Mochi base model, with ZICA output projections initialized to zero so the adapter acts as an identity at training start. A key contribution is a layer adaptability criterion that uses skip-guidance (the difference between full-model and layer-skipped denoisers) as a constructive influence function to precompute which layers most benefit from cross-attention conditioning, avoiding exhaustive search over combinatorial layer subsets. Training employs a Beta-Uniform noise schedule that starts concentrated on low-noise (high-frequency) components and gradually flattens to uniform, preserving the pretrained denoising manifold while learning music-motion correlations. The training data mixes AIST studio clips with in-the-wild YouTube dance videos at 1:1 ratio, and prompt diversification (randomly replacing detailed captions with basic ones) reduces over-reliance on text while maintaining prompt adherence. The entire adaptation trains on a single A100 GPU in roughly 20 hours over 4,000 steps.

## Results

On the AIST test set, MusicInfuser achieves an average dance quality score of 7.95 (style alignment 7.56, beat alignment 8.89, movement realism 8.24, choreography complexity 7.90), outperforming MM-Diffusion (7.16) and Mochi (7.70). For video quality, MusicInfuser scores 8.95 average (imaging 9.60, consistency 9.39), surpassing MM-Diffusion (7.94) and Mochi (8.78). The method also achieves 8.96 average prompt alignment, exceeding Mochi's 8.86. All evaluations use VideoLLaMA 2 and Qwen3-Omni as automatic judges, validated against human evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MM-Diffusion (joint audio-video diffusion trained from scratch), MusicInfuser wins on every dance quality metric, with the largest gap in body representation (7.16 vs. 5.52) and movement realism (8.24 vs. 7.05), and also dominates on all video quality metrics (e.g., imaging 9.60 vs. 8.94, consistency 9.39 vs. 8.38). Against Mochi (the base text-to-video model without audio conditioning), MusicInfuser improves style alignment (7.56 vs. 7.20), beat alignment (8.89 vs. 8.34), movement realism (8.24 vs. 7.68), and choreography complexity (7.90 vs. 7.82), while slightly trailing on body representation (7.16 vs. 7.47) and aesthetic quality (7.87 vs. 7.90). The overall dance quality average rises from 7.70 (Mochi) to 7.95 (MusicInfuser), and video quality from 8.78 to 8.95, confirming that the audio adaptation adds meaningful synchronization without degrading the pretrained video prior. Ground-truth AIST videos still lead on beat alignment (8.95) and movement realism (8.67), validating the metric's reliability as an upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant music-to-dance generation paradigm, which relies on skeletal motion representations (AIST++, EDGE, DiffDance) or trains joint audio-video models from scratch (MM-Diffusion, Sound2Sight). Instead, it repurposes a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model (Mochi) by injecting zero-initialized cross-attention (ZICA) modules and LoRA adapters into a small subset of DiT blocks, guided by a novel layer-adaptability criterion derived from guidance-style influence functions. The approach also draws on efficient fine-tuning literature (LoRA, ControlNet-style conditioning) and extends the Beta-distribution noise scheduling idea to preserve denoising capability during adaptation on limited data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: music-synchronized dance video generation becomes feasible on a single GPU in roughly 20 hours, removing the need for motion capture, pose reconstruction, or large-scale audio-video co-training. The layer-adaptability criterion is a general, training-free diagnostic that could transfer to other modality-adaptation settings beyond dance. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-dance human activities or multi-person interaction, how it scales to higher-resolution or longer-duration generation beyond ~9 seconds, and whether the Video-LLM-based evaluation metrics (Qwen3-Omni, VideoLLaMA 2) remain reliable as generated video quality approaches ground-truth levels.
