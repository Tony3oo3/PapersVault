## Problem

Cross-modal image registration between optical and SAR or infrared sensors is severely challenged by nonlinear appearance discrepancies and complex geometric variations that cause both handcrafted descriptors and photometric-consistency-based optical flow models to fail. Existing learned methods either operate on homogeneous RGB data or rely on modality-specific priors, leaving no unified framework that simultaneously learns modality-independent feature representations and achieves sub-pixel dense alignment under large affine and spectral gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261772/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CRFT consumes aligned image pairs at 512×512 resolution (optical-SAR on OSdataset, optical-infrared on RoadScene), internally processing features at 1/8, 1/4, and 1/2 scales with 64×64 patch sampling during training. The output is a full-resolution dense flow field (pixel-wise displacement vectors) produced through iterative refinement, where each iteration warps target features via a Spatial Geometric Transform and predicts a residual update, ultimately yielding a sub-pixel-accurate correspondence map supervised by ground-truth flow derived from pixel-level alignments.

## Method

CRFT formulates registration as modality-independent feature flow estimation within a coarse-to-fine transformer pipeline. At the coarse stage, a shared ResNet encoder extracts multi-scale features, and transformer self-attention/cross-attention blocks at 1/8 resolution produce modality-invariant descriptors whose global correlation yields an initial flow field. The fine stage injects high-resolution detail through hierarchical window-based SA-CA fusion at 1/4 and 1/2 scales. The core innovation is an iterative Discrepancy-Guided Flow Optimization (DGFO) loop: at each step, the current flow drives a Spatial Geometric Transform to warp local feature windows, the resulting feature discrepancy is inverted to form attention weights that modulate a local attention mechanism predicting a residual flow update, and a confidence network smooths the result before the next iteration. Training uses a hierarchical loss with geometrically decaying weights (γ=0.9) across refinement iterations, assigning progressively larger supervision to later, more accurate predictions.

## Results

On OSdataset (optical-SAR), CRFT achieves an AEPE of 0.65 px and CMR of 99.0% / 95.1% / 89.9% at 3/1/0.7 px thresholds, outperforming the next-best method XoFTR+Flow (AEPE 1.13, CMR@0.7px 41.7%) by 42.5% in AEPE and 2.15× in sub-pixel CMR. On RoadScene (optical-infrared), CRFT attains the best AEPE of 2.37 px and leads in CMR at 3 px (68.2%) and 1 px (18.2%), though it trails RAFT (8.0%) and GDROS (8.8%) slightly at the strictest 0.7 px threshold (4.5%). The model contains 11.96M parameters, runs at 0.033 s per pair, and is trained in ~4 hours on OSdataset with a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRFT dominates all nine baselines on OSdataset across every metric, with the most dramatic gaps against handcrafted descriptors (HOWP, LNIFT, MSG, RIFT2), which collapse to 0% CMR below 1 px due to their inability to handle cross-modal radiometric shifts. Among learned dense methods, CRFT reduces AEPE by 51.5% over GDROS (0.65 vs 1.34) and 42.5% over XoFTR+Flow (0.65 vs 1.13), while its CMR@0.7px of 89.9% is 2.53× higher than GDROS (35.5%) and 2.15× higher than XoFTR+Flow (41.7%). On RoadScene, CRFT still achieves the best AEPE (2.37 vs 4.72 for ADRNet, 8.92 for RAFT) and leads at the 3 px and 1 px thresholds, but it is narrowly outperformed at the strictest 0.7 px threshold by RAFT (8.0%), GDROS (8.8%), and ADRNet (4.8%) versus CRFT's 4.5%, suggesting that the more challenging optical-infrared domain with nonlinear intensity variations and blur still limits sub-pixel precision. Overall, CRFT is the only method maintaining high CMR (>89%) at 0.7 px on OSdataset, confirming that its discrepancy-guided recurrent refinement is critical for sub-pixel cross-modal alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRFT sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: coarse-to-fine transformer matching (LoFTR, XoFTR), recurrent flow refinement (RAFT, GMFlow), and cross-modal registration (GDROS, ADRNet). It departs from optical-flow architectures by explicitly learning a modality-independent feature flow within a shared transformer backbone rather than assuming photometric consistency, and introduces a discrepancy-guided attention mechanism coupled with a Spatial Geometric Transform (SGT) that recurrently corrects local geometric misalignment—an ability conventional flow networks lack under modality gaps. The geometrically decaying iterative loss further distinguishes it from uniformly weighted or single-stage supervision schemes used in prior flow-based matchers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRFT's sub-pixel AEPE (0.65) on OSdataset and the ~2× CMR gap at 0.7 px over the next-best method demonstrate that explicit discrepancy-guided geometric reasoning can close the last sub-pixel gap that prior cross-modal methods leave open. However, evaluation is confined to two relatively small benchmarks (2,673 and 221 pairs), and the RoadScene results (2.37 AEPE, 68.2% CMR@3 px) are markedly weaker, suggesting the advantage is dataset- and modality-pair-dependent. Open questions include scalability to larger and more diverse cross-modal collections, generalization to additional modality pairs (e.g., multispectral, thermal), and whether the SGT-based recurrent refinement can be made real-time for deployment in autonomous navigation or remote sensing pipelines.
