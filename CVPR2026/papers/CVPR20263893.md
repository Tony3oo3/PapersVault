## Problem

Existing person ReID methods are organized around predefined scenario pairs (e.g., visible-infrared or aerial-ground) with task-specific models and evaluation protocols, making them inefficient and complex for real-world deployments where a query can come from any modality and viewpoint against a heterogeneous gallery. No prior benchmark provides tri-spectral, cross-view, day-night coverage for every identity to support a unified any-to-any retrieval task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263893/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single person image from any (modality, viewpoint) scenario—RGB, NIR, or TIR captured by ground or UAV cameras—resized to 128×256 (144×288 for VI-ReID comparisons). A P×M×K sampler (16 identities × 3 modalities × 4 images) ensures each mini-batch contains rich cross-modality and cross-view variability per identity. The output is a d-dimensional ℓ₂-normalized feature embedding from a shared ViT-Base backbone, used to rank a heterogeneous gallery spanning all scenarios by cosine similarity.

## Method

UAD trains a single ViT-Base backbone with two complementary objectives beyond standard cross-entropy and weighted triplet loss. Progressive Center Alignment (ProCA) first aggregates K views per modality into a modality center, then aligns all modality centers toward a stop-gradient identity center, reducing scenario-specific scatter in a pairing-free, linearly-scaling manner. Global Prototype Discrimination (GPD) maintains a momentum-updated prototype memory (α=0.8) seeded by ProCA's identity centers and contrasts each sample against all identity prototypes with a temperature-scaled softmax (τ=0.03), injecting dataset-level large-margin discrimination that local triplet losses cannot provide. The final loss is L_base + 0.01·L_ProCA + 1.0·L_GPD, applied to pre-BN embeddings, requiring no extra branches or pairwise scenario objectives.

## Results

Under the AS-ReID protocol on WHU-MARS-1000, UAD achieves 11.0 mAP and 29.5% Rank-1, outperforming the strongest baselines TransReID-SSL (10.1 mAP, 26.7% R-1) and CLIP-ReID (10.6 mAP, 26.6% R-1). On the larger WHU-MARS-2337 split, UAD reaches 9.6 mAP and 25.7% R-1, again leading all compared methods. In the restricted ground-daytime subset (WHU-MARS-1000-GD), UAD attains 14.5 mAP and 31.9% R-1, confirming that nighttime and aerial viewpoints are the primary sources of difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary AS-ReID task (Table 3), UAD outperforms all 11 compared methods across all three evaluation splits. On WHU-MARS-1000, UAD (11.0 mAP, 29.5% R-1) leads TransReID-SSL (10.1 mAP, 26.7% R-1) by 0.9 mAP and 2.8 R-1 points, and CLIP-ReID (10.6 mAP, 26.6% R-1) by 0.4 mAP and 2.9 R-1 points. On the harder WHU-MARS-2337 split, UAD (9.6 mAP, 25.7% R-1) beats TransReID-SSL (9.2 mAP, 24.4% R-1) and CLIP-ReID (9.3 mAP, 24.1% R-1). In the AG-ReID sub-task (Table 4), UAD achieves the best mAP in both A→G (11.5 vs. 10.1 SeCap) and G→A (13.3 vs. 12.0 SeCap) directions. In VI-ReID (Table 5), UAD wins on the harder TIR→RGB protocol (9.04% R-1 vs. 8.29% for PMT) but trails PMT on NIR→RGB (25.53% vs. 27.12% R-1; 16.58 vs. 18.16 mAP), likely because PMT uses modality-specific branches and VI-specific augmentations that UAD deliberately avoids in favor of a single unified model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in person ReID where each task (VI-ReID, AG-ReID, MM-ReID) is defined around a fixed scenario pair and solved by a dedicated model with modality-specific branches or pairwise alignment losses. Rather than extending any single prior method, it unifies these settings under a new any-to-any retrieval formulation (AS-ReID) and proposes a single-branch ViT trained with two complementary objectives: Progressive Center Alignment (a two-level aggregation that pulls modality centers toward identity anchors) and Global Prototype Discrimination (momentum-updated identity prototypes contrasted against all samples). The dataset construction draws on the multi-spectral and aerial-ground collection traditions but is the first to guarantee tri-spectral, cross-view coverage for every identity, filling a gap left by prior benchmarks that either lack modality variety or viewpoint diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WHU-MARS is likely to become a standard benchmark for the ReID community because it is the largest multispectral person ReID dataset to date and the first to support a deployment-aligned any-scenario evaluation protocol, addressing a real gap between academic pairwise benchmarks and operational systems that query against heterogeneous galleries. The finding that a single unified model without modality-specific design can match or exceed specialized VI-ReID methods on the hardest TIR→RGB protocol suggests that scenario-invariant representation learning may reduce the need for task-specific architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than two viewpoints or more than three modalities, how it performs under truly open-world scenario combinations not seen during training, and whether the prototype-based discrimination generalizes to other ReID domains (vehicle, object).
