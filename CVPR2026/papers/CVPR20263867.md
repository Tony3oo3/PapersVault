## Problem

In instance-dependent partial label learning (ID-PLL), instances from different classes often share overlapping features and candidate labels (instance entanglement), causing severe class confusion. Existing contrastive-learning-based methods that align similar instances can exacerbate this confusion by misaligning entangled pairs, and prior work lacks explicit treatment of similar-class confusion from both the representation and confidence perspectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263867/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (28×28 for Fashion-MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, variable for Flower and Oxford-IIIT Pet) paired with instance-dependent candidate label sets, and produces a single predicted class label. Unusually, the candidate label sets are generated based on instance features rather than random noise, and the diffusion-based augmentation variant additionally consumes text prompts (class names or fine-grained visual descriptions) to guide InstructPix2Pix editing offline before training.

## Method

CAD (Class-specific Augmentation based Disentanglement) tackles entanglement through two complementary modules. For intra-class regulation, it generates class-specific augmentations per candidate label—either via CAM-based feature reweighting (CAD-CAM) or InstructPix2Pix diffusion editing guided by class-name prompts (CAD)—then aligns same-label augmentations across instances using a MoCo-style contrastive loss with momentum-updated query/key encoders and confidence-weighted positive pairs. For inter-class regulation, it applies a weighted penalty loss that normalizes confidence separately within the candidate set S and its complement S̄, imposing proportionally stronger penalties on high-confidence non-candidate classes to push confusable classes apart. The total loss combines the disambiguation classification loss with the augmentation-based contrastive loss, and the framework is trained end-to-end with self-distillation following DIRK.

## Results

On the five standard ID-PLL benchmarks, CAD achieves 92.14% on Fashion-MNIST, 93.57% on CIFAR-10, and 72.03% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the strongest baseline DIRK (91.48%, 90.87%, 68.77%) by 0.66, 2.70, and 3.26 percentage points respectively. Its lightweight variant CAD-CAM (using CAM-based feature reweighting instead of diffusion editing) achieves 91.64%, 92.69%, and 69.08% on the same three datasets, and further leads on the fine-grained Flower (49.67%) and Oxford-IIIT Pet (74.56%) benchmarks. On entangled-instance subsets (top 0.001% most similar pairs), CAD's advantage over DIRK widens to 9.28% on CIFAR-10, confirming that the gains are concentrated on the hardest, most entangled samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD outperforms the strongest prior method DIRK on all three coarse-grained benchmarks: +0.66% on Fashion-MNIST (92.14 vs 91.48), +2.70% on CIFAR-10 (93.57 vs 90.87), and +3.26% on CIFAR-100 (72.03 vs 68.77). On the fine-grained Flower and Oxford-IIIT Pet datasets, CAD-CAM (the CAM-based variant) surpasses DIRK by 5.64% and 9.61% respectively, while the full diffusion-based CAD trails CAD-CAM on these two datasets (47.88 vs 49.67 on Flower; 69.46 vs 74.56 on Pet), which the authors attribute to generic diffusion prompts lacking fine-grained semantic priors. Among contrastive-learning baselines, ABLE is notably weaker on CIFAR-10 (83.92%) and CIFAR-100 (63.92%) compared to CAD, and the paper shows ABLE actually shrinks inter-class distances on entangled pairs (smallest distances in 11 of 15 cases). CEL achieves competitive results on Oxford-IIIT Pet (68.19%) but lags substantially on Flower (38.51%) and CIFAR-100 (68.73%). The gap between CAD and DIRK widens dramatically on the hardest entangled subsets (top 0.001% pairs): CAD reaches 83.37% vs DIRK's 74.09% on CIFAR-10, a 9.28-point margin versus only 2.70 points on the full test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the instance-dependent partial label learning (ID-PLL) line initiated by VALEN and extended by ABLE and DIRK, which use contrastive learning and confidence-based disambiguation. It departs from these methods by explicitly identifying 'instance entanglement'—where different-class instances share overlapping features and candidate labels—as a distinct failure mode that contrastive alignment actively worsens. The CAD framework repurposes MoCo's momentum-contrast architecture for label-conditioned augmentation alignment and extends DIRK's weighted loss with a confidence-normalized penalty for confusable non-candidate classes, while introducing InstructPix2Pix-based diffusion editing (and a CAM-based lightweight alternative) as a novel augmentation mechanism for PLL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, measurable diagnosis (entanglement statistics in Table 1, accuracy drops in Figure 3) for why existing ID-PLL methods struggle on the hardest samples, and shows that a dual intra-/inter-class regulation strategy addresses both the representation and confidence dimensions of the problem. A key open question is the reliance on textual class-name prompts for diffusion-based augmentation, which fails on fine-grained or domain-specific tasks (medical, industrial) where visual semantics resist verbalization; the authors note that domain-specific fine-tuning of generative editors under PLL constraints remains unexplored. The finding that the simple CAD-CAM variant already outperforms all baselines suggests the core disentanglement principle is more important than the generative editing, pointing toward lighter-weight future instantiations.
