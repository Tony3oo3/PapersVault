## Problem

Prior multimodal encoders like ImageBind and LanguageBind bind all modalities through a single anchor, creating severe imbalances: text-anchored models fail on audio-video retrieval, and image-anchored models collapse on audio-text tasks. The audio-video domain remains underrepresented relative to vision-language, and no existing model natively supports joint embeddings across all audio-video-text pairs while covering speech, music, and general sound effects simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262801/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw audio waveforms (encoded via a pre-trained DAC-VAE), raw RGB video frames at 336 resolution sampled at 16 or 30 fps (encoded via PE-L spatial encoder plus a temporal Transformer), and text queries/captions (encoded via ModernBERT with 512-token context). It produces a set of [CLS] embeddings projected into a shared space: separate audio, video, and text embeddings, a fused audio-video embedding, and two joint embeddings (audio+text and video+text) that enable text-conditioned cross-modal retrieval such as V+T→A or A+T→V.

## Method

PE_AV employs separate audio (Transformer with RoPE, up to 28 layers/1.1B params), video (PE-L spatial + 4-layer temporal Transformer), audio-video fusion (6-layer Transformer), and text (ModernBERT) towers, all trained end-to-end with a sigmoid contrastive objective across up to ten modality-caption pairs. A two-stage audiovisual data engine generates O(100M) synthetic captions: Stage 1 uses Llama 3.1 8B to fuse outputs from weak audio captioners (EnCLAP, CoNeTTE) with Joint-CLAP confidence scores and video captions; Stage 2 refines both audio and video captions via a PLM-based multimodal LLM. Training proceeds in two stages—pre-training on 92M samples with 8 contrastive pairs, then fine-tuning on 32M balanced samples adding two joint-embedding pairs—and a BEST-RQ self-supervised loss on the audio encoder preserves fine-grained phonetic detail. The key insight is that scaling the coverage of cross-modal contrastive pairs (rather than relying on a single anchor) consistently strengthens alignment and enables native joint embeddings that outperform max-over-unimodal approximations.

## Results

PE_AV L (1.1B audio encoder, 2.2B total) achieves 45.8 R@1 on AudioCaps text-to-audio retrieval, 48.3 R@1 on VGGSound video-to-audio, and 85.6 R@1 on VCTK speech-to-transcript retrieval (all baselines score near 0 on VCTK). On zero-shot audio classification, it reaches 47.1 on VGGSound, 96.0 on ESC50, 76.8 on GTzan-10, and 83.6 on NSynth-1K. On video benchmarks, PE_AV L attains 66.5 R@1 on ActivityNet text-to-video retrieval and 78.9 top-1 accuracy on Kinetics-700, surpassing PE-G (4× more parameters) by +6.5 R@1 and +1.6 accuracy respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PE_AV L outperforms all audio-focused baselines (CLAP, CLAP-Fusion, MS-CLAP, M2D-CLAP, AudioFlamingo2) by 10–22 R@1 on AudioCaps T→A (45.8 vs. 23.4–35.4) and by 13–24 points on VGGSound A→T classification (47.1 vs. 23.5–36.0). Against audio-visual models, the gap is even wider: ImageBind scores only 6.6 on AudioCaps T→A and 10.8 on VGGSound V→A, while LanguageBind drops to 1.6 on VGGSound V→A, exposing the fragility of single-anchor binding when the anchor modality is absent. On video benchmarks, PE_AV L surpasses PE-L by +20.1 R@1 on ActivityNet T→V (66.5 vs. 46.4) and +6.2 on Kinetics-700 (78.9 vs. 72.7), and beats the much larger PE-G (1.9B video encoder) by +11.8 R@1 on ActivityNet T→V and +2.0 on Kinetics-700. PE_AV L also exceeds InternVideo2 (1.0B, 102M data) by +6.1 R@1 on ActivityNet T→V and +6.1 on Kinetics-700, and outperforms SigLIP2-g-opt by +28.2 R@1 on ActivityNet T→V. Notably, PE_AV L is the only model achieving non-trivial speech-to-transcript retrieval (85.6 R@1 on VCTK), where all baselines score below 1 R@1.

## Relation to Prior Work

PE_AV extends the Perception Encoder (PE) line of work from video-text alignment to a full audio-video-text encoder, building on PE's frame encoder, DAC-VAE for audio tokenization, and ModernBERT for text. It departs fundamentally from single-anchor multimodal models (ImageBind anchors to image, LanguageBind to text) by training a multi-pair contrastive objective across all ten modality-caption combinations, directly addressing the asymmetry problem where performance collapses when the anchor modality is absent. The audiovisual data engine—using LLMs to fuse weak audio captioners (EnCLAP, CoNeTTE) with video captions and confidence scores—represents a new data-scaling recipe that has no direct precedent in prior contrastive multimodal work, which typically relied on curated real captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that the single-anchor paradigm is a fundamental bottleneck in multimodal representation learning, and that scaling cross-modal pair coverage with high-quality synthetic data can close the gap across all modality combinations simultaneously. The practical impact is broad: PE_AV enables previously impossible tasks (speech-to-transcript retrieval at 85.6 R@1 where all baselines scored ~0) and provides a reusable data-engine recipe for generating balanced multimodal supervision at O(100M) scale. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond three modalities, whether the 10-pair objective is truly optimal or whether more structured objectives (e.g., hierarchical or graph-based) would help, and whether the synthetic-caption quality ceiling can be pushed further to match or exceed human annotation at scale.
