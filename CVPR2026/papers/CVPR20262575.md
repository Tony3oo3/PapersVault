## Problem

Bimanual manipulation requires precise spatiotemporal coordination across a high-dimensional joint action space, yet collecting bimanual demonstrations is prohibitively expensive. Pre-trained unimanual policies encode rich manipulation knowledge but existing methods either demand extensive bimanual datasets or fail to effectively compose single-arm policies into coordinated dual-arm behavior. The paper asks whether unimanual policies can be compositionally transferred to bimanual tasks with minimal bimanual data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262575/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes shared RGB-D observations from six cameras at 256×256 resolution, arm-specific proprioceptive states, and a language instruction, structured as per-arm conditioning c^i = (o_t, p_t^i, l). It outputs a sequence of bimanual actions, each specifying 6D end-effector poses and gripper states for both left and right arms, generated by integrating a compositional velocity field via flow matching ODE over N steps. The action space is the concatenation of two unimanual action vectors, and coordination constraints are enforced during generation rather than as post-hoc corrections.

## Method

EnergyAction interprets each pre-trained unimanual flow matching policy as an implicit energy function by identifying the learned velocity field with the negative gradient of that energy (v_θ = −∇E). Bimanual actions are then generated by summing the left and right arm energies, yielding a compositional velocity field derived via a classifier-free-guidance-style formulation that combines conditional and unconditional velocities. To ensure physical feasibility, six energy-based coordination terms (velocity, acceleration, jerk smoothness; inter-arm synchronization; end-effector and joint-space collision avoidance via inverse kinematics) are added to the total energy, with adaptive weights predicted by a lightweight MLP from the current state. Finally, two energy-aware denoising strategies—adaptive step-count selection based on energy thresholds and early stopping when energy drops below a threshold—reduce inference cost from a fixed 5 steps to a mean of ~1.3–2.3 steps while preserving action quality.

## Results

On RLBench2 (13 bimanual tasks), EnergyAction achieves an average success rate of 77.3% with 20 demonstrations and 86.4% with 100 demonstrations, outperforming the strongest baseline 3DFA (44.8% / 81.8%) by 32.5 and 4.6 percentage points respectively. Under the data-limited 20-demo setting, EnergyAction leads the second-best method by a substantial margin, demonstrating superior data efficiency. In real-world experiments on the Galaxea R1 lite robot, EnergyAction reaches 52.5% average success (20-demo) versus 35.0% for 3DFA and 22.5% for AnyBimanual.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnergyAction outperforms all nine baselines across both 20-demo and 100-demo settings on RLBench2. The most directly comparable method, AnyBimanual [29] (which also transfers unimanual policies to bimanual tasks), achieves only 19.0%/32.0% average success, trailing EnergyAction by 58.3/54.4 points; the paper attributes this gap to AnyBimanual's lack of explicit coordination constraints, which causes arm collisions in real-world trials. The strongest overall baseline, 3DFA [12] (a unified 3D flow-matching policy trained directly on bimanual data), reaches 44.8%/81.8%, still 32.5/4.6 points below EnergyAction. Among diffusion-based baselines, KStarDiffuser [30] (28.2%/37.6%) and DP3 [56] (9.7%/22.9%) are substantially outperformed, as are the transformer-based ACT [64] (10.0%), PerAct2 [14] (18.2%/27.5%), and PerAct-LF [14,37] (14.6%/24.5%). The VLA-based π0-keypose [1] (27.7%/50.6%) and RVT-LF [13,14] (6.2%/13.6%) also fall well short. In real-world evaluation (Table 4), EnergyAction (40.0%/52.5% avg) beats 3DFA (22.5%/35.0%), π0-keypose (17.5%/27.5%), and AnyBimanual (12.5%/22.5%) on both Handover and Pick-up-Plate tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the compositional generation principle of Energy-Based Models (EBMs) — previously applied to image and motion generation (Du et al. 2020, 2023) — for the first time in robotic manipulation, framing pre-trained unimanual flow-matching policies as energy functions whose sum yields a bimanual policy. It departs from direct joint-space modeling approaches (3DFA, DP3, KStarDiffuser) and from skill-scheduling transfer (AnyBimanual) by introducing an explicit energy-based decomposition that connects flow-matching velocity fields to energy gradients via a deterministic Langevin limit, and recasts the composition as a classifier-free-guidance-style velocity combination. The temporal-spatial coordination constraints and adaptive denoising strategies are novel additions not present in prior EBM or diffusion-policy work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional EBM framing offers a principled, data-efficient path to bimanual manipulation that avoids the combinatorial explosion of the joint action space, achieving a 32.5% advantage over the strongest baseline at 20 demonstrations. The framework's flexibility across diffusion backends (DDPM, DDIM, Flow Matching) and the energy-aware denoising (reducing mean steps from 5 to ~1.3–2.3) have practical value for real-time deployment. However, the theoretical bridge between flow matching and EBMs relies on a deterministic-limit approximation that may not hold under stochastic sampling, and real-world success rates remain modest (~52.5%), leaving open whether the approach scales to more than two arms, highly asymmetric tasks, or zero-shot bimanual transfer without any bimanual demonstrations.
