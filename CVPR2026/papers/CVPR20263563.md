key_contribution: RetFormer introduces a retrieval cross-fusion module that models cross-modal relationships between a query image and its k-NN neighbors (both image and text) from a large-scale external knowledge base (~1.4B pairs), achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on long-tailed recognition (ImageNet-LT 81.9%, iNaturalist 81.1%) and noisy-label learning (WebVision 81.7%) without increasing model parameters.
limitations: Performance on scene-centric benchmarks (Places-LT) is weaker than on object-centric ones due to a mismatch between object-focused text descriptions in the knowledge base and scene-oriented images, and the retrieval may overcorrect imbalances at the expense of well-covered head classes. The approach also requires ~30 GB of additional storage for precomputed embeddings and depends on access to a large, high-quality external image-text knowledge base.

surprise_factor: The ablation showing that text-only retrieval (78.1 overall) outperforms image-only retrieval (76.0) on ImageNet-LT is mildly counterintuitive, suggesting that high-level semantic descriptions from the knowledge base carry more discriminative signal than low-level visual similarity for tail classes. ## Problem

Real-world vision data exhibits long-tailed class distributions and noisy labels, making standard fine-tuning of large Transformers prone to catastrophic forgetting and poor tail-class performance. Existing retrieval-augmented vision methods (e.g., RAC, MAM) retrieve only from image modalities, leaving the complementary high-level semantic knowledge in paired text descriptions unexploited. The paper asks whether modeling the interplay between retrieved image and text modalities can improve robust recognition without simply scaling model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263563/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB query image (encoded by a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP encoder) and performs an approximate k-NN search (k=32, HNSW via Faiss) against a pre-vectorized external knowledge base of up to 1.4 billion image-text pairs (from DataComp-1B and downstream datasets). The output is a standard class logit vector produced by averaging the backbone MLP branch and the retrieval branch, weighted by a balancing coefficient τ. The knowledge base embeddings are computed once with frozen CLIP encoders and stored in memory (~30 GB for ImageNet-LT), so no re-encoding is needed during training or validation.

## Method

RetFormer splits the image embedding into two parallel branches: a plain two-layer MLP and a retrieval cross-fusion module. The retrieval branch first retrieves k nearest image-text pairs from the knowledge base, then applies a multi-layer cross-attention mechanism where text embeddings serve as queries attending to image keys/values (and vice versa), with residual connections back to the original embeddings at each of L=4 layers. This cross-modal attention explicitly models how retrieved text complements retrieved images for the query, rather than treating them as independent evidence. The two branch outputs are averaged and trained end-to-end with standard cross-entropy (plus LACE for imbalanced data), Mixup, and label smoothing. A gradient-propagation analysis shows the retrieval module injects additional gradient terms from retrieved neighbors, effectively acting as a data-dependent augmentation that supplies virtual samples for under-represented tail classes.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT, RetFormer achieves 81.9% overall top-1 accuracy (85.0% Many, 80.9% Med, 76.8% Few), outperforming the strongest non-retrieval baseline LIFT/PEL (78.3%) by 3.6 points and the retrieval-based RAC (76.7%) by 5.2 points. On iNaturalist 2018, it reaches 81.1% overall accuracy, surpassing RAC (80.2%) and LIFT (79.1%). On WebVision (noisy labels), RetFormer attains 81.7% top-1 on the WebVision validation set and 77.3% on ILSVRC12, exceeding the best prior method Sel-CL+ (79.96% / 76.84%) by 1.7 and 0.5 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RetFormer wins on overall accuracy across all four long-tailed benchmarks. On ImageNet-LT, it beats the strongest fine-tuning baselines LIFT and PEL (78.3%) by 3.6 points and the retrieval-based RAC (76.7%) by 5.2 points, with the largest gap in Few-shot (76.8% vs. 73.4% for LIFT). On iNaturalist 2018, it surpasses RAC (80.2%) by 0.9 points overall and LIFT (79.1%) by 2.0 points, though LiVT (76.1%) is far behind in overall despite competitive Many-shot. On Places-LT, the margin is smaller (52.3% vs. 51.5% for LIFT/PEL), and the authors note that scene-oriented classification is less aligned with object-centric text in the knowledge base. On WebVision (noisy labels), RetFormer outperforms Sel-CL+ (79.96%) by 1.7 points on WebVision and Dynamic Loss (80.12%) by 1.6 points, while RCAL+ (79.56%) is slightly lower on WebVision but higher on ILSVRC12 (76.32% vs. 77.3%). The modality ablation (Table 5) confirms that retrieving both image and text (81.9%) substantially outperforms image-only retrieval (76.0%) and text-only retrieval (78.1%), validating the cross-fusion design.

## Relation to Prior Work

RetFormer extends the retrieval-augmented classification line initiated by RAC [24] and MAM [14], which retrieve from external memory to aid long-tailed recognition, by incorporating both image and text modalities from a large-scale external knowledge base (DataComp, ~1.4B pairs) rather than relying solely on the training set or a single modality. The cross-fusion attention mechanism draws on standard Transformer cross-attention (as in CrossViT [5]) but applies it to model inter-sample, cross-modal relationships between a query and its k-NN neighbors, a use case distinct from intra-batch sample relationship modeling (BatchFormer [13]). The frozen CLIP encoders and HNSW-based approximate nearest-neighbor search are repurposed from their original roles in contrastive pre-training and similarity search to serve as a semi-parametric knowledge-injection mechanism during downstream fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that external multimodal knowledge (image + text) can substantially close the head-tail gap in long-tailed recognition and improve robustness under noisy labels, offering a practical alternative to purely parametric solutions that require ever-larger models. The gradient-propagation analysis (Section 3.2.5) provides a useful theoretical lens: retrieval acts as a data-dependent augmentation that injects virtual samples for under-represented classes. Limitations include weaker-than-expected gains on scene-centric benchmarks (Places-LT) due to object-vs-scene text mismatch, the need for ~30 GB of additional storage for precomputed embeddings, and the dependence on a large, high-quality external knowledge base. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other data-scarce settings (e.g., few-shot, domain shift) and whether smaller, task-specific knowledge bases can match the performance of billion-scale ones.
