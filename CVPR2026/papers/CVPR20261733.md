## Problem

Existing inertial motion capture methods assume an isolated human body, ignoring object contacts and dynamics entirely, while vision-based HOI capture requires dense camera rigs, pre-scanned meshes, or controlled studio environments. This leaves a significant gap for robust, in-the-wild human-object interaction tracking using only sparse wearable sensors without any visual input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261733/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 body IMUs (9 channels each: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer) and a single object IMU, plus an initial state comprising body pose, root position, object orientation, and object position. It outputs 24-joint SMPL body positions, a root translation trajectory, and a 6-DoF object trajectory whose position is a contact-gated convex combination of three hypotheses (left-hand FK, right-hand FK, and IMU integration). Notably, no object mesh or geometry is available; the hand-object contact configuration is encoded as a learned offset in the object frame rather than a geometrically refined contact point.

## Method

IMU-HOI is a three-stage, contact-conscious inertial fusion pipeline. Stage I uses a compact LSTM to predict a calibrated probabilistic contact prior (categorical over left-hand, right-hand, no-contact) and a short-horizon object velocity from local IMU cues, trained with focal cross-entropy to handle imbalanced contact events. Stage II estimates full-body pose via a DynaIP-style part-based SubPoser backbone (lower limbs, trunk, upper limbs) and a two-branch root-translation head (foot-contact-driven and trunk-velocity-driven) inspired by TransPose, yielding stable wrist trajectories. Stage III recovers object translation by fusing two kinematic FK branches (each predicting an object-frame direction and reach length under a quasi-rigid contact assumption) with an IMU-integrated trajectory, combined via a contact-gated softmax that injects the Stage-I contact prior as a multiplicative bias in a Bayesian-style routing. Training follows a staged curriculum (Stage I → freeze → Stage II → freeze → Stage III → joint fine-tune of II+III), and the contact-gated fusion module is modular and plug-and-play with off-the-shelf sparse-IMU pose estimators.

## Results

On the three HOI benchmarks (OMOMO, IMHD², BEHAVE), IMU-HOI achieves the lowest Obj Err and HOI Err across all datasets: 14.15/14.94 cm on OMOMO, 49.76/51.09 cm on IMHD², and 22.26/22.62 cm on BEHAVE. It also attains the best Ang Err (2.84°/4.51°/4.52°) and Pos Err (2.27/3.85/2.72 cm) on all three datasets. On the with-root-translation protocol (Table 2), IMU-HOI reduces Trans Err by 68.5% on IMHD² and 30.6% on BEHAVE relative to the strongest baseline, while matching GlobalPose* within 2.5% on OMOMO. The method runs an order of magnitude faster than GlobalPose*, which relies on per-frame physical optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMU-HOI decisively outperforms all four baselines on the two interaction-centric metrics (Obj Err and HOI Err) across all three datasets. Against the strongest baseline GlobalPose*, it reduces Obj Err by 64.0% (OMOMO), 50.9% (IMHD²), and 44.5% (BEHAVE), with comparable HOI Err reductions of 62.2%, 49.9%, and 43.7%. DIP* and TIP* are consistently the weakest, with TIP* particularly struggling on IMHD² (Pos Err 49.23 cm, Jitter 66.88 mm/s³), likely due to its terrain-generation focus. TransPose* is competitive on Jitter (2.47 on OMOMO) and Pos Err (3.15 on OMOMO) but is outperformed on all other metrics. On the with-root-translation protocol, IMU-HOI matches GlobalPose* on OMOMO Trans Err (11.07 vs 10.80 cm) but substantially outperforms it on IMHD² (18.37 vs 58.36 cm) and BEHAVE (11.14 vs 16.05 cm), suggesting better generalization under limited data. The plug-and-play ablation (Table 4) further shows that attaching the contact-gated fusion to any backbone (DIP, TransPose, GlobalPose) yields 45–54% Obj Err reductions, confirming the fusion module's generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-IMU human pose estimation lineage (DIP, TransPose, TIP, DynaIP, GlobalPose) by adding an object-tracking dimension that all prior inertial mocap methods explicitly ignore. It borrows the part-based pose backbone from DynaIP and the two-branch root-translation design from TransPose, then introduces a novel contact-gated FK-IMU fusion module for object trajectory estimation that has no direct predecessor in the purely inertial setting. The closest prior HOI work (ECHO, Interaction Replica, I'M HOI) either requires visual input, detailed object meshes, or extensive sensor suites, making this the first framework to recover coherent human-object motion from only six body IMUs and one object IMU without any cameras.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play demonstration—where the same contact-gated fusion module upgrades DIP, TransPose, and GlobalPose backbones with 44–64% object-error reductions—suggests a broadly applicable component for the inertial mocap community rather than a monolithic system. However, the quasi-rigid single-contact assumption limits applicability to sliding, multi-point, or deformable-object interactions, and the evaluation is confined to three datasets with relatively simple grasp configurations. Open questions include whether the probabilistic contact signal generalizes to bimanual or tool-mediated interactions, how the framework scales to multiple simultaneous objects, and whether the staged curriculum can be replaced by end-to-end training without sacrificing the interpretability of the contact gate.
