## Problem

Traditional uncertainty- and diversity-based active learning for roadside monocular 3D detection systematically selects inherently ambiguous samples—scenes with distant, blurred, or heavily occluded objects whose 3D properties are fundamentally unlearnable from a single view—wasting annotation budget on labels that provide weaker supervision. No prior work identifies or explicitly suppresses these ambiguous samples in the context of roadside BEV perception, where depth ambiguity, long-tail semantics, and geometric variability create unique challenges not addressed by conventional AL heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular roadside images with fixed, known camera intrinsics K and extrinsics E, from which the LSS-style depth projector yields a per-location probability distribution over D discretized depth bins and a context feature map. The active selection module outputs a per-round subset of image indices (|S_q| = k_q) under a global object budget K_total, while the downstream BEV detector outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized as (x, y, z, d^x, d^y, d^z, ψ) with semantic class labels for each detected object.

## Method

LH3D formulates active selection as a three-stage hierarchical concave-over-modular submodular maximization, where each stage greedily adds images with the largest marginal gain under a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee. Stage 1 computes per-image depth reliability from the normalized Shannon entropy of the depth projector's per-location distributions, then maximizes a log-coverage function over D depth-bin histograms weighted by reliability, suppressing depth-ambiguous scenes. Stage 2 uses predicted per-class object counts to build smoothed class distributions, weights images by their semantic entropy, and maximizes a log-coverage over class categories to prevent vehicle dominance. Stage 3 fits per-class Gaussians to labeled BEV centers and heights, scores unlabeled images by negative log-likelihood of their predicted boxes under these Gaussians, and maximizes a log-coverage over per-class geometric novelty to encourage diverse yet learnable layouts. The three stages are applied sequentially as filters (S_q^A ⊇ S_q^B ⊇ S_q), so each stage operates on a progressively refined candidate pool rather than jointly optimizing all three objectives.

## Results

On DAIR-V2X-I (BEVHeight backbone, 20% queried boxes), LH3D achieves average AP_3D|R40 of 38.77/38.33/38.50 (Easy/Mod/Hard), outperforming the strongest baseline PPAL (37.90/36.33/36.41) by 0.87%, 2.00%, and 2.19% respectively. On Rope3D (BEVHeight backbone), LH3D reaches 14.97/15.77/14.95 average AP, surpassing PPAL by 0.24%, 2.05%, and 1.40% and HUA by 2.50%, 3.34%, and 2.51%. The gains are most pronounced on the Pedestrian and Cyclist categories, where LH3D improves over HUA by up to 5.52% (Moderate) on DAIR-V2X-I, confirming that learnability-driven selection benefits rare classes most.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LH3D outperforms all seven baselines on average AP across both datasets and all three difficulty levels. Against the strongest baseline PPAL (CVPR 2024), LH3D gains 0.87–2.19% on DAIR-V2X-I and 0.24–2.05% on Rope3D, with the largest margins on Moderate and Hard settings where depth ambiguity is most severe. Compared to HUA (ICLR 2023), the improvements are substantially larger: 0.87–5.52% on DAIR-V2X-I and 2.50–3.34% on Rope3D, driven primarily by Pedestrian and Cyclist categories where uncertainty-based selection over-samples inherently ambiguous scenes. ENTROPY and BADGE, which rely on predictive uncertainty or gradient diversity, achieve mid-range performance (33.6–37.5% avg AP on DAIR-V2X-I) but consistently underperform LH3D by 1–5 points, confirming that high-uncertainty samples in roadside BEV are often unlearnable rather than informative. CORESET and BGADL trail further behind (30–35% avg AP), indicating that pure diversity or generative-space uncertainty criteria are poorly suited to the long-tail, depth-ambiguous nature of roadside 3D detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BEVHeight roadside 3D detector (CVPR 2023) and its underlying Lift-Splat-Shoot depth prediction mechanism, repurposing the per-location depth distributions not for detection but as a learnability signal for active sample selection. It departs from the standard active learning paradigm (uncertainty-based and diversity-based sampling) by introducing a 'learnability-driven' criterion grounded in concave-over-modular submodular optimization, a framework previously applied to sensor placement and data subset selection but not to roadside BEV perception. The three-stage hierarchical selector (depth confidence → semantic balance → geometric variation) is a novel composition of submodular objectives tailored to the specific failure modes of monocular roadside 3D detection, where high-uncertainty samples are often inherently unlearnable rather than merely difficult.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical and previously underappreciated bottleneck in roadside 3D perception: a substantial fraction of roadside-only frames are inherently ambiguous and provide weak supervision regardless of annotation effort, wasting annotation budgets that uncertainty-based AL would happily spend on them. The learnability framing and the human study validating that ambiguous samples yield lower AP under matched budgets offer a concrete diagnostic for practitioners deploying roadside perception systems. Open questions include whether the depth-confidence signal generalizes beyond LSS-style backbones, how the method behaves with multi-camera roadside rigs, and whether the submodular formulation scales to the tens of thousands of images in citywide deployments without approximation.
