## Problem

Multi-modal 3D occupancy prediction suffers from two intertwined limitations: sparse LiDAR is occlusion-biased and misses large occupied regions, while mainstream fusion pipelines (dense voxel lifting, BEV transformers) incur prohibitive memory and compute that hinder scalability. Existing Gaussian-based occupancy methods are vision-only, leaving the application of compact Gaussian primitives to multi-modal fusion under real-world sparse LiDAR constraints largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261959/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single-frame sparse LiDAR point cloud (N_P 3D points with intensity) and N_I surround-view RGB images, and outputs a dense voxelized semantic occupancy grid O ∈ R^{|C|×X×Y×Z} over a fixed spatial extent. Internally, the scene is represented as a compact set of N_G semantic 3D Gaussians (center, rotation quaternion, scale, semantic vector) rather than a full volumetric tensor; each voxel only aggregates Gaussians within its local neighborhood. For LiDAR completion training, dense supervision is constructed by aggregating K temporally adjacent ego-motion-aligned sweeps, while at inference only a single sparse scan is required.

## Method

Gau-Occ operates in two stages. First, a LiDAR Completion Diffuser (LCD) performs point-wise local diffusion (perturbing each ground-truth point independently within its neighborhood, without global scaling) to reconstruct dense, metrically faithful geometry from sparse scans, trained with an L2 noise-prediction loss conditioned on the raw LiDAR. Second, the completed points are voxelized into a sparse 3D CNN to extract geometry features, and a hybrid density-based + random initialization selects Gaussian centers. Each Gaussian anchor then drives the Gaussian Anchor Fusion (GAF) module: it reprojects onto all camera views, predicts geometry-conditioned 2D sampling offsets via a two-layer MLP, bilinearly samples multi-scale FPN features at those offsets, compresses the resulting tokens via a geometry-aware VLAD codebook (soft assignments conditioned on the LiDAR feature), applies FiLM modulation, and fuses everything through a single cross-attention layer where the LiDAR anchor is the query and modulated visual tokens are keys/values. The fused features update Gaussian attributes (center offset, scale, rotation, semantic vector) via a two-layer FFN, and the refined Gaussians are locally splatted into voxel space to produce the final occupancy prediction.

## Results

On SurroundOcc-nuScenes, Gau-Occ achieves 44.3 IoU and 32.7 mIoU, setting a new state-of-the-art among multi-modal (LiDAR+Camera) methods and surpassing the previous best, DAOcc, by +1.5 IoU and +0.6 mIoU. On Occ3D-nuScenes, Gau-Occ reaches 55.1 mIoU, outperforming DAOcc by +0.8, SDGOcc by +3.4, and the radar-augmented OccFusion by +6.4. On KITTI-360, Gau-Occ exceeds the strongest LiDAR-only baseline L2COcc by +1.3 IoU and +0.6 mIoU under a challenging single-camera + LiDAR setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gau-Occ outperforms all 23 compared baselines across the three benchmarks. On SurroundOcc-nuScenes, it beats the strongest multi-modal competitor DAOcc [49] by +1.5 IoU (44.3 vs 42.8) and +0.6 mIoU (32.7 vs 32.1), while also surpassing SDGOcc [7] (+2.6 mIoU) and OccMamba [19] (+2.8 mIoU). Among camera-only methods, Gau-Occ exceeds the best Gaussian-based baseline GaussianFormer-2 [16] by +12.6 IoU and +11.9 mIoU, and the best BEV/transformer baseline OccFormer [53] by +12.9 IoU. On Occ3D-nuScenes, Gau-Occ (55.1 mIoU) leads DAOcc (54.3) by +0.8, SDGOcc (51.7) by +3.4, and the radar-augmented OccFusion [52] (48.7) by +6.4, demonstrating that LiDAR completion alone suffices to surpass a three-sensor system. On KITTI-360, Gau-Occ surpasses the LiDAR-only L2COcc [43] by +1.3 IoU and +0.6 mIoU, confirming robustness under sparse single-camera + LiDAR conditions. Notably, Gau-Occ achieves these gains without any detection-level auxiliary supervision, unlike DAOcc which leverages object detection priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gau-Occ sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: Gaussian-based occupancy prediction (GaussianFormer, GaussianFormer-2), which is vision-only and uses random Gaussian initialization, and multi-modal LiDAR-camera occupancy methods (DAOcc, SDGOcc, Co-Occ), which rely on dense voxel or BEV tensors for fusion. The paper repurposes the 3D Gaussian primitive from 3DGS [Kerbl et al.] as a compact scene representation and introduces a LiDAR Completion Diffuser (a local, point-wise variant of DDPM) to address the sparsity problem that prior multi-modal methods handle only implicitly. The Gaussian Anchor Fusion module is a novel combination of VLAD-style codebook aggregation and FiLM modulation, conditioned on LiDAR geometry, which departs from the standard cross-attention fusion used in most prior multi-modal occupancy pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing dense volumetric tensors with a compact set of semantic Gaussians, Gau-Occ offers a practical path toward higher-resolution or longer-horizon occupancy prediction without the memory blowup that currently limits multi-modal systems. The LCD module's use of aggregated multi-sweep supervision as a training target is a clean solution to the occlusion-bias problem, though its generalization to extremely sparse or degraded LiDAR (e.g., heavy rain, low-frequency sensors) remains untested. Open questions include whether the feed-forward Gaussian refinement can be extended to streaming/temporal settings, whether the VLAD codebook size (M=32) is optimal across scenes, and whether the approach transfers to other sensor combinations (e.g., radar-only or event cameras).
