## Problem

Existing diffusion codecs build on text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion, but text conditioning is fundamentally suboptimal for compression: human-generated text is inefficient at describing fine-grained spatial details, and discrete text vocabularies are non-differentiable, preventing end-to-end joint optimization of the encoder and decoder. No diffusion foundation model has been trained from scratch to jointly optimize compression and generation, leaving downstream codecs constrained by a backbone not designed for their task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261096/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CoD takes an RGB image (trained progressively at 256×256 and 512×512) and produces a compressed token sequence at ultra-low bitrate (0.0039 bpp via vector quantization with a 16-entry codebook over 32×32 patches) plus a reconstructed image. The encoder compresses to 1/32 resolution, the condition decoder upsamples to 1/16, and the diffusion model (DiT backbone + DDT head) operates at 1/16 resolution. In pixel space, a 16×16 patch embedding maps noised images to 1/16 resolution and a pixel neural-field head reconstructs each 16×16 patch; in latent space, a 2×2 patch embedding downsamples SD-VAE latents from 1/8 to 1/16 before the DDT head.

## Method

CoD consists of a native image encoder (stacked residual blocks and attention layers), a vector-quantization entropy bottleneck (codebook size 16, enforcing 0.0039 bpp), a condition decoder, and a diffusion model with a decoupled DiT backbone and DDT head. The key training innovation is a unified rectified-flow formulation: (1−α)% of training samples are forced to t=0, where the RF loss reduces to MSE(x, x̂₀), directly minimizing one-step reconstruction distortion alongside the multi-step perceptual objective. This jointly optimizes the distortion and perception terms of the rate-distortion-perception trade-off without requiring explicit perceptual losses. Training is fully self-supervised (no text-image pairs), using REPA alignment with DINOv2, codebook commitment loss, and an auxiliary reconstruction head, on 22M open images in ~20 A100 GPU days—300× cheaper than Stable Diffusion. The model is designed as a general foundation model rather than a fixed codec, intended to replace Stable Diffusion in downstream diffusion-based compression algorithms like DiffC.

## Results

At 0.0039 bpp on Kodak (512×512), pixel-space CoD achieves 19.6 dB PSNR with a single sampling step and the best perceptual quality at 25 steps, while matching VTM in BPP-PSNR (BD-Rate −2.1%) with significantly better perceptual quality. In the latent space, CoD delivers the best reconstruction quality at ultra-low bitrates (<0.02 bpp) compared to all Stable-Diffusion-based codecs. Pixel-space CoD scales to near-lossless ~47 dB PSNR at 4 bpp, far exceeding the SD-VAE ceiling of ~26 dB at 0.6 bpp. Training requires only ~20 A100 GPU days (0.3% of Stable Diffusion's ~6,250 GPU days) on 22M open images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary zero-shot framework DiffC [43], replacing Stable Diffusion with CoD yields substantially better PSNR and LPIPS at low bitrates; text conditions in SD are shown to be actively detrimental to compression. In the latent space, CoD outperforms DDCM [35], PerCo (SD) [26], OSCAR [15], and DiffEIC [30] at ultra-low bitrates (<0.02 bpp), though the gap narrows near the SD-VAE ceiling (~0.26–0.60 bpp). In the pixel space, CoD achieves a BD-Rate of −2.1% relative to VTM [44] while delivering markedly better perceptual quality, and surpasses GAN-based perceptual codecs HiFiC [32], MS-ILLM [34], CDC [50], and TACO [27] in PSNR, DISTS, and FID across all bitrates. Notably, a 49M-parameter CoD outperforms the 181M-parameter MS-ILLM, confirming algorithmic rather than parametric gains. Compared to PerCo [7] at a similar bitrate (0.0039 vs 0.0036 bpp), pixel-space CoD achieves higher reconstruction fidelity and wider bitrate range. StableCodec [53] and OneDC [49] are discussed in the appendix as one-step diffusion baselines, where fine-tuned one-step CoD reportedly achieves real-time and SOTA performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoD departs from the dominant paradigm of repurposing text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) as compression backbones, arguing instead that text conditioning is fundamentally suboptimal for rate-distortion optimization. Architecturally, it builds on the decoupled DiT+DDT design [46], the VQ-VAE-style encoder/quantizer [12], and the pixel neural-field head from PixNerd [45], but reorganizes them around a compression-specific information bottleneck trained end-to-end with rectified flow [31]. The unified training loss, which injects a one-step distortion term at t=0 into the rectified flow objective, is a novel formulation that bridges the gap between perceptual (distribution-matching) and distortion (pixel-fidelity) objectives within a single continuous-flow framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoD provides the first reproducible, open-dataset, low-cost (20 A100 GPU-days vs. ~6,250 for Stable Diffusion) foundation model purpose-built for diffusion codecs, lowering the barrier for future research in this area. The demonstration that pixel-space diffusion can match VTM-level PSNR while surpassing GAN-based perceptual codecs (MS-ILLM, HiFiC) in FID challenges the prevailing assumption that latent-space diffusion is the only viable route for high-quality generative compression. Key open questions include whether CoD scales to 2K+ resolutions, whether distillation can bring inference to real-time speeds, and whether the pixel-space advantage persists at even lower bitrates where the generative prior must fill in more missing information.
