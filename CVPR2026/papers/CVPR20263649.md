## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for generative models either rely on iterative optimization (minutes to hours) or, in the closed-form case (e.g., UCE), fail to guarantee preservation of non-target concepts because least-squares solutions can perturb correlated or non-orthogonal directions in the latent space. The paper asks whether concept erasure can be solved analytically while provably leaving preserved representations untouched.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263649/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pretrained model's linear projection matrices (attention Key/Value matrices in diffusion models, or text-embedding layers in flow-matching models like FLUX), the CLIP text embeddings of the target concept(s) to erase, the embeddings of a set of non-target concepts to preserve, and an optional safe-subspace basis. It outputs a rank-one (or low-rank for multi-concept) update ΔW added to the original matrix, yielding a modified generative model that suppresses the target while leaving preserved directions invariant. The entire computation is data-independent—no image sampling or backpropagation is required—and completes in seconds on a single GPU.

## Method

DP decomposes concept erasure into two sequential closed-form projections. First, the target concept embedding c_i is projected onto a safe subspace S (spanned by non-target directions) via the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse to obtain a proxy c_i*, which reduces the magnitude of the required update. Second, the weight update ΔW is solved as a linearly constrained least-squares problem where the constraint ΔW·C_pres = 0 forces the update to lie entirely in the left nullspace of the preserved-concept matrix, guaranteeing exact invariance of all non-target representations (Theorem 4.1). The solution takes the form ΔW* = W_0(c_i* − c_i) x^T / ‖x‖² · U_2^T, where U_2 is an orthonormal basis for the left nullspace and x = U_2^T c_i. This nullspace constraint is the key geometric distinction from UCE: it decouples erasure from preservation at the subspace level rather than relying on a weighted least-squares trade-off, yielding provable preservation bounds even under SVD truncation of C_pres.

## Results

On SD 1.4 object erasure (10 ImageNet categories), DP achieves a mean erased accuracy of 0.8% (vs. 7.2% for ESD, 5.7% for CP, 9.6% for AGE, and 7.8% for UCE) while incurring only a 2.4% mean preservation drop (vs. 19.5% for ESD, 32.5% for CP, 5.6% for AGE, and 6.7% for UCE). On the FLUX flow-matching model, DP reduces mean erased accuracy to 1.0% with a 0.6% preservation drop, compared to UCE's 23.9% and 2.2% respectively. For style erasure on SD 1.4, DP achieves 11.7% mean erased accuracy and 0.5% preservation drop, outperforming all baselines on preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP outperforms all four baselines on both erasure and preservation metrics across SD 1.4 and FLUX. Against UCE (the closest closed-form competitor), DP reduces mean erased accuracy from 7.8% to 0.8% on SD 1.4 and from 23.9% to 1.0% on FLUX, while also halving the preservation drop (2.4% vs. 6.7% on SD 1.4; 0.6% vs. 2.2% on FLUX). Against iterative methods ESD, CP, and AGE, DP achieves substantially better erasure (0.8% vs. 5.7–9.6%) and dramatically better preservation (2.4% vs. 5.6–32.5%), with CP showing the worst preservation at 32.5% drop. On style erasure (Table 2), DP leads on preservation (0.5% mean drop vs. 1.1% for UCE, 6.7% for AGE) and is competitive on erasure (11.7% vs. 14.5% for UCE), though individual styles like Picasso show UCE slightly ahead (30.0% vs. 26.0% erased accuracy). The gap is most pronounced on FLUX, where UCE's single-projection approach fails on complex categories (Church: 63%, Gas Pump: 73%) while DP achieves near-zero residuals across all ten objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends UCE (Unified Concept Editing), which already provided a closed-form linear transformation for concept erasure in diffusion models. The key departure is a second projection step that constrains the weight update to the left nullspace of preserved concept embeddings, converting UCE's approximate preservation (which Theorem 3.1 shows can still degrade non-target concepts) into an exact preservation guarantee (Theorem 4.1). The work also generalizes the closed-form erasure paradigm from diffusion models to flow-matching architectures (FLUX), where prior iterative methods like ESD are architecturally incompatible, positioning DP as a more broadly applicable alternative to the fine-tuning, pruning, and adversarial erasure families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free, seconds-long, drop-in procedure with provable preservation guarantees lowers the barrier to safe model editing for practitioners who cannot afford iterative retraining. The clean geometric decomposition (safe-subspace projection + nullspace-constrained update) offers an interpretable design principle that could inform future model-editing tasks beyond erasure. Limitations include the residual gap from perfect preservation due to positional embeddings and self-attention interactions, and the evaluation is limited to 10 ImageNet objects and 5 artistic styles, leaving open whether the nullspace constraint scales to hundreds of simultaneously preserved concepts or to more entangled semantic relationships.
