## Problem

Existing 3D world generation methods are fundamentally constrained to single-scale synthesis, producing either landscapes, rooms, or cities but never coherent content spanning multiple spatial granularities. The core obstacle is the absence of a scale-adaptive 3D representation that can grow dynamically as new fine-scale content is synthesized, since all prior multi-scale representations (LoD, hierarchical Gaussian splatting, Mip-NeRF) assume complete multi-scale supervision available upfront for one-pass optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image together with a user-specified sequence of text prompts and camera extrinsics (4×4 matrices) that progressively zoom into regions of interest, each with a larger focal length than the previous. It outputs a nested hierarchy of 3D scenes {E₀, …, Eₙ} represented as Gaussian surfels, where each finer scene is spatially contained within its parent, supporting real-time rendering at any observation scale. The same input image can be re-explored with different prompt and camera sequences to generate alternative zoom paths.

## Method

WonderZoom introduces two coupled components. First, scale-adaptive Gaussian surfels extend standard 3D Gaussian surfels with a native-scale parameter s^native computed from the creation viewpoint; new surfels are simply appended per scale without re-optimizing existing ones, and a log-space opacity modulation (Eq. 3) guarantees a partition-of-unity property (Proposition 1) so adjacent-scale surfels cross-fade seamlessly during zoom, reducing GPU memory from 7.96 GB to 3.40 GB and boosting FPS from 1.4 to 97.2. Second, a progressive detail synthesizer operates in three stages per zoom step: (a) extreme super-resolution (Chain-of-Zoom) conditioned on VLM-extracted semantic context, followed by controllable image editing to insert user-specified novel structures; (b) scale-consistent depth registration by fine-tuning a monocular depth estimator (MoGe) against sparse target depth rendered from the coarser scene, with SAM/Grounded-SAM segment-wise correction; and (c) auxiliary view synthesis via a camera-controlled video diffusion model (Gen3C) to produce temporally consistent neighboring views, enabling full 3D scene optimization beyond the single target viewpoint.

## Results

WonderZoom achieves the best scores across all quantitative metrics: CLIP score 0.3432 (vs. 0.3004 for Gen3C, the strongest baseline), CLIP-IQA+ 0.7035, Q-align IQA 3.926, NIQE 3.695 (lower is better), and Q-align IAA 2.986. In the human study (2AFC), WonderZoom is preferred over all baselines with favor rates of 76–83% on zoom-in accuracy, 82–99% on visual quality, and 91–99% on prompt match. Generation time per new-scale scene is 62.1 s, substantially faster than HunyuanWorld (704.2 s) and Voyager (596.6 s), though slower than WonderWorld (9.3 s) which cannot produce multi-scale content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WonderZoom outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the 3D scene generation methods (WonderWorld and HunyuanWorld), the advantage is most pronounced: CLIP score improves by 0.075 and 0.092 respectively, and Q-align IQA jumps from ~1.06–1.08 to 3.926, reflecting that these single-scale 3D methods produce blurry, incoherent zoom-in views. Against the camera-controlled video generation baselines (Gen3C and Voyager), WonderZoom leads on CLIP score (0.3432 vs. 0.3004 and 0.2609) and CLIP-IQA+ (0.7035 vs. 0.5489 and 0.5746), indicating better prompt alignment and perceptual quality, while also being 5–10× faster per scene. In the human study, WonderZoom is preferred over the 3D baselines with near-unanimous margins (98–99% on visual quality and prompt match), while the gap over video baselines is smaller but still clear (82–84% visual quality, 91–96% prompt match). The paper notes that no prior method supports true multi-scale 3D generation, so these comparisons are somewhat asymmetric: baselines are evaluated on their best single-scale output rather than a multi-scale equivalent.

## Relation to Prior Work

WonderZoom builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (specifically WonderWorld's surfel formulation) as its geometric primitive, but fundamentally departs from the reconstruction paradigm by making the representation dynamically updatable for progressive generation. It draws conceptual inspiration from classical Level-of-Detail and hierarchical 3DGS for multi-scale rendering, yet inverts their assumption: rather than encoding pre-existing content at all scales, it grows the hierarchy incrementally as new content is synthesized. The progressive detail synthesizer composes existing components—Chain-of-Zoom for extreme super-resolution, Gen3C for camera-controlled video diffusion, MoGe/GeometryCrafter for depth, and Grounded SAM for segmentation—into a coherent coarse-to-fine 3D pipeline, extending the 2D multi-scale generation idea of Generative Powers of Ten into the 3D domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstrated approach to multi-scale 3D world generation from a single image, opening a new interactive paradigm where users can zoom from landscapes to microscopic details with on-demand content synthesis. The scale-aware opacity modulation is a practical and elegant technique (log-space linear interpolation yielding a partition-of-unity property) that makes real-time multi-scale rendering computationally feasible—without it, rendering drops from 97 FPS to 1.4 FPS, suggesting the technique could benefit other hierarchical 3DGS applications. Open questions include how error accumulates over many zoom levels, whether the pipeline's reliance on multiple off-the-shelf models (super-resolution, video diffusion, depth estimation) limits scalability, and whether the approach generalizes to very deep hierarchies (beyond the 5 scales demonstrated).
