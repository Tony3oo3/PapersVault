## Problem

Iris recognition for immersive applications (VR/XR) faces three unique challenges absent in traditional controlled setups: off-axis perspective distortion, intra-subject variation from illumination and gaze changes, and quality degradation from non-cooperative users. No large-scale public dataset captured all three challenges simultaneously, and state-of-the-art methods relying on iris normalization generalize poorly to these conditions, as demonstrated by sharp FRR increases when transferring from controlled to immersive data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262072/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes NIR ocular images at 640×640 resolution captured off-axis by side-mounted cameras on a VR headset. Rather than segmenting and polar-unwrapping the iris, it crops a square bounding box around the iris region (expanded by 1.2× to retain periocular context) and resizes it to the network input shape. The output is a fixed-dimensional identity embedding used for 1-to-1 verification (FRR@FAR) or 1-to-N identification (rank-1 accuracy) across single-eye and dual-eye configurations.

## Method

The paper proposes NormFree, a normalization-free iris recognition paradigm that replaces the traditional two-stage pipeline (iris segmentation + polar unwrapping → feature extraction) with a single end-to-end stage. A pre-trained ocular detector provides a bounding box, which is expanded by 1.2× and resized; a ResNet-50 (IR-50) backbone trained with ArcFace angular-margin loss then maps this minimally adjusted crop directly to an identity embedding. The key insight is that modern DNN feature extractors can absorb the invariance that normalization was originally designed to provide, making the normalization stage a fallible bottleneck under off-axis distortion and degradation. The paper also introduces the ImmerIris dataset (499,791 images, 546 subjects) with 8 evaluation protocols of increasing difficulty, and shows NormFree outperforms seven normalization-based SOTAs in most settings while an ablation (NormKeep, same architecture but with normalization) confirms the standalone benefit of removing the normalization stage.

## Results

On the most challenging Immer-Any protocol (dual-eye), the proposed NormFree achieves FRR@FAR of 1.24% / 13.29% / 40.45% at FAR 1e-1 / 1e-3 / 1e-5, outperforming the best SOTA (CM: 1.84% / 16.60% / 38.44%) and the normalization ablation NormKeep (3.85% / 20.95% / 44.69%). On the easiest Immer-Control protocol (dual-eye), NormFree reaches 0.18% / 1.16% / 5.17%, again the best. Cross-domain evaluation shows SOTAs trained on CASIA-Iris-V4 suffer a catastrophic drop from ~5–50% FRR@FAR(1e-5) on CASIA-T to 85–94% on Immer-Any, confirming the unique difficulty of immersive acquisition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NormFree outperforms all seven normalization-based SOTAs on the three protocols where dual-eye testing is available (Immer-Control, Immer-Fix, Immer-Any) at every FAR level. The closest competitor is CM [45], which trails NormFree by 1.36–3.31 percentage points in FRR@FAR(1e-3) across the three protocols (e.g., 16.60% vs 13.29% on Immer-Any). ComplexIrisNet [27] and UE-UGCL [44] are also consistently behind by 2–6 points. The training-free baselines Gabor [9] and OM [38] perform worst, with FRR@FAR(1e-3) exceeding 58% on Immer-Any, confirming that normalization alone is insufficient under immersive conditions. The ablation NormKeep (same ResNet backbone but with normalization retained) is 7.66 points worse than NormFree on Immer-Any (20.95% vs 13.29%), isolating the benefit of removing normalization. Notably, even the best SOTA (CM) still achieves only 38.44% FRR@FAR(1e-5) on Immer-Any, indicating substantial room for further methodological improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two-stage iris recognition paradigm (normalization via polar transform followed by feature extraction) that has been standard since Daugman's foundational work, arguing that normalization becomes a bottleneck under off-axis distortion and unconstrained conditions. Rather than building on prior iris-specific architectures (Gabor filters, ordinal measures, ComplexIrisNet, UE-UGCL, etc.), the proposed NormFree method repurposes a face recognition recipe (ResNet-50 + ArcFace) applied to a simple bounding-box crop, drawing an explicit analogy to how face recognition succeeded in open scenes without elaborate preprocessing. The dataset itself extends the line of iris databases from controlled/semi-controlled on-axis acquisition (CASIA, IITD, UBIRIS) and the closely related PolyU Iris DB to a fully off-axis, unconstrained VR-headset setting with deliberate illumination and gaze variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ImmerIris (499,791 images, 546 subjects) is the largest public iris dataset to date and the first dedicated to immersive applications, providing a 25× larger training set than CASIA-T and systematic protocols that isolate occlusion, dilation, illumination, and gaze effects. The finding that all normalization-based SOTAs degrade catastrophically (FRR@1e-5 jumping from ~5–50% on CASIA to ~50–94% on Immer-Any) while a simple crop-and-classify approach outperforms them challenges the field's core assumption that iris normalization is indispensable. Key open questions include how to specifically address gaze variation (the dominant challenge at ~37% degradation), whether the normalization-free paradigm generalizes to visible-wavelength or cross-sensor settings, and whether more expressive architectures can close the remaining gap on the hardest protocols.
