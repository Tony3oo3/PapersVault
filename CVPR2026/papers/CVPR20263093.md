## Problem

Existing image generation benchmarks focus on text-to-image or single-image editing, leaving the emerging capability of multi-image context generation—where a model must integrate, reason across, and maintain consistency among multiple related reference images—largely unquantified. Even state-of-the-art unified multimodal models (UMMs) distribute attention indiscriminately across reference images, causing identity confusion, spatial errors, and hallucinated content in compositional outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263093/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark and method take 2–5 reference RGB images (generated via Qwen-Image, GPT-4o-Image, or Nano-Banana) together with a compositional text prompt specifying how elements from different references should be combined, and produce a single generated image. The MICON-Bench comprises 1,043 cases across six tasks (object composition, spatial composition, attribute disentanglement, component transfer, FG/BG composition, and story generation) totaling 2,518 images. Evaluation is automated via an MLLM (Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct) that checks binary pass/fail against per-case verifiable checkpoints spanning seven dimensions (instruction following, identity, structure, cross-reference consistency, causality, text grounding, overall usability).

## Method

The core contribution is Dynamic Attention Rebalancing (DAR), a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time mechanism that corrects the UMM's tendency to attend uniformly across reference images. DAR uniformly samples m=64 query tokens from the full noise-token sequence, computes their softmax attention against all reference-image key tokens, and aggregates scores across heads to obtain a per-reference-token relevance score. After min-max normalization, tokens above a high threshold (τ_high=0.7) are amplified by a factor (1+γ) and tokens below a low threshold (τ_low=0.3) are suppressed by (1−γ), with γ=0.15 controlling adjustment magnitude. The reweighted key matrix is then used in the final full-query attention computation, requiring no additional training or architectural modification. This mechanism is applied to both BAGEL and OmniGen2 and consistently improves cross-image coherence, identity preservation, and attribute fidelity across all six MICON-Bench tasks and two external benchmarks.

## Results

On MICON-Bench (1,043 cases, 2,518 images), proprietary UMMs lead: GPT-Image achieves the highest average score of 91.51, followed by Nano-Banana at 89.25. Among open-source UMMs, BAGEL scores 73.55 and OmniGen2 scores 67.83; applying the proposed Dynamic Attention Rebalancing (DAR) improves these to 76.31 (+2.76) and 69.21 (+1.38) respectively, with the largest gains in Component Transfer (BAGEL: 52.40→56.06; OmniGen2: 44.76→48.72) and FG/BG Composition (BAGEL: 64.64→71.24). On the OmniContext benchmark, DAR lifts BAGEL's average from 5.536 to 5.804 and OmniGen2's from 7.532 to 7.768, and on XVerseBench it raises OmniGen2's overall score from 51.14 to 51.73.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven models on MICON-Bench. Proprietary UMMs (GPT-Image at 91.51 avg, Nano-Banana at 89.25) substantially outperform all open-source and diffusion-based baselines, particularly on Object Composition (96.45 and 95.60) and Spatial Composition (94.41 and 93.79). Among diffusion-based models, DreamOmni2 (75.56) and Qwen-Image-Edit-2507 (72.96) are competitive on simpler tasks but collapse on Component Transfer (59.64 and 42.68) and FG/BG (76.16 and 72.08), while UNO (44.76) is far behind on all tasks. The proposed DAR, applied to the two open-source UMMs, consistently improves both: BAGEL gains +2.76 avg (73.55→76.31), surpassing DreamOmni2 and Qwen-Image-Edit, and OmniGen2 gains +1.38 avg (67.83→69.21). However, even with DAR, both open-source UMMs remain 13–22 points below the proprietary leaders, indicating a substantial gap in multi-image reasoning. On the secondary benchmarks (OmniContext, XVerseBench), DAR yields smaller but consistent improvements (e.g., OmniGen2 overall 51.14→51.73 on XVerseBench), confirming generalizability beyond MICON-Bench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multi-image generation benchmarking and inference-time attention control. While prior benchmarks (Geneval, T2ICompBench, ImgEdit-Bench) evaluate text-to-image fidelity or single-image editing, and OmniContext addresses basic multi-subject consistency, none systematically probe cross-image compositional reasoning, attribute disentanglement, or causal narrative continuation. The DAR mechanism repurposes the standard attention computation in transformer-based UMMs by inserting a lightweight, training-free reweighting step at inference, drawing on the broader idea of attention visualization for diagnosis but applying it as an active intervention rather than a passive analysis tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MICON-Bench provides the first structured, automatically verifiable evaluation suite for the multi-image context generation capability that native UMMs (Gemini, Nano-Banana) are beginning to exhibit, giving the community a diagnostic tool as this capability matures. The ablation showing consistent performance degradation from 2 to 5 reference images (Table 5) highlights a concrete scaling bottleneck that current architectures do not handle gracefully. Key limitations include the reliance on AI-generated reference images (Qwen-Image) which may not capture the full diversity of real-world multi-image inputs, and the MLLM-based checkpoint evaluation, which introduces its own judgment biases. Open questions include whether DAR generalizes to non-autoregressive diffusion-based UMMs and whether the benchmark's AI-generated references will remain representative as models improve.
