## Problem

Pixel-wise segmentation achieves high fidelity but incurs prohibitive computational cost in real-time surgical settings, while existing differentiable superpixel methods (SSN, HERS) collapse pixel detail too early by averaging within regions, losing the fine boundary cues critical for instrument-tissue delineation. No prior framework simultaneously delivers superpixel-level efficiency and pixel-level boundary precision in high-stakes surgical scenes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB surgical frames resized to 512×640 and produces a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask (class logits) blended from two paths: a fast coarse prediction for stable region interiors and a context-aware refined prediction for boundary zones. Internally it also outputs a dense boundary-confidence gate (values in [0,1] per pixel) that determines the per-pixel mixing ratio between the two paths. The full pipeline runs at 150.25 FPS with 24M parameters on an RTX-A6000 Pro GPU.

## Method

BRDG organizes a ResNet-34/U-Net backbone into three cooperative agents: (1) a region creator that produces dense features, a temperature-controlled soft superpixel assignment map, and K region descriptors via soft-pooling; (2) a boundary detector MLP that scores each region descriptor for boundary likelihood and reprojects these scores through the assignment map into a dense pixel-wise gate; and (3) a refinement agent that concatenates the original pixel feature, the reprojected region context, and normalized spatial coordinates into a fused tensor processed by a lightweight MLP, then blends its refined logits with the coarse 1×1-conv logits using the gate. An adjacency-boosted contrastive loss (Eq. 4) penalizes feature similarity between neighboring superpixels on opposite sides of a semantic boundary, sharpening region descriptor separation. Training uses a multi-stage schedule (frozen encoder → unfreeze at 0.1× LR → full training) over 100 epochs with AdamW, combining cross-entropy, Tversky, boundary BCE, and the contrastive objective.

## Results

On the three primary surgical benchmarks, BRDG achieves 0.72 mIoU on EndoVis'18 Parts, 0.75 mIoU on EndoVis'18 Tools, and 0.76 mIoU on EndoVis'17, outperforming all pixel-wise and superpixel-based baselines. Boundary F1 (BF1) reaches 0.71 on EndoVis'18 Tools, a +10.88-point margin over the strongest superpixel competitor HERS (0.60). In terms of efficiency, BRDG runs at 150.25 FPS with 23.9M parameters and 157.97 GFLOPs on an RTX-6000 Pro, making it ~10× faster than DeepLabv3+ (15.1 FPS) and SegFormer-B5 (13.84 FPS) while being 3.5× smaller than SegFormer-B5 (84.7M params). On general-domain benchmarks, BRDG attains 0.54 mIoU on Cityscapes and 0.60 mIoU on ADE20K under the foveated/efficient segmentation protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRDG outperforms all superpixel-based baselines by wide margins: on EndoVis'18 Parts it leads HERS (0.45 mIoU) by +27 points and SSN (0.37) by +35 points, while on EndoVis'18 Tools it surpasses HERS (0.70) by +5 points and SSN (0.41) by +34 points. Against pixel-wise SOTA, BRDG beats SegFormer-B5 on EndoVis'18 Parts (0.72 vs 0.57), EndoVis'18 Tools (0.75 vs 0.71), and EndoVis'17 (0.76 vs 0.63), though SegFormer-B5 narrowly edges it on CholecSeg8K (0.74 vs 0.73 mIoU). DeepLabv3+ (R101) slightly exceeds BRDG on EndoVis'18 Tools mIoU (0.78 vs 0.75) but BRDG recovers on BF1 (0.71 vs 0.67) and is 10× faster (150.25 vs 15.1 FPS) and 2.5× smaller (23.9M vs 61.0M params). On boundary-specific metrics, BRDG's BF1 of 0.71 on EndoVis'18 Tools represents a +10.88-point gain over HERS (0.60) and +4.4 points over DeepLabv3+ (0.67), confirming the boundary-gating mechanism's effectiveness. On general-domain benchmarks (Cityscapes, ADE20K), BRDG (0.54 / 0.60 mIoU) surpasses the best FSNet variant (0.52 / 0.56) under the foveated segmentation protocol, demonstrating cross-domain generalizability. The efficiency advantage is consistent: BRDG's 150.25 FPS and 23.9M parameters place it in a favorable accuracy–speed–size trade-off that no single baseline matches across all three axes simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRDG extends the differentiable superpixel lineage (SSN, HERS) by addressing their core limitation: collapsing all pixel information into region-level descriptors before classification, which destroys boundary fidelity. It departs from this by preserving full pixel-level features within stable superpixel interiors and introducing a learned boundary-confidence gate that routes only ambiguous boundary pixels through a dedicated refinement path. The adjacency-boosted contrastive loss further differentiates BRDG from prior region-level contrastive methods (ReCo, PiCIE) by exploiting the superpixel adjacency graph to mine hard negatives specifically across semantic boundaries, coupling geometric proximity with semantic contrast in a single objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For real-time surgical segmentation, BRDG's 150 FPS at 24M parameters (vs. 13–15 FPS at 60–85M for SOTA pixel-wise models) makes high-precision instrument and tissue delineation feasible on edge hardware, directly addressing a deployment bottleneck in robot-assisted surgery. However, the approach's sensitivity to low-frequency contrast degradation (fog drops mIoU by 13+ points) and the gate-misfire failure mode suggest that boundary-gating mechanisms may be fragile under distribution shift, an open question for clinical robustness. Whether the optimal superpixel count (K=500) and the boundary-gating strategy transfer cleanly to other domains beyond surgical scenes remains to be validated at scale.
