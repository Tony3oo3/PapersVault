## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods rely on iterative rendering-guided feature optimization to distill CLIP semantics into 3D Gaussians, making them slow (minutes to hours), memory-heavy (storing 512-dim features per Gaussian), and prone to semantic blurring when Gaussians are projected into 2D. No prior approach achieves fast, training-free semantic injection that avoids both the optimization bottleneck and the per-Gaussian feature storage overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260075/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS scene, multi-view RGB images of that scene, and a natural-language text query at inference time. It produces object-level 3D clusters (each with a single representative CLIP feature) rather than per-Gaussian features, enabling segmentation by comparing the query embedding against ~100 cluster features instead of ~100,000 Gaussian features. The 3DGS scene itself is not modified or retrained; only a 2-byte index field is appended to each Gaussian, reducing per-Gaussian semantic storage from 2048 bytes to 2 bytes.

## Method

LightSplat replaces iterative per-Gaussian feature optimization with a single-step indexed injection: SAM masks and CLIP features are extracted from each view, and each Gaussian is assigned a compact 2-byte mask index based on its alpha-blending contribution to the corresponding 2D mask (above a contribution threshold). A 3D-aware mask filtering step discards masks with insufficient geometric support in 3D, suppressing view-dependent artifacts. An inter-mask graph is then built where edges connect masks whose Gaussian sets overlap (IoU ≥ threshold) and whose CLIP features are semantically similar (cosine ≥ threshold); connected components of this graph yield object-level 3D clusters in one step. Cluster features are computed by averaging the CLIP features of constituent masks, forming a compact cluster-feature mapping that replaces all per-Gaussian feature handling. The entire pipeline is training-free, completing feature distillation in ~4–5 seconds versus minutes to hours for prior methods.

## Results

On LERF-OVS, LightSplat achieves 47.58 mIoU and 68.32 mAcc@0.25 with a feature distillation time of only 4.2 seconds, outperforming the previous SOTA Dr.Splat (43.58 mIoU, 63.87 mAcc@0.25) by 4 mIoU and 4.45 mAcc@0.25 while being ~57× faster. On ScanNet (19-class setting), it reaches 37.11 mIoU and 58.66 mAcc, surpassing Dr.Splat by 7.68 mIoU and 14.06 mAcc, with 64× lower per-Gaussian memory (2 bytes vs. 128 bytes) and 500 FPS inference. On the newly introduced DL3DV-OVS benchmark, LightSplat achieves 44.98 mIoU and 60.82 mAcc@0.25 in 4.8 seconds, beating LUDVIG by 15.77 mIoU with 150× faster distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightSplat outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor Dr.Splat, it gains 7.68 mIoU and 14.06 mAcc on ScanNet (19-class), 4 mIoU and 4.45 mAcc@0.25 on LERF-OVS, while reducing feature distillation from 3 minutes to 4.1 seconds and per-Gaussian memory from 128 to 2 bytes. Versus LUDVIG, LightSplat achieves 15.77 higher mIoU on DL3DV-OVS and 8.64 higher mIoU on ScanNet (19-class), with 150× faster distillation and 1024× less memory (2 vs. 2048 bytes). OpenGaussian, which performs 3D-space segmentation, is surpassed by 7.68 mIoU on ScanNet and 5.43 mIoU on LERF-OVS, with LightSplat completing distillation in 4.1 s versus 30 min. The rendering-based methods LangSplat and LEGaussians are far behind (≤17.42 mIoU on LERF-OVS vs. 47.58), confirming that iterative 2D-rendering-based feature optimization is both slow and semantically lossy compared to LightSplat's direct index injection.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightSplat builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation and the SAM+CLIP pipeline for 2D semantic extraction that underpin recent open-vocabulary 3D understanding methods (LangSplat, LEGaussians, OpenGaussian, Dr.Splat, LUDVIG). It departs fundamentally from this lineage by replacing iterative rendering-guided feature optimization with a single-step indexed injection: instead of storing a 512-dimensional CLIP feature per Gaussian, each Gaussian receives only a 2-byte mask index resolved through a lightweight index-feature mapping. The paper also introduces DL3DV-OVS, an open-vocabulary annotation of DL3DV-10K, extending evaluation beyond the small indoor scenes of LERF-OVS and ScanNet to large, cluttered indoor-outdoor environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50–400× distillation speedup and 64× memory reduction make open-vocabulary 3D understanding viable in real-time and resource-constrained settings (robotics, AR/VR, interactive editing), where prior methods requiring 4–220 minutes of iterative optimization are impractical. The core design insight—that per-Gaussian high-dimensional features are unnecessary and a compact index plus object-level clustering suffices—challenges a prevailing assumption in the 3DGS-semantic literature and could inform other tasks (editing, retrieval, manipulation) that currently store dense per-primitive embeddings. Open questions include whether the single-step clustering degrades on scenes with highly overlapping or visually ambiguous objects, whether the method extends to dynamic or 4D scenes, and how sensitive performance is to SAM mask quality in the absence of any learned refinement.
