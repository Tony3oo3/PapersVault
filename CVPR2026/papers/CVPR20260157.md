## Problem

Existing 3D-GS watermarking methods either select carriers in the image or frequency domain (causing viewpoint-dependent drift) or lack a principled mechanism to jointly optimize robustness and visual fidelity. Two core gaps remain: (i) how to select stable, multi-view-consistent Gaussian carriers from a large heterogeneous primitive set, and (ii) how to embed a distortion-resilient watermark without degrading rendering quality, while providing per-Gaussian interpretability for auditable verification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3D-GS model (a set of Gaussian primitives parameterized by position, scale, rotation, opacity, and spherical-harmonic coefficients) and a binary payload of 32, 48, or 64 bits. It produces a watermarked 3D-GS model in which a subset of Gaussians (carriers) has been modified in selected parameter channels, while the remaining Gaussians (visual compensators) are adjusted to repair local artifacts. Extraction is performed by rendering the watermarked model from multiple views and decoding with a frozen HiDDeN network; the watermark is encoded exclusively in the DWT low-frequency (LL) subband of rendered images, so the output is view-consistent across all camera angles.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages. First, a Trio-Experts module (Geometry, Appearance, Redundancy) extracts representation-native priors directly from Gaussian parameters—scale isotropy, quaternion consistency, opacity gating, high-frequency energy, and neighborhood overlap—producing per-Gaussian evidence packages that separate quality scores from uncertainty. Second, the Safety and Budget-Aware Gate (SBAG) fuses these priors with a single-pass DC+opacity render to compute a symmetric utility score, enforces quantile-based feasibility constraints, and selects top-B carriers under a scene-adaptive bitrate budget; selected parents are then densified into children, with one child routed to the watermark branch and the rest to the visual-compensator branch. Third, a channel-wise Group Mask assigns per-Gaussian, per-channel gradient weights (across five channels: DC color, residual SH, opacity, rotation, scale) so that watermark gradients act only on carriers and visual gradients act only on compensators. During decoupled finetuning, the watermark loss uses Expectation Over Transformation (EOT) over a degradation family (blur, rotation, scaling, crop, noise, JPEG) with BCE on decoded bits plus a DWT-LL regularization term, while the visual loss combines L1, LPIPS, and multi-level DWT high-frequency penalties; the two losses are optimized in separate forward passes to enforce strictly disjoint gradient routing, eliminating the optimization conflict that degrades both fidelity and robustness in coupled training.

## Results

On 32-bit messages averaged over Blender, LLFF, and Mip-NeRF 360, the proposed method achieves 98.46% bit accuracy, 35.98 dB PSNR, 0.982 SSIM, and 0.041 LPIPS, outperforming all three baselines on every metric. At 64-bit messages, it maintains 91.65% bit accuracy and 32.71 dB PSNR, a +0.83 dB PSNR and +1.24% bit-accuracy gain over the strongest baseline (3D-GSW). Under image-space attacks (Table 2), the method retains 91.30% bit accuracy under a combined noise+crop+JPEG attack, versus 90.96% for 3D-GSW and 88.64% for GuardSplat. Under model-level distortions (Table 3), it achieves 98.16% bit accuracy after 20% Gaussian removal, the highest among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: WateRF+3D-GS (a NeRF watermarking method adapted to 3D-GS), GuardSplat (CLIP-guided SH-space watermarking), and 3D-GSW (frequency-domain watermarking with wavelet regularization). The proposed method wins on all four metrics (bit accuracy, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three message lengths (32/48/64 bits). Against the closest competitor 3D-GSW, the gains are +1.24% bit accuracy and +0.83 dB PSNR at 32 bits, narrowing to +0.34% and +0.19 dB at 64 bits. Against GuardSplat, the advantage is larger at 32 bits (+2.88% bit acc, +0.66 dB PSNR) but shrinks at 64 bits (+1.51%, +0.46 dB). WateRF+3D-GS is substantially outperformed, especially at higher capacities where its bit accuracy drops to 74.92% at 64 bits versus 91.65% for the proposed method. Under robustness attacks, the gap over 3D-GSW is smallest (e.g., 91.30% vs 90.96% combined attack), while the gap over GuardSplat is more pronounced (91.30% vs 88.64%), suggesting the decoupled EOT training and 3D-native carrier selection provide the most benefit under compound distortions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D-GS watermarking line initiated by WateRF (NeRF→3D-GS DWT embedding), 3D-GSW (frequency-domain regularization), and GuardSplat (CLIP-guided SH-space embedding), but departs from all three by operating natively in the Gaussian parameter space rather than the rendered image or frequency domain. The Trio-Experts module replaces image/frequency-domain carrier heuristics with geometry, appearance, and redundancy priors computed directly from Gaussian primitives, while the SBAG and channel-wise Group Mask introduce a decoupled optimization that prior methods (which jointly optimize watermark and visual losses) do not employ. The contribution-based pruning is inherited from 3D-GSW, and the EOT adversarial training follows GuardSplat, but the per-Gaussian gradient routing and the explicit separation of watermark carriers from visual compensators represent a structural departure from the coupled finetuning paradigm used by all three baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3D-GS becomes the dominant representation for interactive 3D assets in film, gaming, and autonomous driving, robust and auditable copyright protection is practically urgent; this paper's per-Gaussian attribution (the 'why') offers a level of explainability absent from prior frequency- or CLIP-based methods, enabling verifiable provenance tracking. The decoupled finetuning framework is a reusable design pattern that could transfer to other 3D representations or to dynamic 4D-GS settings. However, the system still depends on a frozen HiDDeN decoder whose capacity and robustness bound overall performance, and the loss-weight sensitivity noted in the limitations section suggests the approach may not generalize cleanly to extreme payload sizes or unseen scene types without retuning.
