## Problem

Existing referring video object segmentation (RVOS) methods segment only the actor object, even when the referring expression explicitly describes an interaction involving distinct actor and target roles (e.g., 'A reaching a hand towards B'). This actor-only focus neglects the target object and the asymmetric relational dynamics between objects, which are essential for understanding many video events. The paper introduces InterRVOS, a task that requires separate segmentation of both actor and target objects from a single interaction expression.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (T frames, uniformly sampled to T' frames for the MLLM) and a natural language referring expression as input. It produces binary segmentation mask sequences (T × H × W) for the actor and, when the expression describes an interaction, a second mask sequence for the target. At inference, the model dynamically decides whether to emit one ([SEG_ACT]) or two ([SEG_ACT] and [SEG_TAR]) special tokens based on whether the expression involves an interaction, so non-interaction expressions yield a single actor mask as in standard RVOS.

## Method

ReVIOSa builds on an InternVL-2.5 MLLM (LoRA-tuned) coupled with a frozen-encoder SAM2 mask decoder. Its two core innovations are: (1) interaction-aware special tokens [SEG_ACT] and [SEG_TAR], whose final-layer hidden states are projected via an MLP into SAM2's prompt embedding space to drive separate mask decodings for actor and target; and (2) an Attention Mask Loss (AML) that applies binary cross-entropy between the vision-attention maps of these tokens (at selected high-vision-attention layers) and the ground-truth object masks resized to patch resolution, forcing each token to attend to its corresponding object region. The total training loss combines pixel-wise cross-entropy and dice losses on both masks, the AML, and a text cross-entropy loss. The model is trained on the newly introduced InterRVOS-127K dataset (127K+ expressions with distinct actor-target mask pairs), constructed via a four-stage automatic annotation pipeline leveraging GPT-4o and LLaMA-70B over VidOR videos.

## Results

On the proposed InterRVOS-127K benchmark, ReVIOSa-4B achieves the best performance across all three evaluation settings: 74.5 J&F on InterRVOS-Actor, 68.3 J&F on InterRVOS-Target, and 64.5 J&F on standard RVOS. The 1B variant also surpasses all baselines, reaching 73.3 J&F on InterRVOS-Actor and 62.0 J&F on RVOS. Notably, only ReVIOSa can produce target segmentation masks; all prior methods are actor-only and thus inapplicable to the InterRVOS-Target setting. In zero-shot transfer to standard RVOS benchmarks (MeViS, Ref-Youtube-VOS, Ref-DAVIS), ReVIOSa-1B trained on InterRVOS-127K achieves the best J&F scores (42.4, 60.3, 66.2 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReVIOSa-4B outperforms the strongest prior baseline Sa2VA-1B by +3.2 J&F on InterRVOS-Actor (74.5 vs. 71.3) and by +7.5 J&F on standard RVOS (64.5 vs. 57.0). Against VideoLISA-3.8B, the gap is even larger: +6.3 J&F on InterRVOS-Actor and +2.8 J&F on RVOS. On the novel InterRVOS-Target setting, no prior method can produce a target mask; zero-shot evaluations (Table 4) show Sa2VA-4B reaches only 24.2 J&F, VISA-7B 19.2, and VideoLISA-3.8B 13.1, while ReVIOSa-4B achieves 68.3, confirming that interaction-aware training is essential. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that the interaction-aware special tokens contribute +2.6 J&F and AML contributes +1.5 J&F over the baseline, with their combination yielding the full +5.0 gain. On zero-shot transfer to MeViS, Ref-Youtube-VOS, and Ref-DAVIS, ReVIOSa-1B trained on InterRVOS-127K surpasses the same architecture trained on ReVOS or Ref-SAV by 1.2–4.7 J&F, demonstrating that the interaction-centric dataset provides stronger general-purpose video understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MLLM+SAM2 paradigm for referring video object segmentation (Sa2VA, VideoLISA, VISA), where a language model produces a [SEG] token that prompts SAM2's mask decoder. It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) replacing the single [SEG] token with role-specific [SEG_ACT] and [SEG_TAR] tokens to handle asymmetric interaction expressions, and (2) introducing an attention mask loss that supervises token-level visual attention toward ground-truth object regions. The dataset construction draws on VidOR's actor-predicate-target annotations but repurposes them through a GPT-4o/LLaMA-70B pipeline to generate natural-language interaction expressions with paired actor-target masks, a formulation absent from all prior RVOS datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot evaluation (Table 4) revealing that even strong MLLM-based RVOS models score only 13–24 J&F on target segmentation provides compelling evidence that interaction directionality is fundamentally unlearned in current video-language models, motivating the new task and dataset. InterRVOS-127K, as the first dataset with explicit actor-target mask pairs at scale, opens a path toward role-aware video understanding beyond single-object tracking. Open questions include whether the automatic annotation pipeline (built on VidOR's closed predicate set) introduces systematic biases, how the approach generalizes to multi-actor or multi-target interactions, and whether the attention mask loss transfers to other MLLM architectures beyond InternVL-2.5.
