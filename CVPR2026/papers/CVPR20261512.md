## Problem

Existing training-free video editing methods rely on an inversion-based paradigm that maps the source video into a latent trajectory before editing. This inversion is inherently inaccurate for video sequences, causing per-frame errors to accumulate into temporal drift, flicker, and motion distortion, while also limiting the magnitude of achievable semantic edits. The paper addresses this by eliminating the inversion step entirely and performing editing as a direct ODE evolution in data space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261512/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video, a target text prompt describing the desired edit, and an optional source text prompt (auto-inferred by an LLM if omitted). It produces an edited video at the same resolution and frame count as the input, evaluated at 41 and 81 frames. The entire pipeline runs training-free on a single NVIDIA H20 141G GPU using the Wan-2.1 1.3B text-to-video backbone with 50 denoising steps and no skip sampling.

## Method

FlowDirector constructs an editing trajectory directly in data space using the FlowEdit unified editing equation, where source and target states at each timestep are derived via Rectified Flow linear interpolation sharing the same noise, so the velocity difference captures only the prompt-induced semantic change. Three training-free flow corrections are applied at every timestep: (1) Direction-Aware Flow Correction performs a token-wise orthogonal decomposition of the editing velocity along the source velocity, suppressing co-directional components and amplifying anti-parallel ones by a factor α to break the 'semantic gravitational pull' of the source; (2) Motion-Appearance Decoupling Flow Correction uses the Tweedie estimator to recover noiseless states, defines motion as the residual after subtracting temporal anchor frames, and minimizes a motion-disagreement energy via a single gradient step that is agnostic to appearance changes; (3) Differential Averaging Guidance averages L_HQ stochastic velocity samples for a high-quality consensus, contrasts it against the K least-similar candidates to extract a noise-drift signal, and steers the trajectory away from high-variance deviations, with a fixed noise reuse in the final denoising steps to suppress texture flicker.

## Results

On 41-frame videos, FlowDirector (1.3B) achieves the best scores across all five metrics: PickScore 21.82%, CLIP-T 34.64×10⁻², CLIP-F 97.34×10⁻², WarpSSIM 78.49×10⁻², and Q_edit 27.19×10⁻⁶. The 14B variant further improves to PickScore 22.61%, CLIP-T 34.95×10⁻², and Q_edit 28.67×10⁻⁶. For 81-frame videos, FlowDirector (1.3B) maintains its lead with CLIP-T 34.63×10⁻², CLIP-F 96.90×10⁻², WarpSSIM 79.10×10⁻², and Q_edit 27.31×10⁻⁶, outperforming the next-best method (FLATTEN) by 1.07 on CLIP-T and 1.20 on Q_edit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowDirector (1.3B) outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric for both 41-frame and 81-frame evaluations. The largest margins appear on text alignment (CLIP-T): FlowDirector scores 34.64 vs. FLATTEN's 33.56 (best baseline) on 41-frame, a gap of over 1.0×10⁻². On the composite Q_edit metric, FlowDirector leads FLATTEN (26.01) by 1.18 and RAVE (25.38) by 1.81. The closest competition is on WarpSSIM (structure preservation), where FateZero achieves 78.37 vs. FlowDirector's 78.49 on 41-frame — a margin of only 0.12×10⁻². On temporal coherence (CLIP-F), FlowDirector's 97.34 substantially exceeds VideoDirector's 95.48, the strongest baseline on this metric. Notably, FateZero and VideoDirector cannot be evaluated at 81 frames due to memory constraints, while FlowDirector handles both lengths on a single H20 GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowDirector extends the inversion-free editing paradigm from FlowEdit (image domain) to video by modeling the edit as a direct ODE evolution in data space, bypassing the latent-space inversion step that dominates prior video editing methods (FateZero, TokenFlow, FLATTEN, RAVE, VideoDirector). The Rectified Flow linear-path formulation provides the interpolation mechanism for constructing source and target intermediate states. The three correction strategies (DA-FC, MAD-FC, DAG) are novel contributions that address video-specific challenges—semantic gravitational pull, motion-appearance entanglement, and high-variance velocity estimates—that do not arise in the image setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the inversion step, FlowDirector removes a primary source of temporal drift and appearance degradation that plagues all prior training-free video editing methods, offering a cleaner conceptual framework for the field. However, the approach still requires multiple forward passes per timestep (DAG uses 3 stochastic samples), limiting practical speed, and evaluation is confined to 150 pairs at 41/81 frames on a 1.3B model. Open questions include scalability to longer videos, more complex multi-object edits, and whether the ODE formulation generalizes beyond rectified-flow-based T2V models to other generative architectures.
