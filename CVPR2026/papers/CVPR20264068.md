## Problem

Continual video-language learning lacks an explicit specification of which model components should remain stable across tasks and where plasticity should be concentrated. Existing approaches either specialize via prompt/adapter banks with static routing or preserve geometry through distillation, leaving the stability-plasticity trade-off implicit and capacity budgeted heuristically rather than driven by online conflict signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264068/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video as a sequence of T frames and a free-form text query, producing one of three output types selected per sample: an open-form generated answer, a temporal span (discrete start/end frame indices with tIoU supervision), or an ordered step sequence. The video is first compressed into a fixed-length sequence of continuous affordance tokens (one per frame) that serve as cross-attention keys/values, while the query is tokenized by the LLM's native tokenizer and pooled into a single routing vector, so the LLM never sees raw frame features directly.

## Method

AFD decomposes the model into a shared affordance head and a plastic LLM scheduler with a strict gradient separation. The affordance head maps each frame through a small network to a sparse Top-L mixture over a fixed affordance vocabulary, yielding slowly varying continuous tokens that are linearly projected into the LLM hidden space; its parameters are stabilized by a combined weak ASR-alignment loss and KL consistency against a frozen teacher from the previous task. The LLM scheduler injects per-layer LoRA experts whose mixture weights are computed by a query-conditioned router at every adapterized layer, and the rank of each expert grows discretely only when a clamped negative-cosine gradient conflict metric exceeds a threshold, capping capacity growth. Training uses a three-term objective (task loss + affordance stability + question-only replay distillation) where task and replay gradients update only the routed adapters while the stability loss updates only the affordance head, and replay stores past questions rather than videos for privacy and memory efficiency.

## Results

On domain-incremental VideoQA across six datasets (iVQA → MSVD → MSRVTT → LSMDC → ANet → TGIF), AFD achieves 51.6% average top-1 accuracy with only −1.8% forgetting, surpassing the strongest continual baseline DAM (50.2%, −2.3%) by +1.4 points and approaching the multitask upper bound (52.6%). On ViLCo-Bench (Ego4D), AFD attains R@1@0.5 of 29.6 (MQ) and 20.7 (NLQ) and stAP@0.25 of 18.4 (VQ), leading all competing methods by 1.9–2.5 points. On time-incremental iVQA, AFD reaches 39.5% average accuracy with −1.6% forgetting, again the best among all continual baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFD outperforms all 18 compared baselines on every primary metric. On domain-incremental VideoQA, AFD beats the closest competitor DAM [6] by +1.4 avg accuracy (51.6 vs 50.2) and reduces forgetting from −2.3 to −1.8; it also surpasses Bisecle [41] (+2.2 avg) and ColPro [2] (+6.1 avg). On ViLCo-Bench, AFD leads DAM by +2.5 R@1 on MQ, +3.8 R@1 on NLQ, and +1.9 stAP on VQ, and outperforms the original ViLCo [43] baseline by 8.4 R@1 on MQ. On time-incremental iVQA, AFD achieves 39.5% avg with −1.6% forgetting versus DAM's 38.1%/−2.2% and Bisecle's 37.6%/−2.5%. In non-continual settings, AFD edges out VQAGuider [4] on CVQA EM (62.8 vs 61.3) and 11-VideoQA (67.4 vs 66.5), and surpasses LongVU [38] on both VideoMME (61.7 vs 61.2) and MLVU (57.9 vs 57.3). Classical regularization baselines (EWC, LwF) suffer severe forgetting (−10.7 to −10.9) and are far behind, while prompt-based methods (L2P, CODA-Prompt, S-Prompts) remain 8–12 points below AFD in average accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

AFD sits at the intersection of continual video-language learning (ViLCo-Bench, ColPro, DAM, Bisecle) and parameter-efficient routing (L2P, CODA-Prompt, MoE adapters), but departs from both by making the stability–plasticity split explicit and architectural rather than implicit. Prior prompt/adapter methods treat the entire backbone as a uniform adaptation target with static or task-indexed routing, while geometry-preserving CL methods protect representations globally without specifying which structures should remain invariant. AFD instead introduces a slowly varying affordance head as a shared, time-aligned substrate (stabilized via weak ASR alignment and teacher KL) and concentrates all task-specific plasticity in a query-routed, conflict-triggered LoRA scheduler, adopting the question-only replay mechanism from Marouf et al. [34] for privacy-friendly rehearsal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a cleaner conceptual and architectural template for continual video-language learning: practitioners can now explicitly identify which parameters should be frozen (affordance substrate) and which should adapt (routed adapters), making the stability–plasticity trade-off diagnosable rather than incidental. The consistent SOTA gains with the lowest forgetting across domain-incremental, time-incremental, and query-incremental protocols suggest the affordance-stabilization hypothesis is practically useful. Open questions include whether the fixed affordance vocabulary generalizes to truly open-vocabulary or embodied settings, whether online affordance discovery (mentioned as future work) can replace the current supervised head, and whether the discrete rank-growth mechanism scales gracefully to very long task streams without capacity explosion.
