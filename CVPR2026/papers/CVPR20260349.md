## Problem

Electromagnetic inverse scattering (EISP) is inherently ill-posed and nonlinear, and existing methods—whether traditional numerical solvers, BP-initialized neural networks, or case-specific optimization approaches like Img-Interiors—fail under sparse transmitter settings, particularly the extreme single-transmitter case where measured data is insufficient to capture adequate physical information for stable inversion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260349/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a real-valued scattered field vector of dimension 2·N·N_r (concatenating real and imaginary parts from N transmitters and N_r receivers) together with a 2D or 3D spatial query coordinate x_i. The spatial coordinate is mapped through a Fourier positional encoding γ(x) = [sin(x), cos(x), …, sin(2^(Ω−1)x), cos(2^(Ω−1)x)] before being fed to the network. The output is a scalar relative permittivity value at the queried location; the full M²-grid permittivity distribution is reconstructed by evaluating the MLP at every grid point.

## Method

The core architecture is a single MLP F_θ that maps (E^s, γ(x_i)) → ε̂_r(x_i), trained end-to-end with a plain MSE loss against ground-truth permittivity. The key insight is that by learning this mapping directly from scattered fields to permittivity across diverse training scenarios, the network implicitly encodes data distribution priors that compensate for the information deficit caused by sparse measurements, thereby resolving the ill-posedness without relying on any traditional numerical solver (e.g., BP) as an initialization or intermediate step. This design eliminates the need for case-specific optimization, enabling fast feed-forward inference (over 70,000× speed-up over Img-Interiors) while maintaining robustness to transmitter sparsity and sensor noise up to 30%. The same architecture extends naturally to 3D by increasing the spatial coordinate dimension from 2 to 3.

## Results

Under the single-transmitter setting (N=1, 5% noise), the proposed method achieves MSE 0.085 / SSIM 0.921 / PSNR 26.09 on MNIST, MSE 0.031 / SSIM 0.931 / PSNR 33.18 on Circular, and MSE 0.128 / SSIM 0.908 / PSNR 24.19 on the real-world IF dataset, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. Under the standard multi-transmitter setting (N=16, 5% noise), it reaches MSE 0.039 / PSNR 32.11 on MNIST and MSE 0.020 / PSNR 36.92 on Circular. On 3D MNIST with a single transmitter, the method attains MSE 0.120 and IoU 0.769, compared to Img-Interiors' MSE 0.372 and IoU 0.094.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and noise level tested. Traditional methods (BP, 2-fold SOM, Gs SOM) degrade severely under N=1: BP drops from PSNR 16.43 to 15.40 on MNIST, while 2-fold SOM collapses to PSNR 12.49 (MSE 0.432), indicating complete failure to reconstruct the scatterer. CNN-based two-stage methods (BPS, PGAN, Physics-Net, Two-Step) all rely on BP initialization, which becomes unreliable with a single transmitter; consequently they 'hallucinate' digit shapes on MNIST (e.g., BPS MSE 0.189, PGAN MSE 0.133) and produce structurally wrong reconstructions. Img-Interiors, the most recent SOTA, suffers from intrinsic ambiguity under N=1: its reconstructed scatterer matches the measured field but deviates substantially from ground truth (MSE 0.305 on MNIST, 0.096 on Circular), and on 3D MNIST it achieves only IoU 0.094 versus the proposed method's 0.769. The proposed method's advantage is most pronounced in the single-transmitter regime, where it achieves 2–4× lower MSE than the best competing method on every dataset, confirming that data distribution priors effectively compensate for the severe information deficit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in EISP: (1) two-stage methods (BPS, Physics-Net, PGAN) that rely on BP initialization followed by CNN refinement, and (2) the recent implicit-function approach Img-Interiors (ECCV 2024) that performs case-by-case optimization with physics-informed losses. Rather than building on either, the authors repurpose the standard MLP-plus-positional-encoding architecture (common in implicit neural representations) as a fully feed-forward inverse solver, trained end-to-end with a simple MSE loss. The key conceptual departure is treating data distribution priors—learned implicitly through supervised training on diverse scattering scenarios—as the mechanism to resolve the ill-posedness, rather than relying on explicit physical regularization or iterative forward simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates for the first time that high-quality permittivity reconstruction is achievable from a single transmitter, a setting where all prior methods (including the recent SOTA Img-Interiors) fail, and achieves a >70,000× inference speedup by replacing per-case optimization with feed-forward prediction. This has direct practical implications for cost-effective electromagnetic imaging in constrained deployment environments. However, the authors acknowledge that the method cannot handle varying transmitter/receiver geometries and struggles with thin structures, leaving open the question of whether the approach generalizes to the heterogeneous sensor layouts encountered in real-world medical or industrial imaging. Extending the framework to accommodate flexible sensor configurations and fine-grained structure recovery remains an important direction.
