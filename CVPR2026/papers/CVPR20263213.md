## Problem

Existing language-guided segmentation models for remote sensing images can only parse simple, single-target, explicit commands and fail on complex geospatial scenarios involving hierarchical granularity (semantic to part-level), multi-target instructions, implicit reasoning, and varied linguistic styles. Prior datasets oversimplify these dimensions, producing models that are brittle in real-world applications such as disaster response and environmental monitoring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263213/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a remote sensing RGB image paired with a free-form textual instruction that may be short or long, explicit or implicit, and reference one or multiple targets. It produces a textual response containing one or more [SEG] tokens (each serving as a segmentation query) alongside a corresponding set of pixel-level masks. The system handles 122 object categories across semantic, instance, and part-level granularities, with mask sizes ranging from a few pixels to over 80% of the image area.

## Method

SegEarth-R2 builds on the Mipha-3B MLLM with a Swin-B hierarchical visual encoder feeding into Mask2Former-style Pixel and Transformer Decoders, and introduces two key mechanisms. First, a spatial attention supervision loss (L_S) directly supervises the [SEG] token's attention maps to image patch tokens across all transformer layers and heads, enforcing maximal foreground-background separation at intermediate layers to improve localization of small and part-level objects. Second, a flexible segmentation query mechanism lets the MLLM dynamically emit multiple [SEG] tokens for multi-target instructions, each independently decoded into a mask, eliminating the redundant 'propose-then-select' matching of prior methods. Training uses a unified loss combining autoregressive cross-entropy, binary cross-entropy, DICE loss, and the spatial attention term (λ_S=0.01), with LoRA (rank 8) on the LLM, frozen visual encoders, and fully fine-tuned decoders.

## Results

On the proposed LaSeRS benchmark, SegEarth-R2-3B achieves an average gIoU/cIoU of 57.2/67.9, outperforming the next-best model GeoPixel-8B (50.4/55.2) by 6.8 gIoU points and 12.7 cIoU points. The advantage is most pronounced at the part-level granularity (64.8 gIoU vs. 43.9 for GeoPixel, a 20.9-point margin) and on explicit instructions (78.4 gIoU). On established benchmarks, SegEarth-R2 sets new SOTA on RRSIS-D (68.8 gIoU), RefSegRS (84.4 gIoU), RISBench (69.8 gIoU), and EarthReason (70.9 avg gIoU), all with only 3B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegEarth-R2-3B decisively outperforms all 13 baselines on the LaSeRS benchmark average (57.2 gIoU vs. 50.4 for GeoPixel-8B, the strongest competitor). The gap is largest at part-level granularity, where SegEarth-R2 scores 64.8 gIoU versus 43.9 for GeoPixel and 32.6 for GLaMM-ft-7B, a margin of 20+ points over the runner-up. On multi-target tasks, GeoPixel-8B (49.2 gIoU) actually surpasses SegEarth-R2 (38.3 gIoU), which the authors attribute to the 3B model's limited capacity for complex multi-target reasoning. On the three referring segmentation benchmarks (Table 3), SegEarth-R2 beats GeoPixel on RRSIS-D (68.8 vs. 68.0 gIoU), surpasses SegEarth-R1 on RefSegRS (84.4 vs. 82.2), and achieves the best RISBench score (69.8 gIoU) with no prior method reporting results there. On EarthReason (Table 4), SegEarth-R2 edges out Text4Seg++ (70.9 vs. 70.1 avg gIoU) and RemoteReasoner (69.2), confirming its advantage on implicit reasoning segmentation. Notably, SegEarth-R2 achieves all these results with only 3B parameters, outperforming 7B and 13B models (LISA, PixelLM, M2A) by 10–20 gIoU points on LaSeRS.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegEarth-R2 extends the SegEarth-R1 line of work by replacing its single-target 'instruction-as-query' design with a flexible [SEG]-token query mechanism borrowed from LISA, enabling multi-target grounding from a single instruction. The segmentation head reuses the Mask2Former Pixel Decoder and Transformer Decoder atop a Swin-B hierarchical encoder, a combination validated against SAM/SAM2+ViT-H alternatives. The paper's key methodological departure is the spatial attention supervision loss, which directly constrains the MLLM's internal attention maps to separate foreground from background — a signal that prior RS segmentation MLLMs (GeoPixel, SegEarth-R1, LISAt) did not exploit. On the data side, LaSeRS generalizes the single-target, explicit-query formulation of RRSIS-D, RefSegRS, and EarthReason into a four-dimensional benchmark covering hierarchical granularity, target multiplicity, implicit reasoning, and linguistic variability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaSeRS fills a clear gap: no prior RS segmentation dataset simultaneously covers part-level granularity, multi-target instructions, implicit reasoning, and long-form descriptions, so models trained on existing benchmarks remain brittle in realistic geospatial scenarios. The 3B-parameter model outperforming 7B/13B baselines suggests that architecture-specific inductive biases (hierarchical encoder, attention supervision) can substitute for raw model scale in RS, a practically important finding for deployment-constrained settings. However, the model still trails GeoPixel on multi-target tasks, and the spatial attention supervision yields only modest gains (≤0.5 gIoU) on benchmarks with limited scale variation, leaving open whether the mechanism generalizes beyond RS's characteristic small-object regime. Broader questions remain about scaling the four-dimensional evaluation to video or multi-sensor RS and whether the semi-automated annotation pipeline can be extended to open-vocabulary settings.
