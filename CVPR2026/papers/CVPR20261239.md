## Problem

Current digital twins from 3D reconstruction remain static, supporting only navigation and view synthesis without embodied interactivity. Existing video world models treat camera motion or imprecise text prompts as action inputs, failing to capture dexterous hand manipulation—the primary driver of scene dynamics in everyday settings. This paper asks: can a video diffusion model predict the residual visual changes induced by specific hand actions in a known static 3D scene while preserving all unaltered regions?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261239/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three conditioning signals: (1) a static-scene video rendered along a specified egocentric camera trajectory (providing spatial consistency), (2) an egocentric hand-mesh rendering video from SMPL-X/MANO (encoding geometry, articulation, and contact cues), and (3) a text prompt for semantic guidance. All video conditions are encoded by a pretrained video VAE and concatenated with the noisy latent along the channel dimension before processing by the DiT. The output is a temporally coherent egocentric interaction video at the same resolution and frame count as the input conditions, depicting physically plausible scene changes (grasping, opening, moving objects) driven by the hand actions.

## Method

DWM formulates generation as learning the residual visual change ΔV induced by hand manipulation, decomposing the target as V = Π(S₀; C) + ΔV. This motivates initializing from a pretrained video inpainting diffusion model (CogVideoX-Fun), which under a full mask behaves as a near-identity operator with a strong generative prior—preserving static appearance and camera motion while the model learns to synthesize only interaction-induced dynamics. The architecture is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) operating in the latent space of a pretrained video VAE, with static-scene and hand-mesh latents concatenated as channel-wise conditions. Training uses the standard latent diffusion noise-prediction loss on a hybrid dataset: synthetic TRUMANS sequences provide precisely aligned egocentric triplets (interaction video, static-scene video, hand-mesh video) under identical camera trajectories, while fixed-camera TASTE-Rob real-world videos contribute diverse physical dynamics (fluids, deformation) by repeating the first frame as the static condition. The hand-mesh rendering conditioning is shown to outperform both parameter-based AdaLN modulation and binary hand-mask conditioning, as rendered meshes provide explicit articulation and pixel-aligned contact geometry.

## Results

DWM achieves the best performance across all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DreamSim) in all three evaluation settings. On synthetic dynamic-camera data, DWM reaches PSNR 25.03 and DreamSim 0.086, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline (CVX-Fun Fine-tuned: PSNR 20.54, DreamSim 0.175). On real-world static-camera data, DWM attains PSNR 21.55 and DreamSim 0.057, and on real-world dynamic-camera data (completely unseen during training) it achieves PSNR 21.65 and DreamSim 0.225, demonstrating strong generalization to novel scenes and interaction types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DWM outperforms all three baselines on every metric in every setting where they are comparable. Against CVX SDEdit (text-guided SDE denoising of static videos), DWM gains roughly 5–6 dB in PSNR and halves the DreamSim distance across all settings, indicating far more physically plausible interactions. Against CVX-Fun Fine-tuned (inpainting model fine-tuned without hand-mesh conditioning), DWM improves PSNR by ~4.5 dB on synthetic data and ~2.6 dB on real-world static data, with DreamSim dropping from 0.175 to 0.086 (synthetic) and 0.089 to 0.057 (static), showing that explicit hand-mesh conditioning is critical for accurate manipulation dynamics. InterDyn, evaluated only on the static-camera setting, achieves a competitive LPIPS of 0.240 but lags DWM in PSNR (19.33 vs. 21.55) and DreamSim (0.135 vs. 0.057), confirming that DWM's scene-conditioned formulation better preserves background consistency while generating interaction dynamics. Notably, on the real-world dynamic-camera setting—where no training data exists—DWM still leads CVX-Fun Fine-tuned by 3.5 dB in PSNR and 0.10 in DreamSim, underscoring the generalization benefit of the hybrid training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DWM departs from prior navigation world models (which treat camera motion as the sole action) and whole-body conditioned egocentric video prediction (which entangles scene synthesis with dynamics) by explicitly conditioning on a rendered static 3D scene and predicting only the residual visual changes induced by dexterous hand actions. Methodologically, it repurposes a pretrained video inpainting diffusion model (CogVideoX-Fun) as an identity-mapping initialization, so the diffusion process learns to generate only manipulation-induced deltas rather than reconstructing the full scene. The hybrid training strategy—combining synthetic TRUMANS sequences for aligned supervision with fixed-camera TASTE-Rob videos for physical realism—addresses the absence of any existing dataset providing paired static-scene and interaction videos under shared egocentric trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DWM establishes a concrete formulation for interactive digital twins: given a reconstructed 3D scene and a hand motion sequence, one can simulate physically plausible human-scene interactions without explicit articulation modeling or physics engines. The action-evaluation capability (simulating candidate actions and scoring against a goal) opens a path toward simulation-based planning for embodied agents. Key limitations include the dependence on accurate 3D scene reconstruction, hand-mesh estimation, and camera tracking, and the current evaluation is limited to 144 samples. Open questions include scaling to longer-horizon interactions, real-time inference, and whether the residual-dynamics formulation generalizes to multi-agent or non-human (robotic) action spaces.
