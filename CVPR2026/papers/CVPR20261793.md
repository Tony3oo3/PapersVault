## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving systems are split into two paradigms: Vision-Action (VA) models that produce precise 3D perception and prediction but lack natural language interaction, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that enable user-friendly dialogue but cannot reliably generate fine-grained 3D occupancy or flow outputs. The paper asks whether a single unified framework can simultaneously deliver the spatial precision of VA models and the interpretability of VLA models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261793/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DrivePI consumes synchronized multi-view camera images (6 views), LiDAR point clouds (which carry temporal information on nuScenes), and free-form language instructions. The vision encoder produces a latent BEV feature map at >100×100 spatial resolution, which is patchified into K×K patches before being projected into the language space. The model produces four types of outputs in parallel: autoregressive text (scene descriptions, QA answers), a 3D occupancy grid, a pixel-level occupancy flow (velocity) field, and a future ego-vehicle trajectory generated by a diffusion head.

## Method

DrivePI builds on a 0.5B Qwen2.5 backbone and introduces a spatial projector that uses cross-attention (with pooled features as queries and patch features as keys/values, following HiRes-LLaVA) to convert high-resolution BEV features into vision tokens while preserving fine-grained spatial detail. Four specialized heads—text, 3D occupancy, occupancy flow, and action diffusion—are attached to the MLLM's hidden states and trained jointly with equal loss weights in a two-stage procedure (first aligning the projector, then fine-tuning all components with only the vision encoder frozen). A multi-stage data engine leverages InternVL3-78B to generate scene captions, text-occupancy QA pairs, text-flow QA pairs, and planning QA pairs (over 1M total), enabling the model to reason about 4D spatial-temporal dynamics in language. The key architectural insight is that the same MLLM hidden states feed both the autoregressive text head and the fine-grained vision heads, so linguistic understanding and geometric perception are co-optimized rather than treated as separate pipelines.

## Results

On the OpenOcc benchmark, DrivePI (0.5B MLLM backbone) achieves 49.3% RayIoU for 3D occupancy and 0.509 mAVE for occupancy flow, surpassing the previous best specialized method ALOcc-Flow-3D by 7.4 RayIoU and 6.3% OccScore. On nuScenes planning, DrivePI with ego status achieves 0.40 m L2 error and 0.11% collision rate, reducing collision rate by 70% over ORION (0.37%→0.11%) and beating OpenDriveVLA-7B on both L2 (0.40 vs 0.66) and collision (0.11% vs 0.25%). On nuScenes-QA text understanding, DrivePI reaches 60.7% overall accuracy, outperforming the 7B-parameter OpenDriveVLA by 2.5%. On the WOD-E2E long-tail benchmark, DrivePI achieves 7.52 RFS (vision head), exceeding OpenEMMA's 6.32 RFS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrivePI decisively wins on 3D occupancy: it outperforms FB-OCC by 10.3 RayIoU (49.3 vs 39.0) and the prior SOTA ALOcc-Flow-3D by 7.4 RayIoU (49.3 vs 41.9), establishing a new state-of-the-art with only a 0.5B backbone. On occupancy flow, DrivePI (0.509 mAVE) beats FB-OCC (0.591) and ALOcc-Flow-3D (0.556) but trails CascadeFlow (0.470) and F-Occ (0.491), so it is not the best on this single metric. For planning on nuScenes, DrivePI with ego status (0.40 m L2, 0.11% Col) beats OpenDriveVLA-7B on both metrics (0.66 m, 0.25%) and reduces ORION's collision rate by 70% (0.37%→0.11%), though it slightly trails VAD-with-ego on L2 (0.40 vs 0.37) and ORION on L2 (0.40 vs 0.34). Without ego status, DrivePI (0.49 m) achieves 32% lower L2 than VAD (0.72 m) but has a higher collision rate (0.38% vs 0.22%). On nuScenes-QA, DrivePI (60.7% Acc.) outperforms OpenDriveVLA-7B (58.2%) by 2.5 points and all other baselines including BEVDet+BUTD (57.0%). On WOD-E2E, DrivePI-vision (7.52 RFS) and DrivePI-text (7.45 RFS) both exceed OpenEMMA (6.32 RFS), demonstrating stronger generalization to long-tail scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

DrivePI sits at the intersection of two established but previously separate paradigms in end-to-end autonomous driving: modular Vision-Action (VA) models (UniAD, VAD, FusionAD) that produce precise 3D perception and planning outputs, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (OpenDriveVLA, ORION, EMMA) that add natural-language interaction via MLLMs but lack fine-grained geometric outputs. The paper departs from both by introducing LiDAR point clouds as an input modality (unlike camera-only VLA methods) and by attaching specialized fine-grained decoding heads (3D occupancy, occupancy flow, action diffusion) directly to a compact 0.5B Qwen2.5 backbone, while also building on the cross-attention spatial projector design from HiRes-LLaVA [14] to preserve high-resolution BEV spatial detail. A data engine leveraging InternVL3-78B generates over 1M QA pairs to train the model on 4D spatial reasoning, extending the typical VQA training recipe used by prior driving MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 0.5B unified model matches or exceeds both 7B VLA models on text understanding and specialized VA models on 3D occupancy/flow is practically significant for deployment-constrained settings, suggesting that fine-grained spatial heads can compensate for limited language-model capacity. The paper's main limitation is the use of simple uniform loss weighting (all λ=1) across four heterogeneous tasks, which the authors acknowledge may not yield optimal trade-offs; more sophisticated multi-task balancing or task-specific scheduling remains open. Generalization beyond nuScenes is only lightly probed (WOD-E2E with 20% data), and the reliance on LiDAR as a required input limits applicability to camera-only platforms. Whether the data-engine approach for generating 4D spatial QA pairs transfers to other driving datasets or sensor configurations is an important open question.
