## Problem

The Forward-Forward (FF) algorithm trains each layer independently via local goodness objectives, but suffers from two core limitations: (1) its greedy layer-wise learning lacks hierarchical coordination, forcing shallow layers to discriminate among all fine-grained classes directly, and (2) the goodness decoupling problem, where removing activation magnitude to prevent cross-layer leakage leaves the remaining features semantically unconstrained and ambiguous. Existing fixes either leak goodness across layers (causing overfitting) or apply contrastive objectives to raw activations rather than the decoupled features, leaving the semantic collapse unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 28×28 for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST) and produces a single class label. At inference, the Signal Integrating and Pruning Module selects an optimal layer interval on a validation set, averages the per-class goodness scores across that interval, and takes argmax to yield the prediction. The architecture uses Channel-Wise Convolution (CW-Conv) layers where the channel dimension is partitioned into K class-specific subsets, so the number of channels scales with the number of classes.

## Method

HCL-FF builds on the DeeperForward FF backbone (17-layer CW-Conv CNN with GroupNorm-based goodness decoupling) and introduces two per-layer objectives. First, a Hierarchical Learning strategy assigns coarse-to-fine supervision: a data-driven class hierarchy (built via hierarchical agglomerative clustering of pre-trained classifier prototypes) defines super-class groupings at each depth, and the HierCwC loss computes softmax cross-entropy over super-class-averaged goodness rather than per-class goodness, reducing early-layer complexity. Second, a supervised contrastive loss is applied to the goodness-decoupled features (after GroupNorm, global-average-pooled, and projected through a linear head), explicitly constraining their relational geometry to preserve semantic structure that magnitude removal would otherwise destroy. The total per-layer loss is HierCwC + λ·Con (λ=1), and all layers are trained independently without gradient propagation across depth, preserving FF's layer-wise parallelism and biological plausibility.

## Results

HCL-FF achieves 91.68% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 70.09% on CIFAR-100, 99.65% on MNIST, 93.87% on Fashion-MNIST, and 48.46% on Tiny-ImageNet, setting new state-of-the-art among all FF-based methods. Compared to the strongest prior FF baseline DeeperForward, HCL-FF delivers gains of +5.46% (CIFAR-10), +17.00% (CIFAR-100), and +12.51% (Tiny-ImageNet). Notably, HCL-FF surpasses the standard BP-trained ResNet-20 (91.25% on CIFAR-10, 67.20% on CIFAR-100) while remaining fully layer-wise trained without backpropagation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCL-FF outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every dataset. Against the strongest prior FF method DeeperForward [52], HCL-FF gains +5.46% on CIFAR-10 (91.68 vs. 86.22), +17.00% on CIFAR-100 (70.09 vs. 53.09), and +12.51% on Tiny-ImageNet (48.46 vs. 35.95). HCL-FF also surpasses the standard BP-trained ResNet-20 (91.25% CIFAR-10, 67.20% CIFAR-100) while using no backpropagation, though it remains below the wider ResNet-20-Wide BP variant (93.98% CIFAR-10, 76.72% CIFAR-100) and the BP ResNet on Tiny-ImageNet (64.40%). Among other non-BP methods, HCL-FF exceeds SigProp (91.66% CIFAR-10, 65.7% CIFAR-100) and DTP (89.38% CIFAR-10) on CIFAR-10, and matches or slightly exceeds rec-LRA (93.88% CIFAR-10) on that dataset. On MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, HCL-FF achieves 99.65% and 93.87%, marginally exceeding both BP ResNet-20 and DeeperForward, confirming consistent gains even on simpler benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

HCL-FF builds directly on the DeeperForward architecture (CW-Conv layers, mean-goodness formulation, GroupNorm-based goodness decoupling, and the Signal Integrating and Pruning Module) and the CwComp channel-partitioning scheme, while remaining rooted in Hinton's original Forward-Forward layer-wise goodness objective. It departs from prior FF variants in two specific ways: (1) it introduces a coarse-to-fine hierarchical curriculum where shallow layers receive super-class supervision derived from data-driven agglomerative clustering of class prototypes, and (2) it applies supervised contrastive learning specifically to the goodness-decoupled features rather than to raw activations as in earlier contrastive FF methods (SCFF, Marginal Contrastive Loss). This positioning resolves what the authors term the 'decoupling dilemma'—the tension between strict goodness removal (needed to prevent cross-layer leakage) and the resulting semantic collapse of the decoupled representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled resolution of the decoupling dilemma in FF learning, demonstrated by the striking linear-probe result where HCL-FF retains nearly identical accuracy before and after normalization (91.93/91.91 on CIFAR-10) while DeeperForward drops from 84.46 to 76.93. The large and consistent gains across five benchmarks (+5.46% CIFAR-10, +17% CIFAR-100, +12.51% Tiny-ImageNet) suggest that hierarchical coordination and contrastive grounding are broadly applicable to FF-style training. However, the method still inherits FF's slower convergence, the CwC loss's channel-count scaling with the number of classes, and has not been validated on ImageNet-scale data, leaving open whether the approach scales to very large label spaces and higher-resolution settings.
