## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering but struggles with specular reflections because its low-order spherical harmonics cannot capture high-frequency view-dependent highlights, causing geometric degradation through fictitious primitives. Existing polarization-assisted NeRF methods offer physical priors for reflection separation but suffer from slow training, mask dependencies, and limited scalability. No prior work has embedded polarization priors directly into the 3DGS optimization loop to jointly resolve reflection-geometry entanglement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view 4-channel polarization images (captured at 0°, 45°, 90°, 135° with a polarization camera) alongside corresponding RGB ground-truth images; polarization cues are used exclusively as training supervision and are not required at inference time. The output is a 3DGS scene rendered in real-time (43–105 FPS) that produces novel-view images, per-pixel surface normal maps, and decomposed specular and diffuse reflection layers. A fixed refractive index of 1.5 is assumed for non-conductive materials during the polarization-to-normal inversion.

## Method

PolarGuide-GSDR establishes a bidirectional iterative optimization between polarization physics and 3DGS geometry. First, a polarization physical model (Nayar model + Mueller matrices) decomposes each polarization image into specular and diffuse components and estimates initial surface normals from DoLP and AoLP. Second, a DoLP-thresholded ambiguity-correction strategy uses coarse 3DGS geometric priors to select among four candidate normals (resolving π and π/2 ambiguities), while low-DoLP regions are masked out to suppress noise. Third, the disambiguated normals, separated specular/diffuse images, and RGB ground truth jointly supervise a 3DGS-DR-based deferred reflection pipeline through four loss terms (RGB reconstruction, specular reflection, diffuse reflection, and normal consistency). As 3DGS geometry improves during training, it in turn enables more accurate polarization disambiguation, creating a self-correcting closed loop that converges even from initially erroneous normal estimates.

## Results

PolarGuide-GSDR achieves the highest PSNR across all five real-world datasets, with gains of ~1 dB over the strongest baseline on Gnome (22.54 vs. 21.65), Automotive and Glass (19.29 vs. 18.31), and Black Ceramic Cup (26.67 vs. 26.48), and ~0.5 dB on Gundam (23.32 vs. 22.93) and Stagnant Water (23.51 vs. 23.01). It also attains the best SSIM on Gnome (0.890), Automotive and Glass (0.774), and Stagnant Water (0.762), and the lowest LPIPS on Gnome (0.216) and Automotive and Glass (0.339). Rendering speed remains real-time (43–105 FPS), comparable to 3DGS-DR and substantially faster than Ref-GS on most scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolarGuide-GSDR outperforms all four baselines on PSNR across every dataset, with the largest margins on reflective-heavy scenes: +0.89 dB over Ref-GS on Gnome, +0.98 dB over 3DGS-DR on Automotive and Glass, and +0.19 dB over Ref-GS on Black Ceramic Cup. Against 3DGS-DR (the closest Gaussian-based competitor), the method gains 0.41–1.41 dB in PSNR while maintaining comparable FPS (43–105 vs. 53–118). Against Ref-GS, PolarGuide-GSDR wins on PSNR in all five scenes and on SSIM in three (Gnome, Automotive & Glass, Stagnant Water), though Ref-GS edges ahead on SSIM for Black Ceramic Cup (0.902 vs. 0.894) and LPIPS (0.186 vs. 0.191). GNeRP, the polarization-NeRF baseline, is consistently the weakest method (PSNR 13–18, FPS ≈ 0.01), confirming the efficiency advantage of the 3DGS formulation. On rendering speed, PolarGuide-GSDR is slower than vanilla 3DGS (by design, due to the deferred reflection pass) but faster than Ref-GS on four of five datasets, and roughly on par with 3DGS-DR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: polarization-aware NeRF methods (PANDORA, GNeRP, NeISF, PISR) that leverage polarization for inverse rendering but suffer from slow training and mask dependencies, and 3DGS-based reflection methods (3DGS-DR, Ref-GS) that achieve real-time rendering but lack physical priors for specular-diffuse separation. The key departure is embedding polarization priors directly into the 3DGS optimization loop rather than treating them as external supervision for a neural field, establishing a bidirectional iterative cycle where 3DGS geometry disambiguates polarization normals and refined polarization normals in turn guide 3DGS geometry and SH coefficients. The deferred reflection framework is inherited from 3DGS-DR, while the polarization-based reflection separation follows the Nayar model and Cui et al.'s polarimetric multi-view stereo formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to integrate polarization priors into 3DGS optimization, offering a practical path toward physically grounded real-time rendering of reflective scenes without the mask dependencies and training costs of polarization-NeRF. The bidirectional iterative disambiguation strategy is a useful training paradigm that could generalize to other sensor-fusion settings where two modalities have complementary ambiguities. Limitations include the fixed refractive index assumption (1.5), the requirement for a polarization camera, and the modest PSNR gains (~0.5–1 dB) that suggest the method's value is primarily in reflection-region fidelity rather than global image quality. Open questions include scalability to very large outdoor scenes, robustness to non-Lambertian materials, and whether the iterative loop converges reliably under severe initialization errors.
