## Problem

Standard cross-entropy training for radiology report generation treats all token positions uniformly and only increases the likelihood of the ground-truth token, without directly suppressing the model's current wrong choice. This leaves models unable to identify or prioritize corrections at clinically critical spans—flipped findings, stray negations, or local semantic contradictions—because no mechanism routes stronger gradients to the tokens that are actually wrong.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261859/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a chest X-ray image (224×224) and a fixed textual prompt as input, producing a free-form radiology report as output. During training, the model first generates a draft report via greedy/beam decoding, which is then compared against the ground-truth reference by a frozen LLaMA3-3B referee to produce sparse <e>...</e>-tagged error spans. At inference, the LLM referee is entirely disabled and only the image plus prompt are required, so the trained model incurs zero additional latency.

## Method

SAT-RRG introduces a self-adaptive push-pull training loop: a frozen LLM referee tags semantically conflicting spans in the model's own draft, yielding binary error/correct token masks. On error tokens, the Error Token Adaptive Penalty Loss (ETAPL) flips the sign of the log-probability of the model's chosen token, driving its probability down; on correct tokens, the Correct Token Adaptive Loss (CTAL) reinforces the chosen token's probability. Update magnitudes are modulated by two complementary detached weights—normalized Shannon entropy (capturing distribution-level uncertainty) and a focal-style confidence coefficient (capturing prediction-level over/under-confidence)—so overconfident mistakes receive stronger penalties while ambiguous tokens are softly corrected. The two masked losses are entropy-weighted-normalized over their respective spans, combined with standard cross-entropy via a balancing coefficient α, and backpropagated through the Swin-Transformer + LLaMA3-3B pipeline. The entire mechanism is differentiable, architecture-agnostic, and adds no inference-time cost.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, SAT-RRG (LLaMA3-3B) achieves BLEU-4 of 0.143, METEOR of 0.167, and ROUGE-L of 0.303, outperforming all baselines including LLM-based methods R2GenGPT (BLEU-4: 0.134) and Bootstrapping (BLEU-4: 0.128) that use larger 7B models. On IU-Xray, it reaches BLEU-4 of 0.196, METEOR of 0.222, and ROUGE-L of 0.400, surpassing EKAGen (BLEU-4: 0.190) and MAN (BLEU-4: 0.170). On clinical metrics (MIMIC-CXR), SAT-RRG attains the best RadGraph F1 (0.205), BERTScore (0.422), GREEN (0.310), and RaTEScore (0.540), while achieving a competitive RadCliQ of 1.150 (lower is better).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAT-RRG (3B LLaMA3) outperforms the two strongest LLM-based baselines—R2GenGPT and Bootstrapping (both 7B)—on BLEU-4 by 7.5% and 12.5% respectively on MIMIC-CXR, and by 13.3% and 6.5% on IU-Xray, despite using a smaller backbone. On MIMIC-CXR, it leads all methods on BLEU-1 through BLEU-4 and ROUGE-L, though Bootstrapping edges it on METEOR (0.175 vs. 0.167). On IU-Xray, SAT-RRG achieves the best score on every NLG metric, beating the closest competitor EKAGen by 0.007 BLEU-1, 0.006 BLEU-4, and 0.012 METEOR. On clinical metrics (Table 3), SAT-RRG attains the highest RadGraph F1 (0.205 vs. KARGEN's 0.203), BERTScore (0.422), GREEN (0.310), and RaTEScore (0.540), while EKAGen (which uses higher-resolution 300×300 images) achieves the best RadCliQ (1.126 vs. 1.150). The ablation confirms both ETAPL and CTAL contribute: removing either degrades BLEU-4 by 0.002–0.007, and the focal parameter γ=1.5 yields the best trade-off over γ=1.0 or 2.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAT-RRG extends LLM-based radiology report generation (R2GenGPT, Bootstrapping) by replacing uniform cross-entropy training with a token-level push-pull objective guided by a frozen LLM referee that localizes semantic conflicts. Conceptually, it repurposes focal-loss-style confidence weighting and entropy-based uncertainty calibration—ideas borrowed from object detection and classification—into a differentiable, training-time gradient modulation scheme for NLP. Unlike prior self-correction approaches (SelfRefine, Reflexion) that operate at inference time via iterative prompting, or RL-based token feedback (SCoRe, S²R) that require hand-crafted rewards, SAT-RRG embeds the correction signal directly into the loss function, making it fully differentiable and architecture-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The push-pull formulation addresses a genuine gap in RRG training: standard CE only raises the ground-truth token probability without suppressing the model's current wrong choice, and treats all positions uniformly. The framework's zero inference-time overhead and robustness to noisy error tags make it practically deployable in clinical settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond chest X-rays to other modalities (CT, MRI), whether the LLM referee can be replaced by a lighter internal mechanism to reduce training cost, and whether the gains persist when scaling to larger LLMs where the base model already has lower error rates.
