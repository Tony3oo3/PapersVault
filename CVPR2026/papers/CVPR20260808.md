## Problem

One-step diffusion-based super-resolution methods face a fundamental distributional gap: LR latents lack the high-frequency content present in the diffusion model's native noise or intermediate-timestep inputs, degrading performance. Adding noise to LR to close this gap is straightforward but corrupts image content, and directly applying latent-space preference optimization (e.g., DiffusionNFT) to SR introduces pronounced grid artifacts in outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260808/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded LR image, a text caption (generated by Qwen3-VL-8B), and random Gaussian noise as inputs, and produces a single HR image in one forward pass. The LR image is encoded to latents, mixed with noise at a fixed mid-timestep t_mid=0.5 to form z_mix, and both z_mix and the original z_LR are patchified and concatenated along the token dimension before entering the DiT blocks. The VAE decoder is kept frozen, and the final HR image is decoded from the predicted velocity at the mid-timestep.

## Method

DNF-SR employs a dual-input architecture built on Flux-Kontext (an image-editing DiT model): noisy LR latents (z_mix) and original LR latents (z_LR) are concatenated with text tokens and processed through MM-DiT and Single-DiT blocks, with the output at the z_mix position extracted as the predicted velocity for one-step reconstruction at t_mid=0.5. Supervised fine-tuning uses a composite loss combining latent alignment (L_z), reconstruction (MSE + DISTS), and a GAN term with a DINOv3 discriminator, all applied via LoRA (rank 64) to the VAE encoder and DiT blocks. For post-training, the paper proposes NF²T, which partitions K=8 sampled outputs into positive and negative subsets using aggregated full-reference (LPIPS, DISTS) and no-reference (CLIPIQA, MUSIQ, MANIQA) IQA rewards, then defines implicit policy improvement directions in both image and feature spaces rather than the velocity/latent space, thereby avoiding the grid artifacts observed with direct latent-space NFT optimization.

## Results

DNF-SR achieves state-of-the-art performance on all no-reference (perceptual quality) metrics across four benchmarks. On RealSR, it scores 0.7257 CLIPIQA, 72.040 MUSIQ, 0.6930 MANIQA, 4.0718 QALIGN, and 4.2646 VQ-R1, surpassing the strongest one-step baseline OMGSR (0.6682, 69.527, 0.6695, 3.8507, 4.1285 respectively). On the no-reference-only RealLQ250 benchmark, DNF-SR leads all metrics with 0.7997 CLIPIQA, 73.700 MUSIQ, 0.7029 MANIQA, 4.4752 QALIGN, and 4.6090 VQ-R1. However, DNF-SR trades off pixel-level fidelity: on RealSR it scores 24.970 PSNR and 0.3239 LPIPS, below OMGSR (25.882 PSNR, 0.2779 LPIPS) and S3Diff (0.2721 LPIPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNF-SR achieves the best score on every no-reference metric (CLIPIQA, MUSIQ, MANIQA, QALIGN, VQ-R1) across all four benchmarks, with margins of 0.03–0.06 over the nearest one-step competitor OMGSR on CLIPIQA and 1.5–2.5 points on MUSIQ. On full-reference metrics, DNF-SR trails the strongest baselines: OMGSR leads on PSNR (25.882 vs. 24.970 on RealSR; 28.928 vs. 28.141 on DrealSR) and S3Diff leads on LPIPS (0.2721 vs. 0.3239 on RealSR), reflecting the known perception–fidelity trade-off of generative SR. Notably, even DNF-SR(sft) without NF²T post-training already surpasses all baselines on CLIPIQA and QALIGN, indicating the dual-input architecture itself provides a strong perceptual-quality advantage. The NF²T post-training further boosts no-reference scores (e.g., MUSIQ 70.672→72.040 on RealSR, QALIGN 3.9162→4.0718) at the cost of slightly higher LPIPS (0.2925→0.3239), confirming that the preference-optimization stage trades pixel-level fidelity for perceptual realism. Among multi-step methods, DiT4SR and SeeSR are the closest on PSNR but fall well behind on all perceptual metrics (e.g., DiT4SR scores 0.6319 CLIPIQA vs. DNF-SR's 0.7257 on RealSR). On the no-reference-only RealLQ250 benchmark, DNF-SR extends its lead, scoring 0.7997 CLIPIQA and 4.6090 VQ-R1 versus 0.7435 and 4.4951 for OMGSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step diffusion-based SR line (OSEDiff, TSDSR, OMGSR, TADSR) by addressing the distributional gap between LR latents and a diffusion model's native noise input through a dual-input strategy: concatenating noisy LR (to match the model's expected input distribution) with clean LR (to preserve fidelity) into an image-editing backbone (Flux-Kontext) rather than a text-to-image model. The post-training component adapts DiffusionNFT's positive/negative preference optimization but deliberately shifts the optimization from the latent velocity space to image and feature spaces, motivated by observed grid artifacts when applying the original latent-space formulation to SR. The use of an editing model as the backbone, combined with LoRA fine-tuning and a GAN loss with DINOv3 discriminator, positions the work at the intersection of diffusion-based restoration and preference-aligned generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step diffusion SR is practically important for deployment where multi-step inference is prohibitive, and this paper demonstrates that the distributional gap problem can be mitigated without sacrificing fidelity, yielding strong no-reference quality scores across four benchmarks. However, the NF²T post-training step degrades LPIPS (reference-based fidelity) while improving perceptual metrics, highlighting the well-known perception-distortion tradeoff that remains unresolved for generative SR. Open questions include whether the dual-input design generalizes to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring), whether the editing-model backbone is essential or merely convenient, and whether the multi-metric reward aggregation can be replaced by a single learned reward model to reduce evaluation overhead during post-training.
