## Problem

Table recognition (TR) with vision-language models has been bottlenecked by the cost and scarcity of large-scale labeled data, causing open-source models to lag far behind proprietary systems like Gemini 2.5 Pro. Existing data-acquisition strategies—synthetic rendering, manual annotation, and distillation from proprietary models—each suffer from limited visual diversity, high cost, or an inherent performance ceiling. The paper asks whether unlabeled table images collected at scale from the wild can be harnessed to push TR performance beyond what supervised fine-tuning alone can achieve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263798/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled table images (RGB crops extracted from web PDFs via DocLayoutYOLO layout detection, ~100K samples) and produces structured table markup in OTSL format, a compact representation that encodes cell adjacency relationships rather than explicit colspan/rowspan attributes, reducing token length. During training, each image is paired with a set of ~30 diverse QA pairs generated by a 72B teacher VLM; the fine-tuned 3B model outputs recognition results that a separate 8B LLM uses to answer those questions, with F1-scored answers forming the reward signal.

## Method

TRivia fine-tunes a base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using table question-answering as a proxy reward, eliminating the need for ground-truth HTML labels. Two key components make this work: (1) response-consistency sampling, which generates K recognition outputs per image, computes pairwise TEDS similarity, and selects images with moderate consistency scores (0.4–1.0) that elicit diverse model responses—aligning with GRPO's principle of optimizing relative advantages; and (2) attention-guided QA generation, which exploits VLM attention distributions to identify the visual source of each QA pair, then greedily selects pairs with minimal visual overlap (IOU < 0.3) to ensure broad table coverage. Invalid recognition outputs are filtered before advantage computation to stabilize training. The full pipeline comprises three stages: OTSL syntax warm-up on ~700K synthetic samples, supervised fine-tuning on ~50K real-world tables, and self-supervised GRPO on ~50K unlabeled images with QA-based rewards.

## Results

TRivia-3B achieves an overall TEDS of 89.88 and S-TEDS of 93.60 across four benchmarks (PubTabNet, OmniDocBench, CC-OCR, OCRBench v2), setting a new state of the art among open-source TR models. On the most challenging real-world benchmarks, it reaches 84.90 TEDS on CC-OCR and 90.76 TEDS on OCRBench v2, surpassing all 12 compared baselines. Notably, this 3B-parameter model outperforms much larger general-purpose VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 83.52 TEDS, InternVL3.5-241B at 78.41 TEDS) and proprietary systems (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 88.93 TEDS, GPT-5 at 78.30 TEDS) on the overall metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRivia-3B (3B params) outperforms all 12 baselines on the overall TEDS metric, with the closest competitor being Gemini 2.5 Pro (88.93 vs. 89.88 TEDS, a 0.95-point margin) and MinerU2.5 (86.82 vs. 89.88, a 3.06-point margin). The only benchmark where TRivia-3B does not lead is CC-OCR, where Gemini 2.5 Pro edges it by 0.66 TEDS (85.56 vs. 84.90). Against expert TR models, the gains are dramatic: TRivia-3B surpasses UniTable by 27.06 TEDS on CC-OCR and 23.03 on OCRBench v2, and SLANNet-plus by 33.97 TEDS on CC-OCR. Among document-parsing VLMs, TRivia-3B leads PaddleOCR-VL by 6.52 TEDS overall and MinerU2.5 by 3.06 TEDS, despite MinerU2.5 being trained on data orders of magnitude larger with manual annotation and distillation from Gemini 2.5 Pro. Notably, TRivia-3B exceeds its own QA-generation teacher (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) by 6.36 TEDS overall, demonstrating that the self-supervised QA-based reward extracts supervision beyond what direct distillation of the teacher's outputs can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the reinforcement learning backbone but fundamentally repurposes it for table recognition by replacing explicit label-based rewards with a table question-answering proxy signal. It departs from the dominant paradigm of supervised fine-tuning VLMs on labeled table data, as well as from synthetic-data and proprietary-distillation approaches that have dominated recent TR progress. The attention-guided QA generation and response-consistency sampling are novel data-curation mechanisms that have no direct precedent in prior TR or VLM fine-tuning literature, positioning the work at the intersection of self-supervised learning, RL for vision-language models, and document understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRivia demonstrates that a compact 3B open-source model can surpass much larger proprietary systems (Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5) and heavily-resourced open-source efforts (MinerU2.5) on table recognition, offering a privacy-preserving, offline-deployable alternative for sensitive document processing. The framework also functions as a scalable automated annotator, potentially replacing costly human labeling or proprietary distillation for constructing TR datasets. Open questions include whether the QA-based reward mechanism generalizes to other structured-output tasks (forms, charts, mathematical layouts), whether the approach scales to very large or multi-page tables, and whether the self-supervised gains persist as base models improve.
