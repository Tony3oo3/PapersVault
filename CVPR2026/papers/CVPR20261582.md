## Problem

Existing cross-view geo-localization methods assume perfectly aligned training pairs, but real-world GPS drift from urban canyons, electromagnetic interference, and adverse weather causes systematic spatial misalignment where only partial correspondences exist between UAV and satellite images. This Noisy Correspondence in CVGL (NC-CVGL) problem is underexplored: prior noisy-correspondence methods either discard misaligned pairs entirely or repurpose them as negatives, wasting the substantial local information they still contain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261582/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB UAV images and nadir satellite tiles (both resized to 384×384) as input, where satellite tiles are cropped from a global basemap using the UAV's recorded GPS coordinates. Training pairs are categorized by IoU thresholds (τ_m=0.39, τ_s=0.14) into well-aligned positives and semi-positive noisy pairs with partial overlap. The output is a shared d-dimensional embedding vector per image, used for retrieval-based geographic localization (top-K satellite matches for a UAV query).

## Method

PAUL builds on Sample4Geo (ViT-Base encoder, InfoNCE loss) and introduces three coordinated mechanisms. First, Co-partition fits a two-component GMM over InfoNCE loss values to assign each pair a soft posterior probability of being clean, avoiding hard thresholds. Second, Co-augmentation formulates batch matching as a K-way classification, computes Dirichlet-based evidential uncertainty, and generates spatial masks via EDL-guided gradients to mask low-confidence regions, producing pseudo-clean augmented samples from noisy pairs. Third, Co-training runs two networks that exchange their partitioned datasets; clean and augmented pairs are optimized with InfoNCE while residual noisy pairs receive an EDL loss that acts as a reliability-aware regularizer, penalizing high-uncertainty predictions. The key distinction from prior work is that noisy samples are not discarded but actively rectified through uncertainty-guided region masking and then exploited for representation learning.

## Results

On GTA-UAV (cross-area split), PAUL achieves R@1 of 61.21% at 0% noise, 58.70% at 30% noise, and 52.61% at 60% noise, consistently outperforming all eight baselines across every noise level. On the real-world UAV-VisLoc dataset, PAUL reaches R@1 of 36.12% (0% noise) and 26.64% (30% noise), again leading all competitors. The method also attains the best Dis@1 (localization error) in most settings, e.g., 384.57 m at 30% noise on GTA-UAV cross-area versus 449.21 m for the next-best CRCL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAUL outperforms all eight baselines on every noise level and both datasets. At 30% noise on GTA-UAV cross-area, the closest competitors are GSC (54.41% R@1), CREAM (54.02%), and CRCL (53.79%), with PAUL leading by 4.3–7.6 percentage points. The gap widens substantially against methods that discard or underutilize noisy pairs: ESC (40.84%), BiCro (41.11%), and NCR (44.40%) trail PAUL by 14–18 points in R@1. On the real-world UAV-VisLoc dataset at 30% noise, PAUL (26.64% R@1) beats the next-best CRCL (23.77%) by nearly 3 points, while NCR and ESC drop below 19%. Notably, CREAM achieves the best Dis@1 at 30% noise (358.19 m) but PAUL still leads on all retrieval metrics (R@1, R@5, AP), indicating superior overall matching quality despite a slightly larger localization error in that single setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAUL builds directly on the Sample4Geo framework (Deuser et al., ICCV 2023) for cross-view geo-localization, adopting its ViT-Base encoder and InfoNCE training protocol as the base model. It departs from prior noisy correspondence methods (NCR, BiCro, RCL, CRCL, ESC, GSC, CREAM) that predominantly operate in cross-modal retrieval and either discard noisy pairs or repurpose them as negatives; PAUL instead treats spatially misaligned but partially overlapping pairs as recoverable signal, leveraging Evidential Deep Learning (Sensoy et al., 2018) to identify high-confidence regions within noisy samples for targeted augmentation. The co-training paradigm with exchanged sample partitions follows the DivideMix lineage, but the evidential regularizer and uncertainty-guided masking represent a distinct integration not seen in prior noisy correspondence work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPS drift-induced misalignment is a pervasive and practically critical issue in UAV deployment that has been largely ignored by the CVGL community, which has predominantly assumed clean training pairs on ground-satellite benchmarks. By formally defining NC-CVGL and demonstrating that noisy pairs retain exploitable signal, the paper opens a direction for more deployment-realistic training protocols. However, evaluation is limited to one synthetic dataset (GTA-UAV) and one small real dataset (UAV-VisLoc, ~6.7K pairs), with experiments on a single GPU over only 5 epochs; validation on larger, more diverse real-world UAV fleets and longer training schedules remains open. The UAV-satellite formulation also differs from the dominant ground-satellite setting (CVUSA, CVACT, VIGOR), so generalizability to other platform pairs is untested.
