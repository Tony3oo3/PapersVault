## Problem

GUI agents must continuously adapt to evolving applications, yet existing training paradigms (SFT or RL alone) fail to balance new-task plasticity with old-task retention: SFT rapidly overwrites prior interaction logic, while GRPO preserves knowledge but converges too slowly on novel tasks. No prior work has systematically analyzed how to synergistically combine SFT and RL for continual GUI learning under strict data isolation (no access to historical training data).

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a GUI screenshot (RGB image), a global natural-language instruction, and the sequence of previously executed actions, then predicts the next action as a text token that is parsed into a coordinate-based GUI command (tap, swipe, or text input). The action space is unified across all interaction types, and the agent operates autoregressively over a variable-length trajectory until the task is complete or a maximum step budget is reached.

## Method

CGL jointly optimizes a GRPO objective and an SFT objective (L = L_GRPO + λ·L_SFT) through three coupled mechanisms. First, Error-Aware Routing triggers SFT on ground-truth demonstrations only when all K sampled RL rollouts for an instruction fail to reach full reward, breaking the reward-sparsity deadlock. Second, Entropy-Regulated Tuning dynamically modulates λ: it linearly ramps up during a warmup phase to inject entropy and break pathological action biases, then follows an exponential decay in policy entropy (λ = (λ_max−λ_min)·min(1, ke^{γH}) + λ_min) so that SFT yields to GRPO's precise convergence once the policy stabilizes. Third, Conditional Gradient Surgery detects directional conflict between SFT and GRPO gradients via cosine similarity; when cos α < 0, it projects the SFT gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the GRPO gradient, surgically removing only the adversarial component while preserving all constructive (orthogonal or aligned) update directions.

## Results

On QwenVL2.5-3b-Instruct (Task Order 1), CGL achieves 82.33% average Step-Accuracy and 38.03% average Trajectory-Accuracy, with a near-zero Forgetting Measure (FM) of −0.02, outperforming all baselines. On the smaller LLaVA-OneVision-0.5b backbone, CGL reaches 77.84% Step-Acc and 24.77% Traj-Acc with FM −0.52. Across three different task orders, CGL consistently leads in Step-Acc (≥82.33%) and Traj-Acc (≥38.03%), and notably achieves a positive FM (+0.13) in Task Order 2, indicating slight performance *improvement* on old tasks rather than forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO [8], CGL improves average Step-Accuracy by 0.80 pp (82.33 vs. 81.53), Trajectory-Accuracy by 1.25 pp (38.03 vs. 36.78), and reduces the Forgetting Measure from −0.62 to −0.02, indicating substantially less degradation on previously learned tasks. Against RIF-RFT [16], CGL gains 1.89 pp in Step-Acc (82.33 vs. 80.44), 5.12 pp in Traj-Acc (38.03 vs. 32.91), and improves FM from −0.58 to −0.02. CGL also surpasses the simpler SFT+KL baseline (80.84% Step-Acc, FM −1.01) and SFT+Replay (79.80%, FM −3.11), confirming that the synergy of entropy-regulated SFT weighting and gradient surgery provides advantages beyond what KL constraints or data replay alone can achieve. The only method exceeding CGL is SFT-Joint-Training (83.48% Step-Acc), which has access to all task data simultaneously and thus does not face the continual-learning constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as its reinforcement learning backbone and positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate literatures: continual learning for vision-language models (e.g., RIF-RFT, MMRE, CREG) and GUI agent training (UI-TARS, CogAgent, GUI-G1/G2). It departs from prior GUI agent work, which treats tasks as static and non-overlapping, by explicitly formulating sequential app-domain adaptation as a continual learning problem. The key conceptual departure is the empirical demonstration that SFT and RL occupy opposite ends of the stability-plasticity spectrum in the GUI setting, motivating their integration via entropy-regulated weighting and gradient surgery rather than using either in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AndroidControl-CL benchmark fills a genuine gap by providing a standardized, multi-order evaluation protocol for GUI continual learning, which the community previously lacked. The near-zero forgetting measure (FM ≈ −0.02) and the rare positive FM (+0.13) in one task order suggest that the SFT-RL synergy can approach the ideal of 'learn new, forget nothing,' though the gap to the SFT-Joint-Training upper bound (which has access to all data) remains non-trivial. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Android to web or desktop GUIs, whether it scales beyond 7 sequential tasks, and whether the gradient surgery overhead is prohibitive at larger model scales. The method's reliance on ground-truth demonstrations for the SFT branch also limits its applicability to fully autonomous settings.
