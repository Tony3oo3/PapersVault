## Problem

While RL has been successfully extended to LLMs and 2D image generation, its application to text-to-3D autoregressive generation remains largely unexplored due to the higher spatial complexity of 3D objects, which demand globally consistent geometry and fine-grained local textures simultaneously. This makes 3D generation significantly more sensitive to reward design and algorithmic choices than its 2D counterpart, and no systematic investigation of which RL strategies transfer effectively to the 3D domain existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a 3D mesh decoded from discrete VQVAE tokens. Before generating 3D tokens, the model first produces textual chain-of-thought reasoning (G=8 sampled per prompt) that conditions the subsequent autoregressive token generation. For reward evaluation, six rendered views are sampled per generated object, and the Hi-GRPO paradigm outputs two intermediate artifacts per iteration: a coarse geometric shape (Step 1) and a texture-refined final mesh (Step 2).

## Method

The core contribution is Hi-GRPO, a hierarchical RL paradigm that decomposes each training iteration into two coarse-to-fine steps aligned with the natural hierarchy of 3D generation. In Step 1, the model generates high-level semantic reasoning (spatial layout, component proportions, color distribution) and produces a coarse 3D shape; in Step 2, conditioned on the prompt and Step 1's CoT, it generates low-level visual reasoning (local textures, element counts, symmetry) and produces the refined 3D object. Two dedicated reward ensembles compute group-relative advantages per step: Step 1 uses HPS V2.1, UnifiedReward (prompt alignment), and Qwen2.5-VL (binary category verification), while Step 2 adds UnifiedReward-2.0 (aesthetic quality), Qwen2.5-VL (multi-view appearance consistency), and ShapeLLM (3D component completeness from point clouds). The Step 2 reward is backpropagated to Step 1 via R_high = R_high + λ·R_low, and each step independently computes its policy loss, yielding L = L_high + L_low. Built on ShapeLLM-Omni with GRPO/DAPO token-level optimization, the resulting AR3D-R1 model achieves state-of-the-art results on both the newly proposed MME-3DR benchmark (249 reasoning-intensive 3D objects) and Toys4K.

## Results

AR3D-R1 achieves the best performance across all four metrics on both MME-3DR and Toys4K. On MME-3DR, it attains a CLIP score of 28.5 (vs. 23.4 for Trellis, the strongest baseline) and KD_incep of 0.194 (vs. 0.302). On Toys4K, it reaches CLIP 29.3 and KD_incep 0.156, outperforming Trellis (26.8 / 0.175) and its own base model ShapeLLM-Omni (22.7 / 0.249). The gains are most pronounced on the reasoning-intensive MME-3DR benchmark, where AR3D-R1 improves CLIP by 5.1 points over Trellis and 8.7 points over ShapeLLM-Omni.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AR3D-R1 outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. Against the strongest competitor Trellis [39], AR3D-R1 gains +5.1 CLIP on MME-3DR (28.5 vs. 23.4) and +2.5 on Toys4K (29.3 vs. 26.8), while also reducing KD_incep from 0.302→0.194 and KD_dinov2 from 4.27→2.74 on MME-3DR. Compared to its own base model ShapeLLM-Omni [42], the RL training yields a substantial +8.7 CLIP improvement on MME-3DR (28.5 vs. 19.8) and +6.6 on Toys4K (29.3 vs. 22.7), confirming the effectiveness of Hi-GRPO. The diffusion-based baselines LGM [29] and 3DTopia-XL [6] trail significantly, with CLIP scores below 17 on MME-3DR, while the autoregressive SAR3D [4] is closer (16.7) but still far behind. Notably, AR3D-R1 also achieves the lowest FD_incep on both benchmarks (25.9 on MME-3DR, 20.4 on Toys4K), indicating superior distributional fidelity beyond single-sample metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-generation paradigm established in 2D (T2I-R1, DPO-based image generation) to the 3D autoregressive setting, using ShapeLLM-Omni as the base model and GRPO as the algorithmic foundation. It departs from prior 2D work by introducing Hi-GRPO, a hierarchical variant that decomposes generation into coarse-geometry and fine-texture steps with dedicated reward ensembles, motivated by the observation that 3D generation naturally progresses from global structure to local detail. The paper also positions itself against existing text-to-3D benchmarks by arguing they fail to probe implicit reasoning, and introduces MME-3DR to fill that gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first systematic study of RL for text-to-3D autoregressive generation, this work provides a practical roadmap (reward design, algorithm choice, scaling behavior) for a direction that was previously unexplored. The MME-3DR benchmark exposes that current models rely heavily on memorization rather than genuine 3D reasoning, which is a useful diagnostic for the field. However, the study is limited to a single base model (ShapeLLM-Omni) and the Toys4K toy-object domain, leaving open whether the hierarchical RL paradigm and reward ensemble design transfer to more complex scenes, non-autoregressive 3D generators, or higher-fidelity asset generation.
