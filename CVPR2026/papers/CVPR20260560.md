## Problem

Existing mmWave radar datasets for human sensing are limited to sparse skeleton annotations, small scale, and simple in-place actions, and no prior work has explored human mesh reconstruction (HMR) directly from raw radar tensors (RT). This constrains the development of privacy-preserving, occlusion-robust HMR systems that can handle diverse, dynamic, and unconstrained indoor motions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260560/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The primary input is a 4D tensor of T=4 consecutive raw radar tensors, each a 3D intensity volume of shape (121×111×31) in Cartesian XYZ coordinates, obtained via FFT processing across range, azimuth, and elevation from a Vayyar vTrigB radar (7 GHz bandwidth, 20 Tx/Rx). Radar point clouds (RPC) are also provided as a sparser alternative derived via CFAR thresholding. The model outputs SMPL-X parameters (root orientation α∈ℝ³, body shape β∈ℝ¹⁰, global translation τ∈ℝ³, body pose θ∈ℝ²²ˣ³, and gender probability g∈[0,1]), which yield a dense 3D mesh with 10,475 vertices and 22 joints. All metrics are computed in the world frame without Procrustes alignment, reported in millimeters and degrees.

## Method

RT-Mesh is the first HMR method operating directly on raw radar tensors, designed as a two-stage architecture to handle the high volume of RT data efficiently. Stage 1 collapses the 4D RT into a 2D bird's-eye-view (BEV) representation and uses a lightweight 2D convolution + self-attention module to localize the human foreground at (x̂, ŷ) under a 2D localization loss. Stage 2 crops a local 3D region-of-interest from the full RT volume based on the predicted location, then applies 3D convolutions and a 3D Transformer to extract fine-grained spatial features, which an HMR head maps to SMPL-X parameters. The model achieves 2.74 ms latency and 2.6 GFLOPs, making it deployable on edge devices. The paper further establishes comprehensive benchmarks comparing RPC-based (mmMesh, P4Transformer) and RT-based (RT-Pose, RETR) methods, RGB-D baselines (TokenHMR, P4Transformer), and multimodal fusion (RPC+RT, RGB+RT, Depth+RT) across three difficulty protocols and three generalization splits.

## Results

On the full M4Human benchmark (Protocol ALL, random split S1), the proposed RT-Mesh achieves 90.9 mm MVE, comparable to the best RPC-based method P4Transformer (90.4 mm) and surpassing RGB (97.5 mm) and mm-Mesh (132.7 mm). Under cross-subject (S2) and cross-action (S3) splits, RT-Mesh (135.1 / 143.1 mm) outperforms all other radar baselines, confirming that raw radar tensors generalize better than sparse point clouds. Multimodal fusion of Depth+RT yields the best overall MVE of 77.5 mm (S1), 115.9 mm (S2), and 120.0 mm (S3), beating all single-modality approaches. RT-Mesh also achieves the highest efficiency among RT-based methods at 2.74 ms latency and 2.6 GFLOPs, versus 39.58 ms / 50.67 GFLOPs for RT-Pose.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among radar-only methods, RT-Mesh (the proposed baseline) outperforms mm-Mesh by ~42 mm MVE on S1 (90.9 vs. 132.7) and by ~10–15 mm on S2/S3, and beats RT-Pose by ~10 mm on S1 (90.9 vs. 100.7) and ~13 mm on S2. Against the strongest RPC-based method P4Transformer, RT-Mesh is nearly tied on S1 (90.9 vs. 90.4) but clearly wins on S2 (135.1 vs. 140.8) and S3 (143.1 vs. 147.8), indicating that raw tensors generalize better to unseen subjects and actions. Compared to vision baselines, RT-Mesh surpasses RGB on S1 and S2 (90.9 vs. 97.5; 135.1 vs. 149.7) but trails Depth on all three splits (e.g., 90.9 vs. 82.7 on S1), confirming that depth still holds an edge for single-modality HMR. The key finding is that fusing Depth+RT achieves the best MVE across all splits (77.5 / 115.9 / 120.0 mm), improving over Depth alone by 5–7 mm and over RGB alone by 20–29 mm, demonstrating that mmWave radar provides a strong complementary signal to camera-based systems. For downstream HAR, RT-Mesh-predicted skeletons retain 64.8% Top-1 accuracy (vs. 75.7% with GT skeletons) under S1, validating the practical utility of radar-based mesh outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mmWave radar-based human sensing lineage (mRI, mm-Fi, mmBody, mmMesh, RT-Pose, MMVR) by being the first to perform HMR directly from raw radar tensors (RT) rather than the sparser, CFAR-filtered point clouds (RPC) used in all prior radar HMR work. The RT-Mesh baseline repurposes the two-stage localization-then-regression paradigm common in RT-based HPE (RT-Pose, RETR) and adapts it for dense mesh parameter regression, while the RGB-D baselines draw on TokenHMR and P4Transformer. Conceptually, the paper positions RT as a strictly richer representation than RPC and demonstrates that the additional spatial intensity context preserved in RT translates into better cross-subject and cross-action generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M4Human is the first benchmark large enough (661K frames, 9× prior largest) and diverse enough (50 actions including free-space sports and rehabilitation) to meaningfully study generalization in radar-based HMR, and it establishes that the scaling law holds for this modality. The finding that radar-only HMR approaches RGB-D performance, and that RT outperforms RPC specifically under unseen-subject/action splits, provides concrete evidence that raw tensor representations are the right level of abstraction for fine-grained RF human modeling. Open challenges remain for fast non-in-place motion (P3 protocol), cross-subject generalization, and the gap between radar and depth on rotation error, suggesting that temporal modeling and human priors are still needed to close the remaining performance gap.
