## Problem

Despite rapid progress in multimodal large language models (MLLMs), their ability to understand infrared images remains largely unexplored, as existing evaluations focus almost exclusively on natural RGB scenes. Prior infrared benchmarks suffer from narrow task coverage, absence of human calibration, and limited model choices, leaving the actual infrared comprehension capability of mainstream MLLMs unclear. Additionally, the domain distribution shift between RGB-trained models and infrared inputs causes significant performance degradation that is difficult to address via supervised fine-tuning due to scarce infrared-text data.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consists of 499 infrared images (minimum 200px in width or height) paired with 680 multiple-choice VQA questions (4 options each) spanning 10 dimensions across three task categories. Each question is evaluated 8 times per model (4 circular option permutations × 2 languages: English and Chinese), and correctness is determined by a hybrid strategy combining exact string matching with Qwen3-7B-based answer extraction. For GenViP, the MLLM receives a composite input of the original infrared image plus a generated RGB counterpart and a brief textual prior describing infrared characteristics, rather than a single image.

## Method

IF-Bench is constructed via a three-stage pipeline: (1) sampling 1,166 high-quality images from 23 infrared datasets in InfPre, (2) generating 4,628 VQA pairs through manual bounding-box annotation on 61 images plus automated question generation by Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and (3) two-stage human calibration (general annotators followed by infrared domain experts) to yield the final 680 pairs. The evaluation protocol employs circular option permutation, bilingual assessment, and hybrid correctness judgment to ensure reliability. GenViP is a training-free inference-time method that uses an image editing model to translate each infrared image into a spatially and semantically aligned RGB counterpart, then feeds both the original infrared and generated RGB images jointly to the MLLM, preserving thermal information while reducing domain gap. To improve the open-source editing component, Qwen-Edit-2509 is fine-tuned on 50,000 RGB-T pairs curated from 370,000+ candidates via DINOv3 feature extraction, Canny-edge Dice filtering, and hierarchical clustering-based balanced sampling, yielding Qwen-Edit-2509-FT that surpasses closed-source editors like Seedream 4.0 on this task.

## Results

On IF-Bench (680 VQA pairs across 10 dimensions), the best overall model is Doubao-Seed-Vision-1.6-250815 with an average score of 84.2, while the best open-source model InternVL3.5-241B-A28B achieves 83.9. The GenViP method consistently improves performance across all tested MLLMs, with the largest gains on smaller models (e.g., +4.2 on InternVL3.5-1B, +4.8 on Qwen2.5-VL-3B) and a peak of 84.4 for Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct, surpassing all closed-source models. Fine-grained perception dimensions remain the weakest, with object counting and spatial relationship understanding averaging below 60 across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Doubao-Seed-Vision-1.6-250815 leads at 84.2 but exceeds the best open-source model (InternVL3.5-241B-A28B, 83.9) by only 0.3 points, while Gemini-2.5-Pro (82.0) and Gemini-2.5-Flash (79.8) are both outperformed by Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct (83.7). Within open-source models, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (78.8) surpasses the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (78.1) using roughly one-tenth the parameters, and MoE architectures show efficiency advantages (InternVL3.5-30B-A3B at 74.4 beats dense InternVL3.5-14B at 73.6 while activating <25% of parameters). For the GenViP editing component, the fine-tuned Qwen-Edit-2509-FT (74.2 avg with Qwen2.5-VL-7B) outperforms both closed-source editors Seedream 4.0 (73.8) and Gemini-2.5-Flash (73.6), while the unmodified open-source Qwen-Edit-2509 (72.7) trails them. With GenViP applied, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct reaches 84.4, exceeding Doubao-Seed-Vision-1.6 (84.2) and all other models in the benchmark. LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B-Instruct (75.9) and Keye-VL-1.5-8B-Thinking (73.3) occupy the mid-range, with the former benefiting from stronger coarse-grained perception and the latter from better target localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior infrared understanding efforts (Infrared-LLaVA, IRGPT) that relied on modality-specific fine-tuning, instead proposing a training-free generative visual prompting approach that leverages image editing models to bridge the RGB-infrared domain gap. The benchmark construction draws on established VQA evaluation protocols (circular evaluation from MM-Bench, bilingual assessment) but applies them to a previously unexplored modality. The GenViP method builds directly on Qwen-Edit-2509 as its editing backbone, fine-tuning it on curated RGB-T pairs to surpass closed-source editors like Seedream 4.0 and Gemini-2.5-Flash for infrared-to-RGB translation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IF-Bench fills a critical gap by providing the first systematic, human-calibrated evaluation of MLLMs on infrared images, and the finding that open-source models (Qwen3-VL-235B, InternVL3.5-241B) are within 0.3 points of the best closed-source model (Doubao-Seed-Vision) is practically significant for resource-constrained deployments in surveillance and aerial monitoring. The observation that thinking mode yields only a marginal +0.34 average gain while degrading fine-grained perception challenges the assumption that extended reasoning universally improves multimodal tasks. Open questions include whether GenViP generalizes to other out-of-domain modalities (hyperspectral, SAR), whether the 680-question benchmark is sufficient to capture the full breadth of infrared understanding, and whether the diminishing GenViP gains on larger models indicate a fundamental ceiling for input-side domain adaptation.
