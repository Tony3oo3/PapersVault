## Problem

Partially Relevant Video Retrieval (PRVR) must retrieve untrimmed videos from queries describing only a subset of events, but existing methods suffer from incomplete global contextual perception: ambiguous queries trigger spurious local spike activations on globally irrelevant videos, and the standard MIL paradigm undertrains non-best-matching clips. Most prior PRVR approaches treat global context as a training-only regularization or rely on temporally constrained teacher models, leaving video embeddings unrefined at inference time.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted video features (1024-d I3D for ActivityNet Captions and Charades-STA; 3072-d concatenated ResNet152+I3D for TVR) and RoBERTa word-level text features (768-d), operating entirely in the embedding space without raw pixels. It produces a weighted combination of frame-level and clip-level max-cosine similarity scores to rank videos. Notably, the diffusion-generated global registers are used only during representation learning and are discarded before final similarity computation, so inference cost is limited to a few extra attention passes.

## Method

DreamPRVR follows a coarse-to-fine paradigm: a Probabilistic Variational Sampler (PVS) constructs a video-centric Gaussian distribution as the diffusion starting point (rather than random noise), and a lightweight MLP-based Diffusion Register Estimator (DRE) iteratively denoises over T=10 timesteps toward text-supervised targets sampled by a Textual Perturbation Sampler (TPS). The textual supervision is structured by a Query Similarity Preservation loss that aligns same-video queries as complementary positives while a diversity loss separates cross-video queries, yielding a well-organized latent space. The refined registers are then fused with video tokens through Register-Augmented Gaussian Attention Blocks using asymmetric masks (video tokens attend to registers and other video tokens; registers attend only to video tokens), with 8 parallel blocks aggregated via MAIM in a dual frame/clip branch. Training optimizes an ELBO-derived objective combining the retrieval similarity loss, textual structure loss, PVS KL constraint, and DRE denoising loss.

## Results

DreamPRVR achieves state-of-the-art SumR of 156.1 on ActivityNet Captions, 80.0 on Charades-STA, and 193.1 on TVR, outperforming all prior PRVR methods. On ActivityNet, it reaches R@1=8.7, R@5=27.5, R@10=40.3, R@100=79.5; on Charades-STA, R@1=2.6, R@5=8.7, R@10=14.5, R@100=54.2; and on TVR, R@1=17.4, R@5=39.0, R@10=50.4, R@100=86.2. The model has 36.14M parameters with an inference time of 4001 ms and retrieval time of 3686 ms on Charades-STA, comparable to HLFormer (28.43M params, 3816 ms inference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamPRVR outperforms all 20 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. On ActivityNet Captions, it surpasses the closest competitors HLFormer and GMMFormerV2 (both SumR=154.9) by +1.2 SumR, and MGAKD (149.6) by +6.5. On Charades-STA, it leads HLFormer (78.7) by +1.3 SumR and GMMFormerV2 (78.2) by +1.8. On TVR, it beats MGAKD (190.5) by +2.6 SumR and GMMFormerV2 (189.1) by +4.0. Against T2VR baselines (e.g., CLIP4Clip at 127.3/63.4/141.0 SumR) and VCMR baselines (e.g., CONQUER at 133.1/66.0/160.8), DreamPRVR achieves substantially higher scores, confirming the advantage of PRVR-specific modeling. The margin over the strongest PRVR baselines is modest (1–3 SumR points), indicating the field is competitive, but DreamPRVR consistently ranks first on every dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamPRVR extends the PRVR line initiated by MS-SL [12] and advanced by GMMFormer [71] and HLFormer [34], which primarily model clip-level semantics via MIL or hyperbolic spaces but treat global context as a training-only regularizer. The key departure is borrowing register tokens from vision transformers [10] and populating them through a truncated diffusion process [21] initialized by a probabilistic variational sampler, rather than simple pooling or one-step mapping. The variational inference framework [29] provides the principled ELBO that unifies register generation (imagination) and cross-modal alignment (concentration) into a single optimization objective, while the textual semantic structure learning (QSP loss + TPS) constructs a structured latent space to supervise the diffusion target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly generating holistic video semantics via a lightweight diffusion process (only 4–8 registers, 10 timesteps) can mitigate the spurious local spike problem that plagues MIL-based PRVR, yielding consistent SOTA across three benchmarks. The coarse-to-fine 'imagine before concentrate' paradigm offers a new generative-discriminative perspective for retrieval that could transfer to other partially-supervised or noisy-input settings. However, the method still depends on pre-extracted I3D/RoBERTa features and the performance margins over strong baselines like HLFormer and GMMFormerV2 are modest (1–4 SumR points), leaving open whether the diffusion mechanism is truly necessary or whether simpler iterative refinement could achieve comparable gains at lower cost.
