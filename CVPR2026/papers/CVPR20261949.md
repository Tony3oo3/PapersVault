## Problem

Existing crop yield prediction methods are predominantly developed for single crops in crop-specific and region-specific settings, making them unable to generalize across diverse crop types or unseen regions. Moreover, they typically fuse satellite and meteorological modalities in a straightforward manner without explicitly modeling how meteorological trends and short-term extremes affect phenology at different growth stages, despite empirical evidence showing that different crops exhibit distinct and sometimes opposite responses to the same weather variables.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261949/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes two time-series inputs: satellite image time series (SITS) of shape T×H×W×B (spectral bands from Sentinel-2 or MODIS) and meteorological time series (MTS) of shape T×N_d×M (higher-frequency daily weather records within each satellite observation period). It produces a single county-level yield estimate per crop type, with the regression loss computed in log-space to handle the large scale differences between crops (e.g., cotton in lb/ac vs. corn in bu/ac). Only the growing-season window for each crop is retained, and spatial sub-regions are aggregated via a spatial Transformer with [CLS] tokens before temporal decoding.

## Method

PhenoYieldNet uses an encoder-decoder architecture where a multimodal encoder (SpectralGPT-initialized ViT for imagery + MLP for weather, fused via cross-attention) produces a unified latent sequence, which is then decoded by a crop-aware temporal decoder. The decoder's key innovation is a Crop Phenology Bank (CPB) of learnable per-crop query vectors that guide a Crop Phenology Attention (CPA) module; CPA explicitly decomposes the latent sequence into multi-scale trend components (via sliding-window pooling at scales 3, 6, 12 with learnable aggregation weights) and a residual variation component, then projects both into a bias vector injected into the attention logits so the model dynamically re-weights temporal positions based on crop-specific phenological sensitivity to trends vs. extremes. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a self-supervised Temporal Contrastive Adaptation (TCA) step applies masked-view contrastive learning to align the frozen RSFM encoder with agricultural temporal dynamics, and second, the encoder is frozen while the decoder and prediction head are fine-tuned with log-space MSE loss.

## Results

On the CropNet multi-crop dataset (4 crops, 11 U.S. states), PhenoYieldNet-MC achieves the best RMSE across all crops: 16.52 (corn), 54.88 (cotton), 5.91 (soybean), and 8.32 (winter wheat), with the highest R² and Pearson correlation for corn, cotton, and soybean. On the single-crop MODIS dataset (corn, 2003–2015), PhenoYieldNet achieves RMSE 5.95, R² 0.663, and Corr 0.814, outperforming the second-best method (UNet-ConvLSTM) by 0.38 in RMSE and 0.077 in R². The multi-crop variant (C=4) slightly outperforms the single-crop variant on corn and cotton while showing a small trade-off on winter wheat due to class imbalance and atypical phenology.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhenoYieldNet-MC dominates all seven baselines on three of four crops. On corn, it reduces RMSE from 21.96 (RF, the best baseline) to 16.52 and lifts R² from 0.408 to 0.516; on cotton, the gap is dramatic—RMSE drops from 84.47 (XGBoost) to 54.88, a 35% reduction, while R² jumps from 0.166 to 0.638. On soybean, PhenoYieldNet-MC achieves RMSE 5.91 vs. 6.58 for MMST-ViT and 6.76 for RF. The only crop where a baseline edges ahead is winter wheat, where PhenoYieldNet-SC (RMSE 8.20, R² 0.180) slightly beats PhenoYieldNet-MC (RMSE 8.32, R² 0.136), attributed to class imbalance and winter wheat's atypical full-year phenology. The sole multi-crop baseline, YieldNet, performs poorly across the board (R² near 0 for soybean and winter wheat), confirming that a shared CNN backbone without explicit phenological modeling fails to capture crop-specific temporal dynamics. On the single-crop MODIS dataset, PhenoYieldNet also outperforms all four deep-learning baselines, with the largest margin over MMVF (RMSE 5.95 vs. 10.13).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-crop yield prediction literature (MMST-ViT, MMVF, UNet-ConvLSTM) into a unified multi-crop setting, departing from the only prior multi-crop attempt (YieldNet) by replacing its shared CNN backbone with a Transformer-based decoder that explicitly models crop-specific phenology. Architecturally, it repurposes time-series decomposition (inspired by FedFormer) to separate multi-scale trend and variation components, and introduces a learnable Crop Phenology Bank as a query mechanism analogous to class-conditional embeddings in generative models. The Temporal Contrastive Adaptation strategy bridges the domain gap between general-purpose remote sensing foundation models (SpectralGPT) and agricultural temporal dynamics, a gap the ablations confirm is non-trivial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying four crop types in a single model and showing that multi-crop training can match or exceed single-crop performance, the paper offers a practical path toward scalable yield prediction for data-scarce crops and regions. The finding that directly fine-tuning a pre-trained RSFM without domain adaptation degrades performance on some crops validates the need for intermediate alignment strategies in agricultural applications. Open questions include whether the species-level phenology bank can generalize to unseen crops (the authors suggest vector quantization as a more flexible alternative), and whether the observed sensitivity to class imbalance and atypical phenologies (e.g., winter wheat's full-year cycle) can be mitigated without sacrificing the multi-crop efficiency gains.
