## Problem

Current AI-generated image detection methods fine-tune pre-trained foundation models with fixed parameters, which capture only limited forgery patterns from training data and fail to generalize to images produced by unseen generators. The core gap is that static learned parameters cannot adapt to the substantial variations in texture, semantics, and visual artifacts introduced by different generative models, leading to significant performance degradation under domain shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261639/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, which is resized to 224×224 for the CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder. During inference, the method additionally generates multiple augmented views (one global resize plus N_v−1 random 224×224 crops with horizontal flipping) from the same image for test-time token tuning and optimal input selection. The output is a binary real/fake classification label accompanied by a confidence score computed as 2|σ(z)−0.5|, where the final decision is the prediction with the highest confidence among the selected views.

## Method

IAPL dynamically adjusts the prompts fed into the CLIP ViT encoder for each test image, rather than fixing them after training. The image-adaptive prompt is constructed by fusing two components via a learnable scaling factor: (1) test-time adaptive tokens, which are optimized at inference on a single sample by minimizing averaged entropy across multiple augmented views to enforce prediction consistency, and (2) conditional information extracted by a Conditional Information Learner that selects the most texture-rich 32×32 patch via DCT scoring, applies high-pass filters, and passes it through two separate CNN extractors to produce forgery-specific and general conditions. The backbone is further adapted with fixed MLP-based adapters (inserted at equal intervals into 6 encoder blocks) and learnable tokens (applied from the 2nd to the 9th block), which provide a stable feature-extraction foundation while the dynamic prompts handle instance-specific forgery cues. At inference, an Optimal Input Selection step reuses the high-confidence views from tuning and selects the prediction with the highest confidence as the final decision.

## Results

On UniversalFakeDetect, IAPL achieves 95.61% mAcc and 99.32% mAP across 19 diverse sub-test sets, surpassing the previous SOTA (C2P-CLIP) by 1.82% in mAcc and RINE by 0.53% in mAP. On GenImage, the method reaches 96.7% mAcc, outperforming ATTSD by 6.0% and MiraGe by 4.1%. Compared to the CLIP-based baseline UniFD, IAPL yields gains of 14.23% mAcc on UniversalFakeDetect and 17.2% mAcc on GenImage, demonstrating strong generalization from a single training generator to 18+ unseen generators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAPL outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both datasets. On UniversalFakeDetect, it surpasses the previous SOTA C2P-CLIP [59] by 1.82% mAcc (95.61% vs. 93.79%) and RINE [29] by 0.53% mAP (99.32% vs. 98.79%), while achieving the largest margin over the CLIP-feature baseline UniFD [43] (+14.23% mAcc). On GenImage, IAPL leads ATTSD [3] by 6.0% (96.7% vs. 90.7%), MiraGe [52] by 4.1%, and C2P-CLIP by 0.9%. Notably, IAPL ranks first or second on 9 of 19 UniversalFakeDetect sub-test sets and 7 of 8 GenImage sub-test sets, whereas several baselines (e.g., AIDE, SAFE) show severe degradation on specific generator families such as DeepFakes or LDM, highlighting IAPL's more uniform generalization across diverse synthesis types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based AI-generated image detection line (UniFD → FatFormer → C2P-CLIP → RINE) by replacing fixed fine-tuned parameters with dynamically adjusted prompts at inference time. The test-time token tuning mechanism directly inherits from TPT's entropy-minimization over augmented views, while the conditional prompt design draws on CoCoOp's input-conditional tokens, and the lightweight backbone adaptation reuses AdaptFormer's MLP adapter architecture. The key conceptual departure from prior detection methods is that prompts are no longer frozen after training but are re-optimized per test image, combining a CNN-based Conditional Information Learner (high-pass filters + DCT-selected patches) with test-time adaptive tokens to produce instance-specific prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the central open problem in AI-generated image detection—generalization to unseen generators—by showing that instance-level prompt adaptation at inference time can close much of the gap between seen and unseen forgeries, as evidenced by the T-SNE separation and consistent top-2 rankings across 19 sub-test sets. A practical limitation is the added inference cost from generating 32 views, selecting 6 confident ones, and running 2 tuning steps per image, which may be prohibitive for real-time or large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D content, how it behaves under adversarial perturbations, and whether the conditional information learner's reliance on a single DCT-selected patch is robust to diverse artifact distributions from future generators.
