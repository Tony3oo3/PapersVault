## Problem

Existing prompt learning methods for Vision-Language Models are designed and evaluated exclusively on standard-resolution inputs (e.g., 224×224 for CLIP-ViT-B/16), yet real-world edge deployment requires processing low-resolution inputs with fewer visual tokens due to storage-memory constraints. This paper identifies that reducing input resolution causes dramatic performance degradation in state-of-the-art prompt learning methods, a challenge that remains unexplored in the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262911/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model accepts a single low-resolution image (φ ∈ {96², 144², 192²}) whose position embeddings are resized to produce fewer visual tokens, along with class names embedded in an attribute-structured text prompt. The output is a standard class prediction via cosine similarity between the projected visual embedding and per-class text embeddings. During training, two student networks simultaneously process the same image at standard and low resolutions, but only the low-resolution student's prediction is used for the task loss at inference.

## Method

LOREAL introduces a dual-student self-distillation framework where two frozen VLMs fed with different-resolution inputs share a set of LoRA-like cross-modality meta-nets that dynamically fill attribute slots in a contextualized prompt ('A photo of a [CLASS] with S₁ [Attr1] S₂ [Attr2]...'). The attributes are pre-generated by GPT-4o using chain-of-thought reasoning to identify resolution-robust visual properties (e.g., coat-color, body-shape) that remain salient under low resolution. Two distillation losses bridge the resolution gap: Low-Level Distillation (LLD) applies contrastive learning to align the meta-net-generated attribute contexts across the two students, while High-Level Distillation (HLD) aligns their output logits via KL-divergence. Only the meta-nets (~104.5K parameters) are trainable, making the approach highly parameter-efficient while adding negligible inference overhead (+1ms, +33MB).

## Results

On the LR-B2N benchmark (11 datasets, 16-shot), LOREAL as a plug-in module improves the Harmonic Mean (HM) of all four SOTA prompt-learning baselines across three resolution settings. At the most challenging φ=96², MMA+LOREAL achieves 63.14% HM (vs. 40.50% for MMA alone, +22.64%), and MaPLe+LOREAL reaches 57.25% HM (vs. 34.85%, +22.40%). At the near-standard φ=192², gains are more modest but still consistent (e.g., MMRL: 75.30%→79.14% HM). The additional cost is minimal: only +104.5K tunable parameters, +1ms inference time, and +33MB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOREAL is not a standalone method but a plug-in self-distillation module that is combined with four existing SOTA prompt-learning baselines (CoOp, MaPLe, MMA, MMRL), and it improves every one of them at every resolution setting. The gains are largest at the lowest resolution (φ=96²): MMA+LOREAL achieves 63.14% HM vs. 40.50% for MMA alone (+22.64%), and MaPLe+LOREAL reaches 57.25% vs. 34.85% (+22.40%). At φ=144², MMRL+LOREAL (86.39%) edges out MMA+LOREAL (85.97%) and MaPLe+LOREAL (86.20%), while at the near-standard φ=192² all methods converge within ~2% HM. The paper notes that multi-modal baselines (MaPLe, MMA, MMRL) benefit more from LOREAL than the uni-modal CoOp at low resolution (average +22.81% vs. +11.3% HM at 96²), likely because their cross-modal architecture better facilitates the meta-net's convergence. In terms of efficiency, LOREAL adds only 104.5K parameters and ~1ms inference overhead, making it a lightweight complement rather than a replacement for the backbone prompt-learning method.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOREAL sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) prompt learning for VLMs (CoOp, MaPLe, MMA, MMRL), which it treats as a plug-in wrapper rather than a replacement; (2) attribute-driven prompting (ATPrompt, ARGuE), which it extends by using LLM-generated resolution-robust attributes instead of generic ones; and (3) self-distillation / knowledge distillation, which it repurposes into a dual-student scheme where the two students differ only in input resolution. The key departure from prior LRR work (ResFormer, MSPE, PixelDistillation) is that those methods modify position embeddings or train heterogeneous networks, whereas LOREAL keeps the backbone frozen and addresses the resolution gap purely through prompt-level self-distillation with cross-modality meta-nets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically critical but previously unexamined failure mode: existing prompt-learning methods degrade sharply (up to ~50% HM loss) when input resolution drops to 96², a regime common on edge devices. LOREAL's plug-and-play design (only ~105K extra parameters, +1ms inference) makes it immediately deployable alongside any existing prompt method, which broadens its practical impact. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP-ViT-B/16 to larger or non-CLIP VLMs, whether the GPT-4o attribute-generation step can be replaced by a lighter model, and whether the dual-student self-distillation extends to other distribution shifts (e.g., domain, noise) beyond resolution.
