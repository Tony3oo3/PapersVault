## Problem

Existing subject-driven video generation methods condition on reference subjects for the full video duration, offering no mechanism to control when a subject appears or disappears. This limitation is critical for applications like storyboarding, advertising, and animation where different characters or objects must appear at user-specified timestamps, yet current models either ignore temporal prompts or bias generation toward continuous subject presence from start to end.

## Input / Output

The model accepts N subject reference images, each paired with a temporal interval [t0, t1] and a short entity word (e.g., 'dog', 'man'), along with a global text caption describing the scene. References are encoded with the same 3D VAE as the video latents and concatenated as additional tokens in the DiT sequence, so the number of input references is variable and unconstrained. The output is a generated video in which each subject's identity is preserved and its appearance is confined to the specified time window, with smooth transitions at interval boundaries.

## Method

AlcheMinT encodes each reference image with the native VAE and sequentially concatenates the resulting tokens with video tokens in a single DiT stream, eliminating the need for additional cross-attention modules or semantic encoders (CLIP, DINO, ArcFace). The key temporal control mechanism, WeRoPE, modifies the 3D RoPE applied to reference tokens by computing a weighted sum of sinusoidal frequencies at the interval midpoint (weight w_p) and at the interval edges (weight w_n, negative), producing an attention decay profile that is high within the target interval and smoothly decays outside it. For multi-reference disentanglement, the model adds per-reference learnable index embeddings and encodes entity word tags through the text encoder with a small MLP, binding caption words to specific reference images. Training uses a flow-matching objective on a rectified-flow DiT backbone, with a data pipeline that extracts per-entity timestamp intervals via Grounding DINO detection and SAM2 tracking, and samples reference frames far from target video frames to prevent copy-pasting.

## Results

On the S2VTime benchmark, AlcheMinT achieves the best timestamp-following metrics across both single- and multi-reference settings: t-L2 of 0.220 and t-IOU of 0.574 (single ref) and t-L2 of 0.235 and t-IOU of 0.552 (multi ref), outperforming the next-best baseline (VACE) by ~16% in t-L2 and ~7% in t-IOU for single reference. It also leads in identity preservation with CLIP_ref of 0.787 (single) and 0.776 (multi), and in text-video alignment with CLIP_text of 0.259 and 0.260. In the user study, AlcheMinT scores highest on Overall quality (3.996/5), ID preservation (4.300/5), and Timestamp following (3.673/5), while MAGREF edges it slightly on Motion quality (4.204 vs. 4.188).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlcheMinT wins on all four metrics (t-L2, t-IOU, CLIP_text, CLIP_ref) against every baseline in both single- and multi-reference settings. The largest gaps are in timestamp adherence: AlcheMinT's t-L2 of 0.220 beats VACE (0.262) by 16% and Alchemist (0.298) by 26% in the single-reference case, while its t-IOU of 0.574 exceeds VACE (0.537) by 7%. For identity preservation, AlcheMinT's CLIP_ref of 0.787 surpasses the previous best (Alchemist, 0.774) by 1.7%, and its CLIP_text of 0.259 edges out Alchemist (0.250) by 3.6%. In the multi-reference setting, the advantage over VACE narrows slightly (t-L2 0.235 vs. 0.263) but remains consistent across all metrics. In the user study, AlcheMinT leads on Overall, ID, and Timestamp scores, while MAGREF marginally outperforms it on Motion quality (4.204 vs. 4.188), suggesting the temporal control mechanism does not sacrifice motion naturalness. MiNT [50] and SDV-Gen [24] are the weakest baselines on timestamp metrics, confirming that prior time-control methods do not transfer well to the multi-reference subject-conditioning setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the subject-to-video (S2V) generation line of work (Video Alchemist, SkyReels-A2, Concept Master, MAGREF) by adding explicit timestamp intervals as a conditioning signal, a dimension entirely absent from prior S2V methods that bias subjects to appear across the full video duration. Architecturally, it departs from the dominant cross-attention paradigm (using CLIP, DINO, or ArcFace encoders with IP-Adapters or Q-formers) in favor of directly VAE-encoding reference images and concatenating their tokens with video tokens in a unified MM-DiT-style stream, eliminating additional cross-attention parameters. The temporal control mechanism (WeRoPE) generalizes MiNT's ReRoPE by supporting overlapping multi-subject intervals and remaining compatible with self-attention-only conditioning, whereas ReRoPE requires rescaling video RoPEs and only handles non-overlapping event sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AlcheMinT is the first framework to enable precise, per-subject temporal control in multi-reference video generation, opening practical applications in storyboarding, advertising, and animation where characters or logos must appear at user-specified times. The S2VTime benchmark fills a clear gap in evaluation by measuring timestamp adherence alongside identity preservation and video fidelity. Key limitations include the high training cost (1680 GB H100 GPUs, 30K iterations), the current restriction to at most two reference subjects in the benchmark, and the WeRoPE mechanism's dependence on RoPE-based DiT backbones. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos with many overlapping subjects, whether the VAE-concatenation strategy generalizes to non-RoPE architectures, and whether the observed tradeoff between timestamp precision and CLIP identity scores (visible in the WeRoPE ablation) can be resolved.
