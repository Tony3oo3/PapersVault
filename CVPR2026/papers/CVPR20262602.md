## Problem

In-the-wild ego-centric driving videos are abundant but lack poses, semantic labels, and LiDAR, making it difficult to learn representations that capture both 3D geometry and dynamic scene structure. Prior self-supervised driving pretraining methods rely on frame-to-frame consistency losses that implicitly assume static scenes, failing to model the dynamic objects and future evolution that are critical for safe, reactive driving. The paper asks whether a single feedforward model can learn a unified pseudo-4D representation—geometry, semantics, motion, and short-horizon future—directly from unposed, unlabeled YouTube driving video.

## Input / Output

The model consumes N consecutive unposed RGB frames from a single front-view camera (trained at 2, 5, and 10 Hz on ~2M OpenDV clips) and autoregressively produces outputs for N+M frames: per-pixel 3D point maps in a shared world frame, 4×4 camera pose matrices, 7-class semantic segmentation, per-pixel confidence scores, and binary motion masks distinguishing dynamic agents from static environment. For the planning fine-tuning stage, the frozen encoder processes 3 front-view frames and a lightweight anchor-based decoder outputs multi-mode future trajectories with confidence scores. Notably, the student only observes the first N frames while the teacher has access to all N+M, forcing the model to extrapolate geometry and motion beyond what it has seen.

## Method

LFG extends the π³ feedforward reconstruction backbone (~1B params) with a 4-layer causal autoregressive transformer (8 heads) that rolls out M future latent scene tokens, which a shared decoder maps to point maps, poses, semantics, confidence, and motion masks. Supervision is provided by multiple specialized teachers in a distillation setup: a π³ teacher (seeing all N+M frames) supplies point-map, confidence, and pose targets; a Cityscapes-trained SegFormer provides soft semantic pseudo-labels; and a pipeline combining Grounded SAM2 instance detection with CoTracker3 2D tracking and π³ 3D back-projection generates per-pixel motion masks by thresholding 3D displacement. Training proceeds in three end-to-end stages (future geometry/pose → segmentation → motion) with a composite loss of weighted BCE for segmentation and motion, geodesic rotation plus Huber translation for poses, scaled L1 for point maps, and BCE for confidence, with future-frame losses upweighted by ω=10. The resulting 1.45B-parameter encoder is then frozen and paired with a lightweight multi-modal anchor-based trajectory decoder for NAVSIM planning, achieving state-of-the-art PDMS with a single front camera.

## Results

On the NAVSIM planning benchmark, LFG achieves 85.2 PDMS using only a single front-view camera (3-frame input), outperforming multi-camera BEV baselines UniAD (83.4, 6 cameras) and Hydra-MDP (84.7, 3 cameras + LiDAR), though trailing DiffusionDrive (88.1, 3 cameras + LiDAR). In the data-efficiency setting, LFG reaches 81.4 PDMS at just 10% labeled data—matching DINOv3's full-data score—and 66.3 PDMS at 1%, surpassing all other pretrained encoders. On semantic segmentation (KITTI-360), LFG achieves 0.768 mIoU overall and 0.751 mIoU on future frames, beating its SegFormer teacher (0.677 mIoU) and MaskFormer (0.760 mIoU overall, no future prediction). For monocular depth on KITTI-360, LFG reports 0.27 AbsRel and 4.38 RMSE, on par with the π³ backbone (0.26 / 4.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIM planning, LFG (1 camera) outperforms UniAD (6 cameras) by 1.8 PDMS and Hydra-MDP (3 cameras + LiDAR) by 0.5 PDMS, while trailing DiffusionDrive (3 cameras + LiDAR) by 2.9 PDMS—though LFG uses a fraction of the sensor modalities. In the data-efficiency comparison, LFG dominates all other pretrained encoders at every label fraction: at 10% data it reaches 81.4 PDMS versus 77.5 (π³), 75.8 (DINOv3), and 65.6 (PPGeo), and at 1% it scores 66.3 versus 56.2 (π³) and 60.0 (DINOv3). For semantic segmentation on KITTI-360, LFG surpasses its SegFormer teacher by 9.1 mIoU points (0.768 vs. 0.677) and edges out MaskFormer (0.760) while additionally predicting future frames (0.751 mIoU on frames 4–6) that MaskFormer cannot produce. On monocular depth, LFG is essentially tied with π³ (0.27 vs. 0.26 AbsRel on KITTI-360) and slightly ahead of VGGT (4.38 vs. 4.46 RMSE), confirming that the autoregressive and semantic/motion heads do not degrade geometric quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LFG extends the feedforward 3D reconstruction paradigm of π³ (and its predecessor VGGT) by appending a lightweight causal autoregressive transformer that predicts future geometry, semantics, and motion, moving beyond the frame-to-frame consistency objectives of prior self-supervised driving pretraining (SelfD, ACO, PPGeo, UniPAD, VisionPAD) that implicitly assume static scenes. Rather than relying on purely self-supervised photometric or contrastive signals, LFG adopts a multi-teacher distillation strategy—SegFormer for semantics, Grounded SAM2 + CoTracker3 for motion, and π³ itself for geometry—converting unlabeled YouTube driving video into dense pseudo-4D supervision. The authors explicitly position the contribution as a pretraining paradigm (label-free, teacher-guided, video-centric) rather than a novel architecture, building on DINOv2-initialized encoders and the π³ alternating-attention backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single front-camera encoder pretrained on ~2M unlabeled YouTube clips can match or exceed multi-camera + LiDAR BEV planners (UniAD, Hydra-MDP) on NAVSIM challenges the prevailing assumption that rich sensor suites are necessary for competitive planning, and the 10%-label data-efficiency finding (81.4 PDMS matching DINOv3's full-data score) suggests large-scale video pretraining can substantially reduce annotation costs. Key limitations include the short prediction horizon (3–6 frames), reliance on a single front view, and the fact that the best end-to-end baseline (DiffusionDrive, 88.1 PDMS) still outperforms LFG (85.2). Open questions include whether multi-view in-the-wild video (e.g., the newly released PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles dataset) and longer autoregressive horizons can close the remaining gap and whether the pseudo-4D representation generalizes beyond the NAVSIM simulation to reactive, closed-loop driving.
