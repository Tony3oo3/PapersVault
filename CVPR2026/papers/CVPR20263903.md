## Problem

Dynamic resolution sampling accelerates diffusion transformers by performing early steps at lower resolution, but existing methods inject independent noise at every resolution transition, breaking cross-stage semantic consistency and forcing the model to relearn global structure. Additionally, these methods upsample the entire latent space indiscriminately without assessing which spatial regions have actually converged, causing aliasing and fragmented geometry.

## Input / Output

Fresco takes a standard text prompt and produces a generated image (evaluated at 1024×1024, 1440×1440, and 2048×2048 on FLUX) or a 125-frame video at 720×1280 (on HunyuanVideo). The method is entirely training-free and operates as a drop-in replacement for the standard sampling loop: it begins denoising at a reduced resolution and progressively upsamples tokens, so the user-facing interface is identical to the base model while the internal computation is redistributed across resolutions.

## Method

Fresco introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, a Token-Encoded Unified Noise Field assigns each latent token a fixed Gaussian vector determined by a hash of its spatial coordinate and feature index, so every resolution transition re-noises from the same shared field rather than sampling fresh noise, preserving a continuous stochastic trajectory (formally proven to yield smaller trajectory error than independent re-noising). Second, a variance-guided progressive upsampling scheme tracks each token's channel variance across timesteps; tokens whose variance drops below a threshold τ are promoted to higher resolution for detail refinement, while unstable tokens continue denoising at low resolution. The upsampling itself uses a 4×4 Hadamard matrix to expand each parent token into four children with controlled orthogonal perturbations, injecting fine-scale stochastic degrees of freedom while preserving coarse semantics. The framework is orthogonal to distillation and feature caching, reaching 22× speedup when combined with step-distilled FLUX.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (1024×1024, DrawBench 200 prompts), Fresco achieves a 10.27× FLOPs speedup at 9 NFE while maintaining an ImageReward of 0.9825 (+0.91%) and CLIP Score of 32.426 (+0.07%) over the original 50-step model. At moderate acceleration (30 NFE), Fresco reaches 2.87× FLOPs speedup with ImageReward 1.0527 (+8.13%) and CLIP Score 32.521 (+0.36%), surpassing all baselines at comparable efficiency. On HunyuanVideo (720×1280, 125 frames, VBench), Fresco achieves a 4.92× FLOPs speedup at 18 NFE with a total score of 80.76 (+0.80%), outperforming TeaCache (78.96) and Jenga-ProRes (79.16). Combined with step distillation, Fresco reaches 22.10× acceleration while retaining CLIP-IQA of 0.8693 and CLIP Score of 32.563.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fresco outperforms all dynamic-resolution baselines (Bottleneck Sampling, RALU) on both ImageReward and CLIP Score at every comparable NFE setting, e.g., at 30 NFE Fresco scores 1.0527 vs. 0.9739 (Bottleneck) and 0.9644 (RALU) on ImageReward. Against feature-caching methods, Fresco at 18 NFE (4.72× FLOPs speedup) surpasses TaylorSeer at 50 NFE (3.84×) on ImageReward (1.0369 vs. 0.9857) while also achieving higher CLIP Score (32.581 vs. 32.413). At extreme acceleration, Fresco at 9 NFE achieves ImageReward 0.9825, exceeding both the original FLUX.1-dev (0.9736) and the step-distilled FLUX.1-schnell (0.9097), while delivering 10.27× FLOPs speedup versus 6.25× for schnell. On HunyuanVideo, Fresco at 18 NFE achieves a VBench total score of 80.76, beating TeaCache (78.96), AdaCache (79.58), Bottleneck Sampling (79.42), and Jenga-ProRes (79.16) at comparable or higher speedups. When combined with step distillation, Fresco reaches 22.10× FLOPs speedup with CLIP-IQA 0.8693, outperforming FLUX.1-schnell at 4 NFE (13.11×, CLIP-IQA 0.8771) on CLIP Score (32.563 vs. 34.056) while being faster in latency (1.87s vs. 2.77s). Fresco also integrates with model distillation (FLUX.1-lite-8B), achieving 10.03× FLOPs speedup with CLIP-IQA 0.9518 (+4.48% over the distilled baseline), confirming orthogonality with existing acceleration techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fresco departs from prior dynamic resolution sampling methods (Bottleneck Sampling, RALU) that inject independent, stage-specific noise at each resolution transition, replacing those heuristic re-noising schedules with a token-encoded unified noise field that provides a single deterministic reference across all stages. It also diverges from cascade diffusion frameworks that require learned upsampling modules or retraining, instead using a training-free Hadamard-based spatial expansion guided by per-token channel variance. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to and composable with feature caching (TaylorSeer, TeaCache) and distillation (step- and model-distillation), rather than competing with them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A training-free, composable acceleration layer achieving 10× on FLUX and 22× when stacked with step distillation is practically significant for real-time and resource-constrained deployment of large diffusion transformers. The observation that Fresco can *improve* ImageReward over the unaccelerated baseline (1.0527 vs. 0.9736) challenges the common assumption that any speedup must trade quality, though the effect is metric-specific and the CLIP score gain is marginal. Open questions include how the variance threshold τ generalizes across architectures and prompt distributions, whether the Hadamard upsampling remains optimal at very large spatial scales, and whether the unified noise field's theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) holds under classifier-free guidance or multi-modal conditioning.
