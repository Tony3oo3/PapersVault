## Problem

Aligning exocentric video with ambient sensors for human action recognition remains unexplored, and the Global Alignment approach prevalent in egocentric-wearable settings fails in this new setting for two reasons: it cannot capture subtle temporal details when visual changes are minimal against static backgrounds, and it over-relies on modality-invariant temporal features, distorting the relationship between hard and easy negatives that share similar motion signatures but differ in spatial context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263522/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized exocentric video clips (10 fps on Opportunity++, 25 fps on HWU-USP) and ambient sensor sequences (30 Hz object-attached IMUs/logic switches or 50 Hz logic switches/motion sensors) in 2-second sliding windows with 1-second overlap. It produces per-modality spatial embeddings (d_S-dim) and temporal embeddings (d_T-dim) that are concatenated into unified representations; downstream, a linear classifier on frozen sensor features yields action class predictions (14 mid-level open/close classes on Opportunity++, 5 high-level classes on HWU-USP). Unlike egocentric-wearable settings, the sensors are environment-centric (attached to objects or walls) rather than body-worn, and the video comes from fixed cameras rather than head-mounted devices.

## Method

DETACH decomposes both modalities into spatial and temporal components and aligns them in two stages. Stage 1 learns spatial representations via online deep clustering (ODC) on sensor channel activations to discover location-specific patterns (K=7 clusters), then trains a video spatial encoder on high-confidence sensor pseudo-labels (75th-percentile distance criterion), followed by video-guided refinement to correct ambiguous sensor cluster assignments. Stage 2 freezes spatial features and aligns temporal representations using a spatial-temporal weighted contrastive loss: hard negatives sharing the same spatial context are up-weighted (λ_hard=3.0) to force fine-grained temporal discrimination, while false negatives with high joint spatial-temporal similarity are down-weighted to prevent incorrect repulsion. Temporal similarity is estimated via momentum encoders (EMA m=0.999) for stability, and the final bidirectional InfoNCE loss operates on concatenated spatial+temporal embeddings with per-pair adaptive weights.

## Results

DETACH achieves state-of-the-art performance on both benchmarks: 0.73 weighted F1 and 0.87 mAP on Opportunity++, and 0.73 weighted F1 and 0.67 mAP on HWU-USP. Compared to the strongest baseline EVI-MAE, DETACH improves by +30.4% in weighted F1 and +50.0% in mAP on Opportunity++, and by +21.7% in F1 and +21.8% in mAP on HWU-USP. The gains are consistent across all four evaluation metrics, with the largest relative improvement observed on Opportunity++ mAP where DETACH nearly doubles the best baseline score (0.87 vs. 0.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DETACH outperforms all four adapted egocentric-wearable baselines on every metric across both datasets. EVI-MAE [53] is the strongest baseline, benefiting from joint contrastive and masked autoencoding objectives, yet still trails DETACH by 17 points in F1 and 29 points in mAP on Opportunity++. COMODO [7] (cross-modal distillation) is the second-best on HWU-USP mAP (0.64) but lags DETACH by 23 points in F1 on Opportunity++. IMU2CLIP [30] and PRIMUS [11], both contrastive methods originally designed for egocentric-wearable alignment, show the largest gaps, with PRIMUS achieving only 0.28 F1 on Opportunity++—less than half of DETACH's score. The consistent superiority of DETACH across both datasets and all metrics confirms that the spatial-temporal decomposition strategy addresses the fundamental limitations of Global Alignment in exocentric-ambient settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Global Alignment paradigm in multimodal HAR (IMU2CLIP, PRIMUS, COMODO, EVI-MAE), which encodes entire video clips and sensor windows into single vectors for contrastive or distillation-based alignment. While effective for egocentric-wearable settings where user motion inherently synchronizes both modalities, the authors argue this approach structurally fails for exocentric-ambient settings due to (P1) static backgrounds overwhelming subtle temporal dynamics and (P2) over-reliance on modality-invariant temporal features that distort negative relationships. Conceptually, the paper extends the fine-grained semantic decomposition paradigm from text-video retrieval (DiCoSA, ProST) to the video-sensor domain, and adapts Online Deep Clustering (ODC) to convert implicit sensor channel activations into explicit spatial prototypes, establishing a cross-modal spatial grounding that prior egocentric-wearable methods did not require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally define and solve the exocentric-ambient alignment problem, addressing practical deployment barriers (privacy, user burden, multi-user scalability) that limit egocentric-wearable systems. The two-stage spatial-then-temporal decomposition provides a general recipe for cross-modal alignment when spatial context is the primary discriminator rather than temporal synchrony, a principle potentially transferable to other sensor-video settings. However, the evaluation is limited to two small single-user datasets (5K and 3K samples) with only motion-dynamics sensors (IMUs, logic switches), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to richer ambient sensor types (audio, light), multi-user scenarios, or larger-scale deployments.
