## Problem

Real-time physics-based animation that generalizes across diverse 3D shapes and discretizations remains unsolved. Existing neural subspace methods require per-object training, while neural rigging/skinning approaches depend on costly expert-annotated data and lack physical constraints. The central question is how to learn a physics-consistent deformation subspace mapping that generalizes across object categories, topologies, and resolutions directly from static geometries without any annotated data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261418/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static 3D mesh as input, from which 4096 surface points (coordinates + normals) are sampled for shape encoding and ~100k cubature points (surface + interior via 128³ voxel grid ray tracing) are sampled for physics computation. It outputs continuous neural skinning fields — m spatially varying weight functions W(X) ∈ R^m defined at any point in space — which are discretization-agnostic and can be evaluated at arbitrary query points. At inference, the skinning fields drive real-time subspace dynamics via implicit time integration over 12m handle coordinates, producing full-space deformations without requiring a fixed mesh topology.

## Method

PhysSkin employs a Neural Skinning Fields Autoencoder: a frozen pretrained Michelangelo transformer encoder extracts shape latents from surface points, and a cross-attention decoder uses m learnable handle tokens to extract handle latents, then spatial queries attend to handle latents to produce point-wise features decoded by an MLP with an ONI orthogonalization layer. Training uses a Physics-Informed Self-Supervised Learning (PISSL) strategy with three competing losses — potential energy minimization (linearly interpolated from linear elasticity to Neo-Hookean), spatial smoothness, and orthogonality — optimized without any annotated skinning data or simulation trajectories. Two key mechanisms ensure stable convergence: on-the-fly ℓ2-normalization of skinning mode columns to prevent numerical drift, and ConFIG conflict-aware gradient correction to resolve destructive interference among the energy, smoothness, and orthogonality gradients. The cubature points sampling (both surface and volumetric interior) captures the full 3D deformation behavior that surface-only samplers miss, enabling physically consistent skinning fields that generalize to unseen shapes in a single feed-forward pass.

## Results

On the RigNet test set (unseen objects), PhysSkin achieves Ω_orth = 0.0033×10⁻², κ_log = 1.0453, and H_spec = 0.9999, outperforming all supervised baselines by large margins (e.g., 160× lower orthogonality error vs. RigNet's 0.5324×10⁻²). On ShapeNet, PhysSkin similarly dominates with Ω_orth = 0.0098×10⁻² and κ_log = 1.0460, beating the closest self-supervised baseline Simplicits (0.2621×10⁻², 1.5205). For animation speed, PhysSkin achieves ~12–14 ms per simulation step regardless of mesh complexity (10K–127K vertices), compared to 79–3171 ms for FEM and 141–252 ms for MPM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysSkin outperforms all four supervised rigging baselines (RigNet, M-I-A, Anymate, Puppeteer) on every metric, with the most dramatic gap in orthogonality (Ω_orth = 0.0033 vs. 0.53–1.57×10⁻²) and condition number (κ_log = 1.05 vs. 2.6–27.7). Against the closest self-supervised competitor Simplicits (per-object training), PhysSkin achieves 79× lower Ω_orth (0.0098 vs. 0.2621×10⁻² on ShapeNet) and 31% lower κ_log (1.046 vs. 1.521), while also generalizing across shapes with a single model. For animation speed, PhysSkin's ~12–14 ms per step is 6.5× faster than LICROM's FEM (79.83 ms on a 10K-vertex airplane) and 11.6× faster than the MPM-based neural stress fields method (141.83 ms), with the gap widening to >200× on high-vertex-count shapes (e.g., 3012 ms FEM vs. 13.39 ms PhysSkin on a 121K-vertex bag). Notably, PhysSkin is the only method that is simultaneously self-supervised, category-agnostic, and discretization-agnostic, whereas all supervised baselines require expert-annotated rigging data and Simplicits requires per-object training.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysSkin sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: subspace physics-based animation (FastCody, CROM), which reduces full-space dynamics to a low-dimensional coordinate update, and neural rigging/skinning (RigNet, Anymate, Puppeteer), which learns deformation weights from geometry. It departs from the supervised rigging methods by eliminating the need for expert-annotated skinning data, and from per-object self-supervised methods like Simplicits by training a single generalizable transformer-based autoencoder (Michelangelo encoder + cross-attention decoder) that produces mesh-free, discretization-agnostic skinning fields. The physics-informed self-supervised training strategy borrows ConFIG for gradient conflict resolution and ONI for orthogonality, but applies them in a new context where energy, smoothness, and orthogonality constraints must be jointly satisfied across diverse shapes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single model trained purely from static geometry can produce physically consistent, orthogonal skinning fields for arbitrary 3D shapes in ~12 ms per animation step, compared to 80–3000 ms for full-space FEM/MPM solvers, and the discretization-agnostic formulation extends naturally to 3D Gaussian Splatting representations. The key open question is whether the framework can incorporate semantic priors (e.g., part-level structure) to handle complex articulated geometries, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. Broader implications include the potential to replace per-object reduced-order model construction in production animation pipelines with a single feed-forward inference, though validation on contact, fracture, or multi-body scenarios remains unexplored.
