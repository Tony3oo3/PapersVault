## Problem

Diffusion Probabilistic Models suffer from an SNR-t bias: during inference, the actual signal-to-noise ratio of a denoising sample systematically deviates from the SNR prescribed by its assigned timestep, because accumulated network prediction errors and numerical discretization errors break the strict SNR–timestep coupling that holds during training. Unlike previously studied exposure bias (which is inter-sample), this bias is a more fundamental intra-sample misalignment that causes the network to produce overestimated noise predictions at every denoising step, degrading generation quality.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, plug-and-play correction applied during the sampling loop of any pre-trained diffusion model. It operates on the intermediate denoising samples (RGB images at 32×32 through 256×256) and the model's own reconstruction output at each step, requiring no additional network forward passes (zero extra NFE). For text-to-image models such as FLUX and Qwen-Image, the input is a text prompt plus Gaussian noise, and the output is the final generated image; the correction is inserted between consecutive denoising steps without altering the model weights or the noise schedule.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 5.1) that the SNR of a reverse-process sample x̂_t is always strictly lower than the forward-process SNR at the same timestep, with the gap governed by a reconstruction-loss coefficient γ_t and a noise coefficient φ_t. To correct this, at each denoising step the method computes the differential signal x̂_{t-1} − x_θ⁰(x̂_t, t), which analytically contains a directional component pointing toward the ideal perturbed sample x_{t-1}, and adds a scaled version of this signal back to x̂_{t-1} (Eq. 17). To exploit the known low-frequency-first denoising behavior of DPMs, the correction is performed in the wavelet domain via DWT, applying separate dynamic weights to the low-frequency (LL) and high-frequency (LH, HL, HH) subbands: the low-frequency weight grows with the reverse variance σ_t (stronger early in sampling), while the high-frequency weight grows as σ_t shrinks (stronger late in sampling). The entire procedure adds less than 0.5% runtime overhead and is compatible with existing bias-correction methods such as ADM-ES and DPM-FR, yielding further FID reductions on top of them.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with the DDIM sampler, DCW reduces FID from 14.40 to 9.36 (10 steps), 6.87 to 4.64 (20 steps), and 4.15 to 3.33 (50 steps), outperforming DPM-AE (13.98/6.76/4.10) and DPM-AT (15.88/6.60/3.34) at all step counts. For deterministic sampling with EDM, DCW achieves a 47.1% FID reduction at 13 NFE (10.66→5.67) and 47.4% at 21 NFE (5.91→3.37). Across resolutions, DCW reduces FID by 42.6% for IDDPM on CIFAR-10 (13.19→7.57, T=20) and by 41% for IDDPM on LSUN 256 (18.69→11.03, T=20), with negligible computational overhead (<0.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCW consistently outperforms the two most recent competing bias-correction methods: on CIFAR-10 with DDIM, DCW achieves FID 9.36/4.64/3.33 (10/20/50 steps) versus DPM-AE's 13.98/6.76/4.10; with ADM, DCW reaches 13.01/5.59/2.95 versus DPM-AT's 15.88/6.60/3.34. Against the SOTA exposure-bias model DPM-FR [63], DCW still yields further gains (e.g., A-DPM-FR 12.38→10.91 at 10 steps; PFGM++-FR 6.62→6.18 at 13 NFE), demonstrating that SNR-t bias and exposure bias are complementary failure modes. Similarly, DCW improves upon ADM-ES [38] (e.g., EDM-ES 6.59→6.13 at 13 NFE; PFGM++-ES 8.79→8.00 at 13 NFE). For the classic baselines (IDDPM, ADM, ADM-IP, DDIM, A-DPM, EA-DPM, EDM, PFGM++), DCW delivers large absolute FID reductions across all resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (LSUN Bedroom), with the largest relative gains (42–47%) observed at low step counts where discretization errors are most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying SNR-t bias as a distinct and more fundamental problem than the previously studied exposure bias in diffusion models. While exposure bias (ADM-IP, ADM-ES, TS-DPM, DPM-AE, DPM-AT, DPM-FR) addresses inter-sample discrepancies between training and inference, SNR-t bias captures the misalignment between a sample's actual SNR and its assigned timestep, which the authors argue can induce exposure bias. The proposed differential correction method is conceptually related to guidance strategies (e.g., classifier-free guidance, minority guidance) but repurposes the inherent prediction-reconstruction difference as a correction signal, and extends it into the wavelet domain to exploit DPMs' known low-to-high-frequency denoising progression. The theoretical analysis departs from prior assumptions (ADM-ES, TS-DPM) by adopting a more general reconstruction model with an energy-loss coefficient γ_t, which the authors argue is better grounded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of SNR-t bias as a systematic, theoretically provable phenomenon affecting all DPMs during inference is a meaningful conceptual advance, and the training-free, plug-and-play correction with <0.5% overhead makes it immediately practical for a wide range of diffusion-based systems. The ability to stack DCW on top of existing exposure-bias corrections (ADM-ES, DPM-FR) for additional FID gains suggests these biases are partially independent, opening the door to further compositional improvements. Open questions include whether the wavelet decomposition is optimal compared to other frequency representations (e.g., Fourier, learned bases), whether the method extends cleanly to video and audio generation where temporal frequency structure differs, and whether the Assumption 5.1 (bounded reconstruction with energy loss) holds universally across all architectures and conditioning regimes.
