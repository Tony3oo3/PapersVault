## Problem

Existing brain MRI modality imputation methods rely on global conditioning signals (binary task indicators, learnable modality queries) or modality-specific decoder branches, which fail to capture region-level cross-modality variability and scale poorly as the number of modalities grows. This limits generalisability across diverse patient cohorts and imaging protocols, making unified any-to-any imputation impractical in clinical workflows where scan time and contrast-agent constraints prevent acquiring the full modality set.

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of 2D brain MRI slices (256×256 on IXI, 240×240 on BraTS 2023) with min-max normalised intensities in [0,1], where missing modalities are zero-padded to form a multi-channel input tensor. It outputs the imputed missing modality as a single-channel image of the same spatial resolution. The framework is modality-agnostic: any subset of the N available modalities (T1, T2, PD, FLAIR, T1Gd) can be provided, and any missing modality can be synthesised, covering all one-to-one and many-to-one imputation scenarios without architectural changes.

## Method

CodeBrain reformulates any-to-any MRI imputation as a region-level code prediction problem in a finite scalar-quantised (FSQ) latent space. In Stage I, a posterior encoder compresses the full modality set into a 6-channel feature map that is element-wise quantised via FSQ with levels L=[8,8,8,5,5,5] (64,000 total codes), while a shared encoder extracts modality-agnostic common features from the incomplete input; a decoder reconstructs all modalities from the concatenation of quantised codes and common features, trained with a differentiable PSNR loss and LSGAN loss. In Stage II, a prior encoder (NAFNet backbone) predicts the full-stack code map from incomplete modalities, supervised not by cross-entropy but by a grading-based ordinal regression loss that converts each scalar code into a sequence of ordered binary decisions, preserving the smooth clustering structure of the quantised space. At inference, predicted codes are decoded with extracted common features to synthesise the target modality, requiring no modality-specific modules.

## Results

On IXI, CodeBrain achieves a mean PSNR of 29.50 dB, SSIM of 93.05%, and MAE of 17.57×10⁻³, outperforming the second-best method (Zhang et al.) by 0.50 dB in PSNR and 0.42% in SSIM. On BraTS 2023, CodeBrain reaches 25.31 dB PSNR, 90.49% SSIM, and 21.01×10⁻³ MAE, again leading all baselines across both O→O and M→O imputation scenarios. In the downstream brain tumour segmentation task on BraTS 2023, CodeBrain-imputed modalities yield a mean 3D Dice of 80.06%, approaching the full-modality upper bound of 87.40% and surpassing the next-best imputation method (MMT) by nearly 1 percentage point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CodeBrain outperforms all five baselines on every metric for both IXI and BraTS 2023. The closest competitor is Zhang et al. [49] (modality-shared/specific modules), which CodeBrain surpasses by 0.50 dB PSNR and 0.42% SSIM on IXI, and by 0.30 dB PSNR and 0.51% SSIM on BraTS 2023. Among the GAN-based (MMGAN [34]) and diffusion-based (M2DN [27]) approaches, CodeBrain leads by 1.86 dB and 1.36 dB in mean PSNR on IXI respectively, indicating that the quantised-code formulation captures cross-modality structure more effectively than pixel-level adversarial or diffusion objectives. The transformer-based MMT [26] and the mixture-of-experts VAE MMHVAE [10] trail CodeBrain by roughly 1.4–1.9 dB PSNR on IXI. In the downstream segmentation experiment, CodeBrain's imputed T1Gd modality achieves 61.44% Dice versus 57.25% for MMT and 47.35% for Zhang et al., confirming that the imputation quality translates into clinically meaningful gains for contrast-enhanced tumour delineation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CodeBrain builds directly on Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) [28] as its core representation mechanism, repurposing a technique originally developed for image compression into a cross-modality medical imaging setting. It departs from prior unified MRI imputation methods (MMGAN, MMT, M2DN, Zhang et al., MMHVAE) that rely on global binary task indicators, learnable modality queries, or modality-specific decoder branches, instead reformulating the any-to-any imputation problem as a region-level quantised code prediction task. The grading-based (ordinal regression) loss for code prediction is a novel loss design that exploits the inherent ordinal structure of the quantised code space, distinguishing it from both classification-based and continuous-regression alternatives. The overall architecture uses NAFNet as a backbone with LSGAN supervision, placing it within the CNN+GAN family while introducing vector-quantisation as the bridging mechanism between available and missing modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, modality-agnostic solution to a clinically important problem: acquiring a full MRI stack is time-consuming, costly, and sometimes contraindicated (e.g., gadolinium injection for T1Gd). By eliminating modality-specific modules, CodeBrain scales naturally to new modalities or datasets without architectural redesign, and the quantised code space provides an interpretable, compact intermediate representation that could bridge synthesis and perception tasks. However, the model operates on 2D slices, may still produce hallucinated structures, and does not incorporate MRI physics—particularly relevant for contrast-enhanced modalities. Open questions include whether the quantised-code paradigm extends to 3D volumetric modelling, whether physics-informed constraints can reduce hallucination, and whether the code space's natural clustering can be leveraged for downstream tasks like segmentation without additional training.
