## Problem

Prototype-based few-shot learning methods are highly vulnerable to noisy support sets, where the dominant 'Filter-Compress' strategy suffers from two structural flaws: Representation Confusion (shared high-variance features conflated with discriminative unique features in a single mean point) and Information Destruction (hard positives discarded alongside true noise). This paper addresses both by proposing a 'Filter-Repair-Expand' paradigm that repairs rather than discards corrupted samples and constructs diverse prototype subspaces instead of fragile points.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy support set of K images per class (10-shot protocol with varying ways) and query images, both processed through a pre-trained vision backbone (Swin Transformer by default) to extract feature embeddings. The support set may be contaminated with OOD samples or symmetric label-swapped noise at rates up to 70%. Output is a predicted class label via a subspace-reconstruction classifier, alongside learned disentangled prototype subspaces: a shared subspace V_sh capturing cross-class commonalities and per-class unique subspaces V_un^(c) capturing discriminative residuals.

## Method

DDSF implements a three-stage pipeline grounded in Determinantal Point Process theory. First, a DPP-based anomaly score s_k = p_k/q_k (fusing marginal probability for diversity and a cohesion score for quality) identifies high-interference samples without discarding them. Second, a parameter-free DDPM reverse process guided by the DPP volume gradient (Eq. 5–6) repairs identified anomalies by transforming corrupted features toward directions that maximize collective diversity with the clean set. Third, a synergistic disentanglement module expands a shared subspace via reconstruction loss L_sh and class-unique subspaces via DPP volume maximization on residuals L_un, jointly structuring the feature space. Classification at inference uses a subspace-reconstruction rule: the query is assigned to the class whose combined projection (P_sh + P_un^(c)) yields minimum reconstruction error, replacing conventional point-prototype distance.

## Results

On the OOD-polluted Meta-Dataset (varied-way 10-shot protocol), DDSF with SwinT backbone achieves 91.9% accuracy at 0% noise, outperforming the strongest same-backbone baseline DETA++ (86.5%) by +5.4 points. The robustness advantage grows substantially with noise: at 50% OOD contamination, DDSF reaches 75.9% versus DETA++'s 64.2% (+11.7), and at 70% noise the gap widens to +14.6 points (61.6% vs. 47.0%). Across five different backbones (Table 2), DDSF maintains above 82.2% accuracy even at 30% noise, with CLIP achieving the peak of 93.1% clean and 86.1% at 30% noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDSF outperforms all 16 baseline configurations across every noise level (0%–70%). The most direct comparison is against DETA++ [53] (same SwinT backbone): DDSF leads by +5.4% at 0% noise and the gap expands to +14.6% at 70% noise, demonstrating that the Filter-Repair-Expand paradigm degrades far more gracefully under contamination. Against the next-best same-backbone method SemFew-Trans [51] (SwinT), DDSF gains +10.0% at 0% and +21.0% at 70% noise. Among cross-backbone competitors, DDSF surpasses LDC [19] (CLIP) by +6.7% at 0% and +17.0% at 70%, and ECER-FSL [24] (Visformer-T) by +6.7% at 0% and +17.8% at 70%. Even the strongest full fine-tuning baseline (DeiT, 82.5% at 0%) trails DDSF by 9.4 points, and adapter-based methods (TSA, eTT) fall 11–16 points behind. The paper also shows DDSF generalizes across five backbones (Table 2), with CLIP yielding the best absolute performance (93.1% clean), confirming the framework is not tied to a specific architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'Filter-Compress' paradigm in robust few-shot learning (exemplified by DETA++ and TraNFS), which identifies and discards noisy support samples before constructing a point prototype. Instead, DDSF proposes a 'Filter-Repair-Expand' pipeline that preserves information by repairing corrupted features via a DPP-guided diffusion process and then expanding the representation into disentangled shared and class-unique subspaces. The unifying use of Determinantal Point Process theory across three distinct algorithmic roles—probabilistic anomaly scoring, volume-maximizing diffusion guidance, and subspace volume expansion—is the paper's central conceptual contribution, building on the DPP literature (Kulesza et al.) and DDPM (Ho et al.) but combining them in a way not previously explored for FSL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'repair rather than discard' paradigm addresses a genuine limitation of prior robust FSL methods: the inadvertent loss of hard positives that carry critical discriminative information. The finding that explicit foreground masking (U-2-Net) provides negligible improvement over the unmasked framework suggests the synergistic disentanglement losses already achieve implicit background invariance, which could simplify practical pipelines. However, evaluation is confined to Meta-Dataset, and the computational overhead of the diffusion-based repair step (even if parameter-free) remains unquantified relative to simpler baselines. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to larger-scale benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-based FSL), whether the repair module handles adversarial or backdoor noise, and how the method scales with increasing shot counts or way counts.
