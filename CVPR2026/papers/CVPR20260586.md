## Problem

Automatic echocardiography video segmentation is hindered by speckle noise that obscures anatomical boundaries, large spatiotemporal shape variations of the left ventricle across the cardiac cycle, and the scarcity of pixel-level annotations (typically limited to end-diastolic and end-systolic frames). Existing semi-supervised and pseudo-labeling approaches either propagate early prediction errors, rely on optical flow that is unreliable under heavy noise, or fail to leverage unlabeled frames for robust temporal representation learning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes uniformly sampled 10-frame echocardiography video sequences (256×256 or 128×128 resolution) with ground-truth masks available only on the first (ED) and last (ES) frames. It outputs per-frame left ventricular endocardium segmentation masks, from which clinical metrics (ejection fraction, end-diastolic/systolic volumes) are computed via Simpson's rule. The semi-supervised setting means 8 of 10 frames per sequence are unlabeled during training, and the model must generalize to all intermediate frames at test time.

## Method

EchoForge combines two modules atop a ResNet-50 backbone. The Anchor Semantic Awareness (ASA) module first performs Anchor Recalibration (ARC): learnable foreground/background anchors are initialized via softmax-weighted pooling of encoder features, refined through KNN neighbor-feature fusion, and then used to linearly interpolate uncertain patches (cosine-similarity confidence in [0.4, 0.6]) toward the more confident anchor, suppressing speckle noise. Temporal Semantic Fusion (TSF) then extracts semantic prototypes from reference frames via mask pooling and injects them into target frames through a Transformer-based In-context Fusion block with bidirectional cross-attention, enforcing spatiotemporal consistency. The Continuous Pseudo-label Reforging (CPR) module refines noisy pseudo-labels by performing channel-level cross-attention where labeled-frame features act as queries and unlabeled-frame features as keys/values, reconstructing cleaner supervision signals. A FlameRise schedule progressively ramps up the pseudo-label loss weight while lowering the confidence threshold over training epochs, preventing early overfitting to unreliable predictions.

## Results

On CAMUS-Semi, EchoForge achieves 94.89 mDice, 3.12 mHD, and 1.18 mm ASD, outperforming the best baseline DSA (93.65 mDice) by 1.24 points. On EchoNet-Dynamic, it reaches 93.63 mDice, 3.05 mHD, and 1.02 px ASD, surpassing DSA (92.75 mDice) by 0.88 points. The method also achieves the strongest LVEF correlation (0.913 on CAMUS-Semi, 0.887 on EchoNet-Dynamic) with the lowest bias (0.23 and −0.51, respectively). At 46 FPS with 67M parameters and 125G FLOPs, EchoForge maintains real-time inference suitable for clinical deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoForge outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets across every reported metric. Against the strongest echocardiography-specific baselines, it gains +1.24 mDice over DSA (93.65) and +1.31 over PKEchoNet (93.58) on CAMUS-Semi, and +0.88 over DSA (92.75) and +0.99 over PKEchoNet (92.64) on EchoNet-Dynamic. The pseudo-labeling methods CLAS (91.46) and TCS (92.15) trail by 3.4 and 2.7 mDice points respectively on CAMUS-Semi, confirming that CPR's iterative reforging substantially reduces error accumulation. Among general video segmentation methods, VideoMamba (90.13 mDice) and Cutie (88.91) are the weakest, lacking domain-specific temporal modeling. In terms of efficiency, EchoForge (46 FPS, 67M params) is slower than VideoMamba (291 FPS, 13M) and P-Mamba (124 FPS, 53M) but far faster than MemSAM (13 FPS, 257M) and CLAS (78 FPS, 1138G FLOPs), striking a favorable accuracy–speed trade-off for real-time clinical use. All mDice improvements are statistically significant (Wilcoxon p < 0.05).

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoForge sits at the intersection of semi-supervised video segmentation (memory-based networks like XMem/Cutie, teacher-student CPS frameworks) and echocardiography-specific methods (optical-flow-based coherence, pseudo-labeling approaches like CLAS and TCS). It departs from the optical-flow lineage by arguing that flow estimation is too fragile under speckle noise, and from standard pseudo-labeling by showing that naive label propagation accumulates errors. The Anchor Recalibration module repurposes the 'anchor' concept from object detection but redefines anchors as learnable foreground/background feature vectors that act as attractors in noisy ultrasound, while Temporal Semantic Fusion borrows the in-context prototype propagation idea from SAM-style models and adapts it to cardiac cycle dynamics. The CPR module with its FlameRise progressive schedule represents a novel departure from both fixed-threshold pseudo-labeling and momentum-teacher approaches by continuously reforging labels through channel-level cross-attention from labeled features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clinically realistic setting (only ED/ES annotations) combined with real-time inference at 46 FPS makes this directly deployable in cardiology workflows, and the mere 0.5% mDice drop between semi-supervised and full-annotation evaluation demonstrates that the temporal consistency mechanisms genuinely capture cardiac dynamics rather than overfitting to keyframes. The paper acknowledges a clear limitation: in extreme low-contrast or severe acoustic shadowing cases where even cardiologists struggle, the model fails, suggesting that purely data-driven approaches have a floor set by image quality. Open questions include whether the anchor-prototype framework generalizes to other cardiac structures (atria, valves), 3D echocardiography, or other speckle-noise-heavy modalities like fetal ultrasound, and whether the FlameRise schedule transfers to other semi-supervised settings beyond echocardiography.
