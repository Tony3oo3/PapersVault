## Problem

Perspective-to-equirectangular (P2E) visual place recognition requires matching a narrow perspective query against a full 360° panoramic database image, but existing methods resort to exhaustive sliding-window comparisons that are computationally expensive and ignore the intrinsic multi-scale structure of panoramas. The paper identifies that the natural hierarchy of fields of view within a panorama is difficult to represent faithfully in Euclidean space, where geometric distortion hinders accurate preservation of local-to-global relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263059/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a perspective query image resized to 224×224 and a database of equirectangular panoramas resized to 224×1792 (8× wider than the query). The query is encoded into a single 768-dim hyperbolic descriptor, while each panorama produces a hierarchical tree of descriptors across L levels (default L=5), where the lowest level contains 2^(L-1) window-level descriptors matching the query resolution. Retrieval is two-stage: top-level descriptors yield initial candidates, which are then rescored using selected lower-level descriptors, enabling a tunable accuracy-efficiency trade-off without retraining.

## Method

HypeVPR divides each panoramic image into L levels by progressively halving its horizontal field of view, then applies a shared CNN backbone to extract features at each level. A Hierarchical Aggregation Module (HAM) pools features at each level via GeM pooling, projects them into the Poincaré ball via the exponential map, and aggregates sibling descriptors using the Einstein midpoint on the Klein model (norm-weighted averaging that respects hyperbolic geometry). Training combines three triplet losses: a hierarchical loss enforcing spatial parent-child consistency across levels, a hyperbolic matching loss on top-level descriptors, and a Euclidean loss on lowest-level window features for stabilization. At inference, the system first retrieves top-K' candidates using the single top-level descriptor, then rescores them with minimum-distance over selected lower-level descriptors, allowing the user to control the speed-accuracy trade-off by choosing which hierarchy levels to activate.

## Results

On Pitts250K-P2E, HypeVPR-O* (ResNet-50, single descriptor) achieves R@1=66.5, R@5=82.1, R@10=86.3, dramatically outperforming the best single-descriptor baseline (ConvNeXt-S at R@1=14.2). The multi-level variant HypeVPR-B* (ResNet-50) reaches R@1=79.6 with only 29.6 ms/query, while HypeVPR-L (ConvNeXt-S) attains R@1=43.4 / R@10=73.4 at 14.0 ms/query. On YQ360, HypeVPR-L (ConvNeXt-S) achieves R@1=52.4, R@10=94.8, and HypeVPR-B* (ResNet-50) reaches R@1=63.6, R@10=96.4. On the large-scale SF-XL test set, HypeVPR-L achieves R@1=85.2 in 2.92 s with 30.3 GB storage, surpassing EigenPlace (R@1=84.1, 7.92 s) while remaining 11× faster than SALAD (R@1=88.6, 32.69 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-descriptor P2E baselines, HypeVPR-O* (ResNet-50) achieves R@1=66.5 on Pitts250K-P2E, outperforming ConvNeXt-S (14.2), SwinT (10.1), Berton et al. (8.0), and NetVLAD (4.0) by a wide margin, while also being faster than NetVLAD (4.0 ms vs. 32.8 ms). Against sliding-window P2E methods, HypeVPR-B (Swin-T) beats PanoVPR ×8 (29.4 vs. 22.0 R@1) at 4.7× lower query time (3.6 vs. 17.0 ms), and HypeVPR-L (ConvNeXt-S) surpasses PanoVPR ×16 (43.4 vs. 40.3 R@1) at 3.5× lower time (14.0 vs. 48.6 ms); both also outperform Orhan et al. (R@1=47.0) in speed by over 50×. In the P2P comparison (Table 2), HypeVPR-B* matches EigenPlace (R@1=79.6 vs. 78.3) at 5× faster retrieval (17.9 vs. 90.5 ms) and lower storage (147.6 vs. 262.4 MB), while HypeVPR-L* surpasses EigenPlace (81.2 vs. 78.3) at 2.2× faster speed; both remain far below SALAD in recall (86.8) but are 9–20× faster with 4–7× less storage. On SF-XL (Table 3), HypeVPR-L (R@1=85.2) edges out EigenPlace (84.1) and CricaVPR (80.6) while being 2.7× faster than EigenPlace and 11× faster than SALAD (88.6), though it trails SALAD by 3.4 R@1 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

HypeVPR extends the line of hyperbolic visual embeddings initiated by Khrulkov et al. (CVPR 2020), which demonstrated that Poincaré ball representations outperform Euclidean ones for hierarchical visual data, and adapts the hierarchical contrastive loss of Kwon et al. (CVPR 2024) into a triplet-based formulation tailored to panoramic scene structure. Conceptually, it departs from existing P2E VPR methods (PanoVPR, Orhan et al.) that treat panoramas as flat collections of perspective crops requiring exhaustive sliding-window comparison, instead modeling the panoramic view as an intrinsic multi-level hierarchy embedded in hyperbolic space. The framework also builds on standard VPR backbones (ResNet, Swin-T, ConvNeXt-S) and GeM pooling, positioning hyperbolic hierarchical aggregation as a drop-in replacement for conventional descriptor aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in mobile and autonomous VPR systems: panoramic databases drastically reduce storage and retrieval cost compared to densely sampled multi-view perspective databases, but existing P2E methods negate this advantage through expensive sliding-window search. HypeVPR's adjustable hierarchical retrieval offers a tunable accuracy-efficiency trade-off without retraining, which is valuable for deployment on resource-constrained platforms. A key open limitation is that hyperbolic descriptors are incompatible with standard efficient kNN libraries like FAISS, which currently restricts scalability to very large databases; resolving this gap is essential for the approach to match the deployment readiness of Euclidean VPR pipelines.
