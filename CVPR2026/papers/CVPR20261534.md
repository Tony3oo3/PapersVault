## Problem

Existing point cloud completion methods rely on pairwise attention (transformers) or geometric priors like symmetry, which cannot capture the multi-to-multi, high-order correlations that govern complex 3D structures (e.g., the aerodynamic coupling between an airplane's wings, tail, and fuselage). This limitation is especially acute for incomplete point clouds, where sparse and missing regions make reliable correlation extraction and unbiased hypergraph construction particularly challenging.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial point cloud (tested at 25%, 50%, and 75% completeness) and produces a dense completed point cloud of N_o points. Internally, the encoder selects N_k key points via a PointNet-based feature embedding with a transformer block, and the decoder refines coarse predictions through a two-stage Cross Fusion Geometry (CFG) Transformer that concatenates global hypergraph features with local geometry-aware features before regressing pointwise offsets.

## Method

Hyper-PCN introduces two hypergraph-based modules. The Hyper Refinement Stack (HyperRS) stacks L=6 hypergraph convolution layers with a linearly annealed distance threshold (τ from 0.20 to 0.16), so shallow layers form broad hyperedges capturing coarse contextual cues while deeper layers form tighter hyperedges extracting fine-grained high-order relations; each layer applies the HGNN+ vertex→hyperedge→vertex message passing with residual SiLU+BN updates. The Anchor-based Hypergraph Neural Network (A-HGNN) uses a two-stage sampling strategy: anchors are deterministically subsampled from key points, and each key point is connected to its α nearest anchors to form hyperedges, mitigating the bias of random or voxel-based sampling toward intact regions. A two-stage CFG Transformer decoder then fuses the global hypergraph features with local geometry to produce the final dense completion. Training is supervised with a weighted sum of Chamfer Distance losses at three stages (coarse, intermediate, and final points).

## Results

On the PCN dataset, Hyper-PCN achieves a CD-L1 of 6.20×10⁻³ and F1-Score@1% of 0.858, outperforming the best prior method SymmCompletion (CD 6.28, F1 0.853) and PointMAC (CD 6.33). On ShapeNet-55, it reaches CD-L2 of 0.65×10⁻³ and F1 of 0.565, and on the more challenging ShapeNet-34/21 split it attains CD-Avg of 0.58 (seen) and 0.94 (unseen) with F1 of 0.571 and 0.550 respectively. On the MVP dataset, Hyper-PCN records CD-L2 of 4.76×10⁻⁴ and F1 of 0.558, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hyper-PCN outperforms all 20 compared baselines on every evaluated dataset (PCN, ShapeNet-55/34/21, MVP, and KITTI). The closest competitors are SymmCompletion (AAAI'25), which relies on symmetry priors, and PointMAC (NeurIPS'25), which uses meta-learning for test-time adaptation; Hyper-PCN beats SymmCompletion by 0.08 in CD-L1 (6.20 vs. 6.28) and 0.005 in F1 on PCN, and by 0.04 in CD-L2 on ShapeNet-55 (0.65 vs. 0.69). Against transformer-based methods like SVDFormer (ICCV'23) and AdaPoinTr (TPAMI'23), the gap widens to 0.34–0.33 in CD-L1 on PCN. On the harder ShapeNet-21 unseen split, Hyper-PCN achieves CD-Avg 0.94 versus 0.97 for SymmCompletion and 1.22 for PointMAC, indicating stronger generalization to unseen categories. The per-category breakdown shows consistent gains across all 8 PCN categories, with the largest improvements on Airplane (3.46 vs. 3.53) and Lamp (4.83 vs. 4.98), suggesting the hypergraph's high-order correlation modeling is particularly effective for objects with complex multi-part geometric couplings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the HGNN+ hypergraph convolution framework (Gao et al., TPAMI 2022) as its core message-passing operation, but departs from the dominant transformer-based point cloud completion paradigm (PoinTr, SeedFormer, SVDFormer, AdaPoinTr) that relies on pairwise query-key attention. It also positions itself against symmetry- and prior-based methods (SymmCompletion, ODGNet) that inject global shape context through geometric assumptions. The key conceptual departure is applying hypergraph learning to *incomplete* point clouds for completion, whereas prior hypergraph work on 3D point clouds (detection, segmentation, quality assessment) assumed complete inputs; the authors identify two specific challenges this introduces—reliable correlation extraction from sparse structures and construction bias toward intact regions—and address them with threshold-annealed stacking (HyperRS) and anchor-based two-stage sampling (A-HGNN).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that high-order, multi-to-multi correlations (e.g., aerodynamic coupling between wings and tail) are a meaningful signal for completion that pairwise attention and symmetry priors cannot capture, and provides a practical recipe (progressive threshold annealing + anchor collaboration) for extracting them from incomplete inputs. However, the performance gains over strong baselines like SymmCompletion and PointMAC are modest (e.g., ~0.08 CD-L1 on PCN, ~0.02 CD-L2 on ShapeNet-34), and the method remains supervised with Chamfer Distance loss, which is known to be sensitive to point density. Open questions include whether the hypergraph formulation scales to very large or unstructured point clouds (e.g., outdoor scenes), whether the distance-based hyperedge construction in feature space is optimal versus learned soft assignments, and whether the approach transfers to other 3D tasks such as segmentation or generation.
