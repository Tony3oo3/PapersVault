## Problem

Existing UDA methods for medical image segmentation suffer from two fundamental limitations: feature alignment operates in a semantically unaware manner, applying uniform transformations that blend style across distinct anatomical structures and destroying class-specific discriminability; and pseudo-label validation relies on pixel-level confidence or local consistency, which cannot prevent the formation of globally implausible anatomical structures such as impossible shapes or spatial arrangements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263980/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a frozen DINOv3 ViT-S/16 encoder over 256×256, 3-channel normalized images (replicated from single-channel CT or MRI) paired with a trainable UNet-style decoder. Source domain images carry ground-truth labels for 4 cardiac structures (AA, LAC, LVC, MYO) or 4 abdominal organs (LIV, RK, LK, SPL); target domain images are unlabeled. The output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask per target image, with the pseudo-label pipeline producing a refined mask where anatomically anomalous classes are masked to an ignore index before supervising the student.

## Method

SHAPE is a three-stage cascade built on a frozen DINOv3 encoder. First, Hierarchical Feature Modulation (HFM) performs class-aware, spatially-differentiated alignment: after a global AdaIN pass, tokens are partitioned into pure semantic cores and impure boundaries via a label-purity score; pure tokens undergo Mixup-style interpolation with same-class target exemplars, while impure tokens receive statistical (mean/std) alignment, preserving intra-class structure that monolithic AdaIN destroys. Second, Hypergraph Plausibility Estimation (HPE) models each ensemble prediction as a structural hypergraph with class hyperedges scoring intra-class shape via the isoperimetric ratio and a layout hyperedge scoring inter-class arrangement via centroid direction cosines, both evaluated as Z-scores against batch statistics and fused multiplicatively with vertex-level confidence to gate sample selection. Third, Structural Anomaly Pruning (SAP) computes a coefficient-of-variation instability score per class across ensemble views and masks classes exceeding a dynamic percentile threshold, purging hallucinated regions. Training combines a supervised loss (averaged over original and HFM-modulated source features) with an unsupervised loss on HPE/SAP-validated pseudo-labels weighted by pixel-wise certainty, using an EMA teacher-student scheme.

## Results

On the cardiac dataset (MMWHS), SHAPE achieves state-of-the-art average Dice scores of 90.08% (MRI→CT) and 78.51% (CT→MRI), outperforming the second-best method DDFP by 5.62 and 2.03 percentage points respectively, and narrowing the gap to the supervised upper bound (93.37% / 84.41%) to just 3.29% / 5.90%. On the abdominal dataset (CHAOS + Multi-Atlas), SHAPE reaches 87.48% (MRI→CT) and 86.89% (CT→MRI) average DSC, surpassing DDFP (85.17% / 86.27%) and IPLC+ (85.71% / 85.06%). Average surface distances are also best: 2.62 mm (cardiac MRI→CT) and 2.81 mm (abdominal CT→MRI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHAPE outperforms all 10 baselines on both cardiac and abdominal datasets across both adaptation directions. The closest competitor is DDFP [45], which leads on cardiac MRI→CT DSC (84.46%) but is surpassed by SHAPE by 5.62 points (90.08%); on abdominal CT→MRI, DDFP (86.27%) is the nearest rival, with SHAPE ahead by 0.62 points (86.89%). Among pseudo-label-based methods, IPLC+ [48] achieves the best ASD on cardiac MRI→CT (3.91 mm) but trails SHAPE (2.62 mm) by 1.29 mm while also scoring 8.65 points lower in DSC. Alignment-based methods (CycleGAN, AdaptSegNet, ADVENT, SIFA, SASAN) all fall 5–30 points below SHAPE in DSC, confirming that global, content-agnostic alignment is insufficient. On the abdominal dataset, SHAPE leads DDFP by 2.31 points (MRI→CT) and 0.62 points (CT→MRI), and surpasses IPLC+ by 1.77 and 1.83 points respectively, demonstrating consistent gains across organ types and modality directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHAPE builds on the DINOv3 self-supervised foundation model as a frozen feature extractor, positioning itself within the pseudo-label-based UDA lineage (IPLC, UPL-SFDA, GenericSSL) that has dominated recent medical segmentation adaptation. It departs from prior work along two axes: (1) replacing monolithic feature alignment (AdaIN, adversarial, Mixup) with a class-aware, spatially-differentiated Hierarchical Feature Modulation that treats semantic cores and structural boundaries differently, and (2) introducing hypergraph-based structural plausibility estimation as a pseudo-label quality gate, moving beyond the pixel-level entropy/consistency checks used by all prior pseudo-label methods. The hypergraph idea itself draws from vision segmentation literature (Hyper-BTS, multi-modal hypergraph contrastive learning) but is repurposed here as a validation mechanism rather than a segmentation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of pseudo-label quality from local pixel confidence to global anatomical plausibility is a conceptually useful shift that could inform future UDA work beyond medical imaging. However, the empirical evidence is constrained by very small datasets (20+20 cardiac, 30+20 abdominal), 2D slice evaluation, and only 4 target classes per task, making it unclear whether the hypergraph validation generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation or higher-cardinality organ sets. The heavy dependence on a frozen DINOv3 encoder also raises questions about robustness when the foundation model changes or when domain gaps exceed what feature modulation can bridge. Open questions include whether the isoperimetric-ratio-based shape scoring and centroid-direction layout scoring remain informative for elongated or highly variable anatomical structures.
