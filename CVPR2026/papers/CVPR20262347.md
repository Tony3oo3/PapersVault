## Problem

Existing remote sensing image semantic segmentation (RSISS) networks are predominantly discriminative, estimating strong posteriors from appearance cues while lacking generative priors that encode the structural dependencies—topology, continuity, and region adjacency—that govern spatial organization in remote sensing scenes. This imbalance produces fragmented boundaries, texture overfitting, and poor cross-dataset generalization, leaving a gap between pixel-level perception and structural reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262347/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB remote sensing image (H×W×3) as input, with a ground-truth segmentation mask available only during training to supervise the generative prior learner. The prior is computed at a downsampled resolution (h×w, where s is the spatial downsampling rate) and expanded into a three-level multi-scale structural pyramid aligned with the backbone's feature hierarchy. The output is a dense per-pixel semantic segmentation mask, and the framework is backbone-agnostic, plugging into CNN (ResNet, ConvNeXt) and Transformer (Swin) backbones without architectural modification.

## Method

HySeg is a two-stage hybrid generative-discriminative framework. In Stage I, the MeanStruct module learns a structure-consistent generative prior via a MeanFlow-based single-step (1-NFE) transport: it defines a linear interpolation path between Gaussian noise and the ground-truth mask embedding, then trains a learnable vector field to approximate the interval-averaged velocity using the MeanFlow identity with JVP-based automatic differentiation. In Stage II, the Prior-to-Affinity Projection (P2A) module converts the learned prior into topology-aware, class-conditional affinity kernels by measuring channel-wise center-neighbor discrepancies in local K×K neighborhoods, converting them into similarity weights via a learnable Gaussian bandwidth, and normalizing with masked softmax. The Dynamic Affinity-driven Segmentation (DAS) head then uses these affinities to modulate local message passing among pixels, combined with a dual-residual design that fuses prior-guided aggregation with self-attention refinement, yielding a posterior estimate regularized by the generative structural prior.

## Results

On LoveDA, HySeg achieves up to 55.63 mIoU (ConvNeXt_B backbone), surpassing the best external baseline In2NeCT (54.3 mIoU) by +1.33. On ISPRS Vaihingen, the best HySeg variant reaches 94.8 mIoU (ConvNeXt_B), outperforming LOGCAN++ (91.8) by +3.0. On ISPRS Potsdam, HySeg attains 93.9 mIoU versus In2NeCT's 91.8 (+2.1), and on UAVid it reaches 71.4 mIoU versus LOGCAN++'s 68.8 (+2.6). Across all four benchmarks and all tested backbones, HySeg consistently improves mIoU by +0.5 to +2.2 over its respective discriminative baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HySeg outperforms all 12 compared baselines across all four datasets. Against the strongest external competitors, it leads In2NeCT (54.3 mIoU on LoveDA) by +1.33 with ConvNeXt_B, surpasses LOGCAN++ (91.8 on Vaihingen) by +3.0, and exceeds In2NeCT (91.8 on Potsdam) by +2.1. The gains are most pronounced on structure-dominated classes: Barren improves by +5.0 mIoU (Swin_B on LoveDA), Building by +2.3–3.1, and Forest by +1.2–3.1. Among backbone-matched comparisons, HySeg adds +0.7 to +2.2 mIoU over UNetFormer, +0.5 to +1.6 over DC-Swin, +0.5 to +1.6 over LSKNet, and +0.3 to +0.5 over D2LS. Topology metrics (clDice, Betti errors) further confirm structural gains: e.g., clDice improves from 0.9110 to 0.9350 and β₁-Error drops from 4.51 to 2.46 with ConvNeXt_B on Potsdam. No baseline is matched or exceeded by HySeg in any reported setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

HySeg builds directly on the MeanFlow framework (Geng et al., 2025), which extends flow matching to model interval-averaged velocity fields for one-step generative modeling, and repurposes it not as a standalone image generator but as a structural prior learner for segmentation. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant discriminative RSISS lineage (UNetFormer, LSKNet, DC-Swin, D2LS) by recasting segmentation as posterior inference constrained by a learned generative prior, and from prior generative-segmentation work (diffusion-based RSISS) by using the generative component only to produce topology-aware affinities rather than to synthesize masks. The P2A module draws on local neighborhood operations reminiscent of CRF-style affinity propagation, but grounds the affinities in a learned transport field rather than hand-crafted or feature-similarity-based kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid generative-discriminative framing offers a principled way to inject structural reasoning into otherwise appearance-driven segmentation, and the 1-NFE MeanFlow prior keeps inference at near-discriminative speed (~80 FPS) while improving topology metrics (clDice, Betti errors) across four benchmarks and multiple backbones. A notable open question is whether the learned 'structural prior' genuinely encodes topological constraints or merely acts as a smoothness regularizer, since the paper explicitly disclaims a strict topological guarantee. The backbone-agnostic design and modest parameter overhead (15.32 M) make the approach practical, but the 1–2 mIoU gains, while consistent, leave room for whether the generative framing yields substantially larger benefits on more structurally complex or out-of-distribution scenes.
