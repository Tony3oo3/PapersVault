## Problem

All existing open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods follow a logits-optimization paradigm: they iteratively minimize the distribution discrepancy between vision-language logits and ground-truth distributions (via time-consuming training) or apply model-specific attention modulation to calibrate logits before constructing segmentation maps. This paper asks whether the segmentation map can be obtained directly by analytically solving the distribution discrepancy itself, thereby eliminating both iterative training and model-specific modulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261481/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a dynamic set of N_c class-specific text prompts as input, with no fixed category list required at inference. It produces a pixel-wise segmentation mask at the original image resolution (H×W). Notably, the method performs direct whole-image inference without sliding-window tiling or post-processing, and the number of classes N_c is arbitrary at test time. Self-attention tensors are extracted from a single-step unconditional denoising pass of Stable Diffusion 2 operating on noise-free latent encodings of the input image.

## Method

The core insight is that the KL divergence between the logits distribution and a degenerate (uniform) surrogate distribution directly encodes semantic information—patches of the same class exhibit consistent discrepancy while different classes diverge—allowing the segmentation map to be read off the discrepancy itself rather than from optimized logits. Two analytic formulations are proposed: (1) an optimal-path mode that solves the Sinkhorn optimal transport problem between the normalized logits and the degenerate distribution, using layer-wise averaged SD2 self-attention tensors as the cost matrix; and (2) a maximum-velocity mode that models the logits-to-degenerate transition as a Markov process (with the self-attention tensor doubly-stochasticized via IPF) and defines each patch's discrepancy as the reciprocal of its convergence step. The pipeline computes CLIP cosine-similarity logits, applies NMS and normalization, solves the chosen discrepancy formulation, upsamples via joint bilateral upsampling, and applies argmax across classes to yield the final mask.

## Results

With CLIP ViT-B/16, the maximum velocity mode achieves 46.9% average mIoU across eight benchmarks, outperforming the best prior training-free method CASS (44.4%) by +2.5 mIoU. With CLIP ViT-L/14, the maximum velocity mode reaches 47.8% average mIoU, surpassing SC-CLIP (45.2%) by +2.6 mIoU. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on VOC21 (67.8/68.9%), Context60 (38.3/38.7%), VOC20 (89.0/90.1%), COCO-Stuff (28.9/29.2%), and Cityscapes (43.3/43.9%) under the base/large scales respectively, all without any training or model-specific attention modulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 19 compared baselines on average mIoU across both CLIP scales. Under ViT-B/16, it beats the strongest training-free competitor CASS by +2.5 mIoU (max velocity) and +1.8 mIoU (optimal path), and surpasses iterative-training methods like CLIP-DINOiser (40.3%) by over 6 mIoU. Under ViT-L/14, it exceeds SC-CLIP (45.2%) by +2.6 mIoU and ProxyCLIP (41.7%) by +6.1 mIoU. The method achieves SOTA on five of eight benchmarks (VOC21, Context60, VOC20, COCO-Stuff, Cityscapes) while remaining competitive on the remaining three. Notably, the maximum velocity mode consistently edges out the optimal path mode by +0.7% (base) and +0.6% (large) average mIoU, and the method requires no training, no GT annotations, and no model-specific attention modulation—unlike all M.M. and I.T. baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant paradigms in training-free OVSS: iterative training methods (MaskCLIP, TCL, GEM) that minimize the KL divergence between logits and GT, and attention-modulation methods (ClearCLIP, SCLIP, NACLIP, ProxyCLIP, CASS) that calibrate CLIP's self-attention to rectify vision-language misalignment. Rather than optimizing logits through either route, the authors reformulate the problem as directly solving the distribution discrepancy analytically, using optimal transport (Sinkhorn) and Markov process convergence as two complementary formulations. The cost/transition matrices are drawn from Stable Diffusion's self-attention tensors, repurposing a technique from diffusion-based interactive segmentation [19, 43] into a dense prediction setting, while the degenerate distribution serves as a GT-free surrogate for the target distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both GT-dependent training and model-specific attention modulation, the method offers a cleaner, more generalizable pipeline for OVSS that achieves SOTA on eight benchmarks with whole-image inference and no sliding-window post-processing. The validated hypothesis—that distribution discrepancy itself encodes semantic structure—opens a conceptual avenue where future work might explore other discrepancy measures or transport formulations without the iterative optimization loop. Open questions include the sensitivity to the Stable Diffusion backbone choice (the paper shows SD2 outperforms ViT-based VFMs but does not explore why), the role of the somewhat arbitrary convergence threshold τ, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D segmentation where the patch-level transport formulation would need rethinking.
