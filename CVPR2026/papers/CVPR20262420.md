## Problem

No 3D foundation model exists for volumetric neuroimaging due to the scarcity of high-quality 3D annotations and the cost of volumetric acquisition, leaving neuron segmentation reliant on data-hungry training from scratch. Moreover, existing segmentation methods optimize voxel-level overlap without explicit structural supervision, producing reconstructions that are topologically fragmented or morphologically inconsistent despite reasonable F1 scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262420/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D volumetric fluorescence microscopy volumes of heterogeneous sizes (ranging from 88×89×93 in Drosophila to 100×2048×2048 in Mouse), processed via sliding-window patch inference with Batch Renormalization to avoid tiling artifacts. The primary output is a per-voxel 3D neuron segmentation mask, which is then fed into downstream tracing algorithms (SmartTracing or NeuTube) to yield skeleton graph reconstructions evaluated by ESA, DSA, and PDS metrics.

## Method

NeurINO decomposes 3D neuron segmentation into 2D intra-slice feature extraction and 3D inter-slice aggregation. A pretrained DINOv3 ConvNeXt encoder is adapted to 3D via an inflation strategy that embeds each 2D convolution kernel at the central slice of a 3D kernel (center inflation), preserving web-scale visual priors while leaving the network free to learn cross-slice structural continuity. The inflated encoder is paired with a symmetric MedNeXt-style decoder producing multi-scale outputs. A novel Topology-Aware Skeleton Loss (TASL) is applied at each scale, decomposing structural mismatch into node-level nearest-neighbor distance, edge-count discrepancy, and mean connected-component length between predicted and ground-truth skeleton graphs. The total loss combines Dice, cross-entropy, and TASL terms under deep supervision, with full fine-tuning of the inflated encoder to align pretrained semantics with filamentary neuronal geometry.

## Results

NeurINO achieves consistent segmentation improvements over both nnUNet and MedNeXt across all four datasets (Drosophila, Mouse, NeuroFly, CWMBS). The Small variant (NeurINO^S) attains the highest F1-scores on three of four datasets (50.19% Drosophila, 52.73% Mouse, 65.44% NeuroFly) and the lowest HD95 on Drosophila (3.02) and Mouse (9.24). On reconstruction, NeurINO variants yield average gains of 2.9% in ESA, 2.8% in DSA, and 3.8% in PDS over the best competing method, with NeurINO^T achieving the best SmartTracing results on Drosophila (ESA 1.62, DSA 4.29) and NeuroFly (ESA 21.93, DSA 28.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeurINO outperforms nnUNet on F1-score across all four datasets, with the largest margin on Drosophila (50.19% vs. 47.20%, +2.99%) and NeuroFly (65.44% vs. 63.36%, +2.08%), and also achieves lower HD95 on every dataset (e.g., 9.24 vs. 10.12 on Mouse). Against MedNeXt, NeurINO^S leads by a wider margin, particularly on Mouse (52.73% vs. 50.61%, +2.12%) and CWMBS (36.55% vs. 33.46%, +3.09%), while also reducing HD95 substantially (9.24 vs. 13.77 on Mouse; 16.27 vs. 18.37 on CWMBS). On reconstruction (SmartTracing), NeurINO^T achieves the best ESA and DSA on Drosophila (1.62/4.29 vs. nnUNet 1.67/4.48 and MedNeXt 1.68/4.44) and NeuroFly (21.93/28.98 vs. 22.78/31.76 and 27.52/34.74), while NeurINO^S edges ahead on Mouse (ESA 2.92 vs. 2.99 and 4.06). Notably, NeurINO^T (39.21M params) matches or exceeds the larger MedNeXt (61.97M params) on most reconstruction metrics, indicating that the DINOv3 initialization and topology-aware loss compensate for the smaller encoder capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior inflation-based adaptation work [11] (which transferred 2D ViT weights to 3D neuron segmentation) to the DINOv3 ConvNeXt backbone, and pairs it with a novel topology-aware skeleton loss (TASL) that operates at the graph level (node, edge, path) rather than the voxel or centerline level used by prior topology-preserving losses like clDice or Skeleton Recall Loss. Conceptually, it positions 3D neuron segmentation as a two-stage problem—2D intra-slice feature extraction (solved by a pretrained foundation model) plus 3D inter-slice aggregation (learned from data)—departing from prior 3D U-Net and MedNeXt-style architectures that learn all spatial features from scratch on limited neuronal volumes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical, data-efficient pathway for 3D volumetric neuroimaging where high-quality annotations are scarce, and the consistent gains across four diverse datasets (Drosophila, Mouse, NeuroFly, CWMBS) suggest the 2D-to-3D transfer is robust. However, the paper itself acknowledges this remains an approximation to true volumetric understanding, and the absence of a genuine 3D foundation model pretrained on large-scale neuroimaging data is the key open question. The limited baseline set (only nnUNet and MedNeXt) and the modest absolute improvements leave room for stronger comparisons, and whether the inflation strategy generalizes beyond ConvNeXt-style encoders to transformer-based 3D architectures remains untested.
