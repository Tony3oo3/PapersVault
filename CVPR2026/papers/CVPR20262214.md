## Problem

Existing sparsity methods for ViT backbones and DETR-style decoders prune or merge image tokens or object queries in isolation, and none jointly sparsifies both 2D tokens and 3D queries in a planner-aligned manner for multi-view 3D detection. Standard benchmarks like nuScenes weight all annotated agents equally, so errors on distant or non-interacting actors dominate metrics and there is no way to evaluate accuracy specifically on planning-critical agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262214/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six synchronized surround-camera RGB images (default 320×800, up to 800×1600) with known intrinsics and extrinsics, plus a temporal query memory carrying top-K propagated 3D object queries from prior frames. It outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels via a DETR-style Hungarian-matched detection head, and internally produces scalar relevance scores for every image token and 3D query at each hierarchical stage, which drive the routing decisions. The relevance supervision uses privileged 5-second future trajectories (interaction corridors) available only at training and evaluation time.

## Method

SToRe3D introduces a unified relevance function that jointly scores 2D image tokens and 3D object queries via mutual gating: query relevance is computed from deformable cross-attended token context plus an ego-motion embedding, while token relevance is aggregated from the attention weights of the top-K most relevant queries. These scores are supervised by binary labels derived from a future interaction corridor (convex hull of 5-second swept ego and agent boxes, with a 1.2 m distance margin). At each backbone and decoder stage, Gumbel-softmax top-k filtering retains a non-increasing fraction of tokens and queries; filtered embeddings are written to lightweight store buffers rather than discarded and are reactivated at the final attention layer, avoiding irreversible pruning. Training uses a joint loss (focal + L1 detection, Gaussian focal relevance, auxiliary ROI) with gradients stopped at the relevance heads, and a linear warm-up schedule that gradually increases sparsity from dense to the target keep ratio to maintain stability under aggressive pruning.

## Results

On nuScenes validation, SToRe3D-1/10 with ViT-B achieves 0.479 mAP and 0.571 NDS at 17.7 FPS, a ~2.9× speedup over dense StreamPETR (ViT-B) at 6.1 FPS with only a 1.8 mAP drop. With ViT-L, SToRe3D-1/10 matches StreamPETR's mAP exactly (0.521) while running at 5.2 FPS vs. 2.2 FPS (2.4× faster), and SToRe3D-1/2 (ViT-L) surpasses dense StreamPETR on both mAP (0.533 vs. 0.521) and NDS (0.618 vs. 0.608) at comparable latency. On the nuScenes-Relevance benchmark, SToRe3D-1/10 (ViT-L) retains 0.478 NDS-RM on planning-critical agents, exceeding StreamPETR's 0.463 NDS-RM while running nearly 2× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StreamPETR (the base architecture), SToRe3D-1/10 (ViT-L) matches mAP exactly (0.521) while nearly doubling throughput (5.2 vs. 2.2 FPS), and SToRe3D-1/2 (ViT-L) actually exceeds it on mAP (0.533 vs. 0.521) and NDS (0.618 vs. 0.608) at similar latency. Against ToC3D, the closest token-compression competitor, SToRe3D wins on both accuracy and speed at every matched sparsity level: at 1/2 keep ratio with ViT-B, SToRe3D achieves 0.493 mAP vs. ToC3D-Fast's 0.460 (+3.3 mAP) at 8.2 vs. 6.6 FPS; at 1/3 with ViT-B, 0.489 vs. 0.453 (+3.6 mAP) at 10.6 vs. 7.3 FPS. Against DynamicViT (token-only pruning in Table 3), SToRe3D-1/2 achieves 0.618 NDS vs. 0.597 at TKR 0.5, and SToRe3D-1/3 achieves 0.609 vs. 0.593 at TKR 0.3, while also running faster (1.26× vs. 1.15× and 1.63× vs. 1.36× speedup). Against ResNet-based baselines (BEVFormer, Sparse4D, SparseBEV, SOLOFusion), SToRe3D-1/2 (ViT-B) achieves 0.493 mAP / 0.581 NDS at 8.2 FPS, surpassing BEVFormer (0.416/0.517 at 3.3 FPS) and Sparse4D (0.444/0.550 at 4.7 FPS) by wide margins, though SparseBEV (0.501 mAP, no FPS reported) and Sparse4Dv2 (0.505 mAP) remain slightly ahead on raw mAP with larger R101 backbones at higher resolution. On the nuScenes-Relevance benchmark specifically, SToRe3D-1/10 (ViT-L) achieves 0.478 NDS-RM vs. StreamPETR's 0.463, confirming that relevance-driven sparsity preserves accuracy on planning-critical agents even under aggressive pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SToRe3D unifies two previously separate sparsity lines—ViT image-token pruning (DynamicViT, ToC3D) and DETR query/encoder-token sparsity (Sparse-DETR, FocalPETR)—into a single joint framework that simultaneously routes both 2D tokens and 3D object queries through a shared relevance signal. It departs from ToC3D's merge-unmerge token compression by introducing a store-reactivate buffer that preserves pruned embeddings for selective reactivation, and from prior planning-aligned perception work (UniAD, ForeSight, SparseDrive) by embedding planner relevance directly into the architectural sparsity mechanism rather than treating it as a downstream objective. The base architecture follows StreamPETR's temporal multi-view DETR3D design with deformable cross-attention, and the relevance supervision via future interaction corridors draws on risk-object identification literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: enabling real-time (~18 FPS) ViT-based multi-view 3D detection on a single GPU removes a major deployment barrier for autonomous driving systems that previously required CNN backbones or aggressive resolution reduction. The nuScenes-R benchmark and the finding that a fixed planner saturates with only 10–20 agents provide concrete evidence that most detection compute is spent on planning-irrelevant objects, reframing the efficiency question from 'detect everything faster' to 'detect what matters.' Open questions include sensitivity to corridor hyperparameters (H, d_min), generalization beyond nuScenes, LiDAR fusion, and whether the store-reactivate mechanism scales to even more aggressive sparsity or longer temporal windows.
