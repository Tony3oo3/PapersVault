## Problem

Current embodied AI systems fragment what should be a unified cognitive pipeline into isolated models for understanding, world modeling, and control, preventing the fusion of multimodal generative capabilities. Additionally, large-scale heterogeneous data (internet videos, egocentric human demonstrations, multi-robot trajectories) remain underutilized because action spaces vary across embodiments and most video data lack action labels, blocking effective pretraining of action experts with general motion priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260100/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a current RGB observation, a language instruction, and optional robot proprioception, and produces a chunk of k future actions (robot control signals) jointly with a downsampled sequence of future video frames. A key design choice is Action-Dense Video-Sparse Prediction: video frames are subsampled to roughly one-sixth the action frame rate so that video and action token counts stay balanced in the shared attention mechanism, preventing the model from overfitting to video prediction at the expense of action quality. The UniDiffuser-style scheduler with modality-specific timesteps and noise scales allows the same weights to be reconfigured at inference into any of five modes (VLA, World Model, IDM, VGM, or Video-Action Joint Prediction) without architectural changes.

## Method

Motus integrates three pretrained experts—a Wan 2.2 5B video generation model, a Qwen3-VL 2B understanding model, and a from-scratch action Transformer—into a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture where each expert retains its own FFN and layer-norm stack but all share a single concatenated multi-head self-attention layer (Tri-model Joint Attention), preserving specialized roles while enabling cross-modal knowledge fusion. Training uses rectified-flow objectives with a UniDiffuser-style scheduler that assigns distinct timesteps and noise scales to video and action tokens, allowing flexible marginal, conditional, and joint distribution modeling. To unlock pretraining on unlabeled video, the paper introduces latent actions: optical flow (computed by DPFlow) is compressed by a deep convolutional autoencoder (DC-AE) into four 512-dim tokens, then projected to a 14-dim vector matching typical robot action dimensions, with weak supervision from a 10% mix of task-agnostic (Curobo-sampled) and task-specific action labels to align the latent space with real control distributions. The full model is trained in three stages—VGM adaptation on multi-robot and human videos, unified three-expert pretraining with latent actions, and target-robot fine-tuning—over a six-layer data pyramid spanning web-scale, egocentric, synthetic, task-agnostic, multi-robot, and target-robotic data.

## Results

On the RoboTwin 2.0 randomized multi-task benchmark (50 tasks, 27.5K demonstrations), Motus achieves 87.02% average success rate, substantially outperforming X-VLA (72.84%) and π₀.₅ (43.84%). In real-world dual-arm manipulation, Motus reaches 63.22% partial success rate on the AC-One platform and 59.30% on Agilex-Aloha-2, compared to π₀.₅'s 14.79% and 48.60% respectively. Ablations confirm that the full three-stage pipeline (video pretraining + latent action pretraining + SFT) is critical: removing all pretraining drops simulation success to 77.00%, and removing the VGM expert alone causes a catastrophic drop from 77% to 25.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Motus outperforms both baselines across all evaluation settings. In simulation (RoboTwin 2.0 randomized, 50 tasks), Motus achieves 87.02% vs. X-VLA's 72.84% (+14.2 pp) and π₀.₅'s 43.84% (+43.2 pp), with the largest gaps on tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation (e.g., Pick Diverse Bottles: 91% vs. 36% for X-VLA and 3% for π₀.₅). In real-world evaluation on the AC-One platform, Motus reaches 63.22% partial success rate versus π₀.₅'s 14.79% (+48.4 pp), with particularly dramatic gains on long-horizon tasks like Grind Coffee Beans (92% vs. 8%) and Brew Coffee (62% vs. 0%). On the Agilex-Aloha-2 platform, Motus achieves 59.30% vs. π₀.₅'s 48.60% (+10.7 pp), with the strongest advantage on Get Water from Water Dispenser (96% vs. 62%). X-VLA is only compared in simulation; it performs competitively on simpler tasks (e.g., Stack Blocks Two: 87% vs. 98%) but degrades significantly on tasks requiring spatial reasoning or multi-step planning (e.g., Stack Blocks Three: 10% vs. 95%, Handover Block: 37% vs. 73%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Motus extends the Unified World Model (UWM) line of work, which first proposed jointly modeling five embodied distributions (VLA, World Model, IDM, VGM, Joint Prediction) within a single diffusion backbone, but departs critically by replacing the from-scratch training with a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture that preserves pretrained priors from a video generator (Wan 2.2), a vision-language model (Qwen3-VL), and a dedicated action expert via Tri-model Joint Attention. The UniDiffuser-style asynchronous scheduler for flexible mode switching builds on the unified multimodal generation literature (e.g., Bagel's MoT), while the optical-flow-based latent action representation draws on the latent action pretraining literature (Genie, LAOM, Moto) but uniquely uses DPFlow optical flow as a universal, embodiment-agnostic motion encoding rather than RGB reconstruction or DINOv2 features. The three-phase training pipeline and six-layer data pyramid operationalize the AnyPos task-agnostic data collection strategy at scale, bridging the gap between internet-scale video pretraining and embodiment-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to unify all five embodied paradigms while retaining the full pretrained priors of both VLMs and VGMs, addressing a gap the authors identify as unaddressed in prior UWM work. The optical-flow latent action mechanism opens a practical path to pretraining action experts on the vast pool of unlabeled internet and egocentric video, which has been a persistent bottleneck in robotic learning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to significantly more embodiments and action spaces, whether deeper VLM integration (beyond token-level conditioning) yields further gains, and whether the three-phase recipe generalizes beyond bimanual manipulation to locomotion or dexterous tasks. The paper acknowledges that latent actions learned from internet-scale universal videos remain a future direction.
