## Problem

Intrinsic image decomposition for outdoor aerial scenes lacks large-scale, real-world training data with dense albedo and shading supervision. Existing datasets are either synthetic (suffering sim-to-real gaps), restricted to controlled indoor environments, or provide only sparse human judgments without pixel-level ground truth, causing models trained on them to fail on real outdoor imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262584/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset pipeline consumes RAW/DNG aerial images (linear radiance, no in-camera tone mapping), photogrammetric camera poses and intrinsics, reconstructed surface meshes, sun position from astronomical ephemeris, and optionally measured HDR sky radiance domes (32-bit EXR, equirectangular). Images are downsampled 8× to 683×455 for decomposition. Outputs include HDR albedo and shading maps (split into sun and sky components), per-pixel confidence masks, metric depth, and surface normals, all stored in linear color space. For the fine-tuning experiments, a single RGB aerial image is the sole input and the predicted albedo map is the output, evaluated at 1920×1080 on MatrixCity.

## Method

Olbedo generates pseudo-ground-truth albedo via a physics-based inverse-rendering pipeline that exploits lit-shadow pixel pairs near cast-shadow boundaries, assuming locally uniform albedo. The image formation model I = R ⊙ (φ ⊙ S_sun + S_sky) is solved by first estimating the sun-to-sky illumination ratio φ from all valid lit-shadow pairs using a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model, then computing R = I / (φ ⊙ S_sun + S_sky); for 2025 flights, a measured HDR sky dome replaces the uniform sky model after least-squares gain alignment. Per-pixel confidence masks are derived from dilated geometry and shadow boundaries to exclude unreliable regions during training. For the empirical study, state-of-the-art diffusion-based IID models (Intrinsic Image Diffusion, Marigold-IID-Appearance, RGB↔X) are fine-tuned with LoRA on the U-Net attention projections (~1.66M parameters, 0.19% of total) for 3 epochs, while the CNN-based NIID-Net uses standard fine-tuning; all are evaluated on the external MatrixCity benchmark to test cross-domain generalization.

## Results

On the MatrixCity benchmark (520 images, 1920×1080), fine-tuning on Olbedo yields consistent gains across all four fine-tunable architectures. The best overall result is Fine-tuned RGB↔X with PSNR 17.735, SSIM 0.611, and LPIPS 0.413, outperforming its pretrained counterpart (15.050 / 0.559 / 0.472) by 17.8% PSNR and 12.5% LPIPS reduction. Marigold-IID-Appearance shows the largest relative PSNR improvement (+68.6%, from 10.153 to 17.118), while NIID-Net gains +33.4% PSNR (12.782→16.152). Among pretrained-only baselines, Colorful Diffuse (PSNR 14.905) and IntrinsicAnything (PSNR 13.422) serve as reference points but are outperformed by all fine-tuned models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four fine-tunable baselines (NIID-Net, Intrinsic Image Diffusion, Marigold-IID-Appearance, RGB↔X) improve substantially after LoRA or full fine-tuning on Olbedo, confirming the dataset's value as a transfer-learning resource. Fine-tuned RGB↔X achieves the best scores on all three metrics (PSNR 17.735, SSIM 0.611, LPIPS 0.413), beating Fine-tuned Intrinsic Image Diffusion (17.249 / 0.531 / 0.485) and Fine-tuned Marigold (17.118 / 0.570 / 0.461) on PSNR and LPIPS, though Marigold edges it on SSIM (0.570 vs. 0.611 is actually RGB↔X winning). Fine-tuned NIID-Net (16.152 PSNR) trails the diffusion-based fine-tuned models by roughly 1–1.6 dB in PSNR but improves its own pretrained version by 3.4 dB. The two pretrained-only baselines, IntrinsicAnything (13.422 PSNR, 0.910 LPIPS) and Colorful Diffuse (14.905 PSNR, 0.554 LPIPS), are outperformed by every fine-tuned model, with IntrinsicAnything showing the weakest perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.910). Notably, even the weakest fine-tuned model (NIID-Net at 16.152 PSNR) surpasses the strongest pretrained-only baseline (Colorful Diffuse at 14.905 PSNR), underscoring that Olbedo fine-tuning provides a consistent 1–7 dB PSNR advantage over simply using a strong pretrained checkpoint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior inverse-rendering pipeline for aerial albedo recovery [36, 37] into a large-scale dataset, scaling the lit-shadow pair formulation and sun-sky illumination model from a methodological contribution to a 5,664-image benchmark. It positions itself against three existing dataset categories—synthetic (InteriorVerse, Hypersim), controlled-lab (MIT Intrinsics, MID), and sparse in-the-wild (IIW)—arguing that none provide dense, real-world outdoor aerial supervision. On the model side, it repurposes state-of-the-art diffusion-based IID architectures (Intrinsic Image Diffusion, Marigold, RGB↔X) and a CNN baseline (NIID-Net) originally trained on indoor or synthetic data, adapting them via lightweight LoRA fine-tuning rather than proposing a new decomposition architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Olbedo fills a clear gap: no prior dataset offered dense, multi-view consistent albedo supervision for real outdoor aerial imagery, which has been a bottleneck for PBR-based 3D content creation and weather-agnostic capture pipelines. The demonstrated applications (relighting, segmentation, material editing, scene change detection) show practical value for digital-twin and urban-planning workflows. Key limitations include the pseudo-ground-truth nature of labels (Lambertian assumption, global-scale ambiguity), limited scene diversity (four landscape types, imbalanced site distribution), and reliance on a single photogrammetry vendor for geometry. Open questions include whether the pseudo-GT quality suffices for non-Lambertian surfaces (glass, specular roofs), whether the approach generalizes beyond the captured sites, and whether true ground-truth albedo can ever be acquired at this scale outdoors.
