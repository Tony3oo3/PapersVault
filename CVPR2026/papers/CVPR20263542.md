## Problem

Existing EEG encoders are largely frequency-agnostic, overlooking the oscillatory neural dynamics (e.g., beta and gamma rhythms) that underpin visual processing, and the cross-modal alignment between EEG representations and visual semantics remains too weak for high-fidelity image generation. This paper tackles both issues by introducing a frequency-aware encoder and a generative cycle-consistency regularizer that jointly enforce tighter EEG–image alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263542/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multi-channel EEG (64- or 128-channel) or MEG (271-channel) time-series recorded during rapid serial visual presentation of natural images, and produces two outputs: a compact EEG embedding in a CLIP-aligned latent space for zero-shot retrieval, and a pixel-space image rendered by a pre-trained SDXL pipeline conditioned via IP-Adapter on a diffusion-generated image embedding. The EEG input is processed as a sequence of channel-by-time samples, while the image side uses frozen CLIP ViT embeddings as the alignment target.

## Method

The FSTDE encoder replaces Mamba's diagonal state matrix with block-diagonal 2×2 oscillatory sub-blocks parameterized by learnable damping (ρ) and angular frequency (ω) per mode, augmented by a learnable log-frequency bias that adaptively tunes each oscillator toward task-relevant bands; spatial topology is captured by a GCN over the electrode graph, and spatio-temporal patterns by EEGNet-style depthwise-separable convolutions. The DDLG introduces two symmetric conditional diffusion modules (E2I and I2E) that denoise Gaussian noise into the opposite modality's embedding using FiLM-conditioned U-Nets, acting as a generative cycle-consistency regularizer in the shared latent space rather than as a final pixel decoder. Training jointly optimizes an InfoNCE contrastive alignment loss plus both diffusion noise-prediction losses (λ=0.5 each), so the learned EEG embedding is simultaneously discriminative for retrieval and generatively informative for synthesis. At inference, the trained FSTDE encodes the EEG, the DDLG's reverse diffusion translates it into an image embedding, and SDXL with IP-Adapter renders the final image.

## Results

On THINGS-EEG (200-way intra-subject retrieval), D²-FOSA achieves 38.0% Top-1 and 70.7% Top-5 accuracy, outperforming the strongest prior method VE-SDN (37.2% / 69.9%). On EEGImageNet, it reaches 31.05% Top-1 and 63.10% Top-5, surpassing MB2C (29.65% / 61.30%). For EEG-to-image reconstruction on THINGS-EEG, D²-FOSA achieves a FID of 146.33, improving over MB2C's 163.94 by 17.61 FID points, while also leading in IS (11.81), KID (0.025), SSIM (0.350), and PCC (0.193).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²-FOSA consistently outperforms all compared baselines across the four evaluated datasets. On THINGS-EEG 200-way intra-subject retrieval, it leads VE-SDN by 0.8% Top-1 (38.0% vs. 37.2%) and MB2C by 9.5% (38.0% vs. 28.5%). On EEGImageNet, it surpasses MB2C by 1.4% Top-1 (31.05% vs. 29.65%) and ATM by 2.5% (31.05% vs. 28.55%). On EEGCVPR40 (raw), it edges out MB2C by 0.47% Top-1 (89.20% vs. 88.73%), while on the 5-95 Hz filtered variant the margin is smaller (93.85% vs. 93.74%) and Top-5 is slightly lower (98.75% vs. 99.18%). For image reconstruction, D²-FOSA achieves a 17.61 FID improvement over MB2C (146.33 vs. 163.94) and also leads on all other metrics (IS, KID, SSIM, PCC). The only marginal loss is Top-5 on EEGCVPR40 (5-95 Hz), where MB2C retains a 0.43% advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mamba's state-space model by replacing the standard diagonal state matrix with block-diagonal oscillatory 2×2 blocks (FOMamba), explicitly encoding damped neural oscillators with learnable frequency biases. It also repurposes denoising diffusion models (DDPMs) in a non-standard role: rather than serving as a final pixel-space decoder as in prior EEG-to-image works (e.g., ATM, MB2C), the Dual Diffusion Latent Generator operates in the shared CLIP-aligned latent space as a bidirectional cycle-consistency regularizer. The spatial and spatio-temporal components draw on GCN and EEGNet-style convolutions, while the final synthesis stage leverages a frozen SDXL pipeline with IP-Adapter conditioning, keeping the generative burden on the pre-trained model rather than the learned encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent bottlenecks in EEG visual decoding—frequency-agnostic encoding and weak cross-modal alignment—through a unified framework validated across four datasets (EEG and MEG). The 17-FID improvement over MB2C on THINGS-EEG reconstruction and consistent retrieval gains suggest that explicit frequency modeling plus generative regularization is a productive direction. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in computational efficiency (1000-step diffusion during training) and cross-subject generalization, where inter-subject performance remains modest (13.1% Top-1 on THINGS-EEG). Open questions include whether the dual-diffusion regularizer can be distilled or replaced by lighter constraints, and whether the frequency-aware SSM generalizes beyond visual tasks to other cognitive domains.
