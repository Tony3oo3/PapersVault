## Problem

While 3D Gaussian Splatting has become a dominant scene representation, encoding general-purpose, transferable features directly from its primitives remains largely unexplored. Prior 3DGS encoders like SceneSplat align predominantly with a single language-teacher signal, leaving broader capabilities—instance grouping, fine spatial structure, and cross-task versatility—unaddressed. The paper asks whether distilling complementary signals from multiple 2D foundation models into a shared 3DGS encoder can yield a holistic scene representation that transfers across diverse downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261456/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a full 3DGS scene (N Gaussians, each with 3D center, anisotropic scale, quaternion orientation, opacity, and RGB color) and produces per-Gaussian feature vectors of dimension 512 in a single feed-forward pass. A point-cloud variant replaces the full Gaussian parameters with only centers, colors, and estimated normals, enabling evaluation on point-cloud-only benchmarks. At inference, the per-Gaussian features can be projected through task-specific heads for segmentation, pooled and fed to a VLM for question answering, or used directly for open-vocabulary query matching.

## Method

Chorus employs a shared 5-stage transformer encoder (adapted from Sonata) over Gaussian primitives, with lightweight 2-layer MLP projectors per teacher to map the shared embedding into each teacher's feature space. Three 2D teachers—SigLIP2 (language-aligned), DINOv3 (generalist), and PE-Spatial (object-aware)—provide supervision via normalized uplifting: 2D feature maps are lifted to per-Gaussian targets using the same alpha-compositing weights as 3DGS rendering, then standardized with PHI-S (PCA rotation + isotropic Hadamard scaling). Training combines per-teacher cosine + SmoothL1 matching losses with optional InfoNCE contrastive terms (class-level for SigLIP2, instance-level for PE-Spatial), using staged introduction of teachers and 3DGS-aware augmentations (rendering-equivalent position noise on low-opacity splats, covariance inflation mimicking early optimization). For out-of-domain adaptation, a render-and-distill recipe replaces offline 3D pseudo-labels with online 2D teacher inference on rendered views, applying the same matching loss in image space.

## Results

On zero-shot 3D semantic segmentation (Table 1), Chorus with joint training (ScanNet + ScanNet++ + MP3D) achieves 24.6 f-mIoU / 47.7 f-mAcc on ScanNet200, 29.6 f-mIoU / 53.5 f-mAcc on ScanNet++, and 15.7 f-mIoU / 24.1 f-mAcc on InteriorGS, outperforming the previous 3DGS SoTA SceneSplat by +2.1/+5.7 f-mIoU and +6.0/+5.8 f-mAcc respectively. On open-vocabulary instance segmentation (Table 2), Chorus reaches 19.6 mAP25 with a +7.6 mAP gain on the 66 tail classes over Mosaic3D. When fully finetuned on point-cloud benchmarks (Table 4), Chorus sets new state-of-the-art on 4 of 5 datasets, including 79.4 mIoU on ScanNet and 40.9 mIoU on ScanNet200 (+6.5 over Sonata), while using ~39.9× fewer training scenes than the point-cloud pretraining baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor SceneSplat [28], Chorus wins on every benchmark: +2.1 f-mIoU on ScanNet200 (24.6 vs 22.5), +4.7 f-mIoU on Matterport3D (18.7 vs 14.0), +1.0 f-mIoU on ScanNet++ (29.6 vs 28.6), and +5.7 f-mIoU on InteriorGS (15.7 vs 10.0) under joint training. Against the point-cloud SoTA Mosaic3D [27], Chorus achieves substantially larger margins (e.g., +8.9 f-mIoU on ScanNet200, +6.6 on InteriorGS) while using 8.32× fewer training scenes. On open-vocabulary instance segmentation, Chorus (19.6 mAP25) surpasses Mosaic3D (17.8) and all other 3D-only methods, with a particularly large +7.6 mAP gain on tail classes (13.0 vs 5.4). On point-cloud finetuning benchmarks, Chorus outperforms the self-supervised Sonata [55] on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., +6.5 mIoU on ScanNet200, +3.0 on ScanNet++) and beats supervised PTv3 [53] on ScanNet200 (40.9 vs 34.7 mIoU). For VQA, swapping Chorus into GaussianVLM improves Nr3D ROUGE from 19.2 to 28.8 and ScanQA ROUGE from 34.8 to 37.4 over the SceneSplat backbone, while reducing training time by ~32%.

## Relation to Prior Work

Chorus extends the lift-then-align paradigm of SceneSplat, which trained a feed-forward 3DGS encoder by distilling a single language-aligned teacher (SigLIP) into per-Gaussian features. The key conceptual departure is importing the multi-teacher distillation paradigm well-established in 2D (RADIO, AM-RADIO, DUNE) into the 3DGS setting for the first time, combining language-aligned (SigLIP2), generalist (DINOv3), and object-aware (PE-Spatial) teachers through a shared backbone with per-teacher projectors. The backbone architecture is adapted from Sonata's 5-stage transformer, and the normalized uplifting mechanism follows Ludvig's learning-free formulation. A notable extension is the point-cloud variant that uses only Gaussian centers, colors, and normals, bridging the 3DGS and point-cloud representation learning literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3DGS can serve as a practical substrate for general-purpose 3D feature learning beyond view synthesis, with the multi-teacher design yielding a single embedding that transfers across semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, open-vocabulary tasks, and VQA. The finding that a point-cloud variant pretrained on 3DGS parameters matches Sonata with ~40× fewer scenes suggests 3DGS optimization dynamics act as an implicit regularizer, opening a path toward unified PC/3DGS encoders. The render-and-distill adaptation recipe removes the need for costly 3D pseudo-label preprocessing, making out-of-domain finetuning more accessible. A remaining limitation is the offline cost of precomputing teacher features for pretraining, and the paper acknowledges the need for a truly unified point-cloud–3DGS encoder as future work.
