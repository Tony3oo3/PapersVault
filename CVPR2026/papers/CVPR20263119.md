## Problem

Existing interleaved multimodal chain-of-thought (i-MCoT) methods for VLMs suffer from two critical limitations: they lack access to low-level visual information (edges, color contrasts, fine-grained details) during reasoning, and they operate in an open-loop fashion where the model cannot re-examine or correct its answers using visual feedback. This leads to poor performance on tasks requiring fine-grained perceptual discrimination, such as identifying subtle visual anomalies among similar objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263119/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (up to 1,003,520 pixels) and a text question asking it to identify the distinguishing visual character of an odd-one-out object and provide its bounding box. During inference, the model may autonomously invoke up to 2 Zoom-In calls and 1 call each of Canny and Color tools, with a maximum sequence length of 12,800 tokens. The final output is a structured response containing a bounding box coordinate and a short text description of the anomalous visual property (e.g., color, orientation, size, shape).

## Method

ForeSight integrates two mechanisms into an RL-driven interleaved reasoning loop over Qwen2.5-VL-7B: (1) a set of low-level visual tools (Canny edge detection, adaptive Zoom-In, and color-difference amplification) that the model learns to invoke mid-reasoning when visual information is insufficient; and (2) a mask-based visual reflection step where, after producing a draft answer with a bounding box, the model masks that region from the image and re-evaluates to verify or revise its prediction (up to 2 iterations). Training is two-stage: first, supervised fine-tuning on 1,425 V²-CoT samples (auto-generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B and rule-cleaned) to establish tool-use and reflection behaviors; then GRPO-based reinforcement learning on 3,929 samples with a composite reward combining format consistency, IoU, F1, tool-usage correctness, and verification alignment. The key insight is that RL with answer-accuracy rewards enables the model to autonomously decide when to invoke tools and when to reflect, rather than following a fixed pipeline.

## Results

On CG-SalBench, ForeSight-7B achieves 62.24% IoU and 84.24% F1, surpassing the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by +29.68% IoU and +19.27% F1. It outperforms all closed-source models on IoU (best competitor: Doubao-Seed-1.6 at 47.66%) and even edges out the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B on F1 (84.24% vs. 82.70%) while remaining within 3.76% on IoU. On generalization benchmarks, ForeSight gains +2.51% on RefCOCO, +2.03% on RefCOCOg, and +37.8 on MME over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, with only a marginal −0.97% drop on MMBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForeSight-7B decisively outperforms its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B on all four metrics (+29.68 IoU, +19.27 F1, +23.06 Precision, +11.37 Recall), confirming the value of the tool-augmented RL training. Against the 10× larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B, ForeSight-7B trails by only 3.76% on IoU but leads on F1 (84.24 vs. 82.70) and Precision (84.52 vs. 82.37), suggesting the reasoning framework partially compensates for model scale. On IoU, ForeSight-7B surpasses every closed-source model tested, beating the strongest (Doubao-Seed-1.6) by 14.58 points; GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Flash, despite high F1/Recall, show very poor localization (IoU 7.85 and 16.41 respectively). On generalization, ForeSight-7B improves over Qwen2.5-VL-7B on all three RefCOCO grounding splits and MME, but slightly underperforms on MMBench (−0.97). Compared to DeepEyes-7B (the closest tool-augmented RL baseline), ForeSight achieves higher IoU on RefCOCO (91.42 vs. 89.8) and RefCOCOg (88.43 vs. 86.7), though it is marginally lower on RefCOCO+ (83.66 vs. 83.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interleaved multimodal chain-of-thought (i-MCoT) paradigm by integrating two previously separate ideas: low-level visual perception tools (Canny edge detection, zoom-in, color amplification) and a mask-based visual reflection mechanism, both driven by GRPO reinforcement learning. It departs from prior tool-augmented VLMs like DeepEyes and DriveAgent-R1, which focus on high-level operations (RoI scaling, 3D detection, depth estimation), by targeting fundamental low-level visual cues that humans naturally use. The closed-loop 'reasoning → answer → visual feedback → re-reasoning' cycle distinguishes ForeSight from GThinker's text-centric reflection and from standard open-loop VLM inference, positioning it as the first framework to combine autonomous tool invocation with iterative visual self-verification under a single RL objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 7B model equipped with low-level tools and visual reflection can approach the grounding performance of a 72B model (62.24 vs. 66.00 IoU) suggests that targeted perceptual augmentation can partially substitute for scale, offering a practical path for resource-constrained deployments. However, the evaluation is confined to a single newly constructed benchmark (CG-SalBench) built on one base model, and generalization results are mixed (MMBench drops while MME improves), leaving open whether the approach transfers to diverse visual reasoning tasks beyond odd-one-out saliency. A key open question is whether the specific tool set (Canny, zoom-in, color) is optimal or whether richer low-level operations (texture, frequency analysis) would yield further gains, and whether the mask-based reflection generalizes to tasks without clear spatial grounding.
