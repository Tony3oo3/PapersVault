## Problem

Existing skeleton-based Temporal Action Segmentation methods suffer from inter-class confusion and blurred segmentation boundaries because their temporal modeling (TCNs, Transformers) inherently acts as a low-pass filter, smoothing away the subtle spectral differences between adjacent actions. No prior STAS work has explicitly exploited frequency-domain analysis to amplify action-specific periodicities and sharpen transition boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261092/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a skeleton sequence X ∈ R^{C0×T×V} (joint positions across T frames and V joints) and produces frame-wise class predictions Y^c ∈ R^{Q×T} and boundary regression scores Y^b ∈ R^{1×T}. At inference, final segmentation is obtained by jointly combining class and boundary predictions via the ASRF postprocessing scheme. The architecture operates with C=64 feature channels, M=4 multi-scale spectral filters (max length 64), and is evaluated on sequences up to 6000 frames on PKU-MMD.

## Method

Spectral Scalpel inserts a frequency modeling stage between spatial and temporal processing. The Multi-scale Adaptive Spectral Filter (MASF) applies FFT along the temporal axis, then performs scale-specific filtering via learnable spectral kernels (interpolated to spectral resolution and applied as Hadamard products), fusing multi-scale results through a dual-branch dynamic-static channel-wise weighting scheme. The Adjacent Action Discrepancy Loss (AADL) segments filtered features by ground-truth boundaries, computes amplitude spectra, and maximizes the spectral difference between adjacent action segments, providing a direct training signal for the filter to suppress shared frequencies and amplify action-specific ones. A Frequency-Aware Channel Mixer (FACM) is embedded in each temporal block, performing FFT on channel features, mixing real and imaginary components via shared point-wise convolutions, and projecting back to the time domain to enable frequency-guided channel evolution alongside Linear Transformer temporal modeling.

## Results

On PKU-MMD v2 (X-view), Spectral Scalpel achieves 76.5% Acc, 74.5 Edit, and 67.2 F1@50, surpassing the previous best STAS method (LPL: 70.0/67.7/58.5) by +6.5/+6.8/+8.7 points respectively. On PKU-MMD v2 (X-sub), it reaches 75.4 Acc, 74.5 Edit, and 66.6 F1@50, improving over LPL (74.7/72.7/64.3) by +0.7/+1.8/+2.3. On the remaining datasets, it attains 67.6 F1@50 on MCFS-130 (+1.0 over LaSA), 74.7 F1@50 on TCG-15 (+0.9 over LPL), and 59.4 F1@50 on LARa (+0.8 over LPL), while using fewer parameters (1.44M) and lower FLOPs (11.56G) than most competing STAS methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spectral Scalpel outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary F1@50 metric across all five evaluation settings. The largest gains are on PKU-MMD v2 (X-view), where it surpasses LPL (58.5) by +8.7 F1@50 and LaSA (57.0) by +10.2, while also leading on Acc (+6.5 over LPL) and Edit (+6.8 over LPL). On PKU-MMD v2 (X-sub), it edges out LPL by +2.3 F1@50 and +1.8 Edit, and LaSA by +3.0 F1@50. On MCFS-130, it wins F1@50 by +1.0 over LaSA (67.6 vs 66.6) and F1@10 by +0.4, but trails LaSA on Edit distance (78.0 vs 79.3). On TCG-15, it ties LPL on F1@10 (81.9) and F1@25 (80.0) while winning F1@50 by +0.9 (74.7 vs 73.8) and Acc by +0.2, though it is slightly behind on Edit (77.0 vs 77.4). On LARa, it leads LPL on F1@50 (+0.8) and F1@25 (+0.1) but is marginally behind on Edit (64.6 vs 65.2) and F1@10 (72.1 vs 72.3). Notably, Spectral Scalpel achieves these results with 1.44M parameters and 11.56G FLOPs, substantially fewer than ME-ST (3.42M/125.9G), MTST-GCN (2.90M/141.9G), and LPL (3.23M/20.77G).

## Relation to Prior Work

Spectral Scalpel builds directly on the STAS pipeline of Ji et al. [27] (spatial GCN + Linear Transformer temporal blocks + ASRF-style prediction refinement) and uses DeST [35] as its architectural baseline. Its core departure is the introduction of frequency-domain processing into skeleton-based action segmentation: prior FFT-based methods in vision (AFF [23], DFFormer [48]) learn filters in an unconstrained, data-driven manner, whereas here the Multi-scale Adaptive Spectral Filter is explicitly guided by a new Adjacent Action Discrepancy Loss that maximizes spectral separation between neighboring action segments. The Frequency-Aware Channel Mixer further extends the temporal modeling stack by performing channel mixing in the spectral domain, a design not previously explored in STAS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to systematically incorporate frequency-domain analysis into skeleton-based temporal action segmentation, offering a principled explanation for why existing temporal models (TCNs, Transformers) blur boundaries: they act as implicit low-pass filters that erase action-specific high-frequency cues. The consistent SOTA gains across five diverse datasets (daily activities, figure skating, warehouse, traffic gestures) with minimal overhead (+0.5 G FLOPs total) suggest the frequency-selective filtering paradigm is broadly applicable. Open questions remain around whether the discrepancy loss generalizes to open-vocabulary or few-shot settings, whether adaptive local (rather than global) spectral editing could further sharpen boundaries, and whether the approach transfers to video-based TAS or other dense prediction tasks.
