## Problem

Existing multi-person 3D motion recovery methods (e.g., 4DHumans, CoMotion) rely on 2D appearance features and Hungarian matching for identity association, which degrades severely under fast motion, heavy occlusion, and viewpoint changes, causing frequent ID switches and fragmented 3D trajectories. They also lack memory-based motion priors, so reconstructions become discontinuous once a subject is occluded. The paper tackles the gap of achieving stable, zero-shot, real-time multi-person 3D motion recovery from monocular in-the-wild video without dataset-specific retraining.

## Input / Output

RAM consumes full-frame monocular RGB video (no oracle cropping) and produces per-person SMPL pose and shape parameters, 3D joint positions, projected 2D keypoints, and consistent tracking identity assignments at 10.32 FPS on PoseTrack21. The system operates online in a streaming fashion, maintaining a FIFO motion queue and a memory bank that are updated frame-by-frame, enabling real-time inference on long sequences (up to ~40 s in TrackID-3x3 outdoor). All evaluation is zero-shot: the model is never retrained on PoseTrack, 3DPW, COCO, or TrackID-3x3.

## Method

RAM integrates four components: (1) SegFollow augments SAM2 with a Kalman-filter-based motion-guided selector that fuses IoU-based motion consistency with mask affinity via a gated sum, plus a confidence-gated posterior update and an exponential-moving-average temporal buffer modulated by motion confidence, replacing SAM2's naive FIFO memory; (2) T-HMR uses a dual-branch attention Memory Cache to select top-k relevant frames from a temporal window, then a MemFormer with stacked self-attention, temporal cross-attention, and spatial cross-attention blocks to inject these priors into SMPL regression; (3) a lightweight Transformer Predictor forecasts the next-frame latent motion state from a FIFO queue of past reconstructions; (4) a Combiner applies a sigmoid-gated element-wise interpolation between the T-HMR reconstruction feature and the predicted motion prior, shifting reliance toward prediction under occlusion. Training follows a three-stage protocol: pretraining T-HMR on single images with multi-term SMPL losses, training the Predictor on 8-frame sequences with scheduled sampling, and fine-tuning the full pipeline with 60% random body-region masking to simulate occlusion.

## Results

On PoseTrack18, RAM (zero-shot) achieves a HOTA of 66.4 with only 15 ID switches, compared to 58.2 HOTA and 232 IDs for CoMotion and 57.8 HOTA with 382 IDs for 4DHumans. On 3DPW, RAM attains the lowest 3D reconstruction error with 53.0 MPJPE and 34.1 PA-MPJPE, outperforming CoMotion (60.0 / 37.3) and HMR 2.0a (70.0 / 44.5). For 2D pose estimation, RAM reaches 0.93 PCK@0.05 on PoseTrack and 0.89 on COCO, surpassing HMR 2.0b (0.90 / 0.86). RAM also runs at 10.32 FPS on PoseTrack21, roughly 2× faster than CoMotion (5.68 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAM outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. Against the two primary multi-person 3D recovery methods, RAM achieves +8.2 HOTA and reduces ID switches from 232 to 15 over CoMotion on PoseTrack18, and +78%/+116% TI-HOTA over CoMotion on TrackID-3x3 indoor/outdoor. On 3D reconstruction (3DPW), RAM lowers MPJPE from 60.0 (CoMotion) to 53.0 and PA-MPJPE from 37.3 to 34.1, a 7-point and 3.2-point improvement respectively. Against single-person baselines (PyMAF, CLIFF, PARE, PyMAF-X, HMR 2.0), RAM leads on both 2D PCK and 3D MPJPE, with the largest gaps over PyMAF (MPJPE 53.0 vs 92.8) and CLIFF (MPJPE 53.0 vs 69.0). Notably, RAM achieves these gains in a zero-shot setting without retraining on PoseTrack or 3DPW, whereas 4DHumans and CoMotion are trained on those benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAM extends the SMPL-regression lineage from HMR and 4D-Humans by adding temporal memory (MemFormer) and a motion predictor, while its tracking backbone departs from the appearance-matching + Hungarian-algorithm paradigm used by 4DHumans and CoMotion. Instead, it builds on SAM2's segmentation foundation but replaces the naive FIFO memory with a Kalman-filter-guided selector and confidence-weighted exponential moving average, explicitly injecting motion dynamics into identity association. The gated Combiner fusing reconstruction and prediction is a novel architectural choice not present in prior multi-person 3D recovery systems, which typically rely on frame-wise regression or queue-based trajectory matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAM's ability to outperform dataset-trained baselines in a zero-shot setting on long, in-the-wild sports videos (TrackID-3x3, Olympic Boxing) suggests a path toward deployable markerless 3D motion capture without per-scene retraining, which is practically significant for sports analytics, rehabilitation, and virtual content creation. The 2–3× speedup over prior methods also addresses a key scalability bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the SMPL parametric model to more expressive body representations, how it handles human-object interaction (acknowledged as future work), and whether the zero-shot advantage persists on truly unseen domains (e.g., medical or industrial settings) rather than sports-adjacent scenes.
