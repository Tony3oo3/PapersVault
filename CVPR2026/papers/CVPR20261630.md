## Problem

Existing event aggregation strategies for event-based object detection continuously accumulate all events within sampled time intervals without selective filtering, inevitably embedding uninformative events (sensor artifacts, motion-related blur) that degrade detection accuracy. The paper identifies this as a fundamental limitation of the 'continuous aggregation' paradigm and asks whether a differentiable, spike-driven mechanism can adaptively retain only informative events for aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams (304×240 for Gen1, 1280×720 for 1Mpx) that are first pre-aggregated via Event Count into dense tensors of shape T_a × 2 × H × W at a fixed 20 ms temporal resolution, then processed by the SDA module. Output is standard COCO-format bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated via mAP_50:95 and mAP_50. The full pipeline is a fully spiking YOLOX-M/S detector (P-LIF + SEW-Residual blocks, 3 time steps), though SDA/SDA-MTF are also shown to plug into ANN backbones (CNN, Transformer, Transformer+RNN).

## Method

The core contribution is the Spiking Discrete Aggregation (SDA) module, which assigns a Gated Recurrent Spiking Neuron (GRSN) to each (x, y, polarity) coordinate and aggregates only those events that drive the neuron to fire a spike, coupling selection and accumulation into a single differentiable operation. The GRSN replaces the LIF constant decay with a learnable reset gate R (filtering accumulated noise) and adds a forget gate F on the input current, with all gate computations using 3×3 convolutions to capture local spatial correlations. Multi-Timescale Fusion (MTF) introduces a coarse-grained branch (SAS with adaptive sampling between spike times at 60 ms intervals) whose membrane potentials generate a soft mask that modulates SDA's input current, enriching the representation with longer-timescale temporal context. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end via surrogate gradients through BPTT, and the paper demonstrates 43.4% mAP_50:95 on Gen1 with 3.79× lower energy than ANN baselines.

## Results

On Gen1, DASNN-MTF (medium, 25.3M params) achieves 43.4% mAP₅₀:₉₅ and 73.1% mAP₅₀, surpassing the prior best fully spiking detector SpikeYOLO (38.9%) by 4.5%. The small variant (8.9M params) reaches 40.5% mAP₅₀:₉₅, still outperforming SpikeYOLO by 1.6%. On 1Mpx, DASNN-MTF(M) achieves 30.4% mAP₅₀:₉₅ (the first fully spiking model evaluated on this dataset), and on N-Caltech101 it reaches 40.1% mAP₅₀:₉₅. Energy consumption is 3.79× lower than the ANN-based YOLOX-S (15.66 mJ vs. 59.35 mJ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among fully spiking detectors, DASNN-MTF(M) clearly dominates: it outperforms SpikeYOLO [23] by 4.5% mAP₅₀:₉₅ (43.4 vs. 38.9), EAS-SNN [36] by 5.9% (43.4 vs. 37.5), CREST [24] by 7.4% (43.4 vs. 36.0), and EMS-ResNet34 [34] by 12.4% (43.4 vs. 31.0) on Gen1. On 1Mpx, DASNN-MTF(M) beats EAS-SNN by 2.3% mAP₅₀:₉₅ (30.4 vs. 28.1), and on N-Caltech101 it gains 3.8% mAP₅₀:₉₅ (40.1 vs. 36.3) over EAS-SNN. However, against strong ANN-based methods the gap remains: SMamba [39] (50.4%), RVT [11] (47.2%), and ASTMNet [18] (46.7%) all exceed DASNN-MTF's 43.4% on Gen1, confirming that the paper's SOTA claim is scoped to fully spiking architectures. The small model (8.9M) still beats SpikeYOLO (23.1M) by 1.6% mAP₅₀:₉₅, demonstrating parameter efficiency. In energy terms, DASNN(S) consumes 15.66 mJ versus 59.35 mJ for ANN YOLOX-S (3.79× reduction) and slightly less than EAS-SNN(S) at 15.91 mJ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EAS-SNN [36], which introduced adaptive sampling and recurrent spiking representations for event-based detection, but departs from its core aggregation strategy: where EAS-SNN and most prior SNN-based methods perform continuous accumulation of all events between spike times, SDA restricts aggregation to only those events that actually trigger a spike, reframing event representation as a discrete selection problem. The gated recurrent spiking neuron extends the standard LIF model with learnable reset and forget gates, borrowing the gating philosophy from GRUs while retaining the biologically-motivated spike-firing threshold. The Multi-Timescale Fusion component reuses the adaptive sampling mechanism from EAS-SNN but introduces a soft mask that modulates the SDA input current, addressing the observation that naive addition of multi-scale representations does not improve detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discrete aggregation perspective offers a principled, differentiable mechanism for filtering uninformative events that is tightly coupled to the SNN's own dynamics, and the demonstrated compatibility with ANN backbones (CNN, Transformer, Transformer+RNN) broadens its applicability beyond the spiking niche. The 3.79× energy reduction over ANN baselines and superior noise robustness are practically relevant for embedded and automotive deployment. However, the absolute performance gap versus strong ANN baselines (43.4% vs. 47.2% mAP on Gen1) remains, and the energy analysis relies on estimated costs for recurrent connections and soft masks rather than measured neuromorphic hardware, leaving open whether the theoretical efficiency gains materialize in practice.
