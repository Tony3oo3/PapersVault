## Problem

Existing token compression methods for Video-LLMs operate at the late stage (after the vision encoder or inside the LLM), leaving the vision encoder as an unoptimized bottleneck. Profiling reveals that vision encoding accounts for 36.3% of TTFT in the baseline and rises to 55.8–68.4% in state-of-the-art methods like HoliTom and VisionZip, yet no prior work performs compression inside the encoder itself. This paper asks whether early-stage compression within the vision encoder can yield substantially greater TTFT reduction without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes 32 uniformly sampled video frames (per LLaVA-OneVision's default configuration) and a text prompt as input, and produces a standard text response as output. Internally, the 32 frames are progressively compressed inside the SigLIP vision encoder via frame merging, then further reduced by spatial token selection before entering the LLM, with the final retained-token ratio configurable from 10% to 25%. The method is entirely training-free and plugs into LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B/7B without any weight modification.

## Method

EarlyTom consists of two training-free stages. Stage 1 (inner vision encoder frame merging) segments the video stream using EMA-smoothed cosine similarity between consecutive frames, merges redundant middle frames within each segment via a local-optimal criterion (merging only when similarity exceeds a threshold and is higher than the next pair), and fuses merged pairs with similarity-weighted averaging to preserve semantic content. Stage 2 (decoupled spatial token selection) splits merged frames into dynamic (head/tail per segment) and static (middle) subsets: dynamic frames undergo global Top-K selection on per-token attention scores, while static frames use local-window Top-K selection to maintain spatial distribution and avoid bias from attention sink tokens. A system-level co-design offloads static token selection to the CPU while the GPU handles dynamic selection, exploiting otherwise idle CPU capacity for further latency reduction.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, EarlyTom achieves a 2.65× TTFT reduction (889.9 ms → 336.2 ms) and 61% FLOPs reduction (82.6T → 32.2T) at 10% token retention, while maintaining 96.2% of the full-token baseline's average accuracy (56.2 vs. 58.4 across MVBench, EgoSchema, LongVideoBench, and VideoMME). At 25% retention, EarlyTom reaches 426.3 ms TTFT and 32.9 tokens/s throughput with 99.7% baseline accuracy (58.2). The method also generalizes to the 0.5B backbone, achieving 280.1 ms TTFT and 43.9 tokens/s at 10% retention with 97.3% baseline accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EarlyTom dominates all seven baselines on both TTFT and FLOPs across every retention ratio tested. At 10% retention, EarlyTom's 336.2 ms TTFT beats VisionZip (458.5 ms) by 122 ms, FastVID (502.1 ms) by 166 ms, HoliTom (556.6 ms) by 220 ms, and PruneVid (592.2 ms) by 256 ms; its 32.2T FLOPs is roughly 13T lower than the next-best method (HoliTom at 44.6T). On accuracy, EarlyTom is competitive but not always the best: at 25% retention, HoliTom edges ahead with 58.8 vs. 58.2 average score, while at 10% retention HoliTom leads by 1.7 points (57.9 vs. 56.2). However, EarlyTom's accuracy degrades far less under aggressive compression—dropping only 4% from baseline at 10% retention versus VisionZip's ~9% drop. FastV and PyramidDrop, which compress inside the LLM, show minimal TTFT improvement (820/813 ms) and are clearly outperformed. DyCoke is the weakest baseline, with TTFT actually exceeding the uncompressed baseline (905.6 ms vs. 889.9 ms) due to its compression overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

EarlyTom extends the video-LLM token compression literature by shifting the compression point from the post-encoder or inner-LLM stages (as in VisionZip, FastV, PyramidDrop, HoliTom) into the vision encoder itself, motivated by profiling that reveals vision encoding dominates TTFT—especially in already-optimized methods where it reaches 68.4%. It incorporates HoliTom's inner-LLM merging as a component but adds two novel elements: streaming frame merging with EMA-based segmentation inside the encoder, and a decoupled dynamic/static spatial selection that addresses the attention-sink bias observed in SigLIP. The method also introduces a CPU-GPU heterogeneous offloading strategy for static token selection, a system-level co-design not present in prior training-free compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.65× TTFT reduction and 61% FLOPs reduction at 10% token retention, while maintaining ~96% of full-token accuracy, make Video-LLM inference substantially more practical for latency-sensitive production workloads such as large-scale video retrieval. The profiling insight that vision encoding—not LLM prefill—is the dominant bottleneck in already-optimized pipelines redirects future efficiency research toward encoder-stage optimization. Limitations include evaluation primarily on LLaVA-OneVision with SigLIP, reliance on attention-score-based selection that may not transfer to all vision encoders, and the added system complexity of CPU-GPU offloading. Open questions include whether the decoupled dynamic/static split generalizes to non-SigLIP encoders and whether even more aggressive compression (below 10%) remains viable.
