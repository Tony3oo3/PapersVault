## Problem

Reconstructing 3D scenes from low-resolution inputs produces blurry textures and missing fine structures that become severe under zoom-in rendering. Existing 3D super-resolution methods either apply per-view image SR (lacking cross-view geometric consistency) or rely on optical-flow-based video SR (failing under occlusion and large parallax), and neither can generate semantically plausible details beyond what is observable in the LR input. The paper argues that zoom-in 3D reconstruction is fundamentally a progressive generative process requiring iterative coupling of geometry-consistent modeling with semantic-guided detail synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes posed low-resolution image sequences with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics; on Mip-NeRF360 the LR inputs are at 1/8 of the original ~3000×4000 resolution with HR targets at 1/2, while Tanks&Temples uses 1/4→1 pairs. For the extreme zoom-in evaluation (up to 64×), the region of interest is defined as the intersection of all camera frustums, and smooth trajectories with continuously varying focal lengths are rendered. The output is a multi-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting representation organized in an expandable continuous LoD hierarchy, where each zoom step adds a new layer of semantically generated high-frequency Gaussians while freezing previous layers, enabling alias-free rendering across the full magnification range.

## Method

GaussianZoom performs iterative progressive zoom-in (per-step factor s=4) with three coupled components. First, a depth-based feature warping module replaces the optical-flow alignment in the DLoRAL video SR backbone with dense geometric correspondence computed from per-view depth maps of a geometry-regularized 3DGS (RaDe-GS), ensuring accurate cross-view feature propagation. Second, a VLM (Qwen-VL 2.5-3B-Instruct fine-tuned by Chain-of-Zoom) processes paired coarse and zoomed-in renderings to infer textual semantic cues (materials, textures) that condition the SR network to synthesize new details beyond the observed resolution. Third, an expandable continuous LoD representation dynamically modulates each Gaussian's opacity via a logarithmic attenuation function w(ψ′/ψ) = max(0, 1−|log_s(ψ′/ψ)|) based on its scale projection coefficient ψ=d/f, producing smooth alias-free transitions without discrete level switching. Training uses a dual-scale loss that simultaneously supervises the HR rendering and its bicubic-downsampled version against the LR input, combined with RaDe-GS geometry regularization, to prevent cross-scale conflicts from accumulating.

## Results

On the 4× super-resolution benchmark, GaussianZoom achieves the best scores across all metrics on both datasets: PSNR 27.16 / SSIM 0.781 / LPIPS 0.261 / FID 19.38 on Mip-NeRF360 (1/8→1/2) and PSNR 23.40 / SSIM 0.812 / LPIPS 0.265 / FID 14.91 on Tanks&Temples (1/4→1). Under the extreme zoom-in setting (16×, 32×, 64× magnification), the method leads on all no-reference metrics, e.g. CLIPIQA 0.436 and NIQE 5.53 at 64×, substantially outperforming the closest baseline (SRGS: CLIPIQA 0.346, NIQE 15.54). The FVD ablation confirms that depth-based warping reduces temporal inconsistency from 168.36 to 107.99 on Mip-NeRF360.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianZoom outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric in both the 4× SR and extreme zoom-in settings. Against the strongest 4× SR competitors, Sequence Matters [14] and SRGS [6], the gains are modest on PSNR/SSIM (e.g. +0.21 dB and +0.005 SSIM on Mip-NeRF360) but substantial on perceptual metrics: FID drops from 26.64 (Sequence Matters) to 19.38, a 27% reduction, reflecting better multi-view coherence. SuperGaussian [24] and plain 3DGS [10] lag far behind, with FID scores of 58.16 and 60.48 respectively on Mip-NeRF360, indicating severe cross-view inconsistency from flow-based or per-view enhancement. Under extreme zoom-in (16×–64×), the advantage widens dramatically: at 64×, GaussianZoom scores CLIPIQA 0.436 vs. 0.346 (SRGS) and 0.302 (Sequence Matters), and NIQE 5.53 vs. 15.54 and 15.25, showing that the VLM-guided semantic synthesis and continuous LoD hierarchy are critical at large magnifications where flow-based methods collapse. Mip-Splatting [39], which does not employ super-resolution, serves as a lower bound and is outperformed by all SR-based methods but still beats plain 3DGS on FID (25.67 vs. 60.48), confirming that aliasing suppression alone is insufficient without detail generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianZoom extends the 3D super-resolution line (SRGS, SuperGaussian, Sequence Matters) by replacing their flow-based or per-view SR pipelines with a depth-guided warping mechanism derived from a geometry-regularized 3DGS reconstruction, and by adding VLM-driven semantic conditioning inspired by Chain-of-Zoom's 2D progressive zoom strategy. It repurposes DLoRAL as a video SR backbone and RaDe-GS for depth extraction, but the key architectural departure is the iterative coupling of 3D reconstruction with generative detail synthesis at each zoom step. The continuous LoD hierarchy reinterprets the conventional efficiency-oriented LoD structures (Octree-GS, FLod, Lodge) as a generative scaffold that grows with magnification rather than a static rendering optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that extreme zoom-in 3D reconstruction (up to 64×) is best framed as a progressive generative process anchored in geometry, providing a practical pipeline that outperforms prior 3D SR methods on both fidelity and multi-view consistency. The continuous LoD with logarithmic opacity attenuation is a clean mechanism for alias-free cross-scale rendering that could be adopted by other 3DGS-based systems. However, the authors acknowledge that current VLMs struggle at very high magnifications (e.g., ×1024), leaving open the question of how to scale semantic detail synthesis to truly microscopic or cosmic scales, and whether more capable generative models can close this gap.
