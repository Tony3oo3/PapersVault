## Problem

Flow matching, the current strongest generative paradigm, has never been applied to human pose due to two barriers: no pre-trained flow prior exists for articulated poses, and human pose is inherently non-Euclidean, living on the product manifold SO(3)^K of joint rotations. Furthermore, using such a prior to solve inverse problems (pose completion, denoising, HMR) requires training-free guidance that respects this geometry, which no prior method provides.

## Input / Output

Depending on the task, the method consumes partially observed joint rotations (pose completion), noisy 3D joint trajectories at 30 Hz in 60-frame segments (denoising), or a 2D image with ViT-Pose-predicted 2D keypoints (HMR). All outputs are SMPL parameters: 21 joint rotations in SO(3)^K (189-dim) plus 16 shape parameters β, from which 22 joint positions and 6890 mesh vertices are obtained via forward kinematics. The optimization operates entirely on the manifold, with the source point x_0 initialized randomly in SO(3)^K and updated via Riemannian Adam.

## Method

PoseD-Flow unifies two components. First, PoseRFM is the first Riemannian Flow Matching model of human pose, trained on AMASS to regress the conditional vector field u_t(x|x_1) = (1/(1-t)) Log_x(x_1) on the product manifold SO(3)^K using geodesic Gaussian kernels and a 4-layer MLP (512 hidden dim, 50K steps). Second, Riemannian D-Flow is a training-free guidance mechanism that inverts the frozen flow by optimizing the source point x_0 on the manifold: at each iteration it integrates the Riemannian ODE forward to obtain x_1, backpropagates a task-specific data loss plus a trajectory regularizer (penalizing large rotation angles) through the ODE, and updates x_0 via Riemannian Adam with parallel transport of momentum. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–4) shows the induced endpoint update is filtered by a self-adjoint operator resembling local data covariance, with an additional curvature correction from the logarithmic map's base-point dependence, yielding an implicit bias toward realistic, high-density poses.

## Results

On unconditional pose generation (AMASS test split), PoseRFM achieves the best FID (0.013–0.014) and nearest-neighbor distance (0.069–0.070 rad), outperforming DPoser (FID 0.019, d_NN 0.073) and the Euclidean PoseFM baseline (FID 0.016, d_NN 0.079). On motion denoising, PoseRFM reduces MPJPE to 18.88 mm at 40 mm noise (AMASS) and 32.95 mm at 100 mm noise (HPS), beating DPoser (19.87 mm and 35.32 mm respectively). On human mesh recovery (EHF), PoseRFM achieves 54.85 mm PA-MPJPE from scratch and 47.24 mm with CLIFF initialization, surpassing DPoser (56.05 mm and 49.05 mm) and all other priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoseRFM outperforms all 10 baselines on generative realism (FID and d_NN), with the closest competitor being DPoser (FID 0.019 vs. 0.014) and the Euclidean PoseFM ablation (FID 0.016 vs. 0.014), confirming the benefit of the Riemannian formulation. On pose completion, PoseRFM beats DPoser on torso occlusion (39.75 vs. 44.60 mm MPVPE) and legs (95.00 vs. 102.46 mm), though DPoser retains a slight edge on left-leg (78.31 vs. 83.81 mm) and arms (104.94 vs. 107.36 mm). For motion denoising, PoseRFM wins 3 of 4 settings against DPoser, with the largest margin at 100 mm HPS noise (32.95 vs. 35.32 mm MPJPE), while DPoser edges ahead at 100 mm AMASS (33.18 vs. 34.68 mm). On HMR, PoseRFM consistently beats DPoser under both initialization schemes (54.85 vs. 56.05 mm from scratch; 47.24 vs. 49.05 mm with CLIFF) and also surpasses all other priors (VPoser, GAN-S, Pose-NDF, NRDF, GMM) by 1–3 mm PA-MPJPE. The Euclidean PoseFM baseline is notably weaker than PoseRFM across all inversion tasks, validating the geometric modeling choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: Riemannian Flow Matching (RFM) from Chen & Lipman [14], which extends flow matching to general manifolds, and D-Flow [4], which inverts frozen flow models by backpropagating through ODE sampling. Prior human pose priors (DPoser, Pose-NDF, NRDF, HuMoR) rely on diffusion, VAEs, or neural distance fields, and no unconditional flow matching model of human pose existed before this work. The key departure is that Riemannian D-Flow is the first training-free guidance mechanism for RFM, extending D-Flow's source-point optimization from Euclidean space to the SO(3)^K product manifold with a Riemannian Adam update, while the theoretical analysis reveals an implicit bias shaped jointly by data covariance and manifold curvature that has no Euclidean analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first RFM pose prior and a principled, training-free inversion mechanism, the paper offers a single framework that handles pose completion, denoising, and inverse kinematics without task-specific retraining, which is practically valuable for deploying pose priors across diverse downstream applications. The theoretical characterization of the implicit bias (Theorems 1–4) gives a geometric explanation for why the method is robust under noise and occlusion, going beyond empirical observation. Key limitations include the lack of real-time performance due to full backpropagation through the ODE, and the authors note that diversity could be improved by injecting stochasticity during inversion. Extending the framework to full motion sequences (beyond single poses) is identified as the natural next step.
