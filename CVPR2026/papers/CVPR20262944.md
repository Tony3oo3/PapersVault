## Problem

Existing VLN agents lack an explicit mechanism to track how far they have advanced within a multi-step instruction. Vision-Language-Action models bury progress implicitly in end-to-end action prediction, while traditional progress estimators rely on numeric proxies (geometric ratios, remaining distance) that quantify spatial displacement but provide no signal about the agent's position within the instruction semantics. This gap hinders coherent long-horizon behavior and interpretability, particularly because the monotonic co-progression structure between accumulating observations and evolving instruction prefixes has been overlooked by prior methods.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language instruction, the current egocentric RGB frame, and a sampled history of past observations at each step, producing a 3-step discrete action sequence (forward at 25/50/75 cm, turn left/right at 15°/30°/45°, or stop) conditioned on a predicted semantic progress span. The progress output is an instruction-style text prefix (a partial sub-instruction), not a scalar completion ratio, and is constrained to be no longer than the full instruction. The system operates on monocular RGB only, without depth or panoramic inputs.

## Method

Progress-Think introduces a Progress Reasoning Module (PRM) that predicts instruction-style progress from visual history, coupled with a Progress-Guided VLA policy, trained in three stages. Stage 1 (Self-Aligned Progress Pretraining) bootstraps the PRM without external annotations via a Prefix-Subset Soft Cross-Entropy Loss that aligns predicted progress with instruction prefixes and a Monotonic Ordering Loss enforcing temporal consistency across timesteps. Stage 2 (Progress-Guided Policy Pretraining) freezes the PRM and trains the VLA policy with cross-entropy on expert and DAgger trajectories, conditioning actions on the predicted progress. Stage 3 (Progress-Policy Co-Finetuning) jointly optimizes both modules via GRPO over groups of 4 rollouts, using a composite reward combining stepwise action correctness, format validity, and a progress-length penalty that prevents over-optimistic completion estimates. The entire framework is built on NVILA-2B backbones and requires no sub-goal or progress annotations beyond the instruction itself.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, Progress-Think achieves SR=60.1, SPL=53.6, OSR=63.6, and NE=4.68 using only monocular RGB with zero external data, outperforming the strongest prior monocular methods (MonoDream: SR=55.8, SPL=49.1; NaVILA: SR=54.0, SPL=49.0) by 4.3 and 4.5 points in SR and SPL respectively. On the unseen-dataset generalization test (RxR-CE Val-Unseen, trained only on R2R-CE), Progress-Think reaches SR=27.5 and SPL=22.7, surpassing MonoDream (SR=25.1, SPL=21.6) and NaVid (SR=23.8, SPL=21.2). The model also achieves faster inference (36.6 s/episode) with a smaller parameter count (2B+2B) compared to 8B-parameter baselines like NaVILA (56.8 s) and Aux-Think (58.0 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Progress-Think outperforms all monocular RGB baselines on R2R-CE Val-Unseen, achieving the highest SR (60.1 vs. 55.8 for MonoDream, 55.7 for Aux-Think, 54.0 for NaVILA) and SPL (53.6 vs. 49.1, 48.7, 49.0 respectively), while also attaining the lowest NE (4.68). Notably, it surpasses depth+panorama methods like BEVBert (SR=59.0) and ETPNav (SR=57.0) using only a single RGB camera, and does so without any external data (0K vs. 1500K–2300K for NaVid-4D, Uni-NaVid, NaVILA). On the cross-dataset generalization test (RxR-CE Val-Unseen, trained solely on R2R-CE), Progress-Think leads MonoDream by +2.4 SR and +1.1 SPL, and NaVid by +3.7 SR and +1.5 SPL. In the efficiency comparison (Table 6), Progress-Think uses a 2B+2B parameter model and completes episodes in 36.6 s, roughly 1.5× faster than the 8B NaVILA (56.8 s) and Aux-Think (58.0 s) while delivering higher SR. The ablation on progress variants (Table 3) further shows that semantic progress reasoning (SR=43.8, SPL=38.5) substantially outperforms numeric progress regression (SR=33.4, SPL=26.2) and full-instruction reconstruction (SR=37.7, SPL=32.1), confirming the core design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VLA navigation paradigm (NaVILA, NaVid, Uni-NaVid) and the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath, but departs from prior progress estimation approaches that rely on numeric completion ratios or externally annotated subgoals. The key conceptual departure is reframing progress as semantic instruction-prefix alignment rather than a scalar, and deriving self-supervision from the instruction's inherent sequential structure (monotonic co-progression) rather than from human or LLM-generated labels. The three-stage pipeline (self-aligned pretraining → progress-guided policy pretraining → GRPO co-finetuning) couples a dedicated progress reasoning module with the action policy in a way that prior VLA systems, which bury progress implicitly in end-to-end action prediction, do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The annotation-free formulation of semantic progress reasoning removes a significant practical barrier: prior progress-aware navigation methods required expensive subgoal annotations or noisy LLM-generated reasoning labels, limiting their applicability. The finding that a 2B+2B two-module architecture outperforms 8B single-model baselines (NaVILA, Aux-Think) in both accuracy and inference speed suggests that explicit structural decomposition (progress reasoning + policy) can be more parameter-efficient than scaling a monolithic VLA. Open questions include whether the monotonic co-progression assumption holds in more complex or multi-goal navigation settings, how the approach scales to outdoor or long-horizon tasks with hundreds of instruction steps, and whether the GRPO co-finetuning stage is necessary or whether the self-supervised pretraining alone suffices at larger scale.
