## Problem

Data-driven initialization methods for implicit neural representations (INRs) consistently outperform standard random sampling, yet the underlying reason for their success remains unclear: do they encode dataset-specific mid-to-high-level features, or do simpler universal signal statistics suffice? This paper investigates whether the initialization benefit can be attributed to basic low-level properties like spectral structure or even pure randomness, and whether equivalently powerful initializations can be generated without any real training data.

## Input / Output

For pretraining, the method consumes N=10 noise samples (Uniform, Gaussian, Dead Leaves, or 1/|f^α| spectral noise) at 178×178 resolution for images or 512×512 for video frames. At test time, a single real signal (CelebA-HQ face, AFHQ image, OASIS-MRI slice, or ~10-second video at 25 FPS) is fitted by fine-tuning the pretrained encoder plus a randomly initialized decoder for 2000 steps with L2 loss. For denoising, the same fitted INR is evaluated on noisy versions of the target signal, producing a denoised reconstruction via early stopping.

## Method

SNP (STRAINER Noise Pretraining) replaces the real-signal pretraining in STRAINER with procedurally generated noise samples while retaining the same architecture: a 6-layer MLP with sine activations (ω=30), where 5 encoder layers are shared across signals and the final decoder layer is signal-specific. During pretraining, the network is jointly fit to 10 noise samples for 5000 iterations; at test time, the frozen encoder is paired with a randomly initialized decoder and both are fine-tuned on the target signal. The key empirical finding is a tradeoff: unstructured noise (Uniform, Gaussian) yields the highest signal-fitting PSNR (~80 dB) but the worst denoising, while 1/|f^α| spectral noise achieves near-STRAINER performance on both fitting and denoising without requiring any real data. For video, the same SNP initialization is applied to the ResFields architecture with shared MLP weights and time-conditioned residual updates across frames.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ image fitting (T=2000 iterations), SNP pretrained on Uniform noise achieves 85.7 dB PSNR, dramatically outperforming all other methods including STRAINER (57.8 dB), SIREN (44.9 dB), and SNP: Spectrum (56.4 dB). On the denoising task, however, the ranking inverts: SIREN achieves the best PSNR (28.3 dB) while SNP: Uniform performs worst (23.0 dB), and SNP: Spectrum (27.6 dB) matches STRAINER (27.3 dB) at roughly 2× faster convergence. For video fitting with ResFields, SNP: Spectrum improves mean PSNR from 29.5 dB (vanilla) to 31.1 dB, and for video denoising from 27.0 dB to 28.0 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNP: Uniform overwhelmingly dominates all baselines on image fitting, reaching 85.7 dB on CelebA-HQ versus 57.8 dB for STRAINER, 53.1 dB for Metalearned, 51.9 dB for TransINR, 49.7 dB for IPC, and 44.9 dB for vanilla SIREN — a gap of 28–41 dB. However, on the denoising task the picture reverses: SIREN achieves the best denoising PSNR (28.3 dB), while SNP: Uniform (23.0 dB) and SNP: Gaussian (23.8 dB) perform worst, indicating that unstructured noise pretraining sacrifices deep image priors. SNP: Spectrum strikes the best balance, matching STRAINER on both fitting (56.4 vs 57.8 dB) and denoising (27.6 vs 27.3 dB) while requiring no real training data. For video tasks, SNP: Spectrum outperforms vanilla ResFields by 1.6 dB on fitting (31.1 vs 29.5) and 1.0 dB on denoising (28.0 vs 27.0), while SNP: Uniform is slightly behind SNP: Spectrum on video fitting (30.9 vs 31.1) but ahead on denoising (27.6 vs 28.0 is actually behind — correction: SNP: Uniform 27.6 vs SNP: Spectrum 28.0). Overall, the paper establishes a clear tradeoff: unstructured noise maximizes fitting capacity but minimizes prior strength, while 1/|f^α| spectral noise offers the best joint performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends STRAINER (Vyas et al., NeurIPS 2024), a data-driven INR initialization method with shared encoder and signal-specific decoder heads, by replacing real-signal pretraining with noise pretraining (SNP). Conceptually, it draws on the 'Looking at Noise' line of work (Baradad et al.) that demonstrated structured noise can pretrain visual recognition networks, repurposing that insight for INR parameter initialization. For video experiments, it applies SNP to the ResFields architecture (Mihajlovic et al., 2023). The paper positions itself as an empirical mechanistic study—using NTK analysis, local complexity, and loss landscape visualization—to answer *why* data-driven initialization works, rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recommendation—that noise with a 1/|f^α| spectral structure can replace real data for INR initialization at near-equivalent quality—directly addresses a key bottleneck in data-poor scientific imaging (e.g., medical, microscopy) where collecting large real-signal datasets is infeasible. However, the study is limited to sinusoidal activations, a fixed 6-layer depth, and 2D signals; how activation choice and depth interact with optimal noise structure remains open. The finding that unstructured noise maximizes fitting capacity but minimizes denoising prior strength also raises a broader question about the capacity-prior tradeoff in neural representations that extends beyond INRs.
