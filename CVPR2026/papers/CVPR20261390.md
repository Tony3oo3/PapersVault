## Problem

Existing text-driven image editing methods are confined to low-resolution settings (≤1K), making high-resolution editing (>1K) an unaddressed task. The straightforward pipeline of editing at low resolution followed by super-resolution fails to recover micro-scale textures because fine-grained source details are not conditioned during the SR stage. This paper introduces the high-resolution image editing task and proposes a test-time optimization framework to solve it without any model training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261390/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution source image (e.g., 2K or 8K), a low-resolution reference image produced by applying a standard editing method (e.g., Nano Banana) to a downsampled version of the source, and a text editing instruction. It outputs a high-resolution edited image that inherits the semantic changes from the reference while preserving the fine-grained texture details of the original source. The source is divided into non-overlapping patches matching the pretrained model's native input resolution (e.g., 512×512 for Stable Diffusion), and the low-resolution reference is upsized to match the high-resolution source before patching.

## Method

ScaleEdit is a test-time optimization framework that performs patch-wise editing on a high-resolution source by leveraging the generative priors of a pretrained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion or FLUX). The core mechanism is a learnable 1×1 convolution transfer function applied to intermediate features (ResNet block outputs in U-Net, or final linear layer outputs in DiT) that is optimized to align the low-resolution reference's denoising trajectory with the high-resolution source's trajectory, thereby injecting fine-grained details. A hyperparameter τ controls the trade-off between detail transfer and content preservation by limiting optimization to early timesteps. To ensure global coherence across non-overlapping patches, a synchronization strategy combines blended Tweedie estimates (spatially interpolating boundary regions between adjacent patches) with a resampling step that re-noises detail-injected latents without re-applying the transfer function, eliminating the need for overlapping inference.

## Results

ScaleEdit achieves the best score on all five metrics (HaarPSI, M-MSE, M-SSIM, M-PSNR, LPIPS) in both the 1K-editing and 2K-editing scenarios. In the 2K-editing setting, ScaleEdit reaches HaarPSI 0.331, M-SSIM 0.806, M-PSNR 21.955, M-MSE 0.053, and LPIPS 0.496, outperforming the strongest baseline (PiSA-SR) by 0.019 in HaarPSI, 0.051 in M-SSIM, and 0.635 dB in M-PSNR. The masked metrics (M-MSE, M-SSIM, M-PSNR) confirm that ScaleEdit better preserves source-image regions that should remain unchanged during editing, a capability absent in SR-based pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaleEdit outperforms all four SR-based baselines (DiT-SR, DiT4SR, PiSA-SR, TSD-SR) on every metric in both 1K- and 2K-editing scenarios. In the 2K-editing setting, the closest competitor is PiSA-SR, which trails ScaleEdit by 0.019 in HaarPSI (0.312 vs. 0.331), 0.051 in M-SSIM (0.755 vs. 0.806), and 0.635 dB in M-PSNR (21.320 vs. 21.955). DiT-SR is the second-closest on HaarPSI (0.316 vs. 0.331) but lags more on M-SSIM (0.754 vs. 0.806). DiT4SR and TSD-SR are consistently the weakest baselines, with DiT4SR scoring the lowest M-SSIM (0.684) and M-PSNR (20.701) in 2K-editing. The consistent margin on masked metrics (M-MSE, M-SSIM, M-PSNR) indicates that ScaleEdit's patch-wise detail transfer preserves source-image regions far more faithfully than SR methods, which generate high-resolution content without conditioning on the original fine-grained details.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the null-text inversion framework (Mokady et al., CVPR 2023) for diffusion-based image-to-image translation, extending its forward-reverse trajectory machinery with a patch-wise strategy and a learnable 1×1 convolution transfer function operating in intermediate feature space. It departs from the low-resolution editing line (Step1X-Edit, ICEdit, KV-Edit) by treating their outputs as references rather than final results, and from diffusion-based super-resolution methods (DiT-SR, DiT4SR, PiSA-SR, TSD-SR) by conditioning on the high-resolution source's fine-grained details during generation rather than post-hoc upscaling. The conceptual bridge is that the pretrained generative prior is inherited through patch-wise inference at the model's native resolution, while a lightweight test-time-optimized transfer function injects source-specific micro-scale texture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing high-resolution image editing as a distinct task and providing a training-free, architecture-agnostic solution that scales to 8K, this work opens a practical pathway for professional-grade content creation workflows that existing 1K-limited editing tools cannot serve. Key limitations include the dependency on a separate low-resolution editing model to produce the reference, the computational cost of per-patch test-time optimization, and the fact that quantitative gains over strong SR baselines, while consistent, are modest (e.g., ~0.01–0.05 on HaarPSI/SSIM). Open questions include whether the synchronization strategy can be further accelerated for real-time use, how the method behaves on video or 3D content, and whether the 1×1 convolution transfer function can be replaced by even lighter or learned alternatives.
