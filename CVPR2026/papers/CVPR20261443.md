## Problem

Modern diffusion models predict noise (ε-prediction) or flow velocity (v-prediction) rather than directly denoising to clean images, despite the original motivation being denoising. Under the manifold assumption, natural data lies on a low-dimensional manifold while noise and velocity are inherently off-manifold, making them far harder to predict in high-dimensional spaces. This causes ViT-style architectures with large patches on raw pixels to fail catastrophically, forcing reliance on latent tokenizers or dense convolutions to work around the capacity bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and a random noise sample as input and produces a natural RGB image at resolutions of 256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024. It operates directly on raw pixel patches of size 16×16, 32×32, or 64×64 (yielding per-patch dimensions of 768, 3072, or 12288), with no tokenizer, no latent space, and no auxiliary losses. The sequence length remains constant at 16×16 tokens across all resolutions, keeping compute roughly fixed while the per-token dimensionality scales with patch size.

## Method

JiT (Just Image Transformers) is a plain Vision Transformer applied to non-overlapping pixel patches, where the network directly predicts the clean image (x-prediction) rather than noise or velocity. Training uses a v-loss (flow matching objective) but the network output is x, with v computed as (x_pred − z_t)/(1−t); sampling solves the ODE via a 50-step Heun solver. The architecture incorporates general-purpose Transformer improvements (SwiGLU, RMSNorm, RoPE, qk-norm) and in-context class tokens, with adaLN-Zero for time conditioning. A key finding is that a bottleneck linear patch embedding (reducing dimension to as low as 16–128 before the Transformer) is beneficial rather than harmful, and the hidden size need not match or exceed the patch dimension—echoing classical manifold learning where low-capacity networks can still recover the underlying low-dimensional structure.

## Results

On ImageNet 512×512, JiT-G/32 achieves FID 1.78 and IS 306.8 (600 epochs) using only 384 Gflops, while JiT-H/32 reaches FID 1.94 with 183 Gflops. On ImageNet 256×256, JiT-G/16 achieves FID 1.82 and IS 292.6 with 383 Gflops. Notably, all JiT models operate directly on raw pixels with no tokenizer, no pre-training, no perceptual loss, and no self-supervised alignment, yet handle per-patch dimensions as high as 12,288 (at 1024×1024 with patch size 64) without catastrophic degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among pixel-space diffusion methods at 512×512, JiT-G/32 (FID 1.78) outperforms SiD UViT/4 (3.02), VDM++ UViT/4 (2.65), SiD2 UViT/4 (2.19), PixNerd XL/16 (2.84), RIN (3.95), and ADM-G (7.72), but trails SiD2 UViT/2 (FID 1.48) which uses a 653-Gflop UViT architecture. At 256×256, JiT-G/16 (FID 1.82) beats SiD UViT/2 (2.44), VDM++ UViT/2 (2.12), PixelFlow XL/4 (1.98), and PixNerd XL/16 (2.15), but is slightly behind SiD2 UViT/2 (1.73) and SiD2 UViT/1 (1.38). Against latent-space methods, JiT-G/32 (1.78) is competitive with SiT-XL/2 (2.62) and REPA (2.08) but trails DDT-XL/2 (1.28) and RAE (1.13); however, JiT requires no pre-trained tokenizer, no VGG perceptual loss, and no DINOv2 self-supervised alignment, while using roughly 30–40% less compute (384 vs. 525–642 Gflops). The key distinction is that JiT achieves these results with a plain ViT on raw pixels and x-prediction alone, whereas all competing methods rely on either a learned latent space or specialized architectural components (hierarchical UViT, NeRF heads, representation alignment) to handle high-dimensional inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture by applying it directly to raw pixel patches with x-prediction, departing from the ε-prediction and v-prediction paradigms that became standard after DDPM and flow matching. It draws a conceptual lineage from the manifold assumption and denoising autoencoders (Vincent et al.), arguing that predicting clean data is fundamentally different from predicting noise or velocity in high-dimensional spaces. Unlike prior pixel-space diffusion methods that rely on dense convolutions (ADM, SiD, SiD2), hierarchical designs (PixelFlow), or NeRF heads with representation alignment (PixNerd), JiT uses a plain ViT with large patches and no auxiliary losses, pre-training, or tokenizer. The flow-matching formulation (Lipman et al.) provides the ODE-based sampling framework, but the key departure is making the network's direct output the clean image rather than a noised quantity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that x-prediction with a plain ViT on large pixel patches is a viable, self-contained alternative to latent diffusion, eliminating the need for pre-trained VAEs, perceptual losses, or representation alignment—opening the door to domains like proteins, molecules, and weather where tokenizers are difficult to design. The counterintuitive finding that bottleneck embeddings improve quality (rather than hurt it) echoes classical manifold learning and suggests the network can be largely decoupled from observed dimensionality. However, the best FID scores (1.78–1.82) still trail the strongest latent-space methods with pre-training (1.13–1.26), and the gap may narrow or widen with further scaling. Open questions include whether the x-prediction advantage persists in non-image modalities, whether the bottleneck effect generalizes beyond ImageNet, and whether the self-contained paradigm can close the quality gap without any external supervision.
