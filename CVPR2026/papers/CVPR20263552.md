## Problem

Lifelong imitation learning requires robots to sequentially acquire new manipulation tasks without catastrophic forgetting, yet existing rehearsal-based methods store raw trajectories (memory-inefficient), rely on task identifiers at test time, or depend on parameter-efficient fine-tuning of pretrained backbones. A key unresolved challenge is representation drift: when new tasks share latent space with previously learned ones, their embeddings overlap and interfere, degrading performance on both old and new tasks in a task-ID-agnostic setting.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes four modalities per timestep: agent-view and eye-in-hand RGB images, a natural-language task instruction, and proprioceptive state (joint positions, velocities, gripper status). It outputs a single robot control action (end-effector command). During the lifelong learning stage, the replay buffer stores only the compact multimodal latent features produced by the frozen CLIP and MLP encoders (equivalent to ~5 demonstrations per task in storage), rather than raw images or full trajectories, reducing memory footprint by orders of magnitude compared to standard experience replay.

## Method

The architecture uses frozen CLIP image and text encoders plus a two-layer MLP state encoder, whose outputs are modulated by FiLM layers conditioned on language features, then processed by a GPT-2 temporal decoder and a policy head. During pretraining all modules are trained jointly via behavioral cloning; during lifelong learning only the temporal decoder and policy head are updated. The first key component, Multimodal Latent Replay (MLR), replays compact latent representations from a buffer instead of raw trajectories, stabilizing training without accessing high-dimensional sensory inputs. The second component, Incremental Feature Adjustment (IFA), introduces an angular-margin loss that penalizes the current task's global latent representation when it is closer to a previously learned task's language reference embedding than to its own, with the margin δ adaptively scaled by the angular distance between the two task references (δ = α·arccos(h_k·h_j)). Task pairs for IFA are selected by requiring simultaneous top-50% similarity in both language and agent-view modalities, and the language embedding serves as the stable per-task reference, yielding a simpler pipeline that avoids PEFT, distillation, and generative replay while achieving state-of-the-art AUC and lowest forgetting on LIBERO benchmarks.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmarks, MLR+IFA achieves AUC of 79.4±1.5 (LIBERO-OBJECT), 77.2±1.8 (LIBERO-GOAL), and 56.1±1.8 (LIBERO-50), setting new state-of-the-art across all three suites. The method attains FWT of 84.6, 80.0, and 60.8 respectively, while reducing Negative Backward Transfer (NBT) to 11.4, 6.9, and 8.6. Compared to the strongest prior methods (M2Distill, LOTUS, ISCIL), MLR+IFA delivers 10–21 point AUC gains and up to 65% reduction in forgetting (e.g., NBT drops from 19.4 to 6.9 on LIBERO-GOAL vs. ISCIL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLR+IFA outperforms all seven baselines on AUC across every benchmark. Against the strongest prior methods, it gains +10.4 AUC over M2Distill on LIBERO-OBJECT (79.4 vs. 69.0), +21.2 over LOTUS on LIBERO-GOAL (77.2 vs. 56.0), and +18.4 over ISCIL on LIBERO-50 (56.1 vs. 37.7). On forgetting (NBT), MLR+IFA achieves the lowest values on LIBERO-GOAL (6.9) and LIBERO-50 (8.6), reducing forgetting by 65% relative to ISCIL (19.4→6.9) and by 80% relative to LOTUS (43.0→8.6) on LIBERO-50. On LIBERO-OBJECT, M2Distill reports a slightly lower NBT (8.0 vs. 11.4), but MLR+IFA still leads on FWT (84.6 vs. 75.0) and AUC (79.4 vs. 69.0). The simpler baselines (Sequential, ER, BUDS, TAIL) are substantially outperformed, with AUC gaps exceeding 30 points on most benchmarks, confirming that compact latent replay with angular regularization is far more effective than raw experience replay or task-specific adapters for lifelong imitation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the rehearsal-based lifelong imitation learning (LIL) lineage, following the two-stage (pretraining + lifelong) paradigm established by LOTUS, TAIL, and ISCIL, but departs sharply from their reliance on parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) or knowledge distillation. Instead of storing raw trajectories as in classical experience replay (ER, BUDS) or synthesizing data via GANs (CRIL), it replays compact multimodal latent features from a frozen CLIP backbone, and introduces an angular-margin regularization (IFA) that has no direct analogue in prior LIL work. The approach thus repurposes the rehearsal idea at the representation level and adds a task-separation loss inspired by angular margin classifiers, positioning itself as a simpler, task-ID-agnostic alternative to adapter-based and distillation-based LIL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a frozen pretrained encoder plus a lightweight replay buffer and a single margin loss can match or exceed methods that fine-tune adapters or perform multimodal distillation, lowering the engineering and compute barrier for deploying lifelong robot policies. The 10–17 point AUC gains and up to 65% reduction in forgetting on LIBERO are meaningful, but all experiments remain in simulation with 10 demonstrations per task, leaving open whether the approach transfers to real-world, data-scarce, or cross-domain settings. Future work on longer task sequences, integration with reinforcement learning, and validation on physical robots would be needed to confirm that the frozen-backbone assumption holds beyond the controlled LIBERO environment.
