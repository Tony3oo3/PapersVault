## Problem

Existing RLHF alignment methods for text-to-image diffusion models achieve high automated reward scores but suffer from Preference Mode Collapse (PMC), a form of reward hacking where the model converges to narrow, homogeneous outputs (monolithic styles, overexposure) at the expense of generative diversity. This collapse is driven by over-optimization along the reward model's inherent biases, yet prior work lacks both a standardized benchmark to quantify it and a mechanism that corrects the optimization direction rather than merely tempering its magnitude.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a single generated image via the FLUX.1.Dev flow-matching model. During training, the prompt is encoded by the reward model's text encoder (HPS-v2.1, CLIP-based), and a learned directional vector is added to the text embedding before computing the reward score. The reward signal is evaluated on a one-step denoised reconstruction of the clean image from a noisy latent, using a ground-truth noise prior for stability across all timesteps.

## Method

D²-Align is a two-stage framework that directionally corrects the reward signal in the text encoder's continuous embedding space. In Stage 1, with the generator frozen, a learnable vector b_v is optimized to construct a guided reward via CFG-style extrapolation: two perturbed embeddings (e_text ± b_v) are normalized, and a guided embedding is formed as e_- + ω(e_+ − e_-), which suppresses the reward model's intrinsic bias (e.g., preference for glossy/over-rendered images). In Stage 2, the frozen b_v* is applied to every reward computation during generator optimization, fundamentally altering the optimization direction so the model is steered away from mode collapse while still maximizing a corrected human-preference signal. The paper also introduces DivGenBench, a 3,200-prompt benchmark spanning four dimensions (ID, Style, Layout, Tonal) with four bespoke metrics (IDS, ASC, SDI, PVS) to quantitatively measure generative diversity and diagnose PMC.

## Results

Under the HPS-v2.1 reward configuration, D²-Align achieves the best scores on ImageReward (1.771), PickScore (0.246), CLIP (0.323), DeQA (4.484), and GenEval (0.636), while maintaining competitive HPS-v2.1 (0.343) and Aesthetic (6.450) scores. On the proposed DivGenBench diversity benchmark, D²-Align attains the best scores across all four metrics: IDS 0.251 (lower is better), ASC 0.253, SDI 0.636, and PVS 0.412, substantially outperforming baselines that suffer severe diversity collapse (e.g., Flow-GRPO drops to ASC 0.044 and PVS 0.168). The method also achieves the best win rate in a human evaluation study, confirming that the corrected reward signal yields outputs more aligned with genuine human preference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²-Align outperforms all three RL baselines (DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO, SRPO) on the majority of quality metrics while simultaneously achieving the best diversity scores, breaking the quality–diversity trade-off. DanceGRPO and Flow-GRPO achieve artificially high HPS-v2.1 scores (0.361 and 0.367 vs. 0.343 for D²-Align) but suffer severe Preference Mode Collapse: Flow-GRPO's ASC drops to 0.044 (vs. 0.253 for D²-Align) and PVS to 0.168 (vs. 0.412), indicating near-total stylistic and tonal homogeneity. SRPO maintains the closest diversity to the unaligned FLUX baseline (IDS 0.259 vs. 0.280) but lags significantly on quality metrics (ImageReward 1.533, DeQA 4.357, GenEval 0.623). On semantic consistency, D²-Align achieves GenEval 0.636, surpassing DanceGRPO (0.522) and Flow-GRPO (0.517) by over 10 points, while SRPO (0.623) is closest but still trails. Under the combined HPS-v2.1+CLIP reward, D²-Align extends its lead, achieving the best Aesthetic (6.671), ImageReward (1.762), CLIP (0.328), DeQA (4.498), and all four diversity metrics, confirming robustness across reward configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL alignment lineage (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO, SRPO) for flow-matching T2I models, but departs from their shared strategy of modulating reward *magnitude* (KL regularization, reward ensembling) by instead correcting the reward *direction* in the text-encoder embedding space. The directional correction mechanism is directly inspired by classifier-free guidance's positive/negative embedding extrapolation, repurposed here as a learned bias-suppression vector rather than a conditioning signal. The two-stage decoupling (learn direction with frozen generator, then guide optimization) and the DivGenBench diversity benchmark are novel contributions not present in prior RLHF-for-diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating Preference Mode Collapse as a distinct failure axis from quality degradation, the paper gives the community a concrete diagnostic and a standardized measurement tool (DivGenBench) that prior work lacked. The simplicity of the fix—a single learned vector in embedding space—makes it easy to adopt, and the ablation showing it generalizes to other RL methods (DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include whether the learned direction transfers across reward model families (beyond CLIP-based HPS), whether it scales to video generation, and whether the two-stage procedure remains stable under multi-reward or iterative alignment settings.
