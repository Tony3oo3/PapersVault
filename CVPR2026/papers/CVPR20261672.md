## Problem

State-of-the-art multi-view 3D reconstruction methods rely on photometric consistency and distinctive texture cues, both of which break down for reflective, transparent, and low-texture surfaces. Existing datasets (DTU, CO3D, MVImgNet, OpenMaterial) focus on diffuse objects or remain purely synthetic, leaving no large-scale hybrid benchmark that exposes these systematic failure modes under realistic conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261672/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 60 multi-view RGB images at 1000×1000 per synthetic instance and 100+ views from 1080×1920 video for real captures, alongside ground-truth 3D meshes, point clouds, dense depth maps, surface normal maps, segmentation masks, camera poses, and material parameters (metallic, roughness, IOR, transmission). Real-world captures are recorded with an iPhone 16 Pro at 30 FPS on a rotating platform, with poses estimated from a detailed tracking base via RealityScan and object isolation via SAM2, decoupling pose estimation from the challenging object surface.

## Method

3DReflecNet is constructed through a unified pipeline combining three data sources: (1) physically-based rendering in Blender over 12K+ shapes with 22 material types and 2700+ HDRI environment maps, including a camera-through-glass setup that simulates view-dependent specular reflection from 60 angles under hundreds of lighting conditions; (2) diffusion-based 2D-to-3D generation that converts real-world and GPT-4o-synthesized 2D reference images into 3D meshes via normal/depth estimation, mesh reconstruction, and canonical pose refinement; and (3) real-world scans of 1000+ objects with challenging materials captured on a rotating platform. The dataset totals 120K+ synthetic and 1000+ real instances (7M+ frames), and establishes standardized benchmarks for five tasks—image matching, SfM, novel view synthesis, reflection removal, and relighting—using an 80/10/10 train/val/test split with material-stratified evaluation.

## Results

On the 3DReflecNet NVS benchmark, all methods achieve PSNR >36 dB on diffuse materials but degrade severely on challenging surfaces: transparent materials drop to 17–21 dB, metallic to 25–28 dB, and glossy-low-texture to 26–28 dB. For surface reconstruction (Chamfer Distance), 2DGS performs best on diffuse (0.060) and transparent (0.142) categories, while PGSR catastrophically fails on transparent (0.502) and metallic (0.412). In image matching, the best method (ROMA) achieves only 32.1 AUC@5° on 3DReflecNet versus 62.6 on MegaDepth, revealing a nearly 2× performance gap. The material parameter sweep shows that smooth metallic surfaces (roughness=0) suffer a 45% PSNR degradation (19 dB vs. 35 dB) compared to high-roughness non-metallic surfaces, and transparency causes a consistent 5.82 dB drop across all configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This dataset paper evaluates 13 existing methods across five tasks to expose their limitations on challenging materials. For image matching, ROMA [18] leads with 32.1 AUC@5° on 3DReflecNet, outperforming ELoftr [73] (21.3), AspanFormer [8] (20.3), and LoFTR [60] (19.8), yet all methods score roughly half their MegaDepth performance (e.g., ROMA: 62.6 on MegaDepth vs. 32.1 here). For NVS, Splatfacto [61] achieves the best PSNR across most categories (37.32 diffuse, 21.31 transparent, 28.61 metallic), narrowly beating 3DGS [28] and Instant-NGP [40], while 2DGS [25] is weakest on transparent (17.12 dB). For surface reconstruction, 2DGS [25] achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance on diffuse (0.060) and transparent (0.142), outperforming Neus2 [71] and Instant-NGP [40], but PGSR [6] fails catastrophically on non-diffuse materials (0.502 transparent, 0.412 metallic vs. 0.062 diffuse). The consistent pattern across all 13 baselines is a 2–5× performance drop from diffuse to transparent/metallic surfaces, confirming that no current method robustly handles the material complexities 3DReflecNet targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions 3DReflecNet against a lineage of 3D reconstruction datasets that have progressively expanded scope but each address only a subset of material challenges: DTU and Tanks and Temples target diffuse geometry, OpenMaterial introduces measured optical properties but remains purely synthetic, and MV Reflectance/NeRO cover reflectivity with limited object diversity. The key departure is the simultaneous coverage of reflective, transparent, and low-texture materials at scale (120K+ synthetic, 1000+ real instances) combined with a unified asset-creation pipeline that merges physically-based rendering (Blender with a camera-through-glass setup) and diffusion-based 2D-to-3D generation from both real and LLM-synthesized references. No prior dataset supports all five benchmark tasks (image matching, SfM, NVS, reflection removal, relighting) under these material conditions with both synthetic and real data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic parameter sweep (48 material configurations) quantifies what practitioners have anecdotally observed: SOTA methods like 3DGS and NeRF variants suffer 45% PSNR degradation on reflective surfaces and a consistent 5.82 dB drop on transparent materials, with PGSR showing an 8× Chamfer Distance blowup on transparency. This provides the field with a concrete, reproducible benchmark to measure progress toward physically-aware 3D vision, and the open question of whether photometric consistency assumptions can be relaxed or replaced without sacrificing the efficiency gains of current Gaussian/NeRF pipelines remains central. Limitations include the relatively small real-world subset (1000+ objects vs. 120K+ synthetic), potential synthetic-real domain gap, and the deferral of reflection removal and relighting results to supplementary material, which weakens the completeness of the five-task benchmark claim.
