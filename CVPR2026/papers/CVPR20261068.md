## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting models trained at a fixed resolution exhibit aliasing, staircasing, and blur when rendered at arbitrary resolutions, while storing resolution-specific Gaussian sets to fix this incurs prohibitive memory costs. Existing anti-aliasing methods rely on scale-dependent filtering, super-resolution decoding, or expanded storage, and anchor-based compression methods (Context-GS, TC-GS) reduce memory but do not address continuous resolution adaptation. The paper tackles both gaps simultaneously: enabling a single compact anchor set to produce alias-free renderings at any resolution while drastically reducing stored parameters.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes SfM point clouds for initialization and RGB images randomly resized to 10%–100% of original resolution at each iteration, along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics. At inference, the stored scene consists only of a compact subset of proxy anchor features (~30% of all anchors) plus the residual predictor weights; given a camera pose and a continuous target resolution ratio ξ, the system reconstructs leaf anchors on demand and renders a novel view image. The resolution is treated as a continuous scalar input rather than a discrete choice, and the number of active 3D Gaussians varies dynamically with ξ.

## Method

Built on Scaffold-GS's anchor-to-Gaussian architecture, the method introduces three key components. First, a FiLM-based resolution-embedding modulates each anchor's latent feature using positional encodings of the anchor position and the target resolution ratio, so the same anchor produces resolution-adaptive 3D Gaussian parameters (scale, rotation, opacity, color) at different resolutions. Second, a Pixel Coverage Gate computes the projected 2D Gaussian's Mahalanobis-area coverage and applies a sigmoid gate that suppresses Gaussians whose footprint falls below a tunable pixel-size threshold, providing an explicit anti-aliasing mechanism tied to the sampling rate. Third, a residual anchor predictor (a small MLP) reconstructs omitted leaf anchor features from the nearest proxy anchor's feature plus a positional encoding of the relative offset, reducing stored anchors to ~30% of the original set. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 optimizes all anchors with the standard L1+SSIM rendering loss under random resolutions; Stage 2 freezes the anchor set and jointly trains the residual predictor with an added L2+cosine reconstruction loss while continuing to refine proxy anchors.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, the proposed method achieves the best PSNR at all four tested resolutions (10%: 29.74, 40%: 28.68, 70%: 27.31, 100%: 26.71), with the largest margin at low resolution (e.g., 29.74 vs. 28.45 for Analytic-Splatting at 10%). On DeepBlending, it similarly leads at every resolution (10%: 30.47, 100%: 29.33 PSNR). In terms of storage, the method uses 41.6 MB on Mip-NeRF360 and 31.1 MB on DeepBlending—substantially less than 3D-GS (220.9/162.1 MB) and Scaffold-GS (78.9/56.2 MB), though more than the extreme-compression baselines Context-GS (9.3/10.4 MB) and TC-GS (8.2/8.7 MB). On the anchor-based comparison (Table 2), the method uses only 67.2K anchors on Tanks&Temples (vs. 208.0K for Scaffold-GS) and 7.0K on NeRF-Synthetic (vs. 25.7K), while achieving higher average PSNR (24.60 vs. 24.06 and 31.06 vs. 30.51 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS at every tested resolution on both Mip-NeRF360 and DeepBlending. The advantage is most pronounced at low resolutions: at 10% on Mip-NeRF360, the method achieves 29.74 PSNR versus 28.45 (Analytic-Splatting), 28.04 (Mip-Splatting), and 24.68 (Scaffold-GS), a gap of 1.3–5.1 dB. At 100% resolution the margin narrows to ~0.2 dB over Mip-Splatting and ~0.2 dB over Scaffold-GS, indicating the method's primary benefit is anti-aliasing at extreme scales. Against the compression-focused baselines Context-GS and TC-GS, the method trades ~32–33 MB of additional storage for a consistent 0.3–5.2 dB PSNR gain across resolutions. In the anchor-count comparison (Table 2), the method uses roughly 3× fewer anchors than Scaffold-GS on Tanks&Temples (67.2K vs. 208.0K) and ~3.7× fewer on NeRF-Synthetic (7.0K vs. 25.7K) while achieving higher average PSNR, demonstrating that the proxy-anchor + residual-predictor design is more expressive per stored anchor than the full-anchor baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Scaffold-GS's anchor-to-Gaussian architecture by adding two resolution-adaptivity mechanisms (FiLM-based resolution-embedding and a pixel coverage gate) and a compression module (residual anchor predictor over proxy anchors). It departs from prior anti-aliasing approaches like Mip-Splatting and Analytic-Splatting, which apply filtering at projection or replace point sampling with area integration, by instead making the same set of anchors produce resolution-adaptive Gaussians through learned conditioning during training. The compression strategy is distinct from Context-GS and TC-GS, which reduce memory by modifying anchor structure or using tri-planes, whereas this paper stores a compact proxy subset and reconstructs omitted leaf anchors on demand via a lightweight MLP predictor inspired by PointMAE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is strong for AR/VR and digital-twin deployments where continuous zoom across a scale range is required and memory is constrained; the method achieves ~5× memory reduction over vanilla 3D-GS while matching or exceeding quality at all tested resolutions. A key open question is the inference-time cost of the residual anchor predictor, which the authors acknowledge can make the compressed model slower than uncompressed 3D-GS. Extending the framework to dynamic scenes and reducing predictor overhead are identified as natural next steps, and whether the FiLM conditioning generalizes beyond the 10%–100% training range remains untested.
