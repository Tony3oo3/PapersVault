## Problem

Existing 3D editing methods either require computationally intensive per-scene iterative optimization with fragile multi-view coverage, or operate in 2D-native feature spaces that cannot reliably handle large geometric changes. Meanwhile, 3D-native generative backbones like TRELLIS use compressed latent tokens that sacrifice high-frequency texture fidelity. The paper addresses how to perform feed-forward, globally coherent 3D editing directly in structured 3D latent space while recovering fine-grained appearance details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260925/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source 3D asset (encoded as TRELLIS SLAT), a single edited RGB view image, and a user-defined 3D region mask specifying the editable area. It produces a globally consistent edited 3D mesh with UV texture in a single feed-forward pass (no per-scene optimization). The texture refinement stage renders 512×512 normal maps from the edited geometry across 6 predefined camera poses to condition multi-view synthesis, and the final texture is fused via visibility-aware, mask-weighted blending in UV space.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages operating in TRELLIS's sparse voxel latent space. First, Voxel FlowEdit constructs an edit-driven velocity field by differencing the pretrained rectified-flow model's conditional velocities under source and target image conditions, then integrates a masked ODE from source to target latent with two auxiliary terms: a silhouette-matching gradient (BCE between rendered occupancy and target silhouette) and a trajectory-consistency correction that projects the evolving state back onto the flow manifold. Second, SLAT Repainting performs masked latent denoising that applies target-conditioned velocity only within the editable region while replaying the forward-diffused source trajectory elsewhere, using a softly feathered mask to prevent seams. Third, an optional texture refinement module combines a frozen ControlNet with a trainable Ctrl-Adapter to inject per-view normal-map features into an ERA3D multi-view diffusion backbone, synthesizing six geometry-consistent auxiliary views that are projected and fused onto the mesh UV texture to restore high-frequency detail lost in the compressed 3D latent.

## Results

On the 3D editing benchmark (100 assets from Sketchfab, NPHM, THuman2.1, and Objaverse), Easy3E achieves the best score on all four metrics: CLIP-T 0.326, DINO-I 0.952, LPIPS 0.138, and FID 25.8. The closest competitor, VoxHammer, trails by 0.014 on DINO-I, 0.055 on LPIPS, and 5.9 on FID. In a user study with 46 participants, Easy3E is rated best in 89–98% of cases across prompt preservation, identity preservation, 3D editing quality, 3D consistency, and overall quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Easy3E outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor VoxHammer (also built on TRELLIS), Easy3E improves DINO-I from 0.938 to 0.952, LPIPS from 0.193 to 0.138, and FID from 31.7 to 25.8, while matching its CLIP-T (0.326 vs 0.295). TRELLIS used as a direct image-to-3D generator is competitive on CLIP-T (0.323) but degrades substantially on perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.243, FID 30.2), indicating it cannot faithfully preserve the source geometry while applying the edit. MVEdit and Instant3DiT, both multi-view inpainting/diffusion approaches, produce plausible textures but introduce negligible geometric changes and view-wise inconsistency (LPIPS ≈ 0.28, FID 49–68). Vox-E, a voxel-based volumetric editing method, performs worst overall (LPIPS 0.673, FID 90.3) and requires significantly longer inference time. The user study confirms these quantitative gaps: Easy3E is preferred in 94.6–97.5% of pairwise comparisons for identity preservation, 3D editing quality, and 3D consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the inversion-free velocity-differencing paradigm of FlowEdit (2D flow-matching editing) to the 3D sparse voxel latent space of TRELLIS, replacing per-scene iterative optimization (Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, GaussianEditor) and 2D multi-view diffusion lifting (MVEdit, Instant3DiT) with a single-pass feed-forward trajectory in structured 3D latents. The key conceptual departure is treating 3D editing as a continuous ODE in the rectified-flow latent space rather than as score-distillation or multi-view inpainting, which naturally handles geometric deformation without requiring paired 3D editing data. For appearance recovery, it repurposes ERA3D's multi-view diffusion with a normal-conditioned Ctrl-Adapter as an external texture prior, addressing the known limitation that compressed 3D latent tokens cannot represent high-frequency detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that flow-based editing transfers cleanly from 2D to 3D-native structured latents, this work validates a feed-forward editing paradigm that avoids the per-scene optimization bottleneck and multi-view inconsistency plaguing prior methods. However, performance remains bounded by TRELLIS's generative capacity under extreme geometric changes, and the texture refinement operates at relatively low resolution (512×512 views), limiting very fine detail recovery. The near-simultaneous appearance of VoxHammer and Nano3D pursuing the same pipeline suggests this is a convergent direction; the open question is whether flow-based 3D editing can scale to topology-changing edits or arbitrary multi-region compositions without additional architectural changes.
