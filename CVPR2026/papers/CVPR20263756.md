## Problem

When applying GRPO-based reinforcement learning to Discrete Diffusion Models, the simulated unmasking procedure with varying mask ratios causes a severe credit assignment imbalance: tokens unmasked later in the generation trajectory accumulate disproportionately larger gradient scales than those unmasked earlier, despite early timesteps carrying higher exploration potential and determining global structure. This mismatch between gradient magnitude and actual importance hinders convergence and reduces training efficiency by up to ~40%.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a discrete-token image via MMaDA-8B-Base, a mask-based discrete diffusion model that decodes multiple tokens per forward pass. During RL training, each prompt yields G=9 rollout completions sampled with 12 inference steps and CFG scale 3.5; the model is then optimized over μ=8 inner GRPO iterations per global step using a global batch size of 72. Evaluation covers compositional generation (GenEval) and aesthetic quality (HPSv3, ImageReward, DeQA).

## Method

RebRL introduces two complementary rebalancing mechanisms into the GRPO objective for DDMs. First, a temporal rebalancing factor λ(t_j) ∈ {t_j, t_j²} is multiplied into the per-timestep loss, down-scaling gradients from later (low-mask-ratio) steps and amplifying those from earlier (high-mask-ratio) steps to counteract the monotonically increasing cumulative scale w(Δt_j) = μ Σ 1/j'. Second, a token-level rebalancing map is constructed by accumulating the mask indicator δ(o_k^{t_j}, m) across all μ iterations into a frequency map F(k), and each token's loss is scaled by 1/F(k), equalizing cumulative gradient contributions across tokens. The approach is plug-and-play, adds no trainable parameters or extra computation, and is derived from a principled policy-gradient analysis showing that the DDM surrogate log-likelihood (sum over masked positions) inherently produces uneven per-token gradient accumulation when the importance ratio is computed at the sequence level rather than per-token.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, RebRL achieves the highest overall score of 0.86 among all discrete diffusion models, outperforming the base MMaDA model (0.74) by 0.12 and the strongest RL baseline MaskGRPO (0.84) by 0.02. The largest gains appear in Counting (0.81 vs. 0.77 for UniGRPO) and Position (0.83 vs. 0.78 for diffu-GRPO), confirming that rebalanced credit assignment improves global structural decisions. On human preference metrics, RebRL scores 13.01 on HPSv3 (vs. 12.35 for MaskGRPO and 12.09 for UniGRPO), 1.28 on ImageReward, and 4.22 on DeQA, while also reducing training steps by ~40% compared to UniGRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RebRL outperforms all five compared baselines on GenEval overall. Against the base MMaDA model (0.74), RebRL gains 0.12, with the largest per-task improvements in Position (+0.22) and Counting (+0.21). Among RL baselines, RebRL beats MaskGRPO (0.84) by 0.02, UniGRPO (0.82) by 0.04, and diffu-GRPO (0.82) by 0.04; the margin over MaskGRPO is smallest, but RebRL leads on Counting (0.81 vs. 0.69) and Position (0.83 vs. 0.77) where MaskGRPO is weakest. On human preference (HPSv3), RebRL (13.01) surpasses MaskGRPO (12.35) by 0.66, UniGRPO (12.09) by 0.92, and diffu-GRPO (11.99) by 1.02. Compared to non-RL discrete models, RebRL exceeds Janus-Pro (0.80) by 0.06 and Show-o (0.53) by 0.33 on GenEval overall. Notably, RebRL achieves these gains while reducing training steps by ~40% relative to UniGRPO, indicating both higher quality and greater training efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL post-training pipeline for Discrete Diffusion Models (DDMs) established by diffu-GRPO and UniGRPO/MaskGRPO, which simulate multi-step denoising by applying varying mask ratios to rollout sequences. The key departure is a theoretical analysis (Eq. 9–10) showing that this standard masking procedure induces a systematic credit-assignment imbalance: tokens unmasked later accumulate monotonically larger cumulative gradients, the opposite of what the exploration potential (ΔHPS) demands. RebRL addresses this by introducing a temporal reweighting factor λ(t_j) and a token-level inverse-frequency weight (1/F), both derived from intrinsic model statistics, making the approach plug-and-play with existing DDM-RL pipelines without additional parameters or compute.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of timestep-level credit imbalance as a fundamental (and previously overlooked) bottleneck in DDM RL training provides a clear diagnostic for why prior methods converge slowly and saturate at lower performance ceilings. The practical impact is notable: a ~40% reduction in training steps alongside SOTA GenEval and HPSv3 scores, achieved with zero extra hyperparameters. An open question the authors flag is whether the empirically chosen rebalancing factors (first-order vs. second-order, token-frequency) can be made learnable or adapted to non-linear schedulers. The analysis is currently validated on a single 8B model (MMaDA) and one compositional benchmark, so generalization to other DDM architectures and continuous-scheduler variants remains to be confirmed.
