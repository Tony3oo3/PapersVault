## Problem

Existing defenses against gradient leakage attacks in Federated Learning invariably sacrifice model accuracy for privacy through noise injection or gradient clipping, while standard FL suffers from slow convergence on non-IID data due to local drift. No prior method simultaneously strengthens privacy protection and improves convergence speed, leaving privacy and performance as apparently antagonistic objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263851/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model x_t from the server and combines it with a locally maintained private momentum vector Ω_{t,i} (never transmitted) to form a shifted initialization x̃_{t,i} = x_t + β_{t,i}Ω_{t,i} for K steps of local SGD on its private data. The communication protocol is identical to FedAvg: clients upload only their trained local models x_{t,i}, with no additional gradient or offset information shared, requiring one model-sized vector of persistent local storage per client.

## Method

FedMOP introduces an initialization-based offset mechanism with two synergistic components. First, gradient orthogonal projection computes a natural shift Ω^{nat} by projecting the global update onto the orthogonal complement of the local update direction, incorporating global statistical context to counteract local drift while guaranteeing the shift does not interfere with local gradient descent. Second, momentum-based trajectory hiding transforms this server-computable offset into a private trajectory: each client initializes Ω_{0,i} with secret Gaussian noise and evolves it via Ω_{t,i} = γ_{t,i}Ω_{t-1,i} + (1−γ_{t,i})Ω_{t,i}^{nat} with independently sampled coefficients γ_{t,i} ∈ [0.8, 0.95], making reconstruction require inferring (d+t) unknowns from indirect observations—a computationally intractable inverse problem. The momentum evolution simultaneously provides exponential variance reduction for faster convergence and expands the attacker's search space exponentially with training duration, achieving O(1/(K²T²)) convergence while rendering brute-force gradient matching infeasible.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 under D1 full participation, FedMOP achieves 42.07% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline HierFed (39.23%) by 2.84%. Against gradient leakage attacks (csDLG, batch size 1), FedMOP achieves MSE=0.85 and PSNR=7.43, roughly 1.8× stronger than the next-best defense OUTPOST (MSE=0.46, PSNR=15.42). For convergence on CIFAR-100 (D1, full participation), FedMOP reaches 30% accuracy in 67 rounds (4.66× speedup over FedAvg), slightly ahead of HierFed (68 rounds, 4.59×). Computational cost is near FedAvg: 6.3 MFLOPs and 10.6 s per round on CIFAR-10, versus 6.2 MFLOPs and 10.1 s for FedAvg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMOP outperforms all 13 baselines on both privacy defense and model utility. Against GLA defenses, FedMOP achieves the highest reconstruction MSE (0.85 for csDLG, 0.98 for FGLA at batch size 1), roughly 1.8–5× stronger than OUTPOST (0.46/0.11), CENSOR (0.48/0.15), Soteria (0.52/0.12), GradAug (0.54/0.16), and DP (0.62/0.19), while also producing the lowest PSNR (7.43/5.18) and highest LPIPS (0.71/0.82). On FL accuracy (Table 3, D1 full participation), FedMOP leads by +0.77% over HierFed on CIFAR-10, +2.84% over HierFed on CIFAR-100, and +1.81% over FedUPS on Tiny-ImageNet; the gap widens under partial participation (e.g., +4.07% over HierFed on CIFAR-100 at 40% participation). For convergence speed (Table 5), FedMOP reaches 30% CIFAR-100 accuracy in 67 rounds under D1 full participation (4.66× over FedAvg), marginally beating HierFed (68 rounds) and substantially outpacing FedLMT (89 rounds) and FADAS (94 rounds). On computational cost (Table 6), FedMOP (6.3 MFLOPs, 10.6 s on CIFAR-10) is nearly identical to FedAvg (6.2 MFLOPs, 10.1 s) and significantly cheaper than FedLMT (19.7 MFLOPs, 21.4 s) or Scaffold (11.2 MFLOPs, 17.9 s), confirming that the orthogonal projection and momentum overhead is negligible.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedMOP builds directly on FedAvg's communication protocol, modifying only the client initialization step by adding a computed offset before local training. It repurposes gradient orthogonal projection—a technique well-established in multi-task learning (Gradient Surgery, GradNorm) and continual learning (GPM)—to counteract non-IID local drift in FL, a context where it had not previously been applied. The momentum-based trajectory hiding departs from all prior GLA defenses (DP, Soteria, CENSOR, GradAug, OUTPOST), which inject noise or clip gradients and thus sacrifice accuracy; instead, FedMOP makes the offset vector computationally unrecoverable through private initialization and randomized momentum evolution, creating a (d+t)-dimensional inverse problem for the attacker.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely-held assumption that privacy and performance in FL are fundamentally antagonistic, showing that a carefully designed initialization shift can improve both simultaneously. However, the security guarantee is computational infeasibility (exponential search space) rather than a formal information-theoretic bound, leaving open whether sufficiently powerful adversaries or side-channel information could weaken the protection. Practical limitations include the need for each client to persistently store a model-sized momentum vector, and the analysis assumes an honest-but-curious server with no collusion among clients. Future work could explore formal security proofs, scalability to very large T, and robustness against adaptive attacks that exploit knowledge of the momentum dynamics.
