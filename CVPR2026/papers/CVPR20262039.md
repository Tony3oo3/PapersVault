## Problem

Fine-tuning Vision-Language Models on driving data inevitably erodes the pre-trained world knowledge that motivates their use for long-tail scenario generalization, yet this catastrophic forgetting has never been systematically quantified or addressed in the VLM-centric driving literature. Existing benchmarks keep similar train/test distributions that mask true knowledge loss, and no dedicated evaluation protocol or mitigation strategy exists for this self-defeating paradox.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262039/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single front-view driving scene image paired with a text prompt (either a free-form instruction for scene description/object perception, or a structured traffic question), plus scene-specific cues such as visibility and traffic density that feed the expert router. Outputs are natural-language: a free-form scene description, a traffic-QA answer, and a list of noteworthy objects with perceptual confidence. The Fidelity Driving Bench evaluation set comprises 1,000 manually verified long-tail images drawn from 15 sources, scored via LLM-as-judge (Qwen3-Max, Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5) on three tasks, with the Knowledge Retention Rate (KRR) computed as the ratio of post-fine-tuning to pre-fine-tuning Noteworthy Objects' Perception Recall.

## Method

The Driving Expert Adapter (DEA) decouples driving adaptation from destructive weight updates via two complementary components operating on a fully frozen VLM. The Prompt Adapter learns a bank of trainable prompt embeddings indexed by query family; at inference it retrieves the most semantically relevant embeddings and prepends them to the input, stabilizing task interpretation without touching any VLM parameters. The Task-Adaptive Expert Module (TAEM) is an external Mixture-of-Experts layer of multiple LoRA adapters, each specialized to a distinct driving scenario (e.g., dense urban intersections, highway merging, adverse weather); a lightweight gating network routes on the enhanced prompt plus scene cues to select and fuse the outputs of the most relevant experts. This prompt-space routing strategy avoids the overfitting-to-phrasing problem of full fine-tuning while overcoming the insufficient capacity of a single LoRA adapter for rare long-tail scenes, achieving 79% KRR versus 64–68% for full fine-tuning or standard LoRA baselines on Qwen2.5VL-3B.

## Results

On the Fidelity Driving Bench, the full DEA framework (Base+TAEM+PA on Qwen2.5VL-3B) achieves a Knowledge Retention Rate (KRR) of 79.0%, Scene Description (SD) score of 58.8, Traffic-QA (T-QA) score of 41.0, and Noteworthy Objects' Perception Recall (NoPR) of 29.0. This substantially outperforms the full fine-tuning baseline (Base+Full: 64.3% KRR, 23.6 NoPR) and the LoRA baseline (Base+LoRA: 64.6% KRR, 23.8 NoPR) while also surpassing the unfine-tuned base model on T-QA (41.0 vs. 28.7). When applied to the ImpromptuVLA-3B checkpoint, DEA further lifts KRR from 68.4% to 76.0% and T-QA from 33.0 to 38.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEA is compared most directly against ImpromptuVLA-3B (full fine-tuning on the same Qwen2.5VL-3B base), where it wins on KRR (79.0% vs. 68.4%), T-QA (41.0 vs. 33.0), and NoPR (29.0 vs. 25.2) while matching SD (58.8 vs. 59.1). Against RecogDrive-8B (InternVL3-8B base, the strongest external baseline in Table 2), DEA's T-QA of 41.0 trails RecogDrive's 45.5, but DEA's NoPR of 29.0 is competitive given the different base model sizes. Against RoboTron-Drive (LLaMA-3.1+SigLIP), DEA leads on SD (58.8 vs. 61.9 is actually a loss) but wins decisively on T-QA (41.0 vs. 28.2) and NoPR (29.0 vs. 30.7 is a near-tie). Against safeAuto (Video-LLaVA base), DEA dominates on every metric (e.g., KRR 79.0% vs. 31.5–41.8%, T-QA 41.0 vs. 10.2–20.1). Against WiseAD (MobileVLM-V2-1.7B) and Mini-Intern (InternVL2-4B), DEA leads on all reported metrics, with KRR gaps of 49 and 25–43 percentage points respectively. Overall, DEA achieves the best KRR among all evaluated methods while maintaining competitive driving-task performance, confirming that prompt-space adaptation with expert routing resolves the retention–adaptation trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of VLM-centric autonomous driving (DriveLM, ImpromptuVLA, RecogDrive) and catastrophic forgetting research in post-training (EProj, MoELoRA, continual VLM tuning). While prior driving VLM work focuses on maximizing in-domain QA performance and prior forgetting work addresses general continual learning, no prior effort has systematically measured or addressed knowledge erosion specifically when fine-tuning VLMs on driving data. The DEA architecture repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm from weight-space routing (MoELoRA) to prompt-space routing, and the IDF/BM25-based long-tail mining pipeline borrows information-retrieval scoring to construct the first forgetting-specific driving benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Fidelity Driving Bench (180K scenes, 900K QA pairs, 15 sources) and the KRR metric provide the field with its first standardized tool to detect and quantify knowledge loss in driving VLMs, a gap that has allowed unsafe degradation to go undetected. The finding that LoRA — the default PEFT choice — actively degrades long-tail perception (NoPR drops of 30–57% across six backbones) challenges the assumption that parameter-efficient tuning is a safe default for safety-critical domains. Open questions include whether the prompt-space routing generalizes to multi-frame/temporal driving, whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, and whether the 1,000-image test set is sufficient to capture the full breadth of real-world long-tail hazards.
