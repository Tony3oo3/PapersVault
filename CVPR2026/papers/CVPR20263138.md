## Problem

Existing generative SR methods either rely on T2I diffusion backbones whose text-aligned semantics are spatially coarse and prone to hallucination, or use vision-only diffusion that lacks sufficient high-level semantic abstraction under severe degradation. The paper asks whether a purely vision-based generative model, trained natively for restoration without any multimodal pretraining, can rival T2I-based SR in both perceptual quality and faithfulness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263138/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded LR image (128×128 for ×4 SR on LSDIR/RealSR, 512×512 on ScreenSR) and produces a 512×512 HR reconstruction. The LR input is consumed in two parallel paths: encoded into the VAE latent space as a spatially aligned structural condition, and passed through a frozen DINOv2 vision encoder to extract dense semantic features injected via cross-attention. Both multi-step (25 NFE) and distilled one-step variants are provided.

## Method

VOSR is a latent diffusion model with a LightningDiT backbone (0.5B or 1.4B params) trained from scratch using velocity parameterization, conditioned on two complementary LR-derived signals: a VAE-encoded structural latent and DINOv2 semantic features. The key architectural insight is a restoration-oriented guidance strategy that replaces the standard CFG unconditional branch with a partially conditioned branch (weakened structural condition at scale α, no semantic condition), so both branches remain anchored to the LR input and the guidance direction interpolates between weak and strong input fidelity rather than between generic generation and conditioned restoration. At inference, a larger guidance scale increases faithfulness while a smaller scale allows more generative freedom—the opposite of T2I-based CFG. The multi-step teacher is distilled into a one-step student via a recursive-consistency-based objective that preserves the same conditioning interface and restoration-oriented guidance, achieving ~0.095s inference at 512×512.

## Results

On LSDIR (×4 SR), VOSR-1.4B-ms achieves the best LPIPS (0.3857), DISTS (0.1977), and MUSIQ (74.03) among all multi-step methods, while VOSR-0.5B-ms attains the best NIQE (4.05). In the one-step setting, VOSR-0.5B-os leads on NIQE (3.65), AFINE-NR (−1.029), and TOPIQ-NR (0.699). On the real-world ScreenSR benchmark, VOSR-1.4B-ms obtains the best MUSIQ (73.31) and MANIQA (0.720); on RealSR, VOSR-1.4B-ms achieves the best LPIPS (0.296) and MUSIQ (70.47). VOSR-1.4B-ms runs in 2.11 s per 512×512 image (multi-step) and VOSR-0.5B-os in 0.094 s (one-step), substantially faster than T2I-based counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior vision-only method ResShift, VOSR substantially outperforms on all perceptual metrics: on LSDIR multi-step, VOSR-1.4B-ms improves LPIPS from 0.478 to 0.386 and MUSIQ from 59.1 to 74.0, while on RealSR one-step, VOSR-1.4B-os improves LPIPS from 0.319 to 0.273 and MUSIQ from 60.8 to 70.6. Compared with T2I-based multi-step methods (StableSR, PASD, SeeSR, DiT4SR), VOSR-1.4B-ms matches or exceeds them on perceptual quality (e.g., best LPIPS 0.386 vs. SeeSR's 0.418 on LSDIR; best MUSIQ 73.31 vs. SeeSR's 72.31 on ScreenSR) while running 1.3–6× faster (2.11 s vs. 2.75–12.55 s). In the one-step setting, VOSR-0.5B-os and VOSR-1.4B-os are competitive with OSEDiff and PiSA-SR on perceptual metrics (e.g., VOSR-1.4B-os achieves LPIPS 0.380 vs. PiSA-SR's 0.376 on LSDIR) while being slightly faster (0.095 s vs. 0.091–0.104 s) and using fewer parameters (1,746 M vs. 1,290–1,760 M). Against the vision-only one-step SinSR, VOSR dominates on perceptual quality (e.g., MUSIQ 73.8 vs. 61.9 on LSDIR; LPIPS 0.380 vs. 0.456) while remaining competitive on PSNR. Overall, VOSR achieves the best or near-best perceptual scores across all three benchmarks in both multi-step and one-step regimes, with the largest margins over prior vision-only methods and competitive results against the strongest T2I-based baselines at a fraction of the training cost (~1/10th).

## Relation to Prior Work

VOSR sits at the crossroads of two SR paradigms: vision-only diffusion methods (SR3, SRDiff, ResShift) that are naturally restoration-oriented but lack semantic guidance under severe degradation, and T2I-based methods (StableSR, SeeSR, DiT4SR) that leverage strong multimodal priors but are not native to input-anchored restoration. The paper builds on latent diffusion and the LightningDiT backbone, but its core departure is twofold: it replaces text-aligned semantic conditioning with spatially grounded DINOv2 visual features, and it modifies classifier-free guidance by substituting the fully unconditional branch with a partially conditioned branch that retains weakened LR structural cues. For one-step inference, it adapts recursive-consistency distillation from the general generative modeling literature to the SR setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a generative SR model trained entirely on visual data—without any text-image pretraining—can rival or exceed T2I-based SR in perceptual quality while producing fewer hallucinations, at roughly one-tenth the training cost. The finding that the guidance scale behaves oppositely to T2I-based methods (larger scale → more fidelity, not more generation) provides a principled knob for the generation-fidelity trade-off that is specific to restoration. The main open question is scalability: the authors acknowledge that their 100M-image training set and 1.4B-parameter model still lag behind 10B+ T2I foundation models, leaving unclear whether the vision-only paradigm can match T2I-based quality at much larger scale or extend to other restoration tasks.
