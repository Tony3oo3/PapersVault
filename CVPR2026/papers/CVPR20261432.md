## Problem

Existing SVD-based LMM compression methods suffer from two key limitations: they inadequately compensate for the errors introduced by truncating small singular values (especially at high compression ratios ≥60%), and they apply a uniform compression ratio across all transformer layers despite significant variation in layer importance. This results in a noticeable performance gap between compressed and original models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261432/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an LMM's linear layer weight matrices, a set of 256 calibration samples from WikiText-2, and a target retention ratio (trr) with a minimum retention ratio (mrr) as hyperparameters. Calibration data is processed via a stack-of-batch strategy that averages shuffled mini-batches to fit within a fixed GPU memory budget (e.g., 80GB A100), enabling use of more samples than would otherwise fit. The output is a compressed model where each linear weight W is replaced by two smaller low-rank factors U_k^σ and V_k^σ^T whose product approximates W, with per-layer rank determined by the adaptive compression ratio.

## Method

AdaSVD introduces two adaptive mechanisms. First, adaComp compensates for SVD truncation error by alternately updating U_k^σ and V_k^σ^T over τ iterations, where each update solves a least-squares problem (treating the other factor times calibration data as input and WX as output) using the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse to ensure numerical stability. Second, adaCR assigns layer-specific compression ratios by measuring each layer's importance as the cosine similarity between its input and output activations, normalizing by the mean importance, and linearly mapping to a retention ratio between mrr and trr. The method is training-free, operates post-hoc on pretrained weights, and can be combined with data whitening (from SVD-LLM) and weight quantization (e.g., GPTQ) for further compression.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B at 60% compression ratio, AdaSVD achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 50.33 (vs. 89.90 for SVD-LLM, 10,003.57 for ASVD, and 27,213.30 for FWSVD) and an average zero-shot accuracy of 36.87% across five reasoning benchmarks (vs. 35.48% for SVD-LLM). At 40% compression, AdaSVD reaches 42.63% average accuracy, outperforming SVD-LLM (40.69%) by nearly 2 points. Across four LMMs (OPT-6.7B, LLaMA2-7B, Mistral-7B, Vicuna-7B) at 60% CR, AdaSVD consistently achieves the lowest WikiText-2 perplexity, with the largest margin on LLaMA2-7B (50.33 vs. 89.90 for SVD-LLM, a 44% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSVD outperforms all three SVD-based baselines (FWSVD, ASVD, SVD-LLM) across every compression ratio and every evaluated metric. Against the strongest baseline SVD-LLM, the advantage grows with compression severity: at 40% CR, AdaSVD reduces WikiText-2 perplexity by 8% (14.76 vs. 16.11) and gains ~2 points in average reasoning accuracy (42.63% vs. 40.69%); at 60% CR, the perplexity gap widens to 44% on WikiText-2 (50.33 vs. 89.90), 41% on PTB (1,217 vs. 2,053), and 57% on C4 (239 vs. 561). FWSVD and ASVD are substantially weaker, with WikiText-2 perplexities in the thousands or tens of thousands at 60% CR, and the paper notes that both methods effectively fail (produce incoherent outputs) at compression ratios below 60% on several LMMs. On the cross-model comparison (Table 2, 60% CR, WikiText-2), AdaSVD achieves the best perplexity on all four models: OPT-6.7B (86.64 vs. 92.10), LLaMA2-7B (50.33 vs. 89.90), Mistral-7B (67.22 vs. 72.17), and Vicuna-7B (56.97 vs. 64.06) compared to SVD-LLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSVD extends the SVD-based LMM compression lineage initiated by FWSVD (Fisher-weighted SVD), ASVD (activation-aware scaling), and SVD-LLM (data whitening before truncation). It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) it introduces adaComp, which explicitly compensates for truncation error by alternately updating the retained singular matrices via Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse solutions, a step largely absent in prior methods; and (2) it introduces adaCR, which replaces the uniform compression ratio with a layer-importance-aware assignment. The method builds directly on SVD-LLM's data whitening as a preprocessing step and is designed to be orthogonal to quantization (demonstrated with GPTQ).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaSVD addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying LMMs on memory-constrained devices: at high compression ratios (≥60%), prior SVD methods suffer severe perplexity degradation, while AdaSVD maintains substantially lower error through its adaptive compensation. The layer-importance finding (first layer consistently most critical, bowl-shaped curve for LLaMA) provides actionable guidance for practitioners choosing where to allocate compression budget. Open questions include whether the alternating-update compensation generalizes to much larger models (70B+), whether the stack-of-batch calibration strategy remains effective with very limited calibration data, and how the method interacts with emerging architectural changes (e.g., mixture-of-experts) that may alter layer importance profiles.
