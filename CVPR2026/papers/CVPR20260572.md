## Problem

Neural Architecture Search faces a fundamental accuracy-efficiency dilemma: training-based methods produce reliable rankings but require prohibitive GPU hours, while training-free proxies are ultra-fast yet yield lower ranking accuracy and are tightly coupled to specific architecture families (CNNs, Transformers, or Mamba), failing catastrophically when transferred across families. No existing method achieves both high cross-family ranking fidelity and near-training-free computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-defined search space (over CNN, Transformer, or Mamba blocks), a total time budget in GPU hours, and ImageNet-1K images to construct the six probing-task datasets. It outputs a single selected architecture (the highest proxy-score candidate among ~500–1000 sampled networks, constrained to ≤17 M parameters) along with the full proxy-score ranking. The six per-task scores are normalized to a common scale and direction (higher-is-better) before being summed into one scalar proxy that drives selection.

## Method

The core contribution is a lightweight training-based NAS framework built on two components. First, six vision-specific probing tasks—Local-Global Jigsaw, Occlusion In-Painting, Rotation Match, Color-Shape Binding, Motion Forecast, and Visual Memorization—each probe a distinct representational capacity (spatial reasoning, generative filling, geometric equivariance, attribute binding, dynamics modeling, and few-shot memorization) with negligible training cost, and their normalized scores are aggregated into a single proxy. Second, a budget-adaptive evaluator models the validation score as a quadratic response surface over dataset size D and training epochs E, fits it via five Latin-hypercube anchor points, and solves for the optimal (D*, E*) under the linear time constraint using a closed-form saddle-point or Lagrange-multiplier solution, thereby maximizing proxy quality within any user-specified deadline without numerical iteration.

## Results

On ranking correlation (Kendall-τ, 'all' setting), the method achieves 92.10 on NAS-Bench-101, 93.31 on NAS-Bench-201, 89.27 on NATS-Bench, 86.00 on NAS-Bench-301, 86.38 on ViT-Bench-101-A, 80.71 on ViT-Bench-101-P, 86.48 on Vim, and 87.15 on VMamba—consistently the highest across all eight benchmarks. In terms of downstream search quality, the discovered architectures reach 80.9% top-1 accuracy (CNN), 80.5% (Transformer), and 80.8% (Mamba) on ImageNet-1K classification with only 3 GPU hours of search time, surpassing all training-based baselines that require 3–453 GPU hours. For object detection (Mask R-CNN 3× MS), the best CNN architecture achieves 49.8 APb, and for semantic segmentation, 44.3 mIoU (SS) / 47.6 mIoU (MS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-free NAS baselines (Table 1), the proposed method achieves the highest Kendall-τ on all eight benchmarks, with margins of 4–11 points over the strongest competitor in each family: AZ-NAS (85.12) on NAS-Bench-101, ZiCo (84.38) on NAS-Bench-201, Zen-NAS (83.39) on NATS-Bench, AZ-NAS (81.90) on NAS-Bench-301, HC (81.14) on ViT-Bench-101-A, and Gradnorm (42.79) on VMamba. The gap is especially dramatic on Mamba architectures, where CNN-oriented proxies (GraSP, Synflow, Snip) collapse to 12–22 while the proposed method reaches 87.15, confirming the generality advantage. Among training-based NAS methods (Table 2), the proposed method at 3 GPU hours (ours-L) surpasses MOTE-NAS (77.2% top-1, 3.1 GPU hrs), QuantNAS (79.3%, 17 GPU hrs), RobustDNAS (79.1%, 179 GPU hrs), AttentiveNAS (75.2%, 420 GPU hrs), and OFA (75.3%, 27 GPU hrs) on ImageNet classification, while also leading on detection (49.8 APb vs. 49.7 for MOTE-NAS) and segmentation (44.3/47.6 mIoU SS/MS vs. 41.8/45.0 for MOTE-NAS). Even at the 1 GPU-hour budget (ours-S), the method already matches or exceeds MOTE-NAS on classification (78.7% vs. 77.2%) and detection (47.5 APb vs. 46.3 APb), demonstrating that the budget-adaptive evaluation extracts near-optimal ranking quality from minimal compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the synthetic-task evaluation paradigm from Poli et al. [41] (ICML 2024), which used 1D token-level memorization/retrieval/compression tasks to predict model quality, by redesigning six probing tasks specifically for 2D visual data (jigsaw, occlusion in-painting, rotation match, color-shape binding, motion forecast, visual memorization). It departs from the training-free NAS literature (Synflow, SNIP, GraSP, Zen-NAS, etc.) that scores architectures at random initialization via single-pass statistics, instead performing brief supervised training on the probing tasks to obtain a more reliable proxy. The budget-adaptive evaluation via quadratic response-surface fitting over dataset size and epochs is a novel engineering layer not present in prior NAS work, drawing on scaling-law-inspired surrogate modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method resolves a long-standing accuracy-efficiency trade-off in NAS: it achieves ranking correlations (Kendall-τ ≈ 86–93) that substantially exceed all training-free proxies while requiring only 1–3 GPU hours of search time, comparable to training-free methods and far below training-based approaches (17–453 GPU hours). Its generality across CNNs, Vision Transformers, and Mamba/SSM architectures—where existing zero-cost proxies collapse to near-random when transferred across families—addresses a key limitation of the training-free NAS literature. Open questions include whether the quadratic surface assumption holds for very large search spaces or non-convex score landscapes, whether the six tasks can be automatically re-weighted per target application (as the authors note as future work), and whether the approach extends to non-vision modalities such as audio or text.
