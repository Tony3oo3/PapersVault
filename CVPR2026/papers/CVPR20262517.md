## Problem

Interleaved multimodal generation—producing coordinated text-image sequences for tasks like step-by-step tutorials and visual planning—remains limited by insufficient high-quality instruction-tuning data, architectural constraints in prior unified models (e.g., image heads that cannot accept multiple conditioning images), and the absence of systematic evaluation. Existing approaches either require expensive unimodal pretraining from scratch or target narrow reasoning domains, leaving a gap for general-purpose interleaved generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262517/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts, user-provided reference images, or partially completed interleaved image-text sequences (for continuation tasks) and produces an interleaved text-image sequence where the MLLM autonomously decides when to emit images via a special <BOV> token. Generated images are 768×768 RGB; all preceding images in the conversation (both user-supplied and model-generated) are stacked as conditioning frames for the DiT, while MLLM hidden states preceding the <BOV> token supply semantic and linguistic guidance through a lightweight connector.

## Method

DuoGen fuses a pretrained MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL 7B) with a video-pretrained diffusion transformer (Cosmos Predict 2.5 2B) in a decoupled architecture that avoids unimodal pretraining. During generation, the MLLM autoregressively predicts text until it emits a <BOV> token; at that point, all preceding images are VAE-encoded and concatenated with the noisy target latent along the temporal axis as conditioning frames, while MLLM hidden states (concatenated across decoder layers) are projected via a cross-attention connector to condition the DiT. Training follows a two-stage decoupled strategy: Stage 1 fine-tunes only the MLLM on 298k curated interleaved instruction-tuning conversations (from a web-data engine plus synthetic samples) under next-token prediction, teaching it when to trigger image generation; Stage 2 freezes the MLLM and fine-tunes the connector and DiT on large-scale interleaved context-alignment data (5M video transition clips plus image generation/editing datasets) using flow-matching loss. This design lets the system leverage strong pretrained visual understanding and video-generation quality while learning interleaved coordination without balancing competing unimodal objectives.

## Results

On the CoMM benchmark, DuoGen achieves an Illustration Relevance Score (IRS) of 7.76, a 2.8× improvement over the second-best method MiniGPT-5 (2.71), and leads on all other metrics (Style 9.22, Entity 9.22, Trend 9.24, Completeness 9.66, Image Quality 9.53). On InterleavedBench, DuoGen attains an average score of 3.87, with text quality of 4.28 (3.4× Emu2's 1.26). On the authors' new benchmark (Cooking-200 / How-to-500), DuoGen outperforms all open-source baselines by large margins and narrows the gap to the commercial Nano Banana model, matching it on image-text coherence for Cooking-200 (4.75 vs. 4.83). On GenEval, DuoGen scores 0.88 overall, the best among unified and interleaved generation models, and on ImgEdit it reaches 4.19 overall with GEdit_EN G_O of 7.35.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuoGen decisively outperforms all open-source interleaved generation baselines. On CoMM, it surpasses Emu2 (the strongest prior open-source model) by 0.81 on Style, 1.66 on Entity, 1.61 on Trend, 2.12 on Completeness, and 5.74 on IRS (7.76 vs. 2.02). On InterleavedBench, DuoGen's average of 3.87 nearly doubles GILL's 1.84 and MiniGPT-5's 1.82, with text quality of 4.28 vs. Emu2's 1.26. On the authors' new benchmark, DuoGen beats Zebra-CoT by 1.51–2.07 points across Cooking-200 metrics and 1.34–1.63 on How-to-500, while also surpassing Nano Banana on Human Elo for text completeness (1428 vs. 1393) and image completeness (1442 vs. 1369), though Nano Banana retains an edge on image quality Elo (1473 vs. 1384). For image generation (GenEval), DuoGen (0.88) outperforms all unified models including Bagel (0.82), OmniGen2 (0.80), and UniWorld-V1 (0.80), and even exceeds the commercial FLUX.1-dev (0.82). For image editing, DuoGen (ImgEdit 4.19, GEdit G_O 7.35) trails Qwen-Image-Edit (4.27, 7.56) and Nano Banana (4.23, 6.93) on overall ImgEdit but leads all unified models and matches or exceeds them on specific sub-tasks like Remove (4.71) and Replace (4.69).

## Relation to Prior Work

DuoGen departs from the early-fusion paradigm adopted by unified models like Chameleon, Show-o, and Bagel, which jointly pretrain text and image generation from scratch on mixed-modality corpora. Instead, it follows the pretrained-fusion line (BLIP3-o, MetaQuery) but extends it specifically to interleaved generation by coupling a pretrained MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) with a video-pretrained diffusion transformer (Cosmos Predict 2.5), treating prior images in the conversation as conditioning 'frames' for the DiT. The data pipeline builds on and substantially improves CoMM's webpage-to-conversation approach by adding MLLM-based denoising, reorganization, and dialogue conversion, while the decoupled two-stage training strategy (instruction-tune MLLM first, then align DiT) is novel relative to prior joint-training recipes. The hidden-state concatenation technique for text conditioning follows Wang et al.'s decoder-only LLM study for text-to-image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic framework addressing general-purpose interleaved generation across data curation, architecture, and evaluation, filling a gap where prior work was limited to narrow reasoning domains (math, navigation) or lacked quantitative benchmarks. The decoupled training strategy is practically significant because it avoids the heavy compute of unimodal pretraining and allows flexible base-model selection, making the approach accessible to groups without massive training budgets. However, the model still trails Nano Banana on several metrics, the evaluation relies heavily on GPT-5 as judge (raising reproducibility concerns), and the 'no unimodal pretraining needed' claim is validated only for one architecture pair (7B MLLM + 2B DiT), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger or different model families. The released 298k instruction-tuning dataset and new benchmark (Cooking-200, How-to-500) will likely become standard resources for the community.
