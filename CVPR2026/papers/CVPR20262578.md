## Problem

Traditional cross-view geo-localization relies on a satellite-centric anchoring strategy, making it fragile when high-resolution or up-to-date satellite imagery is unavailable and underexploiting complementary cues across drone, street-view, and satellite perspectives. Additionally, existing methods largely ignore the complementarity between textual descriptions and multi-view visual inputs, leading to semantic hallucinations and spatial inconsistencies in language-based localization. The drone-to-street-view matching task, with clear value for UAV navigation and emergency response, remains entirely unaddressed in prior work.

## Input / Output

GeoBridge accepts drone images, street-view panoramas, and satellite images (all resized to 224×224) along with an optional concise text description per location, and outputs geographic coordinates by retrieving the co-located reference image from any other view. The text descriptions are generated by ChatGPT-4o and emphasize viewpoint-agnostic cues (roads, buildings, landmarks) while omitting transient or view-specific elements. At inference the text branch is entirely optional: the model performs pure image-to-image matching across any pair of views, or text-to-image retrieval when a description is provided, with retrieval restricted to other views within the same location class for privacy.

## Method

GeoBridge employs three view-specific CLIP-L/14 visual encoders (for drone, panorama, and satellite) and a shared CLIP text encoder, all mapping inputs into a common D-dimensional embedding space. The core mechanism is a semantic anchor: a single unified textual description per location serves as a cross-modal bridge, simultaneously aligning each visual view to the text (text-to-image InfoNCE loss) and aligning the three visual views to each other (image-to-image InfoNCE loss averaged over the three view pairs). The total training objective is the sum of the image alignment loss and the text alignment loss, optimized end-to-end with a learnable temperature coefficient τ and Adam at 1×10⁻⁵ with cosine decay over 200 epochs. Because the semantic anchor operates only during training, inference incurs no additional overhead beyond standard nearest-neighbor retrieval in the shared embedding space, and the model naturally supports bidirectional matching across all six view-pair directions plus three text-to-image directions.

## Results

On the proposed GeoLoc dataset, GeoBridge achieves R@1 of 45.05 (D2S), 44.81 (S2D), 41.22 (D2P), 41.15 (P2D), 38.87 (P2S), and 39.20 (S2P), outperforming the strongest baseline (Sample4Geo) by 15–22 R@1 points across all six retrieval directions. On the standard University-1652 benchmark, GeoBridge reaches 95.82 R@1 / 97.77 AP for Drone-to-Satellite and 97.14 R@1 / 95.05 AP for Satellite-to-Drone, surpassing the previous best (DAC) by ~1.2 and ~0.7 R@1 respectively. On CVUSA, GeoBridge attains 99.14 R@1 and 99.98 R@1%, and on VIGOR-Cross it reaches 73.87 R@1 / 81.51 Hit, both setting new state-of-the-art. For cross-modal retrieval on RSIEval, GeoBridge scores 29.00 R@1, 71.00 R@5, and 88.00 R@10, exceeding CLIP-L/14 by 4, 11, and 14 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoBridge achieves the best R@1 and AP on all six retrieval directions of GeoLoc, with margins of 15–22 R@1 points over the strongest prior method (Sample4Geo), which itself was the best on D2S/S2D but degrades sharply on the novel D2P/P2P tasks. On University-1652, GeoBridge beats DAC (the previous SOTA) by 1.15 R@1 on Drone-to-Satellite (95.82 vs 94.67) and 0.71 R@1 on Satellite-to-Drone (97.14 vs 96.43), while also improving AP by 2.27 and 1.26 points respectively. On SUES-200, GeoBridge matches or exceeds CGSI across all four altitude settings for Satellite-to-Drone (e.g., 98.99 vs 97.50 R@1 at 150 m) and is within 0.27 R@1 on Drone-to-Satellite at 150 m (95.68 vs 95.95) while leading on AP. On CVUSA, GeoBridge surpasses AuxGeo by 0.34 R@1 (99.14 vs 98.80) and 0.13 R@1%; on VIGOR-Same it leads in R@1 (85.82 vs 82.18 for Panorama-BEV) but trails AuxGeo slightly on Hit (92.24 vs 93.78), while on VIGOR-Cross it wins both R@1 (73.87 vs 72.19) and Hit (81.51 vs 76.25). For cross-modal retrieval, GeoBridge outperforms CLIP-L/14 by 4 R@1 points on RSIEval (29.00 vs 25.00) and by 8.67 R@1 on the hardest GeoLoc text-to-image direction (Satellite Description → Street Image: 21.58 vs 12.20), confirming that the semantic-anchor mechanism provides a consistent advantage over both vision-only and text-only baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoBridge departs from the dominant satellite-centric paradigm in cross-view geo-localization (CVUSA, VIGOR, University-1652, SUES-200), where satellite imagery serves as the fixed retrieval anchor, by introducing a closed-loop, bidirectional matching framework across drone, street-view panorama, and satellite views. The method repurposes CLIP's pre-trained vision and text encoders within a contrastive (InfoNCE) training objective, but the key conceptual departure is the use of a unified textual description as a semantic anchor that aligns all three visual views into a shared embedding space during training. The drone-to-street-view matching task is explicitly framed as newly defined, filling a gap left by prior two-view, satellite-anchored formulations. The contrastive learning framework itself follows standard multi-modal alignment practice, so the novelty lies in the multi-view topology and the semantic-bridge role of text rather than in a new encoder architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GeoLoc dataset (52,679 co-located triplets across 36 countries) is the first large-scale, fully aligned multi-view geo-localization benchmark, and the drone-to-street-view task it enables has direct practical value for UAV navigation, emergency response, and low-altitude logistics where satellite data may be unavailable or outdated. A notable limitation is the dependence on ChatGPT-4o-generated text descriptions as the semantic anchor, which introduces a quality ceiling and a single-point-of-failure for the alignment mechanism; the absolute retrieval numbers on GeoLoc (e.g., ~45% R@1 for D2S) are substantially lower than on curated benchmarks like University-1652 (~96%), suggesting the global, multi-scale setting remains far from solved. Open questions include whether the semantic anchor generalizes beyond CLIP-scale encoders, how performance scales with gallery size in real deployment, and whether the text descriptions can be replaced by learned latent tokens to remove the LLM dependency.
