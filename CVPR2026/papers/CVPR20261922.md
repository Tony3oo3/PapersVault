## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation pipelines connect language reasoning to visual perception through either opaque latent query alignment (end-to-end but black-box) or textual localization readout (readable but unconstrained and post-hoc). Neither provides a truly interpretable, verifiable pathway from the model's reasoning process to the final mask prediction, leaving the spatial grounding mechanism unexplainable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261922/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a natural-language instruction as input. The MLLM backbone first generates a chain-of-thought trace followed by K_s concentration tokens (K_s ≤ 6). The SAE encodes the full hidden-state sequence (image tokens, text tokens, and CoT tokens) into a 65,536-dimensional sparse activation space. The final outputs are per-slot spatial heatmaps with confidence scores and the corresponding binary segmentation masks, with the heatmap serving as an intermediate observable that explicitly links reasoning concepts to spatial regions.

## Method

SegCompass inserts a pretrained Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) between the MLLM's hidden states and the segmentation decoder to create an explicit interpretable bridge. The SAE maps image, text, and CoT hidden states into a shared 65,536-dim sparse concept space where each dimension is a dictionary atom; a learned query codebook then selects and aggregates the active (nonzero) dimensions via Transformer encoder blocks into K_s concept representations. These are fused with concentration-token embeddings to form slot queries that attend over ViT-H (SAM) image features through a slot mapper, producing a multi-slot heatmap with per-slot confidences, which a Two-Way Transformer decoder converts to masks. Training unifies GRPO reinforcement learning on the reasoning path (rewarding mask IoU and CoT format) with supervised segmentation losses (BCE on heatmaps, Dice on masks, BCE on confidences) under a single end-to-end objective, making the sparse concept activations both a performance driver and an inspectable explanation of how each instruction component is spatially grounded.

## Results

On the RefCOCO series (Table 1), SegCompass-13B (LLaVA-1.5) achieves 86.3/87.3/84.1 cIoU on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 80.5/84.6/76.9 on RefCOCO+, and 84.0/81.3 on RefCOCOg val/test, setting new state-of-the-art on nearly all splits. The Qwen2.5-VL-7B variant reaches 85.3/87.5/83.6 on RefCOCO and 82.8/81.0 on RefCOCOg, surpassing the 8B-parameter HiMTok (85.9/86.3/83.9 and 80.1/80.9) with fewer parameters. On gRefCOCO (multi-object), SegCompass-13B attains 76.8/72.2 gIoU/cIoU (val) and 77.7/77.3 (testA), outperforming RAS-13B (74.6/70.5 and 77.5/77.0). Zero-shot on ReasonSeg, SegCompass-13B reaches 67.4/67.2 gIoU/cIoU (val) and 64.2/66.5 (test), exceeding the prior best HiMTok-8B (60.7/67.0 and 60.8/66.2) by over 6 points in gIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegCompass-13B outperforms the strongest latent-query baseline HiMTok-8B by +0.4/+1.0/+0.2 cIoU on RefCOCO val/testA/testB and by +3.9/+0.4 on RefCOCOg val/test, while using a 13B LLaVA backbone versus HiMTok's 8B InternVL-2.5. Against X-SAM-3.8B (the strongest small-model baseline), SegCompass-13B leads by +1.2/+0.2/+0.7 on RefCOCO and +0.2/−2.6 on RefCOCOg val/test, with X-SAM retaining a slight edge on RefCOCOg test (83.9 vs. 81.3). Among textual-localization methods, SegCompass-7B (Qwen2.5-VL) surpasses Seg-Zero-7B by +7.2 cIoU on RefCOCO testA and +8.4 on RefCOCOg test, and beats Text4Seg-13B by +4.8/+5.7 on RefCOCO val/testA. On gRefCOCO, SegCompass-13B leads RAS-13B by +2.2/+1.7 gIoU/cIoU (val) and +0.2/+0.3 (testA), and exceeds Text4Seg-13B by +2.0/+2.4 gIoU/cIoU (val). On zero-shot ReasonSeg, SegCompass-13B achieves 67.4/67.2 gIoU/cIoU (val), surpassing HiMTok-8B (60.7/67.0) by +6.7 in gIoU and SAM-R1-7B (64.0/55.8) by +3.4/+11.4, confirming that the SAE-based interpretable alignment generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegCompass repurposes the Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) paradigm—originally developed for post-hoc interpretability of LLM internals (SAE-V, Huben et al.)—as a *functional* architectural component that actively routes information from reasoning to perception. It combines this with GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) for the reasoning path and slot attention (Locatello et al.) for spatial grounding, creating a unified pipeline that departs from both the opaque latent-query alignment family (LISA, PerceptionGPT, HiMTok) and the post-hoc textual-localization family (SAM4MLLM, Seg-Zero, Text4Seg). The key conceptual departure is treating the SAE not as an analysis tool but as a differentiable, trainable interface whose sparse concept space is the explicit medium through which CoT reasoning is grounded to pixels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: existing reasoning-segmentation pipelines either hide their reasoning-to-perception mapping in latent vectors or reduce it to a readable-but-unconstrained text readout, leaving no inspectable intermediate. SegCompass's multi-slot heatmap and per-slot confidences offer a concrete, visualizable alignment that could aid debugging, trust, and fine-grained control in safety-critical applications (robotics, medical imaging). Open questions include whether the 65K-dimensional sparse space yields concepts that are genuinely human-interpretable at scale (the paper shows activation statistics but not named-concept examples), whether the interpretability advantage translates to downstream controllability (e.g., steering a specific concept to change the mask), and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D segmentation where the slot-mapper's spatial assumptions may break down.
