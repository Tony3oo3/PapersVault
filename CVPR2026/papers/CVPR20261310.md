## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based pose refinement methods (e.g., GS-CPR) treat the initial pose prior as a single deterministic estimate and assume all rendered depths are equally reliable, making them fragile when the prior is biased or when the 3DGS reconstruction has spatially varying geometric quality. This paper identifies these two overlooked uncertainty sources—pose prior uncertainty and geometric uncertainty—as the root cause of unstable PnP optimization and degraded localization accuracy in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261310/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query RGB image, a 6-DoF pose prior from any APR or SCR model (e.g., DFNet, ACE, Marepo), a pre-trained Scaffold-GS scene, and camera intrinsics, and produces a refined 6-DoF camera pose. No retraining of the 3DGS, the pose prior network, or the 2D matching module (MASt3R, LoFTR, or SuperPoint+LightGlue) is required; the entire refinement is training-free. The Monte Carlo stage uses N=2 iterations with m=8 particles, yielding an end-to-end runtime of roughly 1.1 s per iteration on an RTX A5000.

## Method

UGS-Loc replaces the single-pose deterministic refinement with a Monte Carlo Localization-inspired particle filter over SE(3): M pose hypotheses are sampled around the prior, each is refined through the standard render-and-compare pipeline (2D-2D matching → depth lifting → PnP), and particles are reweighted by a product of the matcher's confidence score map and a per-pixel geometric uncertainty map. The geometric uncertainty is obtained by extending FisherRF's Fisher Information computation to Scaffold-GS parameters (anchor features and local Gaussian offsets), aggregating the Hessian over training views into a global matrix, and projecting it through the 3DGS alpha-compositing rendering equation to yield a 2D uncertainty map. This uncertainty then biases both the RANSAC sampling weights (s_i = exp(−β·U̅(r_i)) + ε) and the consensus evaluation, steering PnP toward geometrically reliable correspondences. The prediction phase of the particle filter is replaced by a local optimization step that directs each particle toward a nearby likelihood mode, avoiding the prohibitively large particle counts that naive random perturbation would require in 6-DoF space.

## Results

On Cambridge Landmarks, UGS-Loc with DFNet prior achieves a median translation/rotation error of 10.7 cm / 0.20°, reducing the GS-CPR baseline (26 cm / 0.51°) by approximately 30%. On 7Scenes, ACE+UGS-Loc reaches 0.64 cm / 0.21° median error and 95.6% recall at the strict [2 cm, 2°] threshold, outperforming ACE+GS-CPR (0.8 cm / 0.25°, 93.1%). On 12Scenes, ACE+UGS-Loc attains 99.1% recall at [2 cm, 2°] and the lowest median error (0.4 cm / 0.18°) among all methods. The method requires only 2 iterations with 8 particles, yielding ~1.1 s per iteration end-to-end.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGS-Loc consistently outperforms all 18 baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the most direct competitor GS-CPR [30], UGS-Loc reduces median translation error by ~30% on Cambridge Landmarks (10.7 vs 26 cm with DFNet; 10.5 vs 15 cm with ACE) and by ~20% on 7Scenes (0.64 vs 0.8 cm with ACE). Compared to MCLoc [53], which also uses Monte Carlo refinement but with 80 iterations, UGS-Loc achieves substantially lower errors (10.7 vs 27 cm on Cambridge) while running in ~1.1 s per iteration versus MCLoc's ~2.4 s. Against APR baselines (PoseNet, DFNet, Marepo, RAP, MS-Transformer, LENS), UGS-Loc improves median translation by 5–15×, e.g., 10.7 cm vs DFNet's 119 cm on Cambridge. Versus SCR methods (ACE, GLACE), UGS-Loc further reduces median errors (10.5 vs 21 cm for ACE on Cambridge; 0.64 vs 1.1 cm on 7Scenes). Among other NRP methods, UGS-Loc surpasses RAPref [26] (10.7 vs 16 cm), HR-APR [31] (10.7 vs 35 cm), CrossFire [38] (10.7 vs 37 cm), and STDLoc [16] (0.64 vs 0.76 cm on 7Scenes), confirming that the gains stem from the probabilistic formulation rather than iterative refinement alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GS-CPR render-and-compare pipeline (2D-2D matching → depth lifting → PnP-RANSAC) by injecting two forms of uncertainty that prior 3DGS-based refinement methods treat as negligible: pose prior uncertainty, handled via a Monte Carlo Localization-inspired particle framework, and geometric uncertainty, quantified by extending FisherRF's Fisher Information computation to the Scaffold-GS anchor-based representation. Unlike photometric or feature-space alignment approaches (iNeRF, NeFeS) that optimize poses directly, UGS-Loc remains in the correspondence-driven PnP paradigm but biases both particle weighting and RANSAC sampling toward geometrically reliable regions. The method is training-free and agnostic to the upstream pose estimator (APR or SCR) and the 2D matcher (MASt3R, SuperPoint+LightGlue), positioning it as a drop-in refinement module rather than a new end-to-end system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: 3DGS-based localization is rapidly becoming the default rendering backend for pose refinement, yet its sensitivity to imperfect priors and reconstruction artifacts has been under-addressed. By showing that a weak APR prior (DFNet) can be refined to near-ACE-level accuracy, the paper demonstrates that the refinement stage can compensate for upstream errors, which is valuable for deployment where the cheapest/fastest prior may be preferred. Open questions include how the Fisher Information computation scales to very large or dynamic scenes, whether the uncertainty signal degrades when the 3DGS model is trained from very few views, and whether the Monte Carlo particle count can be further reduced without accuracy loss. The method's 1.1 s/iteration runtime is practical but still an order of magnitude slower than a single deterministic PnP call, which may matter for real-time robotics.
