## Problem

Standard VLA finetuning methods inherit language-model training objectives (one-hot cross-entropy or PPO) that treat each action as uniquely correct, ignoring the intrinsic tolerance of physical manipulation where a neighborhood of near-equivalent actions exists. This causes SFT to collapse onto single demonstrated actions (severe overfitting) and makes RFT sample-inefficient at discovering broader, more generalizable action distributions. No prior method explicitly exploits this Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN) structure during training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes third-person RGB images, wrist-camera RGB images, robot proprioceptive state, and a natural-language instruction, and autoregressively outputs 7-DoF discrete action tokens over binned action dimensions. OpenVLA-OFT extends this to output action chunks (multiple timesteps) rather than single actions. The FAN regularizer operates on the per-state policy distribution over these discrete action bins, requiring no architectural changes to the autoregressive decoding pipeline.

## Method

The paper introduces a FAN-guided regularizer that adds a KL divergence between the policy distribution and a target Gaussian N(μ(s), Σ(s)) to both SFT and RFT objectives. In SFT, the covariance is dynamic—computed from the policy's own per-dimension variance—so the target shape adapts during training; in RFT (FAN-PPO), a fixed isotropic covariance σ²I is used to provide a stable anchor for policy updates. Proposition 1 shows the optimal policy under the constrained objective is a geometric interpolation between the previous policy and the target Gaussian, re-weighted by exponentiated Q-values, with interpolation weights governed by the regularization coefficient α and the trust-region size ε. This transforms the learning signal from 'exclusive correctness' to 'weighted tolerance,' guiding the policy from a spiky overconfident distribution toward a smooth, unimodal neighborhood that reflects the true action tolerance of physical manipulation.

## Results

On ManiSkill RFT, FAN-PPO achieves 97.4% in-distribution success and 88.1% average OOD success with OpenVLA, improving over PPO by +1.5% and +6.2% respectively. For OpenVLA-OFT, FAN-PPO yields 97.3% in-distribution and 71.2% average OOD, a +5.0% and +7.9% gain over PPO. In SFT on ManiSkill, FAN-SFT reaches 89.8% in-distribution (vs. 78.1% for vanilla SFT, +11.7%) and 63.3% average OOD (vs. 58.1%, +5.2%). FAN-PPO also dramatically improves sample efficiency, reaching 90% success rate in roughly one-third the training steps of baseline PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the OpenVLA + PPO baseline [22, 39], FAN-PPO wins on every OOD metric: +4.9% on Vision, +7.0% on Semantic, and +6.9% on Execution perturbations, with the largest absolute gain on the Execution OOD setting. Compared to RL4VLA [31], which is the strongest prior RL-for-VLA method, FAN-PPO surpasses it by +3.6% in-distribution and +12.2% average OOD (88.1% vs. 75.9%), indicating that the FAN regularizer provides a more effective generalization mechanism than pure reward-driven exploration. For the OpenVLA-OFT variant, the advantage is even more pronounced in OOD settings (+9.6% Semantic, +11.1% Execution), suggesting the regularizer is particularly beneficial when the action-chunking architecture amplifies distributional sharpness. The paper also compares against label smoothing [43] as an unstructured regularizer and reports that FAN, as a structured Gaussian prior, is consistently more effective (Appendix Table 12). In real-world evaluation on a JAKA robot, FAN-SFT improves success from 1/30 to 7/30 on the most challenging spatial-perturbation task (Task-4), a 7× relative gain over vanilla OpenVLA SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PPO/GRPO trust-region policy optimization framework by adding a structured KL-divergence-to-Gaussian regularizer that explicitly encodes the physical tolerance of manipulation actions. It departs from the standard language-model-style training inherited by VLA models (one-hot cross-entropy for SFT, unstructured PPO for RFT) by introducing the Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN) as a geometric prior on the policy distribution. The approach is conceptually distinct from entropy maximization in RL, which encourages broad but unstructured distributions, whereas FAN imposes a locally unimodal, smooth shape anchored at the policy's own predicted optimum. The method is architecture-agnostic and applies to both autoregressive single-action (OpenVLA) and chunked-action (OpenVLA-OFT) VLA backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FAN formalization provides a principled lens for understanding why standard VLA finetuning overfits to narrow action distributions and fails to generalize, and the Gaussian regularizer offers a simple, drop-in fix that improves both sample efficiency and OOD robustness across SFT and RFT. Limitations include the assumption that FAN geometry is well-approximated by a single Gaussian (non-convex or multi-modal feasible regions may not be captured), the fixed σ hyperparameter in RFT requiring manual tuning per backbone, and real-world validation on only one 7-DoF platform with four simple pick-and-place tasks. Open questions include whether the FAN size should be state-dependent or learned, whether the approach extends to high-DoF or dexterous manipulation with more complex action geometries, and how it interacts with continuous-action VLA formulations (e.g., flow-matching in π₀).
