## Problem

Existing transferable black-box targeted attacks on LVLMs rely on point-wise cosine similarity in high-dimensional feature spaces, producing perturbations that are noisy, redundant, or overfitted to surrogate-specific directions, limiting cross-architecture generalization. These methods also lack structured semantic guidance to steer perturbations toward coherent target regions while repelling source semantics, resulting in poor transferability to proprietary models like GPT-4o, Claude, and Gemini.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263892/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 299×299 clean image, a target ImageNet-1K class label (different from the source), and two reference sets of 50 images each (positives matching the target semantics, negatives matching the source semantics) drawn from ImageNet-1K validation. It produces an L∞-bounded perturbation (ε=16/255) added to the clean image, optimized jointly across 8 heterogeneous CLIP-based surrogate encoders (ViT/ConvNeXt, 224–448px, CLIP/SigLIP/MetaCLIP/DataComp). Success is evaluated via LLM-as-a-Judge (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct) with position-balanced, deterministic decoding to determine whether the generated caption aligns more with the target than the original semantics.

## Method

VCP-Attack replaces single-reference cosine alignment with a multi-sample contrastive objective computed within a dynamically updated PCA subspace (top-d=10 components, refreshed every 10 iterations). The positive loss combines a top-k softmax-weighted term (k=10) that adaptively emphasizes the most semantically aligned references with a max-based anchor term (α=0.8 weighting), while the negative loss targets only the top-m=10 hardest negatives to concentrate repulsive gradients on semantically confusing directions. A margin constraint (γ=0.2) explicitly enforces separation between the best positive and hardest negative in raw cosine space. All contrastive terms are computed per surrogate in its PCA subspace, with gradients projected back to the full feature space, and the shared perturbation is optimized via MI-PGD with late-stage EMA smoothing across the 8-surrogate ensemble.

## Results

Under a fixed perturbation budget of ε=16/255, VCP-Attack achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) of 94.2% across seven open-source LVLMs (InternVL3-14B/38B/78B, Ovis-16B, Qwen2.5VL-7B/32B/72B) and 83.1% across three proprietary models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini-2.5-Flash). The method reaches 95.6% ASR on GPT-4o and 97.3% on Ovis-16B, with the largest single-model gap of 40.8 percentage points over the strongest baseline on InternVL3-78B. Ablations confirm that PCA subspace dimension d=10, positive loss weighting α=0.8, and top-k/top-m sampling of (10,10) are optimal configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCP-Attack outperforms all five baselines on every evaluated model in both open-source and closed-source settings. The strongest competitors are M-Attack and FOA-Attack, which use local cropping/alignment and closed-form feature alignment respectively; VCP-Attack surpasses M-Attack by 22.4 percentage points on average (94.2% vs. 71.8%) on open-source models and by 16.8 points (83.1% vs. 66.3%) on proprietary models. The gap is most dramatic on larger models: on InternVL3-78B, VCP-Attack achieves 94.4% while M-Attack drops to 55.6% and FOA-Attack to 53.6%, a 38.8-point margin. On Claude-3.7-Sonnet, where all baselines struggle (M-Attack 38.8%, FOA-Attack 37.0%), VCP-Attack still reaches 61.1%, a 22.3-point advantage over the next-best method. The weaker baselines (AttackVLM, AdvDiffVLM, AnyAttack) remain far behind, averaging below 25% ASR on open-source models, confirming that structured contrastive supervision with PCA subspace projection is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the transfer-based black-box attack paradigm for LVLMs established by AttackVLM (2023) and extended by CWA, AdvDiffVLM, AnyAttack, M-Attack, and FOA-Attack, all of which optimize perturbations via point-wise cosine similarity or feature alignment against surrogate encoders. VCP-Attack departs from this lineage in two ways: it repurposes multi-sample contrastive supervision (top-k positive / top-m hard-negative) from the adversarial-robustness literature (RoCL, ADVCL, ACL) onto the offensive side of perturbation optimization, and it constrains all contrastive computation within a dynamically updated PCA subspace to filter surrogate-specific noise. The combination of structured semantic contrast with linear subspace projection is novel in the attack setting, though each component draws on well-understood ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results expose a concrete and severe vulnerability in production-grade proprietary LVLMs (95.6% ASR on GPT-4o, 92.6% on Gemini-2.5), reinforcing the urgency of adversarial defenses for deployed multimodal systems. A notable finding is that VCP-Attack maintains >92% ASR across InternVL3 model scales (14B→78B) where M-Attack and FOA-Attack degrade by ~30 points, suggesting that subspace-constrained contrastive optimization is more robust to architectural scaling than point-wise alignment. However, evaluation is limited to image captioning on a single 1,000-image benchmark, and generalization to VQA, multimodal reasoning, or other prompt structures remains untested. The PCA dimension sweet-spot (d=10) and the finding that a simple linear projection outperforms full-space optimization are practical takeaways, but whether they hold for non-CLIP encoders or different task distributions is an open question.
