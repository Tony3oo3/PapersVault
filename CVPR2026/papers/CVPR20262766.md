## Problem

Learning-based SfM methods like ACE-Zero estimate camera poses and scene coordinates from unordered images without ground truth supervision, but their purely local neural refinement lacks global and multi-view consistency constraints, causing pose drift and misalignment in complex or ambiguous scenes. Integrating pose graph optimization into such learning-based frameworks is non-trivial because they do not produce explicit pairwise pose constraints or uncertainty estimates, making naive optimization prone to over-fitting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unordered collection of RGB images with no pose labels, depth annotations, or sequential ordering. Initialization uses ZoeDepth to predict depth maps for seed images, after which the pipeline operates entirely on RGB. The output is a set of globally consistent camera poses (SE(3) transformations) and dense per-pixel scene coordinates in a shared 3D frame, refined iteratively until convergence (99% registration or <1% new registrations per iteration).

## Method

The framework constructs pose graphs directly from ACE-Zero's predicted scene coordinates by projecting 3D points between image pairs, verifying geometric consistency via a threshold on 3D distance, and estimating relative poses with RANSAC+PnP on the verified correspondences. To handle noisy predictions, it introduces two unsupervised uncertainty metrics: an epipolar-based score measuring the perpendicular distance from a projected point to its epipolar line, and an optical-flow-based score (using RAFT) measuring the discrepancy between the projected point and the flow-predicted correspondence. These scores are combined into per-edge confidence values that define diagonal information matrices, allowing the optimizer to down-weight unreliable constraints. PGO is applied at every ACE-Zero iteration (not just once at the end), progressively enforcing global consistency as scene coordinate predictions improve, with the geometric consistency threshold τ annealed from 0.2 m to 0.05 m over training.

## Results

On 7-Scenes, the full method achieves 21.7 dB average PSNR via novel view synthesis, matching COLMAP (default) at 21.2 dB while running in 30 minutes versus 38 hours. On Mip-NeRF 360, it reaches 24.3 dB, approaching COLMAP's 24.7 dB and substantially outperforming ACE-Zero (22.9 dB) and VGGT-SLAM (14.3 dB). On Tanks and Temples (Advanced split), the method achieves 16.8 dB, surpassing ACE-Zero (14.5 dB) and VGGT-SLAM (13.6 dB) while remaining competitive with COLMAP (17.3 dB). The method also reduces total reconstruction time by roughly half compared to ACE-Zero alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ACE-Zero (the primary baseline the method builds upon), the full pipeline consistently improves PSNR: 21.7 vs. 21.2 dB on 7-Scenes, 24.3 vs. 22.9 dB on Mip-NeRF 360, and 16.8 vs. 14.5 dB on the Tanks and Temples Advanced split, while also halving reconstruction time. Against COLMAP (default), the method matches or slightly exceeds on 7-Scenes (21.7 vs. 21.2) and is within 0.4 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (24.3 vs. 24.7), but trails by 0.5 dB on T&T Advanced (16.8 vs. 17.3); however, it runs 76× faster than COLMAP default on 7-Scenes (30 min vs. 38 h). Against VGGT-SLAM, the method achieves a large margin of 10 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (24.3 vs. 14.3) and 3.2 dB on T&T Advanced (16.8 vs. 13.6), as VGGT-SLAM struggles with the large inter-frame differences in unordered collections. Against DROID-SLAM on the long Tanks and Temples sequences, the method leads by 2.9 dB (19.7 vs. 16.8) while being comparable in runtime (1 h vs. 2.2 h). The ablation further shows that vanilla PGO without proper graph construction collapses to 12.6 dB, confirming that the geometric consistency threshold and uncertainty weighting are essential for the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ACE-Zero, a self-supervised scene coordinate regression framework that estimates camera poses from unordered images without ground truth supervision. The key departure is the integration of pose graph optimization (PGO), a well-established technique from SLAM (ORB-SLAM, GTSAM pipelines), into ACE-Zero's purely local refinement loop to enforce global multi-view consistency. The paper also draws on uncertainty-aware optimization ideas from object pose estimation (UAPose, UnPose) and adapts them to the SfM setting by deriving confidence scores from geometric priors (epipolar geometry and RAFT optical flow) rather than learned uncertainty. Conceptually, it follows the hybrid learning-plus-optimization paradigm seen in HERO-SLAM and DeepSFM, but is the first to construct pose graphs directly from neural scene coordinate predictions without explicit pairwise constraints or co-visibility graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that global geometric reasoning (PGO) can effectively complement and correct the local, drift-prone refinements of learning-based SfM, achieving COLMAP-competitive accuracy at roughly half the runtime. This validates a broader principle that neural inference and explicit geometric optimization are complementary rather than competing paradigms. However, several limitations remain: uncertainty estimation relies on heuristic geometric metrics that may not generalize to highly dynamic or textureless scenes, hyperparameters require manual tuning, and the method does not jointly optimize scene structure (leaving room for full bundle adjustment). An open question is whether the uncertainty weighting can be learned end-to-end rather than derived from hand-crafted priors, and whether the approach scales to very large-scale outdoor reconstructions beyond the tested benchmarks.
