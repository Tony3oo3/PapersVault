## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods operate at a single temporal scale, simultaneously modeling semantic alignment and fine-grained motion details, which limits long-term coherence and precise text alignment. The paper observes that humans conceptualize motions hierarchically—first constructing coarse key poses, then refining with dynamic transitions—yet no prior method exploits this coarse-to-fine structure. Additionally, flow matching, a powerful generative paradigm, remains largely underexplored for 3D human motion generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text description as input, encoded at word-level by CLIP (not just a single sentence embedding), and produces a 3D human pose sequence of length L where each frame encodes root angular velocity, root linear velocities on the XZ plane, root height, and local joint positions, rotations, and velocities relative to the root space. Generation occurs in a compact latent space (temporal length L/4, 6 latent joints) produced by a topology-aware Motion VAE, and the final latent is decoded back to full-resolution motion. The hierarchical pipeline operates at three temporal scales {1/3, 2/3, 1}, progressively refining from coarse to fine.

## Method

MotionHiFlow generates motion via K stages of flow matching at increasing temporal scales, linked by a novel cross-scale transition (denoise by extrapolation → upsample → renoise by interpolation) that preserves noise consistency across stages, avoiding the noise inconsistency of naive upsampling. The generative backbone is TMDiT, a dual-stream diffusion transformer where motion and text features are processed in separate streams with shared attention and parameter sharing in the final layers, modulated by timestep, sentence-level embedding, and scale via AdaLN. A key architectural innovation is Joint RoPE, which extends rotary position encoding to encode temporal position, 2D spatial coordinates relative to the pelvis, and kinematic tree depth in a unified rotation framework, enforcing skeletal symmetry. The Motion VAE uses a two-stream GCN (adapted from 2sAGCN) for topology-aware encoding with temporal downsampling by 4 and spatial pooling to 6 latent joints. Training is two-stage: first the VAE is trained with reconstruction, KL, and an augmentation loss on downsampled latents; then TMDiT is trained with the hierarchical flow-matching loss using classifier-free guidance at 10% condition dropout.

## Results

On HumanML3D, MotionHiFlow achieves the lowest FID (0.032) and competitive R-Precision Top-1 (0.563), while SALAD leads on R-Precision (0.581) and MultiModal Distance (2.649). On KIT-ML, MotionHiFlow attains the best R-Precision Top-1 (0.482), lowest FID (0.135), and lowest MultiModal Distance (2.552), outperforming the next-best method SALAD (FID 0.296, MM-Dist 2.585). The hierarchical 3-scale configuration [1/3, 2/3, 1] yields the best FID (0.032) and MM-Dist (2.691) among all scale ablations, while a 2-scale [1/2, 1] setup achieves the highest R@1 (0.565).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On HumanML3D, MotionHiFlow achieves the best FID (0.032, vs. 0.033 for MoGenTS and 0.076 for SALAD) but trails SALAD on R-Precision Top-1 (0.563 vs. 0.581) and MultiModal Distance (2.691 vs. 2.649). On KIT-ML, MotionHiFlow leads on R-Precision Top-1 (0.482 vs. 0.477 for SALAD), FID (0.135 vs. 0.296 for SALAD and 0.143 for MoGenTS), and MM-Dist (2.552 vs. 2.585 for SALAD), though SALAD retains a slight edge on Top-2/Top-3 R-Precision. Against the strongest prior flow/diffusion baselines (MoGenTS, ReMoDiffuse), MotionHiFlow improves FID by 3–4× on both datasets. The user study confirms MotionHiFlow is preferred over MoMask and MoGenTS in both realism and text alignment, with a 47% chance of surpassing ground truth in text alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionHiFlow extends the flow matching paradigm (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) from single-scale to hierarchical multi-scale generation for text-to-motion, drawing architectural inspiration from the dual-stream MMDiT/Flux design (Esser et al., Black Forest Labs) and adapting RoPE (Su et al.) into a joint-aware variant that encodes temporal, spatial, and kinematic-tree structure. It departs from prior hierarchical generative approaches (e.g., PixelFlow, Pyramidal Flow Matching) that directly upsample noisy lower-scale data, instead introducing a denoise–upsample–renoise cross-scale transition that preserves noise consistency across stages. The topology-aware Motion VAE builds on 2sAGCN's graph convolutional architecture, repurposing it for motion tokenization rather than action recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical coarse-to-fine formulation addresses a recognized limitation of single-scale text-to-motion models: the tension between global semantic alignment and fine-grained temporal detail. The empirical observation that R-precision remains stable even at 0.2× downsampling provides a principled justification for the multi-stage design and suggests that overemphasizing fine details can hinder semantic learning. Open questions include whether the three-stage pipeline's inference cost (multiple ODE solves plus cross-scale transitions) is acceptable for real-time applications, how the approach generalizes to longer or multi-person motions, and whether the specific scale schedule {1/3, 2/3, 1} is optimal or dataset-dependent.
