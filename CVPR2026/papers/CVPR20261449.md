## Problem

Existing performance-estimation methods under distribution shift rely exclusively on the outputs of the model being evaluated, whose confidence and agreement signals become unreliable once the input distribution changes. This paper addresses the lack of an external, independent reference that can provide a more faithful surrogate for ground-truth labels without requiring target annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261449/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target-domain images, a source-trained base model (e.g., DenseNet-121 or ResNet-50), a frozen vision-language foundation model (CLIP ViT-B/32 or SigLIP ViT-B/16), and a small labeled source validation set. It produces a single scalar estimated error rate: per-sample inner products between base-model predictions and the fused reference are thresholded at a δ calibrated on the validation set, and the fraction of samples falling below δ gives the predicted error. No target labels are used at any point during estimation.

## Method

FRAP constructs a fused reference distribution in three steps. First, a temperature parameter τ is learned at test time by minimizing the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the foundation model's temperature-scaled softmax and the base model's predictions over the unlabeled test set, aligning their confidence scales without any labeled target data. Second, the calibrated foundation-model distribution and the base-model distribution are combined via confidence-weighted averaging, where each model's weight is its normalized max-probability, yielding a fused distribution that integrates cross-domain generalization with domain-specific expertise. Third, the estimation score for each sample is the inner product between the base model's prediction and the fused reference, and a threshold δ—set so that the fraction of validation samples scoring below δ matches the known source error rate—converts these scores into a binary correctness decision whose aggregate yields the estimated target error. The key design choice is that the JS-divergence calibration objective is not a standard calibration loss but an alignment objective that preserves compatible confidence scales for the downstream fusion, which the authors show outperforms fixed-temperature scaling even when the latter achieves lower ECE.

## Results

FRAP with CLIP as the foundation model achieves the lowest average Mean Absolute Estimation Error (MAE) of 6.53% across 18 dataset-architecture configurations, outperforming the next-best baseline COTT (6.72%) and COT (7.65%). FRAP with SigLIP achieves 7.32% average MAE, remaining competitive with specialized baselines like COT and ATC. FRAP attains the best individual result on 6 out of 18 datasets, with particularly strong gains on CIFAR-10 natural shift (2.14% vs. 7.04% for GDE) and CIFAR-100 natural shift (4.00% vs. 11.72% for GDE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRAP (CLIP) achieves the best average MAE (6.53%), narrowly beating COTT (6.72%) and COT (7.65%), which are the two strongest baselines based on optimal transport. Against confidence-based methods, FRAP substantially outperforms ATC-NE (8.48%) and ATC-MC (8.45%), and decisively beats ProjNorm (10.46%), AC (12.30%), DoC (12.70%), IM (13.76%), and GDE (14.87%). On individual datasets, FRAP (CLIP) wins on 6 of 18 settings, with the largest margins on CIFAR-10/100 natural and synthetic shifts (e.g., 2.14% vs. 7.04% for GDE on C10-N). However, COTT retains the edge on several ImageNet and BREEDS subpopulation-shift settings (e.g., IN-S: 2.14% vs. 4.76%; E30-S: 2.30% vs. 6.94%), indicating that FRAP's advantage is most pronounced when the base model's domain expertise is well-aligned with the target distribution. The paper also notes that COT/COTT scale quadratically or cubically with the number of classes due to optimal transport solvers, whereas FRAP scales linearly, making it more efficient for high-cardinality label spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRAP departs from the dominant paradigm in performance estimation under distribution shift, where all prior methods (agreement-based, confidence-statistics, optimal-transport, projection-norm) derive their estimation signal exclusively from the outputs of the model under evaluation. The key conceptual shift is introducing an external foundation model (CLIP or SigLIP) as an independent reference whose cross-domain generalization compensates for the base model's biased predictions under shift. The test-time calibration via Jensen-Shannon divergence minimization and the confidence-weighted fusion mechanism are novel in this context, though temperature scaling and JS divergence are well-known calibration tools repurposed here for cross-model alignment rather than single-model calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FRAP offers a practically attractive alternative to optimal-transport-based estimators (COT/COTT) that scale quadratically or cubically with class count, since foundation-model inference scales linearly. However, the average MAE improvement over the strongest baseline (COTT: 6.53% vs. 6.72%) is modest, and the method does not dominate on all 18 datasets, with effectiveness bounded by the foundation model's own generalization limits. Open questions include how to systematically select the optimal reference model, whether the framework extends to non-vision modalities, and how to design fusion and calibration objectives more tightly coupled to the downstream estimation goal rather than to calibration error.
