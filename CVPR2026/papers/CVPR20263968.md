## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D occupancy prediction has advanced rapidly for outdoor driving but transfers poorly to indoor scenes, where geometry is denser, layouts more intricate, and semantics far more fine-grained and long-tailed. Existing indoor occupancy methods are confined to small closed taxonomies (e.g., Occ-ScanNet's 11 classes), and early open-vocabulary attempts still rely on fixed base categories during training. The paper tackles the underexplored setting of indoor open-vocabulary occupancy under geometry-only supervision, where only binary voxel occupancy labels are available and no semantic voxel annotations are used.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 518 px on the longer side) and produces a 60×60×36 voxel grid spanning a 4.8 m × 4.8 m × 2.88 m local volume. Each occupied voxel carries a language-aligned embedding; at inference, arbitrary text queries are embedded via CLIP and matched to voxel embeddings by cosine similarity, yielding open-vocabulary semantic occupancy without any closed-set classification head. During training, geometry is supervised by binary occupancy ground truth (from SCFusion) and semantics by 2D features from the training-free open-vocabulary segmenter Trident, with no 2D human annotations required.

## Method

LegoOcc uses Language-Embedded Gaussians (LE-Gaussians) as a unified 3D intermediate representation, where each primitive couples geometric parameters (position, covariance, opacity) with a learnable language-aligned embedding. On the geometry side, the paper introduces an opacity-aware Poisson-based Gaussian-to-Occupancy operator: each Gaussian's effective opacity α_i·p_i(x) is treated as a nonnegative event intensity, and voxel occupancy is computed as 1 − exp(−Σ_i α_i p_i(x)), the probability of at least one event in a non-homogeneous Poisson process. This replaces GaussianFormer2's opacity-agnostic multiplicative aggregation and avoids the opacity-collapse observed with a naive Bernoulli union, yielding stable convergence under binary-only supervision. On the semantic side, a Progressive Temperature Decay schedule applies a tempered sigmoid α_i = σ(α_i^logit/τ) during Gaussian splatting, annealing τ exponentially from 1 to 10⁻³ over training; this gradually sharpens per-Gaussian opacities toward {0,1}, suppressing cross-category feature mixing along rays while preserving end-to-end differentiability. Training jointly minimizes a composite loss of focal + Lovász-Softmax occupancy terms, a scene-class affinity regularizer, a Huber depth loss, and a cosine feature-alignment loss against Trident's open-vocabulary segmentation features (extended to 5 nearby views for multi-view consistency), all optimized with Depth Anything V2 as the depth backbone.

## Results

On Occ-ScanNet under the open-vocabulary (geometry-only annotation) setting, LegoOcc achieves 59.50 IoU and 21.05 mIoU, surpassing the strongest open-vocabulary baselines POP-3D (35.32 IoU / 5.96 mIoU) and LOcc (36.70 IoU / 9.25 mIoU) by +23.18 IoU and +11.80 mIoU respectively. Notably, LegoOcc also attains the highest overall IoU (59.50) among all entries in Table 1, exceeding the best closed-vocabulary method RoboOcc (56.48 IoU) by 3.02 IoU despite using no semantic voxel labels during training. In mIoU, closed-vocabulary methods with full annotations still lead (RoboOcc 47.76, EmbodiedOcc++ 46.20), highlighting the remaining gap in fine-grained category calibration for open-vocabulary occupancy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the open-vocabulary (geometry-only) setting, LegoOcc decisively outperforms both prior open-vocabulary methods: POP-3D (35.32 IoU / 5.96 mIoU) and LOcc (36.70 IoU / 9.25 mIoU), gaining +23.18 IoU and +11.80 mIoU over LOcc and +24.18 IoU and +15.09 mIoU over POP-3D. Against the strongest closed-vocabulary baseline RoboOcc (56.48 IoU / 47.76 mIoU), LegoOcc wins on IoU by +3.02 but trails on mIoU by 26.71 points, reflecting the inherent difficulty of open-vocabulary category calibration without semantic voxel labels. Among closed-vocabulary methods, RoboOcc leads in both IoU and mIoU, followed closely by EmbodiedOcc++ (54.90 / 46.20) and EmbodiedOcc (53.55 / 45.15), while earlier methods like TPVFormer (33.39 / 24.94) and MonoScene (41.60 / 24.62) lag substantially. The re-implemented open-vocabulary baselines (POP-3D, LOcc) show near-zero per-class IoU on several categories (e.g., ceiling, window, television), indicating they fail to produce meaningful semantic predictions under geometry-only supervision, whereas LegoOcc maintains non-trivial scores across all 11 classes. In terms of efficiency, LegoOcc runs at 22.47 FPS with 99.04M parameters, roughly 2× faster than EmbodiedOcc (11.48 FPS / 231.45M) and 6× faster than ISO (3.81 FPS / 303.05M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Language-Embedded Gaussians (LE-Gaussians) representation [32, 59] from open-vocabulary scene understanding to monocular 3D occupancy prediction, and departs from the GaussianFormer2 [9] Gaussian-to-Occupancy operator by replacing its opacity-agnostic multiplicative aggregation with a Poisson-based formulation that remains stable under binary-only supervision. On the semantic side, it builds on Gaussian Splatting [15] rendering and training-free open-vocabulary segmentation (Trident [34]) but introduces a Progressive Temperature Decay schedule to address feature mixing that prior splatting-based semantic alignment (e.g., Dr. Splat [14]) handles only via discrete Top-k selection. The work also positions itself against outdoor open-vocabulary occupancy methods (POP-3D [40], LOcc [52]) by demonstrating that their driving-oriented pipelines transfer poorly to the denser, more fine-grained indoor setting, and against indoor closed-vocabulary occupancy (EmbodiedOcc [46], RoboOcc [56]) by showing that geometry-only supervision can match or exceed their IoU without any semantic voxel labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometry-only supervision paradigm is practically significant because binary occupancy can be harvested at scale from RGB-D sensors (via SCFusion) while semantic voxel annotations remain prohibitively expensive for the long-tailed indoor category space. The Poisson-based G2O and Progressive Temperature Decay are modular techniques that could transfer to other Gaussian-splatting-based occupancy or reconstruction pipelines. However, the mIoU gap between the best closed-vocabulary method (47.76) and this open-vocabulary result (21.05) remains large, suggesting that category calibration under open-ended semantics is still an open problem; the single-dataset evaluation (Occ-ScanNet) also limits confidence in generalizability to other indoor environments or outdoor scenes.
