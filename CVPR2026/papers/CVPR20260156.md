## Problem

Text-only conditioning for 3D human-object interaction (HOI) motion generation lacks the geometric and spatial priors—object shape, scale, contact regions—that are essential for physically plausible synthesis. This forces models into a one-to-many mapping that degrades realism and severely limits generalization to unseen objects. Existing remedies (LLM-based semantic enrichment, explicit contact/kinematic constraints) either remain purely linguistic or focus on localized interaction regions, neglecting global full-body coherence.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a natural-language HOI description plus a static rendered image of the human and object in a neutral pose; a text-to-image model (Nano Banana) synthesizes three temporally coherent reference images from these, which are then fed alongside the text prompt into a frozen Qwen2.5-VL. The output is a full-body 3D HOI motion sequence encoded as SMPL-X body parameters concatenated with 6D object pose (position + rotation) over L timesteps. During training, the three reference images are instead rendered directly from ground-truth motion at the start, middle, and end frames of the contact phase, guaranteeing strict semantic alignment without curated image-motion pairs.

## Method

ViHOI introduces a VLM-based prior extractor that uses Qwen2.5-VL to jointly process three reference images and a structured prompt, then applies a layer-decoupled strategy: visual priors are read from the 3rd LLM layer (preserving geometric/spatial detail) while textual priors are read from the 12th layer (capturing semantic content of the HOI description). Two Q-Former-based adapters, each with learnable queries and two cross-attention layers, distill these high-dimensional, variable-length embeddings into single compact prior tokens. These tokens condition a diffusion-based HOI generator (DiT) via self-attention at every denoising step, guiding reconstruction of the clean motion from noise. The framework is trained with the VLM frozen and the adapters plus generator jointly optimized, and it operates as a plug-and-play module that can be attached to existing HOI generators (CHOIS, ROG, MDM) without modifying their core architecture.

## Results

On the FullBodyManipulation dataset, CHOIS+ViHOI achieves the best overall performance with R-Precision (Top-1) of 0.41, FID of 0.68, Foot Sliding of 0.26, Contact F1 of 0.75, and MPJPE of 14.97 cm. On the unseen-object split, CHOIS+ViHOI further reduces FID to 2.02 (vs. 4.99 for CHOIS alone) and MPJPE to 14.58 cm, demonstrating strong generalization. On BEHAVE, CHOIS+ViHOI attains FID 3.90 and MPJPE 17.09 cm, outperforming all baselines on interaction quality while maintaining competitive diversity (9.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHOIS+ViHOI outperforms the standalone CHOIS baseline on nearly every metric: R-Precision Top-1 improves from 0.37 to 0.41, FID drops from 0.77 to 0.68, Foot Sliding decreases from 0.36 to 0.26, and MPJPE improves from 15.43 to 14.97 cm. Against SemGeoMo (which uses LLM-generated fine-grained text plus an affordance map), CHOIS+ViHOI wins on R-Precision (0.41 vs. 0.36), FID (0.68 vs. 0.79), FS (0.26 vs. 0.36), and MPJPE (14.97 vs. 16.34), though SemGeoMo retains a slight edge on Contact Percentage (0.66 vs. 0.64). When applied to ROG, ViHOI substantially reduces FID (1.54 → 0.68) and improves R-Precision Top-1 (0.33 → 0.39), but the absolute contact metrics remain lower than CHOIS-based results, reflecting ROG's weaker base model. For MDM, ViHOI improves R-Precision (0.31 → 0.36) and FID (1.56 → 1.18) but slightly reduces Contact Precision (0.69 → 0.67), indicating that the visual prior helps semantic alignment more than fine contact geometry on this weaker backbone. On the unseen-object split, CHOIS+ViHOI achieves FID 2.02 and MPJPE 14.58 cm versus CHOIS's 4.99 and 15.42 cm, confirming that the text-to-image-synthesized reference images provide a meaningful generalization advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in HOI motion generation—semantic enhancement via LLM-enriched text and explicit physical constraints (contact points, kinematic chains, affordance maps)—by introducing 2D reference images as a third, complementary source of interaction priors. Architecturally, it builds on the Q-Former compression mechanism from BLIP-2 to distill high-dimensional VLM embeddings into compact conditioning tokens, and on the layer-decoupling insight from VLM interpretability work that early layers retain visual detail while deeper layers encode semantics. The plug-and-play design, demonstrated on MDM, ROG, and CHOIS, positions ViHOI as an orthogonal enhancement module rather than a standalone generator, distinguishing it from prior methods that require architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a drop-in module that improves contact quality, reduces foot sliding, and—most notably—enables generalization to unseen objects by leveraging the world knowledge embedded in a text-to-image model at inference time. The training/inference asymmetry (clean rendered images during training, photorealistic synthesized images at inference) working despite the style gap suggests the VLM extracts robust, style-invariant motion-relevant features, an observation that could inform other vision-conditioned generation tasks. A stated limitation is the absence of fine-grained hand annotations, which prevents detailed finger-level motion; extending the framework to dexterous manipulation (e.g., ARCTIC-style data) is a natural next step. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object or multi-person interactions and whether the three-keyframe protocol is optimal for longer or more complex interaction sequences.
