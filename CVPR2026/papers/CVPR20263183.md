## Problem

Existing facial texture reconstruction methods depend on accurate 3D geometry priors (e.g., 3DMM fitting), which fail under occlusions or in stylized domains where geometry is hard to estimate. Additionally, these methods produce monolithic textures without semantic disentanglement, preventing region-specific editing and style transfer needed for gaming and VR avatars.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263183/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB facial image (realistic or stylized, with or without occlusions such as hands, glasses, or masks) and directly produces a topologically consistent facial UV texture map in the canonical FLAME layout, without any intermediate geometry fitting step. The output texture is editable: specific semantic regions (skin tone, mouth, eyebrows) can be independently modified by manipulating features at designated transformer block layers.

## Method

OMGTex builds on FLUX.1.DEV (a DiT-based diffusion model) with a LoRA condition module trained via flow-matching loss to map a 2D face image directly to a UV texture. The key technical contribution is a gradient-guided refinement strategy at inference time: a lightweight landmark detector predicts facial landmarks on the generated texture, and an iterative gradient step (inspired by classifier guidance) corrects UV misalignment by minimizing landmark deviation from canonical positions. This is motivated by a frequency analysis showing that low-frequency structural components in diffusion are insensitive to ControlNet-style conditioning, making direct gradient guidance more effective. For semantic editing, the authors exploit the observation that double-stream blocks encode visual style while single-stream blocks encode identity, and design a layer-wise ablation training strategy that assigns specific semantic regions (skin, mouth, eyebrows) to distinct groups of attention blocks, enabling localized feature fusion at inference.

## Results

On the stylized CANVAS dataset, OMGTex achieves PSNR 27.22, SSIM 0.77, LPIPS 0.26, and FID 43.55, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., FID 43.55 vs. 50.89 for Free-UV and 60.02 for AvatarTex). On the large-pose LPFF dataset, OMGTex leads with PSNR 28.92 and FID 36.78, surpassing Free-UV (PSNR 26.12, FID 45.91) and AvatarTex (PSNR 27.91, FID 38.93). On the realistic FFHQ dataset, OMGTex is the best inference-based method (PSNR 29.75, FID 28.12) but trails the optimization-based AvatarTex (PSNR 30.03, FID 22.15). Inference time is 7 seconds, roughly 3× faster than Free-UV (20 s), 13× faster than AvatarTex (90 s), and 51× faster than SOAP (360 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMGTex is compared against three baselines: AvatarTex (optimization-based, StyleGAN2), SOAP (multi-view diffusion projection), and Free-UV (inference-based, geometry-guided). On the stylized CANVAS benchmark, OMGTex dominates all three methods across every metric, with FID improvements of 7.3 over Free-UV, 16.5 over AvatarTex, and 25.1 over SOAP. On the large-pose LPFF dataset, OMGTex similarly leads all baselines, with a 9.1-point FID advantage over Free-UV. On the realistic FFHQ dataset, the optimization-based AvatarTex retains a slight edge (FID 22.15 vs. 28.12), but OMGTex is the strongest inference-based method and is 13× faster. All three baselines rely on 3DMM geometry fitting, which causes failures under occlusions and stylized inputs; OMGTex's geometry-free design avoids these failure modes entirely, as evidenced by its consistent performance where baselines degrade or fail (marked 'N/A' in qualitative comparisons).

## Relation to Prior Work

OMGTex builds on the FLUX.1 DiT backbone and the EasyControl conditional generation framework, training a LoRA module with flow-matching loss to map a single facial image directly to a UV texture. It departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in facial texture reconstruction (UV-IDM, FreeUV, SOAP, Ultr-Avatar), all of which rely on 3DMM geometry fitting to extract partial or full UV maps as structural priors. Instead of injecting structural conditions via ControlNet, the paper introduces a gradient-guided refinement strategy at inference time that corrects UV misalignment using a trained landmark detector, and it leverages the natural semantic distribution across DiT attention blocks to enable region-specific editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the geometry-fitting bottleneck, OMGTex removes a major failure mode (occlusions, stylized shapes, large poses) that has plagued prior methods, and the 7-second inference time makes it practical for interactive applications like gaming and VR. The CANVAS dataset fills a critical gap as the first paired multi-style facial texture resource, enabling both training and fair evaluation across realistic and stylized domains. Open questions include whether the semantic disentanglement across DiT blocks generalizes to other architectures or body parts, how the method scales to novel UV topologies beyond the FLAME template, and whether the gradient-guided refinement can be made differentiable for end-to-end training.
