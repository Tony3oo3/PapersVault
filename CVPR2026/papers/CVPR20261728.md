## Problem

Existing GraphRAG methods treat knowledge graphs as static, offline-built structures queried in a single pass, creating three bottlenecks: text-centric fragmentation that collapses multimodal evidence into isolated tuples, frozen structures that cannot incorporate new evidence or correct errors at inference time, and rigid single-pass retrieval without adaptive refinement. This static paradigm misaligns with the inherently iterative nature of knowledge-intensive reasoning, where humans collect evidence, identify gaps, seek additional information, and progressively refine understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes an image-question pair (I_q, q) or a text question along with a multimodal knowledge corpus of text-image document pairs, which is pre-converted into a unified cross-modal hypergraph with entity nodes and n-ary hyperedges. During inference, the agent iteratively observes the evolving hypergraph state and action history, and the final output is a text answer generated conditioned on the fully evolved graph G_T. Notably, the hypergraph itself is a persistent, mutable artifact that accumulates and refines evidence across steps, and the agent can invoke external web search to inject new knowledge beyond the static corpus.

## Method

EvoGraph-R1 formulates multimodal GraphRAG as a Markov Decision Process where the knowledge hypergraph serves as a dynamic environment state that co-evolves with the reasoning agent. The agent selects from four actions—GRAPHRETRIEVE (structured subgraph lookup), WEBSEARCH (external evidence acquisition), GRAPHEDIT (INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE to modify graph structure), and ANSWER (termination)—each inducing a state transition that reshapes the hypergraph. The initial hypergraph is built via n-ary textual relation extraction, visual scene-description anchoring (each image becomes an anchor node grounding all its extracted entities), and cross-modal entity resolution. The agent policy is trained end-to-end with GRPO using a trajectory-level reward that combines structural protocol compliance, token-overlap answer quality (gated on structural correctness), and a cost penalty on action count, encouraging efficient multi-hop reasoning over the evolving graph.

## Results

On multimodal benchmarks, EvoGraph-R1 achieves 43.6% accuracy on E-VQA, 42.3% on InfoSeek, and 68.6% on OK-VQA (average 51.50%), outperforming the strongest RL-augmented baseline MMSearch-R1 (46.03% avg) by +5.47 points. On text-only multi-hop QA, it reaches 68.5% F1 on 2WikiMultiHopQA, 65.4% on HotpotQA, and 56.8% on Natural Questions (average 63.57%), surpassing Graph-R1 (59.20% avg) by +4.37 points. The method also completes queries in fewer retrieval rounds (2.57 on 2Wiki, 1.65 on E-VQA) compared to ablated variants, indicating more efficient reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoGraph-R1 outperforms all 11 baselines across both multimodal and text-only settings. On E-VQA, it leads MMSearch-R1 by +6.7 points (43.6 vs 36.9) and MMKB-RAG by +7.7 points (43.6 vs 35.9); on OK-VQA it surpasses GPT-4o-mini (no retrieval) by +2.7 points (68.6 vs 65.9) and MMKB-RAG by +3.2 points. Among GraphRAG methods, the gap is largest: EvoGraph-R1 exceeds GraphRAG by +23.8 points on E-VQA and HippoRAG2 by +21.0 points, confirming that static graph retrieval is fundamentally limited for multimodal reasoning. On text-only benchmarks, the closest competitor is Graph-R1 (65.0/62.7/49.9 F1), which EvoGraph-R1 beats by +3.5, +2.7, and +6.9 points on 2Wiki, HotpotQA, and NQ respectively. The advantage over web-search-equipped methods (Search-R1, MMSearch-R1) is most pronounced on text-only tasks (+17.5 to +21.8 points), attributed to the persistent hypergraph enabling cumulative multi-hop reasoning rather than transient prompt-level retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoGraph-R1 extends Graph-R1's MDP formulation of agentic GraphRAG by introducing persistent graph-editing operations (INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE) that allow the hypergraph to be structurally modified during inference, rather than merely queried. It departs from static GraphRAG systems (GraphRAG, LightRAG, HyperGraphRAG) that treat knowledge graphs as frozen offline artifacts, and from web-search-augmented MLLMs (Search-R1, MMSearch-R1) that discard retrieved content after each step, by maintaining a single evolving hypergraph as the environment state across all reasoning turns. The multimodal extension—fusing visual scene descriptions as anchored hyperedges with textual n-ary relations—builds on RAG-Anything's cross-modal graph encoding but adds the critical dynamic evolution loop absent in that prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-evolving hypergraph paradigm offers a principled way to address the well-known brittleness of offline-constructed knowledge graphs in open-world, long-tail, and multimodal settings, and the low-resource experiments (gap widening from +7.7 to +13.2 points as corpus shrinks to 1%) suggest the approach is most valuable precisely where static retrieval fails. Open questions include scalability of the hypergraph to very large corpora, the stability and cost of GRPO training over multi-turn trajectories, and whether the RL-learned policy generalizes across domains without retraining. The framework's modality-agnostic design (text-only and multimodal share the same MDP) positions it as a potential unifying substrate for future agentic retrieval systems.
