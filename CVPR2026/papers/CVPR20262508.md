## Problem

Modern MLLMs process every pixel in every video frame equally, wasting computation on spatiotemporal redundancy and preventing scaling to long, high-resolution videos. Existing token-reduction methods prune tokens only inside the LLM while the ViT still encodes the full video, leaving the ViT as the dominant efficiency bottleneck. Additionally, no benchmark exists that jointly stresses both long duration and high resolution for video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262508/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AutoGaze takes a video of arbitrary resolution and duration plus a user-specified reconstruction loss threshold ε. It splits the video into 16×224×224 spatiotemporal tiles, processes each tile independently, and merges gazed positions back. The output is a per-frame sequence of multi-scale patch indices (the 'gazing') together with a predicted reconstruction loss at each decoding step; gazing for a frame stops automatically once the predicted loss drops below ε. The selected patches are then encoded by a modified ViT and fed to an MLLM as usual.

## Method

AutoGaze is a 3M-parameter model comprising a convolutional frame encoder and an autoregressive transformer decoder whose vocabulary consists of multi-scale patch indices rather than words. It interleaves frame encoding and patch decoding across the video, conditioning each frame's gazing on all previously encoded frames and previously selected patches to avoid redundancy. A dedicated head predicts the reconstruction loss after each patch selection, enabling automatic stopping at the minimal gazing length that satisfies the user threshold. Training proceeds in two stages: next-token-prediction pre-training on gazing sequences collected via greedy search over 250K videos, followed by RL post-training with a simplified GRPO algorithm that uses negative reconstruction loss as reward to discover better-than-greedy gazing policies. The downstream ViT is modified to accept multi-scale patch inputs (by interpolating positional embeddings per scale) and to process tokens from all 16 frames in a single sequence, while multi-token prediction (10 tokens per step) accelerates decoding with minimal quality loss.

## Results

NVILA-8B-Video with AutoGaze, scaled to 1024 frames and 3584 resolution, achieves 67.0% on VideoMME (w/o subtitles), 69.7% on MVBench, 61.0% on LongVideoBench, 66.9% on EgoSchema, 71.6% on MLVU, and 52.6% on the new HLVid benchmark. On HLVid, AutoGaze improves the base NVILA-8B-Video by 10.1% (42.5% → 52.6%), surpassing the previous best MLLM (VideoChat-Flash at 46.6%) by 6.0% and GPT-4o (49.3%) by 3.3%. In efficiency experiments (Table 2), AutoGaze reduces ViT latency from 2.20s to 0.55s (4×) and LLM latency from 1.42s to 0.10s (14.2×) at a 6.25% token selection ratio, while retaining comparable accuracy (52.3% V-MME vs. 53.4% no-reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, AutoGaze-equipped NVILA-8B-Video outperforms GPT-4o on MVBench (69.7 vs. 64.6), MLVU (71.6 vs. 64.6), and HLVid (52.6 vs. 49.3), but trails on VideoMME (67.0 vs. 71.9), L-VidBench (61.0 vs. 66.7), and EgoSchema (66.9 vs. 72.2). Versus Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, AutoGaze wins on VideoMME (67.0 vs. 65.0) and HLVid (52.6 vs. 52.2) but Gemini 1.5-Pro remains stronger on VideoMME (75.0) and EgoSchema (71.2). Among open-source MLLMs, AutoGaze surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B on VideoMME (67.0 vs. 65.1), L-VidBench (61.0 vs. 56.0), and HLVid (52.6 vs. 48.1, a 4.5% gap), and beats VideoChat-Flash on HLVid (52.6 vs. 46.6) and VideoMME (67.0 vs. 65.3). In the token-reduction comparison (Table 2), AutoGaze is the only method that reduces ViT latency (0.55s vs. ~2.2s for all baselines), since all other methods (ToMe, VisionZip, FastV, STORM, FastVID, LongVU, PruneVID, VChat-Flash) prune tokens only after the ViT, leaving ViT cost unchanged. On accuracy at 6.25% token ratio, AutoGaze (52.3% V-MME, 50.3% L-Vid) is within 1.1% of the no-reduction baseline (53.4%, 51.1%), comparable to the best token-pruning baselines like STORM (52.7%, 51.5%) and LongVU (52.2%, 50.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoGaze departs from the dominant token-reduction paradigm in video MLLMs (ToMe, STORM, FastVID, LongVU, VideoChat-Flash), which prune or pool tokens *after* the ViT has already processed every pixel, leaving the ViT as the computational bottleneck. Instead, AutoGaze performs selective patch attention *before* the ViT, using a 3M-parameter autoregressive model trained with next-token prediction on greedy-search gazing sequences and RL post-training (GRPO) with reconstruction loss as reward. The reconstruction objective is instantiated with a custom VideoMAE, and multi-token prediction accelerates decoding. This 'attend before attention' framing repositions efficiency gains from the LLM side to the vision encoder side, a fundamentally different architectural intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating up to 100× redundant patches before the ViT, AutoGaze unlocks practical processing of 1K-frame, 4K-resolution videos that were previously infeasible, with up to 19× ViT and 10× MLLM speedups. The introduction of HLVid—the first benchmark requiring both long-form (5-minute) and high-resolution (4K) video understanding—fills a clear gap, as prior long-video benchmarks (EgoSchema, LongVideoBench, MLVU) operate at low resolution. Open questions include whether the 0.7 reconstruction threshold generalizes optimally across diverse downstream tasks, how the method behaves with non-SigLIP2 ViTs or very short/low-FPS videos where redundancy is minimal, and whether the tile-based scaling strategy introduces artifacts at tile boundaries for very long videos.
