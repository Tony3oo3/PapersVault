## Problem

Existing diffusion-based text-to-motion generation methods rely on isotropic Gaussian latent priors and flat, single-stage cross-modal supervision, causing semantically distinct motions to occupy overlapping latent regions and degrading controllability and interpretability. Additionally, prior approaches treat text-motion alignment as a uniform process, neglecting the hierarchical correspondence between linguistic structure (word → sentence) and kinematic structure (joint → sequence), which leads to misrepresentation of complex textual instructions and poor global temporal coherence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt as input, encoded via a CLIP-ViT-B/32 text backbone into word-level and sentence-level embeddings. It produces a 3D human motion sequence of N frames, each represented as a skeletal pose vector over 22 joints (joint rotation and position), following the preprocessing conventions of SALAD and MDM. Generation uses 50-step DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance weight 7.5, and the latent space is organized into K=10 Gaussian components corresponding to semantically coherent motion submanifolds.

## Method

HESP introduces three coupled components within a diffusion-based generation framework. First, an Adaptive Gaussian VAE (AG-VAE) replaces the standard isotropic prior with a temporally adaptive Gaussian mixture prior conditioned on skeletal topology, where mixture weights π_k(t,S) are predicted by a lightweight attention network, allowing the latent distribution to evolve dynamically along the motion sequence and enabling per-timestep soft semantic assignments. Second, a Dynamic Cross-Modal Memory (DCMM) module maintains a retrieval-augmented memory bank of prototypical text-motion pairs, computing similarity-based attention to inject long-range contextual priors into the denoising process. Third, a Hierarchical Cross-Modal Attention (HCA) mechanism performs two-stage alignment—local attention between word embeddings and per-joint motion tokens, and global attention between sentence embeddings and the full trajectory—combined via a learnable gate λ. An adaptive gated fusion layer then balances motion-guided and text-guided information per semantic token before a SkipTransformer produces the diffusion velocity prediction.

## Results

On HumanML3D, HESP achieves the best R-Precision (Top-1: 0.600, Top-3: 0.871) and lowest MM-Dist (2.521) among all diffusion-based methods, tying MoMask on FID (0.045) while outperforming SALAD (FID 0.076, MM-Dist 2.649). On KIT-ML, HESP again leads in R-Precision (Top-1: 0.514, Top-3: 0.844) and MM-Dist (2.499), though MoMask achieves a slightly lower FID (0.204 vs. 0.267). HESP substantially outperforms MDM on both datasets (e.g., FID 0.045 vs. 0.544 on HumanML3D; R-Precision Top-1 0.600 vs. 0.320). The AG-VAE latent space also shows a 4.1× higher Calinski-Harabasz index (48.81 vs. 11.95) and 31% lower Davies-Bouldin index (5.39 vs. 7.83) compared to the standard VAE, confirming superior semantic disentanglement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HESP outperforms the strongest baseline SALAD [16] on all R-Precision metrics and MM-Dist on both datasets (e.g., Top-1 R-Precision 0.600 vs. 0.581 on HumanML3D; 0.514 vs. 0.477 on KIT-ML), and also achieves a lower FID on HumanML3D (0.045 vs. 0.076). Against MoMask [13], HESP wins decisively on R-Precision and MM-Dist but ties on FID for HumanML3D (both 0.045) and trails slightly on KIT-ML FID (0.267 vs. 0.204). HESP substantially surpasses MDM [38] across every metric on both datasets (e.g., FID 0.045 vs. 0.544 on HumanML3D; R-Precision Top-1 0.600 vs. 0.320). Compared to earlier non-diffusion baselines (T2M [11], AttT2M [50], ParCo [51]), HESP leads on FID and MM-Dist while matching or exceeding their R-Precision. Among diffusion-based methods, HESP also beats MLD [6], MotionDiffuse [46], and ReMoDiffuse [45] on R-Precision and MM-Dist, though ReMoDiffuse achieves a marginally lower FID on KIT-ML (0.155 vs. 0.267). Overall, HESP's advantage is most pronounced in semantic alignment (R-Precision, MM-Dist) and latent-space structure, while its FID is competitive but not always the absolute best on KIT-ML.

## Relation to Prior Work

HESP extends the Gaussian mixture VAE lineage (GMVAE, Dilokthanakul et al. 2016) by making the mixture prior temporally adaptive and conditioned on skeletal topology, moving beyond static component assignments. It directly departs from SALAD (Hong et al., CVPR 2025), which assumes an isotropic Gaussian latent prior and flat cross-attention, by replacing both with a structure-aware mixture prior and a two-level (word-joint / sentence-sequence) hierarchical attention. The Dynamic Cross-Modal Memory module draws on retrieval-augmented generation ideas (cf. ReMoDiffuse) but applies them to a prototypical text-motion pair bank rather than a nearest-neighbor motion store, and the gated fusion mechanism is a standard cross-modal blending technique repurposed for the denoising loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured latent space offers a practical path toward interpretable and controllable motion generation, which is valuable for animation pipelines and virtual-human interaction where users need to inspect or steer intermediate representations. However, the paper itself acknowledges a diversity trade-off: the semantically partitioned latent space slightly constrains stochastic variation, and abrupt transitions between distinct motion categories remain unresolved. Open questions include whether the temporally adaptive mixture prior generalizes to multi-person or long-horizon sequences, and whether the hierarchical attention design transfers to other cross-modal generation tasks beyond human motion.
