## Problem

Existing 3D pose transfer methods are largely restricted to characters with similar skeletal structures (e.g., humanoid-to-humanoid) and rely on rigid one-to-one keypoint correspondences, making them fail to generalize to category-free settings such as transferring a humanoid pose to a quadruped or bird. The structural and transformation diversity across distinct character types—varying numbers of joints, different topologies, and distinct rotation patterns—leads to mismatched regions and severe transfer artifacts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source character in canonical pose, its posed instance (with pose parameters), and a target character in canonical pose as input. Keypoints and per-vertex skin weights are estimated by a pretrained rigging model, with the number of keypoints K varying freely across characters (11 to 241 joints in the dataset). The output is a set of per-keypoint transformations (rotation quaternion + translation vector) for the target, which are applied via linear blend skinning to deform the canonical target mesh into the final posed mesh; a test-time ARAP refinement step further smooths the result.

## Method

MimiCAT is a two-stage cascade-transformer: a correspondence transformer G first learns shape-aware soft many-to-many correspondences between variable-length keypoint sets by encoding spatial coordinates and pretrained shape features (Michelangelo) into transformer tokens, then computing a doubly-stochastic matching matrix via the Sinkhorn algorithm. A pose transfer transformer H then refines initial target transformations—obtained by aggregating source rotations via Frobenius-norm-minimizing attitude-matrix averaging weighted by the soft correspondence—using cross-attention to inject residual source deformation cues into shape-conditioned tokens. Training is two-stage: G is supervised with text-guided correspondences (CLIP-encoded keypoint names aligned via the Hungarian algorithm plus a soft Sinkhorn target), while H is trained self-supervisedly with a cycle-consistency reconstruction loss and a matrix-Fisher-distribution pose prior that regularizes rotations toward physically plausible patterns learned from the million-scale PokeAnimDB dataset.

## Results

On the humanoid-to-humanoid (H2H) benchmark, MimiCAT achieves the best PMD of 3.570 (×100) and ELS of 0.923, outperforming the closest competitor SFPT (PMD 3.616, ELS 0.888). On the cross-category transfer (CCT) benchmark, MimiCAT attains PMD 4.264 and ELS 0.927, again leading all baselines. The gap over prior methods is most pronounced in the CCT setting, where NPT degrades severely (PMD 9.889, ELS 0.260) while MimiCAT maintains near-parity with its H2H performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MimiCAT outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both evaluation settings. Against SFPT [30], the closest competitor, MimiCAT improves H2H PMD from 3.616 to 3.570 and ELS from 0.888 to 0.923, and CCT PMD from 4.312 to 4.264 with ELS from 0.913 to 0.927. TapMo [76] is competitive on CCT ELS (0.922 vs. 0.927) but lags on H2H PMD (5.078 vs. 3.570) due to its fixed handle-point design. CGT [9] and NPT [59], both designed for same-topology transfer, degrade substantially in the cross-category setting: NPT's CCT ELS drops to 0.260 (vs. 0.927 for MimiCAT), and CGT's CCT PMD nearly doubles relative to MimiCAT (6.314 vs. 4.264). The paper attributes these gaps to the baselines' reliance on one-to-one correspondences and narrow-category training data, whereas MimiCAT's soft many-to-many keypoint matching and shape-conditioned generation generalize across structurally diverse characters.

## Relation to Prior Work

MimiCAT extends the keypoint-plus-LBS formulation for pose transfer established by SFPT [30] and the matrix-Fisher pose prior from Mohlin et al. [39], but departs fundamentally from the one-to-one correspondence assumption that constrains all prior methods (NPT, CGT, SFPT, TapMo) to structurally similar characters. The key conceptual shift is replacing rigid point-to-point mappings with many-to-many soft correspondences computed via Sinkhorn optimal transport, supervised by CLIP-encoded semantic keypoint names rather than handcrafted or geometrically derived matches. The cascade-transformer architecture (correspondence module followed by a shape-conditioned generation module) is a novel architectural choice, and the large-scale PokeAnimDB dataset provides the cross-category training signal that prior works lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is among the first works to demonstrate plausible pose transfer across fundamentally different character topologies (e.g., humanoid to bird, human to fish), opening a practical path for animating arbitrary 3D assets without per-category retraining. The PokeAnimDB dataset (975 characters, 28K motions) is a valuable community resource that addresses the data scarcity bottleneck for non-humanoid animation. However, the cycle-consistency evaluation is an indirect proxy for transfer quality, the method still depends on a pretrained shape encoder (Michelangelo) and ARAP post-processing for quality, and the quantitative margins over the strongest baseline (SFPT) are modest (PMD 4.264 vs. 4.312 in cross-category). Open questions include whether the approach scales to characters with no semantic keypoint labels, real-time inference, and whether the soft-correspondence formulation can be extended to full animation sequences rather than single poses.
