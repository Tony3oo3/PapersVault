## Problem

Existing multi-focus image fusion methods, whether heuristic-rule-based or deep-learning-based, struggle to generate decision maps with precise boundaries. Deep learning approaches in particular operate as black boxes, producing nuisance false edges and burr artifacts that degrade fusion quality. This paper addresses the gap by deriving a theoretical neurodynamics analysis of coupled neural P (CNP) systems to prevent abnormal continuous neuron firing, enabling interpretable, training-free decision map generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261574/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of same-size multi-focus RGB images captured at different focal depths, preprocesses them with Sum-Modified Laplacian (SML) to enrich input signals beyond raw pixel values, and feeds them into two parallel CNP systems. It outputs a binary decision map (no post-processing required), intermediate spike matrices recording per-neuron firing counts, and the final fused all-in-focus image via pixel-wise selection. Notably, all internal neuron parameters are auto-configured from the input via the derived neurodynamic constraints, eliminating manual tuning, and the entire pipeline runs in 0.18 s per image pair on CPU (C++).

## Method

The core contribution is a neurodynamics-driven CNP system (ND-CNP) that first establishes a closed-form constraint (Theorem 4) between external input intensity and internal parameters (decay rates α, β, γ; threshold weight λ; synaptic weights W) to guarantee neurons do not enter uncontrolled continuous firing. Two parallel CNP systems process the SML-preprocessed source images, where each neuron maintains three memory units (feeding input U, linking input V, dynamic threshold T) updated via a coupled spiking mechanism over t=110 iterations with coupling radius r=16. Focused regions naturally produce more spikes than defocused ones, so the decision map is obtained by simply comparing local spike counts within the coupling radius—no learned classifier or post-processing is needed. The fused image is then assembled by pixel-wise selection guided by this decision map. This design is training-free, fully interpretable (spike matrices directly encode focus information), and generalizes across datasets without parameter re-tuning.

## Results

ND-CNPFuse achieves state-of-the-art performance on four MFIF datasets (Lytro, MFFW, MFI-WHU, Real-MFF), ranking first in 5 of 6 metrics across all datasets. On Real-MFF, it attains Qabf 0.8207, FMIw 0.6666, SSIM 0.9569, PSNR 34.20, QP 0.9237, and QCB 0.8398, outperforming the closest competitor DMANet on all six metrics. The only loss is SSIM on MFFW (0.8362 vs. CCF's 0.8384). Runtime is 0.41 s per image pair in MATLAB (0.18 s in C++) on CPU, faster than GPU-based DMANet (0.21 s on A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ND-CNPFuse outperforms all nine baselines on 5 of 6 metrics across all four datasets, with the sole exception being SSIM on MFFW where CCF (0.8384) edges out ND-CNPFuse (0.8362). The closest competitors are DMANet (AAAI 2025) and PADCDTNP (Neural Networks 2024): on Real-MFF, ND-CNPFuse leads DMANet by +0.0002 Qabf, +0.0182 FMIw, +0.0062 SSIM, +0.185 PSNR, +0.0043 QP, and +0.0036 QCB. On Lytro, the margin over DMANet is tighter (e.g., +0.0015 Qabf, +0.0160 FMIw, +0.0145 SSIM), while on MFFW ND-CNPFuse leads DMANet by +0.0015 Qabf and +0.0187 FMIw. End-to-end methods (CCF, DeepM²CDL, Mask-DiFuser, GIFNet) and Transformer/diffusion-based approaches (TC-MoA, SAMF) trail significantly, with GIFNet and CCF lagging by 0.2+ in Qabf on most datasets. In runtime, ND-CNPFuse (0.41 s MATLAB / 0.18 s C++ on CPU) is faster than GPU-based DMANet (0.21 s on A100) and comparable to SAMF (0.43 s on CPU), while being orders of magnitude faster than CCF (153.68 s) and DeepM²CDL (14.35 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Coupled Neural P (CNP) systems, a bio-inspired spiking model originally proposed by Peng and Wang (2019) for image feature extraction, and on the decision-map paradigm in MFIF where a network predicts per-pixel focus labels. It departs from prior CNP applications—which relied on manually tuned parameters and suffered from uncontrolled continuous firing—by providing the first rigorous neurodynamic analysis of CNP neurons, deriving closed-form constraints between network parameters and input signals. It also departs from DL-based decision-map methods (e.g., DMANet, SAMF) by replacing black-box trained networks with an interpretable, training-free spiking mechanism that generates decision maps through spike-count comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The neurodynamic analysis provides a theoretical foundation that was previously absent for CNP systems, making them more accessible and less dependent on heuristic parameter tuning. The demonstration that a training-free, interpretable spiking model can match or slightly exceed trained deep networks across four diverse MFIF datasets suggests that bio-inspired computation may be a viable alternative to deep learning for structured, rule-based vision tasks. Limitations include modest numerical margins over the second-best method on several metrics, a slight SSIM drop on MFFW attributed to the spike-count clarity measure's sensitivity to low-contrast edges, and the framework's current focus on two-image fusion (multi-image extension is deferred to an appendix). Open questions include whether the neurodynamic constraints generalize to other P-system variants and whether the approach extends to other fusion tasks (e.g., multi-exposure, infrared-visible).
