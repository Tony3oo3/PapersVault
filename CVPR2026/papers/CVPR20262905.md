## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) disentangle polysemantic neural activations into interpretable concepts, but determining the optimal number of active concepts per feature remains an open problem. ReLU-based SAEs require L1 regularization with carefully tuned coefficients and suffer from feature shrinkage, while TopK-based SAEs impose a rigid fixed-K constraint that cannot adapt to varying feature complexity. No existing method dynamically selects the sparsity level per sample without hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a latent feature vector x ∈ R^d extracted from a frozen backbone (CLS + 14×14 patch tokens from ViT-B/16 for images, or hidden embeddings from GPT-2 Small layer 8 for text) and produces a reconstructed feature x̂ along with a sparse set of concept activation weights. Unusually, the number of non-zero activations is not fixed but determined per-sample by a data-dependent threshold τ computed from the similarity scores, so complex features activate more concepts while simple ones activate fewer. The model is trained solely with reconstruction loss, requiring no sparsity penalty or K hyperparameter.

## Method

The paper reformulates the SAE as a cross-attention operation where the input feature serves as the query and a learnable dictionary of M concept vectors serves as both key and value matrices, unifying the encoder and decoder through shared concept representations. In place of softmax, the attention mechanism uses sparsemax, which projects the similarity score vector onto the probability simplex via Euclidean distance minimization, yielding exact zeros for low-scoring concepts. The closed-form solution p_m = max(z_m − τ, 0) computes a per-sample threshold τ by sorting the scores, so the number of active concepts adapts to feature complexity without any regularization term. This eliminates the need for L1/L0 penalties or a fixed K, and the entire model is trained end-to-end with only the reconstruction loss using Adam (lr=3e-4).

## Results

On text reconstruction (GPT-2 Small), Sparsemax SAE achieves NMSE of 0.004–0.008 on OpenWeb and WikiText-103 across dictionary sizes M=3072–24576, substantially outperforming TopK (0.010–0.063) and ReLU-based SAEs (0.059–0.064). In terms of cross-entropy degradation, Sparsemax SAE stays within ±0.4 on OpenWeb and ±2.2 on WikiText-103, while ReLU-based methods degrade by 4.6–6.6 points. On zero-shot image classification across 11 datasets (CLIP ViT-B/16), Sparsemax SAE achieves the best average performance at all top-n settings (n=1,5,10,50), with particularly large margins at small n (e.g., 26.11% vs. 8.64% for TopK at n=1 on Food101).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sparsemax SAE dominates all six baselines on both text reconstruction metrics. On NMSE, it achieves 0.004–0.008, beating the strongest TopK/BatchTopK baselines (0.010–0.063) by 2–15× and ReLU-based methods (0.059–0.064) by over 10×. On CE degradation, Sparsemax SAE stays near zero (±0.4 on OpenWeb, ±2.2 on WikiText-103), while ReLU degrades by 4.6–6.6 points and Gated by 1.3–6.6 points. Among TopK variants, BatchTopK slightly outperforms TopK at larger M (e.g., 0.060 vs. 0.055 on OpenWeb M=24576), but both are consistently worse than Sparsemax SAE. On zero-shot image classification (Food101, n=1), Sparsemax SAE scores 26.11% versus 8.64% (TopK K=32) and 6.46% (BatchTopK K=32), a 3–4× margin. The paper further shows that the average sparsity K*=24 estimated by Sparsemax SAE improves TopK/BatchTopK when used as their K hyperparameter, confirming that the dynamic threshold provides useful tuning guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAE-based interpretability line of work (Huben et al., ICLR 2024; Gao et al., ICLR 2025) by replacing the standard MLP encoder-decoder with a cross-attention transformer where the learned dictionary serves as both key and value matrices, unifying the encoding and decoding stages. The sparsity mechanism departs from both L1-regularized ReLU variants and fixed-K TopK/BatchTopK approaches by adopting sparsemax (Martins & Astudillo, ICML 2016), which projects attention scores onto the probability simplex with data-dependent sparsity. Conceptually, the paper positions sparsemax as a 'more precise version of BatchTopK' that operates at the sample level rather than the batch level, eliminating the need for a manually chosen K or regularization coefficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of hyperparameter tuning (no K, no λ) is a practical advantage for SAE practitioners who currently must grid-search sparsity levels. The cross-attention architecture's sharing of concept vectors between encoder and decoder is a clean architectural insight that could inform future SAE designs. However, validation is limited to relatively small models (ViT-B/16, GPT-2 Small) and the O(M log M) sorting cost of sparsemax may become prohibitive at the dictionary sizes (M > 100K) used in production LLM interpretability. Open questions include whether the dynamic sparsity advantage persists at scale and whether the cross-attention overhead is justified over simpler MLP encoders with sparsemax applied directly.
