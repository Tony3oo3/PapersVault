## Problem

Existing automatic skeleton generation methods rely heavily on purely geometric encoding and treat the 3D object as a monolithic whole, making them unable to handle the increasingly complex structures (detailed clothing, hair, accessories) found in modern 3D assets. Additionally, these methods are end-to-end without conditional control, forcing animators to perform tedious post-processing rather than specifying coarse main bones or adjusting bone density directly during generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh as primary input, uniformly sampled to 8,192 points with normals and encoded by a pretrained Michelangelo point cloud encoder. Optionally, animators can provide a coarse main-bone skeleton (tokenized and embedded as conditional input) and a density level token (a scalar mapped through learnable binning into a probability-weighted embedding). The output is a full skeleton represented as an autoregressive token sequence where each joint contributes 6 tokens (discretized 3D position plus parent 3D position), from which joint positions and bone connectivity are recovered.

## Method

The core model is a decoder-only OPT-350M autoregressive transformer trained with cross-entropy loss to predict discretized skeleton tokens. The key architectural contribution is a semantic-aware tokenization scheme: a GraphTransformer first predicts fine-grained semantic labels (29 categories for humanoids, 31 for tetrapods) for each joint, and bones are then grouped by semantics (main → hair → cloth → other) with DFS traversal within each group, replacing the purely geometric BFS ordering used by prior work. A second contribution is a learnable density interval module that uses K learnable cutpoints (enforced monotonic via cumulative softplus) to map a continuous bone count into soft bin probabilities, which weight a set of learnable embedding vectors to produce a density conditioning vector. Conditioning features (shape embedding, CLS category token, density token, and optional main-bone embeddings) are injected both by prepending before the BOS token and through cross-attention layers inserted after every self-attention layer for deeper fusion. The model is trained on a curated dataset of 82,633 rigged meshes spanning bone counts from 5 to 400 across humanoid, tetrapod, bird, aquatic, vehicle, and weapon categories.

## Results

On the curated 82,633-sample rigged mesh test set, the proposed method achieves a Precision of 0.745, Recall of 0.731, F1-Score of 0.730, and the lowest Chamfer distances (J2J 0.036, J2B 0.027, B2B 0.025) among all compared methods. Compared to the retrained MagicArticulate baseline, the proposed method reduces J2J distance by 18.2%, J2B by 20.6%, and B2B by 21.9%, while improving Precision by 4.6% absolute. Against UniRig and Puppeteer (evaluated without retraining), the proposed method shows a 5- to 9-fold increase in Precision and F1-Score, reflecting the gap between models trained on simple-structure datasets and one trained on high-complexity rigs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: UniRig [26], Puppeteer [18], and MagicArticulate [19]. UniRig and Puppeteer are evaluated with their publicly available checkpoints (not retrained on the paper's dataset) and produce severely under-detailed skeletons, achieving only 0.105 and 0.168 Precision respectively, versus 0.745 for the proposed method — a 5- to 7-fold gap. MagicArticulate, retrained on the same 82,633-sample dataset, is the strongest baseline (Precision 0.712, F1-Score 0.707) but still trails the proposed method by 3.3–4.6% absolute on all accuracy metrics and by 18–22% relative on all three Chamfer-distance metrics. Qualitatively, UniRig and Puppeteer miss fine-grained joints (hands, tails, hair accessories) and clothing-related bones entirely, while retrained MagicArticulate produces disorganized head-region skeletons with misplaced or entangled joints. The proposed method consistently generates structurally complete skeletons that closely align with ground truth across humanoid, tetrapod, and non-humanoid categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive rigging paradigm established by UniRig, Puppeteer, and MagicArticulate, which all rely on purely geometric BFS or joint-based tokenization. It departs from this lineage by introducing a semantic-aware tokenization scheme that groups bones by learned semantic categories (main vs. auxiliary) and applies DFS within each group, complementing rather than replacing geometric ordering. The learnable density interval module draws on soft binning ideas from classification literature but is the first to apply it as a continuous control handle in skeleton generation. The semantic understanding sub-model (GraphTransformer over joint graphs) repurposes standard GNN architectures for a novel rigging-specific labeling task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SG framework to simultaneously address structural complexity (up to 400 bones) and provide two practical control handles (density and main-bone conditioning) that professional animators explicitly requested, making it directly applicable to production pipelines. The 82,633-instance dataset with high bone counts fills a notable gap over prior rigging datasets dominated by simple structures. Key open questions include whether the density control can be extended to region-level granularity, whether underrepresented categories (vehicles, accessories) limit real-world generalization, and whether the produced skeletons can be directly consumed by downstream automated animation systems.
