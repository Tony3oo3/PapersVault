## Problem

Existing multimodal anomaly detection methods rely on unconditional cross-modal feature mapping, which fails to handle the inherent many-to-many relationship between modalities (e.g., identical 3D geometries with diverse RGB colors), causing mapping ambiguity and false-positive anomaly localization. Additionally, these methods depend on heavy decoders or large memory banks, resulting in slow inference and high memory consumption that impede deployment on real-world production lines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263841/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For industrial settings, the method takes a paired RGB image and surface normal map at 224×224 resolution (784 patch tokens each), processed by a DINO ViT-B/8 backbone with L=2 encoding stages. For medical settings, it extends to four MRI modalities (T1, T2, T1ce, Flair) by adding corresponding SQM and CMapping modules. The output is a pixel-level anomaly localization map obtained by averaging cosine-similarity-based discrepancy maps across all layers and modalities, plus an image-level anomaly score computed as the top-0.1% mean of localization scores.

## Method

CPMAD dynamically extracts two types of prototypes to guide cross-modal reconstruction. The Consensus Extraction Module (CEM) initializes learnable anchor embeddings, computes averaged cosine-similarity affinity scores against both modalities' projected features, and aggregates cross-modal tokens via these dynamic anchors; a subsequent cross-attention with layer-wise averaging yields anomaly-free consensus prototypes. The Supplementary Query Module (SQM) employs Complementary Residual Attention, where a small set of learnable queries (G=8) attend to both consensus prototypes and modality-specific tokens, then subtracts the consensus-attention scores from the feature-attention scores and applies ReLU to isolate modality-specific cues not explained by the consensus. The Complementary Mapping module integrates both prototypes via lightweight cross-attention with a channel-wise residual (MLP + spatial averaging) rather than token-wise residual, avoiding the 'identical shortcut' problem. Training uses only a cosine-similarity mapping loss to align reconstructed features with originals, eliminating the need for a heavy decoder and keeping overall complexity linear in token count.

## Results

On MVTec-3D, CPMAD achieves a mean I-AUROC of 97.8% and mean AUPRO of 97.9%, surpassing the previous best (G2SF at 97.1%/97.9%). On Eyecandies, CPMAD reaches a mean AUPRO of 93.4%, outperforming the best prior method (3D-ADNAS at 89.8%) by 4.0%. Under 5-shot settings, CPMAD attains I-AUROC of 89.9% (MVTec-3D) and 88.0% (Eyecandies), exceeding the prior SOTA by 8.8% and 10.6% respectively. The lightweight CPMAD-S variant achieves 101.73 FPS on an RTX 4090 with only 36.34 GFLOPs and 1058 MB memory, while maintaining 96.6% I-AUROC on MVTec-3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPMAD outperforms all 14 baselines on MVTec-3D, achieving 97.8% mean I-AUROC vs. G2SF (97.1%), Cycle-CFM (96.0%), CFM (95.4%), and M3DM (94.5%), with the largest gap over PatchCore (84.8%) and AST (93.7%). On Eyecandies, CPMAD (95.0% I-AUROC, 93.4% AUPRO) surpasses 3D-ADNAS (94.6%/89.8%) and G2SF (90.2%/89.8%), with a notable 4.0% AUPRO advantage over the best prior method, particularly on color-diverse classes like Licorice Sandwich and Gummy Bear. On the BraTS-AD medical benchmark, CPMAD gains 4.2% I-AUROC and 5.6% AUPRO over the strongest baseline (DecoupleMAD at 96.4%/86.3%), demonstrating scalability to four MRI modalities. In few-shot (5-shot) settings, CPMAD leads by 8.8% I-AUROC on MVTec-3D (89.9% vs. 3D-ADNAS at 82.6%) and 10.6% on Eyecandies (88.0% vs. CFM at 79.5%), indicating that complementary prototype learning substantially reduces data dependency. In terms of efficiency, CPMAD-S (36.34 GFLOPs, 1058 MB) is 11.86× fewer FLOPs and 2.59× less memory than CFM (431.14 GFLOPs, 3757 MB) while achieving higher accuracy (96.6% vs. 95.4% I-AUROC on MVTec-3D).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: cross-modal feature mapping for anomaly detection (CFM, Cycle-CFM) and prototype-based normality encoding (INP-Former, DSR, HVQ-Trans). It departs from prior feature-mapping methods by replacing their unconditional cross-modal mapping with a guided mechanism that decomposes features into consensus prototypes (shared, anomaly-free semantics) and supplementary prototypes (modality-specific cues), thereby resolving the many-to-many mapping ambiguity that plagues unconditional approaches. It also departs from unimodal prototype methods by explicitly modeling cross-modal consistency through a dynamic anchor generator, and from memory-bank methods (M3DM, PatchCore) by eliminating stored normal prototypes in favor of lightweight learned prototypes, yielding linear-complexity inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency-accuracy tradeoff: CPMAD-S achieves 5× faster inference and 11.86× fewer FLOPs than the most efficient prior method while maintaining competitive detection performance, making real-time deployment on production lines feasible. The strong few-shot results (89.9% I-AUROC with 5 samples/class) address a critical practical constraint where collecting large normal datasets is expensive. Open questions include whether the complementary prototype decomposition generalizes beyond RGB+3D to other modality pairs (e.g., RGB+thermal, RGB+IR), whether the approach scales to video anomaly detection, and whether the fixed anchor/query sizes (I=12, G=8) remain optimal for higher-resolution or higher-dimensional inputs.
