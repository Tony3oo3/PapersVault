## Problem

Excavated bamboo slips suffer severe nonlinear deformation from dehydration and stress, yet existing restoration methods either lack physical modeling of bamboo's fiber-specific rheology or rely on data-driven black-box approaches that fail to preserve textual details under complex warping. No prior method encodes the material-specific stress-diffusion coupling and viscoelastic creep mechanisms that govern how bamboo actually deforms over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262723/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a deformed bamboo slip image (320×32 pixels) and a 32×4 control point grid (128 points) derived automatically from the slip's contour, and produces a restored image along with per-timestep inverse displacement vectors (128×2) at each of T=150 progressive steps. The sparse control point displacements are mapped to a dense deformation field via bilinear interpolation, reducing parameters by >98% while maintaining spatial smoothness consistent with continuous media mechanics.

## Method

The framework couples a CreepDeformationEngine (forward) with a ControlPointUNet (inverse). The forward engine simulates physically plausible deformation by sequentially applying three coupled effects at each timestep: fiber elongation derived from beam-theory curvature, stress transfer via a 3×3 Gaussian kernel approximating exponential decay, and moisture-stress amplification using displacement gradient magnitude as a stress proxy. The inverse ControlPointUNet (4-level encoder-decoder, channels [64,128,256,512]) is trained with MSE loss to predict single-step inverse displacements for the 128 control points, decomposing the complex nonlinear restoration into 150 simple physics-constrained sub-problems. Because ground-truth displacements are computed to satisfy the energy minimization (bending + stretching), training implicitly enforces material-level physical constraints, transforming restoration from a black-box denoising task into an interpretable parameter prediction problem.

## Results

On the synthetic test set (200 Yunmeng Shuihudi Han Slips), the proposed method achieves the best TRQ (1.0176 ± 0.1061) and DCI (7.9407 ± 2.1738), indicating superior text preservation and physical plausibility. DDRM attains the highest Straightness (0.3476 ± 0.1768) but the lowest DCI (2.1947), while CycleGAN achieves the best LPIPS (0.1184) at the cost of severe textual distortion (TRQ = 0.8480). On 500 real excavated deformed slips, the method is the only one to exceed TRQ = 1.0 (1.004) and leads DCI (7.151 vs. 6.619 for Stable Diffusion), confirming generalization to naturally occurring deformations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines spanning adversarial translation (CycleGAN), specialized document dewarping (DewarpNet), large-scale diffusion (Stable Diffusion), and iterative restoration (DDRM). The proposed method wins on TRQ and DCI across both synthetic and real-data evaluations, with a 5.4% TRQ margin over DewarpNet (1.0176 vs. 0.9651) and a 7.4% DCI margin (7.9407 vs. 7.3919) on the synthetic set. DDRM achieves the highest Straightness (0.3476) but produces physically implausible displacement fields, yielding the worst DCI (2.1947) — 3.6× lower than the proposed method on real data. CycleGAN obtains the best LPIPS (0.1184) but introduces severe textual corruption (TRQ = 0.8480), while Stable Diffusion synthesizes unrealistic textures that distort character strokes (TRQ = 0.7861). On 500 real excavated slips, the proposed method is the only method to maintain TRQ above 1.0 (1.004) and leads DCI by 14% over the second-best DewarpNet (7.151 vs. 6.273), demonstrating that physics-guided modeling provides a fundamentally higher performance ceiling for complex archaeological deformations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in image restoration (diffusion-based denoising, GAN-based translation, document dewarping) by encoding material physics directly into both the data generation pipeline and the prediction space. Rather than using physics as a loss regularizer (as in PINNs) or as a post-hoc constraint, the CreepDeformationEngine uses wood rheology (Kelvin-Voigt viscoelasticity, moisture-stress coupling, fiber bundle mechanics) to synthesize paired training data, while the ControlPointUNet predicts inverse displacements in a physically grounded 128-point control grid. This repurposes the iterative restoration framework (analogous to DDRM's multistep denoising) but replaces abstract noise prediction with physically interpretable displacement fields, and extends document dewarping (DewarpNet) from simple geometric correction to complex anisotropic, time-dependent material deformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap in digital archaeology where paired pristine-deformed samples are essentially unavailable, and where black-box generative models produce physically implausible restorations that destroy textual legibility. The physics-as-data-generator strategy is a practical solution to the data scarcity problem that could transfer to other lignocellulosic or anisotropic cultural materials. However, limitations remain: the fixed 32×4 control grid struggles with severely bent or fragmented slips, material parameters (λ, γ, A_κ) are sensitive to preservation conditions, and the 150-step sequential process introduces computational overhead. Open questions include whether adaptive control-point hierarchies can handle extreme deformations and whether the physics model generalizes across different bamboo species and burial environments.
