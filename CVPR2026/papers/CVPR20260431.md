## Problem

Existing in-context segmentation methods either fine-tune vision foundation models (improving in-domain accuracy but degrading out-of-distribution generalization) or compose multiple frozen models like DINOv2 and SAM (preserving generalization but introducing architectural complexity and fixed mask granularity). The paper asks whether a single self-supervised backbone—specifically DINOv3 with its dense, localized features—can support both semantic correspondence and segmentation without any decoder, fine-tuning, or model composition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260431/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image with a binary mask (at object, part, or instance granularity) and a target image, both resized to 1024×1024. Dense patch-level features are extracted from a frozen DINOv3-Large encoder (304M parameters). The output is a segmentation mask predicted at patch resolution, bilinearly upsampled to original resolution, and refined with a CRF. No additional supervision, decoder, or auxiliary model is required at inference time.

## Method

INSID3 operates in three stages entirely on frozen DINOv3 features: (1) agglomerative clustering partitions the target image into spatially coherent regions using a single similarity threshold τ; (2) seed-cluster selection identifies the best-matching region via backward correspondences in a debiased feature space, where a reference prototype is compared against cluster prototypes using cross-image cosine similarity; (3) aggregation merges the seed with other candidate clusters whose combined score—multiplying cross-image semantic alignment with intra-image self-similarity—exceeds a threshold α. A key technical contribution is a training-free positional debiasing step: DINOv3 features exhibit a systematic bias where patches at the same spatial coordinates spuriously match across unrelated images; this is corrected by estimating a low-dimensional positional subspace via SVD on a noise image and projecting features onto its orthogonal complement before cross-image matching, while original (un-debiased) features are retained for intra-image clustering where spatial structure is beneficial.

## Results

INSID3 achieves 55.1% average mIoU across 10 one-shot segmentation benchmarks (semantic, part, and personalized), outperforming the strongest training-free baseline GF-SAM (47.6%) by +7.5% mIoU and the strongest fine-tuned method SegIC (44.1%) by +11.0% mIoU, while using only 304M parameters (vs. 945M for SAM-based pipelines). Notable per-dataset gains include +27.8% mIoU on Chest X-Ray, +12.9% on PerMIS (personalized), and +6.6% on LVIS-92i over GF-SAM. On the semantic correspondence task (SPair-71k), the proposed positional debiasing yields consistent PCK improvements of +0.9 to +6.6 points across DINOv3 model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest training-free baseline GF-SAM (DINOv2+SAM, 945M params), INSID3 wins on 9 of 10 datasets, with the largest margins on Chest X-Ray (+27.8%), PerMIS (+12.9%), and LVIS-92i (+6.6%), but slightly underperforms on COCO-20i (57.6 vs. 58.7). Against fine-tuned methods, INSID3 is outperformed in-domain (e.g., SegIC reaches 76.1% on COCO-20i) but substantially outperforms them out-of-distribution (e.g., +11.0% on iSAID, +20.3% on Chest X-Ray vs. SegIC). On part segmentation, INSID3 beats GF-SAM by +6.0% (PASCAL-Part) and +2.4% (PACO-Part), and beats fine-tuned SegIC/DiffewS by +10.6%/+16.5% and +12.8%/+15.9% respectively. On personalized segmentation (PerMIS), INSID3 leads all baselines by a wide margin (+12.9% over GF-SAM, +15.2% over SegIC, +31.8% over DiffewS), attributed to its use of backward correspondences for negative evidence. The positional debiasing also improves GF-SAM with DINOv3 by +1.8% average mIoU, confirming its general utility beyond INSID3 itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

INSID3 departs from both dominant trends in in-context segmentation: fine-tuning approaches (SegIC, DiffewS) that inject segmentation capability via task-specific supervision, and training-free multi-model pipelines (Matcher, GF-SAM, PerSAM) that combine DINOv2 for correspondence with SAM for mask generation. The paper's core conceptual departure is showing that DINOv3's dense self-supervised features are sufficient for both semantic matching and spatial segmentation without any decoder, fine-tuning, or mask-level pre-training. It also introduces a positional-bias correction (projecting out a noise-estimated subspace) that addresses a previously unacknowledged limitation of DINOv3's cross-image correspondence, building on the Gram anchoring objective that inadvertently amplifies spatial correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that segmentation emerges directly from self-supervised dense representations challenges the prevailing assumption that mask-level supervision (SAM pre-training) or task-specific fine-tuning is necessary for accurate pixel-level predictions. This opens a path toward more general-purpose, single-backbone segmentation that avoids the architectural complexity and distributional brittleness of multi-stage pipelines. Open questions include whether the agglomerative clustering approach handles extremely fine-grained or very large-scale objects, whether the positional bias correction generalizes to other SSL models beyond DINOv3, and whether the method extends to video or 3D settings. The positional bias discovery itself has broader implications for any task relying on DINOv3 cross-image matching.
