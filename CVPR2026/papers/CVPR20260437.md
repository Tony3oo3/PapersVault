## Problem

Existing egocentric HPE methods treat visible and invisible keypoints indiscriminately during training, and no prior dataset provides keypoint visibility annotations. This undifferentiated treatment introduces ambiguity from invisible joints that degrades the estimation accuracy of visible ones, a problem that is especially severe in egocentric settings where self-occlusion and limited field-of-view make limb keypoints invisible nearly half the time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260437/cropped_page1_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal window of T=24 stereo grayscale egocentric frames (640×480, resized to 448×336 for the ViT-L variant) together with per-frame camera extrinsics [R|T] from the HMD's built-in SLAM system. It outputs per-frame 3D SMPL keypoints in the world coordinate system along with per-keypoint visibility confidence scores. The VQ-VAE decoder reconstructs poses in a canonical coordinate system (head-projected, floor-aligned) that is invariant to arbitrary floor-plane translations and vertical-axis rotations, before transforming back to world coordinates.

## Method

EvaPose comprises three components: (1) a VQ-VAE pretrained on AMASS, MOYO, and AIST++ mocap data encodes 3D keypoints into a discrete 160×2048×256 codebook, serving as a pose prior that is frozen during downstream training; (2) a visibility-aware 3D estimation network that jointly predicts per-frame 3D camera-space keypoints and per-keypoint visibility scores from stereo image features, using visibility-weighted heatmaps (H' = s·H) fed through a ViT encoder; and (3) an iterative intra-and inter-frame attention module that alternates between a Stereo Transformer Decoder (fusing left/right visual features per frame) and a Temporal Transformer Encoder (aggregating across the full T-frame window) for N refinement iterations. Training uses a visibility-based loss-weighting scheme assigning weight 1.0 to visible and 0.1 to invisible keypoints in both heatmap and 3D position losses, followed by a second stage that trains the temporal refinement network with joint-position and acceleration-smoothness losses. Final poses are reconstructed by multiplying predicted codebook logits with the frozen VQ-VAE codebook and passing through the VQ-VAE decoder, avoiding the non-differentiable hard quantization step.

## Results

On the Eva-3M test set, EvaPose-ViT-L achieves the best MPJPE of 34.2 mm and PA-MPJPE of 24.0 mm, outperforming the strongest baseline FRAME (49.8 / 35.1 mm) by 15.6 and 11.1 mm respectively. On the EMHI P2 test set (unseen action categories), the gap widens dramatically: EvaPose-ViT-L reaches 33.3 mm MPJPE versus FRAME's 60.5 mm, a 27.2 mm improvement. EvaPose-ResNet50 achieves 35.6 mm MPJPE on Eva-3M at 48.0 FPS, enabling real-time inference, while EvaPose-ViT-L (9.4 FPS) provides the highest accuracy across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvaPose consistently outperforms all three baselines (UnrealEgo, EgoPoseFormer, and FRAME) on MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, L-PE, FootPE, and HandPE across all three test sets. The most striking margin is on EMHI P2 (unseen actions), where EvaPose-ViT-L achieves 33.3 mm MPJPE versus FRAME's 60.5 mm — a 45% relative reduction — indicating substantially better generalization to out-of-distribution motions. On the Eva-3M test set, EvaPose-ResNet50 reduces MPJPE from 49.8 mm (FRAME) to 35.6 mm while maintaining real-time speed (48 FPS vs. FRAME's 108.7 FPS). FRAME remains competitive on Jitter (2.4 on EMHI P1 vs. EvaPose-ViT-L's 3.0) and inference speed, but is clearly surpassed on all accuracy metrics. The paper further shows (Table 3) that prior methods perform comparably on visible limb keypoints but degrade significantly on invisible ones, whereas EvaPose's visibility-aware design narrows this gap, confirming that indiscriminate treatment of visible/invisible keypoints harms overall accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of SLAM-augmented egocentric HPE methods (FRAME, REWIND) by introducing explicit keypoint visibility modeling, a component entirely absent from prior egocentric HPE work. The VQ-VAE pose prior follows the tokenized representation strategy from TokenHMR [14], and the heatmap decoder design borrows from ViTPose [47], but the visibility-aware loss-weighting scheme and the iterative stereo-temporal attention fusion are novel architectural contributions. The Eva-3M dataset departs from prior real-world egocentric datasets (EMHI, EgoBody3M, SELF) by being the first to provide ground-truth visibility labels alongside SMPL poses, and by being captured on a commercial Pico4 Ultra headset rather than a custom rig.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that indiscriminately supervising visible and invisible keypoints measurably degrades accuracy on visible ones (Table 4: VLK-PE drops from 53.5 to 45.8 mm) validates a practical concern in VR/AR deployment where self-occlusion and limited FoV are pervasive. Eva-3M, as the first large-scale real egocentric dataset with visibility annotations, fills a critical gap that has constrained the field. The main limitation is the reliance on large-scale ground-truth labels that are difficult to obtain in-the-wild, leaving open the question of whether weakly- or self-supervised visibility estimation can close this gap. Broader impact includes enabling more realistic avatar tracking in commercial VR headsets, where the Pico4 Ultra capture setup directly mirrors deployment conditions.
