## Problem

Existing 3D object detection methods for both multi-agent cooperative perception and single-agent perception rely heavily on large-scale human-annotated 3D bounding boxes, which are costly to obtain and unavailable in many real-world deployments. No prior method simultaneously solves both multi-agent and single-agent perception in a fully unsupervised manner, leaving a gap for leveraging inter-agent communication alone to improve detection without any manual labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263455/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point clouds from V connected vehicles (all transformed into the ego-vehicle coordinate frame via homogeneous transformation matrices) along with GPS poses of communicated agents, which serve as positional priors to initialize candidate proposals. It outputs two trained detectors: a multi-agent detector D_m (fusing shared point clouds) and a single-agent detector D_e (ego point clouds only), each producing 3D bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores. The iterative training loop (T=20 iterations × E=10 epochs) progressively refines pseudo labels, so the final outputs are both the detectors and the stabilized pseudo-label sets used for supervision.

## Method

UMS jointly trains a single-agent and a multi-agent PointPillars detector through iterative pseudo-supervision, initialized from GPS-pose-based candidate proposals. Three modules refine noisy proposals: (1) Proposal Purifying Filter (PPF) trains a PointNet++ instance-level classifier using self-supervision from the confidence gap between true and false positives in the denser multi-agent point clouds, filtering proposals below a 0.5 score threshold; (2) Progressive Proposal Stabilizing (PPS) applies curriculum learning via a sigmoid-scheduled confidence threshold (dynamic pruning) and progressively increases the weight of historical proposals stored in a memory bank (dynamic fusion) to stabilize detections across iterations; (3) Cross-View Consensus Learning (CCL) transfers multi-agent cues to the single-agent branch through rotated-IoU geometric matching of unmatched but spatially valid proposals and an L2 BEV semantic alignment loss masked by a visibility indicator. The multi-agent branch is supervised by PPS-stabilized labels while the single-agent branch is supervised by CCL consensus labels plus the BEV alignment loss, with both detectors updated via focal classification and smooth-L1 regression losses each iteration.

## Results

On OPV2V (simulated), UMS achieves 86.71/83.89 AP@0.3/AP@0.5 for multi-agent detection and 76.31/71.30 for single-agent detection, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline DOtA (66.14/52.37 multi-agent, 59.01/46.87 single-agent). On V2V4Real (real-world), UMS reaches 58.12/52.03 AP@0.3/AP@0.5 for multi-agent and 49.72/44.27 for single-agent, improving over DOtA by 3.19 and 3.86 points respectively. Pseudo-label quality on the training set also shows large gains: UMS achieves 85.98% precision and 53.71% recall at IoU=0.5 on V2V4Real, versus 60.42%/43.91% for DOtA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMS outperforms all four unsupervised baselines (DBSCAN, OYSTER, CPD, DOtA) on every dataset and both multi-agent and single-agent settings. The gap is most dramatic on the synthetic OPV2V benchmark, where UMS's multi-agent AP@0.5 of 83.89 exceeds DOtA (52.37) by 31.52 points and CPD (50.49) by 33.40 points, largely because the noise-free simulated LiDAR allows the learned PointNet++-based PPF to cleanly separate vehicles from background. On the real-world V2V4Real dataset, improvements are more modest but consistent: UMS gains 3.19 AP@0.5 over DOtA in multi-agent and 3.86 in single-agent detection. Clustering-based methods (DBSCAN, OYSTER, CPD) trail significantly on both datasets due to their lack of instance-level feature learning and sensitivity to sparse or occluded point clouds. DOtA, the only prior method designed for multi-agent unsupervised detection, is the closest competitor but still falls well short, particularly on OPV2V where its rule-based filtering degrades under the cleaner geometry that UMS's learned filter exploits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of unsupervised 3D object detection and multi-agent cooperative perception, two areas that have been studied largely in isolation. Prior unsupervised methods (OYSTER, CPD, DOtA) rely on hand-crafted clustering heuristics or rule-based filtering, while cooperative perception methods (AttFuse, PointPillars) assume full supervision. UMS repurposes the cooperative point cloud density as a self-supervision signal for a learnable PointNet++-based classifier (PPF), and introduces cross-view consensus (CCL) as a novel mechanism to transfer multi-agent geometric and BEV-semantic cues into single-agent detection without annotations. The curriculum-learning-inspired Progressive Proposal Stabilizing (PPS) module adapts the easy-to-hard principle from Bengio et al. to the iterative pseudo-label refinement setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly address both multi-agent and single-agent 3D perception in a fully unsupervised manner, which is practically important given that V2V penetration remains limited and many vehicles must still rely on single-agent perception. However, the performance gap to supervised baselines remains substantial (e.g., 52.03 vs. 64.75 AP@0.5 on V2V4Real multi-agent), and the largest gains appear on the synthetic OPV2V dataset rather than the noisier real-world V2V4Real, raising questions about robustness in more challenging deployment conditions. Open questions include scalability to more object classes, larger agent counts, and whether the cross-view consensus mechanism generalizes to heterogeneous sensor configurations or non-vehicle-centric scenarios.
