## Problem

Existing unrestricted adversarial attacks based on diffusion models suffer from three structural weaknesses: stochastic re-noising increases gradient variance and weakens cross-model alignment, the time-dependent noise schedule multiplicatively contracts adversarial signals along the trajectory, and the resulting perturbations contain large off-manifold components that purification defenses easily suppress. The paper asks whether operating in the deterministic, smooth velocity field of a flow-matching model—rather than through diffusion-style denoising and re-noising—can simultaneously improve black-box transferability and resilience against adversarial-training and purification defenses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (ImageNet, 224×224) and a target label, and produces a single adversarial image that preserves semantics while flipping the target classifier's prediction. The perturbation is constrained to ‖δ‖∞ ≤ 4/255 at the reconstruction level, and the attack runs T=15 flow-matching steps with K=10 PGD iterations per step using a frozen GMFlow backbone. The lookahead weight is set to w=0.3, coupling the current reconstruction with a one-step rolled-out reconstruction in the loss.

## Method

AdvFM injects adversarial perturbations into the learned velocity field v_θ(x_t, t) of a flow-matching model rather than into pixel space or via diffusion denoising. At each time step, it reconstructs the flow endpoint via R_t(x_t) = x_t + (1−t)v_θ(x_t, t), runs PGD on the reconstruction, converts the resulting perturbation δ into a velocity perturbation δ/(1−t), and advances the probability-flow ODE by an explicit Euler step—amplifying the effective state change by Δt/(1−t) compared to the √(ᾱ_t) attenuation in diffusion-based attacks. A lookahead variant optimizes a two-point objective that jointly considers the current reconstruction and a rolled-out reconstruction at t+Δt, reducing temporal mismatch along the ODE trajectory. Theoretically, the deterministic flow transport yields lower-variance attack directions (improving surrogate–target gradient alignment for transfer), biases perturbations toward manifold-tangent directions (surviving purification that contracts off-manifold components), and aligns with robust gradients of adversarially trained models.

## Results

On ImageNet, AdvFM achieves the highest average black-box ASR when attacking CNN sources: 70.35% (VGG19 source, +4.49 over APA) and 72.05% (RN50 source, +7.64 over APA). For Transformer sources, AdvFM leads on Swin-B (64.88%, +3.39 over APA) but trails APA by 1.51 points on ViT-B/16 (68.22% vs. 69.73%). Against defenses, AdvFM dominates purification: 94.98% on NRP (+13.78 over APA) and 81.35% on Smooth (+10.65), while remaining competitive on DiffPure (61.33% vs. APA's 63.30%). On adversarially trained ViT models, AdvFM reaches 98.53% (RAT) and 97.86% (MIMIR), the best in both columns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdvFM outperforms all six baselines on average ASR for three of four source models (VGG19, RN50, Swin-B), with the largest margins over APA (+4.49 and +7.64 pp) and over diffusion-based methods like DiffAttack (+20.77 pp from VGG19 source) and AdvDiffuser (+30.81 pp). Against the strongest baseline APA, AdvFM wins on 6 of 8 target models from the VGG19 source and 7 of 8 from RN50, but loses on ViT-B/16 source (68.22% vs. 69.73%) where APA's adversary-preference alignment is particularly effective. AdvAD shows highly inconsistent transfer (e.g., 81.24% on RN50→RN50 but only 11.83% on VGG19→DN161), while AdvGAN and DiffPGD remain the weakest baselines across nearly all settings. Under purification defenses, AdvFM's advantage is most dramatic: it surpasses APA by +13.78 pp on NRP and +10.65 pp on Smooth, though APA retains a narrow edge on DiffPure (63.30% vs. 61.33%). On adversarially trained ViT models (RAT, MIMIR), AdvFM achieves the best ASR (98.53%, 97.86%), exceeding even AdvAD and DiffAttack which also score above 95%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-based unrestricted adversarial attack paradigm (AdvDiffuser, DiffPGD, DiffAttack, AdvAD, APA) by reframing the attack in the continuous-time velocity field of a flow matching model rather than in the iterative denoise-renoise loop. It builds directly on the flow matching framework of Lipman et al. [19], using a pretrained GMFlow network as a frozen velocity field, and introduces a lookahead two-point objective that has no direct analogue in prior diffusion-based attacks. The theoretical analysis explicitly contrasts the deterministic ODE transport of flow matching with the stochastic re-noising of diffusion, arguing that the former's smoothness and tangential structure explain both better transferability and purification resistance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified theoretical lens (step amplification, gradient variance reduction, manifold-tangent alignment) that explains why flow-matching-based attacks should outperform diffusion-based ones, giving practitioners a principled reason to prefer ODE-based generative backbones for adversarial synthesis. However, the theoretical results rely on somewhat idealized assumptions (isotropic noise model, purification contracting off-manifold directions more strongly), and the empirical evaluation is limited to ImageNet with a single flow-matching backbone (GMFlow), leaving open whether the advantages persist across other flow architectures, datasets, or modalities. A key open question is whether the lookahead mechanism generalizes to multi-step or video settings where temporal coupling is more complex.
