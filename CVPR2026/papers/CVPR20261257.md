## Problem

Group convolution for 3D point clouds faces a fundamental trade-off: discrete group methods are scalable but lose continuous symmetry, while steerable convolutions guarantee strict equivariance at prohibitive computational cost. The intertwiner kernel framework was proposed to resolve this tension, but prior instantiations either restricted symmetry to SO(3) rather than full SE(3) or lacked the memory efficiency needed for large-scale real-world scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261257/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point cloud coordinates paired with surface normal vectors (or a heuristic augmentation when normals are unavailable) to represent the S² component of the SE(3)/SO(2) quotient space. Local neighborhoods are extracted via ball queries of radius r around farthest-point-sampled centroids, and the double coset parameters [β̄, r̄, z̄] are computed in a scale-normalized local frame. Outputs range from a single class label (classification) to per-point part or semantic labels (segmentation) and a relative SE(3) transformation (pose registration).

## Method

ECKConv defines its convolutional kernel over the double coset space SO(2)\SE(3)/SO(2), where each element is uniquely parameterized by three SE(3)-invariant quantities [β, r, z] derived from the relative orientation and displacement between a centroid and its neighbors. A coordinate-based network with Gaussian positional encoding maps these parameters to unbounded gate coefficients that weight a fixed set of learnable basis matrices, forming an explicit kernel. A key reformulation reorders the summation so that basis matrices multiply pre-aggregated neighbor features rather than individual features, reducing backpropagation memory from O(AK·C_in·C_out) to O(A(K·C_in + C_in·C_out)) and enabling deeper, wider networks. The full block combines farthest point sampling, ball query, the equivariant convolution, and a residual skip connection, and is stacked in U-Net or encoder-decoder architectures for segmentation tasks.

## Results

On ModelNet40 classification, ECKConv-Normal achieves 91.37% accuracy under SO(3) training augmentation (I/SO(3)) and maintains 90.92% when both training and test sets are rotated (SO(3)/SO(3)), outperforming all equivariant baselines. On pose registration, ECKConv+DCP-v2 achieves a mean angle error of 0.63° and translation RMSE of 0.010, dramatically outperforming CSEConv+DCP-v2 (9.34°, 0.17). For part segmentation on ShapeNet, ECKConv reaches 83.78% mIoU under SO(3) evaluation, and on S3DIS indoor semantic segmentation, ECKConv-Normal achieves 61.80% mIoU, the best among rotation-invariant methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECKConv-Normal (91.37%) outperforms all equivariant baselines on ModelNet40 classification under SO(3) augmentation, beating Vector Neurons [9] (90.52%) by ~0.85%, CSEConv [20] (83.75%) by ~7.6%, and E2PN [49] (44.47%) by ~47 points on the I/SO(3) setting. ECKConv without normals (90.52%) ties VN exactly on I/I but provides full SE(3) equivariance rather than SO(3)-only. ECKConv-mini, matched in architecture to CSEConv, surpasses it by ~3.6% (87.40% vs 83.79%), validating the explicit kernel's memory efficiency (Proposition 4.1). On pose registration, ECKConv+DCP-v2 achieves 0.63° mean angle error versus 9.34° for CSEConv+DCP-v2 and 11.70° for E2PN, with a max error of only 8.57° versus 178.95° for CSEConv. On ShapeNet part segmentation, ECKConv (83.78% mIoU) beats VN (81.74%) and massively outperforms CSEConv (35.80%), while on S3DIS it reaches 61.80% mIoU, surpassing RI-MAE [32] (60.3%) and KPConv with SO(3) augmentation (57.42%). ECKConv trails non-equivariant methods like PTv3 (92.54%) and PointConv (92.31%) on the I/I classification setting, but those methods collapse under SO(3) augmentation (13.65% and 5.29% respectively), confirming the value of architectural equivariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECKConv extends the intertwiner kernel convolution framework of Cohen et al. [7,8] to achieve continuous SE(3) equivariance, a gap explicitly identified as unaddressed by prior work. It builds directly on the authors' own CSEConv [20] (which achieved continuous SO(3) symmetry via coordinate-based networks) and the double coset parameterization from E2PN [49], while adopting Gaussian embedding from Zheng et al. [47,48] for coordinate encoding. The key architectural departure is the explicit kernel reformulation (Proposition 4.1), which reorders the summation to reduce backpropagation memory from O(AK·C_in·C_out) to O(A(K·C_in + C_in·C_out)), enabling deeper and wider networks than the implicit-kernel alternative (CSEConv).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a long-standing trade-off in equivariant point cloud processing: strict continuous SE(3) symmetry was previously incompatible with scalability to large-scale scenes, forcing practitioners to choose between discrete approximations (E2PN), SO(3)-only symmetry (CSEConv), or augmentation-based robustness. The demonstrated scalability to S3DIS (6000+ m², 271 rooms) with state-of-the-art mIoU among rotation-invariant methods is practically significant for real-world 3D perception. Open questions include whether the ball-query radius and Gaussian embedding hyperparameters transfer across scales, whether the approach extends to other Lie groups (e.g., SE(2) for 2D, or non-compact groups), and whether the 27.7M parameter count of the full ECKConv model remains tractable for very deep architectures on GPU-constrained settings.
