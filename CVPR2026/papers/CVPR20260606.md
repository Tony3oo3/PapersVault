## Problem

Panoramic image generation suffers from poor perceptual realism and geometric fidelity because high-quality real-world panoramic data is scarce, and prior methods over-rely on synthetic data or focus narrowly on architectural modifications (e.g., alternative convolutions, cubemap stitching) without effectively leveraging the abundant perspective image data available. The key open question is how to endow panoramic generators with real-world photorealistic knowledge when only limited panoramic training data exists.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as primary input and produces equirectangular panoramic images at 1024×2048 resolution. For inpainting and outpainting, a user-provided binary mask derived from camera pose is required for consistent alignment; the framework does not support mask-free image-to-panorama generation. Training uses a hybrid of refined Matterport3D panoramas and high-quality Internet perspective images, with only LoRA modules updated on top of Flux.1-dev.

## Method

DiT360 builds on the Flux DiT architecture with LoRA and introduces a hybrid training paradigm operating at two representation levels. At the image level, panoramic refinement converts ERPs to cubemaps and inpaints blurred polar regions, while perspective image guidance re-projects perspective photos onto lateral cubemap faces back into ERP space with mask-constrained MSE supervision to transfer photorealistic knowledge. At the token level, three mechanisms provide geometry-aware supervision in latent noise space: position-aware circular padding (concatenating first/last columns with matching RoPE positions) enforces 0°/360° boundary continuity, a rotation-consistent yaw loss supervises predictions under random yaw rotations, and a distortion-aware cube loss projects noise features onto six cube faces for face-wise MSE. The overall panoramic loss combines the base Flux MSE with weighted yaw and cube losses (λ₁=λ₂=0.5), and the framework natively supports inpainting/outpainting via feature replacement through inversion without additional training.

## Results

On the Matterport3D validation set, DiT360 achieves state-of-the-art performance across 9 of 11 metrics, including FID (42.88 vs. 46.72 for SMGD), BRISQUE (10.25 vs. 16.37 for Matrix-3D), Inception Score (1.60), and QA_aesthetic (4.19). The method also leads on all three FID variants (FID_clip 41.60, FID_pole 50.88, FID_equ 24.77), indicating superior boundary consistency and polar-region fidelity. DiT360 ties with HunyuanWorld on FAED (2.91) and trails by a marginal 0.05 on CLIP Score (34.68 vs. 34.73), which the authors attribute to CLIP Score being designed for perspective images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiT360 outperforms all six baselines on the majority of metrics. Against the strongest competitor HunyuanWorld, DiT360 wins on FID (42.88 vs. 76.75), BRISQUE (10.25 vs. 39.12), NIQE (3.72 vs. 5.18), IS (1.60 vs. 1.53), QA_aesthetic (4.19 vs. 3.85), and all three FID variants, while tying on FAED (2.91) and trailing by only 0.05 on CLIP Score. Compared to SMGD (the prior best on FID_pole at 5.69), DiT360 achieves a much lower overall FID (42.88 vs. 46.72) and substantially better BRISQUE (10.25 vs. 30.35), though SMGD retains a lower FID_pole. Against Matrix-3D, DiT360 improves FID by 18 points (42.88 vs. 60.91) and BRISQUE by 6 points (10.25 vs. 16.37). The older stitching-based methods (PanFusion, MVDiffusion) are far behind, with PanFusion scoring 124.87 FID versus DiT360's 42.88. The authors note that LayerPano3D and HunyuanWorld, which rely heavily on synthetic data, produce render-like appearances that compromise perceptual realism despite competitive geometric metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiT360 builds directly on Flux (a DiT-based flow-matching diffusion model) and applies it to panoramic generation via LoRA fine-tuning, departing from prior panoramic methods that primarily innovate on architecture (spherical convolutions, cubemap stitching, autoregressive decoding). Its key conceptual departure is shifting the emphasis from model design to a multi-level hybrid training strategy that jointly exploits perspective and panoramic data: at the image level through re-projection guidance and polar-region inpainting, and at the token level through circular padding, yaw loss, and cube loss. This positions the work as a data-and-supervision-driven alternative to the architecture-centric lineage represented by PanFusion, SMGD, Matrix-3D, and HunyuanWorld.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the scarcity of high-quality real-world panoramic data can be partially mitigated by transferring photorealistic knowledge from abundant perspective images, offering a practical recipe for domain-limited generation tasks beyond panoramas. However, the method still relies on Matterport3D (indoor scenes) for its panoramic branch and requires a camera-pose-derived mask for inpainting/outpainting, limiting its applicability to outdoor or mask-free settings. Open questions include whether the hybrid paradigm extends to panoramic video, 3D scene reconstruction, or large-scale open-world generation, which the authors flag as future directions.
