## Problem

Subject-driven generation (SDG) models lose fine-grained subject details—logos, small text, intricate patterns—when rendering a reference subject into a new scene, making outputs unusable for commercial applications. This stems from two obstacles: the absence of large-scale paired training data (clean subject + degraded SDG output + ground truth), and the inherent ambiguity of text prompts in specifying precise geometric and appearance variations. Additionally, standard perceptual metrics (CLIP, DINO) fail to capture the high-frequency structural fidelity that matters for subject identity.

## Input / Output

FlowFixer takes two RGB images as input: a reference subject image (which may be spatially perturbed—cropped, rotated, or color-shifted relative to the scene) and an SDG-generated image from any upstream model. It produces a refined image that preserves the global layout of the generated image while restoring subject-consistent fine details from the reference, entirely without a text prompt. For efficiency, the model optionally refines only a subject-centric crop (identified via keypoint matching) and blends it back into the full image using Poisson blending, avoiding full-resolution global passes.

## Method

FlowFixer is a text-free diffusion refiner built on FLUX.1-Kontext, fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 192) for 50K iterations. It replaces the original text token with a second image input, so the DiT backbone processes three streams—latent noise z₁, the generated image I_gen, and the reference image I_ref—encoded by a shared VAE and separated via 3D RoPE with per-stream timestep offsets (0, 1, 2) while allowing full cross-attention. Training is self-supervised: pseudo-paired data is created by adding noise to a clean image and performing a single denoising step with SDXL, with degradation level controlled by downscaling the image to 1.0×, 0.5×, or 0.25× before VAE encoding, which selectively removes high-frequency details while preserving global structure. The model is trained with MSE loss on 18,450 Unsplash photographs, and at inference uses a guidance scale of 2.5. For evaluation, the paper introduces two ground-truth-free keypoint-matching metrics—Absolute Keypoint Increase (AKI) and Keypoint Matching Gain (K_Gain)—computed with OmniGlue to quantify fine structural fidelity beyond what CLIP or DINO capture.

## Results

On FidelityBench-258K, FlowFixer achieves an average keypoint matching gain (K_Gain) of 77.3% across three SDG backbones (FLUX.1-Kontext-Pro, Qwen-Image-Edit, Nano-Banana-Edit), with absolute keypoint increases (AKI) of 66.5, 54.0, and 64.7 respectively. On the fixed FidelityBench-300 subset, K_Gain rises to 91.2% and VLM (Claude 3.7) judges FlowFixer as the best restoration in 79.0% of cases. Notably, CLIP-I and DINOv2 similarity scores remain nearly unchanged (e.g., 0.778 vs. 0.776 baseline on FLUX), confirming that the gains are in fine-grained structural fidelity rather than global semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowFixer decisively outperforms text-based editing (FLUX.1-Kontext) on AKI across all backbones (66.5 vs. 7.5 on FLUX, 54.0 vs. 11.1 on Qwen) and on K_Gain (77.9% vs. 52.7%, 74.8% vs. 54.1%), though the gap narrows on Nano-Banana-Edit (64.7 vs. 61.7 AKI, 79.2% vs. 77.0% K_Gain). Against OminiControl fine-tuned on FLUX.1-Dev, FlowFixer wins on FLUX (66.5 vs. 29.0 AKI) and Qwen (54.0 vs. 0.48 AKI), but OminiControl+F-Dev reports a higher AKI on Nano-Banana (108.0 vs. 64.7); the authors attribute this to copy-pasting behavior that inflates keypoint counts while degrading global consistency (lower CLIP-I and DINO scores). Against OminiControl+F-Kontext, FlowFixer wins on all three backbones in both AKI and K_Gain, with the latter variant sometimes showing negative AKI (−3.37 on Qwen). On the FidelityBench-300 subset, FlowFixer's K_Gain reaches 91.2% versus 45.9% (text-based), 46.6% (OminiControl+F-Dev), and 38.4% (OminiControl+F-Kontext), and VLM judgment strongly favors FlowFixer (79.0%) over all alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowFixer positions itself as a model-agnostic 'last-mile' refiner that sits on top of any existing SDG pipeline, departing from the dominant paradigm of conditioning diffusion models on text prompts or encoder-injected reference features (IP-Adapter, BLIP-Diffusion, OminiControl). Architecturally it builds directly on FLUX.1-Kontext's flow-matching DiT backbone, but replaces the text conditioning with a dual-image input (generated + reference) and a self-supervised one-step-denoising data curation strategy that sidesteps the need for expensive paired subject-scene triplets. Conceptually, it reframes SDG fidelity as a post-hoc image-to-image translation problem rather than a generation-time conditioning problem, and introduces keypoint-matching metrics (AKI, K_Gain) to fill the gap left by CLIP/DINO-based evaluation that overlooks high-frequency structural detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete commercial pain point—logos, small text, and intricate product patterns are routinely corrupted by SDG models—by providing a drop-in refinement step that is prompt-free and backbone-agnostic. The FidelityBench-258K/300 benchmark and the keypoint-based metrics offer the community a more sensitive evaluation tool for fine-grained fidelity than existing perceptual scores. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-reference or video settings, whether the one-step-denoising proxy faithfully captures all real SDG failure modes (e.g., severe viewpoint changes), and whether the LoRA-based fine-tuning on FLUX.1-Kontext generalizes cleanly to other backbones without retraining.
