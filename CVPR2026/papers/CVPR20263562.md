## Problem

Existing Neural Architecture Generation (NAG) methods suffer from two critical limitations: low generation efficiency due to reliance on complex decoders or iterative denoising that require repeated network inference in high-dimensional latent spaces, and insufficient architectural validity because constraints are typically imposed only on the final output while neglecting intermediate generation steps, allowing error accumulation and structurally invalid architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263562/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PNAG takes a search space definition (NB201, MobileNetV3, DARTS, or AutoFormer) and a task dataset, optionally conditioned on a surrogate performance predictor trained via meta-learning across diverse tasks. The output is a complete neural network architecture produced as an autoregressive sequence of sub-architectures, where each step adds one structural element (layer/block), starting from a minimal 3-operation sub-architecture (input, output, one functional op) and expanding to N operations. The generation process itself requires no network inference—only codebook lookups and linear mappings—making a single architecture generation take ~0.006 seconds.

## Method

PNAG reformulates architecture generation as an autoregressive process where each AR unit is a fully functional sub-architecture, evolving from coarse (simple) to fine (complex). The Multi-scale Sub-architecture Quantization (MSQ) module encodes architectures via a graph encoder into a feature map, then progressively decodes sub-architectures by region-averaging the feature map to smaller spatial scales and performing vector quantization against a shared learnable codebook, with each step adding one more element. The Step-wise Consistency Constraint (SCC) module applies a regularization penalty at every intermediate generation step, measuring the L2 deviation between the interpolated sub-architecture feature map and the original encoded representation, with a Lyapunov stability proof guaranteeing that the generation trajectory asymptotically converges to the input architecture under a bounded learning rate. This network-free quantization decoding eliminates the costly inference steps of prior VAE- or diffusion-based generators, achieving a 1300× speedup over DiffusionNAG while maintaining 100% architectural validity.

## Results

On the NB201 search space, PNAG achieves the known optimal accuracy of 94.37% on CIFAR-10 and 73.51% on CIFAR-100 while requiring only 1 trained architecture, and substantially outperforms all transferable baselines on Aircraft (66.99% vs. 58.83% for DiffusionNAG) and Oxford-IIIT Pets (45.35% vs. 41.80% for DiffusionNAG), yielding average improvements of +8.43% and +5.07% respectively. On the DARTS search space, PNAG attains 97.93% CIFAR-10 and 83.54% CIFAR-100 test accuracy with the lowest search cost (0.03 GPU days). Generation efficiency is dramatically improved: a single architecture is generated in 0.006 seconds, a 1300× speedup over DiffusionNAG (8 s), and 100% architectural validity is maintained across both NB201 and MobileNetV3 search spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PNAG ties with DiffusionNAG, TNAS, and MetaD2A on CIFAR-10 (94.37%) and CIFAR-100 (73.51%) in NB201, as these are the known optimal values in that search space. However, on the harder fine-grained datasets, PNAG achieves 66.99% on Aircraft and 45.35% on Oxford-IIIT Pets, outperforming DiffusionNAG (58.83% / 41.80%), TNAS (59.15% / 40.00%), and MetaD2A (57.71% / 39.04%) by 8.16–9.28% and 3.55–6.31% respectively. On the MobileNetV3 search space, PNAG leads all three transferable baselines on every dataset, with the largest margin on Aircraft (+2.24% over DiffusionNAG). In the DARTS search space, PNAG surpasses OStr-DARTS (97.58% CIFAR-10) and DiffusionNAS (97.35%) on CIFAR-10 (97.93%) and matches the best Top-5 ImageNet accuracy (93.0%) at the lowest search cost (0.03 GPU days vs. 0.40 for OStr-DARTS). For Transformer-based generation on AutoFormer, PNAG achieves 76.6% Top-1 on ImageNet under the Tiny setting, beating AZ-NAS (76.4%) and TF-NAS (75.3%), while matching AutoFormer's Base accuracy (82.4%) with fewer parameters (53.8M vs. 54.0M). In terms of generation efficiency and validity, PNAG generates each architecture in 0.006 s (1300× faster than DiffusionNAG's 8 s) and achieves 100% validity on both NB201 and MBV3, compared to 98.97% and 99.09% for DiffusionNAG.

## Relation to Prior Work

PNAG builds on the Neural Architecture Generation (NAG) paradigm introduced by DiffusionNAG [1] and VAE-based graph generation [23], which aim to directly construct architectures rather than randomly sample them within a search loop. It repurposes vector quantization decoding from VQ-VAE [44] and the next-scale prediction idea from visual autoregressive modeling [43] to reformulate architecture generation as an autoregressive process over progressively larger sub-architectures. The key departure from prior NAG methods is the elimination of network inference during generation (replacing complex decoders or iterative denoising with simple codebook lookups) and the introduction of step-wise consistency constraints that supervise every intermediate generation step rather than only the final output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1300× reduction in single-generation time and 100% architectural validity make PNAG a practical drop-in replacement for random initialization or sampling steps in existing NAS pipelines (evolutionary, Bayesian, gradient-based), potentially improving the reliability and speed of the entire search process. The Lyapunov stability analysis provides a theoretical guarantee that the coarse-to-fine trajectory converges, which is absent in prior NAG work. Open questions include whether the approach scales to substantially larger or more heterogeneous search spaces (e.g., multi-branch or conditional architectures), whether the surrogate model quality becomes a bottleneck for the transferable generation setting, and whether the step-wise constraint overhead becomes prohibitive for very deep generation sequences.
