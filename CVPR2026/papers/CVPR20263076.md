## Problem

Existing Federated Prototype Learning methods increase inter-class prototype distances to improve discrimination but inadvertently disrupt semantic relationships among related classes (e.g., horse–dog should be closer than horse–truck), which is critical for generalization. Under non-IID and model-heterogeneous settings, clients cannot reliably recover these inter-class semantic structures from their limited local data, leaving an open question of how to construct prototypes that inherently preserve class-level semantic topology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263076/cropped_page1_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

On the client side, the method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) and produces class labels via a local image classifier. On the server side, the PLM (CLIP or BERT) processes LLM-generated text prompts of the form 'A photo of {CLASS}: {description}' (k=3 descriptions per class) to produce text prototypes in a shared d-dimensional space (d=512). The key unusual aspect is that the server never sees raw images; it only receives aggregated client image prototypes (mean feature vectors per class) to update its trainable prompts, while the actual classification is performed entirely by heterogeneous client-side image models.

## Method

FedTSP constructs class prototypes from the textual modality by first querying an LLM (e.g., GPT-4o) to generate k=3 fine-grained descriptions per class, then encoding these via a server-side PLM (CLIP or BERT) into text prototypes. To bridge the modality gap between the PLM and client image models, a set of trainable prompt vectors is prepended to each class's text embedding; these prompts are updated on the server by minimizing a contrastive alignment loss between text prototypes and aggregated client image prototypes. On the client side, a contrastive (softmax) alignment loss replaces the L2-based alignment used in prior FedPL methods, optimizing relative rather than absolute similarity so that the PLM's high baseline inter-class similarity does not mislead the model. This is the first framework to inject global semantic priors from pretrained text models into federated prototype learning, and it is agnostic to whether the PLM is vision-language aligned (CLIP) or purely textual (BERT).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with HtFE₉ and Dir(0.1), FedTSP-BERT achieves 87.52% average test accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (FedKD at 86.80%) by 0.72%. On CIFAR-100 under the same setting, FedTSP-BERT reaches 46.08%, surpassing the strongest prototype-based method (FedTGP at 41.37%) by 4.71%. On Tiny ImageNet with Dir(0.1), FedTSP-CLIP attains 34.82%, exceeding the best prior method (FedKD at 32.79%) by 2.03%. The advantage of FedTSP grows with data heterogeneity: at Dir(0.1) on CIFAR-100, the gap over the best SOTA (FedMRL, 42.82%) is 3.26%, while at Dir(1.0) it narrows to 1.66%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedTSP outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. Against the closest SOTA HtFL methods, FedTSP-CLIP leads FedKD (86.80%) by 0.54% on CIFAR-10 Dir(0.1) and FedMRL (42.82%) by 2.79% on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.1). Among prototype-based methods, the gap is largest: FedTSP-BERT exceeds FedTGP (41.37%) by 4.71% and AlignFed (41.88%) by 4.20% on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.1), confirming that text-derived semantic structure substantially improves prototype quality over manually designed or client-aggregated prototypes. In the model-homogeneous GFL setting (Table 3), FedTSP-CLIP achieves 37.98% on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.1) versus FedBABU's 36.37% and FedFA's 35.16%; in PFL, FedTSP-CLIP-FT reaches 62.30% versus FedBABU-FT's 60.10% and FedPAC's 58.19%. The advantage is most pronounced under strong heterogeneity (α=0.1), where FedTSP's text-anchored prototypes are less sensitive to local distribution shifts, and diminishes as data becomes more IID (α=1.0), where all methods converge closer together.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Federated Prototype Learning (FedPL) by departing from the prevailing design principle of maximizing inter-class prototype distances (as in AlignFed, FedTGP, FedSA). Instead, FedTSP injects semantic topology from pretrained language models into prototypes, using LLM-generated class descriptions and a server-side PLM to produce text prototypes that preserve inter-class similarity structure. The trainable-prompt mechanism for bridging the text-image modality gap draws on prompt tuning literature, while the contrastive alignment loss on the client side follows standard contrastive learning. The paper positions itself as the first to transfer text-derived semantic priors into FedPL prototypes, distinguishing itself from CLIP-based FL methods that fine-tune CLIP itself rather than transferring semantics into lightweight client models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that distance-maximizing prototypes disrupt semantic relationships provides a clear diagnostic for a previously underappreciated failure mode in FedPL, and the proposed fix (textual semantic priors) is a practical, plug-in mechanism that works with both vision-aligned (CLIP) and vision-unaligned (BERT) encoders. Limitations include reliance on LLM API access for prompt generation, the assumption that class names are known to the server, and evaluation confined to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse benchmarks like ImageNet. An open question is whether the semantic structure injected from text transfers equally well to fine-grained or domain-specific classification tasks where WordNet-style relationships are less informative.
