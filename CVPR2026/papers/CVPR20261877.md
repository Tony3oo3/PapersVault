## Problem

Existing frame-event fusion methods for arbitrary point tracking rely on synchronous or non-adaptive fusion, causing temporal misalignment between low-rate RGB frames (20–30 Hz) and high-rate event streams (100–200 Hz) and severe degradation when either modality fails. No prior work provides a real-world, manually annotated TAP benchmark with synchronized frame-event data under challenging illumination and motion conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261877/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes RGB frames at 512×512 resolution (20–30 Hz) and asynchronous event streams (100–200 Hz), where events are binned into discrete time steps and converted to multi-channel representations via a maximal-timestamp SBT operator. It also takes an initial query point (t_q, x, y) and outputs per-point pixel trajectories and binary visibility/occlusion states at the much higher event-rate frequency, enabling tracking updates far beyond the native frame rate. Single-modality variants (frame-only or event-only) are also supported by simply omitting the other input branch.

## Method

TAPFormer introduces two core mechanisms. First, the Transient Asynchronous Fusion (TAF) module treats the scene as a temporally continuous latent representation: upon each frame arrival, a transient state is initialized by fusing the frame with events from its exposure window, and between frames this state is continuously refined by incoming event batches through a lightweight cross-attention residual updater, achieving event-rate feature updates without aligning to discrete frames. Second, the Cross-modal Locally Weighted Fusion (CLWF) module performs localized cross-attention where event tokens query spatial neighborhoods of frame tokens with a learned locality bias, adaptively weighting the more informative modality per spatial region to handle blur, sparsity, or overexposure. The fused transient tokens are then aggregated across time by a temporal self-attention block, progressively upsampled with skip connections into multi-scale feature maps, and fed into a TAPTR-style iterative transformer optimizer that refines query point trajectories and occlusion states over M iterations. Training is supervised on the synthetic FE-FastKub dataset (48 FPS, 96 frames, 1024 trajectories per sample) with 12 Hz frame inputs and 48 Hz trajectory supervision, plus 2,878 high-speed samples to improve robustness to motion blur.

## Results

On the proposed InivTAP and DrivTAP real-world TAP benchmarks, TAPFormer achieves a mean δ_avg^vis of 65.0%, improving 28.2% over the best fusion baseline FETAP (50.7%) and 30.3% over the best frame-based tracker CoTracker3 (49.9%). On InivTAP, it reaches 57.0% AJ and 95.2% OA; on DrivTAP, 48.8% AJ and 97.8% OA. For feature tracking on EDS and EC, TAPFormer attains 82.3% FA / 70.4% EFA and 93.3% FA / 92.6% EFA respectively, outperforming all frame-, event-, and fusion-based baselines. Inference speed is 20.2 ms per time step, comparable to or faster than single-modality variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAPFormer decisively outperforms all frame-based trackers: on InivTAP it gains 36.4% AJ over CoTracker3 (57.0 vs 41.8) and 83.3% over Chrono (57.0 vs 31.2); on DrivTAP the AJ gap over CoTracker3 is 31.5% (48.8 vs 37.1). Against the prior fusion method FETAP, TAPFormer improves mean δ_avg^vis by 28.2% (65.0 vs 50.7) and AJ by 35.1% on InivTAP and 32.6% on DrivTAP, while also achieving higher OA (95.2 vs 83.9 on InivTAP). Event-only baselines are far behind: ETAP scores only 25.1% mean δ_avg^vis versus TAPFormer's 65.0%, and even the best event-to-video pipeline (E2Vid–CoTracker3) reaches just 23.2%. On the feature-tracking task (Table 2), TAPFormer leads CoTracker3 by 2.1% FA on EDS (82.3 vs 80.2) and by 8.9% FA on EC (93.3 vs 84.4 for FETAP), while also surpassing event-only methods like MATE (88.5% FA on EC) and ETAP (88.1% FA on EC). The single-modality variants of TAPFormer (frame-only: 50.6% mean δ; event-only: 33.7%) also exceed their respective best prior baselines, confirming that the architectural and training-data contributions are independent of the fusion mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAPFormer extends the established TAP task (TAP-Vid, PointOdyssey, CoTracker3, TAPTR) into the multimodal frame-event domain, building on the iterative transformer optimizer paradigm of TAPTR and the EDI model's insight that a frame and its exposure-window events jointly encode a latent instantaneous scene state. It departs from prior fusion approaches (FETAP, EKLT, DeepEvT) that rely on synchronous or channel-concatenation fusion by introducing a transient asynchronous representation that is continuously updated at event rate between discrete frames. The CLWF module's local cross-attention between modality tokens is conceptually related to the ventral/dorsal stream analogy in biological vision, but applied at the token level for spatially adaptive reliability weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first real-world, manually annotated frame-event TAP benchmark (InivTAP + DrivTAP) fills a critical gap for evaluating multimodal tracking under realistic degradation (motion blur, overexposure, low light), and the FE-FastKub synthetic dataset enables high-frame-rate supervision that prior TAP datasets lack. The frame-rate sensitivity result—maintaining 75.8% FA at 9.375 FPS versus 13.2% for CoTracker3—suggests event-driven transient updates could decouple tracking quality from camera frame rate, which is practically significant for embedded and automotive deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer sequences, whether the synthetic-to-real gap in event simulation (v2e) limits generalization, and whether the mechanism extends to other asynchronous-sensor fusion tasks beyond tracking.
