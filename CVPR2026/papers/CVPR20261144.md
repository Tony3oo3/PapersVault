## Problem

Existing inference-time scaling methods for text-to-image generation rely on fixed iteration budgets or fixed stopping thresholds that cannot adapt to varying prompt difficulty, and their refinement strategies show negligible or even negative gains in subsequent rounds. Training-based reflection-tuned alternatives require costly joint fine-tuning of diffusion and vision-language models on curated reflection datasets, often overfitting to collected reflection paths and lacking transferability. The paper addresses how to achieve genuine, adaptive multi-round self-improvement at inference time without any model retraining.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single free-form text prompt as input and produces one final generated image as output. Internally, it uses FLUX.1-dev (28 diffusion steps) for generation and FLUX.1-Kontext-dev for instructional editing, with Mistral-Small-3.2-24B-Instruct as the shared VLM backbone for all three agents. The number of refinement rounds is adaptive (between K_min=2 and K_max=4), with 8 candidates generated per round, so the total number of images produced varies per prompt based on semantic complexity.

## Method

RAISE is a training-free, requirement-driven evolutionary framework operating as a three-agent system (analyzer, rewriter, verifier) over a shared VLM. The analyzer extracts a structured binary checklist of requirements from the prompt and partitions them into satisfied and unsatisfied subsets each round, driving adaptive computation allocation. Multi-action mutational refinement concurrently explores three complementary strategies—noise resampling, prompt rewriting targeting unsatisfied requirements, and instructional editing (top/random/comprehensive variants applied to the best image)—generating a diverse candidate population of 8 per round. A tool-grounded verifier leverages Grounded SAM 2, Florence-2, and MiDaS to extract object-level grounding evidence (captions, bounding boxes, depth values) that supports interpretable binary verification of each requirement. The process terminates when all major requirements are satisfied, all requirements pass verification, or the maximum round limit is reached, selecting the highest-fitness candidate across all rounds as the final output.

## Results

On GenEval, RAISE achieves a state-of-the-art overall score of 0.94, surpassing the next-best training-free method (Noise & Prompt Scaling, 0.87) and training-based ReflectionFlow (0.91), while using 41.9% fewer generated samples (18.6 vs. 32) and 88.6% fewer VLM calls (7.3 vs. 64). On DrawBench, RAISE attains 0.885 VQAScore with 21.2 average samples and 8.6 VLM calls, outperforming ReflectionFlow (0.844 VQAScore with 32 samples and 64 VLM calls) and T2I-Copilot (0.822 VQAScore). RAISE also achieves 100% on the Two Object category and 98% on Colors in GenEval, demonstrating strong alignment on compositional and attribute-binding challenges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAISE outperforms all 18 baselines on GenEval overall score (0.94), with the largest margins over training-free methods: +0.20 over T2I-Copilot (0.74), +0.17 over TIR (0.71), and +0.07 over Noise & Prompt Scaling (0.87). Against the strongest training-based baseline ReflectionFlow (0.91), RAISE gains +0.03 overall while using 41.9% fewer samples and 88.6% fewer VLM calls. On DrawBench, RAISE (0.885 VQAScore) beats ReflectionFlow (0.844) by +0.041 and T2I-Copilot (0.822) by +0.063, again with substantially lower compute. RAISE also surpasses the strongest UMM baseline Qwen-Image-RL (0.91) by +0.03 on GenEval, despite requiring no additional pretraining. Notably, RAISE is the only method that continues to improve with additional rounds (Pareto frontier in Fig. 4), whereas T2I-Copilot and ReflectionFlow plateau or degrade in perceptual quality with more iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAISE synthesizes ideas from three distinct lines of inference-time scaling for T2I: noise-level resampling (Ma et al., SANA-1.5), prompt-level VLM rewriting (TIR, T2I-Copilot), and training-based reflection tuning (Reflect-DiT, ReflectionFlow). It departs from prior training-free methods by replacing fixed iteration budgets and single-action-per-round designs with a population-based evolutionary framework that concurrently applies resampling, rewriting, and instructional editing, guided by a structured requirement checklist. The key conceptual departure is the requirement-driven adaptive stopping criterion, which allocates compute proportionally to prompt complexity rather than using a uniform budget, and the tool-grounded verification loop (detection, captioning, depth) that provides interpretable evidence for fine-grained requirement checking rather than relying on holistic VLM scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAISE demonstrates that a training-free, multi-agent evolutionary loop can match or exceed reflection-tuned models on prompt-image alignment while using substantially fewer samples and VLM calls, suggesting that carefully structured inference-time search may reduce the need for costly joint fine-tuning of diffusion and VLM components. The adaptive scaling mechanism—converging early on easy prompts and spending more rounds on complex ones—offers a practical recipe for production T2I systems where compute budgets vary. Open questions include how the framework scales to video generation or very long compositional prompts, whether the requirement extraction step (which depends heavily on VLM quality) remains reliable for out-of-distribution or adversarial prompts, and whether the multi-agent overhead can be further reduced without sacrificing the gains from parallel exploration.
