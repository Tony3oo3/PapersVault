## Problem

Reconstructing world-space human motion from unconstrained monocular video faces a fundamental trade-off: end-to-end models learn strong world-space priors but fail to generalize beyond studio-captured motion diversity, while camera-space lifting approaches handle diverse in-the-wild motion but produce locally consistent yet globally drifting trajectories. No existing method simultaneously achieves generalizability to noisy, incomplete observations and physically plausible, globally coherent world-space motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262864/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular video frames along with estimated camera poses and intrinsics, extracting dense 3D keypoints (via SAM 3D Body) and image features (via a frozen PromptHMR encoder) as conditioning signals; an optional body height in meters can be provided to resolve monocular scale ambiguity. The output is a sequence of 595-vertex sparse mesh positions (MHR LOD6) in a per-video world coordinate system defined by the initial camera pose, from which root trajectories are recovered by integrating per-frame root velocities. A learned iterative MLP converter maps the generated mesh vertices to SMPLX parameters for compact representation and comparison with prior work.

## Method

DuoMo factorizes world-space motion reconstruction into two independent DiT-based diffusion models connected by an explicit geometric lifting step. The camera-space model generates root-centered mesh vertices and root positions conditioned on per-frame keypoint ray-direction embeddings and image features, using RoPE positional encoding and windowed attention to handle long sequences. The lifted camera-space motion (transformed via estimated camera poses) serves as a noisy proposal for the world-space model, which denoises it into globally consistent motion while modeling root velocity rather than absolute position to maintain numerical stability. The world-space model is trained with temporal masking to handle occlusions and a contact loss to reduce foot skating, and at inference time two x0-guidance terms (2D reprojection and displacement) correct temporal drift and ensure correct landing positions after long occlusions. Crucially, the world-space model operates in per-video coordinate systems rather than a canonical lab space, eliminating the need for ambiguous ground-plane alignment.

## Results

On EMDB, DuoMo achieves a W-MPJPE of 171.6 mm and RTE of 1.2%, outperforming the second-best method GENMO (W-MPJPE 202.1, RTE 1.2%) by 16% in world-space joint accuracy. On RICH, DuoMo achieves W-MPJPE 80.8 mm and RTE 1.3%, a 30% reduction over GENMO (W-MPJPE 118.6, RTE 1.9%). In camera-space reconstruction (Table 1), DuoMo also sets new state-of-the-art with PA-MPJPE 41.8 mm on EMDB and 34.8 mm on RICH. With optional height conditioning, W-MPJPE drops further to 167.1 (EMDB) and 80.4 (RICH), and foot-skating remains low at 3.7 and 3.1 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuoMo outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both EMDB and RICH in world-space reconstruction. Against the closest competitor GENMO [37], DuoMo reduces W-MPJPE by 16% on EMDB (171.6 vs. 202.1) and 30% on RICH (80.8 vs. 118.6), while also halving jitter (8.7 vs. 17.8 on EMDB; 5.3 vs. 15.0 on RICH) and matching or beating foot-sliding (3.7 vs. 8.8 on EMDB; 3.1 vs. 6.7 on RICH). Against GVHMR [58], which uses post-hoc foot-locking, DuoMo achieves lower W-MPJPE (171.6 vs. 276.5 on EMDB) with comparable foot-skating (3.7 vs. 3.5). TRAM [73] achieves competitive WA-MPJPE on EMDB (76.4 vs. 67.6) but suffers from high foot-skating (11.0) and is not evaluated on RICH. Earlier methods (TRACE, GLAMR, SLAHMR, WHAM) are substantially worse, with W-MPJPE values 2–10× higher. In camera-space reconstruction (Table 1), DuoMo also leads all baselines, beating PromptHMR [74] (PA-MPJPE 41.8 vs. 41.0 on EMDB; 34.8 vs. 37.3 on RICH) and NLF [57] (41.8 vs. 41.2 on EMDB). On the challenging Egobody egocentric dataset (Table 2), DuoMo reduces W-MPJPE-Occ from 688.1 (Cam-model+Lifting) to 193.1, demonstrating its generative prior's ability to complete motion under occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

DuoMo builds on the DiT transformer-diffusion architecture and x0-guidance sampling, but its core conceptual departure is factoring world-space human motion reconstruction into two independent diffusion models (camera-space estimation + world-space refinement) rather than a single end-to-end network. This contrasts with lifting-based methods (TRAM, PromptHMR, WHAM) that rely on post-hoc optimization for global consistency, and with direct-prediction methods (GENMO, GVHMR) that struggle to generalize beyond studio-captured motion diversity. A second departure is defining world coordinates per-video relative to the initial camera pose, sidestepping the canonical-space alignment problem that plagues prior world-space models, and generating sparse mesh vertices directly rather than SMPL/SMPLX parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-diffusion factorization provides a principled way to resolve the long-standing trade-off between generalizability (camera-space priors) and global physical consistency (world-space priors), and the per-video coordinate formulation makes the approach applicable to in-the-wild scenes on varied terrain without ground-plane alignment. The demonstration that direct mesh-vertex generation is competitive with parametric body models opens a path toward category-agnostic motion modeling. Open questions include whether the method can operate without estimated camera poses (currently a hard dependency), whether the 30-second diffusion inference cost is acceptable for real-time applications, and whether the architecture generalizes convincingly to non-human articulated objects.
