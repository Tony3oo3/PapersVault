## Problem

Existing 2D Gaussian Splatting methods for image representation optimize millions of Gaussian primitives independently, ignoring spatial locality and resulting in large model sizes, slow convergence, and limited compression potential at high resolutions. While 3D anchor-based frameworks have shown that structural organization can reduce redundancy, directly applying them to 2D image modeling yields only marginal gains because 2D Gaussians already have fewer per-primitive parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-resolution RGB image (27–76 megapixels across satellite, natural, and biomedical domains) and produces a compact bitstream encoding seed positions, quantized seed attributes, a binary hash grid, and two lightweight MLPs. At decode time, the bitstream is decompressed via arithmetic coding guided by the context model, and the reconstructed image is rendered by splatting the decoded 2D Gaussians. The representation uses 3.5M (low-rate) or 10M (high-rate) Gaussians organized under N seeds, each predicting K=10 associated primitives.

## Method

SGI introduces seed-based 2D neural Gaussians that partition the image into spatially coherent regions, where each seed's feature vector is decoded by two shared MLPs (one for opacity-weighted color, one for covariance) to generate K Gaussian primitives, replacing independent per-Gaussian parameters with a structured, seed-organized representation. To exploit the residual spatial redundancy that structure alone cannot eliminate in 2D (only ~3% reduction vs. 3D), SGI adds a neural entropy coding stage: a learnable binary hash grid provides spatial context to an MLP that predicts per-attribute Gaussian distributions, enabling adaptive-bit arithmetic coding of quantized seed attributes. A coarse-to-fine multi-scale fitting strategy using a Gaussian pyramid (M=3 levels) progressively refines the representation from low to full resolution, substantially accelerating convergence without sacrificing fidelity. The total loss combines L1 rendering error with a rate term (entropy + hash grid bits) weighted by λ, and quantization-aware training via noise injection ensures the learned attributes compress well under the discrete codebook.

## Results

On FGF2 (51 MP satellite images), SGI in the low-rate setting (3.5M Gaussians) achieves 31.24 dB PSNR, 0.9863 SSIM, and 0.0731 LPIPS at 16.33 MB storage and 48.43 min optimization time, outperforming GaussianImage (27.30 dB, 23.37 MB, 322.17 min) by a large margin. In the high-rate setting (10M Gaussians), SGI reaches 36.27 dB PSNR and 0.9961 SSIM at 41.74 MB, surpassing 3DGS (34.93 dB, 787.73 MB) in both fidelity and compactness. On ICB (27–39 MP natural images), SGI high-rate achieves 39.09 dB PSNR at 32.15 MB, and on STimage (76 MP biomedical images) it reaches 38.72 dB PSNR at 22.03 MB. Overall, SGI delivers up to 7.5× compression over non-quantized 2D Gaussian baselines and 1.6× over quantized ones, with 1.6×–6.5× faster optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest 2D Gaussian baselines, SGI (low-rate) outperforms GaussianImage by 3.94 dB PSNR on FGF2 (31.24 vs 27.30) while using 30% less storage (16.33 vs 23.37 MB) and optimizing 6.6× faster (48.43 vs 322.17 min). Compared to LIG, SGI low-rate achieves slightly lower PSNR on FGF2 (31.24 vs 32.10) but at 6.5× smaller size (16.33 vs 106.81 MB) and 1.8× faster optimization; in the high-rate setting SGI surpasses LIG by 4.17 dB on FGF2 (36.27 vs 32.10) at 2.6× less storage. Against 3D Gaussian methods, SGI high-rate beats 3DGS by 1.34 dB PSNR on FGF2 (36.27 vs 34.93) at 18.9× smaller size (41.74 vs 787.73 MB), and outperforms Scaffold-GS by 8.02 dB at 2.7× less storage. Among INR baselines, SGI low-rate exceeds I-NGP by 2.69 dB PSNR on FGF2 (31.24 vs 28.55) with 22% less storage and 33% faster optimization, and surpasses SIREN by 9.19 dB. On the biomedical STimage dataset, SGI high-rate achieves the highest PSNR (38.72 dB) among all methods, outperforming 3DGS (38.51 dB) at 35.8× smaller size (22.03 vs 787.73 MB), while Scaffold-GS fails entirely due to out-of-memory errors at this resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGI builds directly on the 2D Gaussian Splatting line of work (GaussianImage, LIG) for image representation, while drawing its structural organization from 3D anchor-based methods (Scaffold-GS, HAC, ContextGS). The key conceptual departure is that the authors find direct porting of 3D seed/anchor compression to 2D yields only ~3% size reduction—far less than in 3D—because 2D Gaussians already have fewer per-primitive parameters. To close this gap, SGI introduces a binary hash grid context model for neural entropy coding of seed attributes, a component absent from prior 2D Gaussian methods, and a multi-scale fitting strategy that departs from LIG's residual-refinement hierarchy by reusing the full representation at each scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGI offers a practically important compression and speedup for megapixel-scale image representation, achieving 7.5× size reduction over non-quantized 2D Gaussian baselines and 6.5× faster optimization, which is relevant for deployment on resource-constrained devices. The finding that structural organization alone is insufficient in 2D (unlike 3D) and that explicit entropy modeling is necessary provides a useful design principle for future point-based image codecs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D content, how it scales beyond ~10M Gaussians, and whether the binary hash grid context model can be replaced by more expressive (but costlier) alternatives without sacrificing the speed advantage.
