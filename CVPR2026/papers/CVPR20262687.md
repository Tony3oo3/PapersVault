## Problem

Long-form video understanding with MLLMs is bottlenecked by the need to select informative frames from thousands of candidates, where most are visually redundant or irrelevant to the question. Existing approaches either use fixed heuristics that don't generalize, or train a sampler while freezing the MLLM, preventing the MLLM from learning to generate better retrieval queries or adapt its reasoning to sparse key-frame inputs. This paper identifies that true collaboration between the MLLM and sampler requires joint evolution, which no prior method achieves.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long-form video (densely sampled at 1 FPS for CLIP matching, with a sparse preview of K/2 frames at 32 tokens/frame for query generation) and a multiple-choice text question. It produces a final answer generated from K selected key-frames at 256 tokens/frame, along with intermediate reasoning queries and per-frame sampling weights. The sparse preview is masked during answer generation to ensure fair comparison with non-reasoning baselines, while the conversation history from query generation is retained as context.

## Method

MSJoE operates in four stages: (1) the MLLM reasons over a sparse low-resolution preview to generate N_q diverse visual queries relevant to the question; (2) a frozen CLIP model computes a query-frame similarity matrix S ∈ R^{N_q × N_f}; (3) a lightweight 1D U-Net sampler (~2M parameters) transforms S into per-frame sampling probabilities, selecting K diverse key-frames; (4) the MLLM generates the answer from the selected frames. Both components are jointly optimized via RL: the MLLM is trained with GRPO using a composite reward (accuracy 0.8 + format 0.1 + query informativeness 0.1), while the sampler is trained with REINFORCE using a difficulty-aware reward that scales by the base model's pass rate on each question. A pre-training stage with the difficulty-aware REINFORCE objective stabilizes the sampler before joint RL, and the key architectural insight is that the MLLM must simultaneously learn to produce CLIP-aligned queries and to reason effectively over the sparse, non-uniform frame distributions its own sampler produces.

## Results

On four long-video benchmarks (VideoMME, LongVideoBench, LVBench, MLVU), MSJoE built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves an average accuracy gain of +8.0 points over the base MLLM with a 64-frame budget (e.g., MLVU 75.1 vs. 56.0, LVBench 51.1 vs. 39.2) and +6.7 points with only 32 frames. Against the strongest trainable-sampling baseline TSPO (64 frames), MSJoE gains +1.1 points on average, winning on MLVU (75.1 vs. 74.3), VideoMME-Long (57.4 vs. 56.4), and LVBench (51.1 vs. 46.4), though trailing on LongVideoBench (62.2 vs. 64.2). With 32 frames, MSJoE also surpasses heuristic samplers BOLT and Q-Frame by 0.3–3.0 points across benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest trainable-sampling baseline TSPO (64 frames), MSJoE wins on MLVU (75.1 vs. 74.3), VideoMME-Long (57.4 vs. 56.4), VideoMME-Avg (66.2 vs. 65.5), and LVBench (51.1 vs. 46.4), but trails on LongVideoBench (62.2 vs. 64.2), yielding a net +1.1 average gain. Compared to heuristic samplers at 32 frames, MSJoE outperforms BOLT by 0.3–3.0 points (e.g., MLVU 69.3 vs. 66.3, LVBench 46.4 vs. –) and Q-Frame by 0.3–2.5 points. Against the base Qwen-2.5-VL-7B model, MSJoE (64 frames) improves MLVU by +19.1 (75.1 vs. 56.0) and LVBench by +5.8 (51.1 vs. 45.3), though it falls short on LongVideoBench (62.2 vs. 70.2) where the base model uses 768 frames at 2 FPS. Versus closed-source models, MSJoE (64 frames) surpasses GPT-4o on MLVU (75.1 vs. 66.7) and Gemini-1.5-Pro on MLVU (75.1 vs. 64.0) and LVBench (51.1 vs. 33.1), but remains below both on VideoMME-Avg (66.2 vs. 71.9/75.0). Against dense-frame open-source models, MSJoE beats NVILA-8B on MLVU (75.1 vs. 57.7) and VideoMME-Avg (66.2 vs. 64.2) while using far fewer frames (64 vs. 1024), and outperforms LongVU-7B on VideoMME-Avg (66.2 vs. 60.6) despite LongVU using 1 FPS dense sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSJoE extends the trainable key-frame sampling line initiated by TSPO, which first demonstrated that a lightweight temporal agent can learn frame-selection policies via RL. The critical departure is the joint co-optimization of the MLLM and sampler through end-to-end reinforcement learning (GRPO for the MLLM, REINFORCE for the sampler), whereas prior methods (TSPO, K-frames, M-LLM Frame Selector, ViaRL) freeze the MLLM or optimize components separately. Additionally, MSJoE addresses the question-insufficiency problem (Q1) by having the MLLM reason out multiple visual queries from a sparse preview before CLIP-based retrieval, going beyond methods that use the raw question as a single CLIP query. The framework is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the base MLLM and a frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14 for cross-modal similarity, with a 1D U-Net as the lightweight sampler.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint evolution paradigm addresses a fundamental architectural limitation in prior sampling-based video understanding: without co-adaptation, the MLLM cannot learn to generate retrieval-oriented queries nor adapt its reasoning to sparse, non-uniform frame inputs. The practical impact is significant for deployment scenarios where processing hour-long videos at 1 FPS is infeasible, as MSJoE achieves competitive accuracy with only 32–64 frames. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond multiple-choice QA to open-ended video understanding, whether the frozen CLIP bottleneck limits ceiling performance, and whether the RL training recipe (GRPO + REINFORCE with difficulty-aware rewards) transfers to other MLLM backbones or longer temporal horizons. The newly collected 2.8k-video dataset also fills a gap in training data for long-form video reasoning.
