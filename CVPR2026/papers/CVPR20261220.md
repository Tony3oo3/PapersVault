## Problem

Diffusion policies model multi-modal action distributions that can produce plausible yet suboptimal actions, leading the robot into 'robustness blind spots' from which task failure becomes imminent. Existing approaches lack a mechanism to detect when execution has deviated into such states, recover to a safe configuration, and explicitly avoid re-sampling the same failing action on retry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261220/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-camera RGB images and robot proprioception (end-effector pose, finger positions) at 30 Hz, plus a negative action prompt — the buffered failure trajectory embedded via Fourier features to match the action vector shape. It outputs a corrected robot action sequence for execution and a scalar OOD error signal (autoencoder reconstruction MSE) that is compared against a κ-sigma threshold derived from the historical error distribution to gate the retreat-and-resample loop.

## Method

REACH couples three components: (1) a conditional diffusion policy whose denoising step uses an extended classifier-free guidance formulation (Eq. 6) that simultaneously attracts toward a positive condition and repels from a negative prompt (the previously failed action); (2) a feedforward autoencoder trained exclusively on nominal-execution camera images, whose per-pixel MSE reconstruction error serves as a one-class OOD score with a Gaussian κ-sigma threshold; and (3) a safe retreat maneuver that inverts the buffered action sequence — via temporal reversal in absolute-action space or SE(3) inverse composition in relative-action space — to return the robot to the last in-distribution state. Upon OOD detection, the robot retreats, the failed trajectory is injected as the negative prompt, and the diffusion sampler regenerates an action explicitly dissimilar to the failure, repeating until a nominal execution path is found.

## Results

On the Robomimic Can(Paired) task, REACH with OOD detection (S=4.5) achieves 85.0% success rate, substantially outperforming the standard Diffusion Policy (60.8%) and LSTM-GMM/BC-RNN (62.0%). On real-world tasks, REACH with the error detector improves success rates by 16% (Pick Cup: 74% vs 58%), 20% (Banana in Bowl: 68% vs 48%), and 10% (Stack Bricks: 42% vs 32%) over the baseline Diffusion Policy. On MimicGen tasks, REACH achieves the best or near-best success rates across all four tasks (e.g., 64% on Hammer D1 with 200 demos vs 54% for Diffusion Policy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REACH clearly outperforms the standard Diffusion Policy [7] on the Can(Paired) task (85.0% vs 60.8%, a +24.2 pp gain) and on real-world tasks across all three benchmarks (74/68/42% vs 58/48/32%). Against LSTM-GMM/BC-RNN [35], REACH wins decisively on Can(Paired) (85.0 vs 62.0) and Square(MH) (0.97/0.88 vs 0.44/0.24), confirming that diffusion-based policies with OOD-aware recovery surpass classical imitation baselines. IBC/BC [16] is the weakest baseline, achieving 0% on Can(MH) and Square(MH), while REACH reaches near-perfect scores. On MimicGen tasks [55], REACH matches or exceeds the Equivariant Diffusion Policy baseline (e.g., 64% vs 54% on Hammer D1-200, 31% vs 26% on Threading D2-200), though gains are more modest. Notably, REACH slightly underperforms the plain Diffusion Policy on PushT (0.89/0.87 vs 0.91/0.84), suggesting the OOD-triggered retreat mechanism can be counterproductive on simpler, low-variance tasks where the policy rarely enters OOD states.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based visuomotor policies (Chi et al. 2023; Janner et al. 2022) by importing the negative-prompting mechanism from text-to-image diffusion (Ho & Salimans 2022; Ban et al. 2024) into the action space, conditioning the denoising process to steer away from previously failed action sequences. The error-detection component draws on autoencoder-based novelty detection (inspired by Random Network Distillation, Burda et al. 2019) and reconstruction-error OOD scoring, a well-studied paradigm in anomaly detection. The key conceptual departure is the closed-loop 'retreat-and-replan' mechanism: buffered failure trajectories are repurposed as negative prompts at inference time, requiring no retraining of the policy, which distinguishes REACH from prior recovery methods that rely on separate contingency policies or MPC-based fallbacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REACH addresses a practical and underexplored failure mode of diffusion policies—sampling a plausible but fragile action that leads to an OOD state from which the policy cannot recover—without requiring task-specific retraining or additional reward signals. The main limitations are the reliance on a simple feedforward autoencoder (limited representational capacity under complex visual conditions), the need for task-specific detector training, and the absence of a large-scale real-world RGB benchmark to validate generalizability. Open questions include whether the negative-prompt mechanism scales to higher-dimensional or multi-modal action spaces, whether more expressive OOD detectors (e.g., vision transformers) would improve recovery, and whether the retreat-and-replan loop can be made more efficient than the current one-step rollback.
