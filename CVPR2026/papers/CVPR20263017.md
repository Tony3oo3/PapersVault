## Problem

Existing weakly supervised video anomaly detection methods struggle with two intertwined issues: the absence of frame-level annotations limits the model's ability to capture fine-grained anomaly semantics, and normal versus abnormal behaviors (e.g., taking items vs. stealing) share nearly identical appearance features, differing only in subtle behavioral cues like speed. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing semantic grouping of frames and explicit intention reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed RGB videos annotated only at the video level (binary normal/anomaly flag plus anomaly category index). Frame features are extracted via CLIP ViT-B/16, I3D, or C3D, and an LLM (GPT-4 by default) generates auxiliary text descriptions of each anomaly category's characteristic attributes, which are encoded by the CLIP text encoder. Outputs are frame-level anomaly confidence scores for coarse-grained detection and, for fine-grained detection, anomaly event instances with start/end timestamps, category label, and confidence derived from outer-inner scoring with NMS.

## Method

LAS-VAD processes video features through a local Swin-style transformer followed by a GCN for global temporal modeling, then branches into three scoring paths: cross-modal cosine similarity with concatenated category and LLM-generated attribute text features, a binary classifier, and a multi-class classifier. The Anomaly-Connected Component (ACC) module computes pairwise frame similarity, rectifies it using cross-modal text consistency, thresholds into a binary adjacency matrix, and extracts connected components via DFS to assign frame-level pseudo-labels that supervise the multi-class path. The Intention Awareness Mechanism (IAM) derives position, velocity, and acceleration features from the video representation, maintains momentum-updated intention prototypes per category, and applies a cross-intention contrastive loss (infoNCE) that pulls together hard same-intention pairs while pushing apart easily confusable cross-intention pairs. A MIL aggregation over top-K frames produces video-level scores, and an L1 regularization enforces consistency between the binary and multi-class predictions.

## Results

On UCF-Crime (coarse-grained), LAS-VAD achieves 91.05% AUC with I3D features and 90.86% AUC with CLIP features, outperforming the previous best (π-VAD at 90.33% and LEC-VAD at 89.97%) by 0.72 and 0.89 points respectively. On XD-Violence (coarse-grained), it reaches 89.96% AP with I3D and 87.92% AP with CLIP, surpassing LEC-VAD by 1.49 and 1.36 points. For fine-grained detection, LAS-VAD attains an AVG mAP of 36.89 on XD-Violence (+5% over LEC-VAD) and 15.62 on UCF-Crime (+15.2% over LEC-VAD), demonstrating consistent gains across all evaluation granularities and feature backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAS-VAD outperforms all 33 compared baselines across both datasets and all feature backbones (C3D, I3D, CLIP). On UCF-Crime with I3D features, it surpasses the strongest prior method π-VAD [27] by 0.72 AUC points (91.05 vs. 90.33) and LEC-VAD [44] by 2.84 points. With CLIP features, it beats LEC-VAD [44] by 0.89 points (90.86 vs. 89.97) and ITC [22] by 1.82 points. On XD-Violence, LAS-VAD with I3D (89.96 AP) exceeds PE-MIL [2] (88.21 AP, which uses additional audio) by 1.75 points, and with CLIP (87.92 AP) surpasses LEC-VAD [44] (86.56 AP) by 1.36 points. In fine-grained evaluation, the gains are even more pronounced: LAS-VAD improves AVG mAP by 5% on XD-Violence (36.89 vs. 35.14 for LEC-VAD) and by 15.2% on UCF-Crime (15.62 vs. 13.56), indicating the intention-aware and anomaly-connected component mechanisms are particularly effective at localizing precise anomaly boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAS-VAD builds on the standard WS-VAD pipeline of pre-trained feature extraction (CLIP, I3D, C3D) combined with multiple-instance learning, inheriting the GCN-based global temporal modeling from VadCLIP [57] and the outer-inner scoring from AutoLoc [37]. It departs from prior work by introducing two complementary mechanisms: an anomaly-connected component (ACC) approach that partitions frames into semantic groups via graph connected-component detection (rather than k-means or attention-based clustering), and an intention awareness mechanism (IAM) that derives position/velocity/acceleration features to distinguish behaviorally similar but intentionally different actions. The incorporation of LLM-generated anomaly attribute descriptions as auxiliary cross-modal text features extends the vision-language paradigm used by VadCLIP, ITC, and ReFLIP, adding a fine-grained semantic channel beyond category-level prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness in WS-VAD—the inability to learn fine-grained anomaly semantics without frame-level labels—by providing structured pseudo-labels through ACC and intention-discriminative features through IAM. The 15.2% fine-grained improvement on UCF-Crime and the consistent coarse-grained gains across three feature backbones (C3D, I3D, CLIP) suggest the approach is robust to the choice of visual encoder. However, the method's reliance on LLM-generated attribute descriptions introduces a dependency on external model quality (though ablation shows low sensitivity), and the intention features are derived from learned representations rather than physical motion, raising questions about generalization to anomaly types where speed is not the distinguishing factor. Open questions include scalability to very long videos, performance on open-vocabulary or novel anomaly categories, and whether the ACC grouping quality degrades when anomaly and normal segments are highly visually similar.
