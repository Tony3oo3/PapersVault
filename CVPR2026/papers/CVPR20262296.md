## Problem

Existing prompt-based segmentation refiners (e.g., SAMRefiner, DualSight) rely on fixed, content-agnostic prompt heuristics that are brittle to changing failure modes, noisy input masks, and diverse image characteristics. They cannot adapt prompt generation to the specific scene, leading to semantic ambiguity, harmful intra-prompt interactions, and propagated noise in the refined output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262296/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a coarse binary mask M₀ produced by an arbitrary base predictor (e.g., DeepLabV3, FCN-ResNet-50, RMBG, or DAVIS585 perturbed masks), with no requirement that the predictor be known at inference time. It outputs a refined pixel-level segmentation mask by generating a set of spatially diverse inclusion points, a single exclusion point, a tight bounding box around M₀, and a softly encoded version of M₀, all fed into SAM's prompt encoder. The framework is model-agnostic across SAM backbones (ViT-B through ViT-H) and SAM-HQ.

## Method

PromptMoE introduces a Mixture-of-Experts prompting pipeline with three collaborative modules. Image-Informed Prompting (IIP) computes ten diverse per-pixel guidance maps from complementary cues (color, brightness, texture, distance-to-boundary, monocular depth via Marigold, SAM-based region proposals, etc.). The Dynamic Expert Selector (DES) is a lightweight sparse router with two MLP heads—single-expert utility and pairwise interaction—that selects only the best pair of experts per image-mask pair, fusing their maps via softmax-weighted averaging to avoid signal dilution; it is trained in three stages (utility, interaction, joint fine-tuning) on VOC 2012 with gating-entropy and orthogonality regularizers. The Prompt-Placement Explorer (PPE) iteratively samples high-confidence inclusion points from the fused guidance map while enforcing an adaptive suppression radius (λ-quantile distance) to guarantee spatial diversity and prevent point collapse onto a single subregion, then selects one exclusion point in a low-scoring background region. This sparse routing reduces latency by ~60% relative to dense expert evaluation while improving accuracy.

## Results

PromptMoE achieves a mean ΔIoU/ΔBIoU of +6.24/+8.99 over unrefined base masks across five benchmark datasets (BIG, VOC, DAVIS585, ECSSD, MSRA-B), outperforming all SOTA refiners on four of five datasets. The largest per-dataset gains are on BIG (+8.54/+11.01) and VOC (+7.94/+10.43). Gains over the closest SAM-based competitor, SAMRefiner (+5.40/+8.26), are statistically significant with bootstrap 95% CIs of ΔΔIoU [+0.38, +1.30] and ΔΔBIoU [+0.26, +1.19]. The DES module reduces refinement latency by 59.9% relative to a dense expert mixture while improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptMoE outperforms both CascadePSP variants on all five datasets, with the mean ΔIoU gap reaching +7.64 over CascadePSP-Fast (−0.70) and +4.85 over CascadePSP-Slow (+1.39); CascadePSP-Slow degrades on DAVIS585 and ECSSD while PromptMoE improves on both. Against SegRefiner, PromptMoE wins decisively: SegRefiner-LR and SegRefiner-HR both suffer large negative ΔIoU on DAVIS585, ECSSD, and MSRA-B (as low as −15.34), whereas PromptMoE maintains positive gains everywhere; the sole exception is BIG, where SegRefiner-HR's high-resolution fine-tuning yields +9.55 vs. PromptMoE's +8.54 ΔIoU (a gap of −1.01, not statistically significant). Compared to DualSight, PromptMoE leads on all five datasets by a mean ΔIoU margin of +3.69, with the largest advantage on VOC (+4.65) and ECSSD (+4.00). Against the closest competitor SAMRefiner, PromptMoE achieves consistent per-dataset gains (ranging from +0.31 on DAVIS585 to +1.70 on BIG), with the overall improvement confirmed statistically significant via bootstrap CIs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PromptMoE extends the SAM-based refinement line initiated by SAMRefiner and DualSight, which re-framed segmentation refinement as a prompt-generation problem rather than an architecture-design problem. It departs from both by replacing their fixed, content-agnostic prompting heuristics with a learned Mixture-of-Experts router that selects a sparse subset of ten diverse image-processing experts per image-mask pair. Unlike prior MoE-in-CV work (MoSE, Sparse-MoE-SAM) that inserts expert modules into SAM's decoder or shape dictionary, PromptMoE applies MoE at the prompt-generation level, keeping SAM's architecture entirely unchanged and making the framework model-agnostic across SAM backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that context-aware sparse expert selection at the prompting stage can consistently outperform both fixed-heuristic refiners and dense expert fusion, with a 59.9% latency reduction from DES. A key open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond SAM's mask decoder, which remains a bottleneck for thin/porous structures where SegRefiner-HR still wins. The modest but statistically significant gains over SAMRefiner (+0.84 IoU average) suggest the current expert set and routing strategy may not yet capture the full potential of adaptive prompting, leaving room for richer expert libraries or multi-pair routing.
