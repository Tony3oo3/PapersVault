## Problem

Text-Image Person Re-Identification (TI-ReID) systems are widely deployed in intelligent surveillance but their adversarial robustness remains largely unexplored. Existing attacks from conventional ReID (which perturb attribute-agnostic appearance cues) and vision-language pretraining (which targets coarse image-caption alignment) fail to transfer to TI-ReID, which requires disrupting fine-grained, identity-specific attribute semantics such as clothing, colors, and carried items. This paper presents the first systematic security study of TI-ReID models, quantifying their vulnerability to both untargeted and targeted adversarial attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a person image and an adversarial text prompt (a natural-language description of a different identity) as input and produces an ℓ∞-bounded perturbation (ε = 8/255) that, when added to the image, yields a perturbed image. The perturbation is derived as the residual between a reconstructed image (generated by fusing the image and adversarial prompt in latent space) and the original image, rather than being directly optimized. In untargeted mode the perturbation prevents retrieval by the image's true description; in targeted mode it additionally biases retrieval toward the adversarial prompt's identity.

## Method

R²TUA operates in two phases. In the reconstruction phase, a ViT encodes the image into patch latents and a BERT encoder encodes the adversarial prompt into text latents; these are fused by Transformer-based Gradual Multimodal Fusion (TGMF), a stack of transformer layers where a per-layer cross-modal scaling factor γ_l (decaying by η = 0.9 each layer) controls how strongly adversarial semantics are injected—strong in shallow layers to embed attribute features, weak in deep layers to preserve the person's visual structure. In the residual phase, the Residual Perturbation Generator (RPG) subtracts the reconstructed image from the original, applying a fully differentiable Soft Clamp Function (SCF) that enforces the ℓ∞ bound without blocking gradient flow through out-of-threshold regions, plus an SSIM loss for implicit visual subtlety. Training uses paired batch sampling (swapping descriptions between two image batches so each text serves as both a real description to push away and an adversarial prompt to pull toward), Push-Pull Losses (InfoNCE-based) to corrupt the contrastive align stage, and Matching Losses (cross-entropy on a cross-encoder re-ranker with hard negatives) to corrupt the fusion stage, combined as L = α·L_PPLs + (2−α)·L_MtLs.

## Results

On CUHK-PEDES, R²TUA reduces untargeted R@1 to 0.11% (mAP 0.60%) against RaSa, 0.54% (mAP 2.13%) against APTM, and 0.58% (mAP 1.07%) against IRRA, compared to clean performance of 76.51%/76.53%/73.38% R@1 respectively. For targeted attacks on RaSa (CUHK-PEDES), R²TUA achieves 84.10% R@1 and 52.16% mAP, far exceeding the only other targeted baseline AnyAttack (31.26% R@1, 14.35% mAP). On the more challenging ICFG-PEDES dataset, R²TUA still reduces untargeted R@1 to 0.15%/2.51%/4.31% across the three models. In black-box transfer settings (ε=16/255), R²TUA achieves 5.70% untargeted R@1 and 55.43% targeted R@1 on RaSa, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²TUA dominates all seven baselines by large margins across every dataset and model. Against the strongest untargeted baselines on RaSa (CUHK-PEDES), R²TUA achieves 0.11% R@1 versus 8.48% (AnyAttack), 8.61% (MetaAttack), and 10.20% (VLPTAttack) — a 76–99× reduction. The vision-language attacks (SGA, VLPTAttack, MFHA, AnyAttack) and ReID-specific attacks (MisRanking, MetaAttack, MTGA) all fail to drop R@1 below 6.69%, confirming that neither modality-agnostic ReID perturbations nor coarse VLP attacks can effectively disrupt attribute-level TI-ReID matching. For targeted attacks, R²TUA achieves 84.10% R@1 on RaSa versus 31.26% for AnyAttack (the only other targeted-capable baseline), a 2.7× improvement, and 75.50% on IRRA versus 12.39% for AnyAttack. In black-box transfer (Table 3), R²TUA at ε=16/255 achieves 5.70% untargeted R@1 and 55.43% targeted R@1 on RaSa, outperforming AnyAttack (45.31%/3.74%) and MTGA (16.86%/—) by wide margins, and remains competitive even at the stricter ε=8/255 setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established attack lineages—ReID adversarial attacks (MisRanking, MetaAttack, MTGA) and vision-language model attacks (SGA, VLPTAttack, MFHA, AnyAttack)—while demonstrating that neither transfers effectively to the TI-ReID setting due to the fine-grained, attribute-specific nature of text-image person retrieval. Methodologically, it borrows the reconstruction-residual perturbation strategy from diffusion-model adversarial attacks and the ALBEF-style cross-attention fusion architecture, but repurposes both for a novel multimodal attack objective: simultaneously pushing a perturbed image away from its true description and pulling it toward an adversarial one. The TGMF module and SCF are new architectural contributions that address the specific challenge of injecting identity-level attribute semantics while preserving visual structure, a requirement absent from both prior ReID and VLP attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first systematic security study of TI-ReID, this work reveals that mainstream deployment-ready models (RaSa, APTM, IRRA) are catastrophically vulnerable, with untargeted attacks reducing R@1 to below 1%—a level of fragility that has direct implications for surveillance and missing-person search systems. The counterintuitive finding that targeted attacks can *exceed* unattacked retrieval performance (89.4% vs. 76.5% R@1 on RaSa) suggests the reconstruction-residual mechanism exploits a representational shortcut that standard TI-ReID training never guards against, pointing to a fundamental architectural weakness rather than a surface-level vulnerability. Open questions include whether lightweight defenses (e.g., adversarial training, input purification) can close this gap without degrading retrieval accuracy, and whether the vulnerability generalizes to other cross-modal retrieval tasks beyond person ReID.
