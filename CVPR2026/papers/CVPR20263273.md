## Problem

Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for video understanding prioritize high-level static semantics and macro-event logic, leaving fine-grained motion details between frames poorly captured. Video Diffusion Models (VDMs), by contrast, inherently model temporal dynamics through their denoising objective, yet their motion knowledge is locked inside a generative paradigm inaccessible to discriminative VLMs. The paper asks how to transfer VDM motion priors into a VLM without extra parameters, architectural changes, or access to the VDM's training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263273/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a video (sampled as key frames) and a text question, producing a text answer in the standard VLM format. During training, an additional offline step runs 5-step DDIM inversion on a frozen CogVideoX-1.5-5B VDM to extract text-to-vision attention maps for each video, which are then filtered and used as auxiliary supervision. The VDM attention extraction is fully decoupled from VLM training (20–30 s per video on an A100) and the resulting priors can be cached and reused across different VLM backbones and ablation runs.

## Method

MotionEnhancer distills motion priors from a frozen VDM and aligns them with the VLM's text-to-vision attention during supervised fine-tuning. Two parameter-free modules refine the raw VDM attention: Motion-sensitive Head Selection (MHS) scores each transformer head via Diagonal Focus Coefficient, Temporal Continuity Score, and Diagonal Saliency Ratio to keep the top 50% motion-relevant heads, and Motion-salient Text Token Identification (MTTI) ranks text tokens by a motion score combining mean frame-level attention and mean first-order temporal difference to retain the top 50% dynamic tokens. The filtered VDM attention (A_VDM) is then matched to the VLM's pooled text-to-vision attention (A_VLM) through a 3-layer MLP aligner with an L2 (MSE) loss. The total training objective is the standard autoregressive cross-entropy plus a weighted MSE alignment term (λ=1), optimized end-to-end on the VLM's vision tower, merger, and LLM backbone. No new trainable parameters are introduced beyond the small aligner MLP, and no architectural modifications to the VLM are required.

## Results

On MotionBench, MotionEnhancer improves Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 52.81 to 57.04 overall accuracy (+4.23) and from 48.29 to 52.92 average accuracy (+4.63), with the largest gains in Camera Motion (CM: +11.7%) and Motion Recognition (MR: +4.4%). On FAVOR-Bench, Qwen2.5-VL-3B+MotionEnhancer achieves 44.53 overall accuracy (+7.10 over baseline), and InternVL3-8B+MotionEnhancer reaches 48.94 overall, surpassing the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (48.14). Notably, Qwen2.5-VL-3B+MotionEnhancer (56.60 on MotionBench, 44.53 on FAVOR-Bench) outperforms the unenhanced Qwen2.5-VL-7B on both benchmarks, demonstrating that motion priors from a VDM can effectively close the gap between small and large VLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionEnhancer consistently outperforms all directly comparable baselines. Against Qwen2.5-VL (3B/7B/72B) and InternVL3 (2B/8B), it improves overall accuracy by 1.5–4.2 points on MotionBench and 3.1–7.1 points on FAVOR-Bench, with the largest relative gains on smaller models. Compared to MotionSight (the closest motion-focused competitor), MotionEnhancer achieves higher overall scores on both benchmarks (57.04 vs. 55.30 on MotionBench; 46.88 vs. 45.47 on FAVOR-Bench for the 7B Qwen2.5-VL backbone), particularly excelling in Motion Recognition and Camera Motion. Against larger or differently-architected baselines—MiniCPM-V2.6-7B (52), PLLaVA-34B (52), VideoLLaMA3-7B (41.46), LLaVA-Video-7B (38.60), and VideoChat-Flash-7B (43.82) on FAVOR-Bench—MotionEnhancer-enhanced models achieve substantially higher scores. The only baseline that exceeds MotionEnhancer on MotionBench is GLM4-9B+TE Fusion (58 overall), though this method uses a different architecture and is not directly comparable. On FAVOR-Bench, InternVL3-8B+MotionEnhancer (48.94) surpasses even Qwen2.5-VL-72B (48.14), suggesting that motion-prior alignment can compensate for model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-guided VLM paradigm from 2D to video, building most directly on Lavender (which aligns VLM attention with Stable Diffusion for image understanding) and on prior work showing VDMs capture spatiotemporal dynamics during denoising. It departs from prior diffusion-guidance methods (DIVA, GenHancer) by targeting motion-specific attention rather than general visual expertise, and introduces two parameter-free extraction modules (MHS, MTTI) inspired by SparseVideoGen's observations on temporal attention structure in DiT-based VDMs. The theoretical contribution frames the gap as a distributional mismatch: VLMs learn p(t|V) while motion understanding requires p(V|t), which VDM cross-attention naturally approximates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MotionEnhancer offers a practical, parameter-free recipe for improving video VLM motion understanding without architectural changes or additional training data, making it broadly applicable across VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) and VDMs (CogVideoX). The demonstrated ability of a 3B+MotionEnhancer model to surpass a 7B baseline on both benchmarks suggests that targeted motion priors can partially substitute for scale. Key limitations include VDM attention becoming diffuse for large static subjects (a training-data bias) and the method's dependence on a specific VDM's quality; open questions include whether motion latents extracted from VDMs can serve as pretraining signals for downstream temporal tasks like robotic manipulation.
