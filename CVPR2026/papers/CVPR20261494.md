## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving frameworks, particularly RL-based ones, struggle in sparse-critical scenarios where rare but safety-sensitive events (traffic light violations, pedestrian crossings, obstacle avoidance) occur infrequently and provide sparse reward signals. Existing VLM-assisted RL methods generate semantic guidance but do not efficiently target these rare scenarios, and the high inference cost of VLMs makes dense expert experience generation impractical.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a single front-camera RGB image, a vehicle kinematic vector (speed, throttle, steering angle), traffic-light state (distance and color), and a waypoint sequence, and outputs continuous throttle and steering-angle commands. Notably, the framework requires only one monocular camera (no LiDAR or multi-view inputs), and the VLM expert reuses the same RGB frame from the RL replay buffer for semantic reasoning, with the StateHash algorithm gating whether full VLM inference is triggered based on DCT-based perceptual image similarity and weighted vehicle-state similarity.

## Method

EE-RL employs an explorer-expert collaborative paradigm: an actor-critic RL agent (with spatial attention for local image features and self-attention for cross-modal fusion) interacts with the environment, while two LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-32B experts (INT4-quantized, fixed chain-of-thought) generate semantic rewards for sparse-critical scenarios. A dual replay buffer stores experiences from both the RL agent and the VLM experts, with a configurable sampling ratio (optimal at ~19–24% expert experience depending on the scenario) to balance exploration and semantic guidance. The StateHash algorithm computes a weighted combination of per-channel DCT perceptual hash similarity (0.7 weight) and normalized vehicle-state similarity (0.3 weight) to skip redundant VLM calls, increasing expert experience generation from ~8K to ~54K (single VLM) or ~91K (dual VLM) over 1M training steps. Training proceeds in two phases—regular driving followed by sparse-critical scenarios—with the total reward combining rule-based and VLM expert components, normalized to a common scale.

## Results

On the CARLA Leaderboard testing benchmark (Towns 05–06, unseen during training), the EE-RL SAC variant achieves a composite score (CS) of 80.09, surpassing the strongest baseline VLM-RL (CS 65.50) by 22.27%. In the most challenging Town03 during training, EE-RL improves driving score (DS) by +19.82% and infraction score (IS) by +20.98% over VLM-RL. The TD3-based variant achieves 0% accident probability in both static obstacle and red-light running sparse-critical scenarios across 50 evaluation routes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EE-RL (SAC) decisively outperforms all ten baselines on the unseen Town05–06 evaluation, achieving CS 80.09 versus VLM-RL's 65.50 (+22.27%), Revolve's 51.82, and VLM-RM's 47.87. Against the closest competitor VLM-RL, EE-RL improves IS by +20.62% in Town05 and +21.60% in Town06, while maintaining comparable or lower CoR (4.25 vs. 5.58 in Town05). On the Town05 long-route benchmark, EE-RL (TD3) surpasses InterFuser (the strongest IL baseline) by +4.36% IS and +5.54% DS while reducing CoR from 4.35 to 4.20. In sparse-critical scenario evaluation, EE-RL (TD3) achieves 0% accident probability for red-light running and static obstacles, compared to 35% and 1% for VLM-RL and 10% and 1% for Roach, respectively. The DDPG variant of EE-RL consistently underperforms the TD3 and SAC variants, confirming that twin Q-networks (TD3) and maximum entropy (SAC) are better suited for continuous driving control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-assisted RL line for end-to-end driving (VLM-RM, VLM-RL, RL-VLM-F, Revolve) by repurposing the VLM from a reward annotator into a full experience-generating expert whose trajectories are stored in a dedicated replay buffer alongside the RL agent's own experiences. The dual replay buffer with a tunable sampling ratio, the StateHash mechanism for skipping redundant VLM inference via DCT-based perceptual hashing and kinematic similarity, and the two-phase training schedule (regular → sparse-critical) are the main architectural departures from prior single-buffer, single-VLM designs. The actor-critic backbone with multi-level spatial and self-attention follows standard end-to-end RL practice, while the LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-VL-32B with a fixed chain-of-thought template is a practical adaptation rather than a conceptual novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a concrete recipe for addressing the sparse-critical scenario bottleneck in end-to-end driving—traffic lights, pedestrian crossings, static obstacles—without requiring hand-crafted scenario generators or massive data collection, which is a practical gap in current CARLA-based research. The 0% red-light running rate and ~20% driving-score gains over the strongest VLM-RL baseline are encouraging, but all results are confined to CARLA simulation, and the authors explicitly flag sim-to-real transfer as an unresolved challenge. Open questions include whether the StateHash threshold and dual-buffer sampling ratio generalize across real-world sensor noise, whether the 32B VLM expert can be replaced by a smaller model without degrading sparse-critical performance, and how the framework scales to multi-vehicle or highway settings beyond the current single-ego-vehicle CARLA towns.
