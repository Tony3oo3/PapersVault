## Problem

Existing incremental object detection benchmarks assume all tasks come from a single domain, which oversimplifies real-world deployment where both novel categories and substantial domain shifts (e.g., natural scenes → remote sensing → underwater) occur simultaneously. Under this more realistic cross-domain setting, VLM-based detectors suffer severe catastrophic forgetting and fail to balance stability (retaining prior knowledge) with adaptivity (learning new out-of-distribution tasks), a gap that existing single-domain IOD methods cannot address.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and text class prompts as input, producing bounding boxes with class labels via the phrase grounding paradigm of Grounding DINO-T. At inference, the model performs group-wise routing: it extracts feature statistics from the test image, computes a Gaussian approximation, and selects the most similar adapter group via minimum KL divergence, activating only that group's consolidated adapter across all enhancer layers. This replaces the task-ID-based routing used by prior PEFT methods, eliminating per-task routing errors under cross-domain conditions.

## Method

DGS (Dynamic Group Subspace) organizes incremental tasks into dynamically formed groups and constructs evolving group-wise subspaces through three components. Dynamic Task Grouping (DTG) estimates each task's feature distribution as a Gaussian and assigns it to the nearest existing group (via minimum KL divergence) or creates a new group if no group is sufficiently similar (threshold τ). Incremental Group Adapters (IGA) maintain LoRA adapters in the FFNs of both text and image branches within enhancer layers, one per task within each group. Intra-Group Consolidation (IGC) merges each newly trained task adapter into the group's base adapter via weighted averaging (λ=0.2), with two stabilization mechanisms: the new adapter is initialized from the base adapter (group initialization) and trained over the entire group's class space using pseudo labels from the base adapter plus a topology-based KD loss (group alignment). A dynamic training pipeline branches on group membership: new-group tasks train without constraints for adaptivity, while existing-group tasks incorporate the KD regularization for stability.

## Results

On the proposed CDIOD benchmark, DGS achieves 64.7±0.6 AP (0-10, 5 phases) and 60.2±0.2 AP (0-5, 10 phases), surpassing the prior best MR-GDINO by +8.5 and +11.4 AP respectively, while using only 1.2% additional parameters. On conventional IOD (COCO 40-40), DGS reaches 55.3 AP on all classes, and on IVLOD (ODinW-13) it achieves 60.9 average AP, improving +1.2 AP over the prior SOTA (ZiRa) while retaining 46.4 AP zero-shot on COCO (only 1.0 AP below the zero-shot upper bound of 47.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary CDIOD benchmark, DGS decisively outperforms all nine baselines. The closest competitor, MR-GDINO (task-specific adapters with task-ID routing), achieves 56.2 AP (5 phases) and 48.8 AP (10 phases), trailing DGS by 8.5 and 11.4 AP respectively; the gap widens with more phases due to accumulated routing errors. GCD (global KD-based paradigm) reaches 51.0/43.7 AP, showing that updating the full base model causes severe cross-domain forgetting. PEFT-based methods (Zira, MD-DETR) maintain better stability on in-distribution PascalVOC but suffer large drops on out-of-distribution DIOR (27.5–36.8 AP vs. DGS's 58.8–63.2). On conventional IOD (COCO), DGS matches or slightly exceeds fine-tuning (55.3 vs. 55.2 AP, 40-40) and outperforms MR-GDINO (49.1 AP) by 6.2 AP, confirming gains are not limited to cross-domain settings. On IVLOD, DGS improves ODinW-13 average by 1.2 AP over ZiRa (60.9 vs. 59.7) and tops 10 of 13 sub-datasets, though it drops 1.0 AP on zero-shot COCO relative to the untrained model (46.4 vs. 47.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends incremental object detection (IOD) from the single-domain assumption to a cross-domain setting (CDIOD), where both categories and visual domains shift across phases. Methodologically, it departs from both the global KD paradigm (GCD) that updates the base model and the local PEFT paradigm (Zira, MR-GDINO) that isolates each task into a separate adapter with task-ID routing. Instead, DGS introduces a middle-ground group-based formulation: tasks are dynamically clustered by distributional similarity (KL divergence), and LoRA adapters within each group are progressively merged via a consolidation step, drawing on model-merging literature to control parameter growth while preserving shared semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CDIOD benchmark exposes a practical gap: VLM-based detectors that perform well on single-domain IOD degrade sharply when domains shift, and the task-ID routing mechanism that underpins existing PEFT-based IOD methods becomes a critical failure point under cross-domain conditions. The +11.4 AP gain with only 1.2% extra parameters demonstrates that group-level knowledge sharing can substantially outperform both full fine-tuning and isolated task adapters. A key limitation is the reliance on accurate task distribution estimation from limited data, which may lead to suboptimal grouping; scaling to many more domains or very short task streams remains an open question.
