## Problem

Compressing the 576 visual patch tokens in VLMs to a single token via continuous methods (e.g., VoCo-LLaMA) dilutes high-level semantics such as object identity, while discrete quantization preserves categorical structure but erases fine-grained details like texture and pose. The paper asks whether a single-token representation can simultaneously retain both semantic and detail information, and identifies that a purely continuous one-token bottleneck is structurally incapable of encoding both.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263705/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14 into 576 patch embeddings and by MGVQ into a 14112-dim quantized feature vector) plus a text instruction. The image is consumed through two parallel pathways before fusion, and the entire 580-token hybrid visual sequence is compressed into a single trainable <voco> token that the LLM attends to. The output is a standard autoregressive textual response, with the visual side reduced from 576 tokens to 1 (a 580-to-1 ratio including the 4 discrete anchors).

## Method

HTC-VLM disentangles visual information into a continuous channel (576 patch embeddings from CLIP ViT-L/14 projected to 4096-dim) carrying fine-grained details and a discrete channel (4 anchor tokens produced by MGVQ multi-group vector quantization, projected through a 2-layer GELU MLP) carrying high-level semantics. The 4 discrete tokens are prepended to the 576 continuous tokens to form a 580-token hybrid sequence, which is then compressed into a single <voco> latent under a disentanglement attention mask that blocks text from attending directly to visual tokens and suppresses self-attention within the visual block, forcing all visual information to flow through the <voco> bottleneck. Training uses standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss, with the mask redirecting gradients so that the discrete anchors maximize semantic mutual information while the continuous patches preserve detail variance. Ablations confirm that 4 discrete tokens is the optimal count (1→83.9%, 4→87.2%, 8→84.6%) and that pre-fusion (discrete tokens first) outperforms post-fusion or mean fusion, yielding 87.2% average performance retention across seven benchmarks versus 81.0% for the best continuous baseline.

## Results

HTC-VLM achieves an average performance retention of 87.2% across seven benchmarks (GQA, VQAv2, MMBench, MME, POPE, SEED-Bench, ScienceQA-Image) under a 580-to-1 compression ratio (576 ViT patches + 4 discrete tokens → 1 <voco> token), outperforming the strongest continuous baseline VoCo-LLaMA (81.0%) by 6.2 percentage points. Per-benchmark, HTC-VLM reaches 92.9% retention on POPE, 90.4% on MMBench, and 120.7% on ScienceQA-Image (exceeding the uncompressed upper bound). At a 64-token budget, HTC-VLM retains 89.8% average performance, surpassing all competing sparsification methods (SparseVLM 89.3%, VoCo-LLaMA 89.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HTC-VLM outperforms all seven baselines on the primary 1-token compression setting. Against VoCo-LLaMA (the strongest continuous-compression baseline at 81.0%), HTC-VLM gains 6.2 points in average retention, with the largest per-benchmark improvements on MMBench (+5.0 pts), POPE (+4.2 pts), and SEED-Bench (+4.7 pts). Against Q-Former (57.2%) and Avg. Pool (64.1%), the gap is even wider (30.0 and 23.1 points respectively), confirming that attention-based or pooling-based continuous compression suffers severe semantic dilution at extreme compression ratios. At the 64-token budget, HTC-VLM (89.8%) narrowly edges out VoCo-LLaMA (89.6%) and SparseVLM (89.3%), while at 192 and 128 tokens it trails SparseVLM (99.1% / 96.7%) and PDrop (95.9% / 94.3%) by 1–3 points, indicating the hybrid design's advantage is most pronounced under the most aggressive compression. The ablation further shows that removing the discrete channel drops retention to 81.0% (continuous-only) or 33.3% (discrete-only), validating that the hybrid combination is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VoCo-LLaMA (CVPR 2025), which compresses 576 ViT patch tokens into a single <voco> latent, by prepending four discrete semantic tokens from MGVQ multi-group vector quantization before compression, forming a 580-token hybrid sequence. Conceptually, it departs from the continuous-only compression lineage (Q-Former, Avg Pool, VoCo-LLaMA) and the discrete-only lineage (VQ-VAE, MoVQ) by arguing that a single continuous bottleneck cannot simultaneously preserve high-level semantics and fine-grained appearance, and that explicit channel separation prior to compression resolves this. The disentanglement attention mask and variational-inference framing borrow from information-bottleneck theory, but the practical mechanism—concatenating a small set of quantized anchors with continuous patches—is a relatively lightweight architectural addition rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 580-to-1 compression ratio with 87.2% average retention establishes a practical ceiling for extreme visual token compression and offers a reusable design principle: inject a minimal set of discrete semantic anchors before any bottleneck to prevent semantic collapse. However, the discrete-only ablation (33.3% retention) reveals that the discrete channel is nearly useless in isolation, meaning the gains depend heavily on the continuous channel carrying most of the load, which somewhat weakens the 'equal partnership' narrative. Open questions include whether the four-token sweet spot generalizes across architectures and image resolutions, whether jointly learning the VQ tokenizer with the VLM (rather than using a frozen MGVQ) would improve adaptability, and whether the approach extends to multi-image or video settings where temporal structure interacts with the hybrid token design.
