## Problem

Multimodal models can correctly understand complex prompts (counting, spatial relations, color composition) yet fail to faithfully realize that understanding in generated images, revealing an architectural decoupling between comprehension and generation. Existing remedies—post-training fine-tuning, post-hoc refinement, and training-free heuristics—are respectively expensive, non-real-time, or semantically opaque. The paper asks whether a model's own understanding can be repurposed as a lightweight, interpretable, real-time correction signal during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261953/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces a single RGB image; for editing, it takes a reference image plus a text prompt and produces a modified image. Internally, the prompt is encoded into a latent code z₀ by the frozen backbone's encoder, the corrector outputs a residual tensor of the same spatial shape (e.g., [1, 16, 128, 128] for OmniGen2), and the decoder renders the final image. At inference, only one forward pass through the corrector is needed—no reward computation, no additional sampling steps, and no backbone modification—so runtime matches the base generator.

## Method

xLARD inserts a lightweight residual corrector Δ_θ into the latent space of a frozen text-to-image backbone, computing z_c = z₀ + α·Δ_θ(z₀, e_p) before decoding. A Conception Misalignment Detection module decomposes image-prompt consistency into three interpretable sub-rewards—counting (connected-component analysis on token attention maps), color (patch-to-color-embedding cosine similarity), and position (sigmoid directional consistency between attention-weighted centroids)—whose weights are dynamically modulated by a confidence head rather than fixed hyperparameters. Because the decoding step is non-differentiable, a learnable reward projector R_φ maps latent activations and the prompt embedding to approximate these image-level rewards in a differentiable form, enabling gradient-based policy updates. The corrector is trained with PPO to maximize expected latent reward, requiring fewer than 50 M trainable parameters (<1% of the base model). The design is plug-and-play: the backbone remains frozen, the corrector transfers across diffusion and autoregressive architectures, and the decomposed reward dimensions plus Latent Activation Maps provide intrinsic interpretability of what and why each correction is made.

## Results

xLARD achieves the best scores on both primary benchmarks: 86.45 on DPG-Bench and 0.81 on GenEval, outperforming all compared baselines. The largest DPG-Bench gains are over Show-O (+19.18) and OmniGen (+5.29), while on GenEval the most notable improvements are over Emu3 (+0.27) and FLUX-dev/OmniGen/Show-O (+0.13 each). Against the strongest baselines (BAGEL, Janus-pro, UniWorld-V1), xLARD still leads by +2.26 to +5.07 on DPG-Bench and +0.01 to +0.02 on GenEval. The method also improves image editing, raising the overall ImgEdit/GEdit score from 4.46 (OmniGen2) to 5.52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

xLARD outperforms all seven baselines on both DPG-Bench and GenEval. Against the closest competitors—BAGEL (84.07/0.79), Janus-pro (84.19/0.80), and UniWorld-V1 (81.38/0.80)—xLARD leads by +2.26 to +5.07 on DPG-Bench and +0.01 to +0.02 on GenEval. The margin is largest over Show-O (67.27/0.68), where xLARD gains +19.18 on DPG-Bench and +0.13 on GenEval, and over Emu3 (80.60/0.54), with +5.85 and +0.27 respectively. Against OmniGen2 (83.48/0.77), the direct plug-and-play backbone used in the editing experiments, xLARD improves DPG-Bench by +2.97 and GenEval by +0.04. On the editing benchmarks (ImgEdit/GEdit), xLARD raises the overall score from 4.46 to 5.52 over OmniGen2, with the largest gain in the PQ sub-metric (5.53→6.77). No baseline is matched or beaten by xLARD on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

xLARD positions itself against three existing paradigms for improving T2I alignment—post-training fine-tuning (e.g., HermesFlow, UniRL), post-hoc consistency checks, and training-free heuristics—by introducing a lightweight residual corrector that operates entirely in latent space without modifying the frozen backbone. Conceptually, it repurposes PPO-based reinforcement learning and the model's own multimodal understanding as a structured, decomposed reward signal (count, color, position), bridging the gap between non-differentiable image-level evaluation and differentiable latent-space optimization via a learnable reward projector. This departs from CLIP-guided optimization and classifier-free guidance, which steer generation but often degrade visual quality, and from residual adapters that typically require large-scale retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (<50M trainable parameters, ~1% of base model) offering comparable or superior gains to post-training methods that update hundreds of millions of parameters is practically significant for practitioners who cannot afford full retraining. The decomposed, interpretable reward structure and latent activation maps provide a diagnostic tool for understanding where and why generation fails, which is rare in current T2I pipelines. However, limitations remain: the reward functions may not capture aesthetic or cultural nuances, evaluation is restricted to English prompts and standard benchmarks, and the interpretability signals reflect statistical trends rather than exact causality. Open questions include whether this latent-correction paradigm extends to video generation, multilingual settings, or dynamic compositional tasks.
