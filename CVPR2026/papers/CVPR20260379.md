## Problem

Existing 3D stylization methods either transfer only local texture/appearance attributes or rely on text prompts that are too ambiguous to capture holistic geometric style such as distinctive silhouettes, poses, and structural characteristics. No prior approach can transfer image-derived geometric style to a 3D mesh while preserving its valid manifold topology, part-level semantics, and compatibility with downstream assets like UV maps and motion rigs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260379/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source 3D mesh (typically 2k–20k vertices) and 4–12 reference style images, with an optional text prompt for simultaneous content manipulation. It produces a deformed mesh that retains the original topology and part semantics while incorporating the geometric style of the references. The deformation is parameterized by per-face Jacobians rather than vertex positions, and users can optionally restrict stylization to user-selected semantic parts or enforce symmetry.

## Method

Style is extracted by fine-tuning a LoRA adapter (rank 16) on the reference images via DreamBooth, producing a compact style-specific weight for a latent diffusion model (SDXL). Mesh deformation is driven by Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss, but critically uses an approximated VAE encoder—a 4×4 affine matrix fitted by least squares on 500 rendered image-latent pairs—that replaces the full SDXL VAE to enable stable gradient propagation. The deformation uses per-face Jacobians (from Neural Jacobian Fields) solved via a Poisson equation, and a coarse-to-fine pipeline first optimizes per-part OBB cage parameters (scale, rotation, translation) on an auxiliary sphere mesh for large-scale structural changes, then gradually shifts to fine-grained Jacobian optimization. Cage-coefficient regularization and an optional symmetry loss (midpoint-plane and direction-orthogonality terms) maintain semantic coherence and internal consistency throughout.

## Results

The paper evaluates its method primarily through a perceptual user study with 32 participants ranking outputs on three criteria: geometry alignment, content preservation, and aesthetic style transfer. The proposed method achieves the best perceptual ranking in both geometry alignment and aesthetic style transfer across 8 test samples. No standard numerical metrics (e.g., FID, Chamfer distance) are reported; evaluation is qualitative and preference-based. The method also outperforms baselines in qualitative comparisons, producing expressive global geometric deformations while preserving source mesh semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: Paparazzi [34], Neural 3D Mesh Renderer [19], MeshUp [20], Text2Mesh [40], and TextDeformer [8]. Paparazzi and Neural 3D Mesh Renderer perform texture-oriented style transfer and fail to induce meaningful geometric deformation, producing only local shape variations. Text2Mesh produces noisy artifacts due to direct vertex-coordinate optimization without structured per-face Jacobians. TextDeformer, while using Jacobian-based deformation, relies on CLIP text guidance and fails to capture complex geometric styles. MeshUp uses SDS loss but with a pixel-level diffusion model (DeepFloyd-IF) rather than SDXL and lacks the approximated VAE encoder and cage regularization, yielding suboptimal results. The proposed method wins on geometry alignment and aesthetic style transfer in the user study, while baselines that produce minimal deformation (e.g., Paparazzi) may score higher on content preservation trivially, since they remain close to the source mesh without reflecting the desired geometric style.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established threads: DreamBooth-style subject/style extraction via LoRA fine-tuning, Score Distillation Sampling for 3D optimization, and Neural Jacobian Fields for mesh deformation. It departs from prior 3D stylization work (Paparazzi, Text2Mesh, TextDeformer, MeshUp) in two key ways: it reframes 'style' as multi-scale geometric features (silhouettes, poses, structural rigidity) rather than high-frequency surface textures, and it uses reference images via LoRA as the style specification mechanism instead of text prompts. The coarse-to-fine pipeline with cage-based regularization and the approximated VAE encoder are novel engineering contributions that stabilize the optimization in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical pathway for artists to create distinctive 3D assets from visual references while preserving mesh topology, UV maps, and motion rig compatibility—capabilities that volumetric or point-cloud approaches lack. The finding that a simple 4×4 affine approximation of the SDXL VAE encoder is essential for stable gradient-based geometry optimization (without which even text-guided deformation fails) is a practically important insight for the broader SDS-based 3D generation community. Open questions include scalability to complex multi-object scenes, real-time or near-real-time interaction, and whether the approach generalizes to non-manifold or open surfaces.
