## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic 3D smoke from a single video is severely ill-posed due to the lack of multi-view coverage, and existing approaches either rely on time-consuming per-scene optimization (e.g., GlobTrans requiring >30 hours) or use diffusion-based novel view synthesis that suffers from poor cross-view consistency and insufficient physical priors for complex smoke dynamics. The paper tackles the gap between generative quality and physical fidelity in single-view fluid reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261625/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single front-view smoke video (T frames) as input and progressively produces up to 16 novel-view images around the horizontal plane, a 3D density field at 64×112×64 resolution, a velocity field, and dynamic inflow states. The side view (90°) is synthesized first via diffusion, then remaining views are rendered and refined in a near-to-far angular progression. The output density and velocity fields support re-simulation and artist-driven control of the reconstructed smoke.

## Method

SmokeSVD is a progressive multi-stage pipeline that cyclically alternates 2D diffusion synthesis, spatio-temporal refinement, and 3D reconstruction. First, a physically-guided side-view synthesizer (SvDiff) generates 90° views frame-by-frame using a multi-frame training scheme where previously synthesized frames feed back as conditions, with velocity divergence, spatial distribution, and visual fidelity losses applied during low-noise denoising steps. Second, a coarse UNet3+ density generator estimates a rough 3D volume from the available views, which is then used to render novel views at progressively farther angles (near → mid → far); a refinement module (NvRef, also UNet3+-based) enhances each rendered view using adjacent-view context and temporal downsampling, with residual and spatial constraints. Finally, a fine-grained density generator jointly reconstructs high-quality density, velocity, and inflow fields via differentiable advection grounded in the Navier-Stokes equations, ensuring long-term physical consistency.

## Results

On the ScalarFlow dataset, SmokeSVD achieves an input-view PSNR of 38.08 dB and LPIPS of 0.0223, second only to GlobTrans (40.16 dB, 0.0054) but completing 120 reconstruction steps in 15 minutes versus GlobTrans's >30 hours. On the synthetic dataset, the method reaches 28.13 dB PSNR and 0.0293 LPIPS on input views, substantially outperforming NGT (15.65 dB), PICT (16.30 dB), and PINF (13.30 dB). Against FluidNexus, SmokeSVD improves input-view PSNR from 27.83–30.82 dB to 35.55 dB while maintaining competitive novel-view quality without sensitivity to post-processing thresholds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GlobTrans [8], SmokeSVD trades a small gap in input-view fidelity (PSNR 38.08 vs 40.16, LPIPS 0.0223 vs 0.0054) and side-view RMSE (0.0853 vs 0.0352) for a >120× speedup (15 min vs >30 h) and a lower STYLE score (0.2071 vs 0.2167). Compared to NGT [9], the method wins on all input-view metrics (RMSE 0.0127 vs 0.0289, PSNR 38.08 vs 31.07) and STYLE, but NGT achieves a lower side-view RMSE (0.0544 vs 0.0853) and avoids artifacts at 90°. Against PICT [39] and PINF [5], SmokeSVD dominates on every input-view metric and STYLE, though PICT edges out on side-view RMSE (0.0743 vs 0.0853). Versus FluidNexus [10], SmokeSVD substantially improves input-view quality (PSNR 35.55 vs 27.83–30.82 across thresholds) and avoids threshold sensitivity, but FluidNexus's multi-view diffusion yields slightly better novel-view SSIM (up to 0.8435 vs 0.7871) and PSNR (up to 25.36 vs 23.44). Against NeuSmoke [27], SmokeSVD wins on all four metrics (RMSE 0.0331 vs 0.0514, PSNR 30.04 vs 26.50, LPIPS 0.0991 vs 0.1131), confirming that explicit side-view synthesis and progressive refinement outperform implicit neural rendering from sparse views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: physics-informed fluid reconstruction (GlobTrans, PICT, PINF, NGT) and diffusion-based novel view synthesis (Zero-1-to-3, MVDream, FluidNexus). It departs from FluidNexus's 'generate-then-reconstruct' pipeline by introducing a progressive multi-stage strategy that cyclically alternates 2D diffusion synthesis, spatio-temporal refinement via UNet3+, and coarse-to-fine 3D density reconstruction, thereby exploiting volumetric consistency to guide generation rather than relying on it only after the fact. The physically-aware side-view synthesizer (SvDiff) is a notable departure from prior diffusion-based NVS, which typically lacks explicit velocity-field and incompressibility constraints during denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Single-view smoke reconstruction is practically important because non-specialists cannot deploy multi-view capture rigs, and the 15-minute runtime (vs. >30 hours for GlobTrans) makes the approach accessible for downstream applications like re-simulation and artist-driven control. However, the method is limited to horizontal view rotation, smoke-specific physics, and evaluation on a single real-world dataset (ScalarFlow) plus synthetic data, leaving open whether the progressive strategy generalizes to other participating media, vertical view configurations, or higher-order physical constraints. The reliance on a pre-trained density generator for velocity estimation during SvDiff training also introduces a circular dependency that may limit robustness on out-of-distribution inputs.
