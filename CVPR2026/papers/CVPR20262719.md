## Problem

Existing BIQA methods uniformly fuse shallow and deep backbone features, yet the paper demonstrates through controlled experiments that shallow features (stage1–2) contribute significantly less to quality prediction than deep features (stage3–4), and that direct multi-scale fusion introduces noise and redundancy. Additionally, while diverse encoder backbones are widely adopted, effective quality decoding architectures that go beyond simple prediction heads remain largely underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262719/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a distorted RGB image randomly cropped to 224×224 pixels and produces a single scalar quality score. The score is computed by averaging individual regression outputs from N=6 query tokens after a final linear projection and global average pooling. The backbone is a Swin Transformer pretrained on ImageNet-21K and further fine-tuned on ImageNet-1K, yielding approximately 95M total parameters.

## Method

Life-IQA replaces the conventional encoder-fusion paradigm with a Transformer decoder that selectively interacts only the two deepest feature stages. Stage4 features are compressed via 1×1 conv + GAP and broadcast to initialize N learnable query tokens, then refined through a 3-layer GCN with learnable adjacency matrices that explicitly propagates information among query nodes before cross-attention. Stage3 features are compressed into an N×N grid via partition average pooling to serve as key/value, enabling cross-level fusion without the cost of full sequence flattening. At the end of the 4-layer decoder, a Mixture-of-Experts head (4 MLP experts, Top-2 sparse routing) decouples the fused representation into distortion-specific factors; this 'post-posed' placement avoids the parameter explosion and optimization instability that arise when MoE replaces FFN within decoder layers on small IQA datasets. Training combines L1 regression loss with load-balancing and router z-loss terms, using Adam at 2×10⁻⁴ (synthetic) or 2×10⁻⁵ (authentic) for 30 epochs.

## Results

Life-IQA (95M params, Swin-T backbone) achieves SROCC/PLCC of 0.982/0.982 on LIVE, 0.966/0.971 on CSIQ, 0.918/0.930 on TID2013, 0.940/0.943 on KADID-10K, 0.896/0.919 on LIVEC, 0.937/0.948 on KonIQ, and 0.926/0.929 on SPAQ. It attains the best or near-best SROCC on 6 of 7 datasets, with the sole exception being LIVE where VISGA reaches 0.987. In cross-dataset evaluation (trained on KonIQ, tested on CSIQ/TID2013/LIVE), Life-IQA ranks first on 5 of 6 metrics, and in data-efficiency tests (20%/40%/60% training data) it consistently outperforms DEIQT and HyperIQA across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Life-IQA outperforms the closest decoder-based competitor DEIQT (24M) by 0.020 SROCC on CSIQ (0.966 vs 0.946), 0.026 on TID2013 (0.918 vs 0.892), 0.051 on KADID-10K (0.940 vs 0.889), and 0.022 on KonIQ (0.937 vs 0.921), while using roughly 4× the parameters. Against the strongest overall competitor VISGA, Life-IQA wins on CSIQ (+0.006 SROCC), TID2013 (+0.017), KADID-10K (+0.001), LIVEC (+0.014), and KonIQ (+0.006), but trails on LIVE (0.982 vs 0.987). Compared to larger-parameter models LIQE (151M) and TRes (152M), Life-IQA achieves higher SROCC on every dataset where both report results (e.g., 0.940 vs 0.930 on KADID-10K, 0.937 vs 0.919 on KonIQ) while using roughly 37–40% fewer parameters. On the compact-model side, Life-IQA surpasses FsPN (34M) by 0.011 SROCC on CSIQ and 0.021 on TID2013. In cross-dataset transfer (trained on KonIQ), Life-IQA leads all baselines on CSIQ (0.715 SROCC) and TID2013 (0.453 SROCC), and is competitive on LIVE (0.822 vs 0.829 for FsPN). In the data-efficiency study, Life-IQA maintains a 0.010–0.020 SROCC advantage over DEIQT even at 20% training data across LIVE, LIVEC, and KonIQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the encoder-decoder paradigm for BIQA introduced by DEIQT [31], which first demonstrated the viability of a Transformer decoder for quality prediction but relied solely on the CLS token as query source. Life-IQA departs from the prevailing multi-scale feature fusion strategy (shallow + deep) by empirically showing that deep features (stage3/4) dominate quality prediction, and instead focuses on cross-level interaction between only the two deepest stages via GCN-enhanced cross-attention. The MoE-based feature decoupling at the decoder head is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of mixture-of-experts routing in the BIQA domain, repurposing a technique well-established in NLP and vision-language models for distortion-specific quality modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that shallow features contribute minimally to BIQA challenges the default assumption in the field that all hierarchical levels provide complementary information, potentially simplifying future architecture design. The post-posed MoE design (applied only at the final decoding step rather than as an FFN replacement throughout) is a pragmatic choice for small-scale IQA datasets, but it limits the model's capacity for distortion-specific feature extraction at intermediate stages. Open questions include whether deeper MoE integration or distortion-conditioned expert specialization could further close the gap on challenging cross-dataset scenarios (e.g., TID2013 SROCC of 0.453 in cross-dataset evaluation), and whether the GCN's learnable adjacency matrix generalizes beyond the fixed 6-query-token setting.
