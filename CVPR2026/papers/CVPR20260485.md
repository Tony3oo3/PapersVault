## Problem

Fitting 3D Gaussian Splatting models remains slow, especially at high resolutions: a 4K scene with 200 views can take hours to optimize, hindering adoption in dynamic scene modeling and immersive applications. Prior acceleration efforts focused on CUDA kernel optimization, quantization, or learning-based feed-forward priors, but largely neglected the high-resolution regime where tile-Gaussian pair generation and sorting scale proportionally with pixel count and dominate runtime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple posed RGB images at up to 4K resolution along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics (typically from SfM). It produces a 3D Gaussian radiance field (a set of anisotropic Gaussian primitives with position, covariance, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color coefficients) that supports real-time novel-view rendering. During training, only a dilated subset of pixels (a chessboard pattern with dilation factor 2) is rendered per iteration for loss computation, while the final model renders full-resolution images.

## Method

Turbo-GS introduces three complementary mechanisms to accelerate 3DGS fitting. First, dilated rendering generates tile-Gaussian pairs in a downsampled image space (mapping Gaussian centers and 3σ radii via NDC projection) but performs alpha-blending in the original high-resolution space, reducing GPU computation and memory by ~2× while preserving rendering fidelity (PSNR > 70 dB when merging four offset views). Second, a convergence-aware budget schedule models the log-loss vs. log-iteration relationship as a power law, periodically fitting a local exponent and adjusting the densification rate via a tanh-gated correction to balance adding new Gaussians against optimizing existing ones. Third, position-appearance densification thresholds both positional and color gradients (with color threshold set to 10% of the position threshold) to trigger splitting in textureless regions where positional gradients vanish, activated at 20% probability to avoid overfitting. A sensitivity-based pruning step at 60% threshold removes redundant primitives post-training.

## Results

On 4K MipNeRF-360, Turbo-3DGS achieves PSNR 26.80, SSIM 0.805, and LPIPS 0.327 in 24 minutes, matching or exceeding 3DGS (PSNR 26.75, LPIPS 0.410) while training 4.7× faster (24m vs. 113m). Turbo-Scaffold-GS completes the same scene in just 9 minutes with PSNR 26.65 and LPIPS 0.327, outperforming Scaffold-GS (143m, LPIPS 0.359) by 16× in speed. On 4K EyefulTower, Turbo-3DGS reaches PSNR 31.07 and LPIPS 0.294 in 27 minutes versus 3DGS's 200 minutes, a 7.4× speedup with comparable quality. The method also reduces 1K MipNeRF-360 training to 12.18 minutes (Turbo-3DGS) and 6.86 minutes (Turbo-Scaffold-GS) while maintaining or improving LPIPS over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Turbo-3DGS outperforms 3DGS on LPIPS (0.327 vs. 0.410 on MipNeRF-360 4K) and SSIM (0.805 vs. 0.797) while training 4.7× faster (24m vs. 113m), and beats 3DGS-accel on all quality metrics (PSNR 26.80 vs. 26.52, LPIPS 0.327 vs. 0.364) at 1.7× less time. Against Scaffold-GS, Turbo-Scaffold-GS achieves 16× speedup (9m vs. 143m) with better LPIPS (0.327 vs. 0.359) and SSIM (0.800 vs. 0.794), though Scaffold-GS retains a slight PSNR edge (26.84 vs. 26.65). On EyefulTower 4K, Turbo-3DGS matches 3DGS's PSNR (31.07 vs. 31.18) in 7.4× less time (27m vs. 200m) with marginally better LPIPS (0.294 vs. 0.296). In the 1K setting (Table 2), Turbo-3DGS achieves the best LPIPS (0.205) among all 3DGS-based methods, beating Taming 3DGS (0.218), Mini-Splatting (0.217), and Speedy-Splat (0.288), while Turbo-Scaffold-GS reaches LPIPS 0.208 in just 6.86 minutes—faster than all competing methods including Taming 3DGS (15.78m) and Speedy-Splat (12.99m). The method trades slightly higher memory usage (680MB vs. 412MB for 3DGS) for the speed and quality gains, and uses more peak Gaussians (2.80M vs. 1.74M), reflecting its densification strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

Turbo-GS builds directly on 3DGS and Scaffold-GS as its base representations, incorporating the optimized CUDA backward kernel from Taming 3DGS. However, it deliberately departs from the dominant line of work that accelerates Gaussian splatting fitting at the CUDA/rasterizer level (e.g., Taming 3DGS, Speedy-Splat, FlashGS) by shifting the focus to the supervised training process itself. The dilated rendering idea draws a conceptual parallel to NeRF's ray sampling but requires a non-trivial adaptation to tile-based Gaussian splatting, while the convergence-aware budget and position-appearance densification are novel training-schedule and densification strategies not present in prior 3DGS variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing 4K scene fitting from hours to roughly 10 minutes removes a major practical barrier to deploying radiance fields in immersive and production settings, and the dilated rendering pipeline is modular enough to be dropped into other 3DGS variants. The paper is explicitly learning-free and acknowledges it does not explore integration with geometry foundation models, which is an open direction as the field shifts toward large-model-based initialization. A minor limitation noted in ablations is that the convergence-aware budget slightly degrades 3DGS variants (due to their reset operation), suggesting the schedule is more naturally suited to Scaffold-GS-style architectures.
