## Problem

Crop yield prediction at subfield (pixel) resolution requires large, high-quality, multimodal datasets, yet existing resources are scarce, limited to single crops or countries, or restricted to regional-level aggregation. This scarcity hinders the development of scalable, generalizable models, and the few available datasets do not support pixel-level regression with globally accessible inputs. Additionally, severe distribution shifts in ground truth yield data across years and regions cause significant performance collapse in deep learning models, a challenge that remains poorly understood and unaddressed.

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of a 24-step time series of Sentinel-2 L2A multispectral imagery (13 bands at 10 m, ~5-day cadence) fused with ERA5 weather (daily, 30 km), SoilGrids soil properties (250 m, static, 6 depth layers), and SRTM-derived topography (30 m), totaling 72 features per pixel. The target is a rasterized dry-yield map at 10 m resolution (t/ha), derived by averaging combine-harvester point measurements within each S2-aligned pixel; pixels without yield points are masked. The dataset is provided in two formats: a preprocessed xarray with all modalities concatenated into a unified 24-step series, and a raw per-field format preserving individual modalities for flexible model design.

## Method

The paper introduces YieldSAT (2,173 fields, ~12.2 M labeled pixels across 4 countries, 4 crops, 9 years) and benchmarks six architectures (LSTM, 3D-LSTM, 3D-ConvLSTM, Transformer, AFF, MMGF) under both S2-only and S2+ADM settings using stratified grouped 10-fold CV. To probe distribution-shift robustness, the authors evaluate Leave-One-Year-Out and Leave-One-Region-Out protocols and propose a domain-informed Deep Ensemble (5 members) that exploits weight-space diversity: under shift, ensemble members occupy distinct low-loss modes in parameter space, yielding 5-29 percentage-point R2 gains over a deterministic baseline. Incorporating spatial locality via 3D-LSTM further anchors ensemble members in closer weight-space regions, narrowing the gap between shifted and in-distribution performance and approaching standard-CV accuracy under LORO for corn (R2 = 0.76).

## Results

On the YieldSAT benchmark, the best field-level performance is achieved by AFF (Adaptive Feature Fusion) on Argentine soybean (ARG-S) with R²=0.84 and RMSE=0.49 t/ha, while the best subfield (pixel-level) score is R²=0.73 and RMSE=0.79 t/ha on the same subset. Across all five country-crop subsets, 3D-LSTM and 3D-ConvLSTM are competitive, with 3D-LSTM reaching R²=0.84 on Brazilian corn (BRA-C) at the field level. Under distribution shift (Leave-One-Year-Out), the Deep Ensemble 3D-LSTM improves R² by 17 percentage points over the baseline LSTM for soybean, and under Leave-One-Region-Out it gains 29 p.p. for corn, reaching R²=0.76.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFF [32] (Adaptive Feature Fusion) is the strongest overall model, achieving the best field-level R² on four of five subsets (ARG-S 0.84, GER-W 0.74, URG-S 0.81) and the lowest RMSE on ARG-S (0.49 t/ha) and URG-S (0.46 t/ha). 3D-LSTM [32] is the closest competitor, matching AFF on BRA-C (R²=0.84) and achieving the best RMSE on GER-R (0.58 t/ha with S2-only input). 3D-ConvLSTM [13] [Helber et al., IGARSS 2024] performs well on GER-R (R²=0.78) but lags behind AFF and 3D-LSTM on most other subsets. The simpler LSTM [36] [Pathak et al., IGARSS 2023] and Transformer [12] [Helber et al., IGARSS 2023] baselines are consistently 4–12 p.p. lower in R² than the top models, confirming that spatial neighborhood modeling (3D-CNN blocks) and advanced fusion are critical. MMGF [25] [Mena et al., RSE 2025] is competitive but trails AFF by 1–8 p.p. in R² across subsets, suggesting that the adaptive gating in AFF better harmonizes heterogeneous modalities. Under distribution shift, the Deep Ensemble variant of 3D-LSTM substantially outperforms the deterministic LSTM baseline (e.g., +29 p.p. R² for corn under LORO), demonstrating that ensemble diversity in weight space is a key robustness mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Ensemble framework (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) by incorporating domain-informed inductive bias—specifically spatial locality via 3D-LSTM—to improve robustness under distribution shift in crop yield prediction. It builds on a lineage of spatio-temporal models for yield prediction (LSTM, ConvLSTM, Transformers) and multimodal fusion methods (input fusion, adaptive feature fusion, gated fusion), but departs from prior work by formulating the problem as a large-scale pixel regression task across multiple countries, crops, and years simultaneously. The dataset itself carves new ground: prior yield datasets (SwissYield, CropNet) are limited to single countries, single crops, or regional-level resolution, whereas YieldSAT is the first to combine pixel-level yield maps with multimodal, globally available EO inputs at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

YieldSAT fills a critical gap in the EO and agricultural DL community by providing a large, expert-curated, multimodal benchmark (12.2M pixels, 2,173 fields, 4 countries, 4 crops, 9 years) that enables reproducible, scalable yield prediction research. The paper's most impactful finding is the quantification of severe distribution shift in real-world yield data (22 p.p. R² drop under leave-one-year-out), which undermines the practical deployability of current models and motivates the domain-informed DE approach. Open challenges remain: global coverage is absent, field-level modeling independent of spatial extent is unsolved, foundation models for EO regression are nascent, and physical consistency and explainability are largely unaddressed. The dataset and benchmark will likely become a standard reference for the crop yield prediction community.
