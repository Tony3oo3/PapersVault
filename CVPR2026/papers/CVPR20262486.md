## Problem

Active learning for object detection is bottlenecked by expensive bounding-box annotation, yet existing methods either modify detector internals or training schedules (limiting portability) or fail to jointly exploit instance-level uncertainty, image-level diversity, and class-imbalance cues in a unified selection criterion. The paper asks whether a fully inference-driven, detector-agnostic framework can match or exceed state-of-the-art AL baselines without any changes to model architecture or training pipeline.

## Input / Output

PAL consumes only detector inference outputs—pre-NMS box counts, per-detection confidence scores, and predicted class labels—extracted from both the labelled and unlabelled pools, plus pre-computed image embeddings from a frozen ViT encoder. It produces a ranked list of images within a fixed annotation budget b, with per-class sub-budgets b_c allocated via a frequency-inverse weighting scheme that up-weights rare classes. No gradients, intermediate features, or training-loop modifications are required at any point.

## Method

PAL's selection score fuses two components: (1) LIUS, which trains a lightweight per-class binary logistic classifier on two features (pre-NMS box count and detection confidence) to separate true from false positives, then scores each unlabelled instance by the Shannon entropy of its predicted TP probability; and (2) GUIDE, an image-level aggregate of Class-Weighted Image Entropy (balancing frequent vs. rare class contributions), Rare-Class driven Diversity Index (summing class weights over unique classes present), and Rank-Conditioned Similarity Penalty (penalizing images whose ViT embedding is most similar to a higher-ranked candidate, enforcing diversity without discarding both similar images). The final per-image score is α·LIUS + d·GUIDE with d=0.1, and a class-based budgeting mechanism allocates the annotation budget inversely proportional to class frequency to counter long-tail imbalance. Because every signal is derived from inference-time outputs and a frozen encoder, PAL is fully portable across RetinaNet, Faster R-CNN, SSD, YOLOX-Tiny, and YOLO11s without any architectural or training-pipeline changes.

## Results

On BDD100K with RetinaNet, PAL achieves 46.7% mAP@0.5 in round 5, outperforming PPAL (45.5%), Entropy (44.8%), and Random (42.2%). On COCO with RetinaNet, PAL yields a +1.4 AP@0.5-0.95 improvement over PPAL in the final active learning round; on PASCAL VOC with RetinaNet, the gain is +0.9 mAP@0.5. For edge models, PAL maintains ≥+1 mAP@0.5 over entropy sampling with YOLOX-Tiny on BDD from round 3 onward, and ≥+1 AP@0.5-0.95 over random sampling with YOLO11s on COCO across all rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAL consistently outperforms PPAL [27], the strongest prior baseline, across all three datasets: +1.4 AP on COCO (RetinaNet), +0.9 mAP on PASCAL VOC (RetinaNet), and +1.2 mAP on BDD100K (RetinaNet), while PPAL requires on average ~20.7% more annotations to reach comparable budgets. On PASCAL VOC, PAL initially trails PPAL in rounds 1–2 but overtakes it in later rounds as labelled data accumulates. Against MIAL [30], DivProto [26], CoreSet [21], CDAL [1], and LearnLoss [28], PAL achieves superior or equal performance in every reported round on both COCO and PASCAL VOC (Fig. 4). For edge-deployment models, PAL with YOLOX-Tiny on BDD surpasses entropy sampling by ≥1 mAP from round 3, and with YOLO11s on COCO it leads random sampling by 1.0–1.5 AP across all four active rounds, confirming detector-agnostic generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAL departs from prior active learning for detection methods (MIAL, LearnLoss, PPAL) that require access to model internals, intermediate features, or modifications to training pipelines, by operating exclusively on inference-time outputs (pre-NMS box counts and confidence scores). Conceptually, it repurposes the uncertainty-sampling lineage (entropy, margin-based scoring) through a lightweight per-class logistic classifier trained on TP/FP labels, and pairs it with image-level diversity signals (ViT embeddings, class-weighted entropy) that prior methods either ignore or compute via model-internal representations. The class-budgeting mechanism draws on class-balanced active learning for classification but adapts it to the multi-instance, multi-class structure of detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAL's detector-agnostic, inference-only design lowers the integration barrier for active learning in production pipelines, making it a practical tool for teams deploying object detection across diverse architectures without re-engineering training code. The consistent gains across five detectors and three datasets validate the portability claim, though the improvements over the strongest baseline (PPAL) are modest (+1.2–1.4 AP/mAP). Open questions include whether the reliance on an external pre-trained ViT encoder for RCSP introduces a hidden dependency that limits true model-agnosticism, how the heuristic class-budgeting ratio generalizes to extremely long-tailed or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the method's gains persist when the annotation budget is very small (e.g., <1% of data).
