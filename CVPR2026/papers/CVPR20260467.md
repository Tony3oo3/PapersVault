## Problem

Existing diffusion-based one-shot federated learning methods learn only a single conditional embedding per category, which fails to capture the diverse intra-category visual patterns present in each client's local data. This results in synthetic data with low semantic fidelity and insufficient diversity, ultimately degrading the global model's performance under data heterogeneity (both feature skew and label skew).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260467/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a small local image dataset (16-shot per category) with class labels. Clients extract DINOv2 features, perform hierarchical clustering within each category, and upload per-cluster conditional embeddings (J=4 per category) along with GMM parameters (M=4 components) modeling the latent noise distribution. The server uses a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 model to generate 5× the local dataset size in synthetic images, which are then used to train a ResNet-18 global classifier (initialized from ImageNet pre-training) that outputs class predictions.

## Method

Espresso introduces two key mechanisms. First, Fine-Grained Condition Learning identifies intra-category visual subgroups via hierarchical clustering on DINOv2 features and learns a separate conditional embedding for each subgroup by optimizing the diffusion noise-prediction loss with the U-Net frozen, bypassing the text encoder to directly optimize the cross-attention conditioning vector. Second, Semantics-Preserving Sampling improves generation quality in two ways: (a) it models the initial denoising noise z^T as a GMM (fitted on MixUp-augmented latents diffused to t=T) so that sampled starting points are semantically meaningful rather than drawn from N(0,I), and (b) it applies Z-Sampling, a zigzag denoising strategy where above a threshold T_z=800, each step denoises with strong guidance γ₁, performs a DDIMInverse step back with weak guidance γ₂, then denoises again with γ₁, exploiting the guidance gap to iteratively inject conditional information into the latent trajectory.

## Results

On DomainNet (16-shot, feature-skew), Espresso achieves 75.32% average accuracy, outperforming the best diffusion-based OSFL baseline FedBiP (72.81%) by 2.51 points and the best traditional FL method FPL (69.30%) by 6.02 points. On Common NICO++, Espresso reaches 82.16% average accuracy versus FedBiP's 80.54% and FedDEO's 77.31%. On PACS, Espresso attains 80.36% average accuracy, surpassing FedBiP (79.11%) and FedDEO (76.38%). The largest gains appear on stylistically challenging domains such as quickdraw in DomainNet (80.18% vs. 70.18% for FedBiP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Espresso consistently outperforms all 10 baselines on average accuracy across all three datasets. Against the closest diffusion-based OSFL competitor FedBiP [3], Espresso gains +2.51 points on DomainNet (75.32 vs. 72.81), +1.62 on Common NICO++ (82.16 vs. 80.54), and +1.25 on PACS (80.36 vs. 79.11). The margin over FedDEO [50] is larger: +4.51 on DomainNet, +4.85 on Common NICO++, and +3.98 on PACS. Traditional FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FPL, FedSR) trail by 6–9 points on average, while non-diffusion OSFL methods (DENSE, FedD3, Fens) lag by 7–12 points. Notably, Espresso's advantage is most pronounced on stylistically challenging domains (e.g., quickdraw: 80.18% vs. 70.18% for FedBiP), though it is slightly outperformed on a few common-style domains (sketch in DomainNet by FedBiP, photo in PACS by FedDEO), which the authors attribute to the pre-trained diffusion model's strong prior for those styles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of diffusion-based One-Shot Federated Learning methods (FedLMG, FedDEO, FedBiP) that use pre-trained diffusion models to generate synthetic data on the server. Its key departure is replacing the single conditional embedding per category (inherited from Textual Inversion-style personalization) with multiple fine-grained embeddings learned via intra-category clustering, directly addressing the under-modeling of within-class visual diversity. The Semantics-Preserving Sampling component repurposes Z-Sampling's zigzag self-reflection mechanism and introduces GMM modeling of terminal latent noises as a principled way to diversify denoising starting points, building on DDIM/DDIMInverse as the deterministic backbone for the reflection steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for improving diffusion-based OSFL under feature-skew heterogeneity, and the fine-grained conditional embedding idea is a natural but previously unexplored direction that could transfer to other diffusion personalization settings. Limitations include reliance on a fixed pre-trained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5), sensitivity to the number of intra-category clusters J, and evaluation restricted to 16-shot image classification on three domain-generalization benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger client populations, non-image modalities, or label-skew settings (only briefly addressed in the appendix), and whether the GMM-of-noises component generalizes beyond the specific noise schedule of Stable Diffusion v1.5.
