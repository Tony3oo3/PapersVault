## Problem

No prior work has systematically evaluated vision LLMs (VLLMs) on hierarchical visual recognition, where a model must correctly classify an image along the full ancestral path of a taxonomy (e.g., Animal → Vertebrate → Fish → Anemone Fish) rather than just at the leaf level. This paper constructs ~1M four-choice VQA tasks across six taxonomies and reveals that all tested VLLMs—proprietary and open-source—substantially lack hierarchical consistency, with the LLM component identified as the bottleneck for open-source models.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an RGB image with a four-choice VQA prompt specifying a target taxonomy level (e.g., 'what is its taxonomic classification at the phylum level?'). The three distractor choices are selected as the most visually similar labels at the same hierarchy level via SigLIP cosine similarity, making the task harder than random four-way guessing. The model outputs a single letter (A–D), and performance is scored by Hierarchical Consistent Accuracy (HCA, requiring all levels along the root-to-leaf path to be correct) and leaf-level accuracy, with HCA being the stricter primary metric.

## Method

The paper's core diagnostic is a multi-stage ablation that isolates the visual encoder, projector, and LLM within open-source VLLMs. Linear probing of visual tokens at the encoder output, projector output, and final LLM layer shows that discriminative and hierarchical structure is preserved throughout the forward pass, exonerating the visual front-end. Separately, text-only HCA evaluation (replacing images with their ground-truth leaf labels) reveals that the LLMs themselves score far below the VLLMs' visual HCA, and linear probing of LLM text embeddings confirms that hierarchical knowledge is encoded in the representations but cannot be decoded by the autoregressive head. LoRA finetuning on the constructed VQA tasks improves both the VLLM's visual HCA and the LLM's text HCA, but the LLM's text gains consistently exceed the VLLM's visual gains, reaffirming that the LLM's taxonomy knowledge gap—not the visual pipeline—is the binding constraint.

## Results

Across all five taxonomies, every evaluated model exhibits a large gap between hierarchical consistent accuracy (HCA) and leaf-level accuracy (Acc_leaf), confirming that VLLMs fail to produce hierarchically consistent predictions. GPT-4o achieves the best HCA at 42.95% (iNat21-Animal), 35.53% (iNat21-Plant), 27.57% (ImgNet-Artifact), 67.69% (ImgNet-Animal), and 81.96% (CUB-200), while the best open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-72B reaches 35.73%, 32.82%, 21.08%, 64.08%, and 66.36% respectively. Even the domain-specific BioCLIP2, which attains 95.94% Acc_leaf on iNat21-Animal, only achieves 41.84% HCA, demonstrating that high leaf-level accuracy does not translate to hierarchical consistency. The LLM text-only HCA (Table 2) reveals that Qwen2.5-VL's LLM scores only 63.86% on CUB-200 (4-level taxonomy), while GPT-4o's text HCA reaches 98.81%, identifying the LLM as the bottleneck for open-source VLLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o marginally outperforms the best open-source VLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) on HCA across all five taxonomies (e.g., 42.95 vs. 35.73 on iNat21-Animal, 81.96 vs. 66.36 on CUB-200), but both suffer from large HCA-to-Acc_leaf gaps, indicating neither achieves reliable hierarchical consistency. Among open-source VLLMs, the Qwen2.5-VL series shows a clear scaling trend: HCA on iNat21-Animal rises from 19.43% (7B) to 26.90% (32B) to 35.73% (72B), yet even the 72B model makes errors on over 64% of hierarchical paths. The domain-specific BioCLIP2 dramatically outperforms all general-purpose VLLMs on leaf-level accuracy for biological taxonomies (95.94% Acc_leaf on iNat21-Animal vs. 54.20% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B) but still only reaches 41.84% HCA, confirming that high discriminative power alone does not ensure hierarchical consistency. General CLIP models (OpenCLIP, SigLIP) perform near-random on iNat21 (HCA of 1.04% and 2.15%) and are substantially outperformed by VLLMs on fine-grained recognition, though they achieve comparable Acc_leaf on ImageNet. The critical finding is that the LLM component of open-source VLLMs (text-only HCA of 33–64% in Table 2) is the bottleneck: linear probes on visual tokens achieve higher HCA than the full VLLM, and LoRA finetuning improves the LLM's text HCA by more than the VLLM's visual HCA (e.g., +20.66 vs. +11.67 on iNat21-Plant), reaffirming that the LLM's lack of taxonomy knowledge limits the entire system.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical visual recognition literature (Park et al. 2025, Wu et al. 2024) and the VLLM evaluation literature (Zhang et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2024) by being the first to systematically assess VLLMs under a hierarchical consistency criterion rather than flat or fine-grained accuracy. It builds on the HCA metric from hierarchical classification and the taxonomy-aware evaluation ideas from Snæbjarnarson et al. 2025, but departs from prior VLLM benchmarks (MMBench, MME, MMMU) by constructing ~1M four-choice VQA tasks that traverse all levels of six taxonomies. The ablation methodology draws on linear probing techniques from the LLM interpretability literature (Park et al. 2024 on hierarchical orthogonality) to isolate which component of the VLLM pipeline is responsible for the observed failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that open-source LLMs lack basic taxonomy knowledge (e.g., failing to place an Anemone Fish under Vertebrate) while their own internal embeddings encode this structure orthogonally reframes the bottleneck for VLLM improvement: the fix must target LLM pretraining rather than visual encoder design or post-hoc finetuning. This has direct practical implications for the ~1M-task benchmark and for anyone building general-purpose visual recognition systems on top of VLLMs. Open questions include whether taxonomy-aware pretraining objectives can close the gap, whether the finding generalizes to proprietary models (which cannot be ablated), and whether the four-choice VQA format adequately captures the difficulty of open-set hierarchical recognition.
