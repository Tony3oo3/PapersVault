## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion pipelines typically generate many candidate images per prompt and then select the best using post-hoc evaluators like ImageReward or CLIPScore, but these metrics require fully denoised images, forcing every candidate to complete tens to hundreds of expensive sampling steps before any pruning can occur. This paper asks whether final image quality can be reliably predicted from partial generative states—intermediate denoiser activations at only 20% of the trajectory—enabling early termination of low-quality seeds without modifying the underlying generator.

## Input / Output

The probe consumes a single feature map (C×H×W) extracted from a selected U-Net block (e.g., the 3rd upblock in SD2) at an early checkpoint t (typically 0.2) along with the scalar timestep value, and outputs a single scalar score approximating a chosen external evaluator (ImageReward, BLIP-ITM, CLIPScore, HPS, etc.). At inference, N candidate seeds are scored at t=0.2 and only the top-K (e.g., K=1 of N=5) are continued to full denoising, reducing expected compute to roughly η + (1−η)·K/N ≈ 0.36 of full cost. The probe is a tiny vision encoder plus MLP that adds negligible overhead and requires no modification to the generator, sampler, or noise schedule.

## Method

The core insight is that mid-to-late U-Net activations (particularly the 3rd upblock) already encode stable coarse structure—object contours, spatial layout, and semantic grouping—as early as t=0.2, and these patterns evolve slowly, making them reliable predictors of final quality. Probe-Select attaches a lightweight probe (a small vision encoder with global pooling and timestep embedding, followed by a 2-layer MLP) to a chosen denoiser block, trained with two complementary objectives: a softmax-based listwise ranking loss that transfers the relative preference ordering of an external evaluator, and an InfoNCE contrastive loss that aligns probe embeddings with frozen CLIP prompt embeddings for text awareness. The listwise loss uses a decaying temperature and margin schedule to progressively tighten ranking fidelity, while the contrastive term prevents the probe from ignoring prompt semantics. Because the probe is purely additive and operates on cached activations, it is backbone-agnostic (validated on SD2, SD3-M, SD3-L, and FLUX.1-dev) and schedule-agnostic, achieving Spearman correlations ≥0.7 with most evaluators and ≈0.98–0.99 with BLIP-ITM and ImageReward at t=0.2.

## Results

On MS-COCO with 5 seeds per caption, Probe-Select at t=0.2 achieves Spearman correlations ≥0.7 across eight evaluators, reaching ≈0.98–0.99 for BLIP-ITM and ImageReward. Selecting only the top-1 of 5 seeds reduces expected denoising cost by ~64% (cost ratio ≈0.36) while improving final quality: on SD2, ImageReward rises from 0.49 to 1.59 and HPSv2.1 from 26.95 to 29.03; on SD3-L, ImageReward reaches 1.83 and HPSv2.1 reaches 31.81; on FLUX.1-dev, ImageReward jumps from 0.92 to 1.79. FID also improves modestly (SD3-M: 25.26→25.01; SD3-L: 23.72→23.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the standard generate-then-select baseline, where all 5 seeds are fully denoised and their metrics are averaged. Probe-Select (top-1 of 5, selected at t=0.2) consistently outperforms this baseline on every backbone and every metric: e.g., on SD2, ImageReward improves from 0.49 to 1.59 (+224%) and HPSv2.1 from 26.95 to 29.03; on FLUX.1-dev, ImageReward rises from 0.92 to 1.79 (+95%). Between the two selection strategies, IR-guided selection dominates on ImageReward, HPSv2.1, and most perceptual metrics, while BIM-guided selection achieves the highest BLIP-ITM (0.99 across all backbones). The paper does not directly benchmark against other early-stopping or hallucination-detection methods (e.g., HEaD) in the main table; the comparison is framed as a self-contained efficiency-quality tradeoff. The ~64% cost reduction (only 0.36× full denoising) is achieved without modifying the generator, sampler, or schedule, making the gains purely from selective continuation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: diffusion-model efficiency (step reduction, distillation, flow matching) and post-hoc text-to-image evaluation (CLIPScore, ImageReward, HPS). Rather than modifying the sampler or the generator, it repurposes intermediate denoiser activations—features previously studied for correspondence, retrieval, and segmentation—as inputs to a lightweight probe that predicts final quality scores. The closest prior work, HEaD, uses cross-attention maps for binary hallucination detection in multi-object scenes; Probe-Select generalizes this from a task-specific binary decision to a general-purpose scalar quality prediction aligned with multiple external evaluators, and introduces a joint listwise-ranking plus contrastive text-alignment training objective not present in prior early-exit or early-detection methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone running T2I generation at scale: a 64% reduction in expected denoising cost while simultaneously improving final quality (e.g., ImageReward 0.49→1.59 on SD2) makes the 'generate-then-select' pipeline substantially cheaper without architectural changes. The broader conceptual contribution—that stable structural cues emerge as early as 20% of the reverse process and remain nearly invariant thereafter—suggests a general principle applicable to adaptive stopping, dynamic timestep control, and closed-loop generation. Open questions include whether the probe transfers across domains beyond MS-COCO, how it behaves under different guidance scales or samplers, and whether the early structural signal can be exploited for more than ranking (e.g., steering the generation itself).
