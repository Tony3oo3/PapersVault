## Problem

Existing wide-angle correction methods face a fundamental trade-off: geometric approaches (conformal mapping, stereographic projection) offer interpretability but are computationally expensive and rigid, while deep learning methods are fast but depend on costly manual annotations and often fail to preserve natural human body proportions. No prior work has combined the geometric rigor of quasi-conformal mapping with the efficiency of a learned network in an annotation-free pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single wide-angle distorted RGB image as input and produces a corrected image along with a dense correction flow field. Internally, the network operates at 488×488 resolution and predicts the flow as a probabilistic distribution over 50 discrete displacement coordinates per axis (soft-argmin), rather than a single point estimate, allowing it to model uncertainty in ambiguous regions. At inference, a low-rank Tucker factorization reconstruction is applied to low-confidence flow regions to enforce global structural consistency.

## Method

The framework distills quasi-conformal mapping knowledge into a lightweight student network in two stages. The teacher formulates correction as minimizing Beltrami smoothness energy subject to line-straightening and human-body-rigid constraints, solved via alternating Linear Beltrami Solver (LBS) and Proximal Gradient Descent (PGD) iterations to generate annotation-free ground-truth image pairs. The student, QDWC-Net, is a CNN-Mamba encoder-decoder (16 Mamba layers in the encoder, 16 in the decoder) that regresses the correction flow through a soft-argmin head, trained with a hybrid loss combining Sobel-gradient L1, pixel-wise L2, and SSIM terms on both flow and image. At inference, an MLP-based Tucker factorization module reconstructs low-confidence flow regions using the low-rank prior of the ground-truth flow field, improving generalization without additional training cost.

## Results

On Tan's test set (129 images, 5 smartphones), QDWC-Net achieves FaceAcc 98.619, BodyAcc 97.843, and LineAcc 70.299, with an inference latency of only 0.81 s per image. This represents a 32× speedup (96.92% reduction) over the quasi-conformal teacher pipeline (26.33 s) while maintaining or exceeding its correction quality. On the unseen Zhu's dataset (204 annotated images, 4 smartphones), the method generalizes well, achieving LineAcc 88.627, FaceAcc 98.972, and BodyAcc 92.499, outperforming the strongest baseline (Yao et al.) on both LineAcc and BodyAcc.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QDWC-Net outperforms the strongest deep-learning baseline, Yao et al. [35] (ECCV'24), on FaceAcc (98.619 vs. 98.536) and BodyAcc (97.843 vs. 97.081) while trailing by only 0.42 points on LineAcc (70.299 vs. 70.721), yet runs 27.5× faster (0.81 s vs. 22.30 s). Against the geometric method of Shih et al. [27] (TOG'19), QDWC-Net wins on all three metrics (LineAcc +1.98, FaceAcc +0.68, BodyAcc +0.21) and is 14.3× faster. The classical content-aware method of Carroll et al. [3] (TOG'09) is dominated on every metric and is 60× slower. The simple Mercator projection [28] is the only method faster than QDWC-Net (0.49 s) but lags substantially on all accuracy metrics (e.g., BodyAcc 96.945 vs. 97.843). The two prior deep-learning methods, Tan et al. [30] and Zhu et al. [39], report no BodyAcc or latency numbers but are clearly outperformed on LineAcc (70.299 vs. 66.784 / 67.209) and FaceAcc (98.619 vs. 97.490 / 97.500). On the held-out Zhu's dataset, QDWC-Net leads on LineAcc (88.627 vs. 88.563 for Yao) and BodyAcc (92.499 vs. 92.009), though Shih et al. [27] retains a slight FaceAcc edge (99.428 vs. 98.972), confirming strong cross-dataset generalization without any manual annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of wide-angle correction research: geometric transformation methods (LSCM, stereographic/Mercator projections, Carroll et al., Shih et al.) and deep learning approaches (Tan et al. 2021, Zhu et al. 2022, Yao et al. 2024). It departs from prior geometric work by replacing the rigid conformal mapping of LSCM with the more flexible quasi-conformal mapping, parameterized by the Beltrami coefficient and solved via an LBS-PGD alternating iteration with line and human-body mask constraints. It departs from prior deep learning work by eliminating the need for manual annotations entirely: the QC solver acts as an annotation-free teacher whose correction flows are distilled into a Mamba-based student network (QDWC-Net) with a soft-argmin head and low-rank Tucker prior reconstruction. The distillation paradigm—geometric solver as teacher, lightweight neural net as student—is new to this task, whereas prior methods either required hand-labeled correction pairs (Tan) or semi-supervised initialization (Zhu).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The annotation-free pipeline removes the primary bottleneck (expensive manual annotation of both lines and portraits) that has limited dataset scale and diversity in prior deep learning approaches, and the 32× inference speedup (26.33 s → 0.81 s) makes real-time correction feasible on mobile hardware. The introduction of BodyAcc as an evaluation metric and the explicit treatment of human-body proportion preservation address a gap that most prior methods leave unexplored. However, the method's correction quality is upper-bounded by the accuracy of upstream detectors (YOLOv13 for masks, L-CNN for lines), and the performance gains over strong baselines like Yao et al. are modest (e.g., FaceAcc 98.619 vs. 98.536). Open questions include whether the QC teacher's quality degrades on highly occluded or multi-person scenes, and whether the Mamba backbone's advantage over a standard CNN encoder is robust across architectures and scales.
