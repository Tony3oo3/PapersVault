## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks evaluate discriminative understanding (answer selection, classification) and unconstrained generation in isolation, leaving a critical gap in assessing integrated generative reasoning—the ability to simultaneously parse constraints, plan multi-step solutions, and produce verifiable constructions. In geometry specifically, genuine comprehension is inseparable from the ability to build a diagram satisfying all formal constraints, yet no prior benchmark operationalizes this combined understanding-and-construction task with deterministic verification.

## Input / Output

Each GGBench instance provides a natural-language geometric construction instruction (68–483 tokens, averaging 189.83) as input. The expected output is a tri-modal triplet: step-by-step reasoning text, executable GeoGebra code, and a sequence of 3–7 rendered intermediate diagrams (averaging 5.08 per problem, 7,165 total images). The GeoGebra code serves as unambiguous ground truth, enabling deterministic geometric verification of any generated figure rather than relying solely on perceptual similarity metrics.

## Method

GGBench introduces a tri-modal data structure (aligned text, code, and image) with a four-stage evaluation protocol: (1) Planning quality scored by a VLM judge (GPT-4o) on logical coherence, step completeness, and geometric correctness; (2) Middle-process consistency assessed over concatenated intermediate construction panels; (3) Final-result geometric correctness via VLM scoring plus pixel-level LPIPS/PSNR/SSIM; and (4) Overall scores combining intermediate and final VLM judgments, validated against human raters (Pearson r=0.9295). The dataset construction pipeline uses GPT-5 for problem adaptation and solution generation, Gemini 2.5 Pro for GGB prompt engineering, followed by two-tier filtering—LLM-based quality control on code executability, logical consistency, and diagram correctness, then domain-expert review—yielding 1,411 verified instances across eight geometric categories. The key design insight is that executable code bridges reasoning and generation, enabling process-level verification of each construction step rather than only judging the final output.

## Results

On GGBench, the best code-driven LLM/LRM (GPT-5) achieves an overall VLM-I score of 57.08 and a human-rated score of 83.06, substantially outperforming the strongest end-to-end UMM (Nano Banana) at 33.82 VLM-I and 45.75 human. Among LLMs/LRMs, Claude Sonnet 4.5 (54.11 VLM-I, 72.12 human) and DeepSeek-V3.1 (49.77 VLM-I, 68.12 human) follow closely, while GPT-4o and GLM-4.5V score below 16 VLM-I despite reasonable planning scores (~55–60), indicating a severe planning-to-execution gap. Pixel-level metrics (LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM) confirm that code-driven models produce geometrically more faithful diagrams, with Qwen3-VL achieving the best LPIPS (39.40×10⁻²) and PSNR (52.33) among LLMs/LRMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 dominates all LLM/LRM baselines on every metric, achieving 57.08 VLM-I and 83.06 human score versus Claude Sonnet 4.5 (54.11 / 72.12) and DeepSeek-V3.1 (49.77 / 68.12), with the largest margin in final geometric correctness (VLM-I-Res: 37.36 vs. 30.29 and 26.41). Among end-to-end UMMs, Nano Banana leads with 33.82 VLM-I and 45.75 human, yet still trails the weakest LLM/LRM (GPT-4o at 14.43 VLM-I) in planning quality while being far surpassed by top LLMs/LRMs in construction fidelity. A striking finding is the planning-to-execution gap: GPT-4o and GLM-4.5V score 53–60 on VLM-T planning but collapse to 2.66 and 5.02 on VLM-I-Res, indicating they cannot translate coherent plans into executable geometry. Qwen3-VL achieves the best pixel-level fidelity (LPIPS 39.40, PSNR 52.33) among LLMs/LRMs, suggesting its code output renders most faithfully, though its overall VLM-I (36.74) remains below DeepSeek-V3.1 and Claude Sonnet 4.5. The category-level analysis (Figure 4) shows GPT-5 leads across all eight construction categories, while GPT-4o, GLM-4.5V, and Qwen-Image consistently score below 23, confirming broad spatial-grounding limitations in weaker models.

## Relation to Prior Work

GGBench departs from the dominant paradigm of discriminative multimodal math benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, GeoEval) that evaluate models on answer selection or classification, and from code-based verification work (MathCoder-VL, MATP-BENCH, VeriEquivBench) that uses code as a checking mechanism but not as the primary generative output. It also extends the emerging line of construction-aware evaluation (MathOPEval, Geoint-R1) by requiring 100% tri-modal alignment—stepwise reasoning text, executable GeoGebra code, and rendered diagrams—so that every construction step is independently verifiable. The benchmark is positioned as the first to treat geometric construction as an integrated understanding-reasoning-generation task rather than decomposing it into separate perceptual and generative sub-tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stark performance gap between end-to-end UMMs (best: Nano Banana at ~34/100) and code-driven LLMs (best: GPT-5 at ~57/100) reveals that current unified models lose substantial geometric fidelity when translating plans into pixels, a failure mode invisible to pixel-level metrics like PSNR/SSIM. This motivates a shift toward construction-centric evaluation where executable code serves as ground truth, enabling deterministic verification of spatial constraints. Open questions include whether UMMs can be trained to emit verifiable construction code as an intermediate reasoning step, whether the findings generalize beyond 2D plane geometry, and whether the GeoGebra-specific formulation limits transferability to other CAD or drawing systems.
