## Problem

CLIP-based weakly supervised semantic segmentation methods rely on MHSA attention weights to refine Class Activation Maps, but this affinity-based refinement fails to establish correct intra-class semantic relationships, producing under-activated CAMs that miss large portions of target objects. The paper identifies that the full class distribution across all classes (not just target-class activation) encodes richer patch-level semantic relationships that can resolve this under-activation, while also introducing a boundary over-activation problem that requires separate handling.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 320×320 for the frozen CLIP ViT-Base-16 encoder and 308×308 for the frozen DINOv2-ViT-Base-14 encoder), image-level class labels, and text prompts for both target classes and a designed set of super-classes (e.g., 'animal', 'vehicle', 'person'). It outputs a pixel-level segmentation mask, with Dense CRF post-processing and multi-scale inference (factors 1.0 and 1.5) applied at test time. The framework is single-stage, jointly training a lightweight transformer decoder without a separate classification-to-segmentation pipeline.

## Method

CD-CLIP introduces two complementary modules. The Class Distribution Aware (CDA) module computes per-patch class distributions over all target classes plus super-classes via cosine similarity with CLIP text features, then builds a pairwise similarity map using Jensen-Shannon divergence between these distributions. This distribution-based similarity is used to rectify the initial MHSA affinity (via element-wise multiplication after softmax), producing a CDA affinity that selectively strengthens intra-class patch relationships and weakens inter-class ones, yielding more complete refined CAMs. The Super-class Boundary Exploration (SBE) module addresses the resulting boundary over-activation by computing super-class prototypes from DINO features (masked by CLIP-inferred super-class CAMs via class average pooling), generating super-class prototype CAMs, and applying a Dice-based boundary-enhanced loss only to predictions containing multiple super-classes. Training uses a combined objective of segmentation loss plus the boundary-enhanced loss (weighted by λ₁=0.6), with both CLIP and DINO backbones kept frozen.

## Results

CD-CLIP achieves 82.5% mIoU on PASCAL VOC 2012 validation and 82.4% mIoU on the test set, and 54.1% mIoU on MS COCO 2014 validation, establishing new state-of-the-art in single-stage image-level WSSS. This represents a 6.1% improvement over the closest single-stage baseline WeCLIP (76.4% val) and a 4.1% gain over ExCEL (78.4% val), the previous best single-stage method. On MS COCO, CD-CLIP surpasses ExCEL by 3.8% (54.1% vs. 50.3%) and SeCo by 7.4% (54.1% vs. 46.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CD-CLIP outperforms all 25 compared baselines on both PASCAL VOC 2012 and MS COCO 2014. Among single-stage methods, it surpasses the previous SOTA ExCEL [50] by 4.1% on VOC val (82.5% vs. 78.4%) and 3.8% on COCO val (54.1% vs. 50.3%), and exceeds its direct baseline WeCLIP [54] by 6.1% on VOC val. Against multi-stage methods, CD-CLIP beats the strongest entry S2C [19] (78.2% val) by 4.3% and CPAL [37] (74.5% val) by 8.0%, despite using a simpler single-stage pipeline without iterative post-processing. On MS COCO, the margin over the best multi-stage method KTSE [7] (45.9%) is 8.2%, and over the best single-stage competitor ExCEL [50] (50.3%) is 3.8%. The paper also demonstrates that its CDA module alone (Setting #3 in ablation) already reaches 80.2% segmentation mIoU, exceeding WeCLIP's full pipeline, confirming that the class-distribution-based affinity is the primary driver of the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frozen-CLIP paradigm for WSSS established by WeCLIP, adopting its ViT-Base-16 backbone and lightweight transformer decoder, while replacing the standard MHSA-based affinity refinement with a Jensen-Shannon divergence over per-patch class distributions. It also draws on DINOv2 features (building on the observation that DINO features provide superior super-class region separation) to generate boundary-aware prototype CAMs, a use of DINO in WSSS that goes beyond the feature-fusion approach of Frozen CLIP-DINO. Conceptually, the key departure from prior affinity-based methods (AFA, CLIP-ES, CAA) is the observation that the full class distribution across all target and super-classes, rather than the target-class activation alone, encodes more reliable intra-class patch relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.1% mIoU gain over WeCLIP on PASCAL VOC and 7% over the previous best single-stage method on MS COCO are substantial for image-level WSSS, and the class-distribution insight could inform affinity construction in other CLIP-based localization tasks. However, the method remains tied to frozen CLIP and DINOv2 backbones, requires a hand-designed super-class set per dataset, and still relies on Dense CRF post-processing at inference, limiting its generality. Open questions include whether the JS-divergence-based affinity generalizes to open-vocabulary or video segmentation settings, and whether the super-class boundary supervision can be made dataset-agnostic.
