## Problem

Multimodal foundation models exhibit significant deficiencies in spatial intelligence—the ability to understand, reason about, and act within 3D space—despite strong performance on general visual tasks. The root cause is the scarcity and imbalance of spatially grounded training data: existing datasets are fragmented, heterogeneous, and underrepresent critical capabilities like perspective-taking. This paper investigates whether systematic data scaling, guided by a principled taxonomy of spatial capabilities, can cultivate robust spatial intelligence in established multimodal models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260154/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The models consume RGB images, video sequences (up to 16 frames sampled during training, tested up to 128 frames at inference), and text questions, producing text answers in multiple-choice or free-form format for spatial reasoning tasks. A notable detail is that the models are trained on at most 16 frames per sample yet generalize to 32–64 frame sequences at inference, demonstrating extrapolation beyond the training context window. The training corpus comprises 8.5M QA pairs spanning five spatial capability domains, with a deliberate emphasis on perspective-taking tasks that were previously underrepresented in public datasets.

## Method

The approach is deliberately data-centric: rather than modifying model architectures, the authors continue-train three distinct foundation models (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3, and Bagel) for one epoch on a curated 8.5M-sample dataset (SenseNova-SI-8M) organized under a five-capability taxonomy (Metric Measurement, Spatial Relations, Mental Reconstruction, Perspective-taking, Comprehensive Reasoning). The dataset combines ~4M open-source QA pairs with ~4.5M newly generated samples from richly annotated 3D scene datasets (ScanNet, Matterport3D, Ego-Exo4D, etc.), with special emphasis on perspective-taking tasks that prior work largely overlooked. Training uses AdamW at 5×10⁻⁶ learning rate on 128 GPUs with batch size 2048. The paper further ablates text-based chain-of-thought and GRPO reinforcement learning, finding that neither reliably improves spatial reasoning beyond what simple QA-style data scaling achieves, suggesting that spatial intelligence may require fundamentally different reasoning mechanisms than language-based CoT.

## Results

SenseNova-SI-InternVL3-8B achieves the best overall spatial intelligence score of 61.5% (average across 8 benchmarks), with standout results of 68.8% on VSI-Bench, 85.7% on MindCube, and 54.7% on ViewSpatial. The 2B variant (49.4% avg) surpasses all 7B spatial-intelligence baselines (VST-7B at 50.8%, Cambrian-S-7B at 45.1%) with a smaller model. On general multimodal understanding, SenseNova-SI retains 84.9% on MMBench-EN, confirming that spatial data scaling does not cause catastrophic forgetting. The paper also demonstrates that SenseNova-SI outperforms GPT-5 on Perspective-taking (ViewSpatial: 54.7% vs 45.6%) and Mental Reconstruction (MindCube: 85.7% vs 56.3%), while GPT-5 remains stronger on SITE (61.9% vs 47.7%) and EmbSpatial (81.6% vs 72.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SenseNova-SI-InternVL3-8B outperforms all open-source spatial-intelligence baselines by large margins: it beats VST-7B-SFT (50.8% avg) by 10.7 points and Cambrian-S-7B (45.1%) by 16.4 points, with the largest gaps on MindCube (+46.0 over VST, +47.8 over Cambrian-S) and ViewSpatial (+4.2 over VST, +13.4 over Cambrian-S). Against proprietary models, SenseNova-SI surpasses GPT-5 on VSI-Bench (68.8 vs 55.0), MMSI (43.3 vs 41.8), MindCube (85.7 vs 56.3), and ViewSpatial (54.7 vs 45.6), but trails GPT-5 on SITE (47.7 vs 61.9), BLINK (63.9 vs 68.0), 3DSR (55.5 vs 60.3), and EmbSpatial (72.0 vs 81.6). Gemini-3-Pro-Preview remains the strongest proprietary model overall (63.8% avg), edging out SenseNova-SI by 2.3 points, primarily due to advantages on SITE (62.3 vs 47.7) and BLINK (76.0 vs 63.9). Notably, the 2B SenseNova-SI model (49.4% avg) exceeds all 7B spatial baselines, demonstrating that data scaling with a principled taxonomy can compensate for model size. On general multimodal benchmarks (MMBench-EN, MMStar, MMMU, etc.), SenseNova-SI retains performance comparable to its base models, confirming no catastrophic forgetting from spatial data training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the data-centric branch of spatial intelligence research, deliberately contrasting with architecture-modification approaches (Spatial-MLLM, VLM-3R, 3DThinker) that inject 3D expert encoders. It builds directly on the EASI taxonomy [7] to organize spatial capabilities and extends prior dataset-curation efforts (SpatialVLM, SpatialLadder, VST, Cambrian-S) by scaling to 8M samples across three distinct base model families (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3, Bagel) without altering their architectures. The key departure from prior work is the systematic inclusion of perspective-taking data, which the authors identify as a critical but previously underrepresented capability, and the explicit validation that data scaling—not algorithmic novelty—drives the observed gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the most comprehensive empirical evidence to date that spatial intelligence in MLLMs is primarily a data-coverage problem, with clear scaling curves and saturation signals that will guide future investment. The negative result on text-based CoT (including elaborate CogMap and RL variants) challenges the prevailing assumption that chain-of-thought reasoning transfers to spatial tasks, suggesting a need for fundamentally different reasoning mechanisms. Open questions include whether continued scaling will trigger stronger emergent capabilities, how to design multimodal (non-text) CoT for spatial reasoning, and whether the observed capability synergy (PT/SR gains transferring to CR) generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks. The fully open-sourced models and 8M dataset lower the barrier for the community to pursue algorithmic innovation on a spatially capable foundation.
