## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D object detectors still require training scenes (as point clouds or images) and rely on pseudo-labels or distillation strategies, leaving a gap toward truly supervision-free 3D understanding. No prior work has addressed multi-view indoor 3D object detection in a genuinely zero-shot, training-free setting, and the task has never been attempted from unposed images alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261421/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a point cloud (up to 100,000 points), 45 RGB images resized to 480×640, depth maps, camera extrinsics, and intrinsics; in the most constrained mode it requires only unposed images, with DUSt3R inferring both depth and poses. Outputs are axis-aligned 3D bounding boxes (center and size, no rotation) paired with open-vocabulary semantic labels and confidence scores derived from cosine similarity against CLIP text embeddings. The pipeline supports three input modalities—point cloud + posed images, posed images only, and unposed images only—reducing each to the point-cloud-based detection problem.

## Method

Zoo3D decomposes open-vocabulary 3D detection into class-agnostic box prediction and label assignment. Zoo3D₀ (zero-shot) builds a mask graph over 2D SAM instance masks using a view-consensus rate metric (supporter-observer ratio ≥ 0.9), iteratively merges connected components into 3D instances, and extracts axis-aligned bounding boxes from min/max point coordinates; labels are then assigned by projecting each box's points onto the top-5 most-visible views, refining masks with SAM at three scales, and matching aggregated CLIP ViT-H/14 features against text embeddings. Zoo3D₁ (self-supervised) trains a modified TR3D backbone (sparse ResNet, single 16 cm detection head, focal + DIoU loss) on class-agnostic pseudo-labels generated by Zoo3D₀, with an optional second iteration (Zoo3D₂) for further refinement. For image-only inputs, DUSt3R reconstructs a TSDF point cloud from predicted or ground-truth depth maps, keeping the entire pipeline training-free and avoiding data leakage from ScanNet.

## Results

On ScanNet20 with point cloud + posed images, zero-shot Zoo3D₀ achieves 34.7 mAP25, surpassing the best prior self-supervised method OV-Uni3DETR (25.3 mAP25) by +9.4 points without any training. Self-supervised Zoo3D₁ further improves to 37.2 mAP25 on ScanNet20, 32.0 on ScanNet60, and 23.5 on ScanNet200. In the most constrained unposed-image setting, DUSt3R→Zoo3D₁ reaches 27.9 mAP25 on ScanNet20, approaching the performance of point-cloud-based OV-Uni3DETR (25.3 mAP25) despite having no point clouds or camera poses at inference. On ScanNet10, Zoo3D₁ achieves 44.5 mAP25, outperforming OV-Uni3DETR (34.1) by +10.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Zoo3D outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every benchmark and modality. The closest competitor on point-cloud inputs is OV-Uni3DETR† (25.3 mAP25 on ScanNet20, 19.4 on ScanNet60); Zoo3D₀ exceeds it by +9.4 and +7.7 mAP25 respectively in zero-shot mode, and Zoo3D₁ widens the gap to +11.9 and +12.6. On posed images, OpenM3D (19.8 mAP25 on ScanNet20, 4.2 on ScanNet200) is surpassed by DUSt3R→Zoo3D₀ by +10.7 and +10.1 mAP25, and by DUSt3R→Zoo3D₁ by +13.0 and +12.3. In the unposed-image setting, no prior method reports results, but DUSt3R→Zoo3D₁ (27.9 mAP25 on ScanNet20) matches or exceeds OV-Uni3DETR's point-cloud score (25.3) without any point cloud or pose input. On ScanNet10, Zoo3D₁ (44.5 mAP25) beats the strongest baseline OV-Uni3DETR† (34.1) by +10.4, while even zero-shot Zoo3D₀ (42.1) surpasses all prior methods including GLIS (30.9) and INHA† (30.1). In the OpenM3D class-agnostic protocol on ScanNet200, Zoo3D₀ achieves P25=35.9 / R25=58.7, outperforming OpenM3D (P25=32.0 / R25=58.3) and SAM3D (P25=14.5 / R25=57.7), with the gap widening substantially at the stricter IoU-0.5 threshold (P50=27.9 vs. 18.1 for OpenM3D).

## Relation to Prior Work

Zoo3D extends the MaskClustering framework for zero-shot 3D instance segmentation into a full 3D object detection pipeline by converting clustered instance masks into axis-aligned bounding boxes, and pairs this with a novel open-vocabulary labeling module built on SAM and CLIP. For the self-supervised variant (Zoo3D₁), it adapts the TR3D sparse-convolutional detector into a class-agnostic model trained on pseudo-labels generated by Zoo3D₀. Conceptually, the paper departs from all prior open-vocabulary 3D detectors (OV-3DET, ImOV3D, OV-Uni3DETR, OpenM3D) that require exposure to training scenes, by showing that frozen foundation models (CLIP, SAM, DUSt3R) can substitute for learned 3D representations entirely, and further extends the input modality from point clouds to posed and unposed images via DUSt3R-based reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a completely training-free pipeline outperforms all existing self-supervised methods by 10+ mAP25 across multiple ScanNet benchmarks challenges the prevailing assumption that some form of scene-level training is necessary for competitive 3D object detection. Practically, the ability to run detection from unposed smartphone images (no point clouds, no camera poses) opens a path toward consumer-grade spatial understanding. Key limitations include very long inference time (~6 minutes per scene, dominated by DUSt3R reconstruction), restriction to indoor scenes and axis-aligned boxes, and dependence on the quality of DUSt3R's reconstruction (DROID-SLAM fails catastrophically as a substitute). Open questions include whether the pipeline can be accelerated, whether it generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, and whether rotated boxes can be incorporated.
