## Problem

Sparse Upcycling initializes all MoE experts with identical dense weights and a randomly initialized router, creating expert symmetry that prevents meaningful early specialization. Existing remedies—noise injection, partial reinitialization, or domain-specific fine-tuning—either yield marginal gains or disrupt the pretrained representation space, failing to provide a principled basis for expert diversity while preserving dense-model knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260400/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on CLIP ViT-B/32 and ViT-B/16 vision encoders, consuming image-text pairs (LAION-400M) for both dense pretraining and subsequent MoE upcycling. For initialization, it extracts FFN input activations from 128K calibration pairs, reduces them via PCA (8× dimensionality reduction), and clusters them with spherical k-means. The upcycled model uses top-2 routing over 8 experts per MoE layer (every other FFN replaced), producing image-text similarity scores for retrieval and class predictions for zero-shot/few-shot classification.

## Method

Cluster-aware Upcycling partitions the dense model's activation space via spherical k-means (cosine similarity, aligned with the router's directional logit computation), then initializes each expert using a data-aware truncated SVD: a whitening matrix derived from the cluster's activation covariance is applied before SVD, preserving the principal subspace of each cluster while discarding low-energy directions. The router is initialized directly from the ℓ₂-normalized cluster centroids, so early routing decisions reflect the data's semantic structure rather than random assignment. An expert-ensemble self-distillation (EESD) loss complements this by constructing a dense EMA teacher that activates all experts simultaneously; the sparse top-k MoE output is regressed toward this ensemble prediction, providing stronger corrective signal precisely when routing probabilities are near-uniform and expert assignment is ambiguous.

## Results

On upcycled CLIP ViT-B/32 (4.0B+1.3B samples), Cluster-aware Upcycling achieves 39.6 avg. MSCOCO retrieval, 57.3 ImageNet-1k Val accuracy, and 59.1 VTAB-Natural accuracy, outperforming all baselines. On the larger ViT-B/16 model, the gains widen: 43.5 MSCOCO avg., 63.2 ImageNet Val, and 63.3 VTAB-Natural. In few-shot ImageNet-1k fine-tuning (ViT-B/16), the method reaches 51.5% (5-shot) and 58.2% (10-shot), consistently ahead of Sparse Upcycling (50.9/57.8) and CLIP-MoE (51.3/58.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cluster-aware Upcycling outperforms all four baselines on the majority of benchmarks for both model sizes. Against Sparse Upcycling [20], the gains are modest but consistent (e.g., +0.2 MSCOCO avg., +0.2 ImageNet Val, +1.1 VTAB-Natural on ViT-B/32; +0.6, +0.2, +1.3 on ViT-B/16), with the largest advantage appearing in out-of-distribution VTAB-Natural. Drop-Upcycling [30] shows the weakest overall performance, trailing Cluster-aware by 1.5 points on MSCOCO avg. and 1.3 on ImageNet Val (ViT-B/32), and by 0.8 and 1.2 respectively on ViT-B/16, suggesting that partial re-initialization disrupts pretrained representations more than it helps. CLIP-MoE [48] is the closest competitor on VTAB-Natural for ViT-B/32 (58.8 vs. 59.1) but lags on cross-modal retrieval (38.2 vs. 39.6 MSCOCO avg.) and ImageNet classification. DeRS-LM [15] achieves competitive retrieval scores (39.4 MSCOCO avg.) but falls short on ImageNet Val (56.8 vs. 57.3) and VTAB-Natural (58.1 vs. 59.1), indicating that low-rank expert parameterization does not fully compensate for the lack of structured initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Sparse Upcycling [20], which initializes all MoE experts with identical dense weights and a random router, by replacing this symmetric initialization with a cluster-aware scheme derived from the dense model's own activation manifold. The key conceptual departure is treating the pretrained dense model not as a monolithic weight to replicate but as a source of semantic structure: spherical k-means on activations defines expert subspaces (via data-aware truncated SVD), and cluster centroids seed the router. The EESD loss draws on ensemble self-distillation ideas [29] but applies them at the expert level rather than the full-model level, keeping overhead low. The paper positions itself against Drop-Upcycling [30], CLIP-MoE [48], and DeRS-LM [15] as alternative upcycling strategies that address expert diversity through reinitialization, multi-stage contrastive learning, or low-rank decomposition, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The expert symmetry problem in MoE upcycling is a recognized bottleneck that limits early specialization, and this paper offers a principled, data-driven solution that requires no manual domain partitioning or additional fine-tuning stages. The approach is practical (EESD adds only ~5.3% wall-clock overhead) and the analysis linking lower inter-expert similarity and routing entropy to improved OOD generalization provides a useful diagnostic framework. However, the absolute gains over the strongest baselines are modest (typically 0.5–1.5 points), and evaluation is limited to CLIP ViT-B/32 and ViT-B/16 with 8 experts; whether the benefits scale to larger MoE models, more experts, or non-CLIP architectures remains open. The method also depends on a calibration dataset for clustering, which may be a constraint in data-scarce settings.
