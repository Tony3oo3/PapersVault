## Problem

Existing public text-video datasets consist of isolated text-video (T-V) pairs in one-to-one correspondence, failing to model inter-clip relationships. This limits models to single-clip generation and prevents learning of text-and-video-to-video (T&V2V) generation, where both visual context and textual guidance jointly condition continuation. Consequently, multi-clip video generation lacks semantic coherence, consistent characters, and smooth scene transitions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261176/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is an interleaved sequence of video clips (average 4.7 s, 1080p+) and structured text captions, arranged as [clip_caption_1 → video_clip_1 + (clip_caption_2, joint_caption_1) → video_clip_2 → …], where clip-level captions describe individual content and joint captions explicitly capture continuity and change between adjacent clips. The model consumes this via a frozen VAE (4×4 temporal, 8×8 spatial compression) and a pre-trained language model for text tokenization, with diffusion supervision applied only to visual tokens. The output is a continuation video clip that preserves entity identity, visual style, and narrative coherence with the preceding clips.

## Method

CI-VID is built through a three-module pipeline: (1) Similarity-Based Segmentation uses ImageBind embeddings on horizontally concatenated multi-frame representations to partition raw videos into candidate sequences, splitting at scene transitions (cosine similarity < 0.6) and removing overly similar clips (> 0.8); (2) Entity-Based Segmentation leverages Qwen2.5-VL-72B and GPT-4o in a four-step verification (main-entity extraction, per-clip entity check, same-person verification, cross-model validation) to enforce that all clips share a common main entity; (3) Caption Generation uses GPT-4o with sequential-frame input for detailed clip-level captions and joint-frame grid input for inter-clip relationship captions covering six aspects of continuity and change. For validation, they fine-tune NOVA-0.6B (an autoregressive model with diffusion loss, 0.6 B parameters, initialized from Emu3 pre-training) on CI-VID and evaluate via a multi-dimensional benchmark combining human pairwise evaluation, VLM-based scoring (Qwen2.5-VL-72B on six dimensions), and similarity metrics (CLIP, 1-LPIPS, SSIM) at both holistic and entity levels.

## Results

The CI-VID fine-tuned model (NOVA-0.6B) outperforms the Emu3-pretrained baseline across all evaluation dimensions. In VLM-based evaluation (0–5 scale), it improves stylistic consistency from 2.93 to 3.83, entity consistency from 2.84 to 3.73, and background consistency from 2.80 to 3.75. In similarity-based evaluation, overall CLIP similarity rises from 0.512 to 0.670 and entity-level CLIP from 0.601 to 0.702. Human evaluation confirms dominance: the fine-tuned model wins 90.0% of consistency comparisons, 80.9% of narrativity comparisons, and 78.3% of factual correctness comparisons against the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares a NOVA-0.6B model pretrained on Emu3 (baseline) against the same architecture fine-tuned on CI-VID. The CI-VID fine-tuned model wins on every reported metric across all three evaluation protocols. In VLM-based scoring, it gains roughly 0.8–0.9 points on the four coherence dimensions (stylistic, entity, background consistency, and perspective transition) while also slightly improving prompt alignment (+0.08) and visual plausibility (+0.37). In similarity-based evaluation, overall CLIP similarity improves by 0.158 (0.512→0.670) and entity-level CLIP by 0.101 (0.601→0.702), with corresponding gains in 1-LPIPS and SSIM. Human evaluation shows the fine-tuned model winning 90.0% of consistency matchups, 80.9% of narrativity matchups, and 78.3% of correctness matchups, with inter-rater agreement of 91% (including ties) and 97% (excluding ties). No comparison against other external T2V models (e.g., Sora, CogVideoX) is provided; the evaluation is strictly a controlled ablation of CI-VID fine-tuning on top of the same NOVA backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interleaved data paradigm—well established in the image-text domain (Flamingo, KOSMOS-1, MMC4, OBELICS, CoMM)—to the video generation setting, where no prior large-scale interleaved text-video dataset existed. Architecturally, it builds on NOVA (autoregressive video generation without vector quantization) for temporal modeling and MAR for spatial modeling, using a frozen VAE for spatio-temporal compression. The key conceptual departure from existing T2V datasets (OpenVid, InternVid, ShareGPT4Video, Vript, Koala-36M) is the shift from isolated one-to-one text-video pairs to coherent multi-clip sequences with joint captions capturing inter-clip relationships, enabling the previously unsupported T&V2V (text-and-video-to-video) generation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CI-VID fills a genuine gap by providing the first large-scale resource for coherent multi-clip video generation with explicit inter-clip relationship modeling, and its multi-dimensional benchmark (human, VLM-based, and similarity-based) offers a reusable evaluation protocol for this task. However, the dataset's 340K-sample scale is modest compared to existing T2V corpora (tens to hundreds of millions), and the entity-based coherence proxy may exclude valid sequences that maintain narrative continuity without a single persistent main entity (e.g., montage sequences, abstract content). Open questions include whether the interleaved format generalizes to longer sequences, whether the GPT-4o-generated joint captions introduce systematic biases, and whether the T&V2V formulation scales to open-ended creative generation beyond the controlled multi-scene prompts used in evaluation.
