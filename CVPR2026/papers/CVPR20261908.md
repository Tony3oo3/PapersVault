## Problem

Existing training-free, inversion-free editing methods that compute an editing field by differencing drifts conditioned on source and target prompts work well in multi-step diffusion but collapse when forced into one-step distilled models, producing severe object distortion and background disintegration. The root cause is that the naive drift difference is a high-energy, erratic vector field whose errors accumulate catastrophically in a single large integration step. The paper asks how to achieve stable, high-fidelity one-step editing without dedicated training or inversion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 source image, a source text prompt, and a target text prompt, and produces a single edited image. It operates entirely in VAE latent space as a black-box, querying only the model's velocity or noise-prediction output at two time points (t and t−δ) with shared Monte-Carlo noise. The default configuration requires 2 NFEs (one for transport, one for optional proximal refinement) and runs in 0.38 s on a single GPU with ~7 GB VRAM, making it model-agnostic across SD-Turbo, SwiftBrush-v2, and InstaFlow.

## Method

ChordEdit recasts text-guided editing as a dynamic optimal transport problem (Benamou-Brenier formulation) and derives a Chord Control Field: a time-weighted average of the observable drift-difference fields at two nearby time points, û = (t·R(x,t−δ) + δ·R(x,t))/(t+δ). This causal one-sided kernel smoothing acts as an L²-contraction that suppresses high-energy spikes in the naive field, guaranteeing lower supremum norms for the field, its time derivative, and its spatial gradient, which tightens the Euler consistency bound and permits a single large integration step. The transport is executed as x_pred = x_src + λ·û in one NFE, followed by an optional proximal refinement (a single predict-x₀ forward pass with the target prompt) that amplifies target semantics without reinversion. The entire pipeline is training-free, inversion-free, and requires only a single noise sample (n=1) due to the intrinsic variance reduction from the smoothed field construction.

## Results

On PIE-bench, ChordEdit (SD-Turbo, NFE=2) achieves the best CLIP-Edited score of 22.96 and a competitive PSNR of 22.20, while running in 0.38 s with only 6,988 MiB VRAM. The transport-only variant (NFE=1) reaches 23.89 PSNR and 21.87 CLIP-Edited in just 0.20 s, demonstrating the stability of the Chord Control Field. Compared to the closest one-step competitor SwiftEdit on the same model (SwiftBrush-v2), ChordEdit improves PSNR from 21.71 to 22.04, CLIP-Edited from 21.85 to 22.58, and reduces VRAM by 54% (6,988 vs. 15,060 MiB). ChordEdit is 19× faster than FlowEdit and 208× faster than Direct Inversion+PnP while maintaining comparable or superior editing quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step competitor SwiftEdit (same model, SwiftBrush-v2), ChordEdit wins on every metric: PSNR 22.04 vs. 21.71, CLIP-Edited 22.58 vs. 21.85, runtime 0.38 s vs. 0.54 s, and VRAM 6,988 vs. 15,060 MiB, while also being training-free and inversion-free (SwiftEdit is neither). Versus multi-step methods, ChordEdit is 19× faster than FlowEdit (0.38 s vs. 7.22 s) and 208× faster than Direct Inversion+PnP (0.38 s vs. 28.03 s); it matches FlowEdit on PSNR (22.20 vs. 22.17) but trails on CLIP-Edited (22.96 vs. 23.69). Against few-step editors, ChordEdit leads on CLIP-Edited (22.96) over TurboEdit (21.79), InfEdit (21.88), and InstantEdit (21.82), and is 3.4–7× faster, though InfEdit and InstantEdit achieve higher PSNR (24.14 and 23.80 respectively) at the cost of more NFEs and model-specific coupling. The transport-only variant (NFE=1) achieves 23.89 PSNR, competitive with InstantEdit's 23.80, in half the time (0.20 s vs. 1.30 s) and with far less VRAM (6,988 vs. 16,270 MiB). Overall, ChordEdit achieves the best semantic fidelity (CLIP-Edited) among all compared methods while being the fastest and most memory-efficient, at the cost of slightly lower PSNR than the best few-step baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChordEdit extends the drift-differencing editing paradigm established by FlowEdit and InfEdit, which compute an editing field as the difference between source- and target-conditioned drifts. The key departure is recognizing that this naive field, stable under multi-step integration, becomes a high-energy, erratic control signal when applied in a single step on distilled one-step models. The paper reframes the problem through the Benamou-Brenier dynamic optimal transport lens, deriving a temporally smoothed 'Chord Control Field' (a weighted average of drifts at two nearby times) that provably contracts the field's L² energy and L∞ norms. This positions ChordEdit as a principled stabilization of existing training-free, inversion-free editors specifically for the one-step regime, without requiring model-specific training (unlike SwiftEdit) or multi-step averaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ChordEdit delivers true real-time text-guided editing (0.38 s, 1–2 NFE, ~7 GB VRAM) on off-the-shelf one-step models, a practical milestone for interactive creative tools. The model-agnostic, training-free design means the method transfers immediately to any new one-step T2I model without retraining. Open questions include whether the fixed δ window and single-noise-sample assumption hold for more complex multi-object edits or video, and whether the optional proximal refinement can be made adaptive rather than a fixed single forward pass. The paper does not explore editing fidelity on high-resolution or out-of-distribution content beyond the 512×512 PIE-bench setting.
