## Problem

Reconstructing coherent images from SPAD sensors is fundamentally harder than conventional denoising because each pixel records only a binary photon detection (Bernoulli noise) at extreme sparsity (~3.5 photons/pixel), and inter-frame motion at 10k–100k fps makes alignment unreliable. Large pretrained text-to-image diffusion models, which excel at conventional restoration, break down under these non-Gaussian, ultra-sparse statistics, and prior quanta burst methods (QBP, QUIVER) operate without any generative prior. The paper also notes that color SPAD burst reconstruction had no prior learning-based solution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes either a single 3-bit color SPAD nano-burst (7 i.i.d. binary frames averaged per pixel, with a random Bayer CFA) or a longer burst sequence of binary frames (up to 100k fps, 100+ frames). Internally, all processing occurs in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE (8× downsampled), so the network never operates directly on the raw binary pixels. Output is a full-resolution RGB reconstruction (256×256 or 384×384 in experiments) or a temporally consistent video sequence, with the burst stage producing one fused latent per reference frame.

## Method

gQIR is a three-stage pipeline that adapts Stable Diffusion to photon-limited sensing. Stage 1 fine-tunes the VAE encoder with two key modifications—deterministic mean encoding (avoiding stochastic sampling under heavy-tailed Bernoulli noise) and a Latent Space Alignment loss that anchors the encoder output to a frozen copy of the pretrained encoder's clean-image latent—preventing the catastrophic encoder collapse observed with standard degradation pre-removal. Stage 2 adversarially fine-tunes a LoRA-adapted latent U-Net (initialized with diffusion weights for stable GAN start) against a multi-level ConvNeXt-Large discriminator, distilling the generative prior into a single-step perceptual enhancer. Stage 3 introduces FusionViT, a pseudo-3D miniViT that performs sub-quadratic windowed attention across time and space to dynamically weight flow-aligned burst latents (optical flow computed by RAFT on reconstructed frames, not raw quanta), replacing naive averaging with motion-aware fusion and a learned scalar modulation added residually to the center-frame latent.

## Results

On single-frame 3-bit SPAD reconstruction, gQIR achieves the highest perceptual quality scores (MUSIQ 45.61 monochrome, 42.04 color; ClipIQA 0.547 mono, 0.490 color) while fine-tuned NAFNet and Restormer attain slightly higher PSNR (28.28/28.73 vs. 27.28 dB mono) at the cost of over-smoothing. For burst reconstruction under extreme motion, Burst-gQIR achieves a cumulative PSNR of 29.83 dB across XVFI (1k fps), I2-2000fps (2k fps), and the new XD (2k–100k fps) datasets, substantially outperforming QUIVER (22.43 dB) and QBP (13.38 dB). On the full I2-2000fps benchmark, Burst-gQIR reaches 30.81 dB PSNR, surpassing the previous best (QuDi, 28.64 dB) by +2.17 dB despite a minor PPP mismatch (3.5 vs. 3.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InstantIR [19], gQIR wins decisively on all metrics (e.g., PSNR 27.28 vs. 10.79 dB mono), as InstantIR's Poisson-Gaussian noise assumption is fundamentally mismatched to Bernoulli SPAD statistics. Against fine-tuned Restormer [73] and NAFNet [7], gQIR trades a small PSNR deficit (~1–1.5 dB) for substantially higher perceptual quality (MUSIQ +5.2/+5.9, ClipIQA +0.11/+0.06 mono), consistent with the known perception–distortion trade-off. For burst reconstruction, Burst-gQIR outperforms QBP [78] by +16.45 dB cumulative PSNR and QUIVER [8] by +7.40 dB, with the largest gaps on the extreme-motion XD dataset (30.33 vs. 20.10 vs. 12.78 dB). On the I2-2000fps benchmark, Burst-gQIR surpasses QuDi [9] (the previous state-of-the-art) by +2.17 dB PSNR and +0.057 SSIM, and outperforms all other baselines (EMVD, FloRNN, QBP, Transform Denoise, QUIVER) by at least 4 dB. The advantage is most pronounced under high-speed and deforming motion, where classical align-and-merge methods degrade sharply due to unreliable motion estimation under photon starvation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: quanta burst reconstruction (QBP, QUIVER, QuDI), which follows a classical align-and-merge philosophy, and generative image restoration (DiffBIR, SUPIR, InstantIR), which adapts large text-to-image diffusion priors to conventional camera degradations. The key departure is that prior quanta methods operate without large pretrained generative priors, while prior generative restoration methods assume Poisson-Gaussian noise and break down under Bernoulli photon statistics. gQIR bridges this gap by introducing a Latent Space Alignment loss with a frozen encoder reference to prevent catastrophic forgetting, and by generalizing the align-and-merge operation to a latent-space spatio-temporal transformer (FusionViT) that dynamically weights burst frames by motion and proximity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that internet-scale generative priors can be meaningfully adapted to photon-limited SPAD sensing, opening a direction for applying large pretrained models to extreme-noise imaging domains beyond conventional cameras. The introduction of the first real-world color SPAD burst dataset and the XD extreme-motion benchmark provides the community with previously unavailable evaluation resources. Key limitations remain: the fixed 3.5 PPP assumption limits robustness to very low light, the 8-bit VAE decoder caps native HDR capability, and motion cues from the optical-flow stage can degrade under subtle inter-frame drift, suggesting that video-level or multi-frame diffusion priors may be needed for full temporal coherence.
