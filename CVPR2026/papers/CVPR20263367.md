## Problem

Modern trajectory prediction datasets exhibit a pronounced long-tail imbalance in scenario density: low-density cases dominate while safety-critical high-density interactions are severely underrepresented (often <2.5% of samples). This imbalance biases training toward majority scenarios and causes standard aggregate metrics to mask substantial performance degradation in dense, interaction-heavy settings, limiting model robustness where it matters most for driving safety.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263367/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full trajectory prediction dataset (e.g., Argoverse 1 with 323k scenarios, each containing 2s observed and 3s future trajectories at 10 Hz plus map context) and a pre-trained trajectory prediction model (HiVT-64 or QCNet) used solely for gradient feature extraction. It outputs a compact density-balanced training subset of target size B (typically 50% of the original), structured as a union of per-density-region selections. The resulting subset is model-agnostic and can be directly reused to train different backbone architectures without re-running the selection pipeline.

## Method

Den-TP operates in two stages. First, the dataset is partitioned into K disjoint density regions using agent count as a dataset-agnostic proxy for interaction complexity, with a fixed interval τ defining bin boundaries. Second, within each region, a submodular greedy selection is applied: gradient features are extracted by backpropagating the total loss (variety-loss regression + mode classification) through the pre-trained model, fused with decoder latent representations via element-wise multiplication after shape alignment, and then a score function P(S_j) combining cosine similarity to already-selected samples (redundancy penalty) and to unselected samples (representativeness bonus) is minimized iteratively. A DynamicSelect function allocates the global budget B across partitions in reverse density order (highest density first), ensuring scarce high-density regions receive proportional representation before low-density regions are pruned. Partitions where the allocated budget exceeds the partition size are retained in full, skipping gradient computation entirely.

## Results

On Argoverse 1 at 50% data retention, Den-TP achieves 0.706 minADE / 1.074 minFDE / 0.110 MR with HiVT-64, compared to 0.695 / 1.037 / 0.109 for the full dataset — a near-lossless compression. The same subset transfers to HiVT-128 (0.684 minADE vs. 0.666 full) and HPNet (0.661 minADE vs. 0.647 full), all outperforming Random, K-Means Clustering, and Herding baselines at every retention ratio tested (60%, 50%, 40%). On Argoverse 2 at 50% retention, Den-TP reaches 0.754 minADE with QCNet and 0.704 with DeMo, again the best among all selection strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Den-TP outperforms all three baselines on every metric (minADE, minFDE, MR) across all models and retention ratios. At 50% retention on Argoverse 1 with HiVT-64, Den-TP (0.706 minADE) beats Random Selection (0.750) by 0.044, K-Means Clustering (0.725) by 0.019, and Herding (0.728) by 0.022. The gap widens at lower retention: at 40%, Den-TP achieves 0.711 minADE versus 0.752 (Random), 0.732 (Cluster), and 0.722 (Herding). On Argoverse 2 at 50% retention, Den-TP (0.754 minADE, QCNet) leads Random (0.805) by 0.051, Cluster (0.798) by 0.044, and Herding (0.782) by 0.028. The advantage is most pronounced in high-density scenarios (>80 agents), where Den-TP cuts the missing rate by over 8% relative to Random Selection, confirming that density-aware submodular selection captures interaction patterns that generic representativeness criteria (clustering, herding) miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes submodular greedy selection and gradient-based influence scoring from the data subset selection and coreset literature (originally developed for image classification and generic annotation) and adapts them to trajectory prediction with a density-aware partitioning step. It departs from prior long-tail trajectory prediction work (contrastive learning, debiased representations) by operating at the data curation level rather than modifying model features or loss functions. The density-conditioned evaluation protocol also extends standard aggregate metrics (minADE, minFDE, MR) into a stratified framework that has not been systematically applied in the trajectory prediction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that data curation—specifically balancing scenario density—can match or exceed full-dataset performance while halving training cost, offering a practical path to more robust and efficient trajectory prediction. The density-conditioned evaluation protocol exposes safety-critical failure modes (e.g., >8% MR reduction in >80-agent scenarios) that aggregate metrics conceal, which is important for deployment safety. Limitations include non-negligible one-time gradient extraction overhead, performance degradation below ~30% retention, and evaluation restricted to Argoverse 1 and 2; extending to richer density indicators (e.g., interaction graph complexity) and other driving tasks remains open.
