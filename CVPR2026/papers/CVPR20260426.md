## Problem

Billion-parameter vision-language models have set new OCR benchmarks but suffer from imprecise text localization, hallucinated text in complex layouts, and prohibitive computational costs for production deployment. Meanwhile, lightweight two-stage OCR pipelines have hit a performance plateau because prior work focused on architectural tweaks while treating training data as a static, unexamined component. This paper asks whether a meticulously optimized 5M-parameter model can rival billion-parameter VLMs on OCR by systematically scaling data quality rather than model size.

## Input / Output

The system accepts arbitrary RGB images containing text (documents, scene photos, handwritten notes, artistic text) and produces two outputs: polygon-level bounding boxes for each detected text line (from the DB-based detector) and the decoded character sequence for each cropped line (from the recognizer). The two-stage design means the detector first localizes textline regions, which are then individually cropped and fed to the recognition model, enabling tight localization that VLMs typically cannot provide. The total parameter budget is 5M across both stages, making it deployable on edge devices and high-throughput cloud services.

## Method

PP-OCRv5 inherits the PP-OCRv4 architecture (DBNet-based detection with PP-LCNetV3 backbone and SVTR_LCNet recognition with GTC-guided CTC training) but shifts the innovation to a data-centric scaling methodology applied to the recognition model's 22.6M-sample training set. The core mechanism decomposes data quality into three experimentally validated dimensions: (1) Data Difficulty, where a bootstrap model's per-sample confidence score identifies a 'sweet spot' at 0.95–0.97 that balances informativeness against label noise; (2) Data Accuracy, showing the model tolerates up to 20% label corruption with only 1.33 pp accuracy loss, enabling cheap VLM-assisted annotation; and (3) Data Diversity, where CLIP visual features are K-Means clustered into 1,000 groups and uniform sampling across more clusters yields monotonic accuracy gains at fixed data volume. The final dataset is concentrated in the confidence sweet spot (48.5%) while spanning 16 categories from printed Chinese/English to artistic text and emoji, with dynamic oversampling during training to balance category learning.

## Results

On OmniDocBench, PP-OCRv5 (5M params) achieves a Normalized Edit Distance of 0.067 (ALL avg), the best among all specialized OCR engines, compared to 0.090 for Surya, 0.137 for Mathpix, and 0.173 for Qwen2-VL (72B). On the in-house multi-scenario benchmark, PP-OCRv5 raises weighted recognition accuracy from 53.0% (PP-OCRv4) to 80.1%, a 27.1-point gain, with particularly large improvements in handwritten English (25.5%→49.4%), Traditional Chinese (32.2%→72.0%), and Japanese (25.9%→57.9%). PP-OCRv5 also shows exceptional robustness on rotated text (Rotate90: 0.012, Rotate270: 0.139), where all VLMs and other OCR tools degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct predecessor PP-OCRv3, PP-OCRv5 more than doubles the weighted recognition accuracy on the in-house benchmark (80.1% vs. 42.5%), with the largest gains in handwritten English (+27.2 pts), Traditional Chinese (+58.5 pts), and Japanese (+30.0 pts). On OmniDocBench, PP-OCRv5 (5M params) substantially outperforms Qwen2-VL 72B (0.067 vs. 0.173 ALL avg edit distance), a 2.6× improvement, while using roughly 14,000× fewer parameters. PP-OCRv5 also beats all other specialized OCR engines (Surya 0.090, Mathpix 0.137, Tesseract 0.324, EasyOCR 0.329) on the overall metric. The only model that surpasses PP-OCRv5 on OmniDocBench is Qwen3-VL (235B, 0.026 ALL avg), but PP-OCRv5 matches or exceeds it on rotated text (Rotate90: 0.012 vs. 0.023) and uses 47,000× fewer parameters. PP-OCRv5's advantage is most pronounced on challenging layouts (rotation, multi-color backgrounds) where VLMs degrade sharply, confirming that the two-stage pipeline's explicit localization stage provides a structural advantage for precision OCR.

## Relation to Prior Work

PP-OCRv5 extends the PP-OCR lineage (v3, v4) of lightweight two-stage OCR pipelines built on DBNet detection and SVTR-based recognition, but deliberately shifts the optimization focus from architectural innovation to a systematic data-curation framework. The data-centric methodology draws on Data-Centric AI principles, using confidence-based difficulty filtering, controlled label-noise injection, and CLIP-feature clustering to quantify and optimize training data along three axes. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a counterpoint to the VLM-for-OCR trend (GPT-4o, Qwen-VL, Gemini), arguing that the two-stage pipeline's performance ceiling was previously underestimated due to insufficient attention to data quality rather than model capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 5M-parameter, open-source OCR system that is trivially deployable on edge devices and high-throughput servers, with a reproducible data-curation recipe that practitioners can apply to their own OCR or other specialized vision tasks. The finding that feature diversity (not raw volume) is the primary driver of generalization, and that the model tolerates ~20% label noise with minimal degradation, offers concrete guidance for scaling data pipelines. Open questions include whether the data-centric framework transfers to detection or to other modalities, whether the in-house benchmark's 12-category coverage is sufficient to validate the 'rivaling VLMs' claim, and whether the confidence-based sweet-spot finding holds across different backbone architectures or task domains.
