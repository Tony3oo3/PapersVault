## Problem

Backdoor attacks on fine-tuned LVLMs implant trigger-activated malicious behaviors, yet existing defenses either require expensive retraining of adapter/LoRA parameters or rely on pixel-space perturbations that prove largely ineffective against LVLM backdoors. The paper identifies that the spurious trigger-target correlation in LVLMs is insensitive to pixel changes but highly fragile to attention perturbations, and asks whether a training-free, test-time defense can exploit this attention-level vulnerability without degrading clean utility.

## Input / Output

CleanSight consumes a standard image (224×224 or 336×336 depending on the LVLM) and a text prompt, and produces a binary poisoned/clean flag plus the model's text response (VQA answer or image caption). Detection requires a small clean validation set (default 200 samples) to estimate per-head attention-ratio statistics, but no poisoned samples or model retraining. The pruning stage modifies internal attention logits in-place during the forward pass, so the output format is identical to the unmodified LVLM—no additional decoding steps or external models are invoked.

## Method

CleanSight is a training-free, plug-and-play test-time defense built on the observation that backdoored LVLMs exhibit 'attention stealing': trigger-bearing visual tokens abnormally capture cross-modal attention that would otherwise go to the text prompt. For detection, it computes head-specific visual-to-text attention ratios (S^{ℓ,h}) in a small set of middle cross-modal fusion layers (e.g., layers 10–12), concatenates them into a feature vector, and flags inputs whose whitened ℓ₂ deviation from a clean reference distribution exceeds a 99th-percentile threshold. For purification, it identifies visual tokens whose attention in any head at the last detection layer exceeds a pruning threshold τ, forms a global mask as the union across heads, and injects a large negative bias (b ≪ 0) into the pre-softmax attention logits at those positions for all subsequent layers and decoding steps, effectively zeroing their attention and preventing trigger tokens from influencing generation.

## Results

On VQAv2, CleanSight reduces attack success rate (ASR) to 0% for BadNet, Blended, ISSBA, WaNet, and VLOOD, and to 3.14% for the hardest attack (TrojVLM), while preserving clean utility (CU, V-score) within 0.3–2.5 points of the undefended model (e.g., 62.63 vs. 62.89 for BadNet). On OKVQA, ASR is 0–4.74% and CU stays within ~4 points of baseline. On image captioning (COCO, Flickr8k), ASR is 0–4.65% with CIDEr scores nearly matching the undefended model (e.g., 133.28 vs. 135.70 on COCO/BadNet). In contrast, the strongest pixel-based baseline (ZIP) still leaves ASR at 7–95% and often degrades CU by 5–50 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CleanSight decisively outperforms all four pixel-based purification baselines on ASR across every dataset and attack. ZIP is the strongest baseline, reducing ASR to 7.03% (WaNet/VQAv2) and 3.91% (WaNet/OKVQA), but still leaves ASR at 74–99% for patch and global triggers; CleanSight achieves 0% in those same settings. ST defense and BDMAE are particularly damaging to clean utility: ST drops CU from 65.49 to 9.38 (ISSBA/VQAv2) and from 57.55 to 32.55 (ISSBA/OKVQA), while BDMAE drops CU to 5.73 (WaNet/VQAv2) and 35.81 (WaNet/OKVQA). SampDetox is the weakest defense, barely reducing ASR (94–100% on most attacks) while offering no CU benefit. CleanSight matches or exceeds the undefended model's CU in nearly all settings (e.g., 68.36 vs. 67.06 for Blended/VQAv2), whereas every pixel-based baseline incurs some CU loss, confirming that attention-space purification avoids the semantic distortion inherent to pixel perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of backdoor defense for LVLMs, which relies on retraining adapters or LoRA modules on clean data, and also from pixel-space input purification methods (ST defense, BDMAE, SampDetox, ZIP) that perturb the input image to disrupt trigger-target correlation. It builds on the mechanistic analysis of cross-modal attention flow in LVLMs (Zhang et al. [63]) to identify a specific abnormality—attention stealing from text to trigger-bearing visual tokens—and repurposes the visual token pruning concept from the inference-efficiency literature (FastV, LLaVA-PruMerge) as a security mechanism. The key conceptual departure is shifting the intervention from pixel space to attention space, motivated by the empirical finding that backdoored LVLMs are largely insensitive to pixel perturbations but highly fragile to attention perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CleanSight provides the first training-free, plug-and-play defense for backdoored LVLMs that achieves near-zero ASR while preserving clean utility, addressing a practical gap where existing defenses require expensive retraining and degrade downstream performance. The mechanistic finding that backdoor activation in LVLMs is mediated by cross-modal attention redistribution rather than low-level visual trigger features reframes how the field should think about LVLM backdoor vulnerabilities. Open questions include the residual late-layer dynamics observed in the logit lens analysis, the limited effectiveness against semantically entangled attacks like TrojVLM, and whether the approach generalizes to other modalities or more sophisticated adaptive attackers.
