## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based referring segmentation methods suffer from two fundamental spatial-reasoning deficiencies: language encoders (e.g., BERT) lack explicit positional priors so spatial prepositions collapse into weak lexical similarities, and self-reinforcing cross-modal attention amplifies early appearance-based misalignments throughout the Gaussian field. The paper identifies these causes through an empirical study showing that models fail on relational queries (e.g., 'the glass next to the bowl') even when they succeed on simple appearance-based prompts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reconstructed 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (N Gaussians parameterized by position, covariance, opacity, and color) and a natural-language query as input. Language features are extracted with CLIP ViT-B/16, prompt expansion is generated offline by LLaMA-3.1-8B, and object-level representations are obtained via Dr. Splat. The output is a per-Gaussian relevance score that, after rendering through the differentiable 3DGS pipeline, yields a 2D segmentation mask per view; the method operates at object-level clusters rather than individual Gaussians, reducing parameters by ~58% compared to Gaussian-level baselines.

## Method

GeoCGA reformulates referring segmentation as a cross-modal graph alignment problem with four components. (1) Geometry-Aware Prompt Expansion (GAPE) uses an LLM parser to decompose the query into entities and spatial relations, then re-encodes the expanded text to build a semantic-spatial graph with relation-weighted edges. (2) A 3D scene graph is constructed from object-level Gaussian clusters, with kNN edges encoding inter-object distance and direction, refined via learnable message passing. (3) Cross-Modal Graph Alignment (CMGA) performs node-level matching via temperature-scaled softmax similarity and relation-level matching by projecting geometric edges into the linguistic relation space, jointly optimized in a single alignment loss. (4) Geometric Consistency Supervision (GCS) penalizes cross-view variation in rendered Gaussian responses, enforcing viewpoint-invariant grounding. Training uses a joint loss L_align + λ_geo·L_geo with fixed hyperparameters across all three benchmarks.

## Results

GeoCGA achieves an average mIoU of 30.2 on Ref-LERF, surpassing the previous best method ReferSplat (25.0) by +20.8%. On LERF-OVS, GeoCGA reaches 55.6 mIoU (+5.7% over ReferSplat's 52.6), and on 3D-OVS it attains 93.7 mIoU (+1.0% over ReferSplat's 92.9). Notably, GeoCGA also reduces model parameters by 57.8% (128.48M vs. 304.18M) and FLOPs by 39.6% compared to ReferSplat, while delivering consistent gains across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoCGA outperforms all eight baselines on every benchmark. On Ref-LERF, the gains are most dramatic: GeoCGA (30.2) beats ReferSplat (25.0) by +20.8%, GOI (20.6) by +9.6%, and GS-Grouping (14.4) by +15.8 mIoU, with the largest per-scene improvement on Kitchen (+50.7% over ReferSplat). On LERF-OVS, GeoCGA (55.6) leads ReferSplat (52.6) by +5.7% and LangSplat (51.4) by +4.2%, though it slightly trails ReferSplat on the Figurines scene (63.1 vs. 64.1). On 3D-OVS, where baselines are already near saturation, GeoCGA (93.7) edges out ReferSplat (92.9) by +1.0% and LangSplat (93.4) by +0.3%. Among the older baselines, SPIn-NeRF (9.8) and Grounded SAM (15.8) on Ref-LERF are substantially outperformed, confirming that 3DGS-based methods with explicit geometric reasoning are necessary for competitive referring segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoCGA builds directly on ReferSplat (ICML 2025), the first framework for referring segmentation in 3D Gaussian Splatting, and the broader 3DGS-based segmentation literature (LangSplat, Feature-3DGS, GOI, GS-Grouping). Its key departure is reframing the cross-modal grounding problem as an explicit graph alignment task: rather than relying on implicit word-feature similarity or self-reinforcing attention, it constructs a semantic-spatial graph from LLM-expanded text and an object-level geometric graph from Gaussian clusters, then aligns them via relation-aware matching. The empirical study diagnosing two specific failure modes—absence of positional priors in language encoders and self-bootstrapping bias in cross-modal attention—provides a mechanistic justification for why prior methods fail on relational queries, positioning GeoCGA as a targeted fix rather than a from-scratch redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of substantial accuracy gains (+20.8% mIoU on Ref-LERF) with a 57.8% reduction in parameters and 39.6% fewer FLOPs suggests that explicit geometric structure can replace brute-force Gaussian-level processing, a direction likely to influence future 3DGS segmentation architectures. However, the method's reliance on a pretrained object-level model (Dr. Splat) for scene graph construction introduces a dependency that may propagate errors in cluttered or textureless scenes, and the object-level abstraction may limit fine-grained boundary quality. Open questions include whether end-to-end differentiable object discovery can remove this dependency, whether the graph formulation scales to long-range spatial relations in large scenes, and whether the approach extends to 4D or interactive settings.
