## Problem

Existing 3D semantic urban scene generation methods rely on voxel-based representations that suffer from cubic memory scaling, fixed spatial resolution (truncating tall objects like buildings and trees), and poor editability due to the absence of explicit object instances. This paper argues that a primitive-based paradigm—using compact, semantically meaningful 3D elements—is better suited for scalable, controllable, and editable urban scene synthesis, yet remains largely unexplored in generative settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D semantic layouts covering a 64 m × 64 m area, where objects are annotated as 3D cuboids or ellipsoids (11 primitive categories) and ground surfaces as extruded 2D polygons (5 semantic classes). At generation time, the model accepts an optional scene label (vegetation density: low/medium/high) for conditioning, or a binary latent mask specifying editable regions for inpainting/outpainting. Outputs are structured as 9D per-primitive feature vectors (3D center + 6D Cholesky parameters) plus 256×256 BEV raster maps (height + binary occupancy per ground class), requiring only ~2.5 MB per scene versus 3–8 MB for voxel baselines.

## Method

PrITTI is a two-stage latent diffusion framework. Stage 1 trains a Layout VAE (LVAE) with a convolutional ground encoder/decoder and a DETR-based object encoder/decoder, jointly compressing the hybrid scene representation into a disentangled 2D latent (32 channels for ground, 32 for objects) via a scatter-mean operation that maps object features onto a spatial grid. Stage 2 trains a DiT diffusion model on these frozen latents with adaLN-Zero conditioning on scene labels. A key design choice is the Cholesky-decomposition-based 6D parameterization of object orientation and size, which eliminates the sign ambiguity of quaternion encodings and yields more stable training. The disentangled channel structure enables ground-conditioned object generation and localized edits via a RePaint-inspired latent manipulation strategy, while a sliding-window outpainting mechanism (50% overlap) extends scenes iteratively to arbitrary scale without fine-tuning.

## Results

On KITTI-360, PrITTI (DiT-B) achieves a FID of 73.95, Precision of 0.712, and Recall of 0.491 for 3D semantic scene generation, substantially outperforming the best voxel-based baseline XCube (FID 94.82, Prec. 0.482, Rec. 0.230). Scaling to DiT-L further improves FID to 71.03 and Recall to 0.545. PrITTI also generates scenes in 0.58 s per sample, roughly 6× faster than XCube (3.50 s) and 22× faster than PDD (12.84 s). For reconstruction, the voxelized PrITTI achieves mIoU 70.27 with only 2.52 MB per scene, competitive with SemCity (mIoU 70.52 at 8.00 MB) while using ~3× less memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrITTI decisively outperforms all three voxel-based baselines on every generative metric. Against XCube (the strongest baseline), PrITTI (DiT-B) improves FID from 94.82 to 73.95, Precision from 0.482 to 0.712, and Recall from 0.230 to 0.491, while generating scenes 6× faster (0.58 s vs. 3.50 s). SemCity shows a large gap between its strong reconstruction (mIoU 93.81 with 1M query points) and weak generation (FID 177.62), attributed to class-biased sampling and triplane consistency issues. PDD performs worst overall, with near-zero Recall at all hierarchy levels and the longest generation time (12.84 s at Level 3). On reconstruction (Table 1), PrITTI is competitive but inherently disadvantaged: its voxelized mIoU of 70.27 trails XCube (79.47) and SemCity-1M (93.81), yet it achieves this with 2.52 MB per scene versus 3.05 MB (XCube) and 8.00 MB (SemCity), and its native primitive representation enables editability that voxel methods lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant voxel-grid paradigm in 3D semantic scene generation (SemCity, PDD, XCube) by introducing a hybrid primitive-plus-raster representation: objects are parameterized as 3D cuboids/ellipsoids with Cholesky-encoded orientation and size, while ground surfaces are rasterized into BEV height and occupancy maps. The generative pipeline builds directly on the latent diffusion model framework (Rombach et al.) with a DiT backbone (Peebles & Xie), a DETR-based object decoder (Carion et al.), and a RePaint-style latent manipulation strategy (Lugmayr et al.) for inpainting/outpainting. Conceptually, it extends the long history of primitive-based shape abstraction and scene parsing into a generative, scene-level setting for outdoor urban environments, a gap the authors explicitly identify as underexplored relative to indoor or object-centric primitive generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing dense voxel grids with compact, instance-level primitives, PrITTI makes 3D urban scene generation substantially more memory-efficient (2.52 MB vs. 8 MB per scene), faster (0.58 s vs. 2.35–12.84 s), and—critically—editable at the object level without re-running the full generative pipeline. The disentangled ground/object latent structure enables ground-conditioned object generation and localized edits without additional supervision, which is practically valuable for autonomous driving simulation and digital world modeling. Key limitations remain: the primitive geometry is coarse (cuboids/ellipsoids only), appearance synthesis relies on a separate ControlNet pipeline, and the method is validated primarily on KITTI-360. Open questions include whether more expressive or deformable primitives can close the geometric-fidelity gap, and how the approach extends to dynamic, multi-agent traffic scenarios.
