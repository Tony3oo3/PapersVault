## Problem

Current advertisement video creation pipelines are fragmented across modalities, relying on rigid templates or modular retrieval that offers limited global controllability. Existing MLLM-based approaches face constrained context windows and treat multimodal understanding and generation as separate processes, resulting in loose cross-modal coupling and inconsistent editing quality. AutoCut addresses this by unifying video, audio, and text into a shared discrete token space for end-to-end, controllable advertisement editing.

## Input / Output

The model receives product metadata, a pool of candidate video clips (sampled at 1 fps and encoded into 8 discrete tokens per frame via RQVAE with a 256×8 codebook), optional textual scripts, and background music candidates. It outputs task-specific predictions—selected clip indices, an ordered clip sequence, a generated narration script, and a BGM choice—which are then decoded through FAISS nearest-neighbor retrieval against the material database and assembled into a final MP4 via ffmpeg with transitions and subtitles. The key constraint is that all modalities are consumed as discrete token sequences within a single LLM context, rather than as raw pixels or waveforms.

## Method

AutoCut discretizes continuous video embeddings (from a contrastively pre-trained ResNet-50) and audio embeddings (from PANNs) into a unified token vocabulary using Residual Vector Quantization (RQVAE), encoding each frame or audio segment into 8 tokens from a 256-entry codebook. These multimodal tokens are appended to the text vocabulary of a Qwen3-8B backbone, and the model is trained in two stages: first, a multimodal alignment phase on ~700K advertisement samples where only the new embedding layers are updated under next-token prediction; second, a full-parameter SFT phase on ~100K curated samples targeting four editing tasks (video selection, temporal ordering, script generation, and BGM selection). At inference, the model autoregressively predicts token sequences that are mapped back to concrete clips and audio via FAISS similarity search, then rendered into a production-ready video. This token-based formulation enables the LLM to perform structured, interpretable editing decisions—selecting, reordering, and narrating clips—within a single unified reasoning pass rather than through separate retrieval and generation modules.

## Results

On the 364-video evaluation set, AutoCut achieves the best Clips Rank Accuracy (CRA = 0.10714), Script Quality (SQ = 84.63), lowest Word Count Discrepancy (WCD = 3.02), and highest Music Similarity Score (MSS = 0.3475) across all four editing tasks. Its Clips Selection Accuracy (CSA = 0.6593) is competitive with the strongest vision-language baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-32B at 0.6648) despite using lightweight discretized video tokens. Inference cost is roughly 170× lower than a GPT-4o API pipeline (~$0.015 vs ~$2.5 per 100 videos on a single RTX 4090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoCut clearly outperforms GPT-4o+MGSV on CRA (0.107 vs 0.078), SQ (84.63 vs 83.03), WCD (3.02 vs 7.75), and MSS (0.348 vs 0.266), while GPT-4o retains a slight edge on VSC (1.136 vs 1.036). Against the strongest open-source vision-language model, Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct, AutoCut matches CSA (0.659 vs 0.665) but dominates on CRA (0.107 vs 0.025), SQ (84.63 vs 78.32), and WCD (3.02 vs 12.51). The fine-tuned Qwen3-8B (Caption+SFT) baseline, which shares the same SFT data, reaches a higher VSC (1.123) but lags substantially on SQ (59.16 vs 84.63) and WCD (6.82 vs 3.02), confirming that discrete multimodal tokens provide stronger temporal and structural control than caption-based proxies. Text-only baselines (Qwen3-8B/32B Caption) and smaller VLMs (InternVL-3.5-8B, LLaVA-v1.6) are far behind on all video-related metrics, with CSA below 0.25 and CRA below 0.02. In the human evaluation, AutoCut wins against GPT-4o across all five perceptual dimensions, with the largest margin in Script-Visual Alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoCut carves new ground by repurposing residual vector quantization (originally from image generation) to discretize video and audio embeddings into tokens that share a vocabulary with text, then training an LLM (Qwen3-8B) on this unified multimodal token space for end-to-end ad editing. Conceptually, it departs from prior MLLM-based editing systems (Text-to-Edit, VC-LLM, Tree-of-AdEditor) that treat understanding, retrieval, and composition as separate modules, and from retrieval-based pipelines (VideoDiscovery, Write-A-Video) that rely on handcrafted assembly rules. The two-stage training recipe—frozen-backbone multimodal alignment followed by task-specific SFT—follows the standard paradigm for extending LLMs to new modalities, but the specific application of RQVAE tokenization to both video frames and audio segments within a single editing LLM is the paper's core architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a lightweight ResNet-50 + RQVAE token pipeline can match or exceed much larger multimodal models (Qwen2.5-VL-32B, GPT-4o) on most editing metrics at roughly 1/160th the inference cost, which is practically significant for high-volume ad production. However, the evaluation is limited to 364 videos from a single domain, and the material-based rendering paradigm means the system cannot synthesize new pixels or audio, bounding its creative ceiling by the material library. Open questions include whether the unified token space can support frame-level or emotion-aware editing, whether the approach generalizes beyond short-form ads, and whether the observed performance gap over GPT-4o persists at scale or in more diverse creative scenarios.
