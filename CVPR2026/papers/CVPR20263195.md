## Problem

When scene appearance variations outpace photon arrivals at a pixel, the inter-photon interval becomes a fundamental speed barrier to video acquisition, yet this regime is underexplored and beyond the reach of existing reconstruction techniques. Prior methods either require many photons per signal period, are restricted to periodic signals, or cannot exploit long-range spatiotemporal correlations from sparse asynchronous photon events. The paper identifies this gap through a new timescale-invariant parameterization (inter-photon frequency) and demonstrates that nearly all existing imaging systems operate far from this limit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single sequence of photon detections in the form of (x, y, t) tuples—either picosecond-resolution timestamps or binary frame indices from SPAD sensors—with no ground-truth video or external training data required. The temporal integration domain for each output frame can be a finite set of non-consecutive intervals (enabling computational stroboscopy for periodic signals) rather than a single contiguous window. Output is a sequence of reconstructed video frames representing the time-varying photon flux, normalized to [0,1], with frame count and exposure structure determined by the chosen integration scheme.

## Method

Neural Flux Fields (NFF) is a self-supervised neural network that maps a normalized temporal coordinate to a full video frame via three stages: a Fourier-feature temporal encoding (16 octaves), a pixel-independent four-layer MLP that produces a shared 16×16×256 latent tensor, and a convolutional decoder (four residual blocks with 2× upsampling, adapted from NERV) with softplus output. The pixel-independent latent space is the key architectural insight: it forces the network to learn a temporal representation shared across all pixels, so that variations at brighter pixels (low inter-photon frequency) inform recovery at dimmer pixels (high inter-photon frequency) at the same physical frequency. Training minimizes a Poisson negative log-likelihood between predicted frame intensities and per-pixel photon counts within each exposure, using Adam with early stopping over 30 epochs. For periodic signals, a computational stroboscopy module identifies the fundamental frequency via harmonic probing and constructs interleaved exposures separated by one period, effectively folding time to reduce inter-photon frequency by orders of magnitude.

## Results

The paper demonstrates inter-photon-limited videography across f_p values ranging from <1 to >10^6. On the SPAD512 elevator scene (2000 photons/pixel/s, f_p = 2.37), NFF recovers 100 kfps video and remains functional after thinning to f_p = 5284. On the high-speed milk-splashing scene, conventional cameras require f_p = 10^{-6}–10^{-4}, while NFF achieves comparable quality at inter-photon frequencies 5 orders of magnitude higher. For the UWB fan dataset (f_p > 10^6), NFF with computational stroboscopy recovers slow-motion and transient videos that no prior passive method can produce.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Quanta Burst Photography (QBP, Ma et al. 2020), NFF wins decisively: at f_p = 4.21 and 21.07 on the guitar sequence, QBP's temporal aggregation strategy fails because there are insufficient photons to accumulate, causing reconstructions to collapse to binary patterns, while NFF recovers fine guitar-string motion at both levels. Against bit2bit (Liu et al. 2024), NFF wins at aggressive thinning: bit2bit performs adequately at the original f_p = 2.82 but degrades to motion-blurred estimates at f_p = 1995 and 32359 due to its limited temporal context window, whereas NFF exploits long-range spatiotemporal correlations to maintain quality. Against Passive Ultra-Wideband (UWB, Wei et al. 2023), NFF wins on both the quanta fan scene (f_p = 44.7, where UWB's single-pixel approach loses harmonics below the noise floor and produces blurred output) and the UWB fan timestamp dataset (f_p > 10^6, where NFF with computational stroboscopy recovers cleaner slow-motion and transient videos). In all three comparisons, NFF is the only method that successfully reconstructs general (non-periodic) scenes deep into the inter-photon-limited regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

The method (Neural Flux Fields) adapts the convolutional architecture from the NERV neural video representation [4] and the harmonic probing procedure from opportunistic single-photon time-of-flight [28], combining them with a Poisson negative log-likelihood loss to reconstruct video from sparse photon timestamps. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior single-photon videography work (quanta burst photography, bit2bit, ultra-wideband imaging) by identifying a regime—inter-photon-limited videography—where scene variations outpace photon arrivals, and by introducing a timescale-invariant parameterization (inter-photon frequency, periods per photon) that unifies the difficulty of reconstruction across 14+ orders of magnitude in light level and speed. The self-supervised, no-large-scale-training paradigm follows the deep image/video prior literature [8, 19], but the paper's key insight is that these priors must operate over the full acquisition window rather than a limited temporal context to break through the inter-photon barrier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inter-photon frequency parameterization provides a practical diagnostic for practitioners: it reveals that virtually all existing imaging systems, including dedicated single-photon cameras, operate far from the inter-photon limit, meaning a large and previously uncharacterized regime of photon-starved videography remains accessible. NFF demonstrates that a relatively simple neural field with Poisson NLL can reconstruct video in this regime where specialized methods (QBP, bit2bit, UWB) all fail, suggesting that spatiotemporal priors over the full acquisition window are the key ingredient. Limitations include the need for early stopping to avoid overfitting to stochastic photon arrivals, computational cost for long-exposure queries, and quality degradation when interpolating between training time indices. Open questions include whether the inter-photon reparameterization should be used directly in the reconstruction objective, and whether combining NFF with learned generative video priors could push further into the photon-starved regime.
