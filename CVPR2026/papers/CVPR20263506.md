## Problem

Existing long-video understanding systems that rely on naive chunking with retrieval-augmented generation suffer from information fragmentation and loss of global narrative coherence, as retrieval is driven by isolated clip-level signals and lacks a hierarchical representation spanning multiple temporal granularities. This forces agents into inefficient multi-round retrievals to recover cross-segment continuity and makes entity tracking across long temporal spans particularly difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263506/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a long video (with audio track) and a free-form text question as input. Offline, the video is segmented into 30-second windows with 20 sampled frames (0.67 fps) for captioning, and audio is processed via WhisperX for ASR and speaker diarization. At inference, the agent performs up to 10 reasoning steps, sampling 30 frames per segment (1 fps) for query-aware re-captioning and up to 50 frames for visual inspection, ultimately producing a single text answer.

## Method

HAVEN constructs a four-level hierarchical database (global summary, scene, segment, entity) offline, where a key innovation is audiovisual entity cohesion: speaker diarization labels serve as a long-range consistency signal to consolidate fragmented character observations across segments, even when visual evidence degrades due to occlusion, shot changes, or off-screen speakers. Entity consolidation proceeds in two stages—embedding-based clustering followed by LLM-based canonicalization—to mitigate over-merging and over-splitting. At inference, a reasoning LLM (OpenAI o3) drives an iterative think-act-observe loop over a suite of five multi-granularity tools (scene browse, segment caption search, segment visual search via UNITE embeddings, entity search, and localized inspection), dynamically navigating the hierarchy to collect evidence and generate the answer. The entire pipeline is zero-shot, requiring no task-specific training.

## Results

On LVBench, HAVEN achieves 84.1% overall accuracy at 2 fps sampling (81.0% at 0.67 fps), outperforming the strongest baseline DVD w/ subtitle (76.0%) by 8.1 points. The method excels particularly in reasoning (80.1%) and temporal grounding (86.7%), categories where prior methods struggle. On other benchmarks, HAVEN reaches 82.8% on Video-MME (long, with subtitles), 78.2% on LongVideoBench (long val), and 81.6% on EgoSchema (val), all state-of-the-art results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAVEN outperforms all 10 baselines on LVBench overall accuracy. Against proprietary VLMs, it surpasses GPT-4o (48.9%) by 32.2 points and OpenAI o3 (57.1%) by 27.0 points, demonstrating that direct VLM inference without structured retrieval is insufficient for long videos. Among open-source VLMs, it beats Seed1.5-VL-200B (64.6%) by 19.5 points and AdaReTake (53.3%) by 30.8 points. In the RAG/agent category, the closest competitor is DVD w/ subtitle (76.0%), which HAVEN exceeds by 8.1 points (2 fps) or 5.0 points (0.67 fps); notably, HAVEN achieves this with coarser 30-second segments and fewer reasoning steps (≤10) versus DVD's 5-second segments and up to 15 steps. The gap over VideoLucy (58.8%) is 25.3 points, and over VideoRAG (49.2%) is 34.9 points, while VideoTree (28.8%) and VideoAgent (29.3%) trail by over 50 points, indicating that simple tree or iterative retrieval without hierarchical structure is fundamentally limited for long-video reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAVEN sits at the intersection of RAG-based long video understanding (VideoRAG, VGent, AdaVideoRAG) and agentic video search (DVD, VideoAgent, VideoTree), but departs from both by introducing a four-level hierarchical database (global, scene, segment, entity) constructed offline, rather than relying on flat clip-level retrieval or purely online iterative search. Its most direct predecessor is DVD [31], which also uses tool-augmented agentic search, but HAVEN replaces DVD's 5-second flat segments with a structured hierarchy and adds audiovisual entity cohesion—using speaker diarization as a long-range consistency signal for entity consolidation—that no prior video agent framework explicitly exploits. The hierarchical indexing draws conceptual inspiration from LongVLM and VideoTree's local-global aggregation, but unifies it with entity-level tracking and multi-granularity tool access in a single offline-constructed index.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that structured, multi-granularity offline indexing combined with agentic search can substantially reduce the reasoning iterations needed for long video QA while improving accuracy, offering a practical recipe for scaling video understanding beyond context-window limits. The finding that speaker identity serves as a reliable 'glue' for entity continuity across occlusions, shot changes, and temporal gaps is a conceptually useful insight for any multimodal entity-tracking pipeline. However, the heavy reliance on proprietary models (GPT-4.1 for captioning, OpenAI o3 for reasoning and visual inspection) limits reproducibility and raises questions about how much of the gain is attributable to the architectural design versus the underlying LLM quality. Open questions include whether the hierarchy can be learned end-to-end, how the approach generalizes to non-English audio or videos without any audio track, and whether the 30-second segment granularity is optimal across diverse video domains.
