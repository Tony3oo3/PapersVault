## Problem

Existing BEV-based autonomous driving frameworks treat 3D perception and dense captioning as independent tasks, optimizing each in isolation and overlooking the mutual benefits of cross-modal alignment. This separation leads to suboptimal performance on both tasks and incoherent outputs where generated captions may contradict detected objects (e.g., hallucinating a bus as a trash can). The paper asks whether explicit multimodal alignment can simultaneously improve both perception and captioning without adding inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view camera images (6 cameras, 360° FOV at 2 Hz from nuScenes) and produces two outputs: 3D bounding boxes with class labels from a transformer detection head, and per-object natural language captions from an MLLM (Llama-3.2-1B or InternLM2). The BEV perception module (BEVFormer-tiny) lifts perspective-view features into a unified top-down representation, which is then consumed by both the detection head and a Q-Former that bridges visual features into the MLLM's language space. Critically, the proposed alignment modules (BLA and DCA) are active only during training, so the inference-time architecture, parameter count (33.6M), and throughput (4.1 FPS on V100) are identical to the vanilla BEVFormer baseline.

## Method

MTA introduces two training-only alignment mechanisms. BEV-Language Alignment (BLA) enforces a contextual learning objective by projecting intermediate Q-Former hidden states (at layer ℓ=4 of 8) through a trainable MLP and matching them via MSE loss to ground-truth caption embeddings from a frozen CLIP text encoder, effectively giving the Q-Former direct representation-level supervision before it maps queries into MLLM space. Detection-Captioning Alignment (DCA) introduces N learnable prompt tokens as a shared embedding space: detection outputs (class labels and box coordinates) and captioning logits are each projected into this space via attention pooling, and a CLIP contrastive loss enforces consistency between the two modalities' outputs. The full training objective is a weighted sum of detection loss, language modeling loss, BLA loss (λ₁=1), and DCA loss (λ₂=10⁻²), with the LLM frozen except for LoRA-style adapters. The key design insight is that BLA primarily boosts detection (by grounding BEV features in linguistic semantics) while DCA primarily boosts captioning (by tying generated text to predicted geometry), and their combination yields the best joint performance.

## Results

On the TOD3Cap dataset with Llama 3.2, MTA achieves CIDEr@0.5 of 118.7 and CIDEr@0.25 of 122.8, improving over TOD3Cap by 9.2% and 8.6% respectively. On nuScenes 3D detection, MTA reaches NDS of 38.9 and mAP of 27.9 (Llama 3.2), surpassing TOD3Cap by 3.2% in NDS and 4.9% in mAP, and BEVFormer by 4.1% in mAP. In challenging rare-object scenarios, MTA delivers a 10.7% mAP gain over TOD3Cap, and in nighttime conditions a 5.7% gain. Importantly, MTA introduces zero additional inference-time parameters or latency (33.6M params, 4.1 FPS) compared to the baseline architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTA outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the direct predecessor TOD3Cap (ECCV 2024), MTA gains +9.7 CIDEr points at IoU=0.25 and +10.0 at IoU=0.5 for captioning, and +1.2 NDS / +1.3 mAP for detection (Llama 3.2 setting). Against the vision-only BEVFormer baseline, MTA improves mAP from 26.8 to 27.9 (+4.1%), showing that the alignment losses do not sacrifice detection quality. Against earlier captioning methods—Scan2Cap (CVPR 2021), X-Trans2Cap (CVPR 2022), and Vote2Cap-DETR (TPAMI 2024)—MTA leads by large margins on CIDEr (e.g., 122.8 vs. 60.6, 99.8, 110.1 at IoU=0.25), though these older methods use custom language models rather than modern LLMs, making the comparison less direct. The gains are consistent across both Llama 3.2 and InternLM2 backbones, and are particularly pronounced in long-tail scenarios (rare objects: +10.7% mAP; nighttime: +5.7% mAP over TOD3Cap). MTA matches TOD3Cap exactly in inference-time parameter count (33.6M) and throughput (4.1 FPS), confirming no runtime overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTA builds directly on the TOD3Cap framework (ECCV 2024), which first introduced BEV-based 3D dense captioning for autonomous driving by feeding BEV features and detection outputs into an MLLM via a Q-Former. The key conceptual departure is that prior work treats 3D detection and captioning as independently optimized tasks, whereas MTA introduces explicit cross-modal alignment objectives (BLA using CLIP text embeddings and DCA using learnable prompt tokens with CLIP contrastive loss) that jointly supervise both branches during training. The alignment mechanisms draw on established ideas from vision-language pretraining (CLIP contrastive loss, MSE regression) and prompt learning, repurposing them to bridge the BEV perception and language generation pipelines rather than to pretrain a new model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that explicit multimodal alignment yields a 10.7% mAP gain on rare objects and a 9.2% CIDEr gain on captioning—while adding zero inference overhead—makes this practically relevant for safety-critical autonomous driving deployment where both accurate perception and reliable language descriptions matter. A notable limitation is that experiments are confined to camera-only BEVFormer-tiny on a single dataset (nuScenes/TOD3Cap), leaving open whether the alignment mechanisms transfer to LiDAR-fused backbones, larger MLLMs, or other driving datasets. The heuristic choice of attaching BLA at the middle Q-Former layer and the different optimal losses for BLA (MSE) vs. DCA (CLIP) suggest the alignment design space is not yet fully understood, pointing to future work on more principled or adaptive alignment strategies.
