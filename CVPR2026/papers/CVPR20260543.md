## Problem

Vision Transformers frequently rely on spurious background correlations rather than semantically meaningful object features, causing severe degradation under distribution shifts. Existing saliency-regularization methods address this with coarse foreground-background masks that treat the object as a uniform region, overlooking the fine-grained internal structure (e.g., 'wings' vs. 'beak' for a bird) that actually drives robust recognition. No prior approach provides concept-level semantic guidance for steering model reasoning in a fully automated, post-hoc manner without manual annotation or full retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces a class label prediction plus a patch-level relevance map (H×W grid corresponding to the ViT patch grid) computed via AttnLRP. During fine-tuning, the model also receives automatically generated binary semantic guidance masks (same spatial resolution as the patch grid) derived from LLM-proposed concepts and VLM segmentation. The fine-tuning dataset is extremely sparse: only 1,500 images (3 per class across half of ImageNet-1K's 1000 classes), with no human-annotated segmentation required.

## Method

CFT is a three-stage post-hoc fine-tuning framework. First, an LLM (GPT-4o mini) proposes class-discriminative textual concepts per class using a label-free procedure, validated by occurrence rate and spatial coverage checks. Second, GroundedSAM (Grounding DINO + SAM) spatially localizes each concept in training images, producing a binary semantic mask S(I) via the maximum over all concept segmentations. Third, the ViT is fine-tuned to align its AttnLRP relevance map with S(I) through a two-term alignment loss (maximizing relevance within concept pixels, minimizing it in background pixels) combined with a classification-consistency loss that reinforces the model's own predicted class via cross-entropy against a one-hot target. This design requires only 50 epochs of AdamW fine-tuning on 1,500 images, uses fixed loss weights across all models, and generalizes to held-out classes unseen during training.

## Results

CFT consistently improves OOD robustness across all four architectures and five benchmarks. On ImageNet-A (natural adversarial examples), ViT-B jumps from 13.26% to 27.76% Top-1 accuracy (a 14.5-point gain), and on ObjectNet from 16.25% to 27.93%. Gains are most pronounced on SI-Score geometric transformations (e.g., SI-rotation: 40.26% → 52.15% for ViT-B), while in-distribution accuracy on ImageNet-V is largely maintained or slightly improved (80.41% → 81.35% for ViT-B). Relevance map alignment with ground-truth object masks also improves substantially (mIoU: 62.91% → 68.23% for ViT-B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFT outperforms all three baselines (GradMask, RRR, RRDA) on every OOD benchmark and model tested. The margin is largest where background cues are most misleading: on ImageNet-A, CFT achieves 27.76% Top-1 (ViT-B) versus 25.12% for RRDA (the strongest baseline), a 2.6-point gap; on ObjectNet the gap widens to 2.0 points (27.93% vs. 25.89%). On artistic/sketch datasets (IN-R, IN-Sketch) where backgrounds are less varied, CFT still leads but by smaller margins (e.g., 54.19% vs. 52.54% on IN-R for ViT-B). Notably, CFT also slightly improves in-distribution accuracy on most models (e.g., +0.94 points on IN-V for ViT-B), whereas RRR and GradMask sometimes degrade in-distribution performance. The ablation in Table 5 further confirms that concept-level guidance (CFT) beats object-segmentation-level guidance by 1.9–2.0 points on IN-A for both ViT-B and DINOv2, validating the paper's central hypothesis that fine-grained semantic concepts are a stronger supervision signal than uniform foreground masks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFT extends the saliency-guided regularization line (RRR, GradMask, RRDA) by replacing their binary foreground-background masks with fine-grained, class-specific concept masks, and by shifting from full-training regularizers to a lightweight post-hoc fine-tuning procedure. The relevance computation builds directly on AttnLRP, which the authors argue is more faithful for ViTs than gradient-based alternatives. The concept proposal and spatial grounding stages repurpose LLM-based label-free attribute extraction (Oikarinen et al.) and the GroundedSAM pipeline, respectively, in a way that prior work had not combined for supervising internal model explanations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fully automated, annotation-free pipeline (LLM proposes concepts → VLM segments them → fine-tune relevance alignment) is practical for large pretrained models where full retraining is infeasible, and the data efficiency (1,500 images, half the classes) makes it broadly applicable. The ablation confirming that concept-level masks outperform object-level segmentation masks for robustness supervision is a useful design principle for future explainability-guided training. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-ImageNet domains, how sensitive it is to VLM grounding quality, and whether the modest gains on artistic/sketch datasets (where background bias is inherently lower) indicate a ceiling for this type of spatial-regularization strategy.
