## Problem

Evaluating VLM spatial reasoning requires benchmarks that are diverse, scalable, and fully customizable across compositional, relational, and observational scene complexity dimensions, yet existing benchmarks either lack parameterized control (real-world datasets), produce physically implausible layouts at high complexity (LLM-based generators), or fail to generate dense scenes at all (procedural frameworks). No prior method can simultaneously guarantee prompt fidelity, physical plausibility, and theoretically unlimited scene variation needed to isolate specific VLM failure modes.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single natural-language scene description (e.g., 'a 30 m² dining room with 10 chairs of different types') plus optional complexity parameters (object count, occupancy ratio, camera height) and produces a rendered video sequence rather than a static 3D model or point cloud, since VLMs consume video. The camera trajectory is explicitly optimized so every task-relevant object is fully visible and unoccluded in at least one frame, and the pipeline also emits spatial reasoning QA pairs (measurement, perspective-taking, spatiotemporal tracking) tied to the generated scene.

## Method

InfiniBench is a three-stage pipeline. First, an LLM agent (Gemini-2.5-Pro) translates the scene description into procedural constraints via in-context learning over API documentation and few-shot examples, then iteratively refines those constraints using a feedback loop: the layout optimizer's failure reports (bird's-eye-view collision maps plus textual summaries of unmet constraints) trigger Chain-of-Thought reasoning that revises the constraints. Second, a cluster-based layout optimizer (built on Infinigen's asset library) replaces conventional hierarchical placement with movable clusters—groups of parent-child objects treated as single entities—expanding the action space with cluster-level move operations and performing collision checks on collective bounding boxes, enabling high-density scenes that hierarchical methods cannot resolve. Third, a frontier-based camera trajectory optimizer treats unvisited target objects as frontiers, samples candidate viewpoints around each target scored on positional validity, full object visibility, and occlusion, and connects selected viewpoints via Dijkstra's algorithm on a 2D floor plan to produce the shortest collision-free path rendered into video by Blender's Cycles engine.

## Results

InfiniBench achieves the best overall performance across all five metrics (Fidelity, CLIP, Realism, #OB, #CN) in the most challenging high-complexity settings. With a high amount of objects, InfiniBench reaches Fidelity 0.98, CLIP 29.9, Realism 0.81, with near-zero physical violations (#OB 0.1, #CN 0.0). In contrast, LLM-based methods like LayoutGPT see collision counts explode to 13.5 at high object counts, while procedural methods like Infinigen and Luminous see Fidelity collapse to 0.64 and 0.42 respectively, failing to generate the required number of objects. The ablation confirms that combining agentic constraint refinement with cluster-based optimization yields a synergistic jump in Fidelity from 0.64 (base) to 0.92 (full).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfiniBench outperforms all five baselines on the critical high-complexity settings. Against LLM-based layout generators (I-Design, Holodeck, LayoutGPT), InfiniBench matches or exceeds their Fidelity (0.98 vs. 0.90–0.93) while eliminating the physical violations that plague them: at high object counts, LayoutGPT produces 13.5 collisions and 4.5 out-of-boundary objects, Holodeck produces 9.4 collisions, and I-Design produces 10.3 collisions, whereas InfiniBench has 0.0 collisions and 0.1 out-of-boundary objects. Against procedural baselines (Infinigen, Luminous), InfiniBench dramatically improves Fidelity (0.98 vs. 0.64 for Infinigen and 0.42 for Luminous at high object counts) while maintaining comparable physical plausibility. InfiniBench also achieves the highest CLIP score (31.8 at low complexity, 29.9 at high) and Realism score (0.93 at low, 0.81 at high) across all complexity levels, indicating superior text-image alignment and spatial coherence. The key finding is that InfiniBench avoids the fidelity–plausibility tradeoff that characterizes all baselines: LLM-based methods sacrifice physical coherence for prompt adherence, while procedural methods sacrifice prompt adherence for physical coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the procedural 3D scene generation paradigm established by Infinigen and ProcTHOR, which provide the asset library and optimization-based layout engine. It departs from LLM-based layout generators (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, I-Design) that directly produce scene coordinates from text, instead separating high-level semantic planning (LLM agent generating constraints) from low-level physical execution (cluster-based optimizer). The key methodological departure from prior procedural frameworks is replacing hierarchical optimization (fix large objects first, then place small ones) with a cluster-based strategy that treats object groups as movable units, enabling dense and cluttered scenes that were previously intractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InfiniBench addresses a practical gap in VLM evaluation: existing spatial reasoning benchmarks conflate multiple dimensions of scene complexity, making it impossible to isolate whether a model fails due to visual clutter, intricate spatial relations, or occluded viewpoints. By parameterizing compositional, relational, and observational complexity independently, it enables fine-grained failure-mode analysis that aggregate accuracy metrics cannot provide. Open questions include whether the synthetic scenes adequately capture real-world perceptual challenges (e.g., lighting variation, material diversity), whether the benchmark generalizes beyond indoor scenes, and whether the identified VLM failure modes (overcounting, incorrect object referencing) persist after targeted training interventions.
