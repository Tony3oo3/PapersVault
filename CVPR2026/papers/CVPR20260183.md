## Problem

Existing visual similarity metrics (perceptual metrics like LPIPS/DreamSim, foundation models like DINOv3/CLIP) optimize for overall appearance similarity rather than identity consistency, failing to exhibit the 'selective sensitivity' humans display: invariance to contextual changes (background, pose, lighting) while remaining sensitive to subtle identity-altering differences. This gap slows progress on identity-focused tasks such as personalized image generation evaluation, instance retrieval, and re-identification, where confusing two visually similar but distinct objects is a critical failure mode.

## Input / Output

ID-Sim takes a pair of RGB images at 448×448 resolution and produces a scalar similarity score (1 − cosine similarity of projected embeddings) via a single forward pass. Beyond the global score, the model exposes both a CLS-token embedding for holistic identity comparison and a set of patch-token embeddings that support spatially-localized similarity, enabling downstream uses like region-level correspondence in personalized segmentation (PerSAM). The metric is deterministic and feed-forward, avoiding the stochasticity and prompt-sensitivity issues of MLLM-based evaluators.

## Method

ID-Sim builds on a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L backbone with rank-16 LoRA adapters and a dual-headed MLP that projects the CLS token and patch tokens into separate embedding spaces. Training uses a joint supervised contrastive objective: a global InfoNCE loss on CLS tokens (with one hard negative mined from DINOv3 nearest neighbors plus batch negatives) and a local patch loss that treats patch tokens as unordered sets and measures similarity via negative entropically regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn distance via GeomLoss), enabling soft spatial alignment across misaligned views. The training set comprises 10k triplets (30k images) spanning 10 real instance-level datasets, augmented with generative edits: identity-preserving contextual edits (S2a) serve as positives and identity-altering edits (S2b) serve as negatives, providing controlled fine-grained variation that real data alone cannot supply. This dual global+local formulation is the key architectural distinction from prior contrastive metrics, as the patch-level OT term captures fine-grained local identity cues that the global token alone misses, yielding a 40% relative improvement over CLS-only supervision.

## Results

Across 49 evaluation setups spanning instance retrieval, re-identification, and concept preservation, ID-Sim outperforms all 7 baselines in 48 cases. The strongest gains appear on context-shift-heavy retrieval tasks: +0.30 mAP over the second-best on DeepFashion2 (0.797 vs. 0.497) and +0.11 mAP on PODS. On the fine-grained SUBJECTS2K benchmark, ID-Sim achieves 0.367 AP (patch) / 0.406 AP (CLS), surpassing the second-best metric by +0.05 AP. Patch-level supervision yields a 40% relative improvement over DINOv3, and when plugged into PerSAM for personalized segmentation, ID-Sim patch features boost mAP from 0.153 to 0.436 and F1 from 0.18 to 0.409.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ID-Sim outperforms all three perceptual metrics (DreamSim, LPIPS, DiffSim) on every identity-focused task; DreamSim is the strongest of the three and performs best on DreamBench++ (consistent with its human-aligned objective), but all three show the weakest identity sensitivity in the controlled sensitivity analysis, measuring semantic or pixel-level similarity rather than identity. Among foundation models, DINOv3 is the closest competitor, performing well on lighting-variation tasks (CUTE, PetFace) but struggling with background shifts and retrieval; ID-Sim surpasses it by 13–40% depending on whether patch supervision is used. CLIP and OpenCLIP show the weakest identity sensitivity of all baselines, compressing fine-grained visual differences in favor of semantic similarity. The UNED 1st-place retrieval model (ViT-H, millions of labelled examples) achieves the second-strongest overall performance but is still outperformed by ID-Sim (ViT-L, ~10k triplets) on most tasks, particularly on fine-grained identity discrimination (SUBJECTS2K) and concept preservation. Against MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Gemini), ID-Sim matches or exceeds performance on SUBJECTS2K (0.406 vs. 0.290–0.335 AP) while being deterministic and far cheaper, though GPT-4o edges it out on DreamBench++ (0.748 vs. 0.697 Spearman) under its original rubric prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the supervised contrastive learning framework (Khosla et al.) with a dual global-local objective, using Sinkhorn-based optimal transport (Feydy et al.) for patch-level alignment across different images of the same instance. It departs from perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DreamSim, DiffSim) and foundation models (DINOv3, CLIP) by explicitly targeting 'selective sensitivity'—invariance to contextual changes while remaining sensitive to identity-altering changes—rather than general appearance or semantic similarity. The training data curation strategy, combining real instance-level datasets with generative synthetic edits for controlled identity and context variation, builds on recent work using generative data for representation learning but applies it specifically to metric training rather than retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ID-Sim provides a general-purpose, feed-forward identity metric that can be plugged into any application requiring identity sensitivity (personalized generation evaluation, instance retrieval, Re-ID, personalized segmentation), addressing a gap the authors identify as slowing progress across these tasks. The introduction of SUBJECTS2K as a human-annotated benchmark for concept preservation, combined with the systematic sensitivity analysis, gives the field a clearer evaluation framework for identity-focused metrics. Key limitations include the inability to handle multi-entity scenes without external conditioning and the reliance on a narrow definition of visual identity that excludes broader notions like aging or stylistic changes. Open questions include whether the metric can be conditioned for selective identity specification and whether it can bootstrap synthetic data generation for personalization at scale.
