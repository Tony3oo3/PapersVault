## Problem

Humanoid motion tracking suffers from a persistent agility–generalization trade-off: shallow MLP trackers trained on small corpora (~7.2M frames) either excel on in-domain agile motions but fail zero-shot on unseen styles, or generalize modestly but underfit complex dynamics. The paper argues this trade-off is not fundamental but a symptom of insufficient data scale and mismatched architecture, and asks how to scale data, model capacity, and training design jointly to break it.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the model receives a token formed by concatenating the 29-DoF proprioceptive state (per-joint positions and velocities, root angular velocity, projected gravity, previous action) with the retargeted reference joint pose from the motion clip. A sliding window of H such tokens is fed through the causal Transformer, and the output at the final position yields per-joint PD target positions that are converted to actuator torques. The entire pipeline runs in under 1.5 ms on a single RTX 4090 after ONNX/TensorRT compilation, satisfying real-time whole-body control on the Unitree-G1.

## Method

Humanoid-GPT uses a two-stage pipeline. First, the 2B-frame retargeted corpus (aggregating AMASS, LAFAN1, MotionMillion, PHUMA, and in-house data) is clustered into ~300 groups via Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME), which encodes each sequence's per-joint periodic amplitudes and frequencies extracted by Periodic Autoencoders; a PPO expert is then trained per cluster with keypoint-level position, rotation, and velocity rewards. Second, all experts are distilled into a single GPT-style causal Transformer via DAgger, where the student is supervised on multi-step action sequences in parallel, exploiting the Transformer's ability to attend to varying amounts of history and implicitly learn position-invariant temporal prediction. The causal attention mask aligns the architecture with the online deployment constraint (no future observations), and scaling both the corpus (2M→2B tokens) and model (5.7M→80.4M params) yields a predictable, monotonic improvement in tracking success rate and keypoint error, establishing a scaling law for humanoid motion tracking.

## Results

On the AMASS-test split in simulation, Humanoid-GPT-L (80.4M params, 2B training tokens) achieves a 92.58% tracking success rate, 0.0735 rad MPJPE, and 40.99 mm MPKPE, substantially outperforming the MLP baseline (76.89% SR, 0.1191 MPJPE). On real-world Unitree-G1 evaluation across four unseen dance motions, Humanoid-GPT-B achieves the lowest MPJPE (0.0856–0.1075 rad) and MPJVE (0.6158–1.2257 rad/s) across all clips, with end-to-end inference latency under 1.5 ms on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Humanoid-GPT-B outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all four unseen dance motions. Against GMT, it reduces MPJPE by 5–14% (e.g., 0.0974 vs 0.1087 on 'Can Do Can Go!') and MPJVE by 15–22% (e.g., 0.9813 vs 1.2560). Against TWIST, the gains are larger: MPJPE drops by 13–23% and MPJVE by 16–18%, with the most pronounced advantage on the highly dynamic 'Can Do Can Go!' clip (0.0974 vs 0.1253 MPJPE). Against Any2Track, the closest competitor, Humanoid-GPT-B still leads by 6–10% on MPJPE and 10–16% on MPJVE across all clips. The smaller Humanoid-GPT-S variant also beats all three baselines on MPJVE for every motion, confirming that the Transformer architecture itself—rather than sheer parameter count—drives the improvement over MLP-based trackers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-based humanoid motion tracking lineage (Dallard et al., Luo et al.) by reframing it as a GPT-style sequence modeling problem. It builds directly on DAgger (Ross et al., 2011) for the expert-to-generalist distillation stage and on DeepPhase periodic autoencoders (Starke et al., 2022) to construct the Harmonic Motion Embedding used for diversity-aware clustering. The key departure from prior work is twofold: replacing capacity-limited MLPs (ASAP, GMT, UniTracker, SONIC) with a causal Transformer that scales with data, and pushing the training corpus from ~7–10M frames to 2B frames—a 200× increase that the authors argue is necessary to break the previously accepted agility–generalization trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that the agility–generalization trade-off in humanoid tracking is a scale artifact rather than a fundamental limitation, and derives a scaling law relating performance to data volume and model capacity—a concrete roadmap for future whole-body control research. The 2B-frame retargeted corpus and the HME-based diversity-balancing pipeline are reusable resources for the community. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond a single platform (Unitree-G1) to other morphologies, whether richer modalities (vision, language, contact) can be integrated into the same causal Transformer, and whether the scaling law holds when the task space expands to interactive or multi-agent settings.
