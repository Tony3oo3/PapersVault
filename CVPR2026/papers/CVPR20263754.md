## Problem

Existing robust image fusion methods follow either an integrated hard-regression paradigm (end-to-end learning of restoration and integration) or a decoupling optimization paradigm (sequential restoration then integration), both of which fail under complex degradations. The core gap is that these paradigms prevent restoration and integration from mutually reinforcing each other: decoupled restoration cannot exploit cross-modal complementarity, while hard regression is too complex to learn effectively. The paper asks how to redefine the relationship between information restoration and integration as one of reciprocal coupling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of degraded visible and infrared images as input, where visible degradations are drawn from {low light, haze, noise} and infrared degradations from {noise, stripes, low contrast}. A shared autoencoder encoder maps both inputs into latent space, where all diffusion-based restoration and cross-modal integration operate. The output is a single fused image reconstructed by the decoder, trained with clean reference images as supervision for both restoration and fusion objectives.

## Method

ReCoFuse constructs two modality-specific diffusion modules (DiM) based on mean-reverting SDEs for restoration, and inserts time-aware cross-modal integration modules (TIM) at every diffusion sampling timestep. TIM fuses the two modalities' sampling variables into an aggregated variable that is fed back to both DiM branches for the next-step noise estimation, exploiting cross-modal complementarity to enhance degradation removal, while simultaneously serving as the fused representation at t=0. An alternating regularization mechanism trains the system by updating both TIM and DiM under the restoration loss, then freezing DiM and updating only TIM under the fusion loss (texture, contrast, and color terms), ensuring each task operates in its own domain while collaboratively approximating the ideal robust fusion function.

## Results

On the three main datasets (MFNet, FMB, LLVIP), ReCoFuse achieves the best or second-best scores on most of the six standard fusion metrics (SD, MI, EN, CC, SCD, VIF). Headline numbers include SD of 48.473 (MFNet), 38.213 (FMB), and 40.584 (LLVIP); MI of 3.114, 2.487, and 2.289 respectively; and SCD of 1.381, 1.201, and 1.248. On the real-world AWMM-100k generalization set, ReCoFuse attains the highest VIF (0.650) and competitive SD (44.469). For downstream tasks, ReCoFuse achieves the best mAP@0.75 (0.718) and mAP@0.5:0.95 (0.625) in object detection, and the highest mIoU (57.67) in semantic segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCoFuse outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of metrics across MFNet, FMB, and LLVIP, with the most consistent wins on SD, EN, and SCD. The closest competitor is DCEvo (ext.), which achieves SD of 47.524 on MFNet (vs. 48.473 for ReCoFuse) and 42.205 on LLVIP (vs. 40.584), but ReCoFuse surpasses it on MI, EN, CC, and SCD. Text-IF (retrained) is the only baseline that beats ReCoFuse on individual metrics: it leads on VIF for MFNet (0.788 vs. 0.660) and CC for FMB (0.688 vs. 0.658), while also edging ahead on MI for LLVIP (2.145 vs. 2.289 is actually ReCoFuse winning; Text-IF re. has 2.145). OmniFuse, the most recent decoupled-optimization baseline, trails ReCoFuse substantially on SD (27.226 vs. 48.473 on MFNet) and SCD (0.846 vs. 1.381), confirming the paper's argument that decoupling limits restoration quality. On the AWMM-100k real-world set, ReCoFuse achieves the best VIF (0.650) and second-best SD (44.469, behind LRRNet's 44.398 by a negligible margin), while leading on EN (7.296) and SCD (1.373). For downstream tasks, ReCoFuse attains the highest mAP@0.75 (0.718) and mAP@0.5:0.95 (0.625) in detection, and the best mIoU (57.67) in segmentation, surpassing the next-best MRFS (57.36 mIoU) and SHIP (0.623 mAP@0.5:0.95).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established paradigms in robust multi-modal image fusion: the integrated hard-regression approach (ControlFusion, MRFS, Text-IF) that learns restoration and integration jointly via implicit regression, and the decoupling optimization approach (BAFusion, DDBF, OmniFuse) that treats them as sequential, independent stages. ReCoFuse introduces a third 'reciprocal coupling' paradigm in which a time-aware cross-modal integration module (TIM) is embedded at every diffusion sampling timestep, feeding aggregated multi-modal state back into both restoration branches while simultaneously driving fused-image generation. The diffusion restoration backbone builds on mean-reverting SDEs from IR-SDE, and the encoder/decoder with cross-modal feature reorganization is inherited from OmniFuse, but the key architectural novelty is the bidirectional coupling between restoration and integration at each denoising step, trained via an alternating regularization schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reciprocal coupling framing offers a principled answer to a long-standing design question in robust fusion—whether restoration and integration should be coupled or separated—and the consistent gains across five degradation types and three datasets suggest the mutual-reinforcement hypothesis is empirically valid. However, the approach inherits the computational cost of iterative diffusion sampling and is validated only on IR-VIS pairs with a fixed set of degradations; whether the coupling mechanism generalizes to additional modalities (e.g., LiDAR, radar) or to unseen degradation combinations remains open. The alternating regularization, while effective, adds training complexity that may be difficult to tune in practice, and the reliance on clean reference images during training limits deployment in truly unsupervised settings.
