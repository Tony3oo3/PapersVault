## Problem

On-device retraining of pre-trained DNNs is impractical on edge devices because gradient-based layer selection methods (e.g., Fisher Information, ElasticTrainer) require at least one full backpropagation, labeled data, or server-side meta-training, all of which exceed the memory and compute budgets of constrained hardware. No existing approach performs layer selection using forward passes alone while remaining label-free and server-independent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262744/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained DNN (ResNet50, VGG16, MobileNetV2, or ViT-B16 with ImageNet weights) and a local dataset D of 224×224 RGB images that may be unlabeled, along with a resource budget parameter ρ ∈ [0,1] controlling the fraction of layers to retrain. It produces a ranked subset of layers (and optionally a fraction ρ_ch of channels within those layers) selected in a single forward-pass step, which are then fine-tuned on D. A key practical detail is that Betti numbers are computed by pooling activations across multiple small forward passes (e.g., 5 passes of 8 samples) to ensure reliable topological estimates, after which the original small batch size is restored for retraining.

## Method

AdaBet selects layers for retraining by computing the first Betti number b₁ of each layer's activation space via persistent homology (using Ripser) on forward-pass activations from a local data batch, then normalizing by the activation tensor size to balance topological complexity against memory/compute cost. The core insight is that high b₁ indicates entangled, loop-rich activation geometry signaling misalignment with the target task, making those layers the most beneficial to update, while low b₁ layers encode stable, near-linearly-separable features that should be preserved. Selection is a single ranking step: layers are sorted by their normalized b₁ and the top ρ fraction is chosen for retraining, with no backpropagation, labels, or iterative refinement required. The approach extends naturally to channel-level selection within chosen layers and is agnostic to the learning paradigm (supervised, semi-supervised, or self-supervised), though the paper evaluates under standard supervised fine-tuning with SGD.

## Results

Across 16 model-dataset pairs (ResNet50, VGG16, MobileNetV2, ViT-B16 × Stanford Dogs, Oxford-IIIT Pets, CUB, Flowers102), AdaBet with ρ=0.1 achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 76.26%, outperforming the second-best baseline (Last-K Layers, 73.75%) by +2.5% and Full Training (71.79%) by +4.5%. In terms of resource efficiency, AdaBet reduces average peak memory consumption by 40% relative to full-model training (up to 76% for ViT), while its selection step is 45% faster than ElasticTrainer's dynamic-programming-based selection. Per-epoch retraining time is reduced by ~11% compared to Full Training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaBet outperforms all five baselines on average top-1 accuracy (76.26% vs. 71.79% Full Training, 71.39% Transfer Learning, 73.75% Last-K Layers, 59.41% Fisher Information/TinyTrain, 63.35% PruneTrain, and 64.68% ElasticTrainer). The largest margin is over Fisher Information-based selection (+16.9%), which the paper attributes to FI's instability across batches and dependence on a full backpropagation pass; ElasticTrainer is the closest competitor but still trails by +11.6% on average, with AdaBet also using 40% less peak memory and 45% less selection time. AdaBet does not win every individual cell—ElasticTrainer edges it on Stanford Dogs ViT (87.79% vs. 85.12%) and Full Training leads on CUB ViT (85.73% vs. 79.47%)—but it dominates on the fine-grained CUB dataset for CNNs (e.g., +11.6% over Full Training on ResNet50) and on Oxford-IIIT Pets across all architectures. In terms of memory, AdaBet's peak training memory is comparable to inference-only or Transfer Learning (e.g., 261.78 MB vs. 243.04 MB for ResNet50), whereas ElasticTrainer and Fisher Information require full-backpropagation-level memory during their selection phase (777.12 MB for ResNet50).

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaBet repurposes topological data analysis—specifically the first Betti number of activation spaces, previously studied as a theoretical capacity metric by Corneanu et al. (CVPR 2019, 2020)—as a practical, gradient-free layer selection criterion for on-device retraining. It departs from the dominant gradient-based selection lineage (Fisher Information in TinyTrain, dynamic programming in ElasticTrainer, structured pruning in PruneTrain) by eliminating the need for any backpropagation, labeled data, or server-side meta-training during the selection phase. The paper positions itself as the first method satisfying all three constraints simultaneously (gradient-free, label-free, server-free), as summarized in its comparison table against prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a single forward pass through a pre-trained model suffices to identify which layers to retrain, AdaBet opens a path toward truly privacy-preserving, resource-constrained adaptation that prior gradient-based methods could not achieve on edge hardware. The 40% average peak-memory reduction while matching or exceeding accuracy is practically significant for mobile and wearable deployment. However, the evaluation is limited to four image-classification datasets and four architectures, and the catastrophic FI baseline scores on ViT (13–26%) raise questions about implementation fairness; broader validation across modalities, larger datasets, and distribution-shift scenarios remains open. The paper also leaves hardware-aware selection, energy profiling, and unsupervised retraining paradigms as future directions.
