## Problem

Image generation and generated image detection have evolved along separate architectural paradigms—generative networks versus discriminative classifiers—leaving detection perpetually lagging behind rapidly evolving generators. No prior framework co-optimizes both tasks in a closed loop, so detectors overfit to transient forgery cues and generators receive no forensic feedback to reduce detectable artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263823/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For detection, the model consumes an RGB image (H×W×3) paired with a natural-language instruction (e.g., "Does the image look real or fake?") and produces a binary Real/Fake label plus a free-text explanation citing specific visual artifacts. For generation, it takes a text prompt and synthesizes an RGB image via Flow Matching in the FLUX VAE latent space. The same unified backbone handles both tasks, with the image processed through two parallel encoders (SigLIP-2 for detection features, FLUX VAE for generation latents) and a shared Qwen2.5 LLM for reasoning and text decoding.

## Method

UniGenDet builds on the BAGEL Mixture-of-Transformers base and introduces a two-stage training paradigm. Stage 1 (GDUF) jointly fine-tunes detection, explanation, and generation under a single loss (BCE + autoregressive LM + Flow Matching), with a Symbiotic Multi-modal Self-Attention (SMSA) module that injects generation latents into every detector layer via cross-modal attention, letting the detector progressively internalize the generative distribution. Stage 2 (DIGA) freezes the trained detector and uses its patch-level features as an alignment teacher: the generator's intermediate features are projected and cosine-aligned to the detector's representation of ground-truth real images, effectively distilling forensic knowledge into the generator's learned representations. This creates a closed loop where detection feedback refines generation realism and generation knowledge sharpens detection interpretability and generalization.

## Results

On the FakeClue benchmark, UniGenDet achieves 98.0% detection accuracy and 97.7% F1, surpassing the strongest specialized detector NPR (90.2% Acc) by 7.8 points and the best LMM Qwen2-VL-72B (57.8% Acc) by 40.2 points, while also leading in explanation quality (ROUGE-L 56.3, CSS 79.8). On the cross-dataset DMimage benchmark, it reaches 98.6% overall accuracy and 99.1% F1, outperforming the prior best SIDA by 6.8% in accuracy. In the zero-shot ARForensics evaluation against unseen autoregressive generators, UniGenDet attains a mean accuracy of 98.1%, exceeding FakeVLM (97.1%) and D³QE (82.1%). For generation quality, UniGenDet achieves an FID of 17.5, improving over the base BAGEL model (22.9) by 5.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FakeClue, UniGenDet (98.0% Acc, 97.7% F1) decisively outperforms all LMM baselines (best: Qwen2-VL-72B at 57.8% Acc) and specialized detectors NPR (90.2%) and AIDE (85.9%), while matching FakeVLM's accuracy (98.6%) within 0.6 points but substantially surpassing it in explanation quality (ROUGE-L 56.3 vs. 32.2; CSS 79.8 vs. 59.5). On DMimage, UniGenDet (98.6% Acc, 99.1% F1) beats the previous best SIDA (91.8% Acc) by 6.8 points and FakeVLM (94.0%) by 4.6 points, with particularly strong fake-image F1 of 98.6%. In the zero-shot ARForensics setting, UniGenDet (98.1% mean Acc) edges out FakeVLM (97.1%) and far exceeds D³QE (82.1%), NPR (72.3%), and UnivFD (79.6%), demonstrating robustness to unseen autoregressive generators. For generation, UniGenDet achieves FID 17.5, improving over BAGEL (22.9) and BAGEL+GDUF (19.4), confirming that detector-informed alignment enhances visual realism. On GenEval, UniGenDet (mean 0.86) is competitive with BAGEL (0.87), matching or exceeding it in Single Object (0.99 vs. 0.98) and Colors (0.94 vs. 0.93), though slightly trailing in Counting (0.80 vs. 0.86) and Attributes (0.75 vs. 0.77), reflecting a minor multi-task trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unified generation-understanding paradigm established by BAGEL (a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture supporting both image generation and VQA) and adapts REPA's feature alignment mechanism, replacing the general DINOv2 encoder with a purpose-trained forensic detector as the alignment teacher. It departs from prior detection work (FakeVLM, SIDA, Legion) that treats authenticity identification as a standalone understanding task, and from prior generation work (FLUX, SD3) that ignores forensic constraints, by coupling both tasks in a single architecture with bidirectional information flow via the SMSA module and the DIGA alignment loss. The co-evolutionary framing extends the GAN adversarial dynamic to the MLLM setting, where the 'adversary' is a full generative model rather than a lightweight discriminator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The co-evolutionary framework directly addresses the 'detection lag' problem where detectors overfit to transient generator artifacts and fail on novel architectures; the 98.1% zero-shot mean accuracy on ARForensics (spanning seven autoregressive generators) is a strong signal of improved generalization. However, the LlamaGen sub-result (89.4%) reveals that the approach is not uniformly robust, and the generation quality (FID 17.5) still trails dedicated generators like FLUX, suggesting the detection-guided refinement has diminishing returns for pure synthesis fidelity. Open questions include whether the frozen-detector DIGA stage can be made truly adversarial (iterative re-training), whether the framework scales to video generation, and whether the 8×A100 training cost is practical for the rapid iteration cycle of the generation-detection arms race.
