## Problem

Generating physically consistent 6DoF object trajectories from egocentric videos remains challenging due to occlusions, rapid camera motion, and the absence of explicit physical reasoning in existing generative models. Diffusion-based and transformer baselines produce trajectories that accumulate spatial errors over long horizons and frequently violate collision and smoothness constraints, especially in cluttered, unseen scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262431/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D scene point cloud, ~50 nearest oriented bounding boxes (fixtures), an object category label, a target end pose in R^9, a free-text task prompt, and the first 30% of the trajectory as observed history. It outputs the remaining 70% of the trajectory as a sequence of 6DoF poses (3D position + 6D continuous rotation) sampled at 20 fps, with each frame represented as a point in R^9. The flow matching model operates on the full trajectory as a single vector, integrating a learned velocity field over 20 Euler steps from a Gaussian prior.

## Method

EgoFlow formulates trajectory generation as deterministic flow matching in R^9, learning a time-conditioned velocity field via L1 regression on linear interpolations between data and noise, rather than stochastic diffusion. The backbone is a three-stage hybrid: bidirectional Mamba layers (FiLM-conditioned by multimodal scene features) for linear-complexity temporal encoding, six Perceiver-style Transformer blocks with cross-attention to a fused conditioning vector (PointNet++ geometry, self-attended fixture tokens, CLIP text/category embeddings, goal pose) for cross-modal reasoning, and a second Mamba stack for trajectory refinement. At inference, a gradient-guided sampling loop performs 50 gradient descent steps per Euler integration step, minimizing differentiable physical costs—SDF-based collision penalties with a 5 cm safety margin, rotational consistency via cosine similarity of consecutive rotation deltas, and translational smoothness via acceleration magnitude—without any additional training supervision. This decouples motion distribution learning (training) from physical constraint enforcement (inference), avoiding train-test distribution mismatch.

## Results

On HD-EPIC, EgoFlow achieves the best ADE (0.279 m), FDE (0.102 m), Fréchet distance (0.197), and collision rate (2.5%) among all seven baselines. The collision rate of 2.5% represents a 79% reduction over the next-best method (M2Diffuser at 8.5%) and a 93% reduction over the worst (EgoScaler at 35.8%). On the zero-shot HOT3D transfer (trained on Ego-Exo4D), EgoFlow achieves the lowest ADE (0.265 m) and FDE (0.027 m), though EgoScaler attains a lower geodesic distance (0.856 vs. 1.49 rad).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoFlow outperforms all seven baselines on ADE, FDE, Fréchet distance, and collision rate on HD-EPIC. The closest competitor is GIMO, which achieves a slightly lower geodesic distance (0.725 vs. 1.141 rad) but has a 5× higher FDE (0.509 vs. 0.102 m) and a 9.4× higher collision rate (23.5% vs. 2.5%). M2Diffuser, the only other method incorporating differentiable physical costs during inference, achieves the second-best collision rate (8.5%) but still trails EgoFlow by 3.4× on FDE and 3.4× on ADE. The diffusion/flow-based robot manipulation baselines (DP3, SPOT, ManiFlow) show the weakest spatial accuracy, with ADE values 4–5× higher than EgoFlow. On the zero-shot HOT3D transfer, EgoFlow maintains its lead in positional accuracy (ADE 0.265, FDE 0.027) while EgoScaler achieves a lower geodesic distance (0.856 vs. 1.49), suggesting EgoFlow trades some rotational precision for substantially better endpoint and overall trajectory fidelity in unseen scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoFlow extends the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) from image/audio generation to 6DoF trajectory synthesis in R⁹, replacing the stochastic diffusion process with deterministic velocity-field integration. It inherits the multimodal scene-conditioning design from GMT (Zeng et al., 2026) but replaces the pure Transformer backbone with a hybrid bidirectional Mamba–Transformer–Perceiver architecture, motivated by the need for linear-complexity long-sequence modeling. The key methodological departure from prior trajectory generators (EgoScaler, GIMO, CHOIS) is the gradient-guided inference stage that injects differentiable SDF collision penalties and smoothness costs directly into the sampling loop, rather than relying on post-hoc filtering or physics simulators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 79% collision-rate reduction (2.5% vs. 11.6% without the SDF term) demonstrates that differentiable physical constraints at inference can substitute for explicit physics supervision, a practical recipe for any generative model targeting physically grounded 3D motion. The zero-shot Ego-Exo4D→HOT3D transfer suggests the learned flow is not overfit to a single scene distribution, which is important for deploying egocentric motion models in novel environments. However, the formulation assumes static scenes with known geometry and handles only rigid or semi-deformable objects, leaving dynamic clutter, volumetric deformation, and closed-loop sensory feedback as open problems that limit immediate robotic deployment.
