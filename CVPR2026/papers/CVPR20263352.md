## Problem

Low-light scene enhancement remains ill-posed because RGB sensors lose structural and color information in extremely dark regions, while event cameras provide complementary high-dynamic-range cues but suffer from noise and lack color. Existing approaches either operate on individual frames (missing dark-region details), rely on data-driven event-frame fusion (limited generalization), or apply multi-view 3DGS without physically grounded decomposition (unstable in dark areas). The paper addresses the gap of unifying Retinex physical priors, event-derived structural cues, and multi-view geometric consistency within a single self-supervised 3DGS framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view low-light RGB frames (exposure 5–10 ms, pixel intensities mostly below 50) and a synchronized event stream from a DAVIS346C camera, with camera poses estimated via COLMAP. It produces an enhanced normal-light radiance field I^r rendered per viewpoint, along with explicit per-pixel reflectance and illumination maps, and supports novel view synthesis at 83 FPS. The training is fully self-supervised—no clean reference images are required—and the radiance is visualized via gamma correction (γ=2.2) rather than direct supervision.

## Method

eRetinexGS extends 3DGS by replacing the per-Gaussian color attribute with a view-independent reflectance vector r_k and a view-dependent illumination scalar l_k (parameterized with spherical harmonics), so that alpha-blending yields separate R and L maps whose product gives the scene radiance. An event-guided masked total-variation prior penalizes reflectance gradients in non-event regions (where events indicate smooth reflectance) while preserving structure along event-aligned edges, stabilizing the Retinex decomposition under low light. A confidence-guided complementarity module adaptively weights the event loss and image loss using the recovered radiance as a per-pixel confidence proxy, trusting events in dark areas and frames in brighter regions. Two lightweight MLPs model the modality-specific degradation operators (F for events, G for frames) to align noisy observations with the radiance field, and the total loss further includes brightness and gray-world regularization terms. Training proceeds in two stages: a 3,000-iteration 3DGS warmup followed by 27,000 iterations of event-guided Retinex refinement, all self-supervised on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

On the synthetic LLFF dataset, eRetinexGS achieves 23.45 PSNR / 0.8312 SSIM / 0.0888 LPIPS on input-view synthesis and 22.67 PSNR / 0.8152 SSIM / 0.1163 LPIPS on novel-view synthesis, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. The closest competitor, EvLowLight (a learning-based F+E method), reaches only 18.18 PSNR on input views, a gap of over 5 dB. Among scene-level optimization methods, LLNeRF (17.78 PSNR input / 15.72 PSNR novel) is the strongest frame-only baseline, still trailing eRetinexGS by ~5.7 dB on input views and ~7 dB on novel views. The method also supports real-time rendering at 83 FPS on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

eRetinexGS outperforms all ten baselines on every metric for both input-view and novel-view synthesis. Among learning-based methods, EvLowLight [27] (F+E) is the closest at 18.18 PSNR input / 18.02 PSNR novel, still 5.3 dB below eRetinexGS; event-only NeR-Net [28] performs worst (8.14 PSNR) due to lack of color information. Among scene-level optimization methods, LLNeRF [48] (frame-only) leads at 17.78 PSNR input / 15.72 PSNR novel, but eRetinexGS surpasses it by 5.7 dB (input) and 7.0 dB (novel), demonstrating the benefit of event cues and explicit Retinex decomposition. LuSh-NeRF [41] and Luminance-GS [8] (both frame-only 3D/NeRF methods) achieve 16.68 and 15.11 PSNR respectively, confirming that frame-only approaches struggle in extremely dark regions. IncEventGS [12], which uses only a single event camera without frames, scores 12.04 PSNR, highlighting the importance of the complementary frame modality. The consistent large margins across all baselines indicate that the combination of event-guided Retinex decomposition, confidence-guided complementarity, and multi-view 3DGS geometry yields substantially better reconstructions than any single-modality or purely data-driven approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Retinex-based intrinsic decomposition (Cai et al. 2017), 3D Gaussian Splatting for radiance field rendering (Kerbl et al. 2023), and event-camera-assisted low-light enhancement (Liu et al. 2024, Liang et al. 2023/2024). Prior scene-level methods (LLNeRF, LuSh-NeRF, Luminance-GS) perform unsupervised decomposition but rely solely on degraded frames, while prior event-based enhancers (EvLowLight, EvLight, NeR-Net) operate on single frames with data-driven mappings and lack multi-view geometric consistency. eRetinexGS departs from both by embedding a physically-motivated Retinex factorization directly into per-Gaussian attributes (view-independent reflectance, view-dependent illumination via spherical harmonics) and using event streams as structural and photometric priors within a self-supervised multi-view optimization, rather than as a separate enhancement stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that combining event streams with Retinex decomposition inside a 3DGS pipeline can recover scene-level radiance under extreme low light without any clean reference, while retaining real-time rendering (83 FPS). This opens a path toward robust, self-supervised low-light scene understanding for applications like autonomous driving and surveillance where neither frames nor events alone suffice. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include sensitivity to COLMAP pose errors, degraded performance on sparse-event or specular/non-Lambertian surfaces, and the current restriction to static scenes. Open questions include whether the confidence-guided complementarity strategy generalizes to dynamic scenes with motion, and whether the degradation-aware MLPs (F and G) can be replaced by more principled physical models.
