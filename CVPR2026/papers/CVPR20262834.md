## Problem

Existing video diffusion models fundamentally couple scene dynamics with camera motion by treating the frame index as implicit physical time, making it impossible to independently control when events occur (world time) and where the observer is (camera pose). Camera-controlled video diffusion methods enable viewpoint manipulation but still enforce uniformly advancing world time, and two-stage pipelines that remap time before camera-conditioned generation produce poor 4D consistency. The paper addresses this by introducing explicit, disentangled conditioning on both continuous world time and camera trajectory within a single diffusion model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single input video (384×640, 81 frames), a continuous world-time sequence {τ_i} specifying the physical timestamp for each output frame, and a 6-DoF camera trajectory as conditioning signals. The source and target video latents are concatenated along the frame dimension in the DiT, so the model jointly denoises the target conditioned on the source. The output is a generated video whose scene dynamics follow the prescribed temporal pattern (slow motion, pausing, reversal, etc.) while the viewpoint follows the specified camera path, with both controls fully independent.

## Method

Built on CogVideoX-5B-T2V, the method introduces two complementary time-conditioning mechanisms: Time-RoPE, which extends rotary positional embeddings to operate on continuous world-time values so that attention logits encode the actual temporal offset τ_i − τ_j rather than a discrete frame gap, and Time-AdaLN, where a 1D convolution encodes per-frame timestamps into embeddings that drive learned affine scale-and-shift modulation of intermediate DiT features. For full 4D control, Time-RoPE is augmented with a camera-aware rotary component (following relative camera-ray encoding) to form a unified 4D-RoPE, and a parallel Camera-AdaLN branch uses Plücker-ray embeddings aggregated by a 2D convolutional encoder to predict camera-conditioned affine parameters. The model is fine-tuned on a purpose-built synthetic dataset (rendered via PointOdyssey in Blender) where each scene is rendered under multiple independently varied time-remapping functions and camera trajectories, providing explicit disentangled supervision. Training uses a progressive schedule (half then full resolution) for 40K iterations at batch size 64.

## Results

On real-world videos (100 clips from ViPE), BulletTime achieves the lowest camera pose errors (RotErr 1.47°, TransErr 1.32) and best video quality scores (FVD 2292, KVD 139.1) among all compared methods. On the synthetic 4D-controlled dataset (500 videos), it attains PSNR 24.57, SSIM 0.6905, and LPIPS 0.1265, substantially outperforming both baselines. The method also achieves the highest background consistency under disentangled camera-time evaluation (mPSNR 28.29 vs. 25.80 for ReCamMaster), confirming stronger 4D coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BulletTime outperforms both baselines across nearly all metrics. Against TrajectoryCrafter*, the improvement is substantial: rotation error drops from 5.44° to 1.47° (73% reduction) and translation error from 3.31 to 1.32, while FVD improves from 2399 to 2292 and KVD from 150.2 to 139.1. Against ReCamMaster*, the camera accuracy gains are also clear (RotErr 1.47 vs. 2.98, TransErr 1.32 vs. 1.85), with modest but consistent improvements in FVD (2292 vs. 2325) and KVD (139.1 vs. 146.1). The only metrics where BulletTime does not lead are Aesthetic Quality (0.4520 vs. TrajectoryCrafter's 0.4525) and Imaging Quality (0.5598 vs. TrajectoryCrafter's 0.5696), though the paper notes TrajectoryCrafter's camera control is substantially less reliable. On the synthetic dataset, BulletTime achieves PSNR 24.57 vs. 21.86 (ReCamMaster) and 17.72 (TrajectoryCrafter), with LPIPS of 0.1265 vs. 0.1846 and 0.3431 respectively, confirming the largest margin over TrajectoryCrafter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends camera-controlled video diffusion (ReCamMaster, TrajectoryCrafter, CameraCtrl) by introducing explicit continuous world-time as an independent conditioning signal alongside camera pose, rather than treating frame index as implicit physical time. Architecturally, it builds on the DiT backbone of CogVideoX and the Plücker-ray camera encoding of ReCamMaster, but departs from prior work by replacing discrete frame-index RoPE with a continuous-time rotary encoding (Time-RoPE) and adding a dedicated time-conditioned AdaLN branch. The disentangled 4D formulation also contrasts with Cat4D and 4DiM, which model camera and time jointly but require additional 3D/4D reconstruction stages or operate on images rather than video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled time-camera control enables practical applications like bullet-time effects, interactive XR navigation of frozen scenes, and robotics world models that prior camera-only or coarse-motion methods cannot support. Key limitations include reliance on a synthetic human-centric dataset for disentangled supervision (limiting generalization to complex real-world physics and long camera baselines) and the parallel non-autoregressive inference setting, which constrains generation to fixed-length clips. Open questions include whether autoregressive 4D diffusion can enable unbounded temporal generation, and whether the disentangled control can be learned directly from real-world video corpora without synthetic supervision.
