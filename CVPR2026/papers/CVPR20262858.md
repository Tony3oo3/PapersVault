## Problem

Diffusion models produce inconsistent sample quality due to stochastic sampling, forcing users into trial-and-error regeneration workflows. Existing remedies—training-based fine-tuning and inference-time alignment—require full denoising of all candidates and external reward models, incurring substantial computational overhead and domain-mismatch risks. No prior method exploits intrinsic signals within the sampling trajectory itself to filter low-quality samples before denoising completes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262858/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a batch of N candidate latent noise vectors, running them through a standard diffusion sampler (e.g., 50-step Heun or Euler). During the first τ denoising steps (typically τ=10–30 out of 50), it records the L2 norm of the conditional-minus-unconditional score at each step. It outputs a filtered subset of images (e.g., top 4 from 20 or 50 candidates) by discarding trajectories whose partial Accumulated Score Difference falls below a percentile threshold, thereby avoiding full denoising for rejected samples. No external reward model, fixed threshold, or architecture modification is required; the threshold is set per-prompt via percentile ranking.

## Method

The key insight is that the Accumulated Score Difference (ASD)—the sum of squared L2 norms of (S_θ(x;σ,c) − S_θ(x;σ,∅)) across denoising steps—strongly correlates with local sample density on the data manifold, as validated on a 2D fractal toy distribution and ImageNet via Avg-kNN and LOF metrics. CFG-Rejection computes this ASD over a partial window of τ steps from the end of the schedule (rather than the full T steps), normalizing each step by its noise level to remove timestep imbalance. Samples whose partial ASD falls below a percentile-based cutoff γ are terminated early, saving the remaining denoising computation. The approach is entirely plug-and-play: it requires no retraining, no external reward model, no changes to the sampling schedule or model architecture, and is compatible with any CFG-based diffusion framework (validated on EDM2-S, SDv1.5, SDXL, and Flux).

## Results

On ImageNet (EDM2-S, 32-step Heun sampler), filtering the top 10% of samples by full ASD improves PickScore from 20.38 to 20.61 and HPSv2 from 26.13 to 26.57, with τ=10 already capturing most of the gain. On GenEval (SDv1.5, guidance=9), ASD-based filtering (τ=20) raises the overall compositional score from 0.4322 (random) to 0.4785, a +10.7% relative improvement, with the largest gain (+46%) on two-object relationship prompts. On DPG-Bench (SDv1.5, guidance=9), the overall semantic alignment score improves from 62.64 to 65.58 (τ=30). Under equalized 15-second time budgets, CFG-Rejection achieves 21.12 PickScore versus 20.79 for DNO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper directly compares CFG-Rejection against Best-of-N sampling and Direct Noise Optimization (DNO) under equalized inference-time budgets on ImageNet using PickScore. CFG-Rejection outperforms Best-of-N at the tightest 5-second budget (21.04 vs. 20.92) thanks to its ability to prune low-quality trajectories early, but Best-of-N edges ahead at 10 s (21.15 vs. 21.09) and 15 s (21.23 vs. 21.12), which the authors attribute to the coarser proxy nature of intrinsic ASD signals versus human-preference-trained reward models. Against DNO, CFG-Rejection achieves a clear 0.33-point PickScore advantage at the 15-second budget (21.12 vs. 20.79), since DNO's gradient-based optimization is too slow to complete meaningful iterations within that window. The authors note that these comparisons assume external reward models are available and differentiable, a setting where CFG-Rejection's reward-free design still remains competitive, particularly under compute-constrained scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on classifier-free guidance (CFG) [12] and the interpretation from Karras et al. [19] that CFG steers trajectories toward high-density regions of the data manifold. It departs from the broader inference-time alignment literature (Best-of-N, DNO, LGD, BayesDiff) by eliminating the need for external reward models entirely, instead exploiting the score differences already computed within CFG as an intrinsic quality signal. The key conceptual leap is recognizing that the accumulated divergence between conditional and unconditional scores (ASD) serves as a proxy for sample density, enabling early-stage trajectory pruning without any post-hoc evaluation or model retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFG-Rejection offers a genuinely 'free' quality filter that integrates into any existing diffusion pipeline with negligible overhead, which is practically significant for deployment settings where reward models are unavailable or costly. The main open question is theoretical: the paper provides strong empirical evidence for the ASD–density correlation but no formal guarantee of when or why it holds, leaving practitioners without clear failure-mode predictions. The authors also note that ASD may inadvertently favor memorized high-frequency samples, and the method's behavior under different ODE solvers, noise schedules, and non-image modalities (3D, audio) remains unexplored.
