## Problem

Modulation-based flow models for color transfer (e.g., ModFlows) rely on strictly linear transport paths between source and target color distributions, which forces diverse samples through similar intermediate neighborhoods and causes trajectory misalignment, chromatic distortion, and artifacts. The paper asks whether replacing these straight-line paths with learnable nonlinear trajectories can better capture the complex, multimodal color regimes present in real and 3D-rendered imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261397/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image and a style image (RGB, SD to 4K resolution) and produces a single color-transferred image whose color distribution matches the style while preserving the content's structure. At inference, the transfer is executed by integrating the learned velocity field with an 8-step ODE solver, and the encoder (EfficientNet-B6) processes the content image to predict the Bezier flow parameters that govern the transport.

## Method

NCT replaces the linear interpolation of rectified flows with a quadratic Bezier path z_t = (1-t)²z₀ + 2t(1-t)z_g + t²z₁, where the control point z_g is predicted by a lightweight MLP, allowing the transport to bend through perceptually plausible intermediate color regimes. Training proceeds in two phases: Phase 1 independently fits a per-image velocity network v_θᵢ via flow matching against the Bezier-induced velocity, producing a dataset of (image, flow) pairs; Phase 2 trains an EfficientNet-B6 encoder augmented with a 4-expert Mixture of Modules (gated by softmax over global-average-pooled features in the 4th MBBlock) to predict the Bezier flow parameters for unseen images, minimizing a flow matching loss against the Phase 1 ground-truth velocities. The MoE module provides regime-specific expert pathways that handle heterogeneous illumination and material variations, improving generalization over a single shared encoder.

## Results

On the ModFlows benchmark (2,000 Unsplash content-style pairs), NCT achieves the best LPIPS content score (0.3067) and the lowest Edge aggregated score (0.2954), while maintaining a Lipschitz constant of 36.25—substantially lower than ModFlows (47.84)—indicating smoother, more stable color transfer. On the Media Art dataset (480 3D-rendered images), NCT again leads in LPIPS (0.3295 vs. 0.4090 for ModFlows) and PSNR (12.90 vs. 12.10). In reconstruction error (Table 3), NCT achieves 30.60±22.62, nearly halving the error of ModFlows (52.33±38.81) and more than doubling the improvement over raw Rectified Flow (71.91±54.87). A user study with 33 media art professionals shows NCT preferred by 72% of participants over ModFlows (28%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary flow-based baseline ModFlows [14], NCT substantially improves content preservation (LPIPS 0.3067 vs. 0.3927, a 22% reduction) and lowers the Lipschitz constant from 47.84 to 36.25, at the cost of a slightly higher style score (0.2371 vs. 0.2214). Compared to the classical statistical methods CT [5] and MKL [21], NCT achieves markedly better LPIPS (0.3067 vs. 0.3408 and 0.3661) and Edge scores (0.2954 vs. 0.3067 and 0.3062), though CT retains a marginally lower Grayscale score (0.3028 vs. 0.3032) and Lipschitz constant (33.73 vs. 36.25). Against deep-learning baselines WCT2 [22], PhotoNAS [23], DAST [24], and PhotoWCT2 [25], NCT dominates across all content metrics (LPIPS 0.3067 vs. 0.44–0.54) and Lipschitz stability (36.25 vs. 71–111), confirming that the Bezier flow formulation avoids the color bleeding and structural degradation typical of feature-alignment approaches. In the reconstruction error study (Table 3), NCT (30.60±22.62) outperforms raw Rectified Flow [15] (71.91±54.87) by more than 2×, demonstrating that the nonlinear Bezier trajectory preserves color information far better during bidirectional transport. On the Media Art dataset, NCT extends its lead over ModFlows in LPIPS (0.3295 vs. 0.4090) and PSNR (12.90 vs. 12.10), validating generalization to highly textured 3D-rendered content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ModFlows [14], which formulates color transfer as optimal transport via rectified flows with linear interpolation paths. The key departure is replacing the straight-line transport with learnable quadratic Bezier trajectories, motivated by the observation (and prior work [18] on rectified diffusion) that path straightness is not a requirement of OT itself. The encoder architecture draws on Mixture of Experts literature from visual tasks, inserting a gated expert module into an EfficientNet-B6 backbone to handle heterogeneous chromatic regimes. The two-phase training scheme—first learning per-image Bezier flows, then training a generalizable MoE encoder to predict them—adapts the flow matching paradigm [15] to a color-specific setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in high-resolution (4K–8K) media art and 3D-rendered content pipelines where rerendering is infeasible, and validates its approach on a real-world exhibition dataset with a user study of 33 domain professionals. The reconstruction error gap between rectified flow (71.91) and NCT (30.60) suggests that path geometry is a more significant source of color distortion than previously acknowledged in flow-based transfer. However, the method remains image-only (video extension is listed as future work), the MoE adds architectural complexity without a clear scaling story, and the quantitative gains over ModFlows on standard benchmarks are modest, leaving open whether the Bezier formulation's advantage persists at larger scale or with alternative backbones.
