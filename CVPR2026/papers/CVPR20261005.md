## Problem

EMG-based gesture recognition models suffer from severe cross-subject and cross-device generalization failure, with LOSO accuracies often dropping below 50% compared to >95% intra-subject performance. No unified self-supervised pre-training paradigm exists for the highly heterogeneous EMG data landscape (varying channel counts, electrode topologies, sampling rates, and recording protocols), leaving the field stuck in a paradigm of task-specific, user-calibrated models rather than a general-purpose foundation model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multi-channel sEMG signals (C×T matrices) from eight heterogeneous datasets with diverse channel counts and sampling rates. Rather than fixed-size sliding windows, the NCT segments signals into variable-length muscle contraction events detected via adaptive energy thresholding, which are then z-score normalized, 1D-convolutionally projected, and padded/truncated to a fixed sequence length. During pre-training the model outputs discrete indices from an 8192-entry codebook and reconstructed EMG sentences; for downstream evaluation it outputs gesture class labels under LOSO-CV.

## Method

AEMG introduces an 'EMG-as-language' paradigm where individual muscle contractions are 'words' and their multi-channel synergistic coordination forms 'sentences.' The Neural Contraction Tokenizer (NCT) performs energy-threshold-based segmentation to extract physiologically meaningful variable-length tokens, replacing fixed-window slicing. A Neuro-Syntax Transformer (NST) backbone enriches each token with joint spatiotemporal positional embeddings (anatomical electrode location, activation phase, chronological order) and applies self-attention to capture long-range neuromuscular dependencies. Pre-training proceeds in two stages: (1) vector-quantized reconstruction, where continuous token embeddings are discretized into a shared 8192-entry codebook via L2-nearest-neighbor lookup and the original signal is reconstructed, learning morphological vocabulary; and (2) cross-patch masked modeling, where a subset of contraction tokens is masked and the model predicts the corresponding collective token indices from unmasked context, learning synergistic grammar. This two-stage design separates morphology learning (VQ) from syntax learning (masked prediction), and the ablation shows removing either component causes 10–28% accuracy drops.

## Results

Under the leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) protocol, AEMG-Large achieves an average accuracy of 89.81% across four benchmark gesture classification datasets (ULB-MLG, EMG-EPN, Ninapro DB4, Toro-Ossaba), outperforming the best SOTA baseline (SCDEM, 84.02%) by 5.79 percentage points. On Toro-Ossaba, AEMG-Large (91.30%) even surpasses the intra-subject upper bound (88.82%). In few-shot adaptation, fine-tuning on only 5% of a target user's data yields 88.50% on ULB-MLG and 85.18% on Ninapro DB4, reaching approximately 90% of the full-data fine-tuning performance (91.00% and 88.05% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEMG-Large outperforms all four SOTA baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest baseline SCDEM (84.02% avg), AEMG-Large gains +5.79 points on average, with the largest margin on Ninapro DB4 (+5.77 pp: 88.10 vs 82.33) and the smallest on EMG-EPN (+1.37 pp: 88.32 vs 86.95). Against CDEM (83.96% avg), the improvement is +5.85 points, with AEMG-Large leading by 8.50 pp on ULB-MLG (91.50 vs 83.00). Against MDD (80.86% avg), AEMG-Large gains +8.95 points, most dramatically on Ninapro DB4 where MDD drops to 64.67% while AEMG-Large reaches 88.10%. Against VADA and VADA+DIRT-T (81.01% and 80.56% avg), AEMG-Large leads by +8.80 and +9.25 points respectively, with the gap widening on Ninapro DB4 (88.10 vs 72.83/70.50). Notably, AEMG-Large surpasses the intra-subject upper bound on Toro-Ossaba (91.30 vs 88.82), suggesting the pre-trained representations capture cross-subject invariants that even same-subject training does not fully exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly adapts the 'signal-as-language' paradigm from ECG pre-training (Jin et al., 2025) to EMG, replacing QRS-complex tokenization with an energy-threshold-based Neural Contraction Tokenizer that segments muscle activation bursts into discrete 'words.' The vector-quantized reconstruction and masked-modeling training objectives follow the formulation established by Jiang et al. (2024) for EEG foundation models. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant UDA-based cross-subject adaptation literature (CDEM, SCDEM, VADA, MDD) by arguing that feature-space alignment on fixed-window inputs is fundamentally limited, and instead proposes learning a universal discrete vocabulary and motor 'grammar' through large-scale self-supervised pre-training across eight heterogeneous datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AEMG establishes the first large-scale self-supervised pre-training recipe for EMG, demonstrating that a single model can generalize across devices, channel topologies, and subjects with 90%+ accuracy using only 5% of a new user's data—a practical threshold that could dramatically reduce calibration time for prosthetic and HCI applications. The counterintuitive finding that the physiologically-grounded NCT segmentation yields *lower* pre-training prediction accuracy yet *higher* downstream performance challenges the common assumption that easier self-supervised objectives produce better representations. Open questions include whether the linguistic analogy scales beyond gesture recognition to continuous control or clinical applications, whether the 8-dataset vocabulary is sufficient for truly universal deployment, and how the framework performs under real-world sensor drift and occlusion.
