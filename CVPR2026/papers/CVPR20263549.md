## Problem

Existing human motion priors—whether VAE-based (suffering from posterior collapse and temporal drift) or diffusion-based (slow inference, non-trivial conditioning)—fail to jointly model pose, velocity, and acceleration dynamics while respecting the non-commutative geometry of articulated rotations. Methods that do incorporate higher-order terms, such as RoHM and MoManifold, either oversmooth transitions or treat each joint's acceleration in isolation, neglecting inter-joint dependencies. The paper asks: can a single, geometry-aware implicit representation capture all three orders of motion dynamics as a unified, deployable prior?

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous observations—noisy 3D keypoints, partial 3D observations (e.g., below a 0.9 m mask height), 2D joint detections from off-the-shelf estimators, RGB or RGB-D video frames, 3D point clouds, or sparse keyframe poses—and produces a full temporally consistent 3D motion sequence parameterized by SMPL joint rotations, root translations, and shape. Internally, every state is represented on the product manifold (SO(3)^22 × so(3)^22 × R^{3×22}), so the output inherently includes angular velocities and accelerations alongside poses, enabling downstream physics checks (foot-floor contact, acceleration smoothness) without post-hoc differentiation.

## Method

NRMF models the manifold of plausible motions as the zero level set of three conditional neural distance fields—pose f_Φ(θ), transition f_Ψ(θ̇|θ), and acceleration f_Ξ(θ̈|θ,θ̇)—each trained by supervised regression to the geodesic distance to the nearest training example on the respective Riemannian or Euclidean sub-manifold. The network architecture combines a hierarchical encoder (mirroring the kinematic tree) with an MLP decoder, analogous to NRDF and PoseNDF. Two key algorithms make the prior deployable: (i) a three-stage adaptive-step hybrid gradient descent that projects arbitrary states onto the zero level set using Riemannian exponential maps for rotations and Euclidean steps for velocities/accelerations, and (ii) a geometric projected Euler integrator that rolls out motion by updating velocities first (Π^ω(θ̇ + λθ̈)) then integrating rotations (Π^R(Exp(α[θ̇]_x))), correcting drift at every step. At test time, a multi-stage optimization first minimizes a HuMoR-like data+pose-prior objective, then augments it with transition and acceleration prior terms plus foot-floor contact constraints, applying the integrator within each iteration for progressive refinement.

## Results

On motion denoising from noisy 3D keypoints (AMASS, 4 cm Gaussian noise), NRMF achieves 16.4 mm MPJPE (All), 17.1 mm (Legs), and 19.9 mm (Vtx), outperforming the best prior method NRDF+T-NRDF (16.7/17.5/22.6 mm) and RoHM (20.9/24.1/32.4 mm). On partial 3D observation (0.9 m mask), NRMF reduces occluded-joint error to 83.8 mm vs. 88.4 mm (RoHM) and 87.1 mm (NRDF+T-NRDF), while also achieving the lowest acceleration error (2.31 mm/s²). On in-the-wild RGB (i3DB), NRMF achieves 24.67 cm global MPJPE (All) and 11.97 cm root-aligned MPJPE, substantially outperforming HuMoR (28.22/14.51 cm) and all pose-prior-only baselines. For motion generation, NRMF attains the best motion FID (5.32) and lowest mean acceleration norm (3.18 mm/s²) among all motion priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NRMF consistently outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary motion denoising task (Table 1), achieving the lowest position errors (16.4 mm All, 19.9 mm Vtx) and acceleration error (2.25) while matching the best contact rate (0.98). Against the strongest geometric baseline NRDF+T-NRDF, NRMF improves Vtx error by 2.7 mm and Acc Err by 0.72, confirming the benefit of jointly modeling acceleration. On partial 3D observation (Table 2), NRMF reduces occluded-joint error to 83.8 mm vs. 88.4 mm for RoHM and 87.1 mm for NRDF+T-NRDF, with a 0.7 mm lower floor-penetration distance. On 3DPW refinement (Table 3), NRMF(full) achieves 66.13 mm MPJPE and 5.67×10⁻³ transition error, beating +T-NRDF (66.98/7.98) and +RoHM (69.78/12.37). On in-the-wild RGB (i3DB, Table 5), NRMF reduces global All-joint error to 24.67 cm vs. 28.22 cm (HuMoR) and 30.82 cm (NRDF-t), a ~12–20% improvement. The only metric where NRMF does not lead is pose FID in generation (Table 4), where VPoser (0.113) and HuMoR (0.278) achieve lower FIDp, though NRMF still delivers the best motion FID (5.32) and lowest acceleration norm (3.18), indicating more physically stable generated motions.

## Relation to Prior Work

NRMF extends the neural distance field lineage (PoseNDF → NRDF) from modeling only 0th-order pose distributions to the full kinematic state space of pose, angular velocity, and angular acceleration on the SO(3) product manifold. While VAE-based methods (HuMoR, PhaseMP) and diffusion models (MDM, RoHM) attempt to capture temporal dynamics, they either suffer from drift, require conditioning, or neglect higher-order terms; MoManifold introduces accelerations but treats joints in isolation. The paper's key departure is a unified, geometry-respecting formulation where all three orders are modeled as conditional zero-level sets of dedicated neural fields, combined with a novel geometric integrator for drift-free rollout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling 2nd-order dynamics (acceleration) on the Riemannian manifold, NRMF addresses a gap that causes oversmoothing and error accumulation in prior methods, yielding consistent gains across denoising, partial observation fitting, and in-the-wild recovery. The framework is versatile as a plug-in prior for optimization pipelines, but its iterative nature incurs significant runtime (minutes per short sequence), and the projected integrator lacks rigorous theoretical guarantees on optimality. Open questions include whether learning-to-optimize or principled Riemannian MCMC samplers can replace the hand-designed integrator, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or more complex articulated bodies.
