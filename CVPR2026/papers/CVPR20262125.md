## Problem

State-of-the-art feed-forward visual geometry networks (e.g., VGGT, Pi3) enforce global multi-view consistency but are constrained by quadratic global-attention cost to modest resolutions (~518 px) and short sequences, producing blurred thin structures and oversmoothed boundaries. Single-view geometry estimators capture sharp high-frequency detail but lack temporal and cross-view consistency by design. No prior method simultaneously delivers fine-grained, metric-scale geometry, accurate camera poses, and tractable runtime at high resolution over long video sequences.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an uncalibrated set of N RGB frames (video or multi-view images) at arbitrary resolution up to 2K (2048×1024) and sequence length up to 1000 frames. It outputs per-frame dense 3D pointmaps (H×W×3 in local camera coordinates), per-frame camera poses in SE(3) regressed from low-resolution features, and a single scene-wide metric scale factor. The architecture decouples spatial resolution from sequence length: the expensive global-attention path operates at a fixed ≤252 px long side regardless of input size, while the detail path scales freely with native resolution.

## Method

DAGE comprises two parallel streams and a lightweight fusion adapter. The Low-Resolution stream uses a DINOv2 tokenizer followed by alternating frame-wise and global self-attention blocks (inherited from Pi3/VGGT) to build a view-consistent representation and regress camera poses; it is trained with feature distillation from a higher-resolution Pi3 teacher to compensate for aggressive downsampling. The High-Resolution stream is a frozen 24-layer ViT from MoGe2 that processes each frame independently at native resolution to preserve sharp boundaries and small structures. A five-block adapter (each block: cross-attention with HR queries attending to LR keys/values, followed by HR self-attention) injects global multi-view context into the per-frame HR features without disturbing the pretrained single-image pathway. Custom RoPE strategies handle the resolution mismatch: interpolated RoPE for HR self-attention to avoid extrapolation at high resolution, and a 'snap-to-nearest-LR-grid' RoPE for cross-attention to align tokens across scales. Training combines pointmap L1 loss (with a single global alignment scale), pairwise relative camera pose loss, metric scale loss, surface-normal angular loss, multi-scale gradient loss on canonical inverse depth, and the distillation loss.

## Results

On video pointmap estimation (Table 1), DAGE achieves the best average rank (1.6) across 8 datasets, with Rel^p of 4.9 on GMU, 21.5 on Sintel, 2.1 on ScanNet, and 5.9 on KITTI, outperforming Pi3 (5.2, 22.0, 2.2, 6.3) and VGGT (5.4, 23.7, 2.9, 7.5) on most benchmarks. On high-resolution inputs (2K UrbanSyn, 1080p Unreal4K), DAGE shows the largest margins: Rel^p of 8.8 vs. 14.5 (VGGT) and 11.9 vs. 14.5 (VGGT). DAGE runs at 65.4 FPS at 540p (2× faster than Pi3) and 5.6 FPS at 2K, where VGGT and Pi3 run out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAGE outperforms the strongest feed-forward multi-view baselines Pi3 [79] and VGGT [74] on 7 of 8 video pointmap datasets, with the largest gains on high-resolution inputs (e.g., UrbanSyn 2K: Rel^p 8.8 vs. 14.5 for VGGT; Unreal4K 1080p: 11.9 vs. 14.5). Against the diffusion-based GeoCrafter [84], DAGE wins on Rel^p across all datasets (e.g., Sintel 21.5 vs. 25.0; ScanNet 2.1 vs. 8.3) while also predicting camera poses, which GeoCrafter does not. On camera pose estimation (Table 4), DAGE at 252px matches Pi3 at 518px (ATE 0.132 vs. 0.074 on Sintel; 0.014 vs. 0.014 on TUM) and outperforms VGGT at the same 252px resolution (ATE 0.132 vs. 0.228). On multi-view reconstruction (Table 3), DAGE is competitive with VGGT and Pi3 in sparse/dense settings (e.g., NRGBD dense: Acc 0.009 vs. 0.008 for Pi3) and substantially outperforms CUT3R and MapAny in metric-scale reconstruction (7-Scenes metric: Acc 0.034 vs. 0.189 for CUT3R). The only dataset where DAGE trails is Diode (Rel^p 9.7 vs. 9.2 for Pi3), a narrow margin likely due to Diode's specific indoor/outdoor mix.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAGE sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: feed-forward visual geometry transformers (VGGT, Pi3, CUT3R) that enforce cross-view consistency via global attention but are limited to low resolutions (~518px) and short sequences, and single-view geometry estimators (MoGe2, DepthPro) that produce sharp high-resolution pointmaps but lack temporal coherence. The paper's core architectural departure is decoupling these two concerns into parallel LR and HR streams, using MoGe2's frozen ViT as the HR backbone and Pi3's alternating frame/global attention as the LR backbone, then bridging them with a lightweight cross-attention adapter. This design explicitly rejects the prior coupling of resolution and sequence length that constrains all single-transformer approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DAGE processes 2K-resolution inputs at 5.6 FPS where global-attention baselines run out of memory, and scales to 1000+ frame sequences, opening the door to long-video geometry estimation that was previously intractable for feed-forward models. The finding that a 252px LR stream (with distillation from Pi3) matches 518px baselines on pose estimation suggests the global-attention bottleneck can be pushed much lower than previously assumed. Key limitations remain: performance degrades under rapid non-rigid motion or very low view overlap, the HR path is still memory-intensive at extreme resolutions, and dynamic scene reconstruction is not addressed—leaving open whether the dual-stream paradigm can be extended to 4D or streaming settings.
