## Problem

Despite strong performance on captioning and VQA, it remains unclear whether VLMs perform genuine visual reasoning or merely exploit linguistic priors. Existing benchmarks either rely on synthetic stimuli, operate within a single domain, or fail to structure tasks across the perceptual-to-reasoning continuum, leaving it undiagnosed where in the perception-to-reasoning pipeline model failures originate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262757/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task is presented as a single 512×512 RGB image in a four-choice (A–D) format: Level 1 shows a main image with an 80×80 px masked tile alongside four candidate patches, or an occluded scene (50–80% masked) with four full-image candidates; Levels 2 and 3 show a 3×3 Raven-style grid with one missing cell and four candidate completions. The model outputs a single letter selecting the correct option. The image-only, no-text-prompt design (beyond a minimal guided instruction naming the target attribute) is deliberate to suppress linguistic shortcuts and force purely visual reasoning.

## Method

VisRes introduces a 19,000-sample benchmark organized into three levels that operationalize increasing reasoning demands over real-world imagery: Level 1 probes perceptual completion under controlled perturbations (blur, rotation, occlusion) using DINOv2-similarity-based distractors; Level 2 tests single-attribute rule inference (color, count, orientation) over 3×3 Raven-style grids with 12 subtask types; Level 3 requires multi-attribute compositional reasoning where concurrent rules span coupled, independent, and spatially structured (spiral) patterns. The key design choice is the image-only multiple-choice format that eliminates textual supervision, enabling principled diagnosis of whether failures stem from perceptual grounding, incomplete feature extraction, or reasoning integration. The paper further contributes a controlled diagnostic protocol—varying input resolution (512→2048), toggling thinking mode, verbalizing grids as text, and probing single-cell attribute recognition—that isolates the visual-to-symbolic translation gap as the primary bottleneck, showing that models reason well on symbolic descriptions but fail to extract the same structure from pixels.

## Results

Across 19,000 evaluation samples, all evaluated VLMs perform near or below random chance (25%) on most VisRes subtasks. On Level-1 (perceptual completion), the best models achieve only 31–33% average accuracy (GPT-5: 31.10%, Gemini-2.5: 33.28%), far below the ~91% human baseline. On Level-2 (single-attribute rules), Gemini-2.5 leads at 62.29% and GPT-5 at 49.79%, while most open-source models remain between 25–47%. On Level-3 (multi-attribute composition), even the strongest model (GPT-5) reaches only 34.39%, with all models clustered in the 23–34% range, indicating that compositional visual reasoning remains largely unsolved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5 outperforms GPT-5 on Level-2 (62.29% vs. 49.79%) and Level-1 (33.28% vs. 31.10%), while GPT-5 leads on Level-3 (34.39% vs. 33.73%). GPT-4o is a notable outlier, scoring at or below random (23.86–24.12%) on Levels 1 and 3, suggesting its visual grounding is particularly weak on this benchmark. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-32B (47.29% Level-2) and Qwen3-VL-30B (46.75%) lead, but all remain 15–25 points below Gemini-2.5 on Level-2. Smaller open-source models (Intern3.5-VL-4B/8B, Kimi-VL-A3B) cluster near the 25% random baseline across all levels. Critically, even the best model (Gemini-2.5) achieves only 62% on the simplest rule-based level and 34% on compositional tasks, versus ~91% for humans on Level-1, exposing a large and consistent gap between current VLMs and human visual reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisRes extends the Raven's Progressive Matrices paradigm [32] into a multi-level diagnostic benchmark for VLMs, combining it with perceptual completion tasks inspired by amodal completion and Gestalt principles. It departs from prior synthetic-environment benchmarks (CLEVR, RAVEN, PGM, MARVEL) by using naturalistic imagery, and from single-domain naturalistic benchmarks (Bongard-OpenWorld, V-PROM) by structuring tasks across a perceptual → single-attribute → multi-attribute progression. The image-only, multiple-choice format is designed to minimize linguistic priors, building on the observation from BLINK and SalBench that VLMs' apparent competence often reflects textual shortcuts rather than genuine visual understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides a structured diagnostic framework that localizes where the perception-to-reasoning pipeline breaks in VLMs, which prior flat benchmarks could not do. The quantified modality gap (GPT-5: 85% text-only vs. ~50% visual on Level-2) sharpens the argument that current VLMs lack genuine visual-to-symbolic translation rather than logical inference capacity. Open questions include whether architectural changes (e.g., better visual encoders or explicit spatial reasoning modules) can close this gap, and whether the orientation-specific perceptual deficit (39.8% vs. 84.6% for color) reflects a fundamental limitation of patch-based tokenization. The finding that fine-tuning yields gains but remains far below human baselines (43.7% vs. 90.4%) suggests the deficit is not merely a data-exposure problem.
