## Problem

Flow Matching learns curved ODE trajectories because the independent coupling between source and target distributions produces intersecting linear interpolants, forcing the velocity field to average incompatible directions and requiring many integration steps. Existing remedies—Rectified Flow distillation, Consistency Models, and Mean Velocity Models—attempt to enforce straightness within this inherently contradictory structure, suffering from discrete approximation errors, training instability, and convergence difficulties. The paper identifies and proves that straight-trajectory learning is fundamentally incompatible with the independent-coupling formulation, motivating a new approach that disambiguates intersections rather than eliminating them.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single Gaussian noise sample X_0 and one latent code z drawn from N(0, I) (768-dimensional for CIFAR-10, processed through 2–3 MLP layers), both held fixed throughout the ODE integration from t=0 to t=1. For ImageNet 256×256, a class label is additionally provided via class conditioning. The velocity network v_θ consumes the triple (X_t, t, z) at each step, with z fused via adaptive normalization or bottleneck summation. Output is a generated RGB image (32×32 or 256×256) or 2D sample points for synthetic experiments, produced in as few as one function evaluation.

## Method

S-VFM augments the Variational Flow Matching framework with a straightness objective. A variational encoder q_φ(z | X_0, X_1, X_t, t) produces a latent code z that encodes a global 'generation overview' of the source–target pair, allowing the velocity field v_θ(X_t, t, z) to select the correct direction even when linear interpolants intersect under independent coupling. The straightness penalty minimizes the total time derivative D_t v along the trajectory—computed as a Jacobian-vector product over (X_t, t, z) and approximated by replacing the marginal velocity with the conditional velocity Δ^X—thereby enforcing vanishing time variation of the velocity field. The total loss combines the VFM objective (velocity regression + β-weighted KL regularization on the posterior) with an α-weighted straightness term, trained end-to-end. At inference, a single z sampled from the prior is reused across all ODE steps, and the straighter trajectories enable competitive generation quality with NFE as low as 1.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, S-VFM (adaptive normalization) achieves the best one-step FID of 2.81 at NFE=1, outperforming iCT (2.83), sCT (2.85), and MeanFlow (2.92). At higher NFE, S-VFM continues to improve (FID 1.97 at NFE=10, 1.95 with adaptive Dopri5), while Consistency Models degrade (CT goes from 8.71 at NFE=1 to 40.68 at adaptive). On ImageNet 256×256, S-VFM-XL/2 achieves FID 3.31 at NFE=1 and 2.86 at NFE=2, surpassing MeanFlow-XL/2 (3.43 and 2.93 respectively) and iCT-XL/2 (34.24 and 20.30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-VFM (adaptive norm) achieves the best NFE=1 FID on CIFAR-10 (2.81), narrowly beating iCT (2.83) and sCT (2.85), and clearly outperforming MeanFlow (2.92) and all Rectified Flow variants (8.15–378). At NFE=2, IMM (1.98) and sCT (2.06) slightly edge out S-VFM (2.16), but S-VFM is the only method that continues to improve monotonically with more steps, reaching 1.95 at adaptive NFE while CT degrades to 40.68. On ImageNet 256×256, S-VFM-XL/2 wins at both NFE=1 (FID 3.31 vs. MeanFlow 3.43, iCT 34.24, Shortcut 10.60) and NFE=2 (2.86 vs. MeanFlow 2.93, iCT 20.30), demonstrating that the variational latent code plus straightness objective generalizes to large-scale generation. Unlike multi-stage Rectified Flow (which requires 1–3 distillation rounds) and Consistency/MeanFlow models (which need carefully scheduled bootstrapping), S-VFM is fully end-to-end and can be trained indefinitely, with FID improving as training progresses (Figure 7). The key differentiator is that S-VFM resolves the fundamental contradiction between independent coupling and straight-trajectory learning via the latent code, avoiding the discrete approximation errors and training instability that plague Consistency and Mean Velocity Models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Variational Flow Matching (VFM) framework [15] by adding a straightness objective that penalizes the time derivative of the velocity field along trajectories, building on the foundational Flow Matching formulation [28]. It departs conceptually from Rectified Flow's iterative distillation pipeline and from Consistency/Mean Velocity Models' bootstrapping-based self-consistency losses: rather than refining training data across stages or enforcing temporal agreement through discrete approximations, S-VFM introduces a variational latent code that provides a global 'generation overview' to disambiguate intersecting interpolants at each point, combined with a continuous straightness penalty via Jacobian-vector products. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 5) formally establishes that straight trajectories are equivalent to a vanishing time derivative, providing a principled target that prior methods only approximated indirectly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S-VFM offers an end-to-end, single-stage training recipe for few-step generation that avoids the multi-round distillation of Rectified Flow and the carefully scheduled bootstrapping of Consistency/MeanFlow models, which is practically attractive for deployment. The modest but consistent FID improvements over strong baselines (e.g., 2.81 vs. 2.92 on CIFAR-10 one-step; 3.31 vs. 3.43 on ImageNet 256 one-step) suggest the latent-code disambiguation mechanism is a viable alternative to iterative refinement. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or 3D generation, how sensitive performance is to the KL weight β and straightness weight α at larger scales, and whether the variational latent code can be replaced by lighter-weight conditioning without losing the disambiguation benefit.
