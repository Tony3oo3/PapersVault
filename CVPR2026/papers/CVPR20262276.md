## Problem

Existing video frame interpolation methods adopt a frame-centric paradigm, synthesizing each intermediate frame from an independent triplet of adjacent inputs, which ignores temporal dependencies among subsequently generated frames and leads to motion artifacts and temporal inconsistencies. Additionally, vanilla diffusion transformer architectures face prohibitive quadratic attention costs that make high-resolution (e.g., 4K) video processing infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262276/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-frame-rate video at an arbitrary (including non-integer) temporal downsampling ratio (2×–16×) and produces a temporally upsampled high-frame-rate video. Internally, the input is encoded via a spatially tiled VAE (256×256 tiles, stride 192) into fixed-length temporal chunks of 20 latent frames, with a binary mask identifying true observed frame positions. The tiled encoding and sparse attention design allow seamless generalization to arbitrary spatial resolutions (up to 4K) at inference without retraining, using Ulysses Sequence Parallelism across multiple GPUs.

## Method

LDF-VFI is built on a modified Wan2.1 3D DiT backbone trained with diffusion forcing at the temporal-chunk level, where each chunk receives an independent noise level so the model learns to predict velocity conditioned on cleaned prior chunks, enabling auto-regressive inference. To suppress error accumulation (exposure bias) during AR generation, the paper introduces a skip-concatenate sampling strategy: a new chunk is first generated independently of the most recent context (resetting accumulated error), then a subsequent chunk is generated conditioned on both the new skip chunk and prior context to bridge the temporal gap. For resolution scalability, a hybrid sparse attention scheme—spatially local via chunk-based sliding windows but temporally dense across all chunks—replaces full attention, and a ControlNet-inspired conditional VAE decoder injects multi-scale features from the low-frame-rate input throughout the decoding process to improve reconstruction fidelity.

## Results

On the SNU-FILM-entire benchmark, LDF-VFI achieves the best FVD across all interpolation factors: 8.21 (4×), 15.01 (8×), and 26.26 (16×), substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (BiM-VFI: 10.78/21.52/38.26; EDEN: 10.64/26.03/58.83). On the X4K-entire 16× benchmark, LDF-VFI attains FVD 51.41, again the best among all methods. Per-frame quality (LPIPS) is competitive but slightly behind EDEN at 4× (0.032 vs. 0.021) and 8× (0.045 vs. 0.040), while LDF-VFI leads on motion-aware metrics VFIPS and FloLPIPS at 8× and 16×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDF-VFI achieves the best FVD (temporal consistency) across every evaluated setting, with particularly large margins at 16× interpolation (26.26 vs. 38.26 for BiM-VFI and 58.83 for EDEN on SNU-FILM; 51.41 vs. 69.83 for BiM-VFI on X4K). Against the strongest flow-based baseline BiM-VFI, LDF-VFI wins on FVD by 23–56% and on FloLPIPS at 8× (0.063 vs. 0.209) and 16× (0.117 vs. 0.118), while matching its LPIPS at 4× (both 0.032). Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline EDEN, LDF-VFI wins on FVD in all settings (e.g., 15.01 vs. 26.03 at 8×) and on VFIPS/FloLPIPS at 8× and 16×, but trails on LPIPS at 4× (0.032 vs. 0.021) and 8× (0.045 vs. 0.040), indicating EDEN retains a slight per-frame fidelity edge at lower interpolation factors. Against flow-based methods RIFE, AMT, and EMA-VFI, LDF-VFI dominates on all four metrics across all settings by wide margins. On the X4K-16× benchmark, EDEN degrades severely (FVD 1586.99, LPIPS 0.454), while LDF-VFI remains competitive with BiM-VFI on LPIPS (0.071 vs. 0.055) and clearly superior on FVD (51.41 vs. 69.83).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant frame-centric VFI paradigm (RIFE, AMT, EDEN, etc.), which synthesizes each intermediate frame independently from adjacent input pairs, and instead models the joint distribution over the entire high-frame-rate sequence conditioned on the full low-frame-rate input. Architecturally, it builds on the Wan2.1 3D DiT backbone and VAE, adapting them with local spatio-temporal attention and a ControlNet-style conditional decoder. The auto-regressive training strategy is inherited from Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024), but the paper introduces a VFI-specific skip-concatenate sampling scheme to address exposure bias, which is distinct from prior mitigations like scheduled sampling or reverse-KL objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing VFI as a holistic video-sequence modeling problem, this work addresses a persistent source of temporal artifacts that frame-pair methods cannot resolve, and the new video-centric benchmarks (SNU-FILM-entire, X4K-entire) shift evaluation toward long-range coherence rather than isolated triplet quality. The skip-concatenate sampling strategy is a lightweight, general-purpose fix for error accumulation in auto-regressive video generation that could transfer to other strongly-conditioned AR tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 16× interpolation, how it behaves on very long videos (minutes rather than seconds), and whether the multi-GPU requirement for 4K inference limits real-world deployment.
