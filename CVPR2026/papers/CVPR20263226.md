## Problem

Existing proactive defenses against identity-preserving personalized generation (e.g., Anti-DreamBooth, SimAC, AdvDM) are designed around training-based fine-tuning pipelines and degrade sharply when applied to training-free methods such as IP-Adapter, PhotoMaker, and InfiniteID. This failure stems from two structural mismatches: training-based approaches internalize identity through model weights while training-free methods inject identity via external encoders at inference, and the latter exhibit heterogeneous encoder architectures and fusion strategies with no unified defense target.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single facial reference image (224×224 on VGGFace2, 512×512 on CelebA-HQ) and produces a pixel-level perturbed version constrained to an L∞ budget of ε=8/255, optimized via 200 PGD steps with step size α=0.005. The perturbed image is visually indistinguishable (PSNR ≈ 32.2 dB, SSIM ≈ 0.84) but, when fed as a reference into any downstream personalization pipeline—whether training-based (DreamBooth, InfiniteYou) or training-free (IP-Adapter, PhotoMaker, InfiniteID, Blip-Diffusion)—the generated output no longer preserves the original identity. A fixed text prompt ('A photo of a person') conditions the surrogate diffusion model during optimization.

## Method

IDGuardian abstracts identity-preserving personalization into two stages—identity extraction and identity injection—and attacks both simultaneously. For extraction, Cross-Encoder Identity Field Confounding minimizes the cosine similarity between clean and perturbed image embeddings in both FaceNet (low-level facial identity) and CLIP (high-level semantic) spaces, forcing the protected image to appear identity-dissimilar to any encoder. For injection, Guidance-Flow Identity Deflection interprets identity conditioning as a score-function shift (a 'conceptual bridge' from unconditional to identity-conditioned distribution) and constructs an adversarial bridge S* = −(ε_θ(x_t,t,y_adv) − ε_θ(x_t,t,y_clean))/√(1−ᾱ_t) that redirects the denoising trajectory away from the clean identity manifold. The two gradient signals are L2-normalized and combined, then applied via projected PGD updates on the pixel-domain perturbation. Trained on a single white-box surrogate (IP-Adapter SDXL Plus Face), the resulting perturbation transfers to unseen black-box pipelines, FLUX-based generators, and commercial APIs without re-optimization.

## Results

IDGuardian achieves the lowest Identity Similarity Metric (ISM, lower = better) across all seven personalization pipelines, with particularly dramatic reductions on IP-Adapter (0.036 vs. 0.291 for the best baseline IDProtector) and IP-Adapter SDXL Plus Face (0.126 vs. 0.425). On training-based pipelines, IDGuardian also leads: DreamBooth ISM drops to 0.336 (vs. 0.402 for IDProtector) and InfiniteYou to 0.301 (vs. 0.541). In terms of perceptual quality, IDGuardian attains PSNR of 32.19 dB and SSIM of 0.842, comparable to or better than all baselines, while requiring only 47 s of optimization time per image (vs. 4089 s for IDProtector's PGD variant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDGuardian outperforms all five baselines on ISM across every one of the seven personalization pipelines. The margin is largest on training-free pipelines that use cross-attention fusion: on IP-Adapter, IDGuardian reduces ISM to 0.036 versus 0.286–0.307 for the other defenses (a 87–88% relative reduction over the best baseline), and on IP-Adapter SDXL Plus Face it reaches 0.126 versus 0.398–0.548. On training-based pipelines the gap narrows but remains consistent: DreamBooth ISM is 0.336 (vs. 0.337 for AdvDM, the closest competitor) and InfiniteYou ISM is 0.301 (vs. 0.541 for IDProtector). IDProtector [39], the closest concurrent work, is competitive on training-free pipelines but degrades on training-based ones (InfiniteYou ISM 0.541 vs. IDGuardian's 0.301), confirming the paper's claim that encoder-specific alignment does not transfer to fine-tuning-based personalization. On FaceQNet (FQ) scores, IDGuardian is competitive but not uniformly best—AdvDM achieves a lower FQ on DreamBooth (0.192 vs. 0.220)—indicating a slight trade-off between identity suppression strength and face-quality utility in that specific setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of proactive adversarial defenses against personalized generation (Anti-DreamBooth, SimAC, AdvDM, ACE), which were all designed around training-based fine-tuning pipelines, to the training-free regime where identity is injected via external encoders without parameter modification. It also departs from the concurrent IDProtector, which handles training-free pipelines but relies on multiple encoder-specific features and does not generalize to training-based methods. The score-based adversarial conceptual bridge draws on classifier-free guidance and distributional transport ideas (Dual Diffusion Implicit Bridges), repurposing the noise-prediction difference as an explicit adversarial steering signal rather than a generation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As training-free personalization (IP-Adapter, PhotoMaker, InfiniteID) becomes the dominant deployment paradigm due to its zero-shot efficiency, the absence of effective defenses represents a growing privacy gap that this paper directly addresses. The cross-paradigm generalization from a single white-box surrogate to unseen black-box pipelines (including commercial APIs) is practically significant for identity holders who cannot know which generator an attacker will use. Open questions include whether the defense holds against video-based identity preservation, whether the perturbation budget (ε=8/255) remains sufficient as generator fidelity improves, and whether the score-based bridge component transfers to non-diffusion architectures (e.g., autoregressive or flow-matching models) without re-derivation.
