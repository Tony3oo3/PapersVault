## Problem

Existing multimodal diffusion models rely on fixed, hand-crafted fusion strategies—cross-attention, self-attention, or rigid layer-by-layer MoT connections—that impose static conditional signals and uniform token treatment. The paper identifies that these designs create an information mismatch with the dynamic, iterative nature of the denoising process, and that diffusion models do not consume language features in a strictly sequential or layer-aligned manner, making flexible, adaptive selection essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261830/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces images up to 2048×2048 resolution via a 16× spatial-compression VAE (Wan 2.2). For instruction-based editing, it additionally takes a reference image alongside the text instruction and outputs the edited image. The understanding tower processes the context in a single frozen forward pass, while the generation tower iteratively denoises over 50 steps with classifier-free guidance, with the router re-evaluating routing decisions at every denoising step.

## Method

MoS introduces a dual-tower architecture where a frozen understanding tower U (PLM-8B or InternVL-14B) and a learnable generation tower G (3B or 5B) are bridged by a lightweight (~100M parameter) token-wise router R. The router takes the denoising timestep t, the noised image latent z_t, and the context embedding c as inputs, and for each context token predicts a distinct m×n logit matrix (m = understanding depth, n = generation depth) that scores which understanding-layer hidden states to route to each generation block. A sparse top-k selection with ε-greedy exploration during training picks the highest-affinity hidden states, which are reweighted and aggregated before being concatenated with the visual tokens as in-context tokens within each generation block. The entire system is trained end-to-end with rectified flow matching, with only G and R learned from scratch while U remains frozen, enabling asymmetric tower depths and reducing training cost to ~3,000 A100-days.

## Results

MoS-L (5B learnable parameters) achieves state-of-the-art results across text-to-image and image editing benchmarks: GenEval 0.90, DPG 87.01, WISE 0.54, OneIG 0.52, GEdit 7.86, and ImgEdit 4.33. The smaller MoS-S (3B) also leads on GenEval (0.89) and DPG (86.33) among models of comparable size. Notably, MoS-L matches or surpasses Qwen-Image (20B learnable parameters, 4× larger) on GenEval, GEdit, and ImgEdit while using substantially fewer parameters and only ~3,000 A100-days of training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen-Image (20B, self-attention), MoS-L (5B) wins on GenEval (0.90 vs. 0.87), GEdit (7.86 vs. 7.56), and ImgEdit (4.33 vs. 4.27), but trails on DPG (87.01 vs. 88.32) and WISE (0.54 vs. 0.62), making it a near-parity result at 4× fewer learnable parameters. Against Bagel (14B, MoT), MoS-L clearly dominates on image editing (GEdit 7.86 vs. 6.52; ImgEdit 4.33 vs. 3.20) and edges ahead on GenEval (0.90 vs. 0.88). Against FLUX.1[Dev] (12B, self-attention), MoS-L wins decisively on every reported metric (GenEval 0.90 vs. 0.66; DPG 87.01 vs. 83.84). Against SANA-1.5 (4.8B, cross-attention), MoS-L leads on all shared benchmarks (GenEval 0.90 vs. 0.81; DPG 87.01 vs. 84.70; OneIG 0.52 vs. 0.33). In the controlled MoT ablation (Fig. 6), MoS consistently outperforms MoT across all training steps on both GenEval and DPG, confirming that adaptive token-wise routing surpasses rigid layer-by-layer correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoS departs from the three dominant multimodal fusion strategies—cross-attention (single-layer text conditioning), self-attention (concatenated token sequences), and Mixture-of-Transformers (rigid layer-by-layer KV sharing)—by introducing a learnable, token-wise router that sparsely aggregates hidden states from all layers of a frozen understanding tower into each block of a generation tower. Conceptually, it extends the sparse adaptive-computation lineage of Mixture-of-Experts, Mixture-of-Depths, and Mixture-of-Recursions from intra-model routing to inter-model, cross-modal routing, while remaining compatible with asymmetric tower depths that MoT cannot handle. The training objective follows rectified flow matching, and the dual-tower design (frozen understanding + trainable generation) is shared with recent unified multimodal models like Bagel and Qwen-Image, but MoS replaces their fixed interaction patterns with dynamic, timestep- and content-dependent routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× parameter efficiency (5B matching 20B Qwen-Image) with a router adding only ~0.008 s per iteration makes MoS a practically attractive blueprint for scaling multimodal diffusion without proportional compute increases. The ablations convincingly show that fixed layer selection, static conditioning, and sample-wise routing are all suboptimal, providing a clear design recipe for future multimodal architectures. Open questions include whether the routing patterns generalize to video generation or longer-horizon tasks, whether the frozen-understanding-tower assumption limits ultimate quality, and whether the ε-greedy top-k strategy remains effective at much larger scales or with more than two modalities.
