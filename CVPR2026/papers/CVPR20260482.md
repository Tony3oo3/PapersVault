## Problem

Existing hard prompt inversion methods either produce incoherent, near-nonsense token sequences or yield prompts that are overly sensitive to small token-level edits, causing large, unpredictable changes in the generated image. Prior work has focused almost exclusively on reconstruction fidelity under CLIP or captioning metrics, largely ignoring whether the inverted prompt remains stable and controllable under the token swap and append operations that users actually perform in interactive prompt-engineering workflows.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image as input and produces a discrete text prompt of configurable length (16, 32, 64, or ~77 tokens) that is simultaneously aligned with the reference image, human-readable, and robust to downstream token-level edits. The approach is plug-and-play: it requires no finetuning of either the text-to-image diffusion model or the discrete diffusion language model, and the resulting prompts are transferable across multiple T2I backends. The inverted prompt serves as a reusable text handle for downstream generation and localized editing rather than a one-shot description tied to the source image.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the autoregressive LLM prior used in prior gradient-free inversion with a discrete diffusion language model (dLLM) that refines full-length token sequences in T ≪ N parallel denoising steps, enabling global coherence and substantially faster decoding. Two Feynman-Kac steering potentials are applied during the reverse diffusion chain: a CLIP-alignment potential that reweights trajectories toward higher image-text similarity, and a novel token-to-token similarity potential that penalizes off-diagonal entries in the Gram matrix of mean-centered per-position token distributions, thereby encouraging disentangled, modular tokens. The edit reward is a bounded stepwise function of the mean and standard deviation of off-diagonal similarities, incorporated as a telescoping difference potential so that the terminal distribution is tilted toward low-coupling, edit-friendly prompts. The combined steered distribution simultaneously enforces linguistic fluency (via the dLLM prior), image alignment (via CLIP), and edit-friendliness (via token-similarity reduction), achieving ~10× speedup over VGD and ~95× over PEZ while improving interpretability, alignment, and TIFA/GPT-4V editability scores.

## Results

On MS COCO (32 tokens), the method achieves F1 = 0.89, PPL = 37.16, CLIP-Image similarity = 0.71, and CLIP-Text similarity = 0.59, outperforming all baselines on alignment metrics while matching or exceeding them on prompt accuracy. At ~77 tokens, CLIP-Image similarity rises to 0.74 with PPL dropping to 17.10, demonstrating consistent gains with longer prompts. Inversion time is 10.50 s for 77 tokens, yielding a ~10× speedup over VGD (104.20 s) and ~95× over PEZ (194.40 s) on a single A6000 GPU. Gains in CLIP-Image similarity are consistent across all three datasets (MS COCO, Flickr8K, JourneyDB) and all token budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VGD (the strongest interpretable hard-inversion baseline), the method achieves higher CLIP-Image similarity (0.71 vs. 0.68 on MS COCO 32 tokens; 0.74 vs. 0.68 at ~77 tokens) and lower PPL (37.16 vs. 37.78), while matching F1 (0.89); inversion time drops from 40.20 s to 5.60 s (~7× faster at 32 tokens, ~10× at 77 tokens). Against PEZ, the method dramatically outperforms on interpretability (PPL 37.16 vs. 4946.56) and text alignment (CL-T 0.59 vs. 0.21), with comparable CLIP-Image similarity (0.71 vs. 0.69) and ~95× faster inversion. Against BLIP-2, the method wins on CLIP-Image similarity (0.71 vs. 0.48) and CLIP-Text similarity (0.59 vs. 0.52) while matching F1 (0.89) and achieving slightly lower PPL. Against CLIP Interrogator 2.1, the method leads on all metrics: F1 (0.89 vs. 0.86), PPL (37.16 vs. 142.02), CL-I (0.71 vs. 0.52), and CL-T (0.59 vs. 0.55). Against Florence-2 captioning, the method improves F1 (0.89 vs. 0.84) and CLIP-Image similarity (0.71 vs. 0.48) at comparable PPL, confirming that inversion-based prompts align more faithfully with the reference image than direct captioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-free hard prompt inversion framework of VGD (Kim et al., ICLR 2025) by replacing its autoregressive LLM decoder with a discrete diffusion language model (dLLM) backbone and augmenting the objective with an explicit token-token similarity reduction term for edit-friendliness. The CLIP-guided steering mechanism is adapted from the Feynman-Kac (FK) potential framework of Singhal et al. (2025), which provides a general telescoping-potential formulation for reweighting diffusion reverse steps. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior inversion work (PEZ, VGD, EDITOR) that targets only reconstruction fidelity by introducing downstream token-edit robustness as a first-class optimization objective, and from prior dLLM work by applying FK steering with both CLIP alignment and a novel editability reward derived from the dLLM's own predictive distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The edit-friendly framing addresses a practical gap: users who obtain an inverted prompt need to modify it (swap a subject, append a style) without the generated image collapsing, yet prior methods produce brittle prompts where small edits cause large, unpredictable changes. The ~10× speedup over VGD and 95× over PEZ makes interactive prompt inversion feasible, and the token-token similarity metric offers a lightweight, model-agnostic proxy for editability that avoids expensive cross-attention analysis. Open questions include whether the token-similarity signal generalizes beyond swap/append to more complex edits (reordering, deletion), whether the dLLM backbone choice significantly affects edit-friendliness, and whether the approach scales to much longer prompts or multi-image personalization settings.
