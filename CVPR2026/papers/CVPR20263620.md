## Problem

Existing isotropic microscopy reconstruction methods suffer from two coupled limitations: synthetic training pairs rely on Gaussian blur that mismatches the true anisotropic, spatially varying, and aberration-laden PSF of real microscopes, and slice-wise 2D processing discards cross-plane volumetric geometry, producing distorted morphologies. No prior method simultaneously addresses both the degradation-model mismatch and the absence of an explicit 3D structural prior in a unified reconstruction pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263620/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an anisotropic 3D fluorescence volume (e.g., 1024×1024×150 for Mouse Kidney) with a ~5:1 lateral-to-axial sampling disparity, processing 512×512 patches. Training pairs are synthesized by convolving high-resolution lateral slices with a bank of seven randomly sampled, instrument-matched PSFs predicted by SFE-Net from the target microscope's axial images. The output is an isotropic 3D volume reconstructed slice-by-slice with joint XY, YZ, and XZ context, using only two-step Euler sampling at inference.

## Method

MicroFM is a two-stage physics-guided framework. In stage one, SFE-Net is trained on Zernike-wavefront-synthesized degradations (25 modes covering piston through higher-order aberrations, with Poisson and Gaussian noise) to regress spatially varying physical PSFs from axial images; these PSFs then degrade lateral slices to build realistic training pairs matched to the target instrument. In stage two, an MLP-based implicit neural representation is pretrained to render an isotropic volume under the physical degradation operator, yielding a Gaussian-weighted slice-wise geometry prior (n=6 neighbors). A Consistency Flow Matching network with an NCSN++ backbone (2.48M parameters) is then trained to transport a convex blend of the degraded input and the INR prior (x₀ = αx₂ + βM̂, β=0.5) to the high-quality target, using endpoint-consistency losses with piecewise linear paths that enable stable two-step Euler sampling while suppressing hallucinations.

## Results

Across four fluorescence microscopy datasets, MicroFM achieves the best full-reference fidelity on all four: PSNR of 40.19 (Dense Neuron), 47.36 (Thy1-GFP), 30.56 (Kidney), and 33.01 (Liver), with SSIM ≥ 0.854 and VIF up to 0.570. On no-reference axial metrics, MicroFM attains the lowest NIQE (7.57–17.47) and PIQE (56.45–81.39) across all datasets, and the highest NRQM (3.09–5.10) on three of four. The largest absolute PSNR gain over the strongest baseline (SSAI-3D) is +16.57 dB on Thy1-GFP and +9.83 dB on Dense Neuron, while on Kidney and Liver the margin is +9.83 and +11.40 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MicroFM dominates all six baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and VIF across every dataset, with the largest PSNR margins over SSAI-3D (the strongest self-supervised baseline) reaching +16.6 dB on Thy1-GFP and +9.8 dB on Dense Neuron. On LPIPS, MicroFM is best on three of four datasets but UTOM achieves a lower score on Mouse Kidney (0.320 vs. 0.483), reflecting UTOM's content-preserving cycle-consistency objective. On no-reference axial metrics, MicroFM attains the best NIQE and PIQE on all four datasets and the best NRQM on three; on Mouse Liver, UTOM edges out MicroFM on NRQM (3.34 vs. 3.09) and Neuroclear is marginally better on NIQE (7.90 vs. 8.11). The unsupervised GAN/diffusion baselines (CycleGAN, CycleDiffusion, UTOM) degrade most on the strongly anisotropic Kidney and Liver tissues, where their lateral–axial distribution-similarity assumption is violated, while the self-supervised baselines (Self-Net, SSAI-3D) rely on Gaussian PSF degradation and 2D slice processing, limiting their axial fidelity. Overall, MicroFM's physics-matched PSF synthesis and volumetric prior yield consistent, large gains in both full-reference and no-reference quality without the hallucination artifacts typical of unpaired GAN methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MicroFM builds on SFE-Net for blind PSF estimation and Consistency Flow Matching for the generative training procedure, but departs from the dominant self-supervised isotropic reconstruction line (Self-Net, SSAI-3D, CARE) in two fundamental ways: it replaces the ubiquitous Gaussian-kernel degradation model with Zernike-based, instrument-matched physical PSFs, and it introduces a continuous implicit neural representation as a volumetric geometry prior that prior slice-wise or quasi-3D methods lack. The paper is explicitly positioned as the first application of Flow Matching to isotropic microscopy reconstruction, contrasting with the GAN-based (CycleGAN, UTOM, Neuroclear) and diffusion-based (CycleDiffusion, Volume Tells) alternatives that dominate the unpaired/self-supervised literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By coupling physically accurate PSF synthesis with a volumetric prior and observation-anchored flow sampling, MicroFM addresses the two bottlenecks that have limited prior self-supervised methods—degradation mismatch and absent cross-slice geometry—yielding large, consistent gains across four distinct microscopy systems. The practical implication is that isotropic reconstruction no longer requires expensive paired acquisitions or hardware modifications, making high-fidelity 3D fluorescence imaging accessible on standard microscopes. Open questions include whether the PSF estimation and reconstruction can be jointly learned end-to-end, whether online calibration is feasible for live imaging, and whether the approach extends to multichannel or time-lapse settings where the PSF may drift during acquisition.
