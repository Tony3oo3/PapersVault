## Problem

State-of-the-art vision foundation models (DINOv2, SigLIP 2, Web-SSL) rely on proprietary training data and withhold critical training code, creating a significant barrier to reproducibility and scientific progress. Beyond the openness gap, existing self-supervised clustering methods (e.g., Sinkhorn-Knopp in DINOv2) suffer from inherent ambiguity in cluster semantics—requiring very large codebooks to disambiguate—and produce dense patch representations that are biased by absolute spatial position rather than semantic content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262790/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Franca takes standard RGB images (224×224 for pretraining, 364×364 and 518×518 for high-resolution fine-tuning) and splits them into non-overlapping 14×14 patches processed by a ViT backbone. The output is a set of patch embeddings and a [CLS] token, which are then sliced along the feature dimension into nested Matryoshka subspaces (d, d/8, d/16), each producing its own cluster assignments via a dedicated projection head. After RASA post-training, the patch embeddings are further projected to remove linearly predictable positional components, yielding spatially invariant dense features with zero additional inference cost.

## Method

Franca builds on the DINOv2 student-teacher ViT architecture with three key innovations. First, a Matryoshka multi-head clustering projector slices the encoder output into progressively smaller dimension subsets, each with an independent projection head and Sinkhorn-Knopp clustering objective, inducing a coarse-to-fine semantic hierarchy without increasing backbone parameters. Second, CyclicMask circularly shifts masked patches across spatial axes during masked image modeling to prevent the model from exploiting fixed spatial continuity. Third, RASA (Removal of Absolute Spatial Attributes) is a lightweight post-pretraining step that learns a linear position-prediction head, orthonormalizes its weight vectors via Gram-Schmidt to span a 2D positional subspace, then iteratively subtracts each patch embedding's projection onto this subspace until the position loss saturates (~9 iterations); the entire multi-step transformation collapses to a single matrix appended to the final ViT layer, adding zero architectural change or inference overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K linear probing, Franca-G (ViT-G/14, LAION-600M) achieves 86.0% top-1 accuracy, matching DINOv2-G (86.0%) while using 7× fewer parameters than Web-SSL-7B (84.7%). On in-context segmentation (Pascal VOC), Franca-L reaches 79.5% mIoU, surpassing DINOv2-L (distilled, 74.6%) by +4.9% and Web-SSL-L (71.3%) by +8.2%. On unsupervised object discovery (TokenCut VOC12), Franca-B achieves 59.1% CorLoc versus DINOv2-B's 48.3%. On video object segmentation (DAVIS), Franca-B scores 70.6% (J+F)m, outperforming DINOv2-B (68.5%) by +2.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINOv2 (the primary baseline), Franca consistently wins on dense prediction tasks: +4.9% mIoU on in-context VOC segmentation (ViT-L), +2.1% on DAVIS VOS (ViT-B), and +10.8% CorLoc on TokenCut VOC12 (ViT-B), while matching DINOv2-G on ImageNet-1K linear probing (86.0%) without distillation. In the controlled IN-21K comparison (Table 2), Franca outperforms the reproduced DINOv2 by +0.5 to +2.6 points across KNN, linear probing, in-context segmentation, and VOS, confirming gains come from architecture rather than data scale. Against Web-SSL (trained on 2B images), Franca-G matches its linear accuracy (86.0% vs 84.7%) with 7× fewer parameters and surpasses it on in-context segmentation (76.7% vs 73.3% on VOC). Against SigLIP 2, Franca dominates on dense tasks (79.5% vs 46.4% in-context VOC for ViT-L) but SigLIP 2 retains an edge on robustness (IN-R: 95.7% vs 64.3% for ViT-L), reflecting its text-supervised training. On OOD detection (Figure 9), Franca outperforms DINOv2 across all five benchmarks at both ViT-L and ViT-G scales. Against RADIOv2.5, Franca wins on both DAVIS VOS (70.6% vs 66.7%) and TokenCut VOC12 (59.1% vs 48.1%) at ViT-B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

Franca is built directly on the DINOv2 self-supervised framework (student-teacher ViT with multi-crop, Sinkhorn-Knopp clustering, and EMA updates) and extends it in three ways: (1) replacing the single large codebook with a Matryoshka multi-head clustering projector that assigns progressively finer-grained prototypes to nested feature subspaces, (2) introducing CyclicMask to break spatial continuity in masked image modeling, and (3) adding RASA, a lightweight post-pretraining step that removes linearly predictable positional information from patch embeddings. The Matryoshka idea itself comes from Kusupati et al. [49], but here it is repurposed from a single shared projection head into independent per-slice clustering objectives, and the iBOT-style patch-level supervision [91] is retained. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the proprietary-data paradigm of DINOv2 (LVD-142M) and SigLIP 2 (WebLI), arguing that minimally-curated open data (LAION-600M) suffices when the training objective is well-designed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary impact is practical: Franca is the first fully open-source (data, code, intermediate weights) VFM that matches or exceeds DINOv2 and SigLIP 2 across classification, dense segmentation, VOS, OOD detection, and 3D probing, lowering the barrier to reproducible SSL research. The RASA finding—that DINOv2's patch clusters are measurably position-driven (low spatial entropy) and that a few iterations of linear projection removal fix this with zero inference overhead—addresses a subtle but persistent issue that the authors note also affects the concurrently released DINOv3. Open questions include whether RASA generalizes to higher-dimensional or non-linear positional entanglement, whether the Matryoshka hierarchy can be deepened beyond d/16 without degradation, and whether the approach scales to even larger backbones or video pretraining without the distillation that DINOv2 relies on for its smaller variants.
