## Problem

Masked visual modeling for video-language pretraining suffers from two fundamental limitations: severe visual information loss under high masking ratios (unaffordable to compensate with long training schedules as in MAE) and temporal information leakage where masked tokens are trivially reconstructed from adjacent frames. Existing remedies—semantic masking (UMT) that discards background tokens, or low masking ratios (STM) that sacrifice efficiency—fail to simultaneously preserve holistic scene content and enforce temporal coherence among retained tokens.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip of T frames (processed by a ViT-B/16 patch encoder without class token) paired with a text sentence (encoded by BERT-base). A frozen SigLIP-base teacher generates per-frame spatiotemporal masks (75% masking for image tokens, 90% for video tokens) and fine-grained video-text relevance matrices via sliding-window pooling of neighboring tokens multiplied with the pooled text feature. The student outputs reconstructed video-text relevance matrices for masked tokens, which serve as the MRM reconstruction target, alongside aligned video-text embeddings for contrastive and matching objectives.

## Method

ClusterSTM performs a two-stage masking: first, intra-frame clustering via Density Peaks Clustering (DPC) partitions each frame's N visual tokens into N_c = N×(1−r) semantically independent clusters, ensuring retained tokens cover both foreground and background content; second, within each cluster, the token with the highest temporal density—computed as the sum of exp(−d/d_c) over semantic distances to all tokens in adjacent frames—is retained while others are discarded, guaranteeing strong temporal correlation among visible tokens and preventing leakage. Beyond masking, the paper introduces a video-text relevance reconstruction objective (MRM) where a SigLIP teacher produces relevance matrices by aggregating local token neighborhoods through the model's attentive pooling module and multiplying with the text feature, providing a higher-level multimodal reconstruction target than pixel or feature reconstruction. The full training loss combines MRM with video-text contrastive (VTC), video-text matching (VTM), and masked language modeling (MLM) losses, trained for 10 epochs on WebVid-2M + CC3M (≈5M pairs) with AdamW at lr 1e-4.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-video retrieval with 5M pretraining pairs and 4 frames, ClusterSTM achieves Recall@1 of 31.2 on MSRVTT, 36.5 on DiDeMo, 31.4 on ActivityNet, and 40.3 on MSVD, surpassing the prior best (STM) by 1.4%, 2.1%, 0.8%, and 1.6% respectively. On fine-tuned retrieval, ClusterSTM attains R1 scores of 49.7 (MSRVTT), 58.5 (DiDeMo), and 54.9 (ActivityNet), setting new state-of-the-art among models pretrained on comparable data. For video QA, it reaches 73.6 on TGIF-Frame, 46.7 on MSRVTT-QA, and 51.3 on MSVD-QA; for captioning, it achieves CIDEr of 64.4 (MSRVTT) and 145.6 (MSVD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ClusterSTM consistently outperforms the two closest masked-modeling baselines—STM [52] and UMT [27]—across all three task families. On fine-tuned retrieval, it beats STM by +1.2 R1 on MSRVTT (49.7 vs 48.5), +1.6 on DiDeMo (58.5 vs 56.9), and +1.3 on ActivityNet (54.9 vs 53.6); on video QA it leads by +0.6 on TGIF-Frame (73.6 vs 73.0), +0.8 on MSRVTT-QA (46.7 vs 45.9), and +0.6 on ANet-QA (45.5 vs 44.9); on captioning it edges ahead by +0.5 CIDEr on MSRVTT (64.4 vs 63.9) and +0.2 on MSVD (145.6 vs 145.4). Against earlier masked-modeling methods (VIOLETv2, LAVENDER, HiTeA, Singularity), the gains are substantially larger: e.g., +12.5 R1 over VIOLETv2 on MSRVTT and +10.6 over HiTeA on DiDeMo. Notably, despite using only 5M pretraining pairs, ClusterSTM surpasses models trained on 400M pairs (CLIP4Clip: 42.1 R1 on MSRVTT) and approaches UMT-L (25M pairs, 58.8 R1), demonstrating strong data efficiency. The zero-shot setting further confirms the advantage: ClusterSTM leads all 5M-pair baselines on every retrieval benchmark, with the largest margin over STM on MSVD (+1.6) and DiDeMo (+2.1 when frame counts are matched).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked visual modeling lineage for video-language pretraining, building directly on UMT's unmasked-teacher architecture and its semantic (foreground-only) masking strategy. It departs from UMT by replacing semantic masking with cluster-wise spatio-temporal masking that preserves both foreground and background content, and from STM by maintaining a high masking ratio (90%) rather than lowering it to 30%. The temporal-density-based token selection mechanism draws on the DPC clustering framework and the tube-masking intuition of preserving temporally correlated tokens, but generalizes beyond the fixed-spatial-location assumption of tube masking to handle complex motion. The video-text relevance reconstruction target extends the masked reconstruction paradigm from purely visual signals (pixels, HOG, deep features) to a multimodal semantic level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a well-designed masking strategy can close much of the gap between models pretrained on 5M pairs and those on 138M–646M pairs, offering a practical path to efficient video-language pretraining without massive compute budgets. The video-text relevance reconstruction target is a conceptually useful idea that could transfer to other masked multimodal pretraining settings. Open questions include whether the DPC-based clustering scales efficiently to very long videos or high-resolution inputs, whether the approach generalizes beyond the ViT-B/16 + BERT-base architecture, and whether the 1–2% gains over STM persist at larger model scales or with more diverse pretraining data.
