## Problem

Training-free controllable T2I diffusion methods that simultaneously control structure and appearance suffer from a 're-imagining' problem: the model re-synthesizes the appearance subject to fit the target structure, losing fine-grained identity details such as distinctive facial features, clothing patterns, or species-specific markings. Training-based alternatives (ControlNet+IP-Adapter, Uni-ControlNet) avoid this but require heavy per-method training and still exhibit structure-identity conflicts. The paper tackles the gap of achieving faithful subject-level identity preservation alongside structure and text control in a fully training-free setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes four inputs: a structure image (in diverse formats such as Canny edges, pose skeletons, depth maps, or mesh renders), an appearance reference image (RGB), a generation text prompt, and an appearance caption describing the reference subject. The appearance image undergoes a single noise-free U-Net forward pass to cache self-attention K/V tensors before the denoising loop begins. The output is a 512×512 RGB image that simultaneously respects the target structure, preserves the reference subject's identity, and follows the generation prompt, with inference taking approximately 35 seconds on a single GPU.

## Method

SeAl is a training-free framework built on SDXL that replaces re-synthesis with direct feature infusion through three synergistic modules. AnchorAlign uses SAM to extract subject bounding boxes from both inputs, then applies a uniform scale-and-translate to geometrically align the structure to the appearance anchor before diffusion begins. Reference-guided Appearance Infusion caches Key and Value tensors from mid/up-block self-attention layers of the appearance image, then during generation selectively blends these cached features into the current latent's attention output using a cosine-similarity validity mask (threshold τ) and a scheduled blending strength (δ), with top-k=1 matching to preserve sharpness. Delta-Bridge computes the guidance vector difference between the generation prompt and the appearance caption, applies a 2D average-pooling low-pass filter to isolate macroscopic semantic changes from high-frequency identity details, and adds the filtered delta to the final noise prediction with a progress-scheduled weight that fades to zero in late timesteps, allowing the appearance path to render fine details unimpeded.

## Results

SeAl achieves the highest DINO-I identity score across all six dataset-structure combinations, with particularly strong gains on challenging pose conditions (0.7104 on Animal-Pose, 0.7465 on Human-Pose, 0.7887 on Human-Canny). It also leads in CLIP text-alignment in most categories (e.g., 0.3372 on Animal-Mesh, 0.3281 on Animal-Pose) and DreamSim-str structure alignment in several (e.g., 0.8450 on Animal-Pose, 0.7739 on Human-Canny). On human-preference metrics (HPSv2 and ImageReward), SeAl consistently scores highest among all methods. The only notable losses are on DreamSim-str for Animal-Depth (0.6814 vs. DRF's 0.7382) and Human-Mesh (0.6300 vs. T2I-Adapter+IP-Adapter's 0.7004).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeAl decisively outperforms all five baselines on the primary identity metric (DINO-I) across every dataset-structure combination, with margins ranging from ~0.05 (vs. Ctrl-X on Animal-Pose: 0.7104 vs. 0.6318) to ~0.24 (vs. Uni-ControlNet on Animal-Mesh: 0.6990 vs. 0.4802). Among training-free methods, SeAl surpasses Ctrl-X by 0.087–0.168 DINO-I and DRF by 0.103–0.123 DINO-I, confirming that direct K/V infusion preserves fine-grained identity far better than spatial normalization or score-guidance approaches. On text alignment (CLIP), SeAl leads in 4 of 6 categories but trails DRF slightly on Animal-Depth (0.3260 vs. 0.3398) and Uni-ControlNet on Animal-Depth (0.3489). On structure alignment (DreamSim-str), SeAl wins on Animal-Pose (0.8450) and Human-Canny (0.7739) but loses to T2I-Adapter+IP-Adapter on Human-Mesh (0.6300 vs. 0.7004) and DRF on Animal-Depth (0.6814 vs. 0.7382), suggesting the AnchorAlign step trades some geometric fidelity for identity robustness. Overall, the paper's central claim—that training-free methods can match or exceed training-based identity fidelity without per-subject fine-tuning—is strongly supported by the DINO-I dominance, while the structure-alignment gaps indicate a residual trade-off with geometric precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeAl carves new ground within the training-free controllable T2I diffusion paradigm by reframing identity preservation as 'infusion' rather than 're-imagining.' It repurposes standard self-attention K/V tensors (a mechanism well-known in transformer literature) as a cache for appearance identity, gated by semantic similarity matching, and combines this with SAM-based geometric pre-alignment and a CFG-guidance delta to resolve text-appearance conflicts. While prior training-free methods (Ctrl-X, DRF, FreeControl) attempt simultaneous structure and appearance control, they treat appearance as global style transfer; SeAl explicitly targets subject-level identity by selectively injecting fine-grained features into mid/up-block self-attention layers, a granularity not previously exploited in training-free pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeAl offers a practical, training-free alternative to per-subject fine-tuning (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) and heavy adapter training (ControlNet+IP-Adapter), making identity-preserving generation accessible without dedicated training resources. The demonstrated superiority on challenging subjects (distinctive animal features, complex human attire) addresses a real failure mode that limits current deployment. However, the ~35s inference cost (vs. ~13s for Ctrl-X) and reliance on SDXL's U-Net architecture raise open questions about scalability to DiT-based models, multi-subject scenes, and video generation. The semantic matching threshold and blending strength, while ablated, remain hand-tuned hyperparameters that may not transfer across domains without re-tuning.
