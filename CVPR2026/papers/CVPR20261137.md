## Problem

Existing text-driven motion generation methods handle only single-action sequences and simple instructions, struggling with multi-action compositions and precise body-part editing due to limited data diversity, inadequate motion representations, and fragmented pipelines. Most critically, prior approaches train motion models from scratch, forgoing the rich action semantics and long-horizon reasoning already encoded in pretrained Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which prevents generalization to complex language-motion dependencies.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'shoot a basketball then do a cartwheel') and autoregressively decodes discrete motion tokens; for editing, it additionally receives the original motion as context tokens alongside a text instruction specifying the desired modification. The output is a sequence of FSQ-quantized motion tokens that are decoded back into 85-dimensional localized features (yaw angular speed plus relative joint positions per frame) and then converted to SMPL-X parameters via a coarse-to-fine IK solver. Motion and language tokens share the same embedding space, with motion sequences demarcated by <SOM> and <EOM> special tokens within the MLLM's vocabulary.

## Method

MotionMaster finetunes Qwen2.5-VL by substituting the least-used text tokens in its codebook with FSQ-quantized motion tokens, enabling direct cross-modal fusion in a single autoregressive transformer. The FSQ tokenizer encodes 85-dim localized frame-to-frame features (yaw change + relative joint positions) through a 1D convolutional encoder, quantizes each latent dimension independently for full codebook utilization, and supervises reconstruction with global position and velocity losses to prevent trajectory drift. Training on the 10,000-hour MotionGB dataset employs semantic balancing—sampling motions in proportionally to the inverse of their local semantic density estimated via T5 embeddings—to prevent overfitting to common patterns like walking. RoPE position encoding uses separate counters for text and motion modalities to avoid cross-modal interference, and a two-stage coarse-to-fine IK solver (VPoser latent optimization followed by joint rotation refinement) converts keypoint trajectories to physically plausible SMPL-X parameters. Joint training on generation and editing tasks yields mutual benefits, with each task reinforcing the other's performance.

## Results

On single-motion generation, MotionMaster achieves 9.88 semantic alignment and 8.75 physical plausibility (0–10 Gemini scale), outperforming the best baselines (MMM: 7.20/6.70) by ~37% and ~31% respectively. For long-sequence generation, it reaches 7.50 semantic score versus 3.34 for the next-best method (MMM), a more than 2× improvement. On motion editing, MotionMaster scores 9.10 semantic and 8.65 physical plausibility with R-Precision R@1 of 0.77, compared to MotionFix's 7.02/7.35/0.27 — a 29.5% gain in semantic correctness and a 3.4× improvement in retrieval precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionMaster dominates all six baselines on semantic alignment and physical plausibility across every task. Against T2M-GPT and MoMask (both retrained on MotionGB), it leads by 2.7–3.2 points in single-motion semantic score and by 4.1–4.9 points in editing semantic score. Compared to MotionMillion, the strongest single-motion baseline, MotionMaster gains 2.9 points in semantic score and 2.05 in physics, though MotionMillion retains a diversity advantage (3.10 vs. 1.62). For long-sequence generation, the gap widens dramatically: MotionMaster's 7.50 semantic score is more than double the next-best MMM (3.34), reflecting the MLLM's inherited compositional reasoning. In editing, MotionFix is the closest competitor (7.02 semantic, 7.35 physics), yet MotionMaster still leads by 2.08 and 1.30 points respectively, and its R-Precision R@1 of 0.77 is 2.8× higher than MotionFix's 0.27. The only consistent trade-off is diversity: MotionMaster scores lower than MotionMillion and MMM on diversity metrics, which the authors attribute to a fidelity–diversity trade-off rather than a methodological limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training motion generation models from scratch (T2M-GPT, MoMask, MMM, MotionMillion) by finetuning a pretrained MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) on motion data, arguing that action semantics and long-horizon reasoning already encoded in language pretraining transfer to motion understanding. It also unifies generation and editing in a single autoregressive model, whereas prior editing methods (MotionFix, MotionLab, SINC) rely on fragmented pipelines or separate conditioning mechanisms. The FSQ-based localized tokenization draws on vector quantization literature but introduces a novel combination of local feature extraction with global reconstruction supervision to address trajectory drift at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10,000-hour MotionGB dataset and the demonstration that MLLM finetuning yields strong zero-shot generalization to unseen action compositions open a practical path toward general-purpose motion intelligence without task-specific architectures. However, the evaluation relies on Gemini as an automated judge (correlated at 0.89 with humans), introducing potential circularity when evaluating MLLM-generated outputs, and the notably lower diversity scores suggest a fidelity-diversity trade-off that may limit creative applications. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger motion corpora, whether the diversity gap can be closed without sacrificing semantic accuracy, and whether the Gemini-based evaluation protocol generalizes beyond the specific action vocabulary in MotionGB.
