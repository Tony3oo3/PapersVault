## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) for masked video-to-video (MV2V) editing are computationally expensive due to iterative denoising and dense spatio-temporal attention. Existing training-free acceleration methods exploit timestep-level feature reuse but apply uniform caching decisions to all tokens within a step, overlooking the ROI-driven token heterogeneity inherent to editing tasks where context tokens outside the mask contribute unequally to generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261753/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free inference-time wrapper around a pretrained DiT backbone (e.g., Wan2.1-VACE 1.3B/14B, LTX-Video 2B). It consumes a video clip in latent space, a binary spatial mask defining the region of interest, and optionally a text prompt for text-guided editing. The mask is used to partition spatio-temporal tokens into context, margin, and generative groups at each full-compute step; the output is the denoised latent decoded into an edited or completed video at the original resolution (tested at 480P and 720P, up to 57 frames).

## Method

HetCache introduces heterogeneous caching across two dimensions. At the timestep level, it computes a relative L1 difference of the timestep-embedding-modulated input (F_t = T_t ⊙ x_t) between consecutive steps and accumulates this over a window; based on thresholds Δ and 1.5Δ, each step is classified as full-compute (full forward pass + cache refresh), partial-compute (subset of tokens recomputed with EMA-style cache update), or reuse (cached output directly). At the token level, during full-compute steps the editing mask partitions tokens into context (far from mask), margin (adjacent to mask boundary), and generative (inside mask) groups. Context tokens are clustered via K-Means (K=16), and each token's importance is scored by its cached sparse attention to generative tokens; only the top-r_ctx fraction per cluster is retained for subsequent partial-compute steps. This reduces attention complexity from O((X_c + X_m + X_g)²) to O((r_ctx·X_c + X_m + X_g)²) while preserving the critical generative-context and generative-margin interactions that determine editing fidelity.

## Results

On VACE-Benchmark (video inpainting, 50 steps), HetCache-fast achieves a 2.67× speedup (166.81 s vs. 445.52 s baseline) and reduces FLOPs from 145.21 P to 23.60 P, while maintaining PSNR 16.58, SSIM 0.56, and VBench 75.88%. On VPBench (text-guided editing, 50 steps), HetCache-fast reaches 1.91× speedup (128.61 s vs. 246.05 s) with 13.99 PFLOPs, VFID 27.14, and VBench 80.59%. In extended settings (Table 2), HetCache scales to 2.91×–3.06× speedups on higher-resolution (720P) and longer (57-frame) videos, and achieves 2.00× on the LTX-Video backbone with improved PSNR (15.28 vs. 14.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full-computation Wan2.1-VACE baseline, HetCache-fast delivers 2.67× speedup on VACE-Benchmark (166.81 s vs. 445.52 s) while actually improving PSNR (16.58 vs. 16.06) and matching SSIM, and 1.91× on VPBench (128.61 s vs. 246.05 s) with VBench 80.59% vs. 79.26%. Compared to PAB (Pyramid Attention Broadcast), HetCache-fast reduces FLOPs by 46% (23.60 P vs. 43.56 P) and achieves higher speed (2.67× vs. 1.99×) with marginally better PSNR (16.58 vs. 16.46). Against FastCache, HetCache-fast is substantially faster (2.67× vs. 1.86×) and far superior in quality: PSNR 16.58 vs. 15.95, SSIM 0.56 vs. 0.54, and VFID 54.51 vs. 68.55, indicating FastCache suffers notable quality degradation. Versus TeaCache-fast (the strongest caching baseline), HetCache-fast achieves higher speed (2.67× vs. 2.53×), lower FLOPs (23.60 P vs. 36.30 P), and slightly better PSNR (16.58 vs. 16.51), though TeaCache-fast edges out on VBench (76.80% vs. 75.88%) and VFID (54.86 vs. 54.51). On the LTX-Video backbone (Table 2), HetCache achieves 2.00× speedup (70 s vs. 140 s) with improved PSNR (15.28 vs. 14.72) and VBench (81.00 vs. 80.41), while TeaCache on the same backbone yields only 1.05× with no reported quality metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

HetCache builds directly on two complementary lines of training-free diffusion acceleration: timestep-level feature caching (TeaCache, AdaCache, PAB) that reuses intermediate features across denoising steps, and token-level caching in DiTs (Zou et al., ICLR 2025) that selectively prunes or reuses individual tokens. The key departure is that prior methods treat these two dimensions independently or apply uniform decisions to all tokens within a timestep, whereas HetCache couples them through a heterogeneous, ROI-aware strategy: it partitions spatio-temporal tokens into context, margin, and generative groups using the editing mask, then uses K-Means clustering and cached attention scores to select only the most semantically representative context tokens for partial-compute steps. This editing-specific token partitioning is the conceptual novelty over generic token-caching approaches that do not exploit the structural asymmetry between masked and unmasked regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.67× latency speedup with near-identical PSNR/SSIM/VFID on VACE-Benchmark is practically significant for interactive video editing pipelines where DiT inference latency is the primary bottleneck. The ROI-aware token partitioning (context/margin/generative) is a natural and generalizable design principle that could inform acceleration for other mask-conditioned generation tasks. However, the method is inherently tied to the presence of an explicit spatial mask, limiting its applicability to unconditional or text-only video generation. Open questions include whether the K-Means clustering and attention-correlation selection generalize to tasks without clear ROI boundaries, how the approach behaves under very aggressive compression (r_ctx < 0.5), and whether the EMA-style cache update in partial-compute steps can accumulate error over very long denoising trajectories.
