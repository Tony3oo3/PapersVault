## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for diffusion models rely on either manual, architecture-specific heuristics (e.g., Q-Diffusion's hardcoded UNet skip-connection rules) or runtime-dynamic data such as timestep-varying activations (e.g., PTQ4DiT), creating a 'Compiler Gap' that prevents automated integration with modern static graph-based AI compilers like TensorRT. This limits large-scale, production-ready deployment of quantized diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263781/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pre-trained diffusion model (DiT or UNet backbone) represented as a static computation graph (torch.fx) along with a small set of calibration images (256 for SD3/SDXL, 64 for 8-bit FLUX, 32 for 4-bit). It outputs a quantized model (W8A8 or W4A8) whose kernels are fully compatible with standard GPU GEMM execution, Tensor Core parallelism, and CUDA epilogue fusion, requiring no custom operators or non-standard data formats at inference time.

## Method

SegQuant combines two novel modules within a modular PTQ pipeline. SegLinear performs fully automatic, graph-based semantic segmentation: it pattern-matches chunk/split/concat operators in the static computation graph to identify semantically distinct input or output segments of linear layers, then applies independent quantization (with per-segment scales and optimizer hyperparameters) to each segment, preventing cross-segment quantization interference. DualScale addresses polarity-asymmetric activations (SiLU, GELU) by decomposing the activation matrix into positive and negative parts, quantizing each with a separate scale, and recombining via a single BatchedGEMM (CUTLASS) with a fused epilogue, preserving negative-range resolution without custom kernels. The framework wraps these with pluggable Optimizer (SmoothQuant, SVDQuant) and Calibrator (GPTQ, AMax) components, enabling flexible configuration while remaining compiler-native and architecture-agnostic.

## Results

On MJHQ-30K, SegQuant-A (AMax) achieves FID 24.33 and Image Reward 0.924 on SD3.5-DiT at W8A8, approaching the FP16 baseline (FID 23.70, IR 0.952) while outperforming all PTQ baselines. On FLUX-DiT (12B) at W8A8, SegQuant-A reaches FID 22.85 and IR 0.835, substantially closing the gap to the BF16 baseline (FID 23.21, IR 0.837). At the more aggressive W4A8 setting, SegQuant-G achieves FID 27.30 on SD3.5 and FID 23.45 on FLUX, consistently surpassing SVDQuant (27.95 and 23.61 respectively) and dramatically outperforming PTQ4DiT (60.47 and 41.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegQuant consistently outperforms all five baselines across the three evaluated models. Against PTQ4DiT, the advantage is most dramatic at W4A8, where SegQuant-G achieves FID 27.30 on SD3.5 and 23.45 on FLUX versus PTQ4DiT's 60.47 and 41.02, respectively; at W8A8 on FLUX, SegQuant-A (FID 22.85) beats PTQ4DiT (27.34) by over 4 FID points. Against SVDQuant at W4A8, SegQuant-G edges ahead with FID 27.30 vs. 27.95 (SD3.5) and 23.45 vs. 23.61 (FLUX), while also improving Image Reward (0.762 vs. 0.725; 0.789 vs. 0.783). Against Q-Diffusion, SegQuant-G matches or slightly exceeds it on SDXL-UNet at W8A8-fp (FID 17.03 vs. 17.04) but clearly wins on FLUX-DiT at W8A8-int (FID 23.07 vs. 23.99, IR 0.822 vs. 0.732). PTQD and TAC-Diffusion are only evaluated on SD3.5-DiT W8A8, where SegQuant-A substantially outperforms both (FID 24.33 vs. 36.84 and 26.17, respectively). Notably, SegQuant-A on FLUX-DiT W8A8 (FID 22.85) even surpasses the BF16 baseline (23.21), suggesting the segmentation and dual-scale strategies can partially compensate for precision loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegQuant builds on the modular PTQ pipeline established by SmoothQuant (activation smoothing), SVDQuant (low-rank outlier absorption), and GPTQ (layer-wise calibration), repurposing them as swappable Optimizer/Calibrator plugins. It departs from Q-Diffusion's manual, architecture-specific rules (e.g., hardcoded UNet skip-connection splitting) and PTQ4DiT's reliance on runtime-dynamic timestep-varying activations, both of which are incompatible with static graph compilers. The key conceptual departure is deriving quantization segmentation purely from the static computation graph (torch.fx pattern matching over chunk/split/concat operators), and addressing polarity-asymmetric activations (SiLU/GELU) via a hardware-native dual-scale scheme that preserves standard Tensor Core GEMM paths rather than introducing custom bit layouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in closing the 'Compiler Gap': by being fully static-graph-driven and hardware-native (CUTLASS BatchedGEMM + CUDA epilogue fusion), SegQuant can be integrated into industrial deployment pipelines (TensorRT, TVM) without custom kernels, which prior effective methods could not. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image synthesis (no video diffusion), modest FID gains over strong baselines (typically 0.1–0.5 points), and the SegLinear component showing negligible standalone FID improvement in ablation (23.35→23.36), suggesting its value is primarily in enabling stable optimization of shared hyperparameters rather than direct error reduction. Open questions include whether the graph-based segmentation generalizes to architectures with more complex dynamic control flow, and whether the dual-scale approach extends cleanly to sub-4-bit regimes where negative-range resolution becomes even more critical.
