## Problem

Existing VLM spatial intelligence benchmarks are designed for ground-based or egocentric perspectives (indoor, street, handheld camera), leaving the spatial reasoning capabilities of VLMs in UAV/aerial scenarios largely unexplored. The UAV perspective introduces unique challenges—varying object scales, top-down occlusion, absence of natural depth cues, and complex ground-level scene understanding—that current benchmarks cannot capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263710/cropped_page0_idx10.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes single or multi-image UAV aerial views paired with a text query, and produces task-specific structured outputs: axis-aligned bounding boxes and point coordinates enclosed in special <box> and <point> tags, multiple-choice selections in <boxed><choice> tags, or free-form text for captioning, function reasoning, and landing safety assessments. The benchmark spans 13 subcategories across Environmental Perception (localization, color, distance, height, pointing, reverse pointing, free space, spatial relations) and Scene Understanding (single/multi-image captioning, counting, function reasoning, landing safety), with approximately 1,000 QA pairs drawn from unseen images to prevent data leakage.

## Method

Sky-VLM is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and trained in two stages. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on the 1M-sample SpatialSky-Dataset (auto-generated from UAVScenes' 20K images using RGB, semantic masks, LiDAR depth, pose, and bounding boxes via VLM-based QA generation with 20+ templates per task), learning aerial visual representations and task-specific output formats with answer-only gradient computation. Stage 2 applies GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning on 30K samples with task-specific reward functions: a binary L1-distance threshold (≤50 px) for pointing, exact match for multiple-choice, and continuous IoU for bounding boxes, regularized by KL divergence (β=0.01) against the SFT reference policy. This two-stage design yields a 139.6% average improvement over the best baseline (GPT-5) on SpatialSky-Bench, with the RL stage contributing an additional ~5% gain over SFT alone, particularly on localization tasks.

## Results

On SpatialSky-Bench, Sky-VLM (7B) achieves a total average score of 53.30, substantially outperforming all baselines. The best closed-source model (GPT-5) scores only 23.07, while the best open-source model (InternVL3.5-8B) reaches 18.65 and the best spatial-specific model (SpatialVLM-8B) achieves 19.02. Sky-VLM's per-task highlights include 42.68 mIoU on bounding box localization, 84.00 on distance estimation, 79.00 on color recognition, and 61.40 on landing safety analysis — all task scores are the highest among all evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sky-VLM (7B) outperforms all 14 baselines on every single task in SpatialSky-Bench, with a total average of 53.30 versus 23.07 for the strongest closed-source model (GPT-5) — a 139.6% relative improvement. Among closed-source models, GPT-5 leads at 23.07, followed by Gemini-2.5-Pro (22.75) and Gemini-2.5-Flash (21.99); none exceed 23.07. Open-source general-purpose VLMs perform even worse, with InternVL3.5-8B (18.65) as the best, while larger Qwen2.5-VL variants (32B: 13.93, 72B: 16.05) actually underperform the 7B version (16.93), suggesting scale does not help for UAV spatial tasks. Spatial-specific models (SpatialVLM 19.02, VILASR 13.45, SpaceR 12.61) fail to transfer their ground-level spatial training to aerial perspectives, with SpaceR scoring only 4.00 on color and 2.96 on landing safety. The most dramatic gaps appear in bounding box localization (Sky-VLM 42.68 mIoU vs. SpaceR 7.45, a 473% improvement) and free-space detection (43.20 vs. best baseline 14.71 from Gemini-2.5-Flash), confirming that UAV-specific training is critical for pixel-level spatial tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first spatial intelligence benchmark specifically targeting the UAV/aerial perspective, explicitly contrasting with prior spatial reasoning benchmarks (VSI-Bench, MMSI-Bench, RynnEC-Bench, RoboSpatial, Blink) that all operate from ground-based or egocentric viewpoints. Methodologically, it builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath [16] for the second training stage, applied here to spatial localization tasks, and uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the base architecture. The data pipeline repurposes the UAVScenes dataset (RGB + LiDAR + semantic masks) with an automated VLM-based QA generation process, extending the standard SFT paradigm with task-specific reward functions for box, point, and multiple-choice outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier closed-source models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) score below 24 on average across UAV spatial tasks, and that spatial-specific models trained on ground-level data (SpatialVLM, SpaceR, VILASR) fail to transfer to the aerial perspective, suggesting the top-down viewpoint introduces fundamentally different challenges (scale variation, occlusion patterns, absent depth cues). The 1M-sample SpatialSky-Dataset provides a practical resource for training UAV-aware VLMs, though the benchmark's self-contained nature (same data source for training and evaluation) limits its ability to measure true generalization. Open questions include whether the 13-task taxonomy captures the full breadth of real-time UAV navigation decisions, and whether the GRPO-based RFT approach generalizes to other aerial domains (e.g., indoor drones, maritime) without retraining.
