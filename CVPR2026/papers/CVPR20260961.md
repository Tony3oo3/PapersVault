## Problem

While VLMs have been evaluated on multi-image understanding benchmarks (MuirBench, MIBench, MileBench), these focus on small image sets (2–9 images) or discrete Q&A formats and do not assess the practical task of organizing personal photo albums of 30–100 images according to user-specific objectives. The performance of current VLMs on album-level tasks—understanding user intent, leveraging joint album context, and performing semantic grouping—remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

Each task receives an album of 30–100 photos plus a natural-language user query, with two context modes: visual (all album images fed to the VLM simultaneously) and language (a generated album caption plus a single target image). Outputs are structured as JSON: selected image ID lists (Intent Selection), integer relevance scores 0–3 (Intent Rating), a single group label per image (Group Labeling), or autonomous group-to-image assignments without predefined labels (Group Clustering). A secondary post-processor (Gemini-2.5-Flash) is applied to repair malformed JSON outputs without altering content.

## Method

AlbumBench defines four album-organization tasks—Intent Selection, Intent Rating, Group Labeling, and Group Clustering—each with task-specific metrics (F1/mAP, accuracy/MAE/RMSE, ARI/NMI/Jaccard/F1) and evaluates 20 model configurations (open-source instruct and thinking VLMs, closed-source GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro, and caption-only baselines) across 133 test albums from the CUFED dataset. A key design choice is the dual-context protocol: comparing visual context (all images) against language context (album caption + single image) to isolate how much the model actually leverages visual tokens versus textual summaries. The dataset is split 80/20 into training and test albums, with two event types (casual family gathering, beach trip) withheld from training to enable open-set evaluation. The paper further validates benchmark novelty via Spearman correlation analysis against MMMU-val scores, showing that album-organization performance—especially under language context—is only weakly correlated with general multimodal reasoning ability.

## Results

On AlbumBench (visual context), the best closed-source model GPT-5 with full thinking achieves 56.6% accuracy on Intent Rating and 0.661 ARI on Group Labeling, while Gemini-2.5-Pro (thinking minimized) leads Intent Selection with 0.697 F1. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (instruct) achieves the highest Intent Selection F1 (0.708) and Group Labeling ARI (0.484), while Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking dominates Group Clustering with 0.558 ARI. Notably, the Gemini Caption Baseline (text-only, no images) achieves 0.695 F1 on Intent Selection and 0.642 ARI on Group Labeling, nearly matching or exceeding several full visual-context models, indicating VLMs underutilize visual tokens for album tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, GPT-5 with full thinking leads on Intent Rating (56.6% Acc) and Group Labeling (0.661 ARI), while Gemini-2.5-Pro with minimized thinking leads on Intent Selection (0.697 F1) and Group Clustering (0.513 ARI); the two are roughly complementary. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (instruct) achieves the best Intent Selection F1 (0.708) and Group Labeling ARI (0.484), but Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking surpasses it on Group Clustering (0.558 vs. 0.524 ARI) and Intent Rating (54.7% vs. 51.6% Acc), showing that thinking mode is critical for grouping tasks. Keye-VL-1.5-8B and InternVL3.5-8B consistently underperform, with Group Clustering ARI below 0.25 in instruct mode. The most striking comparison is against the Gemini Caption Baseline (text-only, no images): it achieves 0.695 F1 on Intent Selection and 0.642 ARI on Group Labeling, matching or exceeding several full visual-context models (e.g., GPT-5 thinking-minimized at 0.647 F1 and 0.483 ARI), revealing that current VLMs extract little additional value from album images beyond what a text caption provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining album organization as a distinct task family for VLMs, departing from prior multi-image benchmarks (MuirBench, MIBench, MileBench) that focus on small image sets (2–30 images) with discrete Q&A or retrieval formulations. It repurposes the CUFED event-album dataset and introduces four user-intent-driven tasks (intent selection, intent rating, group labeling, group clustering) that require joint contextual understanding of 30–100 temporally sparse images, a regime not covered by existing long-context or multi-image evaluations. The paper positions itself as complementary to aesthetic assessment, semantic retrieval, and classic clustering by emphasizing purpose-driven, album-specific criteria rather than image-intrinsic properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AlbumBench is the first comprehensive benchmark for album-scale VLM evaluation, and its findings expose a critical gap: caption-only baselines perform comparably to (and sometimes better than) full visual-context models on grouping tasks, suggesting current VLMs fail to effectively leverage 30–100 image tokens. This raises open questions about how to design architectures or training procedures that genuinely exploit large, non-temporally-consistent image collections, and whether the observed instruction-following failures (e.g., 15–19% overall grouping error rate) will worsen at real-world gallery scales. The benchmark also provides training splits to seed future work on album-specific fine-tuning, but the narrow scope (single event albums, 23 event types) limits generalizability to broader photo-management scenarios.
