## Problem

Prior dynamic scene understanding methods tie object identity to view-dependent RGB features, making them vulnerable to visibility bias, drifting semantics under deformation, and underrepresentation of occluded regions. No existing approach explicitly disentangles persistent object existence from transient visibility in a continuous 4D formulation, leaving a gap between geometric reconstruction and temporally consistent instance-level semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263070/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular or multi-view RGB video sequences along with temporally consistent 2D instance masks (obtained via DEVA, since neither HyperNeRF nor Neu3D provide ground-truth masks natively). For multi-view Neu3D, all camera views are merged into a single pseudo-monocular sequence, and only instances visible across all views are retained to avoid ill-posed cross-view label inconsistencies. Outputs include rendered novel-view panoptic segmentation masks, a continuous 4D instance field (per-point occupancy probability and conditional identity distribution), and, given a text prompt, open-vocabulary 4D instance localization via Grounded DINO-generated masks reprojected onto the Gaussian representation.

## Method

CIF models each 4D space-time point with a joint distribution γ(x,t,k) = π(x,t)·p(x,t,k), decomposing physical existence (occupancy π) from persistent identity (conditional distribution p), and realizes this field as deformable 3D Gaussians extended with per-primitive occupancy and K-dimensional identity vectors. Field-Aware Splatting extends standard alpha-compositing with an instance transmittance term T_i^inst = ∏(1−π_j·P_j) so that rendered instance maps are shaped by occupancy rather than photometric opacity, decoupling visibility from identity. Instance Identity Estimation aggregates normalized rendering weights across all views and frames to initialize each Gaussian's identity distribution, then applies learnable per-Gaussian calibration factors m_i^k to correct residual visibility bias. Instance-Guided Resampling adaptively relocates Gaussians with weak instance response (γ_i^k = π_i·p_i^k) toward regions with strong response, using a volume-conserving adjustment α_new = 1−(1−α_src)^(1/(n+1)) on both opacity and occupancy to prevent local oversaturation. All parameters are jointly optimized via differentiable rendering with an L1 photometric loss and a cross-entropy instance loss.

## Results

On HyperNeRF, CIF achieves average 96.40 mAcc-pix, 85.69 mAcc-inst, and 79.47 mIoU for novel-view panoptic segmentation, surpassing the second-best method (VLGS) by +2.09, +11.78, and +11.42 respectively. On Neu3D, CIF reaches 94.97 mAcc-pix, 93.19 mAcc-inst, and 88.31 mIoU, outperforming VLGS by +4.72, +2.50, and +5.82. The largest single-scene gain is on 'torchocolate' (HyperNeRF), where mIoU improves by +15.11 over SA4D and +20.89 over VLGS. For open-vocabulary 4D querying, CIF produces sharper boundaries and more accurate instance separation than 4D LangSplat and SA4D, particularly under transparent and reflective materials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIF outperforms all five baselines across both datasets and all three metrics. VLGS [43] is the strongest competitor, achieving the second-best mIoU on both HyperNeRF (68.05 avg) and Neu3D (82.49 avg), but CIF extends the mIoU gap to +11.42 and +5.82 respectively, with the largest per-scene margin of +20.89 mIoU on 'torchocolate'. SA4D [16] performs competitively on some scenes (e.g., 'sear steak' on Neu3D where it reaches 80.12 mIoU) but is consistently outperformed by CIF by 5–15 mIoU points on average. Trace3D [50] and Dr. Splat [19] trail significantly, with Dr. Splat being the weakest baseline (56.13 avg mIoU on HyperNeRF), reflecting its reliance on view-dependent language embeddings without explicit instance modeling. For open-vocabulary 4D querying, CIF qualitatively surpasses both 4D LangSplat [27] and the extended SA4D, producing clearer boundaries around transparent objects (glass cup, steel jug) where baselines exhibit boundary leakage and semantic confusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS, Deformable 3D Gaussians) by augmenting each Gaussian with a probabilistic occupancy-identity decomposition, departing from prior semantic Gaussian methods (SA4D, VLGS, Trace3D, Dr. Splat) that mediate instance supervision through RGB rendering and thus inherit view-dependent visibility bias. The key conceptual departure is treating the 4D scene as a continuous joint distribution over existence and identity rather than a view-dependent feature field, with two supporting mechanisms (visibility-bias calibration and instance-guided resampling) that have no direct analogue in the prior Gaussian segmentation literature. For open-vocabulary querying, it builds on the 4D LangSplat pipeline but replaces language-embedded features with the learned instance field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean probabilistic decomposition (occupancy × conditional identity) offers a principled way to handle occlusion and appearance change in dynamic scenes, and the +11.4 mIoU gain on HyperNeRF is a substantial margin that suggests prior methods' visibility bias was a first-order failure mode rather than a minor artifact. However, the evaluation relies on DEVA-generated pseudo-ground-truth masks and re-implemented baselines, and the multi-view Neu3D protocol (treating views as pseudo-temporal, discarding occluded instances) limits generalizability claims. The authors themselves flag the absence of a standardized 4D open-vocabulary benchmark as an open problem, and whether the instance-guided resampling mechanism scales to scenes with many more instances or longer temporal extents remains untested.
