## Problem

Object detectors trained on limited single-source domains inherit spurious correlations from confounders (illumination, co-occurrence, style), degrading out-of-distribution generalization. Existing domain generalization approaches largely ignore these confounding effects, and prior causal-inference methods for vision require explicit confounder dictionaries or task-specific post-processing (clustering, momentum updates), limiting flexibility and scalability. The paper asks how to block confounders in a principled, end-to-end, plug-and-play manner that integrates seamlessly with frozen Vision Foundation Models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260899/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces bounding boxes with class labels via a Faster R-CNN (or Sparse R-CNN / TOOD) detection head. Internally, the CBB operates on multi-scale feature maps (B×N×C tensors where N=H×W) extracted from frozen VFM backbones (DINOv2-L, DINOv3-L, SAM-Huge, or Stable Diffusion v2.1 latent diffusion features). For diffusion-based backbones, a CrossKD distillation step transfers knowledge to a ResNet-101 student for lightweight deployment, while discriminative VFM backbones remain fully frozen during training.

## Method

Bridge introduces a Causal Basis Block (CBB) that implements Pearl's front-door adjustment through a basis-learning perspective, eliminating the need for explicit confounder specification. The CBB first uses learnable Sample Queries to compute a spatial weighting map that aggregates cross-sample generalizable information (approximating E[X′]), then projects the query-guided features onto a low-rank basis subspace (K≪C) via a closed-form coefficient computation, filtering redundant and task-irrelevant components. The final output combines the estimated expectations with a convolutional mediator feature (F_out = Ê[X] + Ê[M] + M), where the first two terms implement the front-door adjustment to block backdoor confounding paths while the mediator preserves task-specific signal. The entire module is differentiable, trained end-to-end with the detection loss, and requires no additional supervision; at inference the basis projection matrix can be precomputed as a fixed C×C matrix for efficient deployment.

## Results

On the Cross-Camera benchmark (Cityscapes→BDD100K), Bridge with a Stable Diffusion backbone achieves 53.1 mAP, surpassing the previous best (Boost, 49.3 mAP) by +3.8 mAP. With discriminative VFMs, Bridge yields consistent gains: +5.1 mAP over DINOv2 (56.9 vs 51.8), +1.1 mAP over DINOv3 (58.9 vs 57.8), and +2.0 mAP over SAM (50.1 vs 48.1) on BDD100K. Across all five benchmarks, Bridge outperforms the previous runner-up by +3.8, +2.9, +2.4, +0.4, and +1.5 mAP respectively, and improves the VFM baselines (SAM, DINOv2, DINOv3) by average gains of +2.7, +4.5, and +1.8 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bridge consistently outperforms the strongest prior method, Boost [16] (ICCV'25), across all five benchmarks: +3.8 mAP on BDD100K (53.1 vs 49.3), +2.9 on FoggyCityscapes (53.6 vs 50.7), +2.4 on Diverse Weather (44.2 vs 41.8), +0.4 on Real-to-Artistic (63.4 vs 63.0), and +1.5 on DroneVehicle (42.0 vs 40.5) using the SD backbone. Against GDD [17] (CVPR'25), Bridge gains +6.5 mAP on BDD100K and +3.5 on FoggyCityscapes with the SD backbone. Over the frozen VFM baselines without Bridge, the CBB module adds +5.1 mAP (DINOv2), +1.1 mAP (DINOv3), and +2.0 mAP (SAM) on BDD100K, with larger gains on FoggyCityscapes (+5.4, +3.9, +4.1 respectively). A re-implemented front-door adjustment via cross-attention (FACL, from GOAT [66]) yields only marginal or negative gains (e.g., 58.5 vs 57.8 on BDD100K, 48.2 vs 48.6 on DWD), whereas Bridge's basis-learning CBB achieves 58.9 and 50.8 on the same benchmarks. Bridge also generalizes across detector architectures, improving Sparse R-CNN by +1.3–3.3 mAP and TOOD by +2.5–3.3 mAP across the five datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the front-door adjustment framework from Wang et al. [66] (originally applied to vision-and-language navigation) into the domain generalization object detection setting, reformulating the causal intervention as a basis-learning problem rather than a cross-attention mechanism. It departs from prior causal-inference-for-vision works (e.g., [79, 80]) that require explicitly specified confounder dictionaries or task-specific post-processing (clustering, momentum updates) by instead learning low-rank bases end-to-end, making the approach plug-and-play with frozen Vision Foundation Models. The NWGM approximation [71] provides the mathematical bridge that makes the nested expectations in the front-door formula tractable, and the CBB module operationalizes this through Sample Queries and subspace projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CBB offers a practical, backbone-agnostic mechanism to mitigate spurious correlations in single-source DGOD without unfreezing VFM weights, which is important given the compute cost of fine-tuning large models. The new Diverse Weather DroneVehicle benchmark fills a gap in UAV-based domain generalization evaluation under adverse conditions. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that CBB approximates the front-door expectations via learned bases rather than deriving closed-form solutions, leaving open whether the approximation quality degrades in more complex or higher-dimensional representation spaces. Whether the low-rank filtering benefit generalizes beyond detection to other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, tracking) remains unexplored.
