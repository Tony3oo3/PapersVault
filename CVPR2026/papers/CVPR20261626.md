## Problem

Current VLM-based end-to-end autonomous driving planners cannot reliably ground 3D spatial coordinates to visual semantics or predict precise trajectory waypoints, because (1) VLMs lack 3D-data pre-training and thus cannot associate metric coordinates with 2D image features, and (2) language models decode numbers as independent digit classifications, destroying inter-digit proximity needed for continuous coordinate regression. Existing work either introduces task-specific 3D embeddings that sacrifice VLM generalization or generates waypoints as digit-by-digit token sequences that are inherently imprecise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261626/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes six 640×640 surround-view images, a text prompt that may contain BEV coordinate expressions, and historical ego waypoints (T past positions). A frozen UniDepthV2-ViT-L depth estimator produces per-patch metric depths that are back-projected to 3D coordinates before entering the VLM. At output, the VLM emits free text for scene reasoning, but when a special ⟨IND⟩ token is predicted, the next hidden state is routed to a regression MLP that directly outputs a 3D coordinate vector (x, y, z) rather than generating digits autoregressively; for trajectory planning the z-component is omitted, yielding six BEV waypoints over a 3-second horizon.

## Method

SpaceDrive introduces a single universal 3D sine-cosine positional encoding φ(·) that plays two roles simultaneously: it is additively injected onto modality-aligned visual tokens (scaled by a learnable normalization α_PE) to give each patch an explicit metric 3D location, and it replaces all numeric coordinate tokens in both input text prompts and output trajectory waypoints, so the VLM reasons over a consistent spatial representation rather than digit strings. The PE encoder is a translation-invariant 3D sine-cosine function (chosen over RoPE to avoid collision with the base VLM's existing rotary encoding), while the PE decoder is a fully learnable coordinate-wise MLP trained with Huber loss, since the sinusoidal encoding is not analytically invertible. The framework is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with LoRA (rank 16) fine-tuning of the LLM only, keeping the vision encoder and projector frozen, and is trained with a combined language-modeling loss plus coordinate-regression loss. Ablations confirm that the shared PE across perception and reasoning streams, coordinate-wise (rather than task-specific) decoding, and learnable PE normalization are each necessary for the observed gains in L2 error, collision rate, and intersection rate on nuScenes and the 78.02 Driving Score on Bench2Drive.

## Results

On the nuScenes open-loop benchmark, SpaceDrive+ achieves the best L2 error of 0.32 m, Collision rate of 0.23%, and Intersection rate of 1.27% among all VLM-based methods, outperforming OmniDrive-Q++ (0.33 m / 0.30% / 3.00%) and ORION (0.34 m / 0.37%). On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, SpaceDrive+ attains a Driving Score of 78.02 and Success Rate of 55.11%, ranking second among VLM-based planners behind SimLingo (85.07 / 67.27%) but ahead of ORION (77.74 / 54.62%). Notably, SpaceDrive+ does not use BEV features, demonstrating that unified 3D positional encoding alone suffices for spatial modeling in VLM-based driving.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaceDrive+ outperforms all VLM-based baselines on nuScenes open-loop: it reduces L2 error by 0.01 m over OmniDrive-Q++ (0.32 vs 0.33), 0.02 m over ORION (0.32 vs 0.34), and 0.08 m over DriveVLM (0.32 vs 0.40), while also achieving the lowest Collision (0.23% vs 0.27–0.37%) and Intersection (1.27% vs 3.00%) rates among VLM methods. Against the strongest traditional baseline UAD, SpaceDrive+ is competitive on L2 (0.32 vs 0.30) and superior on Collision (0.23% vs 0.27%) and Intersection (1.27% vs 1.37%). On Bench2Drive closed-loop, SpaceDrive+ (78.02) trails SimLingo (85.07) by 7.05 points but edges out ORION (77.74) by 0.28 points and VLR-Drive (75.01) by 3.01 points in Driving Score. Even without ego-status inputs, SpaceDrive surpasses its codebase OmniDrive-Q on all metrics (L2: 1.80 vs 1.98; Collision: 1.88% vs 3.79%; Intersection: 4.21% vs 4.59%), confirming the benefit of explicit 3D spatial encoding independent of ego priors. The ablation further shows that the unified PE applied to both vision tokens and text coordinates (Exp. 4) yields substantially better results than either component alone (Exp. 2 or 3), validating the joint spatial-semantic design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaceDrive builds directly on the OmniDrive codebase [63] but departs fundamentally from the prevailing VLM-based driving paradigm where coordinates are either encoded as digit-by-digit text tokens (DriveLM, OmniDrive) or decoded from task-specific embeddings (ORION, SimLingo). Conceptually, it bridges the spatial-intelligence-in-VLMs literature (SpatialVLM, Scene-LLM, LLaVA-3D) with end-to-end driving by repurposing the Transformer's native positional-encoding mechanism as a universal 3D coordinate interface, rather than introducing new task-specific modules. The key architectural departure is treating metric 3D coordinates as additive positional encodings shared across visual tokens, text-prompt coordinates, and ego-state history, unifying perception and reasoning under a single spatial representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single unified 3D positional encoding can simultaneously replace dense BEV feature maps and digit-wise numerical token generation, suggesting that VLM-based driving systems may not need the heavy 3D perception stacks traditionally assumed. This has practical implications for system simplicity and transferability across VLM backbones. However, the framework lacks explicit uncertainty quantification and multi-frame temporal memory, and the closed-loop evaluation is limited to a single simulator (CARLA v2) with 220 routes, leaving open whether the spatial-PE advantage persists in more diverse or longer-horizon driving scenarios. The finding that pure text-based trajectory generation collapses in closed-loop (<10% success) while spatial tokens restore it also raises broader questions about the limits of language as a control interface.
