## Problem

Current diffusion-based makeup transfer methods rely on off-the-shelf foundation models (e.g., CLIP) to encode makeup styles, but these models pre-trained for generic tasks struggle to capture fine-grained facial makeup characteristics. Additionally, existing methods inject reference makeup features as a single global representation into the denoising model, overlooking facial region-specific makeup (eyes, mouth, skin) and thus lacking the ability to perform region-specific makeup transfer from multiple references.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260659/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference makeup image and a source identity image (both resized to 512×512), along with a 3D mesh reconstructed from the source via 3DDFA-V3. It produces a single makeup-transferred face image in the VAE latent space of Stable Diffusion v2.1. For region-specific transfer, the user can select which facial region embeddings (skin, eyes, nose, mouth — N=4) to source from different reference images, enabling combinatorial regional control without requiring masks at inference time.

## Method

FRAM is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 fine-tunes a CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder into a makeup-specific encoder by synthesizing annotated data (GPT-o3 generates 50 diverse makeup style descriptions; FLUX.1-Kontext-dev applies them to FFHQ faces) and training with joint self-supervised contrastive learning (InfoNCE on TPS/crop/flip-augmented views) and image-text contrastive learning against a frozen CLIP text encoder. Stage 2 constructs before-and-after makeup pairs (with affine alignment via JMLR landmarks and FaRL mask-based filtering) and trains the diffusion model to inject identity (via a ControlNet Union encoding both pixel-level source image and 3D mesh structure) and makeup (via N learnable tokens that query the makeup CLIP to produce facial region embeddings, injected as image prompts through cross-attention in the IP-Adapter/InstantID style). An attention loss (focal + dice) aligns each region embedding's cross-attention map with the corresponding face parsing mask, enabling region-specific control at inference. LoRA is applied to the denoising model's cross-attention layers, jointly trained with the CLIP projector and identity encoder.

## Results

On the three evaluation datasets (MT, Wild-MT, CPM-real), FRAM achieves the best makeup style consistency (CSD) across all datasets: 0.536, 0.571, and 0.528 respectively. It also attains the highest identity preservation (ID) on MT (0.587) and Wild-MT (0.499), the best face structure similarity (SSIM) and lowest non-edited region difference (L2-M) on all three datasets, and the highest aesthetic score (Aes: 5.25, 5.05, 4.95). In the user study, FRAM is selected as the best result by 58.5% of participants, compared to 20.5% for CSD-MT, 16% for Stable-Makeup, and 5% for SHMT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRAM outperforms the closest diffusion-based competitor, Stable-Makeup [46], on CSD (0.536 vs 0.527 on MT; 0.571 vs 0.562 on Wild-MT; 0.528 vs 0.515 on CPM-real) and ID (0.587 vs 0.413 on MT; 0.499 vs 0.428 on Wild-MT), while also achieving higher SSIM and Aes scores. Against SHMT [33], FRAM wins on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest gap in ID (0.587 vs 0.372 on MT). The training-free method MAD [27] is clearly inferior, scoring the lowest CSD (0.328 on MT) and Aes (4.26), confirming that training-based approaches are necessary for effective makeup transfer. Gorgeous [30] achieves the highest ID on CPM-real (0.624 vs FRAM's 0.429) but trails FRAM substantially on CSD (0.399 vs 0.528) and Aes (4.61 vs 4.95), indicating it preserves identity at the cost of makeup fidelity. The GAN-based CSD-MT [34] is the weakest overall, with the lowest SSIM (0.424 on MT) and L2-M (0.146 on MT), reflecting poor structural preservation and non-face region editing. For region-specific makeup transfer, FRAM is compared qualitatively against SCGAN [3], the only prior method supporting regional control, and demonstrates superior quality and controllability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based makeup transfer line initiated by Stable-Makeup (SIGGRAPH 2025) and SHMT (NeurIPS 2024) by replacing off-the-shelf CLIP with a domain-specific makeup CLIP encoder fine-tuned on GPT-o3-synthesized annotated data, and by replacing global feature injection with facial region-aware embeddings extracted via learnable token queries (inspired by Gao & Patras, CVPR 2024). The identity injection leverages ControlNet Union to jointly encode pixel-level appearance and 3D mesh structure, departing from Stable-Makeup's dual-ControlNet design. The makeup injection mechanism borrows the cross-attention image-prompt paradigm from IP-Adapter and the projector structure from InstantID, but adds a novel attention loss that aligns cross-attention maps with face parsing masks to enable region-specific control—a capability absent in prior diffusion-based methods and previously only explored in GAN-based SCGAN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first demonstration of region-specific makeup transfer in a diffusion framework opens a practical path toward composable, multi-reference makeup editing (e.g., skin from one reference, eyes from another), which has direct applications in virtual try-on and content creation. The data synthesis pipeline (GPT-o3 descriptions + FLUX.1-Kontext-dev editing + affine alignment) is a reusable recipe that addresses the chronic scarcity of paired makeup data. Limitations include reliance on synthetic data quality, a fixed set of 4 facial regions, evaluation on relatively small datasets (CPM-real, Wild-MT), and the fact that the regional control mechanism has not been tested at scale or with more granular region decompositions. Whether the attention-loss-based regional supervision generalizes to arbitrary region definitions or more complex multi-reference compositions remains an open question.
