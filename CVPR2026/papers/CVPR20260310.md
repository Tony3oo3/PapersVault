## Problem

Learning fine-grained event representations at scale is hindered by the heavy annotation burden of dense event labeling, and existing self-supervised or cross-modal distillation approaches suffer from semantic collapse due to inherent sparsity and granularity mismatches between event and image domains. In particular, patch-level and superpixel-level alignment objectives exacerbate these cross-modal discrepancies, degrading event representations—especially at higher resolutions—rather than capturing the semantically coherent structures that sparse event edges actually encode.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260310/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized event volumes of shape H×W×B (B=3 temporal bins) aggregated from raw asynchronous event streams via motion-adaptive sampling, alongside spatially aligned RGB images at 640×480 resolution. Both modalities are processed by identical ViT encoders (patch size 16, ViT-S/B/L) initialized from DINOv3 weights, producing D-dimensional feature tokens at downsampled spatial sizes H'×W'. The pretraining corpus comprises ~500K image-event pairs aggregated from 16 real-world and VID2E-synthesized datasets spanning diverse scenes, sensors, and resolutions.

## Method

The core contribution is a structure-aware distillation loss that replaces brittle patch- or superpixel-level alignment with semantic-structure-level correspondence inherited from a frozen DINOv3 teacher. An event activation mask thresholds per-patch event density (τ=64) to restrict distillation to high-activity regions, suppressing spurious alignment in empty patches. On top of masked L1 feature matching, two structure losses are imposed: an intra-modal loss penalizing the discrepancy between masked event-event and image-image pairwise similarity matrices, and a cross-modal loss aligning event-to-image affinities with image-to-image affinities, effectively enlarging the receptive field and grounding correspondences in coherent semantic groups. The total objective is L_dis = L1(K*,Q*) + 10·L_is + 4·L_cs, trained for 10 epochs on 4 A6000 GPUs with AdamW (lr=5e-6). The resulting pretrained event encoder transfers plug-and-play to dense perception tasks via image-domain decoders (EoMT for segmentation, DAv2 for depth, SEA-RAFT for optical flow).

## Results

On semantic segmentation, the proposed method (ViT-L/16) achieves 65.08% mIoU on DDD17-Seg and 69.65% mIoU on DSEC-Semantic, surpassing the previous best event-pretraining method STP (63.29% and 62.05% respectively) by 1.8% and 7.6%. Under linear probing, the method reaches 58.42% mIoU on DSEC-Semantic, exceeding the best RGB-transfer baseline KWYAF (57.75%). For depth estimation, the method reduces RMSE on DSEC-Depth from 8.880 (DepthAnyEvent-R) to 3.694, and on MVSEC-Depth from 6.465 to 5.554. On optical flow (MVSEC-Flow), the method achieves the lowest EPE of 0.29 on indoor flying1, outperforming STP (0.31) and ECDDP (0.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 22 baselines across all three downstream tasks. In semantic segmentation, it surpasses the strongest event-pretraining competitor STP [45] by 1.8% mIoU on DDD17 (65.08 vs 63.29) and by a substantial 7.6% on DSEC-Semantic (69.65 vs 62.05), and beats the RGB-transfer baseline KWYAF [43] (62.41) by 2.7% on DDD17. In depth estimation, the improvement over DepthAnyEvent-R [4] is dramatic: RMSE drops from 8.880 to 3.694 on DSEC-Depth (a 58% reduction) and from 6.465 to 5.554 on MVSEC-Depth, while also outperforming ECDDP [81] (RMSE 9.477 on DSEC-Depth). For optical flow on MVSEC-Flow, the method achieves the lowest EPE (0.29 on indoor flying1) and outlier ratio (0.03%), beating STP [45] (0.31 EPE) and ECDDP [81] (0.36 EPE) with a ViT-S/16 backbone that is not natively optimized for flow. In the few-shot and linear-probing settings, the method consistently leads: at 5% data on DSEC-Semantic it reaches 62.82% mIoU versus OpenESS [38] at 57.21%, and under linear probing it exceeds all RGB-transfer methods (MaskCLIP [86] at 33.08%, FC-CLIP [83] at 43.00%) by a wide margin (58.42%). The ablation confirms that the structure-aware loss (intra-modal + cross-modal) contributes the largest single gain (+2.4% mIoU over mask-only distillation), while the activation mask alone provides a smaller but consistent improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modal knowledge distillation paradigm for event cameras (EventSAM, OpenESS, DepthAnyEvent) by replacing brittle pixel-, patch-, or superpixel-level alignment objectives with a structure-aware loss that leverages the off-the-shelf semantic structure of DINOv3. It departs from prior event self-supervision methods (ECDDP, STP, TESPEC) that rely on event-only pretext tasks, arguing that the sparsity and discreteness of event data fundamentally limit what can be learned without external supervision. The event activation mask and the dual intra-/cross-modal structure losses are the paper's specific methodological contributions, building on the observation that rigid fine-grained correspondences cause semantic collapse at higher resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis of why existing distillation losses fail at scale (sparsity-induced over-coupling) and offers a concrete fix that yields large, consistent gains across segmentation, depth, and optical flow. The near-halving of depth RMSE (8.880 → 4.564 on DSEC-Depth) and strong linear-probing results suggest the learned representations are genuinely more transferable, not just better on a single task. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond DINOv3 as teacher, whether the 500K-pair pretraining corpus is sufficient for even larger backbones, and whether the structure-aware loss can be adapted to other sparse-sensor modalities (LiDAR, audio).
