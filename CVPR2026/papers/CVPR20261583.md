## Problem

Existing prompt stealing methods for text-to-image models either require white-box gradient access, depend on large-scale labeled datasets for supervised training, or rely on direct captioning without explicit optimization toward the target image. No prior black-box approach can simultaneously recover both the primary subject and the stylistic modifiers of a prompt while remaining generalizable across unseen generators and robust to post-processing defenses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261583/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single target image (the output of some T2I model) together with black-box query access to a T2I generator, which is invoked only after a complete prompt string is assembled—no intermediate gradient or feature access is available. The adversary has no access to large-scale image-caption pairs or commercial prompt bases. The output is a single natural-language prompt structured as 'subject, modifiers,' where the subject captures the core visual entity and the modifiers encode stylistic, compositional, and technical cues (e.g., medium, lighting, lens).

## Method

PROMPTMINER decouples prompt stealing into two phases. Phase I formulates autoregressive token selection as a Markov Decision Process and trains a lightweight adapter on top of a frozen captioner using PPO, with potential-based reward shaping that injects dense intermediate signals via CLIP text-image cosine similarity to overcome the sparsity of end-of-episode image-similarity rewards; the adapter is warm-started with imitation learning on the captioner's own outputs before RL fine-tuning. Phase II performs VLM-guided evolutionary search over the prompt space: a capacity-constrained elitist seed pool is populated with the RL-derived prompt, and at each iteration MCTS selects a seed, a VLM mutator (Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct) applies one of five structure-aware operators (Subject-Paraphrase, Subject-Enrich, Modifier-Generate, Modifier-Description, Modifier-Style), the mutated prompt is executed through the T2I model, and the resulting image's CLIP similarity to the target determines whether the candidate is retained. This two-stage design ensures the subject remains semantically grounded while systematically enriching the prompt with modifiers that drive perceptual fidelity.

## Results

PROMPTMINER achieves CLIP similarity up to 0.958 (MS COCO, FLUX.1 dev) and SBERT textual alignment up to 0.751 (MS COCO, Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium), surpassing all six baselines across three datasets and four T2I backbones. On in-the-wild images from DiffusionDB (unknown generator), PROMPTMINER reaches CLIP 0.863, outperforming the strongest baseline (CLIP-IG, 0.803) by 7.5%. Under defensive perturbations (noise, puzzle effect, watermarking), CLIP similarity drops by at most 2.4 points (0.911 → 0.887), confirming robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROMPTMINER dominates on image similarity (CLIP and LPIPS) across all datasets and models, with the largest margins over PH2P (e.g., CLIP 0.958 vs 0.641 on MS COCO/FLUX) and VGD (0.958 vs 0.791). Against the strongest black-box baselines, PROMPTMINER outperforms VLM-as-expert (GPT-4o) by 3.5 points in CLIP on MS COCO (0.958 vs 0.923) and PromptStealer by 6.0 points (0.958 vs 0.898). On textual alignment (SBERT), the picture is more nuanced: BLIP edges out PROMPTMINER on MS COCO/FLUX (0.700 vs 0.683), and PromptStealer slightly leads on Lexica/FLUX (0.600 vs 0.594), but PROMPTMINER wins SBERT on Flickr and achieves the highest SBERT (0.751) on MS COCO with Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium. On in-the-wild DiffusionDB images, PROMPTMINER leads CLIP by 6.0 points over CLIP-IG (0.863 vs 0.803) while SBERT is essentially tied with VLM-as-expert (0.545 vs 0.544). Overall, PROMPTMINER's two-phase design (RL for subject + VLM-guided search for modifiers) yields the best image fidelity while maintaining competitive textual naturalness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of reinforcement learning and prompt inversion by formulating black-box prompt stealing as a Markov Decision Process solved with PPO on a frozen captioner, rather than relying on gradient-based discrete optimization (PEZ, PH2P) or direct captioning without explicit optimization (BLIP, VGD, CLIP Interrogator). The two-phase decomposition—RL for subject reconstruction followed by VLM-guided evolutionary search with MCTS for stylistic modifiers—repurposes ideas from reward shaping, imitation learning warm-starts, and structured text mutation (inspired by fuzzing) into a unified prompt-recovery pipeline. Unlike PromptStealer, which requires large-scale labeled training data, and Prometheus, which depends on a pre-collected prompt base, PROMPTMINER operates entirely in a black-box, data-free setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As prompts become tradeable digital assets on marketplaces like PromptBase, effective black-box prompt stealing has direct implications for intellectual property protection and model provenance. The paper's demonstration that semantics-driven optimization is inherently resilient to low-level visual defenses (noise, patch perturbation, watermarking) suggests that current post-hoc defenses are insufficient, pushing the field toward more fundamental protection mechanisms. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation or multi-image compositions, whether adaptive defenses that perturb semantic (rather than pixel-level) features can meaningfully degrade performance, and whether the RL phase can be made more sample-efficient for very long or highly compositional prompts.
