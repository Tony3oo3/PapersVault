## Problem

Existing visual reprogramming methods for CLIP rely on single-level alignment between prompted images and text descriptions, overlooking two inherent structural properties of data: visual granularity (multi-scale spatial representations) and semantic granularity (label hierarchies). No prior VR framework simultaneously exploits both granularities to guide visual prompt training, leaving a gap in how hierarchical spatial and categorical relational information can be leveraged for more effective alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263065/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images padded to CLIP's input resolution and a set of class text descriptions, operating in a strict black-box setting where CLIP's visual and language encoders are frozen and inaccessible. The learnable visual prompt δ is added to the padded image via a binary mask that excludes the original image region, and the output is a class label produced by cosine similarity between the prompted image embedding and text embeddings. The framework is evaluated in a 16-shot few-shot regime across 12 datasets, with the input transformation being the only trainable component.

## Method

DGA introduces two collaborative modules for visual prompt training. The Visual Granularity module generates multi-scale crops of each image, applies separate learnable VPs at each scale, and fuses predictions via Uncertainty-calibrated Prediction Fusion (UPF), which computes entropy-based weights to down-weight unreliable crop views before aggregating logits across scales. The Semantic Granularity module constructs category hierarchies through Prototype-guided Label Hierarchization (agglomerative clustering on CLIP visual prototypes) and applies Hierarchical Knowledge Propagation (HKP), which expands superclass logits to subclasses, redistributes them by normalized confidence, and aggregates across hierarchy levels to provide top-down guidance. The global-scale VP is formulated as an average of its own parameters and those of all superclass hierarchies, and the final prediction is a weighted combination (λ=0.7) of VG and SG logits. Training minimizes a combined loss of subclass cross-entropy and hierarchical supervision across all levels.

## Results

On 16-shot downstream classification with ViT-B/16-based CLIP, DGA achieves an average accuracy of 83.8% across 12 datasets, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art DVP-cse (79.3%) by 4.5% on average. The largest gains appear on fine-grained datasets: Aircraft improves from 40.3% to 51.8% (+11.5%) and Stanford Cars from 72.5% to 84.4% (+11.9%). DGA also demonstrates consistent improvements across all CLIP backbone architectures (ResNet-50, ResNet-101, ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16), confirming the generality of the dual-granularity alignment strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGA outperforms all four baselines on every one of the 12 datasets. Against the strongest prior method DVP-cse (79.3% avg), DGA gains 4.5% on average, with particularly large margins on fine-grained recognition: +11.5% on Aircraft (51.8% vs 40.3%) and +11.9% on Stanford Cars (84.4% vs 72.5%). Compared to DVP-cls (78.9%), the improvement is 4.9% on average. Over AttrVR (77.7%), DGA leads by 6.1% on average, and over the earliest baselines VP (73.5%) and AR (75.7%), the gaps widen to 10.3% and 8.1% respectively. The paper also reports consistent gains across ResNet-50, ResNet-101, and ViT-B/32 backbones (Fig. 3), though the absolute improvement is smaller on ViT-B/16 due to its already strong self-attention-based feature extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual reprogramming (VR) line for CLIP, which itself descends from the broader model reprogramming paradigm (Chen, AAAI 2024) and the visual prompt concept (Bahng et al., 2022). Prior VR methods—VP, AR, AttrVR, and DVP—progressively refined how a single learnable input transformation aligns prompted images with text descriptions, but all operate at a single alignment level. DGA departs from this by introducing two orthogonal structural axes: multi-scale visual granularity (via uncertainty-weighted crop fusion) and semantic granularity (via prototype-guided label hierarchies with top-down knowledge propagation), arguing that both are underexploited in the single-level alignment paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.5% average gain over the prior SOTA (DVP) across 12 diverse datasets, with consistent improvements on both ViT and ResNet backbones, makes DGA a practical upgrade for anyone deploying CLIP-based VR in few-shot settings. The dual-granularity framing—treating label hierarchies and multi-scale spatial structure as first-class alignment signals—could inspire similar multi-level strategies in other reprogramming or prompt-learning contexts beyond CLIP. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large class spaces where hierarchy construction becomes costly, whether it transfers to other vision-language models (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2-based), and whether the added multi-scale cropping overhead is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployment.
