## Problem

Existing diffusion bridge models for image restoration lack a unified analytical framework that elucidates their mathematical relationships, and they indiscriminately apply global noise injection and removal across all image regions. This causes degraded regions with varying severity to be treated equally while intact regions suffer redundant and imperfect reconstruction due to cumulative error in the reverse process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262959/cropped_page0_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (the terminal state μ) as input and produces a restored high-quality RGB image as output, operating at full resolution with 10 sampling timesteps. Training uses paired (degraded, clean) image crops of 256×256 pixels across five degradation types (rain, haze, snow, blur, low-light) in a unified multi-task setup. The model is also applied to image-to-image translation (edge-to-handbag at 256×256) and image inpainting (CelebA-HQ at 256×256 with binary masks).

## Method

RDBM theoretically reformulates the generalized diffusion bridge by combining the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck mean-reverting SDE with Doob's h-transform, deriving closed-form analytical solutions for both forward and reverse processes governed by parameters λ, θ_t, and π. The key innovation is setting π to the pixel-wise residual (x₀ − μ) rather than a constant, which modulates the noise perturbation so that degraded regions receive stronger stochastic correction while intact regions remain largely undisturbed, yielding a pixel-independent residual-to-noise ratio with smooth monotonic decay. A U-Net backbone is trained via L1 loss to predict the product π·ε (residual times noise) at each timestep, and the reverse process uses a deterministic sampling formula derived from Bayes' theorem. The paper further proves that all mainstream bridge formulations—VE/VP bridge, Brownian bridge, OU bridge, and flow matching—are special cases of RDBM under specific parameter configurations, unifying them within a single theoretical framework.

## Results

On the five-task universal image restoration benchmark (deraining, low-light enhancement, desnowing, dehazing, deblurring), RDBM-L achieves an average PSNR of 31.04 dB and SSIM of 0.917, outperforming the strongest baseline MaIR (29.51 dB / 0.904) by 1.53 dB in PSNR. The lighter RDBM-B variant (3.65M params) also attains the best average SSIM (0.904) while using only ~18% of MaIR's parameters. On zero-shot under-display camera restoration (POLED/TOLED), RDBM achieves 19.83 dB PSNR on POLED and 30.81 dB on TOLED, surpassing the next-best method (DA-CLIP at 16.79 / 27.26 dB) by a wide margin. For image translation and inpainting, RDBM leads with 19.26 dB PSNR (edge2handbags) and 37.88 dB PSNR (CelebA-HQ inpainting), beating GOUB by 2.68 and 6.32 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDBM-L dominates all 13 universal restoration baselines across every task, with the largest gaps on low-light enhancement (+2.77 dB over MaIR) and dehazing (+3.06 dB over MaIR). Among diffusion-based competitors, GOUB (137M params, 379 GFLOPs) trails RDBM-L by 3.44 dB average PSNR despite 18× more parameters, while IRSDE underperforms severely on enhancement (11.29 dB) and desnowing (15.91 dB). The strongest non-diffusion baseline, MaIR (Mamba-based, 20.71M params), is the closest competitor at 29.51 dB average PSNR but still lags RDBM-L by 1.53 dB. Notably, the lightweight RDBM-B (3.65M params, 24 GFLOPs) matches or exceeds MaIR on average SSIM (0.904 vs 0.904) and PSNR (30.24 vs 29.51) while using only 18% of the parameters. On zero-shot under-display camera restoration (POLED/TOLED), RDBM outperforms DA-CLIP by 3.05 dB (POLED) and 3.55 dB (TOLED), and on image translation/inpainting it beats GOUB by 2.68 dB (edge2handbags) and 6.32 dB (CelebA-HQ inpainting), confirming the generality of the residual-modulated bridge formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDBM unifies and extends the line of diffusion bridge models for image restoration (DDBM, BBDM, GOUB) and mean-reverting SDE approaches (IRSDE) by deriving a generalized bridge formulation governed by three parameters (θ_t, λ, π) and showing all existing bridges are special cases. The key conceptual departure is replacing the fixed global noise scale (π=1) used by prior bridges with the pixel-wise residual π = x₀ − μ, which spatially modulates noise injection and removal so that degraded regions receive more reconstruction while intact regions are preserved. The theoretical contribution—closed-form forward/reverse solutions and a residual-to-noise-ratio analysis—provides the first unified analytical lens connecting stochastic interpolants, Brownian bridges, OU bridges, and flow matching under one framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The residual-modulated bridge offers a principled fix for a well-known failure mode of global-noise diffusion restoration: imperfect reconstruction of undegraded regions due to cumulative reverse-process error. The practical impact is broad—RDBM achieves consistent SOTA across five restoration tasks, image translation, and inpainting with a lightweight U-Net (as few as 0.45M params for RDBM-T), and demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization to unseen degradation types (e.g., under-display camera images). Open questions include whether the residual modulation extends naturally to video or 3D restoration, whether the 10-NFE optimum (beyond which performance degrades) reflects a fundamental limit of the bridge formulation or an artifact of the unified multi-task training, and whether the framework can incorporate learned degradation priors without losing its closed-form structure.
