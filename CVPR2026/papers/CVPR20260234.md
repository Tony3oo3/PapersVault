## Problem

Most dynamic driving scene reconstruction methods require per-scene optimization (hours per scene), known camera calibration, or are limited to short fixed-length frame windows, making them impractical as a scalable pre-processing step for large-scale autonomous driving logs. Existing feedforward approaches either handle only static scenes or, in the case of STORM, still require poses and degrade on long sequences. The paper reformulates camera pose as a model output rather than a required input, enabling pose-free, single-pass 4D reconstruction from arbitrary-length unposed image sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of unposed RGB images (e.g., 3–16 camera views × 20 frames per view) with no camera calibration or pose annotations, and in a single forward pass (~0.39 s) produces per-frame camera extrinsics and intrinsics, pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian maps (15 channels: RGB, 3D position, rotation quaternion, scale, opacity, and a lifespan parameter), binary dynamic masks, per-pixel 3D motion displacement vectors, and diffusion-refined novel-view renderings. The first input frame's camera origin is designated as the world coordinate origin, and the model supports an arbitrary number of input views without re-calibration.

## Method

A ViT backbone fuses DINO-pretrained features with an alternating-attention mechanism to produce shared tokens consumed by multiple prediction heads: a camera head for per-frame poses, a DPT-style Gaussian head for pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians, a lifespan head that modulates each Gaussian's opacity over time via a Gaussian temporal kernel (Eq. 1), a dynamic head for motion masks, and a transformer-based motion head that predicts full 3D displacement trajectories (not just velocities as in STORM) for interpolating dynamic Gaussians at intermediate timestamps. Dynamic decomposition aggregates all static Gaussians across frames with only the current frame's dynamic Gaussians plus a hemisphere of sky Gaussians, then a differentiable 3DGS renderer produces the composite image. A single-step diffusion refinement module (SD-Turbo with LoRA-adapted UNet decoder) takes the rendered frame and a random reference image from the input sequence to suppress ghosting and disocclusion artifacts. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with photometric L2+LPIPS losses, BCE losses for sky/dynamic masks, and L1 regularization on lifespan parameters, with the DINO encoder and camera head frozen during training.

## Results

On Waymo (3 views, 20 frames per view), DGGT achieves 27.41 PSNR, 0.846 SSIM, and 3.47 D-RMSE in 0.39 s per scene, outperforming all 13 baselines on every metric. In zero-shot transfer (trained only on Waymo), it reaches 25.31 PSNR on nuScenes and 26.34 PSNR on Argoverse2, surpassing the best prior method (NoPoSplat at 19.75/22.00) by over 5 dB. For 3D motion estimation on Waymo Scene Flow, DGGT attains 0.183 m EPE3D, 85.42% Acc5, and 90.42% Acc10, improving over STORM (0.276 m, 81.12%, 85.61%) by roughly 34% in EPE3D and 5–9 points in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGGT outperforms the closest dynamic feedforward baseline STORM by +1.03 PSNR (27.41 vs 26.38) and +0.052 SSIM while also being pose-free (STORM requires calibrated poses). Among static feedforward methods, it beats NoPoSplat (the only other pose-free method) by +3.10 PSNR and DepthSplat by +4.15 PSNR, and it is the only method that simultaneously handles dynamics and unposed input. Optimization-based baselines (EmerNeRF, DeformableGS, 3DGS) are 35–74× slower (14–29 min vs 0.39 s) and score 2–3 dB lower in PSNR. In zero-shot cross-dataset transfer, DGGT leads NoPoSplat by +5.56 PSNR on nuScenes and +4.34 PSNR on Argoverse2, and even the Waymo-trained model exceeds STORM's trained-on-target numbers (25.31 vs 24.54 on nuScenes). For 3D tracking, DGGT reduces EPE3D by 34% over STORM (0.183 vs 0.276 m) and by ~74% over NSFP/NSFP++, while also halving the angular error (0.328 vs 0.658 rad). VGGT++ achieves the best D-RMSE among pose-free methods (3.80) but lags DGGT by 4.91 PSNR due to limited RGB detail in its attention features.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGGT extends the feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting line (MVSplat, NoPoSplat, DepthSplat) and the dynamic driving reconstruction work of STORM by reformulating camera pose as a model output rather than a required input, enabling reconstruction from unposed images. It builds on 3DGS as the scene representation and on SD-Turbo's single-step diffusion distillation for a lightweight rendering refinement stage. The key conceptual departure from STORM is threefold: (i) pose-free operation, (ii) a lifespan parameter that modulates Gaussian opacity over time to capture static-scene appearance changes, and (iii) full 3D motion trajectories replacing STORM's velocity-only model, together with support for an arbitrary number of input views rather than a fixed window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for per-scene optimization and camera calibration, DGGT makes 4D driving-scene reconstruction a practical, single-pass pre-processing step (0.39 s per scene) that can be applied at scale to large driving logs, unlocking downstream tasks like instance-level scene editing and what-if simulation. The strong zero-shot transfer (7+ PSNR over STORM on nuScenes/Argoverse2) suggests the pose-free formulation genuinely reduces domain gaps, which is important for cross-dataset deployment. Remaining limitations include failure under heavily occluded motion and inaccurate dynamic masks, and the reliance on a separate diffusion refinement module adds inference cost and complexity that may not transfer cleanly to all scene types.
