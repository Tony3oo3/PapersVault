## Problem

ℓp-norm constrained adversarial examples often fail to align with human visual perception, while perceptually-aligned attacks (e.g., AdvAD, DiffAttack) suffer from substantially reduced success rates against robust models. It remains unclear whether the extensive body of ℓp-constrained attack methods can be systematically composed to yield adversarial examples that are both highly effective and perceptually plausible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260016/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DASH takes a clean RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet), a ground-truth label, and either full white-box access to the target model or a surrogate model for black-box transfer. It produces a single adversarial example whose perturbation budget is governed by SSIM (rather than a fixed ℓp-norm) and a set of learned per-stage, per-attack softmax weights (α_{j,i}) that define the optimal composition of 10 base attacks (FGSM, PGD, CW, FAB, TI-FGSM, NI-FGSM, MI-FGSM, DI-FGSM, BIM, PI-FGSM++) plus a 'None' skip operation. The weights are trained once per target model/dataset pair and then reused for inference.

## Method

DASH is a differentiable, multi-stage meta-attack framework that learns soft attention weights over a pool of ℓp-constrained base attacks. At each of N stages, the adversarial examples produced by all M base attacks are combined via a softmax-weighted average (Eq. 3), and the output is chained as input to the next stage to escape poor local optima in the non-convex adversarial landscape. A meta-loss jointly minimizes the ground-truth class confidence (attack effectiveness) and (1 − SSIM) (perceptual distortion), and the weights α are optimized end-to-end with Adam over 100 epochs. The key insight is that different ℓp norms correlate with distinct perceptual characteristics (contrast, texture, edge disruption), so a learned convex combination of attacks optimized under different norms can simultaneously improve attack success and visual fidelity without modifying any base attack's internal mechanism. The optimization operates over a low-dimensional weight space rather than the full pixel space, making it tractable and extensible to new base attacks.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (Cui2024 robust model), DASH achieves 99.77% average ASR with SSIM 94.43, LPIPS 0.0139, and FID 41.08, substantially outperforming the best perceptual baseline AdvAD (79.14% ASR, SSIM 83.18) and the best norm-constrained baseline CW (84.14% ASR, SSIM 94.38). Across all seven robust models on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, DASH consistently achieves near-100% ASR (96.98–99.77% average) while maintaining SSIM scores of 92.91–96.52. DASH also demonstrates strong transferability, achieving the top average ASR (84.62%) and best SSIM (94.43) in black-box surrogate-to-target settings on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DASH outperforms all seven baselines on average ASR across every model and dataset tested. Against the strongest norm-constrained baseline CW (Carlini & Wagner, IEEE S&P 2017), DASH improves average ASR by ~15.6 points on CIFAR-100 Cui2024 (99.77% vs. 84.14%) while matching its SSIM (94.43 vs. 94.38); CW's advantage is that it is faster (0.11 s vs. 0.96 s per example). Against the strongest perceptual-attack baselines, DASH gains 20.6 points in ASR over AdvAD (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024; 99.77% vs. 79.14%) with a 11.2-point SSIM improvement (94.43 vs. 83.18), and 22.5 points over DiffAttack (Chen et al., TPAMI 2024; 99.77% vs. 77.24%) with a 3.3-point SSIM gain. DiffPGD (Xue et al., NeurIPS 2023) is outperformed by 24 points in ASR and 3.3 points in SSIM. AutoAttack (Croce & Hein, ICML 2020) trails DASH by 23 points in ASR and 2.3 points in SSIM. The simpler FGSM variants (DI-FGSM, PI-FGSM++) are the weakest baselines, with DASH exceeding them by 35+ points in ASR. Notably, DASH's advantage is most pronounced under post-processing defenses (e.g., NRP, where CW drops to 57.71% ASR while DASH maintains 99.61%), confirming that the multi-stage chaining and meta-loss optimization provide robustness beyond what any single base attack achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

DASH repurposes the extensive catalog of ℓp-norm constrained attacks (FGSM, PGD, CW, FAB, DI-FGSM, etc.) as interchangeable building blocks, departing from prior ensemble methods like AutoAttack and Composite Attacks that use discrete selection or genetic-algorithm heuristics to pick among attacks. Conceptually, it sits between the classical norm-constrained attack literature and the newer perceptual-attack line (AdvAD, DiffAttack, DiffPGD) by showing that the latter's perceptual goals can be achieved without purpose-built diffusion or frequency-domain machinery—simply by learning soft attention weights over existing attacks guided by a joint ASR/SSIM meta-loss. The multi-stage chaining mechanism extends the two-stage attack chaining used in transfer-based attacks (Suya et al.) to arbitrary depth with continuously optimized compositions at every stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a lightweight, extensible framework that outperforms complex diffusion-based perceptual attacks by large margins (e.g., ~20% ASR improvement over AdvAD) while also achieving better SSIM/LPIPS scores, and whose learned weights transfer across models and datasets with <3% ASR degradation. This suggests that the field may have over-invested in purpose-built perceptual attack architectures when a principled composition of well-tuned norm-constrained attacks suffices. Limitations include reliance on white-box access for weight training, the use of a 'None' skip operation that somewhat dilutes the multi-stage narrative, and the acknowledged difficulty of incorporating FID into the optimization loop. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative or multimodal tasks, whether treating individual base-attack hyperparameters as learnable would further improve results, and whether the framework's gains persist against stronger or adaptive defenses.
