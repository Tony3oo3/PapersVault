## Problem

Existing self-supervised 3D reconstruction methods that jointly learn scene geometry and camera poses still rely on pretrained supervised priors (e.g., MASt3R, DUSt3R, CroCo) for initialization or pseudo-labels, and most require ground-truth camera intrinsics during training. This prevents fully self-supervised learning from uncalibrated, in-the-wild data and limits scalability. The paper asks whether a single network can jointly predict explicit 3D geometry and all camera parameters from raw multi-view images alone, with no annotations and no pretrained priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260978/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes uncalibrated, unposed multi-view RGB images (default: 2 context views, extendable to 10) at 224×224 or 256×256 resolution, with no camera metadata provided. It outputs per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives (centers, quaternions, scales, opacities, SH coefficients), per-view camera intrinsics parameterized by a single FOV value (assuming square pixels and centered principal point), relative extrinsics normalized to the first view as reference frame, per-pixel depth maps, and photometrically rendered novel target views. At inference, only context images are needed; target views are rendered using self-predicted poses.

## Method

NAS3R uses a shared ViT encoder-decoder backbone (compatible with VGGT or MASt3R architectures) where each view's tokens are concatenated with a learnable camera token. A masked-attention decoder regulates cross-view flow: context tokens attend only to other context tokens (preventing target leakage into Gaussian reconstruction), while target tokens attend to all views for pose estimation. Parallel DPT-style heads predict depth maps, Gaussian parameters, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics from the refined tokens. The key design choice is a depth-based (local-to-global) Gaussian formulation: per-pixel depths are lifted into 3D Gaussian centers using the predicted camera parameters, enforcing that centers lie along viewing rays and providing a well-conditioned geometric initialization that stabilizes self-supervised convergence without any pretrained 3D point priors. Training is purely end-to-end photometric supervision (MSE + LPIPS) on target views rendered with predicted camera parameters, effectively performing differentiable photometric bundle adjustment over Gaussians, poses, intrinsics, and depths simultaneously.

## Results

On RE10K (in-domain, 2-view NVS), NAS3R trained without any supervised priors achieves 23.13 PSNR / 0.764 SSIM / 0.193 LPIPS, outperforming the best self-supervised baselines SelfSplat (19.15 PSNR) and SPFSplat* (21.31 PSNR) by 3.8 and 1.8 dB respectively, and approaching supervised MVSplat (24.01 PSNR). For pose estimation on RE10K, NAS3R reaches 51.0% AUC at 10° overall error, surpassing the fully supervised SuperPoint+SuperGlue (40.6%) and all self-supervised NVS methods (≤23.9%). On depth estimation (BlendedMVS), NAS3R achieves 0.206 rel / 71.4% inlier ratio, beating both supervised MVSplat (0.405 / 54.0%) and self-supervised SPFSplat* (0.255 / 60.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among self-supervised NVS baselines, NAS3R consistently outperforms SelfSplat (by ~4 dB PSNR on RE10K) and SPFSplat* (by ~1.8 dB) across all four evaluation datasets, with the gap widening on out-of-domain splits (e.g., +2.4 dB on DL3DV over SPFSplat*). Against supervised methods, NAS3R narrows the gap to pixelSplat and MVSplat to within ~0.9 dB on RE10K and even surpasses NoPoSplat* (which uses MASt3R priors) by 1.75 dB. For pose estimation, NAS3R (51.0% AUC@10° on RE10K) exceeds the supervised feature-matching pipeline SuperPoint+SuperGlue (40.6%) by 10.4 points and dominates all self-supervised NVS methods (SelfSplat 18.4%, SPFSplat* 23.9%) by a wide margin; on the harder DL3DV split the advantage over SP+SG grows to 25.1 vs. 37.2 at 20°. On depth estimation, NAS3R (rel 0.206, τ 71.4%) beats both supervised MVSplat (0.405, 54.0%) and NoPoSplat (0.508, 34.1%) as well as self-supervised SPFSplat* (0.255, 60.3%), indicating that the depth-based Gaussian formulation yields more geometrically consistent reconstructions than canonical-space or cost-volume approaches. When pretrained priors (MASt3R or VGGT) are added, NAS3R further improves to 25.89 PSNR on RE10K, surpassing SPFSplatV2-L (25.67) and matching or exceeding the supervised NoPoSplat (25.06).

## Relation to Prior Work

NAS3R extends the self-supervised pose-free 3D Gaussian Splatting line (SPFSplat, SelfSplat, PF3plat, NoPoSplat) by removing two dependencies that all prior methods retained: pretrained geometric priors (MASt3R, CroCoV2) and ground-truth camera intrinsics. Architecturally, it builds on the shared-backbone transformer design of VGGT and MASt3R, adding masked attention to prevent target-to-context leakage and a depth-based Gaussian center formulation (via DPT) that replaces the canonical-space point regression used by SPFSplat and NoPoSplat. The key conceptual departure is that the depth-based lifting, combined with photometric bundle adjustment through a differentiable 3DGS renderer, provides sufficient geometric conditioning to train from random initialization without any warm-up distillation or prior initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both 3D annotations and pretrained priors, NAS3R opens a path to scaling 3D reconstruction to truly unconstrained, in-the-wild image collections where SfM pipelines fail and no calibration metadata exists. The demonstrated scalability (consistent gains from RE10K to RE10K+DL3DV) and the finding that self-supervised pretraining substantially improves downstream supervised finetuning (e.g., pose 5° AUC: 33.9 vs. 10.7 from scratch) suggest a practical recipe for leveraging large unlabeled corpora. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to highly dynamic or textureless scenes, whether the 3DGS representation's inherent surface ambiguity can be resolved without GT depth, and whether the assumption of shared intrinsics across views limits applicability to truly uncalibrated multi-device captures.
