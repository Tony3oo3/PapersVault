## Problem

Current humanoid locomotion systems are limited to replaying predefined motion clips or following sparse language commands, lacking the capacity for expressive, context-sensitive improvisation. The dominant audio-to-robot pipeline—generating human motion from audio, then retargeting and tracking to the robot—suffers from cascaded error accumulation, high inference latency, and loose coupling between acoustic cues and joint actuation. This paper asks whether a humanoid can directly generate physically plausible, rhythmically aligned motion from raw audio without any explicit motion reconstruction or retargeting step.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes raw audio (music or speech, segmented into 10-second clips at 30 FPS) and a fixed text prompt (e.g., 'The person is dancing to the music') that is encoded once via LaMP-T2M into a content motion latent. The audio is processed by a 6-layer transformer adaptor with temporal attention to produce a kinematically-aligned audio latent, which is then injected into a diffusion policy alongside the content latent and robot state observations. The output is a sequence of executable joint actions for the Unitree G1 humanoid, generated via a 2-step DDIM sampling schedule to ensure real-time deployment.

## Method

The framework decomposes motion into content (a fixed motion latent from a text-to-motion model specifying the core task) and style (the audio signal dictating how the task is performed). A ΔMoE teacher policy—four MLP experts operating on nested conditional subspaces with residual fusion (a structured generalization of Classifier-Free Guidance)—is trained via RL to produce diverse, complementary motion regimes. An audio adaptor (6-layer transformer with temporal attention) is trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss to embed kinematic priors into audio latents, aligning them with a pretrained motion VAE's latent space. The teacher is then distilled into a diffusion-based student policy (4-layer MLP backbone with AdaLN conditioning) via a DAgger-like procedure, where the content latent guides the denoising trajectory and the aligned audio latent is additively injected at multiple layers to modulate rhythmic expression, achieving retargeting-free, low-latency audio-to-action mapping.

## Results

On the BEAT2 speech-to-gesture task, RoboPerform achieves a 0.99 success rate in IsaacGym (vs. 0.98 baseline) with MPJPE of 0.05 m and MPKPE of 0.04 m. On the more challenging FineDance music-to-dance task, RoboPerform reaches 0.93 success rate in IsaacGym (vs. 0.88 baseline) and 0.67 in MuJoCo (vs. 0.61 baseline), reducing MPJPE from 0.24 m to 0.18 m and MPKPE from 0.21 m to 0.16 m in IsaacGym. The retargeting-free design also yields lower inference latency by eliminating the 1000-iteration PBHC retargeting step required by the baseline pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline pipeline combines a pretrained motion generator (EMAGE for speech, FineNet for dance) with a 1000-iteration PBHC retargeting step and an explicit MLP tracking policy. RoboPerform outperforms this baseline on every metric across both datasets and both physics engines. The advantage is modest on BEAT2 (speech-to-gesture), where the baseline already achieves 0.98 success in IsaacGym and RoboPerform reaches 0.99, but becomes substantially larger on FineDance (music-to-dance): success rate improves from 0.88 to 0.93 in IsaacGym and from 0.61 to 0.67 in MuJoCo, while MPJPE drops by 25% (0.24→0.18 m) in IsaacGym. This pattern indicates that the cascaded error from explicit motion generation and retargeting is more damaging for the higher-variance dance motions than for the more constrained speech gestures. The retargeting-free design also eliminates the PBHC optimization overhead, reducing end-to-end inference latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the retargeting-free latent-driven paradigm established by RoboGhost [18], which used language as the conditioning signal, to the audio modality, unifying music-to-dance and speech-to-gesture under a single framework. The ΔMoE teacher policy is explicitly framed as a structured generalization of Classifier-Free Guidance [9] to multi-dimensional conditional subspaces, replacing the binary conditional/unconditional split with a nested hierarchy of partial conditions. The student policy follows a DAgger-style distillation [27] into a diffusion model, with the key departure being the content-style decomposition where a fixed motion latent provides semantic grounding while aligned audio latents inject rhythmic style across diffusion layers via AdaLN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the explicit motion-generation-and-retargeting pipeline, RoboPerform removes cascaded error accumulation and multi-stage latency, enabling real-time (2-step DDIM) audio-reactive performance on a physical Unitree G1 robot. The content-style decomposition offers a clean conceptual framework that could generalize to other conditioning modalities (e.g., video, haptic signals) beyond audio. Open questions include robustness to unseen music genres or speech styles, whether the fixed content latent limits expressiveness for more complex choreography, and whether the approach scales to multi-robot or interactive settings where the audio signal changes dynamically mid-performance.
