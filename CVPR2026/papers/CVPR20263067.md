## Problem

Existing VLM pruning methods operate in a single mode (either parameter or token pruning), and naive joint pruning fails because of (1) intrinsic inconsistency between parameter and token importance metrics—unimportant tokens distort parameter scores and pruned parameters distort token scores—and (2) inflexible fixed-order application of pruning modes that cannot adapt as the model's redundancy profile shifts across progressive stages. This results in substantial accuracy degradation at high pruning ratios (≥0.7).

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Transformer-based VLM (BLIP, CLIP, or LLaVA-7B) and a small calibration set for forward-pass importance estimation. It produces a compressed model with two types of binary masks: structured parameter masks (M^p ∈ {0,1}^D) that permanently remove channels from weight matrices, and dynamic token masks (M^t ∈ {0,1}^N) that cascade sequence reduction at each layer during inference. Parameter pruning is fixed after progressive training, while token pruning remains active at inference time, yielding a model with reduced FLOPs in both dimensions simultaneously.

## Method

CoMP introduces two collaborative modules. The Collaborative Importance Metric (CIM) resolves cross-mode interference: parameter importance (extending Wanda to structured pruning) is reweighted by normalized token importance scores so that redundant tokens cannot dominate the estimate, while token importance (attention-based) is corrected by applying the current parameter pruning mask to the attention matrix before softmax, suppressing the influence of already-pruned heads. The Multi-Mode Pruning Strategy (MPS) decomposes pruning into five modes (vision/language/cross-modal parameter, vision/language token) and at each progressive stage selects the mode with the lowest pruning cost (Δval_acc/ΔFLOPs), augmented by random exploration (probability ρ=0.2) weighted by time-since-last-execution via interval-weighted softmax, and an EMA of historical costs with linearly decaying λ to balance short-term efficiency against long-term stability and avoid convergence to a single suboptimal mode.

## Results

On NLVR2 (BLIP), CoMP achieves 76.08% test accuracy at 0.85 pruning ratio, surpassing the best single-mode baseline MADTP (72.57%) by 3.51% and UPop (62.10%) by 13.98%, while reducing GFLOPs from 132.54 to 20.26. On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, CoMP† attains the highest average score of 69.23 at 0.46 pruning ratio and 66.98 at 0.62, outperforming the strongest training-aware baselines (DART†: 68.80 and 65.92 respectively) while using fewer TFLOPs (2.94 vs. 3.18 and 2.07 vs. 2.38). On image-text retrieval (COCO, 0.7 ratio), CoMP improves R@1 by 2.3% over MADTP for BLIP and 2.5% for CLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMP consistently outperforms the parameter pruning baseline UPop [39] at all pruning ratios ≥ 0.7 on NLVR2, with the gap widening from 2.61% (at 0.7) to 13.98% (at 0.85) in test accuracy, reflecting UPop's inability to handle token redundancy. Against the token pruning baseline MADTP [9], CoMP is comparable at moderate ratios (0.5–0.6) but pulls ahead at 0.7 (+0.59%) and 0.85 (+3.51%), where parameter redundancy becomes critical. The simple joint pruning variant SJP$_{U\to T}$ from Turbo [21] is surpassed by 2.19% at ratio 0.6 and by 1.74% at 0.8, confirming that naive sequential or simultaneous application of both modes without collaboration is suboptimal. On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, CoMP† beats all six token-pruning baselines (FastV [11], SparseVLM [64], PDrop [57], VisionZip [59], DART [51], DivPrune [2]) on average score at both 0.46 and 0.62 ratios, with the largest margin over FastV (10.94% at 0.46) and a consistent 1.06–1.59% advantage over the strongest training-aware competitors (DART†, PDrop†, VisionZip†). CoMP also achieves the lowest TFLOPs among all compared methods at both pruning ratios (2.94 and 2.07), indicating that collaborative multi-mode pruning yields both higher accuracy and greater computational savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the progressive structured pruning paradigm for VLMs (established by UPop [39] for parameters and MADTP [9] for tokens) by unifying both modes into a single collaborative framework. It builds directly on the Wanda importance metric [43] extended to structured pruning following [3], and on attention-based token importance from MADTP [9] and PDrop [57] for LLM-based VLMs. The key departure from prior work is identifying and addressing the intrinsic inconsistency between parameter and token importance metrics when applied jointly, as well as the suboptimality of fixed sequential mode application—issues that prior joint-pruning attempts (Turbo [21], CoreMatching [49]) handled only with rigid, non-adaptive schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are increasingly deployed on edge and mobile devices, effective compression at high pruning ratios (≥0.7) becomes critical, and this paper shows that collaborative multi-mode pruning can recover 3–4% accuracy that single-mode methods lose at those ratios. The empirical finding that the optimal pruning mode shifts from token-dominated (early stages) to parameter-dominated (later stages) provides a useful inductive bias for future compression pipelines. Limitations include the heuristic nature of the cost-based mode selection (requiring a validation set), the restriction to structured pruning, and modest gains at moderate ratios (≤0.6). Open questions include whether the collaborative framework extends to other compression axes (quantization, distillation) and whether the mode-shifting strategy generalizes to architectures with more than two modalities.
