## Problem

Fusing 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction with diffusion-based generation for driving scenes lacks a principled, pixel-level criterion for deciding where, when, and how much to edit. Existing fusion methods rely on coarse view-level heuristics, causing diffusion to overwrite already-correct regions and producing over-restoration and geometric drift under large viewpoint shifts such as lane changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262878/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained street-scene 3DGS model (trained on forward-camera sequences) and laterally offset novel-view camera poses (e.g., 3 m or 6 m lane shifts) as input, requiring no additional priors such as LiDAR, 3D boxes, or HDMap. It produces temporally consistent novel-view video renderings, per-pixel EIG uncertainty maps computed by accumulating Fisher information along each rendering ray, and a progressively updated 3DGS model that has assimilated the generated content. The EIG maps are derived from the Hessian of the negative log-likelihood at the MLE, accumulated over all training views and mapped to pixels via the differentiable rasterizer.

## Method

FaithFusion computes pixel-wise Expected Information Gain via a Laplace approximation of the posterior over 3DGS parameters, where Fisher information is accumulated per-Gaussian during training and projected to pixels through the α-blending renderer. This EIG serves as a unified spatial policy with two roles: on the generation side, it weights a dual-branch EIGent model (a lightweight context encoder cloned from CogVideo-5B-I2V DiT layers plus a DIFIX3D+ cross-attention branch) to suppress editing in low-uncertainty regions and focus restoration on high-uncertainty areas; on the reconstruction side, it acts as a pixel-wise loss weight (λ_EIG) that modulates the novel-view image loss during 3DGS fine-tuning, ensuring the model selectively absorbs generated content only where information gain is highest. The progressive integration alternates between Video DiT (for spatio-temporal coherence) and DIFIX3D+ (for per-frame perceptual refinement) as the trajectory expansion stabilizes, with sparse depth supervision from neighboring-frame point-cloud projections preserving geometric consistency.

## Results

On the Waymo dataset, FaithFusion achieves SOTA performance across all three metrics at both 3 m and 6 m lateral lane shifts without requiring any extra conditions (LiDAR, 3D boxes, or HDMap). At 3 m shift, it reaches NTA-IoU 0.581, NTL-IoU 57.67, and FID 71.51; at the more challenging 6 m shift, it maintains NTA-IoU 0.517, NTL-IoU 55.78, and FID 107.47. The ablation (Table 2) shows the full pipeline reduces FID from 120.24 (DIFIX3D+ baseline) to 107.47 at 6 m, with the largest single gain (−12.77 FID) coming from the progressive EIG-aware 3DGS integration stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaithFusion outperforms all five baselines on NTA-IoU and FID at both 3 m and 6 m lane shifts, while requiring no extra conditions. At 3 m, it edges out the closest competitor ReconDreamer++ (which needs 3D boxes and HDMap) by 0.009 NTA-IoU and 0.51 FID, and surpasses DIFIX3D+ (also condition-free) by 0.003 NTA-IoU and 12.61 FID. At 6 m, the advantage widens: FaithFusion leads DIFIX3D+ by 0.013 NTA-IoU and 12.77 FID, and ReconDreamer++ by 0.028 NTA-IoU and 4.45 FID. The sole exception is NTL-IoU at 6 m, where ReconDreamer++ (56.57) slightly exceeds FaithFusion (55.78), though ReconDreamer++ relies on box and HDMap annotations. FreeVS, which requires LiDAR, trails FaithFusion by 0.016 NTA-IoU and 16.03 FID at 6 m, and OmniRe (the base 3DGS system without any diffusion) is the weakest baseline across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaithFusion sits at the intersection of 3DGS-based driving scene reconstruction (OmniRe, Street Gaussians) and diffusion-prior restoration (DIFIX3D+, ReconDreamer++), but departs from the prevailing view-level heuristic paradigm that governs when and where to invoke diffusion. Its core conceptual move is extending FisherRF's Fisher-information-based uncertainty estimation from the view level to the pixel level via Laplace approximation, and repurposing that pixel-wise EIG as a unified spatial policy that simultaneously weights the diffusion generation branch and the 3DGS reconstruction loss. This replaces the ad-hoc 'render-restore-feedback' triggers used by ReconDreamer and DIFIX3D+ with an information-theoretic criterion, while the dual-branch EIGent architecture (Video DiT for temporal consistency + DIFIX3D+ for per-frame refinement) builds directly on CogVideoX and DIFIX3D+ as pretrained backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, condition-free design (no LiDAR, HDMap, or 3D-box priors) makes FaithFusion broadly applicable to any 3DGS street-scene pipeline, and the EIG-as-loss-weight mechanism offers an interpretable, principled alternative to the heuristic masking strategies common in the field. However, the authors acknowledge that EIG slows but does not eliminate the progressive error accumulation inherent in the render-restore-update loop, suggesting that architectural modifications to 3DGS itself may be needed for further gains. The natural bridge to active mapping and exploration—where EIG is already a standard tool—opens a promising but unexplored direction for autonomous driving world models.
