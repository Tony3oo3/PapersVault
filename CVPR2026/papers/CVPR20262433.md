## Problem

Existing structure compression methods for SfM-based visual localization rely on solving complex optimization problems (NP-hard set cover, integer linear programming, or quadratic programming) to select subsets of 3D points, making them difficult to implement and computationally expensive. This paper asks whether a much simpler, almost trivial strategy—directly selecting points that yield accurate pose estimates—can match or surpass these optimization-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The compression is applied offline to an SfM reconstruction: given the 2D-3D correspondences between database images and 3D points, the method selects a subset of 3D points (each with a single mean descriptor) to form the compressed map. At query time, a query image is matched against the compressed point cloud via direct descriptor comparison (DLoc) or hierarchical retrieval-and-matching (RLoc), and the camera pose is estimated from the resulting 2D-3D correspondences. Descriptors are further compressed via PCA to 16 dimensions with 4-bit scalar quantization (Cambridge/7Scenes) or 32-part product quantization with 10 bits per part (Aachen).

## Method

The core contribution is Trivial Triplet Sampling (TTS): for each database image, randomly sample n_triplets triplets of 2D-3D correspondences, solve P3P for each, and retain only triplets whose estimated pose is within an orientation threshold α of the known SfM pose; the retained triplets are then sorted by position error and their 3D points are added to the selected set until N points per image are covered. Two variants improve compression: Sharing-Based Triplet Selection (STS) scores triplets by how many database images they localize well, preferring widely-shared triplets, and Fine-Grained Point Selection (FPS) prefers triplets containing already-selected points to minimize total point count. The sampling strategy combines uniform and PROSAC-style sampling to balance coverage against the risk of selecting poorly triangulated distant points. Despite its simplicity, TTS outperforms quadratic-programming and greedy set-cover baselines on all three benchmarks, and combined with standard product quantization achieves state-of-the-art compact localization accuracy.

## Results

On the Aachen Day-Night v1.0 dataset (most challenging), TTS with 512 features, N=10, and RLoc achieves 85.1%/92.2%/95.9% recall (day) and 80.6%/87.8%/95.9% (night) at 3.8 MB, outperforming D-PQED (40.75 MB, 86.0% day) and MAD-DR(512) (5.2 MB, 84.6%/92.4%/96.7% day) at a fraction of the memory. On Cambridge Landmarks, TTS (N=1, 16D, 4-bit) uses only 0.02–0.10 MB per scene while achieving 46.6–87.4% recall within 10 cm/1°, surpassing NeuMap (0.3 MB, 31.8% on King's College) and D-PQED (0.43 MB). On 7Scenes, TTS (N=6, 16D, 4-bit) matches D-PQED's 73.0% mean recall at 2.82 MB total, while STS+FPS achieves 70.3% at just 0.89 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the QP structure compression baseline [70] and Greedy Set Cover [61], TTS and all its variants clearly offer a better memory–accuracy trade-off on all three datasets (Fig. 2), establishing a new state-of-the-art for structure compression. On Aachen, TTS (3.8 MB) matches or exceeds D-PQED [56] (40.75 MB) and MAD-DR(512) [107] (5.2 MB) in recall while using 10× and 1.4× less memory respectively; at the fine 25 cm/2° threshold, TTS achieves 85.1% (day) vs. 84.6% for MAD-DR. On Cambridge, TTS (0.02–0.10 MB per scene) substantially outperforms NeuMap [97] (0.3 MB, 31.8% on King's College) and D-PQED (0.43 MB) in both memory and accuracy, and approaches the non-compact HLoc [84,85] and STDLoc [43] baselines at <0.2 MB per scene. On 7Scenes, TTS (2.82 MB, 73.0%) matches D-PQED (1.64 MB, 73.0%) in recall, while STS+FPS (0.89 MB, 70.3%) achieves comparable accuracy at 1.8× less memory. Scene Squeezer [110] (31 MB) is outperformed by TTS at 8× less memory on Aachen. The paper notes that TTS is complementary to D-PQED and MAD-DR, as it can replace their inferior structure compression (QP and Max. Feats.) to further reduce their memory footprints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in SfM point cloud compression for visual localization, where prior methods (QP [70], greedy set cover [61], ILP [35, 79]) formulate compression as solving complex optimization problems with proxy objectives like spatial coverage or descriptor distinctness. Instead, the authors repurpose the P3P pose solver as a selection criterion: triplets of 2D-3D matches that yield accurate poses for database images are retained, directly encoding pose quality into the compression objective. The approach also positions itself against recent learning-based pipelines (D-PQED [56], MAD-DR [107], Scene Squeezer [110]) that jointly learn descriptor and structure compression, showing that standard product quantization combined with the proposed triplet heuristic is competitive or superior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a few lines of code (random triplet sampling + P3P + thresholding) match or beat methods requiring solvers for NP-hard problems, lowering the barrier to deploying compact localization on resource-constrained devices. The paper also demonstrates that the approach composes with existing learned-descriptor pipelines (D-PQED, MAD-DR), offering a drop-in upgrade for structure compression. Open questions include whether triplet-based selection can provide formal guarantees on minimality of the selected point set, and whether the heuristic's reliance on database images as a proxy for query viewpoints degrades in highly novel viewpoints. The authors note that Cambridge Landmarks is 'essentially solved' at under 0.2 MB per scene, suggesting the field should shift focus to harder outdoor benchmarks.
