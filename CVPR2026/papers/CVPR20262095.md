## Problem

Existing slot-attention models for video object-centric learning suffer from severe over-fragmentation, where a single object is split across multiple redundant slots because the reconstruction objective implicitly pressures the model to occupy all available slots. This undermines interpretability, wastes representational capacity, and degrades downstream tasks that expect one slot per object. The paper asks whether over-fragmentation can be prevented a priori by controlling how and when slot capacity is introduced during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled video sequences (up to 76 frames for YouTube-VIS, 24 frames for MOVi) and encodes each frame into patch-level features via a frozen DINOv2 backbone (ViT-B/14 or ViT-S/14). It produces a set of K latent object slots per frame (s ∈ R^{T×K×D}) that are recurrently refined across the temporal axis through slot attention with GRU, along with reconstructed patch features used for the training objective. At inference, a cyclic forward-then-backward pass over the frame sequence yields temporally consistent slot encodings with only ~0.3% additional runtime.

## Method

SlotCurri treats the slot budget as a curriculum variable: training begins with only 2 coarse slots and expands to the full budget over 3 stages, with new slots spawned by duplicating those exhibiting the highest reconstruction error (weighted by their decoding attention maps) and perturbing them with distance-aware Gaussian noise scaled by the parent's nearest-neighbor distance. A 3D SSIM structural loss (computed over 3×3×3 spatio-temporal cubes) complements the pixel-wise MSE to sharpen slot boundaries during the coarse early stages, preventing semantic blurring that would otherwise propagate through subsequent expansions. A cyclic inference strategy—propagating slots forward to the last frame and then backward to the first—aggregates bidirectional context to improve early-frame quality at negligible cost. The full training objective combines MSE reconstruction, slot-slot contrastive loss (from SlotContrast), and the SSIM3D term, all applied on top of a DINOv2 + slot-attention + GRU architecture.

## Results

On the real-world YouTube-VIS benchmark, SlotCurri achieves 44.8 FG-ARI and 35.5 mBO, improving over the prior state-of-the-art SlotContrast by +6.8 FG-ARI and +1.8 mBO. On the synthetic MOVi-C dataset, the method reaches 77.6 FG-ARI (+8.3 over SlotContrast) while maintaining comparable mBO (32.8 vs. 32.7). On MOVi-E, SlotCurri attains 83.7 FG-ARI (+0.8) with a slight mBO trade-off (28.9 vs. 29.2), which the authors attribute to MOVi-E's under-fragmentation challenge rather than over-fragmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SlotContrast (the direct baseline the method builds upon), SlotCurri wins on FG-ARI across all three datasets (+6.8 YouTube-VIS, +8.3 MOVi-C, +0.8 MOVi-E) and on mBO for YouTube-VIS (+1.8) and MOVi-C (+0.1), while slightly losing on MOVi-E mBO (−0.3). Against VideoSAUR/VideoSAURv2, SlotCurri achieves substantially higher FG-ARI on YouTube-VIS (44.8 vs. 28.9/31.2) and MOVi-C (77.6 vs. 64.8), but VideoSAUR retains a higher mBO on MOVi-E (35.6 vs. 28.9), which the authors attribute to VideoSAUR's explicit motion modeling being advantageous for near-rigid synthetic transformations. Against STEVE and SAVi, SlotCurri dominates on all metrics across all datasets. In the targeted over-fragmentation comparison (Table 3, image-level FG-ARI on MOVi), SlotCurri outperforms AdaSlot (81.6 vs. 75.6 on C; 84.9 vs. 76.7 on E) and SOLV (84.9 vs. 80.8 on E), confirming its advantage in preventing fragmentation from the outset rather than post-hoc merging.

## Relation to Prior Work

SlotCurri builds directly on SlotContrast (Manasyan et al., CVPR 2025), adopting its slot-slot contrastive loss and DINOv2 backbone while replacing the fixed slot budget with a reconstruction-guided curriculum that progressively spawns slots in high-error regions. This departs from prior over-fragmentation remedies such as SOLV's post-hoc slot-merging and AdaSlot's dynamic slot count, instead preventing fragmentation a priori by controlling when and where capacity is introduced. The structure-aware SSIM loss draws from image restoration literature to sharpen slot boundaries during the coarse early stages, and the cyclic inference strategy is a novel inference-time mechanism with no training cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Over-fragmentation is a persistent failure mode in video object-centric learning that degrades downstream tasks like tracking, reasoning, and segmentation; SlotCurri offers a practical, training-time solution that requires no architectural changes and adds negligible inference overhead. However, the method's modest gains on MOVi-E reveal a clear limitation: it addresses over-fragmentation but not under-fragmentation of small objects, leaving a gap for scenes with many fine-grained entities. Future directions noted by the authors include scene-adaptive slot schedules and multi-scale slot hierarchies, and the curriculum paradigm could potentially transfer to other slot-based or set-prediction frameworks beyond video.
