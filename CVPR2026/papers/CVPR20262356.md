## Problem

Parallel test-time scaling for multimodal models typically requires training and deploying two separate models—a generator and a verifier—incurs high training and inference costs. Naively unifying them into a single policy introduces two failure modes: severe class imbalance causes verification scores to collapse to uniform values, and aggregating generation and verification rewards enables reward hacking where the model produces incorrect answers with low self-scores to game the objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262356/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a multimodal query (RGB image plus a text question) and produces a structured response containing a thinking trace in <think> tags, a task-specific answer in <answer> tags (a text answer for math, a segmentation region for grounding, or a UI action for agent tasks), and a scalar verification score in [0,1] in <score> tags. At inference, G candidates are sampled via batch decoding and the one with the highest verification score is selected as the final output, replacing the need for a separate judge model.

## Method

ADPO extends GRPO with two mechanisms. First, a preference verification reward replaces the binary correctness-alignment reward with a pairwise ranking objective: within each group of G rollouts, samples are partitioned into contrastive sets by answer quality (positive vs. negative for discrete tasks, or separated by a margin γ for continuous tasks), and the model is rewarded for assigning higher verification scores to better answers, maintaining informative gradients even under severe class imbalance. Second, advantage decoupled optimization computes separate group-normalized advantages for answer rewards and preference rewards, then applies disjoint token-level masks so that answer advantages update only generation tokens while preference advantages update only verification score tokens, preventing gradient interference and eliminating the reward-hacking exploit. The unified objective is a masked sum of two GRPO clipped objectives, one per task, trained end-to-end on a single VLM policy.

## Results

On MathVista, ADPO achieves 62.4% pass@1 accuracy and scales to 64.8%/65.0%/65.3% under best-of-N (N=4/8/12), outperforming GRPO with majority voting by +1.4/+2.1/+1.9 points. On MMMU (OOD), ADPO reaches 50.8%/52.1%/52.3% at N=4/8/12, surpassing GRPO by +1.4/+1.0/+0.6 points. On ReasonSeg, ADPO attains 61.1/61.2/61.6 cIoU at N=4/8/12 (+1.7/+1.6/+2.2 over GRPO majority voting), and on AndroidControl/GUI Odyssey it achieves 72.7/72.7/72.9 and 81.6/81.7/81.4 step success rate, exceeding GRPO by +1.7/+1.9/+1.8 and +0.3/+0.2/+0.3 respectively. ADPO also reduces inference latency by 53.5% compared to a separate GRPO-as-judge setup (2.6s vs 5.6s per sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (the primary RL baseline), ADPO matches or slightly exceeds pass@1 accuracy (62.4% vs 62.2% on MathVista, 70.9% vs 71.0% SR on AndroidControl) while delivering consistent best-of-N gains of +1.4 to +2.1 points on MathVista, +1.6 to +2.2 cIoU on ReasonSeg, and +1.7 to +1.9 SR on AndroidControl. Against the separate generator-verifier system MM-Verifier [33], ADPO outperforms by +5.0/+2.5/+1.2 points on MathVista at N=4/8/12 while using a single unified model instead of two. On ReasonSeg, ADPO surpasses LISA-7B [11] (48.8 cIoU), SegLLM [37] (48.4 cIoU), Seg-Zero-7B [18] (52.0 cIoU), and VLM-R1 [32] (63.1 ACC) at pass@1, and extends the gap under best-of-N. On mobile agent benchmarks, ADPO exceeds UI-TARS-7B [29] (72.5% SR on AndroidControl, 67.9% on GUI Odyssey), SpiritSight-8B [10] (68.1%/75.8%), and AgentCPM-GUI-8B [49] (69.2%/75.0%) at pass@1, with further margins under best-of-N. R1-VL [46] reports 63.5% on MathVista pass@1, slightly above ADPO's 62.4%, but ADPO's best-of-N (65.3% at N=12) demonstrates superior test-time scaling capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) into a unified framework that jointly trains answer generation and self-verification within a single policy, departing from the dominant parallel test-time scaling paradigm that deploys separate generator and verifier models (e.g., MM-Verifier). The two key conceptual departures are: (1) replacing the binary verification reward with a preference-based ranking reward to handle the class imbalance that arises as the model improves, and (2) decoupling advantage computation with token-level masks to prevent reward hacking when generation and verification objectives are naively combined. The work sits at the intersection of RL-based multimodal reasoning (R1-VL, VLM-R1, Visual-RFT) and generative verifier research, but is the first to address the joint optimization conflict between these two objectives within a single GRPO-trained policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified generator-verifier approach eliminates the need for a second model at inference, reducing latency by ~53% compared to a separate judge while matching or exceeding its accuracy — a practically significant efficiency gain for deployment. The class imbalance collapse (verification scores converging to 1 within 17 steps) and the reward hacking failure mode are well-characterized and likely to inform future work on self-verification in RL-trained models. Open questions include whether the preference reward and decoupled advantage mechanism generalize to larger model scales, longer reasoning chains, or domains where answer quality is harder to define, and whether the modest 1–3% accuracy gains over GRPO+majority voting will widen with stronger base models or more diverse task distributions.
