## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving methods require expensive fine-grained annotations (3D bounding boxes, semantic segmentation) and suffer from a severe long-tail distribution where most collected data is trivial (e.g., driving straight) while only a small fraction is safety-critical. No prior active learning strategy has been designed specifically for the planning-oriented E2E-AD paradigm, which involves multi-modal inputs and trajectory-level optimization rather than simple image classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261903/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates at the clip level (20-second sequences from 6 cameras at 2 Hz keyframe frequency on nuScenes). For initial selection, it consumes nearly free metadata—weather, lighting, driving commands, and average speed—without any learned features. For iterative selection, it runs the trained VAD-Tiny model on unlabeled clips and computes three criteria (Displacement Error, Soft Collision, Agent Uncertainty) purely from model inference outputs, requiring no ground-truth annotations. The final output is a ranked set of clip indices to annotate, and the trained model produces a planned ego trajectory over a 3-second horizon.

## Method

ActiveAD consists of two phases. First, Ego-Diversity initialization stratifies the dataset into a two-level tree (weather/lighting × driving command) and allocates the initial annotation budget using a power-law proportion P ∝ n^γ (γ<1 oversamples minority classes like Night-Rainy or Overtake), then selects clips at regular intervals within each stratum sorted by speed. Second, iterative active selection scores unlabeled clips with a weighted sum of three planning-oriented criteria: Displacement Error (mean L2 distance between predicted and recorded ego trajectories), Soft Collision (sum over frames of exp(−min distance between predicted ego and agent trajectories), serving as a continuous proxy for collision risk), and Agent Uncertainty (distance-weighted entropy of multi-mode trajectory predictions for nearby agents). The top-n clips by overall loss are annotated each round, and the model is retrained from scratch. Using only 30% of nuScenes data, ActiveAD matches or slightly exceeds VAD-Tiny trained on 100% data in both open-loop L2/Collision Rate and closed-loop CARLA Drive Score/Route Completion.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop evaluation with VAD-Tiny as the base model, ActiveAD with only 30% annotated data achieves an average L2 displacement error of 0.68 m and a collision rate of 0.21%, surpassing the full 100% data baseline (0.70 m / 0.25%). At the 20% budget, ActiveAD reaches 0.76 m L2 and 0.39% collision rate, outperforming all general active learning baselines (best competitor: Coreset at 0.81 m / 0.43%). In closed-loop CARLA evaluation, ActiveAD with 30% data achieves a Drive Score of 63.24 and Route Completion of 88.04 on Town05 Short, matching the full-data result (63.11 / 88.90), and reaches 31.77 DS / 76.44 RC on Town05 Long, nearly matching full data (33.24 / 76.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActiveAD consistently outperforms all five general active learning baselines (Coreset, VAAL, KECOR, ActiveFT, and Random) across every budget setting on both L2 displacement error and collision rate. At the 30% budget, ActiveAD achieves 0.68 m L2 and 0.21% collision rate, beating the strongest baseline Coreset (0.73 m / 0.54%) by 0.05 m in L2 and 0.33 percentage points in collision rate. VAAL and KECOR perform worst among the active baselines at 30% (0.81 m and 0.82 m L2 respectively), showing that task-agnostic or detection-oriented selection criteria transfer poorly to planning. Notably, ActiveAD at 30% data surpasses the VAD-Tiny model trained on 100% data (0.70 m / 0.25%), suggesting that the long-tail distribution in nuScenes means a large fraction of samples are trivial and actively harmful. In closed-loop CARLA, ActiveAD at 30% matches or exceeds full-data performance on Town05 Short (DS 63.24 vs 63.11) and approaches it on Town05 Long (DS 31.77 vs 33.24), while all baselines lag substantially (best baseline Coreset: DS 58.87 / 22.19).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends active learning from perception sub-tasks (3D object detection, semantic segmentation) to the full end-to-end autonomous driving planning pipeline, where prior work has not addressed data selection. It builds on the planning-oriented philosophy of UniAD and VAD as base architectures, but departs from traditional active learning by replacing random initialization with an Ego-Diversity strategy that exploits free metadata (weather, lighting, speed, driving commands) and by designing planning-specific uncertainty metrics (Displacement Error, Soft Collision, Agent Uncertainty) rather than relying on classification-style predictive entropy. The Soft Collision criterion repurposes multi-mode trajectory prediction uncertainty from motion prediction literature into a continuous collision-risk proxy, and the overall framework treats clips (not individual frames) as the unit of selection, consistent with current AD annotation practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 30% carefully selected data matches or marginally exceeds 100% data performance challenges the prevailing assumption in E2E-AD that scaling annotation volume is necessary for better planning, with direct cost implications for industry deployment. However, the evidence is limited to a single dataset (nuScenes) and a single base model (VAD-Tiny), and the closed-loop CARLA results show the 30% model still trails the full-data model on Town05 Long (DS 31.77 vs 33.24), so the 'comparable or better' claim is dataset-dependent. Open questions include whether the metrics generalize to other E2E-AD architectures (e.g., those without explicit multi-mode prediction), whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse datasets, and how the selection strategy interacts with model capacity and training duration.
