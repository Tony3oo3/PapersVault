## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to place objects in the correct spatial relations specified by the prompt, yet the internal mechanisms responsible for this failure remain uncharacterized. Prior work has proposed various fixes (layout conditioning, attention guidance, curriculum learning) or attributed the failure to specific stages (cross-attention or text encoding), but no study has reverse-engineered the actual attention circuits that implement spatial relation generation in a DiT, nor examined how these circuits depend on the choice of text encoder.

## Input / Output

The model takes a structured text prompt of the form '[descriptor A] [object A] [relation] [descriptor B] [object B]' (e.g., 'red square above and to the left of blue circle'), where objects are drawn from 3 shapes × 2 colors and relations from 8 spatial configurations. It produces a latent image (decoded via the Stable Diffusion VAE) containing two distinct objects on a gray background, sampled with DPM-Solver++ (14 steps) and classifier-free guidance of 4.5. Evaluation uses cv2-based segmentation and classification to independently score color presence, shape presence, shape-color binding, and spatial relation correctness as binary per-sample metrics.

## Method

The paper trains PixArt-style DiT models (DiT-B through DiT-nano) from scratch on a minimal synthetic two-object dataset, comparing three text encoders: T5-XXL, a random token encoder (RTE) with sinusoidal positional encoding, and RTE without positional encoding. The core analytical tool is an 'Attention Synopsis' method that aggregates cross-attention maps over token categories (relation words, shape words, object image regions) and diffusion timesteps, reducing tens of millions of attention maps to interpretable layer×head heatmaps for circuit discovery. With RTE, the model implements a two-stage circuit: a spatial relation head (L2H8) uses QK interaction between sinusoidal positional embeddings and relation-word embeddings to write spatial gradient tags onto image tokens, and a downstream object generation head (L4H3) reads shape tokens at those tagged locations. With T5, the circuit is fundamentally different: T5's self-attention fuses relational information into the contextual embedding of the second shape token, so the DiT decodes both relation and object identity from a single token via a relation head (L3H7) that interacts with the relation factor within that embedding. Causal validation is performed through head-specific ablation, VO-injection manipulation, and targeted vector arithmetic in the T5 embedding space, revealing that the T5 circuit is brittle to filler-word perturbations while the RTE circuit is robust.

## Results

Both RTE-DiT and T5-DiT (DiT-B, 12L/12H/768-d) achieve high in-domain accuracy on all four metrics (color, shape, unique_binding, spatial_relation), with accuracy gains saturating at the DiT-mini scale. Ablating the spatial relation head (L2H8) in RTE-DiT drops relational accuracy from 67% to 33%, while ablating the object generation head (L4H3) reduces shape accuracy from 90% to 76%. In T5-DiT, masking the shape2 token lowers all shape, binding, and relation accuracies by ~50%, and masking shape1 reduces relation accuracy by 15%. The smallest model (DiT-nano, 3L/3H/192-d) fails to develop a spatial relation head and achieves only 5% spatial relation accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key contrasts are between text-encoder configurations and model scales. RTE-DiT and T5-DiT achieve comparable in-domain spatial relation accuracy, but their circuits differ fundamentally: RTE-DiT uses a two-stage modular circuit (spatial relation head L2H8 writes positional tags, then object generation head L4H3 reads tags to place objects), while T5-DiT decodes both relation and object identity from the contextual embedding of a single shape2 token. On robustness, the two mechanisms diverge sharply: T5-DiT's relation accuracy collapses when a filler word (e.g., 'the') is inserted before the object, whereas RTE-DiT remains stable to such perturbations but is highly sensitive to ablation of the explicit relation word. RTE without positional encoding performs significantly worse on binding and relation metrics, confirming that positional cues are necessary for non-commutative relation generation. Across model sizes, the spatial relation head consistently emerges in DiT-B, mini, and micro, but is absent in DiT-nano (5% relation accuracy), indicating a minimum capacity threshold for relational circuit formation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability toolkit (particularly the QK circuit framework of Elhage et al.) to the multi-object spatial relation generation problem in diffusion transformers, going beyond prior single-object composition studies [23, 24]. It unites two previously separate lines of work on T2I spatial failures: cross-attention manipulation methods (Attend-and-Excite, layout conditioning) and text-encoder limitation analyses (Compass, CLIP encoding studies), showing that the text encoder choice determines which circuit the DiT implements. The Attention Synopsis method is a new scalable tool for summarizing the massive cross-attention tensor in iterative diffusion sampling, addressing a practical obstacle that has complicated circuit analysis in diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that T5-based DiTs decode all relational and object information from a single contextual token (shape2) and consequently collapse in accuracy with a single filler word insertion, while RTE-based DiTs with a modularized two-head circuit remain robust, has direct implications for real-world T2I model design: embedding model improvements may be more critical than DiT architectural modifications. The work also provides a systematic, reproducible methodology (Attention Synopsis + weight-space head screening) that can be applied to larger pretrained models, as demonstrated on PixArt-Sigma. Open questions include whether these circuit findings generalize to natural-language prompts with more than two objects, and whether the brittleness of T5-based circuits can be mitigated without sacrificing the linguistic generalization that pretrained encoders provide.
