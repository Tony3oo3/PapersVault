## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for NeRFs cannot separately capture both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties in a single forward pass: likelihood-based models (e.g., NeRF-W) only model aleatoric uncertainty, Bayesian methods require multiple stochastic forward passes, and ensembles require training and storing multiple networks. Additionally, prior UQ benchmarks for NeRFs lack standardization in data splits, architectures, and training protocols, making fair comparison of methods difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260530/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse RGB training views with known camera poses (3 views for LLFF, standard splits for LF and RobustNeRF) and, in a single forward pass, outputs a rendered novel view image, a per-pixel aleatoric uncertainty map, a per-pixel epistemic uncertainty map, and volume densities. The three RGB channels share a common uncertainty estimate (broadcast from a single uncertainty head), reflecting the high correlation of variance across color channels. The model architecture is a standard nerfacto MLP with three additional output neurons in the final layer for the uncertainty and shape-score predictions.

## Method

Evidential NeRF treats the conditional mean and variance of each point's radiance as random variables governed by a higher-order normal-inverse-gamma (NIG) evidential distribution, rather than fixed point estimates. The network directly predicts point-level mean color, aleatoric uncertainty, epistemic uncertainty, and a positive shape score (via softplus), which are propagated to the pixel level as weighted sums using squared volumetric rendering weights under a point-independence assumption. The pixel-level NIG parameters are then reformulated from the rendered uncertainties, and the pixel color marginally follows a Student's t distribution. Training minimizes the negative log-likelihood of this t-distribution plus a regularizer that penalizes high evidence (large 2ν+α) when the prediction is inaccurate, preventing overconfident wrong predictions. This design avoids the key incompatibility of standard evidential deep learning with NeRFs—where supervision is only available at the pixel level after volumetric rendering—by predicting uncertainties directly and recovering the evidential distribution post-hoc.

## Results

On Light Field, Evidential NeRF achieves the best PSNR (29.97 dB), SSIM (0.9345), and LPIPS (0.0359), surpassing the strongest ensemble baselines (29.38 dB, 0.9308, 0.0411) while requiring only a single forward pass. On RobustNeRF, it again leads in all three reconstruction metrics (26.23 dB, 0.8641, 0.1112) and achieves the best NLL (−1.27). On LLFF (sparse-view, 3 training images), it is within 0.04 dB of the ensemble PSNR (17.88 vs. 17.92) but achieves the best LPIPS (0.3751) and the best NLL (0.68 vs. 11.17 for ensembles). Inference speed is 4.67 FPS, only 0.21 FPS below the baseline nerfacto (4.88 FPS) and roughly 5× faster than ensembles (0.96 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the **baseline nerfacto** (no UQ), Evidential NeRF improves PSNR on all three datasets (e.g., +1.42 dB on LF, +1.01 dB on RobustNeRF) while adding uncertainty outputs at negligible speed cost (4.67 vs. 4.88 FPS). Against **Monte Carlo Dropout** [7], Evidential NeRF dominates on every metric: Dropout degrades PSNR by 0.4–1.0 dB and yields NLL values 1–2 orders of magnitude worse (e.g., 90.68 vs. 0.68 on LLFF), while also being ~50× slower at inference (0.09 vs. 4.67 FPS). Against the **Normal** likelihood model [18], Evidential NeRF improves PSNR by 1.9–2.0 dB on LLFF and RobustNeRF and reduces NLL by factors of 18–80×, confirming that random mean/variance captures epistemic uncertainty that a fixed-variance Gaussian cannot. Against **MoL** (mixture of Laplace) [36], Evidential NeRF wins on all reconstruction metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all datasets; MoL retains a slight NLL edge on LF (−2.54 vs. −2.45) and RobustNeRF (−1.39 vs. −1.27) due to its multimodal assumption, but Evidential NeRF is far more competitive on AUSE. Against **Deep Ensembles** [22] and **DANE** [41], Evidential NeRF matches or exceeds PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on 7 of 9 reconstruction metrics (losing only PSNR and SSIM on LLFF by 0.04 dB and 0.004), while being ~13× faster in training (13.6 vs. 59.2 min) and ~5× faster at inference (4.67 vs. 0.96 FPS); ensembles retain a modest AUSE advantage (e.g., AUSE-RMSE 0.0164 vs. 0.0221 on RobustNeRF), reflecting their ability to capture inter-model variability that a single evidential network approximates less precisely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts evidential deep learning (EDL) from the classification/regression literature (Sensoy et al., Amini et al.) to the hierarchical volumetric rendering paradigm of NeRFs, departing from standard EDL by having the network predict aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties directly rather than regressing NIG distribution parameters. It extends the normal-distribution-based NeRF UQ line (NeRF-W, ActiveNeRF, Kendall & Gal) by treating the conditional mean and variance themselves as random variables governed by a higher-order NIG, thereby capturing epistemic uncertainty that fixed-parameter Gaussians structurally cannot. The paper also positions itself against Bayesian (MC dropout, S-NeRF, CF-NeRF) and ensemble (Deep Ensembles, DANE) approaches, which either require multiple forward passes or multiple trained models, and introduces a standardized benchmark to isolate UQ method effects from architectural and training confounders that plagued prior comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first NeRF UQ method to jointly quantify both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties in a single forward pass at near-baseline inference speed, making it practical for safety-critical deployments (autonomous driving, medical imaging, robotics) where ensembles are infeasible. The standardized benchmark is a valuable community resource that enables fair comparison of UQ methods previously evaluated under inconsistent protocols. A key limitation is the assumption of deterministic volumetric density, leaving spatial/geometric uncertainty unmodeled; extending the evidential formulation to the density channel would be a natural next step. The demonstrated applications (AU-based floater removal, EU-guided active view selection) suggest broader utility beyond benchmark metrics.
