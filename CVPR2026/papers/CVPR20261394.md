## Problem

Existing source-free domain adaptation methods assign pseudo labels using simple distance metrics in the embedding space without modeling its underlying geometric structure, leading to unreliable distance estimates and persistent mislabeling under domain shift. Additionally, standard consistency regularization operates solely on output logits, making it insensitive to feature-level reliability and prone to confirming noisy pseudo labels through confirmation bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261394/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained source model (encoder g and classifier f) and unlabeled target-domain RGB images as input, with no access to source data. It produces an adapted model that outputs class labels for target samples. During training, each target image is processed through weak and strong augmentations, and the feature encoder produces D-dimensional embeddings that are organized into a k-NN graph augmented with virtual features sampled from class-wise Gaussian distributions.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part framework: (1) Universe-aware pseudo labeling, which models the target embedding space as class-wise Gaussian distributions, samples virtual features to densify sparse regions, constructs a cosine k-NN graph (the 'feature universe'), and propagates pseudo labels from dynamically selected reliable regions (via Mahalanobis distance quantile scheduling) through graph traversal up to a maximum hop depth; and (2) Gravity consistency (GV), a novel regularization loss that multiplies the cosine similarity between weakly and strongly augmented features by the KL divergence between their logits, adaptively modulating consistency strength so that structurally coherent samples receive stronger regularization while uncertain or out-of-distribution samples are suppressed. The full training objective combines volume-aware cross-entropy (weighted by normalized perceptual manifold volume per class), GV, and information maximization losses.

## Results

On Office-Home (ResNet50), the full method (UP + GV) achieves 74.4% average accuracy across 12 adaptation tasks, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art R-SFDA (74.1%) by +0.3%. On DomainNet-126 (ResNet50), it reaches 73.6% average accuracy, surpassing the best prior method (GKD at 68.7%) by +4.9%. On VisDA-C (ResNet101), it attains 87.7% average accuracy, competitive with TPDS (87.7%) and R-SFDA (89.1%). Additionally, adding GV alone to existing baselines yields substantial gains: SHOT w/GV improves by +1.1% (Office-Home), +4.7% (VisDA-C), and +5.1% (DomainNet-126) on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Office-Home, the full method (74.4% avg.) beats SHOT (71.9%) by +2.5%, GKD (72.4%) by +2.0%, TPDS (73.1%) by +1.3%, and R-SFDA (74.1%) by +0.3%. On DomainNet-126, the gap widens substantially: Ours (73.6%) surpasses SHOT (68.1%) by +5.5%, GKD (68.7%) by +4.9%, and TPDS (67.1%) by +6.5%. On VisDA-C, Ours (87.7%) matches TPDS (87.7%) but trails R-SFDA (89.1%) by 1.4%, though the authors attribute this to GPU memory constraints limiting the full potential of their virtual feature sampling. Notably, GV as a plug-in module consistently improves all baselines: SHOT w/GV gains +1.1% (Office-Home) and +5.1% (DomainNet-126), GKD w/GV gains +0.6% and +4.8%, and TPDS w/GV gains +0.4% and +1.1%, demonstrating GV's generality across diverse SFDA frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) line initiated by SHOT and GKD, which rely on distance-based pseudo labeling in the target embedding space, by replacing direct Euclidean/cosine distance comparisons with a topology-aware 'feature universe' constructed from class-wise Gaussian virtual features and a cosine k-NN graph. It also departs from standard logit-only consistency regularization (as in AdaContrast, Tang et al.) by introducing gravity consistency, which reweights KL-based logit agreement by weak-strong feature similarity. The volume-aware class weighting borrows the normalized perceptual manifold volume formulation from Ma et al. (CVPR 2023), repurposing it for class-balanced pseudo supervision in the SFDA setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of gravity consistency (GV) is practically valuable: it yields consistent gains (+0.4% to +5.0%) when added to diverse existing SFDA baselines (SHOT, GKD, TPDS, UCON) without architectural changes, making it an easy drop-in improvement for practitioners. However, the full framework's gains over the strongest SOTA (R-SFDA, UCON) are modest (+0.3% on Office-Home), and the computational overhead of virtual feature sampling and Mahalanobis scoring limits scalability, as acknowledged by the authors' suboptimal VisDA-C results due to GPU memory constraints. Open questions include whether more principled (non-random) virtual feature sampling could reduce early-training variance, and whether the topology-aware pseudo labeling generalizes to non-image modalities or open-set settings where class-conditional Gaussians may not hold.
