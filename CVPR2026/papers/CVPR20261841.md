## Problem

Current vision-language-action (VLA) models struggle with long-horizon, multi-step robotic manipulation because they lack structured affordance cues to disambiguate which object, region, or motion is relevant at each step, and they have no mechanism for tracking progress within a subtask. This absence of temporal grounding causes characteristic failure modes—repeated actions, skipped subtasks, and premature termination—when policies must execute sequences of heterogeneous subtasks.

## Input / Output

PALM takes three synchronized inputs: a natural-language instruction, an RGB image observation, and a proprioceptive robot state vector. These are encoded by frozen CLIP, MAE, and MLP encoders respectively, then fused into a single token sequence. The model outputs a horizon-n action trajectory (joint-space commands) jointly with a sequence of scalar progress values in [0,1], one per step, generated by a denoising diffusion transformer. An intermediate affordance latent at a future offset t+n is predicted internally but is not part of the final output.

## Method

PALM introduces two tightly coupled modules on a GPT-2-style transformer backbone. First, four learnable affordance sub-queries (<Global>, <Local>, <Spatial>, <Dynamic>) attend over the fused multimodal context to predict a structured future affordance latent at offset t+n, supervised by object masks (Grounding DINO + SAM), contact heatmaps (GLOVER++), placement points (SpatialVLM + RoboPoint), and motion regions (CoTracker). Second, an action-progress query conditions on this affordance latent and feeds a denoising diffusion transformer (DiT) that jointly decodes the n-step action sequence and a continuous within-subtask progress scalar. Training proceeds in two stages: large-scale pre-training on robot data (DROID, BridgeDataV2) and long-horizon video data (EPIC-KITCHENS, RoboCerebra) to learn affordance and progress priors, followed by fine-tuning on 942 human-annotated robot trajectories with affordance and progress labels.

## Results

On the CALVIN ABC→D long-horizon benchmark, PALM achieves an average length of 4.48 (82.0% success on 5 consecutive subtasks), a 12.5% improvement over the prior best (Seer at 3.98). On LIBERO, PALM reaches 94.5% average success across four suites, with 91.8% on LIBERO-LONG — a 22.8% absolute gain over the strongest baseline (CoT-VLA at 69.0%). In real-world long-horizon pick-and-place tasks, PALM achieves 2× the average completed subtask length of OpenVLA and Octo across three generalization settings (random localization, visual distraction, unseen lighting).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PALM outperforms all 16 baselines on both simulation benchmarks. On CALVIN ABC→D, the closest competitor is Seer [112] (prediction-based, avg. len. 3.98), which PALM surpasses by 0.50 in average length and by 17.7 points on the 5-task success rate (82.0% vs. 64.3%); π₀ [8] (diffusion-based, 3.92) and RoboUniview [79] (3D-aware, 3.65) trail further behind. On LIBERO-LONG, PALM's 91.8% success rate exceeds CoT-VLA [148] (69.0%) by 22.8 points, OpenVLA [55] (53.7%) by 38.1 points, and Diffusion Policy [19] (50.5%) by 41.3 points. In real-world experiments, PALM completes 3.05–3.80 subtasks on average versus 0.95–1.60 for OpenVLA [55] and 0.65–1.05 for Octo [111], representing roughly a 2× improvement across all three generalization conditions. Autoregressive baselines (RT-1, Robo-Flamingo, OpenVLA) and 3D-aware methods (3D-VLA, 3D Diffuser Actor) show the weakest long-horizon performance, with average lengths below 3.3 on CALVIN, confirming that direct action prediction without explicit affordance or progress reasoning degrades rapidly over multi-step sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

PALM extends the inverse-dynamics VLA lineage (Seer, VILLA-X, Video Prediction Policy) by replacing dense future-image prediction with a compact set of structured affordance latents (global, local, spatial, dynamic) produced by learnable queries over a GPT-2-style backbone. It also departs from prior affordance-in-robotics work (GLOVER++, RT-Affordance, CoA-VLA) that treats affordances as auxiliary visual outputs or reasoning-chain tokens, instead embedding them as conditioning signals for a diffusion transformer that jointly decodes actions and a continuous progress scalar. The progress-aware formulation draws on imitation-learning progress supervision but is novel in appending a per-step scalar to the action vector within a single diffusion head rather than using it as a separate reward or reweighting signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22.8-point gap over the strongest prior method on LIBERO-LONG and the consistent real-world gains (≈2× average subtask completion) demonstrate that structured intermediate reasoning plus temporal grounding can substantially close the long-horizon manipulation gap without hierarchical planners or explicit task decomposition. The ablation revealing that affordance foresight is most critical during fine-tuning while progress prediction is most critical during pre-training suggests a complementary division of labor that could guide future multi-stage training recipes. Open questions include whether the four-way affordance decomposition is optimal, how the approach scales to bimanual or mobile manipulation, and whether the progress signal can be learned without the semi-automated annotation pipeline used here.
