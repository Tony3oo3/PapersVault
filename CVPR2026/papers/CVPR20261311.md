## Problem

Existing point tracking methods operate exclusively in single-view videos, where geometric ambiguities such as occlusion, erratic motion, and depth uncertainty limit reliability. Naively applying a 2D tracker independently to each viewpoint in a multi-view system discards the complementary cues that other views provide, and prior multi-view approaches either require depth inputs or operate in 3D world space, leaving no established paradigm for pixel-space multi-view point tracking in dynamic scenes.

## Input / Output

The model receives temporally synchronized multi-view video frames at 384×512 resolution, a set of N query points defined independently per view as (t_q, x_q, y_q) tuples (since visible time steps differ across views), and camera parameters consisting of a shared intrinsic matrix K and per-view per-frame extrinsics (R, t). It outputs 2D pixel trajectories of shape V×T×N×2 and per-point binary occlusion states of shape V×T×N×1, covering all views and all time steps simultaneously. Although trained with 1–4 views, the attention-based architecture generalizes to an arbitrary number of views at test time.

## Method

MV-TAP builds on the CoTracker3 backbone, computing local 4D correlation volumes along the temporal axis only (cross-view correlations are excluded because appearance similarity degrades with large inter-view baselines). Two key additions enable multi-view reasoning: (1) a camera encoding module that converts each query point's ray into Plücker coordinates (direction and moment vector) and projects them via an MLP to inject explicit geometric context, and (2) a view-attention module that, alongside temporal and spatial attention, allows the Transformer to exchange information across viewpoints at each refinement step. The architecture iteratively refines trajectories and occlusion states over M=4 steps by predicting incremental updates. Training uses a synthetic Kubric-generated dataset of 5,000 multi-view scenes with Huber loss on trajectories and BCE on occlusion, both geometrically weighted across refinement steps; the feature encoder is frozen and initialized from CoTracker3 pretrained weights while all other parameters are updated.

## Results

On the 8-view evaluation protocol, MV-TAP achieves AJ 44.2 / δxavg 61.9 / OA 78.3 on DexYCB, AJ 40.3 / δxavg 62.8 / OA 73.1 on Panoptic Studio, AJ 87.8 / δxavg 94.0 / OA 96.3 on Kubric, and AJ 42.6 / δxavg 74.9 / OA 65.8 on Harmony4D. These results consistently outperform the strongest single-view baseline CoTracker3 (e.g., +2.7 AJ on DexYCB, +1.2 δxavg on Harmony4D) and the multi-view 3D tracker MVTracker (e.g., +13.5 δxavg on DexYCB, +61.6 δxavg on Harmony4D). The naive multi-view extensions (CoTracker3+Flat. and CoTracker3+Tri.) degrade substantially, confirming that simply flattening views or applying triangulation is insufficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest single-view tracker CoTracker3, MV-TAP improves AJ by +2.7 (DexYCB), +0.7 (Panoptic), +4.3 (Kubric), and +1.2 (Harmony4D), while also gaining 1.3–3.4 points on δxavg across all four datasets. The 3D world-space tracker TAPIP3D (which requires ground-truth depth) slightly edges out MV-TAP on Panoptic Studio AJ (41.9 vs 40.3) and Kubric δxavg (95.7 vs 94.0), but collapses on Harmony4D (AJ 5.0 vs 42.6) due to depth estimation errors in in-the-wild scenes. TAPNext, a heavier SSM+ViT architecture, marginally beats MV-TAP on Harmony4D AJ (42.7 vs 42.6) and OA (68.4 vs 65.8) but is outperformed on δxavg (74.6 vs 74.9) and on all other datasets. The multi-view 3D tracker MVTracker, which also requires depth, is substantially outperformed by MV-TAP on δxavg (e.g., 48.4 vs 61.9 on DexYCB, 13.3 vs 74.9 on Harmony4D). The naive multi-view extensions—flattening views into a single sequence (CoTracker3+Flat.) and triangulation-based reprojection (CoTracker3+Tri.)—both fail: the former collapses to near-random performance (AJ < 5), while the latter offers only marginal gains over the single-view baseline, confirming that explicit cross-view attention is necessary for effective multi-view tracking.

## Relation to Prior Work

MV-TAP builds directly on CoTracker3 as its 2D tracking backbone, extending it from single-view to multi-view by adding a cross-view attention module and Plücker-coordinate camera encoding. It deliberately departs from the 3D world-space formulation of MVTracker, which requires depth input and suffers from reprojection errors, instead operating entirely in 2D pixel space. The paper also positions itself against multi-view matching methods (SIFT, SuperGlue, LoFTR), arguing that those are ill-suited for dynamic tracking because they assume static geometry or ignore temporal consistency. The local 4D correlation volume is inherited from the CoTracker/LocoTrack lineage, but is restricted to the temporal axis to avoid unreliable cross-view appearance matching at large baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper establishes multi-view 2D point tracking as a distinct task with a principled formulation, a large-scale synthetic training set (5,000 Kubric scenes), and four real-world evaluation benchmarks, filling a gap where no prior method addressed pixel-space multi-view tracking without depth. The consistent but modest gains over CoTracker3 (≈2–3 AJ points) and the clear advantage over depth-dependent 3D trackers like MVTracker suggest that cross-view attention in pixel space is a practical and robust route. Open questions include scalability to very large view counts, real-time inference for robotics, and whether the approach transfers to asynchronous or sparse-view settings. The reliance on a synthetic training set also raises questions about domain gap to truly in-the-wild multi-view deployments.
