## Problem

Existing 3D visual pre-training methods for robotic manipulation suffer from two critical gaps: they ignore the sequential, state-action-state dynamics inherent to manipulation (relying on single-frame supervision), and they overemphasize holistic geometric reconstruction that captures background details irrelevant to control. The paper asks whether a scalable 3D pre-training framework can learn dynamics-aware, manipulation-relevant representations entirely in latent space, without explicit action labels or point-cloud reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261674/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

AFRO consumes unlabeled sequences of consecutive point clouds (P_t, P_{t+k} with default interval k=4) from robot manipulation demonstrations, encoded by a PointTransformer backbone into fixed-dimensional feature vectors. It produces dynamics-aware latent features (z_t) that are frozen and plugged into a Diffusion Policy for downstream control, along with 16-dimensional latent action vectors inferred from feature displacements. No action annotations, reconstruction targets, or pixel-level supervision are required at any stage; the only supervision signal is the VICReg matching loss between student predictions and an EMA teacher encoder.

## Method

AFRO jointly trains an inverse dynamics model (IDM) and a diffusion-based forward dynamics model (FDM) in a shared latent space. To prevent feature leakage, the IDM receives the feature difference (z_{t+k} − z_t) rather than raw feature pairs, forcing it to encode only what changed; an inverse-consistency term additionally requires the same pipeline run in reverse (z_t − z_{t+k} → α_{t+k→t}) so that latent actions must support both forward prediction and backward explanation. The FDM is a 4-layer DiT with AdaLN-Zero conditioning on (z_t, α, τ) that performs one-shot denoising of a noised future feature, explicitly modeling multimodal future uncertainty instead of collapsing to a single averaged prediction. The encoder is trained with a VICReg loss (invariance + variance + covariance terms) against an EMA-updated target encoder, and the entire framework operates purely in feature space with no geometric reconstruction objective.

## Results

On the MetaWorld benchmark (14 tasks), AFRO achieves a mean success rate of 76.0%, surpassing the strongest end-to-end baseline DP3 (69.7%) by +6.3 points and the best dynamic-aware 3D pre-training method DynaMo-3D (64.9%) by +11.1 points. On Adroit (Door + Pen), AFRO reaches 83.0% mean success, outperforming DINOv2 (80.0%) and DP3 (75.0%). In real-world experiments on a Franka arm, AFRO attains 70% mean success in-domain and 84% after large-scale out-of-domain pre-training on RH20T, consistently leading all baselines by 14–38 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFRO outperforms all eight baselines on both simulation benchmarks. Against 2D large-scale pre-training (CLIP, DINOv2), AFRO gains +50.1 and +50.1 points on MetaWorld mean, confirming that 3D point-cloud dynamics modeling is critical for manipulation. Among 3D static pre-training methods (PointMAE, PointDif), AFRO leads by +12.1 and +14.9 points on MetaWorld, showing that temporal dynamics capture what reconstruction-only objectives miss. Against dynamic-aware 3D baselines, AFRO surpasses DynaMo-3D by +11.1 points on MetaWorld and +8.5 on Adroit, and FVP by +21.7 and +12.0 respectively, attributing the gap to feature differencing and inverse-consistency supervision that prevent latent-action shortcutting. On Adroit, AFRO (83.0%) also beats the end-to-end DP3 baseline (75.0%) by +8.0 points, demonstrating that pre-training with dynamics-aware representations is more effective than joint perception-control learning from scratch. In real-world evaluation, AFRO leads by +20 points over DP3 and +36 over FVP in-domain, and by +19 to +38 points over all baselines after RH20T out-of-domain pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

AFRO extends the latent-action paradigm (CLAM, CoMo, DynaMo) from 2D image spaces into 3D point-cloud pre-training, and departs from reconstruction-based 3D methods (PointMAE, PointDif, FVP) by performing all state prediction in a learned latent space via a diffusion transformer. The representation-matching objective borrows VICReg's variance-invariance-covariance regularization, while the forward dynamics model adopts DiT's AdaLN-Zero conditioning for one-shot denoising. The key conceptual departure is the combination of feature differencing (instead of raw feature pairs) and inverse-consistency supervision to prevent the inverse dynamics model from shortcutting via feature leakage—a problem the authors identify as under-addressed in prior latent-action work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both action labels and geometric reconstruction, AFRO offers a scalable pre-training recipe that can exploit large unlabeled 3D robot interaction data (e.g., RH20T) without expensive annotation, and the demonstrated scaling with data volume and task diversity suggests practical value for building general-purpose manipulation policies. The finding that dynamic-aware 3D pre-training can substantially outperform static 3D reconstruction methods (and even 2D foundation models) on manipulation benchmarks shifts the design priority from holistic scene fidelity to transition-centric abstraction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond point-cloud inputs (e.g., multi-modal or language-conditioned settings), how it interacts with semantic priors from large vision-language models, and whether the 16-dimensional latent action space is sufficient for higher-DoF or multi-embodiment scenarios.
