## Problem

Existing text-image to video generation methods assume the input text and image are perfectly paired and temporally aligned, with the image serving as the first frame. In realistic out-of-distribution scenarios, the text and image may describe events at different timestamps or appear semantically unrelated, causing current models to produce incoherent videos that either ignore one modality or superficially blend elements without establishing meaningful temporal connections. This paper is the first to formalize and address unpaired text-image to video generation under test-time OOD conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a single RGB condition image that are explicitly unpaired — the image is not guaranteed to depict the same semantic content as the text nor to correspond to the first frame of the output video. The model does not receive the frame index or relative temporal position between the two conditions. The output is a 16-frame video (16-frame clips at 0.2 s intervals in evaluation) that must be semantically coherent with both inputs, with the condition image appearing at its inferred position within the sequence.

## Method

ReasonDiff builds on the Wan2.1 flow-matching DiT backbone and introduces two reasoning modules. The VisionNarrator module prompts a multi-modal LLM to infer the most plausible position of the condition image within the video and generate a per-frame narrative script that temporally aligns the unpaired modalities. The AlignFormer module then uses a Multi-stage Temporal Anchor Attention mechanism, where the condition image feature serves as the Query and the per-frame narrative embeddings serve as Key/Value in a two-stage cross-attention cascade, producing reasoning-enhanced latent representations for every frame. These latents are fused with the condition frame to guide the denoising process. Training is two-stage: first, joint denoising-loss training of the base model and AlignFormer on WebVid with randomly selected condition frames and LLaMA-generated per-frame captions; second, AlignFormer-only fine-tuning with an auxiliary L2 reconstruction loss (β=0.2) between predicted and ground-truth latents, while the base generator is frozen.

## Results

On the self-constructed ActivityNet dataset with unpaired text-image inputs, ReasonDiff achieves the best scores on all metrics except CLIP Score (Image): Imaging Quality 0.528, Motion Smooth 0.986, Dynamic Degree 0.936, CLIP Score (Text) 0.261, and User Rank 1.743. Most notably, ReasonDiff exceeds the strongest baseline (Wan2.1) by 16.5% in CLIP Score (Text) (0.261 vs. 0.224) and by 0.949 in User Rank (1.743 vs. 2.692). On the paired MSR-VTT dataset, ReasonDiff also surpasses its base model Wan2.1 on Imaging Quality (0.571 vs. 0.560), CLIP Score (Text) (0.214 vs. 0.191), and User Rank (1.769 vs. 2.743), confirming general-purpose generative quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReasonDiff is compared against four open-source video generation baselines: Dynamicrafter, LTX-Video-2B, CogVideoX-1.5-5B, and Wan2.1-I2V-14B (its own base model). On the unpaired ActivityNet setting, ReasonDiff decisively outperforms all baselines on CLIP Score (Text) and User Rank—the two metrics most sensitive to cross-modal reasoning—beating the next-best Wan2.1 by 16.5% and 0.949 respectively. On CLIP Score (Image), baselines LTX-Video (0.544) and CogVideoX (0.537) slightly edge out ReasonDiff (0.528), which the authors attribute to baselines over-relying on the visual condition when text and image are unpaired. On the paired MSR-VTT dataset, ReasonDiff still surpasses Wan2.1 on Imaging Quality, CLIP Score (Text), and User Rank, though CogVideoX marginally leads on Dynamic Degree (0.688 vs. 0.673) and LTX-Video on CLIP Score (Image) (0.588 vs. 0.572). Overall, ReasonDiff wins or ties on the majority of metrics across both datasets, with the largest margins appearing precisely on the unpaired evaluation where reasoning over temporal misalignment is required.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Wan2.1 (a DiT-based flow-matching video generator) as its base model and departs from the standard text-image-to-video paradigm (Dynamicrafter, LTX-Video, CogVideoX) that assumes the input image and text are semantically paired and temporally aligned. It draws on the MLLM-reasoning-for-generation line of work (LayoutGPT, VideoDirectorGPT, SmartEdit) but extends it from single-prompt expansion to cross-modal temporal reasoning that produces per-frame narratives and predicts an anchor position for the condition image. The AlignFormer's Multi-stage Temporal Anchor Attention is a novel architectural contribution that bridges high-level MLLM reasoning outputs with frame-wise latent prediction, going beyond the prompt-rewriting or layout-injection strategies used in prior LLM-guided generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of unpaired text-image-to-video generation under test-time OOD conditions, addressing a practical gap where real users provide loosely related or temporally offset inputs. The approach demonstrates that MLLM-driven multi-frame reasoning can substantially improve semantic coherence (16.5% CLIP-Text gain) over strong baselines, suggesting a general recipe for injecting reasoning into diffusion-based video generators. Limitations include reliance on a single MLLM (LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct), evaluation on short 16-frame clips, and a training proxy that uses paired data with artificially increased temporal gaps rather than truly unpaired supervision. Open questions include scalability to longer videos, robustness to more complex multi-condition scenarios, and whether the AlignFormer mechanism generalizes beyond the Wan2.1 backbone.
