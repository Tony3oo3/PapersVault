## Problem

Existing textual adapters for VLM fine-tuning represent each class with a single deterministic vector (one description or a mean over several), failing to capture the rich semantic diversity inherent in how a category can be described. Additionally, while multiple pre-trained visual encoders (e.g., MoCo, DINO) can complement CLIP, there is no principled mechanism to dynamically weight their contributions per sample based on prediction confidence.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image as input in a C-way N-shot setting and outputs a class probability vector over C categories. On the textual side, M=50 diverse descriptions per class are generated by an LLM (following CuPL) and encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder to initialize the adapter. The visual side processes the image through three parallel branches (CLIP ResNet-50, MoCo ResNet-50, DINO ResNet-50), whose predictions are fused into a single softmax-normalized output.

## Method

VRGAdapter constructs a Vertex Random Knowledge Graph where each class node is a Gaussian distribution (mean and diagonal covariance) estimated from M diverse LLM-generated description embeddings, and edges encode inter-class cosine similarity of the means. A two-layer Gaussian-based graph convolution network performs probabilistic message propagation on this graph, updating both mean and variance at each node to produce context-aware distributional representations. The reparameterization trick enables differentiable sampling from the final distributions, and a weighted combination with the initial means (α=0.7) yields the adaptive textual prototypes. An Uncertainty-guided Multi-branch Fusion (UMF) module then dynamically weights the CLIP zero-shot logits against auxiliary MoCo/DINO prototype-classifier logits using a normalized kurtosis confidence score, with a balancing parameter β controlling the relative influence of the auxiliary branches.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K few-shot classification (ResNet-50), VRGAdapter achieves 63.93% (1-shot), 67.45% (4-shot), and 71.37% (16-shot) top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline AMU-Tuning by 1.33–1.53% across all shot settings. On out-of-distribution generalization, VRGAdapter reaches 62.41% on ImageNet-V2 and 41.23% on ImageNet-Sketch (ResNet-50, 16-shot), surpassing AMU-Tuning by 3.77% and 1.19% respectively. With ViT-B/16 backbone, the method further improves to 76.78% on ImageNet-1K and 68.60% on ImageNet-V2, maintaining consistent gains over all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRGAdapter outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every shot setting on ImageNet-1K, with no losses or ties. Against the closest competitor AMU-Tuning [36], gains range from +1.12% (8-shot) to +1.53% (4-shot), while against the direct predecessor GraphAdapter [24], improvements are substantially larger at +2.43% (1-shot) to +5.67% (16-shot), validating the benefit of probabilistic node representations over deterministic vectors. Compared to other adapter-based methods (CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter-F, TaskRes, ClusterAdapter), VRGAdapter leads by 7.78–12.73% in the 1-shot regime, where diverse description modeling is most impactful. On OOD benchmarks (Table 2), VRGAdapter extends its lead: +3.77% over AMU-Tuning on ImageNet-V2 and +1.19% on ImageNet-Sketch with ResNet-50, and +3.18% / +1.41% with ViT-B/16, confirming that the random graph adapter generalizes better under distribution shift. The method also surpasses prompt-tuning approaches (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe) by 2–6% in the 16-shot setting, demonstrating that adapter-based probabilistic modeling with multi-branch fusion is more effective than learnable prompt vectors for few-shot adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-based VLM adapter lineage initiated by GraphAdapter [24], which models inter-class relationships via GCN on deterministic node vectors, by replacing each node with a Gaussian distribution estimated from multiple LLM-generated descriptions (following CuPL [31]). The probabilistic message propagation mechanism is borrowed from Gaussian-based GCN [53], adapted here to preserve distributional form across layers. The key conceptual departure is treating intra-class semantic diversity as a first-class modeling object (via variance) rather than collapsing it into a single mean, and the UMF scheme adds a kurtosis-weighted multi-model fusion layer absent from prior adapter work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic adapter framework offers a principled way to retain the rich discriminative information in diverse textual descriptions that deterministic adapters discard, and the reparameterization trick makes the entire pipeline end-to-end differentiable. The UMF scheme provides a lightweight, uncertainty-aware recipe for combining foundation models that could be adopted beyond this specific adapter. Open questions include whether the diagonal-covariance Gaussian assumption is sufficient for highly anisotropic class distributions, how the method scales to very large class taxonomies (e.g., 10K+ classes), and whether the LLM description generation step introduces dataset-specific biases that limit cross-domain transfer.
