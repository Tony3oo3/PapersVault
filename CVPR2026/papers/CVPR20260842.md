## Problem

Existing IAA/IQA models face a fundamental trade-off: those fine-tuned for fixed scoring criteria achieve high accuracy but cannot adapt to diverse, user-specified evaluation semantics, while CLIP-based prompt-conditioned methods offer flexibility at the cost of limited perceptual accuracy. Achieving both high-precision scoring and fine-grained, prompt-controllable evaluation without requiring prompt-level or attribute-level annotations remains an open problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260842/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model accepts an RGB image, an antonymous text prompt pair (e.g., 'Focused high-quality photo' / 'Defocused low-quality photo'), and optionally a task identifier. It outputs a scalar score in [0,1] (the expected value of a Gaussian) and, when operating in task mode, a probability distribution over 260 pre-sampled prompts. The prompt pool is generated offline by GPT-5 and comprises 20 attribute-specific pairs (4 per attribute across 11 aesthetic and 9 quality dimensions) plus 180 non-attribute antonymous pairs; no prompts are needed during training.

## Method

PPA formulates score prediction as a mixture-of-prompts model: p(s|x,c) = Σ_t p_θ(s|x,t)·p_φ(t|x,c), where p_θ is a Gaussian centered on the CLIP-IQA score (softmax of cosine similarities between image and prompt embeddings) and p_φ is a softmax over 260 LLM-sampled prompts computed via dot products of image-task and text-task embeddings produced by 2-layer MLPs (256-dim hidden, 128-dim output). Training minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the marginalized score distribution using only (task, image, score) triplets, jointly optimizing the prompt-selection weights φ and the last four blocks of the CLIP-B/16 image encoder θ. This marginalization over prompts—rather than fixed prompt ensembling or deterministic selection—enables annotation-free learning while preserving the ability to condition on arbitrary user-specified prompts at test time, effectively treating prompts as interpretable experts in a MoE-style gating mechanism.

## Results

On IAA benchmarks, PPA (shared weights) achieves SRCC 0.913 on PARA and 0.497 on BAID, both state-of-the-art among compared methods, while PPA-T (task-specific) reaches 0.919 on PARA. On IQA benchmarks, PPA achieves SRCC 0.934 on SPAQ (best among all methods) and remains within 4% of the strongest baseline (Gamma-T) on the remaining six datasets (e.g., 0.918 vs. 0.945 on KonIQ-10k, 0.920 vs. 0.960 on KADID-10k). The shared-weight PPA variant trades a small accuracy drop (≈2–5% SRCC) for the ability to handle arbitrary text prompts at inference time, a capability none of the baselines provide.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPA achieves the best SRCC on SPAQ (0.934 shared / 0.941 task-specific) and PARA (0.913 / 0.919), surpassing Gamma-T (0.928 / 0.905) and Charm (0.905) respectively. On the remaining IQA datasets, Gamma-T [77] is the strongest baseline, leading PPA by 2–10% SRCC (e.g., 0.945 vs. 0.918 on KonIQ-10k, 0.960 vs. 0.920 on KADID-10k, 0.982 vs. 0.922 on LIVE). On IAA, IAACLIP [35] and Gamma-T [77] outperform PPA on AVA (0.791/0.785 vs. 0.737) and AADB (0.795/0.793 vs. 0.780), while PPA wins on BAID (0.497 vs. 0.473 for SAAN). The critical distinction is that all baselines are locked to fixed scoring criteria or require large-scale text annotations for prompt conditioning, whereas PPA supports arbitrary user-specified prompts at inference without any additional training, making it the only method in the comparison that offers prompt-controllable evaluation alongside competitive accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPA builds directly on CLIP-IQA's antonymous-prompt scoring mechanism as its score prediction backbone, but departs from the fixed-prompt paradigm by treating prompts as latent variables in a probabilistic mixture model. It draws conceptual inspiration from mixture-of-experts gating and the broader prompt-learning literature (CoOp, MAPLE, prompt ensembling), yet differs by using discrete, interpretable natural-language prompts as fixed experts whose weights are inferred per image-task pair rather than learning continuous prompt embeddings. Unlike feature-space alignment methods (UniQA, IAACLIP, DeCLIP) that require large-scale text-image annotations, PPA trains exclusively on (task, image, score) triplets by marginalizing over LLM-sampled prompts, and unlike Gamma's mixture-of-assessment-experts, it conditions prompt selection on both image content and task identity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPA addresses a practical gap in IAA/IQA: the inability of high-accuracy models to adapt to diverse, user-specified evaluation criteria without retraining or expensive text annotations. The annotation-free probabilistic formulation is a clean contribution that could be adopted in content-moderation, retrieval, and creator-tool pipelines where evaluation semantics shift frequently. However, the paper acknowledges a meaningful accuracy gap versus task-specific SOTA on several IQA benchmarks (e.g., KonIQ-10k: 0.918 vs. 0.945 for Gamma-T), and performance degrades for abstract or emotional aesthetic prompts, suggesting the LLM-generated prompt pool's semantic diversity remains a bottleneck. Open questions include whether scaling the prompt pool or integrating MLLM-based prompt refinement can close both the accuracy gap and the high-level-aesthetics weakness.
