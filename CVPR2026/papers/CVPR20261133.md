## Problem

Existing Transformer and Mamba architectures for point clouds are designed for single-task or single-domain settings and degrade sharply under multi-task domain generalization. Transformers suffer from quadratic attention cost and lack explicit structural ordering, while Mamba-based methods rely on coordinate-driven serialization (axis scanning, Hilbert curves) that is sensitive to viewpoint changes and missing surfaces, causing structural drift and unstable recurrent state propagation across domains and tasks.

## Input / Output

The model receives an unordered target point cloud and a source-domain prompt point cloud, each sampled to 1,024 points and normalized within a unit sphere. Both are tokenized via Farthest Point Sampling and KNN grouping into N local patch tokens. Depending on the task (reconstruction, denoising, or registration), the model outputs a corresponding point cloud; at test time, no parameter updates are performed—the source prompt provides in-context conditioning and a lightweight spectral alignment module shifts target features toward source prototypes.

## Method

SADG is a Mamba-based In-Context Learning framework with three components. First, Structure-Aware Serialization (SAS) constructs transformation-invariant token sequences using two intrinsic spectra: a Centroid Distance Spectrum that performs BFS over a Gaussian-affinity token graph to preserve global topology, and a Geodesic Curvature Spectrum that encodes implicit surface curvature via multi-scale heat diffusion on a geodesic graph, avoiding fragile explicit normal estimation. Second, Hierarchical Domain-Aware Modeling (HDM) processes prompt and query sequences through parallel Mamba branches (intra-domain structural modeling) before interleaving their outputs in shared structural order for a fused Mamba pass (inter-domain relational fusion), preventing the sequential discontinuities that arise from naive cross-domain concatenation. Third, Spectral Graph Alignment (SGA) performs test-time feature shifting in the Graph Fourier Transform domain, adaptively blending target spectral tokens toward averaged source prototypes without any weight updates, preserving topological and geometric consistency under domain shift.

## Results

SADG achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (CD, ×10⁻³) across all five datasets and three tasks (reconstruction, denoising, registration) under the leave-one-domain-out protocol. On the newly introduced MP3DObject real-scan dataset, SADG records CD of 3.55/6.61/2.84, outperforming the closest ICL+DG baseline DG-PIC (5.91/10.40/5.64) by 40–50%. On the real-scan ScanNet target, SADG reaches 2.97/7.67/3.63, nearly halving the error of the best general-purpose baseline PointMamba (15.61/22.43/14.30). The method also runs faster than DG-PIC (0.75 s vs. 0.94 s inference) with fewer parameters (18.87 M vs. 27.57 M) and lower FLOPs (14.89 G vs. 21.07 G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SADG outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and task. Against the closest ICL+DG competitor DG-PIC [28], SADG reduces CD by 12–50% (e.g., 3.55 vs. 5.91 on MP3DObject reconstruction; 2.97 vs. 5.21 on ScanNet reconstruction). The vanilla Mamba ICL ablation (replacing DG-PIC's Transformer with Mamba but retaining coordinate-based serialization) degrades sharply on real scans (8.28/14.19/8.44 on MP3DObject), confirming that structure-aware serialization is critical. Among DG-specific methods, PointDGMamba [91] is the strongest non-ICL baseline (14.39/19.37/12.44 on ModelNet), yet SADG still leads by 58–70% on that dataset. General-purpose backbones (PointNet, DGCNN, PCT, Point-MAE, PointMamba) all degrade substantially on real-scan targets, with PointMamba reaching 20.16/27.08/17.40 on MP3DObject versus SADG's 3.55/6.61/2.84, highlighting the gap between single-domain training and structure-aware multi-task DG. Augmentation-based DG methods (PointMixup, PointCutMix) perform poorly under the multi-task protocol, with CD errors exceeding 22 on real scans, indicating that data mixing alone cannot compensate for structural drift across domains and tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DG-PIC [28], the first framework to unify multiple point cloud tasks (reconstruction, denoising, registration) under a domain-generalization in-context learning paradigm, by replacing its Transformer backbone with Mamba and addressing the resulting order-sensitivity problem. It departs from prior Mamba-based point cloud methods (e.g., PointMamba [43]) that rely on coordinate-driven serialization (Z-order, Hilbert curves) by introducing intrinsic, transformation-invariant serialization via centroid-distance and geodesic-curvature spectra. The hierarchical domain-aware modeling and spectral graph alignment components are novel architectural contributions specific to adapting Mamba's recurrent dynamics to cross-domain, multi-task settings, building on graph signal processing and heat-kernel ideas from the structure-modeling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that Mamba-based sequential modeling can match or exceed Transformer-based ICL for multi-task point cloud DG while being more efficient (18.87M vs 27.57M params, 0.75s vs 0.94s inference), which is practically significant as Mamba architectures scale better to longer sequences. The MP3DObject dataset fills a gap in real-scan, unaligned-pose evaluation that prior benchmarks (ModelNet, ShapeNet) lack. However, the evaluation is limited to 7 shared classes and 3 tasks under a single leave-one-domain-out protocol with one metric (Chamfer Distance), leaving open whether the structure-aware serialization generalizes to broader category sets, more complex scenes, or other SSM variants. The test-time spectral alignment without parameter updates is an appealing direction but its interaction with more aggressive domain shifts (e.g., sensor type changes) remains untested.
