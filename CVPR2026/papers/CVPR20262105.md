## Problem

Existing generative co-speech gesture synthesis methods produce natural, expressive motions but operate offline and non-causally, requiring future speech context and long runtimes that preclude real-time interaction. Conversely, rule-based and early data-driven ECA systems are causal and real-time but yield rigid, low-diversity gestures. No prior framework simultaneously satisfies the three requirements of causality, real-time latency, and expressive full-body gesture quality needed for truly interactive Embodied Conversational Agents.

## Input / Output

The method consumes Moshi's internal speech and text token streams (multi-level RVQ tokens at 12.5 tokens/s, each representing 0.08 s of audio) along with a learned character identity embedding, rather than raw audio or a separate speech encoder. At each timestep it produces a 2D grid of discrete gesture tokens spanning T temporal positions and K kinematic levels (K = 8 upper-body + 8 lower-body + 4 facial RVQ levels), which are decoded into 2 frames of SMPL-X body parameters and FLAME facial parameters at 25 FPS. The body is explicitly partitioned into three regions (upper body with hands, lower body with global translation and foot contacts, and face) that are tokenized and generated independently.

## Method

MIBURI introduces a two-dimensional causal generation architecture that disentangles temporal and kinematic prediction: a temporal transformer autoregressively predicts the first RVQ level across time using causal self-attention over past gesture tokens and dual cross-attention to Moshi's speech/text tokens, while a kinematic transformer then predicts the remaining K−1 levels at each fixed timestep conditioned on the temporal context. Motion is encoded by body-part-specific Residual VQ-VAE codecs (causal encoder-decoder with 1-D convolutions and transformer blocks) that capture hierarchical detail from large arm movements to subtle finger gestures. To prevent the autoregressive model from collapsing to mean poses, the authors add a contrastive InfoNCE loss computed on Gumbel-Softmax-sampled RVQ latents and a binary voice-activation classification head that distinguishes listening from speaking states. Real-time inference (36 ms/frame on an RTX 3090) is achieved through KV-Cache with limited attention contexts (25 tokens self-attention, 50 tokens cross-attention), top-p nucleus sampling, and classifier-free guidance, while cross-attention for lower-body tokens is masked to reduce compute.

## Results

On the multi-speaker BEAT2 test set (23 speakers), MIBURI(+Face) achieves the best FGD of 0.480 and a BeatAlign score of 0.461, outperforming all non-causal baselines on FGD (RAG-Gesture: 0.515, GestureLSM: 0.537, EMAGE: 0.850, MambaTalk: 1.375). The system achieves a per-step latency of 34.9 ms on an A100 (generating 2 frames per step) and 36 ms per frame end-to-end on an RTX 3090, making it the lowest-latency method evaluated. In the single-speaker setting, MIBURI(+Face) reaches FGD 0.753 and BeatAlign 0.790, competitive with EMAGE (FGD 0.552, BeatAlign 0.795) and MambaTalk (FGD 0.530, BeatAlign 0.779).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIBURI(+Face) achieves the best FGD (0.480) among all methods, beating RAG-Gesture (0.515) by 6.8%, GestureLSM (0.537) by 10.6%, and the much weaker EMAGE (0.850) and MambaTalk (1.375). On BeatAlign, RAG-Gesture (0.648) and GestureLSM (0.481) still edge out MIBURI(+Face) (0.461), though MIBURI clearly surpasses EMAGE (0.236), CaMN (0.176), and MambaTalk (0.080). The causal variants of GestureLSM and MambaTalk degrade severely (FGD 2.792 and 1.222 respectively), confirming that naively making existing architectures causal is insufficient. MIBURI is the only method that simultaneously satisfies all three requirements—causal, real-time, and expressive—making it the sole viable plug-and-play option for building interactive ECAs. In the single-speaker setting, MIBURI is competitive but not dominant, with EMAGE and GestureLSM achieving lower FGD, suggesting the causal framework benefits more from diverse multi-speaker training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIBURI sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: expressive co-speech gesture synthesis (EMAGE, ConvoFusion, RAG-Gesture, GestureDiffuCLIP) and real-time Embodied Conversational Agents (Cassell et al., Gesturebot). It builds directly on Moshi, a speech-text foundation model, by tapping into its internal token stream rather than the conventional multi-step pipeline (LLM → speech → audio features → gesture model). The two-dimensional autoregressive transformer design (temporal + kinematic) is inspired by RQ-Transformer, and the Residual VQ-VAE gesture codecs follow the Soundstream paradigm, but the key departure is making the entire pipeline causal and real-time by restricting attention to past context only and using a short 2-frame token window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework that simultaneously satisfies the three requirements Cassell et al. identified for interactive ECAs—causality, real-time latency, and expressiveness—without relying on seed gestures or future speech context. The finding that naively converting existing expressive methods to causal mode degrades performance substantially underscores that the architectural design (two-tier transformers, direct token-stream conditioning) is non-trivial. A key limitation is that MIBURI models only the agent's motion and does not incorporate dyadic or multi-party interaction, which is essential for truly natural conversational gestures. The observation that leaner model variants match or exceed larger ones suggests the bottleneck is architectural rather than capacity, opening questions about how to scale expressiveness without increasing compute.
