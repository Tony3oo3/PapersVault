## Problem

Industrial defect inspection requires recognizing continuously emerging defect types without costly retraining, but existing visual prompting methods suffer from prompt embedding collapse when confronted with the high intra-class variance and subtle inter-class differences characteristic of industrial domains. Prior works treat prompt embeddings as implicit representations learned purely through prompt-to-region matching, leaving the structural design of the prompt embedding space unexplored and preventing robust open-set generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261281/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target RGB image and K annotated prompt exemplar images (5 per class in the InsA protocol), from which RoI patches are extracted based on annotated bounding boxes. It produces defect bounding boxes, pixel-level segmentation masks, and class labels via a DETR-style decoder. Evaluation uses AP50 as the primary threshold rather than stricter IoU values, reflecting the inherently ambiguous spatial boundaries of industrial defects such as dents and scratches. The framework is retraining-free for new defect categories—only new exemplar prompts need to be provided at inference time.

## Method

UniSpector builds on a DETR backbone and introduces three components to structure the prompt embedding space. The Spatial-Spectral Prompt Encoder (SSPE) computes the 2D DFT of each prompt RoI patch, averages magnitude responses along concentric frequency rings to obtain an orientation-invariant radial spectrum, and fuses this spectral code with a spatial code (extracted via masked cross-attention) through dual-path MLP alignment. The Contrastive Prompt Encoder (CPE) then applies an ArcFace-inspired angular-margin contrastive loss to explicitly regularize the prompt embedding manifold, enforcing intra-class compactness and minimum angular separation between classes so that semantically related defects occupy neighboring regions. Finally, Prompt-guided Query Selection (PQS) replaces static learnable queries with a differentiable Gumbel-Softmax top-K selection of vision tokens conditioned on prompt prototypes, enabling prompt-adaptive object queries without non-differentiable heuristics.

## Results

On the InsA benchmark, UniSpector (fine-tuned) achieves an overall AP50^b of 40.9% and AP50^m of 35.4%, outperforming the strongest baseline T-Rex2† (21.2% AP50^b, 19.6% AP50^m) by 19.7 and 15.8 percentage points respectively. On the largest in-domain dataset Real-IAD, UniSpector reaches 69.1% AP50^b and 56.7% AP50^m, while on the cross-domain VisA it achieves 32.8% AP50^b and 27.8% AP50^m. In a closed-set ablation on Real-IAD (Table 5), UniSpector matches strong supervised detectors (90.0% AP50^b vs. MaskDINO's 91.7%), confirming that the open-set framework does not sacrifice seen-class accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniSpector dominates all 12 baselines across both in-domain and cross-domain settings. The strongest visual-prompting baseline, T-Rex2†, reaches 21.2% AP50^b / 19.6% AP50^m, while UniSpector achieves 40.9% / 35.4%—nearly double the detection score. Visual grounding methods (GroundingDINO, YOLO-World) are particularly weak, with overall AP50^b below 10% and near-zero cross-domain performance, confirming that text-based prompts fail on subtle industrial defects. Among visual-prompting baselines, DINOv† and YOLOE† show only marginal gains from fine-tuning (17.1→17.4% AP50^b for YOLOE), whereas UniSpector's spectral-contrastive design yields a 23.5-point improvement over DINOv†. In the closed-set Real-IAD evaluation (Table 5), UniSpector (90.0% AP50^b) is within 1.7 points of MaskDINO (91.7%), while DINOv and T-Rex2 drop to 24.8% and 29.8% respectively, demonstrating that naive prompt-to-region matching collapses under high intra-class variance even for seen classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSpector extends the DETR-based visual prompting paradigm (DINOv, T-Rex2, YOLOE) by arguing that the prompt embedding space itself must be explicitly structured rather than left to emerge implicitly from prompt-to-region matching. It repurposes the ArcFace angular-margin loss from face recognition to regularize prompt embeddings into a semantically organized angular manifold, and introduces a novel spectral (radial DFT) encoding path to achieve orientation invariance—neither of which has been applied to visual prompting before. The Gumbel-Softmax-based query selection departs from both static learnable queries and non-differentiable top-K heuristics used in prior open-set detectors, making the entire pipeline end-to-end trainable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The InsA benchmark is the first to systematically evaluate visual-prompt-based open-set defect detection across in-domain and cross-domain settings, filling a gap where prior open-set detection work assumed text prompts or well-defined natural-image categories. The finding that DINOv, pre-trained on over a billion images, still achieves only ~10% AP50 on industrial defects underscores that scale alone cannot substitute for structured prompt-space design in fine-grained, high-variance domains. However, cross-domain performance remains notably lower than in-domain results due to dataset-specific imaging biases, and the authors acknowledge that domain-robust prompt representations remain an open challenge. The demonstrated data efficiency (1–3 exemplars per class) is practically significant for industrial settings where collecting diverse defect samples is costly.
