## Problem

Transformer-based neural geometry models (VGGT, Pi3, Fast3R) rely on full self-attention whose memory and compute cost grow quadratically with the number of input images, fundamentally capping them at a few hundred views on a single GPU. Existing scalability work either degrades geometric accuracy through token merging or chunking, or still requires encoding all images simultaneously, leaving a gap for high-accuracy reconstruction of large, unordered image collections that exceed device memory.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts an arbitrary unordered set of RGB images (evaluated up to 1,000+ per scene) with no requirement for temporal ordering or calibration. It outputs per-image camera intrinsics and extrinsics, dense depth maps, per-pixel confidence scores, and a globally consistent 3D point cloud. Because the framework is training-free and model-agnostic, the geometric foundation model (VGGT, Pi3, FastVGGT) is used as a frozen black box, and the only additional inputs are DINO features for partitioning and SuperPoint/LightGlue features for cross-cluster tracking.

## Method

MERG3R is a training-free, three-stage divide-and-conquer pipeline. First, it constructs a pseudo-video ordering by computing a DINO visual-similarity matrix and approximating a Hamiltonian path that maximizes consecutive-frame similarity, then interleaves the sequence across K subsequences and slides a fixed-length window with overlap to produce geometrically diverse, overlapping subsets that each fit within GPU memory. Second, each subset is independently reconstructed by a frozen geometric foundation model, reducing attention cost from O(N²) to O(KT²). Third, overlapping subsets are aligned via a confidence-weighted iterative similarity-transform estimator (adapted from VGGT-Long), cross-cluster tracks are built with SuperPoint+LightGlue matching on a sparse k-NN graph with 3D reprojection filtering, and a final confidence-weighted global bundle adjustment jointly refines all camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and 3D point positions over the merged multi-view tracks.

## Results

On 7-Scenes with 1,000 images, MERG3R+π³ achieves RRA@30=100, RTA@30=97.69, and AUC@30=83.63, while VGGT*, FastVGGT, π³, and MASt3R-SfM all run out of memory (OOM). On Tanks & Temples, MERG3R+π³ attains the best ATE of 0.077 m and RRE of 0.178°, outperforming π³ alone (ATE 0.090, RRE 0.229°) and VGGT-Long (ATE 0.585, RRE 0.768°). On Cambridge Landmarks, MERG3R+VGGT* achieves the lowest ATE of 0.661 m. For point cloud quality on 7-Scenes (stride 2/3), MERG3R+π³ achieves the best median accuracy of 0.007 m and completion of 0.008 m, while CUT3R and TTT3R degrade substantially (median accuracy 0.111–0.117 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERG3R's primary advantage is scalability: at 1,000 images on 7-Scenes, VGGT*, FastVGGT, π³, and MASt3R-SfM all fail with OOM, while MERG3R+π³ achieves AUC@30 of 83.63, surpassing the only other scalable baseline VGGT-Long (75.11) by 8.5 points. On Tanks & Temples, MERG3R+π³ dominates all baselines with ATE 0.077 m (vs. 0.090 for π³ alone, 0.522 for VGGT-Long, 0.202 for MASt3R-SfM), and on Cambridge Landmarks, MERG3R+VGGT* achieves the best ATE of 0.661 m (vs. 0.793 for VGGT* and 0.970 for VGGT-Long). Against classical SfM baselines on 7-Scenes (500 images), MERG3R+π³ outperforms GLOMAP (RTA 97.61 vs. 96.67, AUC 83.31 vs. 81.38) and InstantSfM (RTA 97.61 vs. 95.55, AUC 83.31 vs. 76.32) while running in 4 min 48 s versus 10 min 34 s and 8 min 56 s respectively. For point cloud reconstruction, CUT3R and TTT3R degrade sharply with more views (e.g., CUT3R median accuracy jumps from 0.054 to 0.111 m going from stride 3 to 2 on 7-Scenes), whereas MERG3R+π³ maintains median accuracy of 0.007 m across both settings. The method is model-agnostic: pairing with VGGT*, FastVGGT, or π³ all yield competitive or superior results, confirming the framework's generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERG3R positions itself as a training-free, model-agnostic wrapper around monolithic neural geometry transformers (VGGT, Pi3, FastVGGT), borrowing the divide-and-conquer philosophy long established in classical SfM systems like COLMAP and GLOMAP. It departs from prior scalable neural approaches (VGGT-Long's sequential chunking, FastVGGT's token merging, Fast3R's hierarchical attention) by partitioning images into overlapping, interleaved subsets that are reconstructed independently and then merged via global alignment and confidence-weighted bundle adjustment. The interleaved partitioning strategy, which distributes views from the full trajectory into every subset, is the key methodological departure from both classical graph-based clustering and simple sliding-window chunking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes the primary practical barrier to deploying neural geometry models on real-world image collections (thousands of unordered photos), making high-quality 3D reconstruction accessible without multi-GPU clusters or custom model modifications. The model-agnostic design means any future improvement in base geometry models is immediately inheritable. Open questions include how the pipeline behaves at city-scale (10,000+ images) where the Hamiltonian path approximation and global bundle adjustment may face new challenges, and whether the confidence-weighted BA can be further accelerated for near-real-time applications. The reliance on a single AMD MI210 GPU for all experiments also limits generalizability claims across hardware.
