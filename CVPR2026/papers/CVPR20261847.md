## Problem

Extreme low-light RAW restoration is severely ill-posed due to overwhelming sensor noise and clipping, and existing dual-exposure methods that fuse short- and long-exposure frames rely on explicit pixel-level alignment (e.g., optical flow) that is extremely brittle under large exposure gaps and non-rigid camera shake. This misalignment degrades shadow detail recovery and introduces color artifacts, while single-frame generative models hallucinate plausible but inaccurate content. The paper recasts the problem from fragile explicit alignment to robust conditional generation, leveraging diffusion priors to denoise a short-exposure input guided by a potentially misaligned long-exposure reference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy short-exposure RAW image and a clean but potentially motion-blurred long-exposure RAW image (both converted from single-channel Bayer to 3-channel linear RAW via bicubic interpolation), along with a concatenated metadata vector containing ISO, CCM, and exposure values from both captures. It produces a restored 3-channel linear RAW image at the same spatial resolution. The entire pipeline operates in a learned latent space (512×512 crops during training) and performs restoration in a single diffusion step rather than iterative denoising.

## Method

RawMetaDiff is a one-step conditional latent diffusion model built on a UNet backbone (initialized from SD-2.1) with two separate encoders mapping short- and long-exposure Raws into latent space. Two metadata-guided mechanisms handle the conditioning: (1) MACT (Meta-Assistant Color Transfer) feeds the reference latent and a meta-prompt (encoding physical dual-exposure relationships) into a lightweight MLP that predicts channel-wise affine parameters (γ, β) to modulate intermediate features, enforcing global color consistency with the long-exposure reference; (2) MNCA (Meta-Normed Cross Attention) uses the same meta-prompt to predict per-feature scale and shift for Query and Key, rendering them comparable across the large exposure gap, while Value is drawn exclusively from the well-exposed reference to inject shadow details. Training is two-stage: first, a pretrained VAE is adapted to the linear RAW domain with KL, MSE, LPIPS, and GAN losses; second, the dual encoders and UNet are fine-tuned end-to-end (decoder frozen) with the same composite objective. The model is trained on ~90K synthetic pairs generated by applying a calibrated degradation pipeline (heteroscedastic sensor noise, luminance-dependent color distortion, and RAW-domain composite motion blur) to inverse-ISP-converted LSDIR images, validated against a 1K real-world triplet dataset (DERaw).

## Results

On the synthetic low-light RAW dataset, RawMetaDiff achieves the best scores on nearly all metrics: LPIPS 0.1650 (a 33% improvement over the second-best HYPIR at 0.2447), ΔE 5.407, MUSIQ 70.32, MANIQA 0.4363, CLIP-IQA 0.7008, and DeQA 4.115, while matching the best PSNR (23.74, tied with Restormer). On the real-world DERaw dataset, RawMetaDiff ranks first on all five no-reference IQA metrics, with a 15% gain in DeQA (2.343 vs. HYPIR's 2.017) and a 20.7% gain in MUSIQ (44.60 vs. HYPIR's 36.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RawMetaDiff outperforms all six baselines on the majority of perceptual and color-accuracy metrics. Against the strongest single-frame baseline Restormer, it matches PSNR (23.74) but dramatically improves LPIPS (0.1650 vs. 0.4156) and ΔE (5.407 vs. 6.197), indicating far better perceptual quality and color fidelity. Among diffusion-based methods, RawMetaDiff surpasses HYPIR (the best diffusion baseline) by 33% on LPIPS, 6.2 points on MUSIQ, and 0.30 on DeQA, while also achieving a 0.61 lower ΔE. The dual-exposure alignment-based methods LSD2 and LSFNet, despite leveraging a long-exposure frame, suffer from misalignment-induced color blotches and detail errors, yielding higher ΔE (6.948 and 7.655) and worse perceptual scores than RawMetaDiff. On the real-world DERaw benchmark, RawMetaDiff leads all methods on every no-reference metric, with the largest margins over HYPIR on MUSIQ (+7.64) and DeQA (+0.33), confirming robust generalization beyond synthetic conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant dual-exposure RAW restoration paradigm (LSFNet, LSD2) that relies on explicit pixel-level alignment via optical flow, which is brittle under large motion and exposure gaps. Instead, it recasts the problem as conditional latent diffusion generation, building on the one-step diffusion restoration line (OSEDiff, HYPIR) and the SD-2.1 VAE/UNet backbone, while introducing RAW metadata (ISO, CCM, exposure) as an explicit conditioning signal through two novel mechanisms (MACT and MNCA). The data contribution—DERaw and a physically calibrated degradation pipeline—extends the inverse-ISP synthesis approach used in prior RAW denoising work (Brooks et al., 2019) into a dual-exposure training regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing fragile explicit alignment with metadata-normed cross-attention, the paper offers a more robust formulation for dual-exposure RAW restoration that generalizes to real-world camera shake without requiring precise registration. The DERaw benchmark and calibrated degradation pipeline address a genuine gap in the field, enabling reproducible evaluation and large-scale training for a previously under-resourced setting. Open questions include whether the one-step diffusion formulation limits diversity and whether the metadata-guided conditioning transfers to other dual-frame tasks (e.g., HDR, deblurring) or to settings where RAW metadata is unavailable.
