## Problem

Optimal transport-based feature aggregation for VPR (e.g., SALAD) relies on the standard Sinkhorn algorithm, which enforces symmetric marginal constraints between image features and cluster centers. This symmetry assumption breaks down when the two distributions diverge, and existing methods also ignore spatial relationships among features, missing opportunities to leverage geometric structure for more coherent cluster assignments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261426/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (default 224×224, tested up to 588×588) and produces an L2-normalized global descriptor of configurable size (e.g., 8448-dim = 64 clusters × 128-dim + 256-dim global token projection) used for FAISS L2-distance retrieval. The DINOv2 ViT-B/14 backbone yields a 768×H×W local feature map and a 768-dim global token; the aggregation module projects these into cluster assignments and a compact descriptor, with descriptor size scaling from 512+32 to 32768+512 depending on cluster count and per-cluster dimension.

## Method

A²GC replaces the standard Sinkhorn solver with an asymmetric optimal transport mechanism: it performs T iterations of row-column normalization averaging in the log-domain (normalizing rows and columns separately, then averaging the two results), followed by independent marginal calibration where source and target marginals are enforced sequentially rather than jointly, allowing the transport plan to adapt to distributional mismatches. In parallel, geometric constraints are introduced via learnable coordinate embeddings: each spatial location (x,y) is mapped to a d_g-dimensional embedding through a 1×1 convolution, and a geometric compatibility score (dot product with a per-cluster learnable geometric embedding) is added to the feature similarity score with a learnable weight λ_g, encouraging spatially proximal features to co-cluster. The full pipeline uses DINOv2 ViT-B/14 as backbone (last 4 blocks fine-tuned), a two-layer score network for m=64 clusters, and is trained on GSV-Cities (~1.2M images) with MultiSimilarityLoss and AdamW, achieving SOTA Recall@1 on Pitts30k (95.6% with ViTg) and ~1.4× higher Recall@1 than SALAD on Nordland's extreme seasonal shift.

## Results

On Pitts30k, A²GC (ViTg, 33280-dim descriptor) achieves 95.6%/99.3%/99.8% Recall@1/5/10, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses Pair-VPR (95.4%) and CricaVPR (94.9%). On Pitts250k-test, it reaches 97.3%/99.3%/99.7%, outperforming FoL (97.0%) and SelaVPR (95.7%). On the more challenging MSLS-val, A²GC attains 93.6%/97.5%/97.9%, marginally ahead of FoL (93.5%), while on MSLS-challenge it scores 80.6%/90.9%/92.5%, competitive with FoL and Pair-VPR. On the extreme seasonal-shift Nordland benchmark, A²GC achieves 34.71% R@1, approximately 1.4× higher than the next-best method (SALAD at 25.18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A²GC (ViTg) achieves the best Pitts30k R@1 at 95.6%, edging out Pair-VPR (95.4%) and CricaVPR (94.9%), while also leading on Pitts250k (97.3% vs. FoL's 97.0% and SelaVPR's 95.7%). On MSLS-val, A²GC (93.6%) marginally surpasses FoL (93.5%) and clearly outperforms SALAD (92.2%) and SelaVPR (90.8%); however, on MSLS-challenge, Pair-VPR retains a slight R@1 advantage (81.7% vs. 80.6%), though A²GC recovers on R@5 (90.9% vs. 90.2%) and R@10 (92.5% vs. 91.3%). The most dramatic gains appear on the unseen-domain Nordland benchmark, where A²GC's 34.71% R@1 is roughly 1.4× that of SALAD (25.18%) and over 4× that of MixVPR (4.82%), FoL (6.20%), and CricaVPR (6.12%). On SPED, A²GC leads with 83.86% R@1 versus SALAD's 81.71% and FoL's 67.38%. On the SF-XL zero-shot test, A²GC (89.5%/95.2%) outperforms SALAD (88.6%/94.8%) and EigenPlaces (84.1%/90.8%), confirming that the asymmetric transport and geometric constraint design generalizes without dataset-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends SALAD (CVPR 2024), which first cast VPR feature aggregation as an entropic optimal transport problem solved via Sinkhorn iterations. The key departure is replacing SALAD's symmetric marginal constraints with an asymmetric solver that performs row-column normalization averaging followed by independent source/target marginal calibration, motivated by the observation that image feature distributions and learned cluster-center distributions are rarely balanced. A second departure from prior OT-based aggregation is the introduction of geometric constraints via learnable 2D coordinate embeddings fused with feature similarity scores, a spatial-awareness mechanism absent from SALAD and other transport-based methods. The overall pipeline follows the modern VPR trend of using DINOv2 ViT backbones with a lightweight aggregation head, consistent with recent SOTA methods like FoL and Pair-VPR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymmetric transport formulation offers a principled and lightweight fix (only 3 iterations plus two calibration steps) for a distributional mismatch that plagues all Sinkhorn-based aggregation in VPR, and the geometric constraint mechanism is a simple, parameter-efficient way to inject spatial coherence into feature-to-cluster assignment. The ~1.4× Recall@1 gap over SALAD on Nordland (extreme seasonal variation) suggests the asymmetric formulation may be particularly beneficial under domain shift, though this is demonstrated on a single dataset. Open questions include whether the coordinate-embedding approach generalizes to non-grid or variable-resolution feature layouts, whether the asymmetric transport idea transfers to other aggregation or matching problems beyond VPR, and whether the modest gains on standard urban benchmarks (≤0.5% over Pair-VPR on Pitts30k) will hold as backbones and training data continue to scale.
