## Problem

Anomaly segmentation for autonomous driving is severely limited by the scarcity and low diversity of real-world anomaly data, with existing benchmarks covering only clear-weather urban scenes and a handful of anomaly types. Prior synthetic data approaches—copy-paste and unconstrained text-to-image inpainting—introduce contextual inconsistencies and physically implausible object placement, creating domain gaps that undermine model generalization to open-world conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261775/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

ClimaDrive consumes BDD100K semantic maps (H×W class labels) and text prompts encoding scene attributes (weather, scene type, time of day) fused with BLIP-generated captions, and produces 10,230 synthetic driving images paired with binary pixel-level anomaly masks. Anomaly placement is constrained to drivable regions extracted from the semantic layout, with bounding box heights scaled inversely to vertical position (h_i = H/y_i) to enforce perspective-correct depth-size relationships. Final masks are generated via Grounding-SAM and refined with a lightweight noise-denoise pass for boundary smoothness.

## Method

ClimaDrive is a two-module, semantics-guided image-to-image pipeline. The Multi-Scene Weather Generator fine-tunes Stable Diffusion with ControlNet on semantic maps as structural guidance, conditioned on scene-attribute prompts to render geometrically consistent driving scenes across six weather conditions. The AnomPlacer module samples N=64 pseudo-bounding boxes in drivable regions, applies a perspective prior to scale boxes by depth, and trains a DINO-Swin-B detection backbone to predict these boxes via Hungarian-matched supervision with an L1+IoU localization loss. Predicted boxes are then filled with anomalous objects through SD2 diffusion inpainting conditioned on global scene context (e.g., 'Tunnel, Rainy, Daytime') and object concept (e.g., 'dog'). Training proceeds in two stages—localization pretraining followed by joint optimization with the inpainting loss—ensuring both spatial accuracy and visual coherence of generated anomalies.

## Results

Training four state-of-the-art anomaly segmentation methods (RbA, RPL, Mask2Anomaly, UNO) with ClimaOoD yields consistent improvements over original training data across all four evaluation benchmarks. On average, AUROC increases by 0.66% and AP improves by 3.25%. The largest gains appear on RoadAnomaly, where RPL's AP jumps from 71.60 to 78.93 (+7.33) and FPR95 drops from 17.75 to 14.32. On the ClimaOoD test set itself, UNO achieves the best overall performance (AUROC 98.60, AP 80.50, FPR95 7.80), while all methods degrade under adverse weather, with average FPR95 rising from 7.8% (clear) to 11.0% (adverse).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new segmentation model but rather a synthetic dataset (ClimaOoD) and generation framework (ClimaDrive), so the 'baselines' are the four anomaly segmentation methods evaluated under two training regimes: original data vs. ClimaOoD-augmented data. All four methods (RbA, RPL, Mask2Anomaly, UNO) improve when trained with ClimaOoD, with the most dramatic gains on RoadAnomaly: RPL's AP rises by 7.33 points (71.60→78.93) and FPR95 drops by 3.43 points (17.75→14.32), while UNO's AP gains 2.55 points (87.21→89.76). On Fishyscapes LAF, UNO shows the largest AP improvement (+5.63, from 74.49 to 80.12), and Mask2Anomaly gains +4.69 AP (69.46→74.15). A few minor regressions exist (e.g., RbA's AP on RoadObstacle21 drops from 98.40 to 97.95; UNO's AUROC on RoadAnomaly drops from 97.71 to 97.54), but these are within noise given the near-saturated performance on that easy benchmark. On the ClimaOoD test set itself, UNO and RPL lead overall (AUROC 98.60 and 98.30 respectively), while RbA and Mask2Anomaly lag significantly (AUROC 94.00 and 94.90), suggesting that uncertainty-based methods benefit most from the weather-diverse training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

ClimaDrive synthesizes ideas from two established synthetic-data paradigms—copy-paste object insertion (Fishyscapes-Static, StreetHazards) and text-to-image diffusion inpainting (Loiseau et al.)—into a unified image-to-image pipeline that enforces perspective-aware spatial constraints and semantic consistency simultaneously. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes ControlNet-conditioned Stable Diffusion for multi-weather scene generation and couples it with a DINO-based detection module (AnomPlacer) for physically grounded anomaly placement, a combination not previously attempted in the anomaly-segmentation literature. The resulting ClimaOoD benchmark extends the scope of prior datasets (LostAndFound, Fishyscapes, SMIYC) from clear-weather urban scenes to six weather conditions and six driving scenarios, addressing a diversity gap the authors identify as a key limitation of existing benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ClimaOoD provides a substantially larger and more weather-diverse benchmark (10,230 training pairs, 93 anomaly types, 6 scenarios) that enables more realistic evaluation of open-world anomaly segmentation, and the consistent (if modest) gains across four SOTA methods validate the value of weather-diverse synthetic training data. However, the paper itself highlights that adverse-weather robustness gaps persist (average FPR95 rising from 7.8% to 11.0%), suggesting that synthetic data alone cannot fully close the domain gap to real-world anomalies. Open questions include whether the perspective prior generalizes to non-perspective scenes (e.g., aerial or interior views), whether the 93 anomaly categories capture the true long-tail of real-world hazards, and whether the framework can be extended to video or 3D-consistent generation for temporal anomaly detection.
