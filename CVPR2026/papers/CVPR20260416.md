## Problem

Existing adversarial fine-tuning methods for vision-language models (e.g., TeCoA) rely on hard-label supervision that treats the VLM as a conventional classifier, pushing image features toward a single target text while suppressing all others. This disrupts the rich cross-modal similarity structure learned during contrastive pre-training, degrading zero-shot generalization to unseen categories. The paper asks whether adversarial robustness can be improved while preserving the pre-trained visual-textual alignment that enables zero-shot transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260416/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images from ImageNet (224×224) perturbed by ℓ∞-bounded adversarial noise (budget ε ∈ {1/255, 2/255, 4/255}) along with a fixed set of text prompts (class descriptions) encoded by a frozen text encoder. The pre-trained CLIP model's own softmax predictions over all text prompts serve as soft supervision targets rather than one-hot labels. At inference, the fine-tuned image encoder produces a feature vector whose cosine similarities to the frozen text embeddings yield a class prediction, evaluated across 15 zero-shot benchmarks under PGD-20, C&W, and AutoAttack.

## Method

AGFT replaces hard-label cross-entropy with a soft cross-entropy objective where the target distribution is the pre-trained CLIP model's own probabilistic predictions over all text prompts, preserving the relative image-text similarity structure rather than collapsing it to a single class. A distribution consistency calibration module introduces a temperature scaling ratio γ ∈ (0,1] applied to the pre-trained logits (τ/γ), decoupling the semantic similarity structure from the confidence scale so the robust model is not forced to inherit mismatched logit magnitudes. The overall training is a min-max optimization: the inner loop generates adversarial examples via 2-step PGD, and the outer loop minimizes the soft cross-entropy between the robust model's predictions and the calibrated target distribution. Only the image encoder is updated (all parameters), while the text encoder remains frozen, and the method requires just one additional forward pass per step compared to standard adversarial training.

## Results

Under the standard PGD-20 evaluation (ε=1/255), AGFT achieves an average zero-shot robust accuracy of 46.57% across 15 datasets, surpassing the strongest baseline GLADIATOR (43.46%) by 3.11% and the original TeCoA (38.51%) by 8.06%. On clean images, AGFT attains 61.35% average accuracy, exceeding GLADIATOR (60.34%) by 1.01% while remaining close to the untrained CLIP (66.20%). AGFT also leads under stronger attacks (C&W: 43.06%, AutoAttack: 42.81%) and across varying perturbation budgets (ε up to 4/255), confirming broad robustness generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGFT outperforms all four adversarial fine-tuning baselines on average robust accuracy: +8.06% over TeCoA (46.57 vs. 38.51), +4.91% over PMG-AFT (46.57 vs. 41.66), +5.73% over TGA-ZSR (46.57 vs. 40.84), and +3.11% over GLADIATOR (46.57 vs. 43.46). On clean accuracy, AGFT (61.35%) also beats every fine-tuned baseline—GLADIATOR (60.34%), PMG-AFT (59.23%), TeCoA (56.93%), and TGA-ZSR (55.07%)—while retaining 92.7% of the untrained CLIP's clean performance (66.20%). Under stronger attacks (C&W and AutoAttack at ε=1/255), AGFT leads by 0.56% and 0.90% over GLADIATOR respectively, and the gap widens at larger budgets (e.g., +6.69% over GLADIATOR on PGD at ε=4/255). AGFT also achieves the best out-of-distribution robustness on ImageNet-R (45.21%) and ImageNet-S (29.63%), though TGA-ZSR retains an edge on in-domain ImageNet robust accuracy (52.80 vs. 44.95). Across diverse unseen attacks (15 settings including targeted variants), AGFT leads by 3.6% average over the strongest baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ZSAR line initiated by TeCoA (Mao et al., ICLR 2023), which first framed adversarial fine-tuning of CLIP's image encoder under text-guided cross-entropy supervision. AGFT departs from the classification-guided paradigm shared by TeCoA, PMG-AFT, TGA-ZSR, and GLADIATOR—methods that push features toward a single target class via hard labels—by instead using the pre-trained model's own probabilistic predictions as soft supervision, thereby preserving the relative image-text similarity structure learned during contrastive pre-training. The additional temperature-scaled distribution consistency calibration is a targeted fix for the confidence-scale mismatch that arises when the robust feature space diverges from the original, a problem not explicitly addressed in prior ZSAR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why label-based adversarial fine-tuning degrades zero-shot generalization in VLMs (disruption of cross-modal alignment) and offers a principled remedy that is both effective and computationally cheap (one extra forward pass). This matters for practitioners deploying CLIP-based systems in adversarial settings, where robustness and zero-shot transfer must coexist. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger or non-CLIP VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, Flamingo), to multi-modal generation tasks beyond classification, and to black-box or adaptive threat models where the attacker can observe the fine-tuned weights.
