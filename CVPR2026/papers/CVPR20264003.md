## Problem

Vision-based 3D occupancy prediction suffers from two coupled failures: (1) 2D-to-3D view transformation relies on low-resolution, noisy depth estimates that cause geometric misalignment of voxel features, and (2) semantic categories exhibit strong spatial anisotropy (e.g., pedestrians near road edges, buildings at high elevations), creating a long-tail imbalance that uniform-capacity models cannot resolve. Existing methods treat these issues independently or ignore the spatial clustering structure of rare classes entirely.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T consecutive frames (typically T=4) from 6 calibrated surround-view cameras at 704×256 resolution, along with their intrinsic and extrinsic parameters. A frozen MoGe-2 depth estimator (moge-2-vits-normal) is run in parallel to produce per-pixel depth maps and dense depth features for each view. The output is a 3D semantic occupancy grid of shape 200×200×16×18, covering an 80 m × 80 m × 6.4 m volume around the ego-vehicle at 0.4 m voxel resolution, with 18 semantic classes (16 object categories, empty space, and one additional benchmark class).

## Method

Dr.Occ introduces two complementary modules. First, D²-VFormer leverages MoGe-2 depth to voxelize a pseudo point cloud into a geometry-aware occupancy mask (identifying the ~10% non-empty voxels), then performs three-stage refinement: forward projection with 1/16 downsampling for robustness, backward-projection densification via deformable cross-attention (DCA), and selective depth-guided refinement that fuses depth features into occupied voxels while suppressing empty-space computation. Second, R-EFormer partitions the 3D volume into a 3×3 grid along distance (near/mid/far) and height (low/mid/high), assigning a dedicated DCA-based expert to each region with a router selecting top-K regions; its recursive variant R²-EFormer replaces multiple experts with a single expert applied over three iterations with progressively shrinking masks (100%→75%→50% voxel coverage), adaptively concentrating capacity on the most semantically ambiguous regions. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with ResNet-50 image features, 8-head DCA with 4 reference points, AdamW at lr 1e-4 for 24 epochs, and achieves +7.43% mIoU over BEVDet4D and +1.0% mIoU over COTR on Occ3D-nuScenes.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes, Dr.Occ built on BEVDet4D achieves 43.4% mIoU and 73.45% IoU, improving over the BEVDet4D baseline by +7.43% mIoU and +3.09% IoU. When the same modules are plugged into the SOTA COTR framework, mIoU further increases to 44.1% (+1.0% over COTR's 43.1%). The method outperforms all other publicly available vision-only occupancy methods on the benchmark, with the closest competitor being ViewFormer at 41.9% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dr.Occ (on BEVDet4D, 43.4% mIoU) outperforms all ten compared baselines. The largest gains are over older paradigms: BEVFormer (26.9%), CTF-Occ (28.5%), TPVFormer (27.8%), and SparseOcc (30.9%), where improvements range from +12.5% to +16.5% mIoU. Against the more recent forward-projection method FlashOcc (37.8%) and dual-projection FB-Occ (39.1%), Dr.Occ leads by +5.6% and +4.3% mIoU respectively. The closest competitors are ViewFormer (41.9%) and OSP (41.2%), where Dr.Occ still leads by +1.5% and +2.2% mIoU. Most notably, Dr.Occ surpasses the SOTA COTR (43.1%) by 0.3% mIoU when both use the same BEVDet4D backbone, and when Dr.Occ's modules are integrated into COTR itself, the combined system reaches 44.1% mIoU, confirming plug-and-play generalizability across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dr.Occ sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 2D-to-3D view transformation for occupancy prediction, where it extends the dual-projection paradigm of FBOcc and COTR by replacing their low-resolution learned depth with high-quality dense depth from MoGe-2; (2) Mixture-of-Experts and Mixture-of-Recursions, which it repurposes from token-level routing to spatial region partitioning in 3D voxel space; and (3) the BEVDet4D temporal forward-projection pipeline, which serves as the primary integration backbone. The key conceptual departure is that rather than using external depth directly for geometric lifting (which the authors show paradoxically degrades performance), they convert it into a binary occupancy mask that gates where computation is spent, decoupling geometric guidance from feature transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.43% mIoU gain over BEVDet4D and the additional 1% boost to COTR demonstrate that the depth-mask + region-expert combination is a broadly applicable plug-and-play module for vision-based occupancy prediction, not tied to a single architecture. The observation that ~90% of voxels are empty and that spatial semantic anisotropy (pedestrians near boundaries, vehicles in road centers, vegetation at higher elevations) can be exploited for capacity allocation offers a practical recipe for addressing the long-tail problem without exotic loss functions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the Occ3D-nuScenes benchmark (single dataset, single sensor configuration), how sensitive it is to MoGe-2 depth quality in adverse weather, and whether the recursive R²-EFormer's fixed decreasing mask schedule (100%→75%→50%) can be made fully learned.
