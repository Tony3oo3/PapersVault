## Problem

Object motion blur in static scenes is spatially heterogeneous and fundamentally different from camera-shake blur, yet existing datasets either rely on costly beam-splitting hardware with residual misalignment (ReLoBlur) or synthesize blur via simplified frame accumulation that fails to model the continuous photon-integration process during exposure (GoPro). No prior dataset faithfully emulates the physical exposure formation for object motion blur, creating a domain gap that limits generalization to real-world deblurring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263555/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, OMDNet takes a single 1920×360 RGB blurred image and produces a deblurred image of the same resolution. During training, the network consumes multi-frame supervision: a blurred image, a sharp middle-frame ground truth, and an algorithm-generated blur mask, with additional first/last sharp frames used to supervise optical flow. The dataset provides 20,000+ blur-sharp-mask triplets captured at 500 fps with 98% valid exposure ratio, covering diverse in-plane and out-of-plane object motions against static backgrounds.

## Method

OMDNet employs a U-Net backbone in a single-input, multiple-output configuration where skip connections are replaced by a dual-path Motion-Appearance Extract Block (MAEB) that splits features into an appearance branch (NAFBlock) and a motion branch (Differential Transposed Attention Module, Diff-TAM, inspired by differential amplifiers to suppress background responses). A Flow-guided Gate Predictor (FGP) consumes the motion features to produce bidirectional optical flows and a blur-region confidence gate, supervised by warp and merge constraints that enforce physical consistency with multi-frame ground truth. An Adaptive Gated Fusion (AGF) mechanism uses the blur mask only as a soft prior during training—computing two gated predictions (standard and enhanced) jointly supervised by the sharp ground truth—so that the gate learns to activate in blurred regions while remaining closed on static backgrounds, without requiring pixel-accurate mask annotations at test time. The total loss combines warp, merge, restoration, and gated-restoration terms with weights λ₁=0.05, λ₂=0.4, λ₃=1.0, λ₄=0.4.

## Results

On the OMoBlur test set (1,354 pairs at 1920×360), OMDNet achieves the best score across all six metrics: PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.8891, weighted PSNR 34.04 dB, weighted SSIM 0.8716, LPIPS 0.2171, and DISTS 0.0788. The largest margins appear on blur-region-focused metrics (weighted PSNR +0.49 dB over the next-best LMD-ViT) and perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.2171 vs. 0.2301 for MIMO-UNet; DISTS 0.0788 vs. 0.0889), while global PSNR/SSIM gains are modest (≈0.05 dB / 0.0003), consistent with the design choice to preserve static backgrounds rather than force corrections there. Cross-dataset qualitative evaluation on the real-captured ReLoBlur dataset confirms strong generalization, with OMDNet removing cycloidal wheel trails and fine-texture blur that baselines trained on GoPro or frame-interpolated OMoBlur fail to handle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMDNet outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor is LMD-ViT [20], which leads the field among baselines on global PSNR (35.68) and SSIM (0.8888); OMDNet extends these to 35.73 and 0.8891, but the decisive gap is on weighted PSNR (34.04 vs. 33.55, +0.49 dB) and weighted SSIM (0.8716 vs. 0.8660), indicating substantially better restoration specifically within blurred regions. On perceptual quality, OMDNet's LPIPS of 0.2171 beats the next-best MIMO-UNet (0.2301) by 0.013 and its DISTS of 0.0788 beats MIMO-UNet (0.0889) by 0.010, the largest relative margins in the table. LBAG [19] and Restormer [37] cluster tightly around 35.55 PSNR / 0.888 SSIM with weighted PSNR near 33.2, while NAFNet [4] and EVSSM [16] trail slightly. In the cross-dataset generalization study on ReLoBlur, models trained on GoPro (MIMO-UNet, Restormer) produce visible artifacts or minimal restoration, and models trained on the frame-interpolated OMoBlur-FI variant fail on rotation and out-of-plane motion, whereas OMDNet trained on the physically faithful OMoBlur handles all motion types cleanly—demonstrating that the dataset's exposure-integration modeling, not just network architecture, drives the generalization advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper upgrades the multi-frame accumulation blur model from Nah et al. (GoPro, CVPR 2017) into a physically faithful time-integrated signal-accumulation formulation, eliminating the unknown inverse camera response by working directly in RAW space and achieving a 98% valid exposure ratio through programmable industrial camera control (vs. ~10% in prior RAW-based work). It departs from kernel-based synthesis (which cannot model occlusion dynamics or kinematic constraints) and from beam-splitting real-capture datasets like ReLoBlur (which suffer from residual misalignment). On the method side, OMDNet builds on the NAFNet/U-Net backbone but introduces a differential transposed attention module inspired by differential transformers and an adaptive gated fusion mechanism that replaces the hard mask supervision used in LBAG with a soft-prior training strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OMoBlur fills a critical gap as the first large-scale (20K+ pairs), physically grounded dataset for object motion blur, and its cross-dataset generalization to real-captured ReLoBlur blur validates that the synthetic-to-real gap can be substantially narrowed through faithful exposure modeling. The finding that frame-interpolated blur is visually indistinguishable from physically-grounded blur yet generalizes poorly highlights that distributional fidelity, not just perceptual similarity, governs transfer. Open questions include whether the methodology extends to higher resolutions (the current 1920×360 ROI limits applicability), how well it handles the severely ill-posed out-of-plane motion cases, and whether the multi-frame training supervision can be relaxed for more practical single-frame settings.
