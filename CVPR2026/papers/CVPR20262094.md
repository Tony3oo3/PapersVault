## Problem

Whole-body pose estimators trained on full-body datasets lack the hand pose diversity needed for accurate finger articulation, while hand-only estimators trained on hand-centric datasets produce detailed hands but lack global body awareness. Naively attaching hand-only outputs to a body mesh yields anatomically implausible wrist orientations, especially under occlusion or two-hand interaction, because the hand model estimates wrist pose independently of the upper-body kinematic chain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and operates fully feed-forward. Hand regions are localized and cropped to 256×256 for the WiLoR hand estimator, while the full image feeds the SMPLer-X whole-body estimator. The output is a complete SMPL-X parameter set (body, hand, face pose and shape) plus a MANO hand mesh whose finger articulations and shape are transferred from the hand estimator and rigidly aligned to the SMPL-X wrist, with Laplacian smoothing applied at the hand–wrist boundary to reduce seam artifacts.

## Method

CHAM (Conditional Hands Modulator) is a lightweight adapter that injects hand-specific ViT features from a frozen WiLoR estimator into the 24 transformer blocks of a frozen SMPLer-X backbone via additive fusion, enabling anatomically coherent wrist orientation without retraining either model. The hand features pass through 2D positional encoding (preserving global spatial context) and a 3-layer cross-attention encoder (active only when both hands are detected) before being split into left/right branches, each containing 24 zero-initialized 1×1 convolutions matched to SMPLer-X blocks; an inverse affine transform restores spatial alignment and element-wise max merges the two hands. For finger detail, MANO parameters from WiLoR generate a canonical hand mesh that is differentially rigidly aligned to the SMPL-X wrist using the wrist and four MCP joints, replacing the body model's hand vertices while allowing gradients to flow back through CHAM. During training, only CHAM is optimized on a mix of full-body and hand-only datasets with pose, shape, 2D/3D keypoint, and root-pose regularization losses, preserving the generalization of both frozen backbones.

## Results

On full-body datasets, Hand4Whole++ achieves 76.84/49.71 MPVPE (full/hands) on AGORA, 45.95/25.03 on ARCTIC, and 61.24/33.43 on EHF, outperforming SMPLer-X (85.61/52.31, 56.06/31.48, 63.26/46.21 respectively). On hand-only datasets, the gains are dramatic: 9.40/32.30 MPVPE/MRRPE on IH26M, 7.98/16.37 on ReIH, and 17.72/29.09 on HIC, compared to SMPLer-X's 38.64/119.56, 58.86/101.82, and 32.43/67.68. Against hand-only specialists, Hand4Whole++ matches or beats MPVPE (e.g., 7.98 vs. WiLoR's 8.09 on ReIH) while reducing MRRPE by 2–3 orders of magnitude (e.g., 32.30 vs. WiLoR's 94817.41 on IH26M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hand4Whole++ outperforms all three full-body baselines (SMPLer-X, Hand4Whole, OSX) on every dataset for both full-body and hand MPVPE. The largest gains appear on hand-only datasets: on IH26M, hand MPVPE drops from 38.64 (SMPLer-X) to 9.40, and MRRPE from 119.56 to 32.30; on ReIH, from 58.86/101.82 to 7.98/16.37. Against hand-only specialists (Table 5), Hand4Whole++ achieves competitive per-hand MPVPE (9.40 vs. IntagHand's 9.03 on IH26M; 7.98 vs. WiLoR's 8.09 on ReIH) while dramatically reducing MRRPE—e.g., 32.30 vs. IntagHand's 48.04 on IH26M and 16.37 vs. InterWild's 22.38 on ReIH—reflecting superior relative hand positioning from full-body context. On EHF (unseen domain), the hand MPVPE improvement over SMPLer-X is 12.78 mm (46.21→33.43), confirming generalization. The fine-tuned SMPLer-X baseline overfits to hand-centric training data, degrading full-body accuracy on EHF (126.34 vs. 61.24), whereas CHAM preserves the frozen backbone's generalization while still improving hands.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hand4Whole++ sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: whole-body pose estimation via SMPL-X regression (SMPLer-X, OSX, FrankMocap) and hand-centric 3D reconstruction (WiLoR, HaMeR, IntagHand). Architecturally, CHAM directly adapts the ControlNet paradigm—zero-initialized convolutional branches additively fused into a frozen ViT backbone—from image generation to pose estimation, while the finger/shape transfer step extends the joint-level fusion strategy of the authors' earlier Hand4Whole. The key departure from prior adapters (HMR-Adapter, DOPE) is the use of an *external* hand model's features as conditional signals rather than interpolating from the whole-body model's own internal representations, which are often uninformative in hand-centric crops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, freeze-and-modulate design offers a practical recipe for bridging the supervision gap between whole-body and hand-only datasets without expensive joint training or full retraining, making it attractive for practitioners who already have strong pre-trained estimators. A notable side effect is that CHAM improves not only hand accuracy but also upstream upper-body joints (shoulder, elbow), suggesting that hand-specific cues carry useful kinematic information for the entire arm chain. Limitations include the doubled inference cost from running two pre-trained models, weak supervision of non-hand joints on hand-only data, and reliance on a hand detector for cropping. Open questions include generalization to egocentric and heavily occluded settings, and whether the approach extends to other body parts (feet, face) with analogous part-specific estimators.
