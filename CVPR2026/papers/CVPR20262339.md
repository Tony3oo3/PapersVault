## Problem

Existing handwriting trajectory reconstruction methods reproduce writing paths to capture overall character structure but ignore stroke contour (varying width), causing generated handwriting to look like uniform-width printed text rather than natural human writing. This limitation is compounded by the absence of large-scale datasets with simultaneous trajectory, stroke-order, and contour annotations, making supervised training for contour-aware reconstruction infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single character image (256×256 for CHTR-110K, 64×64 for CASIA-OLHWDB) and autoregressively produces a variable-length trajectory sequence where each point is a 6-D vector: 2D coordinates (x, y), stroke width w, and three one-hot pen states (down, up, end). The sequence inherently encodes stroke order through the pen-state transitions, and the per-point width captures the natural width variation of each stroke. For robot deployment, the trajectory is further converted into a refined control sequence via a brush model and reinforcement learning pipeline.

## Method

G-HTR replaces pixel-level image encoding with an explicit contour-aware character graph: the input image is thinned to a skeleton, skeleton points are clustered into key points, and each node is assigned a feature vector [x, y, w] where w is the shortest distance to the character contour; edges encode topological connectivity between adjacent key points. A multi-scale graph encoder stacks graph blocks (each combining a multi-head Graph Attention layer for global structural relationships and a Graph Convolutional layer for local topology), with graph coarsening between blocks to produce features at progressively coarser scales. A multi-scale aggregation decoder then autoregressively generates the trajectory by cross-attending a query (concatenated image feature from a ResNet18+self-attention encoder and previously generated points) over the multi-scale graph features. Training is supervised with an L1 regression loss on (x, y, w) plus a cross-entropy loss on the three pen states, weighted by λ=0.5.

## Results

On the full CHTR-110K test set, G-HTR achieves mIOU 0.641, DTW 12.765, FID 1.228, LPIPS 0.066, and HWD 1.218, outperforming the second-best method (TrajFormer) by 16.12% in mIOU, 27.69% in DTW, 16.75% in FID, 22.83% in LPIPS, and 20.13% in HWD. On the Font subset, G-HTR reaches mIOU 0.750 vs. TrajFormer's 0.643; on the Handwriting subset, 0.633 vs. 0.542. On the public CASIA-OLHWDB dataset, G-HTR also leads with mIOU 0.530, DTW 16.264, and FID 1.050, surpassing TrajFormer (0.445, 23.892, 1.121).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-HTR decisively outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both CHTR-110K and CASIA-OLHWDB. The closest competitor, TrajFormer [24], is surpassed by 16.12% in mIOU (0.552→0.641) and 27.69% in DTW (18.277→12.765) on the full CHTR-110K test set, indicating substantially better contour fidelity and trajectory alignment. The earlier encoder-decoder methods (Cross-VAE, DED-Net, PEN-Net) lag far behind, with mIOU values below 0.17 and DTW above 34, reflecting their inability to model stroke contours or global character structure. Notably, even after the authors modified these baselines to incorporate stroke-width modeling (Table 4), the gap to G-HTR remains large, confirming that the multi-scale graph architecture—not merely the addition of a width channel—is the key differentiator. On CASIA-OLHWDB, G-HTR improves mIOU by 19.1% over TrajFormer (0.445→0.530) and DTW by 31.9% (23.892→16.264), demonstrating generalization beyond the authors' own dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pixel-level encoding paradigm in handwriting trajectory reconstruction (e.g., TrajFormer, PEN-Net, FINet), which models local pixel relationships but overlooks the intrinsic topological structure of characters. Instead, G-HTR represents characters as contour-aware graphs (skeleton key points as nodes, topological connections as edges) and processes them with a multi-scale GNN encoder combining graph attention and graph convolution layers. The multi-scale graph learning strategy, inspired by hierarchical graph coarsening, is a novel architectural choice for this task. The paper also introduces CHTR-110K, the first dataset providing simultaneous trajectory, stroke-order, and contour annotations, filling a gap that made prior supervised contour-aware learning infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CHTR-110K dataset (110,540 samples with contour annotations) is a valuable resource that enables a class of previously intractable experiments in contour-aware handwriting synthesis, and its semi-automatic annotation pipeline (achieving 0.972 mean IOU) offers a reproducible recipe for scaling annotations. The practical deployment on a calligraphy robot demonstrates a concrete path from trajectory generation to embodied writing, which is underexplored in the literature. Limitations include failure on rare characters outside GB2312-80 due to training data scarcity, and the reliance on a thinning-based graph construction that may struggle with highly stylized or overlapping strokes. Open questions include generalization to other writing systems (Arabic, Japanese), handling of connected cursive text, and whether the graph formulation scales to longer multi-character sequences.
