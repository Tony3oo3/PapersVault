## Problem

Object hallucination in LVLMs arises when strong language priors override weak visual evidence, but existing mitigation methods face a fundamental trade-off: contrastive decoding requires expensive reference models or multiple forward passes, while static subspace editing lacks token-level adaptability and risks suppressing genuine visual signals. No prior approach provides a mathematically guaranteed decoupling between prior suppression and visual evidence preservation within a single inference pass.

## Input / Output

HulluEdit operates as a drop-in inference-time intervention on any pre-trained LVLM: it consumes the standard RGB image and text prompt, then intercepts hidden states at a designated edit layer (final transformer layer) during autoregressive decoding. Visual features are extracted once per image from a mid-layer anchor (e.g., layer 26 in LLaVA) and cached, while a sliding-window text cache of non-visual hidden states is maintained across decoding steps. The output is the model's usual text caption or VQA answer, but with hidden states edited before the output projection, requiring no additional forward passes or auxiliary models.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes each hidden state h into three orthogonal subspaces: a sample-adaptive visual evidence subspace U (estimated online via weighted SVD of visual tokens, where weights are softmax-normalized cosine similarities to the current hidden state), an anti-prior subspace P (constructed from the text cache projected into U's orthogonal complement, enforcing U^T P = 0 by construction), and a residual uncertainty subspace R. At the edit layer, a closed-form minimum-norm solution applies adaptive shrinkage to h_P and h_R while preserving h_U exactly, with shrinkage strengths scheduled inversely to the Visual Certainty Ratio and proportionally to the Prior Conflict Ratio. A certificate-aware gate activates editing only when VCR falls below a threshold or PCR exceeds one, preventing unnecessary intervention on well-grounded tokens. The orthogonality constraint provides a theoretical guarantee that prior suppression cannot damage visual grounding, and the entire pipeline adds less than 2% overhead over the base transformer layer.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark, HulluEdit achieves the highest Accuracy and F1 across all 18 evaluation settings (3 models × 3 splits). On the most challenging Adversarial split, it reaches 82.5% Acc / 83.4% F1 on LLaVA-1.5-7B, 82.7% / 84.0% on LLaVA-1.5-13B, and 84.3% / 84.2% on Qwen-VL-Chat-7B, outperforming the strongest baseline (VAF) by 2.4–3.9 points in accuracy. On CHAIR (MSCOCO captioning), HulluEdit achieves CHAIR_i of 4.18 and CHAIR_s of 13.00 on LLaVA-1.5, and 3.35 / 13.60 on mPLUG-Owl2, setting new state-of-the-art while preserving BLEU scores within 0.2–0.5 of the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HulluEdit outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary POPE benchmark across every model and split. Against contrastive decoding methods (VCD, ICD, DoLa), it gains 2–5 points on the Adversarial split where language priors are most confounding; notably, DeCo degrades severely on LLaVA-1.5-13B (72.6% Acc) while HulluEdit maintains 82.7%. Against subspace editing (Nullu) and visual attention enhancement (VAF), HulluEdit leads by 1.4–2.4 points on Adversarial POPE and by 1.1–2.4 CHAIR_i on captioning. On CHAIR, HulluEdit surpasses the strongest prior methods (Nullu: 5.30, HALC: 5.72) by 1.1–2.5 points in CHAIR_i on LLaVA-1.5, and by 2.4–3.7 points on mPLUG-Owl2. The only trade-off appears on MME Count (105.0 vs. 118.33 baseline), while Existence, Position, and Color all improve, confirming that the orthogonal decomposition selectively targets prior-driven hallucinations without harming visual grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

HulluEdit sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: contrastive/dynamic decoding methods (VCD, DoLa, DeCo) that calibrate logits using secondary signals, and static subspace editing (Nullu) that projects out dataset-level hallucination directions. It departs from both by introducing an online, sample-adaptive orthogonal decomposition—weighted SVD over visual tokens for the evidence subspace and SVD over a text cache in the visual complement for the anti-prior subspace—so that suppression of linguistic priors is mathematically guaranteed not to perturb visual grounding. The mid-layer anchoring idea is borrowed from DeCo, but HulluEdit replaces DeCo's coarse logit-level correction with a closed-form hidden-state edit that requires no reference model or extra forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orthogonality guarantee (U⊤P = 0) gives a clean theoretical separation that static subspace methods lack, and the single-pass, reference-free design makes the approach directly deployable without engineering overhead. However, the MME Count regression (−13.33) reveals that fine-grained numeric reasoning may reside in the residual subspace that the method conservatively shrinks, suggesting the decomposition does not yet capture all forms of visual evidence. Open questions include whether the fixed subspace ranks (r=8, q=5) generalize to larger models or video inputs, and whether the certificate-aware gating thresholds transfer across domains without recalibration.
