## Problem

Controllable, high-fidelity 3D mesh editing remains an open problem: SDS-based methods lack multi-view consistency and produce oversimplified geometry, while MVD-based methods fail to preserve the original mesh's structure and texture. Mesh-based generative editing has received far less attention than neural field editing, despite meshes being the dominant representation in professional 3D pipelines, and no existing method achieves seamless geometric and textural integration between edited and unedited regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260923/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original textured 3D mesh and a user intent (text prompt or drag arrows on a rendered view) as input. It renders a reference image from the mesh, edits it with a 2D model (e.g., FLUX Kontext or LightningDrag), lifts the edited image to 3D via an image-to-mesh generator (MeshyAI, Hunyuan3D 2.5, or Trellis), and outputs a complete edited mesh with PBR texture. The framework is model-agnostic for both the 2D editing and 3D generation backends, and supports insertion, deletion, and localized region editing tasks.

## Method

CraftMesh decomposes mesh editing into an image-editing → mesh-generation → seamless-fusion pipeline. The core technical contribution is a Poisson Seamless Fusion strategy operating on a hybrid SDF/Mesh representation: (1) Poisson Geometry Blending merges the edited region mesh with the original via Boolean operations, then optimizes a neural SDF guided by normal maps blended using the classical Poisson Image Editing algorithm, reducing the Poisson solve from O(n³) volumetric to O(kn²) 2D image domain; (2) Poisson Texture Harmonization fuses textures using three losses — a gradient-consistency term preserving high-frequency detail, a distance-weighted boundary loss for smooth transitions, and a novel distribution-aware color alignment loss that minimizes the discrepancy between kernel density estimates of RGB color distributions in the new and preserved regions. This avoids the incompatible connectivity and discontinuous gradient problems of classical Poisson Mesh Editing while achieving seamless geometric and textural integration.

## Results

On a 100-model evaluation set (3 editing prompts per model), CraftMesh (MeshyAI backend) achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CLIPsim 20.801, CLIPdir 18.479, NIQE 4.710, and NIMA 5.928. The largest margin is on CLIPdir (editing effectiveness), where CraftMesh scores 18.479 versus the next-best VoxHammer at 10.482, a gap of nearly 8 points. On perceptual quality (NIQE, lower is better), CraftMesh reaches 4.710, substantially outperforming the best baseline MagicClay (7.344). The ablation confirms that both Poisson Geometry Blending and Poisson Texture Harmonization contribute independently, with the full pipeline yielding the strongest combined performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CraftMesh is compared against four baselines spanning three paradigms: SDS-based (FocalDreamer, MagicClay), MVD-based (Instant3dit), and latent-based (VoxHammer). CraftMesh wins on all four metrics against every baseline. The closest competitor is VoxHammer, which leads the baselines on CLIPsim (17.366) and CLIPdir (10.482), but CraftMesh still surpasses it by +3.4 on CLIPsim and +8.0 on CLIPdir. Among SDS-based methods, MagicClay is the strongest on NIQE (7.344) and NIMA (5.334), yet CraftMesh improves NIQE by 2.6 points and NIMA by 0.6 points. FocalDreamer and Instant3dit are clearly outperformed across the board, with FocalDreamer scoring the worst NIQE (12.340) and the lowest CLIPdir (3.927), indicating poor editing effectiveness and perceptual quality. Qualitatively, the paper notes that baselines produce coarse geometry, flat colors, and unintended alterations to unedited regions, whereas CraftMesh preserves original structure while introducing harmonious new details.

## Relation to Prior Work

CraftMesh departs from both SDS-based editing (FocalDreamer, MagicClay) and MVD-based editing (Instant3dit, CMD) by reframing mesh editing as a three-stage pipeline: 2D image editing, 3D mesh generation, and Poisson-based fusion. It builds directly on MagicClay's hybrid SDF/Mesh representation and implicit neural color field as its optimization backbone, and on the classical Poisson Image Editing algorithm as the blending mechanism. The key conceptual departure from prior Poisson-based mesh editing (Yu et al. 2004) is solving the Poisson equation in the SDF/gradient domain rather than the coordinate domain, avoiding the gradient discontinuities at boundaries that plague coordinate-domain approaches. Unlike SeamlessNeRF and GaussianStitching, which blend implicit radiance fields or Gaussians, CraftMesh explicitly fuses mesh geometry and texture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in 3D content pipelines where artists need part-level mesh control without disturbing unrelated geometry, and demonstrates that combining off-the-shelf 2D and 3D generative models with a Poisson fusion strategy outperforms direct 3D optimization approaches by a substantial margin in both semantic alignment and structural preservation. The model-agnostic design means improvements in 2D editing or 3D generation backends will automatically transfer. However, the method inherits limitations of its constituent models and struggles with complex topologies (open surfaces, multiple layers, noisy meshes), and the evaluation on 100 models with 3 prompts each, while diverse, is modest in scale. Open questions include whether the SDF-domain Poisson fusion generalizes to non-manifold or multi-component meshes and whether the pipeline latency (~6 min on a 4090 GPU) is acceptable for interactive workflows.
