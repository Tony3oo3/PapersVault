## Problem

Conventional knowledge distillation assumes balanced training data, so when the teacher is trained on a long-tailed distribution it becomes biased toward head classes and provides unreliable supervision for tail classes. The student inherits this bias, overfitting to frequent classes while receiving little meaningful guidance on rare ones. No prior work addressed this failure mode from the model-compression perspective, i.e., distilling a smaller student from a biased teacher under class imbalance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of pre-trained CNN models (teacher and student) that both process the same RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produce C-dimensional softmax probability vectors. The distillation loss operates on these probability vectors by decomposing them into a 3-dimensional cross-group distribution (aggregated probabilities over head, medium, and tail class groups) and per-group within-group distributions, rather than on raw logits or intermediate features.

## Method

LTKD first decomposes the standard KL-divergence KD objective into a cross-group term (KL between the teacher's and student's aggregated group-level probability vectors) and a weighted within-group term (sum of per-group KL divergences, each weighted by the teacher's cross-group probability for that group). To counteract the teacher's head-class bias, it introduces two corrections: (1) a rebalanced cross-group loss that computes per-batch scaling factors to equalize the teacher's group-level probabilities before normalization, and (2) a reweighted within-group loss that replaces the teacher-derived group weights with a uniform constant β so all groups contribute equally to the gradient. The final LTKD objective is α·KL(p̂_G^T ‖ p_G^S) + β·Σ_G KL(p̃_G^T ‖ p̃_G^S), where p̂_G^T is the rebalanced teacher cross-group distribution and p̃_G^T/p̃_G^S are the within-group normalized distributions. The framework is architecture-agnostic and requires no changes to the student network, only a modified loss computed from the two models' softmax outputs.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (γ=100), LTKD achieves 27.21% tail accuracy and 51.08% overall accuracy with a ResNet32×4–ResNet8×4 pair, improving tail accuracy by +12.12 points over the best baseline (ReviewKD at 15.09%). On TinyImageNet-LT (γ=100), LTKD reaches 10.48% tail and 36.21% overall (ResNet32×4–ResNet8×4), and on ImageNet-LT (γ=20), it attains 45.88% tail and 60.80% overall (ResNet34–ResNet18). Across all three datasets and all imbalance factors, LTKD consistently surpasses the teacher model's own accuracy in nearly every setting, with tail-accuracy gains ranging from +1.06% (ImageNet-LT, γ=5) to +12.12% (CIFAR-100-LT, γ=100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTKD outperforms all five baselines (KD, DKD, ReviewKD, DIST, CAT-KD) on every dataset, every imbalance factor, and every architecture pair tested. The largest margins appear on tail-class accuracy: on CIFAR-100-LT (γ=100), LTKD beats the strongest baseline (ReviewKD) by +12.12 points (27.21% vs. 15.09%) with ResNet32×4–ResNet8×4, and by +8.59 points (21.04% vs. 12.45%) with ResNet50–MobileNetV2. On TinyImageNet-LT (γ=100), LTKD leads by +1.39 to +4.36 points on tail accuracy depending on the pair, and on ImageNet-LT (γ=20) it gains +2.40 to +3.20 points over the best baseline. Notably, LTKD surpasses the teacher model's own accuracy in nearly all settings (e.g., 27.21% vs. 15.28% teacher tail accuracy on CIFAR-100-LT γ=100), demonstrating that the student can learn a more balanced representation than the biased teacher. The gains are consistent across both homogeneous (e.g., ResNet→ResNet) and heterogeneous (e.g., WRN→ShuffleNet, ResNet50→MobileNetV2) architecture pairs, confirming that the method is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Hinton's KL-divergence-based knowledge distillation [16] by decomposing the standard KD objective into cross-group and within-group components, revealing how class imbalance distorts each term. It departs from prior logit-based KD methods (DKD, DIST, CAT-KD, ReviewKD) that implicitly assume balanced training data, and from long-tailed KD approaches (BKD, LFME) that focus on classification accuracy rather than model compression. The authors position their work as the first to tackle KD under long-tailed distributions explicitly from a compression perspective, using the KL decomposition to motivate targeted rebalancing and reweighting corrections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: real-world deployment often involves imbalanced data and resource-constrained models, and standard KD silently inherits teacher bias, degrading tail-class performance. The finding that a simple rebalancing/reweighting of the KL terms lets the student consistently outperform the biased teacher (by up to +12% on tail accuracy) suggests that much of the 'lost' knowledge in imbalanced settings is recoverable through loss redesign rather than data augmentation or retraining. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CNN architectures (transformers, LLMs), to tasks beyond classification (detection, segmentation), and to more complex imbalance structures (multi-modal, hierarchical labels).
