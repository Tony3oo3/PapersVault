## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods either extend 2D generative models (score distillation, multi-view synthesis) at the cost of poor geometric consistency, or attempt to learn geometry-centric VAEs from scratch, which is hindered by the scarcity of large-scale 3D ground truth. Feed-forward reconstruction models like VGGT already encode rich multi-view geometry in a compact token space, but this latent manifold has not been leveraged as a generative foundation. The paper asks whether the intrinsic latent space of a reconstruction model can directly serve as the geometry latent for diffusion-based 3D scene generation.

## Input / Output

The method accepts 1 or 2 RGB input views (or a full image sequence for reconstruction), an optional text prompt, and optional per-view camera parameters; missing conditioning frames are zeroed out with binary masks. All images are processed at 560×560 resolution with 49-frame video clips. Outputs include a temporally coherent RGB video, globally consistent point clouds (obtained by unprojecting per-view depth maps with generated camera parameters), per-view depth maps, and camera extrinsics/intrinsics. The joint latent representation concatenates appearance and geometry latents along the width dimension (n×h×2w×c), keeping them disentangled yet distributionally aligned.

## Method

Gen3R recasts VGGT as an asymmetric geometry VAE by training a lightweight adapter (causal autoencoder) that maps VGGT's intermediate transformer tokens (from blocks 4, 11, 17, 23) into a compact geometric latent G matching the spatial-temporal resolution and channel dimension (c=16) of the Wan2.1 video diffusion model's appearance latent A. A key design choice is a KL-divergence alignment loss that regularizes the geometry latent distribution toward the pretrained RGB latent distribution, since simple compression alone leaves the two spaces incompatible for joint diffusion. The video diffusion model is then fine-tuned to jointly denoise the concatenated latent Z=[A;G] under flexible conditioning (1-view, 2-view, or full-sequence with optional camera cues), requiring no additional trainable parameters beyond the adapter. At inference, the appearance latent is decoded by the pretrained RGB VAE while the geometry latent is mapped back through the adapter to VGGT tokens and decoded by its DPT heads into point clouds, depth, and camera parameters.

## Results

On 1-view appearance generation, Gen3R achieves PSNR 20.51 / SSIM 0.7388 / LPIPS 0.2281 on RealEstate10K and PSNR 16.38 / SSIM 0.5821 / LPIPS 0.4234 on DL3DV-10K, outperforming all baselines. On 2-view generation, it reaches PSNR 27.05 / SSIM 0.8732 / LPIPS 0.1352 on RealEstate10K. For geometry generation, Gen3R achieves Chamfer Distance of 1.1047 (1-view) and 0.9767 (2-view) on Co3Dv2, substantially outperforming Aether (1.9498 / 1.5520) and WVD (1.6137 / 1.7081). On feed-forward reconstruction, Gen3R matches or slightly improves upon VGGT's CD (0.9625 vs 0.9632 on Co3Dv2) while correcting geometric artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 2D generation baselines, Gen3R clearly outperforms Gen3C (PSNR 20.51 vs 20.26 on 1-view RE10K; 27.05 vs 23.83 on 2-view) and GF (20.51 vs 16.32 on 1-view), while LVSM achieves higher 2-view PSNR (29.58 vs 27.05) as a non-generative interpolation model but produces blurred results in over-exposed scenes and degrades sharply in the 1-view setting. Among explicit 3D generation methods, Gen3R surpasses both Aether and WVD on all appearance metrics (e.g., PSNR 20.51 vs 16.57/17.62 on 1-view RE10K) and all geometry metrics (CD 1.1047 vs 1.9498/1.6137 on 1-view Co3Dv2), demonstrating the advantage of aligning reconstruction-model latents with diffusion latents rather than compressing 3D outputs. DepthSplat, the only reconstruction-based baseline (2-view only), is slightly outperformed on PSNR (27.05 vs 26.67) and significantly on VBench I.Q. (0.6025 vs 0.4379). As a reconstruction reference, VGGT achieves marginally better CD on Co3Dv2 (0.9632 vs 0.9625) but suffers from lower completeness in generation settings since it does not synthesize geometry for novel views; Gen3R's generative model also corrects VGGT's floaters, yielding cleaner depth in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gen3R departs from prior 3D scene generation methods that either optimize 3D representations via score distillation, synthesize multi-view images before reconstruction, or train geometry-centric VAEs from scratch (which is hampered by the scarcity of 3D ground truth). Its key conceptual move is to repurpose VGGT's intermediate transformer tokens as geometric latents, treating the reconstruction model as an asymmetric VAE rather than compressing its explicit 3D outputs as Aether and WVD do. The KL-divergence alignment between geometry and appearance latents, followed by joint diffusion fine-tuning of Wan2.1, distinguishes it from Geometry Forcing, which instead aligns intermediate diffusion features to the reconstruction model during training. This positions Gen3R at the intersection of feed-forward reconstruction (VGGT, Dust3R) and video diffusion (Wan2.1), unifying two previously separate research threads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical and principled recipe for coupling reconstruction and generation models, showing that the latent manifold of a strong reconstruction model can serve as a foundation for high-fidelity 3D scene generation without requiring explicit 3D supervision. The mutual-benefit finding—that jointly modeling appearance and geometry improves reconstruction robustness over VGGT alone—suggests a broader principle that generative priors can regularize deterministic predictors. Limitations include the 560×560 resolution and 49-frame constraint, dependence on 300k+ calibrated multi-view scenes for training, and the fact that geometry quality in novel-view regions still lags behind full multi-view reconstruction. Open questions include whether this adapter-based approach generalizes to other reconstruction backbones, scales to longer sequences or higher resolutions, and can be extended to dynamic scenes.
