## Problem

Existing test-time alignment (TTA) methods for diffusion models operate in unstructured latent or noise spaces, leading to either reward under-optimisation (weak target-reward gains) or over-optimisation (reward hacking via non-semantic noise patterns that degrade other quality metrics and diversity). No prior TTA approach performs optimisation in the structured semantic space of the text-conditioning manifold, which would naturally constrain updates to coherent directions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261273/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt, a target reward function (differentiable scores like HPSv2, PickScore, Aesthetic, or non-differentiable objectives like JPEG file size), and a frozen pretrained diffusion model (SD v1.5 or SDXL), the method produces a single reward-aligned image at 512×512 resolution over 100 denoising steps. No model parameters are updated; only the null-text embedding (a single vector in the CLIP text encoder's output space) is optimised per prompt, and the reward function is queried at each denoising step via Tweedie's posterior mean estimate of the clean sample.

## Method

Null-TTA optimises the unconditional (null-text) embedding φ′ in Classifier-Free Guidance rather than latent or noise variables, exploiting the fact that the text encoder's embedding space is semantically structured and thus provides implicit manifold regularisation against reward hacking. The objective is a KL-regularised reward maximisation: maximise E[R(x₀)] over the generative distribution induced by φ′, penalised by the KL divergence between the full denoising trajectories under φ′ and the original φ, plus a Gaussian KL term on the embedding itself. Using the Markov property and Gaussian structure of DDPM transitions, this decomposes into a closed-form per-step loss involving the squared difference of CFG noise predictions and the embedding L2 distance, with adaptive annealing of the regularisation weight λ₂ across timesteps. A greedy search mechanism draws K candidate latents at each step and retains only the highest-reward one, further steering the trajectory. Backpropagation flows only through cross-attention layers (not the full U-Net), keeping GPU memory at ~17.6 GB and enabling efficient scaling with optimisation steps.

## Results

On SD-v1.5 with PickScore as the target reward, Null-TTA achieves 0.315 PickScore, 0.294 HPSv2, 5.431 Aesthetic, and 0.946 ImageReward, outperforming all baselines on every metric simultaneously. On SDXL, Null-TTA (n_max=45) reaches 0.282 PickScore and 1.276 ImageReward while maintaining 0.293 HPSv2 and 5.613 Aesthetic. Computationally, Null-TTA uses only 17,585 MB GPU memory (vs. 30,595 MB for DAS and 20,449 MB for DNO) and completes generation in 4 min 33 s at n_max=25, faster than both DAS (4 min 48 s) and DNO (19 min 38 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Null-TTA dominates all five baselines on the primary PickScore-target evaluation (Table 1): it beats DNO by +0.026 PickScore and +0.550 ImageReward, and beats DAS by +0.057 PickScore and +0.075 ImageReward, while also improving Aesthetic over both (5.431 vs. 5.075 for DNO, 5.382 for DAS). On SDXL (Table 4), Null-TTA (n_max=45) surpasses DAS on PickScore (0.282 vs. 0.253) and ImageReward (1.276 vs. 1.260), and substantially outperforms DNO on ImageReward (1.276 vs. 0.057). In the Pareto-front analysis (Figure 1), Null-TTA shifts the entire frontier outward relative to DNO, DPS, DSearch, and TDPO, indicating that increasing optimisation intensity (n_max) degrades held-out rewards only weakly, whereas DNO exhibits clear over-optimisation and DPS/TDPO under-optimise the target. Computationally, Null-TTA is the most memory-efficient (17,585 MB) and at n_max=25 is faster than both DAS and DNO, with runtime scaling linearly as n_max increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the null-text embedding optimisation principle from Null-Text Inversion (NTI) [19], which was originally designed for fine-grained image editing, into a general reward-alignment mechanism for text-to-image diffusion models. It departs from all existing TTA methods (DNO, DPS, DAS, DSearch, TDPO) that operate in unstructured latent or noise spaces by shifting optimisation to the structured semantic manifold of the CLIP text encoder, where the null-text embedding in Classifier-Free Guidance serves as the anchor for the generative distribution. The KL-regularised objective is derived from the Markov structure of the diffusion process, yielding a closed-form per-step regulariser that penalises deviation of the denoising trajectory from the pretrained model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that a single low-dimensional semantic-space optimisation can Pareto-dominate high-dimensional noise-space methods on both target and held-out rewards, this work offers a principled and computationally efficient (lowest GPU memory, fastest wall-clock) alternative for inference-time alignment that practitioners can adopt without fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the single-trajectory Monte Carlo approximation and greedy search component remain sufficient for more complex multi-objective or video/3D generation settings, and how the method behaves when the reward model itself is poorly calibrated or adversarially designed. The zeroth-order extension to non-differentiable rewards is promising but only validated on a single proxy task (JPEG compressibility).
