## Problem

Existing IQA models used as reward signals in RLHF for real-world super-resolution output only a single global score and are insensitive to local, fine-grained distortions, causing generators to exploit the coarse signal and produce images with visible local artifacts that still receive high rewards (reward hacking). While fine-grained reward models could address this, their high-variance spatially-complex signals introduce severe training instability, creating a stability-robustness dilemma that has not been resolved.

## Input / Output

The generator consumes a low-resolution RGB image and sampled noise/latent to produce a 4× super-resolved image. FinPercep-RM takes the super-resolved image and outputs two complementary signals: a scalar Fine-grained Calibrated Global score (S_fgc-global) and a spatial Perceptual Degradation Map (M_fg-pdm) at the same resolution as the input, where pixel values in [0,1] indicate local defect likelihood and intensity. The FGR-30k training dataset provides (I_syn, M_gt) pairs—synthesized distorted images paired with ground-truth degradation maps—constructed from 30,000 region-swapped samples across diverse real-world scenes.

## Method

FinPercep-RM uses an encoder-decoder architecture: a CLIP-IQA backbone extracts multi-scale features, a decoder with upsampling and cross-layer fusion reconstructs a spatial Perceptual Degradation Map, and the global score is computed by element-wise modulating the deepest encoder feature with the bilinearly-interpolated degradation map before an MLP head—forcing the score to be explicitly sensitive to local defects. Training combines an L1 heatmap loss, a triplet ranking loss (ensuring S_GT > S_syn > S_SR), and an anchor alignment loss that pins the GT score to the original IQA model's output to prevent score drift across curriculum stages. The Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning (CCL) mechanism resolves the stability-robustness trade-off by co-evolving both models: the RM progressively expands from a simple global IQA (RM_0) to the full fine-grained model (RM_N) by incrementally adding decoder parameters and encoder adapter modules, while the generator's RL training (via REFL, DPO, or GRPO) starts on the stable RM_0 reward and migrates to increasingly strict RM_k versions, achieving easy-to-hard convergence that suppresses reward hacking without sacrificing training stability.

## Results

On RealLQ250, DIT4SR with FinPercep-RM (w/ Ours) achieves MUSIQ 73.456, MANIQA 0.658, and LIQE 4.567, outperforming the DIT4SR baseline (71.832 / 0.632 / 4.356) and the CLIP-IQA reward variant (70.456 / 0.615 / 4.234). On DrealSR, the same configuration reaches MUSIQ 67.823, MANIQA 0.651, and LIQE 4.187, while also reducing LPIPS from 0.365 (baseline) to 0.342. Notably, using CLIP-IQA directly as the reward (w/ IQA) degrades most perceptual metrics despite improving the CLIP-IQA score itself, confirming the reward-hacking phenomenon. In the user study, DIT4SR w/ Ours is preferred over DIT4SR baseline 78.9% of the time for realism and 76.8% for fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FinPercep-RM (w/ Ours) consistently outperforms all external baselines—SUPIR, DreamClear, and ResShift—across every dataset and perceptual metric, with margins of 4–8 points on MUSIQ and 0.05–0.10 on MANIQA. Against the DIT4SR baseline, w/ Ours improves MUSIQ by 1.6 points (73.456 vs 71.832 on RealLQ250) and MANIQA by 0.026, while the CLIP-IQA reward variant (w/ IQA) actually degrades MUSIQ by 1.4 points and MANIQA by 0.017, demonstrating reward hacking. The same pattern holds for DiffBIR and SeeSR: w/ Ours improves all perceptual metrics over their respective baselines, whereas w/ IQA degrades them. In the user study, DIT4SR w/ Ours is preferred over DIT4SR baseline 78.9% of the time for realism and 76.8% for fidelity, and over SeeSR 85.3% for realism. The gains are consistent across three different RLHF algorithms (REFL, DPO, GRPO), confirming that the improvement stems from the reward model rather than a specific optimization strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF paradigm from text-to-image generation to real-world super-resolution, building directly on CLIP-IQA as the reward model encoder backbone and on diffusion-based Real-ISR generators (DiffBIR, SeeSR, DiT4SR). It departs from prior IQA-based reward models by architecturally coupling a global quality score with a spatial Perceptual Degradation Map, addressing the core limitation that single-scalar rewards are insensitive to local artifacts. The Co-evolutionary Curriculum Learning mechanism is a novel training strategy that co-evolves the reward model's complexity with the generator's policy, drawing on curriculum learning principles but applying them jointly to both the reward and the policy in a synchronized manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying reward hacking as the fundamental bottleneck in RL-based Real-ISR and providing a diagnostic (rather than purely evaluative) reward model, this work offers a principled path toward more reliable RLHF for image restoration. The FGR-30k dataset and the CCL mechanism are reusable components that could transfer to other RLHF tasks where fine-grained spatial quality matters. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-diffusion SR architectures, whether the DINOv3-based feature dissimilarity in ground-truth map construction introduces bias, and whether the multi-stage curriculum can be automated or simplified for practical deployment.
