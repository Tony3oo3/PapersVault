## Problem

Most medical vision-language models analyze chest X-rays in isolation, overlooking the core clinical task of comparing a patient's current study with its prior to assess interval change. Even existing temporal encoders lack principled evaluation of directional reasoning, making it unclear whether moderate classification scores reflect genuine temporal understanding or mere reliance on entity presence and label priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261293/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired chest X-rays (previous and current) encoded jointly by a paired-image vision encoder, along with radiology reports processed by a text encoder (CXR-BERT). At inference, the model runs forward and reversed image-order passes and fuses them via inversion-aware scoring, producing a three-class progression label (improved/stable/worsened), a temporal embedding for retrieval, or a binary change/no-change score. Binary change labels for pretraining are auto-generated by Gemini 2.0 Flash comparing follow-up reports against priors.

## Method

TILA is an architecture-agnostic framework that uses temporal inversion—reversing the order of paired images—as a supervisory signal across three stages. During pretraining, a Change-aware Sigmoid Loss extends SigLIP by treating inverted image pairs as positives only for no-change cases and as negatives for change cases, forcing the model to encode directionality. During fine-tuning, a Bidirectional Cross-Entropy (BiCE) loss enforces label-level inversion (improved↔worsened, stable fixed) while a Temporal Consistency Loss (TCL) penalizes mismatches between forward probabilities and the inversion-transformed reversed probabilities. At inference, forward and inversion-adjusted reversed predictions are averaged to reduce order bias. The framework plugs into existing temporal VLP backbones (BioViL-T, ALTA) without architectural modification, and the paper also introduces MS-CXR-T_retrieval, a reproducible pipeline for constructing progression-aware retrieval evaluation sets from any temporal CXR dataset.

## Results

On the supervised MS-CXR-T benchmark (5 findings), BioViL-T+TILA achieves 64.1% Standard macro-accuracy, 63.7% Reversed, 63.6% Combined, and 57.4% Consistency, outperforming its SigLIP-retrained baseline (61.1/53.3/59.7/39.5) and the original BioViL-T (60.1/53.1/60.6/42.2). ALTA+TILA similarly improves to 63.6% Standard and 54.6% Consistency over its SigLIP baseline (61.7/42.9). In retrieval, TILA raises the TEM score from 15.7→17.1 (BioViL-T) and 14.7→16.0 (ALTA) on MIMIC while maintaining comparable Recall@k, and on binary interval-change screening, BioViL-T+TILA improves AUC from 0.565→0.702 on RexGradient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TILA consistently outperforms its own SigLIP-retrained baselines across all four evaluation protocols: BioViL-T+TILA gains +3.0% Standard, +10.4% Reversed, and +17.9% Consistency over BioViL-T SigLIP; ALTA+TILA gains +1.9% Standard, +5.8% Reversed, and +11.7% Consistency over ALTA SigLIP. Against the strongest non-TILA supervised baseline MLRG (60.2% Standard, 43.1% Consistency), BioViL-T+TILA leads by +3.9% Standard and +14.3% Consistency, while ALTA+TILA leads by +3.4% Standard and +11.5% Consistency. The original BioViL-T and ALTA (trained with CLIP loss) are both surpassed by their TILA-augmented counterparts on every protocol. On retrieval, TILA improves TEM by +1.4 (BioViL-T) and +1.3 (ALTA) on MIMIC without degrading Recall@k, and single-image baselines (Ko&Park, BiomedCLIP) achieve substantially lower TEM (≤7.4 vs. ≥16.0), confirming the value of paired encoding. On binary screening, TILA narrows the cross-dataset gap on RexGradient from 0.565 to 0.702, a +13.7 AUC improvement over the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on paired-image temporal VLPs (BioViL-T, ALTA) and the SigLIP contrastive objective, but departs from them by introducing temporal inversion—reversing the (prev, cur) image order—as an explicit supervisory signal at every stage of training and inference. Prior temporal CXR models (BioViL-T, ALTA, TempA-VLP, CheXRelFormer, MLRG) encode paired images and classify progression but treat temporal order as an implicit property of the encoder; TILA instead makes order sensitivity an explicit training objective through three lightweight losses (Change-aware Sigmoid, BiCE, TCL) that require no architectural modification. The paper also extends the evaluation landscape beyond single-direction accuracy by proposing a four-protocol protocol (Standard, Reversed, Combined, Consistency) and a reproducible pipeline for constructing progression-aware retrieval sets (MS-CXR-T_retrieval), neither of which existed in prior temporal CXR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Interval-change assessment is the core clinical workflow in radiology, yet most ML models evaluate single images in isolation; TILA's architecture-agnostic inversion losses offer a drop-in way to make any paired-image VLP more directionally sensitive, and the Consistency metric provides a diagnostic that prior single-label accuracy could not. The transfer to binary interval-change screening (AUC 0.565→0.702 on RexGradient) suggests the learned temporal representations have practical triage value in high-volume longitudinal settings. Key open questions remain: inter-reader variability and the absence of standardized progression criteria limit ground-truth reliability, and the improved↔worsened symmetry assumption underlying BiCE/TCL may not hold for inherently asymmetric findings (e.g., post-surgical or fibrotic changes), so the framework's validity for such cases is still untested.
