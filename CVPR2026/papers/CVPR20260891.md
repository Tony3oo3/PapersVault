## Problem

The intra-modal misalignment hypothesis claims that CLIP's contrastive language-image training produces poorly calibrated image-image distances because it optimizes only inter-modal (image-text) alignment, leaving unconstrained degrees of freedom in the image embedding space. This paper reexamines both the theoretical degree-of-freedom argument and the empirical indicators (cosine similarity histograms, modality gap, retrieval/few-shot metrics) that have been cited as evidence, questioning whether the observed phenomena actually indicate a misalignment specific to inter-modal training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260891/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The proposed PCA← method takes RGB images encoded by a pretrained vision-language model (CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2, etc.) and a fixed set of ImageNet class name text embeddings (n words, each d-dimensional). It computes the first d/2 principal components of these text embeddings and projects all image embeddings onto that subspace, yielding projected image embeddings from which image-image cosine similarities are measured for classification or retrieval. The paper also evaluates standard image-image and text-image cosine similarities as baselines across 11 few-shot classification datasets and 13 image-to-image retrieval datasets, including fine-grained (StanfordCars, FGVCAircraft), generic (ImageNet, Caltech101), and domain-specific (EuroSAT, BDD100k) benchmarks.

## Method

The paper contributes three interlocking results. First, a theoretical analysis showing that in a d-dimensional embedding space, sampling d text anchor embeddings allows unique recovery of all image embeddings via the linear system X_I = (X_T[J])^{-1} · S_inter[J], proving that image-image similarities are a deterministic consequence of image-text similarities with no residual degrees of freedom for miscalibration. Second, a controlled empirical comparison demonstrating that the previously proposed misalignment indicators—same-class vs. opposite-class similarity overlap and the inter-modal vs. intra-modal similarity gap—are nearly identical between inter-modal-only models (SigLIP) and models with additional image-image self-supervised objectives (SigLIP2, DINO), indicating these signals are artifacts of the metrics rather than of the training objective. Third, the PCA← method, which projects image embeddings onto the principal components of ImageNet class name text embeddings to isolate the dominant semantic concept, consistently outperforms OTI (Optimization-based Textual Inversion) by 5–7 mAP on retrieval and matches or exceeds Tip-X and Coder on few-shot classification, all without converting images to pseudo-text tokens.

## Results

On image-to-image retrieval across 14 datasets, the proposed PCA← projection consistently outperforms OTI (the modality-inversion baseline motivated by the misalignment hypothesis) by 5.6–7.4 mAP on average (e.g., SigLIP B/16: 62.8 vs. 60.0; CLIP L/14: 61.3 vs. 57.0). In image-only few-shot classification over 11 datasets, SigLIP (inter-modal training only) with an LDA classifier achieves 85.3% at 16-shot, surpassing DINOv2 (image-only training) at 83.3%, indicating no evidence of intra-modal misalignment. Cosine similarity histograms for SigLIP and SigLIP2 (which adds an image-image self-supervised objective) are nearly indistinguishable, showing the previously cited 'misalignment indicators' are not caused by the absence of an intra-modal loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCA← outperforms OTI (Mistretta et al., ICLR 2025) on every model and dataset in the retrieval benchmark, with average mAP gains of 5.6–7.4 points (e.g., SigLIP B/16: 62.8 vs. 60.0; CLIP L/14: 61.3 vs. 57.0), directly refuting the need to convert images to pseudo-text tokens. PCA← also beats the original image-image cosine similarity by 5–8 mAP, showing that projecting onto dominant class-name axes helps without implying the full embedding space is misaligned. In few-shot VLM adaptation (Fig. 6), Tip-X and Coder—methods explicitly designed to avoid image-image similarity—do not surpass simple image-space baselines like APE feature selection or LDA, undermining the motivation for cross-modal surrogates. In image-only few-shot classification (Table 2), SigLIP (inter-modal only) with LDA achieves 85.3% at 16-shot, exceeding DINOv2 (image-only) at 83.3%, indicating that contrastive language-image training produces well-aligned image embeddings. On the Dogs-vs-Cats retrieval task (Table 1), CLIP ViT-B/16 scores 87.1 mAP, outperforming DINOv2 (81.8) and DINOv3 (84.3), further weakening the claim that CLIP's image-image distances are poorly calibrated. DINOv3 L/16 remains the strongest single model on standard retrieval benchmarks (ROxford 91.4, RParis 93.5), but PCA←-projected SigLIP B/16 surpasses it on fine-grained datasets like Stanford Cars (77.2 vs. 68.6), suggesting task ambiguity rather than misalignment explains the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a critical re-examination of the intra-modal misalignment hypothesis that has motivated a line of work including OTI (Mistretta et al., ICLR 2025), Tip-X/SUS-X (Udandarao et al., CVPR 2023), Coder (Yi et al., CVPR 2024), and modality-gap bridging (Barbier et al., 2025). Rather than proposing a new training objective or architecture, it repurposes the CLIP-vs-DINO/SigLIP2 comparison as a controlled experiment to test whether previously cited indicators (cosine similarity histograms, modality gap magnitudes) are actually caused by the absence of an intra-modal loss or are simply artifacts of the metrics. The theoretical contribution refutes the degree-of-freedom argument from [40] by showing that d text anchors suffice to uniquely recover all image embeddings, leaving no room for arbitrary image-image miscalibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the intra-modal misalignment narrative is incorrect, then a substantial body of methods designed to circumvent image-image similarity (OTI, Tip-X, Coder) are solving a non-problem, and practitioners can rely directly on CLIP/SigLIP image embeddings for retrieval and few-shot classification. The paper's PCA← projection offers a simple, training-free alternative that outperforms OTI across all tested models, suggesting the real bottleneck is task ambiguity (multiple concepts in an image vs. a single dataset label) rather than embedding geometry. Limitations include the narrow task scope (retrieval and few-shot classification only), reliance on a small set of model families, and the acknowledged inability to fully disprove the hypothesis—only to show the cited evidence does not support it. Open questions include whether the findings extend to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, depth) and whether the modality gap, while not harmful for the studied tasks, might matter in other settings.
