## Problem

Existing prompt-pool-based methods for incremental object detection assume disjoint class sets across tasks, but IOD inherently involves co-occurring scenarios where unlabeled objects from previous tasks appear in current images. This causes 'prompts pool confusion'—both matching confusion (objects exhibit high similarity to all task-specific prompts) and task confusion (prompts absorb knowledge beyond their assigned task)—leading to degraded detection performance. The paper argues that prompt structures should instead exhibit adaptive consolidation across tasks with constrained updates.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated via AP50. During inference, a frozen pre-trained detector (Deformable-DETR or Co-DETR) first generates a set of N proposals from the image; these proposals are averaged into a single query vector that feeds the parameterized prompt. Only class embeddings, bounding box embeddings, and the parameterized prompt structure are trainable, while all other detector parameters remain frozen throughout incremental training.

## Method

P²IOD replaces the discrete prompts pool with a parameterized prompt structure implemented as an MLP bottleneck (two FNN layers with a reduced hidden dimension) that generates instance-specific prompts from compressed detector proposals. These prompts are concatenated into the key and value streams of the decoder's multi-head self-attention layers, with independent prompt modules per decoder layer for diversity. To prevent catastrophic forgetting during incremental training, a parameterized prompt fusion strategy (inspired by TIES-merging) is applied after each task: the task vector is decomposed into magnitude and sign components, critical parameters from both current and previous tasks are preserved by top-k% magnitude selection, directionally consistent parameters are averaged, and remaining parameters default to the previous task. An additional L1 sparse loss encourages knowledge to concentrate in distinct parameter subsets, facilitating cleaner fusion. Pseudo-labeling from the previous detector mines latent knowledge of co-occurring background objects to further reduce forgetting.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC2007 (single-step 10+10 setting), P²IOD with Objects365 pretraining achieves 89.8% AP₅₀ overall, outperforming the strongest prompt-based baseline MD-DETR (Objects365) by 5.2% and the next-best non-prompt method BPF by 16.9%. On MS COCO multi-step (40+10+10+10+10), P²IOD (Objects365) reaches 64.8% AP₅₀ at T₅, surpassing MD-DETR (Objects365) by 15.4% and the best non-prompt method LEA by 8.3%. The advantage of P²IOD over MD-DETR grows with co-occurrence level: +1.6% (19+1), +3.9% (15+5), and +5.2% (10+10) on PASCAL VOC2007, confirming that the method specifically mitigates prompts pool confusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P²IOD outperforms all 15 baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the closest prompt-based competitor MD-DETR [2], P²IOD (Objects365) gains +0.8% to +5.2% on PASCAL VOC2007 single-step and +15.4% on MS COCO multi-step (T₅: 64.8% vs 49.4%), with the gap widening as co-occurrence increases. Among non-prompt distillation/replay methods, P²IOD surpasses BPF [27] by 16.9% (10+10), VLM-PL [17] by 11.5% (10+10), and PseudoRM [43] by 20.9% (10+10). On MS COCO multi-step, P²IOD (Objects365) leads LEA [33] by 8.3% at T₅, SDDGR [16] by 10.1%, and ERD [7] by 33.0%, while also outperforming GCD [36] by 9.7% at T₅. The only setting where P²IOD (MS COCO pretraining) trails a baseline is the 19+1 new-class column (62.6% vs VLM-PL's 89.3*), but this is attributed to VLM-PL's overestimated task-specific evaluation (marked with *), and P²IOD still leads on the overall 1-20 metric (77.7% vs 73.6%). On PASCAL VOC2007 multi-step (5+5+5+5), P²IOD (Objects365) achieves 87.1% overall, exceeding MD-DETR (Objects365) by 11.3% and BPF [27] by 24.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MD-DETR (Bhatt et al., ECCV 2024), which first brought prompting into incremental object detection by adapting the prompts pool paradigm from incremental classification (L2P, DualPrompt). The key departure is the identification of 'prompts pool confusion'—a failure mode specific to IOD's co-occurrence phenomenon where unlabeled objects from prior tasks appear in current images, causing both matching confusion and task confusion in discrete prompt pools. To resolve this, the authors replace the discrete pool with a parameterized MLP bottleneck that adaptively consolidates knowledge, and borrow the TIES-merging decomposition (Yadav et al., NeurIPS 2023) to design a constrained fusion strategy that preserves critical parameters across tasks while averaging consistent ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic diagnosis of why prompts pool architectures, successful in incremental classification, degrade in IOD, and offers a lightweight (1.1M parameter) replacement that is practical for edge deployment. The finding that performance gains over MD-DETR grow with co-occurrence level (1.6% → 8.0%) validates the confusion hypothesis and suggests that as detection scenarios become more open-world, parameterized prompts may outperform pool-based approaches. Open questions include whether the fusion strategy scales gracefully beyond 4–5 incremental steps, whether the approach transfers to non-transformer detectors, and whether the co-occurrence assumption holds in truly open-world settings where object distributions shift over time.
