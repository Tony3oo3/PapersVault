## Problem

Existing active mapping methods rely on greedy next-best-view prediction, which leads to suboptimal exploration with dead-ends and redundant back-and-forth motions. Long-term trajectory planning is infeasible with current approaches because (1) the scene geometry is unknown during exploration, creating a chicken-and-egg dependency, and (2) computing surface coverage gain for many candidate viewpoints via Monte Carlo volumetric integration is too slow for efficient search over long horizons.

## Input / Output

The agent receives sequential RGB-D observations at known camera poses and maintains a reconstructed surface point cloud, which together feed a pre-trained occupancy transformer to predict a probabilistic occupancy field over both seen and unseen space. The method outputs a long-horizon exploration trajectory (a sequence of SE(3) camera poses) planned via beam search over N_d steps, executing only N_f steps before replanning in a closed loop. Downstream, the collected images can be used for 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction and mesh extraction.

## Method

The key insight is that the surface coverage gain integral shares the exact structure of the volumetric rendering equation: occupancy maps to density, occlusion to transmittance, and novelty to color. MAGICIAN instantiates this by sampling 3D Gaussian primitives from a pre-trained occupancy network's probabilistic field, encoding occupancy probability as opacity and binary novelty as color (isotropic Gaussians with radius set to half the nearest-neighbor distance). Coverage gain for any candidate pose is then computed by a single fast volumetric rendering pass over these 'Imagined Gaussians,' yielding a 25× speedup over Monte Carlo-based MACARONS. This enables an efficient beam search (N_b=10 beams, N_d=10 look-ahead steps) where each beam maintains an independent Gaussian state and updates novelty values as Gaussians become observed along its trajectory. The system operates in a closed loop: after executing the first N_f steps of the best trajectory, real observations refine Gaussian opacities via the occupancy network and reset novelty, then the beam search replans.

## Results

On the Macarons++ benchmark, MAGICIAN achieves a final surface coverage of 91.9% and AUC of 0.721, surpassing the previous best (MACARONS) by 10 percentage points in coverage and 7.4 points in AUC. On MP3D, MAGICIAN reaches 85.45% completeness (4.93 cm mean distance) for the wheeled-robot setting and 96.83% (2.11 cm) for the drone setting, outperforming all prior methods. The method also achieves the best novel-view rendering (PSNR 17.12, SSIM 0.64) and mesh accuracy (94.20%) on real-world scanned scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGICIAN outperforms all ten baselines across both benchmarks. On Macarons++, it beats MACARONS [19] (the closest competitor) by 10.0 points in final coverage (91.9% vs 81.9%) and 7.4 points in AUC (0.721 vs 0.647), while SCONE [18] and FisherRF [26] trail by 24.9 and 13.3 coverage points respectively. On MP3D (wheeled robot), MAGICIAN improves completeness over NextBestPath [32] (85.45% vs 79.38%) and ANM [44] (85.45% vs 73.15%), with the largest gap over frontier-based FBE [43] (85.45% vs 71.18%) and UPEN [16] (85.45% vs 69.06%). In the drone setting, MAGICIAN edges out ActiveGamer [10] (96.83% vs 95.32%) and NARUTO [15] (96.83% vs 90.18%), with OccAnt [38] (71.72%) and FBE [43] (71.18%) far behind in the wheeled setting. The consistent wins across diverse action spaces (discrete wheeled vs 6-DoF drone) and environments (indoor MP3D vs outdoor Macarons++) underscore the generality of the long-term beam-search planning approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGICIAN extends the surface coverage gain criterion introduced by SCONE and MACARONS, which previously relied on greedy next-best-view selection with expensive Monte Carlo volumetric integration. The key conceptual departure is recasting the coverage gain integral as a volumetric rendering equation (à la NeRF/3DGS), enabling the use of a pre-trained occupancy network's predictions as Gaussian opacities for 25× faster gain evaluation. This speedup is what makes beam-search-based long-horizon trajectory planning tractable, a dimension entirely absent from prior active mapping methods that only optimized single or short-horizon poses. The occupancy prediction model itself follows the transformer architecture of MACARONS, pretrained on ShapeNet and fine-tuned on 3D scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to perform genuine long-horizon trajectory planning in active mapping, and the >10% coverage improvement on Macarons++ demonstrates that greedy NBV selection leaves substantial performance on the table in complex scenes. The 25× speedup in gain computation via Gaussian rendering is a practical enabler that could be adopted by other active mapping or exploration systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger outdoor environments, whether it can be extended to RGB-only settings using 3D foundation models (as the authors note), and whether semantic or goal-directed exploration can be integrated. The method still assumes known camera poses and RGB-D input, limiting direct deployment in pose-uncertain settings.
