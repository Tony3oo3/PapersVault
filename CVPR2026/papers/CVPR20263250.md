## Problem

Current diffusion-based portrait animation methods condition on driving motion (landmarks, driving image, or latent features) without establishing explicit source-to-driving correspondences, leading to ambiguous learning and suboptimal motion transfer under large pose or appearance variations. Learning-based 2D flow estimation is ill-posed and struggles to generalize across diverse subjects. The paper addresses this gap by introducing 3D flows computed directly from parametric 3D head models as a learning-free, geometry-driven motion correspondence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 source portrait and a 512×512 driving portrait as input, from which FLAME coefficients (shape β, pose θ, expression ψ) and camera parameters are estimated via fitting. At inference, users can additionally specify FLAME expression (100-dim) and pose (12-dim) editing vectors to override or augment the driving motion. The output is a 512×512 animated portrait preserving source identity while matching driving pose and expression; temporal layers can be added for video-sequence generation.

## Method

The core contribution is a 3D flow encoding that provides per-pixel 3D displacement from target to source, computed learning-free from FLAME meshes. Specifically, the target FLAME is assembled from source shape coefficients with driving pose and expression, and per-point correspondence is established via Surface Field mapping, yielding 3D flows f = p_src − p_tgt. For each target pixel, N=20 points are sampled along the backprojected ray and their 3D flows are stacked into an H×W×60 tensor fed to a ControlNet as the motion condition. A depth-guided sampling strategy uses the rendered depth map of the target head to concentrate sampling near the actual 3D surface rather than a uniform depth range, ensuring the 3D flows faithfully align with 2D motion. The appearance branch (initialized from X-Portrait) provides multi-source attention key-values, and the entire system is fine-tuned on SD 1.5 with the backbone frozen.

## Results

On the self-reenactment task (VFHQ, 512²), FG-Portrait achieves the best scores across all four metrics: LPIPS 0.158, CSIM 0.807, APD 2.682, and AED 0.327. On the more challenging cross-reenactment task (VFHQ), it attains the lowest FID (87.0), APD (7.764), and AED (0.652), while CSIM (0.462) is slightly below Follow-Your-Emoji (0.484). On FFHQ cross-reenactment, it again leads in FID (99.4), APD (9.297), and AED (0.714), confirming strong generalization to unseen identities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FG-Portrait outperforms all five baselines on every metric in the self-reenactment setting, with the largest margins on APD (2.682 vs. 3.047 for EMOPortrait, the next best) and AED (0.327 vs. 0.341 for HunyuanPortrait). In cross-reenactment on VFHQ, it achieves the best FID (87.0 vs. 91.3 for Follow-Your-Emoji), APD (7.764 vs. 7.785 for Face-Adapter), and AED (0.652 vs. 0.658 for HunyuanPortrait), but trails Follow-Your-Emoji on CSIM (0.462 vs. 0.484), which the authors attribute to the inherent identity–motion trade-off. On FFHQ cross-reenactment, it again leads in FID (99.4 vs. 113.7 for HunyuanPortrait), APD (9.297 vs. 10.570), and AED (0.714 vs. 0.722), while X-Portrait achieves higher CSIM (0.654 vs. 0.558) at the cost of substantially worse motion fidelity (APD 14.696). EMOPortrait, the only non-diffusion baseline, consistently lags on all metrics due to its GAN backbone and inaccurate learned motion flow. The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that the 3D flow encoding (APD 2.682) substantially outperforms both driving landmarks (4.001) and a predicted 2D flow map (4.232) as the motion condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work in portrait animation: (1) diffusion-based methods (MagicPose, X-Portrait, Face-Adapter, HunyuanPortrait) that condition on driving motion via landmarks, driving images, or latent representations but lack explicit source-to-driving pixel correspondence, and (2) learned motion-flow methods (FOMM, EMOPortrait) that predict dense 2D flow maps in a self-supervised manner. The key departure is replacing both learned flow prediction and implicit motion conditioning with a learning-free 3D flow computed directly from FLAME parametric head model vertex correspondences, then projected into 2D via depth-guided sampling to serve as a ControlNet input. This repurposes the geometric structure of 3D head models as an explicit, per-pixel motion correspondence signal rather than relying on the network to infer it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode in diffusion-based portrait animation: without explicit source-to-driving correspondence, motion transfer degrades under large pose or identity changes. The 3D flow encoding provides a principled, geometry-grounded solution that avoids the ill-posedness of 2D flow estimation, and the depth-guided sampling ablation (APD jumping from 2.68 to 9.66 without it) underscores how critical spatial alignment is. The feed-forward expression and pose editing capability adds practical utility. However, the reliance on FLAME's 5023-vertex mesh limits fine-grained expression fidelity, and the method inherits FLAME fitting errors; whether higher-resolution or learned 3D head models can close this gap remains open.
