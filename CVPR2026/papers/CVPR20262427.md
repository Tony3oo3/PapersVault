## Problem

Local tractography methods accumulate errors step-by-step, producing high false-positive rates especially on noisy or low-resolution clinical data, while existing global methods are computationally expensive and prone to optimization failures that yield incomplete tractograms. No prior approach efficiently generates complete, anatomically plausible whole-brain tractograms that remain robust under degraded imaging conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262427/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4D tensor of spherical harmonic fODF coefficients (H×W×D×28 at 1.875 mm³ isotropic resolution, z-score normalized) and produces batches of 3D streamline coordinate sequences (p×3 per streamline, min-max scaled to [-1,1]), which are collated into a full whole-brain tractogram. Unlike local methods, no seeding mask is required; streamlines are sampled directly from the learned conditional distribution. The conditioning tensor is a flattened, channel-concatenated representation of shape (H_c×W_c×D_c, m×C_c) derived from the full fODF volume.

## Method

GenTract reframes tractography as a conditional generative task with two components: (1) an anatomical conditioning encoder that uses m independent MAISI-fine-tuned VAEs (one per SH coefficient) followed by a shared class-conditioned 3D ResNet to produce a compact global context tensor z; and (2) a conditional Transformer (M=8 layers, n=256 embedding) that generates all streamline coordinates simultaneously via self-attention (geometric coherence along the streamline) and cross-attention (anatomical guidance from z). The Transformer is trained with either a Diffusion (DDIM) or Flow Matching objective, with the Diffusion variant outperforming FM at all tested depths. At inference, 10 DDIM steps from Gaussian noise conditioned on z yield a full streamline, and repeating in batches samples the entire conditional distribution. This parallel, non-autoregressive generation eliminates stepwise error propagation and removes dependence on operator-defined seeding masks.

## Results

On the HCP test set, GenTract achieves 61.95% BS precision and 56.35% TO-Net precision, outperforming the next-best methods DDTracking (35.20% / 30.70%) and TractOracle (28.93% / 39.55%) by factors of 1.8× and 2.1× respectively. Under low-resolution (3 mm³) plus Rician noise, GenTract retains 15.73% BS precision and 43.43% TO-Net precision, exceeding the closest competitor (DDTracking at 4.44% BS) by a factor of 3.5. On the external TractoInferno dataset under the same degraded conditions, GenTract still leads with 24.94% BS precision versus 9.74% for TractOracle. The trade-off is a lower bundle count (36.62/51) compared to baselines (42–50/51), attributed to training on a constrained 24-bundle PyAFQ distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenTract decisively outperforms all five baselines on precision metrics across every evaluation condition. Against the classical local methods iFOD2 and SD Stream, GenTract's advantage is overwhelming: both methods collapse to near-zero BS precision (≤4.71%) on clean data and to 0% under low-resolution noise, while GenTract maintains 61.95% and 15.73% respectively. The global method tckglobal recovers many bundles (42.91/51) but achieves negligible precision (0.19% BS), and fails entirely under degraded conditions (0% BS, 0 bundles). Among the deep learning baselines, DDTracking is the closest competitor on clean data (35.20% BS) but degrades sharply under noise (5.41%) and low-resolution (4.44%), whereas GenTract shows only a 2.6% drop under noise and retains a 3.5× margin under LR+noise. TractOracle recovers the most bundles (48.20/51) but lags GenTract by 2.1× on BS precision on clean data and by 2.6× on the external TractoInferno dataset (9.74% vs. 24.94%). The consistent pattern is that GenTract trades a modest reduction in bundle count (~10–14 fewer bundles) for a large, robust gain in streamline precision that persists across noise levels, resolutions, and datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenTract departs from both dominant tractography paradigms: local tracking (iFOD2, SD Stream, TrackToLearn, TractOracle, DDTracking) which propagates streamlines stepwise and accumulates error, and classical global optimization (tckglobal, spin-glass models) which is computationally expensive and prone to suboptimal solutions. The paper's key conceptual move is reframing tractography as a conditional generative task—sampling all streamline coordinates simultaneously from a learned distribution—drawing on diffusion model and flow matching architectures (following Peebles & Xie's scalable diffusion transformers) with a VAE-based anatomical encoder (fine-tuned from MAISI). This is the first application of generative modeling to global tractography, replacing iterative optimization with a single forward sampling process conditioned on the full fODF volume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.8–2.1× precision advantage over the best deep-learning baselines, and the 3.5× gap in low-resolution/noisy settings, suggest that generative global tractography could become a practical alternative to both local and classical global methods, particularly for time-constrained clinical scans. However, the precision gain comes at the cost of a higher false-negative rate (fewer bundles recovered), which the authors attribute to training on a constrained 24-bundle PyAFQ distribution. Critical open questions include validation on real (non-synthetically degraded) clinical data, whether the bundle-recall gap can be closed by expanding the training distribution or using unsupervised objectives, and whether the probabilistic sampling can be exploited for uncertainty quantification in surgical planning.
