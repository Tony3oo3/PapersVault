## Problem

Current medical vision-language pretraining methods feed data in random order, forcing models to learn simple and complex diagnostic concepts simultaneously — an anti-cognitive process that yields suboptimal representations, particularly under distribution shift. No existing work explicitly addresses the unique difficulty structure of medical image-text data, where diagnostic sensitivity varies across diseases, intra-class sample representativeness differs substantially, and medical images exhibit high inter-class similarity that makes symmetric contrastive learning ill-suited.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired medical images (color fundus photography, OCT, or chest X-ray) with either label-level captions generated from domain-knowledge templates or original description-level radiology reports, with a maximum text token length of 256. It outputs pretrained vision and text encoders with aligned feature spaces (512-dim projection for CLIP, 256-dim for FILIP) that are model-agnostic and transferable to zero-shot classification, image-to-text retrieval, and report generation. The method is applied as a pretraining procedure on top of existing architectures rather than introducing a new model.

## Method

MedKCO introduces a two-level curriculum for pretraining data ordering combined with a self-paced asymmetric contrastive loss. The label-level curriculum partitions data into three stages of increasing diagnostic difficulty based on modality sensitivity (visually identifiable structural signs → multi-sign high-probability diagnosis → complementary-modality-dependent diagnosis), with staging determined by physician consensus and LLM-assisted categorization. The description-level curriculum clusters samples by text-label similarity, then orders them by normalized distance from the cluster centroid so that the most representative (farthest-from-centroid) samples are learned first. The self-paced asymmetric loss (Eq. 7) replaces the standard symmetric contrastive objective by linearly ramping the text-to-image loss weight from 0 to 1 across training epochs, allowing the model to initially focus on the easier image-to-text alignment before progressively tackling the harder text-to-image alignment that is bottlenecked by the compact visual feature space in early pretraining.

## Results

On zero-shot classification across three medical modalities (CFP, OCT, CXR), MedKCO achieves an average CXR accuracy of 0.693, outperforming the CLIP baseline (0.616) by 7.7% and the FILIP baseline (0.530) by 11%. On the OOD dataset ODIR 200×3 (CFP), MedKCO reaches 0.863 ACC versus 0.772 for CLIP and 0.835 for CL-log. For zero-shot image-to-text retrieval, MedKCO attains an average R@1 of 11.9 under CLIP and 13.5 under FILIP, surpassing all baselines by 1.7–5.5% (CLIP) and 2.4–3.8% (FILIP). On report generation (OpenI and MIMIC-CXR), MedKCO achieves the best BLEU-1, METEOR, and CIDER scores across all metrics, e.g., BLEU-1 of 0.394 on OpenI vs. 0.362 for CLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedKCO consistently outperforms both the CLIP and FILIP baselines across all three modalities and all downstream tasks. In the CLIP framework, MedKCO surpasses CL-log (self-paced curriculum) by 9.3% and CL-logit (logit-based curriculum) by 10.8% on average CXR accuracy; in the FILIP framework, the margins widen to 8.8% and 17.8% respectively. Notably, CL-logit performs poorly in several settings (e.g., 0.567 ACC on ODIR 200×3 vs. 0.863 for MedKCO), suggesting that logit-based difficulty estimation is ill-suited for medical image-text alignment. On OOD datasets (ODIR 200×3, OCTDL, COVIDx), MedKCO achieves the best results in every case, confirming superior distribution-shift robustness. For image-to-text retrieval, MedKCO under FILIP reaches 9.1 R@1 on MIMIC-CXR, exceeding the FILIP baseline (6.9) by 32% and the best curriculum baseline (6.2) by 47%. On report generation, both curriculum baselines (CL-log, CL-logit) fail to improve over their respective parent frameworks, while MedKCO delivers consistent gains across BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE, and CIDER.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends medical vision-language pretraining (MedCLIP, CXR-CLIP, FLAIR, KeepFIT) by introducing a knowledge-driven curriculum that orders pretraining data according to diagnostic sensitivity and intra-class representativeness, departing from the random-shuffling paradigm used by all prior medical VLP methods. It also draws on curriculum learning in general VLP (Srinivasan et al.'s granularity-based staging, Chen et al.'s visual-task-difficulty ordering) but grounds the difficulty signal in explicit medical domain knowledge rather than generic model feedback. The self-paced asymmetric contrastive loss is a novel objective that addresses the asymmetry between tightly-clustered medical image features and well-separated text embeddings—a problem specific to medical imaging that prior symmetric contrastive losses (CLIP, FILIP) do not account for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic design means MedKCO can be plugged into any existing medical VLP framework, and the consistent OOD advantage (best results on all out-of-distribution benchmarks across three modalities) suggests the cognitive ordering genuinely improves generalization rather than merely fitting in-distribution data. However, the label-level curriculum requires expert (or LLM-assisted) staging of diseases by diagnostic sensitivity per modality, which is a non-trivial annotation burden that limits immediate scalability to new modalities. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-medical VLP, whether automated difficulty estimation can replace expert staging, and whether the benefits persist at much larger model scales or with more diverse pretraining corpora.
