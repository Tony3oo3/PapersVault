## Problem

MLLM-based object detection has rarely matched the performance of regression-based detectors due to two core challenges: (1) cross-entropy loss treats coordinate prediction as discrete classification, creating a geometric discretization mismatch where minor pixel offsets incur disproportionate penalties, and (2) teacher-forced SFT prevents the model from learning to autonomously regulate output quantity, leading to duplicate predictions and missed detections at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261116/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image paired with either a natural-language text prompt (e.g., 'Please detect pigeon, person, truck in this image') or a visual prompt (a bounding box converted into four quantized coordinate tokens). Output is a structured token sequence of PHRASE followed by COORDS, where coordinates are quantized to integers 0–999 and each value is a single special token; the coordinate format adapts per task—four tokens for boxes, two for points, or variable-length vertex sequences for polygons—with multiple detections concatenated by commas.

## Method

Rex-Omni repurposes the final 1,000 vocabulary slots of Qwen2.5-VL-3B as learnable quantized coordinate tokens, reducing coordinate prediction to a 1,000-class problem and representing each bounding box in just four tokens. Training is two-stage: first, SFT on 22 million samples (8.9M public + 13M generated by four data engines for grounding, referring, pointing, and OCR) teaches the token-to-pixel mapping; second, GRPO-based RL post-training on 66K samples uses geometry-aware rewards (Box IoU F1, Point-in-Mask, Point-in-Box) to suppress SFT-induced behavioral failures such as duplicate and large-box predictions. The key empirical finding is that GRPO's primary contribution is behavioral correction—elevating the sampling probability of already-learned correct outputs—rather than substantially improving raw coordinate precision, as confirmed by a controlled analysis showing only +0.5 mIoU gain on perfectly-matched predictions.

## Results

On COCO (zero-shot), Rex-Omni achieves F1@IoU0.5 of 72.0, surpassing Grounding DINO (69.8) and DINO-R50 (68.8), while attaining F1@mIoU of 52.9. On the long-tailed LVIS benchmark, Rex-Omni achieves SOTA F1@mIoU of 46.9, edging out SEED1.5-VL (46.7) and substantially outperforming Grounding DINO (38.8). On dense object detection (Dense200), Rex-Omni reaches F1@IoU0.5 of 78.4, far exceeding other MLLMs (SEED1.5-VL: 76.9, Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 0.8). Across object pointing, GUI grounding, and layout tasks, Rex-Omni consistently outperforms or matches the strongest baselines of comparable scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rex-Omni outperforms Grounding DINO on COCO F1@IoU0.5 (72.0 vs. 69.8) and LVIS F1@mIoU (46.9 vs. 38.8), and beats DINO-R50 on COCO F1@IoU0.5 (72.0 vs. 68.8), all in a zero-shot setting. However, Rex-Omni trails DINO-R50 and Grounding DINO on COCO F1@mIoU (52.9 vs. 55.6 and 56.6 respectively), indicating that regression-based models still hold an edge in fine-grained box precision at high IoU thresholds. Among MLLMs, Rex-Omni surpasses SEED1.5-VL on COCO F1@IoU0.5 (72.0 vs. 71.3) and LVIS F1@mIoU (46.9 vs. 46.7), but slightly lags on LVIS F1@IoU0.5 (64.3 vs. 65.6). On dense detection (Dense200), Rex-Omni dramatically outperforms all other MLLMs (78.4 vs. 76.9 for SEED1.5-VL, 0.8 for Qwen2.5-VL-3B), with the GRPO stage contributing a +18.2 F1@0.5 gain over SFT alone. On object pointing, Rex-Omni leads all MLLMs across six benchmarks (e.g., 80.5 F1@Point on COCO vs. 78.2 for SEED1.5-VL), and on GUI grounding it achieves 88.4/36.8 accuracy on ScreenSpot-v2/Pro, matching or exceeding JEDI-3B (88.6/36.1) and UI-R1-3B (85.4/17.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

Rex-Omni builds directly on Qwen2.5-VL-3B as its backbone, repurposing 1,000 vocabulary tokens as quantized coordinate representations for next-token-prediction-based detection. It departs from prior MLLM detectors (SEED1.5-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, OVIS) that rely solely on SFT by introducing a GRPO-based RL post-training stage with geometry-aware rewards, adapting the GRPO framework from DeepSeekMath to the spatial prediction setting. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of two lineages: regression-based detectors (DINO, Grounding DINO) that excel at localization but lack deep language understanding, and MLLMs that understand language but struggle with precise coordinates—arguing that the behavioral and geometric gaps of MLLM detectors can be systematically closed through targeted RL rather than architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 3B MLLM can match or exceed regression-based detectors on COCO in a zero-shot setting, suggesting MLLMs are viable as unified perception backbones. The key open question it surfaces is the role of RL in MLLM training: the SFT-Sampling experiment (Table 11) reveals that the SFT model already contains the latent capability for correct predictions, and GRPO's primary contribution is reshaping the output distribution to make correct answers more probable rather than teaching new spatial knowledge. Limitations remain for dense/tiny object detection (where even Rex-Omni lags behind SEED1.5-VL) and polygon OCR (where it underperforms specialized PaddleOCR), and whether this two-stage recipe transfers to larger model scales or more complex spatial tasks (e.g., 3D detection, video) is unexplored.
