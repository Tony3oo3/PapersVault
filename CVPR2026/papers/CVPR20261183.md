## Problem

Deep learning superpixel models frequently produce fragmented superpixels—disconnected regions sharing the same label and isolated stray pixels—requiring a non-differentiable Enforced Connectivity (EC) post-processing step to restore spatial contiguity. This breaks end-to-end differentiability, preventing joint optimization with downstream vision tasks. The paper asks whether connectivity can be learned directly through a differentiable loss rather than imposed as a post-hoc correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261183/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of arbitrary resolution and partitions it into a regular grid of square blocks with a 16×16 stride; each pixel is restricted to assignment among only the 9 superpixel candidates in its local 3×3 block neighborhood for computational efficiency. The network outputs a sparse, row-normalized assignment matrix Q ∈ [0,1]^{N×M} (N = H×W pixels, M superpixels), where each entry q_{i,s} is the probability that pixel i belongs to superpixel s. Hard superpixel labels are obtained by argmax over the 9 local candidates, and the method is evaluated across a range of superpixel counts (384–1200) on BSDS500, NYUv2, and Pascal VOC 2012.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully differentiable graph-Laplacian loss: for each superpixel s, an undirected graph is built over its candidate pixels with edge weights w_{ij} = q_{i,s}·q_{j,s} for 8-connected neighbors, and the loss penalizes 1 − tr(L_s)/(8N_s), where L_s is the graph Laplacian. Since the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of L_s equals the number of connected components, maximizing the trace (sum of degrees) implicitly suppresses disconnected fragments without any non-differentiable operation. Two complementary losses are added: a minimum semantic distance loss that maximizes the margin-constrained minimum inter-class embedding distance (sampled at 100 pixels per class), and a weighted reconstruction loss that assigns 10× higher weight to pixels in mixed-class blocks versus single-class blocks, with per-image normalization to keep the total loss scale constant. The combined loss is model-agnostic and is plugged into four existing architectures (SCN, AINet, CDS, SSM) with λ_LAP = 360, λ_MSD = 10⁻³, and λ_WR = 1, requiring no architectural modification.

## Results

On BSDS500 (384–1200 superpixels, AUC summary), the graph-Laplacian variants substantially improve compactness and boundary quality while preserving ASA. Without enforced connectivity (EC), CDS-LAP achieves CO_AUC 0.4339 vs. 0.3501 for CDS (+24%), B_AUC 0.1215 vs. 0.1084, and reduces excess components from 218.7 to 103.8 and stray pixels from 901.6 to 507.5, all while maintaining ASA_AUC at 0.9746 vs. 0.9741. With EC, CDS-LAP reaches CO_AUC 0.4344 and B_AUC 0.1192, the best among all LAP variants, with ASA_AUC 0.9741 matching the original CDS. The paper reports up to 15% better compactness for equivalent ASA across all four architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all four deep baselines (SCN, AINet, CDS, SSM), the LAP variants consistently win on compactness (CO), boundary score (B), and both fragmentation metrics (XC, Stray) without EC, while ASA is preserved or marginally improved (e.g., CDS-LAP: ASA 0.9746 vs. CDS 0.9741; SSM-LAP: 0.9753 vs. SSM 0.9755). The largest gains appear in CO: AINet-LAP jumps from 0.3330 to 0.4687 (+41%) and SCN-LAP from 0.3606 to 0.4663 (+30%) without EC. Against SIN, which enforces connectivity architecturally, the LAP variants achieve comparable or better CO while substantially outperforming SIN on ASA, boundary recall, and boundary precision. Against SLIC (traditional, included only in the with-EC plots), the LAP variants match or exceed SLIC on CO and B while maintaining competitive ASA. The one consistent trade-off is a small ASA drop for SCN-LAP (0.9726→0.9714 without EC) and AINet-LAP (0.9728→0.9726), but CDS-LAP and SSM-LAP show no ASA loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep superpixel segmentation lineage (SCN, AINet, CDS, SSM) by addressing their shared reliance on non-differentiable Enforced Connectivity (EC) post-processing to fix fragmented label maps. It draws on the graph-theoretic fact that the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of a Laplacian equals the number of connected components, repurposing trace maximization as a differentiable training objective for spatial connectivity. The closest prior work, SIN, enforces connectivity architecturally through alternating interpolation but sacrifices accuracy and cannot be applied as a drop-in loss to other models. The auxiliary weighted reconstruction and minimum semantic distance losses build on standard superpixel training objectives but reweight them toward boundary-critical regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By embedding connectivity into the loss function, this work moves superpixel generation closer to fully end-to-end differentiable pipelines, removing the EC bottleneck that has prevented joint optimization with downstream segmentation or detection networks. The model-agnostic, drop-in nature of the Laplacian loss means it can be adopted by any existing superpixel architecture without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the residual fragmentation (nonzero but reduced XC/Stray) is sufficient for all downstream applications without any EC, and whether the approach transfers to 3D volumetric superpixels or video. The new excess-component and stray-pixel metrics fill a genuine gap in the evaluation literature, as prior work had no standard way to quantify pre-EC fragmentation.
