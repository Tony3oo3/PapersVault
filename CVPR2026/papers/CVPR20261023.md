## Problem

Detecting small UAVs in low-altitude environments is severely degraded by camouflage, cluttered backgrounds (buildings, vegetation, power lines, clouds), and the inherent spectral mismatch between RGB and thermal modalities. Existing UAV detection datasets are not specifically designed to capture these complex-background and camouflage challenges, and current RGB-T fusion methods fail to jointly address the frequency-spatial fusion gap (global frequency lacks local context) and the cross-modality discrepancy gap (spectral inconsistency between visible and thermal bands).

## Input / Output

The method consumes coarsely registered RGB (1920×1080) and uncooled VOx thermal (640×512, 8–14 µm) image pairs, which are cropped and resized to a common 640×512 resolution; the two modalities are deliberately not pixel-level aligned to reflect real-world sensor offset. Output is a set of UAV bounding boxes with detection confidence scores, evaluated via AP₅₀, AP₇₅, and AP₍₀.₅:₀.₉₅₎. The UAV-CB dataset provides 3,442 annotated RGB-T pairs spanning five background categories (building, vegetation, powerline, cloud, ground), with most targets occupying less than 5% of image area.

## Method

LFBNet introduces a two-stage frequency-spatial alignment pipeline. The LFCA module splits each modality into 16×16 patches, applies 2D FFT, then performs amplitude alignment (L₂-normalized spectra blended by a learnable per-patch coefficient), phase alignment (linear interpolation with wrap-to-[−π,π] to preserve geometric structure), and inverse-FFT reconstruction with overlap-add to yield a modality-consistent representation injected via cross-attention. The FGSA module converts patch-wise spectral discrepancies into a dense Local Frequency Guidance Map (LFGM) encoding phase-based displacement, high/low-frequency energy ratios, and cross-spectral coherence; a gated modulation gates this map with spatial features, and a frequency-guided offset predictor drives deformable convolution to correct residual parallax. A symmetric cross-conditioned fusion (bidirectional 3×3 convolutions over [Fʳ, F̂ᵗ] and [Fᵗ, F̂ʳ]) produces the final multimodal representation fed to an FPN-based YOLOv5s detection head with a ResNet-50 backbone.

## Results

On the proposed UAV-CB dataset, LFBNet achieves AP50 of 84.6%, AP75 of 57.2%, and AP(0.5:0.95) of 54.4%, surpassing the previous best multimodal baseline C²Former by 5.2% in AP50 and 5.3% in AP(0.5:0.95). Compared with the frequency-domain method SFDFusion, LFBNet improves AP(0.5:0.95) by 5.4% while using fewer parameters (30.2M vs. 21.5M is actually more, but fewer FLOPs: 65.2G vs. 58.5G — actually LFBNet has more of both). On the public DroneVehicle RGB-T benchmark, LFBNet achieves mAP50 of 80.1%, outperforming OAFA (79.4%) and C²Former (72.8%), demonstrating cross-dataset generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFBNet outperforms all 15 compared baselines on UAV-CB. Against the strongest prior multimodal method C²Former (100.8M params, 324G FLOPs), LFBNet gains +5.2% AP50 and +5.3% AP(0.5:0.95) while using only 30.2M parameters and 65.2G FLOPs — roughly 3.3× fewer parameters and 5× fewer FLOPs. Against SFDFusion, which also exploits frequency-domain information, LFBNet improves AP(0.5:0.95) by 5.4%, confirming that localized frequency alignment is more effective than global spatial-frequency fusion for camouflaged UAV detection. Among single-modality detectors, the best thermal model (RT-DETR, 49.2% AP(0.5:0.95)) is surpassed by LFBNet by 5.2 points, and the best visible model (RT-DETR, 44.2%) by 10.2 points. On the cross-dataset DroneVehicle benchmark, LFBNet achieves 80.1% mAP50, narrowly beating OAFA (79.4%) and substantially outperforming C²Former (72.8%) and TSFADet (70.4%), indicating that the frequency-spatial alignment strategy generalizes beyond the UAV-CB training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—UAV detection, RGB-T multimodal fusion, and frequency-domain visual processing—without directly extending any single prior architecture. It draws conceptual inspiration from camouflaged object detection (COD) and spatial-frequency fusion networks (e.g., SFDFusion), but repurposes frequency-domain ideas in a localized, patch-wise manner specifically to address cross-modal amplitude/phase inconsistency between RGB and thermal sensors. The detection backbone (YOLOv5s + ResNet-50 + FPN) is standard; the novelty lies in the LFCA and FGSA modules that operate in localized frequency space to bridge both the frequency-spatial fusion gap and the cross-modality discrepancy gap, a formulation not present in prior RGB-T fusion work for small aerial targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UAV-CB fills a concrete gap: no prior RGB-T UAV dataset deliberately emphasizes camouflage and complex low-altitude backgrounds, which are the dominant failure modes in real-world counter-drone deployment. The dataset (3,442 pairs, five background categories) and the unified evaluation protocol give the community a targeted benchmark for robustness studies. Open questions remain around domain adaptability to unseen weather/lighting conditions, scalability to larger and more diverse collections, and whether the localized frequency alignment strategy generalizes beyond the specific sensor pair and resolution used here. The paper itself flags these as future directions, and the single-dataset, single-sensor evaluation limits confidence in broad generalization claims.
