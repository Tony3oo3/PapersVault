## Problem

State-of-the-art feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (VGGT, π³) rely on global self-attention, causing computational cost to scale quadratically with the number of input images and making them infeasible for large collections. Sequential linear-time alternatives (CUT3R, TTT3R) reduce cost but sacrifice reconstruction quality. ZipMap addresses this gap by achieving linear-time, bidirectional reconstruction that matches or exceeds the accuracy of quadratic-time SOTA methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261170/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N RGB images (from a video or unstructured collection) tokenized via a pretrained DINOv2 encoder into patch-level tokens (patch size 14, with one camera token and four register tokens per image), plus an optional 9-dimensional ray map (ray origin, direction, and cross product) for querying novel viewpoints. In a single forward pass it outputs per-frame camera poses (quaternion, translation, two intrinsics), depth maps with learned confidence, and local 3D point maps in camera coordinates. The TTT fast weights additionally form a compact implicit scene state that can be queried at ~100 FPS to produce novel-view RGB and depth, independent of the number of input views.

## Method

ZipMap replaces all global self-attention with large-chunk Test-Time Training (TTT) layers, interleaved with per-view local window attention (with RoPE) across 24 blocks. Each TTT layer maintains a SwiGLU-MLP as a nonlinear fast-weight function, updated via a single gradient descent step over a virtual key-value reconstruction objective that encourages the MLP to memorize key→value mappings from all input tokens. The gradient is orthonormalized via Newton-Schulz (following the Muon optimizer) and the weights are L2-normalized for stability, with per-token learning rates predicted by a linear layer. This yields O(N) bidirectional reconstruction without sequential processing, avoiding error accumulation. The updated fast weights double as a queryable implicit scene representation: applying them to target ray-map query tokens produces novel-view geometry and appearance in constant time per query, independent of input view count.

## Results

On DTU, ZipMap achieves a mean accuracy of 1.228 and completeness of 1.649, closely matching the best quadratic-time method π³ (1.151 / 1.793) while being 4× more accurate than the best linear-time baselines CUT3R (5.045) and TTT3R (5.337). On ETH3D, ZipMap reaches 0.254 accuracy and 0.865 normal consistency, within 0.066 accuracy of π³ (0.188) and substantially ahead of CUT3R (0.593) and TTT3R (0.763). For video depth on Sintel, ZipMap attains AbsRel 0.248, outperforming VGGT (0.298) and all linear baselines, while remaining close to π³ (0.228). The method reconstructs 750 frames in under 10 seconds on a single H100, over 20× faster than VGGT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the quadratic-time baselines, ZipMap is within a small margin of π³ on DTU (Acc. 1.228 vs 1.151) and ETH3D (Acc. 0.254 vs 0.188), while slightly exceeding VGGT on DTU accuracy (1.228 vs 1.308) and ETH3D normal consistency (0.865 vs 0.841). On camera pose (RealEstate10K AUC@5), ZipMap scores 53.34, falling between VGGT (38.71) and π³ (63.10), and on Co3Dv2 it reaches 62.46, between VGGT (67.84) and π³ (57.12). Against the linear-time baselines, ZipMap delivers a dramatic improvement: on DTU it is 4.1× more accurate than CUT3R (1.228 vs 5.045) and 4.3× more accurate than TTT3R (1.228 vs 5.337), and on ETH3D it is 2.3× and 3.0× better respectively. For video depth on Sintel, ZipMap (AbsRel 0.248) outperforms VGGT (0.298) and CUT3R (0.432) but remains slightly behind π³ (0.228). Overall, ZipMap matches or approaches the best quadratic-time methods on accuracy while being 20×+ faster at scale, and decisively outperforms all prior linear-time approaches by a large margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZipMap sits at the intersection of two research threads: feed-forward 3D reconstruction (VGGT, π³, DUSt3R) and linear-complexity sequence modeling via Test-Time Training (LaCT, TTT3R). It departs from the dominant quadratic-attention paradigm by replacing global self-attention with large-chunk TTT layers that compress all input views into a fixed-size SwiGLU-MLP fast-weight memory, and it departs from prior linear-time 3D methods (CUT3R, TTT3R) by being bidirectional rather than recurrent, avoiding sequential error accumulation. The Muon optimizer's Newton-Schulz orthonormalization is repurposed as the fast-weight update rule, and the DINOv2 encoder and camera head are inherited directly from VGGT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× speedup over VGGT while matching or exceeding accuracy on standard benchmarks makes large-scale feed-forward 3D reconstruction (700+ frames in <10 s on one H100) practically feasible for the first time, and the persistent fast-weight state enables real-time (~100 FPS) novel-viewpoint querying independent of input size—a capability absent from prior feed-forward models. Open questions include how the fixed-size state degrades on very large or highly dynamic scenes, whether the streaming extension (only briefly evaluated) maintains the same quality, and whether the implicit representation can be extended beyond basic geometric priors (walls, floors) to richer scene understanding. The approach also raises the broader question of whether TTT-style associative memory can replace attention in other multi-view geometry tasks.
