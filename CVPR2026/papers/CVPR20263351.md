## Problem

Existing depth completion methods simply embed sparse depth into a network without explicitly distinguishing where to predict from where to preserve, failing to capture the true essence of completion. Meanwhile, the potential of vision language models (VLMs) for geometric perception—particularly fine-grained 3D depth inference—remains largely unexplored, as prior VLM-based 3D work focuses on coarse spatial reasoning rather than per-pixel metric depth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263351/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (3×h×w) and a corresponding sparse depth map (1×h×w) as input. The sparse depth is consumed in three distinct forms: as a visual channel fused with RGB via zero-initialized convolutions, as a binary mask converted into a textual prompt (0 = predict, 1 = preserve), and as natural-language depth descriptions for supervision. The output is a dense depth map (1×h×w) reconstructed by traversing all pixels and extracting per-pixel depth values from the VLM's textual responses, each generated by querying the language decoder with a fixed prompt asking for the distance from that pixel to the camera.

## Method

Built on Qwen2.5-VL (3B) with no architectural modifications, the Sparse Depth Injection Mechanism (SDIM) integrates depth priors through three components. First, a zero-initialized convolutional block performs soft fusion of RGB-D features before the VLM's 3D-convolution tokenization, enabling gradual injection of geometric cues without disrupting pretrained image token distributions. Second, a binary mask derived from the sparse depth is converted into a fixed-template textual prompt that explicitly instructs the model whether to predict or preserve at each pixel. Third, per-pixel depth values are textualized into natural-language descriptions (e.g., 'The distance from this pixel to the camera is 5.20 meters') used as supervision with L1 loss, requiring only one labeled pixel per training sample and eliminating the need for dense ground-truth depth. The model is fine-tuned on 20K synthetic samples from Hypersim and Virtual KITTI, achieving zero-shot generalization across five diverse benchmarks.

## Results

On the seven zero-shot benchmarks, the label-free variant (w/SD) achieves MAE of 0.040 m on IBims-1, 0.185 m on VOID-150, 0.044 m on NYUv2, 0.418 m on KITTI, and 1.353 m on DDAD, outperforming the strongest prior method (Marigold-DC with test-time ensembling) by 12.5%, 4.9%, 9.1%, 3.8%, and 74.7% in MAE respectively. The ground-truth-supervised variant (w/GT) further improves to 0.036 m MAE on IBims-1 and 0.042 m on NYUv2, yielding up to 83.3% MAE reduction over the best prior method on NYUv2. In terms of efficiency, the method runs at 0.327 FPS with 46.1 GB memory, approximately 65× faster than Marigold-DC (0.005 FPS) while using less memory (46.1 GB vs. 8.64 GB for Marigold-DC's multi-step diffusion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is Marigold-DC (with test-time ensembling), a diffusion-based zero-shot method; the proposed VLM approach (w/SD) outperforms it on all seven benchmarks, with the largest margin on DDAD (74.7% MAE reduction: 1.353 vs. 2.364 m) and IBims-1 (12.5%: 0.040 vs. 0.045 m). Among task-specific architectures, BPNet and CFormer are the strongest SPN/transformer-based baselines, but the VLM method surpasses them by 35–45% in MAE on indoor benchmarks (e.g., 0.040 vs. 0.062 on IBims-1) and by 21–26% on KITTI. OGNI-DC, which uses optimization-guided neural iterations for zero-shot generalization, is outperformed on all reported benchmarks, with the gap widening at higher sparsity (VOID-1500: 0.141 vs. 0.175 MAE). VPP4DC, which reframes completion as stereo matching, is competitive on NYUv2 (0.077 MAE) but still trails the VLM method by 43% (0.044 MAE). PromptDA, which leverages the Depth Anything foundation model with LiDAR prompts, is outperformed on every benchmark, most notably on DDAD (1.353 vs. 2.107 MAE). The ground-truth-supervised variant (w/GT) further extends the lead, achieving 83.3% MAE improvement over VPP4DC on NYUv2 and 23.2% over NLSPN on IBims-1, confirming that the VLM framework benefits from denser supervision while remaining competitive in the label-free setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2.5-VL as its VLM backbone and adopts the per-pixel textual depth querying paradigm from DepthLM, but repurposes both for the fundamentally different task of depth completion rather than monocular depth estimation. It departs from the dominant zero-shot depth completion paradigm (Marigold-DC, OGNI-DC, G2-MonoDepth) that relies on diffusion models or large-scale training, instead showing that a VLM's semantic priors and instruction-following ability can substitute for task-specific architectures. The zero-initialized convolution for soft RGB-D fusion is inspired by ControlNet-style conditioning but applied at the input tokenization level rather than within the network, and the textual prompt design (binary mask → 'predict' vs. 'preserve' instructions) is a novel mechanism absent from prior VLM-based 3D perception work like SpatialVLM or SpatialBot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a VLM can perform fine-grained per-pixel geometric perception (dense depth completion) with almost no architectural modification, opening a path toward unifying semantic reasoning and 3D geometry in a single model. The label-free supervision (using only sparse depth, no ground-truth) makes the approach deployable in real-world settings where dense annotations are unavailable. However, the 46 GB memory footprint and ~0.33 FPS inference speed severely limit practical deployment, and the method fails on transparent/reflective surfaces. Open questions include whether this VLM-based paradigm extends to other dense prediction tasks (surface normals, flow) and whether lighter VLMs or distillation can close the efficiency gap.
