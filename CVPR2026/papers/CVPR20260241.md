## Problem

Existing text-enrichment methods for text-video retrieval (e.g., TMASS, TV-ProxyNet) expand text semantics using only text-frame or text-video interactions, ignoring the internal frame-to-frame relationships within a video. As a result, the enriched text embedding cannot capture temporal contextual dependencies among frames, leading to a mismatch between the text representation and the full video semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260241/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text query (max length 32 tokens, 64 for DiDeMo) and a video clip from which M frames are uniformly sampled (12 for most datasets, 64 for DiDeMo), encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16 backbone (d=512). It produces a single enriched text embedding by aggregating S=20 stochastic text candidates with learned attention weights, which is then used as the conditioning signal for text-guided frame fusion (XPool-style cross-attention) to yield the final video embedding. The Energy-Aware Matching module operates only during training via MCMC sampling, so inference cost is unchanged relative to the base CLIP pipeline.

## Method

EagleNet comprises two components. Fine-Grained Relationship Learning (FRL) constructs a heterogeneous text-frame graph with three edge types (text-text, frame-frame, text-frame) over the original text, S stochastic text candidates, and M frames (with learnable temporal positional encodings), then applies a multi-layer Relational Graph Attention Network to extract inter-node relationships; the final-layer text-frame edge weights are averaged across heads and frames to produce per-candidate attention scores that aggregate the candidates into a context-aware enriched text embedding. Energy-Aware Matching (EAM) models the true text-video pair distribution via a Boltzmann energy function (bilinear scoring over individual text-frame pairs, averaged across frames) and is trained with a negative log-likelihood objective using K=20-step MCMC sampling to generate negative samples, thereby reinforcing fine-grained text-frame interaction learning within FRL. Finally, the conventional softmax contrastive loss is replaced with a sigmoid loss that treats each text-video pair independently, mitigating sensitivity to batch size and negative-sample composition while naturally accommodating the multi-matched nature of TVR.

## Results

On MSRVTT (ViT-B/16), EagleNet achieves text-to-video R@1 of 51.0%, R@5 of 76.2%, R@10 of 85.6%, and Rsum of 425.7, outperforming the strongest baselines (Video-ColBERT at 50.0% R@1, GLSCL at 49.9% R@1). On DiDeMo (ViT-B/16), it reaches 51.5% R@1 and 86.8% R@10, surpassing TempMe (50.2% R@1) and TV-ProxyNet (47.9% R@1). Across all four benchmarks (MSRVTT, DiDeMo, MSVD, VATEX), EagleNet attains the highest Rsum (687.7 on the combined DiDeMo+MSVD+VATEX evaluation), with the only R@1 loss being on VATEX where TV-ProxyNet edges ahead by 0.4% (64.0 vs. 63.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EagleNet outperforms all 25 compared baselines on Rsum across the four benchmarks. On MSRVTT (ViT-B/16), it leads Video-ColBERT (50.0% R@1) by 1.0% and GLSCL/TempMe (49.9% R@1) by 1.1% on text-to-video R@1, while also achieving the best video-to-text R@1 (49.2%) and the lowest median rank (MdR=1.0). On DiDeMo, EagleNet (51.5% R@1) beats TempMe (50.2%) by 1.3% and TV-ProxyNet (47.9%) by 3.6%; on MSVD it surpasses TV-ProxyNet (49.7%) by 1.2%. The closest competitor overall is Video-ColBERT on MSRVTT (Rsum 417.8 vs. 425.7) and TV-ProxyNet on VATEX, where TV-ProxyNet narrowly wins R@1 (64.0 vs. 63.6) but EagleNet still leads on Rsum (687.7 vs. 676.6). Notably, after fixing a data-leakage issue in TMASS's original code, TMASS's performance drops below its XPool baseline (48.5% vs. 49.2% R@1 on MSRVTT), whereas EagleNet's multi-candidate FRL approach avoids this degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

EagleNet sits at the intersection of three research threads in text-video retrieval: (1) CLIP-based end-to-end retrieval (CLIP4Clip, XPool, TS2Net), (2) text-enrichment methods that expand brief captions to better match video semantics (TMASS, TV-ProxyNet), and (3) graph-based relational learning (HGR, ACP, SHE-Net). The key departure from prior text-enrichment work is the construction of a joint text-frame graph via RGAT that captures not only text-frame interactions but also internal frame-to-frame temporal relations, which TMASS and TV-ProxyNet ignore. Additionally, EagleNet is the first to integrate an energy-based model (trained via MCMC) into TVR for fine-grained text-frame distribution modeling, and replaces the standard softmax contrastive loss with the sigmoid loss from Zhai et al. for more stable, batch-size-robust training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: brief text captions cannot fully encode video semantics, and prior enrichment methods that only model text-frame interactions miss temporal context, leading to retrieval of semantically similar but contextually wrong videos. The EBM integration and the finding that TMASS (after fixing a data-leakage bug) actually underperforms its XPool baseline suggest that naive stochastic text sampling introduces noise, motivating the more structured multi-candidate aggregation in FRL. Open questions include whether the graph-based approach scales to longer videos or higher frame counts, whether the MCMC-based EAM training cost becomes prohibitive at larger batch sizes, and whether the sigmoid loss advantage persists in retrieval settings with very large candidate pools.
