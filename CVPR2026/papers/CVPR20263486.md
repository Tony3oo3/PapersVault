## Problem

Existing structure-based camera relocalization methods rely on point correspondences built atop SfM maps, textured meshes, or dense point clouds, which are costly to construct, require realistic visual appearance or pose priors, and struggle with cross-modal matching. This paper investigates whether planar primitives—compact, ubiquitous in indoor scenes—can serve as a sufficient geometric basis for lightweight 6-DoF relocalization against untextured 3D planar maps, without any of those auxiliary requirements.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB query image and an untextured 3D planar map (a collection of piecewise planar surfaces, each parameterized by a plane equation and a bounding polygon) as input, and outputs a 6-DoF camera pose (rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation t ∈ R³) mapping camera coordinates to map coordinates. The query image is first decomposed into planar primitives via a frozen monocular geometry model (MoGe-2) plus sequential RANSAC plane fitting, yielding metric-scale plane parameters and binary 2D segment masks; no per-scene training, pose initialization, or map truncation is required at test time.

## Method

PlanaReLoc is a three-stage plane-centric pipeline. (1) Front-end embedding: query primitives are encoded by average-pooling ViT patch features within each 2D segment, while map primitives are encoded by two PointNet-based encoders—an object encoder capturing shape and a scene encoder capturing spatial pose—fused via a learnable weighted sum. (2) Matching: a 4-layer transformer with self- and cross-attention processes both embedding sets jointly, using a RoPE-style positional encoding computed from plane-normal relative rotations; correspondences are extracted from a soft assignment matrix (matchability × similarity) via Mutual Nearest Neighbor with a confidence threshold, trained with a negative log-likelihood objective. (3) Pose estimation and refinement: a minimal two-plane-pair solver determines rotation under RANSAC, translation is recovered by weighted least squares with a joint scale factor compensating monocular metric ambiguity, and a final per-primitive depth-alignment step jointly optimizes a residual transformation and per-plane offset seeds against a rendered depth map.

## Results

On ScanNet, PlanaReLoc (full pipeline) achieves a median rotation error of 3.8° and median translation error of 0.20 m, with pose recalls of 48.5% (0.2 m, 10°), 73.1% (0.5 m, 15°), and 81.8% (1.0 m, 30°), all without requiring pose priors, map appearance, or per-scene training. On 12Scenes, the method reaches 4.7° median rotation error and 0.19 m median translation error, with 50.6% recall at the strictest threshold. In matching evaluation, PlanaReLoc attains 64.3% F1 and 91.8% AP on ScanNet, surpassing all point-based and plane-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlanaReLoc substantially outperforms all structure-based baselines on ScanNet without any auxiliary inputs. Against GeoTransformer (42.1°/1.80 m median) and FreeReg (27.2°/1.41 m), which perform image-to-point-cloud registration, PlanaReLoc reduces median rotation error by 35° and 23° respectively, and translation error by 1.6 m and 1.2 m. Against the strongest MeshLoc variant (SP+LG with coarse initialization and colored map rendering: 12.9°/0.52 m), PlanaReLoc still achieves lower errors (3.8°/0.20 m) while requiring neither pose priors nor map appearance. On 12Scenes, PlanaReLoc (4.7°/0.19 m, 50.6% recall at 0.2 m/10°) beats MatchAnything (7.9°/0.20 m, 46.9%), MASt3R (12.0°/0.30 m, 45.2%), and NOPE-SAC (17.7°/0.54 m, 2.9%) across all thresholds. In the matching evaluation (Table 2), PlanaReLoc's plane-level F1 of 64.3% and AP of 91.8% on ScanNet exceed MASt3R (52.0%/84.1%), MatchAnything (45.0%/67.7%), and the prior plane-based method NOPE-SAC (41.9%/79.1%), confirming the advantage of region-based planar primitives for cross-modal structure matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

PlanaReLoc departs from the dominant point-correspondence paradigm in structure-based relocalization (SfM maps, MeshLoc, image-to-point-cloud registration) by elevating planar primitives to the fundamental matching unit. Architecturally, it repurposes LightGlue's transformer-based soft-assignment matching framework for plane-level features, introducing a RoPE variant that encodes relative plane-normal rotations as positional embeddings. The monocular plane recovery front-end is deliberately plug-and-play (MoGe-2 + sequential RANSAC), and the pose estimation stage adapts classical RANSAC/Kabsch to the plane-parameter formulation, while the depth-alignment refinement draws on per-primitive photometric alignment from SuperPrimitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that compact, untextured 3D planar maps—already widely used in AR and robotics—can serve as a sufficient scene representation for 6-DoF relocalization without requiring realistic textures, pose priors, or per-scene training, which is a practical win for deployment. A key limitation is the dependence on structured indoor environments where planar surfaces dominate; generalization to unstructured or outdoor scenes with curved/irregular geometry remains open. The monocular front-end's metric-scale errors are mitigated but not eliminated by the joint scale optimization, and the approach's performance degrades when plane observations are sparse or over-segmented, suggesting room for improvement in the recovery module.
