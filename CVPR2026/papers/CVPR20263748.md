## Problem

Despite extensive work on visual affordance across fully-supervised, weakly-supervised, and open-vocabulary paradigms, it remains unclear what internal capabilities within Visual Foundation Models actually enable affordance understanding. The paper posits that affordance reasoning requires two complementary capacities—geometric perception of object parts and interaction perception of agent-object engagement—and asks whether these exist as distinct, composable elements within pre-trained VFMs rather than being inseparable artifacts of a particular training recipe.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single RGB image and a templated text prompt encoding an agent-object-verb triplet (e.g., "add a hand to hold the knife") as input. DINOv3 produces dense patch-level feature maps at ViT token resolution, while Flux Kontext generates verb- and object-token cross-attention maps during a synthetic image-editing pass. The output is a spatially aligned, verb-specific affordance mask at the input image resolution, produced entirely without any affordance-specific training data or fine-tuning; evaluation on AGD20K uses egocentric test images paired with exocentric interaction exemplars, while UMD provides categorical pixel masks for qualitative geometric validation.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free fusion of two independently extracted cues from frozen pre-trained models. DINOv3's dense features are cropped to an object ROI (guided by Flux Kontext's object-token attention) and decomposed via PCA into interpretable part-level geometric prototypes, deliberately isolating shape-centric structure from the geometric-semantic entanglement the authors document in DINO features. In parallel, Flux Kontext's verb-token cross-attention—recorded across layers during a controlled image-editing generation of the agent-object-verb triplet—serves as a zero-shot interaction prior that localizes probable contact regions without any supervision. Normalized Scanpath Saliency (NSS) scores each geometric prototype against the verb attention map, and the best-aligned prototype is fused with the verb attention to yield the final affordance mask. The entire pipeline requires no gradient updates, no task-specific supervision, and no dataset-specific training, operating purely on frozen model internals, and the authors show that swapping DINOv3 for DINOv2 yields nearly identical performance, confirming the approach depends on part-centric geometric structure rather than a particular checkpoint.

## Results

On AGD20K, the training-free Interaction × Geometry fusion achieves KLD 1.493, SIM 0.326, and NSS 1.090, competitive with weakly-supervised baselines despite requiring no affordance-specific training. The interaction-only variant (Flux Kontext verb attention alone) scores KLD 1.825, SIM 0.271, NSS 1.050, and adding DINOv3 geometric prototypes via PCA consistently improves all three metrics. On UMD, linear probing shows that models with stronger geometric awareness (DINOv2, DINOv3) achieve higher mIoU, with correlation coefficients of r = −0.61 (depth error) and r = −0.71 (surface-normal error) between Probing3D geometric scores and affordance segmentation accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest weakly-supervised baseline LOCATE (KLD 1.405, SIM 0.372, NSS 1.157), the proposed training-free method trails on all three metrics (KLD 1.493, SIM 0.326, NSS 1.090), but does so without any affordance-specific training data. Compared to the fully-supervised AffordanceLLM, the method is slightly worse on KLD (1.493 vs. 1.463) and SIM (0.326 vs. 0.377) but marginally better on NSS (1.090 vs. 1.070). The method clearly outperforms Cross-View-AG on all metrics (KLD 1.493 vs. 1.787, SIM 0.326 vs. 0.285, NSS 1.090 vs. 0.829) and dramatically surpasses Affordance-R1 (KLD 1.493 vs. 9.730). In the verb-conditioned localization comparison (Fig. 9), Flux Kontext achieves the highest NSS over 25 unseen AGD20K categories, outperforming GroundingDINO, MaskCLIP, and EVF-SAM, which either capture global semantics without localizing functional regions (GroundingDINO, MaskCLIP) or show diffuse verb attention despite part-level segmentation (EVF-SAM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three established affordance learning paradigms (fully-supervised, weakly-supervised, open-vocabulary) by proposing a dual-dimensional decomposition—geometric perception and interaction perception—as a unified analytical lens. It builds on DINOv3's part-level geometric representations (in the Probing3D lineage) and, more notably, repurposes Flux Kontext's text-image cross-attention as a source of verb-conditioned spatial interaction priors, which to the authors' knowledge is the first use of generative model internals for affordance estimation. Rather than training a new model, the work probes existing VFMs and fuses their complementary capabilities in a training-free pipeline, positioning itself as a mechanistic investigation rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled decomposition of affordance into two composable primitives (part-level geometry and verb-conditioned interaction), offering a framework that transcends supervision-specific paradigms and could guide future model design. The training-free fusion achieving competitive zero-shot performance suggests that affordance understanding may already be latent in pretrained models, shifting the research question from 'how to train for affordance' to 'how to extract and compose existing capabilities.' Key limitations include the shallow, open-loop fusion strategy, noisy noun-conditioned attention, and evaluation on only two datasets; the authors note that video generative models could naturally unify both dimensions, pointing toward a promising but unexplored direction.
