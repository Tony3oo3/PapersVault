## Problem

Existing video diffusion models for novel view synthesis can control camera viewpoint but treat temporal progression as strictly monotonic, preventing effects like bullet-time, reverse playback, or retimed motion from novel angles. No prior method enables free, continuous navigation along both the spatial (camera) and temporal (animation state) axes from a single monocular video, and no public dataset provides the paired spatiotemporal supervision needed to train such a model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261018/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a monocular source video (F×C×H×W), a target camera trajectory as per-frame 3×4 extrinsic matrices defined relative to the source's first frame, and an F-dimensional temporal control vector specifying the animation timestamp for each output frame. It produces a target video of the same resolution (81 RGB frames decoded from 21 latent frames via a 3D VAE) that preserves the source scene's dynamics and appearance while following the prescribed camera path and temporal mapping. Unlike prior camera-control methods, the target camera need not start at the same pose as the source, and the temporal signal can be non-monotonic (e.g., constant for bullet-time, reversed for reverse playback).

## Method

SpaceTimePilot builds on the Wan-2.1 T2V1.3B backbone (3D VAE + DiT) and introduces three key mechanisms for space-time disentanglement. First, an 'animation time' embedding computes sinusoidal positional encodings at the original F-frame level, compresses them to the F′ latent-frame level via two 1D convolutions, and adds them to the camera-conditioned video tokens (Eq. 2–3), providing a continuous, fine-grained temporal control signal distinct from RoPE frame indices. Second, a source-aware camera conditioning injects both source and target camera poses into the DiT, enabling the generated video to start from an arbitrary viewpoint rather than being locked to the source's first frame. Third, a temporal-warping training augmentation applies reverse, acceleration, freeze, slow-motion, and zigzag mappings to target videos in existing multi-view datasets, supplying explicit non-monotonic temporal supervision without new data collection. Joint training on these warped sequences plus the newly constructed Cam×Time dataset (360k synthetic videos covering the full camera-time grid across 100 scenes) yields precise, disentangled control over both axes.

## Results

On the Cam×Time test set for temporal control, SpaceTimePilot achieves an average PSNR of 21.29, SSIM of 0.7459, and LPIPS of 0.2308, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ReCamM+jointdata (PSNR 17.86, SSIM 0.7250, LPIPS 0.3073) by ~3.4 dB in PSNR. For camera control on the 90-video OpenVideoHD evaluation set, SpaceTimePilot achieves the best relative rotation error (2.71°) and first-frame alignment (RTA@15 = 35.19%, RTA@30 = 54.44%), compared to ReCamMaster's 7.61% and 10.20% respectively. On VBench visual quality, SpaceTimePilot scores 0.6486 on Image Quality, the highest among all methods, while remaining competitive on other dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaceTimePilot decisively outperforms all baselines on temporal control. Against TrajectoryCrafter (adapted with frame-rearrangement), it gains ~6.7 dB in average PSNR (21.29 vs. 14.56) and reduces LPIPS by more than half (0.2308 vs. 0.5276). Against ReCamMaster with joint-dataset training (the strongest adapted baseline), SpaceTimePilot still leads by 3.4 dB PSNR and 0.077 LPIPS, with the gap widening on bullet-time (21.97 vs. 17.69 PSNR). For camera control on real-world videos, SpaceTimePilot achieves the lowest relative rotation error (2.71° vs. 4.26° for ReCamMaster and 5.94° for TrajectoryCrafter) and dramatically better first-frame alignment (RTA@30 of 54.44% vs. 10.20% for ReCamMaster), confirming that the source-aware camera conditioning in Eq. 3 is critical. Notably, ReCamM+Aug (retrained with non-identical first frames but without source camera conditioning) actually performs worse than the original ReCamMaster on absolute metrics, suggesting that simply augmenting data without the explicit c_src signal introduces confusion rather than improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaceTimePilot extends the camera-conditioned video diffusion paradigm established by ReCamMaster [1] by introducing a dedicated 'animation time' embedding that is disentangled from camera signals, enabling independent control over spatial viewpoint and temporal progression. It departs from prior 4D scene generation work (4DiM, CAT4D) that relies on explicit 3D reconstruction pipelines or joint training on static+dynamic datasets, instead achieving space-time disentanglement purely within a latent diffusion framework. The temporal-warping training scheme repurposes existing multi-view video datasets (ReCamMaster, SynCamMaster) to simulate diverse temporal behaviors without new data collection, while the Cam×Time synthetic dataset provides the dense full-grid supervision that prior datasets lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single video diffusion model can simultaneously and independently control camera trajectory and temporal playback (bullet-time, reverse, slow-motion) from one monocular video, opening the door to interactive 4D scene exploration without explicit 3D reconstruction. The Cam×Time dataset (360k videos with full camera-time grid coverage) and the temporal-warping augmentation are practical contributions likely to be adopted by subsequent work on controllable video generation. Open questions include generalization to longer sequences beyond the 81-frame limit, handling of highly dynamic or topologically changing scenes, and whether the approach scales to real-world uncontrolled footage without synthetic supervision.
