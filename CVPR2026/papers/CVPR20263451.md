## Problem

Existing unsupervised cross-domain image retrieval (UCDIR) methods rely on discrete pseudo-labels from clustering for semantic supervision, which are often inaccurate and fail to provide comprehensive guidance. Additionally, spatial-domain alignment strategies (adversarial, statistical, contrastive) entangle domain-specific and semantic features, causing semantic degradation in learned representations. The phase spectrum, known to encode structural cues robust to domain shifts, remains largely underexplored as a mechanism for domain-invariant feature extraction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images from two source domains (e.g., Art and Real on Office-Home, or Clipart and Sketch on DomainNet) without any class annotations. Each image is processed in two parallel forms: the RGB version feeds a standard image encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-B), while a grayscale version undergoes FFT to extract phase-spectrum features. The output is a shared embedding space where semantically similar images across domains are projected nearby, enabling retrieval by cosine similarity against class prototypes and text-prompt features at test time.

## Method

TPSNet comprises two modules. The Domain Prompt Generation Module (DPG) initializes learnable class-specific text prompts per domain from K-means pseudo-labels, then optimizes only the prompt tokens (with frozen CLIP encoders) via a re-paired contrastive loss that partially corrects clustering errors, yielding refined text priors. The Text-Phase Dual Priors Network (TPDP) adopts a dual-path design: the text branch encodes the learned prompts into text features, while the image branch fuses RGB encoder features with phase features extracted from a lightweight conv subnetwork operating on IFFT-reconstructed phase images (amplitude replaced by a constant, phase spectrum retained). A cross-attention mechanism then integrates the two priors, with text features as queries and fused visual features as keys/values, producing final domain-invariant representations. Training combines a prototype cross-entropy loss (with momentum-updated class prototypes) and an image-to-text contrastive loss with label smoothing to mitigate residual pseudo-label noise.

## Results

On Office-Home with ViT-B, TPSNet achieves 84.53% P@1, 83.44% P@5, and 81.29% P@15, surpassing the previous SOTA (SA-MoE) by 19.82%, 22.00%, and 24.48% respectively. With ResNet-50, TPSNet reaches 61.80% P@1 on Office-Home and 80.36% P@50 on DomainNet, outperforming ShieldIR (SOTA) by 11.51% and 7.11% respectively. TPSNet also demonstrates backbone robustness: even when initialized with MoCo-v2 or DINO instead of CLIP, it still exceeds the SOTA baselines on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPSNet outperforms all six baselines (DD, CoDA, ProtoOT, ShieldIR, DG-UCDIR, SA-MoE) on nearly every metric across both datasets and both backbones. The largest margins are observed with ViT-B on Office-Home, where TPSNet exceeds SA-MoE (the strongest ViT-B baseline) by ~20–24% in P@1/P@5/P@15, and with ResNet-50 where it beats ShieldIR by ~11.5% in P@1. On DomainNet, TPSNet achieves particularly large gains on noisy domains: on Infograph→Real and Infograph→Sketch with ViT-B, it surpasses SA-MoE by more than 20% in P@50. Notably, even when the CLIP backbone is replaced with MoCo-v2 or DINO, TPSNet still outperforms the respective SOTA baselines (ShieldIR for ResNet-50, SA-MoE for ViT-B), confirming that the gains stem from the text-phase dual-prior design rather than CLIP-specific advantages. The only scenarios where TPSNet shows smaller margins are highly abstract domains like Quickdraw, where fine-grained semantic information is inherently limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPSNet sits at the intersection of three research threads in UCDIR: (1) pseudo-label-based contrastive learning and prototype alignment (DD, CoDA, ProtoOT, ShieldIR, SA-MoE), (2) CLIP-based prompt learning for cross-modal semantic supervision, and (3) frequency-domain domain alignment (FDA, FUDA). Rather than building directly on any single prior method, the paper repurposes CLIP's text encoder to generate learnable domain-specific prompts as a text prior, and introduces the phase spectrum—previously underexplored in UCDIR—as a complementary phase prior. The key conceptual departure from prior UCDIR work is the explicit dual-prior synergy via cross-attention, which addresses the semantic degradation problem that plagues spatial-domain alignment strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining a text prior (learned domain prompts) with a phase prior (Fourier phase features) yields substantially more robust domain-invariant representations than either signal alone, with particularly large gains on noisy, high-variation domains like Infograph. This suggests that frequency-domain structural cues and language-grounded semantic cues are complementary rather than redundant, opening a direction for multi-signal domain alignment beyond spatial-domain adversarial or transport-based methods. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with pairwise domain settings, reliance on CLIP's pre-trained text encoder for the prompt module, and the phase feature encoder being a lightweight CNN that may not capture all structural invariants in highly abstract domains (e.g., Quickdraw). Whether the dual-prior framework extends to multi-source, open-vocabulary, or video retrieval settings remains open.
