## Problem

Existing audio-driven 3D facial animation methods either generate isolated single-speaker 'talking heads' or produce multi-person outputs that resemble video conference calls, failing to model the dynamic 3D spatial relationship—relative position, orientation, and mutual gaze—that is essential for realistic in-person dialogue. No prior work explicitly synthesizes co-located, spatially-aware dyadic conversations from a single mixed audio stream with controllable spatial layout.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262469/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 10-second mixed audio waveform at 16 kHz (converted to 768-dim Wav2Vec 2.0 features) containing overlapping speech from two participants, plus an optional natural-language spatial prompt (e.g., 'arguing across a table') processed by a few-shot LLM to yield initial 3D head translations. The output is a synchronized 250-frame sequence (25 fps) of 78-dimensional animation parameters per participant, comprising 63 expression coefficients, 12 skeletal pose values (4 joints × 3), and 3 global translation coordinates in metric meters, all defined in a shared 3DMM coordinate frame.

## Method

The core architecture is a dual-stream denoising diffusion model with a shared U-Net backbone that processes both participants' noisy latents in parallel, ensuring a unified speaker-agnostic representation. Inter-speaker interaction is modeled via frame-level cross-attention layers in the decoder (no temporal attention, preserving immediate reactivity), while speaker role embeddings—learnable speak/listen vectors linearly interpolated by per-frame speaker probability—provide continuous interaction-state conditioning through FiLM modulation alongside Wav2Vec audio features. Training follows a two-stage strategy: pre-training on 50,000+ hours of curated in-the-wild dyadic conversations (2M+ pairs) for interaction dynamics, then fine-tuning on a synthetic dubbing dataset with perfect ground-truth lip motion, applying L2 loss only to the 20 mouth-region expression parameters of the active speaker. A novel auxiliary eye gaze loss (cosine similarity on averaged left/right gaze vectors) is applied selectively to the top-20% head-rotation-variance samples to promote natural mutual eye contact, and the model is conditioned on first-frame translations to enable LLM-driven text-to-3D spatial layout control at inference.

## Results

The full model achieves FD=21.71 and P-FD=22.56 on rendered outputs, with expression MSE of 5.97, translation MSE of 1.50, and rotation MSE of 2.48 on 3DMM parameters. In the forced-choice user study (31 participants, 14 clip groups), the full model is preferred 78.3% of the time for lip-sync quality, 76.2% for listener movements, 73.7% for speaker movements, 73.3% for interaction quality, and 68.6% for eye gaze realism. The two-stage training strategy is critical: the first-stage-only model drops to 17.2% preference for lip-sync versus 78.3% for the full model, confirming the value of high-quality lip fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three categories of baselines: single-speaker models (CodeTalker, FaceFormer, SelfTalk), speaker+listener combinations (DIM, DualTalk, plus retrieval-based listeners), and the two-speaker interaction model L2L. The method achieves the best performance across nearly all quantitative metrics (FD, P-FD, MSE on expression/rotation/translation, vMSE on vertices, and SID for diversity). The single-person baseline slightly outperforms the full model on speaker vertex prediction accuracy, but is far worse on listener and interaction modeling, confirming that cross-attention and role embeddings are essential for dyadic coherence. DualTalk, the closest conversation baseline, is limited to 'video-conferencing'-style talking heads without spatially aware co-located movement, and trails the proposed method by a wide margin in the user study (e.g., 8.8% vs. 68.6% preference for gaze). Retrieval-based listener baselines (Listen-A, Listen-M) occasionally produce more diverse rotations and translations but with significantly lower fidelity. L2L does not generate both speakers simultaneously, making it structurally unable to model real-time mutual interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-driven conversation generation line (DualTalk, L2L, DIM) by adding explicit 3D spatial modeling — relative head positions, orientations, and mutual gaze — that prior 'video-conference-style' methods entirely omit. Architecturally, it repurposes standard diffusion U-Net and cross-attention components into a dual-stream shared-weight design with FiLM conditioning and speaker role embeddings, drawing on Wav2Vec 2.0 for audio features and 3DMM for parametric face representation. The dataset curation pipeline (in-the-wild video processing plus synthetic dubbing from single-speaker clips) is a practical contribution that addresses the data scarcity bottleneck, but the core generative architecture follows well-established diffusion paradigms without introducing new learning objectives beyond the auxiliary gaze loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to produce spatially coherent, co-located 3D dyadic conversations from a single mixed audio track, opening a path toward realistic VR and telepresence applications where participants share a physical space. The 50,000+ hour dataset with 10k+ identities is a significant resource for the field, and the two-stage training strategy (interaction pre-training + lip-sync fine-tuning) offers a practical recipe for handling the quality gap between in-the-wild and studio data. Key limitations include restriction to two participants, reliance on 3DMM (limiting expressiveness to the model's basis), and the LLM-based spatial control being a few-shot heuristic rather than a learned component. Open questions include scaling to groups of three or more, real-time inference, and whether the spatial modeling generalizes to full-body interaction.
