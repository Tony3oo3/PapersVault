## Problem

Existing visual-language grounding methods are limited to coarse object-level localization and do not extend to actionable grasp understanding, while traditional robotic grasping relies on geometric cues without language or semantic guidance. No prior dataset or model unifies fine-grained semantic grounding with grasp-level manipulation reasoning in real-world (non-synthetic) scenes, leaving a gap between perception and language-driven manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261601/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a single RGB image paired with a natural language instruction (e.g., 'grasp the red cup on the left of the bowl') and produces structured outputs in a \n<answer>...</answer> format. Depending on the task, the answer encodes two point coordinates for bounding boxes and contact points, a 4-tuple (x, y, θ, w) for grasp poses, or a bounding box that is passed to a frozen SAM2 to yield the final segmentation mask. The underlying dataset spans ~165K real-world images, ~800 object instances, 1.3M segmentation/detection/language annotations, and ~11B grasp examples.

## Method

RealVLG-R1 fine-tunes a pretrained LVLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) using Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) inspired by DeepSeek-R1, replacing supervised fine-tuning to handle the multi-solution nature of grasp prediction. The policy is optimized via GRPO (token-level importance weights) or GSPO (sequence-level clipped weights) with a KL-divergence regularizer, and a composite reward function combines format compliance with task-specific geometric accuracy: binary IoU thresholding for bounding boxes, IoU + S-measure for segmentation, negative Huber loss over (x, y, cos θ, sin θ, w) for grasp poses, and IoU + L2 point distance for contact points. The supporting RealVLG-11B dataset unifies five real-world grasping datasets through a four-stage annotation pipeline (GPT-4o meta-description → GPT-4o language instruction → Qwen-VL-Max bounding-box verification → SAM2 segmentation) followed by human cross-validation, ensuring semantic-visual alignment across all annotation modalities.

## Results

On the Seen split, RealVLG-R1-7B (GSPO) achieves 89.0% gIoU for bounding box detection, 88.9% Fβ for segmentation, 33.6% mIoU for grasp pose prediction, and 55.3% mIoU for contact point prediction, all with 100% validity rate. On the Novel split (37 unseen objects), the model retains 88.5% gIoU for Bbox and 28.9% mIoU for Contact, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization. Compared to the strongest open-source baseline (Qwen2.5VL-7B + SFT), RealVLG-R1 improves Bbox gIoU by +24.2 points (64.8% → 89.0%) and Grasp mIoU by +28.5 points (5.1% → 33.6%) on the Seen set. The 3B GRPO variant achieves 34.7% Grasp mIoU and 40.3% gAcc on Seen, outperforming the 7B GSPO variant on fine-grained grasp precision while the 7B model leads on Contact accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealVLG-R1 decisively outperforms all open-source baselines on grasping and contact prediction: the 7B GSPO variant achieves 33.6% Grasp mIoU vs. 5.1% for Qwen2.5VL-7B+SFT and 4.1% for the base Qwen2.5VL-7B, a gap of over 28 points. On bounding box detection, Qwen-VL-Max retains a narrow edge (92.3% vs. 89.0% gIoU on Seen), but its validity rate drops to ~61% and its Grasp mIoU (16.0%) is less than half that of RealVLG-R1. Gemini 2.5 Flash performs poorly on segmentation (3.3% Fβ) and grasping (3.2% mIoU), suggesting its architecture is ill-suited for fine-grained geometric prediction despite reasonable Bbox scores (~60%). Among open-source models, SFT provides only marginal gains (~5% gIoU) over the base Qwen2.5VL, while RL fine-tuning (GRPO/GSPO) yields 20–30 point improvements across all tasks. On the Novel split, RealVLG-R1-7B (GSPO) maintains 88.5% Bbox gIoU and 28.9% Contact mIoU, substantially ahead of Qwen2.5VL-7B+SFT (64.9% and 26.4%), confirming that reinforcement learning provides superior zero-shot generalization to unseen objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: visual-language grounding (referring expression comprehension, open-world detection/segmentation via LVLMs) and robotic grasp pose estimation (geometric, language-free methods like GraspNet-1Billion, GraspClutter6D). It departs from prior VLG work by extending the output space from bounding boxes/masks to include grasp poses and contact points, and from prior grasping work by conditioning on fine-grained natural language rather than purely geometric cues. The training methodology draws directly on the RLVR paradigm from DeepSeek-R1, adapting GRPO and GSPO policy optimization to a vision-language grounding setting, which is a novel application of these algorithms to multi-solution geometric prediction tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RealVLG-11B dataset (165K images, 11B grasps, multi-granularity annotations verified by both LVLMs and humans) fills a clear gap: no prior real-world dataset simultaneously provides segmentation, detection, grasp poses, contact points, and fine-grained language descriptions. The benchmark's Seen/Similar/Novel split enables systematic evaluation of zero-shot generalization, which is critical for deploying language-driven manipulation in unseen environments. Key open questions remain: absolute grasping accuracy is still low (mIoU ~30%), the framework is limited to 2D rectangular grasps and ~800 object categories, and the authors themselves note the need to extend to 3D space. Whether the RLVR training paradigm generalizes beyond this specific task formulation to other multi-solution robotic perception problems is an important direction.
