## Problem

Synthetic data generation (SDG) aims to share private data distributions without exposing individual records, but existing privacy defenses (differential privacy, de-duplication, masking, post-hoc filtering) consistently degrade downstream utility. The core difficulty is that evaluating utility requires training a downstream model to convergence on synthetic data, making the resulting loss non-differentiable with respect to the generative model and rendering standard gradient-based optimization infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263877/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a private RGB image dataset, a frozen Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) whose class-specific embeddings are optimized via textual inversion (5,000 steps per class, 50 sampling steps with classifier-free guidance), and either the true downstream task loss J or a surrogate pretrained loss when J is unavailable. It produces a binary selection vector γ ∈ {0,1}^M that retains a subset of the M generated samples (e.g., 500 out of 1,000–2,000 candidates) and the corresponding updated generative model parameters ω_g. The selection operates at the individual-sample granularity, so the output is a pruned synthetic dataset rather than a modified generation process.

## Method

PrivSynth formulates the privacy-utility trade-off as a bi-objective optimization and introduces a data selection parameter γ to decouple generator training from data quality control, yielding an alternating optimization over ω_g and γ. The γ sub-problem is cast as a discrete-time optimal control problem by unrolling T steps of gradient descent on the downstream model, with the selection vector as a time-invariant control and the model parameters as the state. Pontryagin's Maximum Principle is then applied: a forward pass performs steepest-descent training, a backward adjoint pass propagates task sensitivity via Hessian-vector products (implemented with PyTorch's JVP API for scalability), and the Hamiltonian maximization over γ jointly balances cumulative utility gain against a privacy penalty. Privacy is quantified by two attack-agnostic metrics—pixel-level leakage (negative expected LPIPS/MSE distance to the nearest private sample) and membership-level leakage (absolute difference in mean confidence statistics between private and synthetic sets)—both weighted into the control cost. Theorem 1 proves that under standard smoothness and convexity conditions the PMP conditions yield a stationary solution of the surrogate objective, guaranteeing a provably optimal privacy-utility trade-off.

## Results

On DomainNet, PrivSynth achieves a KID of 31.53 and FID of 130.70, with a membership inference attack success rate (MIA ASR) of only 0.02, compared to DP (KID 40.07, MIA 0.38), Masking (KID 54.12, MIA 0.32), and DeDuplication (KID 32.77, MIA 0.51). On ImageNet, PrivSynth reaches KID 73.61 and FID 171.11 with MIA ASR of 0.01, outperforming all baselines on both fidelity and privacy. The ablation on DomainNet shows MIA ASR drops from 48.1% (private data) to 1.9% after just two alternating optimization rounds, while downstream accuracy remains above 67%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrivSynth dominates all four baselines on the joint fidelity-privacy objective. Against Differential Privacy (Abadi et al., CCS 2016), PrivSynth achieves lower KID (31.53 vs. 40.07 on DomainNet; 73.61 vs. 75.66 on ImageNet) and dramatically lower MIA ASR (0.02 vs. 0.38; 0.01 vs. 0.31), showing that DP's noise injection degrades distributional fidelity while still leaking membership information. Against Masking (He et al., IEEE TCSVT 2024), the gap is even wider: KID of 31.53 vs. 54.12 on DomainNet and 73.61 vs. 91.34 on ImageNet, with MIA ASR reduced from 0.32 to 0.02. Against DeDuplication (Kandpal et al., ICML 2022), PrivSynth achieves comparable KID (31.53 vs. 32.77 on DomainNet) but a far superior MIA ASR (0.02 vs. 0.51), indicating that deduplication alone is insufficient for membership-level protection. Anti-Memorization Guidance (Chen et al., CVPR 2024) is compared only in the trade-off curves (Figure 2), where PrivSynth achieves a better Pareto frontier, maintaining utility closer to the No-Defense baseline while reducing both pixel-level and membership-level leakage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Textual Inversion framework for diffusion-based synthetic data generation and the growing literature on privacy defenses (DP, de-duplication, masking, anti-memorization guidance), but departs from the one-size-fits-all mitigation paradigm by formulating privacy-utility as a joint bi-objective optimization. The key methodological lineage is the recent work on data selection via optimal control for language models [14], which this paper adapts to the image-generation setting and augments with explicit pixel-level and membership-level privacy penalties in the control cost. The alternating optimization between generator parameters and a binary selection vector is a structural departure from prior SDG pipelines that apply a single post-hoc privacy filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PrivSynth offers a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic privacy filters, with convergence guarantees under standard smoothness conditions, which is rare in the SDG-privacy literature. However, the framework requires the data provider to retain access to the private dataset for computing pixel-level and membership-level leakage metrics, limiting its applicability in fully federated or one-shot transfer settings. Open questions include scalability to very large candidate pools (the Hessian-vector product cost grows with unroll length T), whether the PMP-based selection generalizes to non-diffusion generators, and how the method behaves under adaptive or multi-round adversaries beyond the semi-honest model assumed here.
