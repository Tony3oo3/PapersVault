## Problem

Cinematic video generation requires producing scene-consistent videos with new dynamic subjects under large camera movements, but existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: 2D context-based approaches lack spatial understanding for large viewpoint changes, while explicit 3D reconstruction methods are complex and limited by inaccurate geometry from sparse inputs. No prior work can simultaneously generate novel dynamic content, follow arbitrary camera trajectories, and maintain scene consistency across large view changes without relying on explicit 3D reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes 20 perspective images (equirectangular-to-perspective projections of a 360° panorama sampled at 18° increments with 90° FoV), a text prompt, and a user-specified camera trajectory (f×3×4 extrinsic matrix per frame) as inputs, and produces a 384×672, 77-frame video with a dynamic subject that preserves the underlying static scene. Notably, the camera trajectory is provided directly by the user rather than estimated from context images, and camera intrinsics are not required, making the pipeline accessible without source-image metadata.

## Method

CINESCENE extracts implicit 3D scene representation from the input scene images using VGGT's transformer backbone, obtaining both image features (encoding depth, point-cloud, and tracking cues) and camera features (encoding pose information), which are fused via element-wise addition. These implicit 3D features, along with the scene images encoded through a causal 3D VAE, are patchified, projected to the model's hidden dimension, and concatenated with the noisy video latents along the frame dimension as context tokens—requiring no architectural modification to the pretrained T2V diffusion model. Camera parameters are injected via a learnable encoder added to the corresponding visual features, while the text prompt enters through cross-attention. A key training strategy is random shuffling of scene image order (fixing only the first image at the starting viewpoint) to prevent the model from exploiting positional priors and instead learn robust alignment between pixel-level context and the implicit 3D representation. The framework is trained on a purpose-built Scene-Decoupled Video Dataset (46K video-scene pairs across 35 Unreal Engine 5 environments) with paired videos containing and excluding dynamic subjects, enabling explicit supervision of scene-subject decoupling.

## Results

On the Scene-Decoupled Video Dataset (300 held-out samples), CINESCENE achieves the best scene consistency across all five metrics: 4617.51K matched pixels, 0.8633 CLIP-V, 14.51 PSNR, 0.4133 SSIM, and 0.4241 LPIPS. It also leads on all three camera accuracy metrics (RotErr 2.68, TransErr 5.15, CamMC 6.88) and overall video quality (VBench 0.8053). The only metric where CINESCENE trails is CLIP-T text alignment (0.3212 vs. FramePack's 0.3269), which the authors attribute to larger inter-frame camera motion reducing frame-text CLIP similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CINESCENE outperforms all five baselines on every scene consistency metric. Against the closest context-based competitor, Context-as-Memory [68], it gains +36K matched pixels, +0.009 CLIP-V, +0.70 PSNR, and −0.025 LPIPS, while also improving camera accuracy (CamMC 6.88 vs. 6.96). The explicit-3D method Gen3C [45] lags substantially on both consistency (PSNR 11.63 vs. 14.51) and camera accuracy (TransErr 10.16 vs. 5.15), and requires ~10× longer inference. Among camera-controlled methods, Traj-Attn [63] and RecamMaster [2] achieve far worse camera accuracy (CamMC 11.06 and 9.24 respectively) and do not report scene consistency metrics. The sole metric where CINESCENE does not lead is CLIP-T text alignment, where FramePack [71] edges ahead (0.3269 vs. 0.3212), which the authors explain as an artifact of larger camera motion in CINESCENE's outputs reducing per-frame text similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CINESCENE sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the context-conditioning paradigm for video diffusion (FullDiT, LCT), which concatenates reference tokens into the transformer sequence; (2) VGGT's implicit 3D scene representation, which encodes spatial layout and camera pose into transformer features without explicit reconstruction; and (3) camera-controlled video generation (RecamMaster, Gen3C). The key departure is from loss-guided 3D integration (Geometry Forcing), which uses VGGT features as a supervisory signal and thus penalizes dynamic content. CINESCENE instead injects VGGT features as additional context tokens alongside scene images, fundamentally decoupling the static background (condition) from the dynamic foreground (generation target) within a single diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that implicit 3D features from a pretrained foundation model (VGGT), injected via simple token concatenation, are sufficient to maintain scene consistency under large view changes (75° pans) without any explicit 3D reconstruction pipeline—eliminating a major source of error and computational overhead (10× faster inference than explicit 3D methods). The scene-decoupled dataset (46K pairs across 35 environments) fills a gap in training data for this task. Open questions include: generalization beyond synthetic UE5 scenes to real-world environments (only 50 OOD samples tested), scalability to longer videos or more complex multi-subject dynamics, and whether the 20-image 360° panorama requirement is practical for real production workflows.
