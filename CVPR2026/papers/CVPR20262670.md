## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks and training data rarely enforce true cross-modal multi-hop reasoning, where both visual and textual evidence are complementary and jointly necessary to reach an answer. Most datasets allow answers to be inferred from a single modality alone, and the scarcity of genuinely interleaved, complementary image-text content means VLMs produce hallucinated, poorly grounded reasoning traces on such tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262670/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each CRIT sample consists of 1–6 interleaved images (natural photos, video frames, or scientific paper figures/tables) accompanied by a generated textual context (stories, diary entries, or documentary scripts) and one or more compositional questions. The model must produce a short answer string and a step-by-step Chain-of-Thought trace that explicitly grounds each reasoning hop in a specific image or text segment. During training, multi-QA samples are concatenated into multi-turn conversations; during evaluation, samples are restricted to single-turn QA with CoT or direct-answer prompting.

## Method

The pipeline constructs a directed multimodal content graph whose nodes are visual entities (from pre-annotated scene graphs) and textual entities (generated by an LLM), with edges encoding intra- and cross-modal relations. It then samples subgraphs spanning both modalities with 1–5 edges and a terminal image node, prompting an LLM to generate a question whose answer requires traversing the full chain while hiding intermediate entities. A staged LLM-based filter removes questions answerable from a single modality (verified by three independent LLMs) and prunes verbose CoT traces. Crucially, no VLM is invoked at any generation stage—only text LLMs operating on the structured graph—thereby avoiding the cyclical bias and grounding failures that plague VLM-generated data. The pipeline is extended to video frames (via CLIP-aligned captions converted to partial scene graphs) and scientific papers (via LLM-extracted graphs from figures and tables with corresponding text sentences removed to enforce cross-modal dependency).

## Results

On the CRIT benchmark, even the strongest proprietary model (GPT-4o) achieves only 35.1 EM on natural images and 8.4 EM on scientific papers, while the largest open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) reaches 38.0 EM on NI. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on CRIT more than doubles NI performance to 58.6 EM and lifts SP to 15.9 EM, surpassing all proprietary baselines. On cross-modal reasoning benchmarks, adding CRIT to Mantis-Instruct training more than triples SPIQA METEOR (10.53 vs. 3.60) and yields 19–39% relative gains on VEGA, 10% on MMQA, and 12% on FCMR, while preserving or slightly improving standard multimodal scores (e.g., BLINK 47.6 vs. 45.7, ChartQA 64.9 vs. 63.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2.5-VL-CRIT (7B) decisively outperforms GPT-4o on all three CRIT domains (NI: 58.6 vs. 35.1 EM; VF: 38.8 vs. 32.0; SP: 15.9 vs. 8.4) and also surpasses Gemini 2.0 Flash (NI: 58.6 vs. 30.4; SP: 15.9 vs. 11.5). Idefics2-CRIT (8B) similarly beats both proprietary models on NI (54.1 vs. 35.1/30.4) and SP (12.3 vs. 8.4/11.5), though it trails GPT-4o on VF (31.2 vs. 32.0). Among open-source baselines, LLaVA-Onevision (31.0 NI EM) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (38.0 NI EM) are the strongest unfine-tuned competitors, yet both are surpassed by the 7B CRIT-finetuned model by 20+ EM points. On cross-modal reasoning benchmarks (Table 3), Mantis-Instruct+CRIT outperforms Mantis-Instruct alone on every metric: SPIQA METEOR 10.53 vs. 3.60, VEGA ROUGE-L 35.1 vs. 29.5, MMQA EM 30.0 vs. 27.3, and FCMR F1 50.5 vs. 44.9. On standard multimodal benchmarks (Table 4), Mantis-Instruct+CRIT matches or slightly exceeds Mantis-Instruct alone (e.g., BLINK 47.6 vs. 45.7, ChartQA 64.9 vs. 63.8) while MuirBench is essentially tied (32.6 vs. 33.1), confirming that CRIT training does not degrade general capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior cross-modal multi-hop reasoning benchmarks (MMQA, FCMR, WebQA) by arguing that most existing datasets are solvable from a single modality, and from interleaved image-text datasets (SPIQA, VEGA) by showing their cross-modal dependency is weak. Methodologically, it carves new ground by using structured scene graphs as an intermediate representation to programmatically sample multi-hop subgraphs, then leveraging LLMs (not VLMs) for question generation—avoiding the cyclical bias and grounding failures inherent in VLM-based data synthesis. The pipeline repurposes existing scene graph annotations (GQA) and video captioning (ActivityNet) as reliable grounding sources, with LLMs handling only the textual augmentation and question formulation steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRIT provides a much-needed benchmark where visual and textual evidence are genuinely complementary, and the finding that even GPT-4o and Gemini score below 36% EM reveals a substantial gap in current VLM capabilities for cross-modal reasoning. The error analysis (55% evidence localization failures) suggests the primary bottleneck is locating relevant information across interleaved sources rather than perceiving or reasoning over it, pointing toward retrieval and grounding as key targets for future work. The scientific paper domain remains particularly challenging (15.9 EM even after fine-tuning), and whether the graph-based pipeline scales to truly open-ended, non-structured content beyond scene-graph-annotated sources is an open question.
