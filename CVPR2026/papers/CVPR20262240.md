## Problem

Blind-spot networks for self-supervised denoising assume pixel-wise independent noise, but real sRGB images exhibit spatially correlated noise from the camera's demosaicing process, causing conventional BSNs to degenerate into near-identity mappings. Existing remedies—pixel-shuffle downsampling or rectangular blind spots—either alter noise statistics and require post-processing, or exclude useful uncorrelated pixels because they do not match the diamond-shaped correlation geometry inherent to demosaiced images.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single noisy sRGB image with no clean reference and outputs a denoised sRGB image at the original resolution, requiring no downsampling or post-processing. Training operates on 128×128 patches from the SIDD Medium set (320 images), while evaluation uses 256×256 patches on SIDD and 512×512 patches on DND. The distilled student (TM-BSN-D) is a 1.02M-parameter U-Net that produces its output in a single forward pass with 26.74 GFLOPs and 3.21 ms runtime on an RTX 3090.

## Method

TM-BSN introduces a Triangular-Masked Convolution (TMC) that zeros out the lower-triangular elements of each 3×3 kernel, so that stacked TMC layers progressively expand the receptive field along the upper-triangular diagonal. A feature-shift operation displaces the extracted features vertically and horizontally by a tunable offset s, carving out a diamond-shaped blind spot centered on the target pixel at full resolution; four rotated branches (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) are concatenated to form a rotationally symmetric blind spot aligned with the demosaicing correlation pattern. The backbone is the VDIR restorer with all convolutions replaced by TMC layers, trained self-supervised with an L1 loss at s=5. A Recharged Distillation (RD) stage then transfers complementary predictions from five teacher shift offsets (S={2,3,4,5,6}) into a lightweight U-Net student, which can directly access the target pixel and achieves higher PSNR with far fewer FLOPs than the teacher.

## Results

On the SIDD Validation set, the distilled TM-BSN (D) achieves 38.08 dB PSNR and 0.952 SSIM, outperforming the previous best self-supervised method APR(RD) (38.00 dB) by +0.08 dB. On the SIDD Benchmark, TM-BSN (D) reaches 38.31 dB PSNR and 0.900 SSIM, surpassing APR(RD) by +0.05 dB. On the DND Benchmark under the fully self-supervised setting, TM-BSN (D) achieves 39.41 dB PSNR and 0.949 SSIM, exceeding TBSN (39.08 dB) by +0.33 dB. The distilled model also runs in 3.21 ms with only 1.02M parameters, compared to 1004.60 ms and 12.97M parameters for TBSN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among self-supervised methods, TM-BSN (D) achieves the best PSNR and SSIM on all three benchmarks. On SIDD Validation it leads APR(RD) [16] by +0.08 dB (38.08 vs 38.00) and AT-BSN(D) [5] by +0.20 dB; on the SIDD Benchmark it edges out APR(RD) by +0.05 dB (38.31 vs 38.26). The largest margin appears on DND under the fully self-supervised setting, where TM-BSN (D) surpasses TBSN [23] by +0.33 dB (39.41 vs 39.08) and APR(RD) by +0.58 dB (39.41 vs 38.83). The non-distilled TM-BSN (s=4) is competitive but trails the distilled version by roughly 0.7–0.8 dB, confirming the benefit of knowledge distillation. In terms of efficiency, TM-BSN (D) uses only 1.02M parameters and 26.74 GFLOPs (3.21 ms runtime), making it ~315× faster than TBSN (1004.60 ms) and ~24× faster than AT-BSN (14.84 ms) while delivering higher accuracy. All supervised methods (Restormer, VDN, DnCNN) still outperform self-supervised approaches by 1–2 dB, as expected given access to clean ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

TM-BSN builds directly on the AT-BSN architecture [5] and its VDIR backbone [33], replacing standard convolutions with a proposed triangular-masked convolution that restricts the receptive field to an upper-triangular region. The key conceptual departure is from the rectangular blind spots used in AT-BSN and APR-RD [16]: the authors observe that demosaicing in the ISP pipeline produces a diamond-shaped spatial correlation pattern in real sRGB noise, and design the blind spot to match this geometry rather than using a generic rectangular exclusion. For knowledge distillation, the paper adopts the Recharged Distillation (RD) loss from APR-RD [16], transferring complementary predictions from multiple shift offsets into a lightweight U-Net student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometrically-aligned diamond blind spot offers a more principled alternative to ad-hoc rectangular or asymmetric exclusions, and the distilled student (1.02M params, 3.21 ms) achieves SOTA self-supervised denoising at a fraction of the cost of prior methods, making real-time deployment more feasible. An open question is whether the diamond shape generalizes across different ISP pipelines and CFA configurations, or whether the correlation geometry shifts with sensor design. The approach remains specific to sRGB demosaicing noise and does not address other sources of spatial correlation (e.g., Bayer-pattern artifacts at extreme ISO or multi-frame stacking).
