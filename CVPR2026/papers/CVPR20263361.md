## Problem

World-model VLAs predict full future visual frames, wasting capacity reconstructing redundant static backgrounds, while latent-action VLAs encode only frame-to-frame transitions and lack temporally continuous dynamic modeling and world knowledge. The paper asks whether a more compact, abstract form of world modeling is possible that preserves temporal reasoning and world knowledge without pixel-level reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263361/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a language instruction and an initial RGB frame (quantized to discrete visual tokens via VQGAN at 224×224 or 200×200 resolution) as input. During pre-training, it outputs a continuous latent motion vector (dimension 1792, derived from a 16-frame video segment) and a predicted terminal frame token. During co-fine-tuning, it additionally consumes sparse keyframes and FAST-quantized action chunks (length 5 or 10), producing autoregressive action token sequences alongside the latent motion prediction at a single learnable query position.

## Method

CoWVLA combines a pretrained VidTwin video VAE as a latent motion extractor with an 8.5B-parameter Emu3-based autoregressive VLA decoder. The VAE explicitly disentangles each 16-frame video segment into a structure latent (via Q-Former aggregation) and two directional motion latents (via spatial averaging along height and width axes), yielding a compact 1792-dim motion vector as supervision. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training aligns the instruction and initial frame with the latent motion chain and terminal frame prediction (with causal masking so the query token cannot attend to the future frame), and co-fine-tuning jointly models sparse keyframes and action chunks in a unified autoregressive sequence while maintaining latent motion supervision at the query position. This 'Chain-of-World' design preserves world-model temporal reasoning and world knowledge while retaining the compactness and interpretability of latent actions, avoiding reconstruction of redundant intermediate frames.

## Results

On LIBERO, CoWVLA achieves an average success rate of 0.956, surpassing the best latent-action method TLA (0.952) and the best world-model method UniVLA (0.950). On SimplerEnv-WidowX, CoWVLA reaches 0.760 average, outperforming FlowVLA (0.740) and UniVLA (0.687). CoWVLA is the only method to achieve top-1 performance on both benchmarks simultaneously, demonstrating superior cross-domain robustness compared to methods that excel on one benchmark but degrade on the other.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoWVLA outperforms all 14 baselines on both benchmarks. Against the strongest VLA baseline π0 (0.942 LIBERO / 0.401 SimplerEnv), CoWVLA gains +1.4 points on LIBERO and +35.9 points on SimplerEnv. Among latent-action methods, CoWVLA beats TLA (0.952/0.480) by +0.4 on LIBERO and +28.0 on SimplerEnv, and villa-X (0.901/0.625) by +5.5 and +13.5 respectively. Among world-model methods, CoWVLA surpasses FlowVLA (0.881/0.740) by +7.5 on LIBERO and +2.0 on SimplerEnv, and UniVLA (0.950/0.687) by +0.6 and +7.3. Notably, TLA achieves near-parity on LIBERO (0.952 vs. 0.956) but collapses to 0.480 on SimplerEnv, while FlowVLA leads SimplerEnv among world models (0.740) yet trails CoWVLA by 7.5 points on LIBERO—highlighting that CoWVLA's structure-motion disentanglement provides the most balanced cross-domain performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoWVLA positions itself as a unification of two competing VLA pretraining paradigms: world-model approaches (UniVLA, WorldVLA, FlowVLA) that predict full future frames, and latent-action approaches (LAPA, villa-X, TLA) that encode compact frame-to-frame transitions. It builds directly on VidTwin's structure-motion disentangled video VAE as a frozen latent motion extractor and on UniVLA's autoregressive VLA decoder architecture, but departs from both by replacing pixel-level frame prediction with a continuous latent motion chain plus a single terminal keyframe. The 'Chain of World' framing explicitly addresses the gap the authors identify: latent actions lack temporal continuity and world knowledge, while world models waste capacity on redundant background reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled middle ground for VLA pretraining that preserves temporal reasoning without the computational cost of multi-frame pixel prediction, and the structure-motion disentanglement provides interpretable supervision that neither pure world models nor pure latent actions offer. However, the approach depends on the quality and domain coverage of the pretrained VidTwin VAE, requires an 8.5B-parameter Emu3 backbone, and is validated only in simulation (LIBERO, SimplerEnv), leaving open whether the latent motion prior transfers to real-robot deployment. Broader questions remain about whether the disentanglement generalizes to more complex multi-object or deformable-object scenes, and whether lighter-weight VAEs or learned extractors could replace the frozen VidTwin component.
