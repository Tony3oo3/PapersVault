## Problem

Existing motion capture pipelines are tightly coupled to a single species or parametric template (e.g., SMPL for humans, SMAL for quadrupeds), making them unable to produce animation-ready rotations for arbitrary rigged assets such as robots, mechs, toys, or IP-specific creatures. The paper formalizes this gap as Category-Agnostic Motion Capture (CAMoCap): given a monocular video and an arbitrary rigged 3D asset as a prompt, reconstruct a rotation-based animation that directly drives that specific character's rig.

## Input / Output

The system takes a monocular RGB video and a reference rigged 3D asset comprising a mesh, a skeleton (joint set, parent-child edges, bone offsets, and canonical joint names), and an optional set of appearance renders. It outputs a per-frame sequence of SO(3) joint rotations in the asset's own local frames, exportable as BVH, with variable joint counts handled via binary validity masks. The asset acts as a structural prompt: the same video can be retargeted to entirely different skeletons by swapping the reference, enabling both same-species mocap and cross-species retargeting without retraining.

## Method

MoCapAnything factorizes motion recovery into 3D joint trajectory prediction followed by constraint-aware IK fitting, avoiding brittle direct rotation regression. A Reference Prompt Encoder distills the asset's skeleton topology (via graph multi-head attention with kinematic-tree bias, following AnyTop), mesh surface geometry, and appearance images into per-joint query tokens. A Video Feature Extractor produces two complementary streams per frame: dense DINOv2 visual tokens and a coarse 4D deforming mesh (1024 sampled points with normals) that bridges the modality gap between RGB features and the point-cloud-like joint space. A Unified Motion Decoder fuses these via four attention stages per layer (graph self-attention, temporal video cross-attention, temporal point-cloud cross-attention, and temporal self-attention) to predict temporally coherent 3D joint positions, supervised only by a masked L1 position loss. A lightweight two-stage IK (closed-form geometric initialization followed by differentiable optimization warm-started from the previous frame) then converts trajectories into asset-specific rotations respecting hierarchy, bone lengths, and joint limits.

## Results

On the Truebones Zoo test set, MoCapAnything achieves MPJPE of 1.06 cm (Seen), 1.28 cm (Rare), and 1.76 cm (Unseen), with corresponding MPJVE of 0.44, 0.37, and 0.36 cm. These results represent a 3.8× improvement over the strongest baseline (GLoT: 3.98/3.58/7.42 cm MPJPE) and a >10× improvement over 2D pose estimators (HRNet, ViTPose) on the Unseen split. The method also maintains the lowest velocity error across all splits, indicating superior temporal coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoCapAnything outperforms all four baselines by large margins on every split and metric. The two 2D pose estimators (HRNet and ViTPose) are the weakest, with Unseen MPJPE exceeding 23 cm, reflecting their inability to produce 3D trajectories without a 3D prior. VIBE (a video-based SMPL fitter) and GLoT (a global-to-local 3D pose estimator) are substantially stronger, achieving 8.72 and 7.42 cm MPJPE on Unseen species respectively, but still trail MoCapAnything by 5× and 4.2×. The gap widens most dramatically on the Unseen split, where MoCapAnything's category-agnostic design (reference prompt encoder + 4D mesh geometry) provides a decisive advantage over methods tied to a fixed human template. On the Seen split, MoCapAnything's 1.06 cm MPJPE is 3.8× better than GLoT (3.98 cm), confirming that the gains are not solely due to generalization but also reflect superior in-domain accuracy from the factorized trajectory-then-IK pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines: (1) species-specific motion capture (SMPL/SMPL-X for humans, SMAL for quadrupeds) that is locked to a fixed parametric template, and (2) category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE, CapeFormer, Pose Anything, CapeX) that generalizes across categories but remains 2D and static. MoCapAnything bridges these by treating the rigged 3D asset itself as a multi-modal prompt (skeleton + mesh + appearance) and factorizing the problem into 3D trajectory prediction followed by constraint-aware IK, borrowing the topology-biased graph attention design from AnyTop [8] for structure-aware message passing. The use of a coarse 4D deforming mesh as an intermediate geometric representation to bridge dense RGB tokens and point-cloud-like joint space is a novel architectural choice not present in prior mocap or CAPE work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoCapAnything addresses a practical gap in content creation pipelines where animators must drive heterogeneous asset libraries (robots, mechs, mascots, VTuber avatars) from video without building a parametric model per species. The Truebones Zoo benchmark (1,038 clips with standardized skeleton-mesh-video triads) provides a reproducible evaluation protocol that the field lacked. Key limitations remain: the pipeline depends on the quality of a pretrained 4D reconstructor, operates in camera space without physics or contact reasoning, and requires access to a rigged asset with known joint structure. Open questions include end-to-end physics-aware IK, world-grounded trajectory recovery, reducing reliance on explicit 4D reconstruction, and extending to multi-character interaction.
