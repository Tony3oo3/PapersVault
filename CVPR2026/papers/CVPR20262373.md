## Problem

Existing membership inference and model inversion research for Latent Diffusion Models treats the VAE latent space as a fixed, geometry-free substrate, overlooking how the encoder/decoder pair shapes memorization. While LDMs are known to be more robust to inversion than data-domain diffusion models, the paper identifies that this robustness is spatially non-uniform and tightly correlated with the local distortion induced by the VAE decoder's pullback metric—a connection that prior work has not exploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262373/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a candidate latent code z (produced by the VAE encoder from an RGB image at 32×32 to 512×512 resolution), the frozen VAE decoder (to compute the pullback metric and per-dimension influence via Jacobian-vector products), and a trained latent diffusion model (to evaluate attack statistics at various timesteps). It outputs a binary membership decision and a per-dimension influence vector of length d (the latent dimensionality), which is used to mask the attack vector by zeroing out the top-40% least-influential coordinates before computing the norm. The entire filtering procedure depends only on the VAE, not on the diffusion model weights.

## Method

The paper defines a per-dimension influence measure Infl_i(z) = G_ii(z) = ||∂D(z)/∂z_i||², where G(z) = J_D(z)ᵀJ_D(z) is the decoder pullback metric, capturing how much each latent coordinate stretches the data-space neighborhood. Rather than constructing the full Jacobian, the diagonal entries are estimated efficiently via the Hutchinson stochastic trace estimator with n_mc=8 random probe vectors and reverse-mode autodifferentiation, scaling as O(n_mc·T_D). The key operational step is a dimension-masking scheme: given any scalar attack statistic ||S(z)|| (from SimA, Loss, SecMI, or PIA), the method retains only the top-k% (k=40) highest-influence coordinates and zeros the rest before computing the norm, effectively removing directions that contribute little to decoder distortion and thus add noise to the attack signal. This geometry-driven filter, computed entirely from the VAE decoder, uniformly improves AUC by 1–4% and TPR@1%FPR by 1–32% across all four attack methods and six datasets, outperforming random dimension removal and confirming that latent-space memorization is anisotropic and governed by decoder geometry.

## Results

Removing the top 40% least-memorizing latent dimensions (ranked by per-dimension decoder influence) before computing attack norms consistently improves all four MIA methods across six datasets. Mean AUROC gains range from +1.13% (Pokémon) to +3.22% (ImageNet), while TPR@1%FPR gains are substantially larger, reaching +14.81% on Pokémon and +13.64% on MS-COCO. For example, SimA on CIFAR-10 improves from 89.10% to 91.26% AUROC and from 19.88% to 24.56% TPR@1%FPR, while on ImageNet it jumps from 69.62% to 72.55% AUROC. A random-drop ablation (removing 40% dimensions without the influence ranking) typically degrades performance, confirming the geometry-driven selection is essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new attack but a geometry-driven dimension-filtering procedure that enhances four existing MIA baselines. Against SimA [27], the filtered variant improves AUROC by 1.3–3.5% and TPR@1%FPR by 4.7–31.7% across all six datasets (e.g., 89.10→91.26% AUC on CIFAR-10; 93.71→96.86% AUC on MS-COCO). Against SecMI [9], gains are comparable: +1.2–2.8% AUROC and +5.9–11.2% TPR@1%FPR. Against PIA [17], the filtered version yields +1.3–3.7% AUROC and +4.2–20.2% TPR@1%FPR. Against the Loss/Naive baseline [23], improvements are the smallest (+1.5–2.5% AUROC, +1.8–5.2% TPR@1%FPR), suggesting that methods already less sensitive to low-influence dimensions benefit least. A random-drop control (removing 40% dimensions without the influence ranking) consistently underperforms the unfiltered baseline, confirming that the decoder-geometry signal—not mere dimensionality reduction—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the decoder pullback metric from Riemannian geometry of VAEs (Arvanitidis et al., Shao et al., Chadebec & Allassonnière) — previously used for interpolation quality and manifold structure — as a lens for privacy analysis of latent diffusion models. It departs from prior LDM inversion work (Carlini et al., Somepalli et al.) that treats the VAE as a fixed pre-processor and focuses exclusively on the diffusion process, and from frequency-domain memorization analyses (Lian et al., Kadkhodaie et al.) that operate in pixel space and do not transfer to nonlinear latent representations. The per-dimension influence measure and masking scheme are new, but the underlying attack methods (SimA, SecMI, PIA, Loss) are all established baselines that the paper augments rather than replaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a frozen VAE decoder's local geometry — not the diffusion model's training dynamics — governs where memorization concentrates reframes how practitioners should think about LDM privacy: the autoencoder architecture choice becomes a first-order privacy decision, not merely a compression step. The practical technique (Hutchinson-estimated per-dimension masking of the attack vector) is cheap to compute and uniformly improves all tested MIA methods, making it immediately applicable. Open questions include whether the optimal masking ratio k is dataset- and sample-dependent, how noise-schedule interactions (the unexplained ImageNet crossover) affect attack tuning, and whether the findings generalize beyond image data to text or tabular diffusion.
