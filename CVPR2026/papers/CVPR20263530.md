## Problem

Existing multimodal summarization methods suffer from three interrelated limitations: heavy reliance on domain-specific paired supervision that hinders cross-domain generalization, implicit latent-space fusion that produces weak cross-modal grounding, and flat temporal modeling that captures only local frame-level patterns without hierarchical event structure or causal transitions. This paper asks whether explicit, structured event-centric reasoning can replace both supervised fusion and flat sequence modeling to achieve robust, training-free summarization across diverse domains.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (up to 72 uniformly sampled frames, segmented into 12 clips of up to 30 frames each) paired with its accompanying article or transcript as input. For datasets lacking textual annotations (VIEWS, VISTA), video descriptions from YouTube or Whisper-generated transcripts are used as the text channel. The output is a single coherent textual summary, refined through a lightweight style-adaptation step that uses five reference summaries sampled from the target domain's training set to align tone and discourse conventions without altering factual content.

## Method

CoE is a fully training-free, four-module reasoning pipeline built on top of a frozen MLLM (default: Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct). Module 1 constructs a Hierarchical Event Graph (HEG) from the text input, decomposing it into a three-level structure of global events, sub-events, and entity-relation triples that serves as a semantic scaffold. Module 2 (Cross-modal Spatial Grounding) uses HEG sub-event nodes as anchors to align video clips to sub-events and extract visually supported entity-relation subgraphs. Module 3 (Event Evolution Reasoning) merges adjacent clips with identical subgraphs into longer temporal segments, then compares successive segment subgraphs to detect emerging, persisting, and vanishing entities/relations, producing event-trajectory descriptions. Module 4 synthesizes these trajectories into an initial summary and refines it via prompt-based style adaptation against domain reference summaries. The key architectural insight is replacing implicit cross-modal attention fusion with explicit graph-structured reasoning over hierarchical events, enabling interpretable, domain-agnostic summarization without any parameter updates.

## Results

CoE achieves the best CIDEr score on all eight datasets, with an average gain of +9.51 over the strongest baseline (e.g., +31.73 on XMSMO-News over TCoT). On ROUGE, CoE leads on seven of eight datasets (e.g., 23.50 on SoccerNet vs. 13.94 for ViTCoT), and on BERTScore it attains the highest score on seven datasets (e.g., 88.65 on XMSMO-News). Entity-level F1-score shows the largest relative gains: CoE reaches 63.37 on SoccerNet and 69.50 on SummScreen³D, compared to 31.45 and 31.06 for the best baseline CoS, respectively. Overall average improvements across all baselines are +3.04 ROUGE, +9.51 CIDEr, and +1.88 BERTScore.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoE outperforms all four video CoT baselines (TCoT, CoF, ViTCoT, CoS) on CIDEr across all eight datasets, with the largest margin being +31.73 over TCoT on XMSMO-News (56.55 vs. 24.82). On ROUGE, CoE leads on seven of eight datasets; the sole exception is BLiSS where CoF scores 12.38 versus CoE's 10.83. On BERTScore, CoE is best on seven datasets, trailing CoF by 1.13 points on BLiSS (83.80 vs. 84.93). Entity F1-score shows the most dramatic separation: CoE reaches 63.37 on SoccerNet and 69.50 on SummScreen³D, nearly doubling the best baseline CoS (31.45 and 31.06 respectively), while on BLiSS ViTCoT edges out CoE (4.44 vs. 3.63). On G-Eval (LLM-as-a-judge), CoE ranks first on six datasets but is slightly behind ViTCoT on XMSMO-News (3.65 vs. 3.81) and SoccerNet (2.70 vs. 2.75). Overall, CoE's advantage is most pronounced on CIDEr and entity F1, reflecting the benefit of explicit hierarchical event-graph grounding over the flat, frame-level reasoning used by all four baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of work: supervised encoder-fusion MMS architectures (MLASK, MMSum) that require domain-specific fine-tuning, and flat frame/clip-level video CoT methods (TCoT, CoF, ViTCoT, CoS) designed primarily for VideoQA. Rather than building directly on any single prior method, CoE repurposes the chain-of-thought paradigm for summarization and introduces a Hierarchical Event Graph (HEG) as an explicit semantic scaffold, drawing conceptually from event-centric multimodal understanding (e.g., Multimodal Event Graphs) but applying it to a training-free, graph-guided reasoning pipeline. The key conceptual departure is replacing implicit latent-space fusion with structured, interpretable event-entity reasoning that operates entirely at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, domain-agnostic design addresses a practical bottleneck in MMS: the need for large paired video-text datasets and per-domain fine-tuning, which limits deployment to low-resource or emerging domains. The large entity F1 gains (+31 to +38 points on SoccerNet and Summ) suggest that explicit entity-relation tracking via HEG is a meaningful lever for factual grounding that flat CoT methods miss. Open questions include how the framework scales to very long videos (beyond 72 frames), whether the HEG can be constructed without a clean transcript, and whether the lightweight style adaptation (5 reference summaries) generalizes to domains with highly divergent rhetorical conventions. The authors also note potential extensions to multimodal output (keyframe selection, highlights) and interactive summarization.
