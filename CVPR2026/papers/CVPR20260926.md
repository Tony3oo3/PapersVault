## Problem

Existing video omnimatte methods rely on slow, multi-stage, or inference-time optimization pipelines that fail to fully exploit powerful generative priors, producing suboptimal decompositions. A critical and unexpected challenge arises when naively fine-tuning a pretrained inpainting model with LoRA on all DiT blocks: while the model mattes foreground objects well, it consistently fails to capture associated effects such as shadows and reflections, even when trained with ground-truth targets that include them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260926/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip, per-frame coarse foreground masks (from a modern segmentor like SAM 2, excluding effects), and a brief environmental text description as input. It produces an alpha matte, a restored background layer (predicted directly by the frozen inpainting backbone), and a foreground layer recovered analytically via the compositing equation rather than predicted directly. The input visual tokens are duplicated into two parallel sets — original tokens for background inpainting and copied tokens for alpha matte prediction — with LoRA applied exclusively to the copied set, keeping the frozen backbone's inpainting behavior intact.

## Method

EasyOmnimatte repurposes a pretrained video inpainting diffusion model (built on Wan) by introducing Branch DiT Blocks that duplicate input tokens and apply LoRA only to the copied set, enabling simultaneous background prediction and alpha matte estimation in a single end-to-end pass. A block-wise attention analysis reveals that effect-related cues are concentrated in middle DiT blocks but actively suppressed in final blocks, explaining why full-LoRA fine-tuning loses effects. To resolve this, the method trains two complementary experts: an Effect Expert with LoRA applied only to the last K DiT blocks (preserving the model's inherent effect perception) and a Quality Expert with LoRA on all blocks (refining matte precision). During sampling, the Effect Expert handles early high-noise timesteps (t > τ) to establish coarse effect-aware structure, while the Quality Expert takes over at later low-noise timesteps (t ≤ τ) to refine details, achieving high-quality results with roughly half the computational cost of running two full diffusion passes.

## Results

On the DAVIS benchmark, EasyOmnimatte achieves the best FVD (105.48) and SSIM (78.83) among all compared methods, with a competitive PSNR of 26.23. In the user study (28 raters, 20 videos), EasyOmnimatte scores 4.08/5 overall, substantially outperforming Gen-Omnimatte (2.85), MatAnyone† (2.82), and BGM-V2 (2.26) across all criteria including foreground integrity (4.07), effect harmony (3.97), and temporal consistency (4.21). Inference completes in under 10 seconds per video, an order of magnitude faster than Gen-Omnimatte's test-time optimization pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EasyOmnimatte decisively outperforms Gen-Omnimatte [15] on all perceptual metrics: PSNR improves from 24.35 to 26.23 (+1.88 dB), SSIM from 69.36 to 78.83 (+9.47), and FVD from 116.32 to 105.48, while reducing inference time from several minutes to under 10 seconds. Against BGM V2 [20], EasyOmnimatte trades a marginal PSNR drop (26.23 vs. 26.61) for a large FVD improvement (105.48 vs. 168.31), reflecting better temporal coherence and effect preservation that BGM V2 cannot capture. Compared to MatAnyone† [47], EasyOmnimatte achieves slightly higher PSNR (26.23 vs. 26.12) and a substantially lower FVD (105.48 vs. 146.44), indicating superior compositional quality. In the human evaluation, EasyOmnimatte leads every criterion by a wide margin (e.g., 4.07 vs. 2.45 for foreground integrity over Gen-Omnimatte; 3.97 vs. 2.96 for effect harmony over BGM-V2), confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptually meaningful improvements in effect capture and layer separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the video inpainting model from Gen-Omnimatte [15] (built on the Wan DiT framework [38]) but inverts its objective: rather than using the model to remove foreground effects, it fine-tunes the same model to extract them as alpha mattes. It departs from the multi-stage, test-time-optimization paradigm of prior omnimatte methods (Omnimatte [27], Gen-Omnimatte [15]) by formulating the task as a single end-to-end feed-forward prediction. The block-wise analysis draws on the effect-perception insights from ObjectDrop [45] and Gen-Omnimatte [15], but extends them into a structural diagnosis of *where* in the DiT hierarchy effects are encoded and suppressed, motivating the Dual Expert Sampling strategy that has no direct precedent in the matting or inpainting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing video omnimatte inference from several minutes of test-time optimization to under 10 seconds, this work makes effect-aware video decomposition practical for interactive editing workflows. The finding that effect-related signals are concentrated in intermediate DiT blocks and actively suppressed in later blocks provides a reusable diagnostic for anyone adapting generative models to tasks requiring preservation of subtle visual cues. The main limitation is that the method is intrinsically tied to the base inpainting model's quality and the authors acknowledge the framework has not yet been validated across diverse generative architectures, leaving open whether the block-level suppression pattern is universal or specific to the Wan DiT design.
