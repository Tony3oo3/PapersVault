## Problem

Adapting generalist video diffusion models into driving world models is prohibitively expensive, requiring 25,000–50,000 GPU-hours of domain-specific fine-tuning because the model must simultaneously learn photorealistic appearance and physically grounded multi-agent dynamics. This high adaptation cost prevents the autonomous driving community from leveraging rapid advances in general video generation. The paper asks whether these two coupled responsibilities can be decoupled to enable orders-of-magnitude more efficient adaptation.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a first RGB frame (1056×704), a text prompt, and optionally a rendered ego-motion video (a synthetic checkerboard sphere with dust particles encoding 3D camera pose) and a sparse object-trajectory video (up to 5 tracked bounding boxes on black background). All visual conditioning signals are encoded through the base model's pre-trained VAE and concatenated to the DiT input sequence, avoiding any new adapter networks. The two-stage output is first a skeletonized pose video (colored joints and edges for cars, pedestrians, and lane lines on black background) at the model's native frame rate, then a photorealistic RGB driving video rendered from that pose sequence.

## Method

MAD decouples the joint motion-and-appearance problem into two sequential stages sharing a single VGM backbone (SVD or LTX) via lightweight LoRA adapters. The Motion Forecaster is fine-tuned to generate skeletonized pose videos using pseudo-labels from off-the-shelf extractors (OpenPifPaf for cars/lanes, DWPose for humans), learning multi-agent dynamics in an abstract, 3D-aware representation that aligns with the VGM's visual priors. The Appearance Synthesizer is then fine-tuned to render photorealistic video conditioned on the pose video, with a targeted latent-space noise injection strategy applied only to skeleton-region latents to bridge the gap between clean ground-truth poses at training and imperfect predicted poses at inference. A key design principle is maximal reuse of the base model: all control signals (poses, ego-motion, object trajectories, first frame) are projected into the VAE's native latent space, and training operates at the model's native resolution and frame rate to stay within its learned distribution, reducing adaptation cost to under 6% of prior SOTA compute.

## Results

On the open-loop motion planning evaluation over a 5-second horizon on OpenDV, MAD-LTX achieves the best minADE₆ of 4.88 m (2B) and 3.64 m (13B), outperforming Base LTX (5.42 m / 4.14 m) and Fine-tuned LTX (5.28 m / 5.83 m). In the human preference study, MAD-LTX-2B is preferred over the Fine-tuned LTX baseline 29% vs. 16%, and MAD-LTX-13B is preferred 33% vs. 15%, confirming the decoupled methodology's advantage. MAD-SVD matches VISTA's generation quality while using only 1,500 GPU-hours (vs. 25,000 for VISTA and 50,000 for GEM) and 139 hours of data (vs. 1,700 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VISTA [11] and GEM [17] (both SVD-based), MAD-SVD achieves competitive human-preference scores while using 16× less compute than VISTA (1,500 vs. 25,000 GPU-hours) and 12× less data (139 vs. 1,700 hours), and only 3% of GEM's compute budget. Against the proprietary Cosmos Predict 1 and 2 [29], MAD-LTX-13B is significantly preferred over Cosmos Predict 1 in human evaluation and reaches near-parity with Cosmos Predict 2 (14B) while being substantially faster at inference. Against Base LTX [15], MAD-LTX improves minADE₆ by 10% (2B) and 12% (13B) while preserving trajectory diversity (APD₆ ≈ 101 cm vs. 101–103 cm for the base model). Critically, against the Fine-tuned LTX baseline (same backbone, same data, same compute, but end-to-end without decoupling), MAD-LTX is strongly preferred in human evaluation (29% vs. 16% for 2B; 33% vs. 15% for 13B) and avoids the severe mode collapse seen in direct fine-tuning, where APD₆ drops by 37.8% (13B) and minADE₆ degrades by 40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage motion-then-appearance video generation paradigm (previously explored with masks, flow fields, or depth cues) to the driving domain, but departs from prior work in two key ways: it uses a single VGM backbone with two lightweight LoRA adapters rather than separate diffusion models, and it selects skeleton/pose videos as the intermediate motion representation rather than geometric or segmentation-based signals. It builds directly on SVD and LTX as base architectures, positioning itself against large-scale fine-tuning approaches like VISTA and GEM (25k–50k GPU-hours on SVD) and from-scratch training like Cosmos, arguing that the base model's pre-trained visual priors can be leveraged far more efficiently through targeted LoRA adaptation in the model's native latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6% compute result (1,500 GPU-hours vs. 25,000 for VISTA) is a substantial practical win that could democratize driving world model research beyond well-funded labs, and the open-source MAD-LTX model provides a strong, controllable baseline for the community. The finding that direct video fine-tuning causes mode collapse (37–40% APD degradation) while pose-space prediction avoids it offers a useful conceptual insight for anyone adapting generative models to structured domains. Open questions include whether the skeleton representation generalizes to multi-view or 3D-consistent settings, how sensitive the pipeline is to the quality of off-the-shelf pose extractors, and whether the approach transfers to other structured-motion domains beyond driving.
