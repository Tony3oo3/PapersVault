## Problem

Existing Open World Object Detection methods identify unknown objects solely through the detector's known-class predictions, causing unknown representations to entangle with background and known object parts in feature space. Additionally, memory replay for continual learning introduces cross-influence between old and new class classifiers, creating a trade-off where preserving previously learned classes degrades the learning of newly introduced ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260888/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces bounding boxes with class labels (one of C known classes or a single 'unknown' label) and objectness scores, operating in an incremental multi-task setting where each task introduces new known classes. At inference, the unknown logit is not simply the classification head's unknown node but is calibrated by a per-image standardized offset derived from the ETF subspace energy scores, effectively rescaling the unknown decision boundary relative to the known-class logits.

## Method

DEUS introduces two complementary modules on top of the OrthogonalDet backbone. First, ETF-Subspace Unknown Separation (EUS) constructs two fixed, non-overlapping Simplex ETF subspaces (64 basis vectors each for known and unknown) and computes Helmholtz free energy scores by projecting each proposal feature onto both subspaces; a margin loss on the unknown-minus-known score offset plus a focal loss on the two subspace scores drive known proposals into the known subspace, pseudo-unknown proposals into the unknown subspace, and background proposals toward the boundary. Second, Energy-based Known Distinction (EKD) splits the classification head into a previous-task and a current-task sub-classifier and applies a pairwise log-sum-exp contrastive loss that penalizes the wrong sub-classifier from scoring higher than the correct one, explicitly reducing cross-influence during memory replay. The total training objective combines standard focal classification loss, L1+gIoU box regression loss, the EUS objective, and (during replay tasks) the EKD loss.

## Results

On M-OWODB, DEUS achieves the best H-Score across all four tasks (65.6, 59.0, 58.0, 46.0), with particularly strong unknown recall (U-Rec) of 65.1, 66.2, and 69.0 for Tasks 1–3, substantially outperforming the next-best method O1O (49.3, 50.3, 49.5). On S-OWODB, DEUS similarly leads in H-Score (70.1, 57.4, 55.4) and U-Rec (68.7, 62.9, 60.7). On the remote-sensing RS-OWODB benchmark, DEUS nearly doubles the H-Score of OrthogonalDet in Task 1 (62.5 vs. 34.8) while maintaining higher known mAP across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEUS outperforms all seven baselines on H-Score across every task on both M-OWODB and S-OWODB. The largest gains are in unknown recall: on M-OWODB Task 1, DEUS achieves 65.1 U-Rec versus 49.3 for O1O (the previous best) and 36.3 for OrthogonalDet, a 15.8-point improvement over O1O. On S-OWODB Task 1, DEUS reaches 68.7 U-Rec vs. 49.8 for O1O and 24.6 for OrthogonalDet. In known mAP, DEUS is competitive but not always the best: on S-OWODB Task 1, OWOBJ achieves 76.2 Current mAP vs. DEUS's 71.6, and CAT reaches 74.2; however, DEUS compensates with far superior U-Rec, yielding the highest H-Score (70.1 vs. 59.1 for O1O). On M-OWODB, DEUS matches or exceeds the best known mAP from Task 3 onward (50.1 vs. 47.3 for OrthogonalDet), demonstrating that EKD effectively mitigates the old-vs-new class trade-off. On the remote-sensing RS-OWODB benchmark, DEUS nearly doubles OrthogonalDet's H-Score in Task 1 (62.5 vs. 34.8) and maintains a 2–4 point known mAP advantage across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEUS builds directly on OrthogonalDet (Sun et al., CVPR 2024) as its detection backbone and on the Helmholtz free energy formulation from Liang et al. (NeurIPS 2020) for scoring. Its key departure from prior OWOD methods (ORE, OW-DETR, CAT, PROB, O1O, OWOBJ) is the introduction of a geometrically separated dual-subspace structure via Simplex ETF: whereas existing energy-based approaches model only the known classifier space, DEUS constructs two fixed, non-overlapping ETF subspaces (known and unknown) and uses energy scores from both to guide proposals. The EKD loss further extends standard memory replay by splitting the classifier into old/new sub-classifiers and applying a pairwise energy contrast, addressing a cross-influence problem that prior replay-based methods leave unregularized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles two persistent pain points in OWOD—weak unknown representation learning and the old/new class trade-off during replay—with a lightweight, principled solution that adds minimal overhead (+1.9% inference time, +0.5% FLOPs). The ETF-based subspace separation offers a reusable geometric tool for any setting requiring a clean known/unknown boundary, and the EKD contrastive loss is a general recipe for reducing interference in multi-task continual classifiers. The authors acknowledge that semantic overlap between known and unknown objects remains unresolved, and the fixed (non-learnable) ETF bases raise the open question of whether adaptive or task-specific subspace geometry could further improve separation. Generalization beyond natural images is demonstrated on a remote-sensing benchmark, but the approach has not been tested on video or 3D detection settings.
