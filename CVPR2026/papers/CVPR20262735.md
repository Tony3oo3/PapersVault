## Problem

Omnimodal large language models (OmniLLMs) process 10–20k joint audio-video tokens, creating severe computational and memory bottlenecks for inference. Existing token compression methods target single modalities (video or audio) in isolation and have not addressed the coupled, cross-modal redundancy structure that arises when both streams are stitched into aligned time windows. This paper presents the first systematic study of joint audio-video token compression for OmniLLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip with synchronized audio (decomposed into T frames and fixed-rate audio segments) plus a text prompt. After encoding, tokens are organized into fixed-length time windows containing 50 audio and 288 video tokens each; the total sequence can reach 10–20k tokens. OmniZip operates as a training-free, inference-time compressor that selects and restructures tokens before the LLM prefill stage, outputting a reduced token sequence (35–55% retained) that the LLM processes to generate a text response. The pruning step itself adds less than 40 ms of overhead.

## Method

OmniZip follows a 'listen-to-prune' paradigm with three stages operating per time window. First, it identifies salient audio tokens via mean-attention scores from the audio encoder's last layer, then consolidates non-salient tokens by uniformly sampling anchors and merging the top-𝒢 most cross-modally similar tokens into each anchor. Second, it computes a per-window audio retention score S_a(i) as a proxy for information density and event-boundary likelihood, then dynamically allocates video pruning rates via ρ_v'(i) = ρ_max − (ρ_max − ρ_min)·S_a(i), so information-dense windows are pruned conservatively while sparse windows are pruned aggressively. Third, the ISTC module compresses video tokens by interleaving temporal redundancy removal (cosine-similarity-based merging of same-position tokens in adjacent frames 2 and 4) with spatial redundancy removal (DPC-KNN density-peak clustering on frames 1 and 3). The entire pipeline avoids accessing LLM attention matrices, maintaining compatibility with FlashAttention and multi-round dialogue.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, OmniZip at 45% token retention (39% FLOPs) achieves 99.1% of full-token accuracy across AVUTBench, VideoMME, and ShortVid-Bench, while at 35% retention (29% FLOPs) it retains 97.6%. On WorldSense, OmniZip (45%) scores 45.9 average vs. 46.8 for full tokens. Inference efficiency: OmniZip achieves a 3.42× prefill speedup and 1.42× end-to-end speedup on the 7B model, reducing GPU memory from 35 GB to 25 GB (10 GB reduction), all without any training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniZip consistently outperforms all three baselines at comparable or lower token budgets. Against FastV [4], OmniZip wins decisively: at 45% retention OmniZip achieves 99.1% overall accuracy (7B) versus FastV's 94.3% at 50% retention, and FastV suffers OOM on the 7B model entirely due to its reliance on explicit attention matrices incompatible with FlashAttention. Against DyCoke (V&A) [48], OmniZip at 35% retention (29% FLOPs, 97.6%) surpasses DyCoke at 50% retention (44% FLOPs, 97.5%), demonstrating that audio-guided dynamic pruning is more effective than uniform temporal compression. Against VisionZip [65] (global selection strategy, Table 4), OmniZip scores 63.0/45.9/69.9 on AVUT/WorldSense/ShortVid versus VisionZip's 61.4/44.2/68.0, confirming that window-level, cross-modal-aware pruning preserves temporal alignment better than independent global token selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied line of single-modal token compression (FastV for images, DyCoke for video, audio-specific pruning) to the joint audio-video setting of OmniLLMs, which the authors identify as previously unexplored. Conceptually, it departs from prior methods in two key ways: (1) it uses audio-encoder attention to dynamically allocate video pruning rates per time window rather than applying a uniform or purely visual criterion, and (2) it introduces an interleaved spatio-temporal compression (ISTC) module that alternates cross-frame merging with DPC-KNN-based intra-frame pruning. Unlike FastV and VisionZip, which require materializing full LLM attention matrices and are incompatible with FlashAttention, OmniZip operates solely on the lightweight audio encoder's attention, sidestepping the OOM bottleneck that limits prior methods on larger models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As OmniLLMs grow in scale and are deployed for real-time audio-video interaction, the quadratic cost of processing 10–20k joint tokens becomes a hard deployment barrier; a training-free method that cuts prefill time by 3.4× and GPU memory by 10 GB while preserving ~99% accuracy is practically significant. The 'listen-to-prune' paradigm—using audio information density as a proxy for event boundaries to guide video compression—opens a direction for cross-modal-aware compression that could generalize beyond Qwen2.5-Omni. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other OmniLLM architectures with different tokenization schemes, how it behaves on streaming or very long (>10 min) inputs, and whether the fixed hyperparameters (ρ_max, ρ_min, G) can be made fully adaptive without task-specific tuning.
