## Problem

Four-class lymph node metastasis classification (negative, ITC, micrometastasis, macrometastasis) suffers from a dual scarcity: inter-class sample imbalance for minority classes and intra-slide sparsity of positive tumor nodes. Existing CNN- and GNN-based approaches rely on pairwise or node-level feature learning, overlooking higher-order topological dependencies among cells and the global distribution insufficiency of minority classes across the training set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263467/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each whole-slide histopathology image is background-removed via Otsu thresholding and tiled into non-overlapping 256×256 patches at 20× magnification, with CONCH providing pretrained image and text encoders. The WSI is encoded as a multi-hypergraph whose nodes carry both a visual feature vector and a positional embedding, and whose hyperedges are formed by k-nearest-neighbor connectivity in feature and topology spaces separately. The model outputs a four-class metastasis label per slide, with the CONCH text encoder consuming learnable class prompt tokens to inject semantic priors during generation.

## Method

Dual-HGNet operates at two levels. At the hypergraph level, a prompt-guided hierarchical VAE (HGVAE) encodes real minority-class hypergraphs into a graph-level latent z_g, which is hierarchically decomposed into node-level z_v and hyperedge-level z_e latents; a frozen CONCH text encoder conditioned on learnable class prompts decodes these latents into synthetic node features, positional embeddings, and hyperedge representations, with k-NN re-linking in both feature and topology spaces to preserve topological consistency. At the node level, an anchor-diffusion mixup selects the highest-attention node in each slide as an anchor and adds features from a same-class auxiliary node sampled across slides, then reassigns the synthetic node to its nearest hyperedge in joint feature-position space. The classification network (a single-layer hypergraph convolution) is trained jointly on real and synthetic hypergraphs with a cross-entropy loss, while the HGVAE is regularized by reconstruction MSE and hierarchical KL divergence terms.

## Results

On the four-class NIMM lymph node metastasis dataset, Dual-HGNet achieves an overall F1-score of 77.19% (AUC 96.95), outperforming the second-best method by 3.10%. For the minority classes, it reaches 57.13% F1 for ITC and 58.30% F1 for micrometastases, improving over the second-best by 4.44% and 6.67% respectively. On TCGA-NSCLC under the 2-shot setting, the method attains 86.73% AUC and 80.96% F1, while on TCGA-RCC 2-shot it reaches 95.10% AUC and 82.86% F1, consistently surpassing prompt-based few-shot baselines like MOC and CoCoOp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-HGNet outperforms all six named baselines on every metric in Table 1. Compared to the strongest MIL baselines (TransMIL: 71.50% overall F1, 47.87% ITC F1, 47.82% Micro F1), the proposed method gains +5.69% overall F1, +9.26% ITC F1, and +10.48% Micro F1. Against the CNN-based TORCH (71.03% overall F1), the improvement is +6.16% overall, +10.87% ITC, and +11.84% Micro. The paper also compares against GNN-based and hypergraph-based methods (not individually named in the readable portion of Table 1); the best GNN method reaches 72.88% overall F1, 50.30% ITC F1, and 49.48% Micro F1, while the best hypergraph method (second-best overall) reaches approximately 74.09% overall F1, 52.69% ITC F1, and 51.63% Micro F1. On the TCGA few-shot benchmarks (Table 2), Dual-HGNet surpasses prompt-based methods CoCoOp [41] and MOC [38] as well as TOP [27] across 2-, 4-, and 8-shot settings, with AUC gains of 2.42% (2-shot) and 3.96% (4-shot) over the second-best on TCGA-NSCLC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three largely separate research threads—hypergraph neural networks for WSI analysis (Di et al., Shi et al., Han et al.), prompt-conditioned generative models (CoCoOp, MSCPT, TOP), and node-level data augmentation for class imbalance (pseudo-bag mixup, RankMix, MeXD)—into a unified dual-level generative framework. Where prior hypergraph methods preserve the original feature distribution and prior augmentation methods operate only at the node level, this work introduces a prompt-guided hierarchical VAE that generates entire minority-class hypergraphs (features + topology) and pairs it with an anchor-diffusion node mixup that enriches individual positive nodes. The CONCH vision-language foundation model serves as the shared backbone for both image patch encoding and text-prompt conditioning, linking the generative and discriminative stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The four-class lymph node metastasis staging task is clinically critical yet severely imbalanced, and the paper demonstrates that addressing both inter-class scarcity (via hypergraph-level synthesis) and intra-slide node sparsity (via anchor-diffusion mixup) yields complementary gains that neither strategy achieves alone. A key limitation is that the primary evaluation relies on an internal, non-public dataset (NIMM), while the TCGA few-shot experiments test a different classification task (RCC/NSCLC subtypes), leaving open whether the dual-level generative strategy transfers to other WSI tasks with similar imbalance profiles. The heavy dependence on CONCH for both visual and textual encoding, plus the multi-stage training pipeline (HGVAE pre-training → joint HGNN + mixup training), raises practical adoption barriers that the paper does not fully address.
