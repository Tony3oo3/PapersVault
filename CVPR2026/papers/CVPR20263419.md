## Problem

Unsupervised cross-modal hashing suffers from three coupled issues: dominant modalities bias the fusion process, contrastive objectives indiscriminately repel semantically related in-batch samples as false negatives, and partial or corrupted observations cause the model to over-rely on a single modality. No existing framework jointly addresses partial-feature robustness, false-negative mitigation, and semantic-structure preservation within a unified unsupervised hashing pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-text pairs, encoding each modality into a sequence of token embeddings (L_v × D for visual, L_t × D for textual) via separate encoders. Before multimodal fusion, independently sampled binary keep masks retain a configurable proportion ρ of tokens per modality, producing both full and partially observed views. The output is a pair of relaxed binary hash codes of length b (16–128 bits) per modality, binarized via sign for Hamming-distance retrieval, along with lightweight decoders that reconstruct the original features from the codes.

## Method

UWMCH introduces pre-fusion token masking: binary keep masks are applied independently to visual and textual token sequences before concatenation and passage through a shared Transformer fusion encoder, yielding masked and unmasked fused representations from two augmented views (four total). A weighted masked contrastive objective (WMCL) aligns all four symmetric masked–unmasked pairings via a modified InfoNCE where positive weights emphasize hard positives and negative weights are modulated by a semantic affinity prior that combines instance-level cosine similarity with a cluster-consensus term from online mini-batch K-means, thereby down-weighting likely false negatives. Dual-scale structural regularization further stabilizes the fusion space: Cluster-Centroid Agreement anchors current K-means prototypes to an EMA bank via InfoNCE to suppress global drift, while Semantic Structure Regularization enforces the semantic affinity matrix as a target for the cosine-similarity geometry of both unmasked and masked features. The full training loss combines WMCL, CCA, SSR, a quantization loss driving relaxed codes toward ±1, and a reconstruction loss preserving semantic fidelity after binarization.

## Results

UWMCH achieves the best mAP in 21 out of 24 evaluation settings (3 datasets × 2 retrieval directions × 4 bit lengths). On MIRFLICKR-25K, UWMCH reaches 91.60% mAP for I→T and 90.59% for T→I at 128 bits. On the more challenging MS COCO, it attains 91.26% (I→T) and 91.10% (T→I) at 128 bits, outperforming the strongest baseline RSHNL by 1.19 and 0.89 points respectively. On NUS-WIDE, UWMCH leads by up to 1.28 points at 16 bits for I→T retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UWMCH outperforms all eight baselines in 21 of 24 settings, with the closest competitor being RSHNL [25]. UWMCH loses to RSHNL in three T→I settings: MIRFLICKR-25K at 64 bits (89.97 vs. 90.12) and 128 bits (90.59 vs. 91.21), and NUS-WIDE at 128 bits (85.34 vs. 85.75). On I→T retrieval, UWMCH dominates across all datasets and bit lengths, with the largest margin on NUS-WIDE at 16 bits (+1.28 over UCCH) and MS COCO at 128 bits (+1.19 over RSHNL). Among older baselines, DJSRH [28] and JDSH [18] trail by 8–13 points, while AGCH [46] and CIRH [51] are competitive on MIRFLICKR-25K but fall behind on NUS-WIDE and MS COCO. VLKD [29] and UCCH [7] are strong on NUS-WIDE I→T but underperform UWMCH by 1–3 points on MS COCO. VTM-UCH [6] is the strongest T→I baseline on MIRFLICKR-25K (91.03 at 128 bits) yet still trails UWMCH on MS COCO T→I by 1.60 points at 128 bits.

## Relation to Prior Work

UWMCH builds directly on InfMasking [39], which introduced pre-fusion token masking to align masked and unmasked multimodal views, and extends it by replacing the standard InfoNCE with a semantic-affinity-weighted variant (WMNCE) that down-weights likely false negatives and up-weights hard positives. The dual-scale structural regularization (Cluster-Centroid Agreement and Semantic Structure Regularization) draws on prototype-stabilization ideas from self-supervised learning and pairwise-structure preservation from contrastive hashing, but combines them in a unified unsupervised cross-modal hashing framework where prior methods (e.g., RSHNL, VTM-UCH, UCCH) address partial-observation robustness, false-negative mitigation, and geometric stability largely in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly addressing partial-feature robustness, false-negative interference, and semantic-geometry drift within a single training objective, UWMCH offers a more principled recipe for unsupervised cross-modal hashing than the patchwork of auxiliary losses common in prior work. The consistent 1–2 point gains over strong baselines across three datasets and four bit lengths suggest the weighted-masking formulation is a reusable component for future cross-modal retrieval systems. Open questions include whether the pre-fusion masking strategy transfers to non-token architectures (e.g., CNN-based encoders), how the method scales to datasets with tens of millions of pairs, and whether the online K-means and EMA prototype bank introduce prohibitive overhead in streaming or online settings.
