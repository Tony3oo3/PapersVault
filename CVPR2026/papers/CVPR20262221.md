## Problem

Generating segmentation datasets via pretrained T2I models faces a tension: without fine-tuning, generated images misalign with the target domain (e.g., wrong viewpoint or style), but standard LoRA fine-tuning overfits and memorizes the training data, destroying the model's ability to produce diverse, informative samples. The paper identifies that fine-tuning should selectively target only the concepts necessary for domain alignment (e.g., viewpoint or style) while preserving all other pretrained knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source labeled dataset (e.g., Cityscapes), a user-specified target concept (style or viewpoint), and text prompts of the form 'Photorealistic first-person urban street view with [class names] in [weather]' as input. It produces 500–3000 image-label pairs where labels are generated by a Mask2Former-shaped label generator that consumes multi-scale generative features (feature maps and cross-attention maps) extracted from the fine-tuned SDXL model at inference time, rather than requiring pixel-level annotations of generated images.

## Method

CA-LoRA first measures per-layer concept awareness by computing the ratio of concept-loss gradient norm to diffusion-loss gradient norm, where the concept loss is defined using concept-augmented prompts (e.g., swapping 'photorealistic' for 'sketch' to probe style, or changing viewpoint descriptors to probe viewpoint). This normalization removes positional bias across layers and isolates which projection layers (Q, K, V, OUT) are truly sensitive to the target concept. LoRA adapters are then attached only to the top-k% most concept-aware projection layers, leaving all other weights frozen. A lightweight Mask2Former-shaped label generator is trained on generative features from the CA-LoRA fine-tuned model, which reduces the domain gap between training and inference-time features. Finally, diverse image-label pairs are generated by varying weather conditions and class names in the prompt, exploiting the preserved text-adherence of the unfrozen layers.

## Results

On in-domain Cityscapes segmentation, CA-LoRA achieves +2.30% mIoU over the baseline at 0.3% data fraction (44.13 vs. 41.83) and +1.34% at 100% (80.74 vs. 79.40), consistently outperforming all baselines across every data ratio. For domain generalization, CA-LoRA yields an average mIoU gain of +2.49% with ColorAug, +1.63% with DAFormer, and +1.53% with HRDA across ACDC, Dark Zurich, BDD100K, and Mapillary Vistas. The gains are most pronounced on challenging target domains (ACDC, Dark Zurich) where adverse weather and lighting drive the domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CA-LoRA outperforms all five baselines across both in-domain and domain generalization settings. Against InstructPix2Pix, CA-LoRA wins decisively: InstructPix2Pix actually degrades performance at 10% (−2.81%) and 100% (−1.34%) Cityscapes fractions, while CA-LoRA gains +1.27% and +1.34% respectively. Against DatasetDM (no fine-tuning), CA-LoRA improves by +1.31 mIoU at 0.3% and +0.29 at 100%, with the gap widening at lower data fractions. Against full LoRA (fine-tuning all layers), CA-LoRA is competitive at 1% (+2.75 vs. +2.65) but pulls ahead at 10% (+1.27 vs. +0.19) and 100% (+1.34 vs. +0.35), confirming that selective concept-aware tuning avoids the memorization that limits full LoRA. Against AdaLoRA, CA-LoRA wins at every fraction, with the largest margin at 10% (+1.27 vs. −0.70). In domain generalization (Table 2), CA-LoRA achieves the highest average mIoU under all three DG methods (ColorAug: 50.39, DAFormer: 51.32, HRDA: 53.61), with the largest gains on ACDC and Dark Zurich where weather/lighting shifts are most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA fine-tuning paradigm [20] by introducing a concept-awareness metric that automatically identifies which projection layers (Q, K, V, OUT) in a diffusion model are sensitive to a specific visual attribute (style or viewpoint), then applies LoRA updates only to those layers. It builds on the DatasetDM [51] pipeline for label generation from generative features, but departs from it by fine-tuning the T2I model with CA-LoRA before training the label generator, thereby reducing the domain gap in generative features at inference. Conceptually, it automates what prior work [2, 50, 55] achieved through manual per-block ablation, and contrasts with uniform fine-tuning methods (LoRA, AdaLoRA) that update all layers equally and thus overfit to irrelevant concepts in the training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-cost recipe (≈1 hour on a single V100) for generating domain-aligned segmentation datasets that are both well-matched to the target domain and diverse enough to improve generalization, which is valuable for practitioners facing data scarcity in urban-scene segmentation. However, the method is currently limited to two concept types (style and viewpoint) and to urban-scene settings; extending the concept-awareness framework to other attributes (e.g., object layout, camera height, season) remains open. The modest absolute gains (+1.3–2.3 mIoU) suggest that while selective fine-tuning is a sound principle, the bottleneck for synthetic-data-driven segmentation may lie elsewhere (e.g., label quality, generation diversity at scale).
