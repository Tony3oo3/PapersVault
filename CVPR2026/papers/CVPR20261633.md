## Problem

Existing surgical triplet recognition methods treat intra-frame label dependencies and inter-frame temporal semantics as separate concerns, failing to jointly model how instrument-verb-target co-occurrence constraints evolve across the surgical timeline. Graph-based approaches capture static label relations but cannot adapt to frame-specific visual context, while CAM-based methods provide spatial cues without explicit relational reasoning, and simple fusion of temporal and relational streams overlooks their complementary structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261633/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes single surgical video frames (3×H×W) at 1 fps, extracting multi-scale features from a CNN backbone and class-specific CAMs, while also ingesting text prompts for each entity label through a SigLIP text encoder to obtain intrinsic semantic priors. Output is a set of IVT triplet predictions with per-triplet confidence scores, evaluated via triplet-level AP, component-level AP (instrument, verb, target), Top-K retrieval accuracy, and the newly proposed TCER metric that decomposes errors into mis-matching (correct entities, wrong pairing) and mis-classification (wrong entity) categories.

## Method

The framework integrates four modules: a multi-scale encoder producing global spatial descriptors and CAMs; a dual-memory temporal attention (inspired by SAM2) maintaining recurrent semantic and decision memory buffers over N=8 frames; a Label Correlation Modeling module that builds a GCN graph where nodes concatenate SigLIP text embeddings with CAM-derived visual activation cues, and edges are computed by MS-CAMRE (CAM-guided cross-attention over multi-scale features with SE fusion and zero-convolution) combined with a Jaccard co-occurrence matrix as a relational prior; and a Bidirectional Temporal-Relational Fusion Attention (BTRFA) that applies symmetric cross-attention between temporal and relational features followed by a learnable sigmoid gate for adaptive fusion. Training proceeds in two stages (spatial encoder pretraining, then joint fine-tuning of all downstream modules) with a composite loss combining per-triplet BCE and a coupling loss that supervises atomic entity predictions derived from triplet-level outputs via max-pooling over containing triplets.

## Results

On CholecT45, TRCoRSurg achieves 40.9% AP_IVT, a 5.1% relative improvement over the best baseline (TERL at 38.9%), and reduces TCER_m from 6.50 to 4.16 (a 36% relative reduction). On ProstaTD, it reaches 37.5% AP_IVT (7.8% relative gain over SDSwin at 34.8%) and lowers TCER_m from 17.70 to 13.20 (a 25% relative reduction). The method also leads on component-level AP (AP_I = 95.7, AP_V = 73.6, AP_T = 51.1 on CholecT45) and Top-5 retrieval (86.4% on CholecT45, 68.1% on ProstaTD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRCoRSurg outperforms all six baselines on AP_IVT across both datasets: on CholecT45 it leads TERL (38.9) by 2.0 points, CoLSurgical (38.2) by 2.7 points, and RDV (29.9) by 11.0 points; on ProstaTD it surpasses MT4MTLKD (35.2) by 2.3 points and RDV (26.1) by 11.4 points. On the proposed TCER_m metric, the gains are even more pronounced—4.16 vs. 6.50 (MT4MTLKD) on CholecT45 and 13.20 vs. 17.70 (MT4MTLKD) on ProstaTD—indicating substantially fewer compositional inconsistencies in predicted triplets. The method also achieves the best component-level AP for instruments (95.7 on CholecT45, 88.0 on ProstaTD) and verbs (73.6, 65.9), while target AP is competitive but not always the highest (e.g., CoLSurgical reports 41.9 vs. 51.1 for Ours on CholecT45, but CoLSurgical does not report TCER). On Top-20 retrieval, CoLSurgical (97.2) slightly edges out Ours (96.2) on CholecT45, the only metric where a baseline marginally wins. Overall, the temporal-relational co-reasoning framework consistently dominates on the primary triplet-level and consistency metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate research threads—GCN-based label dependency modeling (Xi et al., Forest GCN), CAM-guided spatial attention (RDV, SDSwin), and dual-memory temporal attention (inspired by SAM2)—into a single unified architecture. Where prior surgical triplet methods either model intra-frame label correlations (graph-based) or inter-frame temporal dynamics (RIT, MT4MTLKD) as independent modules with simple fusion, TRCoRSurg introduces a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism (BTRFA) that lets temporal and relational streams co-evolve. The MS-CAMRE module repurposes multi-scale class activation maps as attention queries for relational extraction, a use of CAMs that goes beyond their traditional role as spatial priors for classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TCER metric fills a genuine evaluation gap: prior work measured per-entity or per-triplet AP but never quantified whether correctly identified entities were correctly *paired*, a failure mode with direct clinical implications (e.g., pairing a correct instrument with an inappropriate verb). The 36% and 25% relative TCER reductions on CholecT45 and ProstaTD suggest relational consistency is a separable and improvable axis beyond raw accuracy. However, the authors acknowledge the framework's complexity and training difficulty, and open questions remain about whether the bidirectional fusion and multi-scale relational extraction are necessary for real-time intra-operative deployment or whether lighter alternatives suffice.
