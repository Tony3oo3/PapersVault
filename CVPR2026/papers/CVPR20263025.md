## Problem

Existing spatial motion control methods either require task-specific training of conditioning branches, rely on slow inference-time optimization that can get stuck in local minima, or treat user-specified constraints as soft objectives that are never exactly satisfied. What is missing is a sampler that enforces hard spatial constraints exactly, operates zero-shot without any retraining or inner-loop optimization, and still preserves the naturalness of a pretrained motion prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263025/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt together with spatial constraints expressed as a linear observation model y = Ax + ε, where A is a known linear operator (e.g., orthographic projection for 2D-to-3D lifting, or a diagonal mask for keyframe inpainting) and y contains the user-specified measurements. For inpainting, inputs are sparse 3D joint positions at selected frames; for 2D-to-3D lifting, inputs are 2D joint coordinates over time plus known camera parameters (scale, rotation). The output is a complete N×J×3 motion tensor in absolute world coordinates that satisfies the constraints to numerical precision (zero reprojection or trajectory error).

## Method

ProjFlow is a training-free projection sampler for rectified-flow motion models. At each ODE step it predicts the clean endpoint via Tweedie's formula, then solves a closed-form convex quadratic to project that endpoint onto the linear constraint set, using a novel kinematics-aware metric R built from the graph Laplacian of the skeletal topology so that corrections propagate coherently along the kinematic tree rather than in raw Euclidean space. For sparse inputs such as long inpainting gaps, the method introduces time-varying pseudo-observations created by per-joint linear interpolation, whose influence is controlled by a shrinking temporal mask and an adaptive variance that decays with sampling progress and local motion curvature. The corrected endpoint is then stochastically recomposed with mixed noise (adapted from FlowDPS) to produce the next state, preserving the pretrained prior without any task-specific retraining or iterative optimization.

## Results

On HumanML3D, ProjFlow (ACMDM-S-PS22+ProjFlow) achieves exact constraint satisfaction with 0.0000 trajectory, location, and average errors for both pelvis and all-joints control, while remaining zero-shot (no retraining). Its FID of 0.107 (pelvis) and 0.097 (all joints) is competitive with training-based controllers like MaskControl (0.066/0.095) and OmniControl (0.081/0.126), and clearly beats the zero-shot DNO baseline (0.151/0.147). For 2D-to-3D lifting, ProjFlow achieves FID 0.349 (Average) and 0.168 (Cross), outperforming Sketch2Anim (0.525/0.577), while enforcing zero reprojection error (MPJPE-2D = 0.000) versus Sketch2Anim's residual 0.036.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProjFlow is the only zero-shot method that achieves exact (0.0000) constraint satisfaction across all control metrics, while the zero-shot DNO baseline [21] leaves residual errors (e.g., 0.0089 avg. error on pelvis) and has a notably higher FID (0.151 vs. 0.107). Among training-based methods, MaskControl [45] achieves the lowest FID (0.066 pelvis, 0.095 all joints) but still exhibits non-zero average error (0.0093/0.0065), and OmniControl [61] shows larger deviations (0.0338/0.0404 avg. error). The ControlNet variant of the same base model (ACMDM-S-PS22+CtrlNet [39]) improves FID to 0.067/0.070 but still fails to satisfy constraints exactly (0.0100/0.0197 avg. error). For 2D-to-3D lifting, ProjFlow outperforms Sketch2Anim [67] on FID (0.349 vs. 0.525 Average; 0.168 vs. 0.577 Cross) and achieves zero reprojection error (MPJPE-2D = 0.000) where Sketch2Anim retains 0.036. The ablation confirms that the kinematics-aware metric is critical: replacing it with Euclidean distance inflates FID from 0.097 to 1.152, and removing stochastic recomposition (η_t=0) degrades FID to 3.429.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProjFlow adapts the posterior-projection sampling paradigm from image restoration (DDNM, FlowDPS) to the flow-matching regime for human motion, replacing the standard Euclidean geometry with a kinematics-aware metric built from the skeletal graph Laplacian. It departs from the dominant paradigm of injecting spatial constraints as soft guidance terms or ControlNet-style conditioning branches (OmniControl, MaskControl, ACMDM-S-PS22+CtrlNet) by enforcing them as hard equalities via a closed-form projection at each ODE step. The stochastic recomposition step is directly inherited from FlowDPS [23], while the time-varying pseudo-observation mechanism for inpainting is a novel extension not present in prior projection samplers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, training-free route to interactive motion authoring with guaranteed exact constraint satisfaction, which prior guidance- or optimization-based methods cannot provide. The kinematics-aware metric is a principled contribution that could generalize to other structured sequential data beyond human motion. The main limitation is that the closed-form projection only handles linear constraints; extending it to non-linear constraints (e.g., inequality constraints like floor contact or joint limits) remains an open problem. Additionally, evaluation is confined to a single base model (ACMDMS-PS22) and dataset (HumanML3D), leaving open whether the approach transfers to other motion priors or longer sequences.
