## Problem

Existing object ReID methods assume training and testing data share the same object category, requiring separate models for persons, vehicles, animals, etc. This category-consistent assumption is annotation-intensive, computationally costly, and prevents a single model from handling the heterogeneous object spectrum encountered in real-world applications like smart cities and ecological monitoring. The paper introduces Object-Generalized Re-Identification (OG-ReID), which must learn identity representations that generalize across intrinsic structural differences in identity cues (e.g., body parts for persons vs. geometric contours for vehicles vs. texture patterns for animals), a fundamentally harder shift than the appearance-level domain variations addressed by prior domain-generalization work.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images resized to 256×256 with 10-pixel padding and random horizontal flipping (p=0.5), processing query and gallery images that may belong to entirely different object categories. At inference, only the global class-token readout z^G from the ViT-B/16 backbone is used to produce a d-dimensional identity embedding for instance-level match ranking via cosine similarity or learned metric. Training jointly mixes five source categories (person, vehicle, vessel, panda, tiger) into a single 72K-image pool, while evaluation spans 137 categories across nine datasets including wild animals, pet faces, buildings, and regular objects.

## Method

MGOR reinterprets meta-learning as semantic distributional regularization through three coupled components. (1) EDPS constructs category-balanced P×K mini-batches and uniformly splits them into meta-train and meta-test halves, so each episode presents a controlled perturbation of the category mixture. (2) MOIL optimizes identity classification and metric losses on the meta-train split, then evaluates the same frozen parameters on the meta-test split to enforce distributional invariance, supplemented by a Conditional Pairwise Alignment term that aligns positive/negative distance distributions across splits via 1D sliced Wasserstein distance. (3) HSE introduces a dual-token ViT (global token for category-agnostic identity, local token for category-specific detail) with asymmetric supervision: the global identity loss is applied to both episode splits while the category-local loss is active only in meta-train, implicitly separating invariant from category-sensitive factors through complementary gradients on a shared backbone. The total episode loss combines meta-train discrimination, meta-test stability, category-local sharpening, and the alignment penalty, trained with SGD (lr=1e-3) for 60 epochs at batch size 64.

## Results

On the multi-category evaluation (the paper's primary setting), MGOR achieves an average mAP of 32.2 and R1 of 51.1 across CUTE, GZGC, PetFace, and Wildlife71, outperforming the strongest competitor VICP† (21.2 mAP, 43.5 R1) by over 10 mAP points. On single-category benchmarks, MGOR reaches 38.6 mAP on UAV-VeID and 19.5 mAP on University-1652, surpassing all domain-generalizable baselines. The ablation shows EDPS+MOIL lifts the TransReID baseline from 23.3 to 31.6 mAP on CUTE, and adding HSE further improves to 32.9 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGOR achieves the highest average mAP (32.2) and R1 (51.1) across the four multi-category benchmarks, beating the closest non-adaptive competitor PAT (28.4 mAP) by 3.8 points and the LLM-based VICP† (21.2 mAP) by 11.0 points. On PetFace, the gap is most pronounced: MGOR scores 41.4 mAP versus 35.8 for PAT and 30.9 for VICP†, indicating strong fine-grained cross-species generalization. On Wildlife71, MGOR leads in mAP (86.0) while PAT (83.3) and DACS (82.7) are competitive on R1 (95.9/95.8 vs. 95.8). In the single-category regime, CLIP-ReID narrowly edges MGOR on MSMT17 (19.4 vs. 18.1 mAP), but MGOR dominates on UAV-VeID (38.6 vs. 35.3 mAP) and University-1652 (19.5 vs. 15.4 mAP). Notably, several DG-ReID methods (BAU, ADSR, ReNorm) collapse to near-random performance (e.g., BAU: 2.7 mAP on MSMT17, 0.4 mAP on University-1652) when trained on mixed categories, confirming that style-normalization-based domain generalization does not transfer to category shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TransReID ViT backbone and repurposes meta-learning—previously applied in DG-ReID to simulate domain splits—as a mechanism for semantic distributional regularization across object categories. It explicitly departs from the domain-generalization ReID line (PAT, ReNorm, TransMatcher, etc.), which assumes a fixed category and targets appearance-level shifts, by formulating a harder problem where identity-defining cues themselves change across categories (body parts vs. geometric contours vs. texture patterns). The closest prior work, VICP, still relies on category-conditioned prompts and target-domain adaptation, whereas MGOR operates without category priors or target data, positioning OG-ReID as a strict generalization of both single-category ReID and DG-ReID.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that most existing DG-ReID techniques degrade substantially—or even fall below domain-specific baselines—when forced to handle mixed-category training, revealing a fundamental limitation of appearance-invariance strategies in the face of structural semantic shifts. This opens a practical path toward a single ReID model for open-world deployments (smart cities, ecological monitoring, intelligent transportation) that currently require separate per-category models. Key limitations include reliance on a modest 5-category training pool (72K images), a single ViT-B/16 backbone, and the absence of self-supervised or vision-language pretraining; scaling to truly open-ended category spaces and reducing annotation dependence remain open challenges.
