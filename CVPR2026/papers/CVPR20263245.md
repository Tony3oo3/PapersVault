## Problem

State-of-the-art SE(2)-equivariant traffic simulation models (e.g., SMART, BehaviorGPT) achieve equivariance through explicit pairwise relative positional encodings that scale quadratically with the number of scene elements, limiting scalability to larger scenes, models, and datasets. This paper asks whether SE(2)-equivariance can be achieved without this quadratic overhead while maintaining comparable simulation realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263245/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 1 second of context containing agent states (2D position, heading, speed, bounding box dimensions) and map lane graph nodes (length, width, curvature, speed limit, boundary types), each encoded as an 8-dimensional multivector in the 2D projective geometric algebra R*_{2,0,1} plus auxiliary scalar features. It outputs per-agent discretized actions drawn from a class-specific vocabulary of 2048 actions (vehicle, cyclist, pedestrian), which are unrolled through a learned dynamics model to produce 8-second closed-loop trajectories at 10 Hz; 32 rollouts are generated per scenario for distribution-matching evaluation.

## Method

DriveGATr encodes 2D poses as multivectors in the 8-dimensional projective geometric algebra R*_{2,0,1} (a 2D reduction of GATr's 16-dimensional E(3) algebra) and computes SE(2)-invariant attention logits via the algebra's invariant inner product, which naturally captures relative position and orientation without explicit pairwise RPEs. The architecture stacks factorized attention blocks combining agent-to-map cross-attention, temporal causal self-attention, equivariant MLPs, and a novel invariant adapter that converts equivariant multivector features into invariant features by sandwiching them with each agent's local roto-translation. Distance-awareness features are appended to query and key multivectors so that the attention dot product encodes squared Euclidean distance, and the entire attention computation reduces to a single concatenated dot product compatible with FlashAttention kernels. Training is supervised with cross-entropy on discretized next-action predictions using a k-disk-derived vocabulary, with the model unrolled autoregressively for closed-loop simulation.

## Results

On the WOSAC 2024 validation set (Waymo Open Motion Dataset), DriveGATr-3M achieves an RMM of 0.7620 and minADE of 1.4192, outperforming the same-size BehaviorGPT baseline (RMM 0.7438) by 2%. The larger DriveGATr-30M variant reaches RMM 0.7636 and minADE 1.3682, approaching SMART-7M (RMM 0.7678, minADE 1.3532) without closed-loop fine-tuning. DriveGATr also establishes a superior Pareto front in training-loss-vs-FLOPs and inference FLOPs-vs-number-of-agents compared to all baselines, owing to its use of standard dot-product attention rather than explicit pairwise relative positional encodings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-size BehaviorGPT (3M, RMM 0.7438), DriveGATr-3M improves RMM by 2% (0.7620) but is slightly worse on minADE (1.4192 vs 1.3804). Against the leaderboard-top SMART-7M (RMM 0.7678) and its closed-loop fine-tuned variant CAT-K (RMM 0.7709), DriveGATr-30M is within 0.4–0.7% on RMM and within 0.02–0.07 m on minADE, without using closed-loop fine-tuning or top-k sampling. Against the three same-architecture baselines that differ only in how equivariance is introduced—Transformer with data augmentation (RMM 0.7257), Transformer + RPE (RMM 0.7251), and Transformer + DRoPE (RMM 0.7206)—DriveGATr-3M wins decisively on every metric, with RMM gains of 3.6–4.1% and minADE reductions of 0.25–0.50 m. The paper attributes these gains to DriveGATr's true SE(2)-equivariance (vs. approximate or translation-only equivariance in the baselines) and its ability to attend over the full scene without the quadratic memory cost of explicit pairwise RPEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveGATr extends the Geometric Algebra Transformer (GATr) from Brehmer et al., which was E(3)-equivariant and operated on 16-dimensional projective geometric algebra, down to an SE(2)-equivariant 8-dimensional formulation tailored for 2D driving scenes. It departs from the dominant paradigm in equivariant traffic modeling (SMART, BehaviorGPT, GoRela) that achieves SE(2)-equivariance through explicit pairwise relative positional encodings, which scale quadratically with scene size. Instead, DriveGATr encodes poses and geometry directly as multivectors and computes equivariant attention logits via an invariant inner product, enabling drop-in use of optimized kernels like FlashAttention. This positions it against DRoPE (translation-equivariant only) and VN-Transformer (sacrifices true equivariance for stability) as a method that is both fully SE(2)-equivariant and computationally efficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic cost of explicit RPEs has been a practical bottleneck preventing equivariant models from scaling to larger scenes, batch sizes, and lane graphs; DriveGATr removes this bottleneck while maintaining provable equivariance, establishing a superior performance-vs-compute Pareto front on WOSAC. The invariant adapter and distance-awareness features are practical architectural contributions that could transfer to other equivariant transformer designs. Limitations include the restriction to 2D (SE(2)) rather than full 3D, evaluation only on traffic simulation (not forecasting or planning), and the absence of closed-loop fine-tuning or top-k sampling that prior SOTA methods employ. Open questions include whether the geometric algebra approach extends cleanly to 3D, whether it generalizes to other equivariant tasks, and whether the efficiency advantage persists at much larger model and dataset scales.
