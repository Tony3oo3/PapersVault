## Problem

Existing SLAM systems degrade or fail on blurry inputs because feature-based trackers lose correspondences and dense methods lack explicit blur modeling. Prior blur-aware SLAM approaches either handle only motion blur, assume every frame is blurred (incurring unnecessary computation on sharp frames), or cannot jointly model both motion and defocus blur in an online setting. Unblur-SLAM addresses this by being the first method to simultaneously handle both blur types while adapting its computational effort to the actual blur level of each input frame.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a stream of RGB images (which may contain motion blur, defocus blur, or be sharp) along with the running pose history, and produces a camera pose trajectory, per-frame dense depth maps, and a globally consistent 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation. A key design choice is that frames are dynamically classified into three tiers—sharp, successfully deblurred, and heavily blurred—using the ARNIQA quality metric, and each tier triggers a different processing path: sharp frames skip the expensive Gaussian refinement entirely, while heavily blurred frames bypass the tracker and are handled purely through virtual sub-frame rendering. The deblurring network itself runs at approximately 600 ms per frame, which is the primary bottleneck for real-time operation.

## Method

Unblur-SLAM combines a physics-constrained feed-forward deblurring network with a 3D Gaussian Splatting mapping backend in a three-tier pipeline. The deblurring network is trained with a two-stage strategy: first on semi-synthetic motion-blur data (RED, GoPro, ReplicaBlurry) to learn mid-exposure frame extraction under the physical blur formation model B = ∫ I(t)⊗K_defocus dt, then fine-tuned on DPDD for defocus robustness, ensuring 3D-consistent outputs. For successfully deblurred frames, the system runs Droid-SLAM for tracking and refines the 3DGS map via multi-scale optimization using a Blur Proposal Network (BPN) that learns per-pixel, depth-dependent convolution kernels to model residual blur. For frames where deblurring fails, the system generates N_sub virtual cameras along the estimated blur trajectory, renders each sub-frame through BPN, and averages them to match the observed blur, effectively discretizing the temporal integration in the physical blur model. The 3DGS representation is deformed in response to depth updates from the tracker, and global consistency is maintained through loop closure detection and periodic global bundle adjustment with adaptive frame weighting that prioritizes sharp observations.

## Results

On the Deblur-NeRF motion blur benchmark, Unblur-SLAM achieves 29.49 dB PSNR, 0.9213 SSIM, and 0.0728 LPIPS, outperforming the best offline baseline CoMoGaussian (27.85 dB) by 1.64 dB in PSNR. On the Deblur-NeRF defocus blur subset, it reaches 27.45 dB PSNR versus 24.21 dB for the best competing method (Deblur-GS). For tracking, Unblur-SLAM reduces ATE RMSE to 0.336 m on TUM-RGBD and 0.128 m on IndoorMCD, improving over Droid-SLAM (0.380 m and 0.138 m respectively). On the ArchViz extreme-blur synthetic dataset, it achieves an average ATE of 0.0056 m versus 0.0084 m for MBA-SLAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unblur-SLAM outperforms all seven offline deblurring baselines on the Deblur-NeRF motion blur benchmark, with the largest margin over CoMoGaussian (+1.64 dB PSNR) and BAGS (+2.79 dB PSNR), while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.0728 vs. 0.0822 for CoMoGaussian). On the defocus blur subset, it surpasses Deblur-GS by 3.24 dB PSNR (27.45 vs. 24.21), a substantial gap over all five competing methods. For tracking, it improves ATE over Droid-SLAM by 0.044 m on TUM and 0.010 m on IndoorMCD, and beats MBA-SLAM on ArchViz by 33% in average ATE (0.0056 vs. 0.0084 m). In the TUM rendering comparison (Table 5), Unblur-SLAM achieves 28.03 dB PSNR on fr1_desk versus 27.23 for I2-SLAM and 19.93 for Deblur-SLAM, with the gap widening on sequences with higher blur ratios. The only baseline that is faster is Splat-SLAM (1.24 vs. 0.74 FPS), but Splat-SLAM is not a deblurring method and is listed only as a runtime reference; I2-SLAM is dramatically slower at 0.095 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

Unblur-SLAM builds directly on Droid-SLAM for front-end tracking and Splat-SLAM for the 3DGS-based mapping, global optimization, and loop closure infrastructure, while adopting the Blur Proposal Network (BPN) from BAGS for per-pixel residual blur modeling. It departs from prior blur-aware SLAM methods (MBA-SLAM, I2-SLAM, Deblur-SLAM) in two key ways: it handles both motion and defocus blur simultaneously rather than assuming small-aperture motion blur only, and it introduces a physics-constrained training scheme for a feed-forward deblurring network that enforces 3D consistency by modeling the physical blur formation process (Eq. 1). The adaptive blur-detection-and-skip mechanism also distinguishes it from all prior blur-aware SLAM systems that uniformly apply costly refinement to every frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SLAM pipeline to jointly handle motion and defocus blur with adaptive computation, which is practically important since real-world captures (handheld, automotive) routinely mix both blur types in varying proportions. The physics-constrained deblurring training addresses a concrete failure mode identified in I2-SLAM (3D inconsistency of single-image deblur networks), offering a principled path to integrating feed-forward restoration into geometric pipelines. However, the system remains at 0.74 FPS with a 600 ms deblur-network bottleneck, and the authors acknowledge the trade-off between real-time efficiency and accuracy, leaving open whether the multi-component pipeline (deblur net + BPN + virtual sub-frames + multi-scale optimization) can be streamlined for true real-time deployment.
