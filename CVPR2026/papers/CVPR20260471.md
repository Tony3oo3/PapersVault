## Problem

Incremental open-vocabulary 3D instance-semantic mapping remains challenging because existing pipelines either assume a fixed closed-set ontology or fuse dense per-pixel VLM features into volumetric representations, which is computationally prohibitive and temporally inconsistent. Without stable class-agnostic 3D instances, aggregating open-set features across views is noisy due to occlusions and inconsistent 2D segmentations, and running expensive segmentation models per frame precludes real-time operation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260471/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a streaming RGB-D sequence with known camera poses (SE(3)) and produces a class-agnostic 3D instance map stored in a TSDF voxel grid (0.1 m resolution) where each voxel carries an instance identity, plus a per-instance zero-shot semantic embedding obtained from SigLIP. Semantic features are extracted only from a small, adaptively selected subset of views per instance rather than every frame, and the final output supports open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation and natural-language query retrieval over the reconstructed scene.

## Method

OVI-MAP decouples instance formation from semantic inference into two stages. First, each RGB-D frame is segmented into class-agnostic entity proposals (CropFormer) refined by depth-discontinuity boundaries, lifted to 3D, and associated to existing instances via spatial voting over TSDF voxel labels; new instances are created when overlap falls below a threshold, and labels are stabilized through per-voxel support accumulation. Second, an object-centric view-coverage module maintains a 180×240 spherical bin map per instance and selects only views that occupy a sufficient fraction of previously unexplored bins (novelty ratio above a threshold), reducing VLM queries by ~47% versus pixel-counting heuristics. For each selected view, SigLIP embeddings are extracted from both a full object crop and a background-masked crop, then fused into a running per-instance embedding using visibility-aware (pixel-count) weighted averaging, yielding stable, view-invariant open-set descriptors without dense 3D semantic fields.

## Results

On Replica, OVI-MAP achieves 26.5 mIoU and 34.5 AP25 for open-vocabulary 3D semantic-instance segmentation, outperforming the best online baseline OVO-SLAM (24.9 mIoU, 28.1 AP25) and all offline methods (OpenScene 19.8, OpenNeRF 19.2, Semantic Gaussian 16.1 mIoU). On ScanNet, it reaches 17.5 mIoU and 23.4 AP25, again surpassing OVO-SLAM (14.6 mIoU, 19.4 AP25) and all offline baselines. Under a 30 FPS real-time constraint, OVI-MAP retains 27.0 mIoU on Replica (even slightly above its unconstrained score), while OVO-SLAM degrades to 21.8 mIoU. For instance segmentation alone (Table 2), OVI-MAP achieves 36.3 mIoU and 22.0 AP75 on Replica, beating OVO-SLAM (42.7 mIoU but only 11.1 AP75) at high IoU thresholds and approaching offline Mask3D (23.1 mIoU, 14.3 AP75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary online baseline OVO-SLAM, OVI-MAP wins on mIoU by +1.6 (Replica) and +2.9 (ScanNet), and on AP25 by +6.4 and +4.0 respectively, with the largest gap at AP50 (+3.7 on Replica). OVO-SLAM retains a slight edge on mAcc (34.0 vs 32.2) and APall (9.1 vs 8.5) on Replica, but OVI-MAP dominates under the 30 FPS constraint where OVO-SLAM drops 3.1 mIoU while OVI-MAP actually improves by 0.5. Against offline methods, OVI-MAP outperforms OpenScene (19.8 mIoU), OpenNeRF (19.2), Semantic Gaussian (16.1), and both Mask3D+OM3D (18.7) and Segment3D+OM3D (17.3) on Replica mIoU, and all offline baselines on ScanNet where OpenNeRF fails entirely. For instance segmentation (Table 2), OVI-MAP achieves 22.0 AP75 on Replica versus OVO-SLAM's 11.1, a near-doubling at the strictest IoU threshold, though Mask3D (trained on ScanNet) still leads on ScanNet mIoU (47.6 vs 41.2). The view-selection ablation (Table 4) shows OVI-MAP's object-centric coverage strategy matches pixel-counting accuracy while using only 47% of the VLM queries, confirming the efficiency gain is not at the cost of semantic quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TSDF-based volumetric mapping framework of Voxblox++ [13] and the panoptic instance fusion pipeline of Miao et al. [31], but deliberately removes the closed-set semantic coupling that both rely on. It departs from the dominant paradigm of storing dense per-voxel VLM embeddings (OpenScene, OpenNeRF, ConceptFusion) by instead maintaining a purely geometric, class-agnostic instance map and querying a VLM (SigLIP) only from a small set of object-centrically selected views. The spatial-voting instance association mechanism replaces the semantic-label-driven grouping used in prior TSDF panoptic systems, while the object-centric view coverage strategy supersedes the pixel-counting heuristic of OpenMask3D [46] and the per-frame SAM reliance of OVO-SLAM [29].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that instance reconstruction and open-vocabulary semantic inference can be fully decoupled without sacrificing accuracy, this work removes a key architectural bottleneck for real-time embodied perception: agents no longer need dense 3D semantic fields or per-frame segmentation to achieve zero-shot scene understanding. The 47% reduction in VLM queries via view coverage makes open-vocabulary mapping practically deployable on resource-constrained platforms. However, the method remains sensitive to 2D segmentation quality (CropFormer), and the weak visual-textual alignment of current VLMs limits label disambiguation; tighter vision-language coupling and adaptive fusion strategies are identified as open directions.
