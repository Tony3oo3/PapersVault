## Problem

VLA models for autonomous driving hit a performance plateau during RL fine-tuning because SFT-constrained exploration causes all rollouts in long-tail, safety-critical scenarios to yield zero driving scores. The standard scalar reward (e.g., PDMS) signals failure but cannot diagnose its root cause—whether it stems from incorrect high-level planning, flawed cognitive reasoning, or poor trajectory execution—leaving the policy without a targeted gradient to escape these persistent failures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261957/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a front-view camera image, a text navigation command (e.g., 'Turn Left'), ego kinematic state (velocity, acceleration), and the historical trajectory of the last three frames sampled at 2 Hz. During the feedback-refinement pass, the input is augmented with the model's own prior (incorrect) response and a structured diagnostic report from a teacher VLM, covering meta-action analysis, think-process errors, safety/efficiency failures, and actionable lateral/longitudinal corrections. The output is a chain-of-thought reasoning trace (meta actions, key-obstacle analysis, safety checks) followed by a concrete driving trajectory with lateral and longitudinal components.

## Method

ELF-VLA augments GRPO-based RL with a teacher-student feedback loop: when all rollouts for a scenario fall below a PDMS threshold (s = 0.8), a Qwen3-VL-32B teacher generates a structured failure diagnosis, and the student VLA re-rolls out a refined trajectory conditioned on that feedback. The top-k refined responses whose trajectory reward exceeds the original batch maximum are re-injected into the GRPO training batch, providing a goal-directed gradient that was previously absent. To handle the conditioning mismatch (refined tokens generated under the feedback query but optimized under the base query), the paper applies a Policy Shaping function f(x) = x/(x+gamma) that up-weights low-probability tokens, preventing gradient explosion. Training uses a two-stage SFT (driving Q&A pretraining, then mixed base+feedback trajectory training) followed by RL on a curated 24k subset of high-value scenarios, with a composite reward combining PDMS, format compliance, and endpoint accuracy.

## Results

On NAVSIMv1 (vision-only), ELF-VLA-8B achieves a state-of-the-art PDMS of 91.0, surpassing the previous best vision-only method AutoVLA-3B (89.1) by 1.9 points and the SFT-only baseline (87.4) by 3.6 points. On NAVSIMv2, ELF-VLA achieves 87.1 EPDMS, tying with the recent SOTA DriveSuprim and outperforming DriveVLA-W0-3B (86.1) and HydraMDP++ (85.6). For high-level planning, ELF-VLA reaches 80.3% accuracy, exceeding the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (28.7%) by over 51 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELF-VLA outperforms all 13 compared baselines or ties on the primary metrics. On NAVSIMv1 PDMS, it beats the strongest prior vision-only method AutoVLA-3B by 1.9 points (91.0 vs. 89.1) and the LiDAR-augmented methods DriveVLA-W0-3B and GoalFlow (both 90.3) by 0.7 points, while leading UniAD (83.4) and TransFuser (84.0) by 7.6 and 7.0 points respectively. On NAVSIMv2 EPDMS, ELF-VLA ties DriveSuprim at 87.1 but exceeds DriveVLA-W0-3B (86.1), HydraMDP++ (85.6), DiffusionDrive (84.5), and RecogDrive-8B (83.6) by 1.0 to 3.5 points. For high-level planning accuracy, ELF-VLA (80.3%) surpasses the 72B-parameter Qwen2.5-VL-72B (28.7%) by 51.6 points and the untrained InternVL3-8B base (20.1%) by 60.2 points, demonstrating that the feedback-guided RL training internalizes planning knowledge far beyond what scale alone provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL fine-tuning paradigm used by recent VLA driving models (Drive-R1, AdaThinkDrive, AutoVLA) by replacing the information-sparse scalar reward with structured, teacher-generated diagnostic feedback. It draws on the LLM literature on non-numerical feedback (textual critiques, Critique-GRPO) and the Policy Shaping technique from LUFFY to handle the conditioning mismatch when re-injecting feedback-refined rollouts into the GRPO batch. The two-stage SFT pipeline—first infusing driving-domain knowledge via QA pretraining, then jointly training trajectory prediction and feedback-based refinement—repurposes the VLA model as both generator and refiner, a design not present in prior VLA-RL work that treats the policy as a single-stage actor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured failure-diagnosis approach offers a practical path to break the RL plateau in long-tail driving scenarios, and the total-failure reduction (2.73% → 1.08%) provides concrete evidence that targeted feedback outperforms both raw GT injection and rule-based self-refinement. However, the method's ceiling is bounded by the external teacher (Qwen3-VL-32B), and all validation is confined to the non-reactive NAVSIM benchmark with a single base model (InternVL3-8B), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to reactive closed-loop settings, different VLA architectures, or scenarios where the teacher itself lacks domain expertise. A key open question is whether the student can eventually internalize the teacher's diagnostic capability, eliminating the dependency at inference time.
