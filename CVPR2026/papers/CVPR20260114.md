## Problem

Existing shelf-supervised occupancy estimation methods rely on 2D rendering-based supervision (NeRF, 3DGS), which suffers from depth bleeding and geometric inconsistencies because 2D signals cannot precisely capture volumetric extent along viewing rays. While 3D geometry foundation models (e.g., MapAnything, VGGT) now provide metrically consistent 3D geometry from images, they assume static scenes and cannot be naively applied to dynamic multi-camera driving sequences without producing ghosting artifacts and sparse, noisy labels. The paper addresses how to convert these foundation model outputs into clean, dense 3D voxel pseudo-labels suitable for direct supervision of occupancy networks without LiDAR.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes short temporal sequences of multi-view camera images (256×704 resolution, 6 cameras for nuScenes) along with their known intrinsics and extrinsics. It produces metrically-scaled 3D semantic voxel pseudo-labels on a grid spanning [-40m, 40m] in X/Y and [-1m, 5.4m] in Z at 0.4m resolution, plus a per-frame camera visibility mask distinguishing visible free space from unobserved regions. These labels are plug-and-play: any occupancy architecture trained with 3D voxel supervision (COTR, CVT-Occ, STCOcc) can be directly trained on them using standard cross-entropy loss weighted by the visibility mask.

## Method

ShelfOcc generates 3D pseudo-labels through a six-stage pipeline: (1) GroundedSAM produces dense 2D semantic masks using per-class prompts with a sky-grounding trick to suppress false positives; (2) MapAnything predicts metrically-scaled depth maps that are unprojected into 3D point clouds; (3) the scene is split into static and dynamic components using the 2D masks, with only static pixels unprojected for accumulation; (4) static geometry is accumulated across all frames and filtered via ray-based multi-frame confirmation and a minimum point-density threshold (≥4 points per voxel); (5) dynamic objects are reintroduced per-frame at their correct positions; (6) the combined point cloud is voxelized with majority-voting semantics and a visibility mask is generated by ray casting. The key insight is that temporal accumulation of filtered static geometry yields dense, consistent 3D supervision, while per-frame dynamic handling avoids motion trails—enabling direct 3D loss computation that eliminates the depth-bleeding inherent to 2D rendering-based approaches.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes validation set, ShelfOcc+STCOcc achieves 22.87 mIoU and 56.14 geometric IoU, surpassing the previous best shelf-supervised method (GaussianFlowOcc, 17.08 mIoU / 46.91 IoU) by +5.79 mIoU and +9.23 IoU, corresponding to relative gains of 34% and 20% respectively. The plug-and-play nature is confirmed across three architectures: ShelfOcc+COTR reaches 18.65 mIoU and ShelfOcc+CVT-Occ reaches 19.21 mIoU, all exceeding prior shelf-supervised baselines. On the RayIoU metric, ShelfOcc+STCOcc achieves 19.97 mRayIoU (vs. 16.47 for GaussianFlowOcc), with consistent improvements at all depth ranges (1m, 2m, 4m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShelfOcc outperforms all seven prior shelf-supervised baselines on mIoU across all three tested architectures. The largest gap is against GaussianFlowOcc (the previous state-of-the-art shelf-supervised method), where ShelfOcc+STCOcc gains +5.79 mIoU (22.87 vs. 17.08) and +9.23 geometric IoU (56.14 vs. 46.91). Against SfmOcc (17.70 mIoU, 57.70 IoU), ShelfOcc+STCOcc wins on mIoU by +5.17 but trails slightly on geometric IoU (56.14 vs. 57.70), though SfmOcc does not report per-class breakdowns. The 2D rendering-based methods (SelfOcc, OccNeRF, GaussianOcc, GaussTR) are all surpassed by a wide margin of 5–12 mIoU points. On the RayIoU metric, ShelfOcc+STCOcc (19.97 mRayIoU) also beats GaussianFlowOcc (16.47) and GaussianOcc (11.85) consistently across all depth ranges, confirming that the 3D supervision advantage holds under depth-consistent evaluation as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShelfOcc departs from the dominant 2D rendering-based supervision paradigm in shelf-supervised occupancy estimation (SelfOcc, OccNeRF, GaussianOcc, GaussianFlowOcc), which projects 3D scene representations back to image space via NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting and suffers from depth bleeding. Instead, it repurposes off-the-shelf 3D geometry foundation models (MapAnything) and 2D semantic segmentation models (GroundedSAM) to generate native 3D voxel pseudo-labels, shifting the supervision signal from 2D photometric/semantic losses to direct 3D cross-entropy in voxel space. This is conceptually closer to fully supervised training but without LiDAR or manual 3D annotations, and it is orthogonal to architectural choices, enabling plug-and-play integration with any existing occupancy network (COTR, CVT-Occ, STCOcc).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that supervision quality (native 3D pseudo-labels) can close much of the gap to LiDAR-supervised methods without modifying network architectures, ShelfOcc reframes the field's focus from architectural innovation to data-centric supervision strategies for LiDAR-free occupancy estimation. The plug-and-play design makes it immediately applicable to a wide range of existing and future occupancy models. Open questions remain around long-tail/rare classes where foundation models produce noisy semantics, the limited completeness of dynamic object geometry (reconstructed from single frames), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the Occ3D-nuScenes benchmark to other driving datasets or sensor configurations.
