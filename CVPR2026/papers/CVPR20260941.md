## Problem

Existing long-horizon trajectory predictors suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) one-shot decoders apply homogeneous attention across all timesteps, causing motion-inertia-driven near-future signals and semantics-driven distant-future signals to compete under limited representational capacity, while autoregressive alternatives introduce error accumulation; (2) most methods supervise only positions and derive headings via post-hoc interpolation, producing kinematically implausible trajectories with abrupt heading changes that degrade long-horizon accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260941/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes vectorized map elements (lane boundaries, traffic signs) and agent historical states (position, orientation over past timesteps) in a scene-centric coordinate frame, and outputs K=6 multimodal future trajectories parameterized as a mixture of Laplace distributions with associated probability scores. On Argoverse 2 it predicts 6 s of future from 5 s of history, while on WOMD it predicts 8 s from only 1.1 s of history. Unlike dense predictors that generate 64+ candidate modes and apply NMS to select 6, NDPNet directly predicts exactly 6 modes in a single forward pass without ensemble or post-hoc filtering.

## Method

NDPNet decouples trajectory prediction into a near-future branch (0–2 s, motion-inertia-driven) and a distant-future branch (2–6/8 s, semantics-driven), each using independent DETR-style trajectory queries with separate cross-attention to agent and map encodings. A Near-to-Distant Temporal Bridging module lifts the near-future patch via Fourier embedding, condenses it with a GRU encoder, and injects it as cross-attention context into the distant-future queries, providing spatio-temporal priors without full joint modeling. A coarse-to-fine refinement stage fuses both patches along time, applies Fourier embedding and an RNN, then predicts relative offsets via an MLP head. The key training innovation is the Motion-Aware Coherence (MAC) loss, which uses ground-truth velocity and angular velocity to construct virtual kinematically-constrained waypoints via forward Euler integration, penalizing both positional and heading deviations between predicted and constrained points, with an agent-type mask restricting the constraint to non-holonomic (vehicle) agents.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) averaged over 3s, 5s, and 8s horizons, NDPNet achieves minFDE₆ = 1.0319 and minADE₆ = 0.5160 without ensemble or NMS post-processing, ranking 1st in both metrics and outperforming the best Dense+NMS competitor (EDA: 1.1702 / 0.5718) by a substantial margin. On the challenging 8s long-horizon setting, NDPNet is the first method to break the sub-1.75 minFDE₆ barrier (1.7481), surpassing MTR v3 (1.8500) by over 0.1 m. On Argoverse 2, NDPNet achieves minFDE₆ = 1.17 and b-minFDE₆ = 1.83 without ensemble, and further improves to 1.09 / 1.71 with 6-model ensemble, setting new SOTA across all reported metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WOMD (Table 3), NDPNet decisively outperforms all seven baselines in both minFDE₆ and minADE₆ without any post-hoc filtering or ensemble: it beats the strongest Dense+NMS method (EDA, 1.1702/0.5718) by 0.14 in minFDE₆ and 0.06 in minADE₆, and surpasses the best non-dense method (HPTR, 1.1393/0.5565) by 0.11/0.04. On the 8s WOMD leaderboard (Table 2), NDPNet (minFDE₆ = 1.7481) outperforms MTR v3 (1.8500), Wayformer (1.9470), ModeSeq (2.0072), and RMP (1.9745) by 0.10–0.26 m, all without ensemble. On Argoverse 2 (Table 1), the single-model NDPNet (minFDE₆ = 1.17, b-minFDE₆ = 1.83) beats QCNet (1.29/1.91), GANet (1.34/1.96), and HPTR (1.43/2.03); with 6-model ensemble it further improves to 1.09/1.71, surpassing DeMo (1.11/1.73) and QCNet-ensemble (1.19/1.78). The MAC loss ablation (Table 4) confirms plug-and-play gains: adding it to HiVT reduces minFDE₆ from 1.98→1.94 and minAHE₆ from 0.14→0.07, while on QCNet it drops minAHE₆ from 0.10→0.04, validating its generalizability across agent-centric, scene-centric, and query-centric architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

NDPNet builds directly on the query-centric encoder-decoder framework of QCNet (CVPR 2023) for scene encoding and trajectory query initialization, and adopts the Laplace-distribution regression parameterization from HiVT (CVPR 2022). It departs from the dominant one-shot decoding paradigm (MTR, QCNet, SceneTransformer) by splitting trajectory generation into a near-future branch (motion-inertia-driven) and a distant-future branch (semantics-driven), connected by a GRU-based temporal bridging module. It also moves beyond prior kinematic-feasibility strategies—post-hoc NMS filtering or independent heading heads—by introducing a differentiable motion-aware coherence loss that couples position regression to ground-truth velocity and angular velocity during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-stage near-to-distant decomposition addresses a genuine architectural gap: one-shot predictors conflate two distinct dynamical regimes under shared attention, while autoregressive predictors accumulate error. The MAC loss is validated as plug-and-play across three architecturally diverse baselines (agent-centric, scene-centric, query-centric), suggesting broad adoptability. Open questions include whether the fixed 2-second near-future window generalizes to even longer horizons or different driving regimes, whether the GRU-based temporal bridging is optimal compared to attention-based alternatives, and whether the kinematic constraints (currently masked to nonholonomic agents) can be extended to more general motion models. The paper does not explore real-time deployment constraints beyond a single-GPU FPS measurement.
