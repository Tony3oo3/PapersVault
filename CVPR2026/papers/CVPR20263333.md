## Problem

Existing visual explanation methods for object detectors (e.g., ODAM, SSGrad-CAM++, D-RISE) evaluate pixel contributions independently, overlooking the collective influence of multiple pixels on detection. This limitation causes explanations to miss compositional cues (e.g., background context that jointly determines bounding-box size) and to capture spurious correlations, particularly in biased models and failure cases. The paper asks how to explicitly account for both individual and collective pixel contributions when explaining object detector outputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a trained object detector (DETR, Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, or Grounding DINO) and divides the image into a grid of patches. It produces a saliency heat map and an ordered set of identified patches, with separate explanations for bounding-box localization (via IoU) and class prediction (via cosine similarity of probability vectors). The reward function combines both signals, so the explanation reflects the joint detection score rather than class confidence alone.

## Method

VX-CODE formulates explanation as a greedy patch selection problem where, at each step, r patches are chosen to maximize a detection reward over the growing (insertion) or shrinking (deletion) patch set. The key insight is that the greedy objective naturally decomposes into a sum of self-context Shapley values and self-context interactions, so the selection process inherently accounts for collective pixel contributions without requiring exponential computation. To make the method tractable for r≥2, two efficiency modules are introduced: patch selection restricts combination candidates to the top-m patches by single-patch reward impact, and step restriction limits the combination process to the first γn patches before falling back to individual selection. The paper further introduces the π*-index, a new game-theoretic index from a sequential coalitional game perspective that satisfies standard coalitional game axioms and provides a theoretical interpretation of the greedy strategy as an approximation to the optimal player ordering.

## Results

On MS-COCO with DETR, VX-CODE (r=3) achieves an overall AUC of 0.857 (insertion 0.909, deletion 0.052), outperforming the best baseline SSGrad-CAM++ (OA 0.757) by 10 percentage points. On PASCAL VOC with Faster R-CNN, VX-CODE (r=3) reaches OA 0.787 versus 0.750 for SSGrad-CAM++. The paper reports that insertion and deletion AUC scores improve by up to approximately 19% over state-of-the-art methods, with VX-CODE surpassing the second-best method by 20% in insertion and 50% in deletion at the 10% area mark on MS-COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VX-CODE outperforms all seven baselines across every detector–dataset combination on the overall AUC metric. The closest competitor is SSGrad-CAM++, which trails VX-CODE (r=3) by 10.0 points on MS-COCO/DETR (0.757 vs. 0.857), 8.1 points on MS-COCO/Faster R-CNN (0.774 vs. 0.855), 12.4 points on PASCAL VOC/DETR (0.647 vs. 0.771), and 3.7 points on PASCAL VOC/Faster R-CNN (0.750 vs. 0.787). Gradient-based methods (Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, ODAM) and perturbation-based D-RISE all fall well below VX-CODE, with Grad-CAM being the weakest baseline (OA 0.224–0.408). On failure cases (Table 2), VX-CODE (r=3) achieves insertion AUC 0.738 for misclassification and 0.787 for mislocalization, beating SSGrad-CAM++ (0.647 and 0.766) by 9.1 and 2.1 points respectively. On the object-level foundation model Grounding DINO (Table 4), VX-CODE (r=1) surpasses VPS by 1.6 points in insertion (0.962 vs. 0.946) and 8.9 points in deletion (0.211 vs. 0.300), yielding an overall AUC of 0.751 versus 0.646.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the perturbation-based explanation paradigm for object detectors (D-RISE, BSED, FSOD) by incorporating game-theoretic interaction indices alongside Shapley values, building directly on the self-context Shapley value and interaction framework introduced by Sumiyasu et al. (MoXI, CVPR 2024) for classification. It departs from prior gradient-based (ODAM, SSGrad-CAM++) and perturbation-based (D-RISE) methods that evaluate pixel contributions independently, instead modeling collective pixel synergies through a greedy patch-selection strategy. The newly introduced π*-index provides a sequential coalitional game interpretation that formally connects the greedy procedure to an NP-hard optimization, a theoretical framing absent from earlier patch-selection approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical deployments of object detectors (autonomous driving, medical imaging), the ability to identify not just dominant features but also assistive or contextual cues (e.g., background, secondary body parts) is essential for diagnosing biases and failure modes that individual-contribution methods systematically miss. The consistent ~19% AUC improvement across architectures (DETR, Faster R-CNN, Grounding DINO, RetinaNet) and settings (bias models, misclassification, mislocalization) strengthens the case that collective contributions are a first-order factor in detection explanations. Open questions include whether the greedy approximation to π* can be tightened with theoretical guarantees, how the method scales to very high-resolution or video inputs, and whether the interaction-based perspective transfers to other multi-output tasks such as segmentation or tracking.
