## Problem

Internet photo collections follow an extreme long-tailed distribution: a small fraction of famous landmarks are densely photographed and easily reconstructed, while the vast majority of real-world sites are represented by sparse, noisy, weakly-connected image sets on which both classical SfM pipelines and state-of-the-art feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (e.g., DUSt3R, VGGT, π³) fail to recover consistent geometry. The core challenge is that reliable 3D supervision cannot be directly obtained from these long-tail scenes, creating a chicken-and-egg problem for training robust 3D foundation models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse RGB image sets (2–24 views) sampled from Internet photo collections, where views exhibit wide baselines, partial or zero overlap, and multiple disconnected scene components—mimicking the view-graph statistics of real long-tail scenes. Outputs are affine-invariant camera poses and scale-invariant local 3D point maps (following π³'s formulation), with dense depth maps available as intermediate supervision. Test scenes are categorized into 'easy' (D=5, N_cc=1) and 'hard' (D=24, N_cc=4) subsets based on greedy search depth and number of connected components.

## Method

The paper introduces MegaDepth-X (MD-X), a 1,865-scene, 466k-image dataset built from well-reconstructed MegaScenes scenes, with two key quality improvements: (1) filtering out dynamic-content and doppelganger failures via MASt3R-SfM plus a doppelganger classifier and manual verification, and (2) a monocular depth-guided filtering step (using MoGe2 predictions) that removes depth-bleeding artifacts and transient objects beyond MegaDepth's original refinement pipeline. The second contribution is a sparsity-aware sampling strategy that partitions the SfM view graph into communities via Louvain detection, constructs an approximate Steiner tree linking one representative view per community, and performs greedy view sampling (prioritizing community novelty and spatial distance) to produce training batches with wide baselines and partial overlap. Fine-tuning π³ and VGGT (freezing point-cloud and camera decoders, updating only Alternating-Attention modules) with a MIXED schedule of dense and sparse batches yields substantially improved robustness on long-tail and doppelganger scenes while preserving generalization on standard benchmarks.

## Results

On the proposed MegaDepth-X benchmark, fine-tuning yields substantial gains on sparse (hard) scenes: π³-FT improves RRA@5 from 75.31 to 86.40 and AUC@5 from 36.93 to 47.93, while halving the mean rotation error (MRE 12.21° → 5.72°). VGGT-FT shows similar improvements (RRA@5 70.98 → 81.07, AUC@5 29.10 → 41.49, MRE 13.20° → 7.22°). On easier (dense) scenes, gains are more modest (e.g., π³-FT RRA@5 88.97 → 95.64). On standard benchmarks (RealEstate10K, CO3Dv2, DTU, 7-Scenes, NRGBD), fine-tuned models maintain comparable accuracy to their pretrained counterparts, confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper fine-tunes two feed-forward 3D foundation models—π³ and VGGT—on the proposed MegaDepth-X dataset with sparsity-aware sampling, and compares each fine-tuned variant against its pretrained baseline. On the hard (sparse) MegaDepth-X split, π³-FT outperforms π³ by +11.1 points in RRA@5 (86.40 vs. 75.31) and +11.0 in AUC@5 (47.93 vs. 36.93), while reducing MRE by more than half (5.72° vs. 12.21°). VGGT-FT similarly beats VGGT by +10.1 in RRA@5 (81.07 vs. 70.98) and +12.4 in AUC@5 (41.49 vs. 29.10). The gains are larger on hard scenes than on easy ones, confirming that the sparsity-aware training specifically targets the long-tail regime. On standard benchmarks (RealEstate10K, CO3Dv2, DTU, 7-Scenes, NRGBD), both fine-tuned models remain within 1–2 points of their pretrained counterparts, with VGGT-FT actually improving on RealEstate10K (AUC@5 38.09 → 48.23) and CO3Dv2 (RRA@5 96.97 → 97.11), indicating that long-tail robustness does not come at the cost of generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm (π³, VGGT, DUSt3R, MASt3R) by addressing a distributional gap: these models are trained on dense, well-conditioned captures but fail on the sparse, noisy, long-tail Internet photo collections that dominate real-world imagery. Conceptually, it draws an analogy to autonomous driving's rare-event simulation, proposing to subsample well-reconstructed head scenes to emulate long-tail camera distributions rather than seeking unreliable ground truth from tail scenes themselves. The dataset (MegaDepth-X) is a direct 8× scale and quality extension of MegaDepth, built from MegaScenes with additional doppelganger disambiguation and monocular-depth-guided filtering beyond MegaDepth's original pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes the long-tail regime as a distinct and under-addressed challenge for 3D foundation models, providing both a benchmark (MD-X test set) and a practical training recipe (sparsity-aware sampling) that improve robustness without sacrificing generalization to standard benchmarks. A key open question is whether this approach can bootstrap beyond landmark-scale scenes to everyday objects and indoor environments, where the long-tail problem is even more severe and ground truth is even harder to obtain. The finding that unfiltered (DIRTY) data degrades performance below pretrained baselines underscores that data curation quality, not just quantity, is critical for robust 3D learning from Internet photos.
