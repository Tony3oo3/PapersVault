## Problem

Reward models for omni models that handle interleaved text and image sequences remain largely unexplored, with no existing benchmark to measure their quality. Unlike text-only or image-text-to-text settings, omni models generate arbitrary sequences of texts and images, making automatic verification difficult, requiring diverse task coverage, and demanding high-quality responses from models whose capabilities are not always strong enough to produce informative comparisons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260938/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each MMRB2 data point consists of a task prompt and two candidate responses, where responses can be a single image (text-to-image, editing), an interleaved text-image sequence (interleaved generation), or a text response optionally accompanied by intermediate sketchpad images (multimodal reasoning). The reward model outputs a binary preference (which response is better), evaluated via positional consistent dual scoring where each pair is judged in both forward and reverse orderings to mitigate position bias. The benchmark contains 1,000 expert-annotated preference pairs per subtask, drawn from 23 models and agents across 21 source tasks, with >90% inter-annotator agreement.

## Method

MMRB2 is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) stratified sampling of prompts from 21 existing and newly created tasks near the capability boundary of frontier models; (2) response generation from 7–11 state-of-the-art models per prompt, including specialized agents (e.g., Visual Sketchpad) for tasks where even the best models often fail; and (3) an ensemble filtering strategy that uses nine multimodal judges (GPT-5, GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Flash/Pro, Gemma-3-27B/12B/4B, Qwen-2.5-VL-7B) to discard easy pairs where ≥90% of judge evaluations agree, followed by three independent human annotators rating each remaining pair on a 7-point Likert scale with additional quality filters (removing high-disagreement, ambiguous, and tie pairs). The paper then evaluates MLLM-as-a-judge approaches, CLIP-based and preference-trained reward models, and demonstrates that MMRB2 scores strongly correlate (>0.8 Pearson's r) with downstream best-of-N task performance on GenAI-Bench, GEdit-Bench, ISG-Bench, and EMMA.

## Results

On MMRB2, Gemini 3 Pro achieves the highest judge-human agreement at 76.3% average across four tasks (74.4% text-to-image, 74.9% image editing, 76.4% interleaved generation, 79.5% multimodal reasoning), compared to >90% for human annotators. GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro reach 72.2% and 70.9% average, while the best open-source model Qwen3-VL-32B achieves 64.6%. The commonly used GPT-4o attains only 59.7% average, and task-specific metrics like CLIPScore (51.0%) and ImageReward (54.0%) fall well below MLLM-as-a-judge baselines on text-to-image evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among MLLM-as-a-judge baselines, Gemini 3 Pro dominates all four tasks, outperforming GPT-5 by 4.1 points on average (76.3% vs. 72.2%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro by 5.4 points (76.3% vs. 70.9%). The best open-source model, Qwen3-VL-32B (64.6% avg), trails Gemini 3 Pro by 11.7 points but narrows the gap on interleaved generation (70.5% vs. 76.4%). GPT-4o, widely used as an evaluator in prior benchmarks, achieves only 59.7% average—below even Qwen3-VL-32B—confirming it is no longer suitable for frontier multimodal evaluation. Among task-specific metrics, CLIP-based evaluators (CLIPScore 51.0%, ImageReward 54.0%, HPSv2 54.7%, PickScore 58.6%) and Qwen2.5-VL-based evaluators (VQAScore 58.3%, HPSv3 60.2%) all underperform the Qwen3-VL-32B judge (64.1%) on text-to-image. EditReward (67.2%) is the one exception, slightly exceeding Qwen3-VL-32B (66.4%) on image editing, while UnifiedReward (59.8% T2I, 55.1% reasoning) remains well below frontier MLLMs. Overall, preference-trained reward models show consistent gains over their untrained backbones (e.g., +9.4% for EditReward over Qwen2.5-VL-7B on editing) but are outperformed by simply prompting larger MLLMs, suggesting distribution shift from training on earlier-generation model outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Multimodal RewardBench (MMRB1), which evaluated reward models on image-text-to-text understanding tasks, to the substantially harder setting of omni models that generate and understand interleaved text-image sequences. It also builds on the broader reward modeling literature for visual generation (ImageReward, HPSv2/v3, PickScore, EditReward, UnifiedReward) and the MLLM-as-a-judge paradigm, but departs from prior work by covering four subtasks—text-to-image, image editing, interleaved generation, and multimodal reasoning—within a single unified benchmark, rather than evaluating each in isolation. The ensemble filtering strategy for curating challenging preference pairs and the positional-consistent dual evaluation protocol represent methodological contributions beyond simply assembling existing data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMRB2 is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate reward models across the full omni-model capability spectrum, and its finding that GPT-4o—the de facto evaluator in many existing benchmarks—achieves only 51–65% accuracy challenges a widely held assumption about its reliability for multimodal evaluation. The revealed modality bias (judges strongly prefer image-containing responses in reasoning tasks, with gaps up to 49.3%) and the finding that test-time scaling yields only 0.8–1.2% gains for multimodal rewards (versus substantial gains in text-only settings) point to concrete, underexplored failure modes. Open questions include how to design reward models that handle same-model fine-grained comparisons, how to eliminate modality bias in reasoning evaluation, and whether alternative scaling strategies beyond repeated sampling can close the remaining 20–26% gap to human agreement.
