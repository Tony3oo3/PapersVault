## Problem

Open-sourcing instruction-guided image editing models surrenders control over their capabilities to anyone who downloads the weights, and existing protection methods (watermarking, fingerprinting) are reactive—they verify ownership after generation but leave the model fully functional for unauthorized users. The paper tackles how to bake access control directly into model weights so that the model is unusable without a visual trigger in the input, while retaining full editing quality for authorized use, without destabilizing diffusion training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 512×512 RGB input image and a text editing instruction, where the image may contain a small visible spatial key (64 or 128 pixel patch) at a designated location. When the correct key is present, the model outputs a high-quality edited image; when the key is absent, misaligned, corrupted, or replaced, it outputs a degraded result (a fixed authorization-request image, a blurred version, or a noisy version depending on the degraded teacher strategy). The key is a visible spatial patch rather than a text password, making it harder to strip from outputs and enabling provenance signaling to end viewers.

## Method

VisiLock introduces Dual Score Distillation, a two-teacher framework that decouples authorized and unauthorized behaviors to avoid the gradient conflicts that destabilize naive multi-task fine-tuning or collapse contrastive approaches like FMLock. A degraded teacher (fine-tuned InstructPix2Pix to output a fixed target, blur, or noise) defines the locked behavior, while the original InstructPix2Pix teacher preserves genuine editing quality. The student is initialized from the degraded teacher so the released checkpoint begins in a locked state, then selectively regains editing ability only for key-embedded inputs via distillation from the original teacher. A Margin Repulsion Loss (hinge on the L2 distance between authorized and unauthorized noise predictions) provides 1–2% additional separation without overwhelming the distillation objectives. Training runs for 2,000 steps at 512×512 with AdamW (lr=1e-5), using five key configurations per sample (authorized, no-key, misaligned, corrupted, random) sharing the same noise and timestep.

## Results

On MagicBrush, the VisiLock model (fixed-target degraded teacher) maintains strong authorized editing quality with CLIP-I of 0.821 and DINO of 0.726, while unauthorized attempts degrade severely: DINO drops 90% (0.726 → 0.072), CLIP-I drops 41% (0.821 → 0.481), and entropy drops 45% (14.93 → 8.21). The lock remains robust across trigger sizes (16–128 px) with consistent degradation, and adversarial self-distillation fine-tuning for 500 steps only partially closes the authorized–unauthorized gap without fully recovering editing capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external locked-model baselines in a head-to-head table; instead it contrasts its Dual Score Distillation training strategy against two alternatives. Naive mixed-batch fine-tuning (Eq. 6) causes gradient conflicts that destabilize training and destroy both authorized and unauthorized behaviors. FMLock's contrastive objective (Eq. 7) collapses both modes after ~200 steps even at λ=0.01 (Fig. 4), rendering the model unusable in either state. Among degraded-teacher strategies, the fixed-target approach achieves the strongest lock (90% DINO drop) versus blur (55% DINO drop) and noise (21% DINO drop), at a small cost to authorized quality (CLIP-I 0.821 vs. 0.843/0.846). For initialization, starting from the degraded teacher is critical: random initialization fails to converge, and initializing from clean InstructPix2Pix produces weak locks that cannot output the fixed target image.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-locking line of work (DeepLock, NN-Lock, FMLock, password-locked LLMs) to instruction-guided diffusion-based image editing, replacing text-based or parameter-encryption triggers with a visible spatial key embedded in the input image. It builds directly on InstructPix2Pix's latent diffusion architecture but departs from both naive multi-task fine-tuning and FMLock's contrastive objective, which the authors show destabilize the denoising manifold. The core methodological contribution—Dual Score Distillation with a degraded teacher and an original teacher—repurposes knowledge distillation as a mechanism for decoupling two conflicting behaviors within a single diffusion model, a framing not present in prior locking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete commercial tension: model providers want to open-source weights for research acceleration but need to preserve a paid tier, and existing watermarking/fingerprinting methods are purely reactive. The 90% DINO degradation for unauthorized access and the plateau effect under adversarial fine-tuning suggest a practical path toward controlled distribution of generative models. However, the evaluation is limited to InstructPix2Pix at 512×512, the visible key occludes part of the input, and the adversarial attack still partially recovers capability—so the protection is a cost-increase rather than a hard guarantee. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to modern architectures (Flux Kontext, Qwen Image), whether multi-key hierarchical locking is feasible, and how robust the lock is to adaptive attacks beyond the simple self-distillation tested here.
