## Problem

Existing document and scene text understanding benchmarks are heavily skewed toward high-resource languages (English, Chinese) and evaluate Document Parsing and Text-Centric VQA in isolation, leaving a critical gap for the linguistically diverse Southeast Asian region with its mix of Latin, Brahmic, Arabic-based, and logographic scripts. Moreover, prior multilingual annotation strategies that rely on OCR or machine translation introduce visual-semantic misalignment, making it difficult to assess whether models truly understand the visible text or merely decode a translated proxy.

## Input / Output

For Document Parsing, the input is a single document page image (PDF render) and the output is a hierarchical structured representation with page-level attributes (language, layout style, noise indicators), 243,643 block-level annotations across 10 semantic categories with reading-order labels, and line-level text, table (HTML with merged-cell attributes), and formula (LaTeX) content. For TEC-VQA, the input is a scene image containing naturally embedded multilingual text paired with a natural-language question, and the output is a free-form text answer evaluated by a containment criterion against one or more reference answers. The benchmark spans 15,234 document pages across 9 types and 1,839 scene images with 7,496 QA pairs covering five reasoning dimensions.

## Method

SEA-Vision's core technical contribution is a hybrid annotation pipeline that makes large-scale multilingual, multi-task labeling feasible. For Document Parsing, a four-stage process combines layout detection with MLLM-based language/page-type classification, a rule-based scoring function (weighted by block count, text-area ratio, element-type diversity, and presence of tables/figures) for language- and page-type-aware sample selection, multimodal region correction (MLLM OCR refinement, UniMERNet formula re-parsing, two-step table structure recognition with cell-level MLLM correction), and final native-speaker human verification. For TEC-VQA, the pipeline avoids visual-textual misalignment by re-rendering translated text directly into the image via font-matched inpainting, then uses an MLLM to generate English QA pairs, translates questions into Chinese for bilingual consistency checking via a separate judging model, back-translates into the visible text language, and applies native-speaker verification to remove hallucinations and assign capability labels. Evaluation follows OmniDocBench's four-component protocol (NED for text, TEDS+NED for tables, CDM+NED+BLEU for formulas, NED for reading order) for Document Parsing and zero-shot accuracy for TEC-VQA, enabling unified cross-task, cross-script comparison of pipeline, expert, and general MLLM paradigms.

## Results

On Document Parsing (NED↓, lower is better), Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the best average NED of 0.159 across 11 languages, outperforming the best Expert Model (dots.ocr, 0.186) and the best Pipeline Model (PaddleOCR-VL, 0.238). On TEC-VQA (accuracy↑), Qwen3-VL-32B attains the highest average accuracy of 40.14%, followed by Gemini 2.5 Pro (39.49%), while the overall average across all 12 evaluated MLLMs is only 32.36%. The paper reports 3–5× higher NED for Document Parsing and 5–7× lower accuracy for TEC-VQA on low-resource SEA scripts (Lao, Khmer, Burmese) compared to high-resource English and Chinese.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, SEA-Vision evaluates 13 document-parsing models and 12 MLLMs rather than proposing a new method. For Document Parsing, Gemini 2.5 Pro (avg NED 0.159) outperforms all Expert Models (best: dots.ocr at 0.186) and Pipeline Models (best: PaddleOCR-VL at 0.238), and is particularly strong on low-resource scripts like Khmer (0.278) and Lao (0.195). However, PaddleOCR-VL still wins on the two high-resource languages—English (0.108) and Chinese (0.131)—showing that specialized pipelines retain an edge where language-specific data is abundant. For TEC-VQA, Qwen3-VL-32B leads with 40.14% average accuracy, narrowly ahead of Gemini 2.5 Pro (39.49%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (37.43%), while smaller models like DeepSeek-VL2-3B (23.01%) and MiniCPM-V-2.6 (26.16%) lag significantly. The most striking comparison is the language gap: English accuracy reaches 62.85% while Burmese drops to 8.17% (a ~7.7× gap), and Khmer is only 11.26%, confirming that no current model handles low-resource SEA scripts robustly. Across page types, Expert Models (dots.ocr) dominate structured layouts like Academic Paper (0.141) and Book (0.160), whereas General Models (Gemini 2.5 Pro) excel on complex layouts like Magazine (0.123) and Newspaper (0.116).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the document parsing evaluation protocol established by OmniDocBench (NED, TEDS, CDM metrics across text, tables, formulas, and reading order) and the TEC-VQA task from prior multilingual VQA benchmarks (MTVQA, M3Exam) into a unified framework covering 11 Southeast Asian languages, including seven low-resource scripts (Lao, Khmer, Burmese, Thai, Filipino, Malay, Vietnamese) that no prior benchmark jointly addresses. It departs from prior multilingual VQA datasets that rely on text-only translation of QA pairs by introducing a re-rendering pipeline that preserves visual-textual alignment, and from document parsing benchmarks restricted to English/Chinese by incorporating diverse real-world document types (exams, newspapers, receipts, slides). The annotation methodology itself is a departure, combining MLLM-assisted labeling with rule-based scoring and native-speaker verification to make large-scale multilingual annotation feasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark 3–5× NED degradation for document parsing and 5–7× accuracy drop for TEC-VQA on low-resource SEA scripts compared to English/Chinese, quantifying a gap that prior high-resource-only benchmarks could not expose. The finding that general-purpose MLLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro) outperform specialized pipeline and expert models on low-resource languages, while the reverse holds for English/Chinese, suggests that current specialized systems overfit to high-resource scripts and that cross-lingual generalization in MLLMs is the more promising path forward. Open questions include whether targeted fine-tuning on SEA scripts can close the gap, how to handle mixed-script pages common in the region, and whether the re-rendering annotation strategy introduces subtle biases that affect evaluation validity.
