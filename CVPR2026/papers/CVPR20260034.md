## Problem

Existing controllable protein generation models either restrict conditioning to a single modality or rely on fragmented architectures with modality-specific encoders and adapters, which hinders cross-modal interaction, limits generation quality, and makes incorporating new biological conditions require redesigning or retraining the backbone. The paper addresses the absence of a composable, extensible multimodal conditioning framework that can flexibly integrate heterogeneous conditions (structure, function, ligand, text) within a single generative process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any subset of heterogeneous biological conditions—3D backbone geometry (encoded by a frozen GVP-Transformer), ligand molecular graphs (frozen SchNet), functional annotations such as GO terms, IPR domains, and EC numbers (frozen ProtBERT), and free-text UniProt descriptions (frozen ProtBERT)—and produces a full protein amino acid sequence via absorbing discrete diffusion. All pretrained encoders remain frozen; only lightweight linear projectors, modality indicator tokens, learnable queries, and cross-attention adapters are trainable. Generated sequences are validated post-hoc by ESMFold refolding to assess structural fidelity (scTM, pLDDT) and functional consistency via DeepGOSE, InterProScan, and CLEAN.

## Method

MMCP-GEN builds on the DPLM-650M absorbing discrete diffusion backbone and introduces two key conditioning modules: (i) MMCP-IH, which prepends a trainable modality-indicator token to each modality's projected token sequence to preserve semantic disentanglement and guide attention, and (ii) MMCP-LQ, a learnable-query fusion module where a small set of shared and modality-specific queries jointly self-attend with all condition tokens in a transformer stack, producing a compact fixed-length fused representation injected into DPLM via lightweight cross-attention adapters at selected layers. When structural supervision is available, a joint generation-and-scoring objective (MMCP-GS) adds an InfoNCE contrastive loss aligning the diffusion model's sequence embedding with a structure embedding, enforcing sequence-structure consistency without training an additional generative model. The architecture is explicitly extensible: adding a new modality requires only a pretrained encoder, a linear projector, an indicator token, and a set of modality-specific queries, with the DPLM backbone and all existing encoders kept frozen.

## Results

On the inverse-folding benchmark (Table 2), MMCP-GEN with all conditions and the GS loss achieves 78.66% AAR, 0.912 scTM, and 86.88 pLDDT, surpassing the strongest baseline CFP-GEN (77.01% AAR, 0.887 scTM) by +1.65 pp AAR and +0.025 scTM. On functional annotation (Table 1), MMCP-GEN with all modalities attains the best MRR across GO (0.873), IPR (0.661), and EC (0.927), along with the lowest MMD (0.038/0.047/0.043) and highest AUC (0.799/0.993/0.954). In foldability, novelty, and diversity (Fig. 5), MMCP-GEN sustains pLDDT > 85 for sequences > 800 residues, achieves the lowest pdb-TM (highest novelty) beyond 400 residues, and the lowest inner-TM (~0.24) indicating the broadest structural diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMCP-GEN outperforms all ten baselines across every reported metric. The closest competitor, CFP-GEN [40], is surpassed by +1.65 pp AAR and +0.025 scTM on inverse folding, and by +0.049 MRR / −0.003 MMD on GO, +0.029 MRR / −0.003 MMD on IPR, and +0.025 MRR / −0.002 MMD on EC functional generation. DPLM [34], the backbone model, trails by 11.4 pp AAR on inverse folding and by 0.735 MRR on GO, confirming that the multimodal conditioning mechanism (not the diffusion backbone alone) drives the gains. Among PLM-based baselines, ESM3 [11] and ProGen2 [26] are outperformed on IPR (macro F1 0.977 vs. 0.568/0.587) and EC (AUC 0.954 vs. 0.776/0.665), while single-condition specialists ProteoGAN [17] and ZymCTRL [25] are dominated on their respective annotation spaces. On foldability/novelty/diversity (Fig. 5), MMCP-GEN exceeds EvoDiff [1] by >35 pLDDT and UR50 [30] by ~0.05 TM in novelty beyond 400 residues, while maintaining the lowest inner-TM (~0.24 vs. 0.26–0.28 for DPLM/CFP-GEN), indicating broader structural coverage without mode collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMCP-GEN builds directly on DPLM's absorbing discrete diffusion framework and DPLM-650M backbone, but departs from the modality-specific encoder-adapter paradigm used by DPLM-2 and CFP-GEN, where each new condition requires a dedicated projection head and adapter pair. The key architectural departure is the learnable-query fusion module (MMCP-LQ) combined with modality indicator heads (MMCP-IH), which routes heterogeneous conditions through a shared self-attention stack rather than parallel modality-specific blocks, enabling cross-modal interaction and plug-and-play extensibility. The joint generation-and-scoring objective (MMCP-GS) via InfoNCE alignment is a new loss component not present in prior diffusion-based protein generators, drawing on contrastive learning to enforce sequence-structure consistency without training a separate structure predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The composable conditioning mechanism addresses a practical bottleneck in protein design: current pipelines require architectural redesign or full retraining to incorporate a new biological condition (e.g., a new type of ligand or post-translational modification), whereas MMCP-GEN claims only a lightweight projector and indicator token are needed. However, the extensibility claim is demonstrated primarily by adding modalities already present in the training set rather than truly novel ones, and the evaluation relies on a single self-constructed dataset, limiting generalizability evidence. Open questions include whether the fusion mechanism degrades under many simultaneous modalities, how it handles conflicting conditions, and whether the modest GS-loss gains (≈0.5% AAR) justify the additional structural supervision requirement.
