## Problem

Existing 3D indoor scene texturing methods rely on text prompts that cannot convey fine-grained, instance-level visual attributes (e.g., specific wood grain, fabric weave), and their outputs suffer from blurriness, cross-view artifacts, and baked-in shading that prevent reuse under different lighting. No prior method combines per-instance image-driven control with the pixel-level fidelity needed for production-quality textures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260232/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unwrapped 3D scene mesh (with UV coordinates mapping vertices to texels) decomposed into N object instances, along with N reference images—one per instance—specifying the desired appearance. It produces a single unified 4096×4096 diffuse albedo texture map in UV space; no normal, roughness, or other PBR material channels are generated. Training requires approximately 48 hours on a single RTX A800 GPU across 30,000 iterations with 5,000 spherically distributed viewpoints.

## Method

CustomTex formulates texturing as UV-space optimization and employs a dual-distillation strategy within a Variational Score Distillation (VSD) framework. Semantic-level distillation uses a frozen depth-to-image diffusion model (ControlNet) conditioned on per-instance reference image features extracted via IP-Adapter; a novel instance cross-attention mechanism multiplies each reference's key-value contribution by its corresponding instance mask so that each image's appearance is spatially aligned to the correct object in the rendered view. A lightweight LoRA module is alternately trained (frozen while updating texture θ, then updated while θ is fixed) to capture the multi-modal distribution of the reference imagery. Pixel-level distillation adds a gradient from a frozen super-resolution diffusion model that sharpens high-frequency details while preserving the semantic structure established by the first branch. The texture is represented as a multi-resolution hash grid (InstantNGP) decoded through a cross-attention texture decoder, and the two gradients are combined with a learnable weight λ_SR that is annealed from 0 to 1.2 after the first 5,000 iterations.

## Results

On the image-to-texture task (10 scenes from 3DFRONT), CustomTex achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CLIP-I 0.797, CLIP-FID 106.229 (lower is better), Q-Align IQA 4.469, and Q-Align IAA 3.629. On the text-to-texture task, CustomTex similarly leads with CLIP-T 0.766, IS 3.311, Q-Align IQA 4.252, and Q-Align IAA 3.343. The largest margin over the nearest competitor (SceneTex-IPA) is in CLIP-I (+0.056) and CLIP-FID (−14.9), indicating substantially better distributional alignment with reference images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CustomTex outperforms all five baselines on every metric in both evaluation settings. Against the closest image-to-texture competitor SceneTex-IPA [14], CustomTex gains +0.056 CLIP-I, reduces CLIP-FID by 14.9 points, and improves Q-Align IQA by 0.46, confirming that instance-level cross-attention and dual distillation yield both better semantic alignment and higher visual quality. Paint3D [76] and HY3D-2.1 [64] (both image-to-texture and text-to-texture) trail significantly, with Paint3D scoring 0.694 vs. 0.797 CLIP-I and HY3D-2.1 the lowest at 0.682, reflecting their inability to handle multi-instance reference prompts. In the text-to-texture setting, CustomTex beats SceneTex [14] on CLIP-T (0.766 vs. 0.639) and IS (3.311 vs. 3.009), while also surpassing SyncMVD [45] (CLIP-T 0.712, IS 2.467) and TEXture [58] (CLIP-T 0.557, IS 1.372) by wide margins, demonstrating that image-driven prompts convey fine-grained instance attributes far more effectively than text alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

CustomTex extends the diffusion-based 3D texturing lineage (SceneTex, RoomPainter, InstanceTex) by replacing ambiguous text prompts with instance-specific reference images and introducing a dual-distillation architecture that separates semantic control from pixel-level enhancement. The semantic-level distillation builds on VSD (ProlificDreamer) and ControlNet-style depth conditioning, augmented with an instance cross-attention mechanism that uses IP-Adapter features and per-instance masks to spatially align each reference image with its corresponding object in the rendered view. The pixel-level distillation repurposes a pre-trained super-resolution diffusion model as a fidelity prior, a novel combination not seen in prior texturing work. The texture representation (multi-resolution hash grid + cross-attention decoder) is inherited from InstantNGP and SceneTex respectively, positioning the contribution primarily in the optimization and conditioning strategy rather than the representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling users to specify per-object appearance through reference images rather than text, CustomTex offers a more intuitive and precise workflow for 3D scene appearance editing, addressing a practical gap that text-driven methods cannot fill for fine-grained material attributes (wood grain, fabric weave, wallpaper patterns). The demonstrated reduction in baked-in shading and artifacts makes the output textures more suitable for relighting and production pipelines. Key limitations remain: the 48-hour training time on a single A800 GPU is prohibitive for iterative design workflows, the method only produces diffuse albedo (no normal, roughness, or metallic maps), and it requires pre-unwrapped UV coordinates. Open questions include whether the dual-distillation framework can be accelerated (e.g., via few-step distillation), extended to full PBR material generation, and scaled to larger or outdoor scenes.
