## Problem

Diffusion-based video generation models achieve high visual realism but routinely violate fundamental physical laws such as gravity, inertia, and collision, because their denoising objectives provide no mechanism to enforce physical constraints. This paper asks how to effectively incorporate information from a physical simulator into the video diffusion process to achieve physically consistent generation without retraining the generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260628/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a temporally coherent video in which foreground objects obey real-world physics. Internally, the prompt first seeds a template video (via SD3 for the first frame, then CogVideoX-I2V or HunyuanVideo-I2V), which is lifted into a 4D scene representation; the final output is a video generated by an optical-flow-conditioned diffusion model (Go-with-the-Flow) guided by simulator-rendered motion cues, with the first frame of the template video serving as the starting condition.

## Method

PSIVG is a training-free, inference-time pipeline that closes the loop between a pre-trained video diffusion model and an MPM-based physical simulator. A perception pipeline reconstructs 3D foreground meshes (InstantMesh), 4D background geometry and camera poses (ViPE), and initial object velocities (SuperGlue feature matching), which are used to initialize the simulator with physical properties inferred by a vision-language model (GPT-5). The simulator's rendered RGB, segmentation masks, and pixel-to-pixel correspondences are converted into a hybrid optical-flow field (simulator foreground flow + template background flow) that conditions the Go-with-the-Flow generator via warped noise latents. To fix residual texture flickering during object rotation, the authors introduce Test-Time Texture-Consistency Optimization (TTCO), a lightweight test-time procedure that optimizes zero-initialized residual tokens on object-related text embeddings and DiT feature modulations using a pixel-correspondence MSE loss derived from the simulator, localizing adaptation to foreground objects while preserving the background.

## Results

PSIVG achieves the best motion controllability across all baselines, reaching 0.84 SAM mIoU and 0.007 Corr. Pixel MSE, substantially outperforming the next-best controllable method SG-I2V (0.75 mIoU, 0.021 MSE) and text-based PISA-Depth (0.51 mIoU, 0.017 MSE). On general video quality, PSIVG maintains competitive scores (CLIP Text 0.35, Subject Consistency 0.95, Motion Smoothness 0.99) while achieving 0.97 Temporal Flickering. In a user study with 32 participants, PSIVG was preferred as the most physically plausible video in 82.3% of comparisons, far exceeding all baselines (next best: CogVideoX at 7.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSIVG decisively outperforms all seven baseline groups on both motion controllability metrics. Against text-based generators (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, PISA variants), PSIVG improves SAM mIoU by 0.33–0.38 and reduces Corr. Pixel MSE by 42–78%, indicating far more physically consistent trajectories. Among controllable methods, the closest competitor is SG-I2V (0.75 mIoU, 0.021 MSE), which PSIVG surpasses by 0.09 mIoU and 0.014 MSE; MotionClone (0.68 mIoU) and Image Conductor variants (0.55–0.61 mIoU) trail further behind. On general video quality, PSIVG matches or slightly trails PISA-Base/Seg on Temporal Flickering (0.97 vs. 0.99), but the authors note those methods produce near-static videos with poor motion diversity. In the user study, PSIVG dominates with 82.3% preference rate versus 7.2% for CogVideoX and 2.6% for PISA-Seg, confirming that human evaluators perceive substantially greater physical plausibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSIVG builds on optical-flow-conditioned video diffusion (Go-with-the-Flow) and large-scale text-to-video models (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo) as its generative backbone, but departs fundamentally by inserting an MPM-based physical simulator into the generation loop as a physics-aware constraint. It contrasts with prior physics-aware generation work (PhysGen, PhysAnimator, PhysGen3D, WonderPlay) that relies on 2D simulators, user-provided actions, or 3D scene optimization, and with PISA which fine-tunes diffusion models on physics data. The key conceptual departure is the training-free, inference-time integration: a perception pipeline (InstantMesh, ViPE, SuperGlue) lifts a generated template video into 3D/4D assets for the simulator, and the simulator's rendered motion cues (optical flow, segmentation, pixel correspondences) guide the diffusion model without any retraining. The TTCO technique further distinguishes the work by showing that test-time optimization of text-embedding tokens and DiT feature modulations—rather than LoRA or spatial tokens—yields localized texture consistency improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSIVG addresses a widely recognized gap between visual fidelity and physical plausibility in video generation, and does so in a training-free manner that can be applied to any pre-trained video diffusion model, making it broadly applicable. The 82.3% user-study preference and consistent superiority on motion-controllability metrics (SAM mIoU 0.84 vs. 0.75 for the best controllable baseline) suggest a meaningful step toward physically grounded synthetic video for robotics, autonomous driving, and content creation. However, the MPM-based simulator limits applicability to deformable/rigid objects and cannot handle articulated structures, humans, or vehicles; perception errors in 3D mesh reconstruction propagate into simulation; and the method inherits GwtF's difficulties with small or thin objects. Open questions include whether the simulator-in-the-loop paradigm can extend to multi-agent interactions, fluid dynamics, or longer video horizons without compounding perception errors.
