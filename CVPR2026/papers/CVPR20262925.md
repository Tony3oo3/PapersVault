## Problem

When multimodal data (text, video, audio) are integrated into a shared representation space for sentiment analysis, sentiment class centers tend to overlap, blurring fine-grained emotional boundaries. Existing approaches relying on cross-modal attention or multi-level contrastive learning focus primarily on modality interaction rather than separating emotional centers, and they incur high computational costs (e.g., 199M parameters for MCL-MCF). The paper addresses this gap by asking how to explicitly model intra-cluster compactness and inter-cluster separation for fine-grained sentiments without expensive attention mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three unaligned modalities per sample: a text sequence encoded by BERT (using the [CLS] token), a video frame sequence, and an audio feature sequence, each processed by an LSTM to yield a fixed-length vector. All three representations are linearly projected to a common dimension d1 for joint clustering. The output is a continuous sentiment score in [-3, 3], computed as the sum of an MLP-predicted intermediate score (from unimodal representations) and a geometric projection bias derived from the learned metric matrices and cluster centers, scaled by a hyperparameter γ.

## Method

MMRest comprises two modules. The MMC module first aligns all modalities to a shared space, applies k-means clustering, and then learns a global metric M0 plus per-cluster local metrics ΔMi via a hinge loss that pulls same-sentiment pairs together and pushes different-sentiment pairs apart within each cluster's receptive field, augmented by a triplet-style inter-cluster loss enforcing minimum separation between cluster centers. Metric matrices are kept positive semi-definite by eigendecomposition after each Adam step. The PDLF module then computes Mahalanobis distances from each representation to all cluster centers under the respective metric to derive a geometric projection bias (ratio of distance to nearest center over distance between nearest and sub-nearest centers), and adds this bias to an intermediate score produced by Conv1D + MLP over the unimodal representations. The entire model uses only 0.37M parameters—roughly 30% of the lightest Transformer-based baseline—while outperforming all compared methods on Acc-5 and Acc-7.

## Results

On CMU-MOSI, MMRest achieves 56.42% Acc-5, 48.98% Acc-7, 0.683 MAE, and 84.64/86.78% Acc-2, setting new state-of-the-art on all major metrics. On the larger CMU-MOSEI dataset, it reaches 56.52% Acc-5, 54.72% Acc-7, 0.531 MAE, and 83.42/86.88% Acc-2. The model uses only 0.37M parameters—roughly 30% of the lightest Transformer-based baseline (MAG-BERT at 1.22M)—while outperforming all 11 compared methods on accuracy metrics and matching or exceeding them on MAE and correlation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMRest outperforms all 11 baselines on Acc-5 and Acc-7 across both datasets, with the largest margins over MCL-MCF (+1.32% Acc-5, +1.46% Acc-7 on CMU-MOSI) and DEVA (+1.20% Acc-5, +2.46% Acc-7 on CMU-MOSEI). On Acc-2, MMRest surpasses the previous best DEVA by 0.24%/0.49% on CMU-MOSI and 0.16%/0.75% on CMU-MOSEI. The only metric where MMRest does not achieve the absolute best is Pearson correlation on CMU-MOSI, where MCL-MCF edges it by 0.005 (0.807 vs. 0.802); on CMU-MOSEI, MMRest ties DEVA at 0.769. Older fusion-based methods (TFN, LMF, MulT) trail by 10–20+ points on Acc-5, while contrastive-learning-based MCL-MCF, despite using 199.30M parameters (538× more than MMRest's 0.37M), is consistently outperformed on accuracy metrics. The efficiency gap is stark: MMRest uses 4.4 GB GPU memory and 2.46 min training time versus MCL-MCF's 11.1 GB and 4.26 min, while delivering superior accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing multi-metric learning (global + local metric matrices) and k-means clustering—concepts well-established in face recognition and multi-view clustering—into the multimodal sentiment analysis (MSA) domain, where prior work has been dominated by cross-attention Transformers (MulT, MAG-BERT) and multi-level contrastive learning (MCL-MCF, DMD). Rather than building on a single predecessor, it identifies a specific representational pathology (overlapping sentiment class centers) that both fusion-based and representation-learning-based MSA methods share, and addresses it through a clustering-aware metric learning objective with hinge and triplet losses. The PDLF module's geometric projection bias is a novel lightweight alternative to the cross-attention mechanisms that have become standard in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 0.37M-parameter model without any cross-attention or contrastive learning can outperform 199M-parameter contrastive-learning baselines on fine-grained sentiment metrics, challenging the implicit assumption that complex interaction architectures are necessary for MSA. The quantitative overlap metric (Eq. 25) provides a useful diagnostic for representation quality that the field has lacked. However, the approach requires a fixed number of clusters (k) that cannot adapt to dataset size, and the evaluation is limited to two CMU datasets with continuous [-3,3] labels, leaving open whether the clustering-based strategy generalizes to discrete emotion categories, other modalities, or larger-scale benchmarks.
