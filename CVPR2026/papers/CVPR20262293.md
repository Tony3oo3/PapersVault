## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering but relies on brittle, hand-tuned heuristics (Adaptive Density Control) to add, split, and remove Gaussians during optimization. These rules can fail to allocate primitives where the loss demands, leading to poor reconstructions, especially without COLMAP-based initialization. The paper asks whether primitive placement can be governed entirely by gradient descent on a learned probability distribution, eliminating all manual density-control schedules.

## Input / Output

The method takes posed multi-view RGB images with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, requiring no COLMAP point cloud or any other 3D initialization. It produces a set of 3D Gaussians (centers sampled from a learned probability density, attributes from hash grids) that can be rendered in real-time via standard 3DGS rasterization. The scene is handled in a normalized [-1,1]³ cube mapped to unbounded ℝ³ via an L∞-symmetric contraction, and the number of rendered Gaussians is automatically reduced over training as duplicate samples are pruned.

## Method

EGS replaces Lagrangian primitive manipulation with an Eulerian probability density p_θ(μ) over Gaussian centers, optimized end-to-end via stochastic gradient descent. The density is parameterized as a Hashed Probability Pyramid: a 12-level hierarchical grid (resolutions 2³ to 4096³) where each level is a piecewise-constant function normalized within 2×2×2 blocks, with hash-based parameter sharing (budget B=2^18 blocks) to keep memory tractable. Sampling is done hierarchically via inverse transform sampling, made differentiable by chaining fractional-part maps across levels. For gradient estimation, the paper derives an unbiased score-function estimator with control variates that weights each Gaussian's contribution by its individual image impact (I − I_{−i}), computed efficiently via the opacity gradient identity, dramatically reducing variance compared to pathwise autodiff. Gaussian attributes (opacity, color, scale, rotation) are stored in a separate multiresolution hash grid queried at each sampled position, and the full pipeline requires no densification, splitting, or pruning heuristics.

## Results

On mip-NeRF 360 with random initialization, EGS (refined) achieves 28.35 PSNR / 0.84 SSIM / 0.23 LPIPS, outperforming MCMC-Random (28.26 / 0.84 / 0.21) on PSNR and Taming-3DGS-Random (26.24 / 0.76 / 0.30) by a wide margin. On Tanks & Temples (random init), EGS (refined) reaches 24.40 PSNR vs. MCMC-Random's 24.17, and on Deep Blending it scores 29.85 PSNR vs. MCMC-Random's 29.72. Compared to COLMAP-initialized baselines, EGS (refined) is within 0.03 PSNR of MCMC-COLMAP (28.35 vs. 28.38) on mip-NeRF 360, closing most of the gap despite starting from a uniform random distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The two baselines are Taming-3DGS (a proxy for standard 3DGS with a fixed Gaussian budget) and 3DGS-MCMC (a state-of-the-art method that uses MCMC to relocate primitives). Under the fair random-initialization comparison, EGS (refined) beats MCMC-Random on PSNR across all three datasets (28.35 vs. 28.26 on mip-NeRF 360, 24.40 vs. 24.17 on T&T, 29.85 vs. 29.72 on Deep Blending), though MCMC-Random retains a slight LPIPS edge on mip-NeRF 360 (0.21 vs. 0.23). EGS (refined) substantially outperforms Taming-3DGS-Random on every dataset, with the largest gap on mip-NeRF 360 (28.35 vs. 26.24 PSNR, a 2.1 dB improvement). Against COLMAP-initialized baselines, EGS (refined) is nearly on par with MCMC-COLMAP (28.35 vs. 28.38 PSNR on mip-NeRF 360) and slightly ahead of Taming-3DGS-COLMAP (28.35 vs. 28.30), demonstrating that the probabilistic sampling-based training recovers most of the quality that SfM initialization provides without any heuristic densification.

## Relation to Prior Work

EGS positions itself as an Eulerian reformulation of 3D Gaussian Splatting, contrasting with the Lagrangian primitive-tracking approach of 3DGS and its variants (Taming-3DGS, 3DGS-MCMC) that rely on hand-tuned density control heuristics. It builds directly on 3DGS rasterization for rendering, Instant-NGP-style hash encoding for storing Gaussian attributes, and the contraction function from mip-NeRF 360 for unbounded scenes, but introduces a novel hashed probability pyramid as a memory-efficient, globally-normalized volumetric distribution and a control-variate score-function estimator for stable gradient-based optimization of that distribution. Conceptually, it bridges NeRF's continuous-field optimization with 3DGS's discrete-primitive rendering, and distinguishes itself from INPC (which uses octree-based subdivision) and Lagrangian Hashing (which redistributes capacity by error rather than learning a normalized spatial probability).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that all heuristic density-control machinery (splitting, cloning, pruning, MCMC rescheduling) can be replaced by pure gradient descent on a learned probability field, yielding a simpler and more principled training pipeline that matches or exceeds COLMAP-initialized baselines from random initialization alone. However, the method remains significantly slower and more memory-intensive than standard 3DGS due to the large number of stochastic samples and hash-grid queries per iteration, limiting its practical adoption. Open questions include whether more efficient sampling or variance-reduction strategies can close the speed gap, whether the approach scales to dynamic or very large scenes, and whether the probabilistic formulation offers advantages beyond static novel-view synthesis (e.g., uncertainty quantification or generative sampling).
