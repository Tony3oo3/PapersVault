## Problem

Prior loss landscape visualization methods focus on the local geometry around a single trained solution, leaving the global structure—how multiple independently trained minima, basins, and connecting pathways relate to one another—largely invisible. Existing global metrics (CKA, mode connectivity) quantify pairwise similarity but do not produce a continuous geometric representation, and nonlinear dimensionality reduction methods that do (UMAP, Kernel-PCA) either lack a reliable inverse mapping or fail to preserve meaningful global structure in practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of independently trained model checkpoints (e.g., 7 hyperparameter variants of ResNet56 sampled every 10 epochs, or 32 pairs of MNIST-MLP models), each flattened into a single weight vector of dimension D (up to ~8.5M parameters). It produces a low-dimensional latent embedding (typically d=2, but extensible to 3 or higher) where every point is invertible via the decoder back to full model weights for loss evaluation, plus a merge tree whose leaves correspond to distinct basins and internal nodes to saddle points. The critical design constraint is that the embedding must support arbitrary point queries in the latent space, not just the input models, enabling interpolation and pathway tracing.

## Method

Globscope trains a three-layer autoencoder (hidden widths 128→64→16, latent dimension d) with MSE reconstruction loss on the set of flattened weight vectors, learning a nonlinear manifold that captures the intrinsic geometry of the explored parameter space. The decoder provides the inverse mapping that other nonlinear methods (UMAP, Kernel-PCA) lack, enabling loss evaluation at any latent point. On top of this manifold, a merge tree is constructed via the ParaView Topology Toolkit: a persistence diagram identifies critical points of the loss-as-scalar-field, which are then thresholded and simplified into a join tree encoding the hierarchical basin-saddle-basin structure. The latent dimensionality is not fixed at 2, allowing higher-dimensional topological analysis, and the framework is validated by projecting mode-connectivity curves and DEEP-ALIGN re-basin transformations onto the learned landscape, confirming geometric consistency with known theoretical results.

## Results

On ResNet56 (8,557,70 parameters) trained on CIFAR-10, the AutoEncoder achieves a reconstruction loss of 0.000007, dramatically outperforming Kernel-PCA (0.004270) and UMAP (0.501484). For mode connectivity validation, the projected curves on the learned landscape match ground-truth MC curves with a median MAE below 0.01 (range 0.006–0.016 across six model pairs). The merge tree analysis identifies seven distinct leaf nodes corresponding to the seven independently trained ResNet56 variants, confirming correct basin separation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The AutoEncoder-based Globscope outperforms both baselines on reconstruction fidelity by orders of magnitude: its loss of 0.000007 is ~610× lower than Kernel-PCA (0.004270) and ~71,600× lower than UMAP (0.501484). Qualitatively, Kernel-PCA fails to separate the basins of different minima, producing projections that lack meaningful structural interpretation, while UMAP separates basins but does not preserve their internal geometric organization, causing projected mode-connectivity curves to oscillate within each basin. In contrast, the AutoEncoder produces smooth, interpretable trajectories that align with known low-loss connectivity between optima (MAE < 0.016 on all six model pairs in Table 2). For permutation symmetry visualization on MNIST-MLP, UMAP clusters re-basined models correctly but yields an unstable loss range due to its poor inverse transform, whereas Globscope provides a structured view with consistent loss values. Kernel-PCA is excluded from the permutation symmetry comparison entirely because it fails to produce a meaningful landscape representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoencoder-based loss landscape visualization of Elhamod and Karpatne [6] from single-model local views to a global, multi-model setting, and generalizes the merge-tree topological analysis of Geniesse et al. [10] from linear projections to nonlinear learned manifolds. It departs from prior metric-based global analyses (CKA, mode connectivity, LossLens) by producing a continuous geometric embedding with an invertible mapping back to parameter space, rather than discrete similarity scores. The framework also builds on established mode-connectivity algorithms [9] and re-basin methods [27] as validation targets rather than as the core contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Globscope provides the first continuous, invertible visualization of the global loss landscape spanning multiple independently trained models, enabling geometry-aware decisions in model merging, federated learning, and weight-space editing that were previously limited to pairwise metrics. The merge-tree summary offers a topological fingerprint of basin structure beyond 2D plots, though its utility at scale remains untested. A key open question is scalability: the framework is validated only on ResNet56 (~8.5M parameters) and MNIST-MLP, and the authors acknowledge that parameter-space compression will be needed before it can address modern large-scale architectures.
