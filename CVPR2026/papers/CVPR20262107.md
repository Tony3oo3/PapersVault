## Problem

Diffusion models process highly noisy states at full resolution even though those states contain no more information than small, downsampled images, as formalized by the paper's information-hierarchy analysis linking diffusion timesteps to scale-space levels. No prior work has mathematically unified scale-space theory with the diffusion process in a way that handles resolution transitions end-to-end; existing approaches either operate only at the highest resolution, assume isotropic posteriors that do not hold under blurring, or treat scale changes as inference-time approximations rather than part of the diffusion process itself.

## Input / Output

The model starts from random Gaussian noise at the lowest resolution in a multi-scale schedule (e.g., 8×8 for a 64×64 target) and progressively denoises while upsampling through intermediate resolutions to produce a full-resolution RGB image. A key unusual aspect is that the spatial dimensionality of the signal changes across timesteps according to a resolution schedule r(t), so the network must accept variable-sized inputs and sometimes output at a higher resolution than its input. Training uses CelebA (64, 128, 256) and ImageNet (64) with JPEG images.

## Method

The paper replaces the scalar attenuation coefficient √α_t in standard DDPM with a general linear operator M_t (a resize-and-attenuate operator), yielding a family of generalized linear diffusion processes where the marginal is x_t = M_{1:t} x_0 + σ_t ε and DDPM emerges as the identity-operator special case. The reverse posterior q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0) acquires a non-isotropic covariance Σ_{t→t−1} = σ_{t−1}²I − (σ_{t−1}⁴/σ_t²) M_t^T M_t, from which noise is sampled implicitly via the Lanczos algorithm applied to the operator A(v) = v − ρ·M_t^T M_t(v). To support variable-resolution inputs and outputs, the authors introduce Flexi-UNet, a UNet variant that dynamically activates only the encoder/decoder blocks matching the current resolution, using 1×1 convolutions for channel alignment and zero-filled skip connections when upsampling, thereby avoiding the cost of routing small images through the full network. Training uses a Min-SNR-γ weighted loss that predicts the clean image at the next resolution r(t−1) from the noisy state at r(t), with batch construction ensuring all samples in a batch share the same (r(t), r(t−1)) pair.

## Results

On CelebA-64 (1M iterations), SSD (2L) achieves FID 2.14 with 62.63 h training time and 50.61 GFLOPs/iter, slightly beating DDPM-ε (FID 2.22, 70.30 h, 60.05 GFLOPs) while using ~16% fewer FLOPs. At CelebA-256 (300K iterations), the speed advantage grows dramatically: SSD (6L) trains in 42.88 h (less than half of DDPM-ε's 87.31 h) with 209.69 GFLOPs vs. 497.03, at the cost of FID rising from 5.52 to 13.50. On ImageNet-64, SSD (2L) reaches FID 13.08, essentially matching DDPM-x₀ (13.07) and close to DDPM-ε (12.82). The core trade-off is that increasing the number of scale levels (L) monotonically reduces training time and FLOPs but degrades FID, with the 2-level configuration offering the best quality–speed balance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM-ε, SSD (2L) on CelebA-64 actually achieves a slightly lower FID (2.14 vs. 2.22) while training ~11% faster and using ~16% fewer GFLOPs per iteration, making it the only configuration that wins on both quality and efficiency. Against Blurring Diffusion (BD), SSD (2L) is marginally worse in FID (2.14 vs. 2.06) but trains faster (62.63 h vs. 71.79 h); at CelebA-256, BD retains a clear FID advantage (4.76 vs. 7.79 for SSD-3L) but SSD still cuts training time by ~33%. On ImageNet-64, SSD (2L) matches DDPM-x₀ almost exactly (FID 13.08 vs. 13.07) and trails DDPM-ε by only 0.26 FID points, while BD lags behind both (15.34). The consistent pattern across all resolutions is that higher-level SSD configurations (4L–6L) sacrifice FID substantially (e.g., 13.50 vs. 5.52 at CelebA-256) in exchange for near-2× training speedups, positioning SSD as a speed-oriented alternative rather than a quality-improving one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the DDPM forward/marginal/posterior formulation by replacing the scalar attenuation coefficient with an arbitrary linear operator M_t, yielding a family of 'Generalized Linear Diffusion Processes' of which DDPM (identity operator) and Blurring Diffusion (scalar blurring) are special cases. It departs from prior multi-resolution diffusion approaches (Cascaded Diffusion, Relay Diffusion, Pyramidal Flow Matching, PixelFlow) that handle scale transitions only at inference time via ad-hoc noise injection or decorrelation tricks, by embedding the scale-space structure directly into the forward diffusion process with a rigorous non-isotropic posterior derivation. The Flexi-UNet architecture extends the ADM UNet by dynamically routing lower-resolution inputs through only the relevant subset of encoder/decoder blocks, avoiding the full-network pass that a standard UNet would require for small images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal equivalence between diffusion timesteps and scale-space resolutions provides a principled justification for processing highly noisy states at reduced resolution, and the resulting efficiency gains are substantial (SSD-6L at 256×256 trains in under half the time of DDPM). However, the quality cost is significant: FID degrades from 5.52 (DDPM) to 13.50 (SSD-6L) at CelebA-256, suggesting the current formulation trades too much fidelity for speed. Open questions include whether the quality gap can be closed with longer training or better resolution schedules, whether the framework extends to conditional generation and latent spaces, and whether the non-isotropic posterior sampling (via Lanczos) remains tractable at higher resolutions or with more complex degradation operators.
