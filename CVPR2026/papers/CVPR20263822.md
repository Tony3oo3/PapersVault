## Problem

Dexterous hand manipulation for robots is bottlenecked by three intertwined challenges: real-robot teleoperation data is expensive and scales poorly, dexterous hands vary widely in DoFs (6–24), morphology, and kinematics making cross-hand transfer difficult, and the high-dimensional control space demands expressive action representations. Most existing vision-language-action (VLA) foundation models target parallel-jaw grippers, leaving a gap for general-purpose dexterous hand control that can leverage the abundant manipulation data humans naturally produce in egocentric videos.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a single-view colored pointcloud (derived from an RGB-D frame with human hands masked out and the retargeted robot-hand mesh rendered in), a natural-language instruction, and a proprioceptive state vector in FAAS. It outputs an H-step action chunk where each step is an 82-dimensional FAAS vector: the first 18 dimensions encode relative wrist poses (9 per hand, relative to the first frame of the chunk following UMI), and the remaining 64 dimensions (32 slots per hand) encode finger actuator commands mapped by functional role rather than URDF joint index. The pointcloud is cropped and downsampled before encoding, and the single-view setting matches the egocentric RealSense L515 camera used in deployment.

## Method

UniDex couples three components. First, UniDex-Dataset (9M frames, 50K+ trajectories, 8 hands) is built by transforming egocentric human RGB-D videos (H2O, HOI4D, HOT3D, TACO) into robot-executable trajectories via a two-stage human-in-the-loop retargeting: automatic fingertip-based IK (solving a 6-DoF dummy-base offset plus joint configuration via PyBullet) followed by interactive GUI adjustment of the base offset for plausible hand-object contact, combined with visual alignment that masks human hands (WiLoR + SAM2) and attaches the retargeted robot-hand mesh into scene pointclouds. Second, the Function-Actuator-Aligned Space (FAAS) provides a unified 82-dim action representation that maps functionally similar actuators (e.g., thumb-index pinch, finger curling) to shared coordinates across embodiments, enabling post-processing-free cross-hand transfer. Third, UniDex-VLA is a 3D VLA policy that follows the π₀ architecture but replaces the SigLIP 2D encoder with Uni3D (a ViT-based pointcloud encoder initialized from 2D pretrained weights), trained with a conditional flow-matching objective and forward-Euler denoising at inference; it is pretrained on UniDex-Dataset and finetuned with only 50 task demonstrations per task, achieving 81% average task progress on real-world tool-use tasks versus 38% for π₀.

## Results

On five challenging real-world tool-use tasks across two dexterous hands (Inspire and Wuji), UniDex-VLA achieves 81.0±12.1% average task progress and 76.0±17.8% final success rate, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline π0 (38.0±7.4% task progress, 35.0±10.0% success rate) by more than 43 percentage points. The largest relative gain (84.6%) occurs on the hardest task, Use Scissors to Cut Bags. In zero-shot cross-hand transfer, UniDex-VLA achieves 60% task progress on Oymotion and 40% on Wuji without any fine-tuning, while baselines remain near zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDex-VLA decisively outperforms all three baselines on both average task progress and final success rate. Against π0 (the strongest VLA baseline, pretrained on gripper-centric data), UniDex-VLA improves average task progress from 38.0% to 81.0% (+43 pp) and final success rate from 35.0% to 76.0% (+41 pp). Against 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), the closest imitation-learning baseline, the gap is 46 pp in task progress and 46 pp in success rate. Against standard Diffusion Policy (DP), the gap is 52 pp in task progress. The ablation UniDex-VLA (No Pretrain) — which uses the same FAAS action space and architecture but skips pretraining on UniDex-Dataset — scores only 32.5% task progress, confirming that the large gains come primarily from large-scale pretraining on the 9M-frame human-derived dataset rather than from the architecture alone. In the zero-shot cross-hand transfer experiment (Fig. 10), UniDex-VLA achieves 60% on Oymotion and 40% on Wuji, while π0 and UniDex-VLA (No Pretrain) remain at 0–10%, demonstrating that FAAS plus diverse-hand pretraining enables skill transfer that no baseline can match.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDex-VLA's architecture directly extends π0 by replacing its 2D SigLIP encoder with the Uni3D pointcloud encoder and swapping gripper-centric actions for the proposed Function-Actuator-Aligned Space (FAAS), while retaining the conditional flow-matching training objective. The human-to-robot data transformation pipeline departs from prior egocentric-video approaches (EgoVLA, Being-H0) that predict human hand motion and require a separate alignment stage; instead, UniDex generates robot-centric supervision directly via fingertip-based IK with human-in-the-loop retargeting, eliminating post-hoc alignment. FAAS itself contrasts with gripper-oriented unified action spaces (RDT-1B's semantic structure, π0's left-aligned representation, UniVLA's latent actions) by mapping functionally similar actuators across heterogeneous hands into shared coordinates, enabling post-processing-free cross-hand transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniDex-Dataset is the first large-scale dexterous-hand dataset spanning eight morphologically diverse hands (6–24 DoFs) with 9M paired frames, and the ~2:1 human-to-robot data exchange rate demonstrated by UniDex-Cap offers a concrete path to reducing the teleoperation bottleneck that has constrained dexterous manipulation research. The zero-shot cross-hand transfer (40–60% success on unseen hands with no fine-tuning) suggests FAAS + diverse pretraining can partially solve the embodiment-heterogeneity problem, though the evidence is limited to a single task and three hands. Open questions include whether the approach scales to bimanual or whole-body settings, whether the human-robot visual gap can be closed further without the pointcloud re-projection trick, and whether the 50-demo fine-tuning budget generalizes to substantially more complex or longer-horizon tasks.
