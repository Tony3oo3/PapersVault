## Problem

Existing self-supervised depth estimation methods treat degraded regions uniformly, ignoring object-level semantic and structural differences that strongly influence depth perception. Under adverse conditions such as nighttime or rain, this leads to blurred depth boundaries and inconsistent predictions within objects. The paper addresses this by asking how object-level priors from a general-purpose segmentation model (SAM) can be integrated to enforce region-consistent depth and reliable supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260677/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (or two temporally adjacent frames in the multi-frame setting) and outputs a dense per-pixel depth map. SAM masks are pre-generated offline in 'segment-everything' mode and post-processed into non-overlapping object-level partitions; they are consumed only during training as supervisory signals, not at inference. Evaluation is performed on ground-truth depths within 0.1–80 m (nuScenes) or 0.1–50 m (Robotcar), reporting AbsRel, SqRel, RMSE, and δ₁.

## Method

RoSAMDepth builds on the Syn2RealDepth teacher-student paradigm, where a fixed synthetic-trained teacher provides pseudo-labels and a student network is adapted on real data. Three SAM-guided components are introduced: (1) Segment-Guided Representation Contrasting (SRC) applies an InfoNCE loss at multiple decoder scales to pull pixel features toward their SAM-segment prototype and push them away from other segments, embedding object-aware structure into the feature space without directly constraining depth; (2) Adaptive Regional Outlier Masking (AROM) computes per-object mean and standard deviation of inverse teacher depth, derives a spatially adaptive threshold, and generates a soft outlier-suppressed mask that is paired with a regional Gaussian likelihood loss to enforce intra-object depth smoothness; (3) Object-Level Reliability Estimation (ORE) aggregates pixel-wise photometric errors into per-object means and produces a reliability-weighted map that down-weights the distillation loss for objects whose depth is globally inconsistent. The final training loss combines the masked, reliability-weighted depth distillation loss with the SRC and regional Gaussian terms.

## Results

On nuScenes (single-frame), RoSAMDepth achieves AbsRel of 0.1172 (day-clear), 0.1742 (night), and 0.1298 (day-rain), with δ1 of 86.13%, 72.52%, and 82.30% respectively. On Oxford Robotcar (single-frame), it reaches AbsRel of 0.1006 (day) and 0.1066 (night), with δ1 of 88.90% and 87.03%. Compared to the primary baseline Syn2Real-Depth [37], the method yields average relative gains of 2.8% on AbsRel, 2.7% on SqRel, 0.6% on RMSE, and 0.9% on δ1 across all nuScenes conditions and both single/multi-frame settings, and 4.4% on AbsRel and 7.6% on SqRel on Robotcar.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoSAMDepth outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the closest competitor Syn2Real-Depth [37] (the primary real-data-trained baseline it builds upon), it improves AbsRel from 0.1239→0.1172 (day-clear), 0.1792→0.1742 (night), and 0.1331→0.1298 (day-rain) on nuScenes single-frame, and from 0.1063→0.1006 (day) and 0.1103→0.1066 (night) on Robotcar. On Robotcar, the gains over Syn2Real-Depth are particularly large: 4.4% on AbsRel and 7.6% on SqRel for daytime, and 3.3% on AbsRel and 7.2% on SqRel for nighttime. Against synthetic-data-trained methods (md4all [7], DM-MDE [31], Robust-Depth [28]), the advantage is even more pronounced, with AbsRel improvements of 10–20% under nighttime conditions. Older methods (Monodepth2 [8], RNW [35], DeFeat-Net [30], ADIDS [18], WSGD [33]) are substantially outperformed, with RNW showing the largest gap (e.g., AbsRel 0.2872 vs. 0.1172 on day-clear nuScenes). The only method that approaches RoSAMDepth on day-clear δ1 is R4Dyn† [6] (86.97% vs. 86.13%), but R4Dyn relies on additional radar input and is far worse under night and rain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Syn2RealDepth [37] synthetic-to-real teacher-student paradigm for robust depth estimation, extending its real adaptation stage with object-level priors from SAM. It departs from prior work that used semantic segmentation (e.g., [12, 19]) for object-aware depth learning by leveraging SAM's instance-level, open-world segmentation, which avoids the class-limited and domain-specific constraints of semantic models. The three proposed components—segment-guided representation contrasting (SRC), adaptive regional outlier masking with a regional Gaussian likelihood loss (AROM), and object-level reliability estimation (ORE)—collectively repurpose SAM masks as a free, offline structural prior rather than a direct supervision signal, distinguishing this work from methods that treat segmentation as a parallel prediction task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a general-purpose segmentation model (SAM) can serve as a zero-shot, domain-agnostic structural prior for depth estimation without fine-tuning, which is practical and broadly applicable. The conceptual insight that pixel-level photometric error is fundamentally misaligned with object-level depth reliability (Fig. 4) challenges a widely adopted assumption in self-supervised depth methods and motivates a shift toward region-level supervision. Limitations include reliance on pre-generated SAM masks (offline cost), evaluation restricted to driving datasets (nuScenes, Robotcar), and moderate absolute gains (~2–4% relative) over the strong Syn2RealDepth baseline. Open questions include whether SAM priors transfer to non-driving or indoor scenes, whether the approach benefits from SAM's prompt-based (rather than segment-everything) mode, and whether the object-level reliability idea generalizes to other self-supervised tasks like optical flow or pose estimation.
