## Problem

Existing 3D shape matching methods either rely on near-isometric assumptions (functional maps) or require manual part annotations and predefined part proposals (DenseMatcher, ZSC), making them inapplicable to cross-category objects with strong non-isometric deformations. No prior approach can establish dense semantic correspondences across arbitrary object categories in a fully unsupervised, part-prior-free manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263186/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two untextured or textured 3D meshes as input with no category labels, part annotations, or predefined part proposals. It produces a compact functional map (a spectral linear operator) that encodes dense point-to-point correspondences between the two shapes. The input meshes are consumed through multi-view rendering (uniformly distributed elevation and azimuthal angles over [0°, 360°)), class-agnostic segmentation, and MLLM prompting, while the output functional map can be converted back to per-vertex correspondences when needed.

## Method

UniMatch is a two-stage coarse-to-fine framework. In the coarse stage, PartField performs class-agnostic 3D part segmentation to yield non-overlapping semantic regions; these are rendered to multi-view images, named by GPT-5, and mapped to a unified embedding space via FG-CLIP language embeddings, establishing implicit (rather than explicit) coarse correspondences that are robust to naming ambiguity. In the fine stage, the URSSM functional map pipeline is extended with semantic feature fields computed from SD-DINO features (after SyncMVD texture synthesis, multi-view rendering, FeatUp upsampling, and back-projection) concatenated with geometric descriptors. A novel group-wise Rank-n-Contrastive (RnC) loss supervises dense correspondence learning by contrasting per-vertex features at the semantic-group level using ordinal rankings derived from language embedding distances, reducing complexity from O(n_x × n_y) to O(n_x × n_R) and eliminating the need for explicit positive/negative sample selection.

## Results

On the primary inter-class benchmarks, UniMatch achieves average geodesic errors of 0.19 (SNIS), 0.23 (TOSCA), and 0.37 (SHREC07), substantially outperforming the strongest prior method DenseMatcher (0.28/0.30/0.39) by 32–23%. On non-isometric matching, UniMatch reports 4.8 (SMAL) and 5.9 (TOPKIDS), comparable to DenseMatcher (4.7/6.2) and clearly better than functional map baselines like URSSM (6.0/8.9). On near-isometric benchmarks, UniMatch matches or slightly exceeds the best baselines: 1.6 (FAUST), 1.9 (SCAPE), and 3.2 (SHREC19), confirming universality across deformation regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMatch decisively outperforms DenseMatcher [63] on all three inter-class benchmarks (0.19 vs 0.28 on SNIS, 0.23 vs 0.30 on TOSCA, 0.37 vs 0.39 on SHREC07), with the largest margin on SNIS (32% relative reduction). Against ZSC [1], UniMatch wins on all inter-class datasets (0.19 vs 0.36, 0.23 vs 0.56, 0.37 vs 0.60), while ZSC's reliance on predefined part proposals limits its generalization. Functional map baselines (URSSM [9], SimpFMap [41], AttentiveFMaps [28]) show 2–3× higher errors on inter-class matching, confirming that geometric-only representations cannot bridge cross-category semantic gaps. On non-isometric shapes, UniMatch is comparable to DenseMatcher (4.8 vs 4.7 on SMAL) and slightly better on TOPKIDS (5.9 vs 6.2), while clearly surpassing URSSM (6.0/8.9) and Diff3F (28.4/31.0). On near-isometric benchmarks, UniMatch ties URSSM on FAUST (1.6) and SCAPE (1.9) and slightly trails DenseMatcher on SHREC19 (3.2 vs 3.1), indicating no regression in the regime where geometric methods already excel.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMatch extends the functional map framework (specifically URSSM) by replacing purely geometric descriptors with SD-DINO semantic feature fields and introducing a group-wise Rank-n-Contrastive loss for supervision. It departs from prior semantic correspondence methods (Diff3F, DenseMatcher, ZSC) by eliminating the need for predefined part proposals or manual annotations, instead using class-agnostic 3D segmentation (PartField) combined with MLLM prompting (GPT-5) and fine-grained language embeddings (FG-CLIP) to build implicit coarse correspondences. The coarse-to-fine pipeline—where language-embedding-based ordinal relations guide dense matching via rank-based contrastive learning rather than explicit positive/negative pairs—represents a conceptual shift from the discrete part-matching paradigm of ZSC and the annotation-dependent contrastive scheme of DenseMatcher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Universal cross-category shape matching without part priors is a practical bottleneck for applications like robotic manipulation, texture transfer, and shape interpolation on in-the-wild objects; UniMatch's 30–40% relative improvement over the strongest baseline (DenseMatcher) on inter-class benchmarks is a meaningful step toward category-agnostic correspondence. The emergent co-segmentation capability across topologically distinct shapes (human ↔ animal) suggests the learned features encode genuine semantic structure beyond what the matching objective explicitly requires. Open questions include handling ambiguous same-name parts (e.g., chair legs), scaling to shapes with highly irregular topology, and whether the MLLM prompting cost at training time can be further reduced or replaced with a learned part-naming module.
