## Problem

Text-goal instance navigation requires an agent to resolve a single free-form description into the correct object instance among same-category distractors, but existing methods underutilize the full description: they reduce long contextual captions to category labels or short attribute snippets, and their verification is inherently viewpoint-biased. The paper argues that spatial reasoning should serve as a primary exploration signal rather than a post-hoc check, and that viewpoint-dependent spatial relations (e.g., 'left of,' 'near') are a geometric challenge overlooked by prior modular systems.

## Input / Output

The agent receives streaming RGB images, depth maps, and egocentric SE(3) pose alongside a single free-form text goal that mixes intrinsic attributes (color, shape, material) with extrinsic context (named surrounding objects and their spatial relations). It outputs a discrete action sequence (forward, turn-left, turn-right, stop) and internally maintains a 3D occupancy map, a context-conditioned value map over a top-down grid, and per-instance 3D point clouds. The text goal is consumed at two distinct stages: the full caption drives frontier ranking via dense text-image alignment, while parsed relation triples drive geometric verification.

## Method

Context-Nav is a training-free pipeline with two core innovations. First, it encodes the full caption with GOAL-CLIP (a fine-tuned CLIP for long-text-to-region alignment) to compute per-pixel text-image similarities, projects them into a top-down value map using depth and pose, and ranks frontier cells by value—guiding exploration toward regions consistent with the entire description before any detection is trusted. Second, upon detecting a candidate instance, it performs viewpoint-aware 3D relation verification: it samples 24 viewpoints at four radii (0.8–2.0 m) around the relation-pair midpoint, constructs a viewpoint-aligned local frame per candidate, and checks whether all spatial predicates (left, right, front, behind, near, above, below) can be simultaneously satisfied from at least one pose; if not, the candidate is rejected and exploration resumes. A wall-only map (built via RANSAC plane segmentation of height-gated depth points) provides room-level constraints for both a one-shot frontier override and line-of-sight gating. The entire pipeline composes off-the-shelf components—Grounding DINO for open-vocabulary detection, YOLOv7 for COCO-class confirmation, a VLM for intrinsic attribute VQA, and a depth-only point-goal policy for low-level motion—without any task-specific training or fine-tuning.

## Results

On InstanceNav, Context-Nav achieves the highest Success Rate (SR) of 26.2%, surpassing the RL-trained PSL policy (26.0%) and the training-free UniGoal pipeline (20.2%), though its SPL of 9.1 trails UniGoal (11.4) and PSL (10.2). On CoIN-Bench, Context-Nav attains the best SR across all three splits: 13.5% (Val Seen), 20.3% (Val Seen Synonyms), and 11.3% (Val Unseen), with corresponding SPL values of 6.7, 10.9, and 5.2. The method is entirely training-free and requires no human-in-the-loop interaction, yet outperforms both RL-trained and interactive baselines on every CoIN-Bench split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Context-Nav wins on SR against all five baselines on every reported benchmark split. On InstanceNav, it edges out the RL-trained PSL policy by +0.2 SR (26.2 vs 26.0) and beats the training-free UniGoal by +6.0 SR (26.2 vs 20.2), though it trails both on SPL (9.1 vs 10.2 and 11.4 respectively), indicating slightly longer paths. On CoIN-Bench Val Seen Synonyms, the margin is most pronounced: Context-Nav achieves 20.3 SR vs AIUTA's 14.4 (interactive, human-in-the-loop), GOAT's 13.1, and UniGoal's 3.9. On Val Unseen, Context-Nav (11.3 SR) nearly doubles AIUTA (6.7) and PSL (4.6), and is an order of magnitude above GOAT (0.2). VLFM, which uses only a category-level goal, is effectively non-functional on CoIN-Bench (≤0.4 SR), underscoring the necessity of full textual descriptions. The consistent SPL advantage on CoIN-Bench (e.g., 10.9 vs 8.0 on Val Seen Synonyms) shows that context-driven exploration not only finds the right instance but does so more efficiently than baselines that rely on detection-first or interactive strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

Context-Nav builds on the frontier-based value-map exploration paradigm from VLFM [42] and the long-text-to-image alignment capability of GOAL-CLIP [10], but repurposes both for a fundamentally different role: the full contextual caption becomes a global exploration prior rather than a post-hoc matching cue. It departs from the three dominant TGIN lineages—RL-trained policies (PSL, GOAT), detection-driven modular pipelines (UniGoal), and interactive human-in-the-loop agents (AIUTA)—by treating spatial reasoning as a primary exploration signal and introducing viewpoint-aware 3D relation verification that is entirely training-free and geometry-grounded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that geometry-grounded, training-free spatial reasoning can close the gap with RL-trained policies for fine-grained instance disambiguation, suggesting a scalable alternative to heavy policy training or human supervision. However, absolute success rates remain modest (~26% SR on InstanceNav), and the pipeline's reliance on multiple VLM queries and dense per-pixel GOAL-CLIP scoring introduces computational latency that limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the viewpoint-aware relation framework generalizes to more complex or compositional spatial predicates, how it scales to larger or multi-room scenes, and whether the wall-only room segmentation holds in architecturally irregular environments.
