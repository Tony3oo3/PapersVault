## Problem

Existing facial reenactment methods face a fundamental trade-off: holistic end-to-end models produce expressive results but sacrifice fine-grained control over individual components, while control-oriented methods rely on indirect supervision (data augmentation, auxiliary losses) that yields brittle and incomplete disentanglement. The paper argues this stems from modeling facial motion as a monolithic signal rather than a multi-layered composition of spatially and semantically distinct components.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a single reference face image plus up to four independent driving videos (mouth, head pose, eye blink/gaze, and emotion), each of which can be sourced from a different video to enable per-component control. Output is a streaming 512×512 face reenactment video at 20 FPS with 800 ms end-to-end latency on a single NVIDIA 5090 GPU, using a 4-step distilled diffusion model with causal attention and a 4×-accelerated VAE decoder.

## Method

PortraitDirector formulates reenactment as a hierarchical composition task over a frozen Wan-I2V DiT backbone. The Spatial Layer physically separates head pose (encoded by a dedicated PoseEncoder and injected via an independent PoseAdapter) from local expressions by cropping eye and mouth regions before encoding, guaranteeing input-level decoupling. The Semantic Layer extracts a global emotion latent via temporal pooling over a video window using a frozen MotionEncoder. An Emotion-Filtering Module (autoencoder with a 128-dim bottleneck and KL regularization) strips residual emotional cues from the local latents, and a Composite Layer re-injects the target emotion through cross-attention to reconstruct a holistic motion latent. Training is two-stage: 100K steps to pretrain the reenactment model, then 20K steps to fine-tune only the disentanglement module with a face-weighted denoising loss plus a latent-alignment loss against the original MotionEncoder output. Real-time streaming is achieved via DMD distillation (20→4 steps), a sliding-window causal attention mask with KV caching, and a retrained VAE decoder at 1/4 the original width.

## Results

On VFHQ, PortraitDirector achieves the best self-reenactment quality across all metrics: MSE 0.018, SSIM 0.654, and LPIPS 0.316, outperforming the next-best EDTalk (MSE 0.022, SSIM 0.641, LPIPS 0.405). For cross-reenactment, it attains the lowest pose error (4.707) and ties for the best expression similarity (0.010, shared with XPortrait2), while achieving an ID-SIM of 0.880. On the MEAD component-control benchmark, PortraitDirector achieves the best score on all four metrics: Pose (13.695), Expression (0.023), Mouth (0.012), and Eye (0.033).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PortraitDirector outperforms all seven baselines on self-reenactment fidelity (MSE, SSIM, LPIPS), with the largest margin over PDFGC (LPIPS 0.316 vs. 0.563) and EDTalk (LPIPS 0.316 vs. 0.405). On cross-reenactment pose control, it achieves the lowest pose error (4.707), narrowly beating XPortrait2 (4.894) and substantially outperforming diffusion-based methods like HunyuanPortrait (19.037) and FantasyPortrait (19.546). For expression similarity, it ties XPortrait2 at 0.010 while surpassing all other methods. The sole metric where it trails is ID-SIM (0.880 vs. Emoji's 0.892 and AniPortrait's 0.886), a small gap of 0.004–0.012. On the MEAD component-control task, PortraitDirector achieves the best score on all four individual components (Pose, Expression, Mouth, Eye), with particularly large margins over EDTalk on Pose (13.695 vs. 25.999) and PDFGC on Pose (13.695 vs. 33.081), demonstrating superior fine-grained disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan-I2V diffusion backbone and Wan-Animate pretraining pipeline, extending them with a hierarchical motion disentanglement module that replaces the monolithic MotionEncoder. It departs from prior disentanglement efforts (EDTalk, PDFGC) that perform post-hoc decomposition of an already-entangled latent vector, instead separating components at the input level via spatial cropping and a dedicated pose pathway. The real-time streaming capability is assembled from Self-Forcing, DMD distillation, and a lightweight VAE decoder, combining known acceleration techniques into a coherent pipeline for the first time in the controllable face reenactment setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 20 FPS at 512×512 with independent control over mouth, eyes, pose, and emotion on a single consumer GPU makes fine-grained facial animation practical for live applications (virtual avatars, interactive entertainment) that previously required server-class hardware or sacrificed controllability for speed. The information-bottleneck-based Emotion-Filtering Module offers a principled alternative to the brittle neutral-dataset approach for decoupling emotion from motion, though its generalization to extreme expressions or non-frontal faces remains untested. Open questions include whether the 4-step DMD distillation preserves quality at higher resolutions, how the method handles multi-person or occluded scenes, and whether the hierarchical decomposition transfers to other body-part animation tasks.
