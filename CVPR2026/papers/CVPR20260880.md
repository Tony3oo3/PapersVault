## Problem

Single-view 3D hand mesh reconstruction faces a persistent trade-off: parametric-model-based methods (e.g., MANO regression) accumulate errors along kinematic chains, while direct vertex-regression methods lack structural priors and produce artifacts like finger fusion. No existing approach simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and real-time inference speed, especially under occlusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260880/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single cropped RGB image as input and outputs a 3D hand mesh of 778 vertices with MANO topology. Rather than regressing vertex coordinates directly, the network predicts M discrete token categories (indices into a 512-entry codebook), which are then decoded by a frozen, pre-trained decoder to produce the final mesh without any post-processing step.

## Method

TokenHand operates in two stages. In Stage I, a Point Transformer encoder maps a 3D hand point cloud to M token features that are quantized against a shared 512×512 codebook (VQ-VAE-style), and a cascading upsampling decoder with MetaFormer blocks (token counts 48→97→194→389→778, feature dims 256→128→64→32) reconstructs the point cloud; the encoder, codebook, and decoder are jointly trained with L1 reconstruction loss plus a codebook commitment term, using EMA updates and code reset to prevent collapse. In Stage II, a FastViT-MA36 backbone extracts image features, which are upsampled 4× and sampled at predicted 2D keypoint locations to form token features; an MLP-Mixer classification head then predicts the M token categories, and the frozen Stage-I decoder converts the predicted tokens into the 3D mesh. Training of the classification head uses cross-entropy on token labels plus a soft-inference reconstruction loss (linearly interpolating logits with the codebook) that back-propagates through the frozen decoder, supervising 3D vertices, 3D joints, and 2D joints with weighted L1 terms.

## Results

On FreiHAND, TokenHand achieves a PA-MPJPE of 5.7 mm and PA-MPVPE of 5.9 mm at 65 FPS, outperforming the best non-real-time method (Zhou et al., 5.8 mm PA-MPJPE at 33 FPS) and the best real-time method (FastViT, 6.6 mm PA-MPJPE at 84 FPS). On DexYCB, TokenHand reaches 5.4 mm PA-MPJPE, 12.3 mm MPJPE, and 12.0 mm MPVPE, improving over H2ONet by 1.7 mm in MPJPE. The method uses only 3.0 M parameters in the reconstruction head (excluding backbone), roughly 10% of comparable transformer-based decoders (98–102 M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenHand outperforms all 16 compared baselines on both FreiHAND and DexYCB. On FreiHAND, it beats the strongest non-real-time competitor Zhou et al. [55] by 0.1 mm in PA-MPJPE (5.7 vs 5.8) while running nearly twice as fast (65 vs 33 FPS), and surpasses the best real-time method FastViT [1] by 0.9 mm in PA-MPJPE (5.7 vs 6.6). Against transformer-based decoders, TokenHand improves over MeshGraphormer [29] by 0.6 mm and METRO [51] by 1.0 mm in PA-MPJPE, while using only 3.0 M parameters versus 98–102 M. On DexYCB, TokenHand leads H2ONet [43] by 1.7 mm in MPJPE (12.3 vs 14.0) and 1.0 mm in MPVPE (12.0 vs 13.0), and edges out Zhou et al. [55] by 0.1 mm in PA-MPJPE (5.4 vs 5.5). The method also achieves the highest F@05 score (0.768) on FreiHAND, indicating superior vertex-level accuracy at tight error thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenHand bridges two established paradigms in hand mesh reconstruction—parametric model regression (MANO-based) and direct vertex regression—by introducing a VQ-VAE-style discrete codebook as an intermediate representation. The discrete tokenization and codebook quantization are directly inspired by VQ-VAE [42], while the keypoint-guided feature sampling in the token generator follows MobRecon [9]. The key departure is reformulating reconstruction as a multi-class classification problem over codebook entries rather than a continuous regression, which allows a frozen, lightweight decoder (3M parameters) to replace the large transformer decoders (25–102M parameters) used by prior methods like METRO and MeshGraphormer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× parameter reduction (3M vs. 25–102M) combined with SOTA accuracy at 65 FPS makes this approach particularly attractive for deployment in AR/VR and robotic teleoperation where both latency and model size are critical constraints. The discrete token representation also opens a path toward integrating hand reconstruction with other discrete modalities (e.g., text-to-hand generation), though the authors explicitly leave this unexplored. Open questions include whether the codebook-based prior generalizes beyond the MANO topology to more expressive hand models, and whether the classification formulation extends naturally to multi-hand or hand-object interaction settings where the pose space is significantly larger.
