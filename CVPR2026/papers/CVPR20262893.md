## Problem

Reinforcement learning agents typically depend on handcrafted reward functions that require domain expertise and fail to generalize across tasks. While prior work has used VLMs or diffusion models to derive reward signals, none exploit *generated* goal-conditioned videos as a source of world knowledge for both trajectory-level and frame-level goal achievement, leaving a gap in transferring the rich priors learned by video generative models into actionable RL rewards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262893/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (task description) and a task-relevant image to condition CogVideoX-5B-I2V, which generates a 480×480 goal video; during online interaction, the agent's historical RGB frames (uniformly sampled to 16 frames) are encoded by the 3D Causal VAE into a latent of shape (4, h/8, w/8) per frame. The goal image selected via CLIP is encoded into a 384-dimensional DINOv3 feature. Outputs are a scalar video-level reward (cosine similarity in VAE latent space, computed every 128 steps), a scalar frame-level forward-backward reward, and their weighted sum with the environment reward.

## Method

GenReward finetunes CogVideoX (a Video Diffusion Transformer with a 3D Causal VAE) on manipulation videos to produce goal-conditioned videos, then repurposes the VAE encoder as a world-knowledge-aware feature extractor: the video-level reward is the cosine similarity between the latent of the agent's recent observations and the latent of the generated goal video. For fine-grained control, CLIP selects the most task-relevant frame from the generated video as a goal state, and a forward-backward representation (F: S×A×Z→Z, B: S→Z) is trained to approximate long-term state occupancy, yielding a frame-level reward F(s,a,ψ(I*))ᵀB(ψ(I*)) that measures the probability of reaching the goal from the current state-action pair. The final reward r_gen = α·r_video + β·r_FB + r_env is fed into DreamerV3 for model-based policy learning. The key distinction from prior diffusion-reward methods is the dual-granularity design (trajectory-level VAE alignment plus action-aware FB representation) and the use of the generative model's own encoder rather than a separate VLM for video-level scoring.

## Results

On Meta-World manipulation tasks (dense reward setting, 256-step episodes), GenReward significantly outperforms DreamerV3 with raw dense reward: Pick Out of Hole improves from 193 to 582, Bin Picking from 398 to 822, and Shelf Place from 154 to 814 in episode return. GenReward achieves competitive or superior performance across all five evaluated tasks (Pick Place, Pick Out of Hole, Bin Picking, Shelf Place, Disassemble) compared to RoboCLIP, Diffusion Reward, and TADPoLe. Results on the Distracting Control Suite (DCS) with visual background distractors are provided in the supplementary material.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenReward outperforms all three learned-reward baselines (RoboCLIP, Diffusion Reward, TADPoLe) and the raw dense reward baseline across the five Meta-World tasks. Against RoboCLIP and Diffusion Reward, which also exploit video or diffusion-based signals, GenReward demonstrates a significant advantage by transferring the underlying world knowledge from generated goal videos rather than relying on similarity scoring or entropy-based rewards. TADPoLe underperforms all other baselines, failing to provide effective reward signals in these manipulation tasks. The qualitative comparison on Bin Picking (Figure 6) shows that TADPoLe fails to contact the puck and Diffusion Reward moves the grasped puck away from the target, while GenReward completes the grasp in fewer steps and outperforms both Dense Reward and RoboCLIP. Ablation studies confirm that both the video-level reward and the forward-backward frame-level reward contribute meaningfully, with removing either component causing performance degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenReward sits at the intersection of two active research threads: using pretrained generative models as reward signals for RL (Diffusion Reward, TADPoLe, VIPER) and goal-conditioned RL via forward-backward representations (Touati et al.). It departs from prior diffusion-based reward methods by treating the *generated video itself* as an explicit goal and measuring alignment through the video encoder's latent space, rather than exploiting internal diffusion signals like conditional entropy or denoising gradients. The combination of a video-level trajectory reward (via 3D VAE latent cosine similarity) with a frame-level reward (via forward-backward representations anchored to a CLIP-selected keyframe) is a novel architectural choice not present in any single prior work. The approach also differs from VLM-based reward models (RoboCLIP, VLM-RMs) by leveraging a generative model's encoder, which encodes temporal and world-knowledge structure beyond what a discriminative vision-language model captures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent bottleneck in RL—handcrafted reward design—by showing that world knowledge embedded in video diffusion models can be transferred to downstream agents as structured, goal-driven reward signals without task-specific reward engineering. The substantial margins over baselines (e.g., 154→814 on Shelf Place) suggest that generative priors carry actionable behavioral information beyond what entropy or similarity-based rewards extract. However, the evaluation is limited to five Meta-World manipulation tasks with 10-episode evaluation, and the computational overhead of computing video-level and forward-backward rewards at regular intervals during training is a practical concern for scaling to longer-horizon or higher-dimensional tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to tasks where the generated video is only loosely aligned with the true goal, and whether the finetuning requirement can be fully eliminated via zero-shot text-to-video generation.
