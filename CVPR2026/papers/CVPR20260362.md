## Problem

Existing video QA benchmarks focus on coarse-grained tasks (action classification, captioning, retrieval) with easily identifiable entities, leaving a gap in evaluating the fine-grained spatio-temporal understanding required for practical multi-step tasks like furniture assembly. Specifically, no prior benchmark systematically tests whether LVLMs can track visually similar rigid parts across long cluttered videos, detect physical contact events, and reason about the temporal ordering of assembly steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260362/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question pairs an assembly video (either a trimmed in-the-wild clip or a curated key-frame sequence) with 1–2 visual prompt frames where relevant furniture parts are highlighted via segmentation masks and integer labels (set-of-mark style), plus a multiple-choice question and a fixed task instruction. Three prompt delivery formats are evaluated: mixed-media (prompt as separate image), collage (prompt composited into every video frame), and concat (prompt prepended as the first video frame(s)). The model outputs a single letter answer, extracted via regex-based exact match.

## Method

FLAT-PACK BENCH is constructed by augmenting the IMaW furniture-assembly dataset with manually annotated part segmentations on 343 frames across 50 videos and fine-grained part-to-part connection annotations, then curating 602 multiple-choice questions across four task types: MATE (are two parts connected?), TRACK (match shuffled part IDs across two frames), TORD (order of connection events), and TLOC (identify the event immediately before/after a shown state). All questions are manually curated using fixed templates to eliminate static-cue shortcuts. The evaluation protocol tests 25+ proprietary, open, and specialized LVLMs zero-shot under greedy decoding, supplemented by a Temporal Video Agent (TVA) agentic baseline that decomposes tasks into SAM2-based tracking and VLM-based contact-reasoning tool calls. Analysis includes image-only ablations to probe temporal utilization, CoT/self-consistency prompting, visual-prompt rendering ablations, and LLM-assisted error taxonomy from Gemini 2.5 Pro self-explanations.

## Results

On FLAT-PACK BENCH (602 multiple-choice questions across 50 furniture assembly videos), the best open model InternVL3-78B achieves 41.03% micro-average accuracy, while the best proprietary model GPT-5 reaches 37.71%—both far below the 94.18% human performance and only modestly above the 26.74% frequency-chance baseline. The TRACK sub-task is the most challenging for all models (best: Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 45.14%), while TLOC is where GPT-5 performs relatively best (53.40%). Notably, adding GenS frame selection to Gemini 2.5 Pro *degrades* performance from 33.72% to 25.58%, and CoT prompting (ZS-CoT, SC-CoT) also hurts rather than helps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 13 evaluated LVLMs fall dramatically short of human performance (94.18%), with the best model (InternVL3-78B, 41.03%) achieving less than half the human score. Among proprietary models, GPT-5 (37.71%) leads, but Gemini 2.5 Pro (33.72%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (32.89%) trail it; adding GenS frame selection to Gemini 2.5 Pro actually *reduces* accuracy to 25.58%, below even the frequency-chance baseline. Among open models, InternVL3-78B (41.03%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (40.37%) are competitive with proprietary models, while smaller models like Video-LLaVA-7B (23.75%) and LLaVA-NeXT-Vid-7B (25.08%) perform near chance. Specialized models designed for fine-grained regional understanding (PerceptionLM, VideoRefer) and temporal sensitivity (ArrowRL) do not close the gap—PerceptionLM-8B (35.38%) is competitive with much larger models, but VideoRefer (28.57%) and ArrowRL-7B (30.56%) remain weak. The TRACK sub-task is the universal bottleneck: even the best model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 45.14%) is far below human (93.77%), and the agentic TVA baseline using SAM2 tracking achieves only 11.79% overall accuracy with 62.29% abstention, confirming that both LVLMs and dedicated vision tools struggle with spatio-temporal reasoning in cluttered assembly scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video question-answering benchmarking line by shifting from coarse-grained scene semantics (MVBench, EgoSchema) to fine-grained spatio-temporal reasoning over cluttered, multi-part assembly videos. It builds on the IMaW dataset for video content and on set-of-mark visual prompting for unambiguous part references, while departing from prior regional-understanding work (PerceptionLM, VideoRefer, STVG) by requiring the model to track visually similar parts across long, occluded sequences rather than relying on pre-computed tracks. The agentic TVA baseline follows ViperGPT's visual-programming paradigm, repurposing SAM2 and a VLM query tool as primitives for tracking and contact reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark gap—best models reach ~41% versus 94% human accuracy—and, more critically, the image-only ablation reveals that LVLMs largely ignore the video signal, relying instead on static image cues and even part-ID ordering biases. The failure of the agentic decomposition (SAM2 tracking at 0.28 IoU, contact reasoning near chance on positive cases) suggests that the bottleneck is not merely in the LVLM's reasoning but in the underlying perception tools themselves. Open questions include whether task-specific fine-tuning on synthetic assembly data, 3D geometry priors, or depth signals can close the gap, and whether the visual-prompting paradigm can be generalized to domains with deformable or state-changing objects.
