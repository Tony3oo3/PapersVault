## Problem

Existing physics-based humanoid control methods (PDP, CLoSD, UniPhys) are confined to single-agent settings, leaving the rich dynamics of multi-agent interactions—collaborative tasks, social-aware behaviors—largely unexplored. Kinematic multi-agent generators produce physically implausible artifacts (penetration, floating, sliding), while generation-then-tracking pipelines suffer from inherent discrepancies between kinematic priors and physics-based tracking. The paper addresses the absence of an end-to-end framework that produces physically plausible, text-driven multi-agent humanoid interactions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded by a frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14) and a history buffer of h=364 frames of proprioception (root height, joint positions, 6D rotations, linear/angular velocities) and exteroception (interaction graph edges between the two agents), downsampled to 12 frames plus the most recent 4. It autoregressively predicts a 4-frame horizon of 28-dof PD-controller target actions for each of two humanoid agents, with the two agents modeled by cooperative weight-sharing networks. During inference, simulator states are stored in a FIFO buffer and actions are generated iteratively for the desired number of steps.

## Method

InterAgent introduces Inter-DiT, an autoregressive diffusion transformer with a multi-stream block architecture that decouples proprioception, exteroception, and action into independent streams, each processed by dedicated projection layers to mitigate cross-modal interference. Cross-modal information exchange occurs via inter-stream fusion attention (concatenated self-attention across streams) and context-aware conditioning attention (attending to historical observations and the other agent's hidden features). The key exteroception representation is an interaction graph (IG) where each joint of one agent forms a directed edge to every joint of the other, explicitly encoding joint-to-joint spatial dependencies. A sparse edge-based attention mechanism applies Gumbel-Softmax scoring followed by Top-K masking over these edges, dynamically suppressing interaction-irrelevant connections and focusing on salient joint-level relations. Training follows a track-then-distill paradigm: RL-based tracking policies (with curriculum learning and interaction graph rewards) collect noisy state-action trajectories, which are then used to train the diffusion model as a behavior cloner with classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On the InterHuman test set, InterAgent achieves the best R-Precision (Top-1: 0.375, Top-3: 0.615), lowest FID (0.582), and lowest MM distance (3.585) among all physics-based baselines, substantially outperforming the next-best method CLoSD (FID 1.132, R-Precision Top-3 0.470). InterAgent slightly trails InterGen++ in Diversity (2.018 vs. 2.044) and MModality (1.903 vs. 2.482), but leads on all other five metrics. The gap over the strongest baseline is most pronounced in FID (0.582 vs. 0.943 for InterGen++) and R-Precision Top-1 (0.375 vs. 0.287).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterAgent outperforms all four baselines on R-Precision (all three ranks), FID, and MM distance. Against the strongest kinematic-tracking baseline InterGen++ (FID 0.943, R-Prec Top-3 0.542), InterAgent reduces FID by 38% and improves R-Precision Top-3 by 0.073, while also achieving lower MM distance (3.585 vs. 3.751). The autoregressive physics-based baselines CLoSD and PDP are further behind: CLoSD scores FID 1.132 and R-Prec Top-3 0.470, while PDP scores FID 1.268 and R-Prec Top-3 0.375. InterAgent's only losses are in Diversity (2.018 vs. InterGen++'s 2.044) and MModality (1.903 vs. InterGen++'s 2.482 and PDP's 2.402), suggesting the model generates slightly less varied motions per prompt but with higher per-motion quality and text alignment. Qualitatively, the paper notes that kinematic-tracking baselines (InterGen++, InterMask++) often fail to complete full motions or exhibit instability, while CLoSD and PDP miss fine-grained interaction details.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: physics-based single-agent humanoid control (PDP, CLoSD, UniPhys, Diffuse-CLoC) and kinematic multi-agent interaction generation (InterGen, InterMask). It departs from the physics-based line by extending the autoregressive diffusion state-action paradigm from one agent to two, and from the kinematic line by grounding generation in a physics simulator rather than learned motion priors. The interaction graph exteroception is adapted from Zhang et al.'s multi-character simulation work, and the multi-stream DiT block borrows the decoupled-stream design from video and piano-hand generation literature, but the specific combination—sparse edge-based attention on a joint-to-joint interaction graph within a physics-based diffusion policy—is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first end-to-end physics-based multi-agent text-driven controller, InterAgent opens a path toward realistic, physically grounded social interactions in gaming, VR/AR, and humanoid robotics without the tracking-error artifacts of generate-then-track pipelines. Key limitations include the restriction to exactly two agents, a single humanoid morphology (15 joints, 28 actuators), and evaluation on one dataset (InterHuman), leaving open whether the interaction graph and sparse attention mechanisms scale to larger groups or more diverse interaction types. The reactive control via inpainting is a practical but unexplored direction that could enable real-time interactive applications.
