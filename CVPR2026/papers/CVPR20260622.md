## Problem

Depth from differential defocus (DfDD) is a computationally efficient depth-from-defocus algorithm, but it has never been demonstrated in a real-world ultra-low-power hardware system. Existing FPGA-based passive depth cameras are dominated by stereo methods consuming 2–3 W, and the only prior DfDD hardware (Focal Split) draws ~5 W. No sub-Watt total-power passive FPGA-based 3D camera had been reported in the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260622/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The camera simultaneously captures two 480×400 differently-defocused images of the same scene through a beam splitter with sensors at different axial offsets (HM0360 low-power sensors, 9.4°×7.9° FoV). The FPGA processes these in a streaming fashion and outputs a sparse 8-bit depth map at 32.5 FPS over a 52 cm working range (0.45–0.97 m), where only pixels exceeding a spatially-varying confidence threshold are emitted; low-texture or unstable-division regions are suppressed rather than filled.

## Method

SpiderCam replaces the softmax-weighted multi-estimate combination used in prior DfDD work with a single-division weighted-sum joint estimator (Eq. 3) and a zero-cost confidence metric C_i = V_i·W_i (Eq. 4), both calibrated on a 56-depth real-world dataset. To handle Petzval field curvature in the compact optics, all calibrated parameters (a, b, thresholds) vary across 16 radial zones tracked in the pixel stream without buffering coordinates. The SystemVerilog implementation is memory-local and streaming: the Lattice ECP5 LFE5U-85F cannot hold even one image pair, so the design uses zero-interleaved kernel upsampling to fix memory independent of image height, a hybrid fixed-point (homography, preprocessing) / FP16 (depth computation) arithmetic pipeline with subnormal support removed, and small power-of-two-coefficient separable kernels. This yields 624 mW total system power at 32.5 FPS — the first sub-Watt measurement for a passive FPGA-based 3D camera — with a 3.3× power reduction over the lowest full-system power in the literature.

## Results

SpiderCam produces 480×400 sparse depth maps at 32.5 FPS with a working range of 0.45–0.97 m (52 cm) while consuming only 624 mW of full-system wall power (399 mW at 9 FPS), the first sub-Watt measurement for a passive FPGA-based 3D camera. Core DfDD power is 0.24–0.31 W on the Lattice ECP5 (0.42–0.55 W normalized to Kintex-7 for fair cross-method comparison). On-device depth accuracy closely matches offline 32-bit floating-point computation, with mean absolute error well below 10% of true depth across the working range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest DfDD baseline, Focal Split [42], SpiderCam achieves a wider working range (0.43–0.99 m offline vs. 0.60–0.83 m for Focal Split on the same low-power sensor data) while consuming 3.3–5× less full-system power (0.6 W vs. 4.9 W). Compared to the prior FPGA DfD system of Raj & Staunton [30], SpiderCam reduces core power from 0.46–0.58 W to 0.24–0.31 W and extends the working range from a narrow 0.77–0.80 m band to 0.45–0.97 m. Against all seven stereo baselines in Table 1, SpiderCam's normalized core power (0.42–0.55 W on Kintex-7) is competitive with or below the lowest stereo entries (Puglia 0.43 W, Mattoccia 0.44 W), while its actual ECP5 core power (0.24–0.31 W) is the lowest reported. Critically, SpiderCam is the only system in the table to report both a measured full-system power (0.6 W) and quantitative on-device working range, whereas most stereo works report only estimated core power and no real-world depth metrics. The paper also shows that removing spatial-variation calibration drops SpiderCam's working range to 0.51–0.82 m, underscoring that the optical nonidealities of the compact low-power sensors are a key differentiator from prior DfDD work.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpiderCam builds directly on Focal Split [42] for its beam-splitter camera design and DfDD algorithmic framework, and on the multi-estimate DfDD lineage of Guo et al. [19, 20] for combining per-pixel depth hypotheses. The key departure is adapting this algorithmic family to an ultra-low-power FPGA regime where softmax-based combination is infeasible, external DRAM is prohibitively expensive, and sensor noise demands new robustness strategies. The paper positions itself against stereo-based FPGA depth cameras that have dominated the low-power hardware literature, arguing that DfDD's lower FLOP count has never been realized in a real sub-Watt system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first sub-Watt passive FPGA-based 3D camera with measured real-world performance, SpiderCam demonstrates that defocus-based depth sensing is viable for power-constrained embedded applications like wearables and small robots, where stereo's higher power budget has been a barrier. The streaming, memory-local architecture and hybrid fixed/FP16 design patterns are broadly applicable to other vision pipelines on resource-limited FPGAs. Open questions include whether sparse depth maps can be effectively completed for downstream tasks, whether the 9.4° FoV is sufficient for practical use, and whether ASIC implementation (projected 5–20× further savings) can push the system toward milliwatt-scale operation.
