## Problem

Existing learned stereo image compression methods model inter-view correlations in the spatial domain, which entangles high-frequency details with coarse structures and causes significant high-frequency signal loss in reconstructions. Additionally, state-of-the-art methods rely on spatial autoregressive entropy models that incur substantial coding latency, hindering practical deployment. FreqSIC addresses both issues by decomposing stereo context transfer into frequency bands and replacing autoregressive entropy with a checkerboard strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB stereo images (left and right views) of arbitrary resolution and produces a compressed bitstream via arithmetic coding along with reconstructed stereo images. Internally, a shared analysis transform maps each view to a latent representation at 1/16 spatial resolution (M=320 channels), which is quantized and entropy-coded using per-element Gaussian parameters predicted by the context model. The synthesis transform is shared between views, and the entire pipeline is bi-directional, making it independent of input view ordering.

## Method

FreqSIC introduces a Frequency Stereo Context Transfer (FSCT) module that decomposes left and right features into high- and low-frequency components via multi-scale average pooling (rather than Fourier or wavelet transforms), applies separate efficient cross-attention to each band for inter-view information exchange, and dynamically fuses the bands using softmax-weighted attention learned from global max-pooled descriptors. For entropy coding, a bi-directional checkerboard context model partitions quantized latents spatially into anchor/non-anchor regions and channel-wise into slices, using the FSCT module to generate inter-view priors that condition Gaussian mean and variance predictions. This replaces the costly spatial autoregressive paradigm with a parallelizable checkerboard pattern while retaining channel-wise autoregressive dependencies, achieving orders-of-magnitude lower coding latency. The overall framework is a joint autoencoder with mixed quantization (straight-through estimator) trained end-to-end with a Lagrangian rate-distortion loss.

## Results

On InStereo2K, FreqSIC achieves a BD-PSNR gain of 1.78 dB and a BD-Rate reduction of 52.43% (PSNR) / 62.48% (MS-SSIM) relative to the BPG anchor, surpassing the previous SOTA BiSIC by 2.74%–4.36% BD-Rate. On Cityscapes, the gains are even larger: 3.57 dB BD-PSNR and 60.23% BD-Rate (PSNR) / 69.85% BD-Rate (MS-SSIM). FreqSIC also delivers the lowest coding latency at 1.62 s total (encode + decode) on an RTX 3090, compared to 78.60 s for BiSIC and 43.11 s for BCSIC, thanks to the checkerboard context model replacing autoregressive decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqSIC outperforms all 13 baselines on both InStereo2K and Cityscapes across both PSNR and MS-SSIM metrics. Against the strongest learned stereo codec BiSIC (ECCV 2024), FreqSIC gains an additional 2.74% BD-Rate (InStereo2K, PSNR) to 4.36% BD-Rate (Cityscapes, MS-SSIM), attributed to the FSCT module's frequency-aware inter-view modeling. Compared to the VVC standard, FreqSIC achieves 17.12% BD-Rate savings (PSNR) and 31.43% (MS-SSIM) on InStereo2K, and 4.0% / 25.8% on Cityscapes. Among other learned stereo methods, BCSIC and LDMIC are the closest competitors (within ~1 dB BD-PSNR on InStereo2K), while unidirectional methods like HESIC+ and SASIC trail by 1+ dB. In terms of latency, FreqSIC (1.62 s total) is roughly 48× faster than BiSIC (78.60 s) and 27× faster than BCSIC (43.11 s), while also being faster than hyperprior-based methods like CAMSIC (1.75 s) despite achieving superior RD performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreqSIC extends the bidirectional stereo image compression lineage (BCSIC, BiSIC) by shifting inter-view redundancy modeling from the spatial domain to the frequency domain via the FSCT module, which decomposes features into high- and low-frequency bands using multi-scale average pooling and applies separate cross-attention per band. For entropy coding, it adapts the single-image checkerboard context model from MLIC++ to the stereo setting by injecting inter-view priors through FSCT, replacing the costly autoregressive paradigm used in BiSIC and BCSIC. The mixed quantization strategy from Minnen & Singh and the efficient attention mechanism from Shen et al. are adopted as supporting components, but the core novelty lies in the frequency-aware stereo transfer and its integration into a fast checkerboard entropy model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in learned stereo compression: high-frequency detail loss from spatial-domain inter-view transfer and prohibitive coding latency from autoregressive entropy models. The ~48× latency reduction over BiSIC (1.62 s vs. 78.60 s) while simultaneously improving BD-Rate makes stereo compression more deployable in bandwidth-constrained applications like AR/VR and autonomous driving. Open questions include whether the frequency-band decomposition generalizes beyond two views to multi-view or video settings, and whether the simple average-pooling-based decomposition can be further refined for very high-resolution or high-dynamic-range content.
