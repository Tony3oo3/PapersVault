## Problem

Existing world models for robotic manipulation either require curated, environment-specific physics modeling, rely on domain-specific inductive biases (full observability, objectness priors, material specification), or produce photorealistic video without explicit action conditioning and physical consistency. No single pre-trained model can generalize across diverse in-the-wild scenes, heterogeneous embodiments, and manipulation tasks (rigid, deformable, articulated, tool use) from a single RGB-D capture without additional demonstrations or post-training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes one or a few calibrated RGB-D images (back-projected to a 3D scene point cloud with robot pixels masked via forward kinematics) and a horizon of H=10 joint-configuration commands, which are converted to dense 3D point flows over the gripper surfaces (300–500 points per gripper) using the robot's URDF. Output is a full-scene 3D point flow: predicted 3D positions for every scene point at each of the 10 timesteps, produced in a single batched forward pass with ~0.1 s latency. Because the input is a static point set, no external point tracker is needed at inference and point count may vary between calls.

## Method

POINTWORLD unifies state and action in a shared 3D point-flow representation: scene points are featurized with frozen DINOv3 features (projected to calibrated 2D views) and robot action points with temporal embeddings, then concatenated into a single point cloud processed by a PointTransformerV3 backbone (scaled from 50 M to 1 B parameters). A shared MLP head predicts per-point 3D displacements over the 10-step chunk in one forward pass. Training uses a weighted regression objective that reweights points by a soft movement likelihood (sigmoid of ground-truth displacement), adds aleatoric uncertainty regularization (predicted log-variance per point-step), and applies a Huber loss on 3D residuals to handle noisy real-world supervision. For deployment, the model is integrated with MPPI sampling-based MPC: K candidate end-effector trajectories are rolled out through POINTWORLD, scored by a pointwise goal cost plus control regularization, and the nominal trajectory is updated via exponentiated weights, enabling zero-shot manipulation on a physical Franka robot from a single in-the-wild RGB-D image.

## Results

On the DROID test set, the final POINTWORLD model (PTv3-1B backbone) achieves 0.0312 m ℓ₂ error on moving points and 0.0056 m on static points, improving over the GBND baseline (0.0390 / 0.0066) by ~20% on moving-point error. On BEHAVIOR-1K, in-domain held-out performance reaches 0.0087 m ℓ₂ mover. For zero-shot transfer to a held-out real-world lab (D→H), POINTWORLD achieves 0.0305 m, nearly matching a specialist trained from scratch on that lab (0.0293 m), and surpasses it (0.0271 m) after finetuning with only 5% of the original training steps (20× fewer updates).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the GBND (Graph-Based Neural Dynamics) model from Ai et al. [5], which serves as the starting point in the paper's scaling roadmap (Figure 3). POINTWORLD with PTv3-1B reduces ℓ₂ mover error from 0.0390 m (GBND) to 0.0312 m, a ~20% improvement, while also reducing static-point error from 0.0066 m to 0.0056 m. The gains come from a combination of backbone upgrade (PTv3 vs. graph networks), movement-weighted Huber loss with aleatoric uncertainty, frozen DINOv3 features, and scaling to 1B parameters. A second implicit comparison is against a 'From Scratch' specialist trained only on the held-out lab's data: POINTWORLD zero-shot (0.0305 m) is within 4% of the specialist (0.0293 m), and after finetuning with 20× fewer updates it surpasses the specialist (0.0271 m vs. 0.0293 m). Cross-domain zero-shot transfer remains challenging (D→B: 0.1460 m), but finetuning with 5% of training steps narrows the gap to 0.0107 m, well below the from-scratch specialist's 0.0293 m.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in learning-based dynamics models (graph neural dynamics, particle-based simulators) that require scene-specific modeling and embodiment-specific action spaces, by unifying state and action in a shared 3D point flow representation. Conceptually, it repurposes the 'next-token prediction' philosophy of language models to 3D interaction: given a static scene point cloud and a temporal sequence of robot surface points (derived from URDF forward kinematics), predict per-point displacements. The data pipeline leverages recent 3D vision advances (FoundationStereo for depth, VGGT for camera poses, CoTracker3 for point tracking) to extract 3D flow supervision from existing manipulation datasets, while the architecture builds on PointTransformerV3 with frozen DINOv3 features rather than designing a custom dynamics network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POINTWORLD demonstrates that a single pre-trained 3D world model can enable zero-shot, real-time (0.1 s) manipulation on a physical robot from a single in-the-wild RGB-D image, spanning rigid pushing, deformable manipulation, articulated objects, and tool use without task-specific demonstrations or finetuning. The open-sourced ~2M-trajectory / 500-hour 3D dynamics dataset and the rigorous scaling roadmap (backbone, objective, features, model size) provide a concrete recipe for the community to build on. Open questions include whether the 1-second prediction horizon suffices for long-horizon tasks, how the approach handles multi-contact and fluid interactions, and whether the zero-shot generalization holds for substantially different robot morphologies beyond the two embodiments studied.
