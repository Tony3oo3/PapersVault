## Problem

Monocular dynamic scene reconstruction suffers from two intertwined failures: SfM-based initialization (e.g., COLMAP) produces incomplete and entangled point clouds that miss dynamic regions, and existing methods compensate by relying on external dense motion priors (optical flow, point tracking) that propagate estimation errors into the reconstruction. The paper asks whether 3D motion can be learned purely from raw video without any external motion guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261688/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, plus per-pixel 3D coordinates and dynamic masks regressed by a geometry foundation model (MONST3R) on image pairs. It produces a time-varying 3D Gaussian scene with explicitly separated static and dynamic components, where static Gaussians are parameterized by spatial tri-plane features and dynamic Gaussians additionally use temporal planes to encode motion. Notably, no pre-computed optical flow or point trajectories are required at training or inference time.

## Method

ReFlow builds a complete canonical space via hierarchical coarse-to-fine alignment of MONST3R predictions across keyframes and local clips, then disentangles static and dynamic point clouds using per-frame dynamic masks. Static regions are modeled with spatial tri-plane features while dynamic regions add temporal planes, enabling region-specific motion constraints. The core novelty is a self-correction flow matching mechanism: Full Flow Matching synthesizes a 2D flow from the joint camera-scene motion between two frames and warps the source image (both real and rendered) to match the target, while Camera Flow Matching applies an analogous constraint restricted to static regions using camera-only motion. This creates a closed loop where inaccurate 3D motion produces misaligned warps that directly push the deformation fields toward the correct solution, eliminating the need for external flow or tracking supervision.

## Results

On the NVIDIA Monocular dataset (8 scenes), ReFlow achieves a mean PSNR of 28.20 dB, SSIM of 0.903, and LPIPS of 0.103, outperforming the strongest baseline MoDec-GS (26.63 dB) by 1.57 dB in PSNR. On the Nerfies-HyperNeRF benchmark, ReFlow reaches 25.97 dB PSNR / 0.674 SSIM, surpassing 4DGS (24.46 dB) by 1.51 dB. On the HyperNeRF subset, ReFlow attains 26.15 dB PSNR / 0.736 SSIM, exceeding 4DGS (25.16 dB) and MotionGS (24.80 dB) by 0.99 and 1.35 dB respectively. ReFlow wins on all three metrics across all evaluated scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReFlow outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. On NVIDIA Monocular, the closest competitor is MoDec-GS [29] (26.63 dB), which ReFlow surpasses by 1.57 dB PSNR and 0.024 SSIM; 4DGS [55] and Deformable-3DGS [62] trail by ~2.4 dB. The static 3DGS [28] baseline is far behind at 17.69 dB, confirming the difficulty of dynamic scenes. On Nerfies-HyperNeRF, ReFlow leads 4DGS [55] by 1.51 dB PSNR and 0.086 SSIM, while NeRF-based methods (T-NeRF, Nerfies, HyperNeRF, NSFF) all fall below 21 dB. On the HyperNeRF subset, ReFlow beats 4DGS [55] by 0.99 dB and MotionGS [68] by 1.35 dB, with SC-GS [21] and MoDec-GS [29] also trailing by over 1 dB. Notably, ReFlow achieves these gains without any external optical flow or point-tracking supervision, whereas MoDec-GS and MotionGS rely on such external motion priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReFlow builds directly on the 4DGS deformation-based Gaussian splatting framework [55] for its scene representation and baseline loss, and on MONST3R [65] as a geometry foundation model for canonical space initialization. Its key departure from prior dynamic reconstruction work (MotionGS, GaussianFlow, Deformable-3DGS, etc.) is the elimination of external dense motion guidance—optical flow or point tracking—replacing it with a self-correction flow matching mechanism that supervises 3D motion purely from raw video frame differences. The paper also addresses a structural gap in prior methods: COLMAP-based initialization, designed for static scenes, systematically misses dynamic regions, which ReFlow resolves through a hierarchical coarse-to-fine alignment strategy using MONST3R.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on pre-computed optical flow or tracking pipelines, ReFlow simplifies the reconstruction pipeline and eliminates a major source of error propagation that has plagued dynamic 4D reconstruction. The self-correction paradigm—using the video itself as motion supervision through warping-based photometric consistency—is conceptually clean and could influence how future methods handle motion learning. However, evaluation is limited to small-scale datasets (8 NVIDIA Monocular scenes, Nerfies, HyperNeRF), and the approach inherits 4DGS's reliance on deformation fields, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, highly deformable objects, and scenes with topological changes. Whether the self-correction signal remains sufficient under extreme motion or occlusion remains untested.
