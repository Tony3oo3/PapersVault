## Problem

Multimodal LLMs have unified reasoning over images, audio, and video, but extending this to brain imaging remains largely unexplored. Existing fMRI foundation models (BrainLM, Brain-JEPA) rely on neural-only objectives like masked prediction or contrastive learning, lack grounding in language, and require task-specific tuning. Moreover, no natural fMRI-text pairs exist, preventing direct multimodal alignment of brain activity with linguistic semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263045/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 4D resting-state fMRI (T×X×Y×Z) parcellated to 450 ROIs (Schaefer-400 cortical + Tian-Scale III subcortical), resampled to 160 time points at TR=2.0 s, along with natural-language instructions. The tokenizer maps this to a discrete token sequence of length (T/P)×N in the LLM's embedding space. Outputs span class labels, regression values (via linear probing or ordinal-bin discretization), free-form text descriptions, and predicted fMRI token sequences for temporal forecasting.

## Method

fMRI-LM uses a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 trains a ViT-based encoder with a vector quantizer to produce discrete fMRI tokens aligned to a frozen LLM's text-embedding space, using a joint loss combining reconstruction, domain-adversarial training (gradient reversal layer with a domain classifier), and SigLIP-style contrastive alignment against synthetic fMRI-text descriptors. Stage 2 fine-tunes a pretrained LLM (GPT-2 or Qwen3-0.6B) with three objectives: temporal next-step fMRI token prediction (F2F), fMRI-conditioned text generation (F2T), and standard language modeling (T2T), weighted at 0.1, 1.0, and 0.5 respectively. Stage 3 performs multi-task, multi-paradigm instruction tuning (single-QA, multi-QA, open-ended description) across seven datasets. The key enabling component is a large synthetic descriptor corpus that translates functional connectivity, functional gradients, ICA components, and graph-theoretical metrics into standardized natural-language captions, providing the linguistic supervision absent in natural fMRI data.

## Results

On classification tasks, fMRI-LM-B(Q) achieves the best accuracy across all five benchmarks: 94.45% on UKB-Sex, 83.04% on HCP-Sex, 85.27% on ADNI-AD, 78.57% on ADHD200, and 76.56% on ABIDE2. On regression tasks, fMRI-LM-B(Q) attains the lowest MAE on UKB Age (1.80), UKB Fluid Intelligence (1.46), HCP Fluid Composite (4.60), and HCP-A Fluid Composite (4.61), while fMRI-LM-B(G) leads on HCP-A Flanker (MAE 5.11, r=0.50). In the few-shot setting, fMRI-LM reaches performance comparable to full downstream training with only 4 labeled samples on fluid intelligence and ASD prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

fMRI-LM outperforms all five supervised baselines (BrainNetCNN, BrainGNN, BNT, FBNetGen, SWiFT) by a wide margin on every classification task, with the largest gaps on ADHD200 (+16–29% over supervised methods) and ABIDE2 (+14–24%). Against the three foundation-model baselines, fMRI-LM-B(Q) achieves the best accuracy on all five tasks, beating Brain-JEPA by 5.7% on UKB-Sex, 5.2% on HCP-Sex, 3.0% on ADNI-AD, 6.5% on ADHD200, and 19.1% on ABIDE2. The GPT-2 variant (fMRI-LM-B(G)) trails Brain-JEPA on ADNI-AD (77.92% vs. 82.26%) but surpasses it on all other tasks. On regression, fMRI-LM-B(Q) achieves the best MAE on four of six targets (UKB Age, UKB Fluid Intel, HCP Fluid Comp, HCP-A Fluid Comp), while Brain-JEPA retains a slight edge on UKB Age (1.69 vs. 1.80) and HCP Age (2.55 vs. 2.56). BrainMass, the strongest single-task foundation model, is outperformed by fMRI-LM on UKB-Sex (+2.6%), HCP-Sex (+7.7%), ADHD200 (+12.4%), and ABIDE2 (+17.8%), but leads on ADNI-AD AUC (83.35 vs. 81.02 for the Q variant).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EEG-LLM alignment paradigm from NeuroLM [14] to fMRI, adapting its temporal next-step prediction and domain-adversarial embedding alignment, while borrowing the ViT-based encoder architecture from Brain-JEPA [9] and the SigLIP-style contrastive loss [42] for cross-modal alignment. It departs from prior fMRI foundation models (BrainLM, Brain-JEPA) that remain confined to neural-only self-supervised objectives by introducing a synthetic fMRI-text descriptor corpus as linguistic supervision, and from task-fMRI decoding work (MindLLM, Umbrae) by targeting resting-state, task-independent brain activity without requiring stimulus-text pairs. The three-stage pipeline (tokenizer → LLM fine-tuning → multi-paradigm instruction tuning) is a new architectural composition, but each individual component draws directly from established techniques in domain adaptation, vector quantization, and multimodal LLM training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify resting-state fMRI with a language model through a scalable synthetic descriptor corpus, offering a practical pathway toward interpretable, multi-task brain modeling without task-specific architectures. The strong few-shot results (4-shot matching full training on fluid intelligence and ASD) and the ability to handle open-ended generation alongside structured prediction suggest a genuinely flexible foundation model for neuroimaging. However, the 'language alignment' is somewhat artificial—the synthetic text describes numerical features (connectivity values, gradient ranges) rather than true cognitive semantics—raising open questions about whether this grounding captures meaningful brain-language relationships or merely provides a convenient regularization signal. Limitations include underperformance on ADNI-AD relative to Brain-JEPA, weak strict zero-shot performance, and reliance on a single LLM backbone (GPT-2/Qwen3-0.6B) whose scale may constrain the ceiling of semantic understanding.
