## Problem

Medical VLMs rely on LLM tokenizers that decompose numbers into subword tokens (e.g., 42.5 → [4],[.],[5]), preventing the model from treating values as single numerical entities and making next-token prediction indifferent to magnitude errors. This limits clinical utility for tasks requiring precise quantitative outputs such as lesion size regression and bounding-box coordinate prediction, where even small numerical errors can alter diagnosis or treatment decisions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D CT volumes (resized to 144×144×164, clipped to [−1000, 1000] HU) alongside free-form text queries, and produces either a continuous numerical measurement (in mm or mm²), a 2D bounding-box coordinate pair, or a standard text response. Numerical values are represented internally as a single [NUM] token whose embedding encodes the value via rotation, so the autoregressive decoder emits one token per number rather than multiple subword tokens, and the decoded value is re-encoded as input for the next generation step.

## Method

RNED encodes a scalar m by applying a block-diagonal rotation matrix R_d(mΘ) — with d/2 independent 2D rotations at geometrically spaced frequencies θ_j = B^(−2j/d) — to a dedicated [NUM] token embedding, making the representation norm-preserving, ordinal-preserving, and compatible with pretrained LayerNorm/RMSNorm layers. For decoding, the paper derives that the dot product between the rotated embedding and the base [NUM] embedding decomposes into a weighted sum of cosines Σ(1/θ_j)^p ‖x_[NUM],j‖² cos(mθ_j), and defines a score-matching objective: the model is trained to minimize the squared difference between its output embedding's score and a precomputed target score for the ground-truth value, then at inference the predicted value is recovered by nearest-neighbor lookup over a candidate set. Training combines standard cross-entropy next-token prediction with an MSE loss on this score, applied only at [NUM] positions, and the entire scheme is plug-and-play with existing VLM architectures (LLaVA, Llama-3-8B, Qwen2.5-VL) via LoRA fine-tuning without degrading text generation quality.

## Results

On the Opport-CT dataset, RNED achieves a single-value MAE of 4.72 mm and R² of 0.568, and a double-value MAE of 449.23 mm² and R² of 0.320, with an 81.8% format success rate—substantially outperforming the best baseline (Standard Tokenization: MAE 5.53, R² 0.338, 55.7% success). On CT-RATE, fine-tuning CT-CHAT with RNED for one epoch improves R² from 0.370 to 0.592 (single) and from 0.164 to 0.608 (double), with success rate rising from 85% to 90%. On MedSeq-Bench, MedSeq-Grounder+RNED achieves an average IoU of 74.21 and Acc@5 of 82.33, surpassing the SOTA MedSeq-Grounder (72.55 IoU, 79.71 Acc@5) by 1.66% and 2.62% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNED outperforms all numerical-encoding baselines on Opport-CT: it reduces single-value MAE from 5.53 (Standard Tokenization) and 6.71 (xVal) to 4.72, and lifts R² from 0.338 and 0.351 to 0.568, while also achieving the highest format success rate (81.8% vs. 55.7–77.4% for baselines). The prompt-based methods (p10, p100) and Abacus embedding perform worse than plain standard tokenization on R², suggesting that string-format tricks and digit-reversal embeddings do not transfer well to mixed text-number medical prompts. On CT-RATE, RNED fine-tuning of CT-CHAT dramatically improves R² (0.370→0.592 single, 0.164→0.608 double) and success rate (85%→90%), though double-value MAE increases slightly (588.24→622.58 mm²), indicating a modest trade-off in absolute error for the double-value case. On MedSeq-Bench, MedSeq-Grounder+RNED beats the SOTA MedSeq-Grounder on 5 of 8 grounding tasks (notably Multi-view IoU 59.26 vs. 55.03 and Object Tracking IoU 63.15 vs. 62.10) but trails on the two Image Difference Grounding sub-tasks (82.83 vs. 83.29 and 81.13 vs. 83.72 IoU), where the baseline already performs near-saturation. Overall, RNED consistently wins on the quantitative regression metrics (MAE, R²) and format reliability, with the only losses being marginal IoU drops on already-saturated grounding sub-tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RNED draws its core mathematical machinery from Rotary Position Encoding (RoPE), repurposing the norm-preserving rotation structure to encode scalar values rather than positional indices. It departs from xVal, the closest prior numerical-encoding method, by avoiding norm changes that conflict with LayerNorm in modern LLMs, and from prompt-based schemes (p10/p100, Abacus) that inflate prediction length and degrade accuracy. The score-matching decoder is a new contribution relative to xVal's regression head, motivated by the need for robustness to stochastic context noise during autoregressive generation. The work is positioned within the medical VLM lineage (LLaVA-Med, CT-CHAT, MedSeq-Grounder) but targets the quantitative-prediction gap that those models leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Quantitative reliability is a prerequisite for clinical deployment of medical VLMs, yet current architectures treat numbers as arbitrary token sequences with no ordinal structure; RNED offers a drop-in, LoRA-compatible fix that requires no architectural changes and no extra trainable parameters for decoding. The paper's ablation showing that prompt-based numerical formatting (p10/p100) actually underperforms standard tokenization is a useful negative result for practitioners. Open questions include extending the 1D scalar encoding to 2D/3D coordinate tuples (bounding boxes, volumetric measurements), validating across additional modalities (MRI, pathology, ultrasound), and integrating the score-matching objective into reinforcement-learning reward design as the authors note.
