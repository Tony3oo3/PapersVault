## Problem

Current vision-language models for autonomous driving operate as cognitively stateless agents, processing each frame as an isolated snapshot without maintaining a persistent internal representation over time. This lack of 'cognitive inertia' manifests as decision jitter and an inability to execute complex multi-step maneuvers, and no existing dataset provides the continuous, evolving causal narrative needed to train agents that learn temporal coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261777/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes concurrent multi-view camera video streams (not sequential single-view frames), ego-motion parameters (position, velocity, time delta), and natural language driving commands. Outputs are dual-structured: a 3-second driving trajectory plus 3D object bounding boxes and lane geometry (structured), alongside a frame-by-frame narrative reasoning text that evolves causally across the sequence (unstructured). The narrative annotations in the training data are continuous stories of persistent intent rather than disconnected per-frame captions, verified against ground-truth trajectories via Future History Alignment.

## Method

CogDriver-Agent builds on a frozen LLaVA v1.5 VLM with an EVA-02-L vision encoder feeding a QFormer whose core innovation is the Temporal Coherence Module (TCM). The TCM maintains a sparse temporal memory through three stages: geometric propagation (warping historical 3D queries via ego-motion), motion-conditioned state refinement (affine transforms parameterized by the full motion context to modulate positional and context embeddings), and evidence-based fusion (self-attention for state reconciliation followed by cross-attention with spatially-guided keys to ground queries in current visual evidence). Training uses a composite loss combining Focal Loss and L1 regression for 3D detection/lane tasks with autoregressive cross-entropy for the LLM, with spatiotemporal knowledge distillation from the narrative structure of CogDriver-Data explicitly teaching decision coherence. The data pipeline itself is novel: a Multi-View Spatiotemporal MLLM with Conv3D and window attention processes all camera views concurrently, and a Cognitive Inertia Injection mechanism conditions the annotator on structured rules to produce causally-linked narratives rather than per-frame descriptions.

## Results

On Bench2Drive (closed-loop), CogDriver-Agent achieves a Driving Score of 78.21 and Success Rate of 56.93%, representing a 22% and 63% relative improvement over the previous best (DriveAdapter: 64.22 / 33.08%). On nuScenes (open-loop), it attains the lowest average L2 distance of 0.34 m and the best infraction rate of 3.18%, while its collision rate of 0.40% is slightly higher than Senna (0.12%) and DriveVLM (0.27%). On the VQA benchmarks, CogDriver-Agent yields a 37.6% relative CIDEr gain and 224% BLEU-4 gain over the best open-source MLLM baseline on CogDriver-nuScenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Bench2Drive, CogDriver-Agent decisively outperforms all 11 baselines on both Driving Score (78.21 vs. 64.22 for DriveAdapter, the runner-up) and Success Rate (56.93% vs. 35.01% for DriveTransformer), with the gap over the next-best method exceeding 14 points in Driving Score. On open-loop L2, it is essentially tied with DriveTransformer (0.63 vs. 0.62 m) and well ahead of VAD (1.25 m) and UniAD-Base (0.73 m). On nuScenes open-loop, CogDriver-Agent achieves the best average L2 (0.34 m) and infraction rate (3.18%), but trails Senna (0.12%) and DriveVLM (0.27%) on collision rate, suggesting a slight safety trade-off. In the VQA setting, it surpasses all open-source MLLMs by large margins (e.g., 224% BLEU-4 over the best baseline on CogDriver-nuScenes), confirming that the narrative-annotation training signal transfers to language generation. For inference speed, CogDriver-Agent processes 3410.81 input tokens/s versus 970.94 for Qwen2.5-VL 32B, a 3.5× throughput advantage that meets real-time driving latency constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA v1.5 as its VLM backbone and EVA-02 as its vision encoder, but departs sharply from prior VLM-based driving systems (DriveVLM, Senna, CarLLaVA) that the authors characterize as stateless stimulus-response mechanisms. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of 'cognitive inertia'—the persistence of intent over time—as a missing property in current end-to-end driving VLMs, addressed architecturally through a Temporal Coherence Module with sparse temporal memory and a motion-conditioned state refinement scheme. On the data side, CogDriver-Data extends the line of language-annotated driving datasets (BDD-X, DriveLM, CoVLA, OmniDrive) by introducing continuous narrative annotations generated via a multi-view spatiotemporal MLLM, rather than the per-frame rationales that prior works provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22% Driving Score and 63% Success Rate gains on Bench2Drive are among the largest reported for closed-loop driving, suggesting that explicit temporal state maintenance in VLM-based planners can yield substantial practical benefits over reactive architectures. The CogDriver-Data datasets, with their narrative temporal annotations, provide a new training signal that prior datasets lack, potentially enabling a broader class of temporally coherent agents beyond driving. However, the open-loop L2 improvement is marginal (0.63 vs. 0.62), the VQA gains are measured on the authors' own benchmark, and the simulation-only closed-loop evaluation leaves open whether these coherence benefits transfer to real-world deployment. A key open question is whether the sparse temporal memory and narrative distillation approach scales to longer horizons and more complex multi-agent interactions without prohibitive memory overhead.
