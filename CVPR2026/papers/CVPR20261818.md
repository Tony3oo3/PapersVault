## Problem

Generating high-fidelity human-product images requires faithful preservation of fine-grained product details (shapes, patterns, textures, small text), but existing reference-based inpainting methods struggle because diffusion-based denoising tends to average or hallucinate content rather than enforce strict spatial and appearance alignment. Additionally, no large-scale diverse dataset exists specifically for this task, and coarse latent-space supervision alone cannot provide the pixel-level precision needed for detail preservation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a concise text prompt, a masked human image (with the product region specifically masked out), and a product reference image, all processed at 1024×576 resolution, and produces a single high-fidelity human-product image where the product is seamlessly composited into the masked region. The product reference image is consumed in two forms: as a full VAE-encoded token sequence for global appearance conditioning and as a high-frequency map (extracted via DFT-based high-pass filtering) for fine-grained detail enhancement. The output must faithfully preserve intricate details including small text, logos, and patterns even in small mask regions.

## Method

HiFi-Inpaint builds on the EUX.1-Dev MMDiT architecture, introducing a token merging mechanism that concatenates VAE-encoded tokens of the masked human image, product reference image, and noisy ground-truth visual tokens for joint dual-stream DiT processing. The key architectural innovation is Shared Enhancement Attention (SEA), which for each dual-stream block runs a parallel branch on high-frequency map tokens (obtained by DFT high-pass filtering of the product image) using shared parameters, then adds the result back to the main branch with a learnable scalar weight masked to the inpainting region. Training employs a Detail-Aware Loss (DAL) computing L2 distance on high-frequency maps restricted to the masked region in pixel space, complementing the standard latent-space MSE loss under flow matching. The model is trained on a newly constructed dataset, HP-Image-40K (43,632 samples), generated via a self-synthesis pipeline using EUX.1-Dev diptych prompts followed by CLIP-based semantic filtering and InternVL-based textual consistency filtering.

## Results

On the HP-Image-40K test set (1,000 samples), HiFi-Inpaint achieves the best visual consistency scores: CLIP-I 95.0%, DINO 91.9%, SSIM 63.4%, and SSIM-HF 42.9%, outperforming all four baselines. It also attains the highest Q-Align-IQ (4.36) and competitive CLIP-T (36.1%) and LAION-Aes (4.40). The user study confirms these gains, with HiFi-Inpaint receiving 36.4% preference for text alignment, 41.5% for visual consistency, and 39.5% for generation quality—substantially ahead of the next-best method (Insert Anything at 24.9%/21.0%/21.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiFi-Inpaint decisively outperforms Insert Anything [44] on all visual consistency metrics (CLIP-I 95.0 vs 94.1, DINO 91.9 vs 89.8, SSIM 63.4 vs 62.1, SSIM-HF 42.9 vs 40.0) and on Q-Align-IQ (4.36 vs 3.89), confirming superior detail preservation. Against ACE++ [33], the gains are even larger in SSIM-HF (42.9 vs 37.2) and Q-Align-IQ (4.36 vs 4.00), while CLIP-I and DINO improvements are more modest (95.0 vs 93.1, 91.9 vs 90.7). FLUX-Kontext [7] is the only method that beats HiFi-Inpaint on CLIP-T (36.6 vs 36.1) and LAION-Aes (4.54 vs 4.40), but it lags dramatically on visual consistency (CLIP-I 82.5, DINO 63.1, SSIM-HF 32.0), indicating it struggles to faithfully transfer product details into the masked region. Paint-by-Example [53] is clearly outdated, trailing HiFi-Inpaint by 25.9 points on CLIP-I and 8.0 points on SSIM-HF. In the user study, HiFi-Inpaint leads all three criteria by at least 11 percentage points over the runner-up, with the largest margin in visual consistency (41.5% vs 24.9% for Insert Anything).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ELUX.1-Dev MMDiT architecture [6] and the token-merging multi-condition mechanism from OmniControl [45], repurposing them for a specialized reference-based inpainting setting. It departs from prior reference-based inpainting methods (Paint-by-Example, ACE++, Insert Anything) by introducing two targeted innovations: Shared Enhancement Attention, which injects high-frequency map tokens into dual-stream DiT blocks via parameter sharing, and Detail-Aware Loss, which adds pixel-level high-frequency supervision on top of standard latent-space MSE. The conceptual departure is treating frequency-domain detail preservation as a first-class architectural and training objective rather than an emergent property of the denoising process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap in E-commerce and advertising image generation: existing reference-based inpainting methods hallucinate or blur fine-grained product details (text, logos, textures) that are commercially essential. The HP-Image-40K dataset and the SEA/DAL techniques provide a reusable recipe for detail-preserving inpainting that could transfer to other product-centric applications. Open questions include whether the self-synthesis data pipeline captures sufficient real-world diversity, whether the method generalizes beyond the human-product setting, and whether the frequency-domain approach scales to video generation as the authors note as future work.
