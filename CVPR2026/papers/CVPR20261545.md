## Problem

Current video understanding pipelines pair strong reasoners with a fixed, one-shot observation stage: frames are sampled uniformly or via static heuristics before reasoning begins, so the agent cannot shift focus, reallocate its frame budget, or revisit raw video as intermediate hypotheses evolve. This disconnect between reasoning and perception causes sparse but decisive events to be dropped or drowned in redundancy, and prevents stepwise resolution of complex long-video questions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261545/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a raw video (with metadata such as duration and camera perspective) and a natural-language query. Rather than consuming a pre-extracted fixed frame sequence, the agent dynamically selects which temporal intervals to inspect and at what sampling density per turn, subject to per-tool frame budgets (180 frames for Scan Search, 32 for Segment Focus, 128 for Stitched Verify) and a global cap of 20 tool invocations. The output is a final text answer accompanied by a full reasoning trace of parameterized observation plans and the evidence returned at each step.

## Method

LensWalk organizes inference as an iterative reason-plan-observe loop in which an LLM Reasoner formulates, at each step, a fully specified observation plan (tool choice, guiding sub-question, temporal scope, and sampling density) that is executed by a VLM Observer over the raw video. Three complementary tools enable distinct observation strategies: Scan Search partitions a broad interval into parallel slices for cue discovery, Segment Focus applies dense sampling to a single interval for fine-grained fact extraction, and Stitched Verify consolidates frames from multiple non-contiguous segments into one context for cross-segment verification. Two lightweight grounding mechanisms maintain multi-turn coherence: Timestamp Anchors are inserted directly into the Observer's frame sequence to ground evidence with explicit temporal references, and a Subject Memory Table tracks persistent entities and their attributes across rounds to ensure consistent entity reference without re-reading long histories. The framework requires no model fine-tuning and operates as a plug-and-play wrapper around any Reasoner–Observer pair, with the Reasoner's evolving reasoning state directly driving where and how densely the video is observed.

## Results

On the long-video benchmarks, the self-play configuration LensWalk(o3) achieves 68.6 on LVBench, 70.6 on LongVideoBench (long), 71.4 on Video-MME (long, w/o sub), and 74.8 on EgoSchema, surpassing the strongest single-model baselines (o3: 57.1/60.6/64.7/63.2; GPT-5: 59.8/61.8/68.4/73.8) by 7–11.5 points. On the reasoning-focused Video-MMMU benchmark, LensWalk(o3) reaches 78.33 overall accuracy (MC 79.2, Perception 81.33, Comprehension 81.33, Adaptation 72.33), improving over o3 alone (75.44) by nearly 3 points. The framework is plug-and-play: pairing o3 as Reasoner with GPT-4.1 as Observer yields 66.8/69.9/70.0/74.8, and GPT-5 as a unified model still gains 7.1 points over its single-forward baseline on LVBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LensWalk(o3) substantially outperforms its underlying single-model baselines: o3 alone scores 57.1/60.6/64.7/63.2 on LVBench/LongVideoBench/Video-MME/EgoSchema, while LensWalk(o3) lifts these to 68.6/70.6/71.4/74.8 (+11.5/+10.0/+6.7/+11.6). Against GPT-5 (59.8/61.8/68.4/73.8), LensWalk(GPT-5) gains +7.1/+7.0/+0.8/+0.8 respectively. Among agentic baselines, LensWalk(o3) beats MR.Video by +7.8 on LVBench and +9.6 on Video-MME, and surpasses Deep Video Discovery on LongVideoBench (+2.0) and Video-MME (+4.1), though it trails DVD on LVBench (68.6 vs 74.2) and EgoSchema (74.8 vs 76.6). On Video-MMMU, LensWalk(o3) achieves 78.33 overall, exceeding o3 alone (75.44) by 2.89 points and GPT-4.1 (67.44) by 10.89 points. The open-source Reasoner Qwen3-235B-A22B provides smaller gains (+0.1 to +4.3 depending on Observer), confirming that Reasoner cognitive strength is the dominant factor in observation-plan quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LensWalk departs from three established lines of work on long-video understanding: (1) single-shot context selection methods (keyframe sampling, token pruning) that fix the visible context before reasoning begins; (2) retrieval-based agentic pipelines (VideoAgent, MR.Video, DVD) that let an LLM query pre-computed captions or clip embeddings but cannot control temporal scope or sampling density of fresh observations; and (3) test-time scaling approaches that either train models for longer chains of thought or wrap fixed observation stages in multi-turn workflows. The key conceptual departure is treating *how to observe*—temporal interval, frame density, segment composition—as a first-class decision variable in the reasoning loop, rather than a fixed preprocessing step or a limited menu of coarse tool options. The architecture synthesizes ideas from active perception, multimodal tool-use agents, and agentic search into a unified reason-plan-observe cycle with parameterized observation tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a plug-and-play framework requiring no fine-tuning, LensWalk offers a practical path to improving any existing VLM pipeline on long-video tasks, with the 'self-play' configuration (single model as both Reasoner and Observer) providing a low-cost deployment option. The adaptive budget allocation—resolving easy queries in one turn while scaling observation depth for genuinely uncertain cases—addresses a real efficiency concern that prior agentic methods (consuming millions of tokens in pre-scanning) do not. Open questions include whether the lightweight Subject Memory Table scales to videos with hundreds of interacting entities, whether the framework can be trained to learn better observation planning policies over time, and how it performs on tasks requiring sub-second temporal precision beyond what frame-level sampling can capture.
