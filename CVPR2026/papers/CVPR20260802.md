## Problem

Pathology text-to-image generation is hindered by three coupled bottlenecks: scarcity of large, high-quality image-text corpora; reliance on non-semantic controls (masks, reference images) rather than precise text-based semantic conditioning; and terminological heterogeneity, where institution-specific phrasing for the same diagnostic concept defeats general-purpose text encoders. As a result, existing generators optimize for pixel-level appearance rather than diagnosis-grounded morphology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260802/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form pathology text prompt as input and generates a 384×384 histopathology image patch (corresponding to 20× magnification). The prompt is consumed through three parallel pathways: the raw token sequence, learnable queries appended to a frozen 7B pathology MLLM, and a CONCH-encoded query vector for prototype retrieval. Generation occurs in the latent space of a Stable Diffusion 3 VAE (8× downsampling), so the DiT operates on compressed latent representations rather than raw pixels.

## Method

UNIPATH couples a frozen Patho-R1 7B pathology MLLM (understanding) with a 0.6B PixArt-α-derived DiT (generation) through a trainable Multi-Stream Control (MSC) module that fuses three conditional streams into a single composite sequence injected via cross-attention. The High-Level Semantics stream appends 64 learnable queries to the prompt, forcing the frozen MLLM to distill paraphrase-invariant Diagnostic Semantic Tokens; the Raw-Text stream preserves literal prompt intent; and the Prototype stream retrieves up to 16 morphology primitives from an 8K frozen bank using hybrid global-semantic (CONCH cosine) and local fine-grained (inverted-index keyword) retrieval. The DiT is trained with a Flow Matching objective (linear interpolation between Gaussian noise and VAE-encoded ground truth) in a two-stage schedule: 10K steps on 2.65M pairs for semantic alignment, then 500 steps on a 50K expert-verified subset for high-fidelity fine-tuning.

## Results

On the 10K high-quality test set, UNIPATH achieves a Patho-FID of 80.86, a 50.5% relative reduction over the second-best (PixCell, 163.44), and the best standard FID (25.70), KID (0.081), and LPIPS (0.570) among all baselines. For text-image alignment, UNIPATH scores 0.348 CLIP-Score (second only to Show-o2's 0.357) and achieves SOTA on image-to-image retrieval (Recall@50: 11.92, mAP@50: 4.67). In the fine-grained semantic control evaluation (Train-on-Synth, Test-on-Real), UNIPATH-Aug reaches 98.7% of the real-image AUC for hemorrhage detection and 97.9% for cytology type, nearly closing the gap to the Real2Real ceiling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIPATH decisively outperforms all general-purpose T2I baselines (SD1.5, SDXL, PixArt-α, BLIP3o) on every fidelity and alignment metric; for example, its Patho-FID of 80.86 is 3.2× lower than BLIP3o's 358.61. Against the strongest unified model Show-o2, UNIPATH wins on all fidelity metrics (FID 25.70 vs. 98.82; Patho-FID 80.86 vs. 184.19) and on I2I retrieval, but Show-o2 edges ahead on CLIP-Score (0.357 vs. 0.348) and T2I retrieval (Recall@50: 15.26 vs. 14.08). Among pathology-specific models, UNIPATH surpasses PixCell (the closest competitor on fidelity) by 50.5% on Patho-FID (80.86 vs. 163.44) and by a wide margin on standard FID (25.70 vs. 80.74); it also dominates PathLDM and UniMedVL across all reported metrics. In the fine-grained semantic control task, UNIPATH-Aug reaches 98.7% of the Real2Real AUC for hemorrhage, outperforming the next-best model (Show-o2 at 71.72% Wgt.AUC) by a clear margin. In the downstream few-shot augmentation experiment on Kather-CRC-2016, UNIPATH is the only method (alongside Show-o2) that yields positive F1 gains at every K-shot setting, with the largest gains in the extreme few-shot regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIPATH sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) pathology generative models that have relied on non-semantic controls (segmentation masks in PathDiff, reference images in PixCell, topology constraints in ToPoFM) or coarse single-cancer text conditioning (PathLDM); (2) unified understanding-and-generation models from general AI (BLIP3o, Show-o2, BAGEL) that couple a VLM with a generator but lack domain-specific semantic normalization; and (3) the DiT/Flow Matching generation stack (PixArt-α, LDM, rectified flow) that provides the backbone. The paper's key departure is the Multi-Stream Control module, which uses learnable queries into a frozen pathology MLLM (Patho-R1) to extract paraphrase-robust Diagnostic Semantic Tokens and a non-parametric prototype bank for component-level morphological control—neither of which appears in prior pathology generators or general unified models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: pathology generators have been optimizing pixel realism without diagnostic grounding, making them unreliable for augmentation or hypothesis testing. The 2.65M corpus, 68K refined subset, and four-tier evaluation hierarchy (especially the Train-on-Synth/Test-on-Real protocol) provide infrastructure the field lacked. Open questions include whether the frozen-MLLM approach scales to new cancer types without retraining, whether the 8K prototype bank suffices for rare morphologies, and whether the 384×384 patch-level scope can extend to gigapixel WSI-level generation. The reliance on LLM-generated captions (Gemini-2.5 Pro, GPT-5) for the 68K subset also raises questions about systematic annotation bias.
