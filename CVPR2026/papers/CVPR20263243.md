## Problem

Existing drift compensation methods in exemplar-free class-incremental learning assume uniform semantic distributions and uniform semantic shifts across classes, which is unrealistic for random data streams. This causes biased compensation for low-frequency semantics (intra-task non-uniformity) and inappropriate calibration for old classes with large semantic gaps to new tasks (inter-task non-uniformity), degrading the reliability of stored prototypes over long incremental sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263243/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images at standard resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-Subset) and outputs a class label via nearest-class-mean (NCM) inference against compensated prototypes. No raw samples from past tasks are stored; only d-dimensional feature prototypes are retained and updated per task. The module is plug-and-play, requiring only the old and new backbone feature outputs on current-task data to compute the drift estimators, making it composable with any existing EFCIL baseline.

## Method

Dual-E decouples drift compensation into two complementary estimators. The Mixture-of-Experts Estimator (MoE-E) partitions augmented training data into K=4 clusters via K-means in the old representation space and trains one linear expert per cluster to capture local semantic shift patterns, with rotation-based pixel fusion augmenting data so each expert receives sufficient samples for a one-epoch analytical (closed-form) update. The Low-Rank Estimator (LoR-E) learns a rank-r (r=40) global transfer matrix via alternating analytical updates, augmented with a historical-preservation regularizer that anchors compensated prototypes to their original positions to prevent over-adjustment. At inference, each old-class prototype is compensated by a weighted fusion of both estimators, where the weight β_c is the maximum cosine similarity between the prototype and the MoE-E cluster centers—prioritizing local compensation for semantically similar classes and global compensation for those with large semantic gaps to the new task.

## Results

On the 20-task split, Dual-E achieves 55.10% A_inc on CIFAR-100, 44.36% on TinyImageNet, and 59.89% on ImageNet-Subset, outperforming the runner-up (LDC or DP) by 2.28%, 1.66%, and 2.08% respectively. Under the more challenging 50-task setting, Dual-E reaches 31.66% A_inc on TinyImageNet and 40.13% on ImageNet-1K, surpassing DP by 1.42% and 1.25%. On fine-grained benchmarks, Dual-E attains 54.57% A_inc on CUB-200 (10 tasks) and 50.77% on Stanford Cars (14 tasks), leading all drift-compensation baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-E consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and task split. The most competitive comparisons are against the drift-compensation methods LDC (ECCV 2024) and DP (ICML 2025): on the 20-task ImageNet-Subset split, Dual-E gains 2.98% A_inc over LDC (59.89 vs 56.91) and 4.55% over DP (59.89 vs 55.34). Non-drift-compensation EFCIL methods (PASS, NAPA-VQ, FKSR, FCS) trail by 10–20+ points in A_inc, confirming that feature drift is the dominant failure mode. The gap widens with sequence length: under the 50-task setting on ImageNet-1K, Dual-E leads DP by 1.25% A_inc (40.13 vs 38.88) and LDC by 3.15% (40.13 vs 36.98). On fine-grained CUB-200 (10 tasks), Dual-E edges out DP by 1.47% A_inc (54.57 vs 53.10), and on Stanford Cars (14 tasks) it leads ADC by 0.89% (50.77 vs 49.88). When plugged into existing EFCIL frameworks (PASS, NAPA-VQ), Dual-E provides the largest accuracy gains among all drift-compensation options tested (e.g., +7.02% A_inc over PASS baseline on TinyImageNet 20-task).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the drift compensation line of work initiated by SDC, ADC, LDC, and DP, all of which learn a transfer function to align old class prototypes with the evolving feature space in exemplar-free CIL. The key departure is identifying and addressing two specific non-uniformity assumptions (intra-task non-uniform semantic distributions and inter-task non-uniform semantic shifts) that prior methods implicitly ignore. Rather than a single unified linear mapping, Dual-E decouples the problem into a mixture-of-experts estimator for local shifts and a low-rank estimator for global shifts, fused at inference time based on semantic similarity. The method is designed as a plug-in module compatible with existing EFCIL frameworks, building directly on LDC's analytical-solution paradigm for efficient one-epoch updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By challenging the uniformity assumption in drift compensation, this work provides a more realistic treatment of semantic shifts under random class ordering, which is the practical setting for most deployment scenarios. The plug-in design and closed-form updates make it broadly adoptable without retraining pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to NCM-based inference with ResNet-18 backbones, and it remains open whether the dual-estimator decomposition generalizes to transformer backbones, logit-based classifiers, or settings where the feature space undergoes more radical structural changes. The method also inherits the cold-start limitation of exemplar-free approaches and does not address the fundamental tension between plasticity and stability beyond prototype correction.
