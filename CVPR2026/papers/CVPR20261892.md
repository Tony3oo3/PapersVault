## Problem

Existing generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods estimate depth probability using only a single cross-view warp, which underutilizes rich multi-view geometric cues and produces unreliable, coarse depth maps. Since Gaussian means are obtained by unprojecting this depth map, inaccurate depth directly degrades reconstruction quality. The paper asks how to iteratively incorporate multiple warp operations to progressively boost depth probability estimation and refine depth candidates for accurate Gaussian mean prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261892/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two sparse-view RGB images at 256×256 resolution along with their camera projection matrices (intrinsic and extrinsic parameters from SfM) as input. It outputs per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters (mean, opacity, covariance, color) that are rasterized for novel view synthesis, with the depth map serving as an intermediate quantity from which Gaussian means are unprojected. The model uses a pretrained Depth Anything V2 backbone for feature extraction and is evaluated by rendering three novel target views per scene.

## Method

IDESplat introduces a Warp-Index Epipolar Attention (WIEA) mechanism that stores only warp indices rather than full warped features, enabling memory-efficient similarity computation via Sparse Matrix Multiplication. A Depth Probability Boosting Unit (DPBU) stacks multiple WIEA layers and combines their epipolar attention maps multiplicatively (element-wise product with row-wise normalization), so depth candidates with consistently high similarity across warps are amplified while noisy ones are suppressed. The iterative depth estimation process cascades multiple DPBUs, progressively narrowing the depth search range around the previous estimate (interval shrinks as 1/n) while increasing feature resolution from 1/4 to full 256×256, updating the depth map additively at each step. A Gaussian Focused Module handles the remaining Gaussian parameters by reusing the previous layer's correlation map to guide sparse attention, retaining only the top-half weights per row to filter irrelevant Gaussian tokens. The full model uses 37.6M parameters with MSE and LPIPS losses, trained for 300K iterations on 8 RTX 4090 GPUs.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, IDESplat achieves 27.80 dB PSNR, 0.893 SSIM, and 0.108 LPIPS, setting new state-of-the-art across all three metrics while using only 37.6M parameters (10.7% of DepthSplat's 354M). On ACID, it reaches 28.94 dB PSNR, outperforming MonoSplat by 0.31 dB. In cross-dataset transfer (RE10K→DTU), IDESplat gains 2.95 dB PSNR over DepthSplat (18.33 vs. 15.38), demonstrating strong generalization. On ScanNet depth estimation, IDESplat achieves the best Abs Rel (0.039) and RMSE (0.116) among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDESplat outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric for both RE10K and ACID. Against the strongest prior method DepthSplat [47], IDESplat gains 0.33 dB PSNR on RE10K (27.80 vs. 27.47) while using only 10.7% of its parameters (37.6M vs. 354M) and 70% of its memory (2336M vs. 3342M). Compared to MonoSplat [21], which has a similar parameter count (30.3M), IDESplat improves PSNR by 1.12 dB on RE10K and 0.31 dB on ACID. Against MVSplat [5] and PixelSplat [2], the gains are 1.41 dB and 1.71 dB PSNR respectively on RE10K. In cross-dataset transfer (RE10K→DTU), IDESplat achieves 18.33 dB PSNR, surpassing DepthSplat by 2.95 dB and MonoSplat by 3.08 dB, confirming that the iterative depth probability boosting strategy learns robust cross-view geometry rather than dataset-specific features.

## Relation to Prior Work

IDESplat extends the generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting framework established by MVSplat and DepthSplat, which both rely on a single cross-view warp to estimate depth probability for Gaussian mean prediction. The key departure is making this warp-based depth estimation iterative: a Depth Probability Boosting Unit multiplicatively fuses multiple epipolar attention maps from cascaded warps, and the depth search range is progressively narrowed across iterations. The Gaussian Focused Module for remaining parameters draws on progressive focused attention ideas from super-resolution work, while the overall iterative coarse-to-fine strategy is inspired by optical flow estimation (RAFT, PWC-Net) and iterative multi-view stereo methods (IterMVS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA reconstruction quality with dramatically fewer resources—10.7% of DepthSplat's parameters and 70% of its memory—making high-quality generalizable 3DGS more accessible. The strong cross-dataset generalization (2.95 dB gain on DTU, outperforming methods trained directly on ACID) suggests the iterative depth boosting captures more transferable geometric structure. However, the method still requires known camera poses and has slightly higher inference time than DepthSplat, leaving open the question of whether a pose-free variant can maintain these gains. The multiplicative fusion of attention maps is a clean but relatively straightforward extension of ideas already present in iterative MVS and optical flow, so the broader methodological impact is moderate.
