## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods treat modality and class as independent factors, producing fragmented specialized models per modality combination with excessive memory overhead. Reconstruction-based multi-class approaches that attempt unification rely on shared decoding paths, which cannot disentangle heterogeneous domain distributions, leading to distorted normality boundaries, domain interference, and high false alarm rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263890/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts arbitrary combinations of modalities (RGB, depth, surface normal, infrared, 3D point clouds, medical images) at 256×256 resolution, with an input embedding layer padding all inputs to a unified channel dimension C to support flexible modality pairing. It outputs a pixel-level anomaly localization map computed as the decompression discrepancy between uni-modal features and their original counterparts, plus an image-level score derived from the top 0.1% of localization values. A pre-trained WideResNet50 prior generator provides domain-specific priors that guide the decompression process at inference time.

## Method

UniMMAD follows a 'general → specific' paradigm: a general multi-modal encoder with a Feature Compression Module (hierarchical multi-scale bottleneck using parallel 1×1, 3×3, 5×5 convolutions) compresses arbitrary modality combinations into compact, anomaly-suppressed latent features. A Cross Mixture-of-Experts (C-MoE) decoder then decompresses these general features into modality- and class-specific reconstructions via cross-condition routing, where domain priors serve as queries and general features as keys/values to dynamically select top-K experts. To reduce parameter overhead by ~75%, each routed expert (MoE-Leader) is a weighted composition of shared base experts (MoE-in-MoE), and grouped dynamic filtering executes all activated experts within a single group convolution for parallel inference. Training optimizes a decompression consistency loss with focal modulation (γ=2) plus an annealed load-balancing loss, and the framework supports continual learning by fine-tuning less than 10% of parameters (MoE-leaders, router, aggregation) with 1% replay of prior data.

## Results

UniMMAD achieves state-of-the-art image-level AUC_I across all five multi-scene benchmarks: 92.53 on MVTec-3D, 85.57 on Eyecandies, 85.46 on MulSen-AD, 95.84 on BraTs, and 96.34 on UniMed. On the super-multi-class industrial setting (MVTec-AD + VisA), it reaches 99.4 AUC_I / 98.7 MF1_I on MVTec-AD and 95.5 AUC_I / 92.4 MF1_I on VisA, outperforming INP-Former by 10.8% in pixel-level MF1_P on VisA. The model runs at 59.09 FPS with 192.38 MB activated parameters, substantially faster than CFM (13.18 FPS) and memory-bank methods (0.39–1.19 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against specialized multi-modal baselines, UniMMAD outperforms M3DM by +0.4 AUC_I and +0.8 MF1_I on MVTec-3D, and by +8.2 AUC_I / +8.0 MF1_I on Eyecandies; it also beats CFM by +3.7 AUC_I on Eyecandies. On MulSen-AD, UniMMAD surpasses MulSen-TripleAD by +6.6 AUC_I but trails by −2.6 MF1_I, indicating a slight localization gap on that dataset. Among single-modality multi-class methods on UniMed, UniMMAD leads INP-Former by +0.3 AUC_I (96.34 vs 96.06) but is marginally behind on MF1_I (94.18 vs 94.36); it clearly outperforms MambaAD (+0.4 AUC_I), ViTAD (+0.8 AUC_I), and RD (+2.9 AUC_I). Against zero-shot generalist models (AdaCLIP, MVFA, AA-CLIP), UniMMAD achieves 12–33 AUC_I gains across all five datasets, confirming that a trained unified model substantially outperforms prompt-based or CLIP-based generalists. On the super-multi-class industrial benchmark (Table 3), UniMMAD edges out INP-Former on both MVTec-AD (99.4 vs 99.2 AUC_I) and VisA (95.5 vs 95.2 AUC_I), with a notable +2.8 MF1_P improvement on VisA pixel-level localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reverse teacher-student reconstruction paradigm (RD, CVPR 2022) from single-modality, single-class settings to a unified multi-modal, multi-class framework. It departs from prior multi-modal AD methods (M3DM, CFM, MMRD) that train separate per-domain models with parameter-free fusion, and from multi-class AD methods (UniAD, ViTAD, MambaAD) that share a single reconstruction decoder across classes. The key conceptual departure is the 'general → specific' decompression paradigm, where a shared multi-modal encoder compresses heterogeneous inputs and a cross-conditioned MoE decoder selectively expands them into modality- and class-specific reconstructions, drawing on MoE literature (Sparsely-Gated MoE, DeepSeekMoE) but introducing a novel cross-condition router and MoE-in-MoE parameter-sharing structure tailored to anomaly detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying 12 modalities and 66 classes under a single parameter set with 59 FPS inference, UniMMAD addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: real-world inspection pipelines require multiple sensors and product types, and maintaining separate specialized models is costly and brittle. The MoE-in-MoE structure (75% parameter reduction) and the demonstrated continual learning capability (<8% degradation on prior tasks) suggest a path toward scalable, updatable AD systems. Open questions include whether the reliance on a fixed WideResNet50 prior generator limits adaptability to truly novel domains, whether the approach extends to video or volumetric 3D inputs, and whether the cross-condition routing generalizes beyond the 9 evaluated datasets without additional expert capacity.
