## Problem

Adding fine-grained physical camera controls (shutter speed, aperture, color temperature) to pretrained text-to-video diffusion models typically requires large, photorealistic datasets that are expensive to acquire. This paper shows that this assumption is not only unnecessary but counterproductive: fine-tuning on photorealistic data induces catastrophic forgetting and content collapse in the backbone, while sparse, low-fidelity synthetic data yields superior controllable generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260351/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a natural-language text prompt and a single scalar camera parameter normalized to [-1, 1], which is projected through a small MLP into a conditioning embedding. At inference, the scalar drives a continuous physical effect (motion blur, depth-of-field bokeh, or color tone shift) across a 49-frame video generated via 50-step denoising. The conditioning is injected through a dedicated cross-attention path that operates in parallel with the standard text cross-attention, keeping the two signals architecturally disentangled.

## Method

The core architecture combines two jointly trained components: a backbone LoRA that absorbs the domain shift from synthetic training data, and a disentangled cross-attention adapter inserted into the deepest third of the DiT blocks that learns the physical conditioning effect. The key inference-time mechanism is 'decoupled inference': the backbone LoRA weights are selectively discarded from the shallow two-thirds of transformer blocks, restoring the pretrained generative priors while preserving the learned control in the deep blocks. Training uses procedurally generated geometric-primitive scenes with pyramid stratified sampling over the scalar range to ensure continuous, non-overfitting control. The paper further provides a two-stage evaluation protocol (Fast Evaluation Protocol for drift monitoring via single-step CLIP metrics, Slow Validation Protocol for full video quality) and a data-free analysis using intruder-dimension SVD and effective-rank decomposition to formally explain why low-fidelity data prevents catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the VBench 800-prompt benchmark, the Decoupled Inference strategy (the paper's proposed method) maintains semantic fidelity scores within ~2% of the original WAN 2.1 backbone: X-CLIP scores of 25.587–25.595 vs. baseline 25.390, and VQA scores of 0.513–0.532 vs. baseline 0.522, across shutter speed, aperture, and color temperature controls. VBench video quality metrics (Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Motion Smoothness, Aesthetic Quality) remain nearly identical to baseline (e.g., Subject Consistency 0.946–0.951 vs. 0.951). The 'Less is More' ablation shows that fine-tuning on a single photorealistic scene causes catastrophic semantic collapse (X-CLIP and VQA scores drop sharply), while fine-tuning on a single simple synthetic scene preserves baseline-level fidelity, confirming that low-fidelity data is superior for backbone preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: WAN 2.1 with text-based prompting, Bokeh Diffusion [7], and Generative Photography [53]. Against WAN 2.1 text prompting, the paper's method clearly wins on correct physical effect application: the baseline fails to associate shutter speed with motion blur (producing a 'metallic' door instead of glass) and misinterprets color temperature as weather (adding snow), while the paper's method correctly applies the intended physical effect without semantic entanglement. Against Bokeh Diffusion and Generative Photography (both image-domain, data-intensive methods), the paper's method achieves comparable high-fidelity bokeh effects for aperture control, despite training on a single simple synthetic scene rather than large photorealistic datasets. The quantitative comparison in Table 1 is primarily an internal ablation (Decoupled vs. Full-LoRA vs. base), showing that Decoupled Inference preserves backbone fidelity (all VBench metrics within ~2% of baseline) while Full-LoRA degrades VQA by up to 18% (0.427 vs. 0.522 for Aperture) and Imaging Quality by up to 14% (0.531 vs. 0.618 for Shutter Speed). The 'Less is More' ablation further shows that photorealistic fine-tuning data causes catastrophic forgetting (semantic scores collapse), whereas the paper's simple synthetic data preserves baseline-level performance, making it the superior data strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LoRA-based adapter paradigm for controllable generation (Bokeh Diffusion, LiON-LoRA) and cross-attention conditioning modules (IP-Adapter, ControlNet), but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, it introduces a 'separation of concerns' architecture where a backbone LoRA absorbs the synthetic domain shift while a dedicated cross-attention adapter isolates the physical control signal, followed by a decoupled inference strategy that prunes shallow LoRA weights to restore pretrained priors. Second, it repurposes the synthetic-data-for-learning literature (procedural video, simple geometric scenes) not for pretraining but for targeted fine-tuning of a frozen DiT backbone, explicitly arguing that photorealistic data is detrimental rather than beneficial for this adaptation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, low-data recipe for adding physical camera controls to T2V models, which is valuable given the difficulty of acquiring large annotated video datasets for such parameters. The FEP/SVP two-stage evaluation framework for monitoring backbone drift during fine-tuning is a reusable diagnostic tool beyond this specific task. However, the scope is narrow: only three scalar controls are demonstrated on a single backbone (Wan2.1), the ablation uses a single scene per effect, and the 'Less is More' claim has not been validated across architectures or at scale. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to multi-parameter joint control, spatial controls (depth, pose), and whether the decoupled inference strategy generalizes to non-LoRA adaptation methods.
