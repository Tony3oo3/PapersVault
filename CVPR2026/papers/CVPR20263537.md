## Problem

Adapting large pretrained object detectors to new domains with only 1–10 annotated examples remains unstable: direct full fine-tuning overfits, and existing remedies rely on expensive generative data augmentation or per-dataset hyperparameter searching. The paper asks whether consistent few-shot gains can be achieved through lightweight architectural and training modifications that reuse pretrained weights without adding parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263537/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces per-object bounding boxes (x, y, w, h) with class scores, fine-tuned from open-vocabulary pretrained detectors (MMGroundingDINO-B/L) on 1/5/10-shot novel categories. At inference, the final prediction is the arithmetic mean of bounding boxes and class scores across all L decoder layers (both hierarchical and parallel branches), effectively averaging L diverse query variants into a single detection set. Denoising queries are injected only during training and removed at inference, so the output structure is identical to a standard DETR head.

## Method

The Hybrid Ensemble Decoder (HED) reorganizes a standard L-layer DETR decoder into K stacked refinement layers followed by (L−K) parallel branches, each independently processing the K-th layer's query output; during training, denoising queries fed into each parallel branch are randomly re-initialized with probability τ to inject input diversity while object queries remain unchanged. At inference, predictions from all layers are averaged, turning the sequential refinement into an implicit ensemble of sub-networks initialized from different pretrained decoder weights. Training follows a two-stage progressive fine-tuning schedule: the encoder is frozen in stage 1 and all parameters are unfrozen in stage 2, with the stage transition automatically triggered by a plateau learning-rate scheduler that adapts to each dataset's convergence behavior. The entire pipeline uses only simple augmentations (random flip, color jitter, mixup) and requires no per-dataset hyperparameter tuning, making it applicable across 100 heterogeneous downstream tasks.

## Results

On CD-FSOD, the method achieves average mAP of 34.9 (1-shot), 45.0 (5-shot), and 47.9 (10-shot), setting new SOTA across all settings. On RF100-VL (100 heterogeneous datasets), it reaches 41.9 mAP in the 10-shot setting, surpassing SAM3 (35.7) by 6.2 points. On ODinW-13, it achieves 63.1/67.6/68.6 mAP at 1/5/10-shot, matching or exceeding SAM3 and Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro despite using a smaller base model (MMGDINO-L vs. PE-L+ backbone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CD-FSOD, the method outperforms the prior SOTA Domain-RAG by 1.3/2.3/2.5 mAP at 1/5/10-shot respectively, and surpasses ETS by 6.2/3.2/2.9 mAP, while also leading CDFormer, VFMDETR, CD-ViTO, and DE-ViT-FT across all settings. On RF100-VL, the headline result is a 6.2 mAP advantage over SAM3 (41.9 vs. 35.7 in 10-shot), with the 0-shot base model (MMGDINO-L) at only 15.7, demonstrating that the fine-tuning strategy drives most of the gain. On ODinW-13, the method matches SAM3 at 1-shot (63.1 vs. 63.0) and exceeds Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro at 10-shot (68.6 vs. 67.9), though it trails SAM3 at 5/10-shot (67.6/68.6 vs. 69.1/71.6), which uses a larger PE-L+ backbone and more pretraining data. Against MQ-GLIP-L and GLIPv2-H on ODinW-13, the method leads by 1.2–5.2 mAP depending on shot setting. The OOD robustness evaluation on the CD-Mixed set shows that the hybrid ensemble decoder with random denoising query re-initialization reduces mAP degradation under domain shift more effectively than progressive fine-tuning alone or naive decoder parallelism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DETR detection paradigm and its denoising-query variants (DINO, GroundingDINO), using MMGroundingDINO as the base model, and reframes the sequential decoder as a structured ensemble inspired by Deep Ensembles and Packed Ensembles. It deliberately departs from the recent trend of cross-domain FSOD methods that rely on generative data augmentation (Domain-RAG) or augmentation searching (ETS), instead arguing that lightweight architectural modifications (parallel decoder branches) and a progressive fine-tuning schedule with a plateau scheduler are sufficient. The progressive two-stage training connects to DeFRCN and LP-FT, while the OOD robustness analysis links to open-set detection and false-positive robustness literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a fully automated, dataset-agnostic fine-tuning recipe that scales to 100+ heterogeneous domains without per-dataset hyperparameter tuning or expensive generative augmentation—addressing a real bottleneck in deploying few-shot detectors across diverse industrial and scientific settings. The OOD robustness finding (random denoising query initialization reduces overconfident false positives) offers a concrete mechanism for more reliable deployment in semi-automated labeling pipelines. Limitations include dependence on a strong pretrained base model, modest gains when zero-shot performance is already high (ODinW-13), and the SAM3 comparison carrying caveats around model selection protocol. Open questions include whether the parallel decoder structure generalizes to non-DETR architectures and whether the approach retains its advantage when applied to substantially larger foundation models.
