## Problem

Personalizing large vision-language models to recognize user-specific entities remains challenging: existing methods either require per-concept test-time fine-tuning (hurting scalability), depend on external vision modules like detectors or segmentation networks (adding system complexity), or need full reference views reprocessed at every inference call (introducing latency and context-length bottlenecks). No prior approach simultaneously supports single-concept, multi-concept, and video personalization in a unified, training-free, LVLM-agnostic framework with minimal overhead.

## Input / Output

During concept introduction, the method takes one or more reference images of a subject plus its name, and the LVLM generates descriptive keywords and estimates the subject's area fraction to determine how many visual tokens (K_c) to retain. At inference, the query image or video is processed normally by the visual encoder, while the pre-computed concept memories—compact matrices of K_c visual tokens per reference view stored directly in the LLM embedding space—are injected as soft prompts alongside concept names, eliminating any need to re-encode reference images. Outputs span three tasks: binary recognition (Yes/No), VQA (multiple-choice or open-ended, scored by string match or LLM-judged semantic alignment), and personalized captioning (recall of correct concept name in generated text).

## Method

Ego exploits the observation that modern LVLMs internally assign discriminative embeddings to objects for cross-image recognition, and extracts these embeddings via cross-attention between the model's own generated keyword tokens and the visual tokens of a reference image. Specifically, the LVLM is prompted to produce short descriptive keywords for the subject; the cross-attention scores from keyword embeddings to visual tokens are aggregated across a set of automatically selected mid-to-late LLM layers (identified via a one-time COCO-2017 calibration using segmentation-mask overlap), yielding an importance score per visual token. The top-K_c tokens—where K_c is dynamically scaled by the LVLM's estimate of the subject's area in the image—are retained as a compact concept memory. At inference, these memories are concatenated across reference views and injected into the LLM context as soft prompts, leveraging the model's in-context learning to recognize and reason about the personalized subject without any fine-tuning, external modules, or architectural modification.

## Results

On recognition (1 reference view, InternVL3-14B), Ego achieves F1 scores of 90.2 (MyVLM), 80.2 (Yo'LLaVA), and 98.4 (This-is-my Multi), outperforming RAP (77.0, 63.3, 76.5) and R2P (68.5, 65.5, N/A) on most datasets. On VQA and captioning (Table 2), Ego attains 72.2% multi-concept VQA accuracy and 70.9% multi-concept captioning recall, surpassing RAP (53.7%, 43.6%) and PeKit (51.8%, 35.2%). For video QA, Ego reaches 70.0% accuracy versus PeKit's 59.9%. Concept introduction overhead is minimal at 1.40 s per concept (vs. 24 hrs for RAP fine-tuning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RAP (training-based, 24 hrs fine-tuning), Ego wins decisively on multi-concept VQA (72.2% vs. 53.7%, +18.5 pp), multi-concept captioning (70.9% vs. 43.6%, +27.3 pp), and single-concept captioning on MyVLM (91.3% vs. 65.6%, +25.7 pp), while losing on single-concept VQA (92.3% vs. 97.6% on Yo'LLaVA; 88.0% vs. 90.0% on This-is-my). Against R2P (training-free, external retrieval), Ego dominates on recognition F1 (90.2 vs. 68.5 on MyVLM) and captioning (91.3% vs. 77.5%), and R2P does not support multi-concept or video settings at all. Against PeKit (training-free, external segmentation + DINOv2 memory), Ego leads on multi-concept VQA (72.2% vs. 51.8%), multi-concept captioning (70.9% vs. 35.2%), video QA (70.0% vs. 59.9%), and captioning (91.3% vs. 81.1%), though PeKit edges out Ego on single-concept VQA for This-is-my (92.0% vs. 88.0%). In recognition, Ego achieves the highest F1 on MyVLM (90.2), Yo'LLaVA (80.2), and both multi-concept splits (98.4), but trails RAP on This-is-my single-concept (79.1 vs. 87.1 F1). Overall, Ego's advantage is most pronounced in multi-concept and video settings where training-based and retrieval-based baselines degrade sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ego carves new ground within the LVLM personalization landscape by repurposing the model's own cross-attention mechanism as a concept-token selection tool, rather than relying on external vision modules (R2P, PeKit) or per-concept fine-tuning (MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA, RAP). Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of visual token pruning literature (which shows informative subsets outperform full images) and in-context learning, but applies both to the novel purpose of building a persistent, per-concept visual memory. The method departs from prior training-free approaches by eliminating all external dependencies—no segmentation networks, no DINOv2 embeddings, no retrieval databases—instead treating the LVLM's internal attention as both the selector and the memory substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified evaluation protocol across single-concept, multi-concept, and video personalization with standardized metrics addresses a real reproducibility gap in the field, and the finding that a training-free, module-free method can match or exceed 210k-sample fine-tuned baselines (RAP) in multi-concept and video settings challenges the assumption that personalization requires either training or external tooling. Limitations include dependence on sufficiently powerful LVLMs with strong in-context learning, model-specific instruction prompts, and the open question of how the approach scales when the number of stored concepts exceeds context limits. The compact concept memory (a few dozen tokens per concept) also opens the door to long-term, multi-user personalization on resource-constrained devices.
