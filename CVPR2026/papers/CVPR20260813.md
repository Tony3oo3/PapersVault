## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models either require costly fine-tuning that suffers from catastrophic forgetting, or apply coarse inference-time heuristics that fail to capture multi-concept semantic entanglements. Critically, all prior approaches treat concepts in isolation, cannot adapt online to newly emerging concepts, and ignore the relational topology of the latent semantic space, leading to either incomplete erasure or collateral damage to semantically adjacent concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260813/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and one or more target concept names (e.g., 'Snoopy', 'Van Gogh') as input. It produces a modified text prompt with target-concept-associated tokens removed, which is then re-encoded by the pretrained text encoder and fed to the diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.4, SDXL, or FLUX.1-schnell) to generate an image. The entire pipeline operates at the prompt-embedding level before diffusion sampling, requiring no access to model weights, attention maps, or latent representations during generation.

## Method

GrOCE formulates concept erasure as a graph cut problem over a dynamic semantic graph built from the text encoder's vocabulary embeddings. CONSTRUCT creates a weighted graph where edges connect tokens with cosine similarity above an adaptive local threshold (scaled by neighborhood density), making implicit hierarchical relationships explicit. IDENTIFY restricts analysis to an n-hop neighborhood around the target anchor node, then applies a graph Laplacian diffusion process (s = exp(−φL_sub)Y) to score each node's semantic influence, selecting the top-K nodes as the target cluster. SEVER computes a distance-decayed influence score for each prompt token by aggregating projections onto all cluster member embeddings, then removes tokens whose aggregated L2 norm exceeds a threshold δ, yielding a cleaned prompt that preserves global sentence structure and non-target semantics. The entire procedure is training-free, requiring no gradient access or parameter modification, and completes in under 2 seconds for 10-concept erasure.

## Results

On single-target concept erasure (erasing Snoopy), GrOCE achieves a CLIP Score (CS) of 16.92 and a perfect FID of 0 for all non-target concepts (Mickey, Spongebob, Pikachu), outperforming the best training-free baseline AdaVD (CS=20.14, FID=5.12–9.14) and training-based methods MACE (CS=20.12, FID=92.61–101.37) and ConAbl (CS=25.23, FID=36.89–38.67). For artistic style erasure (e.g., Van Gogh), GrOCE reduces CS to 23.28 with FID=0 for all unrelated styles, versus AdaVD's CS=24.26 and FID=2.42–6.93. In terms of efficiency, GrOCE completes 10-concept erasure in 1.73 s on an A100 GPU, roughly 2× faster than AdaVD (3.59 s) and over 2,000× faster than ConAbl (8,529 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GrOCE outperforms all five baselines on both CS (erasure effectiveness) and FID (non-target preservation) across every tested scenario. Against the strongest training-free competitor AdaVD [37], GrOCE reduces CS by 3.22 points (16.92 vs. 20.14 for Snoopy) and achieves FID=0 versus AdaVD's 5.12–9.14, indicating zero collateral damage to non-target concepts. Training-based methods ConAbl [16] and MACE [26] suffer severe over-erasure: MACE inflates FID to 92–101, and ConAbl to 37–39, while also achieving weaker CS scores (25.23 and 20.12 respectively). SPEED [23] is the weakest training-free baseline, with CS=23.35 and FID≈23–25, showing both incomplete erasure and significant non-target degradation. In the multi-target setting (erasing Snoopy+Mickey+Spongebob), GrOCE maintains FID=0 and CS=16.45–18.37, whereas AdaVD degrades to FID=10.14 and CS=19.17–20.47, confirming that GrOCE's graph-guided approach scales without interference between concurrent erasure targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GrOCE departs from both major lineages of concept erasure—parameter-based fine-tuning (ESD, ConAbl, MACE) and inference-time attention/activation manipulation (UCE, SPEED, AdaVD)—by reframing the problem as a graph cut over a dynamic semantic graph built from the text encoder's vocabulary embeddings. Rather than modifying model weights or steering cross-attention maps, it edits the text prompt itself by identifying and removing tokens whose graph-identified influence on the target concept exceeds a threshold. The graph Laplacian diffusion and multi-hop traversal machinery is borrowed from spectral graph theory and applied here for the first time to concept isolation in diffusion models, positioning the work as a structural/topological alternative to the local, token-level heuristics that prior inference-time methods rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, sub-2-second latency makes online, large-scale concept removal practically deployable in safety-critical settings where retraining is infeasible and new harmful concepts emerge continuously. However, the FID=0 results, while striking, are a near-trivial consequence of the prompt-editing mechanism (untouched tokens produce identical embeddings), and the evaluation is limited to 80 instance and 30 style templates with fixed hyperparameters. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to visual-only concepts (e.g., pose-lighting interactions) that have no direct vocabulary anchor, to heavily paraphrased or compositional prompts where token-level graph distances become unreliable, and to very large or rapidly evolving concept sets where the O(|V|²) graph construction may become a bottleneck.
