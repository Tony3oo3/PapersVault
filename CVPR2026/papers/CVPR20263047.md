## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for vision transformers target encoder-only architectures and fail to account for SAM's decoder, which employs bidirectional cross-attention between image and prompt tokens. Under low-bit quantization, this leads to two distinct failure modes: attention dissipation, where inter-modal scale mismatch in the QK^T MatMul flattens attention scores, and reconstruction oscillation, where bidirectional coupling causes cross-branch error feedback that prevents stable convergence.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pre-trained SAM or SAM2 checkpoint and a small unlabeled calibration set of 32 images, then produces a fully quantized model (W4A4 or W6A6) using per-channel asymmetric quantization for weights and per-tensor asymmetric quantization for activations. The initial PatchEmbed layer and final Mask Head are retained in full precision. At inference, the quantized model accepts an RGB image with user prompts (bounding boxes or points) and outputs segmentation masks, with SAM2 additionally producing video segmentation masks across frames.

## Method

CAR-SAM introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, MatMul-Aware Compensation (MAC) formulates explicit reconstruction objectives for the QK^T and AV matrix multiplications in attention, then transfers activation quantization errors from MatMul operands back into the preceding projection weights by solving Sylvester-type matrix equations (AX + XB = C), achieving cross-layer compensation without modifying the quantized activations themselves. Second, Joint Cross-Attention Reconstruction (JCAR) is grounded in a theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) showing that the decoder's error propagation contains a cross-branch feedback term J_g^(T')·J_f^(T)ΔT that couples the two cross-attention modules; JCAR therefore jointly optimizes the quantization scales and rounding offsets of paired token-to-image and image-to-token cross-attention blocks rather than reconstructing them sequentially, suppressing oscillatory loss behavior and enabling stable convergence to a global optimum.

## Results

On COCO instance segmentation, CAR-SAM achieves 39.3 mAP under W4A4 quantization on SAM-B, surpassing PTQ4SAM (24.7) and QDrop (26.4) by 14.6 and 12.9 mAP respectively. On SAM-L, CAR-SAM reaches 48.5 mAP at W4A4, outperforming PTQ4SAM (41.9) and QDrop (38.0) by 6.6 and 10.5 mAP. Under W6A6, CAR-SAM maintains near-full-precision performance (53.3 mAP on SAM-B vs. 55.8 FP), while baselines degrade more noticeably. On SAM2-B+, the method achieves 46.5 mAP at W4A4, exceeding PTQ4SAM (45.5) and QDrop (43.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAR-SAM consistently outperforms both QDrop and PTQ4SAM across all W4A4 settings on SAM-B, SAM-L, and SAM-H, with the largest gap on SAM-B (39.3 vs. 26.4 and 24.7 mAP). At W6A6, CAR-SAM leads QDrop by 2.6 mAP on SAM-B (53.3 vs. 50.7) and PTQ4SAM by 2.4 mAP, though the margin narrows on SAM-H where BRECQ (60.0) slightly edges out CAR-SAM (59.0). BRECQ does not report W4A4 results (near-zero performance), making direct 4-bit comparison impossible. On SAM2-B+, CAR-SAM achieves 46.5 mAP at W4A4, beating PTQ4SAM (45.5) and QDrop (43.5), while on SAM2-L the advantage over PTQ4SAM narrows to a near-tie (38.2 vs. 38.8). Overall, the method's advantage is most pronounced in the aggressive 4-bit regime where baselines suffer severe degradation, confirming that the MAC and JCAR components specifically target the failure modes that emerge at low bit-widths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reconstruction-based PTQ lineage (BRECQ's block-wise reconstruction, QDrop/GPTAQ's activation-error compensation into linear weights) by identifying that SAM's decoder cross-attention introduces two failure modes—attention dissipation and reconstruction oscillation—that encoder-focused PTQ methods and prior SAM-specific quantizers (PTQ4SAM, MIX-QSAM, PQ-SAM) do not address. MAC generalizes the compensation target from projection linears to the MatMul operation itself, while JCAR departs from sequential block-by-block reconstruction by jointly optimizing coupled cross-attention pairs, grounded in a Jacobian-based error-propagation theorem. The work is positioned as the first unified PTQ framework covering both SAM1 and SAM2 architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAM and SAM2 are foundational for zero-shot segmentation and video understanding, yet their >1B parameter size blocks edge deployment; robust W4A4 quantization is the most direct path to practical use. The paper's diagnosis of attention dissipation (inter-modal scale mismatch flattening attention scores) and oscillation (bidirectional cross-branch feedback loops) provides a reusable conceptual framework for quantizing any architecture with coupled attention branches. Limitations include reliance on ground-truth bounding boxes as prompts (isolating segmentation quality but not reflecting real detector noise), keeping PatchEmbed and Mask Head in full precision, and evaluation limited to COCO and DAVIS. Open questions include whether MAC/JCAR transfer to other bidirectional-attention models (e.g., diffusion transformers, MLLMs) and whether the approach remains effective under realistic prompt distributions.
