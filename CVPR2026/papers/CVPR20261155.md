## Problem

Deformation-based dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods rely on a single, globally shared deformation network to model all temporal variation, which forces the network to converge to a temporally averaged representation and produces blurred renderings in regions with complex or rapid motion. Additionally, static 3DGs still participate in repeated deformation computation, wasting resources. Existing partitioning alternatives (e.g., SWinGS) use coarse window-level splits guided by 2D optical flow priors and require cumbersome pre- and post-processing steps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB video sequences (N3DV: 20 cameras, 30 FPS, 1352×1014; Meet Room: 13 cameras, 30 FPS, 1280×720) and produces a deformable 3D Gaussian representation consisting of per-Gaussian embeddings, replicated coarse/fine deformation MLPs for partitioned temporal segments, and a set of static 3DGs. Rendering is performed via differentiable alpha compositing of projected 2D Gaussians, achieving real-time frame rates (75–92 FPS on an RTX A6000) for novel view synthesis at arbitrary viewpoints and timestamps.

## Method

MAPo introduces a dynamic score-based partitioning strategy built on E-D3DGS's dual-deformation paradigm (coarse + fine MLPs with per-Gaussian and temporal embeddings). Each 3DG's motion intensity is quantified by a dynamic score computed from its historical positions, fusing maximum displacement and position variance via a harmonic mean after percentile normalization. High-dynamic 3DGs are recursively partitioned along the temporal axis (up to level 3), with their deformation networks replicated per sub-segment to enable specialized modeling of complex motion; low-dynamic 3DGs are frozen as static to eliminate redundant computation. A cross-frame consistency loss (L_current enforcing rendering agreement between adjacent segments at the boundary, plus L_gt anchoring the adjacent segment's rendering to the ground-truth image) is applied within 5 frames of each partition boundary to suppress visual discontinuities and prevent over-smoothing.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, MAPo achieves a PSNR of 31.33 dB, SSIM of 0.944, and LPIPS of 0.044, outperforming the strongest baseline E-D3DGS (30.79 dB, 0.934, 0.051) by 0.54 dB in PSNR and 0.007 in LPIPS. On the Meet Room dataset, MAPo reaches 26.72 dB PSNR, 0.903 SSIM, and 0.066 LPIPS, surpassing the best baseline 4DGC (26.56 dB, 0.901, 0.070). MAPo also maintains competitive efficiency: 75.64 FPS on N3DV and 92.21 FPS on Meet Room, with storage of only 65 MB and 49 MB respectively, and training times of 1h52m and 1h19m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPo outperforms all 11 baselines on both N3DV and Meet Room across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. On N3DV, the closest competitor is E-D3DGS (30.79 dB PSNR, 0.051 LPIPS), which MAPo surpasses by 0.54 dB and 0.007 LPIPS respectively; 4DGC (30.78 dB) and Ex4DGS (30.76 dB) trail by similar margins. On Meet Room, 4DGC is the nearest baseline at 26.56 dB PSNR and 0.070 LPIPS, with MAPo leading by 0.16 dB and 0.004 LPIPS. In terms of efficiency, MAPo (65 MB, 75.64 FPS on N3DV) uses less storage than 4DGC (225 MB), Ex4DGS (205 MB), and 4DGS (3.6 GB) while rendering faster than E-D3DGS (37.51 FPS) and 4DGC (124.61 FPS is faster but at 225 MB storage). The naive segmentation baseline E-D3DGS (seg) achieves slightly better LPIPS (0.049) but at the cost of 3.3× more storage (215 MB) and 4.5× longer training (8h32m), confirming that MAPo's dynamic score-based partitioning is more effective than uniform temporal slicing. NeRF-based methods (DyNeRF, NeRFPlayer, K-Planes, HyperReel) are consistently outperformed in both quality and speed, with rendering FPS below 2 versus MAPo's 75+.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAPo builds directly on the E-D3DGS dual-deformation paradigm (per-Gaussian embeddings with coarse/fine temporal embeddings) and the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline. It departs from the prevailing unified-model strategy—where a single deformation network handles all 3DGs across the full time range—by introducing a dynamic score that drives recursive, per-3DG temporal partitioning with replicated sub-networks. This contrasts with window-based approaches like SWinGS, which partition at a coarse sequence level guided by 2D optical flow, and with curve-based or 4D-Gaussian methods that trade storage or computational cost for explicit temporal modeling. The cross-frame consistency loss is a new component not present in prior deformation-based 3DGS work, specifically designed to handle the discontinuities that partitioning inherently introduces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'temporal averaging' bottleneck in unified deformation models is a recognized but under-addressed limitation; MAPo's adaptive, per-3DG partitioning offers a principled solution that avoids the fixed-window granularity and 2D-prior dependence of prior partitioning strategies. The practical finding that L_current alone induces over-smoothing (necessitating the L_gt anchor) is a useful design insight for anyone introducing segment-level losses in dynamic reconstruction. Open questions include scalability to much longer sequences (beyond the ~10s clips used here), whether the partitioning strategy extends to streaming or online settings, and how the approach behaves when motion is highly non-stationary across the full time range.
