## Problem

Instruction-based image editing datasets face a fundamental scale-quality trade-off: human-annotated collections are high-fidelity but tiny, while large-scale automated pipelines suffer from error propagation, instruction-image misalignment, and failed edits. Existing benchmarks further lack systematic coverage of complex reasoning and spatial editing tasks, and prevailing VLM-based metrics are insensitive to unintended changes in non-edited regions, preventing fine-grained diagnosis of model weaknesses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263492/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes pre-filtered source images (center-cropped and resized, discarding those requiring >20% cropping) and generates 3–7 content-aware editing instructions per image via Qwen2.5-VL-72B, each mapped to one of 22 sub-tasks. Editing models (FLUX.1-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) produce the edited image, and the post-verification stage outputs a structured JSON containing a boolean is_changed flag and a refined instruction. The final dataset comprises ~10M triplets, with 50% at 1024×1024 resolution, organized into four categories: Scene (3.06M), Attribute (3.53M), Object (3.24M), and Reasoning Editing (1.75M).

## Method

The pipeline replaces multi-tool editing chains with an end-to-end editing model to avoid error propagation, then applies a unified post-verification stage that jointly performs failure detection and instruction recaptioning. To make this scalable, the authors train Qwen-Verify, a 7B dual-task expert model, in two stages: SFT on Normal and No-Edit triplets for foundational discrimination, followed by D²PO (Differential Direct Preference Optimization), which conditions the policy on a visual differential context c_v = V(I_o, I_e) extracted from the image pair and optimizes a Bradley-Terry preference loss over corrected versus hallucinated instruction pairs. For evaluation, UnicBench introduces four metrics—Instruction Following, Non-edit Consistency, Visual Quality, and Reasoning Accuracy—combined via geometric mean, where a zero in any dimension signals a failed edit, ensuring severe failures are not masked by high scores in other dimensions.

## Results

On UnicBench, GPT-Image-1 achieves the best overall score of 8.35 (EN) and 8.45 (CN), followed by Seedream 4.0 (8.04/8.05) and Nano Banana (7.88/8.04). Among open-source models, Qwen-Image-Edit leads with 7.73 (EN) and 7.78 (CN), while all models show a marked drop on Reasoning Accuracy (RA), with even GPT-Image-1 scoring only 8.34 on RA versus 9.16 on IF. For dataset quality (Table 2), UnicEdit-10M attains the highest VIEScore Overall (8.08) and Aesthetic scores (Source 8.00, Target 7.76), surpassing the next-best NHR-Edit (7.78 Overall) and GPT-Image-Edit-1.5M (7.75 Overall). Qwen-Verify outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-72B on all three verification tasks (Normal 6.32 vs 5.25, No Edit 9.80 vs 9.60, Hallucination 6.22 vs 6.12) at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On UnicBench, closed-source models consistently outperform open-source counterparts: GPT-Image-1 leads overall (8.35 EN / 8.45 CN) with the highest IF (9.16) and RA (8.34), while Seedream 4.0 ranks second (8.04/8.05) and notably excels on Non-edit Consistency (8.72). Among open-source models, Qwen-Image-Edit narrows the gap most effectively (7.73 EN / 7.78 CN), outperforming BAGEL (6.98/7.11) and Step1X-Edit-v1.1 (6.92/7.06) by roughly 0.7–0.8 points overall. All models exhibit a significant RA drop relative to IF, with even the best open-source model (Qwen-Image-Edit) scoring only 6.45 on RA versus 8.21 on IF, confirming complex reasoning as a shared bottleneck. For dataset quality, UnicEdit-10M surpasses all compared datasets (SEED-Data-Edit, ImgEdit, X2Edit, NHR-Edit, GPT-Image-Edit-1.5M, Nano-consistent-150k) on VIEScore Overall (8.08 vs. 7.78 max) and Aesthetic scores (8.00/7.76 vs. 7.52/7.54 max). Qwen-Verify outperforms both Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-72B on all three verification sub-tasks, achieving 6.32/9.80/6.22 accuracy versus 5.25/9.60/6.12 for the 72B model, while being substantially cheaper to deploy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two dominant paradigms in automated image-editing data construction: multi-tool pipelines (UltraEdit, ImgEdit, Step1X-Edit) that accumulate errors across vision modules, and end-to-end synthetic approaches (InstructPix2Pix, HQ-Edit) that lack explicit quality verification. Its key departure is a unified post-verification stage that jointly performs failure detection and instruction recaptioning via a single 7B expert model (Qwen-Verify), trained with a novel D²PO objective that conditions preference optimization on a visual differential context extracted from the image pair. On the evaluation side, it extends VIEScore's SC/PQ framework with Non-edit Consistency and Reasoning Accuracy metrics, addressing blind spots identified in prior benchmarks (EditBench, ImgEdit-Bench, KRIS-Bench) that either omit complex reasoning tasks or are insensitive to unintended changes in non-edited regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10M-scale dataset with unified verification provides the open-source community with a resource that was previously unavailable at this scale and quality, directly addressing the data scarcity that the authors identify as the primary driver of the open-vs-closed-source gap. The UnicBench results reveal a consistent and quantifiable weakness across all evaluated models on the Reasoning Accuracy metric, offering a concrete diagnostic target for future research. Open questions include whether the D²PO formulation generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL architecture, whether the 7B verifier can be further compressed without quality loss, and whether targeted data generation on the identified RA bottleneck can close the gap with closed-source systems like GPT-4o.
