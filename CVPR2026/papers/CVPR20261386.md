## Problem

Traditional stereo photogrammetry for lunar DEMs struggles with pushbroom sensor geometry, non-textured terrain, and extreme illumination variations across orbits, while existing neural rendering methods approximate pushbroom cameras with pinhole or RPC models that introduce geometric errors. Additionally, no standardized multi-orbit lunar dataset with rigorous sensor models existed to benchmark learning-based reconstruction under realistic orbital conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261386/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LNEM consumes 2–3 grayscale pushbroom frames per site (LROC NAC at 0.5–2 m/px or KPLO LUTI at 2.5 m/px, cropped to 512×512 px), with per-line camera positions and rotations extracted from SPICE SPK/CK/FK kernels rather than a single global pose. Depth supervision is drawn from NAC DTM where available or SLDEM otherwise, and the solar direction is computed per-line from ephemeris time. The network outputs a volumetric density field from which a metric DEM is extracted, alongside a grayscale radiance field and a per-sample sun-visibility map.

## Method

LNEM performs NeRF-style volume rendering where each camera ray is defined by a per-line origin (spacecraft position at that scan line's ephemeris time) and a per-line rotation matrix, explicitly modeling the pushbroom acquisition process through Rigorous Sensor Models. Positional encoding uses a 32-level multi-resolution hash grid (twice the default) instead of Fourier features, which the ablation shows generalizes across sites without per-site frequency tuning. A shadow-modeling branch predicts a sun-visibility scalar v(x,ω) and an indirect-illumination scalar I_ind(ω) from the solar direction, decomposing the rendered intensity as c_g·(v + (1−v)·I_ind) and enforcing consistency via a shadow-correction loss that aligns solar-ray transmittance with predicted visibility. Training minimizes a combined photometric L2 loss, a depth L2 loss (weight 300) against DEM-derived ground-truth depths for metric scale anchoring, and the shadow-correction loss (weight 0.02), with 512 samples per ray over 50k–100k iterations. The Lunar Studio pipeline preprocesses raw cubes through radiometric/photometric correction and multi-stage bundle adjustment (SPICE initialization + jigsaw) to produce geometrically consistent multi-orbit inputs.

## Results

On the eight LROC NAC benchmark regions, LNEM (with shadow modeling) achieves a bias-corrected RMSE (RMSE_corr) of 0.67–5.67 m against LOLA altimetry, with three-view sites reaching 0.67–3.69 m. The raw RMSE_LOLA ranges from 0.68 m (Marius Hills Pit) to 10.69 m (Eimmart A), where the gap between raw and bias-corrected values reflects global vertical offsets. On KPLO LUTI (four regions), LNEM achieves RMSE_corr of 0.02–29.07 m, with the Apollo 15 outlier attributed to lower-precision reconstructed SPICE kernels rather than the method itself. Shadow modeling reduces multi-orbit geometric dispersion (σ²_total) across all eight regions, e.g., from 17.84 to 6.23 m² at Lacus Mortis Pit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two production DEMs (SLDEM and NAC DTM), LNEM is competitive in raw RMSE_LOLA on several regions (e.g., 0.68 m vs. 0.92 m at Marius Hills Pit) but lags where NAC DTM's stereo pairs are well-constrained (e.g., 4.63 m vs. 0.62 m at Apollo 16). Against the classical ASP stereo pipeline, LNEM is dramatically more robust: ASP achieves 0.32 m RMSE_corr at Apollo 16 but catastrophically fails at Lacus Mortis Pit (109.96 m) due to triangulation instability under narrow baselines, whereas LNEM stays at 2.03 m. EO-NeRF, which uses RPC cameras and no depth supervision, suffers from severe scale ambiguity with biases exceeding 150–490 m and RMSE_corr of 7.3–97.7 m, making it the weakest neural baseline. Sat-NeRF, which adds depth supervision but still relies on RPC approximations, improves over EO-NeRF yet remains 2–20× worse than LNEM in RMSE_corr across most regions (e.g., 39.20 m vs. 1.57 m at Apollo 15). Overall, LNEM's rigorous pushbroom sensor model is the decisive factor, yielding the lowest RMSE_corr in 6 of 8 NAC regions and the most consistent performance across varying illumination and viewing geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

LNEM extends the NeRF volumetric rendering paradigm to the lunar domain by replacing the standard pinhole camera model with per-line Rigorous Sensor Models (RSMs) that capture pushbroom geometry, a departure from prior lunar NeRF methods (LunarNRM, Prosvetov et al.) that relied on RPC or pinhole approximations. The shadow modeling component adapts the sun-visibility and indirect-illumination branches from Earth-observation NeRF work (EO-NeRF, Sat-NeRF) to the lunar setting, while the multi-resolution hash encoding replaces site-specific Fourier features for more stable convergence. The Lunar Studio pipeline consolidates fragmented ISIS3/ASP workflows into a standardized RSM-based preprocessing chain, filling a gap between planetary photogrammetry tooling and learning-based reconstruction pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LNEM provides the first end-to-end neural volumetric reconstruction pipeline that faithfully models pushbroom sensor geometry for lunar DEM generation, offering a scalable complement to conventional stereo and altimetry-based products. The publicly released Lunar Studio dataset and pipeline lower the barrier for future lunar neural rendering research by providing geometrically consistent multi-orbit observations with per-line camera poses. Key open questions include whether photometric shadow modeling can be replaced by physics-based reflectance (e.g., Hapke BRDF) for improved fidelity in shadowed regions, whether the approach extends to south polar and multi-sensor fusion settings, and whether higher-precision SPICE kernels (e.g., smithed kernels) would close the remaining gap for LUTI-quality observations.
