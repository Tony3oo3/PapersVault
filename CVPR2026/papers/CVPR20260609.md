## Problem

Traditional 3D shape metrics like Chamfer Distance and IoU measure geometric accuracy but fail to align with human perceptual fidelity—small detail changes can drastically alter perceived quality while producing negligible geometric error. Frequency-domain metrics such as SAUCD partially address this but still cannot fully capture the complexity of human perception. Moreover, prior evaluation datasets rely on synthetic distortions, introducing a domain gap that limits real-world applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260609/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D meshes (a distorted mesh and its ground truth) as input, where each mesh provides vertex positions and explicit topological connectivity (edge set). Meshes may contain tens of thousands of vertices, and the method is invariant to translation, rotation, and scale via a non-learned alignment preprocessing step. The output is a single scalar fidelity score in which higher values indicate better perceptual quality, trained to correlate with human-annotated scores normalized to [0,1].

## Method

LoCaSE combines a ModelNet-pretrained PointNet backbone with two key innovations: (1) local connection blocks that apply cross-attention between each vertex and its top-5 topological neighbors (truncated to 64), enabling the network to exploit mesh connectivity for fine-grained local detail extraction without the O(N²) cost of global self-attention; and (2) LoRA-style finetuning (rank 32) on the PointNet MLP layers, which adds only r×(C+C′) parameters per layer to adapt the pretrained shape prior to fidelity evaluation while preserving generalization. A 4-layer MLP comparison network then maps the encoded features of both meshes to a single fidelity score. Training uses a composite loss of Smooth L1, Pearson correlation, and Spearman rank-order terms (weighted 5:1:1) against human annotations from the newly introduced RSF dataset, which contains real-world distortions from 16+ reconstruction/generation methods scored by 25–35 subjects per object via Swiss-system pairwise comparisons.

## Results

On the RSF main subset with a 4-object train/test split, LoCaSE achieves PLCC 0.697, SROCC 0.703, and KROCC 0.534, substantially outperforming all baselines. Under 16-fold object-level cross-validation, the averages are PLCC 0.728, SROCC 0.757, and KROCC 0.614, with the lowest standard deviation (0.106–0.116) among all methods, indicating consistent performance across diverse object categories. On the unseen RSF test-only subset (8 new objects, 5 new distortion algorithms), LoCaSE still leads with average PLCC 0.68, SROCC 0.64, and KROCC 0.55, while most baselines drop to negative or near-zero correlations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoCaSE decisively outperforms all five baselines on every correlation metric. Against Chamfer Distance (PLCC 0.377, SROCC 0.310), LoCaSE gains +0.32 PLCC and +0.39 SROCC, confirming that pure geometric matching misses perceptual detail. IoU (PLCC 0.280) and F-score (PLCC 0.087) are even weaker, with F-score showing near-random alignment to human ratings. Unidirectional Hausdorff Distance (PLCC 0.348, SROCC 0.361) is the strongest geometric baseline but still trails LoCaSE by roughly 0.35 in PLCC. The most notable comparison is SAUCD [30], the prior human-aligned spectral metric, which achieves near-zero or negative correlations (PLCC 0.158, SROCC 0.193) on real-world distortions, whereas LoCaSE reaches PLCC 0.697—a gap of over 0.5—demonstrating that frequency-domain analysis alone cannot capture the local connectivity cues that drive human fidelity perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of human-aligned 3D shape evaluation initiated by SAUCD [30], which used spectral analysis on synthetically distorted meshes, by replacing synthetic distortions with real-world reconstruction and generation artifacts and swapping the frequency-domain analysis for a topology-aware local attention mechanism on a PointNet backbone. The LoRA-style fine-tuning strategy [14] is borrowed from NLP parameter-efficient adaptation and repurposed here to preserve ModelNet shape priors while adapting to a small human-annotated fidelity dataset. The invariance alignment module (PCA + centering + normalization) is a standard geometric preprocessing step, but its explicit non-learned design distinguishes it from learned equivariant encoders used in other mesh networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RSF dataset is a practical contribution: it is one of the first to pair real reconstruction/generation distortions with large-scale human annotations (310 subjects, 5,074 scores), addressing the domain gap that limits synthetic-distortion datasets like ShapeGrading. However, the dataset is small (16 main objects, 8 test-only), the metric is shape-only (no texture), and the evaluation relies on a single GPU with a modest PointNet backbone, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse shape collections or to textured meshes. A key open question is whether the local-attention-plus-LoRA recipe generalizes beyond the specific reconstruction/generation methods used to create distortions, and whether the strong performance gap over SAUCD reflects a fundamental limitation of frequency-domain metrics or simply a distribution shift.
