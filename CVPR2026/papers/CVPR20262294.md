## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient image-to-video adapters model temporal dynamics using time-domain convolutions or attention, which our frequency analysis shows concentrates representational energy at very low and very high frequency bands while underutilizing mid-frequency bands (≈1–10 Hz) where fine-grained motion cues reside. This spectral bias limits the ability to distinguish subtle temporal differences that define fine-grained actions such as opening vs. closing a bottle or different somersault postures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a uniformly sampled video clip of 16 or 32 RGB frames, tokenized by a frozen ViT backbone (CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14, or DINOv2) into patch embeddings of shape T×N×D. Only the lightweight adapter parameters (3.6M for Frame2Freq-ST, 7.3M for Frame2Freq-MS) and a linear classification head are trained; the backbone remains frozen throughout. The output is a single action class label per clip, evaluated on five benchmarks spanning fine-grained body movement, human-object interaction, and few-shot settings.

## Method

Frame2Freq inserts frequency-aware bottleneck adapters (FC_down → spectral processing → FC_up) between transformer blocks of a frozen VFM, replacing the temporal convolutions of ST-Adapter with FFT-based operations along the time axis. The single-scale variant (Frame2Freq-ST) applies a Short-Time Fourier Transform with a Hann window to obtain a time-frequency spectrogram, refines it with depthwise 3D convolutions in both temporal and frequency dimensions, and reconstructs via inverse STFT. The multi-scale variant (Frame2Freq-MS) splits channels into a frequency branch that applies K parallel FFTs at different window sizes [T, T/2, T/4] and a temporal branch with a depthwise convolution, then concatenates both outputs before the up-projection. A residual connection adds the adapter output back to the backbone features, preserving spatial semantics while injecting frequency-aware temporal refinement. An ANOVA-inspired Frequency Discriminability Analysis motivates the design by showing that Frame2Freq adaptively shifts discriminative energy toward the mid-frequency bands most correlated with each dataset's fine-grained action structure.

## Results

On Diving48, Frame2Freq-MS achieves 92.2% top-1 accuracy with only 7.3M trainable parameters, outperforming the main PEFT baseline ST-Adapter (90.4%) by +1.8% and surpassing the best fully fine-tuned model (ORViT, 88.0%) by +4.2%. On the three domain-specific fine-grained datasets (Drive&Act, IKEA-ASM, HRI-30), Frame2Freq-ST/MS with DINOv2 backbone delivers top-1 accuracies of 82.0%, 78.1%, and 89.8% respectively, exceeding ST-Adapter by +6.8%, +7.6%, and +4.3%. On SSv2, Frame2Freq-MS reaches 70.4% (ViT-B/16) and 72.1% (ViT-L/14), matching Uniformerv2 (72.1%) while using less than 5% of its trainable parameters. In few-shot SSv2 settings, Frame2Freq-MS attains 55.7%/71.3% (1-shot/5-shot, SSv2-Small) and 66.9%/82.0% (1-shot/5-shot, SSv2-Full), setting new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Frame2Freq-MS outperforms the main PEFT baseline ST-Adapter on all five datasets: +1.8% on Diving48 (92.2% vs 90.4%), +6.8% on Drive&Act (82.0% vs 75.2% with DINOv2), +7.6% on IKEA-ASM (78.1% vs 70.5%), +4.3% on HRI-30 (89.8% vs 85.5%), and +0.9% on SSv2 (70.4% vs 69.5%). Against other PEFT methods, Frame2Freq-MS gains roughly +3.5% over AIM and DualPath on Diving48, and +4–9% over M2-CLIP and VitaCLIP on the domain-specific benchmarks. On SSv2, Frame2Freq-MS (ViT-L/14) matches Uniformerv2 at 72.1% top-1 while using only 19M vs 574M trainable parameters, and slightly edges out DualPath (70.4% vs 70.3% with ViT-B/16). In few-shot SSv2, Frame2Freq-MS surpasses D2ST-Adapter by +0.7 to +0.1% and ST-Adapter by +2.6 to +2.5% across 1-shot and 5-shot settings. Notably, the smaller Frame2Freq-ST variant (3.6M params) is competitive with or slightly better than Frame2Freq-MS on the domain-specific datasets, where a single characteristic temporal scale dominates, while Frame2Freq-MS is clearly stronger on Diving48 where multi-component body movements require multi-scale frequency modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

Frame2Freq extends the PEFT adapter lineage initiated by ST-Adapter [32] by replacing its time-domain depthwise convolutions with FFT/STFT-based spectral operations, while retaining the same bottleneck (FC_down → non-linearity → FC_up) structure. It departs from the broader PEFT-for-video family (AIM, DualPath, M2-CLIP, Omni-CLIP) that models temporal dynamics exclusively through convolutions or attention in the time domain, and from prior Fourier-in-CV work (Fast Fourier Convolution, GFNet, Spectformer, DTF) that used spectral transforms for spatial token mixing or full-model fine-tuning. The paper is the first to position frequency-domain analysis as the primary mechanism for temporal adaptation of frozen image VFMs within a parameter-efficient framework, supported by a novel ANOVA-inspired Frequency Discriminability Analysis that quantifies per-band class separability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Frequency Discriminability Analysis provides a principled, quantitative explanation for why existing temporal adapters struggle with fine-grained and nearly symmetric actions: they underutilize mid-frequency bands where discriminative motion cues reside. This reframes the image-to-video adaptation problem from 'how to model frame-to-frame differences' to 'which frequency bands carry action-specific signal,' opening a new design axis for temporal adapters. Limitations include modest gains on coarse-label datasets (SSv2 +0.9%), reliance on a fixed set of FFT window sizes, and the fact that the spectral analysis is performed post-hoc rather than learned end-to-end. The authors note that wavelets, multi-resolution filters, and learnable time-frequency operators are natural next steps, and the method's applicability to longer videos or streaming settings remains unexplored.
