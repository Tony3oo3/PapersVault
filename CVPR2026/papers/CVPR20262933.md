## Problem

Existing adapter-based continual learning methods for VLMs enforce (near-)orthogonality constraints to isolate task-specific adapters, which reduces interference but suppresses cross-task knowledge transfer and creates knowledge silos. The paper identifies, via PAC-Bayesian analysis, that this orthogonality assumption is suboptimal: after per-task convergence, adapters should instead satisfy phase-like transition constraints that allow smooth coupling between related tasks while still preventing destructive interference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone and produces class labels via independent classification heads on two parallel branches. Adapters are sparsely inserted at only 4 of 12 transformer layers (layers 3, 6, 9, 12), each replicating the architecture of its parallel MLP block, reducing trainable parameters by 36.96% relative to standard per-layer adapter baselines. The two branches share the same backbone features and current adapter but differ in whether frozen historical adapter outputs are aggregated (conditional) or omitted (marginal).

## Method

PACT derives a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound for sequential adapter training whose KL term decomposes into a plasticity component (KL between conditional and marginal posteriors of the current adapter) and a stability component (KL between the current adapter posterior and its initialization prior). This is operationalized through a two-branch ViT: a conditional branch that mean-aggregates all frozen historical adapters with the current adapter, and a marginal branch using only the current adapter, each with its own cross-entropy loss. A Stable Adapter Initialization (SAI) clones the parallel MLP block into each new adapter with a sigmoid gate for convex mixing, ensuring prediction-preserving insertion, while Prior Anchoring (PA) adds a KL penalty against the MLP-induced prior to limit posterior drift. A phase-like transition mechanism uses a fixed bank of Gaussian probes to compute a scale-normalized Frobenius distance between the current adapter's response matrix and the nearest historical adapter's, converting this into an exponential freeze weight that smoothly gates the marginal and stability losses—activating them only in the post-convergence regime where the bounded-loss assumption holds.

## Results

On the MTIL benchmark (Order-I, 11 datasets), PACT achieves 71.76% Transfer, 81.05% Average, and 90.54% Last accuracy, surpassing the strongest baselines (AFA, ConDU) by +1.46, +2.55, and +2.94 points respectively. On CIFAR-100 CIL (10 steps), PACT reaches 87.65% Avg and 80.57% Last, outperforming AFA (86.74/79.33) and MoE-Adapters (85.21/77.52). In the 16-shot FS-MTIL setting, PACT attains 72.7% Transfer and 84.4% Last, the best among all compared methods. The method also reduces trainable parameters by 36.96% relative to standard adapter-based baselines via sparse insertion (one adapter every three ViT layers).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACT outperforms all 16 baselines on the primary MTIL benchmark, with the closest competitors being AFA (70.3 Transfer / 78.5 Avg / 87.2 Last) and ConDU (70.3 / 78.3 / 86.2), both trailing by 1.4–2.9 points. On the 5-shot FS-MTIL setting, PACT leads on Transfer (70.5 vs. AFA's 70.2) and Last (80.7 vs. AFA's 79.4), though AFA edges ahead on Average (74.1 vs. 72.0); in the 16-shot setting PACT dominates all three metrics (72.7 / 76.1 / 84.4 vs. IAP's 70.9 / 72.5 / 77.7). For CIL on CIFAR-100 (10 steps), PACT achieves 87.65% Avg and 80.57% Last, beating AFA by +0.91/+1.24 and MoE-Adapters by +2.44/+3.05; on TinyImageNet (10 steps) the margins are similar (84.22/79.68 vs. AFA's 81.36/78.01). Classical methods (LwF, iCaRL, ZSCL, EEIL, UCIR, Bic, DER, DyTox) are consistently 5–20 points behind PACT across all settings, confirming that adapter-based PEFT with PACT's phase-like constraints substantially outperforms rehearsal- and regularization-based CL approaches. The only metric where PACT does not lead is 5-shot FS-MTIL Average, where AFA's 74.1% exceeds PACT's 72.0% by 2.1 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayesian framework from single-task learning (building on [63]) to the sequential CL setting, deriving a conditional-marginal KL decomposition that separates plasticity and stability terms. It departs from the dominant PEFT-CL paradigm—where task-specific adapters are optimized to convergence under (near-)orthogonality constraints (e.g., ORCO, BiLoRA, orthogonal-LoRA)—by arguing via PAC-Bayes that post-convergence, adapters should instead satisfy phase-like transition constraints that allow smooth coupling across tasks. The two-branch ViT architecture with Stable Adapter Initialization and Prior Anchoring operationalizes this theory, positioning PACT as a principled alternative to the orthogonality-based isolation strategy that has dominated recent adapter-based CL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PAC-Bayesian derivation provides a theoretical justification for why (near-)orthogonality constraints, widely adopted in PEFT-CL, may be suboptimal by suppressing beneficial cross-task transfer—a concern previously noted empirically in deep learning and neuroscience but not formalized in this setting. The 36.96% reduction in trainable parameters through sparse adapter insertion makes the approach practically attractive for resource-constrained deployment. However, the evaluation is limited to CLIP ViT-B/16, the PAC-Bayesian analysis relies on several standing assumptions (bounded loss, finite overlap constant), and the 'phase-like transition' is ultimately a smooth exponential gating mechanism rather than a true thermodynamic phase transition, leaving open whether the theoretical framing adds predictive power beyond a well-tuned adaptive regularizer. Scaling to larger VLMs, longer task sequences, and non-adapter PEFT variants (LoRA, prompts) remains unexplored.
