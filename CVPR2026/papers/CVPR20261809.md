## Problem

Existing HMER methods optimize text-level similarity (BLEU, ROUGE, exact match) as a proxy for recognition quality, but this misaligns with the true goal of visual fidelity: distinct LaTeX sequences can render identically (causing false negatives), while minor syntax errors can catastrophically distort rendering yet receive only mild text-level penalties. This gap prevents text-based rewards from effectively guiding models toward visually correct predictions, especially on complex expressions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-line or multi-line handwritten mathematical expression image as input and produces a variable-length sequence of LaTeX tokens (e.g., \frac, {, }, \sin, a, b) drawn from a vocabulary derived from ground-truth sequences. The output is not evaluated by string comparison; instead, both the predicted and ground-truth LaTeX are rendered to images (via KaTeX at a fixed font size and DPI), binarized, and compared at the pixel level to compute the reward signal during training.

## Method

The core contribution is the Image Matching Score (IMS), which renders predicted and ground-truth LaTeX to images, binarizes them, removes blank columns, encodes each column's vertical binary pattern as an integer, and computes the normalized Levenshtein edit distance between the two integer sequences to yield a [0,1] visual fidelity score. IMS is then used as a sequence-level reward within a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework called IMPO: the policy samples N candidate sequences per input, IMS scores each candidate against the ground-truth rendering, and normalized rewards within the group provide advantage estimates without a separate value network. The policy is updated via a clipped surrogate loss with KL regularization against a reference policy, making the approach model-agnostic and applicable to any sequence-based HMER backbone (PosFormer, CoMER, BTTR, ABM) as a fine-tuning stage after standard supervised pre-training.

## Results

IMPO (applied on PosFormer) achieves state-of-the-art Expression Recognition Rate (ExpRate) across all benchmarks: 63.89% on CROHME 2014, 62.04% on CROHME 2016, 66.34% on CROHME 2019, 70.67% on HME100K, and 59.56% on M²E. Gains over the PosFormer base model range from +1.01% to +1.37% in ExpRate, with larger improvements under relaxed metrics (e.g., +3.03% on ≤1 error for CROHME 2019, +3.32% on ≤1 error for HME100K). The average ExpRate improvement over the strongest prior method is 1.1%, with a maximum of 1.37% on CROHME 2019.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMPO (on PosFormer) outperforms all 21 compared baselines on every dataset and metric. On CROHME 2019, the closest prior methods are PosFormer (64.97%), CoMER (62.97%), and VLPG (62.34%); IMPO leads by 1.37%, 3.37%, and 3.99% respectively. On HME100K, IMPO (70.67%) surpasses PosFormer (69.51%), TAMER (69.50%), and ICAL (69.06%) by 1.16%, 1.17%, and 1.61%. On M²E, IMPO (59.56%) leads PosFormer (58.33%) by 1.23% and the next-best LAST [29] (56.50%) by 3.06%. The gains are consistent across relaxed metrics (≤1 error, ≤2 errors), with the largest relative improvements on longer/complex expressions, confirming that the image-level reward specifically addresses structural errors that text-level objectives miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework from DeepSeekMath [21], repurposing it from mathematical reasoning in language models to the HMER domain. It departs from the dominant sequence-based HMER training paradigm (WAP, DenseWAP, CoMER, BTTR, ABM, PosFormer), which relies on MLE or text-level RL rewards (BLEU, ROUGE), by introducing an image-level reward (IMS) that directly measures visual fidelity of rendered LaTeX. The IMS metric itself is a novel combination of column-wise binary encoding and Levenshtein edit distance, positioned as a lightweight alternative to standard image similarity metrics like SSIM. The framework is explicitly model-agnostic, demonstrated on PosFormer, CoMER, BTTR, and ABM backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine and underappreciated misalignment in HMER training: text-level rewards cannot distinguish semantically equivalent LaTeX variants or adequately penalize small syntax errors that cause large visual distortions. The model-agnostic nature of IMPO means any existing sequence-based HMER model can benefit from a relatively simple fine-tuning stage, making it practically accessible. However, the gains are modest (1–1.5% ExpRate), the approach requires LaTeX rendering during training (adding computational overhead), and it is limited to sequence-based architectures. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to tree-based decoders, whether more sophisticated image-level rewards (e.g., learned perceptual metrics) could yield larger gains, and whether the column-wise encoding is optimal for all expression layouts.
