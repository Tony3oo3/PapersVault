## Problem

Existing pose-free 3D reconstruction methods entangle scene semantics and viewpoint cues within shared feature embeddings, causing pose errors to propagate into geometry, and their pixel-aligned Gaussian heads produce redundant, overlapping splats that degrade fidelity as view count grows. TokenSplat addresses both issues by performing multi-view fusion at the token level in feature space and by architecturally separating pose reasoning from scene encoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261637/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary number N of unposed RGB images (evaluated at 3, 4, 8, 10, and 28 views at 256×256 resolution) and optionally encodes known camera intrinsics as an extra token to mitigate scale ambiguity. It outputs a set of 3D Gaussians in a canonical space (center, opacity, rotation quaternion, scale, and spherical-harmonic color coefficients) together with per-view extrinsic poses relative to a designated reference view, all produced in a single feed-forward pass without iterative refinement.

## Method

TokenSplat uses a shared ViT-Large encoder to produce per-view image tokens, which are then processed by a Canonical Scene Decoder (cross-attention for the reference view) and an Asymmetric Dual-Flow Decoder (ADF-Decoder) for the remaining views. The ADF-Decoder introduces learnable camera tokens that attend to image tokens to extract geometric cues, while only stabilized low-frequency pose signals are propagated back to image tokens, enforcing directionally constrained communication that disentangles pose from scene features. For reconstruction, a Token-aligned Gaussian Prediction module predicts coarse 3D positions and fusion confidence per token, groups spatially proximate tokens, and fuses their multi-scale features via a DPT-based residual fusion network; a one-to-many prediction head then maps each fused token to multiple Gaussians, decoupling splat density from pixel resolution. Training is end-to-end with a combined rendering loss (L2 + LPIPS) and a pose loss (MSE + unit dual-quaternion alignment), initialized from MASt3R weights for the encoder and Gaussian center head.

## Results

On ScanNet with 28 views, TokenSplat achieves 26.87 PSNR / 0.859 SSIM / 0.173 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, outperforming the best pose-free baseline (SPFSplat, 23.54 PSNR) by 3.33 dB and the best pose-required baseline (FreeSplat, 24.30 PSNR) by 2.57 dB. On RE10K with 8 views, it reaches 26.15 PSNR / 0.858 SSIM / 0.135 LPIPS, surpassing FreeSplat (25.20 PSNR) by 0.95 dB. For camera pose estimation on ScanNet 28 views, TokenSplat attains ATE 0.080, RPE-t 0.075, and RPE-r 0.709°, reducing RPE-r by 0.53° over AnySplat (1.239°) and by 3.02° over NoPoSplat (4.551°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenSplat consistently outperforms all six baselines across every view count and dataset. Against pose-free methods, the advantage grows with view count: on ScanNet 28 views, TokenSplat leads SPFSplat (the strongest pose-free baseline) by 3.33 dB PSNR and NoPoSplat by 3.66 dB, while AnySplat (both zero-shot and fine-tuned) lags by over 5 dB. Notably, TokenSplat also surpasses the pose-required FreeSplat in dense-view settings (28 views: 26.87 vs. 24.30 PSNR; RE10K 8 views: 26.15 vs. 25.20 PSNR), demonstrating that its token-level fusion eliminates the redundancy that degrades pixel-aligned Gaussian methods as views increase. For pose estimation, TokenSplat reduces RPE-r by 0.335° over VicaSplat and 0.147° over AnySplat on RE10K 8 views, and by 0.53° over AnySplat on ScanNet 28 views, attributed to the directionally constrained ADF-Decoder. In cross-dataset generalization (trained on RE10K, tested on ScanNet), TokenSplat achieves up to 0.92 dB higher PSNR than FreeSplat and reduces ATE by 0.033 over SPFSplat, confirming zero-shot transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenSplat extends the pose-free feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction line initiated by NoPoSplat and further developed by VicaSplat, AnySplat, and SPFSplat, all of which predict Gaussians in a canonical space from unposed images. Its key architectural departure is replacing pixel-aligned Gaussian prediction with a Token-aligned Gaussian Prediction module that fuses multi-view features in token space before generating Gaussians, and introducing an Asymmetric Dual-Flow Decoder that enforces directionally constrained communication between learnable camera tokens and image tokens to disentangle pose from scene semantics. The overall pipeline (shared ViT encoder, DPT-based fusion, canonical-space formulation, intrinsics token) follows NoPoSplat closely, with MASt3R weights used for initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The token-level fusion strategy addresses a practical limitation of pixel-aligned 3DGS methods—redundant and overlapping Gaussians that accumulate as view count grows—by performing aggregation in feature space rather than in 3D Gaussian space. The 28-view scalability result, where TokenSplat maintains quality while Gaussian-level fusion baselines degrade, suggests this design choice matters for dense multi-view settings that prior pose-free methods have not handled well. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or dynamic scenes, whether the known-intrinsics assumption (ablation shows removing them hurts) can be relaxed, and whether the asymmetric decoder pattern transfers to other joint estimation tasks beyond pose and reconstruction.
