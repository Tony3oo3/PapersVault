## Problem

Current VLA models fail to genuinely leverage VLM reasoning: action loss gradients degrade the VLM into a parameter-heavy fusion encoder that memorizes action patterns rather than performing compositional semantic reasoning. This manifests as severe brittleness under instruction rephrasing and sharp performance drops on reasoning-intensive tasks, exposing that the VLM-to-action-expert interface relies on opaque latent embeddings without explicit semantic supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262140/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB visual observations (from one or more cameras) and a natural-language instruction, producing three outputs: (1) a sequence of normalized 2D spatial trace waypoints generated autoregressively through the VLM's native language interface as textual coordinate tokens, (2) a sequence of discrete latent action tokens indexing into a pretrained VQ codebook, and (3) a continuous robot action chunk of shape H×D decoded via flow matching. The trace and latent token outputs serve as intermediate reasoning representations that condition the final action decoder, rather than being directly executed.

## Method

SemanticVLA employs a dual-path architecture trained in three stages. Stage 1 pretrains a two-stage VQ-VAE tokenizer: the first stage learns geometric manipulation primitives from pure trace coordinate sequences (no visual input), and the second stage grounds these geometric anchors into DINOv2 visual features via cross-attention with dual reconstruction supervision (trace + visual), producing language-free latent action tokens. Stage 2 co-trains the Prismatic-7B VLM to jointly predict trace waypoints as text tokens and latent action tokens as special vocabulary entries, establishing semantic correspondence between the two pathways without exposing the VLM to raw action supervision. Stage 3 fine-tunes a lightweight flow matching decoder conditioned on both the frozen trace encoder output (geometric guidance) and VLM hidden states from latent tokens (semantic grounding), using LoRA on the VLM to preserve reasoning capabilities. The key design principle is modality separation: traces exploit the VLM's pretrained spatial grounding for interpretable planning, while latent tokens provide visually-grounded compact primitives that compensate for trace's numerical sensitivity, and vice versa.

## Results

On LIBERO (Franka), SemanticVLA achieves a 97.0% average success rate across Spatial (98.0%), Objects (98.6%), Goal (96.8%), and Long (94.4%) suites, ranking first among all 15 compared methods. On SimplerEnv WidowX, it reaches 65.1% average success (Put Spoon 83.6%, Put Carrot 54.5%, Stack Block 40.3%, Put Eggplant 81.8%), also ranking first. In real-world experiments, SemanticVLA achieves 62.3% average success across long-horizon and reasoning-intensive tasks, outperforming the next-best baseline π₀ (48.0%) by 14.3 points. Under instruction rephrasing, SemanticVLA degrades by only 9.4% on LIBERO, compared to 18.4% for OpenVLA and 23.9% for UniVLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LIBERO, SemanticVLA (97.0%) outperforms the closest competitors UniVLA (95.2%) by 1.8 points, π₀ (94.2%) by 2.8 points, and GR00T N1 (93.9%) by 3.1 points, with the largest gap over reasoning-enhanced methods like MolmoAct (86.6%) and ThinkAct (84.4%). On SimplerEnv, the advantage is more pronounced: SemanticVLA (65.1%) leads MolmoAct (51.4%) by 13.7 points, Magma (44.8%) by 20.3 points, and π₀ (47.1%) by 18.0 points, indicating that trace-guided latent tokens transfer more effectively across visual domain shifts. In real-world experiments (Table 2), SemanticVLA (62.3% avg) surpasses π₀ (48.0%) by 14.3 points and MolmoAct (40.3%) by 22.0 points, with particularly large margins on reasoning-intensive tasks (Math Calculation: 58% vs. 43% for π₀; Word Spelling: 45% vs. 32% for π₀). Under instruction rephrasing, SemanticVLA degrades by only 9.4% on LIBERO versus 18.4% for OpenVLA and 23.9% for UniVLA, while MolmoAct shows 11.9% degradation—confirming that the dual-path design provides the most stable linguistic generalization. The ablation in Figure 6 further shows that removing trace reasoning drops latent prediction accuracy from 93.6% to 85.6% and task success from 87.6% to 79.2% under rephrasing, while trace-guided pretraining alone (vs. UniVLA's visual-reconstruction pretraining) yields >12% improvement in both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemanticVLA synthesizes two recent threads in VLA research: MolmoAct's use of explicit spatial trace coordinates through the VLM's language interface for interpretable planning, and UniVLA's latent action tokens that impose semantic constraints on discrete action representations. The key departure is the synergistic dual-path design where trace serves as both a reasoning scaffold for the VLM and a geometric supervision signal for latent token pretraining, rather than treating these as independent or competing mechanisms. The three-stage training pipeline (trace-guided VQ-VAE pretraining → VLM co-training without action decoding → flow-matching fine-tuning with weak VLM regularization) is a deliberate architectural choice to prevent the VLM from degrading into an action-fitting encoder, addressing a limitation the authors identify in both co-training and pure latent-action approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented brittleness in VLAs—catastrophic performance drops under semantically equivalent instruction rephrasing—and shows that explicit trace reasoning combined with visually-grounded latent tokens can substantially reduce this gap (9.4% vs. 18–24% degradation for baselines). The TraceX-240K dataset and the trace-guided latent pretraining recipe provide reusable infrastructure for the community. Open questions include whether the three-stage pipeline is strictly necessary or whether end-to-end training with appropriate regularization could achieve similar separation, whether the approach scales to 3D manipulation or multi-robot settings, and whether the 7B VLM backbone is sufficient for the most reasoning-intensive tasks or whether larger models would further close the gap.
