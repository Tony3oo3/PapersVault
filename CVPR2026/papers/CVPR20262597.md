## Problem

Neural scene representations (NeRF, 3DGS) achieve photorealistic rendering but remain purely visual, offering no model of what surfaces feel like when touched. Prior tactile extensions to 3D scenes produce static haptic maps or material descriptors but do not synthesize the time-varying vibrotactile acceleration signal elicited by a specific contact trajectory, speed, and normal force. The paper asks: can we predict, at run time, the tactile acceleration a user would feel when performing an arbitrary action on a reconstructed 3D scene?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262597/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a rendered RGB view and depth map from a NeRF/3DGS scene, a 3D contact trajectory with associated speed and normal-force profiles, and optionally a text action description. A conditional diffusion model first predicts a co-registered haptic map from the RGB+depth pair, which is then encoded into a single material token via a pretrained AlexNet. The output is a continuous vibrotactile acceleration trace a(t) (low-pass filtered at 1 kHz) suitable for streaming to a haptic actuator; intermediate outputs include the haptic map and a material class label used for routing to per-material HNF instances.

## Method

The core contribution is the Haptic Neural Field (HNF), an MLP (4 layers, hidden size 1024) that maps a material token and instantaneous action vector (direction, speed, normal force) to a local acceleration contribution α(t) and a non-negative haptic density σ(t). Rather than integrating along a spatial ray as in NeRF, a transmittance-based temporal renderer composites these values over a short causal window of N=5 neighboring time samples, yielding a differentiable, short-memory acceleration trace. Training uses a frequency-domain loss (MSE on the first 100 positive-frequency DFT bins of 1000-sample segments) motivated by human insensitivity to high-frequency phase. A cross-sensor contrastive module (symmetric InfoNCE with ResNet-50 encoders) aligns GelSight and DIGIT haptic-map embeddings into a shared latent space, enabling the model to transfer material knowledge across sensor formats and to augment training with synthetic action primitives (directional scratches, random rubbing) that expose anisotropic surface effects.

## Results

On the primary action-conditioned vibrotactile signal generation task (Table 3), HNF matches GACM on perceptual similarity (ST-SIM 0.85 vs. 0.85 on GelSight) while reducing spectral MSE by ~47% (3443.15 vs. 6557.32). On the DIGIT sensor (where GACM is unavailable), HNF achieves ST-SIM 0.86 and LSD 0.89, and the augmented variant HNF+ further improves to ST-SIM 0.88 and MSE 3764.00. For cross-domain material classification (Table 2), the contrastive approach lifts HaTT (GelSight) accuracy from 77.29% (GACM) to 92.13% and TnG accuracy from 57.60% (TaRF) to 76.16%, demonstrating sensor-invariant embeddings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GACM [10] on GelSight, HNF ties on perceptual quality (ST-SIM 0.85 vs. 0.85) but incurs a small LSD penalty (0.88 vs. 0.80); however, HNF substantially outperforms GACM on spectral MSE (3443.15 vs. 6557.32, a ~47% reduction), indicating a tighter fit to ground-truth magnitude spectra and less over-smoothing. GACM is also direction-invariant, producing similar spectra for different scratch directions on the same material, whereas HNF captures anisotropy. For material classification, the paper's contrastive approach (92.13% on HaTT with haptic embeddings) surpasses GACM (77.29%) by 14.8 points, and the vision-trained variant (76.16% on TnG) outperforms TaRF [3] (54.70% on TnG alone, 57.60% with TaRF data) by roughly 19–21 points. On DIGIT, where no GACM baseline exists, HNF and HNF+ demonstrate strong cross-sensor transfer (ST-SIM 0.86–0.88), validating the contrastive alignment strategy. Overall, HNF wins on spectral fidelity (MSE) and cross-sensor generalization, ties on perceptual similarity (ST-SIM), and slightly trails GACM on log-spectral distance (LSD) for GelSight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) neural scene representations (NeRF/3DGS) that render visual dynamics conditioned on actions, (2) tactile-augmented radiance fields (TaRF) that predict static haptic maps from RGB+depth via a conditional diffusion model, and (3) action-conditioned haptic texture rendering (GACM) that synthesizes vibrotactile acceleration from material texture and contact parameters. The key departure is architectural: rather than treating haptic response as a static surface property or a per-material lookup, HNF adapts NeRF's transmittance-based volumetric compositing from spatial rays to temporal neighborhoods of action states, producing a differentiable time-domain renderer. A second departure is the cross-sensor contrastive alignment (GelSight ↔ DIGIT) that enables scene-level haptic synthesis across heterogeneous tactile sensing formats, something neither TaRF nor GACM addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical path toward touch-aware 3D virtual environments where users can specify an action and receive material-specific vibrotactile feedback, bridging the long-standing gap between visual realism and physical interaction in reconstructed scenes. However, the work is data-limited: no single dataset jointly provides aligned RGB/depth, multi-sensor haptic maps, time-domain acceleration, force/torque, and dense action trajectories, so the authors must stitch modalities via contrastive matching and per-material training. Evaluation relies on objective spectral metrics (ST-SIM, LSD, MSE) rather than controlled user studies, leaving open whether the generated signals are perceptually convincing in a full XR setting. Future work on expanding action vocabulary, multi-axis sensing, and in-headset user studies will be needed to validate the approach at scale.
