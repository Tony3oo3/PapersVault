## Problem

CASSI hyperspectral imaging requires reconstructing a 3D spectral cube from a single 2D compressed measurement, and downstream semantic segmentation is a critical application. Existing approaches treat reconstruction and segmentation as a sequential two-stage pipeline, which suffers from error accumulation and, more fundamentally, ignores the mutual reinforcement between the two tasks—structural semantics can guide spectral recovery while recovered details improve segmentation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260862/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D CASSI measurement of size H × (W + d(C−1)) as input, where d is the per-band dispersion shift and C is the number of spectral bands. It simultaneously outputs a reconstructed 3D HSI cube (H × W × C) and a pixel-level semantic segmentation map with N class labels. In the FVgNET simulation setup, inputs are 512 × 540 measurements (28 bands, 400–730 nm) and outputs are 512 × 512 × 28 HSIs with 23-class segmentation maps; real CASSI measurements are 1024 × 1092 producing 1024 × 1024 × 35 cubes.

## Method

CRSDUN formulates joint reconstruction and segmentation as a single optimization problem solved by unfolding the half-quadratic splitting (HQS) algorithm into K alternating stages. Each stage performs a closed-form gradient-descent update on the HSI (incorporating the current segmentation estimate via a spectral dictionary term) followed by a proximal network update (a Cross-Aggregated Transformer, CAT), then a LISTA-inspired learned-dictionary update on the segmentation map followed by another CAT proximal update. The key architectural innovation is the Cross-Aggregated Super-Token Attention (CASTA) block in the decoder, which adaptively pools segmentation features into super-tokens, computes a spatial correlation matrix against HSI pixel features, performs multi-head self-attention among the super-tokens, and projects back to the pixel domain—enabling bidirectional information flow so that semantic cues sharpen reconstruction in object regions while pixel-level details refine segmentation boundaries. Stage-specific parameters (penalty coefficients, threshold) are predicted by a degradation-aware estimator conditioned on the previous stage's iterates, and the network is trained end-to-end with a multi-stage weighted sum of MSE reconstruction loss and cross-entropy segmentation loss.

## Results

On the FVgNET simulation dataset (50 test scenes), CRSDUN-5stg achieves 39.88 dB PSNR and 92.33% mIoU, setting new state-of-the-art in both spectral reconstruction and semantic segmentation simultaneously. The lighter CRSDUN-3stg variant reaches 39.35 dB PSNR and 90.11% mIoU with only 4.02M parameters and 59.49 GFLOPs, outperforming all two-stage baselines on segmentation while matching or exceeding their reconstruction quality. On real CASSI measurements (1024×1024×35), CRSDUN-3stg accurately distinguishes real from fake grapes where SSR-3stg+Seg fails, and correctly separates similarly-colored objects like lemon and banana.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRSDUN-5stg outperforms the strongest two-stage baseline SSR-9stg+Seg [46] by 0.38 dB in PSNR and 6.59% in mIoU, while using fewer parameters (6.73M vs. 10.3M) and fewer FLOPs (99.07G vs. 161.0G). Against RCUMP-9stg+Seg [48], CRSDUN-5stg gains 1.53 dB PSNR and 6.67% mIoU at roughly one-third the computational cost. The lighter CRSDUN-3stg matches SSR-9stg+Seg on PSNR (39.35 vs. 39.50 dB) yet surpasses it by 4.37% mIoU (90.11% vs. 85.74%), and beats SSR-6stg+Seg [46] by 2.84% mIoU with fewer FLOPs. Compared to the earlier end-to-end methods MST+++Seg [5] and CST-L-Plus+Seg [3], CRSDUN-3stg improves PSNR by 6.96 dB and 8.99 dB respectively, and mIoU by 12.20% and 18.37%. Notably, all two-stage baselines suffer from error accumulation in their sequential pipeline, which CRSDUN avoids through its cooperative alternating optimization, yielding consistent gains on both tasks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding paradigm for CASSI reconstruction (building directly on SSR's SSRB module and HQS formulation) by coupling it with a segmentation branch through a shared cooperative optimization framework. The segmentation gradient descent step adapts the LISTA learned-dictionary concept from sparse coding, while the CASTA mechanism repurposes super-token attention (from Huang et al.) to perform cross-task feature aggregation between pixel-level HSI features and semantic-level segmentation features. The key departure from prior work is the rejection of the sequential two-stage pipeline in favor of alternating, mutually reinforcing updates within a single unified network, which the authors position as the first joint reconstruction-segmentation unfolding approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that reconstruction and segmentation are mutually reinforcing rather than independent, this work challenges the default two-stage pipeline assumption in CASSI-based applications (remote sensing, material analysis, medical diagnosis) and offers a more efficient single-network alternative with fewer parameters and FLOPs than multi-stage baselines. The finding that increasing unfolding stages beyond 5 degrades segmentation while still improving reconstruction highlights an underexplored tension in deep unfolding design. Open questions include how the joint loss balancing generalizes across tasks, the sensitivity to real-world noise and mask imperfections, and whether the cooperative framework extends to other paired inverse problems beyond reconstruction-segmentation.
