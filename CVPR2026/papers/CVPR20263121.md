## Problem

Existing multi-shot video generation methods treat shot transitions as abrupt frame-level changes, lacking explicit modeling of professional editing conventions (cut-in, cut-out, shot/reverse-shot, multi-angle) and controllable transition dynamics. This results in generated sequences that resemble collections of single-shot clips without intentional cinematic narrative structure or directorial design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263121/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a hierarchical text prompt structured at three levels—global (shared subject attributes, overall narrative, transition type and description), shot-wise (content and cinematographic features per shot), and transition-level (one of four editing patterns: shot/reverse-shot, cut-in, cut-out, multi-angle)—along with per-shot 6-DoF camera extrinsics and intrinsic parameters estimated by VGGT relative to the first shot. The output is a multi-shot video where each shot boundary follows the specified editing pattern, generated by the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B DiT backbone.

## Method

ShotDirector introduces two complementary control mechanisms into a DiT-based diffusion model. First, a dual-branch camera injection module encodes camera geometry: a Plücker embedding branch computes per-pixel viewing rays (o×d, d) and processes them through convolutional layers, while an extrinsic branch feeds flattened camera matrices through an MLP; both are added to visual tokens before self-attention. Second, a shot-aware mask mechanism constrains cross-attention so each shot's tokens attend to global context (first-frame visual tokens + shared subject/transition text) and local context (current-shot visual tokens + shot-specific text), enabling hierarchical alignment of editing-pattern semantics with corresponding visual regions. Training uses a two-stage protocol on ShotWeaver40K (a curated dataset of ~40K multi-shot clips with transition-type annotations and camera poses), followed by augmentation with SynCamVideo at a 7:3 real-to-synthetic ratio to stabilize camera conditioning.

## Results

ShotDirector achieves the best Transition Confidence Score (0.8956) and Transition Type Accuracy (0.6744) among all methods, substantially outperforming the next-best end-to-end method CineTrans (0.7976 / 0.3944). It also leads on overall quality metrics: Aesthetic (0.6374), Imaging (0.6984), and FVD (68.45, lower is better). On cross-shot consistency, ShotDirector ranks second to SynCamMaster (Semantic 0.7918 vs 0.7949; Visual 0.8251 vs 0.8418), but SynCamMaster's consistency advantage comes at the cost of the lowest aesthetic score (0.5453) and weakest semantic adherence (0.7949).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShotDirector decisively outperforms all end-to-end multi-shot baselines on transition control: its Transition Confidence (0.8956) exceeds CineTrans (0.7976) by ~12% and Mask²DiT (0.2233) by ~4x, while its Type Accuracy (0.6744) nearly doubles CineTrans (0.3944). Against shot-by-shot methods (StoryDiffusion, Phantom), ShotDirector achieves far higher transition confidence and FVD (68.45 vs 92.21/86.61), indicating more coherent multi-shot structure. Compared to pre-trained video models (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.2), ShotDirector leads on every transition and quality metric, confirming that explicit camera conditioning and shot-aware masking add capabilities absent in general-purpose video diffusion. The only baseline that surpasses ShotDirector is SynCamMaster on cross-shot consistency (Semantic 0.7949, Visual 0.8418), but this comes at a steep cost: SynCamMaster has the lowest aesthetic score (0.5453) and weakest imaging quality (0.6177), and cannot control transition types (Type Acc. 0.3033). ReCamMaster, designed for smooth camera motion, fails almost entirely on abrupt shot transitions (Confidence 0.0266, Type Acc. 0.0333), highlighting the difficulty of applying single-video camera editing to multi-shot generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShotDirector builds on the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B diffusion transformer as its base architecture, extending it with a dual-branch camera injection module (Plücker embedding + extrinsic MLP) adapted from camera-controlled video generation work (ReCamMaster, CameraCtrl). Conceptually, it departs from both stitching-based multi-shot pipelines (StoryDiffusion, VideoStudio) and end-to-end multi-shot diffusion models (Mask²DiT, CineTrans, LCT, MoGA) by treating shot transitions not as frame-level visual breaks but as intentional directorial decisions governed by explicit editing patterns (cut-in, cut-out, shot/reverse-shot, multi-angle). The shot-aware mask mechanism is a novel architectural contribution that has no direct precedent in prior multi-shot or camera-control literature, bridging hierarchical text conditioning with structured token visibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that multi-shot video generation requires explicit modeling of cinematographic language, not just visual coherence, and provides the first framework and dataset (ShotWeaver40K) that operationalize this principle. The shot-aware mask and dual-branch camera injection are modular techniques likely to be adopted in future multi-shot and camera-controlled generation systems. However, the scope remains limited to four transition types and relatively short sequences, and the evaluation set of 90 prompts is small; scaling to longer narratives, more diverse editing conventions, and interactive directorial control remain open challenges that the paper itself identifies as future directions.
