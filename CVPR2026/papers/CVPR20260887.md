## Problem

Primitive-based splatting methods like 3D Gaussian Splatting achieve real-time novel view synthesis but produce representations incompatible with standard game-engine and AR/VR pipelines that rely on opaque polygonal meshes with depth buffers and occlusion culling. Existing mesh-extraction approaches are non-differentiable post-processing steps that degrade visual quality, while the closest prior work (Triangle Splatting) outputs a disconnected soup of semi-transparent triangles rather than a connected, opaque mesh. The paper asks: can one directly optimize a connected, opaque, colored triangle mesh end-to-end via differentiable rendering, without any post-hoc extraction or conversion?

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB images with SfM-estimated camera parameters and a sparse point cloud (used to initialize equilateral triangles scaled by local neighbor distance). It produces a single connected triangle mesh where each vertex stores a 3D position, a degree-3 spherical-harmonic color (48 coefficients), and an opacity scalar, while each triangle is defined solely by three vertex indices. The final mesh is fully opaque (over-drawing is zero at render time), directly importable into engines like Unity without custom shaders, and is compact—approximately 3M vertices / 100 MB on MipNeRF360 scenes.

## Method

MeshSplatting uses a two-stage optimization. Stage 1 trains an unstructured, semi-transparent triangle soup (no vertex sharing) with per-triangle interpolated vertex quantities, enabling fast scene coverage similar to point-based splatting. Stage 2 applies a restricted Delaunay triangulation over the soup's vertices to impose global connectivity without introducing new vertices, then fine-tunes the now-connected mesh so that gradients from all incident triangles accumulate at shared vertices. Two scheduling mechanisms drive the representation toward opacity: a sigmoid reparameterization of vertex opacity with a linearly increasing floor O_t (0→1), and a global smoothness parameter σ annealed from 1.0 to 0.0001 to sharpen triangle boundaries. Densification uses midpoint subdivision (splitting one triangle into four, adding only 6 new vertices when connected vs. 12 in a soup), and pruning removes low-opacity or never-rendered triangles. The training loss combines L1 + D-SSIM photometric terms with an opacity loss, a vertex-to-surface depth alignment loss, a normal consistency loss, and a Depth-Anything-v2-supervised depth loss.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, MeshSplatting achieves 24.78 PSNR, 0.310 LPIPS, and 0.728 SSIM with only 3M vertices, outperforming the closest mesh-based competitor MiLo (24.09 PSNR, 0.323 LPIPS, 0.688 SSIM, 7M vertices) by +0.69 dB PSNR while using 2.3× fewer vertices. On Tanks & Temples, MeshSplatting reaches 20.52 PSNR, 0.287 LPIPS, and 0.745 SSIM with 2M vertices, achieving the best SSIM and LPIPS among all mesh-based methods. Training takes 48 minutes (vs. 106 min for MiLo, 84 min for RaDe-GS) with only 100 MB memory (vs. 253 MB for MiLo, 1.1 GB for RaDe-GS, 1.5 GB for GOF), and renders at ~220 FPS on a consumer MacBook M4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest mesh-based competitor MiLo [14], MeshSplatting wins on Mip-NeRF360 across all three metrics (+0.69 dB PSNR, −0.013 LPIPS, +0.040 SSIM) while using 2.3× fewer vertices (3M vs. 7M) and training 2.2× faster (48 min vs. 106 min). On Tanks & Temples, GOF [49] and MiLo [14] achieve slightly higher PSNR (21.69 and 21.46 vs. 20.52), but MeshSplatting substantially outperforms them on perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.287 vs. 0.326/0.348; SSIM 0.745 vs. 0.690/0.706), indicating fewer rendering artifacts. Compared to RaDe-GS [50], MeshSplatting gains +1.22 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 with 10× fewer vertices (3M vs. 31M) and 11× less memory (100 MB vs. 1.1 GB). Against 2DGS [19], the improvement is dramatic: +9.42 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 with equivalent vertex count. The opaque Triangle Splatting† [17] variant, which is the only other method producing game-engine-ready output without post-processing, is outperformed by +3.73 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 and +3.25 dB on T&T, while also producing a connected mesh (vs. a disconnected triangle soup). Notably, 3DGS [24] and standard Triangle Splatting [17] achieve higher PSNR (27.21/27.16) but are not mesh-based and cannot be directly used in game engines, making them non-comparable in the mesh-based NVS setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshSplatting extends Triangle Splatting [17] by replacing its independent, semi-transparent triangle soup with a vertex-sharing mesh parameterization (inspired by DMesh++ [40]), then applies a single restricted Delaunay triangulation to enforce global connectivity before fine-tuning. It departs from Gaussian-to-mesh extraction pipelines (2DGS, GOF, RaDe-GS) and MiLo by making the mesh the primary optimization target end-to-end, eliminating non-differentiable post-processing steps. The densification strategy adapts Bernoulli-sampling ideas from 3DGS-MCMC [27], while the opacity and window-parameter scheduling mechanisms are novel contributions that drive the representation from semi-transparent to fully opaque during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce connected, opaque, vertex-colored meshes for large-scale real-world scenes in a single differentiable optimization pass, making the output directly loadable into standard game engines without custom shaders or post-processing. The practical implications—physics simulation, one-click object extraction via SAM 2 masks, ray tracing, and interactive walkthroughs—address a long-standing gap between neural rendering and interactive 3D graphics. A key limitation is that spherical harmonics cannot fully compensate for reduced geometric flexibility under strong regularization (−2.07 dB PSNR when removed), suggesting future work should decouple geometry from appearance via neural textures. The single-Delaunay-pass design (vs. MiLo's per-iteration triangulation) is the main source of the 2× speedup, raising the question of whether more aggressive connectivity enforcement could further reduce training cost.
