## Problem

Sound source localization (SSL) models are constrained by limited-scale (≤144K), uncurated real-world datasets where mid-frame extraction causes audio-visual semantic misalignment, and the field's rapid expansion into new tasks (segmentation, interactive localization) demands more generalizable models. This paper asks whether synthetic data from text-to-X generative models can replace, refine, and scale real SSL training data, and whether a data-centric (rather than model-centric) approach can unlock new performance ceilings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images resized to 352×352 pixels and 10-second audio clips sampled at 16 kHz, processed by a CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder and a BEATs audio encoder respectively. It produces pixel-level localization heatmaps for single-source localization, binary segmentation masks for audio-visual segmentation, and interactive localization maps that shift the highlighted region when the paired audio changes. Training uses 10-second audio clips with a batch size of 16 over 20 epochs, and the audio is projected into text-like tokens compatible with CLIP's text encoder before contrastive alignment with visual features.

## Method

The paper proposes a four-stage scalable pipeline to create synthetic clones of VGGSound: (1) human annotators build per-class concept dictionaries (avg. 3.35 visual, 7.80 audio concepts per class); (2) Mistral-7B generates diverse image caption prompts from randomly sampled concepts, while audio generation uses the raw concepts directly since T2A models struggle with full sentences; (3) Stable Diffusion 3 Medium and Stable Audio Open synthesize images and audio respectively, with no post-hoc filtering; (4) the state-of-the-art ACL-SSL model (CLIP ViT-B/16 + BEATs with self-supervised contrastive learning) is trained on fully synthetic or hybrid real-synthetic data. The key empirical finding is that replacing real mid-frame images with semantically aligned synthetic images while retaining real audio yields large gains, and scaling to 3× data by combining real VGGSound pairs with synthetic-image/real-audio pairs achieves new state-of-the-art across all three tasks (+8.62 cIoU, +5.58 mIoU, +12.16 IIoU over the real-data baseline).

## Results

At the 3× data scale (432K samples), the best variant (C) {(R,R)∪(S,R)∪(S,R)} — combining real VGGSound pairs with two sets of synthetic-image/real-audio pairs — achieves 56.66 cIoU / 69.23 cIoU Adap. (Single Source), 53.14 mIoU / 56.07 mIoU Adap. (Segmentation), and 43.26 IIoU / 59.05 IIoU Adap. (Interactive Localization). At the same 1× scale (144K), fully synthetic data matches real data (e.g., 47.97 vs. 48.03 cIoU), while simply replacing real images with synthetic counterparts (SynI, RealA) already yields +7.10 cIoU and +10.40 IIoU over the real-data baseline. The 3× variant (C) outperforms the real-data-only Original by +8.62 cIoU, +5.58 mIoU, and +12.16 IIoU across the three tasks respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is ACL-SSL [29] (Park et al., WACV 2024) trained on the original VGGSound real data (144K samples), referred to as 'Original.' The paper's best 3× variant (C) {(R,R)∪(S,R)∪(S,R)} decisively outperforms this baseline on every metric across all three tasks: +8.62 cIoU and +7.00 cIoU Adap. in Single Source Localization, +5.58 mIoU and +4.22 mIoU Adap. in Audio-Visual Segmentation, and +12.16 IIoU and +13.35 IIoU Adap. in Interactive Localization. Even at the same 1× scale, the (SynI, RealA) variant — which merely replaces VGGSound images with synthetic ones while keeping real audio — already surpasses the Original by +7.10 cIoU and +10.40 IIoU, confirming that the gains come from data quality (semantic alignment) rather than model changes. Fully synthetic training at 1× scale (47.97 cIoU) essentially ties the real-data baseline (48.03 cIoU), and at 3× scale fully synthetic data (48.91 cIoU) slightly exceeds it. The (RealI, SynA) variant, which pairs real images with synthetic audio, is the only configuration that underperforms the Original, highlighting that synthetic audio is currently less effective than synthetic images due to the relative immaturity of text-to-audio generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ACL-SSL (Park et al., WACV 2024), using its CLIP+BEATs contrastive architecture as the fixed model backbone, and shifts the research focus from model-centric improvements (false-negative-aware learning, negative-free predictive learning, visual priors) to a data-centric perspective. It draws on the broader synthetic-data-for-representation-learning literature (SynthCLIP, StableRep, CLIP data analysis) but is the first to apply text-to-X generative models to audio-visual sound source localization. The concept-dictionary and LLM-prompt-generation pipeline adapts strategies from vision-text synthetic data work, while the T2A component (Stable Audio Open) represents a novel application of audio generation to paired audio-visual training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic evidence that synthetic data can replace, refine, and scale real training data for SSL, providing a practical and reproducible pipeline (with open-source code and synthetic VGGSound clones) that the community can adopt. The finding that replacing mid-frame real images with synthetic counterparts substantially improves performance suggests a previously underappreciated data-quality bottleneck in existing SSL benchmarks. Limitations include reliance on a single model architecture (ACL-SSL), human-annotated concept dictionaries, and a maximum 3× scale due to compute constraints; open questions include whether the recipe generalizes to other SSL architectures, whether concept dictionaries can be fully automated, and whether scaling beyond 3× yields further gains.
