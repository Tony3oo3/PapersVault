## Problem

Camera pose trajectories have historically served only as geometric tools for 3D reconstruction and visual odometry, leaving unexplored whether they encode semantic information about video content. This paper is the first to systematically investigate whether a camera's trajectory—devoid of all pixels—can reveal what is happening in a video, in both egocentric and exocentric settings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 9D camera pose sequence (3D translation + 6D continuous rotation, computed relative to the sequence midpoint) sampled at 5–30 Hz, extended with up to 8 seconds of surrounding temporal context for disambiguation. It produces a 512-dim embedding vector aligned to the frozen CLIP text space, which is then applied to 5-way multiple-choice text retrieval, coarse/fine-grained classification, temporal action localization, and repetitive action counting across both ego and exo domains.

## Method

CamFormer is a lightweight 4-layer Transformer (128-dim internal, 4 heads, 256-dim FFN) trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss against a frozen CLIP text encoder, projecting camera trajectories into a shared semantic space. The key architectural innovation is contextualized trajectory encoding: the input window is extended by a randomly split duration w (into w₁ before and w₂ after), the full extended sequence is processed by the Transformer, but the final embedding is produced by mean-pooling only the output tokens corresponding to the original window—injecting global context without diluting the local representation. Pre-training uses paired trajectory-text data from Ego-Exo4D (egocentric, Aria SLAM poses) and DynPose-100K (exocentric, video captions from Panda70M), and the learned embeddings are evaluated both as frozen features (linear probing) and via end-to-end fine-tuning with a linear head.

## Results

On the egocentric 5-way MCQ text retrieval benchmark (Ego-Exo4D), CamFormer achieves 44.8% top-1 accuracy using only 0.02G MACs and 0.3M parameters, outperforming the much heavier EgoVLPv2 video baseline (38.4%, 89.49G MACs) by +6.4% overall, with particularly large gains on physical activities (56.1% vs. 39.1% in-view; 46.4% vs. 25.6% out-of-view). On the exocentric DynPose-100K retrieval task, CamFormer (ViPE poses) reaches 46.3% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest LMM baseline (ShotVL-7B at 33.1%) by +13.2%. Fusing CamFormer trajectory features with EgoVLPv2 video features yields the best overall performance on procedural activities (51.4% in-view), confirming the trajectory's complementary value. Across 10 downstream tasks on 5 datasets, consistent gains of +3.2% to +13.2% are reported over respective baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CamFormer decisively outperforms all non-video modality baselines on Ego-Exo4D: CLAP (audio, 24.6%), PRIMUS (IMU, 23.2%), and CLIP (image, 22.9%) all fall 20+ points below CamFormer's 44.8%. Against the strongest video baseline EgoVLPv2 (Ego-Exo4D checkpoint, 38.4%), CamFormer wins by +6.4% overall while using ~4,500× fewer MACs (0.02G vs. 89.49G); the gap widens to +20.8% on out-of-view physical activities where visual cues are absent. On Nymeria (zero-shot), CamFormer (31.6%) beats EgoVLPv2 (27.9%) and CLIP (28.7%), with the largest margin on 'legs' narrations (30.8% vs. 22.8%). In the exocentric domain (Table 4), CamFormer with ViPE poses (46.3%) surpasses all LMM baselines: ShotVL-7B (33.1%), Qwen2.5-VL-7B on CameraBench (27.8%), SFT Qwen3-4B-Instruct (25.7%), Gemini-3-Pro (24.7%), and Qwen3-VL-32B (21.5%). The only baseline that exceeds CamFormer standalone is Gemini-2.5-Pro (48.7%), but the authors explicitly note this is not a fair comparison due to Gemini's prohibitive cost and different paradigm; when fused with video, CamFormer reaches 46.0%, closing the gap. For proficiency estimation, CamFormer achieves a +5.4% gain over the ST-Transformer video baseline [5], and for keystep recognition, trajectory+video fusion adds +3.2% over video-only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the CLIP-style contrastive learning framework (InfoNCE loss with a frozen text encoder) to a previously unexplored modality pairing: camera pose trajectories and natural language. While prior contrastive work has connected audio (CLAP), IMU (PRIMUS), and video (EgoVLPv2) to text, camera trajectory has been conspicuously absent despite being an intrinsic property of every video. The paper also departs from the long-standing geometric use of camera pose (3D reconstruction, visual odometry) by treating it as a semantic signal, and extends egocentric motion work (optical flow, IMU-based) to both egocentric and exocentric domains with a broader suite of tasks. The contextualized trajectory encoding—extending the input window beyond the labeled clip to capture disambiguating context—is a novel architectural choice motivated by the low information density of 9D pose sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing camera trajectory as a lightweight (0.3M params, 0.02 GMACs), hardware-free modality that can be retroactively estimated from existing videos, this work opens a practical avenue for video understanding that complements or substitutes for expensive visual encoders. The robustness across pose sources (Aria SLAM, MegaSaM, ViPE, π³) suggests the approach is deployable with standard RGB-only estimation, though meaningful gaps to hardware-based poses remain. Open questions include whether the physical-vs-procedural dichotomy can be bridged with richer trajectory representations, how the approach scales to longer or more diverse activity vocabularies, and whether the proposed semantic benchmark for pose estimators will become a standard evaluation axis beyond geometric error.
