## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation systems for embodied AI fail to simultaneously satisfy open-vocabulary prompting, physical validity, and direct simulator deployability. Rule-based and data-driven pipelines lack flexibility and fine-grained control, while LLM/VLM-driven methods produce semantically plausible but physically invalid scenes without simulator-in-the-loop verification. No prior framework generates scenes that are natively simulation-ready with attached physical attributes and validated stability, which is essential for scalable robot policy training.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt describing an embodied task (e.g., 'pick up a mug on the nightstand and place it on the study desk') with an optional reference image, and produces a complete 3D indoor scene with per-object physical properties (mass, PBR metallic/roughness values, rescaled dimensions) validated for stability in Isaac Sim. Downstream, the pipeline automatically synthesizes robot action demonstrations (grasp poses via M2T2, collision-free trajectories via Curobo IK, navigation paths via RRT) that feed directly into Diffusion Policy training in Isaac Lab, yielding over 28k demonstrations for Pick-and-Place and ~50k for Mobile Manipulation across 50 diverse scenes.

## Method

SAGE operates as an LLM agent (gpt-oss-120b) over the Model Context Protocol (MCP), dynamically orchestrating four generator tools—Scene Initializer (room geometry + object list), Asset Placer (TRELLIS text-to-3D synthesis with VLM-estimated physical properties and DFS collision-avoidance placement), Asset Mover, and Asset Remover—guided by two complementary critics. A visual critic (Qwen3VL-30B) evaluates multi-view renderings for semantic completeness and spatial coherence, while a physics critic loads the scene into Isaac Sim after every edit to reject unstable or colliding placements, enabling iterative self-correction without hard-coded tool ordering. For scaling, multi-level augmentation resamples task-relevant object poses, synthesizes category-level variants via LLM text augmentation + TRELLIS, and regenerates full background layouts through the agent loop, with physics validation after each step. The resulting scenes feed an automatic action synthesis pipeline (M2T2 grasp proposals, Curobo collision-aware IK, RRT navigation) whose demonstrations train Diffusion Policy via Robomimic, demonstrating clear scaling trends and generalization to unseen objects and layouts.

## Results

On common indoor scene types (Bedroom, Kitchen, Living Room), SAGE achieves near-perfect physical stability (99.9%) and the lowest collision rate (1.9%), compared to 67.7% stability and 32.8% collisions for SceneWeaver and 63.8% stability and 22.0% collisions for Holodeck. SAGE also generates significantly more objects (48.2 avg.) with higher visual completeness (8.2/10) and functionality (9.5/10) than both baselines. For embodied policy learning, policies trained on SAGE-generated data reach 50.0% success on Pick-and-Place and 46.0% on Mobile Manipulation, approaching the privileged motion-planning agent (57.7% and 52.8% respectively), while baseline-generated data yields less than one-third of the final success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE dominates both baselines across all physical metrics: it reduces collision rate from 32.8% (SceneWeaver) and 22.0% (Holodeck) to just 1.9%, and raises stability from 67.7% and 63.8% to 99.9%, thanks to its simulator-in-the-loop physics critic. On visual metrics, SAGE leads in completeness (8.2 vs. 7.2 and 6.5) and functionality (9.5 vs. 8.9 and 6.7), while matching or slightly trailing SceneWeaver on realism (8.8 vs. 8.6) and layout (7.9 vs. 7.3). SAGE also generates roughly double the objects of both baselines (48.2 vs. 24.4 and 30.3), reflecting richer scene content. In the embodied learning experiments, policies trained on SAGE data achieve 46.0% success on Mobile Manipulation, while policies trained on SceneWeaver-style (Baseline 1) or Holodeck-style (Baseline 2) data plateau at less than one-third of that rate even with matched demonstration counts. Cross-evaluation (Table 5) further shows SAGE-trained policies generalize to baseline-generated scenes (39.1% and 24.7%), outperforming the baselines' own policies on their own scenes (13.2% and 16.2%), confirming that the quality of training data from SAGE's physics-validated, agent-orchestrated pipeline is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE extends the agentic scene-generation line initiated by SceneWeaver and GenUSD by introducing two critical missing components: simulator-in-the-loop physics validation (via Isaac Sim) and text-to-3D asset synthesis (via TRELLIS), both orchestrated through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) for adaptive tool selection. It departs from static LLM/VFM pipelines (Holodeck, LayoutGPT, ArtiScene) that follow fixed computational graphs, and from data-driven methods (DiffuScene, ATISS, CommonScenes) that inherit closed taxonomies and lack physical attributes. The closest prior work, SceneWeaver, also uses an agent with tool use but omits physics validation and physical property attachment, making its outputs non-deployable in robot simulators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAGE addresses the core data bottleneck for embodied AI by producing unlimited, physics-validated, simulation-ready scenes from open-vocabulary prompts, and the released SAGE-10k dataset provides a practical resource for the community. The clear scaling trends and the cross-evaluation result (SAGE-trained policies outperforming baseline policies even on baseline-generated scenes) suggest that data quality—particularly physical validity and diversity—matters more than distributional match for policy generalization. Key limitations include restriction to indoor scenes, rigid-body physics, and a narrow task set (pick-and-place, mobile manipulation); extending to outdoor environments, articulated/deformable objects, and coupling with online RL or real-robot validation are natural next steps.
