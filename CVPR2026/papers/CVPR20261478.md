## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors suffer severe performance degradation when images are compressed by Online Social Networks, because compression destroys the fragile high-frequency forgery artifacts they rely on while introducing misleading compression artifacts. Prior compression-robust methods require large-scale original-compression paired datasets and explicit compression labels, which are impractical to collect in real-world deployment. This paper asks how to build a compression-robust detector that exploits inherently stable image properties and requires neither paired data nor compression labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) and internally derives its phase spectrum as an auxiliary modality; no separate phase input is required. The output is a real/fake classification label, evaluated under both 2-class and 4-class settings. During training, 80% of data are pristine unpaired images and 20% form a paired subset where each image is accompanied by its JPEG-compressed version at quality factor 40, but the model does not require compression labels to identify which images are compressed.

## Method

The core insight is that JPEG quantization directly attenuates the magnitude of DCT coefficients while preserving their phase (unless quantized to zero), making the phase spectrum a naturally compression-robust signal. CPTFormer implements a dual-branch architecture: a lightweight CNN pyramid extracts hierarchical phase features at three scales, and a Phase-Harmonized Cross-Modal Interaction module performs bidirectional cross-attention where robust phase tokens guide RGB feature refinement and processed ViT tokens in turn sharpen phase representations. A Multi-Domain Modulation Adapter enables parameter-efficient fine-tuning of the CLIP-ViT backbone via parallel spatial bottleneck and DWT-based frequency recalibration branches with Squeeze-and-Excitation attention. Training employs a Difficulty-Aware Consistency Loss that exponentially up-weights the most challenging compressed samples (identified by their cross-entropy) and adds a cosine-similarity contrastive term, maximizing the utility of limited paired data without requiring explicit compression labels.

## Results

Under the quality-aware 2-class GAN setting, CPTFormer achieves a mean accuracy of 77.4%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art ODDN (71.4%) by 6.0 percentage points and QAD (69.2%) by 8.2 points. In the 4-class GAN setting, the gap widens to 6.7 points over ODDN (79.3% vs. 72.6%). On diffusion models (quality-aware), CPTFormer reaches 65.9% mean accuracy, exceeding the best baseline FF++ (62.3%) by 3.6 points. Under the more challenging quality-agnostic setting (random compression ratios), CPTFormer maintains 76.3% on GANs (2-class) and 77.4% (4-class), still leading ODDN by 5.6 and 5.3 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPTFormer outperforms all 11 baselines on mean accuracy across every evaluation setting. The closest competitor is ODDN [32], which also targets compression robustness but relies on paired original-compressed data; CPTFormer leads by 6.0 points (77.4% vs. 71.4%) on the 2-class quality-aware GAN benchmark and by 5.6 points under quality-agnostic conditions. QAD [13], the second-strongest baseline, trails by 8.2 points (69.2% vs. 77.4%). Frequency-domain methods such as FreqNet [31] and F3Net [20] perform near chance level (50.9% and 63.2% respectively), confirming that magnitude-based frequency cues are destroyed by compression. On diffusion models, CPTFormer (65.9%) beats FF++ (62.3%) and QAD (61.4%), though the margin is smaller than on GANs, suggesting diffusion-generated artifacts are inherently harder to detect under compression. Notably, CPTFormer achieves its gains without requiring compression labels or full paired datasets, using only 20% paired data with a difficulty-aware loss, whereas ODDN and QAD depend on explicit compression supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frequency-domain detection paradigm (FreqNet, F3Net) by shifting focus from magnitude-spectrum artifacts to the phase spectrum, which is theoretically and empirically shown to be far more stable under JPEG quantization. It departs from prior compression-robust methods (ADD, QAD, ODDN) that require large-scale original-compressed paired data and explicit compression labels, instead designing a dual-branch architecture (PHCI + MDMA) that learns compression-robust representations using only forgery labels. The use of a CLIP-ViT backbone with parameter-efficient adapters and a difficulty-aware consistency loss positions the work at the intersection of transfer learning, adapter-based fine-tuning, and hard-sample mining, but the core conceptual contribution is the phase-spectrum-driven cross-modal fusion strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical gap between clean-benchmark accuracy and real-world OSN-deployed performance is a critical bottleneck for any forgery detection system, and this paper addresses it with a principled, label-efficient approach that does not require paired compression data. The phase-spectrum robustness insight, while theoretically grounded in DCT quantization, opens a broader question: how much of the forgery signal is truly phase-encoded versus magnitude-encoded, and does this hold for non-JPEG compression (WebP, HEVC, video codecs)? Limitations include reliance on a fixed JPEG quality factor for the 20% paired subset, evaluation limited to GAN and diffusion model outputs (no autoregressive or editing-based generators), and the modest contribution of DACL (+1.4%) suggesting the core architecture does most of the heavy lifting.
