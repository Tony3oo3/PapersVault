## Problem

Existing benchmarks for reference-based image generation are limited to 1–4 reference images and define tasks along vague axes (e.g., 'what to edit'), failing to capture the challenges inherent in combining heterogeneous references such as domain mismatches, scale gaps, rare concepts, and multilingual text. This prevents systematic measurement of how model performance degrades as the number and diversity of references grow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262179/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item provides 1–8 reference images (sourced from LAION-5B with aesthetic score > 6.25 and resolution > 512 px, supplemented by synthetic images from Nano Banana and GPT-Image-1) together with a natural-language editing instruction. The model produces a single generated or edited image that must inherit subject appearance from all references while satisfying the instruction. Evaluation yields five per-criterion scores on a 10-point scale (Text-Instruction Alignment, Reference Consistency, Background-Subject Match, Physical Realism, Visual Quality) combined into a weighted total with weights {3, 3, 1, 1, 1}. The full benchmark contains 3,769 tasks across 36 task types, with at least 70 sets per multi-reference task variant.

## Method

MultiBanana is constructed through a four-stage pipeline: (1) collecting real images from LAION-5B and generating synthetic supplements to correct categorical bias, (2) filtering via YOLOv12, SAM2, CLIP, and Gemini-based harmful-content detection, (3) hierarchical classification into 6 major and 13 subcategories (person, object, background, light, tone, style), and (4) Gemini-generated editing instructions validated by both Gemini and human annotators for visual plausibility. The 36 task types span single-reference editing, 11 two-reference variants, and 24 multi-reference compositions (4 base structures × reference counts 3–8). A key design contribution is the explicit incorporation of four inter-reference diversity factors—cross-domain mixing, scale/viewpoint differences, rare concepts, and multilingual text rendering—that prior benchmarks omit. Evaluation uses VLM judges (Gemini 2.5 and GPT-5) whose agreement with human raters is validated (Pearson r up to 0.69), and the paper additionally tests three agentic baselines (Iterative Prompt Refinement, Context-Aware Feedback Generation, Selective Reference Adaptation) to probe whether multi-step planning can close the observed performance gaps.

## Results

On the MultiBanana benchmark (10-point scale, averaged over Gemini 2.5 and GPT-5 judges), GPT-Image-1 achieves the highest scores across all task categories, ranging from 7.804 on single-reference editing to 5.019 on the most challenging X-1+Background multi-reference task. Nano Banana follows closely on single-reference (7.817) but drops sharply to 3.575 on X-1+Background. Open-source models (Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, DreamOmni2, OmniGen2) score 2.0–3.6 on multi-reference tasks, roughly 2–3 points below closed-source models. Background-replacement tasks are consistently the weakest category for every model, and all models show monotonic score degradation as the number of references increases from 3 to 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-Image-1 outperforms Nano Banana on every multi-reference task, with the largest gap on two-reference editing (6.585 vs. 4.891) and X-1+Background (5.019 vs. 3.575), while the two are nearly tied on single-reference (7.804 vs. 7.817). Among open-source models, OmniGen2 leads on multi-reference tasks (e.g., 3.256 on X Objects) but is limited to at most 6 references, whereas Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 and DreamOmni2 score 2.0–3.0 on the same tasks. The Iterative Prompt Refinement (IPR) agentic wrapper improves GPT-Image-1 by 0.2–0.3 points across multi-reference categories (e.g., 5.019→5.284 on X-1+Background) but slightly degrades Nano Banana on single-reference (7.817→7.606) and X-1+Local (4.118→4.030), suggesting prompt rewriting can lose critical instruction details. Overall, closed-source models maintain a 1.5–3.0 point advantage over open-source models on multi-reference tasks, with the gap widening as the number of references grows from 3 to 8.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiBanana extends the line of reference-based generation benchmarks (MagicBrush, EMU-Edit, DreamOmni2, OmniContext) by pushing the number of references from the typical 1–4 range up to 8 and, more importantly, by introducing explicit axes of inter-reference heterogeneity—cross-domain mixing, scale/viewpoint mismatch, rare concepts, and multilingual text rendering—that prior benchmarks treated as out of scope. Conceptually, it borrows the task-construction philosophy of DreamOmni2 (compositional generation with multiple references) but reframes the evaluation around the intrinsic properties of the reference set rather than merely the editing operation. The VLM-judge evaluation protocol (Gemini 2.5 / GPT-5 with five weighted criteria) follows the trend of AI-feedback-based assessment seen in video and image editing benchmarks, and the agentic baselines (IPR, CAFG, SRA) draw on iterative prompt-refinement ideas from LLM instruction-following literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a clear and actionable trade-off: closed-source models (Nano Banana, GPT-Image-1) incorporate all reference subjects but suffer compositional distortion under high-reference or heterogeneous conditions, while open-source models (Qwen-Image, DreamOmni2, OmniGen2) produce visually cleaner outputs but silently drop reference subjects. The finding that even state-of-the-art closed models degrade substantially on cross-domain, scale-mismatch, and rare-concept tasks indicates that multi-reference generation is far from solved, and the open-source release (3,769 tasks, 36 task types) provides a standardized, reproducible basis for tracking progress. Open questions include whether the closed-vs-open failure-mode dichotomy persists as open models improve, whether agentic scaffolds can close the gap without the information-loss pitfalls observed with Nano Banana, and how to design training objectives that balance per-reference fidelity with global scene coherence.
