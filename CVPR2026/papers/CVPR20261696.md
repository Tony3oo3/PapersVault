## Problem

Deep learning models for chest X-ray multi-label classification are plagued by two distinct spurious correlations: pathological co-occurrence (models exploit visual features of co-occurring diseases as evidence for the target) and non-pathological visual confounders (implants, artifacts, and surgical remnants that create shortcut learning). Existing causal inference approaches address only one confounding source at a time and, for non-pathological confounders, lack pixel-level annotations, forcing reliance on coarse distributional assumptions that limit interventional precision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single chest X-ray image as input and additionally consumes pixel-level confounder segmentation masks (11 classes from CheXconf) and anatomical landmark coordinates as auxiliary conditioning signals during both training and inference. It outputs per-disease probability scores across 14 pathology categories, produced by fusing a confounder-adjusted global feature vector with a counterfactual local feature vector through an MLP head. The entire dual-stream pipeline completes in a single forward pass without modifying input pixels.

## Method

DARC employs a dual-stream causal architecture built on a ConvNeXt backbone. The Global Stream implements back-door adjustment for non-pathological confounders: a segmentation model trained on the newly constructed CheXconf dataset (40,213 pixel-level annotations across 11 confounder classes) produces class-wise masks, which are used for weighted pooling to build a confounder state vector that drives FiLM-based affine modulation of global features. The Local Stream performs counterfactual reasoning for pathological co-occurrence: a pre-trained anatomical landmark detector defines disease-relevant patches, and a disease-query attention mechanism (anatomic-causal attention) extracts label-specific visual evidence, approximating the prediction that would result if the co-occurring pathology were absent. The two streams are fused by channel concatenation followed by an MLP, and training uses a composite loss combining Asymmetric Loss for classification, a KL-divergence causal consistency term aligning final predictions with the local counterfactual stream, and a confounder-disease orthogonality loss that penalizes alignment between confounder embeddings and disease queries when the confounder is absent.

## Results

On ChestX-ray14, DARC achieves a mean AUC of 0.857, outperforming the strongest causal-inference baseline CDCL (0.849) by 0.8 points and the best non-causal method HydraViT (0.841) by 1.6 points. On CheXpert, DARC reaches 0.907 mAUC, surpassing PTRN and CDCL (both 0.896) by 1.1 points. Overall metrics include mAP of 0.326 (CXR14) and 0.427 (CheXpert), with F1 scores of 0.428 and 0.594 respectively. Per-class gains are most pronounced on Nodule (+4.0 over CDCL) and Emphysema (+3.3 over CDCL), while Pneumonia shows a slight drop (0.711 vs. 0.757 for CDCL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARC outperforms all nine baselines on both benchmarks. On ChestX-ray14, the largest margin is against Nie et al. [28] (0.805 mAUC, a causal method handling only non-pathological confounders), where DARC gains 5.2 points; the closest competitor is CDCL [19] (0.849), another causal method targeting only pathological co-occurrence, which DARC surpasses by 0.8 points. On CheXpert, DARC (0.907) beats PTRN [7] and CDCL [19] (both 0.896) by 1.1 points, and ConsultNet [11] (0.889) by 1.8 points. Notably, DARC's advantage over the two single-confounder causal baselines (Nie et al. and CDCL) is consistent across both datasets, supporting the paper's claim that jointly addressing both confounding types yields greater gains than either alone. The only per-class regression is on Pneumonia (0.711 vs. CDCL's 0.757 on CXR14), suggesting the counterfactual masking may occasionally suppress genuine pneumonia signals when co-occurring with other pathologies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate causal-inference threads in CXR classification: back-door adjustment for non-pathological visual confounders (Nie et al. 2023, Carloni et al. 2024) and counterfactual reasoning for pathological co-occurrence (CDCL 2025, Xie et al. 2024). Prior causal methods each addressed only one confounding source and relied on coarse distributional assumptions for confounders due to the absence of pixel-level annotations; DARC departs from this by introducing CheXconf, the first pixel-level confounder dataset, enabling precise, segmentation-guided back-door adjustment via FiLM modulation. The Local Stream's anatomy-guided counterfactual attention draws on anatomical-landmark-detection work (Agu et al. 2021, Karwande et al. 2022) but repurposes it as a causal intervention mechanism rather than a spatial prior for classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CheXconf dataset (40k pixel-level annotations across 11 confounder classes) is a practical resource that the broader CXR community can use for confounder-aware training and evaluation, independent of DARC itself. The unified dual-adjustment framework provides a theoretically grounded template for handling heterogeneous spurious correlations, though its reliance on a pre-trained segmentation model and anatomical landmark detector introduces additional failure modes not fully stress-tested. Open questions include whether the linear-response approximation for the Total Direct Effect holds under larger distributional shifts, whether the 11-class confounder taxonomy generalizes to other imaging modalities, and whether the approach extends to 3D CT or video-based diagnostics.
