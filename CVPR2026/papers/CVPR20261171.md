## Problem

Existing image-event fusion methods for monocular depth estimation rely on CNNs (limited global context) or Transformers (quadratic complexity), making them inefficient for long-sequence depth tasks. Moreover, most fusion approaches directly combine features without addressing the inherent domain gap between sparse, edge-driven events and dense, static images, leading to semantic bias and degraded accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261171/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw event stream discretized into a K-channel voxel grid E ∈ R^{K×H×W} (K=5 temporal bins) and a paired grayscale/RGB image I ∈ R^{C×H×W}, both at the same spatial resolution. The event stream is first converted to an image-like representation via bilinear interpolation and synchronized with the image. The output is a dense per-pixel depth map, predicted as normalized log-depth values and recovered to metric depth via an exponential mapping D_max·exp(−α(1−D̂)).

## Method

AIMDepth is a U-Net-like architecture built entirely on state space models (VSS Blocks with SS2D for the encoder, CVSS Blocks for the decoder) to achieve linear-complexity sequence modeling. Three key modules address cross-modal alignment: (1) Spectral Cross-modal Prior Guidance (SCPG) performs input-level alignment in the frequency domain—Event-targeted Amplitude Transfer replaces low-frequency amplitude of events with that of the image, while Image-targeted Phase Enhancement concatenates phase maps from the two most responsive event channels onto the image; (2) Asymmetric Modal-aware Encoder (AME) uses a weight-shared four-stage VSS encoder but selects different feature levels per modality (shallow layers for image spatial detail, deeper layers for event semantic/temporal structure); (3) Modality-interactive Local Refinement (ModiLocal) fuses features via an Interactive Selective Scan that exchanges the B and D matrices between modalities for cross-modal state modulation, followed by a Local Spatial Selective Scan for fine-grained boundary refinement and a SE block for channel reweighting. Training uses a hybrid L1+L2 loss on normalized log-depth with AdamW (lr=2e-4, weight decay 0.8) for 30 epochs on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On the MVSEC dataset (average of day1 and night1), AIMDepth achieves A=0.306, R=0.371, δ₁=0.622, δ₂=0.804, δ₃=0.905, ranking first in 4 out of 5 metrics. On the DENSE dataset (Town10), it achieves A=0.178, R=0.269, δ₁=0.821, δ₂=0.895, δ₃=0.947, with a 16.2% improvement in log-RMS error and 9.9% in δ₁ over the best baseline. In terms of efficiency, AIMDepth requires only 8.69 GFLOPs and 45.07M parameters, substantially lower than RAMNet (119.89 GFLOPs) and UniCT (59.22 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIMDepth outperforms all 10 baselines on the MVSEC average in 4 of 5 metrics (R, δ₁, δ₂, δ₃), with the closest competitor being UniCT (A=0.266 vs. 0.306) on absolute relative error, while EReFormer is nearest on R (0.374 vs. 0.371). On DENSE, AIMDepth leads in R (0.269 vs. 0.335 for EReFormer), δ₁ (0.821 vs. 0.747), δ₂ (0.895 vs. 0.839), and δ₃ (0.947 vs. 0.908), though EReFormer edges it on A (0.172 vs. 0.178). Transformer-based ER-F2D underperforms significantly (δ₁=0.335 average), confirming the quadratic-attention bottleneck. CNN-based RAMNet and SRFNet trail by large margins (A≈0.37 and 0.29 respectively), reflecting limited global context modeling. In the supplementary comparison, AIMDepth (45M params, trained on 8.5K samples) surpasses DepthAnything V2-base (97.5M params, pretrained on 62M images) by 33–44% in A and 32–73% in δ₁ across day and night sequences, demonstrating that event-image fusion with explicit alignment compensates for orders-of-magnitude less training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends event-image fusion for monocular depth estimation from CNN-based (RAMNet, SRFNet) and Transformer-based (ER-F2D, UniCT, HMNet) architectures to a state-space-model backbone, specifically VMamba's VSS blocks with SS2D scanning. Its key conceptual departure is introducing explicit modality alignment at two levels—frequency-domain input alignment (SCPG) and asymmetric feature-level selection (AME)—before fusion, whereas prior methods fuse features directly without addressing the sparse-event vs. dense-image domain gap. The Modality-interactive Local Refinement module further departs from prior fusion designs by exchanging SSM parameters (B, D matrices) between modalities during state evolution, a cross-modal modulation mechanism not seen in earlier CNN or attention-based fusion blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.69 G FLOPs footprint (vs. 59–120 G for competing fusion methods) while matching or exceeding their accuracy makes Mamba-based fusion practically attractive for real-time robotics and autonomous driving where long event sequences are common. The frequency-domain alignment strategy (SCPG) offers an interpretable, parameter-free mechanism for cross-modal prior transfer that could transfer to other event-image tasks beyond depth. Open questions include whether the asymmetric encoder design generalizes to other sparse-dense modality pairs, whether the linear-complexity advantage persists at higher resolutions or longer temporal bins, and whether the modest accuracy gains over the best Transformer baselines (UniCT, HMNet) on daytime scenes justify the architectural switch when the nighttime advantage is the primary driver.
