## Problem

Estimating 6-DoF relative pose between uncalibrated aerial and ground images is severely degraded by extreme viewpoint, scale, and appearance gaps that cause both classical correspondence pipelines and recent 3D foundation models (MASt3R, VGGT, π³) to fail. Existing cross-view localization methods assume calibrated satellite imagery and constant ground-camera height, leaving the variable-altitude, oblique-angle UAV setting largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261742/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of uncalibrated RGB images (one aerial, one ground) at arbitrary aspect ratios (max dimension 512 px during training) and produces a refined 6-DoF relative pose [R|t] along with per-view intrinsics (vfov, principal point), gravity-aligned pitch/roll, dense 3D point maps in a shared aerial coordinate frame, and canonical BEV point maps on a 200×200 grid (100 m × 100 m for ground, variable scale for aerial) with 24-dim local descriptors. Unlike standard pose estimation benchmarks that assume known intrinsics, VGA predicts them jointly, enabling fully self-calibrated operation.

## Method

VGA extends the MASt3R Siamese ViT backbone with two additional branches: a calibration branch (Transformer Decoder + MLP head) that predicts per-view intrinsics and gravity-aligned pitch/roll, and a Neural BEV Projector that maps geometry-aware tokens into a shared gravity-aligned 2D metric space via learnable queries. Training jointly supervises dense perspective fields (up-vectors and latitude per pixel), calibration parameters, BEV point maps (confidence-aware metric regression), and BEV descriptors (InfoNCE matching) on 309k MatrixCity and 105k AerialMegaDepth pairs. At inference, a lightweight joint optimization refines the feed-forward pose by minimizing a weighted sum of Sampson reprojection error, a gravity-alignment energy (‖R·g_g − g_a‖²) that constrains roll/pitch, and a BEV-derived azimuth energy (‖log(R_z^T R_z(α))‖²) obtained from Procrustes/Kabsch-Umeyama alignment of the two BEV point maps under RANSAC, adding only a few milliseconds of overhead.

## Results

On the MatrixCity (BigCity) benchmark, VGA achieves 45.64% RTA@25°, 46.70% RRA@25°, and 34.97% AUC@30°, outperforming the strongest baseline MASt3R fine-tuned on AerialMegaDepth+MatrixCity (37.10% RTA@25°, 33.54% RRA@25°, 29.12% AUC@30°) by 5–8 percentage points. On the zero-shot ACC-NVS1 and ULTRRA benchmarks, VGA reaches 76.34% RRA@25° and 47.45% AUC@30°, surpassing the next-best method π³ (75.70% RRA@25°, 43.45% AUC@30°) by 6.9% and 4.0% respectively. The inference-time joint optimization alone contributes a consistent 5–8% gain over the feed-forward MASt3R baseline, demonstrating the value of the gravity and planar alignment priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGA decisively outperforms all baselines on both benchmarks. On MatrixCity (BigCity), VGA surpasses the strongest directly comparable baseline—MASt3R fine-tuned on AerialMegaDepth+MatrixCity—by 8.54 points in RTA@25° (45.64 vs. 37.10) and 5.85 points in AUC@30° (34.97 vs. 29.12), with the gap widening at tighter thresholds (e.g., 10.72 points in RRA@25°). The 3D foundation models VGGT and π³, which were not trained on aerial-ground pairs, achieve only 1.66% and 1.52% AUC@30° respectively, confirming their inability to handle extreme viewpoint differences. On the zero-shot ACC-NVS1/ULTRRA benchmarks, VGA edges out π³ (the only other method trained on MatrixCity) by 1.5 points in RRA@25° and 4.0 points in AUC@30°, while maintaining a large margin over MASt3R (AerialMegaDepth) by 23.44 points in RRA@25°. Classical matching methods (LoFTR, SP+SG, ROMA) remain far behind, with ROMA being the strongest among them at 3.59% AUC@30° on MatrixCity, highlighting the difficulty of correspondence-based approaches under extreme aerial-ground viewpoint gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGA extends the MASt3R Siamese vision-transformer backbone by adding two geometric branches: a gravity-guided calibration head (drawing on Perspective Fields and GeoCalib for single-image calibration) and a Neural BEV Projector that maps both views into a shared top-down metric space (inspired by cross-view localization methods like SliceMatch and OrientNet). The key departure from 3D foundation models (DUSt3R, VGGT, π³) is the explicit injection of physically grounded priors—gravity alignment and planar azimuth—into an inference-time joint optimization, rather than relying solely on learned dense correspondences. It also departs from prior cross-view geo-localization work, which typically assumes satellite imagery with known intrinsics and fixed ground-camera height, by handling uncalibrated, variable-altitude oblique UAV views with full 6-DoF estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in ground-air collaboration: existing 3D foundation models degrade sharply on aerial-ground pairs with extreme viewpoint and scale differences, while satellite-ground cross-view methods cannot handle variable UAV altitudes. The inference-time geometric optimization (gravity + Procrustes BEV alignment) offers a principled, lightweight refinement that narrows the pose search space without retraining, and the ~11% AUC@30° gain over the next-best method on MatrixCity is meaningful for deployment. However, the framework remains limited to two-view inference, the BEV alignment prior contributes only marginally beyond the gravity prior (suggesting BEV projection is still an under-solved sub-problem), and generalization to multi-frame or sequential settings is left as future work.
