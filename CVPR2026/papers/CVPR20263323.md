## Problem

Monocular semantic scene completion suffers from a dual imbalance: over 93% of 3D voxels are empty, causing existing methods to waste computation on uninformative regions, and foreground semantic classes are rare, leading to overfitting and poor generalization. Prior approaches apply uniform processing across all voxels and lack explicit mechanisms to differentiate empty from occupied regions during feature refinement or to target enhancement of underrepresented categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263323/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (1280×384 on SemanticKITTI, 1408×384 on KITTI-360) and produces a dense 3D semantic occupancy grid of size 256×256×32 covering 51.2×51.2×6.4 m at 0.2 m resolution, with per-voxel labels across 19–20 semantic categories. During training, the pipeline additionally consumes category-level text prompts (dynamically sampled from retained foreground classes after dropout) and a dense depth estimate from a DepthNet to guide 2D-to-3D feature lifting.

## Method

VoxSAMNet introduces three synergistic mechanisms. First, a Foreground Modulation Strategy combines Foreground Dropout (randomly reassigning foreground labels to the empty class during training) with a Text-Guided Image Filter (TGIF) that uses CLIP-style cross-attention to suppress residual 2D feature responses from dropped categories, producing semantically focused features for 3D lifting. Second, the Dummy Shortcut for Feature Refinement (DSFR) module applies a voxel-wise occupancy classifier to split the volume: empty voxels are assigned a shared learnable dummy embedding via a lightweight Hadamard blend, while occupied voxels undergo multi-head 3D deformable attention against the filtered 2D feature map, reducing deformable-attention FLOPs by 98.5%. Third, a denoising masked-voxel autoencoder with a 3D U-Net completes occluded regions through contextual reasoning. The overall pipeline is trained end-to-end with a joint loss combining scene-class affinity, semantic/geometry/depth terms, and an occupancy-specific precision-recall-specificity BCE loss.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI hidden test set, VoxSAMNet achieves 47.88% voxel IoU and 18.19% mIoU, setting a new state-of-the-art among single-frame methods and surpassing stereo/multi-view baselines (VLScene: 17.52% mIoU; ScanSSC: 17.40% mIoU). On SSCBench-KITTI-360, the method reaches 47.22% IoU and 20.21% mIoU, outperforming the previous best single-frame method VLScene (19.10% mIoU) by 1.11 points. The DSFR module reduces deformable-attention FLOPs by 98.5% (from 24.512 G to 0.377 G) while maintaining full-grid representational completeness, yielding a 71.9% overall FLOPs reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among single-frame monocular methods on SemanticKITTI, VoxSAMNet (18.19% mIoU) outperforms the closest competitor DISC (17.35%) by 0.84 points and VisHall3D (17.46%) by 0.73 points, with the largest gap over MonoScene (11.08%) at 7.11 points. Against stereo/multi-view baselines, VoxSAMNet surpasses VLScene (17.52%) by 0.67% and ScanSSC (17.40%) by 0.79% in mIoU, despite using only a single RGB image. On SSCBench-KITTI-360, the advantage widens: VoxSAMNet (20.21% mIoU) leads VLScene (19.10%) by 1.11 points and LOMA (18.28%) by 1.93 points. The one method that exceeds VoxSAMNet on SemanticKITTI is the temporal method SOAP (19.09% mIoU), which leverages multi-frame inputs; VoxSAMNet does not claim superiority over temporal approaches. Per-class gains are most pronounced on rare foreground categories (e.g., bicycle: 4.4% vs. 4.0% for DISC; traffic sign: 10.5% vs. 8.7% for DISC), consistent with the foreground modulation strategy's design goal.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoxSAMNet extends the monocular SSC pipeline established by MonoScene (2D-to-3D lifting with 3D U-Net) and the sparse voxel initialization paradigm from VoxFormer/CGFormer, while incorporating deformable attention from BEVFormer for 2D-to-3D feature aggregation. Its key departure is the explicit bifurcation of voxel processing: empty voxels are routed through a lightweight dummy embedding (DSFR) rather than undergoing the same expensive deformable attention as occupied voxels, a distinction no prior SSC method makes. Additionally, the Text-Guided Image Filter (TGIF) repurposes CLIP/GroundingDINO-style cross-modal attention to modulate 2D features based on dynamically sampled category prompts, combined with foreground dropout to combat long-tailed overfitting—a combination not previously applied at the voxel level in SSC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 71.9% FLOPs reduction from DSFR (30.96 → 8.69 GFLOPs) is practically significant for real-time deployment in autonomous driving, where inference latency matters. However, the mIoU gains over recent SOTA (18.19% vs. 17.46% on SemanticKITTI) are modest, and the method still depends on a separate depth estimation network as a critical prior, limiting end-to-end robustness. Open questions include whether the text-guided filtering generalizes beyond the fixed driving vocabulary used here, whether the dummy-shortcut mechanism scales to denser or indoor scenes with lower sparsity, and whether the foreground dropout probability can be learned adaptively per scene rather than fixed.
