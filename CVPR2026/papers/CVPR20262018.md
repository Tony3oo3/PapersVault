## Problem

Existing alignment methods for radiology report generation (DPO, PPO, GRPO) require manually constructed ground truth preference datasets that are unavailable during clinical inference. Inference-time adaptation methods that avoid weight updates (prompt tuning, decoding rules) have not been adapted for clinical deployment, leaving a gap for lightweight, self-contained quality improvement of frozen RRG models at inference time without additional ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a chest X-ray image and a standard radiology prompt as input, and produces a corrected radiology report as output. Internally, the RRG model's own generated report is parsed into structured FFL finding patterns (core finding name, presence/absence, anatomical location, laterality) with bounding-box localizations derived from a 36-region chest X-ray lexicon. The corrected report retains the same sentence-level structure but with findings flipped, removed, or re-localized based on fact-checking verdicts, and the phrasal grounding information (IoU overlaps between predicted and indicated finding locations) is used as an auxiliary training signal.

## Method

The paper introduces a phrase-grounded Automatic Preference Optimization (APO) loss that augments the standard DPO objective with a phrasal grounding term: the log-ratio of IoU-based spatial overlaps between corresponding findings in the preferred and dis-preferred responses, weighted by a hyperparameter γ. Preference data is self-generated at inference time: the RRG model's output serves as the dis-preferred response, while a frozen multi-label contrastive regression fact-checking (FC) model predicts finding veracity and location, and an LLM (Llama3.2) applies corrective edits via 5 prompt templates derived from 16 quantized error-analysis combinations. The FC model was trained on 24 million image-finding pairs (RadCheck) using supervised contrastive learning with a non-diagonal similarity matrix. Training is a lightweight fine-tune (≤10 epochs, AdamW, lr=1e-5, batch 32) on a single A100 GPU, updating the RRG model weights in-place during inference without any external ground truth reports.

## Results

On the Chest ImaGenome Gold dataset, APO improves report quality by an average of 39.98% (measured by similarity to ground truth) across 7 SOTA report generators, compared to 26.52% achievable by static FC+LLM correction alone. Using the RQ score (which captures both finding identity and spatial location), APO yields average improvements of 35.2% (Chest ImaGenome Gold), 18.9% (MIMIC-CXR), 9.1% (ChestXray8), 19.6% (VinDr-CXR), and 17.1% (CheXpert). The phrasal grounding loss contributes substantially: DPO without the grounding term achieves only 11.4% average RQ improvement versus 22.7% with APO. Across all metrics in Table 4 (Radgraph F1, RQ, BLEU, SBERT, Green), improvements range from 13.5% to 78.9%, with BLEU showing the largest gain (78.9% average).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary external baseline is GRPO (vanilla implementation using RQ score as reward). APO decisively outperforms GRPO: on Chest ImaGenome Gold, APO achieves 35.2% average RQ improvement while GRPO yields only 0.5%, and GRPO is inconsistent—less-developed models like XrayGPT actually degrade (0.37→0.36) under GRPO, whereas APO improves every generator. The internal ablation against DPO (same preference data generation but without the phrasal grounding loss term) shows APO's grounding term roughly doubles the benefit: 22.7% average RQ improvement (APO) vs. 11.4% (DPO alone) across all five datasets. A third comparison against static FC+LLM correction (no parameter updates) shows APO's parameter-level adaptation adds a further ~13 percentage points (39.98% vs. 26.52% on Chest ImaGenome Gold), confirming that the inference-time fine-tuning step—rather than sentence-level editing alone—drives the largest gains, including the ability to introduce previously missed findings (e.g., adding 'right pleural effusion' in Figure 4e).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO preference optimization framework (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) by adding a phrasal grounding loss term that penalizes spatial misalignment between described findings and their image locations, and by moving the entire alignment process to inference time. The preference data generation pipeline is built directly on the authors' prior phrase-grounded fact-checking model (MICCAI 2025) and static report correction work (ISBI 2026), which together detect finding identity and location errors and produce corrected sentences via LLM prompting. The key conceptual departure from prior alignment methods (DPO, PPO, GRPO) is that no external ground truth preference pairs are required; instead, the RRG model's own output serves as the dis-preferred response and a fact-checking model plus corrective LLM generates the preferred response on the fly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This addresses a genuine clinical deployment bottleneck: RRG models produce hallucinated findings that cannot be fixed by retraining in a live workflow, and existing inference-time methods (prompt tuning, decoding adjustments) do not update model parameters. The demonstrated 30–40% average improvement across seven SOTA generators on multi-institutional datasets is practically significant if it holds under proper evaluation. However, the paper fine-tunes on the same test images used for evaluation, which conflates adaptation with generalization and limits confidence in the reported magnitudes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond chest X-rays, whether bounding-box grounding can be replaced by segmentation for finer localization, and whether the LLM correction step introduces run-to-run variability that would be problematic in a clinical setting.
