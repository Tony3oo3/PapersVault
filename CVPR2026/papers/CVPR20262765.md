## Problem

Standard inter-annotator agreement (IAA) metrics cannot handle the instance correspondence problem inherent to vision tasks, where discrete objects must be matched across raters before agreement can be assessed. Moreover, validating any new IAA metric is circular because no objective ground truth for agreement exists, forcing prior work to rely on unverifiable heuristics or uniform noise assumptions that fail to capture structured human variability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-rater bounding boxes and class labels for the same set of images, requiring at least two raters per image (it strictly needs multi-rater metadata and cannot operate on single-annotator legacy datasets). Under the completeness assumption, any rater assigned to an image who did not annotate a discovered unit is encoded as an explicit NO_OBJECT decision rather than missing data, so false positives and false negatives are penalized as active disagreement. The primary output is a per-image Krippendorff's α score (dataset-wide mean as the headline number), alongside the full nominal reliability matrix that unlocks downstream diagnostics such as annotator vitality, collaboration clustering, and localization sensitivity curves.

## Method

KαLOS is a three-step meta-algorithm built on the 'Localization First' principle: it first resolves spatial correspondence between raters, then computes a nominal Krippendorff's α on the resulting reliability matrix. Step 1 (Principled Configuration) performs data-driven calibration by selecting the localization distance function that maximizes the Kolmogorov-Smirnov separation between observed disagreement (same-image rater pairs) and expected disagreement (cross-image chance pairs), and derives the threshold τ* via a Bayesian crossover rule where signal and chance densities intersect. Step 2 (Execution) uses a Greedy solver with a semantically-sensitive cost function ψ_soft to match annotations into disjoint units, strictly discarding any pair whose distance exceeds τ to prevent implausible associations. Step 3 computes K-α on the per-image reliability matrix, normalizing observed against expected disagreement. The framework is validated with an empirically derived synthetic noise generator that models heavy-tailed, size-dependent geometric variability and semantically ambiguous false positives from real multi-rater data, replacing the uniform-error assumptions of prior testbeds.

## Results

Across LVIS, TexBiG, and VinDr-CXR, the Greedy solver with ψ_soft achieves the highest filtered Rand Index and F1-score among all correspondence solvers, with all methods reaching precision ≈0.97–1.0. On the most challenging real dataset (NuCLS), Greedy demonstrates near-perfect permutation stability (ARI = 0.99998) compared to MGM (0.9606) and SHM (0.9327). KαLOS scores exhibit strict monotonic decrease with increasing synthetic noise magnitude and full-range utilization of the [−1, 1] scale. Return-on-investment analysis shows diminishing consensus gains beyond approximately 6–8 raters, consistent with the NuCLS redundancy analysis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates prior IAA methods as specific KαLOS configurations. Against Tschirschwitz et al. [41] (SHM + ψ_neg for document layout), the proposed Greedy + ψ_soft configuration achieves higher F1 and filtered Rand Index under realistic multi-rater noise, and is substantially more stable to annotation permutations (ARI 0.99998 vs. 0.9327 on NuCLS). Against Amgad et al. [2] (AHC + ψ_neg for nuclei segmentation), Greedy dominates across all noise levels; AHC performs worst due to uncontrolled merge propagation in agglomerative clustering. Against Kahl et al. [18] (MGM, a globally optimal multi-graph matcher), Greedy wins on recall and F1 because MGM is overly conservative, concentrating matches on high-IoU pairs and missing a noticeable fraction of valid high-IoU candidates. The ψ_soft cost function outperforms ψ_neg in both RI and F1, confirming that incorporating class sensitivity during matching improves correspondence recovery. Overall, the paper positions prior methods as special cases of the unified KαLOS framework, with the Greedy + ψ_soft configuration emerging as the empirically optimal default.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the 'Localization First, Classification Second' two-stage principle previously implemented in isolation by Amgad et al. (AHC for nuclei segmentation) and Tschirschwitz et al. (SHM for document layout) into a single meta-algorithm, KαLOS, with data-driven calibration of the distance function and threshold via Kolmogorov-Smirnov separation between observed and expected disagreement distributions (following Braylan et al.'s distributional validation logic). It departs from prior work by replacing heuristic or fixed hyperparameters with a principled calibration step, and by introducing an empirically-derived synthetic noise generator that models non-isotropic human error distributions rather than relying on uniform perturbations or black-box machine-generated labels. The framework also extends Nassar et al.'s pixel-level K-α application to the instance level, enabling their rater vitality and class difficulty diagnostics as downstream analyses on the standardized reliability matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KαLOS addresses a recognized gap in the CV community: the near-absence of rigorous, standardized inter-annotator agreement measurement for instance-based tasks, which undermines claims of benchmark progress when label quality is the true bottleneck. The empirically-derived noise generator provides a methodologically stronger validation path than prior uniform-noise or proxy-based approaches, though it currently lacks coupling to image content, limiting its use for training data augmentation. Key open questions include whether the Greedy solver's advantage over globally optimal methods generalizes to pose estimation and volumetric segmentation (where validation is currently extrapolated), and whether the framework can be applied post-hoc to legacy single-annotator datasets without additional sampling.
