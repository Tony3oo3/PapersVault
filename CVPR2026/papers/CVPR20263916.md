## Problem

Tensor-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (e.g., LoRETTA, LoTR) suffer from two critical bottlenecks: they require repeated reconstruction of tensorized weights during every forward and backward pass, incurring substantial computational and memory overhead, and they rely on multiple interdependent rank hyperparameters across tensor modes that create a heavy tuning burden. This makes tensor-based PEFT impractical for large-scale deployment despite its theoretical parameter-efficiency advantages over matrix-based methods like LoRA.

## Input / Output

ReFTA operates on pretrained Transformer weight matrices (query and value projections) stacked across all K attention layers into a single 3D tensor W0 ∈ R^{d×n×K}, where d and n are input/output feature dimensions. The fine-tuned model then processes standard inputs—RGB images (224×224 for ViT) or tokenized text sequences (for RoBERTa, LLaMA, Mistral)—and produces class labels or answer scores. The key structural detail is that adaptation is applied in feature space via a mode-3 tensor product with a fixed invertible transform (DCT or LSM-3), rather than in weight space, so the model never materializes the full reconstructed weight tensor during training.

## Method

ReFTA decomposes the stacked weight tensor via Tensor SVD (T-SVD) into a principal tensor W0^pri and a residual tensor W0^res, then fine-tunes only the principal components. The critical algebraic insight is exchanging the order of the mode-3 product (×3 U0^T) and the mode-1 product (×1 X), which transforms the computation from a weight-space reconstruction into a feature-space operation: H = H_int ×3 U0^T + W0^res ×1 X, where each slice of H_int is simply X A_k B_k (a per-layer LoRA-like adapter). This eliminates all tensor reconstruction overhead, reducing memory from O(dnK) to O(mnK) with m ≪ d. A single tensor rank R governs all per-slice ranks {R_k} via tensor singular value thresholding, avoiding the multi-rank tuning burden of Tucker/TT methods. The paper further provides the first generalization bound for tensor-based PEFT, showing the expected test error gap scales as √(RnK/m), and demonstrates that fine-tuning only principal tensor components yields lower quantization error under NF4/INT4 schemes.

## Results

On ViT-Large image classification, ReFTA (R=15, 61K params) achieves 85.67% average accuracy across five datasets, surpassing LoRA (r=16, 1.57M params) by 5.68% and LoRETTA (r=12, 152K params) by 7.16%, while using 96% fewer trainable parameters than LoRA. On RoBERTa-Large GLUE tasks, ReFTA (R=5, 0.020M params) reaches 94.8% average accuracy, matching full fine-tuning (96.4% on SST-2) and exceeding LoRA (r=1, 0.147M) by ~0.9% with 86.4% fewer parameters. On ViT-Huge, ReFTA (R=15, 76K params) achieves 76.32% average accuracy while using only 5.4% of LoRA's (r=8, 1392K) trainable parameters. On CommonsenseQA with LLaMA-3-8B, ReFTA (r=15, 119K params) matches LoRA (r=8, 3407K) at 86.08% accuracy with 96.5% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReFTA consistently outperforms all compared PEFT baselines on average accuracy while using the fewest trainable parameters. On ViT-Large, ReFTA (61K) beats LoRA (1.57M) by 5.68% in average accuracy (85.67% vs 79.99%) and LoRETTA (152K) by 7.16% (85.67% vs 78.51%), with LoRETTA showing a notable collapse on OxfordPets (78.28% vs 94.80%). Against PiSSA (r=8, 835K), ReFTA gains 0.58% average accuracy (85.67% vs 85.09%) with 92.7% fewer parameters. On RoBERTa-Large GLUE, ReFTA (0.020M) achieves 94.8% average, exceeding LoRA (r=1, 0.147M) at 93.9% and WeGeFT (0.065M) at 93.7% by roughly 0.9–1.1%, while using 86.4% and 69.2% fewer parameters respectively. On CommonsenseQA with LLaMA-3-8B, ReFTA (r=15, 119K) matches LoRA (r=8, 3407K) exactly at 86.08% accuracy with 96.5% fewer parameters, and outperforms LoRETTA (r=5, 353K) by 0.17% while using 66% fewer parameters. The only case where ReFTA does not lead is ViT-Huge on FGVC, where LoRA (r=8) achieves 56.41% vs ReFTA's 56.65%—a negligible 0.24% gap—while ReFTA still uses only 5.4% of LoRA's parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReFTA sits at the intersection of three research threads: the t-product/T-SVD algebraic framework (Kernfeld et al., 2015), the low-rank adaptation paradigm initiated by LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), and the principal-components-only fine-tuning strategy of PiSSA (Meng et al., 2024). It departs from prior tensor-based PEFT methods (LoTR, FedTT, LoRETTA) that rely on Tucker or Tensor-Train decomposition and require repeated weight tensor reconstruction during training, by exploiting a specific algebraic property of mode products to eliminate that reconstruction entirely. The method also extends PiSSA's matrix-SVD-based principal-component fine-tuning to the tensor setting via T-SVD, while replacing the multiple interdependent rank hyperparameters of Tucker/TT with a single tensor-rank configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReFTA addresses a practical deployment bottleneck—repeated weight tensor reconstruction—that has limited the adoption of tensor-based PEFT in large-scale settings, and does so with a clean algebraic reformulation rather than an engineering workaround. The first theoretical generalization bound for a tensor-based PEFT method, combined with the demonstrated 96% parameter reduction over LoRA at higher accuracy, makes this a practically relevant contribution for practitioners seeking extreme parameter efficiency. A noted limitation is that ReFTA is designed for stacking layers with compatible tensor shapes (the dominant Transformer setting), and the authors identify multi-subspace tensor methods as a natural next step to broaden expressiveness.
