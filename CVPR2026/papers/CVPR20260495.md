## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D understanding methods built on 3D Gaussian Splatting rely on an embedding paradigm that intrinsically binds high-dimensional semantic features to individual Gaussian points. This design suffers from geometry-semantic inconsistency (points lack clear object identity, especially at boundaries), semantic bloat (gigabytes of per-point features), and semantic rigidity (a single feature vector cannot represent the multiple object identities a point may belong to). The paper asks whether semantics can be modeled as a separate, lightweight index layer that references geometry rather than being fused into it.

## Input / Output

The method takes an already-optimized 3DGS scene (positions, covariances, colors, opacities of all Gaussians) together with its corresponding multi-view image sequence, and at query time a single natural-language text string. It produces a set of 3D Gaussian point indices constituting the segmented object, which can be rendered into multi-view images or converted to a point cloud for evaluation. Notably, no per-scene training or optimization is required; the entire semantic index is built in a single forward pass through SAM, DAM2SAM tracking, and a VLM call per object group, reducing scene adaptation from hours to minutes.

## Method

ExtrinSplat constructs an extrinsic semantic index layer that fully decouples geometry from semantics. First, SAM and DAM2SAM extract multi-view, multi-granularity (part/object/scene) segmentation masks, which are back-projected into 3D using the exact 3DGS rendering weights (transmittance × opacity) to form overlapping object groups. A two-stage neutral-point filter—semantic entropy across views followed by an opacity threshold—removes ambiguous boundary points from all groups. For each purified group, a VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) interprets the top-N most visible masked views and generates multiple candidate textual hypotheses, which are encoded by a CLIP text encoder into a set of semantic feature vectors. At query time, the user's text is encoded by the same CLIP text encoder and matched via cosine similarity against all group feature sets; the final segmentation is the union of Gaussian points from all groups exceeding a similarity threshold, natively supporting polysemy through overlapping multi-granularity groups.

## Results

On the LERF dataset (open-vocabulary 3D object selection), ExtrinSplat achieves a mean mIoU of 54.3, outperforming the best 3D training-free baseline LUDVIG (50.4) by 3.9 mIoU and the best 3D training-based method LAGA (64.0) is still ahead, but ExtrinSplat requires no per-scene training. On the ScanNet dataset (open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation), ExtrinSplat achieves SOTA mIoU of 45.5 (19 classes), 47.2 (15 classes), and 53.7 (10 classes), surpassing the previous best InstanceGaussian (40.7/42.5/47.9) by 4.8/4.7/5.8 mIoU respectively. In terms of efficiency, ExtrinSplat reduces CLIP feature storage from ~3 GB to ~3 MB (~1000× reduction) and peak VRAM to ~8 GB (vs. 12–28 GB for baselines), with zero per-scene optimization time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LERF, ExtrinSplat (54.3 mIoU) outperforms all 3D training-based baselines except LAGA (64.0) and Segment-then-Splat (52.0), and beats the only other 3D training-free method LUDVIG (50.4) by 3.9 mIoU. Among 2D methods, Occam's LGS (61.3) and 3DVLGS (62.0) still lead, but these operate in rendered 2D space and lack direct 3D interaction capability. On ScanNet, ExtrinSplat achieves clear SOTA across all class granularities: 45.5 mIoU (19 classes) vs. InstanceGaussian (40.7), LUDVIG (33.9), Dr.Splat (29.6), LAGA (32.5), and OpenGaussian (24.7), with margins of 4.8–20.8 mIoU. The efficiency gap is stark: all embedding-based baselines require 1–2 hours of per-scene optimization and ~3 GB of CLIP feature storage, whereas ExtrinSplat needs no training and stores only ~3 MB of text features. Notably, OpenGaussian and InstanceGaussian, which rely on matching 2D CLIP image features, suffer from viewpoint-dependent semantic inconsistencies that cause incorrect matches in complex ScanNet scenes, while ExtrinSplat's VLM-distilled textual hypotheses remain invariant to occlusion and viewing angle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its geometric substrate but deliberately departs from the dominant embedding paradigm (OpenGaussian, InstanceGaussian, Dr.Splat, LUDVIG) that binds per-point CLIP features to each Gaussian. Instead, it introduces an 'extrinsic paradigm' that treats semantics as a separate index layer: Gaussians are clustered into object groups via multi-view mask back-projection, and a VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) generates textual hypotheses per group, which are encoded by a CLIP text encoder. The method also leverages SAM for multi-granularity segmentation and DAM2SAM for distractor-aware tracking, but the core conceptual contribution is the decoupling of geometry and semantics rather than any single component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extrinsic paradigm offers a compelling efficiency story—reducing semantic storage from ~3 GB to ~3 MB and eliminating per-scene optimization entirely—while matching or exceeding the accuracy of training-based embedding methods. The formalization of 'neutral points' and the multi-granularity overlapping grouping mechanism address real boundary-ambiguity and polysemy issues that prior point-level methods handle poorly. However, the method's dependence on a proprietary VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) for semantic distillation raises reproducibility and cost concerns, and the authors acknowledge that SAM segmentation errors and occasional VLM mislabeling remain unresolved. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large outdoor scenes with hundreds of objects and whether open-source VLMs can match the distillation quality.
