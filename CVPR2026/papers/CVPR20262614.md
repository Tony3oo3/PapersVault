## Problem

Current MLLMs perform markedly worse on video event prediction (VEP) than on standard video understanding tasks, with the best open-source model achieving only ~67% accuracy. The paper identifies two root causes: models lack logical reasoning that connects observed video content to unobserved future events (relying instead on superficial option analysis), and they under-utilize visual information, allocating far less attention to visual tokens than textual tokens during prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262614/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (up to 32 frames at 128×28×28 resolution) and a multiple-choice question as input. It produces a structured temporal event chain—each event marked with special tags containing start/end timestamps and a fine-grained textual description—followed by a logical reasoning passage that links the observed events to the predicted future event. The event chain length is constrained by a hyperparameter L (default 3), and the final prediction is the selected future event from the options.

## Method

The Chain of Events (CoE) paradigm restructures VEP reasoning into two stages: first, the model segments the video into a fine-grained temporal event chain (timestamps + descriptions) to enforce visual grounding; second, it reasons jointly over the video and event chain to predict the future event via causal-temporal logic. Training uses a two-phase protocol on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B: CoE-SFT fine-tunes on a small dataset of logical reasoning traces (generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B) that explicitly connect video evidence to future events without option analysis; CoE-GRPO then applies reinforcement learning with three reward signals—an accuracy reward, a CoE structural reward (enforcing correct event tag format and target chain length), and a cross-modal similarity reward (cropping video at predicted timestamps and computing video-text alignment via VideoCLIP-XL) to prevent reward hacking. This design unlocks the model's temporal localization capability without requiring additional data annotation, and the GRPO objective follows DeepSeek-R1's clipped policy update with group-relative advantage normalization.

## Results

On FutureBench, CoE-GRPO with Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves 75.00% average accuracy (80.9% 1-Hop, 83.9% 2-Hop, 71.6% 3-Hop, 71.4% Interp.), surpassing the strongest open-source baseline Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B (66.86%) by over 8 points and the best commercial model GPT-4o (59.04%) by 16 points. On AVEP, CoE-GRPO-7B achieves 65.16% Noun F1 and 8.29% Action F1 on the test set, outperforming the vanilla GRPO baseline (62.45% and 6.48% respectively). The 3B variant also improves from 52.94% (Instruct) to 68.28% (CoE-GRPO) average on FutureBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoE-GRPO (7B) decisively outperforms all 11 baselines on FutureBench, achieving 75.00% AVG versus 66.86% for the strongest open-source model Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B and 59.04% for GPT-4o. Against the most directly comparable baselines on the same 7B base model, CoE-GRPO improves over (NEP) GRPO (67.28%) by 7.7 points and over (NEP) SFT (64.39%) by 10.6 points, demonstrating that the CoE paradigm adds substantial value beyond standard SFT or vanilla GRPO training. On AVEP, CoE-GRPO-7B leads all baselines on Noun F1 (65.16% vs. 62.45% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-GRPO) and Action F1 (8.29% vs. 6.48%), while also achieving the highest Verb accuracy (12.24% test / 18.75% val). The 3B variant similarly outperforms its own Instruct baseline (46.30% → 68.28%) and the (NEP) GRPO baseline (65.45% → 68.28%), confirming the method's effectiveness at smaller model scales. Notably, even the 7B CoE-GRPO model surpasses the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (58.33%) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (59.94%) vanilla models, highlighting the paradigm's efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1, adapting it with a multi-component reward (accuracy, event-chain structure, and cross-modal similarity) specifically tailored for video event prediction. It also departs from the NEP (Next-Event Prediction) line of work by identifying that vanilla SFT data constructed via option-by-option analysis fails to instill genuine logical reasoning, and by introducing explicit temporal event chain construction as a mechanism to force visual grounding. The Chain of Events paradigm itself draws on the event-chain concept from prior temporal modeling literature (Granroth-Wilding & Clark, Li et al.) but repurposes it as an intermediate reasoning scaffold within an MLLM pipeline rather than as a standalone prediction architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic evaluation revealing that even frontier models (GPT-5, Qwen3-VL) plateau around 67% on VEP establishes a clear benchmark gap and motivates future work on temporal reasoning in MLLMs. The finding that standard visual-attention enhancement strategies (prompt-guided description, constant attention bias) actively degrade VEP performance challenges a common assumption in the MLLM community and suggests that forcing visual grounding requires structural changes to the reasoning process rather than inference-time hacks. Open questions include whether the CoE paradigm generalizes to longer videos or more complex multi-step event sequences, and whether the event-chain construction can be extended to open-set prediction without a fixed label space.
