## Problem

Generating high-fidelity, directly animatable 3D head avatars from a single image remains challenging: Gaussian-based feed-forward methods require excessive primitives for fine details (hair, tattoos) and produce blurry textures in a single pass, while existing mesh-based 3DMM reconstruction methods fail to recover geometry beyond the facial region and do not synthesize high-fidelity textures. The paper tackles the gap of producing complete, topology-preserving, animatable textured meshes in a single forward pass without per-subject optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB head image as input and produces a textured 3D head mesh of approximately 8K vertices (deformed from a FLAME template) along with a high-resolution UV-aligned texture map. The output mesh retains FLAME's parametric rigging (identity, expression, pose parameters) for direct animation, and an optional StyleGAN-like UNet renderer can be applied to the rasterized mesh for enhanced visual quality.

## Method

MeshLAM employs a dual-branch feed-forward architecture sharing a frozen DINOv2 backbone: a shape branch predicts per-vertex deformations relative to a FLAME template via cross-attention, while a texture branch synthesizes a UV texture map from a learnable token grid attending to the same image features. The key innovation is an iterative GRU-based decoding mechanism (2 iterations) that progressively refines both geometry and texture, preventing the mesh collapse that occurs with direct single-pass vertex offset regression. At each iteration, a reprojection module rasterizes the current animated mesh, unwraps the input image and the rendered prediction error into UV space, and feeds these as visual guidance to both the texture GRU and the geometry GRU, creating a closed-loop 3D-2D feedback. Part-aware deformation constraints apply region-dependent clipping (larger for hair, minimal for face, zero for eyeballs), and a topology correction pipeline (subdivision, face flipping, invalid face removal) with barycentric re-interpolation of skinning weights maintains animation compatibility after remeshing.

## Results

On the VFHQ test set, MeshLAM with UNet rendering achieves PSNR 25.233, SSIM 0.879, LPIPS 0.061, and FID 22.699 for one-shot mesh head reconstruction, substantially outperforming the mesh-based ROME baseline (PSNR 22.850, FID 42.542). Identity preservation (CSIM) reaches 0.948, nearly double ROME's 0.681. When the reconstructed mesh is used as a geometric prior for Gaussian rendering (LAM+Ours), the combined pipeline achieves the best overall scores: PSNR 25.889, SSIM 0.893, LPIPS 0.050, and FID 22.576.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the mesh-based ROME [30], MeshLAM w/ UNet wins decisively on every metric: PSNR improves by 2.4 dB (25.233 vs. 22.850), LPIPS drops from 0.098 to 0.061, FID nearly halves (22.699 vs. 42.542), and identity preservation (CSIM) jumps from 0.681 to 0.948. Against the Gaussian-based LAM+FLAME [21], MeshLAM w/ UNet achieves comparable PSNR (25.233 vs. 25.082) and better LPIPS (0.061 vs. 0.077), FID (22.699 vs. 24.270), and CSIM (0.948 vs. 0.879), but trails on motion accuracy (AKD 3.24 vs. 2.07), reflecting the inherent advantage of Gaussian representations for fine motion tracking. Notably, when MeshLAM's reconstructed mesh is used to initialize the Gaussian pipeline (LAM+Ours), the hybrid achieves the best scores across all six metrics, demonstrating that the mesh output serves as a superior geometric prior. Even without the optional UNet renderer, MeshLAM (w/o UNet) already surpasses ROME on PSNR, LPIPS, CSIM, and FID while remaining more computationally efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshLAM directly extends the authors' own LAM framework [21], which introduced feed-forward transformer-based animatable Gaussian head generation from a single image using FLAME priors, by replacing the Gaussian representation with a dual-branch mesh-vertex and UV-texture-map architecture. It departs from Gaussian-based feed-forward methods (LAM, GaussianAvatars) on the grounds that fine-scale appearance (hair strands, tattoos) demands excessive primitive counts, and from mesh-based 3DMM methods (ROME, Deep 3D Portrait) that fail to capture geometry beyond the face or produce high-fidelity textures. The iterative GRU decoding and reprojection-guided texture synthesis are novel architectural choices layered on top of the shared DINOv2 backbone and DPT decoder inherited from the LAM pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing a complete, animatable, textured mesh in a single forward pass (under one second), MeshLAM removes the per-subject optimization bottleneck that has limited the practical deployment of 3D head avatars in VR, gaming, and telepresence. The mesh-plus-texture decomposition offers a compelling efficiency argument over Gaussian representations for high-frequency detail, and the reprojection-based texture guidance provides a reusable mechanism for grounding feed-forward 3D appearance in 2D observations. Key limitations remain: animation expressiveness is bounded by FLAME's parameterization (no dynamic wrinkles or tongue), and reconstruction quality is sensitive to FLAME parameter estimation accuracy. Open questions include whether more expressive differentiable 3DMMs (e.g., MetaHuman-style) can be integrated, and whether the iterative GRU paradigm generalizes to full-body or multi-person reconstruction.
