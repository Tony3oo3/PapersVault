## Problem

Existing multi-view multi-label classification (MVMLC) methods rely on either feature-level fusion, which underutilizes label supervision and limits representation discriminability, or decision-level fusion, which pays insufficient attention to view representation expressiveness. No prior framework jointly exploits both fusion levels to neutralize their respective weaknesses, leaving a gap in achieving simultaneously expressive and discriminative representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes V heterogeneous view features of potentially different dimensions (d_1, d_2, …, d_V) along with binary label annotations of length L for each sample. It produces a probability vector over L labels by aggregating weighted logits from view-specific atom classifiers via a sigmoid function. The multi-label problem is decomposed into L binary subproblems, one per label, with each view contributing an independent prediction that is combined through learned boosting weights.

## Method

DF²-VB first decouples each view into consensus and complementarity representations via separate encoders, enforced by a mean-pooled decoupling loss that increases mutual information between consensus representations while suppressing cross-type dependence. At the feature-level fusion stage, a Fuzzy Dynamic Fusion (FDF) module maps consensus representations into a fuzzy space using K learnable Gaussian membership functions, computes element-wise feature importance via max-pooling over membership degrees, and fuses weighted consensus representations into a comprehensive consensus vector. At the decision-level fusion stage, the fused consensus is concatenated with each view's complementarity representation, and a View-specific Boosting (VB) strategy adaptively weights atom classifiers (zeroing out those with error ≥ 0.5) and up-weights hard-to-classify samples across T boosting iterations. The final prediction aggregates weighted logits across all views and boosting iterations, trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy plus the decoupling loss.

## Results

DF²-VB achieves the best performance on all five metrics (OE, RL, Cov, AP, MiF1) across all six datasets. Headline AP values are 0.840 (Emotions), 0.778 (Yeast), 0.893 (Scene), 0.540 (Corel5k), 0.784 (Pascal), and 0.375 (Espgame). Micro-F1 scores range from 0.348 (Espgame) to 0.779 (Scene), consistently exceeding the strongest baselines by 1–14 percentage points. The Friedman test confirms statistically significant superiority over all six baselines at the 0.05 level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DF²-VB outperforms all six baselines on 29 out of 30 metric-dataset combinations (the sole tie is OE on Espgame, where it matches D-VSM at 0.464). Against the closest competitors D-VSM and VAMS, DF²-VB wins in 90.0% and all 30 cases respectively, with the largest margins on Corel5k (AP: 0.540 vs. 0.475 for D-VSM, 0.452 for VAMS) and Espgame (AP: 0.375 vs. 0.364 for D-VSM, 0.266 for VAMS). The decision-level fusion baselines ML-BVAE and TMvML are substantially weaker, with ML-BVAE achieving AP as low as 0.257 on Espgame and MiF1 of 0.008, while TMvML lags by 5–14 AP points across datasets. Feature-level fusion baselines FIMAN and DIMC show moderate performance but are consistently outperformed, particularly on MiF1 (e.g., DIMC scores 0.029 on Corel5k vs. 0.437 for DF²-VB). The Bonferroni-Dunn post-hoc test confirms that DF²-VB is significantly superior to all baselines at the 0.05 level across RL, Cov, AP, and MiF1.

## Relation to Prior Work

DF²-VB unifies two previously separate MVMLC paradigms—feature-level fusion (EIMAN, DIMC, SIMM) and decision-level fusion (ML-BVAE, TMvML)—into a single architecture, arguing that each level compensates for the other's weakness. The Fuzzy Dynamic Fusion module repurposes Gaussian membership functions from fuzzy set theory as elementlement-wise feature weights, departing from the view-level weighting common in prior feature-fusion methods. The View-specific Boosting strategy adapts AdaBoost-style sequential reweighting of atom classifiers and samples to the multi-view multi-label setting, a use of boosting that prior MVMLC work has not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-level design addresses a genuine structural gap: no prior MVMLC method jointly optimizes expressive feature fusion and discriminative decision fusion in one model, and the ablation confirms both components are complementary. However, the evaluation is limited to six relatively small public benchmarks with complete views, and the boosting is implemented per mini-batch rather than truly sequentially, which may constrain its advantage at scale. Open questions include whether the framework extends to incomplete views, noisy labels, or high-dimensional label spaces where the per-label atom-classifier decomposition becomes computationally expensive.
