## Problem

Automatic skill assessment from first-person data on smart glasses remains unsolved: prior methods rely on third-person camera setups or continuous egocentric video, both of which are impractical for real-time, power-constrained wearable deployment. Although cognitive science establishes that experts and novices exhibit distinct gaze patterns, no prior work has leveraged gaze as a primary signal for skill assessment in dynamic, in-the-wild settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263122/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The teacher model consumes 16-frame egocentric video clips at 2 FPS alongside rich gaze signals (3D fixation points, 3D gaze direction, 2D gaze projection, gaze depth, and glasses translation/quaternion rotation) and outputs a 4-level skill class. The student model takes only the gaze signal at inference (no RGB frames) and produces both a skill-level label and a subtask label (e.g., dribbling, penalty kick) via a multi-branch architecture, processing a single sample in 1.6 ms on one GPU.

## Method

SkillSight is a two-stage teacher-student framework. The teacher (SkillSight-T) fuses three gaze-vision components: (1) a Gaussian gaze-induced attention map injected into the first spatial encoder of a TimeSformer backbone to highlight gaze-attended regions, (2) a sequence of gaze-cropped images encoded by DINOv2 and a temporal transformer to capture attended-object semantics, and (3) a transformer encoder on normalized 3D gaze trajectories to model fixation frequency, saccade speed, and depth dynamics. The student (SkillSight-S) is a lightweight 4-layer transformer on gaze alone, trained via knowledge distillation from the teacher using a DeiT-style distillation token to align feature spaces, plus an auxiliary action-recognition token that classifies the subtask from gaze. This distillation transfers visual skill cues into the gaze representation, enabling the student to achieve competitive accuracy (44.4% overall on EgoExo4D) while consuming 14×–73× less power than video-based methods by keeping the RGB camera off entirely.

## Results

On Ego-Exo4D, SkillSight-T (video+gaze) achieves 50.1% overall accuracy, outperforming the strongest video-only baseline TimeSformer (45.5%) by 4.6 points (10% relative gain) across all seven scenarios. SkillSight-S (gaze-only) reaches 44.4% overall accuracy at just 9.5 mW, surpassing all power-efficient baselines (EgoDistill 42.6% at 16.5 mW, X3D-XS 34.2% at 88 mW) while consuming 73× less power than TimeSformer. On Multi-Sense Badminton, SkillSight-T scores 53.1% vs. TimeSformer's 50.5%, and SkillSight-S scores 47.0% vs. EgoDistill's 43.4%. On Expert-Novice Soccer, SkillSight-S (72.6%) outperforms both the gaze-only baseline (66.0%) and body-motion-only baseline (71.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkillSight-T (50.1% overall) beats every baseline across all seven Ego-Exo4D scenarios, with the largest margins over Beholder (34.1%) and EgoExoLearn (42.3%), the only prior methods that also use gaze. Among power-efficient baselines, SkillSight-S (44.4% at 9.5 mW) outperforms EgoDistill (42.6% at 16.5 mW) by 1.8 points while using 43% less power, and surpasses X3D-XS (34.2% at 88 mW) by 10.2 points at 9.3× lower power. Compared to the full video pipeline TimeSformer (45.5% at 697.5 mW), SkillSight-S sacrifices only 1.1 points of accuracy while reducing energy by 73×. On Expert-Novice Soccer, SkillSight-S (72.6%) exceeds the gaze-only baseline (66.0%) by 6.6 points and the body-motion-only baseline (71.2%) by 1.4 points, validating the knowledge distillation from the teacher. EgoTrigger (34.1%) and E2GoMotion (34.9%) underperform substantially, suggesting that audio-triggered or event-camera proxies cannot capture the fine-grained attentional cues that distinguish skill levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends egocentric skill assessment—previously dominated by video-only approaches (TimeSformer, Skillformer) or limited gaze+video methods (EgoExoLearn, Beholder)—by introducing gaze as a first-class signal for skill understanding and then distilling all visual knowledge into a gaze-only student model. Architecturally, it builds on the DeiT distillation token [83] for the student-teacher transfer and on TimeSformer/DINOv2 as backbone encoders, but departs from prior gaze+video methods by injecting gaze attention at the earliest spatial encoder layer rather than pooling at late-stage features. The two-stage teacher-student design, where the student operates entirely without RGB input at inference, is novel relative to prior efficiency work (EgoDistill, EgoTrigger) that still requires periodic camera activation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes, across cooking, music, and multiple sports, that gaze patterns alone carry substantial skill-discriminative information, opening a path toward always-on, low-power, privacy-preserving skill coaching on smart glasses. The 73× power reduction (9.5 mW vs. 697.5 mW) with only a 1.1% accuracy drop is practically significant for wearable deployment. However, absolute accuracy remains modest (44.4% overall on EgoExo4D for the student), and the paper itself acknowledges failure cases where subtle hand movements (e.g., vegetable slicing) are invisible to gaze. Open questions include whether the gaze-only formulation generalizes to fine-grained multi-level skill scoring, novel domains without expert-annotated gaze, and whether the distillation gap widens as task complexity increases.
