## Problem

Existing 3D garment generation pipelines rely on large vision-language models (7B+ parameters) for sewing-pattern prediction and physics-based simulation engines (e.g., GarmentCode on NVIDIA Warp) for mesh construction, resulting in inference times of 30–60 seconds. The paper asks whether high-quality, simulation-ready 3D garments can be produced without any physics-based solver in the sewing stage, while maintaining compatibility with downstream simulation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262282/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a garment reference image, a text description, or both as multimodal input. PatternMaker outputs a structured sewing pattern (N panels with vertex coordinates, edge connectivity, rigid transformations, and M stitching pairs). GarmentSewer then produces a Garment Geometry Image (GGI) — three aligned images (semantic, geometry, stitching) sharing a common repacked square UV layout — from which a final 3D mesh is reconstructed via remeshing and dynamic stitching. The geometry image stores normalized 3D surface coordinates per pixel, and the stitching image color-codes paired boundary edges for post-processing assembly.

## Method

SwiftTailor is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (PatternMaker) fine-tunes the compact InternVL-3-2B multimodal LLM using AIpparel's tokenizer and MLP regression heads to predict discrete pattern tokens and continuous vertex/transform parameters, achieving higher accuracy than 7B baselines with only 30% of the parameters. Stage 2 (GarmentSewer) is a DPT network (ViT-L encoder + multi-scale convolutional decoder) that maps the semantic UV image to a dense geometry image, trained with an edge-aware L1 regression loss (weighting boundary pixels), a Chamfer-distance stitching loss enforcing 3D alignment of paired edges, and a normal-smoothness regularizer. The key novelty is the Garment Geometry Image representation, which unifies MCGIM-style geometry encoding with semantic panel labels and explicit stitching maps in a single UV space, enabling direct mesh reconstruction through remeshing and dynamic stitching without any physics simulation.

## Results

On the GCD-MM benchmark, SwiftTailor achieves the best MMD of 5.31 and COV of 0.68 for 3D garment mesh generation, outperforming the strongest baseline (AIpparel + GarmentCode) by 23% in MMD and 31% in COV. For sewing-pattern generation, PatternMaker (using only 30% of AIpparel's parameters) achieves 92.3% edge accuracy and 85.1% stitching accuracy versus AIpparel's 79.0% and 73.0%, respectively. In terms of speed, SwiftTailor's total inference time is 14.78 s, roughly 4× faster than all GarmentCode-based baselines (54–64 s), with Stage 2 (mesh construction) taking only 0.02 s versus 34–50 s for physics simulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwiftTailor wins decisively against all three baselines on both mesh quality and speed. Compared to AIpparel + GarmentCode (the strongest baseline), SwiftTailor reduces MMD from 6.94 to 5.31 (−23%) and increases COV from 0.52 to 0.68 (+31%), while also cutting total inference time from 63.74 s to 14.78 s. Against ChatGarment + GarmentCode, SwiftTailor improves MMD by 57% (12.27 → 5.31) and COV by 3.1× (0.22 → 0.68); ChatGarment's advantage in fewer sampling attempts (1.20 vs. 2.98) is offset by its coarse pattern formulation that limits fine-grained control. Against SewingLDM + GarmentCode, SwiftTailor achieves 53% lower MMD (11.33 → 5.31) and 2× higher COV (0.34 → 0.68), while also requiring fewer sampling attempts (5.87 → 2.98). At the pattern-generation sub-task level, PatternMaker outperforms AIpparel on every metric (e.g., edge accuracy 92.3% vs. 79.0%, stitching accuracy 85.1% vs. 73.0%) despite using a 2B-parameter backbone instead of 7B, and substantially outperforms both ChatGarment and SewingLDM, which show markedly weaker structural reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SwiftTailor sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) multimodal sewing-pattern generation, where it builds directly on AIpparel's tokenization and regression-head design but swaps the 7B LLaVA backbone for the 2B InternVL-3-2B model, and (2) geometry-image-based 3D representation, where it extends the multi-chart geometry image (MCGIM) framework by augmenting it with semantic panel labels and explicit stitching maps to form the Garment Geometry Image. The key conceptual departure from prior garment-construction pipelines (AIpparel+GarmentCode, ChatGarment+GarmentCode, SewingLDM+GarmentCode) is replacing iterative physics-based sewing simulation with a single feed-forward dense-prediction transformer (DPT), a shift that GarmageNet partially anticipated but still required a residual simulation step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× total-inference speedup (14.8 s vs. ~50–64 s) and the 0.02 s Stage-2 latency make garment generation viable for interactive design and large-scale batch production, which prior simulation-based pipelines could not support. The GGI representation is a practical, interpretable intermediate that decouples pattern reasoning from mesh construction, enabling modular upgrades to either stage. Open questions remain: the method is validated only on GCD-MM, does not yet handle texture synthesis or wrinkle refinement, and the remeshing/stitching post-processing step (4.8 s) could become a bottleneck at scale. Whether the learned geometry image generalizes to garment categories outside the training distribution (e.g., complex accessories, multi-layer outfits) is untested.
