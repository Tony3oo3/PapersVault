## Problem

Existing B-rep generation methods rely on multi-stage pipelines or graph-based representations that decouple geometry and topology, precluding the use of efficient sequence-based generative frameworks like transformers. The heterogeneity of B-rep's parametric geometric primitives and hierarchical topological dependencies makes it difficult to encode the full model in a single, holistic form amenable to autoregressive sequence modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261442/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model starts from a START token; for class-conditional generation, a category-specific token replaces START. The autoregressive model emits a variable-length discrete token sequence (face blocks followed by edge blocks, delimited by a SEP token and terminated by END), which is detokenized via the VQ-VAE decoder for geometry, linear dequantization for bounding-box positions, and a union-find-based vertex clustering algorithm to recover edge-vertex connectivity and loop structures, finally stitched into a watertight B-rep solid using OpenCascade's sew function. Faces are UV-sampled at 32×32 resolution and edges at 32 points; bounding boxes use 2048-level scalar quantization, and the VQ-VAE codebook has 4096 entries (DeepCAD) or 8192 (ABC).

## Method

BrepARG encodes each B-rep into a single holistic token sequence comprising three token types: Geometry Tokens (4 per face/edge, obtained by mapping a 2×2 VQ-VAE latent map to codebook indices via nearest-neighbor search), Position Tokens (6 per primitive, from coordinate-wise uniform scalar quantization of the bounding box), and Face Index Tokens (1 per face, 2 per edge, encoding face-edge adjacency). Sequence construction is hierarchical: face and edge blocks are assembled from these tokens, then ordered using a topology-aware scheme (DFS from the highest-degree face prioritizing low-degree neighbors for faces; MAX-IDX-A sorting by maximum adjacent face index for edges), and finally concatenated with START/SEP/END markers. A decoder-only transformer (8 layers, 8 heads, d=256) with causal masking is trained via next-token prediction to learn the joint distribution over these sequences, enabling end-to-end co-generation of geometry and topology in a single autoregressive pass. A random re-indexing offset is applied to Face Index Tokens during training to improve generalization, and a codebook restart strategy (CVQ-VAE) mitigates VQ-VAE codebook collapse.

## Results

On DeepCAD, BrepARG achieves the best scores across all six metrics: COV 75.45%, MMD 0.89×10⁻², JSD 1.02×10⁻², Novelty 99.82%, Uniqueness 99.80%, and Validity 87.60%. On the more complex ABC dataset, BrepARG also leads with COV 70.10%, MMD 1.405×10⁻², JSD 1.337×10⁻², and Validity 67.54%. In terms of efficiency, BrepARG trains in ~1.2 days on 4×H20 GPUs and generates a B-rep in ~1.5 s on a single RTX 4090, substantially faster than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrepARG outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric for both DeepCAD and ABC. Against DTGBrepGen (the strongest baseline), BrepARG improves Validity from 79.80% to 87.60% (+7.8 pp), COV from 74.52 to 75.45, and MMD from 1.07 to 0.89, while matching its JSD of 1.02. Against BrepGen, the gains are larger: Validity jumps from 68.23% to 87.60% (+19.4 pp) and MMD drops from 1.13 to 0.89. Against BrepDiff, BrepARG leads on COV (75.45 vs 69.73), MMD (0.89 vs 0.93), and Validity (87.60% vs 62.83%). In addition to quality, BrepARG is the most efficient: 1.2 days training and 1.5 s inference versus 3.0 days / 3.6 s (DTGBrepGen), 7.5 days / 8.4 s (BrepGen), and 1.8 days / 8.3 s (BrepDiff).

## Relation to Prior Work

BrepARG departs from the dominant multi-stage paradigm in B-rep generation (SolidGen, DTGBrepGen) and graph-based diffusion approaches (BrepGen, BrepGiff) by encoding both geometry and topology into a single autoregressive token sequence, drawing on the decoder-only transformer paradigm from LLMs and the VQ-VAE discretization strategy from 3D point cloud/mesh generation. It specifically builds on CVQ-VAE's codebook restart mechanism for stable quantization and GraphGPT's random re-indexing for topological generalization. The work is concurrent with AutoBrep and BrepGPT, which independently explore sequence-based B-rep generation, but differs in its holistic tokenization scheme (unified VQ-VAE for faces/edges, scalar quantization for positions) and topology-aware sequentialization (DFS + MAX-IDX-A).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reformulating B-rep generation as next-token prediction over a unified sequence, BrepARG opens the door to applying the full toolkit of LLM-style architectures (scaling, conditioning, fine-tuning) to CAD model synthesis, with a practical efficiency gain of ~5× faster inference and ~6× faster training over prior methods. Key limitations include VQ-VAE quantization precision loss and the combinatorial complexity of long autoregressive sequences, which manifest as failure cases on complex models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to industrial-grade B-reps with hundreds of faces, whether higher-fidelity geometric quantization (e.g., multi-resolution codebooks) can close the remaining validity gap, and how the method compares to the concurrent AutoBrep and BrepGPT under identical protocols.
