## Problem

Existing RVOS benchmarks are limited to short clips (a few seconds) where target objects are visible in most frames, failing to capture realistic long-video challenges such as frequent occlusion, disappearance-reappearance, and shot changing. Additionally, standard frame-wise metrics (J&F) cannot assess whether a model correctly outputs empty masks when the target is absent, leaving temporal consistency unevaluated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261680/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long-term video (average 60+ seconds, up to ~362 frames) and a natural language description of the target object, which can be of three types: Static (appearance/position), Dynamic (motion/interaction), or Hybrid. The video is decomposed via MPEG-4 into clips of one high-resolution keyframe plus low-resolution motion vectors for up to 11 subsequent frames. The model predicts segmentation masks only on keyframes; a pretrained SAM2 tracker then propagates these anchor masks to all intermediate frames at inference.

## Method

ReferMo decomposes each video into MPEG-4 clips (keyframe + motion vectors) and employs a local-to-global architecture. Within each clip, a local perceiver encodes the keyframe (Swin-Tiny), motion vectors (deformable spatial attention + temporal self-attention), and text separately, then fuses them via dual cross-attention; motion features are integrated into multi-scale image features through spatial-aware and channel-aware gating to suppress noise. Across clips, objects are aligned via the Hungarian algorithm and subjected to temporal self-attention with language cross-attention for global consistency. The model is supervised only on keyframe masks, keeping training cost low while SAM2 handles frame-to-frame propagation, effectively expanding the temporal receptive field from a few frames to multiple clips without proportional memory increase.

## Results

On the Long-RVOS test set, ReferMo achieves the best overall performance across all three metrics: 52.9 J&F, 73.6 tIoU, and 45.2 vIoU, while also being the fastest method at 52.5 FPS. The best non-SAM2 baseline (ReferDINO) reaches 48.4 J&F / 73.5 tIoU / 43.9 vIoU, and the best SAM2-based method (SAMWISE) achieves 40.9 J&F / 66.6 tIoU / 31.1 vIoU. Notably, SAM2-based methods that are SOTA on short-video benchmarks (e.g., VideoLISA at 14.0 J&F, GLUS at 25.7 J&F) degrade severely on long videos, confirming that their gains on short clips do not transfer to realistic long-form scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReferMo outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (J&F, tIoU, vIoU) across all three description types and overall. Against the strongest non-SAM2 baseline ReferDINO (ICCV'25), ReferMo gains +4.5 J&F (52.9 vs. 48.4) and +1.3 vIoU (45.2 vs. 43.9) while matching tIoU (73.6 vs. 73.5) and running 13% faster (52.5 vs. 46.4 FPS). Against the best SAM2-based method SAMWISE (CVPR'25), ReferMo leads by a substantial +12.0 J&F (52.9 vs. 40.9) and +14.1 vIoU (45.2 vs. 31.1), with a 7.5-point tIoU advantage (73.6 vs. 66.6). The MLLM-based SAM2 methods (VideoLISA, GLUS, RGA3) collapse dramatically on long videos—VideoLISA drops to 14.0 J&F and GLUS to 25.7 J&F—far below even the weakest non-SAM2 baseline (SOC at 38.6 J&F), indicating their short-video gains rely heavily on SAM2's tracking rather than genuine language-object reasoning. Across all methods, Dynamic descriptions yield the lowest J&F scores, confirming a persistent weakness in motion understanding, while tIoU remains relatively stable (59–74) regardless of J&F, validating the need for the proposed temporal metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RVOS task from short clips (a few seconds) to minute-level long videos, building architecturally on ReferDINO's dual cross-attention and Hungarian-alignment framework while introducing MPEG-4 keyframe/motion decomposition (inspired by Video-LaVIT) to expand the temporal receptive field without proportional compute cost. It departs from the SAM2-integration trend (SAMWISE, GLUS, RGA3) by using SAM2 only as a post-hoc mask propagator rather than a core reasoning component, and from prior benchmarks (MeViS, Ref-YouTube-VOS) by sourcing videos from multi-source long-video datasets (TAO, VidOR, Ego-Exo4D) rather than annotating existing VOS datasets. The evaluation protocol extends prior frame-averaging J&F with tIoU and vIoU metrics borrowed from spatiotemporal video grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-RVOS fills a critical gap: no prior RVOS benchmark exceeded ~13 seconds average duration, yet real-world applications (video editing, surveillance) involve minute-scale footage with occlusion, disappearance-reappearance, and shot changes. The oracle analysis revealing a ~25-point J&F drop for SAM2 between MeViS and Long-RVOS, and the finding that SAM2-based SOTA methods actually underperform non-SAM2 baselines on long videos, challenges the field's assumption that superior tracking translates to superior language-object understanding. Open questions include whether keyframe-based sparse supervision can scale to even longer videos, how to handle the train-inference frame-count gap more gracefully, and whether the tIoU/vIoU metrics will become standard for temporal consistency evaluation beyond RVOS.
