## Problem

Vision-based 3D spine motion estimation lacks large-scale, anatomically consistent training data: existing resources are either 2D-only with inconsistent labels (SpineTrack), clinical imaging requiring specialized equipment (fluoroscopy, EOS, CT), or marker-assisted capture needing exposed skin and controlled views. No open dataset provides vertebra-level 3D kinematics from unconstrained RGB video, leaving a gap between musculoskeletal biomechanics and computer vision for learning subtle vertebral rotations and postural sway.

## Input / Output

The simulation pipeline consumes synchronized multi-view RGB frames from Human3.6M (7 subjects, 15 actions), calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, subject height/mass for anthropometric scaling, and 2D spinal keypoints from a pretrained SpinePose detector. It produces 15 3D spinal keypoints in absolute metric world coordinates (9 vertebral centroids from sacral base to lower cervical, plus 2 skull, 2 clavicle, and 2 scapular landmarks), per-vertebra Euler rotations about anatomical axes (flexion/extension, lateral bending, axial rotation), and a full 37-marker skeleton with 62 kinematic axes. The resulting SIMSPINE dataset spans 2.14M frames with standard Human3.6M train/test splits (S1,S5,S6,S7,S8 / S9,S11).

## Method

The core pipeline fuses noisy multi-view 2D spinal detections with ground-truth body markers through a subject-scaled OpenSim musculoskeletal model (Rajagopal full-body + Beaucage-Gauvreau lumbar spine) via weighted inverse kinematics, then recovers anatomically consistent 3D vertebral positions through forward kinematics with virtual markers attached to vertebral centroids. The lumbar spine (L1–L5) is fully articulated with three rotational DOFs per intervertebral joint, while cervical and thoracic segments are modeled as rigid bodies with motion permitted only at the cervicothoracic and thoracolumbar transition junctions, approximating rib-cage constraints while keeping the model identifiable from RGB inputs. Pseudo-3D spinal points receive lower IK weights than ground-truth body markers, and a temporal smoothness penalty on joint velocities/accelerations suppresses frame-to-frame jitter. Quality control rejects frames with implausible curvature, clamps gimbal-wrap discontinuities, and applies zero-phase low-pass filtering. For benchmarking, the paper fine-tunes six 2D detectors (SpinePose-s/m/l, HRNet-w32, RTMPose-m, ViTPose-b) using per-batch mixing of 2% SIMSPINE with SpineTrack, evaluates multiview triangulation with confidence-weighted least squares, and trains SimpleBaseline3D MLPs for monocular 3D lifting on both spine-only (15 joints) and full-body (37 joints) keypoint sets.

## Results

On the SIMSPINE indoor benchmark, fine-tuned SpinePose-l achieves 0.803 AUC (PCK), improving over the original SpinePose-l (0.633) by 0.170 and over HRNet-w32 (0.769), RTMPose-m (0.794), and ViTPose-b (0.794). On the SpineTrack outdoor benchmark, SpinePose-m-ft reaches 0.928 AP^S, surpassing the original SpinePose-m (0.914) and all other architectures. For multiview 3D triangulation, the fine-tuned detector yields 31.8 mm MPJPE (29.5 mm P-MPJPE) versus 49.3 mm for zero-shot, while the GT-2D oracle confirms sub-millimeter geometric consistency. For monocular 3D lifting, the full-body variant with GT 2D achieves 13.5 mm P-MPJPE, outperforming the spine-only variant (17.5 mm) by 23%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original SpinePose models [31], fine-tuning on SIMSPINE+SpineTrack lifts indoor AUC from 0.633 to 0.803 (SpinePose-l-ft) and outdoor AP^S from 0.914 to 0.928 (SpinePose-m-ft), confirming that consistent simulation-derived labels substantially reduce annotation noise. Among general-purpose pose architectures, SpinePose-l-ft (0.803 AUC) outperforms HRNet-w32 (0.769), RTMPose-m (0.794), and ViTPose-b (0.794) on the indoor SIMSPINE benchmark, with a 0.009–0.034 margin. On the outdoor SpineTrack AP^S metric, SpinePose-m-ft (0.928) edges out RTMPose-m (0.925) and ViTPose-b (0.921), while HRNet-w32 lags at 0.905. For multiview 3D, the fine-tuned SpinePose-m-ft triangulation (31.8 mm MPJPE) improves over zero-shot SpinePose-m (49.3 mm) by 35%, demonstrating that domain-adapted 2D cues directly translate to 3D accuracy. In monocular lifting, full-body training with GT 2D (13.5 mm P-MPJPE) beats spine-only training (17.5 mm) by 23%, and detected-2D full-body (16.3 mm) still outperforms detected-2D spine-only (18.6 mm), showing that global body context aids vertebral localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior 2D spine tracking work (SpinePose/SpineTrack) into the 3D domain by coupling it with OpenSim musculoskeletal simulation. The simulation pipeline draws on established biomechanical modeling tools—Rajagopal's full-body model, Beaucage-Gauvreau's lumbar-spine variant, and Pose2Sim for data I/O—and applies inverse kinematics to merge 2D-detected spinal landmarks with Human3.6M ground-truth markers. Conceptually, it departs from clinical imaging-based spine kinematics (fluoroscopy, EOS, dynamic MRI) by showing that simulation-driven annotation can produce anatomically plausible 3D vertebral motion from standard multi-view RGB, bridging a gap that neither vision nor biomechanics communities had addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SIMSPINE is the first open 3D spine motion dataset (2.14M frames) and provides the first unified benchmark for vision-based vertebral kinematics, enabling reproducible research in spine-aware pose estimation, digital human modeling, and motion analysis. Key limitations include simulation-derived (not in-vivo) annotations, rigid thoracic/cervical modeling, absence of intervertebral translations and dynamics, and restriction to Human3.6M's indoor action set—so the resource is best viewed as a pretraining and method-development proxy rather than a clinical measurement tool. Open questions include benchmarking the per-vertebra angular annotations, extending to dynamics-consistent optimal control, and broadening to in-the-wild and pathology-specific cohorts.
