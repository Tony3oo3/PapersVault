## Problem

Existing RL-based post-training for Vision-Language Models relies on human-annotated labels or task-specific heuristics to define verifiable rewards, making it costly and difficult to scale. While self-evolution has been explored for LLMs, the paradigm remains largely underexplored for VLMs, which face the additional challenge of grounding reasoning in visual inputs. The paper asks whether a VLM can autonomously improve its visual reasoning from raw, unlabeled images alone, without any human supervision signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260886/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes only raw, unlabeled RGB images (47K web images from Vision-47K spanning charts, medical scans, textbooks, and driving scenes) with no ground-truth question-answer pairs. The Questioner takes a single image and outputs a visual question strictly enclosed in <question> tags; the Reasoner takes the image plus that generated question and outputs a free-form text response. Pseudo-labels for training are derived internally via majority voting over m sampled Reasoner responses, and an informative filter retains only samples whose confidence falls between 0.25 and 0.75, discarding both trivially easy and highly noisy examples.

## Method

VisPlay decomposes a single base VLM into two co-evolving roles—an Image-Conditioned Questioner and a Multimodal Reasoner—both initialized from the same pretrained backbone and jointly optimized with GRPO. The Questioner's reward combines an uncertainty term (1 − |2c − 1|, maximized when the frozen Reasoner's majority-vote confidence c = 0.5, i.e., maximum perceived difficulty) with a BLEU-cluster-based diversity penalty that discourages repetitive questions, all gated by a format-validity indicator. The Reasoner is then trained on a curated subset of Questioner-generated questions filtered to moderate confidence (0.25 ≤ c ≤ 0.75), receiving a binary reward for matching the pseudo-label, and updated via GRPO with group-normalized advantages. This iterative loop—Questioner generates harder questions, Reasoner learns to solve them, then the cycle repeats—drives progressive co-evolution of question difficulty and solution accuracy across multiple self-play iterations without any external supervision.

## Results

VisPlay achieves consistent average-score gains across all three backbone models: Qwen2.5-VL-3B improves from 30.61 (base) to 47.27 (Iter 3), Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 40.41 to 48.61, and MiMo-VL-7B from 43.56 to 45.69. The most dramatic improvement is in hallucination detection, where Qwen2.5-VL-3B's HallusionBench score jumps from 32.81 to 94.95 by Iter 2. Against Standard GRPO trained on human-labeled Vision-47K data, VisPlay(Iter 3) matches the 3B model's average (47.3 vs 47.1) and trails the 7B model by 2.1 points (48.6 vs 50.7), while substantially outperforming on HallusionBench (90.5 vs 67.4 for 3B; 92.3 vs 66.6 for 7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Standard GRPO trained for one epoch on human-annotated image-question-answer pairs from Vision-47K. On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, VisPlay(Iter 3) achieves a marginally higher average (47.3 vs 47.1), winning on RealWorldQA (71.9 vs 63.0), VisNumBench (39.2 vs 36.7), and HallusionBench (90.5 vs 67.4), but losing on MMMU (37.1 vs 40.3), MM-Vet (38.1 vs 49.5), and MathVerse (35.2 vs 42.8). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Standard GRPO retains a 2.1-point average advantage (50.7 vs 48.6), driven by stronger MMMU (39.8 vs 38.3), MM-Vet (51.8 vs 46.3), and MathVerse (53.2 vs 39.1) scores, yet VisPlay still dominates HallusionBench by 25.7 points (92.3 vs 66.6). Overall, VisPlay demonstrates that fully self-generated supervision can match human-labeled training on aggregate accuracy while delivering substantially better hallucination resistance, at the cost of some task-specific reasoning benchmarks (notably MathVerse) where curated data provides a clearer signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisPlay extends the self-evolving/self-play paradigm established for LLMs (R-Zero, Absolute Zero, Language Self-Play, SPICE) to the vision-language domain, where it had been largely unexplored. It builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) as the optimization backbone, repurposing group-normalized advantages for a two-role co-evolution setup rather than single-agent reasoning. The key departure from prior RL-based VLM training (which relies on human-annotated datasets like Vision-47K) and from VLM game-based self-play (Vision-Zero, Game-RL, which still depend on external models for data generation) is the use of a single VLM in dual roles with uncertainty-based pseudo-labels, requiring no external supervision or tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VisPlay demonstrates that a VLM can match human-labeled GRPO training in average accuracy (47.3 vs 47.1 on Qwen2.5-VL-3B) while substantially reducing hallucination (32.8→95.0 on HallusionBench), offering a scalable path to VLM improvement in settings where annotation is costly or unavailable. Two key limitations temper the impact: experiments are restricted to ≤7B models and only three iterations, leaving open whether the approach scales to frontier-scale VLMs or whether pseudo-label error accumulation (accuracy dropping from 72% to 61% across iterations) eventually degrades training signal. A robust automated verification mechanism for self-generated data remains an open problem that could determine whether this paradigm generalizes beyond the demonstrated gains.
