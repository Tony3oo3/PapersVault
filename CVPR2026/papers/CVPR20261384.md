## Problem

Existing video-to-audio (V2A) models generate a single holistic soundtrack from a video, but professional audio production requires selectively synthesizing only a user-specified sound source from multi-object scenes. Prior selective approaches rely on spatial prompts (segmentation masks, points) that fail for diffuse or occluded sources, while text-conditioned V2A models treat text merely as an auxiliary semantic cue rather than an explicit selector of which sound to produce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261384/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an 8-second video clip (which may contain multiple co-occurring sound sources) and a short text prompt (e.g., 'dog barking') specifying the target sound, and produces a 16 kHz audio waveform corresponding exclusively to that source. During training, inputs are constructed by horizontally concatenating two videos at a random ratio (sampled from a Beta distribution), with one video's audio serving as the target. Five learnable [SUP] tokens are prepended to the text embedding before cross-attention, and the generator operates on audio latents from a pretrained VAE.

## Method

SELVA consists of a text-conditioned video encoder and an MM-DiT flow-matching generator. The encoder inserts a cross-attention block after frozen Synchformer spatiotemporal blocks, using text embeddings (from FLAN-T5) as keys/values, with five learnable supplementary tokens prepended to suppress high-norm attention artifacts from irrelevant visual tokens and enforce intent-focused grounding. Training is self-supervised via autonomous video-mixing (two videos concatenated, one's audio as target) and organized in two stages: first, the student encoder is distilled from a frozen teacher Synchformer via L2 regression on mixed inputs; second, the generator (initialized from MMAudio-small) is fine-tuned with conditional flow matching, updating only the video projection layer and adaLN modules (19M and 22M parameters, ~14% of each model). This parameter-efficient design avoids costly full training while enabling selective sound generation without explicit source-separated supervision.

## Results

On VGG-MONOAUDIO (inter-class), SELVA achieves the best KAD (0.676), IS (13.07), IB (0.3251), and DeSync (0.721), outperforming the closest competitor MMAudio-S-16k by 22.6% in KAD and 10.1% in DeSync. On the intra-class subset, SELVA ties VinTAGe on FAD (37.0) while leading in KAD (0.492 vs. 0.690), IB (0.3262 vs. 0.2892), and DeSync (0.639 vs. 1.304). In the human study, SELVA scores 4.53 on text-audio alignment and 4.38 on audiovisual synchronization, surpassing VOS+MMAudio (3.78 and 3.91) and MMAudio (4.12 and 4.05), with audio quality approaching ground-truth levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SELVA outperforms all four baselines on temporal alignment (DeSync) across both subsets, with the largest margin over ReWaS (0.721 vs. 1.364 inter-class; 0.639 vs. 1.377 intra-class) and a consistent ~10% improvement over MMAudio-S-16k. On semantic alignment via ImageBind (IB), SELVA leads all baselines by a clear margin (0.3251 vs. 0.3135 for MMAudio inter-class; 0.3262 vs. 0.3248 for MMAudio intra-class), confirming that text-conditioned video encoding improves source-specific grounding. VinTAGe achieves the best FAD (50.5) and KL (1.69) on inter-class, slightly edging SELVA (51.7, 1.85), but trails substantially on DeSync (1.292 vs. 0.721) and IB (0.2850 vs. 0.3251), indicating its holistic generation strategy sacrifices selectivity. The VOS+MMAudio baseline shows competitive CLAP scores (0.291 vs. 0.292) but degrades sharply on DeSync (0.991 vs. 0.721), revealing that segmentation-based spatial masking struggles with fast-moving or diffuse sound sources. In the human study, SELVA's text-audio alignment MOS (4.53) far exceeds VOS+MMAudio (3.78), exposing a gap between objective CLAP scores and perceived fidelity to the intended sound source.

## Relation to Prior Work

SELVA builds directly on MMAudio's MM-DiT generator and Synchformer video encoder, but repositions the text prompt from an auxiliary semantic cue (as in ReWaS, VinTAGe, and MMAudio) into an explicit selector that modulates which visual features the encoder extracts. It departs from segmentation-based selective generation (VOS+MMAudio, SAM2-based approaches) by eliminating the need for spatial prompts, and from sequential generation (Hayakawa et al.) by performing single-pass selective synthesis. The learnable [SUP] tokens draw conceptually from register tokens in vision transformers, while the self-supervised video-mixing strategy reformulates ideas from audiovisual speech separation for the selective V2A setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in multimedia production where sound designers need individually controllable tracks rather than a single holistic soundtrack, and the text-only interface removes the computational and usability overhead of segmentation models. The introduced VGG-MONOAUDIO benchmark, while small (67 source videos, 1,071 mixed pairs), provides the first dedicated evaluation protocol for selective V2A. Key limitations include reliance on noisy VGGSound training data, simple noun-verb text labels that limit fine-grained control, and residual sound-substitution failures when the encoder loses track of a moving target. Open questions include whether full (non-parameter-efficient) training closes the remaining gap and whether the approach generalizes to more complex, descriptive prompts.
