## Problem

1D visual tokenizers achieve semantic compactness by discarding local spatial priors, but they fundamentally struggle to preserve fine-grained visual content, producing reconstructions with factual errors such as mixed animal traits, incorrect poses, and missing objects. Simply scaling model parameters or increasing token count offers only superficial mitigation, and existing text-guided approaches require compulsory external text and heavy-weight text encoders at inference, breaking the image-only pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262948/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image as input and produces a sequence of 1D hybrid discrete tokens (e.g., 224 visual + 32 textual tokens for 256 total, or 96+32 for 128 total) that can be decoded back into a reconstructed image or fed to a generative model. During training, offline text embeddings from SigLIP2 (computed from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-generated captions) serve as alignment targets, but at inference the pipeline is strictly image-only with no external text or text encoder required. The token sequence is functionally partitioned: visual tokens carry appearance information while textual tokens encode content-level semantic cues.

## Method

VLTok extends the GigaTok CNN-Transformer hybrid architecture by splitting the Q-Former's learnable query tokens into visual (N^v) and textual (N^t) registers, so the 2D-to-1D encoder outputs a unified sequence of hybrid tokens that are jointly vector-quantized. The core novelty is the Self-Prompted Alignment (SPA) loss: an auxiliary Q-Former projects the image-derived textual tokens into the embedding space of a frozen pre-trained text encoder (SigLIP2), and an L2 distance loss distills fine-grained linguistic semantics into those tokens. Because the textual tokens are generated directly from the image rather than from external captions, the approach is termed 'self-prompted,' eliminating any text dependency at inference. Training combines standard tokenizer losses (reconstruction, VQ, PatchGAN, DINOv2 REPA) with the SPA alignment loss, and the same tokenizer is validated under both MaskGIT and LlamaGen generation frameworks.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, VLTok-B-L (622M params, 256 tokens) achieves rFID 0.72 for reconstruction and gFID 1.70 (MaskGIT-UViT-L) / 2.65 (LlamaGen 111M) for generation, setting the best rFID among all discrete 1D tokenizers. Under identical architecture and parameter budget, VLTok reduces rFID by 11.1% (0.81→0.72) and gFID by 18.7% (3.26→2.65) relative to GigaTok-B-L. The method also outperforms the 4.7× larger GigaTok-XL-XXL (2.9B) by 0.07 rFID, and ablation confirms the self-prompted alignment module contributes −0.18 rFID, −0.38 gFID, and +3.7% linear probing accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GigaTok-B-L (same 622M architecture, 256 tokens), VLTok improves rFID from 0.81 to 0.72 and gFID from 3.26 to 2.65 (LlamaGen 111M), a 18.7% relative gFID reduction. VLTok also beats the much larger GigaTok-XL-XXL (2.9B) by 0.07 rFID, whereas scaling GigaTok from 622M to 2.9B yields only 0.02 rFID gain. Compared to TexTok (which requires 128 external text tokens from a 3B T5 encoder plus a smaller patch size of 8), VLTok achieves competitive rFID (0.72 vs 0.69 at 256 tokens) while maintaining a purely image-only inference pipeline with no external text dependency. Against TiTok-S (72M, 128 tokens), VLTok reduces rFID from 1.71 to 1.01 and gFID from 1.97 to 1.79 under MaskGIT. Versus FlexTok (1.4B, 128 tokens), VLTok achieves lower rFID (1.01 vs 1.20) with roughly half the parameters. In the ablation comparing alignment strategies (Table 5), VLTok's self-prompted alignment (SPA) achieves rFID 1.01 versus 1.26 for image-text contrastive learning under identical 128-token settings, a 24% relative improvement over CL.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLTok extends the 1D tokenizer paradigm introduced by TiTok and scaled by GigaTok, adopting the same CNN-Transformer hybrid architecture but partitioning the 1D token sequence into visual and textual subsequences. It departs from text-guided approaches like TexTok and TA-TiTok, which require external captions and a heavy text encoder at inference, by generating textual tokens directly from the image and aligning them with pre-trained text embeddings only during training. It also contrasts with 2D cross-modal tokenizers (VILA-U, UniTok) that rely on global contrastive learning over pooled features, arguing that such coarse alignment is insufficient for the fine-grained correspondences needed in 1D sequential representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-prompted alignment paradigm offers a practical path to multi-modal enrichment of 1D tokenizers without the inference-time cost of external text, making it attractive for image-only reconstruction and generation pipelines. The clean ablation showing SPA outperforms contrastive learning by 24% in rFID under identical conditions provides strong evidence that token-level fine-grained alignment is the right mechanism for 1D settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (512, 1024), whether it transfers to continuous (non-discrete) token spaces, and whether the caption quality dependency (currently requiring a 7B VLM for data construction) can be reduced. The method is validated only on ImageNet-1K at 256×256, leaving its behavior on diverse, high-resolution, or video content untested.
