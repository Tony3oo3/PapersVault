## Problem

Source-free UDA for panoramic semantic segmentation faces a compounded challenge: equirectangular projection introduces severe, latitude-dependent geometric distortions absent in pinhole source domains, while the absence of source data amplifies domain shift, producing unreliable pseudo-labels that disproportionately degrade minority-class performance. Existing SFUDA methods lack mechanisms to simultaneously denoise biased pseudo-labels and handle the scale variance inherent in panoramic imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260192/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only a frozen pre-trained source model (SegFormer-B1/B2 trained on pinhole Cityscapes or Stanford2D3D) and unlabeled target panoramic images (DensePASS at 2048×400 for outdoor, SPan at 2048×1024 for indoor). Internally, each target image is processed at two resolutions: a low-resolution context crop (downsampled by factor s=2) and a high-resolution detail crop extracted from it, with all crop boundaries aligned to the feature-map grid to guarantee pixel-level correspondence. The output is a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over the full panoramic image, evaluated with mIoU across 19 (outdoor) or 8 (indoor) classes.

## Method

DAPASS combines two synergistic modules. The Panoramic Confidence-Guided Denoising (PCGD) module first splits the target set into consistent and inconsistent subsets using a KL-divergence-based consistency score computed between the frozen source model and the self-training model after τ iterations; it then applies a dual-path strategy: Path A performs bi-level optimization where an inner gradient step on a noisy sample is accepted only if it also reduces loss on its nearest stable neighbor in feature space, while Path B constructs a per-class Top-K pool of high-confidence minority-class samples and applies copy-paste augmentation to enrich rare-class supervision. The Cross-Resolution Attention Module (CRAM) addresses ERP distortion by jointly processing a low-resolution context crop (global layout) and a high-resolution detail crop (local fidelity), fusing their predictions via a learnable sigmoid-gated scale-attention map that adaptively weights HR detail against LR context, with the encoder, segmentation head, and attention head trained end-to-end under a temperature-scaled cross-entropy loss on both fused and detail predictions.

## Results

On the outdoor Cityscapes-to-DensePASS (C-to-D) benchmark, DAPASS achieves 55.04% mIoU with SegFormer-B2 and 53.16% with B1, surpassing the previous SOTA 360SFUDA++ by +2.05% and +2.97% respectively. On the indoor Stanford2D3D Spin-to-Span benchmark, DAPASS reaches 70.38% mIoU (B2) and 69.56% (B1), improving over 360SFUDA++ by +1.54% and +3.02%. Notably, DAPASS also exceeds the source-dependent UDA method Trans4PASS+w/MPA (67.16%) by 3.22% on Spin-to-Span, despite operating without access to source data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPASS outperforms all 11 compared baselines in the source-free setting. Against the closest SFUDA competitor 360SFUDA++ [1], DAPASS gains +2.05% mIoU (B2) on C-to-D and +1.54% (B2) on Spin-to-Span, with larger margins on B1 (+2.97% and +3.02%). Over the earlier SFUDA methods SFDA [11] and DATC [39], improvements are substantial: +10.46% and +10.10% mIoU on C-to-D, and +14.80% and +15.62% on Spin-to-Span. Among source-dependent UDA methods, DAPASS (B2, 55.04%) surpasses DPPASS [10] (55.30%) by a narrow margin on C-to-D but falls slightly short of DATR [30] (56.81%); however, on Spin-to-Span, DAPASS (70.38%) exceeds the best UDA method Trans4PASS+w/MPA [6] (67.16%) by 3.22%, demonstrating that the source-free constraint need not sacrifice performance. The largest per-class gains appear on tail categories (e.g., Rider: 20.97% vs. 30.76% for 360SFUDA++ B2 on C-to-D; Door: 53.33% vs. 53.15% on Spin-to-Span), validating the class-balancing copy-paste component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the source-free UDA framework for panoramic segmentation established by 360SFUDA [34] and 360SFUDA++ [1], which first introduced the SFUDA constraint to the panoramic domain. Its denoising mechanism (Path A) is directly inspired by Stable Neighbor Denoising (SND) [35], adapting the bi-level optimization to a panoramic setting with neighbor retrieval in feature space. The cross-resolution attention module (CRAM) draws on multi-scale fusion ideas from distortion-aware panoramic methods like Trans4PASS [6] and DPPASS [10], but repurposes them for the source-free constraint where HR crops calibrate LR global semantics rather than simply combining scales. The overall positioning is as a methodological refinement within an already-established task, combining pseudo-label quality control and distortion-aware feature fusion under a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source-free constraint is practically critical in autonomous driving and VR applications where source data is proprietary or privacy-protected, making this a relevant deployment scenario. The paper identifies and partially addresses two intertwined failure modes—pseudo-label noise amplified by long-tailed class distributions and ERP-induced geometric distortion—that prior SFUDA methods handled separately or inadequately. However, the authors acknowledge that the copy-paste balancing strategy is 'relatively naive' without geometric or contextual alignment, and tail classes (Rider, Person) still show notable performance gaps, leaving open the question of whether geometry-aware or diffusion-based augmentation could close this gap. The modular design (PCGD + CRAM) suggests the components could be transplanted to other SFUDA or panoramic segmentation settings beyond the two benchmarks tested.
