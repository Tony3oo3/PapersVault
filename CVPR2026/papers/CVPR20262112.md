## Problem

Existing synthetic data learning methods (e.g., CutMix, diffusion-based augmentation) improve model robustness only indirectly by diversifying training samples, without explicitly instructing the model which input regions correspond to the target object. As a result, models can latch onto spurious correlations introduced by synthesis biases and artifacts rather than learning true discriminative features. Although the synthesis process inherently knows which pixels originate from which source (provenance), this information has not been exploited as a supervisory signal to guide where the model should attend.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either RGB images or skeleton sequences (P×F×K×V tensors) as input, which are first synthesized via mixing (CutMix, ResizeMix, PuzzleMix, BatchMix) or diffusion-based editing (ALIA). For weakly supervised object localization, the output is a bounding box estimated from class activation maps or attention maps plus a class label; for spatio-temporal action localization, it is a per-frame action label per detected person; for image classification, it is a single class label. The provenance mask M ∈ {0,1}^{H×W} (or {0,1}^{P×F×E} for skeletons) is extracted automatically during synthesis and serves as the auxiliary supervisory signal, requiring no additional annotation.

## Method

The core contribution is a provenance loss L_PG that regularizes input gradients using the provenance mask obtained during data synthesis. For soft-label mixing (CutMix, skeleton BatchMix), the loss suppresses the input gradient of class f_A in regions originating from x_B and vice versa: L_PG = ‖(1−M)⊙∇x̃ f_A(x̃) + M⊙∇x̃ f_B(x̃)‖². For hard-label image editing (diffusion-based), it suppresses the gradient of the retained class f_y in edited (non-target) regions: L_PG = ‖(1−M)⊙∇x̃ f_y(x̃)‖². The total training objective is L_total = L_cls + α·L_PG, where α controls regularization strength. The approach is modality-agnostic and synthesis-method-agnostic: any pipeline that can identify non-target regions during synthesis can incorporate the provenance loss without architectural changes or extra annotations, and the method is robust to moderate imprecision in the provenance mask (performance drop <1% for up to 30% area error).

## Results

On CUB-200-2011 for weakly supervised object localization, the proposed method improves mean MaxBoxAccV2 from 62.3% (VGG16+CAM+CutMix) to 65.1%, and from 91.4% (DeiT-S+SAT) to 92.1% when combined with CutMix. On UCF101-24 for spatio-temporal action localization, AP improves from 37.4% (SKP) to 39.7% with the proposed method. For image classification, the largest gain appears on Waterbirds, where ALIA+Ours achieves 80.7% Top-1 accuracy versus 71.4% for ALIA alone (+9.3 pp), directly demonstrating suppression of background spurious correlations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all mixing-based baselines: CutMix (62.3%→65.1% mean on CUB/VGG16), ResizeMix (57.6%→62.2%), and PuzzleMix (56.8%→58.2%), confirming that provenance-guided gradient regularization is complementary to any mixing strategy. Against the strongest SoTA localization methods, SAT+Ours (92.1% mean) surpasses SAT (91.4%), SCM (89.3%), TS-CAM (78.8%), and GTFormer (77.0% at δ=0.7). On UCF101-24, the method improves AP from 37.4% (SKP) to 39.7%, outperforming both RGB-based baselines (Chéron et al. 17.7%, Arnab et al. 35.0%). For image classification, ALIA+Ours beats ALIA on all three datasets, with the most dramatic gain on Waterbirds (80.7% vs. 71.4%, +9.3 pp), while also improving iWildCam (84.4% vs. 83.5%) and CUB (72.0% vs. 71.7%). The method also outperforms plain CutMix and Random Augment on all three classification benchmarks, indicating that the provenance loss provides benefits beyond simple data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic-data learning line (CutMix, mixup, ALIA/diffusion-based augmentation) by repurposing provenance information—already available as a byproduct of the synthesis process—as an explicit auxiliary supervisory signal for input gradient regularization. The closest conceptual predecessor is Ross et al. (2017), which constrains input gradients to align with human gaze regions; here, the authors generalize that idea to the multi-class setting and, crucially, eliminate the need for external annotations by sourcing the region mask directly from the synthesis pipeline (CutMix masks, skeleton-sequence mixing masks, or Otsu-thresholded difference images from diffusion editing). The paper departs from prior spurious-correlation suppression methods (feature sieves, last-layer retraining, attention branches) that require manual auxiliary labels or architectural modifications, positioning provenance-guided gradient masking as an annotation-free, architecture-agnostic drop-in regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical insight—that provenance information is 'free' supervision already embedded in any mixing or editing pipeline—makes the method broadly adoptable across synthetic learning frameworks without additional annotation cost, and the 9.6 pp gain on Waterbirds demonstrates that this free signal can substantially reduce background-driven spurious correlations. The cross-modality validation (images, skeleton sequences) and the finding that the provenance loss also accelerates convergence (50→15 epochs on CUB) strengthen its practical appeal. Open questions include how the approach behaves when provenance is inherently ambiguous (e.g., soft boundaries in diffusion-edited images beyond Otsu thresholding), whether it composes with other gradient-based regularizers, and whether the second-order gradient overhead becomes prohibitive at larger scale or with deeper transformers.
