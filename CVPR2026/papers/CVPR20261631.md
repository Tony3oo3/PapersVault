## Problem

Rapid HRTEM imaging of nucleation dynamics requires millisecond-scale exposures, producing images with extremely low signal-to-noise ratios where atomic positions are obscured. Existing denoising networks apply uniform processing across all spatial positions or frequency bands, making them unable to distinguish signals from noise in the disordered, semi-ordered atomic arrangements characteristic of nucleation. Additionally, no existing dataset combines realistic HRTEM-specific noise (including column noise absent in STEM) with the disordered structures needed for nucleation observation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel grayscale noisy HRTEM image (H×W×1) captured at short exposure times and produces a clean single-channel image (H×W×1) with a black background and clear white Gaussian dots at each atomic position. The denoised output feeds directly into an atomic localization step where atom positions are extracted for downstream structural analysis. All processing operates on single-channel images, consistent with the intensity-based nature of HRTEM diffraction imaging.

## Method

The proposed SCGN network uses spatial-frequency enhancement blocks (n=8, C=64 channels) with residual connections, where each block splits features into spatial and frequency branches. In the spatial branch, a window-based standard deviation module computes local 3×3 SD (a nonlinear operation not expressible as ordinary convolution) to generate pixel-wise dynamic weights via a 1×1 conv + sigmoid, enabling position-adaptive selection among parallel 3×3 convolutions. In the frequency branch, features are transformed via RFFT, augmented with a 2-channel position embedding, decoupled into frequency bands by a 1×1 conv, and then weighted by a dual-pooling channel-attention-like classifier that assigns per-band weights in [0,2]—enhancing signal bands (>1) and suppressing noise bands (<1)—before inverse RFFT returns to the spatial domain. The network is trained end-to-end with Adam (lr=2e-4) and L1 loss for 100 epochs on the newly introduced NUC dataset (1000 pairs), which generates disordered non-overlapping atomic arrangements via Poisson disk sampling with Perlin noise masks and applies HRTEM-calibrated noise (column noise σ_c=0.6641; signal-dependent pointwise noise σ_p=0.03583·I_clean+1.379).

## Results

On the three synthetic datasets, SCGN achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all settings: 32.55 dB / 0.9638 on TEMImageNet, 38.99 dB / 0.9634 on the SFIN dataset, and 26.55 dB / 0.9717 on the proposed NUC dataset. On the NUC dataset, SCGN outperforms the strongest baseline SFIN by 0.85 dB in PSNR. For the downstream atomic localization task, SCGN achieves the highest IoU on all three datasets (0.7444, 0.6083, and 0.7317), confirming that the denoising gains translate to more accurate atom position recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCGN outperforms all four baselines on every synthetic dataset for both PSNR and SSIM. The closest competitor is SFIN [25] (CVPR 2025), which achieves 38.48 dB on its own dataset and 31.76 dB on TEMImageNet; SCGN edges it out by 0.51 dB and 0.79 dB respectively, and by a larger 0.85 dB on the NUC dataset where disordered structures are present. HINT [20] (ICCV 2025) trails SFIN by roughly 1 dB on TEMImageNet and 0.86 dB on NUC, while AtomSegNet [28] (Sci. Rep. 2021) lags further behind (22.34 dB on TEMImageNet, 23.44 dB on NUC). The traditional Gaussian filter is far inferior, producing blurry outputs with PSNR below 17 dB on all datasets. On real HRTEM nucleation data, SCGN is the only method that avoids qualitative errors such as missing atoms, spurious atoms, and adhesion of neighboring atoms, whereas AtomSegNet, SFIN, and HINT all exhibit such artifacts; UDVD [5] (Science 2025), an unsupervised multi-frame method, produces results even blurrier than the Gaussian filter because frame-to-frame content changes during nucleation break its mutual-supervision assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial-frequency interactive denoising paradigm established by SFIN [25] from STEM to HRTEM imaging, where the noise model differs fundamentally (column noise replaces scan noise). The core architectural idea—using statistical characteristics to guide denoising—draws conceptual inspiration from the classical Yau-Yau nonlinear filter [47], translating its signal/noise separation principle into learnable spatial deviation and frequency band weighting modules. The frequency band classification module is explicitly modeled on channel attention [51] but operates in the RFFT latent space rather than the spatial domain, and the dataset generation pipeline builds on Poisson disk sampling and Perlin noise to produce disordered atomic arrangements absent from prior datasets like TEMImageNet [28] and the SFIN dataset [25].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in in-situ nucleation observation: existing denoising methods and datasets are tailored to ordered crystal structures and STEM noise, making them poorly suited for the disordered, rapidly evolving atomic arrangements captured by HRTEM during amorphous-to-crystalline transitions. The NUC dataset and HRTEM-specific noise calibration (signal-dependent pointwise noise plus constant column noise) provide a more realistic training regime, and the finding that retraining other architectures (AtomSegNet, SFIN, HINT) on NUC substantially improves their real-data performance suggests the dataset itself is a valuable community resource. Limitations include the noise calibration being tied to a single instrument and beam intensity range, and the method's generalizability to other microscopy modalities or noise types remains untested. The authors suggest exploring additional statistical characteristics (median, higher-order moments) as a path toward more general-purpose denoising.
