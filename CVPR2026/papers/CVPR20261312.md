## Problem

Open-vocabulary panoptic segmentation suffers from two coupled failures: mask-transformer objectness heads trained on closed vocabularies systematically suppress masks of unseen categories (mask selection bias), and vision-language models like CLIP, trained for global image-text matching, produce per-pixel features that misalign with object boundaries, degrading mask-level classification. Prior fine-tuning approaches (e.g., MAFT+) address the second issue but introduce heavy memory overhead and show notable performance drops outside their training domain.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a set of text vocabulary terms (class names encoded by CLIP's text encoder). It outputs a full panoptic segmentation: per-pixel class labels distinguishing 'things' (individual object instances with unique IDs) from 'stuff' (background regions), along with binary instance masks. The mask transformer proposes a fixed number of candidate masks per image, which are gated by the adjusted objectness score before classification; the final output is a set of retained masks each assigned a vocabulary class label.

## Method

OVRCOAT builds on a Mask2Former + frozen-CLIP (ConvNeXt-Large, OpenCLIP/LAION-2B) architecture and introduces two complementary modules. COAT (CLIP-conditioned objectness adjustment) computes a CLIP-based classification certainty for each mask proposal via global mask pooling and softmax over vocabulary embeddings, then rescales the mask transformer's objectness score using a trust factor γ=0.5 to prevent valid unseen-class masks from being rejected as background. OVR (open-vocabulary mask-to-text refinement) is a two-stage fine-tuning protocol: stage one pre-trains the mask transformer with CLIP frozen, and stage two unfreezes the CLIP image encoder (keeping final MLP and norm layers frozen) to jointly optimize a combined loss of cross-entropy mask classification (supervised via Hungarian matching) and the standard Mask2Former mask loss. This design avoids the content-dependent vocabulary embeddings and mask-overlap computations of MAFT+, reducing peak training memory by ~56% while improving PQ by 5.5% on ADE20K.

## Results

OVRCOAT achieves 28.6 PQ on ADE20K, 19.6 PQ on Mapillary Vistas, 45.3 PQ on Cityscapes, and 54.6 PQ on COCO, setting a new state of the art on the three out-of-vocabulary benchmarks. The headline gains over the prior SOTA (MAFT+pan) are +1.5 PQ on ADE20K, +3.9 PQ on Mapillary Vistas, and +7.0 PQ on Cityscapes. On COCO (the in-vocabulary dataset), OVRCOAT trails ODISE by 0.8 PQ (54.6 vs. 55.4) due to the COAT module replacing the COCO-tuned objectness head. Training memory is reduced by ~56% (12.5 GB vs. 27.0 GB per image) compared to MAFT+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVRCOAT outperforms the prior state-of-the-art MAFT+pan [12] on all four datasets: +1.5 PQ on ADE20K (28.6 vs. 27.1), +3.9 PQ on Mapillary Vistas (19.6 vs. 15.7), +7.0 PQ on Cityscapes (45.3 vs. 38.3), and +4.3 PQ on COCO (54.6 vs. 50.3). Against FC-CLIP [44], OVRCOAT gains +1.8 PQ on ADE20K, +1.3 on Mapillary, +1.3 on Cityscapes, and +0.2 on COCO. Against ODISE [41], OVRCOAT leads by +5.2 PQ on ADE20K, +5.4 on Mapillary, and +21.4 on Cityscapes, but trails by 0.8 PQ on COCO (54.6 vs. 55.4), which the authors attribute to COAT replacing the COCO-specialised objectness head. Older baselines (MaskCLIP [7], FreeSeg [25], OPSNet [2]) are surpassed by 9–13 PQ on ADE20K. On the secondary semantic segmentation task (Table 3), OVRCOAT (14.0 mIoU on ADE20K-847) falls short of MAFT+sem (15.1) and FC-CLIP (14.8), as the COAT module introduces noise when no masks are rejected in the semantic inference pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

OVRCOAT builds directly on the Mask2Former mask-transformer architecture and the FC-CLIP design of a single frozen convolutional CLIP backbone with mask-pooling classification. It departs from the more complex fine-tuning paradigm of MAFT+, which relies on representation-consistency losses and content-dependent vocabulary embeddings, by proposing a simpler two-component framework: a test-time CLIP-conditioned objectness adjustment (COAT) that corrects the void-token bias without additional training, and a lightweight two-stage mask-to-text refinement (OVR) that fine-tunes CLIP's regional alignment. The paper explicitly positions itself against the mask-selection bias identified by Šarić et al. [34] and the limited localization of contrastively-trained VLMs, framing both as coupled failures that prior methods address only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 56% training-memory reduction relative to MAFT+ while simultaneously improving PQ by 5.5% on ADE20K, making open-vocabulary panoptic segmentation accessible on resource-constrained hardware and enabling larger batch sizes. The modular design (COAT and OVR as independent, swappable components) lowers the barrier for adoption in related segmentation pipelines. Limitations include a small regression on seen-class performance (COCO) due to COAT replacing the original specialized mask evaluator, and the fact that COAT actually degrades semantic segmentation because no masks are rejected in that setting. Open questions the authors raise include extending the framework to video panoptics with temporal consistency and coupling with promptable or interactive segmentation as stronger VLMs emerge.
