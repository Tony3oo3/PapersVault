## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems are limited to perspective cameras with narrow fields of view, creating blind spots that reduce tracking robustness. While omnidirectional SLAM methods exist, none integrate omnidirectional vision with 3DGS as a unified dense scene representation for both tracking and mapping. This paper presents the first omnidirectional 3DGS-based SLAM system to close this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262594/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes sequential equirectangular panorama frames (1920×960 for omnidirectional, 720×960 for perspective) in either RGB or RGBD format, where each pixel corresponds to a ray parameterized by azimuth φ and elevation θ on the unit sphere. It outputs per-frame camera poses (rotation and translation), a persistent 3D Gaussian Splatting map of the scene, and differentiable renders of color, depth, and silhouette panoramas from arbitrary viewpoints. The outdoor scene required half-resolution input due to memory constraints, and the system supports both real Insta360 captures and Blender-rendered synthetic sequences with realistic fisheye distortion.

## Method

ODGS-SLAM extends the ODGS omnidirectional projection pipeline to support camera pose optimization by deriving closed-form analytical gradients of the photometric and geometric losses with respect to camera extrinsics through the two-step tangent-plane-to-equirectangular projection. A distortion correction matrix Q_o applies sec(θ) horizontal scaling to compensate for equirectangular stretching, and a latitude weight w_lat(y)=cos((y/H−1/2)π) down-weights polar regions in all error terms. For memory efficiency, a novel graph-based key frame removal strategy exploits the rotational invariance of omnidirectional overlap: spatially proximate key frames are identified via KD-tree, their rotation-aligned similarity is computed through spherical warping, and a greedy algorithm prunes redundant nodes from the resulting undirected redundancy graph. The overall pipeline follows the tracking–keyframing–mapping structure of Gaussian Splatting SLAM, using the 3DGS map as a unified representation where the same differentiable renderer serves both pose estimation (50 iterations per frame) and map optimization (with adaptive density control every 150 iterations).

## Results

On the synthetic RGB sequences, ODGS-SLAM achieves a mean RMSE ATE of 0.068 m, substantially outperforming MonoGS (0.663 m) and PMSP (0.093 m). For RGBD synthetic input, ODGS-SLAM reaches 0.029 m mean RMSE ATE versus 0.579 m (MonoGS) and 2.295 m (GASL). On real Insta360 Pro RGB data, ODGS-SLAM scores 0.031 m average RMSE ATE compared to 0.310 m (MonoGS) and 0.122 m (PMSP). Mapping quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) is comparable to other 3DGS-SLAM methods, with ODGS-SLAM achieving 28.45 dB PSNR and 0.89 SSIM on synthetic RGB, close to MonoGS (25.99 dB, 0.86 SSIM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODGS-SLAM outperforms all three baselines on tracking accuracy across every evaluated scenario. Against MonoGS (GS-SLAM [55]), the improvement is dramatic: 0.068 m vs. 0.663 m mean RMSE ATE on synthetic RGB (≈10× lower) and 0.029 m vs. 0.579 m on synthetic RGBD (≈20× lower). Against GASL (Gaussian-SLAM [58]), ODGS-SLAM achieves 0.029 m vs. 2.295 m on RGBD synthetic sequences, a roughly 79× reduction. Against the feature-based omnidirectional method PMSP [43], ODGS-SLAM still wins consistently (0.068 m vs. 0.093 m synthetic RGB; 0.031 m vs. 0.122 m Insta360 Pro RGB; 0.032 m vs. 0.068 m Insta360 X4 direct mount), with the gap widening on the extension mount (0.062 m vs. 0.148 m). Statistical tests (Kruskal-Wallis H, p < .001; Dunn's post-hoc with Bonferroni correction) confirm the tracking improvements are significant. On mapping quality, ODGS-SLAM is comparable to MonoGS and GASL (e.g., 28.45 dB PSNR vs. 25.99 dB for MonoGS on synthetic RGB; 29.37 dB vs. 32.36 dB for GASL on synthetic RGBD), indicating the omnidirectional extension does not degrade reconstruction fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODGS-SLAM sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: 3DGS-based SLAM (GS-SLAM, Gaussian-SLAM, Gaussian Splatting SLAM) which handles perspective cameras, and omnidirectional SLAM (OmniSLAM, PMSP) which uses feature-based or volumetric-fusion mapping. The paper extends the 3DGS-SLAM pipeline by integrating the ODGS omnidirectional projection model, deriving closed-form camera-pose gradients for equirectangular rendering, and adding a latitude-weighting scheme to counter polar distortion. A key departure from prior 3DGS-SLAM is the graph-based key-frame removal strategy that exploits the rotational invariance of panoramic overlap, a property unavailable to perspective-camera systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to unify omnidirectional sensing with 3DGS for both tracking and mapping, opening a path toward full 360° dense neural SLAM for AR and autonomous navigation where blind spots are unacceptable. The statistically significant tracking gains (an order of magnitude lower ATE RMSE than other 3DGS-SLAM methods) validate that wider FoV directly benefits gradient-based pose optimization. However, the main open question is runtime: tracking exceeds one second per frame, and the outdoor scene remains challenging (RGB mode fails from scale drift), limiting near-term deployment. The graph-based key-frame pruning is a practical contribution for memory-constrained omnidirectional mapping that could transfer to other panoramic 3DGS applications.
