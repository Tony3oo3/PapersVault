## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for vision-language models relies on sequence-level outcome rewards that are too coarse to diagnose which specific perception or reasoning step failed in a multi-step chain. This sparse-reward regime creates a hard credit-assignment problem: hallucinated objects or spatial relations inserted mid-chain receive the same uniform signal as correct steps, bottlenecking the model's ability to learn fine-grained visual grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262081/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Perceval takes a triple ⟨image, text query, model reasoning response⟩ and produces a structured verification in a think-then-answer format: a  block analyzing each perceptual claim against visual evidence, followed by an <answer> block containing either 'The response is correct' or a Python list of exact hallucinated substrings. These substrings are then mapped to a binary token-level penalty mask over the response. At inference, the system outputs a corrected text response via iterative truncation and regeneration, bounded by a maximum of k refinement iterations.

## Method

Perceval is a perception-centric process reward model trained via supervised fine-tuning on perception-intensive data (visual search, referring-expression grounding) with structured hallucination annotations generated by Gemini-2.5-Pro. The key training-time contribution modifies GRPO's sequence-level advantage into a token-level signal: for each response, Perceval identifies hallucinated spans, and the advantage is rescaled as A′_{i,t} = A_i − α·m_{i,t}·|A_i|, applying a stronger penalty to hallucinated tokens while preserving the original sequence-level preference for correct tokens. This injects direct, fine-grained corrective pressure into the policy gradient without requiring step-level human annotations. At inference, a Truncate-then-Regenerate loop uses Perceval to detect the first erroneous claim, truncates the response before that span, and prompts the policy to regenerate from the verified prefix; a variant appends a brief self-reflection prompt to guide the correction. The approach exploits the fact that perceptual claims (objects, attributes, spatial relations) are directly verifiable against the image, unlike abstract logical steps, making automated process-level supervision feasible.

## Results

On the 3B model, Perceval improves V* (All) from 80.10 to 83.25 (+3.15), MathVision from 23.36 to 26.32 (+2.96), and ChartQA from 83.32 to 86.48 (+3.16) over the GRPO baseline. On the 7B model, V* (Position) jumps from 82.89 to 86.84 (+3.95) and MathVision from 27.96 to 30.92 (+2.96), while BLINK improves from 53.55 to 54.49. Notably, the 7B model without any external tools reaches 86.39 on V* (All), approaching the tool-augmented DeepEyes (87.43) and surpassing Pixel-Reasoner (84.30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct GRPO baseline (same Qwen2.5-VL backbone and training data), Perceval wins on 9 of 10 metrics at 3B and 9 of 10 at 7B, with the largest gains on V* Position (+2.64 at 3B, +3.95 at 7B) and MathVision (+2.96 at both scales); the only regressions are minor (BLINK −0.38 at 3B, ChartQA −0.72 at 7B). Among external 3B baselines, Perceval leads all competitors on V* (83.25 vs. 72.25 for VLM-R1, the next best) and ChartQA (86.48 vs. 84.60 for Jigsaw-R1). At 7B, Perceval surpasses OpenVLThinker on V* Position (86.84 vs. 80.26) and BLINK (54.49 vs. 51.36), but trails Vision-R1 on MathVision (30.92 vs. 36.18) and OpenVLThinker on ChartQA (84.44 vs. 89.00). Critically, Perceval achieves 86.39 on V* (All) without any external tools, closing the gap to tool-augmented DeepEyes (87.43) and exceeding Pixel-Reasoner (84.30), suggesting that intrinsic perceptual improvement can rival tool-based grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO framework (from DeepSeekMath) by replacing its uniform sequence-level advantage with a token-level advantage modulated by a perception-centric process reward model (Perceval). The PRM concept draws on the step-level verification paradigm from Math-Shepherd, but departs from general PRMs by restricting supervision to perceptual claims (objects, attributes, spatial relations) that are directly verifiable against the image, sidestepping the expensive and ambiguous step-level annotation problem. Unlike tool-augmented approaches (DeepEyes, PixelReasoner) that rely on external image manipulation for grounding, Perceval operates purely as a text-based critic that identifies hallucinated spans within the model's own reasoning trace, making it a lightweight, model-agnostic supervision layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a simple, plug-and-play mechanism (binary penalty mask + scalar α) that can be dropped into any GRPO-based VLM training pipeline to improve credit assignment without architectural changes. The generalization finding—that perception-focused PRM supervision transfers to math and chart reasoning—suggests perceptual grounding is a foundational bottleneck whose improvement cascades upward, a hypothesis worth testing across more backbone families and task distributions. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond Qwen2.5-VL, whether the binary mask granularity is sufficient for subtle perceptual errors, and whether the Truncate-then-Regenerate loop can be made more efficient than the current iterative truncation.
