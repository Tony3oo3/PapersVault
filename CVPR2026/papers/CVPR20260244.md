## Problem

Unsupervised 3D instance segmentation methods that transfer object-centric priors from synthetic datasets (e.g., ShapeNet) to real-world scans (e.g., ScanNet) suffer from a geometric domain gap: real objects exhibit morphological variations and occlusion artifacts absent in clean synthetic shapes, causing pretrained objectness scorers to misjudge candidates and mislead the discovery process. This paper specifically addresses the limitation of GrabS, where the frozen object-centric network cannot adapt to target-domain shape diversity or handle incomplete geometries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260244/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unannotated 3D scene point cloud as input and produces per-point binary instance segmentation masks (class-agnostic, e.g., all masks labeled as 'chair'). During training, a dynamic container agent crops variable-sized point subsets (P_c ∈ R^{K×3}) from the scene; these subsets are processed through discerning and completion stages before being scored by a frozen object-centric network. At inference, a trained feedforward segmentation network (f_seg) directly predicts masks from the full scene without the RL agent.

## Method

EvObj extends GrabS with two chained modules bridging the synthetic-to-real gap. First, an object candidate discerning and evolving module (f_dis_evo, a SparseConv per-point binary segmentation network) separates foreground objects from background noise in each cropped subset; it is pretrained on ShapeNet with synthetic wall/floor planes, then continuously evolved during RL training by fine-tuning on the latest accumulated high-scoring pseudo-labels every T epochs, enabling progressive adaptation to target-domain morphology. Second, an object candidate completion module (f_comp, based on AdaPoinTr) reconstructs full 3D shapes from occluded partial candidates before they are scored by the frozen object-centric network (VAE or Diffusion), mitigating the low objectness scores that incomplete real-world geometries would otherwise receive. The RL policy network (dynamic container agent) is optimized via PPO using objectness scores as rewards, while the evolving discerning module and final segmentation network are trained entirely on self-generated pseudo-labels without any scene-level annotations.

## Results

On the ScanNet validation set (chair category), EvObj achieves 55.0% AP and 76.9% AP@50 with the VAE variant, outperforming the strongest unsupervised baseline GrabS-Diffusion (47.1% AP, 70.6% AP@50) by ~8 AP points. On the ScanNet hidden test set, EvObj reaches 34.0% AP, matching the supervised 3D-BoNet (34.5% AP) while remaining fully unsupervised. Cross-dataset generalization to S3DIS Area5 yields 60.6% AP (Diffusion variant), and on the synthetic multi-class dataset EvObj attains 62.1% AP with 90.3% AP@25, demonstrating strong occlusion handling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvObj outperforms all unsupervised baselines across every dataset and metric. Against GrabS (the direct predecessor), EvObj gains +7.9 AP on ScanNet val (55.0 vs 47.1) and +5.0 AP on the hidden test set (34.0 vs 29.0), with the gap widening on S3DIS Area5 (+6.4 AP over GrabS-VAE, +16.4 over GrabS-Diffusion). Against 2D-to-3D transfer methods, EvObj surpasses UnScene3D by +17.8 AP and Part2Object by +20.6 AP on ScanNet val, and by +12.5 and +21.1 AP respectively on S3DIS Area5. Against the geometric-reconstruction baseline EFEM, the margin is even larger: +30.4 AP on ScanNet val and +45.7 AP on S3DIS Area5. On the synthetic multi-class dataset, EvObj leads GrabS-Diffusion by +2.6 AP and +8.5 AP@25, with the AP@25 gain attributed to the object completion module recovering occluded geometries. Notably, on the ScanNet hidden test set EvObj (34.0 AP) nearly matches the supervised 3D-BoNet (34.5 AP), though it remains well below stronger supervised methods like Mask3D (73.7 AP) and SoftGroup (69.4 AP).

## Relation to Prior Work

EvObj is a direct extension of GrabS (ICLR 2025), which established the paradigm of using a pretrained object-centric generative network (VAE or diffusion) as an objectness scorer combined with an RL policy network for unsupervised 3D object discovery. The key departure is addressing the geometric domain gap between synthetic pretraining shapes (ShapeNet) and real-world scans (ScanNet) through two new modules: a per-point discerning/evolving network (SparseConv) that progressively adapts to target-domain morphology via self-supervised pseudo-labels, and a completion network (AdaPoinTr) that reconstructs occluded geometries before scoring. Within the broader unsupervised 3D segmentation landscape, EvObj occupies the 'geometric reconstruction priors' branch alongside EFEM, while distinguishing itself from 2D-to-3D feature transfer methods (UnScene3D, Part2Object) by operating entirely in 3D space without relying on image features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The synthetic-to-real domain gap in 3D object priors is a fundamental bottleneck for annotation-free 3D perception, and EvObj's evolving strategy—continuously refining the discerning module with accumulated high-scoring pseudo-labels—offers a practical mechanism for progressive domain adaptation without requiring any scene-level annotations. The near-parity with supervised 3D-BoNet on the ScanNet test set (34.0 vs 34.5 AP) is notable, though the supervised baseline itself is relatively weak. Limitations include reliance on synthetic pretraining for initialization, the somewhat ad-hoc evolving schedule (fixed T epochs, discard-after-use pseudo-labels), and evaluation primarily on a single object category (chairs) in ScanNet. The authors identify leveraging large-scale 3D generative models and multimodal foundation models as promising future directions to further close the annotation-free gap.
