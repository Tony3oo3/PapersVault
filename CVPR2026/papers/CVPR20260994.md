## Problem

Vision-Language Models apply uniform positional indices (p_i = i) to all tokens regardless of their information content, treating semantically dense text tokens and spatially redundant visual patches with identical positional granularity. This wastes representational capacity on redundant visual regions while under-representing informative content, and fails to adapt to task-dependent shifts in modality contribution (e.g., visual-dominant scene understanding vs. text-dominant chart reading). No prior method dynamically adjusts positional strides based on information-theoretic analysis of modality contributions.

## Input / Output

MODIX operates on the unified embedding matrix E ∈ R^{N×d} (N = n_t + n_v) produced by a pretrained VLM's projection of text tokens and visual patches into a shared space. It outputs a reconstructed positional index vector P′ ∈ R^N where text positions are preserved (p_i′ = i for i < n_t) and vision positions advance with an adaptive stride Δ_vision, which is then fed into the standard RoPE rotation. The method is purely inference-time: it requires no parameter updates, no architectural changes, and adds only ~1% wall-clock overhead (e.g., 0.0014 s on Qwen3-VL-8B).

## Method

MODIX computes a per-modality information contribution from two complementary pathways: (1) intra-modal density, estimated as the log-determinant of the empirical covariance matrix of each modality's embeddings (a Gaussian differential-entropy proxy), and (2) inter-modal interaction, measured by averaging the maximum L2-normalized cross-modal similarity for each token. These are fused via a geometric mean C_m = (I_m^intra)^α (I_m^inter)^(1−α) with α = 0.5, then normalized to yield unified contributions C̃_m. The adaptive vision stride is set to Δ_vision = C̃_text / C̃_vision while text stride is fixed at 1 to preserve pretrained positional priors; vision indices are reconstructed piecewise as p_i′ = n_t + Δ_vision·(i − n_t + 1). The adjusted indices replace the original sequential indices in RoPE's rotation matrices, so attention scores now reflect both token separation and modality information contribution, with no modification to model weights or architecture.

## Results

Across three VLM architectures (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, LFM2-VL) spanning 1.6B–8B parameters, MODIX yields average improvements of +3.23% on ScienceQA, +3.80% on AI2D, +3.13% on DocVQA, +2.75% on ChartQA, +2.09% on RealWorldQA, and +1.79% on BLINK. Gains are most pronounced on smaller models: LFM2-VL-1.6B improves by +8.42% on ScienceQA and +6.80% on RealWorldQA, while Qwen3-VL-2B gains +6.10% on ScienceQA and +6.12% on ChartQA. On Video-MME, MODIX adds +2.23–2.66% on medium/long videos, confirming effective temporal redundancy compression. All improvements are statistically significant (Wilcoxon signed-rank, p < 0.05) with standard deviations below 0.5% over 10 runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MODIX is compared against three multimodal positional encoding variants in Table 3. On Qwen3-VL-8B, MODIX achieves 90.16% on ScienceQA and 72.80% on ChartQA, outperforming CircleRoPE (88.87% / 70.83%) by +1.29% / +1.97% and MHRoPE (89.15% / 72.67%) by +1.01% / +0.13%. On InternVL3.5-8B, MODIX reaches 91.13% on ScienceQA and 59.57% on ChartQA, surpassing V2PE (89.27% / 59.12%) by +1.86% / +0.45%. MODIX wins on every benchmark–architecture pair tested, with the largest margin over V2PE on ScienceQA (+1.86%) and over CircleRoPE on ChartQA (+1.97%). The key distinction is that MODIX is training-free and adapts per-instance, whereas V2PE, CircleRoPE, and MHRoPE rely on fixed design rules or require retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

MODIX builds directly on RoPE's rotation-matrix formulation, treating the positional index as a tunable input rather than a fixed sequential counter. It departs from prior multimodal PE variants (V2PE, CircleRoPE, MHRoPE) that impose fixed design rules or require training-time modification, instead deriving adaptive strides from an information-theoretic analysis of modality contributions at inference time. The conceptual lineage draws on information bottleneck and mutual information principles from multimodal representation learning, but applies them to a previously unexplored target: the allocation of positional granularity across modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature (adding <1% inference overhead) makes MODIX immediately deployable on any RoPE-based VLM without retraining, which is practically valuable for practitioners who cannot fine-tune large models. However, the method operates at modality-level granularity (one stride per modality), limiting its ability to handle heterogeneous regions within a single image, and it is restricted to RoPE-based architectures. Open questions include whether token-level adaptive strides would yield further gains, whether the approach extends to ALiBi or learnable position embeddings, and whether training-aware integration at 70B+ scale preserves the observed benefits.
