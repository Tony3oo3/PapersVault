## Problem

Standard target-domain few-shot fine-tuning in Cross-Domain Few-Shot Learning (CDFSL) significantly exacerbates the visual attention sink in VLMs like CLIP, causing highly attended tokens to become identical across all classes and destroying discriminability. Prior work had not identified or explained why this happens: the model takes a shortcut by over-attending to 'simple' tokens that are already close to all target-domain classes, absorbing domain information at the expense of harder but more discriminative tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263500/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in a source-free N-way K-shot episode setting: a batch of target-domain RGB images is encoded by the CLIP ViT-B/16 visual encoder into patch token sequences, and class names are encoded via a prompt template (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}') into text embeddings. The TIR module is inserted between specific intermediate transformer blocks (after blocks 8 and 10), where it re-weights the visual token features before they propagate to deeper layers. The final [CLS] token is compared against text embeddings via cosine similarity with a temperature of 0.01 to produce the class prediction.

## Method

Token Importance Recalibration (TIR) dynamically re-weights visual tokens based on their cross-class activation pattern during target-domain fine-tuning. At the insertion point, visual tokens are projected into the text embedding space, and cosine similarity is computed against all K class text embeddings; the top-30% most similar tokens per class are marked, and a 'Sum' score counts how many classes each token activates. A linear re-weighting scheme then suppresses sink tokens (Sum=K → weight 0) while enhancing discriminative tokens (Sum=1 → weight 2), with a neutrality threshold at Sum=3. This shifts the model's learning from the shortcut of aligning already-close simple tokens to the harder task of aligning class-specific discriminative tokens, reducing attention sink and improving cross-domain classification accuracy.

## Results

On the 5-way 1-shot setting, CLIP-LoRA-Vision+TIR achieves an average accuracy of 58.20% across four target domains (ISIC 39.38%, EuroSAT 82.53%, CropDiseases 86.91%, ChestX 23.98%), outperforming the best source-free baseline REAP (56.04%) by 2.16 points. On the 5-way 5-shot setting, the method reaches 68.44% average accuracy (ISIC 56.73%, EuroSAT 93.49%, CropDiseases 97.42%, ChestX 26.12%), surpassing the strongest competitor ReCIT (68.06%) and the source-free baseline CLIP-LoRA-Vision (65.99%) by 2.45 points. The method is source-free (no source-domain data used) and uses CLIP ViT-B/16 as backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method (CLIP-LoRA-Vision+TIR) is a source-free approach using CLIP ViT-B/16, and it achieves the best average accuracy in both 1-shot (58.20%) and 5-shot (68.44%) settings. In the 1-shot setting, it outperforms the strongest source-free baseline REAP (56.04%) by 2.16 points and the direct baseline CLIP-LoRA-Vision (55.92%) by 2.28 points, with the largest gains on ISIC (+4.15 over REAP) and EuroSAT (+6.56 over REAP). In the 5-shot setting, it surpasses ReCIT (68.06%) by 0.38 points and REAP (68.03%) by 0.41 points on average, with particularly strong improvements on ISIC (+1.45 over ReCIT) and EuroSAT (+1.91 over ReCIT). Notably, the method also outperforms all source-available methods (which use source-domain data during training) in both shot settings, demonstrating that the token recalibration strategy compensates for the absence of source data. The only dataset where the method does not achieve the top score is ChestX in the 5-shot setting (26.12% vs. ReCIT's 28.88%), suggesting the attention sink phenomenon is less dominant in that particular medical imaging domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP-LoRA-Vision (Zanella & Ben Ayed, CVPRW 2024) as its fine-tuning backbone, inserting a token re-weighting module between transformer blocks. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate research threads: the CDFSL literature (which focuses on domain adaptation strategies like style adversarial training, prompt tuning, and contrastive learning) and the attention sink literature (which has been studied in language models and recently in vision transformers, but not in the context of cross-domain fine-tuning). The key departure from prior CDFSL work is the identification of fine-tuning itself—as opposed to the domain shift—as the primary driver of attention sink exacerbation, reframing the problem as a shortcut learning issue rather than a pure domain gap problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard few-shot fine-tuning *worsens* rather than improves class discriminability by amplifying attention sink is a useful diagnostic for practitioners fine-tuning VLMs in cross-domain settings, suggesting that naive fine-tuning can silently degrade the model's ability to distinguish classes. The token re-weighting approach is simple and plug-and-play, but its evaluation is limited to a single backbone (CLIP ViT-B/16) and four target domains, leaving open whether the shortcut learning interpretation and the re-weighting strategy generalize to other VLMs (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2-based pipelines) or to settings with smaller domain gaps. A broader open question is whether the identified shortcut—preferentially aligning already-close tokens—manifests similarly in other few-shot or domain adaptation paradigms beyond CLIP-based classification.
