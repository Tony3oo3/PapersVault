## Problem

Diffusion Bridge Models (DBMs) enable high-fidelity image-to-image translation but require dozens of function evaluations (e.g., 119 NFEs for Hybrid Heun) to produce coherent outputs. Existing fast samplers for standard diffusion models (DDIM, DPM-Solver++) assume a Gaussian prior, which is invalid for I2I translation where the terminal distribution is an arbitrary image. No efficient, theoretically valid sampler existed for DBMs that could handle arbitrary priors while reducing NFEs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image x_T (the arbitrary prior, e.g., a sketch, depth map, semantic label, or face photo) and a pretrained x0-predicting DBM network D_θ, and produces a translated image x_0. It operates as a drop-in replacement for existing DBM sampling loops with no architectural changes or retraining, tested at resolutions from 64×64 (Edges2Handbags) to 256×256 (DIODE, Face2Comics, CelebAMask-HQ, ImageNet inpainting). The key constraint is that x_T must be a real image rather than Gaussian noise, which is what distinguishes DBM sampling from standard DPM sampling.

## Method

DBMSolver exploits the semi-linear structure of the Bridge SDE and Bridge PF ODE—linear in x_t with non-linear score terms—by applying Exponential Integrators (EI) to obtain exact closed-form solutions for the linear components, combined with Taylor expansions for the non-linear x0-predictor terms. The sampling procedure has two phases: a 1st-order Bridge SDE solution (Proposition 1) handles the initial stochastic step from T to T−ε, and a 2nd-order Bridge ODE solution (Proposition 2, k=2 Taylor approximation of the exponential integral) handles all subsequent deterministic refinement steps. This yields a Markovian, 2nd-order sampler that reduces NFEs by up to 5× (6 NFEs vs. 119 for Hybrid Heun) while improving FID (e.g., 3.38 vs. 4.43 on DIODE at 6 NFEs). The approach is entirely training-free, requiring no distillation, fine-tuning, or retraining of the underlying DBM, and avoids the non-elementary antiderivatives that would arise at k≥3 by restricting to the analytically tractable 2nd-order solution.

## Results

On DIODE (256×256), DBMSolver achieves FID 3.38 at just 6 NFEs and FID 2.06 at 20 NFEs, outperforming Hybrid Heun (FID 4.43 at 119 NFEs) and all DBIM variants (FID 4.23–4.99 at 20 NFEs). On Edges2Handbags (64×64), it reaches FID 0.93 at 6 NFEs and FID 0.53 at 20 NFEs, beating ODES3 (FID 0.54 at 28 NFEs) with fewer evaluations. On Face2Comics (256×256), DBMSolver at 20 NFEs achieves FID 0.96, dramatically surpassing DBIM-3 (FID 8.61) and Hybrid Heun (FID 2.36 at 119 NFEs). On ImageNet inpainting, DBMSolver at 20 NFEs matches DBIM-2/3 (FID 4.07, CA 72.0) while at 6 NFEs it already delivers FID 4.98 with CA 70.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBMSolver at 6 NFEs already outperforms Hybrid Heun [43] (119 NFEs) on DIODE (FID 3.38 vs. 4.43) and Edges2Handbags (FID 0.93 vs. 1.83), demonstrating a ~20× reduction in NFEs with better quality. At 20 NFEs, DBMSolver decisively beats all DBIM variants [42] on DIODE (FID 2.06 vs. 4.23–4.99) and Edges2Handbags (FID 0.53 vs. 1.45–1.74), and on Face2Comics it achieves FID 0.96 versus DBIM-3's 8.61 — a nearly 9× FID gap. On ImageNet inpainting, DBMSolver at 20 NFEs ties DBIM-2/3 (FID 4.07, CA 72.0) but at 6 NFEs it already reaches FID 4.98/CA 70.8, whereas DBIM-1 at 20 NFEs only achieves FID 4.13/CA 71.9. DBMSolver at 20 NFEs slightly edges out ODES3 [39] on DIODE (FID 2.06 vs. 2.29 at 28 NFEs) and Edges2Handbags (FID 0.53 vs. 0.54 at 28 NFEs), using fewer evaluations. On CelebAMask-HQ, DBMSolver (FID 17.56 at 20 NFEs) outperforms DBIM-1/2/3 (FID 23.41/19.86/19.49) and Hybrid Heun (FID 97.75 at 119 NFEs), confirming consistent gains across all evaluated I2I tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Diffusion Bridge Model (DDBM) framework of Zhou et al. [43], which introduced Doob's h-transform to enable I2I translation via diffusion bridges between arbitrary distributions, and on the Exponential Integrator (EI) numerical method of Hochbruck & Ostermann [12] for semi-linear ODEs. It departs from prior DBM samplers—Hybrid Heun (alternating Euler-Maruyama/Heun), DBIM (linear multistep methods), and ODES3 (Euler-Maruyama + Heun)—by identifying the semi-linear structure of the Bridge SDE and PF ODE (linear in x_t with non-linear score terms) and deriving exact closed-form EI solutions, rather than relying on crude step-by-step approximations. N2I fast samplers like DPM-Solver++ are explicitly shown to be theoretically invalid here because their Gaussian-prior assumption breaks for arbitrary bridge endpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DBMSolver offers a training-free, drop-in replacement that reduces NFEs by up to 5× (and ~20× vs. Hybrid Heun) while improving FID, making DBM-based I2I translation practically deployable in near-real-time settings without retraining. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that on more complex tasks like ImageNet inpainting, the non-linear D_θ term dominates approximation error, yielding only marginal gains over prior solvers. Open directions include text-conditioned I2I translation, adaptive step-size selection, and integration with flow-matching frameworks, suggesting the semi-linear EI approach could generalize beyond the current VP/VE bridge setting.
