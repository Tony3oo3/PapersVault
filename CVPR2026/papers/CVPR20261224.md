## Problem

Adapting pretrained vision models to medical imaging domains (ultrasound, CT, MRI) typically requires heavy task-specific fine-tuning that degrades general-image capability, while test-time adaptation blurs the boundary between training and evaluation. The paper addresses the gap of enhancing medical-domain robustness (speckle noise, acoustic shadows, indistinct boundaries) while explicitly preserving natural-image performance under a frozen backbone with fixed, update-free inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261224/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 224×224 RGB images from either medical modalities (fetal ultrasound, cardiac CT/MRI) or natural imagery, processed through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone. Beyond the final class label or per-pixel segmentation mask, the Domain Router emits a soft two-dimensional probability vector [p_medical, p_natural] that continuously conditions all downstream modulation, enabling smooth interpolation between domain-specific parameter regimes rather than hard domain switching. Inference is entirely forward-pass with no gradient-based test-time updates, keeping latency at ~0.3 min/epoch and 4.7 GFLOPs.

## Method

DCRM-ViT keeps a pretrained ViT backbone fully frozen and inserts lightweight Residual Modulation Blocks (RMBs) at selected Q and V projections within each transformer block. Each RMB contains a Task-Aligned Layer (TALer) — a low-rank encoder–ReLU–decoder pair with a rescaling factor — whose weights are not fixed but synthesized per-sample by a Parameter Synthesizer Network (PSN), a small hypernetwork conditioned on input embeddings and soft domain probabilities from a Domain Router (DR). An optional Domain-Aware Bias (DAB) adds a learned, domain-weighted matrix to attention logits with negligible overhead. Training follows a bi-level (MAML-inspired) scheme: an inner loop performs a few gradient steps on RMB parameters for task-specific adaptation, while an outer loop updates the DR weights, PSN parameters, RMB initialization, and inner-loop learning rate using domain-characteristic examples, cleanly separating domain-level meta-parameters from task-level representation learning. Only ~3.3 M parameters (~3.5% of ViT-B) are trainable, with the PSN contributing ~3.0 M of those.

## Results

On fine-tuned classification, DCRM-ViT achieves 63.4% accuracy (F1 0.69) on Fpus23 and 89.3% (F1 0.90) on Fetal Planes, outperforming the strongest PEFT baseline LoRA (63.0% / 88.3%) and full fine-tuning baselines MAE and DINOv2. On fine-grained natural-image benchmarks it reaches 95.7% on Food101, 81.3% on SUN397, and 91.5% on Stanford Cars, all leading among compared methods. For segmentation, DCRM-ViT attains 0.862 Dice on BUS-UCLM and 0.928 on ACDC, surpassing SAMUS by +3.07 and +2.23 points on average across ultrasound and CT/MRI datasets respectively, while training only 3.3 M parameters at 0.3 min/epoch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCRM-ViT outperforms all 13 baselines across every reported benchmark. Among full fine-tuning methods, it leads MAE by +5.7 pts on Fpus23 and +7.8 pts on Fetal Planes, and DINOv2 by +4.1 and +1.5 pts respectively; CLIP is notably weak on Fetal Planes (42.2%) due to its natural-image pretraining. Among PEFT baselines, LoRA is the closest competitor (63.0% vs 63.4% on Fpus23, 94.5% vs 95.7% on Food101), while AdaptFormer and Tip-Adapter trail by 1–2 pts on medical datasets and 1–3 pts on fine-grained natural-image sets. In zero-shot evaluation, DCRM-ViT surpasses MedCLIP, UniMedCLIP, and BioMedCLIP on both fetal datasets (e.g., 30.3% vs 22.1% on Fpus23) and also leads on natural-image zero-shot (75.2% on CIFAR-10 vs 71.9% CLIP). For segmentation, DCRM-ViT improves over SAMUS by +4.5 pts on BUS-UCLM (0.862 vs 0.817), +1.5 pts on BUID, +3.2 pts on BUS-BRA, +2.3 pts on ACDC, +1.9 pts on MMWHS-CT, and +2.5 pts on MMWHS-MRI, while also exceeding U-Net, SAMed, and FetalCLIP on all six datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCRM-ViT synthesizes three distinct lineages: MAML-style bi-level optimization (Finn et al., 2017) for separating domain-level meta-parameters from task-level adaptation, hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2016) for the Parameter Synthesizer Network that generates per-sample low-rank weights, and meta-adapter / domain-adaptation adapters (Bansal et al., 2022; Malik et al., 2023) for the Domain Router that produces soft domain weights. It departs from standard PEFT methods (LoRA, AdaptFormer, Tip-Adapter) by replacing static, task-specific low-rank modules with input-conditioned, per-sample residuals, and from test-time adaptation by keeping inference entirely update-free. The key conceptual departure is treating the medical–natural domain boundary as a continuous, per-sample quantity rather than a discrete routing decision, enabling a single frozen ViT to serve both regimes without weight swapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a single frozen backbone that handles ultrasound, CT, MRI, and natural imagery with only ~3.3 M trainable parameters and 0.3 min/epoch, making it deployable in latency-sensitive clinical pipelines where full fine-tuning or test-time updates are infeasible. Open questions include whether the binary medical/natural domain classifier generalizes to finer-grained or more than two domains, whether the bi-level optimization is strictly necessary versus a simpler joint training schedule (the ablation shows a 4.6-point drop without it, but the gap narrows on natural-image datasets), and how the approach scales to 3D volumetric medical imaging. The very low zero-shot medical accuracy (~30% on Fpus23) also underscores that domain-conditioned modulation cannot fully bridge the representation gap without task-specific training, limiting the method's applicability in truly data-scarce clinical settings.
