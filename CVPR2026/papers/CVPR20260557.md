## Problem

Adversarial attacks (requiring gradient access at inference) and backdoor attacks (requiring training data access) are traditionally designed separately, each tied to a specific permission that may become unavailable when deployment environments change. Moreover, both rely on limiting perturbation magnitude for stealthiness, which inherently reduces point cloud quality and caps attack intensity. The paper asks whether a single attack mechanism can simultaneously serve both threat models while guaranteeing stealthiness through a fundamentally different strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260557/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign unordered 3D point cloud P ∈ R^(n×3) and, depending on the attack mode, either the victim 3D DNN's classification loss gradient (adversarial) or a pre-trained surrogate 3D DNN with a target label y^t (backdoor). Key hyperparameters include the ray direction angles (η, γ), the grid density k of starting points, and a step size t_s controlling per-iteration movement. The output is a single attacked point cloud of the same cardinality as the input, whose points are repositioned to be isotropically distributed on the approximated surface while carrying the malicious perturbation or trigger.

## Method

UAtt3D builds on a Flexible Isotropic Resampling (FIR) pipeline: first, the point cloud surface is approximated by a low-quality 3D mesh via an improved Alpha Shapes method with curvature-adaptive radius (r_i = R·Cur(p_i)) and outlier removal; then, parallel rays are emitted from evenly spaced starting points on three mutually orthogonal tangent planes of the circumscribed sphere, and their intersections with mesh triangles yield the resampled point cloud. For adversarial attack, each point is iteratively displaced by the sum of the loss-gradient vector and its projection onto the sampling ray direction, stopping upon misclassification. For backdoor attack, the ray angles (η*, γ*) are optimized via gradient descent on a surrogate 3D DNN to minimize the feature distance to the target label, making the resampling itself the trigger. The central insight is that stealthiness is achieved not by shrinking perturbations but by improving the isotropy of the attacked cloud (lower CUD/KUV than the benign original), so the malicious deformation is masked by a perceptible quality gain.

## Results

On ModelNet40 backdoor attack, UAtt3D achieves ASR of 97.62–99.91% across four victim 3D DNNs (PointConv, PointNet++, DGCNN, CurveNet) while improving point cloud isotropy to CUD=62.27 and KUV=0.33, both better than the benign baseline (CUD=103.49, KUV=0.51). For untargeted adversarial attack on ModelNet40, UAtt3D reaches ASR of 95.78–100% with CUD=63.61–68.79 and KUV=0.36–0.61, again outperforming the benign quality. On ShapeNet16, UAtt3D achieves 98.75–99.92% ASR (backdoor) and 97.10–100% ASR (adversarial) with CUD reduced from 300.57 (benign) to 1393.63/155.01 respectively. In human inspection (176 participants), 80.62% rated UAtt3D-attacked point clouds as the highest quality, surpassing the benign point cloud (19.31%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAtt3D achieves competitive or superior ASR against all 11 baselines while uniquely improving point cloud quality (lower CUD/KUV). Against backdoor baselines, UAtt3D (98.94% ASR on PointNet++/MN40) slightly trails PointPBA-I (100%) but dramatically outperforms it on quality (CUD 62.27 vs. 152.05; KUV 0.33 vs. 4.34); it also surpasses NRBdoor (99.27%), IRBA (89.82%), PCBA (78.68%), and IBAPC (92.08%) in ASR while improving isotropy over all. Against adversarial baselines on MN40, UAtt3D (100% ASR on PointConv/PointNet++) matches Eidos, HiT, SIA, and GeoA3 (all 100%) but achieves far better quality (CUD 63.61–64.89 vs. 108.92–260.07; KUV 0.36–0.46 vs. 0.98–2.06), and outperforms SS-Attack (98.55%) and AdvPC (99.60%) in ASR. The key differentiator is that UAtt3D is the only method whose attacked point cloud has better isotropy than the benign input, whereas all baselines degrade quality. On the practical ScanObjectNN dataset, UAtt3D achieves 97.66% ASR (backdoor) and 100% ASR (adversarial) with CUD reduced from 365.29 to 157.29, outperforming PointBA-I (96.12%), IRBA (68.80%), NRBdoor (59.17%), and IBAPC (91.25%) in ASR while maintaining the best quality metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant stealthiness paradigm in 3D point cloud attacks, where prior methods (PointBA, PCBA, IRBA, HiT, SIA, GeoA3, etc.) guarantee imperceptibility by constraining perturbation magnitude to minimize shape deformation. UAtt3D inverts this logic: it uses a flexible isotropic resampling (FIR) built on Alpha Shapes surface approximation and parallel ray sampling to *improve* the isotropy of the attacked point cloud, so the malicious perturbation is masked by a quality gain rather than a quality loss. The unification of adversarial and backdoor attacks into a single transformation function T(·) is a methodological extension of both lines of work, which have been studied separately due to their distinct permission requirements (gradient access vs. training data access).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'quality improvement as stealth' paradigm is a conceptually useful reframing that could generalize beyond point clouds to other data modalities where resampling or regularization naturally improves signal quality. However, the practical impact is tempered by the method's slower per-sample runtime (0.528s vs. 0.381–0.493s for strong baselines) and its dependence on surface approximation, which, while robust to defects, adds a non-trivial preprocessing step. Open questions include whether the ray-sampling flexibility scales to very large point clouds, whether the surrogate-model-based angle search (Eq. 11) remains effective against strongly regularized victim models, and whether the quality-improvement-as-stealth principle holds under more aggressive defense pipelines beyond the four tested.
