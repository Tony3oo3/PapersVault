## Problem

Computing volumetric correspondences between 3D shapes remains a largely open problem: piece-wise linear approaches to volumes are extremely challenging and lack robustness, while the functional maps framework—mature and widely used for surfaces—has never been extended to volumetric domains. This paper is the first to demonstrate that the eigenfunctions of the volumetric Laplace-Beltrami Operator define a functional space suitable for high-quality signal transfer between tetrahedral meshes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262012/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two tetrahedral meshes (or two watertight surface meshes that are first tetrahedralized via TetGen or fTetWild) and produces a k×k functional map matrix C relating their LBO eigenbases. From C, point-to-point correspondences are extracted via nearest-neighbor search on the spectral embeddings, and the same matrix enables connectivity transfer (re-meshing a target volume to match a source's topology), segmentation transfer, solid texturing, and surface correspondence. The basis size k is a tunable parameter: using 5–25% of the full spectrum trades off the fraction of inverted tetrahedra in the induced piece-wise map against computational cost.

## Method

The volumetric LBO is discretized on tetrahedral meshes using the n-dimensional cotangent formula, yielding stiffness and mass matrices whose generalized eigenproblem (SΦ = WΦΛ) is solved with Neumann boundary conditions to avoid zero-valued eigenfunctions on the surface. The functional map C is then estimated by porting existing surface alignment techniques—descriptor preservation (FMaps), ZoomOut spectral upsampling, and Orthoprods basis—to the volumetric setting. A key theoretical insight is that boundary traces of eigenfunctions under Neumann conditions are linearly independent (a result from control theory), which allows the volumetric map to be approximated from a known surface correspondence alone, enabling both direct coordinate transfer and spectral extrapolation of interior coordinates from surface data. The framework also demonstrates that a volumetric functional map induces a more accurate surface correspondence than a surface-only map, and that using the volumetric map as an initial guess dramatically improves the success rate of mesh untangling algorithms (recovering 85% of previously failed cases).

## Results

On the shape matching benchmark (Table 2), the volumetric functional map consistently reduces Average Geodesic Error (AGE) compared to its surface-only counterpart: on SHREC'19, LBO-based volumetric matching achieves AGE 6.64×10⁻² vs. 1.03×10⁻¹ for surface (36% reduction, 53.15% success rate); on TOPKIDS, AGE drops from 1.24×10⁻¹ to 8.85×10⁻² (29% reduction, 60% success rate). For connectivity transfer (Table 1), using 20% of the spectrum with the CMH basis yields only 0.34% flipped tetrahedra, and the extrapolation variant is roughly 2× faster than full volumetric transfer. The computational overhead from adding interior vertices is moderate: average slowdown ranges from 1.01× (SHREC'20) to 2.51× (TOPKIDS), closely tracking the vertex-count ratio between surface and volume meshes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between surface-only and volumetric versions of the same functional map algorithms (FMaps, ZoomOut, Orthoprods) on four shape-matching datasets. On SHREC'19 and TOPKIDS, the volumetric approach wins across all three bases, reducing AGE by roughly 30–36% (e.g., LBO on SHREC'19: 6.64×10⁻² vs. 1.03×10⁻¹). On the easy VOL dataset (shared connectivity, watertight shapes), the surface LBO is marginally better, but the Orthoprods basis reverses this, with volumetric Orthoprods achieving 3.19×10⁻⁷ vs. 5.82×10⁻⁷ for surface. On the hardest dataset, SHREC'20 (strongly non-isometric, topological changes), results are basis-dependent: LBO and Orthoprods volumetric versions are worse than surface due to tetrahedralization topological errors, while CMH volumetric is substantially better (1.82×10⁻¹ vs. 2.70×10⁻¹) because its coordinate regularizer compensates for meshing artifacts. For connectivity transfer, the paper shows that bootstrapping state-of-the-art untangling with their volumetric functional map recovers 85% of the 1405 failure cases of the Tutte-based pipeline, a dramatic robustness improvement over the existing piece-wise linear baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional maps framework (Ovsjanikov et al. 2012) from 2D triangular surfaces to 3D tetrahedral volumes, porting the entire spectral pipeline — LBO eigenbasis computation, basis alignment via descriptor preservation (Nogneng 2017) and ZoomOut (Melzi 2019), and alternative bases like Orthoprods (Maggioli 2021) and CMH (Marin 2019) — to the volumetric setting. It deliberately contrasts with the piece-wise linear volume mapping literature, which the authors argue is fundamentally harder (ill-posed untangling, no robust Tutte embedding in 3D), whereas the spectral approach inherits its mesh-agnosticism from the continuous LBO and scales naturally to volumes. The work also connects to the LDDMM tradition of volumetric registration but departs from it by operating on unstructured tetrahedral meshes rather than grid-structured data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first spectral volumetric correspondence pipeline, opening a research direction that the authors argue is more tractable than piece-wise linear volume mapping. Practical impact is demonstrated through connectivity transfer (improving untangling success from 0% to 85% on known failure cases), medical segmentation transfer, and solid texturing. Key limitations include the computational cost of eigendecomposition on dense tetrahedral meshes (roughly 2× slowdown from added interior vertices) and sensitivity to tetrahedralization quality on noisy inputs (SHREC'20 results degrade with LBO/Orthoprods bases). Open questions include a theoretical characterization of invariance under volumetric isometries, scalable algorithms for volume basis computation, and the need for dedicated volumetric benchmarks beyond tetrahedralizations of surface datasets.
