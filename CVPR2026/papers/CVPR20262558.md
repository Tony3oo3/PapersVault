## Problem

Vision-language models process hundreds to thousands of visual tokens per image, making them memory- and latency-bound due to quadratic attention costs. Existing efficiency methods either merge redundant tokens too early in the vision encoder (risking information loss) or drop them uniformly in the language backbone (lacking semantic adaptivity), but none jointly optimizes redundancy removal and context-aware retention across the vision-language interface.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (encoded to 576 tokens via CLIP for LLaVA-1.5, 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT) or 8-frame video sequences (up to 2048 tokens for Video-LLaVA) paired with a text prompt consisting of a system prompt and query tokens. Visual tokens are progressively compressed from the full budget down to 64–192 tokens (or 136 for video) before the final answer is generated. The text prompt is fully preserved through the network, but only a subset of salient text tokens is used to compute cross-attention scores that guide which visual tokens to retain at each pruning stage.

## Method

DUET-VLM is a plug-and-play dual-stage compression framework. Stage 1 (V2V merging) uses CLIP's last-layer self-attention to select k₁ dominant tokens, then partitions the remaining tokens into k₂ small local clusters (width w) via attention-guided centroid selection, producing contextual tokens through local averaging rather than global pooling—this avoids diluting low-frequency semantic features. Stage 2 (T2V pruning) operates inside the LLM: at selected layers (stages), cross-attention scores between a set of salient text tokens (identified by a salient-token estimator, always including the last token as a sink) and the currently retained visual tokens rank them, and the bottom-ranked tokens are dropped according to a target compression ratio λ. The framework is differentiable and can be applied inference-only or during fine-tuning, enabling the model to adapt its representations to the reduced token budget. The key design choice over prior work is the coupling of early structural merging (preserving low-level detail) with late semantic pruning (removing contextually irrelevant tokens), plus the local clustering modification that drops unassigned residual tokens before they enter the language backbone.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B (576-token baseline), DUET-VLM (C) retains 99.0%, 98.1%, and 95.4% of baseline average accuracy at 192, 128, and 64 visual tokens (67%, 78%, and 89% reduction), respectively, outperforming all seven baselines at every budget. Under training with the same compression, DUET-VLM (C) achieves 99.7% at 192 tokens and 97.6% at 64 tokens, while reducing training time by 26–36%. On Video-LLaVA-7B, DUET-VLM (C) surpasses the full-token baseline (100.8% relative accuracy) at 960 tokens and retains 97.6% at an extreme 136-token budget (93.4% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUET-VLM (C) outperforms all seven baselines at every token budget on LLaVA-1.5-7B. At 192 tokens (67% reduction), it leads the nearest competitor FitPrune by 1.2 percentage points (99.0% vs. 97.8%) and VisionZip by 1.3 points (99.0% vs. 97.7%), while FastV trails by a wide 12.2 points. At the extreme 64-token budget (89% reduction), the gap widens: DUET-VLM (C) achieves 95.4% versus VisionZip's 92.8% (+2.6 pts), PyramidDrop's 86.7% (+8.7 pts), and FastV's 70.7% (+24.7 pts). On Video-LLaVA-7B at 136 tokens (93.4% reduction), DUET-VLM (C) retains 97.6% relative accuracy, beating VisionZip (91.0%), SparseVLM (82.4%), and FastV (45.8%) by 6.6, 15.2, and 51.8 points respectively. Notably, at moderate compression (192 tokens) DUET-VLM (C) even exceeds the full 576-token baseline on POPE (86.5 vs. 85.9) and SQA (69.6 vs. 69.5), suggesting the dual-stage compression acts as a mild regularizer. The trained variant DUET-VLM (C) further narrows the gap to 99.7% at 192 tokens and 97.6% at 64 tokens, confirming that end-to-end training under compression recovers most of the remaining accuracy loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUET-VLM unifies two previously separate lines of visual token compression: VisionZip's (CVPR 2025) vision-encoder-side merging of dominant and contextual tokens, and PyramidDrop's (CVPR 2025) progressive layer-wise token dropping in the language backbone. The key departures are (a) replacing VisionZip's global averaging of residual tokens with attention-guided local cluster aggregation (fixed cluster width w), and (b) replacing PyramidDrop's single last-token saliency signal with a salient-text-token estimator that selects query-relevant tokens for cross-attention ranking. Prior methods (FastV, FitPrune, PruMerge, HiRED, SparseVLM) operate in only one of these two stages; DUET-VLM is the first to couple both under a single differentiable pipeline and to train end-to-end under the compressed token budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a plug-and-play 67% token reduction retaining >99% accuracy (and 89% reduction retaining >97%) on LLaVA-1.5-7B, with a 31% training-time savings, makes high-resolution VLMs substantially cheaper to train and deploy without architectural changes. The finding that training under compression (rather than applying compression only at inference) yields consistent gains over inference-only baselines suggests that token-budget-aware fine-tuning is an underexploited lever for VLM efficiency. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer video sequences and higher-resolution inputs (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT at 2880+ tokens), whether stronger vision-side saliency beyond V2V self-attention could further improve compression, and whether the dual-stage paradigm extends to additional modalities such as audio. The authors note they do not report absolute wall-clock times, leaving the practical speedup dependent on kernel-level optimization.
