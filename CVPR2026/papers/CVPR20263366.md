## Problem

Diffusion-based generative augmentation for long-tailed learning commonly employs head-to-tail transfer to mitigate generator bias, but this strategy induces latent non-local feature leakage that entangles inter-class distributions, causing decision boundary overlap and tail class distribution shift. The paper identifies and formalizes this issue as 'boundary ambiguity,' showing that while head-to-tail transfer balances the decision space, it simultaneously blurs class separability and degrades tail accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long-tailed RGB image dataset (CIFAR-10-LT or CIFAR-100-LT at 32×32), a pre-trained diffusion model (trained under the CBDM-based setting), and a classifier trained on the source long-tailed data with logit adjustment. It outputs a filtered set of near-boundary adversarial samples that are appended to the original training set as a plug-and-play auxiliary set, with each generated sample relabeled to its k-nearest confusable class rather than its original label.

## Method

DBG comprises a boundary-aware generator and a classifier-driven bifurcated cleaning pipeline. The generator performs conditional noising by pushing each source sample to a middle diffusion timestep under its own label guidance (erasing class-specific features), then performs conditional denoising toward a target label drawn from the top-k most confusable classes predicted by the classifier, producing samples that sit near the decision boundary. The cleaning pipeline runs two parallel filters: prototype-distance filtering (using cosine distance to class prototypes with class-wise acceptance ranges to discard outliers and boundary-distant samples) and confidence-credibility filtering (removing samples misclassified into non-original, non-target labels with high confidence). The accepted adversarial set is then used to retrain the classifier, yielding a more uniform and separable decision space.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT with ResNet-32, DBG consistently improves overall accuracy across all four diffusion-based baselines, with the largest average gain of +1.57% over CBDM* (51.54% → 53.11%) and +0.60% over DiffuLT (52.79% → 53.39%). Tail-class accuracy gains are most pronounced under high imbalance: at ratio-200, CBDM* tail accuracy rises from 15.60% to 18.67% (+3.07%), and DiffuLT tail accuracy improves from 19.13% to 19.43%. With ViT-B/16 on CIFAR-100-LT, DBG yields even larger average gains, up to +2.00% over DiffuLT (34.23% → 36.23%) and +1.93% over CBDM* (31.25% → 33.18%). On CIFAR-10-LT, improvements are smaller but still positive for most baselines, with CBDM*+DBG reaching 82.74% average (↑0.65) and OCLT+DBG reaching 83.75% (↑0.46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBG is evaluated as a plug-and-play augmentation layered on top of three diffusion-based long-tailed learning baselines: CBDM/CBDM* (Qin et al., CVPR 2023), OCLT (Zhang et al., ICLR 2024), and DiffuLT (Shao et al., NeurIPS 2024). On CIFAR-100-LT with ResNet-32, DBG improves the average accuracy of every baseline, with the largest relative gain over CBDM* (+1.57%) and a clear tail-class boost at ratio-200 (15.60% → 18.67%). Over DiffuLT, DBG yields +0.60% average and +1.35% overall accuracy at ratio-100 (49.72% → 51.07%), though tail accuracy at ratio-100 drops slightly (29.37% → 28.63%). Over OCLT, the gain is more modest (+0.47% average) but tail accuracy at ratio-100 improves from 25.73% to 27.37%. With ViT-B/16, DBG shows stronger gains, particularly over DiffuLT (+2.00% average on CIFAR-100-LT) and CBDM* (+1.93%), suggesting the boundary-aware samples complement transformer-based classifiers more effectively. On CIFAR-10-LT, improvements are smaller and one configuration (CBDM+DBG) shows a slight average drop (−0.13%), likely because CBDM's strong tail-bias loss already makes tail classes easier to classify, leaving less room for boundary refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on diffusion-based generative augmentation for long-tailed recognition (CBDM, OCLT, DiffuLT), which employ head-to-tail transfer to mitigate generator bias toward head classes. It departs from this line by arguing that prior methods optimize for decision-space uniformity while neglecting separability, and that head-to-tail transfer—widely assumed to be purely beneficial—actually induces latent non-local feature mixing that blurs boundaries and shifts tail-class distributions. The boundary-aware generator repurposes adversarial perturbation (previously used for robustness or major-to-minor translation in M2M) as a mechanism to push samples toward decision boundaries, while the bifurcated cleaning pipeline draws on logit-adjusted prototype distances and classifier confidence to filter harmful generated samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'boundary ambiguity' as a distinct failure mode of head-to-tail transfer, along with three concrete diagnostic metrics (inter-class overlap via vMF/Bhattacharyya, outlier rate, generation confidence), gives the community tools to measure and track a problem that was previously only anecdotally suspected. The plug-and-play nature of DBG makes it broadly applicable to existing diffusion-based long-tailed pipelines without architectural changes. However, improvements are modest (0.25–2.0% overall accuracy), experiments are confined to CIFAR-10/100-LT, and the authors acknowledge that residual long-tailed bias in the diffusion model still limits generation quality—scaling to ImageNet-scale datasets and addressing the root generator bias remain open.
