## Problem

Existing ViT backdoor attacks either overwrite the backbone (causing severe accuracy degradation) or rely on static PEFT modules (LoRA, static VPT) that suffer from an irreconcilable 'functional conflict' between benign utility and malicious logic, making them brittle under standard defenses like pruning. The security of the rapidly evolving dynamic, context-aware prompt-based PEFT ecosystem remains critically unexplored, leaving open whether this architectural evolution introduces new, more dangerous threat vectors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (or video frames for UCF101) and produces a class label via softmax over the final CLS token. The VPG operates as a plug-in at layers 3, 6, and 9 of a frozen ViT-B/16, taking each layer's hidden state as input and generating N=8 dynamic prompt tokens that are concatenated into the feature stream. A learnable trigger δ (constrained to ‖δ‖∞ ≤ 4/255) is applied on-the-fly to clean images during training to create poisoned counterparts, while the VPG itself adds only 2.3M trainable parameters and 0.06 ms (1.16%) inference latency.

## Method

VIPER introduces a lightweight two-layer fully-connected Visual Prompt Generator (VPG) that acts as a conditional router: on clean inputs it generates benign prompts preserving accuracy, while on triggered inputs it injects layer-specific malicious prompts to force a target class. Training jointly optimizes the VPG parameters φ and the trigger δ via alternating updates under two competing cross-entropy losses (clean utility and attack effectiveness), with the ViT backbone kept frozen. The key emergent mechanism is Functional Fusion: the joint optimization pressure drives the VPG to consolidate both benign and malicious routing logic into a single sparse, high-magnitude core comprising only 0.27% of its parameters, creating a 'hostage dilemma' where pruning the attack core simultaneously destroys benign performance (91.44% → 2.52% ACC), rendering standard pruning defenses strategically impractical.

## Results

VIPER achieves the highest clean accuracy (ACC) across all six benchmark datasets while maintaining near-perfect Attack Success Rate (ASR ≥ 99.75%). On the most challenging fine-grained action recognition task (UCF-101), VIPER reaches 82.37% ACC, outperforming the next-best PEFT method (Block Expansion, 77.80%) by +4.57% and backbone-overwriting TrojViT (59.74%) by +22.63%. On DTD texture classification, VIPER (75.23%) leads the strongest baseline (BadViT, 67.59%) by +7.64%. The method adds only 0.06 ms (1.16%) inference latency and 2.3M trainable parameters, a 92.6% reduction over Block Expansion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIPER outperforms all eight baselines on clean accuracy across every dataset. Against backbone-overwriting methods, the gains are most dramatic on fine-grained tasks: VIPER beats TrojViT by +22.63% ACC on UCF-101 (82.37 vs. 59.74) and BadViT by +7.64% on DTD (75.23 vs. 67.59), while matching or exceeding their ASR. Among PEFT baselines, VIPER surpasses LoRA (r=8) by +2.14% ACC on ImageNet-100 (91.44 vs. 89.30) and by +8.02% on Food-101 (89.95 vs. 81.93), while both achieve ~100% ASR. Block Expansion (p=3) is the closest competitor on OxfordPets (93.75 vs. 94.36) but trails VIPER by +5.82% on Food-101 and +4.57% on UCF-101. The conditional prompt attack SWARM is the weakest baseline, losing to VIPER by up to +32.85% ACC on Food-101 (89.95 vs. 57.10) and +10.35% on ImageNet-100. In terms of ASR, VIPER matches or slightly exceeds all baselines (≥99.75% everywhere), with the only notable gap being BadNet's much lower ASR (49.68–87.20%), confirming VIPER's simultaneous strength in both utility preservation and attack potency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) from static, input-agnostic learnable tokens to a dynamic Visual Prompt Generator (VPG) that conditions prompt generation on intermediate ViT features, positioning it as the necessary evolution beyond both backbone-overwriting attacks (BadViT, TrojViT, AIBA) and static PEFT attacks (LoRA, Block Expansion, SWARM). The authors frame the shift from static to dynamic generation not merely as an accuracy improvement but as the mechanism that enables a qualitatively new resilience property—Functional Fusion—where malicious and benign logic become computationally inseparable within a shared sparse core. Conceptually, the paper departs from the PEFT security literature by arguing that the very design choice that resolves the accuracy-efficiency-resilience trilemma (dynamic, context-aware generation) simultaneously creates a new strategic vulnerability absent in static paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'hostage dilemma' reframing is practically significant: in the current MLOps ecosystem where third-party PEFT plugins are routinely deployed unaudited, a module whose benign performance gains are architecturally bound to its backdoor function renders the most common defense (parameter pruning) strategically impractical. The paper leaves the defense side largely open—suggesting inference-time feature denoising or dynamic routing inspection as directions without developing them—so the practical countermeasure gap is substantial. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (ViT-B/16), a single trigger type (ℓ∞-bounded pixel perturbation), and only 16 examples per class, leaving open whether Functional Fusion generalizes to deeper architectures, more complex triggers, or larger poisoning budgets.
